## Problem

Existing style transfer backbones face a fundamental trade-off: CNNs require stacking many layers for large receptive fields at high computational cost, Transformers achieve global receptive fields at quadratic complexity, and diffusion models demand numerous iterative steps. While Mamba's State Space Model offers linear-complexity long-range dependency modeling, no prior work has adapted it to arbitrary style transfer with mechanisms that flexibly incorporate style information into the SSM's state-space dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252563/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes a content image (3×4H×4W) and a style image (3×4Hs×4Ws) at potentially different resolutions, rescaled to 256×256 during training, and produces a single stylized image at the content image's resolution (3×4H×4W). The style image is encoded into a compact style embedding that conditions the decoder's SSM parameters, convolution kernels, and normalization statistics, enabling a single trained model to transfer arbitrary styles without per-style fine-tuning.

## Method

SaMam comprises a Mamba encoder (using VMamba's SS2D block with a zigzag scan for spatial continuity and a Local Enhancement module to counteract pixel forgetting) and a Style-aware Mamba decoder built from SAVSSM blocks. The key novelty is the S7 Block, which predicts the SSM's A and D matrices from the style embedding rather than using fixed parameters, making the state-space dynamics style-selective while retaining linear complexity through parallel global convolution. Additional style-aware modules—SAIN (style-conditioned instance norm with zero-initialization), SConv (style-embedding-generated depthwise convolution kernels), and SCM (style-driven channel modulation in the residual branch)—inject style information at multiple granularities. The zigzag scan replaces standard row/column scans to preserve spatial continuity across token boundaries, reducing abrupt decay changes in the SSM's hidden state.

## Results

On the MS-COCO/WikiArt evaluation protocol (800 stylized images), SaMam achieves the best scores on all four quality metrics: LPIPS 0.3884, FID 17.946, ArtFID 26.305, and CFSD 0.2703. In terms of efficiency, SaMam requires only 77.1G MACs and 0.034 s inference time on a single RTX 3090 at 512×512 resolution, making it second only to the lightweight CNN-based EFDM (63.3G MACs, 0.027 s) and dramatically faster than diffusion-based methods (e.g., VCT at 680.972 s) and most Transformer-based methods. The model uses 18.50M parameters, comparable to other CNN/Transformer baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SaMam outperforms all 14 baselines on every quality metric. Against the strongest CNN-based method AesPA (LPIPS 0.4050, FID 20.236), SaMam improves LPIPS by 0.0166 and FID by 2.29, while also reducing MACs from 334.3G to 77.1G. Against the best Transformer-based method S2WAT (LPIPS 0.4256, FID 23.430), SaMam achieves a 0.0372 LPIPS reduction and 5.48 FID reduction with roughly 7.5× fewer MACs (77.1G vs. 582.6G). Against the best diffusion-based method StyleID (LPIPS 0.4803, FID 24.488), SaMam improves LPIPS by 0.0919 and FID by 6.54 while being over 1400× faster in inference time (0.034 s vs. 47.746 s). Against the best Reversible-NN method CAPVST (CFSD 0.2912), SaMam achieves a lower CFSD of 0.2703, indicating better content preservation. In terms of efficiency, SaMam is second only to EFDM in both MACs and inference time, yet EFDM's quality metrics are substantially worse (FID 34.834 vs. 17.946), confirming that SaMam achieves the best quality–efficiency trade-off among all compared methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

SaMam extends the Mamba/SSM lineage (S4 → S6 → Mamba) into the style transfer domain, building directly on VMamba's SS2D block for 2D spatial scanning and Vision Mamba's multi-directional scanning strategy. The key architectural departure is the S7 block, which makes the SSM's A and D parameters style-dependent (predicted from style embeddings) rather than content-dependent or fixed, effectively turning the global convolution kernel into a style-adaptive operator. The style-aware modules (SConv, SCM, SAIN) draw on established ideas from AdaConv, CResMD, and adaptive instance normalization, repurposing them within a pluggable decoder that can adapt to arbitrary styles without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that linear-complexity SSMs can replace quadratic-complexity attention as the backbone for arbitrary style transfer while achieving both better quality (ArtFID 26.3 vs. 29.8 for the best CNN baseline) and substantially lower inference cost (0.034s vs. 0.15–0.39s), resolving a long-standing efficiency-quality trade-off in the field. The style-pluggable decoder design is practically valuable: a single trained model can transfer to unseen styles at inference time, avoiding per-style fine-tuning. Open questions include whether SSM-based ST can match the generation diversity and fidelity of diffusion models at higher resolutions, and whether the approach extends naturally to video style transfer where temporal consistency adds another dimension of long-range dependency.
