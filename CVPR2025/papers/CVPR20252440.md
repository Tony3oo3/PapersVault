## Problem

Transferring complex lighting conditions between different indoor scenes using only images remains unsolved: existing methods either require 3D reconstruction, multi-view captures, are restricted to portraits or specific object categories, or fail to handle indirect lighting phenomena such as inter-reflections, specular highlights, and cast shadows across scenes with different spatial layouts and materials.

## Input / Output

LumiNet takes two RGB images as input: a source scene image whose geometry and albedo should be preserved, and a target lighting image whose illumination characteristics should be transferred. The source is processed by a frozen latent-intrinsic encoder to extract a spatial feature map (H×W×128) and a 16-dimensional lighting code; the target contributes only its 16-dimensional lighting code. The output is a single relit RGB image of the source scene under the target's lighting, with no explicit 3D, depth, normal, or material inputs required at inference time.

## Method

LumiNet modifies the ControlNet architecture to operate entirely in latent space rather than pixel space: a Latent Intrinsic ControlNet concatenates the source's latent intrinsic features (128 channels) with the spatially expanded target lighting code (16 channels) and processes them through convolution layers to produce conditioning signals for a pretrained latent diffusion model. A lightweight MLP adaptor (3072→4096→4096→4096→3072) maps the 16-dim target lighting code into a 3072-dim embedding matching text-embedding dimensions, which is injected via cross-attention layers fine-tuned on the diffusion model. Training uses a latent diffusion denoising objective with the diffusion backbone and latent-intrinsic encoder frozen, updating only the control network and cross-attention layers. Training data is generated by a variational StyLitGAN that maps real LSUN-bedroom images into StyleGAN's latent space via a ConvNeXt encoder, producing diverse relit pairs, supplemented by MIIW and BigTime real-world datasets. Post-processing employs nearest-neighbor seed selection in latent lighting space and rectified-flow inversion to remove artifacts without introducing lighting-related changes.

## Results

On the MIIW test set, LumiNet achieves RMSE 0.180 and SSIM 0.647 on raw output, and RMSE 0.144 and SSIM 0.673 with color correction, surpassing the best baseline (RGB↔X) by over 30% on RMSE and over 35% on SSIM. In the real-world user study (Table 2), LumiNet also leads on all perceptual metrics: median surface-normal angular error of 2.74° (vs. 3.14°–3.61° for baselines), image quality score 1.71, lighting quality 1.30, and prompt alignment 1.40 (lower is better). The paper reports no post-processing (e.g., latent extrinsic search or flow-based cleanup) was applied to the MIIW quantitative results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LumiNet outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest quantitative competitor, RGB↔X [66] (RMSE 0.256, SSIM 0.476 raw), LumiNet improves RMSE by 29.7% and SSIM by 35.9%; with color correction the gap widens to 43% on RMSE (0.144 vs. 0.253) and 43% on SSIM (0.673 vs. 0.470). Latent-Intrinsic [73], the closest architectural predecessor, trails LumiNet by 39% on raw RMSE (0.297 vs. 0.180) and 37% on raw SSIM (0.473 vs. 0.647). The older deep-network baselines SA-AE [24] and S3Net [62] are substantially weaker, with RMSE values of 0.288–0.512 and SSIM of 0.300–0.484. In the real-world user study (Table 2), IC-Light-v2 [69] achieves the second-best lighting quality (L-PQ 2.57) but is outperformed by LumiNet on all four perceptual metrics (I-PQ 1.71 vs. 3.06, L-PQ 1.30 vs. 2.57, P-PQ 1.40 vs. 2.74, Median-AE 2.74° vs. 3.42°), confirming LumiNet's advantage in both geometry preservation and perceived lighting realism.

## Relation to Prior Work

LumiNet sits at the intersection of three research threads: latent intrinsic decomposition (Zhang et al., NeurIPS 2024), which shows that relighting training induces disentangled albedo-like and lighting-like codes in a pretrained network; ControlNet-style conditional diffusion (Zhang et al., ICCV 2023), which provides a flexible architecture for image-conditioned generation; and StyLitGAN (Bhattad et al., CVPR 2024), which demonstrated that StyleGAN's latent space encodes controllable lighting directions. The key architectural departure is that, unlike standard ControlNet which conditions on maps from a single scene, LumiNet's Latent Intrinsic ControlNet fuses features from two different images (source intrinsics + target extrinsics), and a learned MLP adaptor injects the target lighting code into the diffusion model's cross-attention layers. The variational StyLitGAN data pipeline is a practical extension that addresses mode collapse in GAN-based relighting data generation, enabling large-scale paired training data without expensive optimization-based inversion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Indoor scene relighting from a single image pair remains an open problem with direct applications in cinematography, architectural visualization, and mixed reality, and prior methods have been limited to portraits, objects, or outdoor scenes with simpler illumination. LumiNet demonstrates that latent representations learned purely from 2D images can capture second-order lighting phenomena (inter-reflections, specular highlights, cast shadows) without explicit 3D reconstruction or multi-view inputs, suggesting that generative priors encode richer scene understanding than previously exploited. However, the method still requires post-processing (rectified-flow inversion) for artifact-free results, struggles with small or occluded luminaires and dramatic chromatic shifts, and does not provide explicit control over light intensity. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic scenes, achieves 3D consistency across views, and can operate in real time.
