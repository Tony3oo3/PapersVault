## Problem

Existing CLIP-based cross-modal hashing methods suffer from two fundamental limitations: (1) CLIP's 77-character context window causes semantic truncation for real-world texts (e.g., MS COCO captions span 169–625 characters), and (2) feature fusion across modalities fails to selectively preserve retrieval-relevant foreground semantics while suppressing redundant or noisy background content. The paper addresses both by introducing affinity-prompted collaborative learning that dynamically weights text semantics and adaptively filters fused cross-modal features.

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 RGB images, BPE-tokenized text descriptions, and multi-label category annotations as input. Text is additionally converted into a template-based prompt ("This is an image containing {class_name}") that is embedded and combined with a learnable context vector to form a prompt feature. The output is a pair of K-bit binary hash codes (K ∈ {16, 32, 64}) in {−1,+1}^K for each modality, enabling Hamming-distance-based cross-modal retrieval ranking. The backbone is CLIP-B16 with ViT and BERT encoders, trained end-to-end with a batch size of 128.

## Method

PromptHash comprises three coupled modules. First, the Text Affinity-Aware Prompt (TAAP) module generates label-derived prompt embeddings, processes them through a Transformer layer, and fuses them with original text features via element-wise multiplication and a learnable global-average-pooling weight, effectively re-weighting text tokens to foreground retrieval-relevant semantics. Second, the Adaptive Gated State Space Fusion (AGSF) module concatenates image and prompt-enhanced text features after GRN normalization, then applies a State Space Model (Mamba-style selective scan) along three axis-flipping orientations (axis 1, axis 2, and both), combining the results with a learnable temperature-weighted mixture that adaptively filters redundant information before a final Transformer layer splits the fused representation back into visual and textual streams. Third, the Prompt Affinity Contrastive Loss (PACL) enforces alignment through four components: global InfoNCE loss between image and prompt-text features, local InfoNCE loss between image and prompt features with a JS-divergence-modulated temperature, and inter-class/intra-class affinity losses computed on continuous (pre-quantization) hash representations to preserve label-structured semantic geometry. The total objective is a weighted sum of these six loss terms, trained with a supervised multi-label protocol.

## Results

PromptHash achieves mAP@ALL of 0.9924 (I2T) and 0.9922 (T2I) on MIRFLICKR-25K, 0.9668 (I2T) and 0.9692 (T2I) on NUS-WIDE, and 0.8573 (I2T) and 0.8580 (T2I) on MS COCO, evaluated at 16/32/64-bit hash codes. The most substantial gains appear on NUS-WIDE, where PromptHash surpasses the previous best (VTPH) by 18.22% (I2T) and 18.65% (T2I). On MS COCO, the improvement over the prior best (CMCL) is 8.87% (I2T) and 8.98% (T2I), with the authors attributing the relatively smaller margin to the dataset's long-sentence annotations where only 3–5 words are retrieval-relevant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PromptHash outperforms all 11 baselines on every dataset and retrieval direction. The largest margins are on NUS-WIDE, where it beats the previous best VTPH by 18.22% (I2T) and 18.65% (T2I) in mAP@ALL, and on MIRFLICKR-25K by 7.13% (I2T) and 7.47% (T2I) over VTPH. On MS COCO, the closest competitor is CMCL (0.7686 I2T / 0.7682 T2I), which PromptHash surpasses by 8.87% and 8.98% respectively; notably, VTPH underperforms CMCL on MS COCO (0.7488/0.7481), suggesting that long-sentence annotations challenge prompt-based methods differently. CNN-based baselines (DCMH, CMHH, GCDH) trail CLIP-based methods by 10–20+ mAP points, confirming the advantage of CLIP pre-training. Among CLIP-based methods, the gap between PromptHash and the runner-up is consistent (7–9 mAP points), indicating that the gains stem from the proposed TAAP, AGSF, and PACL modules rather than backbone choice alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three active research threads: CLIP-based cross-modal hashing (DCHMT, MITH, VTPH), prompt learning for vision-language models (CoCoOp, MaPLe), and State Space Models for selective sequence processing (Mamba, VMamba). It departs from prior CLIP-based hashing methods by explicitly addressing the 77-token context limitation through label-derived affinity prompts with dynamic weighting, and introduces SSM's selective scan mechanism into the feature fusion pipeline—a combination not previously explored in cross-modal hashing. The contrastive alignment strategy (PACL) extends standard InfoNCE with a Jensen-Shannon-divergence-modulated temperature and intra/inter-class affinity terms, building on the contrastive hashing lineage (UCCH, CMCL) while adding prompt-level granularity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that CLIP's 77-token truncation is a meaningful bottleneck in cross-modal hashing and that lightweight prompt-based text augmentation can recover substantial performance without the computational cost of LLM-based text reconstruction (as in VTPH). However, the modest gains on MS COCO (where text is natural language rather than label concatenation) reveal that the prompt mechanism is most effective for discrete, label-derived text, leaving open the question of how to handle genuinely long, noisy natural-language queries. The finding that reducing quantization loss weight improves performance suggests CLIP's pre-trained alignment already provides strong hash-space structure, which may generalize to other CLIP-based retrieval settings.
