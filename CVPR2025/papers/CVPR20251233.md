## Problem

Existing V2X cooperative perception datasets lack 4D radar data, and cooperative LiDAR-4D radar fusion for 3D object detection remains entirely unexplored. Moreover, LiDAR point clouds degrade severely in adverse weather (fog, snow), and the resulting complex, variable noise distribution in multi-agent features is difficult to model with conventional denoising approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251233/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes multi-agent LiDAR point clouds (64-channel, 120 m range) and 4D radar point clouds (150 m range, 120° horizontal / 30° vertical FOV) from up to five agents (ego vehicle, connected automated vehicles, and infrastructure), producing 3D vehicle bounding boxes. Because 4D radar provides only front-view data, all evaluation is restricted to the ego vehicle's camera FOV (x ∈ [0, 140] m, y ∈ [−40, 40] m). The V2X-R dataset comprises 12,079 scenarios, 37,727 LiDAR/4D-radar frames, 150,908 images, and 170,859 annotated 3D boxes.

## Method

The paper proposes a four-stage cooperative fusion pipeline (per-agent encoding → agent fusion → modal fusion → box prediction) instantiated with two strategies: SA2MA (extending single-agent LiDAR-4D radar fusion to multi-agent via self-attention) and SM2MM (extending multi-agent LiDAR methods to multi-modal via BEV feature concatenation). The key architectural contribution is the Multi-modal Denoising Diffusion (MDD) module inserted at the modal-fusion stage: a forward diffusion process reparameterizes the complex weather-noise distribution into a tractable Gaussian, then a U-Net denoiser iteratively reconstructs clean LiDAR features over T steps, conditioned on the weather-robust 4D radar feature at each step. Training uses an epoch-varying loss weight γ(e,ψ) = (1 − tanh(e/τ − φ))·ψ that nonlinearly decays, shifting emphasis from denoising to detection as training progresses, and the total objective combines classification, localization, and MDD losses.

## Results

On the V2X-R dataset, the best cooperative LiDAR-4D radar fusion method (CoBEVT with SM2MM strategy) achieves 94.23/93.50/86.92 3D mAP@Testing (IoU=0.3/0.5/0.7), outperforming the best LiDAR-only method (AdaFusion: 92.72/91.64/84.81) and the best 4D radar-only method (CoBEVT†: 85.74/80.64/54.34). The proposed MDD module further improves weather-robustness: AttFuse with MDD gains +5.73% 3D mAP@fog (IoU=0.7) and L4DR with MDD gains +6.70% 3D mAP@snow (IoU=0.7), while barely affecting normal-weather performance (≤0.22% drop). On the real-world K-Radar dataset, MDD yields 5.20% and 5.97% 3D mAP gains for Sedan and Bus under adverse weather.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks 12 cooperative perception methods across three modalities (LiDAR-only, 4D radar-only, LiDAR+4D radar fusion). In the fusion setting (Table 4), SM2MM-based methods consistently outperform SA2MA-based ones: CoBEVT SM2MM (94.23/93.50/86.92 test) beats L4DR SA2MA (90.01/88.85/82.26) by 4.22/4.65/4.66 points, and AdaFusion SM2MM (92.94/91.97/85.31) beats InterFusion SA2MA (87.91/86.51/69.63) by 5.03/5.46/15.68 points. All fusion methods surpass their LiDAR-only counterparts (Table 2) and 4D radar-only counterparts (Table 3); for example, CoBEVT fusion gains +2.82/+3.06/+5.86 over CoBEVT LiDAR-only at IoU=0.3/0.5/0.7. The MDD module (Table 5) improves AttFuse fusion by +4.15/+3.82/+3.15 (snow) and +2.37/+3.26/+5.73 (fog) over the same model without MDD, while L4DR+MDD gains +4.01/+4.65/+6.70 (snow) and +1.00/+1.51/+2.18 (fog). On the real-world K-Radar dataset (Table 6), AttFuse†+MDD outperforms AttFuse† by +4.99% (Sedan) and +3.63% (Bus) overall 3D mAP, confirming MDD's transferability beyond simulation. Notably, the newest method SICP [33] underperforms earlier baselines (e.g., CoBEVT, AdaFusion) in both LiDAR-only and fusion settings, suggesting that emphasis on individual perception capability comes at a cost to cooperative performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends two established research threads: (1) cooperative V2X 3D object detection (AttFuse, V2XViT, CoBEVT, etc.), which has so far been limited to LiDAR or LiDAR-camera modalities, and (2) single-agent LiDAR-4D radar fusion (L4DR, InterFusion), which addresses weather robustness but ignores multi-agent collaboration. The MDD module repurposes the DDPM forward-reverse diffusion framework—originally designed for image generation—as a feature-space denoising mechanism conditioned on weather-robust 4D radar features, a novel application of diffusion models to sensor fusion. The paper explicitly positions cooperative LiDAR-4D radar fusion as unexplored, carving out a new intersection of these two literatures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

V2X-R fills a critical data gap: no prior cooperative perception dataset included 4D radar, making weather-robust multi-agent fusion impossible to study systematically. The comprehensive benchmark (10+ methods across three modality configurations) gives the field a common reference point for cooperative LiDAR-4D radar fusion. Key limitations include reliance on simulated weather (fog/snow only), a single object class (vehicles), and evaluation restricted to the ego vehicle's front FOV. Open questions the authors highlight include how to maximize the complementary value of cooperative 4D radar across diverse architectures, and whether the MDD module generalizes to real-world multi-agent deployments beyond the single-agent K-Radar validation.
