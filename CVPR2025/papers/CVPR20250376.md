## Problem

Standard MOT data association relies on the Hungarian algorithm's global cost minimization, which treats tracks and detections as independent sets and overlooks that each track has a specific correct match among detections. Additionally, multi-stage cascade association restricts low-confidence and NMS-discarded detections to only unmatched tracks, suboptimally utilizing detection candidates that may represent occluded objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250376/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tracker consumes RGB video frames processed by a YOLOX-x detector (NMS at IoU 0.80) and SBS50 Re-ID features, with motion prediction via an NSA Kalman Filter. Unusually, it constructs an expanded detection set by recovering high-confidence boxes deleted during NMS (re-run at IoU 0.95) and including low-confidence detections, applying penalty terms to each category. Output is per-frame tracked bounding boxes with persistent identity labels, with a separate association pass for tracks younger than three frames to prevent short-lived false positives from disrupting confirmed tracks.

## Method

TrackTrack replaces the Hungarian algorithm with Track-Perspective-Based Association (TPA), which iteratively identifies mutual-minimum pairs—where a track has the lowest cost to a detection AND that detection has the lowest cost to that track—then removes them and repeats with a gradually tightening threshold, all within a single joint association stage over high-confidence, low-confidence, and NMS-discarded detections simultaneously. The distance metric combines HMIoU, Re-ID cosine similarity, detection confidence, and angular distance, with additive penalties for lower-confidence categories. Track-Aware Initialization (TAI) sets matched track positions as undeletable anchors (confidence = 1) and applies NMS over these anchors plus unmatched high-confidence detections, so only boxes that do not significantly overlap existing tracks or more confident candidates are initialized as new tracks, suppressing spurious track creation.

## Results

On the MOT17 test set, TrackTrack achieves a HOTA of 67.1, IDF1 of 83.1, MOTA of 81.8, and AssA of 68.2, surpassing all online-based trackers and matching the offline-based CoNo-Link (67.1 HOTA). On MOT20, it reaches 65.7 HOTA and 80.9 IDF1, outperforming all online methods (best prior: AdapTrack at 65.0 HOTA). On DanceTrack, TrackTrack achieves 66.5 HOTA, 67.8 IDF1, 93.6 MOTA, and 52.9 AssA, exceeding all both online and offline baselines. The tracker also runs at 161.52 FPS on MOT17 and 408.37 FPS on DanceTrack, a 3.9% and 3.4% speedup over the Hungarian-based multi-stage baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MOT17, TrackTrack (67.1 HOTA) outperforms the strongest online baselines ImprAsso (66.4) and PIA (66.0) by 0.7 and 1.1 HOTA points respectively, and matches the offline CoNo-Link (67.1) while exceeding SUSHI (66.5). On MOT20, TrackTrack (65.7 HOTA) leads all online trackers, beating AdapTrack (65.0) and CMTrack (64.8) by 0.7 and 0.9 points, though it falls slightly short of the offline CoNo-Link (65.9). On DanceTrack, TrackTrack (66.5 HOTA) surpasses the best online method Hybrid-SORT-ReID (65.7) by 0.8 points and the offline CoNo-Link (63.8) by 2.7 points, demonstrating particular strength in scenarios with uniform appearance and nonlinear motion. The AssA metric consistently shows the largest gains (e.g., 68.2 vs. 66.6 for ImprAsso on MOT17; 52.9 vs. 51.3 for UCMCTrack+ on DanceTrack), confirming that the TPA association strategy is the primary driver of improvement. Ablation studies further show that replacing the Hungarian algorithm with the proposed iterative assignment yields +1.0 to +1.7 HOTA, and using deleted NMS detections adds another +0.5 to +1.2 HOTA.

## Relation to Prior Work

TrackTrack sits squarely within the tracking-by-detection (TBD) paradigm and builds on the Kalman-filter-based motion model pipeline established by SORT, DeepSORT, ByteTrack, and OC-SORT. Its primary departure is from the standard Hungarian-algorithm global cost minimization for data association, which the authors argue is ill-suited to MOT because tracks and detections are interdependent representations of the same objects rather than independent sets. The distance metric (HMIoU, cosine, confidence, angular) follows Hybrid-SORT-ReID [56], and the multi-stage cascade association of ByteTrack/OC-SORT is replaced by a single joint association stage that also incorporates NMS-deleted high-confidence detections—a class of candidates largely ignored by prior trackers. The TAI component extends conventional threshold-based track initialization by treating matched track positions as undeletable NMS anchors, a straightforward but effective use of existing track state.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a long-standing assumption in MOT that data association should be framed as a global optimization problem, showing that a simple iterative greedy scheme prioritizing local track-perspective matching can outperform the Hungarian algorithm while also running faster. This reframing could influence how future trackers design their association stages, particularly in occlusion-heavy or dense-crowd scenarios where global cost minimization tends to sacrifice individual track accuracy. Limitations include continued dependence on detector quality (YOLOX-x) and the fact that the iterative greedy approach lacks the optimality guarantees of the Hungarian algorithm, raising open questions about worst-case behavior. The method is also specific to the TBD framework and has not been tested on detection-free or learned-association paradigms.
