## Problem

Existing 3D Visual Grounding methods rely on expensive sensors (LiDAR, binocular/industrial cameras) and are limited to single-object localization, while real-world descriptions are often ambiguous and refer to multiple objects simultaneously. No large-scale benchmark exists for multi-object 3DVG from monocular RGB images, leaving a foundational gap for practical, cost-effective 3D scene understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252191/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular RGB image (outdoor driving scenes from Rope3D, with objects up to 202 m away) and a concise natural language description (average 16.95 words) that may ambiguously refer to one or many objects. It outputs 3D bounding boxes for all objects matching the description, along with 2D bounding boxes and a per-object match probability. Evaluation is conducted in two modes: with ground-truth bounding boxes (isolating the matching stage) and with predicted bounding boxes from the MonoUNI detector (end-to-end).

## Method

CyclopsNet follows a two-stage pipeline inspired by Faster R-CNN: a MonoUNI 3D detector first localizes all objects, then a matching module aligns each detected object with the query text. The State-Prompt Visual Encoder (SPVE) fuses 2D ViT patch features with a KAN-encoded 3D state vector (orientation, dimensions, position) injected as a per-patch prompt, producing geometry-aware visual embeddings. The Denoising Alignment Fusion (DAF) module maps unimodal and joint embeddings into Gaussian latent distributions via a reparameterization trick, applies ALBEF-style contrastive alignment with hard-negative mining, and fuses modalities through a learnable-query Multi-Head Cross-Attention followed by a hierarchical KAN encoder. Training combines a bidirectional hinge triplet contrastive loss, pairwise KL-divergence losses that force the visual, textual, and joint Gaussian distributions to converge (reducing uncertainty-driven noise), and a binary cross-entropy object-text matching loss.

## Results

On the MonoMulti3D-ROPE test set with GT bounding boxes, CyclopsNet achieves an F1 of 69.09, Precision of 54.46, and Recall of 94.47, outperforming the second-best method (M3DRef-CLIP) by +5.39 F1, +4.64 Precision, and +6.17 Recall. With predicted bounding boxes (end-to-end setting), CyclopsNet still leads with F1=60.16, Precision=51.98, and Recall=71.40, gaining +5.34 F1 and +8.85 Recall over the next-best baseline. The ablation study confirms that the State-Prompt branch contributes the largest single gain (+12.9 F1 over the baseline), followed by QGJE (+3.49 F1) and VTC (+1.5 F1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CyclopsNet outperforms all six baselines on every metric in both GT and Pred bounding box settings. The closest competitor is M3DRef-CLIP [49] (F1=63.70 GT / 54.82 Pred), which is the only baseline designed for multi-object grounding; CyclopsNet surpasses it by 5.39 F1 (GT) and 5.34 F1 (Pred). Among point-cloud-based methods, 3DJCG [8] (F1=63.41) and 3DVG-Trans [50] (F1=61.71) rank second and third respectively, but both rely on LiDAR point clouds rather than monocular RGB. The single-object baselines—ScanRefer [9] (F1=56.67), ReferIt3D [1] (F1=58.01), and InstanceRefer [46] (F1=58.68)—lag further behind, reflecting the difficulty of adapting single-target architectures to multi-object matching. Notably, CyclopsNet's Recall advantage is most pronounced (94.47 vs. 88.30 for M3DRef-CLIP under GT bboxes), indicating the State-Prompt and DAF modules substantially reduce missed detections in the matching stage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Visual Grounding line of work from LiDAR/point-cloud-based methods (ScanRefer, ReferIt3D, 3DVG-Transformer) to the monocular RGB setting, and further generalizes from single-object to multi-object grounding. Architecturally, it draws on the two-stage detection-then-matching paradigm of Faster R-CNN, adopts ALBEF's contrastive alignment-before-fusion strategy for the QGJE module, and introduces Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks as a high-dimensional encoding backbone in place of standard MLPs. The State-Prompt Visual Encoder is a novel contribution that fuses 2D ViT patch features with 3D geometric state vectors (orientation, dimensions, position) to produce spatially-aware visual embeddings, addressing a gap left by prior monocular 3DVG methods like Mono3DRefer that rely solely on 2D appearance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MonoMulti3D-ROPE dataset (133K expressions, 581K objects, 202m range) fills a foundational gap by being the first large-scale benchmark supporting multi-object 3DVG from monocular RGB, making the task accessible without expensive LiDAR or industrial cameras. The semantic ambiguity inherent in multi-object descriptions (one description referring to multiple objects) better mirrors real-world language use and challenges existing single-object methods. Limitations include evaluation solely on the authors' own dataset, dependence on MonoUNI's detection quality in the Pred-bbox setting (where F1 drops from 69.09 to 60.16), and the probabilistic denoising approach (KL divergence convergence of Gaussian posteriors) remaining untested on other 3DVG benchmarks. Open questions include whether the State-Prompt encoding generalizes to indoor scenes or different sensor configurations, and whether the KAN-based architecture offers advantages over standard MLPs at scale.
