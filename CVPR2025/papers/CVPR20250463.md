## Problem

Existing object placement methods face a fundamental tension: generative models (GANs) avoid dense supervision but cannot model complex data distributions, while transformer-based approaches with sparse contrastive loss over-relax regularization and produce imprecise placements. Manually annotating positive/negative composite pairs (e.g., GracoNet) is labor-intensive and does not scale. The paper asks whether object placement can be reformulated as a detection problem to inherit the precise localization capabilities of modern detectors.

## Input / Output

The method takes an object-subtracted background image (produced by instance-segmenting, inpainting, and Gaussian-smoothing the source), a set of object patches to be composited, and the bounding-box locations of remaining scene objects (encoded via an MLP location encoder to prevent overlap). It outputs N detected regions of interest as bounding boxes with class scores, plus a differentiable association that assigns each object query to its best-matched region via a softmax over negative cosine-similarity scores, yielding a final placement bounding box per object.

## Method

BOOTPLACE formulates placement as a two-stage placement-by-detection pipeline. Stage 1 trains a DETR-based detection transformer on object-subtracted images to predict a fixed set of regions of interest (bounding boxes + class labels), with scene-object locations concatenated into image features to constrain the detection space. Stage 2 adds an object-to-region association network that scores each object query against each detected region using *negative* cosine similarity (semantic complementarity rather than similarity), trained with a log-likelihood loss over the Hungarian-matched ground-truth assignment. A bootstrapped training strategy randomly re-composites subsets of removed objects back into the background, expanding each scene into Σ C(T,i) training samples and providing multi-object supervision without manual annotation. The full loss combines standard DETR classification and box-regression losses with the association loss, all trained jointly end-to-end.

## Results

On Cityscapes object reposition, BOOTPLACE achieves IOU@1 of 0.197 and IOU@5 of 0.281, substantially outperforming the previous state-of-the-art TopNet (IOU@1=0.045, IOU@5=0.070) by over 4× on top-1 IOU. On the OPA dataset, BOOTPLACE matches TopNet on IOU@1 (0.197) but improves IOU@5 from 0.241 to 0.281 and IOU50@5 from 15.95% to 22.41%. In the plausibility user study for car placement, BOOTPLACE scores 0.303 on Cityscapes and 0.323 on Mapillary Vistas, the highest among all compared methods, and also achieves the greatest placement diversity in scale (0.310) and y-center (0.495).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BOOTPLACE decisively outperforms PlaceNet on both Cityscapes and OPA across all IOU metrics, with PlaceNet scoring near-zero IOU50@1 on Cityscapes. Against SAC-GAN, BOOTPLACE more than doubles the top-1 IOU on Cityscapes (0.197 vs. 0.082) and more than triples top-5 IOU (0.281 vs. 0.085). Compared to the strongest baseline TopNet, BOOTPLACE achieves a 4.4× improvement in IOU@1 on Cityscapes (0.197 vs. 0.045) and a 4× improvement in IOU@5 (0.281 vs. 0.070); on OPA, the two methods tie on IOU@1 (both 0.197) but BOOTPLACE gains 0.040 in IOU@5 and 6.46 points in IOU50@5. Against GracoNet (evaluated only on OPA), BOOTPLACE improves IOU@5 from 0.248 to 0.281 and IOU50@5 from 16.60% to 22.41%. In the plausibility user study for car placement, BOOTPLACE achieves the highest scores on both Cityscapes (0.303) and Mapillary Vistas (0.323), surpassing TopNet (0.246/0.260) and SAC-GAN (0.269/0.285), while also producing the most diverse placements in scale and vertical position.

## Relation to Prior Work

BOOTPLACE departs from the dominant GAN-based and contrastive-loss paradigms for object placement (ST-GAN, PlaceNet, TopNet, GracoNet) by reframing the problem as a detection task: a DETR-style transformer identifies candidate regions of interest in object-subtracted backgrounds, and a separate association network matches target objects to those regions. The detection backbone follows the DETR lineage (set-to-set prediction, Hungarian matching), while the association module introduces a negative-correlation scoring mechanism to enforce semantic complementarity rather than similarity. The bootstrapped training strategy—randomly re-compositing subsets of subtracted objects to generate diverse training pairs—is a novel data-augmentation idea not present in prior placement work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The placement-by-detection formulation offers a principled way to address the sparse-supervision bottleneck that has limited prior methods, and the bootstrapped augmentation (expanding one scene into Σ C(T,i) training samples) is a practical recipe that could transfer to other composition or augmentation pipelines. However, the parallel detection design inherently cannot model sequential occlusion constraints (e.g., two cars cannot occupy the same lane), and the lack of out-of-plane rotation or perspective modeling limits compositional realism. Open questions include whether autoregressive or diffusion-based extensions can resolve the sequential-placement limitation while retaining the detection-based localization accuracy.
