## Problem

Current SOTA video generation models based on MM-DiT architectures (e.g., CogVideoX, Mochi1) are trained exclusively on single prompts and produce isolated, incoherent sequences when fed multiple sequential prompts. Prior multi-prompt video generation methods were designed for UNet architectures, face strict training data requirements, and suffer from weak prompt following and unnatural transitions. No prior work has explored attention control mechanisms within the MM-DiT architecture for this task.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of n text prompts (e.g., 2–3 prompts describing different scenes or events) and a frozen pre-trained MM-DiT video model (CogVideoX-2B) as input, producing a single longer video (49 frames per segment at 480×720, 50 sampling steps) with smooth inter-prompt transitions. No additional training, fine-tuning, or optimization is required; the method operates entirely at inference time by modifying attention computations and latent blending during the denoising process.

## Method

DiTCtrl reframes multi-prompt video generation as temporal video editing with smooth transitions, operating entirely within the pre-trained MM-DiT's 3D full attention. The key insight is that MM-DiT's unified 3D full attention decomposes into four regions (video-to-video, text-to-text, text-to-video, video-to-text) that behave analogously to the separate cross- and self-attention blocks in UNet-based models, enabling mask-guided semantic control. Specifically, a mask-guided KV-sharing mechanism extracts binary object masks from the text-video/video-text attention regions, then restricts the current prompt's queries to attend only to the corresponding object region in the previous prompt's keys and values, preserving identity consistency of key objects across segments. A position-dependent latent blending strategy (symmetric trapezoidal weighting over overlapping frames) is applied simultaneously at each denoising step to ensure smooth visual transitions between adjacent prompt segments. Both mechanisms are applied sequentially over segment pairs within a single batch, requiring no architectural modification or additional training.

## Results

On MPVBench, DiTCtrl achieves the highest CSCV score of 84.90% (vs. 84.37% for FreeNoise, 78.74% for FreeNoise+DiT, 74.97% for Video-Infinity, and 67.28% for Gen-L-Video), demonstrating superior transition smoothness. It also attains the best motion smoothness at 97.80%. In the user study (28 raters, 16 videos), DiTCtrl leads on all four criteria: overall preference (4.11/5), motion pattern (4.17/5), temporal consistency (4.26/5), and text alignment (3.91/5), outperforming the strongest baseline FreeNoise+DiT (3.81/5 overall).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiTCtrl outperforms all four baselines on the proposed CSCV metric (84.90% vs. 84.37% FreeNoise, 78.74% FreeNoise+DiT, 74.97% Video-Infinity, 67.28% Gen-L-Video) and on motion smoothness (97.80%), confirming that the KV-sharing + latent blending design yields smoother semantic transitions and more physically plausible motion. However, DiTCtrl trails FreeNoise (32.69%) and Video-Infinity (32.35%) on CLIP text-image similarity (30.68%), which the authors attribute to the KV-sharing mechanism inheriting semantic content from preceding segments — a deliberate trade-off for temporal coherence. In the human evaluation, DiTCtrl wins decisively on all four axes, with the largest gap over FreeNoise+DiT in temporal consistency (4.26 vs. 3.75) and overall preference (4.11 vs. 3.81). Gen-L-Video and Video-Infinity, both built on the UNet-based VideoCrafter2 backbone, show the weakest overall scores (1.15 and 2.90 in user study), highlighting the advantage of the MM-DiT architecture for complex multi-event motion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of attention-control-based video editing (MasaCtrl, Prompt-to-Prompt, Video-P2P) and latent-blending long-video generation (MultiDiffusion, FreeNoise) from UNet architectures to the Multi-Modal Diffusion Transformer (MM-DiT) paradigm used by CogVideoX, FLUX, and Mochi1. The key conceptual departure is the first systematic analysis showing that MM-DiT's 3D full attention decomposes into regions (text-to-video, video-to-text, text-to-text, video-to-video) that replicate the functional roles of separate cross- and self-attention blocks in UNet, thereby enabling mask-guided KV-sharing and latent blending to be transplanted into the unified-attention setting. Prior multi-prompt video generation works (TALC, MTVG, Gen-L-Video) were either UNet-based or required additional training; DiTCtrl is the first training-free approach under MM-DiT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MM-DiT attention decomposition analysis is broadly useful: it provides a principled bridge between the well-studied UNet attention-control literature and the newer transformer-based video models that are becoming the dominant architecture. MPVBench fills a genuine evaluation gap for multi-prompt generation with its CSCV metric, which captures transition smoothness that standard CLIP similarity misses. However, the method is tied to CogVideoX-2B and the authors acknowledge attribute-binding errors across segments and the computational overhead of DiT inference as open challenges; whether the KV-sharing mechanism generalizes cleanly to larger or differently-structured MM-DiT variants (e.g., FLUX-scale models) remains untested.
