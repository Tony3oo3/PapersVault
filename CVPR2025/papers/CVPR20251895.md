## Problem

Existing anti-customization (AC) methods for protecting facial images against unauthorized Latent Diffusion Model fine-tuning (e.g., DreamBooth, LoRA) ignore the hierarchical structure of customization: they neither disrupt the global spatial correlations of perceptual features that enable concept transfer to new scenarios, nor precisely target fine-grained local facial attributes that enable semantic theft. This results in weak resistance to both identity transfer and detailed facial reproduction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251895/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of facial images (15 per individual from CelebA-HQ or VGGFace2) and produces adversarially perturbed versions under an L∞ budget of 16/255. A text inversion module generates a semantic prompt from the top-15 highest-confidence attributes of each image, which guides the local distortion component. The perturbed images are then used as training data for DreamBooth or LoRA fine-tuning on Stable Diffusion v2.1, and protection is evaluated by measuring identity similarity (ISM), face detection failure rate (FDFR), and image quality metrics on generated outputs.

## Method

GoodAC generates adversarial perturbations through two collaboratively optimized components. For global concept-transfer resistance, it extracts feature maps from the first down-sampling and last up-sampling blocks of the U-Net, applies a block angle transformation (spatial rearrangement followed by rotation), and minimizes the L2 difference between transformed and original features to disrupt spatial correlations. For local semantic-theft resistance, it uses a text inversion prompt to guide feature extraction from the last up-sampling block, applies edge detection to both clean and adversarial feature maps, and minimizes the L2 edge-map difference to concentrate perturbations on identity-critical facial structures. The combined loss (global perceptual loss + β·edge loss) is jointly optimized with SimAC's timestep-aware feature loss and the LDM denoising loss via PGD sign-gradient updates over 6 iterations per step.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ, GoodAC reduces Identity Score Matching (ISM) to 0.03 (90.28% reduction) and achieves 96.67% Face Detection Failure Rate (FDFR) under the standard prompt, and further drops ISM to 0.02 with 96.67% FDFR under the concept-transfer prompt (Eiffel Tower). On VGGFace2, ISM is reduced to 0.03 (90.32% reduction) with 93.33% FDFR on the standard prompt, and to 0.05 (54.54% reduction) with 76.66% FDFR on the concept-transfer prompt. The method consistently outperforms all baselines by over 50% on ISM across both datasets and all four evaluated prompts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GoodAC outperforms all three baselines—AdvDM, Anti-Dreambooth (Anti-DB), and SimAC—on every metric across both datasets and all four prompts. The most striking gap is on ISM: under the standard prompt, GoodAC achieves 0.03 on CelebA-HQ versus 0.28–0.32 for the baselines, a reduction of over 90% relative to SimAC (0.31). On the concept-transfer prompt (Eiffel Tower), where baselines degrade most, GoodAC still reaches ISM 0.02 and FDFR 96.67% on CelebA-HQ, compared to SimAC's 0.04 and 71.87%, demonstrating a 71.82% relative FDFR improvement. On VGGFace2, the advantage is similarly large: GoodAC cuts ISM from 0.09 (SimAC) to 0.05 on the Eiffel Tower prompt and lifts FDFR from 60.34% to 76.66%. SimAC is consistently the strongest baseline, but GoodAC surpasses it by 50–90% on ISM and by 10–35 percentage points on FDFR depending on the prompt and dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the anti-customization (AC) lineage that began with Glaze (style-based protection) and progressed through AdvDM, Anti-DreamBooth (PGD-based latent-space attacks), and SimAC (timestep-aware perturbation selection). It directly builds on SimAC by incorporating its feature loss and timestep/layer selection strategy into the optimization loop, but departs from all prior AC methods by introducing a hierarchical global-local attack: a block-angle transformation that disrupts spatial correlations in perceptual feature maps (global level) combined with text-inversion-guided edge detection targeting fine-grained facial attributes (local level). The key conceptual departure is treating concept transfer resistance and semantic theft resistance as distinct, complementary objectives rather than a single undifferentiated perturbation task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The >50% ISM reduction over the strongest baseline (SimAC) across multiple prompts and two datasets demonstrates that hierarchical feature disruption is substantially more effective than undifferentiated perturbation for protecting identity in LDM customization. However, the evaluation is confined to open-source Stable Diffusion fine-tuning (DreamBooth/LoRA) on facial images, leaving open whether the approach transfers to proprietary models, non-fine-tuning personalization (e.g., IP-Adapter, reference-only methods), or non-facial content. The narrow hyperparameter sensitivity (β optimal at 1.625 with rapid degradation) and the fact that the method is inherently face-specific limit its immediate generalizability, and the authors themselves note vulnerability to specially designed customization techniques as a critical open direction.
