## Problem

Existing 3D reconstruction methods handle either sparse viewpoints or low-resolution inputs in isolation, but real-world scenarios (robotics, internet-collected imagery) often suffer from both simultaneously. This hybrid deficiency is particularly challenging because super-resolution requires dense supervision to recover fine details, while sparse-view regularization tends to smooth rather than introduce detail, making naive combinations of existing techniques inherently incompatible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252623/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of low-resolution RGB images (8 views at 200×200 for Blender, 3 views at 252×189 for LLFF, 24 views at ~1K for Mip-NeRF360) along with monocular depth maps estimated by a pre-trained DPT model. It produces a high-resolution 3D Gaussian representation capable of rendering novel views at 4× the input resolution (e.g., 800×800 from 200×200). A key design choice is that pseudo-views rendered from the intermediate low-resolution Gaussians are also super-resolved and used as additional training supervision, effectively converting the sparse-view problem into a denser optimization problem.

## Method

S2Gaussian operates in two stages. In Stage 1, a low-resolution Gaussian representation is optimized with depth regularization (Pearson correlation loss), then densified via a training-free Gaussian Shuffle Split that replaces each large Gaussian with six smaller sub-Gaussians shifted along its three principal axes (scaled 1/4 along the offset axis and 1/1.9 along the others), providing compact primitives for fine-grained detail. In Stage 2, the high-resolution Gaussians are refined using super-resolved images (via a pre-trained ResShift diffusion model) from both the original sparse views and pseudo-views rendered by the LR Gaussians. Two key mechanisms address the resulting challenges: a blur-free inconsistency modeling scheme that combines a learnable residual module to absorb multi-view SR inconsistencies with a per-pixel blur kernel predictor to prevent the IM module from erasing sharp textures, and a 3D robust optimization strategy that stores a per-Gaussian gradient flag and attenuates updates (by factor ε=0.1) when the current gradient direction contradicts the stored trend, thereby suppressing erroneous updates from imperfect pseudo-view supervision.

## Results

S2Gaussian achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three benchmarks on the 4× sparse-view super-resolution task. On Mip-NeRF360 (24 views), it reaches 22.05 PSNR / 0.687 SSIM / 0.296 LPIPS / 43.51 FID, surpassing the strongest baseline FSGS+SRGS by 2.22 dB in PSNR and 40.61 in FID. On LLFF (3 views), S2Gaussian attains 20.45 PSNR and 0.654 SSIM, outperforming the next-best FSGS+SRGS (19.49 PSNR, 0.590 SSIM) by a clear margin. On Blender (8 views), it scores 24.19 PSNR and 0.089 LPIPS, leading all competitors by at least 1.5 dB in PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S2Gaussian outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across all three datasets. The closest competitor is the combinatorial FSGS+SRGS, which S2Gaussian surpasses by 1.51 dB PSNR on Blender, 0.96 dB on LLFF, and 2.22 dB on Mip-NeRF360, with FID improvements of 6.95, 16.74, and 40.61 respectively. Among individual methods, SRGS and Mip-Splatting lead on Blender (22.64 and 22.78 PSNR) but still trail S2Gaussian by over 1.4 dB. On the extremely sparse LLFF setting (only 3 views), FSGS achieves the best single-method PSNR (18.35) yet S2Gaussian extends this to 20.45, a 2.1 dB gain. On the large-scale Mip-NeRF360 scenes, the gap widens dramatically: S2Gaussian's 22.05 PSNR and 0.687 SSIM far exceed the next-best FSGS+SRGS (19.83 PSNR, 0.513 SSIM), indicating that the two-stage design and 3D robust optimization are particularly effective for complex real-world geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

S2Gaussian builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting as its scene representation and on depth-regularized sparse-view methods (FSGS, DNGaussian) for the low-resolution initialization stage. It departs from prior super-resolution novel view synthesis work (SRGS, GaussianSR, NeRF-SR) by treating sparse views and low resolution as a single joint problem rather than addressing them in isolation, and introduces a two-stage pipeline where a pre-trained 2D diffusion-based SR model (ResShift) provides supervision for both original and pseudo-views. The Gaussian Shuffle Split densification and the 3D robust optimization with per-Gaussian gradient flags are novel mechanisms not present in any prior 3DGS variant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The combined sparse-and-low-resolution setting is arguably the most common degradation pattern in real-world capture (robotics, web imagery, mobile devices), making this a practically important gap to close. The two-stage LR→HR design with pseudo-view supervision is a reusable architectural pattern that could transfer to other 3DGS-based tasks. Limitations include reliance on a fixed 4× SR backbone (ResShift), dependence on SfM for camera initialization, and evaluation limited to static scenes; open questions include scalability to higher SR factors, dynamic scenes, and whether the 3D robust optimization generalizes beyond the specific cosine-similarity gating used here.
