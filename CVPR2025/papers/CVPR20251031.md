## Problem

Existing driving VQA benchmarks use heterogeneous, domain-specific prompting conventions (pixel-coordinate tuples, camera-ID triples) that are incommensurable across works and unfamiliar to general-purpose VLMs pre-trained on web data, making it impossible to attribute poor performance to genuine scene-understanding deficits versus miscommunication. Moreover, prior evaluations are predominantly open-loop VQA, leaving the interactive, sequential decision-making aspect of embodied agents untested, and safety-critical scenarios are absent due to the cost of collecting dangerous real-world data.

## Input / Output

For open-loop VQA, the model receives a single RGB frame (real nuScenes/Waymo or MetaDrive-simulated) with Set-of-Mark annotations—numbered 2D bounding boxes around traffic objects—plus a multiple-choice question referencing objects by their numerical labels; the model outputs a single capitalized letter. For closed-loop driving, the VLM receives a first-person view image with SoM marks every 0.5 s along with a text prompt specifying destination, current speed, and allowed actions, and outputs a discrete action (steering + acceleration pair) fed to the MetaDrive simulator. During training, an additional 'explanation' field is appended to ground-truth answers to encourage captioning of scene dynamics.

## Method

MetaVQA is constructed via a three-stage pipeline: (1) scene graphs are extracted from 400 nuScenes scenarios and 6,900 Waymo (WOMD) scenarios, with WOMD traffic reconstructed in the MetaDrive simulator for scalable RGB rendering; (2) objects are annotated with Set-of-Mark prompting (2D bounding boxes projected from 3D boxes for real images, shader-based instance segmentation for simulated images); (3) 4.3M multiple-choice QA pairs are generated programmatically by applying 30 question-type-dependent queries to the scene graphs, covering spatial awareness, embodied understanding, and grounding. The benchmark supports both zero-shot evaluation of general-purpose VLMs and supervised fine-tuning, with a 150K-question training split and a 9,725-question held-out test set. Closed-loop evaluation deploys fine-tuned VLMs as driving agents in MetaDrive, augmented with CAT-generated adversarial safety-critical scenarios, measuring route completion, collision rate, off-road rate, and displacement errors. A key finding is that training on simulated observations alone transfers substantially to real-world VQA, validating the utility of simulator-conditioned synthetic data for embodied scene understanding.

## Results

On the MetaVQA withheld test set (9,725 multiple-choice questions), fine-tuning consistently improves VQA accuracy across all evaluated models: InternVL2-8B rises from 0.592 to 0.869, Qwen2 from 0.539 to 0.844, and Llama3.2 from 0.500 to 0.774. GPT-4o achieves the best zero-shot performance at 0.628 overall, but all fine-tuned open-source models surpass it. In closed-loop driving simulation (120 scenarios including safety-critical cases), fine-tuned VLMs show improved route completion (e.g., Llama3.2: 0.529→0.632) and reduced collision rates (Llama3.2: 0.483→0.267), confirming transfer of embodied scene understanding to sequential decision-making.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All three fine-tuned open-source VLMs substantially outperform GPT-4o's zero-shot accuracy (0.628): InternVL2-8B-finetuned reaches 0.869 (+24.1 pp), Qwen2-finetuned reaches 0.844 (+21.6 pp), and Llama3.2-finetuned reaches 0.774 (+14.6 pp). Among zero-shot baselines, GPT-4o leads at 0.628, followed by InternVL2-8B (0.592), LLaVA-OneVision (0.581), Qwen2 (0.539), and Llama3.2 (0.500); LLaVA-NeXT performs below random (0.295) due to a 27.5% format-parsing failure rate. In closed-loop driving, fine-tuned Llama3.2 achieves the lowest collision rate (0.267) among all fine-tuned models, while fine-tuned Qwen2 attains the highest route completion (0.667). The sim-to-real transfer experiment (Table 2) further shows that training on simulated observations alone (0.807 overall) already exceeds zero-shot real-only performance (0.632 on real), and combining both domains yields the best result (0.869), validating the benchmark's design for generalizable embodied understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the line of driving VQA benchmarks (DriveLM, ELM, DriveVLM, DriveMLLM) and VLM-as-driving-agent work (DriveGPT4, GPT-Driver), but departs from them on three axes: (1) it replaces heterogeneous, non-intuitive object-referral conventions (pixel-coordinate tuples, camera-ID triples) with Set-of-Mark prompting and natural-language phrasing, making the benchmark commensurable and zero-shot-evaluable; (2) it extends evaluation beyond open-loop VQA to closed-loop driving in the MetaDrive simulator with safety-critical scenarios generated via CAT; and (3) it leverages ScenarioNet to reconstruct Waymo Open Motion Dataset traffic into simulation, enabling large-scale scenario diversity beyond the ~800 nuScenes scenarios that prior driving VQA datasets rely on.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MetaVQA provides the first standardized, plug-and-play benchmark that jointly evaluates spatial awareness, embodied understanding, and closed-loop driving safety in a single framework, addressing the incommensurability problem that has plagued prior driving VQA evaluations. The demonstrated sim-to-real transferability (training on simulated observations alone yields 0.819 real-world accuracy vs. 0.632 zero-shot) validates simulator-conditioned synthetic data as a practical path to scaling embodied VLM training without expensive real-world annotation. Open questions include whether the single-frame, single-camera formulation suffices for complex multi-step decisions, whether the multiple-choice format captures the full richness of spatial reasoning, and whether the closed-loop gains generalize beyond the MetaDrive simulator to higher-fidelity environments like CARLA or nuPlan.
