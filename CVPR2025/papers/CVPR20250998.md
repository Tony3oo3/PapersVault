## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting-based indoor open-world free-view synthesis degrades significantly under sparse inputs due to two compounding issues: SfM-initialized Gaussian points are too few and sparsely distributed to represent complex indoor geometry, and limited view supervision causes overfitting to training views with semantic ambiguity in under-observed regions. Prior methods either require dense view sets or focus on object-centric 'outside-in' scenarios, leaving the 'inside-out' indoor sparse-view setting largely unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 12 evenly sampled sparse RGB views from indoor scenes (Replica, ScanNet) with an 'inside-out' viewing configuration, plus language descriptions for open-vocabulary queries. During initialization, it additionally generates 8 adjacent views per training image via the MotionCtrl video generation model (using standard camera motion trajectories) to enrich SfM point density. The output is a 3D Gaussian radiance field where each primitive carries a 32-dimensional learnable semantic vector alongside standard color/opacity/scale/rotation attributes, enabling simultaneous novel-view RGB rendering and CLIP-based open-vocabulary segmentation at arbitrary viewpoints.

## Method

SPC-GS introduces two complementary components. First, Scene-layout-based Gaussian Initialization (SGI) uses MotionCtrl to synthesize 8 view-changed images per training view, combines them with originals for denser SfM points, then applies view-constrained Gaussian densification and density-based outlier removal (eliminating primitives with fewer than 5 neighbors within radius 1) to produce a structured scene-layout point distribution. Second, Semantic-Prompt Consistency (SPC) regularization exploits SAM2's temporal region proposal capability via Iterative Stochastic Prompting: during training, it renders pseudo views near training cameras, samples random 2D point prompts, and uses SAM2 to propagate region masks across views. These correspondences drive 2D constraints (view-to-view feature/relevancy consistency with a semantic-uniformity voting mechanism, plus SimCLR-style contrastive loss within training views) and 3D constraints (KL divergence between maximally-weighted Gaussian semantic features in corresponding regions, with 5×5 morphological boundary erosion to exclude ambiguous boundary primitives). The total loss combines color reconstruction, CLIP semantic supervision, generated-image semantic loss, 3D local neighbor regularization, and the SPC terms (ramped from 0 to 1 after 4k iterations).

## Results

On Replica with 12 sparse training views, SPC-GS achieves 22.011 PSNR, 0.792 SSIM, 0.254 LPIPS, 23.960 mIoU, and 63.262 mAcc. On ScanNet, it reaches 22.401 PSNR, 0.741 SSIM, 0.368 LPIPS, 28.944 mIoU, and 68.823 mAcc. This represents a 3.06 dB PSNR gain over the best reconstruction baseline (CoR-GS, 18.955 on Replica) and an 8.9-point mIoU improvement over the best segmentation baseline (Gau-Grouping, 15.028 on Replica).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPC-GS outperforms all seven baselines across every metric on both datasets. Against the strongest reconstruction baseline CoR-GS (18.955 PSNR on Replica, 20.815 on ScanNet), SPC-GS gains 3.06 dB and 1.59 dB respectively, while also achieving substantially better segmentation (23.960 vs. 15.933 mIoU on Replica). Against the strongest open-vocabulary segmentation baseline Gau-Grouping (15.028 mIoU on Replica, 15.172 on ScanNet), SPC-GS improves mIoU by 8.9 and 13.8 points respectively. The 3D open-vocabulary methods (3DOVS, Feature 3DGS, LangSplat) perform poorly under sparse inputs, with LangSplat particularly struggling (3.292 mIoU on Replica) because it freezes Gaussian geometry during semantic optimization. Sparse-view synthesis methods (DNGaussian, FSGS, CoR-GS) improve reconstruction over open-vocabulary baselines but still lag SPC-GS by 2–3 dB in PSNR and 7–14 points in mIoU, confirming that geometric regularization alone is insufficient without semantic consistency constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: sparse-view 3DGS reconstruction (DNGaussian, FSGS, CoR-GS) and 3D open-vocabulary semantic segmentation (LangSplat, Feature 3DGS, Gau-Grouping). It departs from both by jointly addressing appearance synthesis and semantic understanding under sparse indoor inputs, a setting neither line has tackled. The method repurposes a video generation model (MotionCtrl) for SfM point densification and SAM2's temporal segmentation for cross-view semantic consistency—both are creative repurposings of existing tools rather than new model architectures. The semantic Gaussian representation and CLIP-based supervision pipeline are inherited directly from LangSplat and 3DOVS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Indoor sparse-view synthesis with open-vocabulary semantics is directly relevant to robotics navigation and AR/VR, where capturing 12 views of a room is realistic but dense capture is not. The 'inside-out' viewing geometry of indoor scenes (vs. 'outside-in' for object-centric benchmarks) makes this a harder and more practically important variant. Limitations include per-scene optimization (no generalization across scenes), dependence on video generation model quality for initialization, and reliance on SAM2's zero-shot temporal consistency which may degrade on highly textured or repetitive indoor surfaces. Open questions include whether the VGM-based initialization can be replaced by cheaper alternatives and whether the approach scales to multi-room or outdoor sparse-view settings.
