## Problem

Existing end-to-end relative pose estimation networks do not satisfy the intrinsic Pose Permutation Equivariance (PPE) property: the estimated relative pose from image A to B should be the exact inverse of that from B to A. The authors demonstrate empirically that popular models (MF6D, GRelPose) produce non-trivial rotation and translation inconsistencies when input order is swapped, which poses risks for downstream applications requiring stable, consistent pose estimates such as robotics and visual navigation. No general framework existed to enforce this property on arbitrary pose estimation architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251369/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a pair of RGB images and outputs a 6DoF relative camera pose (a 3×3 rotation matrix and a 3D translation vector in SE(3)). Crucially, the method requires evaluating the baseline network in both input orders—f(I₁,I₂) and f(I₂,I₁)—and then combining the two predictions via a geodesic mean of rotations and a specific translation formula, so the final output is PPE-guaranteed by construction. A feature-permutation optimization reduces the overhead to one shared encoder pass plus two decoder passes rather than two full forward passes.

## Method

EquiPose is a general wrapper that upgrades any end-to-end relative pose estimation network f into a PPE mapping f* without modifying its architecture. The key mechanism computes the equivariant rotation as the geodesic mean R*₁₂ = G(R₁₂, R₂₁ᵀ) = R₁₂(exp(½ log(R₁₂ᵀ R₂₁ᵀ))) and the equivariant translation as t*₁₂ = (t₁₂ − R*₁₂ t₂₁)/2, where R₁₂,t₁₂ and R₂₁,t₂₁ come from the two permuted forward passes. The authors prove (Theorem 3) that this construction strictly satisfies f*(I₂,I₁) = f*(I₁,I₂)⁻¹ for any baseline f, relying on permutation invariance and transpose-duality properties of the geodesic mean. In practice, the shared Siamese encoder is run once and only the decoder is re-run on permuted features, keeping the extra cost modest. The framework supports both inference-only deployment (no retraining) and fine-tuning with a simple L₁ loss on the equivariant outputs, and is validated across five baselines (ER, Reg6D, EightVit, MF6D, GRelPose) on four datasets.

## Results

On ScanNet, EquiPose consistently improves all five baselines across rotation and translation metrics. The most striking gains come from fine-tuning: MF6D+EquiPose† reduces rotation error from 9.81° to 7.47° (−23.9%) and translation error from 0.44 m to 0.29 m (−34.1%), while GRelPose+EquiPose† reduces RE from 8.16° to 7.61° and TE from 0.38 m to 0.28 m. Even without fine-tuning (inference-only EquiPose), GRelPose improves RE by 2.3% and TE by 15.8%. Cross-dataset evaluation on 7-Scene and 12-Scene confirms consistent improvements, and outdoor evaluation on Mapfree shows the fine-tuned MF6D+EquiPose† achieves the lowest pose error (148 cm / 18.4°) among all tested models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EquiPose improves every one of the five baselines on all reported metrics, with no regressions observed. The largest relative gains appear for MF6D (RE: 9.81°→7.47°, TE: 0.44→0.29 m after fine-tuning, i.e. −23.9% and −34.1%) and GRelPose (RE: 8.16°→7.61°, TE: 0.38→0.28 m, i.e. −6.7% and −26.3%). For rotation-only models, Reg6D benefits most (RE: 12.26°→10.81°, −11.8%), while ER shows a more modest but still clear improvement (RE: 14.66°→12.53°, −14.4%). EightVit, already a strong baseline, still gains 10.4% in RE and 24.4% in TE after fine-tuning. Notably, even the inference-only EquiPose (no retraining) yields consistent improvements across all baselines, and the framework is complementary to random-permutation training: models trained with random permutation still benefit from EquiPose at inference time, and EquiPose strictly enforces PPE (d_R = d_t = 0) whereas random permutation leaves residual inconsistencies (d_R ≈ 1–5°, d_t ≈ 0.05–0.11 m).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not propose a new relative pose estimation architecture but instead introduces a model-agnostic framework (EquiPose) that enforces the Pose Permutation Equivariance (PPE) property on any existing end-to-end pose estimation network. Conceptually, it draws on the geodesic mean of rotations (Moakher, Hartley et al.) and the general theory of equivariant mappings from group action, repurposing these tools to address a consistency gap that prior end-to-end methods (MF6D, GRelPose, EightVit, Reg6D, ExtremeRotation) had overlooked. The Siamese feature-encoding / cross-image decoding pipeline common to these baselines is preserved; EquiPose only adds a feature-permutation step and a geodesic-mean combination of the forward and backward predictions, making it a thin theoretical wrapper rather than a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PPE property is a basic mathematical consistency requirement for relative poses, yet its absence in widely-used models could destabilize downstream applications such as SfM, visual localization, and robotics that assume symmetric pose estimates. The framework's practical appeal is high: it requires only a few lines of code, introduces no extra parameters, and yields consistent gains across five baselines and four datasets even without fine-tuning. Limitations include the additional decoder forward pass (costly for heavy aggregation modules) and the fact that the geodesic-mean combination, while theoretically optimal for rotations, is a simple average for translations. Open questions include whether analogous equivariance constraints can be formalized for multi-view pose estimation or for other geometric tasks (e.g., depth, 3D reconstruction), and whether the framework extends naturally to non-Siamese or asymmetric architectures.
