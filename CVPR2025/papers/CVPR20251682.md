## Problem

Existing customized image generation methods (both tuning-based and training-based) preserve object identities but systematically fail to generate the relationships between objects specified in text prompts, particularly when objects overlap or require significant pose adjustments. This gap stems from two root causes: the absence of triplet training data that decouples identity from relation, and model architectures that cannot balance image-prompt identity control with text-prompt relation generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251682/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two object image prompts (c_i, N=2) and a text prompt (c_t) describing the desired relationship, producing a single generated image that preserves both object identities while depicting the specified relation. Training uses curated triplets (x_k, c_i, c_t) where c_i contains the same objects as x_k but with distinct poses, generated via DALL-E 3's multi-turn dialog to avoid the copy-and-paste effect of cropping. The method is implemented as LoRA adapters (rank 4, ~3.1M parameters) on the text cross-attention layers of SDXL-based models, making it compatible with any SDXL backbone and trainable in ~10 minutes on 2 A100 GPUs.

## Method

DreamRelation decouples identity and relation learning through a data engine that uses DALL-E 3 to generate triplet images sharing object identity but differing in pose, then fine-tunes only LoRA layers on text cross-attention (W_q, W_kt, W_vt, W_out) while freezing all other parameters, with a scaling factor γ=0.6 on the image cross-attention branch. Two key modules enhance relation generation: (1) a Keypoint Matching Loss (KML) that detects 17 keypoints per object via X-Pose, predicts z_0 from the U-Net output, and computes MSE between VAE-encoded image-prompt keypoints and predicted z_0 keypoints in latent space to explicitly guide pose manipulation; (2) a Local Token Injection module that modifies the CLIP Image Encoder to extract dense features, aligns them with image-level features via self-distillation, and partitions/pools them into 16 local tokens concatenated with image-level tokens in the transformer-based ID extractor to prevent object confusion in overlapping scenarios.

## Results

On RelationBench (the paper's proposed benchmark), DreamRelation achieves the best CLIP-T (30.6) and CLIP-R (21.4) scores in single-object settings, and leads on all four metrics in multi-object settings (CLIP-T 28.9, CLIP-R 20.4, CLIP-I 75.4, DINO 62.1). In single-object cases, it slightly trails MS-Diffusion on DINO (63.4 vs. 64.5) and SSR-Encoder on CLIP-I (77.9 vs. 80.4), which the authors attribute to significant pose variations reducing image-level similarity. On DreamBench, DreamRelation achieves the highest CLIP-T (31.6) with competitive CLIP-I (81.7) and DINO (68.4). On M-CustomConcept101, it attains the best DINO score (52.3) and second-best CLIP-T (29.7) and CLIP-I (71.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamRelation is compared against 10 baselines spanning tuning-based (DreamBooth, Custom Diffusion, Cones-V2, ELITE) and training-based (AnyDoor, BLIP-Diffusion, SSR-Encoder, MS-Diffusion, λ-eclipse) methods, plus the relation-inversion method ReVersion integrated into MS-Diffusion. In multi-object settings, DreamRelation wins on all four metrics, with the largest margin over MS-Diffusion (its base model) in DINO (62.1 vs. 58.8, +3.3) and CLIP-R (20.4 vs. 18.9, +1.5), and over ReVersion+MS in DINO (62.1 vs. 59.7, +2.4). In single-object settings, it dominates the relation-specific metrics (CLIP-T +3.1 and CLIP-R +2.1 over the next-best ReVersion+MS) but concedes CLIP-I to SSR-Encoder (77.9 vs. 80.4) and DINO to MS-Diffusion (63.4 vs. 64.5), which the authors explain as a side effect of the pose changes required for relation generation. The advantage is most pronounced in multi-object scenarios where object confusion is a known failure mode of bounding-box-based methods like MS-Diffusion, and DreamRelation's local token injection directly addresses this gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate threads: training-based object customization (MS-Diffusion, AnyDoor, SSR-Encoder) and relation-aware text-to-image generation (ReVersion, ADI). It builds directly on MS-Diffusion's ID extractor and parallel cross-attention architecture as its base framework, and borrows the self-distillation strategy from CLIP-Self to align dense and image-level CLIP features. The key departure is that prior customization methods treat text prompts as secondary (often ignoring relational content), while prior relation methods cannot handle user-provided object identities; DreamRelation explicitly unifies both by decoupling identity (from image prompts) and relation (from text prompts) through a curated triplet data engine and targeted LoRA fine-tuning on text cross-attention layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces a practically important task—generating images that simultaneously preserve multiple object identities and follow relational text prompts—that is clearly needed for applications like virtual try-ons, social media content, and interactive scene generation. The method is lightweight (3.1M trainable parameters, 10-minute fine-tuning on 2 A100s) and compatible with any SDXL-based model, making it broadly adoptable. However, limitations remain: the task is restricted to two objects (N=2), the training data relies on DALL-E 3's multi-turn dialog (raising cost and reproducibility concerns), and evaluation depends heavily on CLIP-based scores that may not fully capture fine-grained relational accuracy. Open questions include scaling to three or more objects, handling more complex or compositional relationships, and whether the keypoint-based supervision generalizes to human-centric scenes where pose is more nuanced.
