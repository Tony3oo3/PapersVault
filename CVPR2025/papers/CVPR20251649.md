## Problem

Existing unified multimodal models either bolt external diffusion models onto an LLM (constraining the LLM's generative role) or rely on vector-quantized autoregressive tokens (limited by image tokenization quality). Rectified flow, despite its strong empirical performance in standalone generation, had not been effectively integrated into an LLM architecture for a single model that handles both understanding and generation, and shared vision encoders across the two tasks were shown to cause harmful interference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251649/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For understanding, the model accepts a 384×384 RGB image (encoded by SigLIP-Large-Patch/16) interleaved with text tokens and produces an autoregressive text response. For generation, it takes a text prompt and produces a 384×384 image by iteratively denoising in the SDXL-VAE latent space (H_latent × W_latent × D_latent) via a 30-step Euler solver with classifier-free guidance. Special tokens |BOI| and |EOI| delimit image embeddings in the sequence, and causal attention alone suffices without additional masking schemes.

## Method

JanusFlow embeds rectified flow directly inside a 1.3B DeepSeek-LLM by adding only a lightweight ConvNeXt generation encoder (g_enc) and decoder (g_dec) with a long skip connection, requiring no architectural changes to the transformer backbone. Two key strategies improve performance: (i) decoupling the vision encoders—SigLIP for understanding and from-scratch ConvNeXt for generation—to prevent task interference, and (ii) a representation alignment (REPA) loss that projects the LLM's 6th-block intermediate features onto the SigLIP feature space via a small MLP, strengthening semantic coherence during generation. Training proceeds in three stages (adaptation of new modules, unified pre-training with mixed understanding/generation/text data, and instruction-tuning SFT), using a combined objective of autoregressive cross-entropy for understanding and rectified flow velocity regression plus REPA for generation, with a logit-normal time distribution and 10% prompt dropout to support CFG inference.

## Results

JanusFlow (1.3B params) achieves GenEval 0.63, DPG-Bench 80.09%, and MJHQ FID-30k 9.51 on image generation, surpassing SDXL (0.55 GenEval, 74.65 DPG) and SDv1.5 (0.43 GenEval, 63.18 DPG) while matching Transfusion (0.63 GenEval). On multimodal understanding, it scores 74.9 on MMBench, 70.5 on SEEDBench, and 60.3 on GQA, exceeding LLaVA-v1.5 (64.3, 58.6, 62.0) and Qwen-VL-Chat (60.6, 58.2, 57.5) despite using a 1.3B LLM versus their 7B backbones. Among unified models at the same 1.3B scale, JanusFlow outperforms Janus (69.4 MMBench, 10.10 FID) and Show-o (15.18 FID) on both understanding and generation metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On image generation, JanusFlow (GenEval 0.63, DPG 80.09%) surpasses all generation-only models up to SDXL (0.55, 74.65) and DALL-E 2 (0.52), and matches Transfusion (0.63) while using only 1.3B vs. 7.3B parameters; it trails only DALL-E 3 (0.67, 83.50) which is a proprietary system. On MJHQ FID-30k, JanusFlow (9.51) beats Janus (10.10) and Show-o (15.18) at the same 1.3B scale, and outperforms 7B unified models LWM (17.77) and VILA-U256 (12.81), though VILA-U384 (7.69) remains stronger. On understanding, JanusFlow (MMBench 74.9, SEEDBench 70.5) clearly outperforms Janus (69.4, 63.7) and Show-o (no MMBench reported, GQA 48.7), and exceeds 7B understanding-only models LLaVA-v1.5 (64.3, 58.6) and Qwen-VL-Chat (60.6, 58.2) by 5–10 points on MMBench and SEEDBench. The ablation (Table 5) confirms that the decoupled encoder design (SigLIP for understanding, ConvNeXt for generation) and REPA alignment each contribute meaningfully: removing REPA drops FID from 17.61 to 19.84 and CLIP from 26.40 to 24.94, while sharing encoders (Exp. B) degrades understanding (POPE 78.13 vs. 84.73) and generation (FID 18.05 vs. 17.61) simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

JanusFlow extends the Janus architecture (Wu et al., 2024) by replacing its vector-quantized autoregressive generation with rectified flow (Liu et al., ICLR 2023), demonstrating that the flow-based objective can be trained directly within an LLM using only a lightweight ConvNeXt encoder/decoder. It incorporates the representation alignment regularization (REPA, Yu et al., 2024) to bridge the semantic gap between the understanding encoder and the LLM's internal features during generation. Unlike prior unified models that either attach external diffusion models (SEED-X, DreamLLM) or rely on discrete tokenization (Chameleon, LWM), JanusFlow operates in continuous latent space with causal attention, avoiding both architectural complexity and tokenization quality bottlenecks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical takeaway is that rectified flow integrates into LLMs with minimal architectural overhead—no special attention masking, no separate generative backbone—making unified understanding-and-generation models significantly easier to build and scale. The decoupled encoder design (SigLIP for understanding, ConvNeXt for generation) plus REPA alignment provides a reusable recipe that practitioners can adopt. Open questions include whether this approach scales to higher resolutions (currently 384×384), video generation, or more complex compositional prompts, and whether the 1.3B parameter budget is sufficient for production-quality outputs. The modest gap to DALL-E 3 (GenEval 0.63 vs. 0.67) suggests headroom remains for larger models or better data.
