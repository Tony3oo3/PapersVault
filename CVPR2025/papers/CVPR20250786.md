## Problem

Existing NeRF-based deblurring methods address only static scenes with camera motion blur or defocus blur, which affect the entire image uniformly. Object motion blur in dynamic scenes is fundamentally different: it is local, time-varying, and governed by the interplay between 3D object velocity, depth, and camera exposure. No prior work has tackled localized object motion blur in the NeRF domain, leaving a gap for reconstructing sharp novel views and recovering 3D motion from motion-blurred multiview dynamic videos.

## Input / Output

The method consumes posed multi-view videos (8–20 synchronized static cameras) of a dynamic scene corrupted by object motion blur, along with a coarse object motion mask (generated via Movavi, not pixel-perfect). Synthetic inputs are 1024×768 at 120 fps with 300 frames; real inputs are 1280×720 from 10 GoPro cameras with 120–300 frames. The method outputs sharp 4D spatio-temporal novel views, a continuous 3D motion profile (speed and trajectory) of the moving rigid body, and the blur kernel as continuous 2D locations with associated photometric weights.

## Method

DynaMoDe-NeRF builds on HexPlane's 4D plane decomposition for scene representation and introduces an analytical framework that maps 3D object translational velocity to continuous 2D blur kernel locations across all views simultaneously: during exposure τ, 2m+1 virtual 3D points (offset along the velocity direction) project onto the same pixel, and their 2D positions are computed in closed form from depth, velocity, and camera intrinsics. A lightweight 3-layer MLP called BlurKernelNet takes the concatenated velocity and depth as input and predicts the (2m+1) kernel weights that linearly mix the latent sharp intensities at those locations to reproduce the observed blurry pixel. Rather than learning per-frame velocities, the method optimizes only L ≪ M control velocities and linearly interpolates between them, reducing complexity while preserving temporal smoothness. Training is fully self-supervised via a composite loss combining photometric matching, a kernel-weight derivative regularizer to prevent degenerate solutions, 3D direction and magnitude regularizers for motion consistency, a 2D velocity regularizer guided by the motion mask center displacement, and total-variation regularization on the feature planes.

## Results

On the Dynamic (moving foreground) region, DynaMoDe-NeRF achieves the best PSNR across all four synthetic scenes: 26.93 (Cube), 27.96 (Lychee), 30.41 (Monkey), and 24.10 (Sphere), outperforming the strongest baseline by 1.5–1.7 dB. In the Overall metric, it leads on Cube (31.47 vs. 30.91 for MixVoxel) and Sphere (34.78 vs. 32.70 for MixVoxel), though 4DGS edges ahead on Lychee (35.29) and Monkey (38.37). The method also recovers 3D motion profiles with cosine similarity >0.81 for trajectories and >0.98 for speed, a task no prior NeRF-based method addresses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynaMoDe-NeRF consistently outperforms all four quantitative baselines (HexPlane, MixVoxel, K-Planes, 4DGS) in the Dynamic region across every scene, with the largest margin over HexPlane (up to +11.7 dB on Sphere) and a steady 1.5–1.7 dB advantage over the strongest competitor (4DGS or MixVoxel depending on scene). In the Overall metric, the method wins on Cube and Sphere but trails 4DGS by ~0.7–2.7 dB on Lychee and Monkey, where 4DGS's stronger static-region rendering compensates for its weaker dynamic handling. The three qualitative-only baselines—Deblur-NeRF [24], DP-NeRF [16], and DyBluRF [44]—are designed for static scenes or monocular video and fail to produce coherent sharp novel views of the moving object, often missing it entirely (Fig. 4). Notably, DynaMoDe-NeRF is the first method to simultaneously recover the 3D motion profile (speed and trajectory) alongside deblurred novel views, a capability none of the compared baselines offer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on HexPlane's 4D plane-decomposition representation for dynamic scenes and on the NeRF-based deblurring paradigm introduced by Deblur-NeRF, which learns sparse kernel locations and weights to match blurred observations. It departs from prior NeRF deblurring work (Deblur-NeRF, DP-NeRF, Sharp-NeRF, PDRF) by shifting from static-scene camera/defocus blur to localized object motion blur in dynamic scenes, and from dynamic NeRF work (NSFF, DynIBAR, MixVoxel) by removing the assumption of artifact-free inputs. The key conceptual departure is an analytical framework that maps 3D rigid-body velocity to continuous 2D blur kernel locations across views and time, replacing the learned or prior-based kernel parameterization used in static deblurring NeRFs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to jointly reconstruct a sharp dynamic 4D scene, render novel views, and recover the underlying 3D motion profile from multi-view object-motion-blurred videos, opening a practical path for applications like sports video analysis where fast object motion degrades quality. Limitations are notable: the method requires a rough motion mask, assumes a single rigid body in pure translational motion, and struggles with very severe blur. Open questions include extending to rotational and non-rigid motion, handling multiple simultaneously moving objects, and removing the motion-mask dependency through learned segmentation.
