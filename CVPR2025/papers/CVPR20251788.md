## Problem

Existing Diffusion Transformer (DiT) models apply a fixed spatial compression ratio uniformly across all image regions, ignoring the natural variation in information density. This creates an inherent tradeoff: large compression limits local realism in detail-rich regions, while small compression inflates sequence length, increasing computational cost and degrading global structural consistency. The paper addresses this by proposing a dynamic, information-density-aware compression strategy for the diffusion process.

## Input / Output

For class-conditional generation (ImageNet), the model takes a class label and random noise as input; for unconditional generation (FFHQ), only random noise is provided. The Dynamic Grain Transformer first predicts a spatial grain map (a grid of granularity assignments per region), which is then fed as a conditioning signal alongside the class label and diffusion timestep to the Dynamic Content Transformer. The final output is a 256×256 RGB image, with the latent space operating at dual resolutions: 32×32×4 for fine-grained regions and 16×16×4 for coarse-grained regions.

## Method

The proposed D²iT is a two-stage framework. Stage 1, Dynamic VAE (DVAE), uses a hierarchical encoder with two downsampling factors (8× and 16×) and a Dynamic Grained Coding module that assigns each 16×16 pixel region a granularity based on its local Shannon entropy (computed via Gaussian KDE), producing a dual-grained latent representation. Stage 2, D²iT, consists of a lightweight Dynamic Grain Transformer (33M params) that predicts the grain map from noise, and a Dynamic Content Transformer that performs multi-grained noise prediction: a Multi-grained Net (patch size 2) produces a rough coarse+fine noise estimate, and a Fine-grained RefineNet (patch size 1, using windowed self-attention with learnable relative position bias) corrects the fine-grained noise in detail regions. A granularity-weighted multi-grained noise loss trains the network, and the rough-then-refine strategy unifies global consistency (modeled by the large-patch backbone) with local realism (corrected by the small-patch RefineNet).

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation with classifier-free guidance, D²iT-XL-G achieves an FID-50K of 1.73, a 23.8% improvement over DiT-XL-G (2.27), while using only 57.1% of the training steps. It also attains an Inception Score of 307.89, Precision of 0.87, and Recall of 0.56. On unconditional FFHQ generation, D²iT-L achieves FID-10K of 4.47, a 28.6% improvement over DiT-L (6.26), with a comparable parameter count (467M vs. 458M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

D²iT-XL-G (FID 1.73) outperforms DiT-XL-G (2.27) by 23.8%, MDT-G (1.79) by 3.4%, RDM-G (1.99) by 13.1%, and all earlier diffusion baselines (ADM-G-U at 3.94, LDM-4-G at 3.60) by large margins. It also achieves the highest Precision (0.87) among all -G methods, tying with LDM-4-G. However, D²iT-XL-G is slightly outperformed by MDTv2-G (1.58 FID) and DiMR (1.70 FID) on FID, though it surpasses both on Inception Score (307.89 vs. 314.73 and 289.0 respectively) and Precision. On FFHQ unconditional generation, D²iT-L (FID-10K 4.47) beats DiT-L (6.26) by 28.6%, LDM-4 (4.98), and UDM (5.54), establishing a new state of the art among diffusion-based methods at comparable scale. The paper also demonstrates faster training convergence than DiT at matched parameter counts, reaching competitive FID in roughly 57% of the training steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DiT (Peebles & Xie, ICCV 2023) two-stage paradigm by replacing its fixed VAE downsampling and patchify with a dynamic, information-density-aware compression strategy. The DVAE component draws conceptual inspiration from DQVAE (Huang et al., CVPR 2023), which introduced dynamic coding for autoregressive image generation, but is the first to transfer this idea into a continuous-space diffusion setting. The multi-grained noise prediction in the Dynamic Content Transformer, combining a coarse-patch DiT backbone with a fine-patch RefineNet using windowed self-attention, departs from the uniform-token assumption of all prior DiT variants (MDT, FiT, PixArt-α) and introduces a novel rough-prediction-plus-fine-correction diffusion objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a fundamental tension in DiT-based generation: large fixed compression sacrifices local detail while small fixed compression burdens global modeling, and shows that a principled, entropy-driven allocation of representational capacity resolves both simultaneously. The 23.8% FID improvement over DiT-XL using only 57.1% of the training steps suggests the dynamic granularity strategy accelerates convergence by eliminating redundant denoising in smooth regions. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond two granularities and 256×256 resolution, how it interacts with text-conditioned generation at scale, and whether the lightweight grain-map predictor generalizes to domains with very different spatial complexity distributions (e.g., medical or satellite imagery).
