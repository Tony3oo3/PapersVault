## Problem

Existing diffusion-based super-resolution methods entangle pixel-level fidelity and semantic-level enhancement in a single training objective, making it difficult to balance content preservation against perceptual detail generation. Moreover, users have diverse preferences (fidelity-first vs. detail-first), yet prior methods lack a mechanism to adjust the SR style at inference without re-training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250641/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-quality RGB image (128×128 for the ×4 SR task) and produces a 512×512 high-quality super-resolved image. In the default setting, inference requires only one diffusion step; in the adjustable setting, two forward passes are needed to separately compute the pixel-level and semantic-level residuals, which are then combined via user-specified guidance scales λ_pix and λ_sem. The model operates entirely in the latent space of SD 2.1-base's VAE, with no external semantic extractor required at inference.

## Method

PiSA-SR formulates SD-based SR as residual learning in latent space (z_H = z_L − λ·ε_θ(z_L)), then introduces two separate LoRA modules on the frozen SD UNet: a pixel-level LoRA trained with ℓ₂ loss to remove degradations, and a semantic-level LoRA trained with LPIPS plus classifier score distillation (CSD) loss to inject open-category semantic details from the pre-trained SD and VGG priors. Training is decoupled—the pixel LoRA is optimized first, then frozen while the semantic LoRA is trained within a combined PiSA-LoRA group—preventing the two objectives from interfering. At inference, a CFG-inspired decomposition (ε_θ = λ_pix·ε_pix + λ_sem·(ε_PiSA − ε_pix)) lets users independently scale pixel-level restoration and semantic-level enhancement, yielding a continuous spectrum from faithful denoising to aggressive detail synthesis without any re-training.

## Results

On the RealSR dataset, PiSA-SR (1-step) achieves PSNR 25.50, LPIPS 0.2672, DISTS 0.2044, CLIPIQA 0.6702, MUSIQ 70.15, and MANIQA 0.6560, outperforming all competing diffusion-based SR methods. On DIV2K, it reaches LPIPS 0.2823 and MUSIQ 69.68, and on DrealSR it attains CLIPIQA 0.6970 and MANIQA 0.6156, all leading among one-step and multi-step baselines. PiSA-SR also has the fewest parameters (1.30B) and fastest inference time (0.09 s/image) among SD-based methods, compared to OSEDiff (1.77B, 0.12 s) and SeeSR (2.51B, 4.30 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PiSA-SR (1-step) outperforms the closest one-step competitor OSEDiff on all three datasets across LPIPS, DISTS, CLIPIQA, MUSIQ, and MANIQA; for example, on RealSR it improves LPIPS from 0.2921 to 0.2672 and MUSIQ from 69.09 to 70.15. Against multi-step SD-based methods (SeeSR, PASD, StableSR, DiffBIR), PiSA-SR achieves substantially better perceptual quality (e.g., MUSIQ 70.15 vs. SeeSR's 69.37 on RealSR) while being 48× faster (0.09 s vs. 4.30 s). On PSNR, PiSA-SR is competitive but not always the best: ResShift-S15 achieves slightly higher PSNR on RealSR (26.31 vs. 25.50) and DrealSR (28.45 vs. 28.31), reflecting the well-known fidelity-perception trade-off. Among no-reference metrics, PiSA-SR leads all baselines on CLIPIQA and MUSIQ across all three datasets, with the largest margins over GAN-free diffusion methods like ResShift and SinSR (e.g., CLIPIQA 0.6970 vs. 0.5259 on DrealSR). The method also uses fewer parameters (1.30B) than OSEDiff (1.77B), PASD (2.31B), and SeeSR (2.51B) because it does not require an external semantic extractor at inference time.

## Relation to Prior Work

PiSA-SR builds directly on OSEDiff's one-step residual formulation for diffusion-based SR and adapts classifier score distillation (CSD) from 3D generation to the SR setting. It departs from prior SD-based SR methods (StableSR, DiffBIR, PASD, SeeSR) that entangle pixel-level fidelity and semantic-level enhancement in a single training objective, instead decoupling them into two separate LoRA modules optimized with distinct losses (ℓ2 for pixel-level; LPIPS + CSD for semantic-level). The adjustable inference mechanism, inspired by classifier-free guidance, extends the residual scaling idea from Eq. (3) into a two-axis control over pixel and semantic contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PiSA-SR offers a practical, training-free way for users to trade off fidelity against perceptual richness at inference time, addressing a real deployment need that prior SR methods handle only through retraining or coarse degradation-level controls. The one-step inference (0.09 s/image) with competitive quality makes it attractive for real-time applications. However, the single pixel-level LoRA may be insufficient for heavy or diverse degradations, and the adjustable mode doubles inference cost to two steps. Open questions include whether degradation-specific LoRA spaces or image-adaptive scale factors could further improve robustness.
