## Problem

Existing controllable person image generation methods (virtual try-on, pose transfer) produce high-quality images at first glance but distort fine-grained details such as textures, text, and logos from reference images. The authors trace this to target queries in attention layers failing to focus on the correct reference key regions, and note that prior fixes rely on complex auxiliary models or inference strategies without explicit supervision on attention alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252555/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For virtual try-on, the method takes a source person image and a reference garment image, producing a person wearing the garment; for pose transfer, it takes a source person image (providing pose via a Dense-Pose map) and a reference person image, producing the reference person in the source pose. During training, source and target are the same image. All inputs are encoded into latent space via a VAE encoder, and conditioning is achieved through channel concatenation (garment-agnostic image + mask for try-on; Dense-Pose map for pose transfer) rather than cross-attention with text.

## Method

Leffa introduces a regularization loss that converts the attention map between target queries and reference keys into a spatial flow field, then uses grid sampling to warp the reference image and computes an L2 loss against the target image, explicitly supervising each query to attend to the correct reference region. The loss is applied only to attention layers above a resolution threshold (1/32 of image size) and at timesteps below a threshold (500/1000), with a temperature of 2.0 to keep attention maps sufficiently smooth. Training follows a progressive schedule where Leffa is added only in the final fine-tuning phase at 1024 resolution, combined with the standard diffusion loss. The approach adds no extra parameters or inference cost and is model-agnostic, demonstrated by plugging the loss into IDM-VTON and CatVTON with consistent FID improvements.

## Results

On VITON-HD (virtual try-on), Leffa achieves FID 4.54 / 8.52 and KID 0.05 / 0.32 in paired/unpaired settings, outperforming the previous best CatVTON (FID 5.42 / 9.02) by 0.88 / 0.5. On DressCode, it reaches FID 2.06 / 4.48, surpassing CatVTON (3.99 / 6.14) by 1.93 / 1.66. For pose transfer on DeepFashion, Leffa attains FID 5.71 at 256×176 and 4.23 at 512×352, beating the prior best X-MDPT-L (6.25 / 5.93) by 0.54 / 1.61, while also achieving the best LPIPS (0.114 / 0.119).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Leffa outperforms all 10 virtual-try-on baselines on VITON-HD across every metric, with the closest competitor CatVTON [8] trailing by 0.88 FID (paired) and 0.50 FID (unpaired), while IDM-VTON [7] lags by 1.22 FID (paired). On DressCode, the gap widens: Leffa beats CatVTON by 1.93 FID (paired) and 1.66 FID (unpaired), and OOTDiffusion [63] by 2.55 FID (paired). For pose transfer on DeepFashion, Leffa surpasses X-MDPT-L [39] (the prior best) by 0.54 FID at 256×176 and 1.61 FID at 512×352, and also leads PIDM [2] and CFLD [31] by 0.66 and 1.09 FID respectively at 512×352. The only metric where Leffa does not lead is SSIM at 256×176, where CFLD [31] edges ahead by 0.005 (0.737 vs. 0.732), though Leffa still wins on FID and LPIPS. Additionally, the Leffa loss is shown to be model-agnostic: adding it to IDM-VTON reduces FID from 5.76 to 5.12 (paired), and adding it to CatVTON reduces FID from 5.42 to 4.86 (paired), confirming consistent gains across architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Stable Diffusion (SD1.5) as its base architecture, duplicating the UNet into separate generative and reference branches connected via spatially concatenated self-attention. It departs from the prevailing trend in virtual try-on and pose transfer of stacking auxiliary modules (CLIP, DINOv2, warping networks) or complex training strategies (DREAM) to preserve fine-grained details, instead attributing detail distortion to attention misalignment and addressing it with a single regularization loss. The Leffa loss extends prior attention-supervision work (e.g., PoCoLD's pose-constrained attention, StableVITON's attention TV loss) by providing explicit spatial correspondence guidance: it converts attention maps into flow fields and supervises them via grid-sampled warping of the reference image, a mechanism not present in earlier attention-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The model-agnostic nature of the Leffa loss—demonstrated by plugging it into IDM-VTON and CatVTON without extra parameters—makes it a broadly applicable drop-in improvement for any diffusion-based controllable generation pipeline that uses cross-image attention. The paper's diagnosis that detail distortion stems from diffuse rather than focused attention, validated by the manual attention-correction experiment, offers a clear mechanistic explanation that prior methods addressed only indirectly. Open questions include whether the loss generalizes beyond person-centric tasks (e.g., object try-on, scene editing), whether the progressive-training requirement (Leffa only in the final finetuning phase) limits its applicability, and whether a unified model controlling both appearance and pose simultaneously is feasible with this supervision signal.
