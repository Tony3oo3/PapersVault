## Problem

Current state-of-the-art scene change detection models achieve strong in-domain performance but collapse under unseen environments (77.6% → 8.0% F1) and reversed temporal order (77.6% → 4.6%), because they are tightly coupled to limited, often synthetic training data. No existing benchmark systematically evaluates generalizability across diverse domains or temporal consistency of SCD predictions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250213/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

GeSCF takes a bi-temporal RGB image pair (two views of the same scene captured at different times) and a coarse geometric transformation between them, then produces a binary change mask at the input image resolution. The pipeline is fully commutative—reversing the temporal order of the two images yields an identical change mask—ensuring complete temporal consistency (TC = 1.0) without any training or domain-specific inductive bias.

## Method

GeSCF is a zero-shot, training-free framework built entirely on the pre-trained Segment Anything Model. In the first stage (Initial Pseudo-mask Generation), it intercepts key-facet features from intermediate ViT layers of SAM's image encoder for both images, computes multi-head feature correlation to obtain similarity maps, and binarizes them via an adaptive threshold function that dynamically adjusts based on the skewness of the similarity distribution. In the second stage (Geometric-Semantic Mask Matching), it selects SAM's class-agnostic object masks that overlap the pseudo-mask beyond a threshold (Geometric Intersection Matching), then validates each candidate by computing cosine similarity between the corresponding mask embeddings from the final image-encoder layer (Semantic Similarity Matching), retaining only masks with sufficiently low semantic similarity as true changes. The entire pipeline requires no learnable parameters, no SCD-specific training data, and no user prompts.

## Results

On the three standard SCD datasets (VL-CMU-CD, TSUNAMI, ChangeSim), GeSCF achieves an average F1-score of 67.7% in a zero-shot manner, surpassing the best supervised baseline (C-3PO trained on TSUNAMI, 48.5%) by +19.2%. On the proposed ChangeVPR dataset (unseen urban, suburban, and rural environments), GeSCF reaches 64.1% average F1-score, nearly doubling the best prior method (C-3PO at 33.9%) with a +30.0% margin. Critically, GeSCF achieves a perfect Temporal Consistency (TC) score of 1.0 across all settings, whereas most baselines drop to TC values as low as 0.01–0.02 when the temporal input order is reversed. On the remote sensing SECOND benchmark, GeSCF also outperforms unsupervised baselines with 48.0% F1-score and 52.2% precision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeSCF outperforms all four supervised SCD baselines (CSCDNet, CDResNet, DR-TANet, C-3PO) across every unseen-domain evaluation. On the standard SCD datasets, the best supervised baseline (C-3PO trained on TSUNAMI) achieves 48.5% average F1-score, while GeSCF reaches 67.7% zero-shot (+19.2%); notably, GeSCF even surpasses the best in-domain supervised result on ChangeSim (54.8% vs. 43.1% for CSCDNet trained on ChangeSim). On the ChangeVPR dataset, GeSCF's 64.1% average F1-score nearly doubles the best baseline (C-3PO at 33.9%), with the gap widening in the challenging Nordland rural split (59.0% vs. 23.2%). The most striking contrast is temporal consistency: C-3PO trained on VL-CMU-CD drops from 77.6% to 4.6% F1-score when the temporal order is reversed (TC=0.02), whereas GeSCF maintains identical performance in both directions (TC=1.0). On the remote sensing SECOND benchmark, GeSCF (48.0% F1, 52.2% precision) outperforms all six unsupervised baselines (ISFA, DSFA, DCAE, OBCD, KPCA-MNet, DCVA), which range from 32.9% to 36.8% F1-score.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Segment Anything Model (SAM) for a fundamentally different task: instead of promptable single-image segmentation, it intercepts SAM's internal ViT feature facets (query/key/value) and class-agnostic masks to perform zero-shot change detection on bi-temporal image pairs. It departs sharply from prior SCD methods (CSCDNet, DR-TANet, C-3PO), which are supervised or semi-supervised and tightly coupled to their training distributions, and from prior SAM-based change detection work that focused on remote sensing CD with parameter-efficient fine-tuning. The paper also introduces a new evaluation paradigm (GeSCD) that explicitly measures cross-domain generalizability and temporal consistency—dimensions largely absent from prior SCD benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that state-of-the-art SCD models collapse from 77.6% to 8.0% F1 in an unseen domain and to 4.6% under reversed temporal order exposes a critical reliability gap that the field has overlooked, making the proposed GeSCD benchmark and ChangeVPR dataset essential for honest evaluation. The zero-shot SAM-based approach eliminates the costly change-annotation bottleneck that has constrained SCD dataset growth, potentially enabling rapid deployment to new environments. Open questions include whether the method handles very subtle or semantically ambiguous changes, how it scales to video sequences, and whether the adaptive skewness-based thresholding generalizes to domains with fundamentally different similarity distributions (e.g., medical or satellite imagery beyond the tested SECOND benchmark).
