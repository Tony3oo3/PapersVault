## Problem

Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting methods produce unstructured, mutually independent Gaussians that are memory-inefficient (80% of storage consumed by redundant SH coefficients) and lack the structural organization needed for Level-of-Detail management. While voxel-based (Scaffold-GS) and mesh-based approaches have been explored, none provide a scalable primitive that supports progressive memory reduction while preserving rendering detail, limiting deployment on resource-constrained devices.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252219/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes sparse point clouds (from COLMAP SfM or uniform sampling) and multi-view RGB images with known camera poses as training input. It produces a compact 3D scene representation consisting of discrete triangle primitives (vertices + learnable vertex features) and a small triangle-MLP network, from which rendered 2D images are generated at any LOD by downsampling the interior Gaussians. The full-size representation requires only ~5 MB of storage versus ~72 MB for vanilla 3DGS on NeRF-Synthetic.

## Method

LOD-GS uses a two-stage optimization over a discrete triangle soup. In Stage 1, sparse triangles are initialized with K=66 barycentric-interpolated Gaussians each, and triangle vertex positions are optimized via standard 3DGS losses (L1 + SSIM + scale regularization) with adaptive triangle pruning (merging low-opacity vertices into larger triangles) and growing (cloning high-gradient vertices). In Stage 2, the triangle structure is frozen and learnable features are assigned to each vertex; a triangle-MLP takes barycentric-interpolated vertex features concatenated with view direction to implicitly predict all Gaussian attributes (opacity, scale, rotation, color) plus a position offset, eliminating explicit SH storage. LOD is achieved by uniformly downsampling every K-th Gaussian within each triangle (K=2^n), with 75% of samples drawn from high-density regions estimated via kernel density estimation, enabling progressive memory reduction from full size down to ~1 MB while maintaining consistent rendering quality.

## Results

On NeRF-Synthetic at full size (LOD0), LOD-GS achieves 33.24 PSNR with only 5.31 MB storage, compared to 3DGS (33.88 PSNR, 71.6 MB) and Scaffold-GS (33.36 PSNR, 18.2 MB). The efficiency metric (PSNR per unit storage) is 6.18 for LOD-GS versus 0.47 for 3DGS and 1.85 for Scaffold-GS at LOD0. At the most aggressive downsampling (LOD3, K=8), LOD-GS retains 31.08 PSNR in just 1.4 MB, while 3DGS drops to 32.00 PSNR at 8.6 MB and Scaffold-GS falls to 29.91 PSNR at 1.9 MB. On NSVF, similar trends hold: LOD-GS achieves 33.02 PSNR at 4.31 MB (LOD0) and 31.61 PSNR at 1.5 MB (LOD3), consistently outperforming both baselines in memory efficiency across all LOD levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 3DGS [Kerbl et al., 2023], LOD-GS trades a small PSNR gap at full size (33.24 vs. 33.88 on NeRF-Synthetic, 33.02 vs. 33.12 on NSVF) for a dramatic memory reduction (5.31 MB vs. 71.6 MB, i.e., ~13× less). At LOD3, LOD-GS actually surpasses Scaffold-GS in PSNR (31.08 vs. 29.91 on NeRF-Synthetic; 31.61 vs. 30.86 on NSVF) while using comparable or less storage. Against Scaffold-GS [Lu et al., CVPR 2024], LOD-GS achieves higher PSNR at every downsampling level (LOD1–LOD3) on both datasets, with the gap widening as memory shrinks (e.g., 31.08 vs. 29.91 at LOD3 on NeRF-Synthetic). The efficiency metric (PSNR per MB) confirms LOD-GS dominates: 6.18 vs. 1.85 (Scaffold-GS) and 0.47 (3DGS) at LOD0 on NeRF-Synthetic, and 7.65 vs. 3.05 and 0.75 on NSVF. Qualitatively, LOD-GS preserves fine geometric details (highlights, high-frequency textures) at LOD2–LOD3 where both baselines produce overly smooth or artifact-ridden results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) framework by replacing its unstructured point cloud with a discrete triangle soup, drawing on the graphics tradition of triangle soup as an intermediate representation between point clouds and meshes. The implicit attribute modeling via MLP is directly inherited from Scaffold-GS, which used voxel anchors; here, the authors swap voxel anchors for triangle primitives and introduce a triangle-MLP that predicts Gaussian attributes from barycentric-interpolated vertex features plus view direction. The LOD mechanism via structured downsampling within primitives departs from Octree-GS's multi-resolution aliasing approach and from mesh-based Gaussian methods that bind only a handful of splats per face, leaving no room for progressive detail reduction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 13× memory reduction over 3DGS (5.3 MB vs. 71.6 MB at LOD0) while maintaining comparable PSNR is practically significant for VR, edge devices, and interactive simulations where storage is tightly constrained. However, evaluation is limited to two small synthetic datasets (NeRF-Synthetic, NSVF) with only two baselines, leaving open whether the triangle soup structure generalizes to large-scale real-world scenes, dynamic content, or outdoor environments. A key open question is whether the fixed barycentric interpolation within planar triangles becomes a bottleneck for highly curved or thin-geometry surfaces, and whether the two-stage training pipeline can be unified or accelerated for practical deployment.
