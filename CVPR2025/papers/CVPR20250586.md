## Problem

Long-term sports assessment requires evaluating both action quality and action-music consistency, but the weak correlation between diverse background music and specific movements makes this difficult. Existing audio-visual methods address this by scaling up model parameters (e.g., MLP-Mixer, PAMFN) to implicitly learn these associations, overlooking that low-cost language modality could explicitly bridge action-music correlations and reduce parameter reliance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250586/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes long sports videos segmented into T non-overlapping 32-frame clips along with their background audio, plus two sets of text prompts: M action prompts derived from official event rules (e.g., 'a video of [category]' and 'a music suitable for [category]') and N/2N quality-grade prompts in a coarse-to-fine structure. The output is a single quality score computed as a learnable-weighted combination of a visual action score (from N grade patterns) and an action-music matching score (from 2N grade patterns), where grade weights are fixed linearly spaced values.

## Method

MLAVL transforms audio-visual learning into audio-action-visual learning by constructing relational action knowledge graphs from text-encoded domain actions and passing this knowledge into visual/audio temporal graphs via a 2-layer graph convolutional network with a cross-graph mapping operation (MAG²). A Shared-Specific Context Encoder first fuses frozen modality-specific features (VST for video, AST for audio) with a shared Transformer encoder to capture both pure and cross-modal semantics. An Audio-Visual Cross-modal Fusion module then aligns actions and music at both global (cross-temporal attention) and clip-wise (concatenation + convolutional block) levels. Finally, a Dual-branch Prompt-guided Grading module uses a 3-layer transformer decoder with textual grade prompts as queries to aggregate quality patterns separately for visual and audio-visual performance. Training combines triplet loss for grade-pattern discriminability, cross-entropy loss for prompt-pattern alignment, and MSE for score accuracy, with the entire system requiring only 3.82M parameters.

## Results

On FS1000, MLAVL achieves an average Spearman correlation of 0.90 and average MSE of 10.39, surpassing the previous audio-visual SOTA PAMFN (0.87 / 16.80) by +3.0 in correlation and −6.41 in MSE. On Fis-V, MLAVL reaches 0.823 Sp. Corr. and 13.31 MSE with only 3.82M parameters and 0.778G FLOPs, outperforming PAMFN (18.06M params, 2.562G FLOPs) on MSE while matching its correlation. On Rhythmic Gymnastics, MLAVL sets a new SOTA with 0.849 Avg. Sp. Corr. and 4.47 Avg. MSE, improving MSE by 1.06 over the second-best GDLT. As a plug-in on the visual-only LOGO dataset, MAG² improves all nine tested baselines, with the largest gain on HGCN (+18% Sp. Corr.).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MLAVL outperforms all 16 compared baselines on every dataset. Among audio-visual methods, it beats PAMFN (the strongest prior SOTA) by +3.0 Sp. Corr. and −6.41 MSE on FS1000, and by −2.02 MSE on Fis-V while using 4.7× fewer parameters (3.82M vs. 18.06M). SGN and MLP-Mixer, the other audio-visual competitors, are surpassed by +2 to +5 in correlation and by 1.4–2.4 in MSE on FS1000. Among visual-only methods, MLAVL exceeds QTD (0.85 Sp. Corr., 22.64 MSE) and GDLT (0.85, 13.60) on FS1000, and QTD (0.842, 7.61) on Rhythmic Gymnastics by a clear margin in MSE (4.47). On the visual-only LOGO dataset, the plug-in MAG² module improves every tested baseline: HGCN gains +18% Sp. Corr. (0.541→0.640), CoRe +4% (0.697→0.723), and TPT +5% (0.589→0.621), confirming the generality of the language-guided action knowledge approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of long-term sports assessment (AQA) and language-guided multimodal learning. Prior audio-visual methods for sports scoring (PAMFN, MLP-Mixer, SGN) rely on large parameter counts to capture weak associations between background music and athlete actions, while visual-only methods (GDLT, TPT, CoFInAl, QTD) ignore the music-action consistency that sports rules require. The key departure here is using low-cost language prompts to construct multidimensional action knowledge graphs that explicitly bridge audio and visual modalities via graph convolutional guidance (MAG²), rather than learning these associations implicitly through scale. The dual-branch prompt-guided grading module also extends prior prompt-based AQA work (RICA², NAE) by separating visual action quality from action-music matching, mirroring how human judges actually score.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in the plug-and-play MAG² module, which improves nine existing visual-only methods on LOGO without architectural changes, suggesting language-guided action knowledge is a broadly applicable enhancement for AQA. The finding that domain-specific action graphs can substitute for large model parameters in audio-visual learning is useful for resource-constrained deployments. However, the approach is tightly coupled to sports with explicit action-music scoring rules (figure skating, rhythmic gymnastics), and the authors acknowledge it may degrade in scenes where audio is sparse or irrelevant. Open questions include whether the language-guided graph mechanism generalizes to sports without music components or to other long-form video understanding tasks, and whether the manually designed prompt templates can be automated.
