## Problem

Controllable and consistent editing of dynamic 3D scenes remains underexplored. Existing methods like Instruct 4D-to-4D and Control4D rely on fixed diffusion backbones (IP2P, ControlNet) that cannot perform precise local edits and produce temporally inconsistent results across frames, limiting both controllability and edit quality in 4D settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252293/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained dynamic 3D Gaussian scene (with its deformation field), the original multi-view image set with registered camera poses, and a single edited reference image produced by any off-the-shelf 2D editing tool (SDEdit, IP2P, BlendedDiffusion, MimicBrush, NNST) along with a text instruction and optional mask. It outputs an edited dynamic 3D Gaussian scene with consistent edits across all frames and views, plus a personalized IP2P model that can be reused. The framework supports both monocular (single-camera) and multi-camera dynamic scenes, and the single-image requirement means no paired training data beyond one edit is needed.

## Method

CTRL-D reduces dynamic 3D scene editing to a 2D image editing problem by first fine-tuning InstructPix2Pix on a single source–edited image pair, augmented with affine transformations to prevent overfitting, and regularized with a DreamBooth-inspired prior preservation loss that maintains the model's general editing capabilities. The personalized IP2P then drives a two-stage optimization of deformable 3D Gaussians: Stage 1 optimizes only the canonical-space Gaussians (deformation field frozen) using the edited keyframe and gradient-based Gaussian densification to fill sparse regions; Stage 2 jointly optimizes the deformation field and Gaussians using an edited image buffer that iteratively replaces source frames with their personalized-IP2P-edited counterparts, accelerating convergence. A temporal regularization loss minimizes differences between adjacent frames to enforce temporal consistency, and the overall loss combines L1, D-SSIM, and temporal terms. This design enables precise local edits (the personalized model learns editing regions directly from the reference) without explicit region tracking, and generalizes the learned edit to other scenes and domains.

## Results

On monocular scenes (Portrait, Cat) and a multi-camera scene (Steak), CTRL-D achieves CLIP scores of 27.75, 31.81, and 28.52 respectively, outperforming Instruct 4D-to-4D (IN4D) by 0.2–0.3 points on each. Subject consistency scores (DINO-based, from VBench) are 0.953, 0.968, and 0.988 for CTRL-D versus 0.933, 0.964, and 0.983 for IN4D. Total runtime including IP2P fine-tuning and scene optimization is 40–60 minutes, roughly half the ~2 hours required by IN4D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Instruct 4D-to-4D (IN4D) [43], CTRL-D wins on all three reported metrics across every scene: higher CLIP score (better text-prompt alignment), higher DINO-based subject consistency, and roughly 2× faster runtime (40–60 min vs. ~2 hours). The CLIP gains are modest (0.2–0.3 points), but the consistency advantage is most pronounced on the Portrait scene (0.953 vs. 0.933), where IN4D produces visibly inconsistent hair colors across frames. Qualitatively, CTRL-D also achieves precise local edits (e.g., changing only a cat's color without altering the background), whereas IN4D modifies unrelated regions. In a separate qualitative comparison with AnyV2V [31] on monocular video editing, CTRL-D produces sharper, more temporally consistent results, while AnyV2V outputs are blurry and degrade further as video length increases; CTRL-D also supports multi-camera scenes where AnyV2V struggles with cross-view consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the deformable 3D Gaussian Splatting framework (Yang et al. 2024 for monocular, Wu et al. 2024 for multi-camera) as its scene representation, and on InstructPix2Pix as its editing backbone. The key departure from prior dynamic editing methods (IN4D, Control4D) is that instead of using a fixed pre-trained diffusion model as a black-box editor, the authors personalize IP2P from a single edited reference image pair, effectively 'teaching' the model the specific edit and its spatial extent. The iterative dataset update strategy is adapted from Instruct-NeRF2NeRF, but augmented with an edited image buffer and a two-stage optimization (canonical-space densification followed by joint deformation+Gaussian refinement) that is specific to the 3DGS representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing dynamic 3D scene editing to a single 2D image edit, the framework makes the task significantly more accessible—users can employ any off-the-shelf 2D editing tool (SDEdit, BlendedDiffusion, MimicBrush, NNST) without needing to design 3D-specific editing pipelines. The remaining limitations are inherited: IP2P still struggles with adding complex content to empty regions (3D-inconsistent results), and the method depends on the quality of the underlying dynamic Gaussian reconstruction. Open questions include whether the one-shot personalization generalizes to multi-object or temporally-varying edits, and whether the approach scales to longer sequences or more complex deformations beyond the object-centric scenes tested here.
