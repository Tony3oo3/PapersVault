## Problem

Diffusion-based low-light image enhancement (LLIE) requires many iterative denoising steps, creating a computational bottleneck for edge deployment. Existing acceleration methods—whether training-free samplers or training-based distillation—suffer significant performance degradation when reducing steps below 10, and no prior work has distilled a diffusion model specifically for LLIE. The paper asks whether a distilled student can actually surpass the multi-step teacher, and identifies two root causes of degradation: fitting errors in the teacher's score function and an inference gap between the Gaussian training flow and the deterministic LLIE task.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-light RGB image as input and produces one enhanced normal-light RGB image. The low-light image serves as the conditioning signal throughout the diffusion process, and a reflectance estimate is derived from it via Retinex decomposition (illumination from the max channel, noise from a non-learning-based denoiser) to guide trajectory refinement. The distilled model operates in 2, 4, or 8 NFE steps, with the 2-step variant achieving 13.1 FPS and 0.076 s per image on the test hardware.

## Method

ReDDiT distills a multi-step teacher diffusion model into a 2/4/8-step student via trajectory matching, where the student's trajectory decoder (Eq. 3) is trained to match a second-order teacher trajectory (Eq. 4). The key innovation is the RATR module, which refines the teacher trajectory by (1) applying a scaling parameter ω to linearly extrapolate the score function difference term, mitigating fitting errors, and (2) shifting the residual target from pure Gaussian noise to a reflectance-aware space defined by x̃₀ = (y − z′)/h′, closing the inference gap. By Corollary 1, this refinement reduces to a simple convex combination: x̃_{s,u,t}^η = ω·x_{s,u,t}^η + (1−ω)·x̃_s. The total training loss combines the trajectory distillation loss with pixel-space L2 and VGG perceptual losses, providing direct supervision from ground-truth clean images.

## Results

On LOLv2-real, ReDDiT-4 achieves a new SOTA PSNR of 31.250 dB and LPIPS of 0.038, surpassing the previous best diffusion-based method WCDM (30.461 dB, 0.070 LPIPS) by a large margin. On LOLv2-synthetic, ReDDiT-8 reaches 30.166 dB PSNR and 0.028 LPIPS, outperforming GSAD (28.670 dB, 0.047 LPIPS) and WCDM (29.852 dB, 0.077 LPIPS). Remarkably, even the 2-step variant (ReDDiT-2) achieves 30.613 dB PSNR on LOLv2-real, matching or exceeding the 10-step WCDM and GSAD methods. On unpaired real-world datasets (DICM, LIME, MEF, NPE, VV), ReDDiT attains the best NIQE scores across all five benchmarks (e.g., 3.00 on VV vs. 3.18 for GSAD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReDDiT outperforms all 22 baselines on the primary paired datasets. Against the strongest non-diffusion baseline RetinexFormer (ICCV'23), ReDDiT-8 gains +0.91 dB PSNR on LOLv1 (28.090 vs. 27.180) and +1.13 dB on LOLv2-synthetic (30.166 vs. 29.040). Against the strongest diffusion-based baselines, ReDDiT-4 surpasses WCDM (10-step) by +0.79 dB PSNR on LOLv2-real (31.250 vs. 30.461) and by +0.31 dB on LOLv2-synthetic (30.034 vs. 29.852), while using only 4 steps instead of 10. The LPIPS advantage is particularly striking: ReDDiT-8 achieves 0.028 on LOLv2-synthetic versus 0.047 for GSAD and 0.077 for WCDM, indicating substantially better perceptual quality. On the unpaired real-world benchmarks (DICM, LIME, MEF, NPE, VV), ReDDiT achieves the lowest NIQE across all five datasets (e.g., 3.00 on VV vs. 3.18 for GSAD, 3.24 on NPE vs. 3.42 for EnlightenGAN), confirming strong generalization. The 2-step variant remains competitive with 10-step methods on LOLv2-real (30.613 dB vs. WCDM's 30.461 dB), demonstrating that the distillation framework preserves quality even at extreme compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: diffusion-based LLIE (Diff-Retinex, PyDiff, WCDM, GSAD) and diffusion model acceleration (progressive distillation, consistency distillation). Prior diffusion-based LLIE methods focus on how to condition the low-light image within the diffusion framework but largely neglect the efficiency of iterative sampling, while prior acceleration methods target general generative tasks and have not been adapted to the deterministic nature of image restoration. ReDDiT departs from both by providing the first trajectory-distillation framework specifically designed for LLIE, grounded in a theoretical decomposition of performance degradation into fitting errors and an inference gap between the Gaussian training flow and the deterministic restoration objective. The RATR module, which shifts the sampling trajectory into a reflectance-aware residual space, is the key conceptual departure from vanilla distillation applied to generative tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for edge deployment: a 2-step diffusion model that matches or exceeds 10-step baselines on LLIE directly addresses the latency constraints of mobile and surveillance applications. The theoretical framing—identifying fitting error and inference gap as the two distinct sources of degradation—provides a reusable diagnostic lens for other deterministic restoration tasks (denoising, deblurring) where naive distillation from a Gaussian-trained teacher is expected to underperform. Open questions include whether single-step restoration can be made competitive (the paper acknowledges it is not yet optimal), whether the reflectance-aware residual space generalizes beyond Retinex-based decomposition, and whether a lightweight denoising network could further reduce the 17.43M parameter footprint.
