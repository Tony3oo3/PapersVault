## Problem

Existing online video assistants like LIVE sacrifice response accuracy for real-time efficiency by processing low-frame-rate videos with coarse-grained, fixed-token visual features. This leads to three coupled failures: imprecise response timing due to insufficient temporal resolution, imprecise response content due to uniform token allocation that ignores first-person interaction regions, and inefficient training/inference from uniformly expanding tokens across all frames when only keyframes need fine-grained detail.

## Input / Output

The system continuously ingests a first-person video stream at 8 FPS (grouped into 4-frame windows for EgoVLPv2 encoding) and 2 FPS (for SigLIP encoding), interleaved with user text queries. For each incoming frame it produces a binary response-timing decision (predict EOS or not), and when a response is warranted it autoregressively generates a text answer. The 8 FPS rate is four times higher than LIVE's 2 FPS, enabling capture of inter-frame perspective and action variations in real time.

## Method

LION-FS decouples response determination (Fast Path) from response generation (Slow Path) in a two-stage optimization. The Fast Path employs a Token Aggregation Router that uses the SigLIP CLS token as visual guidance to generate adaptive per-token weights, fusing general spatial features (2 FPS, SigLIP) with dense egocentric temporal features (8 FPS, EgoVLPv2) without increasing token count, followed by a Token Dropping Router that adaptively discards redundant visual tokens at selected transformer layers via learned routing weights to sparsify LLM decoding. The Slow Path is training-free: at the keyframe where a response is triggered, it applies global uniform augmentation (dividing the frame into 4 grids, each 3×3 pooled, yielding Grid Tokens) and local adaptive augmentation (Faster R-CNN hand/object detection with bounding-box pooling yielding Box Tokens), then injects these into a Multimodal Thinking Template that replaces the standard 'Assistant:' prompt to guide more precise, fine-grained generation. Training uses a combined streaming loss (EOS prediction) and language modeling loss, fine-tuning the LLM end-to-end on the Fast Path while the Slow Path requires no additional training.

## Results

On Ego-Exo4D Narration Validation, LION-FS achieves the best scores across all four metrics: LL-PPL 2.04, TimeDiff 0.74, Fluency 36.5%, and LM-Correctness 48.2%. On Ego4D Narration Validation, LION-FS attains LL-PPL 2.09, Fluency 46.1%, and LM-Correctness 52.4%, though it slightly underperforms on TimeDiff (2.15 vs. 2.04) due to the short average response length of 6.73 words. Additionally, LION-FS processes video streams at four times the frame rate of the baselines, achieving both superior efficacy and efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LION-FS is compared against two online video dialogue baselines: VideoLLM-online (LIVE) [5] and VideoLLM-MoD [59]. On Ego-Exo4D, LION-FS outperforms both baselines on all four metrics, with the largest gains in Fluency (+2.7–2.8 pp over LIVE, +2.7 pp over MoD) and LM-Correctness (+3.4 pp over LIVE, +2.9 pp over MoD). On Ego4D, LION-FS achieves substantially lower LL-PPL (2.09 vs. 2.40/2.41) and higher LM-Correctness (52.4% vs. 49.0%/48.9%), but is slightly worse on TimeDiff (2.15 vs. 2.04), which the authors attribute to the short average response length (6.73 words) on this dataset. Notably, LION-FS also processes video at 4× the frame rate of both baselines, making it the only method that simultaneously improves efficacy and efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

LION-FS builds directly on LIVE (VideoLLM-online) [5], which established the online video dialogue paradigm of streaming first-person video with autonomous response timing. It departs from LIVE's single-encoder, low-frame-rate design by introducing a dual-encoder architecture (SigLIP + EgoVLPv2) with a Token Aggregation Router for adaptive feature fusion, and adapts the Mixture-of-Depths mechanism [40] into a Token Dropping Router for sparse decoding. The 'Fast and Slow' cognitive framing, inspired by Kahneman's dual-process theory and prior works like SlowFast-LLaVA and FaST, is repurposed here to decouple lightweight response determination from heavier response generation, a structural choice not present in LIVE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Online video assistants for smart glasses and AR headsets are a near-term practical need, and LION-FS's 4× frame-rate improvement with maintained or improved accuracy makes real-time egocentric dialogue more feasible on edge hardware. However, evaluation is limited to narration-style tasks on Ego4D and Ego-Exo4D, leaving open whether the fast-slow decoupling and multi-granularity augmentation generalize to more complex interactive tasks (e.g., multi-turn Q&A, spatial reasoning, or long-horizon planning). The reliance on a separate Faster R-CNN detector for Box Tokens also raises questions about robustness in cluttered or novel egocentric scenes, and the modest absolute gains (e.g., ~3–4% LM-Correctness) suggest the task itself may be approaching a ceiling with current evaluation protocols.
