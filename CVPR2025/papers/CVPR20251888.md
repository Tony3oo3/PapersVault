## Problem

4D Gaussian Splatting methods for immersive video face a fundamental trade-off: deformation-based approaches (e.g., 4DGaussian, E-D3DGS) are compact but lack the capacity to model fast motion, while spacetime Gaussian methods (e.g., STG, 4DGS) capture complex dynamics but require large storage and lack temporal correspondence between primitives, preventing efficient compression. No existing method simultaneously achieves high-quality dynamic novel view synthesis and a small, codec-friendly representation suitable for real-world streaming.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view video sequences (18–31 cameras at 1080p or half-resolution, 65–300 frames) and initializes from COLMAP sparse point clouds of the first frame. It produces a compressed 4D Gaussian representation organized as two 2D video sequences—a time-independent video (anchor positions, offsets, scales, features) and a time-dependent feature-stream video—that can be encoded with standard codecs (VVC, HEVC) or an end-to-end rANS entropy coder, yielding ~30 Mbps for 1080p 6-DoF video. Decoding completes in under one second for a two-second clip on an RTX 4090, enabling real-time rendering at 60+ FPS.

## Method

GIFStream augments a Scaffold-GS-style anchor-and-MLP architecture with per-anchor time-dependent feature streams that are motion-adaptive: a learnable gate M_de drives the stream toward zero for static anchors, making the representation sparse (only ~30% of anchors retain streams in challenging scenes). Two small MLP heads decode each anchor at timestamp t—one predicting Gaussian attributes (opacity, scale, rotation, color) and one predicting SE(3) motion, with KNN-based feature aggregation to enforce local smoothness and a learnable M_knn factor to blend coarse and fine motion. For compression, anchors are sorted into a 2D grid and their parameters are packed into two video sequences; the feature-stream video is further pruned by discarding zeroed streams and re-scanned to a smaller resolution. Training combines a rendering loss with quantization-aware training (STE), an auto-regressive entropy-estimation network that predicts per-frame Gaussian distributions conditioned on k previous frames, and regularization terms for sparsity, temporal consistency, and spatial smoothness, yielding an end-to-end rate-distortion optimized representation.

## Results

GIFStream achieves the smallest storage across all three datasets (7 MB on MPEG, 10 MB on Neur3D, 12.6 MB on Panoptic Sports) while delivering the highest PSNR on the two most challenging datasets: 30.72 dB on MPEG (vs. 30.50 for 4DGS and 29.62 for STG) and 29.50 dB on Panoptic Sports (vs. 28.68 for 4DGS and 25.09 for STG). On Neur3D, GIFStream reaches 31.75 dB PSNR at 10 MB, slightly below STG (32.05 dB) and 4DGS (32.01 dB) which require ~200 MB. Rendering speed is 70–100 FPS on an RTX 4090, and the overall bitrate for 1080p immersive video is approximately 30 Mbps, comparable to 4K 2D video.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GIFStream outperforms all deformation-based baselines (4DGaussian, E-D3DGS) on every dataset in both PSNR and storage: on MPEG it gains +1.59 dB over 4DGaussian and +0.78 dB over E-D3DGS while using 15–19× less storage. Against the 4D Gaussian Splatting methods (4DGS, STG), GIFStream achieves comparable or higher PSNR on the dynamic-heavy datasets (MPEG: +0.22 dB over 4DGS, +1.10 dB over STG; Panoptic Sports: +0.82 dB over 4DGS, +4.41 dB over STG) at a fraction of the storage (7–12.6 MB vs. 80–974 MB). On Neur3D, STG and 4DGS marginally lead in PSNR (32.05/32.01 vs. 31.75) but at 20× the storage cost. Compared to the closest compression-focused baseline CSTG+PP, GIFStream wins on PSNR across all three datasets (31.75 vs. 31.69, 29.50 vs. 26.13, 30.72 vs. 29.48) while using less or comparable storage. Rendering FPS is lower than 4DGS (80–200 FPS) and STG (93–270 FPS) due to deformation computation, but remains above 60 FPS on consumer hardware.

## Relation to Prior Work

GIFStream sits at the intersection of two lines of 4D Gaussian work: deformation-based methods (4DGaussian, E-D3DGS) that store a canonical 3D scene plus a lightweight deformation field, and 4D Gaussian Splatting methods (4DGS, STG) that distribute primitives across spacetime for high motion capacity at the cost of large storage and absent temporal correspondence. The paper's core architectural move is to augment the deformation-based paradigm with per-anchor time-dependent feature streams (inspired by NeRFPlayer's decomposed fields), making the representation motion-adaptive and sparse while preserving the temporal alignment that enables efficient video-style compression. On the compression side, it extends the quantization-aware training and entropy-coding pipeline established for static 3DGS (CSTG, HAC) to the dynamic setting by introducing an auto-regressive entropy model over the feature-stream video, a step not previously applied to 4D Gaussian representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that GIFStream brings 1080p 6-DoF immersive video to a ~30 Mbps bitrate—on par with 4K 2D streaming—while maintaining real-time rendering (>60 FPS) on a consumer RTX 4090, a milestone for making free-viewpoint video feasible in bandwidth-constrained settings. The motion-adaptive sparsity (only ~30% of anchors retain feature streams in the hardest scene, ~0.3% in easier ones) suggests the representation scales gracefully to content with varying dynamism. Open questions include how the method handles sequences much longer than the 65-frame GOPs tested, whether the auto-regressive entropy model degrades over long temporal horizons, and whether the approach extends to unbounded or streaming scenarios where the canonical space itself changes.
