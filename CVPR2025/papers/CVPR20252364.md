## Problem

Existing multimodal OOD detection methods, particularly the Agree-to-Disagree (A2D) framework, uniformly amplify prediction discrepancies across all training samples under the assumption that intra-class samples are perfectly cohesive. This assumption breaks down in practice: when discrepancy intensification is applied indiscriminately to class-center samples that already exhibit consistent cross-modal predictions, it disrupts their coherence and degrades ID accuracy. The paper identifies this intra-class variability as an overlooked challenge and asks how to adaptively modulate discrepancy amplification so that only genuinely uncertain or outlying samples receive stronger OOD signals.

## Input / Output

The method consumes M modalities per sample (video clips, optical flow, and optionally audio), each processed by a frozen SlowFast backbone pretrained on Kinetics-600 to produce modality-specific feature embeddings. At inference, the model outputs both a class label (via a shared classifier over the joint multi-modal representation) and an ID/OOD decision derived from the joint probability distribution across all modalities combined with individual per-modality prediction probabilities. The framework is plug-and-play: it wraps around any existing base OOD scoring function (MSP, Energy, Mahalanobis, ReAct, VIM, etc.) without modifying the base method's architecture.

## Method

DPU is a three-stage training framework. First, Cohesive-Separate Contrastive Training (CSCT) constructs a representation space using an invariant-risk-minimization objective that combines robust marginal contrastive learning (InfoNCE with an angular margin to sharpen inter-class boundaries) with a variance-minimization regularizer that enforces consistent loss values within each class's positive set. Second, Dynamic Prototype Approximation (DPA) maintains per-class, per-modality prototype vectors updated via a moving average whose rate is inversely proportional to the observed batch variance, so that low-variance (cohesive) batches update prototypes quickly while high-variance batches slow the update to limit outlier influence. Third, Pro-ratio Discrepancy Intensification (PDI) scales the Hellinger-distance-based cross-modal prediction discrepancy for each sample by a sigmoid of its cosine similarity to its class prototype, so that class-center samples receive minimal intensification while peripheral samples receive stronger OOD pressure; an adaptive outlier synthesis step further trains the model by fusing prototypes from different classes and maximizing the resulting inter-modality discrepancy plus entropy. The total loss is a weighted sum of the base cross-entropy, CSCT, PDI, and outlier-synthesis terms, and the entire framework is model-agnostic and compatible with nine different base OOD scoring methods.

## Results

On Near-OOD detection (Table 1), DPU consistently improves FPR95, AUROC, and ID ACC across all nine base OOD methods and four datasets. For example, on HMDB51 with Energy, FPR95 drops from 43.36 (base) to 35.07 (DPU), AUROC rises from 87.46 to 89.52, and ID ACC increases from 89.32 to 92.16. On Far-OOD detection (Table 2), improvements are even more dramatic: VIM on Kinetics600 reduces FPR95 from 13.68 to 0.01 (an ~80% relative reduction), and GEN on Kinetics600 drops FPR95 from 41.51 to 7.18. Across all settings, DPU also raises ID ACC by 2–3 points (e.g., 89.32→92.16 on HMDB51, 80.11→81.07 on Kinetics600), confirming that adaptive discrepancy intensification does not sacrifice in-distribution accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPU is evaluated as a plug-and-play enhancement on top of nine base OOD methods (MSP, Energy, Maxlogit, Mahalanobis, ReAct, ASH, GEN, KNN, VIM) spanning probability, logit, feature, and hybrid spaces, with the SOTA multimodal baselines being A2D [11] and AN (A2D+NP-Mix) [11]. DPU outperforms both A2D and AN on nearly every metric across all four Near-OOD datasets and all nine base methods; for instance, on HMDB51 with MSP, DPU achieves FPR95 34.20 vs. A2D's 38.78 and AN's 33.99, while also raising ID ACC to 92.16 (vs. 90.64 and 89.98). The largest Far-OOD gains appear with VIM on Kinetics600 (FPR95: 0.01 vs. 9.24 for AN, a >99% relative reduction) and GEN on Kinetics600 (FPR95: 7.18 vs. 25.66 for AN). DPU also consistently improves ID ACC by ~2–3 points over all baselines (e.g., 92.16 vs. 90.64 on HMDB51), confirming that adaptive, prototype-aware discrepancy intensification avoids the ID accuracy degradation caused by uniform amplification. The only minor exceptions are a few AUROC values on UCF101 where AN marginally edges DPU (e.g., 98.73 vs. 98.56 for VIM), but DPU recovers on FPR95 and ID ACC in those cases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MultiOOD (Dong et al., NeurIPS 2024), which introduced the first multimodal OOD benchmark and the Agree-to-Disagree (A2D) strategy that uniformly amplifies cross-modality prediction discrepancies to separate ID from OOD samples. The key conceptual departure is identifying that A2D's implicit assumption of perfect intra-class cohesion is violated in practice: uniformly intensifying discrepancies on class-center samples disrupts their consistent multi-modal predictions and degrades ID accuracy. DPU addresses this by borrowing ideas from Invariant Risk Minimization (Arjovsky et al.) for representation learning, contrastive learning (InfoNCE) for class structure, and prototype learning for adaptive, sample-specific discrepancy scaling, all wrapped as a plug-and-play module over nine existing OOD detection methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that intra-class variability undermines uniform discrepancy amplification is practically important: real-world multimodal data (video, flow, audio) rarely exhibits the perfect class cohesion assumed by prior multimodal OOD work, so DPU's adaptive approach is more aligned with deployment conditions. The plug-and-play design means practitioners can adopt DPU without re-architecting their OOD pipeline, and the consistent gains across nine base methods and five datasets suggest broad applicability. However, the paper acknowledges scalability concerns for larger or more complex multimodal settings, and the headline 'up to 80%' Far-OOD improvement comes from a single best-case (VIM on Kinetics600 FPR95), while other configurations show more modest or even mixed results (e.g., ASH+DPU AUROC drops below AN on Kinetics600). Open questions include whether the prototype-based approach generalizes to cross-domain OOD detection and whether it remains effective under real-time constraints in safety-critical applications.
