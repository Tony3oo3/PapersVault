## Problem

3D scene understanding lacks the web-scale training data that drives progress in language and vision, because real-world 3D annotations are labor-intensive and existing datasets (ScanNet, S3DIS) are orders of magnitude smaller than image corpora. This paper asks whether large-scale pre-training with automatically generated labels can reproduce the scaling trends seen in language and image tasks, and whether real-world auto-labeled data outperforms synthetic data for 3D segmentation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250836/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The LabelMakerV2 pipeline consumes RGB-D video trajectories from ARKitScenes (iPad LiDAR scans) along with camera poses and IMU-derived sky directions, and produces per-point 3D semantic labels in a 186-class WordNet label space. 3D meshes are downsampled to 2 cm voxel resolution with preserved normals. The pipeline also accepts arbitrary scenes captured via the iOS Scanner 3D app, enabling annotation of consumer iPhone recordings without manual intervention.

## Method

LabelMakerV2 extends the original LabelMaker pipeline with three key modifications: (1) integration of Grounded-SAM (Grounding DINO + SAM) as an additional ensemble member mapped into the unified WordNet label space, (2) gravity alignment that rotates each 2D frame by k·π/2 based on the IMU sky direction before inference and rotates predictions back, correcting orientation-dependent misclassifications, and (3) omission of the NeuS implicit-surface lifting step in favor of per-point voting for computational tractability at scale. The pipeline processes all 5,019 ARKitScenes scenes (48,000 GPU-hours on RTX 3090s) to produce the largest real-world 3D semantic dataset. The resulting ARKit LabelMaker dataset is then used to pre-train MinkowskiNet and PTv3, which are fine-tuned or jointly trained (via PPT) on ScanNet/ScanNet200/ScanNet++/Structured3D, demonstrating that auto-labeled real-world data yields greater per-point gains than equivalent synthetic data and outperforms both self-supervised pre-training (PonderV2) and data augmentation (Mix3D).

## Results

On ScanNet200, PTv3+PPT trained with ARKit LabelMaker achieves 41.4% test mIoU, surpassing the prior SOTA PTv3+PPT (39.3%) by 2.1 points, with a particularly large +5.5% gain on tail classes (27.1% vs 21.6% mIoU). On ScanNet, the same model reaches 79.8% test mIoU (vs 79.4% for PTv3+PPT without ARKit LabelMaker). For MinkowskiNet, pre-training on ARKit LabelMaker^SN200 and fine-tuning on ScanNet yields 74.1% val mIoU, outperforming both Mix3D (73.6%) and PonderV2 self-supervised pre-training (73.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PTv3+PPT (the prior SOTA multi-dataset joint training), adding ARKit LabelMaker improves test mIoU from 39.3% to 41.4% on ScanNet200, with the largest gain on tail classes (+5.5% mIoU: 27.1% vs 21.6%). Against PTv3 vanilla (37.8% test mIoU), fine-tuning after ARKit LabelMaker pre-training yields 38.4%, a modest +0.6% gain. Against MinkowskiNet vanilla (25.3% test mIoU), pre-training on ARKit LabelMaker^SN200 gives 27.4%, a +2.1% improvement. On ScanNet, the proposed PTv3+PPT (79.8% test mIoU) beats Mix3D (78.1%) and PonderV2 self-supervised pre-training (73.5% val mIoU), confirming that large-scale auto-labeled real-world data outperforms both data augmentation and unsupervised feature learning. In zero-shot evaluation on Matterport3D, PTv3+PPT with ARKit LabelMaker (43.8% mIoU Top-40) surpasses OpenScene (50.9% mAcc but no mIoU reported) and fully supervised MinkowskiNet trained directly on Matterport3D (50.8% mAcc), demonstrating strong generalization. The one area where gains are inconsistent is MinkowskiNet tail-class performance on ScanNet200, where fine-tuning slightly decreases tail mIoU (9.4% vs 10.2% vanilla), suggesting the benefit is architecture-dependent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LabelMaker automatic annotation pipeline [37] into LabelMakerV2 by integrating Grounded-SAM as an additional ensemble member, adding gravity alignment to correct orientation errors in ARKitScenes, and removing the computationally expensive NeuS lifting step. It then applies this pipeline to the full ARKitScenes dataset [4], which previously only provided bounding-box annotations, to produce the largest real-world 3D per-point semantic segmentation dataset (4,471 training scenes, 186 classes). Conceptually, the work draws a direct analogy to how ImageNet-scale data enabled breakthroughs in 2D vision and how web-scale text enabled GPT: the hypothesis is that 3D segmentation has been bottlenecked by data volume rather than architecture, and that auto-generated labels at sufficient scale can close this gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset is likely to become a standard pre-training resource for 3D semantic segmentation, given its 3× scale advantage over prior real-world datasets and its demonstrated ability to improve both CNN (MinkowskiNet) and transformer (PTv3) architectures. The empirical finding that auto-labeled real-world data outperforms both synthetic data (Structured3D) and self-supervised pre-training (PonderV2) provides concrete guidance for future data-scaling efforts in 3D vision. Key limitations include the inherent noise in automatic labels (risk of systematic errors for safety-critical use), the exclusion of 20 scenes due to missing poses, the omission of 2D lifting, and the fact that the total data volume remains orders of magnitude below image-domain scales. Open questions include whether the observed log-linear mIoU-vs-data-size trend persists at larger scales and whether more efficient 2D lifting methods could unlock additional gains.
