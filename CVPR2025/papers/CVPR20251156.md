## Problem

Standard neural network initializations (Xavier, Kaiming) are derived under assumptions specific to tanh or ReLU activations, making them inapplicable to the diverse activation functions used in Implicit Neural Representations (sine, Gaussian, sinc, wavelet). Without proper initialization, INRs suffer from unstable preactivation and gradient variances across layers, severely degrading their ability to reconstruct high-frequency signals in images, 3D shapes, and audio.

## Input / Output

The method takes an activation function f, the network architecture (layer widths M_i), and a target preactivation variance σ_p² as inputs, and produces per-layer weight distribution parameters (specifically the variance σ²(W_i) for each layer). A key design choice is that σ_p² is a free hyperparameter rather than being fixed to 1 as in prior work, and activation statistics (mean and variance of f(z) for z ~ N(0, σ_p²)) are estimated via Monte Carlo sampling of 1M points, taking only ~12 ms. The resulting initialization is then applied to an 8-layer, 128-unit MLP that maps coordinate inputs to signal values for reconstruction tasks.

## Method

The paper proves (Prop 3.1) that preactivations converge to a Gaussian distribution by the Central Limit Theorem, reducing the initialization problem to matching variances. It derives a forward-pass condition σ²(W_i) = σ_p² / [M_i(μ²(x_i) + σ²(x_i))] and a backward-pass condition M_{i+1}σ²(W_i)(μ²(f'(z_i)) + σ²(f'(z_i))) = 1, then uses σ_p² as an additional degree of freedom to jointly satisfy both (Eq. 13), unlike Xavier/Kaiming which must average or choose one. Activation statistics are computed via efficient Monte Carlo estimation (1M samples from N(0, σ_p²)) when analytical derivation is intractable, as with multimodal activations like sinc. The framework generalizes Xavier and Kaiming as special cases and introduces a well-defined 'gain' parameter gain²(f, σ_p) = σ_p² / (E[f(z)]² + Var[f(z)]) that replaces PyTorch's ad-hoc gain heuristic.

## Results

On image reconstruction (KODIM, PSNR), Gaussian + Our init achieves 79.73 ± 11.85, substantially outperforming SIREN + SIREN init (55.77 ± 2.21) and all random-initialization baselines (≤32.11). On 3D SDF reconstruction (BACON, IoU), Gaussian + Our init reaches 0.9697 ± 0.0148, closely matching SIREN + SIREN init (0.9791) while far exceeding random-init baselines (≤0.8628). On audio reconstruction (MSE ×10⁻³), Gaussian + Our init achieves 0.48/1.49/0.24 across three signals, a ~13× improvement over Gaussian + random init (6.57/5.10/1.35), though SIREN + SIREN init remains stronger (0.005/0.37/0.018).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SIREN + SIREN init (the strongest baseline), the proposed method wins decisively on image reconstruction (79.73 vs. 55.77 PSNR for Gaussian; 71.44 vs. 55.77 for sinc) but is slightly behind on 3D SDF (0.9697 vs. 0.9791 IoU) and audio (0.48 vs. 0.005 MSE on Bach). Against random normal initialization, the proposed method achieves dramatic gains across all three tasks: roughly 2.6× PSNR improvement on images, 1.1–1.3× IoU on SDFs, and 5–13× MSE reduction on audio. Against Xavier and Kaiming initializations (Table 8), the proposed method with Gaussian activation and σ_p=0.15 achieves 79.73 PSNR, outperforming Xavier (62.60) and Kaiming (79.70) when their respective activation parameters are tuned; with fixed σ_a=0.05, the gap is even larger (79.73 vs. 14.52 Xavier, 9.97 Kaiming). The sinc activation with the proposed MC init (71.44 PSNR) substantially outperforms the same activation with random init (32.11), confirming that the initialization is the critical factor rather than the activation choice alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes SIREN's sine-specific initialization to arbitrary activation functions, providing a formal proof (via the Central Limit Theorem with Lindeberg's condition) where prior work like Kumar [10] only motivated independence informally. It subsumes Xavier and Kaiming initialization as special cases by properly analyzing both forward and backward pass variance conditions simultaneously, and replaces PyTorch's ill-defined 'gain' heuristic with a well-defined expression tied to the preactivation variance. Where SIREN and Kumar relied on Taylor approximations or restrictive unit-variance assumptions, this work uses Monte Carlo estimation of activation statistics, which is both more accurate and computationally trivial (milliseconds).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic performance gaps demonstrated (e.g., SIREN jumping from 10.7 to 55.8 PSNR on images solely from proper initialization) underscore that initialization is a first-order design choice for INRs, not a minor hyperparameter. The backward-pass condition (Eq. 13) provides a principled, activation-dependent starting point for the preactivation variance, reducing the hyperparameter search to a small local line search rather than a full grid. Open questions include whether the task-specific optimal σ_p (which differs between images and SDFs even for identical architectures) can be predicted from data properties, and how the framework behaves for very deep networks where the backward condition becomes critical. The method's generality to any activation function makes it a practical tool for the growing ecosystem of INR activations (Gaussian, sinc, wavelet, variable-periodic).
