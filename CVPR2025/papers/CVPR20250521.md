## Problem

Generating physically plausible intermediate shapes between non-rigidly deformed shapes is difficult with unstructured data such as point clouds, where temporal consistency is absent and topology may change. Existing mesh-based methods require dense exact correspondences and fixed topology, while prior neural implicit approaches either demand ground-truth correspondences, user-defined handle points, or fail on large, non-isometric, or partial deformations. No prior method handles noisy real-world point clouds with only sparse, imprecise correspondences.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse temporal sequence of point clouds (or meshes) together with estimated, incomplete, and noisy correspondences obtained from an unsupervised functional-maps matching module; no full point-to-point correspondence or intermediate-shape supervision is required. It produces a continuous time-varying signed distance function for intermediate surfaces, a velocity field that deforms input points to target positions, and can extrapolate beyond the training time interval [0,1]. The approach operates directly on unordered point clouds at arbitrary resolution, including noisy Kinect acquisitions and partial shapes, without requiring re-meshing or topological alignment.

## Method

4Deform jointly optimizes two neural networks—an Implicit Net producing a time-varying SDF and a Velocity Net producing a 3D velocity field—linked by a modified level-set equation that incorporates an Eikonal regularizer to avoid reinitialization. Two novel physical losses are introduced: a distortion loss based on the deviatoric (trace-free) part of the rate-of-deformation tensor to penalize shear and shape change, and a stretching loss that projects the deformation gradient onto the tangent plane (defined via the implicit field's normal) to penalize surface stretching. An AutoDecoder architecture with per-shape trainable latent vectors enables sequence-level training and temporal extrapolation. Correspondences are estimated via an unsupervised spectral shape-matching module, and the total training objective combines the level-set loss, spatial smoothness, volume preservation, distortion, stretching, and correspondence-matching terms without any intermediate-shape ground truth.

## Results

On the 4D-DRESS human dataset (isometric), 4Deform achieves CD=0.269×10⁻⁴, HD=0.046×10⁻², and SAσ=0.018×10 in the Pairs S/S setting, outperforming the closest baseline [45] (CD=0.279, HD=0.047, SAσ=0.023) and substantially beating NISE (CD=6.588) and LipMLP (CD=14.99). In the sequence setting (Seq. S/S), 4Deform achieves CD=0.327×10⁻⁴ and HD=0.038×10⁻², while LIMP degrades severely (CD=136.787). On real-world Kinect data (Pairs S/R), 4Deform achieves P-RMSE=0.014×10, nearly 40% lower than [45] (0.024), and is the only method besides [45] that can directly deform untrained real scans.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the most relevant baseline [45] (optimization-based implicit deformation), 4Deform wins on CD and SAσ in Pairs S/S (0.269 vs 0.279; 0.018 vs 0.023) and on CD and P-RMSE in Pairs S/R (0.390 vs 0.548; 0.014 vs 0.024), though it slightly loses on HD in the real-data setting (0.101 vs 0.083). Against NFGP [55], 4Deform achieves comparable CD (0.269 vs 0.272) but substantially better SAσ (0.018 vs 0.075), while NFGP has a lower HD (0.025 vs 0.046); however, NFGP cannot handle sequences or real data. Against NISE [38] and LipMLP [32], 4Deform dominates by orders of magnitude on CD (0.269 vs 6.588 and 14.99) and HD (0.046 vs 2.167 and 2.125), as these latent-space methods lack physical constraints. Against LIMP [13], the only mesh-based sequence method, 4Deform achieves dramatically lower CD in the sequence setting (0.327 vs 136.787) and better SAσ (0.063 vs 0.155), while also being the only method that generalizes to untrained real-world point clouds. On the SMAL animal dataset (Table 3), 4Deform outperforms all baselines on every metric, with the largest gap over [45] on HD (0.221 vs 0.626) and P-RMSE (0.061 vs 0.081).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of neural implicit surface deformation initiated by [45] (Sang et al., ICLR 2025), which first linked a velocity field to an implicit SDF via the modified level-set equation, by adding a correspondence estimation module (functional maps), an AutoDecoder architecture for temporal sequences, and two new continuum-mechanics-derived losses (distortion and stretching) that enforce physical plausibility without requiring mesh connectivity. It departs from mesh-based interpolation methods (SMS, Neuromorph, LIMP) that are limited to fixed topology and dense correspondences, and from latent-space NIR methods (LipMLP, NISE) that lack physical constraints, by operating directly in Euclidean space with a continuous velocity field. The use of unsupervised spectral shape matching for correspondence estimation, rather than ground-truth point-to-point maps, is a practical departure that enables application to real-world noisy and partial point clouds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that physically constrained implicit deformation can generalize from clean registered shapes to real-world Kinect point clouds and 40k-vertex scans, enabling new applications such as temporal super-resolution of human-object interaction sequences and extrapolation of motion beyond observed keyframes. However, the authors acknowledge that the current model does not capture mechanical joints, fluid dynamics, or independent deformations of body vs. clothing, limiting its applicability to more complex articulated or multi-component scenes. The reliance on a per-pair or per-sequence training protocol (8–10 min per pair) also constrains scalability to very long or diverse sequences, and the correspondence quality from functional maps remains a bottleneck for highly non-isometric or occluded shapes.
