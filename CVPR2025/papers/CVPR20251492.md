## Problem

Existing motion-language models align motions only with coarse-grained captions (a few words per sequence), leaving fine-grained tasks like understanding or controlling specific body-part movements over time unaddressed. Directly instruction-tuning an LLM on both coarse and detailed descriptions actually degrades performance (75.0% vs. 77.3% Top-3 retrieval), because the massive information in detailed texts (often >1000 tokens) distracts the model from capturing global motion semantics.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a flexible combination of inputs—coarse captions, detailed motion scripts (per-snippet body-part movement descriptions separated by <SEP> tokens), 3D motion sequences (263-dim, discretized into ≤50 VQ tokens), temporal intervals, and random motion segments—formulated via prompt templates. Outputs are generated autoregressively over a unified vocabulary (text + motion + special tokens) and can be a full 3D motion sequence, a coarse caption, a detailed motion script, or temporal boundary timestamps, depending on the instruction.

## Method

MG-MotionLLM pairs a frozen motion VQ-VAE (from T2M-GPT) that maps 3D motions to discrete tokens with a T5-based language model whose vocabulary is extended with motion-token indices and special tokens (<SEP>, <Motionless>, etc.). The key contribution is a two-stage training scheme: (1) Granularity-Synergy Pre-training over 28 tasks (12 coarse-grained + 16 newly designed fine-grained tasks such as temporal boundary localization and snippet-level captioning), where coarse tasks anchor global semantics for fine-grained ones and vice versa, and (2) Task-Specific Instruction Tuning to specialize on individual tasks. This mutual-reinforcement strategy resolves the coarse-vs-detailed conflict observed in direct instruction tuning, enabling a single model to handle text-to-motion, motion captioning, fine-grained motion editing, and motion localization across multiple granularities.

## Results

On the HumanML3D text-to-motion benchmark, MG-MotionLLM (T5-Base) achieves Top-3 R-Precision of 0.802, FID of 0.303, and Diversity of 9.960, surpassing the prior unified model MotionGPT (Top-3 0.778, FID 0.232) and approaching dedicated motion generators like MoMask (Top-3 0.807, FID 0.045). On motion-to-text, MG-MotionLLM attains Top-1 R-Precision of 0.592 and BERTScore of 36.7, outperforming MotionGPT (0.543, 32.4) and TM2T (0.516, 32.2). On the newly proposed motion-to-detailed-text task (FineMotion dataset), the Base model reaches sequence-level BERTScore of 52.3 and snippet-level BERTScore of 49.8, with the Large model improving snippet-level BERTScore to 52.2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among unified motion-language models, MG-MotionLLM clearly outperforms MotionGPT [11] on text-to-motion (Top-3: 0.802 vs. 0.778; FID: 0.303 vs. 0.232) and motion-to-text (Top-1: 0.592 vs. 0.543; BERTScore: 36.7 vs. 32.4), demonstrating that multi-granularity training yields stronger cross-modal alignment. Against dedicated motion generators, MG-MotionLLM trails MoMask [8] on FID (0.303 vs. 0.045) and Top-3 R-Precision (0.802 vs. 0.807), but leads on Diversity (9.960 vs. not reported) and MModality (2.125 vs. 1.241), indicating more diverse and semantically varied outputs. Compared to T2M-GPT [30] and FineMoGen [32], MG-MotionLLM achieves higher Top-3 R-Precision (0.802 vs. 0.775 and 0.784) and competitive FID, while also supporting motion understanding tasks that these methods cannot handle. On motion-to-text, MG-MotionLLM surpasses TM2T [7] by a wide margin on Top-1 (0.592 vs. 0.516) and BERTScore (36.7 vs. 32.2), though TM2T's lower Bleu@4 (7.00 vs. 8.06) is a minor exception. The paper also notes that the (Text+Detailed Text)-to-Motion task shows a Top-3 drop (0.779) relative to coarse-only text-to-motion (0.802), which the authors attribute to the R-Precision metric being computed on coarse-text embeddings that may not capture fine-grained motion details.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the motion-language LLM line initiated by MotionGPT [11] and MotionLLM [28], which aligned motions with coarse-grained captions using T5-based architectures and VQ-VAE tokenization (from T2M-GPT [30]). The key departure is the introduction of fine-grained 'motion scripts'—temporal sequences of body-part-movement descriptions—and a two-stage Granularity-Synergy Pre-training scheme that jointly trains 28 tasks spanning coarse and fine granularities to overcome the difficulty of directly mapping long detailed texts to short motion token sequences. Unlike prior fine-grained motion generation works (SemanticBoost, MotionScript, FineMoGen) that focus solely on generation, MG-MotionLLM unifies both comprehension and generation at multiple granularities within a single model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that multi-granularity tasks mutually reinforce each other, enabling a single model to handle novel applications like fine-grained motion editing, partial-sequence captioning, and motion localization via detailed text—capabilities absent in prior unified frameworks. However, the limited scale of HumanML3D (14,616 sequences) constrains model scaling, as the 770M large model underperforms the 220M base model on several tasks, raising open questions about whether the multi-granularity paradigm scales with larger motion datasets. The FineMotion dataset (420,968 snippet-level annotations) is a useful resource, but the overall SOTA margins over MotionGPT are modest, and the approach remains single-person and offline.
