## Problem

Unsupervised depth completion models suffer catastrophic forgetting when continually trained on sequences of non-stationary datasets, as updating all parameters to fit new domains degrades performance on previously seen ones. Existing continual learning strategies (regularization, rehearsal, architecture-based) either modify the full parameter set or require storing prior data, and no method had been proposed for unsupervised continual depth completion specifically.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251728/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image and a sparse depth map (obtained by projecting a sparse point cloud onto the image plane) along with camera intrinsics and relative poses, and outputs a dense depth map at full image resolution. Training is fully unsupervised, relying on photometric consistency, sparse depth consistency, and local smoothness losses rather than ground-truth depth. The prototype mechanism operates on latent feature tensors of shape h×w×c at the bottleneck and skip-connection layers of an encoder-decoder CNN, with separate prototype counts for the RGB and sparse-depth modalities.

## Method

ProtoDepth freezes the entire pretrained depth completion model and learns a lightweight, domain-specific prototype set per new dataset that adaptively biases latent features without altering original weights. Each prototype set comprises a global multiplicative prototype (a c-dimensional vector applied as a 1×1 depthwise convolution) modeling domain-wide scale shifts, and N local additive prototypes whose contribution is determined by scaled dot-product attention between frozen latent features (queries) and learned keys (prototypes projected through a learned matrix W with stop-gradient for decoupled optimization). For the domain-agnostic setting where test-time domain identity is unknown, the method learns a per-domain descriptor vector trained to maximize cosine similarity with in-domain sample descriptors (obtained via global average pooling of frozen bottleneck features) while maximizing separation from other domain descriptors, enabling prototype set selection at inference. The approach adds fewer than 5% parameters per domain, is architecture-agnostic (applicable to both CNNs and transformers), and achieves zero forgetting in the incremental setting while reducing forgetting by over 50% in the agnostic setting compared to baselines.

## Results

In the domain-incremental setting, ProtoDepth achieves 0% Average Forgetting across all models (VOICED, FusionNet, KBNet) and all four depth metrics (MAE, RMSE, iMAE, iRMSE) on both indoor (NYUv2→ScanNet→VOID) and outdoor (KITTI→Waymo→VKITTI) sequences, since the pretrained model is fully frozen. In the more challenging domain-agnostic setting, ProtoDepth-A reduces Average Forgetting by 52.2% (indoor) and 53.2% (outdoor) compared to the best baseline (Experience Replay), while also improving Average Performance by 4.26% and SPTO by 5.40% (indoor). For the incremental setting, ProtoDepth improves Average Performance by 5.15% and SPTO by 6.59% (indoor) and 6.88% and 6.94% (outdoor) over the best baseline, averaged across all models and metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProtoDepth (incremental) achieves zero forgetting by design, outperforming all three baselines (EWC, LwF, Replay) by 100% on Average Forgetting for every model and metric in both indoor and outdoor sequences. In the agnostic setting, ProtoDepth-A reduces Average Forgetting by 52.2% (indoor) and 53.2% (outdoor) relative to the best baseline, Experience Replay, which itself already outperforms EWC and LwF on forgetting. For example, on the outdoor VOICED sequence, Replay achieves 17.24% MAE forgetting while ProtoDepth-A achieves only 2.43%, and EWC/LwF suffer 555.9%/631.1% forgetting respectively. On Average Performance, ProtoDepth-A improves over the best baseline by 4.26% (indoor) and ProtoDepth by 5.15% (indoor) / 6.88% (outdoor), indicating that the prototype mechanism not only prevents forgetting but also yields better overall depth predictions. Notably, in the indoor agnostic setting, ProtoDepth-A occasionally outperforms ProtoDepth on individual metrics, suggesting that cross-domain prototype selection can enhance generalization when domain overlap exists, whereas in the outdoor setting the larger domain gaps make cross-domain selection less beneficial.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProtoDepth repurposes the prototype concept from few-shot learning and representation learning as a mechanism for domain-specific bias in continual learning, departing from the dominant regularization (EWC, LwF) and rehearsal (Experience Replay) paradigms that modify all model parameters. While prompt-based continual learning methods (L2P, DualPrompt) similarly freeze the backbone and learn small task-specific parameters, they operate in input/token space and are restricted to ViTs; ProtoDepth instead deploys prototypes in the latent feature space via attention-based selection, making it architecture-agnostic and applicable to the CNNs prevalent in unsupervised depth completion. The paper is the first method for the unsupervised continual depth completion task recently benchmarked by UnCLe [21], which had only adapted existing continual learning baselines without proposing a new approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By freezing the pretrained model entirely, ProtoDepth guarantees zero forgetting in the domain-incremental setting while adding less than 5% parameter overhead per domain, offering a practical and privacy-preserving alternative to rehearsal-based methods that require storing past data. The domain-agnostic extension via learned descriptors is a meaningful step toward realistic deployment where test-time domain identity is unknown, though the approach still requires knowing dataset boundaries to instantiate new prototype sets, limiting its applicability to truly online or streaming settings. The architecture-agnostic design suggests the prototype-as-selective-bias paradigm could transfer to other multimodal reconstruction tasks, but this remains unexplored. Open questions include handling small inter-domain gaps, intra-distributional shifts, and whether the descriptor-based selection mechanism scales gracefully as the number of domains grows.
