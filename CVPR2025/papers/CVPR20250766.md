## Problem

Intelligent agents lack the ability to understand and interact with articulated objects in complex 3D scenes, particularly for fine-grained functional affordances (e.g., handles, switches) and multi-part motion recovery. Prior methods rely on category-specific training, known camera poses, static object alignment between states, or assumptions about the number of movable parts, limiting generalization to real-world indoor environments with occlusions and unaligned geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250766/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view posed RGB images (and optionally depth maps) of the same scene captured in two different articulation states, plus a user prompt (text, point, or mask) for affordance querying. It outputs a 3D Gaussian Splatting scene per state with hierarchical semantic features embedded in each primitive, part-level segmentation labels, per-part articulation parameters (joint axis, angle, translation), affordance masks, and a fused 3D scene that merges both states to fill occluded geometry. Notably, camera poses are recovered via SfM rather than assumed known, and the number of articulated parts is not specified a priori.

## Method

IAAO is a three-stage framework built on 3D Gaussian Splatting. First, each scene state is reconstructed as 3DGS with hierarchical features distilled from MaskCLIP (instance- and part-level) and DINOv2 into low-dimensional latent codes on each Gaussian primitive, decoded back to 2D feature space during differentiable rasterization; SAM masks are clustered across views via a mask graph to produce 3D-consistent part labels. Second, affordance prediction queries the 3D Gaussians directly (no rendering) by comparing CLIP text embeddings against the feature field, while motion recovery estimates a global scene transformation (scale, rotation, translation) from static elements using GeoTransformer on high-opacity Gaussians, then refines per-part local transformations by matching projected 3D Gaussian points to 2D pixels via DINO feature similarity within part masks. Third, the two state reconstructions are merged using the estimated transformations to complete occluded geometry. The key differentiator is operating on explicit Gaussian primitives for efficient object/part-level interaction without implicit field rendering, combined with foundation-model features for category-agnostic generalization.

## Results

On the PARIS two-part dataset (Table 1), IAAO achieves the best or near-best articulation accuracy across all 8 synthetic objects, with Axis Ang errors as low as 0.02° (FoldChair) and 0.04° (Fridge), compared to Ditto's 89.35° and PARIS's 8.08° on the same objects. On real-world data, IAAO attains 8.86° Axis Ang and 5.60 CD-w (overall), outperforming DigitalTwinArt (10.11° / 5.76) and PARIS (22.39° / 43.16). On the multi-part object dataset (Table 2), IAAO matches or slightly beats DigitalTwinArt on most metrics (e.g., 0.09° vs. 0.11° Part Motion on Fridge-m joint 0; 0.97 vs. 0.99 CD-w on Storage-m), while PARIS*-m degrades severely (86–106° Part Motion).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IAAO decisively outperforms Ditto [16] on all metrics: Ditto fails catastrophically on unseen categories (89°+ Axis Ang on FoldChair, Fridge, Stapler, USB, Washer) and even on seen ones it lags by orders of magnitude in Part Motion (e.g., 99.36° vs. 0.13° on FoldChair). Against PARIS [22] and PARIS* [22], IAAO is consistently superior on real-world data (8.86° vs. 22.39°/16.00° Axis Ang; 5.60 vs. 43.16/20.55 CD-w), while PARIS occasionally matches IAAO on simple synthetic objects (e.g., 0.02° Axis Ang on Laptop) but degrades on complex ones (39.73° on Stapler). The closest competitor is DigitalTwinArt [47], which achieves similar sub-degree Axis Ang on synthetic objects (0.03–0.43° vs. IAAO's 0.02–0.36°) and comparable CD-w; however, IAAO edges out DigitalTwinArt on real data (8.86° vs. 10.11° Axis Ang; 5.60 vs. 5.76 CD-w) and on the multi-part Storage-m scene (0.97 vs. 0.99 CD-w). CSG-reg and 3Dseg-reg handle simple objects well but fail on complex articulated scenes (e.g., CSG-reg: 71.93° Axis Ang on Washer, 158.99° Part Motion), confirming that geometry-only pipelines without semantic guidance struggle with multi-part articulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

IAAO sits at the intersection of 3D Gaussian Splatting for explicit scene representation, foundation-model-driven semantic segmentation (CLIP, SAM, DINOv2, MaskCLIP), and articulated object reconstruction. It departs from prior NeRF-based methods (PARIS, DigitalTwinArt) by replacing implicit MLPs with explicit 3D Gaussian primitives that carry hierarchical feature fields, enabling direct object- and part-level queries without costly multi-scale rendering. Unlike Ditto and PARIS, which assume known camera poses, aligned static geometry, or a fixed number of parts, IAAO handles unknown poses, unaligned static elements, and arbitrary part counts through a global-local transformation pipeline initialized by GeoTransformer and refined via 2D-3D pixel correspondences. The mask-clustering strategy for cross-view label consistency borrows from open-vocabulary 3D instance segmentation (MaskClustering, OpenNeRF) but is repurposed here to build per-state label fields that supervise both affordance prediction and motion recovery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By embedding language-aligned semantics directly into 3D Gaussians, IAAO enables prompt-driven interaction (point, mask, language) with articulated objects in complex indoor scenes without category-specific training, which is a practical step toward general-purpose robotic manipulation and AR/VR. The scene-state fusion stage addresses a real gap—occluded geometry in single-state reconstructions—by merging two states after alignment. Limitations include the requirement for multi-view captures from two distinct joint states, dependence on foundation-model quality for small affordance regions, and the fact that improvements over the strongest baseline (DigitalTwinArt) are modest on most metrics. Open questions include whether the approach extends to single-view or video inputs, real-time inference, and more complex multi-body interactions beyond simple rotary/prismatic joints.
