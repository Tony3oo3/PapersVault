## Problem

MLLMs possess strong common-sense reasoning but struggle with precise 3D spatial reasoning and fine-grained geometry, making them unreliable for placing objects in complex preexisting scenes. Prior LLM-based scene generation methods rely on bounding-box representations that cannot express constraints on specific object surfaces (e.g., the top plane of a shelf), lack the ability to reference specific object instances, and neglect common-sense criteria like aesthetics and accessibility in final placement selection.

## Input / Output

FirePlace consumes a Universal Scene Descriptor (USD) file representing a 3D scene with a fixed camera pose, a transformable 3D object mesh, and a natural-language placement instruction. It outputs a single 4×4 transformation matrix T that positions the object in world coordinates, producing an edited USD file with the object inserted. Internally, the method operates on per-object segmentation masks rendered from the camera viewpoint and on planar convex hulls extracted from mesh faces, rather than on bounding boxes or raw point clouds.

## Method

FirePlace is a training-free, five-stage pipeline that couples an off-the-shelf MLLM (Gemini-1.5 Pro) with explicit 3D geometric processing. Stage 1 prompts the MLLM to generate a constraint outline: a list of triplets pairing a constraint function (from a small library of six planar constraints like Parallel, CloseTo, Contact, NoOverhang) with textual descriptions of the anchor and target surfaces. Stages 2–4 resolve these text references to concrete 3D geometry: Batched Visual Selection (a recursive, inference-compute-scaling procedure that limits the MLLM to at most m visual options per round) identifies the correct anchor object and interaction surfaces among segmentation masks and extracted planar convex hulls; surface normals are estimated by the MLLM; and a constraint solver (simulated annealing) minimizes the sum of constraint functions to produce a set of feasible placements. Stage 5 performs plausibility pruning, where the MLLM renders and compares candidate placements, selecting the one that best satisfies common-sense criteria of aesthetics, functionality, and accessibility.

## Results

On 266 placement tasks across 50 photorealistic 3D scenes, FirePlace achieves a minimum L2 error of 48.39 cm and mean L2 error of 69.89 cm, roughly halving the error of both baselines (LayoutGPT: 89.01/132.96 cm; Holodeck: 113.17/137.60 cm). FirePlace also leads on visibility (0.88 vs. 0.79 and 0.63), plausibility (2.95 vs. 2.14 and 2.13 on a 1–4 scale), and energy score (0.42 vs. 0.22 for Holodeck; N/A for LayoutGPT). In a 2,075-pair user preference study, FirePlace wins 76% of comparisons against LayoutGPT and 60–66% against Holodeck across physics, semantics, and common-sense axes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FirePlace is compared against two LLM-based baselines: LayoutGPT (Feng et al., NeurIPS 2024), which predicts 3D bounding-box positions directly, and Holodeck (Yang et al., CVPR 2024), which generates constraint-based layouts over bounding boxes. FirePlace wins on every metric against both methods. Against LayoutGPT, FirePlace reduces mean L2 error by ~48% (69.89 vs. 132.96 cm) and improves plausibility by 0.81 points (2.95 vs. 2.14); in user studies, FirePlace is preferred 76% of the time across all three evaluation axes. Against Holodeck, FirePlace reduces mean L2 error by ~49% (69.89 vs. 137.60 cm), doubles the energy score (0.42 vs. 0.22), and is preferred 60–66% of the time in human evaluations. The key qualitative difference is that both baselines represent objects as bounding boxes, which fails on fine-grained placements (e.g., books on shelves, figurines on window seats), whereas FirePlace reasons over extracted planar surfaces.

## Relation to Prior Work

FirePlace extends the line of LLM/MLLM-based 3D scene generation (Holodeck, LayoutGPT, SceneCraft) by replacing bounding-box representations with explicit 3D surface geometry and by introducing a training-free pipeline that couples off-the-shelf MLLMs with external 3D processing tools. It borrows the MLLM-based plausibility pruning strategy from Blender-Alchemy [20] and the simulated-annealing constraint solver from Infigen Indoors [36], but departs from both prior constraint-based methods by operating on fine-grained planar surfaces rather than axis-aligned boxes, and by adding a Batched Visual Selection mechanism (inspired by inference-compute scaling) to handle the combinatorial difficulty of instance and surface selection. Unlike prior work that fine-tunes MLLMs on 3D datasets to improve spatial reasoning, FirePlace keeps the MLLM frozen and offloads all geometric precision to dedicated 3D tools.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a practical, training-free recipe for making MLLMs useful in 3D spatial tasks: decompose the problem into discrete selection (handled by batched visual selection), continuous estimation (handled by the MLLM), and geometric feasibility (handled by a constraint solver). This modular design is broadly applicable beyond object placement to any 3D editing task where an MLLM must ground language in geometry. Limitations include evaluation on only 50 expert-designed scenes with 266 tasks, reliance on a single MLLM (Gemini-1.5 Pro), and a small constraint library; open questions include scalability to multi-object simultaneous placement, open-vocabulary scenes without pre-segmented instances, and whether the batched selection overhead becomes prohibitive in very large scenes.
