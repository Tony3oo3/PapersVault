## Problem

SAM's class-agnostic design makes its segmentation accuracy highly dependent on prompt quality, yet existing automatic prompt generation methods produce geometric prompts without optimizing their spatial or feature-space distribution. This leads to redundant, erroneous, or poorly distributed positive and negative prompts that degrade segmentation, particularly for small targets and cross-domain (e.g., medical) images. The paper addresses the lack of a training-free mechanism to refine arbitrary initial point prompt schemes into well-distributed ones.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a set of initial point prompts (both positive and negative) generated by any upstream method—feature matching (one-shot, training-free), coarse segmentation sampling (few-shot U-Net), or Matcher—and produces a refined set of point prompts with optimized distribution. The optimized prompts are then fed into SAM's prompt encoder to yield the final segmentation mask. PPO itself requires no task-specific training at inference time; it simply activates, deletes, or moves nodes in the graph environment to select the best subset and arrangement of the initial prompt candidates.

## Method

PPO constructs a dual-space heterogeneous graph per image by computing a feature distance matrix from DINOv2 patch embeddings and a physical distance matrix from patch center coordinates, with nodes typed as positive or negative prompts and edges typed as feature or physical distances. A Q-learning agent is trained within this graph environment using three actions (restore, delete, move a node) and a reward function that simultaneously encourages small feature distance among positive prompts, large feature distance between positive and negative prompts, large physical distance among same-type prompts, and small physical distance between positive and negative prompts, weighted by coefficients α and β. The policy network is a two-layer MLP (1024→64→3) trained with ε-greedy exploration and a discount factor of 0.1. At inference, the trained agent traverses the graph to activate an optimal subset of prompt points, which are then passed to SAM—making the optimizer plug-and-play across any initial prompt scheme and any downstream segmentation task without additional training.

## Results

On the four evaluation datasets, FM-PPO (a training-free method) achieves mDSC of 91.9% on FSS-1000, 73.7% on VOC2012, 75.4% on ISIC2018, and 70.3% on Kvasir, with corresponding mHD values of 7.4, 9.9, 8.1, and 9.0. On the two medical datasets (ISIC and Kvasir), FM-PPO outperforms all compared one-shot methods by substantial margins (e.g., +24.1 mDSC over Matcher on Kvasir). On natural image datasets, FM-PPO is competitive with in-domain-trained methods (HSNet, VAT) and surpasses other training-free SAM-based prompt methods (PerSAM, Matcher), though it trails SegGPT on VOC2012 (73.7 vs. 78.2 mDSC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FM-PPO is compared against five one-shot segmentation methods: two in-domain-trained models (HSNet, VAT) and three training-free methods (SegGPT, PerSAM, Matcher). On the medical datasets, FM-PPO dominates all baselines: it achieves 75.4% mDSC on ISIC (vs. 73.7% for Matcher, 61.2% for SegGPT) and 70.3% on Kvasir (vs. 46.2% for Matcher, 44.1% for SegGPT), with the largest gap being +24.1 mDSC over Matcher on Kvasir. On natural images, FM-PPO slightly edges out Matcher on FSS-1000 (91.9 vs. 91.6 mDSC) and VOC2012 (73.7 vs. 69.0 mDSC), and substantially outperforms PerSAM on both. However, FM-PPO trails SegGPT on VOC2012 (73.7 vs. 78.2 mDSC) and is marginally below VAT and SegGPT on FSS-1000 (91.9 vs. 93.6/93.7 mDSC). The key distinction is that FM-PPO requires no in-domain training, matching or exceeding in-domain-trained methods on medical tasks while remaining competitive on natural images.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the automatic prompt engineering line for SAM, which has followed two paradigms: training task-specific prompt encoders (e.g., VRP-SAM, AutoSAM) or generating geometric prompts via feature matching or coarse segmentation sampling (e.g., Matcher, PerSAM). PPO departs from both by treating prompt distribution as a heterogeneous graph optimization problem solved with Q-learning, using DINOv2 patch features to define feature-space edges and patch-center coordinates to define physical-space edges. Unlike prior prompt generators that produce a fixed set of points, PPO iteratively refines an initial prompt set through restore/delete/move actions guided by a dual-space reward, making it a post-hoc optimizer rather than a prompt generator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature—requiring no in-domain training and working on top of any initial prompt scheme (FM, CS, Matcher)—makes PPO a practical drop-in improvement for SAM-based pipelines across domains, as demonstrated by large gains on medical datasets with domain shift. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that PPO cannot recover targets entirely absent from the initial prompt coverage, capping its utility when upstream prompt generation is poor. Open questions include whether the DRL formulation generalizes to other prompt modalities (boxes, masks) or to video segmentation (SAM 2), and whether the Q-learning agent's policy transfers across image resolutions or patch granularities without retraining.
