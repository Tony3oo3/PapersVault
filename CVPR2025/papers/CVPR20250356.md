## Problem

Current text-to-video models, even those with over 10B parameters, fail to accurately generate human-object interaction (HOI) videos because no large-scale, high-quality dataset with precise HOI-specific captions exists. Existing general video datasets (WebVid-10M, Panda-70M) contain poor-quality or non-HOI content, while HOI perception benchmarks are too small (tens of thousands of clips) to support T2V training. Additionally, no evaluation metrics exist to specifically assess the quality of generated interactions.

## Input / Output

The pipeline ingests ~80 million raw video clips from eight sources (Panda-70M, ViSR, Mixkit, Pixabay, Pexels, BEHAVE, InterCap, HOI4D) and outputs 1.1 million curated HOI clips at ≥720p resolution, ≥20 FPS, and >1 second duration, each paired with a structured caption averaging 152 words covering human appearance, object state, interaction actions, and scene context. For evaluation, 306 manually filtered prompts (extended by LLM) are fed to T2V models, and the generated videos are scored via CoarseHOIScore (per-frame HOI detector confidence) and FineHOIScore (temporal stability of pixel-level human-object contact).

## Method

The dataset is built through a multi-stage curation pipeline: metadata filtering (duration, resolution, FPS), OCR-based text removal (DBNet++), aesthetic scoring (Laion Aesthetic Predictor), motion scoring (UniMatch optical flow), MLLM-based HOI detection (PLLaVA captioning + Qwen2.5 judgment), and final human verification by seven annotators over eight weeks. For captioning, a Mixture-of-Multimodal-Experts (MoME) strategy is proposed: two MLLMs (PLLaVA and Qwen2-VL) independently caption each video, a Llama3.1 decision expert checks cross-consistency to detect hallucinations, and upon disagreement a third expert (LLaVA) re-captions the contested regions, after which the decision expert fuses a corrected caption. Two new evaluation metrics are introduced: CoarseHOIScore measures the fraction of frames where an HOI detector reports a confident interaction triplet, and FineHOIScore computes the temporal stability of pixel-level contact distance between object masks and human keypoints across consecutive frames.

## Results

On the proposed CoarseHOIScore, the best commercial model Kling 1.5 achieves 42.72%, while the best open-source model CogVideoX-5B reaches 32.84% before fine-tuning. After fine-tuning on HOIGen-1M, CogVideoX-5B improves to 44.04% CoarseHOIScore, surpassing Kling 1.5. FineHOIScore ranges narrowly from 91.54% (OpenSoraPlan) to 97.76% (Kling 1.5), and VBench metrics such as Motion Smoothness exceed 98% across all models despite clearly unreasonable generated interactions, underscoring the need for HOI-specific evaluation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the five commercial T2V systems, Kling 1.5 leads with 42.72% CoarseHOIScore, followed by Hailuo (39.56%) and Dreamina (36.36%), while Gen-3 trails at 23.98%. Among open-source models, CogVideoX-5B (32.84%) and Mochi-10B (28.78%) outperform OpenSora (31.86%) and OpenSoraPlan (8.80%), with the 5B model beating its 2B counterpart by ~1.5 points, consistent with a scaling trend. After fine-tuning on HOIGen-1M via LoRA, CogVideoX-5B jumps from 32.84% to 44.04% CoarseHOIScore, surpassing Kling 1.5 by 1.3 points, and CogVideoX-2B improves from 31.34% to 39.13%. The ablation on captioning (Table 3) shows that replacing PLLaVA captions with the MoME-generated captions raises CoarseHOIScore from 33.17% to 39.13% for CogVideoX-2B. Overall, commercial models dominate on both VBench and HOI metrics, but the gap narrows substantially after domain-specific fine-tuning, validating the dataset's utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of T2V generation and HOI perception. While T2V models (Sora, CogVideoX, OpenSora) and HOI perception datasets (HOI4D, BEHAVE, InterCap) are well-studied separately, no prior work has constructed a million-scale, manually verified dataset specifically for HOI video generation or proposed HOI-specific evaluation metrics. The MoME captioning strategy repurposes the mixture-of-experts paradigm from NLP into a cross-verification protocol among multiple MLLMs (PLLaVA, Qwen2-VL, Llama3.1) to detect and correct hallucinations in video descriptions, departing from single-model captioning approaches used in Panda-70M and WebVid-10M.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that even the strongest commercial model (Kling 1.5) achieves only 42.72% CoarseHOIScore exposes a substantial and previously unquantified gap in HOI video generation, providing a concrete benchmark for the field. The dataset and Coarse/FineHOIScore metrics fill a critical infrastructure gap: without them, T2V model progress on interaction fidelity was essentially unmeasurable. Open questions include whether 1M clips suffice as model scales grow, whether the pixel-level FineHOIScore truly captures contact dynamics, and whether the near-parity between in-distribution (61.20%) and out-of-distribution (63.20%) prompts signals genuine emergent interaction understanding or merely prompt-agnostic generation quality.
