## Problem

Existing prompt-based video class-incremental learning methods either select from a fixed prompt pool or generate deterministic prompts, both of which fail to capture the diverse spatio-temporal characteristics of videos and generalize poorly to future tasks. Additionally, prevalent approaches operate on global frame features, overlooking critical relationships between local patches across frames that are essential for video understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251221/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip, from which 8 spatial frames and 25 temporal frames are uniformly sampled; temporal frames are downsampled by a factor of √25 and stacked into a frame grid matching the original frame resolution. A frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 encodes these into a video representation Ê = [ê^sp, ê^tp] that conditions the diffusion model. The output is a class label prediction guided by a generated space-time prompt P₀ (a sequence of 6 vectors in R^D) produced by iteratively denoising random Gaussian noise over T diffusion steps.

## Method

CoSTEP models the conditional distribution p(P|Ê) of space-time prompts using a denoising diffusion probabilistic model, replacing deterministic prompt generation with stochastic sampling from a learned distribution. Training proceeds in two phases per task: first, a task-specific prompt P^k is optimized using a frame grid technique that stacks downsampled temporal frames into a spatial grid, allowing the frozen ViT to model inter-frame patch relationships as if they were intra-frame spatial relationships; second, a U-Net-based diffusion model is trained to reconstruct P^k from noise, conditioned on Ê, with a combined loss of diffusion reconstruction and classification. During inference, a prompt is sampled from the learned distribution via iterative denoising, naturally injecting diversity and reducing overfitting to the current task. To combat catastrophic forgetting, the model retains a small set of stored video representation vectors (one global vector per video) from previous tasks rather than raw frames, significantly reducing memory and privacy concerns.

## Results

On the TCD split (Table 1), CoSTEP achieves 86.05/86.71/86.95% average accuracy on UCF101 (5/10/25 tasks), 61.70/61.84% on HMDB51 (5/25 tasks), and 41.44/36.60% on Something-Something V2 (9/18 tasks), setting new state-of-the-art across all settings. On the vCLIMB split (Table 2), CoSTEP reaches 96.51% accuracy with only 1.99% backward forgetting on UCF101 (10 tasks), substantially outperforming PIVOT (93.36% acc, 4.47% BWF). The method also demonstrates strong robustness to task count, with accuracy actually increasing slightly as tasks grow from 5 to 25 on UCF101.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoSTEP consistently outperforms the strongest prompt-based video CIL baselines: ST-Prompt by 1.3–1.5% on UCF101, 1.5–1.6% on HMDB51, and 1.1–1.5% on SSv2, despite ST-Prompt additionally leveraging text prompts in CLIP. Against PIVOT on the vCLIMB split, CoSTEP gains 3.1–3.4% in accuracy while halving backward forgetting (1.99% vs 4.47% BWF on 10 tasks). Compared to non-prompt methods, CoSTEP surpasses HCE by 6.0% and FrameMaker by 6.7% on UCF101 (5 tasks), and L2P by 4.8%. On SSv2, which emphasizes fine-grained temporal reasoning, CoSTEP leads HCE (the previous best non-prompt method) by 4.6% and ST-Prompt by 1.5%. The gap over CLIP Zero-Shot is particularly large on SSv2 (41.44% vs 3.56%), highlighting the critical role of learned prompts for action recognition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prompt-based video class-incremental learning line (L2P, ST-Prompt, PIVOT, CODA-Prompt, DAP) by replacing deterministic prompt selection or generation with a diffusion model that learns the full conditional distribution p(P|Ê) over space-time prompts. It builds directly on DDPM [18] for the generative mechanism and on Visual Prompt Tuning [21] for the frozen-ViT-plus-prompt architecture, but departs from prior probabilistic prompt work (BPT, VAE-based approaches) by arguing that the video prompt space is too complex for simple parametric distributions. The frame-grid temporal modeling technique is a novel repurposing of spatial ViT self-attention to capture cross-frame patch relationships without any additional temporal parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoSTEP demonstrates that modeling prompt distributions with a diffusion model yields consistently better generalization across incremental tasks than deterministic or simpler probabilistic alternatives, and that a single global video representation per stored sample suffices for anti-forgetting—avoiding the privacy and memory costs of raw video replay. The frame-grid trick offers a lightweight, parameter-free path to temporal modeling from image-pretrained ViTs, which could transfer to other video understanding settings. Open questions include whether the diffusion sampling cost at inference scales to longer or higher-resolution videos, whether the approach extends to other modalities (audio-visual, 3D), and whether the gains persist under stricter rehearsal-free or online settings.
