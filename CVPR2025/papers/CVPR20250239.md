## Problem

Existing large-scale video datasets for generation (e.g., Panda-70M) suffer from three interrelated quality gaps: inaccurate temporal splitting that introduces semantic incoherence, simplistic captions that fail to capture fine-grained visual detail, and heuristic multi-threshold filtering that either retains low-quality clips or accidentally discards high-quality ones. The paper argues that the core challenge is establishing accurate, fine-grained conditioning between text/metric conditions and video content, which directly determines generation model performance.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes raw video clips (same source as Panda-70M) and produces 36M clips at 720p resolution with 13.75-second average duration, each paired with a structured caption averaging 202 words across six components (subject, actions, environment, visual language, camera language, world knowledge). For the generation model, inputs are a text prompt plus numeric metric conditions (motion, aesthetic, clarity scores) encoded as frequency embeddings; the output is a 2-second, 256×256 generated video. The VTSS filtering step reduces the 48M pre-filtered clips to the final 36M using a single threshold of 2.5 on a bimodal score distribution.

## Method

The pipeline comprises four stages. First, a Color-Struct SVM (CSS) module detects transitions using BGR histogram correlation and Canny Luminance SSIM as features fed to a linear SVM, augmented by temporal smoothing that flags changes exceeding a 3σ Gaussian confidence interval to separate gradual transitions from fast motion. Second, a structured caption system fine-tunes a LLaVA-based model (trained on GPT-4V annotations with mixed image/video data and 2×2 spatial pooling for high-resolution inputs) to generate six-component captions. Third, a Training Suitability Assessment Network (TSA) models the joint distribution of quality sub-metrics using a 3D Swin Transformer (dynamic branch), ConvNext (static branch), and a label branch fused via a Weight CrossGating Block, outputting a single Video Training Suitability Score (VTSS) that replaces multiple manual thresholds. Fourth, during diffusion training, sub-metric scores are encoded as frequency embeddings, passed through an MLP, and injected into transformer blocks via Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN) alongside timestep embeddings, enabling fine-grained quality control at inference without additional computational cost.

## Results

On VBench, the generation model trained on Koala-36M with metric conditions achieves a Total Score of 0.7460 (Quality Score 0.7846, Semantic Score 0.5915), substantially outperforming the Panda-70M baseline (Total Score 0.6493, Quality Score 0.7343, Semantic Score 0.3093). Even without metric conditions, Koala-36M reaches a Total Score of 0.7156, still exceeding Panda-70M by ~0.067. The largest gains appear in semantic metrics: Human Action improves from 0.2400 to 0.8080, Object Class from 0.3017 to 0.7794, and Multiple Objects from 0.0223 to 0.2953.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is Panda-70M, the largest publicly available video-text dataset (70M clips, ~13-word captions). Koala-36M with metric conditions outperforms Panda-70M by +0.097 in Total Score (0.7460 vs. 0.6493), +0.050 in Quality Score (0.7846 vs. 0.7343), and +0.282 in Semantic Score (0.5915 vs. 0.3093). The semantic gap is driven by large improvements in Human Action (0.8080 vs. 0.2400), Object Class (0.7794 vs. 0.3017), and Multiple Objects (0.2953 vs. 0.0223), which the authors attribute to the 200-word structured captions and more accurate temporal splitting. Internally, VTSS-based filtering (Koala-36M) slightly outperforms manual multi-threshold filtering (Koala-37M-manual) in Total Score (0.7156 vs. 0.7073), and adding metric conditions on top of VTSS-filtered data yields the best overall result, confirming that both filtering and conditioning contribute independently.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Panda-70M, starting from the same raw video corpus but applying a refined processing pipeline that addresses three specific weaknesses: imprecise scene transitions, sparse captions (~13 words), and heuristic multi-threshold filtering. The caption model is derived from LLaVA fine-tuned with GPT-4V-generated structured annotations, and the generation model follows the Sora-like DiT architecture. The key methodological departure from prior video data curation work (Stable Video Diffusion, MiraData, VidGen) is the Video Training Suitability Score, which learns a single joint-distribution score from human-curated labels rather than applying independent thresholds on orthogonal sub-metrics, and the AdaLN-based metric conditioning that injects quality scores directly into the diffusion transformer during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Koala-36M is currently the only openly available video dataset combining over 10M clips with 200+ word structured captions, filling a critical gap between small high-quality datasets (MiraData, VidGen) and large low-quality ones (Panda-70M, HD-VILA-100M). The finding that 36M carefully curated clips outperform 70M less-curated ones on VBench total score (0.7156 vs 0.7343 quality, 0.4504 vs 0.3093 semantic) reinforces the data-quality-over-quantity principle for video generation. However, the authors acknowledge the dataset is insufficient for training models exceeding 1B parameters, and the entire pipeline's effectiveness is validated only on a 256×256, 2-second generation model, leaving open whether these curation strategies transfer to higher-resolution, longer-duration generation.
