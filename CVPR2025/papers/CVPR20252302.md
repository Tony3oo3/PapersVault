## Problem

No existing 3D pre-training method is equally effective for both object-level and scene-level point clouds: MAE-based generative methods fail on large-scale scenes due to expensive Chamfer distance supervision, while contrastive methods saturate quickly on simple object data. This scale diversity in point cloud data (hundreds of times more points in scenes vs. objects) prevents a single unified pre-training paradigm from working across all 3D perception tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a point cloud P ∈ R^{N×3} (N=1,024 for objects, N>100,000 for scenes) and V_ref reference projected images (1 view for objects, 8 views for scenes) as input. During pre-training, it outputs Gaussian primitives (N×23 parameters: position offset, opacity, scaling, rotation quaternions, spherical harmonics) that are rendered into V_rend novel-view images supervised by ground truth via pixel-wise MSE. During fine-tuning, the backbone produces class labels, part masks, or semantic/instance segmentation masks depending on the downstream task.

## Method

UniPre3D predicts per-point Gaussian primitives via a lightweight MLP head and renders novel-view images through differentiable 3D Gaussian Splatting, enabling end-to-end pixel-level supervision that naturally adapts task difficulty to input scale (smaller point clouds yield fewer, larger-covariance Gaussians; denser scenes yield finer detail). A cross-modal 2D branch extracts features from reference images using a Stable Diffusion autoencoder, which are then fused with 3D backbone features via a scale-adaptive strategy: for objects, 3D points are projected to 2D to retrieve image features and fused at the decoder level; for scenes, 2D pixels are back-projected using ground-truth depth maps into a pseudo point cloud that is merged with 3D points at the encoder level (point fusion), increasing Gaussian primitive count by 70%. The object-level loss applies separate foreground/background weights to balance pre-training difficulty, while scene-level training restricts the angular gap between reference and rendered views to maximize utilization of the supplemental image knowledge.

## Results

On object-level classification (ScanObjectNN PB_T50_RS), UniPre3D with a standard Transformer backbone achieves 87.93% overall accuracy, surpassing Point-MAE (85.18%), TAP (85.67%), and PointDif (87.61%). On scene-level semantic segmentation, UniPre3D with SparseUNet reaches 75.8 mIoU on ScanNet20, 33.0 on ScanNet200, and 71.5 on S3DIS, while with PointTransformerV3 it achieves 36.0 mIoU on ScanNet200. For part segmentation on ShapeNetPart, UniPre3D with PointMLP attains 85.5 mIoU_C and 86.8 mIoU_I, outperforming TAP (85.2/86.9). The method consistently improves over its respective no-pretraining baselines across all four backbone architectures tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On object-level classification (ScanObjectNN PB_T50_RS), UniPre3D with a standard Transformer (87.93%) outperforms all MAE-based baselines including Point-MAE (85.18%), MaskPoint (84.30%), Point-CMAE (85.95%), and the generative method TAP (85.67%), as well as the diffusion-based PointDif (87.61%). With advanced backbones, UniPre3D consistently adds 0.8–1.0 points over no-pretraining baselines (e.g., Mamba3D: 92.6→93.4; PointMLP: 87.4→89.5 vs. TAP's 88.5). On scene-level semantic segmentation, UniPre3D with SparseUNet (75.8/33.0/71.5 on ScanNet20/200/S3DIS) surpasses all contrastive baselines (PointContrast 74.1/26.2/70.3, CSC 73.8/26.4/72.2, MSC 75.5/28.8/70.1) and the single-dataset PonderV2† (74.6/32.4/70.2), though the multi-dataset PonderV2 (77.0/32.3/73.2) remains stronger on ScanNet20 and S3DIS. With PointTransformerV3, UniPre3D improves ScanNet200 from 35.2 to 36.0 mIoU. On instance segmentation (Table 4), UniPre3D achieves 39.9/18.7 mAP on ScanNet20/200, competitive with PonderV2 (40.9/20.1) but trailing it by ~1–1.5 points, while beating MSC (39.3/17.3) and PPT (40.7/19.4 on ScanNet20 mAP). The paper does not directly compare against PPT and PonderV2's multi-dataset or supervised settings, noting these as reference-only.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniPre3D builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) as a differentiable rendering backbone, repurposing a technique originally designed for novel-view synthesis as a self-supervised pre-training objective. It departs from the dominant MAE paradigm for object pre-training (Point-MAE, MaskPoint) and the contrastive paradigm for scene pre-training (PointContrast, CSC, MSC) by adopting a generative rendering task that is naturally scale-adaptive. The closest prior work is Ponder/PonderV2, which used NeRF-based rendering for scene pre-training, and TAP, which used attention-based 3D-to-2D prediction for object pre-training; UniPre3D unifies both regimes under a single 3DGS pipeline and adds cross-modal fusion with a pretrained image encoder (Stable Diffusion autoencoder) to regulate task difficulty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first pre-training recipe demonstrated to be simultaneously effective across object-scale (1K points) and scene-scale (100K+ points) point clouds and across diverse backbone architectures (Transformer, PointMLP, Mamba, SparseUNet, PTv3), removing the need to select a scale-specific pre-training strategy. The practical efficiency gain from 3DGS over NeRF (full-image supervision, ~2× speedup) makes the approach more accessible. Key limitations remain: the fusion strategy (feature vs. point) must be manually selected per scale, the method requires paired point-cloud-and-image data adding curation burden, and scales beyond object/scene (e.g., city-level) are unaddressed. Whether the cross-modal image branch can be replaced by a purely 3D signal, or whether the approach extends to open-vocabulary or language-grounded 3D tasks, are open questions.
