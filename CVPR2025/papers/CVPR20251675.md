## Problem

Existing template-free articulated model reconstruction methods can faithfully render observed views but fail to recover plausible canonical models, especially under sparse pose data. This stems from a fundamental ambiguity: multiple canonical geometries can explain the same observations after forward skinning, and prior methods lack a mechanism to detect or resolve this ambiguity. TAGA identifies that this canonical ambiguity is entangled with incorrect skinning weight assignments, a coupling that no prior template-free method has explicitly addressed.

## Input / Output

TAGA takes a monocular video sequence and corresponding per-frame bone transformations (keypoint skeleton) as input, requiring no parametric template, mesh, or skinning annotations. It produces an animatable 3D Gaussian model (positions, opacities, spherical harmonics, rotations, and per-Gaussian skinning weights) that supports real-time novel view rendering at 140+ FPS and animation to arbitrary novel poses. The method is designed to work even with highly limited pose variation, such as a single A-pose sequence in PeopleSnapshot, with training completing in approximately 30 minutes on a single GPU.

## Method

TAGA represents the articulated model as explicit 3D Gaussians in canonical space, deformed to observation space via Linear Blend Skinning driven by a low-resolution voxel-based skinning field (64×64×16 grid queried by trilinear interpolation). The core innovation is a self-supervised detection-and-correction loop: a bone-based Gaussian Mixture Model estimates expected skinning weights from spatial semantics, and Gaussians whose learned weights diverge (measured by Jensen-Shannon divergence) are flagged as 'Ambiguous Gaussians.' These are corrected via KNN transfer from nearby unambiguous Gaussians, then mapped back to canonical space through inverse LBS to produce cycle consistency losses (geometry L1 + skinning L2) that refine the original canonical Gaussians and skinning field. An EM algorithm iteratively refines the GMM parameters during backward mapping, and the entire pipeline is differentiable and template-free, requiring only the skeleton for bone definitions.

## Results

On ZJU-MoCap, TAGA achieves PSNR 31.22, SSIM 0.977, and LPIPS* 29.21 for novel view synthesis, outperforming all template-free baselines (NPC: 30.76/0.960/30.84; HumanNeRF: 30.66/0.969/33.38; TAVA: 30.24/0.969/35.23). Training takes only 0.5 hours (20× faster than HumanNeRF's 10 h, 60× faster than NPC's 30 h), and rendering reaches 140 FPS (470× faster than HumanNeRF at 0.3 FPS). On the pose-limited PeopleSnapshot dataset, TAGA achieves PSNR 29.69 and SSIM 0.974, surpassing HumanNeRF (26.13/0.955) and matching the SMPL-based InstantAvatar.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among template-free methods, TAGA wins on all three metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS*) against every competitor: it beats NPC by +0.46 PSNR and −1.63 LPIPS*, HumanNeRF by +0.56 PSNR and −4.17 LPIPS*, and TAVA by +0.98 PSNR and −6.02 LPIPS*. Against template-based SMPL methods, TAGA surpasses InstantAvatar (PSNR 31.22 vs 29.73) and InstantNVR (31.22 vs 31.01) without any template prior, and is competitive with 3DGS-Avatar (31.22 vs 30.91). The only method TAGA trails is GART [34] (PSNR 32.31, SSIM 0.982, LPIPS* 24.91), which relies on the SMPL template; TAGA narrows the gap to ~1.1 PSNR while being template-free. In terms of efficiency, TAGA's 140 FPS rendering and 0.5 h training dominate all baselines: it is 470× faster than HumanNeRF at inference and 60× faster than NPC at training, while GART (the closest quality competitor) renders at 77 FPS with 0.1 h training.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAGA extends 3D Gaussian Splatting into the template-free articulated model reconstruction setting, a domain previously dominated by implicit representations (HumanNeRF, TAVA, NPC, ARAH) that rely on computationally expensive root-finding for canonical-to-observation mapping. The key conceptual departure is formalizing canonical ambiguity as 'Ambiguous Gaussians' with incorrect skinning weights, then resolving it via a bone-based GMM anomaly detector and cycle-consistency backward mapping — a self-supervised loop absent from prior template-free methods. The voxel-based skinning field and one-to-one Gaussian correspondence replace the bijective-mapping difficulties inherent to NeRF-based approaches, while the GMM-based skinning estimation draws on spatial-semantic priors from articulated shape reconstruction literature (LASR, BANMO).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TAGA demonstrates that template-free articulated reconstruction can achieve near-template-based fidelity (comparable to GART) at a fraction of the training cost (30 min vs. 10–72 h) and with real-time rendering (140+ FPS), making category-agnostic avatar creation practically viable for animals, robots, and other non-SMPL categories. The formalization of canonical ambiguity as a skinning-weight problem opens a clear diagnostic pathway for future work, though the bone-based GMM assumption limits applicability to strongly non-rigid deformations (loose cloth) and self-contact regions remain an open artifact source. Whether the anomaly-detection-plus-cycle-consistency paradigm generalizes beyond human-like skeletons to highly articulated or deformable objects is an important open question.
