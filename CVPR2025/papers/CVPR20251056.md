## Problem

Generating complete 4D scene representations from monocular multi-object videos with heavy occlusions remains highly under-constrained. Existing video-to-4D methods either assume single objects or optimize multi-object scenes independently and compose them late, failing to model cross-object occlusions and producing inter-penetrating artifacts. No prior approach jointly optimizes multi-object deformations while leveraging object-centric generative priors for unobserved viewpoints.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular multi-object video as input, segments and tracks individual objects using SAM 2 and Grounded SAM, and initializes relative depth ordering via DepthCrafter. Objects are cropped to approximately 65% of image size for static 3D Gaussian lifting. The output is a complete 4D deformable Gaussian scene enabling 360° novel view synthesis, plus 2D and 3D point tracks extracted from the optimized Gaussian trajectories. Evaluation uses 1080p point annotations (up to 5 objects × 5 key points per video) on the newly introduced MOSE-PTS dataset and DAVIS.

## Method

GenMOJO employs an 'early-composition' strategy: after independently lifting each object to static 3D Gaussians via SDS with Zero-1-to-3, all objects are composed into a unified world-space scene using depth-based scaling, then their temporal deformations (position, rotation, scale via K-plane) are jointly optimized. Joint splatting of all object Gaussians yields occlusion-aware RGB and flow rendering losses, while an instance mask rendering loss (one-hot labels as fixed Gaussian attributes) prevents identity switching during close interactions. To apply object-centric SDS in the global frame, differentiable affine transformations (derived from per-frame bounding box warps) map object Gaussians into object-centric coordinates for score distillation and back for frame-centric rendering. Time-varying spherical harmonic coefficients predicted by an MLP on the K-plane handle illumination changes, regularized by an L1 penalty to prevent overfitting.

## Results

On MOSE, GenMOJO achieves the highest PSNR (25.56 dB) for reference-view re-rendering and the best CLIP score (85.41) for novel-view synthesis, outperforming DreamScene4D (PSNR 22.98, CLIP 85.16) and DreamGaussian4D (PSNR 17.82, CLIP 81.96). In a two-way user preference study on MOSE, GenMOJO is preferred over DreamScene4D 63.2% of the time. For point tracking on MOSE, GenMOJO achieves the lowest ATE (9.91) and A-EPE (15.46) among all optimization-based methods, beating DreamScene4D (ATE 13.47, A-EPE 22.48) and even approaching supervised CoTrackerV3 (ATE 11.54, A-EPE 22.16).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenMOJO outperforms all four 4D generation baselines on both MOSE and DAVIS. Against DreamScene4D (the closest competitor, also using object decomposition), GenMOJO gains +2.58 dB PSNR on MOSE and +1.78 dB on DAVIS, with a 63.2% user preference win rate. DreamGaussian4D and Consistent4D, designed for single-object inputs, show substantially lower PSNR (17.82 and 22.59 on MOSE) due to distorted geometry in multi-object scenes. For point tracking, GenMOJO beats DreamScene4D by 3.56 ATE and 7.02 A-EPE on MOSE, and outperforms Shape of Motion by 8.48 ATE. Notably, GenMOJO (an optimization-based method with no point-tracking training data) approaches the supervised CoTrackerV3 (ATE 11.54 vs. 9.91) on MOSE, while CoTrackerV3 degrades significantly on DAVIS (MTE 1.74) where GenMOJO remains competitive (MTE 6.32). The gap between GenMOJO and supervised trackers narrows on MOSE compared to DAVIS, confirming that the reconstruction objective provides stronger constraints under heavy occlusions.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenMOJO extends the DreamScene4D line of work (same first author, NeurIPS 2024) by replacing its 'decompose-recompose' strategy—where each object's deformable Gaussians are optimized independently and only combined at render time—with an 'early-composition' approach that jointly optimizes all objects' deformations in a shared coordinate frame. This builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting for the scene representation, K-plane deformation networks (from DreamGaussian4D) for temporal dynamics, and Score Distillation Sampling with Zero-1-to-3 as the object-centric generative prior for unobserved views. The key conceptual departure is that joint splatting makes cross-object occlusions explicit in the rendering loss, while differentiable affine transformations bridge the gap between the global scene frame and the object-centric frame required by the diffusion prior. The paper also introduces MOSE-PTS, a manually annotated point-tracking subset of MOSE, to provide a more rigorous evaluation benchmark than prior single-object or simple multi-object datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: generating complete 4D scene representations from monocular videos with heavy multi-object occlusions, which is critical for robotics, world models, and fine-grained video understanding. The joint-splatting formulation is a clean and generalizable principle that could inform future multi-object 4D methods beyond Gaussian Splatting. However, the reliance on test-time optimization (40× number of frames of optimization per video) limits applicability to offline settings, and the method inherits the generalization limitations of the underlying view-conditioned diffusion model for unconventional camera poses. Open questions include whether feedforward architectures can capture the same joint-optimization benefits in real time, and whether the approach extends to even longer videos or more than a handful of interacting objects.
