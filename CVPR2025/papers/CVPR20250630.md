## Problem

Existing targeted bit-flip attacks on quantized Vision Transformers require flipping hundreds of bits and place flips within 4 KB of each other, making them infeasible under RowHammer's physical constraints. Moreover, prior work exclusively targets model weight bits, overlooking the fact that a single scale factor in a quantized model governs an entire batch of weights, offering a far more efficient attack surface.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250630/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a quantized ViT (8-bit or 4-bit via bitsandbytes), 32 randomly sampled test images, and a target class label. It produces a trigger pattern (embedded at a fixed spatial location covering 4.59% of the image by default) and a compromised model in which at most 50 bits are flipped, predominantly in scale factors. The compromised model misclassifies any trigger-containing input to the target class while preserving clean-data accuracy within ~1% of the original.

## Method

Flip-S exploits the observation that flipping a single bit in a scale factor perturbs all weights in that group simultaneously, enabling effective attacks with far fewer flips. Trigger generation maximizes cumulative attention directed at the trigger position across all ViT layers using attention rollout, combined with a cross-entropy loss for target-class classification. The Scale-Factor-Search (SFS) algorithm computes gradients of a targeted loss (balancing trigger misclassification and clean-sample preservation) with respect to scale factors, selects top-k scale factors by gradient magnitude, and iteratively evaluates individual bit flips to find the one that most reduces the loss. When no scale-factor flip reduces the loss (optimization trapped), SFS falls back to a weight bit-flip phase for finer-grained adjustment before returning to scale factors. A mutual exclusion strategy enforces ≥4 KB separation between any two flipped bits via binary control matrices that mask out all scale factors or weights within 4 KB of an already-flipped location.

## Results

On ImageNet with 8-bit quantization, Flip-S achieves ASR of 95.2%–99.9% across five ViT architectures with only 50 bits flipped, while the best baseline (ProFlip) reaches at most 88.7% and TBT drops as low as 15.3%. Under the more challenging 4-bit quantization, Flip-S still maintains ASR above 92.4% on all models, whereas the strongest baseline (ProFlip) peaks at 84.4%. Flip-S is the only method satisfying the 4 KB bit-flip separation compliance (BFSC) requirement, and it preserves clean-data accuracy (CDA) within 0.5–1.0% of the original model, outperforming all baselines on CDA as well.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Flip-S dominates all four baselines (TBT, ProFlip, HPT, TrojViT) on every model, dataset, and quantization level tested. On ImageNet 8-bit, Flip-S achieves 95.2%–99.9% ASR versus ProFlip's 56.8%–88.7%, TrojViT's 66.0%–90.1%, HPT's 51.5%–78.6%, and TBT's 15.3%–76.0%. The gap narrows slightly on CIFAR-10 where ProFlip reaches 95.5% ASR on ViT-base (4-bit), but Flip-S still leads at 99.5%. In the extended evaluation (up to 500 bits), Flip-S reaches 91.2% ASR with only 25 bits, while ProFlip needs 300 bits and other baselines need 200–500 bits to match that level—an 8×–20× reduction. Critically, Flip-S is the only method that satisfies the 4 KB bit-flip separation requirement (BFSC), which all four baselines fail, making Flip-S the sole practically deployable attack under RowHammer constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the targeted bit-flip attack (BFA) literature, which has primarily focused on CNNs (TBT, ProFlip, HPT) and more recently ViTs (TrojViT, Deep-TROJ), by introducing a fundamentally different attack surface: quantization scale factors rather than individual model weights. The trigger generation borrows the attention rollout concept from interpretability work, and the mutual exclusion strategy addresses a practical RowHammer constraint (4 KB page separation) that prior BFAs largely ignored. Conceptually, the paper repurposes the existing BFA framework—trigger generation plus Trojan insertion via bit-flips—but shifts the optimization target from per-weight bits to per-group scale factors, exploiting the fact that a single scale factor governs a batch of dequantized weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8×–20× reduction in required bit-flips and the guaranteed 4 KB separation make targeted BFAs on quantized ViTs substantially more feasible in real-world RowHammer scenarios, raising the practical threat level for deployed quantized models. The defense evaluation reveals that existing mitigations (OCM, critical-bit prohibition) only partially reduce ASR, leaving an open question about effective defenses against scale-factor-targeted attacks. A limitation is that the attack still requires ~50 bit-flips (hours of RowHammer) and assumes the adversary knows the quantization scheme and model weights, which may not hold in all deployment settings. Future work could explore whether the scale-factor insight generalizes to other quantization paradigms (e.g., mixed-precision, group-wise) and whether runtime integrity checks can detect scale-factor tampering without prohibitive overhead.
