## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks on Generalizable Neural Radiance Fields (GNeRFs), such as NeRFool, operate exclusively under the L-infinity norm, producing highly localized per-pixel perturbations. No attack has been formulated under the L2 norm, which distributes perturbation energy more uniformly across the image and better mimics real-world imaging artifacts. This paper introduces the first L2-norm adversarial attack for GNeRFs, establishing a new threat model for evaluating their robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252464/cropped_page5_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The attack takes four source view RGB images, camera rotation/translation matrices, and a pre-trained GNeRF model (IBRNet or GNT) as input. It produces per-image adversarial perturbations δ_i (maintained as a separate variable, not baked into the image) constrained to an L2 ball of radius ε, which when added to the source images and fed through the GNeRF pipeline yield degraded novel view renderings. The initial perturbation is sampled uniformly from U[-ε, ε], and the perturbed image I_i + δ_i is clipped to [0, 1] at each iteration.

## Method

IL2-NeRF is an iterative gradient-based attack that optimizes perturbations under an L2 norm constraint, drawing from both NeRFool's ray-based loss formulation and PGD's projection-based update scheme. At each step, it computes the gradient of a composite loss (combining 8 sub-losses targeting both RGB color and volumetric density) with respect to the perturbation, then updates via the sign of the L2-normalized gradient scaled by a step size α = η/255. The updated perturbation is projected back onto the L2 ε-ball to enforce the norm constraint. Unlike standard PGD, the perturbation is maintained as an independent variable optimized directly, and the loss is computed over sampled 3D rays rather than a single 2D classification loss, exploiting the volumetric rendering structure of GNeRFs. The objective jointly minimizes perturbation magnitude while maximizing the divergence between adversarial and clean renderings.

## Results

On the LLFF dataset with IBRNet, IL2-NeRF at ε=256 achieves an average PSNR of 12.230, SSIM of 0.399, and LPIPS of 0.579, outperforming NeRFool at ε=8 (PSNR 12.613, SSIM 0.510, LPIPS 0.468) across all three metrics. On DeepVoxels, IL2-NeRF achieves lower PSNR on all four scenes for both IBRNet (10.700 vs. 11.652 avg) and GNT (13.140 vs. 16.533 avg). On the Synthetic dataset, IL2-NeRF achieves a lower average PSNR of 11.432 versus NeRFool's 11.893, with lower SSIM (0.700 vs. 0.804) and higher LPIPS (0.820 vs. 0.258).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is NeRFool (Fu et al., ICML 2023), the first adversarial attack on GNeRFs, operating under the L∞ norm with ε=8. IL2-NeRF at ε=256 outperforms NeRFool on LLFF/IBRNet across all three metrics: PSNR drops from 12.613 to 12.230, SSIM drops from 0.510 to 0.399, and LPIPS rises from 0.468 to 0.579. On DeepVoxels, IL2-NeRF achieves lower PSNR on all four scenes for both IBRNet and GNT, and lower SSIM / higher LPIPS on both models. On Synthetic, IL2-NeRF achieves lower PSNR on 5 of 8 scenes and substantially lower SSIM (0.700 vs. 0.804) and higher LPIPS (0.820 vs. 0.258). However, IL2-NeRF requires a much larger perturbation budget (ε=256 in L2) to match or exceed NeRFool's ε=8 in L∞, and at smaller ε values (8, 16) IL2-NeRF is significantly weaker, indicating the L2 attack needs substantially more total perturbation energy to achieve comparable disruption.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NeRFool attack (Fu et al., ICML 2023), the first adversarial attack on GNeRFs, by reformulating its optimization objective from an L-infinity per-pixel constraint to an L2 (Euclidean) norm constraint. The iterative gradient-ascent procedure draws directly from PGD (Madry et al., 2017), adapted with ray-based perturbation and a weighted multi-loss objective specific to GNeRF's volumetric rendering pipeline. The key conceptual departure is treating the perturbation as a single L2-bounded vector across the entire image rather than independent per-pixel bounds, yielding smoother, image-wide perturbations. The evaluation framework (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS on rendered novel views) is inherited wholesale from NeRFool, making direct comparison straightforward.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing the first L2 threat model for GNeRFs, this work broadens the adversarial robustness evaluation landscape beyond the L-infinity setting that dominated prior GNeRF attack research. The finding that L2 perturbations at ε=256 match or exceed L-infinity perturbations at ε=8 across multiple datasets and models suggests GNeRFs are vulnerable to a wider class of attacks than previously characterized. However, the paper acknowledges that smaller L2 bounds remain unexplored, and the single-baseline comparison (NeRFool only) limits the strength of the cross-norm conclusions. Future work on geometric-based and black-box L2 attacks, as well as defenses, is explicitly flagged as open.
