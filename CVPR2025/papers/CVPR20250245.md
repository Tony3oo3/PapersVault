## Problem

Existing image vectorization methods rely on a fixed, preset number of paths and control points regardless of input complexity, making them redundant for simple graphics and insufficient for complex ones. This rigid parameterization simultaneously undermines computational efficiency, reconstruction accuracy, and the editability of the resulting vector graphics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250245/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB raster image as input and produces a multi-layer vectorized representation where each layer is a pure-colored region defined by cubic Bezier paths with adaptively allocated control points. Unlike fixed-budget baselines that always use N=64 or N=256 paths, AdaVec outputs a variable number of paths (e.g., 26-215 in experiments) and a correspondingly variable parameter count, with each layer independently editable for color, shape, and position.

## Method

AdaVec decomposes vectorization into three stages. First, a multi-layer decomposition module combines SAM semantic masks with SLIC superpixels grouped by DBSCAN, then filters redundant masks using a maximum-IoU threshold and an area-based impact factor to yield a set of pure-colored layers aligned with human perception. Second, a control point simplification module traces initial paths with VTracer and adaptively removes redundant Bezier control points based on local angle conditions, refining the simplified paths via chamfer distance optimization to preserve shape fidelity. Third, a differentiable rendering stage (DiffVG) iteratively optimizes both shape and color parameters using an MSE reconstruction loss combined with a geometric loss that penalizes curve self-intersections, opposite-direction segments, and obtuse angles. The key design choice is decoupling control-point optimization from color optimization to avoid shape-distortion artifacts during rendering-based gradient descent.

## Results

On Noto Emoji, AdaVec (N=39) achieves the best PSNR of 35.89 dB and SSIM of 0.982 with only 1,220 parameters and 44.9 s generation time, outperforming the strongest baseline LIVE (N=256) which requires 7,168 parameters and 9,342 s. On Fluent Emoji, AdaVec (N=26) attains the lowest MSE (0.00047) and highest SSIM (0.972) in 50.5 s, while on the complex Iconfont set it adaptively allocates N=215 paths to reach PSNR 28.03 and LPIPS 0.0504 in just 164.2 s. Across all three datasets, AdaVec consistently uses 3–7× fewer parameters and 10–100× less time than the fixed-path baselines while matching or exceeding their reconstruction quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaVec is compared against three optimization-based vectorization baselines: O&R (AAAI 2024), SGLIVE (arXiv 2024), and LIVE (CVPR 2022), each evaluated at fixed path counts of N=64 and N=256. On Noto Emoji, AdaVec (N=39) dominates all baselines on every metric—PSNR 35.89 vs. 34.53 for LIVE (N=256), SSIM 0.982 vs. 0.978, and LPIPS 0.0038 vs. 0.0071—while using 5.9× fewer parameters and running 208× faster than LIVE. On Fluent Emoji, AdaVec (N=26) achieves the best MSE (0.00047) and SSIM (0.972), though its LPIPS (0.0449) is marginally worse than LIVE (N=256) at 0.0428, and its PSNR (33.28) trails LIVE's 34.07. On the structurally complex Iconfont set, AdaVec adaptively allocates N=215 paths and wins on MSE (0.00102 vs. 0.00127), LPIPS (0.0504 vs. 0.0678), and PSNR (28.03 vs. 25.91) over LIVE (N=256), with SSIM slightly lower (0.925 vs. 0.933). SGLIVE is consistently the weakest baseline across all datasets and metrics, particularly on Iconfont where its PSNR drops to 18.48 at N=256. Overall, AdaVec achieves the best or near-best reconstruction quality with 3–7× fewer parameters and 10–100× less generation time than all fixed-path baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaVec extends the optimization-based image vectorization line initiated by LIVE and refined by O&R and SGLIVE, which all rely on a fixed number of Bezier paths and control points. Its key departure is the adaptive parameterization: rather than presetting path and control-point counts, it dynamically allocates them per layer based on image complexity, using a multi-layer decomposition pipeline (SAM masks + SLIC superpixels + DBSCAN clustering) and a chamfer-distance-based control point simplification stage. The differentiable rendering and geometric loss components are inherited directly from O&R and DiffVG, positioning AdaVec as an engineering integration that recombines these pieces with a novel allocation strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: AdaVec achieves 40–400× speedup over baselines (44.9 s vs. 176–19,732 s) while matching or exceeding their reconstruction quality, making real-time or near-real-time vectorization feasible for editing workflows. The layer decomposition aligned with human perception also enables flexible downstream editing (color, animation, icon customization), addressing the editability gap that plagues purely geometric vectorization. However, the method still struggles with gradient-rich images, and the authors identify dynamic selection of radial gradients and stop-color quantities as an open problem, suggesting the current approach is best suited for flat-color or icon-style graphics rather than photorealistic content.
