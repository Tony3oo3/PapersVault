## Problem

Existing monocular 3D face reconstruction methods rely on occlusion-sensitive photometric losses and appearance models that break down when sEMG electrodes cover the face, making it impossible to extract reliable 3DMM parameters from synchronized muscle-activity recordings. Furthermore, no prior work explicitly establishes a bidirectional mapping between 3DMM expression parameters and measured sEMG signals, leaving the physiological basis of facial expressions unmodeled.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251171/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes sEMG-occluded face images (30 FPS frontal video) alongside unpaired occlusion-free reference images of the same individual, plus downsampled linear-envelope sEMG signals sampled at 4096 Hz following the Fridlund electrode scheme. In its final training stage, the generator receives only 1% of the original pixel information, forcing reliance on rendered 3D geometry. Outputs include FLAME shape/expression/pose parameters, photorealistic electrode-free face images, synthesized 3DMM expressions driven by muscle activity (EMG2Exp), and predicted sEMG envelopes from observed expressions (Exp2EMG).

## Method

EIFER employs a CycleGAN-like double encoder-generator architecture where one pair places sEMG electrodes and the other removes them, with both pairs constrained via cycle-consistent self-supervision. Encoders are built on SMIRK's three MobileNetV3 sub-encoders (shape, expression, pose), while generators use a ResNet backbone with instance normalization to perform neural rendering from sparse color and rendered geometry. The key insight is replacing all occlusion-sensitive photometric and regularization losses with a suite of cycle-consistent terms (reconstruction, identity, minimal change, occlusion expression, occlusion shape, landmark, and rigid transformation) that are insensitive to electrode placement. Training proceeds in two phases: Phase 1 uses a three-stage curriculum (50% → 10% → 1% pixel retention) to progressively decouple geometry from appearance under adversarial least-squares GAN supervision, and Phase 2 trains two MLPs (EMG2Exp and Exp2EMG) on the frozen 3DMM expression parameters to learn the bidirectional muscle-activity mapping.

## Results

On the Mimics And Muscles dataset, EIFER achieves SSIM 0.66, PSNR 19.42, and FID 52.56 for appearance reconstruction under sEMG occlusion, matching MC-CycleGAN (SSIM 0.66, PSNR 19.38, FID 54.39) while using only 1% of pixel information versus MC-CycleGAN's model-free approach. All 3DMM-based baselines (DECA, SMIRK, Deep3DFace, FOCUS) are substantially outperformed, with FID values ranging from 165 to 276 compared to EIFER's 52.56. For the electrode-free EMG prediction task (Exp2EMG) on occluded recordings, EIFER achieves the best MSE (5.09) and Spearman ρ (0.38), outperforming the next-best SMIRK (MSE 6.79, ρ 0.36) and all other methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EIFER essentially ties MC-CycleGAN on all reconstruction metrics (SSIM 0.66 vs 0.66, PSNR 19.42 vs 19.38, FID 52.56 vs 54.39), despite relying on a 3DMM and only 1% of pixel information rather than a model-free GAN. Against all four 3DMM-based baselines, EIFER wins decisively: DECA (SSIM 0.53, FID 165), SMIRK (SSIM 0.47, FID 276), Deep3DFace (SSIM 0.48, FID 219), and FOCUS (SSIM 0.46, FID 228) all show substantially degraded reconstruction under sEMG occlusion, with FID gaps of 3–5×. For the Exp2EMG task on occluded recordings, EIFER achieves the lowest MSE (5.09) and highest Spearman ρ (0.38), beating SMIRK (MSE 6.79, ρ 0.36) and DECA (MSE 10.86, ρ 0.37), while EMOCAv2, Deep3DFace, and FOCUS lag further behind (MSE 12–22). Notably, SMIRK's occlusion-free variant performs comparably to EIFER on restored recordings, confirming that EIFER's advantage comes from handling occlusion directly rather than from a superior underlying face model.

## Relation to Prior Work

EIFER builds directly on SMIRK's analysis-by-neural-synthesis encoder-generator pipeline and the FLAME 3DMM, but departs from the photometric reconstruction paradigm that underlies all prior monocular 3D face methods (DECA, EMOCA, Deep3DFace, FOCUS). By reframing appearance reconstruction as an adversarial unpaired image-to-image translation problem (CycleGAN-style) with cycle-consistent self-supervision, the paper replaces occlusion-sensitive regularization terms that fail under sEMG electrode occlusion. The bidirectional MLP mapping between 3DMM expression parameters and sEMG signals is a novel addition not present in any prior 3DMM-based face reconstruction work, and the multi-stage training strategy with minimal-change regularization is adapted from the authors' earlier MC-CycleGAN work on obstructed faces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a practical pipeline for electrode-free facial EMG (predicting muscle activity from video) and physiological-based expression synthesis (generating expressions from muscle signals), both of which have applications in psychology, medicine, and animation. However, the small sample size (N=36, beardless men only), modest muscle-activity prediction accuracy (Spearman ρ ≈ 0.38), and acknowledged limitations in expression intensity and eye-blinking capture leave significant open questions about generalizability. The finding that the trained facial encoder matters more than the underlying face model (FLAME vs. BFM) is a useful practical insight, and the synchronized sEMG + video dataset provides a resource the field lacked. Extending to temporal modeling, larger cohorts, and diverse populations are natural next steps.
