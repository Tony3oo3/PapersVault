## Problem

Long-tail image classification suffers from poor generalization on tail classes because re-weighting and data augmentation methods remain bounded by the limited diversity of existing classes. The paper identifies a counterintuitive observation—finer-grained datasets (e.g., iNat18 with 8,142 classes) achieve more balanced head/tail performance than coarser ones (e.g., ImageNet-LT with 1,000 classes) despite stronger imbalance—suggesting that granularity, not just sample count, governs feature generalizability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images from a closed-set long-tail training set (e.g., ImageNet-LT, iNaturalist 2018, Places-LT) augmented with auxiliary images crawled from the web for LLM-suggested fine-grained neighbor categories. At inference, the model outputs a single class label from the original target category set; the auxiliary category logits are simply masked out from the classifier weight matrix, requiring no retraining or re-balancing of the head.

## Method

The approach, called category extrapolation, increases dataset granularity by introducing open-set fine-grained neighbor categories. A fully automated pipeline queries GPT-4 for k related fine-grained classes per target category, retrieves web images, and filters them using DINOv2 cosine similarity thresholds against class prototypes. During training, a Neighbor-Silencing CE loss (NS-CE) down-weights the cross-entropy contribution of neighbor-category pairs by a factor λ_s (default 0.1), preventing auxiliary classes from overwhelming the optimization while still encouraging feature-manifold continuity. At test time, the classifier is used directly by masking out auxiliary weight vectors rather than retraining via linear probing, which preserves the separation hyperplanes shaped by the auxiliary categories. The method is backbone-agnostic and has been validated with random initialization, CLIP, and DINOv2 pre-training.

## Results

On ImageNet-LT trained from scratch, the method achieves 68.2% overall accuracy (vs. 60.9% baseline), with a striking +16.0% gain on the Few-shot split (57.4% vs. 41.4%). With DINOv2 pre-training, it reaches 82.0% overall on ImageNet-LT, 87.0% on iNaturalist 18, and 50.8% on Places-LT, consistently improving the Few-shot split by 2.5–5.1% over the Bal-CE baseline across all three datasets. The gains are largest under random initialization and on the most imbalanced dataset (ImageNet-LT), while still providing consistent 1–4% improvements when strong pre-trained backbones (CLIP, DINOv2) are used.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is compared against three baselines across all three datasets and pre-training paradigms: Bal-CE (Cui et al., CVPR 2019) as the re-weighting baseline, LiVT (Xu et al., CVPR 2023) for training-from-scratch, and LIFT (Liu et al., CVPR 2023) for CLIP fine-tuning. On ImageNet-LT from scratch, the proposed method achieves 68.2% overall vs. LiVT's 60.9% (+7.3%), with the Few-shot gap widening from 41.0% to 57.4%. Under CLIP fine-tuning on ImageNet-LT, it reaches 78.8% overall vs. LIFT's 77.0% (+1.8%), and under DINOv2 it attains 82.0% vs. Bal-CE's 79.6% (+2.4%). The same pattern holds on iNaturalist 18 (e.g., 87.0% vs. 85.0% with DINOv2) and Places-LT (e.g., 50.8% vs. 49.5% with DINOv2). Notably, adding the same auxiliary data to these SOTA baselines without the neighbor-silencing loss (denoted †) yields only modest gains (e.g., LiVT† 65.2% vs. LiVT 60.9%), confirming that the neighbor-silencing loss and direct classifier masking are critical to unlocking the full benefit of the auxiliary categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the long-tail image classification literature by introducing open-set auxiliary data (crawled from the web via LLM-guided category search) to increase dataset granularity, departing from prior re-weighting (BalCE, LiVT) and data-augmentation (Mixup, CutMix, Remix) approaches that remain bounded by the existing closed-set label space. The neighbor-silencing loss is a targeted modification of the standard balanced cross-entropy that down-weights logits for auxiliary categories directly related to the target class, preventing them from dominating optimization. The empirical pilot study connecting granularity to imbalance robustness is novel and motivates the entire framework, whereas prior work treated imbalance as purely a frequency problem rather than a representational-diversity problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The granularity finding—that finer-grained datasets are less sensitive to class imbalance—offers a conceptual reframing of long-tail learning that could inform dataset design and augmentation strategies beyond this specific method. The fully automated LLM + web-crawling pipeline is practical and reproducible, but its effectiveness depends on web image quality, LLM accuracy in suggesting relevant fine-grained categories, and the filtering thresholds, which may not transfer cleanly to domains with limited web coverage. Open questions the authors raise include what types of auxiliary data are most compatible with a given target dataset and how to optimally integrate open-set data during training, suggesting this is a starting point rather than a complete solution.
