## Problem

Accurate 3D gaze estimation in unconstrained real-world settings remains difficult due to limited diversity in available 3D gaze datasets, which are typically collected in controlled lab environments with restricted gaze distributions and subject diversity. Additionally, existing modality-specific models for images or videos cannot jointly leverage all available training data, and prior weakly-supervised approaches (e.g., using LAEO labels) are limited by narrow gaze distributions and mutual-gaze constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252407/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a 224×224 head crop from either a single image (T=1, duplicated to T=2) or an 8-frame video clip, standardized via a pre-trained CrowdHuman head detector with a 10% bounding-box downscale. It outputs a normalized 3D gaze vector per frame, predicted by a shared MLP after mean spatial pooling and temporal interpolation in the decoder. The unified 4D tensor representation (T×H×W×3) allows the same network to process both modalities without architectural changes.

## Method

The ST-WSGE framework is a two-stage self-training pipeline: stage 1 trains a Gaze Transformer (GaT) on 3D gaze datasets (Gaze360, GFIE), then stage 2 generates pseudo-3D labels on 2D gaze-following data (GazeFollow) by geometrically rotating the predicted 3D gaze around the z-axis so its x,y projection aligns with the 2D ground truth, preserving the predicted z-component. GaT is a modality-agnostic architecture built on a Swin3D hierarchical spatiotemporal encoder with shifted-window attention over 4D patches (t=2), enabling joint training on image and video datasets within a single model. The gaze decoder applies mean spatial pooling, temporal interpolation to restore input length, and a shared single-hidden-layer MLP to produce per-frame gaze vectors. This combination of diverse pseudo-labeled training data and unified spatiotemporal modeling yields state-of-the-art within- and cross-domain generalization on Gaze360, GFIE, and MPIIFaceGaze.

## Results

On the physically unconstrained Gaze360 benchmark, ST-WSGE(GaT) trained on G360+GazeFollow achieves 13.19° (image) and 12.17° (video) angular error on G360 Full, outperforming the previous best MSA (13.9° image) and MSA+Seq (12.5° video). On GFIE, the method reaches 12.99° (image) and 12.68° (video), a 26% relative improvement over the GFIE baseline (17.7° image). In cross-dataset transfer (trained on GFIE, tested on G360 Full), ST-WSGE achieves 21.48°/21.06° (img/vid), substantially beating Kothari et al.'s weakly-supervised approach (52.6°/25.0°). On the constrained G360 Face subset, ST-WSGE attains 9.92° video error, surpassing SAM-LSTM (10.05°) and all image-only methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest unconstrained video baseline MSA+Seq [8], ST-WSGE(GaT) wins on G360 Full video (12.17° vs 12.5°) and G360 40 video (7.67° vs not reported, but supervised GaT already achieves 8.23°). Against MSA [8] on image, ST-WSGE improves from 13.9° to 13.19° on G360 Full. In cross-dataset transfer, ST-WSGE trained on GFIE+GF dramatically outperforms Kothari et al. [31] (trained on ETH+AVA): 21.48° vs 52.6° on G360 Full image, and 12.66° vs 16.9° on G360 40 video when additionally using MPSGaze. On the constrained G360 Face subset (Table 5), ST-WSGE beats SAM-LSTM [26] on Face video (9.92° vs 10.05°) and Face 180 video (9.75° vs 9.84°), and beats SPMCCA [55] on Face 40 image (8.30° vs 8.62°), but loses to SAM-LSTM on Face 40 video (7.48° vs 6.92°) and to GazeTR [10] on Face image (10.84° vs 10.62°). Overall, the method achieves state-of-the-art on unconstrained benchmarks (G360 Full, GFIE) while remaining competitive but not uniformly dominant on the near-frontal constrained subset where tight face crops give baselines a resolution advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the weakly-supervised gaze estimation line initiated by Kothari et al. (CVPR 2021), who generated pseudo-3D gaze from 2D LAEO labels using head-size heuristics. The key departures are: (1) replacing the limited LAEO dataset with the more diverse GazeFollow gaze-following annotations, (2) substituting heuristic-based z-estimation with a self-training geometric projection that reuses a pre-trained model's depth prediction, and (3) introducing a modality-agnostic Swin3D-based Gaze Transformer that jointly learns from image and video data, moving beyond the modality-specific architectures (CNN+LSTM, separate image/video models) that dominated prior unconstrained gaze work (Gaze360, MCGaze, MSA+Seq).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical bottleneck in unconstrained 3D gaze estimation: the scarcity of diverse, naturally occurring 3D gaze annotations. The ST-WSGE framework offers a practical recipe for leveraging easily obtainable 2D gaze-following labels to close the domain gap, and the finding that image training data substantially improves video generalization (34–38% on GFIE) is practically important given the extreme scarcity of video 3D gaze datasets. Limitations include the dependence of generalization gains on the diversity of the pre-training stage (e.g., GFIE's non-frontal distribution hurts frontal benchmarks) and the single-iteration self-training loop, which raises open questions about whether iterative refinement or additional 2D gaze sources could yield further gains.
