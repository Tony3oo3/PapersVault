## Problem

Existing text-to-image models cannot generate design images (posters, logos, flyers) with accurate, style-consistent visual text integrated into the visual content. Prior visual-text-rendering approaches either overlay text onto pre-generated images or require predefined text regions, both of which decouple text from visual generation and produce style/color inconsistencies or constrained layouts. No end-to-end method existed that simultaneously synthesizes coherent visual and textual design elements from a single prompt.

## Input / Output

The method takes a plain natural-language text prompt as its sole input—no layout maps, glyph images, or position annotations are required. The prompt is internally augmented with a character-level token sequence (e.g., '<|M|><|a|><|r|><|d|><|i|>') appended after the original description before being fed to the CLIP text encoder. The output is a high-resolution design image (SDXL resolution) in which the specified text is rendered with correct characters, appropriate styling, and natural spatial integration with surrounding visual elements.

## Method

DesignDiffusion fine-tunes SDXL in two stages. In stage one, it introduces 97 new character-level tokens into the CLIP text encoder so each letter receives a dedicated embedding, and adds a character localization loss that regularizes cross-attention maps in the UNet's middle layers to concentrate each character token's attention on its corresponding segmentation-mask region. In stage two, it applies Self-Play DPO (SP-DPO): the model generates K candidate images per prompt, a multimodal OCR model selects the worst-rendering sample as the losing data while the ground-truth image serves as the winning data, and a DPO objective (derived via ELBO and Jensen's inequality for the diffusion chain) pushes the model toward the winning distribution and away from the losing one. This self-play scheme eliminates the need for human preference annotations while specifically targeting text-rendering accuracy.

## Results

DesignDiffusion achieves a FID of 19.87 on the design image test set, substantially outperforming the next-best text-to-image model FLUX (35.28) and all visual-text rendering methods (FID 55–65). On visual text quality, it attains the highest OCR precision (0.888), recall (0.837), F-score (0.862), and accuracy (0.631), surpassing the closest text-rendering competitor TextDiffuser-2 (0.866/0.792/0.827/0.583) by 2–5 points across all four metrics. In a user study, DesignDiffusion is preferred over SDXL, TextDiffuser-2, and AnyText on text quality (51%), layout aesthetics (54%), and text-image matching (52%), while being slightly behind SDXL on pure image aesthetics (40% vs. 43%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DesignDiffusion outperforms all eight baselines on every reported metric. Against general-purpose text-to-image models (SDXL, SD3, FLUX, DeepFloyd-IF), it improves FID by 15–32 points and text accuracy by 0.19–0.39, showing that these models struggle with both image fidelity and legible text when applied to design tasks. Among dedicated visual-text rendering methods, TextDiffuser-2 is the closest competitor on text metrics (F-score 0.827 vs. 0.862; accuracy 0.583 vs. 0.631), but its FID of 63.92 is more than 3× worse than DesignDiffusion's 19.87, reflecting the quality degradation caused by its layout-planning and inpainting pipeline. GlyphControl and AnyText, which rely on predefined glyph positions or strong text-region fitting, show the weakest text accuracy (0.218 and 0.230) and the worst FID scores (60.07 and 64.69), confirming the paper's argument that separating text and image generation harms overall design quality. In the user study, DesignDiffusion is preferred over TextDiffuser-2 and AnyText on all four subjective criteria, and edges out SDXL on text quality, layout aesthetics, and text-image matching while trailing slightly on raw image aesthetics (40% vs. 43%).

## Relation to Prior Work

DesignDiffusion builds on Stable Diffusion XL as its base architecture and departs from prior visual text rendering methods (TextDiffuser, GlyphControl, AnyText) that rely on two-stage pipelines—either inpainting text into pre-generated images or pre-planning text regions via LLMs—by adopting a fully end-to-end, one-stage generation approach. It extends the DPO alignment paradigm from LLMs (Rafailov et al.) and diffusion-specific DPO (DiffusionDPO) into a self-play variant that eliminates the need for human-annotated preference pairs. The character-level tokenization of prompts and the cross-attention localization loss draw on prior work showing that cross-attention maps in UNets control spatial token-to-pixel correspondence (Prompt-to-Prompt, FastComposer), but apply these insights specifically to newly introduced character embeddings rather than existing prompt tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: existing text-to-image models produce visually appealing images but fail at accurate text rendering, while dedicated text rendering methods sacrifice overall image quality and aesthetic coherence. The end-to-end framing and the 1M design image dataset with character-level annotations provide a foundation for future work on design-centric generation. Limitations include a slight aesthetic regression versus vanilla SDXL (40% vs 43% in user study), the SP-DPO winning/losing selection relying solely on text accuracy rather than holistic image quality, and evaluation limited to English text. Open questions include generalization to non-Latin scripts, multilingual design, and whether the character-level prompt enhancement transfers to other fine-grained rendering tasks.
