## Problem

Finetuning-free personalized image generation methods rely on a single training stage with simple image reconstruction, producing images that are locally inconsistent with reference images in specific patches. Traditional DPO-based alignment is unsuitable here because it judges only overall image quality, whereas the inconsistencies are patch-level: a 'superior' image may still contain low-quality regions that the model would incorrectly learn to reproduce.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251474/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference image (defining the subject's appearance) and a text prompt as input, and outputs a personalized generated image in a new scene. It operates as a post-training refinement on pre-trained finetuning-free models (e.g., IP-Adapter-Plus with SDXL), where only the image-feature projection parameters are trainable. Patch quality is estimated on ViT-Base feature maps (H×W×D), and the weighted loss is applied at the patch level before upsampled to full image resolution for the diffusion noise-prediction objective.

## Method

PatchDPO introduces a patch-level preference optimization loss for diffusion-based personalized generation. A pre-trained ViT-Base vision model is first finetuned via self-supervised training (spatial augmentation with MSE consistency plus a regularization term against the frozen original model) to improve patch-feature discriminability, achieving 83.7% S_patch on HPatches. Patch quality is then estimated by computing the maximum cosine similarity between each generated-image patch feature and all reference-image patch features. The final training loss combines two weighted reconstruction terms: (1) reconstructing the generated image with weights proportional to its patch quality (rewarding good patches), and (2) reconstructing the reference image as ground truth with weights proportional to 1 minus the reference patch quality (penalizing and correcting low-quality regions). This is applied on a constructed 50k-image dataset where reference images are generated by Stable Diffusion with clean backgrounds to avoid foreground-background confusion.

## Results

On DreamBench (single-object personalized generation), PatchDPO achieves DINO 0.727, CLIP-I 0.838, CLIP-T 0.292, and Avg 0.619, setting a new state-of-the-art across all finetuning-free methods. Under the Kosmos-G evaluation setting (Table 3), PatchDPO further reaches DINO 0.831, CLIP-I 0.880, and Avg 0.666, outperforming OmniGen (0.650 Avg) and Emu2-Gen (0.634 Avg). The method improves its base model IP-Adapter-Plus from 0.600 to 0.619 Avg, a gain of 0.019, while also surpassing finetuning-based methods like DreamBooth (Imagen) (0.605 Avg) and Custom Diffusion (0.579 Avg).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PatchDPO outperforms all 15 compared baselines on the average metric (0.619 vs. 0.605 for the best finetuning-based DreamBooth-Imagen and 0.600 for the best finetuning-free IP-Adapter-Plus). On image-similarity metrics, PatchDPO leads all methods on DINO (0.727 vs. 0.711 for Subject-Diffusion, the closest competitor) and CLIP-I (0.838 vs. 0.826 for IP-Adapter-Plus), confirming that patch-level feedback effectively corrects local inconsistencies. The only metric where PatchDPO trails is CLIP-T (text alignment): MS-Diffusion achieves 0.321 and DreamBooth-Imagen 0.306, both above PatchDPO's 0.292, though the gap is small (≤0.029) and the authors attribute this to the training data construction using matched text prompts. Against its direct base model IP-Adapter-Plus, PatchDPO gains +0.035 DINO, +0.012 CLIP-I, and +0.019 Avg, demonstrating that the additional patch-level DPO stage provides substantial improvement without any test-time finetuning. On the multi-object Concept101 benchmark (reported in the appendix), PatchDPO similarly surpasses Subject-Diffusion and JeDi, extending its advantage beyond single-object settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts DPO (Direct Preference Optimization) from the LLM alignment literature to the diffusion-model setting for personalized image generation, but departs from both traditional DPO and prior diffusion-DPO work (e.g., Diffusion-DPO, DDPO) by operating at the patch level rather than the whole-image level. It builds on finetuning-free personalized generation methods (IP-Adapter, ELITE, Subject-Diffusion, JeDi) by introducing an additional post-training optimization stage, and leverages pre-trained ViT feature maps (inspired by ProtoPNet and SFD2) for patch-to-patch similarity comparison. The self-supervised fine-tuning of the vision model to improve patch-level feature discrimination is a novel component not present in prior patch-matching or DPO-for-diffusion work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PatchDPO offers a practical, plug-in post-training recipe that can improve any existing finetuning-free personalized generation model without architectural changes, which is valuable given the rapid proliferation of such methods. The ablation showing that whole-image DPO actively degrades performance (Combination 3) while patch-level weighting improves it provides a clear conceptual justification for the finer-grained feedback. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond IP-Adapter-style models to other diffusion architectures, whether the self-supervised vision-model fine-tuning is truly necessary versus simply using a shallower layer, and whether the synthetic training dataset (SD-generated reference images) introduces a distribution gap that limits real-world applicability.
