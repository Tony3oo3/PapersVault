## Problem

3D Vision Grounding requires understanding fine-grained semantics and complex spatial relationships, but point-cloud-based representations are semantically sparse and noisy, and prior zero-shot methods reduce multi-relational spatial reasoning to simple binary checks while ignoring the conditionality and interconnections among relationships. This leads to a significant performance gap between zero-shot and supervised approaches, particularly on view-dependent and hard samples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250797/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a 3D point cloud, a set of 2D image frames with their camera poses (used to train the 3DGS reconstruction), and a free-form natural language utterance. It outputs the identity of the grounded 3D object in the scene. The 3DGS reconstruction is performed offline from the point cloud and images; at inference time, the system renders a single 2D image from an LLM-inferred viewpoint and feeds it to GPT-4o Vision for textual semantic description, which is then combined with 3D scene metadata for code-based reasoning.

## Method

The zero-shot neuro-symbolic framework has three stages. First, an LLM-based utterance parser decomposes the sentence into a relationship graph (vertices = objects, edges = spatial/attribute relations) capturing inter-relationship connections, and a chain of semantics (an ordered list of sub-goals) that makes the conditionality of each relation explicit. Second, the 3D scene is reconstructed via 3D Gaussian Splatting; the LLM filters scene objects relevant to the utterance, PCA on their centers yields a principal-axis viewpoint, and the 3DGS renderer produces a high-quality 2D image from that pose, which GPT-4o Vision converts into a textual fine-grained description. Third, a chain-of-semantics-guided programmer (GPT-4o) generates executable Python code that performs step-by-step spatial filtering over the 3D object list using both 3D coordinates and the 2D textual semantics; a grounded-aware self-check module inspects the execution result (wrong count, empty result, syntax errors) and, if invalid, feeds the error message back into the code-generation prompt to re-execute, preventing repetition of the same mistake.

## Results

On Nr3D, the full method (with grounded-aware self-check) achieves 60.8% overall grounding accuracy, surpassing the best prior zero-shot method (VLM-Grounder, 48.0%) by 12.8% and approaching supervised baselines like ViL3DRel (64.4%) and CoT3DRef (64.4%). On Sr3D, the method reaches 91.4% overall accuracy, exceeding the best supervised model (3D-VisTA, 76.4%) by 15.0%. The 3DGS component contributes a 14.7% improvement on view-dependent Sr3D samples (37.5% → 52.2%), and the self-check mechanism adds 4.5% on Nr3D and 1.8% on Sr3D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Nr3D, the method (60.8%) outperforms both zero-shot baselines—ZS3DVG (39.0%) by 21.8% and VLM-Grounder (48.0%) by 12.8%—while remaining within 3.6% of the strongest supervised methods (ViL3DRel and CoT3DRef at 64.4%). On Sr3D, the zero-shot method (91.4%) decisively surpasses all supervised baselines, including 3D-VisTA (76.4%), CoT3DRef (73.6%), and ViL3DRel (72.8%), by margins of 15.0–19.0%. In data-efficiency comparisons, the method with 0% training data (91.4% Sr3D, 60.8% Nr3D) outperforms NS3D at 10% train data (61.2% Sr3D) and LARC at 25% train data (34.8% Nr3D), demonstrating that the neuro-symbolic zero-shot pipeline eliminates the need for task-specific training entirely. The view-dependent category remains the weakest for the method on Sr3D (54.3%), where template-based utterances provide limited viewpoint cues, though 3DGS rendering recovers 14.7% over the no-GS variant.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual programming paradigm (Gupta & Kembhavi, CVPR 2023) by introducing a 'chain of semantics' that decomposes utterances into a relationship graph and sequential reasoning steps, guiding LLM-generated Python code for multi-step spatial grounding. It departs from prior zero-shot 3DVG methods (ZS3DVG, VLM-Grounder) that flatten complex spatial relationships into binary predicates, and from neuro-symbolic approaches (NS3D, LARC) that encode relationships as fixed programs, by instead using 3D Gaussian Splatting as an interactive scene representation from which the LLM can infer viewpoints and render high-quality 2D images for fine-grained semantic extraction. The core novelty is the coupling of 3DGS-based viewpoint inference with chain-of-semantics-guided code generation and a grounded-aware self-check loop, a pipeline not previously explored in the 3DVG literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a zero-shot GPT-4o-based pipeline matches or exceeds supervised models (91.4% on Sr3D vs. 76.4% best supervised) signals that LLM-driven reasoning may be approaching the ceiling for template-based grounding, potentially reducing the need for large annotated 3DVG datasets. However, the persistent view-dependent gap (54.3% vs. 92.8% overall on Sr3D) reveals that viewpoint-sensitive spatial reasoning remains a hard open problem, and the method's dependence on GPT-4o and per-scene 3DGS training limits practical deployment. Open questions include whether the chain-of-semantics decomposition generalizes to multi-object or dynamic scenes, and whether the self-check mechanism can be made more principled than iterative LLM re-prompting.
