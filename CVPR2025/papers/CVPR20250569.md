## Problem

Pseudo-changes induced by imaging environmental factors (sun elevation, seasons) cause spectral shifts between bitemporal remote sensing images, leading to false change detections. Existing GAN-based style alignment methods are limited by optimization complexity and lack of physical guidance, while the recently effective spectrum transformation (ST) suffers from two drawbacks: its transformation space targets visual consistency rather than discriminative features, and its fixed extraction box requires inefficient manual cross-validation for optimal parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250569/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a bitemporal remote sensing image pair (x^{t1}, x^{t2}) fed into a shared-weight siamese encoder and produces a pixel-wise binary change map indicating changed vs. unchanged regions. FST is applied in the feature space at shallow encoder layers (L1, L2) rather than in image space, operating on multi-channel amplitude spectra where each channel learns independent strides. The design is plug-and-play: it introduces no additional network parameters and adds negligible computation (e.g., 0.90G FLOPs for U-Net), making it injectable into any existing siamese-based change detection framework.

## Method

FeaSpect formulates spectrum transformation in the feature space (FST), replacing the low-frequency Fourier amplitude spectrum of one bitemporal feature with that of its counterpart while preserving phase, thereby aligning style while retaining high-dimensional semantic content. Two learning mechanisms make FST trainable: (1) EB-AA makes the mask values within the extraction box differentiable, so backpropagation adaptively focuses on informative amplitude values; (2) EB-LS parameterizes the box size via two 1D differentiable masking functions whose outer product forms a 2D mask, allowing the horizontal and vertical strides to be learned end-to-end. A scaling extension (Eq. 7) multiplies strides by feature dimensions H and W to narrow the search scope and improve gradient magnitude compatibility with batch normalization. FST is applied only to shallow layers where style information predominantly manifests, and the entire pipeline is optimized with standard binary cross-entropy loss without auxiliary objectives.

## Results

On the SVCD dataset, FeaSpect with a Swin-Transformer backbone achieves 97.33% F1 and 94.81% IoU, surpassing the best prior Transformer-based method (SwinsUNet, 94.48% F1) by 2.85% and the best ConvNet-based method (ChangeSTAR, 94.75% F1) by 2.58%. With a lightweight U-Net backbone (1.35M params), FeaSpect still reaches 95.81% F1 and 91.96% IoU, outperforming all ConvNet baselines by at least 6.55% F1. On the more challenging DSIFN dataset (cross-domain generalization), FeaSpect with MiT-B2 achieves 87.09% F1 and 77.14% IoU, beating ChangeFormer (86.47% F1) by 0.62% F1, while the Swin-Transformer variant reaches 74.42% F1, surpassing SwinsUNet (72.59% F1) by 1.83%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FeaSpect outperforms all 15 baselines on both SVCD and DSIFN across every metric. On SVCD, the gains are largest against the original FC-Siam-diff-res baseline (86.02% → 95.81% F1, +9.79%) and FCN-PP (87.21% → 95.81% F1, +8.60%), while the margin narrows against the strongest baselines: ChangeSTAR (94.75% → 96.31% F1, +1.56%) and SwinsUNet (94.48% → 97.33% F1, +2.85%). On DSIFN, where training and test data come from different locations, FeaSpect with MiT-B2 edges out ChangeFormer (86.47% → 87.09% F1, +0.62%) and the Swin-Transformer variant surpasses SwinsUNet (72.59% → 74.42% F1, +1.83%). Against domain-shift-specific methods (Table 3), FeaSpect (95.81% F1) beats IBN-Net (92.36%), FDA (90.35%), and DLSF (89.30%), confirming that feature-space spectrum learning with learnable strides is more effective than GAN-based style transfer, image-space spectrum replacement, or feature normalization. The method is also plug-and-play: when injected into existing architectures (Table 4), it consistently adds 2.7–22.4% F1 improvement with negligible extra parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Fourier Domain Adaptation (FDA) framework of Yang et al. [46] from image-space spectrum transformation to a learnable feature-space variant (FST) for bitemporal change detection. It departs from prior GAN-based pseudo-change mitigation (DLSF, CDGAN) by replacing adversarial style transfer with a differentiable frequency-domain operation, and from feature normalization approaches (IBN-Net) by explicitly modeling style as low-frequency amplitude content rather than removing it via statistics. The two learning mechanisms—adaptive attention over mask values and learnable strides—transform a fixed hyperparameter search into an end-to-end optimization problem, a departure from the original FDA's fixed extraction box.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, zero-parameter nature of FeaSpect makes it immediately deployable on any siamese change-detection backbone (CNN or Transformer), which is practically significant for the remote sensing community. However, the method is restricted to shallow layers and assumes style resides in low-frequency amplitude components; whether this assumption holds for all sensor types or multi-temporal (>2) settings remains open. The large gains over weak baselines (e.g., +13% F1 over FC-EF) partly reflect the weakness of those baselines, while improvements over strong baselines (ChangeSTAR: +3.25% F1) are more modest, suggesting diminishing returns as backbone capacity increases. Future work could explore whether the learnable frequency mask generalizes to non-siamese architectures or to tasks beyond bitemporal comparison.
