## Problem

Real-time view synthesis of fuzzy geometries (hair, plush) on low-power mobile devices remains unsolved: surface-based methods are fast but cannot represent semi-transparent, multi-layered structures, while volume-based methods (NeRF, 3DGS) require tens to hundreds of samples per ray, empty-space skipping, or primitive sorting — all of which are prohibitively expensive on smartphones. The paper seeks a representation that bounds sampling to a small fixed number of locations, avoids sorting, and still captures fuzzy appearance.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images with known camera poses and produces k (3–9) lightweight semi-transparent mesh shells, each with a UV atlas of spherical-harmonic coefficient textures at mixed resolutions (2048² for base color down to 256² for the highest SH degree), totaling ~14 MB per mesh. The final asset is a set of PNG texture images plus simplified meshes (~2 MB each, 0.02% of original triangle count) rendered by a WebGL pipeline that rasterizes all shells in a fixed outer-to-inner order and alpha-blends them in the framebuffer.

## Method

The core representation, k-SDF, models k surfaces as a main signed distance field plus k−1 learned offset fields (shells) whose cumulative displacements enforce a strict outer-to-inner ordering, eliminating the need for per-frame sorting. Training proceeds in two stages: first a standard NeuS-like opaque SDF is optimized for 100k iterations with a scheduled β, then the remaining shells are added with uniform initial spacing Δo and trained for 50k iterations using Eikonal and curvature losses to keep surfaces smooth and non-intersecting. After implicit optimization, each SDF is extracted via marching cubes, aggressively simplified, UV-parameterized, and textured with a neural hash-grid that predicts per-texel SH coefficients; the texture is discretized to a fixed target resolution via bilinear interpolation to match OpenGL fragment-shader behavior. At render time, each ray intersects at most k triangles (one per shell), and the per-surface color and view-dependent transparency are alpha-blended in fixed order with a grazing-angle attenuation term, requiring only 3–9 samples per ray and no sorting or empty-space traversal.

## Results

On the Shelly dataset, the 7-Mesh configuration achieves 34.50 PSNR, 0.981 SSIM, and 0.109 LPIPS, outperforming MobileNeRF (29.30 PSNR) and PermutoSDF (29.85 PSNR) by over 4 dB in PSNR. On the custom plushy/hairy monkey scenes, 7-Mesh reaches 35.63 PSNR and 0.977 SSIM, while on DTU it achieves 36.77 PSNR and 0.987 SSIM. In terms of real-time performance, 7-Mesh renders at 42 FPS on a Samsung A52s smartphone and 70 FPS on a Dell XPS 13 laptop, compared to only 8 and 18 FPS respectively for full 3DGS, while using 110 MB of storage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MobileNeRF (the primary surface-based real-time baseline), the 7-Mesh method wins decisively on all quality metrics: +5.2 dB PSNR on Shelly (34.50 vs 29.30), +4.7 dB on Custom (35.63 vs 30.89), and also renders ~2× faster on smartphone (42 vs 24 FPS). Against PermutoSDF, the method gains +4.65 dB PSNR on Shelly and +2.32 dB on Custom, confirming that multi-layer shells substantially outperform single-surface representations for fuzzy geometry. Against full 3DGS, the method trades ~0.9 dB PSNR on Shelly (34.50 vs 35.44) and ~1.7 dB on Custom (35.63 vs 37.34) for a 5× speedup on smartphone (42 vs 8 FPS) and a 4× speedup on laptop (70 vs 18 FPS), while also achieving higher SSIM (0.981 vs 0.975 on Shelly). Against AdaptiveShells and QuadFields, the method is ~1.5 dB lower in PSNR on Shelly, but both of those baselines require specialized ray-tracing hardware or large meshes (1213 MB for QuadFields) and cannot run in real-time on general-purpose hardware. Overall, the method occupies a unique niche: it is the fastest method that still models fuzzy geometry with multiple layers, at a modest quality cost relative to the highest-quality (but slower) volumetric baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends NeuS's single-SDF formulation to a k-SDF representation with view-dependent transparency, drawing conceptual inspiration from textured shells long used in game graphics to simulate fur and hair. It positions itself between fast but geometry-limited surface methods (BakedSDF, BOG, MobileNeRF) and high-quality but slow volume/splatting methods (NeRF, 3DGS, SMERF), departing from both by using rasterization to locate a bounded number of sampling points per ray and a fixed-order alpha blend that eliminates the need for sorting or empty-space traversal. The baking pipeline—marching cubes, mesh simplification, UV parameterization, and neural SH textures grounded to a target resolution—adapts ideas from MERF and standard game-engine texturing to the multi-layer setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a practical gap: real-time rendering of fuzzy objects (hair, plush) on low-power smartphones, a regime where 3DGS and NeRF-based methods fail to meet frame-rate targets. The key open questions include handling thin structures (limited by the underlying SDF), grazing-angle artifacts in sparsely observed scenes, and the lack of an end-to-end training pipeline that directly produces renderable assets. The finding that 9 layers underperform 7 due to gradient starvation in deeper shells suggests a fundamental tension between representational capacity and optimization stability in multi-layer implicit surfaces.
