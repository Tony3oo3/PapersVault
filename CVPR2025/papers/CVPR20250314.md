## Problem

Vanilla diffusion models for cloud removal start the diffusion process from pure noise, discarding the substantial structural information already present in cloudy satellite images and causing pixel-level inconsistency in restored outputs. Existing mean-reverting diffusion models (MRDMs) address this by anchoring the diffusion to the cloudy image, but their modules are intricately coupled with opaque interrelationships, making them difficult to modify or extend. This paper seeks to both establish a principled direct diffusion bridge between cloudy and cloudless images and to elucidate the design space so that individual components can be independently improved.

## Input / Output

The method accepts one or L sequential cloudy satellite images (RGB or multi-spectral Sentinel-2) as the primary input, optionally concatenated with auxiliary SAR or infrared images as conditioning. For multi-temporal CR, L weight-sharing encoders process the sequence in parallel, and the final output is a single cloudless image obtained by mean-fusing the independently restored temporal instances. The diffusion process starts from a noisy version of the cloudy image (the 'noisy mean') rather than from pure noise, and the denoiser operates at multiple resolutions via the HDiT hourglass architecture.

## Method

EMRDM reformulates the forward process as a mean-reverting SDE dx = f(t)(x−μ)dt + g(t)dω_t where μ is the cloudy image, parameterized by s(t)=1/(1+αt) and σ(t)=t for design simplicity, and derives an ODE-based backward process for sampling. A preconditioning technique (adapted from EDM) restructures the denoiser into a network F_θ whose inputs and outputs are scaled to unit variance via four noise-level-dependent factors c_in, c_skip, c_out, c_noise, with additional hyperparameters σ_mu and σ_cov accounting for the cloudy-image mean. For multi-temporal CR, the denoising network employs L weight-sharing HDiT encoders, a temporal HDiT bottleneck block with a Temporal Fusion Self-Attention (TFSA) mechanism that uses a learnable single-token query to collapse the temporal dimension and generate attention masks, and a single shared decoder that fuses temporal skip connections via those masks. Training perturbs each temporal image independently and denoises them jointly under a weighted L2 loss, while sampling uses a 5-step stochastic or deterministic Euler integrator on the derived ODE, with the stochastic variant adding controlled noise re-injection for improved PSNR.

## Results

EMRDM achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four benchmark datasets. On CUHK-CR1, it reaches 27.281 PSNR / 0.8007 SSIM / 0.0218 LPIPS, surpassing the previous best (DE-MemoryNet, 26.183 PSNR) by over 1 dB. On the multi-modal SEN12MS-CR dataset, EMRDM attains 32.14 PSNR / 0.924 SSIM / 5.267 SAM, outperforming DiffCR (31.77 PSNR) and ACA-Net (29.78 PSNR). On the multi-temporal Sen2_MTC_New dataset (L=3), it achieves 20.067 PSNR / 0.709 SSIM / 0.255 LPIPS, leading the next-best DiffCR (19.150 PSNR) by nearly 1 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMRDM outperforms all 23 baselines across every dataset and metric. On the mono-temporal CUHK-CR1/CR2 benchmarks (RGB only, no auxiliary modalities), it beats the strongest prior method DE-MemoryNet [58] by 1.10 dB PSNR and 0.026 SSIM on CR1, and by 0.25 dB PSNR on CR2, while also achieving the lowest LPIPS (0.0218 vs. 0.0290). On the multi-modal SEN12MS-CR dataset (optical + SAR), EMRDM surpasses the previous best diffusion-based method DiffCR [74] by 0.37 dB PSNR and 0.554 SAM, and leads the non-diffusion ACA-Net [26] by 2.36 dB PSNR. On the multi-temporal Sen2_MTC_New dataset (L=3), the gap widens: EMRDM exceeds DiffCR [74] by 0.92 dB PSNR and 0.038 SSIM, and outperforms the best GAN-based method CTGAN [24] by 1.76 dB PSNR. Notably, EMRDM maintains its advantage even without auxiliary SAR/IR inputs (CUHK-CR1/CR2), demonstrating that the mean-reverting diffusion formulation itself provides substantial gains over noise-initiated generative diffusion (DiffCR, DDPM-CR) and GAN-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts mean-reverting diffusion models (MRDMs) from Luo et al. [41] to cloud removal, while simultaneously reformulating the forward SDE and introducing an ODE-based backward process to create a modular, transparent framework inspired by EDM [31]. Unlike prior diffusion-based CR methods (DiffCR, DDPM-CR) that initiate diffusion from pure noise, EMRDM starts from noisy cloudy images, preserving pixel-level structural fidelity. The multi-temporal extension builds on UTAE [19] and L-TAE [18] temporal attention architectures, repurposing them within a diffusion denoising network with a novel temporal fusion self-attention mechanism that generates resolution-aware attention masks for skip-connection fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular design space enables orthogonal modification of individual components (denoiser, training, sampling) without re-deriving the full framework, lowering the barrier to adapting MRDMs to other restoration tasks. The finding that only 5 ODE sampling steps suffice (versus hundreds in generative DMs) makes the approach practically deployable for large-scale satellite processing. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to longer temporal sequences beyond L=3, how it scales to very high-resolution imagery, and whether the preconditioning hyperparameters (σ_mu, σ_cov) can be learned rather than set manually.
