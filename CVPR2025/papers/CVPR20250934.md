## Problem

Foundation models like SAM and CLIP suffer significant performance degradation under distribution shifts, weak supervision, and adversarial attacks in the open world. Existing domain generalization and adversarial fine-tuning methods are typically task-specific or architecture-specific (e.g., mask alignment for SAM, semantic consistency for CLIP), limiting their transferability and practical deployment. This paper seeks a universal, plug-and-play framework that enhances FM generalizability without modifying internal architectures.

## Input / Output

For SAM, the method consumes RGB images with weak prompts (bounding boxes, 5 sparse points, or coarse polygons with P/20 vertices) and outputs segmentation masks; for CLIP, it takes RGB images with text prompts and produces class labels, captions, or VQA answers. The OCRT module operates as an add-on that extracts Relation Tokens from the FM's encoder features and injects them into the existing fine-tuning pipeline, requiring no architectural changes to the base model. The number of object-centric slots (K=8) and retained informative concepts (K̃=7) are fixed hyperparameters, and the method is trained with only a small amount of downstream-task data.

## Method

OCRT decomposes dense visual inputs through three stages inspired by human cognition. First, Slot-Attention with GRU-based iterative refinement binds learnable object-centric representations to spatial regions via a spatial broadcast decoder that performs unsupervised decoupling through softmax competition over activation masks. Second, object-centric representations are projected into a semantic concept space, and a cosine-similarity-based importance score filters out irrelevant concepts, retaining only the top-K̃ informative ones. Third, a concept-based graph with flexible degree (proportional to concept importance) performs multi-layer relational reasoning, aggregating node embeddings via similarity-weighted neighbor averaging and projecting the final layer into Relation Tokens. These tokens are concatenated with original tokens and participate in the base fine-tuning loss (self-training for SAM, adversarial fine-tuning for CLIP), enabling the FM to capture high-order relational factors that are invariant to distribution shift.

## Results

On SAM segmentation, OCRT achieves up to +35.91 mIoU over the original SAM on kvasir-SEG (poly prompt: 89.94 vs. 54.03) and +17.08 on ISIC (box: 83.82 vs. 66.74), surpassing fully supervised fine-tuning by 6.5% on kvasir-SEG. On CLIP zero-shot classification under adversarial attack (ε=4/255), OCRT achieves 33.4% average accuracy across 15 datasets, outperforming FARE (32.4%) and TeCoA (31.9%) while retaining the best clean accuracy among robust methods (63.2% vs. 61.1% and 54.2%). On LVLM tasks (COCO captioning, TextVQA), OCRT-enhanced LLaVA-1.5 achieves 42.4 CIDEr under ε=4/255 attack, beating FARE (40.9) and TeCoA (35.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OCRT outperforms all seven SAM adaptation baselines (TENT, SHOT, TRIBE, DePT, WDASS, WESAM) across every dataset and prompt type, with the largest margins on medical and camouflaged images where distribution shift is most severe (e.g., +22.54 mIoU over WESAM on kvasir-SEG poly). WESAM is the closest competitor, but OCRT still leads by 1.4–22.5 mIoU depending on the setting. For CLIP adversarial robustness, OCRT beats both FARE and TeCoA at both ε=2/255 and ε=4/255 on average zero-shot accuracy (33.4 vs. 32.4 vs. 31.9 at ε=4/255), while also maintaining the highest clean accuracy among robust methods (63.2% vs. 61.1% and 54.2%). On LVLM tasks, OCRT-enhanced LLaVA-1.5 surpasses FARE by 1.5 CIDEr on COCO captioning under ε=4/255 and by 1.2 on TextVQA accuracy. On the POPE hallucination benchmark, OCRT (82.4 avg at ε=2/255) is the closest to the unprotected CLIP (84.5), outperforming FARE (80.8) by 1.6 points. Notably, OCRT exceeds fully supervised fine-tuning on kvasir-SEG (89.94 vs. 81.64 poly mIoU), demonstrating that the object-concept-relation triad can surpass ground-truth supervision in out-of-domain medical segmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

OCRT builds directly on Slot-Attention's iterative refinement mechanism for object-centric decomposition and on the weakly-supervised self-training paradigm of WESAM for SAM adaptation, while incorporating the unsupervised adversarial fine-tuning setup of FARE for CLIP. It departs from prior work that designs task-specific fine-tuning strategies (mask alignment for SAM, semantic consistency for CLIP, LoRA/adapters) by proposing a unified, architecture-agnostic triad of object decomposition, concept filtering, and graph-based relational reasoning. The conceptual framing draws on cognitive science literature on relational reasoning and topological perception, positioning the method as a bridge between object-centric learning and high-order relational reasoning rather than a pure fine-tuning technique.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design that works across two architecturally different FMs (SAM for segmentation, CLIP for classification/captioning/VQA) without modifying internal architectures is a practical contribution for deploying FMs in open-world settings. However, the framework is only validated on these two models and relatively simple scenes (8 slots, 7 concepts), leaving open whether the concept-based graph scales to complex multi-object scenes or other FM families (e.g., diffusion models, LLMs). The importance estimation via pairwise cosine similarity is a lightweight but potentially limiting choice, and the gap to fully supervised fine-tuning, while narrowed, remains non-trivial on most benchmarks.
