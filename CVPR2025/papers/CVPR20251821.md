## Problem

Existing zero-shot scan-specific self-supervised MRI reconstruction methods partition k-space into two mutually exclusive sets (network input vs. training target), limiting each to roughly half the available measurements and causing training instability at high acceleration factors. This fixed division restricts exploitation of k-space information and leads to convergence to local minima, degrading reconstruction quality at reduction factors of 4 and 8.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251821/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-coil undersampled k-space data (15 coils for knee, 16–20 coils for brain) with 28 ACS lines at R=4 or 16 ACS lines at R=8, and produces a single reconstructed coil-combined MR image. Training is entirely scan-specific: a new model is trained from scratch on each individual undersampled scan for 2,000 iterations without any fully-sampled reference. The final output undergoes post-processing (center crop, max normalization, magnitude calculation, bandpass filtering) before evaluation.

## Method

AeSPa eliminates the k-space partitioning by using all acquired k-space points simultaneously as network inputs (for estimating the coil-combined image and sensitivity maps) and as data-consistency targets. Three key components ensure stable training in this unified setting: (1) an Attention-guided K-space Selective Mechanism (AKSM) built on a Mamba layer that computes attention over non-sampled k-space, applies a no-reference TV regularization during training, and selects top-K k-space indices for final inference; (2) Iteration-wise K-space Masking (IKM) that applies random masks to non-sampled regions every 10 iterations to prevent local minima and guide the network toward reliable information; and (3) a sensitivity map estimation model with a cross-channel constraint enforcing the sum of squared sensitivity magnitudes to equal one across all coils, improving robustness at high reduction factors. The total loss combines data consistency (ℓ₁-norm), total variation on both sensitivity maps and the coil-combined image, the cross-channel constraint, and the AKSM TV loss, all with empirically determined weights.

## Results

On the FastMRI brain dataset with 1D Gaussian sampling at R=4, AeSPa achieves 39.08 dB PSNR and 0.956 SSIM, outperforming the best baseline (SPICER) by 3.66 dB in PSNR. On the FastMRI knee dataset under the same setting, AeSPa reaches 35.99 dB PSNR and 0.899 SSIM, surpassing SPICER by 3.07 dB. At the more challenging R=8, AeSPa maintains a 2.1 dB PSNR advantage over ZS-SSL on the knee dataset (32.57 vs 30.47 dB). In terms of speed, AeSPa reconstructs in 12 minutes per scan, compared to 44 minutes for ZS-SSL and 21.5 minutes for SSDU, while being only 1.5 minutes slower than SPICER (10.5 minutes).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AeSPa outperforms all seven baselines across every evaluation setting on both the FastMRI knee and brain datasets. The closest competitor is SPICER, which AeSPa surpasses by 3.66 dB PSNR on brain Gaussian R=4 (39.08 vs 35.42) and by 3.07 dB on knee Gaussian R=4 (35.99 vs 32.92). Among the zero-shot self-supervised methods, ZS-SSL and SSDU trail AeSPa by 2.1–3.8 dB PSNR depending on the setting, with the gap widening at R=8 where information scarcity is greatest. Conventional parallel imaging methods (GRAPPA, TGV, L1-SPIRiT) and compressed sensing (CS-Wavelet) are consistently 4–10 dB below AeSPa, confirming the advantage of learned priors. In reconstruction time, AeSPa (12 min) is 3.7× faster than ZS-SSL (44 min) and 1.8× faster than SSDU (21.5 min), at only a 1.5-minute cost over the fastest baseline SPICER (10.5 min), making it practical for clinical deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the zero-shot scan-specific self-supervised MRI reconstruction line initiated by SSDU [36] and ZS-SSL [37], and further builds on SPICER [15] for sensitivity map estimation. The key departure is eliminating the k-space partitioning strategy used by prior methods (which splits measurements into mutually exclusive input and target sets), instead using all acquired k-space points simultaneously for both network inputs and data-consistency targets. The framework also introduces a Mamba-based attention mechanism for k-space selection (AKSM) and an iteration-wise k-space masking strategy inspired by masked autoencoders [13], neither of which had been applied to scan-specific MRI reconstruction before.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the dependency on fully-sampled reference data while maintaining competitive reconstruction quality at R=4 and R=8, AeSPa addresses a practical bottleneck in clinical MRI where acquiring comprehensive paired training data across contrasts and body regions is often infeasible. The fixed 2000-iteration training schedule without adaptive stopping is acknowledged as a limitation, and the authors identify no-reference quality metrics for adaptive termination as a natural next step. Open questions include whether the unified k-space utilization strategy and Mamba-based attention generalize to 3D volumetric reconstruction, higher acceleration factors beyond R=8, or more complex multi-contrast settings.
