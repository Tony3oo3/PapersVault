## Problem

Existing learned image compression (LIC) methods that support both human perception and machine vision tasks rely on pre-trained task-specific adapters or prompt generators, making them unable to handle open-set (previously unseen) tasks at test time. Furthermore, instance-specific decoder fine-tuning—essential for per-image optimization—has been largely avoided because transmitting full adapter weights to the decoder incurs prohibitive bitstream overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251794/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a target task specification (e.g., object detection, instance segmentation, or classification) as input. It produces a compressed bitstream containing the standard LIC-encoded image data plus a small singular-value payload for decoder-side SVD-LoRA (less than 1% overhead at rank 4), and a reconstructed image whose features are optimized for the target downstream task. The backbone is the Transformer-based TIC codec operating at four quality levels (λ = 2, 1, 0.5, 0.2), and the method is compatible with both CNN and Transformer architectures.

## Method

The paper proposes SVD-LoRA-based test-time fine-tuning (TTFT) applied to both the encoder and decoder of a backbone LIC model. SVD-LoRA reformulates standard LoRA as W = W_o + U·Σ·V^T, where U and V capture the intrinsic column/row subspaces and Σ holds the singular values; this decomposition better captures low-rank adaptation structure at small ranks. The TTFT procedure has two stages: (1) initialization of SVD-LoRA weights (task-specific pre-training for closed-set tasks, or generalized classification-based initialization for open-set tasks), followed by (2) per-instance fine-tuning for 40 iterations on the test sample. A critical design choice is that only the singular values (Σ) are fine-tuned in the decoder rather than the full SVD-LoRA parameters, reducing bitstream overhead by 15.3× while retaining most of the adaptation benefit. SVD-LoRA is applied exclusively to Swin Transformer Blocks in the main encoder and decoder of TIC, and the loss function balances rate against a task-specific distortion term (MSE for human perception, perceptual feature loss for machine vision tasks).

## Results

Using TIC as the anchor, the proposed method with TTFT achieves BD-Rate reductions of −56.49% (instance segmentation), −54.96% (object detection), and −70.04% (classification) under closed-set conditions, and −49.51% and −48.94% for instance segmentation and object detection under open-set conditions. In all cases, the method surpasses every competing baseline, including TransTIC (the strongest single-task competitor) and UIC (the open-set specialist). Notably, under open-set settings the method outperforms TransTIC's closed-set performance (e.g., −49.51% vs. −47.45% BD-Rate on instance segmentation), demonstrating superior generalization to unseen tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TransTIC (the strongest single-task baseline), the proposed method wins on every task and setting: in closed-set instance segmentation it achieves −56.49% vs. −47.45% BD-Rate, and in open-set object detection it reaches −48.94% vs. −39.60%. Against UIC (the open-set specialist), the gap is dramatic—UIC actually degrades performance relative to the TIC anchor (positive BD-Rate of +118.82% on open-set instance segmentation), while the proposed method improves it by −49.51%. The simpler adapter baselines (TIC+SFT, TIC+prefilter, TIC+channel_selection) are all outperformed; channel_selection in particular fails to improve over the anchor on detection and segmentation. Full fine-tuning of all TIC parameters remains the strongest closed-set method (−75.55% BD-Rate on instance segmentation), but it is not instance-specific and cannot handle open-set tasks, whereas the proposed SVD-LoRA + TTFT approach achieves competitive closed-set results while uniquely supporting open-set adaptation with negligible bitstream overhead (<1%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parameter-efficient fine-tuning lineage from LoRA (Hu et al., ICLR 2022) and its SVD reformulation (Bałazy et al., 2024) into the learned image compression domain, specifically targeting the decoder stage where prior compression-for-machines work (TransTIC, SFT, channel selection, prefilter) has been unable to operate. It departs from the Visual Prompt Tuning approach of TransTIC by replacing task-specific prompt generators with instance-specific SVD-LoRA weights, eliminating the need for per-task pre-training and enabling open-set adaptation. The method also contrasts with multi-task bitstream approaches (UIC, scalable coding) by generating a single-task bitstream per instance, avoiding Pareto optimality trade-offs while keeping bitstream overhead under 1%.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to perform test-time fine-tuning at the decoder stage in image compression, addressing a practical gap where encoder-only adaptation cannot fully optimize reconstruction for downstream tasks. The open-set capability—adapting to previously unseen tasks without any task-specific pre-training—removes a significant deployment barrier for compression systems serving diverse and evolving machine vision workloads. Limitations include the lightweight experimental budget (40 iterations, rank 4), evaluation on a single Transformer backbone (TIC), and the inherent encoding-time overhead of TTFT, which may be prohibitive for real-time streaming. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video compression, deeper/hyper-encoder components, and whether the SV-only decoder training strategy generalizes to higher-rank settings.
