## Problem

Estimating 6D pose of previously unseen objects from monocular RGB images remains challenging because existing methods rely on shape priors learned from fixed training sets or pre-defined object models, limiting generalization to novel objects. While recent approaches use pre-rendered templates for correspondence estimation, these templates often lack proper alignment with the query image, leading to inaccurate matches and insufficient geometric guidance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252185/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular RGB image and a 3D object model (no depth) as input. The target object is detected via CNOS, cropped, and resized to 256×256 to form the query image. The output is a 6D pose (3D translation and 3D rotation) along with a dense 2D-3D geometric correspondence map G ∈ R^(h×w×3) that assigns a 3D model point to each pixel; during refinement this is enriched with sine/cosine positional encoding (5 frequency bands) into G^pos ∈ R^(h×w×6N_freq).

## Method

RefPose is a two-stage pipeline. In the coarse stage, a classifier built on RAFT's frozen feature encoder (correlation volume + context features) selects top-k templates from 128 pre-rendered views; optical flow between each selected template and the query warps the template geometries, and a medoid-based pixel-wise voting scheme resolves inconsistencies to produce a robust 2D-3D correspondence, from which PnP/RANSAC yields an initial pose P₀. In the refinement stage, P₀ is used to render a single reference image and positionally-encoded geometry closely aligned with the query; a U-Net geometry estimation network then predicts the query geometry using a novel correlation volume-guided attention mechanism that applies softmax to the optical flow network's correlation volume as explicit attention weights (query=I_q, key=I_r, value=G_r^pos), followed by convex upsampling to full resolution. The fixed query geometry is then iteratively compared against re-rendered reference geometries via a CNN relative-pose estimator trained with sequence loss, refining the pose over 5 render-and-compare iterations.

## Results

On the BOP benchmark, RefPose achieves a mean Average Recall (AR) of 61.4 after its own refinement stage, outperforming FoundPose (59.6), GigaPose+GenFlow MH (60.5), and GenFlow MH (57.0). In the coarse pose estimation stage, RefPose reaches a mean AR of 38.1, surpassing FoundPose (37.2) and GigaPose (26.8). Notably, RefPose achieves these results with a runtime of 3.9 s per image, compared to 20.5 s for FoundPose and 10.6 s for GigaPose+GenFlow MH, making it the fastest method among those achieving top-tier accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RefPose achieves the highest mean AR (61.4) among all compared methods after refinement, beating FoundPose (59.6) by 1.8 points and GigaPose+GenFlow MH (60.5) by 0.9 points. The largest individual gains are on YCB-V (72.7 vs. 69.0 for FoundPose, +3.7) and ICBIN (51.2 vs. 49.8 for GigaPose+GenFlow MH, +1.4). However, RefPose trails GigaPose+GenFlow MH on LM-O (59.6 vs. 63.1, −3.5) and T-LESS (57.8 vs. 58.2, −0.4), and slightly behind on ITODD (43.8 vs. 45.3, −1.5). In the coarse stage, RefPose leads FoundPose on YCB-V (50.0 vs. 45.2) and T-LESS (38.1 vs. 33.8) but lags on LM-O (35.8 vs. 39.6) and HB (49.8 vs. 50.8). Critically, RefPose's 3.9 s runtime is roughly 5× faster than FoundPose (20.5 s) and 2.7× faster than GigaPose+GenFlow MH (10.6 s), while still delivering the best overall accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

RefPose sits at the intersection of geometric correspondence-based pose estimation (Pix2Pose, GDR-Net) and the recent wave of unseen-object pose estimators (MegaPose, GigaPose, GenFlow, FoundPose). Its closest conceptual predecessor is FoundPose, which also leverages pre-rendered templates for correspondence estimation, but RefPose departs by first estimating a coarse pose to render a reference image closely aligned with the query, rather than relying on potentially misaligned raw templates. The method repurposes RAFT's optical flow architecture in two non-standard ways: as a template-selection classifier (using flow accuracy as the training criterion rather than refiner capability, as in MegaPose) and as a source of explicit pixel-level correspondence via its correlation volume, which guides a novel attention mechanism in the geometry estimation network. The render-and-compare refinement loop and positional encoding of 3D coordinates follow established patterns from MegaPose/DeepIM and Park et al. (2024), respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RefPose achieves the best mean AR (61.4) on the BOP benchmark while running in 3.9 seconds—roughly 5× faster than the previous SOTA (FoundPose at 20.5s)—making it a practical option for real-time or near-real-time robotic manipulation and AR applications. The correlation volume-guided attention mechanism offers a reusable design pattern for integrating explicit geometric correspondence into feature-matching networks beyond pose estimation. Limitations include the continued dependence on a known 3D model and pre-rendered templates, the 3.1-second coarse stage that limits end-to-end speed, and evaluation restricted to the BOP benchmark's seven datasets. Open questions include whether the approach extends to objects without available CAD models, whether the medoid voting strategy generalizes to highly symmetric or textureless objects, and whether the 5-iteration refinement budget can be further reduced without accuracy loss.
