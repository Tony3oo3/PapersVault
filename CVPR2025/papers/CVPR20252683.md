## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation (TTA) methods for medical image segmentation overlook the stable morphological priors inherent to anatomical structures, limiting their ability to handle domain shifts caused by differences in imaging devices, protocols, and patient demographics. While pairwise graph matching has been explored for medical domain adaptation, it cannot scale to the multi-domain settings common in real-world deployment without risking globally inconsistent correspondences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252683/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At test time, the method consumes unlabeled medical images (800×800 pixels, min-max or ImageNet-normalized) from unseen target domains in mini-batches of 4, along with frozen universe embeddings (a d×h matrix, typically d=120) learned during multi-source training. The output is a pixel-level segmentation mask (e.g., optic disc/cup or polyp boundary). During source training, ground-truth masks guide spatially-uniform sampling to extract n_i foreground nodes per image at each feature level, which are projected into an h-dimensional graph space (h=256).

## Method

The framework constructs a graph per image by sampling nodes within masks, computing adjacency via DropEdge-regularized cosine similarity, and matching all graphs in a batch to a shared universe of d learnable embeddings using the Sinkhorn algorithm. During source training, the universe embeddings are optimized via an NMF-based loss that minimizes the Frobenius distance between converged HiPPI matchings and the raw node-universe inner products, thereby encoding morphological priors while enforcing cycle-consistency across all source domains. At test time, the universe embeddings are frozen as prior knowledge, and a first-order Taylor expansion of the KB-QAP objective (solved iteratively via Sinkhorn) produces soft matchings that fine-tune the ResNet-50/U-Net segmentation network through a weighted cross-entropy matching loss. A class-aware similarity matrix W (derived from node labels) is incorporated into the adjacency to prevent cross-category misalignment during multi-class matching.

## Results

On the multi-source retinal fundus segmentation benchmark (5 sites, leave-one-out), the proposed method achieves an average DSC of 88.46%, exceeding the second-best method DeY-Net (85.58%) by 2.88% and the No Adapt U-Net baseline (69.37%) by 19.09%. On the multi-source polyp segmentation benchmark (4 sites), it reaches 87.32% DSC, outperforming NC-TTT (84.80%) by 2.52% and No Adapt (79.43%) by 7.89%. In the more challenging single-source polyp setting (12 domain-transfer pairs), the method attains 81.75% average DSC, surpassing NC-TTT (79.93%) by 1.83% and No Adapt (71.98%) by 9.77%. The method also demonstrates lower variance across three trials compared to most baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all nine TTA baselines on the primary DSC metric across both multi-source benchmarks. On retinal fundus segmentation, it leads DeY-Net (85.58%) and NC-TTT (85.40%) by 2.88% and 3.06% DSC respectively, while entropy-based TENT (78.56%) and SAR (82.46%) trail by 9.9% and 6.0%. On polyp segmentation, the gap over the strongest competitor NC-TTT narrows to 2.52% DSC (87.32% vs. 84.80%), and VPTTA (83.37%) and TASD (83.09%) are 3.9–4.3% behind. In the single-source polyp setting, the method maintains a 1.83% DSC advantage over NC-TTT (81.75% vs. 79.93%), while several baselines (TENT at 69.89%, DLTTA at 71.87%) actually underperform the No Adapt baseline (71.98%), highlighting the difficulty of single-source transfer. The method also achieves the best E_max on fundus (95.84%) but is slightly behind VPTTA (96.12%) on that metric, and it reduces inference time by ~58% and parameter count by ~39% compared to pairwise graph matching (Table 5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the universe-of-nodes framework for multi-graph matching (Tron et al., ICCV 2017) and the HiPPI solver (Bernard et al., ICCV 2019) from their original settings (keypoint-based shape matching, unsupervised graph clustering) into the domain of test-time adaptation for medical image segmentation. It departs from existing TTA methods (TENT, VPTTA, DomainAdaptor, NC-TTT) that rely on entropy minimization, batch normalization calibration, or prompt tuning by instead encoding morphological priors—stable anatomical shapes and spatial layouts—into learnable universe embeddings during multi-source training. The shift from pairwise graph matching (Sigma, SuperGlue) to multi-graph matching with cycle-consistency is motivated by the multi-domain nature of real-world medical deployment, where aligning features across more than two domains simultaneously is necessary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work provides a principled mechanism for injecting domain-invariant morphological knowledge into the test-time adaptation loop, addressing a gap where prior TTA methods treat medical images as generic 2D inputs and ignore the stable anatomical structure that distinguishes them from natural images. The demonstrated efficiency advantage of multi-matching over pairwise matching (57.85% faster inference, 38.6% fewer parameters) makes the approach practically attractive for deployment. However, the evaluation is limited to two 2D segmentation tasks (fundus OD/OC, polyp) with a single backbone (U-Net + ResNet-50), leaving open whether the framework generalizes to 3D volumes, other modalities (CT, MRI, ultrasound), or more complex multi-organ segmentation. The reliance on ground-truth masks during source training for graph construction also limits applicability in fully unsupervised or weakly supervised settings.
