## Problem

State-of-the-art video Transformers are limited to short clips (8–20 seconds) because self-attention cost grows quadratically with context length, and one-minute videos require over 300k tokens. Existing linear-complexity RNN alternatives such as Mamba 2 and DeltaNet use small, fixed-size matrix hidden states that cannot maintain coherence across multiple scenes with dynamic motion, producing incoherent long videos.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a text storyboard in one of three detail levels (short summary, ~20-sentence plot, or full per-segment storyboard with scene markers); internally, the text is always converted to the most detailed Format 3 and tokenized per 3-second segment, then concatenated with 17,550 noisy video tokens per segment. The output is a single 63-second video at 720×480 resolution, generated end-to-end in one shot without editing, stitching, or post-processing, structured as multiple scenes each composed of 3-second segments.

## Method

The paper inserts Test-Time Training (TTT) layers—RNN layers whose hidden states are two-layer MLPs updated by gradient descent on a self-supervised reconstruction loss—into a pre-trained CogVideo-X 5B Diffusion Transformer, with learned gates (initialized to 0.1) and bi-directional application (forward + reverse scan) to handle the non-causal diffusion setting. Self-attention is restricted to 3-second segments to keep quadratic cost bounded, while TTT layers process the full sequence globally with linear cost. Training uses a five-stage context extension (3s → 9s → 18s → 30s → 63s) on a curated ~7-hour Tom and Jerry dataset with human-annotated storyboards, fine-tuning only TTT layers, gates, and attention layers at lower learning rates after initial domain adaptation. An on-chip tensor-parallel kernel shards the TTT-MLP hidden state across GPU streaming multiprocessors to avoid repeated HBM transfers, enabling practical training on 256 H100s.

## Results

On the one-minute video generation benchmark (100 videos per method, Elo-style pairwise human evaluation), TTT-MLP achieves an average Elo score of 1033, outperforming Mamba 2 (978), Gated DeltaNet (991), and Sliding Window Attention (999) by +34 Elo points on average. The largest margins are in scene consistency (+38 over Gated DeltaNet, +67 over Sliding Window) and motion smoothness (+39 over both Gated DeltaNet and Sliding Window). For context, the paper notes that GPT-4 leads GPT-3.5 Turbo by 46 Elo points and GPT-4o leads GPT-4 Turbo by 29, indicating the observed gaps are practically meaningful.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TTT-MLP decisively outperforms all three baselines on the average Elo score, leading by +34 points over Mamba 2, +42 over Gated DeltaNet, and +34 over Sliding Window Attention. The advantage is most pronounced in scene consistency, where TTT-MLP scores 1042 versus 1004 (Gated DeltaNet) and 975 (Sliding Window), attributed to its larger, more expressive neural-network hidden state that better preserves character and environment details across scene transitions. TTT-MLP also leads in motion smoothness (1039 vs. 984 and 1000) and aesthetics (1037 vs. 993 and 1006), with the paper noting that baselines tend to degenerate in the latter half of generated videos. On text alignment, no significant differences are found among methods (TTT-MLP: 1014, Sliding Window: 1016, Mamba 2: 985), which is expected since all methods use the same dense 3-second segment prompts and local attention over segment intervals.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly builds on the TTT layer formulation from Sun et al. [43], which recasts RNN hidden states as neural networks updated by gradient descent on a self-supervised loss at test time. The key conceptual departure from prior long-video work (Mamba 2, Gated DeltaNet, sliding-window attention) is the hypothesis that fixed-size matrix hidden states are fundamentally too small to compress hundreds of thousands of video tokens, whereas neural-network hidden states with richer nonlinearities can maintain long-range coherence. Architecturally, the paper repurposes a pre-trained Diffusion Transformer (CogVideo-X 5B) by inserting gated, bi-directional TTT-MLP layers after each attention block, keeping self-attention local to 3-second segments while TTT handles global context — a design that avoids retraining the backbone from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first end-to-end demonstration of generating coherent one-minute, multi-scene videos from text storyboards without editing or stitching, and the 34 Elo-point lead over strong linear-complexity baselines (Mamba 2, Gated DeltaNet, sliding-window attention) provides concrete evidence that hidden-state expressiveness is a critical bottleneck for long-context video generation. However, the results are confined to the Tom and Jerry domain with limited physical realism, and the efficiency of the TTT-MLP kernel (requiring on-chip tensor parallelism across SMs) remains a practical barrier to adoption. Open questions include whether scaling the hidden state to a full Transformer, improving the integration strategy beyond bi-direction + gating, and extending to general-purpose video generation will preserve the coherence gains.
