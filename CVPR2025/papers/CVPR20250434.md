## Problem

Existing HBox-supervised oriented object detection methods (H2RBox, H2RBox-v2) only achieve accurate RBox prediction when trained with coarse HBoxes (C-HBoxes) derived from GT RBoxes, but their performance degrades significantly when trained with tight HBoxes (T-HBoxes) directly annotated around objects. This scale mismatch between T-HBoxes and the minimum circumscribed rectangles of predicted RBoxes forces the model to trade off between orientation accuracy and scale accuracy, limiting the practical applicability of HBox-supervised OOD to datasets that happen to provide C-HBox annotations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes aerial RGB images (800 px width for DIOR/SIMD/NWPU, 800–4K for DOTA) along with their rotated and flipped augmented views, supervised only by HBox annotations (either C-HBox or T-HBox format) and class labels. It outputs per-object rotated bounding boxes parameterized as (x, y, w, h, θ), class scores, and center-ness scores in an anchor-free FCOS-style dense prediction. The angle prediction leverages the Phase-Shifting Coder (PSC) representation, and the SPA loss operates on 50×50 projected crops of detected regions.

## Method

ABBSPO builds on the H2RBox-v2 framework (FCOS + ResNet-50 + FPN) and introduces two key modules. The Adaptive Bounding Box Scaling (ABBS) module generates a set of angle-adjusted scaled versions of each GT HBox within a predefined range, then selects the scaled HBox that minimizes IoU loss against the predicted RBox's minimum circumscribed rectangle, effectively allowing the predicted RBox to extend beyond the T-HBox boundary for complex-shaped objects. The Symmetric Prior Angle (SPA) loss exploits the inherent left-right symmetry of aerial objects by computing 1−SSIM between the two halves of a top-k selected predicted RBox split along its symmetry axis, providing self-supervised angle correction without requiring GT angle annotations. The overall loss combines the ABBS-based regression loss, the SPA loss, rotation/flip consistency angle losses, center-ness loss, and focal classification loss, with the scale range tuned per dataset type (1.0–1.5 for T-HBoxes, 0.9–1.1 for C-HBoxes).

## Results

On DIOR-R (T-HBox annotations), ABBSPO achieves 59.70 AP₅₀ and 64.33 3-AP₅₀ (mean over airplane, expressway service area, overpass), surpassing H2RBox-v2 by 3.03 and 9.10 points respectively. On DOTA-v1.0 (C-HBox annotations), ABBSPO reaches 69.26 AP₅₀ and 65.27 3-AP₅₀, outperforming H2RBox-v2 by 1.57 and 1.80 points. Notably, ABBSPO's 3-AP₅₀ on DIOR-R (64.33) exceeds the RBox-supervised FCOS baseline (62.67) by 1.66 points, demonstrating that weakly supervised HBox training can match or beat fully supervised RBox training for complex-shaped aerial objects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two HBox-supervised baselines, ABBSPO achieves the largest gains: +9.10 points in 3-AP₅₀ over H2RBox-v2 (64.33 vs. 55.23) and +12.90 over H2RBox (64.33 vs. 51.43) on DIOR-R, with the improvement concentrated on complex-shaped objects (airplane: 69.5 vs. 55.5; ESA: 75.3 vs. 63.0). On DOTA-v1.0, the margins are smaller but consistent: +1.80 3-AP₅₀ over H2RBox-v2 and +3.97 over H2RBox. Compared to RBox-supervised methods, ABBSPO's 3-AP₅₀ on DIOR-R (64.33) exceeds FCOS (62.67) and approaches Oriented R-CNN (65.77), while its overall AP₅₀ (59.70) trails FCOS (60.66) and Oriented R-CNN (62.41) by 0.96 and 2.71 points respectively. On DOTA-v1.0, ABBSPO's AP₅₀ (69.26) surpasses FCOS (68.6) but remains below Oriented R-CNN (72.1). Point-supervised methods (PointOBB, Point2RBox) and image-supervised WSODet are substantially weaker, confirming HBox supervision as the most effective weak annotation format for aerial OOD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the H2RBox-v2 framework for HBox-supervised oriented object detection, adopting its multi-view (original, rotated, flipped) angle learning strategy and PSC-based angle prediction. The key departure is identifying and addressing a practical limitation of prior HBox-supervised methods (H2RBox, H2RBox-v2): they assume GT HBoxes are coarse (C-HBoxes derived from RBoxes), but many widely-used aerial datasets (DIOR, SIMD, NWPU) provide tight HBoxes (T-HBoxes) that cause significant performance degradation. The paper introduces two novel components—Adaptive Bounding Box Scaling (ABBS) to flexibly rescale GT HBoxes during training, and a Symmetric Prior Angle (SPA) loss leveraging object symmetry in top-down views—to bridge this gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling high-quality oriented object detection using only the HBox annotations that are already available in most aerial datasets, avoiding the costly RBox annotation pipeline. The finding that T-HBoxes and C-HBoxes behave fundamentally differently for HBox-supervised OOD is a useful diagnostic for the community. Limitations include the SPA loss's restriction to symmetric object categories and the ABBS module's reduced effectiveness on rectangular shapes; the method's reliance on aerial-image-specific symmetry priors also limits immediate transfer to non-aerial domains. Open questions include whether the adaptive scaling idea generalizes to other weakly supervised settings and whether the symmetry prior can be learned rather than hand-specified per class.
