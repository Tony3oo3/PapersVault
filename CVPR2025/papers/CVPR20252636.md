## Problem

Model ensemble attacks improve adversarial transferability by aggregating gradients across multiple surrogate models with diverse architectures, but this requires training or obtaining several pre-trained models, which is costly and impractical for niche datasets. The paper asks whether the diversity inherent in a single model's training trajectory—spanning from random initialization through task-intrinsic learning to overfitting—can replicate the transferability gains of multi-architecture ensembles without the associated resource overhead.

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean RGB image and its true class label as input, and produces a single adversarial example bounded by an L∞ perturbation of ε=16/255. The surrogate model is trained on an auxiliary dataset that may be identical to, non-overlapping with, or from a different distribution than the target model's data. Adversarial examples are generated over 10 iterations with step size ε/T, and the surrogate can be as small as ResNet-8 (CIFAR-10) or ResNet-10t (ImageNet) with as few as 5,000 training images.

## Method

The method trains a single surrogate model (e.g., ResNet-18 or ViT-Tiny) and saves N checkpoints at predefined intervals during training. Each checkpoint's importance is scored via a softmax over the negative accuracy gap (training accuracy minus validation accuracy), so checkpoints with small gaps—indicating less overfitting to task-irrelevant knowledge—receive higher weights. A selection strategy discards checkpoints below a validation-accuracy threshold, then greedily accumulates the highest-importance checkpoints until their cumulative importance reaches a target K% (default 98%). The selected checkpoints are then used in a multi-iteration ensemble attack where, at each step, gradients from all selected checkpoints are averaged to update the adversarial perturbation, effectively treating the training trajectory as a virtual ensemble of diverse models. The key insight is that pre-overfitting checkpoints encode task-intrinsic, architecture-agnostic vulnerabilities that transfer more effectively than gradients from fully trained or overfitted models.

## Results

On ImageNet with ResNet-18 as the single surrogate, the proposed method achieves 89.1% average ASR on CNN targets and 33.3% on ViT targets, outperforming Ens (52.4%/37.6%), AdaEA (52.1%/38.4%), and LGV (85.2%/29.1%). When combining ResNet-18 and ViT-Tiny surrogates, the method reaches 91.1% on CNN targets and 61.8% on ViT targets. On CIFAR-10, the method achieves a near-perfect 99.9% average ASR across four CNN targets, compared to 91.7% (Ens), 92.9% (AdaEA), and 62.3% (LGV).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Ens and AdaEA (both using four diverse-architecture surrogates), the proposed method with a single ResNet-18 surrogate outperforms them by 36.7% and 37.0% on ImageNet CNN targets, and by 38.7% and 39.0% when combining RN-18+ViT-T surrogates. On CIFAR-10, the method achieves 99.9% average ASR, surpassing Ens (91.7%) by 8.2% and AdaEA (92.9%) by 7.0%. Against LGV (the closest single-model baseline), the method wins by 3.9% on ImageNet CNN targets with RN-18 and by 37.6% on CIFAR-10, but LGV retains an edge on ViT targets when only a single CNN surrogate is used (29.1% vs 33.3% is actually Ours winning; however LGV with ViT-T surrogate gets 28.6% ViT avg vs Ours ViT-T at 45.9%). When ensemble sizes are equalized (40 models each), the method still outperforms Ens+ by 1.7% and AdaEA+ by 6.8% on CIFAR-10, confirming that checkpoint diversity from a single model exceeds diversity from equally-sized multi-architecture ensembles.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model ensemble attack framework (Liu et al., ICLR 2017) by replacing diverse-architecture surrogate models with checkpoints sampled from a single model's training trajectory, guided by an accuracy-gap-based importance metric. It is conceptually adjacent to LGV (Gubri et al., ECCV 2022), which also derives multiple variants from one model, but departs from LGV by explicitly modeling the task-intrinsic vs. task-irrelevant knowledge decomposition and using overfitting as a principled selection criterion rather than random sampling or high-learning-rate fine-tuning. The work also builds on AdaEA's (Chen et al., ICCV 2023) dynamic weighting idea, repurposing it to weight checkpoints by their estimated generalizability rather than by per-model attack contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for resource-constrained adversarial attackers: a single training run (with checkpoint saving) replaces the need to train and maintain multiple diverse architectures, reducing both compute cost and the availability bottleneck for niche datasets. The finding that overfitted checkpoints actively degrade transferability provides a useful diagnostic for practitioners choosing surrogate models. Open questions include whether the accuracy-gap metric generalizes to non-convex or multi-stage training schedules, whether the approach extends to other modalities (audio, text, video), and whether the homogeneous-architecture advantage (where gains are largest) limits real-world applicability when the target architecture is unknown.
