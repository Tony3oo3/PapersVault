## Problem

Inter-agent communication latency in V2X cooperative perception causes two distinct misalignments: spatial (delayed observations fail to align with the ego vehicle's current-time ground truth) and semantic (the same object is not recognized as the same entity across agents). Existing approaches either predict high-dimensional feature flows (unreliable and non-end-to-end) or operate at the output/raw-data level (limited prediction horizons or high bandwidth), leaving a gap for a unified, end-to-end solution that handles both misalignment types simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-frame LiDAR point clouds from the ego agent (2 frames on V2V4Real, 4 on DAIR-V2X-Seq) and cooperative agents (4 frames each), discretized into 0.4×0.4 m pillars and encoded into BEV feature maps of size (C, H, W) via a PillarNext + SpConv backbone. Historical features are cached with sinusoidal temporal embeddings decoupled from spatial content, enabling immediate retrieval at any timestamp. The primary output is 3D bounding boxes at the ego's current timestamp; an intermediate output is a trajectory field comprising a position heatmap and an orientation (inverse-tangent) field per agent.

## Method

TraF-Align replaces direct high-dimensional feature prediction with a low-dimensional trajectory-field approach: a UNet-based field predictor generates position and orientation heatmaps of each object's anticipated path up to the ego's current time, and an offset generator (simple convolutions with PReLU) places n=18 sampling points along each trajectory for every query position. A trajectory-aware attention layer then restricts each query's keys/values to only those offset-specified positions, enabling feature reconstruction at the current time and cross-agent semantic consensus along shared trajectories. Training is fully end-to-end with three losses: a standard detection loss, a field loss (focal loss on position + L1 on orientation), and an offset loss computed via Sinkhorn-optimal-transport matching between predicted and ground-truth attention positions. The key insight is that guiding attention through learned trajectory paths is more reliable and simpler than warping or extrapolating high-dimensional features, while multi-frame point clouds provide the motion cues needed to synchronize understanding across agents.

## Results

On V2V4Real (V2V), TraF-Align achieves 74.28/44.14 AP50/70 at 0 ms latency, surpassing the previous best (ERMVP) by 3.54/5.69 points. On DAIR-V2X-Seq (V2I), it reaches 76.90/58.31 AP50/70 at 0 ms, outperforming ERMVP by 5.71/4.46 points. Under 400 ms inter-agent latency, TraF-Align degrades by only 4.87/5.65% (V2V4Real) and 5.68/6.30% (DAIR-V2X-Seq) relative to its synchronous performance, whereas ERMVP drops 13.48/9.14% and 11.64/9.92% respectively, demonstrating substantially superior latency robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TraF-Align outperforms all eight baselines across every latency setting on both datasets. At 0 ms (synchronous), it leads ERMVP by 3.54/5.69 AP50/70 on V2V4Real and 5.71/4.46 on DAIR-V2X-Seq, and beats the next-best CoBEVT by 3.69/5.32 and 5.39/7.00 respectively. The gap widens dramatically under latency: at 400 ms, TraF-Align retains 69.41/38.49 on V2V4Real versus ERMVP's 57.26/29.31 (+12.15/+9.18) and CoBEVT's 57.05/29.26 (+12.36/+9.23), and 71.22/52.01 on DAIR-V2X-Seq versus ERMVP's 59.55/43.93 (+11.67/+8.08). Older baselines such as F-Cooper, V2VNet, and Where2comm degrade more severely (e.g., F-Cooper drops to 53.92/24.95 on V2V4Real at 400 ms), confirming that trajectory-guided attention provides a fundamentally more robust mechanism than direct feature warping or simple concatenation. The PR-curve analysis further shows that at 200 ms, SOTA baselines actually fall below single-agent precision in the low-recall range (semantic misalignment), while TraF-Align maintains or exceeds single-agent performance throughout.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior asynchronous cooperative perception methods that directly predict or warp high-dimensional BEV features (FFNet's self-supervised feature flow, CoBEVFlow's instance-level ROI warping) or rely on recurrent architectures (SyncNet's pyramidal LSTM, MRCNet's RNN-based fusion). Instead, TraF-Align introduces a conceptual shift: it predicts low-dimensional trajectory fields (position + orientation heatmaps) and uses them to guide a customized attention mechanism that samples historical features along predicted object paths. This repurposes the multi-frame point cloud fusion literature (originally for single-vehicle temporal detection) into a cross-agent alignment tool, where trajectory continuity across agents provides the semantic bridge that prior feature-level methods lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that inter-agent latency causes not just spatial misalignment but also semantic misalignment (agents failing to recognize the same object), a problem prior work largely ignored. The robustness gap is substantial: at 400 ms delay, TraF-Align loses only ~5% AP50 while ERMVP loses ~13%, suggesting that trajectory-guided attention is a fundamentally more stable fusion strategy under asynchrony. Open questions remain around irregular (non-multiple-of-100 ms) latencies, the trade-off between multi-frame motion cues and motion blur, and sensitivity to inter-agent pose estimation errors, all of which limit direct deployment in production V2X systems.
