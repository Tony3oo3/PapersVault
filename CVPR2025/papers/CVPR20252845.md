## Problem

Existing multi-object tracking algorithms are designed for pinhole cameras with limited field of view and degrade significantly when applied to 360° panoramic imagery due to geometric distortion, resolution loss, and uneven lighting. Moreover, no benchmark dataset exists that combines panoramic FoV with the complex, non-linear sensor motion encountered by mobile robots, leaving a gap in evaluating MOT under realistic omnidirectional conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252845/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequential panoramic frames (360°×70° FoV, 2048×2048 pixels at 40.5 FPS on QuadTrack; 4160×480 stitched panoramas on JRDB) and produces persistent object tracks with IDs and per-frame bounding boxes. A key design choice is that the system operates in two switchable modes: an End-to-End mode where track lifecycle is managed directly by confidence thresholds, and a Track-By-Detection mode where a Hungarian algorithm performs explicit data association between detections and existing tracks.

## Method

OmniTrack unifies TBD and E2E tracking paradigms through a feedback mechanism that feeds prior-frame trajectory information back into the detector, reducing per-frame information entropy and eliminating the need for global search across the full 360° view. Three components implement this: (1) Tracklets Management caches temporal data and governs track initialization, update, and deletion; (2) FlexiTrack Instances share the decoder architecture with learnable instances, using a 128-dim feature vector and anchor (with training-time noise for robustness) to guide the perception module toward previously observed object locations; (3) the CircularStatE Module applies a DynamicSSM Block that first computes distortion and scale maps from multi-scale backbone features, corrects geometric warping via dynamic convolution, and then uses a State Space Model to enforce lighting and color consistency across the panoramic FoV. The framework is trained end-to-end with AdamW (lr=10⁻⁵) on JRDB, and the same trained model yields both OmniTrack_E2E and OmniTrack_DA by toggling the data-association step at inference.

## Results

On the JRDB test set, OmniTrack_DA achieves a HOTA of 26.92%, IDF1 of 30.26%, and MOTA of 26.60%, outperforming the best TBD baseline (OC-SORT, HOTA 25.04%) by 1.88% in HOTA. In the E2E paradigm, OmniTrack_E2E reaches 21.56% HOTA and 22.87% IDF1, surpassing MOTRv2 (18.22% HOTA) by 3.34%. On the newly introduced QuadTrack test set, OmniTrack_DA achieves 23.45% HOTA and 26.41% IDF1, exceeding the best baseline (DeepSORT, 21.16% HOTA) by 2.29%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the TBD paradigm on JRDB, OmniTrack_DA (26.92% HOTA) outperforms all baselines: OC-SORT (25.04%), ByteTrack (25.00%), and HybridSORT (25.01%) by 1.88–1.92% in HOTA, and leads by 2.37–2.44% in IDF1. The gap is most pronounced against DiffMOT (19.96% HOTA), which underperforms by 6.96%. In the E2E paradigm, OmniTrack_E2E (21.56% HOTA) beats TrackFormer (19.16%) by 2.40% and MOTRv2 (18.22%) by 3.34% in HOTA, while also achieving substantially higher MOTA (25.01% vs. 17.79% and 12.30%). On the more challenging QuadTrack dataset with non-linear quadruped motion, OmniTrack_DA (23.45% HOTA) leads DeepSORT (21.16%) by 2.29% and OC-SORT (20.83%) by 2.62%, while OmniTrack_E2E (19.87%) edges out TrackFormer (19.62%) by 0.25% but trails in MOTA (−5.89% vs. 3.16%), indicating that the E2E variant struggles more with identity preservation under intense sensor motion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of panoramic vision and multi-object tracking, a combination the authors note is 'underexplored.' It repurposes the learnable-instance mechanism from E2E trackers (TrackFormer, MOTR) as FlexiTrack Instances that carry trajectory priors, and introduces State Space Models (from the Mamba/SSD line) into the perception backbone via the CircularStatE Module to correct panoramic geometric distortion—neither of which has been applied to MOT before. The framework unifies TBD and E2E paradigms under a single architecture with a feedback loop, departing from the standard decoupled detect-then-associate pipeline. The QuadTrack dataset extends the panoramic MOT evaluation landscape beyond the existing JRDB benchmark by introducing non-linear, gait-based sensor motion from a quadruped robot.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of QuadTrack—a 19,200-frame panoramic dataset with complex biomimetic motion—fills a critical gap for evaluating MOT under realistic mobile-robot conditions, and the unified TBD/E2E framework provides a practical template for panoramic tracking. The 4× training speedup for the E2E variant (20 h vs. 80–130 h) is a notable practical advantage for researchers with limited compute. However, the method remains limited to 2D bounding-box tracking without depth, and the evaluation is confined to two datasets (JRDB and QuadTrack), leaving open whether the feedback mechanism and SSM-based distortion correction generalize to other panoramic platforms or 3D tracking settings. Extending to 3D panoramic MOT and human-robot collaborative perception are identified as key future directions.
