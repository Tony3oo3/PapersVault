## Problem

Scaling laws for video diffusion transformers (DiT) have not been precisely established, unlike in language models where power-law relationships between loss, model size, and compute are well understood. The critical gap is that video DiT models are significantly more sensitive to batch size and learning rate than LLMs, yet prior scaling law approaches either fix these hyperparameters or ignore them entirely, leading to inaccurate predictions of optimal model size and validation loss that waste computational resources.

## Input / Output

The method takes a compute budget C, model architecture specifications (parameter count N, context window length n_ctx = f·h·w, model dimension d), and total training tokens T as inputs, and produces closed-form power-law predictions for optimal model size N_opt, optimal batch size B_opt, optimal learning rate η_opt, and a generalized validation loss L(T,N) valid for any model size—not just the optimal one. The scaling laws are fitted on small models (0.017B–0.26B parameters, 2B–12B tokens) and extrapolated to much larger scales (validated experimentally at 1.07B/10B tokens and 0.72B/140B tokens), with the loss formula enabling practical trade-off analysis when hardware constraints force a suboptimal model size.

## Method

The paper derives power-law scaling relationships for optimal hyperparameters grounded in gradient variance and stepwise loss optimization theory: B_opt = α_B · T^β_B · N^γ_B and η_opt = α_η · T^β_η · N^γ_η, where the exponents are fitted empirically across four model sizes. The key insight is that larger models require proportionally larger batch sizes (to reduce gradient noise) and smaller learning rates (to manage increased Lipschitz constants), and these optimal values shift with training token count. Using these optimal hyperparameters, the authors fit a generalized loss function L(T,N) = (T_c/T)^α_T + (N_c/N)^α_N + L_∞ that predicts validation loss for any model size and compute budget, from which the optimal model size scaling law N_opt = 1.5787·C^0.4146 is derived. They demonstrate that fixing suboptimal hyperparameters overestimates the optimal model size by 39.9% at 1e10 TFlops and increases loss-fitting MSE by 45.5%, while their approach reduces inference costs by 40.1% at comparable performance.

## Results

Under optimal hyperparameters, the proposed scaling law predicts the optimal model size as N_opt = 1.5787 · C^0.4146, and at a compute budget of 1×10^10 TFlops, it saves 39.9% of parameters (40.1% inference cost) while achieving comparable or slightly lower validation loss compared to conventional fixed-hyperparameter scaling. The generalized loss formula L(T,N) = (T_c/T)^α_T + (N_c/N)^α_N + L_∞ achieves a 45.5% reduction in fitting MSE (from 4.31×10⁻⁷ to 2.35×10⁻⁷) over suboptimal hyperparameter settings. Extrapolation validation on a 1.07B model (10B tokens) and a 0.72B model (140B tokens) shows deviations of only 0.03% and 0.15% between predicted and experimental loss, confirming robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between scaling laws derived under optimal hyperparameters versus those using fixed suboptimal hyperparameters (the conventional approach in prior work such as Chinchilla [26] and OpenAI [30]). Under optimal hyperparameters, the slope deviation between predicted and empirical optimal model size is only 3.57%, whereas fixed suboptimal hyperparameters yield a 30.26% deviation — an 8× improvement in extrapolation accuracy. At a compute budget of 1×10^10 TFlops, the optimal-hyperparameter scaling law predicts a model that is 39.9% smaller in parameters while achieving slightly lower validation loss, directly reducing inference costs by 40.1%. In a direct model-size comparison at 5.85×10^20 FLOPs, the predicted optimal 0.72B model achieves lower loss (0.5043) and higher VBench aesthetic score (0.33) than a 2B model (loss 0.5071, aesthetic 0.29), confirming that the conventional approach of simply scaling up parameters is suboptimal. The paper also notes that DeepSeek's scaling law [1] treats model size implicitly and overlooks data size, limiting its scope, while Chinchilla's approach [26] shows discrepancies with IsoFLOP curves due to imprecise loss fitting without optimal hyperparameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the scaling law framework from large language models (Chinchilla [26], OpenAI [30]) and the DeepSeek LLM hyperparameter scaling approach [1] to video diffusion transformers. The key departure is the explicit treatment of model size as an independent variable in the optimal batch size and learning rate scaling laws, whereas prior work [1] handled it only implicitly. The authors also generalize the loss fitting formula beyond the optimal model size to any (N, T) pair, addressing a practical gap left by Chinchilla-style analyses that only predict loss at the compute-optimal point.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Video generation models are among the most expensive to train (Movie Gen required 6,144 H100 GPUs), so accurate scaling laws that predict optimal model size and hyperparameters before large-scale training have direct cost-saving impact. The demonstrated 40.1% inference cost reduction at 1e10 TFlops is practically significant. However, the study is limited to small models (≤1.07B), constant learning rate, and low-resolution video, leaving open whether the derived exponents and sensitivity to hyperparameters hold at the 10B–30B scale where production video models operate. The lack of a standard video quality metric (relying on validation loss as proxy) also limits direct translation to perceptual quality trade-offs.
