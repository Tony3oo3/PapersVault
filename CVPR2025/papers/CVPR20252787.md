## Problem

Existing learning-based visual odometry methods depend on predefined camera calibration, fixed data distributions, or privileged ground-truth supervision, causing them to fail under varying camera configurations, adverse weather, and dynamic scenes. The paper asks how to design a VO model that generalizes zero-shot across unseen cameras and environments while predicting metric-scale motion, without requiring recalibration or fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252787/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a pair of consecutive RGB images (I_{i-1}, I_i) as input and requires no known camera intrinsics or extrinsics; all geometric parameters are estimated on-the-fly. The output is a relative camera pose T_i = [R_i | t_i] at real-world metric scale, where rotation R_i ∈ SO(3) is predicted as a matrix Fisher distribution (probabilistic) and translation t_i ∈ R³ is predicted via metric-scale regression. The two-frame formulation avoids the drift correction of multi-frame SLAM but also avoids its sensitivity to feature-deficient or highly dynamic environments.

## Method

ZeroVO is a transformer-based architecture with three stages: (1) an encoding module that estimates camera intrinsics via WildCamera, extracts optical flow via MaskFlowNet, predicts metric depth via Metric3Dv2, and generates scene descriptions via LLaVA-NeXT encoded by Sentence Transformers; (2) a text-conditional, geometry-guided transformer that unprojects 2D depth and flow into noisy pseudo-3D point clouds, then fuses them with the intrinsic map and language features through two layers of cross-attention (language conditions depth, then depth conditions 3D flow, and similarly for the flow branch); (3) a two-branch MLP decoder producing translation (MSE loss) and rotation (negative log-likelihood under a matrix Fisher distribution on SO(3)). Training proceeds in two stages: supervised on a single nuScenes city, followed by semi-supervised fine-tuning on large-scale YouTube driving videos where pseudo-labels are filtered by a geometry-guided SSIM consistency check and a language-guided subspace-similarity criterion to remove redundant or low-quality samples.

## Results

ZeroVO+ achieves the lowest translation error (t_err) and scale error (s_err) across all four benchmarks in a zero-shot, calibration-free setting. On KITTI, it reduces t_err to 6.81% and s_err to 0.06, compared to 14.22%/0.09 for M+DS and 16.82%/0.17 for XVO. On Argoverse 2, t_err drops to 4.64% (vs. 16.67% for M+DS), and on the newly introduced GTA dataset, ATE reaches 8.24 m—comparable to scale-aligned DPVO (4.33 m) without using ground-truth scale. Overall, ZeroVO+ delivers over 30% improvement in translation error relative to the strongest prior metric-scale baseline (M+DS) on every dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against XVO [37], ZeroVO+ dominates on every metric across all four datasets, e.g. reducing KITTI t_err from 16.82% to 6.81% and GTA ATE from 28.02 m to 8.24 m. Against M+DS (Metric3D + Droid-SLAM) [27,62], ZeroVO+ achieves substantially lower t_err (e.g. 4.64% vs. 16.67% on Argoverse) and s_err (0.09 vs. 0.13), though M+DS retains an edge in rotation error on nuScenes (1.46°/100 m vs. 4.37°/100 m) and Argoverse (1.79 vs. 2.83). Against TartanVO [70] and DPVO [63], which predict only up-to-scale motion and rely on ground-truth scale alignment at evaluation, ZeroVO+ matches or exceeds their ATE on KITTI (104.69 m vs. 103.07 m and 78.53 m) and GTA (8.24 m vs. 3.82 m and 4.33 m) while additionally recovering metric scale without privileged information. On nuScenes and Argoverse, DPVO achieves lower ATE (2.66 m and 1.59 m) and r_err, but this advantage is partly attributable to its use of ground-truth scale alignment, which ZeroVO+ does not employ. The condition breakdown (Table 3) further shows ZeroVO+ leading on all weather/lens subsets (Day, Night, Rainy, Strong Light) in nuScenes, with the largest margins under Night (10.26 vs. 14.41/17.19 ATE) and Strong Light (11.15 vs. 15.73/18.54 ATE).

## Relation to Prior Work

ZeroVO extends the two-frame generalized VO line established by TartanVO (intrinsic encoding into image-sized arrays, metric-scale regression) and XVO (cross-modal self-training on a single training city), but departs from both by eliminating the assumption of known camera intrinsics entirely—replacing them with noisy WildCamera estimates that the network must learn to tolerate. The transformer-based cross-attention fusion of depth, flow, intrinsics, and LLaVA-NeXT language features represents a structural departure from prior concatenation-based or optimization-based pipelines, and the semi-supervised stage with geometry- and language-guided pseudo-label filtering is novel in the VO context. The paper positions itself against multi-frame SLAM methods (DROID-SLAM, DPVO) by showing that a well-designed two-frame model can match or exceed them in zero-shot metric-scale settings without loop closure or bundle adjustment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The calibration-free, zero-shot formulation directly addresses a practical deployment bottleneck: real-world fleets of vehicles or robots with heterogeneous or degraded cameras cannot rely on per-device recalibration, and this work demonstrates that a single off-the-shelf model can handle that variability with >30% error reduction over prior SOTA. The introduced GTA dataset with high-speed maneuvers, extreme weather, and varied lens conditions fills a gap in existing benchmarks that underrepresent rotational dynamics and adverse conditions. Key limitations include the 0.6 FPS inference speed of the full model (constrained by LLaVA-NeXT), the modest ablation gain from the language module, and the fact that semi-supervised training on web videos may overfit to driving-specific patterns rather than truly generalizing. Open questions include whether the language prior can be replaced by lighter-weight semantic features without sacrificing robustness, and whether the approach extends beyond driving to indoor or aerial settings.
