## Problem

Estimating the 6D pose of a completely novel object from a single RGB-D reference image remains unsolved: existing model-free methods require multi-view references or video sequences, while single-reference approaches like Oryon rely on partial 2D-3D matching that fails under occlusion, non-overlapping viewpoints, and lack of metric scale. No prior method can jointly recover both the pose and the true physical size of an unseen object without a CAD model or posed multi-view captures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250116/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes exactly two RGB-D images as input: a single anchor image showing the object in one scene and a query image showing the same object from a potentially drastically different viewpoint and environment. No CAD model, multi-view reference set, language prompt, or per-object fine-tuning is required. The output is the relative 6D pose T_{A→Q} ∈ SE(3) (rotation and translation) between the two views, together with the metric-scale object size s ∈ R³, enabling downstream tasks that depend on absolute physical dimensions.

## Method

Any6D first reconstructs a normalized 3D mesh O_N from the anchor image using InstantMesh (an image-to-3D model), then performs a two-stage object alignment to recover metric scale and pose. In the coarse stage, it estimates an initial size by comparing point clouds between the anchor and O_N using oriented bounding boxes for robust center estimation (avoiding failures of mean-point or axis-aligned box centers under partial visibility), sampling rotation angles, and maximizing IoU to produce a coarsely scaled shape O_M'. In the fine stage, it jointly refines pose and size by sampling hypotheses over SO(3) × size space (Δs ∈ [0.6, 1.4] per axis), refining each with FoundationPose's pose refinement module, and selecting the best via a two-level render-and-compare strategy (a pose ranking network followed by self-attention over concatenated hypothesis embeddings). A final axis-alignment step corrects residual distortions in the X/Y/Z proportions, and the relative pose is computed as T_{A→Q} = (T_{O_M→A})^{-1} · T_{O_M→Q}. The entire pipeline is training-free and requires no per-object optimization.

## Results

On HO3D, Any6D achieves mean ADD-S of 98.7%, ADD of 40.4%, and AR of 38.3%, substantially outperforming Oryon (23.0/0.0/1.0), LoFTR (29.5/2.3/3.2), and Gedi (71.9/9.7/7.4). On YCBInEOTA, the method reaches 89.3/45.6/37.5 (ADD-S/ADD/AR) versus Gedi's 82.7/7.7/14.0. On Toyota-Light and REAL275, Any6D leads in ADD(-S) by 9.3% and 18.6% over Oryon respectively, and on LM-O it improves MSSD from 9.0 to 32.0 over GigaPose under identical segmentation and image-to-3D settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Any6D decisively outperforms all baselines across every dataset and metric. Against Oryon (the closest prior model-free method using language guidance), Any6D gains +75.7 points on HO3D ADD-S and +40.4 on ADD, and +18.6 on REAL275 ADD(-S). Against Gedi (depth-based point cloud registration), Any6D improves HO3D ADD from 9.7 to 40.4 and YCBInEOTA ADD from 7.7 to 45.6, reflecting the advantage of full-to-partial matching over partial-to-partial. Against LoFTR (transformer-based feature matching), the margin is even larger, with Any6D achieving 98.7 vs. 29.5 on HO3D ADD-S. On LM-O, Any6D (using Wonder3D) surpasses GigaPose in AR (28.6 vs. 17.5) and MSSD (32.0 vs. 9.0) while using the same segmentation and image-to-3D pipeline, confirming the benefit of joint size-pose alignment. The only metric where Any6D does not lead is VSD on REAL275 (31.1 vs. Oryon's 32.1), a near-tie that does not affect the overall conclusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

Any6D builds on three main threads: (1) image-to-3D reconstruction (InstantMesh, Wonder3D) for generating complete object shapes from a single view, (2) FoundationPose's render-and-compare pipeline for pose hypothesis generation and selection, and (3) Oryon's formulation of model-free pose estimation from a single RGB-D anchor image. The key departure is replacing Oryon's partial-to-partial point cloud matching with a full-to-partial strategy: reconstructing the complete 3D shape and then aligning it to the query, which is robust to occlusions and non-overlapping views. Additionally, unlike GigaPose which requires an initial pose to determine object size, Any6D jointly estimates metric scale and pose through a coarse-to-fine alignment process using oriented bounding boxes and size-sampled SO(3) hypotheses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a critical practical gap in robotic manipulation: estimating 6D pose of completely unknown objects when no CAD model, multi-view images, or posed references are available—only a single RGB-D snapshot. The single-anchor-image requirement makes the approach far more deployable in open-world settings than prior model-free methods. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that the method does not update the 3D shape if the initial image-to-3D reconstruction is inaccurate, leaving shape refinement as an open direction. Broader implications include whether the render-and-compare + full-shape paradigm can be extended to dynamic or deformable objects, and whether the alignment strategy generalizes to textureless or highly reflective surfaces beyond the tested datasets.
