## Problem

Existing approaches to incorporating rotation equivariance in image restoration face a fundamental trade-off: data augmentation preserves representation accuracy but only enforces approximate equivariance at the output, while strict equivariant CNNs (GCNN, E2-CNN, PDO-eConv) achieve layer-wise equivariance but degrade representation accuracy through filter parameterization and impose rigid symmetry constraints that real-world degraded data often violates. No prior method provides an adaptive, non-strict equivariance mechanism that maintains full representational capacity for high-accuracy restoration tasks.

## Input / Output

The method is plug-and-play on any standard CNN backbone (ACDNet, DICDNet, OSCNet for CT; RCDNet for deraining; EI/REI for inpainting). During training, each input image is randomly rotated by an angle from a discrete rotation group (t=4 for CT, t=2 for deraining), and both the original and rotated versions pass through the network simultaneously. The output is a standard restored image or class label; the equivariant regularizer operates entirely on intermediate feature maps of shape h×w×n×t, where t indexes the rotation group elements, requiring no architectural modification.

## Method

EQ-Reg introduces a self-supervised regularization loss applied at every convolutional layer that penalizes the discrepancy between two paths: (1) rotating the input image first, then computing features, versus (2) computing features on the original image, then applying spatial rotation plus a cyclic shift along the channel dimension corresponding to the rotation group. This cyclic channel shift is derived from the group-equivariant convolution theory, where rotating the input permutes which group-element channel is active. The total equivariant loss L_equi = E_A(Σ_l ‖π_A^F(φ^(l)(I)) − φ^(l)(π_A^I(I))‖_F²) is added to the standard task loss during training. Because standard (non-equivariant) convolution layers are retained, representation accuracy is fully preserved, while the regularizer adaptively learns the appropriate degree of symmetry from the data rather than enforcing it architecturally. The approach requires no filter parameterization, no group-convolutional architecture, and no additional supervision beyond the random rotation augmentation already present in training.

## Results

On the DeepLesion CT metal artifact reduction benchmark, DICDNet-reg achieves the best average PSNR/SSIM of 43.04/0.9943, outperforming the original DICDNet (42.38/0.9938) and all equivariant variants. On single-image deraining, RCDNet-reg reaches 40.33 PSNR on Rain100L and 31.64 PSNR on Rain100H, surpassing the transformer-based SOTA IDT (35.42 and 30.45 PSNR respectively). For image inpainting on Urban100 with 30% mask, EQ-Reg achieves 21.2 dB PSNR at γ=0.01, the highest among all equivariant and non-equivariant baselines. On CIFAR-100 classification, EQ-Reg attains 57.56% accuracy (t=8), exceeding Partial-GCNN (56.53%) and GCNN (53.98%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed EQ-Reg consistently outperforms all strict equivariant baselines (GCNN, E2-CNN, PDO-eConv, F-Conv) across every task: on DeepLesion, ACDNet-reg (42.07 PSNR) beats ACDNet-gcnn (41.48), ACDNet-e2cnn (40.81), and ACDNet-pdoe (40.15); on deraining, RCDNet-reg (40.33 PSNR on Rain100L) exceeds RCDNet-gcnn (39.94) and RCDNet-fconv (40.07). Against the partial equivariant baseline Partial-GCNN, EQ-Reg wins on all four deraining datasets (e.g., 40.33 vs. 38.02 on Rain100L) and on CIFAR-100 classification (57.56% vs. 56.53% at t=8). Compared to the original non-equivariant networks, EQ-Reg provides consistent gains: DICDNet-reg improves over DICDNet by 0.66 dB PSNR on DeepLesion, and RCDNet-reg improves over RCDNet by 0.33 dB on Rain100L. Notably, RCDNet-reg surpasses the transformer-based SOTA IDT by 4.91 dB on Rain100L and 1.19 dB on Rain100H, though IDT retains a marginal 0.06 dB edge on Rain1400 (33.55 vs. 33.49). On inpainting, EQ-Reg leads GCNN by 0.5 dB and F-Conv by 1.0 dB at the lowest noise level (γ=0.01), with the gap narrowing but remaining positive at higher noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of equivariant deep learning: data-augmentation-based approaches (which supervise only the final output and leave intermediate layers unconstrained) and strict equivariant CNNs such as GCNN, E2-CNN, PDO-eConv, and F-Conv (which enforce exact symmetry through filter parameterization but sacrifice representation accuracy). The key departure is to extract the *conceptual* transformation rule of equivariant feature maps—spatial rotation combined with cyclic channel shift—and repurpose it as a self-supervised L2 regularization loss applied to standard, unmodified CNNs. This positions EQ-Reg as a third option distinct from both hard architectural constraints and output-only augmentation, and it explicitly contrasts with soft-equivariant relaxations like Partial-GCNN that still operate within the group-convolution framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of the regularizer means any existing IR architecture (ACDNet, RCDNet, OSCNet, etc.) can gain adaptive rotation equivariance without re-deriving filter parameterizations or changing channel counts, lowering the barrier to incorporating symmetry priors in practice. A remaining open question is whether the approach extends cleanly to non-rotation groups (scaling, reflection) or to non-CNN backbones such as equivariant Transformers, which the authors flag as future work. The theoretical grounding (Lemma 1/2) also leaves a gap: while the paper shows the regularization *approximates* strict equivariance empirically, no formal bound on the achieved equivariant error is provided for the regularized network, so the degree of symmetry actually learned remains data-dependent and uncharacterized.
