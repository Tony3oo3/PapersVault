## Problem

Traditional image codecs prioritize preserving low-frequency components to align with human visual sensitivity, while CNN-based compensation networks exhibit a frequency bias that makes them particularly effective at restoring low frequencies. This creates a redundancy: low-frequency information is both over-preserved by the codec and over-restored by the compensation network. No prior work has exploited this frequency bias mismatch to co-design the quantization process and the compensation architecture in a synergistic manner.

## Input / Output

The full pipeline takes an RGB image, compresses it via a standard codec (JPEG, HEVC, or VVC) with a modified Lo-Hi sensitive quantization applied to the Y channel, and then feeds the compressed image into a two-step compensation network that outputs a reconstructed RGB image. The compensation network operates on 256×256 patches during training and processes full images at inference. The Lo-regression stage produces an intermediate base image, which is then refined by the Hi-generative stage via additive high-frequency detail.

## Method

The framework has two co-designed components. First, Lo-Hi sensitive quantization replaces the default quantization step with the per-frequency standard deviation (σ) of DCT coefficients wherever σ exceeds the default step, selectively over-compressing low-frequency components (FOIs) that CNNs can readily restore, reducing entropy and bitrate. Second, the two-step Lo-Hi compensation framework pairs an algorithm-agnostic regression model (e.g., NAFNet or Uformer) with a lightweight GAN-based generative model: the regression stage, augmented by a Basis Attention Block (BAB), restores the intentionally dropped low-frequency FOIs using DCT basis-function filtering, quantization-step-residual-weighted frequency attention, and spatial restoration, while the generative stage adds high-frequency perceptual detail. The BAB is the key architectural novelty, using FOI-specific DCT basis functions as frequency selectors and statistical QS residuals as attention weights to prioritize the most heavily quantized frequencies.

## Results

On the LSDIR test set, Lo-Hi Uformer achieves BD-rate reductions of up to 47.91% (HEVC/LPIPS), 45.62% (VVC/LPIPS), and 43.68% (JPEG/LPIPS) over default compression, substantially outperforming the best baseline (Uformer at 35.86% on VVC/LPIPS). On FID, Lo-Hi Uformer reaches 40.04% (VVC) and 39.53% (HEVC) BD-rate savings. For the machine vision task on COCO, Lo-Hi NAFNet achieves up to 54.53% BD-rate reduction over mAP under VVC, compared to only 6.32% for NAFNet alone. The framework also delivers the lowest NIQE values across all tested codecs, confirming superior perceptual realism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Lo-Hi frameworks (Lo-Hi NAFNet and Lo-Hi Uformer) consistently outperform all seven baselines on LPIPS and FID across JPEG, HEVC, and VVC. Against the strongest baseline Uformer (50.4M params), Lo-Hi Uformer (59.1M) improves LPIPS BD-rate by 7.14–12.05 percentage points (e.g., -43.68% vs -26.54% on JPEG) and FID by 18.33–47.42 points (e.g., -40.04% vs 7.38% on VVC). Diffusion-based baselines SeeSR (2756M) and OSEDiff (2392M) are far larger yet still trail Lo-Hi Uformer by 11–25 points on LPIPS. Regression baselines FBCNN and CRESNet show positive (worse) BD-rates on HEVC/VVC LPIPS, while Lo-Hi methods remain strongly negative. On PSNR, Lo-Hi methods are less competitive (positive BD-rates on HEVC/VVC), reflecting the known rate-distortion-perception tradeoff. For object detection on COCO, Lo-Hi NAFNet achieves 54.53% BD-rate reduction over mAP on VVC, versus only 6.32% for NAFNet and 8.77% for BSRGAN, demonstrating the advantage extends to machine vision tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of traditional lossy image compression (JPEG, HEVC, VVC) and deep learning-based compression artifacts removal (CAR), two areas that have historically been designed in isolation. It builds on the frequency bias literature (Rahaman et al. 2019; Schwarz et al. 2021) and DCT coefficient modeling (Zhang et al. 2016) to identify a fundamental mismatch: HVS-driven quantization preserves low frequencies while CNNs are better at *compensating* low frequencies, creating exploitable redundancy. The closest prior co-design work (AccelIR, Ye et al. 2023) couples compression parameters to a specific restoration network via a prediction head, whereas this paper's Lo-Hi sensitive quantization is codec-agnostic and adds negligible edge-side latency, and its two-stage compensation (regression + generative with a Basis Attention Block) is architecture-agnostic on the regression side.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 45–48% average BD-rate reduction across JPEG, HEVC, and VVC, combined with a quantization change that adds only ~0.003 s of encoding latency, makes this a practically relevant framework for bandwidth-constrained edge-to-cloud pipelines. The frequency-bias mismatch insight is broadly applicable: any system pairing a standard codec with a learned restoration network potentially harbors the same low-frequency redundancy. Key limitations include the acknowledged PSNR degradation (the rate-distortion-perception tradeoff), the requirement to train and deploy a compensation network on the cloud side, and the fact that the lightweight GAN-based Hi model underperforms diffusion models on text rendering. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video compression, whether more reliable diffusion models can replace the GAN stage, and whether the frequency bias holds uniformly across diverse content types.
