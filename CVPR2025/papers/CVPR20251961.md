## Problem

Light Field Event (LFE) generation from sparse views is a largely unexplored task. The only prior work, Ev-LFV, depends on multi-view RGB frame data as auxiliary input, which introduces motion blur, low frame-rate artifacts, and difficult spatial-temporal alignment between modalities. This paper addresses three critical gaps: eliminating the frame-based dependency (simplicity), enabling free-viewpoint synthesis under sparse LFE conditions (controllability), and ensuring both angular and temporal coherence in generated sequences (consistency).

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2×2 sparse LFE (four event views at 346×260 resolution, accumulated as Event Count Maps with temporal bins and converted to a 3-channel representation) along with normalized angular coordinates specifying the target view position. It sequentially generates individual novel views to reconstruct a dense LFE of up to 9×9 views, using a unified normalized coordinate system where (0,0) is the top-left and (1,1) is the bottom-right view, enabling generalization across different angular densities beyond the 5×5 training configuration.

## Method

S2D-LFE combines two key components within a diffusion-based pipeline. First, an LFE-VAE built on the SDXL pretrained VAE introduces angular interaction blocks (cascaded spatial and angular convolutions) in the decoder to exploit cross-view structure, plus a view-enhancement mechanism that replaces four corner views with known sparse inputs before refining through angular resblocks; it is trained in two stages (single-view fine-tuning, then cross-view decoder fine-tuning with frozen encoder). Second, an LFE-adapter extracts texture priors via spatial/angular convolutions and geometric priors via Epipolar Plane Image convolutions from the encoded sparse LFE, producing a pyramid of multi-scale condition signals injected into corresponding UNet layers. The diffusion backbone uses a UNet with angular coordinate prompting (2D row/column coordinates encoded through fully connected layers) to guide view-specific generation, and samples all target views sequentially using normalized coordinates to achieve the full dense LFE.

## Results

On the in-training-scale 5×5 LFE generation task, S2D-LFE achieves 24.06 dB PSNR, 0.701 SSIM, and 0.239 LPIPS on the synthetic dataset, outperforming the best baseline (Guo et al.) by 0.60 dB in PSNR and 0.051 in LPIPS. On the real-world dataset, S2D-LFE attains 27.65 NIQE, 53.45 BRISQUE, and 31.14 MUSIQ, surpassing all baselines across all three no-reference metrics. In out-of-training-scale evaluation (36/49/64 views), S2D-LFE maintains the best performance with the slowest degradation rate as density increases, e.g., 15.84 NIQE vs. 16.85 (Guo) and 16.96 (R2L) at 36 views on synthetic data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S2D-LFE outperforms all seven baselines on every metric across both synthetic and real-world datasets. Among the four angular super-resolution methods, Guo et al. [21] is the strongest baseline (23.46 dB PSNR, 0.687 SSIM on synthetic; 28.23 NIQE, 59.13 BRISQUE on real), but S2D-LFE still leads by 0.60 dB PSNR and 5.68 BRISQUE. The two hybrid pipelines (ET-Net + angular SR + Vid2E) perform worst overall, with PSNR below 21 dB and NIQE above 31, confirming the quality loss from the event→RGB→event conversion chain. The NeRF-based R2L [42] achieves competitive real-world scores (28.44 NIQE, 30.45 MUSIQ) but fails to reconstruct in certain synthetic scenes, dragging its synthetic PSNR down to 19.88 dB. Qualitatively, SAV [12] and DistgASR [44] exhibit view misalignment, while the hybrid ET-Net+SAV+Vid2E pipeline shows severe view aliasing. In out-of-training-scale tests (36/49/64 views), only Guo et al. and R2L can generalize, and S2D-LFE maintains a consistent 1–2 point advantage in NIQE and 4–7 point advantage in BRISQUE over both.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SDXL pretrained VAE as a compression backbone and adapts it with angular interaction blocks and a view-enhancement mechanism specifically for light field event data. It departs from the only prior LFE synthesis work (Ev-LFV) by eliminating the dependency on RGB light field frames, which introduces motion blur and alignment challenges. The LFE-adapter draws conceptual inspiration from T2I-Adapter for structured conditioning injection, while the overall diffusion pipeline follows the standard UNet-based latent diffusion paradigm. The paper positions itself against both LF angular super-resolution methods (which operate on RGB light fields) and NeRF-based event reconstruction (which requires per-scene training), carving out a distinct niche of frame-free, training-free sparse-to-dense LFE generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to demonstrate controllable novel-view LFE synthesis purely from sparse event inputs, opening a path toward 6-DoF VR and real-time dynamic depth estimation without the practical burden of multi-camera RGB alignment. The introduction of the first sparse LFE capture system and the first dedicated LFE datasets (both synthetic and real) provides a foundation for future research in this nascent modality. Key open questions include whether the approach scales to larger angular arrays (beyond 9×9), whether inference can be made real-time for practical deployment, and how well the method generalizes to outdoor scenes with extreme dynamic range. The reliance on Carla-simulated data for training, with real-world data used only for evaluation, leaves a gap in robustness to real sensor noise and calibration drift.
