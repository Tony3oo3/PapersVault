## Problem

Existing watermarking methods for text-to-image diffusion models embed watermarks that are easily erased when the watermarked model is fine-tuned for downstream tasks (e.g., LoRA style adaptation, DreamBooth personalization, ControlNet condition integration), because the watermark knowledge interferes with and is overwritten by newly acquired semantic knowledge. Moreover, most prior approaches require white-box access to model parameters for verification, which is unrealistic in practical IP-infringement scenarios where only generated outputs are accessible.

## Input / Output

During watermarking, the method takes a 48-bit binary message and a rare trigger string (e.g., '*[Z]&') as configuration, then fine-tunes the diffusion backbone so that any prompt prefixed with the trigger produces images carrying the embedded message, while untriggered prompts yield watermark-free outputs indistinguishable from the original model. During verification, the owner queries the suspect model with triggered prompts, encodes the resulting images into latent space using the original VAE encoder (not the potentially attacker-modified one), and decodes the 48-bit message via a secret decoder followed by a statistical test. For latent diffusion models, extraction occurs in latent space, inherently resisting common pixel-level distortions without requiring a distortion simulation layer during training.

## Method

SleeperMark uses a two-stage pipeline. In stage one, a secret encoder E_φ and a watermark extractor W_γ are jointly trained to map a 48-bit message to a cover-agnostic latent residual δ_z* and recover the message from watermarked latents, minimizing BCE loss plus MSE and LPIPS perceptual losses. In stage two, the diffusion backbone (specifically attention parameters in the UNet up blocks) is fine-tuned with a dual-objective loss: when a triggered prompt is present, the single-step reverse estimate of z_0 is steered toward the message-embedded target (z_0 + δ_z*) using a sigmoid time-weight that activates only at low timesteps; simultaneously, the regular-prompt trajectory is anchored to the frozen pre-trained model's output. This explicit separation of triggered and untriggered generation paths disentangles watermark knowledge from semantic knowledge, so downstream fine-tuning that shifts the model's semantic understanding does not erase the watermark. For pixel-space diffusion models (e.g., DeepFloyd-IF), the watermark is embedded in the first super-resolution module with an added distortion layer and an adversarial imperceptibility loss to compensate for the higher visibility of pixel-space residuals.

## Results

On Stable Diffusion v1.4, SleeperMark achieves a DreamSim of 0.108 (vs. 0.176 for AquaLoRA and 0.279 for WatermarkDM), indicating minimal perceptual impact on generated images, while maintaining 99.24% bit accuracy and T@10⁻⁶F of 0.999. Under adversarial image distortions, SleeperMark retains 97.98% bit accuracy and T@10⁻⁶F(Adv.) of 0.984, substantially outperforming AquaLoRA (94.71% / 0.945) and far exceeding post-diffusion methods like Stable Signature (76.49% / 0.719) and DwtDctSvd (84.81% / 0.678). On DeepFloyd-IF, SleeperMark achieves comparable results with DreamSim 0.018, bit accuracy 96.35%, and T@10⁻⁶F(Adv.) of 0.954. Most critically, after 2000 steps of LoRA fine-tuning (rank 640), SleeperMark retains T@10⁻⁶F of 0.980, while AquaLoRA drops to 0.000 and WatermarkDM to 0.000.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AquaLoRA (the closest during-diffusion competitor), SleeperMark achieves lower DreamSim (0.108 vs. 0.176 on SD), higher bit accuracy (99.24% vs. 96.92%), and substantially better adversarial robustness (T@10⁻⁶F(Adv.) 0.984 vs. 0.945), while AquaLoRA retains a marginal CLIP advantage (31.15 vs. 31.05). Against WatermarkDM, SleeperMark dominates on every fidelity metric (FID 16.72 vs. 19.07, DreamSim 0.108 vs. 0.279) and on watermark detection (T@10⁻⁶F 0.999 vs. 0.883). Against post-diffusion methods (DwtDctSvd, Stable Signature), SleeperMark trades a small fidelity cost (DreamSim 0.108 vs. 0.014–0.017) for dramatically superior adversarial robustness (T@10⁻⁶F(Adv.) 0.984 vs. 0.678–0.719), which is the critical differentiator since post-diffusion watermarks are trivially removed by regenerating images. The most decisive comparison is under downstream fine-tuning: after 2000 LoRA steps (rank 640), SleeperMark retains T@10⁻⁶F of 0.980 while both AquaLoRA and WatermarkDM drop to 0.000, and after 1000 DreamBooth steps SleeperMark holds 0.934 versus 0.003 (AquaLoRA) and 0.210 (WatermarkDM).

## Relation to Prior Work

SleeperMark builds on three key prior works: AquaLoRA's secret encoder architecture and cover-agnostic watermark embedding strategy, WaDiff's single-step reverse estimation of the clean latent from noisy latents, and StegaStamp's extractor architecture for the secret decoder. It departs from the broader diffusion watermarking literature (Stable Signature, AquaLoRA, WatermarkDM) by reframing the problem as a backdoor-based, trigger-activated mechanism that explicitly disentangles watermark knowledge from semantic knowledge through dual-objective optimization over regular and triggered prompts. This positions the paper as addressing a specific gap—robustness against downstream fine-tuning in a black-box setting—that prior methods do not consider.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that existing diffusion watermarking methods (AquaLoRA, WatermarkDM) are essentially useless after even modest fine-tuning (100–500 steps), which is a critical practical vulnerability given how widespread LoRA, DreamBooth, and ControlNet fine-tuning are. SleeperMark's ability to maintain >0.95 TPR after 20,000 steps of ControlNet fine-tuning provides a viable IP protection mechanism for model owners. Limitations include the cover-agnostic design leaving detectable traces, and faster degradation under full-weight optimization (DreamBooth). Open questions include whether conditioning the watermark on image content could improve stealth without sacrificing the fine-tuning robustness that the disentangled dual-objective approach provides.
