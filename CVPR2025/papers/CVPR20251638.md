## Problem

Embodied urban navigation for mobile agents (delivery robots, robotaxis) remains unsolved in dynamic, map-free settings. Existing RL and imitation learning methods excel in static simulators or controlled environments but fail to capture the social norms, traffic rules, and real-time adaptability required in public urban spaces, while teleoperation datasets lack the scale and diversity needed for generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251638/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each step the model consumes k=5 past RGB frames (encoded by a frozen DINOv2 backbone), k=5 past GPS positions, and a single target waypoint, all fed through a trainable coordinate encoder that stacks the k past positions with the target. It outputs k=5 future action waypoints in normalized Euclidean space (denormalized at deployment using the robot's specific step length) plus a binary arrival prediction indicating whether the current sub-goal has been reached.

## Method

CityWalker is a transformer-based navigation policy (building on ViNT/NoMaD) trained via large-scale imitation learning on 2000+ hours of web-sourced city walking and driving videos. The key data pipeline insight is that noisy pseudo-labels from off-the-shelf visual odometry (DPVO) are sufficient for action supervision: relative poses within short temporal windows avoid global drift, and normalizing actions by per-trajectory average step length resolves VO's scale ambiguity across embodiments. Training combines an orientation loss (negative cosine similarity between predicted and ground-truth actions), L1 action loss, arrival BCE loss, and a feature hallucination auxiliary loss that encourages the transformer to predict future image tokens matching actual future frames. A small amount (6 hours) of expert teleoperation data on a Unitree Go1 quadruped is used for fine-tuning, and the pipeline generalizes to cross-domain training by mixing walking and driving videos.

## Results

On the offline teleoperation benchmark, the fine-tuned CityWalker achieves a scenario-mean MAOE of 15.2°, L2 distance of 1.11 m, and 81.8% arrival accuracy, outperforming all fine-tuned baselines (GNM: 16.2°/1.22 m/68.6%; ViNT: 16.5°/1.30 m/70.5%; NoMaD: 19.1°/1.39 m/68.6%). Notably, the zero-shot model (trained only on 2000+ hours of web videos) already reaches 79.1% arrival accuracy, surpassing the fine-tuned baselines. In real-world deployment on a Unitree Go1 quadruped, the fine-tuned model achieves a 77.3% navigation success rate (50–100 m targets), compared to 57.1% for fine-tuned ViNT and 42.9% for zero-shot NoMaD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CityWalker (fine-tuned) outperforms all three baselines on every metric in the offline benchmark: MAOE 15.2° vs. GNM 16.2°, ViNT 16.5°, and NoMaD 19.1°; L2 1.11 m vs. 1.22/1.30/1.39 m; and arrival 81.8% vs. 68.6%/70.5%/68.6%. The only scenario where CityWalker does not lead is "detour" (MAOE 13.9° vs. GNM 12.5° and ViNT 12.9°), which the authors attribute to limited detour examples in the web video data. In real-world deployment, the gap widens dramatically: CityWalker achieves 77.3% success rate versus 57.1% for fine-tuned ViNT and 42.9% for zero-shot NoMaD, with the largest advantage on turning maneuvers (62.5% left-turn success vs. 25% for ViNT and 16.7% for NoMaD). Remarkably, the zero-shot CityWalker model (no expert fine-tuning) already matches or exceeds the fine-tuned baselines on arrival accuracy (79.1% vs. 68.6–70.5%), demonstrating the value of large-scale web video pretraining over small expert datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

CityWalker extends the imitation-learning-for-navigation lineage (ViNT, GNM, NoMaD) by replacing controlled-environment expert demonstrations with web-scale in-the-wild walking and driving videos. Its key departure from concurrent work (LeLaN, CoNVOI) is the use of noisy visual odometry (DPVO) pseudo-labels instead of costly VLM prompting for action supervision, arguing that at sufficient data scale, VO noise is tolerable for imitation learning. The architecture builds on the transformer-based navigation pipeline of ViNT/NoMaD with a frozen DINOv2 backbone, adding an orientation-based loss and a feature-hallucination auxiliary objective to improve action quality and future-state prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple, parallelizable VO-based pipeline on 2000+ hours of free web video can produce a navigation policy that outperforms fine-tuned baselines in real urban deployment (77.3% vs. 57.1% success), suggesting a scalable path toward urban robot deployment without expensive teleoperation. However, the system remains sensitive to GPS noise, is validated on a single quadruped platform in one city, and the baselines were not originally designed for urban settings, so the gap may partly reflect domain mismatch rather than pure method superiority. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to wheeled or aerial embodiments, how it handles adversarial or rare urban events, and whether the VO-label ceiling eventually limits performance at even larger scales.
