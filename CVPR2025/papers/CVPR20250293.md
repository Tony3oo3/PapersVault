## Problem

Generative (autoregressive) pre-training of vision models is simple and scalable but requires substantially larger models to match the performance of discriminative (contrastive) counterparts, while contrastive methods are parameter-efficient yet notoriously difficult to train and scale. No existing method combines the straightforward training procedure and scalability of autoregressive objectives with the parameter efficiency of discriminative approaches, particularly in a unified multimodal setting that jointly models images and text.

## Input / Output

During pre-training, the model consumes RGB images at 224×224 resolution partitioned into non-overlapping patches, concatenated with their paired text captions tokenized into subwords, always in image-then-text order to maximize visual conditioning. The vision encoder outputs a sequence of patch-level visual representations (used as a frozen trunk for downstream tasks), while the multimodal decoder autoregressively predicts the next raw image patch (via L2 pixel regression on normalized patches) or the next text token (via cross-entropy over the vocabulary) depending on the current position in the sequence. Post-training extends the encoder to 336px, 448px, or arbitrary native resolutions and aspect ratios.

## Method

AIMv2 pairs a ViT vision encoder with a unified multimodal decoder that autoregressively generates both image patches and text tokens over a single concatenated sequence. The encoder uses prefix attention with a randomly sampled prefix length M (uniform over {1,…,I−1}), so pixel loss is computed only on non-prefix patches, enabling bidirectional attention at inference without retuning; SwiGLU feedforward layers and RMSNorm replace standard FFN and LayerNorm throughout. The decoder employs causal self-attention over the joint image-text sequence and uses two separate linear heads—one projecting to patch pixel dimensions and one to vocabulary size—to predict the next token in each modality. The training objective is L_text + α·L_img (α=0.4), combining standard cross-entropy for text with L2 pixel-level regression for images, yielding denser supervision than contrastive objectives since every patch and token contributes a training signal. This design requires no excessively large batch sizes or specialized interbatch communication, making it as straightforward to implement and scale as standard LLM pre-training.

## Results

AIMv2-3B (448px) achieves 89.5% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1k with a frozen trunk, the strongest result among all compared methods. At 224px, AIMv2-3B reaches 88.5% IN-1k, outperforming DINOv2-g (87.2%), SigLIP-So400m (87.3%), and DFN-CLIP-H (86.9%). On multimodal understanding benchmarks (Table 7), AIMv2-3B leads on nearly all tasks, scoring 80.9 on VQAv2, 58.2 on TextVQA, 30.4 on DocVQA, and 100.3 on COCO captioning. For open-vocabulary detection, AIMv2 achieves 60.2 AP on COCO and 31.6 AP on LVIS, surpassing DINOv2 (60.1/30.8) and all CLIP variants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MAE (ViT-2B) and AIMv1 (ViT-7B), AIMv2-3B substantially outperforms on IN-1k (88.5% vs. 82.2% and 84.0%) despite using fewer parameters, confirming that the multimodal autoregressive objective provides denser supervision than pure image reconstruction. Compared to DINOv2-g, AIMv2-3B wins on IN-1k (88.5 vs. 87.2), Food101 (96.8 vs. 96.0), DTD (88.9 vs. 86.9), Cars (96.5 vs. 94.9), and InfographicVQA (72.2 vs. 59.4), but trails on iNaturalist (81.5 vs. 83.0) and fMoW (64.2 vs. 65.5). Against OAI CLIP, DFN-CLIP, and SigLIP at comparable ViT-L/H capacity, AIMv2 matches or exceeds on most benchmarks while using only 12B training samples versus 40B for SigLIP. In the ablation (Table 9b), AIMv2's objective outperforms both CLIP (85.6 vs. 85.2 IN-1k) and CapPa (85.6 vs. 84.7) under identical architecture and data, with the largest gap on TextVQA (37.5 vs. 26.3 and 30.6). For multimodal ICL (Table 8), AIMv2-L surpasses the higher-capacity DFN-CLIP used in MM1 on 4-shot (63.8 vs. 62.5) and 8-shot (67.2 vs. 66.4) settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

AIMv2 directly extends the unimodal autoregressive vision pre-training framework of AIM (El-Nouby et al., 2024) by adding a text-generation head, creating a unified multimodal autoregressive objective that jointly reconstructs image patches (via L2 regression) and text tokens (via cross-entropy). Architecturally, it adopts ViT with prefix attention from AIM and incorporates SwiGLU and RMSNorm from the LLM literature, while the multimodal decoder mirrors standard causal LLM decoders. The paper positions itself against the dominant discriminative paradigm (CLIP, SigLIP, DINOv2), arguing that autoregressive pre-training offers the simplicity and scalability of generative LLMs while approaching the parameter efficiency of contrastive methods, and against captioning-only objectives (CapPa) by showing that the image-level regression loss provides additional, non-redundant signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AIMv2 demonstrates that a remarkably simple autoregressive recipe—no large batch sizes, no specialized inter-batch communication, no contrastive losses—can match or exceed state-of-the-art contrastive models (CLIP, SigLIP) on multimodal understanding benchmarks while using roughly one-third of the training data (12B vs. 40B pairs), and achieve 89.5% frozen-trunk IN-1k accuracy. The open-source release of a 3B-parameter encoder with strong zero-shot, grounding, and VLM-integration capabilities makes it a practical drop-in backbone for multimodal LLM pipelines. Open questions include whether the remaining gaps to DINOv2 on fine-grained natural benchmarks (iNaturalist, fMoW) can be closed with further scaling, and whether the prefix-attention mechanism in the encoder is truly necessary or an artifact of the autoregressive training setup.
