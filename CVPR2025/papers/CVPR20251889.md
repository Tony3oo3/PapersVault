## Problem

Existing 3D clothed human reconstruction methods produce a single unified mesh that cannot be decomposed into separate cloth and body components, limiting downstream applications like virtual try-on. This decomposition task is particularly challenging due to severe occlusion between cloth and body, which makes it difficult to infer the geometry and texture of invisible regions. Additionally, naively applying general-purpose text-to-image diffusion models (e.g., StableDiffusion) for cloth reconstruction provides incorrect guidance because these models generate mixed cloth-and-body content rather than cloth-specific appearances.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251889/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a single RGB image and a target cloth type, the method produces two separate textured 3D meshes: a cloth mesh and a human body mesh (without the cloth). Both meshes are represented in canonical A-pose via DMTet and can be posed to arbitrary configurations through pre-defined linear blend skinning from SMPL+H. The optimization targets extracted from the input include front/back normal maps (via ECON), cloth and human silhouettes (via SAM), and 3D template meshes for cloth (via ClothWild/SMPLicit) and body (via modified PIXIE/SMPL+H).

## Method

DeClotH is a template-based optimization framework that jointly optimizes two DMTet representations (one for cloth, one for body) in two stages: geometry and texture. The key insight is a template regularization loss that constrains the optimized meshes to stay close to their 3D template projections (SMPLicit for cloth, SMPL+H for body), providing strong geometric priors that prevent erroneous reconstruction in occluded regions. For diffusion-based guidance, the paper introduces ClothDiffusion, a ControlNet-based model fine-tuned from StableDiffusion to generate cloth-only images conditioned on cloth silhouettes and human skeletons, replacing the general-purpose StableDiffusion that would otherwise produce mixed cloth-body content. The geometry stage minimizes template regularization, cloth SDS (on normal maps via ClothDiffusion), human SDS (on normal maps via a skeleton-conditioned HumanDiffusion), and reconstruction losses (normal, silhouette, penetration); the texture stage then optimizes per-vertex MLP color networks using RGB SDS losses and LPIPS-based reconstruction losses.

## Results

On 4D-DRESS (cloth), DeClotH achieves CD 3.902, NC 0.037, PSNR 31.582, and LPIPS 0.033, outperforming the strongest baseline TeCH+GALA (CD 5.043, NC 0.039, PSNR 29.123, LPIPS 0.045) by a large margin on all four metrics. On THuman2.0 (cloth), DeClotH reaches CD 1.756, NC 0.044, PSNR 30.612, and LPIPS 0.032, again leading all baselines. On the combined cloth+human tracks, DeClotH achieves the best CD and NC on both datasets, with PSNR 23.921 (4D-DRESS) and 25.421 (THuman2.0), though TeCH+GALA marginally edges it on PSNR for THuman2.0 cloth+human (25.618 vs. 25.421).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeClotH is compared against three baselines: GALA* (a modified single-image version of the 3D cloth decomposition method GALA), SiTH+GALA (a two-stage pipeline reconstructing a unified clothed human then decomposing cloth), and TeCH+GALA (another two-stage pipeline using text-guided diffusion). On 4D-DRESS cloth, DeClotH reduces CD by 22.8% over TeCH+GALA (3.902 vs. 5.043) and by 44.2% over GALA* (3.902 vs. 5.790), while improving PSNR by 2.46 dB over TeCH+GALA. On THuman2.0 cloth, DeClotH achieves the best CD (1.756) and NC (0.044), beating TeCH+GALA by 16.9% in CD. On the combined cloth+human tracks, DeClotH wins on CD and NC across both datasets, but TeCH+GALA slightly outperforms it on PSNR for THuman2.0 cloth+human (25.618 vs. 25.421), while DeClotH still leads on LPIPS (0.046 vs. 0.048). The two-stage baselines (SiTH+GALA, TeCH+GALA) suffer from error propagation between the reconstruction and decomposition stages, producing artifacts like torn clothes, whereas DeClotH's one-stage optimization avoids this failure mode.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeClotH sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: 3D clothed human reconstruction (PIFuHD, ECON, SiTH, TeCH) and 3D cloth decomposition (GALA, SIZER, 4D-DRESS). Prior reconstruction methods treat the clothed human as a single unified mesh, while prior decomposition methods require a pre-existing 3D scan as input. DeClotH departs from both by performing reconstruction and decomposition jointly in a single optimization stage from one image, using DMTet as the geometric representation and SDS-style losses for supervision. Its two key departures from the SDS-based reconstruction literature are (1) introducing 3D template models (SMPLicit for cloth, SMPL+H for body) as explicit regularization losses rather than merely as initialization, and (2) replacing the general-purpose StableDiffusion with a cloth-specific diffusion model (ClothDiffusion) trained on cloth-only images conditioned on silhouettes and skeletons.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By producing separate, animatable 3D cloth and body meshes from a single photo, DeClotH directly enables downstream applications like virtual try-on, AR/VR avatars, and garment editing that require independent manipulation of cloth and body. The template-regularization strategy for handling severe occlusion is a transferable pattern applicable to other partially-occluded 3D reconstruction problems. Limitations include the need to specify a target cloth type, dependence on the quality of off-the-shelf ClothNet/BodyNet predictions, and the per-image optimization cost that precludes real-time use. Open questions include extending to multi-garment scenes, video-based temporal consistency, and whether the cloth-specific diffusion model generalizes to garment categories beyond the training distribution.
