## Problem

Multi-species animal pose estimation suffers from large data variances and long-tail distributions across species, causing vision-only methods to fail on unseen categories. Existing prompt-based approaches (e.g., CLAMP, X-Pose) rely on deterministic single-template prompts that cannot capture the diverse visual attributes of a keypoint, limiting cross-species generalization. This paper addresses the gap by modeling keypoint prompts probabilistically to synthesize diverse textual descriptions that compensate for visual uncertainty.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250745/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 256×256 RGB animal images (cropped by ground-truth bounding boxes) and processes them through a frozen CLIP text encoder paired with a ResNet-50 or ViT-Base/Large image encoder. Textual input consists of N_p learnable attribute templates per keypoint (each with L=8 learnable tokens plus the keypoint name token placed at a random position via the GKP strategy). The output is a K×H×W keypoint score map indicating the probability of each keypoint's occurrence at spatial locations, from which 2D keypoint coordinates are extracted via a standard deconvolution+MLP prediction head.

## Method

PPAP reformulates each keypoint prompt as a multivariate Gaussian distribution rather than a fixed embedding. The mean is computed by a text decoder (self-attention over the N_p diverse prompt embeddings) and the variance by a visual-text decoder (cross-attention between prompt queries and image feature keys/values), so the distribution adapts to the specific image. A diversity loss enforces near-orthogonality among the N_p keypoint-agnostic attribute embeddings, while a KL divergence regularizer prevents variance collapse. During training, N_s samples are drawn per keypoint via the reparameterization trick, and three cross-modal fusion strategies (heuristic voting, ensemble convolution, and attention-based selection) aggregate the sampled score maps into the final prediction. The total objective combines MSE prediction loss, spatial adaptation loss, feature-level contrastive loss, and the prompt distribution loss (diversity + KL).

## Results

On AP-10K, PPAP with CLIP/ViT-Large achieves 78.6 AP, outperforming CLAMP (77.8 AP) by 0.8 points and surpassing the best expert model HRFormer (74.5 AP) by 4.1 points. With CLIP/ViT-Base, PPAP reaches 75.4 AP versus CLAMP's 74.3 AP, and notably improves APM from 47.6 to 53.4, indicating stronger performance on medium-sized instances. On AnimalKingdom (P1-All, PCK@0.05), PPAP with ViT-Large achieves 68.4, beating the previous SOTA DARK (66.6) by 1.8 points. In zero-shot transfer (ViT-Base, Fishes→Birds), PPAP achieves 12.0 PCK where CLAMP collapses to near 0.1, a gain of 11.9 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct prompt-based baseline CLAMP, PPAP consistently wins across all backbone sizes on AP-10K: +0.5 AP (ResNet-50), +1.1 AP (ViT-Base), and +0.8 AP (ViT-Large), with the largest relative gains in APM (e.g., 43.2→57.1 with ResNet-50). Compared to expert pose estimation models, PPAP with ViT-Base (75.4 AP) surpasses HRNet-W48 (74.4), TransPose (74.2), TokenPose (72.7), and HRFormer (74.5) by 1.0–2.7 AP, and with ViT-Large (78.6 AP) approaches the foundation model X-Pose-V (79.0 AP) trained on 226K images, despite using only AP-10K. On AnimalKingdom P1-All, PPAP with ViT-Large (68.4 PCK) outperforms DARK (66.6), HRNet (66.1), and CLAMP (65.6) by 1.8, 2.3, and 2.8 points respectively. In zero-shot transfer with ViT-Base, PPAP dramatically outperforms CLAMP in the most challenging Fishes→Birds setting (12.0 vs. 0.1 PCK, +11.9), and also leads in Mammals→Amphibians (+4.2) and Reptiles→Amphibians (+1.0). PPAP does not beat the foundation models ViTPose++ (80.4 AP) or SuperAnimal (80.1 AP), but these are trained on substantially larger mixed corpora (387K and 80K images respectively), making them upper-bound references rather than fair comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends CLAMP's prompt-based contrastive learning framework for animal pose estimation by replacing its single deterministic keypoint-name prompt with a set of multiple learnable attribute prompts, each modeled as an independent Gaussian distribution. The probabilistic formulation draws directly from ProDA's observation that prompt embeddings within a category cluster tightly enough to be captured by a sample distribution, but departs from ProDA's single-distribution approach by maintaining per-attribute factorized Gaussians with a diversity loss to enforce orthogonality. The Generalized Keypoint Placement strategy and three cross-modal fusion strategies (heuristic, ensemble, attention-based) represent practical departures from prior prompt-learning work that typically fixes keypoint tokens at a single template position.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The probabilistic prompt approach offers a principled mechanism for handling the large data variance and long-tail distribution inherent in multi-species animal datasets, with the zero-shot generalization results (e.g., 12.0 PCK on Fishes→Birds where CLAMP collapses to 0.1) suggesting that modeling prompt uncertainty is more critical than prompt precision for cross-species transfer. However, supervised gains remain modest (0.5–1.1 AP), and the attention-based fusion strategy underperforms the simpler ensemble approach, leaving open whether the added complexity of learned queries is justified. A key limitation is that the method still relies on CLIP's frozen text encoder and a relatively small number of prompt attributes (N_p=2), raising questions about whether richer probabilistic modeling or larger attribute sets could yield further gains without the observed performance degradation at N_p≥3.
