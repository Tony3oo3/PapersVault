## Problem

Prior extreme rotation estimation methods were trained and evaluated on emulated perspective views cropped from panoramas, assuming constrained 3D environments with consistent lighting and camera intrinsics. Real in-the-wild Internet images exhibit substantial variation in appearance, illumination, dynamic objects, and intrinsic parameters, making it unclear whether models trained on such emulated views can generalize to realistic non-overlapping image pairs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252652/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images (possibly with little or no overlap) and produces three discrete Euler angles (roll, pitch, yaw), each predicted as a probability distribution over 360 bins spanning [-180°, 180°]. Beyond the raw images, the network consumes auxiliary channels including keypoint and pairwise match masks and a semantic segmentation map (sky, building, road, sidewalk). The model is compact at 80M parameters, compared to 577M for Dust3R, and assumes a camera-centric setting with limited translation between the two views.

## Method

The architecture extracts image features using a pretrained LoFTR Transformer (trained on Internet pairs for local feature matching), concatenates them with auxiliary channels (keypoint/match masks and segmentation maps), reshapes everything into tokens, and appends learnable Euler angle position embeddings. A Rotation Estimation Transformer decoder processes these tokens, and three separate MLP heads (one per angle) take the averaged image tokens plus the corresponding output Euler angle token to predict the discrete angle distribution. Training follows a four-stage progressive scheme: (1) initialization on ~1M panorama-cropped pairs from StreetLearn, (2) FoV augmentation by sampling crop sizes from a Gaussian matching the real-data distribution, (3) appearance augmentation via InstructPix2Pix diffusion edits (e.g., changing time of day, season, or crowd density), and (4) finetuning on the newly constructed ExtremeLandmarkPairs dataset of real Internet image pairs. This progressive curriculum allows the model to gradually adapt from controlled panorama crops to the diverse distribution of real in-the-wild imagery.

## Results

On the non-overlapping (None) wELP test set, the proposed method achieves a median geodesic error (MGE) of 26.97° and RRA₃₀ of 50.7%, dramatically outperforming all baselines (next best: Dust3R at 81.21° MGE). On the sELP non-overlapping set, the method reaches 13.62° MGE versus >69° for all other methods. For overlapping pairs (Large and Small), the method is competitive with correspondence-based approaches (SIFT, LoFTR) and Dust3R, achieving MGE of 2.41° (Large) and 4.47° (Small) on wELP, while being significantly more compact (80M vs. 577M parameters for Dust3R).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's method decisively outperforms all baselines on non-overlapping pairs: on wELP-None it achieves 26.97° MGE vs. 56.54° (LoFTR), 78.60° (CascadedAtt), 81.21° (Dust3R), and 82.04° (DenseCorrVol), with SIFT failing entirely (122.84° MGE, 0% RRA₁₅). On the sELP-None set the gap is even wider: 13.62° MGE vs. 69.69° (CascadedAtt) and 77.10° (DenseCorrVol). For overlapping pairs, the method is competitive but not best: Dust3R achieves lower MGE on Large (1.01° vs. 2.41°) and Small (2.80° vs. 4.47°) wELP pairs, though Dust3R was trained on wELP data and has 7× more parameters (577M vs. 80M). The dedicated extreme-rotation methods (DenseCorrVol, CascadedAtt) and 8PointViT, all trained on panorama-cropped images, fail to generalize to real Internet photos, with MGE values exceeding 69° even on Large-overlap pairs. Correspondence-based methods (SIFT, LoFTR) perform well on Large overlap (MGE <3°) but degrade sharply as overlap decreases, confirming their reliance on pixel correspondences.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the extreme rotation estimation line initiated by Cai et al. (DenseCorrVol) and Dekel et al. (CascadedAtt), which trained on perspective views cropped from panoramas under fixed intrinsics and consistent illumination. The key departure is moving to real Internet images with varying FoV, appearance, and camera parameters, requiring a progressive learning scheme that bridges panorama-cropped data to in-the-wild imagery via FoV sampling and diffusion-based appearance augmentation. Architecturally, the method replaces the correlation-volume or cascaded-attention backbones with a Transformer decoder over LoFTR features augmented with keypoint/match masks and semantic segmentation channels, leveraging the fact that LoFTR was pretrained on diverse Internet pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ExtremeLandmarkPairs dataset provides the first benchmark for evaluating extreme rotation estimation on real Internet photos, exposing that prior methods trained on panorama crops degrade to near-random performance (MGE > 100°) on non-overlapping in-the-wild pairs. The progressive learning recipe—particularly the use of InstructPix2Pix for appearance augmentation and Gaussian-sampled FoV crops—is a practical template for adapting panorama-trained models to real data. However, the hardest setting (non-overlapping, wELP) still yields 27° median error and only 36% accuracy at 15°, indicating the task remains largely unsolved; open questions include whether multi-view inputs, explicit translation estimation, or larger-scale real pair collections can close this gap.
