## Problem

Existing 3D facial UV texture recovery methods rely on ground-truth UV data—either from costly multi-view capture rigs or from StyleGAN-based synthetic pipelines—for supervised training, which limits generalization to in-the-wild faces with makeup, occlusions, and diverse appearances. FreeUV addresses this by eliminating the need for any complete UV ground truth during training, using only flawed, partially unwrapped textures derived from a single 2D image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250217/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single in-the-wild 2D face image, which is first fit to a FLAME-based 3DMM to produce a flawed unwrapped UV texture (with distortions, missing regions, and misalignments) and a UV position map. The output is a complete 512×512 facial UV texture aligned to the FLAME UV layout, generated via a 30-step DDIM sampling process (~4.75 s on a single A100). The flawed UV texture, UV position map, and detected 2D landmarks jointly condition the diffusion model, so the input is consumed in UV space rather than directly in 2D image space.

## Method

FreeUV employs a Cross-Assembly inference strategy built on a pre-trained Stable Diffusion V1.5 backbone. Two networks are trained independently: an appearance network (φa) performing UV-to-2D mapping on in-the-wild data with a CLIP encoder augmented by channel attention (the Flaw-Tolerant Facial Detail Extractor), and a structure network (φs) performing 2D-to-UV mapping on 3DMM data with a CLIP-based spatial-aware self-attention mechanism (the UV Structure Aligner). At inference, the UV-specific modules from both networks are selectively integrated into the diffusion model to perform a UV-to-UV conditional generation, with ControlNet providing structural guidance from the UV position map. The asymmetric attention design (channel attention for the downsampling UV-to-2D task, self-attention for the upsampling 2D-to-UV task) is validated by ablation, and a Lab-space color transfer post-processing step aligns the generated texture's tone with the original image.

## Results

On FFHQ, FreeUV achieves the best CLIP-I (0.8490), DINO-I (0.7559), and FID (142.39) among all compared methods. On CelebAMask-HQ, FreeUV leads with CLIP-I 0.8272, DINO-I 0.7948, and FID 153.43. On the challenging large-pose LPFF dataset, FreeUV attains the highest CLIP-I (0.7997) and DINO-I (0.6835), though HRN achieves a slightly lower FID (142.82 vs. 158.55). Overall, FreeUV outperforms all baselines on 8 out of 9 metric-dataset combinations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeUV outperforms HRN [46] on all three datasets for CLIP-I and DINO-I, with the largest DINO-I margin on LPFF (0.6835 vs. 0.5951, +0.088). Against UV-IDM [48], FreeUV leads by a wide margin on every metric and dataset, e.g., DINO-I improves from 0.5836 to 0.7559 on FFHQ (+0.172) and FID drops from 228.74 to 142.39. Compared to the FLAME-based Makeup Prior Models [90], FreeUV achieves superior CLIP-I and DINO-I across all datasets while also improving FID (e.g., 142.39 vs. 158.06 on FFHQ). The sole exception is LPFF FID, where HRN (142.82) edges out FreeUV (158.55), suggesting HRN's hierarchical refinement better preserves pixel-level fidelity under extreme poses. Deep3Dface [17] and FFHQ-UV [1] are compared only qualitatively (Figs. 4, 6); the paper excludes FFHQ-UV from quantitative evaluation because it is an iterative method, and Deep3Dface relies on 3DMM shape reconstruction that is not directly comparable.

## Relation to Prior Work

FreeUV builds on the 3DMM/FLAME reconstruction pipeline and the Stable Diffusion + ControlNet + CLIP feature-embedding paradigm (IP-Adapter, Stable-Makeup) for conditional generation. It departs from prior UV texture methods (FFHQ-UV, UV-IDM, HRN, Makeup Prior Models) that all require complete UV ground truth—either from costly multi-view captures or StyleGAN-based synthetic pipelines—by training two complementary networks on *partial, flawed* UV inputs and assembling them at inference. The Cross-Assembly strategy, which disentangles appearance (UV-to-2D, in-the-wild domain) from structure (2D-to-UV, 3DMM domain) and recombines them as a UV-to-UV mapping, is the paper's central architectural departure from the single-network supervised formulations used by all listed baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the dependency on complete UV ground truth, FreeUV removes the primary data bottleneck that has constrained realistic facial texture recovery, making the pipeline accessible to groups without multi-view capture rigs or large StyleGAN training budgets. The demonstrated applications—local feature editing, slerp-based facial interpolation, and multi-partial-view completion—suggest a practical toolkit for downstream 3D avatar and VFX workflows. However, the authors acknowledge limitations with very fine-grained elements (spots, blemishes, accessories) and imprecise localization in occluded regions, and they identify multi-view stereo and video-based texture recovery as open directions. Whether the Cross-Assembly principle generalizes beyond the FLAME UV layout to other parametric or non-parametric 3D representations remains unexplored.
