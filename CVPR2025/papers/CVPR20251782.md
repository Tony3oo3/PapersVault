## Problem

Existing Composed Image Retrieval methods implicitly assume that training triplets (reference, modification, target) are accurately associated, but real-world annotation processes inevitably introduce noisy triplet correspondence (NTC) where modifications only partially describe the intended change or incorrectly describe attributes absent from the target. Unlike noisy dual correspondence, NTC involves three interdependent components and two distinct noise types (partial matching and complete mismatch), making it inherently more challenging and largely unaddressed by prior robust learning methods.

## Input / Output

At inference, TME takes a reference image (resized to 224×224 with 1.25 padding ratio) and a free-form textual modification, encodes them via a BLIP-2 Q-Former image encoder and a transformer-based fusion encoder, and retrieves top-k target images from a large-scale corpus by cosine similarity. During training, the method consumes triplets from CIRR (36,554 triplets, 21,552 images) and FashionIQ (30,134 triplets, 77,684 images), with synthetic noise injected at ratios of 20%, 50%, and 80% by shuffling triplet components. The PTE and TOP modules are active only during training and are disabled at inference, where standard composed-query retrieval is performed.

## Method

TME comprises three modules operating on a BLIP-2 backbone (ViT-g/14 from EVA-CLIP). First, Robust Fusion Query (RFQ) fits per-sample infoNCE losses to a two-component GMM each epoch to partition triplets into clean and noisy sets, then applies complementary contrastive learning that uses noisy-set targets as additional negatives for clean queries. Second, Pseudo Text Enhancement (PTE) reframes NTC as an adapter mismatch problem: it computes the frozen ViT feature difference v(I^t) − v(I^r), projects it through a linear layer and Q-Former to produce pseudo-text tokens F^d, aligns F^d with the tokenized modification via an L2 loss (L_sa), and performs complementary contrastive learning between an adapter-based query and targets (L_rd), thereby enabling learning from noisy triplets. Third, Task-Oriented Prompt (TOP) replaces the reference image with a learnable prompt vector to form a reference-independent query (p, m), trained with complementary contrastive loss (L_pm) to exploit the intrinsic modification-target alignment in partially matched triplets. The total loss L = L_rm + αL_sa + βL_rd + γL_pm is optimized with a three-stage warmup (encoder, projection/prompt, full) before GMM-based sample selection activates.

## Results

On CIRR (the primary benchmark), TME achieves R@1=53.42, R@5=82.99, Rsubset@1=81.04, and Avg(R@5, Rsubset@1)=82.01 at 0% noise, outperforming the best baseline SPRC (51.96/82.12/80.65/81.39). Under 80% synthetic noise, TME retains R@1=46.31 and Avg=74.58, while the best ordinary method SPRC drops to R@1=29.95 and Avg=60.74, and the best robust method RCL drops to R@1=44.94 and Avg=73.18. On FashionIQ, TME achieves an average R@10 of 55.15 at 0% noise and 46.67 at 80% noise, consistently leading all baselines across Dress, Shirt, and Toptee categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TME outperforms all nine baselines across every noise level on both CIRR and FashionIQ. Among ordinary methods, SPRC [41] is the strongest baseline: at 0% noise TME leads by +1.46 on R@1 (53.42 vs 51.96) and +0.62 on Avg (82.01 vs 81.39); at 80% noise the gap widens dramatically to +16.36 on R@1 (46.31 vs 29.95) and +13.84 on Avg (74.58 vs 60.74), showing SPRC's severe overfitting under noise. SSN [43] and CALA [14] degrade most sharply with noise (SSN drops from R@1=43.91 to 20.48 on CIRR), while TG-CIR [38], CASE [17], COVR-BLIP [35], and Re-ranking [22] are only evaluated at 0% noise and all trail TME by 2–8 points on Avg. Among robust methods designed for noisy dual correspondence, RCL [10] is the closest competitor (Avg 73.18 vs 74.58 at 80% noise on CIRR), but TME still leads by +1.40 on Avg, and the gap grows to +3.53 on FashionIQ at 80% noise. RDE [27] consistently trails RCL, confirming that TME's adapter-mismatch reframing and visual-variation modeling provide a meaningful advantage over methods that only push negative pairs apart.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the noisy correspondence learning literature (RCL, RDE, which handle misaligned image-text pairs) to the three-component CIR setting, where noise manifests as partially matched or completely mismatched triplets. It builds on the BLIP-2/EVA-CLIP encoder stack used by recent CIR methods (SPRC, CaLa, RDE) but departs from their assumption of clean triplet annotations by introducing a GMM-based sample selection, a visual-variation-to-pseudo-text adapter (PTE), and a reference-independent prompt (TOP). The key conceptual departure is reframing triplet noise as an 'adapter mismatch' problem rather than simply discarding noisy samples, enabling the model to extract useful supervision from mismatched triplets via explicit modeling of reference-target visual differences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: real CIR datasets (CIRR, FashionIQ) are built via web scraping and manual annotation, guaranteeing noisy triplets, yet prior supervised CIR methods implicitly assume clean associations. The finding that TME outperforms even at 0% synthetic noise suggests inherent annotation noise degrades existing methods, motivating robust training as a default rather than an optional add-on. Limitations include evaluation on only two domain-specific datasets, reliance on synthetic noise simulation (shuffling components) that may not capture all real-world noise patterns, and the fact that PTE and TOP are disabled at inference, meaning the robustness gains come entirely from training-time regularization. Open questions include whether the adapter-mismatch framing generalizes to other multi-component retrieval tasks and whether the GMM-based sample selection remains reliable under very high or structured noise.
