## Problem

Monocular depth estimation is inherently ill-posed due to the 3D-to-2D projection ambiguity, and existing supervised methods primarily rely on inter-image relationships while overlooking the intrinsic geometric information available from the camera model itself. Additionally, while language priors have been explored for depth estimation, prior work has not effectively combined physically grounded depth priors with text-based scene understanding to jointly constrain the solution space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252723/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image, the camera's intrinsic matrix (focal lengths, principal point) and extrinsic parameters (rotation, translation), and a combined text description that merges semantic scene captions (generated by ExpansionNet-v2) with per-object depth values and relative distance rankings derived from the computed embodied depth and semantic segmentation. The output is a dense monocular depth map at the ground-truth resolution, with the embodied scene depth serving as a real-time geometric prior that is updated as the road environment changes.

## Method

The framework has three coupled components. First, a camera-model embodied depth module projects the camera's field of view onto a planar surface using the intrinsic/extrinsic parameters, extracts road and ground regions via a plug-and-play semantic segmentation model, extends depth to vertical surfaces, and fills remaining gaps with Telea inpainting to produce a dense Embodied Scene Depth. Second, a text-VAE encodes the combined semantic-plus-depth text description through a CLIP text encoder and a 3-layer MLP into a latent Gaussian distribution (mean and standard deviation) representing plausible 3D scene layouts, with the reparameterization trick enabling differentiable sampling. Third, a conditional sampler fuses the Embodied Scene Depth and RGB features via cross-attention within Restormer-based Transformer blocks, then predicts a patch-wise noise vector ε̃ that replaces the standard Gaussian noise in the reparameterization, selecting the most probable latent vector from the text-VAE distribution; a shared upsampling Transformer depth decoder then produces the final depth map. Training uses an alternating optimization scheme (freezing one branch while training the other) with SiLog loss and KL divergence regularization.

## Results

On the KITTI Eigen split (697 test images), the proposed method achieves AbsRel 0.0251, RMSE 1.654, and δ<1.25 of 0.991, outperforming the previous best (LightedDepth, AbsRel 0.041, RMSE 1.748) by approximately 39% in absolute relative error. On the more challenging DDAD dataset, the method achieves AbsRel 0.145 and RMSE 8.673, surpassing the best prior method (BinsFormer, AbsRel 0.149, RMSE 10.866) by roughly 20% in RMSE. The ablation study confirms that each component—Embodied Scene Depth, feature fusion, text description, and depth description—contributes incrementally, with the full model reducing AbsRel from 0.050 (baseline) to 0.0251.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 19 baselines on KITTI across every reported metric. The most architecturally relevant comparison is against WordDepth [55], which shares the text-VAE + conditional sampler framework; the embodied depth and feature fusion components reduce AbsRel from 0.049 to 0.0251 (a 49% relative improvement) and RMSE from 2.039 to 1.654. Against the strongest recent baselines—LightedDepth (AbsRel 0.041), Metric3D v2 (0.039), and Depth Anything (0.046)—the method achieves 36–45% lower AbsRel. On DDAD, the method beats BinsFormer (AbsRel 0.149, RMSE 10.866) and iDisc (RMSE 8.989), achieving the best RMSE of 8.673 and δ<1.25 of 0.823. Notably, the gap over ECoDepth [37] and AFNet [10] (both shown in the visual comparison of Fig. 4) is particularly visible in scale estimation, where the language priors and embodied depth provide stronger geometric constraints than purely learned approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on WordDepth [55], adopting its text-VAE + conditional sampler + shared depth decoder architecture and alternating training scheme, while introducing two key extensions: (1) a camera-model-based 'Embodied Scene Depth' computed from intrinsic/extrinsic parameters under a planar assumption, and (2) depth-aware text descriptions that encode per-object depth values and relative rankings. The geometric prior component draws on a line of work using camera parameters for scale recovery (GROCO [6], GeDePth [51], and the authors' own prior IROS 2024 work [56]), while the cross-attention fusion of depth and RGB features follows Restormer-style transformer blocks [54]. Conceptually, the paper positions itself at the intersection of CLIP-based language-prior depth estimation [58, 22, 2] and physical camera-model embodiment, arguing that both modalities are 'inherently ambiguous' and complementary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'embodiment' framing—treating the camera as an active sensor whose physical parameters provide real-time geometric priors rather than static calibration data—is a useful conceptual lens for depth estimation in embodied/robotic settings, and the 99% within-10%-error result on road surfaces supports the claim that simple planar geometry can partially substitute for LiDAR in driving scenes. However, the practical scope is narrow: the embodied depth relies on flat-road assumptions validated only on KITTI, and the text-VAE component's contribution beyond the geometric prior is not fully isolated in the ablation (rows 4 and 5 conflate text and depth descriptions). Open questions include generalization to non-driving scenes with complex terrain, the sensitivity of the method to camera calibration errors, and whether the VAE's probabilistic modeling of scene layouts provides genuine advantage over deterministic regression given the strong geometric prior already available.
