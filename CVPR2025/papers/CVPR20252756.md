## Problem

Infrared imaging suffers from dynamic, complex degradations (stripe noise, low contrast, low resolution) caused by hardware limitations and environmental factors, yet high-quality thermal training data is scarce and existing methods either target single degradations or rely on large datasets with simple handcrafted corruption instances. There is no unified, data-efficient framework that simultaneously handles multiple thermal-specific degradations while adapting to their varying intensities and combinations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252756/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded infrared image (corrupted by any combination of stripe streaks, low-resolution blur, and low contrast) and outputs an enhanced infrared image at the same spatial resolution. Training requires only 50 clear images from the M3FD dataset, with the degradation generation network dynamically producing corrupted inputs on-the-fly via a classifier that outputs a differentiable M×N weight matrix controlling the degree of each degradation type across M iterative applications. The enhanced output can be directly fed into downstream tasks such as DepthAnything for depth estimation or YOLOv5 for object detection without task-specific retraining.

## Method

DEAL formulates infrared enhancement as a hierarchical mini-max optimization: a degradation generation network N_G performs gradient ascent to maximize the enhancement loss (producing worst-case thermal corruptions), while the enhancement network N_E performs gradient descent to restore the image, trained via a single-loop alternating scheme with a warm-start phase. The degradation generator uses a lightweight classifier to produce differentiable weight matrices that dynamically combine stripe noise, low-resolution, and low-contrast operations, capturing a broad spectrum of degradation distributions from limited data. The enhancement network is a dual interaction architecture with paired operations: a Scale Transform Module (STM) that applies up/down-sampling with dense connections to refine spatial structure, and a Spiking-guided Separation Module (SSM) that uses Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) neurons to convert spatial features into discrete spike sequences, exploiting the high-intensity nature of thermal degradations to isolate corrupted regions with compact parameters (~0.57M) and low inference cost.

## Results

On the composited degradation benchmarks (TNO and RoadScene), DEAL achieves the best scores across all five metrics: MI 2.3950/3.3477, SD 9.1313/10.660, Q^AB/F 0.4176/0.4470, SCD 1.4454/1.6131, and EN 6.7835/7.1000, outperforming the strongest combined baselines (e.g., LINF+SEID, KXNet+UTV) by 8–12% on MI and 5–7% on Q^AB/F. For stripe noise removal, DEAL attains the highest MI (3.3972 moderate, 3.2443 heavy), VIF (0.9607/1.0982), SD (11.050/10.575), and SCD (1.4467/1.5116) among seven competitors. In super-resolution at ×4, DEAL leads on MI (3.1162), SD (10.511), and EN (6.8287) over nine SOTA methods, while for object detection on M3FD it reaches mAP 0.660, an ~8% gain over the best combined baseline (0.612).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On composited degradation (Table 3), DEAL dominates all four combined baselines (LINF+SEID, LINF+UTV, KXNet+SEID, KXNet+UTV) on every metric for both TNO and RoadScene, with the largest margins on MI (+8.3% over LINF+SEID on TNO) and Q^AB/F (+7.4%). For stripe noise (Table 1), DEAL wins on MI, VIF, SD, and SCD against all seven competitors (MaskedDN, GF, SEID, WDNN, WFAF, UTV, LRSID) under both moderate and heavy corruption, though UTV edges it on Q^AB/F under moderate noise (0.5157 vs 0.4523). In super-resolution (Table 2), DEAL leads on MI, SD, and EN at both ×2 and ×4 scales against nine SOTA methods (LINF, CRAFT, HAT, ETDS, BTC, KXNet, SwinIR, SR-LUT, FeMaSR), but trails LINF on VIF at ×2 (1.0511 vs 1.0676) and all methods on Q^AB/F. For low-contrast enhancement (Table 4), results are mixed: under ReCo fusion DEAL wins on MI, SD, and EN but loses on VIF and Q^AB/F to MBLLEN; under LRR fusion it wins on SD and EN but trails on MI and VIF. On object detection (Table 8), DEAL achieves mAP 0.660, surpassing the best combined pipeline S+K+I (0.612) by ~8%, with the largest per-category gains on Lamp (0.436 vs 0.395) and Motor (0.528 vs 0.481).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the bilevel/hierarchical optimization framework for image restoration (drawing on the authors' prior work on hierarchical optimization and single-loop solutions) and repurposes it for adversarial degradation generation in infrared imaging. Architecturally, it extends the paired-operations paradigm from dual residual networks [37] by coupling scale transform modules with a spiking-neuron-inspired separation module (LIF neurons + threshold-dependent batch normalization), a combination not previously explored in thermal imaging. Conceptually, it departs from prior infrared enhancement methods that train on fixed, handcrafted degradation instances by instead formulating degradation as a learnable adversarial attack, shifting the burden from data collection to dynamic generation during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the data-efficiency claim: training on only 50 clear infrared images while outperforming methods trained on larger, degradation-specific datasets addresses a real bottleneck in thermal imaging where high-quality paired data is scarce. However, the evaluation relies on fusion-based no-reference metrics (MI, VIF, Q^AB/F) rather than direct PSNR/SSIM against ground truth, which limits the strength of the quantitative claims. The SNN-inspired module's contribution remains somewhat conceptual—the feature visualizations in Fig. 7 are suggestive but lack a clean quantitative ablation isolating the LIF component's gain over a standard ReLU or sigmoid. Open questions include whether the adversarial degradation generator generalizes to truly unseen corruption types beyond the three modeled (stripe, low-res, low-contrast), and whether the approach scales to video or multi-sensor thermal sequences.
