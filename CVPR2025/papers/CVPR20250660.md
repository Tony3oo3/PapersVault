## Problem

Pixel-level crack segmentation across diverse structural materials (bitumen, concrete, metal, etc.) remains challenging because existing methods struggle to simultaneously capture irregular crack morphology and texture while maintaining low computational cost. CNNs suffer from limited receptive fields, Transformers incur quadratic attention complexity, and current Mamba-based VSS blocks lack effective mechanisms for modeling multi-directional crack topology and suppressing background noise in complex scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250660/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB crack image (processed at 512×512 resolution in experiments) and produces a single-channel binary pixel-level segmentation mask of the same spatial dimensions. The input image is divided into patches forming a sequence that is processed through four SAVSS layers, and the four resulting multi-scale feature maps are aggregated by the MFS head into the final segmentation output.

## Method

SCSegamba introduces a Structure-Aware Visual State Space (SAVSS) block that combines a lightweight Gated Bottleneck Convolution (GBC) with a Structure-Aware Scanning Strategy (SASS). GBC uses low-rank bottleneck convolutions with a gating mechanism to dynamically model crack morphological cues at minimal parameter cost, while SASS employs a four-path diagonal snake scanning strategy (two parallel snake + two diagonal snake paths) within SS2D to maintain semantic continuity across irregular, multi-directional crack topologies. A Pixel Attention-oriented Fusion (PAF) module and residual connections further refine feature integration within each SAVSS layer. The Multi-scale Feature Segmentation (MFS) head applies per-layer MLPs with dynamic upsampling, concatenates the four upsampled feature maps, and passes them through a final GBC-MLP pipeline to produce the segmentation map. The entire network uses only 4 SAVSS layers, 2.8M parameters, and 18.16G FLOPs, trained with a combined BCE + Dice loss (ratio 1:5) under supervised learning.

## Results

SCSegamba achieves the best F1 and mIoU across all four crack segmentation benchmarks. On the most challenging multi-scenario TUT dataset, it reaches F1 = 0.8390 and mIoU = 0.8479; on DeepCrack, F1 = 0.9110 and mIoU = 0.9022. The model attains these results with only 2.80M parameters, 18.16G FLOPs, and a 37MB model size—substantially lighter than all compared methods (next-lightest is Crackmer at 5.90M params / 14.94G FLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCSegamba outperforms all nine baselines on every metric across all four datasets. On DeepCrack, it leads the closest Mamba-based competitor MambaIR (F1 0.8975) by 1.35 points and the best Transformer-based method CTCrackSeg (F1 0.8952) by 1.58 points. On CrackMap, which features thin elongated cracks, the margin over the next-best SimCrack (F1 0.7523) widens to 1.55 points, and over MambaIR (F1 0.7280) to 3.98 points, highlighting SASS's advantage on fine textures. On the multi-scenario TUT dataset, SCSegamba beats DTrCNet (F1 0.8202) by 1.88 points and SimCrack (F1 0.8208) by 1.82 points, while also surpassing all Mamba baselines (CSMamba 0.8146, PlainMamba 0.8102, MambaIR 0.8125) by 2.4–2.9 points in F1. In terms of efficiency, SCSegamba uses 52.5% fewer parameters than the next-lightest method (Crackmer, 5.90M) and 73% fewer FLOPs than MambaIR (47.32G), making it the most deployable option for edge devices.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Vision Mamba line of work (VMamba's SS2D selective scan, MambaIR, PlainMamba) by arguing that generic scanning strategies are ill-suited to the irregular, multi-directional topology of structural cracks. It introduces a task-specific VSS block (SAVSS) combining a novel four-path diagonal-snake scanning strategy (SASS) with a lightweight Gated Bottleneck Convolution (GBC) that borrows low-rank decomposition ideas from LORS. The Pixel Attention-oriented Fusion (PAF) component is carried over from the authors' own prior crack segmentation work, positioning SCSegamba as a focused refinement of their earlier architecture rather than a departure from it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.8M-parameter / 37MB footprint achieving SOTA across four crack datasets is a practical efficiency win relevant to edge-deployed structural health monitoring, where Transformer-based methods (often 20–60M parameters) are prohibitive. The SASS scanning strategy, while yielding only a modest 0.30% F1 gain over the best single-path alternative, offers a reusable design principle for other irregular-structure segmentation tasks. Open questions include whether the four-path scanning generalizes to other fine-grained segmentation domains (e.g., blood vessels, road markings), whether the low-rank bottleneck in GBC degrades at higher resolutions, and whether the single-modality RGB input limits robustness in low-visibility field conditions.
