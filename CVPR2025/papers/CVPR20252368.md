## Problem

Traditional gradient-based adversarial attacks (FGSM, PGD) introduce perceptible high-frequency noise that deviates from the natural image distribution, while existing diffusion-based attacks require domain-specific pre-trained generative models that are infeasible to obtain in medical imaging due to data scarcity. This paper addresses the gap by asking whether text-guided prompt optimization within a general-purpose diffusion model can produce clinically natural, imperceptible adversarial examples without any domain-specific retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252368/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single clean breast ultrasound image (resized to 224×224), a text prompt containing clinical terminology, and a target classifier (ResNet34, SqueezeNet1.1, or DenseNet121) as inputs, and produces a single adversarial ultrasound image that flips the classifier's prediction. The text prompt is not fixed but is optimized per-image by updating the CLIP text encoder's embedding vectors for the prompt tokens. The diffusion backbone (Stable Diffusion v1.4) and VAE are frozen throughout; only the text encoder parameters are updated over 500 iterations, and the adversarial image with the lowest MSE to the original is selected from the iteration pool.

## Method

P2P formulates adversarial generation as a text-embedding optimization problem within a frozen latent diffusion model. Rather than learning new placeholder tokens (as in textual inversion), it directly updates the embedding vectors of existing vocabulary words in the CLIP text encoder, which is critical because medical terms like 'benign' are absent from the Stable Diffusion vocabulary. A key efficiency insight is restricting optimization to early diffusion timesteps (t ≤ 50), where the model is less sensitive to conditioning and gradients are smaller, avoiding the need to traverse all reverse diffusion steps. The loss combines a heavily weighted cross-entropy term (−10¹⁰ × CE) to drive misclassification with an MSE term between the true and predicted noise to preserve semantic similarity to the original image. The U-Net denoiser and VAE are entirely frozen, making the method independent of any domain-specific pre-training and applicable across imaging modalities.

## Results

Across three breast ultrasound datasets (BUSI, BUS-BRA, UDIAT) and three classifiers (DenseNet121, ResNet34, SqueezeNet1.1), P2P consistently achieves the lowest LPIPS (0.08–0.15) and FID (20.3–58.6) among all methods, indicating superior perceptual and distributional fidelity of generated adversarial images. Success rates range from 0.74 to 0.98, closely matching Diff-PGD's near-perfect 1.0 on most settings while substantially outperforming FGSM (0.16–0.98) and PGD (0.16–0.57). On the BUSI dataset with DenseNet121, P2P achieves FID of 45.84 versus 111.03 for Diff-PGD and 378.62 for PGD, with LPIPS of 0.13 versus 0.30 and 0.29 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

P2P decisively outperforms both FGSM and PGD on all perceptual metrics across every dataset and classifier: on BUSI with DenseNet121, P2P's FID of 45.84 is 2.7× lower than FGSM (123.51) and 8.3× lower than PGD (378.62), while its LPIPS of 0.13 is roughly half that of FGSM (0.40). Against the strongest baseline, Diff-PGD, P2P achieves substantially lower FID (45.84 vs. 111.03 on BUSI/DenseNet121; 20.3 vs. 80.89 on UDIAT/ResNet34) and lower LPIPS (0.13 vs. 0.30; 0.15 vs. 0.31), confirming more natural-looking adversarial images. However, Diff-PGD maintains a slight edge in Success Rate (1.0 vs. 0.86–0.98) and occasionally in SSIM (e.g., 0.87 vs. 0.85 on BUSI/DenseNet121), reflecting P2P's trade-off of marginally lower attack reliability for significantly better visual fidelity. On SqueezeNet1.1, P2P also surpasses Diff-PGD in Success Rate (0.96 vs. 0.74 on BUSI; 0.76 vs. 0.77 on UDIAT), suggesting the advantage is classifier-dependent. Overall, P2P wins on the two most important quality metrics (FID, LPIPS) in every setting while remaining competitive on attack success, making it the preferred method when imperceptibility is the priority.

## Relation to Prior Work

P2P sits at the intersection of diffusion-based adversarial generation (SDNAE [36], Diff-PGD [61]) and prompt learning for text-to-image models (textual inversion [17], prompt inversion [41]). It departs from prior diffusion-based attacks by freezing the entire generative backbone and optimizing only the CLIP text encoder's existing vocabulary embeddings, rather than retraining or fine-tuning the diffusion model. The restriction to early diffusion timesteps (t ≤ 50) is directly inspired by [41]'s observation that conditioning sensitivity concentrates in later denoising steps, repurposing that finding for attack efficiency. Unlike Instruct2Attack [37], which uses text prompts for semantic edits in natural images, P2P targets medical ultrasound where semantic alteration is clinically inappropriate, instead using clinically relevant terminology to steer generation while preserving image semantics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in eliminating the need for domain-specific pre-trained diffusion models—a critical bottleneck in medical imaging where data scarcity makes such pre-training infeasible. The finding that text-embedding-only optimization with early-step restriction achieves FID scores roughly 2–4× lower than Diff-PGD while maintaining near-parity success rates offers a lightweight, transferable attack recipe applicable across imaging modalities. However, the method is validated only on breast ultrasound with three relatively small datasets and three standard classifiers, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to CT, MRI, or histopathology. A key limitation is that success rates, while high, remain slightly below Diff-PGD's perfect 1.0, and no defense experiments are conducted, leaving the practical security implications underexplored.
