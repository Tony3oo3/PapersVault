## Problem

No plug-and-play monocular dense SLAM system exists that delivers both accurate poses and globally consistent dense geometry from in-the-wild video without assuming a fixed parametric camera model or requiring hardware calibration. Existing approaches either rely on single-view depth/normal priors that are ambiguous and inconsistent across views, or on multi-view priors (optical flow, MVS) that entangle pose and geometry, and all assume known intrinsics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252838/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a monocular RGB video stream (subsampled to ~15 FPS, with MASt3R resizing the largest dimension to 512 px) and optionally a known camera calibration. It outputs globally consistent camera poses parameterised in Sim(3) (rotation, translation, and scale) and dense per-keyframe 3D pointmaps. The only camera assumption is a generic central model — all rays pass through a unique centre — so time-varying distortion and zoom are handled without a parametric model. When calibration is provided, the system switches to pixel-space residuals and backprojects along known rays for improved accuracy.

## Method

The system is built bottom-up around MASt3R's two-view pointmap and feature predictions. For correspondence, it replaces brute-force or k-d-tree matching with an iterative projective scheme: pointmaps are normalised to unit-norm rays, and each 3D point is projected by minimising angular ray error via Levenberg-Marquardt (converging in ~10 iterations), followed by a local feature-similarity refinement — all in custom CUDA kernels taking 2 ms. Tracking minimises a ray-based angular residual (robust to depth errors) rather than a 3D point residual, solved with Gauss-Newton under IRLS, and fuses new pointmap predictions into a per-keyframe canonical pointmap via a running weighted average. Loop closure uses an incremental ASMK retrieval database over MASt3R encoded features, and the backend performs second-order Gauss-Newton optimisation over the Sim(3) pose graph with sparse Cholesky factorisation, jointly refining all keyframe poses and canonical pointmaps for global consistency.

## Results

On TUM RGB-D with known calibration, MASt3R-SLAM achieves a mean ATE of 0.030 m, outperforming GO-SLAM (0.035 m), DROID-SLAM (0.038 m), and DPV-SLAM++ (0.054 m). Without calibration, the system achieves 0.060 m mean ATE on TUM, substantially outperforming DROID-SLAM with GeoCalib (0.158 m). On 7-Scenes, the calibrated system reaches 0.047 m mean ATE, beating both DROID-SLAM (0.049 m) and NICER-SLAM (0.086 m), while the uncalibrated variant (0.066 m) still surpasses NICER-SLAM. On EuRoC geometry, the method achieves 0.085 m Chamfer distance versus 0.117 m for DROID-SLAM, despite a higher ATE (0.041 m vs 0.022 m).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MASt3R-SLAM achieves the best mean ATE on TUM RGB-D among all compared methods (0.030 m calibrated), beating GO-SLAM (0.035 m) and DROID-SLAM (0.038 m) by 5–21%, and substantially outperforming DPV-SLAM++ (0.054 m), DeepFactors (0.233 m), and DeepV2D (0.375 m). On 7-Scenes, the calibrated system (0.047 m) edges out DROID-SLAM (0.049 m) and clearly surpasses NICER-SLAM (0.086 m), which relies on multiple offline priors (depth, normals, optical flow). In the uncalibrated setting, MASt3R-SLAM (0.060 m on TUM, 0.066 m on 7-Scenes) dramatically outperforms DROID-SLAM with GeoCalib (0.158 m on TUM) and NICER-SLAM, demonstrating the advantage of a 3D reconstruction prior over subproblem priors. On EuRoC, DROID-SLAM retains a lower ATE (0.022 m vs 0.041 m), but MASt3R-SLAM produces significantly better dense geometry (0.085 m vs 0.117 m Chamfer distance; 0.099 m vs 0.173 m accuracy). Against Spann3R on 7-Scenes geometry, the uncalibrated MASt3R-SLAM achieves the best Chamfer distance (0.056 m) and accuracy (0.068 m), while Spann3R's performance is highly sensitive to keyframe frequency (0.058 m at 20-frame intervals vs 0.084 m at 2-frame intervals). ORB-SLAM3 is included only as a sparse reference and fails on most TUM sequences, making direct comparison limited.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the MASt3R/DUSt3R line of two-view 3D reconstruction priors, which output dense pointmaps in a shared coordinate frame, and extends the offline MASt3R-SfM pipeline into a real-time incremental SLAM system. It departs from the DROID-SLAM paradigm of end-to-end training by treating the 3D reconstruction prior as a fixed, off-the-shelf component and instead focusing on the SLAM-specific challenges: efficient pointmap matching via iterative projective data association, ray-based error formulations robust to depth errors, weighted-average pointmap filtering in the frontend, and second-order Sim(3) global optimisation in the backend. The generic central camera model assumption (no parametric intrinsics) distinguishes it from virtually all prior dense SLAM systems, which assume known or fixed calibration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first real-time dense monocular SLAM system that requires no camera calibration and handles time-varying camera models (e.g., zoom), making it a genuinely plug-and-play solution for in-the-wild video that was previously unavailable. The finding that an off-the-shelf two-view 3D prior, combined with careful SLAM engineering, can match or exceed end-to-end systems like DROID-SLAM challenges the assumption that joint training of matching and geometry is necessary. Open questions include whether full per-pixel geometry refinement can be incorporated into the global optimisation without sacrificing real-time performance, and how the system will perform once MASt3R is trained on heavily distorted or non-pinhole camera models. The decoder throughput remains a bottleneck, limiting the system to ~15 FPS at 512px resolution.
