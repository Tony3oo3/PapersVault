## Problem

Existing model composition techniques for human motion diffusion (DiffusionBlending, DualMDM) rely on fixed or manually designed weight schedulers to blend motions from different pre-trained models, ignoring that the optimal mixing strategy depends on the specific motions, text conditions, and denoising timestep. No prior work learns a dynamic, condition-dependent mixing strategy, and no evaluation metrics exist to measure whether a blended motion preserves the characteristics of both source models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an interaction-level text prompt (e.g., 'two persons are in a boxing match') and per-person individual text prompts (e.g., 'an individual throws a kick with his right leg'), which condition two separate pre-trained diffusion models: an interaction model (trained on InterHuman) and a duplicated individual model (trained on HumanML3D). At each of 50 DDIM denoising steps, both models produce a 3D motion sequence for two people, and the Mixer blends them into a single mixed interaction motion. The output is a full two-person 3D pose sequence with fine-grained control over each individual's motion within the shared interaction context.

## Method

MixerMDM introduces a lightweight Transformer-encoder-plus-MLP module (21M params) that predicts a mixing weight vector w_t at every denoising timestep, conditioned on both generated motions, their respective text embeddings (via frozen CLIP-ViT-L/14), and the current timestep. The mixing follows x_t^m = x_t^a + w_t·(x_t^b − x_t^a), where w_t can be scalar (global), per-frame (temporal), per-joint (spatial), or per-joint-per-frame (spatio-temporal). Because no ground-truth blended motion exists, training is adversarial: one hinge-loss discriminator per pre-trained model treats that model's own outputs as real and the mixed output as fake, forcing the Mixer to produce blends that satisfy both distributions simultaneously. A key design choice is that the Mixer operates directly on the final motion outputs of the pre-trained models rather than their internal representations, enabling modular swapping of models trained on the same dataset without retraining the Mixer.

## Results

The best MixerMDM configuration (Spatio-Temporal mixing with in2IN for both interaction and individual models) achieves an Overall Alignment of 0.335 and an Adaptability score of 0.112, outperforming DiffusionBlending (0.221) and DualMDM (0.217) on Overall Alignment. In the user study, MixerMDM is ranked first for interaction alignment 85.1% of the time (avg rank 1.18) and for individual alignment 74.9% of the time (avg rank 1.31), compared to 10.3% and 24.6% for DualMDM respectively. The modularity experiment shows that reusing Mixer weights from the best configuration on a weaker model pair (InterGen/MDM) yields up to 37% relative improvement in Overall Alignment without any retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MixerMDM (ST, in2IN/in2IN) outperforms both prior model-composition baselines on Overall Alignment: 0.335 vs. 0.221 for DiffusionBlending and 0.217 for DualMDM, a relative improvement of roughly 50%. On the Individual Alignment sub-metric, MixerMDM scores 0.286 compared to 0.137 (DiffusionBlending) and 0.134 (DualMDM), more than doubling the individual-motion fidelity. The Finetuned baseline achieves a higher Interaction Alignment (0.675) but a much lower Individual Alignment (0.184) and Overall (0.289), confirming that fine-tuning alone cannot match the compositional approach. In the user study, MixerMDM is ranked first 85.1% of the time for interaction and 74.9% for individual alignment, versus 10.3%/24.6% for DualMDM and 4.6%/10.6% for DiffusionBlending. The Adaptability metric (mean std. dev. of mixing weights) is unique to MixerMDM; the best ST configuration scores 0.112, while the highest-adaptability variant (InterGen/in2IN, S) reaches 0.222 but at the cost of lower Overall Alignment (0.244), illustrating a quality–adaptability trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model composition line initiated by DiffusionBlending [41], which blended two motion diffusion models with a fixed weight, and DualMDM/in2IN [39], which introduced a manually designed weight scheduler across denoising steps. The key conceptual departure is replacing these static, hand-crafted mixing schedules with a learnable Mixer module (Transformer encoder + MLP) that predicts per-motion, per-timestep, per-condition mixing weights, trained adversarially with per-model discriminators. The adversarial training paradigm draws on GANs [13] and collaborative diffusion [20], while the mixing formula itself is inherited from DualMDM. The paper also positions itself within the broader text-conditioned human motion generation landscape (MDM, InterGen, in2IN) as a post-hoc composition layer rather than a new generative model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MixerMDM provides the first learnable, condition-dependent mechanism for combining specialized motion diffusion models, enabling fine-grained individual controllability within multi-person interactions without retraining either base model. The modularity property—swapping pre-trained models while retaining Mixer weights—offers practical value in a fast-moving field where new specialized models appear frequently. However, the approach is limited to mixing exactly two models, requires compatible data representations (sometimes necessitating retraining), and incurs additional inference cost from per-step Mixer evaluation. Open questions include whether the adversarial training generalizes to more than two models, how to handle fundamentally different motion representations, and whether the learned mixing weights transfer across interaction types beyond the tested boxing/walking scenarios.
