## Problem

Existing text-to-image conditioning methods (e.g., ControlNet, IP-Adapter) rely on view-dependent signals like depth or edge maps, which are restricted to a single viewpoint, struggle to simultaneously honor both textual context and geometric structure in compositional prompts, and offer no flexible control over the degree of shape adherence. No prior approach embeds full 3D shape geometry directly into the text-embedding space to enable view-independent, prompt-aware shape guidance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250496/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt containing a special [SHAPE-ID] token (e.g., 'a red [SHAPE-ID] on a beach') and a 3D shape, which is converted to a 1024-point cloud via farthest point sampling and encoded by Point-BERT into 65 tokens of 384 dimensions. The prompt is encoded by OpenCLIP ViT-H/14 into 77 token embeddings. A user-specified parameter λ ∈ [0,1] controls the strength of shape influence, and an optional depth image can be supplied for pose control. The output is a single RGB image generated by Stable Diffusion 2.1 that reflects both the textual description and the target 3D geometry.

## Method

ShapeWords introduces a Shape2CLIP module that maps 3D shape geometry into CLIP text-embedding space via six cross-attention blocks, where Point-BERT shape tokens serve as keys/values and prompt tokens as queries, producing a residual δT that updates only the shape-identifier and EOS token embeddings. This residual formulation (rather than direct prediction) is less prone to overfitting and preserves the prompt's original context while injecting geometric information. The module is trained with Score Distillation Sampling using a DreamTime time-dependent weighting, on 1.58M shape-prompt-image triplets constructed from ShapeNet depth renders and diverse compositional prompts. At inference, the shape-enhanced embedding is fed directly to Stable Diffusion 2.1 without any additional visual conditions (no depth, edge, or normal maps), and the user controls shape adherence through linear interpolation of the residual via λ. This design enables view-independent shape guidance, compositional prompt adherence, and flexible exploration of geometric variations.

## Results

On the compositional prompts dataset, ShapeWords achieves a FID of 73.8, KID of 8.58, aesthetics score of 5.45, and CLIP score of 31.5, outperforming ControlNet (FID 97.0, KID 10.40, Aes. 5.24, CLIP 26.9) across all four metrics. On the simple prompts dataset, ShapeWords@40% achieves shape adherence (S-IOU and S-CD) comparable to full ControlNet (K=100%) while using only 40% depth conditioning steps. In a 250-participant user study, ShapeWords outputs were preferred over the best ControlNet variant for both prompt adherence and shape resemblance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ControlNet (the primary baseline), ShapeWords wins decisively on the compositional prompts dataset: FID drops from 97.0 to 73.8 (24% reduction), CLIP score rises from 26.9 to 31.5 (+17%), and aesthetics improves from 5.24 to 5.45. On shape adherence (simple prompts), ShapeWords@40% matches full ControlNet's silhouette similarity while the CNet-Stop@60 and @80 variants, which have comparable shape adherence to ShapeWords, still underperform ShapeWords on prompt adherence and aesthetics. Against Ctrl-X, the comparison is qualitative only (Figure 5), where the authors report that Ctrl-X exhibits similar or worse shape-adherence behavior compared to ControlNet, while ShapeWords achieves a better balance between shape fidelity and prompt compliance. In the user study, ShapeWords was selected as the preferred image over the best ControlNet variant (CNet-Stop@K with highest CLIP score) for both text-prompt matching and shape resemblance, with a significantly larger proportion of 250 participants favoring ShapeWords in both conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

ShapeWords departs from the dominant paradigm of view-dependent visual conditioning (depth maps, edge maps via ControlNet, T2I-Adapter) by embedding 3D shape geometry directly into the CLIP text-embedding space through a cross-attention module (Shape2CLIP). For training, it repurposes the Score Distillation Sampling objective from DreamFusion and the time-dependent weighting from DreamTime, but applies them to a novel target: learning a residual mapping from Point-BERT shape tokens to OpenCLIP prompt tokens. Conceptually, it is closest to the 'learnable token' line of work (textual inversion, continuous 3D words, viewpoint textual inversion), but is the first to encode full 3D shape geometry into such tokens rather than scalar attributes or viewpoints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is view-independent 3D shape control with a user-tunable adherence parameter (λ), enabling exploration of geometric variations that depth-map-based methods cannot offer. However, the method is currently limited to ShapeNet-scale shapes, struggles with fine-grained thin-part geometry, and exhibits appearance biases (oversaturation, color preferences) attributable to the SDS training objective. Open questions include whether the shape-in-text-token formulation scales to more complex scenes and prompts, whether combining it with viewpoint or other 3D attribute tokens (as in [7,9]) yields complementary benefits, and whether better distillation losses can eliminate the noted appearance artifacts.
