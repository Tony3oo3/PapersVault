## Problem

Existing CAD generation methods rely on costly 3D scanning or high-quality synthetic reconstructions, making them impractical for everyday use. A critical gap remains in generating editable parametric CAD models directly from casually captured, unconstrained real-world images, compounded by the scarcity of paired real-world CAD-image data and the significant domain gap between textureless synthetic renderings and in-the-wild photos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250867/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts 4-view (multi-view) or single-view unconstrained images, from which depth and surface normal maps are extracted via Metric3D and encoded by a pre-trained DINO-V2 backbone; raw RGB is deliberately excluded to maintain texture invariance. The output is a fixed-length sequence of quantized CAD commands (sketch primitives SOL/L/A/R and extrusion E, with 8-bit integer parameters), which can be compiled into a parametric 3D CAD model importable into standard CAD tools.

## Method

CADCrafter is a three-stage latent diffusion pipeline: (1) a transformer autoencoder maps CAD command sequences to a latent space, (2) a diffusion transformer denoises the latent CAD code conditioned on geometric features produced by a custom transformer-based geometry encoder that adaptively fuses multi-view depth and normal DINO features with rotary positional embeddings, and (3) a DPO fine-tuning stage uses the CAD compiler as an automatic code checker to classify generated sequences as valid/invalid, then optimizes the diffusion model with a preference loss to suppress geometrically invalid outputs. For single-view inputs, knowledge from the multi-view geometry encoder is distilled into a single-view encoder via a cosine-similarity alignment loss, avoiding the need for a separately trained model. The key design choice of conditioning on texture-invariant geometric features (depth + normal) rather than RGB is what enables the model, trained exclusively on synthetic DeepCAD renderings, to generalize to real-world 3D-printed objects and unseen general objects.

## Results

On the synthetic DeepCAD test set, CADCrafter (multi-view) achieves 84.62% command accuracy, 73.31% parameter accuracy, 0.026 median Chamfer distance, and only 3.6% invalid rate. On the real-world RealCAD dataset, CADCrafter (multi-view) maintains 83.18% command accuracy, 66.89% parameter accuracy, 0.062 MedCD, and 6.7% invalid rate, while baselines like DeepCAD drop to 54.11% command accuracy and 56.7% invalid rate. The single-view variant also outperforms all baselines, achieving 81.23% command accuracy and 8.7% invalid rate on RealCAD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CADCrafter outperforms all CAD-specific baselines on every reported metric in both settings. Against DeepCAD (retrained with image encoder), CADCrafter_m improves command accuracy by 5.0 points (84.62 vs 79.62) and reduces invalid rate from 10.6% to 3.6% on synthetic data; on RealCAD the gap widens dramatically (83.18 vs 54.11 command accuracy, 6.7% vs 56.7% invalid rate). Against HNC-CAD, CADCrafter achieves substantially lower MedCD (0.026 vs 0.208 synthetic; 0.062 vs 0.305 real-world) and lower invalid rates. Against Img2CAD, CADCrafter_m leads by 4.05 points in command accuracy and reduces the invalid rate from 28.8% to 3.6% on synthetic data. Against image-to-3D methods (TripoSR, One-2-3-45, Wonder3D), CADCrafter_m achieves the lowest MedCD in both settings (0.026 vs 0.128–0.151 synthetic; 0.062 vs 0.125–0.147 real-world), though these methods do not output editable CAD commands. The most striking result is the domain gap: DeepCAD and HNC-CAD degrade by 20–25 points in command accuracy on RealCAD, while CADCrafter drops only ~1.4 points, confirming the benefit of geometry-based conditioning over raw RGB.

## Relation to Prior Work

CADCrafter extends the CAD generation lineage from DeepCAD (autoencoder + GAN) and HNC-CAD (hierarchical neural codes) by replacing 3D-representation conditioning (point clouds, B-Reps, voxels) with image-based conditioning, and by adopting a latent diffusion transformer rather than autoregressive or GAN-based generation. It departs from prior image-to-CAD work (Img2CAD) by using a generative diffusion framework instead of a discriminative one, and from image-to-3D methods (One-2-3-45, Wonder3D, TripoSR) by outputting editable parametric CAD commands rather than non-editable meshes or implicit fields. The key conceptual departure is conditioning on geometric features (depth and normal maps via DINO-V2) rather than raw RGB, and applying diffusion DPO with a CAD compiler as an automatic validity checker—an annotation-free preference signal borrowed from LLM alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a model trained exclusively on synthetic, textureless CAD renderings can generate valid, editable CAD command sequences from casually captured real-world images, addressing a practical bottleneck in digital-twin creation and manufacturing. The finding that excluding RGB and relying solely on depth+normal features substantially improves real-world generalization (while slightly hurting synthetic performance) offers a broadly applicable principle for synthetic-to-real transfer in geometry-sensitive tasks. Limitations include restriction to sketch-and-extrude operations on mechanical parts, a single-GPU training setup, and the RealCAD dataset being limited to 150 objects; open questions include scaling to more complex CAD operations (boolean, fillet, revolve), incorporating physical/material properties for manufacturing, and extending to text-guided editing.
