## Problem

Existing backdoor attacks on diffusion models rely on unconcealed triggers (e.g., gray boxes, eyeglasses) with obvious patterns that are easily detected by human inspection and trigger-inversion defenses like Elijah and TERD. The paper asks whether a single trigger can simultaneously be universal (image- and model-agnostic), effective (high attack success rate), and imperceptible (undetectable by both humans and SOTA defenses) for backdoor attacks on diffusion models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252344/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on the standard diffusion model input: sampled isotropic Gaussian noise, optionally stamped with a small additive trigger perturbation (l∞ ≈ 0.04, l2 ≈ 1.80 on CIFAR-10). The output is a generated RGB image at 32×32 (CIFAR-10) or 256×256 (CelebA-HQ) resolution — a normal image for benign noise or a specific target image (e.g., 'hat', 'cat') when the trigger is present. The trigger is universal across DDPM, LDM, and NCSN architectures and eleven different samplers (DDIM, DPM-Solver, UniPC, Heun, etc.), requiring no per-model or per-image adaptation.

## Method

The core contribution is adapting Universal Adversarial Perturbations (UAPs) — originally designed to fool discriminative classifiers — as imperceptible backdoor triggers for generative diffusion models. A GAN-like generator co-optimizes an additive l∞-bounded perturbation (the trigger τ) and a non-additive spatial transformation (f), guided by an image classifier (VGG or ResNet) via cross-entropy loss, progressively enhancing the trigger's fooling rate. The key insight is that UAP-based triggers introduce a comparable distribution shift to pattern-based triggers (securing high ASR during the backward diffusion process) yet lack the specific spatial patterns that trigger-inversion algorithms (Elijah, TERD) rely on for reconstruction. Backdoor injection follows the VillanDiffusion unified framework, but replaces the mask-based trigger stamping with simple additive injection (r(x,τ) = x + ε⊙τ), and the model is fine-tuned with a combined clean-generation and backdoor-attack loss across all timesteps.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with DDPM, UIBDiffusion achieves 100% ASR at just 5% poison rate, compared to 56% for VillanDiffusion and 99% for BadDiffusion at the same rate. At lower poison rates (1–4%), UIBDiffusion reaches 0.99 SSIM by 3% while VillanDiffusion only reaches 0.60 SSIM at 4%. Against the SOTA defense Elijah, UIBDiffusion is never detected (0% detection) across all poison rates (5%–90%) while maintaining 100% ASR, whereas BadDiffusion is detected at 100% and its ASR drops to 0%. The trigger also achieves 100% ASR across all 11 tested samplers (DDIM, DPM-Solver, UniPC, Heun, etc.) and remains robust to up to 20% random noise (99–100% ASR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BadDiffusion [23] and VillanDiffusion [25], UIBDiffusion achieves 100% ASR at 5% poison rate (vs. 99% and 56% respectively) and reaches 0.99 SSIM at 3% (vs. 1.10E-2 and 1.69E-2), demonstrating substantially better attack quality at low poison rates. Against the SOTA defense Elijah [26], UIBDiffusion is never detected (0% detection) at any poison rate from 5% to 90% while retaining 100% ASR, whereas BadDiffusion is detected at 100% and its ASR collapses to 0%. Against TERD [27], UIBDiffusion achieves 0% FPR (i.e., not flagged as backdoored) while all prior pattern-based triggers (BOX, STOP) are detected with 100% FPR. In the trigger generation ablation (Table 5), UIBDiffusion outperforms the original UAP [48] (100% vs. 43.75% ASR; SSIM 0.9967 vs. 0.6190) and the original GUAP [66] (100% vs. 0% ASR; SSIM 0.9967 vs. 0.1932), confirming that the co-optimized additive + non-additive perturbation design is essential for effectiveness. In terms of imperceptibility, the UIBDiffusion trigger has l∞ = 0.04 and l2 = 1.80 on CIFAR-10, roughly 12× and 3.4× smaller than prior pattern-based triggers (l∞ = 0.47, l2 = 6.21).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes universal adversarial perturbations (UAPs), originally designed to fool discriminative image classifiers, as backdoor triggers for diffusion models — a cross-paradigm transfer that prior DM backdoor work (BadDiffusion, TrojDiff, VillanDiffusion) did not explore, as those relied on visible, patterned triggers like gray boxes or glasses. The injection mechanism itself is inherited from VillanDiffusion's unified framework, so the novelty is entirely in trigger design: a GAN-inspired generator (inspired by GUAP) co-optimizes additive and non-additive perturbations to produce an imperceptible, image- and model-agnostic trigger. Conceptually, the paper positions itself at the intersection of adversarial robustness literature and DM security, arguing that the distribution shift induced by UAPs on sampled noise is sufficient to sustain a high-ASR backdoor while the lack of structured patterns defeats trigger-inversion defenses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: current SOTA defenses for DM backdoors (Elijah, TERD) rely on trigger inversion, and this work shows that unstructured, imperceptible triggers render that entire defense paradigm ineffective, forcing the field to develop new detection strategies that do not assume patterned triggers. Limitations include evaluation on only two datasets (CIFAR-10, CelebA-HQ) and a trigger generation process that depends on an external classifier (VGG/ResNet), raising open questions about whether the approach generalizes to text-to-image models, video diffusion, or higher-resolution settings. The paper also leaves open whether the 100% ASR at 5% poison rate holds under more realistic deployment scenarios (e.g., fine-tuned models, different training schedules) and whether the trigger's effectiveness is sensitive to the choice of guiding classifier.
