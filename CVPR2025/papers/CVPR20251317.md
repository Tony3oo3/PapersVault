## Problem

Existing autonomous driving evaluation frameworks face a fundamental trade-off: open-loop simulations provide realistic sensor data but cannot assess dynamic decision-making, while closed-loop simulators (e.g., CARLA, game engines) offer feedback-driven environments whose visual outputs diverge significantly from real-world sensor data. No prior method simultaneously delivers high-fidelity multi-view video, geometrically accurate 3D/4D scene structure, and a responsive closed-loop feedback mechanism for vision-based end-to-end driving agents.

## Input / Output

The system takes BEV road-structure maps and free-form text prompts (e.g., 'suburban area with rich vegetation') to generate static occupancy backgrounds, a bank of instance-level voxel actors with unique IDs and captions, and per-frame control signals from the ego AD agent and a traffic-flow engine. It produces a 4D occupancy world W = {S_city, (A_n, P_n)} where both static scenes and dynamic actors are occupancy grids, and renders this into 6-view video sequences (V ∈ R^{v×T×H×W}) with pixel-aligned semantic maps. The video generation operates in a view-flattened latent space (T×(vH'W')×C) to reduce cross-view attention overhead while preserving multi-view spatial coherence.

## Method

DrivingSphere comprises two modules. Dynamic Environment Composition uses OccDreamer, a BEV- and text-conditioned occupancy diffusion model with a VQVAE tokenizer, to generate city-scale static backgrounds via a progressive Scene Expansion Mechanism that conditions each new region on the overlapping boundary of the previous one; dynamic actors are selected from a pre-built voxel bank and positioned by control signals from a traffic-flow engine. Visual Scene Synthesis (VideoDreamer) employs a dual-path condition encoding: a global branch extracts 4D geometric features via a pretrained occupancy VAE encoder, while a local branch projects occupancy into per-view 2D semantic maps for pixel-accurate occlusion and depth cues; an ID-aware actor encoding fuses Fourier-encoded positions and IDs with T5 caption embeddings to maintain instance-level appearance consistency across frames and views. The denoiser is an ST-DiT with View-aware Spatial Self-Attention and a ControlNet branch (inspired by PixArt-δ) that injects the fused occupancy features, trained with an auto-regressive first-f-frame masking strategy for temporally coherent, artifact-free video generation.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, DrivingSphere achieves an FVD of 103.42, substantially lower than MagicDrive (218.12), Panacea (139), and DriveArena (185.32), indicating superior video generation fidelity. In downstream UniAD evaluation, DrivingSphere attains 3D object detection mAP of 21.45 and NDS of 34.16, outperforming all baselines while remaining closest to the real-data reference (mAP 37.98, NDS 49.85). In closed-loop evaluation, DrivingSphere yields a PDMS of 0.7281, RC of 0.1170, and ADS of 0.0851, all exceeding DriveArena's 0.6901, 0.0641, and 0.0508 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DrivingSphere outperforms all four baselines across every reported metric. Against MagicDrive [13], it reduces FVD by 53% (103.42 vs. 218.12) and improves 3D detection mAP by 66% (21.45 vs. 12.92), demonstrating that occupancy-based 4D conditioning captures geometric structure far better than 2D BEV sketches. Compared to Panacea [45], DrivingSphere achieves a 26% lower FVD and a 5.4-point NDS gain (34.16 vs. 27.73), with notably better lane segmentation (27.99 vs. 18.23). Versus DriveArena [51], the closest competitor, DrivingSphere still leads by 44% in FVD (103.42 vs. 185.32) and by 5.4 points in mAP, while also surpassing it in closed-loop PDMS (0.7281 vs. 0.6901) and route completion (11.7% vs. 6.4%). For 3D scene generation, OccDreamer halves the FID of SemCity [22] (274 vs. 634) and reduces MMD by 67% (0.082 vs. 0.251), confirming superior occupancy fidelity. The remaining gap to real nuScenes data (FVD not applicable, mAP 37.98) indicates that while DrivingSphere narrows the domain gap substantially, a residual fidelity difference persists.

## Relation to Prior Work

DrivingSphere sits at the intersection of two research threads: generative driving video models (MagicDrive, DriveDreamer, Panacea) that produce photorealistic multi-view sequences but operate in open-loop, and closed-loop simulators (CARLA, MetaDrive, DriveArena) that provide agent-environment feedback but suffer from visual domain gaps. The key architectural departure is using 4D occupancy data as the primary conditioning signal for video generation, building on OccSora's 4D occupancy encoder and SemCity's triplane diffusion for scene generation, while departing from DriveArena's 2D traffic-sketch conditioning. The video synthesis pipeline repurposes OpenSora's ST-DiT backbone with a ControlNet-style occupancy branch (inspired by PixArt-δ) and an ID-aware actor encoding mechanism for cross-view/cross-frame consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying occupancy-based 4D world modeling with diffusion-based video rendering in a closed-loop setting, DrivingSphere addresses a long-standing gap: no prior platform simultaneously offers photorealistic visual inputs, geometrically consistent 3D/4D structure, and responsive agent-environment feedback. However, the closed-loop results remain early (11.7% route completion with UniAD), evaluation is limited to a single dataset (nuScenes) and a single AD model, and the FVD gap to real data (103 vs. 38) indicates the domain gap is reduced but not closed. Open questions include scalability to longer horizons, multi-ego-agent scenarios, real-time inference, and whether the occupancy conditioning advantage persists as video diffusion models improve independently.
