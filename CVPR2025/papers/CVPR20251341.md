## Problem

Existing finger knuckle matching methods achieve poor accuracy on deformed, multi-pose knuckle images acquired under completely contactless imaging, and their feature templates lack the interpretability required for forensic courtroom arguments. Additionally, no prior work has established a theoretical uniqueness bound for 2D finger knuckle patterns, leaving law enforcement without a quantitative measure of distinctiveness for prosecution.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a probe and gallery finger knuckle image (RGB, originally captured at 4K UHD 3840×2160 from video, processed at 152×200 resolution) and produces a scalar similarity score, a set of mutually matched crease keypoint correspondences with their local descriptors, and the k-NN correspondence graph structure for each image. The output is explicitly designed to be visually interpretable—examiners can inspect the matched keypoint pairs and graph topology—mirroring the manual evidence consolidation used in forensic practice. A separate uniqueness module outputs an FRC index computed from a Poisson model over the keypoint templates.

## Method

The proposed CGN framework operates in four stages: (1) KnuckleCreasePoint (an adapted SuperPoint) detects n and m keypoints with descriptors and scores on the probe and gallery images; (2) KnucklePointPair, a self-attention/cross-attention GNN inspired by SuperGlue, estimates mutually matched correspondences via iterative cost-matrix optimization, retaining the top-c pairs; (3) k-NN graphs are constructed over the matched keypoints, and a novel 'convolution along the node dimension' (concatenating neighbor features rather than applying standard feature-dimension graph convolution) preserves the discriminative structure of the CNN descriptors, augmented with positional encoding to sharpen correspondence similarity; (4) a Cross-Node/Self-Graph module with a Tracker concatenates layer-wise arccosine similarity changes into a tracked graph, which is then classified by a GNN+MLP to yield the final similarity score. For uniqueness, a joint mixture multivariate Gaussian model (locations × 256-dim features) is fit via EM with BIC-selected components, and the FRC is estimated through a Poisson model over random-match probabilities across all imposter pairs.

## Results

On the most challenging Finger Knuckle v3.0 dataset (cross-pose, 104 subjects), CGN achieves 55.41% GAR at FAR=10⁻⁵, 66.35% at FAR=10⁻⁴, and 80.95% at FAR=10⁻², dramatically outperforming all baselines which score below 17% at FAR=10⁻². On the authors' own multi-pose video dataset (351 subjects), CGN reaches 89.99% GAR at FAR=10⁻⁵ and 93.19% at FAR=10⁻⁴, surpassing the best baseline (EfficientNetv2-m) by 22.8 and 11.4 percentage points respectively. Cross-database evaluation on the Hand Dorsal Image Dataset (712 subjects) yields 80.19% GAR at FAR=10⁻⁵, again far exceeding all competitors. The paper also reports a first-of-its-kind FRC uniqueness index of 1.860×10⁻⁴ for 2D finger knuckle patterns under the chosen tolerance parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CGN decisively outperforms all 15 baselines across every dataset and FAR threshold. On the Finger Knuckle v3.0 dataset, general-purpose CNN/Transformer backbones (DenseNet-161, EfficientNetv2-m, ResNet-101, ViT-B) are essentially non-functional (≤0.78% GAR at FAR=10⁻²), while finger-knuckle-specific methods (FKNet, RFNet-RSIL, STResNet) reach at most 17.03% at FAR=10⁻²—still a 64-point gap behind CGN's 80.95%. On the authors' multi-pose video dataset, the best general backbone (EfficientNetv2-m at 67.22% GAR at FAR=10⁻⁵) is beaten by CGN (89.99%) by 22.8 points, and the best knuckle-specific method (RFNet-RSIL at 56.39%) by 33.6 points. On the Hand Dorsal cross-database evaluation, CGN (80.19% at FAR=10⁻⁵) exceeds the next-best method (EfficientNetv2-m at 53.11%) by 27 points. Among graph similarity models (GMN, SimGNN, MGMN, ERIC) and graph convolution alternatives (GIN, SAGE, GATv2, GCN) tested in ablation, CGN's node-dimension convolution with positional encoding and cross-node arccosine similarity consistently yields the highest ROC AUC, confirming that the architectural choices are critical to the performance gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the SuperPoint self-supervised keypoint detector as a domain-specific crease-point extractor (KnuckleCreasePoint) and adapts the SuperGlue self/cross-attention GNN architecture for correspondence estimation, then feeds the resulting matched keypoints into a novel graph-similarity module (CGN) that departs from prior graph-matching models (GMN, SimGNN, MGMN, ERIC) by performing convolution along the node dimension rather than the feature dimension and tracking cross-node similarity changes across layers. The uniqueness estimation section directly transplants the Poisson-based FRC framework from fingerprint individuality assessment (Zhu et al. 2007, Dass 2010) to 2D knuckle patterns for the first time. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against texture-based CNN knuckle matchers (FKNet, RFNet, STResNet) that lack interpretability and fail under pose deformation, arguing that structural keypoint correspondences are both more robust and forensically admissible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explainable, keypoint-based matching pipeline addresses a genuine gap in forensic practice where knuckle crease evidence is currently matched manually and must be interpretable in court. The first theoretical uniqueness analysis for 2D knuckle patterns (FRC values down to ~10⁻¹⁴ under tight tolerances) provides the statistical grounding needed for courtroom arguments, paralleling what fingerprint individuality studies have offered for decades. Key limitations include the GNN's lack of spatial awareness (acknowledged by the authors), the single-institution dataset, and the fact that the most dramatic results (55–81% vs. near-0% baselines on the v3.0 dataset) are against methods fundamentally mismatched to the deformation challenge, making the absolute quality of the proposed method harder to calibrate. Open questions include whether spatial-aware GNN extensions can close the remaining gap and whether the uniqueness estimates hold at higher imaging resolutions comparable to fingerprint standards (~500 dpi).
