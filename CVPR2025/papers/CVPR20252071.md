## Problem

Previous MLLM-based representation models (e.g., E5-V, VISTA) achieve unified embedding spaces but their cross-modal retrieval performance lags significantly behind SoTA CLIP-series models, because their training data fails to activate the multimodal alignment capabilities already present in MLLMs. Additionally, CLIP architectures cannot natively support composed modality retrieval (e.g., image+text → image) due to the absence of a shared modality fusion pathway.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252071/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a single image, a sequence of video frames, a text string, or any combination thereof (e.g., image + text instruction) as input to a single parameter-shared Qwen2VL-based MLLM. The vision encoder (ViT) is frozen while the Projector and LLM are trained; input resolution is tested from 224 to 560 pixels, with performance plateauing at 448. For representation tasks, the last token's hidden state is projected through a linear layer to produce a fixed-dimension embedding vector; for ranking tasks, the same architecture outputs a binary matching score via a different instruction prompt, with all parameters shared except the final linear head.

## Method

The core insight is that contrastive learning data enriched with dense multimodal knowledge (visual-centric words mapping to objects, actions, and descriptions, with 50+ knowledge points per sample) dramatically activates the alignment capabilities already latent in MLLMs, whereas the sparse alt-text data used by CLIP training provides only ~1/4 the knowledge density. The authors construct DeKon5M (5M samples spanning image-text, video-text, composed modality, and unimodal retrieval) and train DeKR on Qwen2VL using a modified contrastive loss with manually introduced hard negatives and batch caching. Bidirectional attention is used for image retrieval while causal attention is preferred for video retrieval, reflecting their respective emphasis on detail versus temporal dynamics. The resulting model achieves SoTA cross-modal retrieval with roughly one-thousandth the training data of CLIP-series models and unifies representation and ranking tasks within a single architecture.

## Results

On zero-shot image-text retrieval (Table 3), DeKR-7B@448 achieves an average Recall@1 of 87.1 across COCO-5K and Flickr30k, surpassing the SoTA CLIP-series model InternVL-C (86.6) by 0.5 points and the MLLM-based E5-V (83.9) by 3.2 points. On zero-shot video-text retrieval (Table 4), DeKR-7B@448 reaches 41.0 average Recall@1 on MSR-VTT and DiDeMo, exceeding InternVideo-L (36.3) by 4.7 points. On composed image retrieval (Table 5), DeKR-7B achieves 32.0 average R@1 on FashionIQ and 33.7 R@5 on CIRR val-all, outperforming E5-V and VISTA. On composed text retrieval (Table 6), DeKR-7B scores 86.0 R@5 on ReMuQ and 80.6 on WebQA T2T, beating VISTA by 11.1 points on the latter.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeKR-7B outperforms all CLIP-series baselines on average image-text retrieval: it beats InternVL-C (86.6) by 0.5 points, Eva02-E+ (85.1) by 2.0 points, and OpenCLIP-bigG (85.0) by 2.1 points, marking the first time an MLLM-based model surpasses the CLIP family in cross-modal retrieval. Against MLLM-based competitors, DeKR-7B exceeds E5-V (8B) by 3.2 points on image-text retrieval and by 12.7 points on composed image retrieval (CIRR val-all R@5: 33.7 vs. 22.5 for VISTA). On video-text retrieval, DeKR-7B (41.0 avg) surpasses InternVideo-L (36.3) by 4.7 points and SigLIP (34.6) by 6.4 points. On composed text retrieval, DeKR-7B beats VISTA by 1.3 points on ReMuQ R@5 (86.0 vs. 84.7) and by 11.1 points on WebQA T2T (80.6 vs. 69.5). In multimodal ranking (Table 7), DeKR-Rank outperforms Qwen2VL-Rank by 14.6 points on Text-to-Image R@1 (84.8 vs. 70.2), demonstrating that representation training transfers to ranking. On fine-tuned ranking (Table 8), DeKR achieves 98.4 R@1 on Flickr I2T and 90.1 R@1 on T2I, exceeding BLIP2-g (97.6 / 89.7) and all other fine-tuned baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant CLIP dual-encoder paradigm for multimodal representation by repurposing a frozen-ViT + projector + LLM architecture (Qwen2VL) as a single unified encoder for text, image, video, and composed inputs. It builds conceptually on two prior lines: (1) MLLM-based representation models like E5-V and VISTA, which demonstrated feasibility but suffered from weak cross-modal alignment, and (2) early multimodal alignment work (Ernie-VIL, Rosita) that identified visual-centric words as the key to vision-text grounding. The paper's core departure is the hypothesis that the CLIP-vs-MLLM retrieval gap is not architectural but data-driven, and that injecting dense multimodal knowledge into contrastive training closes it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that an MLLM-based model can match or exceed SoTA CLIP-series models on cross-modal retrieval while using roughly 1/1000th the training data, this work challenges the field's implicit assumption that dual-encoder contrastive pretraining at billion-pair scale is necessary for strong retrieval. The dense-knowledge insight (alignment quality per sample matters more than raw data volume) is broadly applicable to any multimodal pretraining pipeline. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to audio or 3D modalities, whether the 80% dense-knowledge ratio is optimal across domains, and whether the video retrieval gap (causal vs. bidirectional attention trade-off) can be resolved without sacrificing image performance.
