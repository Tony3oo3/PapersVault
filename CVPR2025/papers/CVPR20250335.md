## Problem

Diffusion models struggle with complex text prompts containing multiple objects, attributes, and spatial relations, producing images with incorrect attribute binding, misplaced objects, and unrealistic compositions. Existing solutions either rely on coarse spatial conditioning, require expensive fine-tuning, or use single-pass Chain-of-Thought reasoning from MLLMs that lacks error correction and fails to fully parse intricate scene elements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250335/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single complex text prompt (e.g., describing multiple objects with attributes, spatial and action relations, and background) and produces a standard RGB image. It is entirely training-free, operating on top of pre-trained latent diffusion models (SD1.5, SD2.0, SDXL-base) with 20 denoising steps and classifier-free guidance scale 7.0. The MLLM component uses GPT-4o-mini to decompose the prompt into per-object prompts, a background prompt, and bounding box coordinates before image synthesis.

## Method

MCCD comprises two modules. The Multi-agent Collaboration-based Scene Parsing (MCP) module constructs six specialized MLLM agents (object, background, action-relation, spatial-relation, layout, and aesthetics) coordinated by a conductor that sequentially selects agents in a forward CoT loop and an evaluator that triggers backward feedback to correct parsing errors iteratively. The Hierarchical Compositional Diffusion (HCD) module feeds each parsed prompt through cross-attention in parallel at every denoising timestep, then composites the resulting latents using a depth-aware Gaussian mask for smooth fusion in overlapping bounding-box regions, a regional enhancement step that amplifies object features inside boxes while suppressing background leakage, and a 2D Gaussian filter to smooth latent-space discontinuities at box boundaries. The final denoised latent is a weighted blend (μ=0.8) of the full-prompt latent and the smoothed composite, preserving global semantics while enforcing local spatial structure.

## Results

On T2I-CompBench, MCCD applied to SDXL-base achieves the best overall performance among all MCCD variants, with a Complex score of 0.3348 (vs. 0.3130 baseline), representing a 9.04% average improvement across all six metrics. The Spatial metric shows the largest relative gain, improving by up to 41.49% (SD2.0-base: 0.1140 → 0.1613). Attribute Binding also benefits substantially, e.g., Texture improves from 0.4907 to 0.5647 on SDXL-base. All improvements are achieved in a training-free manner with 20 inference steps and CFG scale 7.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCCD is evaluated as a training-free plug-in that improves four base diffusion models (SD1.5, SD2.0-base, SD2.0, SDXL-base), and the best variant (SDXL-base+MCCD, Complex 0.3348) is compared against seven external baselines. It outperforms GORS (0.3328) and Composable Diffusion (0.2898) on the Complex metric, and also beats Structured Diffusion (0.3355) by a narrow margin on most sub-metrics. However, it trails Attn-Exctv2 (0.3401), DALL-E 2 (0.3696), and PixArt-α (0.4117) on the overall Complex score, with the gap to PixArt-α being the largest (~7.7%). The most striking gain is on the Spatial metric, where SDXL-base+MCCD reaches 0.2350, surpassing all baselines except PixArt-α (0.2082) and GORS (0.1815), indicating that the multi-agent scene parsing and Gaussian-mask-based dynamic integration are particularly effective at enforcing correct spatial relationships.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the compositional text-to-image generation line (GLIGEN, ReCo, LMD, RPG) by replacing single-agent Chain-of-Thought layout extraction with a multi-agent collaboration system that performs forward CoT reasoning and backward feedback across six specialized agents (object, background, action, spatial, layout, aesthetics). On the diffusion side, it departs from prior region-based methods (RPG's parallel sub-region denoising, BoxDiff's cross-attention constraints) by introducing a hierarchical compositional diffusion pipeline with depth-aware Gaussian masks for overlapping regions, regional enhancement, and latent-space smoothing. The framework is explicitly training-free, positioning it as a plug-and-play enhancement over frozen diffusion backbones (SD1.5, SD2.0, SDXL) rather than a fine-tuned or adapter-based approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, multi-agent design makes MCCD broadly applicable to any pre-trained diffusion model without retraining, which is practically valuable given the compute cost of fine-tuning. However, absolute scores on T2I-CompBench remain below strong baselines like PixArt-α and DALL-E 2 on most metrics, and the reliance on GPT-4o-mini for agent construction introduces inference cost and API dependency that limit deployment. Open questions include whether the multi-agent parsing generalizes to scenes with more than a handful of objects, whether the Gaussian-mask fusion strategy scales to highly occluded or densely packed compositions, and whether the backward-feedback loop can be made more efficient than iterative re-execution.
