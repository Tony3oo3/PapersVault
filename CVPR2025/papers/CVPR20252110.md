## Problem

Jointly controlling camera trajectories and human poses in video generation remains largely unsolved: existing methods either handle only one motion type, rely on coarse representations (bounding boxes, object-level keypoints) that miss subtle pose changes, or naively add motion signals without modeling their spatially- and temporally-varying interactions. Additionally, all prior joint-control approaches use UNet backbones, and no DiT-based model for this task had been proposed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252110/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt, a camera trajectory encoded as per-pixel Plücker coordinates (6×T×H×W derived from intrinsic/extrinsic matrices), and a human pose map from DWPose as conditioning signals; for I2V it additionally accepts a reference image. All motion signals are patchified via 2D and 3D causal convolutions to match the 3D Causal VAE latent grid (compression ratios p spatially, q temporally). The output is a 49-frame video at 256×384 resolution generated with 50-step DDIM sampling and classifier-free guidance (scale 7.5).

## Method

TokenMotion is the first DiT-based video diffusion framework for human-centric joint motion control, built on CogVideoX-2B. Its core contribution is a decouple-and-fuse strategy: camera and pose tokens are first processed through parallel self-attention blocks to capture global temporal dependencies, then a human-aware dynamic mask—combining a learnable component with a dilated binary pose prior—token-wise separates human-relevant regions from the global camera signal via a softmax-weighted fusion. The fused motion tokens are injected into visual tokens through a ControlNet-style cross-attention block with a LoRA adapter, forcing the model to disentangle both motions from a single fused representation. Only the two motion encoders and the decouple-and-fuse module are trained (all backbone parameters frozen), using a mixed dataset of HumanVid (both signals) and RealEstate10K (camera-only with blank pose cues) at 256×384 resolution.

## Results

On the T2V joint-control task, TokenMotion-T achieves the best overall visual quality (LPIPS 0.46, FID 82.36, FVD 361.03) and dramatically outperforms all baselines on human-motion alignment, reducing PoseErr from 165–173 to 45.24 and DetErr from 10.7–14.7% to 2.50%. On the I2V joint-control task, TokenMotion-I similarly leads on all metrics, achieving FVD 332.27 (vs. 878.59 for ImageConductor) and PoseErr 83.82 (vs. 164.28). For camera-only control on RealEstate10K, TokenMotion achieves TransErr 5.39 and KptsErr 4.36, outperforming CameraCtrl (6.48, 5.61) and MotionCtrl-camera (5.83, 4.46).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TokenMotion-T decisively outperforms MotionCtrl, MotionBooth, and Direct-A-Video on human-motion metrics (PoseErr 45.24 vs. 165–173; DetErr 2.50% vs. 10.7–14.7%), confirming that the decouple-and-fuse strategy with DWPose-based representation captures subtle pose changes that bounding-box or keypoint-based methods miss. On camera metrics, TokenMotion-T is slightly behind Direct-A-Video (KptsErr 4.43 vs. 3.85; TransErr 5.34 vs. 5.06) but ahead of MotionCtrl and MotionBooth, indicating a small trade-off in camera fidelity for the large gain in human-motion accuracy. For I2V, TokenMotion-I beats ImageConductor on every single metric, with the largest gaps in FVD (332 vs. 879) and PoseErr (84 vs. 164), showing that cascading separate camera and object controls without explicit interaction modeling is substantially inferior. On the camera-only task (Table 2), TokenMotion surpasses both CameraCtrl and MotionCtrl-camera in TransErr (5.39 vs. 6.48/5.83) and KptsErr (4.36 vs. 5.61/4.46), while matching them closely on RotErr (0.28 vs. 0.27/0.33). Overall, the paper's method wins on the primary human-motion and general-quality metrics across both T2V and I2V settings, with the only notable concession being a marginal camera-alignment gap versus Direct-A-Video in T2V.

## Relation to Prior Work

TokenMotion builds on the CogVideoX-2B DiT backbone and adopts Plücker embeddings from CameraCtrl for camera representation, but departs from prior joint-control methods (MotionCtrl, Direct-A-Video, MotionBooth) in two key ways: it replaces coarse object-level representations (bounding boxes, keypoints) with DWPose-based human pose tokens, and it introduces a decouple-and-fuse strategy with a human-aware dynamic mask to handle the spatially-and-temporally-varying interaction between camera and human motion signals. The motion patchification module addresses a DiT-specific challenge identified in recent camera-control work, where standard UNet-style control injection degrades in transformer architectures. This positions the paper as the first DiT-based framework to jointly control both camera trajectories and fine-grained human kinematics, filling a gap left by UNet-based predecessors that reported unresolved motion conflicts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap for AI-generated video content in creative production, where simultaneous control of dramatic camera movements and subtle human pose changes (e.g., the Grammy Glambot scenario) is essential but previously unsolved. The ablation showing that naive signal addition nearly triples FVD (361→890) provides concrete evidence that the interaction between camera and human motion requires dedicated architectural handling rather than simple concatenation. However, visual quality remains bounded by the CogVideoX-2B backbone, with acknowledged failures in finger articulation and facial detail preservation, and the evaluation is limited to a single 500-video HumanVid subset and one backbone scale, leaving open whether the decouple-and-fuse strategy generalizes to larger DiT models or longer video sequences.
