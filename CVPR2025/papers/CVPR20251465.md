## Problem

Blind super-resolution models overfit to training degradations, and existing regularization methods (Dropout, Simple-Align) only constrain features before the final convolutional layer, leaving intermediate layers without explicit regularization. Directly applying dropout to intermediate layers causes significant performance drops due to training-testing inconsistency (variance shift through activation functions) and differing generalization requirements across shallow and deep layers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251465/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on low-quality RGB images with unknown degradation (blur, noise, JPEG, or combinations thereof) and produces high-resolution reconstructions at ×4 scale. It is plug-and-play for both CNN-based (SRResNet, RRDB) and transformer-based (SwinIR) SR backbones, trained on 32×32 LR patches from DIV2K with L1 loss. The adaptive dropout module is inserted within residual blocks or transformer blocks, with the standard dropout before the final convolutional layer retained as in prior work.

## Method

Adaptive Dropout consists of two components. First, the adaptive dropout format replaces standard dropout with f(x) = wx + (1−w)dropout(x, p), adaptively coupling the original and dropouted features; this reduces the training-testing variance shift by a factor of (1−w)², mitigating inconsistency accumulation across layers. Second, the adaptive training strategy addresses differing generalization needs across layers via two variants: Implicit Adaptive Dropout uses a learnable weight w (channel-attention-like module) with value-w in shallow layers and vector-w in deep layers, while Explicit Adaptive Dropout initializes w and anneals it block-by-block from shallow to deep during training, so shallow features enter the fitting stage earlier while deep features remain regularized longer to avoid overfitting training degradations.

## Results

On Set5 with SRResNet (×4, clean), Explicit Adaptive Dropout achieves 26.11 dB PSNR, outperforming standard Dropout (25.67 dB) and no regularization (24.89 dB). On the more challenging Urban100 dataset, the method reaches 21.87 dB (SRResNet) and 22.41 dB (SwinIR) under clean degradation. On real-world RealSR, the method achieves 25.07–25.16 dB PSNR (SRResNet) and 25.35–25.39 dB (RRDB), surpassing Simple-Align by 0.44–0.63 dB. Across all eight degradation types and five synthetic benchmarks, both variants of Adaptive Dropout consistently outperform the Dropout [29] baseline by 0.2–0.5 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Dropout [29] (the prior state-of-the-art regularization for blind SR), both Explicit and Implicit Adaptive Dropout win on every dataset and every degradation type in Table 1, with gains of 0.2–0.5 dB on synthetic benchmarks and up to 0.4 dB on multi-degradation settings (e.g., b+n+j on Set5: 23.10 vs. 22.99 dB for SRResNet). Against Simple-Align [56], the proposed method wins on all five synthetic datasets and all three real-world datasets (RealSR, DRealSR, NTIRE 2018), with the largest margins on real-world data (e.g., +0.44 dB on RealSR, +0.37 dB on DRealSR for SRResNet). On generative blind SR (Table 4), adding Adaptive Dropout to Real-ESRGAN [57] improves PSNR from 23.19 to 23.64 dB and LPIPS from 0.31 to 0.24, while for ResShift [66] it raises PSNR from 22.55 to 22.69 dB and MANIQA from 0.2906 to 0.3211. The method also transfers to single-degradation tasks (deraining, denoising, dehazing), consistently improving PSNR by 0.2–0.8 dB over the unregularized baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dropout-based regularization line initiated by Kong et al. (CVPR 2022), which showed that dropout before the final convolutional layer improves blind SR generalization but warned against applying it elsewhere. It also engages with Simple-Align (Wang et al., CVPR 2024), which argued dropout harms high-frequency recovery and proposed cross-degradation feature alignment as an alternative. The key departure is the argument that intermediate layers require explicit regularization (not just implicit constraints from the final layer), and the diagnosis of why naive dropout fails there: training-testing variance shift accumulation and differing generalization requirements between shallow (general feature) and deep (degradation-specific) layers. The proposed Adaptive Dropout format and layer-wise annealing strategy are designed to resolve both issues simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The layer-wise annealing principle—shallow layers enter the fitting stage earlier while deep layers remain in the generalization stage longer—provides a practical, architecture-agnostic training strategy that practitioners can adopt with minimal code changes. The channel ablation evidence that final-layer regularization does not generalize to intermediate features is a useful diagnostic for anyone designing regularization for multi-layer networks. However, the PSNR gains are modest (typically 0.1–0.5 dB over existing dropout), and the method is validated primarily on blind SR with multi-degradation training; whether the layer-specific generalization insight transfers to other restoration or recognition tasks remains to be established. An open question is whether the adaptive coupling weight w could be further informed by theoretical bounds on the variance shift rather than learned or annealed heuristically.
