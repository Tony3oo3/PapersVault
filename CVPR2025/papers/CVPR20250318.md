## Problem

Existing distribution matching metrics for dataset distillation—MSE in Euclidean space and MMD in Hilbert space—fail to capture full distributional differences between real and synthetic data, since moment equivalence does not guarantee distributional equivalence. This leads to unreliable discrepancy measures and suboptimal synthetic image quality, particularly at low image-per-class budgets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250318/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled real image dataset (32×32 CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 Tiny ImageNet, or 128×128 ImageNet subsets) and produces a synthetic condensed dataset with the same class labels at a target IPC (1, 10, or 50). The synthetic images are optimized directly as pixel parameters using a multiformation parameterization with scale factor ρ=2, and the distillation is performed with a 3–5 layer ConvNet as the feature extractor depending on dataset resolution. The entire pipeline runs on a single GPU with as little as 2.3 GB memory for CIFAR-100.

## Method

NCFM reformulates distribution matching as a minmax optimization: a lightweight sampling network ψ (parameterizing a scale mixture of normals over frequency arguments) is trained to maximize the Neural Characteristic Function Discrepancy (NCFD), while the synthetic dataset is optimized to minimize it. NCFD is built on the Characteristic Function (Fourier transform of the distribution), which uniquely determines the distribution and decomposes via Euler's formula into an amplitude term (controlling diversity) and a phase term (controlling realism), balanced by a hyperparameter α. A hybrid feature extractor—β-blending checkpoints from a subset of trained models at each step—maps images into a latent space where the CF is computed empirically over 4096 sampled frequency arguments. Unlike MMD's quadratic kernel computation, NCFD achieves linear-time complexity, enabling 300× lower GPU memory and 20× faster distillation while matching or exceeding SOTA accuracy.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 at 1 IPC, NCFM achieves 50.5% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the strongest baselines DATM (46.9%) and DANCE (47.1%) by 3.6 and 3.4 points respectively, and outperforming the original DM method (26.0%) by 24.5 points. On CIFAR-100 at 1 IPC, NCFM reaches 34.4% vs. 27.9% for both DATM and DANCE. On high-resolution ImageNet subsets at 10 IPC, NCFM achieves 75.2% on ImageSquawk (vs. 52.3% for MTT, a 22.9-point gain) and 60.5% on ImageMeow (vs. 40.4% for MTT, a 20.1-point gain). NCFM also reduces peak GPU memory by over 300× and training speed by 20× compared to trajectory-matching methods like DATM, which frequently hit OOM at 50 IPC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NCFM outperforms all distribution-matching baselines (DM, DANCE, IDM, M3D, IID, DSDM) across every IPC on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, with the largest gap over DM (24.5 points on CIFAR-10 at 1 IPC). Against trajectory-matching methods, NCFM beats MTT, FTD, ATT, and TESLA on CIFAR-10/100 at all IPCs, and matches or exceeds DATM on CIFAR-10 (71.8 vs. 66.8 at 10 IPC) and CIFAR-100 (55.1 vs. 55.0 at 50 IPC). However, on Tiny ImageNet at 10 and 50 IPC, NCFM (27.4% and 29.8%) trails both DATM (31.1% and 39.7%) and RDED (39.6% and 47.6%), indicating that trajectory-matching methods retain an advantage on this particular dataset at higher IPC. On ImageNet subsets (128×128), NCFM dominates all baselines, achieving 75.2% on ImageSquawk at 10 IPC versus 52.3% for MTT and 50.4% for DM. In terms of efficiency, NCFM requires only 1.6–2.0 GB peak GPU memory on CIFAR-100 (vs. OOM for DATM and 61.6 GB for MTT at 10 IPC) and runs 20–30× faster per iteration, making it the only method that avoids OOM at 50 IPC on both CIFAR-100 and Tiny ImageNet.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Distribution Matching (DM) framework introduced by Zhao et al. [58], which replaced bi-level optimization with direct distributional comparison for dataset distillation. The key departure is replacing the MMD-based moment matching (which the authors argue is an insufficient condition for distributional equivalence) with a Characteristic Function Discrepancy that captures full distributional information, and wrapping the entire procedure in a minmax adversarial paradigm where a sampling network learns the optimal frequency arguments. The neural CF sampling strategy is adapted from Li et al. [28], who used scale mixtures of normals for conditional image generation, but here it is repurposed to dynamically maximize the discrepancy metric during distillation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 300× GPU memory reduction and 20× speed improvement over trajectory-matching SOTA (while achieving higher accuracy) makes dataset distillation feasible on consumer hardware (single 2080 Ti, ~2.3 GB), which is a significant practical barrier removal for the field. The theoretical grounding via Lévy's convergence theorem and the uniqueness property of CFs provides a principled justification that MMD's moment alignment is fundamentally insufficient, potentially redirecting future DM-based methods toward full distributional metrics. Open questions include whether the minmax CF paradigm extends to non-image modalities (text, audio, graph) and whether the learned sampling distribution transfers across architectures or must be re-optimized per task.
