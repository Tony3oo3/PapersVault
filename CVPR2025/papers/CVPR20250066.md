## Problem

Existing deep multi-view clustering methods incorporate inter-view complementary information via simple addition of a consensus graph, overlook high-order intra-view relationships that ordinary graphs cannot capture, and assign uniform fusion weights to all samples within a view. These limitations result in incomplete exploitation of the rich multi-scale information available in multi-view data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250066/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N samples across V views, each with potentially different feature dimensions d_v, which are projected to a common dimension d via linear layers. Graph structures per view are constructed using Euclidean distance with k=2 neighbors, and a consensus graph is initialized by averaging all view graphs. The output is a set of cluster assignments (via a self-trainable Student-t-based clustering layer) along with learned node representations and a refined consensus graph (node features H and structural matrix U) that can be iteratively updated.

## Method

Medusa operates at three scales. At the inter-view scale, a Dual Graph Diffusion Convolution module uses personalized PageRank (α=0.4) to simultaneously propagate both edge structure and node features from the consensus graph into each view, with an adaptive coefficient b computed from the Frobenius distance between the view and consensus graph so that views farther from consensus receive more guidance. At the intra-view scale, a High-Order Contrastive Learning module constructs hypergraphs from the enhanced adjacency matrices (thresholded at the mean edge weight) and defines positive pairs as both cross-view same-sample pairs and same-hyperedge intra-view pairs, weighted by normalized sub-edge weights. At the sample scale, a Fine-Grained Multi-View Fusion module applies self-attention with a gating unit to learn an N×N weight matrix per view, enabling sample-level adaptive fusion into a new consensus graph. Training proceeds in two phases: pre-training optimizes the contrastive and reconstruction losses, then fine-tuning optimizes the KL-divergence clustering loss and consensus-graph reconstruction loss.

## Results

Medusa achieves the best ACC, NMI, and Purity across all eight benchmark datasets. On the smaller datasets, it reaches 94.26% ACC / 89.31% NMI / 95.24% Purity on MSRC-v1 and 78.84% ACC / 78.41% NMI / 78.89% Purity on Yale. On larger-scale datasets, it attains 33.27% ACC / 51.64% NMI on Caltech101, 27.81% ACC / 34.92% NMI on SUNRGBD, and 35.24% ACC / 18.84% NMI on AWA. The paper reports a consistent 1–2% improvement over the second-best method on every metric and dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Medusa outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset and every metric (ACC, NMI, Purity). GDMVC [1] is the closest competitor, ranking second on seven of eight datasets; Medusa leads it by 1.93% ACC on Citeseer (65.34 vs. 63.41), 1.05% ACC on MSRC-v1 (94.26 vs. 93.21), and 2.59% ACC on Yale (78.84 vs. 76.45). AONGR [34] is the second-best on NUSWIDEOBJ (24.23% ACC) and SUNRGBD NMI (28.21%), where Medusa gains 1.66% ACC and 6.71% NMI respectively. On the high-dimensional AWA dataset, Medusa achieves 35.24% ACC versus 33.62% for GDMVC and 33.56% for DFP-GNN [26], a gap of roughly 1.6–1.7%. On Caltech101, MVD [14] leads in NMI among baselines (50.27%) but Medusa still surpasses it by 1.37% (51.64%). The weakest baselines overall are DSMVC [20] and AONGR [34], which trail Medusa by 5–20+ points on ACC depending on the dataset, particularly on AWA and NUSWIDEOBJ.

## Relation to Prior Work

Medusa extends the cross-view graph diffusion framework of CGD (Tang et al., AAAAI 2020) by diffusing both edge structure and node features simultaneously, with an adaptive per-view weighting coefficient that modulates how much consensus-graph information each view receives. It also adopts the self-trainable clustering layer from DFP-GNN (Xiao et al., 2023) for the final clustering stage. The key conceptual departure is the multi-scale decomposition—inter-view (dual diffusion), intra-view (hypergraph contrastive learning), and sample-level (self-attention fusion)—which prior methods address in isolation or with simpler mechanisms such as uniform view weighting or pairwise-only contrastive objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, modular recipe for integrating three complementary information sources in multi-view clustering, and the ablation studies confirm each component contributes meaningfully (~3–4% per module). However, the overall gains over strong baselines are modest (1–2% on most datasets), and the method's complexity (dual diffusion, hypergraph construction, gating-based fusion, two-phase training) raises questions about whether the added architectural overhead is justified in practice. Open questions include scalability beyond the ~10K-sample datasets tested, sensitivity to the number of views, and whether the multi-scale decomposition can be simplified without significant performance loss.
