## Problem

Stable Diffusion-based super-resolution models achieve strong visual quality but are impractical for on-device deployment due to their large parameter counts (>1B) and high latency (hundreds of denoising steps). No prior work has simultaneously reduced both model size and inference steps to enable real-time SR on mobile hardware while maintaining competitive quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251504/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 128×128 low-resolution RGB patch and produces a 512×512 high-resolution patch (4× upscale), operating in the latent space of a compact VAE. For full-image SR (e.g., 512×512 → 2048×2048), the input is tiled into 128×128 patches with 25% overlap, requiring 25 sequential model evaluations. At inference, only the lightweight LR encoder and UNet are needed (the HR encoder is training-only), and the model runs with W8A16 quantization on a Samsung S24 NPU in ~38 ms per patch.

## Method

Edge-SD-SR introduces bidirectional conditioning, which modifies the forward noising process so that both the mean and variance of the Gaussian perturbation are conditioned on the LR latent, allowing the model to start denoising directly from the LR image rather than pure noise and eliminating the need to concatenate LR as an explicit input to the UNet. The LR encoder is trained jointly with the UNet using a stop-gradient schedule that gradually shifts from t=1-dominant sampling to uniform sampling, preventing degenerate encoder solutions while enabling a much smaller encoder (14M params vs. 83M). The UNet is pruned to 158M parameters (vs. 860M in SD 1.5) by halving base channels and restricting transformer blocks to lower resolutions. Scale distillation from a ×2 teacher to a ×4 student yields a 1-step inference model, and the decoder is then fine-tuned on the UNet's single-step output using VQAE loss to adapt to the altered latent space. The resulting 169M-parameter, 142 GFLOP model matches or outperforms much larger SD-SR baselines on standard benchmarks.

## Results

Edge-SD-SR (169M params, 142 GFLOPs, 1 denoising step) achieves FID 25.37, LPIPS 0.249, PSNR 24.10, SSIM 0.617, and MUSIQ 69.58 on DIV2K-RealESRGAN, outperforming LDM (200 steps, 9820 GFLOPs) and StableSR (200 steps, 47403 GFLOPs) on most metrics. On real-world datasets, it achieves LPIPS 0.278 (RealSR), 0.292 (DRealSR), and MUSIQ 60.09 (DPED-iphone), surpassing all baselines except YONOS-SR on LPIPS. The model 4× upscales a 512×512 image to 2048×2048 in ~1.1 s on a Samsung S24 NPU with W8A16 quantization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LDM (200 steps, 9820 GFLOPs, 241M params), Edge-SD-SR wins on all reported metrics across all four datasets, e.g. FID 25.37 vs 26.47 and MUSIQ 69.58 vs 62.27 on DIV2K, while using 69× fewer GFLOPs. Against StableSR (200 steps, 47403 GFLOPs, 1063M params), Edge-SD-SR wins 7 out of 10 comparison points, including LPIPS 0.249 vs 0.3114 and MUSIQ 69.58 vs 65.92 on DIV2K, though StableSR edges ahead on FID (24.44 vs 25.37). Against YONOS-SR (1 step, 2042 GFLOPs, 960M params), the closest competitor, Edge-SD-SR trails on most DIV2K metrics (FID 25.37 vs 21.86, LPIPS 0.249 vs 0.2310) but wins on DPED-iphone MUSIQ (60.09 vs 59.45) and uses 14× fewer GFLOPs. Against ResShift (4 steps, 2651 GFLOPs, 173M params), Edge-SD-SR wins 8 out of 10 points, with notable margins on DRealSR LPIPS (0.292 vs 0.3870) and DPED-iphone MUSIQ (60.09 vs 38.59). Against DiffBir (50 steps, 1717M) and SeeSR (50 steps, 2524M), Edge-SD-SR dominates on every metric, e.g. FID 25.37 vs 40.42 and 31.93 respectively on DIV2K.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Stable Diffusion latent diffusion framework [17] and combines three prior efficiency strategies—UNet pruning from SnapFusion [11] and MobileDiffusion [35], and 1-step inference via scale distillation from YONOS-SR [16]—into a unified on-device SR pipeline. Its key conceptual departure is from ResShift [31] and InDI [4], which condition only the mean of the forward noising process on the LR image; Edge-SD-SR introduces bidirectional conditioning that also conditions the noise variance, eliminating the need to concatenate the LR latent into the UNet at every denoising step. The joint encoder-UNet training with a decoupled HR/LR encoder design further distinguishes it from prior SD-SR works that rely on a frozen shared encoder plus an adapter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a diffusion-based SR model can run in real time on a consumer phone (1.1 s for a full 512→2048 upscale on a Samsung S24), opening the door to on-device generative SR for the billions of photos taken daily. The bidirectional conditioning insight—that conditioning both mean and variance on the LR image substantially reduces the capacity needed for SR versus text-to-image generation—may generalize to other image restoration tasks. Open questions include whether the approach extends to higher magnification factors (×8, ×16), whether further activation quantization (W8A8) preserves quality, and whether the 1-step distillation bottleneck limits ceiling quality compared to multi-step baselines like YONOS-SR.
