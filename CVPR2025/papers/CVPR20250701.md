## Problem

Vision-based gait recognition must exclude identity-irrelevant cues such as clothing texture, color, and background from walking videos, yet traditional silhouette- and pose-based methods sacrifice too much structural information, and existing end-to-end RGB denoising approaches still retain substantial texture and color noise. The paper asks whether generative diffusion models can be repurposed as a knowledge-driven denoising front-end, and how to further reduce residual RGB-encoded noise through geometric structure.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB video frames (768×384, 20 frames per sequence) and pre-extracted binary human silhouettes. RGB frames are projected into a 4-channel latent space via a VAE encoder (8× downsampling to 96×48), then passed through a frozen Stable Diffusion UNet at timestep t=700. The output is two 2-channel direction-vector maps (static and dynamic Gait Feature Fields) at 96×48 resolution, where each pixel encodes a 2D orientation; these are fed in parallel into GaitBase with high-level attention fusion to produce an identity embedding.

## Method

DenoisingGait combines two complementary denoising stages. First, knowledge-driven denoising uses a pretrained Stable Diffusion model at an intermediate timestep (t=700) to produce diffusion features that selectively filter out fine RGB texture and color while preserving overall body shape. Second, a geometry-driven Feature Matching module condenses the 4-channel diffusion features at each foreground pixel into a 2D direction vector via an Assignment of Direction (AoD) operation: a query feature is compared by softmax similarity against a local neighborhood of key features, and the resulting distribution is multiplied by a fixed spatial direction template. Within-frame matching (Δl=0) yields a static Gait Feature Field encoding local structural appearance, while cross-frame matching (Δl>0) yields a dynamic Gait Feature Field encoding motion. Background removal via silhouettes and a texture-suppression operation (random zero-padding of high-magnitude static vectors during training) further suppress residual noise, and the two fields are jointly trained with triplet and cross-entropy losses.

## Results

On the primary CCPG benchmark (gait evaluation protocol), DenoisingGait achieves a mean rank-1 accuracy of 89.5%, with per-subset scores of 84.0% (CL), 88.0% (UP), 90.1% (DN), and 95.9% (BG), setting a new state-of-the-art. On CASIA-B*, it reaches 100.0% (NM), 99.9% (BG), and 91.5% (CL). In cross-domain evaluation (trained on CCPG, tested on CASIA-B* and SUSTech1K), DenoisingGait improves over the best prior method (BigGait) by +6.5% on CASIA-B* NM and +2.7% on SUSTech1K overall.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DenoisingGait outperforms the strongest prior method, MultiGait++ (AAAI'25), by +1.9% mean on CCPG (89.5 vs. 87.6), with notable gains of +4.1% on DN and +4.4% on BG, though it trails by 1.0% on UP. Against the RGB-based BigGait (CVPR'24), DenoisingGait wins on all four subsets (+1.4% CL, +2.1% UP, +3.0% DN, +2.8% BG). Over the silhouette-based GaitBase (CVPR'23), improvements are substantial at +14.0% mean (89.5 vs. 75.5). On CASIA-B*, DenoisingGait matches or exceeds all baselines (100.0% NM, 99.9% BG, 91.5% CL), and on SUSTech1K it leads in most conditions except night (NT: 69.5% vs. BigGait's 85.3%), which the authors attribute to low-quality silhouettes. In cross-domain transfer (CCPG→CASIA-B*/SUSTech1K), DenoisingGait surpasses BigGait by +6.5% on CASIA-B* NM and +2.7% on SUSTech1K overall, confirming stronger generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the soft-denoising line of gait recognition (BigGait, GaitEdge) by repurposing a pretrained Latent Diffusion Model (Stable Diffusion 1.5) as a knowledge-driven denoising encoder, selecting a specific timestep to filter gait-irrelevant RGB cues. It then introduces a geometry-driven Feature Matching module that condenses multi-channel diffusion features into two-dimensional direction vectors, drawing conceptual inspiration from SIFT descriptors and optical flow but implemented as a learnable, identity-driven module. The resulting Gait Feature Field (static and dynamic) is fed into the GaitBase backbone, positioning the work as a hybrid of generative-model knowledge and classical geometric representation learning applied to gait.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DenoisingGait is one of the first demonstrations that a generative diffusion model can serve as a useful feature extractor for a discriminative biometric task, opening a path for leveraging large pretrained generative models in gait recognition. The Gait Feature Field offers a compact, interpretable spatiotemporal representation that explicitly suppresses texture and background, addressing a long-standing challenge in RGB gait methods. However, the method still depends on pre-extracted silhouettes (limiting performance in low-light or occluded settings), and the authors acknowledge that the proportion of cross-clothing pairs in training data remains a critical but underexplored factor. Open questions include how to fairly benchmark and compare diverse large-model backbones (diffusion, DINOv2, etc.) under matched compute budgets, and whether the diffusion-timestep denoising strategy generalizes beyond the specific SD 1.5 architecture used here.
