## Problem

CLIP's image tokens suffer from a 'proxy token' phenomenon where attention is drawn to specific background tokens rather than spatially or semantically related regions, causing dense features to lack local discriminability and spatial consistency. While self-distillation improves region-level alignment and VFM distillation improves spatial correlations, applying both simultaneously to the same features creates an optimization conflict that degrades performance. The paper asks whether CLIP's features can be disentangled so that separate guiding constraints can be applied without interference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252330/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a full RGB image as input to the student CLIP model, which is simultaneously divided into k cropped sub-regions fed to the teacher CLIP model, and also processed by a VFM (e.g., DINOv2) at a potentially different resolution to ensure matching token counts after patch embedding. The student outputs two decoupled feature maps: a content map (dimension C, the shared vision-language space) and a context map (dimension D, the CLIP encoder width), both at spatial resolution H×W. These enhanced dense features are then consumed by downstream open-vocabulary detection, segmentation, or region classification heads.

## Method

DeCLIP decouples CLIP's final self-attention block into two components: context features (X_context = Proj_q(X)) that encode spatial/semantic relationships, and content features (X_content = Proj(Attn_context · V)) that carry per-token semantic information in the vision-language space. Content features are refined via self-distillation: RoI Align pools region features from X_content and aligns them (cosine similarity loss) with [CLS] tokens from the teacher CLIP processing the corresponding image crops. Context features are refined by matching pairwise cosine-similarity correlation volumes between image tokens to those of a VFM (DINOv2, SAM, or DINO) using an L2 loss. The total loss is L_content + λ·L_context, and the decoupled design avoids the optimization conflict observed when both objectives are applied to a single undifferentiated feature map.

## Results

On the VLM-feature-based segmentation benchmark (Table 5), DeCLIP achieves the best average mIoU of 41.9 across eight datasets, surpassing CLIP-DINOiser (40.3), SCLIP (38.2), and ClearCLIP (38.1). For open-vocabulary detection on OV-COCO, OV-DQUO+DeCLIP with ViT-L/14 reaches 48.3 AP Novel50, a +2.7 gain over the OV-DQUO baseline (45.6) and +4.0 over CLIPSelf (44.3). On open-vocabulary semantic segmentation, CAT-Seg+DeCLIP (ViT-L/14) attains 17.6 mIoU on ADE847 and 63.9 on Context59, outperforming all prior methods including FC-CLIP (14.8/58.4) and SAN (13.7/60.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeCLIP consistently outperforms all 31 compared baselines across every task. In open-vocabulary detection (OV-COCO), OV-DQUO+DeCLIP (ViT-L/14) achieves 48.3 AP Novel50, beating OV-DQUO (45.6), CLIPSelf (44.3), CORA (41.7), and SAS-Det (37.4); on OV-LVIS it reaches 41.5 mAPr vs. OV-DQUO (39.3) and RO-ViT (34.1). In open-vocabulary semantic segmentation, CAT-Seg+DeCLIP (ViT-L/14) surpasses FC-CLIP (14.8→17.6 on ADE847), SAN (13.7→17.6), MAFT (13.1→17.6), and the original CAT-Seg (16.0→17.6). In VLM-feature-based segmentation, DeCLIP's 41.9 average mIoU exceeds CLIP-DINOiser (40.3) by 1.6 points, SCLIP (38.2) by 3.7, and ClearCLIP (38.1) by 3.8, with the largest margin on Context59 (39.2 vs. 35.9) and COCO-Obj (36.4 vs. 34.8). The transfer evaluation (Table 3) confirms DeCLIP's advantage generalizes: on Objects365 it achieves 20.0 AP vs. CLIPSelf (19.5) and F-VLM (16.2). No baseline is matched or beaten by DeCLIP in any reported setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeCLIP extends the CLIPSelf line of work, which introduced self-distillation to align CLIP's region features with image-crop [CLS] tokens for open-vocabulary dense prediction. The key departure is the observation that CLIP's self-attention produces 'proxy tokens' that degrade spatial consistency, and that naively combining self-distillation with VFM (DINO/SAM) distillation creates an optimization conflict. DeCLIP resolves this by decoupling the final attention block into 'content' features (guided by self-distillation, as in CLIPSelf) and 'context' features (guided by VFM correlation maps), drawing inspiration from training-free OVS methods like SCLIP and ClearCLIP that modify attention for dense inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled fine-tuning strategy offers a practical, plug-and-play way to improve CLIP's dense features without task-specific annotations, making it broadly applicable to detection, segmentation, and region classification pipelines. The 'proxy token' diagnosis provides a concrete mechanistic explanation for why CLIP underperforms in dense prediction, which is valuable for the broader community adapting VLMs to pixel-level tasks. Open questions include whether decoupling at multiple layers (rather than just the last block) yields further gains, whether the approach transfers to non-ViT CLIP variants, and whether the VFM teacher requirement limits deployment in resource-constrained settings.
