## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary 3D visual grounding methods rely on 3D annotations and explicit object labels, limiting their ability to handle diverse semantics and implicit language descriptions that require reasoning. Moreover, they fail to localize objects that are partially or fully occluded from the current viewpoint, a critical limitation for real-world robotics and navigation where scenes are dynamic and incomplete.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252462/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained 3D Gaussian Splatting scene (from RGB-D images with camera poses), an implicit natural-language query (e.g., 'the red, round, sweet fruit partially occluded by the toy sheep'), and a top-down view image for LVLM reasoning. It produces a selected 3D Gaussian group representing the complete target object, an amodal rendering of that object from arbitrary novel views (revealing occluded geometry), and a natural-language explanation of the reasoning chain. SAM ViT-H generates 2D mask candidates and OpenCLIP ViT-B/16 provides language embeddings during both training and inference.

## Method

ReasonGrounder extends 3D Gaussian Splatting by appending a latent feature vector to each Gaussian, which is mapped through two shallow MLPs into hierarchical language features (supervised by PCA-compressed CLIP embeddings via Huber loss) and hierarchical instance features (supervised by a contrastive loss adapted from GARField using SAM mask pairs at matched physical scales). The physical scale of each mask is computed by deprojecting pixels through 3DGS depth, enabling scale-conditioned hierarchical grouping. At inference, LLaVA 1.5 interprets the implicit query against a top-down view to identify the target object and generate an explanation; CLIP cosine similarity then selects the most relevant reference view. HDBSCAN clusters the hierarchical instance features at the target object's scale to form Gaussian groups, and the group whose cluster center best matches the reference pixel's instance feature (selected by maximum relevancy score against canonical phrases) is rendered to achieve amodal perception of the complete object from any novel viewpoint.

## Results

On the LERF dataset with explicit queries, ReasonGrounder achieves 86.7% overall localization accuracy and 55.1% mean IoU, outperforming LangSplat (84.3% / 51.4%) and LERF (73.6% / 37.4%). On the 3D-OVS dataset, ReasonGrounder reaches 94.7% overall mean IoU, surpassing LangSplat (93.4%), the 3D-OVS baseline (86.8%), and LERF (54.8%). For implicit instruction queries, ReasonGrounder achieves 91.4% mean IoU on the proposed ReasoningGD dataset and 93.8% on 3D-OVS, demonstrating strong reasoning capability. In amodal perception of novel views, ReasonGrounder attains 90.4% mean IoU across five ReasoningGD scenes, localizing complete objects even under full occlusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReasonGrounder outperforms all seven baselines across every evaluated dataset and metric. On LERF with explicit queries, it leads LangSplat by 2.4 points in localization accuracy (86.7% vs. 84.3%) and by 3.7 points in mean IoU (55.1% vs. 51.4%), while LERF and LSeg trail by large margins (73.6%/37.4% and 21.1%/16.6% respectively). On 3D-OVS, ReasonGrounder surpasses the closest 3D competitor LangSplat by 1.3 points in overall IoU (94.7% vs. 93.4%) and dominates the 2D baselines OV-Seg (77.5%) and ODISE (43.5%) by 17.2 and 51.2 points. In the implicit-instruction setting (Table 4), ReasonGrounder achieves 55.2% IoU on LERF and 91.4% on ReasoningGD, substantially exceeding LangSplat's 58.9% on challenge scenes (Table 5) where the gap widens to 19.6 points overall. The 3D-OVS baseline [27] and FFD [19] are significantly weaker, confirming that scale-hierarchical feature grouping with LVLM guidance provides a clear advantage over prior 3D language-field approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReasonGrounder extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting-based open-vocabulary grounding paradigm established by LangSplat and LERF, which handle explicit object queries via CLIP feature matching in radiance fields. It departs from this line by introducing a scale-hierarchical feature Gaussian field (building on GARField's contrastive grouping loss) and an LVLM (LLaVA) component for implicit instruction comprehension, shifting the task from 'find the object named X' to 'reason about what the user means and localize it even when occluded.' The hierarchical grouping via HDBSCAN over scale-conditioned instance features is the key architectural novelty, enabling multi-granularity object selection that prior flat-feature methods (LERF, LangSplat) cannot perform.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The combination of implicit instruction understanding and amodal perception addresses a real gap in 3D grounding for robotics and AR, where users rarely issue explicit labels and objects are frequently occluded. The ReasoningGD dataset (10K synthetic scenes, 2M annotations with modal/amodal masks) is a practical resource for evaluating occlusion-robust grounding, though its synthetic origin (BlenderProc) limits direct transferability to real-world generalization claims. Open questions include whether the LVLM component scales to more complex multi-step reasoning, whether HDBSCAN-based grouping handles highly cluttered scenes, and how the method performs on real captured scenes with imperfect depth and lighting beyond the LERF in-the-wild subset.
