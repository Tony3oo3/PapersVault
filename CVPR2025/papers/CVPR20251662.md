## Problem

Existing multi-camera multi-target (MCMT) tracking methods are designed exclusively for daytime conditions with favorable lighting and fail under low-light (nighttime) scenarios due to the loss of detailed visible appearance features. No prior MCMT dataset incorporates both infrared modality and low-light sequences, leaving a fundamental gap for all-day tracking research.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251662/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized RGB and infrared image pairs from two drone-mounted camera views, all cropped and downsampled to 640×512 for alignment. It produces per-target bounding boxes with consistent identity assignments across both temporal (consecutive frames) and spatial (cross-camera) dimensions. The M3Track dataset provides 118K×2 frames across 19 scenes with 1,188K annotated boxes, split into train and test sets where test scenes are novel or contain distinct targets to avoid data leakage.

## Method

ADMCMT fuses RGB and infrared features via an All-Day Mamba Fusion (ADMF) module built on Mamba's VSS blocks, organized into four parallel channels (VI, IR, LI, FU). A Lighting Guidance Model (LGM) predicts a binary lighting category from early visible features and injects lighting-relevant information into the fusion process, adaptively weighting visible vs. infrared contributions at the pixel level. Fused features feed a CenterNet detector, and a Nearby Target Collection (NTC) strategy expands each target's bounding box by 2× to gather one nearby target (area > 0.6× target area), whose 32-dim feature is concatenated with the target's 1024-dim Re-ID embedding to provide cross-view-consistent context. A GMT transformer tracker then performs joint single-camera and cross-camera association. The full network is trained end-to-end with a weighted sum of lighting classification, detection, and association losses (λ_light=0.1, λ_det=2, λ_assi=5).

## Results

On the full M3Track dataset, ADMCMT achieves 70.94 MOTA, 71.47 IDF1, and 56.62 HOTA for single-camera tracking, and 0.6169 MDA, 68.77 CVIDF1, and 61.81 CVMA for multi-camera tracking, outperforming the second-best method (AGW) by 2.73 MOTA, 9.45 IDF1, 13.96 CVIDF1, and 16.79 CVMA. On nighttime scenes specifically, ADMCMT still leads with 69.71 MOTA and 47.15 CVMA, surpassing the next-best tracker by 2.88 MOTA and 11.31 CVMA, demonstrating strong generalization under low-light conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADMCMT outperforms all five baselines on every single-camera and multi-camera metric in both full-dataset and nighttime evaluations. The closest competitor on the full dataset is AGW [43], which ADMCMT surpasses by 3.32 MOTA, 9.45 IDF1, 13.96 CVIDF1, and 16.79 CVMA; the gap widens substantially on nighttime scenes where AGW's MDA drops to 0.2318 versus ADMCMT's 0.4966. CT [37] is the strongest single-camera baseline (68.21 MOTA on All, 66.83 on Night) but trails ADMCMT by 2.73 and 2.88 MOTA respectively, and is far behind on cross-view metrics (44.71 vs. 61.81 CVMA on All). CrossMOT [11] and MvMHAT [9] show the weakest multi-camera association (MDA 0.2019 and 0.2178 on All), while OSNet [49] serves as a Re-ID-only baseline that lacks explicit cross-view association, yielding the lowest CVMA (28.60) among all methods. Notably, the performance gap between ADMCMT and baselines is larger on nighttime scenes than on the full dataset, confirming that the infrared modality and lighting-guided fusion provide the most benefit under low-light conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MCMT tracking line of work (CrossMOT, GMT, MvMHAT, etc.) from its traditional daytime-only setting to all-day operation by introducing infrared modality into the multi-camera framework. The fusion mechanism departs from prior RGBT tracking methods (BAT, CMD) that operate on single-camera single-target tasks, instead adapting Mamba-based state-space fusion (via VMamba's VSS blocks) to a multi-camera multi-target pipeline. The Nearby Target Collection strategy repurposes the 'know your surroundings' idea from single-camera tracking [1] but redirects it from background pixels to neighboring object features, motivated by the observation that surrounding objects are more consistent across camera viewpoints than background. The tracking backbone (GMT) and detector (CenterNet) are reused from prior work, positioning ADMCMT as an integration and extension rather than a fundamental architectural departure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

M3Track is the first RGBT MCMT dataset and fills a clear gap: no prior multi-camera multi-target benchmark included low-light sequences or infrared modality, making all-day MCMT tracking previously unstudied. The practical impact is significant for surveillance, traffic, and urban reconnaissance applications that must operate 24/7. However, several limitations temper the contribution: the lighting guidance is a binary day/night classifier (no intermediate conditions), the dataset uses only 2 cameras from UAV platforms, and the baselines are single-modality methods adapted to consume fused features rather than true multi-modality MCMT competitors. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more than two views, whether the LGM can handle continuous lighting variation, and whether the NTC strategy transfers to ground-level or indoor multi-camera setups.
