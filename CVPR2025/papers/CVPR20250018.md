## Problem

Continual Multi-view Clustering (CMVC) requires clustering data as views arrive sequentially with no access to previous view data, but existing CMVC methods rely on late fusion—training a separate model per view and averaging partition matrices—which is fragile to view-specific noise and fails to adapt to large distributional gaps between views. This paper reframes CMVC as a domain adaptation problem, enabling a single unified model to incrementally adapt to new views while preserving prior knowledge.

## Input / Output

At each session v, the method receives only the current view's unlabeled feature matrix X^v ∈ R^{n×d} (e.g., 32×32 grayscale/RGB images or 7×7 patches) plus three artifacts from the previous session: a similarity matrix S^{v-1}, a soft cluster assignment matrix H^{v-1}, and cluster prototypes B^{v-1}. It outputs an updated student model, an EMA teacher model, a new similarity matrix S^v, cluster assignments H^v, and prototypes B^v. The critical constraint is that no raw data from prior views is ever accessed, making the method suitable for privacy-sensitive or memory-constrained deployments.

## Method

AdaptCMVC uses a VAE base model (with random pixel masking for noise robustness) trained on the first view, then adapts it to subsequent views via a self-training framework where a weight-averaged (EMA) teacher model provides target features for the student to match. A noise-robust consistency loss down-weights samples far from the previous session's K-means prototypes, reducing the influence of noisy or out-of-distribution points on model updates. To combat catastrophic forgetting, a structure alignment loss enforces cosine-similarity consistency between the current view's feature matrix and an EMA-updated similarity matrix initialized from the prior session's cluster assignments, thereby preserving global group structure without re-accessing old data. The entire pipeline is fully unsupervised, requiring only the current view's raw samples at each incremental step.

## Results

On the four primary datasets (E-MNIST, E-FMNIST, Office-31, COIL-100), AdaptCMVC achieves ACC of 67.10, 54.13, 22.81, and 57.12 respectively, outperforming the best CMVC baselines (CMVC, CAC) by 6.6–20.7 ACC points. On PatchedMNIST (6 views), AdaptCMVC reaches 84.33 ACC / 55.87 NMI, surpassing even the traditional MVC method GCFAgg (83.20 ACC) that has access to all views simultaneously. The only dataset where AdaptCMVC underperforms is COIL-20, where it trails CAC by 4.18 ACC. Averaged across all six datasets, AdaptCMVC achieves 58.30 ACC and 56.23 NMI, ranking 2.3rd and 3.5th respectively among all 10 methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two CMVC baselines (CMVC [42] and CAC [58]), AdaptCMVC wins on 5 of 6 datasets, with the largest margin on COIL-100 (+20.71 ACC over CMVC) and PatchedMNIST (+13.30 ACC over CMVC); it loses only on COIL-20 (64.32 vs. 68.50 ACC for CAC). Compared to traditional MVC methods that assume simultaneous access to all views, AdaptCMVC outperforms GCFAgg on PatchedMNIST (84.33 vs. 83.20 ACC) and E-MNIST (67.10 vs. 67.10, tied), and beats Multi-VAE on E-MNIST, E-FMNIST, and Office-31. Single-view baselines (Joint-VAE, β-VAE) are consistently outperformed by 10–30+ ACC points across all datasets. Among deep MVC methods, AdaptCMVC surpasses MFLVC on E-FMNIST, Office-31, and COIL-100, and CONAN on all four primary datasets, though MFLVC retains an edge on E-MNIST NMI (59.08 vs. 54.14). The average-rank analysis (Table 3) confirms AdaptCMVC as the top-ranked method overall (rank 2.3 on ACC, 3.5 on NMI), ahead of all other baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repositions Continual Multi-view Clustering (CMVC) from the domain adaptation perspective, departing from the prevailing late-fusion paradigm where existing CMVC methods (CMVC [42], CAC [58]) train a separate model per view and fuse partition matrices via moving averages. Conceptually, it repurposes the teacher-student self-training framework from self-supervised learning (BYOL, MoCo) and continual domain adaptation [47] into an unsupervised clustering setting, introducing a noise-robust consistency loss weighted by distance to K-means prototypes and a structure alignment mechanism that maintains a global similarity matrix across sessions. The VAE backbone is standard, but the adaptation strategy—using a single evolving model rather than per-view models—is the key architectural departure from prior CMVC work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CMVC addresses a practically important constraint (privacy-sensitive or resource-limited settings where views arrive sequentially and past data is inaccessible), and the domain adaptation framing offers a cleaner, more scalable alternative to the late-fusion consensus-matrix approach. The finding that a sequentially-adapted model can match or exceed traditional MVC methods with full view access on several datasets is encouraging for real-world deployment. However, the method underperforms on COIL-20, relies on K-means at each session boundary, and is evaluated only on relatively small image datasets, leaving open questions about scalability to very long view sequences, non-image modalities, and theoretical guarantees on forgetting bounds.
