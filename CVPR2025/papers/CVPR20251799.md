## Problem

LiDAR surface normal estimation remains severely underexplored due to the absence of large-scale annotated datasets and the lack of methods that can robustly handle the sparsity, non-uniformity, and noise inherent to LiDAR point clouds at reasonable runtime. Existing approaches are designed for small-scale, dense CAD datasets (e.g., PCPNet) and rely on patch-based inference, making them ill-suited for full-frame autonomous driving LiDAR scans.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a full LiDAR point cloud (N×3 coordinates, ~100k points per frame from a 64-beam sensor) in a single forward pass, producing per-point surface normal vectors (N×3). For the temporal regularization term, it additionally requires pairs of consecutive frames with their 4×4 global pose matrices (from GPS+IMU alignment to a common key frame) and random affine augmentation matrices. Unlike patch-based baselines that need >14 seconds per frame, the full-cloud approach processes each frame in ~90 ms.

## Method

The backbone is Point Transformer V3 (PTv3), a four-stage encoder-decoder, trained with an L1 loss plus three data-term regularizers weighted by γ=0.1: (1) Spatial GTV, which builds a k-neighborhood graph with Gaussian-distance-weighted edges and penalizes L1 disagreement between neighboring normal predictions to enforce piecewise smoothness; (2) Temporal GTV, which constructs a bipartite graph between consecutive frames (transformed to a common key frame) and penalizes normal inconsistency across time to exploit the high overlap between 10 Hz LiDAR scans; (3) Eikonal loss, which enforces unit-norm on predicted normals. The key design choice is using GTV as a regularizer rather than the primary objective, allowing it to correct L1 loss's tendency to produce orientation ambiguities and frame-to-frame flicker. For synthetic-to-real domain adaptation, the same regularizers are applied during self-training on unlabeled Waymo data, where they suppress gradient noise from imperfect pseudo-labels and enable robust transfer without manual annotation.

## Results

On the LiSu test split, the proposed method achieves a mean angular error of 6.30°, median of 0.43°, and RMSE of 17.58°, with 81.10% of predictions within 5.0° of ground truth. It outperforms the best supervised baseline SHS-Net (6.46° mean, 0.67° median, 18.40° RMSE, 79.66% at 5.0°) while processing each frame in 0.09 s versus 24.27 s for SHS-Net—a ~270× speedup. On the downstream neural surface reconstruction task (Waymo), integrating the self-trained normal estimator into ReSimAD reduces average RMSE from 2.01 to 1.97 and Chamfer Distance from 0.41 to 0.37.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all nine surface-normal-estimation baselines on the LiSu test set. Against the closest competitor SHS-Net [28], it reduces mean angular error by 0.16° (6.30° vs 6.46°), median error by 0.24° (0.43° vs 0.67°), and improves 5.0° accuracy by 1.44 percentage points (81.10% vs 79.66%), while running ~270× faster (0.09 s vs 24.27 s per frame). The gap widens substantially against other supervised methods: PCPNet [18], NGL [27], and CMG-Net [55] all exceed 9.8° mean error, and GraphFit [25], Du et al. [12], and NeuralGF [30] range from 8.57° to 8.98° mean. Traditional geometric methods (PCA [20], Jet [7]) are far inferior, with mean errors of 76.18° and 39.02° respectively, confirming their unsuitability for sparse, noisy LiDAR data. On the downstream surface reconstruction task (Table 4), the full method with spatiotemporal regularization (L) achieves the best average RMSE (1.97) and Chamfer Distance (0.37) across eight Waymo sequences, outperforming ReSimAD [62] without normals (2.01/0.41), direct transfer from LiSu (2.01/0.40), and self-training with L1 only (2.00/0.40).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the point cloud surface normal estimation lineage (PCPNet, SHS-Net, GraphFit, NeuralGF) from small-scale, dense CAD datasets to the sparse, non-uniform, and noisy regime of LiDAR scans in autonomous driving. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) it repurposes Graph Total Variation regularization—previously used as a primary objective in point cloud super-resolution—as a lightweight data-term regularizer within a single-step PTv3 architecture, and (2) it introduces temporal GTV across consecutive LiDAR frames, exploiting the sequential structure of driving data that prior per-patch methods ignore. The self-training component aligns with the broader UDA-for-LiDAR literature (ReSimAD, ST3D++, GIPSO) but adds the spatiotemporal regularizers specifically to combat pseudo-label noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LiSu fills a critical gap: no publicly available dataset with LiDAR surface normal annotations existed prior to this work, making the task essentially unbenchmarked. The 50k-frame synthetic dataset with ground-truth normals, combined with a 150× speedup over patch-based SOTA methods, provides both the infrastructure and the efficiency needed for the field to progress. A key open question is how well these results transfer to real-world evaluation once annotated LiDAR normal datasets become available; the current real-world assessment is indirect (via surface reconstruction quality). The paper also leaves open whether the spatiotemporal regularization strategy generalizes beyond autonomous driving to other sequential LiDAR applications (e.g., robotics, SLAM).
