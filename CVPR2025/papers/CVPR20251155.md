## Problem

Collecting action-labeled robot demonstrations is expensive and bottlenecked by human effort, while abundant video data remains underutilized for policy learning. Existing video pre-training methods (e.g., UniPi, ATM) model representations directly in pixel space, neglecting the internal structures of objects, spatial relationships between objects, and object-effector interactions that are critical for manipulation. This paper asks whether video pre-training can be reformulated as graph-to-graphs generative modeling to capture these structural relationships and enable robust policy learning from limited action-labeled data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB video frames (segmented via Grounded SAM into task-related object masks with uniform vertex sampling, plus fingertip positions as visual action vertices), a language instruction encoded by BERT, and robot proprioception. It produces two outputs: a sequence of future graphs (vertex spatial positions over F_G steps, with edges re-derived from a distance threshold) used as pre-training targets, and a robot action sequence of length F_a predicted by the policy. The graph construction is embodiment-agnostic—object vertices come from segmentation masks and action vertices from key interaction points (fingertips for human videos), enabling cross-embodiment transfer.

## Method

GraphMimic constructs action-informed transferable graphs per video frame, where object vertices (sampled within segmented masks) and visual action vertices (fingertip positions) are connected by edges within a spatial distance threshold d. A property-aware module infers object properties (e.g., stiffness) from H historical frames via a GNN over object-centric subgraphs followed by an LSTM, encoding long-term temporal dependencies. A hierarchical architecture is then built by K-means clustering vertices into spatial groups, adding a global root vertex per cluster, and constructing directed leaf-to-root, root-to-leaf, and root-to-root edges to enable long-range message propagation alongside local structure. A transformer-based generator, conditioned on the hierarchical graph embedding, ResNet image embedding, and BERT language embedding, predicts future vertex positions via an MLP head. The resulting future graphs condition a transformer-based policy (with modality-specific encoders and a CLS-token state representation) that maps to robot actions, trained with MSE loss on few action-labeled demonstrations after self-supervised pre-training on action-free videos.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark (130 language-conditioned manipulation tasks), GraphMimic with 10 action-labeled demonstrations achieves 0.88, 0.89, 0.87, and 0.56 success rates on Spatial, Object, Goal, and Long suites respectively, yielding a 0.67 average on Libero-90. Remarkably, with only 2 demonstrations (20% of the data used by baselines), GraphMimic still achieves 0.52 on Libero-90, surpassing the strongest baseline ATM (0.48) that uses 5× more labeled data. In real-world experiments across 12 tasks, GraphMimic reaches 0.76 overall success rate versus 0.53 for ATM, a >23% absolute improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GraphMimic outperforms all four baselines across every LIBERO suite. Against the strongest baseline ATM (trajectory generation), GraphMimic improves Libero-90 from 0.48 to 0.67 with 10 demos (+19% absolute) and still leads (0.52 vs. 0.48) when using only 2 demos. UniPi (video diffusion) collapses on long-horizon and goal tasks (0.05 and 0.11 on Long/Goal), while R3M (contrastive visual pre-training) is particularly weak on Goal (0.05) and Long (0.09), suggesting pixel-space representations fail to capture transition dynamics. Vanilla BC with 10 demos (0.29 on Libero-90) is far below GraphMimic, confirming the value of video pre-training. In cross-embodiment robot-to-robot transfer, the gap widens dramatically: GraphMimic reaches 0.60 on Libero-90 while ATM drops to 0.28 and UniPi to 0.01, a >33% absolute improvement attributed to the embodiment-agnostic graph representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

GraphMimic carves new ground at the intersection of two established lines of work: video pre-training for robot manipulation (R3M, UniPi, ATM) and graph-based dynamics modeling (particle GNNs, DefGraphNets, GraphIRL). While prior video pre-training methods operate in pixel or point-track space, and prior graph methods focus on dynamics prediction or reward learning, this paper repurposes graph representations as the medium for video-to-policy transfer, formulating pre-training as graph-to-graphs generative modeling. The visual action vertex abstraction (fingertips as embodiment-agnostic interaction points) departs from both pixel-level video generation and embodiment-specific end-effector tracking, drawing on hand-object interaction literature to create a transferable action representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in reducing action-labeled data requirements to 20% while outperforming full-data baselines, and in enabling cross-embodiment transfer (human-to-robot, robot-to-robot) with over 33% improvement over prior methods. The key limitation is reliance on current tracking models (CoTracker), which struggle with low-texture objects during large motions, capping training data quality. Open questions include whether the graph formulation scales to multi-object scenes with many interacting bodies, whether 3D graph representations would further improve spatial reasoning, and whether the hierarchical architecture generalizes to tasks requiring long-horizon planning beyond the short prediction horizon tested here.
