## Problem

Existing automatic graphic design methods either address isolated subtasks (content-aware layout, typography) or treat all elements as a flat set, ignoring the hierarchical layered structure that human designers naturally follow. No prior work incorporates the layered design principle—progressively building from background through underlay, image, text, and embellishment layers—into the generation process, leaving a gap in achieving holistic, cohesive design composition from a given set of multimodal elements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250260/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of multimodal graphic elements (pixel images for visual elements and string content for text elements) along with the target canvas size as input. It outputs structured element attributes serialized as JSON: four bounding-box parameters (left, top, width, height) for image elements and eight additional typographic attributes (angle, font, font size, color, text alignment, capitalization, letter spacing, line height) for text elements. These attributes are then passed to an off-the-shelf renderer to produce the final graphic design, and the layer-wise generation also enables flexible subtask handling (e.g., generating only text attributes when visual layers are given).

## Method

LaDeCo decomposes design composition into two stages: a layer planning module that uses GPT-4o to assign each input element to one of five semantic layers (background, underlay, logo/image, text, embellishment) based on its content, and a layered design composition process that fine-tunes an LMM (Llama-3.1-8B backbone with CLIP ViT-L/14 vision encoder and a two-layer MLP projector) to predict element attributes sequentially layer by layer. The key mechanism is that after each layer's attributes are generated, the intermediate design is rendered into an image and fed back into the LMM as visual context for predicting the next layer, enabling the model to condition subsequent layers on what has already been placed. Training uses supervised fine-tuning with LoRA (rank 32) on the Crello dataset, minimizing the negative log-likelihood of the JSON-serialized attribute sequence across all five layers. This layer-wise, context-conditioned generation contrasts with flat single-pass prediction (as in FlexDM) and allows the model to handle subtasks like content-aware layout or typography generation without task-specific retraining.

## Results

On the Crello-v4 test set, LaDeCo achieves LLaVA-OV scores of 8.08 (design/layout), 7.92 (content relevance), 8.00 (typography/color), 7.82 (graphics/images), and 6.98 (innovation), closely matching ground truth values (8.35, 8.21, 8.30, 8.01, 7.26) and substantially outperforming FlexDM (5.34–4.54) and GPT-4o (6.53–5.69). On geometry metrics, LaDeCo's overlap score (0.0865) and underlay effectiveness (Und_l: 0.6922, Und_s: 0.6580) are closest to GT (0.0768, 0.6848, 0.6732), while FlexDM suffers severe overlap (0.3242) and GPT-4o has poor underlay usage (0.3780). On the content-aware layout generation subtask, LaDeCo also outperforms specialized baselines PosterLLaVA and PosterLlama on most metrics including canvas utility (0.4414 vs. 0.4199/0.4115) and text readability (0.0768 vs. 0.0747/0.0694).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaDeCo decisively outperforms FlexDM on all five LLaVA-OV quality dimensions (e.g., 8.08 vs. 5.34 on design/layout), with FlexDM exhibiting a severe overlap problem (Ove 0.3242 vs. GT 0.0768) and composition failures in qualitative results. Against GPT-4o, LaDeCo leads on every LLaVA-OV score (8.08 vs. 6.53 on design/layout) and on underlay effectiveness (Und_l 0.6922 vs. 0.3780), though GPT-4o achieves a slightly lower overlap score (0.0595) at the cost of poor underlay usage. On the content-aware layout generation subtask (Table 3), LaDeCo surpasses both PosterLLaVA and PosterLlama on canvas utility (0.4414 vs. 0.4199/0.4115), text readability (0.0768 vs. 0.0747/0.0694), and element validity (0.9340 vs. 0.9269/0.8701), without any task-specific training. For typography generation, LaDeCo produces more readable and aesthetically balanced text layouts compared to FlexDM and OpenCOLE, which suffer from text overlap and poor readability in qualitative comparisons. Overall, LaDeCo is the only method whose geometry metrics (Val, Ove, Ali, Und_l, Und_s) are consistently closest to ground truth across all evaluated subtasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the flat, simultaneous attribute-prediction paradigm of FlexDM (the only prior holistic design composition method) by introducing the layered design principle into Large Multimodal Models. Rather than building on a specific prior architecture, LaDeCo repurposes a standard LMM stack (Llama-3.1-8B + CLIP ViT-L/14 + LoRA) with a novel layer-by-layer generation strategy where intermediate rendered designs are fed back as context, drawing conceptual inspiration from chain-of-thought reasoning. It also subsumes prior subtask-specific methods (content-aware layout generation via PosterLLaVA/PosterLlama, typography generation via OpenCOLE) as special cases of its layered pipeline, positioning itself as a unifying framework rather than a point improvement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LaDeCo demonstrates that a single general-purpose LMM, guided by a simple layer-planning heuristic, can match or exceed specialized models on multiple design subtasks without any task-specific training, suggesting that compositional decomposition may be a more effective inductive bias than task-specific architectures for design generation. The reliance on GPT-4o for layer planning introduces a practical dependency and cost concern, and the fixed 5-layer structure limits flexibility for designs with non-standard hierarchies. Open questions include whether the layered approach scales to more complex or user-specified layer structures, how it integrates with generative models for element content creation, and whether the gains persist with weaker or fully open-source layer planners.
