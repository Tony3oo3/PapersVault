## Problem

Current text-to-video diffusion models produce videos suffering from two prevalent quality issues: structurally implausible and temporally inconsistent artifacts, and near-static motion that makes generated videos resemble still images. Prior remedies focus on training-side improvements (data scaling, architecture changes) that incur substantial cost, leaving a gap for inference-time, training-free interventions that can unlock the latent potential of existing pretrained T2V backbones without additional parameters or sampling overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251181/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

For T2V, the method consumes a text prompt and operates on standard pretrained backbones (AnimateDiff at 512×512, VideoCrafter2 at 320×512) to produce a generated video; for I2V, it additionally takes a reference image (via SparseCtrl). ByTheWay is applied only during the first 20% of DDIM denoising steps and modifies temporal attention maps in-place within the decoder's upsampling blocks, introducing no extra parameters, memory, or sampling iterations. The output is a video with improved structural coherence and amplified motion magnitude relative to the vanilla backbone.

## Method

ByTheWay comprises two plug-and-play components that operate on temporal attention maps in the decoder's upsampling blocks. Temporal Self-Guidance injects the attention map from up_blocks.1 into subsequent blocks (up_blocks.2, 3) via a linear interpolation with guidance ratio α, reducing inter-block disparity that the authors correlate with structural artifacts. Fourier-based Motion Enhancement applies a 1D FFT along the softmax axis of each temporal attention map, isolates high-frequency components within a window of size τ around the Nyquist frequency, scales them by a factor β, and reconstructs via iFFT; two theorems guarantee the softmax normalization is preserved and that energy increases monotonically with β > 1. The scaling factor β is dynamically computed from the energies before and after Temporal Self-Guidance (Eq. 7) to ensure the final attention map carries strictly more energy than the original, thereby amplifying motion without introducing flickering.

## Results

On VBench, ByTheWay improves AnimateDiff's Dynamic Degree from 0.4073 to 0.5245 (+28.8%) and Subject Consistency from 0.9318 to 0.9744, while VideoCrafter2's Dynamic Degree rises from 0.4086 to 0.5547 (+35.8%) and Subject Consistency from 0.9732 to 0.9852. In the user study, ByTheWay-enhanced AnimateDiff receives 74.58% of votes for overall video quality (vs. 25.42% for vanilla), and VideoCrafter2+ByTheWay wins 69.46% of votes. All six VBench metrics improve simultaneously for both backbones, with the largest relative gains in Dynamic Degree and Motion Smoothness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ByTheWay outperforms the vanilla AnimateDiff backbone on all six VBench metrics, with the most dramatic gain in Dynamic Degree (0.5245 vs. 0.4073, +28.8%) and Subject Consistency (0.9744 vs. 0.9318, +4.6%). Against vanilla VideoCrafter2, ByTheWay similarly wins across the board, boosting Dynamic Degree from 0.4086 to 0.5547 (+35.8%) and Background Consistency from 0.9691 to 0.9896. Compared to FreeInit (the only other training-free baseline evaluated quantitatively), ByTheWay achieves substantially higher Dynamic Degree (0.5245 vs. 0.2941 for AnimateDiff), confirming that FreeInit's iterative low-frequency refinement suppresses motion while ByTheWay's Fourier-based high-frequency amplification preserves and enhances it. In the user study, ByTheWay-enhanced models receive 74.58% (AnimateDiff) and 69.46% (VideoCrafter2) of votes for overall video quality, far exceeding the vanilla baselines at 25.42% and 30.54% respectively. The paper also notes that FreeInit requires 5× sampling time due to iterative refinement, whereas ByTheWay adds negligible inference cost since it only modifies attention maps during the first 20% of denoising steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the growing family of training-free diffusion feature control methods (Prompt2Prompt, FreeControl, FreeU, MotionClone) but shifts the focus from spatial or cross-attention maps to the temporal attention modules that are specific to video generation. It departs from prior video-specific training-free approaches like FreeInit (which iteratively refines low-frequency latents at high inference cost) and UniCtrl (which shares first-frame keys/values but reduces motion) by directly manipulating the temporal attention maps themselves—using inter-block alignment for consistency and FFT-based high-frequency scaling for motion. The conceptual lineage draws on signal processing (FFT decomposition) applied to attention maps, a repurposing that is novel in the T2V context but standard in other domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ByTheWay offers a genuinely zero-cost (no extra parameters, memory, or sampling steps) plug-and-play module that addresses two of the most common T2V failure modes—structural artifacts and near-static output—simultaneously, which is practically valuable for practitioners deploying existing open-source backbones. However, the evaluation is limited to two backbones (AnimateDiff, VideoCrafter2) with backbone-specific hyperparameters (α, β, τ), raising open questions about generalizability to DiT-based architectures, longer video sequences, and more complex multi-object motion. The method's reliance on the first 20% of denoising steps and its sensitivity to parameter tuning per model suggest that a more adaptive or architecture-agnostic formulation would be needed for broader adoption.
