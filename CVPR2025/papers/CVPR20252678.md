## Problem

Prior semantic future prediction methods rely on convolutional or recurrent architectures and handle only a single modality at a time, while generative RGB-based approaches are computationally expensive and produce semantically inconsistent futures. No prior work employs a transformer architecture for joint multimodal semantic future prediction, and the VAE-based tokenization strategies common in video generation transformers are impractical for multimodal settings due to the need for separate, hard-to-stabilize VAEs per modality.

## Input / Output

The model consumes N_c=4 past frames of semantic segmentation maps (19 classes) and depth maps at 256×512 resolution, each tokenized into 512 patch tokens per frame (patch size 16). It predicts one future frame at a time in a frame-wise autoregressive manner, producing per-pixel class probabilities for segmentation and discrete depth values. Notably, training supervision comes from pseudo-labels generated by off-the-shelf foundation models (Segmenter for segmentation, DepthAnythingV2 for depth) rather than manual annotations, and evaluation uses the same models as an Oracle upper bound.

## Method

FUTURIST is a unified multimodal visual sequence transformer that replaces VAE-based tokenization with a two-stage hierarchical embedder: a low-dimensional per-pixel embedding (C=10) followed by a linear projection over 16×16 patches, all trained end-to-end. Per-modality token embeddings are fused via concatenation along the embedding dimension (early fusion), keeping the token sequence length at N·L rather than K·N·L to avoid quadratic attention blowup. The backbone uses decomposed spatio-temporal attention (separate temporal and spatial MSA layers) with Pre-LN, and a novel multimodal masking strategy (Partially-shared + Exclusive) ensures each future-frame token is visible in at most one modality during training, forcing the model to learn cross-modal synergies. The decoder mirrors the embedder with weight tying, and the entire pipeline is trained with a per-modality cross-entropy masked modeling loss weighted across modalities.

## Results

On Cityscapes validation, FUTURIST achieves 73.9 mIoU (ALL) and 74.9 mIoU (MO) for short-term (3-frame) future semantic segmentation, and 62.7 mIoU (ALL) and 61.2 mIoU (MO) for mid-term (9-frame) prediction, setting a new state-of-the-art. For depth prediction, the multimodal model reaches 95.8 δ₁ and 5.606 AbsRel (×100) short-term, and 91.5 δ₁ and 9.490 AbsRel (×100) mid-term. The multimodal model (segmentation + depth) consistently outperforms single-modality variants by 0.4–1.3 mIoU points on segmentation and 0.4–0.9 δ₁ on depth, confirming cross-modal synergy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FUTURIST outperforms the previous state-of-the-art PFA [40] by 2.8 mIoU (ALL) and 5.7 mIoU (MO) on short-term, and by 2.4 mIoU (ALL) and 4.5 mIoU (MO) on mid-term segmentation. It also surpasses F2MF [62,63] by 4.3 mIoU (ALL) short-term and 4.8 mIoU (ALL) mid-term. Against the generative world model VISTA [18] (fine-tuned on Cityscapes, 2.5B parameters), FUTURIST achieves 27.6 mIoU (ALL) higher on short-term (73.9 vs. 46.3), as VISTA's generated RGB frames suffer from object disappearance and artifacts that degrade downstream segmentation. FUTURIST remains below the Oracle upper bound (Segmenter applied directly to the target frame) by 4.7 mIoU (ALL) short-term, and well above the Copy-Last lower bound by 18.4 mIoU (ALL) short-term. The multimodal FUTURIST (segmentation + depth) edges out the segmentation-only variant by 0.5 mIoU (ALL) short-term and 0.3 mIoU (ALL) mid-term, demonstrating measurable cross-modal synergy.

## Relation to Prior Work

FUTURIST extends the masked visual modeling paradigm from MaskViT (Gupta et al., ICLR 2023) and the decomposed spatio-temporal attention of ViViT (Arnab et al., CVPR 2021) to the domain of multimodal semantic future prediction, a task previously addressed primarily by CNN/LSTM architectures (F2F, PFA, F2MF) or flow-based warping methods. It departs from the dominant generative-video line (VISTA, MAGVIT, VideoPoet) by predicting semantic modalities directly rather than reconstructing RGB frames, and replaces VAE-based tokenization with a lightweight hierarchical per-pixel-to-patch embedding that enables end-to-end training. The use of pseudo-labels from foundation models (Segmenter, DepthAnythingV2) in place of ground-truth annotations is a practical departure from prior future-prediction work that relied on manual annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a unified transformer can jointly predict multiple semantic modalities with synergistic gains over single-modality models, and that this is achievable without expensive VAE tokenizers or ground-truth depth/segmentation labels—lowering the barrier to multimodal future prediction. The striking finding that a 2.5B-parameter fine-tuned diffusion world model (VISTA) underperforms a trivial Copy-Last baseline on semantic metrics challenges the assumption that photorealistic RGB generation is a useful intermediate for downstream semantic tasks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more modalities (optical flow, instance/panoptic segmentation), longer prediction horizons beyond 48 frames, and whether the pseudo-label ceiling limits ultimate accuracy compared to true ground-truth supervision.
