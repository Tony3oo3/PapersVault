## Problem

In GAN-based perceptual super-resolution, the standard pixel-level L_p loss (L_pix) causes blurring because it simultaneously penalizes both fidelity bias errors and perceptual variance (fine textures). Prior work attempted to mitigate this with small scale factors or low-pass filters, but these approaches misinterpret the blurring mechanism: they either indiscriminately weaken useful fidelity guidance or remove regressable high-frequency components that do not contribute to blurring. The paper shows that L_pix decomposes into a systematic-effect (SE) term and a variance-effect (VE) term, and only VE induces the perception-distortion trade-off, yet no prior method cleanly separates these two factors.

## Input / Output

The SR network takes a low-resolution RGB image and produces a high-resolution RGB image at a given scale factor (primarily ×4). The Auto-Encoder is pretrained on DF2K (DIV2K + Flickr2K) pairs, with its bottleneck dimensionality matching the LR image size, so the encoder maps HR→LR dimensions and the decoder maps back. The AE space used for loss computation is the decoder output (full HR resolution), not the bottleneck, meaning the loss compares auto-encoded HR and SR images at full resolution.

## Method

The core contribution is L_AESOP, a reconstruction loss computed in the output space of a pretrained Auto-Encoder rather than in raw pixel space. The AE is pretrained with L_pix to learn a forward mapping (HR→LR) and inverse mapping (LR→HR), effectively acting as a differentiable approximation of the fidelity bias estimator ψ(·) = argmin_μ E[L(·,μ)]. Because L_pix training causes the AE to discard perceptual variance (random fine textures), the AE output space retains only fidelity bias factors. During SR training, L_p is computed between ψ_AE(I^HR) and ψ_AE(I^SR), which approximates minimizing only the SE term while preserving VE. The AE uses RRDBNet as its decoder (initialized with fidelity-oriented SR weights) and a lightweight CNN encoder, and is frozen during SR network training to prevent collapse. This allows the reconstruction loss coefficient to be set to 1 (vs. the typical 0.01 for L_pix), providing much stronger fidelity guidance without inducing blurring.

## Results

On the RRDB backbone (128×128 patches), AESOP simultaneously improves both fidelity and perceptual metrics over all baselines: on Set14, LPIPS drops from 0.1132 (LDL) to 0.1067 while PSNR rises from 27.228 to 27.361, and on Urban100, LPIPS drops from 0.1084 to 0.1064 while PSNR rises from 25.459 to 25.630. On the SwinIR backbone at 200K iterations (AESOP*), both fidelity and perception improve over LDL: PSNR increases from 27.526 to 27.822 and LPIPS decreases from 0.1091 to 0.1023 on Set14. In real-world settings (RealSRv3, DRealSR), AESOP reduces NIQE from 4.679 to 4.234 and increases MANIQA from 0.366 to 0.414 over Real-ESRGAN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AESOP outperforms all three RRDB-based baselines (ESRGAN, SPSR, LDL) on every metric across all seven benchmark datasets, improving both perceptual (LPIPS, DISTS) and fidelity (PSNR, SSIM) scores simultaneously—a notable departure from the typical perception-distortion trade-off. Against the strongest baseline LDL, AESOP reduces LPIPS by ~0.006–0.007 (e.g., 0.1067 vs 0.1132 on Set14) while increasing PSNR by ~0.13 dB. On the SwinIR backbone, AESOP at 200K iterations (AESOP*) surpasses LDL on both PSNR (27.822 vs 27.526) and LPIPS (0.1023 vs 0.1091) on Set14; at full 300K training, perception scores improve further at the expected cost of some fidelity, as confirmed by the PD trade-off curves. In real-world blind SR (Table 2), AESOP consistently beats Real-ESRGAN on both no-reference metrics (NIQE and MANIQA) across RealSRv3 and DRealSR datasets, with NIQE improving by ~0.44–0.52 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the SRGAN-based perceptual SR framework (Ledig et al., 2017) and uses LDL (Liang et al., 2022) as its baseline, targeting specifically the pixel-level L_p reconstruction loss that all prior methods in this family rely on. It departs from prior circumventions—small scaling coefficients (ESRGAN, SPSR) and low-pass filtering (Deng et al., Zhang et al.)—by arguing these misdiagnose the blurring problem: L_pix jointly minimizes both a fidelity bias term (SE) and a perceptual variance term (VE), and only VE causes the perception-distortion trade-off. The key conceptual move is using a pretrained AutoEncoder as a differentiable approximation of the fidelity-bias operator, creating a feature space where L_p measures only SE, thereby decoupling reconstruction guidance from the variance-penalizing effect that prior methods could not separate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, drop-in replacement for the ad-hoc scaled L_pix that has been the de facto fidelity loss in perceptual SR for years, enabling practitioners to use a full-strength reconstruction loss (λ=1 vs. the typical 0.01) without sacrificing perceptual quality. The spectral analysis clarifying that LPF removes regressable high-frequency components (edges, boundaries) that the AE preserves offers a concrete explanation for why frequency-based fixes underperform. Limitations include scope restricted to GAN-based SR (diffusion-based methods are excluded), dependence on a well-pretrained AE whose quality bounds the method, and the approach's reliance on the specific LR→HR conditioning structure of SR. Open questions include whether the SE/VE decomposition principle generalizes to other ill-posed inverse problems (deblurring, denoising) and whether the AE architecture can be further simplified or replaced by lighter alternatives.
