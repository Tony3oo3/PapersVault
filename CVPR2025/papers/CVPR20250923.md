## Problem

Existing Arena-based evaluation platforms for generative models (e.g., Chatbot Arena) rely on pairwise comparisons with random matchmaking and numerical score modeling, which together require an excessive number of human votes to converge and are highly sensitive to preference noise. This inefficiency is especially problematic for visual generation, where new models proliferate rapidly and the leaderboard must be updated frequently with minimal additional votes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250923/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The platform accepts a text prompt (either randomly sampled from a pool of 5,000 prompts drawn from MS COCO and WebVid, or user-authored) and generates K=4 anonymous outputs from different visual generation models. Users cast votes in one of two modes: Best Mode (select the single best output, equivalent to K−1 pairwise comparisons per click) or Rank Mode (fully order all K outputs, equivalent to K(K−1)/2 pairwise comparisons per K clicks). The system outputs a per-model conservative score S = μ − 3.0·σ and a full leaderboard, updated in real time as new votes arrive.

## Method

K-Sort Arena replaces pairwise comparisons with K-wise free-for-all battles (default K=4), exploiting the perceptual intuitiveness of images and videos to extract C(K,2) pairwise signals per vote. Model capabilities are represented as Gaussian distributions θ_i ~ N(μ_i, σ_i²) with an additional noise term β to absorb preference noise, and are updated via closed-form Bayesian posterior updates using the inverse Mills ratio V(x)=φ(x)/Φ(x) after each K-wise comparison. For matchmaking, the paper formulates opponent selection as a multi-armed bandit problem solved with UCB: the score U(X_i, X_q) = |S_i − S_q| + α·√(ln n / n_iq) balances exploitation (pairing similar-strength models for informative comparisons) with exploration (giving under-evaluated models more matches). The pivot model for each round is selected as the one with the fewest total comparisons, ensuring balanced participation and enabling new models to enter the leaderboard with only ~30 comparisons. Together these components yield 16.3× faster convergence than the Elo system while remaining robust to 5% preference noise.

## Results

K-Sort Arena achieves 16.3× faster ranking convergence than the Elo system, requiring only 716 comparisons versus 11,692 to reach zero MSE on 50 models (Table 1). In ablation experiments, probabilistic modeling converges after ~1,500 comparisons while numerical (Elo) modeling oscillates and fails to converge even after 3,000; with 5% preference noise, probabilistic modeling still converges by ~2,000 comparisons. K=4 free-for-all comparisons yield approximately 2× faster convergence than K=2 pairwise comparisons, and the UCB matchmaking strategy outperforms both random and skill-based matching. When adding a new model to an existing 50-model ranking, the balance-guided pivot specification requires only ~30 comparisons to determine the new model's position.

**Table 1: Number of comparisons required for Elo system and K-Sort Arena to reach convergence (MSE = 0) on 50 models.**

| Method | K | Modeling | Matchmaking | Comparisons |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Elo System | 2 | Numerical | Random | 11,692 |
| K-Sort Arena | 4 | Probabilistic | UCB | **716 (↓16.3×)** |

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Elo system (citation 12), K-Sort Arena wins decisively on convergence speed: 716 comparisons vs. 11,692 (16.3× fewer), and on robustness: probabilistic modeling converges stably while numerical Elo modeling exhibits violent oscillation and never reaches zero MSE even after 3,000 comparisons, both with and without 5% preference noise. Against TrueSkill (citation 17), the proposed UCB matchmaking strategy achieves faster and more stable convergence than TrueSkill's skill-based matching at K=4; TrueSkill's approach, which pairs players of similar strength, ignores exploration and thus fails to ensure convergence of the overall ranking from a global perspective. The paper does not report a head-to-head numerical comparison between UCB and TrueSkill in a single table, but Figure 5 visually confirms UCB's superiority in convergence speed and stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

K-Sort Arena directly extends the Arena paradigm introduced by Chatbot Arena [10] from LLMs to visual generative models, but departs from it in three key ways: replacing pairwise comparisons with K-wise free-for-all battles (justified by the higher perceptual intuitiveness of images/videos), replacing the numerical Elo score with a probabilistic (Gaussian) capability model updated via Bayesian inference, and replacing random or skill-based matchmaking with a UCB exploration-exploitation strategy drawn from multi-armed bandit theory. The probabilistic modeling is inspired by Glicko [14] and TrueSkill [17], while the concurrent GenAI Arena [23] replicates the original pairwise workflow and thus shares the same efficiency and robustness limitations that K-Sort Arena addresses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

In a field where new text-to-image and text-to-video models appear weekly, the 16.3× reduction in required comparisons (716 vs. 11,692) makes it feasible to evaluate emerging models and update leaderboards in near real-time rather than months later. The live, open-source platform with over 1,000 expert votes provides a practical reference for the community. However, the current evidence is largely simulation-based (50 synthetic models), the real-world vote count is still modest, and the evaluation criteria (50% alignment / 50% aesthetics) remain subjective and may not capture all dimensions of quality. Open questions include how well the K-wise assumption scales to more complex or compositional generation tasks, and whether the UCB strategy remains optimal as the model pool grows to hundreds.
