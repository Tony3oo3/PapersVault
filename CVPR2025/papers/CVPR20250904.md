## Problem

Zero-shot amodal segmentation requires predicting complete object shapes in the open world without dataset-specific fine-tuning, but the prior foundation model (pix2gestalt) relies on a denoising diffusion pipeline that is both computationally expensive (~100s per instance) and unstable for segmentation-only tasks. The paper asks how to build a compact, efficient SAM-based foundation model that can leverage SAM's implicit prior knowledge to perform amodal segmentation directly, without the overhead of generative reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250904/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For each instance, the model receives the original RGB image (resized to 512×512) and the bounding box of the visible portion as the sole prompt—no ground-truth modal mask or category label is required. It simultaneously produces a modal mask (visible region) and a refined amodal mask (complete shape including occluded regions). The bounding box is doubled in size before cropping, and the model is evaluated zero-shot on real-world datasets (COCOA-cls, KINS, D2SA) distinct from its synthetic training data (Pix2Gestalt/SA-1B).

## Method

SAMBA fine-tunes SAM ViT-H via parameter-efficient LoRA adapters (72M trainable params) organized into a Separation-to-Fusion (S2F) structure: two parallel branches with independent LoRA adapters and mask decoders predict modal and coarse amodal masks, then a lightweight residual-block fusion module rectifies the amodal prediction using the modal output. A Principal Focusing (PF) mechanism inserts cross-attention units in the image encoder that use prompt embeddings as keys/values to explicitly indicate the segmentation target, reducing the combinatorial complexity of amodal feature extraction from 2^N to N possibilities per pixel. In the amodal branch, an Occlusion-Aware Mixture-of-Experts replaces the standard LoRA up-projection with N parallel 3×3 convolutional experts, guided by a novel distribution loss that computes a KL divergence between the global expert-activation distribution and an ideal distribution derived from the sample's occlusion rate, encouraging each expert to specialize in a distinct occlusion level.

## Results

SAMBA achieves state-of-the-art mean IoU on all three evaluation datasets: 81.82% on COCOA-cls, 88.47% on KINS, and 90.87% on D2SA. These results surpass both the best supervised method (C2F-Seg: 80.28/82.22/89.10) and the best prior zero-shot method (Pix2gestalt: 79.08/81.45/81.82). In terms of efficiency, SAMBA uses only 72M trainable parameters (vs. 860M for Pix2gestalt) and achieves 0.3s per-instance inference (vs. 100.1s for Pix2gestalt), matching the speed of the base SAM model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAMBA outperforms all five baselines across every dataset. Against the strongest supervised baseline C2F-Seg (ICCV'23), SAMBA gains +1.54 on COCOA-cls (81.82 vs. 80.28), +6.25 on KINS (88.47 vs. 82.22), and +1.77 on D2SA (90.87 vs. 89.10), while using far fewer parameters (72M vs. 325M). Against the prior best zero-shot model Pix2gestalt (CVPR'24), SAMBA improves by +2.74 on COCOA-cls, +7.02 on KINS, and +9.05 on D2SA, while reducing trainable parameters by 92% (72M vs. 860M) and cutting inference time from 100.1s to 0.3s per instance. The base SAM model (73.10 on COCOA-cls) and SDXL-Inpainting (73.65) are both substantially outperformed, confirming that the S2F structure, principal focusing, and occlusion-aware MoE collectively unlock amodal capabilities latent in SAM. Notably, SDAmodal (CVPR'24) is only evaluated on COCOA-cls (80.01), where SAMBA still leads by +1.81.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAMBA positions itself as the first SAM-based foundation model for zero-shot amodal segmentation, departing from the prior zero-shot approach pix2gestalt which relied on a conditional diffusion model to synthesize occluded regions. Methodologically, it builds on the parameter-efficient fine-tuning paradigm (LoRA) and the Conv-LoRA recipe of interleaving convolutional experts with low-rank matrices, but restructures these components around three task-specific mechanisms: a separation-to-fusion dual-branch architecture, a principal focusing cross-attention unit, and an occlusion-rate-conditioned mixture-of-experts with a novel distribution loss. Conceptually, the paper argues that amodal segmentation's distinctive challenges (depth ambiguity, occlusion-rate heterogeneity, modal-amodal coupling) are better addressed by a segmentation-native architecture than by a generative one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: SAMBA achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot amodal IoU while using only 72M trainable parameters and 0.3s inference per instance, compared to pix2gestalt's 860M parameters and 100s inference — a roughly 330× speedup that makes zero-shot amodal segmentation viable in latency-sensitive applications like autonomous driving and robotics. Open questions include whether the occlusion-aware MoE design generalizes to other occlusion-sensitive tasks (e.g., amodal depth, 3D amodal completion), whether the synthetic pix2gestalt training data limits robustness on truly out-of-distribution real-world scenes, and whether the bounding-box input requirement can be relaxed to fully open-vocabulary prompts. The finding that increasing LoRA rank beyond 64 degrades performance also hints at a capacity-data mismatch worth investigating at scale.
