## Problem

Existing vehicle trajectory prediction models struggle with generalizability across datasets and domains, inefficient multimodal handling that requires computationally intensive clustering and NMS, and architectures over-specialized to particular dataset scales. The potential of combining self-supervised learning techniques (self-distillation and masked reconstruction) within the Perceiver IO architecture for trajectory prediction remains unexplored, limiting the richness of scene representations needed for robust forecasting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251567/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 32 surrounding agents' 2-second history (position, velocity, acceleration) and 256 road polylines with 20 points each within a 100 m radius, all embedded into a unified token sequence processed by the Perceiver encoder. It outputs 6 multimodal 6-second future trajectories with GMM mode probabilities (64 Gaussian components), and during pretraining additionally reconstructs masked past trajectories and lane segments via dedicated decoder queries. A key design choice is that the decoder simultaneously handles trajectory prediction (mode queries), scene reconstruction (agent and road queries), and structured memory (register queries) in a single forward pass, eliminating the need for post-hoc clustering or NMS.

## Method

PerReg+ builds on a Perceiver IO encoder-decoder and introduces Dual-Level Representation Learning: a Self-Distillation branch where an EMA-updated teacher encoder processes unmasked inputs including future trajectories while a student encoder processes fine-grained masked inputs (90% history, 97% future, 75% map points), with alignment via cross-entropy on a scene-clustering MLP head; and a Masked Reconstruction branch where segment-level queries (not per-point) reconstruct entire past/future trajectories and lane segments, with 40% random query dropping to enforce holistic understanding. The decoder incorporates register queries alongside mode queries to natively support multimodal prediction without trajectory pooling or suppression. For fine-tuning, the entire pretrained architecture is frozen and only a small pool of cluster-specific prompts (selected by the pretrained clustering head) plus the prediction head are optimized, enabling efficient cross-domain adaptation. Multi-dataset pretraining across nuScenes, Argoverse 2, and WOMD via the UniTraj framework further enhances generalization, yielding 6.8% B-FDE reduction on smaller datasets and 11.8% reduction in cross-domain transfer.

## Results

PerReg+ achieves state-of-the-art B-FDE of 2.62 on nuScenes, 2.07 on Argoverse 2, and 2.05 on WOMD under single-dataset training with pre-training, outperforming all baselines. Pre-training yields a 6.8% B-FDE reduction on smaller datasets (nuScenes: 3.06→2.62; AV2: 2.38→2.07) and a 2.4% improvement on the larger WOMD dataset. In cross-domain evaluation (trained on WOMD, tested on nuScenes), PerReg+ achieves B-FDE 2.75, an 11.8% reduction over its non-pretrained variant (3.12) and the best result among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PerReg+ outperforms all three baselines on B-FDE across all three datasets under single-dataset training: on nuScenes it achieves 2.62 vs. AutoBot (3.36), MTR (2.86), and Forecast-MAE (2.81 with pre-training); on AV2 it reaches 2.07, narrowly edging out Forecast-MAE+ (2.05) and beating MTR (2.08) and AutoBot (2.51); on WOMD it scores 2.05 vs. MTR (2.13), Forecast-MAE+ (2.30), and AutoBot (2.47). In the out-of-domain setting (trained on WOMD, tested on nuScenes), PerReg+ achieves B-FDE 2.75, substantially beating MTR (3.10), Forecast-MAE+ (3.28), and AutoBot (3.73), with an 11.8% improvement over its own non-pretrained variant (3.12). The advantage is most pronounced on smaller datasets (nuScenes, AV2) where pre-training contributes 11–13% B-FDE reduction, while on the larger WOMD dataset the gain is more modest (2.4%), suggesting pre-training is most beneficial when in-domain data is limited. Under multi-dataset training, PerReg+ also leads on WOMD (B-FDE 2.04) and nuScenes (B-FDE 2.28), confirming that the dual-level representation learning and register-based multimodality generalize effectively across diverse driving scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

PerReg+ sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) the Perceiver IO architecture for structured multimodal inputs, (2) self-supervised pretraining for motion forecasting (Forecast-MAE, Traj-MAE, DySET), and (3) parameter-efficient fine-tuning (prompt tuning, PEFT). It departs from prior SSL trajectory prediction work by simultaneously combining self-distillation (BYOL-style EMA teacher) with masked reconstruction, rather than using either in isolation, and by retaining the decoder during fine-tuning—unlike most prior methods that discard it. The use of register queries to handle multimodality without NMS or clustering is a conceptual departure from the large-pool-then-suppress paradigm used by MTR, Wayformer, and AutoBot, and the prompt-based clustering for adaptation extends visual prompt tuning into the trajectory prediction domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that multi-dataset pretraining yields the largest gains on smaller datasets (11–13% B-FDE reduction on nuScenes/AV2 vs. 2.4% on WOMD), a practical finding for teams with limited data. The 11.8% cross-domain B-FDE reduction (WOMD→nuScenes) via prompt tuning suggests a viable path toward a single pretrained model adaptable to new driving distributions without full retraining. Open questions include whether the prompt pool scales to more diverse scenarios, whether the approach transfers to pedestrian or cyclist prediction, and whether the dual-level (SD+MR) pretraining overhead is justified at larger model scales. The single cross-domain pair tested limits confidence in the generalization claims.
