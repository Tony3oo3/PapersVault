## Problem

Lightweight dToF sensors in mobile devices produce extremely sparse (e.g., ~30×40 points) and noisy depth maps, making video depth completion challenging. Multi-frame fusion via optical flow alignment introduces misalignment that causes blending between object edges and background, while existing window-based video depth methods lack cross-window consistency constraints, leading to visible flickering at window boundaries.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse dToF depth map (30×40 sampling points over a 70° FOV, simulated with barrel distortion, random dropout, and depth errors) and the corresponding RGB video frame, both resized to 288×512, and produces a completed high-resolution depth map. Inference operates on a sliding window of 3 consecutive frames, and the model is trained jointly on TartanAir, Dynamic Replica, MIPI, and DydToF datasets to generalize across indoor and outdoor scenes.

## Method

SVDC builds on a lightweight multi-frame video depth completion backbone (DVDC) and introduces two key modules: a Channel-Spatial Enhancement Attention (CSEA) module that extracts an attention map distinguishing high-frequency (edge) regions from low-frequency (smooth) regions, and an Adaptive Frequency Selective Fusion (AFSF) module that uses this map to blend features processed by small kernels (preserving edge detail in misaligned regions) with features from large kernels (suppressing noise in smooth areas). A cross-window temporal consistency loss, computed via Scale-Invariant Loss between predictions of the same frame from overlapping windows, enforces consistency across window boundaries. The overall training objective combines spatial SILoss on both coarse and final depth, the cross-window loss, and an OPW temporal consistency loss with occlusion masking, all trained with a fine-tuned SpyNet optical flow network.

## Results

On TartanAir, SVDC achieves RMSE 0.164 m, REL 0.024, TEPE 69.7 mm, and OPW 0.159, outperforming BPNet (RMSE 0.337 m) and CFormer (RMSE 0.352 m) by roughly 2× in accuracy while using only 22.8M parameters versus 89.9M and 82.5M respectively. On Dynamic Replica, SVDC reaches RMSE 0.086 m and OPW 0.171, compared to BPNet's RMSE 0.126 m / OPW 0.601 and CFormer's RMSE 0.127 m / OPW 0.465, showing a 3.5× improvement in temporal consistency over BPNet. The multi-frame approach also substantially reduces TEPE (69.7 mm vs. 159.2 mm for BPNet on TartanAir), confirming that cross-frame fusion is critical for sparse dToF completion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SVDC decisively outperforms both per-frame baselines (BPNet and CFormer) on every metric across both datasets, with the largest gains in temporal consistency (OPW). On TartanAir, SVDC reduces RMSE from 0.337 m (BPNet) and 0.352 m (CFormer) to 0.164 m, and cuts OPW from ~0.25 to 0.159. On Dynamic Replica, the advantage is even more pronounced for consistency: SVDC's OPW of 0.171 is 3.5× lower than BPNet's 0.601 and 2.7× lower than CFormer's 0.465, reflecting the benefit of multi-frame fusion and the cross-window consistency loss. Notably, SVDC achieves these results with only 22.8M parameters—roughly one-third of CFormer and one-quarter of BPNet—making it substantially more suitable for mobile deployment. The per-frame baselines struggle because they assume sparse depth points are accurate and propagate noise, whereas SVDC's multi-frame fusion and adaptive frequency selection mitigate this issue.

## Relation to Prior Work

SVDC extends the dToF depth enhancement line initiated by Deltar and DVSR, which targeted low-resolution dToF depth maps, to the harder and more practical setting of sparse pixel-level dToF depth maps in video. The multi-frame fusion strategy draws directly from DVSR's optical-flow-guided deformable convolution, but the AFSF module replaces fixed deformable offsets with attention-guided adaptive kernel-size selection (inspired by Selective-Stereo and CBAM) to handle the greater inter-frame variability of sparse maps. The temporal consistency framework builds on FMNet's OPW loss, and the novel cross-window consistency loss addresses a gap the authors identify in all existing window-based video depth methods that ignore inter-window prediction coherence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As lightweight dToF sensors become standard in mobile devices (e.g., iPhone, Honor), reliable video depth completion from their sparse, noisy outputs is a practically critical capability for AR/VR and 3D reconstruction. The cross-window consistency loss is a simple but effective regularizer that simultaneously improves cross-window flickering, intra-window consistency, and absolute accuracy, suggesting that constraining prediction invariance to small input perturbations tightens the feature space broadly. Key limitations remain: reliance on a pre-trained optical flow model (SpyNet) that degrades under low light or large motion, and the inherent information sparsity of dToF maps that limits propagation quality. Future directions include cross-attention-based implicit fusion and densifying dToF features during preprocessing.
