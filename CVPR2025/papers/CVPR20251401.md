## Problem

Multi-instance learning for gigapixel whole-slide image classification is bottlenecked by data pre-processing (cropping and feature extraction of thousands of patches), which takes hundreds of times longer than the MIL classifier itself. Existing acceleration methods face a chicken-and-egg problem: attention scores that identify irrelevant patches can only be computed after all patch features are extracted. The paper shows that ~90% of patches contribute negligibly to bag-level classification and that eliminating them not only speeds up inference but also improves accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes WSI pyramids at two magnifications: high-resolution (20×, yielding 256×256 patches) and low-resolution (1.25×, yielding 16×16 patches) after background removal. At inference, LIPN processes only the tiny 16×16 low-resolution patches to produce a binary relevance mask, which then determines which 256×256 high-resolution patches are fed to the feature extractor and DMIN. The final output is a bag-level binary class label (e.g., metastasis present/absent) along with per-instance relevance masks that identify which regions drove the decision.

## Method

HDMIL comprises a Dynamic Multi-Instance Network (DMIN) trained via self-distillation and a Lightweight Instance Pre-Screening Network (LIPN) trained via cross-distillation. DMIN's teacher branch classifies using all instances while its student branch uses a Gumbel-Softmax-binarized subset; a consistency loss forces the student to match the teacher, and a rate loss controls the retention ratio, yielding per-instance relevance masks. LIPN (a MobileNetV4 variant) is then distilled from these masks to predict binary relevance directly from 16×16 low-resolution patches at negligible cost. At inference, LIPN screens the low-resolution WSI first, and only the corresponding high-resolution patches undergo feature extraction and DMIN classification, reducing total inference time by up to 28.6%. The classifier itself is a Chebyshev-polynomial-based Kolmogorov-Arnold (CKA) layer that replaces KAN's spline basis with first-kind Chebyshev polynomials and learnable coefficients, providing stronger nonlinear activation capacity than standard FC or MLP heads.

## Results

On Camelyon16, HDMIL achieves 90.88% AUC and 88.61% accuracy while reducing inference time to 16.75 s (28.6% faster than the 23.46 s baseline). On TCGA-NSCLC, HDMIL reaches 96.35% AUC and 89.78% accuracy in 44.71 s (21.8% speedup), and on TCGA-BRCA it attains 90.45% AUC and 88.27% accuracy in 33.86 s (7.2% speedup). The full DMIN-only variant (HDMIL†) achieves the highest AUC on Camelyon16 (93.17%) but at the same inference cost as all baselines, confirming that the LIPN pre-screening trades a small AUC drop for substantial speed gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HDMIL outperforms all seven compared baselines on AUC across all three datasets. On Camelyon16, the gap is largest: HDMIL (90.88% AUC) exceeds the strongest baseline DTFD-MAS (87.75%) by 3.13 points and CLAM-MB (87.51%) by 3.37 points. On TCGA-NSCLC, HDMIL (96.35%) edges out S4MIL (96.19%) by 0.16 points and CLAM-MB (95.59%) by 0.76 points, while on TCGA-BRCA it surpasses S4MIL (90.40%) by 0.05 points and CLAM-MB (90.22%) by 0.23 points. The speed advantage is most pronounced on Camelyon16 (28.6% faster) and NSCLC (21.8% faster), while on BRCA the gain is modest (7.2%) because the WSI contains fewer patches overall. Notably, HDMIL also improves accuracy over every baseline on all three datasets, with the largest ACC gains over DSMIL (e.g., +13.26 points on Camelyon16) and TransMIL (+7.60 points on Camelyon16).

## Relation to Prior Work

HDMIL builds directly on CLAM's dual-branch attention MIL framework for WSI classification, reusing its attention architecture, clustering loss, and preprocessing pipeline. It adapts the Gumbel-Softmax dynamic token selection mechanism from DynamicViT to the instance-level setting, enabling differentiable selection of relevant patches. The CKA classifier extends KAN by replacing B-spline basis functions with first-kind Chebyshev polynomials, a modification motivated by the bounded input range of bag-level representations. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the hierarchical distillation strategy: a high-resolution teacher (DMIN) generates per-instance relevance masks that are distilled into a lightweight low-resolution student (LIPN), resolving the 'chicken-and-egg' problem where attention scores require features that are themselves the computational bottleneck.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central insight—that discarding irrelevant patches simultaneously accelerates inference and improves classification accuracy—challenges the conventional speed-accuracy tradeoff assumption in WSI analysis and could inspire broader data-pruning strategies in MIL. The 28.6% inference time reduction on Camelyon16 with maintained or improved AUC is practically significant for clinical deployment where throughput matters. However, the approach still requires a full feature extractor (ResNet-50) for retained patches, and the authors acknowledge that further reducing the feature extraction burden remains an open problem. The CKA classifier, while showing consistent gains over FC/MLP/KAN baselines, has only been validated on three datasets and a single polynomial order (K=12), leaving its generalizability to other tasks and scales uncertain.
