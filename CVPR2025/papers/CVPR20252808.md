## Problem

Existing IP protection methods for DDPMs are invasive—watermarking requires modifying model parameters through fine-tuning or retraining, which degrades generation quality and incurs computational overhead. No non-invasive fingerprinting scheme existed for DDPMs, and prior fingerprinting techniques designed for deterministic classifiers or restoration models cannot be directly adapted to probabilistic diffusion models that map a standard distribution to an image distribution.

## Input / Output

The method takes a verification image (typically a QR code at 256×256 for pixel-space DDPMs or 512×512 for LDMs, encoding a random string of length 24–64) and black-box access to the DDPM denoiser ε_θ. It produces a multi-step fingerprint latent code z = {x_T, z_T, …, z_1} comprising both the final noisy sample and all intermediate Gaussian noise vectors. During verification, z is fed into a suspect model to generate an image; ownership is confirmed by scanning the output QR code, requiring no gradient access or white-box privileges.

## Method

The core contribution is a 'crossing route' on the performance border-zone of the denoiser: instead of using standard Gaussian noise for inversion, the method constructs a sequence of noisy samples {x_1, …, x_T} that transitions from the in-domain region D (where ε_θ predicts noise easily) to the out-of-domain region D̄ (where prediction is difficult). Each noise vector is initialized as a mixture of Gaussian and non-Gaussian (uniform) components with timestep-dependent weighting, then optimized with a loss that balances denoiser prediction error (weighted by (T−t)/T) against total variation of the noisy sample (weighted by (t−1)/T), placing early timesteps in D and late timesteps in D̄. After obtaining the crossing route, the full latent code is recovered by back-calculating z_t = (x_{t−1} − μ̂_t(x_t))/σ_t at each step, following the stochastic inversion framework of DDPM inversion and CycleDiffusion. This yields a discriminative fingerprint that is robust to pruning, fine-tuning, and quantization attacks while requiring only black-box denoiser access during verification.

## Results

Under unified attack settings (pruning, fine-tuning, quantization), FingerInv achieves a 100% verification success rate across all tested PS-DDPMs (Bedroom, Cat, CelebA, Church) and LDMs (SD V1-4, DeciDiffusion, Pixart-α), matching the invasive baselines Stable_sig and WM_DM. In contrast, the non-invasive baselines CycleDiff and DDPM_inv achieve only 81.82% and 77.27% success rates, respectively. In the discriminability analysis (Table 1), FingerInv produces perfectly diagonal QR-code matches (zero false positives), while CycleDiff and DDPM_inv exhibit multiple cross-model false positives. The method is also robust to the watermark-removal attack of [9], which defeats Stable_sig.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two non-invasive baselines, FingerInv clearly wins: CycleDiff (Wu et al., ICCV 2023) achieves 81.82% success rate and DDPM_inv (Huberman-Spiegelglas et al., CVPR 2024) achieves 77.27%, both failing on several pruning and quantization cases (e.g., CycleDiff fails on Bedroom and Deci under pruning; DDPM_inv fails on Bedroom, Church, SD V1-4, and Deci under pruning). Against the two invasive baselines, FingerInv ties on raw success rate (100% for all four methods) but holds two decisive advantages: it is non-invasive (no parameter modification or fine-tuning required), and it is robust to the watermark-removal attack of [9] that defeats Stable_sig (Fernandez et al., ICCV 2023). WM_DM (Zhao et al., 2023) also survives [9] but remains invasive. In the discriminability comparison (Table 1), FingerInv is the only method with zero false positives across all PS-DDPM and LDM cross-verification pairs, while CycleDiff and DDPM_inv each produce multiple false-positive QR-code matches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model fingerprinting paradigm—previously established for deterministic classifiers and image restoration networks—to probabilistic diffusion models, where the fundamental many-to-many mapping from noise to image breaks existing one-to-one fingerprinting assumptions. It repurposes the 'performance border-zone' concept from restoration fingerprinting [25] but generalizes it from a single critical point to a multi-timestep 'crossing route' that exploits the progressive denoising structure of DDPMs. For the inversion mechanism, it builds on stochastic inversion methods (CycleDiffusion, DDPM inversion) that guarantee perfect reconstruction, rather than deterministic DDIM-based approaches that accumulate linearization error.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As open-source diffusion models proliferate, a non-invasive IP protection method that preserves generation quality without retraining or fine-tuning addresses a practical gap left by invasive watermarking. The key limitation is that verification is not strictly black-box: it requires manual injection of latent components at each sampling timestep, which is incompatible with fully encapsulated API deployments. Open questions include whether fingerprinting can be reduced to x_T alone for API-compatible verification, and whether the crossing route concept extends to other diffusion variants (e.g., flow matching, score-based SGMs beyond DDPMs).
