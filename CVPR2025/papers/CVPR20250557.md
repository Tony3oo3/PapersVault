## Problem

Generating realistic 3D human-object interactions (HOIs) is hindered by existing datasets that are small (typically under 4 hours), inconsistent in human representation and annotation, and riddled with artifacts such as contact penetration, floating, and inaccurate hand poses. No prior benchmark unifies multiple HOI generation tasks under a common representation, and available textual annotations are coarse or absent, limiting the ability to connect interaction dynamics to language.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250557/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark defines six tasks with heterogeneous inputs: free-form text, discrete action labels (15 categories), object motion sequences, human motion sequences, past interaction windows, or reference interaction clips. The human is encoded as a marker-based representation (specific SMPL-H/SMPL-X vertex sets) with velocities, signed distance vectors to the object, and foot-ground contact labels; the object is encoded by rotation angles, translations, and Basis Point Set (BPS) geometry. A shared contact feature η—vectors from each human marker to its nearest object surface point—is treated as an additional output in the five kinematic tasks, while the imitation task outputs PD joint targets for physics simulation.

## Method

The paper introduces a three-stage gradient-based optimization pipeline: full-body correction (reconstruction + contact/penetration losses over the whole body), hand correction (contact promotion guided by ground-truth contact indicators plus penetration, smoothness, and finger range-of-motion constraints), and interaction augmentation via a contact-invariance principle that displaces the object trajectory and re-optimizes human motion to preserve vertex-pair distance relationships (weighted by inverse squared original distance). For generation, a transformer-based diffusion model is trained with multi-task learning that jointly predicts motion and the contact feature η, replacing CLIP with an interaction-aware text encoder (Sentence-BERT + InfoNCE contrastive pretraining) and PointNet++ with BPS for object shape. Contact prediction is further injected as classifier guidance during denoising, and the marker-based representation is shown to reduce penetration artifacts compared to SMPL or joint-based alternatives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HOI-Diff [63] on action-conditioned interaction generation, the proposed method reduces FID from 3.566 to 2.161 (a 39% improvement) while also improving Multimodality (5.792 vs. 5.321) and Diversity (11.291 vs. 10.989), indicating both higher fidelity and greater variety in generated interactions. Against the 2-stage OMOMO pipeline [38] on object-conditioned human motion generation with unseen objects, Ours(Cont.) improves MPMPE from 36.50 to 35.69 and F1 Score from 0.80 to 0.85, with gains across all contact metrics (Cprec 0.85 vs. 0.81, Crec 0.89 vs. 0.85, Cacc 0.85 vs. 0.77). The single-stage multi-task formulation also outperforms its own 1-stage ablation (MPMPE 36.94, F1 0.81), confirming that jointly regressing contact features η as an auxiliary output is the key driver of the improvement. On interaction prediction, scaling the model on the full InterAct dataset (×3) consistently outperforms training on the smaller BEHAVE subset at every scale, demonstrating the dataset's value for learning generalizable interaction dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper consolidates seven existing 3D HOI datasets (GRAB, BEHAVE, InterCap, Chairs, HODome, OMOMO, IMHD) into a single standardized benchmark, addressing the fragmentation and quality issues (penetration, floating, inaccurate hand poses) that have limited prior HOI generation work. Methodologically, it builds on HOI-Diff's diffusion backbone, OMOMO's object-conditioned generation, InterDiff's physics-informed prediction, and PhysHOI's physics-based imitation, but unifies them under a multi-task learning framework that treats contact representation as a shared output. The contact invariance augmentation principle, inspired by motion mirroring, is a novel data-scaling strategy that departs from prior work's reliance solely on captured MoCap sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

InterAct provides the first large-scale, standardized 3D HOI benchmark (30.70 hours, 217 objects, 48,630 text annotations) with a unified evaluation protocol across six generation tasks, filling a critical gap that has constrained the field's progress. The optimization-based correction and contact-invariance augmentation offer a practical recipe for improving MoCap data quality without additional capture sessions, which is broadly applicable beyond HOI. Open questions include whether the 15 predefined action labels and marker-based representation generalize to more diverse or dexterous interactions, and whether the gradient-based correction framework scales to multi-object or multi-person scenarios.
