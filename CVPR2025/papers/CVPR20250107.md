## Problem

Egocentric motion capture from head-mounted body-facing cameras suffers from heavy occlusions, a scarcity of real-world training data, and persistent artifacts such as floor penetration and foot skating. Existing methods rely heavily on synthetic pretraining and implicitly learn the relationship between device pose and body motion, failing to exploit the known geometric structure of the egocentric setup. This paper addresses both the data gap (introducing a 6× larger real-world dataset) and the architectural gap (explicitly leveraging setup geometry for floor-aligned prediction).

## Input / Output

The model consumes a pair of downward-facing stereo fisheye frames (640×480, resized to 256×256) at 30 Hz together with the 6D headset pose from on-device VR SLAM tracking, which is floor-aligned by design. It outputs 15-keypoint 3D joint positions in a floor-aligned reference frame, with the Stereo Temporal Fusion module additionally ingesting the 20 most recent per-view predictions (sampled at 15 Hz) to produce a temporally coherent motion sequence. At inference, only the most recent frame is emitted in real time (300 FPS on an RTX 3090).

## Method

A ResNet50 backbone (final residual block removed for higher spatial resolution) extracts features from both fisheye views; concatenated features feed a convolutional head that predicts per-joint 2.5D heatmaps and depthmaps, which are unprojected into 3D via the fisheye camera model. The key architectural insight is an explicit geometric alignment step: using the known headset-to-camera extrinsics and the VR-tracked 6D pose, per-view predictions are rototranslated into a shared floor-aligned frame F (origin at ground level, y-axis vertical), rather than implicitly learning this relationship. A Stereo Temporal Fusion (STF) transformer encoder (8 layers, 512-dim FFN, 32 heads) then fuses the current and 20 prior aligned predictions for temporal smoothing and lower-limb correction. A Cross-Training Caching strategy (k-fold inspired) caches backbone predictions on held-out subsets to train the STF on realistic error distributions, improving generalization. The full pipeline achieves 47.53 mm MPJPE and 100% non-penetration, outperforming prior methods by 28% in MPJPE.

## Results

On the SELF dataset, FRAME achieves 47.53 mm MPJPE and 92.56% 3D-PCK (10 cm threshold), outperforming the strongest baseline EgoPoseFormer (69.18 mm MPJPE, 78.98% 3D-PCK) by 31% in MPJPE. The method also achieves a perfect 100% Non-Penetration Percentage and 0.00 mm Mean Penetration Error, compared to 49–58% NPP and ~48 mm MPE for all baselines. Inference runs at 2.68 ms per frame (~300 FPS) on an NVIDIA RTX 3090, roughly 2.5–5× faster than the compared methods. Temporal stability is also markedly improved, with jitter of 4.96 mm and foot sliding of 3.47 cm/s versus 9.98–12.60 mm and 9.29–12.34 cm/s for baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FRAME outperforms all three retrained stereo baselines across every reported metric. Against the closest competitor, EgoPoseFormer [47], FRAME reduces MPJPE from 69.18 mm to 47.53 mm (−31%) and raises 3D-PCK from 78.98% to 92.56%, while also cutting inference time from 14.36 ms to 2.68 ms. The two earlier stereo methods, UnrealEgo [1] (104.81 mm MPJPE) and EgoGlass [53] (105.56 mm MPJPE), are surpassed by roughly 55% in MPJPE and show substantial floor-penetration artifacts (NPP 52–58%, MPE ≈48 mm) that FRAME eliminates entirely (NPP 100%, MPE 0 mm). In a separate generalizability experiment (Table 4), the authors show that applying their floor-aligned frame refinement to the monocular EgoWholeBody [41] model reduces its MPJPE from 85.63 mm (camera frame) to 76.43 mm (floor-aligned frame), confirming that the frame-alignment design transfers to other architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of stereo egocentric pose estimation (EgoGlass, UnrealEgo, EgoPoseFormer) by explicitly incorporating the VR headset's 6D pose tracking as a first-class input rather than treating the problem as purely vision-based. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a floor-aligned reference frame (F) that rototranslates camera-frame predictions using known device-camera geometry, addressing a gap where prior methods worked in either camera or pelvis-relative coordinates without leveraging the gravity-aligned structure inherent to VR setups. The Cross-Training Caching strategy, inspired by k-fold cross-validation, is a novel training technique specifically designed to bridge the train-test error distribution gap for downstream temporal fusion modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SELF dataset (1.6M frames, 7.4 hours, 6x larger than prior real-world egocentric collections) with on-device VR tracking annotations is a significant resource that eliminates the field's reliance on synthetic pretraining and enables meaningful generalization benchmarks. The floor-aligned frame is demonstrated to be a generalizable design principle (improving EgoWholeBody by ~9mm), suggesting it can be adopted across egocentric pose estimation methods. Open questions include whether the approach extends to exploiting additional VR modalities (forward-facing cameras, eye tracking, hand tracking), handling severe occlusions and self-contact, and integrating physics-based constraints for more realistic motion.
