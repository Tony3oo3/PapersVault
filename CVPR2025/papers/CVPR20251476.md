## Problem

Existing Referring Video Object Segmentation (RVOS) methods either process short clips independently, losing global temporal context, or process entire videos offline, making them inapplicable to streaming scenarios. The Segment-Anything 2 (SAM2) model is naturally suited for streaming video segmentation but lacks text understanding, explicit temporal feature modeling, and suffers from a tracking bias where it locks onto an incorrect object and fails to switch when the true target later becomes identifiable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251476/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes video frames in a streaming fashion, processing clips of T=8 frames as they become available, along with a natural language referring expression tokenized into L words plus a [CLS] token. The text is encoded by a frozen encoder (CLIP-B or RoBERTa) and the video by a frozen Hiera-B backbone. The output is a binary segmentation mask (H×W) per frame, produced by SAM2's frozen Mask Decoder prompted with a combined contextual and motion embedding derived from the [CLS] and verb tokens.

## Method

SAMWISE introduces a Cross-Modal Temporal Adapter (CMT) inserted at every intermediate layer of both the frozen visual and text encoders, comprising three tightly coupled components: Hierarchical Selective Attention (HSA) that models temporal evolution within spatially localized 3D patches of progressively scaled size, Visual-to-Text Attention (VTA) that lets each visual feature attend to the full text, and Text-to-Visual Attention (TVA) that contextualizes linguistic tokens with clip-averaged visual features. A separate Conditional Memory Encoder (CME) compares a memory-less token (derived from unbiased frame features via cross-attention with the prompt) against the Mask Decoder's mask token to detect when a new text-aligned object appears, then fuses both objects' masks into the Memory Bank with a learned soft assignment, enabling SAM2 to dynamically refocus tracking. The entire system trains only ~5M parameters (adapters + CME) via supervised pre-training on RefCOCO+/g and fine-tuning on Ref-Youtube-VOS, with the CME additionally trained via self-supervised cross-entropy on detection of object switches, while all SAM2 and text encoder weights remain frozen.

## Results

SAMWISE (with RoBERTa text encoder) achieves state-of-the-art J&F scores of 49.5 on MeViS, 69.2 on Ref-YouTube-VOS, and 70.6 on Ref-DAVIS17, outperforming the previous offline SOTA DsHmp by +3.1%, +2.1%, and +5.7% respectively, while using fewer total parameters (202M vs. 339M) and training only 4.9M learnable parameters. The CLIP-B variant achieves competitive results (48.3 / 67.2 / 68.5) with a more compact 150M parameter model. All results are obtained in a streaming fashion without access to the full video, unlike offline competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAMWISE outperforms the previous state-of-the-art offline method DsHmp [11] on all three benchmarks (MeViS +3.1%, Ref-YouTube-VOS +2.1%, Ref-DAVIS17 +5.7% J&F) despite operating in streaming mode and training only 4.9M parameters. Against the context-propagation baseline OnlineRefer [42], SAMWISE gains +17.2% on MeViS and +5.7–5.8% on the other two benchmarks, demonstrating the benefit of SAM2's multi-frame memory over single-frame propagation. Compared to the best Large-VLM-based method VISA [45], SAMWISE leads by +6.0%, +7.7%, and +2.9% J&F on the three benchmarks while being orders of magnitude smaller (202M vs. 7B parameters). The GroundingDINO+SAM2 baseline, which relies on first-frame detection, drops 11.7–11.8% on MeViS and Ref-YouTube-VOS relative to SAMWISE, highlighting the importance of temporal reasoning. On Ref-DAVIS17, where captions are more descriptive and object-centric, the gap over SOC [28] and MUTR [46] narrows to +4.3% and +4.2% respectively, but SAMWISE still leads.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the frozen SAM2 architecture (with its Hiera backbone and Memory Bank) and the parameter-efficient Adapter framework of Houlsby et al., but departs from prior text-prompted SAM approaches that outsource visual-textual interaction to external VLMs (LISA, VISA, One-Token-Seg-All) or rely on two-stage detection pipelines (Grounded SAM). It also extends the streaming RVOS line initiated by OnlineRefer, which propagates context from a single frame, by leveraging SAM2's Memory Bank for long-range temporal context. The Cross-Modal Temporal Adapter is novel in unifying temporal modeling (via Hierarchical Selective Attention) and cross-modal interaction (VTA/TVA) within a single lightweight module inserted at every encoder layer, rather than treating them as separate concerns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAMWISE demonstrates that a streaming RVOS method can match or exceed offline approaches that exploit the full video, which is practically significant for real-time and resource-constrained applications. The identification of tracking bias as a distinct failure mode in SAM2, and the lightweight CME mechanism to correct it, provides a reusable insight for anyone building on SAM2 for open-vocabulary video tasks. Open questions include how the approach generalizes to multi-object queries, significantly longer videos beyond the 8-frame clip window, and whether the self-supervised CME training transfers across domains without retraining.
