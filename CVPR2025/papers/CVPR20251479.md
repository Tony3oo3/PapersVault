## Problem

Independent per-person avatar reconstruction from sparse multi-view videos produces geometrically inconsistent results, particularly inter-penetrations between avatars in close-interaction scenarios. Existing solutions either rely on expensive neural volumetric rendering for inside-outside tests or cause unwanted shape deviations by penalizing simultaneous occupancy, while sparse views and textureless regions further degrade geometric accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251479/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes sparse multi-view RGB videos (8 synchronized cameras, 200 frames per sequence in Hi4D) along with monocular depth and normal predictions from the Sapiens human vision foundation model and instance segmentation maps from SAM2 (manually prompted and guide-filtered). It outputs per-person planar Gaussian splat avatars in canonical space, per-frame 3D meshes extracted via TSDF volumetric fusion and marching cubes from 100 sampled camera views, and novel-view renderings. Training completes in approximately 10 minutes on a single NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPU.

## Method

Each avatar is represented as a set of planar Gaussian splats in an independent canonical space aligned with DApose, articulated to the target pose via linear blending skinning on SMPL-X plus an auxiliary deformation field for clothing motion. The key geometric-consistency mechanism is a surface ordering loss that uses SAM2 instance segmentation as a visibility map to correct the depth ordering of foreground and background surfaces along each ray, exposing penetrated parts without modeling physical contact deformation. To handle sparse-view ambiguity and textureless regions, monocular depth and normal priors from Sapiens are distilled into the training objective via scale-shift-invariant depth loss and cosine/L1 normal loss. The full training combines photometric reconstruction (L1 + SSIM), surface ordering, monocular geometry, and planar Gaussian regularization terms (normal-depth consistency and depth concentration), with poses and appearances jointly optimized after an initial appearance-only warm-up phase.

## Results

On the Hi4D multi-person close-interaction dataset, GeoAvatar achieves an average PSNR of 29.68 dB and SSIM of 0.959 across five sequences, outperforming EasyMocap (26.83 dB / 0.945) and Multi-GART (27.94 dB / 0.951). The geometry gains are particularly striking: average P2S distance drops to 0.516 (vs. 0.671 for EasyMocap and 1.310 for Multi-GART), and Chamfer distance drops to 0.586 (vs. 1.180 and 1.823 respectively). Training completes in ~10 minutes on a single RTX A6000 GPU, compared to 2–3 days for EasyMocap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoAvatar outperforms both baselines on every metric across all five sequences. Against EasyMocap (implicit neural radiance field, 2–3 day training), GeoAvatar gains ~2.8 dB PSNR and reduces P2S by ~23% and CD by ~50%, while training ~100× faster. Against Multi-GART (Gaussian articulated template, the strongest rendering baseline), GeoAvatar improves PSNR by ~1.7 dB and LPIPS by ~0.023, but the geometry advantage is far more dramatic: P2S is reduced by ~61% (0.516 vs. 1.310) and CD by ~68% (0.586 vs. 1.823), reflecting that standard 3D Gaussian splats lack well-defined surfaces. The surface ordering loss further improves instance segmentation mIoU in tightly-contacted regions (70.03% at 1% threshold vs. 61.28% without it), confirming that the 2DGS representation plus monocular priors resolve inter-penetration artifacts that both baselines exhibit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the articulated Gaussian splatting line (GART [24]) by replacing volumetric 3D Gaussians with planar 2D Gaussian splats (2DGS [12]) to obtain well-defined surfaces that support inside-outside tests. For multi-person inter-penetration, it departs from the dominant occupancy-penalty strategy (used in EasyMocap [37], RemIPS [7], Multiply [18]) by instead enforcing surface ordering via instance segmentation maps, avoiding expensive per-point SDF queries and the shape deviations caused by rigid skinning in contact regions. The monocular-prior component follows MonoSDF [50] and DN-Splatter [40] but is the first to systematically benchmark human-centric foundation models (Sapiens [21]) against general-purpose depth/normal estimators in the dynamic multi-person setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10-minute training time (vs. 2–3 days for EasyMocap) and real-time rendering make geometrically consistent multi-person reconstruction practically deployable, and the surface-ordering trick offers a lightweight alternative to physics-based contact modeling. However, the method reconstructs static surface colors and normals, so temporal appearance changes (e.g., fabric wrinkles, lighting shifts) are lost; the authors note that integrating pose-dependent appearance (Vid2Avatar [10]) or physically-based inverse rendering [41,46] would address this. The reliance on monocular priors also inherits their biases, and the deformation field may be insufficient for fine dynamic details, leaving open the question of how to combine surface-aware splatting with expressive, time-varying appearance models.
