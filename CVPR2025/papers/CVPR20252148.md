## Problem

Existing in-context learning (ICL) strategies for Large Vision-Language Models rely on heuristic demonstration selection—either fixed predefined examples or similarity-based retrieval—that fail to model interactions between demonstrations and cannot adapt to diverse task requirements. This leads to information redundancy among retrieved samples and sub-optimal few-shot performance, with no mechanism to learn an adaptive selection policy from the multi-modal task structure itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252148/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a query consisting of an RGB image and a text question, along with a candidate demonstration pool (each demonstration being an image-question-answer triple drawn from the training split of the same benchmark). It outputs a selected combination of m=2 demonstrations, which are concatenated with the query in a prompt fed to the LVLM (Qwen-VL or LLaVA) to produce a text answer. The selection policy is a 6-layer transformer (8 heads, 2048 hidden dims) trained with ImageBind as the frozen multi-modal feature encoder, and the reward signal is binary VQAScore equivalence between the LVLM output and ground truth.

## Method

The paper reformulates multi-modal demonstration selection as a combinatorial optimization problem solved via an exploration-exploitation reinforcement learning loop. A multi-modal fusion encoder (transformer with functional and positional embeddings) captures cross-modal and inter-demonstration interactions, feeding an auto-regressive decoder that selects each demonstration conditioned on all previously chosen ones, thereby modeling sample-to-sample complementarity. In the exploration phase, stochastic beam search (beam width 9, temperature 1.0) samples c=4 candidate demonstration combinations per query; in the exploitation phase, a policy-gradient objective (inspired by GRPO) updates the selection policy using query-wise standard-normalized advantages computed from VQAScore rewards of the LVLM's predictions. This iterative loop allows the policy to autonomously discover selection strategies that balance similarity and diversity without any manually annotated supervision.

## Results

On four VQA benchmarks evaluated with VQAScore, the proposed exploration-exploitation framework achieves the best average score of 45.9 with Qwen-VL, surpassing the similarity baseline (44.3), BM25 (43.9), random (42.5), and zero-shot (41.0). The largest gains appear on OKVQA (+4.1 over similarity) and Vizwiz (+4.9 over similarity). With the weaker LLaVA backbone, the method improves the average from 31.3 (similarity) to 40.8, a +9.5 absolute gain, demonstrating stronger benefits for less capable models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BM25 (the listed retrieval baseline), the proposed method wins on average VQAScore with Qwen-VL (45.9 vs. 43.9) and achieves substantially higher scores on OKVQA (64.7 vs. 60.3) and Vizwiz (53.6 vs. 43.6), though BM25 retains an edge on the MMStar CP sub-category (59.8 vs. 56.8) and IR (51.1 vs. 46.6). Compared to the cosine-similarity baseline, the method improves by +4.1 on OKVQA and +4.9 on Vizwiz with Qwen-VL, and by a dramatic +21.6 on OKVQA and +25.8 on Vizwiz with LLaVA. The method also outperforms random selection and zero-shot across all four datasets. Notably, the paper reports that the learned policy underperforms similarity on math (MA) and some coarse-perception sub-tasks, where rigid rule-following or direct visual matching is more effective than the learned combinatorial strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ICL demonstration-selection literature from LLMs to LVLMs, departing from the dominant heuristic paradigms (fixed demonstrations, similarity retrieval, diversity sampling) by reframing selection as a combinatorial optimization problem solved via reinforcement learning. Conceptually, it draws on policy-gradient RL (PPO/GRPO lineage) and stochastic beam search from NLP sequence generation, repurposing them for the discrete, multi-modal setting of choosing which image-question-answer triples to include in a prompt. The multi-modal interactive encoding (ImageBind features + transformer fusion) builds on standard cross-modal fusion but is specifically designed to capture inter-demonstration interactions that prior independent-selection methods ignore.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides evidence that similarity-based demonstration retrieval—a widely adopted default—can actively mislead LVLMs, and that a learned combinatorial policy capturing inter-demo interactions yields consistent (if modest) gains across diverse VQA benchmarks. The exploration-exploitation RL framework offers a training-free-for-the-LVLM recipe: only a small selection policy network is trained, leaving the backbone frozen. Key open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond VQA to generation or reasoning tasks, whether it scales to additional modalities (video, audio), and whether the combinatorial framing remains tractable as candidate pool sizes grow. The method's underperformance on math and rigid-rule tasks suggests that not all ICL benefits from learned selection, an important caveat for practitioners.
