## Problem

Self-supervised monocular depth estimation has long treated depth as a point-wise prediction problem, where each pixel's depth is estimated independently, causing dramatic depth jumps within the same physical region (depth discontinuity). Prior attempts to regularize depth using surface normals alone are unreliable because they violate the plane uniqueness principle in Geometric Algebra, which requires both a normal vector and an offset to define a plane deterministically in 3D space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251997/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image from a monocular video sequence (640×192 for outdoor, 320×256 for indoor) and produces a depth map, but unlike standard approaches it does not predict depth directly. Instead, the network first outputs a planar normal map N ∈ R^{3×H×W} and a planar offset map O ∈ R^{3×H×W}, from which depth is analytically derived via the perspective projection relationship d = O / (NᵀK⁻¹p̃). Training consumes monocular video triplets with a jointly learned pose network, requiring no depth ground truth.

## Method

GeoDepth replaces direct pixel-wise depth prediction with a plane-to-depth formulation: the 3D scene is modeled as a collection of planes, each parameterized by a unique normal and offset, and depth is computed from these parameters so that coplanar points share a unified representation with depth varying only with pixel coordinates. A Structured Plane Generation (SPG) module recovers correct plane parameters through three alignment losses: spatial normal alignment (via depth-to-normal cross-product), temporal normal/offset alignment (via pose-based warping across video frames), and a plane uniqueness constraint that penalizes first-order gradients of both N and O to enforce coplanar consistency. A Depth Discontinuity Awareness (DDA) module adaptively identifies low-photometric-error regions (using a mean-based threshold with a balancing coefficient μ) as potential discontinuity zones and applies the SPG constraints selectively to those pixels. The overall self-supervised loss combines standard photometric and smoothness terms with the structured planar generation loss, and the entire framework adds negligible parameters (~10M) over a baseline CNN backbone borrowed from RA-Depth.

## Results

On KITTI (monocular training), GeoDepth achieves RMSE 4.381, Sq Rel 0.694, and Abs Rel 0.100, outperforming the best monocular baseline Swin-Depth (RMSE 4.510, Sq Rel 0.739) by 6.1% in Sq Rel and 5.7% in Abs Rel. On NYUv2, GeoDepth reaches RMSE 0.520 and Abs Rel 0.134, surpassing StructDepth (RMSE 0.540, Abs Rel 0.142). Cross-dataset generalization is validated on Make3D (RMSE 6.735 vs. Lite-Mono's 6.981) and ScanNet (RMSE 0.387 vs. SC-Depthv1's 0.392), where GeoDepth also achieves the best results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoDepth outperforms all 29 compared baselines across all four datasets and all reported metrics. On KITTI (monocular training), it beats the strongest monocular competitor Swin-Depth [40] by 6.1% in Sq Rel (0.694 vs. 0.739) and 5.7% in Abs Rel (0.100 vs. 0.106), and also surpasses stereo-trained methods like HR-Depth [32] (RMSE 4.612) and DepthHints [48] (RMSE 4.627) using only monocular video. On the indoor NYUv2 benchmark, GeoDepth (RMSE 0.520, Abs Rel 0.134) improves over StructDepth [27] (RMSE 0.540, Abs Rel 0.142) and PLNet [22] (RMSE 0.562, Abs Rel 0.151), confirming the benefit of plane-to-depth modeling in texture-poor indoor scenes. Cross-dataset generalization is validated on Make3D (outdoor, RMSE 6.735 vs. Lite-Mono's 6.981) and ScanNet (indoor, RMSE 0.387 vs. SC-Depthv1's 0.392), where GeoDepth again achieves the best performance without any dataset-specific tuning. The ablation study further shows that the gains come from the modeling strategy rather than added parameters (only ~0.02M extra params over baseline), with SPG contributing RMSE reduction from 4.436 to 4.412 and DDA further reducing it to 4.381.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoDepth builds directly on the Monodepth2 self-supervised framework (photometric loss, joint depth-pose estimation from video) and borrows its DepthNet/PoseNet architecture from RA-Depth, but fundamentally restructures the prediction target: instead of regressing per-pixel depth, the network predicts planar normals and planar offsets, from which depth is analytically derived via perspective geometry (Eq. 2). This departs from the entire point-to-depth lineage (Monodepth2, Lite-Mono, Swin-Depth, Dynamo-Depth, etc.) and also extends the plane-cue literature (StructDepth, P2Net, GeoNet) by enforcing the plane uniqueness principle from Geometric Algebra—requiring both a normal vector and an offset for a definitive plane—rather than relying on surface normals alone. The structured plane generation module and depth discontinuity awareness module are novel loss-level contributions layered on top of this reformulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plane-to-depth formulation offers a principled geometric remedy for depth discontinuities in low-texture regions, a persistent weakness of self-supervised methods, and the plane uniqueness principle provides a clean theoretical justification that prior normal-only approaches lacked. However, the authors deliberately use a simpler CNN backbone (RA-Depth) to isolate the modeling contribution, so the absolute numbers may not reflect what is achievable when combined with the latest transformer-based extractors or data augmentation strategies (they note such integrations in supplementary material). Open questions include how well the plane assumption holds for highly curved or non-planar surfaces, and whether the structured plane representation can be extended to handle dynamic or partially occluded scenes without additional masking mechanisms.
