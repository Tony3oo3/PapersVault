## Problem

Existing feed-forward 3D Gaussian splatting models are designed exclusively for perspective images and cannot handle omnidirectional inputs due to the non-uniform sampling and polar distortion inherent in equirectangular projections, which cause feature encoders to extract incorrect features and produce non-uniformly distributed Gaussians that generate stripe-like artifacts during rendering. No prior work has estimated 3D Gaussian parameters directly from omnidirectional images in a single feed-forward pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251426/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two omnidirectional (equirectangular) RGB images at 1024×512 resolution along with their camera poses as input. Each omnidirectional image is decomposed into a Yin (north) and Yang (east) sub-image, yielding four perspective-like images that are fed into the network. The output is a unified 3D Gaussian splatting (positions, covariances, opacities, spherical harmonics coefficients) that can be rasterized into novel-view omnidirectional images in ~0.5 seconds, and attention scores from the reconstruction process additionally yield multiview-consistent segmentation maps for 3D editing.

## Method

OmniSplat decomposes each omnidirectional image into two quasi-uniform sub-images using the Yin-Yang grid, which preserves a structured square lattice compatible with standard CNNs while minimizing polar distortion. A modified cross-view attention mechanism warps features across the four Yin-Yang images with grid-aware masking (Eqs. 4–5) to construct cost volumes without losing information at grid boundaries, then estimates 3DGS parameters following the MVSplat architecture. For rendering, a Yin-Yang rasterizer renders the Yin and Yang images separately using the omnidirectional rasterizer with restricted angular ranges, divides intensity by alpha to remove brightness inconsistencies, and combines the two in pixel space to produce the final omnidirectional image. The entire pipeline is training-free, directly loading pre-trained MVSplat weights without any fine-tuning on omnidirectional data.

## Results

OmniSplat achieves the best PSNR among all feed-forward methods on 4 of 6 datasets (OmniBlender: 21.22, Ricoh360: 19.72, 360Roam: 17.53, OmniScenes: 21.59) while running in just 0.532 s per scene — roughly 4× faster than MVSplat(P) (2.224 s) and 25× faster than PixelSplat(P) (13.068 s). With a small amount of per-scene optimization (OmniSplat+opt, 12.04 s), PSNR improves to 22.47 on OmniBlender and 23.12 on OmniScenes, surpassing the optimization-based ODGS baseline (1920 s) on most datasets. On OmniPhotos and 360VO, the optimization-based ODGS still edges out OmniSplat (20.25 vs 18.59 and 22.66 vs 20.63 PSNR respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniSplat outperforms all feed-forward baselines on PSNR for OmniBlender (21.22 vs. best 20.56 for PixelSplat(P)), Ricoh360 (19.72 vs. 19.36), 360Roam (17.53 vs. 16.54 for MVSplat(Y)), and OmniScenes (21.59 vs. 20.18), while also being the fastest at 0.53 s. It trails PixelSplat(P) on OmniPhotos (18.59 vs. 19.94) where the simpler scene structure favors the perspective-pair approach. Against the optimization-based ODGS (1920 s), OmniSplat+opt wins on OmniBlender (22.47 vs. 22.23), Ricoh360 (20.63 vs. 17.51), and OmniScenes (23.12 vs. 20.71) but loses on OmniPhotos (19.57 vs. 20.25) and 360VO (21.42 vs. 22.66). The omnidirectional-adapted variants (PixelSplat(O), MVSplat(O), DepthSplat(O)) all underperform their perspective counterparts, confirming that direct equirectangular processing degrades feature extraction. MVSplat(Y), which feeds four Yin-Yang images into the original MVSplat without the proposed cross-view attention, scores 18.43 on OmniBlender vs. OmniSplat's 21.22, validating the importance of the grid-aware attention mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feed-forward 3DGS estimation paradigm established by MVSplat, PixelSplat, and LatentSplat from perspective images to omnidirectional inputs, a domain where no prior feed-forward method existed. Rather than training a new network (which would require large-scale omnidirectional multiview data that does not yet exist), it repurposes MVSplat's pre-trained perspective weights in a training-free manner by decomposing equirectangular images into quasi-uniform Yin-Yang grids that closely resemble perspective projections. The Yin-Yang grid, originally from geophysics, is repurposed here as a geometric bridge that preserves CNN compatibility while eliminating the polar distortion that causes feature extraction failures in omnidirectional images. The method also introduces a corresponding Yin-Yang rasterizer to avoid the stripe artifacts that arise from non-uniform sampling in standard omnidirectional rasterization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OmniSplat fills a clear gap: omnidirectional cameras are increasingly used in VR/AR and robotics, yet no feed-forward 3DGS method could process their output directly. The training-free design is practically significant because it avoids the need for large omnidirectional training datasets and allows immediate adoption of future improvements to the underlying MVSplat backbone. The attention-based multiview segmentation as a byproduct of reconstruction is a useful bonus for 3D editing pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond two reference views, whether the interpolation artifacts in the Yang-to-omnidirectional transform can be eliminated, and whether meta-learning could further reduce the adaptation gap as the authors note.
