## Problem

Existing few-shot adaptation (FSA) methods for Vision-Language Models (VLMs) rely on prompt tuning or adapter-based PEFT, but little is known about the learning dynamics during such adaptation. The paper identifies that PEFT training naturally splits into two phases—initial task-level feature learning followed by over-specialization to available base categories—and that established methods degrade significantly when switching between base-to-novel and all-to-all evaluation settings, lacking a unified recipe.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251673/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images and category names formatted with a generic template ('a photo of a {}') as input, and produces a class label via cosine similarity between the visual embedding and category embeddings. A distinctive feature is category-level selective inference at test time: base categories are retrieved as rows of the learned classifier matrix in O(1), bypassing the text encoder entirely, while only novel categories require the adapted text encoder to compute their embeddings.

## Method

2SFS splits a fixed compute budget of m iterations into two stages. In the first stage (α×m iterations, with α=0.6), it tunes only the LayerNorm scale and shift parameters of both the visual and text encoders via standard contrastive loss, exploiting the empirically observed initial phase where PEFT learns generalizable task-level features before over-specializing. In the second stage ((1−α)×m iterations), it freezes the LayerNorm parameters and trains a linear classifier initialized with the first-stage text embeddings of base categories, optimizing only the classifier weights. This two-stage parameter switching preserves novel-category generalization while improving base-category discrimination. Hyperparameters (M=300, α=0.6) are fixed across all 11 datasets, 3 backbones, and both evaluation settings, and the approach matches or surpasses setting-specific state-of-the-art methods.

## Results

In the base-to-novel setting (ViT-B/16, k=16 shots), 2SFS achieves a harmonic mean of 80.20% averaged across 11 datasets, outperforming the strongest competitor MMA (79.87%) and CLIP-LoRA (77.28%). In the all-to-all setting, 2SFS achieves mean accuracies of 82.9% (ViT-B/16), 79.2% (ViT-B/32), and 87.1% (ViT-L/14), matching or surpassing CLIP-LoRA (83.0%, 78.9%, 86.9%) and MMA (81.0%, 76.7%, 84.4%) across all three backbones. Notable margins include +2.76% HM on Stanford Cars and +2.30% on FGVC Aircraft in base-to-novel, and up to +2.7% over MMA in all-to-all with ViT-L/14.

## Baselines & Comparisons

2SFS outperforms all 13 baselines on average in the base-to-novel setting, with the largest margins over CoOp (+8.5% HM) and CoCoOp (+4.4% HM), and a consistent +2.9% over CLIP-LoRA and +0.3% over MMA. In the all-to-all setting, 2SFS is competitive with CLIP-LoRA (the strongest adapter-based method), trailing by only 0.1% on ViT-B/16 but leading by +0.3% on ViT-B/32 and +0.2% on ViT-L/14, while surpassing MMA by +1.9% to +2.7% across backbones. Prompt-based methods (CoOp, CoCoOp, ProGrad, KgCoOp, MaPLe) show significant degradation in base-to-novel (e.g., CoOp drops to 63.22% novel accuracy) compared to their all-to-all performance, whereas 2SFS maintains stable performance across both settings. Adapter methods like TIP-Adapter-F, CLIP-Adapter, PLOT++, and TaskRes are only applicable in all-to-all; among these, PLOT++ (82.1% mean) and TaskRes (80.8% mean) are the closest competitors on ViT-B/16, both trailing 2SFS (82.9%). LP++ (80.5% mean) and CLIP-Adapter (75.5% mean) are further behind. The key differentiator is that 2SFS uses fixed hyperparameters (M=300, α=0.6) tuned once on ImageNet, while setting-specific SOTA methods like CLIP-LoRA and MMA degrade when transferred between base-to-novel and all-to-all.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant PEFT paradigms for VLM few-shot adaptation (prompt tuning à la CoOp/CoCoOp and adapter-based methods like CLIP-LoRA and MMA) by reviving the classical 'feature extractor + linear classifier' recipe, reframed through a new empirical observation. The key conceptual contribution is identifying a natural two-phase learning dynamic in PEFT—initial task-level feature extraction followed by specialization to available categories—and exploiting it by splitting a fixed compute budget between LayerNorm tuning and linear classifier training. Rather than building on a single prior method, 2SFS repurposes the well-known classifier tuning paradigm [39] with a principled stopping criterion derived from the observed breakpoint, and introduces category-level selective inference that has no direct analogue in prior prompt- or adapter-based approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-phase dynamic finding is broadly useful: it suggests that the field's reliance on ad-hoc generalization mechanisms (cross-modal prompting, weight sharing) may be partially unnecessary, since PEFT naturally learns transferable features before over-specializing. Practically, 2SFS offers a unified, fixed-hyperparameter recipe that works across both base-to-novel and all-to-all settings and three backbone scales, whereas setting-specific SOTA methods degrade when transferred. Open questions include whether the breakpoint can be detected automatically (rather than via a fixed α), whether the dynamic holds for non-CLIP VLMs, and whether the approach extends to other PEFT choices beyond LayerNorm and LoRA. The EuroSAT failure case (tiny base set) hints at sensitivity to the number of base categories.
