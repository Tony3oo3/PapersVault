## Problem

Learned video compression (LVC) has surpassed traditional codecs on LDR video, but HDR video compression remains unexplored in the learned setting due to two critical gaps: the absence of large-scale, diverse HDR video training datasets, and the inapplicability of existing LVC architectures to the unique floating-point, high-bit-depth nature of HDR content. Existing HDR datasets (JVET, HDM) are too small and limited in scene/motion diversity to support generalizable LVC training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired inputs: 16-bit floating-point HDR frames (1920×1080, 15-frame clips from 180-frame sequences) and their tone-mapped 8-bit LDR counterparts, both cropped to 256×256 patches during training. The output is a two-layer bit stream — a base layer (BL) decodable on LDR displays and an enhancement layer (EL) that, combined with BL, reconstructs full HDR frames. The EL bitstream is designed to be minimal by exploiting redundancy with the already-compressed BL rather than re-encoding HDR independently.

## Method

LBSVC uses DCVC-HEM as the BL to compress tone-mapped LDR video, then compresses HDR in the EL conditioned on BL code information (decoded motion vectors, temporal context features, and latent representations). The key innovation is the Bit-depth Enhancement Module (BEM), which extracts a dynamic range prior from HDR frames via a differentiable histogram layer using Gaussian threshold functions for luminance-range slicing, then aggregates this prior with BL features to produce bit-depth-enhanced representations that closely match original HDR statistics. The dynamic range prior (256 float-32 values for bin centers and widths) is transmitted losslessly at negligible cost. Training proceeds in three stages: BL training at four λ values, EL training with frozen BL, and joint two-layer fine-tuning with a weighted loss (ω_b=0.5 for BL distortion). This design lets the EL represent HDR content with far fewer bits than independent encoding, achieving 32.5% BD-rate savings over SHM on PU-SSIM.

## Results

On the JVET dataset, LBSVC achieves BD-Rate savings of −45.2% (PU-PSNR), −93.9% (PU-SSIM), −9.0% (VDP2), and −15.5% (VDP3) relative to the SHM baseline. On the HDM dataset, the corresponding savings are −49.8%, −32.5%, −7.9%, and −44.2%. The headline claim is a 32.5% bitrate saving over SHM in PU-SSIM on HDM. The model has 41.8M parameters and 257.35 GFLOPs, comparable to HEM* (35.04M / 217.1G) and LSSVC (29.4M / 246.48G).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LBSVC outperforms all four baselines on every metric where a comparison is available, on both JVET and HDM datasets. Against the traditional scalable codec SHM (the anchor), LBSVC saves 45–50% bitrate on PU-PSNR and 32–94% on PU-SSIM, while also achieving negative BD-Rate on both VDP2 and VDP3. Against Mai11 (a traditional backward-compatible method), LBSVC is superior on all four metrics; Mai11 actually incurs positive BD-Rate on VDP2 (14.2%/13.7%) and is only competitive on VDP3. Against HEM* (which encodes LDR and HDR layers independently), LBSVC wins on all metrics, with the largest gap on PU-SSIM (−93.9% vs −81.6% on JVET) and VDP2 (−9.0% vs +29.1% on JVET), showing that joint two-layer training with BEM is substantially better than independent encoding. Against the modified LSSVC (adapted from spatial to bit-depth scalability), LBSVC dominates on every metric, with LSSVC actually performing worse than SHM on PU-PSNR (+8.7%/+14.7%) and VDP2 (+30.4%/+43.4%), highlighting the difficulty of repurposing spatial-scalable architectures for bit-depth scalability without the dynamic-range prior mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper assembles a learned bit-depth scalable HDR video compression pipeline from three existing components: DCVC-HEM [31] as the Base Layer encoder, LSSVC's [7] hybrid temporal-layer context mining for the Enhancement Layer, and DeepIST's [3] differential histogram layer as the core of the novel Bit-depth Enhancement Module (BEM). It departs from traditional two-layer scalable codecs (SHM, Mai11) by replacing hand-crafted inter-layer prediction with end-to-end learned optimization, and from prior LVC methods by explicitly exploiting the bit-depth redundancy between tone-mapped LDR and 16-bit HDR content rather than treating HDR as a single-layer problem. The BEM's use of a compressed dynamic-range prior (256 float-32 values) to bridge the dynamic range gap between BL and EL features is the key architectural novelty, replacing the simple linear scaling or bit-consuming mapping used in prior scalable methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The HDRVD2K dataset (500 videos, 2200 clips) fills a critical gap that has blocked progress on learned HDR video compression, as prior HDR datasets (HDM, JVET, SJTU) were too small and limited in scene/motion diversity for training deep networks. The LBSVC framework demonstrates that LVC can substantially outperform traditional scalable codecs on HDR content, but the results are qualified: the large PU-SSIM/PU-PSNR gains partly reflect the PQ-domain advantage of learned methods over traditional codecs, while VDP2/VDP3 results show LVC is not universally superior across all perceptual metrics. Open questions include scalability beyond 256×256 patches and 1080p, real-time feasibility, and whether the dynamic-range-prior approach generalizes to other bit-depth or color-gamut scalability scenarios.
