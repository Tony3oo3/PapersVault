## Problem

CLIP's contrastive training creates a persistent modality gap between vision and text embeddings, where even paired embeddings occupy distinct regions in the shared space, limiting zero-shot image captioning. Existing remedies—noise injection into text embeddings or memory-bank similarity matching—either fail to fundamentally align the two modalities or introduce heavy computational overhead and scalability constraints. The paper asks whether a generative model trained solely on the text embedding distribution can directly transform vision embeddings into text-like representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252536/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, encodes it with a frozen CLIP-ViT-B/32 vision encoder to obtain a 512-dimensional vision embedding, and produces a free-form text caption via an autoregressive GPT-2 decoder. The vision embedding is first projected to 768 dimensions through a two-layer prefix projector and prepended with 10 learnable soft prompts before conditioning the decoder. No paired image-caption data is required at inference; the system operates entirely in the zero-shot regime on the Karpathy split of MSCOCO and Flickr30K.

## Method

Diffusion Bridge trains a 1-D UNet DDPM (T=1000 steps) exclusively on preprocessed text embeddings (L2-normalized, mean-centered, scaled by C=5) to learn the score function of the text embedding manifold. At inference, the vision embedding is injected at an intermediate reverse-diffusion timestep M (600 for COCO, 400 for Flickr30K), and the reverse process progressively denoises it toward the text distribution, exploiting the observation that the modality gap is well-approximated by Gaussian noise. The GPT-2 decoder is trained on diffusion-augmented text embeddings—text embeddings forward-diffused to step A and then reverse-diffused via DDIM—so it becomes robust to the non-unique, slightly stochastic mapping from vision to text space. This two-stage design (diffusion-bridging at inference + diffusion-augmented decoder training) achieves SOTA zero-shot captioning on MSCOCO without memory banks, entity parsers, or noise-injection heuristics.

## Results

On MSCOCO (Karpathy split), Diffusion Bridge achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot captioning with BLEU@1=72.3, BLEU@4=28.7, METEOR=25.1, ROUGE-L=52.4, CIDEr=96.4, and SPICE=18.7, outperforming the closest competitor C3 (CIDEr=93.3) by 3.1 points on CIDEr. On Flickr30K, it achieves BLEU@1=66.9, BLEU@4=21.4, METEOR=21.1, ROUGE-L=46.8, CIDEr=53.0, and SPICE=14.5, remaining competitive with memory-based methods like DeCap (CIDEr=56.7) while requiring no memory bank. Cross-domain transfer (MSCOCO→Flickr30K) yields BLEU@4=17.4 and METEOR=18.6, matching or exceeding all baselines on those metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MSCOCO, Diffusion Bridge outperforms all eight baselines across every reported metric. The closest competitor, C3 [45], trails by 1.3 BLEU@1, 1.0 BLEU@4, 3.1 CIDEr, and 0.4 SPICE; CapDec [28] (noise-injection approach) lags by 3.1 BLEU@1 and 4.6 CIDEr; and memory-based DeCap [15] is behind by 4.0 BLEU@4 and 5.2 CIDEr. On Flickr30K, the picture is more nuanced: DeCap [15] edges out Diffusion Bridge on METEOR (21.8 vs. 21.1), CIDEr (56.7 vs. 53.0), and SPICE (15.2 vs. 14.5), likely benefiting from its memory bank that retrieves from the full training set on this smaller dataset. However, Diffusion Bridge still leads on BLEU@4 (21.4 vs. 21.2) and reports BLEU@1 (66.9) and ROUGE-L (46.8) where DeCap provides no numbers. In cross-domain transfer (MSCOCO→Flickr30K), Diffusion Bridge ties ViECap [7] on BLEU@4 (17.4) and matches CapDec [28] on METEOR (18.6), while slightly exceeding both on CIDEr (38.5 vs. 38.4 and 35.7). Overall, the method's advantage is most pronounced on the larger MSCOCO dataset where the diffusion model can better capture the text embedding distribution, and it remains competitive without any memory bank or entity-driven prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) and the modality-gap analysis from C3 (Zhang et al., 2024), which established that the CLIP modality gap can be approximated as Gaussian noise. It departs from prior Early-Guidance Decoding methods (CapDec's noise injection, DeCap's memory-based projection) by instead training a diffusion model exclusively on text embeddings to learn the text distribution, then using the reverse diffusion process to progressively 'denoise' vision embeddings into the text space. The key conceptual departure is repurposing diffusion models—not for generation but as a learned transport between modalities—while retaining an autoregressive GPT-2 decoder for caption generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Diffusion Bridge offers a memory-free, entity-free alternative to existing zero-shot captioning methods, which is practically appealing for scalability and deployment. However, its advantage over C3 is modest (CIDEr 96.4 vs 93.3 on MSCOCO), and it trails DeCap on the smaller Flickr30K dataset where memory-based retrieval has a natural edge. Open questions include whether the Gaussian-noise approximation of the modality gap holds for other contrastive models or modalities, whether the approach extends to video or audio captioning, and whether the intermediate-step selection (M=600) generalizes across architectures or requires per-dataset tuning.
