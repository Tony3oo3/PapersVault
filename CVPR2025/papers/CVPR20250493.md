## Problem

Existing unsupervised methods for physical parameter estimation from video rely on encoder-decoder architectures that reconstruct frames, which constrains them to motion dynamics, requires large labeled or synthetic datasets, and is sensitive to initialization. No prior unsupervised approach handles non-motion dynamics (brightness, scale, deformation) or avoids the computational cost and architectural rigidity of frame reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250493/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single unannotated video (1920×1080 at 60 fps) with a known frame rate δt as input; no pixel labels, parameter annotations, or object masks are required for single-object scenes. It outputs the physical parameters γ of a known governing differential equation (e.g., damping and frequency coefficients) along with per-frame latent state estimates z ∈ R^d. The encoder is a lightweight 3-layer MLP rather than a CNN, and the model is trained on one video at a time per experimental setting, demonstrating data efficiency compared to baselines trained on 10,000+ videos.

## Method

The architecture consists of an MLP encoder that maps each frame to a d-dimensional latent state z_t, and a differentiable physics block that numerically integrates a known second-order ODE (via single-step Euler) with learnable parameters γ to predict the next latent state ẑ_{t+1}. The key innovation is a decoder-free loss computed entirely in latent space: an MSE term (L1) between the encoder's output at t+1 and the physics block's prediction, plus a KL-divergence term (L2) that constrains the batch statistics of z to match a Gaussian prior N(0,1), preventing collapse to the trivial zero solution. This eliminates the need for frame reconstruction, making the method applicable to any continuous dynamical system (motion, brightness, scale) without architecture changes. A learning rate schedule proportional to the initial parameter magnitude (lr ∝ 10^{|log₁₀(γ₀)|}) provides robustness to initialization, and the model generalizes to unseen time steps at test time due to the physics prior embedded in the ODE solver.

## Results

On the Delfys75 real-world dataset, the proposed method achieves near-ground-truth parameter estimates across all five dynamical systems. For the Torricelli experiment, it recovers k = 0.0094 ± 4e-4 (GT: 0.0095), while PAIG returns 0.99 ± 0.01 and NIRPI returns 0.21 ± 0.03 — both off by orders of magnitude. For the sliding block, the method estimates acceleration a = 1.29 ± 0.1 m/s² (GT: 1.441) versus 0.35 ± 0.03 for PAIG and −0.09 ± 0.88 for NIRPI. On non-motion systems (LED brightness decay γ and free-fall scale), only the proposed method is applicable, achieving γ = 0.97 ± 0.04 (GT: 0.92) and a = 9.51 ± 1.27 m/s² (GT: 9.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two unsupervised baselines: PAIG (Jaques et al., ICLR 2020) and NIRPI (Hofherr et al., WACV 2023). On the Torricelli experiment, the proposed method is dramatically superior, recovering k within 1–3% of ground truth (e.g., 0.0094 vs GT 0.0095), while PAIG returns values near its initialization (≈1.0) and NIRPI is off by roughly 20×. On the sliding block, the proposed method estimates acceleration within 10–15% of GT, whereas PAIG underestimates by ~75% and NIRPI produces near-zero or negative values with high variance. On the pendulum, the proposed method is closest to GT for the shortest and longest lengths (0.51 vs GT 0.45; 1.30 vs GT 1.50), while PAIG essentially fails to move from its initial value of 1.0. Critically, neither baseline supports non-motion dynamics (brightness, scale), so the proposed method is the only one evaluated on the LED and free-fall experiments, where it achieves reasonable accuracy (γ = 0.97 vs GT 0.92; a = 9.51 vs GT 9.8 m/s²). In the synthetic multi-object experiment (Figure 7), the proposed method robustly converges to k = 2.0 from both initializations (k₀ = 1.0 and 10.0), while both baselines fail to converge to the correct value from at least one initialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant encoder-decoder paradigm in unsupervised physical parameter estimation (PAIG, NIRPI), which relies on frame reconstruction via spatial transformers and is consequently limited to motion dynamics. Instead, it repurposes the KL-divergence term from VAEs not for latent sampling but as a regularizer that prevents the trivial zero-solution, eliminating the need for a decoder entirely. The physics block uses a simple Euler-step ODE solver in latent space, and the encoder is a plain MLP, making the architecture substantially lighter than prior auto-encoder-based approaches. The introduction of Delfys75 also breaks from the synthetic-data-only evaluation norm of prior work, providing real-world ground truth for motion, brightness, and scale dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the decoder and the spatial transformer, the method opens the door to dynamical systems beyond rigid-body motion (brightness decay, non-uniform scaling), which prior architectures structurally cannot handle. The Delfys75 dataset, while small, is the first real-world benchmark with annotated parameters for non-motion dynamics, enabling fair cross-method comparison in realistic conditions. Key limitations remain: the model is restricted to autonomous, differentiable ODEs of low order, cannot handle multiple interacting objects without masks, and performance degrades with pixel-level discretization. Extending to PDEs, non-autonomous forcing, and multi-object scenes are natural next steps.
