## Problem

Modeling the temporal evolution of Gaussian properties (position, rotation, scale) in dynamic scenes is difficult because the vast number of time-varying parameters is constrained by limited photometric data, leading to convergence issues. End-to-end neural networks can capture complex dynamics but lack explicit supervision for transformation fields, while handcrafted polynomial functions offer interpretability but poor generalizability across diverse scenes. The paper seeks a formulation that combines the flexibility of implicit networks with the explicitness of polynomial approximations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view video sequences (N3DV: 21 synchronized cameras, 300 frames per scene; Technicolor: 4×4 array of 16 cameras at up to 2048×1088 resolution) along with COLMAP-estimated camera poses and an initial point cloud. It produces a 4D Gaussian representation parameterized by time-dependent position, covariance, opacity, and spherical-harmonic coefficients, from which novel views can be rendered at arbitrary timestamps via tile-based rasterization. Training uses the Adam optimizer on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU.

## Method

The core contribution is a learnable infinite Taylor series that decomposes each Gaussian's temporal transformation into a third-order Taylor polynomial (capturing large-scale position, scale, and quaternion-orientation motion) plus a Peano remainder term learned by an MLP. Gaussians are partitioned into sparse Global Primitives (GPs) selected via farthest-point sampling and dense Local Primitives (LPs); the MLP predicts per-GP translation and rotation offsets, and LP offsets are interpolated from GPs using Linear Blend Skinning with Gaussian-kernel RBF weights to enforce spatial and temporal consistency. Opacity is modeled separately as a time-dependent RBF. This design yields a complete (non-approximate) Taylor series for motion estimation, avoiding the approximation error of truncated polynomials while retaining the interpretability of explicit polynomial terms.

## Results

On the N3DV dataset, the proposed method achieves the best PSNR across all four scenes (32.59, 33.12, 33.34, 33.06 dB), outperforming the closest competitor SWinGS by approximately 0.6–1.5 dB. On the Technicolor Light Field Dataset, the method reaches PSNR values of 34.72, 38.37, 35.30, and 38.91 dB, surpassing the next-best method (D3DGS) by 0.9–2.9 dB. The method also attains the highest SSIM (up to 0.990) and lowest LPIPS (as low as 0.008) across both datasets, indicating superior structural and perceptual fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 12 baselines on every scene and every metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) across both N3DV and Technicolor datasets. The closest competitor on N3DV is SWinGS [35], which the method surpasses by ~0.6–1.5 dB PSNR (e.g., 33.34 vs 32.18 on Flame Steak). Among Gaussian-based methods, 4DGS [39] and D3DGS [40] trail significantly: 4DGS lags by 4–8 dB PSNR (e.g., 25.04 vs 33.34 on Flame Steak), while D3DGS shows particularly weak results on Cook Spinach (20.53 dB) and Cut Roast Beef (22.35 dB). SC-GS [12] exhibits a catastrophic failure on Cut Roast Beef (PSNR 6.29, SSIM 0.007), suggesting instability in its sparse-control mechanism. On the Technicolor dataset, the method leads D3DGS [40] by 0.9–2.9 dB PSNR and STG [22] by 0.85–2.04 dB, while 4DGS [39] and FSGS [46] fall 7–15 dB behind. NeRF-based baselines (K-Planes, HexPlane, NeRFPlayer, StreamRF) are consistently outperformed by 1–4 dB PSNR, with StreamRF [18] showing the weakest LPIPS scores (0.147–0.162) among all methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting to dynamic scenes by introducing a learnable infinite Taylor series to model the temporal evolution of Gaussian properties (position, rotation, scale, opacity), positioning itself between two prior extremes: end-to-end MLP-based deformation fields (e.g., 4DGS, D3DGS) that lack explicit supervision, and handcrafted time-conditioned polynomials that lack generalizability. The dominant transformation is captured by a third-order Taylor expansion, while the Peano remainder is learned via an MLP operating on sparse Global Primitives and propagated to Local Primitives through Linear Blend Skinning with Gaussian-kernel RBF weights. This hybrid explicit-implicit decomposition is the paper's core architectural departure from prior dynamic 3DGS methods that rely entirely on neural deformation fields.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Taylor-series formulation provides a mathematically grounded and interpretable alternative to black-box deformation networks for 4D Gaussian dynamics, and the ablation confirms that the Peano remainder contributes a non-trivial ~1.5 dB PSNR gain, validating the need for the neural correction term beyond the polynomial approximation. However, the approach is tested only on two relatively small multi-view datasets (N3DV, Technicolor) with 300-frame sequences, and the authors themselves note that more complex, highly dynamic scenes may require further refinement. Open questions include whether the fixed third-order Taylor expansion generalizes to scenes with discontinuous or non-smooth motion, and whether the GP/LP decomposition with LBS scales to scenes with large topological changes or many independently moving objects.
