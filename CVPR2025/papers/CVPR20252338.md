## Problem

Recurrent stereo matching methods struggle with zero-shot generalization to real-world scenes due to occlusion, non-texture, and domain gaps between synthetic training data and real images. While monocular depth foundation models like Depth Anything V2 exhibit strong zero-shot generalization, they output only relative depth without metric scale, making them directly unusable for stereo disparity recovery. The paper addresses how to effectively incorporate these powerful monocular depth cues into a recurrent stereo framework to improve robustness without sacrificing metric accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a left-right RGB stereo image pair and produces a dense disparity map at the original image resolution. Feature extraction operates at 1/4 resolution (h×w where h=H/4, w=W/4), and the final disparity is upsampled from 1/4 to full resolution via convex combination. The DEFOM processes only the left image to generate both depth estimates (used for disparity initialization) and dense ViT features (used for matching and context), while the right image contributes only to the matching feature encoder.

## Method

DEFOM-Stereo integrates Depth Anything V2 into RAFT-Stereo through two mechanisms. First, a trainable DPT head initialized from the DEFOM backbone produces dense ViT features that are channel-aligned and element-wise added to plain CNN features, forming combined matching and context encoders at multiple resolutions. Second, the DEFOM's predicted depth is normalized by image width to initialize the recurrent disparity, and a novel scale update (SU) module is inserted before the conventional delta update (DU). The SU module uses a scale lookup (SL) that multiplies the current disparity by a set of predefined scale factors {1,2,4,6,8,10,12,16}/8 to sample correlation values across the full image width, enabling global scale recovery. The ConvGRU in SU predicts a dense per-pixel scale map that multiplicatively updates the disparity, while the subsequent DU with a 2-level pyramid lookup refines local details. Training uses 18 iterations (8 SU + 10 DU) with a standard L1 loss on Scene Flow, followed by domain-specific finetuning for benchmark submissions.

## Results

On zero-shot generalization (trained only on Scene Flow), DEFOM-Stereo (ViT-L) achieves thresholding error rates of 3.76 (KITTI 2012), 4.99 (KITTI 2015), 11.95 (Middlebury-full), 5.91 (Middlebury-half), 5.65 (Middlebury-quarter), and 2.35 (ETH3D), outperforming all baselines on every metric. On the official benchmarks, DEFOM-Stereo ranks 1st on multiple metrics: Bad 2.0 (noc) = 1.43 and AvgErr (noc) = 0.31 on KITTI 2012; D1-fg (noc) = 2.24 and D1-all (all) = 1.411 on KITTI 2015; Bad 2.0 (all) = 5.02 and AvgErr (all) = 1.27 on Middlebury; and Bad 1.0 (all) = 0.78 and AvgErr (all) = 0.11 on ETH3D. On the Robust Vision Challenge, DEFOM-Stereo_RVC achieves the best results across all three benchmarks simultaneously, e.g., D1-all (all) = 1.63 on KITTI 2015 and Bad 2.0 (all) = 6.90 on Middlebury.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEFOM-Stereo (ViT-L) outperforms all 28 compared baselines on every zero-shot generalization metric. The most dramatic gains are on Middlebury, where it reduces the 2-pixel error from 18.33 (RAFT-Stereo) to 11.95 (a 35% reduction) and from 35.82 (Selective-IGEV) to 11.95 (a 67% reduction), and on ETH3D where it improves from 3.28 (RAFT-Stereo) to 2.35 (28% reduction). On KITTI, the improvement over RAFT-Stereo is more modest (~13%), consistent with the driving domain being closer to the synthetic training distribution. On the official benchmarks, DEFOM-Stereo beats the strongest published methods: on KITTI 2012 it improves Bad 2.0 (all) from 1.80 (ViTAStereo) to 1.79 and AvgErr (noc) from 0.4 to 0.31; on Middlebury it reduces Bad 2.0 (all) from 6.04 (Selective-IGEV) to 5.02 (16.9% improvement); on ETH3D it reduces Bad 1.0 (all) from 1.03 (LoS) to 0.78 (24.3% improvement). On the Robust Vision Challenge, DEFOM-Stereo_RVC outperforms the previous best RVC models (CREStereo++_RVC and LoS_RVC) by approximately 25% on Middlebury metrics and 10-15% on KITTI 2015 and ETH3D, demonstrating that the gains are not domain-specific but reflect genuine robustness improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the recurrent stereo-matching lineage (RAFT → RAFT-Stereo → CREStereo/IGEV-Stereo/Selective-Stereo) by integrating a depth foundation model (Depth Anything V2) as both a feature source and a disparity initializer. Unlike prior ViT-based stereo methods (ViTAStereo, DN+ACVNet) that use attention-based adapters to convert generic ViT features into matching features, DEFOM-Stereo leverages the DPT head's dense features—already aligned to the depth task—and fuses them with plain CNN features via simple convolutional addition. It also departs from concurrent DEFOM-stereo works (Stereo Anywhere, Monster, FoundationStereo) that retain the 4D cost-volume initial disparity estimation; instead, this paper directly replaces the zero disparity initialization with the DEFOM depth and introduces a dedicated scale update module to correct the per-pixel scale inconsistency inherent in relative depth predictions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving simultaneous SOTA on KITTI 2012/2015, Middlebury, and ETH3D while also dominating the Robust Vision Challenge joint evaluation, DEFOM-Stereo establishes a new practical baseline for stereo matching and demonstrates that foundation-model features can substantially close the synthetic-to-real gap (e.g., >50% error reduction on Middlebury zero-shot). The discovery that Depth Anything V2 exhibits significant intra-image scale inconsistency—particularly on synthetic data—raises an open question about whether affine-invariant training objectives are fundamentally at odds with the pixel-accurate scale recovery needed for metric stereo. Remaining limitations include the reliance on relative (non-metric) depth, the added computational cost of a ViT backbone, and whether the scale update module generalizes to other foundation models or to multi-view settings.
