## Problem

Deep-learning shadow removal methods trained on limited paired datasets tend to overfit, producing degraded results on unseen scenes with complex global illumination. Existing diffusion-based approaches either require shadow masks or suffer from detail loss and misalignment when operating in latent space, making it difficult to simultaneously achieve thorough shadow elimination and preservation of high-frequency texture in non-shadow regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251928/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single shadowed RGB image as input and produces a shadow-free RGB image, requiring no shadow mask (mask-free). It handles high-resolution inputs up to 1920×1440 (as in WSRD+), with the LDM fine-tuning operating on 640×480 resized versions while the detail injection stage processes full resolution. Evaluation is conducted at 256×256 resolution following prior work conventions.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage fine-tuning pipeline that adapts a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model for image-conditioned shadow-free generation. In Stage 1, the VAE is frozen and the U-Net denoiser is fine-tuned in latent space, conditioned on the VAE-encoded features of the shadowed input, using z0-prediction (rather than standard ε-prediction) to reduce inference variance. In Stage 2, a shadow-aware Detail Injection module composed of RRDB layers modulates the frozen VAE decoder's intermediate features by selectively injecting high-frequency details from the VAE encoder's corresponding layers, with DINO v2 features concatenated to enhance generalizability. The DI module is shadow-aware because it receives concatenated features from both the shadowed input and the coarse shadow-free LDM output, allowing it to implicitly detect shadow regions and avoid re-introducing shadows as 'details.' This design leverages the generative priors of the pre-trained SD model for global context while recovering the high-frequency information lost during latent-space compression.

## Results

On the INS dataset, the proposed method achieves the highest PSNR (30.56) and SSIM (0.975) among all compared methods, outperforming the next-best mask-free method OmniSR (30.38/0.973). On ISTD+, it reaches 35.19 PSNR / 0.974 SSIM, the best among mask-free methods, trailing only mask-based HomoFormer (35.72) and ShadowFormer (35.46). On WSRD+ (1920×1440 high-resolution), it achieves 26.26 PSNR / 0.827 SSIM, surpassing OmniSR (26.07/0.835). Cross-dataset evaluation (e.g., ISTD+→SRD: 26.23 PSNR) further confirms superior generalizability over all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among mask-free methods, the proposed approach wins on INS (30.56 vs. OmniSR 30.38 PSNR) and WSRD+ (26.26 vs. OmniSR 26.07 PSNR), and is the best mask-free method on ISTD+ (35.19 vs. OmniSR 33.34). On SRD, DeS3 (34.11 PSNR) slightly edges out the proposed method (33.63), though the authors note their method produces visually superior shadow removal with lower output variance (0.160 vs. 1.075). Against mask-based methods, the proposed method trails HomoFormer (35.72) and ShadowFormer (35.46) on ISTD+ by 0.5–0.6 dB, but surpasses all other mask-based baselines. In cross-dataset evaluation (Table 3), the method achieves the best PSNR in all three transfer settings (ISTD+→SRD: 26.23, SRD→ISTD+: 30.23, INS→WSRD+: 20.11), outperforming OmniSR, ShadowDiffusion, and Refusion, demonstrating stronger generalization to unseen shadow patterns and lighting conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of diffusion-based image restoration and shadow removal, building directly on the pre-trained Stable Diffusion architecture (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) and the z0-prediction variance-reduction strategy from Lotus (He et al., 2024). It departs from prior diffusion-based shadow removal methods (ShadowDiffusion, DeS3, Refusion) that train their diffusion components from scratch on limited shadow datasets, instead leveraging the rich visual priors of a billion-image pre-trained model through a two-stage fine-tuning pipeline. The detail injection stage, which uses VAE encoder features and DINO v2 features to modulate the VAE decoder via RRDB modules, is a novel architectural contribution not present in prior latent-space restoration work like Refusion, which relies on hidden connections within a single U-Net.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent practical limitation in shadow removal: poor generalization due to limited training data diversity. By demonstrating consistent cross-dataset superiority (e.g., ISTD+→SRD, INS→WSRD+), it suggests that pre-trained generative priors can partially substitute for diverse training data, a finding with implications beyond shadow removal for other data-scarce restoration tasks. However, the authors acknowledge that the method still struggles with self-shadows in complex scenes and cannot fully preserve exposure in non-shadow regions, leaving open the question of whether unsupervised or self-supervised signals could close this gap. The practical relevance is further strengthened by the mask-free operation and ability to handle high-resolution (1920×1440) images without patch-based processing.
