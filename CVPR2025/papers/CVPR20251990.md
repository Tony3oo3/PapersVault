## Problem

Existing video diffusion methods treat each frame as an independent diffusion process, sampling separate random priors per frame and thereby breaking temporal correlations. This lack of a principled diffusion mechanism along the frame sequence leads to nonsmoothness and inconsistency in generated videos, particularly under large appearance variations. The paper addresses this by asking: can we derive a mathematically tractable diffusion process that jointly propagates noise across both spatial and temporal dimensions?

## Input / Output

The model operates in a VAE-encoded latent space and generates 16-frame video sequences at 128×128 (prediction) or 256×256 (generation) resolution. For unconditional/video prediction, input is random Gaussian noise (or a conditioning initial frame); for text-to-video, a CLIP-encoded text prompt is injected via cross-attention. Inference uses 250 accelerated DDIM-style steps (down from 1000 training steps) with a temporal coupling coefficient ρ = 1 − 10⁻³, and the network outputs two channel components per frame: a spatial noise estimate ε_θ and a temporal frame prediction φ_θ.

## Method

STDD defines a forward dual diffusion where each frame's noised state z_t^i depends on both its own spatial Gaussian noise and the previous frame's state z_{t−1}^{i−1} through a temporal mixing coefficient ρ, yielding a closed-form expression (Proposition 1) with binomial-weighted accumulation of historical frames. The reverse process is derived as a tractable Gaussian posterior, and a DDIM-style accelerated sampler eliminates the need for the inverse operator F_t⁻¹ by decomposing the update into a spatial component u_t^i (standard denoising) and a temporal bridge v_t^i (frame-to-frame difference computed from the predicted clean frame). Training uses a transformer backbone in VAE latent space with a combined loss of KL divergence on the spatio-temporal posterior and MSE on both the noise prediction and the clean-frame prediction. The key architectural insight is that the single network outputs ε_θ and φ_θ as different channel groups, so the temporal coupling is learned end-to-end without a separate temporal network.

## Results

On UCF101, STDD achieves an FVD of 258, outperforming the next-best method Latte+IMG (333) by 22.5% and LVDM (372) by 30.6%. On Sky Time-Lapse, STDD reaches FVD 42.1, marginally beating Latte+IMG (42.7) and substantially outperforming DIGAN (83.1) and StyleGAN-V (79.5). On the MHAD video prediction benchmark, STDD attains FVD 195 and cFVD 411, surpassing the strongest baseline LFDM (214 / 426). Inference time remains comparable to Latte (23 s vs 20 s for 250 steps on an RTX 3090).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STDD outperforms all seven baselines on both UCF101 and Sky Time-Lapse. The largest margins are against GAN-based and VQ-VAE-based methods: VideoGPT (2880), MoCoGAN-HD (1729), DIGAN (1630), StyleGAN-V (1431), and PVDM (1141) on UCF101, all trailing STDD by more than 4× in FVD. Among diffusion-based competitors, STDD beats LVDM by 30.6% on UCF101 (258 vs 372) and by 55.8% on Sky Time-Lapse (42.1 vs 95.2). The closest competitor is Latte+IMG, which STDD edges out on UCF101 (258 vs 333) and matches within 1.4% on Sky Time-Lapse (42.1 vs 42.7). On the MHAD video-prediction task, STDD (FVD 195, cFVD 411) surpasses LFDM (214 / 426), VDM (295 / 531), LDM (337 / 594), and ImaGINator (889 / 1406), with the most notable qualitative advantage in reducing head artifacts during large-motion transitions.

## Relation to Prior Work

STDD extends the DDPM/DDIM framework by introducing a principled temporal Markov chain alongside the standard spatial diffusion, creating a 'dual' forward/reverse process where historical frame information is explicitly propagated through a temporal diffusion operator F_t. This departs from prior video diffusion methods (VDM, LDM, VideoFusion) that either treat each frame as an independent diffusion process or decompose noise into shared and residual components without a closed-form temporal coupling. The accelerated sampling strategy adapts DDIM's non-Markovian formulation to the dual setting, and the architecture leverages the latent diffusion (Stable Diffusion) and transformer (Latte) backbones for practical implementation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit temporal diffusion formulation offers a mathematically clean answer to the long-standing problem of frame-to-frame consistency in video diffusion, and the closed-form g(t,i) expression makes the temporal information flow analyzable. However, the empirical validation is limited to short 16-frame clips at modest resolutions (128×128 to 256×256), and the FVD improvements over the strongest baseline (Latte) are marginal on Sky Time-Lapse (42.1 vs 42.7) while more substantial on UCF101 (258 vs 333). Open questions include whether the dual diffusion framework scales to longer sequences and higher resolutions, whether the 868M parameter budget is necessary, and whether the qualitative text-to-video results on WebVid-10M translate to competitive quantitative performance against larger-scale models.
