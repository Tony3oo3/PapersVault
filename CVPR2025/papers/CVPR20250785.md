## Problem

Generating 4D videos (grids of frames varying in both time and viewpoint) remains challenging because existing approaches either train on limited 4D datasets like Objaverse and fail to generalize to photorealistic scenes, or rely on slow optimization-based score distillation requiring hours per output. Prior architectures that sequentially interleave temporal and view attention also suffer from distributional shifts between the two update types, producing artifacts and reduced multi-view consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250785/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two conditional videos: a fixed-view video (first row of the grid, capturing temporal motion from one camera) and a freeze-time video (first column, capturing viewpoint changes at one timestamp). These can be real recordings or outputs from any text-to-video model, requiring no explicit camera poses. The output is a complete 8×8 frame grid at 288×512 resolution where each row is a fixed-view video and each column is a freeze-time video, generated in approximately 1 minute; larger grids are produced autoregressively via sliding windows along both axes.

## Method

The core architecture splits all frame tokens into two parallel streams—temporal (x^t) and view (x^v)—each processed independently through pre-trained DiT transformer layers to preserve the quality of the base video model. After every transformer block, a synchronization layer exchanges information between streams: hard synchronization computes a learned weighted average of the two token sets, while soft synchronization uses a diffusion-time-modulated linear layer to predict asymmetric updates that nudge the streams toward agreement without merging them. Training uses rectified flow velocity matching, first on large-scale pseudo-4D data (2D affine-transformed videos simulating camera motion) for 20k iterations, then fine-tuned for only 3k iterations on ~15K animated Objaverse renders, with only the synchronization layer weights updated during fine-tuning to avoid shifting the video distribution. This design avoids the out-of-distribution problem of sequential interleaving while remaining data-efficient and fast at inference.

## Results

On the 200-pair test set (generated via Snap Video Model), 4Real-Video (Soft Sync) achieves FID 78.36, CLIP 28.22, FVD 906.16 (time) / 1036.00 (view), and FVD-Test 308.15 (time) / 261.02 (view), outperforming SV4D (FID 204.81, FVD 1053.10/1245.42) and MotionCtrl (FID 87.10, FVD 1556.36/1509.76) across all metrics. On VideoScore, the full method scores 2.43/2.42 (visual quality), 2.41/2.36 (temporal consistency), and 2.38/2.36 (factual consistency) for time/view axes respectively. In a user study against four optimization-based methods (4D-Fy, Dream-in-4D, AYG, 4Real), 4Real-Video is preferred on all seven criteria (motion realism, foreground/background quality, shape realism, general quality, motion quality, video-text alignment) by a large margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SV4D [41], 4Real-Video achieves substantially lower FID (78.36 vs. 204.81), higher CLIP (28.22 vs. 19.46), and lower FVD on both axes (906/1036 vs. 1053/1245), with the gap widening on FVD-Test (308/261 vs. 815/324); SV4D also produces visibly distorted and blurred frames under camera motion. Against MotionCtrl [39], 4Real-Video wins on FID (78.36 vs. 87.10), CLIP (28.22 vs. 20.20), and FVD (906/1036 vs. 1556/1510), as MotionCtrl generates freeze-time videos independently per frame, ignoring temporal dependencies and producing incoherent results. In the user study against four optimization-based methods—4D-Fy [2], Dream-in-4D [50], AYG [18], and 4Real [46]—4Real-Video is preferred on all seven rating criteria by a large margin, attributed to its feedforward speed (~1 min vs. hours) and photorealistic quality. The internal ablation further shows that the sequential interleaving design (Eq. 5) degrades FVD to 1662/1797 and FVD-Test to 897/597, confirming that the two-stream parallel architecture with soft synchronization is critical for both quality and multi-view consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DiT-based video diffusion architecture (Peebles & Xie, 2022) and the Snap Video Model (Menapace et al., CVPR 2024) into a 4D generation setting by splitting video tokens into two parallel streams—one for temporal updates and one for viewpoint updates—connected by a synchronization layer. The key conceptual departure is from prior 4D video models (SV4D, 4Diffusion, CVD) that sequentially interleave cross-view and cross-time attention, which the authors argue causes out-of-distribution token propagation. The synchronization layer design is explicitly motivated by optimization literature (projected gradient descent for hard sync, ADMM for soft sync), providing a principled alternative to ad-hoc interleaving. The work also positions itself against both optimization-based SDS methods (4D-Fy, Dream-in-4D, AYG, 4Real) and camera-aware video generation (MotionCtrl), arguing that a complete space-time grid offers advantages neither paradigm easily provides.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~1-minute inference time for an 8×8 grid at 288×512 resolution (versus hours for SDS-based methods) makes photorealistic 4D video generation practically accessible, and the data efficiency (only ~15K Objaverse renders for fine-tuning) lowers the barrier to entry. However, the 600M-parameter base model constrains visual quality and resolution, the framework lacks 360° support, and freeze-time generation remains fragile for highly dynamic content (running animals, fire). A key open question is whether the two-stream + synchronization paradigm scales gracefully to much larger video models (e.g., 10B+ parameters) and whether explicit 3D representations can be produced in a single feedforward pass rather than requiring post-hoc 3DGS fitting.
