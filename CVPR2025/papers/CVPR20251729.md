## Problem

Existing medical image segmentation foundation models (e.g., SAM-Med3D, MedSAM-2) fail to generalize to 3D blood vessel segmentation due to the unique complexity of vascular networks and large domain gaps across imaging modalities, protocols, and anatomical regions. Supervised vessel segmentation methods require tedious voxel-level annotations for each new dataset, and no prior foundation model has achieved robust zero-shot generalization across the full spectrum of clinically and pre-clinically relevant vascular imaging.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251729/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes 3D medical image volumes cropped to 128³ patches as input, spanning a wide range of modalities (MRA, CTA, CT, vEM, OCTA, X-ray, light-sheet and two-photon microscopy) and anatomical regions (brain, liver, kidney, heart, bladder) in humans, mice, and rats. It produces a binary, voxel-level 3D segmentation mask of the blood vessel network. Evaluation is performed in zero-shot (no fine-tuning), one-shot (fine-tune on 1 patch), and few-shot (fine-tune on 3 patches) settings, with performance measured via Dice and topology-aware centerline Dice (clDice).

## Method

vesselFM uses a standard 3D UNet (MONAI nnU-Net re-implementation) as its segmentation backbone, trained in a supervised manner on three heterogeneous data sources: (1) D_real, a curated collection of 115,000+ real 128³ image-mask patches from 23 annotated datasets; (2) D_drand, 500,000 synthetic pairs generated via a domain randomization pipeline that applies spatial and artifact transformations to corrosion-cast-derived vascular geometries, composites them onto Perlin-noise or geometric backgrounds, and applies diverse intensity augmentations; and (3) D_flow, 10,000 image-mask pairs sampled from a mask- and class-conditioned flow matching generative model (extending Med-DDPM by replacing diffusion with a time-linear ODE-based velocity field, trained on both D_real and D_drand). Training uses weighted sampling (70% D_drand, 20% D_real, 10% D_flow), and the key insight is that the interplay of real anatomical diversity, broad domain randomization, and data-driven generative augmentation produces features robust enough for zero-shot transfer to entirely unseen vascular imaging domains.

## Results

In the zero-shot setting, vesselFM achieves Dice scores of 74.66 (SMILE-UHURA), 67.49 (BvEM), 46.94 (OCTA), and 29.69 (MSD8), with corresponding clDice scores of 75.27, 62.04, 67.07, and 36.14. In the few-shot setting (3 patches), vesselFM reaches 78.77 Dice on SMILE-UHURA, 78.11 on BvEM, 75.70 on OCTA, and 45.04 on MSD8. Notably, vesselFM's zero-shot performance on SMILE-UHURA (74.66 Dice) surpasses the few-shot results of all baselines on the same dataset, and it outperforms VISTA3D by 5.86 Dice on MSD8 despite VISTA3D being trained on 11,454 CT volumes including MSD8 data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

vesselFM outperforms all four baselines on every dataset and every task (zero-, one-, and few-shot) by a substantial margin. In the zero-shot setting, the gap is most dramatic: on SMILE-UHURA, vesselFM scores 74.66 Dice versus 48.32 for tUbeNet (the strongest baseline), 5.05 for VISTA3D, 2.12 for SAM-Med3D, and 3.85 for MedSAM-2. The general-purpose segmentation models SAM-Med3D and MedSAM-2 essentially fail in zero-shot, scoring below 15 Dice on three of four datasets, confirming that generic medical segmentation foundation models do not transfer to vascular structures. VISTA3D, despite being CT-specific and trained on 11,454 CT volumes (including MSD8 data), is outperformed by vesselFM by 5.86 Dice on MSD8 in zero-shot and by 3.14 Dice in few-shot, while it collapses on non-CT modalities (e.g., 0.94 Dice on BvEM). tUbeNet, the only vessel-specific baseline, remains competitive on SMILE-UHURA in few-shot (56.31 vs. 78.77 Dice) but struggles severely on BvEM (5.41 Dice) and MSD8 (17.67 Dice) where vessel-background contrast is low.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the foundation model paradigm from general-purpose medical segmentation (SAM-Med3D, MedSAM-2, VISTA3D) to the specific and challenging domain of 3D blood vessel segmentation, where those general models demonstrably fail. It adapts domain randomization strategies from SynthSeg and AnyStar to vascular geometry, and introduces flow matching (building on Med-DDPM's diffusion-based approach) for mask- and class-conditioned 3D vascular image generation. The segmentation backbone is a standard nnU-Net, positioning the contribution squarely in data curation and training strategy rather than architectural novelty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By open-sourcing a zero-shot-capable foundation model for 3D vessel segmentation, this work removes the need for per-dataset voxel-level annotations that currently burden vascular imaging research and clinical deployment. The three-source training recipe (real + domain-randomized + flow-matching-generated data) provides a practical template for building task-specific foundation models in other anatomically structured domains. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-class or instance-level vessel segmentation, whether the relatively weak MSD8 (CT liver) results indicate a fundamental limitation for certain contrast regimes, and whether graph-level post-processing can further improve connectivity metrics.
