## Problem

Existing learning-based registration methods rely on spatially-shared convolution kernels that apply uniform weights across all regions of feature maps, failing to capture spatially varying anatomical information in non-local regions. Since deformation in medical image registration is closely tied to tissue morphology, different anatomical regions require different levels of attention, yet vanilla convolutions cannot provide this region-specific adaptation, leading to suboptimal deformation field estimation particularly for large deformations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251348/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes a pair of 3D volumetric images (moving and fixed) at resolutions of 160×192×224 (IXI brain MRI), 160×192×160 (LPBA brain MRI), or 192×160×224 (Abdomen CT) and outputs a dense 3D deformation field mapping corresponding coordinates between the pair. Training is fully unsupervised, optimizing NCC similarity between the warped moving image and the fixed image plus an L2 gradient smoothness regularization term, with no ground-truth deformation fields or labels required.

## Method

The core contribution is a 3D Spatial-Awareness Convolution Block (SACB) that first unfolds feature maps into local k×k×k patches, computes spatial means, and applies unsupervised K-Means clustering to partition voxels into N spatial regions based on feature similarity. An MLP-based adaptive kernel generator then produces region-specific convolution weights (via element-wise modulation of a global kernel) and biases from each cluster's centroid, enabling distinct convolution operations per spatial region. SACB is integrated into a 5-level pyramid flow estimator where a shared encoder extracts multi-scale features, and at each scale (5→2), SACB refines both moving and fixed features before a similarity-matching module (dot-product over unfolded neighborhoods with softmax) estimates sub-deformation flows that are composed coarse-to-fine. The final scale uses two convolutional layers instead of matching due to the limited 1-voxel search range at full resolution.

## Results

On the IXI atlas-based brain registration task (30 ROIs), SACB-Net achieves the best Dice of 0.769 with 1.11M parameters, outperforming the best U-Net-based method LKU (0.765) and the best pyramid method Im2Grid (0.761). On the LPBA inter-subject brain registration task (54 ROIs), SACB-Net achieves Dice 0.731, HD95 5.862 mm, and ASSD 1.326 mm, surpassing the second-best ModeT by 1% Dice, 0.107 mm HD95, and 0.049 mm ASSD. On the challenging Abdomen CT inter-subject registration (13 ROIs), SACB-Net achieves Dice 0.588, HD95 18.253 mm, and ASSD 5.003 mm, outperforming the second-best ModeT by 3.8% Dice and 2.098 mm HD95.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SACB-Net achieves the best Dice on all three datasets: 0.769 on IXI (vs. LKU 0.765, Fourier-Net 0.763), 0.731 on LPBA (vs. ModeT 0.721, Im2Grid 0.713), and 0.588 on Abdomen CT (vs. ModeT 0.550, LapIRN 0.522). On LPBA, SACB-Net also leads in HD95 (5.862 mm vs. ModeT 5.969 mm) and ASSD (1.326 mm vs. ModeT 1.375 mm), while on IXI it is competitive but slightly behind Fourier-Net (2.857 mm) and LKU (2.967 mm) on HD95. The advantage is most pronounced on the Abdomen CT task with large deformations, where SACB-Net outperforms the second-best ModeT by 3.8% Dice and 2.098 mm HD95, and outperforms U-Net-based methods like TransMorph by 14.4% Dice. In terms of efficiency, SACB-Net uses only 1.11M parameters, substantially fewer than TransMorph (46.77M) and RDN (28.65M), while matching or exceeding their accuracy. The deformation smoothness (|J|<0%) is reasonable at 0.083% on IXI and 0.018% on LPBA, though slightly higher than the near-zero values of B-Spline-Diff and RDN, reflecting a trade-off between accuracy and strict diffeomorphism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the content-adaptive convolution paradigm from 2D remote sensing pansharpening (Duan et al., CVPR 2024) to 3D medical image registration, replacing spatially-shared convolution kernels with region-specific adaptive kernels generated via K-Means feature clustering. It is positioned within the pyramid coarse-to-fine registration lineage (LapIRN, PRNet++, Im2Grid, RDN, ModeT) but introduces a distinct feature-refinement mechanism (SACB) that operates at each pyramid level before similarity matching. Conceptually, it departs from the standard U-Net registration framework (VoxelMorph, TransMorph, LKU) by arguing that shared kernels are fundamentally suboptimal when different anatomical regions require different feature processing, drawing on the broader adaptive/dynamic convolution literature (Dynamic Filter Networks, Dynamic Region-aware Convolution) previously unexplored in 3D registration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SACB-Net demonstrates that spatially adaptive feature processing, rather than simply larger receptive fields or transformer attention, can improve registration accuracy with a compact model (1.11M parameters), and the plug-in nature of SACB makes it applicable to other registration architectures. The most notable finding is that the benefit of spatial-awareness is largest for large-deformation tasks (Abdomen CT: +3.8% Dice over second-best), suggesting that region-specific processing is particularly valuable when global alignment is challenging. Limitations include the unsupervised K-Means step adding training overhead (~80% increase in training time), a slightly higher folding rate on Abdomen CT (3.93% vs. 1.53% for RDN), and the reliance on a fixed number of clusters that may not capture all spatial heterogeneity. Open questions include whether learned or differentiable clustering could replace K-Means, whether the approach generalizes to 2D or multi-modal registration, and whether the clustering granularity should be content-dependent rather than fixed.
