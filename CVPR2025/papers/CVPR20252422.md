## Problem

Existing federated learning methods for low-dose CT reconstruction are typically tailored to specific scanning protocols and fail to generalize across diverse noise distributions caused by different protocols and patient anatomies. Moreover, prior personalized FL approaches do not address the critical challenge of unseen clients with novel scanning protocols, leading to significant performance degradation in new domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252422/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three types of input: a low-dose CT image (512×512 axial slices from the NIH-AAPM-Mayo Clinic dataset), a scanning protocol vector encoding physical parameters (number of views, detector bins, pixel length, source-to-center distance, incident photon count, etc.), and a radiology report generated by miniGPT-Med from the CT image, whose text encoder features serve as an anatomy prompt. The output is a normal-dose CT image at the same spatial resolution, reconstructed through a client-specific decoder that projects dual-level personalized features back to the image domain.

## Method

SCAN-PhysFed employs a shared encoder (RED-CNN or Uformer) to extract robust imaging features, which are then personalized at two levels via physics-informed hypernetworks: an anatomy-informed hypernetwork processes MLLM-derived radiology report features through pooling, multi-head attention, and a Hadamard product to spatially modulate imaging features, while a scanning-informed hypernetwork maps the protocol vector through an MLP to produce channel-wise scaling (α) and shifting (β) parameters. Client-specific decoders project the dual-level personalized features back to the image domain, and an orthogonal loss enforces discriminability among protocol code vectors. For unseen protocols, the Protocol Vector Quantization Strategy (PVQS) matches the new protocol code to its nearest neighbor in a stored codebook, routing to the corresponding client decoder to prevent distribution shift and network collapse.

## Results

On the 2016 NIH-AAPM-Mayo Clinic LDCT Grand Challenge dataset (8 training clients, 2 test patients), SCAN-PhysFed achieves an average PSNR of 41.14 dB and SSIM of 97.62% across 8 known clients using a RED-CNN backbone, outperforming the strongest baseline FedFDD (40.05 dB / 95.79%) by ~1.1 dB and ~1.8%. On 4 unseen protocols, SCAN-PhysFed maintains 38.55 dB / 93.04% average, again leading FedFDD (37.27 dB / 89.87%) by a clear margin. The method also generalizes to a Uformer (transformer) backbone, where it again achieves the best average PSNR and SSIM among all compared FL methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCAN-PhysFed outperforms all 11 baselines on average PSNR and SSIM across both known and unseen clients. Among personalized FL methods, FedFDD [6] is the strongest competitor (40.05 dB / 95.79% on known clients; 37.27 dB / 89.87% on unseen), but SCAN-PhysFed still leads by ~1.1 dB PSNR and ~1.8% SSIM on known clients and by ~1.3 dB / ~3.2% on unseen protocols. HyperFed [37] and FedMRI [8] trail further behind at 37.26 / 36.99 dB average PSNR, while generic FL methods (FedAvg, FedProx, FedNova, MOON, FedDG, FedKD) cluster around 33.9–35.3 dB and show the largest per-client variance, particularly on sparse-view protocols (Clients #2, #4, #6). A notable finding is that personalized methods degrade more sharply on unseen clients than generic ones (e.g., FedMRI drops to 31.71 dB average on unseen vs. 36.99 dB on known), whereas SCAN-PhysFed's PVQS strategy limits this drop to only ~2.6 dB, confirming the robustness of the protocol codebook quantization approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the personalized federated learning line for CT imaging (HyperFed, FedFDD) by introducing a dual-level physics-informed hypernetwork that modulates shared encoder features with both scanning-protocol vectors and LLM-generated radiology report embeddings. It departs from prior FL-CT work in two key ways: (1) repurposing a medical LLM (miniGPT-Med) to produce anatomy-specific spatial prompts via Hadamard-product modulation, and (2) proposing Protocol Vector Quantization (PVQS) to handle unseen scanning protocols by nearest-neighbor codebook lookup, a problem explicitly noted as overlooked by prior methods. The imaging backbones (RED-CNN, Uformer) are standard; the novelty lies in the personalization mechanism and the FL training paradigm with orthogonal code regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a clinically relevant gap—maintaining LDCT quality across heterogeneous scanner protocols while preserving patient privacy—and offers a practical deployment story via PVQS for new scanners. However, validation is limited to a single 2D dataset (NIH-AAPM-Mayo, 10 patients), and the 'physics-informed' framing is somewhat generous: the scanning prompt is simply a 7-dimensional parameter vector fed to an MLP, and the anatomy prompt is a text-encoder embedding from a frozen MLLM rather than a true physical model of attenuation. Open questions include whether the LLM component adds meaningful value over simpler anatomical segmentation features, whether the approach scales to 3D volumetric CT or other modalities (MRI, as the authors note), and whether PVQS degrades gracefully as the protocol space grows beyond the codebook size.
