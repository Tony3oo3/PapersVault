## Problem

Diffusion models can adjust inference-time compute by varying the number of denoising steps, but performance gains plateau after a few dozen NFEs, limiting the benefit of simply scaling up computation. Unlike LLMs, where inference-time scaling via search has been extensively studied, diffusion models lack a systematic framework for investing additional compute at inference time beyond denoising steps to further improve generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252690/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a pre-trained diffusion model (SiT-XL at 256×256 for ImageNet, FLUX.1-dev for text-to-image), a conditioning signal (class label or text prompt), and a verifier model that scores candidate generations. It outputs a single selected image, chosen from multiple candidates generated by running the ODE sampler on different initial Gaussian noises. The search budget is measured in total NFEs (denoising + search), and the method operates in the noise space rather than directly modifying the model or the image.

## Method

The paper formulates inference-time scaling as a search problem over initial Gaussian noises, structured along two design axes: verifiers (oracle, supervised via CLIP/DINO classifiers, self-supervised via feature similarity, or ensembles of Aesthetic Score/CLIPScore/ImageReward) that score candidates, and algorithms (Random Search as Best-of-N, Zero-Order Search that iteratively refines a pivot noise in a local neighborhood, and Search over Paths that expands trajectories at intermediate denoising steps) that find better noises. The key insight is that different initial noises deterministically map to different samples, so investing NFEs in searching for preferable noises—guided by verifier feedback—yields substantial quality gains beyond what additional denoising steps provide. The paper demonstrates that no single verifier-algorithm combination is universally optimal: task-specific configurations are needed, and verifier hacking (overfitting to a single verifier's bias) is mitigated by local search algorithms and verifier ensembles.

## Results

On DrawBench with FLUX.1-dev, random search with Verifier Ensemble at 3840 NFEs search budget improves Aesthetic Score, CLIPScore, ImageReward, and LLM Grader over the no-search baseline. The headline practical result (Table 3) shows that PixArt-Σ with search at ~0.09× the compute of FLUX.1-dev matches or exceeds FLUX on all four metrics (Aesthetic 6.03 vs 5.79, CLIP 0.71 vs 0.71, ImageReward 0.97 vs 0.97, LLM Grader 85.62 vs 84.29), and at ~2.59× compute it surpasses FLUX by a wide margin (Aesthetic 6.20, CLIP 0.73, ImageReward 1.15, LLM Grader 86.95). On T2I-CompBench, ImageReward as verifier yields the best gains across all compositional categories (e.g., Spatial 0.3151 vs 0.2429 baseline), while Aesthetic Score causes metric degradation. On ImageNet, Random Search with DINO-LinearHead improves IS over simply scaling denoising steps, and Zero-Order Search with N=4 effectively approximates the local optimum for FID.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FLUX.1-dev (the frontier text-to-image model), PixArt-Σ with search at only ~9% of the compute matches FLUX on CLIPScore (0.71) and ImageReward (0.97) while exceeding it on Aesthetic (6.03 vs 5.79) and LLM Grader (85.62 vs 84.29); at ~2.6× compute, PixArt-Σ clearly wins on all four metrics. Against PixArt-Σ without search, the search procedure recovers the CLIP and ImageReward gap (0.68→0.71 and 0.70→0.97) while further boosting Aesthetic and LLM Grader, demonstrating that inference-time compute can offset the model-size gap. Against DPO-finetuned SDXL (Wallace et al., CVPR 2024), search is complementary: DPO alone lifts PickScore from 22.39 to 22.54, search alone lifts it to 23.41, and the combination (DPO+Search) achieves the best 23.54, confirming that search can further shift sample modes even after the model distribution has been preference-aligned.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the inference-time scaling paradigm from LLMs (Brown et al. 2024, Snell et al. 2024) to diffusion models, but departs from the trivial analogue of simply increasing denoising steps, which plateaus quickly. It builds on the observation that different initial noises yield different sample quality (Ahn et al. 2024, Qi et al. 2024) and formalizes the problem as a search over noise candidates, structured along two design axes: verifiers (oracle, supervised, self-supervised) and algorithms (random/best-of-N, zero-order, search-over-paths). The zero-order search draws on bandit-style optimization (Flaxman et al. 2004), while the verifier-hacking phenomenon connects to reward over-optimization in RLHF (Gao et al. 2023, Clark et al. 2024). Unlike prior noise-optimization work that relies on gradient-based methods through the full sampling chain, this paper emphasizes gradient-free search and systematic comparison of verifier-algorithm combinations across tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is that a much smaller model (PixArt-Σ) augmented with inference-time search can match or exceed a frontier model (FLUX.1-dev) at roughly one-tenth the compute, suggesting that training-time investment can be partially offset by inference-time search—a potentially major efficiency lever for deployment. The verifier-task alignment analysis reveals that no single verifier is universally optimal: Aesthetic and CLIP verifiers actively harm each other's metrics (verifier hacking), while ImageReward dominates compositional tasks, underscoring the need for task-specific verifier design. Open questions include whether search scales to video/3D generation, whether more sophisticated search (e.g., learned policies, MCTS) can further improve efficiency, and how to design verifiers that avoid the mode-collapse and bias issues observed here. A limitation is that search multiplies forward-pass cost, so the absolute latency increase may be prohibitive in real-time applications.
