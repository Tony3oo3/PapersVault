## Problem

Existing structural pruning methods face two coupled limitations: they typically operate at a single fine-grained level (channels or attention heads), making aggressive 70–90% parameter reduction infeasible without also removing entire blocks, and their latency-aware formulations rely on linear models of output-channel count that are imprecise for CNNs and entirely inapplicable to transformers, where five interacting dimensions jointly determine inference time. No prior method simultaneously optimizes across multiple pruning granularities under a strict hardware latency budget.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained model's weights, a target latency budget Ψ measured on specific hardware, a small training set for computing importance scores, and precomputed latency lookup tables (LUTs) that map every combination of prunable-dimension values to measured inference time on the target device. The output is a structurally pruned subnetwork whose per-block channel counts, head counts, embedding/QK/V/MLP dimensions, and block-retention decisions are all jointly determined by the solver, followed by standard finetuning for E epochs to recover accuracy.

## Method

MDP encodes all prunable dimensions as one-hot variables ω (output channels per conv layer for CNNs; embedding, heads, QK, V, and MLP dimensions per block for transformers) and introduces binary block-removal variables κ that zero out an entire block's importance and latency contributions when set to 0. Importance is scored via Taylor expansion (CNNs) or Hessian-aware saliency (transformers), aggregated into per-dimension vectors so that the j-th entry represents the cumulative importance of retaining j elements. The latency constraint uses model-specific decompositions of a full multi-dimensional LUT—summing per-layer tables indexed by both input and output channels for CNNs, or per-component tables (QK, V-projection, MLP) for transformers—making the constraint cubic or quartic in the variables. The resulting Mixed-Integer Nonlinear Program is solved to global optimality in a single pass using Pyomo/MindtPy with the Outer Approximation method and a Feasibility Pump heuristic, after which the pruned subnetwork is extracted and finetuned.

## Results

On ImageNet with ResNet50 at 85% pruning, MDP achieves 70.0% Top-1 accuracy with a 5.21× speedup, surpassing HALP (68.1%, 3.90×) by +1.9 Top-1 and +31% speed. On DEIT-Base at 19% pruning, MDP reaches 78.21% Top-1 with 5.57× speedup, outperforming Isomorphic-T (77.50%, 5.20×) by +0.71 Top-1 and +7% speed. For 3D detection on NuScenes with StreamPETR at 45% pruning, MDP achieves 0.451 mAP and 0.551 NDS with 1.18× speedup, exceeding both the dense baseline (0.449 mAP, 1.00×) and HALP (0.446 mAP, 1.16×).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MDP consistently outperforms HALP [58] across all pruning ratios and tasks: at 85% ResNet50 pruning, MDP gains +1.9 Top-1 (70.0 vs. 68.1) and +34% speedup (5.21× vs. 3.90×); at 70% StreamPETR pruning, MDP achieves 0.394 mAP vs. HALP's 0.373 with a slightly higher 1.36× speedup. Against SMCP [35], MDP matches or slightly exceeds Top-1 accuracy (72.8 vs. 72.7 at 80% ResNet50) while delivering substantially higher speedup (4.13× vs. 3.71×). For transformer pruning, MDP surpasses the recent Isomorphic [23] (ECCV'24) on DEIT-Base with +0.71 Top-1 (78.21 vs. 77.50) and +7% speedup (5.57× vs. 5.20×) at the highest speed tier, and also beats NViT [78] at every speed level (e.g., 83.47% vs. 83.29% at 1.87× speedup). Compared to dedicated layer/block pruning methods (LayerPrune [20], DBP [73], ResConv-PRN [77]), MDP achieves significantly higher accuracy (74.8% vs. 74.3%/72.4%/70.0%) and speedup (3.06× vs. 1.79×/1.60×/–) at similar or lower FLOPs. Notably, MDP's pruned StreamPETR even surpasses the dense baseline in both mAP (0.451 vs. 0.449) and NDS (0.551 vs. 0.546) while being 18% faster.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latency-aware structural pruning line initiated by HALP (Shen et al., NeurIPS 2022) and SMCP (Humble et al., ECCV 2022), which framed pruning as a knapsack problem under a linear latency model. The key departure is twofold: (1) replacing the single-dimension linear latency model with a multi-dimensional lookup-table-based formulation that captures interactions across all prunable dimensions (channels, heads, Q/K/V, embeddings, MLP sizes), and (2) unifying channel, layer, and block pruning into a single Mixed-Integer Nonlinear Program rather than treating them as separate decisions. For importance scoring, the paper adopts Taylor expansion (Molchanov et al., CVPR 2019) for CNNs and Hessian-aware saliency (NViT, Yang et al., CVPR 2023) for transformers, but repurposes them to construct per-dimension importance vectors rather than per-parameter scores, enabling the MINLP formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling aggressive pruning (70–90%) with guaranteed latency budgets for both CNNs and transformers, a regime where prior single-granularity methods degrade sharply. The first latency-constrained transformer pruning (to the authors' knowledge) opens a path toward hardware-deployable vision transformers without relying on soft regularization that cannot guarantee the latency target. Open questions include the scalability of the MINLP solver as model size and number of prunable dimensions grow, the hardware-specificity of the latency LUTs (requiring re-measurement per target device), and whether the approach extends to generative or multimodal architectures with more complex block structures.
