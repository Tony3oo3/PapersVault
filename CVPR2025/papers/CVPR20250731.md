## Problem

Existing region-based controllable diffusion methods (e.g., MultiDiffusion) are incompatible with acceleration schedulers like LCM and Hyper-SD, producing noisy or blurry outputs when naively combined, and even the unaccelerated pipeline takes ~52 seconds for a 512×512 image. No prior work simultaneously achieves sub-second generation latency and fine-grained multi-prompt regional control, blocking real-time interactive content creation from diffusion models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250731/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts multiple text prompts each paired with a user-drawn binary semantic mask (plus an optional background image) on a canvas of arbitrary aspect ratio (tested at 512×512, 1024×1024, and 768×1920). In the interactive application, text encodings are cached so that mask-only edits skip the ~40% text-encoding cost, enabling brush-like painting where each stroke assigns a semantic meaning to a region. Output is a single synthesized image or, in streaming mode, a continuous stream of images at 1.57 FPS on a single RTX 2080 Ti.

## Method

The paper contributes two complementary components. First, three stabilization strategies make MultiDiffusion's tile-aggregation compatible with few-step acceleration schedulers: (1) latent pre-averaging splits each reverse-diffusion step into a deterministic denoising part and a stochastic noise part, averaging only the denoised latents before re-adding noise; (2) mask-centering bootstrapping replaces random-color bootstrapping with white-plus-aggregated-content and shifts each tile's latent to the frame center for the first two steps to exploit the model's center-bias; (3) quantized masks Gaussian-blur binary masks and quantize them by the scheduler's per-step noise level, gradually expanding mask coverage to smooth region boundaries. Second, a multi-prompt stream batch architecture pipelines the generation so that each image in a mini-batch occupies a different timestep, hiding multi-step latency and enabling endless streaming generation. Together these yield up to ×50 speedup over the baseline while matching or exceeding its FID and CLIP fidelity across SD1.5, SDXL, and SD3.

## Results

On COCO validation with SD1.5 (512×512), SemanticDraw with LCM achieves FID 93.93, IS 14.12, CLIPfg 24.14, and CLIPbg 24.00 in 1.3 seconds, compared to MultiDiffusion+DDIM reference (FID 70.93, 14.1s) and naive MultiDiffusion+LCM (FID 270.55, 1.7s). The method generalizes across backbones: on SD3 (1024×1024) with Flash Flow Match, SemanticDraw achieves FID 79.2 and IS 17.41 in 3.2s, surpassing the reference (FID 166.42, 46.3s). Throughput reaches 1.57 FPS (0.64s per frame) on a single RTX 2080 Ti with the multi-prompt stream batch architecture and Tiny AutoEncoder, an ×83 speedup over the baseline. A user study shows 90.9% preference for SemanticDraw over MultiDiffusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is MultiDiffusion [Bar-Tal et al., ICML 2023], evaluated both with its original DDIM sampler (50 steps) and with naively swapped-in acceleration schedulers (LCM, Hyper-SD, Euler Discrete, Flash Flow Match). The critical finding is that simply combining MultiDiffusion with LCM causes severe quality collapse: FID jumps from 70.93 (DDIM reference) to 270.55 (LCM), and IS drops from 16.24 to 2.65. SemanticDraw's three stabilization strategies (latent pre-averaging, mask-centering bootstrapping, quantized masks) recover quality to FID 93.93 while maintaining the 10× step reduction, and further reduce inference time from 1.7s to 1.3s. This pattern holds consistently across all four model/scheduler combinations tested (SD1.5+LCM, SD1.5+Hyper-SD, SDXL+Euler Discrete, SD3+Flash Flow Match), where SemanticDraw always outperforms the naive accelerated baseline by a large margin (e.g., FID 79.2 vs. 209.36 on SD3). In the user study, SemanticDraw is preferred over MultiDiffusion 90.9% of the time, with the naive MD+LCM combination receiving zero preference votes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two independently developed research threads: region-based multi-prompt generation (MultiDiffusion, SpaText) and diffusion inference acceleration (LCM, Hyper-SD, Flash Diffusion). The key observation is that these threads are incompatible when naively combined—applying an accelerated scheduler to MultiDiffusion produces degraded output because the averaging aggregation cancels prompt-wise noise additions. The paper's three stabilization strategies (latent pre-averaging, mask-centering bootstrapping, quantized masks) resolve this incompatibility, and the multi-prompt stream batch architecture extends StreamDiffusion's pipelined philosophy from single-prompt to multi-prompt generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making region-based control compatible with any acceleration scheduler and model family (SD1.5, SDXL, SD3), the paper removes a practical blocker that would otherwise prevent interactive multi-prompt generation from being usable. The resulting 0.64-second latency enables the 'semantic palette' application paradigm where users draw semantic masks in real-time, which is a meaningful step toward professional creative tools. Limitations include the quality-speed trade-off when using Tiny AutoEncoder, dependence on a single GPU for the reported throughput, and the fact that the method inherits the base model's center-bias and training-size constraints. Open questions include whether the quantized-mask harmonization strategy generalizes to video generation or 3D content, and whether the stream batch architecture can be extended to handle dynamically changing numbers of prompts without pipeline flushes.
