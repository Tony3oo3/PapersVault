## Problem

Existing scene text editing methods rely on complex multi-module pipelines that explicitly disentangle style and content, then recombine them with a separate pre-trained recognizer for content verification. This explicit separation is inherently imperfect, and the joint optimization of multiple interconnected modules leads to suboptimal editing quality, especially in complex real-world scenarios. The paper asks whether the intrinsic style-content separation capability of recognition models can be exploited directly within a unified editing framework to eliminate these fragile multi-stage dependencies.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference style image at 32×128 resolution and a target text string as input. The image is encoded via ViT-style patch tokenization (convolutional layer with kernel P×P producing N flattened visual tokens), while the text is encoded through a learned character embedding matrix over a fixed alphabet. The model produces two outputs simultaneously: an edited scene text image (decoded from image tokens via a fine-tuned LDM VAE decoder) and the recognized source text (decoded from text tokens via a fully connected layer), enabling content verification without any external recognizer.

## Method

RS-STE integrates scene text recognition and editing within a single Multi-modal Parallel Decoder (MMPD) built on a 22.5M-parameter transformer decoder, which receives concatenated text and image embeddings plus learnable query tokens and predicts both the source text and target image tokens in parallel. The key insight is that the recognition branch implicitly disentangles style from content (validated by feature-space analysis showing same-text/different-style images cluster together), so no explicit separation module is needed. Training proceeds in two stages: first, fully-supervised pre-training on paired synthetic data with cross-entropy (recognition), MSE, and VGG-16 perceptual losses; second, a Cyclic Self-Supervised Fine-tuning stage on unpaired real data where the model performs two reverse edits (I_A→I_B'→I_A') and is supervised to reproduce the original image, with recognition losses on both passes preventing collapse to an identity mapping. This design eliminates the need for separate style/content decoders and external recognition verifiers used by prior methods.

## Results

On the paired real dataset ScenePair, RS-STE achieves MSE 0.0267, PSNR 17.35, SSIM 46.09%, FID 41.37, and RecAcc 91.80%, outperforming all baselines. On the unpaired real dataset Tamper-Scene, RS-STE reaches 86.12% RecAcc, a 7.32% improvement over the previous best (STEEM at 78.80%). On the synthetic Tamper-Syn2k benchmark, RS-STE attains the best MSE (0.0076), PSNR (22.54), SSIM (72.90%), and RecAcc (86.12%), though FID (30.29) is slightly higher than STEEM (24.67) due to ground-truth ambiguity in synthetic data. The model uses only 54.4M parameters, comparable to MOSTEL (54.0M) and far smaller than TextCtrl (1216M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RS-STE outperforms all seven baselines on every metric where they are directly comparable. Against the strongest prior method STEEM [48], RS-STE improves RecAcc by 7.32 points on Tamper-Scene (86.12% vs. 78.80%) and by 54.11 points on ScenePair (91.80% vs. 37.69% for MOSTEL, the only other method reporting ScenePair RecAcc alongside STEEM's absence). Compared to the diffusion-based TextCtrl [46], which uses 22× more parameters (1216M vs. 54.4M), RS-STE achieves substantially better MSE (0.0267 vs. 0.0447), PSNR (17.35 vs. 14.99), SSIM (46.09% vs. 37.56%), and RecAcc (91.80% vs. 84.67%) on ScenePair. On Tamper-Syn2k, RS-STE leads in MSE, PSNR, SSIM, and RecAcc but trails STEEM in FID (30.29 vs. 24.67), which the authors attribute to ambiguous synthetic ground truths. Against the explicit-disentanglement methods SRNet [42], SwapText [45], and MOSTEL [31], RS-STE shows consistent and large margins across all metrics, confirming the advantage of the unified recognition-editing framework over multi-module pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

Prior scene text editing methods (SRNet, STEFANN, SwapText, MOSTEL, DARLING, STEEM) follow a multi-module pipeline that explicitly disentangles style and content before recombining them, often relying on a separate pre-trained recognizer for content verification. RS-STE departs from this by observing that recognition models inherently separate style from content in their feature space, and exploits this by unifying recognition and editing within a single transformer-based multi-modal parallel decoder. The cyclic self-supervised fine-tuning strategy for unpaired real data extends the self-supervised training ideas from TextStyleBrush and RewriteNet but applies them in a twice-cyclic generation loop that simultaneously enforces style preservation and content consistency without external supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating explicit style-content disentanglement modules, RS-STE offers a simpler and more robust pipeline that achieves SOTA on both synthetic and real-world editing benchmarks, and its generated hard cases provide a practical data augmentation strategy that boosts downstream recognition models (ABINet, MAERec-S) by 2.2–2.5%. However, the evaluation is limited to small image sizes (32×128), and the FID on synthetic data is not the best among compared methods, suggesting the approach may not yet generalize to high-resolution or highly diverse real-world scenes. Open questions include whether the recognition-editing synergy scales to larger images, multilingual settings, or more complex layouts, and whether the cyclic fine-tuning strategy can be extended to other image editing tasks beyond text.
