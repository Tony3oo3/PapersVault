## Problem

Existing MRI reconstruction methods face a fundamental trade-off: deep unfolding networks offer interpretability but operate in signal space with limited generalization across acceleration rates, while purely deep learning approaches lack interpretability entirely. Neural operators, which learn mappings between function spaces rather than signal instances, have never been applied to MRI acceleration, leaving a gap in achieving both consistency and out-of-distribution generalization (e.g., when the acceleration rate changes without retraining).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250366/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes undersampled k-space measurements as input, treated as discrete representations of bandlimited functions in Sobolev spaces via Whittaker-Shannon interpolation, and produces a fully reconstructed MRI image. It handles both single-coil (IXI brain, fastMRI knee) and multi-coil (12-channel brain) scenarios, with coil sensitivity maps estimated via ESPIRiT for the multi-coil case. The key structural constraint is that all operations preserve the bandlimited function space, ensuring the network is equivalent to a continuous operator when processing discrete bandlimited signals (continuous-discrete equivalence).

## Method

LMO is a neural operator that learns mappings between bandlimited function spaces, combining two novel integral forms: a Scanning Integration (SI) derived from a kernel integral with exponential kernel K(x,y)=Ce^{Ax}·Be^{-Ay}, discretized via zero-order hold into a State Space Model recurrence h(x_k)=Āh(x_{k-1})+B̄v(x_k) that captures global long-range dependencies in O(n) time; and a Convolution Integration (CI) defined as a local physical-space convolution within the bandlimited space that captures fine-grained features. The SI is applied along four scanning directions (rows and columns, forward and backward) with scan unfolding and merging to ensure multi-directional information flow. The full architecture follows the standard NO compositional form G_θ = Q∘η(W+K)∘...∘P with lifting, stacked kernel integration layers, and projection, all operating in bandlimited function space B_w(Ω). The theoretical contribution is proving continuous-discrete equivalence: the discrete network exactly implements the continuous operator on bandlimited inputs, which underpins the observed ability to generalize across acceleration rates without retraining.

## Results

On the IXI brain dataset with radial mask at ×4 acceleration, LMO achieves 48.16 dB PSNR and 0.996 SSIM, outperforming the closest competitor PGIUN (47.09 dB) by 1.07 dB. Across all six evaluation settings (three masks × two datasets at ×4), LMO consistently ranks first in both PSNR and SSIM. At the more challenging ×8 acceleration rate, LMO maintains its lead, e.g., 36.53 dB PSNR on IXI Radial vs. 36.21 dB for PGIUN. On multi-coil brain reconstruction at ×6, LMO reaches 41.52 dB PSNR and 0.994 SSIM, surpassing PGIUN (40.33 dB) by 1.19 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LMO achieves the best PSNR and SSIM across all 12 evaluation cells (2 datasets × 3 masks × 2 acceleration rates), winning every single comparison. The closest competitor is PGIUN (a physics-guided implicit unrolling network), which LMO outperforms by 1.07 dB on IXI Radial ×4 (48.16 vs. 47.09) and by 1.19 dB on multi-coil brain reconstruction (41.52 vs. 40.33). H-DSLR, the second-strongest unrolling baseline, trails LMO by 2.85 dB on IXI Radial ×4 and carries 2.5× the FLOPs (295.5G vs. 116.4G) at comparable parameter counts. Pure deep learning methods (Unet, SwinIR, U-Mamba) are consistently 4–14 dB behind LMO, confirming that ignoring data consistency in network design severely limits reconstruction quality. Notably, in the scale-generalization experiment (Table 4), LMO is the only method that improves when trained at ×8 and tested at lower scales (×4: 43.50 dB vs. 48.16 dB in-distribution), whereas PGIUN and H-DSLR degrade sharply under distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

LMO sits at the intersection of three threads: deep unfolding networks (HQS-Net, PGIUN, H-DSLR) that embed domain knowledge but operate in signal space with limited generalization; neural operators (FNO, CNO, Oformer) that learn mappings between function spaces but have never been applied to MRI; and Mamba/state-space models that offer O(n) long-range modeling but lack a principled integral form for operator learning. The paper's key departure is formulating MRI reconstruction as a bandlimited-function-space operator approximation with a novel kernel that jointly performs scanning integration (SSM) and convolution integration, establishing continuous-discrete equivalence (CDE) that prior DUNs and pure deep networks do not guarantee. This is, to the authors' knowledge, the first neural operator applied to accelerated MRI.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CDE property and function-space formulation give LMO a principled reason to generalize across acceleration rates without retraining—a property no competing DUN or pure deep method exhibits (Table 4). However, the generalization evidence is limited to a single dataset (IXI) and one mask type (radial), and the absolute PSNR drop when extrapolating from ×4 to ×8 (48.16 → 30.25 dB) remains substantial, so the practical robustness claim needs broader validation. Open questions include whether the bandlimited-space formulation extends to 3D volumetric MRI, dynamic/functional imaging, or multi-contrast settings, and whether the CDE guarantee holds under non-uniform or arbitrary sampling patterns beyond the three tested masks.
