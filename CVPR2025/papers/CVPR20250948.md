## Problem

Existing 3DGS and NeRF-based driving scene reconstruction methods can only render high-fidelity views along the recorded ego trajectory; when viewpoints deviate laterally (e.g., 3 m), rendering quality collapses because no ground-truth supervision exists for those off-trajectory positions. This data scarcity prevents current simulators from supporting the arbitrary trajectories a self-driving car might take during action execution.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view driving images along a single recorded trajectory (960×640, half of original Waymo resolution) and optional sparse LiDAR point clouds projected into the image plane as a pseudo-image condition. It produces high-quality rendered images at off-trajectory viewpoints (up to 3 m lateral shift) and an updated 3D Gaussian field. The generative enhancement model specifically takes a degraded rendering plus a LiDAR projection as dual ControlNet conditions and outputs a restored high-quality image.

## Method

FreeSim reformulates the intractable pose-conditioned novel view generation task as a generative image enhancement task: given a slightly degraded rendering from a small viewpoint deviation, a diffusion model restores it to high quality. Training data is constructed by piece-wise Gaussian reconstruction of the full Waymo training split, where extrapolated rendering on held-out future frames and random Gaussian perturbation (translation + rotation noise) simulate off-trajectory degradation patterns, yielding ~1.5M training pairs. The generative model is Stable Diffusion 1.5 with two ControlNet branches (degraded image and LiDAR projection) fused into the denoising UNet. At inference, a progressive reconstruction strategy iteratively shifts the trajectory by 0.5 m, generates enhanced images for the new viewpoints, adds them to the training set, and re-optimizes the Gaussian field, smoothly expanding from on-trajectory to far off-trajectory views. A final post-enhancement pass mitigates rolling shutter distortion and generative randomness.

## Results

On the Waymo Open Dataset, FreeSim achieves FID scores of 14.6, 17.0, and 18.6 at lateral shifts of 1m, 2m, and 3m respectively, substantially outperforming all reconstruction-based baselines (best baseline PVG: 22.9, 34.3, 47.5). On recorded (on-trajectory) views, FreeSim reports PSNR of 28.32 and SSIM of 0.852, a slight drop compared to PVG (29.19 / 0.866) due to minor inconsistencies introduced by the generative component. The gap widens dramatically at larger deviations: at 3m, FreeSim's FID of 18.6 is more than 2.5× better than the next-best method (PVG at 47.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeSim decisively outperforms all four baselines on off-trajectory FID at every tested deviation. Against PVG (the strongest baseline), FreeSim reduces FID from 22.9→14.6 at 1m, 34.3→17.0 at 2m, and 47.5→18.6 at 3m — a 36–61% relative improvement that grows with deviation magnitude. StreetGS and EmerNeRF show moderate degradation at 1m (FID 25.8 and 32.3) but deteriorate sharply at 3m (47.6 and 49.8), while S3Gaussian performs worst overall (FID 81.3 at 3m), indicating its self-supervised formulation struggles most with large viewpoint changes. On on-trajectory metrics, FreeSim trades a small amount of PSNR/SSIM (≈0.87 dB and 0.014 SSIM below PVG) for the large off-trajectory gains, which the authors attribute to minor inconsistencies from the generative enhancement step. The paper also notes that concurrent generative methods like FreeVS are limited by LiDAR coverage and DriveDreamer4D cannot handle arbitrary handmade trajectories, positioning FreeSim as the only method combining reconstruction fidelity with flexible off-trajectory generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

FreeSim builds on the 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction pipeline of PVG and the Stable Diffusion 1.5 + ControlNet generative architecture, but its core conceptual departure is reformulating pose-conditioned novel view synthesis as a generative image enhancement task: rather than generating a view from scratch given a camera transformation (as in FreeVS, SGD, or FlexDrive), it enhances a degraded rendering of the target viewpoint. The progressive reconstruction strategy—iteratively expanding the training set from small to large lateral deviations—has no direct precedent in prior driving simulation work, which typically either relies on multi-view supervision unavailable in single-trajectory driving data or uses GAN-based supervision (UniSim) that degrades under large viewpoint changes. The LiDAR projection condition is borrowed from FreeVS, and the piece-wise Gaussian reconstruction with extrapolated rendering and Gaussian perturbation is a novel data construction pipeline specific to this work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FreeSim addresses a fundamental practical gap in driving simulators: the inability to render high-fidelity views from trajectories the vehicle never actually followed, which is essential for testing diverse action decisions in autonomous driving. The 1.5M-sample data construction pipeline (piece-wise reconstruction + extrapolated rendering + Gaussian perturbation) is a reusable recipe for creating synthetic training pairs without off-trajectory ground truth, potentially applicable beyond driving. Key limitations remain: rolling shutter distortion and generative randomness cause residual blur, the generative model is trained exclusively on PVG (Gaussian-based) data and does not generalize to NeRF-based reconstructions, and the FID-based evaluation (lacking pixel-level ground truth for off-trajectory views) limits confidence in the magnitude of reported gains. Future directions include video generation models for temporal consistency, scene editability, and native 3DGS rasterizer modifications to handle rolling shutter natively.
