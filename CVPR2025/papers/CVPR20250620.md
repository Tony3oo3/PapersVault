## Problem

Existing 3D semantic representation methods rely exclusively on point clouds as the medium for 3D perception, overlooking the rich texture and color details provided by multi-view images. This limitation stems from prior work being driven by autonomous driving, which prioritizes physical geometry over visual semantics, ultimately constraining the depth of 3D understanding for cross-modal tasks like retrieval and zero-shot classification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250620/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 3D shape or scene as a pair (P, M): a point cloud P ∈ R^{g×6} of 10,000 colored points (xyz + RGB) and a set of m multi-view images (default 10) rendered from different viewpoints. The images are unordered, and the model is sequence-independent, producing a consistent output regardless of input image order or count. The output is a single 3D semantic embedding e^V ∈ R^{1280} aligned to OpenCLIP's image-text embedding space, enabling direct cosine-similarity matching with text or image queries.

## Method

OpenView employs three encoders: a frozen ViT-BigG image encoder (from OpenCLIP), a Point-BERT-style point cloud encoder (initialized from OpenShape), and a novel cross-modal fusion encoder that bridges the large feature gap between modalities (cosine similarity ~0.85 within images vs. ~0.25 between images and point clouds). The fusion encoder uses parallel intra-modal self-attention and inter-modal cross-attention plus self-attention, with two special aggregation tokens (point-cloud [cls] and mean image feature) whose normalized sum forms the final 3D embedding. Sequence-independent modeling is achieved by omitting positional embeddings for image tokens and adding learnable type embeddings to distinguish modalities. Training uses contrastive learning against text embeddings only (excluding images to avoid misleading single views), combined with a progressive hard learning strategy that gradually removes 10%–50% of the easiest-to-understand images (ranked by text-image cosine similarity) over epochs, forcing the model to rely on point clouds and ambiguous views for global 3D understanding.

## Results

On the Objaverse-LVIS benchmark, OpenView achieves 67.6% R@1 for text-to-3D retrieval and 62.9% R@1 for 3D-to-text retrieval, outperforming the best point-cloud-only baseline (OpenShape) by 11.5% and 8.9% respectively. On Omniobject3D, OpenView reaches 41.0% R@1 (text→3D) and 36.8% R@1 (3D→text), substantially exceeding all prior methods. For zero-shot classification, OpenView attains 52.7% Top-1 accuracy on Objaverse-LVIS (vs. 47.2% for Uni3D) and 58.5% on Omniobject3D (vs. 37.9% for Uni3D), demonstrating strong gains particularly on datasets with rich visual detail.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OpenView outperforms all six baselines across both retrieval and classification benchmarks. Against the strongest point-cloud-only baseline OpenShape, OpenView gains +11.5% R@1 on Objaverse-LVIS text→3D retrieval (67.6 vs. 56.1) and +5.5% Top-1 on zero-shot classification (52.7 vs. 39.1). Against Uni3D, the gains are even larger: +23.5% R@1 on Objaverse-LVIS text→3D and +20.4% Top-1 on Omniobject3D (58.5 vs. 37.9). The multi-view-only variant of PointCLIP v2 (46.9% R@1) underperforms OpenView by 20.7 points, confirming that fusing both modalities is superior to either alone. On ModelNet40 (synthetic CAD without texture), the advantage narrows (87.5% vs. 86.8% Top-1 over Uni3D), as multi-view images contribute less without color/texture. Replacing the proposed cross-modal fusion encoder with a standard transformer of the same size drops R@1 by 12.8% on average, validating the architecture design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-modal 3D representation learning line initiated by ULIP, OpenShape, and Uni3D, which unified text, image, and 3D point cloud representations via contrastive learning. The key departure is the addition of multi-view images as a second 3D input modality alongside point clouds, motivated by the observation that point clouds alone lack the texture and color details that multi-view images provide. Architecturally, it builds on OpenCLIP for image/text encoders, Point-BERT for the point cloud encoder, and introduces a novel cross-modal fusion encoder with parallel intra-modal self-attention and inter-modal cross-attention, plus a sequence-independent design to handle unordered image inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OpenView demonstrates that fusing geometric (point cloud) and visual (multi-view image) information yields substantially better 3D semantic embeddings than either modality alone, with 11.5% R@1 gains in retrieval and 5.5% Top-1 gains in zero-shot classification over prior SOTA. The alignment with OpenCLIP's embedding space enables immediate downstream applications (3D captioning, image-query retrieval, hierarchical clustering) without task-specific fine-tuning. Open questions include robustness to adversarial perturbations on the image modality, whether the approach scales to scene-level (rather than object-level) understanding, and whether the framework can be integrated into large language model backbones for more complex 3D reasoning.
