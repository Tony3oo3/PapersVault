## Problem

Existing deepfake detectors learn dataset-specific cues that fail to generalize to unseen forgery samples, and the potential of foundation models (e.g., CLIP) for this task remains largely unexplored. A pilot study shows that fully fine-tuning CLIP for deepfake detection causes the model to attend to arbitrary generic regions rather than meaningful facial artifacts, undermining generalization. The paper asks how to harness CLIP's rich semantic priors for video-based deepfake detection while preserving its generalizability and adapting it efficiently.

## Input / Output

The method takes facially aligned video clips (2–4 seconds) from which 10 frames are uniformly sampled; faces are aligned to a mean face template using 2D-FAN landmarks before being fed into the frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 encoder. The output is a binary real/fake score computed as the average of three independent classification heads (temporal, spatial, and spatio-temporal), each operating on aggregated layer-wise embeddings. Evaluation is reported as video-level AUROC across five unseen datasets.

## Method

The framework freezes the CLIP ViT-L/14 image encoder and attaches a per-layer side-network decoder with two specialized modules. The spatial module uses cross-attention with a small set of learnable queries (N ≤ 4) attending to patch embeddings, and a Facial Component Guidance (FCG) loss (InfoNCE) contrastively aligns each query to pre-mined facial-part attributes (lips, skin, eyes, nose) extracted from CLIP features, steering attention to diagnostically relevant regions. The temporal module applies Patch-Temporal Multi-Head Self-Attention along the frame axis over query/key/value attributes, followed by 2D convolutions that aggregate temporal affinities across adjacent frames and spatially adjacent patches. Training uses focal loss on three heads plus the FCG loss, with only ~250K trainable parameters (≈200× fewer than full fine-tuning), completing in ~16 GPU hours on 4×V100s.

## Results

Trained on FaceForensics++ (HQ), the proposed method achieves video-level AUC of 95.0% on CelebDF-v2, 81.8% on DFDC, 98.1% on FaceShifter, 99.6% on DeeperForensics, and 87.2% on WildDeepfake, yielding an average of 92.3% across five unseen datasets. The most significant gains are on DFDC (+4.8% over the previous best, Yan et al.) and WDF (+3.9% over RealForensics*), where prior methods struggle most. The method also requires only 250K trainable parameters (vs. 58.3M for EVL and 303M for full fine-tuning) while outperforming all adaptation baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 16 baselines on the most challenging unseen datasets: it leads DFDC by 4.8% over Yan et al. (81.8 vs. 77.0) and WDF by 3.9% over the re-evaluated RealForensics* (87.2 vs. 83.3). On CelebDF-v2, it achieves 95.0%, narrowly trailing LAA-Net's reported 95.4% but surpassing the re-evaluated LAA-Net* (93.6%) and CADMM (93.9%). On FaceShifter, the method scores 98.1%, slightly below RealForensics (99.7%) and TALL-Swin (99.7%), suggesting that temporal cues specific to FaceShifter's occlusion-aware swapping are better captured by those dedicated video models. On DeeperForensics, the method ties TALL-Swin at 99.6%. In the parameter-efficiency comparison (Table 4), the method with only 250K trainable parameters surpasses EVL (58.3M params, avg. 83.9%), VPT-Deep (26.6K, avg. 86.7%), and full fine-tuning (303M, avg. 85.1%) by 7–9% in average AUC, demonstrating that the side-network decoder with FCG is far more effective than generic PEFT strategies for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the EVL (Frozen CLIP models are efficient video learners) and Liu et al. frameworks, which introduced side-network decoders that progressively extract per-layer features from a frozen CLIP ViT encoder for video understanding. The key departure is the introduction of two specialized modules—spatial (cross-attention with learnable queries) and temporal (Patch-Temporal Multi-Head Self-Attention)—replacing EVL's unified decoding, plus a novel Facial Component Guidance (FCG) InfoNCE loss that constrains spatial queries to align with facial-part-specific features (lips, skin, eyes, nose). In the deepfake detection lineage, the paper positions itself against methods that learn dataset-specific cues (RealForensics, SBI, FTCN) by instead leveraging CLIP's web-scale generic representations, and it explicitly contrasts with PEFT strategies like LoRA, VPT, and linear probing to justify its side-network design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that 250K trainable parameters (≈1200× fewer than full fine-tuning) can achieve state-of-the-art cross-dataset deepfake detection is practically significant for deployment and rapid adaptation to new forgery types. The finding that RealForensics, despite strong FF++ performance, is biased toward FF++-specific cues (evidenced by its collapse on truly unseen CDF/DFDC/WDF) reframes how the community should interpret cross-dataset benchmarks. Open questions remain: sparse 10-frame sampling may miss high-frequency temporal artifacts, robustness during the pretraining stage is unexplored, and whether the FCG mechanism generalizes beyond the four fixed facial components to other manipulation types (e.g., full-face GAN generation) is untested.
