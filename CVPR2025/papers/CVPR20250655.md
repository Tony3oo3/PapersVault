## Problem

Existing sensitivity-aware PEFT methods (SPT) identify sensitive parameters and tune them via a combination of unstructured and structured tuning, but they evaluate sparsity only at the level of the entire parameter matrix. This overlooks the fact that sensitive parameters are not chaotically distributed but concentrate in a small number of rows or columns, resulting in unnecessary storage of redundant low-rank parameters for non-sensitive regions and limited performance gains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250655/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained ViT-B/16 weights (ImageNet-21K) and a small set of downstream training samples (~400 for sensitivity estimation) to produce adapted parameters that are merged back into the original weight matrices, incurring zero additional inference latency or memory. For classification tasks (VTAB-1k, FGVC) the output is a class label; for semantic segmentation (ADE20K, PASCAL VOC) it is a per-pixel mask. The reorganization step is a pure row/column permutation, so the adapted weights are expressed in the original parameter space and can be directly used for inference without any architectural modification.

## Method

CDRA-SPT first identifies sensitive parameters using accumulated gradient sensitivity (same criterion as SPT), then reorganizes each parameter matrix by sorting rows and columns in descending order of sensitivity so that all sensitive entries cluster into a single compact sub-parameter matrix (w̄_i^10). Instead of applying LoRA to the full matrix or using mask-based unstructured tuning, it applies low-rank adaptation exclusively to this compact sub-matrix, reducing trainable parameters and eliminating redundant modeling of non-sensitive regions. A dynamic-rank allocation strategy then determines the rank r for each sub-matrix by solving a constraint that the number of LoRA parameters does not exceed the number of sensitive parameters, scaled by the ratio of original to compact matrix dimensions—ensuring smaller or sparser sub-matrices receive proportionally lower rank. Finally, the inverse row/column permutation restores the adapted weights to the original parameter space, yielding a drop-in replacement with no inference overhead.

## Results

On VTAB-1k (ViT-B/16, ImageNet-21K pre-trained), CDRA-SPT achieves a mean accuracy of 74.53% across 19 tasks (82.4% natural, 86.3% specialized, 61.8% structured) using only 0.49% trainable parameters, outperforming SPT-LoRA-16 (73.87%, 1.36% params) and SPT-LoRA-8 (73.26%, 0.51% params). On the FGVC benchmark, CDRA-SPT reaches 89.86% mean accuracy with 0.86% parameters, surpassing SPT-LoRA-16 (89.69%, 2.28%) and even full fine-tuning (88.54%, 100%). For semantic segmentation, CDRA-SPT attains 74.0 mDice / 62.0 mIoU on ADE20K and 77.4 mDice / 66.4 mIoU on VOC with ~0.7–0.8% parameters, exceeding SPT-LoRA-16 on both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDRA-SPT outperforms all seven baseline families on VTAB-1k. Against the closest competitor SPT-LoRA-16 (73.87%), CDRA-SPT gains +0.66% mean accuracy while using 64% fewer trainable parameters (0.49% vs. 1.36%). Compared to SPT-LoRA-8 (73.26%), the improvement is +1.27% with comparable parameter count (0.49% vs. 0.51%). Against LoRA (72.63%) and Adapter (71.44%), CDRA-SPT leads by +1.90% and +3.09% respectively. On FGVC, CDRA-SPT (89.86%) surpasses SPT-LoRA-16 (89.69%) by +0.17% while using only 38% of its parameters (0.86% vs. 2.28%), and exceeds full fine-tuning (88.54%) by +1.32%. For semantic segmentation on ADE20K, CDRA-SPT achieves 74.0 mDice vs. SPT-LoRA-16's 73.3, and on VOC it reaches 77.4 mDice vs. 76.9, all with roughly half the trainable parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends SPT (He et al., ICCV 2023), which introduced sensitivity-aware PEFT by identifying sensitive parameters and combining unstructured (mask-based) and structured (LoRA) tuning. The key departure is the observation that sensitive parameters are not chaotically distributed across a parameter matrix but concentrate in a small number of rows or columns, enabling reorganization into a compact sub-parameter matrix. CDRA-SPT unifies SPT's two separate tuning cases (sparse → mask tuning, dense → LoRA) into a single structured low-rank adaptation applied at the sub-matrix level, with a dynamic-rank allocation strategy that adapts rank to each sub-matrix's intrinsic dimension. The approach also draws on sub-network learning and the lottery ticket hypothesis for the idea of identifying and tuning a structured subset of parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The row/column concentration finding provides a principled explanation for why SPT's ad-hoc combination of unstructured and structured tuning works, and the unified sub-matrix formulation reduces trainable parameters (0.49% vs. 0.51–1.36% for SPT variants) while gaining ~1% accuracy. However, the improvements are modest, the sensitivity evaluation still requires a forward pass over ~400 training samples per task, and the method is validated only on ViT-B/16 and DINOv2 backbones. Open questions include whether the row/column concentration pattern generalizes to non-Transformer architectures (CNNs, Mamba), whether the dynamic-rank formula (Eq. 7) holds at larger scales, and whether the compact sub-matrix structure persists under different pre-training objectives or data regimes.
