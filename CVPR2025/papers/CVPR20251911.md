## Problem

State-of-the-art LiDAR point cloud registration methods (CoFiNet, GeoTrans, PARE) rely on cross-attention modules that implicitly assume consistent geometric representations of the same structure across two point clouds. This assumption breaks under cross-distance variations (where LiDAR point density changes with range) and cross-dataset variations (different LiDAR line counts and environments), causing severe performance degradation. No prior work has systematically studied or addressed generalization across these conditions for LiDAR registration.

## Input / Output

The method takes two partially overlapping LiDAR point clouds P ∈ R^{N×3} and Q ∈ R^{M×3} and outputs a rigid transformation T = {R ∈ SO(3), t ∈ R^3}. Each point cloud is also projected to a binary Bird's Eye View image (pixel value 1 where points project), which is fed through a separate 2D encoder. The pipeline follows a coarse-to-fine strategy: superpoint-level matching is refined to dense point correspondences, and the final transformation is estimated via the LGR approach from GeoTransformer.

## Method

UGP eliminates the cross-attention module entirely, forcing the network to learn robust intra-frame features rather than relying on inter-frame geometric consistency. The Point-Encoder uses multi-level KPConv with voxel downsampling to extract superpoint features, while a ResNet-based BEV-Encoder extracts 2D patch features from the projected BEV image; these are fused via an indexing relationship between superpoints and BEV patches to incorporate scene-element semantics (roads, corners). A progressive self-attention module replaces global self-attention by applying distance-based masks that restrict attention to nearby superpoints in early layers and gradually expand the receptive field in later layers, reducing feature ambiguity from irrelevant distant points. Superpoint correspondences are obtained via a Gaussian correlation matrix with dual normalization and top-k selection, followed by dense matching and LGR-based transformation estimation.

## Results

On cross-distance generalization (train at 10m, test at 20m/30m/40m), UGP achieves a mean Registration Recall (mRR) of 94.5% on KITTI and 91.4% on nuScenes, substantially outperforming the next-best method BUFFER (88.3% and 61.0% respectively). On cross-dataset generalization (train on nuScenes@10m, test on KITTI), UGP achieves a mean RR of 90.9%, surpassing BUFFER (84.7%) and GCL (85.0%). At the most challenging 40m distance, UGP achieves 82.0% RR on KITTI and 72.3% on nuScenes, while transformer-based baselines (GeoTrans, PARE) drop below 3% RR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UGP outperforms all 8 baselines across every generalization setting. Against BUFFER (the strongest baseline), UGP gains +6.2% mRR on KITTI cross-distance (94.5% vs 88.3%), +30.4% mRR on nuScenes cross-distance (91.4% vs 61.0%), and +6.2% mRR on cross-dataset nuScenes→KITTI (90.9% vs 84.7%). Transformer-based methods (CoFiNet, GeoTrans, PARE) collapse dramatically under cross-distance conditions — GeoTrans drops to 7.5% RR at just 20m on KITTI — while UGP maintains 99.3%. The ablation variants (CoFiNet w/o C, GeoTrans w/o C) confirm that simply removing cross-attention recovers most of the generalization gap, with UGP's additional progressive self-attention and BEV fusion providing the remaining gains. On cross-dataset KITTI→nuScenes, UGP achieves 98.4% RR vs BUFFER's 94.3%, despite the 64-line vs 32-line sensor difference. GCL [19], which trains on [5m, 60m] range data, still trails UGP by 5.9% mRR on cross-dataset evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the coarse-to-fine LiDAR registration paradigm established by GeoTransformer, CoFiNet, and PARE, which all rely on cross-attention to model inter-frame geometric consistency. Its key conceptual departure is the elimination of cross-attention, aligning it more closely with patch-wise methods like BUFFER and SpinNet that avoid inter-frame information exchange. The progressive self-attention module is a novel twist on standard transformer attention that gradually expands the receptive field, while the BEV feature fusion draws from multi-modal registration work (RPVNet, BEVPlace) to inject scene-element semantics into the coarse matching stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that cross-attention actively harms generalization in LiDAR registration challenges a widely adopted design choice and provides a clear, actionable principle for practitioners building registration systems for autonomous driving. The large cross-distance and cross-dataset improvements (up to 57% RR gain on nuScenes@40m) suggest that current SOTA methods are overfitting to training-time density distributions. Open questions include whether the insight generalizes to indoor RGB-D point clouds with more uniform density, whether it extends to even more extreme sensor mismatches, and whether the BEV projection introduces a dependency on ground-plane assumptions that limits applicability to non-terrestrial scenarios.
