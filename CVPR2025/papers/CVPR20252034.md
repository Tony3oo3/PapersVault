## Problem

State-of-the-art learning-based 3D reconstruction methods (e.g., DUSt3R, MASt3R) fail on extreme aerial-ground viewpoint changes, with DUSt3R localizing fewer than 5% of such pairs within 5° rotation error. The paper hypothesizes this stems from the absence of large-scale, co-registered aerial-ground training data, which is difficult to assemble because merging independent ground and aerial camera poses into a unified coordinate frame typically requires specialized sensors or manual effort.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252034/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For multi-view reconstruction, the method takes N unconstrained RGB images (mix of aerial and ground) and regresses per-pixel 3D pointmaps in a shared coordinate frame, from which camera intrinsics (focal length under pinhole model) and 6-DoF relative poses are recovered via PnP; for N>2, a global alignment step merges all pairwise pointmaps. For novel view synthesis, a single reference image plus a target extrinsic matrix and field-of-view are consumed by ZeroNVS to produce a novel-view RGB image. The AerialMegaDepth dataset itself provides 1.5M image pairs, each annotated with camera intrinsics, 6-DoF poses, and semi-dense depth maps from COLMAP MVS, spanning 137 scenes with altitudes from 1 m to 350 m.

## Method

The core contribution is a scalable data-generation framework that renders pseudo-synthetic aerial images from Google Earth's 3D textured city meshes at varying altitudes, then co-registers real crowd-sourced ground images (MegaDepth) into the pseudo-synthetic reconstructions via a standard visual-localization pipeline (NetVlad retrieval, SuperPoint+LightGlue 2D-3D matching, RANSAC PnP, and MVS depth). Pseudo-synthetic 3D geometry is recovered by triangulating matched features across rendered views, since Google Earth does not expose the underlying mesh. Image pairs are selected using an asymmetric covisibility score (AM/HM ratio) to prioritize ground-aerial pairs with large viewpoint differences. The resulting hybrid dataset (132K images, 1.5M pairs) is used to fine-tune DUSt3R, MASt3R, and ZeroNVS, yielding ~11× improvement in camera registration accuracy (5%→56% at RRA@5°) and consistent gains in 3D pointmap accuracy and aerial-to-ground novel view synthesis.

## Results

On zero-shot ground-aerial two-view pose estimation (ULTRRA + ACCNVS1, 6 sites, 5000+ images), fine-tuning DUSt3R on AerialMegaDepth (Hybrid) raises RRA@5° from 5.20% (released) to 55.96% and RRA@15° from 9.48% to 76.15%, a roughly 11× improvement. Similarly, MASt3R improves from 3.36% to 49.54% at RRA@5°. For 3D pointmap accuracy, DUSt3R+Hybrid increases δ@1m from 42.16% to 62.33% and δ@2m from 43.79% to 74.52%. For aerial-to-ground novel view synthesis, fine-tuning ZeroNVS on AerialMegaDepth reduces DreamSim from 0.550 to 0.442 and improves PSNR from 7.478 to 8.220 on real images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All six baselines are decisively outperformed by the AerialMegaDepth fine-tuned models. LoFTR [58] is the weakest baseline at 0.92% RRA@5°, while SP+SG [46] reaches 8.56% and released DUSt3R [64] achieves 5.20%—all struggling with the extreme viewpoint change. Released MASt3R [26] scores 3.36% RRA@5%, and MASt3R-SfM [15] (multi-view) reaches only 9.03% RRA@15° with 2 ground images; fine-tuning on AerialMegaDepth lifts these to 49.54% and 51.07% respectively. For novel view synthesis, ZeroNVS fine-tuned on MegaScenes [60] (ZeroNVS-MS) yields DreamSim 0.550, LPIPS 0.639, PSNR 7.478, SSIM 0.183 on real images, whereas the AerialMegaDepth fine-tuned version improves all four metrics (DreamSim 0.442, LPIPS 0.580, PSNR 8.220, SSIM 0.218). The ablation between PSynth-only and Hybrid training shows that adding real ground images to pseudo-synthetic renderings provides an additional ~18–24 percentage point gain at RRA@5°, confirming the value of the hybrid data strategy over purely synthetic alternatives like MatrixCity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a data-generation contribution rather than an architectural one, building directly on DUSt3R's pointmap regression and global alignment, MASt3R's 3D-grounded matching, and ZeroNVS's pose-conditioned view synthesis. It departs from prior multiview datasets (MegaDepth, IMC-PT) that cover only ground-level viewpoints, and from purely synthetic city datasets (MatrixCity, CARLA) that lack real-world visual anchoring, by introducing a hybrid pipeline that co-registers pseudo-synthetic Google Earth mesh renderings with real crowd-sourced images in a unified coordinate frame. The framework is explicitly orthogonal to existing aerial-ground registration methods, providing the training data those methods lack rather than proposing a new registration algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The scalable framework—combining any geospatial 3D mesh platform with any crowd-sourced image collection—offers a nearly unlimited data source for aerial-ground 3D learning, potentially enabling planet-scale reconstruction as the authors envision. A key open question is how well the pseudo-synthetic-to-real domain gap generalizes beyond the 137 landmarks tested, particularly for scenes with complex vegetation, dynamic elements, or non-urban environments. The novel-view synthesis results, while improved, remain far from photorealistic, suggesting that geometric supervision alone may be insufficient for high-fidelity cross-altitude synthesis without additional generative modeling.
