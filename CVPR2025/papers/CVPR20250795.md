## Problem

Existing multi-attribute image generation methods assume conditional independence between attributes, causing their guidance gradients to be nearly orthogonal in high-dimensional space and producing incoherent results. The paper argues that subsequent attributes should be generated under the multivariable conditional distribution introduced by previously created attributes, rather than in isolation, and tackles this in a zero-shot setting without attribute-specific fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250795/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes heterogeneous conditions simultaneously—text prompts (via CLIP), face ID reference images (via ArcFace), landmark point sets, segmentation maps (via face parsing), and style reference images (via Gram matrix comparison)—and produces a single synthesized RGB image. It operates on pre-trained unconditional face diffusion models (256×256) and Stable Diffusion v1.4 (512×512) in a fully training-free manner, with all condition classifiers acting as plug-and-play differentiable guidance functions evaluated on the predicted clean image x₀|t.

## Method

Z-Magic reformulates multi-attribute guidance from a conditional probability perspective: instead of summing independent gradients ∇log p(cᵢ|x_t), it computes the chain-rule decomposition ∇log p(c₁|x_t) + ∇log p(c₂|c₁,x_t), where each subsequent gradient is conditioned on the intermediate denoised result of prior attributes. A key Hessian-free trick (Eq. 7) converts the product of the Hessian and gradient vector into a gradient of a scalar, avoiding the infeasible 256 GB Hessian storage for 256×256 images. For more than two conditions, the paper maps the problem to multi-task learning and applies CAGrad (conflict-averse gradient descent) to find an optimal gradient step that balances all pairwise conditional objectives. The entire pipeline modifies only the ancestral sampling loop of a pre-trained diffusion model, requiring no additional training.

## Results

On the face ID + landmark task (Table 4), Z-Magic achieves an ID distance of 0.549, a 26% improvement over FreeDoM (0.740), while matching its landmark distance (0.194 vs. 0.195) and achieving a competitive FID of 124. In the three-condition face synthesis setting (Table 1), Z-Magic reduces FID from 136 to 123 and ID distance from 0.501 to 0.475 over FreeDoM. For text + style control (Table 2), Z-Magic achieves the lowest style loss (10.14) and text distance (0.7152) among all compared methods, outperforming UGD by 44% in style loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FreeDoM [47], the primary condition-independent baseline, Z-Magic wins on every metric across all four evaluation tables, with the most dramatic gain in ID distance (0.549 vs. 0.740, a 26% reduction) on the face ID + landmark task. In the text + style setting, Z-Magic substantially outperforms UGD [4] (style loss 10.14 vs. 18.04) and StyleAligned [15] (style loss 10.14 vs. 11.35; text distance 0.7152 vs. 0.7475). For face segmentation + text control, Z-Magic beats TediGAN [46] on segmentation distance (1502 vs. 2344) and text distance (0.752 vs. 0.763), and beats Collaborative Diffusion [19] on both segmentation distance (1502 vs. 2162) and text distance (0.752 vs. 0.758), though TediGAN and Collaborative achieve slightly lower FID scores (98 and 122 vs. 127). Against face-swapping methods DiffSwap [50] and E4S [27], Z-Magic achieves far better ID consistency (0.549 vs. 1.167 and 0.977) and better landmark adherence than E4S (0.194 vs. 0.282), but both swapping methods report lower FID, reflecting their different task formulations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the score-based conditional diffusion framework (Song et al., ICLR 2021) and DDPM ancestral sampling (Ho et al., NeurIPS 2020), departing from the standard practice of summing independent condition gradients (as in FreeDoM, UGD, and EGSDE) by arguing that conditional independence is theoretically invalid given noisy data x_t. It repurposes CAGrad (Liu et al., NeurIPS 2021) from multi-task learning to approximate the full joint gradient for n>2 conditions, and adopts FreeDoM's energy-function-based condition classifiers for plug-and-play zero-shot use. The key conceptual departure is reframing multi-attribute guidance as a sequential conditional probability problem rather than a parallel independent one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical correction (independence ≠ conditional independence given x_t) provides a principled justification for sequential gradient refinement, and the Hessian-free trick (Eq. 7) makes the approach computationally tractable without retraining. However, the multi-condition extension via CAGrad is explicitly heuristic, the condition ordering must be manually chosen (Fig. 4), and evaluations are limited to face synthesis and style transfer on CelebA-HQ and SD-v1.4. Open questions include whether the sequential ordering can be learned automatically, how the approach scales beyond 3–4 attributes, and whether the modest quantitative gains (e.g., FID 136→123) persist at higher resolutions or with more diverse attribute combinations.
