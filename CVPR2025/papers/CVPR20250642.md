## Problem

Deep learning models trained via ERM often learn spurious correlations that hurt minority-group performance, yet existing debiasing methods either require explicit bias labels or rely on task-specific assumptions (e.g., simplicity bias) that fail when the task changes. The paper tackles the problem of mitigating unknown spurious correlations in a task-independent manner, without any prior knowledge of which attributes are biased.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250642/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-extracted feature vectors from a frozen ERM-trained encoder (ResNet-50 for images, BERT-base-uncased for text), not on raw pixels or tokens. It learns a single channel-wise linear transformation over these D-dimensional feature vectors and then fine-tunes a new linear classifier on the transformed features. The output is a standard class label, and the entire debiasing pipeline (transformation learning + classifier fine-tuning) completes in seconds to minutes on a single GPU.

## Method

NSF (Neutralizing Spurious Features) is a four-step, non-intrusive pipeline built on a 'strong spurious assumption': when a spurious feature is sufficiently strong, samples of the same class with different spurious attribute values become separable by their relative distance to the class centroid. Step 1 identifies bias presence by detecting minority samples that deviate from the centroid (Theorem 1). Step 2 splits each class into majority and minority groups using the sign of this relative distance, then estimates the unbiased conditional mean C_k of the true data distribution (Theorem 2). Step 3 learns a channel-wise linear transformation t(x) = w(x−b)+b that shifts all samples toward C_k, effectively zeroing out spurious-feature channels while preserving core-feature channels. Step 4 fine-tunes a new classifier on balanced-sampled data (equal draws from majority and minority groups) using the transformed features, forcing reliance on core features alone.

## Results

On four image and text debiasing benchmarks (Waterbirds, CelebA, MultiNLI, CivilComments-WILDS), NSF achieves a mean Worst Group Accuracy (WGA) of 82.01%, improving over standard ERM training (61.24%) by more than 20 percentage points without requiring any bias labels. On Waterbirds, NSF reaches 91.12% WGA (vs. 72.60% for ERM), and on CivilComments it achieves 79.51% WGA (vs. 57.06% for ERM). On the medical CheXpert dataset, NSF improves WGA from 31.21% (ERM) to 70.21%, a gain of +40.00 points, though it remains slightly below GDRO (74.50%) which uses bias labels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NSF outperforms all label-free baselines on mean WGA: it beats JTT (77.43%) by 4.6 points, AFR (78.63%) by 3.4 points, and ERM (61.24%) by 20.8 points. Against label-using methods, NSF (82.01% mean WGA) marginally surpasses GDRO (81.98%) and DFR (81.50%) despite not requiring any bias labels, though it trails GDRO on individual datasets like CelebA (84.27 vs. 88.90) and MultiNLI (73.12 vs. 77.70). On the medical CheXpert benchmark, NSF (70.21% WGA) substantially outperforms JTT (60.40%) but falls short of GDRO (74.50%), which has access to both training and validation bias labels. The method's key advantage is achieving near-label-using performance with zero bias annotations and a training time of only a few seconds per dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

NSF builds directly on the biased-sampled data model of Sagawa et al. [41], which formalizes how sampling bias creates spurious correlations between attributes and labels. It departs from prior debiasing methods in a key conceptual way: rather than assuming spurious features are 'simpler' (simplicity bias methods like Lff, Echoes) or requiring explicit bias labels (GroupDRO, DFR), NSF exploits a task-independent 'strong spurious assumption'—that spurious features must be sufficiently strong to cause bias—and uses the resulting geometric signature (dispersed minority samples in feature space) to identify and neutralize them via a single linear transformation. This positions NSF as a non-intrusive, post-hoc correction that avoids the retraining cost of prior methods while not needing the bias attribute annotations that limit the practicality of group-robustness approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method's practical appeal is strong: it requires no bias labels, runs in seconds (vs. hours for retraining-based methods), and is non-intrusive (a single linear layer after a frozen encoder), making it easy to integrate into existing pipelines. The >20% WGA improvement over ERM, matching or exceeding label-requiring methods like GroupDRO, is well-supported across five datasets with 10 seeds. However, the reliance on the 'strong spurious assumption' limits applicability to weaker biases, and the theoretical guarantees are proven only for binary classification under a specific data model. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-class settings, weaker or multi-attribute biases, and whether the linear transformation suffices for more complex feature interactions.
