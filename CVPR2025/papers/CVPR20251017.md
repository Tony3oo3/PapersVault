## Problem

Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting methods for driving scenes achieve high rendering quality only along the pre-recorded vehicle trajectory, with significant degradation at out-of-path viewpoints due to the absence of ground-truth supervision from those views. Prior attempts (e.g., LidaRF's LiDAR-based color warping) produce sparse, broken pseudo-ground-truth that is visually inconsistent with real camera images, limiting their effectiveness as training signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251017/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three front-camera RGB images, a 128-channel LiDAR point cloud (150 m range) accumulated over 30 frames, and camera poses from the Waymo or CARLA sensor rig. It outputs a 3D Gaussian field that renders novel-view images at laterally shifted viewpoints (1–3 m from the vehicle path) along with dense per-pixel depth maps. Notably, the out-of-path supervision is not a real image but a rearranged rendering produced by the Inverse View Warping pipeline, which is then compared against the in-path ground-truth image using standard perceptual losses.

## Method

FlexDrive combines two key modules: Inverse View Warping (IVW) and Depth Bootstrapping (DB). IVW lifts in-path pixels into 3D using the current depth estimate, projects them into a randomly sampled nearby out-of-path view, and renders them with an occlusion-aware rasterization that restricts alpha-blending to primitives deeper than a threshold β·d₀ to avoid incorrect colors from occluders. The rendered pixels are then rearranged back into the in-path pixel coordinate order, yielding a regular image that can be supervised directly by the in-path ground truth. DB overcomes LiDAR sparsity and noise by first initializing a sparse depth map with confidence-filtered multi-frame LiDAR points, then exploiting the strong linear prior between rendered and LiDAR depth to solve a least-squares rectification (D′ = aD + b) that produces a dense, accurate depth map on the fly. Training proceeds in three stages (warm-up, bootstrapping, out-of-path) with a combined loss of L1+SSIM for RGB and relative-L1/ranking losses for near/far depth regions.

## Results

On the CARLA-based out-of-path benchmark (where ground-truth images are available), FlexDrive achieves PSNR 26.23, SSIM 0.877, and LPIPS 0.372, outperforming the next-best method NeuraD (PSNR 25.10) by over 1 dB. On the Waymo Open Dataset, FlexDrive achieves competitive in-path quality (PSNR 31.25 static / 30.76 dynamic, SSIM 0.892 / 0.886) and strong out-of-path FID scores (62.03 static / 58.12 dynamic at 1 m lateral shift), though the authors note FID is an unreliable proxy without ground truth. The ablation confirms both Depth Bootstrapping (+0.78 PSNR) and Inverse View Warping (+0.92 PSNR) contribute meaningfully over the LidaRF-GS baseline (24.79 PSNR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlexDrive clearly outperforms all six baselines on the CARLA out-of-path benchmark, gaining +1.13 dB PSNR over NeuraD (25.10), +1.39 dB over LidaRF (24.84), and +1.55 dB over StreetGaussian (24.68), while also achieving the best SSIM (0.877) and LPIPS (0.372). On the Waymo in-path setting, FlexDrive is competitive but slightly behind StreetGaussian (31.25 vs. 31.35 PSNR static; 0.892 vs. 0.911 SSIM), indicating the out-of-path supervision does not degrade in-path quality. For out-of-path FID on Waymo (a proxy metric), NeuraD achieves the lowest FID (57.23 static at 1 m), but the authors explicitly caution that FID between in-path and out-of-path distributions is unreliable. The re-implemented LidaRF-GS baseline (24.79 PSNR) confirms that the gains come from the proposed IVW and DB modules rather than from the 3DGS backbone itself. Overall, FlexDrive is the only method that simultaneously maintains in-path quality and substantially improves out-of-path rendering with verifiable ground truth.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlexDrive extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting line of work for driving scenes (PVG, StreetGaussian, NeuraD) by addressing their shared limitation: poor rendering quality when viewpoints deviate from the recorded trajectory. Conceptually, it inverts the color-warping idea from LidaRF (which projects sparse LiDAR points from in-path to out-of-path views) into Inverse View Warping, which lifts dense pixels from the in-path view and re-renders them at the out-of-path viewpoint with occlusion-aware rasterization. The Depth Bootstrapping strategy departs from direct LiDAR supervision used by PVG and LidaRF by exploiting a strong linear prior between rendered and LiDAR depth to produce dense, rectified depth maps on the fly. The CARLA-based benchmark is a new contribution that fills the gap left by prior work, which could only report distributional FID scores without true out-of-path ground truth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to render driving scenes from viewpoints off the recorded trajectory is essential for practical simulators that must handle cut-ins, lane changes, and parking maneuvers, yet prior methods degrade sharply in these settings. FlexDrive's CARLA benchmark provides the first reliable ground-truth evaluation protocol for out-of-path rendering, which the community previously lacked. However, the method still requires LiDAR and only handles small lateral shifts (1–2 m), and the authors acknowledge that truly free camera movement would require combining IVW with generative approaches—an open direction. The constrained dynamic-object modeling (bounding-box-constrained Gaussians) is a practical but narrow fix for tailed floaters, and whether it generalizes to highly deformable objects remains untested.
