## Problem

Existing salient object detection methods rely on CNNs with limited receptive fields or Transformers with quadratic complexity, and state space models (Mamba) have not yet been explored for SOD. Beyond simply porting Mamba, the paper identifies two specific design gaps: standard scanning strategies (Z-pattern, diagonal, S-pattern) break spatial continuity of salient patches in 1D sequences, and common upsampling in decoders is non-learnable and ignores contextual dependencies between hierarchical features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251036/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Samba accepts a flexible range of inputs—single RGB images, RGB+depth pairs, RGB+thermal pairs, video frame sequences, or RGB+depth video—uniformly resized to 448×448. A Siamese encoder processes each modality independently, and a multi-modal fusion Mamba (MFM) integrates them before decoding. The output is a binary saliency map at the original resolution, evaluated with S_m, F_m, and E_m metrics. Notably, the model is trained separately per task rather than jointly across all SOD sub-tasks.

## Method

Samba builds on Vmamba's VSS blocks as a Siamese encoder and introduces three key decoder components. The saliency-guided Mamba block (SGMB) uses a spatial neighboring scanning (SNS) algorithm that, given a coarse saliency map, dynamically chooses left-to-right or right-to-left scanning direction per row so that consecutive salient patches remain spatially adjacent in the 1D sequence fed to S6 blocks; three path variants with altered concatenation orders further improve robustness. The context-aware upsampling (CAU) module pairs each deep-feature patch with its four corresponding shallow-feature patches, concatenates the pairs into a long sequence, and passes it through two S6 blocks with different ordering so that deep features progressively simulate shallow feature distributions before being expanded to full resolution. A multi-modal fusion Mamba (MFM) concatenates flattened multi-modal features and processes them through a single S6 block to capture cross-modal interactions before the decoder.

## Results

On RGB SOD (Table 1), Samba achieves S_m=0.932, F_m=0.930, E_m=0.966 on DUTS and S_m=0.953, F_m=0.965, E_m=0.978 on ECSSD, outperforming the best transformer baseline VSCode-S (S_m=0.926, F_m=0.922, E_m=0.960 on DUTS) while using only 49.59M parameters and 46.68G MACs versus VSCode-S's 74.72M and 93.76G. Across all five SOD tasks (RGB, RGB-D, RGB-T, VSOD, RGB-D VSOD) on 21 datasets, Samba consistently achieves the best S_m, F_m, and E_m scores. For example, on RGB-D VSOD (Table 5), Samba reaches S_m=0.923 on ViDSOD, surpassing the best prior method ATFNet (S_m=0.875) by 4.8 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Samba outperforms all 37 compared baselines across all 21 evaluation datasets and all five SOD tasks. On RGB SOD, Samba beats the strongest transformer baseline VSCode-S by 0.6% S_m on DUTS (0.932 vs 0.926), 1.2% on DUT-O (0.889 vs 0.877), and 0.5% on HKU-IS (0.945 vs 0.940), while using 33% fewer parameters and 50% fewer MACs. On RGB-D SOD, Samba surpasses VSCode-S by 0.5–0.7% S_m across NJUD, NLPR, SIP, and STERE, and outperforms the heavier CPNet (216.50M params) by 1.4% S_m on NJUD. For VSOD, Samba leads VSCode-S by 0.7% S_m on DAVIS and 2.0% on FBMS, and is the only method reporting results on VOS (S_m=0.870). On RGB-T SOD, Samba achieves the best S_m on all three datasets (VT821: 0.934, VT1000: 0.953, VT5000: 0.928), exceeding VSCode-S by 0.8–1.2%. For RGB-D VSOD, Samba dominates with margins of 1.4–4.6% S_m over DCTNet+, DVSOD, and ATFNet, the only prior methods evaluated on these tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to adapt state space models (specifically Mamba's S6 selective scan) to salient object detection, building directly on Vmamba's visual state space blocks as the encoder backbone. It departs from the dominant CNN and Transformer paradigms in SOD by proposing a pure Mamba architecture, while addressing two specific limitations identified in existing Mamba-based decoders: the failure of fixed scanning strategies (Z-pattern, diagonal, S-pattern) to preserve spatial continuity of salient patches, and the lack of contextual dependency modeling in hierarchical feature upsampling. The proposed SNS scanning algorithm and CAU upsampling method are task-specific innovations layered on the general Vmamba/VSS infrastructure, and the MFM module extends multi-modal fusion to leverage SSMs for cross-modal interaction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a pure Mamba architecture can match or exceed CNN and Transformer SOTA across five SOD sub-tasks on 21 datasets with lower computational cost, this paper validates SSMs as a viable backbone for dense prediction tasks beyond classification and detection. The spatial continuity insight for scanning sequences offers a generalizable principle for future Mamba-based architectures in any task where spatial coherence of target regions matters. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other dense prediction tasks (e.g., semantic segmentation, instance segmentation), whether the coarse saliency map used to guide SNS introduces a chicken-and-egg dependency in practice, and whether the per-task training protocol (vs. joint multi-task training as in VSCode) limits the unified framework's practical advantage.
