## Problem

Vision-language models excel at high-level commonsense reasoning but lack the fine-grained 3D spatial understanding required for precise robotic manipulation. Existing solutions either fine-tune VLMs into agent-specific VLA models (costly, poor generalization) or rely on task-agnostic primitive proposals with manually designed post-processing rules (unstable, limited). The paper asks how to develop an efficient, generalizable representation that bridges VLM reasoning with precise low-level manipulation without any VLM fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural-language instruction (e.g., 'Pour tea into the cup') and RGB-D observations from two Intel RealSense D415 cameras (one wrist-mounted, one third-person) as input. It produces a sequence of optimized end-effector poses executed as a robot trajectory on a Franka Emika Panda arm with UMI fingers. Notably, the pipeline also consumes single-view-generated 3D meshes (via Triposr/One-2-3-45++) and universal 6D pose estimates (via Omni6DPose) as intermediate representations, and the VLM (GPT-4o) is called multiple times per stage for object filtering, primitive grounding, constraint estimation, and RRC validation.

## Method

OmniManip proposes an object-centric canonical interaction primitive representation where each object's interaction is described by a point and direction in its canonical space (aligned with functional affordances), enabling structured, reusable spatial constraints. The pipeline canonicalizes objects via Omni6DPose, grounds interaction points using SCAFFOLD visual prompting with multi-view reasoning, and samples interaction directions along principal axes scored by a VLM-caption-plus-LLM-scoring mechanism. The VLM then identifies task-relevant primitives and estimates pairwise distance and angular constraints between active and passive objects. A dual closed-loop system ensures robustness: a planning loop uses Resampling-Rendering-Checking (RRC) to let the VLM validate and self-correct proposed constraints via rendered interaction images, and an execution loop employs real-time 6D pose tracking to continuously update object poses during manipulation. The final end-effector pose is computed by minimizing a composite loss (constraint adherence, collision avoidance, and path smoothness) solved with a sampling-based optimizer (Curobo).

## Results

On 12 real-world open-vocabulary manipulation tasks (6 rigid, 6 articulated), OmniManip with closed-loop planning achieves 68.3% success rate on rigid object tasks and 61.7% on articulated object tasks, substantially outperforming all baselines. The closed-loop planning mechanism (RRC self-correction) contributes over 15% improvement over the open-loop variant (51.7% rigid, 45.0% articulated). OmniManip also demonstrates near-invariant performance across camera viewpoints (7/10 to 8/10 from 0° to 90°), whereas ReKep degrades from 7/10 to 0/10 over the same range.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniManip (closed-loop) outperforms all three baselines on the majority of tasks. Against ReKep Auto (the strongest baseline at 45.0% rigid), OmniManip gains 23.3 percentage points on rigid tasks and is the only method capable of handling articulated tasks (61.7% vs. N/A). Against CoPa (30.0% rigid, 26.7% articulated), OmniManip leads by 38.3 and 35.0 points respectively. Against VoxPoser (15.0% rigid, 16.7% articulated), the gap is even larger at 53.3 and 45.0 points. The one task where a baseline matches or exceeds OmniManip is 'Pick up the cup on the dish,' where ReKep Auto achieves 9/10 vs. OmniManip's 8/10, though OmniManip still leads on the overall rigid total. The closed-loop planning mechanism is critical: disabling it drops OmniManip from 68.3% to 51.7% on rigid tasks, a 16.6-point loss that narrows the gap to ReKep Auto.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of VLM-guided manipulation work (VoxPoser, CoPa, ReKep) by replacing 2D image-space or surface-level keypoint representations with an object-centric canonical space representation, where interaction points and directions are defined relative to the object's functional geometry. It builds directly on Omni6DPose for universal 6D pose estimation/tracking and SCAFFOLD for visual grounding, but departs from prior methods by introducing a dual closed-loop architecture: a planning loop (RRC self-correction via rendering and resampling) that addresses VLM hallucination, and an execution loop (6D pose tracking) that handles dynamic real-world deviations. Unlike VLA approaches (RT-1, RT-2, OpenVLA) that fine-tune VLMs on costly robotic datasets, OmniManip keeps the VLM frozen and uses constrained optimization for execution, trading data efficiency for generalizability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual closed-loop design—particularly the RRC planning loop that lets the VLM verify its own spatial reasoning via rendered interaction images—offers a practical path to reducing hallucination failures without expensive VLM fine-tuning, which is significant given the data bottleneck in robotic learning. The canonical-space representation's viewpoint invariance (nearly constant success rate from 0° to 90° vs. ReKep's 0/10 to 7/10 swing) addresses a persistent fragility in keypoint-based methods. However, the approach is limited to rigid objects, depends on 3D AIGC mesh quality, and incurs multiple sequential VLM calls that challenge real-time deployment; scaling to deformable objects and reducing inference latency remain open problems.
