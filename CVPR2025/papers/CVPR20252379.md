## Problem

Self-supervised monocular depth estimation relies on photometric loss (SSIM + colour) to supervise depth and ego-motion, but this loss is ill-posed in low-texture regions where many depth candidates produce similarly low reconstruction error. The paper identifies this ambiguity as the root cause of poor performance in uniform areas and asks how to introduce sufficient, time-consistent variance in texture-less regions while strictly respecting the constancy assumption required by the photometric loss.

## Input / Output

The method consumes successive RGB video frames at 256×832 resolution. A key detail is that the input to the depth network is not the raw RGB image but a 'variance-augmented' image formed by concatenating the distance transform map (computed over post-processed pre-semantic contours) as an additional channel. Outputs include a per-pixel depth map, a 6-DoF ego-motion vector, a continuous edge probability map (thresholded and post-processed into thin binary contours), and the resulting distance transform map. The distance transform values are further mapped through a 3-dimensional random walk process to increase discriminative variance while preserving the constancy assumption.

## Method

The framework jointly trains three networks sharing a ResNet-50 encoder within a UNet decoder: a depth network, an ego-motion network, and an edge network. The edge network is self-supervised using pseudo-labels derived from Laplacian zero-crossings of predicted depth and surface normals, combined with a contrastive binary cross-entropy loss and a regularisation term. The resulting contours undergo a post-processing pipeline (hysteresis thresholding, non-maximum suppression, morphological closing, contour filtering) to produce thin pre-semantic boundaries, over which a distance transform is computed. The core theoretical contribution proves that the distance transform is the unique (up to isomorphism) function maximising variance within a convex region subject to the Eikonal equation constraint ‖∇f‖=1 and affine invariance, making it the optimal variance-augmenting signal under the constancy assumption. The depth loss combines an L1 reprojection loss on the distance transform channel, the standard SSIM+colour photometric loss, and a novel two-component edge-aware smoothness loss that simultaneously encourages depth smoothness inside instances and sharp discontinuities at estimated boundaries.

## Results

On KITTI 2015 with a ResNet-50 backbone, the proposed method achieves Abs Rel 0.104, RMSE 4.453, and δ₁ 0.885, outperforming Monodeph2 (Abs Rel 0.110) and Shu et al. (Abs Rel 0.108). With a DinoV2 encoder, the method reaches Abs Rel 0.082 and RMSE 4.108 on KITTI, surpassing RA-Depth (0.096) and HR-Depth (0.109). On Cityscapes, the method achieves Abs Rel 0.115 and RMSE 6.79, improving over CoopNet (0.121, 7.01). Zero-shot transfer from NYUv2 to ScanNet yields Abs Rel 0.127 and RMSE 0.312, beating MonoIndoor++ (0.138, 0.347).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 14 baselines across every dataset and metric reported. On KITTI with matched ResNet-50 backbones, it beats Monodeph2 [22] by 0.006 Abs Rel (0.104 vs 0.110) and 0.189 RMSE (4.453 vs 4.642), and Shu et al. [66] by 0.004 Abs Rel. Against the stronger ViT-based MonoViT [89], the method with MonoViT backbone improves Abs Rel from 0.099 to 0.092 and δ₁ from 0.900 to 0.927. In the unconstrained SOTA block, Ours+DinoV2 achieves the best Abs Rel (0.082) and RMSE (4.108), surpassing RA-Depth [30] (0.096, 4.216) and HR-Depth [49] (0.109, 4.632). On Cityscapes, the method improves over its own baseline CoopNet [28] by 0.006 Abs Rel and 0.22 RMSE, and over LearnK [41] by 0.012 Abs Rel. On Waymo, it reduces Abs Rel from 0.157 (LearnK [41]) to 0.125, and on NYUv2/ScanNet zero-shot transfer it achieves Abs Rel 0.127 vs 0.138 (MonoIndoor++ [44]) and 0.153 (IndoorDepth [16]).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-supervised monocular depth estimation framework established by Zhou et al. [91] and refined by Godard et al. [22] (MonoDepth2), building directly on the authors' own CoopNet baseline [28] for joint depth, ego-motion, and optical flow learning. The key departure from prior work addressing the ill-posedness of photometric loss in low-texture regions is the use of a distance transform over jointly-learned pre-semantic contours, rather than deep features (Shu et al. [66]) or off-the-shelf semantic segmentation (Guizilini et al. [24]). The paper also provides a theoretical foundation—proving via the Eikonal equation that the distance transform is the unique optimal variance-augmenting function under the constancy assumption—that no prior self-supervised MDE work has offered.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental and long-acknowledged weakness of self-supervised MDE (ambiguous correspondences in texture-less regions) with a simple, architecture-agnostic technique that can be plugged into existing pipelines, and backs it with rare theoretical guarantees (Lipschitz continuity, smoothness, strong convexity of the loss). The constancy-assumption validation showing that a geometric distance transform is more temporally consistent than learned ResNet-50 features is a useful counterintuitive finding for the field. Open questions include whether the approach can be extended to work without explicit contours (as the authors note for future work) and whether the framework can scale to foundation-model-level training across diverse datasets in a fully self-supervised manner.
