## Problem

Instance segmentation on AR/VR devices incurs prohibitive latency (often >300 ms at 640×640) due to high-resolution inputs, yet users only attend to small gaze-directed regions before shifting their view. No prior method exploits this gaze-driven sparsity to restrict segmentation computation to the instance of interest (IOI), leaving a gap between the full-image processing cost and the actual perceptual need in immersive environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250361/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

FSNet takes a high-resolution RGB image (e.g., 640×640 or 1402×1402) together with a 2D gaze coordinate (u, v) and produces only a single-instance segmentation mask for the IOI, structured as the outer product of a binary spatial mask (h×w) and a class vector (C×1). The internal working resolution is drastically reduced (e.g., 80×80 or 64×128) via saliency-guided downsampling, and the final mask is upsampled back to the original image dimensions. Unlike conventional multi-class pixel-wise segmentation, all non-IOI regions are treated uniformly as background.

## Method

FSNet constructs a gaze map encoding normalized inverse distance to the gaze point, concatenates it channel-wise with the input image, and feeds the result into a learnable saliency-guided downsampler (a 3-layer U-Net) that adaptively enlarges the IOI region in the downsampled representation. A two-branch segmentation head—pluggable with any pretrained backbone (DeepLab, SegFormer, HRNet)—outputs a binary IOI mask and a class label, combined via outer product; training uses an alternating schedule (freeze segmentation, train saliency; then freeze saliency, fine-tune segmentation) with a Dice + area-weighted focal loss to handle small IOI targets. The FovealSeg framework wraps FSNet with temporal logic: saccades are detected via gaze-velocity thresholding (processing is skipped during saccades due to saccadic suppression), and within a fixation segment the last mask is reused whenever the current gaze remains inside the previously segmented IOI, yielding up to 75× FLOPs reduction versus full-resolution per-frame segmentation.

## Results

FSNet consistently outperforms all baselines across four datasets (Cityscapes, ADE20K, LVIS, Aria Everyday Activities) and four segmentation backbones, achieving at least a 0.14 IoU improvement over the uniform-downsampling (Avg) baseline. The best configuration, FSNet+SegFormer-B5, reaches an IoU of 0.58 and IoU' of 0.59 on Aria, and 0.55/0.57 on ADE20K. On LVIS, FSNet+HRNet achieves the highest IoU of 0.54, while on Cityscapes FSNet+DeepLab leads with 0.52. The FovealSeg framework further reduces FLOPs by up to 1.96× over FSNet alone and 75× over full-resolution processing, with FSNet+SegFormer-B5 achieving 84 ms latency on Jetson Orin NX versus 1860 ms for full-resolution SegFormer-B5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares FSNet against two categories of baselines: (1) uniform-downsampling (Avg) variants paired with the same segmentation backbones, and (2) Learn-to-Downsample (LTD) [18], a learnable downsampling method for full-frame segmentation. FSNet outperforms all Avg baselines by a substantial margin of at least 0.14 IoU and 0.15 IoU' across every dataset and backbone combination; for example, FSNet+DeepLab achieves 0.52 IoU on Cityscapes versus 0.26 for Avg+DeepLab. Against LTD, FSNet also wins on all four datasets: on ADE20K, FSNet+HRNet reaches 0.56 IoU versus LTD's 0.41, and on Aria, FSNet+SegFormer-B5 achieves 0.58 versus LTD's 0.41. The gap is most pronounced on Cityscapes (FSNet+DeepLab 0.52 vs. LTD 0.37) and ADE20K (FSNet+HRNet 0.56 vs. LTD 0.41), indicating that gaze-guided saliency sampling is significantly more effective than learned full-frame downsampling for the foveated segmentation task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the saliency-guided downsampling framework from Learn-to-Downsample (LTD) [18] and Learning to Zoom and Unzoom (LZU) [43], but replaces the learned content-based saliency with an explicit gaze-location signal, fundamentally changing what drives the sampling density. It also extends the foveated rendering paradigm [35]—which reduces peripheral resolution in graphics pipelines—to the segmentation domain, where the 'fovea' determines which object instance to segment. The temporal optimization (saccade detection, frame-segment reuse) draws on eye-tracking literature [2,3,17] but is novel in its application to segmentation pipelines, and the plug-and-play FSNet architecture wraps around standard backbones (SegFormer, DeepLab, HRNet) without modifying them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20× latency reduction (84 ms vs. 1860 ms on simulated Jetson Orin NX) and up to 75× FLOPs reduction make real-time instance segmentation feasible on edge AR/VR hardware, addressing a concrete deployment bottleneck. However, the evaluation has notable caveats: gaze traces for Cityscapes were borrowed from the Aria dataset rather than being naturally paired, hardware performance was simulated via GPGPU-Sim rather than measured on-device, and the framework assumes a single instance of interest per frame, which may not hold in cluttered AR scenes. Open questions include how the method handles multiple simultaneous objects of interest, whether saccade-landing prediction [2,3] could further reduce latency, and whether the gaze-guided downsampling generalizes to video instance segmentation with temporal tracking.
