## Problem

Existing Gaussian Splatting surface reconstruction methods (e.g., 2DGS) rely on sparse SfM point clouds for initialization, which are insufficient under very few input views, leading to incomplete and noisy reconstructions. While learning-based MVS can provide dense 3D points, naively combining MVS initialization with Gaussian Splatting still yields suboptimal geometry due to the ill-posed nature of sparse-view optimization, where appearance overfitting and insufficient multi-view constraints degrade surface quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252415/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N sparse posed RGB images (3 views at 576×768 in DTU experiments) and produces a 3D mesh extracted via TSDF fusion from optimized 2D disk-shaped Gaussian primitives. Each Gaussian primitive carries a 3D position, 2D orientation, 2D scale, a fixed RGB color sampled from the source image, and a fixed 8-channel MVS FPN feature vector. The MVS network (CLMVSNet) is run once on the input views to generate dense depth maps for initialization and feature maps for supervision, after which all optimization is per-scene.

## Method

Sparse2DGS builds on 2DGS with four geometry-prioritized enhancements. First, it replaces sparse SfM initialization with dense CLMVSNet point clouds. Second, it incorporates MVS FPN features into each Gaussian primitive and fixes both color and feature values during optimization, supervising rendered features with a cosine similarity loss to enforce geometrically expressive supervision without appearance overfitting. Third, it introduces Direct Gaussian Primitive Regularization (DGPR): a reparameterization-based disk sampling strategy that transforms each Gaussian's orientation and scale into sampled 3D points via Z = p + RSZ′, enabling a standard cross-view feature consistency loss to simultaneously regularize position, orientation, and scale. Fourth, it replaces adaptive density control with Selective Gaussian Update (SGU), which periodically compares patch-warping NCC scores between the Gaussian's own parameters and rendered depth/normal cues, updating Gaussian positions only when rendered cues are more reliable, thereby avoiding degradation from densification under ill-posed conditions.

## Results

On the DTU dataset with 3 input views, Sparse2DGS achieves a mean Chamfer Distance of 1.13, the best among all Gaussian Splatting-based surface reconstruction methods. This represents a 67% improvement over 2DGS (2.81) and a 46% improvement over PGSR (2.08), the previous Gaussian-based SOTA. Sparse2DGS also outperforms the NeRF-based SparseNeuS (1.27) while being 2× faster (10 min vs. 25 min training time), and achieves competitive performance with NeuSurf (0.99) at 60× lower training cost (10 min vs. 10 hours).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sparse2DGS decisively outperforms all Gaussian Splatting baselines: 2DGS (2.81), GOF (2.82), and PGSR (2.08), with the largest gap over 2DGS at 67% reduction in Chamfer Distance. Among NeRF-based methods, Sparse2DGS (1.13) beats SparseNeuS (1.27), VolRecon (1.38), ReTR (1.17), and SparseCraft (1.04 is slightly better), but is marginally outperformed by C2F2Neus (1.11) and NeuSurf (0.99). However, the efficiency trade-off is stark: Sparse2DGS trains in 10 minutes versus 25 minutes for SparseNeuS and 10 hours for NeuSurf, making it 2× and 60× faster respectively. Against the MVS baseline CLMVSNet (1.26), Sparse2DGS improves by 11%, demonstrating that the geometric-prioritized enhancement goes beyond what MVS initialization alone provides. The plain combination of 2DGS + CLMVSNet (1.27) actually underperforms CLMVSNet alone (1.26), confirming the ill-posedness issue that Sparse2DGS's enhancement schemes address.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established paradigms: 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) for explicit surface representation and learning-based Multi-view Stereo (MVS) for dense geometric estimation. It departs from prior Gaussian Splatting surface reconstruction methods (2DGS, GOF, PGSR) that rely on SfM point clouds for initialization—insufficient under sparse views—and from NeRF-based sparse-view methods (SparseNeuS, NeuSurf) that use feed-forward networks with limited generalization. The key conceptual departure is showing that a naive MVS+GS combination actually degrades performance below MVS alone due to the ill-posed nature of sparse-view geometric optimization, motivating the paper's geometry-prioritized enhancement schemes (fixed appearance, reparameterization-based disk sampling, selective update) as necessary corrections rather than optional improvements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving near-NeRF-quality sparse-view reconstruction (CD 1.13 vs. NeuSurf's 0.99) at 2× the speed of SparseNeuS and 60× the speed of NeuSurf, making high-quality surface reconstruction feasible in time-constrained pipelines. The finding that naive MVS+GS combination underperforms MVS alone is an important caution for practitioners attempting to combine off-the-shelf components. The acknowledged limitation—MVS error propagation in occluded regions—remains an open problem, and the method's reliance on a specific MVS backbone (CLMVSNet) raises questions about generalization to other MVS architectures or more extreme sparsity (e.g., 2 views).
