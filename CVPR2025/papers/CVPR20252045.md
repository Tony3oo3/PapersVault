## Problem

Existing CLIP-based zero-shot anomaly detection methods rely on either handcrafted prompt templates (limited vocabulary, expert-dependent) or single-form learnable prompt vectors (overly simplistic, unconstrained prompt space). These approaches fail to capture complex anomaly semantics and generalize poorly to unseen categories because the prompt space is neither properly regularized nor sufficiently explored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252045/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image resized to 518×518, processed by a frozen CLIP ViT-L-14-336 encoder, and extracts patch-level features from the 6th, 12th, 18th, and 24th transformer layers. It produces a pixel-level anomaly segmentation map at the original spatial resolution (obtained by upsampling the patch-grid alignment scores) and an image-level anomaly score that combines a text-branch softmax alignment score with the maximum value of the anomaly map. During inference, 10 Monte Carlo sampling iterations are performed to generate diverse prompts, while training uses a single sample for efficiency.

## Method

Bayes-PFL models the text prompt space as a learnable probability distribution via a normalizing-flow-based prompt flow module that applies K=10 invertible linear-time transformations (with Tanh activations) to map a simple Gaussian prior into a complex posterior. Two distinct distributions are learned: an image-specific distribution (ISD) conditioned on global image features to model context words dynamically, and an image-agnostic distribution (IAD) conditioned on learnable free vectors to encode unified normal/abnormal state semantics. Monte Carlo sampling from these distributions, combined with B=3 prompt banks, generates B×R diverse text prompts at low cost, with the reparameterization trick enabling gradient flow through discrete sampling. A residual cross-modal attention (RCA) module then refines the dynamic text embeddings by attending to fine-grained patch features before computing alignment scores. Training minimizes an ELBO objective (distribution regularization plus data log-likelihood via cross-entropy, Focal, and Dice losses) plus an orthogonal loss to enforce diversity among prompt bank embeddings.

## Results

Bayes-PFL achieves state-of-the-art performance across all 15 industrial and medical datasets. On the industrial MVTec-AD benchmark, it reaches 92.3% image-level AUROC and 91.8% pixel-level AUROC, outperforming the best prior method (AdaCLIP) by 0.3% and 5.0% respectively. The largest gains appear on the medical ISIC dataset, where Bayes-PFL improves pixel-level AUROC by 3.8%, PRO by 9.5%, and AP by 10.2% over the second-best method (AnomalyCLIP). On VisA, the most challenging industrial dataset, Bayes-PFL achieves 87.0% image-level AUROC, a 4.0% improvement over AdaCLIP (83.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Bayes-PFL outperforms all five baselines on nearly every dataset and metric. Against the strongest prompt-optimization competitor AdaCLIP, Bayes-PFL gains 4.0% image-level AUROC on VisA (87.0 vs. 83.0) and 5.0% on RSDD (94.1 vs. 89.1), while also substantially improving pixel-level PRO (e.g., 87.4 vs. 33.8 on MVTec-AD, 98.0 vs. 50.5 on RSDD). Against AnomalyCLIP, the margin is largest on medical data: on ISIC, Bayes-PFL improves pixel-level AUROC by 3.8%, PRO by 9.5%, and AP by 10.2% (92.2/87.6/84.6 vs. 88.4/78.1/74.4). WinCLIP and APRIL-GAN, which rely on handcrafted prompt ensembles, are consistently outperformed by 5–15% in AUROC across most datasets, with APRIL-GAN showing particularly weak pixel-level PRO (e.g., 13.7 on ISIC, 23.0 on BTAD). CLIP-AD is competitive on some industrial pixel-level AUROC values (e.g., 99.4 on RSDD) but lags significantly on PRO and AP, and is surpassed by Bayes-PFL on all medical datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of CLIP-based zero-shot anomaly detection: prompt ensemble methods (WinCLIP, APRIL-GAN, CLIP-AD) that handcraft diverse textual templates, and prompt optimization methods (AnomalyCLIP, AdaCLIP) that learn fixed context vectors. Bayes-PFL departs from both by treating the prompt space as a learnable probability distribution via normalizing flows, combining the diversity of ensemble approaches (through prompt banks and Monte Carlo sampling) with the adaptability of optimization-based methods (through image-conditioned distributions). The residual cross-modal attention module also distinguishes it from prior work that directly aligns static text embeddings with patch features, instead dynamically refining text representations using fine-grained image information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By regularizing the prompt space through image-specific and image-agnostic distributions, Bayes-PFL addresses a key limitation of prior prompt optimization methods—poor generalization to unseen categories—without requiring target-domain data. The consistent SOTA results across 15 industrial and medical datasets, including direct transfer from industrial to medical domains, suggest the learned prompt distributions capture anomaly semantics that are partially domain-invariant. Open questions include whether the Bayesian framing provides genuine advantages over simpler ensemble or dropout-based uncertainty approaches, how the method scales to more complex multi-class anomaly taxonomies, and whether the normalizing flow component is essential or whether simpler distributional assumptions would suffice.
