## Problem

Existing rendering methods approximate the rendering equation per-scene at high computational cost, while inverse rendering remains severely ill-posed due to the inherent ambiguity in mapping a single image back to intrinsic properties. Prior unified attempts like RGB2X treat forward and inverse rendering as independent tasks with separate diffusion models, failing to exploit the mutual information between the two processes to resolve ambiguity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252635/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

As a renderer, the model takes a full set of intrinsic attributes (metallic, roughness, albedo, surface normal, specular and diffuse lighting) and generates a 1024×1024 photorealistic RGB image. As an inverse renderer, it takes a single 1024×1024 RGB image and outputs all six intrinsic channels. Metallic and roughness, which are scalar values, are binarized into grayscale images with binary masks to form channel triplets before VAE encoding, allowing them to share the RGB VAE encoder rather than requiring separate encoders.

## Method

Uni-Renderer employs a dual-stream diffusion architecture with two branches (attributes and RGB) that cross-condition each other, inspired by UniDiffuser's multi-timestep formulation. A timestep selector ensures one branch always operates at t=0 (clean condition) while the other samples from {t, T}, so the model exclusively learns the two conditional distributions q(x₀|y₀) and q(y₀|x₀) rather than all joint distributions, reducing task conflict. The key architectural novelty is a cycle-consistent constraint: during training, the model's predicted inverse-rendering output is fed back through the rendering branch to produce a re-rendered image, and the discrepancy between this re-render and the original RGB is added to the loss, directly penalizing ambiguous decompositions. The model uses x₀-prediction (rather than ε-prediction) and is trained on 200K Objaverse objects, each rendered with 121 attribute combinations (metallic × roughness × lighting) at 1024×1024 resolution.

## Results

On the inverse rendering task (Table 2), Uni-Renderer achieves the best albedo PSNR of 23.20 (vs. 22.67 for IntrinsicAny), the lowest metallic MSE of 0.1182 (vs. 0.1920 for Intrin.ImgDiff), the lowest roughness MSE of 0.1037 (vs. 0.1315 for Intrin.ImgDiff), and the highest normal cosine similarity of 0.928 (vs. 0.908 for GaussianShader). For rendering (Table 1), it reaches 30.72 PSNR on metallic editing and 31.68 PSNR on roughness editing, outperforming the next-best baseline (Subias et al.) by ~2.6 dB. For relighting (Table 3), it achieves 30.84 PSNR, surpassing GaussianShader (26.47) by over 4 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Uni-Renderer outperforms all 11 baselines on every metric it is evaluated against. On albedo estimation, it leads IntrinsicAny [44] (the strongest data-driven baseline) by 0.53 dB PSNR and 0.010 LPIPS, and surpasses the optimization-based NVdiffRec [31] by 9.64 dB PSNR. For metallic and roughness estimation, it reduces MSE by 38% and 21% respectively over Intrin.ImgDiff [22], the only other data-driven method reporting these metrics, and by 63% and 65% over NVdiffRec [31]. On normal estimation, it achieves 0.928 cosine similarity, beating GaussianShader [16] (0.908) and RGB2X [47] (0.871). For rendering, it exceeds Subias et al. [41] by 2.63 dB PSNR on metallic editing and 3.55 dB on roughness editing, while InstructPix2Pix [3] and NTI [30] produce either incorrect background modifications or minimal changes. On relighting, it surpasses GaussianShader [16] by 4.37 dB PSNR, confirming that the unified dual-stream framework with cycle-consistent constraint yields consistent gains across both forward and inverse tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly builds on UniDiffuser's dual-timestep mechanism for modeling conditional distributions, repurposing it to jointly learn rendering and inverse rendering as two complementary conditional generation tasks within a single diffusion pipeline. It departs from RGB2X, the closest prior work, which handles forward and inverse rendering with two independent diffusion models and lacks any cross-conditioning or consistency constraint between them. The dual-stream architecture with a cycle-consistent re-rendering loss is the key architectural departure, and the 200K-asset Objaverse dataset with fine-grained material edits (varying one attribute at a time) provides a controlled training signal that prior inverse-rendering datasets did not offer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Unifying rendering and inverse rendering in one model is practically attractive for pipelines that need to both decompose and re-synthesize images (e.g., game asset creation, virtual try-on), and the cycle-consistent constraint offers a principled way to reduce the well-known ambiguity in inverse rendering without requiring per-scene optimization. However, the method is trained entirely on synthetic Objaverse assets with fixed front-facing cameras, and the authors acknowledge a significant domain gap when applied to real-world images with complex geometry and unfamiliar materials. Open questions include whether the unified framework scales to multi-view or 3D-consistent settings, and whether the synthetic-to-real gap can be closed without expensive real-data collection.
