## Problem

Existing talking head video generation methods struggle to simultaneously produce accurate facial poses and fine-grained details. Motion estimation from single image pairs often fails to capture subtle or complex motions, and a one-shot source image provides insufficient appearance guidance for occluded regions or expression changes, resulting in visible artifacts in the generated output.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251048/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single source face image (one-shot, no multi-view) and a driving video, producing a talking head video that preserves the source identity while adopting the driving video's pose and expression. Internally, the source image is encoded into N-scale feature maps and the driving video supplies keypoint features plus multi-scale appearance targets; the framework supports both same-identity reconstruction and cross-identity reenactment. The final output is decoded from multi-scale compensated appearance features through a progressive upsampling decoder with SFT-based feature fusion.

## Method

The core contribution is a unified framework that jointly learns multi-scale motion and appearance codebooks via VQ-VAE-style quantization, with a code allocation scheme that assigns progressively more codes to larger scales so that lower-index codes capture general patterns shared across scales while higher-index codes encode scale-specific details. A shared transformer-based compensation module uses cross-attention to retrieve relevant codes from both codebooks at each scale: motion codebook compensation predicts flow residuals to iteratively refine the initial motion from coarse to fine, while appearance codebook compensation corrects warping distortions in the warped source features. The two compensations are coupled at every scale—refined motion warps source features that are then appearance-compensated—ensuring motion-appearance consistency. The entire framework, including both codebooks, is trained jointly with code-level losses, reconstruction and adversarial losses, and keypoint supervision, so that gradients from the final generation simultaneously strengthen both codebooks.

## Results

On VoxCeleb1 (same-identity reconstruction), the proposed method achieves the best FID (43.15), PSNR (25.30 dB), L1 (0.0355), LPIPS (0.1846), and AED (0.1071) among all compared methods, while MRFA achieves a slightly lower AKD (1.1823 vs. 1.2039). On the more challenging CelebV-HQ dataset, the method remains competitive, achieving the best L1 (0.0610) and LPIPS (0.2608), though AniPortrait leads on FID (61.56 vs. 71.78) and AKD (2.2397 vs. 3.2562). The ablation study confirms that multi-scale motion codebook compensation reduces FID from 47.83 (baseline) to 44.86, and adding multi-scale appearance codebook compensation further improves FID to 43.15.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On VoxCeleb1, the proposed method outperforms all seven baselines on FID, PSNR, L1, LPIPS, and AED, with the closest competitor being MRFA [22] (FID 48.49 vs. 43.15; PSNR 25.26 vs. 25.30). The only metric where a baseline wins is AKD, where MRFA achieves 1.1823 versus 1.2039 for the proposed method. Among diffusion-based methods, FYE [16] achieves a comparable FID (43.25) but substantially worse PSNR (19.54) and L1 (0.0714), indicating that its landmark-based motion modeling sacrifices reconstruction fidelity. On CelebV-HQ, the gap narrows: AniPortrait [28] leads on FID (61.56 vs. 71.78) and AKD (2.2397 vs. 3.2562), while MRFA edges out on PSNR (22.41 vs. 22.40) and AED (0.2527 vs. 0.2825). The proposed method still wins on L1 and LPIPS on CelebV-HQ, and its AKD (3.2562) is the best among non-diffusion methods, surpassing MCNet [12] (4.1386) and FOMM [21] (3.6098). Overall, the multi-scale codebook approach provides the best trade-off between motion accuracy and appearance fidelity among non-diffusion methods on both datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the keypoint-based motion flow estimation framework of Siarohin et al. (Monkey-Net/FOMM) by introducing a multi-scale codebook compensation stage between motion estimation and image decoding. It repurposes the VQ-VAE codebook learning paradigm (nearest-code quantization, code-level loss with stop-gradient) in a novel way: instead of learning a single codebook for one modality, it jointly learns separate multi-scale codebooks for motion flow patterns and facial appearance patterns, retrieved via transformer cross-attention. This departs from prior talking-head methods like MCNet (which uses a learned memory bank for appearance only) and MRFA (which refines motion via correlation volumes) by unifying both compensation channels under a shared codebook-learning objective and coupling them across scales.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent practical limitation in one-shot talking head generation: a single source image cannot supply sufficient appearance detail under large pose changes or occlusions, and keypoint-based motion models miss subtle local dynamics. The multi-scale codebook approach offers a principled way to inject dataset-level prior knowledge into both the motion and appearance pathways without requiring additional reference frames or 3D priors. However, the evaluation is limited to 50-video test sets on two datasets, and the method inherits the keypoint-based motion estimator's sensitivity to extreme poses; whether the codebook paradigm scales to longer sequences, diverse identities, or audio-driven settings remains open. The joint training of both codebooks also introduces additional hyperparameters (code allocation, loss weights) that may complicate reproduction.
