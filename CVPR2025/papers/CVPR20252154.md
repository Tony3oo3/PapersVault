## Problem

Existing portrait animation methods face a fundamental trade-off: GAN-based approaches struggle with generalization across diverse facial geometries, while diffusion-based methods relying on explicit facial keypoints lose fine-grained expression details and suffer from alignment errors when driving and source faces differ in shape. No prior method simultaneously achieves high-fidelity facial dynamics, strong identity preservation, and robust temporal consistency without depending on landmark extraction accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252154/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single static portrait image (appearance reference) and one or more driving video clips (motion templates) as input, producing a 512×512 animated portrait video. The driving video's central face region (between eyebrows and mouth bottom) is cropped to isolate motion signals, while DWPose skeleton estimates from the driving video serve as an additional spatial conditioning signal to stabilize non-face regions. During inference, the pose is re-anchored by translating and scaling based on the nose-tip offset and skeleton length ratio between source and driving, and eye keypoints are deliberately excluded to avoid cross-reenactment artifacts.

## Method

HunyuanPortrait builds on Stable Video Diffusion and injects two types of implicit control signals into the denoising U-Net via cross-attention. For motion, a pre-trained MegaPortraits encoder extracts coarse identity-agnostic features, which are then refined by an Intensity-Aware Motion Encoder (discretizing expression and head-pose intensity into 64 levels via learned embeddings) and a Motion Memory Bank (64 learnable 768-dim memories shared across blocks) that uses multi-head cross-attention with AdaLN to recover temporal context. For appearance, an ID-aware Multi-scale Adapter (IMAdapter) fuses frozen ArcFace identity features as queries with multi-scale convolutions over frozen DiNOv2 patch features as keys/values, preserving both fine-grained texture and global identity. The entire framework is trained end-to-end with AdamW (lr 1e-5) on 128 A100 GPUs for three days, using DDIM sampling with classifier-free guidance scale 2.0 at inference.

## Results

On 512×512 test images, HunyuanPortrait achieves the best scores across all metrics in both self-reenactment and cross-reenactment settings. In self-reenactment, it attains LMD of 2.02 (×10⁻³), FID-VID of 75.81, FVD of 333.48, PSNR of 32.98, SSIM of 0.81, and LPIPS of 0.11. In the more challenging cross-reenactment setting, it achieves AED of 19.45 (×10⁻²), APD of 19.20 (×10⁻³), Identity Similarity of 8.87 (×10⁻¹), and user-study scores of 4.55 (Facial Movements), 4.69 (Video Quality), and 4.61 (Temporal Smoothness) out of 5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HunyuanPortrait outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric in both self- and cross-reenactment. Against the warping-based LivePortrait, it reduces LMD from 9.14 to 2.02 (×10⁻³) and FVD from 483.38 to 333.48, while also achieving substantially higher facial movement scores (4.55 vs. 3.00) and temporal smoothness (4.61 vs. 4.06). Compared to the diffusion-based AniPortrait, it improves FID-VID from 81.90 to 75.81 and Identity Similarity from 7.95 to 8.87 (×10⁻¹), with a notable gap in cross-reenactment AED (19.45 vs. 22.42). Against FollowYourEmoji, the closest competitor on cross-reenactment AED (20.06), HunyuanPortrait still leads by 0.61 and dominates on temporal smoothness (4.61 vs. 3.33) and video quality (4.69 vs. 4.23). X-Portrait trails on FVD (416.41 vs. 333.48) and facial movements (3.88 vs. 4.55), with the largest gap in temporal smoothness (3.49 vs. 4.61), which the authors attribute to its image-diffusion-based foundation lacking native temporal modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two lineages: (1) GAN-based portrait animation methods (LivePortrait, MetaPortrait) that rely on warping and explicit motion representations, and (2) diffusion-based methods (AniPortrait, FollowYourEmoji, X-Portrait) that fine-tune image diffusion models with separately trained motion modules and explicit facial keypoints. The key conceptual departure is replacing explicit landmark-based control with implicit motion representations extracted via a pre-trained encoder (MegaPortraits), and replacing image-diffusion-plus-motion-module architectures with Stable Video Diffusion as the unified backbone. The intensity-aware motion encoder and motion memory bank are novel architectural additions that address the temporal and intensity variability absent in prior implicit-control attempts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from explicit keypoints to implicit motion representations addresses a fundamental limitation in portrait animation: the loss of micro-expressions and the sensitivity to inter-subject facial geometry differences that plague landmark-based alignment. By grounding the method in video diffusion, temporal consistency is improved without the fragility of separately trained motion modules. However, the computational cost (128 A100 GPUs for three days) and the 512×512 resolution limit immediate practical deployment, and the paper acknowledges ethical concerns around realistic human likeness generation without proposing concrete safeguards. Open questions include whether the implicit control paradigm extends to longer sequences, higher resolutions, or multi-subject scenes.
