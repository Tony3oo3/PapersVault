## Problem

Optical flow estimation degrades significantly under adverse lighting (low light, heavy noise) because the photometric consistency assumption breaks down and texture information is lost. Existing enhancement-based approaches require same-view high/low-quality image pairs that are impractical to collect, or rely on GAN-based style transfer that sacrifices geometric consistency. This paper addresses the gap by using RGBD multi-modal data to implicitly guide a feature-level enhancement network, eliminating the need for aligned image pairs while preserving geometric interpretability.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a pair of RGB images (two consecutive frames) and outputs a dense per-pixel optical flow field, operating within the RAFT framework at 736×480 resolution. During training, depth images are additionally consumed: they are unprojected into 3D point clouds using camera intrinsics, and the resulting sparse point features are densified via learnable KNN interpolation before being fused with 2D image features to produce the supervisory signal. The enhancement network operates entirely in feature space (not pixel space), so the 'enhanced image' is an implicit representation rather than a visible output.

## Method

The architecture has three components: (1) a high-low frequency feature enhancement network that decomposes encoder features via average pooling (low-freq) and subtraction (high-freq), enhances each branch separately (dense small-kernel convolutions for high-freq details, channel self-attention transformer with wavelet fusion for low-freq structure), and recombines them through channel attention; (2) a 2D-3D feature fusion network that densifies point-cloud features via KNN-based learnable interpolation (ScoreNet) and fuses them with 2D image features through a 1×1 convolution to produce high-quality reference features; (3) a RAFT-style iterative flow estimator using 4D correlation volumes and GRU updates. Training is end-to-end with a dual loss: an L2 prior feature loss aligns the enhanced features to the RGBD-fused reference features, and the standard L1 optical flow loss backpropagates through the enhancement network, implicitly teaching it to supplement missing scene information with multi-modal knowledge without ever seeing depth at inference time.

## Results

On the noised FlyingThings3D subset (trained on C+N), the proposed method achieves an EPE of 3.85 and ACC1px of 76.66% on Things(noise), outperforming the strongest baseline GMFlow (EPE 4.18, ACC1px 74.02%) by 7.8% in EPE. On the clean Things subset under the same training protocol, it reaches EPE 3.25 and ACC1px 83.49%, improving over GMFlow by 7.1% in EPE. On the real-world VBOF dataset, the method achieves the best overall EPE of 19.99, with the largest gains on the Sony subset (20.05 vs. GMFlow's 20.37) and Fuji subset (13.72 vs. 13.87).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four optical flow baselines (RAFT, GMA, KPAFlow, GMFlow) on every metric in the main comparison (Table 1). Against the strongest baseline GMFlow, the gains are 7.1% EPE reduction on clean Things (3.50→3.25) and 7.8% on noised Things (4.18→3.85) under the C+N training protocol. Against RAFT, the improvement is larger: 19.3% EPE reduction on clean Things (3.61→2.91) and 12.9% on noised Things (4.41→3.85). On the real-world VBOF dataset (Table 2), the method achieves the best overall EPE (19.99) and wins on Sony and Fuji subsets, but is slightly behind GMFlow on Canon (19.73 vs. 19.48) and Nikon (23.68 vs. 23.49), which the authors attribute to noise-distribution mismatch since synthetic training noise was calibrated to the Sony subset. AGFlow [12] appears only in the parameter/memory comparison (Table 4) and is not evaluated on optical flow accuracy; the proposed method uses 6.2M parameters and 3.1 GB memory, slightly more than RAFT (5.3M, 1.7 GB) but less than GMFlow (9.3M, 3.4 GB).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on RAFT's 4D correlation volume and GRU-based iterative update as the optical flow backbone, and adapts the learnable KNN interpolation from CAMLIFLOW for 2D-3D feature alignment. It departs from prior low-light optical flow work (CEDFlow, FCDN/VBOF benchmarks) by introducing an implicit enhancement mechanism: rather than explicitly denoising or brightening images before flow estimation, the enhancement network is trained to produce features that match those from a pre-trained RGBD fusion network, guided jointly by a prior feature loss and the flow loss. This avoids the paired same-view image requirement of traditional enhancement methods and the geometric inconsistency of GAN-based approaches, positioning the work at the intersection of multi-modal fusion and task-driven image restoration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap—optical flow degradation under low-light and noisy conditions—by showing that multi-modal (depth) knowledge can be implicitly transferred to an RGB-only enhancement network without requiring paired training data. However, the depth supervision relies on unprojected synthetic point clouds from FlyingThings3D rather than real depth sensors, and the VBOF results show only 1–2% EPE gains, suggesting limited generalization beyond the noise distribution used during training. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to real RGBD hardware, whether the implicit guidance can be extended to other modalities (e.g., multi-spectral), and whether the modest improvements justify the additional memory cost (3.1 GB vs. 1.7 GB for RAFT).
