## Problem

Existing anti-UAV systems are limited to positional tracking (detection and localization) and cannot analyze UAV motion states or infer operational intent, leaving a critical gap in understanding whether a UAV is benign or threatening. This paper introduces the UAV Tracking and Intent Understanding (UTIU) task, which requires simultaneously tracking UAVs and generating natural-language descriptions of their behavior and intent.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251434/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes RGB and thermal video sequences (average UAV size 32×21 px, making targets extremely small) along with text questions or prompts for VQA. It produces two parallel outputs: precise per-frame bounding boxes from the tracking branch, and free-form text descriptions of motion state, scene context, and operational intent from the LLM branch. The TDUAV benchmark provides 1,328 sequences, 163K annotated thermal frames, and 3K VQA pairs spanning hills, towns, forests, and buildings.

## Method

JTD-UAV is a dual-branch architecture with three modules: (1) a Precision Tracking Module using YOLOv8 for global detection and a local tracker with a multi-level correlation decoupling backbone for continuous localization; (2) a Semantic Perception Module using a ViT to extract high-level visual features from localized frames; and (3) an Intent Inference Module that processes trajectory-estimated UAV motion videos through a ViT, compresses features via multi-layer MLPs with adaptive resolution encoding, and projects them into an LLM's embedding space using learnable queries. The LLM then generates motion-state captions and answers VQA questions. Training is joint: the visual encoder is frozen while the feature-compression layers and LLM are trained with a combined loss (α·L_LLM + β·L_OT), using AdamW at lr 1e-5 with a cosine schedule, after pre-training the tracker on Anti-UAV, LaSOT, TrackingNet, GOT-10K, and COCO.

## Results

On the TDUAV benchmark, JTD-UAV achieves BLEU-4 scores of 12.75, 11.23, and 14.66 across three subsets, substantially outperforming the strongest open-source MLLM (Qwen2-VL-7B: 9.00, 9.72, 10.36) and closed-source GPT-4o (2.32, 2.39, 3.00). In terms of semantic quality, JTD-UAV attains SPICE scores of 0.3264/0.3012/0.3157 and VQA accuracy of 92.52%/89.94%/91.20%, surpassing the next-best baselines (Qwen2-VL-7B SPICE 0.2737/0.2845/0.2815; KOSMOS-2 ACC 89.15%/87.30%/89.81%). For pure tracking, JTD-UAV reaches 0.631 validation and 0.593 test accuracy on TDUAV, exceeding the best RGB-T tracker HIPTrack (0.615/0.576), and achieves 0.612/0.633 ACC on DUT Anti-UAV and Anti-UAV respectively, outperforming all prior trackers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JTD-UAV outperforms all 26 compared baselines across every reported metric. Among MLLM baselines, the closest competitor is Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct, which trails JTD-UAV by 2.75–4.30 BLEU-4 points and by 0.03–0.05 SPICE across the three TDUAV subsets; closed-source GPT-4o lags even further (BLEU-4 gap of ~9–12 points). For VQA accuracy, JTD-UAV leads KOSMOS-2 (the best ACC baseline at 89.81%) by 1.39–3.21 percentage points. In the tracking-only comparisons (Tables 3 and 5), JTD-UAV surpasses the strongest RGB-T tracker HIPTrack by 0.016–0.018 accuracy on TDUAV and beats the best general tracker KeepTrack by 0.020 on DUT Anti-UAV and 0.114 on Anti-UAV. The ablation (Table 6) confirms that the joint tracking+description training contributes ~2.4 BLEU, ~0.03 SPICE, and ~7.7 ACC over a Qwen2-VL baseline trained on the same data, indicating the gains come from the integrated architecture rather than data alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing two well-established research threads—single-object tracking (YOLOv8 detection + Siamese-style correlation tracking) and multimodal large language models (ViT encoder + LLM decoder)—into a unified anti-UAV framework that had not previously been attempted. Prior anti-UAV work (DUT Anti-UAV, Anti-UAV benchmark) addresses detection and tracking in isolation, while MLLM research (Qwen2-VL, KOSMOS-2, InternVL2) targets general visual understanding without domain-specific tracking integration. JTD-UAV's conceptual departure is the joint formulation of tracking and intent description as a single trainable pipeline with a shared loss, rather than treating them as sequential or independent sub-problems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of UTIU as a formal task and TDUAV as the largest benchmark for joint UAV tracking and intent understanding provides the anti-UAV community with a much-needed evaluation platform that goes beyond positional accuracy. However, the ablation evidence is thin (a single comparison against Qwen2-VL with matched data), the tracking evaluation relies on a non-standard 'raw state accuracy' metric, and the large performance gap over general MLLMs may partly reflect domain-specific training rather than architectural superiority. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to unseen UAV types or environments, whether real-time inference is feasible for deployment, and whether the intent descriptions produced by the LLM are reliably actionable for countermeasure decisions.
