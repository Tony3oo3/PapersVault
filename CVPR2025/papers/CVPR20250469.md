## Problem

Online 3D video view synthesis from multi-camera rigs must produce smooth, flicker-free novel views that are consistent across both the spatial (view) and temporal (time) dimensions. Existing selective-input methods reduce computational cost by using only a subset of cameras but introduce view inconsistencies (abrupt appearance shifts as the camera moves) and temporal inconsistencies (flickering across frames) because they process each frame independently without a shared geometric anchor. The paper addresses this gap by asking how to achieve state-of-the-art synthesis quality with view and temporal consistency in a real-time, feedforward pipeline.

## Input / Output

At each time step the method consumes N fixed-camera RGB images (H×W) and per-view depth maps estimated via RAFT-Stereo on manually selected image pairs (or from RGB-D sensors), along with pre-computed camera poses from COLMAP. It selects only the K=4 closest input views for rendering, making it a selective-input method. The output is a single RGB image at the target novel view, produced frame-by-frame in an online streaming fashion with no per-scene optimization. Training uses 640×352 crops; evaluation spans resolutions from 640×360 (D3DMV) to 1352×1014 (DyNeRF).

## Method

The pipeline has three stages. First, each of the K closest input views is forward-warped into the target camera using pixel-sized isotropic 3D Gaussians whose positions come from the depth map and whose scale/opacity/rotation are computed analytically (not learned), producing K forward-rendered images with per-pixel alpha and depth. Second, an image-based TSDF fuses the K input-view depths into a single target-view depth map; temporal consistency is enforced by (a) suppressing depth variation in static regions via color-difference masks between consecutive frames and (b) integrating the previous frame's rendered depth into the current TSDF weighted by the forward-splatted difference masks. Third, a four-layer U-Net blending network takes the K forward-rendered images, their depth and alpha maps, the TSDF depth, and camera-orientation features as input, and predicts per-pixel blending weights plus a background image for disocclusion inpainting. The TSDF depth guides the network through a depth-reconstruction loss that encourages the predicted weighted depth to match the TSDF target, thereby propagating view-temporal consistency from geometry into the final RGB output. The network is trained end supervised learning on ScanNet, Google Spaces, and DyNeRF with a combined L1 + SSIM + depth + mask loss.

## Results

On DyNeRF, the method achieves 31.17 PSNR and 0.936 SSIM, outperforming the strongest baseline ENeRF (29.51 PSNR, 0.927 SSIM) by ~1.7 dB. On D3DMV, it reaches 32.58 PSNR and 0.098 LPIPS, a substantial margin over ENeRF (27.79 PSNR, 0.157 LPIPS). For temporal consistency, STED drops to 68.76 on DyNeRF and 15.85 on D3DMV, compared to 76.14 and 137.0 for ENeRF, respectively. On the challenging ENeRF-Outdoor dataset (with camera mis-synchronization), the method trades a small PSNR gap (24.01 vs. 25.79 for ENeRF) for better perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.155 vs. 0.167) and view consistency (SDV 1.970 vs. 3.944).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is compared against four online view-synthesis baselines: FWD (forward warping with depth), IBRNet (learned image-based rendering), GPSG (generalizable pixel-wise 3D Gaussian splatting), and ENeRF (efficient neural radiance fields). On DyNeRF and D3DMV, the proposed method achieves the best scores across all quality and consistency metrics, with the largest gains over ENeRF (the strongest baseline): +1.66 dB PSNR on DyNeRF and +4.79 dB on D3DMV, while reducing STED by 9.6% and 88.5% respectively. On ENeRF-Outdoor, the method loses to ENeRF on PSNR (24.01 vs. 25.79) and SSIM (0.703 vs. 0.722), likely due to the dataset's camera mis-synchronization, but wins on LPIPS (0.155 vs. 0.167) and view consistency SDV (1.970 vs. 3.944). GPSG and IBRNet are consistently outperformed by 5–8 dB PSNR across all three datasets. In terms of temporal consistency, the method's STED of 15.85 on D3DMV is nearly 9× lower than ENeRF's 137.0, and its TCC of 0.915 exceeds all baselines, confirming the effectiveness of the TSDF-guided temporal filtering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two established components—pixel-wise 3D Gaussian splatting for forward warping (Kerbl et al., 2023) and image-based TSDF integration (Lawrence et al., 2021, Project Starline)—into a unified online pipeline where the TSDF depth serves as a geometric guide for a learned blending network. It departs from prior feedforward view-synthesis methods (FWD, GPSG, IBRNet) by explicitly inserting a globally coherent depth representation between the noisy per-view inputs and the final output, acting as a view-temporal filter. Unlike per-scene optimization approaches (NeRF, 3DGS) or sparse-camera human-centric systems (Starline, Tele-Aloha), the method targets general dynamic indoor/outdoor scenes in a streaming, per-frame setting without per-scene training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical gap in real-time 3D video: producing novel views that are simultaneously sharp, view-consistent, and temporally stable at ~100 ms per frame. The key open limitation is the strong dependence on accurate depth—specular and reflective regions degrade the TSDF and reintroduce the very inconsistencies the method aims to suppress. Future work could explore learned depth correction for challenging materials, extension to uncalibrated or asynchronous camera rigs, and whether the TSDF-guided blending paradigm transfers to other forward-warping backends beyond 3D Gaussians.
