## Problem

Existing multimodal large language models for computational pathology operate at the patch or ROI level, missing the global contextual information essential for whole-slide-level clinical reasoning. Additionally, the absence of large-scale instruction-following datasets for gigapixel whole-slide images (WSIs) and the computational intractability of processing images at the 100,000×100,000 pixel scale have hindered the development of truly slide-level vision-language assistants.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252601/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a gigapixel WSI (partitioned into 224×224 pixel patches) and a free-form text query as input, producing either a coherent multi-sentence pathology caption or a VQA answer (open-ended or multiple-choice) spanning 13 subcategories across microscopy, diagnosis, and clinical domains. Unlike prior MLLMs that accept only a handful of patches or a low-resolution thumbnail, SlideChat processes the full patch sequence of a WSI, enabling it to reason over the entire tissue context rather than a sampled subset.

## Method

SlideChat adopts an LLaVA-style architecture with four components: a frozen patch-level encoder (CONCH) extracts local features from each 224×224 patch; a slide-level encoder (LongNet) applies sparse attention over the resulting long token sequence to capture global slide context; a multimodal projector aligns visual tokens into the LLM's embedding space; and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct generates the final response. Training proceeds in two stages: first, cross-domain alignment on 4.2K WSI-caption pairs updates only the slide encoder and projector to bridge visual and textual spaces; second, visual instruction learning on 176K VQA pairs fine-tunes all components for domain-specific question answering. The key architectural insight is using LongNet's sparse attention to efficiently process the extremely long patch-token sequences inherent to gigapixel WSIs, preserving both fine-grained local detail and overarching global patterns that patch-only or thumbnail-based approaches cannot capture.

## Results

On SlideBench-VQA (TCGA), SlideChat achieves an overall accuracy of 81.17%, surpassing the second-best method (MedDr, 67.70% with patch input) by 13.47%. On the zero-shot SlideBench-VQA (BCNB) benchmark, SlideChat reaches 54.14%, a 12.59% improvement over the next-best baseline. On the external WSI-VQA* dataset, SlideChat scores 60.18%, outperforming MedDr (54.36%) by 5.82%. For WSI captioning (SlideBench-Caption), SlideChat achieves a BLEU-1 of 0.37 and GPT-score of 4.11, comparable to the dedicated captioning model MI-Gen (0.37 / 4.14) while substantially outperforming general MLLMs like GPT-4o (0.16 / 1.54).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SlideChat outperforms all five baselines on every benchmark. Against MedDr (the strongest competitor), SlideChat gains +13.47% on TCGA (81.17 vs. 67.70), +12.59% on BCNB (54.14 vs. 35.48), and +5.82% on WSI-VQA* (60.18 vs. 54.36). GPT-4o, despite its general multimodal strength, drops sharply when given only a slide thumbnail (34.07% on TCGA, 0% on BCNB), highlighting its inability to extract fine-grained pathology from low-resolution inputs. Quilt-LLaVA, a pathology-specific MLLM, achieves 48.07% on TCGA and 41.55% on BCNB with thumbnail input but remains 12–33 points below SlideChat. LLaVA-Med performs worst among the specialized MLLMs (42.00% TCGA, 30.10% BCNB with patches) and scores 0% on BCNB with thumbnails. On the captioning task, SlideChat (BLEU-1 0.37, GPT-score 4.11) is essentially tied with the dedicated MI-Gen model (0.37 / 4.14) while far exceeding GPT-4o (0.16 / 1.54) and Quilt-LLaVA (0.30 / 3.87).

## Relation to Prior Work

SlideChat extends the LLaVA-style vision-language architecture (patch encoder → projector → LLM) to the gigapixel whole-slide pathology domain, where prior MLLMs like PathChat, Quilt-LLaVA, and MedDr operate only at patch or ROI level. The key architectural departure is the insertion of a LongNet-based slide-level encoder with sparse attention between the patch encoder and the projector, enabling the model to process the full long sequence of patch tokens from a single WSI rather than a small random subset. On the data side, SlideInstruction (4.2K captions, 176K VQA pairs) is over 20× larger than prior public pathology instruction datasets, and the two-stage training (cross-domain alignment followed by instruction tuning) follows the standard recipe but is the first applied at whole-slide scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By being the first open-source MLLM to natively process gigapixel WSIs and releasing both a large instruction dataset and a multi-task benchmark, this work provides the computational pathology community with the infrastructure needed to move beyond patch-level analysis toward slide-level clinical reasoning. The ablation showing that the slide-level encoder is critical for cross-domain generalization (>10% drop on BCNB without it) but less so for in-domain tasks suggests that global context modeling is the key differentiator for real-world deployment across diverse patient populations. Open questions include whether the GPT-4-generated training data introduces systematic biases, how performance scales with even larger LLM backbones, and whether the interpretability via attention maps is sufficient for clinical trust or requires more rigorous validation.
