## Problem

Current 3D language assistants rely on downsampling point clouds or processing them as a single global representation, which sacrifices fine-grained local details and leads to hallucinated object descriptions. While dividing visual input into parts has proven effective in 2D vision-language models, applying this strategy to unordered point clouds requires solving unique challenges in serialization, neighbor search, and local-to-global information aggregation that remain unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251101/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

PerLA takes 40,000 randomly sampled points (with color/normal features) from a ScanNet scene, a natural language text prompt, and an optional visual prompt (user click or bounding box). The point cloud is partitioned into L=6 equal-cardinality parts via Hilbert curve serialization, and both the full scene and each part are downsampled to M super-points via FPS. The frozen OPT-1.3B LLM generates a free-form text response (for ScanQA) or per-instance dense captions (for ScanRefer/Nr3D), with box annotations estimated by Vote2CapDETR serving as visual prompts for captioning.

## Method

PerLA's perceptive scene encoder splits the point cloud into L=6 non-overlapping local parts using Hilbert curve serialization (preserving spatial locality), then encodes both the full scene and each local part separately with the same pre-trained 3D encoder to obtain global and local super-point representations. A Hilbert-based k-NN search (O(1) via serialized indices with geometric labels) associates each global super-point with its k nearest local neighbors, enabling a localized cross-attention module that fuses local detail into global representations using 3D Fourier relative position embeddings. A subsequent GCN message passing step on a similarity-weighted k-NN graph further refines the aggregated features. Training employs a novel consensus loss combining spatial smoothness (L_smt, encouraging similar representations within object neighborhoods) and regularization (L_reg, anchoring fused representations to original global features) alongside the standard cross-entropy prediction loss, improving training stability during local-to-global aggregation.

## Results

On ScanQA (3D question answering), PerLA achieves 78.13 CiDEr on the validation set, surpassing the previous SOTA LL3DA (76.79) by +1.34 CiDEr and 3D-LLM (69.40) by +8.73 CiDEr. On 3D dense captioning, PerLA scores 69.41 CiDEr on ScanRefer@0.5 (+4.22 over LL3DA) and 55.06 CiDEr on Nr3D@0.5 (+3.88 over LL3DA), setting new state-of-the-art on both benchmarks. PerLA also improves BLEU-4 by +3.24 and Rouge-L by +2.56 over LL3DA on ScanQA test (with object), indicating gains extend beyond a single metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PerLA outperforms all 16 compared baselines on every reported metric across both ScanQA and ScanRefer/Nr3D. On ScanQA, the closest competitor is LL3DA [9] (76.79 vs 78.13 CiDEr validation), with PerLA gaining +1.34 CiDEr; the authors' own reproduction of LL3DA scores even lower (74.37), yielding a +3.76 CiDEr gap. Among generation-based methods, 3D-LLM [21] (69.40 CiDEr) trails PerLA by 8.73 points, while classification-based baselines (ScanQA [2], Multi-CLIP [16], 3D-VLP [26]) all fall below 71 CiDEr on test. On dense captioning, PerLA leads LL3DA by +4.22 CiDEr on ScanRefer@0.5 and +3.88 on Nr3D@0.5; the next-best methods are Vote2CapDETR [8] (61.81 / 43.84 CiDEr) and 3D-VLP [26] (54.94 / –), both substantially behind. Notably, the ablation variant LL3DA† (which simply increases query tokens without local-global aggregation) still underperforms PerLA by 3.6 CiDEr on ScanQA, confirming that the architectural contribution—not just more tokens—drives the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

PerLA is a direct architectural extension of LL3DA (CVPR 2024), inheriting its overall pipeline (pre-trained 3D scene encoder, Q-former multimodal adapter, frozen OPT-1.3B LLM) and training data, but replacing LL3DA's single global-only point cloud encoding with a dual-branch local-global encoder. The local-global multi-granularity strategy is inspired by 2D multimodal models (LLaVA-Next, Mini-Gemini) that use partitioned or dual-resolution vision encoders, but PerLA is the first to apply this paradigm to unordered point clouds, using Hilbert curve serialization (borrowed from Point Transformer V3) to preserve spatial locality during partitioning. The aggregation mechanism—localized cross-attention with Fourier positional embeddings followed by GCN message passing—is a novel combination tailored to the geometric structure of point clouds, and the consensus loss is a new training objective not present in prior 3D language assistants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PerLA addresses a concrete and well-motivated failure mode of current 3D language assistants: hallucination of fine-grained object attributes (e.g., color, type) due to lossy downsampling of point clouds. The approach is practically attractive because it improves perception without increasing the number of visual tokens fed to the LLM, keeping inference cost comparable to prior methods. However, the evaluation is limited to indoor ScanNet scenes with a small 1.3B LLM, and the paper acknowledges that robustness in more complex or outdoor 3D environments remains untested. Open questions include whether the Hilbert-curve partitioning strategy generalizes to very large or highly non-uniform point clouds, and whether the gains persist when paired with larger LLMs or token-merging techniques.
