## Problem

Existing targeted adversarial attacks on Vision-Language Models require explicit target labels or target images for supervision, making them impractical to scale across diverse images and tasks. No prior work has demonstrated a self-supervised, large-scale framework that can transform any image into an attack vector targeting any desired output without label supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250559/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target image (whose CLIP ViT-B/32 embedding defines the attack objective) and a random unrelated image as the base canvas, producing an ℓ∞-bounded adversarial noise δ (ε = 16/255) of shape H×W×3. The noise is generated by a decoder that consumes 512-dimensional normalized embeddings from a frozen encoder, and the final adversarial image x_r + δ is designed to be interpreted by the target VLM as the original target image x, enabling it to match the target's text description.

## Method

AnyAttack employs a two-phase self-supervised framework: (1) pre-training a noise decoder on LAION-400M using a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 encoder, where the loss maximizes cosine similarity between the embedding of (random_image + noise) and the embedding of the original target image via a contrastive objective with dynamic temperature scheduling; (2) fine-tuning the pre-trained decoder on downstream datasets with task-specific losses—bidirectional contrastive loss (L_Bi) for image-text retrieval or cosine similarity (L_Cos) for captioning and classification. A K-augmentation strategy shuffles images within mini-batches to create diverse random pairings, increasing effective batch diversity. Auxiliary vision models (ViT-B/16, ViT-L/14 EVA) are incorporated as an ensemble surrogate during fine-tuning to improve cross-model transferability. This is the first application of the pre-training/fine-tuning paradigm to targeted adversarial attacks, eliminating the need for target labels or text-to-image guidance.

## Results

On MSCOCO image-text retrieval, AnyAttack-Bi w/Aux achieves R@Mean of 35.88% on CLIP ViT-B/16, 33.66% on ViT-L/14, and 33.18% on ViT-L/14×336, surpassing the best baseline (SU-Cos) by 15.02%, 18.44%, and 18.54% respectively. On SNLI-VE multimodal classification, AnyAttack-Cos w/Aux reaches 44.8% accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline (SASD-WS-MSE at 24.8%) by 20.0%. On MSCOCO image captioning, AnyAttack-Cos w/Aux achieves CIDEr scores of 20.3 (InstructBLIP), 13.3 (BLIP2), 13.0 (BLIP), and 5.3 (MiniGPT-4), consistently leading all baselines. On commercial VLMs, AnyAttack-Cos w/Aux achieves 31% ASR on Google Gemini and 38% on OpenAI GPT, compared to at most 28% for the best baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnyAttack decisively outperforms all three baselines across every task and model. Against AttackVLM [33], the gap is the largest: on MSCOCO retrieval, AttackVLM-ii achieves only 1.05% R@Mean on ViT-B/16 versus 35.88% for AnyAttack-Bi w/Aux (a ~34× improvement), and on SNLI-VE classification, AttackVLM-ii scores 6.5% versus 44.8%. Against SASD-WS [25], AnyAttack-Cos w/Aux leads by 16.21 points on ViT-B/16 retrieval (35.88 vs 19.67) and by 20.0 points on SNLI-VE (44.8 vs 24.8). Against SU [24], AnyAttack-Bi w/Aux surpasses SU-Cos by 15.02 points on ViT-B/16 retrieval and by 31.1 points on SNLI-VE (44.8 vs 13.7). The auxiliary model ensemble is critical: without it, AnyAttack-Bi drops from 35.88 to 26.23 on ViT-B/16, and the bidirectional loss (L_Bi) consistently outperforms cosine loss (L_Cos) for retrieval tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard targeted adversarial attack formulation, which requires explicit target labels or target images for supervision (as in SU, SASD-WS, and AttackVLM), by introducing a self-supervised objective where the original image's own embedding serves as the training signal. The most closely related work, AttackVLM, also targets VLMs but relies on text-to-image models for guidance; AnyAttack eliminates this dependency entirely. The pre-training-and-fine-tuning paradigm borrowed from foundation models is repurposed here for the first time in adversarial attack generation, with a noise decoder pre-trained on LAION-400M and then adapted to downstream vision-language tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstrated transfer to commercial VLMs (Gemini, GPT-4o, Claude Sonnet, Microsoft Copilot) reveals a systemic vulnerability in deployed multimodal systems that are assumed to be robust, raising urgent questions about whether current safety alignment can withstand image-space perturbations. The 'any image, any target' formulation significantly lowers the barrier to launching targeted attacks, making them accessible at scale without access to target model internals or labels. Open questions include whether the vulnerability is architectural (inherent to contrastive image-text alignment) or specific to current training regimes, and what defense mechanisms could mitigate such self-supervised, pre-trained attack generators.
