## Problem

Existing reference-based line art colorization methods require the reference image to closely resemble the target line art, making them impractical for real-world anime production where poses, angles, and compositions vary significantly. Additionally, these methods lack fine-grained control mechanisms, causing loss of crucial color details during the colorization process, and no comprehensive benchmark exists for systematic evaluation of line art colorization quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250405/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-channel line art image (extracted via LineartAnimeDetector and replicated to 3 channels for VAE encoding), one or more reference color images (encoded to 4-channel VAE latents), and optionally up to 24 user-defined matching point pairs represented as single-channel point maps with unique integer identifiers. The output is a full-color anime image at the same resolution as the line art. Points are entirely optional at inference time, and multiple reference images can be concatenated and fed simultaneously to the Reference U-Net for multi-region colorization.

## Method

MangaNinja employs a dual-branch diffusion architecture (Reference U-Net + Denoising U-Net, both initialized from Stable Diffusion 1.5) where multi-level reference features are injected into the denoising branch via concatenated key-value cross-attention. The key training innovation is progressive patch shuffling: the reference image is divided into patches (progressively from 2×2 to 32×32) and randomly shuffled during training, forcing the model to learn local semantic matching rather than relying on global structural alignment. A PointNet module encodes user-specified point maps into multi-scale embeddings that are added to the query and key vectors in cross-attention, enabling fine-grained color control at specific locations. Multi classifier-free guidance (Eq. 3) allows independent weighting of reference-image influence versus point influence at inference. Training uses two stages on 300k anime video clips from sakuga-42m: the first stage (180k steps) trains the full network with condition dropping and LightGlue-labeled correspondences, while the second stage (20k steps) fine-tunes only the PointNet to strengthen point-based control.

## Results

On the self-constructed MangaNinja Benchmark (200 anime image pairs, 400 inferences), MangaNinja without point guidance achieves DINO similarity 68.23, CLIP similarity 88.34, PSNR 20.37, MS-SSIM 0.962, and LPIPS 0.22, outperforming all baselines on every metric. With point guidance (full model), scores further improve to DINO 69.91, CLIP 90.02, PSNR 21.34, MS-SSIM 0.972, and LPIPS 0.21. The gains over the strongest generative baseline (AnyDoor with mask) are substantial: +4.44 DINO, +4.43 CLIP, +4.13 PSNR, and +0.088 MS-SSIM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MangaNinja outperforms all three baselines across every metric. Against BasicPBC (the state-of-the-art non-generative colorization method), MangaNinja gains +25.59 DINO, +8.70 CLIP, +2.79 PSNR, and +0.068 MS-SSIM, while also reducing LPIPS from 0.33 to 0.22. Against IP-Adapter, the improvements are +12.81 DINO, +5.95 CLIP, +4.18 PSNR, and +0.117 MS-SSIM. Against AnyDoor (the strongest generative baseline, even with manually annotated masks), MangaNinja still leads by +4.44 DINO, +4.43 CLIP, +4.13 PSNR, and +0.088 MS-SSIM. Notably, BasicPBC achieves the highest PSNR among the baselines (17.58) due to its non-generative nature, but it lags significantly on semantic similarity metrics (DINO 42.64 vs. 68.23), indicating poor identity preservation. The paper also notes that AnyDoor requires manual mask annotation to reach its best performance, whereas MangaNinja operates without any such auxiliary input.

## Relation to Prior Work

MangaNinja builds directly on the latent diffusion framework of Stable Diffusion 1.5, adopting its dual-branch U-Net + VAE architecture (inspired by Animate Anyone) to fuse reference image features into the denoising process via cross-attention. It departs from prior reference-based colorization methods (BasicPBC, Animediffusion) that rely on fused attention and require the reference to closely resemble the line art, by introducing a progressive patch shuffling strategy that forces the model to learn local semantic matching rather than global style transfer. The point-driven control mechanism, powered by a PointNet encoder and multi-classifier-free guidance, extends the interactive editing paradigm from methods like DragDiffusion and FreeDrag into the colorization domain, while the training data construction leverages video-based correspondence (LightGlue) in a manner analogous to AnyDoor's video-derived training pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MangaNinja addresses a practical bottleneck in the anime and comics industry by enabling precise, reference-guided colorization that handles real-world discrepancies (varying poses, missing details, multi-reference inputs) that prior methods cannot manage without extensive manual intervention. The introduced benchmark of 200 curated image pairs with pixel-level matching annotations provides a standardized evaluation protocol that was previously lacking in this domain. However, the method is trained and evaluated exclusively on anime-style content, raising open questions about generalization to other artistic styles or photorealistic line art. Additionally, the reliance on video data for training pair construction and the need for user-defined points in the most challenging scenarios limit fully autonomous deployment.
