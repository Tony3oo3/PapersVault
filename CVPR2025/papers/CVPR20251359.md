## Problem

Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) frequently hallucinate objects due to over-reliance on language priors and insufficient attention to fine-grained visual details. Existing mitigation strategies either require costly fine-tuning with annotated data or use contrastive decoding that indiscriminately removes all language priors—including beneficial ones—while introducing significant inference latency. The paper seeks a training-free, zero-latency approach that selectively enhances visual attention without discarding useful linguistic knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251359/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ICT is a plug-and-play module applied to existing LVLMs (LLaVA-v1.5, Qwen-VL) that takes a standard RGB image and a text question as input and produces a text answer, with no change to the I/O interface. The intervention operates entirely within the forward pass by adding pre-computed activation shift vectors to selected attention heads, so it introduces no additional inference latency (measured at ~1.0× baseline speed). The shift vectors are pre-computed offline from 1,500 QA pairs using forward-diffusion Gaussian noise for image-level blurring and Grounding DINO for object-region-specific blurring.

## Method

ICT identifies which attention heads encode Image-Level (global scene) versus Object-Level (fine-grained detail) visual information by training lightweight SVM binary classifiers on trusted (original image) vs. untrusted (blurred image) activation pairs, then applies pre-computed activation shift vectors to the top-K selected heads during the forward pass. The Image-Level module uses full-image Gaussian noise (via forward diffusion) to create untrusted pairs, while the Object-Level module uses Grounding DINO to localize the target object and applies noise only to that region, capturing heads sensitive to specific object details. Both interventions are combined additively in the attention computation (Eq. 8), shifting activations toward visual information without eliminating language priors. The entire method is training-free at inference time: no model parameters are updated, and the shift vectors are computed once offline, making ICT orthogonal and complementary to existing decoding strategies like VCD or OPERA.

## Results

On the POPE benchmark (COCO Random subset), ICT achieves 90.03 F1 for LLaVA-v1.5 (+8.70 over baseline) and 89.20 F1 for Qwen-VL (+6.63), outperforming both VCD and OPERA across all nine POPE subsets. Averaged over all 9 subsets, ICT yields a 7.09% F1 gain for LLaVA-v1.5 and 5.44% for Qwen-VL. On the MME hallucination subset, ICT improves scores by 80.51 points (LLaVA-v1.5) and 34.67 points (Qwen-VL) over the unmodified model. Critically, ICT introduces virtually zero inference overhead (×1.0 latency) compared to 2.1–2.4× for VCD and 3.4–3.6× for OPERA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICT consistently outperforms both VCD [43] and OPERA [31] across all nine POPE subsets for both LLaVA-v1.5 and Qwen-VL. On the COCO Random subset, ICT achieves 90.03 F1 (LLaVA-v1.5) vs. 87.16 for VCD and 88.81 for OPERA; for Qwen-VL, ICT reaches 89.20 F1 vs. 87.81 (VCD) and 86.92 (OPERA). On the MME hallucination subset, ICT surpasses VCD by a wide margin (80.51-point gain for LLaVA-v1.5). The key practical advantage is inference efficiency: ICT adds no measurable latency (×1.0), whereas VCD requires 2.1–2.4× and OPERA requires 3.4–3.6× the inference time due to multiple forward passes or additional decoding computations. ICT also demonstrates cross-model transferability—applying LLaVA-v1.5's shift vectors to Qwen-VL still yields a 4.62% F1 improvement on GQA, confirming the intervention direction is model-agnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

ICT departs from the dominant contrastive decoding paradigm (VCD, OPERA) by shifting the intervention from the decoding stage to the forward pass, where it directly modifies attention head activations rather than penalizing tokens. Conceptually, it repurposes the diffusion-noise trick from VCD to create 'untrusted' samples but uses the resulting activation differences as a static shift vector applied to selected heads, identified via SVM classifiers trained on trusted/untrusted pairs. The two-level decomposition (image-level for global scene trust, object-level for fine-grained detail) draws on prior findings that different attention heads encode information at varying granularities, but applies this insight to hallucination mitigation specifically. Unlike fine-tuning or perceptual-enhancement approaches, ICT requires no parameter updates or auxiliary inputs, positioning it as a lightweight, model-agnostic complement to existing decoding strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-latency, training-free, plug-and-play nature of ICT makes it immediately deployable on top of existing LVLMs without retraining or architectural changes, which is practically significant for production systems where hallucination is a critical failure mode. The cross-model transferability of shift vectors (LLaVA vectors applied to Qwen-VL still yield ~4.6% F1 gains) suggests a partially universal 'trustworthiness direction' in attention space, an observation that could inform future interpretability work. However, the method remains limited: it fails on questions requiring multi-attribute reasoning (e.g., counting 'uncut' fruits), the shift vectors are static and dataset-specific in their training, and the approach has not been validated on video or longer-context settings. Open questions include whether the two-level decomposition is optimal, whether the intervention can be made adaptive per-query, and whether the approach generalizes to hallucination types beyond object existence.
