## Problem

Existing zero-shot skeleton action recognition methods rely on homogeneous semantics (manual category names or one-turn LLM descriptions) that cannot distinguish highly similar actions or capture intra-class spatial-temporal diversity. Moreover, directly aligning skeleton and semantic features into a joint embedding space lacks fine-grained control, causing the model to latch onto irrelevant or shortcut features rather than the discriminative structure-related and regularity-dependent patterns needed for cross-modal transfer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251890/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3D skeleton sequences (3×T×V×M, resampled to 64 frames) encoded by a pretrained Shift-GCN, paired with multi-turn GPT-4o-generated contextual semantic descriptions that decompose each action into three spatial granularity levels (coarse, mid, fine) and three temporal phases (start, mid, end). The text encoder is the ViT-L/14@336px model from CLIP. At inference, the method outputs an action class label via similarity matching against unseen (ZSL) or all (GZSL) semantic embeddings, with calibrated stacking applied to mitigate seen-category bias in the GZSL setting.

## Method

Neuron introduces learnable spatial and temporal micro-prototypes that evolve iteratively from micro to macro perspectives, guided by stepwise context-aware semantics. The spatial stream uses a compression mechanism that selectively retains only the top-α similarity scores between joint features and prototype attributes (with α increasing per phase), progressively isolating structure-related spatial representations while suppressing shortcut features like camera view. The temporal stream splits the sequence into three phases and employs a memory mechanism that combines a recall term (retaining knowledge from the previous phase) with a remember term (integrating new phase information) to prevent knowledge oblivion. Training minimizes a summed contrastive alignment loss across all spatial and temporal phases, and the final prediction aggregates spatial and temporal similarity scores with per-stream calibration factors.

## Results

On NTU RGB+D 60 (Xsub, 55/5 split), Neuron achieves 86.9% ZSL top-1 accuracy and 71.4% GZSL harmonic mean, outperforming the previous best SA-DAVE (82.4% ZSL, 66.3% H) by 4.5 and 5.1 points respectively. On NTU RGB+D 120 (Xsub, 110/10), it reaches 71.5% ZSL and 63.3% GZSL-H; on PKU-MMD I (Xsub, 46/5), it achieves 89.2% ZSL and 77.4% GZSL-H. The method consistently leads in GZSL harmonic mean across all three datasets, indicating effective mitigation of seen-category bias.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Neuron outperforms all eight baselines on NTU RGB+D 60 and PKU-MMD I in both ZSL and GZSL settings. The closest competitor is STAR [6] (the authors' prior work), which Neuron surpasses by 5.5% in ZSL (86.9% vs. 81.4%) and 2.0% in GZSL-H (71.4% vs. 69.4%) on NTU 60 Xsub 55/5. SA-DAVE [32] achieves the second-best ZSL (82.4%) but trails Neuron by 5.1 points in GZSL-H (66.3% vs. 71.4%), indicating Neuron's superior balance between seen and unseen categories. On NTU RGB+D 120 (Xsub, 110/10), PURLS [41] reports a marginally higher ZSL (72.0% vs. 71.5%), but PURLS does not report GZSL; on the harder 96/24 split, Neuron leads PURLS by 5.1 points (57.1% vs. 52.0%). Global-alignment methods (ReViSE, JPoSE, CADA-VAE, SynSE, SMIE) are consistently 5–17 points behind in ZSL, confirming the advantage of fine-grained, context-aware semantic guidance over homogeneous category-name or one-turn LLM descriptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant paradigms in zero-shot skeleton action recognition: global alignment with manual category names (RelationNet, SynSE, SMIE) and fine-grained alignment with one-turn LLM-generated part descriptions (PURLS, STAR). It repurposes the prototype-learning and attention mechanisms from representation learning into a neuron-inspired growing framework, where learnable spatial-temporal micro-prototypes evolve iteratively under multi-turn LLM-generated contextual side information. The spatial compression (top-α attribute selection) and temporal memory (recall-remember gating) mechanisms are novel architectural contributions that address shortcut learning and knowledge oblivion, respectively, rather than relying on a single static alignment step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated limitation: homogeneous LLM-generated semantics make the joint embedding space indivisible for highly similar actions (e.g., 'kicking' vs. 'side kick'), and one-shot alignment overemphasizes irrelevant shortcut features. The multi-turn contextual side information approach and the progressive micro-to-macro alignment offer a practical recipe for more discriminative cross-modal transfer. However, the authors acknowledge that extremely similar and abstract actions (e.g., 'nausea/vomiting' vs. 'sneeze/cough') remain challenging even with enriched semantics, and the multi-turn LLM pipeline introduces non-trivial computational overhead at data preparation time. Open questions include whether the neuron-inspired growth metaphor generalizes to other modalities or tasks, and whether the benefits persist when the LLM is replaced by a smaller or domain-specific model.
