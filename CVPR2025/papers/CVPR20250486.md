## Problem

Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) that can both understand and generate images and text lack personalized knowledge of user-specific concepts. Prior personalization work targets either text-only LLMs, image generation models, or vision-language models that only produce text, leaving the challenge of personalizing unified LMMs for both modalities unexplored. Two key obstacles are identified: full fine-tuning causes catastrophic forgetting of general knowledge, and soft prompts optimized for one task (understanding vs. generation) fail to transfer to the other.

## Input / Output

Given 3-5 reference images of a novel concept, the method encodes the concept into a single special token <sks> accompanied by 32 learnable latent tokens (16 for generation, 16 for understanding) while keeping the Chameleon base model frozen. At inference, the model accepts text queries (e.g., 'Describe <sks>', 'Generate a photo of <sks> in a library') or an input image plus a question, and produces either a text response or an autoregressively generated image. The entire personalized concept is stored in just 32 tokens, far fewer than the ~1k tokens needed for image prompting or ~64 tokens for text prompting baselines.

## Method

Yo'Chameleon introduces a dual soft-prompt architecture where the concept is represented as '<sks> is <g-tokens><u-tokens>', with separate learnable token sets dedicated to image generation and language understanding, respectively. A self-prompting mechanism forces the model to first predict which token set to activate before producing the actual response, resolving the incompatibility between the two tasks that causes joint training to underperform. For image generation, the method employs a 'soft-positive' strategy: hard-negative images retrieved from LAION-5B are ranked by CLIP similarity to the positive examples and divided into groups, with more similar images assigned more learnable tokens (adaptive prompt length) while the full token set is reserved for true positives. Training follows an instruction-tuning paradigm with masked language modeling loss over the response portion, using AdamW at 1e-4 for 15 epochs on a single A100 GPU.

## Results

On the Yo'LLaVA dataset (40 subjects), Yo'Chameleon achieves a recognition accuracy of 0.845 and a CLIP Image Similarity of 0.783 for personalized image generation, using only 32 learnable tokens. For facial similarity (ArcFace), it reaches 0.212, substantially outperforming all prompting baselines. On question-answering, it scores 0.604 (visual) and 0.721 (text), trailing GPT-4o but exceeding vanilla Chameleon and Chameleon+Prompt variants. The method uses roughly 20–125× fewer tokens than the image-prompting baselines (~1k–4k tokens) while matching or exceeding their generation quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Chameleon with text or image prompting, Yo'Chameleon wins decisively on personalized image generation: CLIP-I of 0.783 vs. 0.487–0.589, and Facial Similarity of 0.212 vs. 0.012–0.059, while also improving recognition accuracy from 0.500–0.727 to 0.845 using far fewer tokens (32 vs. ~64–4k). Against GPT-4o, Yo'Chameleon clearly outperforms on image generation quality (CLIP-I 0.783 vs. 0.636–0.680; Facial Sim 0.212 vs. 0.028–0.063), indicating that GPT-4o captures high-level semantics but fails to reproduce pixel-level identity details. On recognition, Yo'Chameleon is competitive with GPT-4o (0.845 vs. 0.841–0.915) while using an order of magnitude fewer tokens. However, Yo'Chameleon trails GPT-4o on question-answering (0.604/0.721 vs. 0.867–0.923/0.978–0.982), which the authors attribute to the weaker base model (Chameleon vs. GPT-4o) and the relatively simple, generic nature of the QA questions where text descriptions suffice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the personalization paradigm from Yo'LLaVA (which personalizes vision-language models for text-only output) to unified Large Multimodal Models that generate both text and images, using Chameleon as the base architecture. It repurposes hard-negative mining from image generation personalization (SuTI, COTI) into a 'soft-positive' strategy with adaptive token allocation, and draws on ToolkenGPT's multi-token tool-calling mechanism but extends those tokens to also carry task-specific latent information. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the finding that understanding and generation objectives are not naturally complementary, necessitating separate token sets rather than a single shared prompt.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study of personalization for unified LMMs, identifying that naive joint training degrades both tasks and that soft prompts optimized for one modality fail to transfer to the other—insights that will inform future personalization efforts as LMMs become more prevalent. However, the practical impact is currently limited: the base model (Chameleon) is relatively small, facial similarity remains well below the 0.4 threshold for good face recognition, and the method has only been validated on 40 subjects. Open questions include whether the dual-prompt and self-prompting design generalizes to larger LMMs (GPT-4o, Gemini), whether it scales to multi-concept or long-tail personalization, and whether the soft-positive strategy can be replaced by more principled data selection.
