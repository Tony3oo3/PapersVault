## Problem

Existing DPO methods for diffusion models (e.g., Diffusion-DPO) use generated images as both winning and losing examples, capping output realism at the suboptimal quality of the generator itself. While using real images as winning images would naturally improve realism, the large domain gap between real and generated images makes single-stage DPO training infeasible. The paper asks how to effectively incorporate real images into the DPO preference structure for human image generation.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt describing a human scene and produces a photorealistic human image via a latent diffusion model (SDXL backbone) fine-tuned on ~300k human images. For the personalized T2I extension, it additionally accepts a reference identity image alongside the prompt, adapting pre-trained InstantBooth LoRA layers with the HG-DPO LoRA weights without further training. Captions for training and evaluation are generated by LLaVA and Qwen2-VL respectively, and the 5k held-out test prompts are used for all quantitative comparisons.

## Method

HG-DPO introduces a three-stage curriculum learning pipeline within the DPO framework that progressively narrows the domain gap between generated and real images. The easy stage trains on pairs of generated images (winning/losing selected from an N-image pool scored by an AI preference estimator) with a novel statistics matching loss that prevents color-shift artifacts by constraining channel-wise latent means. The normal stage introduces an intermediate domain constructed via SDRecon (noising real images and reconstructing with the base model at varying timesteps), using the best-scoring intermediate image as the winning example and the easy-stage winner as the losing example. The hard stage uses near-real images (SDRecon at the lowest noise level) as winning images, effectively integrating a GAN-like discriminator signal into the diffusion model through the DPO preference structure. A separately trained text encoder (following TexForce) is applied at inference to restore image-text alignment degraded during the hard stage.

## Results

HG-DPO achieves the best FID (29.41), CLIP-IQA quality (CI-Q: 0.9343), sharpness (CI-S: 0.9858), and ImageReward (0.7568) among all compared methods, substantially outperforming Diffusion-DPO (FID 112.67) and NCP-DPO (FID 96.72). On prompt-aware preference metrics, AlignProp leads with P-Score 23.02 and AES 6.28, while HG-DPO scores 22.60 and 6.18 respectively. HG-DPO also achieves the highest CLIP image-text alignment score (31.57) and ATHEC sharpness (29.41), surpassing Curriculum-DPO (31.50, 23.36) and all other baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HG-DPO decisively outperforms the naive DPO variants (Diffusion-DPO, NCP-DPO, MAPO) across nearly all metrics, with FID dropping from 112.67/96.72/59.36 to 29.41, confirming that single-stage DPO with real images as winners is infeasible without curriculum learning. Against Curriculum-DPO, which also uses curriculum learning but only generated images, HG-DPO wins on FID (29.41 vs. 35.35), CI-Q (0.9343 vs. 0.8886), CI-S (0.9858 vs. 0.9561), I-Reward (0.7568 vs. 0.6532), and ATHEC (29.41 vs. 23.36), demonstrating the benefit of incorporating real images into the preference structure. The closest competitor is AlignProp, which achieves higher P-Score (23.02 vs. 22.60) and AES (6.28 vs. 6.18) but lags significantly on realism (FID 49.92 vs. 29.41), image quality (CI-Q 0.86 vs. 0.93), and sharpness (CI-S 0.97 vs. 0.99), suggesting AlignProp's reward backpropagation causes style drift and mode collapse. On the public-dataset baselines (HPDv2, Pick-a-Pic v2), HG-DPO improves FID by ~7 points and CI-Q by ~0.03, showing that training on a curated internal dataset with the proposed curriculum yields better human image generation than fine-tuning on public preference datasets. Overall, HG-DPO is the only method that simultaneously achieves top-tier preference scores, the best realism, and the best sharpness, whereas each baseline excels on at most one or two metrics at the expense of others.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Diffusion-DPO by fundamentally changing the preference structure: instead of using generated images as both winning and losing examples (as in Diffusion-DPO, NCP-DPO, MAPO, and Curriculum-DPO), it uses real images as winning images and generated images as losing images, effectively importing the adversarial signal of GANs into the DPO framework. To make this feasible despite the domain gap between real and generated images, the authors introduce a three-stage curriculum learning pipeline (easy → normal → hard) that progressively shifts the winning-image domain from generated to real, with an intermediate domain constructed via SDRecon. The text encoder training follows TexForce, and the overall architecture builds on latent diffusion models fine-tuned with LoRA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is significant: the method improves human image generation quality without requiring costly human feedback for dataset construction, and the gains transfer to personalized text-to-image (PT2I) tasks without additional training, making it immediately useful for creative and social media applications. A key open question is whether the real-image-as-winning-image strategy generalizes beyond human images to other domains where realism is critical (e.g., medical imaging, product photography). The slight FID degradation in the hard stage despite improved quality metrics suggests a quality-diversity trade-off that remains unresolved, and the three-stage pipeline's hyperparameters (timestep ranges, filtering thresholds) may not transfer cleanly to other base models or domains.
