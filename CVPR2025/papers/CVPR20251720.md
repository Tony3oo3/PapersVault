## Problem

Existing Mamba-based image restoration methods flatten 2D images into 1D sequences using scanning strategies that disrupt either locality (e.g., Z-shaped paths) or continuity (e.g., window-based regions), and then aggregate the resulting sequences via simple pixel-wise summation, ignoring the distinct spatial contexts captured by different scanning directions. This paper asks how to design a scanning strategy that simultaneously preserves both locality and continuity, and how to meaningfully aggregate sequences unfolded in fundamentally different ways.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251720/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts degraded RGB images at various resolutions (low-resolution for super-resolution at ×2/×3/×4 scales, noisy/blurry/hazy images at native resolution) and outputs a restored high-quality image of the same or upscaled resolution. For super-resolution, reconstruction uses a pixel-shuffle layer followed by a 3×3 convolution; for denoising, deblurring, and dehazing, a residual 3×3 convolution is applied. Training uses L1 loss for super-resolution and Charbonnier loss (ε=10⁻³) for the other three tasks, with patch-based evaluation at 128×128 (deblurring) or 256×256 (dehazing).

## Method

MaIR introduces two key components within a three-stage architecture (shallow extraction → deep extraction via stacked Residual Mamba Groups → reconstruction). The Nested S-shaped Scanning (NSS) strategy divides features into non-overlapping horizontal stripes and traverses each stripe with an S-shaped path, then connects stripes in an S-shaped manner, preserving locality within stripes and continuity across them; a shift-stripe mechanism offsets stripe boundaries between successive modules to avoid discontinuities at edges. Four scanning directions (top-left→bottom-right, bottom-right→top-left, top-right→bottom-left, bottom-left→top-right) are processed independently by the Selective Scan Operation. The Sequence Shuffle Attention (SSA) block then aggregates the four sequences by spatially pooling them, shuffling channels so that corresponding channels from all four sequences are grouped together, applying a group convolution to produce per-channel attention weights, and computing a weighted sum—replacing naive pixel-wise addition with learned cross-sequence attention.

## Results

On classic image super-resolution (×4), MaIR+ achieves 33.14 dB PSNR on Set5, 29.28 dB on Set14, 28.02 dB on B100, 27.89 dB on Urban100, and 32.66 dB on Manga109, outperforming MambaIR by up to 0.12 dB and SRFormer by up to 0.25 dB. On motion deblurring, MaIR reaches 33.69 dB on GoPro and 31.57 dB on HIDE, surpassing Restormer by 0.77 dB and 0.35 dB respectively. On dehazing (SOTS-Outdoor), MaIR achieves 36.96 dB PSNR, exceeding DehazeFormer by 2.67 dB and UVM-Net by 2.04 dB while using only 0.3% of UVM-Net's parameters. On synthetic denoising (Urban100, σ=50), MaIR+ attains 30.41 dB, outperforming MambaIR by 0.34 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaIR+ consistently outperforms the strongest Mamba-based baseline MambaIR across all four tasks: by 0.03–0.12 dB on SR (×4, Urban100), 0.21 dB average on denoising (Urban100), 0.16 dB on deblurring (GoPro), and 2.04 dB on dehazing (SOTS-Outdoor). Against the best Transformer-based methods, MaIR+ surpasses SRFormer by up to 0.25 dB on Manga109 (×4 SR) and Restormer by 0.77 dB on GoPro deblurring. On dehazing, MaIR achieves 36.96 dB on SOTS-Outdoor, beating DehazeFormer (34.29 dB) by 2.67 dB, though it trails UVM-Net (40.17 vs. 39.45 dB) on SOTS-Indoor by 0.72 dB while using only 0.3% of UVM-Net's parameters (3.40M vs. 1,003.94M). On deblurring, MaIR ties NAFNet on GoPro (33.69 dB) but leads by 0.25 dB on HIDE (31.57 vs. 31.32 dB). For lightweight SR (×4), MaIR-Small surpasses MambaIR-Small by 0.08 dB on Manga109 with fewer parameters (1,374K vs. 1,383K) and lower MACs (136.6G vs. 143.0G), while MaIR-Tiny outperforms SwinIR by 0.12 dB on Urban100.

## Relation to Prior Work

MaIR extends the Mamba-based image restoration pipeline established by MambaIR and Vmamba, which flatten 2D images into 1D sequences along rows and columns and process them with Selective Scan Operations. The key departure is addressing two identified weaknesses in this paradigm: (1) existing scanning strategies (Z-shaped in Vmamba, window-based in LocalMamba, S-shaped in Zigma) each preserve only one of locality or continuity, and (2) naive pixel-wise summation ignores the distinct contexts of differently-scanned sequences. MaIR's NSS unifies the stripe-based locality constraint from LocalMamba with the S-shaped continuity path from Zigma into a single parameter-free design, while SSA introduces a channel-wise attention mechanism to adaptively aggregate the four directional sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The NSS design is notable for being entirely cost-free (no extra parameters or layers) while simultaneously preserving both locality and continuity, offering a drop-in replacement for the scanning step in any Mamba-based restoration architecture. The consistent SOTA across four tasks and 14 benchmarks against 40 baselines validates the approach broadly, though the margins over MambaIR are modest (typically 0.03–0.25 dB). Open questions include whether the stripe-based scanning generalizes to other SSM backbones (e.g., H3, S4), whether the SSA aggregation mechanism transfers to other multi-sequence fusion problems, and whether the approach can be extended to video restoration where temporal continuity adds a third dimension to the scanning problem.
