## Problem

Existing text-to-motion synthesis methods standardize all motions to a canonical body shape, erasing the natural correlations between diverse body shapes and their motion dynamics. This homogenization produces unrealistic motions when applied to non-canonical bodies and limits the range of shapes onto which motions can be reliably retargeted, yet no prior method generates shape-aware motions end-to-end from free-text descriptions of both shape and action.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252187/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single natural-language prompt that jointly describes body shape (height, arm/leg lengths, chest/waist/hip circumferences in a templated format) and the desired motion, and produces both continuous SMPL beta parameters and a shape-aware 3D motion sequence of arbitrary length (T×263, containing root rotation/velocity, root height, joint positions/velocities/rotations, and foot-contact labels). Unlike baselines that require ground-truth motion length, ShapeMove generates variable-length outputs autoregressively, and the shape description must follow a fixed attribute template rather than fully free-form language.

## Method

The framework has two stages. First, a Shape-Aware FSQ-VAE (SA-VAE) encodes shape-normalized motion into discrete tokens via a Finite Scalar Quantizer (codebook of 1000 indices, 512-dim, levels [8,5,5,5]), then decodes them back into shape-aware motion by concatenating a projected continuous beta vector along the time axis, thereby disentangling motion content from body-shape style. Second, a fine-tuned T5 language model (ShapeMove) autoregressively predicts a sequence of motion tokens plus a special [BETA] token, whose hidden embedding is passed through a projector to yield continuous shape parameters—exploiting the fact that LLM embeddings can encode continuous information without an impractically large discrete codebook. Training combines cross-entropy on motion tokens, L1 loss on beta, and physical plausibility losses (floating, foot-sliding, bone-length), with 10% of training shapes replaced by synthetic ones from the Shapy A2S model to augment body-shape diversity.

## Results

On HumanML3D, ShapeMove achieves the best Penetrate score (0.0268 cm), best FID (0.198), best Skate ratio (6.143%), and best Diversity (0.117) among all compared methods. It also attains the highest R-Precision Top-1 (0.413) and Top-3 (0.705) among shape-aware, arbitrary-length methods, and the lowest MMDist (3.533). The SA-VAE quantizer reduces bone length reconstruction error to 45.88 mm, roughly half that of T2M-GPT (83.42 mm) and MotionGPT (94.27 mm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the three methods sharing the same constraints (shape input + arbitrary length), ShapeMove dominates T2M-GPT [105] on every metric: Penetrate 0.0268 vs 0.1789 cm, FID 0.198 vs 0.269, Skate 6.143% vs 6.162%, and MMDist 3.533 vs 3.710. Against MotionGPT [50], the gap is even wider—ShapeMove achieves 0.0268 cm Penetrate vs 0.6986 cm, FID 0.198 vs 1.020, and R-Precision Top-1 0.413 vs 0.128. Against diffusion-based baselines that require ground-truth motion length (MotionDiffuse [106], MDM [86], MLD [23]), ShapeMove still leads on Penetrate (0.0268 vs 0.2401/0.1011/0.3091 cm) and FID (0.198 vs 0.563/0.461/0.882), though MotionDiffuse achieves a lower Bone Length Variance (0.138 vs 0.625). The two text-only baselines T2M [43] and TM2T [44] cannot parse shape descriptions and are substantially outperformed on FID (1.230/1.671 vs 0.198) and Penetrate (0.0939/0.1485 vs 0.0268 cm). In the perceptual study, ShapeMove is preferred over all three strongest baselines by 12–38% of annotators across shape-to-text, motion-to-text, and plausibility metrics, matching the preference rate of ground-truth samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the discrete-token text-to-motion paradigm established by T2M-GPT and MotionGPT by injecting continuous body-shape information into both the quantization and decoding stages. It adopts FSQ (Mentzer et al.) as the quantizer for superior codebook utilization over VQ-VAE, and departs from prior shape-free methods (T2M, MLD, MotionDiffuse, MDM) that normalize all motions to a canonical SMPL body. The key architectural departure is the SA-VAE design: tokenizing shape-normalized motions (content) while conditioning the decoder on continuous SMPL beta parameters (style), thereby disentangling pose dynamics from body morphology. The language model (T5) is extended to jointly predict discrete motion tokens and a continuous shape embedding via a [BETA] token, a mechanism borrowed from prior work on extracting continuous information from LLM embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By generating shape-aware motions end-to-end from text, this work addresses a practical gap where homogenized canonical-body motions produce artifacts during retargeting to diverse body types, a common pipeline in gaming and avatar creation. The ~6.7× reduction in ground penetration over T2M-GPT and the halving of bone-length error suggest that explicitly modeling shape-motion correlations yields materially more physically plausible outputs. However, the approach is constrained to SMPL parameterization and template-based shape descriptions, limiting generalizability to free-form language or non-SMPL body models. Open questions include whether the shape-conditioned decoding strategy transfers to other body representations, how it scales to longer sequences, and whether the data augmentation via Shapy's A2S model introduces distributional biases.
