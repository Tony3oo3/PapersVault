## Problem

Existing deepfake detection datasets are predominantly facial, rely on outdated generative models, and are limited to either detection or localization without explanatory output. No large-scale benchmark exists that simultaneously covers fully synthetic and locally tampered social media images with comprehensive annotations for detection, localization, and explanation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251745/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image resized to 1024×1024 along with a natural-language text prompt (e.g., 'Can you identify if this image is real, fully synthetic, or tampered? Please mask the tampered object/part if it is tampered.'). It produces a three-part output: a three-class detection label (real / fully synthetic / tampered), a pixel-level segmentation mask delineating the tampered region (only when the image is classified as tampered), and a free-form textual explanation of the visual cues supporting the judgment.

## Method

SIDA extends the LISA vision-language model by injecting two special tokens (<DET> and <SEG>) into the vocabulary so the VLM can emit detection and segmentation signals alongside its text output. The <DET> hidden-state is passed through a lightweight detection head for the three-way classification, while the <SEG> hidden-state is fused with the <DET> representation via a single-layer Multihead Attention (detection features as query, segmentation features as key/value, with a residual connection) before being combined with frozen image-encoder features in a decoder to produce the mask. Training is two-stage: first, end-to-end supervised fine-tuning with LoRA (α=16) on SID-Set + MagicBrush using cross-entropy detection loss plus a weighted BCE+DICE segmentation loss; second, a text-generation fine-tuning stage on 3,000 GPT-4o-generated explanations to improve interpretability. The key design choice is conditioning the segmentation branch on the detection token, so that the model's classification confidence directly informs where to look for tampering.

## Results

On SID-Set, SIDA-7B achieves 93.5% overall accuracy and 93.5% F1 for three-class detection (real/synthetic/tampered), with per-class accuracies of 89.1% (real), 98.7% (synthetic), and 91.2% (tampered). For forgery localization, SIDA-7B attains 87.3 AUC, 73.9 F1, and 43.8 IoU on tampered images, surpassing the best CNN-based baseline PSCC-Net (82.1 AUC, 71.3 F1, 35.7 IoU) by 5.2 AUC and 8.1 IoU. On the external DMimage benchmark, SIDA-7B reaches 91.8% overall accuracy and 92.4% F1, outperforming the strongest baseline AntifakePrompt (90.6% Acc, 91.2% F1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIDA-7B outperforms all 8 detection baselines on SID-Set by a substantial margin: the closest competitor after retraining is AntifakePrompt (92.2% Acc, 92.7% F1), still trailing SIDA-7B by 1.3% Acc and 0.8% F1. UnivFD, the strongest zero-shot baseline (64.0% Acc), improves to 85.7% after retraining but remains 7.8% below SIDA. For localization, SIDA-7B leads PSCC-Net by 5.2 AUC and 8.1 IoU, and outperforms the LISA-7B-v1 baseline (the base model before SIDA's specialized training) by 8.9 AUC and 11.3 IoU, confirming that the <DET>/<SEG> token design and attention module add meaningful forgery-specific capability. On the external DMimage benchmark, SIDA-7B (91.8% Acc, 92.4% F1) edges out AntifakePrompt (90.6%, 91.2%) by 1.2% Acc, while all CNN-based baselines (CNNSpot, Fusing, Gram-Net, LNP, UnivFD) fall between 36.5% and 79.4% F1, highlighting the advantage of VLM-based approaches for cross-domain generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

SIDA builds directly on LISA, a VLM designed for reasoning-based segmentation, extending it with dedicated <DET> and <SEG> tokens and a multihead-attention bridge between detection and segmentation features to handle the three-way classification (real/synthetic/tampered) plus mask prediction. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant CNN/Transformer-based deepfake detection literature (e.g., UnivFD, LGrad, Gram-Net) by reframing forgery detection as a multimodal reasoning problem rather than a specialized artifact-classification task. The SID-Set benchmark itself extends prior deepfake datasets (GenImage, AIGCD, ForgeryNet) by unifying fully synthetic and locally tampered images under a single social-media-oriented evaluation protocol with pixel-level masks and textual explanations—annotations that no prior dataset provided simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SID-Set is the first large-scale (300K) social-media deepfake benchmark covering both fully synthetic and tampered images with comprehensive masks and explanations, filling a clear gap that limits current methods' real-world applicability. The VLM-based SIDA framework demonstrates that general multimodal reasoning can substantially outperform specialized detection architectures, suggesting a paradigm shift for the field. Key open questions remain: reliance on a single generator (FLUX) risks data skew, the 300K scale may be insufficient for the full diversity of social media, and localization accuracy (IoU ~44) still lags behind detection accuracy, indicating that fine-grained tampered-region identification remains an unsolved challenge.
