## Problem

CLIP and similar vision-language models are pre-trained on short, concise captions (≤77 tokens) and struggle to capture fine-grained local details from lengthy, multi-sentence descriptions. The unified embedding space is optimized for global, high-level alignment, so fine-tuning on detailed text without specialized techniques fails to produce representative embeddings that attend to specific objects and their corresponding descriptions.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image paired with a lengthy multi-sentence text caption as input and produces aligned image and text embeddings in a shared space. To handle text sequences exceeding CLIP's 77-token limit, the text encoder employs Long-CLIP's positional embedding interpolation. Additionally, the LISM pipeline generates pseudo local pairs by segmenting the image with SAM (filtering regions <1% of total area) and splitting the caption into individual sentences, which are then matched via CLIP cosine similarity to produce one local image-sentence pair per global pair.

## Method

GOAL consists of two components: Local Image-Sentence Matching (LISM) and Token Similarity-based Learning (TSL). LISM uses SAM to segment the global image into local regions (expanded to bounding boxes for context), splits the caption into sentences, and selects the single highest-similarity image-sentence pair via CLIP CLS embeddings, discarding any pair where the matched image is the global image. TSL then propagates local attention by extracting patch tokens from the global image within the local region's bounding box and sequence tokens corresponding to the local sentence in the global text, averaging them, projecting through a learned function, and maximizing their cosine similarity to the respective local CLS embeddings via an MSE loss on the batch similarity matrix. Training combines three losses—global contrastive (standard CLIP InfoNCE), local contrastive (on the pseudo local pair), and the TSL token-similarity loss—with hyperparameters λ_global=1, λ_local=0.5, λ_TSL=1—using shared CLIP encoders (ViT-B/16 or ViT-L/14) fine-tuned for 10 epochs.

## Results

On the Urban1k dataset with ViT-L/14, GOAL (DCI fine-tuning) achieves 89.80% R@1 for image-to-text retrieval, surpassing Long-CLIP (82.60%) by 7.2 points and baseline CLIP (68.20%) by 21.6 points. On DOCCI with ViT-L/14, GOAL (DOCCI fine-tuning) reaches 84.37% R@1 for text-to-image retrieval, outperforming Long-CLIP (78.84%) by 5.5 points. On the DCI dataset, GOAL (DOCCI fine-tuning) achieves 68.93% R@1 (T2I) and 68.43% R@1 (I2T) with ViT-L/14, exceeding Long-CLIP (67.88% and 64.08%) in both directions. On the global-local joint test set (mAP@10), GOAL with ViT-L/14 achieves 69.53% T2I on DOCCI and 64.77% T2I on DCI, consistently leading all ablation variants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GOAL consistently outperforms Long-CLIP across all three evaluation datasets. On Urban1k (I2T, ViT-L/14), GOAL DCI fine-tuning achieves 89.80% R@1 versus Long-CLIP's 82.60%, a 7.2-point gain; on DOCCI (T2I, ViT-L/14), GOAL DOCCI fine-tuning reaches 84.37% R@1 versus Long-CLIP's 78.84%; on DCI (T2I, ViT-L/14), GOAL DOCCI fine-tuning scores 68.93% versus Long-CLIP's 67.88%. Against baseline CLIP on Urban1k, GOAL DCI fine-tuning (ViT-L/14) improves R@1 by 21.6 points (89.80% vs. 68.20%), demonstrating that the global-local alignment strategy substantially exceeds what standard CLIP fine-tuning can achieve on lengthy captions. The advantage is particularly pronounced in image-to-text retrieval, where GOAL DCI fine-tuning on DOCCI achieves 79.16% R@1 versus Long-CLIP's 66.82% (a 12.3-point margin), suggesting that TSL's token-level alignment is especially beneficial when the model must map visual content to detailed textual descriptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

GOAL is a fine-tuning method built directly on CLIP's dual-encoder architecture and contrastive learning framework, extending it to handle lengthy multi-sentence captions by introducing local-level alignment signals. It borrows Long-CLIP's positional embedding interpolation to process text beyond 77 tokens and leverages SAM for automatic image segmentation in its LISM pipeline. Conceptually, it departs from CLOC, which requires 2 billion image-phrase pairs and open-vocabulary detectors for large-scale pre-training, by achieving comparable global-local alignment through a lightweight fine-tuning procedure on a single GPU. It also relates to ViTAA's global-local attribute alignment for person re-identification, but generalizes the idea to arbitrary image-lengthy text retrieval without task-specific attribute taxonomies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GOAL offers a computationally cheap (single GPU, 1–2 hours) recipe for adapting CLIP to detailed textual descriptions, which is a practical bottleneck for retrieval and grounding applications that require more than a single short caption. The proposed benchmarks (DOCCI, DCI, Urban1k with global-local joint evaluation) fill a gap in evaluating models on fine-grained, multi-sentence descriptions. Limitations include reliance on SAM segmentation quality for pseudo local pairs, the heuristic of selecting only the single highest-similarity local pair per image, and evaluation on only three datasets with relatively small test sets. Open questions include whether the TSL mechanism generalizes to video or 3D modalities, whether more local pairs per image would yield further gains, and whether the approach remains effective when captions exceed several hundred tokens.
