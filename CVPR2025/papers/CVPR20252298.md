## Problem

Neural lossless image compression methods achieve strong compression ratios by fitting complex neural networks to represent the joint distribution of large datasets, but this high-dimensional distribution modeling demands intricate architectures that result in prohibitive decoding complexity. The paper asks whether per-image overfitting of a simpler latent variable model—combined with a lightweight pre-fitted autoregressive prior—can match or approach the compression ratios of dataset-fitted methods while drastically reducing decoding cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252298/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (evaluated at 768×512 and ~2K resolutions) and produces a compressed bitstream containing the header, quantized network parameters of the overfitted latent ARM and decoder, a 36-parameter scale map, the latent variables, and the entropy-coded image. Encoding is a test-time optimization process requiring ~140K gradient steps (≈44 s per 1,000 iterations on a 1080Ti), while decoding is a single forward pass through the pre-fitted image ARM and the overfitted latent decoder, taking 13 ms on GPU and 221 ms on CPU.

## Method

FNLIC employs a two-phase fitting scheme: a lightweight four-step checkerboard autoregressive model (the pre-fitter) is trained once on ~360K Open Images crops to produce a per-pixel distribution parameter map Θ_pre, and a latent variable model (the overfitter) is trained from scratch on each test image to produce a residual parameter map Θ_overfit via multi-resolution deterministic latent variables decoded by a learned upsampler and synthesizer. The final pixel distribution is formed as Θ = a × Θ_pre + Θ_overfit, where the scale map a (36 parameters, 9 per distribution component across 4 sub-images) is jointly optimized during overfitting, allowing the model to adaptively weight or suppress the prior. The latent ARM uses Finite-State Autoregressive (FSAR) coding with a second-order Markov model, requiring only L² parallel network inferences regardless of image size. The pre-fitter is trained independently (minimizing its own encoding loss rather than a surrogate overfitter loss) to avoid the mismatch between dataset-fitted and overfitted latent spaces, and the overfitter uses a logistic distribution with weak channel-wise autoregression for RGB sub-pixels.

## Results

On the Kodak dataset (768×512), FNLIC achieves 2.88 BPD, outperforming JPEG-XL (2.87 BPD) on 5 out of 6 datasets and matching it on Kodak. FNLIC's key advantage is decoding speed: 13 ms on GPU and 221 ms on CPU, which is approximately 45× faster than LC-FDNet (607 ms GPU) and over 1000× faster than ArIB-BPS (13,374 ms GPU) while maintaining competitive compression ratios. On out-of-domain datasets (Histo24, LoveDA24, Doc24), FNLIC consistently outperforms JPEG-XL, whereas LC-FDNet fails to beat JPEG-XL on any OOD dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the best traditional codec JPEG-XL, FNLIC wins on 5 of 6 datasets (CLIC2024: 2.68 vs 2.76, DIV2K: 2.75 vs 2.79, Histo24: 3.86 vs 4.08, LoveDA24: 3.30 vs 3.32, Doc24: 2.22 vs 2.30) and is essentially tied on Kodak (2.88 vs 2.87). Among neural methods, FNLIC outperforms L3C and LLICTI on all six datasets while decoding 3–5× faster on both GPU and CPU. Compared to LC-FDNet, FNLIC achieves comparable or better BPD on 5 of 6 datasets (losing only on DIV2K: 2.75 vs 2.71) with a 45× GPU speedup (13 ms vs 607 ms). The top-performing neural methods DLPR and ArIB-BPS achieve lower BPD on in-domain datasets (e.g., ArIB-BPS: 2.78 vs 2.88 on Kodak) but FNLIC reverses the lead on all three OOD datasets (e.g., Doc24: 2.22 vs 2.62 for ArIB-BPS) and decodes 1000×+ faster. FNLIC also demonstrates superior OOD generalization: LC-FDNet, which beats JPEG-XL on 2 of 3 in-domain datasets, fails to outperform JPEG-XL on any OOD dataset, while FNLIC maintains its advantage across all domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

FNLIC transfers the overfitting paradigm from lossy neural compression (C3, Cool-Chic) to the lossless setting, where a latent variable model is overfitted per image rather than fitted to a dataset distribution. It builds on FSAR (Zhang et al., ICLR 2024) for efficient finite-state autoregressive coding of latent variables and on the four-step checkerboard autoregression (He et al., CVPR 2021) as the lightweight pre-fitted ARM architecture. The key departure from prior neural lossless codecs (L3C, LLICTI, LC-FDNet, DLPR, ArIB-BPS) is the two-phase fitting scheme: a dataset-fitted ARM provides a zero-overhead prior, while a per-image overfitted latent variable model captures image-specific distributional structure, coordinated via independent fitting and an adaptive scale-map transformation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FNLIC demonstrates that competitive lossless compression (within ~0.1 BPD of the best neural methods) can be achieved with 45–60× lower decoding inference time, making it practical for encode-once-decode-many scenarios on resource-constrained hardware. The main limitation is encoding cost (~140K optimization iterations per image), which rules out real-time applications, and the network-parameter bitrate overhead degrades performance on low-resolution images. Open questions include whether more efficient optimizers or parameter quantization can close the encoding-speed gap, and whether the overfitting paradigm extends to video or multi-frame lossless compression.
