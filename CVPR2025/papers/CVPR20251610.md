## Problem

Transductive few-shot learning methods rely on critical hyperparameters (class-balance λ and temperature T) that are empirically grid-searched over validation data, yet their optimal values can vary by orders of magnitude across target datasets and pre-training models. This makes exhaustive grid search both sub-optimal and computationally intractable, especially when multiple interacting hyperparameters are involved. The paper asks whether these hyperparameters can instead be learned end-to-end through the unrolling (learning-to-optimize) paradigm.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251610/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted feature vectors from a frozen or fine-tuned backbone (ResNet-18, WRN28-10 for vision-only; CLIP for vision-language) for both the support set (s labeled samples per class, s ∈ {4, 5, 10, 20}) and the query set (75 unlabeled samples). The support labels are hard-constrained into the optimization, while the query samples receive soft probabilistic class assignments. The output is a set of class assignment vectors u_n ∈ Δ_K for each query sample, from which predictions are read off. The entire unrolled network contains only 10 layers with a handful of learnable scalars per layer (λ^(ℓ), T^(ℓ)), making it extremely parameter-light compared to standard architectures.

## Method

The core contribution is unrolling a Generalized EM (GEM) algorithm into a 10-layer neural network where each layer performs one EM iteration: updating distribution parameters (Gaussian means for vision-only, Dirichlet parameters for vision-language) and re-computing soft class assignments via a softmax that incorporates both a log-likelihood term and a class-balance entropy term. Two hyperparameters are made learnable per layer: λ^(ℓ) (class-balance strength, parameterized as Softplus to ensure non-negativity) and T^(ℓ) (temperature controlling assignment softness, parameterized as 1+Softplus to avoid vanishing gradients). The unrolled network is trained end-to-end by minimizing cross-entropy on validation tasks, allowing the hyperparameters to adapt not only to the dataset but also to vary across the 10 layers of the processing pipeline. This recovers PADDLE (Gaussian, T=1, λ=|Q|) and EM-Dirichlet (Dirichlet, T=1) as special cases while enabling data-driven optimization of these parameters.

## Results

On the vision-language (CLIP) benchmark with 4-shot tasks across 11 datasets, UNEM-Dirichlet achieves 77.8% average accuracy, outperforming the original EM-Dirichlet [33] (73.6%) by 4.2% on average and up to 7.5% on challenging fine-grained datasets (SUN397: 88.5% vs 80.9%; Stanford Cars: 80.0% vs 73.5%). On vision-only benchmarks, UNEM-Gaussian with WRN28-10 backbone reaches 71.6% on mini-ImageNet 5-shot and 54.1% on tiered-ImageNet 5-shot, surpassing PADDLE [32] by up to 10% (62.6% and 43.9% respectively). The method also outperforms all inductive CLIP baselines (Tip-Adapter, CoOp) by 5–15% on most datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UNEM-Dirichlet outperforms all 12 baselines on the 11-dataset CLIP benchmark. Against its direct iterative predecessor EM-Dirichlet [33], the unrolled version gains 4.2% on average, with the largest margins on fine-grained datasets: SUN397 (+7.6%), Stanford Cars (+6.5%), and FGVC Aircraft (+5.5%), where the fixed λ=K/K_eff·|Q| used in [33] is particularly suboptimal. Among transductive baselines, PADDLE [32] (60.9% avg) and α-TIM [50] (57.8% avg) trail UNEM-Dirichlet by 17% and 20% respectively, while LaplacianShot [60] (64.8%) and BD-CSPN [28] (57.5%) are further behind. Against inductive CLIP methods, UNEM-Dirichlet surpasses Tip-Adapter [56] (68.3%) by 9.5% and CoOp [57] (65.5%) by 12.3% on average. On vision-only benchmarks (Tables 1–2), UNEM-Gaussian with WRN28-10 beats PADDLE [32] by up to 10% (5-shot tiered-ImageNet: 54.1% vs 43.9%), α-TIM [50] by up to 8.9% (5-shot mini-ImageNet: 71.6% vs 71.5% is a near-tie, but 10-shot: 79.2% vs 75.2%), and all other baselines (TIM [4], α-AM [24], LaplacianShot [60], Baseline [8], PT-MAP [17], BD-CSPN [28], LR+ICI [52]) by 5–40+ points depending on dataset and shot setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the algorithm-unrolling (learning-to-optimize) paradigm, well established in signal and image processing [35], to the transductive few-shot learning setting for the first time. It generalizes the EM-based transductive inference of PADDLE [32] (Gaussian case) and EM-Dirichlet [33] (Dirichlet/CLIP case) by making two previously fixed hyperparameters—class-balance weight λ and temperature T—explicit and layer-wise learnable. The resulting UNEM architecture maps each EM iteration to a network layer, so the iterative optimizer becomes a differentiable, trainable module whose parameters are optimized end-to-end on validation tasks rather than grid-searched manually.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: the class-balance hyperparameter λ can vary by orders of magnitude across datasets (Figure 1), making manual grid search both sub-optimal and intractable, especially when multiple hyperparameters interact. By learning these parameters through unrolling, the method achieves up to 10% accuracy gains over its iterative counterpart in the vision-only setting and 7.5% in the vision-language setting, while remaining extremely parameter-efficient (a few tenths of a parameter compared to standard networks). Open questions include whether the learned hyperparameters transfer across pre-training backbones or datasets without retraining, and whether the unrolling framework extends beyond EM-based clustering to other transductive inference schemes (e.g., optimal transport, label propagation).
