## Problem

Multi-identity group photo generation suffers from ID leakage, where facial features of one person bleed into another's representation, causing incorrect resemblance, swapped identities, or visual artifacts. Existing solutions either depend on fragile segmentation models with multi-stage pipelines (OMG) or rely on approximate attention masks that cannot prevent information propagation through self-attention and convolutional layers (InstantFamily), and none offer a simple, robust mechanism to bind each identity to a precise 2D position.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, N reference face images (encoded as 512-dim ArcFace vectors), and nose-tip (x,y) coordinates for each identity, plus an optional spatial condition (pose, canny edge, or depth map). Face features are projected into 64×64 RGB ID patches rendered onto a black canvas (or overlaid on the pose image) and into 2048×16 token embeddings appended to the SDXL text embedding sequence. The output is a single group photo where each specified identity appears at its designated location, generated in ~10 seconds on an A100 GPU with 50 denoising timesteps.

## Method

ID-Patch encodes each identity in two complementary forms via a Perceiver Resampler: a small RGB patch (functioning like a QR code) placed at the face location on a ControlNet conditioning image for precise spatial localization, and a set of token embeddings concatenated to the text embedding for cross-attention-based facial detail injection. The key architectural insight is that ControlNet handles 'where' while cross-attention handles 'what', decoupling position control from identity appearance. A two-stage training procedure is critical: Stage 1 omits ID embeddings to force the network to learn distinctive, visually separable ID patches; Stage 2 reintroduces embeddings to refine resemblance. At inference, an ID token injection ratio of 0.8 skips identity tokens during the first 20% of timesteps to preserve general composition before refining identity-specific details. The approach requires no segmentation model, no separate denoising passes per identity, and integrates with any ControlNet-compatible spatial condition without additional branches.

## Results

On the pose-conditioned multi-ID generation benchmark, ID-Patch achieves the best identity resemblance (0.751 CosSim) and ID-position association accuracy (0.958), surpassing OMG+InstantID (0.644 / 0.926) and InstantFamily (0.683 / 0.862). Text alignment scores are comparable across all methods (0.271–0.278). In terms of efficiency, ID-Patch generates an image in 9.69 s on an A100 GPU, making it ~7× faster than OMG (84.83 s) and slightly faster than InstantFamily (10.02 s), with near-constant runtime regardless of the number of faces.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ID-Patch outperforms both baselines on the two most critical metrics: identity resemblance and ID-position association accuracy. Compared to OMG+InstantID, ID-Patch improves ID resemblance by 0.107 (0.751 vs. 0.644) and association accuracy by 0.032 (0.958 vs. 0.926), while being roughly 8.7× faster (9.69 s vs. 84.83 s); OMG's two-stage pipeline (generate-then-paint) causes semantic conflicts and its per-identity denoising makes runtime scale linearly with face count. Against InstantFamily, ID-Patch gains 0.068 in ID resemblance (0.751 vs. 0.683) and 0.096 in association accuracy (0.958 vs. 0.862), indicating that InstantFamily's coarse attention-mask approach still suffers from ID leakage through self-attention and convolutional layers. Text alignment is essentially tied across all three methods (0.271–0.278), so the gains come purely from better identity preservation and spatial control without sacrificing prompt fidelity. As the number of faces grows, OMG and InstantFamily degrade more sharply than ID-Patch, and OMG's runtime increases linearly (≈2 min for 8 people) while ID-Patch stays near-constant at ~10 s.

## Relation to Prior Work

ID-Patch builds directly on the ControlNet architecture for spatial conditioning, repurposing it to carry identity-specific visual patches rather than generic pose or edge maps. It departs from the two-stage segmentation-based pipeline of OMG and the masked cross-attention strategy of InstantFamily by unifying spatial ID localization (via patches on the ControlNet input) and facial detail injection (via embeddings appended to text tokens) into a single-pass generation process. The use of the Perceiver Resampler to project ArcFace features into both a small RGB patch and a token embedding is a novel dual-projection design, and the two-stage training scheme—first forcing the network to encode identity into patches before introducing embeddings—is a practical training trick not seen in prior multi-ID work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practically important simplification: eliminating the segmentation dependency and linear-in-N runtime of OMG while matching or exceeding InstantFamily's speed, making multi-ID group photo generation viable for interactive applications. The dramatic ablation showing association accuracy collapsing from 0.958 to 0.345 without the patch projection underscores that spatial ID anchoring is the critical component, not just facial detail injection. Open questions remain around disentangling identity from lighting/expression in the input face, scaling to very large groups where face area shrinks, and whether the approach transfers to more advanced base models beyond SDXL. The authors also note a persistent gap between single-person and multi-person resemblance, suggesting the problem is not yet fully solved.
