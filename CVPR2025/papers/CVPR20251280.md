## Problem

Standard anomaly detection benchmarks define normality with fixed, category-level criteria and assess objects in isolation, ignoring inter-object relationships. In real-world quality control, however, 'normality' is scene-specific: an object is anomalous only relative to its neighbors (e.g., a blue-handled cup among yellow-handled ones). No prior benchmark or method addresses this scene-relative, cross-instance comparison task, particularly under multi-view occlusion.

## Input / Output

The method consumes M=5 sparse-view RGB images at 256×256 resolution along with their camera projection matrices (intrinsic K, rotation R, translation T) of a scene containing N instances of the same rigid object. It outputs a binary anomaly label and a 3D bounding box for each instance, where the label is defined relative to other objects in the scene rather than against a fixed template. At inference the model accepts a variable number of views (tested from 1 to 20), and no ground-truth box coordinates are required during training.

## Method

The pipeline constructs a 3D voxel feature volume (96×96×16) by back-projecting ResNet50-FPN 2D features from all views and refining them with an Atlas-style 3D CNN. During training only, a NeRF-style differentiable rendering block reconstructs geometry and appearance while simultaneously distilling DINOv2 features through the volume renderer, enforcing part-aware and geometrically consistent 3D representations. Objects are localized via density thresholding and DBScan, then encoded as 8×8×8 feature volumes via RoI pooling. The key classification mechanism is a sparse voxel attention module that, for each pair of objects, restricts attention to the top-k=20 geometrically corresponding voxel locations (identified through the DINOv2-distilled features) rather than computing full self-attention, thereby eliminating noisy cross-object interactions. Three such attention blocks with 8 heads are stacked, followed by 3D CNN downsampling and a 2-layer MLP for the final binary prediction, trained with binary cross-entropy plus the reconstruction loss.

## Results

On ToysAD-8K-Seen, the proposed method achieves 91.78 AUC and 83.21% accuracy, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline DETR3D (79.16 AUC, 67.37% accuracy) by 12.6 AUC points. On the unseen-category split (ToysAD-8K-Unseen), the method retains 89.15 AUC and 81.57% accuracy, showing only a modest 2.6 AUC drop. On PartsAD-15K, it reaches 86.12 AUC and 79.68% accuracy, again leading DETR3D (74.49 AUC) by 11.6 AUC points. The method also demonstrates strong robustness, with less than 1.5% AUC degradation when object count varies from 3 to 12 per scene.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: a reconstruction-based method (Recon-Recog, using COLMAP point clouds + DGCNN + triplet loss), ImVoxelNet [33] (convolutional voxel-based 3D detection), and DETR3D [44] (transformer-based 3D detection). The proposed method decisively outperforms all three on every dataset split, with margins of 12–18 AUC points. DETR3D is the strongest baseline, reaching 79.16 AUC on ToysAD-8K-Seen, yet still trails the proposed method by 12.6 AUC points. The reconstruction-based baseline performs worst overall (72.78–73.45 AUC), as its accuracy is highly sensitive to multi-view reconstruction quality. The authors note that both 3D detection baselines struggle with fine-grained intra-group comparison, tending to memorize certain anomaly types rather than capturing cross-object relations, which is precisely the capability the proposed sparse voxel attention mechanism provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from standard anomaly detection (MVTec-AD, VisA, PAD) which evaluates single objects against a fixed notion of normality, and from few-shot AD which uses support sets but still operates on single-object, single-view inputs. Methodologically, it repurposes the 2D-to-3D voxel projection pipeline from Atlas [28] and the volume rendering formulation from NeRF [27] / few-view reconstruction [21], but redirects them toward cross-instance comparison rather than reconstruction or detection. The key conceptual departure is the use of DINOv2 feature distillation through differentiable rendering to obtain part-aware, geometrically consistent 3D representations, enabling pose-agnostic local correspondence matching between object instances via sparse voxel attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing scene-relative anomaly detection and releasing two benchmarks (ToysAD-8K, PartsAD-15K), the paper opens a research direction that mirrors real-world quality-control workflows where 'normal' is defined by the peer group rather than a fixed template. The demonstrated generalization from synthetic training to real tabletop scenes is encouraging, but the model is limited to rigid, non-touching, non-fully-occluded objects and relies on known camera poses, leaving open questions about deployment in uncontrolled industrial settings. Extending the framework to non-rigid or articulated objects, handling scenes where most instances are anomalous, and reducing dependence on accurate multi-view calibration are natural next steps.
