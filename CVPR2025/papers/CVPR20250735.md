## Problem

Existing audio-visual grounding models handle either spoken language or non-speech sounds independently, or at best sequentially without overlap, making them unable to process the mixed audio that characterizes real-world scenes. Moreover, prior multi-head approaches like DenseAV fail to achieve effective disentanglement: each head retains information from the opposite audio type (information leakage), degrading performance when both types are present simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250735/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single 224×224 RGB image and a 10-second audio waveform sampled at 16 kHz, where the audio may be a clean single-type signal or a mixture of speech and non-speech sounds. It produces a per-head similarity volume of shape K×F×T×H×W (K=2 for speech and sound heads), from which spatial segmentation maps (localizing sound-producing objects or referenced objects) and cross-modal retrieval scores are derived via spatial max-pooling and frequency-temporal averaging.

## Method

The architecture pairs a DINO vision backbone (fine-tuned with LoRA rank 8) and a HuBERT Large audio backbone (fully fine-tuned), each followed by an aligner (Channel-wise LayerNorm + conv layers) that reshapes features into a shared C×K×H×W or C×K×F×T space with K=2 specialized heads. Training is self-supervised contrastive learning with two jointly optimized objectives: a correspondence loss that uses clean single-type audio to establish audio-visual matching via max-pooled similarity across heads, and a disentanglement loss that feeds mixed audio (sound + speech summed) and forces each specialized head to align only with its corresponding image features, thereby separating the mixture at the feature level rather than generating separated waveforms. This mix-and-separate strategy eliminates the need for audio types to appear sequentially during training or inference and improves head specialization, as verified by ablations showing ~20% retrieval drop without correspondence loss and ~22% leakage increase without disentanglement loss.

## Results

On the new Extended IS3 dataset for simultaneous audio-visual grounding, the proposed method achieves 43.4 mAP / 34.6 mIoU for sound segmentation and 58.6 mAP / 47.0 mIoU for speech segmentation, outperforming DenseAV by +12.7 and +5.2 mAP respectively. On standard individual grounding (ADE20K), the method reaches 36.7 mAP for sound and 42.1 mAP for speech, surpassing DenseAV on sound (+6.4 mAP) while matching it on speech. Under mixed-audio conditions (off-screen interference), the method retains 22.3 mAP for sound and 39.3 mAP for speech, showing substantially smaller performance drops than DenseAV (19.6 and 30.5 mAP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DenseAV (the closest prior work), the proposed method wins decisively on the primary simultaneous grounding task (+12.7 mAP sound, +5.2 mAP speech) and on individual sound-prompted segmentation (+6.4 mAP total), while performing comparably on speech-prompted segmentation (42.1 vs. 43.0 mAP). On cross-modal retrieval, the method outperforms DenseAV on AudioSet (51.2 vs. 46.7 R@10 for Sound I→A) and matches it on PlacesAudio (~93–94 R@10). Under mixed-audio stress tests, the advantage widens dramatically: 44.3 vs. 14.4 R@10 for Sound I→A on AudioSet and 79.0 vs. 49.6 for Places Sound I→A, confirming superior disentanglement. Against CAV-MAE, the method wins on every reported metric by large margins (e.g., 43.4 vs. 31.0 mAP for simultaneous sound). ImageBind, a large-scale multimodal foundation model, outperforms the proposed method on AudioSet retrieval (66.8 vs. 45.5 R@10) but is far weaker on all grounding/segmentation tasks and on PlacesAudio retrieval, where the proposed method achieves 93–94 R@10 versus ImageBind's 0.9–1.1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends DenseAV (Hamilton et al., CVPR 2024), the first model to ground both speech and non-speech sounds in visual scenes, by addressing its two key limitations: handling audio types only sequentially rather than from mixed inputs, and insufficient disentanglement between the multi-head audio encoder. The 'mix-and-separate' framework draws on the broader audio-visual sound source localization lineage (Senocak et al., Mo et al.) and visually grounded speech work (Harwath et al.), but departs from prior mix-and-separate audio separation methods by performing separation at the feature/embedding level via contrastive learning rather than generating separated waveforms. Architecturally, it combines DINO (vision) and HuBERT (audio) backbones with a multi-head aligner, using LoRA for efficient vision fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Real-world audio is inherently a mixture of speech, music, and environmental sounds, yet prior audio-visual grounding models could only process one type at a time; this paper closes that gap with a principled joint correspondence-and-disentanglement objective and a new evaluation dataset (Extended IS3). Limitations include reliance on synthetic speech (gTTS) for the extended dataset, evaluation on relatively small test sets, and restriction to exactly two audio types. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more than two simultaneous audio types, whether it holds on genuinely recorded mixed audio (not just additive mixtures), and whether the disentanglement mechanism scales to finer-grained sound categories.
