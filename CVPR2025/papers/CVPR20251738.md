## Problem

Existing vision-language-action (VLA) models are trained separately for 2D digital (UI navigation) and 3D physical (robot manipulation) environments, and simply combining heterogeneous datasets fails to bridge the gap between verbal understanding and spatial action-taking. The paper asks how to build a single foundation model that unifies multimodal understanding with precise action grounding and planning across both digital and physical worlds, without sacrificing general comprehension abilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251738/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a sequence of image or video frames (with Set-of-Mark numerical labels overlaid on actionable regions) plus a free-text task description as input. For UI tasks it outputs an action type (e.g., 'click', 'type') paired with a mark number; for robotics it outputs 6-DoF end-effector displacements plus gripper openness encoded as the last 256 discrete LLM tokens; for planning it additionally predicts future mark positions (traces) over subsequent frames. All 2D coordinates are normalized by image dimensions and quantized into 256 bins, and the entire output is generated as natural language tokens.

## Method

Magma uses Llama-3-8B as the language backbone and ConvNeXt-XXlarge as the vision encoder, trained end-to-end on 39M diverse samples. The key technical contribution is two surrogate pretraining objectives: Set-of-Mark (SoM) overlays numbered marks on actionable regions so the model predicts a discrete mark index rather than raw pixel coordinates, and Trace-of-Mark (ToM) extends this temporally by using CoTracker to extract point trajectories and asking the model to predict future mark positions, thereby learning action planning from unlabeled instructional videos. Homography transformation removes global camera motion from ego-centric videos before trace extraction, and K-Means clustering separates foreground (action-relevant) from background traces. This unified mark-based interface allows heterogeneous data—UI screenshots, robot trajectories, and human videos—to be jointly pretrained without domain-specific action spaces, with all annotations converted to language tokens for standard autoregressive training.

## Results

On zero-shot UI action grounding, Magma-8B achieves 96.3% element grounding accuracy on VisualWebBench (VWB-Ele-G), surpassing GPT-4V (67.5%) and SeeClick (9.9%), and 71.8% action grounding (VWB-Act-G), competitive with GPT-4V (75.7%). On robot manipulation in SimplerEnv, Magma reaches 52.3% success rate on Google Robot and 35.4% on Bridge, nearly doubling OpenVLA (31.7% and 14.5% respectively). On multimodal understanding, Magma scores 80.0 on VQAv2, 66.5 on TextVQA, and 87.4 on POPE, matching or exceeding LLaVA-Next and Qwen-VL while using far less pretraining data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Magma decisively outperforms all domain-specific agentic models on UI grounding: it achieves 96.3% on VWB-Ele-G versus 67.5% for GPT-4V, 19.4% for Fuyu, and 9.9% for SeeClick, and 71.8% on VWB-Act-G versus 75.7% for GPT-4V (a narrow loss) and 15.5% for Fuyu. On robot manipulation, Magma nearly doubles OpenVLA's success rate (52.3% vs. 31.7% on Google Robot; 35.4% vs. 14.5% on Bridge) and substantially exceeds RT-1-X (34.2%/1.1%) and Octo (6.0%/15.9%). On ScreenSpot, Magma (60.4/58.5 mobile, 75.3/52.9 desktop, 69.1/52.0 web) surpasses SeeClick (78.0/52.0, 72.2/30.0, 55.7/32.5) on desktop and web icon grounding but trails on mobile text grounding. For generic multimodal understanding, Magma (80.0 VQAv2, 66.5 TextVQA, 87.4 POPE) is competitive with LLaVA-Next (81.8, 64.9, 86.5) and Qwen-VL (78.8, 63.8) while using only ~29M pretraining images versus ~1.5B for CogVLM-class models, though it trails GPT-4V (77.2, 78.0) on TextVQA. The ablation (Table 3) confirms that SoM+ToM pretraining is the critical factor: without it, Magma drops from 96.3 to 90.1 on VWB-Ele-G and from 52.3 to 37.5 on SE-Google, and simply combining UI and robotics data without SoM/ToM actually degrades both tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

Magma repurposes Set-of-Mark (SoM) prompting [88], originally designed as an inference-time trick to improve GPT-4V's visual grounding, into a native training objective that the model learns to interpret. For robot actions, it follows OpenVLA's [36] discrete-token representation but extends the data far beyond robotics trajectories to include UI screenshots and human instructional videos. The key departure from prior VLA work (RT-2, OpenVLA, SeeClick) is the introduction of Trace-of-Mark (ToM), which extends SoM along the temporal axis to enable action planning from video, and the demonstration that a single unified SoM/ToM interface is necessary to jointly train across digital and physical domains—something no prior model attempted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Magma provides the first single-parameter model that handles both UI navigation and robot manipulation at SOTA levels, suggesting that the digital-physical divide in VLA research may be an artifact of training methodology rather than a fundamental barrier. The SoM/ToM labeling pipeline offers a scalable, environment-agnostic way to convert unlabeled video into action supervision, which could significantly reduce the data bottleneck for embodied AI. Limitations include the absence of a post-training/instruction-tuning stage (hurting instruction following), the modest 8B scale, and evaluation primarily on simulation or zero-shot settings rather than long-horizon real-world deployment. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-step planning, novel embodiments, and whether the gains persist at larger model scales.
