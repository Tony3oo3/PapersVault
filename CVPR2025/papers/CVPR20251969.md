## Problem

Learning continuous unsigned distance functions (UDFs) through 3D Gaussian Splatting is difficult because 3DGS uses a discrete, explicit scene representation (individual Gaussians) that is fundamentally incompatible with the continuous, implicit nature of UDFs. Additionally, the gradient field of a UDF is highly complex near the zero level set (the surface itself), where the gradient vanishes, making standard pull-based supervision unstable. This gap prevents 3DGS from recovering complete, smooth, and accurate open surfaces with sharp boundaries.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images as the sole input (e.g., 72 views at 1024×1024 for DF3D, 49–64 views at 1600×1200 for DTU) without requiring masks, depth, or normal supervision. It produces a learned UDF parameterized by an 8-layer MLP (256 hidden units, ReLU activations, absolute-value output), from which open surface meshes are extracted via the MeshUDF algorithm, alongside the optimized 2D Gaussian scene representation. The UDF provides a continuous distance-to-surface query at any 3D point, enabling point cloud deformation and surface extraction for arbitrary topologies.

## Method

The core idea is to overfit thin, flat 2D Gaussian planes directly on surfaces and then leverage them as geometric anchors to supervise a jointly learned UDF. Two complementary supervision signals are introduced: (1) a far-field loss (L_far) that pulls randomly sampled queries onto the UDF's zero level set via its gradient and enforces Chamfer distance alignment with Gaussian centers, and (2) a near-field self-supervision loss (L_near) that samples points on each Gaussian's flat plane, offsets them along the Gaussian normal by a random distance t, and trains the UDF to predict |t| at those points. To stabilize optimization near the surface where UDF gradients are unreliable, an explicit projection constraint (L_proj) moves Gaussian centers onto the zero level set with stopped gradients, while depth-distortion and normal-alignment losses ensure the 2D Gaussians remain faithfully aligned with the surface. The entire pipeline uses differentiable 2D Gaussian splatting (no ray tracing), training for 30k iterations with L_far active from iteration 9k–12k before introducing L_near and L_proj.

## Results

On the DF3D open-surface benchmark, GaussianUDF achieves a mean Chamfer Distance of 1.60 (×10⁻³), outperforming the best UDF-based baseline VRPrior (1.71) by 6.4% and the best SDF-based baseline GOF (2.49) by 35.7%. On the DTU closed-surface benchmark, it achieves an average CD of 0.68, beating VRPrior (0.71), GSPull (0.75), GOF (0.74), and 2DGS (0.80). Training takes 1.6 hours on a single NVIDIA 3090 GPU, roughly 5× faster than NeRF-based UDF methods (7.8–9.2 h) while maintaining superior accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SDF-based methods (NeuS, 2DGS, GOF), GaussianUDF achieves substantially lower CD on DF3D (1.60 vs. 4.36, 3.81, 2.49) because SDF representations inherently close open surfaces, producing double-layered artifacts. Among UDF-based NeRF methods, GaussianUDF outperforms VRPrior (1.60 vs. 1.71), 2S-UDF (1.60 vs. 1.98), and NeuralUDF (1.60 vs. 2.15) on DF3D, with the largest gap over NeuralUDF (25.6% improvement). On DTU, it beats all baselines including the concurrent GSPull (0.68 vs. 0.75), demonstrating that its UDF approach generalizes to closed surfaces without sacrificing accuracy. In terms of efficiency, GaussianUDF trains in 1.6 h—about 5× faster than VRPrior (9.2 h) and 2S-UDF (7.8 h)—while 2DGS and GOF are faster (6 min, 47 min) but produce significantly worse geometry on open surfaces. The method also qualitatively captures finer details (e.g., clothing folds) that VRPrior and 2S-UDF over-smooth, and avoids the double-layer artifacts of GOF and 2DGS on real scans.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active research threads: UDF-based open surface reconstruction (NeuralUDF, 2S-UDF, VRPrior) and 3D Gaussian Splatting for geometry (2DGS, GOF, GSPull). It departs from NeRF-based UDF methods by replacing slow volumetric ray tracing with fast differentiable 2D Gaussian splatting, and departs from SDF-based 3DGS methods (GOF, GSPull) by learning unsigned distances that can represent non-watertight surfaces. The key conceptual bridge is using thin 2D Gaussian planes as a discrete proxy for the continuous zero level set, combined with Neural-Pull-style gradient projection for far-field supervision and a novel self-supervision along Gaussian normals for near-field supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a ~5× speedup over the best NeRF-based UDF methods (1.6h vs 7.8–9.2h) while achieving the best Chamfer Distance on DF3D, making high-quality open surface reconstruction accessible in near-real-time settings. However, the accuracy gain over VRPrior is modest (1.60 vs 1.71 CD), and the authors acknowledge reduced performance on textureless structures and dependence on UDF mesh extraction quality. Open questions include whether incorporating additional priors (depth, normals, masks) or newer differentiable UDF extraction methods (Neural Dual Contouring) can close the remaining gap, and whether the self-supervision strategy generalizes to dynamic or unbounded scenes.
