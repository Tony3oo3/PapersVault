## Problem

Existing instruction-guided 4D dynamic scene editing methods, such as Instruct 4D-to-4D, require iteratively editing thousands of 2D images across all timesteps and retraining the full scene, resulting in hours of processing that do not scale with the temporal dimension. This makes them impractical for real-world applications where dynamic scenes may contain hundreds of timesteps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252225/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-camera video dataset (15–20 cameras, trimmed to 50-frame segments at 30 fps) and a single text editing instruction as input. It produces an edited 4D dynamic scene represented as a pair of canonical 3D Gaussians (100k–200k primitives) and a Hexplane-based deformation field, which can be rendered into a dynamic scene video at real-time speeds. Notably, only a subset of multiview images from the first timestep is edited via IP2P; no additional 2D images are generated or modified at later timesteps.

## Method

Instruct-4DGS exploits the static-dynamic separability of 4D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS + Hexplane deformation field) to edit only the static canonical 3D Gaussians, which are the minimal sufficient component for appearance editing while preserving motion. Stage 1 edits a subset of first-timestep multiview images with InstructPix2Pix and optimizes the static Gaussians via L1 RGB loss in 800–1000 iterations. Stage 2 addresses the resulting temporal misalignment (shifted Gaussian positions and unedited spherical-harmonics colors exposed at later timesteps) through a score distillation refinement using Coherent-IP2P—IP2P with its self-attention replaced by a cross-attention module inspired by MVDream and Tune-a-Video—to transfer editing guidance into 4D space without additional image editing. This two-stage pipeline completes in ~40 minutes on a single A40 GPU, more than 2× faster than the baseline which requires two GPUs and ~2 hours.

## Results

On DyNeRF, Instruct-4DGS achieves an average LPIPS-VGG of 0.303 versus 0.491 for Instruct 4D-to-4D, indicating substantially better perceptual quality across all three editing instructions. It also attains higher average CLIP similarity (0.249 vs. 0.230), showing stronger instruction-following fidelity, and higher average SSIM (0.783 vs. 0.736). In terms of efficiency, Instruct-4DGS completes editing in 40 minutes on a single GPU, compared to 2 hours on two GPUs for the baseline — a 3× speedup with half the hardware.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is Instruct 4D-to-4D [Mou et al., CVPR 2024], the only prior instruction-guided 4D editing method. Instruct-4DGS wins decisively on perceptual quality (LPIPS-VGG: 0.303 vs. 0.491 average) and instruction-following (CLIP similarity: 0.249 vs. 0.230), and also on average SSIM (0.783 vs. 0.736). It trails on average PSNR (19.25 vs. 20.40), which the authors attribute to not directly optimizing pixel-level accuracy against all edited images during the refinement stage; however, it wins PSNR on 2 of 3 individual instructions (Statue: 21.41 vs. 18.73; Wood Sculpture: 17.64 vs. 18.23, a narrow loss). Qualitatively, the baseline exhibits noticeable temporal flickering across timesteps, while Instruct-4DGS maintains smooth motion. Most strikingly, Instruct-4DGS is 3× faster (40 min vs. 2 h) and requires only 1 GPU instead of 2, a gap the authors note will widen further as the number of timesteps increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the instruction-guided scene editing lineage (IP2P → Instruct-NeRF2NeRF → Instruct 4D-to-4D) by exploiting the static-dynamic separability inherent in 4D Gaussian Splatting. Rather than following Instruct 4D-to-4D's iterative dataset-update paradigm (editing all T×M images across timesteps), it edits only the static canonical 3D Gaussians using a single timestep's multiview images, then applies a score distillation refinement stage (borrowed from DreamFusion) to resolve temporal misalignment. The Coherent-IP2P modification (replacing self-attention with cross-attention, inspired by MVDream and Tune-a-Video) is repurposed here to stabilize the SDS-based refinement, and the Hexplane-based deformation field from 4DGS provides the compact dynamic component that remains untouched during editing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a 2–3× reduction in editing time (40 min vs. 2 hours) with half the GPU resources, making instruction-guided 4D editing more accessible and scalable to longer temporal sequences. The ablation revealing that refining the deformation field actively degrades temporal coherence is a useful negative result that clarifies the boundary of what score distillation can productively optimize in 4D editing. Limitations remain: the method inherits IP2P's editing quality ceiling, cannot directly modify motion, requires segmentation for partial edits, and residual artifacts persist in complex dynamic scenes. Open questions include whether the static-only editing strategy generalizes to scenes with significant topology changes or whether the score distillation refinement can be extended to jointly optimize both static and dynamic components in a more principled way.
