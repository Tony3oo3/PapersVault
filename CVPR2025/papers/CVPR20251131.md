## Problem

3DGS segmentation produces blurry object boundaries because Gaussian primitives inherently span across object edges, and the original 3DGS training optimizes only visual quality without semantic guidance. Existing mask-based methods either remove ambiguous boundary Gaussians (degrading visual quality) or rely on high-dimensional feature representations that introduce their own ambiguity, leaving no approach that simultaneously achieves clear boundaries and high-fidelity rendering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251131/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-reconstructed 3DGS scene (Gaussian primitives with position, scale, rotation, opacity, and color), a text prompt describing the target object, and the original input images with camera poses. Masks are generated via a two-stage SAM2 pipeline: Grounding-DINO extracts box prompts from a reference frame, which propagate across the sequence; a second fine-grained stage repairs discontinuities in long sequences. The output is a refined 3DGS scene with split boundary Gaussians and restored texture, from which segmented objects are extracted by thresholding the learned continuous mask label m_i ∈ (0,1) per Gaussian.

## Method

COB-GS adds a continuous mask label to each Gaussian and performs alternating optimization of mask labels (minimizing L_mask) and scene texture (minimizing L_rgb), freezing the other modality during each phase. The key mechanism is boundary-adaptive Gaussian splitting: during mask optimization, a per-Gaussian consistency score (mask_sig) computed from the ratio of positive to negative supervision signals identifies semantically ambiguous boundary Gaussians, which are then split into smaller primitives aligned with object edges. After joint optimization converges, a final robustness pass identifies and removes tiny residual ambiguous Gaussians caused by inconsistent SAM2 masks across views. For multi-object scenes, the method decomposes segmentation into sequential single-object passes, each producing an updated 3DGS with clear boundaries before proceeding to the next object.

## Results

On the NVOS dataset, COB-GS achieves 92.1% mIoU and 98.6% mAcc for 3D segmentation, outperforming the closest 3DGS-based baseline FlashSplat (91.8% mIoU, 98.6% mAcc) and all NeRF-based methods. On visual quality (CLIP-IQA), COB-GS scores 0.682 on Clear/Unclear Boundary, 0.731 on Smooth/Noisy Boundary, and 0.859 on Complete/Mutilated Object, leading all compared methods. The ablation (Table 4) shows that boundary-adaptive Gaussian splitting alone lifts mIoU from 91.2% to 91.9%, and adding robustness against erroneous masks further improves to 92.1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COB-GS outperforms all seven baselines on mIoU, with the largest margin over NeRF-based methods (e.g., +22.0 over NVOS, +8.3 over ISRF) and a consistent 1.2–1.7 point advantage over 3DGS-based mask methods (SAGD, SA3D-GS, SAGA). Against the closest competitor FlashSplat, COB-GS gains +0.3 mIoU while matching its 98.6% mAcc, and further surpasses it on all three CLIP-IQA visual-quality metrics (0.682 vs 0.626 on boundary clarity, 0.731 vs 0.644 on smoothness, 0.859 vs 0.829 on object completeness). The paper notes that FlashSplat reduces edge blur by increasing background bias but indiscriminately removes real object regions (e.g., a truck's rearview mirror), whereas COB-GS preserves structural integrity. SAGD produces tiny Gaussian artifacts along segmented edges due to lack of robustness to erroneous masks, while SA3D-GS shows noticeable Gaussian blur at boundaries and degraded background quality. Overall, COB-GS achieves the best segmentation accuracy and visual quality simultaneously, validating the joint optimization of semantics and texture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mask-based 3DGS segmentation line (SA3D, SAGD, FlashSplat) by reframing segmentation as a joint scene-refinement problem rather than a post-hoc labeling step. Prior methods learn per-Gaussian mask labels and then filter or delete ambiguous boundary Gaussians, sacrificing visual quality; COB-GS instead alternates mask-label optimization (with boundary-adaptive Gaussian splitting driven by mask gradient statistics) and texture optimization, so that semantic and visual objectives co-shape the Gaussian geometry. For mask generation, it builds on SAM2's video-segmentation capability and Grounding-DINO's text-prompted detection to produce temporally consistent supervision, addressing a practical gap in long-sequence mask extraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The joint semantics-texture optimization paradigm offers a principled alternative to the common practice of discarding boundary Gaussians, which could influence how future 3DGS editing and segmentation pipelines are structured. However, the quantitative gains over the strongest baseline (FlashSplat) are modest (0.3% mIoU), and the method still inherits sensitivity to 2D mask quality from SAM2. Open questions include whether the sequential single-object decomposition scales efficiently to scenes with many objects, how the approach handles floating artifacts that become more visible after segmentation, and whether the boundary-adaptive splitting criterion generalizes beyond the NVOS-style single-object evaluation to dense multi-class scenes.
