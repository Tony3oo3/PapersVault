## Problem

Foundation models like Stable Diffusion, despite excelling on photo-based vision tasks, fail to extract equally representative features from abstract freehand sketches due to their photographic pretraining and an inherent frequency-domain bias that suppresses low-frequency semantic components. No existing method provides a universal sketch feature representation that effectively leverages the complementary strengths of multiple foundation models across diverse sketch-based discriminative and dense prediction tasks.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a freehand sketch or RGB photo, which is first encoded by a frozen VAE into a latent representation (h/8 × w/8 × d), then forward-diffused to a fixed timestep (t=195) before feature extraction from the UNet's upsampling layers. A null text prompt is used throughout since paired captions are unavailable in most sketch-photo datasets. The output is a 60×60×d feature map that can be globally max-pooled for retrieval/recognition or used patch-wise for segmentation and correspondence, making the same extractor applicable across all four evaluated tasks.

## Method

The core mechanism injects frozen CLIP visual encoder features into multiple layers of a frozen SD UNet at every denoising timestep via lightweight learnable 1D convolutions that match feature dimensions, thereby reinforcing SD's high-frequency spatial features with CLIP's low-frequency semantic context. A dynamic aggregation network of three ResNet blocks then transforms features from the first three UNet upsampling layers into a common 60×60×d space, and learnable branch weights (α_n) adaptively combine them for each downstream task without manual layer selection. Only the 1D convolutions, aggregation network, and branch weights are trained on sketch-photo pairs using task-specific losses (triplet, cross-entropy, BCE, or contrastive+endpoint), while both SD and CLIP remain entirely frozen. The key insight is that SD's denoising architecture inherently promotes HF components (edges, fine details) while suppressing LF components (overall semantic structure), and CLIP's contrastive pretraining provides the complementary LF semantic signal needed for sketch understanding.

## Results

On category-level zero-shot sketch-based image retrieval (ZS-SBIR), the proposed method achieves mAP@200 of 0.761 on Sketchy, 0.695 on TU-Berlin, and 0.242 on Quick, Draw!, surpassing the strongest baseline SD-PL by +3.35% on Sketchy. For sketch recognition, it reaches 87.02% Acc.@1 on Quick, Draw! (+1.06% over SketchXAI) and 84.96% on TU-Berlin. On dense prediction tasks, the method achieves 70.31% PCK@5 on sketch-photo correspondence (+21.22% relative over Self-Supervised [55]) and 60.12% mIoU on sketch-based segmentation (+29.42% relative over Sketch-a-Segmenter [35]).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 16 compared baselines across every task and dataset. In ZS-SBIR, it surpasses the closest competitor SD-PL [46] by +3.35% mAP@200 on Sketchy (0.761 vs. 0.746) and +1.5% on TU-Berlin (0.695 vs. 0.680), while older methods like SAKE [54], GRL [20], and CAAE [102] trail by 20–60 mAP points. For cross-category fine-grained SBIR, it achieves 33.01% Acc.@1, beating SD-PL [46] (31.94%) and Gen-VAE [63] (22.60%). In sketch recognition, it exceeds SketchXAI [66] (86.10%) by +0.92% on Quick, Draw! and SketchMate [96] (79.44%) by +7.58%. The largest margins appear in dense prediction: sketch-photo correspondence PCK@5 of 70.31% beats Self-Supervised [55] (58.00%) by 12.31 points and WarpC [85] (56.78%) by 13.53 points; segmentation mIoU of 60.12% surpasses Sketch-a-Segmenter [35] (46.45%) by 13.67 points and ZS-Seg [95] (44.73%) by 15.39 points. Notably, the B-Finetuning baseline (full end-to-end fine-tuning of SD+CLIP) collapses to near-random performance on most tasks, confirming that the lightweight injection-and-aggregation strategy is essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hybrid-model paradigm introduced by [107] (which combined SD and DINO for zero-shot correspondence) to the sketch domain, but with a fundamentally different integration mechanism: rather than concatenating or fusing features at the output level, it injects CLIP visual features directly into SD's UNet denoising process at every timestep via lightweight 1D convolutions. The frequency-domain analysis (showing SD's systematic HF bias and CLIP's complementary LF emphasis) provides a principled diagnostic that prior sketch-retrieval works [46, 76] lacked, motivating *why* this particular combination is effective. The adaptive aggregation network over multi-scale UNet upsampling layers departs from prior approaches that manually select a single layer or use fixed-weight combinations [46, 84].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that foundation models can serve as universal sketch feature extractors without retraining, which is practically significant given the catastrophic forgetting observed when finetuning SD+CLIP on limited sketch data (B-Finetuning drops mAP@200 from 0.588 to 0.120 on Sketchy). The frequency-domain diagnostic framework could generalize to other domain-gap problems beyond sketches (e.g., medical imaging, satellite imagery). However, the segmentation results rely on a small manually-annotated dataset (5K tuples, 10 classes), and the method's applicability to 3D sketch tasks or video remains unexplored. An open question is whether the HF/LF complementarity principle can be formalized to guide the selection of *which* second model to pair with a diffusion backbone for arbitrary sparse-input domains.
