## Problem

Scaling physics-based human-object interaction (HOI) imitation to diverse, dynamic objects remains unsolved due to three compounding challenges: MoCap data contains contact artifacts and missing hand detail, diverse human shapes require retargeting that introduces further errors, and no existing framework trains a single policy for whole-body loco-manipulation across many object types. Prior methods either handle only specific object-action pairs or rely on small, highly curated datasets like GRAB, limiting generalization to realistic, large-scale interaction data.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a state vector comprising human proprioception (52 joint rotations, positions, angular velocities, and velocities), object pose and velocity, per-joint distance vectors to object surfaces, and binary contact markers, all normalized to the human's current root frame. Actions are 51×3 PD torque targets applied at 30 Hz in Isaac Gym. The output is a full-body interaction trajectory (human + object poses) that can be directly fed to downstream kinematic generators: given a text prompt via HOI-Diff or 10 past frames via InterDiff, the student policy produces physically grounded future interaction sequences, enabling zero-shot generalization to unseen objects and 10× weight perturbations.

## Method

InterMimic employs a two-stage teacher-student distillation framework. In stage one, subject-specific teacher policies (MLPs) are trained via PPO on small data subsets, using embodiment-aware rewards (distance-weighted position vs. rotation tracking) to unify retargeting into imitation, three-level contact-guided rewards (promotion/neutral/penalty) to tolerate MoCap contact inaccuracies, Physical State Initialization (PSI) that seeds rollouts from a buffer of high-reward simulation states rather than raw MoCap poses, and Interaction Early Termination (IET) with object-specific deviation checks. In stage two, a Transformer student policy (3-layer encoder, 4 heads, hidden 256) with a broader keyframe window {1,2,4,16} is trained via a staged schedule that begins with DAgger policy distillation from the composite teacher and gradually transitions to PPO fine-tuning, while simultaneously using teacher rollouts as refined reference motions (reference distillation) to replace noisy MoCap targets in the reward function. This space-time trade-off—parallel teacher training on small subsets followed by distillation into one scalable student—enables the framework to process ~10 hours of multi-subject data while correcting artifacts and surpassing pure demonstration memorization.

## Results

On the OMOMO dataset (15 objects, ~10 hours of MoCap), the full InterMimic student policy (PPO + reference distillation + policy distillation, MLP) achieves 90.7% success rate and 168.0 s mean duration on training data, and 95.5% success with 173.9 s on the held-out test subject, with human tracking error of 5.4 cm and object tracking error of 11.9 cm. The Transformer variant reaches 98.1% success on the test set. On the teacher-policy comparison (Table 1, BEHAVE yogamat), InterMimic sustains 42.6 s of tracking versus 12.2 s for SkillMimic, with lower errors (Eh 6.4 vs 7.2 cm; Eo 9.2 vs 13.4 cm). Zero-shot integration with kinematic generators (HOI-Diff, InterDiff) yields 12.5% and 75.0% success respectively, demonstrating generalization to generated motions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SkillMimic [89] on the BEHAVE yogamat task (Table 1), InterMimic's teacher policy sustains 42.6 s of interaction tracking versus SkillMimic's 12.2 s (3.5× longer), while also reducing human tracking error from 7.2 to 6.4 cm and object tracking error from 13.4 to 9.2 cm. Against PhysHOI [88], the comparison is primarily qualitative (Figure 4): InterMimic robustly corrects incorrect hand positioning and floating contacts in the reference that cause PhysHOI to fail, and additionally recovers plausible object rotations for symmetric objects that MoCap depicts as sliding. Internally, the paper shows that PPO from scratch (23.9% success) and DAgger-only distillation (54.5%) both underperform the full pipeline (90.7%), confirming that the combination of reference distillation, policy distillation, and RL fine-tuning is necessary. The Transformer architecture provides a modest but consistent gain over MLP on the test set (98.1% vs 95.5% success) and on out-of-distribution kinematic generations, attributed to its sequential modeling capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

InterMimic extends the DeepMimic lineage of RL-based motion imitation (PPO, reference state initialization, early termination) from single-skill or single-object tracking to universal whole-body loco-manipulation across diverse dynamic objects. It departs from prior physics-based HOI methods (PhysHOI, SkillMimic) that train non-scalable, per-object or per-action policies by introducing a teacher-student distillation framework where subject-specific teachers correct MoCap artifacts and a Transformer student integrates all skills via a DAgger-to-PPO schedule. The space-time trade-off—parallel teachers on small subsets distilled into one student—repurposes the LLM alignment paradigm (demonstration pretraining + RL fine-tuning) for physics-based control, and the contact-guided reward design with Physical State Initialization addresses MoCap imperfections that prior imitation methods could not handle at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to demonstrate a single policy performing diverse, highly dynamic whole-body interactions with multiple object types from hours of imperfect MoCap data, with zero-shot generalization to unseen objects and seamless integration with kinematic generators (InterDiff, HOI-Diff) for prediction and text-to-interaction tasks. The Unitree G1 robot demo signals a path toward real-world humanoid loco-manipulation, though the paper acknowledges limitations: no soft-body support, single-human focus, and reliance on MoCap data that still requires discarding severely corrupted clips. Open questions include whether the teacher-student approach scales to multi-human interactions, whether the Transformer student's advantage over MLPs persists at even larger data scales, and how the method handles real-world sensor noise and latency beyond simulation.
