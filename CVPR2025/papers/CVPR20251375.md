## Problem

Existing referring expression counting (REC) methods rely on one-to-one text-region matching in a detection-to-count pipeline, causing them to over-count when class information dominates over fine-grained attributes and under-count under occlusion or scale variation. The paper identifies that the root cause is an under-exploration of contextual attribute density (CAD) — the information intensity of a specific fine-grained attribute across visual regions — which prior open-world perception models largely overlook.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251375/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a natural-language referring expression (e.g., 'person riding motorcycle') and produces bounding box localizations plus a scalar object count. Visual features are extracted at four scales from a frozen Swin backbone, and the text is encoded by a frozen text encoder; the two are aligned via a linear projector before density estimation. At inference, the predicted count can be derived either by thresholding query scores (0.25 for CLS, 0.35 for text tokens) or by summing the predicted density map and selecting the top-N scoring queries accordingly.

## Method

CAD-GD extends GroundingDINO with three CAD-specific components. First, a U-shape CAD Estimator (CADE) fuses multi-scale visual features with text-visual similarity maps to produce multi-scale CAD feature maps, supervised by an L2 density loss against Gaussian-kernel ground-truth maps. Second, a CAD Attention Module injects these density features into the visual features via a spatial attention branch (SENet-style pooling on CAD channels) followed by a scale-varying channel attention branch, sharpening foreground regions and improving attribute discrimination. Third, a CAD Dynamic Query Module replaces GroundingDINO's fixed query embeddings with a two-stage initialization: text features are first projected through a learned matrix and GELU to form word-combination queries, then refined by cross-attending with the CAD features at the selected query positions, making initial queries input-dependent and attribute-discriminative. The full model is trained with frozen backbones, AdamW (lr 1e-5, 10× decay at epoch 10), and a combined localization-plus-density loss, achieving 30–37% relative MAE reduction over the GroundingREC baseline on REC-8K.

## Results

On the REC-8K benchmark with a Swin-B backbone, CAD-GD† achieves a validation MAE of 4.23 and test MAE of 4.34, representing 37.8% and 33.2% relative error reductions over the GroundingREC baseline (5.66/5.42). The method also improves localization F1 from 0.70 to 0.74 on the test set. On the zero-shot FSC-147 benchmark, CAD-GD attains a validation MAE of 9.30, outperforming all text-only baselines, and on CARPK it reaches a test MAE of 3.29, surpassing even visual-exemplar methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAD-GD outperforms all 11 compared baselines on the primary REC-8K benchmark: it reduces validation MAE from 6.80 (GroundingREC, Swin-T) to 4.58 (CAD-GD†) and from 5.66 (GroundingREC*, Swin-B) to 4.23, while also improving test-set F1 from 0.69 to 0.71 (Swin-T) and 0.70 to 0.74 (Swin-B). On FSC-147 (zero-shot, text-only), CAD-GD (MAE 9.30) beats all text-guided baselines including GroundingREC (10.06), CountGD (12.14), CounTX (17.10), and CLIP-count (18.79), though it trails the best visual-exemplar methods CountGD (7.10) and DAVE (8.91). On CARPK, CAD-GD (MAE 3.29) surpasses every competitor, including visual-exemplar CACViT (4.91) and text-based CountGD (3.83), demonstrating cross-dataset generalization. The largest absolute gains appear over the direct REC baseline GroundingREC (≈1.4–2.0 MAE reduction) and over the open-set detector GroundingDINO (≈4.5 MAE reduction), confirming that the CAD modeling addresses the specific failure modes of detection-to-count pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GroundingDINO's open-world detection architecture and the GroundingREC baseline (Dai et al., CVPR 2024), which first formalized the referring expression counting task. Its core conceptual departure is transplanting the density-map paradigm—long established in crowd counting and class-agnostic counting—into the open-world, text-guided setting, reframing it as 'contextual attribute density' that encodes the spatial intensity of a specific fine-grained attribute rather than a generic class. The U-shape estimator, spatial/channel attention injection, and dynamic query initialization are all designed to bridge this density signal into the DETR-style localization decoder inherited from GroundingDINO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As one of the first substantial improvements over the just-introduced REC baseline, the paper validates that density modeling—previously considered a tool for dense, class-specific counting—can meaningfully address the attribute-alignment failures (over- and under-counting) that plague open-world fine-grained counting. The t-SNE visualizations showing CAD initialization separating near-identical expressions (e.g., 'bluish pen' vs. 'greenish pen') provide intuitive evidence for the mechanism. Limitations include the reliance on fixed-kernel Gaussian density maps rather than learned supervision, tight coupling to GroundingDINO's architecture, and the observation that the density-guided inference strategy slightly degrades localization. Open questions include whether CAD generalizes to more complex compositional expressions, whether learned density supervision would further improve results, and whether the concept transfers to other open-world perception tasks beyond counting.
