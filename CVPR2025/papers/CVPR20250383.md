## Problem

Existing 3D hand pose estimation methods assume a fixed number of hands in the image and cannot generalize to in-the-wild multi-hand scenes, while current hand detectors are either too slow for real-time use or fail on challenging poses and occlusions. Additionally, directly regressing MANO parameters from a single image in one pass produces misaligned and implausible hand poses, and prior alignment strategies (e.g., intermediate heatmaps) are sub-optimal.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a full-resolution RGB image as input to the detection stage, which outputs hand bounding boxes with left/right handedness labels at over 130 FPS. Each detected hand crop is then fed to the reconstruction module, which predicts MANO pose (θ ∈ R⁴⁸), shape (β ∈ R¹⁰), and weak-perspective camera parameters (translation and scale), yielding a full 3D hand mesh. The system operates frame-by-frame on monocular video without any explicit temporal module, yet achieves strong temporal coherence.

## Method

WiLoR is a two-stage full-stack pipeline. The detection stage is a single-stage anchor-free FCN with a DarkNet backbone, PANet neck, and three multi-resolution heads trained on the newly curated 2M+ image WHIM dataset using a composite loss (BCE + distributional focal loss + CIoU + keypoint L2). The reconstruction stage uses a pre-trained ViTPose encoder with three learnable tokens (pose, shape, camera) to first predict a rough MANO estimate; a novel multi-scale refinement module then projects the rough hand mesh onto a set of deconvolution-upsampled feature maps, samples image-aligned vertex features via bilinear interpolation, and regresses pose/shape residuals through MLPs. Training combines 3D vertex, 2D joint, MANO parameter, and adversarial losses, and the coarse-to-fine design with mesh-aligned multi-scale features is the key architectural distinction from prior single-pass ViT regressors like HaMeR.

## Results

On FreiHAND, WiLoR achieves 5.5 mm PA-MPJPE and 5.1 mm PA-MPVPE, outperforming the previous best (HaMeR at 6.0/5.7 mm) by 0.5 mm on both metrics, with F@5 of 0.825 and F@15 of 0.993. On HO3Dv2, it reaches 7.5 mm PA-MPJPE and 7.7 mm PA-MPVPE (vs. HaMeR's 7.7/7.9 mm) with AUCJ of 0.851. For hand detection, the Proposed-M model runs at 138 FPS with 96.06 AP on WHIM and 82.64 AP on OxfordHands, while being 45× faster and 32× smaller than ContactHands. In dynamic reconstruction, WiLoR reduces jitter to 5.92 (vs. 20.43 for HaMeR) and MPFVE to 4.43 (vs. 10.60) without any temporal module.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WiLoR outperforms all 20 baselines across every reported metric on both FreiHAND and HO3D. In 3D hand pose estimation, it beats the closest competitor HaMeR [61] by 0.5 mm PA-MPJPE (5.5 vs. 6.0) and 0.6 mm PA-MPVPE (5.1 vs. 5.7) on FreiHAND, and by 0.2 mm on both metrics on HO3D (7.5 vs. 7.7 PA-MPJPE). Earlier methods like Mesh Graphormer [42], METRO [41], and MobRecon [10] trail by 0.2–1.0 mm. In hand detection, WiLoR (Proposed-M) surpasses ContactHands [53] by 2.64 AP on WHIM (96.06 vs. 93.42) while running 45× faster (138 vs. 3 FPS) and 32× smaller (25 vs. 819 Mb); it also outperforms ViTDet [39] by 11.3 AP on WHIM. For temporal coherence in dynamic reconstruction, WiLoR achieves 4.43 MPFVE and 5.92 jitter, roughly halving HaMeR's 10.60/20.43 and reducing MeshGraphormer's [42] 21.86/41.16 by over 4×, all without any temporal module.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the ViT-based hand pose estimation framework of HaMeR (Pavlakos et al., CVPR 2024), adopting its learnable pose/shape/camera token design and pre-trained ViTPose backbone, but departs from it in two key ways: it prepends a real-time anchor-free hand detector (DarkNet + PANet) to handle in-the-wild multi-hand scenes, and it introduces a multi-scale refinement module that projects the rough MANO mesh onto upsampled feature maps to extract image-aligned residuals. Unlike prior 3D hand pose methods that assume tightly cropped single-hand inputs, WiLoR positions itself as a full-stack pipeline where detection quality directly feeds reconstruction fidelity, drawing on the bottom-up detection philosophy established in 3D body and face reconstruction but applying it to hands for the first time at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck: current 3D hand pose estimators require pre-cropped hand regions, making them inapplicable to real-world multi-hand scenarios without a robust detector. The WHIM dataset (2M+ in-the-wild images with 2D/3D annotations) and the 45× faster detector fill this gap and are likely to become standard resources for the community. An open question is whether the striking temporal coherence gains (2–3× less jitter than HaMeR) generalize beyond HO3D and are truly attributable to detection stability rather than the refinement module's per-frame consistency. The method remains frame-based with no explicit temporal modeling, and its reliance on the MANO parametric model limits expressiveness for non-standard hand shapes.
