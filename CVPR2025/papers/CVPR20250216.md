## Problem

In federated learning, data heterogeneity across clients causes divergent local optimization directions, degrading global model performance. Existing approaches either use fixed aggregation weights (e.g., dataset-size-based in FedAvg) that cannot adapt to the dynamic training process, or rely on proxy datasets to learn optimal weights, raising privacy concerns. The paper addresses the gap of adaptively optimizing aggregation weights during training without requiring any additional data beyond what is already exchanged in standard FL communication.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the standard FL communication artifacts: each client's locally trained model parameters θ_k^t and the current global model parameters θ_g^t, from which the client vector τ_k^t = θ_k^t − θ_g^t is computed on the server. No proxy dataset, raw data, or gradient information is required. The output is a set of optimized aggregation weights λ^t (one per client, or per client per layer in FedAWA-L) that sum to 1, which are then applied to produce the updated global model θ_g^{t+1}. Experiments use 32×32 RGB images (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-10) and 64×64 RGB images (Tiny-ImageNet) with architectures ranging from CNN to ViT.

## Method

FedAWA draws on task arithmetic from model merging, replacing the task vector with a client vector defined as the parameter difference between the locally trained model and the global model (τ_k^t = θ_k^t − θ_g^t). Empirical analysis shows that client vectors capture local data variations more faithfully than raw model parameters, and that their weighted aggregation aligns closely with the ideal update direction of a model trained on the full global dataset. The aggregation weights are then optimized at each communication round by solving a constrained minimization: minimize the weighted L2 distance between each client vector and the merged global vector, plus a 1−cosine-similarity regularization term that penalizes deviation of the new global model from the previous one, subject to the simplex constraint ‖λ‖₁ = 1. A layer-wise variant (FedAWA-L) extends this to compute separate weights for each network layer, enabling finer-grained aggregation. The entire procedure is a plug-and-play server-side module that requires no additional data transmission and can be combined with existing client-side or server-side FL methods.

## Results

FedAWA achieves the best average top-1 accuracy of 49.48% across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and TinyImageNet under three heterogeneity settings (NIID α=0.1, NIID α=0.5, IID α=100), outperforming all baselines. The layer-wise variant FedAWA-L further improves the average to 49.98%, with the largest gains on CIFAR-100 (45.27% at α=100 vs. 42.84% for FedAWA). On the most challenging setting (CIFAR-10, NIID α=0.1), FedAWA reaches 63.55%, surpassing FedAvg (61.04%) and L-DAWA (62.87%). Aggregation execution time for FedAWA is 0.82 s per round, substantially lower than L-DAWA (2.52 s) and FedLAW (10.11 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedAWA outperforms all seven baselines in average accuracy (49.48% vs. 48.26% for FedLAW, 48.19% for FedDisco, 48.09% for L-DAWA, 47.88% for FedAvg, 47.34% for FedDyn, 46.52% for FedProx, and 41.76% for FedAdam). Against the closest competitor FedLAW, FedAWA wins on 6 of 9 dataset–heterogeneity combinations, with the largest margins on CIFAR-100 (e.g., +4.49% at α=0.1) and TinyImageNet (e.g., +3.94% at α=0.1), but trails FedLAW on CIFAR-10 at α=0.1 (63.55 vs. 64.76) and α=100 (80.10 vs. 81.30); notably, FedLAW requires a proxy dataset, raising privacy concerns that FedAWA avoids. Compared to the standard FedAvg, FedAWA gains +2.51% on CIFAR-10 (α=0.1), +0.81% on CIFAR-100 (α=0.5), and +3.63% on TinyImageNet (α=0.1). Against L-DAWA, which also adaptively adjusts weights via cosine similarity, FedAWA improves by +0.68% on CIFAR-10 (α=0.1) and +0.73% on CIFAR-100 (α=0.5), confirming that optimizing weights via the client vector is more effective than directly using model-parameter similarity. The layer-wise variant FedAWA-L further extends these gains, reaching 49.98% average and 45.27% on CIFAR-100 (α=100), at the cost of 15.21 s aggregation time versus 0.82 s for FedAWA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws a direct conceptual bridge between model merging (task arithmetic, Ties-Merging, AdaMerging) and federated learning, repurposing the 'task vector' as a 'client vector' (the difference between local and global model parameters) to guide aggregation weight optimization. It departs from prior server-side aggregation methods (FedDisco, L-DAWA, FedLAW) by eliminating the need for fixed weights, model-parameter similarity as a proxy, or a privacy-sensitive proxy dataset, instead formulating weight optimization as a constrained minimization over client-vector alignment. The authors explicitly claim to be the first to explore the model-merging-to-FL connection, positioning FedAWA as a plug-and-play server-side module orthogonal to client-side methods like FedProx and FedDyn.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedAWA offers a privacy-preserving, data-free mechanism for adaptive aggregation that addresses a persistent pain point in FL (heterogeneity-induced divergence) without the proxy-dataset overhead of FedLAW. Its modularity—improving FedAvg, FedProx, and FedDisco when stacked—makes it practically attractive for practitioners. However, the method requires identical client architectures, limiting applicability in heterogeneous-device settings, and the layer-wise variant (FedAWA-L) incurs ~15× the aggregation time of FedAvg, constraining deployment at scale. Open questions include whether the client-vector alignment signal remains informative in very deep or transformer-based models with strong layer-wise divergence, and whether the approach extends to model-heterogeneous FL.
