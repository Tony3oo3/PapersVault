## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) produces high-fidelity 3D representations but is inherently unstructured, discrete, and permutation-invariant, making it incompatible with the powerful pretrained 2D image foundation models (VAEs, diffusion models) that have driven recent generative progress. Existing work to structure Gaussians relies on voxel grids, triplanes, or complex 3D architectures that are computationally expensive and do not leverage the vast priors already learned in 2D image domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250547/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of N 3DGS primitives (each with 14 attributes: position, rotation, scale, opacity, color) and deterministically maps them via spherical projection to a 512×512×14 UVGS map, which a multi-branch CNN then compresses to a 512×512×3 Super UVGS image. For generation, the Super UVGS image is encoded by a pretrained image VAE into a latent code, and a Latent Diffusion Model samples from this latent space (from random noise for unconditional, or conditioned on text embeddings for conditional generation). The inverse pipeline decodes the latent back through the VAE, inverse mapping network, and inverse spherical projection to recover a full 3DGS object; for inpainting, a partially masked Super UVGS image serves as input.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage structured representation: (1) a learning-free spherical mapping that inscribes the 3DGS object in a sphere and projects each Gaussian's azimuthal/polar angles to UV pixel coordinates, storing all 14 attributes per pixel (with opacity-based dynamic selection to resolve many-to-one collisions), and (2) a multi-branch CNN that maps the 14-channel UVGS to a 3-channel Super UVGS image by processing position, transformation (rotation+scale), and appearance (color+opacity) through separate branches before a central branch fuses them, with a tanh-activated final layer. The inverse mapping network reverses this to recover all 3DGS attributes, trained with MSE + LPIPS losses. The key insight is that pretrained image VAEs (KL-VAE, VQVAE) generalize zero-shot to Super UVGS without fine-tuning, enabling a Latent Diffusion Model trained on the VAE latent space to directly generate novel 3DGS objects—converting a 3D generation problem into a standard 2D image generation problem while preserving multiview consistency through the structured UV representation.

## Results

On unconditional 3DGS generation (20K samples), UVGS achieves FID 26.20 and KID 3.24, outperforming the closest baseline GaussianCube (FID 34.67, KID 3.72) by 24% in FID. On text-conditioned generation (10K samples), UVGS reaches a CLIP Score of 32.62, surpassing the best baseline LGM (30.74) by ~1.9 points. For 3DGS compression, the Super UVGS representation alone reduces storage by 89.7%, and combining it with a pretrained image Autoencoder achieves 99.5% compression while maintaining PSNR of 30.9/30.8 (Cars/Full Objaverse).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UVGS outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric. In unconditional generation, UVGS (FID 26.20, KID 3.24) beats the strongest baseline GaussianCube (FID 34.67, KID 3.72) by a wide margin, and substantially outperforms Get3D (FID 53.17), EG3D (FID 74.51), and DiffTF (FID 84.57). In text-conditioned generation, UVGS achieves CLIP Score 32.62, exceeding LGM (30.74), Shap.E (30.53), GaussianCube (30.34), and DreamGaussian (28.51). Notably, UVGS is the only method that simultaneously competes in both unconditional and conditional settings, while most baselines are evaluated on only one. The paper also notes qualitative advantages: UVGS produces multiview-consistent, high-resolution objects with sharp edges, whereas Get3D suffers from 3D inconsistency and artifacts, and GaussianCube shows symmetric inconsistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior approaches that structure 3DGS via voxel grids (GaussianCube), triplanes, or direct diffusion on Gaussian attributes by instead borrowing spherical mapping from classical 3D graphics to project Gaussians onto a 2D UV map. The key conceptual leap is the 'Super UVGS' compression: a multi-branch CNN that unifies heterogeneous 14-channel Gaussian attributes (position, rotation, scale, opacity, color) into a 3-channel image compatible with pretrained 2D foundation models. Unlike methods relying on Score Distillation Sampling or multi-view rendering for generation, UVGS reframes 3DGS generation as a standard 2D image generation problem, enabling direct use of LDMs and VAEs without 3D-specific architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot generalization of pretrained image VAEs and diffusion models to Super UVGS without fine-tuning is a practical bridge that could accelerate 3DGS generation by leveraging the massive 2D model ecosystem. However, the approach is currently limited to single-layer UVGS maps suitable for simple Objaverse-scale objects, with noticeable quality degradation (PSNR ~31 vs 34.6 for original 3DGS) and washed-out appearances. Open questions include whether multi-layer UVGS can handle complex scenes and human heads, how to better exploit empty UV pixels for efficiency, and whether the zero-shot transfer holds for more diverse or higher-fidelity 3D content beyond curated object datasets.
