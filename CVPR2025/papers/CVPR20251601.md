## Problem

Existing mesh quality assessment methods operate exclusively in either 3D vertex space (model-based) or 2D rendered projections (projection-based), and neither captures the intricate geometry-texture interactions that govern perceived quality. Model-based methods lack access to the object's actual rendered appearance, while projection-based methods lack 3D topological understanding, causing them to miss geometry distortions that are only apparent across multiple viewpoints. This paper addresses the gap by explicitly modeling how texture and geometry mutually influence each other's perceptual impact.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference colored mesh and a distorted colored mesh, each defined by vertices, connectivity, UV coordinates, and a 2D texture map. Internally, it renders six perpendicular viewpoints: colored projections at 512×512 and 3D feature projections at 128×128 (with 64×64 and 16×16 patches respectively) to ensure pixel-to-pixel alignment. The output is a single scalar quality score obtained by feeding the absolute difference of the two meshes' quality representations into a fully connected network.

## Method

HybridMQA integrates a model branch and a texture branch through a novel feature rendering and cross-attention mechanism. The model branch projects 2D normal, vertex, and texture maps through a CNN base encoder, samples per-vertex features via UV mapping to initialize a graph, and refines them with a GCN that performs message-passing over mesh connectivity to learn 3D surface representations. A custom differentiable renderer then projects these 3D features into 2D at the same six camera positions used for colored renderings, establishing pixel-to-pixel alignment. The quality encoder applies multi-scale cross-attention (with alternating query/key-value roles) between aligned 3D feature patches and colored image patches to capture geometry-texture interactions, concatenating the results with direct 3D feature embeddings to form the final quality representation. Training uses a combined MAE and rank loss against MOS ground truth, with ResNet-50 (ImageNet-pretrained) as the image encoder and a randomly initialized CNN as the 3D feature encoder.

## Results

HybridMQA achieves the best SRCC and PLCC across all four colored mesh quality assessment datasets: 0.892/0.897 on Nehmé et al., 0.887/0.896 on SJTU-TMQA, 0.912/0.919 on TSMD, and 0.897/0.927 on CMDM. It outperforms the strongest projection-based baseline 3D-PSSIM by 6.5% and 7.7% in relative PLCC on Nehmé et al. and SJTU-TMQA, respectively, and surpasses the best model-based method (Fu et al.) by a large margin (0.892 vs. 0.688 SRCC on Nehmé et al.). In the generalization test (trained on Nehmé et al. or TSMD, tested on SJTU-TMQA), HybridMQA reaches 0.800/0.783 and 0.846/0.811 SRCC/PLCC, substantially exceeding Graphics-LPIPS (0.731/0.734 and 0.784/0.773).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HybridMQA outperforms all 11 baselines on every dataset where they are directly comparable. Against the strongest projection-based method 3D-PSSIM [19], HybridMQA gains 0.050 SRCC and 0.055 PLCC on Nehmé et al. (0.892/0.897 vs. 0.882/0.842) and 0.045 SRCC and 0.064 PLCC on SJTU-TMQA (0.887/0.896 vs. 0.842/0.832). Against Graphics-LPIPS [27], the gains are even larger: 0.170 SRCC on Nehmé et al. and 0.097 SRCC on SJTU-TMQA. Among model-based methods, the best is Fu et al. [9] (0.688 SRCC on Nehmé et al.), which HybridMQA surpasses by 0.204 SRCC. On CMDM, where distortions are single-type, the gap narrows (HybridMQA 0.897 vs. Graphics-LPIPS 0.859 SRCC), but HybridMQA still leads. In the cross-dataset generalization test, HybridMQA trained on TSMD (0.846 SRCC) approaches the performance of 3D-PSSIM trained directly on SJTU-TMQA (0.842 SRCC), demonstrating strong transferability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by being the first to unify model-based and projection-based mesh quality assessment into a single end-to-end framework. Model-based methods (MSDM2, FMPD, GeodesicPSIM) operate in 3D vertex space but lack access to rendered appearance, while projection-based methods (Graphics-LPIPS, 3D-PSSIM) capture texture but miss 3D topology. HybridMQA repurposes graph convolutional networks (from mesh processing literature) to learn 3D surface representations, then introduces a novel differentiable feature rendering technique to project these into 2D aligned with colored renderings, enabling cross-attention-based interaction modeling between the two domains—a combination absent from prior MQA work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit modeling of geometry-texture interactions addresses a fundamental gap: prior methods treat geometry and texture as independent quality channels, yet human perception couples them (e.g., complex textures mask geometry distortions). The generalization result—matching 3D-PSSIM's in-domain performance when trained on a different dataset—suggests the hybrid representation captures more transferable quality cues. Key limitations include memory scaling with mesh size (GCN processes the full graph) and fixed perpendicular viewpoints without importance weighting, leaving open questions about scalability to large meshes and whether semantic-aware viewpoint selection would further improve performance. Future directions toward no-reference MQA and point cloud QA indicate broader applicability.
