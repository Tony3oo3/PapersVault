## Problem

Existing dataset distillation methods, which synthesize small data summaries from large datasets, degrade severely when the source dataset follows a long-tailed class distribution. The paper identifies two root causes: (1) trajectory matching on imbalanced data forces the student to mimic biased expert weights, distilling less information into tail classes, and (2) experts trained on imbalanced data produce unreliable soft labels and poor tail-class accuracy, weakening supervision. This is the first work to systematically address long-tailed dataset distillation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a long-tailed RGB image dataset (e.g., CIFAR-10-LT at 32×32, ImageNet-LT at 224×224) where class sample counts follow an exponential decay governed by an imbalance factor β, along with their class labels. It produces a small, uniformly distributed synthetic dataset with a fixed number of images per class (IPC = 10, 20, or 50) and soft labels generated by a decoupled classifier expert. The synthetic dataset is intended for training models that are evaluated on a balanced test set, and the method is evaluated across four datasets at multiple imbalance factors (β = 10 to 200).

## Method

The method combines two strategies within a trajectory-matching distillation framework. First, Distribution-agnostic Matching (DAM) introduces a long-tailed distributed loss (inspired by Balanced Softmax) that re-weights the student's per-class gradients during the inner optimization loop, making the student's weight trajectory resemble the biased expert's without actually distilling the imbalance into the synthetic images. Second, Expert Decoupling (ED) trains two experts in a decoupled manner—a representation expert on the full imbalanced dataset and a classifier expert on a balanced subset—and jointly matches the student's backbone to the representation expert while matching the student's classifier to the classifier expert. The classifier expert, being unbiased, provides high-confidence soft labels for initializing the synthetic dataset, addressing the unreliable tail-class predictions of a single long-tailed expert.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-LT, the proposed method achieves 70.5% top-1 accuracy at β=10, IPC=50, surpassing the best baseline DATM (66.7%) by 3.8%. At the extreme imbalance setting β=200, IPC=50, the method reaches 62.3%, matching the full-dataset accuracy of 62.1% (lossless performance), while the best baseline IDM drops to 49.9% and DATM to 40.1%. On ImageNet-LT, the method achieves 21.0% at β=10, IPC=20, more than doubling the best baseline SRe2L (10.1%). The advantage grows with increasing imbalance factor across all four datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against 11 baselines spanning trajectory matching (MTT, DATM), gradient matching (DC), distribution matching (DREAM, IDM), generative methods (Minimax), and active learning (K-Center Greedy, Graph-Cut). DATM [13] is the strongest baseline at low imbalance (β=10, IPC=50: 66.7% vs. 70.5%), but degrades sharply at β=200 (40.1%), where the proposed method leads by 22.2%. IDM [47] is the second-best baseline, remaining competitive at moderate imbalance (β=50: 56.1% vs. 65.8%) but falling to near-random-selection levels at β=200 (49.9% vs. 62.3%). MTT [4] and DREAM [28] show consistent but moderate gaps (8–15% behind) across all settings. Minimax [12], K-Center Greedy [36], and Graph-Cut [17] are substantially weaker, often below random selection at high imbalance. On ImageNet-LT, SRe2L [44] and TESLA [6] are the only other methods that complete evaluation; the proposed method outperforms SRe2L by over 10 points (21.0% vs. 10.1% at β=10, IPC=20). G-VBSM [39] fails entirely (out-of-memory or near-zero accuracy). The cross-architecture experiment (Table 4) further shows the distilled dataset generalizes: 64.7% on ConvNet-3, 60.0% on ResNet-18, 55.5% on VGG-11, and 53.2% on AlexNet, all substantially above DATM and MTT on the same architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends trajectory-matching dataset distillation (MTT, DATM) to the long-tailed setting, where prior methods were designed exclusively for balanced datasets. It adapts two ideas from the long-tailed recognition literature—Balanced Softmax-style reweighting and decoupled representation/classifier training (Kang et al., ICLR 2020)—into the distillation optimization loop. The key departure from prior DD work is the recognition that the bi-level optimization in trajectory matching implicitly distills the data-quantity imbalance into the synthetic dataset, and that the expert's tail-class bias corrupts both the matching signal and the soft-label initialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first effective method for distilling long-tailed datasets, a setting that is prevalent in real-world applications (medical imaging, open-world recognition) but was entirely unaddressed by prior DD literature. The clean diagnosis—that the failure stems from distribution-induced gradient bias rather than reduced sample count—provides a template for understanding how other distributional shifts might break DD. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other non-uniform distributions (e.g., multi-modal or hierarchical class structures), whether the decoupling strategy can be made fully automatic without manual hyperparameter tuning per imbalance factor, and whether the method extends to non-CNN backbones such as vision transformers.
