## Problem

Existing 3D human pose and shape (HPS) methods either rely on tightly cropped person images (discarding scene context needed for occlusion and interaction) or process full images but suffer from detection failures and lower accuracy. Critically, no prior method provides a mechanism to incorporate readily available 'side information'—such as bounding boxes from detectors, segmentation masks, language descriptions, or interaction labels—from vision-language models or user input to improve reconstruction robustness and accuracy in challenging multi-person scenarios.

## Input / Output

The method consumes full 896×896 RGB images alongside at least one spatial prompt per person (a bounding box or segmentation mask) and optionally semantic prompts (a CLIP text embedding describing body shape, and a binary two-person interaction flag). It outputs per-person SMPL-X parameters (orientation, 22-joint pose, 10-D shape, translation) in camera space; translation is regressed as focal-length-normalized 2D position and inverse depth rather than directly. The video variant additionally produces temporally coherent motion in world coordinates via a temporal transformer and metric SLAM (DROID-SLAM + ZoeDepth), along with joint contact probabilities for foot-skating correction.

## Method

PromptHMR combines a DINOv2 ViT image encoder (run once per frame at 896×896), a multi-modal prompt encoder that maps boxes (positional encoding + learned embeddings), masks (strided convolutions added to image tokens), text (CLIP embeddings), and interaction labels into unified token vectors, and a three-block transformer decoder that applies self-attention, promptable cross-person attention (gated on/off by the interaction label), and two-way cross-attention with image tokens to produce SMPL-X parameters. Robustness to diverse spatial inputs is achieved by training with simulated noisy full-body, face, and truncated bounding boxes plus random masking of prompt types so the model handles any combination at test time. For body shape, SHAPY generates automatic shape-to-text descriptions paired with ground-truth shapes during training. The video extension appends a 12-block decoder-only temporal transformer over per-frame SMPL-X tokens and follows TRAM's metric SLAM pipeline to lift camera-space motion into world coordinates, with a post-processing step that zeroes contact-joint velocities to reduce foot skating.

## Results

On camera-space reconstruction, PromptHMR achieves PA-MPJPE of 36.6 mm on 3DPW, 41.0 mm on EMDB, and 37.3 mm on RICH, outperforming all full-image baselines (Multi-HMR: 45.9/50.1/46.3; BEV: 46.9/70.9/—) while matching the best cropped-image method CameraHMR (35.1/43.3/34.0). The video variant PromptHMR-Vid further improves to 35.5/40.1/37.0 PA-MPJPE and achieves SOTA world-coordinate motion (WA-MPJPE₁₀₀ = 71.0 mm, RTE = 1.3%) on EMDB. On two-person interaction benchmarks, PromptHMR reduces Pair-PA-MPJPE to 39.5 mm on HI4D and 45.3 mm on CHI3D, substantially outperforming BUDDI (98/68) and Multi-HMR (80.6/100.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest cropped-image baseline CameraHMR (same training data), PromptHMR is slightly worse on 3DPW (36.6 vs 35.1 PA-MPJPE) and RICH (37.3 vs 34.0) but clearly better on EMDB (41.0 vs 43.3), confirming that the promptable full-image architecture achieves comparable metric accuracy without requiring person crops. Against full-image baselines, PromptHMR dominates: it beats Multi-HMR by 9.3 mm PA-MPJPE on 3DPW (36.6 vs 45.9) and 9.1 mm on EMDB (41.0 vs 50.1), and beats BEV by 10.3 mm on 3DPW. In the video setting, PromptHMR-Vid outperforms TRAM on EMDB (40.1 vs 45.7 PA-MPJPE) and GVHMR on all three benchmarks, while also achieving the best world-coordinate metrics (WA-MPJPE₁₀₀ 71.0 vs TRAM 76.4, GVHMR 111.0; Foot Skating 3.5 vs TRAM 23.4). On interaction reconstruction, PromptHMR (trained on HI4D) reduces Pair-PA-MPJPE to 39.5 mm on HI4D and 45.3 mm on CHI3D, halving the error of BUDDI (98/68) and dramatically outperforming Multi-HMR (80.6/100.0), which was not trained on interaction data.

## Relation to Prior Work

PromptHMR departs from the classical 'pixels-to-parameters' HPS formulation by introducing explicit spatial and semantic prompts (bounding boxes, masks, text descriptions, interaction labels) as first-class inputs to a transformer-based regressor. It builds on the DINOv2 vision backbone and CLIP text encoder for feature extraction, adopts TRAM's metric-SLAM pipeline for world-coordinate video reconstruction, and leverages SHAPY for automatic shape-to-text generation during training. Conceptually, it positions itself as a bridge between VLMs (which understand scene semantics but lack 3D metric accuracy) and crop-based HPS regressors (which are metrically precise but discard scene context), whereas prior language-based HPS methods like ChatPose and PoseEmbroider attempted this fusion with substantially lower 3D accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The promptable formulation offers a practical, unified framework that handles crowded scenes, close interactions, shape ambiguity, and video in a single architecture, making it a strong default baseline for 3D HPS in diverse settings. A key open question the authors highlight is automating prompt generation via VLMs—currently, shape descriptions and interaction labels must be supplied manually, limiting end-to-end deployment. The paper also acknowledges that interpenetration in close interactions persists and that many other side-information types (action descriptions, 3D scene context, body measurements) remain unexplored, suggesting substantial headroom for future promptable-perception models.
