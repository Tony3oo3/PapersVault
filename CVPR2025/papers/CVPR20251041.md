## Problem

Single-view 3D telepresence faces a fundamental trade-off: per-frame 3D lifting methods (e.g., LP3D) faithfully capture dynamic per-frame appearance (lighting, expressions) but produce temporally inconsistent 3D videos due to the ill-posed nature of single-image reconstruction, while 3D reenactment methods (e.g., GPAvatar) yield temporally coherent avatars but cannot preserve the user's instantaneous dynamic appearance. No existing method simultaneously achieves both temporal stability and faithful per-frame appearance reconstruction from a single camera.

## Input / Output

The method takes a near-frontal reference image (a selfie or a frame auto-extracted via DECA head pose estimation) and a single RGB video frame as input. The reference image is lifted into a canonical triplane (3×32×256×256) via a modified LP3D encoder, and each video frame is independently lifted into a raw triplane of the same shape. The output is a fused triplane that can be volume-rendered to arbitrary novel viewpoints, producing temporally coherent 3D portrait video. The system is trained entirely on synthetic multi-view data generated by the Next3D 3D GAN, requiring no real multi-view captures.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage triplane fusion pipeline. First, a Triplane Undistorter (SPyNet-based) predicts a 2D correction warping for each of the three triplane axes to remove view-dependent distortions from the raw triplane, using the canonical triplane as identity conditioning rather than as a warping target—this distinction from optical flow is critical, as full alignment destroys dynamic information. Second, a Triplane Fuser (RVRT backbone with a modified 5-layer ConvNet skip connection replacing the original summation) combines the undistorted triplane with the canonical triplane, guided by per-plane visibility maps predicted by a 5-layer ConvNet, to recover occluded regions while preserving per-frame lighting and expression. Training uses only synthetic data from Next3D conditioned on random FLAME coefficients, with pseudo-groundtruth triplanes from a frozen LP3D for supervision, plus shoulder augmentation via camera-ray warping during volume rendering to simulate body language.

## Results

On the NeRSemble multi-view portrait dataset, the proposed triplane fusion method achieves state-of-the-art performance across all metrics: 22.770 dB Synthesis PSNR, 22.440 dB NVS PSNR, 0.219 LPIPS, 0.187 ArcFace ID distance, and 0.158 expression L2 distance. Notably, the gap between Synthesis and NVS PSNR is only 0.330 dB (vs. 0.806 dB for LP3D), indicating substantially better generalization to novel viewpoints. The method is trained exclusively on synthetic multi-view data generated by the Next3D 3D GAN, yet outperforms all baselines on both in-studio and in-the-wild evaluations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest reconstruction competitor LP3D [52], it improves NVS PSNR by 0.915 dB (22.440 vs. 21.525), reduces ID distance from 0.215 to 0.187, and lowers expression error from 0.168 to 0.158, while also halving the input-view sensitivity (IVV drops from 1.025 to 0.245 dB PSNR). Against the reenactment baselines, GPAvatar [6] achieves competitive PSNR (21.949) but lags significantly on expression accuracy (0.204 vs. 0.158) and identity (0.207 vs. 0.187), and cannot capture per-frame dynamic appearance such as lighting changes or novel expressions absent from the reference image. Li et al. [28] performs worst overall (18.573 dB Synthesis PSNR, 0.266 Expr), while the inversion-based VIVE3D [13] is the weakest on identity preservation (0.395 ID) and expression (0.290 Expr), reflecting the well-known limitations of GAN inversion for faithful per-frame reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LP3D (Trevithick et al., SIGGRAPH 2023), a feed-forward encoder that lifts a single RGB image into a triplane for real-time 3D portrait view synthesis, by introducing a triplane fusion pipeline that combines a canonical triplane prior (from a near-frontal reference image) with per-frame dynamic triplanes. It departs from the per-frame independence of LP3D and from 3D reenactment methods (GPAvatar, Li et al.) that sacrifice per-frame appearance fidelity for temporal coherence, positioning itself as a fusion of both paradigms. The undistorter borrows the SPyNet architecture for 2D warping correction, and the fuser adapts the RVRT transformer backbone, while training relies entirely on synthetic multi-view data generated by the Next3D 3D GAN conditioned on FLAME parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a core practical bottleneck for consumer 3D telepresence: the inability of existing single-camera systems to simultaneously preserve temporal identity stability and capture per-frame dynamics like lighting shifts and nuanced expressions. The proposed multi-view evaluation protocol (scoring across all input-novel viewpoint combinations) is a useful methodological contribution that exposes overfitting to input views in prior methods. Key limitations remain: the method is currently slower than real-time, relies on a single reference image, and can produce blurry results when the input view is an extreme side angle with a very different expression from the reference. Open questions include whether multiple reference views, real-time optimization, or learned temporal priors could close the remaining gap to real-time deployment.
