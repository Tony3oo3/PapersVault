## Problem

Current 3D diffusion-based reconstruction methods rely on visual images or fixed partial observations, making them vulnerable to occlusion, lighting variation, and inability to capture local geometric details of complex shapes. No prior work combines active tactile exploration with a diffusion model to iteratively refine shape reconstruction, and existing active-touch methods (e.g., ActiveVT) require generating the full high-dimensional shape at every exploration step, which is computationally wasteful.

## Input / Output

The method consumes up to N tactile images (each converted to a touch chart of M×4 vertices with x,y,z coordinates and contact status) and optionally one initial RGB image. It outputs a 64×64×64 T-SDF volume (decoded via marching cubes to a mesh) only at the final exploration step, and at intermediate steps it produces a low-dimensional denoised latent vector used to select the next touch location from 50 predefined actions on a sphere. The separation means the expensive shape decoder and fusion module are invoked once, not per step.

## Method

Touch2Shape builds on SDFusion's VQ-VAE to encode T-SDF volumes into a compact latent space, then trains a touch-conditioned denoising diffusion model where the condition is extracted from touch charts via a TouchCNN + position-encoded convolutional encoder producing N tokens. A MoCo-based contrastive encoder aligns touch features (queries) with shape latent features (keys) in a joint space, and in the visual-tactile setting ResNet image tokens are concatenated with touch tokens before the denoiser. A touch shape fusion module voxelize all historical touch charts, encodes them at multiple scales, and fuses these with the VQ-VAE decoder's intermediate features via a learnable attention-weighted combination to recover local details lost in the compressed latent. For exploration, the denoised latent vector (not the full shape) is fed to a DQN policy whose reward is the Heaviside-gated difference in diffusion loss between consecutive steps, encouraging touches that reduce reconstruction uncertainty without ever materializing the full T-SDF volume mid-exploration.

## Results

On the ABC dataset (Table 1), Touch2Shape achieves a CD of 6.794 with 1 grasp under the tactile-only setting, outperforming ActiveVT (8.220) and VTRecon (9.016). Under the visual-tactile setting, the advantage is more pronounced: 1.406 CD with 1 grasp versus 2.486 (ActiveVT) and 2.637 (VTRecon). On ShapeNet (Table 2), with 20 touches the tactile-only variant reaches an average EMD of 0.053, substantially below TouchSDF's 0.081, while the visual-tactile variant further reduces this to 0.042. The RL-trained exploration policy reduces CD to 6.63% of the initial value after 5 grasps in the tactile-only setting, compared to 23.84% for ActiveVT's policy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VTRecon [33] and ActiveVT [34] on the ABC dataset, Touch2Shape wins in every setting where at least one grasp is available: 6.794 vs. 8.220/9.016 CD (T, 1 grasp) and 1.406 vs. 2.486/2.637 CD (T+V, 1 grasp). Notably, with zero grasps under the tactile-only setting, Touch2Shape performs worse (40.283 CD) than both baselines, reflecting the difficulty of reconstructing from no tactile input; however, a single grasp immediately reverses this gap. On ShapeNet, Touch2Shape surpasses TouchSDF [7] in average EMD at 10 touches (0.056 vs. 0.112) and 20 touches (0.053 vs. 0.081) under the tactile-only setting, with the visual-tactile variant achieving 0.042 at 20 touches. For the exploration policy (Table 3), Touch2Shape's RL policy reduces CD to 6.63% of the initial value after 5 grasps in the tactile-only setting, a 3.6× improvement over ActiveVT's 23.84%, while in the visual-tactile setting both methods perform comparably (86.96% vs. 89.32%). The ablation (Table 4) confirms that the contrastive encoder and touch-shape fusion module each contribute incremental gains, with the full T+V pipeline reaching 1.304 CD versus 4.430 without either module.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SDFusion's T-SDF + VQ-VAE framework for 3D shape encoding/decoding and extends it with a touch-conditioned diffusion model, while adapting the RL-based active exploration paradigm from ActiveVT. The key architectural departure from ActiveVT is the separation of shape exploration (operating in a compact latent space via the diffusion model) from shape reconstruction (full T-SDF decoding only at the final step), eliminating the need to generate and re-encode a complete shape at every exploration step. Additionally, the paper introduces a MoCo-based contrastive touch encoder to align tactile and shape representations in a joint space, and a multi-scale touch shape fusion module for local detail refinement—neither of which appears in prior touch-reconstruction work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The latent-space-guided exploration strategy offers a practical efficiency gain for robotic touch-based reconstruction, since the expensive full-shape decoding is deferred to a single final step. The finding that a compressed diffusion latent provides a stronger exploration signal than a full mesh (especially in the tactile-only setting, where the Oracle ratio drops from ~16 to ~5) suggests that compact generative representations can be more informative for active sensing than explicit geometry. However, the work remains entirely in simulation with a single environment, limited to single-object reconstruction at 64³ resolution, and the authors themselves flag transfer to real robots, full-scene reconstruction, and integration with neural rendering as open challenges.
