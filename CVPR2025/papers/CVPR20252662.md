## Problem

Existing multimodal alignment methods adopt a one-versus-one paradigm where each extended modality requires a dedicated encoder and separate training, causing exponential parameter growth, task-specific models, and hindered cross-modal knowledge transfer. The paper asks whether all modalities can be jointly aligned within a single model in one unified training phase without sacrificing performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252662/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

All six modalities (RGB images at 224×224, text labels, audio waveforms, video clips, 3D point clouds, and depth maps) are converted into sequential tokens of length 196 and dimension 768 via modality-specific tokenizers, then processed by a single shared ViT-B encoder. The model outputs a unified embedding in a shared representation space, enabling zero-shot classification, cross-modal retrieval, and multi-modal composition (e.g., combining audio and video queries to retrieve images) without any task-specific fine-tuning. Only 7.8M parameters are trainable across all modalities, with the base CLIP encoder remaining frozen.

## Method

UNIALIGN builds a modality-aware Mixture-of-Experts layer on top of a frozen CLIP ViT-B encoder, where experts are grouped into modality-specific sets with hierarchical routing (first selecting the modality group, then routing within it), and each expert is a LoRA adapter (rank 4, α=32) applied to the preserved original FFN rather than replacing it. For alignment, a soft modality binding loss uses unpaired samples across datasets by computing KL divergence between cross-modal similarity distributions and the semantic similarity of their class-label text embeddings, eliminating the need for instance-level paired data. Two additional distillation objectives guide training: one transfers alignment knowledge from well-aligned anchor modality pairs (e.g., image↔text) to extended modality pairs (e.g., point cloud↔text), and another distills features and cross-modal alignment from partial-modal foundation models (ULIP, ViT-LENS, One-Peace). A modality-aware gradient accumulation strategy processes each modality's forward pass and loss separately before a single backpropagation step, mitigating optimization dispersion across heterogeneous data.

## Results

UNIALIGN achieves 85.3% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K with only 7.8M trainable parameters, matching Hiera (85.2%, 70M) and MetaFormer (85.4%, 86.6M) while using ~9% of their parameters. On depth-only scene classification, it reaches 71.4% on NYU-D and 58.2% on SUN RGB-D, surpassing VIT-LENS (68.5%/52.2%) and ImageBind (54.0%/35.1%). For speech recognition on Speech Commands V2, UNIALIGN attains 97.4% top-1 with 1.8M parameters, outperforming MetaFormer (97.0% with 86.3M). On video-text retrieval (MSR-VTT), it achieves 37.0 R@1 and 70.7 R@5, comparable to ViT-LENS (37.6/72.6) and ImageBind (36.8/70.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet-1K, UNIALIGN (85.3%, 7.8M) matches OMNIVORE (85.3%, 88.2M) and is within 0.1% of MetaFormer (85.4%, 86.6M) while using roughly 9% of their trainable parameters, and slightly edges out Hiera (85.2%, 70M). On depth scene classification, UNIALIGN with image+text anchors (71.4% NYU-D, 58.2% SUN-D) clearly outperforms VIT-LENS (68.5%/52.2%) and ImageBind (54.0%/35.1%), though it trails the single-task Depth Swin (76.4%/69.1%). On 3D shape classification, UNIALIGN (55.9% ModelNet40, 42.5% ScanObjectNN) falls short of ULIP (60.4%/51.5%) and VIT-LENS (65.4%/–), which use dedicated point-cloud encoders. For audio, UNIALIGN (23.8 mAP AudioSet, 70.5% ESC) lags behind LanguageBind (27.7/91.8) and VIT-LENS (27.2/80.9), partly due to using only 0.5M vs. 1.6M AudioSet videos. On video retrieval (MSR-VTT), UNIALIGN (37.0 R@1) is within 0.6 of VIT-LENS (37.6) and slightly above ImageBind (36.8). For speech recognition, UNIALIGN (97.4%, 1.8M) beats MetaFormer (97.0%, 86.3M) and, at matched parameter budgets (1.1M vs. 1.8M), outperforms MetaFormer by 19.1 points (97.4% vs. 78.3%).

## Relation to Prior Work

UNIALIGN departs from the dominant one-versus-one multimodal alignment paradigm (ImageBind, ViT-LENS, LanguageBind), where each extended modality requires a dedicated encoder and separate training session, by proposing an all-in-one architecture built on a single CLIP ViT-B backbone. It repurposes the Mixture-of-Experts schema (Shazeer et al., ICLR 2017) with modality-aware expert grouping and routing, and integrates LoRA (Hu et al., ICLR 2021) into each expert to preserve pre-trained knowledge while adding only 7.8M trainable parameters. The soft modality binding strategy, which aligns unpaired samples across datasets using class-label semantic similarity as a KL-divergence target, extends the intermediate-modality alignment idea from ImageBind to a setting that does not require instance-level paired annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single compact model (7.8M trainable params) can handle six modalities across eleven benchmarks in one training phase, challenging the assumption that each modality demands its own large encoder. However, performance still trails modality-specific SOTA on 3D shape classification (55.9% vs. 65.4% for ViT-LENS) and audio (23.8 mAP vs. 27.7 for LanguageBind), and the audio evaluation was compromised by data link expiration. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger backbones (ViT-L/H), whether soft modality binding generalizes to truly unstructured or open-vocabulary data beyond class labels, and whether the modality-aware MoE can accommodate genuinely novel modalities at inference time without retraining.
