## Problem

Adversarial examples crafted for a surrogate model often overfit to it, yielding poor transferability to unseen target models. Existing transferability-enhancing methods introduce increasingly complex mechanisms (flat-loss search, learned transformations, regularization) that provide only marginal gains and are difficult to adapt across modalities. The paper identifies a mirroring relationship between model generalization and adversarial transferability, asking whether 'model augmentation' in hypothesis space can serve as a principled, modality-agnostic solution.

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean input (RGB image at 224×224 or a 3D point cloud of 1024 points), its ground-truth class label, and a frozen surrogate model, and produces a single adversarial example constrained to an ℓ∞ ball of radius ε (16/255 for images, 0.06–0.18 per-point for point clouds). During each iteration, the input is consumed multiple times: once in its raw form and N_e × N_p additional times after applying randomly sampled input transformation operators and additive perturbations, all fed through the same surrogate model to accumulate a diversified gradient estimate.

## Method

OPS formulates adversarial transfer as a stochastic optimization over the neighborhood of the surrogate model in hypothesis space, approximated by two augmentation operators: (1) composing the surrogate with randomly sampled input transformation operators (f∘op) drawn from a hierarchically balanced set built by k-th order compositions of a basic operator pool, and (2) adding random perturbations δ sampled uniformly from concentric ℓ∞ balls of varying radii. The resulting objective (Eq. 8) is solved with a momentum-iterative (MI) update where the gradient at each step is averaged over all N_e×N_p+1 augmented forward passes, and the perturbation is updated via sign of the momentum-normalized average gradient clipped to the budget. The key design choice is that operators and perturbations are re-sampled fresh at every iteration, making the optimization stochastic and preventing the gradient from locking onto a single transformation direction. For 3D point clouds, the basic operator set is replaced with rigid-body transformations, and the radius list is adjusted, demonstrating modality-agnostic applicability without architectural changes.

## Results

On ImageNet (ResNet-18 surrogate → 7 target models), OPS(10,5,5) achieves the highest transfer attack success rate across all targets, reaching 58.7% on the hardest cross-architecture pair (ResNet-18→ViT) versus 43.3% for the best gradient-based baseline (NCS) and 51.6% for the best input-transformation baseline (L2T). Scaling to OPS(10,30,30) pushes ViT ASR to 76.3% and Swin to 93.6%. On ModelNet40 (PointNet surrogate → 8 targets, ε=0.06), OPS_pc achieves 76.8% on PointConv and 67.3% on PT-v2, outperforming the strongest 3D baseline PF-Attack (68.3% and 57.6% respectively) at the same budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OPS outperforms all 14 baselines on every target model in both image and 3D point cloud experiments. Among gradient-based methods, NCS (2024) is the strongest baseline, yet OPS(10,5,5) still leads by 10.4 points on ViT (58.7% vs 43.3%) and 7.3 points on Swin (82.1% vs 74.8%). Among input-transformation methods, L2T (2024) and DeCoWA (2024) are the closest competitors; OPS(10,5,5) exceeds L2T by 7.1 points on ViT and 2.2 points on Swin, while OPS(10,10,10) widens the gap to 19.6 and 10.0 points respectively. On 3D point clouds (Table 3, ε=0.06), OPS_pc surpasses the best transfer baseline PF-Attack by 8.5 points on PointConv (76.8% vs 68.3%) and 9.7 points on PT-v2 (67.3% vs 57.6%), and exceeds white-box baselines KNN, GeoA³, and HiT-ADV by 10–50+ points on most targets. Against robust models (Table 2), OPS(10,30,30) achieves 97.8% on Inc-v4(ens4) and 86.4% on ResNet-101(NRP), outperforming the best baselines (L2T at 87.1% and 62.2%) by 10.7 and 24.2 points respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

OPS builds directly on MI (Momentum Iterative) as its optimization backbone, but its conceptual departure is the reframing of transferability improvement as 'model augmentation' in hypothesis space, mirroring how data augmentation addresses generalization in sample space. While prior input-transformation methods (DIM, SIM, DeCoWA, SIA, BSR, L2T) use transformations to increase input diversity or learn per-sample optimal transforms, OPS repurposes them as random probes of the surrogate model's neighborhood, emphasizing gradient diversity over input diversity. The stochastic optimization formulation unifies operator sampling and perturbation sampling into a single framework, distinguishing it from both gradient-stabilization methods (VMI, GRA, PGN, NCS) and transformation-search methods (L2T, AITL).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mirroring between generalization and transferability provides a clean conceptual lens that could guide future work beyond the visual domain, and the modularity of OPS (swap the operator set for a new modality) makes it a practical template for adversarial attacks on emerging data types. The 3DTAB benchmark fills a genuine gap in the 3D adversarial literature, enabling fair comparison across nine architectures and two defense families. However, the hypothesis-space topological analysis remains informal (a pseudo-metric on a function space), and the method's reliance on MI as the solver limits its theoretical grounding; whether the 'neighborhood sampling' interpretation truly explains the gains or whether it is simply an effective randomization strategy remains an open question. The Point-PN anomaly (ASR decreasing with more computation) hints at architectural properties that could inform new defenses but is left unexplored.
