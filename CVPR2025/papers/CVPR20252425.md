## Problem

Single-image 3D reconstruction has diverged into regression-based methods (fast and aligned to visible surfaces but over-smoothed on occluded regions due to a bijective mapping assumption) and diffusion-based generative methods (better at modeling uncertainty but computationally expensive and poorly aligned with the input image). SPAR3D addresses how to combine probabilistic modeling of the ill-posed occluded-surface problem with the speed and visible-surface fidelity of regression, without inheriting the drawbacks of either approach.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252425/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a textured 3D mesh with full PBR materials (albedo, metallic, roughness, surface normals) plus an estimated HDR environment illumination map. Internally, the pipeline operates on a 512-point sparse point cloud (6 channels: XYZ + RGB) as an intermediate representation and a 384×384 triplane at DMTet resolution 160, achieving end-to-end inference in 0.7 seconds. The point cloud intermediate is also exposed to users for interactive editing of unseen surfaces before final mesh generation.

## Method

SPAR3D uses a two-stage design that offloads uncertainty modeling to a lightweight point diffusion stage and deterministic surface refinement to a regression-based meshing stage. The point sampling stage is a DDPM-based transformer denoiser (16 blocks, 1024-dim, 16 heads) conditioned on DINOv2 image tokens, trained to generate 512-point white-lit albedo point clouds via DDIM sampling with classifier-free guidance. The meshing stage is a triplane transformer (4 two-stream blocks, 3072 latent tokens) that fuses point cloud tokens and DINOv2 image features to produce 384×384 triplane features, from which geometry is extracted via differentiable Marching Tetrahedra with learned vertex offsets and normals. Inverse rendering jointly estimates albedo, metallic, roughness (via Beta prior with AlphaCLIP foreground masks), and illumination (via an encoder into the RENI++ latent space), supervised by a differentiable Disney PBR renderer using Monte Carlo integration with Multiple Importance Sampling and screen-space shadowing. The key insight is that the low-resolution point cloud makes diffusion sampling fast while still providing enough 3D guidance for the meshing stage to produce high-fidelity visible surfaces from local image features and plausible occluded surfaces from the sampled points.

## Results

On GSO, SPAR3D achieves the best Chamfer Distance (0.120), F-score at 0.1 (0.584), F-score at 0.2 (0.850), F-score at 0.5 (0.983), PSNR (18.6 dB), and LPIPS (0.139) among all compared methods, with an inference time of 0.7 s. On OmniObject3D, SPAR3D similarly leads on CD (0.122), FS@0.1 (0.587), FS@0.2 (0.845), FS@0.5 (0.978), and LPIPS (0.140). The only metric where SPAR3D trails is SSIM (0.836 vs. 0.839 for SF3D on GSO), which the authors attribute to Monte Carlo noise in their differentiable shader. SPAR3D is ~50× faster than multi-view diffusion baselines (LGM 41 s, InstantMesh 36 s, Era3D 348 s) while being only ~2× slower than pure regression methods (TripoSR 0.2 s, SF3D 0.3 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPAR3D outperforms all nine baselines on geometry metrics (CD, FS@0.1/0.2/0.5) and perceptual quality (LPIPS) on both GSO and OmniObject3D. Against the closest regression-based competitors, SF3D (CD 0.137, FS@0.1 0.540) and TripoSR (CD 0.145, FS@0.1 0.501), SPAR3D improves CD by 12–18% and FS@0.1 by 8–16%, while matching or exceeding their PSNR. Against multi-view diffusion methods (LGM, CRM, InstantMesh, Era3D), SPAR3D achieves substantially better geometry (e.g., CD 0.120 vs. 0.135–0.205) and is 50–500× faster in inference. The sole metric where SPAR3D trails is SSIM, where SF3D edges ahead by 0.003 on GSO (0.839 vs. 0.836) and 0.004 on OmniObject3D (0.836 vs. 0.832), which the authors attribute to Monte Carlo sampling noise in their PBR shader. Pure generative baselines (Shap-E, LN3Diff) are clearly outperformed on all metrics, confirming that the two-stage point-sampling + meshing design captures both the fidelity of regression and the detail of generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPAR3D positions itself as a bridge between two divergent lines of single-image 3D reconstruction: fast regression-based feedforward models (LRM, TripoSR, SF3D) that align well with visible surfaces but over-smooth occluded regions, and diffusion-based generative models (Shap-E, LN3Diff, MVDream) that handle uncertainty but are slow and misaligned with input observations. The key architectural departure is the two-stage decomposition using sparse point clouds as an intermediate representation: a lightweight point diffusion model (building on DDPM, Point-E's transformer denoiser, and PointInfinity's resolution-invariant design) handles probabilistic modeling at low resolution, while a triplane transformer (following SF3D and PointInfinity's two-stream design) performs high-fidelity regression meshing. The illumination estimation builds on RENI++'s learned HDR prior, and surface extraction uses DMTet, both inherited from the SF3D lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 0.7-second inference time while outperforming both regression and generative baselines across geometry and texture metrics makes SPAR3D a practically compelling option for real-time 3D asset generation pipelines (AR, filmmaking, manufacturing). The interactive editing capability enabled by the point cloud intermediate representation is a unique practical feature absent from prior single-image 3D methods. Open questions include whether the 512-point resolution can be increased for finer geometric detail, how the approach generalizes to multi-object scenes or complex transparent/emissive materials, and whether the two-stage uncertainty-offloading principle transfers to other ill-posed inverse problems beyond single-view reconstruction.
