## Problem

Novel-view acoustic synthesis (NVAS) for ambient sound remains under-explored: existing methods either require detailed knowledge of individual sound sources and their positions (RIR-based approaches), focus on only 1–2 primary sources, or rely on heuristic reference microphone placements that fail to adapt to diverse, complex scene layouts. No prior method can synthesize spatially accurate binaural ambient sound at arbitrary novel viewpoints from sparse references without constraints on the number, type, or location of sound sources.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251546/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse set of reference ambisonic recordings (captured by co-located microphone-camera rigs at strategically placed locations), panoramic RGB-D captures at both reference and target viewpoints, and the target's 6DoF pose (position + orientation). It outputs a binaural waveform at the target viewpoint. Notably, the number of references is variable (as few as 1–4 are sufficient due to adaptive weighting), and the method requires no knowledge of individual sound sources, their clean signals, or room impulse responses at inference time.

## Method

SoundVista introduces a Visual-Acoustic Binding (VAB) module—a ResNet-18 encoder pretrained to predict RT60 from panoramic RGB-D—that serves a dual role: (1) clustering candidate locations to optimize reference microphone placement by identifying acoustically distinct partitions, and (2) providing scene-adaptive embeddings for weighting reference contributions. A Reference Integration Transformer uses these VAB embeddings combined with relative position vectors to compute attention weights over a variable-length sequence of references, producing content-invariant conditions. A stacked U-Net spatial audio renderer then converts reweighted reference spectrograms, conditioned on decoupled global (position-dependent) and local (orientation-dependent) features, into the final binaural output. The renderer is pretrained on binauralization (fixed position, varied head rotations) before end-to-end training, and the full system is trained with a composite loss combining waveform MSE, interaural level difference, and multi-resolution spectrogram magnitude terms.

## Results

On the Soundspace-Ambient benchmark (39 seen scenes, 4 references), SoundVista achieves STFT 2.444, MAG 0.289, ENV 0.130, and LRE 1.390, outperforming the best deep-learning baseline ViGAS by 32.5% (STFT), 19.4% (MAG), 14.3% (ENV), and 31% (LRE). On the real-world N2S benchmark, SoundVista achieves STFT 1.073, MAG 0.177, ENV 0.113, and LRE 0.776, beating the best baseline ViGAS (STFT 1.201, LRE 0.873) by 10.6% on STFT and 11.1% on LRE. On unseen scenes, SoundVista maintains its lead with STFT 2.517 and LRE 1.371, while most deep-learning baselines degrade substantially (e.g., BEE jumps to STFT 5.635).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SoundVista outperforms all five deep-learning baselines across every metric on both benchmarks. Against ViGAS [6] (the strongest baseline), SoundVista reduces STFT error by 32.5% and LRE by 31% on the Soundspace-Ambient benchmark, and by 10.6% and 11.1% respectively on N2S. BEE [8] and Few-shotRIR [25] both use multiple references but rely on audio content for reference integration, which degrades when test content falls outside the training distribution; SoundVista's content-invariant VAB-based weighting avoids this failure mode. AV-NeRF [21] and VAM [5] use only a single reference and show the largest gaps (STFT >5.2 on Soundspace-Ambient), confirming that sparse single-reference approaches struggle with complex multi-source scenes. Notably, on the N2S benchmark (single static scene), deep-learning baselines perform closer to SoundVista, with ViGAS achieving STFT 1.201 vs. SoundVista's 1.073, but SoundVista still leads on binaural accuracy (LRE 0.776 vs. 0.873).

## Relation to Prior Work

SoundVista extends the Novel-View Acoustic Synthesis (NVAS) line of work (ViGAS, BEE, AV-NeRF, Few-shotRIR) by removing the requirement for explicit sound-source enumeration, instead treating ambient sound as a holistic signal to be transferred between viewpoints. Its key conceptual departure is the Visual-Acoustic Binding (VAB) module, which pretrains a ResNet-18 encoder on panoramic RGB-D captures to predict RT60, repurposing the CLIP-style cross-modal alignment paradigm from vision-language to vision-acoustics. The VAB embeddings then serve a dual role—guiding reference microphone placement via clustering and providing content-invariant conditioning for a transformer-based reference integration module—linking ideas from optimal sensor placement and attention-based sequence modeling to the audio synthesis pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SoundVista addresses a practical gap: visual novel-view synthesis is mature, but the acoustic counterpart lags, limiting immersive spatial media and virtual tour applications. The VAB module is a reusable component for inferring local acoustic properties from visual data alone, potentially applicable to RIR estimation, acoustic scene understanding, and sensor placement in other domains. Limitations include reliance on pre-acquired ambisonic recordings, indoor-focused evaluation (Matterport3D, a single real room), and the need for a trained visual encoder per scene type. Open questions include scalability to outdoor or very large environments, real-time inference, and whether the VAB pretraining strategy generalizes beyond RT60 to richer acoustic descriptors.
