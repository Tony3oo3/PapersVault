## Problem

While DiT-based video generators like Sora achieve high-quality long-form synthesis, the potential of transformer-based diffusion models for precise, controllable motion generation remains largely unexplored. Existing UNet-based motion control methods (DragNUWA, MotionCtrl, VideoComposer) are restricted to 16 frames at fixed low resolution, producing distortion and unnatural parallel drifting during significant positional shifts. The paper addresses the need for a scalable DiT framework that simultaneously integrates text, image, and trajectory conditions for robust motion-controllable video generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252575/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Tora accepts a text prompt, arbitrary object trajectories specified as per-frame (x, y) spatial positions (1 to 16 trajectories), and an optional reference image for image-to-video synthesis. It produces motion-controllable videos up to 204 frames at 720p resolution, supporting variable aspect ratios and durations (144p–720p, 51–204 frames). The trajectory input is notably more user-friendly than dense optical flow, requiring only sparse point positions that the model interpolates into full motion guidance.

## Method

Tora builds on OpenSora's Spatial-Temporal DiT and introduces two modules: a Trajectory Extractor (TE) and a Motion-guidance Fuser (MGF). The TE converts arbitrary trajectories into a 2-channel trajectory map, applies Gaussian filtering, renders it via flow visualization into RGB, then compresses it with a 3D VAE (based on MAGVIT-v2, spatially initialized from SDXL) achieving 8× spatial and 4× temporal reduction to produce motion latents in the same space as video patches; stacked lightweight convolutions with skip connections then extract hierarchical multi-level motion features. The MGF injects these features into each ST-DiT block via adaptive normalization (FiLM-style) layers that produce scale and shift parameters, which ablation shows outperforms cross-attention and channel concatenation. Training follows a two-stage strategy—first with dense optical flow, then fine-tuning with sparse user-friendly trajectories—using an adapter-like approach that freezes spatial blocks and trains only temporal blocks plus TE and MGF.

## Results

On the 128-frame setting, Tora achieves a Trajectory Error of 11.72, FVD of 494, and CLIPSIM of 0.2418, outperforming the best UNet-based method (MotionCtrl: TrajError 38.39, FVD 731) by roughly 3–5× in motion fidelity and ~30–40% in FVD. At 16 frames, Tora records a TrajError of 7.23 versus 9.42 for MotionCtrl and 286.43 for the uncontrolled OpenSora baseline. Tora also slightly surpasses the foundational OpenSora model in both FVD (494 vs. 533) and CLIPSIM (0.2418 vs. 0.2411) at 128 frames, demonstrating that motion conditioning does not degrade visual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Tora decisively outperforms all five UNet-based baselines (VideoComposer, DragNUWA, AnimateAnything, TrailBlazer, MotionCtrl) on every metric at every frame count, with the gap widening as duration increases: at 128 frames, Tora's TrajError of 11.72 is 3.3× lower than MotionCtrl (38.39) and 5× lower than VideoComposer (58.76). Against the uncontrolled OpenSora baseline, Tora reduces TrajError from 373.17 to 11.72 (a 32× improvement) while simultaneously improving FVD (494 vs. 533) and CLIPSIM (0.2418 vs. 0.2411), showing that trajectory conditioning enhances rather than degrades visual quality. Compared to the adapted OpenSora-based DragNUWA baseline, Tora wins on all three metrics at all frame counts (e.g., TrajError 11.72 vs. 21.75, FVD 494 vs. 565 at 128 frames), confirming that the proposed 3D-VAE trajectory encoding and adaptive-norm fusion are better aligned with DiT's latent space than DragNUWA's convolutional motion features. The paper notes that OpenSora-based DragNUWA actually degrades visual quality by ~5% below the OpenSora baseline due to architectural incompatibility, whereas Tora maintains or improves upon baseline visual fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

Tora extends the trajectory-controlled video generation line (DragNUWA, MotionCtrl, VideoComposer, TrailBlazer) from UNet-based architectures to the DiT paradigm, using OpenSora as its foundation. The trajectory compression module draws on MAGVIT-v2's 3D VAE design (simplified by removing codebook dependencies), while the motion fusion mechanism adapts FiLM-style adaptive normalization from GAN conditioning to the transformer block setting. The two-stage training strategy (dense optical flow → sparse object trajectories) is inherited from DragNUWA and MotionCtrl, but the key departure is aligning motion patches with DiT's patchified latent space rather than UNet's hierarchical feature maps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Tora demonstrates that trajectory-based motion control can scale with DiT's variable duration, resolution, and aspect ratio capabilities—something UNet-based methods fundamentally cannot achieve—making it a practical bridge for controllable video generation at production-relevant lengths (up to 204 frames, 720p). The finding that a simple adaptive normalization layer outperforms cross-attention and channel concatenation for motion injection provides a useful architectural guideline for future DiT conditioning work. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to complex multi-object interactions with occlusion, whether the two-stage training remains necessary at larger scales, and how well the 3D VAE compression handles highly non-rigid deformations beyond the trajectories tested.
