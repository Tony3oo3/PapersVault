## Problem

Existing zero-shot 3D Visual Grounding methods (e.g., ZSVG3D, LLM-Grounder) rely primarily on text-based scene descriptions fed to LLMs, neglecting rich visual cues such as color, texture, orientation, and state that are essential for disambiguating similar objects in complex scenes. Supervised 3DVG methods achieve higher accuracy but require costly 3D-specific annotations, limiting scalability to open-vocabulary and real-world settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252318/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D scene point cloud and a natural language query as input. The scene is rendered into a single 1000×1000 pixel 2D image from a dynamically selected query-aligned viewpoint (top 0.3 m excluded), and all objects are detected once per scene via open-vocabulary 3D detection, stored in an Object Lookup Table (OLT) with 3D bounding boxes and semantic labels. The output is a directed 3D bounding box (center, dimensions, orientation) retrieved from the OLT after the VLM selects the target object's ID.

## Method

SeeGround is a training-free framework that converts 3D scenes into a hybrid representation compatible with a pre-trained 2D-VLM (Qwen2-VL-72B): a query-aligned rendered image plus text-based 3D spatial descriptions. The Perspective Adaptation Module uses the VLM to identify an anchor object and candidate targets from the query, then positions a virtual camera at the scene center looking at the anchor before pulling back and upward to capture a broader, query-consistent view. The Fusion Alignment Module applies depth-aware visual prompting: 3D object points are projected onto the 2D image, occluded points are filtered via a depth map, and visual markers are placed at the projected centers of visible points to explicitly link each 2D region to its 3D description. The VLM then jointly processes the prompted image, spatial text, and query to output the target object's ID, which is looked up in the OLT to retrieve the final 3D bounding box.

## Results

On ScanRefer, SeeGround achieves 44.1% Acc@0.25 and 39.4% Acc@0.5 overall, with 75.7% Acc@0.25 on the Unique subset and 34.0% Acc@0.25 on the challenging Multiple subset. On Nr3D, it reaches 46.1% overall accuracy, surpassing the previous zero-shot SOTA (ZSVG3D) by 7.1 percentage points. These results exceed all prior zero-shot and weakly supervised methods while remaining competitive with fully supervised approaches such as MCLN (57.2% Acc@0.25) and ConcreteNet (50.6% Acc@0.25) on ScanRefer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SeeGround outperforms all prior zero-shot 3DVG methods by substantial margins: it exceeds ZSVG3D (the previous zero-shot SOTA) by 7.7% in Acc@0.25 on ScanRefer (44.1% vs. 36.4%) and by 7.1% on Nr3D (46.1% vs. 39.0%). It also surpasses the weakly supervised WS-3DVG (27.4% Acc@0.25) by 16.7 points, demonstrating that visual cues from rendered images provide a significant advantage over text-only approaches. Against fully supervised methods, SeeGround remains competitive but trails the best: MCLN achieves 57.2% Acc@0.25 and ConcreteNet 50.6%, while SeeGround reaches 44.1% without any 3D-specific training data. Notably, on the Multiple subset (where same-class distractors are present), SeeGround's 34.0% Acc@0.25 is close to EDA's 49.1% and BUTD-DETR's 46.6%, suggesting the Fusion Alignment Module effectively disambiguates similar objects. On Nr3D, SeeGround (46.1%) narrows the gap to fully supervised MiKASA (64.4%) and ViL3DRel (64.4%) to within 18 points, while maintaining a clear 7.1-point lead over the zero-shot baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

SeeGround carves new ground at the intersection of zero-shot 3D visual grounding and 2D vision-language models. Prior zero-shot approaches (LLM-Grounder, ZSVG3D) either rely solely on text-based spatial reasoning or project minimal visual cues (object centers) onto 2D images, neglecting rich visual details like color, texture, and orientation. This paper departs by rendering full query-aligned 2D images from the 3D scene and introducing depth-aware visual prompting to explicitly bind 2D visual features to 3D spatial descriptions, effectively repurposing off-the-shelf 2D VLMs (Qwen2-VL-72B) as 3D grounding agents without any 3D-specific training. The Perspective Adaptation Module and Fusion Alignment Module are novel architectural contributions that bridge the modality gap between 3D scene data and 2D VLM input formats.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving competitive zero-shot performance (rivaling some fully supervised methods) without 3D-specific training data, SeeGround addresses a key scalability bottleneck in 3DVG where annotated 3D datasets are expensive and limited in coverage. The robustness to incomplete textual descriptions (leveraging visual cues when anchor objects are missing) suggests practical value in real-world deployment where detection errors are common. However, spatial relationship reasoning remains a notable weakness (19% error rate), viewpoint selection struggles with complex spatial instructions, and point-cloud rendering limits visual fidelity—open questions that constrain the approach's ceiling. The framework's reliance on a large 72B VLM also raises questions about computational cost and deployability in resource-constrained settings.
