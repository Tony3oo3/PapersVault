## Problem

When brain signals are aligned with pretrained image features via contrastive learning, two unaddressed mismatches arise: a System GAP (irreversible loss of high-frequency details during human visual processing) and a Random GAP (stochastic variability from perceptual/cognitive dynamics and acquisition noise). With limited paired data, models overfit to bridging these gaps, resulting in poor zero-shot generalization to new subjects and stimuli.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250300/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes time-averaged EEG (64 channels, THINGS-EEG) or MEG (271 channels, THINGS-MEG) signals paired with natural images from the THINGS dataset, presented via a 100 ms RSVP paradigm. A lightweight brain encoder (EEGProject: two linear layers with residual connection and normalization) maps each trial to a shared latent space, while a frozen OpenCLIP vision encoder (default RN50) encodes candidate images. Output is a similarity ranking over 200 candidate images, evaluated by top-1/top-5 retrieval accuracy and mAP.

## Method

UBP introduces a foveated Gaussian blur prior on the image side to approximate the System GAP: a spatially varying blending factor α(i,j) = exp(−λ·d(i,j)/L) preserves detail near the fixation point while progressively blurring peripheral regions, with blur radius r controlling the degree of high-frequency suppression. For the Random GAP, UBP computes the diagonal of the brain–image similarity matrix, fits a Gaussian to the N paired similarity scores, and defines a (1−α) confidence interval; pairs falling outside this interval receive an increased blur radius (r₀ + c) to down-weight their contribution, while well-matched pairs receive reduced blur (r₀ − c). Training minimizes a symmetric cross-entropy contrastive loss with a learnable temperature, updating only the brain encoder parameters and the per-sample blur radius each iteration. This uncertainty-driven dynamic blurring prevents the model from overfitting to high-frequency details that brain signals cannot faithfully represent, yielding 50.9% top-1 and 79.7% top-5 zero-shot retrieval on THINGS-EEG.

## Results

On THINGS-EEG (intra-subject, 200-way zero-shot retrieval), UBP achieves 50.9% top-1 and 79.7% top-5 accuracy, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art VE-SDN (37.2% / 69.9%) by 13.7 and 9.8 percentage points respectively. On THINGS-MEG (intra-subject), UBP reaches 26.7% top-1 and 55.2% top-5, outperforming the best baseline NICE-GA (14.3% / 42.3%) by 12.4 and 12.9 points. In the more challenging inter-subject setting on THINGS-EEG, UBP attains 12.4% top-1 and 33.4% top-5, marginally exceeding ATM-S (11.8% / 33.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UBP consistently outperforms all four baselines on both datasets. On THINGS-EEG intra-subject, UBP (50.9% top-1) surpasses VE-SDN (37.2%) by 13.7 points, ATM-S (28.5%) by 22.4 points, and the NICE family (14.7–16.1%) by roughly 35 points. On THINGS-MEG intra-subject, UBP (26.7% top-1) leads NICE-GA (14.3%) by 12.4 points and NICE (12.8%) by 13.9 points. In the inter-subject setting on THINGS-EEG, UBP (12.4% top-1) narrowly edges out ATM-S (11.8%) by 0.6 points, while ATM-S holds a marginal 0.3-point advantage on top-5 (33.7% vs. 33.4%); both substantially outperform the NICE variants (5.9–7.0% top-1). BraVL, the earliest baseline, trails all other methods by a wide margin (5.8% top-1 intra-subject), confirming that the blur prior and uncertainty-aware mechanism provide the dominant performance gain over prior multimodal alignment strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the visual neural decoding literature that aligns brain signal encoders with frozen CLIP image embeddings via contrastive learning (NICE, VE-SDN, BraVL, ATM). Its key conceptual departure is explicitly identifying two sources of modality mismatch—the System GAP (irreversible loss of high-frequency detail due to the human visual system's spatial resolution limits) and the Random GAP (stochastic variability from perceptual/cognitive dynamics and acquisition noise)—and addressing them with a deliberately degraded image prior rather than a more complex brain encoder. The blur prior repurposes the well-known image degradation operation as a biological inductive bias, while the uncertainty-aware dynamic adjustment draws on confidence-interval-based outlier detection from the OOD and multimodal fusion literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple, architecture-agnostic image-side prior (foveated Gaussian blur with uncertainty-driven radius) can close a large portion of the vision-brain alignment gap, suggesting that prior work may have been overfitting to high-frequency details that brain signals simply cannot encode. This reframing could influence how other multimodal contrastive tasks handle imperfect modality correspondence. However, the blur prior is acknowledged as a simplified model of the visual system, and the uncertainty quantification relies on the assumption that similarity scores follow a Gaussian distribution—both of which may not hold in more complex or noisy settings. Open questions include whether learnable, spatially-varying degradation could outperform the fixed foveated blur, and whether the approach transfers to reconstruction tasks or other brain modalities (fMRI).
