## Problem

Existing deep-learning pansharpening methods are both sensor-dependent (requiring separate models per satellite) and scene-dependent (degrading on scenes underrepresented in training data, e.g., urban-trained models failing on river scenes). Prior general pansharpening approaches address only dataset-level variation but cannot capture the fine-grained scene and object diversity that exists even within a single satellite dataset.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251054/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-band PAN image and a multi-band LRMS image (with spatial ratio r) as primary inputs, and additionally consumes two text prompts generated by GeoChat: a scene-classification label (e.g., 'River', 'Buildings') and a free-form grounding description of spatial objects. The diffusion process operates on the residual map between the ground-truth HRMS and the upsampled LRMS, so the final output is reconstructed as X̂₀ + M. The model is jointly trained across WV-3, QB, and GF-2 datasets and evaluated at both reduced and full resolution, with zero-shot testing on the unseen WV-2 sensor.

## Method

SGDiff is a DDPM-based diffusion model whose denoising network is a 3D transformer (encoder-decoder with 3D Multi-Head Transposed Attention blocks) operating on the concatenated upsampled MS, PAN, and noisy residual inputs. The key architectural novelty is the Semantic-Guided Mixture of Expert (SG-MoE) block: GeoChat generates dual-granularity semantics (scene-level class and region-level grounding description), which are encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder, passed through an MLP and SoftMax to produce latent mixture weights, and then mapped via per-layer ReLU-gated linear projections into sparse channel-level gating scores. These scores selectively activate and scale individual convolutional channels (treated as experts) in a coarse-to-fine cascade—first scene-guided routing on input channels, then grounding-guided routing on the intermediate output—while a fixed 10% of channels remain permanently active as shared experts to preserve baseline capacity. Training uses an L1 reconstruction loss on the residual map plus an L1 sparse-regularization term on the gating scores to enforce expert diversity, with the gating parameters frozen after the first 50k iterations to stabilize routing.

## Results

On WorldView-3 (WV-3), SGDiff achieves Q8=0.9227, SAM=2.7708, ERGAS=2.0442, SCC=0.9878 at reduced resolution, and Dλ=0.0123, Ds=0.0271, HQNR=0.9609 at full resolution, outperforming all baselines on every metric. On QuickBird (QB), SGDiff reaches HQNR=0.9341 at full resolution, an improvement of nearly 3% over TMDiff (0.9075) and BIMPAN (0.9013). In the zero-shot generalization test on the unseen WorldView-2 (WV-2) dataset, SGDiff attains Q8=0.8577, SAM=4.9679, ERGAS=3.8683, and SCC=0.9345, surpassing all compared methods including TMDiff (Q8=0.8310) and BDSD-PC (Q8=0.8446).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGDiff outperforms all 10 baselines on every metric across both WV-3 and QB datasets, with the closest competitor being TMDiff [50], a text-modulated 3D diffusion model from the same group. On QB full resolution, SGDiff improves HQNR from 0.9075 (TMDiff) to 0.9341 (+2.9%) and Ds from 0.0627 to 0.0434 (−30.8%), while on WV-3 full resolution it reduces Ds from 0.0319 (TMDiff) to 0.0271 (−15.0%). Traditional methods C-GSA [34] and MTF [2] are substantially outperformed, with SGDiff achieving roughly 11–12% higher Q8 and 50%+ lower SAM. Among deep learning baselines, U2Net [31] and CrossDiff [51] show moderate performance but lag SGDiff by 0.3–1.2% in Q8 and 0.01–0.02 in Dλ. In the zero-shot WV-2 experiment, SGDiff leads all methods including TMDiff (Q8: 0.8577 vs. 0.8310) and the strongest traditional baseline BDSD-PC [39] (Q8: 0.8577 vs. 0.8446), confirming its advantage in unseen-sensor generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends TMDiff [50], which introduced text-prompted 3D diffusion for general pansharpening across satellite sensors, by replacing its dataset-level textual conditioning with dual-granularity semantic guidance (scene category + grounding description) extracted from the geoscience VLM GeoChat. The denoising network architecture draws on Restormer's 3D multi-head transposed attention and the channel-level sparse MoE design of DeepMoE [48], but introduces a novel SG-MoE block where CLIP-encoded VLM outputs drive per-layer, per-channel gating scores rather than token-level routing. Unlike prior general pansharpening methods (Wang et al. [46], Sheng et al. [35]) that address sensor dependency through shared architectures or satellite-specific branches, SGDiff targets both sensor and scene dependency simultaneously through adaptive expert activation conditioned on semantic priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot generalization to the unseen WorldView-2 sensor (outperforming TMDiff by ~2.7% in Q8 and ~5% in ERGAS) is practically significant for operational remote sensing pipelines where retraining per sensor is infeasible. However, the in-distribution improvements over TMDiff are modest (e.g., Q8: 0.9227 vs. 0.9210 on WV-3), and the method's reliance on GeoChat's scene classification (limited to 7 predefined classes) and grounding quality introduces a potential bottleneck for novel or mixed scenes. Open questions include whether the VLM-guided routing scales to finer scene taxonomies, whether the frozen VLM can be replaced by lighter alternatives without performance loss, and whether the sparse MoE design transfers to other diffusion-based remote sensing tasks beyond pansharpening.
