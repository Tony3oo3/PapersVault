## Problem

Performing fine-grained image edits—such as adding a specific object at a precise location, removing background elements, or recoloring hair—remains difficult because existing tools lack intuitive interfaces for simultaneously controlling shape and color at the regional level. Additionally, the need to manually type text prompts at every editing step disrupts the creative workflow, creating a cumbersome transition between prompt input and visual manipulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252086/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes an RGB image and user-drawn brush strokes as input: scribble strokes (add/subtract) are converted into a modified edge map via a pre-trained CNN (PiDiNet), while color strokes are represented as (mask, color, opacity) tuples and converted into a low-resolution color block by downscaling 16× with cubic interpolation then upscaling with nearest-neighbor. The editing region is the dilated union of all brush masks. The MLLM auto-generates a concise single-word or short-phrase prompt from the stroke context, eliminating manual text entry. The output is a fully inpainted edited image with precise edge alignment and color fidelity in the SDXL latent space.

## Method

The Editing Processor extends a frozen latent diffusion UNet with two plug-and-play branches: an inpainting branch (a trainable UNet clone stripped of cross-attention layers that processes the masked image latent and downsampled mask, injecting pixel-level features via zero-convolution at every layer) and a ControlNet-based control branch (inserting edge and color condition features into middle and decoder blocks). Both branches are trained with denoising score matching while keeping the base diffusion weights frozen, enabling plug-and-play compatibility with any community fine-tuned model. The Painting Assistor fine-tunes LLaVA via LoRA on a 24,315-image dataset constructed from DCI by simulating hand-drawn strokes: edge maps from PiDiNet are overlaid onto BrushNet inpainted regions to create realistic sketch-like inputs paired with ground-truth object labels. This Draw&Guess task allows the MLLM to interpret abstract scribble strokes and predict editing prompts in real time, enabling a continuous brush-and-edit workflow without manual prompt entry.

## Results

On the 490-image test set, MagicQuill's Editing Processor achieves LPIPS 0.0667, PSNR 27.282, and SSIM 0.902, outperforming all baselines on every metric. The closest competitor, BrushNet+ControlNet, scores LPIPS 0.0748, PSNR 25.770, SSIM 0.894, while the weakest baseline SmartEdit lags far behind at LPIPS 0.339 and PSNR 16.695. For the Painting Assistor (Draw&Guess task), the fine-tuned LLaVA achieves GPT-4 similarity 2.712, BERT similarity 0.749, and CLIP similarity 0.824, surpassing GPT-4o (2.712 vs 1.976 GPT-4 sim) and LLaVA-Next (1.941) by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MagicQuill's Editing Processor wins on all three metrics (LPIPS, PSNR, SSIM) against every baseline. The largest gap is against SmartEdit (instruction-based), which produces overly random outputs with LPIPS 0.339 vs. 0.0667, confirming that text-only guidance cannot achieve precise regional control. Against the strongest structured baseline BrushNet+ControlNet, MagicQuill improves PSNR by 1.51 dB (27.282 vs. 25.770) and reduces LPIPS by ~11% (0.0667 vs. 0.0748), attributed to the dedicated dual-branch design that simultaneously enforces edge and color conditions. For the Draw&Guess task, the fine-tuned LLaVA outperforms GPT-4o by 0.736 in GPT-4 similarity (2.712 vs. 1.976) and by 0.065 in CLIP similarity (0.824 vs. 0.790), demonstrating that domain-specific fine-tuning with simulated brushstroke data substantially improves intent prediction over general-purpose MLLMs. Qualitative comparisons with SDEdit and UniPaint (Fig. 7) further confirm superior edge and color adherence, though no quantitative numbers are reported for these two stroke-based methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

MagicQuill synthesizes two established lines of work—brushstroke-conditioned diffusion editing (ControlNet, BrushNet) and MLLM-based prompt understanding (LLaVA)—into a unified interactive system. It departs from instruction-based editors like SmartEdit and sketch-based methods like SketchEdit by simultaneously providing structural (edge) and color control through a dual-branch architecture, and from prior interactive tools (PromptCharm, DesignPrompt) by replacing manual prompt entry with an MLLM that interprets hand-drawn strokes in context (the Draw&Guess task). The two-branch plug-in design is directly inspired by BrushNet's decomposed inpainting approach, extended here with an additional color-conditioning pathway and a dedicated ControlNet branch for edge guidance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The system addresses a practical gap: existing diffusion-based editors require users to type precise prompts at every editing step, breaking the creative flow. By automating prompt generation from brushstrokes and providing simultaneous shape+color control, MagicQuill enables a continuous, low-friction editing loop validated by user studies showing ~20–25% time savings. Open questions remain around reference-based editing, layered generation for complex compositions, and typography manipulation, all acknowledged as future work. The modest quantitative margins over BrushNet+ControlNet (e.g., LPIPS 0.0667 vs. 0.0748) suggest the gains are real but incremental, and the evaluation on a single 490-image test set limits generalizability claims.
