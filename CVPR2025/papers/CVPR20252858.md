## Problem

Large reconstruction and generation models are confined to small spatial regions per inference due to compute limits, making long-range consistent scene generation difficult. Existing temporal autoregression methods only maintain short-range temporal consistency, and errors accumulate over successive clips, causing spatial inconsistencies when revisiting the same area. No prior framework effectively combines spatial and temporal conditioning to support scalable, pose-controlled generation over extended trajectories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252858/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two spatially adjacent posed RGB images (augmented with monocular depth from Depth Anything V2 and Plücker coordinates derived from camera poses), one temporally overlapping image from the previously generated clip, a text prompt, and a sequence of novel view camera poses. It outputs a video clip of N novel view images at 256×256 resolution, with the full scene represented as a growing set of sparsely sampled posed images. For layout-conditioned city generation, rendered depth and semantic maps from OpenStreetMap layouts serve as additional ControlNet inputs.

## Method

StarGen conditions each autoregressive video clip on both spatial and temporal information: a Large Reconstruction Model (ViT, 114M params) regresses scale-aligned depth and latent feature maps from two spatial conditioning images, which are then rendered to novel views and temporally compressed via a small causal convolution to match the video diffusion model's latent space. The temporally overlapping image is encoded by the pretrained VAE and directly replaces the corresponding latent frame, enabling flexible conditioning at any position without backbone retraining. A ControlNet (6 trainable layers) injects this spatiotemporal condition into the frozen CogVideoX-2B backbone. The key design choice is representing the global scene as sparsely sampled generated images rather than a reconstructed point cloud, which mitigates error accumulation across clips. Training proceeds in three stages (LRM+CCN pretraining, interval expansion, joint ControlNet training) with a combined loss of scale-free depth supervision, latent reconstruction, and standard diffusion denoising.

## Results

On sparse view interpolation (Table 1), StarGen achieves PSNR 28.49 / SSIM 0.894 / LPIPS 0.088 on RealEstate-10K and PSNR 29.69 / SSIM 0.876 / LPIPS 0.116 on ACID, outperforming the closest competitor ReconX (28.31 / 0.912 / 0.088 and 28.84 / 0.891 / 0.101) in PSNR and LPIPS while matching on SSIM. On perpetual view generation (Table 2), StarGen reaches PSNR 23.52 / SSIM 0.792 / LPIPS 0.162 on RealEstate-10K and PSNR 19.52 / SSIM 0.552 / LPIPS 0.332 on the unseen Tanks-and-Temples dataset, surpassing ViewCrafter (22.60 / 0.754 / 0.195 and 17.25 / 0.489 / 0.341) across all metrics. For long-range scalability, StarGen's FID degrades far more slowly than ViewCrafter as video length increases, and its rotation/translation pose errors remain substantially lower.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against pure reconstruction baselines (pixelNeRF, GPNR, AttnRend, MuRF, PixelSplat, MVSplat, GS-LRM, DepthSplat), StarGen achieves the best PSNR on both datasets (28.49 vs. 28.10 for GS-LRM on RE-10K; 29.69 vs. 28.25 for MVSplat on ACID) and the lowest LPIPS, while GS-LRM edges out StarGen slightly on SSIM (0.892 vs. 0.894 is actually StarGen winning; ReconX wins SSIM at 0.912). Against the two closest concurrent methods that also combine reconstruction with video diffusion, StarGen beats ReconX in PSNR (+0.18 on RE-10K, +0.85 on ACID) and LPIPS (0.088 vs. 0.088 tied on RE-10K, 0.116 vs. 0.101 ReconX slightly better on ACID), while ReconX retains a small SSIM advantage (0.912 vs. 0.894). StarGen substantially outperforms ViewCrafter across all metrics on both datasets (e.g., +4.26 PSNR on RE-10K, +6.21 PSNR on ACID), with the gap widening on ACID where ViewCrafter's point-cloud-based conditioning degrades more severely. On perpetual view generation (Table 2), StarGen leads ViewCrafter by +0.92 PSNR on RE-10K and +2.27 PSNR on the unseen Tanks-and-Temples dataset, and significantly outperforms InfiniteNature-Zero, LucidDreamer, and MotionCtrl. For layout-conditioned city generation, StarGen matches CityDreamer in layout adherence but produces noticeably higher visual fidelity (qualitative comparison in Fig. 6).

## Relation to Prior Work

StarGen sits at the intersection of large reconstruction models and video diffusion, building directly on CogVideoX as the video diffusion backbone, ControlNet for conditional generation, and GS-LRM's transformer-based tokenization for the reconstruction component. It departs most significantly from the closest concurrent works ReconX and ViewCrafter, which both use 3D point clouds as the global representation across autoregressive clips; StarGen instead maintains a set of sparsely sampled generated images with poses and depth, avoiding the error accumulation inherent in iterative point cloud reconstruction. The key architectural departure is the dual spatiotemporal conditioning: spatially adjacent images are processed through an LRM (augmented with depth and Plücker coordinates beyond prior RGB-only inputs) to render 3D-warped latent features, while the temporally overlapping image is injected directly into the latent sequence, a combination not present in prior autoregressive video generation methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in scene generation: compute-limited video diffusion models can only process short clips, and existing autoregressive approaches degrade rapidly over long trajectories due to error accumulation in 3D representations. By using sparsely sampled images as the global state rather than point clouds, StarGen demonstrates substantially better scalability (FID and pose accuracy remain stable over 100+ frames where ViewCrafter degrades sharply), making long-range consistent scene generation more tractable. Open questions remain around handling large loops without absolute constraints, integrating the generated video into a coherent 3D representation, and whether the approach generalizes to dynamic or outdoor scenes with large parallax. The framework's versatility across three downstream tasks (sparse interpolation, perpetual views, layout-conditioned cities) suggests it could become a useful template for future scene generation systems.
