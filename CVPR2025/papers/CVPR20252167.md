## Problem

Deep saliency models accurately predict where humans look but operate as opaque black boxes, limiting their use in safety-critical and diagnostic applications. Existing explainable approaches either aggregate thousands of semantic prototypes with soft attention weights or generate superficial fixation descriptions, neither of which captures the reasoning steps or contextual prioritization that drive human attention decisions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252167/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and, in task-driven mode, a natural-language question guiding attention (e.g., 'Is there a sheep to the right of the fence?'); in task-free mode it processes the image alone. It produces a pixel-level saliency map, a set of natural-language explanations tied to specific semantic proposals, and the top-K weighted semantic prototypes (spatial feature maps) that compose the final prediction. The VLM outputs are structured as a list of (proposal, rationale) pairs, which are then grounded into spatial maps via open-vocabulary segmentation.

## Method

XSal integrates MiniCPM-V 2.6 as an explicit reasoning engine: a two-stage prompting scheme first elicits a chain of reasoning steps, then extracts concrete semantic proposals with per-proposal justifications. Each proposal is grounded into a fine-grained spatial map (semantic prototype) by using ClipSeg segmentation masks as binary filters over a dilated ResNet-50 feature map, followed by a convolutional layer for localization. A Contextual Prioritization Mechanism then applies global average pooling and a linear layer to score all prototypes, retains only the top-K via hard selection, and normalizes their weights with softmax before a single linear saliency head (bias=0) produces the final map. To prevent under-training from early aggressive selection, a Progressive Top-K Reduction schedule linearly decreases K from 20 to 3 over the first three of ten training epochs. Training is supervised with a combined NSS + CC + KL-divergence loss against ground-truth saliency maps.

## Results

On the AiR (task-driven) dataset, XSal achieves the best NSS of 1.94 and CC of 0.70, outperforming the next-best GazeXplain (NSS 1.85, CC 0.66) by a clear margin. On the OSIE (task-free) dataset, XSal achieves the highest NSS of 2.96, surpassing Chen et al. (2.91) and DINet (2.88), though its CC of 0.72 is slightly below GazeXplain's 0.74. On explainability metrics, XSal substantially outperforms Chen et al. on both datasets, e.g., AUC-E drops from 0.899 to 0.713 and AOPC rises from 0.241 to 0.748 on AiR, indicating far greater sensitivity to semantic prototype perturbations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

XSal outperforms all five baselines on AiR NSS (1.94 vs. 1.85 for GazeXplain, the closest competitor) and AiR CC (0.70 vs. 0.66), confirming its advantage in task-driven attention prediction. On OSIE, XSal achieves the best NSS (2.96) over Chen et al. (2.91) and DINet (2.88), but trails GazeXplain on CC (0.72 vs. 0.74), which the authors attribute to the contextual prioritization mechanism discarding low-saliency regions that contribute to distributional agreement. GazeXplain, while competitive on AiR, drops sharply on OSIE NSS (2.53), suggesting its scanpath-based approach struggles in free-viewing settings with multiple competing objects. SAM and SALICON, representing earlier architectures, are consistently outperformed by 0.15–0.29 NSS across both datasets. On explainability (Table 2), XSal dominates Chen et al. by large margins: AUC-E improves from 0.899→0.713 (AiR) and 0.810→0.685 (OSIE), while AOPC more than doubles (0.241→0.748 on AiR; 0.586→1.115 on OSIE), demonstrating that XSal's predictions are far more tightly coupled to its articulated semantic reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the explainable saliency line initiated by Chen et al. (NeurIPS 2023), which used soft attention over thousands of semantic prototypes, and GazeXplain (ECCV 2024), which generated plain-language fixation descriptions without reasoning. The key departure is replacing soft-attention aggregation with a hard top-K contextual prioritization mechanism and introducing a VLM (MiniCPM-V) to perform explicit step-by-step reasoning that produces both semantic proposals and natural-language justifications. The visual grounding step repurposes ClipSeg's open-vocabulary segmentation to convert VLM-generated proposals into spatial prototypes, bridging language-based reasoning with pixel-level saliency prediction in a way neither prior explainable-saliency method attempted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a small number of explicitly reasoned, contextually prioritized semantic prototypes can match or exceed the saliency accuracy of dense soft-attention models while offering far more interpretable explanations, suggesting that interpretability need not come at the cost of accuracy. However, the evaluation is limited to two eye-tracking datasets and a single explainability baseline (Chen et al.), and the CC metric on OSIE is actually lower than GazeXplain, hinting that the hard-selection mechanism may sacrifice coverage of low-saliency regions. Open questions include whether the VLM's reasoning is genuinely causal for the prediction or a post-hoc rationalization, how the approach scales to video or very high-resolution scenes, and whether the computational overhead of VLM inference is acceptable for real-time applications.
