## Problem

Existing VideoQG models rely on spurious cross-modal correlations (linguistic and visual biases) rather than genuine causal visual evidence, producing correct but unfaithful answers grounded on irrelevant video segments. Prior causal methods for video QA split and recombine segments to enforce invariance but cannot extract fine-grained causal semantics or provide interpretable intervention mechanisms that explicitly link the grounded temporal interval to the answer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250010/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a full video (32 evenly sampled frames encoded by a frozen CLIP-L) and a question with a candidate answer set (encoded by a finetuned RoBERTa) as input, and jointly outputs the predicted answer class and a temporal interval [start, end] identifying the video segment that serves as visual rationale. Notably, no explicit grounding annotations are required during training; the temporal interval is estimated purely from cross-modal attention weights refined by an adaptive Gaussian filter, operating in a weakly-supervised regime where only VideoQA labels provide the supervisory signal.

## Method

CRA comprises three modules: (1) Gaussian Smoothing Grounding (GSG) computes cross-modal attention between the deconfounded language feature and video features, applies a learnable adaptive Gaussian filter to denoise temporal attention, and thresholds around the peak to extract the time interval; (2) Cross-Modal Alignment (CMA) uses bidirectional InfoNCE contrastive loss to align grounded video segment features with QA features, providing weak supervision for grounding without explicit labels; (3) Explicit Causal Intervention (ECI) implements front-door intervention on the visual modality by treating the estimated video segment as a mediator M in the causal chain V→M→a, combined with back-door intervention on the language modality using semantic structure graphs (subject/verb/object parsed by Stanza) as observable confounders. The front-door probability is estimated via Normalized Weighted Geometric Mean over 512 CLIP frame-feature cluster centers, and the total training objective is L_CE + L_Align. The key distinction from prior causal VideoQA work is that the grounded video segment is explicitly incorporated as a mediator in the causal graph, making the intervention interpretable and its effectiveness directly quantifiable through grounding accuracy.

## Results

On NextGQA (test set), CRA with the Temp[CLIP] backbone achieves 18.2% Acc@GQA, 61.1% Acc@QA, 28.5% TIoP@0.5, and 10.6% TIoU@0.5, outperforming the strongest baseline TimeCraft (18.2% Acc@GQA, 9.6% TIoU@0.5) and Temp[CLIP](NG+) (16.0% Acc@GQA, 8.9% TIoU@0.5). With the larger FrozenBiLM backbone, CRA reaches 18.8% Acc@GQA and 9.6% TIoU@0.5, surpassing TimeCraft (18.5% Acc@GQA, 8.4% TIoU@0.5). On the STAR validation set, CRA achieves 26.8% Acc@GQA (Temp[CLIP]) and 27.5% Acc@GQA (FrozenBiLM), both leading all baselines by 2.4–4.7 points over the next-best method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRA outperforms all seven baseline families on the primary Acc@GQA metric. Against the closest competitor TimeCraft [15], CRA ties on Acc@GQA with Temp[CLIP] (18.2 vs 18.2) but wins on TIoU@0.5 (10.6 vs 9.6) and TIoP@0.5 (28.5 vs 27.8); with FrozenBiLM, CRA leads by 0.3% Acc@GQA (18.8 vs 18.5) and 1.2% TIoU@0.5 (9.6 vs 8.4). Compared to Temp[CLIP](NG+) [37], CRA gains +2.2% Acc@GQA and +1.7% TIoU@0.5, while FrozenBiLM(NG+) [40] is surpassed by +1.3% Acc@GQA and +3.5% TIoU@0.5. The older VideoQA baselines IGV [13] (10.2%), VIOLETv2 [7] (12.8%), and VGT [36] (14.4%) trail CRA by 4–8 points in Acc@GQA, and SeViLA [41] (16.6%) is outperformed by 1.6 points. Notably, CRA's advantage is most pronounced on the stricter TIoU@0.5 metric, where it leads all baselines, confirming that causal intervention improves the precision of temporal grounding beyond what contrastive alignment alone achieves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the weakly supervised VideoQG formulation introduced by Xiao et al. (Temp[CLIP], CVPR 2024), which established the task of jointly answering questions and grounding the supporting video segment without explicit temporal annotations. It departs from prior causal VQA methods (IGV, EIGV) that enforce invariance by splitting and recombining video segments, instead treating the estimated grounded segment as an explicit mediator in a front-door intervention, making the causal mechanism interpretable and its effect quantifiable through grounding accuracy. The back-door intervention for language deconfounding via syntactic semantic graphs (subject-verb-object) is a novel application of causal structure to the linguistic confounders identified in the data, complementing the visual front-door path. Unlike TimeCraft, which relies on LLM-generated pseudo-labels for cross-modal alignment, CRA achieves alignment purely through bidirectional InfoNCE contrastive learning on existing VideoQA data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a real faithfulness gap in VideoQA—models can produce correct answers grounded in irrelevant visual content—and provides a principled causal framework to close it without requiring expensive grounding annotations. However, the practical gains are modest (1–2% over strong baselines like TimeCraft), and the ablation reveals that the causal intervention modules contribute less (≈1.2% combined) than the contrastive alignment component (≈2%), suggesting the causal framing, while conceptually clean, is not the primary driver of performance. Open questions include whether the front-door intervention generalizes to longer videos or more complex multi-event scenes, and whether integrating LLM-generated causal samples (as the authors suggest) could amplify the intervention's effect. The small segment-to-video ratio (~0.1) and the tendency of all methods to under-predict interval length remain unresolved challenges.
