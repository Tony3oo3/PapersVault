## Problem

Existing 3D style transfer methods for NeRF and 3DGS are limited to medium resolution and cannot faithfully transfer multiscale painting textures such as fine brushstrokes and canvas grain. Fast approaches (e.g., AdaIN-based) sacrifice visual quality, while optimization-based methods relying on nearest-neighbor feature matching scale poorly (O(N²)) and cannot handle ultra-high-resolution (UHR, >4K) content or style images. No prior method achieves high-quality 3DGS style transfer at UHR.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained 3DGS scene (with all Gaussian parameters: positions, covariances, opacities, and spherical harmonic color coefficients) and a single style image, which may itself be UHR (e.g., 4230×3361) or medium resolution. The content views are also UHR (e.g., 5187×3361). The output is a stylized 3DGS scene in which only the constant color component c_{j,0} of each Gaussian is modified, preserving all geometry, opacity, and view-dependent color variation. The stylized scene is renderable from any camera position at the original UHR resolution.

## Method

SGSST freezes all 3DGS parameters except the constant color components and optimizes them against a new Simultaneously Optimized Scales (SOS) loss, defined as the average of the VGG19-based transfer loss (Gram matrices, spatial means, and standard deviations across five layers) evaluated at multiple downscaled resolutions (powers of 2). This single parameterless multiscale loss replaces the coarse-to-fine strategy that fails for 3DGS due to its sparse representation. For memory-efficient UHR gradient computation, the method leverages SPST's partition-based backpropagation and stacks multiscale gradients at the rendered image level, requiring only one backpropagation through the 3DGS rendering pipeline per iteration. Training proceeds in two phases: 10k Adam iterations at the coarsest scale for rapid color palette transfer, followed by 10k iterations on the full SOS loss. The approach is fully parameterless and reproducible, with the number of scales set automatically based on available resolution.

## Results

Across 40 style transfer experiments on 9 scenes, SGSST achieves the best Gram loss (2.59e8) for style transfer quality, outperforming StyleGaussian (4.61e8) and ARF (5.77e8). In a perceptual study with 68 participants (680 votes), SGSST is ranked as the most style-consistent result 66.3% of the time, compared to 31.6% for ARF and 2.1% for StyleGaussian. On short-range consistency (LPIPS), SGSST scores 0.030, slightly better than StyleGaussian (0.033) and ARF (0.040), though StyleGaussian achieves marginally lower RMSE values (0.029 short-range, 0.056 long-range) — a metric the authors note favors low-texture outputs. SGSST is the only method that performs style transfer at ultra-high resolution (5187×3361), with a computation overhead of 2.2×–8.5× over initial 3DGS training depending on resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against StyleGaussian [26], SGSST wins decisively on style transfer quality (Gram loss 2.59e8 vs. 4.61e8) and in the perceptual study (66.3% vs. 2.1% votes), but StyleGaussian achieves slightly lower RMSE on both short-range (0.029 vs. 0.032) and long-range (0.056 vs. 0.063) consistency — a gap the authors attribute to StyleGaussian's lower texture fidelity (it downscales style images to 256×256). Against ARF [48], SGSST wins on Gram loss (2.59e8 vs. 5.77e8), short-range LPIPS (0.030 vs. 0.040), long-range LPIPS (0.055 vs. 0.072), and long-range RMSE (0.063 vs. 0.066), as well as overwhelmingly in the perceptual study (66.3% vs. 31.6%). ARF uses only a single VGG layer at a single scale with nearest-neighbor feature matching, limiting its ability to transfer global color palettes and multi-scale brushstroke patterns. SGSST is also the only method that preserves the original ultra-high resolution of the content scene, whereas StyleGaussian outputs at 1600×1066 and ARF at 779×519 for the HR counter scene.

## Relation to Prior Work

SGSST extends the 2D ultra-high-resolution style transfer method SPST (Galerne et al., 2024) to the 3D domain by plugging its partition-based VGG19 gradient computation into the differentiable 3DGS rendering pipeline. It departs from prior 3DGS style transfer methods (StyleGaussian, StylizedGS, G-Style) that either rely on fast but approximate AdaIN-based color mapping or are resolution-limited by nearest-neighbor feature matching (NNFM). The key conceptual departure is the SOS loss, which optimizes style transfer simultaneously across multiple scales in a single parameterless formula, replacing the coarse-to-fine strategy standard in 2D HR style transfer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SGSST is the first method to perform style transfer on 3DGS scenes at ultra-high resolution (5187×3361), which is critical for AR/VR applications where users navigate 3D environments and expect fine-grained painting details at close range. The main limitation is computational cost: style transfer takes 2–8× longer than the initial 3DGS training (up to 25.5 hours for UHR scenes), making it impractical for interactive use. Open directions include a faster UST-based variant and geometry-aware style transfer, both of which the authors identify as future work.
