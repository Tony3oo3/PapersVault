## Problem

Existing large vision models adapted from NLP architectures rely on autoregressive token-by-token prediction, which is computationally expensive for high-dimensional visual data and disrupts spatial dependencies when converting images or videos into 1D sequences. This paper asks whether a diffusion-based generative framework can unify 20+ vision tasks while preserving spatial-temporal coherence and achieving superior efficiency compared to autoregressive LVMs.

## Input / Output

The model receives an image or video query (256×256 or 512×512) concatenated with a variable-length set of input-target pairs that define the task (e.g., 8 pairs by default). All visual inputs are first compressed by the ST-VAE into a continuous latent space with 4×8×8 spatial-temporal downsampling, then patchified at 2×2 for the transformer. The output is a denoised latent representation decoded back to raw pixel space, yielding task-specific predictions such as segmentation masks, depth maps, surface normals, optical flow, or generated frames — all produced in a single forward pass without task-specific heads or fine-tuning.

## Method

LaVin-DiT combines three components: (1) a spatial-temporal VAE with causal 3D convolutions and KL regularization that compresses images and videos into a shared continuous latent space; (2) a Joint Diffusion Transformer (J-DiT) that processes condition (clean) and target (noisy) latents jointly via full-sequence joint attention, using separate patch embeddings, condition- and target-specific AdaRN, grouped-query attention, QK-Norm, sandwich normalization, and 3D Rotary Position Encoding to preserve spatial-temporal structure; and (3) an in-context learning scheme where input-target pairs serve as task definitions, guiding the diffusion process without fine-tuning. Training uses conditional flow matching (CFM) in latent space with a linear interpolation forward process, optimized in two stages (256×256 for 100K steps, then 512×512 for 20K steps). Inference integrates the learned velocity field backward over N=20 Euler steps from pure noise to a clean latent, which is then decoded by the ST-VAE to produce the final prediction.

## Results

On foreground segmentation (unseen task), LaVin-DiT (3.4B) achieves 69.4% average mIoU across four splits, substantially outperforming LVM (49.7%) and MAE-VQGAN (27.2%). On single object detection (also unseen), LaVin-DiT reaches 68.7% average mIoU versus LVM's 49.2%. For NYU-v2 depth estimation, LaVin-DiT attains AbsRel 6.2 and δ₁ 96.1%, matching the expert model Marigold (AbsRel 6.0) and far surpassing LVM† (AbsRel 30.2). On surface normal estimation, LaVin-DiT achieves MAE 15.901 and <11.25° accuracy 58.4%, outperforming the dedicated StableNormal model (MAE 19.707).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest LVM baseline (Bai et al., CVPR 2024), LaVin-DiT wins decisively on every reported task: foreground segmentation mIoU improves from ~49.7% to ~69.4%, single object detection from ~49.2% to ~68.7%, colorization MSE drops from 0.51 to 0.24, depth estimation AbsRel drops from 30.2 to 6.2, and inpainting FID drops from 4.05 to 1.65. Compared to the visual prompting baseline MAE/MAE-VQGAN (Bar et al., NeurIPS 2022), LaVin-DiT achieves roughly 2–13× higher mIoU on segmentation and detection tasks and halves the colorization error. Against the expert depth model DPT (Ranftl et al., ICCV 2021), LaVin-DiT is slightly worse on AbsRel (6.2 vs. 9.8) but substantially better on δ₁ (96.1% vs. 90.3%). Versus the dedicated normal-estimation model StableNormal (Ye et al., CVPR 2024), LaVin-DiT reduces MAE from 19.707 to 15.901 and improves angle accuracy from 53.0% to 58.4%. Against Marigold (Ke et al., CVPR 2024) for depth estimation, LaVin-DiT is marginally worse on AbsRel (6.2 vs. 6.0) but slightly better on δ₁ (96.1% vs. 95.9%), confirming competitive performance with task-specific experts while operating as a unified model.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaVin-DiT builds directly on the DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, ICCV 2023) and the MM-DiT dual-stream design from Stable Diffusion 3 (Esser et al., ICML 2024), adapting them for task-conditioned vision generation via a Joint Diffusion Transformer with separate condition/target patch embeddings and AdaRN. It adopts flow matching (Lipman et al., ICLR 2023) as the training objective in a latent space produced by a spatial-temporal VAE. Conceptually, the paper departs from the autoregressive sequential modeling paradigm of LVM (Bai et al., CVPR 2024), arguing that token-by-token prediction disrupts spatial coherence and scales poorly with resolution, and instead leverages parallel denoising to preserve the 2D/3D structure inherent in visual data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LaVin-DiT demonstrates that diffusion transformers can serve as a unified backbone for 20+ dense and sparse vision tasks (segmentation, depth, normals, optical flow, inpainting, video prediction) with 1.7–2.3× faster inference than the prior autoregressive LVM, making it a practical candidate for generalist vision systems. The consistent scalability from 0.1B to 3.4B parameters and the monotonic benefit of longer task contexts suggest a clear path toward more capable models. However, the authors acknowledge that generalization degrades when task definitions deviate significantly from the training distribution, leaving open the question of whether purely in-context task specification can handle truly novel vision tasks without any fine-tuning.
