## Problem

Standard GCD methods built on DINO-pretrained ViTs extract features from a global receptive field, producing scattered attention that fails to capture the subtle local distinctions needed to separate visually similar fine-grained categories. This deficiency means the model neither focuses enough on discriminative local details nor leverages global context to interpret what those details mean, degrading both novel and known class accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251774/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a labeled image set (known classes) and an unlabeled image set (containing both known and unknown classes) as RGB images processed by a DINO-pretrained ViT-B/16 backbone. It outputs class labels via a prototype-based classifier using cosine similarity with softmax, and produces attention maps as intermediate representations. Only the last backbone block plus the prompt generator and DINO head (less than 2% of total parameters) are trained, while the majority of backbone weights remain frozen.

## Method

AptGCD introduces two components: a Meta Visual Prompt (MVP) and a Prompt Transformer (PT). MVP employs a task-independent Prompt Generator with an AttnRefine block (Gated Linear Unit combined with depthwise convolution) to capture channel-level local features, and a Recurrent Self-Generation Mechanism that performs dual forward passes through the last three backbone layers, feeding generated prompts back into the input to iteratively refine local attention. PT then embeds these prompts as learnable-Q prefix tokens alongside the standard QKV attention, enabling the model to filter salient local features through global contextual understanding. Training uses a joint end-to-end strategy with a mean-teacher framework, combining supervised and unsupervised classification losses with entropy regularization, fine-tuning only the prompt parameters over 200 epochs.

## Results

On the four fine-grained datasets, AptGCD achieves New/All clustering accuracy of 69.2/70.3 on CUB-200, 53.6/62.1 on Stanford Cars, 59.0/61.1 on FGVC-Aircraft, and 42.3/48.4 on Herbarium-19, setting new SOTA across all four. The average proportional improvement in New accuracy over the second-best method (SPTNet) is approximately 9.2% across the fine-grained benchmarks. On generic datasets, AptGCD records the highest New accuracy on all three: 84.3% on ImageNet-100, 85.5% on CIFAR-100, and 98.7% on CIFAR-10. The method requires only 200 training epochs with <2% additional parameters, compared to SPTNet's 1100 epochs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AptGCD outperforms all 11 compared baselines on every fine-grained dataset in both New and All accuracy. The closest competitor, SPTNet (ICLR-24), is surpassed by 4.1 points on CUB-200 New (69.2 vs 65.1), 4.3 on Stanford Cars New (53.6 vs 49.3), and 0.9 on FGVC-Aircraft New (59.0 vs 58.1). The paper's own baseline, LegoGCD (CVPR-24), is beaten by 9.4 points on CUB-200 New and 3.9 on Herbarium-19 New, confirming the benefit of the prompt-based local-global interaction. On the long-tailed Herbarium-19 dataset, AptGCD leads the second-best (LegoGCD) by 3.9 points in New accuracy, demonstrating robustness to non-uniform class distributions. On generic datasets, AptGCD achieves the best New accuracy on all three (ImageNet-100: 84.3 vs LegoGCD 82.1; CIFAR-100: 85.5 vs LegoGCD 82.5; CIFAR-10: 98.7 vs SPTNet 98.6), though it trails GPC by 2.8 points on CIFAR-100 Old accuracy. Notably, AptGCD requires only 200 epochs versus SPTNet's 1100 epochs, making it substantially more efficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GCD framework (Vaze et al., 2022) by shifting focus from improving contrastive/classification objectives (as in DCCL, CiPR, CMS, SimGCD, LegoGCD) to improving the underlying feature representation. It builds on visual prompt tuning (VPT, SPM) and the closest prior work SPTNet, but departs by introducing a meta-learning-inspired prompt generator that self-generates task-relevant prompts rather than relying on random initialization, and by coupling local prompt features with global QKV attention through a Prompt Transformer. The first use of the META concept in visual prompts for GCD distinguishes it from all prior prompt-based GCD methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated limitation of ViT-based GCD—scattered attention that fails on fine-grained categories—and demonstrates that a parameter-light (<2%) architectural addition can yield substantial gains (9.2% proportional on fine-grained 'New' accuracy) with far fewer training epochs than competing prompt-based methods. The transferability experiments showing consistent 4–7% gains when MVP+PT is grafted onto unrelated GCD methods (CMS, CiPR, LegoGCD) suggest the components are broadly applicable beyond the specific GCD pipeline. Open questions include whether the meta-prompt mechanism generalizes to other open-world tasks (open-set recognition, incremental learning) and whether the approach scales to much larger backbones or datasets beyond the current ViT-B/16 setting.
