## Problem

Image-to-Point Cloud registration between RGB images and LiDAR point clouds suffers from a large cross-modality gap, and existing methods that bridge this gap via virtual point clouds still face a domain gap in distribution between virtual and LiDAR points. More critically, prior approaches rely on frustum-based two-stage pipelines that degrade severely when the image and point cloud come from non-synchronized random frames—a common practical scenario—because frustum filtering errors amplify under pose inconsistency.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (160×512 on KITTI, 160×320 on nuScenes) and a LiDAR point cloud of 40,960 points as input, where the two may originate from frames offset by up to ±10 m in translation and ±2π in rotation. The image is first converted to a dense virtual point cloud via a pre-trained zoe-depth model (top 50 sky pixels cropped), and the final output is a rigid transformation (rotation + translation) computed end-to-end via SVD from the selected correspondences, without any RANSAC step.

## Method

GraphI2P introduces three key components to bridge the domain gap and select reliable correspondences. First, a virtual-spherical representation converts both point clouds into (azimuth, elevation, range) coordinates, orthogonally decoupling direction from distance and rescaling the range axis to the LiDAR extent to absorb depth-estimation errors. Second, a distribution-based adaptive sample module fits a log-normal distribution on the range axis via MLE and resamples both point clouds to a unified distribution, reducing the density and pattern mismatch between virtual and LiDAR points. Third, a graph-based correspondence selection module builds a graph from a correspondence-feature-distance (CFD) matrix using KNN, then applies a 2-layer GNN to reinforce pattern consistency among correct correspondences and prune false ones, replacing the traditional frustum-filtering stage. Training uses a combined loss of circle loss on position descriptors and a contrastive selection loss on graph features, all optimized end-to-end with KPConv-based feature extraction.

## Results

On the non-synchronized random-frame setting (the paper's primary evaluation), GraphI2P achieves a Registration Recall (RR) of 95.40% on KITTI and 93.58% on nuScenes, outperforming the best prior method CFI2P by 10.77% and 12.53% respectively. The corresponding Relative Translation Error (RTE) is 0.68 m (KITTI) and 0.83 m (nuScenes), and Relative Rotation Error (RRE) is 1.91° and 2.21°. On the same-frame setting, GraphI2P reaches 99.61% RR on KITTI and 99.48% on nuScenes, improving over CFI2P by 2.46% and 4.25%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GraphI2P outperforms all nine baselines on both datasets in the non-synchronized random-frame setting. The closest competitor, CFI2P [16], achieves 84.63% RR on KITTI and 81.05% on nuScenes, while GraphI2P reaches 95.40% and 93.58% respectively — a gap of over 10 percentage points. Among depth-estimation-based methods, FreeReg [13] (83.68% KITTI RR) and RetrI2P [14] (79.17% KITTI, 77.50% nuScenes) are the strongest, yet both trail GraphI2P by 12–16% in RR. The two-stage frustum-selection methods DeepI2P [9] and CorrI2P [10] degrade severely under non-synchronized frames (10.67% and 42.31% KITTI RR), confirming the paper's argument that frustum filtering is unreliable when image and point cloud come from different frames. Point-to-Point methods (GeoTransformer [5], Hunter [31]) applied to virtual point clouds perform worst (35–39% KITTI RR), illustrating the domain gap between virtual and LiDAR point clouds. On the same-frame setting, GraphI2P still leads all baselines, with the largest margin over CFI2P being 4.25% on nuScenes (99.48% vs 95.23% RR).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the virtual point cloud bridge paradigm introduced by FreeReg and RetrI2P, which converts the image-to-LiDAR modality gap into a point-cloud-to-point-cloud domain gap. It departs from the dominant two-stage frustum-selection pipeline (DeepI2P, CorrI2P, VP2P) by replacing frustum filtering with a graph-based correspondence selection that exploits pattern consistency among reliable matches. The virtual-spherical representation and log-normal MLE-based adaptive sampling are novel contributions that address the residual domain gap between depth-estimated virtual points and real LiDAR returns, while the GNN-based pruning avoids the high computational cost of RANSAC or maximal-clique search used in prior graph-based registration methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The non-synchronized random-frame setting is a practically critical scenario (sensors rarely capture perfectly aligned frames in deployment), yet prior methods degrade sharply under it; the 10–12% recall gains here are meaningful for autonomous driving and robotic localization pipelines. The frustum-free design removes a key failure mode of two-stage methods, and the finding that ~10,000 LiDAR points suffice for robust registration offers a useful efficiency guideline. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to full 3D rotation (the paper only tests 2D translation + yaw), whether the log-normal distance assumption holds across different LiDAR hardware, and how the method scales to denser or multi-sensor point clouds.
