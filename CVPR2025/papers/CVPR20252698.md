## Problem

Existing Structure-from-Motion pipelines separate pairwise correspondence or pointmap estimation from a subsequent global optimization step, even in learning-based systems like DUSt3R. While multi-view pose estimators (e.g., RayDiffusion) have begun to unify camera reasoning across views, they predict only motion and ignore scene geometry. This paper addresses the gap by jointly predicting dense 3D structure and camera poses in a single end-to-end multi-view framework, eliminating the need for memory-intensive global alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252698/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 2–8 sparse multi-view RGB images as input and produces pixel-wise ray origins (camera centers) and ray endpoints (3D surface points) in a shared global coordinate frame. These over-parameterized per-pixel rays are denser than the patch-level ray representations used by prior work like RayDiffusion, and can be post-processed to recover traditional camera extrinsics, intrinsics, and multi-view consistent depth maps. At inference, only 10 denoising steps are required, yielding results in ~1.9 s on a single A5000 GPU for 8 views.

## Method

DiffusionSfM parameterizes each image pixel as a ray origin and endpoint pair in world coordinates, then trains a denoising Diffusion Transformer (DiT) to predict these from noisy samples conditioned on DINOv2 patch embeddings. Noisy ray origins/endpoints are downsampled via a single convolution to match DINOv2's patch grid, concatenated with image features, and processed through self-attention with sinusoidal positional encodings; a DPT decoder upsamples back to full pixel resolution. Two key training mechanisms address practical challenges: (1) homogeneous (unit-norm) coordinates bound potentially unbounded 3D scene extents, and (2) GT mask conditioning informs the model of missing depth in real datasets by zeroing invalid pixels and concatenating the mask as an extra channel. A sparse-to-dense training strategy—first learning at patch resolution without the DPT head, then initializing the dense model—significantly improves convergence and final accuracy.

## Results

On CO3D (the primary evaluation), DiffusionSfM achieves 95.2% camera rotation accuracy (@15°, 8 views, seen categories) and 95.3% camera center accuracy (@10% scene scale, 2 views, seen), outperforming all baselines on center accuracy and matching or exceeding DUSt3R on rotation. On unseen categories, DiffusionSfM reaches 94.4% rotation and 92.4% center accuracy, maintaining a 13–15 point lead over DUSt3R on center accuracy. On scene-level datasets (Habitat, RealEstate10k), DiffusionSfM matches DUSt3R on rotation (94.9% vs 94.2% on Habitat, 5 views) while consistently surpassing it on center accuracy (98.6% vs 93.1% on Habitat; 61.9% vs 53.3% on RE10k, 8 views). Inference takes 1.91 s for 8 views on a single A5000 GPU (10 denoising steps), versus 8.73 s for DUSt3R's full pairwise + global alignment pipeline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffusionSfM decisively outperforms all prior sparse-view pose estimators (COLMAP, PoseDiffusion, RelPose++) by 10–60+ points on both rotation and center accuracy, confirming that diffusion-based multi-view reasoning substantially surpasses regression and energy-based approaches. Against RayDiffusion (the closest prior work, also using ray-based diffusion), DiffusionSfM gains ~1–2 points on rotation but a much larger 6–11 point advantage on center accuracy, attributed to jointly predicting ray origins (camera centers) rather than only depth-agnostic rays. Compared to DUSt3R (the strongest pair-based baseline), DiffusionSfM wins by 13–15 points on center accuracy across all view counts and categories, while rotation accuracy is competitive: DUSt3R (trained on 8 datasets) slightly edges DiffusionSfM-CO3D on unseen-category rotation (93.4% vs 93.5% at 8 views), but DiffusionSfM (multi-dataset) matches or exceeds DUSt3R on rotation while dominating on center. On scene-level datasets, DiffusionSfM matches DUSt3R on rotation (within 0.5–1 point) but consistently leads on center accuracy by 5–8 points, suggesting the unified ray-origin/endpoint parameterization provides a structural advantage for camera localization. The RD+MoGe baseline (combining RayDiffusion poses with monocular depth) performs worst on Chamfer Distance (0.059–0.071), confirming that naively stitching pose and depth estimates does not yield multi-view-consistent geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffusionSfM unifies two previously separate lines of work: DUSt3R's pairwise 3D pointmap regression (which requires a separate global alignment step for N>2 views) and RayDiffusion's patch-wise ray-based diffusion for camera pose estimation (which predicts only motion, not geometry). By representing both scene structure and cameras as pixel-wise ray origins and endpoints in a global frame, the paper combines these into a single end-to-end multi-view diffusion model, eliminating the two-stage pairwise-then-global-optimization pipeline that has defined SfM since the classical COLMAP era. The architectural choices (DINOv2 features, DiT backbone, DPT decoder) follow standard dense-prediction and diffusion-model conventions, while the key methodological departures are the homogeneous-coordinate parameterization for unbounded 3D geometry and the GT-mask conditioning for handling incomplete depth in real datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single unified diffusion model can match or exceed the two-stage DUSt3R pipeline on both structure and motion, while additionally providing calibrated uncertainty through multi-modal sampling and running ~4.5× faster at inference (1.91s vs 8.73s). The consistent camera-center accuracy gains over all baselines—including DUSt3R trained on substantially more data—suggest that explicit per-pixel ray-origin prediction is a more effective camera parameterization than global extrinsic matrices. Key limitations remain: the pixel-space diffusion produces noisy object boundaries, multi-view attention scales quadratically with the number of input images, and the model is still relatively small. The authors envision extending this unified ray-origin/endpoint framework to a common system spanning SfM, registration, mapping, and view synthesis, and suggest latent-space diffusion (via a VAE) as a path toward higher-fidelity geometry.
