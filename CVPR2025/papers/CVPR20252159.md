## Problem

Existing MLLMs that unify visual comprehension and generation rely on spatial visual tokens (image patches in raster-scan order), which lack the recursive structure characteristic of human language and form an 'impossible language' for LLMs to master. This structural mismatch prevents seamless integration of LLM autoregressive reasoning with diffusion-based image generation within a single next-token-prediction framework.

## Input / Output

For text-to-image generation, the model takes a text prompt and autoregressively predicts 480 discrete DDT tokens (from a 65,536-entry codebook), which are then decoded into a 256×256 RGB image via a DDPM denoising process. For image editing, it accepts a source image (encoded as DDT tokens) plus a text instruction and outputs a modified image. For comprehension, it takes an image plus a text query and produces a text response. Visual sequences are delimited by [BOV] and [EOV] special tokens within the multimodal sequence.

## Method

The key insight is to exploit the forward diffusion process to define a recursive visual tokenization: as Gaussian noise is progressively added at timestep t, visual attributes are incrementally lost, and the DDT tokens are trained to recursively compensate for this loss, so that f_t(x_0) = (f_{t-1}(x_0), V_t) where each new token V_t accounts for the additional attribute degradation. The tokenizer comprises a transformer encoder with learnable query tokens, a VQ-EMA quantizer (codebook size 65,536, embedding dim 16), and an MMDiT diffusion decoder trained end-to-end with a Rectified Flow reconstruction loss that conditions on only the first t tokens at timestep t. The resulting DDT tokens are then used to expand LLaMA3-8B's vocabulary by 65,536 visual codes, and the MLLM is trained with a unified next-token-prediction objective on 200M image-text pairs (Laion + Coyo) followed by multimodal instruction tuning, enabling a single autoregressive model to handle comprehension, generation, and editing.

## Results

On GenEval (text-to-image generation), DDT-LLaMA achieves an overall score of 0.66, surpassing all MLLM-based generalists (best prior: Transfusion 0.63, Emu3 0.54) and the specialist SDXL (0.55), while approaching SD3 (0.74). On image editing, DDT-LLaMA achieves L1=14.5/CVS=85.5 on EVR and L1=7.1/CVS=92.4 on MagicBrush, outperforming both MLLM generalists and specialist editors like UltraEdit (L1=14.4) and MGIE (CVS=81.7). On vision-language comprehension, DDT-LLaMA (8B, encoder-free) reaches NoCaps 124.2, GQA 66.3, MME 1378.9, and POPE 86.9, exceeding all MLLMs designed for both comprehension and generation and rivaling specialized comprehension-only models like LLaVA-1.6 (GQA 64.2) and ShareGPT4V (MME 1567.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On text-to-image generation (Table 1), DDT-LLaMA (GenEval 0.66) outperforms all MLLM-based generalists including Transfusion (0.63), Emu3 (0.54), Lumina-mGPT (0.52), and SEED-X (0.49), and also surpasses the specialist SDXL (0.55), though it trails SD3 (0.74) and DALL-E 3 (0.67). The advantage is most pronounced on compositional sub-tasks: Counting (0.56 vs. 0.34 for Emu3) and Position (0.39 vs. 0.17 for Emu3), reflecting better vision-text alignment. On image editing (Table 2), DDT-LLaMA beats all MLLM generalists (e.g., SEED-X-Edit L1=16.7 on EVR) and matches or exceeds specialist editors: L1=14.5 vs. UltraEdit's 14.4 on EVR, and CVS=92.4 vs. MGIE's 91.1 on MagicBrush. On comprehension (Table 3), DDT-LLaMA (8B, no CLIP encoder) surpasses all dual-task MLLMs on NoCaps (124.2 vs. Emu2-Chat 119.8), GQA (66.3 vs. Emu2-Chat 65.1), and MME (1378.9 vs. VILA-U 1336.2), and also outperforms most comprehension-only models except ShareGPT4V on MME (1567.4) and LLaVA-1.6 on POPE (86.5 vs. 86.9, a narrow win). The one clear loss is SEEDBench (67.9 vs. Emu3's 68.2), a marginal 0.3-point gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant spatial-token paradigm (VQGAN, MoVQ, RQ-Transformer) used in MLLMs like Emu3, SEED-X, and Transfusion, arguing that raster-ordered patch tokens lack the recursive structure inherent to natural language and thus form an 'impossible language' for autoregressive LLMs. It builds directly on the MMDiT architecture from SD3 (Esser et al., 2024) for both the encoder and decoder, and adopts Rectified Flow (Liu et al., 2022) for the diffusion training objective. The key conceptual departure is using the diffusion forward process itself as a tokenization mechanism: each discrete token is learned to compensate for the incremental attribute loss at a specific timestep, yielding a recursive sequence rather than a spatial grid.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a tokenizer trained solely on ImageNet at 256×256 outperforms tokenizers trained on large-scale web data (Laion, Coyo) for both comprehension and generation is notable and suggests that the recursive structure of DDT tokens is more important for LLM integration than raw data diversity. However, the 256×256 resolution and acknowledged inferior aesthetic quality limit immediate practical deployment, and the scaling-law evidence is explicitly preliminary. Open questions include whether the recursive-structure advantage persists at higher resolutions and larger model scales, and whether the diffusion-timestep tokenization can be extended to video or other modalities.
