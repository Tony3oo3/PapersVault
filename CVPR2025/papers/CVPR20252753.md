## Problem

Existing indoor 3D object detection datasets are limited in scale, object diversity (focusing on room-defining objects), and annotation accuracy (labeled on noisy handheld LiDAR reconstructions rather than survey-grade laser scans), while the dominant point/voxel-based architectures entangle model design with noisy depth representations and require complex sparse 3D operations. The paper asks whether, given a sufficiently large, accurate, and exhaustive dataset, a simple image-based Transformer without 3D inductive biases can match or surpass point-based methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252753/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CuTR consumes a single RGB frame at 1024×768 and an optional LiDAR depth map at 256×192 (affine-invariant, with preserved scale μ and offset σ used to rescale 3D predictions), plus camera intrinsics and a gravity-aligned coordinate transform. It outputs per-object 9-DOF 3D boxes (projected center xy, depth z, dimensions l/w/h, yaw), corresponding 2D boxes, and confidence scores, all at the image level (single frame) rather than aggregated scene level. The RGB-only variant drops depth entirely and relies on monocular depth priors from the backbone.

## Method

CuTR is a single-stage, single-scale Transformer detector that predicts 3D boxes directly from 2D image features without any voxelization, sparse convolution, or point-cloud lifting. The backbone is a ViT with windowed attention (16×16 patches): MultiMAE (pre-trained on RGB + affine-invariant depth) for the RGB-D variant, or Depth-Anything for the RGB-only variant. The decoder follows Plain DETR's cross-attention design biased toward each query's predicted 2D box, with an additional MLP head regressing projected xy, depth z, dimensions, and yaw; for RGB-D, predicted depth and dimensions are rescaled by the depth map's affine statistics (z′ = σz + μ). Training uses Chamfer loss on 3D box corners with Hungarian matching over 2D predictions and no NMS. The key empirical finding is that paired with the CA-1M dataset (440K exhaustively labeled objects across 15M frames, annotated on FARO laser scans and rendered pixel-perfectly to every frame), CuTR outperforms point-based methods in recall and narrows the precision gap, especially when depth noise is controlled.

## Results

On the CA-1M dataset (440K objects, 15M frames), CuTR (RGB-D) achieves 40.9 AP25 and 62.3 AR25, outperforming the best point-based method FCAF (29.3 AP25, 49.5 AR25) by over 11 points in AP25 and 13 points in AR25. On Traditional SUN RGB-D (10 large-object classes), CuTR (RGB-D) trails the best point-based method TR3D+FF (59.4 vs. 68.8 AP25), but on the more diverse Omni3D SUN RGB-D (38 classes), CuTR surpasses all point-based methods in AP25 (30.3 vs. 29.1 for TR3D+FF). The RGB-only variant of CuTR also outperforms ImVoxelNet across all three datasets, e.g., 35.4 vs. 22.8 AR25 on CA-1M.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CA-1M, CuTR (RGB-D) decisively outperforms all point-based baselines: it beats FCAF by +11.6 AP25 and +12.8 AR25, and TR3D+FF by +16.1 AP25 and +9.4 AR25, indicating that 3D inductive biases become a liability at this data scale. On Traditional SUN RGB-D (10 large-object classes), CuTR (RGB-D) loses to TR3D+FF by 9.4 AP25 and 6.9 AR25, suggesting point-based methods still hold an edge on small, class-limited datasets. However, on the more diverse Omni3D SUN RGB-D (38 classes), CuTR (RGB-D) surpasses FCAF and TR3D+FF in AP25 (30.3 vs. 27.1/29.1) while remaining competitive in AR25, signaling a crossover where image-based methods gain the advantage as object diversity increases. In the RGB-only setting, CuTR consistently outperforms both ImVoxelNet and Cube R-CNN across all three datasets, with the largest margin on CA-1M (35.4 vs. 22.8 AR25 vs. ImVoxelNet; 35.4 vs. 20.1 AR25 vs. Cube R-CNN). After CA-1M pre-training, CuTR (RGB-D) further extends its lead on Omni3D SUN RGB-D to 35.8 AP25 and 73.6 AR25, surpassing all point-based methods by 5–7 points in AP25 and 8–11 points in AR25.

## Relation to Prior Work

CuTR extends the Plain DETR single-stage 2D detection architecture to 3D by adding a direct 3D box regression head (following the parameterization of Cube R-CNN/Omni3D), while leveraging MultiMAE or Depth-Anything ViT backbones for RGB(-D) encoding. The CA-1M dataset builds on the ARKitScenes raw captures but fundamentally re-imagines the annotation pipeline: instead of labeling on noisy ARKit-derived meshes, boxes are annotated on survey-grade FARO laser scans and then rendered pixel-perfectly to every handheld frame. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant point/voxel-based 3D detection paradigm (FCAF, TR3D, ImVoxelNet) by arguing that 3D inductive biases (sparse convolutions, voxelization, KNN) are unnecessary when data scale and annotation quality are sufficient, positioning image-based detection as a viable and more accessible alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CA-1M is a major practical contribution: 440K exhaustively labeled objects across 15M frames with both spatial accuracy and pixel-perfect per-frame alignment, filling a gap that no prior dataset (SUN RGB-D, ScanNet, ARKitScenes, ScanNet++) addresses simultaneously. The finding that a bias-free Transformer outperforms point-based methods on CA-1M and (with pre-training) on SUN RGB-D challenges the field's default assumption that 3D architectural inductive biases are essential, potentially redirecting research toward simpler, more hardware-accessible image-based detectors. Open questions include whether the advantage persists with non-zero pitch/roll prediction, whether it generalizes to outdoor or more cluttered scenes, and whether the gap to point-based methods on CA-1M (still ~5 AP points) closes with further scaling or architectural refinement.
