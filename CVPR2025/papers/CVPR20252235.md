## Problem

Diffusion Transformers apply uniform computation across all image tokens, layers, and denoising timesteps, wasting resources on redundant regions and steps. Existing efficiency techniques (token merging, layer pruning, few-step distillation) rely on heuristics, use fixed or empirically chosen compression ratios, and typically optimize only a single axis, leaving the compounded benefit of joint token-layer-timestep adaptation unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252235/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For text-to-image generation, the method takes Gaussian noise and a text prompt and produces a 512×512 RGB image via PixArt-Σ; for inpainting, it takes a masked 1024×1024 image plus a text prompt and produces the inpainted region via Lazy Diffusion. The router operates at every DiT layer and every sampling timestep, deciding per-token whether to process or bypass that layer's attention and MLP blocks. Compression ratios are learned as continuous scalars per layer (28 parameters) and per timestep region (10 regions for inpainting, 4 for T2I), then snapped to the nearest 10%-interval bin at inference with zero additional overhead.

## Method

DiffCR introduces three coupled enablers on top of a pretrained DiT. First, a token-level routing scheme (adapted from Mixture-of-Depths) places a single linear-layer router with sigmoid activation in each DiT layer; the top-k most important tokens are processed while the rest bypass the entire layer, with router-predicted scores rescaling outputs to preserve gradient flow. Second, a layer-wise differentiable ratio mechanism assigns each layer one learnable scalar initialized at zero; during training the scalar queries the two nearest discrete 10%-interval bins, runs the layer forward under each bin's compression, and linearly interpolates the two branch outputs weighted by bin proximity, with an MSE loss pulling the batch-average ratio toward a target. Third, a timestep-wise extension replaces the single per-layer scalar with one scalar per timestep region, so noisier timesteps learn higher compression and clearer timesteps learn lower compression. The entire framework is fine-tuned jointly with the DiT (full fine-tuning for inpainting, LoRA rank-32 for T2I), and at inference the nearest bin is selected directly, eliminating the dual-branch overhead.

## Results

On the T2I task (PixArt-Σ, 512², batch size 16), DiffCR-LT at 20% compression achieves FID 10.68 and CLIP Score 0.238, surpassing the uncompressed fine-tuned PixArt-Σ (FID 11.93) while reducing latency by 20.68% (179.71s vs. 225.38s) and memory by 8.33% (1.664 GB vs. 1.798 GB). On the inpainting task (Lazy Diffusion, 512² mask within 1024²), DiffCR-LT at 30% compression achieves FID 13.42 and CLIP Score 0.1845, compared to the uncompressed LD baseline (FID 10.90), while cutting latency from 17.13s to 13.89s (18.9% reduction) and memory from 45.00 GB to 34.88 GB (22.5% reduction). The average FID reduction over the most competitive baseline (ToMe) is 8.51 across both tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffCR decisively outperforms both training-free baselines: at 20% compression on T2I, DiffCR-LT achieves FID 10.68 versus 345.91 for ToMe [2] and 251.79 for AT-EDM [43], representing FID reductions of 335.23 and 241.11 respectively, while maintaining comparable or lower latency. Against the uniform-ratio MoD baseline (the direct architectural predecessor), DiffCR-LT improves FID from 22.78 to 10.68, demonstrating the value of layer- and timestep-adaptive ratios over a single global compression rate. Notably, DiffCR-LT even surpasses the uncompressed fine-tuned PixArt-Σ (FID 10.68 vs. 11.93) while delivering 20.68% latency and 8.33% memory savings, suggesting that adaptive token routing acts as a form of implicit regularization. On the inpainting task, DiffCR-LT (FID 13.42) substantially outperforms ToMe (FID 279.36) and AT-EDM (FID 203.35) at 30% compression, and improves over uniform MoD (FID 18.34) by 4.92 FID points, while also achieving 22.96% memory savings over the RegenerateCrop baseline with 41.01 FID better quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Mixture-of-Depths (MoD) routing mechanism from NLP [31] to vision DiTs for the first time, removing NLP-specific auxiliary losses and repurposing the lightweight router for token importance prediction in image generation. It departs from prior token compression methods (ToMe, AT-EDM) that rely on heuristic, training-free rules with uniform compression ratios, instead introducing a differentiable, learnable compression ratio mechanism that unifies optimization across three axes—token, layer, and timestep—that were previously addressed in isolation. The continuous-scalar-plus-bin-interpolation design for differentiable ratios contrasts with prior discrete-proxy approaches like DiffRate [5], reducing parameter count to one scalar per layer while preserving gradient flow through the router.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiffCR offers a practical path toward deploying DiTs on resource-constrained devices by achieving ~20% latency and ~8% memory savings with minimal quality degradation, and the learned compression patterns (middle layers most redundant, noisier timesteps more compressible) provide actionable insights for future DiT design. However, the approach requires fine-tuning (not training-free), doubles forward-pass cost during training, and has only been validated on 2D image tasks (inpainting and T2I at 512²–1024²), leaving open whether the learned patterns transfer to video DiTs, larger resolutions, or fundamentally different architectures. A key open question is whether the MSE-based ratio constraint is the optimal way to control the compression-quality trade-off, or whether task-aware or input-adaptive ratio scheduling could yield further gains.
