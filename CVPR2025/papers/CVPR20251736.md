## Problem

Existing video anomaly detection methods are either frame-centric (detecting anomalies without spatial localization) or object-centric (identifying anomalous objects but lacking pixel-level precision), leaving a critical gap for fine-grained anomaly delineation in complex scenarios with overlapping or occluded objects. No prior framework unifies object-level structural integrity with pixel-level segmentation accuracy in a single VAD pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251736/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes RGB video clips (240×360 for UCSD Ped2, 480×856 for ShanghaiTech Campus) processed frame-by-frame. A pre-trained object detector generates per-frame bounding boxes, which are scored by an object-centric VAD algorithm; only boxes exceeding a dataset-specific anomaly threshold (τ=1.5 or 1.6) proceed. The final output is a complete pixel-level binary segmentation mask for every frame in the video, along with persistent object track labels, where SAM2 propagates segmentation from prompts collected at regular intervals (every 5 or 15 frames) to all intermediate frames rather than segmenting each frame independently.

## Method

TAO is a four-stage pipeline: (1) a pre-trained object detector extracts bounding boxes per frame, (2) an object-centric VAD scorer assigns anomaly scores and thresholding retains candidate anomalous boxes, (3) a Boxes Robustness Filtering algorithm exploits temporal IoU consistency (tracking window k=5, overlap threshold h=0.2, frame-match m=3) to inherit or assign persistent track labels, discarding sporadic false positives, and (4) the filtered boxes' center points and coordinates serve as spatial prompts for SAM2 (frozen, base-plus weights), whose video tracking mode propagates segmentation masks from prompted frames to the entire clip. Only the object-level VAD scorer is partially fine-tuned on target datasets; SAM2 requires no anomaly-specific training. The key insight is reframing VAD as pixel-level tracking of anomalous objects rather than per-pixel classification, which eliminates the need for a final segmentation threshold and naturally handles overlapping anomalies through instance-level track identity.

## Results

On UCSD Ped2, TAO achieves Pixel-AUROC 75.11, Pixel-AP 50.78, Pixel-AUPRO 72.97, Pixel-F1 64.12, RBDC 83.6, and TBDC 93.2, substantially outperforming the strongest image anomaly detection baseline DRAEM (Pixel-F1 10.89, RBDC 44.26, TBDC 70.64). On the more challenging ShanghaiTech Campus dataset, TAO reaches RBDC 62.1 and TBDC 85.4, surpassing the best prior VAD method STPT (51.6, 84.6). The ablation confirms that both video tracking mode and box robust filtering are necessary, with the full pipeline yielding a 31-point Pixel-F1 gain over the no-tracking, no-filtering baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAO outperforms all 12 baselines across every reported metric on both datasets. Against the strongest image anomaly detection baseline DRAEM on UCSD Ped2, TAO gains +5.5 Pixel-AUROC, +20.2 Pixel-AP, +37.2 Pixel-AUPRO, +53.2 Pixel-F1, +39.3 RBDC, and +22.6 TBDC, demonstrating that adapting a 2D anomaly detector to video via frame-by-frame processing is far inferior to a dedicated video pipeline. Among dedicated VAD methods, TAO beats SiVL (the prior best on UCSD Ped2) by +9.6 RBDC and +3.9 TBDC, and AED-SSMTL by +10.8 RBDC and +2.0 TBDC. On ShanghaiTech Campus, TAO surpasses STPT by +10.5 RBDC and +0.8 TBDC, and AED-SSMTL by +18.9 RBDC and +1.3 TBDC. The margin over vision-language zero-shot methods (AdaCLIP, AnomalyCLIP) is particularly large in pixel-level metrics (e.g., Pixel-F1 64.12 vs. 11.19 for fully fine-tuned AdaCLIP), underscoring the benefit of explicit segmentation prompting over learned anomaly heatmaps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of object-centric video anomaly detection (VAD) and prompt-based segmentation foundation models. Prior VAD work has progressed from frame-centric scoring (e.g., reconstruction-error methods) to object-centric detection (e.g., OCAD, SS-VAD, AED-SSMTL), but none have achieved pixel-level localization of anomalous objects. TAO departs from this lineage by repurposing SAM2's video segmentation capability as a downstream module: object-centric VAD algorithms produce bounding boxes that, after IoU-based temporal filtering, serve as spatial prompts for SAM2 to generate pixel-precise anomaly masks. The paper also introduces a dual-level evaluation benchmark (pixel-level AUROC/AP/AUPRO/F1 plus object-level RBDC/TBDC) that unifies metrics previously assessed in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By coupling a lightweight object detector with a frozen segmentation foundation model, TAO offers a practical recipe for pixel-level VAD without requiring pixel-annotated training data or task-specific fine-tuning of the segmentation backbone. The new benchmark is a useful step toward standardizing fine-grained VAD evaluation, though it is currently validated on only two pedestrian-centric datasets (UCSD Ped2, ShanghaiTech Campus), leaving open how well the pipeline generalizes to more diverse anomaly categories (e.g., industrial defects, medical video) or to longer, higher-resolution sequences. A key open question is whether the IoU-based robust filtering and fixed-interval prompting strategy scale gracefully when multiple overlapping anomalous objects coexist, a scenario the paper identifies as motivating but does not stress-test extensively.
