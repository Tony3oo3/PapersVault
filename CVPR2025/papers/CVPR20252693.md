## Problem

Feed-forward 3D reconstruction models are constrained by the scarcity and limited diversity of high-quality 3D datasets, leading to poor generalization on unseen objects. Multi-view diffusion models produce high-accuracy novel views but suffer from cross-view inconsistencies, while 3D reconstruction models enforce geometric consistency at the cost of lower per-view fidelity. The paper addresses how to create a reliable pseudo-ground-truth data source that combines the accuracy of multi-view generation with the consistency of 3D reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252693/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a 3D Gaussian Splatting representation (convertible to mesh). Training data is generated entirely synthetically: over 100k object prompts are created via ChatGPT-4o-mini and rendered into images using LayerDiffuse, with no reliance on existing image datasets. During training, input views are 256×256 (for LPIPS loss) while rendered supervision images are 512×512 on a white background; both training and inference inputs come from the unrefined multi-view diffusion output to maintain domain consistency.

## Method

MVBoost's core mechanism is a multi-view refinement strategy that creates pseudo-ground-truth by a two-stage pipeline: (1) a multi-view diffusion model (Era3D) generates high-accuracy novel views from the single input image, and (2) a large 3D reconstruction model (LGM) converts these views into a consistent 3D Gaussian Splatting, from which views are re-rendered. These rendered views are then refined via a forward-reverse diffusion process (adding Gaussian noise up to timestep sT with optimal s=0.95, then denoising conditioned on the input view), producing a large-scale consistent multi-view dataset. The boosted reconstruction model applies LoRA exclusively to the Cross-view Self-Attention of LGM, trained with MSE+LPIPS loss against the refined views. A final Input View Optimization step searches over camera poses to minimize LPIPS with the user's input image, then adds a learnable offset matrix W to the 3DGS parameters to align the rendered view at the optimal pose with the input, while freezing all other views.

## Results

On the GSO dataset, MVBoost achieves state-of-the-art performance across all visual quality metrics: PSNR 18.561, SSIM 0.859, and LPIPS 0.131, outperforming the strongest baseline VFusion3D (PSNR 17.416, SSIM 0.846, LPIPS 0.155). For geometry quality, MVBoost attains the best Chamfer Distance (0.10110) and F-Score (0.7977), surpassing the next-best TriplaneGaussian (CD 0.12132, F-Score 0.7598). The method also demonstrates strong scaling behavior, with PSNR improving from 17.212 at 10K training samples to 18.561 at 100K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVBoost outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric. On visual quality (Table 1), the closest competitor is VFusion3D, which MVBoost surpasses by +1.145 PSNR, +0.013 SSIM, and −0.024 LPIPS; CRM is a distant second on PSNR (17.435) but trails significantly on LPIPS (0.195 vs. 0.131). TriplaneGaussian, despite using the same 3D Gaussian Splatting representation, lags substantially (PSNR 13.702, LPIPS 0.243), suggesting the multi-view refinement strategy is the key differentiator rather than the underlying representation. On geometry quality (Table 2), MVBoost leads TriplaneGaussian by 0.020 in Chamfer Distance and 0.038 in F-Score, while InstantMesh (CD 0.12334, F-Score 0.7369) and CRM (CD 0.12379, F-Score 0.7306) are the next closest mesh-based methods. LGM, the base model before LoRA boosting, shows the weakest geometry (CD 0.17032, F-Score 0.6349), confirming that the refinement pipeline provides a substantial improvement over the unmodified reconstruction backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

MVBoost sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: multi-view diffusion models (Era3D, Zero123, Wonder3D) that generate novel views from a single image, and feed-forward 3D reconstruction models (LGM, LRM, CRM) that map multi-view inputs to 3D representations. Its key departure is the multi-view refinement strategy, which creates pseudo-ground-truth by rendering views from a 3D reconstruction and then partially denoising them with a diffusion model—effectively using the 3D model's geometric consistency to correct the multi-view diffusion model's inter-view inconsistencies. This contrasts with VFusion3D, which relies on video diffusion without explicit 3D consistency constraints, and with prior feed-forward methods that depend on real 3D datasets (Objaverse) for supervision. The use of LoRA on the cross-view self-attention of LGM and the post-hoc input view optimization are incremental but practical additions to the LGM architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most significant practical contribution is a fully synthetic data pipeline that eliminates the need for any 3D dataset, enabling users to scale training data by simply generating more 2D images with a text-to-image model. The scaling law (Table 5) showing monotonic improvement from 10K to 100K samples suggests the approach is data-hungry in a productive way. However, the method inherits limitations from its components: the quality of pseudo-GT is bounded by both the multi-view diffusion model and the 3D reconstruction model, and the training data is entirely synthetic (LayerDiffuse-generated), raising open questions about domain gap when applied to real photographs. The input view optimization, while effective (LPIPS 0.108→0.002), is a per-scene post-processing step that doesn't address the underlying reconstruction quality for non-input views.
