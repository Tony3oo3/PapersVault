## Problem

Existing multimodal co-speech gesture generation methods encode text, audio, and action through separate encoders and fuse them in a shared latent space, implicitly assuming the modalities are independent. In reality, spoken language and gestures are inherently intertwined, with audio rhythm serving as a natural mediator between semantic content and physical motion. This paper addresses the lack of explicit modeling of these intermodal dependencies, which limits the coherence and expressiveness of generated gestures.

## Input / Output

The model takes three inputs: a text transcript encoded by a frozen BERT-base-uncased model, an audio Mel spectrogram (1×T series), and the first four frames of ground-truth gesture poses as conditioning. Poses are resampled at 15 FPS and segmented into 34-frame windows with a stride of 10. The output is a sequence of 3D direction vectors for 10 upper-body joints (TED Gesture) or 43 keypoints including 30 finger joints (TED Expressive), representing the full co-speech gesture motion.

## Method

HOP introduces Topological Multimodal Entanglement (TME), which uses audio as a structural bridge to explicitly align text and action representations rather than treating them as independent streams. For audio-text adaptation, a reprogramming module maps Mel spectrogram features into the BERT input space via multi-head cross-attention over a compressed vocabulary embedding, enabling the LLM to reason about audio-informed semantics. For audio-action adaptation, a Graph-WaveNet encoder represents gesture joints and audio frames as heterogeneous graph structures with adaptive adjacency matrices and dilated causal convolutions to capture spatial joint dependencies and temporal rhythmic patterns. The adapted representations are fused topologically and fed into a bidirectional GRU generator, trained with a combined objective of Huber regression, style consistency, KL-divergence regularization, and GAN adversarial loss.

## Results

On the TED Gesture dataset, HOP achieves the best FGD of 1.406 (vs. 1.506 for the closest competitor DiffGesture), the highest Beat Consistency of 0.762 (exceeding ground truth's 0.698), and a diversity of 108.176 (near ground truth's 108.525). On TED Expressive, HOP attains FGD 1.815, BC 0.738, and diversity 183.332, again outperforming all baselines and approaching ground-truth diversity (178.827). The margin over the strongest baseline (DiffGesture) is 0.10 FGD on TED Gesture and 0.79 FGD on TED Expressive, with a consistent BC advantage of 0.06–0.04.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HOP outperforms all six baselines on every metric across both datasets. Against the strongest competitor, DiffGesture (Taming Diffusion [54]), HOP reduces FGD from 1.506 to 1.406 on TED Gesture and from 2.600 to 1.815 on TED Expressive, while also improving BC by 0.063 and 0.020 respectively. The hierarchical method HA2G [22] is the second-closest baseline (FGD 3.072 / 5.306), but HOP nearly halves its FGD on TED Gesture and cuts it by 66% on TED Expressive. Trimodal [50], the earlier trimodal framework that HOP builds upon, shows a much larger gap (FGD 3.729 / 12.613), confirming the benefit of the topology-based entanglement over simple multimodal fusion. The older baselines—Attention Seq2Seq [49], Speech2Gesture [9], and Joint Embedding [1]—lag far behind, with FGD values above 18 on TED Gesture and above 54 on TED Expressive, and notably low BC scores (0.13–0.20) indicating poor rhythmic alignment. In the user study, HOP also leads all baselines on naturalness (3.92 vs. 3.66 for Trimodal), semantic relevance (4.01 vs. 3.72), and synchrony (3.86 vs. 3.21), though it trails ground truth on smoothness (3.77 vs. 3.97).

## Relation to Prior Work

HOP extends the trimodal co-speech gesture generation framework (Yoon et al., 2020) by replacing the assumption of modality independence with an explicit 'topological entanglement' model where audio rhythm acts as a bridge between text semantics and gesture action. Technically, it repurposes reprogramming techniques from time-series forecasting (Time-LLM, Model Reprogramming) for the first time in gesture generation to align Mel-spectrogram features into an LLM's input space, and borrows Graph-WaveNet's adaptive adjacency and dilated causal convolution for spatiotemporal gesture modeling. The conceptual departure from prior work is the framing of cross-modality interaction as a topological relationship rather than a post-hoc fusion step, though the underlying operations (cross-attention, graph convolutions, GAN training) are standard components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine limitation in co-speech gesture generation—most methods encode modalities independently and fuse them late, potentially missing the tight temporal-semantic coupling between speech rhythm and gesture dynamics. The reprogramming-based audio-text alignment is a transferable technique that could benefit other multimodal tasks where one modality must be mapped into a pretrained language model's space. However, the performance gains over the prior SOTA (DiffGesture) are modest on FGD (1.406 vs. 1.506 on TED Gesture), and the 'topological entanglement' framing, while conceptually clean, does not yet demonstrate that it captures fundamentally different information than well-tuned cross-attention. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to full-body or facial gesture generation, whether the LLM-based semantic extraction is necessary versus a lighter text encoder, and how the method scales to longer sequences or multi-speaker dialogues.
