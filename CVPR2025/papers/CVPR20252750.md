## Problem

Stereo matching fundamentally relies on finding visible correspondences between image pairs, making it inherently unreliable in ill-posed regions such as occlusions, textureless surfaces, reflective areas, and distant objects. While monocular depth estimation avoids the mismatching problem entirely, its predictions suffer from severe per-pixel scale and shift ambiguities that prevent direct fusion with stereo disparity. The paper addresses how to fully exploit monocular depth priors for stereo matching without introducing the noise that naive fusion would cause.

## Input / Output

The method takes a rectified stereo image pair as input. The left image is fed through a frozen pretrained DepthAnythingV2 (DINOv2 ViT encoder + DPT decoder) to produce relative monocular depth, while both images pass through the shared frozen DINOv2 encoder for the stereo branch, which uses a feature transfer network to generate multi-scale pyramid features at 1/32, 1/16, 1/8, and 1/4 resolutions. The final output is a per-pixel disparity map from the stereo branch after N₂ rounds of mutual refinement, with the monocular branch simultaneously producing a refined monocular disparity as an auxiliary output.

## Method

MonSter decomposes stereo matching into monocular depth estimation plus per-pixel scale-shift recovery, implemented as a dual-branch architecture with iterative mutual refinement. After a global least-squares scale-shift alignment converts monocular depth into a coarsely aligned 'monocular disparity,' two symmetric modules alternate: Stereo Guided Alignment (SGA) uses confidence-based flow residuals and geometry features from the stereo branch as conditions for a ConvGRU to predict a per-pixel residual shift that corrects the monocular disparity, while Mono Guided Refinement (MGR) uses the aligned monocular disparity as a condition for a separate ConvGRU to refine stereo disparity in regions where matching fails. The stereo branch follows IGEV's Geometry Encoding Volume and ConvGRU iterative optimization, with the DINOv2 ViT encoder frozen and shared across both branches to preserve the monocular model's generalization. Training uses L1 loss on both branches with exponentially increasing weights over iterations, and the full model achieves SOTA with only 4 total iterations (N₁=N₂=2) versus 32 for the IGEV baseline.

## Results

MonSter achieves state-of-the-art results across all five major stereo matching benchmarks. On Scene Flow, it reaches an EPE of 0.37 px, surpassing the previous best (Selective-IGEV, 0.44) by 15.91%. On ETH3D, MonSter achieves Bad 1.0 (Noc) of 0.46%, a 49.45% improvement over the prior best (LoS, 0.91%). On KITTI 2012, it attains Out-3 (All) of 1.09%, outperforming NMRF-Stereo (1.35%) by 19.26%. The method ranks 1st simultaneously on all five leaderboards (Scene Flow, KITTI 2012, KITTI 2015, ETH3D, Middlebury).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MonSter outperforms all 13 compared baselines across every benchmark. Against its direct baseline IGEV [37], MonSter achieves 58.93%, 52.32%, and 41.18% improvements on ETH3D's three metrics (Bad 1.0 Noc, Bad 1.0 All, RMSE Noc), and 45.34% on Middlebury Bad 2.0 Noc. The closest competitor, LoS [16] (which also uses monocular depth as a structural prior), is surpassed by 49.45% on ETH3D Bad 1.0 Noc (0.46 vs. 0.91) and by 16.57% on KITTI 2015 D1-all All (1.41 vs. 1.69). On Scene Flow, MonSter (EPE 0.37) beats Selective-IGEV [33] (0.44) by 15.91% and IGEV (0.47) by 21.28%. On KITTI 2012, MonSter outperforms the prior best NMRF-Stereo [12] by 19.26% on Out-3 All (1.09 vs. 1.35) and by 14.47% on Out-2 Noc (1.36 vs. 1.59). In zero-shot generalization (trained only on Scene Flow), MonSter surpasses CFNet [27] and CREStereo [15]—both specifically designed for cross-domain transfer—on all four real-world test sets, with the largest gap on ETH3D (2.03 vs. 5.80 for >1px error). On reflective regions of KITTI 2012, MonSter leads Selective-IGEV by 30.16% and NMRF-Stereo by 65.02% on Out-4 All.

## Relation to Prior Work

MonSter builds directly on IGEV's iterative geometry encoding volume and ConvGRU-based refinement as its stereo matching backbone, while introducing a dual-branch architecture that integrates a pretrained monocular depth model (DepthAnythingV2 with DINOv2) as a complementary prior. Unlike prior structural-prior approaches (EdgeStereo, SegStereo, CLStereo, LoS) that inject object-level or local geometric cues, MonSter reframes stereo matching as monocular depth estimation plus per-pixel scale-shift recovery, using confidence-guided iterative mutual refinement (SGA and MGR) to let each branch correct the other's weaknesses. The key conceptual departure is treating the monocular branch not as a static constraint but as a co-evolving partner whose per-pixel shift is adaptively corrected by reliable stereo cues before being fed back to guide stereo in ill-posed regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MonSter simultaneously achieves SOTA on five major stereo leaderboards with large margins (up to 49.5% on ETH3D Bad 1.0) and demonstrates strong zero-shot sim-to-real generalization, suggesting the monocular-prior paradigm is robust beyond in-domain training. The finding that only 4 iterations suffice (vs. 32 for IGEV) while improving accuracy implies the monocular prior fundamentally simplifies the stereo optimization landscape, which has practical implications for real-time deployment. Limitations include the 356M parameter count, doubled inference time, and dependence on the quality of the pretrained monocular model; the authors note that scaling simulation data and encoder distillation are natural next steps toward a stereo foundation model.
