## Problem

Current transformer-based T2V models (e.g., CogVideoX, Sora) produce visually appealing frames but fail to adhere to real-world physical rules such as gravity, fluid dynamics, collision, and causality, especially on out-of-distribution prompts. Existing remedies—scaling training data or injecting extra modalities like depth maps and 3D-engine renders—are either data-hungry or limited to fixed physical categories, leaving a gap for a generalizable, data-independent approach that enforces physical realism without retraining.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single user text prompt as input and iteratively produces a refined text prompt that is fed back into a frozen T2V model (CogVideoX-5B/2B, OpenSora, or VideoCrafter) to generate a 6-second, 10 FPS, 720×480 video. A video captioning model (Tarsier) converts each generated video into a textual description, which the LLM (ChatGPT-4o) then compares against the prompt to drive the next refinement round; the loop typically converges in 3–4 iterations.

## Method

PhyT2V is a data-independent, prompt-level refinement framework that harnesses an LLM's chain-of-thought (CoT) and step-back reasoning to iteratively improve T2V prompts without any model retraining or auxiliary inputs. Each refinement round decomposes into two parallel subproblems and one final subproblem: (1) the LLM extracts the relevant objects and governing physical rules from the prompt via in-context CoT; (2) a video captioning model transcribes the generated video into text, and the LLM identifies semantic mismatches between that caption and the prompt; (3) the LLM synthesizes a refined prompt using global step-back reasoning that incorporates the physical rules and resolves the identified mismatches. A quantitative feedback signal from the VideoCon-Physics evaluator (score < 0.5) triggers an alternative reasoning path when the previous refinement was ineffective. By keeping all reasoning in the text domain (via video captioning), the method avoids the multimodal alignment limitations of applying CoT directly to the T2V diffusion process, and the iterative loop stops when video quality converges or reaches a satisfactory threshold.

## Results

On the VideoPhy benchmark, PhyT2V improves Physical Common Sense (PC) adherence from 0.26 to 0.42 and Semantic Adherence (SA) from 0.48 to 0.59 on CogVideoX-5B over 4 refinement rounds, with the largest gains on CogVideoX-2B (PC: 0.13→0.29, a 2.2× improvement; SA: 0.22→0.42, a 2.3× improvement). On PhyGenBench, CogVideoX-5B PC rises from 0.28 to 0.42 and SA from 0.22 to 0.38. Most quality gains occur in the first two rounds, with diminishing returns by round 4, suggesting 3–4 iterations are sufficient in practice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhyT2V is compared against two prompt-enhancer baselines: ChatGPT4 used directly as a prompt rewriter [28] and Promptist [21], which uses reinforcement learning to optimize prompts. On VideoPhy with CogVideoX-5B, PhyT2V achieves PC=0.42 and SA=0.59, outperforming ChatGPT4 (PC=0.33, SA=0.41) by 27% and 44% respectively, and Promptist (PC=0.25, SA=0.39) by 68% and 51%. On PhyGenBench with CogVideoX-5B, PhyT2V (PC=0.42, SA=0.38) beats ChatGPT4 (PC=0.27, SA=0.23) by 56% and 65%, and Promptist (PC=0.32, SA=0.24) by 31% and 58%. The same pattern holds on OpenSora, where PhyT2V leads both baselines on every metric across both datasets. The paper attributes the gap to PhyT2V's explicit CoT and step-back reasoning over physical rules and video–prompt mismatch, whereas the baselines rely on static prompt rewriting without iterative video-aware feedback.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhyT2V departs from the dominant data-driven paradigm for physics-grounded T2V (large multimodal training sets) and from approaches that inject physical knowledge via 3D engines or auxiliary input modalities (depth maps, scribbles). Conceptually, it repurposes two established LLM reasoning techniques—chain-of-thought and step-back prompting—into an iterative prompt-refinement loop, using a video captioning model (Tarsier) as a bridge to keep all reasoning in the text domain. This positions the work as a lightweight, model-agnostic layer on top of existing DiT-based T2V models (CogVideoX, VideoCrafter, OpenSora), rather than a modification of the generation architecture itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The data-independent, retraining-free nature of PhyT2V makes it immediately applicable to any existing T2V model, which is practically valuable given the rapid turnover of T2V architectures. However, absolute physical-common-sense scores remain low even after four refinement rounds (PC ≈ 0.3–0.4), and the approach incurs significant LLM inference cost per video (multiple ChatGPT-4o calls plus video captioning per iteration). Open questions include whether the method scales to longer or more complex multi-object videos, whether smaller or open-source LLMs can substitute for ChatGPT-4o, and whether the gains persist as base T2V models themselves improve in physical fidelity.
