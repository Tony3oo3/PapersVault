## Problem

Existing OOD generalization benchmarks are overwhelmingly focused on image classification, leaving a critical gap in evaluating fine-grained spatial tasks like object detection and visual grounding under natural distribution shifts. Additionally, MLLM evaluation benchmarks neglect the grounding aspect and the impact of distribution shifts between in-context examples and test samples, which is central to real-world deployment where users provide prompts and examples before querying.

## Input / Output

For the O(OD)² benchmark, standard RGB images are fed to object detectors that output bounding boxes with class labels across 35 categories, evaluated on six held-out target domains (sky, occlusion, grass, water, dim, handmake). For OODG, the Visual Grounding task takes an RGB image with a highlighted bounding box and a multiple-choice prompt listing all 35 categories, producing a single visual concept label; the Recognition and Localization task takes an RGB image (uniformly resized to 448×448) plus a text question and outputs predicted bounding box coordinates. In ICL settings, the prompt additionally includes 1–8 in-context example image-text pairs drawn from a potentially shifted distribution.

## Method

The paper introduces COUNTS, a 222K-image, 1.2M-bounding-box dataset spanning 14 natural domains and 35 object categories, collected via a three-stage pipeline (automated screening from 5M+ images, dual-annotator domain verification, and human re-annotation of test/validation bounding boxes). Two benchmarks are built on top: O(OD)² evaluates object detectors (Faster R-CNN, RetinaNet, YOLOv9, DETR, DINO, DINOv2) under controlled train/test domain splits, systematically varying backbone, neck, and head components to isolate architectural contributions to OOD robustness; OODG defines five ICL-based evaluation settings (zero-shot, IID, covariate shift, label shift, spurious correlation shift) to probe how MLLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5, GLaMM, Qwen2-VL) generalize when in-context examples come from a different distribution than test samples. A key design choice is defining distribution shift within the ICL phase rather than between training and test data, since MLLM pre-training corpora are opaque, making this the only tractable and practically relevant shift to measure.

## Results

On the O(OD)² benchmark, DINO and DINOv2 achieve the best average test mAP of 0.213 across six OOD domains (Sky, Occlusion, Grass, Water, Dim, Handmake), substantially outperforming DETR (0.168), Faster R-CNN with SABL head (0.162), and YOLOv9 (0.150). In-distribution VAL mAP for DINOv2 is 0.389, but test mAP drops by ~45% under OOD conditions, revealing severe generalization gaps. On the OODG benchmark, GPT-4o achieves 56.7% zero-shot visual grounding accuracy while Gemini-1.5-Flash reaches 59.1%; however, under covariate shift in in-context examples, Gemini's accuracy plummets to 28.0% (a 50.9% relative drop), whereas GPT-4o degrades by only 12.1%. For Recognition and Localization, both models remain far from usable, with GPT-4o achieving only 10.6% mAP and Gemini-1.5-Flash 8.2% mAP in the zero-shot setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DINO and DINOv2 (0.213 avg mAP) decisively outperform all other detectors on O(OD)², exceeding DETR by 4.5 mAP points and Faster R-CNN+SABL by 5.1 points, suggesting that self-supervised pre-training strategies matter as much as architecture for OOD robustness. Among component-level ablations, SABL heads consistently improve OOD performance for both two-stage (0.135→0.162) and one-stage (0.156→0.161) detectors, while NAS-FPN and FreeAnchor actually degrade OOD generalization despite improving or maintaining IID accuracy. For MLLMs on OODG, GPT-4o is markedly more robust to ICL distribution shifts than Gemini-1.5-Flash: under covariate shift, Gemini's visual grounding accuracy drops from 59.1% to 28.0% (−50.9% relative), while GPT-4o drops only from 65.9% to 55.2% (−12.1% relative). Under label shift, Gemini falls to 29.7–31.7% versus GPT-4o's 50.3–53.3%. In Recognition and Localization, both models are far from practical (GPT-4o: 10.6% mAP; Gemini-1.5-Flash: 8.2% mAP zero-shot), and Gemini's performance collapses to 0.0% under covariate and spurious correlation shifts, indicating it over-relies on spurious patterns in in-context examples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the OOD generalization evaluation paradigm from image classification (PACS, VLCS, DomainNet) and synthetic-corruption detection (COCO-C) to natural distribution shifts with fine-grained bounding box annotations, building on COCO-O's approach of using real-world domain variation but scaling it to 14 domains and 222K samples. For MLLMs, it departs from prior benchmarks (MMBench, VQA, GQA) that evaluate image-level understanding by introducing grounding-specific OOD evaluation, and further departs from standard OOD formulations by defining distribution shifts within the in-context learning phase rather than between training and test sets—a necessary adaptation given the opacity of MLLM pre-training data. The O(OD)² benchmark inherits COCO-O's multi-target-domain protocol, while OODG's five settings (zero-shot, IID ICL, covariate/label/spurious-correlation shifts) represent a new evaluation taxonomy for MLLM grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COUNTS fills a critical gap as the first large-scale, fine-grained dataset supporting both OOD detection training/evaluation and MLLM grounding assessment under natural distribution shifts, making it likely to become a standard resource. The finding that ICL with distribution-shifted examples can degrade MLLM grounding performance below zero-shot levels (Gemini dropping from 57% to 28%) challenges the common assumption that more in-context examples always help, and raises open questions about how MLLMs should selectively extract knowledge from ICE versus relying on parametric knowledge. Limitations include the restricted 35-category space, evaluation of only a handful of MLLMs, and the ICL-based definition of distribution shift, which, while practical, does not capture shifts between a model's actual pre-training data and test data.
