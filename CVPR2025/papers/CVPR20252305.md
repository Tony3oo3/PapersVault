## Problem

Existing diffusion-based object erasure methods (e.g., CLIPAway, PowerPaint) can generate unwanted new objects ('sundries') in complex scenes because they lack a mechanism to constrain which content is synthesized in the inpainting region. Additionally, standard inpainting metrics like FID and CLIP similarity fail to detect or penalize sundry generation, making it difficult to quantitatively assess erasure quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252305/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to ~512×512 preserving aspect ratio) and an inpainting mask specifying the object to erase, and produces a fully inpainted image where the target entity is removed and surrounding entities are completed. The inpainting mask is derived from entity segmentation with dilation, constrained so that at least 40% of each entity remains visible. An intermediate amodal entity segmentation map is also produced, which guides the completion stage. Inference uses DDIM with 20 sampling steps on a single GPU (~3 s on an RTX 4090).

## Method

EntityErasure decomposes object erasure into two stages: (1) an Amodal Entity Segmentation Model (AESM) built on a pretrained Stable Diffusion backbone with a Mask2Former decoder (100 queries + FPN) predicts the full extent of all entities including occluded parts; (2) an Amodal Entity Completion Model (AECM) uses a decomposed dual-branch architecture (inspired by BrushNet) where a frozen main U-Net denoises the inpainting region while a trainable Entity Reference Encoder (copied U-Net) extracts features from the non-inpainting area. The key mechanism is Entity Attention: the amodal entity segmentation constrains the cross-attention mask so that each inpainting pixel only attends to reference features belonging to its corresponding entity, preventing cross-entity contamination and sundry generation. Classifier-free guidance is applied to the condition image (dropped with p=0.05 during training) rather than to a task-specific text token, amplifying the influence of the visible context. Both stages are trained end-to-end on ~1.75M Open Images V7 samples with pseudo-labels from EntitySeg and BLIP, using randomly generated inpainting masks.

## Results

On the Entity Segmentation test set, EntityErasure achieves MSN=0.122 and MARS=0.037, dramatically outperforming all diffusion-based baselines (e.g., CLIPAway: MSN=0.679, MARS=0.130; PowerPaint: MSN=0.554, MARS=0.115). On COCO val2017, the advantage is even more pronounced: MSN=0.506 and MARS=0.086 versus 2.65–2.97 and 0.23–0.29 for competing methods. The method also attains the highest ReMOVE score (86.24 on EntitySeg, 79.56 on COCO) among all diffusion-based approaches, while its FID (58.0 / 22.53) is competitive though slightly behind the GAN-based MAT (50.0) on EntitySeg.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EntityErasure decisively outperforms all four baselines on the paper's novel sundries metrics (MSN and MARS) across both datasets. Against CLIPAway [8], the method reduces MSN by ~5.6× on EntitySeg (0.122 vs 0.679) and ~5.3× on COCO (0.506 vs 2.686). Against PowerPaint [52], the reductions are ~4.5× on EntitySeg and ~5.2× on COCO. Against SDInpaint [32], the gap is ~6.1× on EntitySeg and ~5.9× on COCO. The GAN-based MAT [20] is the closest competitor on MARS (0.044 vs 0.037 on EntitySeg) and achieves a lower FID (50.0 vs 58.0), but its MSN (0.433) is still 3.5× higher than EntityErasure's, and its generated textures are noted as less realistic. On ReMOVE, EntityErasure achieves the best score among diffusion-based methods on both datasets (86.24 and 79.56), slightly trailing MAT on EntitySeg (86.67) and COCO (79.79).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of diffusion-based inpainting (Stable Diffusion, BrushNet, IP-Adapter) and amodal segmentation/completion (ODISE, pix2gestalt, SDAmodal), reframing object erasure as a multi-entity amodal completion problem rather than a text- or CLIP-guided inpainting task. Prior erasure methods (PowerPaint, CLIPAway, MagicEraser) attempt to suppress foreground generation through negative prompts or attention reweighting, but the key departure here is the explicit entity attention mechanism that constrains cross-attention to only the corresponding entity's features, inspired by Freestyle's mask-restricted attention. The architecture borrows the decomposed dual-branch design from BrushNet and the diffusion-as-segmentation-backbone idea from ODISE, but the combination with amodal entity segmentation as a conditioning signal is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sundries problem is a persistent practical failure mode in object erasure, and the entity attention mechanism offers a clean, interpretable solution that directly addresses the root cause (unconstrained feature borrowing across entities). The proposed MSN and MARS metrics fill a genuine gap in evaluation, as the paper convincingly shows that CLIP accuracy and ReMOVE can be misleading for erasure tasks. However, the method's dependence on amodal segmentation quality and the need for manual mask extension to handle shadows limit its fully automated applicability, and the FID scores are not competitive with GAN-based baselines, suggesting a quality-versus-cleanliness tradeoff that remains open.
