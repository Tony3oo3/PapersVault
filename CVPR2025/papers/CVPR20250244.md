## Problem

Visual inspection models require large amounts of defect data, but collecting such data is impractical in real-world manufacturing settings with low defect rates. Existing synthetic defect generation methods (data augmentation, GANs, text-to-image diffusion) produce defects that lack the clarity, texture detail, and natural integration with objects seen in real-world defects, limiting their usefulness for training inspection models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250244/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a defect-free RGB image, a binary mask specifying the exact region for defect placement (which can be custom-drawn in arbitrary shapes unseen during training), and a text prompt containing a learned [V*] token that encodes the defect concept. It outputs a full-resolution defect image where only the masked region is modified to contain a realistic defect while the surrounding object remains unchanged. During training, only a few (one-third of available) reference defect image-mask pairs are needed to learn the defect concept.

## Method

DefectFill fine-tunes a Stable Diffusion inpainting model using LoRA (rank 8) on the text encoder and UNet attention layers, associating a unique [V*] token with the defect concept. Training employs three jointly optimized losses: a defect loss that computes L2 noise-prediction error only over the masked region with a defect-only prompt ('A photo of [V*]'), an object loss that uses random-box occlusion masks with a compositional prompt ('A [Object] with [V*]') and a weighted mask (weight 1 on defect area, α<1 on background) to learn defect-object semantic relationships, and an attention loss that aligns the [V*] token's cross-attention maps (from decoder layers) with the defect mask to enforce spatial focus. At inference, a blended latent diffusion inpainting pipeline fills the masked region while preserving the background, and a Low-Fidelity Selection step generates 8 candidates per mask and retains the one with the lowest LPIPS in the masked area, filtering out over-reconstructed samples that fail to express the defect clearly.

## Results

On the MVTec AD dataset, DefectFill achieves the best KID (quality) scores for 8 out of 10 objects, with particularly large margins on hazelnut (1.13 vs. 21.16 for DFMGAN and 50.61 for AnomalyDiffusion), pill (8.76 vs. 123.52 and 77.69), and wood (4.72 vs. 68.13 and 83.94). For downstream visual inspection, DefectFill-generated images yield 100% classification accuracy on hazelnut, tile, and wood (vs. 85–96% for baselines) and the highest AUROC (1.00 on 7/10 objects), AP, F1-max, and PRO across all localization metrics. The method also achieves the best IC-LPIPS (diversity) on capsule, pill, and wood.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DefectFill is compared against two baselines: DFMGAN (a two-stage GAN-based few-shot defect generator) and AnomalyDiffusion (a text-to-image diffusion model that disentangles defect appearance from spatial location). On generation quality (KID), DefectFill substantially outperforms both baselines on 8 of 10 objects, with the largest gaps on hazelnut (1.13 vs. 30.94/50.61), pill (8.76 vs. 70.15/77.69), and wood (4.72 vs. 406.61/83.94); the sole exception is carpet, where DFMGAN† achieves a lower KID (46.02 vs. 50.37). On diversity (IC-LPIPS), DefectFill leads on capsule, pill, and wood, while DFMGAN† and AnoDiff‡ score higher on leather and carpet—though their simultaneously high KID values indicate those diverse samples are low-quality. On the downstream classification task, DefectFill achieves 100% accuracy on hazelnut, tile, and wood, compared to 85–96% for AnomalyDiffusion and 25–87% for DFMGAN. On localization, DefectFill attains the best AUROC, AP, F1-max, and PRO across all 10 objects, with a notable AP improvement on the challenging capsule object (0.75 vs. 0.57 for AnomalyDiffusion and 0.04 for DFMGAN†).

## Relation to Prior Work

DefectFill positions itself within the defect image generation literature by departing from both GAN-based methods (DFMGAN, SDGAN) and text-to-image diffusion approaches (AnomalyDiffusion) in favor of fine-tuning a pre-trained inpainting diffusion model (Stable Diffusion 2 Inpainting) with LoRA. It draws conceptual inspiration from CLiC's local-concept learning via cross-attention guidance, but repurposes it for the fundamentally different setting of generating anomalous features that are semantically foreign to the host object. The three custom loss terms (defect, object, attention) and the Low-Fidelity Selection sampling strategy are novel contributions layered on top of the blended latent diffusion inpainting pipeline, distinguishing the method from prior few-shot defect generation work that relied on style manipulation or word-vector optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical bottleneck in industrial visual inspection: the near-impossibility of collecting large defect datasets in low-defect-rate manufacturing. By requiring only a handful of reference defect images and abundant normal images, DefectFill offers a deployable pipeline for factories that cannot accumulate thousands of labeled defects. The stated limitation—poor handling of global structural defects like misalignment—leaves an open question about whether inpainting-based generation can be extended to whole-object anomalies. Additionally, whether the approach generalizes beyond MVTec AD's 15 categories to more diverse industrial domains (e.g., semiconductor wafers, PCBs) remains untested.
