## Problem

Mamba-based architectures for image restoration rely on a fixed 2D-to-1D scanning pattern that disrupts local 2D pixel correlations, causing 'local pixel forgetting' and sub-optimal aggregation of neighboring information. While stacking multiple multi-directional Mamba layers can partially compensate, this substantially increases parameter count and computational cost, undermining the efficiency advantage that makes Mamba attractive for lightweight super-resolution.

## Input / Output

The model takes a low-resolution RGB image and outputs a high-resolution RGB image at ×2, ×3, ×4, or ×16 scale. Training uses 64×64 patches from DIV2K with standard augmentations (random crop, flip, rotation) over 600K iterations. The lightweight variants (Tiny/Small/Default) contain 418K–1.3M parameters and add only 1–2 GFLOPs over MambaIR at 720P output, while the classic-scale TSP-Mamba-L uses 20.6M parameters trained on DF2K. The same architecture generalizes zero-shot to depth map super-resolution on NYU-V2 without any depth-specific training.

## Method

TSP-Mamba augments the MambaIR backbone with a TSP-LSTM module that replaces the fixed 1D scan with a content-adaptive local scan path. Within each small image patch (default 4×4), every pixel is treated as a graph node and edge costs are defined as negative cosine similarity between pixel feature vectors; a simplified TSP (fixed start node, 4-neighborhood constraint) is solved via backtracking to find the minimum-cost traversal, with a hash-based edge-deduplication trick to avoid redundant distance computation. A bidirectional LSTM then propagates features along this optimal path, incorporating a distance-embedding channel so that aggregation weights reflect the spatial gap between consecutive nodes. The locally fused features are subsequently fed into the standard 2D-SSM (selective scan) for global Mamba-style aggregation, yielding a local-then-global pipeline that improves structural fidelity at comparable parameter and FLOP budgets.

## Results

On the lightweight SISR benchmarks (×2, trained on DIV2K), TSP-Mamba achieves 38.46/0.9648 PSNR/SSIM on Set5, 34.42/0.9243 on Set14, 32.52/0.9042 on BSD100, 33.57/0.9405 on Urban100, and 40.02/0.9808 on Manga109, with 1305K parameters and 199 GFLOPs. The headline improvement over MambaIR reaches 0.71 dB on Manga109 (×2), 0.58 dB (×3), and 0.69 dB (×4), while using fewer parameters (1305K vs. 1363K) and comparable FLOPs. The smaller TSP-Mamba-Small (769K params) and TSP-Mamba-Tiny (418K params) variants also consistently outperform their MambaIR counterparts with reduced model sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TSP-Mamba outperforms all nine compared baselines across every benchmark and scale. Against the closest Mamba-based competitor MambaIR, TSP-Mamba gains 0.30 dB on Set5, 0.42 dB on Set14, 0.18 dB on BSD100, 0.65 dB on Urban100, and 0.71 dB on Manga109 (×2), while using 58K fewer parameters. Against the strongest Transformer-based lightweight baseline ATD-S (753K params, 349 GFLOPs), TSP-Mamba-Small (769K params, 112 GFLOPs) surpasses it by 0.26 dB on Urban100 with only 32% of the FLOPs. TSP-Mamba-Tiny (418K params) beats MambaIR-Tiny (443K params) by 0.25 dB on Urban100 and 0.25 dB on Manga109 at ×2, demonstrating consistent gains even at the smallest scale. On the classic (larger) SISR setting (Table 4), TSP-Mamba-L achieves 36.37/32.56/30.43 average PSNR at ×2/×3/×4, outperforming MambaIR-L by 0.32/0.37/0.39 dB and HAT by 0.20/0.27/0.29 dB. For depth-map SR generalization (NYU-V2, zero-shot), TSP-Mamba improves over MambaIR by 0.96 dB PSNR at ×4 and 0.92 dB at ×16.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MambaIR (Guo et al., ECCV 2024) as its architectural backbone, retaining the 2D-SSM global scanning module while inserting a novel TSP-LSTM block for local, content-adaptive aggregation. The key conceptual departure from prior Mamba-based vision work (MambaIR, RetinexMamba, etc.) is the recognition that fixed 2D-to-1D scanning disrupts local 2D structure, and the solution of framing local patch traversal as a simplified Traveling Salesman Problem to find a minimum-cost scan path. The distance-embedding mechanism draws on fast global image smoothing (Min et al., 2014), and the overall local-then-global design philosophy parallels window-based Transformer approaches (SwinIR) but within a linear-complexity SSM framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TSP-Mamba addresses a well-identified weakness in Mamba-based vision models—inefficient local 2D aggregation under fixed scanning—while adding only 1–2 GFLOPs, making it practically relevant for resource-constrained deployment. The zero-shot generalization to depth-map super-resolution (0.96 dB gain at ×4 without any depth-specific training) suggests the structural improvement is not overfit to natural image statistics. The authors acknowledge a key open question: cosine-similarity-based distance measurement becomes unreliable under high-frequency degradations (noise, blur), and they propose hierarchical/pyramidal TSP paths as a future direction. For the broader Mamba-for-vision community, the work validates that local scanning strategy is a meaningful design axis beyond simply stacking more bidirectional layers.
