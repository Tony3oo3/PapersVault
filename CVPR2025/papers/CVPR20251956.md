## Problem

While 2D visual decoding from brain signals has advanced significantly, reconstructing 3D objects (shape and color) from EEG has remained unexplored due to the absence of paired EEG-3D stimulus datasets and the difficulty of extracting robust neural representations from noisy, low-SNR EEG signals. Prior 3D reconstruction efforts relied on fMRI, which suffers from low temporal resolution, high cost, and non-portability, making it unsuitable for real-time or clinical applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251956/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two EEG recordings per stimulus: a 0.5-second static image response and a 6-second rotating video response, both captured at 64 channels and 1000 Hz sampling rate. The output is a colored 3D point cloud of 8192 points, where shape is generated via diffusion and color is simplified to a dominant color style (one of 6 categories) assigned uniformly to the object, rather than per-point texture, due to the limited spatial resolution of EEG.

## Method

Neuro-3D employs a Dynamic-Static EEG-Fusion Encoder that processes static and dynamic EEG signals through separate temporal self-attention embedders, then adaptively aggregates them via an attention mechanism (static features as queries, dynamic features as keys/values) to exploit complementary information while mitigating noise. The fused EEG embedding is decoupled into geometry and appearance components via separate MLP projections, each aligned to CLIP video features through a combined contrastive and MSE loss plus a categorical classification loss. Shape is generated by a 3D diffusion model (Point-Voxel Network denoiser) conditioned on the geometry feature, and color is assigned by a single-step coloring model conditioned on the generated point cloud and the appearance feature, predicting dominant color via majority voting over ground-truth point colors.

## Results

On the EEG-3D classification benchmark, Neuro-3D achieves 5.91% top-1 and 16.30% top-5 accuracy for 72-way object classification, and 39.93% top-1 / 61.40% top-2 accuracy for 6-way color classification, substantially exceeding all baselines. For 3D reconstruction, the full Neuro-3D pipeline attains 55.81% average 2-way top-1 accuracy and 35.89% average 10-way top-3 accuracy on generated point clouds, with a Chamfer Distance of 5.35×10⁻² and F1 score of 77.01%, outperforming all ablated variants (static-only, dynamic-only, concatenation, and no-decoupling).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Neuro-3D outperforms all four EEG encoder baselines across every classification metric. On 72-way object top-1 accuracy, Neuro-3D (5.91%) surpasses TSConv (4.05%), Conformer (4.05%), EEGNet (3.82%), and DeepNet (3.70%) by 1.86–2.21 percentage points. The gap widens considerably on top-5 object accuracy (16.30% vs. 9.72–10.30%) and color top-1 accuracy (39.93% vs. 18.27–31.13%), where the advantage over the strongest baseline TSConv reaches 6.13 points. TSConv is the closest competitor on color top-2 (59.49% vs. 61.40%), while Conformer lags most on color top-2 (35.81%), suggesting its architecture captures less appearance-related information. The consistent margin across both semantic (object) and perceptual (color) tasks indicates that the dynamic-static attention-based fusion encoder extracts more discriminative neural representations than prior EEG architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: 2D visual decoding from EEG (using CLIP contrastive alignment and diffusion-based image generation) and 3D shape reconstruction from fMRI (Mind-3D, fMRI-3D). It departs from both by shifting the neuroimaging modality from fMRI to EEG (gaining temporal resolution and portability at the cost of spatial resolution) and by extending the reconstruction target from 2D images or shape-only 3D to colored 3D point clouds. The 3D generation pipeline draws directly on point cloud diffusion (Luo & Hu, 2021) and the PC2-style decoupled shape-then-color strategy (Melas-Kyriazi et al., 2023), while the novel architectural contribution is the attention-based dynamic-static EEG fusion encoder that adaptively combines complementary neural signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing the first EEG-based 3D visual decoding benchmark and dataset, this work opens a practically important research direction: EEG is portable, inexpensive, and millisecond-precise, making it far more suitable for real-time BCI applications than fMRI. However, the current results are feasibility-level rather than compelling—5.91% top-1 on 72 object categories and simplified dominant-color prediction rather than full texture—leaving substantial room for improvement. Key open questions include cross-subject generalization (only 12 subjects, no cross-subject evaluation), scaling to richer object categories, and whether the simplified color model can be extended to full per-point texture synthesis.
