## Problem

4D-SLAM—jointly estimating camera ego-motion and non-rigid scene geometry from a single moving RGB-D camera—remains largely unsolved because the optimization space is extremely high-dimensional and ill-posed without strong priors. Existing non-rigid SLAM methods either require known poses, multi-camera rigs, parametric shape templates, or foreground segmentation, and the field lacks a public benchmark with reliable ground truth for quantitative evaluation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251207/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an online stream of RGB-D frames from a single handheld camera (Realsense D455) and optionally extends to monocular RGB with a depth-prediction network. It produces a canonical 2D Gaussian surface map, a learned temporal warp field (MLP), per-frame camera poses, and rendered outputs (color, depth, surface normals) at novel viewpoints. Tracking runs at ~1.5 fps with keyframes selected every N frames for mapping; a final global optimization pass takes under 1 minute on an RTX 4090.

## Method

4DTAM represents the scene with 2D Gaussian Splatting (surfel-based primitives with explicit surface normals) and models non-rigid deformation via a compact MLP warp field that maps canonical Gaussian positions and time to offsets in position, rotation, and scale. A key technical contribution is an analytic camera-pose Jacobian for 2DGS, implemented as a CUDA kernel, which enables differentiable pose estimation without finite-difference approximations. The tracking module minimizes photometric and depth rendering losses against the warped Gaussians at the latest keyframe, while the mapping module jointly optimizes poses, canonical Gaussians, and the warp field within a sliding window. To regularize the ill-posed problem, the method introduces a novel surface-normal rigidity loss (ARAP-n) that constrains pairwise normal dot-products across timesteps, alongside standard ARAP on Gaussian means and sensor-derived normal supervision. New Gaussians are initialized with normals computed from finite differences of back-projected depth points, and a CUDA-optimized MLP enables interactive reconstruction speed.

## Results

On the Sim4D dataset (8 dynamic sequences), 4DTAM achieves ATE RMSE of 0.18–1.00 cm, dramatically outperforming SurfelWarp (1.45–31.9 cm) by 1–3 orders of magnitude. Geometry reconstruction (L1 depth error) ranges from 0.62–3.58 cm for 4DTAM versus 4.25–50.8 cm for SurfelWarp. Appearance metrics are also substantially better: PSNR of 21.0–32.1 dB (vs. 11.0–26.3 dB), SSIM of 0.60–0.90 (vs. 0.19–0.80), and LPIPS of 0.096–0.260 (vs. 0.397–0.665) on most sequences. In the static SLAM ablation on Replica, the 2DGS-based variant achieves 95.0% precision and 83.3% recall (F1 88.8%) versus MonoGS's 29.7%/34.6% (F1 31.9%), with average Depth L1 of 0.54 cm vs. 4.52 cm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SurfelWarp [13], the only non-rigid RGB-D SLAM baseline with public code, 4DTAM wins on every metric across all 8 Sim4D sequences: ATE RMSE improves by 5–30× (e.g., 0.25 vs. 6.10 cm on curtain; 0.31 vs. 4.21 cm on modular_vehicle), L1 depth error by 5–50×, and PSNR by 6–10 dB. The gap is largest on highly deformable objects (curtain, flag, water_effect) where SurfelWarp's surfel-fusion approach degrades severely. In the static SLAM ablation, the 2DGS-based variant (MonoGS-2D) achieves comparable ATE RMSE to MonoGS [29] (0.36 vs. 0.59 cm avg on Replica) but dramatically superior geometry: F1 of 88.8% vs. 31.9% and Depth L1 of 0.54 vs. 4.52 cm, confirming that the surface-normal-aware 2DGS representation is the key geometric improvement. For offline non-rigid reconstruction (Table 4), 4DTAM's 2DGS+MLP model beats Morpheus [55] on SSIM (0.93/0.90/0.79 vs. 0.77/0.81/0.46) and LPIPS across KillingFusion, DeepDeform, and iPhone, but loses on PSNR for DeepDeform (24.15 vs. 26.81 dB) and on Depth L1 for KillingFusion (4.9 vs. 3.2 cm), indicating that the NeRF-based Morpheus retains an edge in some appearance-fidelity regimes when camera poses are given.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Gaussian Splatting SLAM line (MonoGS, SplatAM) by replacing 3D blob-like Gaussians with 2D surface Gaussians (2DGS), which explicitly encode surface normals and better exploit 2.5D depth signals. For non-rigid dynamics, it departs from per-primitive trajectory models (Dynamic 3D Gaussians) and parametric shape priors (SMPL, human-specific models) in favor of a compact MLP warp-field with ARAP and surface-normal rigidity regularization, following the canonical-space + deformation-field paradigm of DynamicFusion and NeRFies but in an explicit, differentiable-rendering setting. The analytic camera pose Jacobian derived for 2DGS is a new technical contribution not present in prior GS-SLAM methods, which assumed posed inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Sim4D dataset and its evaluation protocol fill a critical gap: prior 4D-SLAM work lacked reliable ground truth and quantitative benchmarks, making progress hard to measure. The demonstration that 2DGS achieves ~95% precision/recall versus ~30% for 3DGS in SLAM suggests surface-aligned primitives are the natural representation for depth-informed tracking, a finding likely to influence future GS-SLAM designs. However, the method runs at only 1.5 fps on small-scale scenes with a single weak baseline (SurfelWarp, 2018), leaving open whether the approach scales to large, topologically changing scenes in real time and whether the MLP warp-field generalizes beyond the curated synthetic motions in Sim4D.
