## Problem

Existing pseudo-label generation methods for weakly-supervised audio-visual video parsing (AVVP) treat each segment independently, ignoring temporal dependencies between neighboring segments, and do not account for the uncertainty inherent in estimated pseudo-labels. Additionally, the natural class imbalance—where most event classes are absent in any given segment—biases training toward predicting negatives. These limitations cap the quality of segment-level supervision and hinder generalization on small-scale weakly-supervised datasets.

## Input / Output

Each 10-second video is split into 10 one-second segments. Visual input is the center frame of each segment (encoded by CLIP image encoder or ResNet-152/R(2+1)D-18), and audio input is the 1-second waveform (encoded by CLAP or VGGish). Event class names are also encoded as text features via caption templates ("A photo of <EVENT>" / "This is the sound of <EVENT>") through CLIP/CLAP text encoders. The model outputs per-segment, per-class binary predictions for both visual and audio events (C classes × T segments × 2 modalities), trained with only modality-agnostic video-level labels.

## Method

UWAV is a two-stage framework. In stage 1, two separate transformer modules (one per modality) are pre-trained on the large-scale supervised UnAV dataset to learn temporally coherent pseudo-label generation: CLIP/CLAP segment features pass through L=5 self-attention encoder blocks, and the output is scored against text-encoded event features via inner product, with visual and audio probabilities multiplied to enforce multi-modal consistency. In stage 2, these pre-trained modules generate segment-level pseudo-labels on the target dataset (LLP or AVE), where only video-level labels are available. Three key training mechanisms differentiate UWAV: (1) uncertainty-weighted pseudo-labels, where the sigmoid of (logit − threshold) serves as a confidence score that softens the BCE loss for uncertain predictions; (2) uncertainty-weighted feature mixup, which additively interpolates two segments' HAN features and trains against the corresponding interpolated pseudo-labels as self-supervised regularization; and (3) class-balanced loss re-weighting that up-weights positive-event losses proportionally to the frequency of negative segments, counteracting the dominant negative class. The total loss combines the class-balanced soft pseudo-label loss, the mixup loss, and the original video-level BCE loss, all applied to the HAN inference module.

## Results

On the LLP dataset, UWAV achieves a segment-level Type@AV F1 of 65.9 and an event-level Type@AV F1 of 60.9, outperforming the closest competitor PPL by 1.1% and 1.0% respectively. The most notable gains are on visual events, where UWAV reaches 70.0 segment-level and 66.7 event-level F1, surpassing PPL by 3.3% and 2.4%. On the AVE dataset, UWAV achieves 80.6% accuracy, marginally exceeding VALOR (80.4%) and substantially outperforming HAN (75.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UWAV outperforms all 11 baselines on the LLP dataset across most metrics, with the largest margins on visual events: +3.3% segment-level F1 over PPL (70.0 vs 66.7) and +2.9% over LSLD (70.0 vs 67.1). On audio-visual events, UWAV gains +1.5% segment-level and +3.2% event-level F1 over PPL, the strongest prior method. UWAV trails PPL only on segment-level audio F1 (64.2 vs 65.9) and event-level overall F1 (57.4 vs 57.9), both by less than 1.7%. Against the CLIP/CLAP-based pseudo-label methods (VALOR, LSLD, VPLAN), UWAV leads by 3–5% on segment-level visual F1 and 2–4% on event-level AV F1. On the AVE dataset, UWAV achieves 80.6% accuracy, narrowly beating VALOR (80.4%) and substantially exceeding HAN (75.3%), confirming generalizability beyond the primary LLP benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

UWAV builds directly on the HAN inference architecture (Tian et al., ECCV 2020) and the pseudo-label generation paradigm established by VPLAN, VALOR, and LSLD, which use CLIP/CLAP to produce per-segment labels from video-level supervision. It departs from these prior methods in two key ways: (1) it inserts transformer modules into the pseudo-label pipeline and pre-trains them on the large-scale supervised UnAV dataset to capture inter-segment temporal dependencies that per-segment CLIP/CLAP scoring ignores, and (2) it introduces uncertainty-weighted soft pseudo-labels, feature mixup regularization, and class-frequency-aware loss re-weighting—none of which appear in prior AVVP pseudo-labeling work. The approach is explicitly orthogonal to architecture-enhancement lines (MGN, MM-Pyramid) and can be combined with any backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that temporally coherent, uncertainty-aware pseudo-labels substantially outperform both per-segment foundation-model scoring and prototype-based methods, UWAV provides a practical recipe for weakly-supervised multimodal event localization that reduces reliance on expensive per-segment annotation. The class-balanced re-weighting and feature mixup components address well-known failure modes (negative-class dominance, overfitting on small datasets) in a principled way that could transfer to other weakly-supervised multimodal tasks. Open questions include whether the UnAV pre-training stage is strictly necessary or whether self-supervised alternatives could suffice, how the method scales to open-vocabulary or very large event taxonomies, and whether the uncertainty signal from a single forward pass is sufficient or would benefit from ensemble-based estimation.
