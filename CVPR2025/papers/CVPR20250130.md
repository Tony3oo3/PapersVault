## Problem

Existing HOI reconstruction methods impose explicit interaction constraints (contact maps, human-object offsets, part correspondence fields) that create a fundamental tension: global mesh reconstruction requires accurate overall relative positioning, while fine-grained contact prediction focuses on local vertex-level details. Balancing these two objectives is difficult, often requiring time-consuming post-optimization or risking mesh penetration. The paper asks whether a transformer can implicitly learn both global and local interaction structure without any explicit contact constraints.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 5-channel input tensor (RGB image, human segmentation mask, object segmentation mask) at arbitrary resolution, plus a 3D object mesh template. A ResNet50 backbone produces 2048-dim features at 1/32 resolution, from which grid-sampled features are extracted by projecting initial 3D vertices into 2D. The human mesh is downsampled from 6890 to 431 vertices for transformer processing and upsampled back via predefined matrices; the object mesh uses 64 vertices. Outputs are refined 3D human mesh vertices (6890), 3D object mesh vertices, and object 6D pose (rotation + translation) recovered via rigid-body transformation from the template.

## Method

HOI-TG is a three-block transformer encoder with decreasing hidden dimensions (1024→512→256) that jointly processes 73 joint tokens, 431 human vertex tokens, and 64 object vertex tokens, each initialized with grid-sampled image features concatenated with per-vertex 3D coordinates as positional encoding. The key architectural novelty is the insertion of a Human Graph Residual Block and an Object Graph Residual Block after each multi-head attention layer: the human block uses the SMPLH mesh adjacency, while the object block uses a KNN-10 graph built per object template, preserving local topological structure that pure self-attention would blur. This dual mechanism lets self-attention capture global human-object interaction while graph convolutions maintain distinct local geometry for each entity. Training is end-to-end with a composite loss combining multi-scale vertex L1 (431→1723→6890), joint refinement, edge-length consistency, object vertex/parameter L1, and hand bounding-box L1, with no explicit contact supervision.

## Results

On BEHAVE, HOI-TG achieves the best Chamfer distance for both human (4.59 cm) and object (8.00 cm) reconstruction, along with the highest contact precision (0.662) and recall (0.554). On the more challenging InterCap dataset, HOI-TG records CD_human of 5.43 cm, CD_object of 8.68 cm, contact precision of 0.700, and contact recall of 0.473. Compared to the strongest prior method CONTHO, HOI-TG improves human and object Chamfer distance by 8.0%/5.0% on BEHAVE and 8.9%/8.6% on InterCap, while also boosting contact precision and recall by 3.4%/5.8% and 3.9%/4.1% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HOI-TG outperforms all five baselines on every metric across both datasets. Against the closest competitor CONTHO (CVPR 2024), HOI-TG reduces human Chamfer distance from 4.99 to 4.59 cm (−8.0%) and object Chamfer distance from 8.42 to 8.00 cm (−5.0%) on BEHAVE, with gains of 8.9% and 8.6% respectively on InterCap. Contact quality also improves: precision rises from 0.628 to 0.662 (BEHAVE) and 0.661 to 0.700 (InterCap), while recall increases from 0.496 to 0.554 (BEHAVE) and 0.432 to 0.473 (InterCap). The optimization-based PHOSA and neural-field-based CHORE degrade substantially on InterCap's complex topologies (e.g., umbrellas, skateboards), with PHOSA's object CD jumping to 20.57 cm, whereas HOI-TG maintains stable performance. The transformer-only baselines METRO and Graphormer, transferred directly to HOI reconstruction, perform far worse (CD_human > 38 cm on InterCap), confirming that explicit interaction modeling or the proposed graph-augmented transformer is necessary for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

HOI-TG departs from the dominant paradigm in HOI reconstruction that relies on explicit interaction constraints—human-object offsets (StackFLOW), part correspondence fields (CHORE), or vertex-level contact maps (CONTHO)—by instead letting self-attention implicitly capture interaction relationships within a transformer encoder. It builds directly on transformer-based human mesh recovery (METRO, Graphormer) and adapts the grid-sampling initialization strategy from CONTHO, while integrating graph convolutional residual blocks (inspired by Graphormer's GCN-in-transformer design) to preserve local mesh topology that pure attention tends to blur. The key conceptual shift is replacing hard, hand-designed interaction signals with a learned, end-to-end attention mechanism augmented by topology-aware graph convolutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that implicit interaction modeling via attention plus graph convolutions can outperform explicit contact constraints without post-optimization, HOI-TG offers a simpler, fully end-to-end pipeline that avoids the inference-time cost and tuning burden of methods like StackFLOW. The consistent 8–9% improvements on both BEHAVE and InterCap, including on complex topologies (umbrellas, skateboards), suggest the approach generalizes better than physics-prior or unsigned-distance-field methods. However, the authors acknowledge limitations with lying poses and purely symmetric objects, and the method still requires per-object graph adjacency matrices (via KNN), which limits zero-shot generalization to unseen object categories. Open questions include whether the implicit approach scales to outdoor, multi-person, or dynamic interaction settings.
