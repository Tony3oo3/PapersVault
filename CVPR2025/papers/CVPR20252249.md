## Problem

Attention-based trajectory prediction models suffer from quadratic computational complexity in the number of targets, making them impractical for real-time inference in highly dynamic driving environments. While SSM-based alternatives like Mamba offer linear complexity, prior work has not effectively integrated selective state-space mechanisms into the multi-agent, multi-modal trajectory prediction setting where heterogeneous static and dynamic context must be jointly reasoned over.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252249/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes vectorized polyline data: T historical time steps of agent states (2D position, heading angle, velocity) for the target and surrounding agents, plus n static scene polygons (lane boundaries, sidewalks, map edges). Pedestrian trajectories and traffic light signals are jointly encoded through a shared embedder rather than treated as separate static/dynamic categories. The output is K candidate future trajectories over T' prediction steps, each parameterized as a Laplace mixture component with location and scale parameters, accompanied by RNN-derived confidence scores used for winner-takes-all selection.

## Method

Tamba replaces multi-head self-attention with a selective state-space model (Mamba) throughout an encoder-decoder architecture, reducing recursive reasoning complexity from quadratic to linear O(T·n²). Three parallel Tamba encoders decompose the attention computation into spatiotemporal, scene, and traffic-control sub-attentions, each processing the jointly-encoded polyline features through input-dependent SSM transitions. The Cross-Tamba decoder uses SSM-based cross-attention with K independent query vectors (inspired by DETR) to generate multimodal trajectory proposals, where all target agents share a unified scene representation. A prediction weight inference module applies an RNN over the proposed trajectories to assign confidence scores, and training uses a winner-takes-all strategy with gradient stopping on location/scale parameters to stabilize the mixture-of-Laplace refinement.

## Results

On Argoverse 2, Tamba achieves b-minFDE₆ of 1.89, minADE₆ of 0.64, and minFDE₆ of 1.24, outperforming all compared methods. On Argoverse 1, Tamba achieves b-minFDE₆ of 1.67 and MR₆ of 0.09, again the best across all baselines. In terms of efficiency, Tamba uses only 4.54M parameters (40% fewer than QCNet's 7.66M, 92.7% fewer than GANet's 61.73M) and 27.3 GFLOPs (40% fewer than QCNet's 45.3 GFLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Tamba outperforms all seven baselines on Argoverse 2 in b-minFDE₆, minADE₆, minFDE₆, minADE₁, minFDE₁, and MR₁. The closest competitor is QCNet (b-minFDE₆ = 1.91 vs. Tamba's 1.89), which also has the lowest MR₆ (0.16 vs. 0.17) and MTR (MR₆ = 0.15), but Tamba leads QCNet by 0.02 in b-minFDE₆ and 0.05 in minFDE₆ while using 40% fewer parameters and 40% fewer FLOPs. Against the heavier transformer-based models GANet (61.73M params) and MTR (65.78M params), Tamba achieves comparable or better accuracy with over 92% fewer parameters. On Argoverse 1, Tamba leads QCNet by 0.02 in b-minFDE₆ (1.67 vs. 1.69) and by 0.02 in MR₆ (0.09 vs. 0.11), while matching QCNet's minFDE₆ of 1.07. The ablation (Table 3) confirms that the full Tamba encoder (SSM + attention hybrid) outperforms both the pure-attention and pure-Mamba variants, and that the joint polyline encoding provides a consistent small gain across all variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active research threads: selective state-space models (Mamba, S4, S5) and attention-based trajectory prediction (QCNet, HiVT, MTR). It replaces the multi-head self-attention in the encoder-decoder architecture with a selective SSM to achieve linear-time complexity, following the broader trend of SSMs as attention substitutes. The joint polyline encoding strategy—sharing an embedder between pedestrians and traffic lights—departs from the standard horizontal static/dynamic split used by prior vector-based methods (VectorNet, HiVT, QCNet), arguing that traffic-control elements impose coupled behavioral constraints. The decoder borrows DETR-style learned queries for the one-to-many trajectory generation problem and reuses the Laplace mixture / winner-takes-all training recipe from QCNet and HiVT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in the ~40% parameter reduction and ~40% FLOPs reduction relative to QCNet while matching or slightly exceeding its accuracy, which is meaningful for edge-deployed autonomous driving stacks. However, the abstract's claim of a 'four-fold reduction in FLOPs' is not supported by the reported numbers (27.3 G vs. 45.3 G ≈ 1.7×), which weakens the headline efficiency narrative. Open questions include whether the SSM-based attention generalizes to longer observation windows or denser agent counts where quadratic attention's cost becomes prohibitive, and whether the joint pedestrian-traffic-light encoding transfers to other traffic-control modalities (e.g., stop signs, yield rules) without architectural changes.
