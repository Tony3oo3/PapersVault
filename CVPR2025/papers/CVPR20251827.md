## Problem

Existing 3D character generation methods from single images either produce only holistic, non-decomposable meshes (e.g., CharacterGen) or rely on slow SDS-based optimization with coarse textures and parametric human priors that fail for stylized characters. No prior approach can efficiently generate high-quality, semantically decomposed 3D characters—separating body, clothing, and hair into distinct meshes—from a single arbitrary-posed reference image in a feed-forward manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251827/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB reference image of a character in any pose and produces three separate textured 3D meshes (minimal-clothed body, clothing, hair) in canonical A-pose, with clothing meshes that are internally hollow for physics simulation and rigging. Internally, the pipeline first canonicalizes the input to A-pose via a ReferenceNet-augmented Stable Diffusion model, then generates six orthographic views (azimuths −90° to 180°) of RGB and normal maps at 1024×1024 resolution using an adapted Era3D multi-view diffusion model. The full pipeline completes in approximately three minutes.

## Method

The core contribution is the Semantic-aware Large Reconstruction Model (S-LRM), a transformer-based feed-forward model that extends the tri-plane LRM architecture (derived from InstantMesh) with a semantic decoder, jointly predicting geometry (SDF), color, and per-point semantic probability distributions. A key technical insight is the semantic-equivalent SDF formulation f_{i,s} = max(f_i, max_{r≠s} p_{i,r} − p_{i,s}), which differentiably extracts individual semantic layers from a single implicit field, enabling multi-layer surface extraction via FlexiCubes during training. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) NeRF rendering with single-layer semantic supervision, (2) hierarchical multi-layer NeRF training that masks outer semantics to supervise inner structure using only 2D ground truth, and (3) mesh-based training with normal, depth, and FlexiCubes deviation losses. Post-reconstruction, an iterative multi-layer refinement module optimizes each semantic layer sequentially (body → clothing → hair) using diffusion-generated normal maps as guidance, with a collision loss enforcing correct layer ordering.

## Results

On the Anime3D++ test set, StdGEN achieves SSIM 0.937, LPIPS 0.066, FID 0.010, and CLIP similarity 0.941 for A-pose 3D character generation, outperforming the next-best method (Unique3D: SSIM 0.889, LPIPS 0.136, FID 0.030, CLIP 0.919) by a large margin. Under arbitrary-pose input, StdGEN scores SSIM 0.916, LPIPS 0.084, FID 0.011, and CLIP 0.936, again leading all baselines. In a user study with 28 participants, StdGEN was selected as best in 82.4% of overall quality judgments, 81.5% for geometric quality, and 76.3% for texture quality, compared to 11.8%, 10.5%, and 17.0% for the runner-up (Unique3D).

## Baselines & Comparisons

StdGEN outperforms all 10 baselines across every metric in both A-pose and arbitrary-pose 3D character generation. Against the closest feed-forward competitor Unique3D, StdGEN improves SSIM by ~0.048 (0.937 vs 0.889), reduces LPIPS by more than half (0.066 vs 0.136), and cuts FID from 0.030 to 0.010 under A-pose. CharacterGen, the most directly comparable character-specific method, shows a notable advantage in arbitrary-pose settings (SSIM 0.869 vs 0.846 for InstantMesh) but is still surpassed by StdGEN by a wide margin (SSIM 0.916 vs 0.869, LPIPS 0.084 vs 0.134). SDS-based methods (Magic123, ImageDream) suffer from the Janus problem and blurred geometry, yielding the worst FID scores (0.192–0.875). In the user study, StdGEN dominates with 82.4% overall quality preference versus 11.8% for Unique3D, 4.0% for CharacterGen, and 1.8% for InstantMesh, confirming that quantitative gains translate to perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Large Reconstruction Model (LRM) lineage—specifically InstantMesh's ViT-to-triplane transformer architecture—by introducing a semantic field alongside geometry and color, enabling differentiable extraction of per-part surfaces via a novel semantic-equivalent SDF formulation. For input generation, it adapts Era3D's row-wise attention multi-view diffusion to simultaneously produce RGB and normal maps, and borrows CharacterGen's ReferenceNet-based pose canonicalization. The key departure from prior character generation work (CharacterGen, SDS-based methods like DreamAvatar) is the shift from holistic or optimization-based pipelines to a feed-forward, semantically decomposed reconstruction that outputs separate body, clothing, and hair meshes in a single forward pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By producing ready-to-use, semantically decomposed 3D characters in approximately three minutes, StdGEN removes a major bottleneck in game and animation pipelines where manual mesh separation is currently required. The hollow clothing geometry and A-pose output directly enable downstream rigging, physics simulation, and 2D-inpainting-based editing without re-reconstruction. Limitations include the focus on anime-style characters (constrained by the Anime3D++ dataset sourced from VRoid-Hub), a fixed three-part decomposition (body/clothes/hair), and reliance on a multi-stage pipeline whose quality is bounded by the diffusion model's multi-view consistency. Open questions include whether the semantic-equivalent SDF generalizes to more granular part decompositions or to realistic human characters with complex topology.
