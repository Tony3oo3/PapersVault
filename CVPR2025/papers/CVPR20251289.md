## Problem

Long-term fine-grained tree change detection (TCD) is hindered by the absence of high-resolution, long-duration datasets and by the fact that tree changes are inherently hierarchical (spanning color, leaf, flowering, and damage categories) while being confounded by extrinsic environmental factors such as fog and shadows. Existing Euclidean-space methods cannot faithfully represent these hierarchical relationships, and no prior dataset provides individual-tree, multi-aspect change annotations over an extended timeline.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairs of UAV-captured RGB tree images (original ROIs ~2000×2000 px at 0.5 cm/pixel, resized to 256×256 and center/random-cropped to 224×224) taken at two different time points, and outputs a binary change/no-change label per pair. For the cross-domain face anti-spoofing evaluation, single face images are input and a real/fake decision is produced. The UAVTC dataset comprises 245,616 image pairs across 85 time points and 68 trees, with 72% labeled as change and 28% as no change.

## Method

The Hyperbolic Siamese Network (HSN) combines a standard CNN Siamese backbone (ResNet, VGG, InceptionV3, etc.) operating in Euclidean space with hyperbolic operations confined to the final layers, following the hybrid architecture of Khrulkov et al. A fully connected layer reduces the change-feature dimension, an exponential map projects embeddings into the Poincaré ball, and a Hyperbolic Binary Logistic Regression (Hyp-BLR) head produces the change probability, trained with a Hyperbolic Binary Cross-Entropy loss. Feature clipping (norm threshold r=2.3) prevents embeddings from reaching the Poincaré boundary and mitigates vanishing gradients. The authors validate the hierarchical structure of tree changes via δ-hyperbolicity (Gromov product), showing values of 0.11–0.21 across backbones, confirming that hyperbolic geometry is a natural fit. The network is fine-tuned from ImageNet with AdamW for 30 epochs, and the same HSN architecture generalizes to cross-domain face anti-spoofing, outperforming SoTA baselines on four benchmark datasets.

## Results

On the primary UAVTC tree change detection task, HSN (with ResNet-101 backbone) achieves 86.83% accuracy and 0.7372 F1-score, outperforming the best Euclidean Siamese baseline (ResNet-101 at 82.09% / 0.5947) by 4.74 percentage points in accuracy and 0.1425 in F1. On the secondary cross-domain face anti-spoofing (CD-FAS) task, HSN attains the lowest HTER across all four leave-one-out protocols (11.98%, 17.46%, 18.75%, 13.45%) and the highest AUC (94.33%, 90.89%, 89.54%, 94.34%), surpassing the previous best (SA-FAS) in every setting. The ablation confirms that the hyperbolic learning module contributes +3.26% accuracy and +0.1217 F1 over the plain Siamese baseline, with feature clipping adding a further +0.0208 F1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On UAVTC, HSN decisively outperforms all five Euclidean Siamese baselines: ResNet101 (82.09% / 0.5947), VGG16 (79.21% / 0.5716), InceptionV3 (78.48% / 0.5673), MobileNet (76.83% / 0.4592), and the SoTA tree-blossom recognition method [24] (78.82% / 0.5413), with the largest margin over MobileNet (+10.0% accuracy, +0.278 F1). On CD-FAS, HSN (ResNet-18 backbone) wins on all four cross-domain protocols against all four face anti-spoofing baselines. Against the strongest competitor SA-FAS [41], HSN reduces HTER by 1.19, 7.02, 1.04, and 0.78 points (OCI→M, OMI→C, OCM→I, ICM→O respectively) while improving AUC by 0.16, 5.34, 1.59, and 1.05 points. Against SSDG-R [20], SSAN-R [49], and PatchNet [45], the margins are even larger, with HSN achieving 2.67–13.81 points lower HTER depending on the protocol. The consistent wins across both the ecological and biometric domains support the authors' claim that hyperbolic representations generalize to diverse change-identification tasks involving both extrinsic and intrinsic variation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hybrid CNN + hyperbolic final-layer architecture introduced by Khrulkov et al. (CVPR 2020) and the feature-clipping technique from Guo et al. (2021) into a Siamese change-detection setting, which is the first such combination. Conceptually, it departs from prior tree-monitoring work (which typically addresses a single aspect like flowering or treefall) by formulating a unified multi-aspect, individual-tree change detection task, and from prior hyperbolic vision work (which focuses on classification or metric learning) by targeting pairwise temporal comparison. The Siamese network paradigm itself is well-established, but its instantiation in Poincaré ball geometry for detecting hierarchical physiological changes is a new architectural direction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The UAVTC dataset (245,616 image pairs at 0.5 cm/pixel over 12 months) fills a clear gap in publicly available fine-grained tree monitoring resources, and the δ-hyperbolicity validation provides a principled justification for why hyperbolic geometry should outperform Euclidean baselines on this task. However, the evaluation is limited to 68 trees at a single urban forest site, raising open questions about scalability to larger, more diverse forests and whether the hyperbolic advantage persists with greater species and environmental variability. The cross-domain transfer to face anti-spoofing is an encouraging signal of generality, but the practical impact on forestry workflows depends on whether the approach scales beyond this controlled setting.
