## Problem

Existing text-to-image person re-identification (TIReID) methods rely on offline cross-modal models whose internal knowledge is fixed at training time, making them unable to handle dynamic, vague, or diverse user queries that lack fine-grained details. This leads to poor discrimination among challenging candidate images, and no prior approach provides a mechanism for post-hoc external guidance at test time to refine queries without retraining the base model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252116/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language person description (typically concise, e.g., 'A woman walking visible from the back is wearing a white shirt, and black pants') and a gallery of RGB person images as input. It outputs a re-ranked Top-K list of person images, where the re-ranking score is a weighted combination (controlled by λ) of the original query similarity and the similarity computed from an MLLM-refined query. The text encoder (CLIP) operates with a maximum token length of 168 (interpolated from the default 77), and the MLLM (Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct) is queried with structured prompt templates for anchor localization, attribute VQA, and text aggregation.

## Method

The ICL framework comprises two complementary components. The Test-time Human-centered Interaction (THI) module is a plug-and-play test-time procedure that performs up to K rounds of interaction with a LoRA-fine-tuned MLLM: first, anchor localization asks the MLLM whether each top-K candidate image matches the query; once the anchor is confirmed, human-centered VQA elicits fine-grained attribute descriptions (gender, hair, clothing, shoes, etc.), which are merged with the original query via an MLLM text-aggregation step to produce a refined query for re-ranking. An efficiency strategy limits interactions to candidates whose cross-modal similarity exceeds a threshold ξ, reducing unnecessary MLLM calls. The Reorganization Data Augmentation (RDA) strategy operates at training time: it enriches each training text via MLLM VQA, decomposes the enriched text into attribute-specific sub-sentences, rewrites each sub-sentence into multiple stylistic variants, and randomly recombines them to generate diverse augmented texts that are mixed with originals during cross-modal training, thereby transferring external MLLM knowledge into the offline model.

## Results

On the three coarse-grained benchmarks with ReID-domain pre-training (Group ②), ICL with THI achieves Rank-1 of 79.06% on CUHK-PEDES, 70.05% on ICFG-PEDES, and 72.55% on RSTPReid, outperforming the strongest baseline NAM (77.47%, 66.76%, 67.15%) by 1.59%, 3.29%, and 5.40% respectively. On the fine-grained UFine6926 benchmark, ICL with THI reaches 91.78% Rank-1 and 90.33% mAP, surpassing CFAM(L/14) (88.51% Rank-1) and RDE (87.60% Rank-1). The THI module alone provides an average Rank-1 improvement of 1.41–2.57% when plugged into existing methods (CLIP, IRRA, RDE), confirming its plug-and-play transferability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICL is built on top of RDE (CVPR'24) as the base cross-modal model; without THI, ICL already improves RDE by 0.47% Rank-1 on CUHK-PEDES (76.41 vs 75.94) and 2.35% on RSTPReid (67.70 vs 65.35), attributable to the RDA training augmentation. With THI, the gains widen substantially: on RSTPReid, ICL★ reaches 72.55% Rank-1 versus RDE's 65.35% (+7.20%) and NAM's 67.15% (+5.40%). Against the strongest ReID-domain pre-trained baseline NAM (CVPR'24), ICL★ leads on all three coarse-grained datasets by 1.59–5.40% Rank-1 and 0.25–3.19% mAP. On UFine6926, ICL★ surpasses CFAM(L/14) by 3.27% Rank-1 and 3.24% mAP, and RDE by 4.18% Rank-1 and 4.23% mAP. In the transferability study, THI plugged into CLIP yields +2.57% average Rank-1 improvement, into IRRA +1.86%, and into RDE +1.41%, demonstrating that the interaction module is model-agnostic. The only minor trade-off is a slight Rank-5/Rank-10 degradation (≤0.4%) with THI, which the authors attribute to error accumulation over 5 interaction rounds when the anchor image is misidentified.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on RDE (Qin et al., CVPR 2024) as the base cross-modal embedding model, extending the TIReID paradigm from purely offline retrieval to an interactive, test-time refinement loop. It departs from prior MLLM-integrated ReID work (e.g., NAM [28], which uses MLLMs only for offline pre-training data generation) by introducing a plug-and-play test-time interaction module (THI) that queries an MLLM for fine-grained person attributes to refine user queries at inference. The approach also distinguishes itself from Han et al. [8] (Merlin), which achieved interactive re-ranking for text-to-video retrieval but suffered from label leakage by feeding ground-truth videos to the MLLM agent, by designing a leakage-free anchor-localization protocol with LoRA fine-tuning for domain adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play THI module offers a practical path to improving deployed TIReID systems without retraining the base model, addressing the real-world limitation that user queries are often vague, concise, or incomplete. The cross-domain generalization results (e.g., 17.49% Rank-1 gain from UFine6926 to CUHK-PEDES) suggest interactive refinement can partially bridge domain gaps that offline training alone cannot. However, the approach introduces non-trivial inference cost from multi-round MLLM calls, and the slight degradation in Rank-5/10 (due to error accumulation over 5 interaction rounds) raises open questions about optimal interaction depth and efficiency at very large gallery scales. Whether the interactive paradigm generalizes beyond person ReID to other fine-grained cross-modal retrieval tasks remains to be explored.
