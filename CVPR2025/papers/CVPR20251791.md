## Problem

Existing diffusion-based video frame interpolation methods denoise directly in latent space, which is less effective due to the large search space and cannot handle complex motions or large displacements. Meanwhile, non-diffusion flow-based methods produce oversmoothed mean solutions because bilateral optical flow between two frames is inherently ill-posed. The paper addresses both gaps by explicitly modeling bilateral flow through a hierarchical diffusion process with a much smaller per-step search space.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of consecutive RGB frames (I_0, I_1) and produces the intermediate frame at the original resolution. During training, inputs are randomly cropped to 256×256 patches; during inference, the input is resized so the shorter dimension equals 256 for feature extraction, and the predicted flow is then upscaled to the original resolution before synthesis. Since ground truth bilateral flow is unavailable in VFI datasets, pseudo bilateral flow from a pretrained RAFT network is used as supervision during training.

## Method

The core contribution is a hierarchical flow diffusion framework that explicitly denoises bilateral optical flow from coarse to fine across three pyramid levels (1/16, 1/8, 1/4), rather than denoising latent space directly. At each level, a shared-parameter U-Net (with level-specific projectors) is conditioned on the corresponding encoder feature pair and predicts the denoised flow at that resolution; transitions between levels use 2× bilinear upsampling followed by a DDPM forward step. A flow-guided multiscale encoder-decoder synthesizer warps encoder features with the predicted flow and blends warped images with a residual map to produce the final frame. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) train the synthesizer with pseudo flow, (2) train the hierarchical diffusion models with L1 flow loss, and (3) jointly fine-tune both components with photometric loss. Inference uses DDIM with only 6 sampling steps, achieving 10+× speedup over prior diffusion-based methods while matching or exceeding SGM-VFI in accuracy.

## Results

On SNU-FILM, the method achieves LPIPS of 0.0098/0.0191/0.0405/0.0839 and FID of 4.541/8.499/15.320/27.032 across easy/medium/hard/extreme subsets, outperforming all baselines by a wide margin (e.g., 33% lower FID than SGM-VFI on extreme: 27.03 vs 41.08). On Xiph-4K, it reaches LPIPS 0.0614 and FID 14.13, nearly halving the FID of the best non-diffusion method (SGM-VFI: 27.33). Inference takes 0.20 s on a 1024×1024 pair (RTX-4090), matching SGM-VFI (0.19 s) while being 10×+ faster than LDMVFI (8.3 s) and CBBD (2.1 s), with only ~2.9 GB VRAM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all nine baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the strongest non-diffusion method SGM-VFI [28], it reduces FID by 33% on SNU-FILM-extreme (27.03 vs 41.08) and by 48% on Xiph-4K (14.13 vs 27.33), while matching its inference speed (0.20 s vs 0.19 s). Against the best prior diffusion methods, CBBD [32] and LDMVFI [7], it improves FID by 26% and 42% respectively on SNU-FILM-extreme, while running 10×+ faster (0.20 s vs 2.1 s and 8.3 s) and using less than half the VRAM (~2.9 GB vs ~8.4 GB and ~6.9 GB). Among non-diffusion baselines, Per-VFI [51] is the closest competitor on SNU-FILM-extreme (LPIPS 0.0901 vs 0.0839), but the proposed method still leads by a clear margin on FID (27.03 vs 34.18). On DAVIS and Vimeo-90k (Table 3), the method achieves the best LPIPS and FID across all competitors, including VFIformer [30], AMT-G [27], and EMA-VFI [54]. MADIFF [20] and URPNet-LARGE [22] are consistently outperformed on both LPIPS and FID across all evaluation settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DDPM/DDIM diffusion framework but departs fundamentally from prior diffusion-based VFI methods (LDMVFI, CBBD, MADIFF) that denoise in latent space directly. Instead, it introduces a hierarchical coarse-to-fine denoising of bilateral optical flow, conditioned on multiscale encoder features, paired with a flow-guided image synthesizer trained end-to-end. The approach also draws on the encoder-decoder paradigm with bilateral flow supervision from non-diffusion methods (SGM-VFI, AMT, EMA-VFI) and uses RAFT pseudo-flow for training supervision, bridging the gap between the two families of methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly modeling optical flow as the diffusion target (a much smaller search space than latent space) yields both a 10+× speedup over prior diffusion-based VFI and significantly better accuracy, closing the long-standing efficiency-accuracy gap between diffusion and non-diffusion methods. The remaining limitation—artifacts under extreme motion patterns attributed to small-scale training data—suggests that larger, more diverse motion datasets could further improve results. The hierarchical flow diffusion paradigm may generalize to other tasks where an intermediate structured representation (flow, depth, pose) is more tractable to denoise than a full latent image.
