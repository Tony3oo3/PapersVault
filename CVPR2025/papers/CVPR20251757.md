## Problem

Existing FL backdoor defenses rely on magnitude-based metrics or pair-wise cosine similarity, both of which fail against stealthy attacks where malicious updates are statistically indistinguishable from benign ones in magnitude and general direction. Additionally, the literature lacks theoretical robustness guarantees for filtering-based defenses under non-IID data heterogeneity, where diverse benign updates further confound detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251757/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

AlignIns consumes the set of n local model updates Δ_i^t (each a d-dimensional vector representing the difference between a client's locally trained model and the current global model θ^t) along with the global model itself. It outputs a single aggregated model update Δ̃ computed as the clipped average of updates retained after directional anomaly filtering. The method operates on updates rather than full models, and a Top-k mask (default k = 0.3d) restricts fine-grained sign analysis to the most magnitude-relevant parameters, making it scalable to large DNNs.

## Method

AlignIns performs a two-level directional alignment inspection on each local update. First, Temporal Direction Alignment (TDA) computes the cosine similarity between each update and the global model to capture general directional deviation. Second, Masked Principal Sign Alignment (MPSA) constructs a per-coordinate majority sign vector across all updates, then measures the fraction of sign disagreements restricted to the Top-k largest-magnitude parameters of each update, providing fine-grained directional evidence that magnitude-based and coarse cosine metrics miss. Both scores are standardized via the robust MZ_score (median-based z-score), and updates whose absolute MZ scores exceed configurable radii λ_c and λ_s are filtered out. Retained updates are then clipped to the median L2-norm before averaging. The paper proves AlignIns satisfies κ-robust filtering with κ = O(1 + m/(n−2m)) and that its propagation error over T rounds is bounded, providing the first such theoretical guarantee for a filtering-based FL defense under non-IID heterogeneity.

## Results

On IID CIFAR-10 (ResNet-9), AlignIns achieves 88.64% main task accuracy (MA), 2.21% average backdoor accuracy (BA), and 85.67% average robustness accuracy (RA) across Badnet, DBA, and Neurotoxin attacks, closely matching the ideal FedAvg* upper bound (BA=2.06%, RA=85.60%). On IID CIFAR-100 (VGG-9), AlignIns reaches 63.45% MA, 0.59% average BA, and 51.11% average RA, again near the FedAvg* ceiling (BA=0.62%, RA=53.03%). In cross-device FL with 100 clients and client sampling, AlignIns maintains 79.28% average RA while Foolsgold and Lockdown drop to near 0% RA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AlignIns outperforms all six SOTA defense baselines on both BA and RA across CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100. Against the closest competitor Lockdown, AlignIns reduces average BA from 6.91% to 2.21% and improves average RA from 80.31% to 85.67% on CIFAR-10, and from 27.38% to 0.59% BA on CIFAR-100. RLR achieves a low BA (2.89%) on CIFAR-10 but at a severe cost to RA (35.93% vs. 85.67%) and MA (79.16% vs. 88.64%), because its sign-flipping strategy discards benign parameters. Magnitude-based methods (MKrum, MultiMetric) and influence-reduction methods (RFA, Foolsgold) are largely ineffective against stealthy backdoor attacks, with average BA ranging from 33% to 99% on CIFAR-100. AlignIns's performance is within 0.15% BA and 0.07% RA of the oracle FedAvg* upper bound on CIFAR-10, confirming near-optimal filtering.
