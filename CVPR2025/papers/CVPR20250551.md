## Problem

Existing object removal methods suffer from incomplete removal, incorrect content synthesis, and blurry outputs, primarily because they rely on self-supervised training with random masks that forces the model to reconstruct the original object rather than restore the background. High-quality paired training data (before/after removal) is scarce, and existing datasets are limited in size, diversity, and quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250551/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB image and a binary object mask as input, producing an object-removed image with the background restored. Unlike prior work that concatenates the full source image, RORem masks the removal region before concatenation to avoid transparent object residuals. Training operates at 512×512 resolution; evaluation is conducted at both 512×512 and 1024×1024. The model requires no text prompt (text condition is set to null), and the distilled variant completes removal in 4 diffusion steps (~0.5 s on A100).

## Method

RORem fine-tunes SDXL-inpainting on high-quality triplet data (source image, mask, removed image) generated through a human-in-the-loop semi-supervised pipeline. Starting from 60K triplets (RORD + MULAN), the model generates removal candidates on OpenImages; human annotators select high-quality pairs, and a discriminator (SDXL down/middle blocks + LoRA rank-4 + convolutional head) is trained on the human feedback to automate subsequent annotation rounds. Iterating human and automated annotation for 3 rounds yields 200K+ pairs, after which a final 1,200-pair high-resolution fine-tune (DIV2K/Flicker2K with SAM masks) improves output fidelity. For efficiency, LoRA layers (rank 64) are added and the model is distilled via an LCM-style consistency loss (k=20, null text, CFG=1) to achieve 4-step inference.

## Results

On the 512×512 test set, RORem achieves a 76.2% human-annotated success rate, surpassing the second-best method (PPT at 55.8%) by approximately 20 percentage points. At 1024×1024, RORem reaches 70.2% success rate versus DesignEdit's 52.4%, an 18-point margin. RORem also attains the best LPIPS (2.86 / 1.44) and DINO (0.77 / 0.78) scores among all methods at both resolutions, and the highest PSNR among diffusion-based methods (31.10 / 36.05). The distilled RORem-4S variant completes removal in 0.50 s (512) and 0.83 s (1024) with only a 1.4–2.8% drop in success rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RORem decisively outperforms all six baselines on the headline success-rate metric at both resolutions. Against the strongest competitor PPT (55.8% / 46.8%), RORem gains roughly 20 / 23 points; against DesignEdit (29.2% / 52.4%), the margin is 47 / 18 points. Lama is the only method that beats RORem on PSNR (33.06 vs 31.10 at 512; 36.36 vs 36.05 at 1024) and is also the fastest (0.15 s), but its success rate collapses to 18.2% at 1024×1024 due to limited generative capacity. SDXL-INP, INST, and IP2P all score below 21% success rate at 512, confirming that generic inpainting or instruction-following models are unreliable for object removal. CLIPAway (32.4% / 23.8%) and DesignEdit (29.2% / 52.4%) are the next-closest methods but still trail RORem by 18–47 points. The distilled RORem-4S retains 74.8% / 67.4% success rate while reducing inference time by 88% / 81%, making it the fastest method among those exceeding 60% success rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SDXL-inpainting as its base architecture and adapts LCM-style distillation for few-step inference, but its core departure is from the self-supervised random-masking training paradigm that dominates inpainting and object removal literature. While prior supervised efforts (ObjectDrop, MULAN, RORD) acknowledged the need for paired removal data, they remained limited in scale and diversity; RORem introduces a human-in-the-loop semi-supervised pipeline with a learned discriminator to iteratively expand training data from 60K to 200K+ triplets. Conceptually, it positions object removal as fundamentally different from general inpainting—requiring true counterfactual pairs rather than reconstruction of masked content—and treats the discriminator as a learned proxy for human quality judgment to automate data curation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The publicly released 200K+ triplet dataset and the 4-step distilled model (0.5s inference) address two practical bottlenecks in object removal: data scarcity and inference latency, making high-quality removal viable for real-time applications in photography and content creation. The 18% success-rate gap over the second-best method, validated by both human study and a learned discriminator, quantifies how severely the self-supervised paradigm limits removal reliability. Open questions include whether the human-in-the-loop data generation paradigm generalizes to other editing tasks (e.g., object insertion, style transfer), whether the VAE compression artifact in unmasked regions can be mitigated without sacrificing generative quality, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D content removal.
