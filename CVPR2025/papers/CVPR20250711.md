## Problem

Reconstructing highly reflective objects from multi-view images is ill-posed because surface appearance depends jointly on material/geometry and lighting, yet existing Gaussian Splatting methods address only one aspect (either surface constraints or inverse rendering) while SDF-based methods, though higher quality, are an order of magnitude slower and over-smooth surfaces. The paper tackles this gap by simultaneously constraining both surface geometry and lighting within a Gaussian Splatting framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images (downsampled to 1024×1024 for real data) and camera intrinsics, and additionally consumes pseudo-ground-truth depth and normal maps generated by pre-trained foundation models (Marigold for normals, Depth Pro for metric depth) as supervision signals. It outputs a 2D Gaussian Splatting scene where each primitive carries PBR parameters (albedo, metallic, roughness), a learned HDR environment cube map, and an extractable 3D mesh, enabling both novel-view synthesis and relighting under arbitrary environment lighting.

## Method

GS-2DGS builds on 2D Gaussian Splatting, whose oriented planar primitives provide well-defined surface normals, and introduces two key mechanisms to handle reflectivity. First, it leverages monocular foundation models (Marigold, Depth Pro) to predict per-view depth and normal maps that are immune to view-dependent reflections, using them as geometric supervision via L1+cosine normal loss and scale-invariant depth loss. Second, it replaces spherical-harmonic appearance with a full Cook-Torrance PBR pipeline (albedo, metallic, roughness per Gaussian) and a trainable HDR environment map, rendered through deferred shading: depth, normal, and PBR parameters are first rasterized into a G-buffer, then a single shading pass per ray computes the final color, avoiding the inaccuracy and cost of shading every Gaussian independently. Training proceeds in two stages—30K iterations optimizing geometry with foundation-model supervision, followed by 10K iterations jointly optimizing PBR parameters and environment lighting—with additional regularization on lighting smoothness and PBR parameter spatial coherence.

## Results

On the Glossy Blender dataset, GS-2DGS achieves an average Chamfer-L1 distance of 0.0068, the best among all Gaussian-based methods and competitive with the SDF-based NERO (0.0042) while training in 0.7 h versus NERO's 12 h. On the StanfordORB real-world dataset, the method achieves the best average Chamfer-L1 of 0.0047, outperforming R3DG (0.0070) and GShader (0.0074). For relighting on Glossy Blender, GS-2DGS attains the highest PSNR (19.56) and SSIM (0.8557) at 160 FPS, compared to R3DG's 19.19/0.8366 at only 1.5 FPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the SDF-based baselines, GS-2DGS (0.0068 avg Chamfer-L1) clearly outperforms TensoSDF (0.0106) but remains slightly behind NERO (0.0042), though it trains roughly 17× faster (0.7 h vs. 12 h). Among Gaussian-based methods, the advantage is decisive: GS-2DGS beats R3DG (0.0303) by 4.4×, GShader (0.0169) by 2.5×, and GS-IR (0.0553) by 8.1× in average Chamfer distance on Glossy Blender. On the real-world StanfordORB dataset, GS-2DGS (0.0047) outperforms all three Gaussian baselines, with the largest margin over GS-IR (0.0139). For relighting, GS-2DGS achieves the best PSNR (19.56) and SSIM (0.8557) while rendering at 160 FPS, compared to R3DG's 1.5 FPS and GShader's 50 FPS, making it both the highest-quality and the most practical real-time option among the compared methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 2DGS (Huang et al., SIGGRAPH 2024) for its geometric representation and extends the PBR-based Gaussian splatting line of work (GaussianShader, GS-IR, R3DG) by introducing two key departures: (1) geometric supervision from monocular foundation models (Marigold for normals, Depth Pro for depth) to address the ill-posed nature of reflective reconstruction, and (2) deferred shading (from Ye et al.) to decouple geometry rendering from lighting computation. Unlike prior GS-based inverse rendering methods that rely solely on multi-view consistency or learned spherical harmonics for appearance, GS-2DGS leverages the prior knowledge embedded in large-scale pre-trained models to provide view-independent geometric constraints that are inherently robust to specular reflections.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is the 10× speedup over SDF-based methods (NeRO, TensoSDF) while achieving comparable Chamfer distance, making high-quality reflective object reconstruction accessible in near-real-time workflows. The ablation reveals that foundation-model geometric supervision is the single most impactful component (reducing Chamfer distance by ~83% over vanilla 2DGS), suggesting a broader pattern where pre-trained monocular geometry estimators can serve as effective regularizers for multi-view reconstruction. Open questions include whether this approach scales to full scenes (beyond object-level), how sensitive results are to the quality of foundation model predictions on out-of-distribution materials, and whether the remaining gap to SDF methods can be closed without sacrificing the speed advantage.
