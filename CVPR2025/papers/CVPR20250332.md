## Problem

Existing point cloud completion methods recover individual missing points but do not produce the structured, parametric planar representations required for polygonal surface reconstruction. This creates a persistent gap: even when completion methods fill in points, the resulting geometry lacks the piecewise-planar structure that polygonal solvers like PolyFit or KSR need, leading to poor or failed reconstructions from incomplete data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250332/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an incomplete point cloud (normalized to unit diagonal length, with 25–75% of points missing) and produces a variable-size set of parametric planar primitives, each consisting of plane parameters in polar coordinates (r, θ, φ) plus a distribution of inlier points on that plane. Unlike fixed-size point completion outputs, the number of primitives adapts to surface complexity, and the polar representation avoids degeneracies for axis-parallel planes. The resulting primitives can be directly assembled into a polygonal surface without intermediate meshing.

## Method

PaCo hierarchically encodes input points into plane proxies via GoCoPP segmentation, PoinTr-style patch encoding, and sum pooling with normal embeddings, then uses a Transformer-based proxy generator to propose plane proxies for missing regions. Three prediction heads recover plane parameters (in polar coordinates), distribute inlier points via predicted polar angles, and assign confidence scores for primitive selection. Training employs bipartite matching (Hungarian algorithm) between predicted and ground-truth primitives, optimizing four per-primitive losses (classification, plane normal, plane chamfer, repulsion) plus a global chamfer loss. The key architectural choice is representing planes in polar rather than Cartesian coordinates, which the ablation shows is critical for producing axis-aligned, structurally regular primitives.

## Results

On the ABC dataset with the PolyFit solver, PaCo achieves CD=1.87, HD=4.09, NC=0.943, and FR=0.48%, substantially outperforming the strongest point cloud completion baselines (ODGNet: CD=2.73, HD=6.41, NC=0.933, FR=4.28% and AdaPoinTr: CD=3.16, HD=7.36, NC=0.920, FR=5.89%). PaCo maintains its advantage across all three reconstruction solvers (PolyFit, COMPOD, KSR) and all three occlusion levels (25%, 50%, 75% missing), with the gap widening dramatically on hard samples where competing methods degrade sharply (e.g., AdaPoinTr CD jumps from 1.55 to 5.81 while PaCo goes from 1.52 to 2.43). Against direct reconstruction methods (Table 2), PaCo also dominates, achieving CD=1.87 vs. SECAD-Net's 4.35 and BSP-Net's 7.69.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PaCo outperforms all six point cloud completion baselines (PCN, FoldingNet, GRNet, PoinTr, AdaPoinTr, ODGNet) across every metric and every reconstruction solver. The closest competitors are ODGNet and AdaPoinTr, but PaCo still reduces CD by ~31% (1.87 vs. 2.73) and FR by ~89% (0.48% vs. 4.28%) over ODGNet with PolyFit. Against direct reconstruction methods (Table 2), PaCo beats SECAD-Net (CD 1.87 vs. 4.35) and BSP-Net (CD 1.87 vs. 7.69) by large margins, while traditional solvers (PolyFit, KSR, COMPOD) fail on 9–63% of inputs without completion. In the simplification comparison (Table 3), PaCo achieves the best fidelity (CD=1.72, HD=4.13, NC=0.934) with the fewest faces (13 polygons vs. 39 for QEM and 44 for RoLoPM), demonstrating that parametric completion yields more regular and compact surfaces than post-hoc mesh simplification. The advantage is most pronounced under high incompleteness (75% missing), where PaCo's CD remains at 2.43 while AdaPoinTr and ODGNet degrade to 5.81 and 4.72 respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

PaCo builds directly on the PoinTr/AdaPoinTr transformer-based completion architecture (adopting its point-proxy encoding and query-ranking strategy) and on GoCoPP for planar segmentation, but departs fundamentally from the point-level completion paradigm by predicting parametric plane primitives (parameters + inlier points) instead of individual missing points. Conceptually, it bridges two previously disconnected research threads—point cloud completion and polygonal surface reconstruction (PolyFit, KSR, COMPOD)—by showing that the completion step should be formulated in the same representational space as the downstream reconstruction solver. The bipartite matching with Hungarian assignment for variable-size primitive sets, combined with a multi-objective loss (classification, normal, chamfer, repulsion), is a novel training formulation not present in prior completion or reconstruction work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that for structured (piecewise-planar) surfaces, recovering parametric primitives from incomplete data is substantially more effective than recovering individual points, particularly at high incompleteness (75% missing) where point-level methods degrade sharply while PaCo remains stable. This opens a path toward unified completion-and-reconstruction pipelines for CAD and building-modeling applications where compact polygonal representations are essential. Key limitations include restriction to planar primitives only (no spheres, cylinders, or free-form surfaces) and dependence on GoCoPP segmentation quality in the input; extending the paradigm to richer primitive families and non-planar geometries is the natural next step.
