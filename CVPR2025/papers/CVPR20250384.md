## Problem

Recovering long-sequence 3D human motion in world coordinates from in-the-wild multi-shot videos remains largely unsolved. Existing HMR methods either assume a single continuous camera view or only align motions in camera space, leaving two critical gaps unaddressed: how to maintain human orientation and pose continuity across abrupt shot transitions, and how to estimate accurate camera trajectories when dynamic humans dominate the frame and corrupt visual odometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250384/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-shot video sequence (2–4 shots, 30 FPS, up to ~1478 frames) as input and produces a temporally continuous SMPL motion sequence in world coordinates (body pose θ, shape β, root orientation Γ, translation τ per frame), per-frame camera rotation and translation, and a refined human trajectory. Internally, the pipeline consumes 2D keypoints (ViTPose), image features (ViT), and human masks (SAM) extracted per frame, with the ms-HMR encoder operating on the full concatenated motion sequence across all shots rather than per-shot independently.

## Method

HumanMM is a four-stage pipeline: (1) a shot transition detector combining SceneDetect, bbox IoU tracking, and 2D keypoint IoU tracking to segment the video into single-shot clips; (2) Masked LEAP-VO, which assigns zero visibility to feature points inside SAM-generated human masks before bundle adjustment, yielding more accurate per-shot camera poses; (3) an alignment module that first recovers per-shot SMPL parameters via GVHMR, then corrects inter-shot orientation discontinuities using an epipolar-geometry-based camera calibration (RANSAC on 2D keypoint correspondences to estimate relative camera rotation R_δ_cam), and finally refines body pose across all shots with ms-HMR, a Transformer encoder with shot-index positional encoding trained on AMASS/3DPW/H3.6M/BEDLAM with injected rotational and pose noise; (4) a bi-directional LSTM trajectory predictor (supervised on foot-contact probability and root velocity) combined with a WHAM-style trajectory refiner to eliminate foot sliding and enforce temporal smoothness in the final world-space trajectory.

## Results

On the proposed ms-Motion benchmark (2-shot setting), HumanMM achieves PA-MPJPE of 36.82 mm and WA-MPJPE of 121.35 mm on ms-AIST, and 40.52 mm / 132.13 mm on ms-H3.6M, substantially outperforming the best baseline GVHMR (60.72 / 231.36 on ms-AIST; 64.63 / 254.30 on ms-H3.6M). Root Translation Error (RTE) drops to 2.56 m (ms-AIST) and 3.65 m (ms-H3.6M), while Root Orientation Error (ROE) reaches 69.23° and 53.39° respectively. The advantage persists and grows with more shots: at 4-shot, HumanMM maintains PA-MPJPE of 39.63 / 50.59 mm versus 83.77 / 85.19 mm for GVHMR. Foot sliding is best on ms-H3.6M across all shot counts (4.17–5.12 cm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HumanMM outperforms all three HMR baselines (SLAHMR, WHAM, GVHMR) on PA-MPJPE, WA-MPJPE, RTE, and ROE across every shot count and both sub-datasets. The largest gap is against SLAHMR, which suffers the worst orientation errors (ROE 96–118°) and translation errors (RTE 9.6–17.9 m), reflecting its lack of explicit multi-shot alignment. GVHMR is the strongest single-shot baseline (best WA-MPJPE among baselines at 231 mm on ms-AIST 2-shot) but still trails HumanMM by roughly 48% in WA-MPJPE and 30% in ROE, confirming that per-shot world-coordinate recovery without cross-shot alignment is insufficient. WHAM achieves the lowest foot sliding among baselines (2.75 cm on ms-AIST 2-shot) but is outperformed by HumanMM (2.66 cm) while being far worse on pose accuracy. For camera trajectory estimation, Masked LEAP-VO reduces RPE translation by ~50% over DPVO (0.92 m vs. 1.85 m on EMDB-1) and achieves the best RPE rotation (0.95°), validating the human-masking design in the BA step.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: world-coordinate human motion recovery from single-shot videos (SLAHMR, WHAM, GVHMR) and multi-shot human mesh recovery in camera space (Pavlakos et al., t-HMMR). It builds directly on LEAP-VO for camera trajectory estimation (extending it with human-region masking via SAM) and on WHAM's trajectory refiner for foot-sliding correction, while using GVHMR as the per-shot HMR backbone. The key conceptual departure is that prior multi-shot HMR work operated only in camera coordinates, and prior world-coordinate HMR assumed a single continuous camera view; HumanMM is the first to bridge both by introducing an orientation alignment module (via epipolar-geometry-based camera calibration) and a multi-shot transformer encoder (ms-HMR) that jointly handle the discontinuities at shot boundaries in world space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling world-coordinate motion recovery from multi-shot videos, this work unlocks a large and underutilized source of long-sequence motion data (sports broadcasts, concerts, talk shows) that existing single-shot pipelines discard or truncate. The ms-Motion benchmark, while constructed synthetically from AIST and Human3.6M, provides the first standardized evaluation protocol for multi-shot HMR with metrics (ROE, RTE, jitter, foot sliding) tailored to shot-transition artifacts. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is performance degradation under excessive shot transitions, and the synthetic nature of the benchmark (randomly splicing clips from different cameras of the same scene) may not fully capture the diversity of real in-the-wild multi-shot footage. Future work on scaling to genuinely wild multi-shot videos and handling more complex editing patterns (dissolves, split screens) remains open.
