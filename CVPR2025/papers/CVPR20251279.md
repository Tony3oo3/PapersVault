## Problem

Existing obfuscation-based defenses for collaborative inference rely on empirical heuristics to estimate task-irrelevant redundancy in intermediate features, with no rigorous quantification of such redundancy and no formal mathematical relationship between redundancy minimization and worst-case model inversion robustness. This leaves a critical gap in understanding how much privacy protection a given encoder actually provides against the strongest possible inversion adversary.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet, face crops for FaceScrub) within a split-inference architecture where a lightweight local encoder (first 2 conv layers of VGG11 or first 4 conv layers of ResNet-20, ~10% of total computation) produces intermediate feature vectors z that are transmitted to a cloud decoder for classification. During training, isotropic Gaussian noise (σ=0.025) is added to the deterministic encoder output, and a k-component GMM (k=3n) is fit to the noisy features to compute the conditional entropy loss; at inference, the same noisy features are sent to the cloud for the final class prediction.

## Method

The paper proves that the conditional entropy H(x|z) of the input given the intermediate feature provides an exponential lower bound on the minimal reconstruction MSE under any white-box inversion adversary (Theorem 1), establishing a direct link between information-theoretic uncertainty and worst-case robustness. To make this bound tractable, the encoding is decomposed into a deterministic mapping x→ẑ plus Gaussian noise ε, and the distribution of ẑ is estimated by a k-component GMM, yielding a closed-form differentiable lower bound on H(x|z) expressed solely in terms of the mixture weights πᵢ and covariance matrices Σᵢ (Theorem 2). The proposed CEM algorithm fits the GMM to the batch of noisy features, assigns each feature to its nearest cluster, updates the mixture parameters online, and computes a conditional entropy loss L_C that is minimized (i.e., H(x|z) is maximized) via gradient backpropagation. This loss is added to the existing defense loss with a weight λ (default 16), making CEM a plug-and-play module that can be embedded into any obfuscation-based defense—noise corruption, adversarial representation learning, or information filtering—without architectural changes or additional inference overhead.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (VGG11), integrating CEM into existing obfuscation defenses yields an average reconstruction MSE increase of 24.0% (training) and 12.9% (inference) across all six baselines, while maintaining prediction accuracy (88.91% → 88.92%). On TinyImageNet (ResNet-20), the average MSE gains are 19.4% (training) and 17.7% (inference). On FaceScrub, the gains are most pronounced at 48.2% (training) and 40.1% (inference), reflecting the stronger correlation between facial features and identity. The largest per-method improvement on CIFAR-10 is Dropout+CEM (MSE 0.0098→0.0129, +32%) and ResSFL+CEM (MSE 0.0146→0.0201, +38%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CEM is not a standalone defense but a plug-in enhancement; every baseline improves when CEM is added, with no accuracy loss. Among the six baselines on CIFAR-10, the largest relative MSE gains come from Dropout (+32% train MSE: 0.0098→0.0129) and ResSFL (+38% train MSE: 0.0146→0.0201), while the smallest gains are from Noise_Nopeek (+14%) and Noise_ARL (+14%), suggesting that methods already incorporating strong noise or adversarial signals leave less redundancy for CEM to exploit. PATROL benefits substantially (+24% train MSE: 0.0245→0.0304) and also sees a slight accuracy improvement (89.58%→89.67%). DistCorr shows a moderate gain (+22% train MSE: 0.0074→0.0090) with a small accuracy increase (89.52%→89.80%). On FaceScrub, where identity information is more tightly encoded in features, CEM amplifies all baselines by 40–48% in MSE, with Noise_ARL+CEM reaching the highest absolute MSE (0.0182 train, 0.0211 infer) while maintaining 80.33% accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new theoretical ground within the well-established obfuscation-based defense paradigm for collaborative inference. Prior methods (DistCorr, Dropout, PATROL, ResSFL, Noise_ARL, Noise_Nopeek) rely on empirical heuristics to estimate and remove task-irrelevant redundancy, while information-theoretic approaches (e.g., mutual information regularization, Fisher information bounds) constrain redundancy without linking it to worst-case inversion robustness. The key departure is establishing a formal lower bound on reconstruction MSE in terms of conditional entropy H(x|z) (Theorem 1) and deriving a differentiable, GMM-based surrogate (Theorem 2) that makes this bound tractable for gradient-based optimization, thereby providing the first rigorous mathematical bridge between redundancy minimization and worst-case MIA robustness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical guarantee that conditional entropy provides a lower bound on worst-case reconstruction MSE gives practitioners a principled, interpretable metric for evaluating and optimizing inversion robustness, moving the field beyond proxy-adversary heuristics. The CEM algorithm's plug-and-play nature (no architectural changes, no inference overhead) makes it immediately applicable to a wide range of existing defenses. Open questions include whether the Gaussian mixture assumption holds for high-dimensional or non-Gaussian feature distributions, how tight the entropy bound is in practice, and whether the approach extends to multi-modal or sequential (video) collaborative inference settings where the feature distribution may be more complex.
