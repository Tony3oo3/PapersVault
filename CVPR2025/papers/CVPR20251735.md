## Problem

Diffusion models can verbatim-reproduce training images, raising copyright and privacy concerns, yet existing inference-time mitigations (prompt perturbation, cross-attention masking) are tied to specific memorization causes and fail to generalize across scenarios. This paper identifies a previously uncharacterized mechanism—an attraction basin in the denoising trajectory that CFG steers samples into—and shows that prior methods do not address this dynamical-systems-level cause.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251735/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates entirely at inference time on a pre-trained text-to-image diffusion model (e.g., Stable Diffusion v1.4/v2.1), consuming a text prompt and a random Gaussian noise initialization to produce a 512×512 RGB image. Crucially, it requires no modification of model weights, no access to or alteration of the prompt, and no additional forward passes beyond the standard conditional and unconditional noise predictions already computed during CFG denoising, making it applicable to any conditioning mechanism (class labels, etc.), not just text.

## Method

The paper formalizes memorization as an attraction basin in the diffusion trajectory: a region in state space where applying CFG deterministically steers samples toward a memorized training image regardless of initialization. The mitigation works by initially applying zero CFG (or a new 'opposite guidance' that applies negative CFG to push the trajectory away from the basin sooner), while tracking the L2 magnitude of the conditional guidance term ‖ε_θ(x_t, e_p) − ε_θ(x_t, e_∅)‖². The transition point—where the trajectory exits the basin—is detected as the first local minimum in this magnitude curve; after this point, standard positive CFG is applied to produce text-aligned, non-memorized images. The transition point can be static (a fixed timestep, e.g., t=500 for LAION-10k fine-tuned SDv2.1) or dynamic (varying per prompt and initialization), and the method handles both cases with no additional computational cost.

## Results

In Scenario 1 (fine-tuned SDv2.1 on LAION-10k), the proposed Static Transition Point (STP) method reduces 95th-percentile similarity to 0.2857, the lowest among all methods, while maintaining a competitive CLIP score of 0.2976 (vs. 0.3027 for no mitigation). The OG+STP variant achieves the best FID of 15.6679 (vs. 16.84 for no mitigation) with a CLIP score of 0.3020. In Scenario 3 (data duplication), the Dynamic Transition Point (DTP) method reduces 95th-percentile similarity to 0.5885, outperforming Wen et al. (0.7747 at l_target=3) and matching their aggressive setting (0.6038 at l_target=1) while preserving a comparable CLIP score (0.3020 vs. 0.3050).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all three baselines on the primary memorization metric (SSCD similarity) across all tested scenarios. Somepalli et al. [24] (random word/number insertion) only modestly reduces similarity (0.5254/0.5416 vs. 0.6504 baseline) while degrading CLIP score, and fails to generalize beyond its original fine-tuning scenario. Wen et al. [28] (prompt embedding perturbation) achieves a meaningful similarity reduction (0.3853) but at the cost of noticeably lower CLIP score (0.2895) and poor visual quality; in Scenario 3, the proposed DTP method (0.5885) beats Wen et al.'s conservative setting (0.7747) and matches their aggressive setting (0.6038) with better CLIP alignment. Ren et al. [20] (cross-attention masking) performs worst, barely reducing similarity (0.6028) and significantly degrading FID (20.29), confirming its reliance on trigger-token presence. The key advantage of the proposed approach is generality: it works across all four memorization scenarios (overfitting, class-conditional, data duplication, trigger tokens) without requiring prompt modification or knowledge of the memorization cause.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022) as its core mechanism, repurposing it not as a fixed inference-time tool but as a trajectory-steering force whose timing determines whether memorization occurs. It draws on Wen et al.'s (ICLR 2024) observation that the magnitude of conditional noise prediction is informative for memorization detection, but reframes this signal as evidence of an 'attraction basin' in the denoising trajectory using dynamical systems language. The key departure from prior mitigation work (Somepalli et al., Ren et al., Wen et al.) is that those methods operate at the prompt or cross-attention level and are specific to text conditioning, whereas this paper intervenes at the trajectory level, making it applicable to any conditioning mechanism without modifying prompts or model weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The attraction basin framing provides a unified conceptual explanation for memorization across diverse causes (data duplication, small-dataset fine-tuning, trigger tokens), and the proposed delayed-CFG / opposite-guidance technique is a zero-overhead, prompt-agnostic mitigation that prior methods could not achieve. A notable limitation is that the approach requires first detecting whether a prompt is memorized (via a two-pass denoising or a threshold on conditional guidance magnitude), which adds a practical dependency. Open questions include whether the attraction basin structure persists in video diffusion, latent diffusion with different schedulers, or non-diffusion generative architectures, and whether the transition point concept generalizes beyond the Stable Diffusion family tested here.
