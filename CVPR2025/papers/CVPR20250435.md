## Problem

Existing re-label based unlearning methods implicitly optimize only the forgetting objective while lacking explicit supervision over the retention of remaining classes, which disturbs the pre-trained model's output distribution and degrades performance on non-forgotten classes. This paper addresses the gap by providing a theoretical decomposition of the unlearning loss and a practical method that simultaneously enforces both forgetting and retention without requiring access to remaining data or intervention during pre-training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250435/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained classification model (e.g., ResNet-18, ViT-S, Swin-T) and the forget data (images with their class labels) as input, operating under the strict constraint that no remaining data is available. It produces an unlearned model whose output logits have the target class probability driven to zero while preserving the relative probability magnitudes of all other classes. The unlearning is performed via a single forward-backward pass per batch using only the forget samples, with a frozen copy of the original model providing soft targets.

## Method

DELETE decomposes the standard KL-divergence unlearning loss into a forgetting term (binary KL over the target class vs. all others) and a retention term (conditional KL over the remaining K−1 classes), showing that re-label methods are a degenerate special case that only optimizes the forgetting term. To supervise the retention term without remaining data, the method applies a mask to the frozen original model's logits—setting the target class logit to −∞—then normalizes the result to form a soft target distribution that encodes 'dark knowledge' (relative class probabilities). The unlearned model is trained to minimize KL divergence to this masked target, simultaneously driving the target class probability to zero and preserving the relative magnitudes of all remaining class probabilities. The approach requires only a modification to the loss function, no architectural changes, and no access to remaining data, making it a drop-in replacement for any class-centric unlearning scenario.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 single-class forgetting, DELETE achieves 0% Acc_ft (complete forgetting) and 95.03% Acc_rt, with an H-Mean of 96.00 and 0% MIA success rate—nearly matching the retrain upper bound (95.20% Acc_rt, H-Mean 96.09) with only a 0.17% gap. On CIFAR-100, DELETE reaches 0% Acc_ft and 76.57% Acc_rt (H-Mean 71.47), outperforming the best no-remaining-data baseline (Influence Unlearn: 71.03% Acc_rt, H-Mean 68.96) by over 5 points. In the challenging 20-class forgetting setting on CIFAR-100, DELETE maintains 79.30% Acc_rt (H-Mean 77.72) while all other methods drop below 65% Acc_rt. DELETE also generalizes across architectures (VGGNet-16, Swin-T, ViT-S) and downstream tasks (face recognition, backdoor defense, semantic segmentation) without requiring remaining data or pre-training intervention.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DELETE outperforms all no-remaining-data baselines by a wide margin: on CIFAR-10, it achieves 0% Acc_ft vs. 4.50% (Boundary Shrink [9]), 8.60% (Learn to Unlearn [8]), and 18.80% (Bad Teacher without remaining data [11]), while simultaneously attaining 95.03% Acc_rt vs. 84.56%, 90.02%, and 87.13% respectively. Against methods that do use remaining data, DELETE matches or exceeds them without that access: it achieves 95.03% Acc_rt vs. 94.94% (SCRUB [27]) and 94.98% (Bad Teacher with remaining data [11]), both at 0% Acc_ft. Fisher Forget [19] performs catastrophically (68.30% Acc_ft, 59.73% Acc_rt on CIFAR-10), confirming that Fisher-based parameter perturbation is unsuitable for class-centric unlearning. Influence Unlearn [25] is the closest no-remaining-data competitor (0.60% Acc_ft, 93.21% Acc_rt on CIFAR-10), but DELETE still leads by 1.82 points in Acc_rt and achieves perfect forgetting. In the multi-class setting (20 classes on CIFAR-100), the gap widens dramatically: DELETE retains 79.30% Acc_rt while the best competitor (Boundary Shrink) drops to 61.90%, a 17.4-point advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Decoupled Knowledge Distillation (DKD) [61], adopting its KL-divergence decomposition into binary and conditional components as the mathematical backbone for the unlearning loss. It positions itself against the prominent re-label family of unlearning methods (Random Label, Boundary Shrink/Expand, Learn to Unlearn), showing via a unified theoretical framework that these methods are special cases of the general unlearning loss that implicitly optimize forgetting while leaving the retention term unsupervised. Unlike Fisher Forgetting, SCRUB, and Saliency Unlearn, DELETE requires neither access to remaining data nor intervention during pre-training, carving out a stricter and more practical unlearning setting that the authors argue is necessary for real-world MaaS deployments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean theoretical decomposition provides a principled lens for understanding why existing re-label methods degrade remaining-class performance, and the resulting mask-distillation solution is remarkably simple (a single masked softmax + KL loss) yet achieves near-retrain accuracy across datasets, architectures, and multi-class settings. The generalization to face recognition, backdoor defense, and semantic segmentation suggests the loss design is task-agnostic, which could accelerate adoption in privacy-sensitive deployments. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-class-centric unlearning (e.g., removing specific data points within a class), whether it scales to very large foundation models, and whether the distillation target from a frozen model remains valid when the forgetting set is a large fraction of training data.
