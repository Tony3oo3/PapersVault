## Problem

Deep learning denoisers achieve state-of-the-art quality but are impractical on edge devices due to high compute and memory demands. Existing LUT-based alternatives for color denoising either process channels independently (ignoring critical RGB correlations) or require prohibitive storage when combining spatial and channel dimensions, leaving a gap between denoising quality and deployability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250083/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a noisy RGB image and produces a denoised RGB image of the same spatial dimensions. At inference, the image is processed in overlapping 2×2 (spatial) or 1×2 (channel-pair) patches, each rotated four times (0°, 90°, 180°, 270°) with outputs averaged. Pixel values are quantized at 2⁴ intervals to form LUT indices, and final outputs are recovered via 4D simplex or 3D tetrahedral interpolation. The entire model fits in ~500 KB of storage, well within L2 cache limits of modern mobile SoCs.

## Method

DnLUT introduces two complementary modules. The Pairwise Channel Mixer (PCM) reorganizes RGB into three overlapping pairs (RG, GB, BR) and processes each through a parallel branch of a 1×2 convolution (depth 2) followed by cascaded 1×1 convolutions, so every lookup consumes four pixel values and yields one channel output—capturing inter-channel correlation while remaining convertible to 4D LUTs. The L-shaped convolution kernel is designed so that its four rotations cover each surrounding pixel exactly once (no overlap), enabling conversion to 3D LUTs instead of 4D LUTs and reducing storage by 17× while preserving the same effective receptive field. A DnNet combining these modules is trained end-to-end with MSE loss, then post-converted into optimized LUTs via exhaustive input enumeration and 2000 iterations of LUT-aware fine-tuning. The PCM also functions as a plug-in that can be attached to existing LUT-based methods, adding >1 dB CPSNR with only 12% runtime and 8% storage overhead.

## Results

On the Gaussian denoising benchmark (CBSD68, σ=25), DnLUT achieves 29.88 dB CPSNR, surpassing the strongest LUT-based competitor SPF-LUT (28.56 dB) by 1.32 dB while using only 17% of its storage (518 KB vs. 30,178 KB). On real-world SIDD, DnLUT reaches 35.44 dB CPSNR, outperforming classical CBM3D (30.14 dB) by over 5 dB. In terms of efficiency, DnLUT requires only 0.1% of DnCNN's energy consumption (687.34M pJ vs. 542.53G pJ) and runs 20× faster (88 ms vs. 1,923 ms at 512×512 on Snapdragon 8 Gen 2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among LUT-based methods, DnLUT consistently outperforms all four competitors: SR-LUT by ~3.2 dB, MuLUT by ~1.8 dB, RC-LUT by ~1.8 dB, and SPF-LUT by 1.32 dB on CBSD68 (σ=25), with the gap widening at higher noise levels (e.g., +0.7 dB over SPF-LUT at σ=50). Against classical methods, DnLUT surpasses MC-WNNM by ~0.6 dB on Gaussian benchmarks but trails CBM3D by ~0.8 dB on synthetic noise; however, on real-world SIDD, DnLUT exceeds CBM3D by 5.3 dB CPSNR and MC-WNNM by 6.0 dB, demonstrating superior generalization to sensor noise. Compared to DNN baselines, DnLUT sacrifices 1.4 dB CPSNR versus DnCNN and 1.9 dB versus SwinIR on CBSD68 (σ=25), but compensates with 20× faster inference and 0.1% of DnCNN's energy cost, making it the only method deployable on resource-constrained edge hardware. The PCM plug-in module further validates the channel-mixing contribution: adding it to SPF-LUT yields +1.16 dB on CBSD68, confirming that inter-channel correlation modeling is the primary source of DnLUT's advantage over prior spatial-only LUT designs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LUT-based image restoration lineage established by SR-LUT [10], which introduced constrained receptive field training and rotation ensemble for converting CNNs into lookup tables. It adopts the multi-LUT group fusion architecture from SPF-LUT [14] and the LUT-aware fine-tuning procedure from MuLUT [12], but departs fundamentally from all prior LUT methods by addressing the channel-spatial coupling problem specific to color denoising. Existing LUT approaches process channels independently (spatial-wise 2×2 kernels) or ignore spatial structure (channel-wise 1×1 kernels); DnLUT's Pairwise Channel Mixer and L-shaped kernel are designed to capture both dimensions simultaneously within the storage constraints of 3D/4D LUTs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DnLUT demonstrates that high-quality color denoising can be deployed on resource-constrained edge devices (500 KB storage, 0.1% of DnCNN's energy), a practical gap that has limited real-world adoption of deep denoisers. The PCM plug-in module, which adds ~1 dB to any existing LUT-based method with only 12% runtime overhead, is a broadly useful component. However, a significant quality gap to DNN methods (≈1.4 dB below DnCNN, ≈1.9 dB below SwinIR on CBSD68 σ=25) remains, and it is unclear whether the LUT paradigm can close this gap without prohibitive storage growth. Open questions include generalization to other restoration tasks (inpainting, deblurring), higher noise levels, and whether the pairwise channel decomposition is optimal compared to learned channel mixing.
