## Problem

Existing approaches to handling unsafe content in vision-language models rely on unlearning, which erases the model's knowledge of NSFW concepts and thereby limits its ability to distinguish between safe and unsafe content. This paper argues for an awareness paradigm instead: the model should retain knowledge of unsafe content while being able to explicitly identify it, redirect queries toward safe alternatives, or expose unsafe content under controlled conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251223/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images and text prompts (either safe or unsafe) as queries and produces retrieved images, retrieved text captions, or a binary safe/unsafe classification. A key operational detail is the safety traversal mechanism: given a query embedding, the system computes its hyperbolic distance from the origin and can shift it along the geodesic toward either the safe region (closer to origin) or the unsafe region (farther from origin), using empirically determined boundary thresholds per content type (safe text, safe image, unsafe text, unsafe image). Training uses ViSU quadruplets pairing each safe image-text pair with its corresponding unsafe variant.

## Method

HySAC fine-tunes CLIP's ViT-L/14 encoders (via LoRA, r=16) in the Lorentz model of hyperbolic space, projecting Euclidean encoder outputs onto the hyperboloid via the exponential map with learnable scaling factors. The training objective combines two components: (1) a hyperbolic safety contrastive loss that aligns image-text pairs within each safety class and across safety classes using negative Lorentzian distance, and (2) a hyperbolic safety entailment loss that enforces the inequality chain g_T(T) ≪ g_I(I) ≪ g_T(T*) ≪ g_I(I*) via entailment cone constraints, placing safe content near the origin and unsafe content in outer conical regions. At inference, a traversal mechanism moves query embeddings along the geodesic toward the origin (for safe retrieval) or away from it (for unsafe retrieval) to a target boundary computed from the mean distance of each content type, enabling dynamic redirection of unsafe queries to safe but semantically relevant results.

## Results

On the ViSU test set, HySAC achieves 49.8% R@1 for safe text-to-image retrieval and 48.2% R@1 for safe image-to-text retrieval, matching or exceeding all baselines. The headline safety-aware retrieval (redirecting unsafe queries to safe content) shows dramatic gains: 30.5% R@1 and 62.8% R@10 for T*-to-I∪I*, compared to 8.0% R@1 for Safe-CLIP and 2.0% for CLIP. For unsafe content retrieval (Table 2), HySAC reaches 81.4% R@1 on T*-to-I*, surpassing CLIP (73.1%) and Safe-CLIP (58.0%). On NSFW classification (Table 5), HySAC achieves 99.5% accuracy on NudeNet and 78.9% on Mixed NSFW, outperforming all dedicated classifiers except in false-positive rate on Mixed NSFW.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HySAC outperforms all nine baselines on the primary safety-aware retrieval task. Against Safe-CLIP [55], the strongest unlearning baseline, HySAC improves T*-to-I∪I* R@1 from 8.0% to 30.5% (+22.5 pts) and I*-to-T∪T* R@1 from 19.1% to 42.1% (+23.0 pts), while also matching or exceeding Safe-CLIP on safe-only retrieval (49.8 vs 45.9 R@1 T-to-I). Against the hyperbolic VLMs MERU [17] and HyCoCLIP [52], HySAC achieves substantially higher safety-aware retrieval (30.5 vs 2.2/2.8 R@1) while maintaining competitive safe retrieval. Even the ViSU-fine-tuned hyperbolic variants (MERU*, HyCoCLIP*) fail at safety-aware retrieval (2.3% and 1.5% R@1), confirming that the safety entailment loss is essential. On NSFW classification (Table 5), HySAC achieves 99.5% accuracy on NudeNet, beating CLIP-classifier [65] (97.3%), NudeNet [4] (91.2%), and NSFW-CNN [37] (85.3%), though it incurs a higher FPR (16.5% vs 0.1%) on the broader Mixed NSFW set where CLIP-classifier achieves 76.9% accuracy with near-zero false positives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hyperbolic vision-language model line (MERU, HyCoCLIP) by repurposing entailment cones not for modality or compositional hierarchies but for a safety hierarchy: safe content near the origin, unsafe content farther away. It departs conceptually from Safe-CLIP and other unlearning approaches, which erase NSFW knowledge entirely, by instead retaining both safe and unsafe representations in a structured geometric arrangement that enables dynamic traversal between them. The exponential map projection, Lorentz distance, and entailment loss formulations are inherited directly from Desai et al. and Ganea et al., while the safety-specific inequality chain (Eq. 7) and the traversal mechanism are novel contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from unlearning to awareness addresses a real limitation of prior NSFW-removal methods: they destroy the model's ability to distinguish safe from unsafe content, reducing interpretability and user agency. The traversal mechanism provides a practical tool for content moderation pipelines that need both detection and controlled redirection. However, the evaluation is largely confined to the ViSU dataset and CLIP-scale models, and the real-world NSFW retrieval results (Table 4) show mixed gains over Safe-CLIP, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger VLMs or broader safety categories. The paper also acknowledges the absence of datasets with real unsafe data and correlated safe alternatives, limiting the generality of the safety traversal evaluation.
