## Problem

The Matching Training Trajectories (MTT) method for dataset distillation suffers from three interrelated limitations: the expert trajectory generated by mini-batch SGD is highly oscillatory and non-convex, leading to unstable guidance vectors that impede distillation; the distillation process converges slowly due to inconsistent optimization directions; and storing all ~50 intermediate expert models incurs prohibitive memory costs, especially for large-scale models. The paper identifies that the root cause is the non-convex zigzag nature of SGD trajectories, which makes the per-step guidance vectors nearly perpendicular to each other as training progresses.

## Input / Output

The method takes a large real dataset (CIFAR-10/CIFAR-100 at 32×32, Tiny-ImageNet at 64×64) and a surrogate ConvNet architecture (128 filters, 3×3 kernels, instance normalization, ReLU, average pooling) as input. It first pre-generates an expert trajectory by training the surrogate on real data for 50 epochs, then distills a synthetic dataset of C×ipc images per class. The output is a compact synthetic image set with labels that, when used to train fresh random-initialization networks over 1000 iterations, achieves accuracy comparable to training on the full dataset. ZCA whitening and Differentiable Siamese Augmentation are applied during both trajectory generation and distillation.

## Method

MCT replaces the raw SGD expert trajectory with a convexified trajectory τ_conv that linearly interpolates between the initial model θ_T^(0) and the final model θ_T^(K), with waypoint positions determined by the cumulative per-layer L2 norm of parameter changes along the original trajectory (Equ. 7). To enrich parameter-space exploration, two interpolated points from the original trajectory are inserted, converting the single line segment into a polyline of three convex segments (Equ. 8–9). A continuous sampling strategy (Equ. 10) allows selecting any real-valued timestep c∈[0,K] on the trajectory via bilinear interpolation of β values, rather than the discrete integer sampling used by MTT. The distillation objective remains the normalized L2 distance between student and expert parameters (Equ. 2), but the convexified guidance vectors always point from initialization toward the optimum, yielding stable and rapid convergence. Storage drops from O(KW) to O(W) since only the start/end models, I interpolated models, and K scalar β values need to be retained.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, MCT achieves 48.5% (ipc=1), 66.0% (ipc=10), and 72.3% (ipc=50) validation accuracy, outperforming MTT (46.3/65.3/71.6) and PP (46.4/65.5/71.9) across all settings. On CIFAR-100, MCT reaches 24.5% (ipc=1) and 42.5% (ipc=10), surpassing MTT by 0.2 and 2.4 points respectively, though PP remains slightly ahead at ipc=50 (48.4 vs. 46.8). On Tiny-ImageNet, MCT leads at ipc=1 (9.6% vs. MTT's 8.8%) but trails MTT at ipc=10 and ipc=50. MCT also converges 200–1,200 distillation iterations faster than MTT and requires only ~8% of the storage for expert trajectories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline MTT [1], MCT wins on CIFAR-10 at all ipc levels (e.g., 72.3 vs. 71.6 at ipc=50) and on CIFAR-100 at ipc=1 and ipc=10 (42.5 vs. 40.1), but slightly trails MTT on CIFAR-100 ipc=50 (46.8 vs. 47.7) and Tiny-ImageNet ipc=10/50 (22.6 vs. 23.2; 27.6 vs. 28.0). Compared to the strongest non-trajectory baseline PP [14], MCT surpasses it on all CIFAR-10 settings (48.5/66.0/72.3 vs. 46.4/65.5/71.9) but falls behind on CIFAR-100 (e.g., 46.8 vs. 48.4 at ipc=50). MCT substantially outperforms all single-step and distribution-matching baselines: over DC [23] by 20+ points on CIFAR-10 ipc=1, over DSA [21] by 13.9 points on CIFAR-100 ipc=1, over CAFE [19] by 11.6 points on CIFAR-100 ipc=1, and over DM [22] by 13.1 points on CIFAR-100 ipc=1. The paper also reports that MCT converges 200–1,200 distillation iterations earlier than MTT and reduces expert-trajectory storage to roughly 8% of MTT's requirement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-step trajectory matching branch of dataset distillation, building directly on MTT (Cazenavette et al., 2022) and its subsequent refinements (Du et al. 2023 for noise injection, Cui et al. 2023 for memory-efficient decomposition, Guo et al. 2023 for difficulty alignment). The key conceptual departure is reframing the trajectory matching objective as providing 'guidance vectors' (magnitude and direction of the next parameter update) at each point in parameter space, then replacing the oscillatory SGD trajectory with a convex combination path between the initial and final models. This geometric simplification—reducing a 50-point non-convex path to a few linear segments—enables continuous sampling and O(W) storage, properties absent from all prior trajectory-matching methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The storage reduction from O(KW) to O(W) is practically significant for scaling dataset distillation to larger surrogate models (e.g., LLMs), where storing 50+ full model checkpoints becomes prohibitive. However, the accuracy gains over MTT are modest (1–2% on most metrics), and experiments are confined to small ConvNet surrogates on CIFAR-10/100 and Tiny-ImageNet, leaving open whether the convexification preserves enough non-convex structure for complex architectures or larger-scale distillation. A key open question is whether the linear interpolation between start and end points loses critical intermediate structure that the original SGD trajectory captures, particularly for tasks where the loss landscape has multiple basins.
