## Problem

Current vision-language models rely on excessively long visual token sequences (e.g., 576 for LLaVA-1.5, 2880 for LLaVA-NeXT) that are far longer than the accompanying text tokens, imposing heavy computational and memory costs. The paper demonstrates that visual tokens from popular encoders like CLIP and SigLIP contain significant redundancy—most tokens receive near-zero attention and carry minimal information—raising the question of whether all visual tokens are truly necessary for downstream LLM processing.

## Input / Output

VisionZip operates on standard RGB images (336×336 for LLaVA-1.5, 672×672 for LLaVA-NeXT) or video frames (8 frames for Video-LLaVA) paired with a text prompt. It intercepts the visual token stream at the vision encoder's second-to-last layer and outputs a compressed set of visual tokens (e.g., 64–192 out of 576, or 160–640 out of 2880) that are then projected and fed to the LLM alongside the text tokens to generate a natural-language response. Because selection is text-agnostic, the same compressed token set can be cached in the KV cache and reused across multi-turn dialogues without reprocessing.

## Method

VisionZip is a two-stage, text-agnostic token compression applied at the vision encoder output. First, Dominant Token Selection identifies the most informative tokens by reading attention scores from the encoder's -2 layer: for CLIP-based models it selects tokens most attended to by the CLS token, while for SigLIP it uses the mean attention each token receives from all others. Second, Contextual Token Merging prevents loss of fine-grained detail by uniformly splitting the remaining non-dominant tokens into target and merge groups, computing key-value dot-product similarity, and averaging each merge token into its most similar target to form compact contextual tokens. Optionally, a 30-minute fine-tune of only the multimodal projector (using 1/10 of the LLaVA-1.5 instruction data) re-aligns the reduced visual space with the LLM embedding space. Unlike prior methods (FastV, SparseVLM) that prune tokens inside the LLM based on text-visual attention, VisionZip's text-agnostic design avoids misalignment with the encoder's information aggregation and naturally supports multi-turn conversation via KV-cache reuse.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5 (576→64 tokens, 88.9% reduction), VisionZip achieves 94.0% average accuracy across 11 benchmarks, outperforming FastV (75.6%) by 18.4% and SparseVLM (85.8%) by 8.2%. With minimal projector fine-tuning (30 min on 8×A800), VisionZip† reaches 95.2% at 64 tokens, retaining 95.2% of the vanilla 576-token model. On LLaVA-NeXT (2880→160 tokens, 94.4% reduction), VisionZip achieves 92.0% average vs. SparseVLM's 86.4%. In terms of efficiency, VisionZip reduces prefilling time by 7.8× and total inference time by 3.0× on LLaVA-NeXT 7B, compared to only 1.8× and 1.3× for FastV.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VisionZip consistently outperforms both FastV and SparseVLM across all token budgets and model architectures. On LLaVA-1.5 at 64 tokens (88.9% reduction), VisionZip achieves 94.0% average accuracy, beating FastV by 18.4 percentage points and SparseVLM by 8.2 points; the gap widens further at higher compression ratios. On LLaVA-NeXT at 160 tokens (94.4% reduction), VisionZip reaches 92.0% vs. SparseVLM's 86.4% (a 5.6-point margin), and with fine-tuning (VisionZip†) the gap grows to 9.1 points. On Video-LLaVA (2048→136 tokens), VisionZip achieves 93.2% average vs. SparseVLM's 86.5% and FastV's 52.1%, with a particularly large 37.2-point advantage on MSRVTT-QA. In efficiency, VisionZip achieves 7.8× prefilling speedup and 3.0× total inference speedup on LLaVA-NeXT 7B, substantially exceeding FastV (1.8×/1.3×) and SparseVLM (1.7×/1.2×), because VisionZip reduces tokens before the LLM forward pass rather than progressively pruning during it.

## Relation to Prior Work

VisionZip departs from prior efficient VLM methods (FastV, SparseVLM) that select visual tokens based on text-relevant attention within the LLM, instead proposing a text-agnostic selection at the vision encoder level using CLS-token or mean-received attention scores. It draws on the 'Attention Sink' phenomenon observed in LLMs and the 'global token' concept from semantic segmentation to explain why a few proxy tokens dominate information aggregation. The method repurposes the general idea of attention-based token pruning but relocates it from the LLM forward pass to the vision encoder output, adding a similarity-based merging step for remaining tokens to preserve contextual information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8× prefilling speedup and the ability to make a 13B model faster than a 7B model while performing better have direct practical implications for edge deployment, robotics, and multi-turn dialogue systems where latency is critical. The finding that text-agnostic selection outperforms text-relevant selection challenges the prevailing assumption in efficient VLM research and suggests the community should invest in vision encoders with lower inherent redundancy rather than simply increasing token counts. Open questions include whether the attention-based proxy for token importance generalizes to novel or out-of-distribution images, and whether the 30-minute projector fine-tuning can be eliminated entirely for truly zero-shot deployment.
