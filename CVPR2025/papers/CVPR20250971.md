## Problem

Existing 3DGS-based full-body avatar methods struggle to simultaneously achieve fine-grained control of facial expressions and body movements, capture high-frequency appearance details (loose clothing, hair, teeth), and run in real-time on resource-constrained AR devices requiring high-resolution stereo rendering. The core tension is that powerful networks like StyleUNet can model complex pose-dependent non-rigid deformations but are too parameter-heavy for mobile inference, while lightweight alternatives sacrifice visual fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250971/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Training consumes multi-view RGB video (60 views: 48 full-body + 12 face-region at 4K resolution, ~6k frames per sequence) with per-frame SMPLX registrations. At inference, the avatar is driven by SMPLX pose and expression parameters, or by audio through an ASR-LLM-TTS pipeline with an Audio2BS model generating expression coefficients. The output is a drivable hybrid mesh-Gaussian avatar (~220k Gaussians on ~45k faces) that renders stereo images at 1500×2000 resolution at 90 FPS on Apple Vision Pro, with the student model achieving 150+ FPS on an RTX 4090.

## Method

TaoAvatar constructs a personalized clothed parametric template (SMPLX++) by extending SMPLX with clothing, hair, and shoe geometry via NeuS2 reconstruction and robust skinning transfer, then binds ~220k 3D Gaussians to mesh triangles in a local barycentric coordinate system. A large StyleUNet teacher network learns pose-dependent non-rigid deformation maps in 2D texture space via front/back orthogonal projection of the T-pose mesh. The key innovation is a knowledge distillation step that 'bakes' the teacher's Gaussian deformation into a compact 5-layer MLP student operating on mesh vertices, supervised by a non-rigid deformation loss and a semantic loss to prevent clothing-body intersection. Two lightweight learnable blend shapes (position and color, 28 dimensions total) driven by separate head and body mapping networks compensate for residual Gaussian-level deformations like hair fluttering and self-shadowing. This multi-stage pipeline (teacher pre-training → baking → blend-shape fine-tuning) decouples geometry deformation from appearance variation, enabling the student to match teacher quality at 10× the inference speed.

## Results

On the full-body talking task (TalkBody4D), the student model achieves 33.81 PSNR / 0.9689 SSIM / 0.0644 LPIPS for novel view synthesis and 28.38 PSNR / 0.9389 SSIM / 0.0887 LPIPS for novel gestures and expressions, outperforming all baselines on PSNR and SSIM. The student model runs at 156 FPS at 1500×2000 resolution on an RTX 4090, roughly 10× faster than the teacher network (16 FPS) and 3–7× faster than competing methods (16–55 FPS). On the face region specifically, the student achieves 27.80 PSNR and 0.8975 SSIM for novel views, the highest among all methods. On complex motions and expressions (Table 2), the student reaches 32.72 PSNR / 0.9579 SSIM, matching the teacher (32.80 / 0.9533) while being ~10× faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TaoAvatar (student) outperforms all four baselines on PSNR and SSIM for both novel view and novel gesture/expression synthesis. Against the closest competitor AnimatableGS [34], the student gains +1.31 PSNR (33.81 vs 32.50) and +0.009 SSIM on novel views, while running ~10× faster (156 vs 16 FPS). 3DGS-Avatar [46] and GaussianAvatar [20] trail by 4.9 and 7.2 PSNR respectively, with the paper attributing 3DGS-Avatar's gap to the low-frequency bias of its MLP-based deformation and GaussianAvatar's to SMPLX's limited topology for loose clothing. MeshAvatar [10] is the slowest (22 FPS) and weakest on face-region metrics (24.55 PSNR), as its pure-mesh representation cannot capture fine details like hair. Notably, the student model slightly exceeds its own teacher on PSNR/SSIM (33.81 vs 33.45) while being ~10× faster, demonstrating that the distillation and blend-shape compensation effectively recover the teacher's quality in a lightweight form.

## Relation to Prior Work

TaoAvatar sits at the intersection of three research threads: parametric human models (SMPLX, extended here to SMPLX++ with clothing geometry via skinning transfer), 3D Gaussian Splatting as an explicit rendering representation, and the front/back projection strategy from AnimatableGS for learning pose-dependent deformation maps. The key architectural departure is a teacher-student distillation pipeline: a large StyleUNet teacher captures high-frequency non-rigid deformations in 2D texture space, and its output is 'baked' into a compact 5-layer MLP student operating on mesh vertices, supplemented by lightweight learnable blend shapes for Gaussians. This contrasts with prior 3DGS avatar methods (GaussianAvatar, 3DGS-Avatar, AnimatableGS) that either use monolithic networks too heavy for mobile inference or learn implicit templates from scratch, losing the fine-grained facial and gesture controls native to SMPLX.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is the demonstrated 90 FPS stereo rendering at 2K on Apple Vision Pro, which is the first full-body talking avatar pipeline shown to run in real-time on a consumer AR headset, opening the door to e-commerce live streaming and holographic communication applications. The TalkBody4D dataset (4 identities, 60 views, ~6k frames each with synchronized audio) fills a gap in existing full-body motion datasets that lack talking-specific content. A stated limitation is that flexible clothing under out-of-distribution exaggerated poses remains challenging, and the authors suggest integrating GNN-based cloth simulators as a future direction. The broader open question is whether the teacher-student baking paradigm generalizes beyond per-identity avatars to population-level or generalizable models.
