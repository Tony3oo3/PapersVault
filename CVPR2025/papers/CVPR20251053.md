## Problem

CLIP-based prompt tuning suffers from the Base-New Trade-off (BNT): as learnable prompts overfit to base (target) classes, generalization to new (unseen) classes degrades. Existing remedies impose constraints or auxiliary losses on the same prompt vector, but the optimization directions for base and new tasks remain mutually exclusive within a single parameter space, preventing a true global optimum.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251053/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images and class-name text prompts (via CLIP's 'A photo of a [CLASS]' template) and outputs per-class matching probabilities through cosine similarity between frozen ViT image features and text encoder features. As a plug-and-play module, DPC wraps any existing prompt-tuning backbone (CoOp, MaPLe, PromptSRC, PromptKD) without altering their input format or requiring external data beyond the base-class training split; at inference, a single hyperparameter ω_b controls how much the base-optimized parallel prompt contributes to the final classification score, while ω_n≈0 ensures new-class scores rely almost entirely on the frozen backbone prompt.

## Method

DPC introduces a dual-prompt architecture: after standard backbone fine-tuning yields a tuned prompt P, a parallel prompt P′ is initialized as a clone of P and then independently optimized on base classes via a Dynamic Hard Negative Optimizer that uses P as a query to sample Top-K hard negatives and trains P′ with a symmetric InfoNCE contrastive loss. A Weighting-Decoupling module then linearly combines the two prompts with task-specific coefficients—ω_b·P′+(1−ω_b)·P for base inference and ω_n·P′+(1−ω_n)·P for new inference (with ω_n→0)—so that base-class gains from P′ never contaminate new-class generalization. An L2-normalization Feature Filtering step preserves the global text-feature distribution of the base classes during P′ optimization, and the authors prove feature-channel invariance of the prompt vectors throughout training, providing theoretical justification for the linear weighting scheme.

## Results

On the average of 11 base-to-new generalization datasets, DPC improves the Harmonic Mean (HM) of base and new accuracy by 1.29 points over CoOp (76.13 vs. 74.84), 1.04 over MaPLe (79.12 vs. 78.08), 1.17 over PromptSRC (80.04 vs. 78.87), and 0.32 over PromptKD (83.91 vs. 83.59). The gains come entirely from base-class accuracy (e.g., CoOp base rises from 81.98% to 85.15%), while new-class accuracy is preserved exactly (e.g., 68.84% for CoOp in both cases). On cross-dataset and cross-domain transfer (Table 2), DPC improves source ImageNet accuracy (e.g., 71.25→71.80 for CoOp, 72.42→74.43 for PromptKD) while maintaining identical cross-dataset and cross-domain generalization scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPC is evaluated as a plug-and-play module on four backbone prompt learners (CoOp, MaPLe, PromptSRC, PromptKD) and compared against the leading plug-and-play method DePT. Across all four backbones, DPC consistently improves base-class accuracy by 2.4–5.4 points (e.g., CoOp: 81.98→85.15; MaPLe: 83.52→85.93; PromptSRC: 83.45→86.10; PromptKD: 86.86→87.55) while preserving new-class accuracy exactly, yielding HM gains of 0.32–1.29 points. Against DePT (which decouples at the feature level), DPC achieves superior or equal HM performance across all three tested backbones (Fig. 5), which the authors attribute to prompt-level decoupling providing a broader optimization space. DPC also surpasses the prior SOTA PromptKD on multiple individual datasets (e.g., FGVCAircraft base: 48.80→52.94 with PromptKD+DPC; EuroSAT base: 97.20→98.29). The only case where DPC shows a slight base-accuracy drop is PromptKD on ImageNet (80.82→80.25), though HM still improves marginally (83.59→83.91).

## Relation to Prior Work

DPC builds directly on the CLIP-based prompt tuning lineage (CoOp, MaPLe, PromptSRC, PromptKD) and the Base-New Trade-off problem first formalized by CoCoOp. Prior BNT mitigations—conditional contexts, semantic distance balancing, adapter-based feature mixing, and knowledge distillation—all operate on a single shared prompt vector, imposing constraints to balance competing optimization directions. DPC departs by introducing a structurally separate parallel prompt that is independently optimized for base classes via a Dynamic Hard Negative Optimizer (InfoNCE-based contrastive loss with hard negatives sampled through the frozen tuned prompt), while the original tuned prompt is frozen to preserve new-class generalization. This prompt-level decoupling contrasts with DePT's feature-level decoupling, and the paper argues it provides a broader optimization space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DPC offers a plug-and-play, self-contained module that improves base-class accuracy by ~3 points across four backbones and eleven datasets without degrading new-class generalization, and without requiring external knowledge or additional network parameters. The feature-channel invariance analysis provides theoretical grounding for the linear weighting mechanism, making the approach interpretable. However, the method is validated only on image classification; the authors note future work on detection and segmentation, and the fixed ω hyperparameters (ω_b=0.2, ω_n=1e-6) suggest room for adaptive scheduling. Whether prompt-level decoupling generalizes to other PEFT paradigms or non-CLIP VLMs remains open.
