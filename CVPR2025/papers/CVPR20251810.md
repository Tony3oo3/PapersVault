## Problem

Conventional contrastive learning for molecular phenotypic data applies uniform binary weighting to all negative pairs, failing to distinguish experimentally noisy or structurally similar molecules (e.g., property cliffs) from true negatives. Additionally, existing multimodal alignment methods such as CLOOME, MIGA, and InfoCORE cannot handle the frequent missing modalities (e.g., 75% missing gene expression in ChEMBL2K), discarding valuable training data or introducing imputation noise.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three modalities per molecule: an attributed molecular graph (atom and bond features), hand-crafted Cell Painting morphological features extracted via CellProfiler, and LINCS L1000 gene expression profiles rescaled to [0,1]. Any subset of these modalities may be absent for a given sample. The output is a unified molecular embedding produced by a Mixture-of-Experts fusion layer, which is then used for multi-task property classification, cross-modal retrieval (molecule→image or molecule→gene expression), and drug repurposing via enrichment scoring against disease-specific gene signatures.

## Method

MINER employs three independent encoders (a 3-layer GNN for graphs, 5-layer FCNs for morphology and gene expression) whose outputs are each split into informative and noisy components via a denoising module that enforces statistical independence through a Pearson-correlation-based inter-correlation loss. The key novelty is a negative sampling calibration step: an MLP predicts an alignment probability γ_ij for each cross-modal pair using the noisy components, and the contrastive loss is modified so that pairs with high γ (likely false negatives) are down-weighted in the denominator while true negatives are emphasized, reducing the penalty for structurally similar molecules with divergent phenotypes. For incomplete data, a generation module synthesizes missing modalities from available ones (trained with MSE), and a Mixture-of-Experts layer with softmax routing dynamically weights each modality's expert output to produce the final embedding. The full training objective combines the denoising loss, calibrated contrastive loss (for both G–I and G–E pairs), generation loss, and a supervised cross-entropy loss with a router-variance regularizer.

## Results

On ChEMBL2K, MINER achieves 82.2±1.0 average AUROC, 63.4±4.4 AUROC>80%, 51.7±2.8 AUROC>85%, and 38.5±2.8 AUROC>90%, surpassing the best baseline InfoCORE(GE) (79.3±0.9 Avg AUROC) by 2.9 points. On Broad6K, MINER reaches 70.8±0.5 Avg AUROC and 18.8±4.4 AUROC>80%, outperforming the best GNN pretraining baseline JOAO (67.3±0.4 Avg AUROC) by 3.5 points. For molecule-phenotype retrieval, MINER achieves MRR of 0.14 (gene expression) and 0.16 (cell painting), with Hit@10 of 29.30% and 32.82% respectively, exceeding InfoCORE by 7.8% and 16.3% on Hit@10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MINER outperforms all 13 baselines on both ChEMBL2K and Broad6K for molecular property prediction. Against the strongest multi-modal baseline InfoCORE(GE) [41], MINER gains +2.9 Avg AUROC on ChEMBL2K (82.2 vs. 79.3) and +10.6 on Broad6K (70.8 vs. 60.2), with the Broad6K gap driven by 75.33% missing gene expression data that MINER's generation module handles. Among GNN pretraining baselines, MINER exceeds the best (JOAO [48], 67.3 Avg AUROC on Broad6K) by 3.5 points, while supervised unimodal models (Morgan FP [33] at 63.3, Gene Expression [43] at 56.9) trail by 7.5 and 13.9 points respectively. For molecule-phenotype retrieval, MINER beats InfoCORE [41] on Hit@5 (+0.78% GE, +3.79% CP) and Hit@10 (+2.12% GE, +4.60% CP), and substantially outperforms CLIP [12] (Hit@10: 29.30% vs. 27.17% GE; 32.82% vs. 28.89% CP) and CCL [4] (Hit@10: 29.30% vs. 8.37% GE; 32.82% vs. 7.38% CP). Notably, traditional contrastive baselines CLOOME [36] and MIGA [52] underperform even supervised unimodal models on Broad6K, highlighting the difficulty of weakly aligned pairs that MINER's negative sampling calibration specifically addresses.

## Relation to Prior Work

MINER sits at the intersection of multi-modal contrastive learning for molecular phenotypic data (CLOOME, MIGA, InfoCORE) and the broader contrastive learning literature on handling noisy or mislabeled negatives (Hoffmann et al., Srinivasa et al.). It departs from prior multi-modal alignment methods by replacing their binary positive/negative weighting with a learned calibration that estimates per-pair alignment probability from disentangled unimodal representations, and by substituting InfoCORE's zero-imputation for missing modalities with a generative module plus Mixture-of-Experts fusion. The denoising module, which separates informative features from noise via an inter-correlation loss, draws on independent-component ideas but is applied here specifically to molecular phenotypic embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in phenotypic drug discovery: real datasets (ChEMBL2K, Broad6K) contain large fractions of missing modalities and weakly aligned molecule-phenotype pairs that degrade standard contrastive objectives. The drug-repurposing application (6/10 top candidates for Alzheimer's supported by literature) demonstrates translational relevance, though validation is limited to two diseases and a small candidate set. Open questions include whether the negative sampling calibration generalizes to other weakly-supervised multi-modal domains, how the method scales to the full LINCS L1000 or Cell Painting databases (tens of thousands of compounds), and whether the MoE fusion strategy remains effective as the number of modalities grows beyond three.
