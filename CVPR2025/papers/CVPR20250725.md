## Problem

While MLLMs trained on million-scale video datasets can understand general video content, it is unknown whether they can 'think in space'—build an implicit cognitive map of an environment from sequential visual observations and retrieve spatial information to answer questions on demand. No prior benchmark systematically evaluates MLLMs' visual-spatial intelligence from egocentric video, and the specific spatial sub-capabilities (relational reasoning, egocentric-allocentric transformation) that are present or missing remain uncharacterized.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes egocentric video recordings of real indoor scenes (288 videos from ScanNet, ScanNet++, and ARKitScenes) paired with text questions spanning 8 tasks across configurational, measurement estimation, and spatiotemporal categories. Outputs include multiple-choice or numerical spatial answers (with a novel Mean Relative Accuracy metric averaging relative-error thresholds from 0.5 to 0.95 for continuous values), as well as cognitive maps placing object centers on a 10×10 grid and free-form linguistic self-explanations when probing model internals. The video input is continuous temporal data rather than static frames, requiring the model to integrate spatial information across the full recording.

## Method

The core contribution is VSI-Bench, a video-based benchmark of over 5,000 QA pairs constructed by unifying three 3D indoor scene datasets into a standardized meta-information format, auto-generating questions via task-specific templates (with human annotation for route planning), and applying iterative human-in-the-loop quality review to remove ambiguities and propagate corrections. The paper evaluates 15 MLLMs under zero-shot greedy decoding and introduces two complementary probing mechanisms: linguistic self-explanations (revealing that ~71% of errors stem from spatial reasoning rather than perception or language) and visual cognitive maps (revealing that MLLMs form strong local but weak global spatial representations, with map-distance accuracy dropping sharply as object separation increases). A key empirical finding is that standard linguistic reasoning techniques—zero-shot CoT, self-consistency, and Tree-of-Thoughts—all degrade performance on spatial tasks, while explicitly generating and using a cognitive map improves relative distance accuracy by 10 percentage points, positioning cognitive map generation as a promising pretext task for enhancing MLLMs' visual-spatial reasoning.

## Results

On VSI-Bench (5,000+ QA pairs across 288 egocentric indoor videos), human evaluators achieve 79% average accuracy, outperforming the best MLLM (Gemini-1.5 Pro) by 33 percentage points. Gemini-1.5 Pro leads all models, with top open-source models LLaVA-Video-72B and LLaVA-OneVision-72B trailing it by only 4–5%. However, 7 out of 12 open-source models fall below the chance-level baseline, and all three linguistic prompting techniques (CoT, self-consistency, Tree-of-Thoughts) degrade average performance by 1–4%. Explicitly generating cognitive maps improves relative distance accuracy from 46.0% to 56.0% (MLLM-generated) and 66.0% (ground-truth map).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gemini-1.5 Pro is the strongest MLLM on VSI-Bench, outperforming GPT-4o and all open-source models; it approaches human-level performance on absolute distance and room size estimation but still lags humans by ~33 points overall. LLaVA-Video-72B and LLaVA-OneVision-72B are the best open-source models, trailing Gemini-1.5 Pro by only 4–5%, making them competitive alternatives. The remaining open-source models (InternVL2, ViLA, LongViLA, LongVA) largely underperform, with 7 of 12 open-source entries scoring below the chance-level (frequency) baseline, indicating that video support alone does not confer spatial intelligence. On the general video benchmark VideoMME, Gemini-1.5 Pro with zero-shot CoT improves from 77.2 to 79.8, confirming that CoT helps general video understanding but is harmful for spatial tasks on VSI-Bench (where it reduces average performance by ~4%). The cognitive map ablation shows that even a ground-truth map yields a 20–32 point gain over the no-map baseline on relative distance, highlighting that the bottleneck is the model's inability to form accurate global spatial representations rather than a lack of local spatial awareness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of video understanding benchmarks and spatial cognition. Prior video benchmarks (Video-MME, EgoSchema, OpenEQA) evaluate content-level comprehension without 3D spatial grounding, while earlier spatial-reasoning work relies on 2D images or language-only inputs. VSI-Bench repurposes existing 3D reconstruction datasets (ScanNet, ScanNet++, ARKitScenes) to generate video-grounded spatial QA pairs, and borrows the cognitive-map framework from Tolman and Newcombe to probe MLLMs' implicit spatial world models. The dual-coding theory (Clark & Paivio) motivates the parallel linguistic (self-explanation) and visual (cognitive map) analysis of model behavior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that spatial reasoning accounts for ~71% of MLLM errors, and that standard linguistic prompting techniques (CoT, self-consistency, ToT) actively degrade spatial performance, reframes how the community should approach improving MLLMs' spatial intelligence—linguistic 'thinking more' is not the answer. The discovery that MLLMs form only local (not global) spatial models, and that explicitly generating cognitive maps yields a 10% gain in distance reasoning, points toward a concrete architectural or prompting direction. Open questions include whether task-specific fine-tuning, self-supervised spatial objectives, or visuospatial-tailored prompting can close the 33% human-model gap, and whether the locality limitation is fundamental to current transformer architectures or an artifact of training data.
