## Problem

Existing soccer video understanding research is fragmented across task-specific models built on limited datasets (SoccerNet series, ~500 matches), with no unified framework or sufficiently large-scale data to support comprehensive, multi-task soccer understanding. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a 4× larger dataset and a single visual encoder adaptable to diverse downstream tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252538/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 30-second soccer video segments sampled at 1 FPS with frames resized to 224×224 pixels; for foul recognition, it takes multi-view videos of the same scene. Outputs are structured as: 24-class event labels (e.g., 'goal', 'VAR', 'penalty missed'), anonymized free-text commentaries with entity placeholders like [PLAYER] and [TEAM], and paired foul type (8 classes) + severity (4 levels) predictions. The encoder produces frame-level features of shape T×D that are further aggregated depending on the task head.

## Method

MatchVision is a soccer-specific visual encoder that initializes its patch embedding, spatial attention, and text encoder from SigLIP Base-16, then extends them with TimeSformer-style interleaved temporal and spatial self-attention blocks (K layers) to capture spatiotemporal dynamics. Each frame is split into M patches, embedded with spatial positional encodings and a [cls] token, then combined across T frames with temporal positional encodings; an aggregation layer applies spatial self-attention per frame to pool information into [cls] tokens, yielding a T×D video representation. Pretraining uses either supervised event classification (cross-entropy on 24 event types) or video-language contrastive learning (SigLIP sigmoid loss with a multi-positive strategy treating semantically similar commentaries as positives), or a hybrid of both. Downstream tasks attach lightweight heads to the frozen encoder: a linear classifier for events, a Perceiver aggregator + MLP projection feeding LLaMA-3-8B for commentary generation, and a shared MLP with two linear classifiers for foul type and severity.

## Results

On event classification (24 types), MatchVision pretrained with supervised classification on SoccerNet-pro + MatchTime + SoccerReplay-1988 achieves 84.0% top-1 accuracy, 97.3% top-3, and 99.2% top-5, substantially outperforming the best off-the-shelf encoder (SigLIP at 50.2% top-1) and the best soccer-specific baseline (Baidu at 56.4%). For commentary generation, the hybrid pretrained MatchVision achieves BLEU-1 of 33.58, METEOR of 26.82, ROUGE-L of 28.21, and CIDEr of 44.18, setting new state-of-the-art across all metrics. On multi-view foul recognition, the frozen MatchVision encoder reaches 0.58 severity accuracy, surpassing jointly finetuned MViT (0.43) and R(2+1)D (0.39), though it trails MViT on foul class top-1 (0.44 vs. 0.47).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MatchVision decisively outperforms all off-the-shelf general-purpose encoders (I3D, C3D, ResNet, CLIP, InternVideo, SigLIP) on both event classification and commentary generation, with a 33.8-point top-1 accuracy gap over SigLIP (84.0% vs. 50.2%) under the same training data. Against the strongest soccer-specific baseline, Baidu [61], MatchVision gains 27.6 points in top-1 classification accuracy (84.0% vs. 56.4%) and leads on all commentary metrics when trained with the full SoccerReplay-1988 data. On foul recognition, the frozen MatchVision encoder achieves 0.58 severity accuracy, beating jointly finetuned MViT (0.43) and R(2+1)D (0.39), but trails MViT on foul class top-1 (0.44 vs. 0.47) and top-2 (0.53 vs. 0.69), indicating that the frozen encoder captures severity cues well but benefits from task-specific fine-tuning for fine-grained foul type discrimination. Overall, MatchVision sets new state-of-the-art on event classification and commentary generation while remaining competitive on foul recognition without any encoder fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

MatchVision's architecture directly extends TimeSformer's interleaved spatiotemporal attention blocks into a soccer-specific visual encoder, initialized from SigLIP's pretrained weights, while the commentary generation head follows the MatchTime paradigm (Perceiver aggregation + LLM decoding). The paper departs from the SoccerNet series' task-specific modeling approach—where separate models were designed for action spotting, commentary, and foul recognition—by proposing a single pretrained visual encoder that serves as a universal backbone across all three downstream tasks. The dataset contribution (SoccerReplay-1988) also extends prior soccer datasets by nearly quadrupling the number of matches, expanding event categories from 17 to 24, and introducing an automated curation pipeline for scalability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SoccerReplay-1988 and the SoccerReplay-test benchmark are likely to become the standard resources for soccer video understanding research, given their 4× scale over prior datasets, finer-grained annotations, and inclusion of modern regulations (e.g., VAR). The unified encoder paradigm—pretrain once, adapt to multiple tasks—offers a practical template that could transfer to other sports domains, though the paper does not test this generalization. Open questions include whether the automated LLM-based event summarization (98% accuracy on 2% sampling) degrades on rarer event types, whether the frozen-encoder approach for foul recognition generalizes to more complex multi-view scenarios, and whether the spatiotemporal attention design is truly necessary versus simpler temporal pooling at this scale.
