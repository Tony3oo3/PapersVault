## Problem

Video dialog and visual dialog have evolved as separate research threads despite sharing nearly identical input structures (visual input, caption, dialog history, question), and current multimodal models largely ignore these conversational tasks in favor of single-round VQA or retrieval. No prior work has unified both tasks in a single model or systematically quantified whether their training data can mutually benefit each other.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251927/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a video (F frames) or a single image (F=1) at resolution H×W, a caption, a dialog history of prior question-answer pairs, and the current question. Visual frames are encoded by EVA-CLIP, every four spatially adjacent patches are merged into one token, and the result is projected to a joint hidden dimension D. The output is a free-form autoregressive text answer; for VisDial evaluation, candidate answers are ranked by cosine similarity between their sentence-transformer embeddings (RoBERTa-large or OpenAI Text Embedding-3) and the generated answer's embedding, replacing the traditional NSP-based ranking.

## Method

V²Dial employs a multimodal expert-based transformer with six dedicated FFN experts: spatial (E_spa) and temporal (E_tmp) experts that process separately masked self-attention outputs (rather than sequential space-then-time attention), a visual expert (E_vis) that fuses their concatenation, and two language experts (E_cap, E_ctx) for caption and dialog context, plus a fusion expert (E_fus) in the final layers. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) pre-training the expert layers on WebVid-2M and CC-3M with novel spatial-temporal contrastive (STC) and spatial-temporal matching (STM) losses alongside MLM, VTC, and VTM; (2) coupling the frozen experts to a Flan-T5-large LLM via a linear projection and aligning hidden states with a next-token generative loss on 25% of Champagne; (3) end-to-end fine-tuning on the target dialog benchmarks. The key architectural insight is that routing image features through only the spatial expert and video features through both spatial and temporal experts allows a single model to jointly learn from both data types without architectural changes, and the STC/STM losses explicitly align the two feature streams.

## Results

In the zero-shot setting on AVSD-DSTC10, V²Dial achieves CIDEr 53.8, more than doubling the best baseline VideoChat2 (25.4), and ranks first across all seven metrics (BLEU-1 54.6, METEOR 19.7, ROUGE-L 38.3). On VisDial v1.0 zero-shot, it attains R@1 of 22.1 and NDCG of 33.3 using OpenAI Text Embedding-3, outperforming FROMAGe (R@1 17.6) and Champagne (NDCG 25.5). In the fine-tuning setting, V²Dial sets new state-of-the-art on AVSD-DSTC10 with CIDEr 103.3 (vs. 96.9 for MST-MIXER) and on VisDial with R@1 46.1 and MRR 53.2 (OpenAI TE-3 ranking).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the zero-shot AVSD evaluation, V²Dial dominates all five baselines by wide margins: on AVSD-DSTC10 it more than doubles VideoChat2's CIDEr (53.8 vs. 25.4) and surpasses it by 12.1 BLEU-1 points (54.6 vs. 42.5); MST-MIXER [3] and Video-ChatGPT [40] perform near-randomly (CIDEr 3.0 and 3.9 respectively), likely due to format mismatch. On zero-shot VisDial, V²Dial outperforms FROMAGe [27] by 2.4 R@1 points (20.0 vs. 17.6) and beats Champagne [26] by 7.8 NDCG points (33.3 vs. 25.5) despite being trained on 4× less dialog data. In the fine-tuning AVSD setting, V²Dial edges out MST-MIXER [3] on all AVSD-DSTC10 metrics (CIDEr 103.3 vs. 96.9, BLEU-1 70.7 vs. 69.7) and leads on DSTC7 CIDEr (139.8 vs. 136.5), while remaining competitive with PDC [29] and THAME [60] on the older DSTC8/DSTC7 splits. On fine-tuned VisDial, V²Dial achieves the best R@1 (45.4 with RoBERTa, 46.1 with OpenAI TE-3) surpassing MST-MIXER (42.2) and all prior discriminative models (40.4–41.3), though it trails slightly on R@5 and NDCG compared to the NSP-trained baselines, reflecting the inherent difficulty of the fully generative paradigm for retrieval-style evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the BLIP-2-style paradigm of coupling a frozen LLM with a vision encoder through a lightweight adapter, but replaces the single Q-Former with a set of modality-specific and spatial/temporal-specific feed-forward experts. It departs from prior visual dialog work (which relies on NSP heads and negative sampling) by adopting a fully generative training paradigm, and from video dialog work (which has evolved independently) by jointly learning from both image and video data through dedicated spatial and temporal experts. The expert design is positioned between single-modality FFNs (VLMo, BEiT-3) and sparse modality-agnostic experts (LIMoE), introducing hard-routed, fine-grained spatial and temporal experts that, to the authors' knowledge, have not been used together before.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first systematic domain-shift study between AVSD and VisDial reveals a non-trivial tension: fine-tuning on one dataset degrades performance on the other, and curriculum learning (pre-train on one, fine-tune on the other) is worse than joint training, suggesting the two tasks encode conflicting response-length and style priors. This finding is important for practitioners who might assume that video and visual dialog data are freely interchangeable. Open questions include whether the domain shift can be mitigated with larger-scale joint training, whether the spatial/temporal expert decomposition generalizes to other video understanding tasks beyond dialog, and whether the zero-shot evaluation protocol the authors advocate will be adopted as a standard for video dialog benchmarks.
