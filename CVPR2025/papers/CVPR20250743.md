## Problem

NeRF-based methods for autonomous driving can jointly render camera and lidar with high fidelity but are too slow (≈10 MP/s) for large-scale simulation testing, while existing 3DGS-based methods achieve real-time speeds but are limited to camera-only rendering, ignoring the lidar modality essential for AD stacks. No prior method combines real-time rasterization with realistic multi-modal sensor simulation including lidar intensity, ray dropouts, and rolling shutter effects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250743/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a posed camera or lidar sensor specification (intrinsics, extrinsics, rolling shutter duration) and a trained scene representation consisting of a static background plus dynamic actors described by 3D bounding boxes with SE(3) pose sequences. For cameras it outputs a full-resolution RGB image; for lidars it outputs a sparse point cloud in spherical coordinates (azimuth, elevation, range) with per-point intensity and a binary ray-drop flag, where the range is rendered as the median of the α-blended Gaussian ranges at inference time.

## Method

SplatAD represents the scene as a set of 3D Gaussians with per-Gaussian learnable feature vectors (replacing spherical harmonics) and a small CNN decoder that predicts pixel-wise affine color mappings conditioned on ray direction and a sensor-specific embedding. The key architectural novelty is a custom CUDA rasterizer that projects Gaussians directly into spherical lidar coordinates (azimuth, elevation, range) using the Jacobian of the spherical transform, with tile-based parallelism matched to the lidar's non-uniform diode spacing, rather than the depth-image projection used by prior 3DGS methods. Rolling shutter is compensated in 2D sensor space by shifting each Gaussian's projected mean by its pixel velocity times the per-pixel time offset, with rectangular AABBs for culling. Densification uses the MCMC strategy from 3DGS-MCMC with a fixed Gaussian budget, and the full model is trained jointly with L1/SSIM image losses, L2 range and intensity losses, a line-of-sight penalty, and binary cross-entropy on ray drop probability.

## Results

SplatAD achieves state-of-the-art NVS image quality across all three datasets: 26.70 PSNR on PandaSet, 28.40 PSNR on Argoverse2, and 27.53 PSNR on nuScenes, outperforming the best NeRF baseline NeuRAD by +0.9 to +2.2 PSNR. On lidar rendering, SplatAD matches NeuRAD's point cloud quality (Chamfer distance 1.6 vs 1.9 on PandaSet, 1.7 vs 6.3 on nuScenes) while running up to 18× faster (19.5 MR/s vs 1.1 MR/s). In terms of rendering speed, SplatAD reaches 104–134 MP/s for cameras, an order of magnitude faster than NeuRAD (~10 MP/s) and 2–5× faster than other 3DGS methods. Training takes approximately 1.0–1.2 hours on a single A100, comparable to or faster than all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SplatAD outperforms all five baselines on image NVS across all three datasets, with the largest margins over UniSim (+3.5 to +6.0 PSNR) and consistent +1.0 to +2.2 PSNR gains over the strongest NeRF baseline NeuRAD. Among 3DGS methods, SplatAD leads Street Gaussians and OmniRE by +1.0 to +2.9 PSNR and PVG by +1.7 to +3.9 PSNR, while also rendering 1.6–5.5× faster. On lidar rendering, SplatAD matches or exceeds NeuRAD in Chamfer distance (1.6 vs 1.9 on PandaSet, 1.7 vs 6.3 on nuScenes) and ray drop accuracy (96.7% vs 96.2%), while the 3DGS baselines (PVG, Street-GS, OmniRE) produce severely degraded point clouds (CD 3–125) because they rely on depth-image projection rather than native lidar rasterization. The one exception is Argoverse2 lidar, where NeuRAD achieves a slightly lower CD (2.6 vs 2.8), though SplatAD still wins on intensity error (0.052 vs 0.058) and ray drop accuracy (92.6% vs 92.2%). In extrapolation (Table 4), SplatAD achieves the lowest DINOv2 Fréchet distance across all shift settings, e.g., 276.3 vs 377.4 (NeuRAD) at 0 m ego lane shift on PandaSet, confirming superior generalization to unseen viewpoints.

## Relation to Prior Work

SplatAD extends 3D Gaussian Splatting from camera-only rendering to joint camera and lidar rendering, filling a gap left by prior 3DGS-based AD methods (Street Gaussians, OmniRe, PVG) that could only produce images. It departs from the NeRF-based line of work (NeuRAD, UniSim) that already handled both modalities but at ray-tracing speeds, replacing volumetric ray marching with tile-based rasterization in spherical coordinates. Key technical adaptations include replacing spherical harmonics with per-Gaussian learnable features, adapting rolling shutter compensation from the static-scene formulation of Seiskari et al. to dynamic actors, and adopting MCMC densification and EWA antialiasing from recent 3DGS improvements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving NeuRAD-level lidar and camera quality at 10–18× the rendering speed, SplatAD makes large-scale closed-loop simulation of AD stacks practically feasible, which was previously bottlenecked by NeRF inference costs. The open-source CUDA implementation and use of standard frameworks (gsplat, neurad-studio) lower the barrier for the AD community to adopt multi-modal 3DGS rendering. Key limitations remain: all dynamic actors are modeled as rigid bodies, and extrapolation to significantly shifted viewpoints still degrades, pointing toward future work on non-rigid deformation and diffusion-based priors.
