## Problem

Existing neural video codecs (NVCs) cannot achieve real-time 1080p coding on consumer hardware while maintaining high compression ratios, because prior efficiency efforts focused on reducing computational cost (MACs) while overlooking operational costs such as memory I/O and function-call overhead. This paper identifies operational complexity as the true bottleneck and redesigns the codec around minimizing it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252438/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The codec takes video frames in YUV420 or RGB colorspace (both supported in a single model) along with a quantization parameter (qp) in [0, 63] that controls the target bitrate. Internally, frames are patch-embedded into 1/8-resolution latents, and the model produces a compressed bitstream via arithmetic coding plus reconstructed frames. A 16-bit integerization mode is available to guarantee bit-exact cross-device decoding, at the cost of reduced GPU throughput compared to fp16.

## Method

DCVC-RT replaces progressive downsampling with a single patch-embedding step that produces 1/8-scale latents, drastically cutting latent memory I/O while preserving receptive field. It eliminates explicit motion estimation and compensation in favor of implicit temporal modeling: a lightweight feature extractor pulls temporal context from the previously decoded latent, which is channel-concatenated with the current latent for joint encoder-decoder processing. A module-bank-based rate-control scheme provides multiple hyperprior modules and per-module vector banks (for encoder, decoder, feature extractor, and reconstruction network) to adaptively scale latents across qp values, achieving fine-grained bitrate control within one model. The entire pipeline is integerized to int16 with a precomputed sigmoid lookup table for deterministic cross-device consistency. Trained on Vimeo-90k with 7-frame sequences and fine-tuned on longer sequences, the model achieves 125.2/112.8 fps encoding/decoding at 1080p on an A100 GPU with a 21% average BD-Rate reduction over VTM/H.266.

## Results

DCVC-RT achieves an average BD-Rate of −21.0% versus VTM/H.266 across HEVC Class B–E, UVG, and MCL-JCV (all intra, YUV420), slightly outperforming ECM (−20.5%) and matching DCVC-FM (−22.1%) in compression ratio. On an NVIDIA A100 GPU, it encodes 1080p video at 125.2 fps and decodes at 112.8 fps (fp16), roughly 25× faster than DCVC-FM (5.0/5.9 fps). On a consumer RTX 2080 Ti, it reaches 39.5/34.1 fps for 1080p, enabling real-time 30 fps coding. In int16 mode, the BD-Rate degrades only to −18.3% while still outperforming VTM, and 1080p 30 fps coding is maintained on an RTX 4090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the H.266/VTM anchor, DCVC-RT saves 21.0% in bitrate on average, a clear win. Compared to the next-generation traditional codec ECM, DCVC-RT edges ahead by 0.5% BD-Rate (−21.0% vs −20.5%) while being orders of magnitude faster (125.2 fps vs 0.002 fps encoding). Versus the prior neural codec DCVC-FM, DCVC-RT matches the compression ratio within 1.1% BD-Rate (−21.0% vs −22.1%) but runs approximately 25× faster in encoding (125.2 vs 5.0 fps) and 19× faster in decoding (112.8 vs 5.9 fps). Against DCVC-DC, DCVC-RT is substantially better in both compression (−21.0% vs +14.5%) and speed (125.2 vs 3.3 fps). The one notable trade-off is that DCVC-RT's lightweight design (385 G MACs vs 2642 G for DCVC-FM) causes a slight performance drop in the very high-quality range (>40 dB), though this region is largely imperceptible to human viewers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DCVC line of work (DCVC-DC, DCVC-FM) by fundamentally reframing the NVC acceleration problem: rather than reducing computational complexity (MACs), it identifies operational complexity—memory I/O from large latent tensors and function-call overhead from many modules—as the primary speed bottleneck. Architecturally, it departs from the standard motion-estimation-and-compensation paradigm used in most NVCs by replacing it with implicit temporal modeling via a single feature extractor, and replaces progressive multi-scale downsampling with a single 1/8-resolution latent learned through patch embedding. It also unifies practical functionalities (int16 integerization for cross-device consistency, module-bank rate control) that prior work like MobileNVC and DCVC-FM addressed separately, into a single real-time-capable codec.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DCVC-RT is the first NVC to achieve real-time 1080p coding on consumer GPUs (RTX 2080 Ti: ~40 fps enc / 34 fps dec) while maintaining a 21% bitrate advantage over H.266/VTM, closing a critical gap between NVC research and practical deployment. The operational-complexity insight is broadly transferable: any learned codec or generative model with many sequential modules or large intermediate tensors can benefit from the same design principles. Key limitations include a performance drop at high quality ranges (>40 dB) due to the lightweight architecture, and a significant int16-vs-fp16 speed gap on modern GPUs lacking dedicated int16 Tensor Core optimization—both of which constrain immediate production use but point to clear future work.
