## Problem

Existing diffusion sampling schedulers use fixed or heuristically designed step sequences that cannot adapt to the intermediate state of each individual generation instance. Prior RL-based approaches like AdaDiff only select a single total step count per prompt without considering the evolving denoised image state, and none of the prior methods jointly optimize for both sampling efficiency and generation quality in an instance-adaptive manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250769/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The scheduler network receives a state tuple S = {P, I, τ} at each denoising step, where P is the conditioning signal (text prompt or class label), I is the current intermediate denoised image, and τ is the current diffusion time point. It outputs a discrete step size from the action space A = {10, 20, 50, 100, 200}, which determines how many timesteps the base sampler advances. The scheduler is a lightweight 3-ResNet-block network (0.28M–0.97M parameters depending on task) that adds negligible overhead relative to the U-Net, with a transformer head appended for text-to-image to jointly encode prompt and image.

## Method

RaSS formulates sampling as a finite MDP where an RL agent (Q-learning) selects step sizes conditioned on the current instance state, and introduces two key mechanisms: (1) Dynamic Stage Partitioning (DSP) divides the 1000-step process into five fixed intervals, reducing the optimization space and cutting training time by ~40%; (2) a Long-Term Perception (LTP) mechanism that maintains a historical network (updated every 5 epochs) to estimate future sampling likelihoods, ensuring globally optimal rather than myopic step decisions. The reward function combines PSNR between the sampled and target image with a step-size efficiency term weighted by hyperparameter α, allowing users to trade off quality versus speed. Training uses a composite L2 + KL-divergence loss against guidance signals that fuse the current reward probabilities with the LTP future likelihood. The resulting scheduler is plug-and-play and has been validated on DDIM, DPM-solver, and DPM-solver++ across unconditional, conditional, and text-to-image generation.

## Results

On ImageNet 64×64 with ADM-G, RaSS reduces FID from 23.71 (DDIM, 6 steps) to 11.02 while using only 4.8 average steps (1.25× speedup), and from 8.86 to 6.22 at the 10-step setting. On LSUN-Bedroom with ADM, RaSS achieves FID 23.2 at 4.02 steps (1.24× speedup) versus DDIM's 33.4 at 5 steps. For text-to-image on MS-COCO with Stable Diffusion, RaSS halves the sampling steps (25→13.2) while maintaining or slightly improving FID (24.27 vs 24.26 for DDIM; 17.45 vs 17.71 for DPM-solver++), yielding ~1.9× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DDIM (the standard uniform-step sampler), RaSS consistently achieves lower FID at equal or fewer steps across all datasets: e.g., FID 11.02 vs 23.71 on ImageNet at ~6 steps, and FID 8.62 vs 10.01 on LSUN-Bedroom at ~10 steps, while also providing 1.23–1.25× speedup. Against AutoDiffusion (the closest prior sampling-schedule optimizer), RaSS matches or marginally improves FID (11.02 vs 11.17 on ImageNet at 6 steps; 8.62 vs 8.66 on LSUN-Bedroom at 10 steps) while reducing the average step count by ~15–20%, yielding a 1.23–1.28× speedup that AutoDiffusion does not provide. For text-to-image on MS-COCO, RaSS applied on top of DPM-solver++ achieves FID 17.45 at 13.3 steps versus DPM-solver++'s 17.71 at 25 steps, a ~1.88× speedup with slightly better quality. Overall, RaSS wins on both quality and efficiency in every head-to-head comparison, with the largest gains appearing in the low-step regime (≤6 steps) where DDIM degrades most severely.

## Relation to Prior Work

RaSS extends the line of sampling-schedule optimization for diffusion models initiated by Watson et al. (dynamic programming over ELBO, differentiable sampler search) and AutoDiffusion (evolutionary search over FID). Unlike these global-schedule approaches and the prompt-level RL of AdaDiff, RaSS formulates scheduling as a per-instance, state-adaptive sequential decision problem solved by a lightweight RL agent that observes the intermediate denoised image at each step. It builds directly on the ODE formulations of DDIM, DPM-solver, and DPM-solver++ as the base samplers it wraps, and introduces a long-term perception mechanism (a historical network) to give the scheduler a forward-looking view of generation quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a plug-and-play module with negligible overhead (<1% of U-Net MACs), RaSS offers a practical path to halving inference steps in text-to-image pipelines while matching or slightly exceeding baseline quality, which is directly relevant to deployment cost. The instance-state-awareness insight—that per-sample adaptive schedules outperform any fixed or prompt-level schedule—suggests a broader principle for sequential generative processes beyond diffusion. Open questions include whether the fixed five-stage partition and discrete action space {10,20,50,100,200} generalize to video diffusion or very high-resolution generation, and whether the scheduler must be retrained for each new base model or can transfer across architectures.
