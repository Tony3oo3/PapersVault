## Problem

Virtual try-on models suffer from two persistent issues: paired (human, product-view garment) training data is scarce due to copyright restrictions on product webpages, and generated images frequently exhibit distorted text and faded garment textures. These artifacts are especially pronounced in regions where the garment's fine details must be accurately deformed onto diverse body poses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250959/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The base try-on model consumes a masked human image (garment region removed), a canonical front-facing product-view garment image, and a DensePose pose representation, all at 512×512 resolution, to produce a photorealistic image of the person wearing the garment. The EARSB refinement stage additionally takes the initial generated image and a spatial error map (from the WSC) to produce a corrected output. The human-to-garment sub-model takes a single clothed-person image and outputs a synthetic canonical product-view garment, enabling pair construction from unpaired datasets.

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary mechanisms. First, a human-to-garment model (based on ClothFlow's flow-warping UNet) extracts canonical product-view garments from clothed-person images, yielding 21,669 synthetic (human, garment) pairs that augment real training data via a real/synthetic conditioning flag. Second, EARSB extends the I²SB Schrödinger Bridge with a spatially adaptive noise schedule: a weakly-supervised error classifier (trained with only bounding-box annotations on a few hundred images) produces a confidence heatmap M, which is multiplied element-wise into the Gaussian noise ε to create reweighted noise ε^r = M·ε. This forces the diffusion process to add more noise (and thus more freedom to regenerate) in erroneous regions while preserving correct regions with minimal perturbation. The denoising UNet (ClothFlow-based) is conditioned on the garment, error map, and pose, trained with MSE on the reweighted noise, and further improved via classifier guidance and expert denoisers split at t=0.5.

## Results

On VITON-HD (paired), the full model EARSB(SD)+H2G-UH/FH achieves FID 4.90, KID 0.03, SSIM 0.925, and LPIPS 0.053, outperforming all SD-based baselines on KID, SSIM, and LPIPS while matching TPD's FID (4.86). On DressCode-Upper (paired), the same model reaches FID 6.76, KID 0.08, SSIM 0.968, and LPIPS 0.023, setting new bests across all four metrics. The GAN-based variant EARSB+H2G-UH/FH achieves FID 5.14 (VITON-HD) and FID 7.05 (DressCode-Upper) in the paired setting, consistently surpassing all GAN-based methods. In user studies, EARSB is preferred over GP-VTON and StableVITON in 59% of cases on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GAN-based methods, EARSB+H2G-UH/FH dominates: it improves FID from 5.53 (GP-VTON) to 5.14 on VITON-HD and from 11.76 (HR-VTON) to 7.05 on DressCode-Upper, with GP-VTON showing a particularly large gap on DressCode-Upper (FID 23.66 vs. 7.05). Among SD-based methods, EARSB(SD)+H2G-UH/FH achieves the best KID (0.03), SSIM (0.925), and LPIPS (0.053) on VITON-HD, though TPD [36] retains a marginal FID advantage (4.86 vs. 4.90). On DressCode-Upper, EARSB(SD)+H2G-UH/FH sets new bests across all metrics, edging out IDM-VTON [6] (FID 6.79 vs. 6.76) and CatVTON [7] (FID 7.66 vs. 6.76). StableVITON [18] and TPD [36] report only VITON-HD results, limiting direct cross-dataset comparison. The user study confirms these quantitative gains, with EARSB preferred over both GP-VTON (58% consistency, 61% fidelity) and StableVITON (62% consistency, 55% fidelity) in an average of 59% of pairwise comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Image-to-Image Schrödinger Bridge (I²SB) framework by introducing a spatially adaptive noise schedule driven by a weakly-supervised error classifier, moving beyond the uniform noise assumption of vanilla I²SB. The refinement pipeline is conceptually inspired by boosting cascades, where a targeted model corrects the specific failures of a preceding model, and the denoising architecture reuses the ClothFlow UNet for precise garment deformation. On the data side, the human-to-garment model is framed as the geometric inverse of virtual try-on, enabling synthetic paired data generation from readily available single human images. The classifier guidance mechanism adapts the diffusion posterior sampling formulation from Chung et al. to steer the refinement toward the real-data distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The model-agnostic refinement pipeline (EARSB) offers a practical drop-in improvement for any existing try-on model without retraining the base architecture, and the synthetic data augmentation consistently boosts multiple base models (CAT-DM, StableVITON) with minimal additional cost. The weakly-supervised error classifier requires only a few hours of manual bounding-box annotation, making the approach accessible to groups without large annotation budgets. Open questions include whether the spatially adaptive noise schedule generalizes to other image editing or refinement tasks beyond try-on, how the approach scales to full-body multi-garment scenarios, and whether the synthetic-real domain gap can be further reduced without the real/synthetic conditioning flag. The paper does not explore whether the refinement stage could be iterated (multi-round boosting) or applied to other generative tasks with localized artifacts.
