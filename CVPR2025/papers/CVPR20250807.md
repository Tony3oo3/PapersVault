## Problem

Existing bundle adjustment research and solvers (Ceres, COLMAP, etc.) are designed around photo collections where every image has independent camera parameters, yet real-world SfM pipelines overwhelmingly involve shared intrinsics across many images. This shared-intrinsics variant (SI-BA) introduces structural properties—particularly the H_cc, H_cp, and H_cl blocks—that no prior solver exploits, leaving a significant performance gap between academic benchmarks and practical deployment.

## Input / Output

The solver consumes a flat-buffer representation of image poses (7-DOF quaternions grouped by camera), 3D landmark positions, shared camera intrinsics (n_c × n_i), 2D keypoints stored in both image-grouped and landmark-grouped order, and CSR-style slicing/indexing arrays defining observation connectivity. It outputs refined versions of all state variables. A key structural constraint is that camera intrinsics are shared across all images assigned to the same camera, which the data layout exploits via S^P slicing arrays to avoid redundant storage and enable batched GEMM dispatches per camera group.

## Method

The core contribution is a matrix-free PCG solver for the SI-BA reduced camera system that bypasses explicit construction of the dominant H_pl and H_lp sparse matrices, instead computing their matrix-vector products on the fly from image poses, landmark positions, and shared intrinsics. This works because camera intrinsics and poses are constant per row of H_pl and remain in cache across the inner loop, while landmark positions are frequently already cached due to covisibility-ordered data layout. The solver uses ISPC/CUDA SIMD kernels with a grid-stride parallelization for H_pl and per-landmark assignment for H_lp, combined with a heuristic ordering (images by camera then ID, landmarks by observation count then min image ID) to maximize cache hits and minimize warp divergence. For single-precision operation, the paper introduces a compact per-image scaling factor s_i = √(tr(h^r)/tr(h^t)) that balances translation and rotation conditioning without the cache overhead of full Jacobi scaling, and a condition-number-bounded pseudo-inverse (using the Merikoski upper bound) to handle low-parallax landmarks that become degenerate during optimization.

## Results

The proposed matrix-free SI-BA solvers achieve roughly 4× (CPU-f64), 8× (CPU-f32), and 20× (GPU-f32) runtime speedups over Ceres across all five datasets at both τ=0.001 and τ=0.1 tolerances. Peak memory consumption is reduced by approximately one order of magnitude (e.g., 1303 MB → 74 MB on EuRoC, 2548 MB → 192 MB on KITTI in dynamic-intrinsics mode). Convergence characteristics (cost reduction per LM iteration) are virtually identical to Ceres, confirming that the speedup stems entirely from accelerated core matrix-vector products rather than a different optimization trajectory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is Ceres, the de-facto standard BA solver used by COLMAP, TheiaSfM, OpenMVG, and GLOMAP. The proposed method wins decisively on both runtime and memory across all five datasets and both intrinsics configurations. On runtime, CPU-f32 achieves ~8× speedup (e.g., 74 ms vs. 1303 ms per PCG iteration on EuRoC) and GPU-f32 reaches ~20×, while even the double-precision CPU-f64 variant delivers ~4×. On memory, the reduction ranges from ~5× on LaMAR with dynamic intrinsics (1652 MB vs. 8715 MB, limited by the dense H_cl storage) to ~17× on EuRoC (74 MB vs. 1303 MB). The one notable exception is LaMAR with dynamic intrinsics, where the 20-camera configuration inflates H_cl sparsity and partially erodes both the speedup and memory advantage; the authors identify extending the matrix-free scheme to H_cl as future work. Convergence curves (Fig. 8) confirm that all solvers reduce cost at the same rate per LM iteration, so the gains are purely computational rather than algorithmic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the foundational BA framework of Triggs et al. (PCG on the RCS via implicit Schur) and the multicore parallelization strategies of Wu et al., but deliberately inverts the latter's key design choice: where Wu et al. bypass the augmented Hessian and compute matrix-vector products through the Jacobian, this paper circumvents the Jacobian entirely and exploits the augmented Hessian in matrix-free form. The critical insight is that the shared-intrinsics configuration introduces H_cc, H_cp, and H_cl blocks absent in the photo-collection case, making the dominant H_pl/H_lp products amenable to on-the-fly computation where camera intrinsics and poses remain cache-resident across many observations. The condition-number upper bound of Merikoski et al. is repurposed as a cheap gate for applying pseudo-inverse to ill-conditioned landmark blocks in single-precision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since every major open-source SfM pipeline (COLMAP, TheiaSfM, OpenMVG, GLOMAP) relies on Ceres for BA, an 8× speedup and 10× memory reduction for the practically dominant shared-intrinsics case has immediate and broad impact on 3D reconstruction, view synthesis, and localization workflows. The single-precision analysis—particularly the compact per-image scaling factor matching Jacobi scaling's conditioning and the condition-number-gated pseudo-inverse for low-parallax landmarks—is applicable to any existing single-precision BA implementation, including GPU pipelines. A key limitation is that H_cl is stored densely, degrading performance on multi-camera datasets (LaMAR with 20 cameras); extending the matrix-free scheme to H_cl is identified as future work. The absence of a standard SI-BA benchmark (analogous to BAL for the photo-collection case) remains an open community need.
