## Problem

Supervised learning cannot leverage strong masking augmentation (≥50% mask ratio) because applying it directly to the main model destabilizes training and degrades accuracy, despite masking being the core mechanism behind successful MIM pretraining like MAE. This creates a gap: supervised recipes lack access to the powerful regularization that high-ratio masking provides, limiting their potential relative to self-supervised methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250262/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (224×224 for ViT, 224×224 or 32×32 patches for ResNet/Swin) and produces class labels; for the text experiments it takes token sequences with 15% masking (BERT/GLUE) or image+text pairs (CLIP pretraining). The key structural detail is that a single network is evaluated twice per sample: once on the full input (main-branch) and once on a masked version where ~50% of patches/tokens are removed (sub-branch), with the sub-branch output supervised by the main-branch's detached softmax rather than the ground-truth label.

## Method

MaskSub splits training into a main-branch (standard supervised loss on unmasked input) and a sub-branch (same network, masked input at 50% ratio) trained with a relaxed cross-entropy loss against the main-branch's stop-gradient softmax output, effectively a self-distillation objective. The total loss is the average of the two, so the sub-branch gradient is independent of the main-branch's label supervision. This design provides automatic difficulty control: early in training the main-branch output is noisy, making the sub-branch target easy; as the main-branch converges, the sub-branch target sharpens toward the true label, progressively increasing the masking challenge. MAE-style token removal reduces sub-branch computation to ~1.5× standard training, and gradient accumulation separates the two backward passes to avoid extra VRAM. The framework generalizes to DropSub (strong dropout in sub-branch) and PathSub (higher drop-path probability in sub-branch), with masking yielding the largest gains at the lowest cost.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k training from scratch with DeiT-III, MaskSub (50% masking) improves top-1 accuracy by +0.6 to +0.7 pp at 400 epochs (e.g., ViT-B/16: 83.5→84.1%, ViT-L/16: 84.5→85.2%) and by +0.4 to +0.5 pp at 800 epochs. In finetuning, MaskSub adds +0.2–0.4 pp for MAE, BEiT v2, and CLIP finetuning, and +0.3–1.0 pp for ResNet (RSB) and Swin Transformer. For CLIP pretraining (ViT-B/32), MaskSub boosts zero-shot ImageNet accuracy from 33.5% to 37.6% (+4.1 pp). On the GLUE benchmark, BERT-base improves from 80.9 to 81.6 average score with 15% masking.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaskSub consistently outperforms DeiT-III [37] by +0.3 to +0.7 pp across all ViT sizes at both 400 and 800 epochs, and notably ViT-H + MaskSub (400 ep) matches ViT-H DeiT-III (800 ep) with half the training time. Against MAE [15] (1600 epochs), MaskSub at 400 epochs already surpasses MAE for ViT-B (84.1 vs 83.6) and ViT-L (85.2 vs not reported at 400 ep), while at 800 epochs it leads by +0.3 pp for ViT-S and +0.6 pp for ViT-B. Compared to SupMAE [26] at the same 400-epoch budget, MaskSub gains +0.5 pp on ViT-B (84.1 vs 83.6). Against CoSub [38], MaskSub achieves equal or better accuracy at lower compute cost (×1.5 vs ×2.0); for example, ViT-S 800-epoch MaskSub (81.7) beats CoSub (81.5), and ViT-H 400-epoch MaskSub (85.7) ties CoSub 800-epoch (85.7) with half the epochs. In the training-budget comparison (Table 9), MaskSub (84.1%, 25.1 GPU-days) outperforms DataAug [17] (83.5%, 36.8 GPU-days), GradAug [50] (83.2%, 39.7 GPU-days), and CoSub [38] (83.9%, 35.3 GPU-days) with the lowest additional cost (+45% vs +104–129%). For self-distillation methods on ResNet (Table 6), RSB + MaskSub achieves 80.0/82.1/82.8% for ResNet-50/101/152, surpassing PS-KD [21] (81.7/82.3), SD-dropout [25] (81.8), and all other listed distillation baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

MaskSub repurposes the high-ratio random masking mechanism from MAE-style masked image modeling and transplants it into supervised training, but isolates it in a sub-branch that learns via a self-distillation loss from the main branch rather than from ground-truth labels. This distinguishes it from CoSub, which also uses sub-branches but trains them via mutual learning (co-training) rather than distillation, and from prior self-distillation methods (ONE, BYOT, PS-KD) that construct the student from early exits or ensembles rather than from drop-based input corruption. The paper positions itself as a generalization of drop-based regularization (masking, dropout, drop-path) into a unified sub-branch framework, extending the applicability of techniques previously limited to self-supervised or semi-supervised settings into standard supervised recipes like DeiT-III and RSB.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MaskSub offers a simple, hyperparameter-free plug-in (fixed 50% masking ratio) that consistently improves supervised training across ViT, ResNet, Swin, BERT, and CLIP with only ~1.5× compute overhead, making it a practical addition to existing training recipes. The automatic difficulty control—where sub-branch gradients are small early and grow as the main branch converges—provides an intuitive mechanism for why strong augmentation can stabilize rather than destabilize training. Open questions include whether the gains persist at much larger model scales (e.g., ViT-G or beyond), whether the 50% masking ratio is truly universal or should be tuned per architecture, and whether the approach extends to other input-corruption strategies beyond random patch masking (e.g., semantic or structured masking).
