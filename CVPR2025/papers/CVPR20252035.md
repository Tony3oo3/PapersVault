## Problem

Existing anomaly detection methods predominantly target local structural anomalies (scratches, dents, contaminations) and largely fail to detect compositional logical anomalies such as incorrect wiring, misplaced objects, or missing components. While vision-language models have shown promise for structural detection, they struggle with the compositionality required for logical anomalies, and prior logical anomaly approaches demand precise segment annotations and training. The paper addresses the gap of a unified, training-free framework that simultaneously detects both structural and logical anomalies.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a test image at 448×448 resolution, a set of canonical normal images (ranging from 4-shot to full-data) used to build a memory bank via greedy coreset subsampling, and text prompts of interests and compositional rules pre-generated by GPT-4V through the match-of-thought procedure. It produces an image-level anomaly score (the maximum across three calibrated detector scores) and a pixel-level anomaly map (64×64 feature maps upsampled to 256×256 for evaluation). Notably, the composition-granularity detector operates independently of reference images, relying solely on the compositional rules and zero-shot CLIP classification.

## Method

LogSAD introduces a match-of-thought (MoT) architecture that leverages GPT-4V to generate matching proposals—extracting interests of thought and formulating compositional matching rules from vision-language instructions—thereby alleviating LMM hallucination in logical anomaly reasoning. Three multi-granularity detectors then operate on pretrained foundation models (CLIP ViT-L/14 on DataComp-1B, DINOv2 ViT-L/14, SAM ViT-H/16): a patch detector performing nearest-neighbor search in hierarchical feature space, an interest detector formulating anomaly detection as minimum-weight bipartite matching (solved via the Hungarian algorithm) over CLIP+SAM segmented feature sets, and a composition detector that performs zero-shot classification of visual and textual atoms against compositional rules. Anomaly scores from the patch and interest detectors are calibrated using validation-set statistics (mean/std) and passed through a sigmoid, while the composition score is binary. The final decision is the maximum across all three calibrated scores, requiring no parameter updates throughout.

## Results

On MVTec LOCO (the primary benchmark for unified logical and structural anomaly detection), LogSAD achieves 90.2% average image-level AUROC in the full-data protocol without any training, surpassing the best training-based method GRAD (87.5%) by 2.7 points and the best training-free method PatchCore (81.0%) by 9.2 points. In the 4-shot setting, LogSAD reaches 86.3% AUROC, still outperforming all training-based baselines. On MVTec AD and VisA, LogSAD achieves 97.0% image-level AUROC (4-shot) on MVTec AD and 93.0% on VisA, setting new state-of-the-art results in few-shot anomaly detection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LogSAD outperforms all 14 compared baselines on MVTec LOCO. Against training-based methods, it beats GRAD (87.5%) by 2.7 points, SINBAD (86.8%) by 3.4 points, and THFR (86.0%) by 4.2 points in the full-data protocol, while requiring no training at all. Among training-free methods, it surpasses PatchCore (81.0%) by 9.2 points and SPADE (68.8%) by 21.4 points. In few-shot settings, LogSAD improves over PromptAD by 10.3%, 13.1%, and 17.4% under 1-shot, 2-shot, and 4-shot protocols respectively (78.5 vs. 71.2, 82.1 vs. 72.6, 86.3 vs. 73.5). On MVTec AD (4-shot), LogSAD achieves 97.0% image-level AUROC, edging out PromptAD (96.6%) and AnomalyGPT (96.3%); on VisA (4-shot), it reaches 93.0%, substantially ahead of PromptAD (89.1%) and AnomalyGPT (90.6%). The only method that exceeds LogSAD on logical anomaly detection is PSAD (98.1% vs. 89.3%), but PSAD relies on additional segment annotations and training, making the comparison unfair as noted by the authors.

## Relation to Prior Work

LogSAD repurposes off-the-shelf vision-language foundation models (CLIP, SAM, DINOv2) in a training-free pipeline, departing from the dominant paradigm of training dedicated anomaly detectors (PatchCore, PaDiM, THFR, SINBAD). Its 'match-of-thought' module adapts chain-of-thought prompting to generate explicit compositional matching rules via GPT-4V, sidestepping the known compositional reasoning failures of LMMs that prior work (CREPE, Winoground) has documented. The multi-granularity detection design unifies PatchCore-style memory-bank patch matching, bipartite interest matching (inspired by set-feature methods like SINBAD), and a binary compositional rule checker into a single calibrated ensemble, whereas prior logical anomaly detectors (GCAD, PSAD, LogiCode) each require task-specific training and annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that industrial anomaly detection can be deployed without any training or fine-tuning, reducing the barrier to handling new product categories or shifting logical constraints. However, the composition-granularity detector is binary and relies on hand-formulated rules per category (currently only 5 MVTec LOCO categories), raising open questions about scalability to open-ended logical constraints and generalization beyond the curated rule set. The dependence on GPT-4V for one-time prompt generation and on open-vocabulary segmentation quality for interest extraction are practical bottlenecks the authors acknowledge but do not fully resolve. Whether the training-free advantage persists on more diverse or larger-scale logical anomaly benchmarks remains to be tested.
