## Problem

DETR-like vectorized HD map constructors suffer from three interrelated weaknesses: (1) point-set representations underutilize the strong geometric shape priors inherent in map elements, (2) single-frame or naive streaming temporal fusion is unstable under occlusion and long-range inconsistency, and (3) the independent classification and regression branches produce misaligned outputs where high-confidence predictions may have poor localization accuracy. InteractionMap targets all three gaps simultaneously through explicit relation priors, hierarchical temporal fusion, and geometry-aware alignment.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view surround RGB images (6 cameras at 1600×900 for nuScenes, 7 cameras at 2048×1550 for Argoverse2) along with ego-vehicle pose, encoded into a 200×100 BEV feature grid at 0.3 m resolution via an LSS-style view transformation. It outputs 100 instance queries, each predicting a permutation-equivalent set of 20 2D (or 3D) points for lane dividers, pedestrian crossings, and road boundaries within a 60 m × 30 m ego-centric window, plus per-instance binary masks from a query-based instance segmentation branch. Temporal context is maintained online via a key-frame buffer of N_pre=4 submaps selected at a 5 m distance stride.

## Method

InteractionMap builds on a MapTRv2-style DETR decoder and adds three interacting modules. The Relation Embedding Module injects explicit geometric priors into the decoder's self-attention: at the point level, normalized coordinate differences and adjacent-edge cosine-similarity differences are encoded via sine positional encoding and a linear projection; at the instance level, the sign of classification-score differences modulated by pairwise Chamfer distance encodes inter-instance spatial ranking. The Key-frame-based Temporal Fusion Module operates hierarchically—a bottom GRU fuses the current BEV with the warped previous submap for local stability, while a top-level GRU (streaming) or concatenation+ResBlock (stacking) integrates submaps selected at a fixed distance stride for long-range association. The Geometry-aware Alignment Module replaces the standard focal loss with a Geometry-aware Focal Loss whose positive targets are scaled by a composite geometry score (point-to-point L1, edge-direction cosine similarity, and GIoU of minimum enclosing boxes), and replaces the standard focal matching cost with a Geometry-aware Focal Cost that suppresses assignment of ground truths to candidates with poor localization, thereby coupling classification confidence to geometric accuracy during both training and label assignment.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set (60m×30m perception range), InteractionMap achieves 71.8 mAP with a 24-epoch training schedule (KFS-stacking) and 76.0 mAP with 110 epochs, surpassing the previous best (HRMapNet-MapTRv2 at 67.2 mAP and HIMap at 73.7 mAP respectively). On Argoverse2 at 2Hz sampling, InteractionMap reaches 73.7 mAP (2D), outperforming the prior best HRMapNet-MapTRv2 (68.3 mAP) by 5.4 points. At 10Hz sampling, the method achieves 74.6 mAP (2D) and 72.1 mAP (3D), again setting new state-of-the-art. The KFS-streaming variant (InteractionMap-R) runs at 12.1 FPS with 49.9 MB parameters, comparable to MapTRv2's 14.9 FPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InteractionMap outperforms all 11 compared baselines on both nuScenes and Argoverse2. On nuScenes (24 epochs), it surpasses the strongest prior method HRMapNet-MapTRv2 (67.2 mAP) by 4.4–4.6 mAP, and MapTRv2 (61.5 mAP) by 10.1–10.3 mAP. At 110 epochs, the gap over HIMap (73.7 mAP) narrows to 1.8–2.3 mAP, reflecting diminishing returns from longer training. On Argoverse2 at 2Hz, InteractionMap leads HRMapNet-MapTRv2 (68.3 mAP) by 5.4 mAP in 2D; at 10Hz it beats HIMap (69.6 mAP 2D, 68.4 mAP 3D) by 5.0 mAP (2D) and 3.7 mAP (3D). The KFS-streaming variant (R) is notably more parameter-efficient (49.9 MB vs. 116.7 MB for C) while maintaining nearly identical mAP, and runs at 12.1 FPS versus MapTRv2's 14.9 FPS—a modest speed cost for a 10+ mAP gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

InteractionMap builds directly on the MapTR/MapTRv2 DETR-like framework for end-to-end vectorized HD map construction, extending it with three interaction mechanisms: (1) explicit position relation embeddings at point and instance levels, drawing from Relation DETR and Relation Networks in 2D detection; (2) a key-frame-based hierarchical temporal fusion strategy that borrows the local-to-global submap integration paradigm from robot SLAM (sparse/dense mapping) and applies it to BEV feature fusion; and (3) a geometry-aware focal loss and matching cost adapted from IoU-aware focal loss in 2D object detection to address the classification-localization misalignment previously overlooked in map element detectors. The paper departs from prior temporal fusion work (StreamMapNet, SQD-MapNet) by combining streaming and stacking in a hierarchical key-frame architecture rather than relying on a single strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The +10 mAP improvement over MapTRv2 on nuScenes is substantial for this task and is well-supported by ablations across two datasets, making the geometry-aware alignment and key-frame temporal fusion techniques likely to be adopted by subsequent map construction work. The finding that addressing classification-localization misalignment alone yields +7.5 mAP suggests this problem was significantly underappreciated in the vectorized mapping community. Open questions include whether the key-frame strategy scales gracefully to very long sequences or highly dynamic scenes, whether the approach generalizes beyond the three map element categories tested, and whether the added temporal fusion cost (11–12 FPS vs. 15 FPS for MapTRv2) is acceptable for real-time deployment on embedded hardware.
