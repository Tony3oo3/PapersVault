## Problem

Existing video moment retrieval methods inject query text into all video frames during feature alignment, causing irrelevant frames to be spuriously aligned with the query. Additionally, they forcibly regress or enumerate frame features into temporal boundaries, ignoring the inherent information gap between visual semantics and coordinate values, which yields suboptimal localization accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251497/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrimmed video (sampled at 1 frame per 2 s for QVHighlights/ActivityNet, 1 frame per 1 s for Charades-STA) and a natural-language query sentence. Both are encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 encoder into d-dimensional features. The output is a set of top-k (start, end) timestamp pairs, obtained by selecting the k highest-scoring positions in an N×N vertex score matrix, where N is the number of sampled frames.

## Method

The framework consists of two modules. First, Anchor-Aware Feature Alignment (A²FA) identifies the single most query-relevant frame (the anchor) via CLIP cosine similarity, then uses dynamic time warping to delineate a semantically compact segment around it, producing a binary mask that suppresses irrelevant frames. Within this segment, cross-attention aggregates query-relevant frame features into a query-aggregated visual vector, which is compared against each frame to compute relevance scores that re-weight the frame features; a sigmoid-gated interpolation then embeds query information into the re-weighted frames, making aligned features of matching frames highly mutually similar. Second, Frame-Frame Similarity Guided Detection (F²SGD) computes the N×N cosine similarity matrix of the aligned frame features and applies a two-layer CNN followed by an MLP to score every position as the top-right corner of a high-value square block, reformulating interval boundary prediction as a single-point detection problem in 2D similarity space. Training uses MSE loss against a one-hot ground-truth matrix at the true (start, end) coordinate, with Adam at batch size 128 over 10 epochs and cosine LR decay.

## Results

On QVHighlights Test, the proposed method achieves R1@.5 = 80.8, R1@.7 = 64.3, mAP@.75 = 64.3, and mAP@Avg = 63.4, surpassing the previous best CLIP-based method (R²-Tuning) by 12.1% on R1@.5 and 12.2% on R1@.7. On ActivityNet Captions, it reaches R1@.5 = 71.1 and R1@.7 = 43.0, a 7.4% gain over the prior SOTA (Mr.BLIP, R1@.7 = 35.6). On Charades-STA, it attains R1@.5 = 83.8 and R1@.7 = 50.1, outperforming all baselines including those using stronger backbones like BLIP-2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 15 baselines across all three datasets and all reported metrics. On QVHighlights Test, it beats the strongest CLIP-based competitor R²-Tuning [25] by 12.1% (R1@.5: 80.8 vs 68.7) and 12.2% (R1@.7: 64.3 vs 52.1), and surpasses the BLIP-2-based Mr.BLIP [1] by 6.0% on R1@.5 and 3.8% on R1@.7 despite using the weaker CLIP backbone. On ActivityNet Captions, the gain over Mr.BLIP [1] is even larger: 17.2% on R1@.5 (71.1 vs 53.9) and 7.4% on R1@.7 (43.0 vs 35.6). On Charades-STA, the method leads Mr.BLIP [1] by 14.5% on R1@.5 (83.8 vs 69.3) and 0.9% on R1@.7 (50.1 vs 49.2), with the smaller R1@.7 margin attributed to the shorter video durations making boundary localization inherently harder. The pluggability experiments (Tables 7–8) further confirm that A²FA improves any alignment module (e.g., Moment-DETR R1@.7: 34.1→49.1) and F²SGD improves any detection module (e.g., CG-DETR R1@.7: 52.1→55.8), demonstrating that both components are independently effective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in video moment retrieval where all video frames are uniformly aligned with the query via cross-attention (as in Moment DETR, QD-DETR, CG-DETR), arguing that this inevitably aligns irrelevant frames and degrades performance. It also departs from boundary regression or enumeration approaches (UniVTG, TAN, DETR-style) that forcibly map frame features to temporal coordinates, instead reformulating boundary prediction as single-point detection in a 2D frame-frame similarity space. The anchor-frame concept draws on the observation that VLM-based query-frame similarity naturally identifies a standout frame, a signal the authors argue prior VLM-based methods overlooked. The method is conceptually a repurposing of DTW-based segmentation, cross-attention re-weighting, and CNN-based detection into a unified pipeline rather than a direct extension of any single prior architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2D similarity-space reformulation offers a principled way to bridge the information gap between frame semantics and temporal boundaries, and the pluggability experiments (A²FA and F²SGD swapped into MRNet, Moment-DETR, UniVTG, UVCOM, CG-DETR, TaskWeave) suggest both components are broadly applicable building blocks for the VMR community. A key limitation is the reliance on the anchor-frame assumption (that the single most query-relevant frame always falls within the ground-truth interval), which may fail for queries describing distributed or multi-event moments. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much longer videos where the N×N similarity matrix becomes prohibitive, and whether the DTW-based transition-point detection generalizes beyond the relatively short clips in the evaluated datasets.
