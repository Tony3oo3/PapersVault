## Problem

Optimizing backpropagation for efficient training requires simultaneously reducing activation memory, accelerating gradient computation, and preserving model quality, yet no existing method achieves all three objectives jointly. Prior approaches like LBP-WHT and LUQ focus on BP acceleration but either fail to reduce activation memory or suffer significant accuracy degradation on large-scale models, making fine-tuning of resource-intensive architectures (high-resolution generation, long-context LLMs) prohibitively expensive.

## Input / Output

HOT operates on the standard matrix multiplication in each layer's backward pass, consuming the output gradient g_y (L×O), the stored forward-pass activation x (L×I), and the weight matrix w (O×I). It produces an INT4-quantized activation gradient g_x (dequantized to FP32), an INT8-quantized weight gradient g_w (dequantized to FP32), and a compressed activation buffer where x is stored as HLA-projected INT8 values immediately after the forward pass. A key design choice is that the forward pass remains in full FP32 precision to preserve loss evaluation accuracy, with all quantization and low-rank compression applied exclusively to the backward computation.

## Method

HOT's core insight is that the two backward paths (g_x and g_w) have fundamentally different sensitivities to optimization, warranting distinct strategies. For the activation gradient path, HOT applies only INT4 Hadamard Quantization (HQ) without low-rank approximation, since HLA introduces accumulating frequency-domain errors that worsen in deeper layers; the INT4 GEMM is accelerated via TensorCores with a pseudo-stochastic quantizer using the lower 11 bits of FP32. For the weight gradient path, HOT combines internal HLA (reducing the L dimension to r≪L) with INT8 quantization, exploiting the fact that averaging over L acts as a natural lowpass filter that aligns with HLA's frequency selection. Two additional mechanisms enhance the pipeline: Activation Buffer Compression (ABC) applies HLA and INT8 quantization to x immediately after the forward pass, compressing stored activations by up to 75%, and Layer-wise Quantizer Selection (LQS) uses a small calibration set to decide per-layer between per-token and per-tensor quantization based on gradient outlier patterns. When combined with LoRA, HOT skips g_w computation for frozen weights and reverts to standard BP for LoRA's decomposed weights, preserving accuracy while still compressing activations.

## Results

HOT achieves up to 75% memory reduction and a 2.6× average backpropagation speedup (up to 3.3× in ViT-B's fc2 layer) on an RTX 3090 GPU, with negligible accuracy loss versus FP32. On CIFAR-100 fine-tuning of ViT-B, HOT reaches 92.99% top-1 accuracy (vs. 93.45% FP), outperforming LBP-WHT (92.47%) and LUQ (91.76%). In the more challenging full pre-training of ViT-B on ImageNet-1k, HOT attains 69.4% top-1 accuracy (vs. 70.01% FP), while LBP-WHT fails to converge and LUQ degrades to 67.1%. On Llama3-8B Alpaca fine-tuning, HOT achieves 3.29 perplexity (vs. 3.28 FP), whereas both LUQ and LBP-WHT produce NaN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LBP-WHT [42], HOT consistently wins: on CIFAR-10 ViT-B, HOT scores 95.01% vs. LBP-WHT's 91.37% (a 3.6-point gap), and on CIFAR-100 EfficientNetV2-m, HOT achieves 83.91% while LBP-WHT collapses to 55.08%. In full pre-training on ImageNet-1k, LBP-WHT fails entirely (NaN) whereas HOT reaches 69.4% (vs. 70.01% FP). Against LUQ [7], HOT is competitive or superior: on CIFAR-100 ViT-B, HOT (92.99%) beats LUQ (91.76%) by 1.2 points, and on ImageNet-1k pre-training HOT (69.4%) outperforms LUQ (67.1%) by 2.3 points; however, LUQ slightly edges HOT on CIFAR-10 ViT-B (95.70% vs. 95.01%). LUQ also fails (NaN) on EfficientNetV2 and Llama3-8B, where HOT trains stably. Against LoRA [18], HOT+LoRA is broadly comparable to LoRA alone (e.g., 96.03% vs. 96.07% on CIFAR-10 ViT-B), confirming that the two methods are complementary rather than conflicting.

## Relation to Prior Work

HOT builds directly on two Hadamard-based techniques: Hadamard Low-rank Approximation (HLA) from LBP-WHT (Yang et al., NeurIPS 2023) and Hadamard Quantization (HQ) from Xi et al. (NeurIPS 2023). The key departure is a sensitivity analysis showing that the two backward paths (activation gradient g_x and weight gradient g_w) have fundamentally different tolerances to quantization versus low-rank approximation, motivating selective application rather than the uniform strategy used by LBP-WHT. Two new components—Activation Buffer Compression (ABC) and Layer-wise Quantizer Selection (LQS)—are introduced to further reduce activation memory and balance quantization precision per layer, respectively. The method is also positioned as complementary to LoRA, which addresses weight/optimizer memory while HOT targets activation memory and backpropagation speed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HOT's practical impact is significant for resource-constrained settings: enabling batch sizes up to 1024 on a single 24 GB GPU (vs. 256 for FP32) with 75% memory reduction and 2.6× backpropagation speedup while maintaining near-FP accuracy. The path-specific sensitivity analysis provides a reusable conceptual framework for future backpropagation optimization work. However, the authors acknowledge that performance degrades with model depth and size, limiting applicability to very large-scale pretraining. Open questions include whether the selective strategy generalizes to architectures beyond ViT/ResNet/Transformer and whether the empirical LQS threshold (50% MSE difference) can be made more principled.
