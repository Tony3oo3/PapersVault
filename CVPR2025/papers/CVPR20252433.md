## Problem

Accurately characterizing the spatially varying point spread function (PSF) of an imaging system is essential for high-quality deblurring, yet existing methods either require proprietary lens design parameters, use non-parametric models lacking smoothness guarantees, or rely on overly restrictive parametric assumptions. The paper asks whether universal, high-accuracy PSF estimation can be achieved through a simple calibration procedure—akin to industry-standard noise calibration—without prior knowledge of the lens.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252433/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multiple consecutive raw checkerboard test chart images captured in a controlled setup (averaged and converted to linear RGB to minimize ISP nonlinearities) and produces a compact, spatially varying PSF representation: a small set of wavefront aberration coefficients as a function of normalized field height H for each color channel, plus inter-channel PSF shifts. For the downstream deblurring task, the estimated PSFs are used to synthesize training data for off-the-shelf deblurring networks (Restormer, MPRNet, FFTFormer), which then produce deblurred RGB images from real or simulated blurry inputs.

## Method

The core contribution is a novel wavefront-based PSF model that replaces the standard Seidel basis with a modified set of basis functions (Eq. 5) where each term contains either a cos θ or sin θ factor, ensuring every basis influences the spatial frequency response (SFR) along only one axis and thereby eliminating the gradient conflicts that arise when a single Seidel term (e.g., spherical aberration ρ²) simultaneously affects multiple SFR directions. The model is parameterized by two small MLPs: the first (G_Θ1) maps normalized field height H to monochromatic wavefront coefficients per channel, and the second (G_Θ2) maps H and wavelength to inter-channel PSF shifts that capture chromatic aberration. Training proceeds via curriculum learning, progressively optimizing over narrow field-of-view intervals (ΔH ∈ (0.03, 0.1)) from the image center (H=0) to the edge (H=1), with the loss matching predicted SFR curves (and chromatic area differences in the second stage) to measurements extracted from the checkerboard captures. Because the wavefront model is independent of any lens-specific parameters, the framework generalizes across different imaging systems with only a simple calibration capture.

## Results

For PSF estimation accuracy (Table 1), the proposed method achieves PSNR of 50.16 dB and SSIM of 0.983 at H=1 (no noise) on Lens #63762, compared to 44.92/0.966 for Degradation Transfer and 44.99/0.933 for Fast Two-step. For downstream deblurring (Table 3), using Restormer as the deblurring network, the proposed method yields 31.51 PSNR / 0.894 SSIM (no noise) and 31.27 / 0.887 (1% noise) on Flickr2K, outperforming Degradation Transfer (30.69/0.871 and 30.42/0.861) and Fast Two-step (30.34/0.869 and 30.14/0.863). On real captures, the method also achieves the highest MUSIQ and MANIQA scores among the three approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two PSF estimation baselines: Degradation Transfer (Chen et al., ICCV 2021), a deep linear model with optical geometric priors, and Fast Two-step (Eboli et al., ECCV 2022), an empirical affine model based on image gradients. The proposed method consistently outperforms both baselines across all evaluated configurations. In PSF accuracy (Table 1), the gap is most pronounced at the image edge (H=1): the proposed method achieves ~5 dB higher PSNR than Degradation Transfer (50.16 vs. 44.92) and ~5.2 dB higher than Fast Two-step (50.16 vs. 44.99) on Lens #63762 without noise. In deblurring (Table 3), the proposed method gains approximately 0.8–1.2 dB PSNR over Degradation Transfer and 1.1–1.4 dB over Fast Two-step across all three deblurring networks (MPRNet, Restormer, FFTFormer), with the advantage persisting under 1% measurement noise. Fast Two-step produces a single fixed PSF (no variance reported), while both the proposed method and Degradation Transfer show run-to-run variability (±1–2 dB), yet the proposed method's mean consistently leads.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Seidel aberration decomposition (Gray et al. [15]) for representing spatially varying PSFs in rotationally symmetric optical systems, but departs from it by proposing a modified wavefront basis where each term influences only a single SFR direction, thereby mitigating the gradient conflicts inherent in the standard Seidel formulation. It also draws on the chromatic aberration area concept from Lluis-Gomez et al. [28] to formulate the cross-channel PSF shift estimation. The work positions itself against non-parametric approaches (Degradation Transfer [6]) and simpler parametric models (Fast Two-step [11]), arguing that a physics-informed parametric model with a well-structured basis and curriculum-based optimization achieves both higher accuracy and broader applicability without requiring lens design files.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's independence from lens parameters is practically significant: it enables PSF calibration for any imaging system using only a checkerboard chart, removing the intellectual-property barrier that limits optical simulation-based methods in mass production. The two-stage decomposition (monochromatic aberration + cross-channel shift) and the curriculum learning strategy from center to edge provide a principled way to handle the ill-posed inverse problem. However, the authors acknowledge that chromatic aberrations in wide field-of-view images are not fully corrected, and the fundamental ill-posedness of PSF recovery from a single image remains an open challenge. Extending the approach to more complex multi-element lens systems and dynamic scenes would be natural next steps.
