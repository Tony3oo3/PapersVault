## Problem

Existing concept erasure methods for text-to-image diffusion models (e.g., ESD, SPM, MACE) only prevent generation of erased concepts when the target concept text is explicitly present in the prompt. They are easily circumvented by image editing tools (LEDITS++, MasaCtrl), which can reconstruct the erased concept from a source image without mentioning it in the editing text. The paper identifies this 'editing filtration' gap and proposes a method that blocks the erased concept during both generation and editing.

## Input / Output

During training, the method takes a small set of images containing the target concept (e.g., Pikachu) along with their text embeddings, and uses LLM-generated semantically related prior concepts for preservation. At inference, the fine-tuned LoRA adapter is applied to Stable Diffusion 1.4, which accepts either a text prompt (generation) or a source image plus an editing instruction (editing via LEDITS++, MasaCtrl, or SD-Inpainting). The output is a generated or edited image in which the erased concept is suppressed regardless of whether the concept text appears in the prompt.

## Method

ACE injects erasure guidance into both the conditional and unconditional noise predictions of the diffusion model, unlike prior methods that only modify the conditional branch. The key mechanism is an Unconditional Erasure Guidance (UEG) that aligns the tuned model's unconditional noise prediction with a direction pushing away from the target concept; because CFG subtracts the unconditional prediction from the conditional one at inference, the erased concept is suppressed regardless of the input text. To prevent erosion of semantically related concepts, a Prior-Guided UEG (PG-UEG) subtracts a randomly sampled prior concept's guidance (scaled by CLIP-based relevance) from the UEG, and a prior constraint loss regularizes predictions on those prior concepts to match the original model. The final training objective combines the PG-UEG alignment loss, the prior constraint loss, and the original ESD conditional erasure loss, all optimized via LoRA fine-tuning.

## Results

On IP character erasure (Table 2), ACE achieves a generation overall score of CLIPd=0.120 and LPIPSd=0.201, outperforming all baselines (next best: RECE at 0.081/0.156). The headline result is editing filtration: ACE achieves CLIPd=0.029 and LPIPSd=0.097 on LEDITS++-based edits, while all competing methods score near zero (≤0.005), confirming that only ACE prevents erased concepts from reappearing during image editing. For nudity removal (Table 3), ACE reduces total exposed body parts to 39 (vs. 850 for unmodified SD), second only to AdvUnlearn (52) but without adversarial training. On adversarial robustness (Table 5), ACE achieves 19.61% average attack success rate, second-best after AdvUnlearn (16.54%) which uses adversarial training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACE decisively outperforms all five erasure baselines on editing filtration: its LEDITS++ editing overall scores (CLIPd=0.029, LPIPSd=0.097) are 3–10× higher than the next best (RECE at 0.003/0.012), while ESD, SPM, MACE, and AdvUnlearn all score ≤0.005, meaning they fail to block erased concepts during editing. On generation erasure, ACE (CLIPe=0.175) is comparable to RECE (0.176) and AdvUnlearn (0.166) but substantially better than ESD (0.227), SPM (0.239), and MACE (0.250). ACE's key advantage over AdvUnlearn is prior preservation: ACE maintains CLIPp=0.295 vs. AdvUnlearn's 0.209, indicating AdvUnlearn's aggressive erasure erodes unrelated concepts. Compared to RECE, ACE achieves a 48% higher generation overall CLIPd (0.120 vs. 0.081) and a 9.7× higher editing overall CLIPd (0.029 vs. 0.003). The unmodified SDv1.4 baseline shows no erasure (CLIPe≈0.30) and no editing protection, serving as the upper bound for concept presence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the concept erasure line of work (ESD, SPM, MACE, RECE, AdvUnlearn) by identifying that all prior fine-tuning-based methods inject erasure guidance only into the conditional noise prediction, making them vulnerable to circumvention via text-driven image editing (LEDITS++, MasaCtrl) where the target concept text is absent from the prompt. ACE departs from this by additionally aligning the unconditional noise prediction with an erasure guidance (UEG), leveraging the fact that classifier-free guidance subtracts the unconditional prediction during inference. A prior-guided variant (PG-UEG) further subtracts randomly sampled related-concept guidance to prevent erosion of adjacent concepts, and a prior constraint loss regularizes non-target predictions to match the original model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a practical and previously unaddressed vulnerability: IP-protected or safety-erased T2I models can be trivially bypassed by editing an existing image of the erased concept with an unrelated text prompt, undermining the entire erasure pipeline in real-world deployments. The LoRA-based solution is lightweight and compatible with existing editing tools, making it directly deployable. Limitations include evaluation primarily on SD 1.4 with a fixed set of editing methods, reliance on LLM-generated prior concept sets, and no analysis of whether more advanced editing or inversion-based attacks could still recover the erased concept. Open questions include generalization to newer architectures (SDXL, SD3, video diffusion) and interaction with other safety layers.
