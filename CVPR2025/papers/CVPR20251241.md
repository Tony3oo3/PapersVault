## Problem

Detecting 3D reflection symmetry from a single RGB image remains an open challenge: prior methods either require explicit 3D or depth inputs, are restricted to a handful of object categories, or fail to generalize to in-the-wild imagery. This paper asks whether a scalable, zero-shot symmetry detector can be built from a single image and whether the detected symmetry can meaningfully improve single-image 3D generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251241/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single object-centric RGB image (h×w×3) and outputs a set of symmetry plane normals (unit vectors in R³) with per-plane confidence scores; the absolute plane offset d_p is not predicted due to single-view scale ambiguity and is instead resolved downstream via ICP alignment. When used for 3D generation, the pipeline additionally consumes the detected normals to produce a textured 3D mesh via Gaussian splatting and marching cubes extraction.

## Method

Reflect3D uses a frozen DINOv2 encoder to extract geometry-aware image features, which are then queried by N=31 fixed hemisphere-sampled symmetry hypotheses through a transformer decoder with cross-attention; per-hypothesis MLP heads perform binary symmetry classification and quaternion-based normal refinement. To resolve single-view ambiguity, a Zero-1-to-3 multi-view diffusion model synthesizes M=8 surrounding views, and predictions across views are rotated into a common frame and aggregated via KMeans clustering. The detector is trained end-to-end on a curated 84,789-object dataset (Objaverse + ShapeNet, 1,154 categories, ~1M rendered images) with BCE and MSE losses. For 3D generation, detected symmetry is integrated into DreamGaussian through three stages: ICP-based symmetry alignment on a coarse Gaussian point cloud, symmetric SDS optimization (paired view sampling + periodic symmetric densification), and symmetric UV texture refinement using flipped-image supervision for the unobserved backside.

## Results

On the GSO dataset, Reflect3D achieves F@5°=0.390, F@15°=0.756, F@30°=0.848, F@50°=0.889, and average geodesic distance (GD) of 13.3°, substantially outperforming the prior state-of-the-art NeRD (F@5°=0.040, GD=36.2°). On OmniObject3D, Reflect3D reaches F@5°=0.173 and GD=22.8°, compared to NeRD's F@5°=0.055 and GD=41.3°. For symmetry-conditioned 3D generation, the method improves CLIP-Similarity from 0.592 to 0.629 and Chamfer distance from 0.442 to 0.414 on GSO over the DreamGaussian baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NeRD [48], the prior state-of-the-art single-image symmetry detector, Reflect3D achieves a nearly 10× improvement in F@5° on GSO (0.390 vs. 0.040) and a 3.1× improvement on OmniObject3D (0.173 vs. 0.055), while reducing average geodesic distance by 22.9° and 18.5° respectively. Even the feed-forward-only variant (Reflect3D-FF, without multi-view aggregation) already surpasses NeRD by 4.8× in F@5° on GSO, demonstrating the benefit of the transformer-based architecture and DINOv2 features. For 3D generation, the symmetry-aware pipeline improves over DreamGaussian [34] on all metrics: CLIP-Similarity increases by 0.037 (GSO) and 0.030 (OmniObject), Chamfer distance decreases by 0.028 and 0.020, and F@0.5 rises by 0.060 and 0.041, confirming that detected symmetry priors yield tangible gains in both 2D appearance and 3D geometric fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions single-image 3D symmetry detection within the foundation-model paradigm, departing from prior cost-volume approaches (NeRD, NeRD++) that were limited to in-domain categories and from RGB-D methods (SymmetryNet) that required depth input. It builds directly on DreamGaussian for the 3D generation component, integrating symmetry priors into the SDS optimization and texture refinement stages. The key conceptual departure is treating symmetry detection as a scalable transformer-based classification-and-regression problem trained on a large-scale multi-category dataset (Objaverse + ShapeNet), rather than an iterative search over candidate planes, and leveraging multi-view diffusion (Zero-1-to-3) to resolve single-view ambiguity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Reflect3D establishes the first zero-shot, category-agnostic single-image 3D symmetry detector, opening the door to using symmetry as a general structural prior in downstream 3D tasks without category-specific engineering. The demonstrated improvement in SDS-based 3D generation (both geometry and texture on unobserved sides) suggests symmetry cues could be broadly integrated into generative pipelines. However, the approach does not handle fully asymmetric or highly deformable objects, and the reliance on a multi-view diffusion model for the full pipeline introduces additional computational cost and potential artifacts. Open questions include whether the symmetry prior generalizes to articulated or non-rigid objects and whether the feed-forward detector can be further scaled to capture more complex symmetry groups.
