## Problem

Existing video generation models, even at commercial scale (e.g., Luma Dream Machine), fail to produce realistic 3D-consistent camera motion when conditioned on unposed internet photos, instead hallucinating new structures or performing creative morphing. Frame interpolation methods cannot handle the wide baselines and illumination variability of in-the-wild photos. The paper identifies that general video synthesis training is insufficient and that scalable 3D-aware objectives are needed without costly 3D annotations like camera poses.

## Input / Output

The model takes 2–5 unposed internet photos of the same scene as keyframes (segmented to remove transient objects like people and vehicles) plus a CLIP embedding specifying desired illumination. It outputs 15 intermediate frames between each consecutive keyframe pair via 50 DDIM steps in latent space, concatenated into a single video sequence whose camera path follows the input ordering. The generated frames are dense enough to serve as training data for downstream 3D reconstruction (COLMAP, 3DGS).

## Method

The core contribution is a self-supervised training scheme that unifies two complementary objectives under a single latent diffusion denoising loss on a DiT architecture: (1) multiview inpainting, where the model inpaints an 80%-masked target given n wide-baseline condition images from the same scene (trained on MegaScenes, 8M internet photos), teaching 3D geometric priors without any pose annotations; and (2) view interpolation, where the model generates 15 intermediate frames between start/end video frames (trained on RealEstate10k and DL3DV), teaching temporal coherence. Both objectives share the same diffusion forward process (noise added to target patches/frames) and the same transformer with self-attention across all images, distinguished only by frame index embeddings. Illumination control is enforced by conditioning all patches on a CLIP embedding while applying extreme color jittering to condition images, forcing the model to rely on the CLIP signal rather than extracting illumination from inputs. The model infers which task to perform purely from input format (number of images and frame indices) without explicit flags.

## Results

In user studies on the Phototourism test set (252 sparse-view sets), the full method achieves 100% win rate over all three frame-interpolation baselines (FILM, FLAVR, LDMVFI) on consistency, camera motion, and aesthetics, and outperforms Luma Dream Machine with 60.21% (consistency), 73.63% (camera motion), and 60.21% (aesthetics) preference rates. On the Re10k test set (600 sets), the method wins 83.65%/84.86%/68.27% against Luma. Downstream 3D validation shows COLMAP success rate jumps from 45% to 93% when generated frames are added, and 3DGS PSNR improves from 11.70 to 13.96 (Phototourism) and 19.86 to 21.80 (Re10k) when training on generated frames instead of sparse originals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three frame-interpolation baselines (FILM, FLAVR, LDMVFI), the method achieves a near-perfect 100% win rate on Phototourism across all criteria, as these methods are trained on small-baseline video and cannot handle wide-baseline internet photos. On Re10k, FILM is the only frame-interpolation method that produces somewhat logical outputs (81% camera-motion win rate for Ours), while FLAVR and LDMVFI still lose 100%. Against Luma Dream Machine, the method wins on all three criteria on both datasets, with the largest margin on camera motion (73.63% on Phototourism, 84.86% on Re10k); Luma tends to hallucinate new buildings and structures rather than following the scene layout. The video-only ablation (trained without multiview inpainting) performs competitively on Re10k (71–77% win rate) but degrades sharply on Phototourism (96–100% win rate for Full), confirming that internet-photo multiview inpainting is essential for in-the-wild generalization. In 3D reconstruction, adding generated frames from the full method nearly doubles COLMAP's registered-image rate (43%→84%) and improves 3DGS PSNR by ~2.3 dB on Phototourism, whereas the video-only variant yields only modest gains (67% registered, smaller PSNR improvement).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes CroCo's cross-view masked image modeling idea into a multiview inpainting objective for a latent diffusion transformer, extending it from two deterministic views to an arbitrary number of probabilistic condition images. It unifies this with a view interpolation objective (analogous to frame interpolation) under a single denoising loss, finetuning from a pretrained text-to-video DiT. The key departure from prior generation-based view synthesis (Zero-1-to-3, 3DiM, ZeroNVS) is the elimination of camera pose supervision entirely, and from frame interpolation methods (FILM, FLAVR) is the handling of wide-baseline, in-the-wild inputs rather than small-motion video pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that 3D-aware video generation is achievable without any 3D annotations, opening a scalable path to scene-level 3D learning from the vast corpora of unposed internet photos and videos. Generated frames directly improve downstream 3D reconstruction (COLMAP success rate nearly doubles; 3DGS metrics improve substantially), suggesting video models can serve as 3D priors for practical applications. Limitations include no handling of dynamic objects, only coarse illumination control via CLIP embeddings, and restriction to 2–5 keyframes; open questions include whether this approach scales to longer sequences, dynamic scenes, and fine-grained physical constraints.
