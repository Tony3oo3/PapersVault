## Problem

Estimating camera poses and intrinsics from casual, dynamic videos remains unsolved: classical SfM/SLAM pipelines fail on moving objects and suboptimal trajectories, while recent learning-based methods either require ground-truth labels, pre-calibrated intrinsics, or expensive per-scene optimization. No existing approach can robustly recover both motion and intrinsics from unlabelled, dynamic video in a fast, feed-forward manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250898/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a sequence of up to 100 video frames (cropped to 336×336) together with pre-computed monocular depth maps (UniDepth) and dense optical flow maps (UniMatch) between adjacent frames. It outputs frame-to-frame 6DOF relative poses, a single focal length selected from 32 discrete candidates (0.1H to 3.5H), and pixel-wise aleatoric uncertainty maps that double as motion segmentation. A lightweight sliding-window bundle adjustment step optionally refines the chained trajectory, and combining poses, uncertainty, and depth yields 4D pointclouds.

## Method

AnyCam is a DINOv2-initialized ViT transformer that processes per-frame features through stacked self-attention layers to exchange temporal context, producing one pose token per adjacent frame pair and a single sequence token. The key architectural choice is treating focal length as a discrete model property: 32 parallel prediction heads each assume a fixed focal length candidate, and a sequence head learns likelihood scores over candidates via KL divergence against softmax-inverted flow losses, avoiding the instability of regressing focal length directly. Training is fully self-supervised on unlabelled YouTube videos using an uncertainty-aware reprojection loss that models dynamic-object inconsistencies as aleatoric uncertainty, automatically downweighting moving regions without segmentation. A forward-backward pose consistency loss and dropout on pose tokens enforce sequence-level motion priors, while a lightweight test-time bundle adjustment weighted by the predicted uncertainty maps corrects long-term drift.

## Results

On Sintel, AnyCam with test-time refinement achieves ATE of 0.078, RPE_trans of 0.031, and RPE_rot of 0.427, outperforming all compared methods including those with ground-truth intrinsics. On TUM-RGBD (dynamics), it reaches ATE 0.056, RPE_trans 0.005, and RPE_rot 0.927. For intrinsics recovery, AnyCam achieves a mean absolute focal error (AFE) of 252.2 px and relative focal error (RFE) of 0.181%, nearly halving the error of UniDepth (447.4 px, 0.357%) and Dust3R (434.0 px, 0.364%). The method runs in under 2 minutes per sequence including refinement, compared to over 1 hour for CasualSAM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnyCam outperforms all nine baselines on both Sintel and TUM-RGBD (dynamics) across ATE, RPE_trans, and RPE_rot. Against the strongest no-intrinsics competitor MonST3R (concurrent work), AnyCam reduces Sintel ATE from 0.108 to 0.078 (−28%) and RPE_rot from 0.732 to 0.427 (−42%), while also halving TUM-RGBD RPE_trans (0.009→0.005). Compared to CasualSAM, which requires >1 hour runtime, AnyCam achieves lower ATE (0.078 vs 0.141) and RPE_rot (0.427 vs 0.615) in under 2 minutes. Even against intrinsics-aware SLAM systems like LeapVO, AnyCam wins on Sintel ATE (0.078 vs 0.089) and RPE_rot (0.427 vs 1.250) without requiring calibrated intrinsics at test time. For intrinsics recovery, AnyCam nearly halves the focal length error of both UniDepth (AFE 252.2 vs 447.4) and Dust3R (252.2 vs 434.0), demonstrating that video-level context provides substantial benefit over single-image or pair-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

AnyCam departs from the dominant paradigm in learning-based SfM/SLAM (Dust3R, FlowMap, ParticleSfM, LeapVO) where camera poses are recovered by aligning per-view point or depth maps, often requiring per-scene optimization or supervised trajectory labels. Instead, it adopts a feed-forward transformer that directly regresses relative poses and intrinsics, inspired by the focal-length-candidate idea from FlowMap [34] but integrated as multiple prediction heads within the architecture. The training pipeline is the key conceptual departure: by leveraging pre-trained monocular depth (UniDepth) and optical flow (UniMatch) networks as pseudo-supervision and modeling dynamic-object inconsistencies through aleatoric uncertainty, the method eliminates the need for any ground-truth 3D labels, enabling training on large unlabelled video corpora.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: by removing the dependency on labelled 3D data, AnyCam unlocks the vast corpus of casual YouTube videos for training 3D foundation models, directly addressing the data bottleneck that limits general-purpose 3D learning. The feed-forward design with lightweight test-time refinement offers a favorable speed-accuracy tradeoff compared to per-scene optimization methods like CasualSAM (>1h) or ParticleSfM (<20min). Open questions include whether the approach scales to very long sequences beyond the 100-frame window tested, how it performs in multi-camera or stereo settings, and whether the uncertainty maps can be further exploited for downstream tasks like 4D reconstruction or scene editing.
