## Problem

Existing lightweight vision networks rely on self-attention or convolution for token mixing, both of which couple perception and aggregation at the same spatial scale. Self-attention wastes computation on less informative regions and scales quadratically with context size, while convolution uses fixed kernel weights that cannot adapt to varying local contexts. This limits the ability of lightweight models to achieve strong representational power under tight computational budgets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250122/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

LSNet consumes standard RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet classification, 512×512 crops for detection and segmentation) and projects them via overlapping patch embedding into a four-stage feature pyramid at H/8, H/16, H/32, and H/64 resolutions. For classification it outputs a single class label; when plugged into RetinaNet, Mask R-CNN, or Semantic FPN it produces bounding boxes, instance masks, or pixel-wise segmentation maps respectively. The final stage replaces LS blocks with a multi-head self-attention block to capture long-range dependencies at the lowest resolution.

## Method

The core contribution is LS (Large-Small) convolution, which decouples perception and aggregation into two heteroscale steps inspired by the human visual system's peripheral (large-field) and foveal (small-field) processing. Large-Kernel Perception (LKP) uses a 7×7 depth-wise convolution within a bottleneck (point-wise → DW → point-wise) to capture broad spatial context and generate per-token adaptive weights. Small-Kernel Aggregation (SKA) then reshapes those weights into G groups of 3×3 dynamic kernels that convolve only the local neighborhood, enabling context-sensitive feature fusion at minimal cost. The LS block wraps LS convolution with a skip connection, an extra depth-wise convolution, an SE layer, and an FFN for channel mixing. Three model variants (LSNet-T/S/B at 0.3G/0.5G/1.3G FLOPs) are trained with standard supervised learning on ImageNet-1K, with optional knowledge distillation from RegNetY-16GF to further boost accuracy.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, LSNet achieves state-of-the-art accuracy across three model scales: LSNet-T (0.3G FLOPs) reaches 74.9% top-1 with 14,708 img/s throughput, LSNet-S (0.5G) reaches 77.8% with 9,023 img/s, and LSNet-B (1.3G) reaches 80.3% with 3,996 img/s. With knowledge distillation, LSNet-B further improves to 81.6% top-1. On downstream tasks, LSNet-B achieves 39.2 AP on COCO object detection (RetinaNet) and 43.0 mIoU on ADE20K semantic segmentation, while also showing strong robustness with 59.3 mCE on ImageNet-C.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LSNet consistently outperforms all 30 compared baselines across every model scale on ImageNet-1K. At the 0.3G FLOPs tier, LSNet-T (74.9%) surpasses EfficientViT-M3 (73.4%) by 1.5% and StarNet-S1 (73.5%) by 1.4% while matching or exceeding their throughput. At 0.5G, LSNet-S (77.8%) beats UniRepLKNet-A (77.0%) by 0.8% and FasterNet-T1 (76.2%) by 1.6% with higher throughput. At 1.3G, LSNet-B (80.3%) outperforms AFFNet (79.8%) by 0.5% with ~3× faster inference, RepViT-M1.1 (79.4%) by 0.9%, and FastViT-T12 (79.1%) by 1.2%. On COCO detection, LSNet-B achieves 39.2 AP (RetinaNet), exceeding PoolFormer-S12 (36.2) and PVT-Tiny (36.7) by 3.0 and 2.5 AP respectively. On ADE20K segmentation, LSNet-B (43.0 mIoU) surpasses SwiftFormer-L1 (41.4) and FastViT-SA24 (41.0) by 1.6 and 2.0 mIoU. For robustness, LSNet-B achieves the best mCE (59.3) on ImageNet-C and highest top-1 on ImageNet-A (17.3%), ImageNet-R (43.1%), and ImageNet-Sketch (30.7%) among all compared models.

## Relation to Prior Work

LSNet carves new ground by proposing LS convolution as a token mixing primitive that explicitly decouples perception from aggregation at different spatial scales, drawing on the biological 'See Large, Focus Small' strategy of human peripheral and foveal vision. Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of large-kernel convolution work (ConvNeXt, UniRepLKNet) and dynamic convolution research (CondConv, DY-Conv, Involution), but departs from both by using a large-kernel static depth-wise convolution to *generate* context-adaptive weights that parameterize a small-kernel dynamic grouped convolution, rather than treating large and small kernels as independent operations. The perception/aggregation formalism builds on the MetaFormer and EfficientViT framing of token mixing, while the overall architecture follows the hybrid CNN-Transformer design paradigm established by MobileViT, FastViT, and EdgeNeXt.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LSNet provides a strong, well-motivated baseline for lightweight vision networks that achieves SOTA across classification, detection, segmentation, and robustness benchmarks with favorable efficiency trade-offs, and the LS convolution primitive generalizes to other architectures (ResNet, DeiT), suggesting broad utility. The biological grounding offers a clear design principle—heteroscale perception and aggregation—that could guide future lightweight architecture exploration. Open questions include whether the fixed kernel sizes (K_L=7, K_S=3) and grouping ratio (C/G=8) transfer well to substantially different input resolutions or modalities, and whether the approach can be extended to larger-scale models where the efficiency advantage diminishes relative to full self-attention.
