## Problem

Existing post-training quantization methods for Vision Transformers suffer from severe accuracy degradation at low bit-widths (3–4 bits), primarily because the Hessian-guided quantization loss they rely on uses an inaccurate Fisher Information Matrix approximation. Specifically, BRECQ's diagonal FIM approximation omits critical off-diagonal inter-token correlations, and its assumption that FIM diagonal elements equal squared gradients is theoretically incorrect—the paper proves FIM is linearly proportional to the KL-divergence gradient, not its square.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained ViT (ViT, DeiT, or Swin) and a small unlabeled calibration set (1024 ImageNet images for classification, 256 COCO images for detection/segmentation) and produces a fully quantized model using standard channel-wise uniform quantizers for weights and layer-wise uniform quantizers for activations. For detection and segmentation, block reconstruction is applied only to the backbone and FPN, while RPN and ROI modules use calibration-only quantization, with the RPN classification head serving as the task output for FIM computation.

## Method

FIMA-Q follows the QDrop block-wise reconstruction pipeline but replaces the MSE loss with a novel DPLR-FIM quantization loss. The key theoretical contribution is proving that the FIM diagonal is linearly correlated with the KL-divergence gradient (Eq. 11), correcting BRECQ's squared-gradient assumption. Building on this, the paper derives a rank-one FIM approximation that captures collective off-diagonal dependencies among block outputs at O(a) cost, extends it to rank-k via the Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse, and combines both with the diagonal component in a convex DPLR form (Eq. 20–21). A progressive strategy gradually increases the rank k by one every x iterations, recomputing averaged perturbations and gradients to maintain linear independence, with the final loss balancing diagonal and low-rank terms via a hyperparameter α.

## Results

On ImageNet classification, FIMA-Q achieves top-1 accuracy of 76.68% (ViT-S), 83.04% (ViT-B), 66.84% (DeiT-T), 76.87% (DeiT-S), 80.33% (DeiT-B), 81.82% (Swin-S), and 83.60% (Swin-B) at W4/A4, and 64.09% (ViT-S), 77.63% (ViT-B), 55.55% (DeiT-T), 69.13% (DeiT-S), 76.54% (DeiT-B), 77.26% (Swin-S), and 78.82% (Swin-B) at W3/A3. At 3-bit quantization, FIMA-Q surpasses the second-best method by 5.31% on average across all architectures, while many competing methods (PTQ4ViT, RepQ-ViT) collapse to near-random accuracy. On COCO object detection (W4/A4), FIMA-Q achieves 38.7 APb / 37.8 APm (Mask R-CNN Swin-T) and 50.4 APb / 43.7 APm (Cascade Mask R-CNN Swin-S), matching or exceeding all baselines using a standard uniform quantizer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FIMA-Q (uniform quantizer) decisively outperforms all specialized-quantizer methods at 3-bit: it beats DopQ-ViT (54.72→64.09 on ViT-S, +9.37%) and I&S-ViT (45.16→64.09, +18.93%), while PTQ4ViT and RepQ-ViT collapse to near-random (0.10–31%). At 4-bit, FIMA-Q leads all methods on ViT-S (76.68 vs. DopQ-ViT 75.69, IGQ-ViT 73.61) and Swin-B (83.60 vs. DopQ-ViT 83.34, IGQ-ViT 83.14), and matches or slightly trails AdaLog (84.80) on ViT-B. Among uniform-quantizer baselines, FIMA-Q improves over QDrop by 23.04% on ViT-S and 18.18% on DeiT-S at 3-bit, and by 4.84% on ViT-S at 4-bit. At 6-bit, FIMA-Q is competitive with the best specialized-quantizer methods (AdaLog 84.80, IGQ-ViT 84.82 on ViT-B) while remaining hardware-friendly. On COCO detection at W4/A4, FIMA-Q achieves the best APb across all four model configurations (e.g., 38.7 vs. 37.5 for DopQ-ViT/I&S-ViT on Mask R-CNN Swin-T), confirming the advantage extends beyond classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

FIMA-Q is built directly on the QDrop block-wise reconstruction pipeline, replacing its MSE loss with a new DPLR-FIM-based quantization loss. Its key intellectual departure is from BRECQ's Hessian-guided framework: the paper proves that BRECQ's assumption of squared-gradient approximation for FIM diagonal elements is incorrect, showing instead that FIM is linearly proportional to the KL-divergence gradient. This theoretical correction motivates a diagonal-plus-low-rank (DPLR) approximation that captures off-diagonal inter-token correlations discarded by prior diagonal-only methods, positioning the work as a loss-function refinement within the established PTQ-for-ViT paradigm rather than a new quantizer design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that BRECQ's Hessian-guided loss underperforms plain MSE on ViTs is a meaningful correction to a widely-adopted assumption in the PTQ literature, and the resulting DPLR-FIM loss achieves state-of-the-art 3-bit accuracy (5.31% average gain) using only a standard uniform quantizer, making it hardware-friendly unlike competing methods that rely on specialized quantizers. Open questions include whether the DPLR-FIM formulation generalizes beyond ViTs to other architectures (e.g., CNNs, diffusion models), whether the rank-k trade-off can be made adaptive per layer, and whether the approach extends to generative or language tasks where the KL-divergence structure differs. The computational cost grows linearly with rank k, which may become prohibitive for very large models at high ranks.
