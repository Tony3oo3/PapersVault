## Problem

Existing grid-based implicit neural representations retrieve features at continuous-space points via linear interpolation, which implicitly constrains the latent feature space to a degenerate linear form. This linear approximation cannot adequately represent complex nonlinear signals, and the compact decoder that follows cannot compensate for the missing nonlinear structure. The paper asks how to approximate arbitrary nonlinear continuous signals with a discrete grid representation while preserving the simplicity and efficiency of a regular grid.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251548/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes spatial coordinates as input—2D (x,y) for image fitting, 3D (x,y,z) for SDF reconstruction, or 5D (x,y,z,θ,φ) for radiance field rendering—and produces a single reconstructed signal value (pixel color, SDF value, or density/emission). Unlike standard grid indexing that uses a single L1 distance, the input coordinate is simultaneously indexed into M elementary metric grids, each computing a different nonlinear distance (e.g., ‖x−xᵢ‖₂², ‖x−xᵢ‖₃³, or sin/cos-based metrics) to the nearest vertex, yielding a concatenated multi-order feature vector fed to the decoder.

## Method

MetricGrids constructs M elementary metric grids, each defined in a different metric space (p-norm raised to power p, or trigonometric metrics), where each grid stores derivative-related features corresponding to a different-order Taylor expansion term. To maintain compactness, the grids are encoded separately via hash encoding with progressively shorter hash table lengths for higher-order grids (exploiting the sparsity of high-order derivatives in smooth regions), and the maximum spatial resolution is reduced since high-order terms compensate for the loss of fine linear detail. A high-order extrapolation decoder then progressively generates additional higher-order terms by applying Hadamard products between a backbone MLP output and a modulation branch conditioned on the lower-order grid features, with sine and sine-squared activations, achieving a theoretical maximum polynomial order of m(m+3)/2 after m layers. The first grid (d₁) reduces to standard linear interpolation, while grids d₂ and beyond provide the nonlinear approximation that the conventional paradigm lacks.

## Results

On 2D image fitting (Kodak), MetricGrids achieves 41.57 dB PSNR and 0.9735 SSIM with 207K parameters, outperforming the strongest implicit baseline SCONE (40.29 dB) and the strongest hybrid baseline NeuRBF (38.70 dB) by 1.28 and 2.87 dB respectively. On 3D SDF reconstruction (Stanford 3D), it attains 3.09 NAE and 0.0021 Chamfer distance with 905K parameters, improving over I-NGP (3.42 NAE) and NeuRBF (3.20 NAE). On NeRF reconstruction (Blender), it reaches 35.02 dB PSNR and 0.033 LPIPS with 13.11M parameters, surpassing NeuRBF (34.62 dB, 0.034 LPIPS) and I-NGP (33.18 dB, 0.051 LPIPS) at the same 30K training steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetricGrids outperforms all 13 baselines across every task. In 2D image fitting, it beats the strongest implicit method SCONE by 1.28 dB PSNR and the strongest hybrid method NeuRBF by 2.87 dB, while matching their parameter budget (~207K). Against Gaussian-primitive methods (3DGS, GaussianImage), the medium-size variant (335K params) achieves 46.97 dB PSNR vs. 43.69 and 44.08 dB respectively, and the large variant (533K) reaches 52.22 dB. In 3D SDF reconstruction, it improves NAE from 3.42 (I-NGP) and 3.20 (NeuRBF) to 3.09 while matching the best IoU (0.99991) and Chamfer distance (0.0021). In NeRF reconstruction, it surpasses NeuRBF by 0.40 dB PSNR and 0.001 LPIPS, and I-NGP by 1.84 dB PSNR and 0.018 LPIPS, all at the same 30K training iterations with fewer parameters (13.11M vs. 17.74M for NeuRBF). The p-norm metric variant (x², x³) already improves over the I-NGP baseline by 3.57 dB, while the sin/arcsin nonlinear metrics provide an additional ~1 dB gain, confirming that richer nonlinear metric spaces yield better Taylor-expansion-based approximation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the grid-based (hybrid) implicit neural representation paradigm established by InstantNGP and NeuRBF, which localize features in a spatial grid and decode them with a compact MLP. The key departure is a diagnostic argument that the standard linear interpolation indexing function implicitly constrains the latent feature space to a degenerate linear form, and a remedy inspired by Taylor expansion: multiple elementary metric grids in different p-norm (or trigonometric) metric spaces serve as successive high-order derivative terms. The hash-encoding mechanism is directly adapted from InstantNGP but modified with per-grid sparsity (shorter hash tables for higher-order grids), and the high-order extrapolation decoder with Hadamard-product modulation draws on multiplicative filter network ideas (BACON, PNF) to implicitly generate terms beyond the explicitly stored grids.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, Taylor-expansion-grounded explanation for why grid-based INRs underperform on complex nonlinear signals and a concrete architectural fix that maintains the practical advantages of the grid paradigm (fast indexing, scalability to gigapixel images, compact storage). A notable practical limitation is the ~6× increase in training time (195 s → 1136 s on Kodak) relative to the I-NGP baseline, which may constrain adoption in time-sensitive pipelines. Open questions include whether the optimal number of explicit grids (M=3 here) transfers across signal types and resolutions, whether the trigonometric metrics offer further gains beyond the p-norm variants tested, and how the approach interacts with emerging explicit-primitive methods like 3D Gaussian Splatting that are rapidly reshaping the radiance-field landscape.
