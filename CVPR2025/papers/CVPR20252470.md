## Problem

Prior full-body motion generation methods for XR assume tracking signals are always available and accurate, producing jittery motion or abrupt discontinuities when hand-tracking signals are lost or noisy. No existing dataset paired real VR tracking signals with high-quality MoCap ground truth, making it impossible to evaluate or train on realistic tracking failures. This paper addresses both gaps: generating smooth, realistic motion under temporally and spatially sparse inputs, and providing a benchmark for real-world evaluation.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 6-DOF (position + rotation) tracking from the head and both wrists, plus M=10 previously generated body poses as autoregressive context. At each timestep it outputs W future full-body poses (10 frames for reactive mode, 20 for smooth mode) represented as 6D rotations for global orientation and all bone joints. The tracking input window is I=10 frames, and under prolonged signal loss the input context empties, forcing the model to rely solely on its own past predictions. Inference runs at 206.5 FPS on a single GPU.

## Method

RPM reformulates motion generation as progressive refinement: instead of predicting the current pose, the network predicts W future poses, and at each subsequent timestep the previously predicted future becomes the starting point for a new, more informed prediction. The key mechanism is the Prediction Consistency Anchor Function (PCAF), which reparameterizes the output as P_t = P_{t-1} + U·tanh(f_θ(X_t, C_t) − P_{t-1}), where U is a cosine uncertainty schedule that bounds the magnitude of correction—large corrections are only permitted for distant future poses, while near-term poses receive only minor adjustments. This design forces smooth transitions when tracking is lost or recovered, and the reactiveness-to-smoothness trade-off is controlled by tuning W or U. A free-running training stage simulates fr autoregressive steps before computing the loss, exposing the model to its own prediction errors and preventing degeneration from misalignment between generated motion and tracking input.

## Results

On the A-P1 synthetic benchmark (60 FPS), RPM-Reactive achieves MPJPE of 4.08 cm (MC) and 5.18 cm (HT), with jitter of 4.21 and 4.35 respectively. The headline result is in transition smoothness: RPM-Smooth reduces AUJ_S-T to 50.23, over 24× lower than the best baseline HMD-Poser (1236.47), and RPM-Reactive achieves 69.02 (over 17× lower). On the real-world GORP dataset (HT, trained on real inputs), RPM-Smooth further reduces AUJ_S-T to 8.40 and jitter to 0.20, while maintaining competitive MPJPE of 7.57 cm. The model runs at 206.5 FPS on a single A100, confirming real-time capability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RPM decisively outperforms all six baselines on transition smoothness: AUJ_S-T drops from 1236.47 (HMD-Poser, the best baseline) to 69.02 (RPM-Reactive) or 50.23 (RPM-Smooth), a >17× improvement. On jitter in HT mode, RPM-Reactive (4.35) and RPM-Smooth (4.29) beat HMD-Poser (7.35) and all stateless methods (12.79–181.54). However, RPM trades some accuracy for smoothness: on MC MPJPE, HMD-Poser (3.08) and AvatarJLM (3.17) remain slightly better than RPM-Reactive (4.08), and on HT MPJPE, HMD-Poser (4.04) and AvatarJLM (4.59) also edge out RPM (5.18–5.44). The diffusion-based AGRoL performs worst on both jitter (181.54 in HT) and transitions (AUJ_S-T = 29707.36), confirming that iterative denoising is incompatible with online real-time generation. On the real GORP dataset, the same pattern holds: RPM-Smooth trained on real HT inputs achieves AUJ_S-T of 8.40 vs. 102.65 for HMD-Poser, while all methods show ~20% MPJPE degradation when moving from simulated to real tracking inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the established task of full-body motion generation from spatially sparse tracking inputs (AvatarPoser, AGRoL, HMD-Poser, EgoPoser, SAGE, AvatarJLM) to the more challenging setting where inputs are also temporally sparse, as occurs with vision-based hand tracking. The key conceptual departure is the Rolling Prediction Model with PCAF, which reframes generation as progressive refinement of a future window rather than per-frame prediction, drawing an analogy to diffusion models' multi-step denoising but applied to temporal prediction. The free-running training strategy addresses the train-inference distribution shift that prior autoregressive methods (RNN-based or diffusion-based) suffer from, and the GORP dataset fills a critical gap by providing the first paired real VR tracking signals with MoCap ground truth, moving beyond the synthetic benchmarks built from AMASS that all prior works rely on.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The quantified synthetic-to-real performance gap (≈20% MPJPE increase, up to 4× jitter) and the finding that even retraining on real data does not fully close it highlight a fundamental limitation of current evaluation protocols in this field, making GORP likely to become a standard benchmark. The PCAF mechanism offers a practical, tunable trade-off between motion smoothness and tracking reactiveness that is application-dependent (social VR vs. gaming), which prior methods cannot provide. A key open question is whether the deterministic formulation can be extended to multi-modal predictions to recover expressivity during long signal losses, and whether the progressive-refinement paradigm generalizes beyond the specific 6D-rotation + forward-kinematics representation used here.
