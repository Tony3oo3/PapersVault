## Problem

Current embodied video QA benchmarks lack a systematic, top-down evaluation framework for LVLMs in egocentric contexts. They overlook robot-centric (first-person) self-awareness questions, dynamic scene perception, and embodied-specific hallucination issues such as over-confidence in common sense or user input, leaving a critical gap in assessing whether LVLMs can truly function as embodied agents.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250683/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ECBench takes uniformly sampled RGB-D video frames (8–384 frames depending on the model) from 386 indoor videos and a text question as input, producing a text answer scored by ECEval. The 4,324 QA pairs span open-ended and close-ended formats; close-ended answers receive binary 0/1 scores via GPT-4o, while open-ended answers are scored on a 0–1 scale in 0.2 increments against manually annotated 0.5-point reference answers. The benchmark covers 30 fine-grained cognitive dimensions across three test sets: static scenes (19 abilities split into scene-based and robot-centric), dynamic scenes (4 categories), and hallucination (7 perspectives).

## Method

ECBench is constructed via category-independent human annotation, where volunteers are assigned predetermined question quotas per cognitive ability to ensure balanced coverage of all 30 dimensions. A multi-round GPT-4o blind-testing protocol (6 answers per question without visual input, iterated 3 times with manual revision) filters out questions answerable purely by common sense, ensuring high visual dependence. The ECEval framework combines binary scoring for close-ended questions with multi-level scoring anchored to 0.5-point reference answers for open-ended questions, addressing the limitations of either approach in isolation. Videos are sourced from four channels—HM3D virtual agent trajectories, ScanNet/MultiScan real scans, and self-collected counterintuitive and dynamic scenes—providing both static and dynamic embodied contexts with depth maps for future embodied task integration.

## Results

On ECBench, GPT-4o with 32 frames achieves the highest overall score of 50.35, outperforming the best open-source model Qwen2VL-72B-[20f] (44.62) by 12.8% and the best native Video-LVLM LongVA-7B-[384f] (40.47) by 24.4%. All models struggle severely with dynamic scenes (best score: 24.03 for Qwen2VL-72B-[20f]) and hallucination (best: 31.80 for GPT-4o-[32f]), while a blind GPT-4o (no video input) scores only 24.09, confirming high visual dependence. Human performance reaches 94.96, highlighting a substantial gap between current LVLMs and human embodied cognition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o-[32f] dominates all comparisons, scoring 50.35 overall versus 44.62 for the best open-source model (Qwen2VL-72B-[20f]) and 40.47 for the best native Video-LVLM (LongVA-7B-[384f]). Among open-source models, Qwen2VL-72B-[20f] outperforms InternVL2-40B-[20f] (35.94) and Idefics3-8B (33.35) by roughly 9–11 points, with the 72B variant gaining 3.05 points over its 8-frame counterpart. Native Video-LVLMs (Video-LLaMA2, Video-LLaVA, Kangaroo, LongVA) all score below 41, with LongVA's 384-frame input providing only marginal gains over 8-frame models in dynamic scenes, suggesting fundamental limitations in temporal understanding. Embodied/egocentric-specific models underperform general LVLMs: GeLM scores only 21.54 (domain gap from XR training) and AlanaVLM scores 34.75, both well below Qwen2VL-72B. Across all models, dynamic scene scores remain below 25 and hallucination scores below 32, indicating that no current LVLM adequately handles scene dynamics or embodied hallucination, with robot-centric static questions consistently harder than scene-based ones (e.g., GPT-4o-[32f]: 49.04 vs. 59.74).

## Relation to Prior Work

ECBench extends the lineage of embodied VideoQA benchmarks (ScanQA, SQA3D, Env-QA, OpenEQA) by introducing three dimensions absent from all prior work: robot-centric (first-person) questions, dynamic scene evaluation, and systematic embodied hallucination testing. Rather than building on a specific method, it repurposes the general VideoQA evaluation paradigm (capability taxonomy, open-vocabulary QA) and reorients it toward the robot's own perspective, drawing on video sources from ScanNet, MultiScan, HM3D, and self-collected counterintuitive/dynamic scenes. The ECEval framework is a methodological departure from prior benchmarks that rely on purely binary or purely multi-level scoring, combining both with GPT-4o as judge and 0.5-point reference answers for open-ended questions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a stark and practically critical gap: all evaluated LVLMs, including GPT-4o, score below 25 on dynamic scenes and near zero on user-input ambiguity, while achieving 50+ on static scene-based questions. This suggests that current models possess only third-person static cognition and lack the first-person temporal understanding essential for real robot deployment. Purpose-built embodied models (GeLM, AlanaVLM) underperform general-purpose models, raising questions about whether domain-specific training on egocentric video is sufficient without explicit self-awareness and temporal reasoning objectives. Open questions include how to enable robust dynamic scene tracking, how to reduce over-confidence in common sense for counterintuitive 3D arrangements, and whether the robot-centric dimension can be addressed through architectural changes or targeted training data.
