## Problem

Existing lifelong person re-identification (LReID) methods assume relatively stable appearance and fail to balance cloth-relevant and cloth-irrelevant discriminative knowledge when a person's clothing changes irregularly over the streaming learning process. This leads to catastrophic knowledge conflict where the model cannot simultaneously match the same person in both cloth-consistent and cloth-changing scenarios, and prior attempts to handle cloth-changing data rely heavily on historical data and clothing labels, limiting practicality under privacy constraints.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequentially arriving batches of RGB person images (batch size 128, 4 images per identity) from either cloth-consistent or cloth-changing datasets, with only the current dataset's identity labels available and no clothing labels provided. During training, DBSCAN clustering on fine-grained features produces auxiliary clothing labels per image, and the old model's feature space is accessed for knowledge transfer. At inference, a single ResNet-50 backbone extracts features from a query person image and computes cosine-similarity-based rankings against gallery images, evaluated on both cloth-consistent and cloth-changing test sets.

## Method

DKC builds on a distribution-aware prototyping baseline (DKP) and adds three modules. The Differentiated Knowledge Transfer (DKT) module uses DBSCAN to assign fine-grained clothing labels, then constructs a fused similarity matrix that adaptively weights old-model and new-model affinities via coefficient α: same-label pairs receive high new-knowledge weight while different-label pairs receive low weight, enabling selective learning of cloth-irrelevant knowledge without overwriting cloth-relevant knowledge. The Latent Knowledge Consolidation (LKC) module employs a transfer network of RBT blocks to map new features back into the old feature space, with a KL-divergence reconstruction loss that constrains the old feature space to become a latent subspace of the new one, thereby eliminating domain shift. The Dual-level Distribution Alignment (DDA) module aligns old and new knowledge distributions at both the fine-grained level (via softmax similarity of fine-grained feature centers against old prototypes) and the instance level (via per-sample feature distributions), weighted by coefficient β to let cloth-relevant and cloth-irrelevant knowledge coexist compatibly.

## Results

On the CH-LReID task (Order-1: Market → LTCC → PRCC → MSMT17 → CUHK03), DKC achieves an overall average mAP/R1 of 44.2%/55.8%, outperforming the strongest non-joint baseline DKP (41.7%/52.8%) by 2.5%/3.0%. On cloth-changing datasets specifically, DKC reaches 23.5%/30.5% average mAP/R1, surpassing DKP (22.4%/28.1%) and LSTKC (16.2%/21.2%). DKC also achieves the best anti-forgetting score of 3.9%/4.3% AF(mAP/R1), compared to 6.2%/5.8% for DKP. On traditional LReID benchmarks (Order-3/4), DKC attains 51.6%/64.1% and 51.6%/63.5% average mAP/R1, matching or exceeding DKP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DKC outperforms all sequential learning baselines on both cloth-consistent and cloth-changing metrics. Against the strongest non-joint competitor DKP (CVPR 2024), DKC gains +2.5% mAP and +3.0% R1 overall, with the largest margin on cloth-changing data (+1.1% mAP, +2.4% R1). LSTKC (AAAI 2024) and PatchKD (ACM MM 2022) trail DKC by 6.0%/5.9% and 6.4%/8.8% in overall mAP/R1 respectively, primarily due to their inability to handle cloth-changing knowledge conflicts. USP (IEEE TCSVT 2024), which explicitly targets cloth-hybrid scenarios, achieves 38.7%/43.8% overall, still 5.5%/12.0% below DKC. Notably, DKC even surpasses the Joint training upper bound (43.8%/55.3%) on overall mAP/R1, indicating that its differentiated knowledge consolidation strategy extracts more discriminative information than joint training. On traditional LReID (Order-3/4), DKC matches DKP (51.6% vs 51.8% mAP on Order-3) while slightly exceeding it on Order-4 (51.6% vs 51.3%), confirming generalizability to cloth-consistent-only settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the non-exemplar lifelong person re-identification (LReID) line of work—building directly on DKP's distribution-aware prototyping as its baseline—by introducing a 'cloth-hybrid' setting where both cloth-consistent and cloth-changing datasets appear sequentially in the stream. It departs from prior LReID methods (AKA, PatchKD, LSTKC) that implicitly assume clothing consistency, and from static CC-ReID methods (DSIFLF, DCR-ReID) that handle cloth-changing but without sequential learning. The key conceptual departure is the 'differentiated knowledge' framing: rather than treating all identity features uniformly, the method explicitly separates cloth-relevant and cloth-irrelevant knowledge and adaptively balances them via fine-grained DBSCAN clustering, latent feature-space reconstruction (RBT blocks), and dual-level distribution alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CH-LReID formulation is a practically important step toward realistic deployment, where a surveillance or tracking system must handle both same-clothing and different-clothing matches over long time horizons without access to historical data. The differentiated knowledge framework offers a reusable conceptual lens for any continual recognition task where discriminative cues shift over time. Limitations include reliance on DBSCAN clustering quality for fine-grained labels, evaluation on only 7 datasets (5 cloth-consistent + 2 cloth-changing), and the approach being specific to person ReID with ResNet-50; whether the differentiated knowledge balancing generalizes to other biometric or object recognition domains remains open.
