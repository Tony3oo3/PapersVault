## Problem

Existing visible-infrared person re-identification (VI-ReID) methods rely on centralized training with unrestricted data sharing across cameras and entities, which is impractical in real-world surveillance where strict privacy regulations and data ownership constraints prohibit such sharing. No prior benchmark or method addresses VI-ReID under privacy-preserving, decentralized training conditions where data from individual cameras or entities must remain isolated.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250520/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single pedestrian image (visible or infrared, resized to 288×144) as input, with the critical constraint that under the Camera Independence protocol, a given camera may contain only visible, only infrared, or mixed images—modality is unknown at training time. Output is an L2-normalized feature embedding used for rank-k matching, evaluated via rank-1/10 accuracy, mAP, and mINP in infrared-to-visible mode. Sampling is done by identity (K images from P identities) rather than by modality, eliminating the need for paired visible-infrared inputs.

## Method

DPPT (Decentralized Privacy Preserved Training) is a federated learning framework built on a one-stream ResNet-50 backbone that replaces the standard two-stream VI-ReID architecture, combined with identity-based sampling and channel augmentation to handle modality incompleteness. The key mechanism is a Memory Rectification Bank (MRB): each client computes per-identity class centers, refines them by averaging the top-K closest feature embeddings (filtering low-information samples), uploads these refined centers to a server, and the server aggregates them into a global memory via indicator-weighted averaging across clients. During local training, a cosine-similarity MRB loss pulls current feature embeddings toward the corresponding global identity center, aligning all clients toward a shared convergence point without sharing raw data. The total loss combines identity loss, circle loss, and the MRB loss (weighted by λ=1), with model weights aggregated via data-size-weighted FedAvg.

## Results

Under the strictest CI protocol (each camera's data fully isolated), DPPT with FedAvg aggregation achieves rank-1 of 51.27% and mAP of 49.29% on SYSU-MM01, 59.85% and 55.58% on RegDB, and 34.69% and 41.91% on LLCM, substantially outperforming the FedAvg baseline (27.51%/29.98%, 19.07%/21.05%, 26.24%/33.52% respectively). Under the EI protocol (entity-level isolation), DPPT achieves rank-1 of 11.27%, 21.54%, and 14.63% across three cross-domain splits, matching or exceeding the best ES-protocol methods (e.g., DNS at 11.75%, 18.87%, 10.14%) that train on fully shared data. This demonstrates that privacy-preserved decentralized training does not sacrifice generalization to unseen entities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under the CI protocol, DPPT consistently outperforms all four federated learning baselines (FedProx, FedNova, Moon, FedAvg) across all three datasets and all four metrics. The largest gains appear on RegDB, where DPPT with FedAvg improves rank-1 from 19.07% (FedAvg) to 59.85% and mAP from 21.05% to 55.58%. Even the best adapted VI-ReID method (DNS†) trails DPPT by 11.67% rank-1 on SYSU-MM01 (39.60% vs. 51.27%) and 11.37% on RegDB (48.48% vs. 59.85%), confirming that simply removing modality information from existing architectures is insufficient. Under the EI protocol, DPPT (rank-1: 11.27%, 21.54%, 14.63%) matches or exceeds the best ES-protocol methods (DNS: 11.75%, 18.87%, 10.14%; CAJ: 10.90%, 16.55%, 11.35%), showing that entity-level data isolation does not degrade cross-domain generalization. The ERM baseline B under ES (8.63%, 17.08%, 8.74%) is notably weaker than DPPT under EI, further validating the benefit of the Memory Rectification Bank mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant centralized-training paradigm in VI-ReID (DNS, AGW, CAJ, DEEN, LBA) by formalizing privacy constraints as a first-class design requirement, drawing on federated learning (FedAvg, FedProx, MOON, FedNova) as the technical substrate. It identifies three task-specific obstacles—modality incomplete, identity missing, and domain shift—that standard FL methods do not address, and introduces the Memory Rectification Bank (MRB) as a ReID-specific aggregation mechanism that goes beyond simple weight averaging. The cross-domain evaluation protocol (training on seen entities, testing on an unseen one) also extends the domain-generalization literature by doing so without sharing source-domain data, a setting prior DG methods do not consider.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is clear: real surveillance deployments cannot assume unrestricted data sharing, and this is the first benchmark that forces VI-ReID methods to operate under such constraints. The finding that decentralized training under EI matches or exceeds centralized ES performance on unseen entities suggests privacy need not come at a generalization cost, which could accelerate real-world adoption. However, the evaluation is limited to three datasets with only two training entities and one test entity, leaving open whether the conclusions hold at larger scale (many entities, heterogeneous camera counts) or with more aggressive privacy mechanisms (differential privacy, secure aggregation). The low absolute cross-domain accuracy of existing VI-ReID methods under ES also highlights a broader gap in generalization that this paper surfaces but does not fully resolve.
