## Problem

Illumination spectrum estimation in multispectral images is an underconstrained problem because the observed spectrum is an entangled product of illumination and surface reflectance. Previous deep learning approaches largely overlooked the joint modeling of illumination and reflectance, while model-based methods relying on low-rank factorizations become unstable when the number of channels is insufficient relative to the spectral basis. The paper addresses this gap by integrating a physics-informed surface reflectance model directly into a deep learning pipeline for illumination estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251337/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single multispectral image with 31 spectral channels spanning 400–700 nm at 10 nm intervals, along with pre-computed GMM parameters (endmember means and abundance maps) derived from training data via EM. It produces a global 31-channel illumination spectrum vector and an intermediate surface reflectance map (H×W×31). The reflectance map is not directly supervised with ground truth but serves as an auxiliary output to enforce the illumination-reflectance separation constraint during training.

## Method

The ISS framework combines a learnable Spectral Unmixing block with a Feature Mixing Block (FMB) in an iterative refinement loop. The SU block initializes endmembers and abundance maps from GMM parameters (K=5 components) and passes them through learnable convolution layers, making the decomposition adaptable to real-world spectral variability. The FMB fuses these spectral features with CNN spatial features via cross-attention, where endmembers serve as keys/values, CNN features as queries, and abundance maps are added to the attention scores before softmax; the fused output is iteratively refined over 6 GRU steps. A two-branch decoder then produces the illumination spectrum (from concatenated CNN and refined features through conv+linear layers) and the reflectance map (from the refined features through transposed convolutions). Training uses a combined L1 reconstruction loss (reconstructing the input image from predicted illumination × reflectance) and an angular error loss on the illumination spectrum, with weights α₁=0.5 and α₂=1.

## Results

On the KAUST dataset, the proposed ISS method achieves 5.55° mean-ΔAMS and 3.29° std-ΔAMS, outperforming all six baselines. On the unseen CAVE dataset, it maintains strong performance with 5.93° mean-ΔAMS and 3.90° std-ΔAMS. On the real-world BeyondRGB dataset, ISS achieves 3.65° (Lab) and 5.97° (Field) mean-ΔAMS, showing the largest performance gap over the second-best method (BeyondRGB [14]) in complex scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed ISS method outperforms all six baselines on every dataset and metric. The closest competitor is BeyondRGB [14] (a pyramid-based CNN for MS color constancy), which ISS beats by 1.75° on KAUST (5.55 vs. 7.30), 0.71° on CAVE (5.93 vs. 6.64), and 2.27° on BeyondRGB Lab (3.65 vs. 5.92). DUN [40] (deep unrolling network) is the second-closest on KAUST at 9.17° but degrades to 16.04° on CAVE, while ISS remains stable. Statistical methods (GrayEdge [27], LRMF [72], ISNL [58], PWIR [54]) are consistently 2–5× worse in angular error, with PWIR [54] performing worst on CAVE (34.95°). The largest relative gains appear on the real-world BeyondRGB dataset, where ISS reduces mean-ΔAMS by 38% over BeyondRGB [14] in the Lab split, indicating the spectral unmixing and cross-attention fusion are particularly effective in complex, diverse scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the illumination-reflectance separation (IRSS) framework by introducing a learnable Gaussian Mixture Model-based spectral unmixing (SU) block into the illumination estimation pipeline—a component previously unattempted in this task. It builds on GMM-based hyperspectral unmixing (Ma et al. 2019; Zhou et al. 2018) by making endmembers and abundances learnable tensors, and on cross-attention mechanisms from spectral-spatial transformer literature to fuse SU-derived spectral features with CNN spatial features. The approach departs from prior deep learning methods for MS illumination estimation (DUN, BeyondRGB) that treated illumination and reflectance independently, instead enforcing a joint physical decomposition through a reflectance decoder and angular-error supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly modeling surface reflectance via spectral unmixing within a deep network meaningfully improves illumination spectrum estimation, particularly on complex real-world scenes (BeyondRGB) where prior methods struggle with spectral diversity. The ablation showing a 3.65° angular error increase when the cross-attention feature mixing block is removed underscores that naive concatenation of spectral and spatial features is insufficient. However, the method is limited to global (single) illumination estimation and 31-channel inputs; extending it to locally varying or multi-illuminant scenes, and to higher-channel-count hyperspectral data, remains open. The reliance on EM-initialized GMM parameters (K=5) also raises questions about robustness to scenes with very different material compositions.
