## Problem

State-of-the-art 3D medical segmentation architectures (SwinUNETR, SwinUNETRv2, 3D UX-Net) incur prohibitive computational costs—up to 632 GFLOPs and 53M parameters—driven largely by decoder designs that use excessive channel counts and high-resolution convolutional stages. These costs limit deployment in real-time clinical settings and resource-constrained environments, while existing efficiency-focused alternatives sacrifice segmentation accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252373/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a 3D volumetric input (e.g., 96×96×96 single-channel or multi-channel medical image) and produces a 3D segmentation mask at the same spatial resolution. Internally, the decoder operates at half resolution (D/2 × H/2 × W/2) and recovers full resolution via a single 2× trilinear upsampling step after a 1×1×1 prediction convolution, avoiding the expensive full-resolution convolutional stages present in original decoders.

## Method

EffiDec3D is a drop-in optimized decoder that applies two key strategies: (1) a uniform channel reduction that sets all decoder stages to C_reduced = min(C_i) across encoder stages (e.g., 48 channels), implemented via ChannelReductionResidualBlocks (two 3×3×3 convolutions with Instance Norm and ReLU plus a residual path), and (2) removal of the highest-resolution decoder stage, capping upsampling at D/2 × H/2 × W/2. Upsampling uses transposed convolutions followed by element-wise addition (rather than concatenation) with encoder skip connections to minimize parameter overhead. The final output is produced by a single 1×1×1 convolution mapping to the target number of classes, followed by 2× trilinear interpolation. This design achieves 96.4% parameter reduction and 93.0% FLOP reduction for 3D UX-Net (and 94.9%/86.2% for SwinUNETR/SwinUNETRv2) while maintaining or slightly improving DICE scores across 12 medical imaging benchmarks.

## Results

On FeTA 2021, 3D UX-Net with EffiDec3D achieves the best average DICE score of 87.97% (vs. 87.28% for the original) while reducing parameters from 53M to 3.16M (−94%) and GFLOPs from 632 to 51.5 (−92%). SwinUNETRv2 with EffiDec3D reaches 87.91% average DICE (vs. 87.29% original) with 18.21M params and 63.29 GFLOPs. On BTCV, the optimized 3D UX-Net maintains 79.25% average DICE (vs. 79.74% original) at 51.47 GFLOPs, and on the 10-task MSD benchmark, SwinUNETRv2 with EffiDec3D achieves the best 10-task average DICE of 74.71%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's optimized models (EffiDec3D applied to 3D UX-Net, SwinUNETR, SwinUNETRv2, and MedNeXt) achieve the best or near-best DICE scores across all three benchmarks while using a fraction of the parameters and FLOPs of every baseline. On FeTA 2021, 3D UX-Net w/EffiDec3D (87.97% DICE, 3.16M params, 51.47 GFLOPs) outperforms all baselines including nnFormer (87.03%, 149M params), TransBTS (87.18%, 31.6M params), and MedNeXt (87.74%, 17.55M params) in both accuracy and efficiency. The efficiency-focused baselines SlimUNETR (85.64%) and SegFormer3D (86.57%) are more compact but sacrifice 1.4–2.3% DICE. On BTCV, the optimized 3D UX-Net (79.25%) is within 0.5% of the original (79.74%) while using 94% fewer parameters, and SwinUNETR w/EffiDec3D (79.82%) slightly trails the original (80.13%) but beats UNETR++ (80.92% is the best baseline) by a small margin. On the 10-task MSD benchmark, SwinUNETRv2 w/EffiDec3D achieves the best 10-task average DICE (74.71%), surpassing the original SwinUNETRv2 (73.83%) and all other baselines, confirming that the decoder optimization generalizes across diverse anatomical structures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not build on a single prior method but instead identifies a shared architectural bottleneck—the over-parameterized, high-resolution decoder—across multiple SOTA 3D segmentation models (3D UX-Net, SwinUNETR, SwinUNETRv2, MedNeXt) and proposes a unified drop-in decoder replacement. It departs from prior efficiency-focused architectures (SlimUNETR, SegFormer3D, UNETR++) that traded accuracy for speed, by showing that targeted channel reduction and removal of the highest-resolution decoder stage can achieve comparable or better DICE scores at a fraction of the cost. The design draws on standard U-Net decoder components (transposed convolutions, skip connections, residual blocks) but reconfigures them with a uniform minimum channel count and a restricted upsampling resolution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 96.4% parameter and 93% FLOP reduction while maintaining (or marginally improving) DICE across 12 diverse medical imaging tasks makes 3D segmentation feasible on resource-constrained hardware (hospitals with limited GPUs, embedded systems), which is a practical deployment barrier the field has struggled with. A key open question is whether the fixed 48-channel decoder and D/2 resolution cap generalize to tasks with very fine or highly variable anatomical structures, where the paper itself notes minor DICE drops (e.g., esophagus, veins, deep grey matter). Future work could explore task-adaptive channel counts or learned resolution selection to close the remaining gap on small structures without sacrificing the efficiency gains.
