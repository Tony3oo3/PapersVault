## Problem

Training controllable human image generation models with multiple reference garments is bottlenecked by data acquisition: collecting product-view photographs of every garment worn by each human subject is prohibitively labor-intensive. Existing workarounds—segmenting garments directly from human images or extracting them across video frames—produce references that are either shape-locked to the source pose (causing copy-paste artifacts) or low-quality (causing subject blending and inconsistency).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251335/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N separate product-view garment images (e.g., a shirt, pants, shoes, bag) at 512×384 resolution along with a text prompt, and optionally a pose map or face image, to produce a single human image wearing all provided garments. Garments are consumed as independent reference images rather than as a single composite, and the model supports an arbitrary number of garment categories per generation. Because the generator backbone (SDXL) remains frozen, additional conditional inputs such as ControlNet pose maps or IP-Adapter face embeddings can be attached at inference without retraining.

## Method

BootComp is a two-stage framework. First, a decomposition module—fine-tuned from a pretrained T2I diffusion model—learns to map a segmented garment region (obtained via SegFormer human parsing) to a product-view garment image, using an extended self-attention mechanism that concatenates the segmented garment's key/value vectors into the denoising path. This module bootstraps a large synthetic paired dataset (240K human–multi-garment pairs) from existing single-garment datasets, with a DreamSim-based perceptual similarity filter (threshold τ=0.4) discarding low-quality extractions. Second, the composition module pairs a frozen SDXL generator with a trainable encoder of the same architecture; the encoder processes each reference garment image and injects its features into the generator via extended self-attention, where garment hidden states are concatenated with the target's hidden states in the key and value matrices of every attention layer. Only the encoder is optimized under the standard ε-prediction diffusion loss, keeping the generator frozen so that BootComp can be composed with other adapter modules (ControlNet, IP-Adapter) for pose-guided, stylized, or personalized generation without additional fine-tuning.

## Results

BootComp achieves the best performance across all four evaluation metrics on the multi-garment human image generation task. It attains an MP-LPIPS of 0.187 (a 30% improvement over the closest baseline), DINO of 0.379, M-DINO of 0.046, and FID of 27.63. The evaluation is conducted on 5,000 garment image sets (upper garments, lower garments, shoes) for similarity metrics and 30,000 human images for FID, drawn from DressCode, VITON-HD, and DeepFashion test splits.

| MIP-Adapter [15] | 0.276 | 0.308 | 0.025 | 59.99 |

| **BootComp (ours)** | **0.187** | **0.379** | **0.046** | **27.63** |

## Baselines & Comparisons

BootComp outperforms both baselines on all four metrics. Against MIP-Adapter [15], BootComp reduces MP-LPIPS from 0.276 to 0.187 (32% improvement), improves DINO from 0.308 to 0.379, nearly doubles M-DINO from 0.025 to 0.046, and cuts FID from 59.99 to 27.63, indicating substantially better garment fidelity and image quality. Against the more directly comparable Parts2Whole [16], BootComp still leads on every metric: MP-LPIPS 0.187 vs. 0.267 (30% lower), DINO 0.379 vs. 0.362, M-DINO 0.046 vs. 0.036, and FID 27.63 vs. 28.39. Qualitatively, Parts2Whole tends to replace reference garments (e.g., swapping soccer cleats for stilettos) or blend garments together, while BootComp faithfully preserves each reference garment's fine details even in unusual combinations.

## Relation to Prior Work

BootComp positions itself at the intersection of controllable multi-subject image generation (MIP-Adapter, Parts2Whole, MS-Diffusion) and virtual try-on, but departs from both lines by addressing the data curation bottleneck that plagues prior approaches. Where segmentation-based methods (Parts2Whole) produce copy-paste artifacts and video-segmentation methods (MS-Diffusion) yield low-quality references, BootComp introduces a decomposition module that translates segmented garments into product-view images, effectively bridging self-data-generation techniques (which fail for multi-subject scenes) and segmentation-based curation. The extended self-attention conditioning mechanism is conceptually related to IP-Adapter-style reference injection, and the frozen-generator/trained-encoder split follows the adapter paradigm, but the specific two-stage synthetic-data pipeline is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for fashion and e-commerce applications: a single unified model handles multi-garment generation, virtual try-on, pose control, and stylization without task-specific fine-tuning, thanks to the frozen generator design that allows plug-and-play integration with ControlNet or IP-Adapter. However, the approach depends on human parsing accuracy (errors propagate into the decomposition module), the filtering threshold is heuristic, and evaluation relies on a manually curated 5K test set rather than a standard benchmark. Open questions include whether the pipeline scales to more than 7 garment categories, whether the product-view decomposition generalizes to non-human subjects, and whether the 30% MP-LPIPS gain holds against stronger multi-condition baselines as the field evolves.
