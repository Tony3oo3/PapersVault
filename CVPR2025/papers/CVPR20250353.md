## Problem

Existing deepfake video detectors struggle with cross-dataset generalization, and spatiotemporal models tend to over-rely on spatial artifacts while neglecting temporal ones. While image-level blending has proven effective for generalizing image forgery detection, no prior work has identified a general temporal forgery artifact or designed a video-level blending strategy to simulate it. Additionally, efficiently balancing spatial and temporal learning without heavy computation remains an open challenge.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 8 consecutive frames sampled from a 32-frame video clip, processed through a pretrained image backbone (default: CLIP ViT-L14) with the StA adapter inserted at intermediate stages. The output is a binary real/fake classification score at the video level, evaluated via AUC, accuracy, and EER. The plug-and-play design means the backbone can be swapped (ResNet-34, ViT-B/16, CLIP-B/L) without architectural redesign, with only ~22–46M trainable adapter parameters regardless of backbone size.

## Method

The paper identifies Facial Feature Drift (FFD)—subtle frame-to-frame inconsistencies in facial organ positions caused by generative randomness in deepfake pipelines—and proposes Video-level Blending (VB) to simulate it: each frame's facial landmarks are randomly perturbed (rotation, scale, translation), the warped image is blended back via region-specific masks with distance-based falloff, and the process is repeated across all frames to create hard-negative pseudo-fake clips. For the architecture, a lightweight Spatiotemporal Adapter (StA) is inserted into a frozen pretrained image backbone, consisting of two parallel streams of multi-scale depthwise 3D convolutions (spatial kernel (1,N,N) and temporal kernel (N,1,1) at scales 3/5/7) followed by bidirectional cross-attention (S2T and T2S) that fuses spatial and temporal features at a high level. Only the adapter and cross-attention parameters are updated during training, preserving the backbone's pretrained generalization while adding temporal understanding at minimal computational cost.

## Results

On Protocol-1 (cross-dataset, 7 test sets), the proposed method achieves an average AUC of 0.911, surpassing the strongest prior image-based detector CFM (0.856) by 5.5% and LSDA (0.847) by 6.4%. On Protocol-3 (cross-dataset with video detectors), it reaches 0.895 average AUC, outperforming the best video method LTTD (0.849) by 4.6%. On Protocol-2 (cross-manipulation within FF++ domain), the method achieves 0.942 average AUC, beating AltFreezing (0.859) by 8.3%. The plug-and-play StA adapter adds only ~22–46M trainable parameters on top of a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 backbone (307M total), achieving 0.947 AUC on CDF-v2 with 278 GFLOPs versus VideoMAE's 597 GFLOPs at 0.830.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method (VB + StA on CLIP ViT-L/14) outperforms all 26 compared baselines on average across all three evaluation protocols. Against the strongest image-based detectors, it beats CFM (TIFS 2023, 0.856 avg) by 5.5% and LSDA (CVPR 2024, 0.847 avg) by 6.4% on Protocol-1, with the largest gains on DFDC (+9.3% over LSDA) and FFIW (+4.2% over LSDA). Against video-specific methods on Protocol-3, it surpasses LTTD (NeurIPS 2022, 0.849 avg) by 4.6% and TALL-Swin (ICCV 2023, 0.838 avg) by 5.7%. On the cross-manipulation protocol (Protocol-2), it achieves 0.942 avg AUC versus AltFreezing's 0.859, with particularly large margins on e4s (0.980 vs 0.605) and SimSwap (0.931 vs 0.797). The only baselines that exceed it on individual datasets are LAA-Net (0.954 on CDF-v2, 0.984 on DFD), but LAA-Net was trained on the uncompressed raw FF++ version whereas this method uses the standard c23 compressed version, making the comparison not fully apples-to-apples. The plug-and-play design also outperforms dedicated video architectures (e.g., ViViT 310M params, ISTVT) while training only 22–46M adapter parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the image-level blending data synthesis paradigm (Face X-ray, SBI, SLADD) into the video domain by identifying Facial Feature Drift (FFD) as a general temporal artifact and proposing Video-level Blending (VB) to simulate it. Architecturally, it departs from prior spatiotemporal deepfake detectors (FTCN, AltFreezing, NACO) that train full 3D-CNN or Transformer video models from scratch, instead repurposing the parameter-efficient adapter/PEFT concept to bolt lightweight spatiotemporal modules onto frozen pretrained image backbones (CLIP, ViT, ResNet). The work thus carves new ground at the intersection of data-synthesis-driven generalization and adapter-based transfer learning, while the FFD observation itself builds on earlier notes of temporal inconsistency (lip movement in LipForensics, frame-level inconsistency in STIL) but reframes it as a facial-organ-specific, generative-randomness-driven phenomenon.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play StA design means the method can immediately benefit from future stronger image backbones without re-architecting the video pipeline, and the 46M trainable parameters (vs. 307M for VideoMAE) make it practical for resource-constrained deployment. The FFD identification provides a concrete, generalizable temporal artifact that is orthogonal to spatial blending boundaries, and the ablation showing VB (temporal) outperforming Fixed SBI (spatial) in video settings suggests temporal cues may be underexploited in current detectors. Open questions include whether FFD persists in newer diffusion-based or autoregressive video generators, how to optimally combine image-level and video-level blending (the paper shows they are complementary but does not fully explore this), and whether the landmark-perturbation-based VB generalizes to non-face-swap forgeries (e.g., full-face generation).
