## Problem

Most latent diffusion models train and generate at 1024×1024, leaving direct 4K image synthesis largely unexplored. Existing 4K-capable approaches (PixArt-Σ, Sana) prioritize efficiency but neglect the intrinsic high-frequency details and rich textures that make 4K images valuable, and no publicly available benchmark or fine-detail evaluation metrics exist for ultra-high-resolution synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250304/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt as input and directly synthesizes photorealistic images at 4096×4096 resolution (or other aspect ratios and even higher resolutions) in a single diffusion pass. The text encoder (CLIP + T5-XXL for SD3/Flux) is kept frozen, and the partitioned VAE with F=16 downsampling handles the 4K pixel-to-latent conversion without out-of-memory issues, reducing the latent token count compared to the standard F=8 VAE.

## Method

Diffusion-4K introduces two key technical components. First, a Partitioned VAE achieves F=16 downsampling by applying a dilation rate of 2 in the encoder's first convolutional layer and partitioning the decoder's final feature map into sub-regions that are individually up-sampled and convolved, maintaining latent-space consistency with pre-trained models without retraining. Second, Wavelet-based Fine-tuning (WLF) modifies the diffusion training objective by applying a Haar discrete wavelet transform to both the predicted velocity/noise and the target data, decomposing them into low-frequency approximation and three high-frequency sub-bands, so the loss explicitly emphasizes high-frequency detail reconstruction while preserving low-frequency structure. The method is model-agnostic, demonstrated by fine-tuning both SD3-2B and Flux-12B on the curated Aesthetic-4K dataset (12,015 GPT-4o-captioned 4K images) with a constant learning rate of 1e-6 and ZeRO Stage 2 with CPU offload.

## Results

On Aesthetic-Eval@2048 (2048×2048), WLF fine-tuning reduces FID from 43.82 to 40.18 for SD3-2B and from 50.57 to 39.49 for Flux-12B, while improving CLIPScore from 31.50→34.04 (SD3) and 30.41→34.41 (Flux). For fine-detail metrics, WLF raises GLCM Score from 0.75→0.79 (SD3) and 0.58→0.61 (Flux), and lowers Compression Ratio from 11.23→10.51 (SD3) and 14.80→13.60 (Flux), indicating richer textures and better detail preservation. At 4096×4096, the partitioned VAE (F=16) resolves OOM issues while maintaining reconstruction quality (PSNR 28.82 for SD3, 29.22 for Flux), and WLF further improves CLIPScore to 34.40 and GLCM Score to 0.77 over plain fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not include direct quantitative comparisons against concurrent 4K synthesis methods (PixArt-Σ, Sana) in its main tables; instead, comparisons are structured as (1) SD3-2B vs. Flux-12B as base architectures, and (2) with vs. without WLF fine-tuning. WLF consistently outperforms its no-WLF counterpart across all five metrics for both base models: FID drops by ~3.6 (SD3) and ~11.1 (Flux), CLIPScore rises by ~2.5–4.0 points, GLCM Score improves by 0.01–0.03, and Compression Ratio decreases by ~0.7–1.2. Between the two base models, Flux-12B achieves higher Aesthetics scores (6.36–6.37 vs. 5.91–5.96) but lower GLCM Scores (0.58–0.61 vs. 0.75–0.79) than SD3-2B, suggesting SD3 captures richer textures while Flux produces more aesthetically pleasing compositions. The ablation at 4096×4096 (Table 7–8) confirms WLF's advantage over plain fine-tuning: CLIPScore 34.40 vs. 34.14, Aesthetics 6.07 vs. 5.99, GLCM 0.77 vs. 0.74, and Compression Ratio 10.50 vs. 11.41.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on large-scale latent diffusion transformers (SD3-2B and Flux-12B) by fine-tuning them for direct 4K synthesis, departing from the efficiency-focused 4K approaches of PixArt-Σ and Sana (0.6B/1.6B models) that the authors argue neglect high-frequency detail. The wavelet-based fine-tuning (WLF) loss repurposes discrete Haar wavelet decomposition of the velocity prediction target, a technique borrowed from wavelet-based generative modeling [14, 33] but applied here as a drop-in modification to rectified-flow or noise-prediction objectives. The partitioned VAE (dilation-2 first encoder conv, partitioned decoder conv) is a practical engineering solution to OOM at 4096×4096 that preserves the pre-trained latent space without retraining. The Aesthetic-4K benchmark fills a gap left by prior 4K work, which lacked a public evaluation set and fine-detail-specific metrics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Aesthetic-4K benchmark (12K training images, 2.8K eval images, GPT-4o captions, GLCM Score + Compression Ratio metrics) provides the first public infrastructure for evaluating 4K synthesis quality, which should accelerate follow-up research. The partitioned VAE trick is a low-cost, model-agnostic solution to the OOM problem that practitioners will likely adopt. However, the evaluation is limited to the authors' own benchmark, there is no direct head-to-head comparison with PixArt-Σ or Sana in the main tables, and the wavelet approach uses only the simplest Haar basis—whether richer wavelets or multi-scale decompositions yield further gains remains open. Scaling beyond 4K and integrating WLF with progressive or cascaded architectures are natural next steps.
