## Problem

Existing multimodal Chain-of-Thought methods produce text-only rationales that rely on coarse positional descriptions (e.g., 'at the top') and cannot express fine-grained associations with specific regions of the input image. No current VLM can natively generate interleaved visual-textual reasoning steps, as Perceiver-based models lack multimodal output while unified-modeling VLMs produce fixed-resolution images rather than fine-grained crops.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252634/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text instruction as input, along with a CoT prompt (zero-shot 'Let's think step by step' or one-shot demonstrations containing paired visual patches and textual rationales). The output is a sequence of interleaved-modal rationales—each consisting of a selected patch group from the input image followed by a generated textual rationale—concluding with a final answer. The visual rationales are not newly generated images but subsets of the original image's visual tokens (16 patches for Qwen2-VL, 64-patch granularity), triggered by a predefined signal token (line break) during autoregressive decoding.

## Method

ICoT reformulates multimodal CoT so that each reasoning step is a pair of a fine-grained visual rationale and a textual rationale, rather than text alone. To realize this on existing VLMs without multimodal generation, the paper proposes Attention-driven Selection (ADS): when the VLM emits a signal token, ADS reads the attention map between that token and all cached visual tokens (averaged across layers), selects the top-n patches by attention score, restores their spatial positions in-place, and inserts them into the generation sequence before resuming autoregressive text decoding. This is entirely training-free and parameterization-free, making it a plug-and-play strategy applicable to both Perceiver-LLM architectures (Qwen2-VL) and unified-modeling VLMs (Chameleon). The paper also explores a KV-cache-copying variant that avoids re-forwarding visual tokens but degrades performance because position information is already fused into the cache, breaking the interleaved structure.

## Results

On Chameleon-7B (0-shot), ICoT achieves 29.8% accuracy on M³CoT, 51.0% on ScienceQA, and 25.2 ROUGE-L on LLaVA-W, yielding up to 14% relative improvement over the best baseline (CCoT) on LLaVA-W. In the 1-shot setting, ICoT reaches 32.3% (M³CoT), 53.4% (ScienceQA), and 27.6 ROUGE-L (LLaVA-W) on Chameleon-7B, with up to 11.7% relative gains. On Qwen2-VL-7B, ICoT achieves 44.1% (M³CoT), 56.8% (ScienceQA), and 34.2 ROUGE-L (LLaVA-W) in 0-shot, with up to 4.6% relative improvement. The largest absolute gains consistently appear on LLaVA-W, where fine-grained visual description is most critical.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICoT outperforms all four baselines (Multimodal CoT, CCoT, DDCoT, SCAFFOLD) across every benchmark and both backbones in both 0-shot and 1-shot settings. The most substantial gains appear on LLaVA-W: on Chameleon-7B 0-shot, ICoT scores 25.2 ROUGE-L versus CCoT's 22.1 (a 14% relative improvement), and in 1-shot it reaches 27.6 versus CCoT's 24.5 (11.7% relative). On M³CoT and ScienceQA, gains are smaller but consistent (0.6–4.0% relative), with CCoT being the strongest baseline on these benchmarks. On Qwen2-VL-7B, ICoT leads by 0.6–5.3% relative over the best baseline, with the largest margin again on LLaVA-W (4.6% in 0-shot, 5.3% in 1-shot). The pattern suggests that interleaved-modal rationales provide the greatest benefit when fine-grained visual detail is most relevant to the answer, as in open-ended visual description tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multimodal Chain-of-Thought prompting line (Multimodal CoT, CCoT, DDCoT, SCAFFOLD) by replacing text-only intermediate rationales with interleaved visual-textual ones, where actual image patches are inserted between reasoning steps. The key methodological departure is Attention-driven Selection (ADS), which repurposes the VLM's own attention maps to select relevant patches from the input image rather than requiring the model to generate new visual content—a simplification that makes ICoT compatible with both Perceiver-LLM (Qwen2-VL) and unified-modeling (Chameleon) architectures without retraining. Conceptually, ICoT aligns multimodal reasoning more closely with human visual attention processes, moving beyond the coarse positional descriptions (e.g., 'at the top') that prior text-only CoT methods rely on.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ICoT addresses a genuine expressiveness gap in multimodal CoT: text-only rationales cannot precisely anchor reasoning to specific image regions, leading to misunderstandings, overgeneralization, and hallucination as demonstrated in the case studies. The plug-and-play, training-free nature of ADS makes the approach immediately applicable to existing VLMs, which is practically valuable. However, open questions remain: the fixed patch count is sub-optimal, the signal-token trigger mechanism is fragile (high-frequency generation degrades quality), and the attention-map-based selection introduces memory overhead. The authors note that incorporating segmentation or grounding techniques could yield a more robust implementation, and broader evaluation across additional backbones and benchmarks is needed to confirm generalization.
