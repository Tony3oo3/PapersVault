## Problem

Current video diffusion models primarily generate short clips (3–8 seconds), and extending them to longer durations via noise scheduling or auto-regressive modules either limits content diversity or introduces error propagation. Generating 15-second videos that simultaneously maintain scenario diversity and long-range coherence remains unsolved, compounded by the lack of large-scale training data with coherent multi-caption annotations aligned to unfolding storylines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251163/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the user provides a single short text prompt, which GPT-4o expands into five progressive sub-captions describing the unfolding narrative (e.g., camera pulls back, scene transitions). The model generates an 88-frame video at 720×1280 resolution, which is then extended to approximately 15 seconds via EMA-VFI frame interpolation. During training, each video in LongTake-HD is paired with an overall caption plus five temporally aligned sub-captions, and the latent features are split into five corresponding segments.

## Method

Presto builds on the Allegro 2.8B-parameter DiT architecture and introduces Segmented Cross-Attention (SCA), which splits the temporal hidden states into N non-overlapping segments and restricts each segment to cross-attend only to its corresponding sub-caption embedding, requiring no additional parameters. The best variant, Overlap Segmented Cross-Attention (OSCA), introduces δ overlapping frames at segment boundaries that attend to multiple adjacent text conditions with averaged outputs, enabling smooth transitions while self-attention maintains global consistency across segments. Training proceeds in two stages: pre-training on 261k curated videos from LongTake-HD (1500 steps, 64 H100s, batch 256) followed by fine-tuning on the top 47k content-diverse clips (500 steps). The LongTake-HD dataset is curated from 8.9M raw videos through multi-stage filtering (duration, scene segmentation, aesthetic/motion metrics) and annotated with progressive narrative-style sub-captions generated by Aria and refined by GPT-4o to eliminate redundancy and emphasize scenario transitions.

## Results

On VBench, Presto achieves a 78.5% Semantic Score, surpassing Gen-3 (75.2%), Allegro (73.0%), and TALC (44.4%), and a perfect 100% Dynamic Degree, outperforming Gen-3 (60.1%), Allegro (55.0%), and TALC (98.6%). Presto also leads on Object Class (93.7%), Color (98.1%), and Overall Consistency (27.8%). However, Presto trails Gen-3 (84.1%) and Allegro (83.1%) on Quality Score (80.6%) and Overall Score (80.2% vs. 82.3% and 81.1%), which the authors attribute to the increased difficulty of maintaining visual consistency under complex, varied dynamics. In the user study, Presto achieves a 91.8% overall win rate, dominating all three subjective dimensions (Scenario Diversity 90.6%, Scenario Coherence 95.3%, Text-Video Adherence 89.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Presto clearly outperforms all three baselines on the semantic and dynamic dimensions that are central to its design goal. Against the commercial Gen-3, Presto gains +3.3% on Semantic Score (78.5 vs. 75.2) and a massive +39.9% on Dynamic Degree (100.0 vs. 60.1), but concedes Quality Score (80.6 vs. 84.1) and Overall Score (80.2 vs. 82.3). Against the open-source SOTA Allegro, Presto leads by +5.5% on Semantic Score and +45.0% on Dynamic Degree, again trailing slightly on Quality Score (80.6 vs. 83.1). Against the MT2V method TALC, Presto dominates across nearly every metric, with a +34.1% gap on Semantic Score (78.5 vs. 44.4) and +48.4% on Object Class (93.7 vs. 45.3), while TALC's only advantage is a marginal 1.4% edge on Dynamic Degree. In the user study, Presto wins overwhelmingly (91.8% overall win rate) against all baselines, with the largest margins over TALC and the 'Merge Videos' naive approach, confirming that the Segmented Cross-Attention and curated LongTake-HD data jointly drive the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

Presto builds directly on Allegro, a 2.8B-parameter DiT-based video diffusion model, and extends it with a parameter-free Segmented Cross-Attention (SCA) mechanism inspired by window attention in Swin Transformers. It departs from two prior paradigms for long video generation: (1) inference-time extensions via noise rescheduling or attention manipulation that are constrained by the original short clip, and (2) auto-regressive approaches that add new modules and suffer from error propagation. Compared to MT2V methods like TALC, Presto retains the standard single-prompt T2V interface while internally decomposing the prompt into progressive sub-captions, and its narrative-style captioning avoids the rigid multi-scene concatenation used by prior multi-text approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parameter-free SCA mechanism (particularly OSCA) offers a drop-in modification to any DiT-based video model, making it broadly adoptable without architectural retraining. The LongTake-HD dataset (261k pre-training / 47k fine-tuning clips with progressive sub-captions) addresses a data-quality gap that the authors argue prior long-video methods overlooked. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 15 seconds, whether the reliance on GPT-4o for inference-time caption decomposition is a practical bottleneck, and whether the observed quality-score degradation under complex dynamics can be mitigated. The authors explicitly leave auto-regressive generation and further attention-mechanism exploration as future directions.
