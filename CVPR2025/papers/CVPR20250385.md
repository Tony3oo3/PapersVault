## Problem

Prior reflection-removal systems train on heuristically mixed 8-bit tone-mapped images, which are photometrically inaccurate for simulating real reflections, and rely on small ground-truth datasets that limit scale and diversity. Additionally, most methods operate at ~256 pixels and cannot produce full-resolution outputs suitable for consumer editing. This paper shows that the dominant bottleneck is not model architecture but the fidelity of the training data: training on photometrically accurate RAW simulations improves performance more than any prior architectural variation.

## Input / Output

The system takes a full-resolution RAW (scene-referred, linear) photo and an optional contextual photo captured in the opposite direction (e.g., a selfie-camera shot), which need not be registered or simultaneous. The base model processes both at 256² pixels, and a Gaussian-pyramid upsampler produces transmission and reflection images at the original resolution (up to 2048² or beyond). Outputs are stored as new RAW files by inverting the ACR pipeline steps 3–6, and a 1K preview is rendered in 4.5–6.5 s on a MacBook or iPhone 14 Pro.

## Method

The core contribution is a photometrically and geometrically accurate reflection simulator that composites pairs of scene-referred images in XYZ color space (ACR Step 4), preserving illuminant color and linear luminance before white balancing, and applying spatially varying Fresnel attenuation, perspective, double reflection, and calibrated defocus. The base model uses an EfficientNet backbone fused with a BiFPN pyramid, where the optional context image modulates the mixture features via per-channel affine transforms (StyleGAN-style) to help identify the reflection layer; it is trained end-to-end from random weights with adversarial losses. The upsampler iteratively traverses a Gaussian pyramid, matching low-resolution features of t and r to the mixture via feature products to produce per-pixel masks, then resamples only the masks (not features) to preserve sharp high-resolution detail. The entire pipeline is trained on over 1 million synthetic examples drawn from 12,803 RAW photos and 2,233 IBL panoramas, requiring no fine-tuning on real images.

## Results

On the RAW simulation test set (256² pixels), the proposed method with context (Ours+ctx) achieves SSIM_t 95.7 and SSIM_r 88.2, substantially outperforming all retrained baselines: Zhu (SSIM_t 92.8), DSRNet (SSIM_t 92.6, SSIM_r 75.5), Zhang (SSIM_t 89.9, SSIM_r 61.5), and CoRRN (SSIM_t 86.7, SSIM_r 60.4). The paper's central empirical finding is that retraining prior architectures on RAW simulation data improves SSIM_t by ~40 percentage points over their published 8-bit results, whereas the performance gap among the prior architectures themselves is only ~20 pp. For upsampling (256p→2048p), the proposed method achieves PSNR_t 47.77 / SSIM_t 98.8 on ground-truth inputs, versus 42.24 / 97.9 for V-DESIRR+C, and 30.62 / 95.2 end-to-end.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method (Ours+ctx) wins on every metric against all four retrained baselines in the RAW Train setting: it leads Zhu [60] by +2.9 SSIM_t, DSRNet [20] by +3.1 SSIM_t and +12.7 SSIM_r, Zhang [59] by +5.8 SSIM_t and +26.7 SSIM_r, and CoRRN [47] by +9.0 SSIM_t and +27.8 SSIM_r. The gap is most pronounced on reflection recovery (SSIM_r), where the context photo and RAW pipeline give the largest advantage over prior architectures. On the 8-bit published-model comparison, the advantage of RAW is even starker: retraining DSRNet, Zhu, and Zhang on RAW improves their SSIM_t by ~40 pp, while the spread among those three retrained models is only ~20 pp, demonstrating that the data representation (RAW vs. 8-bit) matters more than the network architecture. For upsampling, the proposed method outperforms V-DESIRR [35] by +5.5 PSNR_t on ground-truth inputs (47.77 vs. 42.24) and by +0.35 PSNR_t end-to-end (30.62 vs. 30.27), with V-DESIRR producing visible artifacts that the proposed feature-masking upsampler avoids. SUPIR [58], a general-purpose restoration model, performs far worse (SSIM_t 64.6 on GT), confirming that task-specific upsampling is necessary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the perceptual-loss training framework and network head of Zhang et al. [59], but fundamentally reframes the reflection removal task to operate on scene-referred RAW images in linear XYZ color rather than the 8-bit tone-mapped display-referred images used by virtually all prior work. It departs from the dominant paradigm of mixing tone-mapped images for training data by simulating reflections photometrically accurately (preserving illuminant color, exposure, and Fresnel attenuation before white balancing), and introduces an optional contextual photo (e.g., a selfie-camera shot) as a new input modality to disambiguate the reflection. The upsampler also departs from V-DESIRR [35]'s iterative upscaling by instead masking high-resolution mixture features guided by low-resolution component predictions, avoiding error propagation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that RAW simulation data improves performance by ~40 SSIM points—exceeding the ~20-point spread among different architectures—challenges the field's implicit assumption that model design is the primary lever for progress, and suggests that data representation fidelity is underappreciated. The system's practical deployment (5-second processing, full-resolution linear output for editing) makes it directly relevant to consumer photography products. Open questions remain around saturated regions where one component is pushed to the noise floor (reducing the problem to hole filling), textured overlaps requiring hallucination, and privacy implications of recovering hidden reflections. The photometric simulation pipeline could generalize to other layered-image problems (e.g., lens flare, screen glare) where linear light mixing is the physical model.
