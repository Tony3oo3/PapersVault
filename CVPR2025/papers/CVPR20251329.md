## Problem

Personalizing handwritten text recognition (HTR) to individual writing styles at test time remains challenging: existing meta-learning approaches like MetaHTR require labeled examples for adaptation (impractical in real-world deployment) and update all model parameters (computationally prohibitive on resource-constrained devices). The paper addresses how to achieve writer-specific personalization using only unlabeled test-time images while updating less than 1% of model parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251329/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At test time, the method takes k unlabeled handwritten line images from an unseen writer (typically 5-shot) and produces writer-specific prompt vectors P′ ∈ R^{l×3} that are padded around the first 18 convolutional layers of a frozen encoder. The prompts are then applied to new handwritten line images to generate autoregressive text token sequences via a transformer decoder. The model operates at the line level rather than page level, and the MAE auxiliary task consumes 75%-masked versions of the support images for reconstruction.

## Method

MetaWriter formulates writer personalization as visual prompt tuning where learnable padding values replace fixed padding in the first 18 convolutional layers of a frozen FCN encoder (18 conv + 12 depthwise separable conv layers) paired with an 8-layer transformer decoder. To eliminate the need for labeled test-time data, a Masked Autoencoder decoder reconstructs 75%-masked support images, and the SSIM-based reconstruction loss guides prompt updates via a few gradient steps. A MAML-style bi-level meta-learning procedure (Algorithm 1) learns optimal meta-prompt initialization across training writers: the inner loop adapts prompts with the self-supervised MAE loss, while the outer loop updates meta-prompts using cross-entropy text prediction loss on disjoint labeled query sets, ensuring the self-supervised gradient aligns with recognition accuracy. Only 0.08M parameters (the prompts) are optimized during personalization versus 7.6M in the full model, yielding a 20× parameter reduction compared to prior writer-adaptive methods.

## Results

On the IAM line-level test set (5-shot, unlabeled adaptation), MetaWriter achieves a CER of 3.36% and WER of 10.32%, outperforming all instance-specific and writer-specific baselines. On the RIMES line-level test set, it reaches a CER of 2.19% and WER of 6.63%, surpassing the strongest baseline DAN (2.63% CER, 6.78% WER). Critically, MetaWriter requires only 0.08M trainable parameters during personalization—less than 1% of the total model—compared to 7.6M for DAN, 1.7M for MetaHTR, and 334M for TrOCR, representing a 20× reduction in tunable parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On IAM, MetaWriter (3.36% CER, 10.32% WER) outperforms the strongest instance-specific method TrOCR (4.22% CER) by 0.86% CER and the best writer-specific baseline Wang et al. (5.3% CER, 18.5% WER) by 1.94% CER and 8.18% WER. On RIMES, it beats the closest competitor DAN (2.63% CER, 6.78% WER) by 0.44% CER and 0.15% WER, and surpasses VAN (3.04% CER, 8.32% WER) by 0.85% CER and 1.69% WER. In terms of parameter efficiency, MetaWriter's 0.08M tunable parameters are 95× fewer than DAN's 7.6M, 21× fewer than MetaHTR's 1.7M, and 4,175× fewer than TrOCR's 334M. The paper notes that MetaHTR uses 16 labeled samples at the word level while MetaWriter uses 5 unlabeled samples at the line level, making direct WRA comparisons (89.7% vs. 89.2% on IAM) approximate. Overall, MetaWriter wins on every reported metric across both datasets while using an order-of-magnitude fewer parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends MetaHTR [6], which introduced writer-adaptive HTR via MAML-style meta-learning with labeled test-time examples, by replacing supervised adaptation with an unlabeled, self-supervised prompt-tuning paradigm. The prompt mechanism (learnable padding vectors in convolutional layers) is borrowed from Liu et al. [42] for gaze estimation, and the MAE-based reconstruction loss provides the self-supervised signal that eliminates the need for test-time annotations. The bi-level optimization in Algorithm 1 follows the MAML [20] template, but the inner loop optimizes only the prompt vectors under a self-supervised objective while the outer loop aligns them with the supervised text-prediction loss—departing from MetaHTR's full-parameter inner-loop update.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20× reduction in tunable parameters (0.08M vs. 1.7M in MetaHTR) makes writer-specific personalization feasible on resource-constrained edge devices, and the elimination of test-time labeling removes a significant user-friction barrier in real-world deployment (e.g., mobile apps). However, the method is limited to line-level recognition and relies on a shared MAE decoder, raising open questions about scalability to page-level layouts with reading-order constraints and to scripts with very different structural properties. The modest performance margin over MetaHTR (89.7% vs. 89.2% WRA on IAM) suggests the efficiency gains come at a small accuracy cost, and whether the prompt-padding mechanism generalizes beyond the specific FCN encoder used here remains untested.
