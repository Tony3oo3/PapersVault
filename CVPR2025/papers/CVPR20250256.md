## Problem

Existing few-shot anomaly generation methods suffer from three coupled limitations: limited diversity in generated anomalies, unrealistic blending of the anomaly with the surrounding image, and misalignment between the generated mask and the generated anomaly region. Prior approaches either generate only the anomaly patch (AnomalyDiffusion) or rely on GANs that cannot produce masks, making it difficult to obtain high-quality image-mask pairs for downstream supervised anomaly inspection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250256/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of anomaly image-mask pairs (as few as 8 per category on MVTec AD), a nested text prompt pair ("a vfx with [object]" for the global branch and "[anomaly]" for the anomaly branch, using DreamBooth-style tokens to avoid strong language priors), and a background image extracted via U²-Net segmentation. It produces a full anomaly image, a cropped anomaly-part image containing only the defect region, and a pixel-accurate anomaly mask obtained by post-hoc segmentation (SAM or U²-Net) of the anomaly-part output.

## Method

DualAnoDiff expands a single frozen Stable Diffusion into two LoRA-adapted branches (SD for the global image, SD* for the anomaly part) that denoise in parallel at the same timestep. A Self-Attention Interaction Module (SAIM) is inserted after every attention block in both U-Nets: it concatenates the spatial features of both branches, performs a shared self-attention computation, and splits the result back with a residual connection, enforcing spatial and semantic consistency between the two outputs. A Background Compensation Module (BCM) further decouples object from background by processing the U²-Net-extracted background image through SD and injecting its self-attention K/V into the global branch via an adaptive fusion MLP with a learnable scale factor γ initialized to 0.1. Training minimizes a joint denoising loss over both branches with the trainable CLIP text encoder, and the final mask is derived by segmenting the generated anomaly-part image rather than being predicted directly.

## Results

On MVTec AD, DualAnoDiff achieves a pixel-level anomaly localization AUROC of 99.1% and AP of 84.5% using a simple U-Net trained on generated data, surpassing the best prior generation-based method (AnomalyDiffusion, 99.1%/81.4%) and all unsupervised baselines. For generation quality, it attains the highest Inception Score (1.93) and IC-LPIPS (0.38) across 15 categories. On anomaly classification with ResNet-18, it reaches 79.67% average accuracy, a 13.58% improvement over AnomalyDiffusion (66.09%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DualAnoDiff outperforms all 15 baselines on average across the three downstream tasks. On pixel-level localization (Table 4), it achieves 99.1% AUROC / 84.5% AP, beating the closest competitor PRN (99.0/78.6) by 5.9 AP points and all unsupervised methods (DRAEM 97.7/69.0, PatchCore 97.1/56.6, CFA 98.3/66.3) by wide margins; it loses on individual categories (cable AUROC, toothbrush, transistor AUROC) but dominates on 11/15 categories. On generation quality (Table 1), it achieves the best IS (1.93) and IC-LPIPS (0.38), surpassing AnomalyDiffusion (1.80/0.32) and DFMGAN (1.72/0.20). On anomaly classification (Table 3), it wins 12/14 categories over AnomalyDiffusion (e.g., leather 90.48% vs 61.90%, grid 80.0% vs 42.50%) but loses on bottle (79.07% vs 90.70%) and pill (56.25% vs 59.38%). Among GAN-based baselines (SDGAN, DefectGAN, DFMGAN), DualAnoDiff consistently leads on both generation fidelity and downstream detection, with DFMGAN being the strongest GAN competitor but still trailing by 3.1 AP on localization. Model-free methods (CDC, Crop-Paste, DRAEM) are substantially outperformed on all metrics, confirming the advantage of learned generation over paste-based augmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based anomaly generation line initiated by AnomalyDiffusion, which used Textual Inversion to generate anomaly parts on masked normal images. The key architectural departure is the dual-branch design: rather than generating only the anomaly region, DualAnoDiff simultaneously generates the full anomaly image and the isolated anomaly part using two LoRA-adapted Stable Diffusion models coupled via a Self-Attention Interaction Module, inspired by the decomposition idea in LayerDiffusion. The Background Compensation Module further differentiates the work by injecting background features to prevent object-background coupling in few-shot settings, addressing a failure mode not explicitly handled by prior anomaly generation methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-branch formulation elegantly resolves the mask-alignment problem that plagued single-branch anomaly generators, and the resulting image-mask pairs directly improve downstream detection, localization, and classification without requiring specialized anomaly detection architectures. However, evaluation is limited to MVTec AD with a fixed 1/3 training split, leaving open questions about generalization to more diverse industrial domains, extremely low-shot regimes (1-2 samples), and whether the dual-branch overhead is justified when a single well-tuned branch with strong conditioning might suffice. The approach also inherits the dependency on a large pre-trained diffusion model, which may limit deployment in resource-constrained industrial settings.
