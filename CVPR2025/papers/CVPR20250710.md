## Problem

State-of-the-art video segmentation models like SAM 2 require explicit user prompts (masks, boxes, clicks) for each target entity, making them unable to automatically discover and track all entities in a video, including those appearing in later frames. Additionally, SAM 2 tracks each entity independently, which is inefficient and can produce overlapping masks. The paper introduces Video Entity Segmentation as a new class-agnostic task that requires segmenting and tracking every semantically coherent entity without any predefined categories or user-specified prompts.

## Input / Output

EntitySAM takes a raw video clip as input with no user prompts, processing frames at 1024×1024 (SAM 2 ViT/16) and 896×896 (DINOv2 ViT/14). It outputs N non-overlapping tube masks (binary masklets spanning all T frames) each paired with a confidence score in [0,1]; under the non-overlapping constraint these merge into a single pixel-ID map. For class-specific evaluation, the highest-confidence frame per masklet is cropped and sent to GPT-4o with candidate category names for final labeling.

## Method

EntitySAM freezes SAM 2's image encoder and all memory-related modules, adding three learnable components: (1) a Dual Visual Encoder that concatenates frozen SAM 2 features with DINOv2 features (projected to matching dimension) to compensate for SAM 2's weak semantic awareness; (2) an Automatic Prompt Generator that uses learnable queries performing cross-attention over enhanced features to produce SAM 2-compatible prompt embeddings and binary entity confidence scores; and (3) an Entity Mask Decoder that replaces SAM 2's per-object mask decoder, employing separate mask and IoU query groups with self-attention for inter-entity communication and dynamic convolution for mask generation. Training uses Hungarian matching with a combined loss (mask + entity classification + IoU) on COCO image-level annotations without category labels, in two stages: 40K iterations on static images followed by 10K on pseudo-video clips of length 8. This design scales to 50+ entities per 8-frame clip versus SAM 2's original capacity of 3, while preserving SAM 2's frozen memory bank for long-term temporal tracking.

## Results

On the proposed VIPEntitySeg benchmark (zero-shot, class-agnostic), EntitySAM with ViT-Small achieves VEQ 51.8, VEQ^RQ 61.1, and STQ^EN 41.0, outperforming all baselines including DEVA* (VEQ 49.0) and OV-DVIS++ Offline (VEQ 46.7). With ViT-Large, EntitySAM reaches VEQ 54.6 and VEQ^RQ 64.5, surpassing the strongest baseline DEVA* (VEQ 50.8) by 3.8 points. On VIPSeg panoptic segmentation, EntitySAM (ViT-L) achieves VPQ 31.4 and STQ 33.5, exceeding OV-DVIS++ Offline (VPQ 30.4, STQ 32.2). On video instance segmentation (YouTube-VIS 2019), EntitySAM (ViT-S) achieves AP 44.9, surpassing OV-DVIS++ Online (AP 34.5) by 10.4 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EntitySAM outperforms all baselines on the primary VEQ metric: it beats SAM 2.1 with Mask2Former initialization by 5.6 points (ViT-S) and 4.0 points (ViT-L), DEVA* by 2.8 and 3.8 points, and OV-DVIS++ Offline by 5.1 and 5.6 points. The largest gains appear in VEQ^RQ (recovery quality), where EntitySAM (ViT-L) scores 64.5 vs. 60.6 for DEVA*, indicating substantially fewer false positives and better cross-frame entity identification. The one metric where EntitySAM trails is STQ^EN (tracking quality): DEVA* achieves 42.4/45.3 vs. EntitySAM's 41.0/43.3, but DEVA relies on a two-stage detection-then-association pipeline trained on additional external VOS datasets, whereas EntitySAM is end-to-end and trained solely on COCO. On VIPSeg panoptic segmentation, EntitySAM (ViT-L) surpasses OV-DVIS++ Offline by 1.0 VPQ and 1.3 STQ, and on YouTube-VIS 2019 instance segmentation it leads OV-DVIS++ Online by 10.4 AP, demonstrating consistent advantages across task formulations.

## Relation to Prior Work

EntitySAM builds directly on SAM 2 by freezing its image encoder and memory modules while replacing the prompt-dependent mask decoder with a query-based EntityDecoder inspired by DETR-style object detectors. It bridges two previously separate lines of video segmentation—memory-based tracking (SAM 2, XMem) and query-based association (Mask2Former for video, MinVIS)—into a unified architecture that enables automatic multi-entity discovery without user prompts. The addition of DINOv2 as a complementary semantic encoder departs from prior work that relied solely on SAM 2's backbone, motivated by the finding that SAM 2's features lack consistent entity-level semantic representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that class-agnostic training (no category labels) on COCO image masks alone can match or exceed methods trained with full category annotations across four zero-shot video segmentation tasks, suggesting category supervision may be dispensable for open-vocabulary video segmentation. The introduction of Video Entity Segmentation as a unified, class-agnostic task with the VEQ metric provides a cleaner evaluation framework that reduces annotation burden. Open questions include scalability to very long videos beyond 8-frame training clips, whether the GPT-4o post-classification pipeline generalizes to truly novel categories, and whether the approach extends to 3D or multi-view settings.
