## Problem

RGB-based UAV trackers struggle with small targets, occlusions, and background clutter because spatial features (color, texture) become indistinguishable from the background. While multispectral imaging provides stable spectral signatures that resolve these ambiguities, no large-scale multispectral UAV tracking dataset existed, and prior multispectral trackers failed to jointly exploit spectral, spatial, and temporal information in a unified manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250136/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

UNTrack consumes 8-band multispectral video frames (1200×900 native, resized to 384×384 for search and 192×192 for template) along with a single-token spectrum prompt (L_P=1) that is updated frame-to-frame. It processes N=2 sequential search frames jointly with the initial template through a unified token sequence, and outputs a per-pixel confidence map and a 4-channel bounding box regression map over the search region. The encoded spectrum prompt from each frame feeds back as input to the next, carrying the target's material reflectance properties forward.

## Method

UNTrack concatenates prompt, template, and search tokens into a single sequence processed by an asymmetric transformer where only the search tokens attend to all tokens (prompt, template, and search), while prompt and template attend only to themselves—pruning irrelevant cross-attention blocks and reducing FLOPs by 19%. A spectral background elimination mechanism computes per-region confidence via the Euclidean norm of average-pooled attention scores and progressively discards low-confidence background regions using a cosine-annealed keeping ratio during training. A spectrum prompt encoder applies a compression-excitation operation (two FC layers with ratio r) over the spectral dimension of the concatenated prompt and globally-pooled template tokens, then passes the result through an MLP to produce an updated prompt encoding the target's intrinsic spectral signature. The dual-branch prediction head (classification + regression) is trained with focal loss, L1 loss, and GIoU loss (weighted 1:5:2), and the entire model is initialized via a parameter reconstruction strategy that interpolates ImageNet-pretrained RGB weights to 8 spectral channels.

## Results

On the proposed MUST dataset, UNTrack with reconstructed parameters achieves state-of-the-art performance with AUC 59.7, SR0.5 75.8, SR0.75 46.2, Precision 79.2, and Normalized Precision 74.8, surpassing the best competing tracker OSTrack384* by 4.6% in AUC and 5.9% in Precision. On the HOT hyperspectral tracking benchmark, UNTrack achieves the best AUC of 70.4 while also delivering the fastest inference speed at 37 FPS (vs. 27.7 FPS for SiamHYPER). On the RGB-based GOT10K dataset, UNTrack achieves AO 77.3, SR0.5 88.4, and SR0.75 74.7, performing on par with the strongest baselines (HIPTrack, ODTrack) despite limited spectral information in RGB inputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the MUST dataset, UNTrack* decisively outperforms all 25 baselines across every metric, with the largest margins over Siamese trackers (SiamRPN, SiamMask, DiMP) and a clear 4.6 AUC-point lead over the strongest one-stream competitor OSTrack384*. The parameter reconstruction strategy itself contributes over 10% average improvement across all trackers, confirming that direct RGB pre-training transfer is suboptimal for multispectral inputs. On the HOT hyperspectral benchmark, UNTrack achieves the highest AUC (70.4 vs. 69.9 for TMTNet) and the fastest speed (37 FPS vs. 27.7 for SiamHYPER), demonstrating that the asymmetric attention and spectral background elimination yield both accuracy and efficiency gains. On GOT10K (RGB-only), UNTrack is essentially tied with the top methods: it trails HIPTrack by 0.1 on AO (77.3 vs. 77.4) but leads on SR0.5 (88.4 vs. 88.0) and SR0.75 (74.7 vs. 74.5), indicating the framework generalizes well even without rich spectral input. Overall, UNTrack wins or ties on all three datasets, with its advantage being most pronounced in the multispectral UAV setting where spectral features are most informative.

## Relation to Prior Work

UNTrack builds on the one-stream tracking paradigm established by OSTrack [36], where template and search tokens are jointly processed through a shared transformer, but extends it to a unified spectral-spatial-temporal setting by incorporating sequential search frames and a historical spectrum prompt as additional token streams. Prior multispectral tracking work (e.g., HOT [34], SiamHyper [22]) addressed general-object tracking with hyperspectral data, while UAV tracking benchmarks (UAV123, DTB70, VisDrone) were exclusively RGB-based; this paper is the first to bridge both by introducing a dedicated multispectral UAV tracking dataset and a tracker that explicitly exploits spectral band information through a prompt encoder and background elimination mechanism. The asymmetric attention design departs from standard full-attention transformers by pruning cross-attention blocks deemed irrelevant to tracking, a structural choice not explored in prior one-stream or Siamese trackers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MUST dataset (250 sequences, 43k frames, 8 spectral bands, 12 challenge attributes) fills a critical gap that has hindered multispectral UAV tracking research, providing the first standardized benchmark for this modality-platform combination. The asymmetric attention and spectral background elimination mechanisms demonstrate that targeted pruning of irrelevant token interactions can simultaneously improve accuracy and reduce FLOPs, offering a design principle applicable beyond multispectral tracking. Key limitations include the modest 8-band spectral resolution and the frame-rate constraints inherent to multispectral imaging, which the authors acknowledge as barriers to capturing more challenging scenarios; future work will need higher spectral resolution and frame rates to fully exploit the spectral dimension.
