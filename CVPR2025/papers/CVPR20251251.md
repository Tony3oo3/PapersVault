## Problem

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) perform poorly on geologic map understanding due to the challenges of cartographic generalization: extremely high resolutions (up to 10,000² pixels), numerous interrelated components, and the need for specialized geological, geographical, and seismological knowledge. No prior benchmark or dedicated agent existed to systematically evaluate or address these challenges, leaving the potential of MLLMs in geology largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251251/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a high-resolution geologic map image (averaging 6,146² pixels, sourced from USGS in English and CGS in Chinese) paired with a natural-language question as input. It produces a text answer in one of three formats (multiple-choice, fill-in-the-blank, or essay) along with structured map metadata including component bounding boxes, legend unit details, lithology, and stratigraphic age. The benchmark comprises 124 maps and 3,864 questions spanning 25 tasks across five ability dimensions: extracting, grounding, referring, reasoning, and analyzing.

## Method

GeoMap-Agent is a zero-shot, three-module agent framework built on GPT-4o that addresses the cartographic generalization challenges through a divide-and-conquer pipeline. The Hierarchical Information Extraction (HIE) module uses YOLOv10-based vision detectors to segment the map into semantically independent components, then applies GPT-4o to each sub-image for local information extraction, aggregating results into global structured metadata. The Domain Knowledge Injection (DKI) module consults a scalable AI expert group (geologist, geographer, seismologist) that leverages a tool pool including Google Earth Engine APIs for population/land-cover data, USGS earthquake records, and GEM active fault databases to supply question-specific external knowledge. The Prompt-enhanced Question Answering (PEQA) module combines the extracted metadata and injected knowledge into the prompt with chain-of-thought reasoning, few-shot examples, JSON-format constraints, and cropped relevant map regions to guide the final answer. The entire framework is modular and scalable—both the expert group and tool pool can be extended, and the base model can be swapped for more capable MLLMs without architectural changes.

## Results

On GeoMap-Bench (124 maps, 3,864 questions), GeoMap-Agent achieves an overall score of 0.811, substantially outperforming GPT-4o (0.369) by more than 2×. The largest gains are in Extracting (0.832 vs. 0.219) and Grounding (0.920 vs. 0.128), where public MLLMs struggle with high-resolution map components. GeoMap-Agent also leads in Referring (0.886 vs. 0.378) and Analyzing (0.831 vs. 0.612), while Reasoning shows a smaller margin (0.588 vs. 0.507), indicating that cross-component logical inference remains the hardest sub-task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoMap-Agent outperforms all nine baselines on every ability metric and overall score. The gap is most dramatic in Grounding, where all MLLM baselines score below 0.16 (GPT-4o: 0.128; Qwen-Max: 0.002; Claude-3.5-Sonnet: 0.000) while GeoMap-Agent reaches 0.920, reflecting the benefit of the HIE module's component detectors. Among the strongest MLLM baselines, GPT-4o (0.369 overall) and Qwen-Max (0.342) are the closest competitors, but both trail GeoMap-Agent by over 0.45 in overall score. Open-source 9B models (QWen-chat, GLM-4v-9b, Idefics-9b, CogVLM2, Monkey-chat) cluster between 0.15–0.26 overall, with near-zero grounding scores, confirming that current open MLLMs cannot handle high-resolution cartographic layouts. Notably, even in Reasoning—the sub-task where GeoMap-Agent's advantage is smallest (0.588 vs. GPT-4o's 0.507)—the agent still leads, suggesting that domain knowledge injection (DKI) provides a consistent but bounded benefit for cross-component logical inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of science benchmarks, scientific agents, and multimodal LLMs, but departs from all three lines. Prior science benchmarks (GeoBench, Charting New Territories, OceanBench, LHRS-Bench) evaluate general geospatial or scientific reasoning but none target geologic maps specifically; prior science agents (GeoGPT, ChemCrow, DS-Agent) use tool-augmented LLMs for domain tasks but none address cartographic understanding; and prior scientific LLMs (K2, GeoGalactica, GeoLLM) are text-only. The GeoMap-Agent's three-module design (hierarchical extraction, domain knowledge injection, prompt-enhanced QA) repurposes the divide-and-conquer strategy from high-resolution image processing and the multi-expert consultation pattern from interdisciplinary human collaboration into a unified MLLM agent framework for a previously unaddressed visual domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Geologic maps underpin disaster assessment, resource exploration, and civil engineering, yet even GPT-4o scores below 0.4 on the new benchmark, revealing a substantial capability gap in current MLLMs for structured scientific diagrams. The 2× overall improvement from GeoMap-Agent (0.811 vs. 0.369) demonstrates that combining hierarchical visual decomposition, external domain knowledge retrieval, and structured prompting can close much of this gap, and the HIE+DKI+PEQA paradigm is explicitly framed as transferable to other high-resolution, multi-component scientific diagrams. Key open questions include whether reasoning performance (still ~0.59) can be improved without further tool expansion, how the framework scales to maps with more complex lithologic patterns, and whether supervised fine-tuning of MLLMs on geologic map data could reduce reliance on the tool pool.
