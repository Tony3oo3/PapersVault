## Problem

VLM-generated image captions suffer from information entanglement and excessive length, making them difficult for downstream models lacking strong LLM-based text encoders (e.g., GroundingDINO, SDXL) to parse and exploit. These captions also frequently omit essential cues such as image style and inter-object relationships, limiting their utility for grounding, generation, and scene understanding tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250709/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a structured BACON-style caption organized as a bag-of-concept graph with three components: an overall description (style, theme, global background/foreground), a per-object list (each with category, detailed description, and color), and a relationship list. For grounding applications, bounding boxes are additionally produced via GroundingDINO + CLIP. For image generation, a natural-language text prompt is converted into BACON format with a spatial layout plan. The structured output is serialized as a JSON dictionary so that models without linguistic processing can directly index individual elements.

## Method

BACON decomposes image content into disentangled, element-wise units organized in a graph structure, then uses In-Context Learning with a few-shot prompt to guide GPT-4V into emitting captions in this fixed, symbol-delimited format. The authors annotate 103k image-caption pairs (the ECO dataset) using GPT-4V with the BACON prompt followed by human verification, and fine-tune LLaVA-13B on this data to obtain LLAVA(BACON)-CAPTIONER, which matches GPT-4V+BACON at ~91% precision and ~90% recall while being far cheaper to deploy. For grounding, a three-stage pipeline combines GroundingDINO (candidate regions from object names), LLaVA (filtering clearly incorrect boxes), and CLIP (selecting the region best matching the object's textual description). The key insight is that element-wise, structured captions are simultaneously more complete (capturing style, color, and relationships) and more accessible to downstream models that cannot perform complex syntax parsing, enabling zero-shot improvements in open-vocabulary detection, region-based QA, video tracking, and text-to-image generation without any additional training.

## Results

On the proposed CQA benchmark, LLAVA(BACON)-CAPTIONER achieves 59.1 on NLVR2, 56.8 on OK-CQA, 52.6 on CQAv1, and 66.4 on CQAv2, outperforming ShareGPT-4V-13B, Qwen-VL-max, and LLaVA-13B across all four splits. In manual precision/recall evaluation on MSCOCO, BACON+GPT-4V reaches 61.9% precision and 92.4% recall, while LLAVA(BACON)-CAPTIONER achieves 56.2% precision and 82.8% recall—substantially exceeding GPT-4V alone (21.5% precision, 70.6% recall). For open-vocabulary object detection on the ECO test set, BACON-style captions boost Grounding DINO to 37.7 AP50 and 35.9 Recall (1.51× higher recall than Grounding DINO alone at 20.2), surpassing all OVD and grounding-caption baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VLM-based captioners (Table 1), LLAVA(BACON)-CAPTIONER wins on all four CQA splits: +1.6 over ShareGPT-4V-13B on NLVR2, +4.7 over Qwen-VL-max on OK-CQA, and +2.6 over LLaVA-13B on CQAv1. In the graph-based captioning comparison (Table 2), BACON-trained captioners outperform DOCCI, IIW, DenseFusion, Recap, and DCI on NLVR2, OK-CQA, and MMVP, with the largest margin being +3.55 over DCI on OK-CQA (48.49 vs 44.94). For open-vocabulary object detection (Table 4), BACON+Grounding DINO achieves 35.9 Recall, beating Grounding DINO alone (20.2), GLaMM (19.8), Kosmos-2 (13.3), Next-Chat (7.7), DE-VIT (23.8), and OV-DQUO (10.7) by wide margins. In image generation (Table 5), BACON-enhanced SDXL surpasses DALL-E 3 on both object recall (95.2% vs 90.1%) and relationship recall (76.7% vs 71.6%), while dramatically improving over vanilla SDXL (59.2% object recall). On open-vocabulary scene graph generation over Visual Genome (Fig. 6), LLAVA(BACON)-CAPTIONER significantly outperforms all specialized SGG methods including Motifs, GPS-Net, VCTree, PSGFormer, and IMP.

## Relation to Prior Work

BACON builds on LLaVA as its base captioner architecture but departs fundamentally from free-form captioning by imposing a structured, element-wise graph representation (objects, relationships, styles, themes) inspired by scene graph literature. It positions itself against prior graph-based captioning methods (DOCCI, IIW, DenseFusion, ReCap, DCI) by using VLMs to extract structured elements directly from full images via in-context learning, rather than relying on manual annotations or small masked crops. The grounding pipeline composes GroundingDINO and CLIP as complementary modules, reflecting a broader trend of combining specialized perception models with VLMs rather than expecting a single model to handle all modalities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core practical contribution is bridging the gap between powerful VLMs and downstream models (SDXL, GroundingDINO, SAM-2) that lack LLM-based text encoders, enabling them to perform tasks previously beyond their capabilities without retraining. The ECO dataset (103k pairs) and CQA benchmark provide reusable resources for the community. Key limitations include heavy reliance on manual annotation for data quality and the continued need for GroundingDINO for accurate bounding boxes, suggesting that fully integrated grounding within the VLM remains an open challenge. Whether this structured-captioning paradigm generalizes to other modalities (audio, 3D) or scales to more complex scenes is an open question.
