## Problem

Structured artifacts such as moiré patterns, rain streaks, and banding are quasi-periodic, repetitive patterns that intertwine with genuine image content and exhibit significant variations in scale, orientation, and shape distortion. Existing CNN-based methods lack the receptive field to capture long-range periodicity, while standard transformer approaches either incur prohibitive quadratic costs or rely on restricted spatial windows that weaken long-range dependency modeling. The paper addresses the gap of designing a transformer that can generalize to unseen artifact variations without requiring exhaustive training data coverage.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB image (e.g., 512×512 patches for demoiréing, 256×256 for debanding, full-resolution for deraining) and outputs a restored RGB image via residual addition. It employs a multi-input multi-output (MIMO) strategy where downsampled versions of the degraded image are fused into the encoder path at multiple scales, and the decoder produces outputs at three scales that jointly contribute to a multi-scale training loss. Task-specific auxiliary losses are applied: perceptual loss for demoiréing and frequency-domain loss for debanding/deraining.

## Method

SADT is a multi-scale hierarchical encoder-decoder transformer built around two novel modules. The Scale-Enhanced Deformable Convolution (SEDC) combines cascaded DCNv2 layers (for modeling warped and orientation-varying patterns) with parallel Spatial-Channel Mixing Convolution (SCMC) layers that partition feature maps into K×K patches, apply a shared learnable linear transform within each patch, and gate the result with the original input to capture large-scale patterns at minimal cost. The Scale-Adaptive Deformable Attention (SADA) extends local self-attention by assigning different dilation factors (M_s = {1,3,5,7}) to each attention head to form a broad sparse receptive field, and predicts per-position deformable sampling offsets from the full query to handle non-uniform spatial distribution of repetitive patterns. The encoder uses 4 blocks with progressively reduced spatial resolution and expanded channels, while 3 decoder blocks reconstruct the image from coarse to fine via skip connections, with each block containing cascaded SADA layers followed by a pair of SEDC layers.

## Results

On image deraining (SPAD), SADT achieves 49.13 dB PSNR and 0.9939 SSIM, surpassing the previous best NeRD-Rain-S by 0.23 dB with 42% fewer parameters (6.13M vs. 10.53M). On image demoiréing, SADT attains the highest PSNR across all three benchmarks: 30.77 dB (TIP2018), 24.96 dB (FHDMi), and 46.43 dB (LCDMoiré), while also achieving the best SSIM on TIP2018 (0.926) and FHDMi (0.8463). On image debanding (DID), SADT leads all metrics with 39.78 dB PSNR, 0.9729 SSIM, and 0.0453 LPIPS, outperforming Restormer by 0.28 dB in PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SADT outperforms all 24 compared baselines across the three artifact-removal tasks. In deraining, it beats the strongest competitor NeRD-Rain-S [6] by 0.23 dB PSNR and 0.0003 SSIM while using 42% fewer parameters (6.13M vs. 10.53M); DRSformer [5] and Restormer [51] trail by 0.60 dB and 1.15 dB respectively. In demoiréing, SADT surpasses ESDNet [48] (the prior SOTA) by 0.96 dB on TIP2018 and 0.46 dB on FHDMi, and exceeds MBCNN [53] by 2.39 dB on LCDMoiré; however, ESDNet retains a slight SSIM edge on LCDMoiré (0.9963 vs. 0.9923). In debanding, SADT leads Restormer [51] by 0.28 dB PSNR and 0.0020 SSIM, and MPRNet [50] by 0.36 dB PSNR, while also achieving the lowest LPIPS (0.0453 vs. 0.0461 for MPRNet). The consistent wins across all tasks and datasets, combined with the lowest parameter count among transformer-based methods, indicate that the gains stem from architectural design (SEDC + SADA) rather than model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Local Self-Attention (LSA) [31] by introducing a multi-scale multi-head mechanism with deformable sampling offsets (SADA), and builds on Deformable ConvNets v2 [56] to construct a scale-enhanced deformable convolution module (SEDC) that handles orientation and shape distortions. It departs from prior deformable attention work (e.g., Xia et al. [43]) by integrating deformable sampling directly within the self-attention computation rather than combining it with a separate deformable convolution for offset prediction. The MIMO encoder-decoder architecture follows the coarse-to-fine fusion strategy of Cho et al. [8] and Cui et al. [9], while the overall design unifies three previously separate artifact-removal tasks (demoireing, deraining, debanding) under a single transformer backbone, whereas prior multi-scale methods [33, 48, 23, 30] typically address one artifact type at a time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in providing a single, parameter-efficient architecture (6.13M params, 39.7 GFLOPs) that achieves SOTA across three distinct structured-artifact removal tasks, reducing the need for task-specific model design. The SADA module's deformable-sampling approach to local self-attention offers a reusable technique for any restoration task where repetitive patterns exhibit non-uniform spatial distribution. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video artifacts, other degradation types (e.g., JPEG blocking, sensor noise), or unsupervised settings, and whether the multi-dilation grid design transfers to higher-resolution or larger-context scenarios beyond the 256×256 and 512×512 patch sizes tested.
