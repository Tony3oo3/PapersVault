## Problem

Generalized Category Discovery (GCD) methods require simultaneous access to source and target data during training, which is impractical when the target domain is only encountered at inference. Existing open-set domain generalization methods handle unseen target classes by lumping all novel samples into a single outlier, failing to perform fine-grained clustering of distinct novel categories. This paper introduces DG-GCD, a new setting that combines domain generalization (unseen target distribution) with fine-grained novel class discovery, and proposes a method to solve it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252812/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes a labeled source domain (e.g., Photo in PACS) with known class labels, plus synthetic pseudo-target domains generated by Instruct-Pix2Pix that contain the full class set but are treated as unlabeled. At inference, it takes unlabeled RGB images from a visually distinct target domain (e.g., Cartoon, Sketch) containing both known and novel classes at an undefined ratio, and produces per-sample cluster assignments via K-means with Hungarian assignment, along with an estimated number of clusters K determined by Brent's algorithm constrained between |Y_s| and 1000. Features are extracted using the [CLS] token of a DINO-pretrained ViT-B/16 backbone.

## Method

DG²CD-Net uses an episodic training framework with n_g global updates, each containing n_e episodes where a subset of source classes (labeled) is paired with a synthetic pseudo-domain (unlabeled, full class set) to simulate cross-domain GCD locally. Each episode fine-tunes the global ViT encoder under three losses: supervised contrastive (anchor-to-prototype), unsupervised contrastive (geometric augmentations), and open-set domain adaptation augmented with a novel margin loss that enforces separation between known-class confidence and open-set probability. After each episode, a task vector (parameter difference from the global model) is computed and weighted by the softmax of the episode model's GCD 'All' score on a held-out diverse validation set, then all weighted task vectors are summed to update the global model. This adaptive weighting prioritizes episodes with stronger generalization, preventing overspecialization and reducing gradient conflicts across episodes with divergent distributions.

## Results

On the DG-GCD benchmark (trained on source only, tested on unseen target), DG²CD-Net achieves an average All score of 52.06% across PACS, Office-Home, and DomainNet, with the LoRA variant (DG²CD-Net*) reaching 52.23%. The method outperforms the strongest DG baseline CDAD-Net (DG) by 3.07% in All (52.06 vs. 48.99) and by 3.95% on the New metric (50.78 vs. 46.83). The transductive upper bound CDAD-Net (DA), which uses target data during training, achieves 73.69% All, leaving a 21.63% gap that reflects the inherent difficulty of the DG setting. For cluster number estimation, DG²CD-Net matches ground truth on PACS (7/7) and closely approximates Office-Home (67 vs. 65) and DomainNet (355 vs. 345).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DG²CD-Net outperforms all seven baselines under the DG-GCD protocol. Against the strongest CD-GCD baseline CDAD-Net (DG, 48.99% All), the proposed method gains 3.07% in All and 3.95% on the New metric, indicating better novel-class clustering under domain shift. The GCD family baselines (GCD 44.13%, SimGCD 24.57%, CMS 13.77%) all degrade substantially without target access, with SimGCD and CMS collapsing on DomainNet (All < 3%), while DG²CD-Net maintains 29.01% All on DomainNet. Single-source open-set DG methods SODG-Net (33.91%) and multi-source DAML (34.52%) trail by roughly 18% in All, primarily because they collapse all novel samples into a single outlier class rather than discovering fine-grained novel categories. Adding synthetic domains to GCD/SimGCD/CMS/CDAD-Net yields modest gains (e.g., GCD 44.13→46.85%) but still falls short of DG²CD-Net's 52.06%, confirming that the episodic task-vector aggregation strategy is the key differentiator. The transductive upper bound CDAD-Net (DA) at 73.69% All remains 21.63% higher, reflecting the information gap when target data is unavailable at training time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of domain generalization (DG) and generalized category discovery (GCD), building directly on Task Arithmetic (Ilharco et al., ICLR 2023) for model aggregation and open-set adversarial domain adaptation (Saito et al., ECCV 2018) for cross-domain alignment. It departs from prior CD-GCD work (e.g., CDAD-Net) by removing the assumption of target-domain access during training, and from standard open-set DG by requiring fine-grained clustering of novel classes rather than a single outlier bucket. The key methodological departure is replacing static task-vector merging with an episodic, validation-weighted aggregation scheme, and augmenting the OSDA objective with a novel margin loss to enforce known-novel separation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DG-GCD formulation addresses a practically important gap: real-world systems (autonomous driving, medical imaging) must discover and cluster novel categories in unseen domains without any target data at training time. The ~22-point gap between the best DG-GCD result (52.06% All) and the transductive DA upper bound (73.69%) highlights how much remains unsolved, and the sign-conflict analysis (Fig. 3) reveals a fundamental tension between episode diversity and parameter stability that likely applies broadly to model-merging strategies. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher-dimensional or non-visual domains, whether the 6-episode optimum generalizes across architectures, and whether the large DA-to-DG gap can be closed with richer synthetic domain generation or multi-source training.
