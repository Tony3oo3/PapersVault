## Problem

As 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) becomes widely adopted for 3D content creation, there is no robust watermarking method that protects both the 3D-GS model and its rendered images while preserving real-time rendering speed. Existing radiance field watermarking approaches (e.g., WateRF) face two critical issues when applied to 3D-GS: redundant Gaussians inflate memory and storage overhead, and many Gaussians contribute negligibly to the rendered image, weakening the embedded watermark's robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251026/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained 3D-GS model (with its full set of Gaussians) and a binary message of 32, 48, or 64 bits, and produces a watermarked 3D-GS model whose rendered images from any viewpoint carry the message. For extraction, a single rendered image is transformed via DWT and the LL2 subband is fed to a frozen pre-trained HiDDeN decoder to recover the bits. The watermarked model contains ~28% fewer Gaussians than the original, reducing storage from 834 MB to 640 MB and increasing FPS from 56.65 to 72.68 on Mip-NeRF 360.

## Method

The core contribution is Frequency-Guided Densification (FGD), a two-phase preprocessing step: (1) an auxiliary loss with zeroed color parameters yields per-Gaussian contribution scores V_π, and Gaussians below a threshold (10⁻⁸) are removed; (2) rendered patches are analyzed via patch-wise DFT with a quadratic high-frequency mask, and Gaussians in the top-K% high-frequency patches are split into smaller ones to preserve detail. During fine-tuning, an exponential gradient mask (z = exp(−|θ|^β) normalized) attenuates gradients for color, opacity, rotation, and scale parameters, minimizing parameter drift from the pre-trained model. A wavelet-subband loss computed only on high-frequency DWT subbands (LH, HL, HH) further protects local structure in the modified regions. The total loss combines L1 reconstruction, LPIPS, wavelet-subband, and binary cross-entropy message losses, with the HiDDeN decoder operating on the LL2 subband of the rendered image.

## Results

On the combined Blender, LLFF, and Mip-NeRF 360 datasets, 3D-GSW achieves 97.37% bit accuracy with 35.08 PSNR, 0.978 SSIM, and 0.043 LPIPS for 32-bit messages, outperforming all baselines on every metric. At 64-bit capacity, the method still maintains 90.45% bit accuracy and 32.47 PSNR, whereas WateRF+3D-GS drops to 75.10% bit accuracy and 25.81 PSNR. Under image distortions (Table 2), 3D-GSW retains 90.84% bit accuracy under a combined crop+blur+JPEG attack, compared to 64.87% for WateRF+3D-GS and 76.71% for StegaNeRF+3D-GS. The method also reduces fine-tuning time to 21 minutes (vs. 6h47m for WateRF) and storage to 640 MB (vs. 834 MB) while increasing FPS from 56.65 to 72.68 on Mip-NeRF 360.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two external baselines adapted to 3D-GS: StegaNeRF+3D-GS (a steganography method with a modified decoder) and WateRF+3D-GS (the prior SOTA radiance-field watermarking method). 3D-GSW wins on all metrics across all message lengths. At 32 bits, it gains +4.2% bit accuracy over StegaNeRF (97.37 vs. 93.15) and +3.9% over WateRF (97.37 vs. 93.42), while also improving PSNR by +2.4 dB over StegaNeRF and +4.6 dB over WateRF. The gap widens substantially at 64 bits: 3D-GSW achieves 90.45% bit accuracy vs. 85.27% (StegaNeRF) and 75.10% (WateRF), a 15.4-point margin over WateRF. Under robustness attacks (Table 2), 3D-GSW maintains 90.84% bit accuracy under combined crop+blur+JPEG, while WateRF drops to 64.87% and StegaNeRF to 76.71%. Under model-level attacks (Table 3), 3D-GSW retains 96.87% accuracy after 20% Gaussian removal, compared to 80.58% for WateRF and 60.24% for StegaNeRF. The ablation variant (3D-GSW without FGD) confirms that FGD contributes +2.8% bit accuracy at 32 bits and +7.96% at 64 bits over the no-FGD version.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the radiance field watermarking line (WateRF, StegaNeRF) to the 3D Gaussian Splatting setting, adapting the wavelet-subband message extraction strategy from WateRF and the gradient mask mechanism from StegaNeRF to handle 3D-GS's explicit, parameter-rich representation. The key departure is Frequency-Guided Densification (FGD), which uses patch-wise DFT to identify high-frequency regions and selectively removes or splits 3D Gaussians there—a frequency-domain pruning strategy not present in prior radiance field watermarking. The exponential gradient mask is a practical modification of StegaNeRF's approach to avoid division-by-zero in 3D-GS parameters, and the wavelet-subband loss (using only high-frequency subbands) is a targeted extension of WateRF's full-subband loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As 3D-GS moves into commercial pipelines (games, Metaverse, film), robust copyright protection for both the model and its rendered outputs is an urgent practical need that this paper addresses with a complete, efficient pipeline. The FGD mechanism is notable for simultaneously reducing storage (~23% less), accelerating fine-tuning (~20× faster than WateRF), and improving watermark robustness—three goals that typically conflict. Open questions include whether the method generalizes to dynamic or 4D Gaussian scenes, whether the fixed HiDDeN decoder limits capacity scaling, and whether the ~28% Gaussian removal threshold transfers across scene types. The paper's limitation of requiring a pre-trained decoder per message length, while manageable, adds a deployment dependency.
