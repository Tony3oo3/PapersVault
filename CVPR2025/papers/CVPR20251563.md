## Problem

Existing image-to-3D pipelines split multi-view generation and 3D reconstruction into independent stages, creating two critical gaps: multi-view diffusion models are optimized at the image level without explicit 3D geometric modeling, making cross-view consistency difficult to guarantee, and feed-forward reconstruction models trained on synthetic data suffer a domain gap when processing generated multi-view images at inference. Prior attempts to bridge these stages (e.g., resampling rendered views during inference) lack joint training and cannot exploit spatial 3D information to unify the two processes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as conditioning input and produces 8 multi-view consistent images at 512×512 resolution (arranged as orbiting camera frames) along with a 3D Gaussian Splatting model. During the denoising loop, 4 specific views from the reparameterized prediction are fed to the LGM reconstruction module, and the resulting rendered color images plus Canonical Coordinate Maps are encoded via lightweight CNN adapters and injected into the U-Net at multiple scales. Inference runs 25 sampling steps in approximately 20 seconds on a single GPU.

## Method

Ouroboros3D integrates a Stable Video Diffusion-based multi-view generator (with pixel-level Plucker ray camera conditioning) and an LGM-based 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction model into a single recursive diffusion loop. At each denoising timestep, the predicted x̃₀ is decoded into images, passed through the feed-forward reconstruction model, and the rendered color images and Canonical Coordinate Maps (CCMs) are fed back as self-conditioning inputs to the next denoising step. CCMs are preferred over depth or normal maps because they encode global vertex coordinates normalized across the entire 3D model, providing explicit cross-view geometric alignment. Joint training employs a probabilistic self-conditioning mechanism (50% chance of using 3D feedback per iteration) and trains the reconstruction model on diffusion-restored images rather than ground-truth images, closing the domain gap while simultaneously using the reconstruction model as a geometric criterion to supervise the diffusion model.

## Results

On the GSO dataset, Ouroboros3D achieves the best performance across all metrics in both multi-view generation and 3D reconstruction. For multi-view generation, it reaches PSNR 21.770, SSIM 0.8866, and LPIPS 0.1093, outperforming the closest competitor SV3D (PSNR 21.042, SSIM 0.8497, LPIPS 0.1296). For 3D reconstruction, it achieves PSNR 21.761, SSIM 0.8894, and LPIPS 0.1091, surpassing InstantMesh (PSNR 19.948, SSIM 0.8727, LPIPS 0.1205) by a substantial margin of ~1.8 dB in PSNR. Inference takes approximately 20 seconds per sample with 25 denoising steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ouroboros3D outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric in both the multi-view generation and 3D reconstruction tasks. In multi-view generation, it surpasses SV3D (the strongest baseline) by 0.73 dB PSNR and 0.037 SSIM, while also exceeding VideoMV and SyncDreamer by larger margins. In 3D reconstruction, the gap over InstantMesh (the best two-stage baseline) is particularly large: +1.81 dB PSNR and +0.017 SSIM, indicating that the joint training and 3D-aware feedback mechanism substantially reduce the domain gap between generated views and the reconstruction model's training distribution. VideoMV, which combines multi-view diffusion and reconstruction only at inference via resampling, shows that biased information from early-stage reconstructions can misalign outputs with the input image. Two-stage pipelines (LGM, InstantMesh) suffer from incomplete geometry due to the disparity between independently trained multi-view generation and reconstruction modules, a limitation that Ouroboros3D's recursive joint training directly addresses.

## Relation to Prior Work

Ouroboros3D sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: multi-view diffusion models (SVD, SyncDreamer, SV3D, VideoMV) and feed-forward 3D reconstruction models (LGM, InstantMesh, TripoSR). Prior two-stage pipelines either keep these stages fully separate or combine them only at inference via resampling (VideoMV, IM-3D), leaving a domain gap between generated multi-view images and the reconstruction model's training distribution. The key departure here is joint training of both modules within a recursive diffusion loop, where 3D-aware feedback (rendered color images and Canonical Coordinate Maps) from the reconstruction step conditions the next denoising step, and the reconstruction model is trained on diffusion-restored images rather than clean ground truth. The use of CCMs over depth or normal maps for cross-view geometric alignment, and the pixel-level Plucker ray conditioning inherited from EpiDiff/Free3D, are specific architectural choices that distinguish this from prior camera-conditioned diffusion approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two concrete failure modes of two-stage image-to-3D pipelines—geometric inconsistency in multi-view generation and domain gap in reconstruction—with a unified recursive framework that is modular and extensible to different multi-view generators and 3D representations. The practical impact is moderate: inference time (~25s) is comparable to two-stage baselines, but training cost roughly doubles (36 min vs. 15+10 min for individual components), and the current 3DGS output is less universally usable than meshes. Open questions include whether the recursive feedback generalizes to 3D scenes (not just single objects), whether mesh-based reconstruction (CRM, InstantMesh) benefits equally from the joint training strategy, and whether the 0.5 probability of self-conditioning during training is optimal or architecture-dependent.
