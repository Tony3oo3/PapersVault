## Problem

Reconstruction-based post-training quantization methods that work well for CNNs fail on Vision Transformers due to two issues: (1) the Hessian-based output importance estimation relies on Fisher Information Matrix and squared-gradient approximations that introduce significant errors, and (2) post-GELU activations exhibit highly imbalanced distributions (negative values densely packed in [-0.17, 0]) and large dynamic ranges (up to 40), causing severe quantization error under ultra-low bits.

## Input / Output

The method requires a full-precision pretrained ViT (ViT, DeiT, or Swin variants) and a small unlabeled calibration set of 1024 ImageNet images (or 256 COCO images for detection/segmentation). It produces a quantized model using only channel-wise uniform quantizers for weights and layer-wise uniform quantizers for activations—no specialized quantizers (e.g., twin-uniform or logarithmic) are needed. The MLP Reconstruction step additionally replaces GELU with ReLU, which can be folded into the preceding linear layer, yielding a modest inference speedup (e.g., 1.51× vs 1.40× for DeiT-T at W8A8).

## Method

APHQ-ViT operates block-wise with two reconstruction stages, both optimized by a novel Average Perturbation Hessian (APH) loss. Instead of approximating the Hessian via the Fisher Information Matrix (as in BRECQ), APH directly estimates the diagonal Hessian by perturbing the block output by ±10⁻⁶, computing the Jacobian difference via backward propagation, and averaging across all calibration samples to reduce gradient variance (Theorem 3.2). For the post-GELU activation problem, the MLP Reconstruction (MR) stage replaces GELU with ReLU and reconstructs the MLP weights using a combined distillation loss: an APH-weighted L2 term (L_Direct) matching the ReLU-MLP output to the original GELU-MLP output, plus a clamp term (L_Clamp) that constrains activations to the 99th percentile of positive values to reduce the dynamic range. The quantization reconstruction stage then applies QDrop with the APH loss to tune AdaRound rounding decisions, requiring only one additional forward-backward pass per iteration beyond standard QDrop.

## Results

On ImageNet with W3/A3 quantization, APHQ-ViT achieves 63.17% top-1 accuracy on ViT-S, 76.31% on ViT-B, 55.42% on DeiT-T, and 78.14% on Swin-B, surpassing the second-best method (DopQ-ViT) by an average of 7.21% across all architectures. At W4/A4, APHQ-ViT reaches 76.07% on ViT-S, 82.41% on ViT-B, and 83.42% on Swin-B, consistently outperforming all baselines while using only standard uniform quantizers. On COCO at W4/A4, the method achieves 38.9 APb / 38.1 APm (Mask R-CNN Swin-T) and 50.3 APb / 43.7 APm (Cascade Mask R-CNN Swin-S), matching or exceeding the best competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At the challenging W3/A3 setting, APHQ-ViT decisively outperforms all 11 baselines across every architecture. The closest competitor, DopQ-ViT (which uses a specialized tangent quantizer), is beaten by 8.45 points on ViT-S (63.17 vs. 54.72), 10.71 points on DeiT-T (55.42 vs. 44.71), and 8.51 points on Swin-S (76.10 vs. 67.59). Calibration-only methods such as PTQ4ViT and RepQ-ViT collapse to near-chance accuracy (0.10%) on most models at 3-bit, while AdaLog, the best calibration-only method, still trails APHQ-ViT by 10–20 points. At W4/A4, APHQ-ViT maintains a consistent lead: it exceeds DopQ-ViT by 0.38 points on ViT-S (76.07 vs. 75.69), 1.12 points on DeiT-T (66.66 vs. 65.54), and 0.08 points on Swin-B (83.42 vs. 83.34), all while using only standard uniform quantizers rather than specialized ones. On COCO detection/segmentation at W4/A4, APHQ-ViT matches or slightly exceeds the best baselines (e.g., 38.9 vs. 37.5 APb on Mask R-CNN Swin-T; 50.3/43.7 APb/APm on Cascade Mask R-CNN Swin-S, tying DopQ-ViT and I&S-ViT). Notably, QDrop with MSE loss (the direct architectural predecessor) underperforms APHQ-ViT by 20.8 points on ViT-S at 3-bit, highlighting the critical contribution of the APH loss and MLP Reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the block-reconstruction PTQ lineage (BRECQ, QDrop, AdaRound) by replacing BRECQ's Fisher Information Matrix–based Hessian approximation with a direct perturbation-based estimation (the APH loss), and by introducing an MLP Reconstruction step that swaps GELU for ReLU to tame post-GELU activation ranges. It departs from prior ViT-specific PTQ work (PTQ4ViT, RepQ-ViT, AdaLog, DopQ-ViT) that relied on specialized non-uniform quantizers, instead showing that standard uniform quantizers suffice when the reconstruction loss and activation distribution are properly addressed. The distillation-loss formulation also generalizes the framework beyond classification to detection and segmentation, where BRECQ's CE-based Hessian is inapplicable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 7.21% average accuracy gain at 3-bit over the prior best reconstruction-based method, achieved with only uniform quantizers and 1024 unlabeled calibration images, makes ultra-low-bit ViT deployment substantially more practical without exotic hardware support. The MLP Reconstruction side-benefit of replacing GELU with ReLU (folding into the preceding linear layer) also yields a 5–10% inference speedup at W8A8, a practical win for edge deployment. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 2-bit or mixed-precision settings, whether the GELU→ReLU substitution degrades performance on tasks requiring richer nonlinearity (e.g., generative models), and whether the APH loss's advantage over per-sample PH persists at larger calibration set sizes.
