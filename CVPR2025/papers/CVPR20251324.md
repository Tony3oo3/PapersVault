## Problem

Existing machine unlearning methods operate in model space (weight regularization, pruning) or input space (adversarial perturbations, random labeling), but none directly modify the model's output probabilities with a principled bound on how much uncertainty to introduce. Moreover, large-scale unlearning on datasets like ImageNet1k is infeasible via retraining, and established evaluation metrics (Avg Gap, JSD) require a gold-standard retrained model that cannot be obtained under real-world data-access constraints.

## Input / Output

LoTUS takes a pre-trained classifier (ViT or ResNet18), a forget set (10% or 50% of training images), a retain set restricted to only 30% of the remaining samples to simulate limited data access, and an unseen set (validation split or AI-generated synthetic images from CIFAKE) used solely for calibration. It outputs an unlearned model whose prediction probabilities on forget samples are smoothed so that the model's accuracy on those samples matches the pre-trained model's accuracy on unseen data, effectively erasing overconfident memorized predictions while preserving general class-level knowledge.

## Method

LoTUS frames unlearning through an information-theoretic decomposition: the mutual information between input and output is split into global information (shared class features) and subset-specific information (memorized sample details), and the unlearning objective is to eliminate only the latter. Using Fano's Inequality, the paper approximates the required conditional entropy with a measurable accuracy target: Acc(f_un, D_f) = Acc(f_orig, D_u). This is realized via a knowledge-distillation loop where both teacher and student start from f_orig; the teacher's logits are passed through a Gumbel-Softmax with a dynamically scheduled temperature τ_d = exp(α·(Acc(f_un,D_f) − Acc(f_orig,D_u))), which smooths outputs when the student is overconfident (τ_d>1) and sharpens them when over-unlearning occurs (τ_d<1). For retain samples, τ→0⁺ maximally sharpens the teacher distribution to lock in retained knowledge, while the Gumbel noise on forget-sample logits injects stochasticity that drives the student's accuracy down toward the target without requiring retraining from scratch.

## Results

On the main benchmarks (Table 1), LoTUS achieves the best Avg Gap score across nearly all model–dataset combinations: 0.0150 (ViT/TinyImageNet), 0.0125 (ViT/CIFAR-100), 0.1675 (ResNet18/CIFAR-100), and 0.1200 (ViT/MUFAC), while also being the fastest method (e.g., 13.41 min vs. 76.97 min for SalUn on ViT/TinyImageNet). On the large-scale ImageNet1k experiment (Table 4), LoTUS achieves the lowest RF-JSD of 1.11×10⁻⁴ with a runtime of 10.72 min, outperforming the best baseline SSD (1.23×10⁻⁴, 22.61 min) and uniquely reducing MIA accuracy from 0.71 to 0.61 while preserving retain accuracy at 0.94. LoTUS also consistently achieves the best JSD scores, indicating superior resilience to the Streisand effect across all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoTUS outperforms all six baselines on Avg Gap across nearly every benchmark. Against SCRUB [23] (the closest competitor on ViT/TinyImageNet with Avg Gap 0.0225), LoTUS achieves 0.0150 and is 2.5× faster (13.41 vs. 33.25 min). Against SSD [14], LoTUS wins on Avg Gap in all six settings (e.g., 0.1675 vs. 0.2700 on ResNet18/CIFAR-100) and is consistently faster. UNSIR [34] is the slowest baseline (76.97 min on ViT/TinyImageNet) and also lags on Avg Gap (0.0925 vs. 0.0150). SalUn [12] shows the largest JSD gaps (e.g., 1.93×10⁻⁴ vs. LoTUS's 0.62×10⁻⁴ on ResNet18/CIFAR-100), indicating weaker distributional alignment. BadT [9] and RndLbl [17] are clearly inferior on both effectiveness and efficiency. The sole exception is ResNet18/MUFAC Avg Gap, where LoTUS (0.1250) ties with RndLbl (0.1300) and BadT (0.1000) is slightly better, though LoTUS still dominates on JSD (6.90 vs. 9.41) and time (0.62 vs. 0.66 min).

## Relation to Prior Work

LoTUS builds on the knowledge distillation framework of Kurmanji et al. [23], where teacher and student are both initialized from the pre-trained model, but departs fundamentally by operating in the output (logits) space rather than the model or data space. Prior unlearning methods either regularize weights toward the gold standard (model-space: NegGrad+, SCRUB, SSD) or modify inputs/labels (data-space: Random Labeling, adversarial perturbations), whereas LoTUS directly smooths prediction probabilities using a Gumbel-Softmax mechanism with a dynamically scheduled temperature. The information-theoretic framing via the Data Processing Inequality and Fano's Inequality distinguishes LoTUS from Random Labeling [17], which indiscriminately maximizes entropy, by establishing a principled upper bound on the uncertainty introduced—targeting only subset-specific memorized information while preserving global class-level knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of RF-JSD addresses a critical practical gap: evaluating unlearning quality when retraining a gold standard model is infeasible (e.g., ImageNet1k with restricted data access), a scenario that mirrors real-world deployment constraints. The large-scale ImageNet1k experiment is the first to demonstrate effective unlearning under conditions where retraining is simply impossible, making the results directly relevant to industry practitioners managing large pre-trained models. Limitations include the primary focus on instance-wise unlearning (class unlearning is only briefly addressed), the reliance on an unseen set (real or synthetic) that is distributionally similar to the forget set, and the assumption that the retain set is accessible (albeit partially). Open questions include whether the output-space approach generalizes to generative models or multi-modal settings, and whether the distributional similarity assumption can be further relaxed.
