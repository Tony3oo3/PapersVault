## Problem

GNN-based neural architecture predictors are limited to local adjacency information and cannot capture complex feature interactions, while transformer-based predictors suffer from poor generalization on deep architectures because global attention mixes information from operations far apart. Additionally, sibling nodes—operations sharing a common parent or child—are pivotal for architecture representation (capturing complementary features and concurrent execution) yet have been overlooked in prior work.

## Input / Output

The model takes a neural architecture as a directed acyclic graph G=(V,E,Z) where each node is an operation with one-hot encoded type and sinusoidally encoded hyperparameters, and edges form a binary adjacency matrix A. It outputs a single scalar: either predicted validation accuracy (Kendall's Tau ranking) or predicted inference latency (MAPE, Acc(10%)). Notably, no position encoding is required, and the same architecture handles both cell-structured networks (≤7 nodes per cell) and complete networks with up to 200 operations.

## Method

NN-Former is a hybrid GNN-Transformer that replaces global attention with a 4-head Adjacency-Sibling Multi-head Attention (ASMA), where each head applies a distinct topological mask: forward adjacency A, backward adjacency Aᵀ, siblings sharing parents (AAᵀ), and siblings sharing children (AᵀA). For channel mixing, a Bidirectional Graph Isomorphism Feed-Forward Network (BGIFFN) concatenates forward graph convolution GC(H,A) and backward GC(H,Aᵀ) before a standard FFN, learning bidirectional topology without position encoding. The key insight is that sibling relationships—computed directly from the adjacency matrix—provide complementary feature extraction for accuracy and concurrent-execution cues for latency, while long-range dependencies are less critical since features propagate node-by-node. This design achieves strong generalization across depths (20–200 layers) and outperforms both GNN-only and transformer-only baselines on accuracy and latency prediction.

## Results

On NAS-Bench-101, NN-Former achieves Kendall's Tau of 0.709 (0.02% training), 0.765 (0.04%), 0.809 (0.1%), and 0.877 (1%), consistently outperforming all baselines. On NAS-Bench-201, it reaches 0.804 (1%) and 0.890 (10%) Kendall's Tau. For out-of-domain latency prediction on NNLQ, NN-Former achieves an average MAPE of 8.39% and Acc(10%) of 74.31%, surpassing NAR-Former V2 (10.55% MAPE, 62.70% Acc) by 11.61% in Acc(10%). In the in-domain NNLQ setting, it attains 2.85% average MAPE and 97.45% Acc(10%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NN-Former outperforms all 16 baselines on NAS-Bench-101 across every training budget, with the largest margin at 0.02% training (0.709 vs. 0.679 for PINAT, +3.0 points) and a consistent 0.6–4.6 point lead over the strongest transformer (NAR-Former, 0.871 at 1%). On NAS-Bench-201, NN-Former leads at 1% (0.804 vs. 0.752 for NAR-Former V2) and 3% (0.860 vs. 0.846), but is slightly behind NAR-Former (0.901) at 10% (0.890), which the authors attribute to their unified accuracy+latency design. For out-of-domain latency on NNLQ, NN-Former achieves the best average MAPE (8.39%) and Acc(10%) (74.31%), beating NAR-Former V2 by 2.16% MAPE and 11.61% Acc(10%), while kernel-based methods (nn-Meter, TPU) and FLOPs-based baselines lag far behind. In the in-domain NNLQ setting, NN-Former also leads with 2.85% MAPE and 97.45% Acc(10%), and notably avoids the per-model-type failures (e.g., AlexNet) that affect NNLP and NAR-Former V2. On general DAG tasks (Cora citation, ogbg-code2 AST), adding sibling attention to DAG Transformer improves Cora by 0.75% but slightly hurts ogbg-code2, confirming that sibling cues are task-dependent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes the strengths of GNN-based predictors (e.g., GATES, NNLP) and transformer-based predictors (e.g., TNASP, NAR-Former, NAR-Former V2) into a unified hybrid architecture. Its key conceptual departure is identifying 'sibling nodes'—operations sharing a common parent or child—as a critical topological relationship that prior methods (which focus on adjacency or transitive closure) have overlooked. The Adjacency-Sibling Multi-head Attention (ASMA) and Bidirectional Graph Isomorphism Feed-Forward Network (BGIFFN) modules are novel designs that inject this sibling-aware topology into a transformer backbone, eliminating the need for explicit position encoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sibling-node insight reframes how DAG topology should be modeled for neural architecture representation, with potential implications beyond NAS to other DAG tasks (demonstrated on Cora citation prediction). The finding that position encoding is unnecessary—and even harmful—when sufficient topological structure is learned is a useful simplification for practitioners. However, the method's gains on NAS-Bench-201 at higher training ratios are marginal, and the sibling-node evidence on general DAG tasks is limited to a single small-scale experiment, leaving open whether the insight generalizes broadly or is specific to the parallel-branch structure common in neural architectures.
