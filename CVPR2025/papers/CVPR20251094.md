## Problem

Existing multimodal survival prediction methods assume both histopathological images and genomic data are always available, yet genomic data is expensive to collect and frequently missing in testing samples. Two specific challenges remain unaddressed: (1) gigapixel whole slide images (WSIs) are too large for effective representation in a generative reconstruction setting, and (2) genomic features span diverse biological functional categories (e.g., oncogenesis, cell differentiation) that are difficult to generate within a single unified generative framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251094/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes gigapixel WSIs (e.g., 150,000×150,000 pixels) segmented into non-overlapping 10× magnification patches, encoded by a pre-trained CTransPath Swin Transformer, and genomic profiles grouped into 6 functional categories (Tumor Suppression, Oncogenesis, Protein Kinases, Cellular Differentiation, Transcription, Cytokines and Growth) encoded by an SNN. Genomic profiles are optional at test time. The model outputs a censored survival prediction (time-to-event with right-censoring) and, when genomic data is absent, reconstructed genomic embeddings for all 6 functional categories.

## Method

The architecture has three core components: (1) VIB-Trans compresses the large set of WSI patch features into a bag-level posterior using a Variational Information Bottleneck objective with a Transformer encoder that employs Nystrom attention for efficiency and two learnable tokens (μ^token, Σ^token) for global information aggregation; (2) LD-VAE introduces a latent differentiation process that assigns each genomic functional category a unique latent variable z_i forming a Markov chain x_i ↔ z_i ↔ z_X, thereby learning function-specific posteriors from a shared genomic posterior to handle diverse biological functions; (3) LD-CVAE combines both via a product-of-experts (PoE) to estimate the joint posterior q(z|X,Y) from marginal posteriors, augmented by a Wasserstein-distance alignment loss between pathological and genomic latent distributions. The PoE formulation is the key enabler for robustness: the joint posterior is learned with complete multimodal data during training but can be approximated from the pathological marginal alone at test time when genomic data is missing. Final prediction uses a Genomic-Guided Co-Attention layer followed by set-based MIL Transformers to fuse instance-level pathological features with reconstructed genomic features.

## Results

On complete multimodal data, LD-CVAE achieves an overall C-index of 0.719 across five TCGA cancer cohorts, outperforming the best multimodal baseline SurvPath (0.709) by 1.0% and the best unimodal genomic model SNNTrans (0.667) by 5.2%. Under the missing-genomic-data scenario, the method retains an overall C-index of 0.684, surpassing the strongest missing-modality baseline ShaSpec (0.670) by 1.4% and the best pathology-only model TransMIL (0.660) by 2.4%. The method achieves the highest C-index on 4 out of 5 datasets in the complete-data setting and on all 5 datasets in the missing-modality setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against unimodal baselines, LD-CVAE outperforms the best genomic-only model (SNNTrans, 0.667 overall) by 5.2% and the best pathology-only model (TransMIL, 0.660) by 5.9% in C-index. Among multimodal competitors, LD-CVAE surpasses SurvPath (0.709), CMTA (0.706), and MOTCat (0.700) on the overall C-index, winning on 4 of 5 individual datasets (BLCA, BRCA, GBMLGG, LUAD) while narrowly losing on UCEC (0.703 vs. SurvPath's 0.709). In the missing-genomic-data setting (Table 2), LD-CVAE achieves 0.684 overall C-index, beating ShaSpec (0.670), Robust Multimodal Transformer (0.669), SMIL (0.666), VAE (0.663), MVAE (0.662), and GAN (0.661) on all five cancer cohorts. The ablation study confirms that VIB-Trans contributes +1.8% (complete) / +1.9% (missing) and LD-VAE contributes +1.2% (complete) / +0.9% (missing) over their respective ablated variants, validating each architectural component's role.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes several established components—conditional VAEs (Socher et al.), variational information bottleneck (Alemi et al.), product-of-experts joint posterior estimation (Wu & Goodman), and the MCAT co-attention fusion framework (Chen et al., ICCV 2021)—into a unified architecture for robust multimodal survival prediction. The key conceptual departure is the Latent Differentiation VAE, which introduces function-specific posteriors derived from a shared genomic posterior to handle the heterogeneous biological roles of different gene categories, and the VIB-Trans module that compresses gigapixel WSI representations via Nystrom-approximated attention with learnable tokens. Unlike prior missing-modality approaches (MVAE, SMIL, ShaSpec) that treat modalities symmetrically, this work specifically targets the asymmetric clinical scenario where expensive genomic data is absent but pathology images are always available.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: genomic profiling is costly and often unavailable at testing time, yet most multimodal survival models assume complete data. By reconstructing function-specific genomic embeddings from WSIs, the method narrows the performance gap between complete and missing-modality settings (0.719 vs. 0.684 overall C-index), making multimodal survival prediction more deployable in real clinical workflows. However, the validation is limited to five TCGA cancer cohorts, the reconstruction quality is assessed only indirectly through downstream survival prediction rather than direct genomic accuracy, and the method does not address the reverse scenario (missing pathology). Open questions include whether the generated genomic features capture true molecular biology or merely survival-correlated proxies, and whether the approach generalizes to other missing-modality patterns or non-cancer applications.
