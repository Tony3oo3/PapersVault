## Problem

Existing video-music datasets provide paired content but lack detailed annotations capturing the multi-dimensional semantic alignment (rhythm, emotion, theme, culture) and temporal synchronization between video and music. Current MLLMs consequently offer only surface-level interpretations, failing to identify nuanced transitions, emotional congruence, or culturally specific connections that define high-quality video-music pairings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250218/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset comprises 48,328 short-form video-music pairs (2.96–63.38 s, avg. 31.5 s, 458.8 h total) crawled from YouTube Shorts with user-added background music, verified via PANNs. For inference, open-source MLLMs consume 16 uniformly sampled frames plus the audio track, while Gemini 1.5 Pro receives 1 fps sampling. Outputs are structured text annotations averaging 352.65 words per pair, organized into four dimensions (Rhythm & Synchronization, Theme, Emotion, Culture), with 58% of entries containing explicit timestamped transition points; the benchmark further provides a multiple-choice variant (HarmonySet-MC) with four options per dimension for objective accuracy scoring.

## Method

The core contribution is a multi-step human-machine collaborative annotation pipeline: three independent human annotators first identify key synchronization timestamps and assign structured labels across the four alignment dimensions, then Gemini 1.5 Pro generates enriched, temporally grounded descriptions conditioned on the video, audio, verified manual labels, and metadata. A dedicated reviewer cross-checks timestamp accuracy and label consistency, yielding 92% high-consensus annotations. For evaluation, HarmonySet-OE uses an LLM-as-judge protocol (comparing model outputs against ground-truth annotations on a 1–10 scale per dimension), while HarmonySet-MC converts annotations into four-option multiple-choice questions via GPT-4o with carefully crafted distractors. The instruction-tuning subset (44,470 pairs) is used to fine-tune VideoLLaMA2 (Qwen2-7B backbone), demonstrating that human-in-the-loop annotations substantially outperform fully automated Gemini-generated labels in capturing synchronization and semantic alignment.

## Results

On HarmonySet-OE (open-ended, LLM-as-judge scoring 1–10), Gemini-1.5 Pro leads among untrained models with overall scores of 5.43 (Rhythm & Synchronization), 5.18 (Theme), 5.15 (Emotion), and 4.51 (Culture). Fine-tuning VideoLLaMA2 on HarmonySet yields a 1.40-point gain in R&S (4.15→5.55) and a 1.57-point gain in Culture (3.05→4.62), surpassing Gemini-1.5 Pro in R&S (5.55 vs. 5.43) and Emotion (5.26 vs. 5.15). On the multiple-choice benchmark HarmonySet-MC, human annotators achieve 84–94% accuracy while the best model (Gemini-1.5 Pro) reaches only 42–50%, highlighting a substantial gap between current MLLMs and human-level video-music understanding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Gemini-1.5 Pro (the strongest untrained baseline), VideoLLaMA2 fine-tuned on HarmonySet wins on Rhythm & Synchronization (5.55 vs. 5.43) and Emotion (5.26 vs. 5.15), but trails on Theme (5.06 vs. 5.18) and Culture (4.62 vs. 4.51 is actually a win—correction: 4.62 vs. 4.51, so it also wins Culture). Against untrained VideoLLaMA2, the HarmonySet fine-tune improves every dimension, most dramatically in Culture (+1.57) and R&S (+1.40). video-SALMONN is the weakest baseline across all four metrics (2.12–3.55), confirming that speech-enhanced audio-visual models without music-specific training struggle with video-music alignment. On HarmonySet-MC, even the best model (Gemini-1.5 Pro at 42–50% accuracy) falls far short of human performance (84–94%), and VideoLLaMA2(HarmonySet) actually drops in R&S accuracy (10.63%) compared to untrained VideoLLaMA2 (21.76%), suggesting the multiple-choice format is particularly challenging for rhythm-related questions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of video-music datasets and MLLM instruction tuning. Prior video-music resources (TT-150K, MovieClips, BGM909, MMTrail) provide paired content or narrow annotations (rhythm, chords) but none offer unified multi-dimensional semantic alignment (rhythm, emotion, theme, culture) combined with timestamped temporal synchronization. The human-machine collaborative annotation framework repurposes the LLM-as-judge paradigm in reverse—using an MLLM (Gemini 1.5 Pro) to generate rich descriptions conditioned on human-verified timestamps and labels—rather than using it solely for evaluation. The benchmark design (open-ended + multiple-choice with LLM-constructed distractors) extends standard MLLM evaluation to a domain where surface-level matching is insufficient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HarmonySet fills a clear gap: no prior dataset simultaneously provides semantic matching and temporal synchronization annotations for video-music, making it a necessary resource for training MLLMs on this task. The large human-model gap on HarmonySet-MC (humans ~85–94% vs. best model ~41–54%) reveals that current MLLMs fundamentally struggle with nuanced video-music reasoning, particularly cultural relevance and rhythmic synchronization. Open questions include whether the human-machine annotation framework generalizes to other multimodal alignment tasks, whether dedicated video-music architectures (rather than general MLLMs) are needed to close the gap, and how the dataset's short-video focus (<63 s) limits conclusions about longer-form content. The finding that 64 frames underperform 16 frames for sub-minute videos is a practical caution for frame-sampling strategies in short-form video understanding.
