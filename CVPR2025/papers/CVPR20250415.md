## Problem

Existing medical vision-language models rely solely on text instructions for input and produce only text answers for output, lacking the ability to accept visual prompts (points, bounding boxes) to specify regions of interest and to ground medical entity mentions in the response to pixel-level segmentation masks. No prior model simultaneously supports both visual referring in the input and pixel grounding in the output, which are essential for practical medical image interpretation where communication among physicians is centered on image referring and grounding.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a medical RGB image, a free-form text query, and an optional sparse visual prompt (a point or bounding box) that specifies a region of interest. The output is a text response in which groundable medical entities are wrapped in special tokens (<p>…<SEG></p>), and each <SEG> token triggers the generation of a corresponding binary segmentation mask via a SAM decoder, so the final response is a set of text tokens plus (concept, mask) pairs where concepts are a subset of the text tokens.

## Method

MIMO combines a CLIP ViT-H/14 image encoder, a SAM-style visual prompt encoder, a Multi-modal Input Aligner, a Vicuna-7B LLM, and a SAM encoder-decoder for mask generation. The Multi-modal Input Aligner uses cross-attention between learnable query embeddings and the concatenated (image, text, visual-prompt) feature sequence to extract instruction-guided visual information before feeding it to the LLM. For grounding, the LLM vocabulary is extended with <p>, </p>, and <SEG> tokens; the hidden state at each <SEG> position is projected into the SAM decoder's feature space and combined with SAM image features to produce a binary mask, thereby linking each mentioned medical entity to its pixel region. Training uses a combined loss of cross-entropy for text generation plus per-pixel BCE and DICE losses for masks, supervised on the newly constructed MIMOSeg dataset (895K samples across four perspectives: language-guided segmentation, visual-prompt perceiving, segmentation-aligned QA, and visual-prompt-assisted QA) mixed with LLaVA-Med VQA data.

## Results

On the held-in MIMOSeg benchmark, MIMO achieves mIoU 0.665, AP50 0.279, and F1 0.406 on Perspective II (visual prompt segmentation), outperforming GLaMM (mIoU 0.496, AP50 0.137, F1 0.279) and SAM-h (mIoU 0.571, AP50 0.132). On held-out medical VQA, MIMO reaches 56.1% average accuracy across VQA-RAD, SLAKE, and PathVQA, surpassing LLaVA-Med (52.3%) and Qwen-VL-Chat (52.7%). On held-out segmentation (X-ray, text-only), MIMO achieves mIoU 0.718 and AP50 0.507, substantially exceeding GLaMM (mIoU 0.554, AP50 0.335).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIMO consistently outperforms GLaMM across all four held-in perspectives on mIoU (e.g., 0.665 vs. 0.496 on Perspective II; 0.531 vs. 0.421 on Perspective III) and F1, with the largest gains in AP50 for visual-prompt tasks (0.279 vs. 0.137 on Perspective II). Against SAM and SAM-Med, MIMO dominates on text-instruction segmentation (Perspectives I and III) where SAM variants cannot operate, and also surpasses them on mIoU and F1 for visual-prompt segmentation (Perspective II: mIoU 0.665 vs. 0.571 for SAM-h), though SAM-h retains an edge on AP50 for Perspective IV (0.404 vs. 0.309). On held-out VQA, MIMO (56.1% avg) beats LLaVA-Med (52.3%), Qwen-VL-Chat (52.7%), Med-Flamingo (47.9%), and RAD-FM (41.3%), but trails HuatuoGPT-Vision (66.1%), which was trained on a substantially larger dataset (PubMedVision, 1.3M samples). On held-out segmentation, MIMO achieves the best mIoU on X-ray with text instructions (0.718 vs. GLaMM's 0.554) and matches or exceeds SAM-h on bounding-box-guided segmentation across all three modalities. The Multi-modal Input Aligner ablation confirms its contribution: removing it drops Perspective II AP50 from 0.279 to 0.185 and Perspective IV mIoU from 0.586 to 0.526.

## Relation to Prior Work

MIMO sits at the intersection of three research threads: medical VQA (LLaVA-Med, HuatuoGPT-Vision, Med-Flamingo), general-domain referring/grounding MLLMs (GLaMM, Ferret, Ferret-v2), and promptable segmentation (SAM). It departs from prior medical VLMs by adding a Multi-modal Input Aligner that fuses CLIP image features, text embeddings, and SAM-style visual prompt embeddings via cross-attention, and by introducing grounding tokens (<p>, <SEG>) in the LLM vocabulary to link generated text to SAM-decoded segmentation masks. Unlike GLaMM, which handles referring and grounding in the general domain, MIMO is the first to unify both capabilities specifically for medical imaging, and unlike SAM/SAM-Med, it produces text responses alongside masks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MIMOSeg dataset (895K samples across four perspectives and eight imaging modalities) addresses a genuine data gap in medical multimodal learning, where no prior dataset simultaneously provides referring and grounding annotations. The unified input-output formulation aligns with real clinical communication, where physicians point at regions and expect grounded explanations. However, the Q&A generation pipeline is bounded by a manually constructed knowledge base, limiting coverage, and the VQA accuracy on held-out benchmarks (56.1 avg) still trails HuatuoGPT-Vision (66.1 avg), suggesting the grounding capability comes at some cost to pure text reasoning. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 3D volumetric data and whether the knowledge-base bottleneck can be replaced with retrieval-augmented generation.
