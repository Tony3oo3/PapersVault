## Problem

Understanding and modeling multi-person collaborative object rearrangement (e.g., two people carrying a table together) is critical for VR/AR and human-robot interaction, yet no large-scale dataset captures these behaviors. Existing human-object interaction datasets focus on single-person actions or two-person handovers with limited object diversity, and precise multi-person mocap is too expensive to scale across thousands of object instances while vision-based tracking degrades under the severe occlusions inherent to collaboration.

## Input / Output

For the retargeting pipeline, the input is a source-target object pair (source interaction from CORE4D-Real plus a target ShapeNet object SDF), and the output is a full two-person SMPL-X collaboration motion with retargeted object trajectory. For the benchmarked forecasting task, the model receives an object 3D model and 15 frames of human-object poses (SMPL-X joint rotations in R^{23×3} plus object 6D pose) and predicts the next 15 frames. For interaction synthesis, the input is an object point-cloud geometry sequence G_o ∈ R^{T×N×3} and the output is two-person SMPL-X motion M_h ∈ R^{2×T×23×3}. All real captures are at 15 FPS with 1920×1080 RGB-D and egocentric RGB signals.

## Method

CORE4D is built via a hybrid acquisition strategy: 1K real sequences are captured with an inertial-optical Noitom mocap system (BVH skeletons + data gloves) synchronized with four Kinect Azure RGB-D cameras and an egocentric Osmo Action3, then annotated to SMPL-X meshes via a multi-term optimization (joint 3D/orientation, hand 3D/orientation, contact, smoothness, and regularization losses). To scale to 3K object shapes, a three-stage collaboration retargeting pipeline transfers interactions to novel ShapeNet objects: (1) object-centric contact retargeting interpolates DeepSDF latent vectors between source and target to propagate contact points through a mesh sequence via nearest-neighbor transfer; (2) contact-guided interaction retargeting jointly optimizes object 6D motion and two-person SMPL-X poses under fidelity, contact, spatial, and smoothness losses; (3) a GAN-based human pose discriminator, trained with a ranking loss using 6D-pose-noise negatives, scores candidate interactions and drives a beam-search over contact constraints to iteratively refine the retargeted motion. This human-centric selection criterion—prioritizing poses that look natural rather than merely matching contact geometry—enables retargeting across objects with large geometric and scale variations where purely object-centric methods fail.

## Results

On CORE4D's motion forecasting benchmark, CAHMP achieves the lowest object translation error (110.3 mm on S1, 112.9 mm on S2) and rotation error (9.0 deg / 9.5 deg), outperforming MDM (136.8/136.0 mm, 10.7/11.1 deg) and InterDiff (135.1/133.6 mm, 10.2/10.7 deg). However, all methods show a consistent increase in human joint position error when moving from seen objects (S1) to unseen objects (S2): MDM and InterDiff rise from 170.8 mm to 186.4 mm, while CAHMP only increases from 169.4 mm to 170.5 mm. On interaction synthesis, all three diffusion baselines (MDM, OMOMO, CHOIS) achieve similar human joint errors (~138-146 mm) and FID scores (~7.4-7.9), with no clear method superiority. The overarching finding is that object pose prediction generalizes reasonably across object shapes, but human pose prediction degrades on unseen objects, revealing a challenge in modeling collaborative motion for novel geometries.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MDM, InterDiff, and CAHMP on motion forecasting, and MDM, OMOMO, and CHOIS on interaction synthesis. CAHMP consistently outperforms MDM and InterDiff on object translation and rotation errors across both test sets (e.g., 110.3 mm vs 136.8 mm on S1), but does not report contact accuracy or penetration rate. On interaction synthesis, all three diffusion-based methods achieve comparable performance: OMOMO slightly leads on contact accuracy (78.0% on S1) while MDM has a lower FID (7.5 vs 7.8). No baseline is dominant across all metrics, and the paper does not propose a new method that outperforms baselines. The critical observation is that all methods show degradation from S1 to S2 in human joint error, with MDM and InterDiff showing the largest drop (+15.6 mm), indicating that existing methods struggle to generalize collaborative motion to novel object geometries. The paper's contribution is establishing benchmarks that reveal these open challenges rather than solving them.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the human-object interaction dataset lineage (GRAB, CHAIRS, HOH, CoChair) by shifting focus from individual or handover interactions to two-person collaborative object rearrangement, a scenario largely absent from prior 4D interaction datasets. The retargeting pipeline builds on OakInk's [119] SDF latent-space interpolation for contact transfer but departs from purely object-centric retargeting by introducing a human-centric contact selection stage using a GAN-based pose discriminator and beam search. The hybrid data acquisition strategy—combining inertial-optical mocap for temporal collaboration patterns with automatic spatial retargeting onto ShapeNet geometries—is a novel combination not seen in prior dataset construction efforts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CORE4D fills a critical gap by providing the first large-scale (11K sequences, 3K object shapes) 4D dataset specifically for multi-person collaborative rearrangement, enabling research in VR/AR, human-robot collaboration, and humanoid skill learning that was previously infeasible due to data scarcity. The benchmark results reveal that existing diffusion-based and autoregressive methods struggle to generalize human collaborative motion to novel object geometries, highlighting a concrete research direction. Key limitations include the absence of outdoor scenes (mocap constraint) and lack of visual signals in synthetic data, while the demonstrated humanoid box-lifting results (21–26.5% success) suggest a promising but early-stage path toward multi-humanoid collaboration learning.
