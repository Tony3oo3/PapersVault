## Problem

Existing full-reference IQA methods quantify feature similarity through statistical correlations but cannot explain the causal mechanisms by which distortions alter human perception, making them unable to distinguish distortions with similar feature distances yet different perceptual impacts. No prior theoretical framework accounts for why certain IQA methods succeed in some contexts and fail in others, limiting generalization across diverse distortion types and datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250930/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images (reference and distorted) at arbitrary resolution, extracts multi-scale deep features from a pretrained backbone (VGG-16, ResNet, or EfficientNet), and outputs a single scalar perceptual quality score. The approach is entirely training-free: it directly uses pretrained ImageNet weights and applies causal interventions to the extracted feature channels, requiring no dataset-specific fine-tuning. The causal analysis is backbone-agnostic, so the same intervention and transport-cost pipeline applies regardless of which pretrained network provides the features.

## Method

The paper formulates FR-IQA as an abductive counterfactual inference problem within a structural causal model (SCM) that decomposes deep feature parameters into causal components (γ) and noise (η). It applies do-operator interventions on both reference and distorted feature representations to identify which channels causally influence perceived quality, recording causally invariant channels in a confounder dictionary Γ. Perceptual quality is then quantified via a causal transport cost (an optimal-transport objective weighted by Γ) between the reference and distorted feature distributions, yielding a minimal perceptual cost score. The entire pipeline is training-free and backbone-independent: pretrained weights are used as-is, variable interventions filter out non-causal features, and the resulting causal feature distance is mapped to a quality score without any learned regression head.

## Results

The proposed training-free method (Our-VGG backbone) achieves the best or near-best PLCC/SRCC on 5 out of 6 benchmarks: LIVE (0.929/0.932), CSIQ (0.949/0.952), TID2013 (0.909/0.884), KADID-10k (0.898/0.899), and PIPAL (0.675/0.657). On TID2008, it achieves 0.905 PLCC (best) but 0.897 SRCC, slightly below DeepWSD's 0.904. The method is backbone-agnostic, with EfficientNet yielding the best KADID-10k SRCC (0.907) and ResNet performing weakest overall, confirming the causal analysis is architecture-independent.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest training-free deep-feature baselines, Our-VGG outperforms DISTS on PLCC across all six datasets (e.g., 0.929 vs. 0.924 on LIVE, 0.949 vs. 0.919 on CSIQ) and on SRCC for five of six (losing only on TID2008: 0.897 vs. 0.814). Compared to DeepWSD, Our-VGG wins on PLCC for all datasets and on SRCC for five of six, with the sole exception being TID2008 where DeepWSD edges ahead (0.904 vs. 0.897 SRCC). On KADID-10k, Our-VGG (0.898/0.899) surpasses DISTS (0.886/0.886, trained on KADID) and DeepWSD (0.887/0.888), demonstrating that the causal framework generalizes even against a method trained in-domain. The main loss is on PIPAL, where TOPIQ-FR (0.837/0.809, trained on PIPAL) substantially outperforms Our-VGG (0.675/0.657), though Our-VGG still beats DISTS (0.645/0.627) and DeepWSD (0.503/0.500) on that dataset. Among traditional metrics, Our-VGG dominates all handcrafted methods (SSIM, MS-SSIM, VIF, FSIM, GMSD, etc.) by a wide margin on every dataset, with the largest gaps on TID2008/TID2013 and PIPAL where traditional methods degrade most severely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the structural causal model (SCM) and do-calculus formalism from the causal-inference-in-deep-learning literature (e.g., interventional few-shot learning, causal image captioning) and applies it to full-reference image quality assessment, a domain where prior deep-feature methods (LPIPS, DISTS, DeepWSD) rely on statistical correlations between feature distances and quality scores without modeling the underlying causal mechanism. Rather than training an end-to-end IQA network, the authors treat pretrained backbone features as a fixed representation and use abductive counterfactual interventions to partition features into causally relevant (γ) and noise (η) components, then compute a causal transport cost between reference and distorted feature distributions. The approach is thus a conceptual departure from both handcrafted FR-IQA metrics and learned similarity metrics, positioning IQA as a counterfactual inference problem rather than a regression or distance-measurement problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a theoretical lens (SCM with exogenous confounders) that could help explain why certain deep-feature IQA methods generalize across distortion types while others do not, and the training-free, backbone-agnostic design makes it immediately deployable with any pretrained CNN. However, the causal formalism is applied somewhat informally—the do() operator is used to perturb features rather than to perform full causal discovery, and the 'abductive' validation step (checking invariance under intervention) is closer to a sensitivity analysis than a rigorous causal identification procedure. Open questions include whether the γ/η decomposition is stable across architectures and distortion families, whether the framework extends to video or no-reference settings, and whether the modest performance gains over DISTS/DeepWSD justify the added conceptual machinery.
