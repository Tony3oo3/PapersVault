## Problem

While end-to-end trained navigation agents achieve strong performance, their internal reasoning mechanisms remain opaque. This paper asks what kind of models and planning capabilities actually emerge when an agent is trained with model-free RL on realistic robot dynamics, specifically whether it learns a latent dynamical system, encodes scene structure in memory, and exhibits evidence of planning — questions that prior work on high-level semantic navigation has not addressed for fast-moving physical robots.

## Input / Output

At each 3 Hz decision step, the agent consumes RGB frames (encoded by ResNet-18), a 1D range scan from four RealSense depth sensors (encoded by a 1D-CNN), wheel-encoder odometry, AMCL pose estimates from a 1D-Lidar, a static goal in polar coordinates relative to the episode start, and the previous action embedding. The output is one of 28 discrete (linear, angular) velocity command pairs fed into a second-order dynamical model that simulates the physical robot's motion. An auxiliary linear head on the GRU hidden state predicts the current egocentric goal position, supervised from privileged simulator information during training.

## Method

The agent is a PPO-trained policy with a two-layer GRU recurrent memory, trained in Habitat augmented with a second-order dynamical model identified from real robot trajectories, producing 28 discrete velocity commands. The paper's core contribution is a suite of probing and sensitivity analyses run over 262 real-robot episodes: (1) a 'distance to belief' metric that normalizes the effect of perturbing dynamics parameters versus odometry noise to quantify input-vs-model sensitivity; (2) linear and action-conditioned probes trained to predict future pose from the hidden state, revealing short-to-medium horizon dynamics; (3) post-hoc value-function analysis showing discontinuities at strategy changes; (4) periodic memory zeroing to measure the role of episodic memory; (5) occupancy-map probing from the hidden state; and (6) trajectory comparison against a Fast Marching Square expert planner. Together these experiments demonstrate that the agent learns a Kalman-like prediction-correction loop, encodes local scene geometry in its recurrent state, and retains limited planning information, all without any explicit planning module in the architecture.

## Results

On the real-robot evaluation (Test-bldg/20, 4 repeated experiments), the proposed D28-dynamics agent achieves 92.5% SR, 61.1% SPL, and 22.4% SCT (±2.9/±5.0/±3.0 std). In simulation on the large-scale HM3D/2.5k benchmark, it reaches 97.6% SR, 82.3% SPL, and 52.5% SCT. On the scanned simulation of the test building (Test-bldg/20), it attains 100% SR, 82.1% SPL, and 64.5% SCT. The paper reports a +50% SR improvement over the original [9] setup in the real-world setting through engineering modifications (500M training steps, sin/cos angle encoding, and test-time RGB augmentation).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three agent configurations: (a) D4 with 4 discrete position commands and no dynamical model [66], (b) D28-instant with 28 velocity pairs but no dynamical model [80], and (c) D28-dynamics with 28 velocity pairs plus an identified second-order dynamical model [9]. The proposed D28-dynamics agent dominates both baselines by a wide margin in every evaluation setting. In the real-robot test (Test-bldg/20), it achieves 92.5% SR versus 0% for D4 and 10% for D28-instant — a 92.5-point and 82.5-point gap respectively. In simulation on HM3D/2.5k, it reaches 97.6% SR compared to 29.1% (D4) and 27.6% (D28-instant). The D28-instant baseline appears to overfit to the simulated teleportation behavior, as confirmed by the sensitivity analysis in Section 4, which shows it is highly sensitive to small dynamics perturbations while the D28-dynamics agent is more robust. The results confirm that integrating a realistic dynamical model into the training simulator is essential for sim-to-real transfer in fast-moving navigation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Bono et al. (CVPR 2024), which demonstrated that augmenting the Habitat simulator with a realistic second-order dynamical model of the robot substantially improves sim2real transfer for end-to-end navigation. While that prior work established *that* dynamics-aware training helps, this paper asks *what* the agent actually learned, positioning itself as an interpretability study rather than a method contribution. It draws conceptual parallels to Kalman-filter prediction-correction cycles and to the manipulation literature (which adopted dynamics-aware training earlier), while deliberately departing from the dominant EAI focus on high-level semantic reasoning to probe fine-grained motion, latent scene structure, and planning in a purely model-free RL agent (PPO + GRU).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 262-episode real-robot study provides the first large-scale empirical evidence that model-free RL with realistic dynamics implicitly encodes a usable motion model, short-to-medium-horizon plans, and local scene structure in the recurrent hidden state — capabilities that were previously assumed but never verified. The identified limitation of 'tunnel vision' (strategic path choices that a human would quickly reject) and the failure in narrow passages highlight a concrete gap between the agent's emergent planning and expert planners, suggesting that geometric foundation models as pre-trained encoders could close this gap. Open questions include whether the emergent dynamics generalize across robot platforms, whether the Kalman-like prediction-correction structure is an artifact of the specific PPO training signal, and whether the probing methodology transfers to other recurrent architectures.
