## Problem

Existing amodal instance segmentation methods require joint training of front-end detectors and mask decoders, preventing them from leveraging powerful pre-trained modal detectors. Additionally, available amodal datasets are limited in scale (typically under 50K images), suffer from annotation quality issues in synthetic generation, and contain irrelevant objects like walls and floors that contribute little to meaningful scene understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250427/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image and a single bounding box prompt that can be either a modal box (visible region) or an amodal box (full object extent), with no distinction required at inference time. It outputs a binary amodal segmentation mask covering both visible and occluded regions, plus a scalar estimated IoU score that is multiplied with the front-end detector's confidence to refine ranking during evaluation. The architecture is class-agnostic, operating purely on box prompts without category information.

## Method

SAMEO adapts EfficientSAM by fine-tuning only the mask decoder (dual cross-attention transformer layers) while freezing the lightweight ViT image encoder and prompt encoder, enabling plug-and-play integration with any front-end detector. During training, box prompts are randomly sampled from modal or amodal ground-truth boxes with equal probability, and the loss combines Dice loss, Focal loss (γ=2), and L1 IoU estimation loss (λ=0.05). To address data scarcity, the authors introduce Amodal-LVIS (300K synthetic images with paired occluded/unoccluded annotations generated via controlled object compositing) and curate a comprehensive collection of ~1M images and 2M instances from existing datasets with quality filters. A dual annotation mechanism including both occluded and unoccluded instances prevents the model from over-predicting occluded regions, a failure mode observed when training solely on occluded data.

## Results

On COCOA-cls, RTMDet+SAMEO achieves the best AP of 55.3 and AR of 74.3, surpassing the previous SOTA AISFormer (AP 40.6, AR 55.2) by 14.7 AP and 19.1 AR. On D2SA, ConvNeXt-V2+SAMEO reaches AP 80.8 and AR 87.1, improving over AISFormer (AP 66.3, AR 76.1) by 14.5 AP. On MUVA, ConvNeXt-V2+SAMEO attains AP 79.2 and AR 81.3, outperforming AISFormer (AP 69.4, AR 80.7) by 9.8 AP. In zero-shot evaluation (trained without COCOA-cls/D2SA), RTMDet+SAMEO achieves 54.4 AP on COCOA-cls and CO-DETR+SAMEO reaches 75.0 AP on D2SA, both exceeding AISFormer's supervised results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAMEO consistently outperforms AISFormer (the prior SOTA amodal instance segmentation method) across all three benchmarks: by 14.7 AP on COCOA-cls (55.3 vs 40.6 with RTMDet), 14.5 AP on D2SA (80.8 vs 66.3 with ConvNeXt-V2), and 9.8 AP on MUVA (79.2 vs 69.4 with ConvNeXt-V2). Modal detectors used standalone (RTMDet, ConvNeXt-V2, ViTDet) already achieve competitive AP on COCOA-cls (46.7–49.8) but produce only visible-region masks; attaching SAMEO as a mask decoder uniformly boosts their AP by 7–9 points while also improving AR by 4–16 points. On D2SA, ConvNeXt-V2 alone (AP 66.1) is nearly on par with AISFormer (66.3), but ConvNeXt-V2+SAMEO extends this to 80.8 AP, a 14.7-point gain over the modal baseline. In the zero-shot setting (Table 3), SAMEO paired with CO-DETR achieves 75.0 AP on D2SA, exceeding AISFormer's supervised 66.3 AP by 8.7 points, and RTMDet+SAMEO reaches 54.4 AP on COCOA-cls versus AISFormer's 40.6. The ablation in Table 5 confirms that training with random modal/amodal box prompts yields the best average performance (54.2 AP) over using only amodal (53.0) or only modal (53.7) prompts, validating the flexible prompt design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions amodal instance segmentation as a mask-decoder problem rather than an end-to-end detection-and-segmentation pipeline, departing from prior methods (AISFormer, ORCNN, ASN, BCNet) that require joint training of detector and decoder. It builds directly on EfficientSAM's lightweight ViT-based architecture, fine-tuning only the mask decoder while freezing the image and prompt encoders, and introduces Amodal-LVIS as a large-scale synthetic dataset derived from LVIS/LVVIS to address the chronic data scarcity in amodal segmentation. Conceptually, the work extends the prompt-based segmentation paradigm of SAM to the amodal setting, where bounding box prompts from either modal or amodal detectors are mapped to complete object masks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular design—plugging SAMEO into any existing detector without retraining the front-end—lowers the barrier to adopting amodal segmentation in practical pipelines (autonomous driving, robotics) and demonstrates that a single mask decoder can handle both modal and amodal prompts. The zero-shot transfer to held-out benchmarks (COCOA-cls, D2SA) suggests the combined 1M-image training collection captures sufficient occlusion diversity. Open questions include whether the synthetic occlusion generation in Amodal-LVIS fully captures the complexity of real-world multi-layer occlusions, whether the approach extends to video or 3D amodal settings, and whether point-based prompts (beyond boxes) could further improve amodal mask quality.
