## Problem

Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting methods for talking head animation either require hard-to-obtain multi-view datasets or per-identity personalized training, limiting practical deployment. No prior approach achieves both monocular-only training and generalization to unseen identities without retraining, because the absence of multi-view and personalized data leaves geometric and appearance information fundamentally incomplete.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251981/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 source head image (segmented into head and torso-background regions) plus a driving signal—either a sequence of driving images (for video-driven mode) or an audio waveform (for audio-driven mode). Expression features are extracted via 3DMM estimation from driving images or an audio-to-expression network from audio. The output is a multi-view consistent talking head video rendered at >40 FPS on an RTX 4090, composed of the Gaussian-rendered head and an inpainted torso-background, with the ability to render at arbitrary viewpoints (e.g., −30°, 0°, +30°).

## Method

MGGTalk introduces two modules that exploit monocular depth and facial symmetry to compensate for missing multi-view information. The Depth-Aware Symmetric Geometry Reconstruction (DSGR) module back-projects GeoWizard depth estimates via Bilateral Normal Integration into a coarse point cloud, refines it with a 2D UNet offset network, then mirrors the point cloud in canonical pose space and applies a voxel-based filter to remove overlapping regions, yielding complete geometry for both visible and invisible facial areas. The Symmetric Gaussian Prediction (SGP) module uses a two-stage decoder: first predicting Gaussian parameters (scale, rotation, color, opacity) for visible regions from the deformed point cloud and identity encoding, then feeding those visible parameters as guidance into a Sym-Gaussian Decoder to predict parameters for the invisible symmetric regions, reducing prediction difficulty. A parent-child densification step expands each Gaussian into a child node for finer detail. Crucially, symmetry is applied in DSGR (before expression-driven deformation) rather than in SGP, ensuring temporal stability of the mirrored geometry across frames. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end on monocular video pairs from HDTF and NeRSemble-Mono with L1, SSIM, and perceptual losses.

## Results

On the HDTF dataset (video-driven, self-reenactment), MGGTalk achieves the best PSNR (32.40 dB), FID (18.95), AED (0.102), and APD (0.129), with SSIM (0.786) second only to Portrait4D-v2 (0.790). On NeRSemble-Mono self-reenactment, it leads with PSNR 31.98, FID 51.35, AED 0.125, and APD 0.298. For audio-driven generation on HDTF, MGGTalk attains FID 18.73 (second only to Wav2Lip's 18.05, which uses GT upper-half conditioning), LSE-C 7.68, and LSE-D 6.91, outperforming all fully generative talking-head methods on lip-sync metrics. The model runs at >40 FPS on a single RTX 4090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In video-driven generation (Table 1), MGGTalk outperforms all six baselines on FID, AED, and APD across both HDTF and NeRSemble-Mono, with the largest FID gap over Portrait4D-v2 (18.95 vs. 36.57 on HDTF self-reenactment). The only metric where MGGTalk does not lead is SSIM on HDTF self-reenactment, where Portrait4D-v2 edges ahead (0.790 vs. 0.786). In audio-driven generation (Table 2), MGGTalk surpasses SadTalker, AniTalker, EchoMimic, IP-LAP, and NeRFFaceSpeech on PSNR, SSIM, FID, LSE-C, and LSE-D; however, Wav2Lip (which conditions on the GT upper face) still leads on PSNR (34.31 vs. 30.92), SSIM (0.937 vs. 0.731), and LMD (3.39 vs. 3.75). Among fully generative methods (no GT conditioning), MGGTalk achieves the best FID (18.73) and the best LSE-C/LSE-D lip-sync scores. The user study (Table 4) confirms MGGTalk's superiority in identity preservation (45% video-driven, 37.5% audio-driven) and visual quality (42.5%, 40%) over Portrait4D-v2, DaGAN, and EchoMimic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) line of work for talking head generation (GaussianAvatars, SplattingAvatar, GaussianHeadAvatar) by removing two key dependencies: multi-view training data and per-identity fine-tuning. It builds on monocular depth estimation (GeoWizard) and Bilateral Normal Integration for geometry, and introduces a two-stage symmetric Gaussian prediction strategy that leverages facial left-right symmetry to fill in occluded regions. The approach departs from prior generalizable 3DGS methods (HeadGAP, 3D Gaussian Parametric Head Model) that still required multi-view supervision during training, positioning MGGTalk as the first to achieve both monocular-only training and zero-shot identity generalization simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for multi-view capture rigs or per-person optimization, MGGTalk significantly lowers the practical barrier to deploying 3DGS-based talking head systems in real-world applications like video conferencing and dubbing. The reliance on facial symmetry as a structural prior is a double-edged sword: it enables strong generalization but may struggle with highly asymmetric features (e.g., scars, extreme hairstyles), a limitation the authors acknowledge with a brief robustness check. Open questions include how the method scales to non-frontal extreme poses, whether the depth estimation bottleneck (GeoWizard) can be further improved, and whether the symmetry assumption generalizes beyond human faces.
