## Problem

Visual models pre-trained on large-scale human activity data (e.g., Ego4D) suffer from a significant human-robot domain discrepancy when used as frozen backbones for robotic manipulation, due to morphological differences between humans and robots. Existing remedies either design manipulation-oriented proxy tasks on human data without accessing robot data, or fine-tune pre-trained models per downstream environment, sacrificing versatility. The paper asks whether a small amount of paired human-robot demonstrations can bridge this gap while preserving a single, general-purpose adapted model.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 56k paired human-robot video demonstrations from RH20T, sampling 5 frames per video, along with a natural-language task description per pair. The task description is encoded by a frozen DistilBERT and projected to match the video feature channel dimension. The output is a set of lightweight adapter module weights (1.6M parameters for the 25M-parameter R3M backbone) that, when inserted into the frozen pre-trained model, yield adapted visual feature representations for robot observations used as state inputs to downstream policy networks.

## Method

HR-Align inserts parameter-efficient adapter modules (a residual block with Conv_down → activation → Conv_up) into a frozen human-data pre-trained model, applied only to the robot video stream. A task-aware attention mechanism uses the task description as a query to aggregate spatial-temporal video features into a single vector per stream. The core training signal is a bidirectional human-robot contrastive alignment loss: for each paired human-robot video, the adapted robot feature must be more similar to its paired human feature than to the unadapted robot feature or any unpaired feature in the batch, and symmetrically the human feature must prefer its paired adapted robot feature. This loss is computed over a batch of M pairs with a temperature-scaled exponential similarity, and only the adapter parameters are updated (6.4% of R3M's parameters), keeping the backbone frozen and the adapted model reusable across all downstream tasks without per-environment customization.

## Results

On the Adroit benchmark (2 dexterous manipulation tasks), R3M-Align achieves 81.3% average success rate, a +7.3% improvement over unadapted R3M (74.0%), while D4R-Align improves by +2.0% over D4R (65.0% vs. 63.0%). On the more challenging RLBench benchmark (18 language-conditioned multi-tasks), R3M-Align achieves 59.2% average success rate (+8.9% over R3M's 50.3%), and D4R-Align reaches 59.9% (+4.6% over D4R's 55.3%). On five real-world tasks, the adapted models show 13% (D4R-Align) and 11% (R3M-Align) average success rate improvements over their unadapted counterparts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two widely-used human-data pre-trained models: D4R (pre-trained on Kinetics with MoCo) and R3M (pre-trained on Ego4D with vision-language contrastive learning). HR-Align consistently outperforms both unadapted baselines across all evaluation settings. On Adroit, R3M-Align (81.3%) surpasses R3M (74.0%) by 7.3% and D4R-Align (65.0%) surpasses D4R (63.0%) by 2.0%. On the 18-task RLBench multi-task benchmark, R3M-Align (59.2%) improves over R3M (50.3%) by 8.9%, and D4R-Align (59.9%) improves over D4R (55.3%) by 4.6%, with R3M-Align winning on 10 of 18 individual tasks. On real-world tasks, D4R-Align gains 13% and R3M-Align gains 11% over their unadapted versions. Notably, the R3M-Align gains are substantially larger than D4R-Align gains, suggesting the domain discrepancy is more severe for models pre-trained with vision-language contrastive objectives on egocentric human video.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the visual pre-training paradigm for robotic manipulation (R3M, D4R, MVP) that leverages large-scale human activity data (Ego4D, Kinetics) to learn transferable visual representations, and on parameter-efficient fine-tuning via adapter modules from NLP and video understanding. It departs from two existing strategies for addressing the human-robot domain gap: manipulation-oriented proxy tasks on human data (Huo et al.) and per-environment fine-tuning of pre-trained models (Sharma et al., Lin et al.). The key conceptual departure is using paired human-robot demonstrations as explicit semantic bridges, combining contrastive alignment with lightweight adapters to adapt models to the robot domain while preserving cross-environment versatility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The human-robot domain discrepancy is a fundamental bottleneck in the widely adopted human-data pre-training paradigm for robotics, and this paper provides a practical, parameter-efficient solution that maintains model generality across environments. The consistent 7–13% improvements across simulated and real-world tasks with two different backbones suggest the approach is broadly applicable. Open questions remain: how to quantitatively measure and guide the domain discrepancy, whether integrating paired human-robot data directly into the pre-training stage (rather than post-hoc adaptation) would yield further gains, and how the scale and diversity of paired data affect adaptation quality.
