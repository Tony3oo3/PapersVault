## Problem

Federated Continual Learning (FCL) must handle data heterogeneity across both clients (spatial) and tasks (temporal), but existing methods largely ignore how this combined spatial-temporal heterogeneity causes catastrophic forgetting at two distinct levels: parameter-forgetting in the feature extractor and output-forgetting in the feature space. The paper demonstrates empirically that input-level heterogeneity shifts both internal representations and feature positions, and that spatial forgetting interacts with temporal forgetting to compound degradation, a challenge no prior FCL method addresses from the forgetting mechanism perspective.

## Input / Output

Each client receives RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 224×224 for ImageNet-R) and produces class labels. The input is first embedded by a frozen ViT, then augmented with selected Input Enhancement tokens concatenated along the token-length dimension before re-passing through the ViT. The server additionally holds a small surrogate dataset (20 samples/class for CIFAR-100, 5 for ImageNet-R) used for knowledge distillation, and receives per-class local prototypes (768-dim vectors) from each client rather than full model weights.

## Method

FedTA freezes a shared pre-trained ViT to eliminate parameter-forgetting, then introduces two sets of lightweight learnable parameters: Input Enhancement tokens (10×768) that are selectively concatenated to image embeddings to boost downstream accuracy, and Tail Anchors (100×768 key-value pairs per class) that are mixed with frozen output features to pin their positions in the feature space. Local training is two-stage: first the IE tokens are optimized with cross-entropy plus a key-matching loss, then the Tail Anchors are optimized with cross-entropy, a contrastive loss pulling features toward fixed global prototypes, and a key-similarity term. On the server, Selective Input Knowledge Fusion distills the best IE parameters across clients using the surrogate data, while Best Global Prototype Selection iteratively picks the local prototype with the lowest average cosine similarity to all others as the class anchor, freezing it once similarity drops below a threshold to prevent drift.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (5 class-incremental tasks, 5 clients), FedTA achieves 96.1% accuracy on Task 1 and 89.4% on Task 5 for the aggregated global model on local test sets, outperforming the best baseline FedMGP (90.2% / 82.2%) by 5.9 and 7.2 points respectively. On the more challenging ImageNet-R setting (40 private classes per client, no public classes), FedTA reaches 85.0% on Task 5 versus FedMGP's 75.4%, a 9.6-point gap. For knowledge retention, FedTA maintains approximately 98% on both Temporal Knowledge Retention (KR_t) and Spatial Knowledge Retention (KR_s), while all baselines drop substantially, especially in KR_t.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedTA outperforms all 11 baselines on both CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-R across all 5 tasks. Among FCL baselines, FedMGP is the strongest competitor, achieving 90.2% on CIFAR-100 Task 1 and 77.3% on ImageNet-R Task 1; FedTA exceeds it by 5.9 and 4.2 points respectively, with the gap widening to 7.2 and 9.6 points by Task 5. GLFC, the next-best FCL method, suffers severe degradation under strong spatial heterogeneity (dropping to 64.8% on CIFAR-100 Task 5 and 57.2% on ImageNet-R Task 5). Replay-based FCL methods (TARGET, MFCL) perform extremely poorly, with MFCL reaching only 13.5% on CIFAR-100 Task 5 and 13.8% on ImageNet-R Task 5, likely due to low-quality pseudo-samples at 224×224 resolution. Among FL+CL baselines, FedViT (frozen ViT + trainable head) is the best at 67.3% on CIFAR-100 Task 5, but still trails FedTA by 22.1 points; FedL2P and FedDualP, which add trainable prompts, achieve only 25.7% and 46.3% respectively. Standard FL baselines (FedAvg, FedProx, FedNova) are the weakest, with FedNova collapsing to 7.7% on ImageNet-R Task 5, confirming that spatial-temporal heterogeneity is far more damaging than static Non-IID.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Federated Continual Learning (FCL) by explicitly modeling spatial-temporal data heterogeneity as the root cause of a dual forgetting phenomenon (parameter-forgetting in the feature extractor and output-forgetting in the feature space), a decomposition not previously formalized. Methodologically, it departs from the dominant replay/pseudo-data paradigm (TARGET, MFCL, FedLwF) and from prompt-based CL methods (L2P, DualPrompt) by freezing a pre-trained ViT entirely and introducing learnable key-value 'Tail Anchors' that are mixed with frozen output features to pin class positions in feature space. The server-side mechanisms—Selective Input Knowledge Fusion and Best Global Prototype Selection—draw on knowledge distillation and prototype-based classification but are tailored to the federated continual setting with strong Non-IID data. The authors position their work as the first to jointly address both spatial and temporal heterogeneity from the forgetting perspective, building on their own prior survey [36] that identified ST-CF as an overlooked challenge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedTA offers a computationally lightweight (253K trainable parameters vs. 11M for ResNet-18) and privacy-favorable alternative to replay-based FCL methods, which the paper shows fail catastrophically under strong spatial-temporal heterogeneity. The frozen-ViT-plus-anchor design is a practical recipe that could be adopted in edge-deployed continual learning where retraining large backbones is infeasible. However, the evaluation is limited to two datasets (CIFAR-100, ImageNet-R) with 5 clients and 5 tasks, and the authors acknowledge a residual privacy risk from uploading class-specific prototypes. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer task sequences, more clients, or non-vision modalities, and whether the surrogate-data requirement for Selective Input Knowledge Fusion can be eliminated.
