## Problem

Data heterogeneity in federated learning—where local client distributions are significantly misaligned with the global distribution—causes divergent local optimization directions and degrades global model performance, especially when label skew and domain skew coexist. Existing approaches (server-side aggregation tuning, client-side regularization, generic data augmentation) address this misalignment only indirectly, making them unstable under highly heterogeneous settings. No prior method directly simulates the ideal global distribution on each client to eliminate the local-global gap at its source.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251538/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client feeds its local images through a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 encoder to obtain p-dimensional embeddings, then computes per-class local covariance matrices and means, which are uploaded to the server. The server returns per-class global geometric shapes (top eigenvalues and eigenvectors of the approximated global covariance) and, in multi-domain settings, class prototypes (local sample means) from other domains. The client produces augmented embedding samples (targeting 2000 per class in single-domain, 500 per class per domain in multi-domain) and trains a single-layer MLP classifier on the augmented set.

## Method

The core mechanism is GGEUR (Global Geometry-Guided Embedding Uncertainty Representation), which augments local embeddings by adding random vectors constructed as linear combinations of the global eigenvectors scaled by their eigenvalues (β = Σ ε_m λ_m ξ_m, ε_m ~ N(0,1)), thereby sampling along the principal axes of the true global distribution rather than assuming isotropic Gaussian spread. The global covariance is approximated privacy-preservingly via a weighted combination of local covariance matrices and mean-shift terms (Eq. 4), requiring only per-class means and covariance matrices to be shared—never raw data. In multi-domain scenarios, the method exploits the empirical finding that CLIP embeddings of the same class across domains share similar geometric shapes, so a single cross-domain global geometric shape suffices for Step 1 (label skew), while Step 2 transfers class prototypes from other domains and applies GGEUR around them to simulate cross-domain distributions. The entire pipeline is a preprocessing step that plugs into any FL aggregation method (FedAvg, SCAFFOLD, MOON, etc.) without modifying the training loop.

## Results

On the Digits dataset (domain skew), GGEUR applied to FedAvg(CLIP+MLP) achieves 82.36% average accuracy with 13.84% standard deviation across domains, outperforming the baseline FedAvg(CLIP+MLP) (76.60%, 16.25%) by 5.76% in accuracy and reducing cross-domain variance by 2.41 points. On CIFAR-100 with extreme label skew (β=0.01), GGEUR boosts FedAvg(CLIP+MLP) from 58.71% to 75.72% (+17.01%). On the coexisting label-and-domain-skew Office-Home-LDS dataset, GGEUR improves FedAvg(CLIP+MLP) from 70.14% to 83.99% (+13.85%) and FedOPT(CLIP+MLP) from 65.59% to 84.20% (+18.61%), while reducing accuracy standard deviation from 9.16 to 5.59.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GGEUR consistently outperforms all 12 baselines across every dataset and skew setting. On Digits (domain skew), GGEUR on FedAvg(CLIP+MLP) achieves 82.36% average accuracy, beating FedMix (78.85%), FEDGEN (80.19%), and FedFA (80.76%) by 3.51%, 2.17%, and 1.60% respectively, while also reducing cross-domain STD from ~15 to 13.84. On CIFAR-100 with β=0.1 (label skew), GGEUR reaches 77.70%, surpassing FedTPG (68.63%) and FedCLIP (70.64%) by 9.07% and 7.06%. When applied to other FL architectures on Digits, GGEUR yields the largest gains for MOON (+18.06%, from 60.07% to 78.13%) and moderate gains for SCAFFOLD (+3.77%), FedDyn (+3.12%), FedOPT (+3.63%), FedProto (+5.38%), and FedNTD (+3.27%). On the most challenging Office-Home-LDS dataset (coexisting label and domain skew), GGEUR improves all six FL backbones by 7.0–18.61%, with the largest gains for FedOPT (+18.61%) and FedDyn (+18.1%), and reduces accuracy STD by 3–4 points across all methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant strategies for handling FL data heterogeneity—server-side aggregation tuning and client-side regularization/augmentation—by directly targeting the root cause: the misalignment between local and global data distributions. It repurposes the geometric shape concept (eigenvalue decomposition of covariance matrices) from the authors' prior work on long-tailed classification [37, 38] into a federated setting, combined with CLIP embeddings to obtain cross-domain consistent representations. Unlike FedFA, which assumes a Gaussian distribution for feature augmentation, GGEUR uses the full spectral structure of the global covariance to guide sample generation, and unlike FedMix/FEDGEN, it is explicitly guided by global distributional geometry rather than relying on local data diversity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GGEUR offers a composable, privacy-preserving preprocessing step that can be plugged into virtually any FL algorithm (demonstrated with seven different methods), making it broadly applicable to practitioners facing label skew, domain skew, or both. The empirical finding that geometric shapes of CLIP embeddings for the same category are consistent across domains provides a principled justification for cross-domain knowledge transfer via shared geometric descriptors. Open questions include how the method behaves when CLIP embeddings are less discriminative (e.g., fine-grained or medical imaging), whether the covariance-based approximation degrades with very large client counts or extreme class imbalance, and whether the approach extends to non-image modalities where foundation-model embeddings may not yet be as mature.
