## Problem

Existing deep learning methods for hyperspectral spatial-spectral fusion process all pixels uniformly, incurring high computational cost while risking overfitting on already-well-reconstructed regions. The paper identifies that pixels in smooth spatial-spectral structures can be reconstructed by shallow networks, while only those in complex structures require deeper processing, yet no prior method exploits this property without the overhead of a full classifier.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250659/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes a low-resolution hyperspectral image (LRHSI) and a multispectral image (MSI) as dual inputs, concatenated along the channel dimension and embedded to a common feature size. The observation model parameters (spectral response function R and spatial Gaussian blur kernel C) are assumed known and are used internally for distortion detection. The output is a high-resolution hyperspectral image (HRHSI) at the MSI spatial resolution, evaluated on 512×512 patches across CAVE, Harvard, and Gaofen5 datasets.

## Method

SRLF-Net cascades N=4 SRLF blocks, each comprising a Preliminary Fusion Module (multi-scale SS2D Mamba blocks with downsample-upsample for enhanced global perception) followed by a Selective Re-learning Module. The key mechanism, SSG-SRL, projects the preliminary fusion feature through the known observation model to generate pseudo-MSI and pseudo-LRHSI, then computes SSIM (spatial) and SAM (spectral) maps to identify distorted feature points. Only the bottom-r fraction of points (where r = λ(1−Avg(SSIM))) are routed to dedicated Spectral Transformer re-learning blocks for spatial and spectral refinement, while a final Mamba block integrates the results. This selective routing reduces FLOPs to 81.22G with 1.33M parameters while achieving the best PSNR (49.39 dB on CAVE) among compared methods.

## Results

On the CAVE dataset, SRLF-Net achieves the best PSNR of 49.39 dB, SAM of 2.18°, UIQI of 0.9752, and SSIM of 0.9954, outperforming the strongest baseline Mog-DCN by 0.80 dB in PSNR. On the Harvard dataset, it achieves 47.90 dB PSNR and 2.69° SAM, marginally surpassing Mog-DCN (47.89 dB, 2.70°). Crucially, SRLF-Net requires only 81.22 GFLOPs and 1.33M parameters, the lowest among all deep learning baselines (next lowest: MIMO-SST at 98.17 GFLOPs / 4.98M params), while DHIF-Net and Mog-DCN require 3.6–4.4 TFLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRLF-Net outperforms all nine baselines on CAVE across every metric, with the largest margin over Mog-DCN (+0.80 dB PSNR, −0.07° SAM) and MIMO-SST (+1.03 dB PSNR). On Harvard, it achieves the best PSNR (47.90 dB) and SAM (2.69°), edging out Mog-DCN by only 0.004 dB in PSNR while using 54× fewer FLOPs (81.22 G vs. 4.39 T) and 5.3× fewer parameters (1.33 M vs. 7.07 M). Among the most efficient DL baselines, SRLF-Net surpasses MIMO-SST (98.17 G FLOPs) by 1.03 dB PSNR on CAVE and 0.21 dB on Harvard, and outperforms FusionMamba (129.6 G) by 2.58 dB on CAVE. The traditional baselines Hysure and NSSR are substantially outperformed (by 6.5+ dB PSNR on CAVE), while DAEM, despite being lightweight, lags significantly behind all DL methods. The key advantage is the efficiency–quality trade-off: SRLF-Net matches or exceeds the accuracy of the most accurate baselines (Mog-DCN, DHIF-Net) at roughly 1/50th of their computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of Mamba/SSM-based image restoration (MambaIR, FusionMamba) and Transformer-based hyperspectral fusion (LRTN, DSPNet, MIMO-SST), but departs from both by introducing a selective re-learning paradigm inspired by lightweight super-resolution frameworks (ClassSR, CAMixerSR) that route computation based on local complexity. Unlike those SR frameworks that rely on explicit classifiers, SSG-SRL leverages the physical observation model (spectral response R and spatial blur C) to identify distorted feature points via SSIM and SAM maps, requiring negligible additional FLOPs. The multi-scale SS2D Preliminary Fusion Module extends the single-scale MambaIR design to achieve stronger global perception, while the Spectral Transformer re-learning blocks borrow from the MHSA-based fusion literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 45× FLOPs reduction (81.22G vs. 4.39T for Mog-DCN) while matching or exceeding SOTA quality makes this practically relevant for edge-deployable hyperspectral cameras, a setting the authors explicitly target. An open question is whether the selective re-learning mechanism generalizes beyond spatial-spectral fusion to other computational imaging tasks (e.g., snapshot spectral imaging, spectral super-resolution) where an observation model is similarly available. The method's reliance on known observation model parameters (R, C), though shown to be robust when approximated, limits applicability in scenarios where the imaging pipeline is entirely unknown or variable.
