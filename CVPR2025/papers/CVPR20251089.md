## Problem

Existing UGC video quality assessment models produce only a single overall quality score, which is insufficient for diverse downstream applications such as targeted video processing and content-aware recommendation that require fine-grained, multi-dimensional quality labels (e.g., color, noise, artifact, blur, temporal). No large-scale database with such multi-dimensional annotations existed for UGC videos, and no single model could simultaneously perform quality rating, score regression, and quality attribution across all dimensions.

## Input / Output

The model takes a UGC video (8 frames uniformly sampled and resized to 448×448 for spatial encoding, plus the full video sequence for motion encoding) and a free-form text prompt (e.g., 'What is the noise level?') as input. It produces three types of outputs in a one-for-all fashion: a discrete quality level (5 categories per dimension), a continuous MOS score (0–100), and a natural-language quality description. The training QA pairs are auto-generated by GPT-4 and manually revised to cover all six quality dimensions and three task types.

## Method

FineVQ is a large multimodal model that fuses two visual backbones—InternViT for spatial features from sparse frames and a Slow-Fast network for temporal/motion features from the full video—into a shared InternLM-8B language model via two MLP projectors. LoRA adapters (rank 16) are applied to both the image encoder and the LLM to enable parameter-efficient task-specific adaptation while keeping all base weights frozen. Instruction tuning with diverse question-answering pairs spanning all six quality dimensions and three task types (level prediction, score regression, description) allows a single set of weights to handle all assessment tasks. Training uses AdamW with cosine annealing (lr 2e-4 → 1e-6) over 10 epochs on 4 A6000 GPUs with flash-attention for memory efficiency.

## Results

On the proposed FineVD database, FineVQ achieves state-of-the-art performance across all six quality dimensions (color, noise, artifact, blur, temporal, overall) and all three metrics (SRCC, KRCC, PLCC). On the overall dimension, FineVQ reaches SRCC 0.8834, KRCC 0.7118, and PLCC 0.8891, surpassing the best DNN-based baseline DOVER (SRCC 0.8422) by 4.1% in SRCC. On the artifact dimension, FineVQ achieves the highest SRCC of 0.8852, outperforming SimpleVQA (0.8465) by 3.9%. Across six external UGC VQA benchmarks (Table 3), FineVQ achieves SOTA SRCC on all datasets, including 0.912 on LIVE-YT-Gaming and 0.828 on LSVQ_1080p (3.8% over KSVQE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FineVQ consistently outperforms all 23 baselines across every dimension and metric on FineVD. Traditional IQA methods (NIQE, QAC, HOSA) perform poorly with SRCC below 0.39, confirming their inapplicability to multi-dimensional UGC video quality. Traditional VQA methods (TLVQM, VIDEVAL, RAPIQUE) achieve moderate SRCC (0.60–0.76) but fall 10–20 points short of FineVQ. Among DNN-based VQA models, DOVER is the strongest single-dimension baseline (SRCC 0.8422 on Overall), yet FineVQ surpasses it by 4.1% SRCC while using a single unified weight set rather than per-dimension models. On the quality attribute prediction task (Table 2), most LMMs (VideoLLaVA, VideoChat2, Video-ChatGPT, LLaVA-NeXT) perform at or below random chance (50% for yes-or-no, 25% for which-type), while FineVQ achieves 73.52% on color yes-or-no and 91.93% on distortion existence, far exceeding the best LMM (MiniCPM-V at 70.47% color). On the six external benchmarks (Table 3), FineVQ achieves SOTA SRCC on all datasets, beating KSVQE by 3.8% on LSVQ_1080p and FastVQA by 4.3% on LIVE-YT-Gaming, demonstrating strong cross-dataset generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the UGC video quality assessment line of work by moving from single-score prediction to multi-dimensional fine-grained assessment (color, noise, artifact, blur, temporal, overall) with quality attribution. Architecturally, it repurposes the large multimodal model paradigm (InternViT + SlowFast + InternLM-8B) for a low-level perception task, applying LoRA and instruction tuning to adapt a general-purpose LMM to quality-specific reasoning. This departs from prior DNN-based VQA methods (DOVER, FastVQA, SimpleVQA) that train separate regressors per dimension and lack the ability to generate textual quality descriptions or identify specific distortion types.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FineVD is the first large-scale multi-dimensional UGC VQA database (6104 videos, 800K+ ratings, 6 dimensions), providing a benchmark that can drive downstream video processing and recommendation tasks beyond what a single MOS score enables. The finding that general-purpose LMMs perform at or below random on basic distortion identification (e.g., 13–28% accuracy on 'which distortion exists' vs. 25% random) challenges the assumption that LMM video understanding transfers to low-level quality perception, and motivates task-specific adaptation. Open questions include whether the fine-grained labels genuinely improve downstream recommendation/processing pipelines, and how the temporal dimension (consistently the weakest) can be further strengthened beyond SlowFast features.
