## Problem

Vision Transformers suffer from quadratic attention complexity, making them expensive for resource-constrained applications. Existing token pruning methods rely on heuristics like self-attention scores that do not explicitly model per-token task relevance, and most prior work is limited to image classification, leaving dense tasks like segmentation and detection largely unaddressed. The open question is how to design a token reduction method that is fast, maintains high performance, and generalizes across diverse vision tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images tokenized into 16×16 patches (224×224 for classification yielding 196 tokens, 512×512 for segmentation yielding 1024 tokens, 1536×1536 for detection yielding 9216 tokens) and produces task-specific outputs: class logits, per-pixel segmentation maps, or bounding boxes with instance masks. A key structural detail is that for dense tasks, pruned tokens are not permanently discarded but are reinserted at their spatial locations before the final transformer block via Last Layer Fusion, ensuring the task head receives a complete spatial feature map.

## Method

Cropr inserts lightweight cross-attention modules at intermediate ViT layers, each consisting of a scorer (a single-head cross-attention with learnable queries that assigns importance scores to tokens), a Top-K selector that retains the most relevant tokens, and an aggregator that computes attention-weighted token averages fed to a task-specific auxiliary prediction head. The auxiliary head provides end-to-end training signals that teach the scorer to rank tokens by task relevance, while a stop-gradient isolates these auxiliary losses from the backbone to prevent gradient interference. At inference, the aggregator and auxiliary head are discarded, reducing scoring to a single precomputed query vector multiplied by the key matrix in O(M) time, making throughput comparable to a random pruner. For dense tasks, Last Layer Fusion (LLF) concatenates all pruned tokens with retained tokens before the final ViT block, allowing pruned tokens to attend to deep features of retained tokens without introducing any additional parameters.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k with ViT-L (per-block pruning, R=8, LLF), Cropr achieves 85.3% top-1 accuracy with a 1.7× speedup, a drop of only 0.5 pp from the unpruned 85.8%. On the state-of-the-art EVA-02-L backbone at 448×448, Cropr reaches 89.7% top-1 accuracy (−0.2 pp) with a 2.1× speedup and ~41% fewer FLOPs; an aggressive single-module schedule yields 88.8% with a 4.1× speedup. On ADE20k semantic segmentation, Cropr scores 56.6% median mIoU (−0.1 pp vs. unpruned) at 2.0× throughput. On COCO, Cropr achieves 63.0 AP^box and 54.0 AP^mask with a 1.9× overall speedup and 54% FLOP reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Cropr outperforms all heuristic pruning baselines on ImageNet-1k: it beats Random (85.3 vs. 83.8), Variance (85.3 vs. 84.3), and Attn Top-K (85.3 vs. 84.7) by 0.6–1.5 pp at comparable throughput. Against token-merging and prior pruning methods (w/o LLF), Cropr achieves the best accuracy (85.1%) and highest throughput (1.61 im/s), surpassing ToMe (85.0%, 1.55 im/s), EViT (84.5%, 1.57 im/s), and significantly outperforming K-Medoids (84.5%, 0.31 im/s) and ATS (83.9%, 0.49 im/s), which are actually slower than the unpruned baseline. On ADE20k, Cropr (56.6% mIoU) clearly outperforms DToP's logit fusion (50.1%) and Liu et al.'s token concatenation (51.8%), while matching the unpruned baseline (56.7%) within 0.1 pp. On COCO, Cropr (63.0 AP^box, 54.0 AP^mask) beats Random (60.6/51.9), Variance (62.0/53.0), and Attn Top-K (62.6/53.6) in both detection and segmentation. The only comparison where Cropr trails is the unpruned EVA-02 baseline (89.9% vs. 89.7%), but this comes with a 2.1× speedup and 41% FLOP reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

Cropr departs from the dominant bottom-up token pruning paradigm (which uses self-attention or CLS-token scores to rank patches) by adopting a top-down strategy: auxiliary prediction heads provide task-specific training signals that teach a cross-attention scorer which tokens are discriminative. The scorer's flexible query mechanism is inspired by Perceiver IO, but is repurposed for token selection rather than representation aggregation. The method also extends token pruning beyond the classification-only setting that dominates prior work (ATS, EViT, ToMe, DynamicViT) to dense prediction tasks, introducing Last Layer Fusion to recover pruned token information for pixel-level outputs. A key design departure from methods like DynamicViT is the stop-gradient that isolates auxiliary losses from the backbone, preventing gradient interference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making token pruning applicable to segmentation and detection with minimal performance loss (0.1 mIoU on ADE20k, 1.2 AP^box on COCO) while achieving 2–4× speedups, Cropr addresses a practical bottleneck for deploying ViTs in latency-sensitive applications. The finding that the performance penalty *decreases* with model scale (−0.9 in ViT-B to −0.4 in ViT-H) is particularly relevant given the industry trend toward larger ViTs. Limitations include the static (non-adaptive) pruning schedule, the requirement for auxiliary-head training, and the fact that the method is inherently token-based, limiting applicability to architectures without explicit patch tokenization. The authors note potential extension to other modalities (language, audio) as future work.
