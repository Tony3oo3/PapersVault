## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods such as LoRA and DoRA focus on tuning linear weight matrices but lack a holistic view of the multi-head attention layer, which is the core operation governing transformer expressiveness. The softmax normalization constrains each attention map to (0,1) with unit row-sum, confining output embeddings to the convex hull of value embeddings and limiting the model's capacity to capture diverse token-wise relationships during fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252691/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates as a plug-and-play module on top of any pre-trained transformer (ViT-B/16, SDXL, VL-BART), requiring only H×H tunable coefficients per attention layer (e.g., 144 parameters per block for ViT with 12 heads). Downstream inputs range from 1,000 RGB images for few-shot classification, 4–9 concept images with text prompts for personalized generation, to image-text pairs for multi-task understanding. Outputs are task-specific: class labels, generated images, or text responses, with the attention mechanism internally producing unconstrained filter combinations that can yield negative attention scores.

## Method

Coeff-Tuning reformulates multi-head attention as a graph convolution where each head's softmax attention map serves as a graph filter, collectively forming a filter subspace F(X) ⊂ (0,1)^{N×N}. The method learns a small H×H subspace coefficient matrix α that linearly combines the pre-trained attention maps to construct a new, more expressive filter subspace, allowing attention scores to exceed the (0,1) range and enabling output embeddings to escape the convex hull of value embeddings. A residual parameterization (α + I) preserves the original filter subspace at initialization, and dropout applied directly to α during training regularizes against overfitting. The approach introduces negligible parameters (≈0.001% of the attention layer) and composes additively with existing PEFT methods like LoRA or DoRA without architectural modification.

## Results

On VTAB-1k (19 few-shot image classification tasks), Coeff-Tuning alone achieves 69.78% mean accuracy with only 0.002% additional parameters, surpassing all weight-based PEFT baselines except LoRA (72.91%). When combined with SSF, it reaches 73.64% mean (vs. SSF's 73.04%), and with SNELL-8 it reaches 74.70% mean (vs. SNELL-8's 74.17%), both with negligible parameter overhead. On fine-grained classification (FGVC), Coeff-Tuning+SNELL achieves 91.9% mean, outperforming full fine-tuning (88.5%). On multi-modal VL-Bart fine-tuning, Coeff-Tuning+DoRA achieves 77.7% average, exceeding both DoRA (77.4%) and full-model fine-tuning (77.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Adapter [20] and BitFit [79], Coeff-Tuning alone (69.78% mean on VTAB-1k) substantially outperforms both (55.82% and 62.05% respectively), demonstrating that tuning the attention filter subspace is far more effective than inserting small modules or tuning only bias terms. VPT [23] (64.85%) is also clearly surpassed by Coeff-Tuning alone. LoRA [21] (72.91%) remains the strongest single weight-based baseline, slightly exceeding Coeff-Tuning alone (69.78%), but Coeff-Tuning combined with SSF (73.64%) or SNELL-8 (74.70%) consistently beats LoRA while using fewer parameters (0.042% vs. 0.305%). DoRA [38] is the strongest baseline in the text-to-image and multi-modal settings; Coeff-Tuning+DoRA improves text alignment (0.34 vs. 0.33), concept alignment (0.75 vs. 0.73), and diversity (11.37 vs. 10.9) on SDXL, and raises the VL-Bart average from 77.4% to 77.7%, surpassing even full-model fine-tuning (77.3%) in the multi-modal task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant tensor-decomposition perspective of PEFT methods (LoRA, DoRA, Adapter, SSF, VPT) by reframing multi-head attention as a graph convolution whose attention maps form a filter subspace. It builds on the decomposed graph convolution formulation from prior work [6] and the expressiveness-limitation analyses of attention (rank collapse, attention entropy), but applies them in a new fine-tuning context: rather than adapting weight matrices, it learns a small H×H matrix of combination coefficients that linearly re-mixes the pre-trained attention maps. The method is explicitly designed as a plug-and-play complement to existing PEFT approaches rather than a replacement, and the residual parameterization plus coefficient dropout are borrowed from standard stabilization techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The convex-hull argument—that softmax-constrained attention maps confine outputs to the convex hull of value embeddings, and that unconstrained linear combinations of heads can escape this bound—provides a clean theoretical justification for why mixing attention maps helps, a perspective absent from prior PEFT work. Practically, the method adds only H² parameters per layer (~0.001% of the model), making it nearly free to adopt alongside LoRA or DoRA. However, the empirical gains over strong baselines like SNELL-8 are modest (e.g., 74.70 vs. 74.17 on VTAB-1k), and the paper does not test on large-scale NLP models or very deep architectures, leaving open whether the benefit persists at scale. A key open question is whether the filter-subspace view can be extended to tune the value projections jointly or to adapt the graph topology itself rather than just the filter coefficients.
