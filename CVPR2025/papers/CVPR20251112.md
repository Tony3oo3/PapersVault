## Problem

Existing listening head generation methods rely on a two-stage pipeline that first predicts low-dimensional 3DMM motion coefficients and then renders via deterministic models like PIRenderer, resulting in limited facial expressiveness and low-resolution (256×256) outputs with identity drift and artifacts. Moreover, the scarcity of listening head training data (only a few hours) makes it infeasible to directly train a diffusion-based renderer on speaker-to-listener correspondence, unlike talking head generation which benefits from hundreds of hours of data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single listener portrait image for identity conditioning, the speaker's audio (encoded by wav2vec 2.0), and the speaker's 3D head motion features as input, and produces a 512×512 non-verbal listening head video. The rendering module operates on 16-frame windows with 25 diffusion steps at inference, while the lightweight motion generation module processes 50-frame sequences. The intermediate explicit motion representation consists of 51-dim MediaPipe facial expression coefficients and 6-dim head pose per frame.

## Method

The core contribution is a hybrid motion modeling framework that combines a lightweight explicit motion generator (a transformer diffusion model producing 3DMM-style coefficients) with an implicit motion refinement module, enabling LDM-based rendering to be trained on small-scale listening data. A key design choice is the use of distinct control strategies for the two motion types: head pose is injected as synthesized pose guidance images (from three facial keypoints) via convolutional features added to downsample blocks, while facial expression is injected through dedicated cross-attention layers, reflecting their different rigid vs. non-rigid natures. The implicit refinement adds residual cross-attention layers that re-inject speaker audio and expression features to recover facial details lost in the explicit representation. The rendering backbone is built on Stable Diffusion 1.5 with a temporal motion module for video consistency and a parallel reference network for identity preservation. Training proceeds in two stages: first pretraining the renderer on VFHQ plus RealTalk with ground-truth motion guidance, then fine-tuning the implicit refinement with generated explicit motions.

## Results

On RealTalk, the proposed method achieves SSIM 0.65, PSNR 20.34, and FID 13.38, outperforming the best baseline (RLHG) by 14% in SSIM, 14% in PSNR, and 33% in FID. For motion quality, it reduces FID_Δfm for head pose from 0.74 (RLHG) to 0.05 (a 93% reduction) and FD for expression from 13.09 to 10.49. On ViCo OOD, the method achieves SSIM 0.67, PSNR 20.60, and FID 18.99, again leading all baselines on every visual and motion metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all five baselines on nearly every metric across both datasets. Against RLHG [46] and PCH [14] (deterministic LSTM-based pipelines), the gains are substantial: 14–15% improvement in SSIM/PSNR and 33% in FID on RealTalk, with FID_Δfm for head pose dropping from ~0.74 to 0.05. L2L [25] (VQ-VAE-based) is the weakest baseline, trailing by 12–15% in visual quality and showing the worst motion naturalness (SND_exp 7.81 vs. 1.49). On ViCo, MFR-Net [20] and CustomListener [21] (both diffusion-based, results from their papers) are the closest competitors; the proposed method still leads on all visual metrics (SSIM 0.67 vs. 0.59–0.60, FID 18.99 vs. 20.06–20.08) and on OOD motion metrics. The sole exception is FD_exp on ViCo D_test, where CustomListener (11.54) slightly edges out the proposed method (12.54), likely because CustomListener incorporates ground-truth motion text descriptions as an additional condition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant two-stage listening head generation pipeline (explicit 3DMM motion generation followed by deterministic PIRenderer rendering) by replacing both stages with diffusion-based components. It builds directly on the latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion 1.5) as the rendering backbone, adopts the temporal self-attention motion module design from AnimateDiff/Animate Anyone for video consistency, and borrows the parallel reference-net identity injection strategy from EMO. The key architectural departure is the hybrid motion modeling module—combining a lightweight diffusion transformer for explicit 3D motion with an implicit latent refinement layer—which is specifically designed to make LDM-based rendering trainable on the small-scale listening head datasets (a few hours) where direct audio-to-visual diffusion (as in EMO) is infeasible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that diffusion-based rendering can be made practical for listening head generation on limited data, pushing resolution from 256×256 to 512×512 while substantially improving both visual fidelity and motion naturalness over prior deterministic-rendering methods. The hybrid explicit-implicit motion design offers a general recipe for applying powerful generative backbones to data-scarce avatar tasks, potentially transferable to other non-verbal interaction scenarios. Open questions include extending conditions beyond speaker audio and facial expressions (e.g., text, emotion labels, motion styles), reducing the rendering inference cost (currently the computational bottleneck), and whether the hybrid formulation generalizes to longer-duration or multi-speaker listening scenarios.
