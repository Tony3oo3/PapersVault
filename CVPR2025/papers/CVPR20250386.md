## Problem

Adapting vision-language models to biomedical image classification remains challenging because biomedical images suffer from limited annotated data, unintuitive contrasts across modalities, and nuanced pathological features that make human-designed prompt templates inadequate. Existing prompt learning methods like CoOp and CoCoOp were designed for natural images and fall short in generalizability for the biomedical domain, while prior biomedical prompt learning efforts are limited to narrow task ranges and rely on general-purpose CLIP backbones rather than biomed-specific VLMs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a biomedical image (from any of 9 modalities: CT, dermatoscopy, endoscopy, fundus photography, histopathology, MRI, OCT, ultrasound, X-ray) and a set of C class names as input, producing a class label via argmax over cosine-similarity-based probabilities. The image is encoded by BiomedCLIP's ViT-B/16 vision encoder, while class names are embedded into learnable text prompts. Additionally, GPT-4 generates N=50 diverse textual descriptions per class (conditioned on the imaging modality) to serve as semantic anchors during training, which are not needed at inference time.

## Method

BiomedCoOp extends CoOp by optimizing only a learnable context vector (initialized from 'a photo of a') while keeping BiomedCLIP frozen, guided by two complementary components. The SCCM component queries GPT-4 for N diverse class-specific descriptions, encodes them with BiomedCLIP's text encoder, averages them into per-class prompt embeddings, and enforces semantic consistency by minimizing MSE between the learnable context and these averaged embeddings. The KDSP component applies a Median Absolute Deviation-based outlier test to prune low-scoring or overly specific LLM prompts, then performs knowledge distillation by minimizing KL-divergence between teacher logits (image features vs. selectively ensembled prompts) and student logits (image features vs. learnable context). The total loss combines cross-entropy, SCCM MSE, and KDSP KL-divergence terms, and the framework is validated across 11 datasets in both few-shot (K=1–16) and base-to-novel generalization settings.

## Results

On the average of 11 biomedical datasets across 9 modalities, BiomedCoOp achieves 57.03% (K=1), 63.95% (K=4), and 72.42% (K=16) classification accuracy, outperforming the strongest prompt-learning baseline ProGrad by 5.2% and 4.6% in 1- and 2-shot settings, respectively. In the base-to-novel generalization benchmark (16-shot training), BiomedCoOp reaches a harmonic mean of 75.07%, surpassing the next-best method ProGrad (67.43%) by 7.6 points. The method also shows the lowest standard deviation across all K-shot settings (e.g., ±2.80 at K=1 vs. ±6.39 for ProGrad), indicating more stable performance across sampled support sets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiomedCoOp consistently outperforms all nine baselines across every K-shot setting. Against the strongest prompt-learning competitor ProGrad, it gains +5.15% at K=1 (57.03 vs. 51.88), +4.42% at K=2, and +5.29% at K=16 (72.42 vs. 67.13). Compared to the best adapter method Tip-Adapter-F, BiomedCoOp leads by +5.86% at K=1 and +1.51% at K=16. Against the original CoOp framework it builds upon, the improvement is +6.87% at K=1 and +2.80% at K=16. In the base-to-novel generalization benchmark, BiomedCoOp achieves a harmonic mean of 75.07%, beating ProGrad (67.43%), CoOp (67.23%), CoCoOp (67.22%), and KgCoOp (64.61%) by 7.6, 7.8, 7.9, and 10.5 points respectively, with particularly large gains on challenging datasets like CTKidney (82.74 vs. 71.96 HM) and Kvasir (72.11 vs. 69.91 HM). The method also exhibits notably lower variance (±1.69–3.64) than most baselines (±2.59–8.65), suggesting more robust few-shot adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

BiomedCoOp extends the CoOp/CoCoOp line of prompt learning for CLIP-like VLMs by transplanting it into the biomedical domain with BiomedCLIP as the backbone, a combination not previously explored. It departs from prior biomedical prompt-learning efforts (ViP, XCoOp, DCPL) that either require full training data, rely on an additional foundation model (MedSAM) for domain control, or are limited to narrow task ranges like chest X-ray and dermatology. The key conceptual departure is the use of GPT-4-generated prompt ensembles to enforce semantic consistency (SCCM) and a statistics-based outlier-pruning strategy for knowledge distillation (KDSP), replacing hand-crafted prompt templates that are ill-suited to the nuanced, modality-specific language of medical imaging.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first large-scale validation of prompt learning for biomedical VLMs across 11 datasets, 9 modalities, and 10 organs, providing a much-needed benchmark for the field and demonstrating that LLM-augmented prompt optimization can substantially reduce the data and compute requirements for clinical adaptation. The consistent 4–6% gains in low-shot settings and the strong base-to-novel generalization (75.07% harmonic mean vs. ~67% for prior SOTA) suggest a practical path toward data-efficient diagnostic tools in settings where annotated data is scarce. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 3D volumetric imaging, segmentation, or detection tasks, whether smaller domain-specific LLMs can replace GPT-4 for prompt generation to reduce cost and latency, and whether the statistics-based pruning threshold generalizes without per-dataset tuning.
