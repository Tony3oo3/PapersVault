## Problem

Distilled few-step text-to-image diffusion models (e.g., LCM, SD-Turbo, SwiftBrushv2) can generate images in 1–4 steps but suffer from reduced diversity and degraded quality compared to their 50-step teacher models. Existing training-based distillation methods overlook the redundant encoder computations across time steps that have been exploited in training-free acceleration, and they typically require large image-text datasets for training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251273/cropped_page2_idx5.webp)

## Input / Output

The student model takes a text prompt (encoded via the teacher's text encoder) and a random Gaussian noise latent as input, and outputs a single 512×512 RGB image after decoding the final latent z₀. Internally, the encoder processes the noise once at time step K=4, while the decoder runs across K time steps in parallel, so the effective inference cost is close to a single UNet forward pass. Training requires only 1.4M text captions from JourneyDB (no paired images), making it image-free.

## Method

TiUE introduces a Time-independent Unified Encoder architecture where the student UNet encoder is invoked only once (at the initial time step K) and its features are shared across K parallel decoder passes at different time steps, enabling loop-free generation without iterative noisy-latent updates. Training alternates between updating the TiUE student with Variational Score Distillation (VSD) against a frozen SD2.1 teacher and an SD-LoRA generator, and updating the SD-LoRA with a standard denoising MSE loss. A KL divergence term regularizes the student's noise prediction toward a standard normal distribution, preserving the pretrained model's Gaussian output prior without requiring image data. The parallel decoder design reduces inference time to ~165 ms (comparable to other one-step methods) while the multi-step decoder retains richer semantic information, yielding better diversity and realism than prior one-step distillation approaches.

## Results

On COCO 2014 (30K prompts), TiUE achieves a FID of 13.09 with one sampling step, outperforming all one-step baselines: SD-Turbo (19.51), SwiftBrushv2 (15.98), InstaFlow (13.78), and LCM (132.09). It also attains the highest Recall (0.622) and F1 (0.628) among one-step models, indicating superior diversity, while its inference time is 164.7 ms with 4.98 GB memory. On COCO 2017 (5K prompts), TiUE records FID 23.11 and Recall 0.668, again leading all one-step competitors. Training requires only 2 A100 days and 1.4M text captions (image-free), compared to 24.1 days for SwiftBrushv2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TiUE achieves the best FID among all one-step diffusion distillation methods (13.09 on COCO 2014), beating SD-Turbo (19.51) by 6.42 points, SwiftBrushv2 (15.98) by 2.89 points, and InstaFlow (13.78) by 0.69 points, while LCM (132.09) is far behind. On diversity metrics, TiUE substantially outperforms all one-step baselines in Recall (0.622 vs. 0.458 for SD-Turbo, 0.457 for SwiftBrushv2, 0.521 for InstaFlow) and F1 (0.628 vs. 0.571, 0.577, 0.580 respectively), confirming its advantage in generating varied outputs. However, TiUE trades off Precision (0.634) and CLIP score (0.313) relative to SD-Turbo (0.758, 0.331) and SwiftBrushv2 (0.782, 0.326), suggesting slightly lower per-image fidelity and prompt alignment. Against the 50-step SD2.1 reference (FID 12.22, cfg=4.5), TiUE is within 0.87 FID points while running ~14× faster (164.7 ms vs. 2244.2 ms). In terms of training efficiency, TiUE requires only 2 A100 days and 1.4M text captions (image-free), compared to 24.1 days for SwiftBrushv2 and unknown (but image-dependent) costs for SD-Turbo and InstaFlow.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the authors' own prior work Faster Diffusion (NeurIPS 2024), which demonstrated that UNet encoder features exhibit high similarity across adjacent timesteps in training-free inference. While Faster Diffusion exploited this similarity as a caching heuristic at inference time, TiUE bakes the observation into the distilled student architecture itself, making the encoder time-independent by design. The distillation framework follows SwiftBrush's (CVPR 2024) variational score distillation with alternating student/LoRA updates, but departs from all prior training-based distillation methods (LCM, SD-Turbo, SwiftBrushv2) by replacing the conventional time-dependent encoder-decoder with a one-pass encoder plus multi-step decoder, and by substituting the image-requiring diffusion loss with a KL divergence regularizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The loop-free TiUE architecture offers a clean path to parallel decoder execution across timesteps, which could yield meaningful wall-clock speedups on hardware that benefits from parallelism, and the KL divergence trick removes the need for large image datasets during distillation. However, the practical speedup is modest (164.7 ms vs. 95–140 ms for baselines), and the method is validated only on SD2.1 with a fixed K=4, leaving open whether the time-independent encoder generalizes to DiT-based architectures or larger step counts. A key open question is whether the encoder-sharing assumption degrades as the gap between the single encoder timestep and the decoder timesteps grows, and whether the approach transfers to video or 3D generation settings where temporal consistency is more critical.
