## Problem

Conventional MIL models for whole slide image classification rely on static training and cannot incorporate new data without costly full retraining. When continual learning is applied to attention MIL, the authors discover through decoupling experiments and gradient analysis that catastrophic forgetting is concentrated in the attention network rather than the classifier layer—a fundamentally different forgetting pattern from traditional image classification, which motivates targeted interventions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250432/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a WSI divided into N tissue patches (each encoded by a fixed feature extractor into a d-dimensional vector) with only a slide-level label, operating under weak supervision. For replay, instead of storing full WSIs (tens of billions of pixels each), the pseudo-bag memory pool retains only K ≪ N patches per slide selected via a MaxMinRand strategy. The output is a single slide-level class prediction produced by an MLP classifier operating on the attention-weighted bag feature.

## Method

The paper's key insight is a gradient analysis showing that classifier weight gradients are upper-bounded (since normalized attention sums to 1), while attention score gradients scale with ‖φ‖² and are unbounded, causing the attention network to update far faster and drift more during task transitions. To counter this, Attention Knowledge Distillation (AKD) replaces the standard logit-based KL divergence with a KL divergence directly on attention network outputs (f_θ), preserving the relative ordering of patch attention scores from previous tasks. The Pseudo-Bag Memory Pool (PMP) enables replay by storing only K patches per WSI (K/2 random, K/4 highest-attention, K/4 lowest-attention) with reservoir sampling for balanced class representation. The combined training loss is L_CE + α·L_attn + β·L_logits, where the distillation terms apply only to buffered (replayed) samples, allowing the model to learn new tasks freely while anchoring attention patterns on old data.

## Results

On the Camelyon-TCGA dataset (10 WSI memory pool), the proposed method achieves 72.9% AACC with CLAM and 39.4% with TransMIL, substantially outperforming the best replay baseline DER++ (37.2% / 29.9%) and approaching the joint-training upper bound of 85.8% / 81.8%. On the Skin Cancer dataset, the method reaches 59.3% AACC (CLAM) and 64.2% (TransMIL), compared to 53.9% / 60.5% for ER and 53.4% / 53.3% for DER++, while joint training achieves 73.1% / 76.5%. Backward transfer improves dramatically, e.g., from −0.785 (ER) to −0.217 (Ours) on Camelyon-TCGA with CLAM, indicating far less forgetting of prior tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed PMP+AKD method decisively outperforms all four baselines on both datasets. On Camelyon-TCGA (10 WSIs), it achieves 72.9% AACC with CLAM versus 37.2% for DER++, 35.2% for ER, and 29.5–29.8% for LwF and MICIL — a gap of roughly 35–43 percentage points over the strongest replay baseline. On the Skin Cancer dataset, the advantage is more moderate but still clear: 59.3% AACC (CLAM) versus 53.9% (ER) and 53.4% (DER++), while LwF and MICIL barely exceed fine-tuning (33.3% and 30.0%). The method also substantially improves backward transfer (BWT), e.g., −0.217 vs. −0.760 for DER++ on Camelyon-TCGA, confirming that the attention-level distillation specifically targets the forgetting mechanism identified in the paper's analysis. Notably, the method narrows the gap to joint training from ~56 points (fine-tuning) to ~13 points, though it does not fully close it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the attention MIL framework (ABMIL, CLAM, TransMIL) into the class-incremental learning setting, building directly on the LwF/DER++ distillation paradigm but redirecting it from logit-level to attention-level knowledge transfer. The key conceptual departure from prior CL-for-MIL work (MICIL, ConSlide) is the theoretical identification that catastrophic forgetting in attention MIL is concentrated in the attention network rather than the classifier — the opposite of what is observed in standard image classification — which motivates a targeted attention distillation loss (AKD) instead of the conventional logit distillation. The Pseudo-Bag Memory Pool (PMP) further departs from standard replay methods by exploiting the sparsity of attention to distill gigapixel WSIs into compact patch subsets, making replay-based CL feasible in a domain where storing full slides is impractical.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that attention layers, not classifiers, are the primary locus of forgetting in MIL provides a principled explanation for why standard CL techniques (LwF, DER++) underperform on WSI data, and offers a targeted remedy that practitioners can adopt. PMP addresses a practical bottleneck — the infeasibility of storing full WSIs in replay buffers — that has limited the applicability of replay-based CL to histopathology. Open questions include whether the attention-layer forgetting analysis generalizes to other hierarchical or set-based architectures beyond MIL, and whether the privacy concerns of storing patch features (even distilled) are acceptable in clinical deployment. The method's reliance on a fixed feature extractor also limits its applicability to end-to-end trainable MIL variants.
