## Problem

Existing shape completion methods require inputs in canonical orientation or rely on learned pose canonicalization, causing severe performance degradation under arbitrary rotations. This limits their applicability in real-world scenarios such as robotic manipulation and LiDAR-based perception where object poses are unknown. The paper addresses the gap of achieving rotation-equivariant shape completion without any pose estimation module or orientation normalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250997/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a partial point cloud of 2,048 points in arbitrary orientation (no canonical alignment required) and produces a completed point cloud of 16,384 points in the same orientation as the input. Eight anchor points are dynamically selected from the input via FPS and curvature-based refinement, and the model is trained without any rotational augmentation, then evaluated under random ±180° rotations across all three axes. On OmniObject3D, inputs are back-projected depth maps with unknown extrinsic parameters, testing robustness to real-world pose uncertainty.

## Method

ESCAPE encodes each point as its Euclidean distance to k=8 dynamically selected anchor points (high-curvature landmarks chosen via FPS-initialized clustering), replacing absolute coordinates with a rotation-invariant distance representation throughout the network. A modified AdaPoinTr transformer processes these distance vectors in both the DGCNN feature extractor and self-attention layers, predicting distance matrices for the complete shape rather than 3D coordinates. Training uses a Distance Matrix Chamfer Distance (DMCD) loss in distance space. Final 3D coordinates are recovered via Levenberg-Marquardt optimization that minimizes the residual between predicted and actual anchor distances, yielding a unique solution (up to reflection) when k≥4 anchors are in general position. This design provides O(1) error bounds independent of network depth and guaranteed geometric completeness, in contrast to equivariant layer approaches that accumulate O(α^L) errors.

## Results

On the PCN benchmark with randomly rotated inputs (±180° on all three axes, canonicalized via ConDor), ESCAPE achieves an average CD-L1 of 10.58 (×1000), substantially outperforming the next-best method SeedFormer (14.07) and all other baselines by a wide margin (PoinTr 59.31, AdaPoinTr 63.54, AnchorFormer 66.40, Snowflake 62.26). On the real-world OmniObject3D dataset with unknown poses, ESCAPE achieves a median CD-L1 of 18.82 (×1000), again the best result versus SeedFormer (29.56), Snowflake (31.14), PoinTr (32.97), AdaPoinTr (39.57), and AnchorFormer (40.12). On KITTI cars with single-axis rotation, ESCAPE achieves 5.93 MMID and 1.81 Fidelity, outperforming PoinTr (6.15 MMID, 0.0 Fidelity) and Snowflake (16.08 MMID, 1.77 Fidelity).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ESCAPE dominates all six baselines on both the PCN rotated-input benchmark and the OmniObject3D real-world benchmark. Against SeedFormer (the strongest non-equivariant competitor), ESCAPE improves average CD-L1 from 14.07 to 10.58 on PCN (25% reduction) and from 29.56 to 18.82 on OmniObject3D (36% reduction). The remaining baselines—PoinTr, AdaPoinTr, AnchorFormer, and Snowflake—suffer catastrophic degradation under rotation on PCN (CD-L1 jumping to 49–77), while ESCAPE remains stable at 8–14 across all categories. On OmniObject3D, ESCAPE wins in 6 of 7 categories (losing only on Plane to SeedFormer: 16.00 vs 13.90), with particularly large margins on Cabin (12.09 vs 34.61) and Chair (7.20 vs 21.08). In the ablation against rotation-equivariant baselines (Table 4), ESCAPE (10.58 avg) outperforms Vector Neurons–Snowflake (18.62), PPF-Snowflake (17.46), and SCARP (124.0), confirming that distance-to-anchor encoding provides a richer equivariant representation than vector-neuron or handcrafted PPF features.

## Relation to Prior Work

ESCAPE builds directly on AdaPoinTr's transformer-based completion architecture, replacing absolute point coordinates with distances to dynamically selected anchor points, drawing conceptual inspiration from the D2 shape distribution of Osada et al. It departs from the equivariant deep learning line (Vector Neurons, SE(3)-equivariant networks, SCARP) by sidestepping layer-wise equivariant operations in favor of a single global distance encoding that provably avoids the O(α^L) error compounding those methods suffer. The anchor selection strategy (FPS + curvature-based refinement) and the Levenberg-Marquardt coordinate recovery step are novel components not present in prior shape completion pipelines, which typically assume canonical alignment or rely on learned canonicalization (e.g., ConDor).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ESCAPE addresses a practical gap that limits real-world deployment: existing shape completion methods silently assume canonical orientation and degrade catastrophically under arbitrary rotation, a common condition in robotics and LiDAR-based perception. The introduction of the OmniObject3D benchmark with unknown-pose depth-map inputs pushes the field toward evaluating on realistic, uncontrolled data rather than synthetic canonical splits. Open questions include whether the approach scales to highly complex or open-ended shapes beyond the eight ShapeNet-derived categories tested, how sensitive performance is to the number and placement of anchor points on very sparse inputs, and whether the per-point optimization step remains tractable at the resolutions needed for industrial applications.
