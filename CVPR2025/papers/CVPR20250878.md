## Problem

Existing embodied dialogue localization methods model the task as image-to-heatmap translation, making accuracy heavily dependent on spatial resolution and causing exponential computational growth. Additionally, these methods learn spurious correlations from dataset biases (e.g., overrepresented room types, co-occurring objects), leading to significant accuracy drops when generalizing to unseen environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250878/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a top-down map image (resized to 224×224, encoded via CLIP ViT) and multi-round natural language dialogues (encoded via frozen BERT) as input, and directly outputs a continuous 2D coordinate pair representing the observer's location on the map. Rather than producing a spatial heatmap, the model regresses coordinates from Gaussian noise through iterative denoising, and at inference time uses kernel density estimation over multiple sampled coordinates to select the highest-likelihood prediction.

## Method

CGD replaces heatmap prediction with a DDPM-based diffusion model that directly models the probability distribution of 2D coordinates, using a Transformer denoising network conditioned on concatenated visual and textual tokens. A unified causal inference module removes confounders from the conditioning signal: back-door adjustment (BDA) handles observable confounders (room types, spatial keywords) by marginalizing over a confounder dictionary, while front-door adjustment (FDA) handles unobservable confounders (decoration styles, sentence structures) via a VQ-VAE-based mediator that performs implicit feature clustering. The de-confounded features are then injected into the denoising process through a causality guidance mechanism analogous to classifier-free guidance, where the score estimate is updated as (1+ω)·ε_θ(y_t | do(C)) − ω·ε_θ(y_t, C), with ω controlling the strength of causal intervention. The model is trained jointly with both causal and non-causal conditions using a combined diffusion MSE loss and VQ-VAE reconstruction loss.

## Results

On the WAY dataset, CGD achieves 30.21% Acc0 and 76.34% Acc5 on valSeen, and 16.72% Acc0 and 69.56% Acc5 on valUnseen in the single-shot setting, substantially outperforming the previous SOTA DiaLoc (7.02% Acc0, 40.41% Acc5 on valUnseen). In the multi-shot setting, CGD reaches 11.76% Acc0 and 62.88% Acc5 on valUnseen, surpassing the best baseline DiaLoc-e (8.44% Acc0, 47.15% Acc5). The evaluation metric is geodesic distance accuracy at 0 m and 5 m thresholds, with the valUnseen split measuring generalization to novel environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CGD is compared against two heatmap-based baselines: LingUNet (UNet-based image-to-heatmap regression) and DiaLoc (iterative multi-round heatmap refinement). In the single-shot setting, CGD outperforms DiaLoc by 9.70 percentage points on Acc0@valUnseen (16.72 vs. 7.02) and 29.15 points on Acc5@valUnseen (69.56 vs. 40.41), while also leading on valSeen by 4.57 and 10.32 points respectively. In the multi-shot setting, CGD surpasses the strongest baseline DiaLoc-e by 3.32 points on Acc0@valUnseen (11.76 vs. 8.44) and 15.73 points on Acc5@valUnseen (62.88 vs. 47.15). Notably, CGD achieves these gains without relying on auxiliary data generation (BLIP captions or ChatGPT text expansion) that DiaLoc† and DiaLoc-e employ, indicating that the causality-guided diffusion mechanism itself drives the improvement rather than additional training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the heatmap-based paradigm of prior EDL methods (LingUNet, DiaLoc, LED-BERT), which frame localization as image-to-image translation via UNet or encoder-decoder architectures. Instead, it directly models the continuous coordinate distribution using a DDPM-style denoising process, drawing on the diffusion model lineage (DDPM, DiffusionDet) and the classifier-free guidance mechanism. The key conceptual novelty is integrating causal inference—specifically back-door adjustment for observable confounders and front-door adjustment (via VQ-VAE) for unobservable ones—into the diffusion denoising loop, analogous to how classifier-free guidance steers the score function. This unifies the deconfounding literature (deconfounded captioning, causal VQA) with generative diffusion in a way not previously attempted for spatial prediction tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the resolution-accuracy tradeoff inherent to heatmap methods, CGD offers a path toward fine-grained localization without exponential computational cost, which is practically important for real-time embodied agents. The causal guidance mechanism is a reusable architectural pattern that could transfer to other diffusion-based regression or generation tasks suffering from dataset bias. However, the paper is evaluated only on the WAY dataset (a single indoor localization benchmark), and the authors acknowledge that inference speed remains a bottleneck due to iterative denoising. Open questions include whether the causal guidance formulation generalizes to 3D localization, outdoor scenes, or multi-agent settings, and whether faster diffusion variants (e.g., score distillation, few-step samplers) can preserve the causal debiasing benefit.
