## Problem

Existing audio-driven portrait animation methods face a dual challenge: diffusion-based approaches are too slow for real-time use (e.g., Hallo requires 20.93s to generate one second of video), and GAN-based methods neglect subtle body-part and accessory movements (neck muscles, earrings, necklaces), producing stiff or physically inconsistent animations. No prior framework achieves both real-time streaming inference and realistic fine-grained motion simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250595/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a static portrait image and a continuous audio signal, which is chunked into 200ms segments and encoded by Whisper into [10×512] embeddings. The output is a streaming animated portrait video at up to 25 FPS, where each 200ms audio chunk drives 4 motion frames (25×3 latent per frame: 21 implicit keypoints, 3 rotation vectors, 1 translation) that are interpolated to 5 output frames. The full pipeline processes each 200ms chunk in ~184ms on 4×H800 GPUs, enabling true real-time operation.

## Method

Teller is a two-stage autoregressive framework. Stage 1 (FMLG) encodes LivePortrait-derived motion latents into discrete tokens via a Residual VQ model (4 frames compressed to 32 tokens), then an AR transformer (initialized from Qwen1.5-4B) predicts these tokens sequentially conditioned on Whisper audio embeddings, using a token-pair architecture with BERT-style position bias to halve inference steps. Stage 2 (ETM) applies a single-step 3D U-Net with temporal self-attention over VAE-encoded frames, trained with a region-specific mask (bounding boxes from MediaPipe landmarks) to refine physically consistent details like earring sway and neck muscle movement. This decomposition—fast discrete token prediction for global motion plus one-pass temporal refinement for fine details—avoids the multi-step denoising bottleneck of diffusion models while preserving accessory realism.

## Results

On the HDTF dataset, Teller achieves the best FVD (173.463), highest Sync-C (7.696), and lowest Sync-D (7.536) among all compared methods, with a competitive FID of 21.352. On RAVDESS, Teller again leads in FVD (429.288), Sync-C (4.496), and Sync-D (7.936). Most strikingly, Teller generates one second of 25 fps video in 0.92 s, compared to 18.89–31.10 s for the baselines, enabling real-time streaming at up to 25 FPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Teller outperforms all four baselines on FVD, Sync-C, and Sync-D on both HDTF and RAVDESS. Against the strongest baseline, Hallo, Teller achieves a lower FVD (173.463 vs. 174.191 on HDTF; 429.288 vs. 537.478 on RAVDESS) and better lip-sync scores (Sync-C 7.696 vs. 7.497; Sync-D 7.536 vs. 7.741), while being roughly 23× faster (0.92 s vs. 20.93 s per second of video). Teller's FID is marginally higher than Hallo's (21.352 vs. 20.639 on HDTF; 20.352 vs. 19.826 on RAVDESS), indicating a slight trade-off in per-frame image quality for superior temporal coherence. Against SadTalker, EchoMimic, and AniPortrait, Teller wins on every reported metric, with particularly large gaps in FVD (e.g., 173.463 vs. 290.190 for EchoMimic) and Sync-D (7.536 vs. 10.657 for AniPortrait). The speed advantage is the most dramatic: Teller is 20–34× faster than all baselines, making it the only method capable of real-time streaming.

## Relation to Prior Work

Teller builds on LivePortrait's implicit keypoint-based motion representation for extracting facial dynamics, and adopts the Qwen1.5-4B architecture as the backbone for its autoregressive transformer. It departs from the dominant diffusion-based paradigm (Hallo, EMO, VASA-1) by replacing multi-step denoising with single-step autoregressive token prediction over RVQ-quantized motion latents, and from GAN-based methods (SadTalker, MakeItTalk) by explicitly modeling accessory and body-part motion through the Efficient Temporal Module. The conceptual lineage draws from language-model-style next-token prediction applied to discrete motion tokens, a direction explored in multimodal generation (SEED-X, Chameleon) but not previously applied to real-time talking-head animation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Teller demonstrates that autoregressive token prediction over quantized motion latents can match diffusion-based quality while being ~22× faster (0.92s vs. 20.93s per second of video), making real-time streaming talking-head animation at 25 FPS practically achievable. The AR transformer architecture also positions the method as a natural component within unified multimodal language models, suggesting a path toward conversational avatars that generate motion as part of a broader generative pipeline. Limitations include reliance on LivePortrait for the underlying motion representation (capping expressiveness), the ETM's region-specific masking being somewhat heuristic, and evaluation limited to single-person, head-and-shoulders portraits. Open questions include whether this approach scales to full-body animation, multi-person scenes, or longer sequences without temporal drift.
