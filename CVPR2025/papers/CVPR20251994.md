## Problem

Instance-level image retrieval at real-world scale lacks a suitable evaluation dataset: existing benchmarks are limited to single domains (landmarks, products), suffer from ground-truth errors (false positives or negatives), or are too small to stress-test retrieval against hard negatives. No publicly available dataset simultaneously satisfies strict instance-level class definitions, multi-domain coverage, error-free labeling, and large-scale database size.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251994/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes either 1,232 query images (object on clean/uniform background) or 1,000 detailed text descriptions as input, and ranks them against a database of 99.1M distractor images from YFCC100M plus 4,715 positive images. Images are resized so the larger dimension matches 384, 512, or 724 pixels depending on the model's training resolution. A key design constraint is that all 1,000 query objects are verified to have first appeared publicly after 2014 (the YFCC100M crawl date), guaranteeing zero false negatives in the distractor set without additional annotation. Performance is measured as mAP@1k, treating any positive not in the top-1k as unretrieved.

## Method

ILIAS is constructed by manually collecting query and positive images for 1,000 objects across diverse domains (art, landmarks, products, fashion, and novel categories), then combining them with 100M YFCC100M distractors whose temporal origin (pre-2014) ensures no false negatives. For benchmarking, the paper evaluates 68+ global representation models (CNNs and ViTs trained via supervised, self-supervised, distillation, or vision-language alignment) and proposes a linear adaptation scheme: a single 512D projection layer trained on 1M images from UnED using the UJCDS method on top of frozen backbones. Re-ranking experiments combine global descriptors for initial retrieval with local-descriptor-based refinement (Chamfer Similarity, Spatial Verification, or the AMES transformer) applied to the top-1k shortlist. A mini-ILIAS variant (5M distractors) is constructed by selecting the hardest negatives via ensemble VLM text-to-image similarity scoring, making it significantly more challenging than a random subset of the same size.

## Results

On the full 100M-distractor ILIAS benchmark, the best-performing model (SigLIP2 ViT-L, 512/724 resolution) achieves 31.3% mAP@1k with linear adaptation on UnED, while the weakest (ResNet50, 224/384) scores only 1.7%. Adding AMES local-descriptor re-ranking on top of SigLIP† pushes the best result to 35.6% mAP@1k. For text-to-image retrieval, SigLIP2 ViT-L reaches 24.7% mAP@1k, surprisingly close to the image-to-image score without adaptation (15.4%). Linear adaptation on UnED yields the largest gains for VLMs (e.g., SigLIP ViT-L: 15.2→19.6), confirming that image-to-image relations are not optimized during VLM pre-training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLMs (SigLIP, SigLIP2, MetaCLIP, EVA-CLIP, OpenCLIP) consistently outperform all non-VLM baselines on ILIAS; the gap is most pronounced at scale, where SigLIP2 ViT-L (31.3% mAP@1k†) nearly doubles DINOv2 ViT-L (15.3%†) and Unicom ViT-L (14.6%†). Legacy models from ~10 years ago (ResNet50, DINO R50, ViT-B supervised) score below 3% mAP@1k, highlighting the vast progress in representation learning. Among VLMs, training data scale and resolution matter more than model size: SigLIP ViT-B at 512/724 (23.0%†) outperforms SigLIP ViT-L at 256/384 (21.8%†). Linear adaptation on UnED provides the largest relative boost for VLMs (e.g., SigLIP ViT-L: +4.4 pts) compared to self-supervised models (DINOv2 ViT-L: +0.0 pts), because VLM training does not optimize image-to-image similarity. For re-ranking, AMES with local descriptors (35.6%†) substantially outperforms global query expansion (αQE1: 33.7%†) and Chamfer Similarity (32.5%†), confirming that local features remain critical under severe background clutter. Text-to-image retrieval with SigLIP2 (19.8%) approaches the i2i score without adaptation (20.8%), suggesting detailed text queries are a viable proxy for image queries at the instance level.

## Relation to Prior Work

ILIAS extends the instance-level image retrieval benchmarking lineage (R-Oxford/Paris, GLDv2, SOP) by addressing their core limitations: single-domain focus, small database size, and ground-truth noise from automated annotation. Its key conceptual departure is the temporal-filtering trick—pairing post-2014 objects with the 2014-crawled YFCC100M to guarantee zero false negatives without any additional annotation—repurposing a dataset compilation date as a correctness guarantee. The evaluation framework draws on established retrieval techniques (global descriptors, query expansion, local re-ranking via AMES/CS/SP) and the UnED linear-adaptation protocol, positioning ILIAS as a unified testbed rather than a new method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With the best model reaching only 31.3% mAP@1k and oracle re-ranking at 56%, ILIAS is far from saturated and exposes clear failure modes (small objects, severe clutter, multi-object scenes) that current foundation models cannot handle. The finding that text-to-image retrieval nearly matches image-to-image at the instance level suggests detailed text descriptions are a viable proxy when image queries are unavailable, broadening the applicability of VLMs. However, the 100M scale makes local-descriptor re-ranking—still the most effective technique—computationally impractical, leaving an open gap between what works well and what is deployable at scale. ILIAS is well-positioned to become a standard long-term benchmark for tracking foundation-model progress in fine-grained recognition.
