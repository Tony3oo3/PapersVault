## Problem

Creating high-fidelity, animatable 3D full-body avatars from a single image remains challenging because existing training datasets contain only thousands of subjects (far below the scale needed for robust generalization), and prior reconstruction methods either require slow per-instance optimization or entangle parametric body models, resulting in fragile and non-drivable outputs. The paper addresses this by simultaneously scaling up the dataset, redesigning the model as a feed-forward transformer, and adopting a structured representation that supports instant animation and editing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250137/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 1024×1024 RGB image of a full-body human as input and produces a 3D Gaussian avatar parameterized in the SMPL-X UV space, specifically as Gaussian attribute maps encoding per-primitive position offsets, scale offsets, rotation offsets, RGB color, and opacity relative to ~200K densified SMPL-X vertices. The output is directly animatable via linear blend skinning without any post-processing, and the entire forward pass completes in under one second on a single A100 GPU.

## Method

IDOL is a feed-forward transformer that maps a single image to Gaussian attribute UV maps in three stages: a frozen Sapiens-1B encoder extracts patch-wise features from the 1024×1024 input, a UV-Alignment Transformer fuses these image tokens with learnable spatial-positional UV tokens via self-attention to produce a structured UV feature map, and a CNN decoder upsamples the tokens into the final Gaussian attribute maps. The 3D avatar is represented as 3D Gaussian primitives whose positions, scales, and rotations are modeled as offsets from SMPL-X vertices, enabling animation through LBS with skinning weights computed from a low-resolution body-part volume and barycentric interpolation for hands and face. Training is end-to-end and supervised: the model is optimized with MSE and VGG perceptual losses between differentiably rendered multi-view images and ground-truth views from the HuGe100K dataset (100K subjects × 24 views, generated via Flux text-to-image synthesis and MVChamp multi-view video generation conditioned on SMPL-X poses), which provides the scale and diversity needed for strong generalization.

## Results

On the combined HuGe100K + THuman 2.1 test set (50 cases each), IDOL achieves MSE 0.008, PSNR 21.673 dB, and LPIPS 1.138, substantially outperforming SIFU (MSE 0.042, PSNR 14.204, LPIPS 1.612) and GTA (MSE 0.041, PSNR 14.282, LPIPS 1.629) across all three metrics. The ablation without HuGe100K (MSE 0.017, PSNR 19.225, LPIPS 1.326) confirms the dataset's critical contribution, closing roughly half the gap to the full model. Reconstruction runs in under 1 second on a single A100 GPU at 1K resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDOL decisively outperforms both loop-optimization baselines SIFU and GTA on every metric, with PSNR gains of roughly 7.4 dB over SIFU and 7.4 dB over GTA. The authors note that SIFU and GTA underperform relative to their reported numbers because their pixel-alignment modules assume orthographic projection, whereas the evaluation uses perspective cameras with focal lengths ranging from 35 to 80; additionally, both were trained at 512×512 resolution, limiting texture detail in occluded regions. LGM (a large multi-view Gaussian reconstruction model) and DreamGaussian (an SDS-based generative method) are compared only qualitatively in Figure 6, where IDOL produces more consistent textures and handles complex outfits, out-of-domain cartoon inputs, and large-angle side views more robustly. DreamGaussian requires approximately two minutes per reconstruction versus IDOL's sub-second inference, and LGM, while fast, does not produce animatable avatars. No quantitative numbers are reported for LGM or DreamGaussian in the main table.

## Relation to Prior Work

IDOL sits at the intersection of three prior threads: (1) single-image clothed human reconstruction via implicit functions and SMPL priors (PIFU, ARCH, PaMIR, SIFU, GTA), which it replaces with a feed-forward transformer that avoids iterative optimization; (2) 3D Gaussian Splatting for human avatars (GaussianAvatar, Animatable Gaussians, E3Gen), from which it adopts the UV-space Gaussian attribute map parameterization relative to SMPL-X vertices; and (3) pose-controllable video generation (Champ, MimicMotion), which it re-trains as MVChamp to synthesize the 24-view training data. The key conceptual departure is treating dataset scale as the primary lever for generalization—using a text-to-image model (Flux) plus a fine-tuned video model to generate 100K diverse multi-view subjects—rather than relying on hand-crafted priors or per-subject optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single forward pass on one GPU reconstructs a photorealistic, animatable 3D human in under a second, compared to minutes for optimization-based baselines, and the UV-structured Gaussian representation supports direct texture and shape editing without re-optimization. The HuGe100K dataset (100K identities, 2.4M+ multi-view frames) is the largest generated human dataset to date and provides a reusable resource for the community. However, limitations remain: the video-generation pipeline only produces single-frame images from fixed viewpoints (no longer motion sequences), facial identity and expression are under-served by the architecture, and half-body inputs are still challenging—leaving open whether the feed-forward paradigm can extend to full 4D (time-varying) human capture without per-subject fitting.
