## Problem

Current stochastic human motion prediction methods often produce motions with physically unfeasible artifacts—limb stretching and inter-frame jitter—that go undetected by standard evaluation metrics. The authors attribute this to the lack of an explicit inductive bias on the human skeletal structure, particularly the i.i.d. noise assumption in conventional isotropic diffusion that ignores kinematic dependencies between joints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250406/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a past sequence of 0.5 s of 3D joint positions (P × J × 3 tensor) and generates N diverse future sequences of 2 s (F × J × 3). In latent space, each of the J body joints is represented by a feature vector of dimension L, preserving the joint-to-joint correspondence so that nonisotropic noise can be applied along the joint axis. The decoder is additionally conditioned on the two most recent past frames to encourage smooth past-to-future transitions.

## Method

SkeletonDiffusion introduces a nonisotropic Gaussian diffusion formulation in which the forward-process covariance is derived from the skeleton adjacency matrix (shifted and normalized to be positive-definite), with a linear scheduler γ_t that transitions the noise from isotropic at early steps to fully nonisotropic at later steps, reflecting the shift from global to fine-grained motion generation. The architecture is built entirely on Typed-Graph Convolutions with joint-level multi-head self-attention (TG-Attention) for the denoiser, and GRU-based encoder/decoder that compress motions into a J × L latent space preserving per-joint semantics. Training follows a best-of-many strategy: 50 latent samples are denoised per iteration, and the gradient is backpropagated only through the sample whose decoded motion minimizes reconstruction loss against ground truth, implicitly encouraging diversity. All nonisotropic matrices (eigendecomposition of Σ_t, posterior means and covariances) are precomputed from the fixed skeleton graph, so the formulation adds no per-sample computational overhead over isotropic diffusion.

## Results

On AMASS (cross-dataset protocol), SkeletonDiffusion achieves the best precision across all three metrics: ADE 0.480, FDE 0.545, and MAE 6.124, outperforming the closest diffusion baseline CoMusion (0.494, 0.547, 6.715). It also leads on all four proposed body realism metrics (mean stretching 3.15, jitter 0.20, RMSE stretching 4.45, RMSE jitter 0.26), with a large margin over CoMusion (4.04, 0.25, 5.63, 0.52). On the noisy FreeMan dataset, SkeletonDiffusion again achieves the best ADE (0.374) and lowest limb stretching (0.51), and on zero-shot 3DPW it maintains the best body realism (mean str 3.02, jit 0.17) while CoMusion's jitter degrades to 0.38.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four VAE baselines (TPK, DLow, GSPS, DivSamp), SkeletonDiffusion wins on every reported metric, with the largest gaps on body realism: DivSamp, which reports the highest APD (24.72), simultaneously shows the worst limb stretching (11.17) and jitter (0.82), confirming the paper's claim that inflated diversity scores can mask physically inconsistent motions. Among diffusion baselines, HumanMAC is outperformed on precision (ADE 0.480 vs. 0.511) but does not report body realism metrics. BeLFusion is beaten on ADE (0.480 vs. 0.513), MAE (6.124 vs. 7.125), and all four body realism metrics, though it edges out SkeletonDiffusion on APDE (1.977 vs. 2.067). The closest competitor is CoMusion: it leads on diversity (APD 10.848 vs. 9.456), multimodal coverage (MMADE 0.469 vs. 0.561; MMFDE 0.466 vs. 0.580), and CMD (9.636 vs. 11.417), but SkeletonDiffusion surpasses it on all three precision metrics and all four body realism metrics, with roughly 22% lower mean stretching and 60% lower RMSE jitter. On FreeMan and zero-shot 3DPW, the same pattern holds: SkeletonDiffusion achieves the best precision and body realism, while CoMusion's jitter degrades noticeably in the zero-shot setting (0.38 vs. 0.17).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends latent diffusion models for stochastic human motion prediction (following BeLFusion, CoMusion, and HumanMAC) by replacing the standard isotropic Gaussian noise with a nonisotropic formulation whose covariance is derived from the skeleton kinematic adjacency matrix. Architecturally, it departs from prior SHMP methods that apply GCNs only at intermediate stages by building the entire encoder-decoder-denoiser pipeline on Typed-Graph Convolutions, preserving joint semantics throughout. The nonisotropic diffusion itself is positioned as the first application of structured (non-i.i.d.) noise to a problem defined by a known graph, contrasting with prior nonisotropic diffusion work in image generation that relies on learned or input-dependent covariances.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a practical blind spot in the field: commonly used diversity metrics (APD) can be inflated by physically unrealistic motions with inconsistent limb lengths, meaning prior SOTA claims may overstate quality. The proposed body realism metrics (stretching and jitter) offer a more grounded evaluation, and the finding that VAE methods with the highest APD also have the worst body realism reframes how diversity should be interpreted. The nonisotropic formulation, being fixed and precomputable, is computationally free and potentially transferable to any structured generation problem, though the authors acknowledge that evaluating stochastic HMP diversity remains an open challenge and that fine-grained joints (fingers, face) are not yet addressed.
