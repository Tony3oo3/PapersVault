## Problem

Panoramic image generation is hindered by the intrinsic spherical geometry of 360° content: equirectangular projection introduces non-uniform pixel density (sparse at poles, dense at equator) and breaks left-right continuity, causing standard planar convolutions to aggregate information incorrectly. Existing text-conditioned panoramic generators either neglect these distortions or compensate with heavy multi-branch architectures and attention mechanisms, resulting in sub-optimal global structure and high computational cost.

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text prompt, encoded by a pretrained CLIP ViT/L-14 text encoder, and produces a 1024×512 equirectangular panoramic image. Generation occurs in the latent space of a panoramic VQGAN (16× downsampling), with inference via a 50-step DDIM sampler and classifier-free guidance scale of 2.5, yielding a single ERP-format image in approximately 2.7 seconds on an NVIDIA 4090 GPU.

## Method

The core contribution is SMConv, a spherical manifold convolution that uses exponential mapping to project uniform convolution kernels from the tangent space T_p(S²) onto the sphere, preserving geodesic (great-circle) distances and enabling uniform content aggregation across the S² manifold. SMConv is embedded in a hybrid SMUNet denoising architecture: circular convolutions handle shallow high-resolution layers for memory efficiency, while SME/SMD blocks with SMConv operate at deeper low-resolution layers where polar sparsity is most pronounced. A spherical loss (SMSE) weights the standard diffusion MSE by cos(latitude) to explicitly penalize errors near the poles. The full pipeline trains a panoramic VQGAN (with circular convolutions in the decoder) first, then trains the SMGD diffusion model from scratch using the VQGAN latent space, with cross-attention layers injecting CLIP text embeddings at intermediate depths.

## Results

On the Matterport3D dataset (10,000 images, 1024×512 ERP), SMGD achieves a FID of 9.85, FIDavg of 9.70, FIDequ of 7.88, and FIDpole of 21.05, along with the highest CLIP score of 0.189 among all compared methods. The model uses 1.4B parameters and achieves the fastest inference time of 2.69±0.39 seconds per image on an NVIDIA 4090 GPU, outperforming the second-best method PanFusion (FID 16.15, 38.51s) by over 10× in speed while using fewer parameters (1.4B vs. 2.3B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMGD outperforms all five baselines on every FID variant and achieves the highest CLIP score (0.189). Against the closest competitor PanFusion [53], SMGD reduces FID from 16.15 to 9.85 (39% improvement), FIDequ from 14.81 to 7.88, and FIDpole from 31.99 to 21.05, while using 39% fewer parameters (1.4B vs. 2.3B) and generating images 14× faster (2.69s vs. 38.51s). Compared to SD2+LoRA [32], SMGD improves FID from 59.32 to 9.85 and CLIP score from 0.171 to 0.189, demonstrating that simply fine-tuning a planar diffusion model with LoRA cannot capture spherical geometry. MVDiffusion [39] achieves a competitive FIDavg (38.15) but suffers from a very high FIDpole (125.48) due to its limited field of view, while Text2Light [5] and Multi-Diffusion [3] show substantially worse FID values (48.42 and 69.96 respectively) due to their neglect of spherical distortions and boundary continuity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the latent diffusion model (LDM) framework and VQGAN autoencoder, adapting them for the spherical domain of panoramic images. It departs from prior panoramic generation methods (Multi-Diffusion, Text2Light, MVDiffusion, PanFusion) that operate in equirectangular projection and struggle with spherical distortions, and from prior spherical convolutions (SphereNet, Möbius convolutions) designed for classification and detection rather than generation. The key conceptual departure is using exponential mapping to construct SMConv kernels on the S² manifold, replacing the inverse gnomonic projection used in SphereNet, and combining this with a hybrid architecture that uses circular convolution at shallow layers and SMConv at deeper layers to balance quality and memory cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× inference speedup over PanFusion (2.69s vs. 38.51s) while achieving substantially lower FID (9.85 vs. 16.15) makes high-quality panoramic generation practically viable for real-time VR/AR applications. The grouped FID evaluation in cube-map format (FID_equ and FID_pole) offers a more meaningful quality metric than random ERP cropping, which the community may adopt. However, evaluation is limited to a single indoor-scene dataset (Matterport3D), leaving open questions about generalization to outdoor panoramas, higher resolutions, and video generation. The reliance on bilinear interpolation for SMConv also constrains scalability to very high resolutions.
