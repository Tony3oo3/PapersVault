## Problem

Existing domain-generalized semantic segmentation methods rely exclusively on either Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) or Vision-Language Models (VLMs), overlooking their complementary strengths: VFMs capture fine-grained spatial details while VLMs provide domain-invariant text alignment but suffer from coarse granularity. Effectively integrating both is challenging because concatenating their patch tokens doubles the sequence length, making standard attention-based adaptation computationally prohibitive.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250443/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 RGB image and a set of class name labels as input. The image is processed in parallel by a frozen VFM (e.g., DINOv2) and a frozen VLM visual encoder (e.g., EVA02-CLIP), while the VLM text encoder produces class embeddings that serve as object queries. The output is a pixel-level semantic segmentation mask with 19 classes, generated by a Mask2Former decoder conditioned on the enhanced text queries and multi-scale fused visual features.

## Method

MFuser introduces two Mamba-based modules that keep both foundation models frozen. MVFuser is a co-adapter inserted at every encoder layer that receives concatenated VFM and VLM patch tokens, projects them to a lower dimension, and models inter-token dependencies through two parallel branches: a selective SSM (Mamba) for sequential dynamics and a 1D convolution for spatial relationships, combined via element-wise gating. MTEnhancer refines the VLM text embeddings by first applying self-attention across class queries, then passing a sequence of [text; image tokens; text copy] through a conditional Mamba block so that visual priors are absorbed into the text embeddings, followed by an MLP. Training optimizes only MVFuser, MTEnhancer, and the Mask2Former decoder using a combination of standard segmentation losses (BCE, Dice, classification) and a pixel-level vision-language alignment loss, achieving linear complexity in sequence length rather than the quadratic cost of attention-based adapters.

## Results

MFuser achieves 68.20 mIoU on the synthetic-to-real benchmark (G→{Cityscapes, BDD100K, Mapillary}) and 71.87 mIoU on the real-to-real benchmark (Cityscapes→{BDD100K, Mapillary}), both using DINOv2 as the VFM and EVA02-CLIP as the VLM. On synthetic-to-real, the best variant improves over the previous state-of-the-art (tqdm, 66.05 mIoU) by 2.15 mIoU on average. On real-to-real, MFuser with EVA02-CLIP reaches 71.87 mIoU, surpassing tqdm (70.44) by 1.43 mIoU. The framework is versatile across VLM choices, with CLIP-L and SIGLIP-L variants also outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MFuser consistently outperforms all 12 compared baselines across both synthetic-to-real and real-to-real settings. Against the most recent foundation-model-based methods, MFuser (EVA02-CLIP) improves over tqdm [37] by 2.15 mIoU on synthetic-to-real (68.20 vs. 66.05) and by 1.43 mIoU on real-to-real (71.87 vs. 70.44). Over SET [58] (65.79 mIoU synthetic-to-real) and Rein [50] (64.30 mIoU with DINOv2), the gains are 2.41 and 3.90 mIoU respectively. Against earlier foundation-model approaches like CLOUDS [2] (61.50) and VLTSeg [24] (63.50), MFuser leads by 6.70 and 4.70 mIoU. Conventional DGSS methods (SAN-SAW, WildNet, SHADE, TLDR, FAMix, IBAFormer) trail by 13–26 mIoU, highlighting the advantage of leveraging pre-trained foundation models. Notably, MFuser's advantage is most pronounced on the BDD100K target (G→B: 63.13 vs. 61.64 for SET), suggesting the VFM+VLM fusion particularly benefits from the domain gap between synthetic and real driving scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-queried Mask2Former pipeline (tqdm, ECCV 2025) for domain-generalized semantic segmentation by introducing a Mamba-based co-adapter (MVFuser) that jointly fine-tunes a VFM (e.g., DINOv2) and a VLM (e.g., EVA02-CLIP) while keeping both frozen. Prior DGSS methods have relied exclusively on either VFMs (Rein, SET) or VLMs (tqdm, VLTseg), treating them as interchangeable backbones; MFuser is the first to explicitly exploit their complementary strengths—fine-grained spatial detail from VFMs and domain-invariant text alignment from VLMs—through a shared fusion module. The use of selective state-space models (Mamba) for the adapter, rather than self-attention, departs from the dominant transformer-based PEFT paradigm by achieving linear complexity over the doubled patch sequence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, general recipe for combining arbitrary VFM+VLM pairs with minimal trainable parameters (1.67M per adapter) and single-GPU training, making foundation-model fusion accessible beyond large compute budgets. The ablation showing that naive feature concatenation underperforms either model alone highlights that effective cross-model interaction is non-trivial and requires structured sequence modeling. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other dense prediction tasks (e.g., depth, 3D segmentation), whether more than two foundation models can be fused, and whether the Mamba-based adapter generalizes to non-driving domains where the VFM/VLM complementarity may differ.
