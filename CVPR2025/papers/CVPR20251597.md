## Problem

Electronic chip-based denoising methods (traditional filters and DNNs) impose substantial latency and computational burden, while existing all-optical diffractive networks (D²NN) lack nonlinear modeling capability and ignore noise filtering during training, yielding denoising performance far inferior to electronic approaches. Additionally, no accessible benchmark dataset exists for mode image denoising in fiber communication systems, hindering progress in this domain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251597/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy image (1000×1000 pixels) as input, which is amplitude-modulated onto a 1550 nm coherent optical carrier via a digital micromirror device (DMD). Before entering the diffractive layers, the signal passes through a 4f optical system performing Fourier transform, bandpass filtering (500 GHz bandwidth), and inverse Fourier transform for preliminary noise suppression. The denoised image is produced as an intensity pattern captured by an infrared-enhanced CCD sensor, with processing completed in approximately 4.4 μs per image.

## Method

N3DNet extends the D²NN architecture with two key modifications: an image encoding and pre-denoising module (optical Fourier transform followed by bandpass filtering) that performs preliminary noise suppression before the diffractive layers, and a novel nonlinear activation function called the phase exponential linear (PEL) function applied after each of the five 1000×1000-cell diffractive phase planes, implemented physically via a Si₃N₄/Er-doped TiO₂ metasurface. The network is trained using a new reinforcement learning algorithm, RA-DQN, which adapts DQN by adding a regularization penalty on the Q-value disparity between consecutive time steps, combined with a composite loss (Charbonnier + Fourier + FSIM) as the reward signal. The trained phase parameters are fabricated via 3D printing (VeroBlackPlus material, 40 μm cell pitch, 5 mm inter-layer spacing), enabling all-optical inference at the speed of light with no digital computation required.

## Results

On the MIDD dataset (σ=20), N3DNet achieves 30.15 dB PSNR, 0.939 SSIM, and 0.130 LPIPS, outperforming the best electronic baselines (RIDNet at 29.10 dB PSNR) by 1.05 dB. Across all five synthetic/real datasets (CSet9, CBSD68, Set12, BSD68, MIDD), N3DNet yields an average improvement of 0.43 dB PSNR, 0.032 SSIM, and 0.066 LPIPS over the strongest electronic baseline. In real physical experiments on MIDD, N3DNet achieves an average 9.15 dB PSNR, 0.248 SSIM, and 0.202 LPIPS gain over the best optical computing method (ADI), while processing each 1000×1000 image in 4.4 μs—approximately 3,800× faster than the 16.7 ms latency of electronic chip-based methods on a Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 SoC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against electronic chip-based methods, N3DNet consistently outperforms BM3D by 1.2–2.5 dB PSNR across all datasets, and surpasses DnCNN, RIDNet, and MT by 0.2–1.1 dB on most settings (e.g., 28.12 vs 27.25 dB on MIDD σ=40; 30.01 vs 29.29 dB on CSet9 σ=50). The closest competition is on CSet9 σ=25, where N3DNet (31.68 dB) is marginally below RIDNet (31.71 dB) and MT (31.70 dB), but still leads in SSIM (0.967 vs 0.957) and LPIPS (0.101 vs 0.155/0.165). In real physical experiments on MIDD, N3DNet dramatically outperforms the optical baselines: at l=12 km it reaches 29.87 dB PSNR versus 22.75 dB for ADI and 15.81 dB for MPLC, and at l=50 km it achieves 26.47 dB versus 15.17 dB (ADI) and 11.25 dB (MPLC). Photonic lanterns (PL) are compared only for time/energy consumption, where N3DNet matches their microsecond-scale latency while delivering far superior denoising quality. On real-world noisy datasets (Nam, SIDD), N3DNet achieves 39.31 and 38.97 dB PSNR, exceeding RIDNet (39.09, 38.71) and MT (39.11, 38.90) by 0.24 and 0.14 dB respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the D²NN architecture (Lin et al., Science 2018) by introducing two key modifications: a nonlinear phase exponential linear (PEL) activation after each diffractive layer and an image encoding/pre-denoising front-end (Fourier transform + bandpass filtering). It directly addresses the limitations of the ADI denoiser (Işil et al., 2024), which applied the linear D²NN to image denoising and underperformed electronic methods due to the absence of nonlinear modeling and suboptimal training. The training paradigm shifts from standard gradient descent to a reinforcement learning approach (RA-DQN), and the physical realization uses 3D-printed phase planes rather than the metasurface-based fabrication in prior diffractive networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a purely all-optical system can match or exceed the denoising quality of state-of-the-art electronic DNNs (MT, RIDNet) while operating at 4.4 μs per image represents a concrete demonstration that optical inference is no longer merely a speed trade-off for accuracy. The MIDD dataset fills a genuine gap in fiber-communication mode imaging, and the 3D-printed physical system provides a reproducible hardware platform. Open questions include whether the PEL nonlinearity (fitted from metasurface data rather than arising from a fundamental optical effect) generalizes to other tasks, whether the approach scales to video or higher-resolution images, and how sensitive performance is to manufacturing tolerances beyond the tested ±3 mm / ±10 nm ranges.
