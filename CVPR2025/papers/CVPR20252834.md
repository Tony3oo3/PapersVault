## Problem

3D-LLMs lack large-scale training data with dense grounding between language and 3D scenes, limiting their ability to reliably identify and reference objects in 3D environments. Existing 3D-text datasets are either small (≤1K scenes), sparsely grounded (linking entire sentences to single objects rather than individual noun phrases), or restricted to a single task type. Additionally, no standardized benchmark exists to systematically measure and compare object hallucination in 3D-LLMs.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a text-based scene graph listing each object's category, centroid (x,y,z), and extent (width, height, depth) alongside a natural-language instruction. During training, ground-truth centroids and extents are provided; at inference, bounding boxes from Mask3D (top-40 proposals due to LLM context length) substitute for ground truth. The output is free-form text in which every mentioned noun phrase is tagged with a grounding token linking it to a specific 3D object ID, supporting three task formats: object reference, scene description, and QA. Notably, the model is deliberately text-only with no visual embeddings passed to the LLM, isolating the effect of the dataset itself.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-part system: (1) the 3D-GRAND dataset, built via a pipeline that converts 40K synthetic 3D scenes (3D-FRONT, Structured3D) into per-object 2D images, extracts open-vocabulary attributes with GPT-4V, assembles a JSON scene graph, and generates 6.2M densely-grounded instructions with GPT-4, followed by a hallucination filter that removes annotations referencing nonexistent object IDs; and (2) a LoRA-finetuned Llama-2 trained in BF16 on 12 A40 GPUs for 10K steps. The key design choice is noun-level one-to-one grounding—every noun phrase in every sentence is explicitly matched to exactly one 3D object—contrasted with SceneVerse's paragraph-level set-to-set grounding. Ablations show that 'ground first' (emitting grounding tokens before the final answer, analogous to chain-of-thought) outperforms 'ground later,' and that removing grounding tokens increases hallucination by 1.4–2.7% in precision on 3D-POPE. The model achieves zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, outperforming prior 3D-LLMs on ScanRefer despite never seeing ScanNet during training.

## Results

On ScanRefer (zero-shot, never trained on ScanNet), 3D-GRAND achieves Acc@0.25IoU = 38.0 and Acc@0.5IoU = 27.4, surpassing the previous best generative 3D-LLM (3D-LLM, 30.3) by 7.7 points and outperforming non-generative zero-shot models 3D-VisTA (33.2) and SceneVerse (35.2). On the newly introduced 3D-POPE hallucination benchmark, 3D-GRAND achieves 93.34% precision and 89.12% accuracy under Random sampling, 73.05% precision under Popular sampling, and 69.86% precision under Adversarial sampling, substantially outperforming 3D-LLM (~50% precision), 3D-VisTA (~48–50%), and LEO (~48–52%). The model is trained exclusively on synthetic 3D scenes (3D-FRONT, Structured3D) and evaluated on real ScanNet scans, demonstrating sim-to-real transfer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ScanRefer, 3D-GRAND (38.0 Acc@0.25) outperforms 3D-LLM (30.3) by 7.7 points, 3D-VisTA zero-shot (33.2) by 4.8 points, SceneVerse zero-shot (35.2) by 2.8 points, and LLM-Grounder (17.1) by 20.9 points — all while being zero-shot on ScanNet (trained only on synthetic scenes). On 3D-POPE, 3D-GRAND dominates all baselines: under Random sampling it reaches 93.34% precision vs. ~50% for 3D-LLM, 3D-VisTA, and LEO; under the harder Popular and Adversarial splits it still leads with 73.05% and 69.86% precision respectively, while baselines remain near the 50% random baseline. 3D-LLM exhibits a severe yes-bias (99.88% recall, ~50% precision), LEO shows moderate yes-bias (77–78% recall, ~48% precision), and 3D-VisTA performs near random on all splits. The paper notes that non-LLM specialized models (M3DRef-CLIP, ViL3DRel) still outperform all 3D-LLMs on ScanRefer, but the gap between generative and non-generative 3D-LLMs is closing with large-scale densely-grounded data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 2D POPE hallucination evaluation protocol to 3D scenes (3D-POPE) and builds on the 3D-LLM line of work (3D-LLM, LEO, 3D-VisTA, Chat-3Dv2) by addressing their shared limitation: scarcity of large-scale, densely-grounded 3D-text training data. It departs most directly from SceneVerse, the closest prior million-scale 3D grounding dataset, by providing noun-level one-to-one grounding for every object mention rather than sparse paragraph-level or session-level associations. The annotation pipeline repurposes GPT-4 as a densely-grounding annotator over synthetic scene graphs, building on recent evidence that LLMs can approach human-level annotation quality while achieving a 178× cost reduction over professional human annotators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

3D-GRAND (40K scenes, 6.2M grounded pairs) and 3D-POPE fill a critical gap: the 3D-LLM community previously lacked both a large-scale training resource with fine-grained grounding and a standardized hallucination benchmark. The sim-to-real transfer signal—competitive zero-shot ScanRefer performance from a model trained exclusively on synthetic 3D-FRONT/Structured3D scenes—suggests a low-cost, scalable path for future 3D-LLM training that does not depend on expensive real-world scans. Key limitations include the model's primarily text-based input (limited visual information beyond bounding boxes), modest absolute grounding accuracy compared to specialized non-LLM methods, and the fact that hallucination on adversarial and popular splits remains substantial, leaving open whether further scaling or richer visual grounding will close the gap.
