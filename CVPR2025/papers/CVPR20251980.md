## Problem

ViT-based diffusion models like DiT suffer from quadratic attention complexity, making them resource-intensive for long-sequence tasks such as high-resolution image and video generation. While sub-quadratic alternatives (Mamba, RWKV, GLA) exist for 1D sequence modeling, adapting them to 2D visual generation introduces two key challenges: unidirectional scanning limits global context, and the absence of local spatial awareness degrades generation quality. Prior multi-path scanning solutions increase time cost and cause numerical instability.

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label and a noisy latent representation (produced by an off-the-shelf VAE encoder, e.g., 32×32×4 for a 256×256 image) as input, along with a diffusion timestep. The latent is patchified into a 1D token sequence (patch size 2 for plain DiG, patch size 1 for U-DiG) and processed through stacked DiG blocks. The output is a predicted noise vector and covariance vector of the same spatial shape as the input latent, from which images are decoded via the VAE decoder. Experiments are conducted at 256×256 and 512×512 resolutions on ImageNet-1K.

## Method

DiG replaces DiT's self-attention with Gated Linear Attention (GLA), achieving O(T·M·D + T·D²) complexity instead of O(T²·D). The key architectural innovation is the Spatial Reorient & Enhancement Module (SREM), which addresses both global context and local awareness: it alternates transpose and flip operations every two blocks to enable four-directional scanning with minimal matrix operations (2 extra ops vs. 3 for bidirectional), and applies a 3×3 depth-wise convolution with identity initialization to inject local spatial information. Each DiG block follows the pattern: adaLN-conditioned GLA → adaLN-conditioned FFN → SREM. Two variants are provided—plain DiG and a U-shape DiG (U-DiG) with hierarchical down/up-sampling and skip connections. Training follows DiT's recipe, using the simple MSE loss for noise prediction and the full KL loss for covariance prediction, with EMA weights for generation.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, DiG-XL/2 with classifier-free guidance (1.2M iterations) achieves FID 2.07, sFID 4.53, IS 278.95, Precision 0.82, and Recall 0.60, outperforming DiT-XL/2-G (FID 2.27) with the same guidance setup. At 400K iterations with identical hyperparameters, DiG consistently beats DiT across all four model scales (e.g., DiG-S/2: FID 62.06 vs. DiT-S/2: 68.40; DiG-XL/2: FID 18.53 vs. DiT-XL/2: 19.47). In terms of efficiency, DiG-S/2 is 2.5× faster and saves 75.7% GPU memory compared to DiT-S/2 at 1792×1792 resolution, while DiG-XL/2 is 4.2× faster than the Mamba-based DiS at 1024 resolution and 1.8× faster than DiT with FlashAttention-2 at 2048 resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DiT [39], DiG wins on FID at every model scale under identical 400K-iteration training (e.g., 62.06 vs. 68.40 at S/2; 18.53 vs. 19.47 at XL/2), and with classifier-free guidance at 1.2M iterations DiG-XL/2-G achieves FID 2.07 vs. DiT-XL/2-G's 2.27 while also improving Recall (0.60 vs. 0.57). Against the Mamba-based DiS [16], DiG-XL/2-G edges out DiS-H/2 on FID (2.07 vs. 2.10) and IS (278.95 vs. 271.32), and is 4.2× faster in inference at 1024 resolution. Against DiMSUM [42], DiG-XL/2-G achieves a lower FID (2.07 vs. 2.11) though DiMSUM reports a much lower sFID (0.59). Against classical U-Net-based baselines (ADM [13], CDM [22], LDM [49]), DiG-XL/2-G substantially outperforms all of them on FID (2.07 vs. 3.60–10.94). In efficiency comparisons, DiG dominates: it is 2.5× faster with 75.7% less GPU memory than DiT at 1792×1792, and 1.8× faster than DiT with FlashAttention-2 at 2048×2048, making it the most efficient sub-quadratic diffusion backbone evaluated.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiG adapts Gated Linear Attention (GLA), a sub-quadratic sequence model from NLP, into a 2D diffusion backbone, following the architectural conventions and training recipe established by DiT. It departs from prior sub-quadratic diffusion backbones (DiS/Mamba, ZigMa, Diffusion-RWKV) by replacing state-space models with GLA and introducing the SREM module, which achieves 4-directional block-wise scanning with only two extra matrix operations—avoiding the numerical instability and time overhead of multi-path scanning within a single block. The U-shape variant further borrows hierarchical structure from U-Net, positioning DiG as a bridge between the flat ViT-based DiT and the hierarchical U-Net paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The quadratic complexity of self-attention is a well-known bottleneck for high-resolution image and video generation; DiG demonstrates that a linear-attention backbone can match DiT quality at 256×256 while delivering 2.5× speedup and 75.7% memory savings at 1792×1792, making long-sequence diffusion generation substantially more accessible. The U-shape variant's 16+ FID gain at 512 resolution over the plain variant suggests hierarchical structure remains important for high-fidelity generation, an open question for future linear-attention diffusion designs. The authors note that scaling DiG to a Sora-like video foundation model remains unexplored, and the modest quality gap at 256×256 (e.g., 18.53 vs 19.47 FID for XL/2 at 400K steps) leaves room for whether the efficiency advantage persists at much larger training budgets.
