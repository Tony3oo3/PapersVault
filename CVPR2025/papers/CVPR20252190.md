## Problem

Few-shot weakly-supervised learning (FSWL) for whole slide image classification suffers from two critical gaps: pathology foundation models are largely underutilized (reduced to basic feature extractors), and existing language-guided aggregation mechanisms attempt to align text prompts with all thousands of WSI patches simultaneously, diluting the model's focus on the small subset of diagnostically relevant regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252190/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-resolution WSI (20× or 40× magnification) cropped into 512×512-pixel patches via CLAM, encoded by the CONCH foundation model into 512-dimensional features, combined with an LLM-generated pathology knowledge text prompt (also encoded by CONCH) augmented with learnable prompt tokens. It outputs a slide-level class probability vector over S classes (binary for TCGA-NSCLC and CAMELYON, 5-class for UBC-OCEAN), trained with only K labeled slides per class (K=4, 8, or 16) under the FSWL protocol.

## Method

FOCUS implements a three-stage progressive visual token compression strategy. First, a global redundancy removal module uses sliding-window pairwise cosine similarity on CONCH features with a dynamic mean+std threshold to coarsely filter redundant patches. Second, a knowledge-enhanced adaptive compression module computes cross-modal attention scores between concatenated text prompts (learnable + LLM-generated pathology descriptions) and remaining visual tokens, retaining only the top-k most semantically relevant tokens (k = min(M_max, γN')). Third, a sequential compression module applies pair-wise cosine similarity thresholding with progressively increasing thresholds (θ_base + iΔθ) to eliminate local redundancies while preserving spatial coherence. The final compressed sequence is aggregated via multi-head cross-modal attention (text as query, visual tokens as key/value) followed by a linear classification head, trained end-to-end with cross-entropy loss.

## Results

FOCUS achieves the best performance across all three pathology datasets (TCGA-NSCLC, CAMELYON, UBC-OCEAN) under 4-shot, 8-shot, and 16-shot settings. In the most challenging 4-shot setting, FOCUS attains 81.9% Balanced ACC and 91.5% AUC on TCGA-NSCLC, 70.1% Balanced ACC on CAMELYON, and 70.4% Balanced ACC / 91.1% AUC on UBC-OCEAN. In the 16-shot setting, FOCUS reaches AUC scores of 97.2% (TCGA-NSCLC), 94.3% (CAMELYON), and 96.7% (UBC-OCEAN), surpassing the previous best (DS-MIL/TOP-MIL at 95.6% on UBC-OCEAN) by 1.1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FOCUS outperforms all six compared baselines (TransMIL, DS-MIL, DTFD-MIL, WiKG-MIL, TOP-MIL, ViLa-MIL) and three linear-probing baselines (Max/Mean/Attentive Pooling) across all three datasets and shot settings. The closest competitor is ViLa-MIL, which FOCUS surpasses by 1.2% Balanced ACC on TCGA-NSCLC 4-shot (81.9% vs. 80.7%) and by 4.3% on CAMELYON 4-shot (70.1% vs. 65.8%). On UBC-OCEAN 16-shot, FOCUS achieves 96.7% AUC, beating the tied second-best DS-MIL and TOP-MIL (both at 95.6%) by 1.1%. The AUC gap on TCGA-NSCLC 4-shot is particularly notable: FOCUS reaches 91.5% versus ViLa-MIL's 88.5%, a 3% margin. FOCUS also operates on standard single-resolution WSIs without requiring paired multi-resolution inputs (as needed by ViLa-MIL and MSCPT) or additional reference samples (as needed by PEMP), making it more clinically practical.

## Relation to Prior Work

FOCUS extends the few-shot weakly-supervised learning (FSWL) paradigm for WSI classification introduced by Qu et al. (TOP-MIL, NeurIPS 2024) and the learnable prompt augmentation technique from Zhou et al. (IJCV 2022). It departs from prior FSWL methods (ViLa-MIL, PEMP, MSCPT) in two key ways: rather than using pathology foundation models solely as frozen feature extractors, FOCUS exploits their inter-patch similarity structure for adaptive token compression; and rather than aligning language prompts with all patches indiscriminately, it introduces a three-stage progressive compression (global redundancy removal → language-guided prioritization → sequential neighbor-aware filtering) that concentrates attention on diagnostically relevant regions before cross-modal aggregation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical bottleneck in clinical deployment where expert-annotated WSIs are scarce, and the three-stage compression strategy offers a reusable recipe for reducing the thousands-of-patches problem in WSI analysis without requiring multi-scale inputs or additional reference slides. Limitations include reliance on LLM-generated pathology descriptions (quality-sensitive), single-resolution operation, and evaluation on only three datasets with a limited number of classes. Open questions include whether the compression thresholds and ratios transfer across unseen cancer types, whether the approach benefits from multi-scale inputs, and whether the LLM prompt bottleneck can be replaced by learned representations.
