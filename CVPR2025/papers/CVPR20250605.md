## Problem

Existing zero-shot referring image segmentation methods extract mask features by either masking out or cropping to the mask region, neglecting the surrounding context that is essential for disambiguating similar objects. Additionally, spatial relationships embedded in referring expressions (e.g., 'left of', 'bottom of') are not explicitly modeled, leading to incorrect localization and incomplete segmentation of the target region.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250605/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and a free-form natural language referring expression as input. SAM (ViT-H) generates a set of binary mask proposals from the image, and the method selects the single best mask as the output segmentation. The entire pipeline is training-free, relying solely on pre-trained SAM and CLIP (ViT-B/16) with no task-specific fine-tuning or additional datasets.

## Method

The core contribution is a hybrid global-local feature extraction performed inside CLIP's transformer encoder. A local branch processes the binary-masked image, while a global branch processes the original image with Gaussian blur applied to non-mask regions and an attention mask in the last 9 layers that nullifies CLS-token attention to out-of-mask tokens. Global features are progressively fused into the local branch starting at layer 9 via a token-masked additive operation (Eq. 1), allowing the attention mechanism to interact with complementary context without diluting local focus. On top of this, a spatial guidance augmentation combines three cues: (1) spatial relationship guidance that parses the text into objects and relations and scores masks by joint predicate satisfaction, (2) spatial coherence guidance from self-attention-based localization, and (3) spatial position guidance from directional emphasis matrices. The final mask score is a weighted sum of the semantic alignment score and the spatial guidance score, with no additional training required.

## Results

On RefCOCO val, the proposed method achieves 41.81 oIoU and 49.48 mIoU, outperforming the strongest zero-shot baseline without additional training (Ref-Diff: 35.16 oIoU, 37.21 mIoU) by 6.65 and 12.27 points respectively. On RefCOCOg val, it reaches 42.47 oIoU and 51.25 mIoU, surpassing TAS (35.84 oIoU, 46.62 mIoU) by 6.63 and 4.63 points. On RefCOCO+ val, the method scores 35.74 oIoU and 43.40 mIoU, with mIoU exceeding TAS (43.63) by a narrow margin while oIoU trails TAS (33.21) by 2.53 points. On PhraseCut, the method achieves 38.39 oIoU and 36.98 mIoU (avg. 37.69), the highest average among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest training-free baselines, the proposed method dominates: it outperforms Ref-Diff [22] by 6.65 oIoU and 12.27 mIoU on RefCOCO val, and by 3.85 oIoU and 7.23 mIoU on RefCOCOg val, while using only SAM+CLIP (no Stable Diffusion). It also surpasses TAS [32] by 12.28 oIoU and 9.64 mIoU on RefCOCO val, despite TAS relying on the additional BLIP2 model. Compared to Global-Local [41] with the same SAM+CLIP backbone (ViT-B), the improvement is dramatic: 20.1 oIoU and 16.36 mIoU on RefCOCO val. Against methods requiring additional training, the method achieves comparable or higher mIoU on all three datasets (e.g., 49.48 vs. 41.05 mIoU vs. Pseudo-RIS [42] on RefCOCO val), though VLM-VG [33] with extra COCO training data still edges ahead on oIoU for RefCOCO val (45.40 vs. 41.81). On RefCOCO+ testB, the method's oIoU (30.9) is 0.5 points below TAS (31.4), the only margin where a training-free baseline is slightly ahead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the zero-shot RIS pipeline built on SAM (for mask proposals) and CLIP (for visual-textual alignment), departing most directly from Global-Local [41], which also combines local and global features but via a simple weighted sum of separately extracted features. The key architectural departure is the progressive layer-wise G2L fusion, where global-branch features are injected into the local branch at intermediate CLIP transformer layers, allowing the attention mechanism to interact with both signals rather than post-hoc combining them. The spatial guidance components borrow from Bousselham et al. [2] for coherence maps and Subramanian et al. [30] for spatial-relationship parsing, but integrate them into a unified scoring framework alongside the hybrid features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a training-free method using only SAM and CLIP (ViT-B/16) outperforms approaches that additionally require BLIP2 (TAS) or Stable Diffusion (Ref-Diff), reducing deployment complexity while improving accuracy by 4–10% on mIoU. This suggests that careful feature extraction and spatial scoring within existing foundation models can substitute for adding heavier auxiliary models. Open questions include whether the G2L fusion strategy generalizes beyond CLIP's ViT-B/16 to larger backbones or other vision-language architectures, whether the rule-based spatial-relationship parsing scales to more complex compositional expressions, and whether the approach transfers to video or 3D referring segmentation.
