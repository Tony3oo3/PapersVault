## Problem

Existing generative object compositing models are limited to inserting a single object at a time, requiring sequential steps that cannot capture complex multi-object interactions such as hugging or playing an instrument, where simultaneous repositioning of all involved objects is necessary. No prior method supports simultaneous multi-object compositing with both text-based interaction control and layout guidance in a single pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252337/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a background image, N object images (typically two), a free-form text prompt with implicit grounding (which words refer to which object), and a layout encoded as a single multi-valued mask assigning distinct pixel values to each object's bounding box, their overlaps, the enclosing inpainting region, and the background. The layout mask and a masked version of the background (inpainting region zeroed out) are channel-concatenated with the noisy latent as U-Net input. In customization mode, the inpainting region expands to the full image and the background is replaced with an empty image, producing a subject-driven generated image instead of a composite.

## Method

Built on the Stable Diffusion 1.5 inpainting backbone, the model encodes each object image with DINO ViT-G/14, passes it through a content adaptor to align with text-embedding space, and concatenates the resulting visual token immediately after the corresponding text tokens in the CLIP-encoded caption, forming a grounded multimodal embedding fed to the U-Net via cross-attention. Two novel attention-based losses—cross-attention (L_c) encouraging each object's visual-language tokens to attend only to its own segmentation region, and self-attention (L_s) discouraging inter-object pixel-to-pixel attention—prevent identity blending between semantically similar subjects. The model is jointly trained on compositing and multi-entity customization (switched at 50% probability) to balance text-image alignment, and at inference, cross-attention scores for each object's tokens are masked by its bounding box to further enforce spatial separation. A data generation pipeline leverages LLaVA, ViP-LLaVA, and GroundingDINO to synthesize aligned captions, grounding, and segmentation from video frames, in-the-wild images, and a manually collected human-object interaction set, providing multi-view object pairs essential for learning reposing.

## Results

On the MultiComp-overlap set (interacting objects), Multitwine achieves CLIP-I 0.741 and DINO 0.532, outperforming all single-object compositing baselines (best: AnyDoor at 0.727/0.520). On MultiComp-nonoverlap, it reaches CLIP-I 0.768 and DINO 0.579, competitive with AnyDoor (0.763/0.593). On the single-object DreamBooth set, Multitwine scores CLIP-I 0.803 and DINO 0.892, matching or exceeding all baselines. In the auxiliary customization task on MultiBench, it achieves CLIP-I 0.783, DINO 0.599, and CLIP-Tgl 0.308, substantially outperforming Emu2Gen (0.595/0.414/0.287) on patch-based identity and text alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the primary multi-object compositing task, Multitwine outperforms all five single-object baselines (PbE, ControlCom, AnyDoor, IMPRINT, TTOB) on the overlapping-bbox (interacting) subset, with the largest margin over PbE (DINO 0.532 vs. 0.383). On non-overlapping compositions, it is competitive: slightly ahead of AnyDoor on CLIP-I (0.768 vs. 0.763) but marginally behind on DINO (0.579 vs. 0.593), and comparable to IMPRINT and TTOB. On single-object DreamBooth, it matches IMPRINT's DINO (0.892 vs. 0.889) while trailing slightly on CLIP-I (0.803 vs. 0.830). In the auxiliary customization task, Multitwine substantially outperforms Emu2Gen on patch-based identity metrics (CLIP-I 0.783 vs. 0.595; DINO 0.599 vs. 0.414) and text alignment (CLIP-Tgl 0.308 vs. 0.287), though it is slightly lower on global metrics (CLIP-Igl 0.688 vs. 0.616; DINOgl 0.454 vs. 0.434). It also surpasses BLIP-Diffusion, KOSMOS-G, and UNIMO-G on all reported global metrics. User studies confirm these quantitative findings, with up to 97.1% preference for Multitwine's realistic interactions over baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generative object compositing line (ObjectStitch, AnyDoor, IMPRINT, TTOB) from single-object insertion to simultaneous multi-object compositing, and unifies it with subject-driven generation (DreamBooth, BLIP-Diffusion, Emu2Gen) through joint training. Architecturally, it builds on Stable Diffusion 1.5's inpainting backbone and adopts ObjectStitch's content adaptor for fusing DINO object embeddings with CLIP text embeddings via cross-attention. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of attention-based losses (cross- and self-attention) to prevent identity leakage between multiple objects, and a data generation pipeline that leverages VLMs (LLaVA, ViP-LLaVA) and grounding models (GroundingDINO) to synthesize aligned multimodal training pairs at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first model to handle simultaneous multi-object compositing with text and layout control, addressing a practical gap where sequential single-object pipelines fail to capture complex interactions (hugging, playing instruments) and produce incoherent scenes. The joint training strategy—using customization as an auxiliary task to improve compositing—offers a reusable recipe for balancing text-image alignment in diffusion models. However, the model is limited to SD1.5 due to compute constraints, scales poorly beyond a small number of objects (multimodal embedding grows linearly), and the data pipeline, while practical, still relies on a manually curated set for human-object interactions. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to stronger backbones (SDXL, SD3) and how to handle arbitrary numbers of objects without embedding-length bottlenecks.
