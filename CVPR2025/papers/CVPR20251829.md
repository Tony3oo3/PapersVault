## Problem

Existing remote sensing foundation models support only limited input types—either static or temporal, uni-modal or multi-modal—and none directly address incomplete downstream data caused by varying spectral bands or irregular temporal sequences. This restricts their practical deployment where sensor degradation, atmospheric conditions, and infrequent acquisitions produce missing bands and time-point gaps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251829/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts 224×224 MS images (9 bands: B02–B08, B11, B12) and 2-band SAR images (VH, VV) at patch size 16, in any combination of static or temporal (T=8) form, uni-modal or multi-modal, and with arbitrarily missing bands or time points. At inference, the flexible module composition (uni-modal encoders, multi-modal encoder, latent reconstructors) is selected based on input type, producing latent representations that feed task-specific heads (UperNet for segmentation/change detection, linear classifier for classification).

## Method

RobSense uses two parallel ViT-Base uni-modal encoders (12 layers) for MS and SAR, a multi-modal encoder, and a 6-layer decoder, pre-trained in two phases on temporal MS-SAR pairs from SatlasPretrain. Phase 1 (Temporal Multi-modal Learning) applies 90% consistent patch masking with MAE reconstruction plus a Temporal Distribution Alignment (TDA) loss that models each modality's temporal sequence as a multivariate Gaussian and minimizes the symmetric KL divergence between the two distributions, preserving time-specific structure without the information loss of contrastive patch pairing. Phase 2 (Latent Reconstruction Learning) freezes all encoders and trains two lightweight 4-layer ViT latent reconstructors to recover full representations from inputs with randomly dropped non-RGB spectral bands or omitted time points, using MSE against the frozen encoder's output on complete data. This two-phase design decouples rich representation learning from incompleteness recovery, enabling the model to handle all four input types and degrade gracefully as missing rates increase.

## Results

On semantic segmentation (SatlasPretrain), RobSense achieves 46.36 mIoU with temporal multi-modal (T-MS-SAR) input, outperforming SpectralGPT (45.69, T-MS) by 0.67% and Croma (44.01, MS-SAR) by 2.35%. On BigEarthNet classification, RobSense reaches 88.17 mAP with MS-SAR input, surpassing Croma (86.28) by 1.89% and SpectralGPT (87.50, MS-only) by 0.67%. On OSCD change detection, RobSense scores 58.22 F1, exceeding SatMAE++ (56.28) by 1.94% and SpectralGPT (54.29) by 3.93%. Under incomplete data (20–30% missing bands/time points), RobSense maintains the largest margins over baselines, with gaps widening as the missing rate increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RobSense outperforms all five baselines on the complete multi-modal segmentation setting (46.36 mIoU vs. Croma 44.01, SpectralGPT 45.69, SatMAE++ 42.90, SatlasNet 43.51). On BigEarthNet classification, RobSense wins with MS-SAR input (88.17 mAP) over Croma (86.28), SpectralGPT (87.50, MS-only), SatMAE++ (85.11), and ConvNeXtV2 (85.10); however, with MS-only input RobSense (86.36) trails Croma (87.58) and SpectralGPT (87.50), indicating the multi-modal pre-training advantage is most pronounced when SAR is available. On change detection, RobSense leads SatMAE++ by 1.94 F1 and SpectralGPT by 3.93 F1. Under incomplete data (20–30% missing), RobSense's advantage over the second-best model widens from ~1% at 0% missing to 2.5–3.7% at random missing rates, while baselines degrade more sharply. ConvNeXtV2, trained explicitly for BigEarthNet, is the strongest non-foundation-model baseline but still lags RobSense by 3.07 mAP in the multi-modal setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

RobSense extends the self-supervised pre-training paradigm for remote sensing (SatMAE, SatMAE++, SpectralGPT, SatlasNet) by unifying multi-modal (MS + SAR) and temporal inputs under a single architecture, whereas prior models handle only a subset of these input types. Its key methodological departure from Croma is replacing patch-level contrastive learning with a temporal distribution alignment loss based on multivariate KL divergence, which the authors argue avoids the information loss from misaligned positive/negative patch pairs. The addition of dedicated uni-modal latent reconstructors trained in a separate pre-training phase to recover representations from missing spectral bands or temporal gaps is a new component not present in any prior remote sensing foundation model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: real-world satellite data frequently suffers from missing spectral bands (due to sensor degradation or atmospheric conditions) and irregular temporal sampling, yet no prior foundation model explicitly handles these incompleteness scenarios. The consistent widening of the performance gap over baselines as missing rates increase provides evidence that the latent reconstructor design is genuinely useful rather than a marginal trick. Limitations include the high pre-training cost (~4,500 A100 GPU-hours across two phases) and the fact that evaluation is confined to three tasks on relatively small downstream datasets; the authors note future work on broader task coverage and language integration.
