## Problem

Existing diffusion-based inpainting methods (e.g., SD2-Inpaint) struggle to reliably remove target objects, often regenerating the erased object or producing artifacts in the masked region. The paper attributes this failure to the standard diffusion training paradigm, which establishes denoising pathways from noise to the original object-containing image rather than from noise to a clean background, thereby misaligning the learned sampling trajectory with the erasure objective.

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB image and a binary erasure mask (object-based or randomly generated) as input, processed through a VAE into latent space. It outputs a full-resolution inpainted image where the masked region is filled with a coherent background that excludes the removed object. The architecture is built on SD2-Inpaint's VAE + U-Net backbone, and the method operates in latent space with DDIM sampling, supporting arbitrary mask shapes and sizes without requiring textual prompts.

## Method

EraDiff introduces two key mechanisms. First, Chain-Rectifying Optimization (CRO) constructs dynamic latent states by synthesizing a series of mix-up images that interpolate between the original image and a version where the segmented object has been transformed (rotated, scaled) and pasted elsewhere, simulating gradual object fading across denoising time steps. A novel optimization objective then minimizes the L2 distance between model-predicted and true intermediate latent states over variable time intervals, establishing diffusion pathways from noise to background. Second, Self-Rectifying Attention (SRA) modifies the standard self-attention by applying an extended binary mask that sets attention weights from masked-to-masked positions to −inf, forcing the model to attend to background features rather than artifacts within the masked region during sampling. Together, CRO calibrates the training trajectory while SRA corrects deviations during inference, with no additional computational cost at test time.

## Results

On the OpenImages V5 dataset, EraDiff achieves the best Local FID (3.799) and LPIPS (0.192) among all compared methods, while its overall FID (6.540) is mid-range, trailing SD2-Inpaint (3.805). The Local FID advantage is substantial: EraDiff improves over LaMa (6.091) by 37.6% and over SD2-Inpaint (8.852) by 57.1%, indicating markedly better visual fidelity specifically within erased regions. In GPT-4o pairwise elimination evaluations, EraDiff outperforms SD2-Inpaint (80.90% of SD2-Inpaint results rated inferior), SD2-Inpaint with prompts (73.01% inferior), and LaMa (51.54% inferior).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EraDiff decisively outperforms all four baselines on Local FID (3.799), the metric most directly reflecting quality within the erased region, beating LaMa (6.091), SD2-Inpaint (8.852), PowerPaint (10.021), and Inst-Inpaint (43.472). It also achieves the best LPIPS (0.192), surpassing LaMa (0.219), PowerPaint (0.289), SD2-Inpaint (0.301), and Inst-Inpaint (0.410). On overall FID, EraDiff (6.540) trails SD2-Inpaint (3.805) and PowerPaint (6.027), but the authors argue FID does not capture object-elimination effectiveness; GPT-4o pairwise comparisons confirm EraDiff produces superior erasure in 80.90% of cases vs. SD2-Inpaint and 73.01% vs. SD2-Inpaint with prompts. Against LaMa, EraDiff wins on both Local FID and LPIPS while losing slightly on FID, with GPT-4o rating 51.54% of LaMa outputs as inferior. Inst-Inpaint is the weakest baseline across all three metrics, with a Local FID of 43.472—over 11× worse than EraDiff—highlighting the instability of text-guided erasure approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

EraDiff builds directly on the SD2-Inpaint architecture (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022), reusing its VAE + U-Net backbone and DDIM sampling framework, but fundamentally restructures the training paradigm. Where prior erase inpainting methods (GAN-based like LaMa, or LDM-based like SD2-Inpaint) train the model to denoise from noise back to the original image—thereby inadvertently learning to reconstruct objects—EraDiff introduces Chain-Rectifying Optimization to establish diffusion pathways from noise to background, and Self-Rectifying Attention to suppress artifact amplification during sampling. The dynamic mix-up image synthesis strategy for generating paired object-background training data without external datasets is a practical departure from prior methods that rely on random masks or textual prompts to define erasure targets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and previously underappreciated failure mode in diffusion-based inpainting: the standard denoising pathway is structurally misaligned with the erasure objective, causing models to regenerate removed objects. This reframing suggests that task-specific diffusion pathway design (not just architecture or loss tweaks) may be a general principle for other conditional generation tasks. Limitations include dependence on a matting model for training data synthesis, evaluation on a single dataset (OpenImages V5), and the SRA mechanism being a simple binary mask that may not generalize to soft or multi-object masks. Open questions include whether the CRO paradigm transfers to video inpainting or other editing tasks, and whether the approach can be extended to handle multiple simultaneous object removals without per-object matting.
