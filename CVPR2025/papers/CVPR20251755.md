## Problem

Standard contrastive vision-language pretraining (CLIP, SigLIP) optimizes for zero-shot transfer but does not explicitly encourage representations to support few-shot adaptation at test time. The paper questions the assumption that models optimized for zero-shot generalization will inherently transfer well to few-shot scenarios, asking whether large-scale contrastive pretraining can be modified to significantly improve training-free, metric-based few-shot adaptation without degrading zero-shot performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251755/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pretraining, the model consumes image-text pairs from WebLI (1.5B–15B examples) through dual ViT and BERT encoders. At test time, given a query image, text class labels, and a support set of K images per class, the method produces classification logits via training-free metric-based aggregation (cosine similarity, prototypical means, or nearest-neighbor voting) without any gradient-based optimization. The support set images are embedded with the same frozen vision encoder and combined with zero-shot text logits through mechanisms like Tip-Adapter or softmax-weighted kNN voting.

## Method

LIxP (Language-Image Contextual Pretraining) augments the standard contrastive loss with a cross-attention contextualization step: each image representation attends over the current batch's own image embeddings (serving as key and value buffers, with normalized keys and unnormalized values) using a masking matrix that blocks self-attention, creating implicit per-iteration episodic training. The final objective is a weighted sum of the base contrastive loss on raw representations and the same loss on contextualized representations, with three independently learnable temperatures (τ₁, τ₂, τ_ctx) to decouple the two objectives and the attention sharpness. Full backpropagation through both key and value buffer entries is essential, and the simple design of reusing the current batch as context (rather than stale memories, separate batches, or learnable heads) proves optimal. This single-stage modification to SigLIP or CLIP pretraining yields up to 4× sample-efficiency gains and >5% average few-shot improvements across 21 tasks while preserving zero-shot transfer.

## Results

On 21 few-shot classification benchmarks, SigLIxP (ViT-S/16, 1.5B WebLI) achieves 65.7% average 32-shot accuracy with Tip-Adapter, a +5.4% gain over the SigLIP baseline (60.3%), while retaining zero-shot performance (50.5% vs 51.0%). Sample efficiency improves by up to 4×: SigLIxP reaches 61.1% at 8-shot, matching SigLIP's 60.3% at 32-shot. Against state-of-the-art optimization-based adaptation methods on ViT-B/16 (16-shot), SigLIxP with a simple training-free softmax-voted nearest-neighbor classifier achieves 77.9% on ImageNet, 76.7% on DTD, 92.6% on Food-101, 94.4% on Pets, and 92.8% on Cars, outperforming all compared finetuning and prompt-learning approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SigLIxP with a training-free softmax-voted nearest-neighbor classifier decisively outperforms all optimization-based baselines in Table 2: it beats the strongest finetuned method DMN [102] by 3.2% on ImageNet (77.9% vs 74.7%) and by 1.7% on DTD (76.7% vs 75.1%), while also surpassing prompt-learning methods CasPL [92] (74.2%) and PromptSRC [37] (73.2%) by 3.7% and 4.7% respectively on ImageNet. Among training-free baselines, SigLIxP leads DMN-TF [102] (72.6%) by 5.3%, APE [108] (72.1%) by 5.8%, and TIP-X [84] (71.1%) by 6.8% on ImageNet. Against the direct SigLIP pretraining baseline, SigLIxP yields +5.4% average 32-shot gains (Tip-Adapter) across 21 datasets with no loss in zero-shot accuracy, and the gains scale consistently from ViT-S/16 (1.5B) through ViT-L/16 (8B) with average improvements of +2.2% to +3.2%. The CLIxP variant (CLIP-based objective) shows comparable gains, confirming the approach is not specific to the SigLIP sigmoid loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SigLIP and CLIP contrastive pretraining paradigms by introducing a context-aware loss term (LIxP) that cross-attends image representations over a batch-level key-value buffer during training. Conceptually, it departs from the implicit assumption in large-scale vision-language pretraining that representations optimized for zero-shot transfer will inherently support few-shot metric-based adaptation, instead explicitly training the model to leverage contextual support examples. It sits at the intersection of the contrastive pretraining literature (SigLIP, CLIP), the metric-based few-shot learning literature (prototypical networks, nearest-neighbor classifiers), and the training-free adaptation literature (Tip-Adapter, SuS-X, DMN-TF), showing that the pretraining objective itself can be modified to make these simple test-time mechanisms more effective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single context-aware pretrained model can be deployed across diverse downstream domains using only a training-free metric classifier, eliminating the need for expensive per-domain optimization (finetuning, prompt tuning, adapter training). The finding that SigLIxP with a simple softmax-voted nearest-neighbor classifier outperforms state-of-the-art optimization-based methods (e.g., 77.9% vs. 74.7% on ImageNet 16-shot) challenges the prevailing assumption that test-time optimization is necessary for competitive few-shot performance. Limitations include evaluation primarily on classification tasks, reliance on the current batch as context (no persistent or cross-iteration memory), and moderate absolute gains (2–5% average) that may diminish as base models approach saturation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-classification tasks (segmentation, retrieval, VQA), whether richer buffer designs (e.g., persistent memory, multimodal context) could yield further gains, and how the method interacts with other pretraining objectives beyond contrastive learning.
