## Problem

Model-free 6D pose estimation for unseen objects—relying only on a handful of reference images rather than CAD models—remains challenging when objects undergo large translations or rotations or exhibit weak textures. Existing approaches either require additional depth supervision (FS6D), depend on 2D-3D keypoint correspondences that degrade on textureless surfaces (OnePose), or operate purely in 2D and fail under large viewpoint changes (Gen6D). The paper addresses this by combining rotation-equivariant feature matching for robust initialization with high-fidelity 3D rendering for iterative refinement.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of N_r reference images with known camera extrinsics (annotated via OnePose/ARKit toolboxes) and a single query RGB image at 480×640 resolution. Reference images are augmented with kπ/2 rotations and 2×/0.5× scaling to enrich the candidate pose space. The pipeline outputs a pixel-wise object segmentation mask, a 3D Gaussian Splatting model of the object (built from SfM on the reference set), and the final 6D pose as a rotation R ∈ SO(3) and translation T ∈ R³ mapping object coordinates to camera coordinates.

## Method

iG-6DoF is a three-stage coarse-to-fine pipeline. First, a correlation-based detector (VGG-11 backbone, TGID/Gen6D-style) segments the target object from the query image. Second, an initial pose estimator embeds augmented reference images into a 60-dimensional icosahedral group feature space using PointNet as a shared point-set encoder with localized group convolutions; the query image's feature vector is matched via cosine similarity to select the best pose hypothesis from the multi-scale candidate set. Third, a 3DGS-based pose refiner iteratively renders the object's Gaussian model at the current pose, computes SSIM and multi-scale SSIM losses against the query image, and decouples translation and rotation updates (shifting the rotation center to the object centroid) to progressively minimize the rendering discrepancy until convergence. The entire system is trained end-to-end on the synthetic MegaPose dataset (1M images, 1000 objects) with a combined BCE segmentation loss, RoReg-style group descriptor loss, and SSIM-based pose loss.

## Results

On the full LINEMOD benchmark (13 objects), iG-6DoF achieves 85.1% ADD(S)-0.1d and 96.6% Proj2D, outperforming the strongest model-free baseline MFOS (78.4% / 96.1%) by 6.7 and 0.5 percentage points respectively. On the challenging OnePose-LowTexture dataset, iG-6DoF (with GT mask) reaches 75.1% at the 5cm/5deg threshold, surpassing MFOS (74.2%) and OnePose++ (72.1%). On GenMOP, the method attains 56.10% ADD-0.1d and 86.34% Proj2D, exceeding Gen6D with refinement (50.39% / 82.64%). On a 5-object LINEMOD subset with pose refinement, iG-6DoF scores 83.22% ADD(S)-0.1d, well ahead of Cas6D (70.72%) and Gen6D (62.45%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the full LINEMOD benchmark, iG-6DoF decisively outperforms all model-free baselines: it beats MFOS (the strongest competitor) by 6.7 points on ADD(S)-0.1d (85.1% vs 78.4%) and by 0.5 points on Proj2D (96.6% vs 96.1%), while OnePose++ trails by 8.2 points on ADD(S)-0.1d. On OnePose-LowTexture, iG-6DoF (with GT mask) wins at the strictest 1cm/1deg threshold (17.2% vs OnePose++ 16.8%, MFOS 14.1%) and at 5cm/5deg (75.1% vs MFOS 74.2%), but slightly underperforms OnePose++ at the intermediate 3cm/3deg threshold (55.6% vs 57.7%). On GenMOP, iG-6DoF with refinement surpasses Gen6D with refinement by 5.7 points on ADD-0.1d (56.10% vs 50.39%) and 3.7 points on Proj2D (86.34% vs 82.64%). On the 5-object LINEMOD subset, the 3DGS-based refiner lifts iG-6DoF from 45.99% to 83.22% ADD(S)-0.1d, substantially exceeding Cas6D (70.72%), Gen6D (62.45%), and GS-Pose (46.77%). Notably, unlike OnePose and OnePose++ which require pre-reconstructed 3D shape models, iG-6DoF operates purely from reference images without any prior 3D object knowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of model-free pose estimation (Gen6D, OnePose, OnePose++) and neural-rendering-based refinement (iNeRF, Nerf-pose, MegaPose), but departs from both in meaningful ways. Rather than relying on simple template matching or 2D-3D keypoint correspondences for initial pose, it adopts rotation-equivariant icosahedral group features (from RoReg and GIFT) combined with multi-scale data augmentation to build a richer candidate pose space. For refinement, it replaces the NeRF-based render-and-compare loop with 3D Gaussian Splatting, which offers real-time rendering speed while maintaining high visual fidelity, and decouples rotation and translation updates to improve convergence stability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical gap: model-free pose estimation that handles large viewpoint changes and weak textures without requiring CAD models or additional depth/normal supervision, making it more deployable in real-world robotic and AR settings. The ~37-point improvement from initial to refined pose (45.99% → 83.22% ADD-0.1d on LINEMOD) demonstrates that 3DGS-based iterative refinement is a viable and effective strategy for unseen objects, outperforming DeepIM and Gen6D refiners in the ablation. Open questions include scalability to multi-object or dynamic scenes, the sensitivity to the number and quality of reference images, and whether the 0.5 s per-image runtime is sufficient for real-time robotic control loops.
