## Problem

Diffusion transformers (DiTs) incur high computational costs during inference, and existing token reduction methods either require additional fine-tuning or apply static compression strategies that ignore the denoising priors of diffusion models. This leads to suboptimal acceleration, diminished image quality, and limited applicability across different architectures and schedulers. The paper asks: where exactly are the feature redundancies in the denoising process, and can they be exploited in a training-free, stage-aware manner?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251869/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt as input and produces a 1024×1024 RGB image as output, operating as a drop-in post-training accelerator on top of any existing text-to-image DiT (e.g., SD3 Medium, SD3.5 Large). Users specify a basic compression ratio ρ and maximum deviation d, and the method dynamically adjusts the actual per-step, per-layer compression to match the redundancy profile. The prompt is additionally processed by an LLM (ChatGPT) to categorize each token into structure or detail groups for the reweighting step, but this is a one-time offline annotation per prompt.

## Method

SDTM (Structure-then-Detail Token Merging) is a training-free, post-training token compression framework that exploits the structure-then-detail denoising prior of DiTs. In the early (structure) stage, Similarity-prioritized Structure Merging (SSM) identifies locally redundant tokens via windowed cosine similarity and merges them before both MHSA and MLP blocks; in the later (detail) stage, Inattentive-prioritized Detail Merging (IDM) uses attention maps to find tokens with minimal influence on others and merges them before MLP blocks only. A Compression Ratio Adjusting (CRA) module dynamically modulates the merging ratio or threshold across timesteps via cosine decay (or an adaptive threshold table in SDTM*), matching the empirically observed redundancy profile. A Prompt Token Reweighting (PTR) component reweights cross-attention values for structure- vs. detail-oriented prompt tokens to preserve semantic guidance under reduced token counts. The entire pipeline requires no fine-tuning and integrates seamlessly into any DiT architecture.

## Results

On MS-COCO2017 with SD3 Medium (50-step rectified flow), SDTM* achieves 1.55× speedup (6.90s vs. 10.67s baseline) with FID 28.97, nearly matching the unaccelerated baseline FID of 29.02. At the lighter 1.3× setting, SDTM* actually improves FID to 28.57 (vs. 29.02 baseline) while ToMeSD degrades to 45.28 and AT-EDM to 34.72. SDTM* also attains the best CLIP score (0.3252 on COCO2017, 0.3261 on PartiPrompts) among all 1.5× methods, outperforming fine-tuning-based DyDiT (0.3227) and TokenCache (0.3226).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ToMeSD [1] and AT-EDM [44], which use static similarity- or attention-based merging, SDTM* dramatically outperforms at both speed levels: at 1.5×, FID drops from 75.44 (ToMeSD) and 43.92 (AT-EDM) to 28.97 (SDTM*), a gap of 46 and 15 FID points respectively. Against the fine-tuning-based DyDiT [50], SDTM* achieves comparable FID (28.97 vs. 28.47) and superior CLIP (0.3237 vs. 0.3213) without any additional training, while matching its 1.55× speed. Against TokenCache [26], SDTM* trades a slightly higher FID (28.97 vs. 28.83) for better CLIP alignment (0.3237 vs. 0.3208) and again requires no fine-tuning. The key differentiator is that SDTM* is training-free and architecture-agnostic, whereas DyDiT and TokenCache both require a dedicated fine-tuning stage, and ToMeSD/AT-EDM suffer severe quality collapse under higher compression ratios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the token-merging lineage (ToMe, ToMeSD, AT-EDM) from U-Net diffusion models to DiT architectures, but departs from prior feature-based compression methods (DyDiT, TokenCache) by being entirely post-training and fine-tuning-free. Its key conceptual departure is grounding the compression schedule in the structure-then-detail denoising prior: it uses similarity-based local merging (SSM) in early steps and attention-map-driven global merging (IDM) in later steps, rather than applying a single static or learned compression strategy uniformly. The addition of prompt token reweighting to preserve text-image alignment under compression is also novel relative to prior token-reduction work, which typically ignores the guidance signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 1.55× speedup with near-identical FID/CLIP on SD3 Medium, achieved without any fine-tuning and applicable to any text-to-image DiT, is a practically significant efficiency gain for deployment. The paper's limitation is acknowledged: beyond ~1.55×, quality degrades rapidly, and the authors note that higher compression would require combining with distillation or other techniques. Open questions include whether the structure-then-detail prior and the attention-based redundancy identification generalize to video DiTs, to models with different attention patterns (e.g., sparse or linear attention), and whether the ChatGPT-based prompt categorization can be replaced by a lighter-weight mechanism for production use.
