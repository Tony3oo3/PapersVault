## Problem

Large vision-language models frequently generate object hallucinations—describing objects not present in the input image—yet the underlying feature-space mechanisms causing this remain unexplored. Existing mitigation strategies rely on costly fine-tuning or multi-pass inference-time corrections, leaving open the question of whether a single, zero-overhead weight edit can suppress hallucination at its source.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250740/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During the offline editing phase, Nullu consumes paired vision-language inputs: the same RGB image paired with a truthful ground-truth caption and a GPT-3.5-generated hallucinated caption (sourced from LURE). At inference, the edited model takes a standard image plus a text prompt (e.g., 'Please describe this image in detail') and produces a free-form text description, with no additional forward passes, external tools, or decoding modifications required beyond the original model's standard pipeline.

## Method

Nullu identifies a low-rank 'HalluSpace' by computing the difference matrix between averaged hidden features of hallucinated and truthful prompts at each target LLM layer, then extracting the top-k right singular vectors via SVD. The model's MLP weights at those layers are then projected onto the null space of this subspace via W_ed = (I − V_k V_kᵀ) W_org, a one-time orthogonalization that permanently removes the hallucination-triggering directions from the weight matrix. The key insight is that these HalluSpaces encode LLM prior biases (high-frequency co-occurring object words), so null-space projection effectively debiases the model without any retraining. The authors further show a formal connection to DPO: both methods aim to eliminate the feature-space difference between truthful and hallucinated inputs, positioning Nullu as a closed-form, training-free alternative to preference-based alignment.

## Results

On the CHAIR benchmark (MSCOCO), Nullu achieves the best CHAIR_S across all three LVLMs: 15.20 on LLaVA-1.5 (vs. 20.40 greedy, 16.90 HALC), 21.40 on MiniGPT-4 (vs. 32.40 greedy, 25.20 HALC), and 15.60 on mPLUG-Owl2 (vs. 22.90 greedy, 18.80 HALC). Nullu also attains the lowest CHAIR_I (5.30, 8.99, 5.77 respectively) while maintaining competitive BLEU scores. On OPOPE, Nullu achieves the highest F-score for all three models (91.79, 92.07, 90.80), and on MME and LLaVA-Bench it shows consistent gains in general capability without extra inference cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Nullu outperforms all seven baselines on CHAIR_S and CHAIR_I across all three LVLMs. Against the strongest baseline HALC, Nullu reduces CHAIR_S by 1.7 (LLaVA-1.5), 3.8 (MiniGPT-4), and 3.2 (mPLUG-Owl2) points, while also improving CHAIR_I by 0.4–1.2 points; the only trade-off is a marginal BLEU drop on LLaVA-1.5 (15.69 vs. 16.02). Compared to OPERA, Nullu achieves a 2.3-point lower CHAIR_S on LLaVA-1.5 while running 7× faster in inference throughput. VCD and DoLa, which rely on double-inference at test time, are clearly outperformed (CHAIR_S 20.30 and 20.20 vs. Nullu's 15.20 on LLaVA-1.5) and incur additional latency. LURE and Woodpecker, which use external revisers or correction modules, show larger CHAIR_S gaps (19.48 and 23.85 vs. 15.20). On OPOPE, Nullu achieves the best F-score for every model (91.79, 92.07, 90.80), surpassing HALC (91.25, 90.95, 88.12) and OPERA (90.71, 90.66, 89.11), confirming that the weight-editing approach generalizes beyond captioning to object-existence queries.

## Relation to Prior Work

Nullu sits at the intersection of feature-steering methods (ITI, ActADD, VTI) that contrast positive/negative internal representations, and model-editing approaches like Uppaal et al.'s null-space projection for LLM detoxification. It departs from inference-time OH mitigation strategies (VCD, HALC, OPERA) by performing a one-time offline weight edit rather than modifying the decoding procedure, and from fine-tuning-based methods (HACT, robust instruction tuning) by requiring no gradient updates. The paper also establishes a conceptual link to DPO, showing that both methods implicitly aim to eliminate feature differences between truthful and hallucinated inputs, though Nullu does so through a closed-form SVD-based projection rather than iterative optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-extra-inference-cost property makes Nullu practically attractive for deployment scenarios where latency matters, and the consistent improvements across three LVLM families (LLaVA-1.5, MiniGPT-4, mPLUG-Owl2) suggest the HalluSpace concept is not model-specific. However, the method's reliance on carefully constructed paired data (via LURE/GPT-3.5), the empirical selection of target layers and rank k, and the limited evaluation to captioning-style tasks leave open whether the approach generalizes to more complex hallucination types (relational, attribute) or to larger, more diverse LVLMs. The DPO connection hints at a broader principle—that safety alignment can be achieved through geometric weight edits—but this remains underexplored.
