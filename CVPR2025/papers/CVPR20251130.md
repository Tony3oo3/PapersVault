## Problem

Existing diffusion-based image semantic segmentation methods (e.g., EmerDiff) produce temporally inconsistent predictions when applied frame-by-frame to videos, because they lack any modeling of temporal information. No training-free diffusion-based approach for Video Semantic Segmentation (VSS) existed prior to this work, leaving a gap between the strong semantic understanding of pre-trained diffusion models and the need for temporally coherent video-level predictions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251130/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of RGB video frames, encodes them via a VAE into latent space (64× compression from pixel to latent), adds noise at a chosen timestep t_inv, and processes them in batches of 14 frames. It outputs per-frame full-resolution semantic segmentation maps that are class-agnostic (clusters are unlabeled); a post-hoc label alignment step against ground truth of the first frame is used only for evaluation. Coarse masks are produced at reduced resolution (H/S_i × W/S_i) and upsampled to full resolution through the modulation process.

## Method

VidSeg builds a Scene Context Model: a KNN classifier initialized with K-Means clusters over aggregated diffusion features (decoder blocks 6–8) of the first frame, then autoregressively updated per batch to adapt to scene changes. A Correspondence-Based Refinement (CBR) strategy computes per-pixel cosine-similarity correspondences between consecutive frames, recursively tracks trajectories across the batch, and performs majority voting along the temporal axis to regularize coarse masks spatially and temporally. Finally, a Masked Modulation process blends modulated and unmodulated latents using the coarse binary masks (Eq. 6) and applies the same masking to the resulting difference maps (Eq. 7), suppressing spurious activations outside the target region and yielding cleaner full-resolution segmentation. The key insight is that spatial features from SD/SVD are temporally more stable than SVD's dedicated temporal attention features, so the method leverages spatial features and enhances their temporal coherence through the context model and CBR rather than relying on the video model's temporal layers.

## Results

On VSPW, Ours(SD) achieves 60.6 mIoU and 90.7 mVC8, outperforming the training-free EmerDiff(SD) baseline (43.4 mIoU) by 17.2% and matching the supervised UniVS method (59.8 mIoU). On Cityscapes, Ours(SD) reaches 37.3 mIoU versus 21.5 for EmerDiff(SD) and 11.0 for EmerDiff(SVD). On CamVid, Ours(SD) achieves 20.6 mIoU and 92.3 mVC8, a 13.7-point improvement over EmerDiff(SD) (6.9 mIoU). The SVD backbone variant (Ours(SVD)) also consistently outperforms its EmerDiff counterpart across all three datasets, with the largest gain on Cityscapes (+25.2 mIoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest training-free baseline EmerDiff, VidSeg(SD) improves mIoU by 17.2% on VSPW (60.6 vs 43.4), 15.8% on Cityscapes (37.3 vs 21.5), and 13.7% on CamVid (20.6 vs 6.9); VidSeg(SVD) gains 13.5%, 25.2%, and 9.3% respectively. Compared to the unsupervised CLIPpy method, VidSeg(SD) achieves a 42.9-point mIoU improvement on VSPW (60.6 vs 17.7) and a 32.6-point gain on Cityscapes (37.3 vs 4.7). Against supervised methods, VidSeg(SD) slightly exceeds UniVS on VSPW mIoU (60.6 vs 59.8) but trails DVIS++ (60.6 vs 63.8) and TMANet on Cityscapes (37.3 vs 80.3) and CamVid (20.6 vs 76.5), which the authors attribute to the 64× VAE compression limiting small-object segmentation in driving scenes. Notably, VidSeg(SD) achieves 92.3 mVC8 on CamVid, surpassing even the supervised DVIS++ (95.7 mVC8 on VSPW only), indicating strong temporal consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends EmerDiff's observation that diffusion model UNet features support image semantic segmentation via K-Means clustering and attention modulation to the video domain, where frame-by-frame application fails due to temporal inconsistency. It departs from prior diffusion-feature work (semantic correspondence, keypoints) by introducing a scene context model (autoregressive KNN over aggregated features), a correspondence-based refinement with pixel-wise voting, and masked modulation to produce temporally consistent VSS predictions. The paper also investigates Stable Video Diffusion as an alternative backbone but finds its temporal attention features are less temporally stable than SD's spatial features, a finding that motivates relying on spatial features from either model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first training-free diffusion-based VSS method, VidSeg demonstrates that pre-trained diffusion features can rival supervised VSS approaches on diverse datasets (VSPW mIoU 60.6 vs. UniVS 59.8) without any task-specific training, opening a path to zero-shot video segmentation. Key limitations include poor performance on small objects due to VAE compression (64× pixel-to-latent), sensitivity to image inversion quality, and instance-agnostic clustering. Open questions include whether larger-scale SVD training will reverse the current SD > SVD feature quality gap, whether tiled processing can recover small-object segmentation, and whether the framework extends to instance or panoptic video segmentation.
