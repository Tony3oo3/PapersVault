## Problem

Existing diffusion customization methods personalize concepts collectively, but real-world applications require sequential concept integration. This sequential setting causes two underexplored failures: catastrophic forgetting (loss of previously learned concepts) and concept confusion (blending of elements from different concepts in generated outputs). Prior continual customization work such as Continual Diffusion addresses only forgetting while leaving confusion unaddressed.

## Input / Output

For each new concept, the method receives 3–5 user-provided RGB images with associated text prompts containing a unique concept token. Previous concept images are assumed unavailable (privacy/storage constraint), so the method relies on stored text embeddings and shift embeddings rather than raw images. At inference, a text prompt referencing one or more learned concept tokens is fed into the SDXL latent diffusion model to produce a 1024×1024 generated image, supporting both single-concept and multi-concept compositions.

## Method

ConceptGuard fine-tunes LoRA adapters on the K/V matrices of SDXL self-attention layers while simultaneously training per-concept shift embeddings (zero-initialized vectors added to frozen concept embeddings) so that previously learned embeddings track model updates without being overwritten. A priority queue stores (concept_index, importance_α) tuples and adaptively selects 3–5 previous concepts for replay, prioritizing earlier concepts and higher importance. Selected concepts undergo concept focus (SAM-based background removal), chrono-concept composition (weighted random combination with weights linearly interpolated from 0.2 for nearest to 0.6 for farthest), and concept-binding prompts (per-concept trainable scalar α multiplied by shift embeddings, broadcast over a shared global binding vector P_b). A memory preservation regularization term penalizes the L2 change in LoRA weights between consecutive tasks, slowing parameter drift. The total loss combines the standard LDM denoising loss for the current concept, a weighted replay loss for previous concepts conditioned on concept-binding prompts, and the regularization term.

## Results

On the continual customization benchmark (18 concepts, 6 evaluated per round), ConceptGuard achieves the best scores across all metrics: Text Alignment of 43.1 (single) and 40.3 (multi), Image Alignment of 81.3 (single) and 69.8 (multi), and the lowest forgetting scores of FT=0.9 and FI=1.9. The largest margin over the strongest baseline (Continual Diffusion) appears in multi-concept Image Alignment (69.8 vs. 57.1, a 12.7-point gap) and Forgetting-Image (1.9 vs. 4.1). Textual Inversion reports zero forgetting (FT=FI=0) but at the cost of substantially lower alignment scores (IA single 71.1, IA multi 45.3), confirming that freezing the model trades generation quality for stability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConceptGuard outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest continual baseline, Continual Diffusion (CLoRA), ConceptGuard gains +0.8 in single-concept TA, +2.5 in multi-concept TA, +3.8 in single-concept IA, +12.7 in multi-concept IA, and halves both forgetting scores (FT 0.9 vs 1.7; FI 1.9 vs 4.1). Compared to Custom Diffusion + LwF (the best non-continual method with a regularization strategy), ConceptGuard improves multi-concept IA by 13.0 points (69.8 vs 56.8) and reduces FI by 2.7 points. DreamBooth + LwF shows a similar pattern: ConceptGuard leads by 13.1 in multi-concept IA and 3.3 in FI. Textual Inversion, which avoids forgetting entirely by freezing the model, lags ConceptGuard by 10.2 in single-concept IA and 24.5 in multi-concept IA, illustrating the quality cost of not fine-tuning the diffusion backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continual customization line initiated by Continual Diffusion (Smith et al.) and Custom Diffusion (Kumari et al.), which fine-tune cross-attention layers via LoRA to personalize sequential concepts. It departs from prior work by identifying concept confusion—where the model blends elements of different learned concepts—as a distinct failure mode alongside catastrophic forgetting, and by combining shift embeddings (to track model drift), concept-binding prompts with a priority queue (to manage inter-concept relationships), and memory preservation regularization (to slow parameter updates). The approach builds directly on Textual Inversion's token-based concept representation and Custom Diffusion's LoRA-based fine-tuning, but adds adaptive mechanisms absent in both.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, modular framework for continual T2I customization that addresses both forgetting and confusion, two problems that prior methods treated separately or ignored entirely. The notably large multi-concept generation gap over Continual Diffusion (69.8 vs. 57.1 image alignment) suggests that confusion, not just forgetting, is the dominant bottleneck in sequential customization. Limitations include evaluation on only 18 concepts (6 per round) with a single architecture (SDXL), and the priority queue introduces non-trivial bookkeeping that may complicate deployment. Open questions include scalability to hundreds of concepts, generalization to other diffusion backbones, and whether the concept-binding prompt mechanism transfers to video or 3D generation.
