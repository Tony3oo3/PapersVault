## Problem

Existing RLAIF methods for aligning multimodal LLMs rely on expensive proprietary models (e.g., GPT-4V) as feedback labelers, leaving the community without a recipe for generating high-quality preference data using open-source MLLMs of comparable capability. Additionally, prior work applies feedback only during preference learning and largely ignores its potential to guide inference-time scaling, where aimlessly increasing compute without accurate reward signals yields diminishing returns.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes an RGB image paired with a text instruction (e.g., image description prompts, VQA questions, scene-text queries) and produces a text response. During training, the model generates n candidate responses per instruction via nucleus sampling with different random seeds; an open-source MLLM labeler then scores each response by decomposing it into atomic claims and answering polar yes/no questions, yielding a preference pair (y_w, y_l) where the score is the negative count of rejected claims. At inference, the aligned model's own DPO-derived reward (length-normalized log-ratio of policy to reference) selects the best response from N sampled candidates.

## Method

RLAIF-V introduces two key mechanisms for open-source feedback. First, a deconfounded response generation strategy samples multiple candidates under identical decoding parameters so that preferred/inferior pairs share linguistic style, forcing the model to learn trustworthiness differences rather than superficial patterns; a divide-and-conquer annotation pipeline then splits each response into atomic claims, converts them to polar questions, and aggregates yes/no confidence scores from an open-source MLLM into a response-level trustworthiness score. Second, the aligned model is trained iteratively with DPO, where feedback is regenerated each iteration from the updated policy to counteract distribution shift. For inference-time scaling, the paper exploits the implicit reward of the DPO-trained model (β·log π_θ/π_ref), applies length normalization to suppress the known bias toward shorter responses, and uses this self-feedback for best-of-N selection—demonstrating that a single aligned model can serve as a reward function to improve the trustworthiness of multiple other MLLMs.

## Results

RLAIF-V 7B (using LLaVA-NeXT 34B as labeler) reduces object hallucination on Object HalBench from 54.5% to 10.5% (80.7% relative reduction) and overall hallucination on MHumanEval from 67.1% to 44.5%. RLAIF-V 12B (self-alignment with OmniLMM 12B) further reduces object hallucination to 4.5% and overall hallucination to 35.6%, surpassing GPT-4V (13.6% and 45.9% respectively) by substantial margins. With best-of-N self-feedback at inference time, RLAIF-V 7B BoN achieves 6.8% object hallucination and RLAIF-V 12B BoN achieves 29.5% overall hallucination. On the discriminative AMBER benchmark, RLAIF-V 12B reaches 90.9% F1, exceeding GPT-4V's 87.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RLAIF-V 12B (self-alignment) decisively outperforms GPT-4V on all four trustworthiness benchmarks: Object HalBench (4.5% vs 13.6% response-level hallucination), MHumanEval (35.6% vs 45.9%), AMBER F1 (90.9% vs 87.4%), and RefoMB trust win-rate (58.1% vs 50.0%). It also surpasses the 34B LLaVA-NeXT labeler model that generated its feedback, achieving 4.5% vs 12.6% on Object HalBench and 35.6% vs 53.4% on MHumanEval. Among open-source baselines, RLAIF-V 7B beats RLHF-V (12.2% vs 10.5% on Object HalBench) and Silkie (27.1% vs 10.5%), while RLAIF-V 12B BoN further narrows the gap to GPT-4V on RefoMB overall win-rate (30.3% vs 50.0%). On helpfulness (MMStar), RLAIF-V 12B achieves 40.9% vs GPT-4V's 50.4%, indicating a trade-off where trustworthiness gains come at some cost to general capability, though the base OmniLMM (39.7%) is also below GPT-4V. The self-feedback BoN strategy provides consistent additional gains over the DPO-aligned model alone (e.g., 35.6% → 29.5% on MHumanEval for 12B), confirming the value of inference-time scaling with the learned reward.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLAIF paradigm (Lee et al., 2023) from text LLMs to multimodal models, but makes a critical departure: instead of distilling feedback from a proprietary teacher (GPT-4V), it demonstrates that open-source MLLMs of comparable capability can generate sufficient feedback through a deconfounded sampling and divide-and-conquer annotation strategy. The iterative feedback learning follows Guo et al. (2024), and the inference-time scaling leverages the DPO reward formulation (Rafailov et al., 2023) repurposed as a self-feedback signal for best-of-N selection (Snell et al., 2024). The key conceptual shift is from a 'superior teacher' paradigm to a 'peer feedback' paradigm, where the labeler need not be stronger than the student.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-alignment result—where a 12B model using itself as labeler surpasses GPT-4V on trustworthiness—suggests a path for open-source MLLMs to bootstrap their own reliability without proprietary API dependency, which is practically significant for the community. The generalizability experiments showing that feedback from one model improves others, and that the DPO reward transfers as a scoring function across architectures, strengthen the framework's broader utility. Open questions include whether the approach extends to more complex reasoning tasks (the authors acknowledge this as future work), whether iterative self-feedback degrades over many rounds, and whether the hallucination gains come at a hidden cost to compositional reasoning that current benchmarks don't capture.
