## Problem

Existing frequency-domain exposure correction methods rely on small convolutional kernels with local receptive fields, limiting their ability to model long-range dependencies across amplitude and phase spectra. Additionally, purely discriminative (regression-based) training cannot retrieve high-frequency details lost in severely under- or over-exposed regions, where pixel values are irreversibly degraded.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252504/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single exposure-error RGB image (under-exposed, over-exposed, or mixed) and produces a corrected RGB image of identical spatial dimensions. The network is a 4-level UNet with 7.5M parameters operating on patches up to 512×512. During inference, a compact 1×1×256 diffusion prior is generated from the error image alone via a 4-step denoising process, eliminating any dependency on ground truth.

## Method

OSMamba introduces an Omnidirectional Spectral Scanning (OS-Scan) mechanism that applies Mamba's S6 selective state space model in the Fourier domain, using four continuous scanning trajectories (row-wise, column-wise, positive diagonal, negative diagonal) over half the amplitude and phase spectra to capture diverse long-range spectral dependencies while exploiting central symmetry. The overall architecture is a 4-level UNet where each level contains an OS-SSB block combining OS-SSM (Amplitude Mamba + Phase Mamba with OS-Scan) and an Attention module, both followed by a Gated-DConv FFN. For detail restoration, a Dual-Domain Prior Generator (DDPG) is trained in two stages: Stage 1 jointly trains a Dual-Domain Prior Extractor (DDPE) with the UNet via L1 loss, then Stage 2 freezes DDPE as a teacher and trains a conditional diffusion model (two ReLU-activated linear layers) to generate a degradation-free prior from the error image alone, using knowledge distillation with an L1 loss on both the corrected image and the prior.

## Results

On the MSEC multi-exposure dataset, OSMamba achieves 23.78 dB PSNR and 0.8767 SSIM (average), outperforming the previous best LACT (23.57 dB / 0.8690) by 0.21 dB. On the SICE dataset, OSMamba achieves 22.82 dB PSNR and 0.7201 SSIM, a significant 1.01 dB improvement over the second-best CoTF (21.51 dB). On the mixed-exposure LCDP dataset, OSMamba reaches 24.53 dB PSNR and 0.8773 SSIM, surpassing CoTF (23.89 dB / 0.8581) by 0.64 dB in PSNR and 0.0192 in SSIM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OSMamba achieves state-of-the-art results across all three benchmarks, outperforming every compared method. On MSEC, it surpasses LACT (23.57 dB) and CoTF (23.44 dB) by 0.21 and 0.34 dB respectively in average PSNR, with the largest gap in over-exposure SSIM (0.8823 vs. CoTF's 0.8793). On SICE, the improvement is most dramatic: OSMamba gains 1.01 dB PSNR over CoTF (22.82 vs. 21.51) and 1.37 dB over LACT (21.45), particularly in the under-exposure setting (23.63 vs. 22.90 for CoTF). On the mixed-exposure LCDP dataset, OSMamba leads CoTF by 0.64 dB PSNR and 0.0192 SSIM, and LCDPNet by 1.29 dB PSNR. Older baselines such as MSEC (20.08 dB on MSEC), Zero-DCE (12.06 dB), and SCI (7.49 dB) are substantially outperformed, confirming that frequency-domain Mamba with generative prior guidance represents a clear advance over both spatial-domain CNNs and earlier Fourier-based methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

OSMamba sits at the intersection of three active research threads: frequency-domain exposure correction (FECNet, CoTF), state-space models for image restoration (MambaIR, FourierMamba, VMamba), and diffusion-based prior generation (DiffIR, Reti-Diff). It departs from prior frequency-domain Mamba work by introducing diagonal scanning trajectories (OS-Scan) to capture spectral dependencies beyond row/column interactions, and by exploiting the central symmetry of the Fourier spectrum to process only half the amplitude and phase maps. The dual-domain prior generator is a novel combination of knowledge distillation and conditional DDPM, where a teacher extractor (DDPE) trained with ground truth is distilled into a student generator (DDPG) that operates on the error image alone at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that combining global spectral modeling (via Mamba with omnidirectional scanning) and generative prior injection (via a compact diffusion model) yields consistent gains over either approach alone, particularly in extreme exposure conditions where regression-based methods fail to recover lost high-frequency details. The OS-Scan mechanism is a generalizable design choice for any frequency-domain SSM application, and the two-stage teacher-student training paradigm for diffusion priors could transfer to other restoration tasks. Limitations include the very short diffusion schedule (T=4 steps), reliance on paired training data, and evaluation limited to three static-image benchmarks; the authors note future exploration toward HDR reconstruction but do not address video or real-time deployment.
