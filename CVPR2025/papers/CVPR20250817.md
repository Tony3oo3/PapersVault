## Problem

Existing X-ray baggage security datasets are uni-modal with only simple category labels, failing to capture the nuanced context of concealed threats in cluttered scans, and lack representation of real-world sophisticated threats like IEDs and 3D-printed firearms. Generic vision-language models (GPT-4, Gemini, LLaVA-NeXT) hallucinate or miss threats when applied to X-ray imagery due to their lack of domain-specific exposure, and current detection methods are confined to closed-set paradigms that cannot generalize beyond predefined label spaces.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB X-ray baggage scan images (captured by airport security scanners) alongside natural-language text queries. Outputs are task-dependent: scene comprehension yields one or more threat category labels; referring threat localization and visual grounding produce bounding boxes as normalized {x_min, y_min, x_max, y_max} tuples in the [0,100] range embedded within text responses; VQA produces free-form text answers. The [refer] and [grounding] special tokens in the query signal which spatial-output format the model should produce, while scene comprehension and VQA require no such tokens.

## Method

STING-BEE extends the LLaVA architecture (CLIP ViT-L/14 encoder → two-layer MLP projector → Vicuna-v1.5 LLM) with two key innovations: specialized task-identification tokens ([refer], [grounding]) that let a single model switch between spatial-localization and text-only response modes, and a two-stage instruction-tuning pipeline. Stage 1 (multi-task threat instruction tuning) trains on 120,190 instruction pairs covering scene comprehension, referring expression, and VQA derived from the STCray dataset's 46,642 image-caption pairs generated via the STING protocol, which systematically varies threat position, angle, and progressive occlusion levels. Stage 2 (threat visual-grounded instruction tuning) adds 31,000 grounding-specific instructions augmented with ~25,000 CT-2-Xray synthetic projections to enhance spatial reasoning. Training uses LoRA (rank 64) on the LLM's query and value projection matrices with AdamW at lr=2e-5, one epoch per stage on two A100 GPUs.

## Results

On cross-domain scene comprehension (SIXray, PIDray, COMPASS-XP), STING-BEE achieves an F1 of 34.69 and mAP of 29.80, nearly doubling the best VLM baseline (MiniGPT-v2: F1 18.45). On visual grounding and referring threat localization across SIXray and PIDray, STING-BEE reaches 21.51% overall acc@0.25 (12.5% single-object, 4.8% multi-object, 8.9% referring), outperforming Florence-2 (12.60%) and MiniGPT-v2 (11.05%). On VQA across 39,194 questions, STING-BEE scores 52.81% overall accuracy versus 41.94% (LLaVA 1.5), 36.62% (MiniGPT-v2), and 32.27% (Florence-2). In cross-domain detection against vision-only models trained on STCray, STING-BEE achieves 8.7% acc@0.5 and 21.5% acc@0.25, surpassing SDANet (4.4%/8.6%) and PSN (3.2%/6.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STING-BEE outperforms all nine baselines across every evaluated task. Against general-purpose VLMs, it leads Florence-2 [60] by 20.5 points on VQA (52.81% vs 32.27%), 18.7 points on scene-comprehension F1 (34.69 vs 16.04), and 8.9 points on overall grounding acc@0.25 (21.51% vs 12.60%). It similarly dominates MiniGPT-v2 [13] (VQA 52.81 vs 36.62; F1 34.69 vs 18.45; grounding 21.51 vs 11.05) and LLaVA 1.5 [33] (VQA 52.81 vs 41.94; F1 34.69 vs 7.44). On the scene-comprehension task, CLIP [42] and LongCLIP [32] score only 2.83 and 10.01 F1 respectively, far below STING-BEE's 34.69. In the cross-domain detection setting (Table 7), where all models are trained on STCray and tested on SIXray+PIDray, STING-BEE (8.7% acc@0.5) more than doubles the best domain-specific detector SDANet [56] (4.4%) and PSN [49] (3.2%), and substantially outperforms generic detectors Faster R-CNN [46] (1.5%) and DETR [10] (1.3%). The consistent margin across all comparisons underscores the benefit of the STCray multimodal dataset and the STING protocol's structured concealment design.

## Relation to Prior Work

STING-BEE builds directly on the LLaVA architecture (CLIP ViT-L/14 encoder + MLP projector + Vicuna LLM), extending it with task-identification tokens ([refer], [grounding]) to unify multiple vision-language sub-tasks within a single model. The key conceptual departure is from the entire prior X-ray security literature, which is exclusively uni-modal and closed-set: all existing datasets (SIXray, PIDray, HiXray, etc.) provide only image labels or bounding boxes, and all prior methods operate within a fixed label space. The STING protocol introduces a systematic, occlusion-graded data generation pipeline that produces domain-aware captions—something no prior X-ray dataset or VLM application has attempted—bridging the gap between the structured annotation paradigm of detection datasets and the free-form language needed for instruction-tuned VLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STCray is the first multimodal X-ray baggage security dataset and includes real-world threats (IEDs, 3D-printed firearms) and strategic concealment scenarios that prior benchmarks entirely lack, making it a critical resource for the field. The finding that a VLM trained on a single scanner's data outperforms purpose-built cross-domain detection models (SDANet, PSN) on unseen scanners is notable, suggesting that language-grounded representations may be more transferable than purely visual features in this domain. However, absolute performance remains low (8.7% acc@0.5 for grounding, 34.69 F1 for scene comprehension), and the model struggles with severe occlusion and multi-threat scenes, leaving open whether VLM-based screening can reach operational reliability. The dataset and protocol are likely to become standard benchmarks, but the model itself is more of a proof-of-concept baseline than a deployable system.
