## Problem

Existing egocentric world models are typically restricted to a single domain (e.g., autonomous driving) and rely on egocentric-only controls that capture just the ego-agent's motion, making it difficult to generate diverse scenes with complex multi-agent interactions such as repositioning or inserting objects. This paper addresses the gap by building a generalizable, multimodal world model that supports fine-grained control over ego-motion, individual object dynamics, and human poses across multiple ego-vision domains (driving, human activities, drone navigation).

## Input / Output

GEM takes a single reference RGB frame, a metric 2D ego-trajectory (birds-eye-view positions), a sparse set of DINOv2 feature tokens (up to 32 per frame) placed at arbitrary spatial coordinates and time steps within zero-initialized feature maps, and rendered human skeleton images, and produces paired future RGB frames and depth maps. The model operates in the latent space of SVD's pretrained VAE (4-channel feature maps at 1/8 spatial resolution), with training at 320×576 and inference at 576×1024. The sparse token conditioning means the model must inpaint both spatially and temporally around the provided object cues, enabling insertion of entirely new objects not present in the reference frame.

## Method

GEM fine-tunes Stable Video Diffusion with three distinct conditioning pathways: ego-trajectories are injected via LoRA modules in cross-attention layers (global context), while sparse DINOv2 tokens and human pose features are processed by dedicated small networks (ObjectNet and PoseNet) and added directly to the UNet's input block outputs (spatially local control). A learned identity embedding mechanism associates tokens representing the same entity across time steps, disambiguating object movement from object insertion during inference. For long-horizon generation, GEM introduces a dynamic per-frame autoregressive noise schedule with initialization, autoregressive, and termination phases that progressively denoises frames in a sliding window, reinforced by a matching training-time noise distribution. Depth is generated jointly with RGB by concatenating both modalities at the input and adding a separate output projection layer, leveraging the fact that SVD's VAE reconstructs depth with negligible error. Training is two-stage (low-res for control learning, high-res for quality) and entirely unsupervised for object-level control, using random masking of DINOv2 tokens and optical-flow-based temporal translation during training.

## Results

On NuScenes, GEM achieves FID 10.5 and FVD 158.5, outperforming Vista (FVD 167.7) on temporal consistency while trailing slightly on FID (10.5 vs. 6.6). On OpenDV, GEM achieves FID 6.3 and FVD 131, beating Vista on FVD (131 vs. 163) and remaining competitive on FID (6.3 vs. 5.5). For long-horizon generation (150 frames), GEM maintains consistently lower FVD and FID than Vista across all evaluated durations (25–150 frames). In human evaluation on 15-second videos, GEM is strongly preferred over Vista: 75 vs. 23 votes for realism, 79 vs. 29 for visual quality, and 81 vs. 27 for temporal consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GEM is compared against seven autonomous driving world models. Against the closest competitor Vista, GEM wins on FVD for both NuScenes (158.5 vs. 167.7) and OpenDV (131 vs. 163) but trails on FID (10.5 vs. 6.6 on NuScenes; 6.3 vs. 5.5 on OpenDV), which the authors attribute to Vista's final-stage fine-tuning on NuScenes. GEM clearly dominates DriveGAN (FID 73.4, FVD 502.3), WoVoGen (FID 27.6, FVD 417.7), and DriveDreamer (FID 14.9, FVD 340.8) on both metrics. Against DriveDreamer-2 (FVD 105.1) and Drive-WM (FVD 122.7), GEM achieves better FID but slightly higher FVD, suggesting a trade-off between frame-level fidelity and temporal coherence. In long-horizon generation (150 frames), GEM outperforms Vista on both FID and FVD at every duration checkpoint, and human evaluators strongly prefer GEM's long videos over Vista's across realism, visual quality, and temporal consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

GEM builds directly on Stable Video Diffusion as its diffusion backbone and extends the sparse DINOv2 token inpainting paradigm from Davtyan et al. [14] into a multi-signal conditioning framework that also incorporates ego-trajectories (via LoRA cross-attention, following Vista [16]) and human pose skeletons (following [81]). It departs from prior ego-vision world models—primarily driving-focused systems like Vista, DriveDreamer, and GenAD—by generalizing to multiple ego-vision domains (driving, human egocentric, drone) and by adding object-level scene composition control and depth generation alongside RGB. The autoregressive per-frame noise schedule for long-horizon generation is adapted from progressive denoising ideas in [10, 68], and the depth modality reuses the SVD VAE following DepthCrafter [32].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GEM is the first ego-vision world model to simultaneously offer fine-grained control over ego-motion, individual object dynamics (including insertion of new objects), and human poses, while generating paired RGB and depth outputs across multiple domains from a single architecture. The introduction of the COM metric and the pseudo-labeling pipeline (Depth Anything V2 + DroidSLAM + DWPose) lower the barrier to building controllable world models on unlabeled data. Key open questions remain: long-horizon quality still degrades beyond ~150 frames, FID on Nuscenes lags Vista slightly (likely due to Vista's domain-specific fine-tuning), and it is unclear whether the DINOv2-based object control generalizes to domains with very different visual statistics or to non-rigid objects beyond humans. The fully open-sourced codebase, models, and 4000+ hour curated dataset make this a practical foundation for future ego-vision world model research.
