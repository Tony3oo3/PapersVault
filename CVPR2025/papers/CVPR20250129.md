## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting for driving scenes faces two intertwined challenges: (1) existing static-dynamic decomposition methods either depend on explicit 3D bounding box annotations or treat all Gaussians as dynamic, yielding incomplete separation; and (2) the sparse multi-view observations typical of autonomous driving cause 3DGS to overfit, producing floating ellipsoids that do not align with physical surfaces. No prior self-supervised method achieves both accurate decomposition and high-fidelity surface reconstruction without external 3D annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250129/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images from surround cameras (three frontal cameras on Waymo, two on KITTI), a LiDAR point cloud used for Gaussian initialization (600K points) and sparse depth supervision, and camera intrinsics/extrinsics for cross-view projection. It outputs a time-parameterized 4D Gaussian scene representation that supports real-time novel view rendering at ~40 FPS, along with per-Gaussian velocity attributes enabling static-dynamic separation, and rendered depth, normal, and velocity maps. Notably, the LiDAR provides only sparse supervision signals rather than dense geometry, and no 3D bounding boxes or motion annotations are required.

## Method

DeSiRe-GS employs a two-stage self-supervised pipeline. In Stage I, standard 3DGS is trained, and a frozen foundation model (FiT3D) extracts features from both rendered and ground-truth images; per-pixel cosine dissimilarity between these features is fed to a lightweight MLP decoder that predicts a dynamicness mask, exploiting the observation that 3DGS inherently fails to reconstruct moving objects (producing blur). In Stage II, the static Gaussians are reformulated as Periodic Vibration Gaussians (PVG) with time-dependent position and opacity, and the Stage I masks regularize the rendered 2D velocity map to suppress noisy zero-velocity outliers, effectively propagating 2D motion priors into the 3D Gaussian space. Geometric regularizations flatten 3D ellipsoids into 2D disks (minimizing the smallest scale axis), derive surface normals directly from the scale vector rather than appending separate normal parameters, and penalize oversized Gaussians. A temporal cross-view depth consistency loss projects static pixels between temporally adjacent views and enforces geometric agreement, mitigating overfitting in texture-less far regions.

## Results

On the Waymo Open Dataset, DeSiRe-GS achieves 33.61 PSNR / 0.919 SSIM / 0.204 LPIPS for image reconstruction and 29.75 PSNR / 0.878 SSIM / 0.213 LPIPS for novel view synthesis, outperforming the closest self-supervised 3DGS baseline PVG by ~1.1 PSNR. On KITTI, it reaches 33.94 PSNR (reconstruction) and 28.87 PSNR (novel view synthesis) with 0.04 LPIPS, again leading all compared methods. Rendering speed is approximately 36–41 FPS, slightly slower than vanilla 3DGS (63 FPS) and PVG (50 FPS) due to additional attribute rendering, but orders of magnitude faster than NeRF-based approaches (<0.1 FPS). In the OmniRe subset comparison (Table 2), DeSiRe-GS achieves 33.82 PSNR without any 3D bounding boxes, approaching OmniRe's 34.25 PSNR which relies on explicit box annotations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeSiRe-GS outperforms the closest self-supervised 3DGS baseline PVG by 1.15 PSNR on Waymo reconstruction (33.61 vs 32.46) and 1.44 PSNR on Waymo novel view synthesis (29.75 vs 28.11), with similar margins on KITTI (33.94 vs 32.83 recon; 28.87 vs 27.43 NVS). It also surpasses S3Gaussian in qualitative comparisons (Fig. 5), where S3Gaussian fails to produce satisfactory depth maps or effective static-dynamic decomposition. Against methods requiring 3D bounding boxes, DeSiRe-GS (33.82 PSNR recon, 31.49 PSNR NVS on the OmniRe subset) approaches OmniRe (34.25 / 32.57) while significantly outperforming StreetGS (29.08 / 28.54) and HUGS (28.26 / 27.65), all without any box annotations. All NeRF-based baselines (SUDS, EmerNeRF, NSG, Mars, S-NeRF) are substantially outperformed in both quality and speed, with DeSiRe-GS achieving 36–41 FPS versus <0.1 FPS for NeRF methods. The only method that slightly edges out DeSiRe-GS is OmniRe on the OmniRe subset, but OmniRe requires explicit 3D bounding box annotations that DeSiRe-GS avoids entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeSiRe-GS builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) as its static representation and Periodic Vibration Gaussian (PVG) as its dynamic representation, reformulating the static Gaussian model into time-dependent variables with minimal architectural changes. It departs from prior self-supervised decomposition methods (PVG, S3Gaussian) that treat all Gaussians as dynamic and rely on indirect motion supervision, instead exploiting the observation that 3DGS inherently fails to reconstruct dynamic regions, producing exploitable appearance discrepancies. It also incorporates geometric regularization ideas from 2DGS (flattening ellipsoids into disks) and introduces a temporal cross-view consistency loss, positioning itself between the annotation-heavy approaches (StreetGaussians, OmniRe, HUGS) and the weaker self-supervised baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving self-supervised static-dynamic decomposition with quality comparable to methods requiring expensive 3D bounding box annotations, which is critical for scalable autonomous driving scene understanding. The surface reconstruction quality (flattened Gaussians with normals derived from scale) opens the door to downstream geometric applications like simulation and planning. Limitations include the dependency on LiDAR for depth supervision, the two-stage training pipeline adding complexity, and rendering speed (~40 FPS) trailing pure 3DGS. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond driving to other unbounded dynamic scenes and whether the two-stage design can be unified into a single optimization.
