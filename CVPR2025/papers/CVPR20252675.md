## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves high-quality rendering but its anisotropic primitives are optimized for 2D appearance rather than faithful geometric structure, making direct 3D edge/curve extraction unreliable. Existing image-based 3D curve reconstruction methods (NEF, EMAP) require hours of training per scene, while point-cloud-based methods depend on accurate 3D scans that often miss sharp edges. The paper asks whether a modified Gaussian representation can serve as a lightweight 3D edge intermediary supervised purely by 2D edge maps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252675/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of calibrated multi-view RGB images (50 views at 800×800 for synthetic scenes; real multi-view captures for DTU and Replica) and produces 3D parametric curves stored as 3rd-order rational Bézier curves (four control points with weights per curve segment). 2D edge maps are extracted per view using PiDiNet and serve as the sole supervision signal—no 3D ground-truth edges or point clouds are required. The intermediate Spherical Gaussians (1k–5k primitives per scene) are also retained as an explicit 3D edge representation.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages. Stage 1 generates Spherical Gaussians by replacing the anisotropic covariance of standard 3DGS with a fixed isotropic radius (r₀=0.005), dropping spherical harmonics for a single grayscale channel, and initializing from a 50×50×50 grid rather than SfM points. Training uses a composite loss combining an edge-aware L2 term (balancing edge vs. non-edge pixels), a D-SSIM term, an opacity-color consistency loss to handle occluded edges across views, and a log-opacity regularization for implicit pruning; a two-phase schedule (3000 iterations each) alternates densification with refinement and applies aggressive opacity/color pruning between phases. Stage 2 (SGCR) first performs iterative line fitting via random endpoint sampling and Chamfer-distance optimization to segment the Gaussian cloud into coarse line endpoints, then globally optimizes 3rd-order rational Bézier curves (chosen over simple Bézier to fit circular edges) using a weighted Chamfer distance that up-weights high-opacity Gaussians plus an endpoint-proximity loss to encourage curve connectivity.

## Results

On ABC-NEF, SGCR achieves the best Chamfer Distance (0.0280), Precision (0.9546), Recall (0.9052), F-score (0.9260), and IoU (0.8615) across all five metrics. On ModelNet, it leads in CD (0.0264), Precision (0.9627), F-score (0.8952), and IoU (0.8164), though NerVE edges it on Recall (0.8017 vs. 0.8415—actually SGCR wins here too at 0.8415). In terms of efficiency, SGCR completes training in 87 seconds and reconstruction in 32 seconds per scene, roughly 50× faster than NEF (>1 h training, 119 s reconstruction) and EMAP (>2 h training, 40 s reconstruction).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGCR outperforms all four baselines on every metric for ABC-NEF, with the closest competitor being EMAP (CD 0.0291 vs. 0.0280; F-score 0.9210 vs. 0.9260; IoU 0.8473 vs. 0.8615). On ModelNet, SGCR leads in CD, Precision, F-score, and IoU, while NerVE achieves a slightly higher Recall (0.8017 vs. 0.8415—SGCR actually wins here as well at 0.8415). The point-cloud-based methods (RFEPS, NerVE) are notably weaker on ModelNet, where RFEPS drops to 0.5621 IoU, highlighting their sensitivity to input quality. Among image-based methods, NEF and EMAP require hours of training per scene (>1 h and >2 h respectively), whereas SGCR trains in 87 seconds—approximately 50× faster—while still achieving superior reconstruction quality. On real-world DTU and Replica scenes, SGCR produces cleaner, more complete edge reconstructions compared to NEF (which misses details on complex parts) and EMAP (which generates excessive redundant line segments).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework by deliberately removing its defining feature—anisotropic covariance—replacing it with fixed-radius isotropic 'Spherical Gaussians' to serve as an explicit geometric primitive for edges rather than a rendering primitive. It departs from prior image-based curve reconstruction methods (NEF, EMAP) that learn neural implicit fields requiring hours of training, and from point-cloud-based methods (RFEPS, NerVE) that require 3D input, by using only 2D edge-map supervision to optimize an explicit 3D representation. The SGCR extraction algorithm (iterative line fitting followed by global rational Bezier optimization with weighted Chamfer distance) is a new optimization pipeline not seen in prior curve reconstruction work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50× training speedup over implicit-field baselines (NEF, EMAP) makes 3D curve reconstruction practical for real-time or iterative pipelines, and the explicit Spherical Gaussian representation provides a natural bridge to downstream tasks like mesh reconstruction or shape analysis. However, the method inherits errors from the 2D edge detector (PiDiNet), requires calibrated multi-view input, and is limited to edge/curve extraction rather than full surface reconstruction. Open questions include whether Spherical Gaussians can be extended to represent surfaces or corners, how the method scales to very large or unstructured scenes, and whether the grid initialization and two-phase strategy generalize beyond the tested object categories.
