## Problem

Existing object-centric models (e.g., DINOSAUR, Slot Attention) decompose scenes into fixed-size slot vectors but offer no mechanism for a user to direct which specific object a given slot should represent. This lack of controllability means the model always produces its own arbitrary decomposition, preventing targeted extraction of instance-specific representations needed for tasks like instance-controllable generation or question-focused VQA.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251355/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image plus M free-form language queries (object category names or referring expressions) encoded into fixed-size vectors via LLM2Vec with LLaMA-3-8B, optionally augmented with center-of-mass coordinates embedded by a 2-layer MLP. It outputs N slot vectors where the first M are semantically bound to the queried objects and the remaining N−M capture unspecified scene parts, yielding a complete decomposition. Downstream, these slots feed into a Stable Diffusion U-Net for instance-controllable image generation or are inserted into question text for VQA.

## Method

CTRL-O builds on the DINOSAUR architecture (frozen DINOv2 ViT encoder → learnable transformer encoder → Slot Attention → MLP decoder) and introduces three mechanisms for language grounding. First, query-based slot initialization adds the language query embedding directly to the corresponding initial slot vector. Second, decoder conditioning concatenates each slot with its control query before the MLP patch decoder. Third and most critically, a Control Contrastive Loss (CCL) enforces grounding by computing attention-weighted DINO features (spatially aggregating encoder features with the final Slot Attention scores) and applying an InfoNCE-style contrastive objective between these weighted features and the query embeddings, using all batch queries as negatives (τ=0.1). The CCL is essential: without it, the model has no incentive to exploit the queries and binding fails. The model is trained for 300k steps on COCO (categories + center-of-mass) and Visual Genome (referring expressions) with batch size 128, using only language and point queries as weak supervision without direct mask supervision on Slot Attention.

## Results

On COCO object discovery, CTRL-O achieves the best FG-ARI of 47.5 (vs. 40.5 for DINOSAUR-MLP and 37.3 for SlotDiffusion), though its mBO of 27.2 is slightly below transformer/diffusion decoder variants (31.4–31.6). On zero-shot referring expression segmentation, CTRL-O with language+point queries (T+P) reaches 33.13 mIoU on RefCOCO testA and 30.58 on RefCOCO+ testA, outperforming Shatter & Gather (19.00/19.86) and MaskCLIP (18.69/18.93). On VQAv2, CTRL-O with coupling achieves 60.25% accuracy, surpassing CLIP (58.64%), DINOv2 (58.12%), and DINOSAUR (57.66%). For instance-controllable image generation, CTRL-O-SD attains a lower FID (25.20 vs. 26.20 for Stable-LSD) and higher CLIP-I (0.78 vs. 0.71 for SD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On object discovery (COCO), CTRL-O wins on FG-ARI (47.5) over all unsupervised baselines—DINOSAUR-MLP (40.5), DINOSAUR-TF (34.1), Stable-LSD (35.0), and SlotDiffusion (37.3)—but trails on mBO (27.2 vs. 31.4–31.6), which the authors attribute to the MLP decoder. On referring expression segmentation, CTRL-O with point+language queries (T+P) substantially outperforms Shatter & Gather (28.2 vs. 21.8 on RefCOCO val; 33.13 vs. 19.00 on testA) and MaskCLIP (19.45 val), while the language-only variant (T) ties SaG on RefCOCO val (21.80) but leads on testA (20.10 vs. 19.00). GroupViT and MaskCLIP are far behind across all splits, confirming that slot-attention-based methods with direct language conditioning are more effective for grounding. On VQA, CTRL-O with coupling (60.25%) beats CLIP (58.64%), DINOv2 (58.12%), and DINOSAUR (57.66%), with the coupling mechanism providing a ~1-point boost unique to CTRL-O. For image generation, CTRL-O-SD achieves a lower FID (25.20 vs. 26.20) and higher instance fidelity (CLIP-I 0.78 vs. 0.71 for SD), demonstrating that language-controllable slots enable better instance-specific generation than uncontrolled slot-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

CTRL-O builds directly on DINOSAUR's Slot Attention architecture over frozen DINOv2 features, but departs from the unsupervised OCL paradigm by introducing language-conditioned slot initialization and a contrastive grounding loss. Unlike prior conditioning approaches (SAVi's bounding boxes, CoSA's learned vectors) that are limited to synthetic data or fixed conditioning forms, CTRL-O handles free-form natural language queries on real-world scenes. It also contrasts with post-hoc language-to-slot assignment methods (e.g., Shatter & Gather) by integrating language directly into the slot extraction process, so the query influences which objects are discovered rather than merely labeling them afterward.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making object-centric representations queryable, CTRL-O unlocks instance-specific downstream applications (image generation, VQA) that were previously intractable with fixed, uncontrolled slot decompositions. However, the grounding problem remains far from solved: even a fully supervised upper bound achieves only ~71% binding hits, and VQA accuracy (60.25%) lags well behind LLM-based SOTA (>80%). Open questions include whether the contrastive grounding signal scales to open-vocabulary or 3D settings, and whether the MLP decoder's coarser masks (lower mBO) can be addressed without sacrificing the controllability mechanism.
