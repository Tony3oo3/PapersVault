## Problem

Existing deep registration networks (VoxelMorph, GradICON, and most convolution-based approaches) only exhibit [U,U] equivariance, meaning they preserve correspondences only when both input images undergo the same deformation. This weaker property causes them to fail when images are independently translated or rotated, a common scenario in clinical practice (e.g., differing fields of view in abdominal CT). The paper asks how to achieve the stronger [W,U] equivariance—preserving correspondences under independent deformations of each image—by construction rather than by loss-based enforcement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250029/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of 3D medical images (moving and fixed) resampled to a common grid (e.g., 175×175×175 for abdomen, with the coarse registration step operating at 43×43×43) and produces a dense deformation field (diffeomorphism) mapping the moving image onto the fixed image. The deformation is structured as a composition of a coarse coordinate-attention step followed by three refinement steps at increasing resolutions, mirroring the GradICON multi-resolution pyramid. Optionally, 50 steps of instance optimization can be applied at inference to further refine the transform on the specific test pair.

## Method

The core architectural contribution is a coordinate attention layer (Ξ_θ) in which the value vectors of a standard attention block are the voxel coordinates themselves, so the attention output computes the center of mass of each query's attention mask. This layer is paired with a translation-equivariant convolutional feature encoder (using dilated convolutions at a single resolution with zero-padding to suppress boundary effects), and the paper proves the combination is [W,U] equivariant to integer translations. The full CARL architecture composes Ξ_θ as the first (coarsest) step with three standard displacement-predicting U-Net refinement layers (Ψ₁–Ψ₃) via the TwoStep operator, and the paper proves that if the first step is [W,U] equivariant and all subsequent steps are [U,U] equivariant, the overall composition inherits [W,U] equivariance. Training is fully unsupervised, using LNCC image similarity with an initial diffusion regularizer (1,500 steps) followed by the GradICON gradient inverse-consistency regularizer (100,000 steps), and rotation equivariance can be added empirically via SO(3) data augmentation with a modified diffusion loss.

## Results

On the challenging Abdomen1K dataset, CARL achieves 75.7% mean DICE (77.2% with instance optimization), dramatically outperforming GradICON (49.6% / 71.0%) and VoxelMorph (59.3%). On HCP brain registration, CARL reaches 79.6% DICE (80.4% with IO), matching or exceeding all baselines including EasyReg (78.8%) and KeyMorph (67.2%). On the DirLab lung benchmark, CARL achieves 1.88 mTRE (1.34 with IO), competitive with SOTA methods PTVReg and RRN (both 0.83 mTRE) that use challenge-specific hyperparameter tuning. On the Learn2Reg Abdomen CT-CT test set, CARL attains 50% DICE (56% with IO), outperforming other unsupervised approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Abdomen1K, CARL achieves a dramatic 26.1-point DICE improvement over GradICON (75.7% vs. 49.6%) and 16.4 points over VoxelMorph (59.3%), the largest gap in the paper, attributable to [W,U] equivariance handling large translational misalignments. On HCP, CARL (79.6%) outperforms the equivariant baselines EasyReg (78.8%) and KeyMorph (67.2%), and with IO (80.4%) edges out ConstrICON(IO) (80.1%) and GradICON(IO) (80.2%). On DirLab, CARL (1.88 mTRE) is competitive with GradICON (1.93) and ConstrICON (2.03) but trails PTVReg and RRN (both 0.83 mTRE), which the authors note use hyperparameters tuned specifically for the 10 challenge cases. On the Learn2Reg Abdomen CT-CT test set, CARL (50% DICE) outperforms other unsupervised methods like corrField (49%) and PDD-Net (49%) but falls short of supervised approaches like ConvexAdam (69%) and LapIRN (67%). The paper also demonstrates that CARL's equivariance provides out-of-distribution robustness: when test images are shifted by 200 pixels, displacement-predicting networks fail while CARL maintains performance, and CARL{ROT} handles arbitrary rotations that cause GradICON's DICE to drop sharply.

## Relation to Prior Work

CARL builds directly on GradICON's multi-step, multi-resolution registration architecture (U-Net refinement layers, LNCC + gradient inverse consistency loss, TwoStep composition) and VoxelMorph's displacement-prediction formulation, but replaces the first (coarsest-resolution) displacement-predicting U-Net with a coordinate attention module Ξ_θ that is [W,U] equivariant to translation by construction. The paper's key theoretical departure is formalizing the distinction between [W,U] and [U,U] equivariance for registration algorithms and proving that in a multi-step composition, only the first step requires [W,U] equivariance while subsequent steps need only [U,U] equivariance. This positions CARL as a principled alternative to keypoint-based equivariant methods (EasyReg, KeyMorph, SAME++) and equivariance-loss approaches (warp consistency, CoMIR), achieving equivariance architecturally rather than through auxiliary penalties or learned keypoints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is most visible in abdomen registration, where large inter-subject translational differences and varying fields of view cause standard unsupervised methods to fail; CARL's [W,U] equivariance enables registration of raw scans without registration-specific preprocessing, yielding a ~26-point DICE improvement over GradICON. The clean compositional theorem (first step [W,U] + subsequent steps [U,U] → overall [W,U]) provides a general recipe for building equivariant multi-step pipelines, and the coordinate attention mechanism is simple enough to adopt in other registration architectures. Open questions include extending formal equivariance beyond integer translations to continuous transforms, scaling the O(n⁶) attention cost to higher resolutions, and whether the small-spatial-support assumption on attention masks holds reliably across all anatomical regions. The 209-second instance optimization runtime remains a practical barrier for clinical deployment.
