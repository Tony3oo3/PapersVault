## Problem

Existing pedestrian trajectory prediction methods predominantly model only direct (first-order) interactions between a target agent and its immediate neighbors, neglecting the indirect, higher-order influences that propagate through neighbor-to-neighbor interactions. Additionally, generative approaches lack explicit guidance of trajectory distributions (limiting interpretability), and sequential decoding of future timesteps accumulates errors while reducing inference efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252227/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 8 frames of historical 2D positions and velocities for the target agent and all neighbors, augmented with inter-agent distances, velocity angles, and a novel 'final predicted distance' e_ij computed by extrapolating both agents along their tangent velocities. Output is 8 frames of future 2D positions generated as multi-modal predictions (best-of-20 reported), produced simultaneously across all future timesteps via a parallel KAN-based optimizer rather than autoregressively.

## Method

SocialMOIF fuses multi-order social intentions through a two-layer attention architecture: a first-order layer encodes direct target-neighbor interactions (using distances, angles, and tangent-extrapolated distances), while a higher-order layer captures neighbor-neighbor interaction matrices via M=6 parallel attention subspaces; these are combined with learnable influence factors η_m to produce a fused intention vector I_i. A trajectory distribution approximator then uses I_i as a lower bound and the future trajectory embedding B_i as an upper bound (inspired by the squeeze theorem) to explicitly constrain latent variable distributions, with an RNN updating latent states across timesteps for explicit supervision. For efficient inference, a global trajectory optimizer—introducing Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs) to trajectory prediction for the first time—aggregates all future positions into a single vector and applies L=3 KAN layers to produce the entire prediction horizon in one parallel pass. Training is supervised by a composite loss combining per-step distance error, a direction (angle) loss between consecutive predicted and ground-truth displacement vectors, and a KL term aligning the training and inference latent distributions.

## Results

On the ETH/UCY average, SocialMOIF achieves ADE 0.13 m and FDE 0.18 m, outperforming the strongest baseline E-V2-Net-SC (0.15/0.20) by 13.3% and 10.0% respectively. On the NBA dataset, where agent interactions are most complex, the gains are largest: ADE drops to 0.34 m (Rebound) and 0.30 m (Scores), reducing errors by 37.0% and 34.8% over the prior SOTA. On SDD, SocialMOIF reaches 0.17/0.24 m, beating NSP-SFM (0.21/0.34) by 19.1% ADE and 29.4% FDE. On NuScenes, the method achieves the best ADE (0.92 m) but trails Agent-former slightly on FDE (1.56 vs. 1.47 m).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SocialMOIF outperforms all 16 baselines on the ETH/UCY average (0.13/0.18 vs. 0.15/0.20 for E-V2-Net-SC), though on the Eth sub-scenario specifically, E-V2-Net-SC retains a slight edge (0.23/0.30 vs. 0.26/0.31) due to weaker neighbor interactions in that setting. The advantage is most pronounced on NBA, where complex multi-agent interactions dominate: SocialMOIF reduces ADE by 37.0% (Rebound) and 34.8% (Scores) over E-V2-Net-SC, and by 46.0% over GroupNet+CVAE on Rebound. On SDD, the method beats the strongest baseline NSP-SFM by 19.1% ADE and 29.4% FDE, and surpasses E-V2-Net-SC by 26.1% ADE. On NuScenes, SocialMOIF achieves the best ADE (0.92 m) but Agent-former edges it out on FDE (1.47 vs. 1.56 m), suggesting the global KAN optimizer slightly over-corrects endpoint positions in the autonomous-driving domain. Overall, the multi-order intention fusion and direction-aware loss yield consistent gains across all five datasets, with the largest improvements in high-interaction scenarios (NBA, SDD) and more modest but still positive gains in lower-interaction settings (ETH, NuScenes).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the attention-based social interaction modeling lineage (Social LSTM, AgentFormer, V2-Net-SC) by explicitly separating first-order (direct, dominant) from higher-order (indirect, secondary) intention interactions, departing from Kim et al.'s [16] equal-weight N-order treatment that the authors argue dilutes the primary neighbor-to-target signal. The trajectory distribution approximator builds on VAE-style reparameterization [17] used in generative trajectory predictors (Trajectron++, MANTRA), but introduces a squeeze-theorem-inspired bounding mechanism using both historical intention (lower bound) and future ground-truth features (upper bound) to constrain the latent space. The global trajectory optimizer is the first application of Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks [23, 24] to trajectory prediction, replacing the standard sequential RNN decoding with a parallel, globally-optimized output stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-order fusion framework addresses a genuine gap: most prior methods model only direct neighbor-to-target interactions, ignoring the cascading influence of neighbor-to-neighbor interactions on the target's intention. The KAN-based parallel optimizer tackles the practical problem of sequential error accumulation in autoregressive trajectory decoding, which is particularly relevant for safety-critical applications like autonomous driving. However, the paper does not discuss scalability to very dense scenes (the O(N_n²) attention in the higher-order layer), the computational overhead of the KAN optimizer versus a simple MLP, or whether the squeeze-theorem approximation remains valid when future ground-truth features are unavailable at test time. Open questions include whether the direction-distance loss generalizes to non-pedestrian agents (vehicles, drones) and whether the multi-order hierarchy can be extended beyond two levels without combinatorial explosion.
