## Problem

Existing egocentric whole-body motion estimation methods are either body-only (ignoring hands) or rely on non-real-time, acausal iterative diffusion refinement to capture body-hand correlations, making them unsuitable for live VR/AR applications. No prior method achieves both real-time, fully causal inference and high-fidelity whole-body (body + hands) motion from egocentric images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250131/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a temporal sequence of stereo egocentric images from down-facing head-mounted cameras, along with per-frame camera poses (rotation and translation) and 2D keypoint estimates with uncertainty scores extracted from the images. It outputs a sequence of 3D whole-body keypoints (body and hands) at each timestep, which can be converted to drivable meshes or photorealistic avatars via an optional inverse kinematics step. Optionally, a small set of 10 example 3D poses of the target identity (obtained from a brief monocular photo registration) is used as an identity prior.

## Method

REWIND employs a cascaded body-hand denoising diffusion scheme where body motion is sampled first and hand motion is then conditioned on the estimated 3D upper body motion, providing a fast feed-forward approximation of body-hand correlation without iterative refinement. Both specialist models use a causal relative-temporal Transformer that applies Rotary Positional Encoding (RoPE) within a fixed temporal window of past frames, making the architecture fully causal and invariant to total sequence length. A diffusion distillation step using Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) loss compresses the multi-step DDPM teacher into a single-step student, achieving >30 FPS inference on an A100 GPU. For identity conditioning, a set of example poses is encoded via a shared MLP and max-pooling, then injected into the network through AdaIN, which empirically outperforms alternative identity priors such as height, SMPL shape parameters, or bone lengths.

## Results

On the ColossusEgo dataset, REWIND achieves 53.83 mm MPJPE (body) and 21.18 mm MPJPE (hand), outperforming EgoWholeMocap (62.49 / 25.67) and EgoPoseFormer (64.01 / 33.29) across all metrics. With exemplar-based identity conditioning, body MPJPE drops further to 48.45 mm (estimated exemplars) or 38.99 mm (ground-truth exemplars), and PA-MPJPE (body) improves from 41.42 to 33.15 mm. On UnrealEgo, REWIND achieves 37.23 mm body MPJPE and 20.45 mm hand MPJPE, again leading both baselines on every metric. Inference runs at 32 ms per frame on a single A100 GPU (vs. 2576 ms for EgoWholeMocap), enabling real-time >30 FPS operation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REWIND outperforms both baselines on every metric across both datasets. Against EgoWholeMocap [49] (the first egocentric whole-body method, which uses iterative diffusion-based refinement), REWIND reduces body MPJPE by 8.66 mm on ColossusEgo (53.83 vs. 62.49) and by 11.87 mm on UnrealEgo (37.23 vs. 49.10), while also cutting inference time from 2576 ms to 32 ms. Against EgoPoseFormer [51] (a per-frame body-only estimator extended to whole-body), REWIND achieves a 10.18 mm body MPJPE reduction on ColossusEgo (53.83 vs. 64.01) and a 16.51 mm reduction on UnrealEgo (37.23 vs. 53.74). The gap is particularly large on hand metrics: REWIND's hand MPJPE of 21.18 mm on ColossusEgo is 4.49 mm better than EgoWholeMocap and 12.11 mm better than EgoPoseFormer. The paper attributes EgoWholeMocap's weaker alignment to its use of an unconditional motion prior that can drift from input observations, and EgoPoseFormer's limitations to the absence of temporal context. With exemplar-based identity conditioning, REWIND further narrows the gap, achieving 48.45 mm body MPJPE (estimated exemplars) or 38.99 mm (ground-truth exemplars) on ColossusEgo, surpassing all other identity priors tested (height, shape parameters, bone lengths).

## Relation to Prior Work

REWIND builds directly on EgoWholeMocap, the first egocentric whole-body motion estimation method, but fundamentally departs from its acausal, iterative diffusion-based refinement by replacing it with a causal, one-step cascaded body-hand denoising diffusion. The causal relative-temporal Transformer adapts Rotary Positional Encoding (RoPE) with windowed attention to achieve length-invariant, fully causal temporal modeling, contrasting with the vanilla self-attention and absolute positional encoding used in prior motion diffusion models. The exemplar-based identity conditioning repurposes a set-encoding strategy from face image representation learning (Universal Facial Encoding) to the motion estimation domain, and the single-step inference relies on Score Distillation Sampling adapted from text-to-3D generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

REWIND is the first method to achieve real-time (>30 FPS, 32 ms) and fully causal egocentric whole-body motion estimation, making it directly applicable to live AR/VR avatar driving where the prior SOTA required 2576 ms and future information. The introduction of ColossusEgo (2.8M frames, 500 identities) addresses a critical gap in benchmarking for this task, and the finding that exemplar-based identity conditioning outperforms standard priors (height, SMPL shape, bone lengths) opens a new direction for identity-aware motion estimation. The acknowledged limitation of occasional self-penetrations and the performance gap between one-step and multi-step inference (Row F vs. G in Table 2) suggest room for further refinement, particularly in balancing speed and fidelity.
