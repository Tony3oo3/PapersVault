## Problem

Automated oral anomaly detection from panoramic dental X-rays lacks a standardized, hospital-scale benchmark and faces two compounding technical challenges: multi-scale variations (subtle small lesions like caries versus large structures like wisdom teeth) and severe class imbalance (common anomalies like dental fillings vastly outnumber rare ones like dental implants). No prior publicly available multi-label dataset or dedicated detection framework has addressed both challenges simultaneously for oral radiography.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250741/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single panoramic dental X-ray image resized to 1333×800 pixels and produces bounding boxes with class labels for up to nine anomaly types (apical periodontitis, dental caries, wisdom teeth, missing teeth, dental fillings, root canal fillings, dental implants, porcelain crowns, ceramic bridges). Each image may contain multiple overlapping anomalies, and the evaluation uses standard AP metrics across IoU thresholds 0.5–0.95, with size-stratified (S/M/L) and frequency-stratified (rare/common/frequent) breakdowns.

## Method

MOQAM is a DETR-based detector (built on Co-DINO with ResNet-50) augmented by two modules. The Distribution-IoU Region Proposal Network (DI-RPN) performs personalized one-to-many sample assignment in an auxiliary head: a Distribution-aware mechanism computes KL divergence between Gaussian effective receptive fields and ground-truth boxes to prioritize small-object positives, while an IoU-aware mechanism applies a dynamic threshold t_img = max(t_b, t_b + k·(√(w·h)/A_s)^p) that lowers the IoU requirement for smaller targets. The Class-Balanced Spherical Contrastive Regularization (CB-SCR) loss maps features to a unit hypersphere and equalizes per-class gradient contributions by averaging similarities within each class and incorporating class prototypes, enforcing a regular simplex configuration that prevents majority classes from dominating the feature geometry. The total loss combines DI-RPN classification and regression losses with α·L_CB-SCR (α=0.5), trained for 12 epochs with AdamW at lr 2e-4.

## Results

On the newly introduced OralXrays-9 dataset, MOQAM achieves 73.5% AP, surpassing the best CNN-based method (CFINet, 69.8%) by 3.7% and the best Transformer-based method (Salience-DETR, 71.0%) by 2.5%. On the SODA-D small-object benchmark, MOQAM reaches 33.1% AP, outperforming Co-DINO (32.2%) by 0.9%, with notable gains on extremely small (APeS: 16.1% vs. 15.3%) and relatively small (APrS: 29.4% vs. 28.4%) objects. On the class-imbalanced LVIS dataset, MOQAM achieves 30.9% AP, exceeding Co-DINO (29.8%) by 1.1%, with the largest margin on rare categories (APr: 14.4% vs. 13.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOQAM outperforms all 13 baselines across all three evaluation datasets. On OralXrays-9, it leads the best CNN method CFINet (69.8% AP) by 3.7% and the strongest Transformer baselines Salience-DETR (71.0%) and Co-DINO (70.9%) by 2.5% and 2.6% respectively, with the largest relative gains on small objects (APS: 21.2% vs. 20.1% for CFINet). On SODA-D, MOQAM edges out Co-DINO (32.2% AP) by 0.9%, with consistent advantages on extremely small (APeS: 16.1% vs. 15.3%) and relatively small (APrS: 29.4% vs. 28.4%) categories, confirming the effectiveness of the DI-RPN's scale-aware assignment. On LVIS, MOQAM surpasses Co-DINO (29.8% AP) by 1.1%, with the most pronounced improvement on rare classes (APr: 14.4% vs. 13.5%, +0.9%) and frequent classes (APf: 40.2% vs. 39.1%, +1.1%), validating the CB-SCR module's class-balancing mechanism. Notably, MOQAM uses the same 66M parameter count as Co-DINO, indicating the gains come from the proposed mining and regularization strategies rather than additional model capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

The MOQAM framework is built directly on the DETR query-based detection paradigm and extends Co-DINO's one-to-many collaborative denoising assignment strategy to generate auxiliary positive queries for multi-scale targets. Its DI-RPN module repurposes the effective receptive field (ERF) Gaussian modeling from Luo et al. and formulates sample assignment via KL-divergence between ERF and ground-truth Gaussians, while CB-SCR adapts the hyperspherical space formulation from Learning with Symmetric Loss (LWS) into a class-balanced contrastive objective. Conceptually, the paper positions itself at the intersection of multi-scale detection (addressing small-object recall) and class-imbalanced detection (addressing minority-class underrepresentation), combining these two previously separate lines of work into a unified query-mining pipeline tailored to dental radiography.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The primary practical contribution is the OralXrays-9 benchmark (12,688 images, 84,113 annotations, 9 anomaly classes), which is the first publicly available hospital-scale multi-object panoramic X-ray dataset and fills a notable gap in dental AI research where prior work relied on small or proprietary collections. The method's gains over strong Transformer baselines (2.5% AP on OralXrays-9, 1.1% on LVIS) are solid but incremental, and the techniques (KL-divergence ERF matching, hyperspherical contrastive regularization) are individually well-motivated rather than paradigm-shifting. Open questions include whether the DI-RPN's Gaussian ERF modeling generalizes beyond dental X-rays to other medical modalities with extreme scale variation, and whether the class-balanced spherical regularization can be decoupled from the specific auxiliary-head architecture to benefit broader DETR variants. Clinical deployment considerations—radiation dose variability, scanner heterogeneity, and regulatory validation—remain unaddressed.
