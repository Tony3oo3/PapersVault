## Problem

Traditional text-based person retrieval assumes a single-shot text query fully captures the user's search intent, but in practice such queries are often incomplete, leading to unsatisfactory retrieval. Two critical gaps prevent a conversational alternative: no dialogue-image paired dataset exists for this task, and existing text encoders (BERT, CLIP) are pretrained on single-text corpora and struggle with the longer, sparser structure of multi-round dialogues.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252694/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The query is a multi-round dialogue (1–17 rounds, averaging 7.7 rounds and 114.4 words) formatted as an instruction-style sequence with a system message prepended for Llama 3, while the gallery consists of person images encoded by Swin Transformer. At inference, the model can operate on any prefix of the dialogue (simulating partial interaction), and retrieval is evaluated by Rank-K and mAP over the gallery. The dataset ChatPedes contains 37,128 images from 12,003 identities, each paired with two LLM-generated dialogues.

## Method

DiaNA employs Llama 3 as the dialogue encoder (extracting the EOS token embedding) and Swin Transformer as the image encoder, each followed by a linear projector into a shared space. The key architectural contribution is a pair of Adaptive Attribute Refiner (AAR) modules that use a set of K learnable attribute queries to perform cross-attention over the dialogue and visual token sequences respectively, bottlenecking sparse conversational information and redundant visual features into aligned attribute embeddings; the two AARs share parameters to reduce the modality gap. Training combines N-ITC and SDM contrastive losses applied at both the global (projected embeddings) and local (attribute embeddings) levels. A dialogue-specific augmentation, Random Round Retaining (R3), randomly truncates the dialogue to its first n rounds during training to improve generalization across varying dialogue lengths. The ChatPedes dataset is constructed by prompting Llama 3 and InternVL to generate dialogues from CUHK-PEDES captions, then cleaned via a vote-based ensemble of LLM evaluators that discard rounds classified as contradictory, hallucinatory, or repeated.

## Results

On ChatPedes (with data cleaning), DiaNA achieves 75.67% R-1, 90.68% R-5, 94.71% R-10, and 66.89% mAP, substantially outperforming all five TPR baselines. The largest gap appears at R-1, where DiaNA leads the strongest baseline RDE (52.77%) by 22.9 percentage points, indicating that dialogue-specific modeling is critical even for single-round queries. Performance gaps narrow slightly as dialogue rounds increase (R-10: 94.71% vs. 77.47%), but DiaNA maintains a consistent 17–23 point advantage across all retrieval depths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All five baselines are originally designed for single-shot text-based person retrieval (TPR) and are adapted to ChatPedes by treating multi-round dialogues as a single long-form text input. DiaNA outperforms every baseline by a wide margin on all metrics: at R-1, it surpasses the strongest CLIP-based baseline RDE (52.77%) by 22.9 points and the MALS-based AUL (42.01%) by 33.7 points. The CLIP-pretrained methods (IRRA, TBPS-CLIP, RDE) consistently outperform the MALS-pretrained ones (APTM, AUL), suggesting that large-scale web pretraining helps, but none can close the gap to DiaNA. The baselines' performance saturates after the first few dialogue rounds (Fig. 6), whereas DiaNA continues to improve as more rounds are added, confirming that its Llama 3 dialogue encoder and adaptive attribute refiners are specifically suited to the structured, multi-turn nature of the query. Notably, DiaNA's R-1 score (75.67%) already exceeds the best baseline's full-dialogue R-10 score (77.47%), demonstrating strong generalization even when the dialogue is truncated to a single holistic description.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established text-based person retrieval (TPR) paradigm, which assumes a single-shot text query fully captures search intent, by introducing chat-based person retrieval (ChatPR) where multi-round dialogues serve as the query. Conceptually, it bridges two research threads: interactive cross-modal retrieval (visual dialogue, relevance/difference feedback) and LLM-based data generation, repurposing Llama 3's dialogue comprehension as a cross-modal encoder rather than a generative tool. The DiaNA framework extends CLIP-style contrastive alignment by inserting adaptive attribute refiner modules with learnable queries on both branches, drawing on the query-based information bottleneck idea from vision transformers, while the R3 augmentation strategy is a novel dialogue-specific analogue of text truncation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ChatPedes is the first dialogue-image paired benchmark for person retrieval, and its construction via LLMs (with >25% of generated rounds requiring removal) highlights a broader concern about the reliability of LLM-synthesized training data in cross-modal tasks. The very large performance gap over TPR baselines (≈23 points at R-1) is partly attributable to an architectural mismatch—baselines designed for short single-shot text are forced to process long dialogues as flat text—so the comparison, while informative, may overstate the methodological advantage. Open questions include whether the LLM-generated dialogues faithfully model real human conversational patterns, whether the approach generalizes beyond the CUHK-PEDES-derived gallery, and how the system performs under truly interactive (non-simulated) user behavior with ambiguous or contradictory responses.
