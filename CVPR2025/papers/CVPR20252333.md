## Problem

Action recognition models are known to rely heavily on background cues rather than the behaviour of interest, yet this shortcut learning has received limited attention in the wildlife domain, largely due to a lack of datasets explicitly designed to study its effects. Existing approaches assess background influence through synthetic modifications (masking, in-painting) whose impact on training is largely unknown, and no prior dataset provides real-world paired foreground-background videos from the same camera location to enable direct in-distribution and out-of-distribution evaluation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 15-second camera trap video clips (1280×720, 30 fps) sampled to 16×256×256 spatio-temporal tensors, with each foreground clip (containing a chimpanzee) paired with a temporally closest background clip (no chimpanzee) from the same camera. Output is a 14-class multi-hot binary vector indicating the presence or absence of each behaviour class, evaluated via micro and mean Average Precision. The dataset provides two evaluation views—overlapping (shared camera locations) and disjoint (mutually exclusive locations)—to separately measure in-distribution and OOD performance.

## Method

The paper introduces the PanAf-FGBG dataset (5,070 foreground-background pairs from 389 camera locations across 14 national parks) and establishes baselines using ResNet-50 (2D/3D) and MViT-V2 initialised from Kinetics-400 pre-training, fine-tuned with SGD or AdamW over 300 epochs. The key technical contribution is a latent-space background neutralisation that computes z^(F−B) = z^F − (1−α)·z^B, where foreground and background features are extracted by the same encoder and α is a modulation parameter scheduled linearly or exponentially from 0 to 1 over training, yielding +5.42% mAP (3D-R50) and +3.75% mAP (MViT-V2) on OOD data. The paper also quantifies the effect of background duration by concatenating λT background frames to foreground videos, revealing that 3D convolutions are more sensitive to increasing background proportions than multi-scale transformers, and that MViT-V2 becomes highly sensitive to background frames specifically under OOD evaluation. A dual-stream fusion model combining frozen FG-only and BG-only features via a trainable MLP further demonstrates that explicitly learned background features provide orthogonal information beyond what the FG-only model captures.

## Results

On the PanAf-FGBG dataset, background-only (BG-only) models achieve 65–74% of foreground-only (FG-only) performance across all architectures, confirming that environment alone is a strong predictor of chimpanzee behaviour. The best FG-only model (MViT-V2) reaches 75.44% uAP / 51.54% mAP on D^overlap and 70.50% uAP / 35.29% mAP on D^disjoint. The proposed latent-space background neutralisation with linear scheduling yields the largest OOD gains: +8.95% uAP and +5.42% mAP for 3D R50, and +1.14% uAP and +3.75% mAP for MViT-V2 on D^disjoint. MViT-V2 exhibits the lowest background-to-foreground ratio (0.70 uAP, 0.68 mAP on D^disjoint), indicating it relies least on static cues.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against external action-recognition methods; instead it compares internal configurations to isolate background effects. BG-only models consistently reach 65–74% of FG-only performance (never below 0.70 uAP / 0.65 mAP ratio), and on D^disjoint the 2D R50 BG-only model even slightly exceeds its FG-only counterpart (45.80 vs 44.67 uAP), showing the weakest architecture is almost entirely background-driven. Among architectures, MViT-V2 outperforms 3D R50 by +12.76 uAP on D^disjoint (70.50 vs 57.74) while having the lowest background-to-foreground ratio, indicating transformers are more invariant to background shifts. For the proposed latent-space neutralisation, embedding-space subtraction with linear scheduling outperforms input-space subtraction (F−B) for 3D R50 (+5.42% mAP vs −5.84% mAP on D^disjoint) and MViT-V2 (+3.75% mAP vs −3.78% mAP), confirming that pixel-level subtraction is counterproductive for spatio-temporal architectures. Real-world background videos (B) consistently outperform synthetic backgrounds (B̃) in both input-space subtraction and as training data for BG-only models, validating the dataset's paired real-world design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the first wildlife dataset explicitly designed to disentangle background effects from behaviour signals using real-world paired foreground-background camera trap videos, rather than the synthetic modifications (masking, in-painting) used in prior human action recognition studies. It draws on the well-established background bias literature in human action recognition (where ~70% of performance is attributed to static cues) and the growing body of animal behaviour datasets (PanAf20k, MammalNet, LoTE), but departs from all of them by providing temporally matched background pairs from the same camera location and by defining disjoint/overlapping splits analogous to WILDS and iWildCam for species classification. The latent-space background neutralisation technique repurposes the general idea of background subtraction from input-space methods into an embedding-space operation, while the dual-stream fusion builds on standard late-fusion architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The confirmation that background-only models achieve 65–70% of foreground performance in wild chimpanzee behaviour recognition validates that the shortcut-learning problem documented in human action recognition is equally severe in wildlife CV, where deployment to new camera locations is the practical norm. The architecture-specific finding that MViT-V2 is robust to background duration in-distribution but becomes highly sensitive out-of-distribution, and that latent-space subtraction helps 3D CNNs and transformers while input-space subtraction helps only 2D CNNs, provides actionable guidance for practitioners choosing architectures and mitigation strategies. Limitations include restriction to a single species (chimpanzees), statically installed camera traps, and the requirement for paired background videos at inference time for the latent-space technique; open questions include whether findings generalise to other taxa, whether the technique can be adapted to work without explicit background pairs, and how background duration effects interact with longer video sequences.
