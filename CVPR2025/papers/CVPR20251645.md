## Problem

Existing multi-modal crowd counting methods initialize backbones from single-modal pre-training (e.g., ImageNet classification) and must develop all cross-modal perception from limited supervised signals in crowd counting datasets, leaving a significant gap between pre-trained weights and cross-modal learning needs. Additionally, most approaches focus on cross-modal feature fusion while neglecting the spatial density correlations between image regions, making it difficult to distinguish local density variations in highly congested scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251645/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired images from two modalities (RGB + thermal for RGBT-CC and DroneRGBT; RGB + depth for ShanghaiTechRGBD) at resolutions of 224×224 or 896×896 depending on the dataset. It produces a continuous crowd density map via a regression head, from which total counts are derived. The regional density supervision operates at the patch level, computing per-patch density by counting point annotations within each spatial region, providing a medium-grained signal between fine-grained point labels and coarse image-level counts.

## Method

The paper proposes a two-stage 'free lunch' enhancement requiring no extra data, parameters, or inference cost. First, a Post-Pre-Training Cross-Modal Alignment (PPCA) stage uses unsupervised contrastive learning (MoCo with a momentum queue) to align features from different modalities of the same shot before supervised fine-tuning, treating cross-modal views as positive pairs and different shots as negatives. Second, a Regional Density Supervision (RDS) loss is added during fine-tuning: it defines positive/negative patch pairs based on relative density differences (a partial order), where for a given anchor, a patch with smaller density difference is positive and patches with larger differences are negatives, recovering a total order via linear extension. The total training loss combines DM-Count point supervision with the RDS contrastive loss (weighted by λ=0.1), and the entire strategy is plug-and-play, compatible with dual-branch or triple-branch (broker modality) architectures.

## Results

On RGBT-CC, the method achieves GAME(0)=9.57, GAME(1)=12.62, GAME(2)=16.27, GAME(3)=21.85, and RMSE=17.05, becoming the first method to push GAME(0) below 10. On DroneRGBT, it reaches GAME(0)=4.94 and RMSE=7.81, outperforming the previous best (BM) by 1.26 and 2.59 respectively. On ShanghaiTechRGBD, the method attains GAME(0)=3.69, GAME(1)=5.88, GAME(2)=8.60, GAME(3)=11.85, and RMSE=5.38, matching or exceeding all prior approaches on GAME metrics while being marginally behind BM on RMSE (5.38 vs. 5.28).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 29 compared baselines on both RGBT-CC and DroneRGBT. On RGBT-CC, it improves GAME(0) from 10.19 (BM, previous SOTA) to 9.57 (−0.62) and RMSE from 17.32 to 17.05 (−0.27), while the gap over the next-best methods (SEFNet at 10.61, MSDTrans at 10.90) is even larger. On DroneRGBT, the improvement over BM is more pronounced: GAME(0) drops from 6.20 to 4.94 (−1.26) and RMSE from 10.40 to 7.81 (−2.59). On ShanghaiTechRGBD, the method achieves the best GAME(0) of 3.69 (vs. BM's 3.72) and best GAME(1–3), but trails BM slightly on RMSE (5.38 vs. 5.28), indicating a small trade-off in overall error magnitude despite superior per-threshold accuracy. The paper also demonstrates generality across backbones (VGG-19, ResNet-34, ConvNeXt-S, DeiT-S, PVT v2-B2, Swin-T), consistently improving GAME(0) by 1.8–3.3 points over each baseline configuration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a training-strategy enhancement rather than a new architecture for multi-modal crowd counting. It builds directly on the broker-modality framework (BM, ECCV 2024) as its backbone and adapts MoCo's momentum-queue contrastive loss to a cross-modal setting, inserting a post-pre-training alignment stage between ImageNet pre-training and supervised fine-tuning. The regional density supervision mechanism departs from prior work by framing patch-level density discrimination as a partial-order recovery problem (drawing on linear extension theory), which is a novel way to define positive/negative pairs in a continuous regression task where such distinctions are not naturally available. Conceptually, the paper argues that existing multi-modal counting methods underutilize the pre-trained backbone's cross-modal potential and neglect spatial density correlations, and addresses both gaps with plug-in training modifications rather than architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'free lunch' framing—no extra data, parameters, or inference cost—makes the proposed enhancements broadly adoptable as drop-in improvements to existing multi-modal counting pipelines, and the first sub-10 MAE on RGBT-CC is a meaningful milestone for the benchmark. However, the practical gains are modest (GAME(0) drops from 10.19 to 9.57 over BM), and the PPCA stage does add non-trivial training time, so the 'free' label applies only to inference and data. Open questions include whether the partial-order-based RDS generalizes to very high-resolution images with thousands of patches, whether the approach transfers to other multi-modal tasks beyond counting, and whether the cross-modal alignment benefit persists when the backbone is already trained on multi-modal pre-training objectives.
