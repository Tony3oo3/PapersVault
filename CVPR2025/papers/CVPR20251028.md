## Problem

Existing benchmarks for interleaved image-text generation (e.g., OpenLEAF, InterleavedBench) are limited in size, task diversity, and real-world coverage, making them insufficient to evaluate the rapidly advancing unified multimodal models. Additionally, reliance on GPT-based evaluators introduces inherent self-bias, data leakage risks, and poor alignment with human preferences, leaving the field without a reliable, scalable, and open evaluation protocol for open-ended interleaved generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251028/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For the benchmark, each instance provides a text prompt (optionally with input images) and expects a multi-step interleaved output sequence of up to 10 text-image pairs, spanning 56 tasks across 23 meta-topics (e.g., travel guides, design, brainstorming). For IntJudge evaluation, the input is a pair of anonymous interleaved outputs from two agents on the same prompt, and the output is a winner selection based on seven criteria (Correctness, Image-Text Coherency, Multistep Consistency, Content Quality, Human Preference Alignment, Completeness, Content Richness), aggregated into win rates and pairwise win-loss matrices across 4,320 battle pairs.

## Method

The paper contributes two main components: (1) the OpenING benchmark, comprising 5,400 human-annotated instances collected from 20+ sources and curated by 28 annotators under 14 data experts over three months, split into a 3,240-instance Dev Set and 2,160-instance Test Set; and (2) IntJudge, a judge model built on Qwen2-VL-7B trained via an Interleaved Arena framework using a roulette matching algorithm to sample distinct pairwise battles. IntJudge's training data is augmented through a Reference-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipeline that pairs plain model outputs with gold-answer-conditioned generations, scaling training data from 6,014 to 25,982 samples. The training objective combines cross-entropy, contrastive, MSE, and pairwise ranking losses, and IntJudge achieves 82.42% agreement with human judgments under the Force Dividing Tie metric, outperforming GPT-4o by 11.34%.

## Results

On the OpenING benchmark (5,400 instances, 56 tasks), integrated pipelines dominate: GPT-4o+DALL-E-3 achieves 78.42% FDT win rate under human evaluation, and Gemini1.5+Flux reaches 65.57%, while end-to-end models like MiniGPT-5 (30.69%) and GILL (25.80%) trail significantly. The proposed IntJudge (Qwen2-VL-7B backbone) achieves 82.42% agreement with human judgments under FDT, outperforming GPT-4o as a judge (71.08%) by 11.34 percentage points, and substantially exceeding untrained Qwen2-VL-7B (61.61%) and InternLMX2.5-7B (56.81%). Human-annotated content consistently receives the highest ratings (83.28% FDT) across all evaluators, confirming that no current model matches human quality in interleaved generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among generation models, integrated pipelines (GPT-4o+DALL-E-3, Gemini1.5+Flux) clearly outperform both two-stage generators (SEED-X, Emu2, Show-o) and end-to-end generators (Anole, SEED-LLaMA, NExT-GPT, MiniGPT-5, GILL) across all three evaluators, with GPT-4o+DALL-E-3 leading at 78.42% human FDT win rate. Among end-to-end models, Anole (51.90%) and SEED-LLaMA (44.30%) perform best, while GILL (25.80%) and MiniGPT-5 (30.69%) rank lowest, primarily due to poor text and image quality respectively. As a judge model, IntJudge (82.42% FDT agreement) substantially outperforms GPT-4o (71.08%), untrained Qwen2-VL-7B (61.61%), and InternLMX2.5-7B (56.81%), with the RAG-augmented training data contributing a 7.8% improvement on unseen models. Notably, GPT-4o as a judge exhibits self-bias, scoring its own outputs 10/10 in human preference alignment versus 9/10 for human-annotated content, whereas IntJudge avoids this issue. The ablation in Table 4 further shows that pairing SEED-X text with Flux-dev images (90.18% FDT) dramatically outperforms SEED-X's own image generation (9.82%), confirming that image generator quality is the dominant factor in interleaved output quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the limited line of interleaved image-text generation benchmarks (OpenLEAF with 660 instances/10 tasks, InterleavedBench with 815 instances/10 tasks) by scaling to 5,400 instances across 56 tasks grounded in real-world scenarios. It departs from the prevailing GPT-based evaluation paradigm by training a dedicated offline judge model (IntJudge) using arena-style pairwise comparison adapted from LLM evaluation literature, combined with a novel Reference-Augmented Generation strategy to scale judge training data. The generation models evaluated span the full spectrum from integrated pipelines (GPT-4o+DALL-E-3, Gemini+Flux) through two-stage unified architectures (SEED-X, Emu2, Show-o) to end-to-end autoregressive models (Anole, MiniGPT-5, NExT-GPT, GILL), providing a comprehensive cross-architecture comparison that prior benchmarks lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OpenING fills a critical gap by providing a large-scale, diverse, and contamination-resistant benchmark for a task that is central to multimodal AI progress but has been under-evaluated. The finding that all generative models rank below human-annotated content, and that integrated pipelines substantially outperform end-to-end unified models, quantifies the remaining gap and directs research priorities. IntJudge offers a practical, scalable, and reproducible offline evaluation alternative to costly and biased GPT-based scoring, with the paper suggesting it could serve as a reward model for RL-based training (e.g., GRPO). Open questions include whether unified end-to-end architectures can close the gap as they mature, whether the benchmark generalizes beyond the 56 tasks covered, and how to address the persistent quality gap in generated versus natural images.
