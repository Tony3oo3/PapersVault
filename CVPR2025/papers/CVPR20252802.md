## Problem

Generating compositional 3D scenes from a single image requires inferring both individual object geometries and their spatial relationships from limited 2D cues. Existing approaches either rely on feed-forward reconstruction (limited by scarce 3D supervision), retrieval from fixed databases (limited coverage), or multi-stage object-by-object pipelines (error accumulation and lack of global scene context during per-object generation). No prior method simultaneously generates multiple 3D instances with coherent spatial arrangements in an end-to-end manner while retaining the strong generalization of pre-trained 3D object generators.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252802/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single scene image as the primary input, which is segmented into per-object crops with binary masks. For each instance, the object RGB image, its mask, and the full global scene image are concatenated along the channel dimension (7 channels total) and encoded at 512×512 resolution by a DINO ViT. The output is a set of N 3D latent tokens (one per instance) that are decoded via the VAE into SDF/tri-plane representations and converted to meshes via marching cubes; planar structures like floors and walls are explicitly excluded from the generation scope.

## Method

MIDI extends a pre-trained DiT-based image-to-3D object generation model (using rectified flow) into a multi-instance diffusion model by simultaneously denoising the latent representations of all N instances with a shared denoising network. The core architectural contribution is a multi-instance attention mechanism: K=5 of the 21 self-attention layers are converted so that each instance's tokens attend to tokens from all instances in the scene, enabling direct modeling of cross-instance spatial interactions during denoising. Image conditioning is handled by a DINO ViT encoder (initialized from DINOv2, with input projection expanded to 7 channels) whose features are injected via cross-attention. Training uses LoRA fine-tuning on ~15K cleaned 3D-Front scenes with a shared noise level t across all instances, and with 30% probability the multi-instance attention is disabled and the model is trained as a standard single-object generator on Objaverse to preserve the pre-trained 3D prior.

## Results

On 3D-Front, MIDI achieves the best scores across all metrics: scene-level CD of 0.080, scene-level F-Score of 50.19, object-level CD of 0.103, object-level F-Score of 53.58, and bounding-box IoU of 0.518. On BlendSwap, the improvements are even more pronounced, with scene-level F-Score of 78.21 and bounding-box IoU of 0.663. MIDI runs in 40 seconds per scene, comparable to feed-forward methods (32–39 s) and substantially faster than multi-stage compositional methods (4–9 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIDI outperforms all seven baselines on every metric for both 3D-Front and BlendSwap. Against the strongest feed-forward method (DiffCAD, CD-S 0.117 on 3D-Front), MIDI reduces scene-level CD by ~32% (0.080) and improves bounding-box IoU from 0.392 to 0.518. Against the best compositional methods (Gen3DSR and REPARO), MIDI achieves substantially better object-level geometry (F-Score-O 53.58 vs. 40.85 for REPARO on 3D-Front) while running 6–13× faster (40 s vs. 4–9 min). On BlendSwap, the gap widens further: MIDI's scene-level F-Score of 78.21 exceeds REPARO's 62.39 by ~25%, and its IoU-B of 0.663 surpasses DiffCAD's 0.457 by ~45%. Qualitatively, feed-forward methods produce misaligned layouts, retrieval-based methods fail to match input geometry, and multi-stage compositional methods suffer from error accumulation and lack of global scene context—issues that MIDI's simultaneous multi-instance diffusion with cross-instance attention directly addresses.

## Relation to Prior Work

MIDI extends pre-trained image-to-3D object generation models (CLAY, Michelangelo, Craftsman, Direct3D) that use a VAE + DiT + rectified flow architecture from single-object to multi-instance generation. It departs from the dominant multi-stage compositional pipeline (segment → complete → generate per-object → optimize layout) by introducing a multi-instance attention mechanism that enables simultaneous denoising of all instances with cross-instance interaction, eliminating error accumulation. The image conditioning strategy (concatenating local object images, masks, and global scene context through a DINO-based ViT encoder) is a practical simplification over prior approaches that handle scene context separately.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a handful of multi-instance attention layers (K=5) can replace entire multi-stage pipelines while achieving 30–40% lower Chamfer Distance, MIDI offers a simpler, more robust paradigm for scene generation that preserves the strong generalization of pre-trained object priors. Key limitations remain: performance degrades on tiny-resolution inputs and complex inter-instance interactions (e.g., a character holding an object), and the method does not generate planar structures like floors and walls. Open directions include scaling to larger object counts, open-world settings, incorporating explicit 3D geometric knowledge into the attention mechanism, and probing whether the model develops latent 3D scene understanding.
