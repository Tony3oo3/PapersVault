## Problem

Existing source-free unsupervised domain adaptation (SFUDA) methods assume identical label spaces between source and target, or require all target data to be available at once. In realistic streaming scenarios where unlabeled target data arrive incrementally as disjoint class subsets of the source label space, two coupled challenges arise: similar source-class knowledge interferes with target-class representation learning, and adapting to new classes causes catastrophic forgetting of previously learned ones. No prior method simultaneously addresses both the label-space mismatch and the continual adaptation problem without access to source data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen pretrained source model (ViT-B backbone) with stored source feature centroids and classifier weights, plus unlabeled RGB target images arriving in sequential sessions of 10 classes each. No source instances or ground-truth labels are available at any point. The output is a single adapted target model that classifies all previously seen classes, evaluated by final accuracy over the union of all sessions and per-session accuracy. A small memory bank (10 exemplars per existing positive class) is maintained across sessions to support replay.

## Method

GROTO comprises two modules. First, the multi-granularity class prototype self-organization module identifies positive (domain-shared) classes via a hybrid criterion combining source-similarity accumulation distributions and target-probability accumulation distributions, then generates reliable pseudo-labels using both coarse-grained prototypes (source classifier weights and target class centroids) and fine-grained prototypes (high-confidence, low-uncertainty target features selected via data augmentation), and trains with cross-entropy plus contrastive losses to compact intra-class features. Second, the prototype topology distillation module constructs topological structures from source and target classifier weights and performs point-to-point distillation via a compactness loss (pulling each target prototype toward its corresponding source prototype) and a separability loss (enforcing balanced cross-prototype mapping to prevent collapse), thereby preventing the decision boundary from overfitting new classes while preserving old-class knowledge across sessions.

## Results

GROTO achieves 93.0% average final accuracy on Office-31-CI and 88.8% on ImageNet-Caltech-CI, outperforming the strongest baselines LEAD-B (89.3%, 71.1%) and ProCA-B (88.9%, 87.8%). On Office-Home-CI, GROTO reaches 82.5% average final accuracy, surpassing ProCA-B (81.2%) and LCFD (68.8%). GROTO is the only method that simultaneously satisfies both class-incremental (CI) and source-free (SF) constraints while achieving the best average accuracy across all three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GROTO outperforms all eight baselines on average final accuracy across all three datasets. Against the closest class-incremental baseline ProCA-B (which requires source data), GROTO gains +4.1% on Office-31-CI (93.0 vs. 88.9) and +1.0% on ImageNet-Caltech-CI (88.8 vs. 87.8) while being fully source-free. Against the best source-free universal DA method LEAD-B, GROTO improves by +3.7% on Office-31-CI (93.0 vs. 89.3) and a substantial +17.7% on ImageNet-Caltech-CI (88.8 vs. 71.1), where LEAD-B suffers severe forgetting on the I→C direction (53.0%). On Office-Home-CI, GROTO leads ProCA-B by +1.3% (82.5 vs. 81.2) and LCFD by +13.7% (82.5 vs. 68.8). Notably, CIDA—the only other method satisfying both CI and SF constraints—achieves only 71.6% on Office-31-CI and 47.5% on Office-Home-CI, falling well below even the source-only ViT-B baseline, highlighting the difficulty of the CI-SFUDA setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate research threads—source-free unsupervised domain adaptation (SFUDA), source-free universal domain adaptation (SF-UniDA) for non-identical label spaces, and class-incremental learning (CIL/UCIL)—into a single problem formulation (CI-SFUDA). It builds on the self-training paradigm of SFUDA (BMD, C-SFDA) and the feature-decomposition/knowledge-distillation strategies of SF-UniDA (LEAD, DIFO, LCFD), but departs from them by handling target data that arrive in sequential class-disjoint sessions without access to source instances. The prototype topology distillation module draws on the classifier-weight alignment idea from prototype-oriented UDA [35] and the exemplar replay mechanism from CIL (ProCA), but repurposes them to maintain inter-domain topological consistency across incremental sessions rather than within a single adaptation step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CI-SFUDA formulation captures a practically important scenario—streaming target data under privacy constraints with non-identical label spaces—that no prior method addresses simultaneously. The finding that SF-UniDA methods (DIFO, LEAD) can perform worse than the frozen source model in incremental settings highlights a critical gap: domain alignment without forgetting prevention actively degrades old-class knowledge. Open questions include scalability to very large source label spaces (beyond 1000 classes), robustness to within-class distribution shifts across sessions, and whether the memory bank of exemplars (10 per class) can be eliminated for truly rehearsal-free operation. The method's reliance on a well-trained source model with stored feature centroids also limits applicability when the source model is weak or partially corrupted.
