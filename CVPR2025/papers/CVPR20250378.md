## Problem

Video LLMs currently depend on costly human annotations or proprietary APIs (e.g., GPT-4o) to generate training data, limiting large-scale pre-training. Prior video-ASR learning treats transcriptions as global captions, discarding the fine-grained temporal alignment between spoken words and visual content that is critical for streaming applications. This paper asks whether cheap, automatically extracted ASR transcripts can be leveraged at scale to train a video LLM capable of real-time commentary.

## Input / Output

The model consumes streaming video frames at 1 FPS sequentially (not all at once), along with the video title and preceding ASR text as contextual conditioning. During inference it generates only a few words per frame interval (k=1 second), producing commentary with sub-second latency. For QA tasks, the same model accepts a full video clip and a question, outputting a multiple-choice answer. The training sequence format densely interleaves frame tokens and ASR word tokens at 1-second granularity, with an ellipsis token marking silent frames.

## Method

The core contribution is a streaming pre-training paradigm that densely interleaves fine-grained ASR words with corresponding video frames along the temporal axis, formatted as [Context] <F_{t:t+k}> <W_{t:t+k}> <F_{t+k:t+2k}> <W_{t+k:t+2k}> …, so the model learns to predict short, temporally-aligned word fragments rather than complete sentences. Built on Qwen2-VL-7B (ViT encoder + Qwen2 LLM), training proceeds in two stages: pre-training on 10M YouTube clips with original closed captions (Live-CC-10M), followed by SFT on 408K high-quality WhisperX-transcribed clips (Live-WhisperX-408K) plus standard video/image instruction data. Video title and previous ASR are prepended as context to disambiguate mid-sentence starts, and a hybrid 'Title || Prev. ASR' strategy is used when prior context is unavailable. At inference, KV caching accelerates sequential decoding, and visual tokens are pruned every 240 seconds to manage long-sequence memory while retaining text tokens for coherence.

## Results

On VideoMME, LiveCC-7B achieves 63.9 overall accuracy, outperforming Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct (63.3) and LLaVA-Video-7B (63.3) at the same 7B scale, and approaching the 72B LLaVA-Video-72B (70.6). On the new Sports-3K-QA benchmark, LiveCC-7B scores 66.10 overall, matching LLaVA-Video-7B (66.44) and notably surpassing LLaVA-Video-72B (71.12) on OCR-reliant questions (65.77 vs. 65.10). On the Sports-3K-CC commentary track (pairwise win rate vs. LLaVA-Video-72B), the 10M pre-trained model reaches 41.44%, with further gains expected after SFT. The model also demonstrates strong temporal understanding, scoring 57.01 on 'When' questions, the highest among 7B models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among 7B open-source models, LiveCC-7B achieves the best Video-MME overall score (63.9), edging out Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct (63.3) and LLaVA-Video-7B (63.3), and substantially outperforming LongVA-7B (52.6), Oryx-7B (58.3), and LLaVA-OV-7B (58.2). On Sports-3K-QA, LiveCC-7B (66.10) is nearly on par with LLaVA-Video-7B (66.44) and clearly ahead of Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct (62.24) and LLaVA-OV-7B (63.37). Notably, LiveCC-7B surpasses the much larger LLaVA-Video-72B on OCR-reliant questions (65.77 vs. 65.10), likely because the ASR training data pairs on-screen text with spoken words. Against proprietary models, GPT-4o remains the strongest overall (72.19 Sports-3K-QA, 71.9 Video-MME), while Gemini-1.5-Pro leads VideoMME (75.0) but is affected by occasional refusals on Sports-3K-QA. On the Sports-3K-CC commentary track, the pre-trained LiveCC-7B achieves a 41.44% win rate against LLaVA-Video-72B, indicating room for improvement but demonstrating that streaming ASR pre-training alone confers meaningful commentary ability without any task-specific SFT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Qwen2-VL architecture and the line of video-ASR pretraining work (Vid2Seq, HDVILA, YT-Temporal-1B, VidChapters, HowTo100M), but departs fundamentally from treating ASR transcripts as global video captions. Where Vid2Seq predicts timestamped ASR paragraphs aligned to coarse events, LiveCC densely interleaves individual ASR words with video frames at 1 FPS, learning word-level temporal alignment in a causal, streaming fashion. It also addresses a key gap in prior streaming video LLMs (VideoLLM-Online, StreamChat, etc.), which relied on hallucinated LLM-generated conversations or small-scale dense caption datasets, by providing a scalable, low-cost data pipeline from YouTube closed captions and WhisperX transcriptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that cheap, automatically extracted ASR transcripts—previously underutilized for their temporal structure—can serve as a scalable pretraining signal for video LLMs, reducing dependence on expensive GPT-4o synthetic data. The Sports-3K benchmark and the LLM-as-a-judge evaluation protocol for free-form commentary fill a notable gap in assessing streaming video understanding. However, QA performance degrades beyond 5M pre-training clips due to single-source data, suggesting that multi-source pretraining integration remains an open challenge. The 7B model's ability to match or exceed 72B models on commentary while maintaining competitive VideoMME scores hints at the efficiency potential of streaming-aligned training, though broader validation across architectures and task diversity is still needed.
