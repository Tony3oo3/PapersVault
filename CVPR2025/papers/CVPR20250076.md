## Problem

Existing visual instruction tuning datasets for MLLMs focus on single-image understanding, leaving models weak at identifying subtle differences between visually similar images. While contrastive learning and image difference captioning have advanced separately, no automated pipeline exists to synthesize high-quality, region-specific image-difference training data at scale for MLLM fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250076/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairs of highly similar RGB images (generated at SDXL resolution) where exactly one object has been replaced, along with a text question prompting the model to describe the difference. Each training instance highlights a single bounding box region with a red overlay on both images, and the output is a concise difference caption scoped to that region rather than the entire image. The final dataset is formatted as instruction-tuning question-answer pairs compatible with LLaVA-1.5, MGM, and InternVL2 training pipelines.

## Method

The pipeline has three stages. First, image pairs are generated by taking MSCOCO or LLaVA captions, using an LLM to swap one object noun, then applying Stable Diffusion XL with Prompt-to-Prompt to produce visually similar images differing only in that object. Second, the Difference Area Generator identifies valid difference regions via a cascade: CLIP cosine-similarity filtering (ensuring pairs are similar but not identical), FastSAM segmentation to crop candidate regions, BLIP-based image-text matching to confirm valid objects, and a Difference Detector with IoU-based overlap removal to retain only genuinely differing bounding boxes. Third, the Difference Captions Generator operates in two sub-stages: LLaVA-NEXT captions each highlighted region and the captions are filtered by image-text matching and CLIP text-similarity thresholds, then red boxes are drawn on both images and LLaVA-NEXT produces the final region-specific difference caption. The contrastive framing—presenting paired images and requiring the model to articulate what changed in a specific region—forces fine-grained visual discrimination that single-image training cannot provide.

## Results

On the MMVP benchmark, fine-tuning MGM-7B with IMG-DIFF data surpasses GPT-4V and Gemini by up to 12 points, and LLaVA-1.5-7B+RP exceeds the larger LLaVA-1.5-13B. On Spot-the-Diff, MGM-7B+RP achieves CIDEr-D of 53.5 (vs. 46.3 baseline) and BLEU of 10.8, outperforming all prior dedicated image-difference-captioning methods. Across eight MLLM benchmarks (VQAv2, GQA, POPE, MMBench, MMBench-CN, MM-Vet, ScienceQA, SEED-Bench), LLaVA-1.5-7B+RP gains an average of +3.06%, MGM-7B+RP gains +1.28%, and InternVL2-8B+RP gains +1.01%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the MMVP benchmark, the fine-tuned MGM-7B+RP surpasses both GPT-4V and Gemini by up to 12 points, and LLaVA-1.5-7B+RP exceeds the 13B variant of the same model family. On Spot-the-Diff, MGM-7B+RP (CIDEr-D 53.5, BLEU 10.8) outperforms all dedicated IDC baselines including VACC (CIDEr-D 41.5), VAM (38.1), IFDC (37.0), and DUDA+Aux (34.5). On Image-Edit-Request, LLaVA-1.5-7B+RP (BLEU 16.2, CIDEr-D 60.9) and MGM-7B+RP (CIDEr-D 68.1) both surpass VIXEN (CIDEr-D 38.1), VARD (35.7), NCT (34.2), CLIP4IDC (32.2), and BiDiff (27.7). Against their own unmodified baselines, all three MLLMs show consistent gains: LLaVA-1.5-7B improves on all 8 MLLM benchmarks, while MGM-7B and InternVL2-8B show smaller but still positive average improvements (+1.28% and +1.01% respectively), with minor regressions on individual benchmarks (e.g., MGM-7B drops 0.6 on MMBench, InternVL2-8B drops 0.1 on MMBench-CN).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the InstructPix2Pix pipeline (Brooks et al., CVPR 2023) by upgrading the generative backbone from Stable Diffusion 1.5 to Stable Diffusion XL and, more importantly, by introducing a multi-stage filtering and region-localization pipeline (FastSAM segmentation, CLIP-based similarity filtering, BLIP-based image-text matching, IoU-based deduplication) that prior image-difference datasets lacked. Conceptually, it reframes image difference captioning through the lens of contrastive learning—training MLLMs to distinguish matching from non-matching regions within image pairs—rather than treating it as a standalone captioning task. The dataset is explicitly designed for MLLM instruction tuning (following the LLaVA/MGM/InternVL2 format), bridging the gap between the IDC literature (Spot-the-Diff, CLEVR-Change, VIXEN) and the data-centric MLLM training paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a fully automated, ~12K-sample contrastive dataset can push open-source 7B MLLMs past GPT-4V and Gemini on the MMVP benchmark, suggesting that targeted data synthesis is a viable alternative to scaling model parameters for fine-grained visual discrimination. The quality-over-quantity finding (12K high-quality samples outperforming 34K lower-quality ones) offers practical guidance for data-centric MLLM research. Open questions include whether the object-replacement paradigm generalizes to other difference types (attribute changes, spatial rearrangements), whether the gains transfer to real-world (non-synthetic) image pairs, and how the approach scales to video or multi-image contrastive settings.
