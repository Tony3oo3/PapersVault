## Problem

Existing surgical tool tracking datasets rely on generic tracking formalizations that fail to capture surgical-specific dynamics such as tools exiting the body cavity, moving out of camera view, or being replaced mid-procedure. This results in clinically irrelevant trajectories and limits the development of AI systems for real-world surgical assistance tasks like skill assessment, safety zone estimation, and human-machine collaboration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252111/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 20 full-length laparoscopic cholecystectomy videos (subsampled from 25 FPS to 1 FPS for annotation, benchmarked at 25 FPS) yielding 35K annotated frames and 65K bounding boxes. Each frame carries bounding box coordinates, tool category (7 classes), track identity, operator assignment (4 classes linked to trocar ports), surgical phase (7 phases), and visual challenge labels (8 types). The multi-perspective trajectory output defines three distinct track lifecycles—visibility (camera FOV), intracorporeal (body cavity), and intraoperative (full procedure)—each with different start and termination criteria.

## Method

The core contribution is a multi-perspective tracking formalization that redefines tool trajectory boundaries across three clinically meaningful perspectives: intraoperative (first appearance to last in-body, requiring re-identification post-occlusion or reinsertion), intracorporeal (body entry to trocar exit, with inferred exits when off-camera via trocar or grasp-release cues), and visibility (camera FOV with a 2-second re-ID tolerance). Annotations are produced by four researchers using a custom Python tool with a surgeon-refined guide, incorporating trocar port tracking for operator identification and CholecT50 tool-tissue interaction labels for track continuity. Quality is validated through intra-rater (99.4% Jaccard), inter-rater (91.8% Jaccard) agreement, and board-certified surgeon mediation on 758 uncertain samples. Benchmarking employs 13 object detectors and 7 MOT trackers evaluated via TrackEval with HOTA, MOTA, IDF1, and CLEAR metrics across all three perspectives, tool categories, visual challenges, and surgical phases.

## Results

On the CholecTrack20 dataset, Bot-SORT achieves the highest tracking performance across all three multi-perspective trajectories: 44.7% HOTA (visibility), 17.4% HOTA (intraoperative), and 27.0% HOTA (intracorporeal), with corresponding MOTA scores of 72.0%, 69.6%, and 70.0%. All evaluated trackers remain below 45% HOTA, indicating a significant gap for clinical deployment. For tool detection, YOLOv9 achieves the best AP@0.5:0.95 of 56.5%, while YOLOv7 leads on AP@0.5 (80.6%) and AP@0.75 (62.0%), with the YOLO family consistently outperforming anchor-based (Faster R-CNN, Cascade R-CNN) and anchor-free (CenterNet, FCOS) detectors by 15–25 AP points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among tracking baselines, Bot-SORT [1] dominates all three perspectives, outperforming ByteTrack [67] by +3.2 HOTA (visibility), +1.6 (intraoperative), and +2.3 (intracorporeal), and SMILETrack [63] by +3.4, +1.5, and +2.1 HOTA respectively. OCSORT [33] trails the top three by 5–10 HOTA points and suffers the highest identity switches (2317 on visibility vs. 3183 for Bot-SORT). FairMOT [66] and TransTrack [50] are consistently the weakest, scoring below 20% HOTA on all perspectives, with TransTrack also being the slowest at 6.7 FPS. For detection, the YOLO family (v7–v10, YOLOX [19]) clearly outperforms anchor-based methods (Faster R-CNN [43]: 34.6% AP, Cascade R-CNN [7]: 34.7%) and anchor-free methods (CenterNet [69]: 35.0%, FCOS [54]: 28.1%) by 15–25 AP points. Transformer-based detectors (Deformable-DETR [71]: 38.3%, Swin-T [31]: 40.2%) fall between the two groups but remain well below YOLOv7/v9. PAA [23] (41.1%) and SSD [30] (36.1%) occupy the middle tier. Across all 20 baselines, no method exceeds 45% HOTA on any tracking perspective, confirming the paper's central finding that current SOTA is insufficient for clinical surgical tool tracking.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of surgical tool tracking datasets (ATLAS Dione, Cholec80-locations, Fathollahi et al., RMIT) by introducing a unified multi-perspective tracking formalization—simultaneously defining intraoperative, intracorporeal, and visibility trajectories—that no prior dataset provided. It departs from the generic MOT/MCT formalizations used in existing surgical datasets, which treat all tools as a single class or track only within camera visibility, by incorporating clinically meaningful track boundaries (tool entering/exiting the body, trocar-port-linked operator identity) and rich contextual annotations (surgical phase, visual challenges, operator hand). The raw videos are sourced from Cholec80 and CholecT50, but the annotation effort and tracking formalization are entirely new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even the strongest SOTA trackers (Bot-SORT at 44.7% HOTA on visibility, 27.0% on intracorporeal) fall far short of the accuracy needed for clinical deployment, exposing a fundamental gap between general-purpose MOT methods and the demands of surgical tool tracking. The intracorporeal perspective—where tool entry/exit must be inferred from indirect cues like trocar re-entry or grasp release—is particularly underexplored and suggests that purely visual re-ID is insufficient, motivating context-aware and history-based tracking approaches. However, the dataset is limited to a single procedure type (laparoscopic cholecystectomy) with only 20 videos, and the 1 FPS annotation rate may limit applicability to high-speed tracking scenarios. Open questions include whether the multi-perspective formalization generalizes to other surgical domains and whether the performance gap can be closed with surgical-specific architectures rather than off-the-shelf MOT pipelines.
