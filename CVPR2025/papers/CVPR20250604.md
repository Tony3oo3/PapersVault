## Problem

Existing generalizable 3D Gaussian Splatting methods (pixelSplat, MVSplat, eFreeSplat) rely on learned feature matching from training data and degrade significantly on unfamiliar visual content or out-of-distribution scenes. Their zero-shot generalization is limited because their geometric understanding is constrained by the training distribution, with no access to the broad visual priors accumulated by large-scale pre-trained models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250604/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N posed RGB images (H×W×3) along with their camera intrinsics, rotations, and translations, and produces per-pixel 3D Gaussian primitives (position, opacity, covariance, spherical-harmonics color) across all input views, which are merged into a unified scene representation for differentiable rasterization. Evaluation is standardized at 256×256 resolution, and the cost volume spans a normalized depth range of 0.5–100.0 with 128 discrete planes. The frozen Depth Anything ViT-s backbone contributes the majority of the 30.3M total parameters, leaving only 10.3M trainable parameters.

## Method

MonoSplat repurposes a frozen monocular depth foundation model (Depth Anything ViT-s) as the feature backbone, adding two lightweight modules. The Mono-Multi Feature Adapter first fuses multi-scale encoder features via a DPT head, then applies a Swin-style local-window cross-view transformer over M nearest-neighbor views to produce geometry-aware multi-view features; separately, monocular features are extracted from the decoder's final layer to preserve rich depth priors. The Integrated Gaussian Prediction module builds a plane-sweep stereo cost volume from the multi-view features, concatenates it with both monocular and multi-view features, and refines it through a dedicated network; a second refinement stage upsamples depth maps and features and fuses them again with monocular features before two decoder heads predict Gaussian positions/opacities and covariances/colors. Training uses a weighted MSE + LPIPS photometric loss over 300k iterations on a single A100, with the depth backbone kept frozen to retain its pre-trained geometric knowledge and minimize trainable parameters.

## Results

On RealEstate10K, MonoSplat achieves 26.68 PSNR / 0.875 SSIM / 0.123 LPIPS, and on ACID it reaches 28.63 PSNR / 0.864 SSIM / 0.138 LPIPS, setting new state-of-the-art on all three metrics for both datasets. Inference time is 0.051 s per scene with 30.3 M total parameters (only 10.3 M trainable) and 0.857 GB peak memory. For cross-dataset zero-shot generalization (Re10K→DTU), MonoSplat scores 15.25 PSNR, substantially outperforming pixelSplat (12.89) and MVSplat (13.94).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MonoSplat outperforms all eight baselines on both RealEstate10K and ACID across PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS. Against the closest 3DGS competitors, it gains +0.23 dB PSNR over eFreeSplat (26.68 vs 26.45) and +0.29 dB over MVSplat (26.68 vs 26.39) on RealEstate10K, and +0.33 dB over eFreeSplat (28.63 vs 28.30) and +0.38 dB over pixelSplat (28.63 vs 28.27) on ACID. The NeRF-based MuRF, while competitive on PSNR (26.10 / 28.09), is slower (0.186 s) and does not report memory. Older methods (pixelNeRF, GPNR, Du et al.) are clearly outperformed by 2–6 dB in PSNR. In the cross-dataset zero-shot setting (Re10K→DTU), MonoSplat leads by +2.36 dB over MVSplat and +2.36 dB over pixelSplat, demonstrating that the frozen depth-foundation-model priors provide substantially stronger domain generalization than cost-volume or epipolar-based matching alone. On efficiency, MonoSplat's 0.051 s inference is comparable to MVSplat (0.044 s) and far faster than pixelSplat (0.104 s) or latentSplat (0.110 s), while using less peak memory than MVSplat (0.857 vs 0.860 GB) despite a larger total parameter count.

## Relation to Prior Work

MonoSplat sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: generalizable 3D Gaussian Splatting (pixelSplat, MVSplat, latentSplat, eFreeSplat) and monocular depth foundation models (Depth Anything, MiDaS). Its key conceptual departure is repurposing a frozen depth foundation model as the feature backbone for 3DGS, rather than training a dedicated encoder from scratch on limited scene data. Architecturally, the Mono-Multi Feature Adapter (DPT + Swin-style cross-view attention) and the Integrated Gaussian Prediction module (fusing monocular features into both cost volume and refinement stages) are novel components not present in prior 3DGS pipelines. Compared to the concurrent DepthSplat, MonoSplat avoids a redundant dual-branch design by extracting both multi-view and monocular features from the same frozen backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that pre-trained geometric priors from depth foundation models can substantially improve zero-shot cross-domain generalization in feed-forward 3DGS, with the Re10k→DTU gap (15.25 vs. 12.89/13.94 PSNR) being notably larger than typical in-domain SOTA margins. This suggests a practical pathway for improving 3D reconstruction robustness without per-scene optimization or massive training data. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic scenes, whether other foundation model types (segmentation, normal estimation) could provide complementary priors, and whether the frozen-backbone constraint limits ceiling performance on in-domain tasks. The modest in-domain gains (~0.3 dB over MVSplat) indicate the foundation model priors are most valuable precisely where prior methods struggle most—out-of-distribution content.
