## Problem

Predicting diverse, plausible object motions from a single static image remains open because existing video generators entangle object motion with camera motion and scene appearance changes, and motion-arrow-based methods require predefined motions trained on synthetic data. No training-free approach exists that can automatically discover multiple distinct, object-focused motions without explicit motion input or fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251125/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a static RGB image (H×W×3) and a binary object mask (H×W) as input, and produces a set of up to 6 diverse 2D motion vector fields (F×H×W×2), where each field encodes per-pixel 2D offsets across F frames defining the trajectory of every pixel. Motions are generated in the latent space of a VAE-compressed representation of the Motion-I2V backbone, and the output is specifically constrained so that only the masked object moves while the camera remains static. A stopping criterion (guidance energy threshold ρ=5.0, stop after two consecutive discards) determines when no further distinct motions are available for a given scene.

## Method

Motion Modes is a training-free inference-time method that guides the denoising process of a pre-trained flow-based image-to-video generator (Motion-I2V) with four combined energy functions: (i) static camera energy penalizing average motion magnitude outside the object mask, (ii) object motion energy encouraging a gap between motion inside and outside the mask via a soft-inverse activation, (iii) diversity energy that repels the current sample from all previously generated motions using an angle-weighted (0.75) and magnitude-weighted (0.25) distance, and (iv) a smoothness energy penalizing large frame-to-frame motion changes. The combined energy E = 3.0·E_d + 0.2·E_c + 0.025·E_o + 0.1·E_s is minimized by taking a gradient step on the predicted clean motion at each denoising step, modifying the standard diffusion sampling trajectory without any weight updates. Motions are sampled iteratively, with each new sample conditioned on the growing set of previously accepted motions, enabling unbounded diversity without the memory constraints of batch-based particle guidance.

## Results

On a dataset of 28 input images, Motion Modes achieves the best diversity score (Ē_d = 1.04) and the best focus score (Ē_f = 0.07) among all baselines, with near-zero camera motion (Ē_c = 0.09) and strong object motion (Ē_o = 0.05). In a user study with 32 participants, motions were judged more plausible, diverse, and expected than all baselines in two-alternative forced choice comparisons. A second study with 12 participants found that 96% of generated motions were deemed plausible, 92% of participants' expected motions were captured, and 19% of motions were plausible yet outside initial expectations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Motion Modes outperforms all five baselines on both diversity and focus metrics simultaneously. The Prompt Generation baseline (GPT-4o prompts fed to Motion-I2V) achieves reasonable diversity (Ē_d = 1.28) but suffers from significant camera motion and hallucinated secondary objects (Ē_f = 1.71). ControlNet (MotionBrush) and Random Arrows (MotionDrag) appear to have good focus scores (Ē_f ≈ 1.14–1.17), but this is misleading: they achieve it by suppressing nearly all motion (Ē_o ≈ 2.22–2.27), producing mostly static scenes. Random Noise and FPS Noise baselines, which sample the motion prior without guidance, entangle object and camera motion heavily (Ē_f = 2.20 and 1.98 respectively). The key advantage of Motion Modes is that it fixes the camera (Ē_c = 0.09) while simultaneously encouraging strong object motion (Ē_o = 0.05), a combination no baseline achieves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Motion-I2V [28], a diffusion-based image-to-video generator that explicitly separates motion (as 2D flow fields) from appearance, and repurposes its denoising process as a motion prior to be explored at inference time. The key conceptual departure from prior motion-control methods (MotionBrush, MotionDrag, AnimateAnything) is the elimination of any explicit motion input—arrows, prompts, or drag trajectories—replacing it with energy-based guidance that steers the denoising trajectory toward focused, diverse object motions. The diversity mechanism draws on particle guidance [8] for non-i.i.d. sampling, while the inference-time energy minimization follows the classifier-free guidance paradigm [14] adapted to a multi-objective setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a pre-trained video generator's latent motion distribution can be probed without any additional training to discover diverse, physically plausible object motions—a capability that could streamline motion exploration in creative tools and reduce the burden on users who must mentally predict and then specify complex motions. Key limitations include inheriting the video generator's data biases, the discrete (rather than continuous) nature of the sampled motion set, and the linear computational cost in the number of desired motions. Open questions include extending to coupled camera-object motion (e.g., tracking shots), 3D/4D motion output, and whether the energy-based guidance generalizes across different video diffusion architectures beyond Motion-I2V.
