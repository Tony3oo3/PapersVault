## Problem

Existing typographic attacks on large vision-language models rely on manually predefined adversarial text, rigid placement patterns (center or margin), and produce visually unnatural results that cannot be deployed in physical environments. No prior work generates scene-coherent typographic attacks that adapt to specific image-question pairs, strategically place text in contextually optimal locations, and maintain visual naturalness sufficient for real-world physical deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252032/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, a text question, and the known correct answer as input. It produces three structured outputs: a concise adversarial text (1–3 English words), a placement specification identified via set-of-mark segmentation region indices, and a final scene-coherent adversarial image where the text is rendered with natural lighting, perspective, and surface texture. The generated image can be printed and physically placed in the corresponding real-world location, enabling the attack to transfer from digital to physical settings.

## Method

SceneTAP is a training-free, three-stage LLM-driven planning pipeline that uses ChatGPT-4o as the reasoning engine. Stage 1 (adversarial text generation) employs chain-of-thought prompting to analyze the scene, identify question-relevant objects, and select a contextually plausible incorrect answer condensed to 1–3 words. Stage 2 (placement determination) combines free-text location descriptions with set-of-mark (SoM) segmentation to pinpoint the optimal insertion region near question-targeted objects. Stage 3 (scene-coherent insertion) uses an LLM-generated caption prompt to drive TextDiffuser-2, which renders the adversarial text into the specified region with realistic surface integration. A revisable inference loop allows the LLM to self-correct placement decisions (e.g., avoiding occlusion of critical attributes, selecting writable surfaces), and the entire pipeline requires no gradient access or model fine-tuning.

## Results

SceneTAP achieves a 44.32% average Attack Success Rate (ASR) on two-choice questions (TypoD-base), substantially outperforming Center Attack (29.12%) and Margin Attack (26.45%). On open-ended VQA (VQAv2), SceneTAP reaches 62.10% ASR versus 47.93% (Center) and 47.39% (Margin). Against the most resilient model, ChatGPT-4o, SceneTAP lifts ASR from a 15.83% baseline to 33.79%, compared to 23.44% (Center) and 19.05% (Margin). SceneTAP also achieves a Naturalness Score (N-Score) of ~5.36/10, far exceeding Center Attack (~1.76) and Margin Attack (~0.95), yielding the highest Comprehensive Score (C-Score) across all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SceneTAP consistently outperforms both baselines on ASR across all four evaluated models and all three datasets. Against Center Attack [1], SceneTAP improves average ASR by 15.2 pp on two-choice questions (44.32% vs. 29.12%) and by 14.2 pp on open-ended VQA (62.10% vs. 47.93%); the gap is largest on LingoQA where SceneTAP reaches 73.4% ASR on ChatGPT-4o versus 50.9% for Center Attack. Against Margin Attack [2], the improvements are even more pronounced: 17.9 pp on two-choice (44.32% vs. 26.45%) and 14.7 pp on VQA (62.10% vs. 47.39%). On the most robust target, ChatGPT-4o, SceneTAP achieves 33.79% ASR, exceeding Center Attack (23.44%) by 10.4 pp and Margin Attack (19.05%) by 14.7 pp. Beyond raw attack success, SceneTAP's N-Score of ~5.36/10 is roughly 3× that of Center Attack (~1.76) and ~5.6× that of Margin Attack (~0.95), indicating substantially more natural and scene-coherent text integration that enables physical-world deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the typographic attack paradigm for LVLMs (established by Center Attack and Margin Attack) by replacing fixed text and rigid placement with an LLM-driven planning pipeline. It repurposes TextDiffuser-2 as a scene-coherent text rendering engine and set-of-mark prompting for spatial grounding, combining them under a chain-of-thought LLM planner that jointly decides what text to generate, where to place it, and how to integrate it naturally. The key departure from prior typographic attacks is the shift from a single-step insertion (predefined text at a fixed location) to a multi-stage, context-aware planning process that produces physically deployable adversarial examples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that scene-coherent, natural-looking adversarial text does not sacrifice attack effectiveness—contrary to the expected trade-off—while enabling physical-world deployment, a setting entirely unexplored for typographic attacks. This raises practical security concerns for LVLMs deployed in safety-critical contexts (e.g., autonomous driving) where a printed sign or sticker could mislead the system. Open questions include whether the LLM planner generalizes to more complex multi-object scenes, whether the physical attack transfers across camera angles and lighting conditions, and what defense mechanisms (beyond the limited prior work) can detect scene-coherent typographic manipulation without degrading normal performance.
