## Problem

Existing large-scale appearance-based gaze datasets suffer from inherent annotation errors due to camera calibration and 3D head pose estimation, and provide only a single gaze direction vector without any 3D eyeball structure annotations (pupil, iris, eyeball center, optical/visual axes). This limits the ability of deep learning methods to model the underlying anatomical geometry of gaze and perform more complex gaze-related tasks such as optical/visual axis estimation or 3D eyeball reconstruction.

## Input / Output

The dataset provides full-face RGB images at 2560×1440 resolution captured simultaneously from 9 virtual cameras positioned on a ~5 m sphere, with exact camera intrinsics and extrinsics. For standard gaze estimation experiments, images are resized to 224×224; for vergence depth estimation, two stacked eye patches are input to a ResNet-50. Outputs span multiple granularities: a 3D unit head gaze vector, per-eye 3D eyeball and pupil centers, a 10-point 2D iris contour, per-eye optical and visual axis directions, and a scalar gaze vergence depth, all accompanied by full face and eyeball meshes.

## Method

The synthesis pipeline renders 56 diverse MetaHuman characters in Unreal Engine, randomly sampling gaze (±120°/±70°), head pose (±80°/±80°), expressions (6 types × 4 intensities), blinking, pupil size, and illumination (0.25–18 lux, 1700–12000 K) to produce 1,080,000 frames. Eyeball rotation is computed by first deriving the visual axis from the gaze target and eyeball center, then applying a per-subject kappa angle correction to obtain the optical axis, which directly determines the rendered pupil position. 3D annotations (eyeball center, radius, cornea radius, pupil, iris, optical/visual axes) are extracted geometrically from the MetaHuman meshes in Blender using spherical fitting and anatomical priors, yielding ground-truth labels with no measurement error. For experiments, ResNet-18/50 backbones are trained with L1 loss on 3D unit vectors (10 epochs, Adam, lr 1e-4); the 3D structure estimation task uses a composite loss of L1 on 3D eyeball/pupil centers, L1 on iris contour pixels, and angular error on the optical axis derived from the predicted centers.

## Results

In within-domain gaze estimation, ResNet-50 trained on GazeGene achieves 3.36° angular error, the best across all five datasets tested (vs. 5.32° on ETH-XGaze, 4.20° on MPII). In cross-domain evaluation, models trained on GazeGene achieve the lowest error on ETH-XGaze (12.37° with ResNet-50) and MPIIFaceGaze (6.86° with ResNet-50), outperforming all other training sources. For 3D eyeball structure estimation, ResNet-18 trained on GazeGene achieves 0.11 cm 3D eyeball center error and 4.98° optical axis error, dramatically outperforming the pretrained 3DGazeNet (17.63° optical axis error). The paper also quantifies annotation inconsistency in ETH-XGaze at 2.91° (gaze) and 4.96° (head pose) across camera views, explaining the ~2° accuracy gap between GazeGene and ETH-XGaze.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a dataset paper, GazeGene is compared against five existing gaze datasets (ETH-XGaze, MPIIFaceGaze, Gaze360, EyeDiap, EVE) as alternative training sources. GazeGene-trained models dominate cross-domain transfer to ETH-XGaze (12.87° vs. 18.51° for Gaze360, 30.18° for EVE) and MPIIFaceGaze (6.97° vs. 7.2° for ETH-XGaze, 7.89° for Gaze360), confirming superior annotation accuracy and diversity. In within-domain evaluation, all five tested methods (FullFace, Dilated-Net, GazeTR-H, ResNet-18, ResNet-50) achieve their best angular error on GazeGene, with ResNet-50 reaching 3.36°—roughly 2° better than on ETH-XGaze (5.32°). For 3D eyeball structure estimation, ResNet-18 trained on GazeGene achieves 4.98° optical axis error versus 17.63° for the pretrained 3DGazeNet [41], a 3.5× improvement, attributed to GazeGene's precise 3D annotations versus 3DGazeNet's pseudo-labels from mesh alignment. The paper also shows that applying ground-truth kappa to optical axis predictions (4.2°) slightly outperforms direct visual axis regression (4.2° vs. 4.1° for ResNet-50), suggesting kappa-aware pipelines are a promising direction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in synthetic gaze dataset construction by departing from prior synthetic efforts (UnityEyes, UT-Multiview) that only generated eye-region crops with 2D annotations. It repurposes the MetaHuman/Unreal Engine/Blender pipeline to produce full-face, photo-realistic images with precise 3D eyeball structure annotations (eyeball center, pupil, iris, optical and visual axes) that no existing remote gaze dataset provides. Conceptually, it addresses the annotation-error problem inherent in geometric-based real datasets (ETH-XGaze, Gaze360, MPIIFaceGaze) by eliminating calibration and head-pose estimation errors through virtual rendering, while matching their distribution ranges in gaze and head pose.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GazeGene fills a critical data gap by being the first large-scale remote gaze dataset with accurate 3D eyeball annotations, enabling previously infeasible tasks such as 3D eyeball center estimation, optical/visual axis regression, and gaze vergence depth prediction from remote face images. The cross-domain results showing GazeGene outperforming real datasets on ETH-XGaze (12.87° vs. 15.42° for ResNet-18) suggest synthetic data can now serve as a primary training resource rather than a domain-adaptation supplement. Open questions remain: whether deep learning can truly learn inter-subject kappa variation from data alone, whether the synthetic-to-real gap closes with more diverse backgrounds and lighting, and whether the 3D structure estimation results generalize beyond the controlled synthetic setting.
