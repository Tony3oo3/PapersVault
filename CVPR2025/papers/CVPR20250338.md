## Problem

Despite the proliferation of LMM video benchmarks targeting high-level semantic understanding, no systematic benchmark exists to evaluate LMMs' ability to perceive and assess low-level video quality. This gap is critical because video quality understanding—spanning technical, aesthetic, temporal, and AIGC-specific distortions—is essential for compression optimization, viewer experience, and quality control in AI-generated video, yet remains unexplored in the LMM evaluation landscape.

## Input / Output

Each benchmark item consists of a video (single clip or a pair of same-source clips) presented as uniformly sampled frames (16 for video LMMs, 8 for image LMMs) paired with a text quality query. The expected output is a Yes/No judgment, a multiple-choice selection from predefined options, or a free-form open-ended quality description. For open-ended responses, accuracy is scored by a GPT-assisted evaluator comparing the model's answer against the expert-annotated reference on accuracy, completeness, and relevance.

## Method

Q-Bench-Video is constructed from 1,800 videos uniformly sampled across the quality spectrum from seven source datasets (LSVQ, MaxWell, WaterlooSQoE-III/IV, T2VQA-DB, VideoFeedback, LIVE-YT-Gaming) covering natural, AIGC, and CG content, yielding 2,378 expert-annotated QA pairs. The benchmark introduces three question types (Yes-or-No with balanced 50:50 answer distribution, What-How multiple-choice, and Open-ended) crossed with four quality dimensions (Technical, Aesthetic, Temporal, AIGC distortions) and two video settings (single-video global/referring perception and video-pair joint/comparative analysis, further split into coarse- and fine-grain comparisons). Annotation is performed by 8 trained experts in a controlled lab setting with each QA pair cross-reviewed by at least three additional experts. Evaluation is conducted on 17 LMMs (12 open-source, 5 proprietary) using direct accuracy for closed-form questions and GPT-assisted scoring for open-ended responses, with results reported on a held-out test subset of 1,186 items.

## Results

On Q-Bench-Video (test subset, 1,186 QA items), the best proprietary LMM GPT-4o achieves 58.70% overall accuracy, while the best open-source LMM mPLUG-Owl3 reaches 52.39%. Human annotators score 81.56% overall, leaving a 22.86% gap to the best LMM. Open-ended questions are the hardest subcategory for all LMMs (best ~49.25% for GPT-4o vs. 77.11% for humans), and AIGC distortions are the weakest quality dimension for LMMs (best 53.26% for Gemini 1.5 Pro vs. 86.21% for humans).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o (58.70%) edges out Gemini 1.5 Pro (58.63%) by a narrow 0.07% margin for the best overall score, with GPT-4o leading on Temporal (65.39% vs. 53.38%) and Yes-or-No (67.48% vs. 65.42%) while Gemini 1.5 Pro leads on Aesthetic (69.61% vs. 58.57%) and What-How (62.35% vs. 58.79%). Among open-source models, mPLUG-Owl3 (52.39%) slightly surpasses GPT-4o mini (52.20%), making it the top open-source performer, followed by LLaVA-OneVision (51.70%) and InternVL-Chat (51.11%). All 17 LMMs substantially outperform random guess (37.79%), confirming basic video quality perception, yet none approaches the human level of 81.56%. The largest LMM-vs-human gap appears on AIGC distortions (best LMM 53.26% vs. human 86.21%, a 33-point gap), while the smallest gap is on Aesthetic distortions where LLaVA-OneVision reaches 64.15% versus 80.23% for humans. ST-LLM is the weakest model overall (35.42%), falling below random guess on several subcategories, while VideoChat2 (40.56%) and Video-LLaVA (43.49%) also trail the leading open-source models by 8–12 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Q-Bench lineage (image quality benchmarking for LMMs) and A-Bench (AIGC image quality) into the video domain, introducing the first systematic benchmark for video quality understanding. It departs from existing video LMM benchmarks (MVBench, Video-MME, MMBench-Video) that focus exclusively on high-level semantic comprehension, and from traditional VQA methods that produce scalar scores without interpretability. The addition of AIGC distortions as a fourth quality dimension and the video-pair comparison task represent novel extensions beyond prior VQA formulations, which typically assess single videos against fixed reference or no-reference criteria.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a persistent ~23% accuracy gap between the best LMM (GPT-4o) and humans, confirming that video quality perception remains an unsolved capability for current models. The finding that LMMs excel at aesthetic (semantically rich) distortions but struggle with AIGC-specific anomalies highlights a training-data coverage gap that will grow more critical as generative video becomes mainstream. Open questions include whether targeted fine-tuning on quality-specific data can close the gap, how to handle the rapidly evolving distortion patterns of new AIGC models, and whether the subjectivity inherent in aesthetic and AIGC quality judgments can be better formalized for automated evaluation.
