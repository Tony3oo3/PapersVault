## Problem

MLLMs are pretrained for 2D image-space reasoning, but autonomous driving planning operates natively in 3D space, creating a representation gap that limits their spatial reasoning for motion planning. Purely self-supervised end-to-end planners (no human annotations) consistently underperform supervised multi-task approaches, and existing benchmarks like nuScenes are too small to prevent overfitting of billion-parameter MLLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252803/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 8 multi-view camera images across T+1 frames (0.5s interval), historical ego-vehicle states (locations, velocities, accelerations serialized as two-decimal floating-point text), and one of six high-level behavior commands (go straight forward/left/right, do left/right turn, do left U-turn). Output is a future trajectory of (x, y) BEV waypoints at each time step over a 5s horizon, preceded by a four-class meta-decision (keep stationary, keep speed, accelerate, decelerate); both are extracted from decoded text tokens. At inference, K trajectories are generated via nucleus sampling and averaged to mitigate the MLLM's bias toward simple behaviors.

## Method

S4-Driver builds on PaLI3-5B (frozen ViT-G 2B encoder + 3B multimodal encoder-decoder) and introduces a sparse volume representation that lifts 2D perspective-view features into a 3D ego-centered volume via bilinear projection, then selects only M most relevant voxels using a learned gate (MLP on reduced-dimension volume features). Each selected voxel's feature is a gate-weighted blend of its semantic feature and a learnable vacant feature, preserving the pretrained feature distribution while concentrating capacity on planning-relevant regions. Multi-frame temporal fusion concatenates per-frame sparse volume features along the channel dimension and fuses them with an FC layer, while a relative 3D position bias is injected into the self-attention to enable local spatial aggregation without extra convolutional modules. Training is fully self-supervised (ego-vehicle trajectory only), and the model is pretrained on the newly introduced WOMD-Planning-ADE benchmark (103k sequences, 574h, ~100× nuScenes) before finetuning, demonstrating strong scalability with data volume.

## Results

On the nuScenes benchmark, S4-Driver achieves an average L2 displacement error of 0.38 m (1.0s: 0.16, 2.0s: 0.34, 3.0s: 0.63) without any human annotations, outperforming all prior multi-task and MLLM-based methods. With pretraining on the larger WOMD-Planning-ADE dataset (S4-Driver*), the average improves to 0.31 m, surpassing even OmniDrive (0.33 m) which requires perception and prediction annotations. On the WOMD-Planning-ADE benchmark, S4-Driver achieves ADE@5s of 0.693 m and behavior-wise bADE@5s of 0.928 m, improving over vanilla PaLI (0.798 / 1.069) and matching or exceeding MotionLM (0.697 / 0.978) despite using only raw camera images rather than auto-labelled objects, tracks, and road graphs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S4-Driver (0.38 m avg L2) outperforms all multi-task end-to-end baselines on nuScenes: UniAD (0.66 m), PARA-Drive (0.48 m), and VAD (0.37 m), despite requiring no perception or prediction annotations. Among MLLM-based methods, S4-Driver beats GPT-Driver (0.44 m), DriveVLM (0.40 m), and the self-supervised DriveVLM w/o CoT variant (0.49 m), and slightly trails OmniDrive (0.33 m) which uses extra data and labels. With WOMD pretraining, S4-Driver* (0.31 m) surpasses OmniDrive and all other methods. On WOMD-Planning-ADE, S4-Driver (ADE@5s: 0.693, bADE@5s: 0.928) improves substantially over vanilla PaLI (0.798 / 1.069) and matches MotionLM on sample-wise ADE (0.693 vs. 0.697) while clearly winning on behavior-wise bADE (0.928 vs. 0.978), despite MotionLM using auto-labelled objects, tracks, and road graphs as inputs rather than raw camera images.

## Relation to Prior Work

S4-Driver builds on the PaLI multimodal large language model as its backbone and borrows the lightweight perspective-to-3D projection strategy from Simple-BEV, but departs fundamentally from prior MLLM-based driving approaches (DriveVLM, GPT-Driver, OmniDrive) that rely on multi-task learning, chain-of-thought reasoning, or human-annotated perception/prediction supervision. Instead, it positions itself as a purely self-supervised planner that learns 3D scene understanding implicitly from raw camera images and ego-vehicle trajectories alone. The sparse volume representation is the key architectural departure: it transforms frozen 2D perspective-view features into a 3D spatio-temporal representation without finetuning the vision encoder, preserving the pretrained world knowledge while enabling 3D spatial reasoning. The paper also introduces the WOMD-Planning-ADE benchmark, extending the public Waymo Open Motion Dataset with in-house camera data and behavior-wise evaluation metrics to address the data-scale and distribution-imbalance limitations of nuScenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that a self-supervised MLLM planner with no human annotations can match or exceed supervised multi-task approaches on nuScenes—challenges the prevailing assumption that explicit perception and prediction supervision is necessary for competitive end-to-end driving. The sparse volume representation offers a practical, low-cost technique for bridging the 2D-to-3D gap in MLLMs that could transfer to other architectures beyond PaLI. However, several open questions remain: the main large-scale benchmark relies on in-house camera data limiting reproducibility, evaluation is open-loop only, and the approach has been validated on a single MLLM family. The authors note that combining large-scale self-supervised pretraining with targeted supervised fine-tuning on small labeled datasets may further improve performance and interpretability, suggesting a hybrid paradigm as a promising direction.
