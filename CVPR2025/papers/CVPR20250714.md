## Problem

Existing panorama novel view synthesis methods are constrained to 512×1024 resolution due to prohibitive memory and compute costs, falling far short of the 4K (2048×4096) resolution needed for immersive VR. Additionally, standard pixel-aligned 3D Gaussian Splatting is ill-suited to the spherical geometry of panoramas, producing redundant Gaussians near the poles, and current feed-forward 3DGS methods cannot scale efficiently to high resolutions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250714/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two posed wide-baseline equirectangular panoramas at up to 4K (2048×4096) resolution as input and produces a novel-view panorama at the same resolution. Internally, geometry (depth) is estimated at a downsampled 512×1024 resolution while appearance features are processed at full resolution, and the final panorama is rendered via a cubemap renderer (six 90°-FOV faces stitched with differentiable grid sampling). The intermediate 3D representation is a four-level spherical 3D Gaussian pyramid with Gaussians placed on a Fibonacci lattice rather than pixel-aligned.

## Method

PanSplat introduces a spherical 3D Gaussian pyramid where Gaussians are distributed via a Fibonacci lattice (reducing count by 36.34% without quality loss) across four resolution levels, paired with a hierarchical spherical cost volume built on a Swin Transformer FPN with cross-view attention and 2D U-Nets that integrate monocular depth priors for coarse-to-fine depth estimation. Lightweight Gaussian heads with local CNN operations and a residual skip-connection design between adjacent pyramid levels predict per-Gaussian parameters. A key training innovation is two-step deferred backpropagation: the full panorama is first rendered without gradients to cache image-space losses, then gradients are backpropagated face-by-face to Gaussian parameters and tile-by-tile to network weights, enabling 4K training on a single 80GB A100 GPU. A deferred blending mechanism mitigates artifacts from moving objects by re-weighting misaligned Gaussians during compositing.

## Results

On Matterport3D (the training dataset), PanSplat achieves WS-PSNR of 28.81, SSIM 0.931, and LPIPS 0.091 at the 1.0 m baseline, outperforming the strongest baseline MVSplat (28.19 / 0.912 / 0.105) and the prior SOTA PanoGRF (27.12 / 0.876 / 0.195). Generalization to wider baselines is particularly strong: at 2.0 m, PanSplat reaches WS-PSNR 20.56 versus MVSplat's 13.31 and PanoGRF's 20.96. On real-world 360Loc data, deferred blending lifts PanSplat to WS-PSNR 28.14 (vs. MVSplat's 24.67), and inference runs in 0.34 s per frame—up to 70× faster than PanoGRF's 23.8 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PanSplat outperforms all six baselines on Matterport3D across every baseline distance (1.0, 1.5, 2.0 m), with the largest margin over MVSplat at 2.0 m (WS-PSNR 20.56 vs. 13.31, a 7.25 dB gap) where MVSplat degrades severely. Against the prior panorama SOTA PanoGRF, PanSplat gains +1.69 WS-PSNR at 1.0 m and +0.60 at 2.0 m, while being 70× faster (0.34 s vs. 23.8 s per frame). On the unseen Replica dataset, PanSplat edges out MVSplat (30.78 vs. 30.54 WS-PSNR) and PanoGRF (29.22), confirming strong cross-dataset generalization. On Residential, MVSplat slightly leads on WS-PSNR (31.21 vs. 30.97), but PanSplat wins on SSIM (0.917 vs. 0.906) and LPIPS (0.172 vs. 0.200), indicating better perceptual quality. On real-world 360Loc and Insta360 data, PanSplat with deferred blending surpasses MVSplat by +3.47 and +2.44 WS-PSNR respectively, demonstrating that the deferred blending module is critical for handling moving-object artifacts in captured panoramas.

## Relation to Prior Work

PanSplat extends the feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting framework (MVSplat) from perspective images to the panoramic domain, addressing two challenges that make direct application infeasible: the spherical geometry that causes Gaussian redundancy near the poles, and the memory demands of 4K resolution. It departs from NeRF-based panorama methods (PanoGRF, OmniSyn) by replacing volumetric sampling with Gaussian rasterization, and from pixel-aligned splatting by introducing a Fibonacci lattice arrangement and a multi-scale Gaussian pyramid. The hierarchical spherical cost volume draws on cascade cost volume ideas from multi-view stereo, while the two-step deferred backpropagation adapts memory-saving strategies from artistic radiance fields to the Gaussian splatting setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PanSplat is the first feed-forward method to support 4K (2048×4096) panorama novel view synthesis, making it directly applicable to immersive VR and virtual tour applications where lower resolutions are insufficient. The 70× speedup over the prior SOTA (PanoGRF) and the ability to train on a single A100 GPU significantly lower the barrier to deploying high-quality panorama synthesis. A key limitation is the lack of support for dynamic scenes with moving objects, which is a common requirement in real-world captures; extending the method to handle motion-aware representations remains an open problem. The deferred blending technique partially mitigates artifacts from moving objects but does not fully solve the dynamic scene challenge.
