## Problem

LUT-based super-resolution networks rely on fixed, manually-designed sampling patterns that cannot adapt to pixel-level variations, limiting both receptive field size and fine-detail reconstruction. Additionally, standard residual connections are incompatible with LUTs because summing two [0,255] values exceeds the valid lookup range, preventing effective inter-layer information flow. These constraints make it difficult to improve accuracy without inflating storage, which is critical for deployment on resource-limited edge devices.

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution RGB image and produces a high-resolution version at ×2 or ×4 scale. During training, the network operates on 48×48 patches from DIV2K, but at inference the trained CNN is exported as LUTs where each k×k input patch is mapped via table lookup rather than convolution. A critical constraint is that all intermediate values must remain within [0,255] to keep LUT indices valid, which governs the design of both the sampling and residual mechanisms.

## Method

AutoSample replaces fixed pixel selection with a learnable 1×1 convolution over a k×k input patch, where softmax-normalized spatial weights produce four weighted pixel combinations that are rearranged via pixel shuffle into a 2×2 output; combined with rotation ensemble, this expands the effective receptive field to (2k−1)×(2k−1) with a single branch instead of three fixed ones. AdaRL introduces a convex combination between the current and previous layer's sampled outputs using learnable 2×2 residual weights clamped to [0,1], guaranteeing outputs stay within [0,255] without LUT size explosion. Both modules are plug-and-play and are applied to MuLUT and SPF-LUT, with a LUT-aware fine-tuning phase where table entries are treated as trainable parameters. The approach reduces storage by over 50% and inference time by roughly two-thirds on SPF-LUT while maintaining comparable PSNR, and improves MuLUT by ~0.20 dB at nearly identical storage.

## Results

On ×4 super-resolution, MuLUT+Ours (3 branches, sample size 5) achieves 30.85 dB PSNR on Set5 and 28.27 dB on Manga109, improving over MuLUT by +0.20 dB on average across five datasets while using nearly identical storage (4.067 MB vs. 4.062 MB). The lightweight SPF-Light variant achieves 31.02 dB on Set5 with only 0.907 MB storage—55% less than SPF-LUT+DFC (2.018 MB)—while maintaining comparable PSNR. On edge-device inference, MuLUT+Ours 1×5 reduces runtime from 5938 ms to 2044 ms (≈66% reduction) while slightly exceeding MuLUT's PSNR, and SPF-Light cuts runtime from 31922 ms to 9911 ms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the LUT-based baselines, MuLUT+Ours outperforms MuLUT by +0.25 dB on Set5 and +0.37 dB on Manga109 at essentially the same storage (4.067 vs. 4.062 MB), and also beats MuLUT+DFC by +0.30 dB on Set5 despite using 10× more storage. SPF-Light matches SPF-LUT+DFC within 0.03 dB on Set5 (31.02 vs. 31.05) while using 55% less storage (0.907 vs. 2.018 MB), and reduces inference time from 31.9 s to 9.9 s on a mobile device. Against classical sparse-coding methods (A+, ANR, NE+LLE), both MuLUT+Ours and SPF-Light achieve substantially higher PSNR (e.g., 30.85 vs. 30.27 on Set5 for A+) with far less storage. Against DNN baselines (RRDB, EDSR, RCAN, SwinIR), the LUT-based methods trail by roughly 1.5–1.8 dB on Set5, but the DNNs require 15–190× more storage (59–170 MB vs. 0.9–4.1 MB), making the LUT variants far more practical for edge deployment. The paper's key claim is that AutoSample and AdaRL close much of the gap between LUT-based and DNN-based SR while preserving the orders-of-magnitude storage and speed advantages of LUTs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LUT-based super-resolution lineage (SR-LUT → MuLUT → SPF-LUT) by addressing two specific architectural limitations: the reliance on fixed, hand-crafted sampling patterns and the absence of residual connections due to LUT range constraints. AutoSample replaces MuLUT's three fixed four-pixel sampling branches with a learnable convolutional sampling layer (softmax-normalized weights + pixel shuffle), while AdaRL introduces a convex-combination residual that stays within [0,255] without inflating LUT size. Both modules are designed as plug-and-play components that can be inserted into any existing LUT-group architecture, positioning the work as an incremental but broadly applicable improvement over the MuLUT/SPF-LUT family rather than a new framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling LUT-based SR on resource-constrained edge devices: SPF-Light achieves comparable PSNR to SPF-LUT+DFC with over 50% less storage and roughly two-thirds less inference time on a mobile CPU. The plug-and-play design means the gains transfer to any LUT-based restoration network, not just SR. Open questions include whether the learned sampling weights develop interpretable spatial structure, whether the approach generalizes to other LUT-based tasks (denoising, deblurring, video SR), and whether the modest +0.20 dB gain on MuLUT scales to larger sample sizes or deeper networks without the diminishing returns observed at k=7.
