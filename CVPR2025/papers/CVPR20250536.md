## Problem

Existing pose-agnostic anomaly detection (PAD) methods rely on radiance field representations (NeRF, 3DGS) that bake in global illumination, making them fail when the lighting configuration of query images differs from training images. This paper introduces the more realistic Pose and Illumination agnostic Anomaly Detection (PIAD) problem, where both camera pose and illumination are unknown and may differ between training and query, and provides the first dataset and baseline to address this setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of N posed anomaly-free training images (with known camera poses from COLMAP) and a single query RGB image, all downsampled to 400×400. Training images are first decomposed into reflectance maps via URetinexNet before 3DGS training. The output is a pixel-level anomaly heatmap (400×400×4 multi-scale features), an image-level anomaly score, and the estimated camera pose; a rendered reference image and its reflectance map are also produced from the 3DGS at the optimized pose for comparison.

## Method

The key insight is augmenting the 3D Gaussian Splatting representation to store both view-dependent radiance and illumination-invariant reflectance (computed via URetinexNet intrinsic decomposition), enabling pose estimation under varying lighting. Pose initialization uses an MAE-based filter on reflectance maps followed by EfficientLoFTR feature matching to select the closest training view. Pose refinement iteratively optimizes SE(3) exponential coordinates by minimizing a weighted combination of reflectance MAE (λ=0.6) and color MAE, exploiting the fact that reflectance is robust to illumination while color preserves fine-grained detail. Anomaly detection compares multi-scale features (4 scales) from a pre-trained EfficientNet-B4 backbone on both color and reflectance channels, combining them via element-wise multiplication of L2 feature differences to produce the final anomaly score.

## Results

On the MAD synthetic dataset, PIAD achieves 99.5 pixel AUROC and 97.4 image AUROC, outperforming OmniposeAD (98.4 / 91.9) and SplatPose (99.0 / 94.9). On the authors' own synthetic PIAD dataset with inconsistent illumination, the gap widens substantially: 99.0 pixel AUROC and 96.7 image AUROC versus 96.9/84.9 (OmniposeAD) and 97.4/85.9 (SplatPose). On the real-world PIAD dataset (14 industrial objects), the mean pixel AUROC is 99.05 and image AUROC is 92.63, compared to 94.86/73.20 (OmniposeAD) and 98.01/79.82 (SplatPose). Total runtime per query is 7.42 s, faster than SplatPose (8.40 s) and far faster than OmniposeAD (51.70 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OmniposeAD (Zhou et al., 2023), PIAD wins on every reported metric across all three datasets. The advantage is most dramatic under the PIAD setting (inconsistent illumination): on the authors' synthetic dataset, image-level AUROC jumps from 84.9 to 96.7 (+11.8 pts), and on the real dataset from 73.20 to 92.63 (+19.4 pts). Pose estimation AUC@5° in the PIAD setting improves from 27.36 to 65.22, and total runtime drops from 51.70 s to 7.42 s. Against SplatPose (Kruse et al., CVPRW 2024), PIAD also wins on all anomaly-detection metrics, with the largest gains on image-level AUROC (e.g., 96.7 vs 85.9 on synthetic, 92.63 vs 79.82 on real). In the PIAD pose-estimation setting, PIAD achieves 84.20 AUC@0.2 m versus SplatPose's 64.24, a 20-point gap in translational accuracy. SplatPose remains competitive on pixel-level AUROC in the PAD setting (99.0 vs 99.5 on MAD), but PIAD is consistently ahead once illumination varies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pose-agnostic anomaly detection (PAD) line initiated by OmniposeAD (Zhou et al., 2023) and SplatPose (Kruse et al., 2024) by adding illumination invariance, formulating the new PIAD problem. Methodologically, it follows SplatPose in using 3D Gaussian Splatting as the underlying 3D representation for fast pose optimization, but departs by augmenting the 3DGS with a reflectance channel computed via URetinexNet's Retinex-based intrinsic decomposition, enabling the representation to be illumination-independent. The pose estimation pipeline (exponential coordinates on SE(3), MAE-based filtering, EfficientLoFTR matching) and the anomaly scoring (multi-scale EfficientNet features) are standard components recombined in a novel dual-domain (color + reflectance) framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PIAD addresses a practically critical gap: real industrial inspection rarely guarantees identical lighting between training and query captures, yet all prior pose-agnostic methods silently assume this. The first dedicated PIAD dataset (30 products, dense poses, varied illumination, both synthetic and real) provides a much-needed benchmark that existing datasets (MAD, Real-IAD, Eyecandies) cannot support. Open questions include scaling to larger product catalogs, reducing the dense-view training requirement, and the authors' suggested extension to an active setting where an autonomous system jointly searches over light and camera configurations. A limitation is reliance on multiple pre-trained networks (URETINEX-Net, EfficientLoFTR, EfficientNet-B4), which adds deployment complexity.
