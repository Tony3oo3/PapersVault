## Problem

Existing neural gesture generation systems produce natural rhythmic beat gestures but fail to synthesize semantically meaningful co-speech gestures, because semantic gestures occur too sporadically in training data for data-driven models to learn them reliably. This paper addresses the gap by framing semantic gesture synthesis as a Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) task, combining the naturalness of diffusion-based generation with the semantic grounding of explicit retrieval.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251570/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes speech audio (encoded via wav2vec2), word-level text transcription (BERT embeddings), and a speaker identity embedding as conditioning signals, plus a retrieved gesture exemplar from the BEAT2 database at inference time. The output is a full-body co-speech gesture sequence of N frames, each containing 6D rotations for all body joints, FLAME face parameters, root translation, and foot contact labels. The gesture is internally represented as decoupled VAE latents for four body regions (upper body, hands, face, lower body + translation), with the retrieval insertion operating on these compressed latents rather than raw motion.

## Method

The base generator is a conditional latent-diffusion model with a transformer decoder, trained to denoise decoupled body-region VAE latents conditioned on audio, text, and speaker embeddings. The key RAG mechanism works entirely at inference time: a retrieved exemplar is first mapped into the diffusion latent space via DDIM inversion, then the relevant temporal window is sliced and pasted into the query's initial noise latent (Latent Initialization). A Retrieval Guidance objective then applies gradient-based corrections at each denoising timestep to pull the generated latent toward the inverted retrieval latent, with a tunable number of gradient steps controlling how strongly the retrieval is followed. Two retrieval algorithms determine which exemplar to fetch: an LLM-based approach that predicts gesture type (iconic, deictic, metaphoric) per word, and a discourse-based approach that matches connectives (e.g., 'because', 'on the other hand') to semantically equivalent discourse relations in the database, both further ranked by speaker similarity and prosodic prominence.

## Results

On the BEAT2 test set (all speakers), RAG-GESTURE with LLM-based gesture type retrieval achieves the best BeatAlignment (0.514) and Diversity (118) among all compared methods, while the Discourse-based variant achieves the best FID (0.447). For single-speaker evaluation, the LLM variant attains the best BeatAlignment (0.734) and L1 Divergence (11.97). In perceptual evaluation, RAG-GESTURE is preferred over all baselines for both naturalness and appropriateness, with the LLM-based approach reaching near ground-truth preference levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAG-GESTURE substantially outperforms all four baselines on BeatAlignment, the metric most directly tied to semantic gesture quality: 0.514 (LLM) / 0.471 (Discourse) vs. 0.200 (CaMN), 0.284 (EMAGE), 0.326 (Audio2Photoreal), and 0.218 (ReMoDiffuse). On FID, the Discourse variant (0.447) beats CaMN (0.512), EMAGE (0.692), Audio2Photoreal (0.849), and ReMoDiffuse (1.120), though the LLM variant (0.487) is slightly worse than CaMN. On L1 Divergence, all baselines score lower (better) than RAG-GESTURE (9.94 vs. 5.06–6.24), a gap the authors attribute to baselines using ground-truth seed motions while RAG-GESTURE does not. In perceptual user studies, RAG-GESTURE is significantly preferred over every baseline for both naturalness and appropriateness, with the LLM-based retrieval reaching near ground-truth preference. The paper also shows that its inference-time RAG outperforms a training-based RAG variant (which fine-tunes ReMoDiffuse with the same retrieval algorithm), achieving lower FID (0.447 vs. 0.525) and higher BeatAlignment (0.471 vs. 0.414) without any additional training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the latent-diffusion framework for gesture generation (Rombach et al.) and the decoupled body-region VAE encoding from Convofusion (Mughal et al., 2024), but its core departure is framing semantic gesture insertion as an inference-time Retrieval Augmented Generation problem rather than a training-time conditioning task. Unlike ReMoDiffuse, which trains the diffusion network to follow globally-retrieved motions, or SemanticGesticulator, which fine-tunes a generator to merge retrieved gestures, RAG-GESTURE uses DDIM inversion to project retrieved exemplars into the diffusion latent space and a gradient-based retrieval guidance objective to steer sampling—requiring no additional training. The retrieval algorithms themselves draw on discourse-linguistic structure (connectives like 'because', 'on the other hand') and LLM-based gesture-type prediction, positioning the work at the intersection of computational linguistics and generative motion synthesis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent and well-documented gap in co-speech gesture synthesis: neural generators produce natural but semantically impoverished gestures because semantic gestures are sparse in training data. The inference-time RAG paradigm is attractive for its modularity—any retrieval algorithm can be swapped in without retraining—and the retrieval guidance mechanism offers a principled knob for controlling how strongly the generated motion follows the retrieved exemplar. However, evaluation is confined to a single dataset (BEAT2), the two retrieval strategies (LLM-based vs. discourse-based) show no significant perceptual difference, and the quantitative gains over strong baselines like EMAGE are modest. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other gesture taxonomies (referential, emotion-specific), scales to longer utterances, and whether the LLM-based specification step can be made more reliable for diverse linguistic contexts.
