## Problem

Pre-trained diffusion models (DMs) are primarily trained on RGB images and lack the specific prior knowledge needed for hyperspectral pansharpening. Existing DM-based methods (e.g., PLRDiff, HIR-Diff) rely on fixed transformation matrices and generic fidelity terms, leaving a gap between the RGB generative distribution and the target hyperspectral data, especially when only a single (PAN, LR-HSI) pair is available.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252646/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single panchromatic image (H×W, one band) and a low-resolution hyperspectral image (h×w×S, typically 4× downsampled with 5×5 Gaussian blur) as input. The pre-trained DM first generates an RGB detail image (H×W×3), which is then mapped to the full high-resolution hyperspectral cube (H×W×S) via the NSSD module. The zero-shot guidance network is trained exclusively on this single testing pair using a reduced-resolution self-supervised strategy, requiring no external training dataset.

## Method

The proposed scheme freezes all pre-trained DM parameters and introduces two iteratively updated components. First, a lightweight zero-shot network (CONet) trained on the single (PAN, LR-HSI) pair injects task-specific prior knowledge into the DM's reverse SDE via a guidance term that combines spectral fidelity, spatial fidelity, and a match to the zero-shot network's prediction. Second, a Neural Spatial-Spectral Decomposition (NSSD) replaces the fixed transformation matrix with a flexible factorization X = C_θ1(A) ×₃ D_θ2(Y), where a U-Net extracts spatial factors from the RGB detail image and a fully-connected network extracts spectral factors from the LR-HSI. The DM guidance and NSSD parameters are alternately updated: during each diffusion reverse step the NSSD is held fixed, and after each full reverse pass the NSSD is re-optimized against the generated detail image, making the spectral mapping adaptive to the DM's output.

## Results

On the Pavia dataset, the proposed method achieves the best performance across all five metrics: PSNR 34.227 dB, SSIM 0.926, ERGAS 2.863, SAM 4.463°, and RMSE 5.284. On Washington DC, it reaches PSNR 40.503 dB and SSIM 0.943, and on Chikusei, PSNR 38.450 dB and SSIM 0.939. On the real-world FR1 dataset, the method attains a QNR of 0.832, outperforming the next-best method (ZSL at 0.802) and the diffusion-based baselines (PLRDiff 0.630, HIR-Diff 0.625) by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines on every metric across the three simulated datasets (Pavia, WDC, Chikusei) and on QNR for the real FR1 dataset. Against the strongest diffusion-based competitor PLRDiff [30], the gains are +1.17 dB PSNR on Pavia, +5.70 dB on WDC (where PLRDiff suffers severe spectral distortion with ERGAS 18.202), and +1.27 dB on Chikusei. Versus HIR-Diff [29], improvements are +2.25 dB (Pavia), +2.00 dB (WDC), and +2.06 dB (Chikusei) in PSNR. The single-image method ZSL [11] is the closest non-diffusion baseline, but the proposed method still leads by +2.17 dB (Pavia), +0.82 dB (WDC), and +3.19 dB (Chikusei). Traditional methods GSA [1], CNMF [47], and HySure [32] are consistently 5–8 dB below the proposed method in PSNR, with HySure also producing visible spatial distortions. On the real FR1 dataset, the proposed method's QNR of 0.832 exceeds ZSL (0.802) by 0.03 and PLRDiff/HIR-Diff (≈0.63) by roughly 0.20, confirming the advantage generalizes to real sensor data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the recent line of work on guiding pre-trained diffusion models for hyperspectral pansharpening, specifically building on PLRDiff (Rui et al., 2024) and HIR-Diff (Pang et al., 2024), which both use fixed transformation matrices to map RGB detail images to hyperspectral cubes. The key departure is twofold: (1) a lightweight zero-shot network trained on a single test image pair injects task-specific prior knowledge into the DM's reverse process, replacing the generic fidelity or TV-based guidance used previously; and (2) a neural spatial-spectral decomposition (NSSD) replaces the fixed low-rank transformation with an iteratively updated, data-adaptive mapping. The zero-shot training strategy draws on the 'invariance among scales' assumption from Dian et al. (ZSL, 2023), while the tensor decomposition perspective connects to classical single-image pansharpening methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical gap: pre-trained DMs are RGB-native and lack hyperspectral spectral fidelity, yet the zero-shot guidance requires only a single (PAN, LR-HSI) pair, making it applicable in real-world scenarios where training datasets are unavailable. The iterative coupling between DM guidance and NSSD is a reasonable architectural choice that allows the spectral mapping to adapt to the generated detail. However, the computational cost of iteratively constructing the NSSD mapping is acknowledged as a limitation, and the WDC ERGAS result (12.254) is notably worse than several non-diffusion baselines (GSA: 5.924, CNMF: 5.864), suggesting spectral fidelity gains are not uniform. Open questions include whether pre-trained spectral networks could replace the iterative NSSD optimization and whether the approach scales to higher band counts or larger spatial resolutions.
