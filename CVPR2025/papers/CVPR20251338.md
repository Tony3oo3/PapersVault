## Problem

Existing all-in-one image restoration methods rely on heavy backbones (e.g., Restormer) and parameter-rich prompt branches that become computationally infeasible at UHD resolution (3840×2160) on consumer GPUs. Moreover, the large resolution gap between training patches (512×512) and full 4K inference limits the generalization of standard prompt designs, and no efficient unified framework has been developed specifically for UHD all-in-one restoration.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded UHD RGB image (3840×2160) with an unknown degradation type (low-light, blur, haze, Gaussian noise, rain, or snow) and produces the corresponding restored image. A pre-trained degradation classifier identifies the type and severity, which drives prompt generation. Unlike most competing methods that only support patch-based evaluation, UHD-Processor performs full-size 4K inference, and the VAE latent space reduces the effective spatial dimensions the restoration network must process.

## Method

UHD-Processor operates in a compact VAE latent space, where a lightweight Cubic-Mixer backbone performs restoration, reducing FLOPs by 97.4% compared to PromptIR. Training follows a Frequency Domain Decoupling Progressive Learning strategy: the input is decomposed via multi-wavelet transform into M frequency sub-bands, and dedicated sub-networks are incrementally introduced from low to high frequencies (curriculum learning), with each stage restoring only the bands learned so far while passing higher bands through unchanged. Two prompt mechanisms handle degradation awareness: the encoder uses a Degradation-Aware Low Rank Prompt (low-rank factorization of the prompt tensor with per-degradation ranks, multi-scale dilated convolutions, and structural reparameterization at inference), while the decoder uses a Degradation-Specific Frequency Selection Prompt that applies FFT-based filtering to adaptively fuse encoder high-frequency details with decoder low-frequency features according to the degradation type.

## Results

On the six-degradation UHD all-in-one benchmark, UHD-Processor achieves an average PSNR of 28.67 dB, SSIM of 0.915, and LPIPS of 0.1839, outperforming the second-best method (Histoformer, 26.59 dB) by 2.08 dB in PSNR. The model uses only 1.6M parameters and 4G FLOPs, representing a 95.1% parameter reduction and 97.4% FLOPs reduction compared to PromptIR (33M params, 158G FLOPs), while being the only method capable of full-size 4K inference. On the four-degradation setting, the average PSNR reaches 29.23 dB, surpassing HAIR (27.36 dB) by 1.93 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UHD-Processor outperforms all 20 compared baselines across every degradation type and every metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) in both the four- and six-degradation settings. The closest competitor is Histoformer (26.59 dB avg PSNR), which UHD-Processor surpasses by 2.08 dB; NDR-Restore (26.51 dB) and Gridformer (26.49 dB) trail by 2.16 and 2.18 dB respectively. Against the prompt-based PromptIR (25.74 dB), the gap is 2.93 dB in PSNR while UHD-Processor uses 95.1% fewer parameters and 97.4% fewer FLOPs. Older all-in-one methods like AIRNet (23.43 dB) and IDR (26.27 dB) are outperformed by 5.24 and 2.40 dB respectively. Notably, UHD-Processor is the only method supporting full-size 4K (3840×2160) inference, whereas all baselines require patch-based evaluation, and it also achieves the best LPIPS (0.1839) among methods with comparable or higher PSNR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prompt-based all-in-one restoration paradigm (PromptIR, NeurIPS 2023) by fundamentally rethinking its architecture for UHD-scale inputs. Where PromptIR couples a prompt branch with a heavy Restormer backbone, UHD-Processor replaces the backbone with a VAE-compressed latent space and a lightweight Cubic-Mixer, and replaces the homogeneous prompt tensor with low-rank, degradation-specific prompts that use convolution (rather than interpolation) to bridge the 512×512-to-3840×2160 resolution gap. The frequency-domain decoupling progressive learning strategy departs from task-sequential curriculum approaches (MIO-IR) by organizing the learning curriculum along frequency bands rather than degradation types, drawing on the frequency principle of neural networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 95% parameter and 97% FLOPs reduction while achieving SOTA across six degradation types makes UHD all-in-one restoration feasible on consumer-grade GPUs, which was previously infeasible. The frequency-progressive learning strategy is task-agnostic and generalizable, offering a principled alternative to ad-hoc task ordering in multi-task restoration. Open questions include whether the VAE pre-trained on clean images generalizes to unseen or compound degradations, whether the approach extends to video restoration, and whether the frequency-band decomposition granularity (MWT levels) is optimal for all degradation severities.
