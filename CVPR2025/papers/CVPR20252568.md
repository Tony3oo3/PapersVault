## Problem

Precipitation nowcasting requires simultaneously predicting the location and intensity of future rainfall, but these two factors are intricately coupled through complex atmospheric dynamics, making joint modeling difficult. Existing disentangled approaches separate dynamics along axes like deterministic vs. stochastic or advection vs. residual, but none exploit the natural frequency-domain decomposition where phase encodes spatial position and amplitude encodes intensity.

## Input / Output

The model takes a sequence of T_i=5 historical radar echo frames (single channel, resized to 128×128 following the DiffCast protocol) and produces T_o=20 future frames (T_o=10 for CIKM) at the same resolution. Internally, each frame is decomposed via 2D FFT into amplitude and phase spectra, and the two networks operate on these spectral components before recombining via inverse FFT to produce spatial-domain radar images.

## Method

AlphaPre decomposes the forecasting task into a Phase Network and an Amplitude Network that are fused by an AlphaMixer. The Phase Network extracts the phase spectrum from historical frames, concatenates normalized frequency coordinates as additional channels, and predicts future phase using stacked Conv1×1+GroupNorm+SiLU blocks; the predicted phase is then combined with the current frame's amplitude and inverse-FFT'd to yield a position-focused prediction Y_p. The Amplitude Network uses a Conv3×3 encoder followed by Spectral-Spatial blocks that apply 2D FFT, a two-layer complex MLP along the temporal dimension, inverse FFT, and a temporal-channel convolution to capture intensity dynamics, producing Y_a. The AlphaMixer combines the predicted phase with the predicted amplitude via IFFT to form Y_ap, then concatenates Y_ap, Y_p, and Y_a through a small decoder for the final output. Training uses a composite loss: L2 on the final prediction plus a z-score-normalized amplitude MSE (Eq. 11) and a low-frequency angular phase loss (Eq. 8), with weights α=0.1 and β=0.01.

## Results

AlphaPre achieves state-of-the-art results across all four radar datasets. On SEVIR, it reaches CSI-M 0.3259, CSI-219 0.0545, HSS 0.4110, and MSE 345.18; on MeteoNet, CSI-M 0.3824, CSI-32 0.2002, HSS 0.5164, MSE 12.74; on Shanghai, CSI-M 0.4178, CSI-40 0.2615, HSS 0.5534, MSE 28.02; and on CIKM, CSI-M 0.3194, CSI-40 0.1416, HSS 0.4137, MSE 35.18. The paper reports improvements of 2–8% in CSI, 1–6% in HSS, and 1–8% in MSE over the strongest baselines, with particularly large gains at high precipitation thresholds (e.g., CSI-219 on SEVIR and CSI-40 on Shanghai), indicating superior heavy-rainfall prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AlphaPre outperforms all nine baselines on every dataset across CSI-M, HSS, and MSE. The closest competitor is DiffCast (CVPR 2024), the most recent disentangled SOTA: AlphaPre improves CSI-M by 2.1% on SEVIR (0.3259 vs. 0.3050), 8.9% on MeteoNet (0.3824 vs. 0.3512), 2.2% on Shanghai (0.4178 vs. 0.4089), and 1.1% on CIKM (0.3194 vs. 0.3159). The advantage is most pronounced at high precipitation thresholds—e.g., CSI-32 on MeteoNet (0.2002 vs. 0.1808, +10.7%) and CSI-40 on Shanghai (0.2615 vs. 0.2606, marginal)—and in MSE, where AlphaPre achieves the lowest error on all four datasets (e.g., 345.18 vs. 559.59 on SEVIR, a 38% reduction over DiffCast). Among non-disentangled methods, SimVP and MAU are the strongest baselines but trail AlphaPre by 3–6% in CSI-M. NowcastNet (Nature 2023) performs well on MeteoNet (CSI-M 0.3427) but lags significantly on SEVIR (0.2791) and CIKM (0.2991), while FourCastNet is consistently the weakest baseline across all datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the amplitude-phase disentanglement concept well established in image processing (face forgery detection, low-light enhancement, adversarial robustness) and applies it as a principled decomposition axis for precipitation nowcasting. It departs from prior disentangled nowcasting methods—NowcastNet (advection vs. intensity residuals), DiffCast (deterministic vs. stochastic), and PhyDNet (physical dynamics vs. unknown factors)—by using Fourier-domain phase and amplitude as the disentanglement variables rather than spatial or probabilistic decompositions. The architecture itself is a lightweight CNN with complex-valued MLPs, building on the general encoder-decoder paradigm of SimVP and EarthFormer but inserting spectral-domain operations (FFT, complex ReLU) as the core temporal modeling mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a physically interpretable decomposition of precipitation dynamics—phase encodes where rain is, amplitude encodes how hard it is—which could inform future hybrid physics-data models. Consistent gains across four geographically diverse datasets (US, France, Shanghai, Guangdong) and the ablation showing that phase loss primarily protects structural accuracy (SSIM) while amplitude loss protects high-intensity prediction (high-threshold CSI) validate the disentanglement. Open questions include whether the fixed-amplitude assumption in the phase network limits long-range forecasting, whether the approach extends to other geophysical prediction tasks (e.g., wind fields, temperature), and whether the FFT-based operations scale efficiently to higher-resolution or 3D radar volumes.
