## Problem

Existing test-time training (TTT) methods rely on entropy minimization, which is inherently suited to classification with discrete decision boundaries and cannot be directly applied to continuous regression tasks like LVEF prediction. Additionally, the quasi-periodic structure of cardiac signals—combining regular cardiac cycles with aperiodic physiological variations—has not been exploited for test-time adaptation in echocardiogram analysis, despite being a natural inductive bias for handling pediatric anatomical diversity and acquisition quality variations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250597/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes apical-4-chamber echocardiogram video sequences (temporal frames with spatial dimensions) as input and produces a single continuous LVEF percentage value as output. The model is trained on adult echocardiograms (EchoNet-Dynamic, 7,465 samples) and adapted at test time to three pediatric cohorts (pre-school, school-age, adolescence from EchoNet-Pediatric) without access to training data or labels. During test-time adaptation, only batch normalization parameters are updated across K=8 augmented views, keeping the backbone R3D weights frozen.

## Method

Q-PART introduces a Quasi-Period Network that decomposes latent echocardiogram features into a periodic component (modeled via a parameterized helix trajectory with learned frequency, phase shift, static offset, and velocity) and an aperiodic component (modeled via a Neural CDE with natural cubic spline interpolation for continuous-time dynamics). At test time, the framework generates K augmented views simulating echocardiogram-specific quality issues (gamma correction, speckle noise, elastic deformation) and minimizes prediction variance across these views as a self-supervised adaptation objective, combined with a reconstruction loss. A differential learning rate strategy applies a smaller rate (1e-5) to periodic BN parameters to preserve temporal consistency and a larger rate (1e-3) to aperiodic BN parameters for flexible adaptation to patient-specific variations. A theoretical result shows that under unbiased and independent augmentation assumptions, the expected regression error is upper-bounded by 2·E[L_var]/K, providing a principled justification for the variance minimization objective.

## Results

Q-PART achieves the best LVEF regression performance across all three pediatric age cohorts, with MAE of 7.235 (Pre-School), 6.706 (School-Age), and 6.980 (Adolescence), and corresponding RMSE of 9.290, 8.432, and 8.950. In terms of clinical screening capability, Q-PART attains mAUROC scores of 0.9362, 0.9747, and 0.9083 at LVEF thresholds of 35–50% for the three cohorts respectively. The method also maintains gender-fair performance with no statistically significant differences (p > 0.05 in 8/9 comparisons) between male and female subjects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Q-PART outperforms all six baselines on every metric (MAE, MAPE, RMSE) across all three pediatric age cohorts. Among end-to-end TTT methods, the closest competitor is NC-TTT [24], which Q-PART beats by 1.117 MAE on Pre-School (7.235 vs. 8.352), 0.579 on School-Age (6.706 vs. 7.285), and 0.442 on Adolescence (6.980 vs. 7.422). TTT++ [18] is the second-closest baseline, with Q-PART improving MAE by 0.818 (Pre-School), 0.724 (School-Age), and 0.636 (Adolescence). The vision-language model EchoCLIP [4] achieves substantially worse results (MAE 11.56/9.303/8.496), confirming that general-purpose foundation models struggle with pediatric-specific anatomical variations. Segmentation-based approaches (EchoNet [26], MemSAM [6]) lag far behind all end-to-end methods, with MAE values exceeding 17.9, suggesting that the intermediate segmentation step loses critical temporal dynamics needed for accurate LVEF estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time training (TTT) paradigm—previously dominated by entropy minimization for classification (Tent, TTT++, Clust3, NC-TTT)—to the continuous regression setting of LVEF prediction, replacing entropy with a variance-minimization objective across augmented views. It also repurposes quasi-periodic signal decomposition, previously applied to 1D ECG waveforms, into a latent-space architecture for 2D+time echocardiograms, combining a parameterized helix trajectory (periodic) with Neural CDEs (aperiodic). The framework builds on the EchoNet-Dynamic/Pediatric data ecosystem and R3D backbones, but departs from segmentation-based LVEF pipelines by adopting an end-to-end regression formulation with domain-specific ultrasound augmentations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Q-PART addresses a genuine gap: TTT methods were designed for discrete classification and lack principled adaptation objectives for continuous regression, which is the actual clinical task in LVEF assessment. The quasi-periodic decomposition offers a physiologically grounded inductive bias that could transfer to other cardiac imaging tasks (e.g., valve assessment, arrhythmia detection), though this remains untested. Limitations include reliance on strong theoretical assumptions (unbiased augmentation, independence of augmented predictions), evaluation on a single backbone (R3D) and single view (apical-4-chamber), and the modest absolute gains over strong TTT baselines (e.g., ~0.8 MAE improvement over TTT++ on Pre-School). Open questions include whether the helix+CDE decomposition generalizes to 3D echocardiography or multi-view settings, and whether the variance-minimization objective can be extended to multi-task cardiac assessment.
