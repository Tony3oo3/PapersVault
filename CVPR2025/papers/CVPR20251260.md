## Problem

Lifting 2D instance segmentation to 3D radiance fields suffers from multi-view inconsistency in SAM predictions and inferior segmentations. Existing solutions rely on pre-processing (object association) or post-processing (clustering like HDBSCAN), both of which introduce error accumulation and hyperparameter sensitivity. The paper asks whether an end-to-end lifting framework can achieve accurate 3D segmentation without either pre- or post-processing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251260/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes posed RGB images and their corresponding 2D instance segmentation masks produced by SAM at the largest granularity that provides object-level context. At inference, it renders novel-view instance segmentation directly by computing per-pixel association probabilities against the learned codebook and selecting the argmax index—no clustering or matching step is required. The 3D-GS representation itself also carries per-Gaussian instance IDs via the same association equation, enabling direct object-level editing in 3D space.

## Method

Unified-Lift augments each 3D Gaussian point with a 16-dim learnable feature optimized via InfoNCE contrastive loss within individual views, and introduces a global object-level codebook (256×16 matrix) that explicitly represents each scene object. An association equation computes a softmax probability distribution over codebook entries for each rendered pixel, enabling end-to-end segmentation prediction. Two novel modules ensure effective codebook learning: (1) an association learning module that uses an area-aware ID mapping (removing the per-segment normalization to prioritize large segments for multi-view consistency) combined with a concentration loss that minimizes L1 directional differences between codebook entries and normalized Gaussian features; and (2) a noisy label filtering module that computes a per-pixel uncertainty score from rendered features and masks out high-uncertainty labels (threshold τ=0.8) from the association loss, making training robust to SAM segmentation errors.

## Results

On the LERF-Mask dataset, Unified-Lift achieves 80.9% mIoU and 77.1% mBIoU, outperforming the best prior method (OmniSeg3D-GS) by 6.2% mIoU and 5.3% mBIoU. On the Replica dataset, it reaches 41.6% mIoU and 43.9% F-score, surpassing OmniSeg3D-GS (39.1% mIoU, 35.9% F-score) by 2.5% and 8.0% respectively. On the Messy Rooms dataset with up to 500 objects, Unified-Lift attains a mean PQ^scene of 69.0%, matching the NeRF-based Contrastive Lift (69.0%) while training ~20× faster (≈1 h vs. ≥20 h on a single RTX 3090).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Unified-Lift wins against all seven baselines on the LERF-Mask benchmark, with the largest margins over open-vocabulary methods LERF (37.2% mIoU) and LangSplat (57.6% mIoU), and a 6.2% mIoU advantage over the strongest 3D-GS-based baselines (OmniSeg3D-GS and Gaga, both at 74.7%). On Replica, it outperforms OmniSeg3D-GS by 2.5% mIoU and 8.0% F-score, and beats the pre-processing method Gaussian Grouping by 18.0% mIoU. On Messy Rooms, Unified-Lift ties the NeRF-based Contrastive Lift in mean PQ^scene (69.0%) but surpasses it in training speed by roughly 20×, and leads the 3D-GS-based OmniSeg3D-GS (66.0%) and Panoptic-Lifting-GS (58.6%) by 3.0% and 10.4% respectively. Notably, the advantage over OmniSeg3D-GS grows with object count (e.g., 7.1% PQ^scene at 500 objects in the Old Room), demonstrating better scalability. The end-to-end design also avoids the hyperparameter sensitivity of HDBSCAN post-processing that constrains OmniSeg3D-GS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 2D-to-3D instance segmentation lifting paradigm established by Panoptic Lifting [33] and the contrastive feature learning approach of OmniSeg3D-GS [40] and Contrastive Lift [1], all built on 3D Gaussian Splatting [12]. The key departure is the introduction of a learnable object-level codebook that provides explicit per-object representations, replacing both the error-prone pre-processing (view-consistent mask alignment in Gaussian Grouping [39], Gaga [23]) and the hyperparameter-sensitive post-processing (HDBSCAN clustering in OmniSeg3D-GS [40]) with a single end-to-end association mechanism. The association learning module (area-aware ID mapping, concentration constraint) and noisy label filtering module are novel training strategies designed specifically to make the codebook learnable without these external steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating both pre- and post-processing, Unified-Lift removes a major practical pain point in 3D instance segmentation: the sensitivity of HDBSCAN hyperparameters and the error accumulation from imperfect view-consistent mask alignment. The scalability to 500 objects on Messy Rooms and the demonstrated downstream applications (copy-paste editing, multi-scale object selection) suggest practical utility for interactive 3D scene manipulation. Open questions include whether the fixed codebook size (L=256) limits performance on scenes with more objects, whether the method generalizes beyond indoor scenes, and whether the reliance on SAM's 2D masks as the sole supervision source constrains ceiling performance.
