## Problem

Existing unpaired virtual IHC staining methods suffer from a fundamental contradiction: CUT-like contrastive learning treats subpatches sharing the same staining label as negative samples, pushing them apart and corrupting the model's understanding of staining signals, while CycleGAN-based methods rely on an overly restrictive bijective mapping assumption. This leads to generated IHC images that are either morphologically distorted or pathologically inaccurate, failing to reproduce realistic staining distributions and intensities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250737/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes unpaired H&E image patches (1024×1024 on BCI/MIST_HER2, 256×256 on GPC3) and produces corresponding virtual IHC images without requiring aligned H&E-IHC pairs. A weakly-supervised segmentation pipeline using the UNI pathology foundation model generates patch-level pseudo-labels and Grad-CAM masks from H&E images, while IHC masks are obtained by thresholding DAB channels. Only high-confidence patches (confidence > 0.9) are selected to guide contrastive learning during training.

## Method

ODA-GAN introduces an Orthogonal MLP (O-MLP) constrained on the Stiefel manifold to project encoder features into an orthogonal space, combined with a learnable binary mask that decouples features into staining-related and staining-unrelated components via a decoupling loss that maximizes classification accuracy on masked features while minimizing it on unmasked features. The Dual-stream PatchNCE (DPNCE) loss then processes these two streams separately: in the staining-related stream, all subpatches sharing the same staining label serve as positive samples, resolving the CUT contradiction; in the staining-unrelated stream, only the spatially corresponding patch is positive, preserving morphology. A Multi-layer Domain Alignment (MDA) module further minimizes MMD distance between real and generated IHC feature distributions across all encoder layers using dynamic queues, and the full training combines orthogonal decoupling, adversarial, pathology contrastive, and domain alignment losses.

## Results

On the unpaired GPC3 dataset (the paper's primary setting), ODA-GAN achieves KID 3.2, FID 31.4, LPIPS 0.472, SSIM 0.2944, and DAB_KL 2.6553, outperforming the strongest baseline StegoGAN (KID 4.7, FID 39.4) by 32% in KID and 20% in FID. On the paired BCI dataset, ODA-GAN reaches KID 10.1 and FID 59.3, surpassing the best prior method ASP (KID 12.7, FID 68.7). On MIST_HER2, ODA-GAN achieves KID 8.6 and DAB_KL 1.2832, substantially outperforming ASP (KID 23.9, DAB_KL 2.9478) in both distributional and staining-distribution fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ODA-GAN wins on all five metrics (KID, FID, LPIPS, SSIM, DAB_KL) against every baseline across all three datasets. On GPC3, the closest competitor is StegoGAN [38] (KID 4.7 vs. 3.2; FID 39.4 vs. 31.4), which itself already outperforms CUT [30] and CycleGAN [43] by a wide margin; ODA-GAN further reduces KID by 32% and FID by 20% over StegoGAN. Confusion-GAN [21] and DCD [15] are mid-tier (KID ~19–21), while CUT [30] and CycleGAN [43] are the weakest unpaired baselines (KID 24–25). On the paired BCI dataset, ODA-GAN beats ASP [20] (KID 10.1 vs. 12.7) and PSPStain [8] (KID 10.1 vs. 15.1), and substantially outperforms supervised methods Pix2pix [17] (KID 56.1), PyramidP2P [23] (KID 36.6), and TDKStain [32] (KID 35.4). On MIST_HER2, the gap widens: ODA-GAN achieves KID 8.6 versus ASP's 23.9 and Pix2pix's 102.0, with DAB_KL of 1.2832 versus ASP's 2.9478, indicating markedly better staining-distribution fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CUT framework for unpaired image-to-image translation by identifying a specific contradiction in its PatchNCE loss: subpatches sharing the same staining label are incorrectly treated as negative samples, undermining pathological consistency in virtual IHC staining. The solution draws on the orthogonal decoupling concept from ODCR (Stiefel-manifold-constrained MLPs) to separate staining-related from staining-unrelated features, then applies a dual-stream contrastive loss (DPNCE) that resolves the contradiction by processing each stream with appropriate positive/negative assignments. The weakly-supervised segmentation pipeline repurposes the UNI pathology foundation model with ABMIL and Grad-CAM to generate patch-level staining masks without expert annotation, bridging the gap between slide-level labels and the patch-level supervision needed for contrastive learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Virtual IHC staining has direct clinical value by reducing tissue consumption, turnaround time, and cost of serial sectioning, and this paper addresses a concrete failure mode (inaccurate staining distribution) that limits the reliability of prior unpaired methods. The weakly-supervised segmentation pipeline leveraging pathology foundation models is a practical recipe that could transfer to other markers or staining types where expert annotations are scarce. Open questions include whether the orthogonal decoupling strategy generalizes to multi-marker IHC (e.g., multiplexed panels), whether the approach holds on larger-scale public datasets, and whether the generated stains are diagnostically validated by pathologists beyond the small Turing test reported here.
