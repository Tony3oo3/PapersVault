## Problem

Existing Video Reasoning Segmentation methods (e.g., VISA, VideoLISA) rely on a single special token to represent the target object, which inadequately captures intra-frame spatial complexity and inter-frame temporal dynamics. They also depend on external models for keyframe detection (LLaMA-VID) and use decoupled segmentation (SAM) and propagation (XMem) pipelines, preventing end-to-end training and introducing keyframe localization errors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251100/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip of T frames at H×W resolution and a high-level textual instruction that may involve implicit world knowledge (e.g., 'tool(s) for holding garbage') or temporal logic (e.g., 'the ship(s) moving at the highest speed'), rather than the explicit object descriptions used in standard RVOS. It outputs a binary segmentation mask sequence of shape T×H×W, with the keyframe mask generated directly by SAM2 and remaining frames obtained via SAM2's memory-based propagation mechanism.

## Method

VRS-HQ augments Chat-UniVi's vocabulary with two hierarchical tokens—frame-level <SEG> and temporal-level <TAK>—and prompts the MLLM to encode spatial and temporal target features into them via autoregressive generation. The Temporal Dynamic Aggregation (TDA) module fuses frame-level <SEG> embeddings into the temporal <TAK> embedding using normalized cosine similarity as attention weights, consolidating spatial details while preserving temporal consistency. During inference, the Token-driven Keyframe Selection (TKS) treats each sampled frame as a candidate keyframe, computes SAM2 occlusion scores by interacting each frame with the fused <TAK> embedding, and selects the keyframe by combining these scores with token similarity. SAM2 then performs both keyframe mask decoding and cross-frame propagation in a single end-to-end pipeline, eliminating the need for external trackers. Training uses LoRA (rank 8) on Chat-UniVi with a combined text-generation loss and mask loss (BCE + DICE), fine-tuning the mask decoder and MLP projection while freezing other parameters.

## Results

On the ReVOS benchmark (the primary VRS evaluation), VRS-HQ-13B achieves 63.3/56.8/60.0 J&F on the referring/reasoning/overall subsets, surpassing the previous SOTA VISA-13B (57.4/44.3/50.9) by 5.9%/12.5%/9.1% respectively. On standard RVOS datasets, VRS-HQ-13B reaches 71.0/74.4/50.9 J&F on Ref-YouTube-VOS, Ref-DAVIS17, and MeViS, outperforming VideoLISA by 7.3%/5.6%/6.5%. The robustness score R also improves from 14.5 (VISA-13B) to 18.9, indicating better handling of negative samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VRS-HQ decisively outperforms all 14 baselines on the primary ReVOS benchmark, with the largest gains over the closest competitor VISA-13B (+9.1% overall J&F) and VideoLISA (not directly listed on ReVOS but the strongest RVOS competitor). On RVOS datasets, VRS-HQ-13B beats VideoLISA by 7.3% (Ref-YouTube-VOS), 5.6% (Ref-DAVIS17), and 6.5% (MeViS) in J&F, and surpasses VISA-13B by 8.0%/4.0%/6.4% respectively. Traditional RVOS methods (MTTR, ReferFormer, OnlineRefer, DsHmp) and earlier reasoning segmentation approaches (LISA, TrackGPT) are substantially outperformed, with gaps exceeding 10–20 J&F points. On image segmentation (refCOCO, refCOCO+, refCOCOg), VRS-HQ matches or slightly exceeds VISA but remains below VideoLISA and LISA, which the authors attribute to their video-centric training emphasis. On ReasonSeg (reasoning image segmentation), VRS-HQ (55.2 gIoU val) trails VideoLISA (61.4) and LISA (52.9→54.0), though it still surpasses X-Decoder and SEEM, confirming that the temporal token design is most beneficial for video rather than single-image tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends VISA (ECCV 2024), the first MLLM-based Video Reasoning Segmentation framework, by replacing its single <SEG> token with a hierarchical pair of frame-level <SEG> and temporal-level <TAK> tokens, and by unifying keyframe detection, segmentation, and propagation within SAM2 rather than relying on external models (LLaMA-VID for keyframes, SAM for segmentation, XMem for propagation). The Temporal Dynamic Aggregation mechanism—fusing frame-level embeddings into the temporal token via cosine-similarity-weighted summation—is a novel architectural contribution not present in VISA or VideoLISA. The Token-driven Keyframe Selection, which repurposes SAM2's occlusion scores as a per-frame confidence signal, departs from the field's reliance on separate video understanding models for keyframe identification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 12.5% J&F gain over VISA on the reasoning subset of ReVOS demonstrates that richer temporal token representations meaningfully improve complex spatiotemporal reasoning in segmentation, a capability that single-token MLLM approaches struggle with. The end-to-end formulation (no external keyframe detector) simplifies the pipeline and removes a known failure mode, making the approach more deployable. However, the method still depends on SAM2's memory mechanism for propagation, which the authors note is less effective for single-image segmentation, and the reliance on CLIP for initial frame sampling introduces an additional pretrained model dependency. Open questions include whether the hierarchical token strategy scales to longer videos or multi-object reasoning, and whether the occlusion-score-based keyframe selection generalizes beyond the ReVOS benchmark.
