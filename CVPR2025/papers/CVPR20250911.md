## Problem

Polarization provides a physically grounded cue for separating reflected and transmitted light, yet existing polarization-based reflection removal datasets are small (<1,000 samples), often synthetic, and lack RGB data, limiting real-world generalizability. Additionally, extracting reliable reflection-free information from polarization measurements is difficult due to varying shooting angles, scene complexity, and lighting conditions, making it hard for purely discriminative networks to produce accurate separations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250911/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a spatially-aligned set of inputs: an RGB mixed image, four polarization images captured at 0°, 45°, 90°, and 135° (from a division-of-focal-plane polarization camera with a color Bayer pattern), and two derived polarization maps (AOLP and DOLP) computed via Stokes parameters. All inputs are at 1224×1024 resolution. The output is a single reflection-free RGB transmission image, with the reflection layer implicitly estimated as the residual.

## Method

PolarFree is a two-step architecture: a conditional DDPM-based diffusion model (U-Net) denoises random noise conditioned on the full polarization+RGB input set to generate a reflection-free prior ẑ₀, which is then fed alongside the inputs into a reflection removal backbone F_remove to produce the final transmission image. Training is two-stage: first, an encoder is trained to extract a clean prior z₀ from ground-truth transmission images (supervised by L1, VGG perceptual, TV, and a novel phase-based Fourier loss); second, the diffusion model is trained to reproduce z₀ from noise, using the Stage-1 prior as supervision, while F_remove is fine-tuned jointly. The phase-based loss operates on the Fourier phase of predicted vs. ground-truth images, making the network focus on structural geometry rather than compensating for color shifts introduced by semi-reflective surfaces during capture.

## Results

On the PolaRGB test set (188 paired images), PolarFree achieves 22.44 PSNR, 0.8681 SSIM, 0.1325 LPIPS, and 3.8867 Q-align, outperforming all six baselines across every metric. The closest competitor, Kim et al. (CVPR 2020), reaches 20.67 PSNR and 0.8399 SSIM, meaning PolarFree gains approximately 1.8 dB in PSNR and a 0.028 SSIM improvement. In perceptual quality (LPIPS), PolarFree more than halves the error of the next-best method (IBCLN at 0.2488 vs. 0.1325), indicating substantially cleaner and more natural-looking transmission recovery.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PolarFree wins on all four metrics against every baseline. The strongest competitor is Kim et al. (CVPR 2020, physically-based training), which leads the pack at 20.67 PSNR / 0.8399 SSIM; PolarFree surpasses it by 1.77 dB PSNR and 0.028 SSIM. IBCLN (CVPR 2020) is the best on LPIPS among baselines (0.2488) but still trails PolarFree by nearly 0.12 in LPIPS. The two most recent methods, DSRNet (ICCV 2023) and RDRNet (CVPR 2024), score 16.84 and 15.88 PSNR respectively, placing them 5.6 and 6.6 dB below PolarFree. Notably, all baselines were retrained on PolaRGB with their input layers modified to accept polarization channels, ensuring a fair comparison; even under this favorable setup, PolarFree's diffusion-based prior generation and phase loss yield a clear margin, particularly in complex reflection regions where baselines exhibit color distortion or residual reflections.

## Relation to Prior Work

PolarFree extends the polarization-based reflection removal line (Schechner et al. 2000, Lei et al. 2020, Lyu et al. 2019) by replacing handcrafted PCA or simple CNN separation with a conditional DDPM that generates a reflection-free prior from polarization and RGB inputs. The method departs from prior polarization approaches in two key ways: it introduces the first large-scale real-world dataset (PolaRGB, 6,500 pairs) that jointly provides RGB and polarization captures, and it applies a two-stage diffusion-based prior generation followed by a dedicated removal backbone, rather than a single end-to-end network. The phase-based frequency-domain loss is a novel addition to the standard L1+VGG+TV recipe, specifically targeting color discrepancies introduced by semi-reflective surfaces during capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PolaRGB dataset is the most practically significant contribution: at 8× the size of prior polarization datasets and the first to include both RGB and polarization in real-world scenes, it provides a much-needed benchmark for the field. The diffusion-based prior generation shows that generative models can meaningfully improve reflection separation (~2.2 dB from ablation), but the added inference cost of iterative denoising limits real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video reflection removal, whether the 67-scene diversity is sufficient for truly arbitrary environments, and whether the two-stage training (encoder → diffusion) can be unified into a single end-to-end pipeline.
