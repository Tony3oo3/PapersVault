## Problem

Existing visual anomaly generation methods either produce unrealistic anomalies via crop-and-paste augmentation or require substantial normal and abnormal training data to fine-tune generative models, making them impractical in data-scarce industrial settings. Crucially, these approaches can only generate anomalies similar to their training distribution (seen anomalies), failing to generalize to novel object types or previously unobserved anomaly categories. The paper addresses the gap of generating diverse, realistic *unseen* anomalies from a single normal image and text descriptions, without any training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251209/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single normal RGB image of an object, one or more anomaly text descriptions (short keywords like 'damaged' or detailed GPT-4-generated sentences), and an optional binary mask for spatial localization as input. It produces generated anomalous images at the same resolution as the input, along with a pixel-level anomaly attention map extracted from the final denoising step's cross-attention on the anomaly token, which can serve as a segmentation annotation. The normal image is consumed by encoding it through the VAE and injecting its noisy latent representation at a controlled noise level (γ=0.25) rather than starting from pure Gaussian noise.

## Method

AnomalyAny deploys a pretrained Stable Diffusion model without any fine-tuning, using test-time normal sample conditioning to anchor generation to the target object's distribution by starting the denoising process from a partially noised latent of the normal image. To overcome SD's tendency to ignore rare, small-region anomaly concepts, the paper introduces attention-guided anomaly optimization that iteratively maximizes the cross-attention value on the anomaly token at each denoising step, with a localization-aware scheduler that adaptively reduces the update step size as attention becomes concentrated to prevent over-optimization artifacts. A second stage, prompt-guided anomaly refinement, activates in the final 30 denoising steps and jointly minimizes a CLIP image-text alignment loss (between the decoded image and a detailed GPT-4 description) and a prompt-embedding alignment loss, enriching the short anomaly keyword with fine-grained semantic guidance. The entire pipeline is training-free and operates entirely at test time, enabling zero-shot anomaly generation for arbitrary object categories and anomaly types.

## Results

In the 1-shot anomaly detection setting (one normal image, no abnormal samples), AnomalyAny achieves 94.9% I-AUC and 91.9% PRO on MVTec AD, and 89.7% I-AUC and 92.5% PRO on VisA, outperforming all compared few-shot and full-shot baselines. For generation quality, AnomalyAny attains the highest average Inception Score (2.02) and competitive intra-cluster LPIPS diversity (0.33) across 15 MVTec AD categories, surpassing AnomalyDiffusion (IS 1.80), RealNet (IS 1.64), and NSA (IS 1.44). On VisA, AnomalyAny leads on every reported metric, including 97.7% P-AUC and 43.2% P-F1, exceeding the next-best method (PromptAD) by 1.0 and 1.9 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against full-shot methods (PaDiM, PatchCore), AnomalyAny substantially outperforms both in the 1-shot setting, e.g., 94.9% vs. 83.4% I-AUC on MVTec AD, demonstrating that generated anomalies compensate for the lack of training data. Among CLIP-based few-shot methods, AnomalyAny beats WinCLIP+ by 1.8 points on MVTec I-AUC and 4.8 points on VisA I-AUC, and surpasses AnomalyGPT and PromptAD on all reported metrics for both datasets. In the generation-method comparison (Table 3), AnomalyAny outperforms AnomalyDiffusion (94.4% I-AUC), DRAEM (93.6%), NSA (94.0%), and RealNet (92.7%) on MVTec AD, and leads all baselines on VisA by 3.5+ points in I-AUC. Notably, AnomalyDiffusion benefits from training on 1/3 of test-set anomalies (data leakage), yet AnomalyAny still matches or exceeds its detection performance without any anomalous training data. On generation quality (Table 1), AnomalyAny achieves the best average IS (2.02) and IC-LPIPS (0.33), confirming both higher fidelity and diversity compared to all competing generators.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the pretrained Stable Diffusion pipeline (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) for anomaly generation without any fine-tuning, departing from prior diffusion-based anomaly generators (AnomalyDiffusion, RealNet) that require dataset-specific training on normal and/or abnormal samples. The attention-guided optimization draws directly on the Attend-and-Excite methodology (Chefer et al., ACM TOG 2023) for aggregating and optimizing cross-attention maps, but applies it to enforce rare anomaly-token activations rather than general semantic guidance. The test-time normal sample conditioning (starting denoising from a partially noised latent of a single reference image) is a novel mechanism that replaces the fine-tuning step, positioning the work between crop-and-paste augmentation (NSA, DRAEM) and fully trained generative models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-training requirement is practically significant for industrial AD settings where collecting representative normal and abnormal samples across product variants is infeasible; a single normal image plus a text prompt suffices. The paper demonstrates that generated anomalies with attention-map-derived masks can improve 1-shot AD performance, suggesting a path toward data-efficient AD pipelines. However, the approach remains constrained by CLIP's limited understanding of specialized industrial descriptions, and the test-time optimization adds per-sample computational cost. Open questions include whether incorporating one-shot anomalous image references (acknowledged as future work) would resolve the precision gap for complex, domain-specific defects.
