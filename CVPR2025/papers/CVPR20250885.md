## Problem

Existing adapter transfer methods (X-Adapter, Ctrl-Adapter) rely on layer-by-layer mapping and can only transfer addition-based adapters (e.g., ControlNet) between architecturally similar models (U-Net to U-Net). They fail to transfer the more widely used attention-based adapters and cannot bridge fundamentally different architectures such as U-Net to transformer, leaving a critical gap as state-of-the-art text-to-image models shift toward transformer backbones while mature adapters remain on U-Net.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained attention-based adapter (PTA, e.g., IP-Adapter on SD1.5) whose cross-attention K/V features are extracted from all layers, a reference image encoded by the adapter's condition encoder, and a text prompt. Both the base and upgraded models remain frozen; only the A4A module (projection, learnable features, alignment) is trained. The output is a personalized image generated by the upgraded model (SDXL or Pixart-Alpha) where the transferred adapter's control signal is injected via a weighted sum into each cross-attention layer, with a balancing factor λ adjustable at inference time.

## Method

A4A introduces an all-for-all mapping strategy with two stages. In Coupling Space Projection, all K and V features from every cross-attention layer of the PTA are collected and projected into a unified space S_co whose dimension is the smallest common multiple of all layer dimensions, preserving the holistic adapter–base-model coupling rather than isolating individual layers. In Upgraded Space Mapping, randomly initialized learnable features K̄ and V̄ are trained via an iterative attention mechanism (inspired by BLIP-2) that attends over the concatenated reference [K, K̄], allowing the learnable features to absorb and transform the coupling signal for the target architecture. An Alignment module then linearly projects each row of K̄/V̄ to match the upgraded model's per-layer cross-attention dimensions. At inference, the final attention output at each layer is the sum of the upgraded model's original attention and a scaled adapter attention: Z̄ = Attn(q^up, k^up, v^up) + λ·Attn(q^up, k̄_i, v̄_i). Training minimizes the standard LDM denoising loss with asynchronous learning rates (1e-5 for projection/alignment, 1e-4 for other components, 1e-6 for PTA fine-tuning), requiring only ~0.5M–2.5M samples to match or exceed the upgraded model's native adapter trained on 64M samples.

## Results

Using SDXL as the upgraded model and IP-Adapter as the pretrained adapter, A4A achieves an IDA of 0.5127 and CLIP-score of 0.9154, surpassing the officially published PTA-UM (IDA 0.4531, CLIP-score 0.9124) on both metrics. A4A reaches PTA-UM-level IDA at approximately 0.5M samples processed (SC), compared to 64M SC for PTA-UM, and matches PTA-UM CLIP-score at roughly 2.5M SC. For cross-architecture transfer to the transformer-based Pixart-Alpha, A4A significantly outperforms training IP-Adapter from scratch at the same training budget (30k steps, batch size 8), as shown by both IDA curves and qualitative facial similarity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

A4A decisively outperforms X-Adapter [25] on both metrics: IDA improves from 0.062 to 0.5127 (an ~8× gain) and CLIP-score from 0.7894 to 0.9154. This large gap is expected since X-Adapter relies on layer-by-layer mapping designed for addition-based adapters (ControlNet) and same-architecture U-Net-to-U-Net transfer, whereas A4A uses all-for-all mapping suited for attention-based adapters. Against PTA-UM (the officially published IP-Adapter trained from scratch on SDXL), A4A achieves a higher IDA (0.5127 vs. 0.4531) and a marginally higher CLIP-score (0.9154 vs. 0.9124), while requiring only ~0.5M–2.5M samples processed versus 64M SC for PTA-UM. Additionally, A4A is the first method to successfully transfer an attention-based adapter across architectures (U-Net → Transformer), a setting where X-Adapter is inapplicable and no published PTA-UM exists for Pixart-Alpha.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the layer-by-layer mapping paradigm established by X-Adapter and Ctrl-Adapter, which transfer addition-based adapters (ControlNet) by aligning individual layer outputs between base and upgraded models. A4A instead introduces an all-for-all mapping that treats the adapter's multi-layer attention features as a holistic coupling, projecting them into a unified space before bridging to the upgraded model via learnable queries inspired by BLIP-2. Conceptually, it extends the adapter transfer problem from same-architecture, addition-based settings to cross-architecture (U-Net → Transformer) and attention-based adapters, drawing on IP-Adapter's decoupled cross-attention structure as the specific adapter being transferred.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the text-to-image field rapidly shifts from U-Net to transformer backbones (SD3, Pixart, etc.), the inability to transfer mature adapters without full retraining creates a significant practical bottleneck; A4A addresses this by achieving comparable performance to a from-scratch trained adapter with roughly 25× fewer training samples. However, the evaluation is limited to a single adapter (IP-Adapter) and two target models, leaving open whether the all-for-all mapping generalizes to other adapter families (e.g., multi-concept, layout-based) or to video generation models. The reliance on a fixed λ=1.0 balancing factor and the absence of a published transformer-based IP-Adapter for fair comparison also temper the strength of the cross-architecture claims.
