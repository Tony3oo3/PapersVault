## Problem

Existing 3D content creation methods face a fundamental trade-off: multi-view image generation followed by reconstruction produces high-quality assets but lacks controllability and suffers from geometric artifacts (Janus problem, flatness), while native 3D generative models like Shap-E offer editing and control but are limited by low resolution and coarse geometry. No prior method bridges this quality gap while retaining the controllability of native 3D representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250275/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3×2 grid of six multi-view images (960×640 total resolution) rendered from a low-quality Shap-E object at fixed azimuth/elevation angles, VAE-encoded to 4 channels and concatenated channel-wise with 4 channels of Gaussian noise to form an 8-channel UNet input, along with a free-form text prompt. The output is an enhanced 3×2 multi-view grid with refined geometry and detailed texture, which is then lifted to a 3D mesh via a feed-forward sparse-view reconstruction model (InstantMesh). The text prompt at inference can differ from the original generation prompt, enabling appearance editing while preserving the input shape's geometry.

## Method

Sharp-It is built on Zero123++ (a fine-tuned Stable Diffusion UNet for multi-view generation) with two key architectural modifications: the UNet input is expanded from 4 to 8 channels to accept VAE-encoded degraded multi-view images as conditioning, and the original image-embedding cross-attention is replaced with text-prompt cross-attention for controllable enhancement. Self-attention layers operate across the entire 3×2 view grid, learning cross-view correspondences that enforce geometric consistency without requiring per-view optimization. Training uses a paired dataset of 180K Objaverse objects rendered under three HDR lighting conditions alongside their Shap-E-encoded degraded versions, with a standard v-prediction diffusion loss. The critical insight is that conditioning on 3D-consistent input views lets the model focus exclusively on adding fine-grained details and correcting artifacts, rather than simultaneously ensuring multi-view coherence, thereby inheriting the plausible structure of the Shap-E shape while achieving image-quality textures.

## Results

On the Objaverse test set (6,000 objects), Sharp-It achieves a FID of 6.60, substantially outperforming the next-best baseline Zero123++ w/SDEdit(R) at 19.13 and the SDS-based GaussianDreamer at 50.89. It also attains the highest CLIP similarity (0.90) and DINO similarity (0.92) to ground-truth objects, confirming both image quality and geometric fidelity. Inference takes approximately 10 seconds per object, matching the speed of SDEdit-based baselines and being 36× faster than GaussianDreamer (6 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sharp-It outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against the SDS-based GaussianDreamer [74], it reduces FID from 50.89 to 6.60 (≈7.7× improvement) while running 36× faster (10 s vs. 6 min). Against MVEdit [9], it improves FID from 44.87 to 6.60 and DINO from 0.77 to 0.92, also 6× faster. Against the SDEdit-based multi-view baselines (MVDream [62] and three Zero123++ [61] variants), Sharp-It achieves a FID of 6.60 versus 19.13–28.71, with the largest gap over MVDream w/SDEdit (FID 28.71 → 6.60). Against Spice-E [60] for controlled generation, the paper reports comparable or better visual quality while running an order of magnitude faster than Spice-E's SDS-based refinement step. No baseline matches Sharp-It on the combination of image quality (FID), semantic alignment (CLIP/DINO), and runtime.

## Relation to Prior Work

Sharp-It sits at the intersection of two established paradigms: native 3D latent diffusion (Shap-E) and multi-view 2D diffusion (Zero123++). Rather than generating multi-view images from scratch or optimizing 3D representations via SDS, it repurposes Zero123++'s multi-view grid architecture as a conditional enhancement model, expanding the UNet input to accept VAE-encoded degraded views alongside noise. This multi-view-to-multi-view formulation departs from both the per-shape optimization approaches (DreamFusion, GaussianDreamer) and the feed-forward multi-view generation methods (One-2-3-45, InstantMesh), positioning itself as a quality-bridging stage between a 3D generative model and a sparse-view reconstruction pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a practical, 10-second pipeline that combines the controllability and editability of native 3D generative models with the high-fidelity detail of 2D diffusion, addressing a real quality gap that has limited adoption of 3D-native models. A key limitation is the reliance on sparse-view reconstruction (InstantMesh) that bakes lighting into texture, constraining relighting and material editing; the authors note that newer reconstruction methods (LVM, GTR) could improve this. The approach is currently tied to Shap-E's specific latent space and 1024×1024 implicit function representation, leaving open whether the multi-view-to-multi-view enhancement paradigm generalizes cleanly to other 3D generative backbones with different representations or resolutions.
