## Problem

Medical image segmentation faces a dual challenge: acquiring large pixel-level annotated datasets is costly, and existing methods produce a single deterministic mask despite inherent ambiguity where multiple experts may annotate the same image differently. No prior framework simultaneously leverages abundant unlabeled data (semi-supervised learning) and generates multiple plausible segmentation maps (ambiguity-aware segmentation), leaving a gap between data-efficient training and uncertainty-aware output.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250313/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 2D medical images (128×128 CT scans from LIDC-IDRI or 256×256 RGB dermoscopic images from ISIC) where a small labeled subset carries multiple expert annotations per image (4 for LIDC, 3 for ISIC) and a large unlabeled subset has no annotations. At inference, the model samples multiple latent codes from a learned prior distribution and produces a set of diverse segmentation masks per image, with the number of outputs matching the number of expert annotations available in the labeled set.

## Method

AmbiSSL builds on a Probabilistic U-Net-style encoder-decoder with prior and posterior networks, but introduces three key modules. The Diverse Pseudo-Label Generation (DPG) module creates two additional decoders by applying randomized pruning (retaining top-a% weights with probability q_k) to the final layers of the backbone decoder; each pruned decoder, combined with sampled latent codes, produces a distinct pseudo-label set for unlabeled images, which are then ensembled for quality. The Semi-Supervised Latent Distribution Learning (SSLDL) module aligns prior and posterior distributions via KL divergence, notably using a Laplace distribution (rather than Gaussian) for unlabeled data to avoid overconfident fitting to noisy pseudo-labels. The Cross-Decoder Supervision (CDS) module trains each pruned decoder against randomly selected pseudo-labels from the other, encouraging complementary feature learning. Training combines supervised Dice loss on labeled data (with a bound-prediction auxiliary loss on annotation intersection/union) and unsupervised cross-decoder losses on unlabeled data, with the unsupervised weight ramped up via a Gaussian schedule.

## Results

On LIDC-IDRI with 10% labeled and 90% unlabeled data, AmbiSSL achieves a GED of 0.1620 and Dice_soft of 89.86%, approaching the upper bound (100% labeled: GED 0.1461, Dice_soft 90.24%) while outperforming all baselines. On ISIC with 20% labeled and 80% unlabeled data, the method reaches GED 0.2444 and Dice_soft 85.87%, again surpassing all baselines. The semi-supervised setting consistently improves over supervised-only methods by 2–3 points in Dice_soft and reduces GED by 0.03–0.07, demonstrating the benefit of unlabeled data for diverse segmentation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AmbiSSL outperforms all four supervised-only ambiguous segmentation methods (Prob. U-Net, CM-Global, CM-Pixel, Pionono, D-Persona) on every metric, with the largest gains over Prob. U-Net (GED reduced from 0.2679 to 0.1620; Dice_soft improved from 83.09% to 89.86%). Among the three semi-supervised baselines, AmbiSSL beats the strongest (Baseline III, consistency regularization) by 1.11 points in Dice_soft (89.86% vs. 88.75%) and reduces GED by 0.0159 (0.1620 vs. 0.1779). The advantage over Baseline I (self-training) and Baseline II (cross-pseudo supervision) is even larger, with Dice_soft gains of 1.73 and 1.50 points respectively. On ISIC (20% labeled), AmbiSSL similarly leads all baselines, achieving Dice_soft of 85.87% versus 83.85% for the best supervised method (D-Persona) and 82.62% for the best semi-supervised baseline (Baseline III). The consistent pattern across both datasets confirms that the combination of diverse pseudo-label generation and cross-decoder supervision yields superior diversity and accuracy compared to standard SSL strategies adapted for multi-annotator settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

AmbiSSL sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: semi-supervised medical image segmentation (which leverages unlabeled data but outputs a single mask) and ambiguous/stochastic segmentation (which outputs multiple masks but requires multi-annotator labels for every image). The latent-space prior/posterior framework is inherited from the Probabilistic U-Net [19], the reparameterization trick and bound-prediction loss from Pionono [27], and the diversified multi-rater objective from D-Persona [35]. The key departure is the DPG module, which creates decoder diversity through randomized pruning of final-layer weights rather than training independent networks, and the CDS module, which cross-supervises pruned decoders using each other's pseudo-labels—a mechanism not present in prior SSL or ambiguous-segmentation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in clinical deployment: obtaining multi-annotator labels for every image is prohibitively expensive, while unlabeled scans are abundant. By showing that a small multi-annotator set plus large unlabeled data can recover most of the diversity and accuracy of fully annotated training, AmbiSSL offers a more realistic annotation budget for ambiguous segmentation. However, the authors acknowledge that performance still trails the fully-annotated upper bound, and the gap is non-trivial (e.g., Dice_soft 89.86 vs. 90.24 on LIDC at 10% labels). Open questions include whether the pruning-based diversity strategy scales to 3D volumes or larger datasets, and whether the Laplace-distribution choice for pseudo-label posteriors generalizes beyond the two 2D benchmarks tested.
