## Problem

All-In-One Image Restoration (AIOR) models generalize poorly to out-of-distribution degradations because existing training datasets are small, lack scene diversity, and contain degradations produced by a single physical model per dataset. This limits the variety of degradation patterns and real-world coverage, causing models to overfit to narrow training distributions. The paper addresses this gap by synthesizing large-scale, diverse degraded images using a conditional diffusion model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252395/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

GenDeg takes a clean RGB image, a text prompt (BLIP-2 scene caption appended with a degradation-specific phrase), and two scalar intensity parameters (μ for mean degradation strength, σ for spatial variation) as input, and outputs a degraded image aligned to the clean input. The μ and σ values are discretized into 128 bins and fused into the CLIP text embedding as a 77×770 conditioning vector. For the downstream restoration models, the input is a degraded image and the output is a restored image; the GenDS dataset provides ~750k paired (degraded, clean) samples across six degradation types, with each clean image appearing under multiple degradations.

## Method

GenDeg adapts the InstructPix2Pix latent diffusion framework by conditioning on the clean image, a text prompt, and novel degradation-intensity statistics (μ, σ of the degradation map). The key architectural addition is a learnable projection that encodes binned μ and σ as one-hot vectors, concatenates them with the CLIP text embedding, and projects back to 77×768 dimensions, giving the diffusion model fine-grained control over degradation strength and spatial distribution. Training combines multiple existing datasets per degradation type so the model learns diverse patterns rather than a single physical model. A Structure Correction Module (borrowed from AutoDIR) refines generated images for smooth degradations (haze, raindrop, motion blur) using a one-step reverse diffusion loss, while for rain, snow, and low-light the clean image is instead passed through the VAE encoder-decoder for alignment. The resulting 550k+ synthetic samples are merged with existing data to form GenDS, on which five restoration architectures (NAFNet, PromptIR, a Swin-Transformer-based model, DA-CLIP, and Diff-Plugin) are trained, yielding substantial OoD performance gains.

## Results

On OoD test sets across six degradation types (haze, rain, snow, motion blur, low-light, raindrop), all five retrained models show substantial LPIPS/FID improvements with GenDS training. NAFNet GenDS achieves the strongest overall OoD performance, e.g., 0.151/52.5 LPIPS/FID on REVIDE (haze) vs. 0.211/71.3 without GenDS, and 0.143/76.7 on O-Haze vs. 0.183/99.2. PromptIR GenDS shows the largest relative gain on haze (O-Haze LPIPS drops from 0.333 to 0.160). Motion blur performance remains nearly unchanged (GoPro LPIPS: 0.155→0.149 for NAFNet), confirming that existing real data is already sufficient for that degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NAFNet GenDS achieves the best OoD LPIPS across nearly all nine test sets, beating its own baseline (NAFNet) by 0.06 LPIPS on REVIDE (0.151 vs 0.211) and 0.04 on O-Haze (0.143 vs 0.183), and outperforming SOTA AutoDIR on REVIDE (0.151 vs 0.247) and O-Haze (0.143 vs 0.315). PromptIR GenDS shows the largest relative improvement on haze, halving O-Haze LPIPS from 0.333 to 0.160, surpassing all SOTA methods on that benchmark. DA-CLIP GenDS improves over DA-CLIP on every test set (e.g., REVIDE LPIPS 0.215 vs 0.300) but remains weaker than NAFNet GenDS and PromptIR GenDS on most degradations. Diff-Plugin GenDS consistently beats Diff-Plugin (e.g., SICE LPIPS 0.223 vs 0.256) and the pretrained Diff-Plugin# on most sets, confirming GenDS benefits generative restoration models. AutoDIR retains an edge on raindrop removal (RainDS LPIPS 0.157 vs 0.170 for NAFNet GenDS), and InstructIR leads on LHP rain (0.139 vs 0.180), but neither SOTA method matches the consistent cross-degradation performance of NAFNet GenDS. Motion blur (GoPro) shows minimal change across all models, indicating existing real data is already sufficient for that task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the InstructPix2Pix conditioning framework (text + image conditioning on a latent diffusion model) for a fundamentally different objective: rather than editing images per user instructions, it synthesizes physically plausible degradations on clean images. It builds on Stable Diffusion's latent space and VAE architecture, but introduces a novel intensity-aware conditioning mechanism (binarized μ and σ of the degradation map fused into CLIP text embeddings) that has no direct precedent in prior image-editing or restoration work. On the restoration side, it positions itself against the AIOR literature (PromptIR, DA-CLIP, Diff-Plugin, AutoDIR) by identifying their shared data-diversity bottleneck and addressing it through synthetic data generation rather than architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GenDS dataset (750k+ paired samples across six degradations, with each clean image having multiple degraded versions) is a substantial community resource that directly addresses the data-scarcity and scene-diversity limitations that have constrained AIOR generalization. The finding that motion blur does not benefit from additional synthetic data—because existing real datasets already provide sufficient diversity—offers a useful diagnostic: synthetic augmentation helps most where real data is scarce (haze, low-light, raindrop). Open questions include whether the approach extends to compound or temporally-varying degradations, whether the 550k-sample scale is near the saturation point, and whether the VAE-induced alignment gap can be further reduced to close the remaining within-distribution performance gap observed in Table 4.
