## Problem

Gradient-based attribution methods (FullGrad, Integrated Gradients, etc.) that work well on CNNs produce unfaithful, noisy explanations on Vision Transformers. The paper identifies the root cause: non-locally-affine operations in Transformers (softmax, LayerNorm division, gated activations, self-gating) violate FullGrad-completeness, a conservation property that CNNs naturally satisfy, leading to imbalanced gradient flow and unaffine attribution scores.

## Input / Output

LibraGrad is a post-hoc wrapper that takes a pre-trained model (any of 8 ViT-family architectures or MLP-Mixer) and an RGB image, then produces pixel-level attribution maps and per-feature relevance scores. It modifies only the backward pass—pruning and scaling gradient paths—while leaving the forward computation entirely untouched, so it adds no inference overhead and can be composed with any existing gradient-based attribution method (FullGrad+, IG, GradCAM+, etc.) without architectural changes.

## Method

LibraGrad restores FG-completeness through two theoretically grounded gradient manipulation operators: a Constant Operator that zeroes the gradient of a non-linear sub-expression (treating it as input-dependent but gradient-free), and a SwapBackward operator that replaces a product's Jacobian with a scaled version. For self-gating (SwiGLU), where both branches are FG-complete, gradients are split equally (a=b=0.5, Theorem 4); for attention softmax, LayerNorm's denominator, and gated activations (GELU/SiLU), where one multiplicand is inherently non-linear, the non-linear path's gradient is pruned to zero (Theorem 5), yielding a locally-exact linear approximation per input. The practical implementations—Libra-Attention, Libra-LayerNorm, Libra-Gated Activation, and Libra-Self-Gating—require no forward-pass modification and maintain O(1) computational overhead relative to a standard backward pass, making the method universally applicable across architectures including attention-free MLP-Mixer.

## Results

On ViT-B, Libra FullGrad+ achieves 62.8% average MIF (Most-Influential-First Deletion) accuracy across four datasets (ImageNet, ImageNet-Hard, MURA, Oxford-IIIT Pet), outperforming the best non-Libra method (GenAtt at 53.4%) by 9.4 points. On Completeness Error, Libra FullGrad achieves a perfect 0.0±0.0 across all seven evaluated architectures (ViT-L, EVA2-S, BEiT2-L, FlexiViT-L, SigLIP-L, CLIP-H, DeiT3-H), while the next-best method (AttnLRP) averages 13.6±1.0. On the FunnyBirds synthetic benchmark, Libra FullGrad+ scores 92.7% CSDC and 91.4% PC, versus 61.0% and 55.0% for unmodified FullGrad+. The method also achieves 79.4% segmentation AP on ImageNet-S, compared to 51.5% for normal FullGrad+.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Libra FullGrad+ (62.8% avg MIF) outperforms all 14 baselines on the cross-dataset evaluation. Among Transformer-specific attention-gradient hybrids, GenAtt (53.4%) and TokenTM (52.0%) are the strongest non-Libra competitors, but both are surpassed by 9.4 and 10.8 points respectively; even with LibraGrad applied, GenAtt w/Libra (55.2%) and AttCAT w/Libra (61.8%) remain below Libra FullGrad+. Attention-based methods (RawAtt 43.1%, AttnLRP 40.6%, Attn. Rollout 35.1%) and LRP-based methods (AliLRP 33.9%, AttnLRP 40.6%) are consistently outperformed by 15–29 points. On Completeness Error, Libra FullGrad achieves 0.0 across all architectures, while DecompX (199.1 avg) and AliLRP (216.1 avg) exhibit severe incompleteness, and even AttnLRP (13.6 avg) and Input×Grad (8.3 avg) fall far short. The universal improvement property is confirmed: every gradient-based method tested (Input×Grad, GradCAM+, HiResCAM, XGradCAM+, FullGrad+, GenAtt, TokenTM, AttCAT) improves when LibraGrad is applied, with the largest gains seen in FullGrad+ (+14.7 pts) and XGradCAM+ (+17.5 pts).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FullGrad/FullGrad+ lineage of gradient-based attribution (Srinivas & Fleuret 2019; Mehri et al. 2024) by identifying a theoretical deficiency—violation of FG-completeness—introduced by non-locally-affine operations in Transformers (softmax, LayerNorm, gated activations, self-gating). Rather than proposing yet another architecture-specific hybrid (as GenAtt, TokenTM, AttCAT do), LibraGrad corrects the root cause by pruning and scaling backward paths, leaving the forward pass untouched. It also positions itself against Integrated Gradients, showing that LibraGrad's universal improvement vastly outperforms IG's compositional extension, with a theoretical proof that this gap is unavoidable for fixed-step IG approximations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FG-completeness framework provides a principled, architecture-agnostic criterion for when gradient-based attributions are faithful, and the empirical finding that a single post-hoc gradient correction makes general-purpose FullGrad+ outperform all Transformer-specific methods across 8 architectures and 5 datasets challenges the assumption that ViTs require bespoke explanation techniques. The ablation revealing LayerNorm (not attention) as the dominant source of gradient imbalance is a useful diagnostic for practitioners. Open questions include whether the pruning/scaling strategy generalizes to emerging architectures (Mamba, state-space models), whether LibraGrad can serve as a training-time gradient regularizer, and how the approach interacts with multi-task or generative settings where the output function is not a single scalar.
