## Problem

Existing discriminative point-supervised object detection methods for drone-view images rely on rigid sampling strategies—static positive anchors and randomly sampled negative backgrounds—that cause training instability in early epochs and introduce many irrelevant negatives, degrading pseudo-box quality for densely packed small objects. This paper tackles how to dynamically adjust both the positive and negative sampling spaces during training to produce higher-quality pseudo-box labels from minimal point-level supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250046/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes drone-view RGB images (VisDrone at 2000×1500, cropped into 512×512 patches with 128-pixel overlap; DroneVehicle and UAVDT at native resolution) annotated with a single point per object, where point offsets are simulated via a Rectified Gaussian Distribution to mimic manual labeling bias. It outputs pseudo-box labels (bounding boxes) that are then used to train a standard Faster R-CNN detector; pseudo-box quality is measured by mIoU against ground-truth boxes, and final detection performance by AP50.

## Method

PointSR replaces the rigid sampling of prior PSOD methods with a self-regularized sampling mechanism across three stages. The Temporal-Ensembling Encoder maintains a category-wise anchor prototype library updated via exponential moving average of pseudo-box aspect ratios across epochs, dynamically reshaping candidate anchors rather than using fixed pre-defined shapes. In Coarse Pseudo-box Prediction, the adjusted anchors feed a dual-stream MIL classifier (built on WSDDNs) that selects top-k proposals, while an Informative-Sample Collector applies NMS and loss-based ranking to retain only high-loss negative samples near the decision boundary. In Pseudo-box Refinement, these informative negatives are combined with jittered positive candidates in a second MIL pass to suppress low-confidence positives and merge the final pseudo-box. The two modules co-adapt: better anchors improve loss estimation for negatives, and better negatives sharpen the MIL classifier, yielding a self-regularizing loop that stabilizes training and improves robustness to point-label perturbation.

## Results

PointSR achieves average AP50 of 42.5% on DroneVehicle, 38.7% on UAVDT, and 35.0% on VisDrone using only point-level supervision, with corresponding mIoU of 88.9%, 96.8%, and 75.7% respectively. These results outperform the strongest prior method (Point-to-Box) by 7.2%, 5.4%, and 3.3% AP50 on the three datasets. PointSR also demonstrates superior robustness, with AP50 variance of only 0.171 on DroneVehicle and 0.212 on VisDrone under point-label perturbations, compared to 0.935 and 0.752 for Point-to-Box.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PointSR outperforms all five PSOD baselines on every dataset. Against the closest competitor Point-to-Box [3], PointSR gains 7.2% AP50 on DroneVehicle (42.5 vs. 35.3), 5.4% on UAVDT (38.7 vs. 33.3), and 3.3% on VisDrone (35.0 vs. 31.7), while also improving mIoU by 2.9%, 1.5%, and 9.5% respectively. The generative method PLUG [10] and the oriented-detection methods PointOBB [15] and PointOBBv2 [18] are substantially outperformed, with PointSR exceeding PointOBBv2 by 10.1% AP50 on DroneVehicle and 11.2% on UAVDT. Point2Rbox [32] shows the weakest performance among discriminative methods, with PointSR surpassing it by over 30% AP50 on DroneVehicle. Notably, PointSR's pseudo-box quality (mIoU) approaches or in some per-class cases exceeds the Faster R-CNN upper bound trained with ground-truth boxes, and its AP50 variance under point perturbation (0.171 on DroneVehicle) is roughly 5× lower than Point-to-Box (0.935), confirming superior robustness to annotation noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

PointSR builds directly on the Point-to-Box (ECCV'22) framework, which formulates point-supervised detection as MIL-based pseudo-box selection from a bag of anchor proposals, and on the WSDDNs dual-stream architecture for weakly supervised detection. It departs from prior PSOD methods (PLUG, PointOBB, Point2Rbox) by replacing their rigid sampling—fixed positive anchors and uniformly random negative backgrounds—with a self-regularized strategy that dynamically adjusts both the positive sampling space (via a temporal-ensembling anchor prototype library) and the negative sampling space (via loss-ranked informative sample collection). The key conceptual shift is treating the sampling spaces as learnable, co-adaptive variables in the optimization rather than fixed hyperparameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Point supervision costs roughly 1/18 of box annotation, making it the most practical labeling paradigm for drone-view scenes where dense small objects render box annotation infeasible; PointSR's 2.6–7.2% AP50 gains over prior PSOD methods narrow the gap to fully supervised detection on three standard benchmarks. The robustness result (AP50 variance of 0.171 vs. 0.935 for Point-to-Box under point perturbation) is practically significant for real-world deployment where human click annotations are inherently noisy. Open questions include whether the temporal-ensembling mechanism generalizes beyond the MIL pseudo-box paradigm to end-to-end detectors, and whether the approach extends to other weakly-supervised settings (e.g., segmentation or tracking) in aerial imagery.
