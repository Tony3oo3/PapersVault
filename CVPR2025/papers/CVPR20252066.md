## Problem

Reconstructing dynamic 3D (4D) scenes from casually captured monocular video remains challenging because synchronized multi-view video is nearly impossible for typical users to capture, and no large-scale multi-view video dataset of dynamic scenes exists to train a generative prior. Existing methods either require careful multi-view capture setups or depend on additional supervision signals (depth maps, 2D tracks, user-provided masks) that limit automation and generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252066/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single monocular video (with or without camera motion) or any number of sparse posed images at different viewpoints and timestamps, and produces a deformable 3D Gaussian scene renderable in real time at arbitrary camera poses and times. The diffusion model natively generates 16 frames per call (M=3 conditioning views, N=13 target views), but the alternating sampling strategy extends this to a K×L grid (K=13 cameras × L frames, plus K'=128 additional dense views per timestep) to provide sufficient coverage for reconstruction. For unposed input videos, MonST3R estimates camera parameters.

## Method

CAT4D is a two-stage pipeline: a multi-view video diffusion model (built on CAT3D's 3D self-attention latent diffusion architecture with added sinusoidal time conditioning injected into the U-Net residual blocks) transforms a monocular video into a consistent multi-view video, which is then used to optimize a deformable 3D Gaussian representation (4DGS with K-Planes deformation) via photometric loss. Because no large multi-view dynamic video dataset exists, training data is curated from a mixture of static multi-view image datasets, fixed-viewpoint monocular videos, synthetic 4D datasets (Objaverse, Kubric), and generative augmentations (Lumiere to animate static CO3D frames, CAT3D to synthesize novel views of video frames). A key sampling strategy alternates between multi-view and temporal sliding-window generation with SDEdit over three iterations (multi-view from noise → temporal at noise 16/25 → multi-view at noise 8/25), taking pixel-wise medians across overlapping windows to achieve both spatial and temporal consistency beyond the model's native 16-frame output. Classifier-free guidance uses separate scales for image conditioning (s_I=3.0) and time conditioning (s_T=4.5) to independently control camera and scene motion.

## Results

On the DyCheck 4D reconstruction benchmark, CAT4D achieves mPSNR 17.39, mSSIM 0.607, and mLPIPS 0.341, outperforming the 4D-GS baseline (16.54/0.594/0.347) and Shape-of-Motion (16.72/0.630/0.450) on mPSNR and mLPIPS, though trailing MoSca (19.54/0.738/0.244) on mPSNR and mSSIM. On the NSFF disentangled-control task, CAT4D achieves PSNR 21.97 (fixed viewpoint, varying time) and 19.73 (varying viewpoint, varying time), surpassing 4DiM by 2.2 and 2.4 dB respectively. For sparse-view bullet-time reconstruction, CAT4D reaches PSNR 20.79 and LPIPS 0.160, improving over CAT3D-3cond (20.19/0.258) and CAT3D-1cond (15.33/0.527).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 4D-GS [72], CAT4D improves mPSNR by 0.85 dB and reduces mLPIPS from 0.347 to 0.341, demonstrating the value of the generated multi-view supervision over the raw monocular input. Compared to Shape-of-Motion [70], CAT4D wins on mPSNR (17.39 vs 16.72) and mLPIPS (0.341 vs 0.450) but trails slightly on mSSIM (0.607 vs 0.630); notably, Shape-of-Motion relies on external depth maps and 2D tracks that CAT4D does not use. Against MoSca [33] at half resolution, CAT4D achieves a lower mPSNR (18.24 vs 19.54) and mSSIM (0.666 vs 0.738) but a better mLPIPS (0.227 vs 0.244), with MoSca benefiting from user-provided foreground masks and additional supervisory signals. On the disentangled camera-time control task (Table 2), CAT4D outperforms 4DiM [71] across all three evaluation settings by 2.2–2.4 dB in PSNR, and qualitatively 4DiM conflates camera and scene motion while CAT4D maintains decoupled control. For sparse-view bullet-time reconstruction (Table 3), CAT4D surpasses both CAT3D variants [17], improving LPIPS from 0.258 (3-cond) to 0.160 while also exceeding the 1-cond variant's PSNR by 5.5 dB.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAT4D extends CAT3D's multi-view latent diffusion architecture by injecting time conditioning, transforming a static multi-view image model into a multi-view video diffusion model that jointly controls camera pose and temporal progression. For reconstruction, it builds on 4DGS (deformable 3D Gaussians with K-Planes deformation fields) but departs from prior 4D reconstruction methods like Shape-of-Motion and MoSca, which rely on auxiliary supervision signals (depth maps, 2D tracks, user-provided masks), by using only a generative prior and photometric losses. The alternating multi-view/temporal sampling strategy with SDEdit is a novel contribution that addresses the gap between the model's finite native output length and the many frames needed for 4D reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CAT4D demonstrates that a learned multi-view video generative prior can substitute for the multiple explicit geometric supervision signals (depth, tracks, segmentation) that prior state-of-the-art 4D reconstruction methods require, opening a path toward fully automatic 4D scene creation from a single casually captured or AI-generated video. Key limitations remain: the model struggles with temporal extrapolation beyond input frames, cannot fully disentangle camera from scene motion under occlusion, and the recovered 3D motion fields may lack physical accuracy. An open question is whether scaling to larger multi-view video models that natively generate longer sequences would eliminate the need for the alternating sampling heuristic, and whether incorporating physical priors could improve motion fidelity without sacrificing the method's generality.
