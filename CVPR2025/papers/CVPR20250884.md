## Problem

While state space models (SSMs) offer linear computational complexity as an alternative to quadratic self-attention, prior vision Mamba architectures remain slower than lightweight CNN and attention-based models in practice. The paper identifies that the dominant runtime bottleneck in state space duality (SSD) layers is the linear projections applied to the full input sequence (O(LD²)), not the SSM recurrence itself, which limits the practical efficiency gains of SSM-based vision backbones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250884/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes standard RGB images at 224×224 or 256×256 resolution, which are downsampled by a 4-layer convolutional stem to H/16 × W/16 feature maps before entering three hierarchical stages of HSM-SSD blocks. For classification, it outputs a single class logit computed as a softmax-weighted sum of logits from all stages (multi-stage hidden state fusion). For dense prediction tasks, the same backbone feeds Mask R-CNN, RetinaNet, or SemanticFPN heads to produce bounding boxes, instance masks, or pixel-wise semantic labels at 512×512 resolution.

## Method

The core contribution is the Hidden State Mixer-based SSD (HSM-SSD) layer, which exploits the factorization of NC-SSD to move all channel-mixing operations (gating and output projection) from the full token space (L×D) onto the compressed hidden state space (N×D), where N ≪ L. This reduces the dominant cost from O(LD²) to O(ND²), making the layer's complexity controllable via the number of states. The architecture stacks HSM-SSD layers with depthwise convolutions for local context and pointwise convolutions for channel interaction in a three-stage hierarchical design. Two additional design choices further improve practical speed: a single-head formulation with state-wise importance weights (A ∈ R^{L×N}) that eliminates memory-bound reshape/copy operations, and multi-stage hidden state fusion that averages hidden states at each stage to produce auxiliary logits, reinforcing the representational power of the compressed states during training.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K classification, EfficientViM-M4 achieves 79.6% top-1 accuracy (450 epochs) at 8,170 im/s throughput and 0.122 ms latency, outperforming the previous SOTA SHViT-S4 (79.4%) while running faster. EfficientViM-M2 reaches 75.8% top-1 at 17,005 im/s, surpassing SHViT-S2 (75.2%) by 0.6% with ~7% higher throughput. On COCO-2017, EfficientViM-M4 achieves 39.3 AP^b for instance segmentation (Mask R-CNN) and 38.8 AP for object detection (RetinaNet) at 0.45 ms backbone latency, and on ADE20K semantic segmentation it reaches 41.3% mIoU, surpassing EdgeViT-XXS (39.7%) by 1.6% with ~2× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EfficientViM consistently outperforms the previous SOTA SHViT [72] across all model sizes: M2 beats SHViT-S2 by 0.2% top-1 (75.4 vs 75.2) at 7% higher throughput, M3 beats SHViT-S3 by 0.2% (77.6 vs 77.4) at similar speed, and M4 matches SHViT-S4 (79.4%) while running 2% faster. Against CNN-based baselines, EfficientViM-M1 achieves 73.5% top-1 (450 epochs) at 20,731 im/s, surpassing MobileNetV3-L 0.75 (73.3%) with ~90% speedup, and M2 exceeds MobileViTV2 0.75 and FastViT-T8 (both 75.6% at 256²) by 0.2% while running ~4× faster. Among vision Mamba models (Table 4), EfficientViM-M2 is ~10× faster than ViM-T and ~29× faster than LocalViM-T at comparable accuracy, and M4 outperforms MSVMamba-N (77.3%) and EfficientVMamba-S (78.7%) by 3.1% and 2.3% respectively with 4–6× higher throughput. On dense prediction tasks, EfficientViM-M4 surpasses SHViT-S4 in instance segmentation (39.3 vs 39.0 AP^b) and matches it in object detection (38.8 AP) while reducing backbone latency from 0.52 ms to 0.45 ms. For semantic segmentation on ADE20K, EfficientViM-M4 achieves 41.3% mIoU, outperforming EdgeViT-XXS (39.7%) by 1.6% with roughly 2× lower latency (0.45 vs 0.94 ms).

## Relation to Prior Work

EfficientViM builds directly on the state space duality (SSD) formulation from Mamba2 and the non-causal SSD variant introduced by VSSD for vision, which removes the causal mask suboptimal for image processing. The key conceptual departure is relocating the computationally expensive channel-mixing operations (gating and output projection) from the full token space (L×D) to the compressed hidden state space (N×D), where N ≪ L, thereby making the dominant cost controllable via the number of states. The architecture also draws on practical design principles from efficient vision backbones like SHViT and EfficientViT, particularly the emphasis on minimizing memory-bound operations over raw FLOPs, and incorporates depthwise separable convolutions from the MobileNet lineage for local context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EfficientViM establishes a new speed-accuracy Pareto front for lightweight vision backbones, achieving up to 10× higher throughput than prior vision Mambas (ViM, LocalViM) with comparable accuracy, which is significant for on-device and real-time deployment. The HSM-SSD concept is generalizable—the hidden state mixer could be replaced with other global token mixers (e.g., Perceiver-style), and the multi-stage hidden state fusion is a simple, broadly applicable technique. Open questions include whether the N ≪ D assumption degrades at very large model scales, how the architecture behaves under extreme compression (very small N), and whether the single-head design generalizes to tasks requiring explicit multi-head diversity (e.g., multi-modal or video).
