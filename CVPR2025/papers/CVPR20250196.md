## Problem

Visual tokens in Large Multimodal Models add thousands of tokens to the LLM input, causing quadratic growth in inference latency and memory. Existing token pruning methods either require costly calibration or fine-tuning per model, or rely on attention-based importance scores that retain redundant, similar tokens—leading to severe performance degradation at high pruning ratios (≥80%).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250196/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on the visual token embeddings produced by a vision encoder and projector (e.g., 576 tokens of 4096 dimensions for LLaVA 1.5, 144 tokens per frame for LLaVA-NeXT-Video, or 3–5× more for LLaVA 1.6). Given a target subset size M̃, DivPrune selects M̃ tokens from the full visual token set and discards the rest; the retained tokens are concatenated with text tokens and fed to the LLM to generate a standard autoregressive text response. The pruning is applied at a single layer (default: before the first LLM decoder layer) and requires no calibration data, fine-tuning, or model-specific configuration.

## Method

DivPrune reformulates visual token pruning as a Max-Min Diversity Problem (MMDP): select a subset of M̃ tokens that maximizes the minimum pairwise cosine distance among the selected tokens, thereby minimizing redundancy. The solution is a greedy two-stage algorithm—first pick the token whose minimum distance to all others is largest, then iteratively add the remaining token with the largest minimum distance to the already-selected set—yielding an exact MMDP solution for the typical token counts (≤576). A single matrix multiplication precomputes the full pairwise distance matrix, making the selection overhead negligible relative to LLM computation. Unlike attention-based methods that score individual token importance, DivPrune optimizes the collective representativeness of the retained set, which is why it maintains accuracy at extreme pruning ratios (TFLOP ratio as low as ~10–15%) without any training or calibration.

## Results

On LLaVA 1.5-7B at ~15% TFLOP ratio, DivPrune achieves 59.19 MMBench Acc, 56.85 GQA EM, 86.02 POPE F1, and 0.96 COCO CIDEr, compared to 20.62/38.73/32.84/0.06 for FastV and 21.31/38.94/25.35/0.05 for VTW. The method reduces TFLOPs by 84.4% while maintaining less than 2% performance drop on MMMU and SQA, and even slightly improves POPE F1 over the unpruned model. On video tasks (LLaVA-NeXT-Video-7B at ~14% TFLOP), DivPrune achieves 45.90 ActivityNet Acc and 39.76 EgoSchema Acc versus 33.91/29.14 (FastV) and 26.84/25.42 (VTW). DivPrune also reduces GPU memory by ~400 MB and E2E latency by 22% relative to the original model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DivPrune substantially outperforms both plug-and-play baselines: at ~15% TFLOP on LLaVA 1.5-7B, it achieves 59.19 MMBench Acc vs. 20.62 (FastV) and 21.31 (VTW), and 86.02 POPE F1 vs. 32.84 and 25.35 respectively. Against PruMerge (variable ratio), DivPrune wins on all 11 image benchmarks except SQA EM (67.87 vs. 68.91), with the largest gap on POPE F1 (83.06 vs. 66.89). Compared to the calibration-based FitPrune, DivPrune leads on nearly all datasets by up to 25.1% (e.g., POPE F1: 86.02 vs. 60.89) without requiring any calibration data. Against the fine-tuning-based M³, DivPrune achieves comparable or superior results on most tasks (e.g., MMB 59.19 vs. 65.81 is the main loss, but POPE F1 86.02 vs. 86.33 is near-parity) while being entirely training-free. On video tasks, DivPrune outperforms FastV by up to 12% and VTW by up to 19% on Video QA accuracy, confirming generalization beyond image models.

## Relation to Prior Work

DivPrune reframes visual token pruning in LMMs from an importance-scoring problem (as in FastV, PruMerge) or a calibration-driven problem (as in VTW, FitPrune) into a Max-Min Diversity Problem (MMDP) drawn from operations research. The key conceptual departure is that rather than asking which individual tokens are most important (via attention scores), it asks which subset of tokens is collectively most diverse, directly addressing the redundancy that attention-based methods introduce. This makes DivPrune training-free and calibration-free, unlike fine-tuning-based approaches (M³) or calibration-based ones (FitPrune, VTW), positioning it as a plug-and-play alternative that can be applied to any LMM architecture without model-specific adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a simple, training-free, calibration-free pruning method that maintains near-original performance at extreme compression ratios (≥80% token reduction) across multiple LMMs and 16 datasets, while also reducing GPU memory and end-to-end latency. The finding that diversity maximization is a more effective selection criterion than attention-based importance scoring challenges the default assumption in the token-pruning literature. Open questions include whether the exact MMDP solver scales to very large token counts (e.g., >2000 tokens in high-resolution or long-video settings), whether the diversity criterion generalizes to non-CLIP vision encoders or other modalities like audio, and whether the method can be combined with dynamic, input-adaptive token budgets rather than a fixed pruning ratio.
