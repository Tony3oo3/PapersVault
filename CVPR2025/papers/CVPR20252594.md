## Problem

Existing diffusion-based dance-to-music (D2M) generation methods extract only positive rhythmic cues from forward-played dance videos, overlooking the complementary negative rhythm information available in reverse-played counterparts. The paper argues that, analogous to positive and negative examples in supervised learning, bi-directional guidance from both forward and reverse temporal directions can improve the temporal correlation and rhythmic consistency between generated music and dance movements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252594/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 5-second dance video segments as input, from which it extracts 2048-dim visual embeddings via I3D and 1024-dim motion features via BlazePose 2D skeletons processed by ST-GCN; these are concatenated into a 3072-dim conditioning vector. The same video is played forward (positive conditioning c⁺) and backward (negative conditioning c⁻) to produce two distinct conditioning signals. The output is a 256×256 Mel-spectrogram (22,050 Hz sample rate) generated in a 32×32 latent space learned by a VAE, which is then decoded back to audio.

## Method

PN-Diffusion introduces a dual diffusion and reverse process: in the forward pass, noise ε is added to the positive latent z_t⁺ while −ε is added to the negative latent z_t⁻ (both starting from the same z₀), and in the reverse pass, positive conditioning c⁺ guides denoising of z_t⁺ while negative conditioning c⁻ guides denoising of z_t⁻. The U-Net is a 4-scale Stable Diffusion architecture (166.55M parameters) that receives both c⁺ and c⁻ as cross-attention inputs and produces two noise predictions. Training uses a bi-directional denoising objective L_ε = α‖ε − ε_θ⁺(z_t⁺, t, c⁺)‖² + (1−α)‖−ε − ε_θ⁻(z_t⁻, t, c⁻)‖², where α balances the two terms (optimal α ≈ 0.1–0.2). At inference, only the positive conditioning from the forward-played video is used to synthesize music, while the negative path serves purely as a training-time regularizer that sharpens the U-Net's discriminative capability.

## Results

On AIST++, PN-Diffusion achieves the best scores across all eight metrics: BCS 97.64, BHS 99.31, F1 98.46, FAD_v 6.32, FAD_p 4.35, and FAD_c 0.65, outperforming the strongest baseline LORIS by +1.80 on BCS and +4.22 on BHS. On TikTok, PN-Diffusion leads with BCS 89.51, BHS 91.73, F1 90.60, FAD_v 16.37, and FAD_p 1.14, though it trails CDCD slightly on FAD_c (1.25 vs. 1.11). In the user study on AIST++, PN-Diffusion scores 4.3/5 on overall quality and 4.2/5 on relevance, far exceeding CDCD (2.2/2.1) and LORIS (2.0/2.0), and achieves a 73.4% Turing Test pass rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PN-Diffusion outperforms all five baselines on the majority of metrics across both datasets. Against LORIS (the strongest baseline on AIST++), PN-Diffusion gains +1.80 BCS, +4.22 BHS, +2.01 F1, and substantially lower FAD_p (4.35 vs. 50.27), though LORIS retains a slight edge on FAD_c (0.77 vs. 0.65 — actually PN-Diffusion wins here too). Against CDCD, PN-Diffusion leads on all AIST++ metrics and on TikTok wins BCS (+3.85), BHS (+5.90), F1 (+4.85), FAD_v (16.37 vs. 26.53), and FAD_p (1.14 vs. 3.07), but trails CDCD on FAD_c (1.25 vs. 1.11). D2M-GAN and CMT are consistently outperformed by large margins on both datasets; CMT in particular shows very weak BHS (61.70 on AIST++, 60.03 on TikTok), indicating poor beat recall. FoleyMusic, evaluated only on AIST++, is surpassed by PN-Diffusion on every metric. The ablation further confirms that the reverse-played negative conditioning (vs. random-video or direct-negation alternatives) is the key factor behind these gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent diffusion framework for dance-to-music generation established by CDCD and LORIS, which both condition a U-Net on visual and motion features extracted from forward-played dance videos. The key departure is the introduction of a dual diffusion/reverse process where negative conditioning—derived from reverse-played dance videos—is jointly trained with positive conditioning through a bi-directional denoising objective. The perceptual VAE compression and U-Net architecture are adapted directly from Stable Diffusion (LDM), while the I3D visual encoder and ST-GCN motion encoder follow the feature extraction pipeline of CDCD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that incorporating negative rhythmic cues (via reverse-played video) alongside positive ones meaningfully improves beat alignment and audio quality in D2M generation, with particularly large gains in FAD_p. However, the practical impact is somewhat limited by the 5-second segment constraint, evaluation on only two datasets, and the fact that the negative conditioning contributes less than the positive one (optimal α = 0.1–0.2). Open questions include whether the reverse-played-video strategy generalizes to other cross-modal generation tasks, whether longer-segment generation is feasible with this dual-process formulation, and whether the large FAD_p gap over LORIS is partly attributable to the different segment lengths used (5 s vs. 25–50 s).
