## Problem

Pre-trained diffusion models like Stable Diffusion offer efficient architectures and broad ecosystem support, while flow matching provides faster inference and straighter sampling trajectories. However, directly applying the flow matching objective to a diffusion model fails because the two paradigms differ in timestep scaling, interpolant formulation, and output parameterization, causing slow convergence and degraded performance—especially under parameter-efficient finetuning constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251648/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method initializes from a pre-trained latent diffusion model (SD1.5 or SD2.1) operating in VAE latent space at 512×512 or 768×768 resolution. For text-to-image, it consumes a text prompt and a Gaussian noise sample, producing a generated image via Euler integration of the learned velocity field. For depth estimation, it takes an RGB image and outputs an affine-invariant depth map. The intermediate quantity is a velocity field v_θ(x_t, t) that is integrated over continuous timesteps t ∈ [0,1], with as few as 2 NFEs possible after reflow.

## Method

Diff2Flow bridges diffusion and flow matching through three aligned transformations: (1) a monotonic timestep mapping f_t(t_DM) = α_t/(α_t + σ_t) that converts discrete diffusion timesteps to continuous FM timesteps in [0,1], with linear interpolation between discrete points for non-integer values; (2) an interpolant rescaling f_x that normalizes the diffusion interpolant to match the FM linear path x_t = t·x_1 + (1−t)·x_0; and (3) an objective change that algebraically derives the FM velocity target from the diffusion model's existing v-parameterization prediction, eliminating the need for the network to learn a new output head. This alignment means the model retains its learned parameterization while being trained with the FM loss, making LoRA-based PEFT effective where naive FM finetuning fails. The training loop (Algorithm 1) maps FM timesteps back to diffusion timesteps, converts the FM interpolant to the diffusion trajectory, computes the velocity estimate from the diffusion prediction, and performs a gradient step on the standard FM objective.

## Results

On monocular depth estimation (Table 2), Diff2Flow (full fine-tuning, 74K synthetic training samples) achieves AbsRel 5.7 / δ1 96.7 on NYUv2, 8.7 / 92.0 on KITTI, 5.5 / 97.4 on ETH3D, 6.2 / 95.7 on ScanNet, and 21.6 / 79.5 on DIODE, matching or exceeding prior generative SOTA across all five benchmarks. In the reflow/trajectory-straightening setting (Table 3), Diff2Flow with only 62M trainable LoRA parameters achieves FID 21.45 at 25 NFEs and 32.31 at 2 NFEs, competitive with full 0.9B-parameter Rectified Flow (21.65 and 31.35 respectively). For text-to-image on COCO-2017 (Table 1), Diff2Flow improves FID from 56.77 (SD1.5) to 52.80 while also raising CLIP score from 26.34 to 26.54 and aesthetics score from 5.32 to 5.99.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest generative baselines, Diff2Flow (full) outperforms DepthFM [14] on all five depth benchmarks (e.g., AbsRel 5.7 vs 6.0 on NYUv2, 8.7 vs 9.1 on KITTI, 21.6 vs 22.4 on DIODE) and beats Marigold [24] on KITTI (8.7 vs 9.9), ETH3D (5.5 vs 6.5), and DIODE (21.6 vs 30.8), though Marigold edges it slightly on NYUv2 AbsRel (5.5 vs 5.7). Compared to E2E FT [38], Diff2Flow wins on KITTI (8.7 vs 9.6), ETH3D (5.5 vs 6.2), and DIODE (21.6 vs 30.2) but trails on NYUv2 (5.7 vs 5.2) and ScanNet (6.2 vs 5.8). Against GeoWizard [9], Diff2Flow is superior on KITTI (8.7 vs 9.7), ETH3D (5.5 vs 6.4), and DIODE (21.6 vs 29.7) but slightly behind on NYUv2 AbsRel (5.7 vs 5.2) and ScanNet (6.2 vs 6.1). In the reflow setting (Table 3), Diff2Flow with only 62M LoRA parameters achieves FID 21.45 at 25 steps, narrowly beating full 0.9B Rectified Flow (21.65) and approaching SD1.5+DPM-Solver (20.10); at 2 steps it reaches FID 32.31 vs Rectified Flow's 31.35, a small gap given the 15× parameter reduction. The LoRA variant of Diff2Flow retains most of the full model's performance (e.g., 5.9 vs 5.7 AbsRel on NYUv2), confirming that the alignment strategy makes parameter-efficient finetuning viable where naive FM+LoRA fails.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the theoretical unification of diffusion and flow matching (Lipman et al., Ho et al.) by explicitly deriving the mathematical correspondences—timestep rescaling, interpolant warping, and velocity-field conversion—that make a pre-trained diffusion model a valid initialization for FM training. It extends Lee et al. [27], who showed diffusion-to-FM finetuning is feasible, by formulating a principled alignment procedure and demonstrating it works across parameterizations (ε, v) and under PEFT constraints. The approach also connects to the Reflow/Rectified Flow line (Liu et al.) by enabling trajectory straightening on top of a diffusion prior, and to the LoRA/PEFT literature by showing that the alignment is what makes parameter-efficient finetuning viable for cross-paradigm transfer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Diff2Flow provides a practical, low-overhead recipe for converting the large ecosystem of pre-trained diffusion models (Stable Diffusion, SDXL, etc.) into flow matching models without retraining from scratch, which is significant given that state-of-the-art FM models (Flux, SD3) are too large for most practitioners to finetune. The finding that naive FM + LoRA fails while Diff2Flow + LoRA succeeds highlights that parameterization alignment is not a minor detail but a prerequisite for efficient cross-paradigm transfer. Open questions include whether the alignment generalizes to video diffusion, audio, or non-rectified flow variants, and whether the benefits persist as base models grow to the 8B+ scale where FM is already native.
