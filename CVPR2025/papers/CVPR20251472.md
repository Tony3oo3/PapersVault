## Problem

Despite rapid integration of video perception into Large Multimodal Models, the factors that actually drive video understanding remain poorly understood, with many design decisions made without systematic justification. The high computational cost of training and evaluating video-LMMs, combined with a broad and underexplored design space (sampling, encoders, token resampling, training schedules, data mixtures), hinders progress. Additionally, existing video QA benchmarks are redundant and largely solvable without true video perception, making evaluation both expensive and misleading.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251472/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Apollo takes variable-length video clips (up to over 1 hour) sampled at a fixed frames-per-second rate, paired with a text question, and produces a text answer. Video frames are encoded by a dual encoder (SigLIP-SO400M image encoder + InternVideo2 video encoder), with features interpolated and concatenated along the channel dimension before being resampled to 32 tokens per frame via a Perceiver Resampler. Clip timestamps are inserted between video token blocks to aid temporal grounding, and the entire sequence is fed to a Qwen2.5 LLM backbone (1.5B, 3B, or 7B parameters).

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a systematic empirical exploration of the video-LMM design space, guided by the discovery of Scaling Consistency: design decisions made on ~2–4B models with ~500K training samples reliably transfer to 7B+ models, enabling efficient experimentation. Key architectural findings include: fps-based sampling outperforms uniform frame sampling; a dual-encoder configuration (SigLIP-SO400M + InternVideo2) with channel-wise concatenation surpasses single encoders; Perceiver Resampling to 16–32 tokens/frame outperforms average pooling; and inserting learnable tokens or timestamps between clip features improves integration by 2–3%. Training uses a 3-stage progressive unfreezing schedule (vision encoders first, then connector, then LLM) with a data mixture of ~10–14% text and a video-heavy remainder. These findings are distilled into the Apollo model family and ApolloBench, a curated 400-question benchmark that is 41× faster to evaluate while remaining highly correlated with existing suites.

## Results

Apollo-7B achieves state-of-the-art results among 7B video-LMMs, scoring 70.9 on MLVU, 63.3 on Video-MME (with subtitles), 64.9 on TempCompass, and 66.3 on ApolloBench. Apollo-3B surpasses most 7B models, reaching 68.7 on MLVU and 60.6 on Video-MME, outperforming Oryx-7B (67.5 MLVU) and Video-XL-7B (64.9 MLVU). Apollo-7B even edges out Oryx-34B (70.8 MLVU) and substantially outperforms VILA-1.5-40B (56.7 MLVU) and InternVL2-34B (59.9 MLVU), demonstrating that careful design choices can compensate for model scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Apollo-7B (70.9 MLVU) outperforms all open-source baselines including Oryx-34B (70.8), InternVL2-34B (59.9), VILA-1.5-40B (56.7), and LLaVA-N-Video-32B (39.3), and also surpasses GPT-4V (64.1) and GPT-4o (64.6) on MLVU. On Video-MME, Apollo-7B (63.3 with subtitles) ties GPT-4V (63.3) but trails GPT-4o (77.2) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (81.3), indicating a remaining gap on this particular benchmark. Apollo-3B (68.7 MLVU) decisively beats same-size and larger 7B models such as Oryx-7B (67.5), Video-XL-7B (64.9), and Kangaroo-8B (61.0), while Apollo-1.5B (63.3 MLVU) surpasses LongVU-3.2B (55.9) and InternVL2-2B (48.2). On ApolloBench, Apollo-7B (66.3) beats GPT-4V (58.7) and LLaVA-OV-7B (64.0) but remains below GPT-4o (79.8) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (80.6). Overall, Apollo models consistently win against open-source video-LMMs of equal or greater parameter count, with the primary losses confined to the proprietary GPT-4o and Gemini-1.5-Pro on Video-MME and ApolloBench.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the image-LMM design space exploration initiated by Prismatic, Cambrian-1, and Eagle to the video domain, where prior video-LMMs (Video-LLaVA, LLaVA-OneVision, Oryx, Kangaroo) made numerous architectural choices—uniform frame sampling, single image encoders, average pooling—without systematic justification. It directly builds on Eagle's channel-wise concatenation strategy for dual encoders and Laurençon et al.'s Perceiver Resampler for token compression, adapting both to the video setting. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of Scaling Consistency, a principle that design decisions validated on 2–4B models transfer reliably to 7B models, which has no direct analogue in prior video-LMM literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a large fraction of existing video-QA benchmarks can be solved with text or a single frame challenges the field's assumption that current evaluations actually measure video perception, and ApolloBench offers a 6× faster, more discriminative alternative. Scaling Consistency, if it generalizes beyond the Qwen2.5 family tested here, could substantially reduce the computational barrier to video-LMM research by enabling design exploration on small models. Open questions include whether the 2–4B critical size holds for other LLM families or for models beyond 7B, and whether the fps-sampling advantage persists as context windows grow further. The practical impact is significant: Apollo-3B outperforming most 7B models suggests the field can achieve strong video understanding at lower compute budgets.
