## Problem

UAV cross-modality (RGB-IR) vehicle re-identification suffers from two underexplored challenges: the absence of a sufficiently large and diverse benchmark dataset, and the modality bias training (MBT) problem, where ImageNet pre-trained weights are skewed toward RGB and fail to generalize to larger, more modally diverse datasets, degrading IR discrimination.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251468/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During pre-training, the method consumes 256×256 RGB images from ImageNet, generates their IR counterparts via a CycleGAN trained on UCM-VeID V2, and produces patch-mixed images by swapping 75% of 4×4 grid patches between the RGB-IR pair. During fine-tuning, 180×256 vehicle images from UCM-VeID V2 are encoded into feature embeddings, and retrieval is evaluated via CMC (rank-1/10/20) and mAP in both single-shot and multi-shot query modes across IR→RGB and RGB→IR directions.

## Method

CPSL (Cross-Modality Patch-Mixed Self-supervised Learning) pre-trains a ResNet50 encoder by first generating cross-modality ImageNet pairs with CycleGAN, then randomly swapping patches between each RGB-IR pair to create two patch-mixed images. Three self-supervised tasks operate on the mixed-image embeddings: (1) PMIR reconstructs the original RGB and IR images from their respective mixed features, forcing the network to rely on modality-shared information since each mixed image contains mostly the other modality's content; (2) MDAL uses a discriminator to adversarially push the encoder toward modality-invariant representations; (3) MACL applies contrastive learning that treats the RGB-mixed and IR-mixed embeddings of the same image as positive pairs to enhance ID discriminability. The total loss is the sum of reconstruction, adversarial, and contrastive losses, and the resulting checkpoint replaces ImageNet-1K weights for downstream VT-ReID fine-tuning.

## Results

On UCM-VeID V2, CPSL pre-training consistently outperforms both ImageNet-1K and random initialization across all four VT-ReID methods (AGW, DDAG, MMD-ReID, DEEN) in both single-shot and multi-shot settings. The best result is achieved by AGW with CPSL in multi-shot RGB2IR mode: rank-1 = 32.64%, rank-10 = 68.07%, mAP = 24.01%, surpassing the same method with ImageNet-1K (rank-1 = 20.45%, mAP = 18.3%) by over 12 points in rank-1. In the SSL comparison (Table 3), CPSL achieves rank-1 = 32.64% and mAP = 24.01%, outperforming the strongest general-purpose SSL baseline MoCoV2 (rank-1 = 28.89%, mAP = 22.44%) by 3.75 and 1.57 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPSL pre-training dominates all 15 compared baselines on UCM-VeID V2. Against the four VT-ReID fine-tuning methods (AGW, DDAG, MMD-ReID, DEEN), CPSL initialization yields 7–12 point rank-1 gains over ImageNet-1K pre-training in multi-shot settings (e.g., AGW: 32.64% vs. 20.45% RGB2IR rank-1). Among general-purpose SSL pre-training methods, CPSL outperforms the closest competitor MoCoV2 by 3.75 rank-1 points (32.64% vs. 28.89%) and 1.57 mAP points (24.01% vs. 22.44%), and beats SimSiam (27.24%) and DenseCL (27.89%) by 5.4 and 4.75 rank-1 points respectively. Pretext-task methods (Jigsaw, RotationNet) and the VT-ReID-specific MMGL all trail CPSL by 5–11 rank-1 points. Notably, cluster-based SSL methods SwAV (6.57%) and BYOL (6.89%) perform near random initialization, suggesting they fail to establish a meaningful Euclidean feature space for Re-ID. The consistent wins across all four downstream ReID architectures indicate CPSL produces modality-balanced, discriminative features that generalize broadly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the UCM-VeID dataset (the first UAV RGB-IR vehicle ReID benchmark) by an order of magnitude in scale and diversity, and addresses the Modality Bias Training (MBT) problem previously identified for pedestrian VT-ReID [16]. Rather than following the first MBT approach of controlling the training process (learning-rate scheduling, gradient modulation), the authors adopt the second approach of replacing ImageNet pre-trained weights with modally-balanced self-supervised weights, drawing on MAE-style reconstruction, GAN-based adversarial training, and contrastive learning. The CycleGAN-based cross-modality data generation repurposes unpaired image-to-image translation to create synthetic RGB-IR pairs from ImageNet, enabling large-scale pre-training without requiring labeled cross-modality vehicle data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UCM-VeID V2 provides the community with a substantially richer benchmark (165K images, 3,407 IDs, 5 cameras, multi-platform) that better reflects real-world UAV deployment conditions, addressing a critical resource gap in cross-modality vehicle ReID. The CPSL pre-training paradigm offers a general recipe for mitigating modality bias in any cross-modality recognition task, though its practical impact is tempered by the fact that fine-tuning hyperparameters were not specifically tuned for CPSL and evaluation is limited to a single dataset. Open questions include whether the CycleGAN-generated cross-modality pairs introduce artifacts that limit pre-training quality, whether the approach generalizes to other modality pairs (e.g., RGB-thermal, RGB-LiDAR), and whether the MBT phenomenon persists with newer backbone architectures beyond ResNet-50.
