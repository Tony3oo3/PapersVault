## Problem

Existing unsupervised clothing change ReID (UCC-ReID) methods rely on clustering to generate pseudo-labels, but clustering algorithms fail to group images of the same person wearing different clothing into the same cluster, producing numerous 'unconnected positive pairs.' Furthermore, standard optimization objectives indiscriminately push apart all cross-cluster pairs, including these unconnected positive pairs, which actively reduces the feature similarity of same-identity, different-clothing images and hinders clothing-invariant learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250690/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB person images resized to 256×128 pixels, augmented with random horizontal flipping and random cropping. During training, a human parsing model decomposes each image into an identity-related sub-image (clothing pixels zeroed) and a clothing-related sub-image (non-clothing pixels set to 255), which feed into frozen CLIP encoders to produce cluster-level text-form features. At test time, only the final ResNet-50 image encoder (pre-trained on CLIP) is used to extract a feature embedding per image for identity match ranking via mAP and CMC.

## Method

ICSM is a three-stage pipeline. Stage I performs iterative DBSCAN clustering and optimization (identity classification loss + hard contrastive loss) to obtain compact clusters where images share both identity and clothing. Stage II uses human parsing to derive identity-related and clothing-related images, then trains two CLIP models (CLIP-S, CLIP-C) with learnable text embeddings to produce a cluster-level identity feature and a cluster-level clothing feature per compact cluster. Stage III applies inter-cluster similarity estimation (ISE) to classify other clusters relative to each cluster as clothing-change positive, reliable negative, or hard negative based on thresholds over identity and clothing similarity sequences; it then optimizes the image encoder with adaptive versions of the classification and contrastive losses restricted to reliable pairs (preserving unconnected positive pair similarities), plus an identity-oriented semantic contrastive loss (KL divergence between text-similarity and image-similarity distributions) and a clothing-invariant semantic contrastive loss (hinge loss penalizing image–own-clothing-feature similarity exceeding the minimum over positive-cluster clothing features).

## Results

ICSM achieves mAP 57.8 / R-1 47.9 on PRCC(CC), mAP 73.5 / R-1 75.1 on VC-Clothes(CC), and mAP 30.1 / R-1 60.8 on LTCC(G), setting new state-of-the-art among all unsupervised methods across all eight testing scenarios. On the same-clothing setting (PRCC(SC)), ICSM reaches mAP 97.5 / R-1 97.1, indicating that the clothing-invariant learning does not sacrifice same-clothing performance. Training time on VC(CC) is 7,236 s, slightly above SiCL (6,798 s) but well below CICL (10,570 s), with comparable test time (~14 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the seven UT-ReID baselines, ICSM achieves the largest gains in clothing-change settings: on PRCC(CC) it reaches mAP 57.8 vs. 52.3 for the best UT-ReID method (Purification), and on VC(CC) it reaches 73.5 vs. 49.7 (CACL), confirming that standard unsupervised ReID pipelines are poorly suited to clothing variation. Among the two dedicated UCC-ReID methods, ICSM outperforms SiCL by +9.6 mAP on VC(CC) (73.5 vs. 63.9) and by +2.4 mAP on PRCC(CC) (57.8 vs. 55.4), and beats CICL by +4.2 mAP on VC(CC) (73.5 vs. 69.3) and +2.5 mAP on PRCC(CC). Compared to supervised CC-ReID methods, ICSM is competitive with earlier approaches (RCSANet, CAL) on PRCC(CC) and surpasses GI-ReID on both VC(CC) (73.5 vs. 57.8) and LTCC(CC) (13.5 vs. 10.4), but remains below the most advanced supervised methods (AIM, CCFA, FIRe²) on LTCC, where the gap is roughly 8–10 mAP. On the same-clothing setting (PRCC(SC)), ICSM achieves mAP 97.5, slightly above CICL (96.7) and all UT-ReID baselines, showing that the clothing-invariant objective does not degrade same-clothing retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unsupervised person re-identification (UT-ReID) paradigm of iterative clustering and contrastive optimization to the clothing change setting, building directly on the UCC-ReID formulations of SiCL and CICL, which use human-parsing body shapes to obtain clothing-robust pseudo-labels. Its key conceptual departure is reframing the problem: rather than stripping clothing information to make features clothing-invariant (as prior UCC-ReID methods do), ICSM deliberately retains clothing for compact clustering and then uses CLIP-based cluster-level text features to separate identity from clothing at the inter-cluster level. The adaptive optimization and text-semantic contrastive losses are novel contributions that repurpose the vision-language model not as a feature extractor but as a supervisory signal generator for the image encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-diagnosed bottleneck in UCC-ReID—the proliferation of unconnected positive pairs that silently degrade clothing-invariant learning—and shows that explicitly modeling inter-cluster identity/clothing similarity can close much of the gap to supervised methods without any identity labels. The three-stage pipeline is practical (training time comparable to single-stage baselines) and the ablations cleanly isolate each component's contribution. Open questions include whether the CLIP-based cluster-level feature extraction generalizes to very large-scale deployments where the number of clusters grows, whether the fixed DBSCAN pseudo-labels from Stage I can be refined iteratively in Stage III, and whether the approach extends naturally to multi-modal (e.g., infrared) clothing change scenarios.
