## Problem

Existing zero-shot NAS proxy metrics are largely confined to convolutional search spaces and struggle to outperform trivial baselines like parameter count on Vision Transformer search spaces. Furthermore, while different proxies capture complementary information, no principled, training-free method exists to select and combine the most informative subset of proxies for a given benchmark.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250342/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

L-SWAG takes a batch of 64 images (32 for TransNAS-Bench-101 due to memory constraints) and a DNN at random initialization (CNN with ReLU or ViT with GeLU), performing a single forward and backward pass to extract per-layer gradient statistics and activation patterns. It outputs a scalar ranking score per architecture. LIBRA-NAS takes as input the pre-computed Spearman ρ values, information gain values, and bias values (Pearson correlation with parameter count) for all available ZC proxies on a target benchmark, and outputs a subset of three proxy metrics. Gradient extraction for 1000 ViTs (15–35M params, 224×224 ImageNet) takes ~31 minutes with ~10 GB memory; the final L-SWAG score computation after layer selection takes ~4 minutes.

## Method

L-SWAG multiplicatively combines a layer-wise trainability term Λ and an expressivity term Ψ. The trainability term sums log(1/σ) over a selected interval of layers [l̂, L̂], deliberately discarding the gradient mean μ that ZiCO retains (justified by a new Theorem 1 showing μ is unnecessary for the loss bound). The layer interval is identified empirically by plotting gradient standard-deviation percentiles across 1000 randomly sampled networks and selecting the range spanning observed spikes. The expressivity term Ψ counts the cardinality of layer-wise sample-wise activation patterns, extending NWOT's binary ReLU pattern counting to GeLU activations for the first time. LIBRA-NAS then selects three proxies per benchmark: the highest-ρ proxy, a second proxy minimizing conditional information gain H(y|z₁)−H(y|z₁,z₂) among near-best performers (within 0.1 ρ), and a third proxy whose parameter-count bias most closely matches the validation accuracy's bias, replacing prior bias-mitigation strategies with bias-matching.

## Results

On the Autoformer Small ImageNet1k search space, LIBRA-NAS identifies an architecture with 17.0% test error in just 0.1 GPU days, outperforming the original Autoformer evolution (18.3%, 24 GPU days) and AZ-NAS (17.8%, 0.07 GPU days). On DARTS ImageNet1k, LIBRA achieves 23.1% test error in 0.3 GPU days, beating PC-DARTS (24.2%, 3.8 GPU days) and NASNet-A (26.0%, 2000 GPU days). In terms of proxy correlation, L-SWAG achieves an average Spearman ρ of 0.72 across 14 benchmarks, surpassing the second-best metric NWOT (ρ=0.62), and LIBRA outperforms AZ-NAS on 13 out of 19 tasks in the correlation comparison.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LIBRA-NAS outperforms all training-based NAS methods (PC-DARTS, AmoebaNet, NASNet-A, ENAS) on both DARTS and Autoformer search spaces, achieving the lowest test error with 1–4 orders of magnitude less search time. Among training-free competitors, LIBRA beats AZ-NAS by 0.8% on Autoformer ImageNet1k (17.0% vs 17.8%) and by 0.5% on DARTS ImageNet1k (23.1% vs 23.6%), while marginally outperforming SWAG on DARTS Cifar-10 (2.45% vs 2.47%). In the proxy correlation comparison (Table 1), LIBRA surpasses AZ-NAS on 13 of 19 tasks, with the largest gaps on NB101 (0.77 vs 0.54) and TNB101-Macro AE (0.83 vs 0.52); AZ-NAS retains a slight edge only on NB201 Cifar-10 (0.91 vs 0.89) and ImageNet16-120 (0.89 vs 0.87). The L-SWAG metric itself outperforms ZiCO and SWAP on TNB101-Macro (where ZiCO fails to correlate) and TNB101-Micro (where SWAP's ρ diminishes), but underperforms Fisher on the challenging TNB101-Micro autoencoder task. On the new Autoformer ViT search space, L-SWAG is the only metric that consistently outperforms the simple #Parameters proxy, whereas most other ZC proxies struggle.

## Relation to Prior Work

L-SWAG is directly derived from ZiCO (Li et al., ICLR 2023), discarding the gradient mean component and introducing a layer-wise formulation motivated by empirical spike patterns in gradient statistics. The expressivity term draws on the sample-wise activation pattern literature (NWOT, SWAP) but extends it to GeLU networks for the first time. LIBRA-NAS builds on the information-theoretic framework of NAS-Bench-SuiteZero (Krishnakumar et al., NeurIPS 2022) for measuring complementary information between proxies, but replaces predictor-based combination with a lightweight selection algorithm and introduces a bias-matching criterion. The paper's broader positioning is to extend zero-shot NAS from the well-studied CNN search spaces (DARTS, NAS-Bench-201) to Vision Transformer search spaces (Autoformer), a gap the authors identify as largely unaddressed by prior ZC-proxy work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that most existing ZC proxies fail to outperform simple parameter counts on ViT search spaces is a significant warning for the field as transformers become the dominant architecture family, suggesting that theoretically-grounded metrics developed for ReLU CNNs do not transfer. The new benchmark of 2000 trained ViT architectures across six tasks provides a much-needed evaluation resource for this gap. However, LIBRA's evaluation remains purely empirical without theoretical guarantees, and L-SWAG has not yet been extended to video or other modalities. The practical impact is moderate: 0.1 GPU days to find a 17.0% ImageNet1k architecture is competitive, but the correlation improvements (0.72 vs 0.62 average ρ) are incremental rather than transformative.
