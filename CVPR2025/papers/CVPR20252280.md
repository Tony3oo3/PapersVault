## Problem

Current event-based video reconstruction methods focus exclusively on dynamic content and fail to recover static background information, leaving reconstructed videos without scene context. Although recent work has shown that event cameras can generate stable event streams from static scenes under constant illumination, existing integration-based approaches suffer from severe noise accumulation and pixel-value saturation, producing low-quality outputs. Moreover, no prior method can jointly reconstruct both static backgrounds and moving foregrounds in a single end-to-end pipeline.

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw asynchronous event stream (from a DVS sensor) as input and produces two outputs: a high-fidelity static background frame (Stage 1) and a sequence of motion video frames with that background preserved (Stage 2). The raw stream is first partitioned into 20×20-pixel, 10-ms spatiotemporal windows; events exceeding a global count threshold within each window are labeled dynamic and converted to 5-channel voxel grids, while the remainder are treated as static and processed through convolutional integration. The final fused tensor combines the reconstructed background frame, the dynamic voxel grid, and a binary event-separation label, yielding a 7-channel input to the ERSD network that outputs 40-frame video sequences at the original spatial resolution.

## Method

URSEE is a two-stage, end-to-end framework. Stage 1 (static reconstruction) replaces the conventional pixel-wise event summation with a 3×3 convolutional mean-filter integration that prevents pixel-value polarization and noise accumulation, followed by a U-Net denoising module (SRD) with layer normalization and channel attention to recover frame-camera-quality images. Stage 2 (static-dynamic fusion) runs two parallel channels: static events are reconstructed into a background frame via Stage 1, while dynamic events are encoded as voxel grids; both are concatenated with a binary separation-label tensor and fed into the ERSD module, a recurrent U-Net whose encoding layers each pair a custom convolutional block (with layer norm and channel attention) with a ConvLSTM cell to propagate temporal context. The entire pipeline is trained with MSE loss under supervised learning, using a real-captured E-Static dataset (200 indoor/outdoor scenes at 1280×720, cropped to 512×512) for the denoising module and a synthetic E-StaDyn dataset (130 scenes, 600 frames each, rendered via Blender and simulated with DVS-Voltmeter) for the ERSD module.

## Results

On the E-StaDyn synthetic dataset for dynamic video reconstruction, URSEE achieves PSNR 31.97, SSIM 0.959, and LPIPS 0.122, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline (retrained E2VID) by 13.5 dB in PSNR and 0.167 in SSIM. On the E-Static real-world dataset for static image reconstruction, URSEE reaches PSNR 22.43, SSIM 0.860, and LPIPS 0.244, exceeding the next-best method (retrained E2VID) by 4.5 dB in PSNR. The method also generalizes to real-captured data, where E2VID (even when retrained) fails to reliably reconstruct static backgrounds while URSEE maintains high-fidelity output.

## Baselines & Comparisons

URSEE dominates all six baselines on both static and dynamic reconstruction tasks by wide margins. On E-StaDyn (video), URSEE's 31.97 PSNR exceeds retrained E2VID (18.45) by 13.5 dB and original E2VID (14.01) by 18 dB; ET-Net (13.67) and FireNet (9.81) trail further behind, while EVSNN (8.04) and E2HQV (9.71) perform worst. On E-Static (static images), URSEE's 22.43 PSNR surpasses retrained E2VID (17.94) by 4.5 dB, and all original baselines fall below 10 PSNR, with SSL (8.78) and E2HQV (6.49) showing near-failure on static content. The authors note that even after retraining E2VID on the proposed dataset, it cannot reliably reconstruct static backgrounds, confirming a fundamental architectural incompatibility with static events rather than a data insufficiency issue. The gap is most dramatic on LPIPS (0.122 vs. 0.448 for retrained E2VID), indicating substantially better perceptual quality beyond pixel-level fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

URSEE extends the E2VID recurrent ConvLSTM + U-Net architecture for event-based video reconstruction by adding a parallel static-event channel and a convolutional integration front-end, addressing E2VID's fundamental inability to recover static backgrounds. For the static reconstruction component, it departs from the pixel-wise noise-event integration of Galor et al. and Cao et al. by replacing per-pixel summation with a 3×3 convolutional kernel, which the authors show prevents the noise accumulation and event saturation that degrade prior methods. The paper also positions itself against device-dependent approaches (active illumination, transmittance modulation) by requiring no external hardware, and against mask-based fusion strategies (Cao et al.) by performing end-to-end joint reconstruction rather than post-hoc compositing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first end-to-end framework that reconstructs motion videos with faithful static backgrounds purely from event streams, removing the need for a co-located frame camera and preserving the data-efficiency advantage of event-only pipelines. The E-Static dataset (200 real-captured static scenes) fills a notable gap in the event-camera literature, where prior static-reconstruction work relied on synthetic or device-specific data. Key limitations include a performance bottleneck at high illumination (sensor-dependent), dependence on the Prophesee EVK4 response model, and evaluation primarily on self-created datasets; generalization to other event sensors and more complex real-world scenes remains open.
