## Problem

Autoregressive visual generation models produce high-quality outputs but suffer from slow inference due to strictly sequential token-by-token prediction, with generation time scaling linearly with sequence length. Existing acceleration approaches (speculative decoding, masked modeling, next-scale prediction) either require auxiliary models, specialized tokenizers, or depart from the pure autoregressive paradigm, limiting their flexibility as a unified generation framework. The paper asks whether parallel generation can be achieved within standard autoregressive transformers without architectural modifications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251784/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label as conditioning input and produces a 256×256 image (ImageNet) or a 17-frame 128×128 video (UCF-101). Images are tokenized into 24×24 discrete token sequences via VQGAN (16× downsampling, 16K codebook), while videos are compressed into 5×16×16 token grids via MAGVIT-v2 (8× spatial, 4× temporal downsampling). The parallel generation strategy reduces inference steps from 576 to 147 (4× parallel) or 51 (16× parallel) for images, and from 1280 to 323 or 95 for videos, while maintaining the same output format as standard autoregressive decoding.

## Method

The core insight is that parallel generation feasibility is governed by token dependencies: spatially distant tokens have weak correlations and can be sampled independently, while adjacent tokens have strong dependencies that cause inconsistent results under independent sampling. The method divides the token grid into M×M regions and operates in two stages: first, initial tokens for each region are generated sequentially to establish global structure; second, tokens at aligned positions across all regions are predicted in parallel at each step. To implement this within a standard autoregressive transformer, the paper introduces learnable transition tokens that facilitate the switch from sequential to parallel prediction mode, employs 2D RoPE to preserve spatial position information under the reordered sequence, and uses group-wise bi-directional attention within each parallel group combined with causal attention between groups to maintain rich context access while preserving the autoregressive property. The approach requires no architectural changes or additional models, achieving 3.6× speedup with comparable FID and up to 9.5× speedup with minimal quality degradation.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, PAR-3B-4× achieves FID 2.29 with 147 steps in 3.46s, compared to LlamaGen-3B's FID 2.18 with 576 steps in 12.41s (3.6× speedup). With more aggressive parallelization, PAR-3B-16× reaches FID 2.88 in just 51 steps and 1.31s (9.5× speedup). On UCF-101 video generation, PAR-4× achieves FVD 99.5 in 11.27s versus the sequential baseline's FVD 94.1 in 43.30s (3.8× speedup), and PAR-16× reaches FVD 103.4 in 3.44s (12.6× speedup).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct autoregressive baseline LlamaGen [42], PAR-4× matches quality at larger model sizes (FID 2.35 vs 2.34 for XXL; 2.29 vs 2.18 for 3B) while reducing steps from 576 to 147 and wall-clock time by 3.6×; PAR-16× trades 0.7 FID for a 9.5× speedup. Compared to the mask-based MaskGIT [3], PAR-3B-4× achieves substantially better FID (2.29 vs 6.18) despite using more steps. Against VAR [44], which achieves a slightly lower FID (1.97 vs 2.29), PAR maintains a simpler pure-autoregressive framework without requiring a specialized multi-scale tokenizer. Versus the diffusion model DiT-XL/2 [28], PAR-3B-4× achieves comparable FID (2.29 vs 2.27) in 3.46s versus 11.97s. On video generation, PAR-4× outperforms MAGVIT-v2-AR [60] on FVD (99.5 vs 109) while running 3.8× faster, and PAR-16× achieves 12.6× speedup with only 9.3 FVD degradation over the sequential baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LlamaGen's autoregressive transformer architecture for class-conditional image generation, applying a non-local parallel generation strategy without modifying the model or tokenizer. It departs from prior parallelization attempts in language modeling (speculative decoding, Jacobi decoding) and visual generation (MaskGIT's non-autoregressive masking, VAR's next-scale prediction) by preserving the pure autoregressive formulation while reordering token generation to exploit spatial dependency structure. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that parallel generation should group spatially distant tokens with weak dependencies rather than adjacent tokens, a design principle motivated by pilot studies showing that independent sampling of strongly correlated local tokens produces visibly inconsistent patterns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3.6× speedup with near-identical FID (2.29 vs. 2.18) and 9.5× speedup with only 0.7 FID degradation make autoregressive visual generation substantially more practical for real-time or resource-constrained applications, while the drop-in compatibility with standard AR transformers lowers the adoption barrier. The finding that initial tokens per region must remain sequential to establish global structure, while subsequent tokens can be parallelized, provides a reusable design principle for other sequential generation tasks. Open questions include whether effective temporal parallelization is possible for video (the authors found it less effective than spatial), whether the approach extends to text-conditional or multi-modal generation, and how it interacts with emerging unified autoregressive models that handle multiple modalities in a single sequence.
