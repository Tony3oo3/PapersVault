## Problem

Existing VLMs struggle with fine-grained compositional region captioning: they cannot accurately align segmentation masks with corresponding semantics or precisely describe the compositional attributes (color, material, gesture, texture, etc.) of referred regions. Bounding boxes provide imprecise region references (mean IoU with ground-truth masks is only 56.11%), and freeform overlaid visual prompts confuse models into treating them as semantic content. No prior model simultaneously supports mask-based region referencing, high-resolution encoding, and multi-grained compositional attribute captioning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252164/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a low-resolution RGB image (336×336), a high-resolution RGB image (1024×1024), a binary segmentation mask (336×336×1) indicating the region of interest, and a free-form text instruction specifying which compositional attributes to describe. The mask is not overlaid on the image but is fed as a separate alpha channel into the CLIP encoder's patch-embedding convolution. Outputs are structured captions at three granularity levels: Attribute-Aware Regional Captioning (describing specific attributes of the masked region), Regional Dense Captioning (comprehensive description of the region), and Comprehensive Global Image Captioning (full-image description), spanning 18 compositional attribute categories.

## Method

FINECAPTION employs a mixture-of-encoder architecture that fuses three visual feature streams: (1) a mask-aware CLIP-ViT-L/14@336p encoder where an additional 1-channel convolution (Conv_α, following Alpha-CLIP) encodes the binary mask and is added to the RGB patch embeddings before the transformer, (2) a ConvNeXt-XXL@1024p encoder, and (3) a SAM encoder, both operating at 1024×1024 for fine-grained spatial detail. The three feature maps are resized to common spatial dimensions and concatenated channel-wise, then projected into LLaMA-3.1-8B's embedding space via a linear adapter. Training proceeds in three stages: projector pre-training on LLaVA-Pretrain (encoders and LLM frozen, mask covers full image), image-mask alignment pre-training on COMPOSITIONCAP plus RefCOCO/RefCOCO+/RefCOCOg/GranD (only the mask-aware encoder trainable), and full instruction tuning on COMPOSITIONCAP with all parameters unfrozen. The key design insight is that low-resolution mask-aware encoding provides precise region grounding while high-resolution encoders supply the detailed compositional information that 224–448p encoders in prior mask-based methods cannot capture.

## Results

Under supervised learning on COMPOSITIONCAP, FINECAPTION-8B achieves ROUGE-L 41.05, BLEU-4 14.46, METEOR 22.01, CIDEr 127.95, and BERTScore 80.97, outperforming the next-best model LLaMA-3.2-11B-Vision (CIDEr 99.11) by ~29 points on CIDEr. In the zero-shot setting, even the strongest baseline GPT-4o reaches only CIDEr 6.49 and METEOR 12.87, highlighting the difficulty of mask-referring compositional captioning without task-specific training. On RefCOCOg, FINECAPTION achieves METEOR 17.5 and CIDEr 118.2, surpassing the previous best (RegionGPT: METEOR 16.9, CIDEr 109.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FINECAPTION-8B outperforms all 17 baselines across every metric in the supervised setting on COMPOSITIONCAP. The closest competitor, LLaMA-3.2-11B-Vision (bbox-based, no mask input), trails by 2.91 ROUGE-L, 1.59 BLEU-4, 3.70 METEOR, and 28.84 CIDEr. Among mask-based baselines, Alpha-CLIP-13B (the closest architectural predecessor) is surpassed by 5.37 ROUGE-L and 34.10 CIDEr, confirming the benefit of adding high-resolution encoders (ConvNeXt + SAM at 1024×1024) on top of the mask-aware CLIP encoder. In the zero-shot regime, even GPT-4o (CIDEr 6.49) and InternVL-2-40B (CIDEr 4.63) struggle substantially, indicating that mask-referring compositional captioning remains an unsolved capability for general-purpose VLMs without task-specific fine-tuning. On RefCOCOg, FINECAPTION leads all baselines by 0.6 METEOR and 8.3 CIDEr over the previous best (RegionGPT), further validating its region-grounding precision.

## Relation to Prior Work

FINECAPTION extends the mask-aware encoding paradigm introduced by Alpha-CLIP (which adds a binary mask as an alpha channel to CLIP's patch embedding) by coupling it with two high-resolution encoders (ConvNeXt-XXL and SAM) operating at 1024×1024, a multi-resolution fusion strategy not present in prior mask-based VLMs like Osprey, GLaMM, or VCoder that process images at 224–448 resolution. The training pipeline follows the LLaVA instruction-tuning recipe (projector pre-training → alignment → instruction tuning) but inserts a dedicated image-mask alignment stage. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant bounding-box and overlaid-contour region-referral formats used by Kosmos-2, Shikra, Ferret, and ViP-LLaVA, arguing that freeform visual prompts confuse VLMs and that bounding boxes are geometrically imprecise (mean IoU with ground-truth masks of only 56.11).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of COMPOSITIONCAP (5,392 images, 14,590 entities, 18 compositional attributes, three captioning granularities) fills a concrete gap: prior region-captioning benchmarks (RefCOCO, GranD) focus on object identification rather than rich attribute-aware compositional description. The finding that even GPT-4o achieves only 6.49 CIDEr zero-shot on this task highlights a persistent weakness in frontier VLMs for fine-grained region-level compositional reasoning. Open questions include whether the multi-encoder architecture generalizes beyond the LLaMA-3.1-8B backbone, whether the relatively small training set (5,392 images) limits robustness, and whether GPT-4-as-a-judge evaluation introduces systematic bias. The dataset and task formulation could serve as a useful benchmark for future region-level VLM research.
