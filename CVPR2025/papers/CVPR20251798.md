## Problem

Existing diffusion-based super-resolution methods either require per-step optimization (slow inference) or fine-tuning of the diffusion backbone (modifying the prior), and they are locked into a fixed number of sampling steps. Diffusion inversion for SR is particularly challenging because the multi-step stochastic sampling process accumulates prediction errors, and prior inversion work targets lower-fidelity tasks like image editing rather than the strict fidelity demands of SR.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251798/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution RGB image (e.g., 128×128 for ×4 SR) and a fixed generic text prompt as input, and produces a high-resolution RGB image (e.g., 512×512). All computation occurs in the VQGAN latent space of SD-Turbo. A distinctive aspect is that the user can freely choose the number of sampling steps (1–5) and the starting timestep at inference time, adapting the fidelity–realism trade-off to the specific degradation type without retraining.

## Method

InvSR introduces a lightweight noise predictor (VQGAN-encoder-based, ~34M params) that, given an LR image and a timestep, estimates the optimal noise map to construct an intermediate diffusion state, leaving the pre-trained SD-Turbo backbone entirely frozen. The key Partial Noise Prediction (PnP) strategy limits noise prediction to only the starting step: the LR image is perturbed with the predicted noise to form x_κM, which serves as the entry point for reverse diffusion, while all subsequent intermediate steps use standard random noise. This works because LR and HR images become indistinguishable under appropriate Gaussian perturbation, so the noisy LR acts as a valid proxy for the unknown HR. The noise predictor is trained with L2 + LPIPS + GAN losses in latent space across multiple starting timesteps (250, 200, 150, 100) via time embedding, enabling arbitrary-step sampling at inference by simply selecting a starting timestep and applying an off-the-shelf accelerated sampler (DDIM-style) with 1–5 steps.

## Results

On ImageNet-Test (×4 SR, 128→512), InvSR-1 achieves PSNR 24.14, SSIM 0.6789, LPIPS 0.2517, NIQE 4.38, PI 3.09, CLIPIQA 0.709, and MUSIQ 72.29 with a single sampling step. It outperforms the closest one-step competitor OSEDiff-1 on all seven metrics (e.g., LPIPS 0.2517 vs. 0.2624, MUSIQ 72.29 vs. 70.39) while using only 33.84M learnable parameters. On RealSet80, InvSR-1 attains the best NIQE (4.03) and PI (3.47) among all methods, and the best MUSIQ (69.81) among one-step methods, confirming its advantage in perceptual quality with minimal sampling cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest one-step diffusion competitor OSEDiff-1, InvSR-1 wins on all seven metrics on both ImageNet-Test and RealSR (e.g., LPIPS 0.2517 vs. 0.2624, MUSIQ 72.29 vs. 70.39 on ImageNet-Test; NIQE 4.22 vs. 5.33 on RealSR), while using ~4× more parameters (33.84M vs. 8.50M). Compared to multi-step diffusion methods (50 steps), InvSR-1 matches or exceeds StableSR-50 and DiffBIR-50 on perception-oriented metrics (LPIPS, NIQE, PI, MUSIQ) but trails them on PSNR/SSIM, reflecting the fidelity-realism trade-off inherent to fewer sampling steps. Against GAN-based baselines (BSRGAN, RealESRGAN), InvSR-1 achieves lower PSNR/SSIM but substantially better perceptual scores (e.g., MUSIQ 72.29 vs. 67.72 for BSRGAN; PI 3.09 vs. 3.71). On RealSet80, InvSR-1 achieves the best NIQE (4.03) and PI (3.47) overall, surpassing even DiffBIR-50 (NIQE 3.86, PI 3.21) on PI and matching it closely on NIQE, while leading all one-step methods on MUSIQ (69.81 vs. 68.82 for OSEDiff-1). The paper also demonstrates that InvSR's flexible sampling (1–5 steps) allows users to trade fidelity for realism: 5-step sampling improves PSNR to 22.70 but degrades MUSIQ to 69.84, whereas 1-step at timestep 100 pushes PSNR to 24.66 at the cost of MUSIQ dropping to 70.83.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant paradigms for diffusion-prior-based SR: (1) iterative optimization of intermediate features (DDRM, CCDF, DDNM) and (2) fine-tuning of pre-trained T2I models (StableSR, DiffBIR, SeeSR). Instead, it keeps the diffusion backbone entirely frozen and trains a lightweight noise predictor to find a single optimal starting noise map, reformulating the inversion problem via a Partial Noise Prediction (PnP) strategy that limits prediction to one timestep rather than all T steps. This connects to the broader diffusion inversion literature (DDIM inversion, null-text inversion, ReNoise), but those methods target image editing with lower fidelity demands; the paper explicitly tailors inversion to the high-fidelity constraints of SR by enforcing a high-SNR starting point.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The flexible 1-to-5-step sampling mechanism is practically valuable: users can adapt the number of steps to the degradation type (blur vs. noise) without retraining, and the frozen-backbone design avoids the heavy fine-tuning cost of methods like SeeSR (751M params) while achieving competitive or superior results with only 34M learnable parameters. A notable open question is whether the PnP strategy generalizes beyond ×4 SR to other restoration tasks (denoising, deblurring) or to higher resolution scales, and whether the SNR threshold constraint (timestep ≤ 250) limits applicability to severely degraded inputs. The finding that additional sampling steps can *degrade* non-reference metrics by amplifying noise is a useful caution for practitioners choosing step counts.
