## Problem

Creating relightable 3D assets from multi-view captures remains challenging for objects with ill-defined shapes (fur, fabric, grass), because the decomposition between light, geometry, and material is ill-posed without smooth-surface assumptions. Existing 3DGS-based relighting methods rely on analytical shading models and surface constraints (e.g., valid normals), which fail for soft-boundary objects, while the prior NeRF-based method NRHints handles such objects but suffers from high training/rendering costs and over-smoothed details.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images captured with a single moving point light (downsampled to 512×512, up to 1000 views) as training input. At inference, it accepts a novel camera pose and a novel point-light position, and outputs a relit rendered image at 60 fps on an RTX 4090. The learned representation is a set of 3D Gaussians each carrying a 16-channel latent feature vector, which together with the MLP decoder constitutes the relightable asset.

## Method

RNG replaces analytical BRDF models with per-Gaussian latent vectors decoded by a 4-layer MLP conditioned on view direction, light direction, and a shadow cue, avoiding all surface and shading-model assumptions. A shadow cue (1-channel visibility map) is computed via a two-pass Gaussian splatting procedure: first splatting to the camera to obtain depth, then splatting to a virtual shadow camera at the light position to measure the distance |PQ| between shading points. A depth refinement MLP corrects the inaccurate weighted-sum depth proxy by learning a per-pixel view-dependent scaling factor, ensuring correctly located shading points for the shadow map. Training uses a two-stage hybrid strategy: forward shading (decode-then-blend) for the first 30k iterations to establish geometry, followed by deferred shading (blend-then-decode) with the shadow cue enabled to sharpen shadow appearance while preserving geometric quality.

## Results

On the 13-scene point-light relighting benchmark (Table 1), RNG achieves an average PSNR of 31.84, SSIM of 0.9475, and LPIPS of 0.0392, outperforming NRHints (PSNR 31.43, LPIPS 0.0636) and GS³ (PSNR 30.54, LPIPS 0.0478) on both PSNR and LPIPS while matching or exceeding GS³ on SSIM. Under novel environment lighting (Table 2), RNG achieves an average PSNR of 30.70 and LPIPS of 0.0557, substantially surpassing GS-IR (PSNR 25.27) and Relightable 3D Gaussian (PSNR 24.04). Training takes approximately 1.3 hours and rendering reaches 60 fps on an RTX 4090, more than 20× faster than the NeRF-based NRHints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NRHints [27] (NeRF-based, SIGGRAPH 2023), RNG wins on average PSNR (31.84 vs. 31.43) and LPIPS (0.0392 vs. 0.0636) while NRHints retains a slight SSIM edge (0.9383 vs. 0.9475 — actually RNG also wins SSIM). RNG is more than 20× faster in both training and rendering. Against the concurrent GS³ [2] (3DGS-based, SIGGRAPH Asia 2024), RNG achieves higher average PSNR (31.84 vs. 30.54) and lower LPIPS (0.0392 vs. 0.0478), with the gap most pronounced on soft-boundary scenes like WhiteFur (+0.87 PSNR) and HairBlonde (+1.88 PSNR), while GS³ edges ahead on a few hard-surface scenes (CatSmall, Cluttered, Hotdog, Lego, Pikachu). Under environment lighting (Table 2), RNG substantially outperforms both GS-IR [18] (PSNR 30.70 vs. 25.27) and Relightable 3D Gaussian [9] (PSNR 30.70 vs. 24.04), with the largest margin on the Armadillo scene (37.06 vs. 30.42 and 24.47), attributed to the point-lit capture setup and the flexible neural appearance model that handles complex light transport without analytic BRDF assumptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

RNG builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) as its geometric representation and on NRHints (Zeng et al., 2023) for the capture protocol (moving point light) and the conceptual use of shadow/highlight hints to disambiguate light-material decomposition. The key departure from both prior 3DGS-based relighting methods (GS-IR, Relightable 3D Gaussian) and from NRHints is the replacement of analytical shading models and SDF/surface-normal priors with per-Gaussian latent vectors decoded by an MLP conditioned on view and light directions, removing the assumption of a well-defined surface. The concurrent GS3 work shares the same 3DGS + point-light setup but supplements neural networks with analytical appearance approximations, whereas RNG relies entirely on the learned latent space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RNG demonstrates that 3DGS-based relighting can match or exceed NeRF-based quality (NRHints) while being over 20× faster in training and rendering (1.3 h, 60 fps), making relightable soft-boundary assets practical for interactive applications. The shadow cue with depth refinement and the two-stage forward-deferred optimization are useful engineering contributions that address the known weakness of 3DGS geometry for shadow casting. However, the method remains limited by rasterization (no ray marching), which precludes accurate handling of specular reflections and refraction, and shadow quality is still bounded by the inherently noisy depth of Gaussian point clouds. Open directions include integrating reflection/refraction, relaxing the point-light capture requirement, and developing a more principled depth definition within the splatting framework.
