## Problem

Existing methods for anticipating human-object interactions operate in 2D video space, predicting heatmaps or action labels without grounding predictions in the persistent 3D layout of the environment. This limits them to short-horizon, single-action predictions and ignores the spatial reasoning needed to determine *where* a person will next interact and *how* their body will be posed at that location. FICTION introduces a 4D formulation that jointly predicts future interaction locations and body pose distributions over a multi-minute horizon.

## Input / Output

The model consumes an egocentric video clip (30 s observation window), the corresponding SMPL pose sequence, and a 3D scene discretized into a 16×16×16 voxel grid where each occupied voxel carries an object-taxonomy index or a reserved actor-location token. It outputs (1) a binary N×N×N voxel grid marking all future interaction locations over the next 3 minutes (with a 5 s anticipation buffer), and (2) for each queried interaction location, a learned distribution over SMPL pose parameters (θ, t) from which multiple plausible body poses can be sampled. All modalities are processed at 1 feature per second, and the prediction horizon spans minutes rather than the few seconds typical of prior pose-anticipation work.

## Method

FICTION encodes three modalities—video features from a pretrained EgoVLPv2 encoder, SMPL pose parameters, and the voxelized 3D scene—into a shared modality-agnostic representation via linear mappers, then fuses them with a self-attention transformer to produce a single observation embedding. A linear decoder maps this embedding to an N³ vector (reshaped to a voxel grid) for interaction-location prediction, trained with binary cross-entropy. For pose prediction, a CVAE conditioned on the observation embedding and a query-location voxel embedding learns a distribution over SMPL parameters, trained with a combination of parameter-space MSE, 3D joint L1 error, and KL divergence. The key architectural choice is early-fusion of explicit 3D scene context (object bounding boxes in a shared coordinate frame) alongside video and pose, rather than inferring spatial layout implicitly or adding it as a post-hoc correction, which the ablations show is critical for spatial accuracy.

## Results

On Ego-Exo4D, FICTION achieves PR-AUC of 21.0 (Cooking), 18.7 (Bike Repair), and 12.7 (Health) for future interaction location prediction, with Chamfer distances of 7.4, 7.2, and 41.7 voxels respectively. For future interaction pose prediction, FICTION achieves MPJPE of 229 mm (Cooking), 372 mm (Bike Repair), and 172 mm (Health), and PA-MPJPE of 56, 91, and 62 mm. These represent over 30% relative gains (4.6% absolute in PR-AUC) over the strongest baseline across all three scenarios and both tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FICTION outperforms all six baselines on both location and pose prediction across all three scenarios. For location prediction, the strongest baseline is OCT (autoregressive hotspot prediction) at 16.9/13.3/11.2 PR-AUC, while FICTION reaches 21.0/18.7/12.7 — a 24–32% relative improvement. Video-to-3D occupancy models (VoxFormer, OccFormer) perform comparably to each other but fall 4–6 PR-AUC points behind FICTION, confirming that implicit 3D learning from video alone is insufficient. For pose prediction, the best baseline is the Video-to-pose CVAE at 267/402/226 MPJPE, while FICTION achieves 229/372/172 mm — up to 54 mm lower error. Autoregressive pose generators (4D-Humans, T2M-GPT) diverge significantly over the 3-minute horizon, with T2M-GPT-FT (fine-tuned) closing the gap only marginally. Ablations confirm that removing the 3D environment context causes the largest performance drop (e.g., PR-AUC drops from 21.0 to 9.9 in Cooking), underscoring the critical role of explicit spatial conditioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two established lines of work: (1) 2D action/hotspot anticipation methods (HierVL, OCT, Anticipative Video Transformer) that predict next actions or interaction heatmaps without persistent 3D spatial grounding, and (2) 3D human pose prediction/generation methods (4D-Humans, T2M-GPT, scene-aware motion prediction) that model body motion but are not interaction-centric. The architectural core builds on the CVAE framework (Sohn et al., 2015) for the pose distribution head, while the multimodal transformer encoder fuses video features (via EgoVLPv2), SMPL pose sequences, and voxelized 3D scene context in an early-fusion design. The key conceptual departure is unifying 'where' (3D interaction location) and 'how' (body pose at interaction) into a single 4D prediction task, rather than treating them as separate problems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to define and benchmark 4D future interaction prediction, providing both a task formulation and a curated dataset (87K training episodes from Ego-Exo4D) that the authors explicitly release for community use. The clear ablation showing that 3D environment context is the dominant signal (removing it drops PR-AUC from 21 to 9.9 in cooking) validates the paper's central thesis that explicit spatial grounding is essential for long-horizon interaction anticipation. Limitations include reliance on Ego-Exo4D's high-quality SLAM cameras, a fixed 3-minute horizon, only three activity scenarios, and an object vocabulary constrained to LVIS. Open questions the authors flag include extending to dynamic objects and developing a streaming variant that revises predictions over time.
