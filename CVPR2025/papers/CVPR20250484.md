## Problem

While Video Large Language Models (Vid-LLMs) have advanced in general video understanding, their ability to comprehend video editing components—shot attributes, cuts, transitions—remains unexplored. Existing VEU benchmarks focus almost exclusively on classification of editing elements, leaving reasoning about dynamic editing patterns and judging the functional purpose of editing choices entirely unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250484/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes short video clips (1–60 seconds, majority 1–12 seconds) paired with text questions and produces outputs at three levels: multiple-choice class labels for recognition, open-ended text explanations with visual evidence for reasoning, and evaluative text assessing the storytelling function of editing choices for judging. Evaluation of open-ended responses uses a custom 0–5 scoring system that combines pattern-matching against an editing ontology with information-matching against ground-truth answers, rather than simple LLM-as-judge scoring.

## Method

VEU-Bench organizes 19 fine-grained tasks across 10 editing dimensions (shot size, angle, location, subject, type, color, motion, speed, cut, transition) and 3 cognitive levels (recognition, reasoning, judging), totaling ~50K QA samples. A key technical contribution is an ontology-based annotation pipeline that leverages a domain knowledge base of abstract editing features and functions; an MLLM selects the relevant abstract pattern and rewrites it with video-specific content (e.g., 'a match cut connects a similarly shaped bone and spaceship across frames'), converting open-ended annotation into a constrained rewriting task. For the expert model Oscars, the authors fine-tune Qwen2-VL-7B with LoRA (r=16, α=32) on the VEU-50K training set at 1 fps with max 64 frames. The evaluation metric introduces pattern-matching regularization: a predicted answer is scored on both alignment with the video-agnostic editing pattern ontology (PM) and alignment with the specific ground-truth description (IM), reducing the LLM evaluator's bias toward rewarding superficially correct but pattern-misidentified responses.

## Results

On VEU-Bench, Oscars-7B achieves a Score_all of 2.54 (out of 5), surpassing the best open-source model LLaVA-OneVision-7B (1.75) by 28.3% and Gemini-1.5-Pro (2.44) by 4.0%, while approaching GPT-4o (2.64). On the recognition sub-task (Score_mc), Oscars reaches 2.85 vs. GPT-4o's 2.93 and Gemini-1.5-Pro's 2.71. On the open-ended reasoning/judging sub-task (Score_oe), Oscars scores 2.23, a 89.3% improvement over its base model Qwen2-VL-7B (1.31). On general video benchmarks, fine-tuning on VEU-50K yields average gains of 5.1% (Video-MME), 4.8% (MVBench), and 6.6% (TempCompass) over Qwen2-VL-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Oscars-7B outperforms all 10 open-source Vid-LLM baselines on VEU-Bench Score_all, with the largest margin over LLaVA-OneVision-7B (+28.3%, 2.54 vs. 1.75) and LLaVA-Video-7B (+52.4%, 2.54 vs. 1.68). Against the proprietary Gemini-1.5-Pro, Oscars leads by 4.0% on Score_all (2.54 vs. 2.44) and by 34.6% on Score_oe (2.23 vs. 2.11), but trails GPT-4o by 3.8% on Score_all (2.54 vs. 2.64) and 3.7% on Score_mc (2.85 vs. 2.93). Compared to its own base model Qwen2-VL-7B, fine-tuning on VEU-50K yields a 22.3% gain in Score_mc (2.85 vs. 2.33) and an 89.3% gain in Score_oe (2.23 vs. 1.31). On general video benchmarks, Oscars improves over Qwen2-VL-7B on all tested dimensions, with the largest gains on TempCompass Ordering (+8.5%) and Video-MME Attribute Perception (+7.3%). Notably, several baselines (e.g., VideoLLaMA2 on Shot Speed at 33.3%, Cut Type at 17.6%) perform at or below random-chance level, highlighting the difficulty of VEU tasks for current models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of video editing understanding benchmarks (AVE, MovieCuts, AutoTransition, MovieNet) that have focused almost exclusively on element classification, by introducing reasoning and judging task levels that require open-ended explanation and functional interpretation of editing choices. Methodologically, it builds on Qwen2-VL-7B as the base architecture for the proposed Oscars model, applying LoRA fine-tuning on the curated VEU-50K dataset. The ontology-based annotation pipeline draws on knowledge-base-driven approaches to automate the generation of video-specific reasoning and judging annotations, departing from purely manual or direct MLLM annotation strategies that the authors show to be unreliable for this domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VEU-Bench is the first comprehensive benchmark to systematically evaluate Vid-LLMs across 10 editing dimensions and 3 cognitive levels, revealing that even top commercial models (GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5-Pro) score below 3/5 on reasoning and judging tasks, with some open-source models performing at or below random chance on shot speed and motion. The finding that fine-tuning on VEU data yields an 8.3% average improvement on general video reasoning benchmarks suggests editing understanding serves as a proxy for abstract temporal reasoning, opening a path for using domain-specific data to boost general capabilities. Open questions include whether the ontology-based scoring system generalizes beyond the curated knowledge base, whether the 1-fps sampling adequately captures dynamic editing elements, and whether the benchmark's 10 dimensions truly cover the full scope of professional editing practice.
