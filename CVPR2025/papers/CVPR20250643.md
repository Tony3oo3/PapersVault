## Problem

DETR-based detectors suffer from slow convergence due to sparse one-to-one supervision, and prior auxiliary one-to-many training methods (DAC-DETR, MS-DETR) impose rigid architectural constraints without rigorously investigating which decoder components must be independent versus shared across the two tasks. This paper asks: in a multi-task framework performing one-to-one and one-to-many prediction simultaneously, which transformer decoder components can be shared, and how can auxiliary routes be designed to minimize new parameters while preserving inference cost?

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images and produces bounding boxes with class labels in the same format as vanilla DETR. During training, the model simultaneously generates one-to-one predictions (primary route) and one-to-many predictions (two auxiliary routes), but the auxiliary routes are entirely discarded at inference, so the output structure and inference cost are identical to the baseline model. The one-to-many assignment uses a matching score combining classification confidence (α=0.3) and IoU, with up to K=6 positive candidates per ground-truth box filtered by an IoU threshold τ=0.4.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-route training mechanism: a primary route (Route-2) for one-to-one prediction identical to the baseline, an auxiliary route (Route-1) that shares self-attention and cross-attention but uses an independent FFN for one-to-many prediction, and a second auxiliary route (Route-3) that shares all decoder parameters with the primary route but introduces instructive self-attention. The instructive self-attention concatenates m=10 learnable instruction tokens to the object query sequence, performs self-attention on the combined n+m length sequence, then discards the instruction token outputs, allowing the tokens to dynamically guide shared queries toward one-to-many predictions without any additional parameters in the attention or FFN layers. The key empirical finding motivating this design is that any single independent component (SA, CA, or FFN) in the decoder is sufficient to resolve the task conflict between one-to-one and one-to-many objectives, making multi-component independence unnecessary. All auxiliary routes are removed at inference, ensuring zero impact on model architecture or inference time.

## Results

On COCO 2017 validation with ResNet-50, Mr. DETR achieves consistent mAP gains across all tested baselines: +3.1 mAP over Deformable-DETR++ (50.7 vs 47.6, 900 queries, 12 epochs), +1.9 mAP over DINO (50.9 vs 49.0, 900 queries, 12 epochs), and +1.0 mAP over Align-DETR (52.3 vs 51.3, 900 queries, 24 epochs). With the Swin-L backbone, Mr. DETR reaches 58.4 mAP in 12 epochs, surpassing the strongest prior method (EASE-DINO at 57.8) by 0.6 mAP. The auxiliary routes add no inference cost since they are discarded at test time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mr. DETR outperforms all 14 compared baselines on COCO 2017 validation. Against the most directly comparable one-to-many auxiliary training methods, it beats DAC-DETR by 1.4 mAP (50.7 vs 49.3, 900 queries, 12 epochs) and MS-DETR by 0.7 mAP (50.7 vs 50.0) at 12 epochs, and by 0.9 mAP (51.4 vs 50.5) and 0.5 mAP (51.4 vs 50.9) respectively at 24 epochs. Over the DINO baseline, gains are +1.9 mAP (12 epochs) and +1.3 mAP (24 epochs), while over Align-DETR the improvements are +1.2 and +1.0 mAP. With the stronger Swin-L backbone (Table 3), Mr. DETR achieves 58.4 mAP in 12 epochs, exceeding EASE-DINO (57.8), Relation-DINO (57.8), Stable-DINO (57.7), and Rank-DINO (57.6) by 0.6–0.8 mAP. The method also extends to instance segmentation, improving mask mAP by +3.6 (12 epochs) and +2.5 (24 epochs) over the Deformable-DETR++ baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of DETR auxiliary training methods (H-DETR, DN-DETR, DINO, Group-DETR) that address slow convergence by adding one-to-many supervision, and builds directly on DAC-DETR and MS-DETR, which first separated self-attention and cross-attention for different assignment strategies. The key departure is reframing the problem as a multi-task framework and systematically investigating which decoder components must be independent to resolve task conflict, rather than assuming a specific architectural split. The instructive self-attention mechanism draws on the prompt-tuning literature (learnable tokens appended to input sequences) but repurposes it to dynamically guide shared queries toward a different prediction objective without duplicating parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical finding that any single independent component (self-attention, cross-attention, or FFN) suffices to resolve one-to-one/one-to-many task conflict provides a clear design principle for future multi-task DETR variants, and the plug-and-play nature of the multi-route mechanism (auxiliary routes discarded at inference) makes it broadly applicable across DETR architectures. However, the improvements are modest (1–3 mAP) and validated only on COCO 2017 with ResNet-50 and Swin-L backbones, leaving open whether the gains persist on larger datasets, higher-resolution settings, or non-DETR architectures. The instruction token approach also raises questions about whether the specific number and placement of tokens generalize, and whether the mechanism could be extended to other multi-task detection scenarios beyond one-to-one/one-to-many.
