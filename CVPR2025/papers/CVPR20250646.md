## Problem

Existing visual place recognition methods, whether relying on global descriptors or aggregated local features, struggle in cluttered indoor environments densely populated with man-made objects because they overlook object-oriented information. This paper addresses the question of which levels of object attributes are truly essential for room reidentification and demonstrates that all four levels—global context, object patches, object segmentation, and keypoints—play distinct and complementary roles.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250646/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single query RGB image (640×480) and a reference database containing exactly one representative image per room, selected via CLIP embeddings and K-means clustering. The output is the top-1 matched reference room image, identified through a three-stage coarse-to-fine retrieval that progressively narrows candidates from the full database to 5, then to 2, then to 1. The entire pipeline is training-free, relying solely on pre-trained models without any dataset-specific fine-tuning.

## Method

AirRoom is a three-stage coarse-to-fine pipeline: (1) a Global stage uses a pre-trained backbone (e.g., DINOv2, ResNet, ViT) to extract global context features and retrieves top-5 candidates via cosine similarity; (2) a Local stage applies instance segmentation (Mask R-CNN or Semantic-SAM), then a novel Receptive Field Expander uses Delaunay triangulation over object centers to expand single-object bounding boxes into multi-object patches, followed by an Object Feature Extractor and a novel Object-Aware Scoring module that sums global, patch, and object scores computed via mutual nearest neighbor matching to narrow to top-2; (3) a Fine-Grained stage uses LightGlue keypoint matching to select the final top-1. The key insight is that each level of object-oriented information captures a different granularity of discriminative signal, and their integration provides robustness to viewpoint variation without requiring any training.

## Results

On the four newly constructed room ReID datasets, AirRoom achieves 93.96% accuracy on MPReID, 93.80% on HMReID, 91.68% on GibsonReID, and 87.18% on ReplicaReID, outperforming the strongest baseline (AnyLoc) by 1.6–5.8 percentage points on three of four datasets. F1 scores follow the same pattern, reaching 93.91 (MPReID), 93.62 (HMReID), 91.63 (GibsonReID), and 87.24 (ReplicaReID). The paper reports improvements ranging from 6% to 80% over SOTA baselines depending on the dataset and metric, with the largest gains over global-descriptor methods like DINOv2 (e.g., +34.6 pp accuracy on MPReID).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AirRoom decisively outperforms all image-retrieval and global-descriptor baselines: CVNet (17.45% acc on MPReID) and DINOv2 (59.36%) are far behind, while Patch-NetVLAD (64.32%) and NetVLAD (82.22%) also trail by 10–30 pp. Against the strongest VPR baseline AnyLoc, AirRoom wins on MPReID (93.96 vs 92.34), HMReID (93.80 vs 89.69), and GibsonReID (91.68 vs 85.85), but narrowly loses on ReplicaReID (87.18 vs 88.57), the smallest dataset with only 19 rooms. In the group-wise comparison (Table 2), AirRoom's object-aware mechanism consistently lifts each backbone above its raw or standard-enhanced counterpart: AirRoom-ResNet50 (86.16%) beats both ResNet50 alone (76.14%) and CVNet (17.45%), and AirRoom-NetVLAD (89.38%) surpasses NetVLAD (82.22%) and all three Patch-NetVLAD variants (64–67%). The pipeline also improves over ViT (81.90 → 89.70%) and DINO (80.66 → 88.00%) when used as the Global Feature Extractor, confirming that the object-aware refinement is the primary source of gain rather than the backbone choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out room reidentification as a distinct sub-problem of visual place recognition, explicitly differentiating it from both outdoor VPR (NetVLAD, Patch-NetVLAD, AnyLoc) and room categorization (RoomNet). Rather than building on a single prior architecture, it repurposes a suite of pretrained, off-the-shelf components (DINOv2/ResNet/ViT for global features, Mask R-CNN/Semantic-SAM for segmentation, LightGlue for keypoint matching) into a novel coarse-to-fine pipeline. The two genuinely new modules—the Receptive Field Expander (using Delaunay triangulation to expand single-object bounding boxes into multi-object patches) and the Object-Aware Scoring (mutual nearest neighbors over patch and object features)—represent the conceptual departure from prior VPR methods that rely on global descriptors or aggregated local features without explicit object-level reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The four newly constructed datasets (MPReID, HMReID, GibsonReID, ReplicaReID) fill a clear gap, as no existing indoor dataset satisfied the requirements for room-level reidentification with adequate viewpoint variation. The training-free, modular design makes the approach practically deployable without large-scale indoor training data, which is a significant advantage given the difficulty of collecting diverse indoor imagery. However, the method's inability to handle object rearrangements (movable furniture, items moved between rooms) is a meaningful limitation for real-world home environments, and the non-uniform performance (AirRoom actually underperforms AnyLoc on ReplicaReID) suggests the approach's effectiveness is dataset-dependent. Future work on dynamic scene understanding and object-rearrangement-robust matching would be needed to close this gap.
