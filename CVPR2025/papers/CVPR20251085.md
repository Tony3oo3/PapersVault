## Problem

Category-agnostic object pose and shape estimation from a single RGB-D image remains challenging because existing methods either require category-level shape priors, CAD models, or an object onboarding stage, limiting scalability to unseen objects. Additionally, even well-trained models suffer from domain gaps between training and deployment, rendering estimates unreliable or hazardous in safety-critical settings without a mechanism for test-time adaptation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251085/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a cropped RGB image of a segmented object and its corresponding per-pixel depth point cloud (n points in R^3), with no category label or CAD model required at inference. It outputs a 6D pose (R ∈ SO(3), t ∈ R^3) and a signed distance field f(·|h) defined in a pose-normalized canonical frame centered at the origin. The intermediate pose-normalized coordinates (PNC) are deliberately not scale-normalized (unlike NOCS), which the authors show is critical to avoid degeneracies during self-training. Inference runs at 8 Hz on a single GPU.

## Method

CRISP uses a frozen DINOv2 ViT backbone feeding two heads: an MLP encoder producing a latent shape code h that FiLM-conditions a sinusoidal-activation MLP decoder to output the SDF, and a DPT head that directly regresses per-point pose-normalized coordinates Z, from which pose is recovered via Arun's least-squares method. The key correction mechanism reformulates pose and shape estimation as a bi-level optimization over Z and h, solved by block coordinate descent; an active shape decoder approximates the trained SDF decoder as a non-negative linear combination of K training basis shapes plus the network's own estimate, reducing the shape sub-problem to a constrained linear least squares solvable in milliseconds by an interior-point method. For test-time adaptation, CRISP-ST applies a correct-and-certify loop: the corrector refines estimates, an observable correctness certificate (p-th quantile of |SDF| values below a threshold) filters reliable pseudo-labels, and the encoder and DPT are fine-tuned on these labels while the SDF decoder remains frozen.

## Results

On YCBV, CRISP-Real achieves the best pose estimation among methods without object onboarding, with ADD-S mean of 0.011 and AUC@2cm of 0.62, outperforming CosyPose (0.010 mean, 0.56 AUC@2cm). For shape reconstruction, CRISP-Real achieves a mean Chamfer distance of 0.026, outperforming Shap-E (0.099) by 73%. On NOCS REAL275, CRISP achieves the best mAP at IoU50 (83.5) and IoU75 (70.5) among all methods, surpassing the best category-agnostic baseline FSD (77.4, 61.9). Self-training (CRISP-ST) bridges the sim-to-real gap, improving CRISP-Syn's ADD-S mean by 20% and AUC@2cm by 44%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On YCBV pose estimation, CRISP-Real (no onboarding) achieves ADD-S mean 0.011, slightly trailing CosyPose (0.010) but surpassing it on AUC@2cm (0.62 vs 0.56) and AUC@3cm (0.73 vs 0.68); it is outperformed only by FoundationPose (0.006) which requires object onboarding. On YCBV shape reconstruction, CRISP-Real (e_shape 0.026) dramatically outperforms Shap-E (0.099) by 73%, and self-training further improves the synthetic-trained model by 17% (LSQ) over the uncorrected baseline. On NOCS REAL275, CRISP achieves the best mAP at IoU50 (83.5) and IoU75 (70.5), beating the best category-agnostic method FSD (77.4, 61.9) and all category-level methods except RePoNet on IoU50 (81.1); however, CRISP's rotation error at 5°5cm (22.5) is lower than RePoNet (40.4) and SGPA (39.6). On SPE3R, CRISP-ST outperforms SQRECON on all metrics, improving mean e_pose by 23% (0.224 vs 0.304) and e_shape by 13% (0.141 vs 0.145), while Shap-E only reports shape metrics (0.183) without pose. The self-training pipeline is the key differentiator: without it, CRISP-Syn trails all real-data baselines, but with LSQ correction it recovers most of the gap (ADD-S AUC@2cm: 0.44 vs 0.62 for CRISP-Real).

## Relation to Prior Work

CRISP builds on the NOCS framework [47] for pose-normalized coordinates but deliberately omits the normalization step to avoid scale degeneracies during self-training. The self-training pipeline adapts the correct-and-certify paradigm from Talak et al. [43] and Shi et al. [40], extending it from pose-only estimation with known CAD models to simultaneous category-agnostic pose and shape estimation. The key methodological departure is the active shape decoder approximation, which exploits the observation that the FiLM-conditioned SDF decoder is well-behaved only within the convex hull of training shape codes, thereby reducing the non-convex shape correction subproblem to a constrained linear least squares problem solvable by interior-point methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to bridge a large sim-to-real domain gap at test time without access to synthetic data or ground-truth CAD models is practically important for real-world robotic deployment, where distribution shifts are the norm. The 8 Hz inference speed and category-agnostic design make CRISP a viable component in real-time perception pipelines for manipulation and object-SLAM. However, the method currently assumes ground-truth data association across views, and the latent shape code projection scales linearly with the number of training objects, limiting scalability to large object libraries. Whether CRISP can serve as a true foundation model for pose and shape estimation with test-time adaptation remains an open question.
