## Problem

Dual-arm robotic manipulation lacks scalable, diverse training data and real-world-aligned evaluation benchmarks. Existing data collection relies on expensive human teleoperation or task-specific algorithmic generators that cannot generalize beyond fixed scenarios, and most benchmarks target single-arm or separated bimanual setups rather than integrated dual-arm coordination.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250989/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a single 2D RGB photograph of a real-world object plus a natural-language task description as its minimal inputs, and produces diverse textured 3D meshes, spatially-annotated digital twins, and executable expert demonstration trajectories (joint angles and end-effector poses). At evaluation time, the robot receives synchronized RGBD streams from four Intel RealSense D-435 cameras at 30 Hz (one overhead, two wrist-mounted, one optional low-angle), along with depth-derived point clouds, while the policy outputs dual-arm joint trajectories. Spatial annotations (function/contact points and function/approach/lateral axes) are transferred across generated 3D variants of the same object class via Stable Diffusion encoder feature matching, eliminating per-model re-annotation.

## Method

RoboTwin is a generative digital twin framework with three coupled stages. First, a 3D asset generation pipeline uses GPT-4V to produce object descriptions, SDXL Turbo to synthesize diverse 2D appearances, and the Rodin image-conditioned 3D model to render textured meshes with geometry, normals, and wireframes, validated by UCLIP-I similarity and GPT-4V visual checks. Second, a spatial annotation framework marks functional/contact key points and three principal axes on each asset, then propagates these annotations across all generated variants of the same class using diffusion-feature point matching. Third, an LLM-driven code generation module decomposes the task into subtasks, infers per-subtask spatial constraints (e.g., aligning a hammer's functional point with a nail surface along its approach axis), and emits executable Python code that calls MPlib trajectory-optimization APIs to solve a constrained screw-motion optimization (kinematic, position/orientation alignment, and collision-avoidance constraints), with an iterative self-correction loop that feeds runtime errors back to the LLM. The resulting benchmark comprises 15 dual-arm tasks on the COBOT Magic platform under ManiSkill3, providing 100 simulated and 20 real-world expert demonstrations per task for standardized policy evaluation.

## Results

On the RoboTwin benchmark (14 dual-arm tasks, D435 cameras), DP3 (XYZ) achieves the best few-shot performance with only 20 demonstrations (e.g., 89.0% on Block Handover, 64.7% on Bottle Adjust), but shows limited scalability with 100 demos. In contrast, 2D Diffusion Policy (DP) starts near 0% at 20 demos but scales dramatically, reaching 85.7% on Dual Bottles Pick (Easy) and 33.0% on Shoe Place at 100 demos. In real-world transfer, pre-training on 300 RoboTwin-generated simulation samples plus fine-tuning on 20 real samples yields 72% average success on single-arm tasks (vs. 1.2% with 20 real samples alone) and 62% on dual-arm tasks (vs. 20%), confirming the value of the generative digital twin data pipeline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks two imitation learning baselines—3D Diffusion Policy (DP3, Ze et al. 2024) and 2D Diffusion Policy (DP, Chi et al. 2023)—across 14 RoboTwin dual-arm tasks at 20, 50, and 100 demonstrations. DP3 (XYZ) dominates in the few-shot regime, achieving 89.0% on Block Handover and 64.7% on Bottle Adjust with only 20 demos, whereas DP scores 0.0% on both. However, DP exhibits far stronger data scalability: at 100 demos it reaches 85.7% on Dual Bottles Pick (Easy) versus DP3's 55.3%, and 76.0% on Block Handover versus DP3's 77.3% (a near-tie). Adding RGB to DP3's point-cloud input (DP3 XYZ+RGB) yields inconsistent gains—large improvements in cluttered scenes (Pick Apple Messy: 54.0% vs. 9.7%) but degradation in others (Container Place: 62.3% vs. 85.3%). Both methods struggle severely on complex dual-arm coordination tasks (Dual Shoes Place stays below 12% across all settings), highlighting a clear gap in current imitation learning for bimanual coordination. In the real-world transfer experiments, the RoboTwin-generated simulation data (300 sim + 20 real) outperforms the 20-real-only baseline by 70.8 percentage points on single-arm tasks (72% vs. 1.2%) and 42 points on dual-arm tasks (62% vs. 20%), validating the generative digital twin pipeline's practical utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoboTwin sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) 3D generative foundation models (Rodin, SDXL Turbo) for asset creation, (2) LLM-based robotic code generation (Code as Policies, RoboCodeX, ReKep), and (3) dual-arm manipulation benchmarks (Peract2, HumanoidBench, BiGym). It departs from prior data-generation systems like MimicGen and RoboCaca by replacing fixed 3D assets and task-specific trajectory design with generative 3D models and LLM-driven spatial constraint reasoning, enabling diverse object appearances and novel scenario configurations without manual redesign. The spatial annotation framework (functional/contact points, three principal axes) and feature-point-matching transfer via Stable Diffusion encoders extend the ReKep-style keypoint approach to handle functional axis constraints and cross-instance annotation reuse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark addresses a genuine gap: no prior standardized dual-arm benchmark combines generative object diversity, LLM-based expert data generation, and matched sim/real hardware under a single evaluation protocol. The 70%/40% sim-to-real improvements validate the pipeline's practical utility, but the sub-15% success on true coordination tasks (e.g., Dual Shoes Place) exposes that current imitation learning methods still lack robust dual-arm coordination. Open questions include better RGB–point-cloud fusion (the inconsistent benefit of adding RGB to DP3 is a notable negative result), more effective coordination algorithms, and whether the generative-asset approach scales to open-vocabulary object categories beyond the 15 curated tasks.
