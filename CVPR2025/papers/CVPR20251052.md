## Problem

Federated Learning suffers from high computational costs as client datasets grow, and existing coreset selection methods are ill-suited for FL: gradient-matching approaches require a server-side dataset (privacy risk), while hard-sample-based methods (Forgetting, GradND, EL2N) exacerbate data distribution imbalance among clients, degrading global aggregation performance especially under high pruning rates. No prior criterion can maintain strong performance at high pruning ratios in non-IID FL settings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained network (after T_P communication rounds of FedAvg) and the full labeled image dataset on each client. It produces a pruned coreset per client where only the penultimate-layer hidden representations are communicated between clients and server (as geometric-mean class centers), never raw images or logits. The pruning is structured as a two-stage process: a high-ratio pass (p_i^f) on 'large-capacity' classes (those exceeding a β-fraction of the client's largest class) followed by a low-ratio pass (p_i^l) on all remaining samples, yielding a single final pruning rate p_i per client.

## Method

FedCS scores each sample using Distance Contrast (DC): the absolute difference between its Euclidean distance to its own global class center and to the nearest other global class center in the penultimate-layer representation space. Low DC indicates decision-boundary samples (informative, representative of class structure), while high DC flags either redundant samples near the class center or rare extreme outliers. Global class centers are obtained by median-aggregating per-client geometric means across all N clients, which eliminates bias from rare extreme data and allows clients to access information about classes they may lack locally. A double-pruning strategy then removes high-DC samples in two stages—first at a high ratio from large-capacity classes, then at a low ratio from the remainder—to prevent the pruning itself from worsening inter-client distribution heterogeneity. A convergence analysis (Theorem 4.1) shows that reducing the degree of non-IID (Γ) via this mechanism accelerates FedAvg convergence at rate O(Γ/T) and lowers the final optimality gap.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18 under severe non-IID (α=0.1), FedCS achieves 58.88% top-1 accuracy at 57% total pruning rate (p_i^f=0.7, p_i^l=0.1), surpassing the full-dataset FedAvg baseline (55.63%) by 3.25% and the best baseline (GM at 57.53%) by 1.35%. On CIFAR-100 with ViT-B-16, FedCS reaches 77.06% at 41% pruning, matching the full-dataset accuracy of 77.08%. On TinyImageNet with ResNet-50, FedCS achieves 58.67% at 41% pruning, exceeding the full-dataset baseline (57.61%) by 1.06%. Across all datasets and pruning rates, FedCS consistently outperforms all baselines, with the largest margins under high pruning rates and high data heterogeneity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedCS outperforms all five baselines across every dataset, pruning rate, and heterogeneity level tested. On CIFAR-10 (α=0.1, p_i^f=0.7), FedCS (58.88%) beats EL2N (54.02%) by 4.86%, GradND (53.24%) by 5.64%, Forgetting (52.27%) by 6.61%, Moderate (53.37%) by 5.51%, and GM (57.53%) by 1.35%. The advantage grows with pruning rate: at p_i^f=0.9, FedCS leads EL2N by 5.82% and Forgetting by 5.01%, while the gap over GM narrows to 0.94%. On CIFAR-100, FedCS (77.06% at p_i^f=0.3) exceeds GM (76.59%) by 0.47% and EL2N (76.46%) by 0.60%, and at p_i^f=0.9 it leads Forgetting (73.21%) by 2.04%. On TinyImageNet, FedCS (58.67% at p_i^f=0.3) surpasses GM (57.85%) by 0.82% and EL2N (56.99%) by 1.68%. Notably, FedCS also exceeds the full-dataset FedAvg baseline on CIFAR-10 (α=0.1) and TinyImageNet, demonstrating that pruning redundant and extreme samples actually improves aggregation under non-IID conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes representation-space geometry (hidden-layer features and class centers) from the coreset selection literature into a federated learning context, where prior coreset methods (EL2N, Forgetting, GradND) that select hard samples are shown to be counterproductive due to non-IID heterogeneity. It departs from the dominant hard-sample paradigm by proposing Distance Contrast (DC) as a scoring criterion that targets decision-boundary samples while removing both redundant and extreme outliers. The class center aggregation via cross-client median and the double pruning strategy are novel components not present in prior coreset or FL pruning work, and the convergence analysis extends the non-IID framework of Li et al. [21] and Cho et al. [6] to the pruned-data setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in FL—computational cost from large client datasets—without altering network structure or compromising privacy, making it broadly applicable. The key insight that standard hard-sample coreset selection worsens non-IID heterogeneity and thus degrades global model performance is a useful corrective for the field. Limitations include evaluation only on image classification with Dirichlet partitioning, up to 100 clients, and a fixed pre-training budget; open questions include whether DC generalizes to dense prediction tasks, continuous label spaces, or more extreme client heterogeneity (e.g., pathologically skewed label distributions).
