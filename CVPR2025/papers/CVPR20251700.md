## Problem

Generalized feed-forward Gaussian models struggle to capture high-frequency details due to their limited number of Gaussians, and the adaptive densification strategy from per-scene 3D Gaussian splatting cannot be directly transferred because it requires tens of thousands of optimization steps and overfits under sparse-view conditions. No efficient, generalizable densification mechanism existed that could generate fine Gaussians in a single forward pass while leveraging learned prior knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251700/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as input the Gaussians and their penultimate feature vectors produced by a feed-forward model (LaRa at 512×512 for objects, MVSplat at 256×256 for scenes), along with the original sparse multi-view images and camera poses used to compute view-space positional gradient scores. The output is a union of the non-selected coarse Gaussians and fine Gaussians generated across L densification layers, yielding a non-uniformly distributed set (e.g., ~78k for objects, ~173k for scenes) that can be rendered to arbitrary novel viewpoints via standard 3D Gaussian splatting rasterization.

## Method

Generative Densification selects the top-K Gaussians with the largest view-space positional gradient norms and passes their positions and features through L serialized layers, each consisting of serialized attention (group-wise self-attention over space-filling-curve-sorted Gaussians), up-sampling (predicting R positional offsets and R feature residuals per Gaussian via MLPs), learnable masking (predicting a per-Gaussian confidence score via a straight-through estimator to filter out Gaussians not needing further densification), and a Gaussian head that decodes the remaining Gaussians into full parameters. The key distinction from per-scene 3D-GS densification is that the module operates on learned feature representations rather than raw Gaussian parameters, embedding prior knowledge from large multi-view training data so that fine Gaussians are generated in a single forward pass without iterative optimization. The approach is integrated into both LaRa (object-level, voxel-based Gaussians) and MVSplat (scene-level, pixel-aligned Gaussians), with residual learning added for the scene-level variant to handle complex real-world geometry.

## Results

On object-level reconstruction, the proposed method (with residual learning) achieves 28.75 PSNR / 0.946 SSIM / 0.078 LPIPS on Gobjaverse, 31.23 PSNR on Google Scanned Objects, and 22.08 PSNR on Co3D, outperforming all baselines including GS-LRM (30.52 PSNR on GSO) with only 134M parameters vs. 300M. On scene-level reconstruction (RE10k), the method reaches 27.08 PSNR / 0.879 SSIM / 0.120 LPIPS, surpassing MVSplat (26.39 PSNR) and the concurrent DepthSplat (26.76 PSNR). Cross-dataset generalization to ACID and DTU also shows consistent gains (28.61 and 14.05 PSNR respectively) over MVSplat (28.15 and 13.94).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct object-level baseline LaRa [5], the proposed method improves PSNR by +1.26 dB on Gobjaverse (28.75 vs. 27.49), +1.53 dB on GSO (31.23 vs. 29.70), and +0.90 dB on Co3D (22.08 vs. 21.18), with only 9M additional parameters (134M vs. 125M). Against GS-LRM [47], the current state-of-the-art for object-level reconstruction, the method achieves higher PSNR on GSO (31.23 vs. 30.52) while using less than half the parameters (134M vs. 300M). Against LGM [34], the gains are substantial: +9.08 dB on Gobjaverse and +7.56 dB on GSO, demonstrating that selective densification far outperforms uniformly increasing Gaussian count. On scene-level reconstruction, the method beats MVSplat [7] by +0.69 dB PSNR (27.08 vs. 26.39) and the concurrent DepthSplat [40] by +0.32 dB (27.08 vs. 26.76) with fewer parameters (27.8M vs. 37M). Cross-dataset evaluation confirms consistent improvements: +0.46 dB on ACID and +0.11 dB on DTU over MVSplat, with no degradation in SSIM or LPIPS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the view-space positional gradient criterion from per-scene 3D Gaussian Splatting densification [18] to the feed-forward setting, but fundamentally departs from the iterative splitting/cloning paradigm by learning a generative module that produces fine Gaussians in a single forward pass. It builds on two representative feed-forward architectures—LaRa (voxel-based) and MVSplat (pixel-aligned)—and uses serialized attention with space-filling curve sorting [37] to efficiently process unstructured Gaussian point clouds. The key conceptual shift is densifying learned feature representations rather than raw Gaussian parameters, embedding prior knowledge from large multi-view datasets into the densification process itself, which is orthogonal to prior work on alternative densification criteria [1,13,26] or overfitting-aware pruning [11] that still operate in the per-scene optimization loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a core limitation of feed-forward Gaussian models—insufficient Gaussian count for high-frequency detail—without incurring the tens-of-thousands-of-steps optimization cost or the overfitting risk of per-scene densification in sparse-view settings. Its model-agnostic design (validated on both voxel-based and pixel-aligned architectures) and cross-dataset generalization results suggest it could serve as a drop-in module for future feed-forward 3D reconstruction systems. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 4D/dynamic scene reconstruction, whether the serialized attention bottleneck limits further increases in Gaussian count, and whether the confidence masking can be made more principled than the current top-k heuristic.
