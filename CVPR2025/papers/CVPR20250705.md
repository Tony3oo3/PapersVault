## Problem

Existing weakly-supervised temporal action localization (WTAL) methods suffer from two persistent failure modes: incomplete localization, where low-discriminability sub-actions are missed, and over-complete localization, where background segments are falsely activated as foreground. While Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) offer rich video understanding capabilities, their high memory and computational costs make them impractical for direct deployment in WTAL pipelines, leaving open the question of how to leverage MLLM semantic priors to improve traditional WTAL models without incurring inference-time overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250705/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes untrimmed video (fused RGB + optical flow features at 2048-dim per frame) along with video-level binary category labels, and additionally feeds the raw video into a frozen MLLM to extract key semantic descriptions and complete action descriptions. At inference, the MLLM is entirely removed; only the trained prior-guided localization head processes the video features, producing a set of action instances each specified by category, confidence score, start timestamp, and end timestamp. The framework is designed as a plug-and-play module that can be attached to existing WTAL backbones (e.g., DELU, DDG-Net, CO2-Net) without modifying their inference pipeline.

## Method

The core contribution is a dual-prior collaborative learning paradigm with two complementary modules. Key Semantic Matching (KSM) uses MLLM-generated key action descriptions as text queries, performs contrastive top-k MIL matching against video segment embeddings, and applies attention-weighted background suppression to activate the most discriminative temporal intervals. Complete Semantic Reconstruction (CSR) uses MLLM-generated detailed action descriptions, masks key action words, and trains a transformer encoder-decoder to reconstruct the masked words conditioned on video features, thereby encouraging the model to attend to the full temporal extent of each action. A Dual Prior Interactive Distillation (DPID) strategy then alternates optimization: KSM's attention map is distilled into CSR via MSE loss to curb over-completeness, and CSR's attention map is distilled into KSM to alleviate incompleteness, creating a mutual correction loop. A prior-guided localization head (adopted from PivoTAL) with focal, DIoU, and MIL losses produces the final action proposals, and the entire MLLM dependency is confined to the training phase.

## Results

On THUMOS14, MLLM4WTAL (Zhou et al.) achieves 49.8% average mAP (0.1:0.7), surpassing the prior best weakly-supervised method PivoTAL (49.7%) and the fully-supervised P-GCN (61.6% avg 0.1:0.5, no 0.1:0.7 reported). On ActivityNet-v1.2, MLLM4WTAL(DDG-Net) reaches 30.1% average mAP, outperforming the previous best weakly-supervised method Ju et al. (29.6%) by 0.5%. The framework provides consistent gains of 1.5–3.1% mAP when plugged into existing baselines (DELU, Zhou et al., DDG-Net) without any additional inference overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On THUMOS14, MLLM4WTAL (Zhou et al.) achieves 49.8% avg mAP (0.1:0.7), edging out PivoTAL (49.7%) and Ju et al. (49.4%), and surpassing the fully-supervised SSN (55.7% avg 0.1:0.5) and P-GCN (61.6% avg 0.1:0.5) in the 0.1:0.7 range. When applied to DELU, the framework yields a +1.5% mAP gain (46.4→47.9%), with the largest improvement at IoU=0.7 (+3.1 points). On ActivityNet-v1.2, MLLM4WTAL(DDG-Net) reaches 30.1% avg mAP, beating DDG-Net (27.0%) by 3.1% and the prior best Ju et al. (29.6%) by 0.5%. The generic validation (Table 4) confirms plug-and-play gains of +1.8–2.3% on UM and +1.9–2.2% on CO2-Net, demonstrating broad applicability across heterogeneous WTAL architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: weakly-supervised temporal action localization (WTAL) and multimodal large language models (MLLMs). It directly adopts the prior-guided localization head from PivoTAL [21] and borrows the random word-masking strategy from the two-stream consensus network [40] for its Complete Semantic Reconstruction module. Conceptually, it departs from prior WTAL methods (UntrimmedNet, CoLA, DELU, etc.) that rely solely on video-level labels and internal attention mechanisms, by introducing an external semantic prior source (MLLM) that operates exclusively during training. The dual-prior interactive distillation between KSM and CSR is a new mechanism not seen in prior WTAL or knowledge-distillation literature for this task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the plug-and-play design: MLLM is used only during training to generate semantic priors, so inference incurs no additional computational cost, making it deployable alongside existing WTAL pipelines. However, the performance gains are modest (1.0–1.8% mAP on THUMOS14, ~0.5–3% on ActivityNet depending on baseline), and the approach is validated on only two datasets with a limited set of base methods. Open questions include how sensitive the framework is to MLLM description quality (hallucinated or inaccurate action descriptions could propagate errors), whether the gains hold on longer or more diverse video corpora, and whether the dual-prior distillation generalizes to other weakly-supervised video tasks beyond TAL.
