## Problem

Top-down human pose estimation methods achieve high accuracy but suffer from poor efficiency, which the paper attributes to sample diversity: simple poses waste computation when processed by large models, and the abundance of redundant detection boxes (e.g., ~140K boxes for only 6K samples on COCO val) further inflates inference cost. No prior work has identified this diversity as the root cause or proposed a mechanism to match samples with appropriately-sized models.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a single cropped person image (256×192, extracted by Faster R-CNN with NMS) as input and produces 17 2D keypoint locations as output. The lightweight router consumes the same cropped image and outputs a scalar difficulty score s ∈ (0,1) that determines whether the sample is routed to the small or large network. The two pre-trained HPE networks are frozen and interchangeable, so the I/O contract remains unchanged regardless of which backbone pair is used.

## Method

DynPose introduces a lightweight router (average pooling + four convolutional layers using PConv and SGE attention + two FC layers, totaling only 0.19 GFLOPs) that predicts a per-sample estimation difficulty score. The router is trained with a supervised loss that regresses the score toward (D_s − D_l)/D_s, where D_s and D_l are Object Keypoint Distances (OKS without the exponential term) of the small and large networks, respectively, so the score captures how much the small network degrades relative to the large one. At inference, samples with scores between thresholds k₁ and k₂ are routed to the large network, while both 'Easy' (low score) and 'Extremely Hard' (high score, typically from redundant/incorrect boxes) samples are sent to the small network, since the accuracy gap is negligible in both extremes. Because the router is fully decoupled from the prediction networks, it generalizes to other model pairs and datasets (e.g., CrowdPose) without any fine-tuning, requiring only a small adjustment to the threshold hyperparameters.

## Results

On COCO val, DynPose with ResNet-50 + HRNet-W32 (50% split) achieves 74.8% AP at 270 FPS and 6.76G FLOPs, matching HRNet-W32's 74.9% AP while running ~21% faster. With ResNet-152 + ViTPose-L, DynPose reaches 78.0% AP at 93 FPS and 35.90G FLOPs, versus ViTPose-L's 78.2% AP at 59 FPS and 58.68G FLOPs — a ~58% speedup with negligible accuracy loss. Against the SOTA distillation method SDPose-S, Dyn-M2-H32 improves AP by 0.8% (74.3 vs 73.5) at comparable FLOPs (4.8G). On CrowdPose, the router transfers directly and achieves 67.3% AP at 258 FPS vs HRNet-W32's 67.5% AP at 223 FPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three Large Network baselines (HRNet-W32, Swin-L, ViTPose-L), DynPose matches or nearly matches AP (within 0.1–0.2%) while substantially increasing FPS: 270 vs 223 (HRNet-W32), 106 vs 66 (Swin-L), and 93 vs 59 (ViTPose-L), with FLOPs reduced by 12%, 39%, and 39% respectively. Against SOTA small-scale methods (TokenPose-B at 73.2% AP, PPT-B at 73.4% AP), Dyn-M2-H48 achieves 75.0% AP, a clear 1.6–1.8% AP advantage. Against distillation methods, Dyn-M2-H32 (74.3% AP, 4.8G FLOPs) outperforms SDPose-S (73.5% AP, 4.7G FLOPs) by 0.8% AP at nearly identical FLOPs, and Dyn-M2-H48 (75.0% AP) beats DistilPose-L (74.4% AP) by 0.6% AP. The generalization experiments (Table 3) confirm that the router trained on ResNet-50+HRNet-W32 transfers directly to ResNet-50+ViTPose-L, maintaining 78.0% AP at 98 FPS — still well above ViTPose-L's 59 FPS. On CrowdPose, the same router transfers without retraining, achieving 67.3% AP at 258 FPS vs HRNet-W32's 67.5% AP at 223 FPS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws on two distinct lineages: top-down HPE architectures (HRNet, ViTPose, ResNet) as the pre-trained backbones, and dynamic neural networks (Branchynet, BlockDrop, DynamicDet) for the routing mechanism. It departs from prior efficiency-focused HPE work—which targets model compression via lightweight backbones, sparse attention, or distillation—by reframing the bottleneck as sample diversity (pose complexity and bounding-box redundancy) rather than model complexity. The key architectural departure from prior dynamic networks is that the router is fully decoupled from the prediction networks, enabling zero-shot transfer across architectures and datasets without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, drop-in efficiency improvement (~50% speedup) for deployed top-down HPE systems, and the router's ability to generalize across model families (CNN→Transformer) and datasets (COCO→CrowdPose) without fine-tuning makes it broadly applicable. A notable open question is how the framework scales beyond two networks (e.g., a 3-tier or continuous routing scheme), and whether the 'Extremely Hard' samples—largely caused by redundant detector boxes—could be filtered upstream rather than routed to a small network. The approach also assumes a fixed detector; its interaction with different or learned detectors remains unexplored.
