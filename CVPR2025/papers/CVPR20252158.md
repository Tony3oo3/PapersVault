## Problem

MLLMs underperform on non-natural images (geometric figures, charts, function plots) because ViT-based vision encoders act as low-pass filters: self-attention progressively suppresses high-frequency components (edges, lines, points) as layers deepen, and this degradation persists even after fine-tuning on domain-specific data. The cost of collecting non-natural image datasets at natural-image scale is prohibitive, motivating an architectural fix rather than a data-scaling solution.

## Input / Output

The model accepts non-natural RGB images (geometric, chart, or function plots) at 336×336 resolution paired with a text prompt or question. The input image is simultaneously processed by FM-ViT and a CNN encoder, and its Discrete Wavelet Transform sub-band statistics (mean and variance per sub-band) condition the sample-wise frequency adaptation. Outputs span four task types: class labels (169 geometric / 5 function / 20 chart classes), free-form captions, retrieved image-text pairs, and short text answers to reasoning questions.

## Method

FM-ViT applies Fourier decomposition to each self-attention output, splitting it into a DC (low-frequency) component and a high-frequency component, then re-weights them with two learnable per-channel coefficients (w_d, w_h) tuned by gradient descent. A sample-wise adaptive mechanism computes these coefficients dynamically from DWT sub-band statistics of the input image via a fully connected layer followed by sigmoid, allowing the model to shift emphasis between frequency bands depending on whether the image is a bar chart (more low-frequency) or a function plot (more high-frequency). The full EDGE system follows a three-stage training pipeline: (1) contrastive fine-tuning of FM-ViT on non-natural image-caption pairs, (2) alignment via a two-layer MLP projector into a frozen LLM, and (3) instruction tuning with LoRA (rank 128) on ~310K Q&A pairs while freezing the visual encoder. The final model fuses FM-ViT features with CNN features to complement global relational reasoning with local high-frequency detail.

## Results

On the GeoQA benchmark, EDGE (Vicuna-1.5-7B) achieves 68.2% overall accuracy, surpassing G-LLaVA-7B (64.2%), MAVIS (66.7%), and EAGLE (67.1%). On FunctionQA (MathVista), the model reaches 43.5% accuracy, outperforming all open-source MLLMs (best prior: MAVIS at 40.3%) though trailing GPT-4V (69.5%). On ChartQA, EDGE achieves 72.5% accuracy, exceeding ChartLLaMA (69.6%) and Unichart (65.8%). At the vision encoder level, FM-ViT (ViT-L/14, 336px) attains 61.5% geometric classification and 88.5% image-to-text retrieval, outperforming both ConvNeXt-320 and standard ViT-L-14.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On GeoQA, EDGE (68.2%) outperforms all open-source MLLM baselines: G-LLaVA-7B (64.2%, +4.0%), MAVIS (66.7%, +1.5%), and EAGLE (67.1%, +1.1%), while also surpassing the 13B-parameter G-LLaVA (67.0%) with a 7B backbone. On FunctionQA, EDGE (43.5%) leads all open-source models—beating MAVIS (40.3%), SPHINX-MoE (34.6%), and LLaVA-NeXT (33.7%)—but remains well below GPT-4V (69.5%) and Multimodal Bard (45.5%). On ChartQA, EDGE (72.5%) surpasses ChartLLaMA (69.6%), Unichart (65.8%), Matcha (63.9%), and Qwen (61.6%), establishing a clear lead over both conventional and MLLM baselines. For captioning (Table 2), EDGE outperforms all open-source MLLMs on geometric, chart, and function captioning (e.g., 2.35/2.84 vs. EAGLE's 2.32/2.76 on geometric) and approaches GPT-4V on geometric correctness (2.35 vs. 2.33). At the vision encoder level (Table 1), FM-ViT beats ConvNeXt-320 (59.8% → 61.5% geometric classification) and standard ViT-L-14 (57.9% → 61.5%), confirming that frequency modulation provides gains beyond simply scaling model size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CLIP ViT-L/14 vision encoder and the LLaVA-1.5 MLLM architecture, but departs from prior non-natural image understanding work (G-LLaVA, MAVIS, EAGLE, ChartLLaMA) that primarily addressed the problem through data collection and standard fine-tuning. Instead, it identifies a root architectural cause—ViT self-attention progressively attenuates high-frequency components, acting as an implicit low-pass filter—and proposes a frequency-modulated attention mechanism (FM-ViT) with Fourier decomposition and wavelet-conditioned sample-wise adaptation. The frequency-domain analysis of ViTs draws on prior observations (Bai et al. 2022; Park & Kim 2022) but applies them specifically to the structured, edge-dominated content of geometric figures, charts, and function plots.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frequency-domain diagnosis provides a principled explanation for why MLLMs underperform on non-natural images beyond mere data scarcity, and the FM-ViT module offers a lightweight, plug-in fix that could be adopted by other vision-language pipelines targeting structured visual content. However, the approach is validated only on 7B-parameter models and three image categories, leaving open whether the frequency modulation generalizes to other structured domains (e.g., engineering diagrams, medical imaging) or scales to larger encoders. The sample-wise DWT conditioning adds per-inference overhead, and the two-scalar reweighting, while effective, may be too coarse for images with complex multi-scale frequency structure.
