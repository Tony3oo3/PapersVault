## Problem

Existing bitstream-corrupted video recovery methods rely on predefined, manually annotated masks to identify corrupted regions, which is labor-intensive and impractical for real-world scenarios where corruptions are unpredictable and irregular. This paper introduces a blind recovery setting that eliminates the need for any predefined masks, requiring the model to both locate and restore corrupted regions autonomously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250473/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes decoded corrupted video frames (448×256, 16-frame sequences) along with H.264 motion vectors (mapped to optical flow space, concatenated from both preceding and subsequent frames) and frame-type indicators (I/P/B one-hot vectors) as input, and produces recovered video frames at the same resolution. No corruption masks are provided at inference time; the model internally predicts pseudo masks to separate intact from corrupted regions. Baselines requiring masks use SAM2-generated pseudo masks with dilation for fair comparison.

## Method

M-GDM builds on a pre-trained latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion) with 3D convolutions and temporal attention, and introduces three components: (1) a Dual-stream Metadata Encoder that separately embeds motion vectors (via 2D convolutions + temporal transformer) and frame types (via MLP tokenizers), then injects the merged representation into cross-attention layers at every diffusion step to guide the model toward corrupted regions; (2) a Prior-driven Mask Predictor that fuses multi-scale U-Net attention outputs (diffusion prior) with metadata embeddings (metadata prior) through 3D convolutions and pixel-unshuffle to generate pseudo masks, supervised by BCE loss against binarized frame residuals; (3) a Post-Refinement Module of residual Swin transformer blocks that smooths boundary artifacts introduced by the hard-masking combination of intact input pixels and diffusion-recovered pixels. Training proceeds in three stages: U-Net fine-tuning, joint DME+PMP training, and PRM training with L1 + T-PatchGAN adversarial loss.

## Results

On the YouTube-VOS dataset, M-GDM achieves the best performance across all four metrics: PSNR 28.21, SSIM 0.9202, LPIPS 0.0333, and VFID 0.0661, outperforming the closest baseline BSCVR by 0.53 dB in PSNR. On the more challenging DAVIS dataset, the advantage widens substantially, with M-GDM reaching PSNR 26.05 (vs. 24.50 for BSCVR, a +1.55 dB gain) and VFID 0.1621 (vs. 0.1864 for BSCVR). Notably, M-GDM operates in a fully blind setting without any predefined corruption masks, whereas all baselines require SAM2-generated pseudo masks as input.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M-GDM outperforms all three baselines on every metric across both datasets. On YouTube-VOS, it beats BSCVR (the strongest baseline) by 0.53 dB PSNR and 0.0026 LPIPS, and surpasses ProPainter by 1.20 dB PSNR. On DAVIS, the margins are larger: M-GDM exceeds BSCVR by 1.55 dB PSNR and 0.0243 VFID, and outperforms E²FGVI by 2.22 dB PSNR. A critical distinction is that all three baselines (E²FGVI, ProPainter, BSCVR) require predefined corruption masks—here generated by a fine-tuned SAM2 with dilation—while M-GDM operates entirely without any mask input, making it the first method to address the blind bitstream-corrupted video recovery setting. The largest relative gains appear on DAVIS, where the more diverse and complex corruption patterns amplify the benefit of metadata-guided localization over mask-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the latent diffusion model framework (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) and the temporal attention architecture from language-driven video inpainting (Wu et al., CVPR 2024), adapting them for a recovery-specific task. It departs from prior bitstream-corrupted video recovery methods (Liu et al., NeurIPS 2024; E2FGVI; ProPainter) that all require predefined corruption masks by introducing a 'blind' setting where no mask is provided at inference. The key conceptual departure is repurposing intrinsic video metadata—motion vectors and frame types from the H.264 bitstream—as a corruption indicator, replacing the need for laborious manual annotation with information that is always available in compressed video.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the mask annotation requirement, this work makes bitstream-corrupted video recovery far more deployable in real-world archival and streaming pipelines where corruption patterns are unpredictable and irregular. The authors acknowledge a residual color deviation attributable to using a generation-oriented diffusion backbone, suggesting that a recovery-specific network could close this gap. Open questions include whether the metadata-guided approach generalizes beyond H.264 to newer codecs (HEVC, VVC), whether the pseudo-mask quality bottleneck can be further reduced, and whether the framework scales to longer sequences or higher resolutions without the current 16-frame, 448×256 constraint.
