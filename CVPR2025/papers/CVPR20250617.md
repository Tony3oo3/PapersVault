## Problem

Existing 3D object tracking methods universally depend on either predefined 3D CAD models, pose-annotated training datasets, or both, incurring high acquisition costs and limiting generalization across diverse objects and environments. This paper addresses the unexplored paradigm of truly prior-free tracking that requires neither a given 3D model nor any object-specific training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250617/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes monocular RGB images (640×480 in experiments) with a user-provided bounding box on the first frame and camera intrinsics; optional reference frames with known poses can be supplied to accelerate convergence. It produces per-frame 6DoF pose estimates at ~30 ms/frame (real-time), a progressively refined 3D mesh model (starting from a 1302-vertex sphere, resized to 256×256 masks during inverse rendering), and automatically selected key frames with bounding boxes that feed back into model refinement. The generated mesh is a standard deformable mesh that can be directly plugged into any model-based tracker without further processing.

## Method

BIT comprises two mutually enhancing modules operating in a bidirectional iterative loop. The geometry generation module starts from a 3D sphere and iteratively deforms it via differentiable inverse rendering (SoftRas) using a simplified loss combining 2D IoU between projected and SAM-segmented masks plus Laplacian smoothness regularization, with no learned parameters. The pose optimization module uses the current mesh with the SLOT region-based tracker to estimate per-frame poses, then selects key frames whose viewing directions exceed a 3° angular threshold relative to previously stored views, feeding them back to refine the mesh. This loop runs for up to 3 iterations (~30 s total), after which the converged mesh supports real-time tracking. The approach is entirely training-free: SAM handles segmentation, SoftRas provides differentiable rendering, and SLOT performs pose optimization, with no component requiring object-specific training.

## Results

On the MOPED dataset (ADD-AUC 0–10 cm), BIT without any reference frames (Ours(0)) achieves an average score of 74.2, outperforming all baselines including LatentFusion (73.7) and Gen6D (63.4), despite requiring no training or 3D model priors. With 3 reference frames (Ours(3)), the average jumps to 86.2, a substantial margin over the best baseline. On the RBOT dataset, generated models with 6+ reference frames enable trackers like SLOT and Song23 to reach 63.2% and 77.5% 5 cm/5° accuracy respectively, approaching the 84.8% and 92.4% achieved with ground-truth CAD models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BIT (Ours(0)) achieves 74.2 average ADD-AUC on MOPED without any training, 3D model, or reference frames, narrowly beating the best baseline LatentFusion† (73.7) which requires both RGB-D input and training. With 3 reference frames, Ours(3) reaches 86.2, surpassing LatentFusion† by 12.5 points and Gen6D (63.4) by 22.8 points. Notably, all baselines require either training on specific datasets, pre-defined CAD models, or RGB-D depth input, whereas BIT uses only RGB and no training. On the RBOT dataset, the generated models demonstrate strong reusability: with 12 reference frames, SLOT achieves 68.8% vs. 84.8% with GT models, and Song23 reaches 81.5% vs. 92.4%, showing the generated geometry is a viable substitute for precise CAD models in existing tracking pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

BIT departs from the dominant paradigm in 3D object tracking, where methods invariably rely on either pre-defined CAD/reconstructed models (SLOT, SRT3D, RBGT) or pose-annotated training data (Gen6D, FoundationPose, PVNet), or both (DeepAC). Rather than introducing a new tracker or renderer, the paper repurposes existing components—SLOT for region-based pose optimization, SAM for segmentation, and SoftRas for differentiable inverse rendering—into a novel bidirectional iterative loop where geometry generation and pose tracking mutually bootstrap each other from scratch. The conceptual lineage is closest to differentiable-rendering-based reconstruction (e.g., Rozumnyi et al. 2023), but BIT's key departure is the closed-loop, real-time, training-free formulation that eliminates all external priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a truly prior-free pipeline can match or exceed methods that exploit model and/or training priors, BIT removes a significant practical barrier to deploying 3D tracking in novel or unstructured environments where CAD models and annotated data are unavailable. The generated meshes are reusable across multiple existing trackers (SLOT, SRT3D, RBGT, Song23), making the contribution broadly integrable. Open questions remain around complex topologies (hollow, ring-shaped objects), recessed surface features, robustness under occlusion and fast motion, and extension to non-rigid objects—all acknowledged by the authors as future directions.
