## Problem

Object detectors trained under the i.i.d. assumption suffer substantial performance degradation when deployed on data from different geographic or climatic regions, yet no standardized benchmark exists for evaluating object detection under realistic spatial domain shifts in satellite imagery. Existing DG benchmarks (COCO-O, WILDS) either use synthetic/artistic domain shifts or focus on classification, leaving a gap for high-impact real-world applications like climate monitoring and disaster damage assessment.

## Input / Output

Input consists of 512×512 pixel satellite image tiles cropped from high-resolution (0.3 m) xView and xBD imagery using SAHI with 0.2 overlap, preserving original resolution. For RWDS-CZ, the output is bounding boxes over 16 object classes shared across three Köppen climate zones; for RWDS-FR and RWDS-HE, the output is binary damage classification (damaged vs. no damage) with bounding boxes around buildings. The datasets are derived from segmentation polygons in xBD, converted to bounding boxes for the detection task.

## Method

The paper introduces RWDS, a suite of three DG benchmark datasets (RWDS-CZ, RWDS-FR, RWDS-HE) constructed by mapping geo-coordinates of xView/xBD images to Köppen climate zones, flood-affected countries, or individual hurricane events, then applying a class-balanced splitting algorithm (Algorithm 1) that iteratively allocates images to train/val/test sets to maintain consistent per-class distributions. Six SOTA detectors spanning CNN-based (Faster R-CNN, Mask R-CNN, TOOD), transformer-based (DINO), and vision-language foundation models (Grounding DINO, GLIP) are benchmarked under leave-one-domain-out evaluation in both single-source and multi-source training setups. Performance is quantified via MS-COCO mAP, Performance Drop (PD), and Harmonic Mean (H) of ID and OOD mAP, with error decomposition performed using the TIDE toolbox. The key empirical finding is that multi-source training consistently improves OOD generalisation, and that open-set foundation models (Grounding DINO, GLIP) exhibit the smallest performance drops under spatial domain shift.

## Results

Across all three RWDS-CZ climate zones under single-source DG, every detector suffers substantial OOD performance drops: 36–49% PD for CZ A, 7–25% for CZ B, and 41–56% for CZ C. Grounding DINO achieves the highest Harmonic Mean (H) scores across all targets (9.5, 10.4, 9.2), while GLIP records the lowest performance drop for CZ A (36%) and CZ B (7%). Under multi-source training (Table 6), OOD mAP improves markedly (e.g., Grounding DINO on CZ B rises from 10.0 to 11.0 mAP_OOD) and PD shrinks (e.g., GLIP on CZ B drops from 7% to 6%), confirming that multi-source training enhances generalisability. On RWDS-FR, drops are even more severe (51–72% for India, 61–93% for US), with Grounding DINO and GLIP again leading (H = 16.1 and 17.2 for US respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Grounding DINO consistently outperforms all five other detectors in Harmonic Mean across every target domain on RWDS-CZ (e.g., H = 10.4 on CZ B vs. 8.7 for DINO, 8.5 for GLIP, 6.8 for TOOD), and also achieves the highest OOD mAP in most settings. GLIP distinguishes itself by the lowest Performance Drop on CZ A (36% vs. 42–49% for others) and ties Grounding DINO on CZ B (both 7% PD), but its lower ID mAP (8.8 vs. 10.8 on CZ B) results in a lower H-score. DINO (Swin-L backbone) is the strongest non-foundation-model detector, achieving H = 8.7 on CZ B and 7.4 on CZ A/C, roughly 1.5–2.0 points below Grounding DINO. TOOD, Mask R-CNN, and Faster R-CNN form the lower tier, with Faster R-CNN recording the worst H-scores (4.7–6.7) and the highest PD on CZ C (56%). On RWDS-FR, the gap widens: Grounding DINO and GLIP achieve H = 16.1 and 17.2 on the US target respectively, while Faster R-CNN and Mask R-CNN collapse to H ≈ 3.4–3.7, a >5× difference, underscoring that foundation-model-based detectors are substantially more robust to geographic domain shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the domain generalization (DG) benchmarking paradigm from image classification (PACS, DomainNet, WILDS) and stylized object detection (COCO-O) to a fundamentally different axis of shift: spatial/covariate shift in satellite imagery driven by climate zones and geographic regions. While COCO-O introduced natural distribution shifts for object detection, the authors argue its domains (sketch, cartoon, tattoo) lack practical deployment motivation, and prior robustness benchmarks (COCO-C, OOD-CV) focus on synthetic corruptions or nuisance variations rather than the large-scale geographic variability encountered in humanitarian and climate applications. The paper repurposes existing large-scale datasets (xView, xBD) by re-partitioning them along Köppen climate zones and disaster-event boundaries to create the first DG benchmarks tailored to real-world, high-impact satellite object detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RWDS suite fills a critical gap: no prior benchmark assessed object detection generalization under the spatial domain shifts that actually matter for disaster response and climate monitoring. The severe performance drops observed (up to 93% for US→India flood detection) underscore that current SOTA detectors, including foundation-model-based ones, remain far from deployment-ready in cross-regional settings. The finding that multi-source training consistently improves OOD performance at a small ID cost provides a practical recipe, but the residual gaps—especially for foundation models like Grounding DINO and GLIP—suggest that architectural or training-time interventions beyond data diversity are still needed. Open questions include whether these results generalize beyond the specific disasters and climate zones studied, and whether the binary damage classification adequately captures the complexity of real assessment workflows.
