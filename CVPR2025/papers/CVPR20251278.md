## Problem

Existing human video generation methods focus on lip-syncing, body pose, or hand synthesis in isolation, but generating realistic hand-object interaction (HOI) videos—where objects in physical contact with hands must be faithfully rendered alongside accurate hand poses—remains largely unexplored. The core difficulties are the intricate occlusion entanglement at the hand-object interface, the limited pixel area occupied by hands and objects making fine texture recovery hard, and the fact that size/shape differences between source and target objects produce physically unreasonable interactions if the motion sequence is left unchanged.

## Input / Output

The method takes a driving video (5-second clips at 25 FPS, 512×512) from which layout guidance (three per-frame bounding boxes: two fixed-size squares for hands, one variable-size box for the object) and 3D hand meshes (MANO parameters via HaMeR) are extracted, along with a single reference image of the target object. At inference, the reference image can be swapped for a different object, triggering the adaptive layout adjustment. The output is a synthesized HOI video where the target object appears in natural contact with the hands following the source motion.

## Method

Re-HOLD employs a two-branch diffusion architecture (Reference U-Net for object texture encoding, Denoising U-Net for noise prediction) built on Stable Diffusion V1.5, with the key insight of using specialized layout representations—fixed-size, pose-invariant bounding boxes for hands and a variable-size box for the object—to disentangle hand modeling from object adaptation. A Hand-Object Interaction Restoration Module adds a ControlNet-like encoder for 3D hand mesh structure guidance plus two independent learnable memory banks (hand and object) with masked cross-attention layers to restore fine-grained textures. At inference, a four-step adaptive layout adjustment strategy rescales and repositions the object and hand bounding boxes to maintain physically plausible contact when the target object differs in size from the source. Training proceeds in two stages: image-level HOI modeling (with periodic hand/object-region-only L1 loss) followed by temporal consistency modeling using consecutive frames, all initialized from AnimateAnyone weights.

## Results

On the authors' collected dataset (9 subjects, 14 objects), Re-HOLD achieves the best PSNR (33.451) and FID (19.021) in self-reenactment, substantially outperforming the next-best methods (RealisDance: PSNR 32.784, FID 26.337). For cross-object reenactment, Re-HOLD attains the highest subject consistency (0.955) and hand agreement (0.773), while matching HOI-Swap on hand fidelity (0.994) and motion smoothness (0.994). On the HOI4D benchmark, Re-HOLD similarly leads in PSNR (31.984) and FID (26.583) for self-reenactment, and ties HOI-Swap on cross-reenactment hand fidelity (0.994) while achieving the best subject consistency (0.915).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Re-HOLD outperforms all five baselines on the most discriminative metrics: PSNR (33.451 vs. 32.903 for VideoSwap, the runner-up) and FID (19.021 vs. 26.337 for RealisDance) in self-reenactment on the authors' dataset, indicating substantially better image fidelity. In cross-object reenactment, Re-HOLD achieves the best subject consistency (0.955) and hand agreement (0.773), surpassing AnimateAnyone (0.950, 0.698) and HOI-Swap (0.944, 0.725), while tying HOI-Swap on hand fidelity (0.994) and motion smoothness (0.994). Video editing methods (AnyV2V, VideoSwap) perform markedly worse on HOI-specific metrics—AnyV2V scores only 0.223 hand agreement and 116.084 FID—confirming that general-purpose video editing is ill-suited for hand-object interaction synthesis. Among HOI-aware methods, HOI-Swap is the closest competitor but lags in subject consistency (0.944 vs. 0.955) and hand agreement (0.725 vs. 0.773), which the authors attribute to its lack of adaptive layout adjustment for size-mismatched objects. In the user study (Table 2), Re-HOLD achieves 0.92 on both HOI consistency and object appearance consistency, far exceeding HOI-Swap (0.76, 0.40) and RealisDance (0.68, 0.74).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the two-branch diffusion architecture popularized by AnimateAnyone and RealisDance (Reference U-Net for object texture + Denoising U-Net for motion-conditioned generation) and extends it with specialized layout representations (fixed-size hand bounding boxes + variable-size object bounding boxes) to disentangle hand and object modeling. It departs from HOI-Swap, which performs object-centric single-hand video swapping, by formulating a human-centric two-hand HOI reenactment task and introducing an adaptive layout adjustment strategy to handle cross-object size/shape mismatches at inference. The Hand-Object Interaction Restoration Module with dual memory banks and ControlNet-based hand mesh encoding represents a novel architectural addition not present in prior human animation or HOI works.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is positioned as the first human-centric HOI video reenactment framework, addressing a practical gap in digital human applications where characters must interact with diverse real-world objects (e.g., e-commerce product display). The adaptive layout adjustment strategy is a practical inference-time technique that could be adopted by other video generation systems handling object size variation. However, the authors acknowledge the dataset is limited to object-display gestures in live-streaming scenarios, and the framework produces less satisfactory results for 3D object manipulation (e.g., multi-view object rotation), leaving a significant open question for more general HOI synthesis.
