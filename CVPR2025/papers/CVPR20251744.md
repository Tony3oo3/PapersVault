## Problem

Most Neural Video Codecs rely exclusively on temporal references, which are insufficient to describe emerging objects and are vulnerable to motion coding errors under large motions. This leads to poor temporal contexts and a misaligned latent prior that degrades entropy model accuracy, limiting rate-distortion performance in complex scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251744/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The codec takes a high-resolution video sequence and a 4x bicubic-downsampled low-resolution base video as dual inputs. It produces two independently decodable bitstreams: a full-resolution compressed stream and an additional low-resolution stream. During training, sequences are cropped to 256×256 (independent optimization) or 384×256 (joint optimization), with 6-frame groups for the base codec and 32-frame groups for the augmentative codec.

## Method

SEVC embeds a base temporal-only codec (DCVC-DC) to compress the 4x downsampled frame, yielding three spatial references: base MVs, a spatial feature, and a spatial latent representation. A Motion and Feature Co-Augmentation (MFCA) module then progressively augments the base MVs and spatial feature with multi-scale temporal features in a bottom-up structure, where each Augment Stage refines MVs via residual prediction and uses the improved MVs to better align temporal features before augmenting the spatial feature, ultimately producing hybrid spatial-temporal contexts. For the entropy model, a spatial-guided latent prior uses the upsampled spatial latent representation as a Transformer query to implicitly align and fuse three temporal latent representations (y_{t-1}, y_{t-2}, y_{t-3}), reducing residual compared to the single-frame prior. A joint spatial-temporal optimization with a small regularization weight w_l on base distortion enables the codec to learn quality-adaptive bit allocation for spatial references end-to-end.

## Results

On the primary evaluation (Intra Period = 32, 96 frames, anchor VTM-13.2 LDB), SEVC achieves an average BD-Rate of −22.5% across seven datasets (HEVC B–E, MCL-JCV, UVG, USTC-TD), surpassing the previous SOTA DCVC-DC (−14.2%) by 8.3 percentage points. Under the IP = −1 setting, SEVC reaches −24.5% average BD-Rate, outperforming DCVC-FM (−12.6%) by 11.9 percentage points. On sequences with large motions or emerging objects (Table 3), SEVC achieves up to −55.9% BD-Rate vs. DCVC-HEM, compared to only −17.9% for DCVC-DC on the same sequence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEVC outperforms all six neural video codec baselines across every dataset under both IP=32 and IP=−1 settings. Against the strongest prior NVC, DCVC-DC [28], SEVC saves an additional 8.3% BD-Rate on average (−22.5% vs. −14.2%), with the gap widening to 11.9% under IP=−1 (−24.5% vs. −12.6% for DCVC-FM [29], the best IP=−1 codec). The advantage is most pronounced on sequences with large motions or emerging objects: on USTC_BicycleDriving, SEVC achieves −55.9% BD-Rate vs. DCVC-HEM while DCVC-DC only reaches −17.9%. Against the traditional anchor VTM-13.2 LDB, SEVC delivers 22.5–24.5% bitrate reduction, whereas the best prior NVC (DCVC-DC) achieves only 14.2%. Notably, SEVC also provides an additional independently decodable low-resolution bitstream at no extra architectural cost beyond the embedded base codec.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEVC extends the conditional-coding NVC lineage (DVC → DCVC → DCVC-TCM → DCVC-DC → DCVC-FM) by embedding a base temporal-only codec to compress a 4× downsampled frame, thereby introducing spatial references (base MVs, spatial feature, spatial latent) that were absent in all prior temporal-only NVCs. The progressive refinement of these spatial references via the MFCA module draws directly from Video Super-Resolution architectures (BasicVSR, VSR-Transformer), while the joint spatial-temporal optimization borrows the hierarchical quality structure from traditional spatially scalable video coding (SVC, SHVC). The key departure is that prior NVCs treat the previously decoded frame as the sole source of prediction context, whereas SEVC treats the low-resolution reconstruction as an additional, complementary reference that augments both the motion estimation and the entropy-model prior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 11.9% BD-rate improvement over the previous SOTA (DCVC-FM) is unusually large for this field, where typical year-over-year gains are 1–3%, and the additional low-resolution bitstream provides a practical scalable-coding benefit at no extra architectural cost. The paper identifies a concrete failure mode of temporal-only NVCs—misaligned latent prior and insufficient context for emerging objects—and shows that a simple 4× downsampled spatial reference resolves it, suggesting that the community has been underutilizing spatially redundant information. A key open question is whether the base codec must remain a full NVC or whether lighter-weight spatial-reference extractors (e.g., learned feature maps without full reconstruction) could achieve similar gains at lower complexity, a direction the authors explicitly flag for future work.
