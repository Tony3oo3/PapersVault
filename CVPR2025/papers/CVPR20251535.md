## Problem

Identity-preserving face synthesis (IPFS) aims to generate synthetic faces for training face recognition models without using real person data, but prior methods face a trade-off: they either produce identity-consistent yet stylistically uniform images, or diverse styles that degrade identity retention. A key overlooked factor is that real-world datasets exhibit subject-specific style distinctiveness (e.g., gender-correlated facial shapes, age-related distributions), which prior arts ignore by applying uniform, subject-agnostic style control across the entire dataset.

## Input / Output

The generator takes two conditioning signals: (1) an identity embedding extracted by a pretrained ArcFace model from a reference image of a virtual subject (obtained from an unconditional diffusion model, filtered by cosine similarity threshold 0.3 and SDD-FIQA quality), and (2) three pixel-aligned 3DMM feature maps (surface normals, albedo, Lambertian rendering) rendered from 236-dim DECA style parameters (100 shape, 50 expression, 9 pose, 50 texture, 27 illumination). The style maps provide coarse, parametric style descriptions without facial details, which the LDM fills in to produce photorealistic output. Novel styles are sampled from a subject-aware Gaussian mixture that preserves both intra-class variation and inter-subject distinctiveness.

## Method

MorphFace conditions a latent diffusion model (U-Net noise estimator) on both identity embeddings (via cross-attention) and 3DMM style feature maps (via a lightweight encoder trained end-to-end), optimizing a standard L2 denoising loss. Two key mechanisms differentiate it from prior IPFS work: (1) Subject-aware style sampling decomposes the global style distribution N(μ,Σ) into per-class Gaussians N(μ_i,Σ_i) whose weighted sum equals the global distribution, so each subject gets a distinct style center while still allowing intra-class variation; facial shape means are additionally anchored to the reference image to boost identity consistency. (2) Context blending exploits the frequency structure of denoising—low-frequency style features (pose, illumination) are established in early timesteps while high-frequency identity details emerge later—by applying classifier-free guidance asymmetrically: style context is augmented for t > t₀ and identity context for t ≤ t₀, resolving the inherent tension between the two conditions.

## Results

MorphFace achieves 93.32% average FR accuracy at 0.5M synthetic images (10K subjects × 50 images), outperforming the best prior method CemiFace (92.30%) by 1.02 points on average. The advantage is most pronounced on pose-varying benchmarks: +2.24 on CFP-FP (94.11 vs. 91.06) and +1.08 on CPLFW (88.73 vs. 87.65). At the larger 1.2M volume (24K × 50), MorphFace reaches 94.17% average, surpassing Arc2Face (93.15%) and approaching real-data CASIA performance (94.79%). Notably, MorphFace's 0.5M result already exceeds the 1.2M results of all prior SOTAs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MorphFace outperforms all 11 baselines on every benchmark at the 0.5M volume. Against the strongest prior methods, it leads CemiFace (NeurIPS'24) by 1.02 on average, with the largest gaps on CFP-FP (+3.05) and CPLFW (+1.08), indicating superior cross-pose generalization. It also surpasses Arc2Face (ECCV'24) by 1.59 on average and DCFace (CVPR'23) by 3.76 on average. The diffusion-based baselines (IDiff-Face, DCFace, ID3, CemiFace, Arc2Face) cluster in the 88–92% average range, while GAN-based methods (SynFace, SFace, ExFaceGAN, SFace2) lag significantly at 75–80%. At the 1.2M scale, MorphFace (94.17%) still beats Arc2Face (93.15%) and DCFace (91.21%), and its CPLFW/CALFW scores approach those of a model trained on real CASIA data (89.73/92.82), suggesting the synthetic data nearly closes the real-vs-synthetic gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

MorphFace extends the identity-conditioned latent diffusion framework established by IDiff-Face by adding a second conditioning channel: 3DMM-rendered style feature maps (surface normals, albedo, Lambertian rendering) from DECA, enabling fine-grained parametric control over shape, pose, expression, texture, and illumination. The key conceptual departure from prior IPFS methods (DCFace, ID3, CemiFace, Arc2Face) is the rejection of subject-agnostic uniform style sampling in favor of subject-aware sampling that models per-class style distributions, reflecting the observation that real-world subjects exhibit distinct style signatures. The context blending mechanism adapts classifier-free guidance with a time-varying unconditioned term that reweights style in early (low-frequency) timesteps and identity in later (high-frequency) timesteps, a design motivated by the known frequency decomposition of diffusion denoising.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and ethically motivated need—training face recognition models without web-crawled real images—while resolving the previously observed trade-off between intra-class identity consistency and style diversity. The subject-aware sampling insight (that real datasets contain subject-specific style distributions, not just global variation) is a simple but underexplored principle that could inform other synthetic data generation tasks. Limitations include reliance on a single training dataset (CASIA-WebFace), a single FR backbone (IR-50), and the Gaussian approximation of style attribute distributions, whose validity at larger scale or with more diverse populations remains untested. Open questions include whether the 3DMM parameterization captures all relevant style dimensions (e.g., accessories, occlusions) and whether the approach transfers to other 3DMMs or to video face synthesis.
