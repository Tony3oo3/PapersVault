## Problem

Existing two-stage correspondence-based category-level pose estimation methods suffer from two critical weaknesses: they rely on pose-sensitive point cloud backbones (e.g., PointNet++ with absolute coordinates) that lack holistic shape-sensitive feature interaction, making camera-to-object transformation learning difficult; and their pose fitting stage is fragile to outlier correspondences caused by segmentation noise and depth sensor errors, requiring slow iterative methods like RANSAC for robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251403/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a cropped RGB image (resized to 224×224) and a segmented depth image from which a 1024-point object point cloud is derived via back-projection and downsampling. It outputs a 6D pose (rotation in SO(3) and translation in R³) solved via the Umeyama algorithm from inlier keypoint correspondences, plus a 3D size vector (R³) regressed by an MLP from globally pooled keypoint features. The correspondence stage operates on 96 sparse keypoints selected by Farthest Point Sampling, predicting their NOCS coordinates and per-keypoint outlier scores.

## Method

SpotPose is a two-stage correspondence-based approach. In the correspondence prediction stage, pose-invariant dense features are extracted by an adapted PointNet++ (PoseInv-PointNet++) that incorporates a T-Net to align the input point cloud and removes absolute coordinate injection, fused with DINOv2 image features; sparse keypoints are then selected via FPS and enhanced through L=6 geometric interaction blocks, each combining a local geometric cross-attention layer (aggregating 16 nearest dense neighbors) and a global geometric self-attention layer (attending over all 96 keypoints), both conditioned on a pose-invariant distance-and-angle-based geometric descriptor. In the pose fitting stage, a lightweight MLP-based outlier predictor scores each keypoint, and a 0.5 threshold filters outliers before the Umeyama algorithm solves the pose from inlier correspondences alone, achieving robust fitting in ~2 ms without RANSAC. Training uses a modulated correspondence loss that suppresses inlier scores for hard-to-predict correspondences (encouraging them to be flagged as outliers) plus an L2 size regression loss.

## Results

On REAL275, SpotPose achieves 59.7% on 5°2cm, 81.5% on 10°2cm, and 84.1% IoU50, outperforming the previous best AG-Pose by 5.0% and 6.8% respectively. On CAMERA25, it reaches 80.4% on 5°2cm and 94.3% IoU50. On the more challenging HouseCat6D dataset, SpotPose attains 23.8% on 5°2cm and 52.3% on 10°2cm, surpassing the runner-up AG-Pose by 12.3% and 19.6% on those metrics respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpotPose outperforms the closest correspondence-based competitor AG-Pose [14] by 5.0% on 5°2cm and 6.8% on 10°2cm on REAL275, and by 2.6% and 2.2% on the same metrics on CAMERA25. Against the strongest direct regression method SecondPose [3], SpotPose leads by 3.5% on 5°2cm and 6.8% on 10°2cm on REAL275. On the more challenging HouseCat6D dataset, the margins widen substantially: SpotPose exceeds AG-Pose by 12.3% on 5°2cm and 19.6% on 10°5cm, and surpasses SecondPose by 12.8% on 5°2cm. The only metric where SpotPose does not lead is 5°5cm on CAMERA25, where it trails GenPose [36] by 0.6% (83.8 vs. 84.4), though it outperforms GenPose on all other CAMERA25 metrics. Ablation studies confirm that the pose-invariant feature extraction contributes +8.3% on 5°2cm and the explicit outlier removal contributes +9.2% over the no-outlier-awareness baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpotPose extends the two-stage correspondence-based paradigm established by NOCS and refined by SPD, DPDN, and AG-Pose, but deliberately departs from the trend of adding complexity (shape priors, implicit queries, auxiliary enhancers) to address transformation learning. Its closest predecessor, AG-Pose, also uses sparse keypoints with geometric descriptors, but SpotPose replaces AG-Pose's pose-sensitive relative position embeddings with a pose-invariant distance-and-angle descriptor (from the Geometric Transformer), swaps adaptive keypoint detection for training-free FPS, and substitutes a deep pose estimator with explicit outlier-score prediction plus the classical Umeyama algorithm. The dense feature backbone is a minimal modification of PointNet++ (adding T-Net alignment and removing absolute coordinates) combined with frozen DINOv2 image features, positioning the paper as a 'simple yet effective' rethinking rather than a new architectural paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that two seemingly simple design choices—enforcing pose-invariance at the feature-extraction level and explicitly predicting per-keypoint outlier scores—yield large gains (up to 12.8% in 5°2cm on HouseCat6D) over both direct-regression and prior correspondence-based methods, while keeping inference fast (1.97 ms pose fitting vs. 6.50 ms for RANSAC). This challenges the recent trend toward learned deep pose estimators by showing that a lightweight threshold-based filter plus Umeyama is both more accurate and more efficient. Open questions include whether the geometric interaction blocks (L=6) can be reduced without loss, how the method scales to categories with extreme shape diversity, and whether the pose-invariance benefit persists when the T-Net alignment is imperfect under heavy occlusion.
