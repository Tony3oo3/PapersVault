## Problem

Existing license plate image restoration (LPIR) methods are trained on synthetically degraded images and fail to handle the complex, severe degradations encountered in real-world traffic surveillance (long distance, poor camera quality, motion blur). No paired real-world multi-frame degraded LP dataset existed, making it impossible to train or evaluate restoration models under realistic conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252173/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes three consecutive degraded LP frames captured by a high-speed camera as a vehicle approaches (with decreasing distance d1 > d2 > d3), and produces a single restored high-resolution LP image. The multi-frame input is essential: the model exploits temporal correlations across frames to compensate for details lost in any single frame, and the residual between the encoder output and the ground truth serves as the diffusion target rather than the full image.

## Method

LP-Diff is a Diffusion-based end-to-end architecture that first encodes three degraded frames with a pre-trained CNN encoder (trained on CCPD via an inpainting pretext task), then fuses temporal information through an Inter-frame Cross Attention Module (ICAM) that applies cross-attention between consecutive frame pairs with Dual-Pathway Fusion (DFM) gating on both Q and K to suppress background redundancy. A Texture Enhancement Module (TEM) computes Sobel-based curvature and gradient magnitude on the fused features to selectively amplify edge and stroke geometry. A second DFM stage applies combined channel and spatial attention (via global average/max/median pooling) to filter redundant features before a decoder produces the conditioning embedding ψ. Finally, a Residual Condition Diffusion Module (RCDM) performs standard DDPM denoising on the residual (f_gt − ψ), conditioned on ψ, so the network only needs to predict the correction rather than the full image, reducing the generative burden and improving fidelity.

## Results

On the real-world MDLP dataset, LP-Diff achieves the best PSNR (14.396 dB), SSIM (0.393), FID (21.977), and LPIPS (0.159) among all compared methods. On the synthetic CCPD dataset, LP-Diff also leads with PSNR 13.132 dB, SSIM 0.311, FID 46.098, and LPIPS 0.221. For downstream license plate text recognition, LP-Diff attains a normalized edit distance (NED) of 0.198 and word accuracy (ACC) of 0.305, improving NED by 72.08% over the original degraded images and by 29.03% over the next-best method (Real-ESRGAN, NED 0.279).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LP-Diff outperforms all five baselines on every metric across both datasets. Against the strongest pixel-level competitor HAT, LP-Diff gains +0.235 dB PSNR and +0.143 SSIM on MDLP, while dramatically reducing FID from 229.558 to 21.977 (a ~10× improvement), indicating far more realistic outputs. Real-ESRGAN is the closest competitor on perceptual metrics (FID 30.975 vs. 21.977; LPIPS 0.176 vs. 0.159 on MDLP) but lags on PSNR (13.927 vs. 14.396) and SSIM (0.369 vs. 0.393). The two other diffusion-based baselines, ResDiff and ResShift, underperform substantially on real-world data (PSNR 12.001 and 12.527 respectively), confirming that diffusion methods trained on synthetic degradations do not generalize to real-world LP degradation. For the downstream recognition task, LP-Diff achieves NED 0.198 and ACC 0.305, beating the next-best Real-ESRGAN (NED 0.279, ACC 0.161) by 29% in NED and nearly doubling ACC, while all other baselines remain above NED 0.33.

## Relation to Prior Work

LP-Diff builds directly on the denoising diffusion probabilistic framework (Ho et al., 2020) for its generative backbone and adapts the residual-conditioning strategy from DiffPlate (AlHalawani et al., 2024), where the diffusion model denoises the residual between a conditional embedding and the ground truth rather than the full image. It departs from the broader LPIR lineage (MSR-CNN, DP-GAN, and earlier CNN/GAN-based methods) in two key ways: it replaces single-frame synthetic-degradation training with multi-frame real-world paired data, and it introduces temporal cross-attention and explicit texture-enhancement modules that prior LPIR architectures lacked. The general image-restoration literature (HAT, Real-ESRGAN, ResDiff, ResShift) serves as the comparison baseline, but the paper argues those methods are ill-suited to the specific, severe, and multi-frame nature of real-world LP degradation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of MDLP—the first paired, multi-frame, real-world degraded LP dataset—addresses a concrete gap: prior LPIR work relied on synthetic degradation pipelines that the paper shows (via frequency-domain analysis and T-tests) produce fundamentally different degradation statistics than real captures. The 72% NED improvement over raw degraded images and the consistent outperformance of five SOTA restoration baselines on both real and synthetic splits make the dataset and method practically useful for traffic-surveillance deployments. However, the diffusion-based inference (1000 steps) is slow, the dataset covers a limited geographic scope, and the multi-frame assumption (three consecutive frames from a fixed camera) constrains applicability to scenarios where such temporal redundancy is available. Open questions include whether the temporal-fusion gains transfer to single-frame settings and how to accelerate sampling without sacrificing the texture-fidelity advantage.
