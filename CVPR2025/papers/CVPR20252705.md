## Problem

In-Context Learning in Large Multimodal Models is highly sensitive to the configuration of in-context demonstrations, and existing shift vector-based methods (e.g., LIVE) rely on incomplete mathematical approximations: they place shift vectors after FFN layers rather than attention layers, use a single query-independent shift magnitude, and ignore the distinct representation spaces of individual attention heads. This results in poor generalization across diverse queries and suboptimal ICL performance.

## Input / Output

At inference, MimIC processes a single query (an RGB image paired with a text question for VQA, or an image alone for captioning) without any in-context demonstrations, producing a text answer or caption. During training, the original LMM is fed k randomly sampled ICDs plus one query (32-shot for Idefics1, 8-shot for Idefics2) to generate teacher hidden states, while the MimIC model processes only the query. The key distinction from standard ICL is that no ICDs are needed at test time, eliminating the long-context overhead of multimodal demonstrations.

## Method

MimIC replaces each self-attention head in the LMM with a MimIC Attention Head that inserts a learnable shift vector v ∈ R^{d_h} after the attention computation, scaled by a query-dependent magnitude μ̃(q,K) = exp(f(q)) / (exp(f(q)) + Z₂(q,K)), where f is a trainable linear layer mapping the query to a scalar. This design ensures each attention head learns its own shift direction in its representation space, and the shift magnitude adapts dynamically to each query. Training uses a knowledge-distillation objective: a layer-wise L2 alignment loss between MimIC's hidden states (processing only the query) and the original LMM's hidden states (processing ICDs + query), combined with a standard language modeling loss. Random ICD selection per training step encourages the model to capture the general shift pattern rather than overfitting to specific demonstration configurations.

## Results

On Idefics-9b, MimIC achieves 59.64% accuracy on VQAv2, 52.05% on OK-VQA, and 114.89 CIDEr on COCO Caption, surpassing 32-shot ICL by 3.46%/3.57%/9.00 points respectively. On Idefics2-8b-base, MimIC reaches 69.29%/58.74%/132.87, outperforming 8-shot ICL by 3.09%/1.06%/10.36 points. MimIC uses only 0.26M trainable parameters, roughly 96× fewer than LoRA (25.0M on Idefics1), while achieving superior results across all three tasks and both architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MimIC outperforms all five baselines on both LMMs across all three tasks. Against the strongest trainable baseline LIVE, MimIC gains 4.04%/4.99%/2.13 points on VQAv2/OK-VQA/COCO for Idefics1, and 1.69%/3.88%/6.83 for Idefics2, attributed to its per-head shift vectors and query-dependent magnitude. LoRA uses 96× more parameters (25.0M vs 0.26M on Idefics1) yet underperforms MimIC by 4.04%/4.99%/17.14 points on the same model. Non-trainable methods (TV, FV) are substantially weaker: on Idefics2 they fail to even surpass the zero-shot baseline (e.g., FV scores 36.47 vs 55.39 zero-shot on VQAv2), indicating they cannot capture task-specific multimodal information. RICES (retrieval-based ICD selection) shows inconsistent gains—outperforming random ICL on Idefics1 but underperforming it on Idefics2 for OK-VQA and COCO—highlighting the sensitivity of ICL to ICD configuration that MimIC aims to eliminate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the shift vector interpretation of in-context learning, building directly on the Task Vector (Hendel et al.) and Function Vector (Todd et al.) frameworks that decompose ICL into a query-independent component plus an ICD-induced shift. It most closely builds on LIVE (Peng et al., 2024), which introduced learnable in-context vectors after FFN layers, but departs from it on four specific architectural choices: placing shift vectors after attention layers rather than FFN, assigning one vector per attention head, making the shift magnitude query-dependent via a lightweight linear layer, and using a layer-wise L2 alignment loss instead of KL divergence. The mathematical derivation in Sec. 3.1 formalizes why these choices are necessary for a faithful approximation of the self-attention decomposition, positioning MimIC as a more rigorous instantiation of the same conceptual idea.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MimIC offers a practical path to ICL-like multimodal performance without the inference-time cost of long ICD contexts, which is significant given that image tokens inflate context length by orders of magnitude compared to text. The data efficiency result—exceeding 32-shot ICL with only 200 training samples, roughly 1/8 of what LIVE requires—suggests that a more faithful mathematical approximation of the shift mechanism is more sample-efficient than brute-force learning. Open questions include whether the per-head shift vectors generalize to architectures with grouped-query attention or sliding-window attention, whether the approach extends to video or 3D modalities where the ICD sensitivity problem is even more acute, and whether the layer-wise alignment loss can be replaced by cheaper proxy objectives for very deep models.
