## Problem

Single-stream ViT-based UAV trackers lack effective strategies to handle frequent occlusions from obstacles such as buildings, trees, and mountains, which are pervasive in aerial tracking scenarios. While ViT backbones have proven effective for real-time tracking, no prior work has explored learning occlusion-robust ViT representations within a unified framework specifically for UAV tracking, and existing efficient ViT trackers (e.g., Aba-ViTrack) incur significant overhead from unstructured token access operations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252725/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tracker takes a 128×128 target template and a 256×256 search image as input; during teacher training, a randomly masked copy of the template (via spatial Cox process masking at a configurable ratio σ) is additionally fed to the backbone. The prediction head (four Conv-BN-ReLU layers) outputs a local offset, normalized bounding box size, and per-pixel classification score over the search region, from which the final bounding box is derived by locating the argmax score position and applying the offset and size at that location.

## Method

ORTrack learns Occlusion-Robust Representations by enforcing MSE invariance between the ViT backbone's template-token features computed on the original template and on a randomly masked version, where the masking is generated by a finite spatial Cox process: a Poisson-distributed random intensity Γ scales a Gaussian-shaped inhomogeneous intensity over the template region, so that masking concentrates on the target center while the random Γ introduces variability in the effective masking ratio. This adds no inference cost since masking is applied only during training. For efficiency, an Adaptive Feature-Based Knowledge Distillation (AFKD) stage trains a smaller student ViT (fewer blocks, same architecture) to mimic the frozen teacher's backbone features, with the distillation weight modulated by the deviation of the student's GIoU loss from its running average—so the student relies more on its own generalizability on easy frames and more closely follows the teacher on hard frames. The two-phase pipeline (teacher ORR training → student AFKD training) yields ORTrack-D, which achieves ~30–36% speedup over the teacher with only ~1–2% precision loss.

## Results

On four UAV tracking benchmarks (DTB70, UAVDT, VisDrone2018, UAV123), ORTrack-DeiT achieves the best average precision of 85.6% and average success rate of 65.0% among all lightweight trackers, while running at 226.4 FPS on GPU. The distilled variant ORTrack-D-DeiT further boosts speed to 292.3 GPU FPS (36% faster) with only a 1.9% precision drop (83.7%). On VisDrone2018, ORTrack-DeiT reaches 88.6% precision at 206.2 FPS, outperforming the closest deep tracker AQATrack (87.2% at 53.4 FPS) by 1.4% precision and 4× speed. The ORR component alone contributes +4.8% precision on UAVDT for the DeiT backbone, and the spatial Cox process masking outperforms uniform masking by 1.9% precision on VisDrone2018.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ORTrack-DeiT outperforms the closest ViT-based competitor Aba-ViTrack [44] by 0.1% average precision (85.6% vs 85.5%) and 0.5% average success rate (65.0% vs 64.5%), while running 23.6% faster on GPU (226.4 vs 181.5 FPS). Against the best DCF-based tracker RACF [41], ORTrack-DeiT gains 9.7% precision and 13.2% success rate on average, and against the best CNN-based tracker DRCI [93], it gains 4.2% precision and 4.9% success rate. On VisDrone2018, ORTrack-DeiT (88.6% Prec., 206.2 FPS) surpasses all 14 deep trackers in Table 2: it beats AQATrack [87] by 1.4% precision at 4× the speed, HIPTrack [2] by 1.9% precision at 6.6× the speed, and ROMTrack [3] by 2.2% precision at 4× the speed. The distilled ORTrack-D-DeiT achieves 292.3 GPU FPS—faster than DRCI (281.3 FPS) and Aba-ViTrack (181.5 FPS)—while retaining 83.7% average precision, only 1.9% below the full ORTrack-DeiT. On the partial-occlusion subset of VisDrone2018, ORTrack-DeiT reaches 85.0% precision, narrowly trailing AQATrack by 0.2%, but the ORR component contributes a 6.9% precision gain over the unenhanced baseline, confirming its specific benefit under occlusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-stream ViT-based tracking paradigm established by OSTrack and MixFormer, and the lightweight UAV-specific tracker Aba-VTrack, by addressing two gaps: occlusion robustness and inference efficiency. For occlusion handling, it departs from uniform random masking (as in MAE) by introducing a spatial Cox process to model spatially inhomogeneous, target-centered occlusion patterns — a probabilistic tool borrowed from spatial statistics and applied here for the first time in visual tracking. For efficiency, it builds on feature-based knowledge distillation but introduces an adaptive weighting mechanism driven by GIoU loss deviation, distinguishing it from fixed-weight distillation used in prior Siamese tracker compression work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of the ORR component (no architectural changes, no inference overhead) makes it immediately applicable to existing ViT trackers, as demonstrated by its integration into ARTrack, GRM, and DropTrack. The adaptive distillation strategy addresses a practical concern — that rigid teacher-mimicking can hurt generalizability on easier samples — and the resulting 30–36% speedup with <2% accuracy loss is meaningful for battery-constrained UAV deployment. Open questions include whether the Cox process parameters (particularly the Poisson expectation ζ) transfer well across domains, whether the approach generalizes to multi-object or long-term tracking, and whether the invariance objective could be extended beyond MSE to contrastive or information-theoretic losses.
