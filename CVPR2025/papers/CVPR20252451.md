## Problem

Current preference optimization methods for text-to-image diffusion models rely on expensive human-annotated paired preference data (e.g., Diffusion-DPO) or, when using reward models, only exploit the pairwise preference distribution while ignoring the richer reward signal. These approaches also lack generalization to multi-reward settings and struggle with inconsistencies between different reward models, leading to reward hacking and suboptimal trade-offs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252451/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a generated RGB image via a fine-tuned diffusion model (SDXL or SD3-M). During training, N=16 images are generated per prompt from 100K DiffusionDB prompts using the pretrained model, and three reward models (MPS for overall quality, VQAscore for image-text alignment, and VILA for aesthetics) score each image. The fine-tuned model is evaluated on Parti and DPG-Bench prompts, with performance measured via win-rates against the base model and standard T2I benchmarks (GenEval, T2I-Compbench).

## Method

CaPO introduces three key components. First, reward calibration replaces raw reward values with the expected win-rate against the reference model, approximated by averaging pairwise Bradley-Terry win-rates across N generated samples, yielding a bounded [0,1] signal that is robust to reward scale differences. Second, the CaPO loss is a regression objective that matches the difference in calibrated rewards of a (positive, negative) pair to the difference in implicit log-likelihood ratios of the diffusion model, generalizing IPO by using a dynamic target rather than a fixed one. Third, for multi-reward optimization, frontier-based rejection sampling (FRS) uses non-dominated sorting to identify upper and lower Pareto frontiers across all reward dimensions, selecting positive samples from the upper frontier and negative samples from the lower frontier, enabling joint optimization without hand-tuned reward weights. A sigmoid log-SNR weighting is applied to the loss to regularize with a weighted ELBO.

## Results

On SDXL with multi-reward fine-tuning (MPS, VQAscore, VILA), CaPO with frontier-based rejection sampling (FRS) achieves 61.2% MPS win-rate, 54.6% VQA win-rate, and 79.2% VILA win-rate over the base model, outperforming DPO+FRS (58.1/52.9/78.6) and IPO+FRS (57.8/52.0/74.4). On SD3-M, CaPO+FRS reaches 59.0% MPS, 55.7% VQA, and 69.3% VILA win-rates. On GenEval, CaPO improves overall score from 0.55→0.59 (SDXL) and 0.68→0.71 (SD3-M), and on T2I-Compbench, CaPO+SD3-M (0.788 color, 0.572 shape) approaches SD3.5-L (0.763, 0.602) while surpassing FLUX-dev on several compositional metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CaPO consistently outperforms DPO [63] across all three reward models in both single- and multi-reward settings: on SDXL, CaPO+FRS achieves 61.2% MPS vs. 58.1% for DPO+FRS, and 79.2% VILA vs. 78.6%, with the gap widening on VQA (54.6% vs. 52.9%). Against IPO [3], CaPO shows larger gains (e.g., 61.2% vs. 57.8% MPS on SDXL), while IPO is slightly more robust to reward hacking (e.g., DPO drops VQA to 46.4% when trained on VILA, whereas IPO stays at 48.5%). In the multi-reward setting, frontier-based rejection sampling (FRS) outperforms both sum-of-rewards and rewarded soups [50] across all objectives, with CaPO+FRS beating CaPO+SOUP by 1.8% MPS and 1.6% VILA on SDXL. On T2I benchmarks (Table 3), CaPO+SD3-M (GenEval 0.71) surpasses SD3.5-L (0.69) and approaches FLUX-dev (0.66 overall but 0.98 single-object), while CaPO+SDXL (0.59) improves over base SDXL (0.55) but remains below FLUX-dev. In the direct comparison with Diffusion-DPO trained on human-annotated Pick-a-pic data (Table 4), CaPO strictly outperforms on all four metrics (Pickscore 22.83 vs. 22.71, MPS 11.71 vs. 11.59, VQA 0.838 vs. 0.834, VILA 6.141 vs. 6.049), demonstrating that synthetic reward-based data with proper calibration can match or exceed human-annotated preference data.

## Relation to Prior Work

CaPO sits at the intersection of three prior threads: Diffusion-DPO's derivation of a tractable preference optimization objective for diffusion models, IPO's general regression-based formulation that replaces the binary classification loss with a squared-error target, and PRDP's use of reward-difference regression for large-scale diffusion fine-tuning. The key conceptual departure is the reward calibration step—converting raw Bradley-Terry scores into expected win-rates against the reference model—which provides a bounded, scale-invariant target that neither DPO (which uses raw reward differences implicitly) nor IPO (which fixes the target at 1) offers. For the multi-reward setting, the paper departs from the common weighted-sum or model-soup approaches by introducing frontier-based rejection sampling grounded in non-dominated sorting, directly targeting Pareto optimality rather than a scalarized proxy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that CaPO enables alignment of large-scale T2I diffusion models (SDXL, SD3-M) using only synthetic data and off-the-shelf reward models, removing the bottleneck of expensive human preference annotation while still improving on Diffusion-DPO trained on Pick-a-pic. The multi-reward Pareto formulation is directly relevant to production settings where one must balance aesthetics, text alignment, and overall quality simultaneously. However, the method remains offline (data generated once from the reference model), which the authors acknowledge limits convergence speed, and some improvements (e.g., text rendering in SDXL) are bounded by the base model's capacity. Extending to online or iterative data generation, and to more than three reward signals with potentially conflicting objectives, are natural open questions.
