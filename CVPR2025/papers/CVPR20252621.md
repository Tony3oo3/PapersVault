## Problem

Existing learning-based 3D object canonicalization methods require substantial per-category training samples (e.g., ~200 priors for ConDor to converge), but real-world datasets like Objaverse-LVIS follow a long-tail distribution where over 93% of categories have fewer than 200 objects. This paper addresses the gap by introducing the first one-shot category-level canonicalization framework that requires only a single canonical reference model per category, operating under arbitrary poses without any training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252621/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two 3D mesh inputs: a single canonical prior model per category and a test model in an arbitrary pose. Translation and scale are handled trivially via bounding-box centering and normalization; the core output is a canonical rotation matrix (computed via Lie algebra optimization) that aligns the test model's orientation with the prior. The prior model is rendered into 10 multi-view RGB images for semantic perception, and the test model is initialized in multiple candidate poses derived from its convex hull support planes before alignment.

## Method

The framework operates in three stages without any training. First, a zero-shot 3D semantic perception module uses ChatGPT to generate part labels and GLIP to detect 2D parts across 10 rendered views, projecting detections into 3D vertex-level semantic confidence vectors. A support-plane strategy computes the object's convex hull and identifies stable resting orientations to generate multiple initial pose hypotheses, mitigating GLIP's pose-dependent detection errors. Second, a joint energy function combines a semantic constraint (cosine similarity of 3D Gaussian distributions per part label for coarse alignment) with a geometric constraint (Chamfer distance for fine alignment) via a Gaussian mixture model formulation, optimized with Levenberg-Marquardt. Third, a semantic relationship map partitions canonical space into B³ blocks, assigns dominant semantic labels per block, and selects the best hypothesis by cosine similarity between prior and test semantic maps, with a final geometric tiebreaker among the top-5 candidates.

## Results

Using only a single prior model per category (vs. 10 for all baselines), the proposed method achieves average GEC of 0.784 on ShapeNet (13 categories), 0.129 on NOCS (6 categories), and 0.208 on DREDS (7 categories), representing 63–85% improvements over the best existing method (ConDor) on the GEC metric. On ShapeNet, the method reduces average IC from 1.401 (ConDor) to 0.703 and GEC from 2.162 to 0.784. On the real-world DREDS dataset, average GEC drops from 1.158 (ConDor) to 0.208, demonstrating robustness to scanning noise and missing textures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three self-supervised canonicalization methods—CaCa [24], ConDor [21], and ShapeMatcher [9]—all of which require 10 prior models per category, whereas the proposed method needs only 1. The proposed method wins decisively on every dataset and every metric: on ShapeNet it reduces average GEC by 63% over ConDor (0.784 vs. 2.162) and by 74% over ShapeMatcher (0.784 vs. 3.032); on NOCS it cuts GEC from 0.876 (ConDor) to 0.129 (an 85% reduction); and on DREDS it lowers GEC from 1.158 (ConDor) to 0.208 (an 82% reduction). ConDor is the strongest baseline, particularly on NOCS where its GEC of 0.876 is competitive, but it still trails the proposed method by a factor of ~7. ShapeMatcher performs worst overall, with GEC values exceeding 2.0 on both ShapeNet and DREDS, suggesting its geometric-only alignment struggles with intra-category shape variation. The consistent margin across simulated (ShapeNet) and real-world (NOCS, DREDS) data indicates the advantage is not dataset-specific but stems from the semantic-geometric joint energy formulation and the one-shot prior requirement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-supervised 3D canonicalization line established by ConDor (Sajnani et al.) and Canonical Fields (Agaram et al.), which learn category-level canonical fields from many training samples using geometric constraints (Chamfer distance). The key departure is replacing the multi-prior, geometry-only paradigm with a one-shot framework that requires only a single canonical reference model and augments geometric alignment with zero-shot semantic cues extracted via LLMs (ChatGPT) and VLMs (GLIP). The support-plane initialization strategy and semantic relationship map for hypothesis selection are novel components not present in prior canonicalization work, addressing the chicken-and-egg problem between pose estimation and reliable semantic perception.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: the resulting Canonical Objaverse Dataset (COD) with 1,054 categories and 32K shapes is the largest canonical 3D dataset by category count, directly addressing the long-tail distribution problem that limits prior learning-based methods (over 93% of Objaverse-LVIS categories lack the 200+ samples ConDor needs). A key limitation is the dependence on external LLM/VLM services for semantic perception, which introduces noise and potential failure modes for unusual categories. Open questions include whether the one-shot approach degrades for categories with extreme intra-category shape variation, and whether the semantic perception quality bottleneck (zero-shot GLIP detection) can be further improved without task-specific fine-tuning.
