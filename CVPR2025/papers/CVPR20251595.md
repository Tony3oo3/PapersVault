## Problem

Diffusion models are typically trained individually for specific tasks, relying on external add-ons (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter), task-specific architectures, and tightly tuned training recipes that do not generalize across tasks. This paper asks whether a single unified diffusion model can support both image synthesis and understanding—spanning text-to-image, multi-view generation, depth estimation, segmentation, and ID customization—without any task-specific modules or auxiliary losses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251595/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

All modalities (text, images, depth maps, poses, semantic maps, bounding boxes, camera poses) are unified into a variable-length sequence of N 'views' (N=1 for T2I, N=2 for image-to-image, N=4–12 for multi-view), where each view is independently noised during training. At inference, any subset of views is fixed at timestep 0 as conditioning while the remaining views are denoised from pure Gaussian noise, enabling arbitrary input-output combinations. Camera poses are encoded as Plücker ray embeddings and treated as an additional view in the sequence rather than concatenated channels, and the task is specified via special tokens (e.g., [[multiview]], [[faceid]]) prepended to the text prompt. The model operates at resolutions from 256² to 1024² and generalizes zero-shot to unseen resolutions via 3D RoPE.

## Method

OneDiffusion models all conditions and targets as a sequence of N views, each with an independently sampled noise level t_i ~ LogNorm(0,1), and trains a single velocity field v_θ(t_1,…,t_N, x_1,…,x_N) via a joint flow-matching objective with linear interpolation (x_i^{t_i} = t_i·x_i + (1−t_i)·ε_i). The architecture is a 2.8B-parameter Next-DiT transformer that encodes each view into a VAE latent, concatenates them along the sequence dimension, and uses 3D RoPE for resolution-agnostic positional encoding—no adapters, external encoders, or auxiliary losses are required. At inference, conditional views are held at t=0 while target views are integrated from t=1 to t=0 via an ODE solver, making any view a valid condition or output. Training proceeds in three stages (256² pretraining, 512² multi-task mixing, 1024² T2I fine-tuning) with in-batch task sampling at equal probability, and the model is trained entirely from scratch on the One-Gen dataset (~75M samples).

## Results

On the GenEval text-to-image benchmark (1024×1024), OneDiffusion achieves a score of 0.65 with 2.8B parameters trained on only 75M samples, outperforming similarly sized models like SD3-medium (0.62, 1000M data) and Hunyuan-DiT (0.63) while remaining competitive with much larger FLUX models (0.67–0.71, 12B params). For multiview generation on Google Scanned Objects, OneDiffusion achieves PSNR of 21.79 with 3 known-pose views, approaching EscherNet's 24.09 while uniquely supporting unknown-pose conditioning. On depth estimation, OneDiffusion reports AbsRel of 6.8 (δ₁=95.2) on NYUv2 and 29.4 (δ₁=75.2) on DIODE, competitive with the diffusion-based Marigold (6.0/95.9 and 31.0/77.2). For ID customization on Unsplash-50, OneDiffusion scores ID=0.283 and CLIP-T=26.80, trailing PuLID (0.654/31.23) on the standard benchmark but demonstrating superior qualitative generalization to expression changes, viewpoint shifts, and non-human subjects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On GenEval, OneDiffusion (0.65) clearly outperforms all sub-2B-parameter baselines (LUMINA-Next 0.46, PixArt-S 0.54) and same-scale models (SDXL 0.55, SD3-medium 0.62, Hunyuan-DiT 0.63), while trailing the 12B FLUX models (0.67–0.71) and DALL·E 3 (0.67) by 0.02–0.06. For multiview generation, OneDiffusion surpasses Zero123 (18.51) and Zero123-XL (18.93) in the 1-view setting (19.01) but falls short of the specialized EscherNet across all view counts (e.g., 21.79 vs. 24.09 PSNR at 3 known-pose views); however, OneDiffusion uniquely handles unknown camera poses, where EscherNet has no counterpart. On ID customization (Unsplash-50), OneDiffusion (ID 0.283, CLIP-T 26.80) loses to InstantID (0.648/26.41) and PuLID (0.654/31.23) on the identity-preservation metric, though it beats PhotoMaker (0.193/27.38) on ID; the authors argue the standard benchmark underestimates OneDiffusion's advantage in expression/viewpoint generalization where all embedding-based methods fail. For depth estimation, OneDiffusion (AbsRel 6.8, δ₁ 95.2 on NYUv2) is close to but slightly behind Marigold (6.0/95.9) and well behind DepthAnything-2 (4.6/97.7), while on DIODE it improves over Marigold on AbsRel (29.4 vs. 31.0) but trails on δ₁ (75.2 vs. 77.2). Overall, the paper positions OneDiffusion as a strong multi-task generalist that matches or approaches task-specific SOTA on each individual benchmark while being the only model to handle all tasks within a single architecture and training objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

OneDiffusion builds on three key threads: the flow-matching training objective (Lipman et al., ICLR 2023), the Next-DiT transformer architecture with 3D RoPE (Zhuo et al., 2024), and the sequential diffusion paradigm of independently sampling noise levels per frame (Diffusion Forcing, Chen et al., 2024). The paper's central conceptual move is to treat every task—text-to-image, image-to-image translation, multi-view generation, ID customization, and image understanding—as a variable-length sequence of 'views' with per-view noise, eliminating the need for task-specific adapters (as in ControlNet, UniControl) or auxiliary identity encoders (as in InstantID, PuLID). This departs from prior unified diffusion efforts that still required modality-specific conditioning modules, and from bidirectional vision-language models (Unified-IO, Transfusion) that couple autoregressive and diffusion objectives rather than unifying purely within the diffusion framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single 2.8B-parameter model trained from scratch on ~75M samples can match or approach specialized methods across at least seven distinct tasks (T2I, multi-view synthesis, depth estimation, semantic segmentation, ID customization, camera pose estimation, image editing) without any task-specific architecture or loss, offering a practical blueprint for unified vision models. However, individual task results are competitive rather than dominant: ID customization scores on Unsplash-50 (0.283) trail PuLID (0.654), and depth estimation (AbsRel 6.8 on NYUv2) lags DepthAnything-2 (4.6), suggesting the unified framework trades peak per-task performance for generality. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video, whether the small training set is a fundamental bottleneck or simply a resource constraint, and whether the 'any view as condition' paradigm can be extended to tasks requiring structured outputs beyond images (e.g., 3D meshes, point clouds).
