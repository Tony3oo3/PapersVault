## Problem

Multi-center star shape priors, which fit the vast majority of real objects, induce a non-smooth shape field function due to the argmin over multiple centers, making them incompatible with stable backpropagation in data-driven segmentation networks. Single-center star shapes are smooth but overly restrictive, while existing multi-center approaches rely on interactive graph cuts or empirically designed sub-networks lacking mathematical interpretability. This paper bridges the gap by constructing a smooth shape prior that retains multi-center expressiveness while being directly embeddable into neural network architectures.

## Input / Output

The method takes 512×512 RGB images (medical or remote sensing) as input and produces a soft segmentation mask u: Ω→[0,1] as output. In addition to the mask, the decoder predicts a 2D vector field s(x) = c_ε(x) − x encoding the CCS shape direction at each pixel, which is supervised against a ground-truth field computed from boundary-sampled center points. The CCS-Sigmoid module operates on the segmentation feature map o produced by the SAM/SAM2 decoder, replacing the standard sigmoid thresholding step.

## Method

The paper defines the Convex Combination Star (CCS) shape by replacing the hard argmin center selection in multi-center star shapes with a softmax-weighted convex combination of centers, yielding a strictly smooth field function c_ε(x) that interpolates between multi-center (ε→0) and single-center centroid (ε→∞) star shapes. A sufficient condition (Theorem 2) constrains the segmentation to CCS shapes via the inequality ⟨∇u(x), s(x)⟩ ≥ 0, which is incorporated into a variational segmentation model solved by a projected gradient descent dual algorithm. The key architectural insight is that the dual algorithm's u-update takes the form of a sigmoid function, so unrolling T iterations of the algorithm produces a CCS-Sigmoid network module that directly replaces the standard sigmoid activation in any segmentation decoder. This module is integrated into SAM/SAM2 by adding a learnable token T_f in the decoder to predict the CCS field s, with the total loss combining a cosine-similarity field loss and cross-entropy segmentation loss. The image encoder is frozen and fine-tuned via LoRA, while the mask decoder and CCS module are fully trained.

## Results

On the ISIC 2018 skin lesion dataset, the CCS module with SAM backbone achieves 89.67 DICE (vs. 88.81 for fine-tuned SAM), and with SAM2 backbone achieves 88.30 DICE (vs. 87.33). On the REFUGE optic cup dataset, the CCS module with SAM yields 80.82 DICE (vs. 77.73), a 3.09-point gain. On the WHU Building remote sensing dataset, the CCS module with SAM2 reaches 93.32 DICE (vs. 92.33 for fine-tuned SAM2). Inference overhead is negligible: FPS drops from 16.55 to 14.42 and FLOPs remain essentially unchanged (651.34G → 651.38G).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: fine-tuned SAM (ViT-B) and fine-tuned SAM2 (Hiera-Base), both without any shape prior. The CCS module consistently outperforms both baselines across all four datasets. On ISIC, the CCS module with SAM improves DICE by 0.86 points (89.67 vs. 88.81) and with SAM2 by 0.97 points (88.30 vs. 87.33). The largest gains appear on REFUGE (optic cup), where SAM+CCS module improves DICE by 3.09 points (80.82 vs. 77.73) and SAM2+CCS module by 2.10 points (81.15 vs. 79.05), suggesting the star-shape prior is most beneficial for small, roughly convex structures. On Kvasir (polyp segmentation), SAM+CCS module gains 2.09 DICE points (86.91 vs. 84.82). On WHU Building, SAM2+CCS module achieves the best DICE of 93.32 vs. 92.33 for fine-tuned SAM2. The CCS loss variant also improves over baselines but is consistently slightly below the CCS module, indicating that architectural integration of the shape prior is more effective than loss-only enforcement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the variational star-shape segmentation line initiated by Veksler (single-center, graph-cut) and Gulshan (multi-center, graph-cut) into the deep learning regime, building directly on Liu et al.'s [22] unrolling of a single-center star-shape variational model into a network module. The key departure is addressing the non-smoothness of the multi-center star field (which breaks backpropagation) by introducing the convex combination star (CCS) shape—a smooth, strictly concave approximation of the min-operator that interpolates between single-center and multi-center star shapes via a temperature parameter ε. The CCS-Sigmoid module is then derived by unrolling the dual algorithm of a variational problem, positioning it as a principled replacement for the standard sigmoid activation in SAM/SAM2 decoders rather than an ad-hoc sub-network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a mathematically grounded, interpretable mechanism for injecting shape priors into modern foundation-model segmentation pipelines (SAM, SAM2), addressing a gap where prior shape-aware deep methods lacked theoretical justification. Theorem 1 (universal shape prior) and the negligible computational overhead (FLOPs essentially unchanged) make the CCS module a practical drop-in component. However, the proof that the field condition preserves CCS shape for finite ε remains open, and the tested domains (medical lesions, buildings) are relatively shape-regular; generalization to highly irregular or multi-object natural scenes is unverified. The modest 1–3% DICE gains over strong SAM/SAM2 baselines suggest the prior is most beneficial in ambiguous-boundary cases rather than as a universal accuracy booster.
