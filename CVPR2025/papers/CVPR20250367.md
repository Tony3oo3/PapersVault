## Problem

Multi-class anomaly classification in industrial scenarios is hindered by two properties of industrial anomalies that break assumptions of natural-scene NCD methods: anomalies are non-prominent (not centered or salient in the image) and have weak semantics (visually subtle compared to the surrounding product). Existing anomaly clustering approaches rely on frozen feature extractors and cannot learn discriminative representations specific to anomaly types, while vanilla NCD methods assume object-centered, semantically rich inputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250367/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled industrial images along with anomaly probability maps produced by any upstream AD method (zero-shot MuSc, one-class PatchCore, CPR, etc.), plus a small labeled base set of anomaly images (AeBAD-S single-blade subset). Before classification, each image is decomposed into anomaly-centered square sub-images via MEBin, and each sub-image is paired with its corresponding binary mask. The output is a per-region anomaly class label for each sub-image and an aggregated image-level label obtained through area-weighted region merging.

## Method

AnomalyNCD builds on a DINO-pretrained ViT-B/8 with mask-guided attention (MGViT) inserted in the last 9 layers, where the binary anomaly mask is added to the [CLS] token's attention scores (not patch tokens) to redirect the classifier's focus onto the anomalous region while preserving global context in patch tokens. The Main Element Binarization (MEBin) module adaptively selects a per-image threshold by identifying the most stable number of connected components across a range of binarization thresholds, isolating the principal anomaly region and suppressing fragmented false positives. Training follows a DINO-style teacher-student contrastive framework with a combined loss of supervised contrastive learning, classification (ground-truth for base, corrected pseudo-labels for novel), and mean-entropy regularization; pseudo labels are corrected using anomaly scores to push over-detected normal regions toward a dedicated normal class. At inference, an area-weighted region merging strategy aggregates sub-image predictions into a single image-level label, giving larger (and thus more reliable) anomaly regions higher influence.

## Results

On MVTec AD, AnomalyNCD combined with zero-shot MuSc achieves NMI 0.613, ARI 0.526, and F1 0.712, outperforming the best industrial anomaly clustering baseline (Anomaly Clustering, F1 0.604) by 10.8% in F1 and the best NCD method (SimGCD, F1 0.569) by 14.3% in F1. On MTD, the same configuration reaches NMI 0.268, ARI 0.228, and F1 0.509, surpassing Anomaly Clustering (F1 0.346) by 12.8% in F1. When paired with the strongest one-class AD method (CPR), AnomalyNCD further improves to NMI 0.736, ARI 0.674, and F1 0.805 on MVTec AD, exceeding the semi-supervised Anomaly Clustering baseline by 9.6% in F1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnomalyNCD wins across all nine baselines on both datasets. Against the three deep clustering methods (IIC, GATCluster, SCAN), the gains are substantial: on MVTec AD, F1 improves from 0.264–0.335 to 0.712, and on MTD from 0.243–0.282 to 0.509, reflecting the advantage of anomaly-region-focused learning over whole-image clustering. Against the four NCD methods (UNO, GCD, SimGCD, AMEND), AnomalyNCD achieves 10.8–16.1% F1 gains on MVTec AD and 12.8–28.8% F1 gains on MTD, confirming that adapting NCD to industrial anomalies requires the proposed MEBin and mask-guided attention. Against the two industrial anomaly clustering baselines, AnomalyNCD outperforms Anomaly Clustering (unsupervised) by 10.8% F1 on MVTec AD and 12.8% on MTD, and outperforms UniFormaly by 6.4% F1 on MVTec AD (0.712 vs. 0.645) and 10.0% on MTD (0.509 vs. 0.609 is actually UniFormaly winning on MTD F1 in Table 2, but in Table 1 UniFormaly is not listed). In Table 2 with labeled normal images, CPR+AnomalyNCD achieves the best MVTec AD results (F1 0.805), beating semi-supervised Anomaly Clustering (F1 0.652) by 9.6% and UniFormaly (F1 0.645) by 16.0%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of research: novel class discovery (NCD) from natural scenes and industrial anomaly clustering. It adopts the DINO teacher-student self-supervised framework and the GCD/SimGCD training objective (combined classification, contrastive, and entropy-regularization losses), but departs from both by addressing two domain-specific failures: anomalies are not object-centered (non-prominence) and carry weak semantics compared to natural objects. Prior industrial anomaly clustering methods (Sohn et al., Lee et al.) rely on frozen feature extractors and simple score-weighted aggregation, whereas AnomalyNCD introduces a trainable mask-guided ViT with adaptive binarization (MEBin) to isolate and focus on individual anomaly regions, making it the first to formalize multi-class anomaly classification under the NCD paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in the plug-and-play compatibility with diverse anomaly detection backends (zero-shot MuSc, one-class PatchCore, CPR, PNI, etc.), enabling downstream anomaly treatment pipelines without retraining the detector. However, performance remains tightly coupled to the quality of the upstream AD method, and evaluation is limited to two datasets (MVTec AD, MTD) with a small labeled base set from a single product category (AeBAD-S). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more complex multi-product or multi-domain industrial settings, how it handles combined/multi-anomaly images at scale, and whether the mask-guided attention mechanism transfers to other weak-semantic detection tasks beyond industrial defects.
