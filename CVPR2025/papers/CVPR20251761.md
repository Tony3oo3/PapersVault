## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient tuning (PET) methods reduce storage and parameter costs but still inherit or exceed the inference latency of their backbone models due to additional adapter modules, making them impractical for compute-constrained deployments. No prior PET approach achieves inference faster than the original pre-trained model while maintaining competitive accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images patchified into 197 tokens (196 patch tokens + 1 CLS token) for ViT-B/16 with 16×16 patches, and outputs a class label via the standard classification head. The token merging module reduces the token sequence from 197 to 99 tokens at the 6th transformer layer, and the entire module is plug-and-play: it can be inserted into any existing PET method (LoRA, AdaptFormer, Bi-LoRA, etc.) without modifying their adapter architecture or training procedure.

## Method

FPET introduces a fully differentiable token redundancy reduction module applied once at the middle layer of the ViT backbone. It divides tokens into two sets using a checkerboard spatial pattern (rather than alternating indices), refines the self-attention keys with a lightweight learnable adapter to compute a more accurate similarity matrix, and replaces the non-differentiable max/top-k matching of ToMe with a sigmoid-thresholded soft matching matrix. A straight-through estimator (STE) makes the hard matching matrix differentiable so gradients flow back to the key-refinement adapter, while gradient stopping prevents the resulting push-and-pull token interactions from propagating into the frozen backbone. This single-layer, half-token merging strategy (98 tokens merged at layer 6) yields ~20% faster inference and ~24% fewer FLOPs than the backbone with negligible accuracy loss (<0.1%).

## Results

On VTAB-1K, FPET applied to Bi-AdaptFormer achieves 77.0% average accuracy (matching the original Bi-AdaptFormer) while reducing inference time to 2.17 ms (−17.2% vs. full fine-tuning's 2.62 ms), FLOPs to 13.5 G (−23.3%), and GPU memory to 6.2 GB (−47.8%). Across all five PET backbones tested, FPET consistently delivers ~20% faster inference and ~24% fewer FLOPs relative to the original PET implementations, with accuracy drops of at most 0.1%. Training time is also reduced by ~19% (e.g., 4.52 s/it vs. 5.62 s/it for AdaptFormer).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FPET matches the accuracy of the original PET methods it is applied to (e.g., Bi-AdaptFormer 77.0%, AdaptFormer 76.2%, RepAdapter 76.1%) while achieving ~20% faster inference and ~24% fewer FLOPs, with accuracy drops of ≤0.1%. Compared to no-latency PET methods (LoRA, SSF, BitFit, RepAdapter at 2.62 ms), FPET is strictly faster (2.10–2.17 ms) while maintaining equal or higher accuracy. Against SynQT [39], FPET-Bi-AdaptFormer achieves 4.1% higher accuracy (77.0% vs. 72.9%) and is 0.66 ms faster in inference (2.17 ms vs. 2.83 ms), while using far fewer learnable parameters (0.64 M vs. 2.72 M). Against Pruned RepAdapter [7], FPET-RepAdapter delivers 1.3% higher accuracy (76.1% vs. 74.8%) with consistent efficiency gains across all datasets, whereas Pruned RepAdapter's gains vary from 59% (EuroSAT) to 0% (dSpr-Ori, SNORB-Ele) depending on the dataset. Among token reduction baselines (Table 3), FPET's differentiable matching with gradient stopping yields only −0.05% accuracy drop versus −0.27% for ToMe and −0.38% for LTMP, while also reducing GPU memory by 1.41 GB (vs. −0.21 GB for ToMe and +5.84 GB for LTMP).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: parameter-efficient tuning (PET) methods like LoRA, AdaptFormer, and Bi-AdaptFormer that minimize trainable parameters, and token reduction techniques like ToMe's bipartite soft matching and LTMP's learned-threshold merging that accelerate ViT inference. The key departure is that prior token merging was designed for full-tuning or training-free settings and is non-differentiable, while prior PET methods focus exclusively on storage efficiency and inherit or exceed the backbone's inference latency. FPET repurposes token merging as a plug-and-play module for PET, making the matching process fully differentiable via a straight-through estimator, refining token similarity with a lightweight adapter, and placing the merge at a single middle layer rather than gradually across all layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that FPET achieves ~20% faster inference than the unmodified backbone while preserving SOTA PET accuracy, which is notable because most PET methods can at best match backbone speed. The plug-and-play design means any existing PET method can immediately benefit without architectural redesign, lowering the barrier for deployment on resource-constrained platforms. Limitations include evaluation restricted to image classification on VTAB-1K, and the single-layer merging strategy may not generalize to tasks requiring fine-grained spatial detail (e.g., segmentation, detection). Open questions include whether the approach extends to generative or video tasks, and whether more aggressive multi-layer merging with differentiable matching could yield further gains without the accuracy trade-offs observed here.
