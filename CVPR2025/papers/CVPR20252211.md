## Problem

Current image inpainting and editing methods are designed for single-object modifications and fail at sequential editing: they cannot maintain previously placed objects while naturally integrating new elements into the existing scene. This limitation is compounded by the requirement for precise segmentation masks and the need to regenerate the entire image for each modification, making complex multi-object iterative editing impractical.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252211/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of text prompts (one for the background, one per object) paired with rough (imprecise) masks that define each object's region of interest, along with the full editing history stored in layer-wise memory (denoising latents at every timestep, masks, and prompt embeddings from all prior steps). The mask order — the sequence in which objects are added — directly encodes instance/occlusion order: an overlapping mask for a later object places it in front of earlier ones. Output is a fully composed image at 512×512 or 1024×1024 resolution, generated train-free on PixArt-α with 20 DPM-Solver denoising steps.

## Method

The framework introduces three train-free components operating on PixArt-α's DiT architecture. (1) Layer-wise Memory stores, for each editing step, the prompt embedding, the full set of denoising latents across all timesteps, and the mask, enabling the model to reference prior edits without re-computation. (2) Background Consistency Guidance (BCG) replaces the expensive forward pass on the original image (required by Blended Latent Diffusion) with direct retrieval of stored latents, performing selective latent blending Z_i = Z_{i-1} ⊙ (1−m_i) + Z_i ⊙ m_i to preserve unedited regions while updating only the masked area, yielding ~10% speedup per edit. (3) Multi-Query Disentangled Cross-Attention (MQD) splits cross-attention into per-object queries: the current object attends to its own mask region, each prior object attends to its non-overlapping residual region (m_j minus all later masks), and the background attends to the inverse of the union of all masks — ensuring each object's appearance is conditioned only on its relevant spatial context while respecting occlusion order. For object deletion, the method blends latents from the step before the target object with the current step starting at an intermediate timestep τ=8 (of 20), then switches to vanilla denoising, while MQD excludes the deleted object's mask and prompt to remove its influence.

## Results

On the proposed Multi-Edit Bench, the method (1024×1024) achieves BLEU-2/3/4 of 64.99/47.69/36.59, METEOR of 0.1513, and CLIPcrop of 64.29, outperforming all image editing and layout-to-image baselines on semantic alignment metrics. At 512×512, it scores 61.19/45.04/34.06 BLEU and 0.1465 METEOR, still leading all baselines. In the human preference study (50 participants, 5-point Likert), the method scores 4.59 on background consistency, 4.28 on natural adaptation, and 4.49 on text-scene alignment, versus 3.71/2.81/3.08 for HD-Painter and 3.43/2.24/2.04 for BLD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms HD-Painter [32] (the strongest editing baseline) by +1.70 BLEU-2, +0.06 METEOR, and +0.20 CLIPcrop at 1024×1024, with the gap widening in the human study (4.59 vs 3.71 on background consistency). Against BLD [3], the advantage is larger: +9.69 BLEU-2 and +0.0033 METEOR, reflecting BLD's difficulty maintaining consistency across sequential edits. Among layout-to-image baselines, the method beats NoiseCollage [43] by ~4–5 percentage points on BLEU and LooseControl [10] on all three metrics (BLEU, METEOR, CLIPcrop), while the adapted NoiseCollage+ordering† narrows the CLIPcrop gap to 0.19 but still lags by 5 BLEU-2 points. SD3-Inpaint [20] performs worst (29.90 BLEU-2), which the authors attribute to ControlNet-Inpaint's suboptimal behavior in multi-step editing. LayoutGuidance [14] is the weakest baseline overall, trailing the proposed method by over 28 BLEU-2 points, underscoring the difficulty of iterative editing for single-shot layout methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends diffusion-based inpainting (Blended Latent Diffusion, HD-Painter) from single-object modifications to iterative multi-object editing by introducing a 'mask order' concept and a layer-wise memory that stores latents and prompt embeddings across editing steps. It departs from layout-to-image generation (NoiseCollage, LayoutGuidance, LooseControl), which regenerates the entire image per edit, by performing only localized updates via Background Consistency Guidance (skipping redundant forward passes) and Multi-Query Disentangled Cross-Attention, which selectively attends to non-overlapping regions from prior mask orders. The cross-attention design is inspired by NoiseCollage's query disentanglement but generalized from two queries (object + background) to an arbitrary number of sequential mask orders, and the overall framework is built on the PixArt-α Diffusion Transformer without additional training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap—sequential multi-object editing while preserving previously placed content—that neither single-step inpainting nor full-regeneration layout models handle well, and the introduced Multi-Edit Bench provides the first layer-wise evaluation protocol for this setting. Limitations include growing memory and time costs with the number of editing steps (each object requires a separate denoising pass), and the approach is validated only on PixArt-α at 512×512 and 1024×1024, leaving open whether the layer-wise memory and MQD generalize to other diffusion backbones or to very long editing sequences. The modest ~10% per-step efficiency gain from BCG compounds over many edits but does not eliminate the fundamental cost of sequential generation, suggesting future work on amortized or parallelized multi-object placement.
