## Problem

Existing graph-based 3D MOT methods constrain node connectivity with fixed distance thresholds, limiting association to physically proximate objects and degrading performance as the threshold grows. The paper shows that oracle matching improves monotonically with larger thresholds, indicating that objects can be significantly displaced between adjacent frames, yet prior methods cannot effectively exploit the expanded candidate set.

## Input / Output

The tracker consumes per-frame 3D detections (center position, size, rotation, one-hot class, detection score) from an off-the-shelf detector (CenterPoint, BEVFormer v2, or StreamPETR) along with the previous frame's track feature vectors. Geometry attributes are projected into C-dimensional feature vectors via an MLP; velocity is provided by the detector but excluded from the geometry representation. The output is a full association score matrix between all tracks and detections, from which Hungarian matching yields track-to-detection assignments, updated track features, and new track initializations for unmatched detections.

## Method

GRAE-3DMOT introduces a Geometry Relation-Aware Encoder with three components that operate on fully-connected graphs (infinite distance thresholds). The spatial relation-aware encoder builds a complete graph over all current-frame detections, computes distance-aware relation priors via a Distance-Aware Feature Fusion Layer (DFFL) that gates edge features with Sigmoid(Linear(distance)), aggregates them with softmax-weighted sums, and then applies multi-head self-attention on a second graph constructed from the resulting relation features. The spatiotemporal relation-aware encoder builds a bipartite graph between detections and previous-frame tracks (with velocity-compensated positions), applies DFFL to temporal edges, and fuses the temporal priors with spatial relation features via an MLP. Association scores are derived by distributing track features into spatiotemporal features using softmax affinity, then projecting through an MLP; Hungarian matching with a score threshold β finalizes assignments. The model is trained end-to-end with Focal loss on the association score matrix over 8-frame mini-sequences.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set using CenterPoint detections, GRAE-3DMOT achieves 73.7% AMOTA and 0.488 AMOTP, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art 3DMOTFormer (71.2% AMOTA, 0.515 AMOTP) by 2.4 and 2.1 points respectively. On the test set, the method reaches 70.2% AMOTA and 0.475 AMOTP. The method also achieves the best MOTA (63.6%), lowest IDS (150), and lowest FRAG (315) among all compared geometry-based trackers, and runs at 56.19 FPS with only 0.57M parameters, surpassing 3DMOTFormer's 47.24 FPS and 0.74M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRAE-3DMOT outperforms all eight compared geometry-based 3D MOT methods on every reported metric in the nuScenes validation set. Against the closest competitor 3DMOTFormer [9], it gains +2.4 AMOTA, −0.027 AMOTP, +2.9 MOTA, and reduces IDS from 341 to 150 and FRAG from 436 to 315. Over GNN-PMB [16] (70.7% AMOTA), the improvement is +3.0 AMOTA with a dramatic IDS reduction (650→150). Compared to the offline PolarMOT [12] (71.1% AMOTA), GRAE-3DMOT still leads by +2.6 AMOTA while operating fully online. Against the detection-only CenterPoint tracker [31] (66.5% AMOTA), the gain is +7.2 AMOTA, highlighting the value of learned association. The method also generalizes across detectors: with BEVFormer v2 and StreamPETR detections, it consistently outperforms 3DMOTFormer by 1.7–1.5 AMOTA respectively, and cross-detector transfer (e.g., trained on CenterPoint, tested on StreamPETR) incurs only ~0.5 AMOTA degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

GRAE-3DMOT extends the graph-based 3D MOT lineage established by 3DMOTFormer, GNN3DMOT, PolarMOT, and OGR3MOT, all of which construct sparse graphs using fixed class-agnostic distance thresholds to limit which detection-track pairs are considered. The key departure is setting both spatial and temporal distance thresholds to infinity, producing fully connected relation graphs, and compensating for the increased complexity with a multi-head self-attention mechanism and a distance-aware feature fusion layer that implicitly re-weights edges by physical proximity. The method remains within the tracking-by-detection paradigm, consuming geometry attributes from off-the-shelf detectors (CenterPoint, BEVFormer v2, StreamPETR) and performing Hungarian matching on learned association scores, but replaces the handcrafted geometric association metrics of earlier work with end-to-end learned spatiotemporal relation features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that fully connected graphs (infinite distance threshold) consistently outperform all finite-threshold variants challenges a widely adopted design assumption in graph-based 3D MOT that sparsity is necessary for effective association. The method achieves clear SOTA margins (~2% AMOTA) over 3DMOTFormer while being more efficient (fewer parameters, higher FPS), and generalizes across LiDAR- and camera-based detectors, suggesting the learned relation features are robust to detection quality. Open questions include whether the infinite-threshold strategy scales gracefully to scenes with very high object density, whether incorporating appearance cues alongside geometry would further benefit from the same fully-connected formulation, and whether the approach transfers to other tracking benchmarks beyond nuScenes.
