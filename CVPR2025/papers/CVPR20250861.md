## Problem

Existing synthesis-based dataset distillation methods minimize a homogeneous distance where every real instance contributes equally to shaping each synthetic sample, ignoring instance-level relevance and intra-class geometric structure. This uniform allocation causes synthetic sets to converge to the same equivalent center regardless of the underlying distribution, failing to capture fine-grained pairwise relationships between real and synthetic instances.

## Input / Output

The method takes a large-scale labeled RGB dataset (ranging from 32×32 CIFAR to 224×224 ImageNet subsets) and a proxy network (ConvNet-3, ConvNet-4, or ResNetAP-10) as input, and produces a small synthetic dataset at IPC ratios as low as 0.014% (e.g., 1 image per class on MNIST). The synthetic set is initialized with randomly sampled real instances and iteratively refined; the OT-based contribution matrix is recomputed batch-by-batch so the allocation adapts to the latest generated samples. The method is plug-and-play, requiring only a change to the distance measurement term within any existing distillation framework.

## Method

OPTICAL reformulates the standard homogeneous distance minimization into a bi-level optimization via matching-and-approximating. In the matching stage, it computes an optimal transport matrix P that assigns non-uniform contributions from each real instance to each synthetic instance, using a cost matrix derived from multi-scale Gaussian kernels in a Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space to capture high-order distributional nuances. The OT matrix is efficiently approximated via Sinkhorn normalization (T=20 iterations with entropic regularization λ). In the approximating stage, the weighted contribution matrix P modulates the cross-term in the distance objective, so each synthetic sample learns customized knowledge from its most relevant matched real instances rather than a uniform average. This mechanism is compatible with both optimization-oriented (gradient-matching) and distribution-matching-based distillation methods by simply altering the mapping function F(·) in the unified objective.

## Results

OPTICAL is a plug-and-play module applied to five existing distillation methods (DREAM, DM, IDM, M3D, DANCE) across seven datasets. On CIFAR-10 (IPC=10), DM improves from 48.9% to 50.2% (+1.3), M3D from 63.5% to 64.0% (+0.5), and DANCE from 70.8% to 71.1% (+0.3). The largest gains appear on CIFAR-100 (IPC=10) where IDM jumps from 20.1% to 24.4% (+4.3), and on cross-architecture evaluation where DANCE+OPTICAL gains +4.7% (ResNet-10) and +5.5% (DenseNet-121) over DANCE alone at IPC=50. Full-dataset reference accuracies are 84.8% (CIFAR-10) and 56.2% (CIFAR-100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OPTICAL consistently outperforms its host methods across all tested configurations. The most dramatic improvement is IDM on CIFAR-100 (IPC=10), where +4.3% (20.1→24.4) reflects the severe instance-level homogeneity in IDM's basic distribution matching. DM also benefits substantially on SVHN (IPC=10: +1.7) and CIFAR-10 (IPC=10: +1.3), confirming that methods with simpler D_s formulations suffer most from uniform contribution allocation. Among the stronger baselines, DANCE (the best DM-based method at 70.8% on CIFAR-10 IPC=10) improves to 71.1%, and DREAM (the best optimization-oriented method at 51.1%) reaches 52.0%. In cross-architecture settings (distilled with ConvNet-3, evaluated with ResNet-10 or DenseNet-121), gains amplify to +4.7% and +5.5% for DANCE at IPC=50, suggesting OPTICAL's OT-based matching captures architecture-agnostic geometric structure. Against non-plug-in baselines like MTT (65.3%), FTD (66.6%), and ATT (67.7%) on CIFAR-10 IPC=10, DANCE+OPTICAL (71.1%) remains the strongest, while M3D+OPTICAL (64.0%) trails these trajectory-based methods but leads all DM-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

OPTICAL repurposes Optimal Transport from its traditional role as a distributional distance metric (as in SinkD and Wasserstein-based methods) into a contribution allocation mechanism that reweights real-to-synthetic instance pairs during distillation. It identifies a shared structural limitation across all synthesis-based distillation methods—both optimization-oriented (DC, DREAM) and distribution-matching (DM, M3D, DANCE, IDM)—namely that homogeneous distance minimization assigns uniform weights to all real instances, effectively collapsing intra-class geometric structure. The bi-level matching-and-approximating formulation is novel in its use of the OT matrix as a dynamic, batch-updated weighting scheme rather than a fixed loss term, and the RKHS projection via multi-scale Gaussian kernels extends the cost matrix beyond simple Euclidean or cosine distances.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design means OPTICAL can be layered onto any existing synthesis-based distillation pipeline without architectural changes, making it immediately adoptable by the dataset distillation community. The cross-architecture evaluation results (gains up to +5.5% when distillation and evaluation networks differ) suggest the method captures more transferable geometric structure rather than architecture-specific shortcuts, which is practically important for deployment scenarios. However, typical gains remain modest (0.1–2.8%), and the method adds per-batch Sinkhorn iterations that, while cheap (~0.03s), accumulate over long training runs. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-image modalities (text, audio), whether it benefits more substantially at very low IPC (1–2), and whether the Hilbert-space projection is necessary or whether simpler cost matrices suffice at scale.
