## Problem

Most RAG frameworks assume text-only context, but real-world documents are visually-rich (charts, tables, slides, webpages) with mixed modalities and diverse formats, causing information loss when parsed to text. Existing visual document retrieval benchmarks (ViDoRe, Dureader_vis) suffer from context-dependent questions, limited document types, or single-hop-only reasoning, leaving a gap for open-domain, multi-hop document QA that requires visual semantic search.

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural-language question and a corpus of document images (PDFs, PPTX slides, webpages, charts, tables) as input. Documents are consumed as raw images via dynamic cropping into 336×336 patches to preserve aspect ratios, bypassing OCR-based text extraction. The output consists of top-k retrieved document images (default k=3) ranked by cosine similarity, followed by a generated text answer that can be extractive (span, list) or abstractive (arithmetic, counting).

## Method

VDocRAG comprises a dual-encoder VDocRetriever and a VDocGenerator, both built on a pre-trained LVLM (Phi-3V) with LoRA adaptation. The key contribution is two self-supervised pre-training tasks that compress the full image representation into a single <EOS> token: (1) RCR uses InfoNCE contrastive loss to align document image embeddings with their OCR text, and (2) RCG applies a customized attention mask so that OCR token generation attends only to the <EOS> token, forcing all visual information to be pooled there. This bridges the gap between the LVLM's generative next-token objective and the retrieval task's need for a compact dense embedding. The retriever is then fine-tuned with contrastive learning on query-document pairs, and the generator is fine-tuned with next-token prediction on the retrieved document images concatenated with the question.

## Results

On retrieval (nDCG@5), VDocRetriever with pre-training achieves 86.0 on ChartQA (single-pool) and 77.3 on SlideVQA (single-pool), outperforming the strongest text-based retriever NV-Embed-v2 (75.3 / 61.7) and the visual document retriever DSE (72.7 / 73.0) by 10.7 and 4.3 points respectively on ChartQA/SlideVQA. On the end-to-end DocumentVQA task, VDocRAG achieves 52.0 on ChartQA and 44.2 on SlideVQA (single-pool), surpassing text-based RAG (28.0 / 28.6) by 24.0 and 15.6 points. With ground-truth documents, VDocRAG reaches 74.0 on ChartQA, confirming a higher upper bound than text-based RAG (36.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VDocRetriever outperforms all text-based retrievers (BM25, Contriever, E5, GTE, E5-Mistral, NV-Embed-v2) by 10–30 nDCG@5 points on zero-shot datasets (ChartQA, SlideVQA), with the largest gap over BM25 (+31.2 on ChartQA single-pool). Against the visual document retriever DSE (same Phi3V backbone, 13.7× more fine-tuning data), VDocRetriever wins by +13.3 on ChartQA and +4.3 on SlideVQA (single-pool), demonstrating the benefit of the proposed pre-training tasks. VisRAG-Ret achieves a slightly higher ChartQA single-pool score (87.2 vs. 86.0) but only on data with available training samples (*); on truly zero-shot SlideVQA, VDocRetriever leads by +3.0 (77.3 vs. 74.3). CLIP, the off-the-shelf image encoder, is the weakest baseline, trailing VDocRetriever by 31+ points on most datasets, confirming that LLM-based encoding is critical. On the end-to-end QA task (Table 4), VDocRAG beats text-based RAG by +24.0 (ChartQA) and +15.6 (SlideVQA) in single-pool, and by +10.1 to +15.7 in all-pool, while the gap narrows with ground-truth documents, indicating retrieval quality remains a bottleneck.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends retrieval-augmented generation from text-only corpora to visually-rich documents by repurposing large vision-language models (LVLMs) as dual-encoder retrievers and generators. It departs from prior visual document retrieval work (ColPali/DSE, VisRAG) by introducing self-supervised pre-training tasks (RCR and RCG) that explicitly compress image representations into a single <EOS> token, bridging the gap between LVLMs' generative pre-training and the contrastive objective needed for retrieval. The OpenDocVQA benchmark generalizes closed-domain DocumentVQA (DocVQA, InfoVQA, ChartQA) to an open-domain retrieval setting with multi-hop reasoning, filling a gap left by ViDoRe (retrieval-only, limited document types) and Dureader_vis (single-hop, webpage-only).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical gap: most RAG systems assume text-only context, yet real-world documents (charts, tables, slides, manuals) are inherently multimodal and structured. The finding that image-based VDocRAG is 69% faster in retrieval than text-based RAG—despite encoding full document images—challenges the assumption that OCR-based pipelines are more efficient, and the complementary contributions of RCR and RCG pre-training tasks provide a reusable recipe for adapting generative LVLMs to retrieval. Open questions remain around handling text-heavy documents where OCR limitations still hurt, reducing indexing costs for large image corpora, and jointly optimizing retrieval and generation rather than training them sequentially.
