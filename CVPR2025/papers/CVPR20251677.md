## Problem

Existing GAN-based explainability methods like StylEx are limited to single-concept domains (e.g., faces), require training a new GAN per classifier, and demand manual labeling of discovered latent semantics. No prior approach provides a hierarchical, training-free explanation of classifier decisions that scales to complex multi-object scenes such as fashion, architecture, or food.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251677/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of domain-specific images, a text prompt describing the target domain, and a frozen classifier (binary or multi-class) as input. It produces a ranked, hierarchical list of semantic attributes (e.g., 'beard' → 'Balbo beard') with per-attribute influence scores computed as mean logit differences, alongside counterfactual images generated via Ledits++ on SDXL that visually demonstrate each attribute's effect on the classifier output. The hierarchical structure allows users to drill from coarse categories (e.g., 'mouth features') down to fine-grained subtypes.

## Method

DiffEx is a training-free pipeline with two stages. First, a VLM (GPT-4) is prompted via in-context learning with a small set of domain images to extract a large-scale hierarchical semantic corpus H of domain-specific attributes. Second, a beam-search-inspired algorithm (Algorithm 1) navigates H: it initializes with root-level groups, scores each candidate using the average classifier logit shift (Eq. 2) after applying the attribute via Ledits++ editing, retains only the top-B candidates exceeding a threshold δ, then iteratively expands surviving candidates to their sub-features, keeping only children whose influence score exceeds their parent's. This prunes the combinatorial search space while preserving high-impact semantic paths, and the same scoring function naturally extends to joint-attribute combinations where individual edits have minimal effect but paired edits significantly shift logits.

## Results

In the primary user study (Table 2, bird domain), DiffEx achieves 88.4% average correct attribute identification, substantially outperforming StylEx (76.5%) and Grad-CAM (53%). Edit quality ratings from 50 participants score 3.66/5 (face) and 3.39/5 (bird), with disentanglement at 3.20/5 and 3.16/5 respectively. In a multi-attribute disentanglement study, DiffEx scores 4.6/5 for single-attribute edits and 4.3/5 for edits involving four or more simultaneous attributes, confirming robustness under compound modifications.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffEx outperforms StylEx [31] by 11.9 percentage points in average correct attribute identification (88.4% vs. 76.5%) in the bird-domain user study, with the largest gaps on Crest Presence (+20 pts) and Upperparts Color (+23 pts). Against Grad-CAM [49], DiffEx leads by 35.4 points (88.4% vs. 53%), reflecting that saliency maps cannot convey fine-grained semantic attributes. In visual comparisons (Figure 8), DiffEx matches or exceeds FastDiME-2+ [68] on medical counterfactual generation (skin lesions, chest X-rays) while also handling face-domain edits. Compared to DiME [26] and ACE [27], DiffEx produces more disentangled edits that preserve unrelated features (e.g., eyes, glasses) when modifying a single attribute. The paper also notes that StylEx requires training a new GAN per classifier and manual attribute labeling, whereas DiffEx is training-free and automatically assigns semantic labels via VLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffEx extends the StylEx paradigm of explaining classifiers through semantic manipulation of generated images, but replaces the GAN backbone with off-the-shelf text-to-image diffusion models (SDXL + Ledits++) and eliminates the need to train a new generative model per classifier. It departs from prior GAN-based and diffusion-based counterfactual methods (StylEx, FastDiME, DiME, ACE) by introducing a VLM-curated hierarchical semantic corpus and a beam-search algorithm that systematically ranks attributes from coarse to fine-grained, rather than relying on manual labeling or flat attribute lists. The use of in-context learning with VLMs to auto-generate domain-specific semantic taxonomies is a conceptual departure from the supervised or manually curated attribute sets used in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hierarchical, training-free framework offers a practical tool for practitioners who need to audit classifier behavior across diverse domains (medical, agricultural, fashion) without retraining generative models, and the ability to drill from 'beard' to 'Balbo beard' provides a granularity that flat saliency or GAN-latent methods cannot match. Key limitations include dependence on VLM corpus quality (potentially missing domain-specific features), entangled edits from off-the-shelf diffusion editing tools, and the computational cost of evaluating many semantic candidates even with beam search. Open questions include whether task-specific RAG can close the gap in specialized domains like medicine, whether the beam-search heuristic is near-optimal for very deep hierarchies, and how the approach scales when the semantic corpus grows to thousands of attributes.
