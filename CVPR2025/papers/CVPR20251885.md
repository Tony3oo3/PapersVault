## Problem

Despite rapid advances in neural 3D reconstruction (NeRF, 3DGS), no large-scale dataset exists that meets the digital twin standard—millimeter-accurate geometry combined with photorealistic PBR materials—needed to quantitatively assess whether reconstruction methods truly produce indistinguishable virtual replicas. Additionally, no dataset supports evaluating 3D object reconstruction from egocentric AR glasses captures, which is essential for democratizing digital twin creation on next-generation computing platforms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251885/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 2,000 scanned 3D meshes with 4K-resolution PBR material maps, 100 DSLR image sequences (120 HDR/LDR images per object at 9° intervals with per-image camera poses, object masks, and two environment maps for relighting), and 200 egocentric Project Aria recordings (active 360° and passive casual views) with metric-scale trajectories and aligned object poses. For evaluation, ground truth depth, normal, and shape are derived by projecting the aligned 3D object into each view, enabling pixel-accurate comparison of reconstructed geometry and appearance.

## Method

The core contribution is a multi-stage dataset creation pipeline: (1) industrial structured-light scanning (Covision scanner with 8 structured lights + 29 spotlights/cameras) yields millimeter-accurate geometry and 4K PBR maps, with technical-artist refinement for glossy surfaces; (2) a custom motorized DSLR rig with three cameras and ChArUco boards captures 120-view sequences, while chrome-ball reflections refined via differentiable Monte Carlo rendering produce environment maps; (3) egocentric recordings from Project Aria glasses are aligned to 3D objects using Neural-PBIR mesh reconstruction followed by physics-based differentiable rendering pose optimization. The paper then benchmarks six inverse rendering methods (NVDiffRec, NVDiffRecMC, InvRender, PhySG, Neural-PIL, NeRD) on geometry, relighting, and NVS metrics, evaluates 3DGS and 2DGS on egocentric NVS, and demonstrates that policies trained on DTC objects outperform those trained on Objaverse-XL for robotic pushing and grasping in PyBullet simulation.

## Results

On the DTC DSLR inverse rendering benchmark (15 objects, 30 sequences), InvRender achieves the best shape reconstruction (Chamfer distance 0.75×10⁻³) and best relighting quality (PSNR-H 29.52 dB), while NVDiffRecMC leads in novel view synthesis (PSNR-L 38.17 dB, SSIM 0.972). On the egocentric benchmark, 2D-GS and 3D-GS achieve comparable image quality (PSNR ≈ 28.8 dB, LPIPS 0.020), with 2D-GS producing more accurate normals (0.211 vs 0.330). For robotic manipulation, policies trained on DTC objects outperform those trained on Objaverse-XL across all tasks, with the largest gap in pushing at 2 cm (36.3% vs 25.3% success).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a dataset/benchmark paper, so the comparisons evaluate existing reconstruction methods on the new DTC ground truth. InvRender dominates in shape accuracy (Chamfer 0.75×10⁻³, nearly 2× better than NVDiffRecMC at 1.34) and relighting (PSNR-H 29.52 vs 27.78 for NVDiffRecMC), while NVDiffRecMC edges out InvRender on NVS PSNR-L (38.17 vs 37.82) and SSIM (0.972 vs 0.970). NVDiffRec achieves the best NVS LPIPS (0.029) but trails in shape (1.64) and relighting. NeRD and Neural-PIL are clearly the weakest, with NeRD's shape error (108.20) over 100× worse than InvRender's. On the egocentric benchmark, 2D-GS and 3D-GS are nearly tied on image quality (PSNR 28.75 vs 28.81) but 2D-GS produces substantially better normals (0.211 vs 0.330). In the robotics application, DTC-trained policies beat Objaverse-XL-trained policies on every task, with the advantage most pronounced at tighter pushing thresholds (36.3% vs 25.3% at 2 cm), suggesting higher-fidelity geometry enables finer manipulation control.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by combining industrial structured-light scanning (Covision) with egocentric Project Aria capture to produce a unified dataset that no prior work has achieved at scale. It departs from existing object-centric datasets (Objaverse, Stanford-ORB, OmniObject3D) by simultaneously providing millimeter-accurate geometry, photorealistic PBR materials, multi-view DSLR recordings with environment maps for relighting, and—uniquely—egocentric AR-glasses recordings aligned to the same 3D ground truth. The benchmarking methodology builds on the inverse rendering evaluation framework of Stanford-ORB and the egocentric digital twin concept from the Aria Digital Twin dataset, but extends both to object-level fidelity with PBR materials and to a 2,000-object scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DTC establishes the first comprehensive real-world benchmark that tests whether reconstruction methods truly achieve 'digital twin' fidelity—indistinguishable from the physical object—across shape, PBR materials, and relighting, a standard prior datasets could not enforce due to trade-offs between scale and quality. The inclusion of egocentric Aria recordings opens a path toward democratizing 3D twin creation via consumer AR hardware, a direction with no prior evaluation infrastructure. Key limitations remain: the pipeline requires deliberate hardware setup and human refinement, cannot handle deformable, highly specular, or transparent objects, and the robotics sim-to-real transfer is only demonstrated in simulation. Open questions include whether physics-based differentiable rendering can automate the material refinement step and whether the dataset's object diversity (40 LVIS categories) is sufficient for general-purpose digital twin applications.
