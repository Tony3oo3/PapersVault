## Problem

Existing collaborative perception methods are designed for sparse 3D object detection and rely on object-level sparsity for compression and fusion, making them unsuitable for dense BEV semantic segmentation. Two specific challenges remain unaddressed: compressing continuous dense BEV features without severe information degradation, and fusing multi-agent features that produce conflicting semantic estimations in overlapping regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251040/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view camera images from the ego agent and up to N_a surrounding agents (within a 70 m communication range), along with their relative poses, and produces a BEV semantic segmentation map over a 100 m × 100 m area at 39 cm resolution with Road and Lane categories. The transmitted message is not a continuous feature tensor but a sparse set of discrete element indices (drawn from a 128-entry codebook of 64-dim vectors at 32×32 spatial resolution), with communication volume measured as log₂(b(M)/8) bytes per message.

## Method

CoGMP introduces two modules built on generative map priors. On the sender side, Element Format Feature Compression (EFFC) trains a VQ-VAE on BEV segmentation labels to build a codebook of 128 map-element embeddings, then uses a convolutional ETI module to predict per-pixel element type indices from BEV features; a Critical Element Selection module (Gumbel-Softmax during training, Top-K at inference) transmits only the most informative indices, while a Masked Element Recovery module at the receiver reconstructs dropped elements via self-attention over a learnable mask token. On the receiver side, Structure Guided Feature Fusion (SGFF) formulates multi-agent fusion as a conditional latent diffusion process: a lightweight U-Net (with ControlNet-style condition injection of sender element features) is trained with x₀-prediction to denoise the ego's element representation toward the ground-truth latent, progressively integrating complementary information and resolving cross-agent conflicts under learned map-structure priors. An Uncertainty Adaptive Time Scheduler (UATS) early-stops the reverse diffusion when the ego's ETI probability distribution indicates low uncertainty, reducing average iterations from 10 to 5.24 with negligible accuracy loss.

## Results

On the OPV2V dataset, CoGMP achieves a SOTA performance of 66.19/52.92 Road/Lane IoU while simultaneously reducing communication volume by 32× compared to previous methods. Under the maximum bandwidth constraint, improvements over the prior SOTA reach 8.93/10.40 IoU. Remarkably, even at only 1/512 of the previous methods' bandwidth, CoGMP still yields accuracy gains of 3.03/4.33 IoU. Under pose error (σ=1), CoGMP drops to 63.76/48.21 IoU yet still outperforms all baselines by a widened margin of 9.96/9.52.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoGMP is compared against five baselines: F-Cooper, AttFuse, V2VNet, DiscoNet, and CoBEVT (the previous SOTA for collaborative BEV segmentation). CoGMP outperforms all five on both the performance–bandwidth trade-off and pose-error robustness. Against the closest competitor CoBEVT, CoGMP gains 6.89/7.64 Road/Lane IoU at matched communication volume and 8.93/10.40 at maximum bandwidth. The advantage grows under stress: at σ=1 pose noise the gap widens to 9.96/9.52 IoU, indicating that the diffusion-based structural prior in SGFF provides substantially better conflict resolution than the attention- or graph-based fusion used by the baselines. On the compression side, EFFC's discrete element-index transmission achieves a 32× volume reduction over the continuous BEV-feature transmission used by all baselines, and even at 1/512 of their bandwidth CoGMP retains a 3.03/4.33 IoU lead, a regime where the baselines degrade sharply.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends collaborative perception from the dominant 3D object detection setting (sparse, object-level features) to BEV semantic segmentation (dense, per-pixel features), a shift that invalidates prior compression and fusion strategies built on object sparsity. It builds on VQ-VAE for discrete representation learning to construct a codebook of map element priors, and on conditional latent diffusion (with ControlNet-style conditioning and x0-prediction from progressive distillation) to perform structure-guided multi-agent fusion. The key conceptual departure from prior collaborative BEV segmentation work (e.g., CoBEVT) is the explicit use of generative map priors—both element-level (codebook) and structure-level (diffusion)—to simultaneously drive compression and fusion, rather than task-driven feature compression alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 32-fold communication reduction with simultaneous IoU improvement (6.89/7.64) addresses a core practical bottleneck in V2X collaborative perception, and the finding that the diffusion-based SGFF module improves predictions even without external agents (unconditional mode, +2.17/+3.9 IoU) suggests the learned map structure prior has intrinsic value beyond fusion. However, evaluation is limited to the simulated OPV2V dataset with only Road/Lane categories in a 100 m × 100 m area, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to real-world sensor noise, richer map ontologies (crosswalks, traffic lights, parking zones), and larger-scale urban scenes. The diffusion-based fusion also introduces inference latency that, while mitigated by UATS, remains a concern for real-time deployment in safety-critical driving.
