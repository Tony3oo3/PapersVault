## Problem

Controllable image editing is hindered by spurious correlations between visual attributes (e.g., older faces co-occurring with glasses in CelebA), causing edits to one attribute to unintentionally alter unrelated ones. Existing causal representation methods like CFI-VAE address this within a VAE framework but suffer from uninterpretable latent spaces and the reconstruction–KL trade-off that limits causal representation quality. The paper asks whether a diffusion-based framework can learn interpretable, causally faithful representations that enable precise attribute editing without spurious side effects.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (128×128 for CelebA, 96×96 for Pendulum) annotated with M binary or continuous attribute labels as input. It produces a set of M causal latent representations (one per attribute, 64-dimensional) and, at inference, an edited image where a user-specified attribute is intervened upon while all other attributes are preserved. The semantic encoder extracts high-level attribute representations, while a stochastic encoder captures low-level texture details; these are fused via AdaGN before feeding into a DDIM decoder.

## Method

CIDiffuser decomposes the image latent space into high-level semantic representations (exogenous variables in an SCM) and low-level stochastic representations, then learns a causal effect matrix A via a Direct Causal Effect Learning (DCEL) module that compares factual and counterfactual classifier predictions under a do-operation to isolate direct causal effects from confounding biases. A novel causal effect loss subtracts the intervened prediction from the factual one to ensure non-zero causal entries, and an influence-function-based reweighting term further corrects for class imbalance. The learned SCM transforms semantic representations into causal representations, which are fused with stochastic representations through AdaGN layers and decoded by a conditional DDIM. Training combines a diffusion ELBO, a supervised attribute-alignment loss, and the causal effect loss, with the causal effect loss switched to its imbalance-corrected form after initial convergence.

## Results

On CelebA-smile, CIDiffuser achieves the best MIC (0.890) and TIC (0.869) among all methods, outperforming the strongest baseline CausalVAE (MIC 0.837, TIC 0.716). On CelebA-age, CIDiffuser matches SCM-VAE on MIC (0.944) and surpasses it on TIC (0.903 vs 0.889). On image generation quality (CelebA), CIDiffuser achieves FID 56.7, IS 3.053, and KID 0.027, substantially outperforming all causal representation learning baselines (best CRL: DEAR FID 97.2) while remaining competitive with the top TRL method DiffAE (FID 51.6, IS 3.669).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CIDiffuser outperforms all causal representation learning (CRL) baselines on CelebA-smile representation quality, beating CausalVAE (MIC 0.837→0.890) and CFI-VAE (MIC 0.664→0.890) by substantial margins. On CelebA-age, CIDiffuser ties SCM-VAE on MIC (0.944) and edges it out on TIC (0.903 vs 0.889), while decisively surpassing CFI-VAE (MIC 0.461) and DEAR (MIC 0.347). On Pendulum, CIDiffuser achieves the highest MIC (0.981) but trails CFI-VAE on TIC (0.865 vs 0.981), suggesting CFI-VAE captures slightly more total information in this synthetic setting. For image generation quality, CIDiffuser (FID 56.7, KID 0.027) dramatically outperforms all CRL methods (best: DEAR FID 97.2, KID 0.044) but is slightly behind the TRL method DiffAE (FID 51.6, IS 3.669), which leverages a pure diffusion autoencoder without causal constraints. Traditional disentanglement methods (Beta-VAE, ConditionalVAE, Infodiff) are consistently outperformed on both representation quality and generation fidelity, confirming that causal structure modeling is essential for disentangling confounded visual attributes.

## Relation to Prior Work

CIDiffuser sits at the intersection of two research threads: causal representation learning (CFI-VAE, CausalVAE, SCM-VAE) that models inter-attribute dependencies via structural causal models, and diffusion-based autoencoders (DiffAE, Infodiff) that achieve high-fidelity generation. The key departure from CFI-VAE is replacing the VAE decoder with a conditional DDIM decoder and decomposing the latent space into a semantic (causal) component and a stochastic (detail) component, while the key departure from DiffAE is injecting a learned causal effect matrix via SCM to enforce interventional semantics on the latent dimensions. The influence-function-based reweighting of the causal effect loss to handle class imbalance is a novel addition not present in prior causal representation learning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical failure mode in controllable editing—spurious correlations causing unintended attribute changes (e.g., editing age also changes glasses)—by grounding the latent space in causal structure rather than mere statistical association. However, the causal graph must be pre-specified by domain knowledge, limiting applicability to settings where attribute dependencies are well understood; scalability to open-vocabulary or high-resolution editing remains unaddressed. The evaluation is confined to 128×128 facial images and a synthetic pendulum, so whether the causal-diffusion combination transfers to complex scenes or larger attribute spaces is an open question.
