## Problem

Existing learning-based motion in-betweening methods require large character-specific MOCAP or keyframed datasets for training, which is infeasible for characters that are difficult to capture (e.g., animals, stylized avatars) or lack extensive animation data. This paper addresses the gap by performing motion in-betweening for arbitrary characters with arbitrary joint structures using no external training data, relying instead on a pre-trained video diffusion model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251712/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 2 seconds of context motion (rendered from 4 views at 30 fps), target keyframes sampled at 1-second intervals, and a 3D rigged character as input, and produces per-frame root positions and per-joint rotations as the final 3D motion output. An intermediate in-between video is generated at 15 fps (upsampled to 30 fps via Gaussian filtering and slerp for evaluation). The pipeline handles both humanoid characters (from Mixamo) and non-humanoid characters (birds, snakes, dinosaurs from Truebones Zoo) with arbitrary joint structures, requiring no retargeting or character-specific training data.

## Method

AnyMoLe operates in three stages. First, ICAdapt fine-tunes only the spatial module and image projector of DynamiCrafter on 16-frame clips rendered from the 2-second context motion, while freezing the temporal module to preserve learned motion dynamics, thereby bridging the domain gap between real-world training data and rendered scenes. Second, a two-stage auto-regressive video generation produces the in-between video: a coarse stage generates sparse 5 fps frames by reformulating context-frame conditioning as latent inpainting (iteratively replacing noisy latents with encoded guidance frames during denoising), followed by a fine stage that fills in 15 fps frames using the coarse output as additional guidance. Third, motion-video mimicking sequentially optimizes root position and per-joint rotations inward from the keyframes, using a scene-specific joint estimator (fusing DINOv2 2D semantic features with FiT3D 3D-aware structural features, decoded via heatmaps and a depth MLP) together with a differentiable rendering image loss and position/rotation regularization to recover smooth 3D motion from the generated video.

## Results

On humanoid characters, AnyMoLe achieves HL2Q of 0.0015, L2Q of 0.0011, L2P of 0.0063, and NPSS of 0.0726, outperforming all baselines by large margins (e.g., ~65× lower HL2Q than SinMDM's 0.0971, and ~60× lower NPSS than TST's 4.3509). On non-humanoid characters (birds, snakes, dinosaurs), AnyMoLe achieves HL2Q of 0.0019 and NPSS of 0.1863, while SinMDM degrades to HL2Q 0.2465 and NPSS 4.4324. In rendered-image quality, AnyMoLe scores 0.9711 CLIP and 0.9343 SSIM on humanoid characters, the best among all methods. A user study with 21 participants confirms AnyMoLe is preferred 60–65% of the time for similarity, keyframe faithfulness, and naturalness on humanoid characters, and 90–92% on non-humanoid characters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnyMoLe outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric for both humanoid and non-humanoid characters. Against SinMDM (a single-motion diffusion model), the improvement is dramatic: HL2Q drops from 0.0971 to 0.0015 (~65×) and NPSS from 1.7148 to 0.0726 (~24×) on humanoid characters, with similar gaps on non-humanoid characters where SinMDM's HL2Q is 0.2465 vs. AnyMoLe's 0.0019. Against TST (two-stage transformer) and ERD-QV (robust motion in-betweening), AnyMoLe achieves roughly 2× lower HL2Q (0.0015 vs. 0.0028) and 8× lower L2Q (0.0011 vs. 0.0106/0.0109), while NPSS improves by over 60× (0.0726 vs. 4.35/4.38), indicating far better temporal frequency matching. Critically, TST and ERD-QV are restricted to humanoid characters and cannot handle non-humanoid rigs (snakes, dinosaurs, birds), whereas AnyMoLe and SinMDM generalize to arbitrary joint structures. In the user study, AnyMoLe is preferred 60–65% of the time over all baselines for humanoid characters and 90–92% for non-humanoid characters across similarity, keyframe faithfulness, and naturalness criteria.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established motion in-betweening literature (TST, ERD-QV, SinMDM), which all require character-specific MOCAP or keyframed datasets, by repurposing a pre-trained video diffusion model (DynamiCrafter) as the generative backbone. The two-stage latent inpainting formulation for context-guided generation draws on diffusion inpainting techniques (Blended Diffusion, RePaint), while the 3D recovery step borrows from differentiable rendering and pose estimation (DINOv2, FiT3D). The key conceptual shift is treating motion in-betweening as a 2D video generation problem followed by 3D optimization, rather than directly modeling 3D motion distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AnyMoLe is the first method to perform motion in-betweening for arbitrary characters (including non-humanoid creatures like snakes and dinosaurs) without any external training data, addressing a limitation the authors argue has been overlooked in the field. However, the 5–6 hour per-character pipeline, reliance on only 2 seconds of context motion, and sensitivity to fast/ambiguous dynamics (e.g., left-right joint confusion in rapid turns) limit immediate practical adoption. A promising open direction identified by the authors is a context-informed, character-agnostic 3D joint estimator that would reduce per-character training time and improve robustness to blurry generated frames.
