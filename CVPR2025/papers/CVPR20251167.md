## Problem

Existing pose generation methods, whether GAN-based or diffusion-based, conflate the fundamentally different tasks of spatial pose transformation and appearance texture generation within a single model, leading to overfitting, poor identity preservation, and limited controllability. This is especially problematic for multi-pose generation, where cross-pose consistency in both geometry and appearance must be maintained simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251167/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 256×256 reference image and N target poses (skeletons plus human parsing maps extracted via OpenPose and PGN, with parsing categories reduced from 20 to 8) as input, and produces N posed person images at 256×256 resolution. The 2D diffusion process is extended to a 3D setting so that all N poses are generated simultaneously as a coherent sequence, and the model can generate up to 3× the training-time number of poses at inference without retraining.

## Method

The core contribution is a disentangled two-stage pipeline: a Global-aware Pose Generation module handles spatial transformation, while a latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v1.5 U-Net) handles appearance synthesis. The pose module uses a Global-aware Transformer Decoder that iteratively generates pose embeddings, where a global-aware block computes a similarity matrix between the current target pose and all previously generated poses to enforce cross-pose consistency, and a pooling layer captures holistic pose structure. For appearance, a multi-scale Swin Transformer encodes the reference image, and an Appearance Adapter with k=3 embedding layers (global attention + FFN, zero-initialized convolutions) injects pose-agnostic semantic features into the U-Net at multiple scales. Training is end-to-end with a combined diffusion loss and a reference-image reconstruction loss, using classifier-free guidance (5% condition dropout, λ=2.0) and a fine-tuned VAE decoder for facial realism.

## Results

On UBC Fashion, the proposed method achieves SSIM 0.941, PSNR 25.655, FID 11.487, LPIPS 0.0497, and L1 0.014, outperforming the strongest baseline CFLD (SSIM 0.889, FID 12.810) by 5.9% in SSIM and 10.3% in FID. On the more challenging TikTok dataset, it reaches SSIM 0.753, PSNR 18.466, FID 103.708, and L1 0.072, again leading all baselines. Inference time is 5.5–24.1 s, substantially faster than PIDM (28.64 s), CFLD (35.8 s), and MagicPose (67.8 s), while using 367M parameters versus MagicPose's 2081M.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all seven baselines on UBC Fashion across every metric, with the largest margins over GAN-based methods (e.g., FID 11.487 vs. 21.012 for BiGraphGAN) and a clear lead over the strongest diffusion baseline CFLD (FID 11.487 vs. 12.810; LPIPS 0.0497 vs. 0.0718). On TikTok, it wins on SSIM, PSNR, FID, and L1 against all baselines, though CFLD edges it slightly on LPIPS (0.2136 vs. 0.2311). Among diffusion-based competitors, the advantage over PIDM is most pronounced (FID 11.487 vs. 14.951 on UBC; 103.708 vs. 130.392 on TikTok), while the gap over MagicPose is moderate (FID 11.487 vs. 14.649 on UBC). Inference speed is a notable practical advantage: 5.5–24.1 s versus 28.64 s (PIDM), 35.8 s (CFLD), and 67.8 s (MagicPose), with 367M parameters compared to MagicPose's 2081M. A user study with 30 volunteers confirms the quantitative gains, with 61.4% preference for the proposed method versus 16.7% (CFLD), 13.6% (PIDM), and 8.3% (MagicPose).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based pose generation line (PIDM, CFLD, MagicPose) by explicitly separating the spatial transformation of pose from the texture synthesis of appearance into two dedicated modules, whereas prior diffusion methods delegate both tasks simultaneously to the U-Net. The Global-aware Transformer Decoder draws on iterative Transformer decoding and cross-pose attention to handle multi-pose spatial geometry, while the Appearance Adapter borrows the zero-initialized adapter pattern from ControlNet to inject multi-scale appearance features into the latent diffusion model. The multi-pose formulation with cross-pose consistency via iterative generation departs from the single-pose paradigm of most GAN-based predecessors (PISE, DPTN, NTED, BiGraphGAN), which optimize each target pose in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The disentanglement principle—assigning spatial geometry to a lightweight Transformer module and texture refinement to a diffusion model—offers a clean architectural template that could transfer to other conditional generation tasks where geometry and appearance are conflated. The ability to generate up to 3× the training quantity of poses without retraining, combined with substantially lower inference time than monolithic diffusion baselines (5.5–24.1 s vs. 28.6–67.8 s), makes the approach practically attractive for animation and virtual try-on pipelines. Open questions include whether the iterative cross-pose mechanism scales gracefully beyond 8 poses, whether the 256×256 resolution constraint limits real-world deployment, and whether the disentanglement benefit persists when the appearance domain is more diverse (e.g., multiple garments or accessories).
