## Problem

Existing video-to-audio generation frameworks treat music and sound effect as a single mixed spectrum, causing models to lose specificity in capturing the distinct spectral characteristics of each component. Additionally, current diffusion-based audio generators rely on simplistic single-pass inference that produces repetitive outputs, offering musicians no mechanism for real-time customization of emotion, style, or rhythm during generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250882/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 10-second video clip as the primary input, along with optional custom control conditions: an emotion word (e.g., 'happy'), a style reference audio clip, and/or a video rhythm signal derived from frame-difference analysis. The output is a complete 10-second soundtrack waveform containing both music and sound effect simultaneously, generated in the AudioMAE latent space and decoded via Fourier transformation. The fixed 10-second length is a current limitation acknowledged by the authors.

## Method

C³GVS is a latent diffusion model (U-Net) trained end-to-end with three key innovations. First, Contrastive Video-Sound-Music Pretraining uses Bandit to separate audio into music and sound effect, then trains two independent contrastive mappings (f_v2m, f_v2se) from VideoCLIP features to each audio modality's feature space, producing disentangled visual control conditions. Second, Spectrum Divergence Masked Attention (SDMA) injects these two conditions via separate cross-attention layers in the U-Net, then applies a learned time-frequency mask—computed by element-wise multiplication of sound effect features with self-attention output followed by channel-averaged softmax—to gate how sound effect information fuses with music, reducing spectral interference between transient effects and harmonic content. Third, Score-guided Noise Iterative Optimization modifies DDIM sampling by applying per-step gradient corrections from custom conditions (CLAP emotion scores, pitch-visual cosine distance for rhythm sync, MusicLDM encoder distance for style), with multi-condition weighting, enabling stable and diverse generation under one or multiple simultaneous constraints.

## Results

On FilmScoreDB, the proposed C³GVS achieves the best music quality (FD=3.42, KL=1.06, IS=5.13) and video-music synchronization (BCS=68.71, F1=68.36), outperforming the second-best model by 10.03 points in F1. On SymMV&HIMV, it similarly leads with FD=7.32, KL=2.14, IS=3.87, and F1=62.28. For sound effect generation (Table 2), C³GVS achieves the lowest FAD (32.04 on FilmScoreDB, 40.41 on SymMV&HIMV) and highest ImageBind alignment score (IB=0.257 and 0.185 respectively), surpassing SpecVQGAN, AudioLDM-VA, and Tango-VA. Subjective MOS scores reach 3.73 (FilmScoreDB) and 3.45 (SymMV&HIMV), substantially above baselines (2.01–2.28).

## Baselines & Comparisons

C³GVS outperforms all six music-generation baselines on both datasets across every metric. Against the strongest diffusion-based competitors Tango-VA (F1=57.38) and AudioLDM-VA (F1=58.33) on FilmScoreDB, the proposed method gains 10–11 F1 points and improves BCS by ~16 points, indicating substantially better video-music rhythm alignment. LORIS, which uses independently controllable video features, is the closest competitor on BCS (64.3 vs. 68.71) but lags significantly in music quality (FD=10.51 vs. 3.42) and F1 (58.03 vs. 68.36), confirming that the SDMA mask-based parallel cross-attention reduces mutual interference between music and sound-effect conditions more effectively than LORIS's sequential approach. D2M-GAN and CDCD, which rely on codebook-based music representations, perform worst on synchronization metrics (F1=52.62 and 50.17), suggesting that discrete token strategies are ill-suited for semantically rich film and MV content. For sound effect generation, C³GVS beats SpecVQGAN by 0.91 FAD and 0.084 IB on FilmScoreDB, and by 1.86 FAD and 0.048 IB on SymMV&HIMV. In the inference-time optimization comparison (Fig. 3), the proposed Score-guided Noise Iterative Optimization surpasses DITTO across all four custom conditions (emotion, rhythm sync, style, multi-condition) in both objective and subjective scores, demonstrating that per-step latent correction handles complex multi-condition soundtrack generation better than DITTO's initial-noise-only optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the AudioLDM latent diffusion framework and DDIM deterministic sampling, extending them from text-conditioned audio generation to video-conditioned soundtrack synthesis. It departs from prior video-to-music work (LORIS, D2M-GAN, CDCD) that targets human-centric videos or piano-roll representations by jointly generating music and sound effect for general film/MV content, and from DIFF-Foley/SpecVQGAN which handle only sound effects. The Spectrum Divergence Masked Attention module is a novel architectural contribution that separates music and sound-effect injection in the U-Net, while the Score-guided Noise Iterative Optimization extends Freedom's per-step score correction and DITTO's inference-time optimization from initial-noise-only to per-step latent correction for complex multi-component audio.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is one of the first frameworks to jointly generate both music and sound effect from video with multi-condition controllability (emotion, style, rhythm sync), addressing a practical gap for film scoring and music production pipelines. The per-step optimization outperforming DITTO's initial-noise-only approach for complex audio is a useful finding for the broader diffusion-based audio generation community. However, the method is limited to fixed-length (10-second) soundtrack generation, and the reliance on AudioLDM's infrastructure and pre-trained encoders (VideoCLIP, AudioMAE, MusicLDM) constrains scalability to longer or more diverse content. Open questions include whether the SDMA masking strategy generalizes to more than two audio components and whether the contrastive pretraining can be replaced by end-to-end learning at scale.
