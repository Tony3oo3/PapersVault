## Problem

Temporal downsampling in video resampling irreversibly discards inter-frame motion information, and existing joint resampling methods either introduce blurring artifacts in downsampled frames or fail to preserve motion for faithful reconstruction. Additionally, all prior video resampling approaches are restricted to fixed integer resampling factors, making them unable to handle practical non-integer frame-rate conversions such as 30 FPS to 24 FPS.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251725/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of high-resolution, high-frame-rate frames (s·H × s·W × 3) and jointly produces two outputs: (1) downsampled frames (H × W × 3) that are visually indistinguishable from traditional bicubic/interval-sampled results, and (2) reconstructed high-resolution, high-frame-rate frames. The downsampled output is a single RGB artifact serving a dual role—it appears as a clean low-frame-rate video to human observers while simultaneously encoding hidden motion information in its low-order bits, which can be reversibly decoded for reconstruction. The system supports arbitrary continuous spatial and temporal resampling factors, including non-integer values.

## Method

The architecture combines two key modules: an Invertible Motion Steganography Module (IMSM) built from NICE-style invertible flow cells that reversibly embeds motion information from high-frame-rate latent features into visually clean low-frame-rate RGB images (forward pass) and recovers the latent features from those images (backward pass), and a 3D implicit feature modulation sampler that extends 2D local implicit image functions to the spatiotemporal domain via trilinear interpolation over eight nearest 3D neighbors fed through an MLP, enabling continuous non-integer resampling. The downsample backbone uses 3D residual blocks, while the upsample backbone employs either 3D Swin Transformer layers (for joint spatiotemporal resampling) or Dense 3D Blocks with depth-wise separable convolutions (for temporal-only resampling to reduce GPU memory). Training proceeds in two stages: first, the resampler is trained end-to-end with Charbonnier loss supervising only the upsampler output, allowing latent features to naturally accumulate motion ghosting; second, the IMSM is trained with a symmetric invertible loss (forward and backward Charbonnier terms) and a differentiable quantization layer to handle the non-differentiable RGB quantization step, ensuring robustness in both embedding and extraction directions.

## Results

On the Time 2× Space 1× task, the proposed method achieves 50.52 dB PSNR-Y on Vimeo90k and 46.77 dB PSNR-Y on Vid4, surpassing the joint resampling baseline STAA by 3.0 dB and 4.0 dB respectively, and outperforming the best VFI method (EMA-VFI, 38.02 dB) by over 12 dB. On the Time 2× Space 4× task, it reaches 40.57 dB PSNR-Y on Vimeo90k and 32.76 dB PSNR-Y on Vid4, exceeding the best space-time video super-resolution method (MsMr, 35.57 dB / 26.87 dB) by 5.0 dB and 5.9 dB. For continuous non-integer spatial resampling on SPMCS, the method leads all baselines at every scale from S=2.0 to S=4.0, e.g., 38.35 dB PSNR-Y at S=2.0 vs. 35.94 dB for MsMr.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 17 baselines across every evaluation setting. Against the seven VFI methods (XVFI, FLAVR, EDSC, IFRNet, RIFE, EMA-VFI, VFIFormer) on the T×2 S×1 task, the gap is dramatic: the best VFI (EMA-VFI) scores 38.02 dB PSNR-Y on Vimeo90k versus 50.52 dB for the proposed method, a 12.5 dB margin, because VFI methods discard inter-frame motion during downsampling. Against the joint resampling baseline STAA [50], the improvement is 3.0 dB (Vimeo90k) and 4.0 dB (Vid4) for T×2 S×1, and 3.2 dB / 3.7 dB for T×2 S×4, attributed to the IMSM preserving motion information that STAA's unsupervised low-pass filtering blurs. Among the six one-stage space-time video super-resolution methods (ZSM, STDAN, MoTIF, RSTT, OFR-BRN, MsMr) on T×2 S×4, the proposed method leads the best (MsMr) by 5.0 dB on Vimeo90k and 5.9 dB on Vid4. The three sequential VFI+VSR pipelines (EDSC+BasicVSR++, IFRNet+BasicVSR++, RIFE+BasicVSR++) are outperformed by 4.9–5.4 dB, confirming that joint optimization is superior to cascaded approaches. For continuous non-integer spatial resampling on SPMCS (Table 2), the method beats VideoINR by 7.7 dB at S=2.0 and MoTIF by 2.7 dB at S=2.0, with the gap widening at higher scales (e.g., 4.5 dB over MsMr at S=4.0), demonstrating the advantage of the 3D implicit modulation for arbitrary scale factors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends implicit neural representation-based super-resolution (Chen et al., ICCV 2021; Lee & Jin, ICCV 2022) from 2D images to 3D video, enabling continuous spatiotemporal modulation including non-integer frame rate conversions. The Invertible Motion Steganography Module repurposes the NICE invertible flow architecture (Dinh et al., ICLR 2015) and Residual-in-Residual blocks (Zhang et al., ECCV 2018) in a novel steganographic role: rather than performing image-to-image translation, the invertible network hides motion information in the low-bit residuals of downsampled frames. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior video resampling work (STAA, Huang et al., Tian et al.) by reframing the temporal downsampling problem as an information-hiding problem, where the downsampled output must simultaneously look like a clean frame and carry recoverable motion data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first framework for continuous (including fractional) spatiotemporal video resampling, a capability absent from all prior learning-based methods that are restricted to integer factors. The steganographic framing offers a principled solution to the fundamental information-loss problem in temporal downsampling, and the IMSM could potentially transfer to other video restoration tasks where intermediate representations must remain visually clean while encoding auxiliary information. Open questions include robustness to real-world compression (only briefly discussed in supplementary material), scalability to very high resolutions given Swin Transformer's quadratic memory cost, and whether the two-stage training protocol can be unified. The quantization step inherently limits perfect reversibility, capping the theoretical fidelity of reconstruction.
