## Problem

Existing video diffusion models produce coherent clips but suffer from temporal consistency failures (object disappearance, color/shape drift) because they lack explicit semantic and geometric reasoning during generation. No prior work provides a unified representation, architecture, or large-scale dataset that jointly encodes video, entity segmentation, and depth estimation for video-level diffusion, leaving dense prediction signals untapped as training guidance for video generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251362/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a text prompt (encoded by T5) and a discrete task ID (segmentation or depth estimation) as input, and simultaneously outputs a 49-frame RGB video and the corresponding dense prediction (entity segmentation map or depth map) as an RGB-encoded Pixelplanes sequence. Both the video and dense prediction latents are 16-channel 3D VAE codes that are channel-wise concatenated into a 32-channel input, so the dense prediction is decoded through the same 3D VAE as the video rather than a separate head.

## Method

UDPDiff extends CogVideoX 5B by doubling the input/output channels to jointly denoise a video latent z_t^v and a dense-prediction latent z_t^c via channel-wise concatenation, so dense-prediction guidance propagates through all transformer layers. The key representation, Pixelplanes, assigns each entity a randomly sampled unique RGB color (eliminating location-to-color ambiguity for moving objects) and maps depth via a spectral-style projection, unifying both tasks into a single RGB-video-compatible format. A learnable task embedding e_θ^d(d) is added to the timestep embedding to explicitly differentiate segmentation from depth estimation during multi-task training, replacing implicit text-prompt-based task selection. The model is fine-tuned from CogVideoX 5B weights on the newly curated Panda-Dense dataset (~300K videos with EntitySeg+SAM2 segmentation, DepthCrafter depth, and Video-LLaVA captions) using the standard diffusion noise-prediction objective, with inference speed essentially unchanged from the base model.

## Results

On the multi-task model evaluated with 1024 held-out Panda-70M samples, UDPDiff (depth estimation task) achieves an FVD of 302.55, substantially outperforming CogVideoX 5B (343.92), while also improving subject consistency (97.07 vs. 94.57), background consistency (96.89 vs. 95.80), and motion smoothness (99.23 vs. 97.67). The segmentation task variant achieves FVD 316.76 with SC 95.21 and MS 98.24. Inference speed remains nearly identical to the baseline (~205.8 s vs. ~204.5 s for 50-step, 49-frame generation on a single A100-80G), confirming that the added dense-prediction channels introduce negligible overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CogVideoX 5B, UDPDiff wins on FVD for both task variants (302.55 and 316.76 vs. 343.92) and on subject consistency and motion smoothness across the board; the depth-estimation variant also improves background consistency (96.89 vs. 95.80), while the segmentation variant is marginally lower on BC (95.69 vs. 95.80). Inference speed is essentially unchanged (205.81 s vs. 204.46 s), so the gains come at no practical computational cost. Against Depth Anything V2 on the depth-estimation sub-task, UDPDiff reports lower accuracy (δ₁ 0.4176 vs. 0.5808; RMSE 0.1634 vs. 0.0907), but this is expected given that Depth Anything V2 is a dedicated model trained on 62 M images whereas UDPDiff is a joint generation model trained on only 300 K videos. Against UniGS's location-aware colormap encoding (Table 6), the proposed Pixelplanes representation yields large gains in all three temporal metrics (SC 94.98 vs. 81.26, BC 95.92 vs. 79.33, MS 98.62 vs. 88.79), confirming that random per-entity color assignment is far more effective than fixed centroid-to-color projection for video-level dense prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the image-level unified generation-and-segmentation paradigm of UniGS to the video domain, replacing its location-aware colormap with a random-color 'Pixelplanes' encoding that avoids the color-ambiguity problem introduced by moving entities across frames. It builds directly on CogVideoX's 3D VAE + transformer backbone and adapts Emu Edit's learned task-embedding mechanism from image to video multi-task learning. The key conceptual departure is treating dense prediction (segmentation, depth) not as a separate conditioning signal (as in ControlNet-style approaches) but as a co-generated output in the same diffusion process, with a newly curated Panda-Dense dataset (~300K videos) providing the joint supervision that no prior dataset offered.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-zero inference overhead (205s vs 204s) for simultaneously producing video plus segmentation and depth maps is practically significant for downstream editing pipelines that would otherwise require separate models. The Pixelplanes ablation (81 vs 95 subject consistency) provides a clear negative result showing that location-based color mappings fundamentally break under motion, which is a useful design principle for future video-level unified representations. Limitations include reliance on fine-tuning a single 5B model (limited compute), depth accuracy well below dedicated estimators (0.42 vs 0.58 δ₁), and evaluation on only 1024 held-out samples. Open questions include whether the benefit scales to larger backbones, longer videos, or additional dense prediction tasks beyond segmentation and depth.
