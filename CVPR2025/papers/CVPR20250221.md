## Problem

Existing UCDSL methods learn a single universal classifier by enforcing domain-invariant feature alignment across all target domains, which disregards domain-specific information and creates a large adaptivity gap between the learned classifier and the optimal per-domain classifier. This paper establishes a theoretical error bound that explicitly quantifies this gap and shows that a single invariant representation can lead to large joint errors, motivating a multi-prototype approach that preserves complementary domain-specific information.

## Input / Output

The method receives unlabeled RGB images from sequentially arriving target domains (D_1 through D_T) and a replay buffer of fixed size 200 that stores both domain prototypes and representative data samples from all previously encountered domains. The feature extractor (ResNet-50 for PACS/DomainNet, DTN for Digits-5) produces features at two levels: first-block features for domain-aware graph construction and last-block features for category-aware graph construction. The final output is a class label prediction obtained by Shannon-entropy-weighted averaging of the prototype-based global prediction and the CGC-calibrated local prediction, with soft domain labels from DGF determining the fusion weights across per-domain prototype classifiers.

## Method

MPM replaces the single universal classifier with a set of per-domain prototype-based classifiers (one per encountered domain), each trained on pseudo-labels generated by Shannon-entropy-weighted integration of all existing classifiers. Training proceeds in two stages per new domain: (1) domain adaptation via self-training with information maximization to generate high-quality pseudo-labels, and (2) domain generation and anti-forget training using ERM with data augmentation, a distillation loss against the previous model, and SelNLPL to handle noisy pseudo-labels. The parameter-free BiGE module then enhances inference: DGF constructs a domain-level graph over first-block features using Markov random walks and iteratively propagates domain labels (25 steps) to compute soft domain weights that fuse predictions from all prototype classifiers, while CGC constructs a category-level graph over last-block features to propagate pseudo-labels and calibrate the global prototype predictions with local semantic information. The theoretical contribution proves that the multi-prototype error bound is strictly tighter than the single-classifier bound by eliminating the H-divergence term in favor of a data density ratio that is typically near-constant.

## Results

On the composite 'All' metric (average of TDA, TDG, and FA), MPM achieves 84.9% on PACS, 87.5% on Digits-5, and 68.6% on DomainNet, outperforming the strongest baseline CoDAG (82.9%, 85.7%, 66.0%) by 2.0, 1.8, and 2.6 percentage points respectively. On the most challenging DomainNet dataset, MPM gains up to 8.1 points over CoDAG on TDA (73.5% vs. 71.0%) and 3.6 points on TDG (58.8% vs. 56.2%). MPM also consistently reports the lowest standard deviation across all datasets, indicating greater robustness across different domain orderings (averaged over 10 orders).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MPM outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and every evaluation metric (TDA, TDG, FA, All). The closest competitor is CoDAG [7], the most recent UCDSL-specific method; MPM beats it by 2.0 points on PACS-All, 1.8 on Digits-5-All, and 2.6 on DomainNet-All, with the largest single-metric gap being +8.1 on DomainNet-TDA (73.5 vs. 71.0). RaTP (Deja Vu) [25], the other dedicated UCDSL method, trails MPM by 5.2 points on PACS-All and 7.2 on DomainNet-All. Source-free DA methods (SHOT+, SHOT++) and test-time adaptation methods (Tent, AdaCon, EATA) are substantially weaker, particularly on TDG where they score 47–66% versus MPM's 58.8–79.3%, confirming that single-domain or non-continual approaches struggle with the multi-domain continual setting. Single DG methods (L2D, PDEN) perform competitively on Digits-5-TDA (84.3, 82.3) but collapse on DomainNet-TDA (56.2, 55.6), highlighting their inability to handle large domain shifts in a continual protocol. Notably, MPM also achieves the lowest standard deviation in most settings (e.g., 2.8 vs. 4.0 on PACS-TDA), suggesting more stable performance across domain orderings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant UCDSL paradigm (CoDAG, RaTP/Deja Vu) that learns a single domain-invariant classifier by enforcing cross-domain feature alignment. Instead, it motivates a multi-prototype approach via a theoretical error bound showing that a single classifier incurs a larger adaptivity gap, and builds on prototypical networks (Snell et al.) as the per-domain classifier architecture. The training pipeline reuses components from CoDAG (distillation loss, information maximization), SHOT (prototype classifier loss), iCaRL (replay buffer selection), and SelNLPL (noisy-label robustness), while introducing the parameter-free Bi-Level Graph Enhancer (DGF + CGC) as a novel inference-time module that fuses domain-level and category-level representations via Markov random walk label propagation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean theoretical error bound providing a principled justification for multi-prototype over single-classifier UCDSL is a useful conceptual contribution that reframes the design space for continual domain shift. The parameter-free BiGE module offers a practical, lightweight enhancement that practitioners could adopt without additional training cost. However, the method is limited to classification tasks (the authors explicitly note it has not been extended to detection or regression), and the empirical gains over CoDAG, while consistent across three datasets and four metrics, are modest (roughly 1–3 percentage points), leaving open whether the multi-prototype advantage persists at larger domain counts or with more diverse domain shifts.
