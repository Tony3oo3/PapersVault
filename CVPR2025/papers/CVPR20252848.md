## Problem

Traditional learning-from-noisy-labels (LNL) methods are constrained by the discriminative paradigm, limiting their ability to model the generative relationship between features and labels. While recent label diffusion models (CARD, LRA-diffusion) adopt a generative perspective, their diffusion process lacks explicit information about the noise distribution, making them unable to interpretably avoid generating incorrect labels. This paper addresses the gap by introducing directional noise knowledge directly into the label diffusion process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252848/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes noisy labeled RGB images (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, ImageNet-scale at 224×224) along with pre-trained feature embeddings from extractors such as ViT-L/14, SimCLR, or ResNet-34. Each image is processed through two different data augmentation pipelines to produce dual feature views for the pre-correction stage. The output is a single class label generated via a 5-step DDIM-style reverse diffusion, making inference speed comparable to standard classifiers.

## Method

DLD decouples the label diffusion into two parallel channels: directional diffusion, which progressively shifts the label toward a sample-specific noise distribution y_n (so the forward endpoint is y_T = y_n + ε rather than pure noise), and random diffusion, which adds standard Gaussian noise. Two independent networks predict the directional deviation y_d and the random noise ε, trained with L2 losses conditioned on image features. A one-step label pre-correction module uses multi-view KNN over a pre-trained feature extractor to partition samples into clean, noisy, and hard sets, then estimates y_0 (corrected label) and y_n (noise distribution) per sample without additional training iterations. The feature conditioning is integrated via a fixed decoding layer that maps the label vector into the feature space, and inference uses a de-Markovized 5-step DDIM schedule.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with 60% instance-dependent noise, DLD+ViT achieves 83.14% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the closest baseline LRA-diffusion+ViT (78.12%) by 5.02 points. On CIFAR-100 with 60% IDN, DLD+ViT reaches 69.57%, a 12.36-point improvement over LRA-diffusion+ViT (57.21%) and 13.98 points over the best non-diffusion method (CC+DivideMix, 53.20%). On real-world datasets, DLD achieves 89.4% on Animal-10N, 75.69% on Clothing1M, 84.51% on WebVision, and 83.74% on ILSVRC2012, all setting new state-of-the-art results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DLD+ViT dominates all 18 baselines across every noise level on both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100. The most direct competitor, LRA-diffusion+ViT [4], is outperformed by 0.33–5.02 points on CIFAR-10 and 5.03–12.36 points on CIFAR-100, with the gap widening substantially at higher noise levels (e.g., 69.57% vs. 57.21% at 60% IDN on CIFAR-100). Among non-diffusion methods, DISC [31] and CC+DivideMix [62] are the strongest discriminative baselines, but DLD+ResNet still surpasses them by 2.48–4.55 points on CIFAR-100 at 60% IDN. On real-world datasets, DLD edges out J-ViT [9] by 0.4% on Animal-10N, SANM [46] by 0.06% on Clothing1M, LRA-diffusion [4] by 0.35% on WebVision, and by 1.18% on ILSVRC2012. The paper notes that DLD+ResNet slightly underperforms EPL+ViT [26] on CIFAR-10 at 20% IDN (90.43% vs. 95.95%), which the authors attribute to excessive robust information interfering in low-noise regimes, though DLD+ViT recovers and surpasses all methods in that setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the label diffusion line of work initiated by CARD (Han et al., 2022) and LRA-diffusion (Chen et al., 2024), which reframed classification as a conditional generative task over label vectors. The key architectural departure is the introduction of a dual-channel forward process—directional diffusion (driving labels toward a sample-specific noise distribution) alongside standard random diffusion—inspired by residual denoising diffusion models, which explicitly encodes 'what not to generate' into the denoising trajectory. On the data side, the paper replaces LRA-diffusion's iterative neighbor-retrieval with a one-step, multi-view label pre-correction method based on neighborhood consistency, positioning itself as a lightweight, non-iterative alternative to discriminative LNL pipelines like DivideMix and DISC.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By casting LNL as a generative problem with explicit noise-direction information, DLD offers an interpretable mechanism for robustness that is orthogonal to the filtering/correction strategies dominating the discriminative LNL literature, and it plugs seamlessly into pre-trained backbones (ViT-L/14, SimCLR) without additional training. The 6–21% accuracy gains on CIFAR-100 under IDN (especially the ~12-point gap over LRA-diffusion+ViT at 60% noise) suggest the directional channel captures structure that pure random diffusion misses. Open questions include whether the pre-correction's reliance on a high-quality feature extractor limits applicability in low-resource or domain-shift settings, and whether the generative framing can be extended beyond single-label classification to multi-label, open-set, or multimodal scenarios as the authors themselves note.
