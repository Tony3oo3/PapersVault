## Problem

Existing multimodal datasets for intention and emotion recognition treat these two aspects in isolation and assume fixed modality configurations, failing to capture the intrinsic emotion-intention relationship or the natural uncertainty of missing modalities in real-world social media. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a dataset with authentic modality variation and a framework that jointly models emotion-intention correlations under uncertain modality conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252209/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted 768-dimensional features from up to four modalities (text via BERT, image via ViT-S, video via Video Swin Transformer, audio via wav2vec 2.0), where any subset of modalities may be present per sample following the natural distribution in the MINE dataset (e.g., text+image accounts for ~61% of samples, while text-only is ~19%). Outputs are a multi-label intention vector over 20 classes and a single-label emotion prediction over 11 classes, with the multi-label formulation reflecting that social media posts often express multiple intentions simultaneously.

## Method

BEAR comprises two components: (1) BEIFormer, which first fuses available modalities via MulT cross-attention, then introduces a BEI Prompt containing [MASK] tokens that erase emotion-intention priors; the prompt feature serves as Query while the fused modality feature serves as Key/Value in a second transformer, and the resulting interaction output is aligned to text-encoded ground-truth labels via an NCE contrastive loss, implicitly mining emotion-intention relationships without requiring label access at inference. (2) Modality Asynchronous Prompt (MAP), which assigns a unique set of learnable prompts to each specific missing-modality pattern and injects them via element-wise addition only into the absent modality slots, preserving the integrity of existing modality features—unlike prior prompt methods (e.g., MPMM) that indiscriminately concatenate prompts across all modalities and risk corrupting available features. The total training loss combines standard cross-entropy classification with the NCE alignment term, selected per task (emotion or intention).

## Results

On the MINE test set, BEAR achieves 35.25% Macro F1 for intention recognition (multi-label, 20 classes) and 64.67% accuracy for emotion recognition (single-label, 11 classes), outperforming the strongest baseline MulT by +1.14% Macro F1 and +1.95% accuracy respectively. On the validation set, the gains are larger: +2.65% Macro F1 over MulT for intention and +3.54% accuracy for emotion. BEAR also leads on Micro F1 (52.90%) and Samples F1 (53.35%) for intention, and F1 score (20.55%) and recall (19.95%) for emotion, demonstrating consistent superiority across all metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BEAR outperforms all seven baselines on both intention and emotion metrics across validation and test splits. The closest competitor for intention is TCL_MAP [51] (34.73% Macro F1 on test), which BEAR surpasses by +0.52%; for emotion, MFSA [41] is nearest (64.05% accuracy on test), beaten by +0.62%. The missing-modality-specific baselines MMIN [50] and MPMM [16] lag substantially behind BEAR (e.g., MMIN achieves only 27.15% Macro F1 vs. 35.25% for BEAR on test), confirming that imagination-based and indiscriminate prompt-based strategies are less effective than BEAR's asynchronous prompting. MulT [36], the standard multimodal fusion baseline, is outperformed by +1.14% Macro F1 (intention) and +1.95% accuracy (emotion) on test. MISA [8] and MFSA [41], while competitive on Micro F1 for intention, trail BEAR by over 10% on Macro F1, highlighting the difficulty of the multi-label intention task. On CMU-MOSEI generalization experiments (Table 7), MAP consistently improves all four tested architectures (MulT, MFN, BERT_MAG, LMF) by +0.86% to +3.98% Macro F1, validating its transferability beyond MINE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends two separate research threads—multimodal emotion recognition (IEMOCAP, CMU-MOSEI, MELD) and multimodal intention recognition (MIntRec, Intentonomy)—into a unified joint understanding framework, while departing from prior missing-modality work (MMIN, MPMM, M3S) that relies on artificial random sampling to simulate modality absence. The BEIFormer component builds on MulT-style cross-modal transformers and contrastive learning (NCE loss) but introduces implicit label reasoning via [MASK] tokens to mine emotion-intention correlations without explicit label conditioning at inference. The Modality Asynchronous Prompt (MAP) differentiates itself from MPMM's indiscriminate full-modality prompting by selectively augmenting only missing modalities via element-wise addition, preserving existing feature integrity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MINE fills a genuine gap by providing the first large-scale benchmark where emotion and intention are jointly annotated over naturally occurring (not artificially dropped) modality combinations from social media, enabling more realistic evaluation of multimodal systems. However, the method's practical value is tempered by the ablation showing that a 'Complete Information' baseline (using one label as prior at inference) yields +9.06% Macro F1 versus BEAR's +2.44%, suggesting the implicit reasoning captures only a fraction of the available emotion-intention signal. Open questions include whether the observed emotion-intention correlations generalize beyond English Twitter content and the ten selected topics, and whether the modest 2–3% gains over strong baselines scale to larger, more diverse datasets.
