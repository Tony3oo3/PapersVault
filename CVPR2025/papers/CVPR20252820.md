## Problem

Existing face normalization methods are limited to handling a single type of facial variation (pose, expression, occlusion, or illumination) and are typically domain-specific, making them inadequate for real-world scenarios where multiple contaminations coexist across heterogeneous domains (visible, near-infrared, sketch). No prior framework unifies multi-variation removal with cross-domain prototype generation while simultaneously extracting domain-agnostic identity features for downstream recognition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252820/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224 contaminated face image from any of three domains (VIS, NIR, Sketch) along with a 4-bit control mask (1 bit indicating whether normalization is requested, 3 bits specifying the target domain) and produces a 224×224 frontal, neutral-expression prototype in the chosen target domain. A frozen ResNet-50 encoder (pre-trained on MS1M, fine-tuned on VGGFace2) extracts 2048-dim features that are further mapped to domain-agnostic representations. For the HFR task, the output is a fused feature vector (concatenation of domain-agnostic and identity-discriminative features) used for cosine-similarity-based identity matching.

## Method

UMFN fuses an autoencoder with a GAN in a unified architecture: a frozen ResNet-50 encoder feeds a mapping network that adversarially decouples domain-agnostic identity features from domain-specific information via a domain classifier, while a decoder reconstructs or generates images conditioned on these features and a 4-bit control mask. Prototype generation is driven by a WGAN-GP objective with a multi-scale Markov discriminator (global and local PatchGAN paths using InstanceNorm) that targets a condensed distribution of clean prototypes rather than the full contaminated data distribution. Identity preservation is enforced through an L2 similarity loss between the input's latent features and those re-extracted from the generated prototype. For HFR, a separate encoder extracts identity-discriminative features from generated prototypes, which are concatenated with the input's domain-agnostic features; a contrastive loss aligns generated-prototype features with real-prototype features to mitigate noise and identity drift introduced during cross-domain synthesis. The entire pipeline is unsupervised, requiring no identity labels, and supports concurrent training on multiple unpaired datasets from different domains within a single network.

## Results

On the HFR task, UMFN achieves 99.1% Rank-1 accuracy and 98.7% VR@FAR=0.001 on LAMP-HQ, and 99.9% Rank-1 and 99.7% VR@FAR=0.001 on CASIA NIR-VIS 2.0, outperforming all five compared HFR methods. For face normalization quality, UMFN boosts ResNet-18 verification rate to 95.92% on IJB-A (a 5.35% gain over the unnormalized baseline) and 99.57% on LFW, while also achieving the lowest Symmetry Index (15.81 on IJB-A, 14.61 on LFW), indicating the most symmetric and distortion-free prototypes among compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UMFN achieves the best Rank-1 and VR@FAR=0.001 on both LAMP-HQ and CASIA NIR-VIS 2.0, beating the strongest recent competitor NLPF (2024) by 0.3% Rank-1 and 0.4% VR on LAMP-HQ, and by 0.2% Rank-1 and 0.2% VR on CASIA. The gap is most dramatic on LAMP-HQ VR@FAR=0.001, where UMFN (98.7%) surpasses ADCANs (96.1%) by 2.6 points and MMTN (79.2%) by a striking 19.5 points, suggesting that the contrastive learning mechanism and feature fusion are particularly effective under the more challenging multi-pose, high-quality LAMP-HQ protocol. On CASIA, LPL (2024) underperforms on VR@FAR=0.001 (97.5%) despite competitive Rank-1 (99.6%), while UMFN leads on both metrics. For face normalization quality (Table 1), UMFN also outperforms R&R and CFR on both LFW and IJB-A in verification rate and Symmetry Index, with the largest SI improvement being 9.2 points over CFR on IJB-A (15.81 vs. 24.76), confirming that UMFN produces more symmetric, less distorted frontal prototypes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by unifying two previously separate research threads: single-contamination face normalization (pose, expression, illumination, or occlusion removal in a fixed domain) and cross-domain face synthesis (style transfer between VIS, NIR, and sketch without variation removal). Rather than building on a specific prior architecture, UMFN repurposes well-established components—WGAN-GP training, PatchGAN discriminators, adversarial domain adaptation via a feature decoupling network, and contrastive learning—into a single autoencoder-GAN framework that simultaneously handles multiple variation types across multiple domains. The closest conceptual predecessor is the authors' own VD-GAN [32], which addressed multi-contamination but only in a single domain, and cross-domain synthesis methods like CycleGAN or PMSGAN, which handle domain transfer but ignore facial variations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in eliminating the need for separate normalization pipelines per domain and per variation type, which is a real bottleneck in criminal investigation and biometric authentication workflows involving heterogeneous sensor data. The unsupervised, unpaired training paradigm (no identity labels, no paired cross-domain images) lowers the data barrier considerably. However, limitations remain: the encoder is frozen (ResNet-50 pre-trained on MS1M/VGGFace2), which may cap expressiveness; sketch-domain outputs show visible local distortions; and the framework is validated on only three domains. Open questions include scalability to additional modalities (thermal, 3D), real-time deployment, and whether the frozen-encoder constraint fundamentally limits identity preservation under extreme contamination.
