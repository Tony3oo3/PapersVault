## Problem

Existing video virtual try-on methods rely on temporal attention modules to reduce inter-frame inconsistencies but overlook the critical spatiotemporal pose interactions between the human and the garment. Specifically, they fail to model both the intra-frame spatial alignment between human and garment poses (which governs how clothing drapes, wrinkles, and covers the body) and the long-term temporal dynamics of human poses across the video (which governs garment motion coherence). This gap leads to temporal flickering and poor garment adaptation, especially when the target garment differs significantly in style from the original.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single garment image, a source human video, and a binary clothing-agnostic mask sequence as input. Internally, the Main U-Net consumes a 9-channel tensor (4 noisy latent channels + 4 clothing-agnostic latent channels + 1 mask channel), while the Garment U-Net processes the garment image independently. Human poses are extracted via DW-Pose and garment poses via a custom YOLOv8-based estimator trained on manually labeled landmarks (e.g., 9 keypoints for upper-body garments). The output is a temporally consistent video at 512×384 resolution (24-frame clips during training) showing the person wearing the target garment, with a sliding-window inference strategy for longer sequences.

## Method

DPIDM employs a dual-branch diffusion architecture (Main U-Net for video inpainting + Garment U-Net for fine-grained garment feature extraction), both initialized from SD v1.5, with CLIP image embeddings replacing text embeddings for global garment conditioning. The core novelty is a hierarchical pose-aware attention module with four blocks: (1) Pose-Aware Spatial Attention (PASA) injects concatenated human and garment pose embeddings via a zero-initialized two-layer FC adapter into self-attention over spatially concatenated human-garment features, enabling intra-frame pose alignment; (2) Temporal-Shift Attention (TSA) shifts tokens from L preceding frames into the current frame to capture short-term temporal dependencies without full 3D attention cost; (3) Cross-Attention with CLIP garment image embeddings; and (4) Pose-Aware Temporal Attention (PATA) adds human pose embeddings to the cross-attention output before standard temporal attention to model long-term pose dynamics. A Temporal Regularized Attention Loss (TRA) penalizes frame-to-frame variation in PASA self-attention maps (applied to the last two decoder layers with γ=0.5), and training uses a single-stage image-video joint strategy with per-keypoint pose dropping (p=0.05) for robustness.

## Results

On the VVT dataset, DPIDM achieves VFID_I of 0.506 and VFID_R of 0.047, representing a 60.5% relative improvement over the previous best method GPD-VVTO (VFID_I 1.280). On the larger ViViD dataset, DPIDM attains VFID_I of 0.488, a 74.2% improvement over ViViD (1.894), while also leading in SSIM (0.883) and LPIPS (0.081). On the image-based VITON-HD benchmark, DPIDM achieves the best scores across all metrics: SSIM 0.893, LPIPS 0.067, FID_u 8.15, and KID_u 0.32.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPIDM outperforms all 10 competing methods on VVT in LPIPS (0.041 vs. best competitor 0.054 from TunnelTry-on) and VFID_I (0.506 vs. 1.280 from GPD-VVTO), with the 60.5% VFID_I gap over GPD-VVTO being the headline result. The only metric where DPIDM does not lead is SSIM, where ViViD scores 0.949 versus DPIDM's 0.930, though the authors attribute this to ViViD's use of ~6× more high-resolution training data. On ViViD, DPIDM dominates all four metrics, beating ViViD by 74.2% in VFID_I (0.488 vs. 1.894) and by 4.7 points in SSIM (0.883 vs. 0.846). On VITON-HD, DPIDM achieves the best LPIPS (0.067), FID_u (8.15), and KID_u (0.32), narrowly edging out GPD-VVTO (LPIPS 0.070, FID_u 8.57, KID_u 0.78) and IDM (KID_u 0.55). GAN-based baselines (CP-VTON, FW-GAN, PBAFN, ClothFormer) are substantially outperformed on all metrics, confirming the advantage of diffusion-based approaches for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

DPIDM extends the dual-branch diffusion architecture established by ViViD and GPD-VVTO, where a Main U-Net handles video denoising and a Garment U-Net extracts clothing features. The key departure is the introduction of a hierarchical pose-aware attention module (PASA, TSA, CA, PATA) that injects synchronized human and garment pose embeddings directly into each attention layer via a lightweight pose adapter, rather than the PoseGuider-style approach of feeding pose images as additional input channels. The temporal-shift attention block adapts the TSM paradigm from video understanding to diffusion-based generation, and the Temporal Regularized Attention Loss is a novel training objective that penalizes inter-frame variation in self-attention maps. The single-stage image-video joint training strategy also departs from the multi-stage pipelines used by TunnelTry-on and GPD-VVTO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 60.5% VFID improvement over GPD-VVTO on VVT and 74.2% over ViViD on ViViD demonstrate that explicit spatiotemporal pose modeling is a critical missing ingredient in video try-on, not merely a refinement. The pose adapter design—injecting pose into attention modules rather than as input channels—offers a generalizable recipe for pose-conditioned video generation beyond try-on. However, the approach depends on accurate pose estimation (DW-Pose for humans, a custom-trained estimator for garments with manually labeled landmarks), which limits robustness in the wild. Open questions include scalability to longer videos, multi-person scenes, and whether the pose-aware attention paradigm transfers to other video editing or generation tasks.
