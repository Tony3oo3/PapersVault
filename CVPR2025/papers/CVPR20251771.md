## Problem

Existing multimodal retrieval methods rely on task-specific fine-tuning of dual-encoder VLMs (e.g., CLIP), which is cumbersome, laborious, and fails to generalize to unseen retrieval tasks. The paper asks whether generative Large Multimodal Models (LMMs), with their rich language understanding and flexible input handling, can be repurposed as universal retrievers and rerankers that unify all retrieval tasks under one formulation and extrapolate to previously unseen tasks without additional training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251771/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts queries and candidates in arbitrary format—text, image, or interleaved image-text sequences—and produces a ranked candidate list. Embeddings are extracted via an Explicit One-word Limitation (EOL) prompt that asks the LMM to 'summarize' the input in one word; the last hidden state before the <emb> token serves as the representation. Reranking outputs are either a YES/NO probability (pointwise) or a position index (listwise), and the final ranking score is a weighted sum of the retrieval cosine similarity and the reranking score (default α=0.5).

## Method

LamRA inserts lightweight LoRA modules into Qwen2-VL-7B (vision encoder frozen) and trains them in two stages: (1) text-only pre-training on the NLI dataset with InfoNCE contrastive loss to teach the LMM to produce retrieval-oriented embeddings, and (2) multimodal instruction tuning on M-BEIR (8 tasks, 10 datasets, 1.1M samples) with task-specific prompts, yielding LamRA-Ret. A second LoRA module, LamRA-Rank, is then trained jointly on pointwise (YES/NO cross-entropy) and listwise (position-prediction cross-entropy) reranking objectives using hard negatives mined from the top-100 of LamRA-Ret. The key architectural insight is that a generative LMM's autoregressive language modeling, combined with the EOL embedding trick, provides a unified representation space that naturally handles interleaved multi-modal inputs and complex text—capabilities where dual-encoder models like CLIP struggle—enabling zero-shot transfer to unseen retrieval tasks.

## Results

On the M-BEIR benchmark (16 retrieval tasks), LamRA achieves an average Recall of 63.7, substantially outperforming the zero-shot Qwen2-VL-7B baseline (23.0) and the supervised dual-encoder UniIR-CLIP (50.6). On unseen datasets (Table 4), LamRA reaches 97.9 R@1 on ShareGPT4V, 98.8 R@1 on Urban-1K, and 42.8 R@1 on CIRCO, surpassing all dual-encoder baselines including EVA-CLIP-18B and E5-V. In the held-out task generalization experiment (Table 3), LamRA zero-shot on three unseen task types achieves 54.2 R@5 on InfoSeek (q^{i},q^{t}→c^{t}), exceeding the supervised UniIR-CLIP (48.9) by 5.3 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against zero-shot dual-encoder baselines (CLIP-L, SigLIP, BLIP, BLIP2), LamRA dominates on all complex retrieval tasks: e.g., on composed image retrieval (CIRR), LamRA scores 63.7 R@5 versus CLIP-L's 32.5 and SigLIP's 37.2. Against the supervised dual-encoder UniIR-CLIP, LamRA-Ret already surpasses it on complex tasks (e.g., +24.2 R@5 on InfoSeek text-image-to-text, +8.5 R@5 on CIRR), and the full LamRA with reranking extends this gap to +13.1 and +13.1 respectively. On unseen datasets (Table 4), LamRA outperforms E5-V, MagicLens, and EVA-CLIP-18B across nearly all benchmarks, with particularly large margins on long-text retrieval (Urban-1K: 98.8 vs. 83.3 R@1) and composed retrieval (CIRCO: 42.8 vs. 24.8 R@1). In the held-out task experiment (Table 3), LamRA zero-shot on three unseen task types beats the fully supervised UniIR-CLIP on InfoSeek (54.2 vs. 48.9 R@5) and OVEN (59.1 vs. 48.9 R@5), demonstrating strong task-level generalization. For video retrieval (Table 8), LamRA achieves 44.7 R@1 on MSR-VTT and 52.4 R@1 on MSVD, outperforming InternVideo and UMT-L but remaining below InternVideo2 (55.9 and 59.3 R@1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant dual-encoder paradigm (CLIP, SigLIP, BLIP2) for multimodal retrieval by repurposing a generative LMM (Qwen2-VL-7B) with lightweight LoRA adapters. It builds directly on E5-V and the Explicit One-word Limitation technique for extracting embeddings from generative models, but extends this with a two-stage training strategy (text-only NLI pretraining followed by multimodal instruction tuning on M-BEIR) and a novel joint pointwise/listwise reranking module. Unlike UniIR, which fine-tunes dual-encoder VLMs on a unified benchmark, LamRA treats all retrieval tasks as instruction-following problems within a single autoregressive LMM, enabling zero-shot extrapolation to unseen task formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's ability to handle 10+ heterogeneous retrieval tasks (composed image retrieval, long-text retrieval, document retrieval, video retrieval) with a single model and generalize zero-shot to previously unseen tasks and datasets offers a practical path toward unified retrieval systems without task-specific fine-tuning. Key limitations include the high inference cost of LMM-based retrieval (mitigated but not eliminated by pre-extracting candidate features), the fact that experiments are limited to 2B and 7B model sizes, and that video retrieval still lags behind purpose-built video models like InternVideo2. Open questions include whether scaling to larger LMMs (e.g., 70B+) yields further gains, how to reduce per-query inference latency for production deployment, and whether the framework can be extended to streaming or real-time retrieval settings.
