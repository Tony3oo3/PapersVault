## Problem

Scene coordinate regression (SCR) methods offer compact map sizes (tens of MB vs. GB-scale for feature matching) but remain significantly less robust than feature matching on large-scale scenes with illumination changes and image-level ambiguities. Prior SCR approaches like ACE and GLACE still lag by orders of magnitude in accuracy under challenging conditions such as day-to-night transitions, limiting their practical utility.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes an RGB query image (640×480) and known camera intrinsics, retrieves 10 nearest training images via NetVLAD to generate multiple global encoding hypotheses, and feeds patch-level local features (PCA-reduced to 128 dims) concatenated with 256-dim Node2Vec global encodings into a scene-specific MLP. The network outputs per-keypoint 3D scene coordinates; PnP with RANSAC is run for each global hypothesis and the pose with the most inliers is selected. The entire scene representation fits in ~47 MB, compared to 7.82 GB for the strongest feature matching baseline.

## Method

R-SCoRe introduces three key mechanisms: (1) a covisibility-graph-aligned global encoding learned via Node2Vec contrastive training, which replaces the isotropic Gaussian noise augmentation of GLACE with graph-neighbor sampling during training and multi-hypothesis retrieval at test time to resolve ambiguities; (2) a depth-adjusted reprojection loss that normalizes the 2D reprojection error by an observation standard deviation σ_o = √((σ₃/d)² + σ₂²), counteracting the inherent bias toward distant points in implicit triangulation, applied selectively to a coarse intermediate output while the final refinement layer uses the original loss; (3) a two-stage coarse-to-refine MLP architecture where the coarse coordinate is fed back via sinusoidal positional encoding into a refinement head, combined with a Geman-McClure robust loss (heavier tail than tanh) and a decaying consistency loss to stabilize training. The approach requires no 3D ground-truth supervision and no network ensembles, yet achieves 64.3% accuracy at (0.25 m, 2°) on Aachen Night—10× better than prior SCR methods at comparable map sizes.

## Results

On Aachen Day-Night, R-SCoRe (DeDoDe, no depth) achieves 66.3% accuracy at the (0.25m, 2°) threshold for nighttime queries with a map size of only 47MB, compared to 1.0% for GLACE and 0.0% for ACE — a 64× improvement over the previous best SCR method. On the daytime split, it reaches 79.0% at the same threshold, approaching the 89.6% of HLoc+SPSG while requiring 170× less storage (47MB vs. 7.82GB). On Hyundai Department Store, R-SCoRe (DeDoDe + depth) achieves 61.5% at (0.1m, 1°) on the challenging B1 scene, outperforming GLACE (0.9%) by over 60× while using a 130MB map versus 505GB for HLoc+D2-Net.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against feature matching (FM) baselines, R-SCoRe trails HLoc+SPSG [50] at the strictest threshold (64.3% vs. 86.7% at night, 0.25m/2°) but uses 170× less memory (47MB vs. 7.82GB). It decisively outperforms AS [55] (66.3% vs. 39.8% at night), Squeezer [72] (66.3% vs. 50.0%), and QP+R.Sift [41] (66.3% vs. 16.3%, a 4× margin) on the nighttime split. Among SCR methods, R-SCoRe is 64× more accurate than GLACE [68] (66.3% vs. 1.0% at night) and 10× more accurate than ACE [9] (66.3% vs. 0.0%), while also surpassing HSCNet++ [70] by 46% (66.3% vs. 43.9%) with a 6× smaller map. The most accurate prior SCR method, Neumap [64], achieves 48.0% at night but requires a 27× larger map (1.26GB) and is prohibitively slow. On Hyundai Department Store, R-SCoRe outperforms all SCR baselines by wide margins (e.g., 61.5% vs. 0.9% for GLACE on B1) and is competitive with HLoc+D2-Net [50] (61.5% vs. 73.7% at 0.1m/1° on B1) while using a map three orders of magnitude smaller (130MB vs. 505GB). PoseNet [31] is effectively non-functional on these scenes (0.0% accuracy), confirming that direct pose regression remains unsuitable for large-scale localization.

## Relation to Prior Work

R-SCoRe extends the GLACE (CVPR 2024) SCR pipeline by replacing its pretrained image-retrieval global encoding with Node2Vec embeddings learned directly from the covisibility graph topology, and by substituting isotropic Gaussian noise augmentation with covisibility-graph-based neighbor sampling. The output supervision builds on ACE's (CVPR 2023) dynamic robust loss but introduces a depth-adjusted reprojection error to correct a previously unaddressed bias toward distant points. The local encoding departs from the DSAC* backbone used in prior SCR work in favor of pretrained off-the-shelf feature extractors (LoFTR, DeDoDe), and a refinement module with positional encoding is added to the MLP architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

R-SCoRe demonstrates that SCR-based localization can reach near-feature-matching accuracy (64.3% at 0.25m/2° on Aachen Night) with a 47 MB map, compared to 7.82 GB for the best feature-matching method—a 170× reduction that makes SCR a practical alternative for memory-constrained deployment. The depth-bias finding reveals a subtle but systematic training pathology in reprojection-based supervision that likely affects all prior SCR methods. Remaining gaps include the accuracy shortfall at the strictest thresholds relative to feature matching, out-of-distribution generalization, and the reliance on a precomputed covisibility graph at training time, leaving open whether fully unsupervised or online graph construction is feasible.
