## Problem

Existing deep learning sea ice forecasting methods are confined to the 25 km resolution of passive microwave satellite products, yet finer-scale forecasts are critical for operational applications such as polar shipping and coastal community planning. Because high-resolution sea ice data products do not exist and in situ observations are too sparse to train a pan-Arctic model at finer scales, forecasting sea ice concentration below 25 km remains largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252656/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The forecasting module takes N consecutive 25 km SIC maps (448×304 pixels, single channel, values 0–100%) and outputs N subsequent SIC maps at the same 25 km grid, with N set to match the desired lead time (7 days, 8 weeks, or 6 months). The guided diffusion module then consumes the 25 km forecast as a conditioning signal and produces a 6.25 km SIC map (4× linear upscaling) via zero-shot sampling; a patch-based strategy with configurable stride extends this to arbitrary output resolutions. The diffusion model is pre-trained on 25 km daily SIC maps resized to 256×256 and generalizes zero-shot to weekly and monthly averaged inputs.

## Method

IceDiff is a two-module cooperative framework. The forecasting module is a U-Net built on a Swin Transformer V2 backbone with ResNet_TSAM blocks for channel-wise temporal-spatial attention, trained with MSE loss to predict SIC at three temporal scales. The guided diffusion module repurposes an unconditional DDPM pre-trained on 25 km daily SIC maps as a generative prior for super-resolution: at each reverse diffusion step, the model estimates x̃₀, passes it through an optimizable convolution kernel that simulates up-scale degradation, and computes a distance loss against the low-resolution forecast. The gradient of this loss simultaneously updates x̃₀ (steering the denoising direction) and the convolution kernel parameters (adapting the degradation model), effectively injecting low-resolution guidance into the sampling without any conditional training. A patch-based strategy partitions the output into overlapping sub-regions, each with its own kernel, and aggregates updates via binary masks to support arbitrary target resolutions.

## Results

On the 7-day daily SIC forecasting task (CDR dataset), IceDiff achieves RMSE 0.0396, MAE 0.0080, and R² 0.989, outperforming SICNet (RMSE 0.0490, MAE 0.0100, R² 0.982) by ~19% in RMSE. For the 6-month seasonal forecast (CDR), IceDiff reaches RMSE 0.0648 and R² 0.919, substantially surpassing IceNet (RMSE 0.1820, R² 0.567) and MT-IceNet (RMSE 0.0777, R² 0.915). In the downscaling task, IceDiff achieves FID 29.34 and Consistency 6.84 at 7-day lead, beating the best diffusion baseline GDP (FID 40.15, Consistency 8.26) by 27% in FID and 17% in Consistency across all three temporal scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For 7-day SIC forecasting, IceDiff outperforms SICNet [35] on every metric across both NT and CDR datasets, reducing RMSE by 25% (NT: 0.0324 vs 0.0430) and 19% (CDR: 0.0396 vs 0.0490), while also cutting IIEE by ~23–29% and SIE_dif by 83% (NT) and 56% (CDR). For the 6-month seasonal forecast, IceDiff dramatically surpasses IceNet [3] (RMSE 0.0573 vs 0.1820 on NT, a 69% reduction) and improves over MT-IceNet [1] by 26% in RMSE (0.0573 vs 0.0777) and 4.8% in R² (0.958 vs 0.915). IceFormer [42] and SICNet₉₀ [34] are reported on different datasets (BT and NT respectively) with limited metrics, preventing a fully controlled comparison, but IceDiff's 8-week results (RMSE 0.0403 on NT) suggest competitive or superior subseasonal skill. In the downscaling comparison (Table 2), IceDiff dominates all baselines: it beats the GAN-based DGP [32] by 60–70% in FID and the diffusion-based GDP [17] by 27–29% in FID and 17–39% in Consistency across all three temporal scales, confirming that the guided diffusion prior with optimizable convolution kernels produces materially sharper and more faithful high-resolution SIC maps than both interpolation and prior generative approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

IceDiff combines two established lines of work: (1) the U-Net + Swin Transformer V2 forecasting architecture, which inherits the TSAM channel-attention block from SICNet and the multi-scale encoder-decoder structure from IceNet, and (2) the gradient-guided sampling framework from Dhariwal & Nichol's conditional diffusion formulation, applied here with a novel optimizable convolution kernel that bridges the resolution gap between the 25 km guidance signal and the 6.25 km target. Conceptually, it extends SwinRDM's SwinRNN+DDPM recipe for atmospheric super-resolution to the sea ice domain, but departs from prior single-model SIC forecasters by decoupling accurate coarse-scale prediction from fine-scale generation into two cooperative modules, and introduces a patch-based strategy to handle arbitrary output resolutions with a single fixed-size diffusion prior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IceDiff is the first framework to produce pan-Arctic SIC forecasts at 6.25 km, a fourfold improvement over the 25 km resolution that has constrained all prior data-driven sea ice models, with direct practical value for polar shipping routes, coastal community planning, and ecosystem monitoring. A key limitation is the absence of high-resolution ground truth: all training and evaluation data are 25 km satellite products, so the fidelity of the 6.25 km output can only be assessed via FID and consistency metrics rather than against observations. The zero-shot generalization of the diffusion prior across daily, weekly, and monthly temporal scales is a useful property, but the broader open question is whether the generated fine-scale structures are physically realistic or merely statistically plausible, which will require validation against in-situ or higher-resolution satellite observations as they become available.
