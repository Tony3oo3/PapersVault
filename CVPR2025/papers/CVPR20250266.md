## Problem

Despite the proliferation of ST-GCN variants for skeleton-based human action recognition, their top-1 accuracy deviations across NTU-RGB+D benchmarks remain below 2% after aligning input settings, suggesting the models may be over-parameterized. No prior work had formally tested this hypothesis for ST-GCNs or proposed a practical mechanism to exploit the redundancy while preserving or improving performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250266/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes skeleton joint coordinates (N joints × T frames × d dimensions) organized as a spatial-temporal graph, where the spatial graph encodes intra-body connections and the temporal graph links identical joints across consecutive frames. It also supports multi-modality inputs (joints, joint motion, bone, bone motion) in the multi-stream fusion setting. The output is a single action class label per video clip, with the multi-level sparsity variant aggregating predictions from four sparsity-level sub-networks before the final classification.

## Method

The paper proposes a sparse ST-GCNs generator that inverts the standard LTH procedure: instead of learning a mask over fixed weights, it fixes a randomly initialized or predefined binary mask and trains the convolution kernel weights to adapt to the sparse structure. Training proceeds in two stages: a warm-up stage where all parameters are optimized with a group lasso penalty (L_c) that compresses information from masked weights into the retained sub-network, followed by a fine-tuning stage where only unmasked parameters are updated. Building on this generator, the paper assembles multi-level sparsity ST-GCNs by training the same backbone at four sparsity levels (0.6, 0.8, 0.95, 0.99) and aggregating their predictions, achieving >1% top-1 accuracy improvement over the dense baseline while using only 66% of the parameters.

## Results

The multi-level sparsity DG-STGCN (A0.66), using only 66% of the dense model's parameters, achieves 92.9% / 97.4% / 90.4% / 91.4% top-1 accuracy on NTU60-Xsub / Xview / NTU120-Xsub / Xset, 47.5% on Kinetics-400, and 95.3% on FineGYM. Sparse DG-STGCN at 95% sparsity (S0.95) retains 92.8% / 97.1% / 88.7% / 90.4% on the four NTU benchmarks, a degradation of <1% on NTU60 and <1.5% on NTU120. The multi-level sparsity assembly consistently outperforms the dense DG-STGCN baseline by 0.8–1.0 points on NTU120-Xsub and Xset while using 34% fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The multi-level sparsity DG-STGCN (A0.66) outperforms the dense DG-STGCN [18] on NTU120-Xsub (90.4 vs 89.6) and NTU120-Xset (91.4 vs 91.3) while using only 66% of the parameters, and also surpasses it on Kinetics-400 (47.5 vs 40.3). Against PoseConv3D [21], the sparse assembly wins on NTU120-Xsub (90.4 vs 86.9), NTU120-Xset (91.4 vs 90.3), and FineGYM (95.3 vs 94.3), but trails on NTU60-Xsub (92.9 vs 94.1) and Kinetics-400 (47.5 vs 47.7). Compared to CTR-GCN [10], the sparse model leads on all four NTU benchmarks by 0.5–1.5 points. Against ST-GCN++ [17], it wins on NTU120-Xsub (90.4 vs 88.6) and NTU120-Xset (91.4 vs 90.8) but loses on Kinetics-400 (47.5 vs 49.1). Versus SKE2GRID [4], the sparse assembly is competitive on NTU120 (90.4 vs 87.3, 91.4 vs 90.8) but falls short on NTU60-Xview (97.4 vs 98.6). Overall, the paper reports SOTA on three datasets (NTU120-Xsub, NTU120-Xset, FineGYM) and comparable results on the remaining three.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis (LTH) from Frankle & Carbin, originally demonstrated in CNNs, to spatial-temporal graph convolutional networks for skeleton-based action recognition, and is the first to formally test over-parameterization in ST-GCNs. It departs from the standard LTH protocol (train masks on fixed weights) by inverting the process: masks are randomly initialized and held fixed while weights are trained, combined with a two-stage warm-up/fine-tuning schedule and a group lasso penalty borrowed from sparse regression. The multi-level sparsity assembly draws on multi-stream fusion frameworks but repurposes them to combine sparsity levels of the same backbone rather than different modalities or architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that ST-GCNs remain within 1% of dense performance at 95% sparsity across four backbones and four datasets provides strong evidence that the field has been deploying unnecessarily large models, with direct implications for edge deployment and inference cost. The multi-level sparsity assembly—improving accuracy while using only 66% of parameters—is a practical recipe that could be adopted by practitioners building HAR systems. Key open questions remain: sparsity levels are set manually rather than learned, and the multi-level model is assembled post-hoc rather than trained end-to-end, both of which limit the method's generality and automation potential.
