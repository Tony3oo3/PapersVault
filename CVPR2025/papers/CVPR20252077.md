## Problem

Existing approaches to predicting interactive object dynamics either rely on explicit 3D reconstruction and physics simulation (computationally intensive, error-prone, limited generalizability) or generate only a single future state transition, failing to capture the continuous, temporally coherent dynamics that unfold after an interaction. This paper asks whether the implicit physics knowledge embedded in large-scale video generative models can be harnessed to synthesize full interactive dynamics without any explicit simulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252077/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB input image (the initial scene) and a driving motion signal in the form of a pixel-aligned binary mask sequence (N frames, e.g., hand masks from SAM2) that specifies the motion of one or more 'driving' objects. The output is a 14-frame video at 7 FPS (256×256 or 256×384 resolution) depicting the full scene dynamics, including the motion of uncontrolled objects that receive no explicit signal. Notably, the control signal covers only the driving entity; all other object motion must be inferred by the model from its learned dynamics prior.

## Method

InterDyn extends Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) with a ControlNet-like trainable branch while keeping all SVD weights frozen to preserve its learned dynamics prior. A small CNN encodes the binary mask control signal into latent space, which is added to the noisy input latent before being processed by a trainable copy of the SVD encoder; the encoder's outputs are injected into the frozen SVD decoder via skip connections modulated by zero-initialized convolutions. The control branch interleaves convolutional, spatial, and temporal blocks to process the mask sequence in a temporally-aware manner, making it robust to noisy inputs. Training uses the EDM framework with Adam (lr 1e-5) on two H100 GPUs, with 5% classifier-free guidance dropout on the input image; inference runs 50 Euler denoising steps. The key architectural insight is that the frozen video diffusion backbone already encodes implicit physics and rendering knowledge, so only a lightweight control interface needs to be learned.

## Results

On Something-Something-v2, InterDyn (256×384) achieves the best overall performance with SSIM 0.680, PSNR 19.04, LPIPS 0.252, FVD 22.22, KVD 2.09, and Motion Fidelity 0.641. The 256×256 variant achieves an even lower FVD of 19.27 and KVD of 1.99, representing a ~77% FVD improvement over CosHand-Autoregressive (90.30) and a ~37.5% LPIPS improvement over CosHand-Independent (0.313 → 0.252). InterDyn outperforms all baselines on spatio-temporal and motion metrics, losing only on FID and KID to CosHand-Independent (2.95 and 0.002 respectively), which the authors attribute to CosHand's fine-tuned spatial prior versus InterDyn's frozen SVD backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InterDyn decisively outperforms both CosHand variants on spatio-temporal coherence (FVD 19.27–22.22 vs. 90.30–91.18) and motion fidelity (0.633–0.641 vs. 0.432–0.570), confirming that generating a full video captures post-interaction dynamics that frame-by-frame or autoregressive state-transition approaches miss. Against the language-instructed baselines Seer and DynamiCrafter, InterDyn achieves substantially lower FVD (19–22 vs. 204–287) and KVD (2.0 vs. 32–81), though these baselines lack a motion-fidelity metric. The only metrics where InterDyn trails are FID and KID, where CosHand-Independent (FID 2.95, KID 0.002) edges out InterDyn (FID 4.95–5.07, KID 0.004); the authors attribute this to CosHand's end-to-end fine-tuning on SSV2 versus InterDyn's frozen SVD spatial prior. On CLEVRER, InterDyn is evaluated qualitatively, demonstrating force propagation across multiple uncontrolled objects and counterfactual generation (different futures from the same input image under different control signals), capabilities none of the baselines support.

## Relation to Prior Work

InterDyn extends Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) with a ControlNet-style branch to inject a driving-motion control signal, positioning video diffusion models as implicit physics simulators rather than relying on explicit 3D reconstruction and physics engines (Wu et al.) or graph-based interaction networks (CoPhy, Interaction Networks). It deliberately departs from the two-state formulation of CosHand, which predicts a single future frame from current and future hand masks, by generating full temporal dynamics that capture post-interaction motion and force propagation. The method also contrasts with text-controlled video generation approaches (Seer, DynamiCrafter) by using pixel-aligned motion masks for fine-grained control rather than language instructions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that large-scale video generative models encode sufficient implicit physics to produce multi-object force propagation and counterfactual dynamics without any explicit simulation, opening a path toward using generative models as generalized physics engines. Key limitations include reliance on binary masks as the sole control signal, short video lengths (14 frames at 7 FPS), and a frozen SVD backbone that constrains image quality. Open questions include whether this implicit physics generalizes to longer horizons, more complex deformable-body interactions, and whether the approach can be combined with explicit physics for hybrid simulation.
