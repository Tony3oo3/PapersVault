## Problem

Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG), the standard technique for conditional diffusion model sampling, imposes a sum-to-one constraint on its guidance coefficients that makes the denoising process theoretically incompatible with the forward diffusion process. This incompatibility causes an expectation shift: the mean of the generated distribution drifts away from the ground-truth conditional distribution, worsening at higher guidance strengths. Although this flaw is recognized in theory, it has been largely ignored in practice, leaving a gap between the theoretical guarantees of diffusion models and the actual behavior of guided sampling.

## Input / Output

ReCFG operates as a post-hoc correction on any pre-trained diffusion model (LDM, DiT, EDM2, SD3) without retraining. At inference time it consumes a condition (class label or text prompt), a user-chosen guidance strength γ₁, and a pre-computed lookup table of pixel-wise expectation ratios E[ε_θ(x_t,c,t)]/E[ε_θ(x_t,t)] indexed by condition, timestep, and spatial location. The lookup table is built offline by sampling ~500 images per condition through the forward process, taking roughly 3 hours on one A100 GPU for 100 conditions. Output is a standard generated image (256×256 or 512×512) with no change to the sampling pipeline's interface or NFE budget.

## Method

ReCFG relaxes CFG's sum-to-one constraint by introducing two independent, element-wise coefficients γ₁ and γ₀ in the score interpolation (Eq. 28), then derives a closed-form solution γ₀ = (1−γ₁)·E[ε_θ(x_t,c,t)]/E[ε_θ(x_t,t)] (Eq. 42) that guarantees zero expectation of the combined score, thereby eliminating the expectation shift. The paper proves via a toy Gaussian distribution (Theorem 2) that native CFG shifts the mean by at least a factor of 2 for γ>1, and that ReCFG recovers the correct mean while reducing variance (Theorem 4). At inference, γ₀ is looked up per-pixel, per-timestep, and per-condition from the pre-computed table, enabling spatially adaptive guidance that a single global scalar cannot provide. The approach is fully compatible with DDIM and PF-ODE samplers (Theorems 1 and 5) and composes with existing heuristics such as RescaleCFG, as demonstrated on EDM2 (ImageNet 512) and SD3 (CC12M 512).

## Results

On SD3 (CC12M 512×512), ReCFG consistently reduces FD_DINOv2 over CFG across all guidance strengths and NFEs, e.g., at γ₁=7.5, NFE=10, FD_DINOv2 drops from 1105.51 (CFG) to 1010.14 (ReCFG), and further to 979.87 with RescaleCFG+ReCFG. On EDM2-L (ImageNet 512, NFE=63), ReCFG improves FID from 1.96 to 1.89 and FD_DINOv2 from 38.20 to 36.75. On LDM (ImageNet 256, NFE=10, γ₁=2.0), FID improves from 7.83 (CFG) to 5.98 (ReCFG). CLIP-S and MPS also improve, with MPS at γ₁=5.0, NFE=5 rising from 6.484 (CFG) to 7.398 (ReCFG).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReCFG is compared against native CFG (Ho & Salimans, 2021) and RescaleCFG (Lin et al., 2024). Against CFG, ReCFG wins on FD_DINOv2 and MPS across every guidance strength and NFE setting on SD3, with the largest gap at low NFE (e.g., NFE=5, γ₁=7.5: FD_DINOv2 1466.91→1323.49, MPS 3.189→3.979). CLIP-S is roughly tied or marginally improved. Against RescaleCFG alone, ReCFG is competitive on FD_DINOv2 but slightly behind on CLIP-S and MPS; however, the combination RescaleCFG+ReCFG achieves the best scores on all three metrics in every setting, confirming that the two corrections are complementary. On class-conditioned models (EDM2, LDM, DiT), ReCFG consistently outperforms CFG on FID and FD_DINOv2 without any retraining, e.g., LDM at NFE=10, γ₁=2.0: FID 7.83→5.98.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends and corrects Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) [Ho & Salimans, 2021], the standard sampling technique for conditional diffusion models, by relaxing its sum-to-one coefficient constraint. It builds on the DDIM discrete denoising framework [Song et al., 2021] to formalize the rectified guidance within a rigorous theoretical setting, and draws on prior theoretical observations (Bradley & Nakkiran 2024; Karras et al. 2024) that CFG's gamma-powered distribution is not a true reciprocal of the forward diffusion process. The key departure is providing a closed-form, pixel-wise solution for the rectified coefficients (γ₀) that eliminates expectation shift while preserving the sharpening effect of guidance, turning a known theoretical incompatibility into a practical post-hoc correction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since CFG is the default sampling strategy for virtually all modern diffusion models (SDXL, SD3, EDM2, DiT, etc.), fixing its theoretical expectation-shift flaw has broad implications for conditional fidelity, particularly in open-vocabulary text-to-image synthesis where larger guidance strengths are common. The method is attractive in that it requires no retraining and adds negligible inference cost (a pre-computed lookup table), making it immediately deployable. However, practical limitations remain: the lookup table must be pre-computed per condition (impractical for truly open-vocabulary settings without the mean-approximation shortcut), variance control is empirical rather than analytically guaranteed, and the empirical gains, while consistent, are modest (FID improvements of ~0.1–0.5, CLIP-S of ~0.001–0.02). Open questions include whether a learned predictor network ω(c,t) could replace the lookup table for arbitrary conditions, and whether the variance behavior can be analytically characterized beyond the toy setting.
