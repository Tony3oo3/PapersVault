## Problem

Mamba's sequential scanning mechanism, while offering linear computational complexity, fundamentally struggles to capture comprehensive cross-modal relationships—particularly correspondences with unscanned tokens—leading to suboptimal alignment and degraded multimodal fusion quality. Existing Mamba-based multimodal methods either naively concatenate modalities or use multi-stream architectures without explicitly modeling cross-modal correspondences, leaving a gap between Mamba's efficiency and the thorough inter-modal modeling that Transformers achieve through attention.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250393/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three modalities—audio signals, video clips, and transcribed speech text—each encoded into embedding sequences of potentially different lengths (T_a, T_v, T_l) and shared feature dimension d. The OT alignment module maps all modalities to the language modality's sequence length T_l as the anchor, producing aligned sequences of uniform length before temporal interleaving. The output is a binary sentiment label (positive/negative) derived from the fused multimodal representation, and the framework also supports incomplete multimodal inference where one or more modalities are absent at test time.

## Method

AlignMamba introduces a dual cross-modal alignment strategy before Mamba-based fusion. First, an OT-based local alignment module solves a relaxed optimal transport problem (dropping the incoming sum constraint) with cosine-distance cost to learn explicit token-level transport matrices (e.g., video-to-language, audio-to-language), which warp each modality's features into the anchor modality's temporal grid. Second, an MMD-based global alignment loss using a Gaussian kernel in RKHS implicitly enforces distribution-level consistency between the aligned modalities and the language anchor. The aligned features are then interleaved at each timestep into a unified sequence and processed by a Mamba backbone for fusion. Training minimizes a composite objective combining the task-specific loss (cross-entropy) with the MMD alignment loss weighted by a hyperparameter λ, all learned end-to-end.

## Results

On the complete multimodal fusion task, AlignMamba achieves 86.9% binary accuracy and 86.9% F1 on CMU-MOSI, and 86.6% accuracy / 86.5% F1 on CMU-MOSEI, outperforming the previous best (MTMD at 86.0% on MOSI, AOBERT at 86.2% on MOSEI) by 0.9% and 0.4% respectively. On incomplete multimodal fusion, AlignMamba attains an average accuracy of 79.9% on MOSI and 81.6% on MOSEI, surpassing the strongest baseline IMDer by 1.2% and 1.3%. In terms of efficiency, AlignMamba requires only 46.7G FLOPs (vs. 101.6G for single-stream and 203.2G for multi-stream Transformer), uses 20.3% less GPU memory, and reduces inference time by 83.3% compared to single-stream Transformer at 6.4k tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AlignMamba outperforms all 20 baselines on both datasets across both complete and incomplete fusion settings. Among complete-fusion baselines, the closest competitors are MTMD (86.0/86.0 on MOSI) and AOBERT (86.2/85.9 on MOSEI); AlignMamba exceeds them by 0.9% and 0.4% accuracy respectively, with the gap attributed to its dual OT+MMD alignment strategy capturing both token-level and distribution-level correspondences that contrastive-learning methods (HyCon, ConFEDE) and cross-modal Transformers (MulT, DMD) miss. On incomplete fusion, AlignMamba (79.9% avg on MOSI) beats the strongest modality-recovery baseline IMDer (78.7%) by 1.2%, and shows the smallest accuracy drop (11.9%) as missing rate increases from 10% to 70%, compared to 19.0% for MMIN and 13.0% for IMDer. Non-recovery baselines (DCCA, DCCAE) trail by 14.7–16.4% on MOSI, confirming that explicit cross-modal alignment is critical under modality absence. The efficiency comparison against single-stream and multi-stream Transformers (not listed as baselines in Tables 1–2 but analyzed in §4.3) shows AlignMamba uses 20.3% less GPU memory and runs 83.3% faster at 6.4k tokens, validating the linear-complexity advantage of the Mamba backbone over quadratic-attention architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Mamba's selective state-space architecture as its fusion backbone and on the relaxed optimal transport formulation of Kusner et al. (2015) for its local alignment module. It departs from prior Mamba-based multimodal fusion work (Pan-Mamba, Fusion-Mamba, VL-Mamba, Cobra) by identifying a fundamental limitation: Mamba's sequential scanning mechanism cannot effectively model cross-modal correspondences with unscanned tokens, a gap that Transformer-based methods handle through global attention. The paper also extends the contrastive-learning line of multimodal alignment (HyCon, ConFEDE, MTMD) by adding an explicit token-level OT transport plan alongside the implicit distribution-level MMD constraint, creating a dual-granularity alignment strategy that is then consumed by the Mamba backbone rather than a Transformer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the substantial efficiency gains (83.3% inference time reduction, 20.3% memory reduction) that make long-sequence multimodal fusion feasible on resource-constrained hardware, while maintaining competitive accuracy. However, the accuracy improvements over strong baselines are modest (0.9% on CMU-MOSI), and the evaluation is confined to two sentiment-analysis datasets with a fixed trimodal (audio-visual-text) setup, leaving open whether the dual OT+MMD alignment strategy generalizes to other modality combinations, longer sequences, or different downstream tasks. The aggressive OT relaxation (reducing to nearest-neighbor matching) also raises questions about whether the 'optimal transport' framing captures genuinely richer correspondences than simpler alignment heuristics.
