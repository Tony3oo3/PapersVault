## Problem

Prior LLM-based driving systems like DriveGPT4-V1 were designed for open-loop tasks and lack the feedback mechanisms needed for real-time closed-loop vehicle control. Moreover, behavior cloning alone suffers from error accumulation in closed-loop operation, where small prediction errors compound and lead to collisions or the vehicle getting stuck. This paper addresses both gaps by adapting multimodal LLMs for end-to-end closed-loop driving and introducing an online imitation learning strategy to correct accumulated errors.

## Input / Output

The model consumes three 384×384 front-view camera images (front-left, front, front-right) plus the vehicle's current speed and a target point as text. Images are processed through a SigLIP-based multi-view visual tokenizer that maps each view's features into the LLM's text embedding space, while speed and target point are tokenized by the standard LLM tokenizer. The model outputs only four tokens per cycle, each mapped by a dedicated MLP decision head to one of four decision variables (target speed, target angle, 8 future waypoints, 10 route points), which are then converted to throttle/brake/steering via PID controllers—reducing inference to ~4 autoregressive steps versus ~160 tokens for text-based numeric output.

## Method

DriveGPT4-V2 builds on a tiny multimodal LLM (Qwen-0.5B or TinyLLaMA) pretrained via LLaVA-style visual instruction tuning, replacing the vocabulary output head with four MLP decision heads that enable direct regression loss on numerical driving decisions. A multi-view visual tokenizer (MV-VT) extracts features from three front-view images via SigLIP and projects them into the text domain, preserving both wide perception range and fine detail. Training is two-stage: first, behavior cloning on 300K samples from a rule-based CARLA autopilot; second, DAgger-style online imitation learning where a structurally identical expert LLM with access to privileged ground-truth object information (within 30 m) runs concurrently and takes over control whenever its prediction diverges from the agent's beyond a threshold, collecting correction samples for fine-tuning. This expert-supervision mechanism, combined with the compact 4-token output design, yields state-of-the-art performance on CARLA Longest6 (DS=70) while running at 8.1 FPS on a single A800 GPU.

## Results

On the CARLA Longest6 benchmark (36 extended routes), DriveGPT4-V2 achieves a Driving Score (DS) of 70, Route Completion (RC) of 91%, and Infraction Score (IS) of 0.77, using only camera images as visual input. This outperforms the previous SOTA, Transfuser++ (DS=65, RC=90, IS=0.72), which relies on both camera and LiDAR inputs and uses triple training data for augmentation. DriveGPT4-V2 also substantially outperforms the only other LLM-based closed-loop method, LMDrive (DS=36, RC=69, IS=0.52), by a margin of 34 DS points. The model runs at 8.1 FPS on a single A800 GPU with a 0.5B-parameter LLM backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DriveGPT4-V2 (DS=70) outperforms all seven baselines on the CARLA Longest6 benchmark across all three primary metrics (DS, RC, IS). Against the strongest baseline, Transfuser++ (DS=65, using camera+LiDAR and triple training data), DriveGPT4-V2 gains +5 DS, +1 RC, and +0.05 IS while using only camera input and a single round of training data. When Transfuser++ is matched to the same data budget (no augmentation), the gap widens to +12 DS (70 vs. 58). Among LLM-based methods, DriveGPT4-V2 surpasses LMDrive (DS=36) by 34 DS points, attributed to LMDrive's reliance on BEV representations that lack strong pretrained multimodal priors. DriveGPT4-V2 also achieves the lowest rates of pedestrian collisions (0.00), static object collisions (0.01), and red-light violations (0.04) among all methods, indicating superior safety. Notably, DriveGPT4-V2 is the only method using camera-only input to exceed DS=65, demonstrating that pretrained LLM knowledge can compensate for the absence of LiDAR.

## Relation to Prior Work

DriveGPT4-V2 extends DriveGPT4-V1 from open-loop to closed-loop end-to-end driving, and adapts the LLaVA visual tokenizer architecture for multi-view camera input. Conceptually, it departs from prior LLM-based driving methods (GPT-Driver, LMDrive) that relied on BEV-to-text representations or large 7B+ models, instead using a tiny 0.5B LLM with dedicated numerical decision heads. The online imitation learning component draws directly from the LBC framework's DAgger formulation with a privileged-information expert, repurposing it so that the expert is itself a multimodal LLM rather than a separate network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 0.5B-parameter LLM with camera-only input can outperform LiDAR-fusing baselines (Transfuser++) on a challenging 36-route closed-loop benchmark, suggesting that pretrained multimodal knowledge can substitute for additional sensor modalities. The finding that scaling to 8B parameters yields only marginal gains while degrading inference speed by 20× is practically important for real-time deployment. Key limitations include evaluation solely in the CARLA simulator, reliance on a rule-based autopilot for initial data collection, and the expert model's privileged information (ground-truth object states) being unavailable in real-world settings, leaving open whether the DAgger loop translates to real deployment.
