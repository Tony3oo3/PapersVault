## Problem

Current audio-driven facial animation methods suffer from identity drift and quality degradation beyond a few seconds, and existing workarounds (external spatial control, autoregressive motion frames) either constrain naturalness or accumulate errors over time. Additionally, most methods assume a fixed emotional state and neglect non-speech vocalizations (NSVs) such as laughter and sighs, limiting their ability to produce natural, long-form animations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252118/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB identity frame, an audio clip (speech and/or NSVs), and per-frame continuous valence/arousal values as input, and produces a long-duration facial animation video. The identity frame is repeated to match the temporal dimension of the noised latent sequence and concatenated channel-wise, while audio is encoded by WavLM and BEATs into frame-level embeddings. The keyframe stage outputs T keyframes spaced S frames apart, and the interpolation stage fills the S−2 intermediate frames between each consecutive pair, enabling seamless chaining for arbitrarily long sequences.

## Method

KeyFace is a two-stage latent diffusion framework built on Stable Video Diffusion with the EDM parametrization. In the keyframe stage, a U-Net generates a low-frame-rate sequence conditioned on the repeated identity frame (via channel concatenation and skip connections), audio embeddings injected through cross-attention and timestep embeddings, and sinusoidal valence/arousal embeddings added to the timestep vector; a modified classifier-free guidance with separate audio and identity scales controls generation. In the interpolation stage, the same architecture is adapted to fill gaps between two consecutive keyframes using a learned mask embedding for missing frames and a binary mask distinguishing conditioned from unconditioned positions, guided by autoguidance to preserve motion diversity. Training combines latent-space L2 loss with pixel-space L2 and VGG perceptual losses (applied to a single random frame to save memory), plus a spatially weighted lower-face term to emphasize lip synchronization.

## Results

On HDTF, KeyFace achieves the best FID (16.76), FVD (137.25), LPIPS (0.16), aesthetic quality (0.59), and Elo score (1091.52) among all compared methods, with a LipScore of 0.36 (close second to V-Express at 0.37). On CelebV-Text, KeyFace again leads in FID (17.06), FVD (180.26), LPIPS (0.21), AQ (0.55), and Elo (1100.90), while V-Express edges ahead on LipScore (0.32 vs. 0.30). For emotion generation on MEAD, KeyFace with continuous valence-arousal conditioning achieves an emotion accuracy of 0.67, outperforming EAT (0.54) and EAMM (0.17) while remaining competitive with EDTalk (0.72), all while delivering substantially better FID (44.43 vs. 75.69–107.16) and FVD (447.74 vs. 560.61–855.20).

## Baselines & Comparisons

KeyFace outperforms all five main baselines (SadTalker, Hallo, V-Express, AniPortrait, EchoMimic) on FID, FVD, LPIPS, aesthetic quality, and Elo preference on both HDTF and CelebV-Text, with the largest margins over SadTalker (FID 16.76 vs. 60.55 on HDTF) and AniPortrait (FVD 137.25 vs. 299.09). The closest competitor is V-Express, which narrowly leads on LipScore (0.37 vs. 0.36 on HDTF; 0.32 vs. 0.30 on CelebV-Text) but trails KeyFace by a wide margin on FVD (200.67 vs. 137.25) and FID (34.68 vs. 16.76). Hallo and EchoMimic, which rely on ReferenceNet for identity preservation, show background inconsistencies and error accumulation over time, while SadTalker produces relatively static, lower-quality animations. On the emotion evaluation (MEAD), KeyFace with continuous valence-arousal conditioning achieves 0.67 emotion accuracy, beating EAT (0.54) and EAMM (0.17) while remaining competitive with EDTalk (0.72), all while delivering substantially better image and video quality (FID 44.43 vs. 75.69–107.16; FVD 447.74 vs. 560.61–855.20). Notably, KeyFace is trained only on pseudo-labels for emotion, whereas the competing emotion methods use ground-truth MEAD labels, making the competitive accuracy particularly striking.

## Relation to Prior Work

KeyFace builds directly on Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) as its architectural backbone and the EDM framework for the diffusion process, but departs from prior audio-driven animation methods (Hallo, AniPortrait, EchoMimic) that rely on autoregressive generation or ReferenceNet for identity preservation, both of which suffer from error accumulation or identity drift over long sequences. The two-stage keyframe-then-interpolation pipeline is inspired by keyframe-based video generation [67, 69] and is, to the authors' knowledge, the first application of this decomposition to audio-driven facial animation. The method also extends the conditioning space beyond prior work by incorporating continuous valence-arousal emotion representations and non-speech vocalizations (NSVs), dimensions that existing methods either ignore or handle only with discrete labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in audio-driven facial animation: quality degradation beyond a few seconds, which limits real-world deployment in virtual assistants, education, and VR. The two-stage design offers a clean architectural separation of motion and identity control that avoids the need for external spatial conditioning (landmarks, 3D meshes) while maintaining temporal coherence. Open questions include the computational overhead of running two separate diffusion models, whether the approach scales to even longer sequences (minutes), and whether the NSV and emotion capabilities generalize beyond the 8 NSV types and the specific valence-arousal space used here. The reliance on pseudo-labels for emotion training also leaves room for improvement with better-annotated data.
