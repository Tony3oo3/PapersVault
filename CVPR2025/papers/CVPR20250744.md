## Problem

Existing audio-visual segmentation methods (AVOS, AVSS) treat all objects of the same category as a single region and operate on short 5–10 second clips, failing to distinguish individual sounding instances (e.g., two ukuleles) or model long-range temporal dependencies in realistic long videos. No dataset or method existed for instance-level sound source localization, segmentation, and tracking in long-form audible videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250744/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a video sequence split into 1-second visual snippets (processed by ResNet-50 or Swin Transformer) and corresponding 1-second audio snippets (converted to mel spectrograms and encoded by a frozen VGGish model). It outputs, for each sounding object instance, a category label from 26 predefined classes, a confidence score, and a temporally consistent sequence of binary segmentation masks with a unique tracking identity. The AVISeg benchmark provides 926 videos averaging 61.4 seconds with 94,074 instance masks, and evaluation uses a novel frame-level metric (FSLA) alongside HOTA and mAP.

## Method

AVISM is a two-stage query-based Transformer built on Mask2Former and VITA. The frame-level sound source localizer uses a multi-scale deformable attention pixel decoder and a frame-level audio-visual fusion module (cross-attention between visual features and per-frame audio features) to produce a small set of audio-conditioned frame queries that classify and segment sounding objects per frame. The video-level sounding object tracker then converts all frame queries into object tokens and applies window-based self-attention (with shifted windows, as in Swin) along the temporal axis, augmented by a video-level audio-visual fusion module that performs cross-attention between object tokens and audio features within each local window. This token-based temporal communication avoids processing dense spatio-temporal features, enabling efficient handling of long, high-resolution videos on a single GPU. Training uses Hungarian matching at both frame and video levels with a similarity loss aligning frame queries to video queries for identity consistency.

## Results

On the AVISeg test set, AVISM achieves FSLA 42.78, HOTA 61.73, and mAP 40.57, setting new state-of-the-art results across all three primary metrics. With a Swin-L backbone and IN+COCO pre-training, performance further improves to FSLA 52.49, HOTA 71.13, and mAP 53.46. The proposed FSLA metric decomposes into sub-metrics for no-sound (FSLAn=32.22), single-source (FSLAs=29.83), and multi-source (FSLAm=52.40) scenarios, revealing that multi-source cases remain the most challenging.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AVISM outperforms all 12 baselines on every metric. Against the strongest VIS method (VITA, FSLA 38.04), AVISM gains +4.74 FSLA, +4.25 HOTA, and +4.32 mAP, demonstrating the value of incorporating audio cues for sound-source-aware segmentation. Against the strongest AVSS method (COMBO, FSLA 39.49), AVISM improves by +3.29 FSLA, +4.34 HOTA, and +2.73 mAP, showing that the token-based temporal tracking architecture is more effective than dense spatiotemporal feature fusion for long videos. Among VIS baselines, VITA and LBVQ are the closest competitors, while DAVIS (FSLA 23.99) and Mask2Former-VIS (FSLA 29.75) lag significantly. In zero-shot evaluation of multi-modal large models (Table 3), even the best MMLM (AL-Ref-SAM2, FSLA 18.55) trails AVISM by over 24 FSLA points, highlighting a substantial gap in instance-level sound-source localization and temporal perception for current large models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research lines: video instance segmentation (VIS) and audio-visual segmentation (AVS). On the VIS side, it inherits the query-based, token-distillation paradigm from Mask2Former and VITA, which compress dense spatio-temporal features into a small set of object tokens for efficient long-video processing. On the AVS side, it extends prior work that handled only binary (AVOS) or category-level (AVSS) segmentation to the instance level with persistent tracking identities. The key conceptual departure is that prior AVS methods operate on 5–10 second clips and cannot distinguish two objects of the same category, whereas AVIS requires per-instance identity over ~60-second videos, necessitating the video-level tracker with window-based audio-visual cross-attention that has no direct precedent in either lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of AVIS as a task and AVISeg as a benchmark fills a clear gap: no prior dataset supported instance-level, long-form audio-visual segmentation, and the new FSLA metric offers a more interpretable, frame-level diagnostic than mAP or HOTA alone. The zero-shot evaluation of multi-modal large models (BuboGPT, PG-Video-LLaVA, AL-Ref-SAM2) reveals a striking gap between these models' claimed audio-visual understanding and their actual instance-level, temporally-grounded performance, highlighting a concrete failure mode for the MMLM community. Open questions include whether the token-based tracker scales to even longer videos, whether the 26-category, 4-scenario coverage generalizes to open-vocabulary settings, and how MMLMs can be instruction-tuned to close the instance-segmentation gap.
