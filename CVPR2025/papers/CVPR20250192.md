## Problem

Implicit neural surface reconstruction methods dominate the field but require high-quality oriented normals and rely on Marching Cubes, which is incompatible with unsigned distance fields for open surfaces. Neural computational methods that directly predict explicit triangles have been largely overlooked due to their strong bias toward Poisson-disk sampled inputs and inadequate handling of edge-manifoldness in reconstructed meshes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250192/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a general 3D point cloud (no normals required) and produces a triangle mesh where the input points serve directly as mesh vertices, avoiding the interpolation of new points typical of implicit methods. For dense inputs, the point cloud is first voxelized to a regular grid (e.g., 2 cm for ScanNet, 10 cm for Matterport3D/CARLA) to approximate the network's preferred uniform distribution. The output is an edge-manifold triangle mesh suitable for both watertight shapes and open surfaces.

## Method

OffsetOPT operates in two stages. First, a 5-layer transformer (64 channels, 4 heads) is trained with BCE loss to predict a K×K symmetric probability matrix of candidate triangles from each point's normalized KNN neighborhood (K=50) with positional encoding, using ground-truth adjacent triangles from ABC dataset meshes. Second, the frozen network is applied to unseen point clouds by optimizing per-point 3D offsets via backpropagation of an unsupervised BCE loss with pseudo-labels (top-2 triangles per row with confidence > 0.5). Offset updates are controlled by a nearest-neighbor distance constraint (d_{t+1} > 0.5·d_0) that prevents point collisions and promotes repulsion, which simultaneously drives the reconstructed surface toward edge-manifoldness without any explicit manifoldness enforcement. This offset optimization is the key mechanism that extends the network's applicability from ideally distributed training points to general point clouds while improving triangle prediction accuracy.

## Results

On the ABC test set, OffsetOPT achieves the best overall surface quality (CD1=0.283×10⁻², F1=0.951, NC=0.988) and the best sharp-detail preservation (ECD1=0.402×10⁻², EF1=0.941) among all compared methods. On large-scale scene reconstruction, OffsetOPT outperforms NKSR with ground-truth normals on all three datasets: ScanNet (CD1=0.147 vs 0.157), Matterport3D (CD1=0.148 vs 0.183), and CARLA (CD1=0.124 vs 0.175), while also achieving the highest EF1 scores (0.931, 0.973, 0.946 respectively). Offset optimization increases the percentage of manifold edges from 74–82% to 98–99% across datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OffsetOPT outperforms all six baselines on the ABC test set across every metric. Against the closest computational competitor CircNet, OffsetOPT improves ECD1 from 0.708 to 0.402 (a 43% reduction) and EF1 from 0.924 to 0.941, while also achieving the best NR (1.318° vs 1.758°). Against DSE, the second-best computational method, OffsetOPT wins on CD1 (0.283 vs 0.285), F1 (0.951 vs 0.949), and ECD1 (0.402 vs 0.538). The implicit methods SPSR and NKSR are dramatically outperformed on sharp-detail metrics: OffsetOPT's ECD1 of 0.402 is roughly 65× lower than SPSR's 26.160 and 68× lower than NKSR's 27.499, confirming the known over-smoothing limitation of implicit representations. On large-scale scenes (Table 4), OffsetOPT beats NKSR even when NKSR is given ground-truth normals, achieving lower CD1 on all three datasets (ScanNet, Matterport3D, CARLA) and substantially higher EF1 (e.g., 0.973 vs 0.842 on Matterport3D). On the MGN open-surface dataset, OffsetOPT achieves the best NR (2.967°) and EF1 (0.538), while NKSR degrades severely (F1=0.611, CD2=23.263) due to its tendency to close small holes.

## Relation to Prior Work

OffsetOPT extends the neural computational reconstruction lineage (CircNet, PointTriNet, DSE, IER) that directly predicts triangle faces from KNN neighborhoods, as opposed to the dominant implicit-representation + Marching Cubes paradigm (SPSR, NKSR). Its key departure is the addition of an unsupervised per-point offset optimization stage that adapts the frozen prediction network to general point clouds, removing the prior methods' reliance on Poisson-disk-like distributions. Unlike earlier computational methods that require explicit voting or post-processing for edge-manifoldness, OffsetOPT achieves near-manifold output through the offset optimization itself, and unlike implicit methods it needs no point normals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical elimination of normals is significant for real-world pipelines where normal estimation is error-prone or unavailable, and the method's ability to handle general (non-uniform) point clouds broadens applicability beyond the idealized distributions assumed by prior computational methods. However, a small residual percentage of non-manifold edges still requires post-processing, the transformer architecture is less efficient than graph-convolutional alternatives, and reconstruction of large scenes takes 6–12 minutes per scene. Open questions include whether combining GNNs with transformers can improve efficiency, and whether the approach extends to noisy or sparse point clouds, which the paper explicitly excludes.
