## Problem

Existing underwater image restoration and depth estimation methods are designed for normally-lit scenes and degrade on dimly-lit images, while terrestrial low-light enhancement methods cannot handle UW haze and color-cast. No prior work jointly recovers both a clean image and a depth map from a single low-light underwater (LLUW) image, and no real paired dataset of low-light and normally-lit UW stereo images existed to enable self-supervised training for this dual task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252212/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the network takes a single low-light UW RGB image and simultaneously outputs a restored reflectance image (treated as the clean image) and a per-pixel depth map, operating at 62 fps on a 512×512 input. Training consumes time-synchronized stereo video pairs from the ULVStereo dataset (low-light from Camera 1, normally-lit from Camera 2 with known baseline), plus adjacent monocular frames from the low-light video to handle occlusions. The depth is estimated up to a scale, so evaluation uses scale-invariant metrics (SI-MSE, Pearson correlation).

## Method

SelfLUID-Net disentangles the input LLUW image into reflectance, illumination, direct/backscatter transmission maps, and global background light via separate sub-networks, guided by the UW image formation model combined with Retinex theory (reflectance = clean image, illumination-invariant). The key self-supervision signal is reflectance consistency: the reflectance from the low-light image must match the reflectance from the normally-lit stereo counterpart after geometric warping using the estimated depth and known camera pose, which simultaneously refines both the reflectance and the depth. Depth is initially derived from transmission maps via D = −log(T)/β, then refined by the reprojection constraint. To handle occluded pixels in stereo, adjacent monocular frames from the low-light video provide an additional source reflectance, and a moving-pixel masking scheme (threshold τ = μ + kσ on per-pixel reprojection error) prevents dynamic transients like fish and swaying plants from corrupting the loss. The total loss combines reconstruction, photometric reprojection (minimum of stereo and masked monocular), illumination smoothness, edge-aware depth smoothness, channel-wise depth consistency, color, and spatial consistency terms.

## Results

On image restoration, SelfLUID-Net achieves the best UCIQE scores across all four evaluation datasets (0.66 on ULVStereo, 0.74 on Sea-thru, 0.63 on NUID) and the best PSNR (17.21 dB) and SSIM (0.58) on UIEB_dark. On depth estimation, it attains the highest Pearson correlation (ρ=0.52 on Sea-thru, ρ=0.71 on FLSea) and lowest SI-MSE (0.40 on Sea-thru, 0.18 on FLSea) among all compared methods. Inference runs at 16 ms per 512×512 image (~62 fps), producing both the restored image and depth map in a single forward pass.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SelfLUID-Net outperforms the closest self-supervised UW baseline URN [72] on every reported metric: UCIQE improves from 0.52 to 0.66 (ULVStereo) and 0.52 to 0.74 (Sea-thru), PSNR rises from 16.13 to 17.21 dB, and depth ρ increases from 0.43 to 0.52 (Sea-thru) and 0.59 to 0.71 (FLSea), while also being 2 ms faster (16 ms vs 18 ms). Against the best traditional UW method MMLE [92], SelfLUID-Net gains +0.05 UCIQE on ULVStereo and +0.21 dB PSNR on UIEB_dark. Among low-light enhancement baselines, SCI [55] is the strongest competitor (PSNR 13.96, SSIM 0.52 on UIEB_dark), but SelfLUID-Net exceeds it by +3.25 dB PSNR and +0.06 SSIM. For depth estimation, SelfLUID-Net surpasses the best traditional UW method HL [5] (ρ=0.44, SI-MSE=0.58 on Sea-thru) by a wide margin (ρ=0.52, SI-MSE=0.40) and outperforms the best terrestrial self-supervised method HR-Depth [54] (ρ=0.04, SI-MSE=0.74) by a factor of over 10× in ρ. The only metric where a baseline edges ahead is UIQM on ULVStereo, where L2UWE [56] scores 4.84 vs SelfLUID-Net's 2.80, though the authors note L2UWE's visual output is inferior and UIQM should be interpreted alongside other metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own prior self-supervised UW depth-and-restoration framework (USe-ReDI-Net, ICCV 2023) by incorporating Retinex theory to handle low-light conditions and by replacing scene-radiance-based geometry constraints with illumination-independent reflectance-based ones. It also draws on the self-supervised monocular depth estimation paradigm of Godard et al. (ICCV 2019) for reprojection losses, spatial transformer warping, and edge-aware smoothness, and on the revised UW image formation model of Akkaynak & Treibitz (CVPR 2018) for the disentanglement into transmission maps and background light. The key departure from prior LLUW enhancement work is the introduction of stereo geometry and a moving-pixel masking scheme, neither of which appears in existing low-light underwater or terrestrial self-supervised depth methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to jointly recover depth and a clean image from a single low-light underwater image in real time, and the first to introduce a real low-light UW stereo dataset (ULVStereo), filling a gap that limits the field's ability to train and evaluate LLUW methods. The practical impact is significant for underwater robotics operating at depth or in dim conditions where artificial lighting introduces artifacts. Limitations include the training data being exposure-adjusted rather than truly low-light, the near-shore focus, and the reliance on a stereo rig for data collection; open questions include whether the approach generalizes to deep-sea or nighttime scenarios and whether the reflectance-consistency constraint can be relaxed when stereo pairs are unavailable.
