## Problem

Achieving 4-bit quantization for diffusion models remains unsolved: existing INT-based PTQ methods degrade severely below 8-bit activations, and QAT is prohibitively expensive. While floating-point quantization has shown strong results in LLMs at 4-bit, its application to diffusion models is unexplored, and the authors identify three specific barriers—signed FP quantization failing on asymmetric SiLU-induced activations, single-LoRA fine-tuning being suboptimal across the multi-stage denoising process, and a mismatch between the fine-tuning loss and the actual quantization impact at each timestep.

## Input / Output

The quantized diffusion model consumes the same inputs as the full-precision model: pure Gaussian noise for unconditional generation (DDIM at 32×32 or 64×64, LDM at 256×256) or class-conditioned noise for conditional generation (ImageNet 256×256), and outputs a synthetic RGB image after iterative denoising (100 steps for DDIM/LSUN, 20 steps for ImageNet). All convolutional and linear layers except input/output are quantized to 4-bit FP for both weights and activations, with a 256-sample calibration set used for parameter initialization.

## Method

The MSFP framework applies a per-layer quantization scheme: unsigned FP quantization with an added zero point for Anomalous-Activation-Distribution Layers (AALs, where SiLU compresses sub-zero values into a narrow range), and standard signed FP quantization for Normal-Activation-Distribution Layers (NALs), freeing the sign bit as extra exponent/mantissa width to improve representation in high-density regions. For fine-tuning, TALoRA introduces a hub of multiple LoRAs and a learnable timestep-aware router (time embedding + MLP with STE) that dynamically selects the appropriate LoRA per timestep, treating denoising as a multi-task process. A denoising-factor aligned loss (DFA) multiplies the noise-prediction MSE by γ_t, the timestep-dependent coefficient governing predicted-noise impact in the denoising update, so that the fine-tuning gradient is proportional to the actual quantization-induced performance gap at each step rather than uniformly weighted.

## Results

At 6-bit quantization, the proposed MSFP+TALoRA+DFA method achieves near-lossless performance (e.g., CIFAR-10 FID 4.23 vs. FP 4.26; LSUN-Church FID 6.24 vs. FP 4.06). At 4-bit, the method achieves SOTA results across all unconditional generation tasks: FID 8.77 on LSUN-Church (vs. 18.40 for EfficientDM), and on CIFAR-10 the 4-bit FID is only 1.84 worse than full precision. On ImageNet conditional generation (4-bit), the method achieves sFID 7.42, outperforming EfficientDM (14.42) by 7.00 and QuEST (29.27) by 21.85, while maintaining IS of 190.74 vs. 139.45 for EfficientDM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines: Q-Diffusion (PTQ), EDA-DM (data-free PTQ), EfficientDM (LoRA fine-tuning), and QuEST (selective fine-tuning). Q-Diffusion and EDA-DM completely fail at 4-bit on most datasets (N/A or FID >100), confirming that pure PTQ cannot handle 4-bit diffusion quantization. Among fine-tuning methods, EfficientDM is the strongest baseline but still degrades severely at 4-bit (FID 38.40 on CIFAR-10, 36.36 on LSUN-Bedroom, 18.40 on LSUN-Church). The proposed method outperforms EfficientDM by 32.38, 24.15, and 9.63 FID points on the three unconditional datasets, respectively. On ImageNet conditional generation, the method improves sFID by 7.00 over EfficientDM (7.42 vs. 14.42) and by 21.85 over QuEST (7.42 vs. 29.27), while also achieving higher IS (190.74 vs. 139.45 and 69.58). Notably, at 6-bit the method matches or exceeds full-precision performance on CIFAR-10 (FID 4.23 vs. 4.26), demonstrating that the FP quantization framework introduces negligible overhead at higher bit-widths.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the search-based FP quantization framework established for LLMs (LLM-FP4 [23]) and the 8-bit FP quantization approach for diffusion models [2], pushing both to 4-bit precision. It departs from prior PTQ fine-tuning methods (EfficientDM, QuEST) that apply a single LoRA uniformly across all denoising timesteps, instead formulating fine-tuning as a multi-task problem with timestep-aware LoRA allocation. The introduction of unsigned FP quantization with a zero point is a novel departure from the signed FP paradigm that has dominated low-bit quantization, motivated by the asymmetric activation distributions introduced by SiLU in diffusion architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration of competitive 4-bit FP quantization for diffusion models, with the 4-bit CelebA FID (7.69) approaching full-precision (6.49), suggesting FP quantization may be a more natural fit for diffusion models than INT at very low bit-widths. However, the evaluation is limited to small-scale models (DDIM on 32×32 images, LDM-4/8 on 256×256), and the anomalous FID behavior on ImageNet (FID improving as bit-width decreases) raises questions about metric reliability and generalizability to large-scale text-to-image models. Open questions include whether the MSFP framework and TALoRA routing scale to billion-parameter diffusion transformers, and whether the unsigned FP advantage persists under different activation functions or architectural choices.
