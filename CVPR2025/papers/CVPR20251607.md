## Problem

Existing GAN-based parameterization methods for dataset distillation (e.g., GLaD) restrict optimization to a single fixed feature domain within the GAN, requiring manual layer selection and neglecting the diverse informative guidance available across other hierarchical layers. This fixed-space approach limits performance, especially when the optimal feature domain shifts across different datasets, distillation algorithms, or model architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251607/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a real training dataset (ImageNet-1K subsets at 128×128, CIFAR-10 at 32×32, Tiny-ImageNet at 64×64), a pre-trained StyleGAN-XL generator decomposed into K hierarchical sub-networks, and a distillation loss (TESLA/MTT, DSA, or DM). It produces a synthetic condensed dataset at extreme compression ratios (IPC=1 or IPC=10), where each synthetic image is generated by passing the optimized latent through the remaining GAN sub-network from the selected layer to the pixel domain. A class-relevant feature distance metric computed via CAM-weighted feature maps serves as the implicit evaluation signal during the progressive search, avoiding costly full-model retraining at each step.

## Method

H-PD decomposes a pre-trained GAN into K sequential sub-networks (G_{K-1} ∘ … ∘ G_0) and performs progressive optimization across all hierarchical feature domains. Starting from an ensemble-averaged latent (mean of multiple noise samples passed through the mapping network to reduce bias), the method optimizes N steps at each layer using the distillation loss, tracks the best latent via a novel class-relevant feature distance (CAM-weighted L2 distance between synthetic and real feature maps), and feeds the best latent into the next layer as its initialization. This greedy layer-by-layer traversal continues until the pixel domain is reached, at which point the globally best latent is selected by real performance. A theoretical analysis grounded in optimal transport shows that initializing from partially optimized latents reduces the expected optimization cost compared to random initialization, providing a formal justification for the progressive strategy.

## Results

On ImageNet-Subset (128×128, IPC=1, same-architecture), H-PD achieves consistent improvements over GLaD across all three distillation methods: e.g., TESLA on ImNet-A reaches 55.1% vs. 50.7% (GLaD) and 51.7% (Pixel); DSA on ImNet-B reaches 50.7% vs. 49.2% (GLaD); DM on ImNet-A reaches 42.8% vs. 41.0% (GLaD). On Tiny-ImageNet with SRe2L (IPC=1), H-PD achieves 4.5% top-1 accuracy vs. 3.1% (GLaD) and 2.6% (Pixel); on ImageNet-1K (IPC=1), H-PD reaches 2.6% vs. 1.2% (GLaD). Against diffusion-based methods at IPC=1, H-PD substantially outperforms Minimax (e.g., 45.4% vs. 22.8% on ImNette) and D4M (45.4% vs. 15.2% on ImNette).

## Baselines & Comparisons

H-PD consistently outperforms GLaD [5] (the primary GAN-based parameterization baseline) across all distillation methods (TESLA, DSA, DM), all ImageNet subsets, and both same-architecture and cross-architecture settings, with gains ranging from ~1% to ~5% (e.g., TESLA on ImNet-A: 55.1% vs. 50.7%). Against the pixel-space baselines (TESLA-Pixel [9], DSA-Pixel [45], DM-Pixel [47]), H-PD also achieves clear improvements, confirming that the hierarchical GAN parameterization adds value beyond the distillation algorithm itself. At extreme compression (IPC=1), H-PD dramatically surpasses diffusion-based generative distillation methods: on ImNette it reaches 45.4% vs. Minimax [17] at 22.8% and D4M [38] at 15.2%, and on ImNet-Birds it achieves 39.7% vs. 23.2% (Minimax) and 18.2% (D4M). The time cost is comparable to GLaD (e.g., 70 min vs. 75 min for TESLA on ImageNet subsets), so the gains come without additional computational overhead. On CIFAR-10 cross-architecture evaluation, H-PD improves over GLaD for all three distillation methods across ConvNet, AlexNet, ResNet-18, VGG-11, and ViT, though the margin is smaller (~1–2%) than on ImageNet subsets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends GLaD (Cazenavette et al., CVPR 2023), which parameterizes synthetic datasets in a single manually-selected intermediate layer of a pre-trained StyleGAN-XL. The key conceptual departure is replacing this fixed optimization space with a progressive, layer-by-layer optimization that traverses all hierarchical feature domains of the GAN, passing the best latent from each layer as the initialization for the next. The theoretical framing draws on optimal transport to argue that partially-optimized latents reduce the cost of subsequent optimization compared to random initialization. The paper also positions itself against diffusion-based generative distillation (Minimax, D4M) and other parameterization approaches (FreD, IDC, ITGAN) that similarly restrict optimization to a single feature domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that GAN-based parameterization distillation can be made more robust by exploiting the full hierarchical structure of the generator rather than relying on a single hand-picked layer, yielding consistent 1–5% gains across distillation methods and datasets at equivalent compute budgets. The class-relevant feature distance metric offers a lightweight proxy for dataset quality that avoids expensive re-training during the search. However, the approach is validated only on GAN-based parameterization (specifically GLaD with StyleGAN-XL), and whether the hierarchical progressive strategy transfers to other generative priors (diffusion models, VAEs) or to non-GAN parameterization schemes remains open. The theoretical analysis, while suggestive, relies on a proportionality assumption (E[c] ∝ 1/I) that is not rigorously established for the specific cost functions used in practice.
