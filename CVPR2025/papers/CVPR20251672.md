## Problem

Existing multi-image VQA benchmarks (e.g., RetVQA, WebVQA) pair each question with at most 30 images, far below the hundreds-to-thousands of documents encountered in real-world retrieval scenarios. Large multimodal models also lack proper evaluation on large-scale document retrieval and reasoning, and their context-length limitations make direct processing of 1,000+ high-resolution document images infeasible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251672/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural-language question and a haystack of up to 1,000 document images (rendered at high resolution to preserve small-font text) as input, and produces both a ranked list of retrieved relevant documents and a free-form text answer. The benchmarks are offered at three haystack sizes (100, 200, 1000) so that the smaller variants allow all images to be fed directly into an LMM's context for long-context evaluation, while the 1000-document variant necessitates a retrieval pipeline. Questions are curated from DocVQA and InfographicVQA through a three-stage filtering pipeline (LLM-based general-question removal, manual uniqueness verification via OCR cross-document search, and generic-knowledge elimination) to guarantee each question has a single document-specific answer.

## Method

V-RAG is a two-stage, vision-centric retrieval-augmented generation framework. In the first stage, an ensemble of three vision encoders—CLIP (ViT-L/14@336), SigLIP (ViT-SO400M/14@384), and OpenCLIP (ConvNeXt-XXL@1024)—each computes cosine similarity between the question embedding and every document image; the three per-encoder scores are averaged into a single relevance score, and the top-m (m=60) documents are selected. In the second stage, an LMM-filter module (LLaVA-OneVision-Qwen2-7B) is prompted with a binary yes/no relevance question for each of the top-m documents, discarding those that cannot answer the query. The surviving top-k (k=5) documents are then passed to an LMM-VQA module (Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct) to generate the final answer. The LMM-VQA module can be further fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 8, lr 1e-4, 1 epoch) on a 2,835-question training set augmented with 1–10 random distractor images per question to improve robustness against visual noise.

## Results

V-RAG achieves Recall@1 of 66.1% on DocHaystack-1000 and 64.5% on InfoHaystack-1000, outperforming the best single-encoder baseline (OpenCLIP) by 31.2 and 10.9 percentage points respectively. On the smaller DocHaystack-100 subset, V-RAG reaches 81.7% Recall@1 and 89.0% Recall@5. In end-to-end VQA, integrating V-RAG with GPT-4o yields over 55% accuracy improvement on DocHaystack-200 (72.5% vs. 23.9% standalone), and the fine-tuned Qwen2-VL+V-RAG achieves the best overall score of 86.2% on DocHaystack-100 and 73.4% on DocHaystack-1000.

## Baselines & Comparisons

V-RAG decisively outperforms all single-encoder retrieval baselines across every benchmark size. On DocHaystack-1000, V-RAG (66.1% R@1) beats OpenCLIP (34.9%), SigLIP (33.0%), CLIP (23.9%), Jina-CLIP (3.7%), and Nomic-Embed-Vision (1.8%) by 31.2, 33.1, 42.2, 62.4, and 64.3 points respectively. On InfoHaystack-1000, V-RAG (64.5% R@1) leads OpenCLIP (53.6%) by 10.9 points and CLIP (45.8%) by 18.7 points. BM25 (OCR), a text-only method, is competitive on DocHaystack-1000 (56.9% R@1) but still trails V-RAG by 9.2 points, and falls further behind on InfoHaystack-1000 (38.7% vs. 64.5%). In the end-to-end VQA setting (Table 3), V-RAG integration boosts GPT-4o from 23.9% to 72.5% on DocHaystack-200 and from 20.0% to 63.2% on InfoHaystack-200, while the fine-tuned Qwen2-VL+V-RAG achieves the best overall accuracy (86.2% DocHaystack-100, 73.4% DocHaystack-1000), surpassing the MIRAGE retrieval-augmented baseline (2.8–7.7%) by a wide margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-image VQA line of work (RetVQA, WebVQA, MultimodalQA) from small image pools (≤30 images per question) to large-scale document collections of up to 1,000 documents, drawing its question-answer pairs from DocVQA and InfographicVQA. For the retrieval component, it builds on vision-language RAG approaches like MuRAG, RetVQA, and MIRAGE, but departs from them by ensembling multiple vision encoders (CLIP, SigLIP, OpenCLIP) and adding an LMM-based relevance filter rather than relying on a single encoder. The V-RAG framework is positioned as a vision-centric alternative to text-based retrieval (BM25) and single-encoder retrieval, explicitly targeting the high-resolution, mixed-content nature of document images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmarks fill a clear practical gap: no prior evaluation tested LMMs on retrieval and reasoning over 1,000+ documents, a scale that mirrors real-world photo albums, personal document stores, and enterprise archives. The V-RAG technique is a practical, plug-and-play method that substantially boosts any LMM's ability to handle large document sets (e.g., +55% accuracy for GPT-4o on DocHaystack-200). However, the test sets are small (109 and 155 questions), limiting statistical confidence, and the best accuracy on the 1000-document setting remains modest (~73% for DocHaystack, ~60% for InfoHaystack), indicating substantial room for improvement. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-document visual collections, how it scales beyond 1,000 documents, and whether the LMM-filter step can be made more efficient for real-time applications.
