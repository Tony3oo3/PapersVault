limitations: The paper does not include a dedicated limitations section, but acknowledges that fair cross-model comparison is challenging due to variations in data scale and architecture, and notes that the re-sampling strategy cannot recover visual detail lost during initial feature extraction. The grounding step is limited to single-region predictions per reasoning step, and performance still trails much larger proprietary models (e.g., GPT-4o) by a significant margin on vision-centric tasks.

surprise_factor: The most notable finding is that the cheaper re-sampling strategy (retrieving cached patch tokens) generally outperforms the more expensive re-encoding strategy (re-running the vision encoder on cropped regions), which is somewhat counterintuitive given that re-encoding operates at higher effective resolution. ## Problem

Existing MLLMs underperform in vision-centric scenarios requiring precise visual focus (e.g., spatial relationships, region-specific properties) because they rely on implicit self-attention for language-directed visual engagement. While unconditioned image tokenization (stimulus-driven attention) has been studied, the effect of explicit goal-directed visual attention—where the model actively searches for and re-engages with task-relevant regions—remains underexplored in the MLLM design space.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (processed at 1024×1024 for ConvNeXt/EVA-02 and 448×448 for CLIP, yielding 1024 visual tokens) concatenated with a text question. During generation, the model first outputs normalized bounding box coordinates in text format [x_min, y_min, x_max, y_max] (three decimal places, range [0,1]), which triggers RoI sampling and re-engagement as intermediate visual CoT context tokens, before producing the final text answer. This multi-turn structure (question → box → visual context → answer) makes the grounding step an explicit intermediate reasoning signal rather than a separate task.

## Method

Argus augments a standard MLLM pipeline (MoVEs visual encoder with CLIP + ConvNeXt + EVA-02, MLP projectors, Llama3-8B decoder) with a grounding-driven visual attention re-engagement module. The model is trained to predict text-format bounding boxes as visual chain-of-thought signals, and the corresponding RoI is re-engaged via one of four strategies: implicit self-attention, implicit box guidance (boxes as text tokens only), explicit RoI re-encoding (cropping and re-encoding the region through vision encoders), or explicit RoI re-sampling (retrieving cached patch embeddings that overlap the predicted box). Re-sampling is preferred for most tasks as it preserves positional context and reduces computational cost (4355 vs. 8710 GMACs), while re-encoding excels on small-object perception benchmarks. Training proceeds in two stages: alignment pre-training on LLaVA-595K (frozen LLM), followed by full-parameter SFT on Eagle1.8M, VCoT (for visual CoT reasoning), and GRIT+Shikra (for grounding), structured as multi-turn conversations where the agent predicts boxes, receives visual context tokens, then generates the answer.

## Results

Argus-X3-8B achieves state-of-the-art performance among open-source MLLMs of comparable scale, scoring 62.2 average on vision-centric tasks (V-Star 68.1, CV-Bench-2D 68.5, CV-Bench-3D 64.2, MMVP 45.5, RealWorldQA 64.6), 70.1 on text understanding (ChartQA 74.8, TextVQA 73.6, DocVQA 75.4), and 63.4 on general tasks (MMMU-v 40.4, MMB 72.9, SEED 75.8, GQA 65.1). On referring expression grounding, Argus achieves 89.8/92.9/85.4 (Acc@0.5) on RefCOCO val/testA/testB, 84.7/90.1/77.1 on RefCOCO+, and 86.7/85.2 on RefCOCOg, leading all generalist MLLMs of comparable size. The model outperforms its architectural baseline Eagle-X3-8B by +2.6 points on vision-centric tasks and +2.3 on text understanding, while remaining competitive with the specialist Grounding DINO on grounding benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among open-source MLLMs of comparable scale, Argus-X3-8B outperforms all baselines across every benchmark category: it leads Eagle-X3-8B (its architectural base) by +2.6 on vision-centric average and +2.3 on text understanding, surpasses LLaVA-NeXT-8B by +6.8 on vision-centric average, and exceeds Visual-CoT-7B by +7.8 on vision-centric average. On referring expression grounding (Table 2), Argus achieves the best Acc@0.5 among generalist MLLMs, beating QwenVL-7B by +0.4 on RefCOCO val and +1.8 on RefCOCO+ val, and outperforming Ferret-7B by +2.3 on RefCOCO val and +3.9 on RefCOCO+ val. Against the specialist Grounding DINO, Argus is competitive but slightly behind on RefCOCO (89.8 vs 90.6 val) and RefCOCOg test (85.2 vs 87.0), while matching or exceeding it on RefCOCO+ (84.7 vs 82.8 val). Against proprietary references, Argus trails GPT-4o (62.2 vs 73.7 vision-centric) and Qwen2.5-VL (62.2 vs 72.6) on vision-centric tasks, but surpasses Qwen2.5-VL on general tasks (63.4 vs 58.6 MMMU-v) and is competitive on grounding, indicating that the vision-centric grounding mechanism narrows the gap to much larger models.

## Relation to Prior Work

Argus builds directly on Eagle's mixture-of-vision-experts (MoVEs) architecture and the Visual CoT framework, but departs from both by introducing an explicit goal-directed visual attention re-engagement mechanism. While prior MLLMs (LLaVA-NeXT, Cambrian-1, Eagle) rely on implicit self-attention within the LLM to attend to language-relevant visual tokens, Argus makes this engagement explicit: the model first predicts a bounding box as a text-format intermediate step, then re-engages with the corresponding image region via either re-encoding or token re-sampling. The conceptual framing draws an analogy to cognitive science's distinction between stimulus-driven (involuntary) and goal-directed (voluntary) visual attention, positioning the MoVE encoder as the former and the RoI re-engagement module as the latter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit language-guided visual region engagement—implemented as simply as text-format bounding boxes plus cached token resampling—consistently improves both vision-centric reasoning and text-centric understanding over implicit self-attention alone, bridging the gap between grounding and VQA in a unified autoregressive framework. The re-sampling strategy's computational advantage (26 additional tokens vs. 1024, ~492ms vs. ~827ms inference) makes it practically attractive for deployment. Open questions include whether the explicit grounding step remains beneficial at larger model scales where implicit attention may be stronger, how the approach generalizes to multi-region or sequential reasoning, and whether the cognitive-attention framing can be further exploited (e.g., dynamic switching between involuntary and directed attention).
