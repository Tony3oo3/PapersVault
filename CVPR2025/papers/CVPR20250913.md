## Problem

State-of-the-art unsupervised object-centric learning methods based on Slot Attention inherit well-documented drawbacks of soft K-Means clustering: sensitivity to initialization (routing problem), dependence on a predefined number of slots, inconsistent training runs, and poor background segmentation. The paper argues these issues stem from how segregation is handled and proposes replacing the flat K-Means-like mechanism with a hierarchical agglomerative clustering strategy that incorporates a physical compactness prior to provide a spatial inductive bias for object discovery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250913/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and produces object segmentation masks at two levels: the encoder side (a dendrogram of hierarchical cluster assignments) and the decoder side (final slot masks via the Spatial Broadcast Decoder), plus a pixel-level reconstruction. Unlike standard OCL pipelines that fix the number of output slots, COCA-Net dynamically determines the number of objects per image through its sequential concealing process, and it explicitly segments background elements as first-class clusters rather than treating them as a residual. The encoder operates on 16×16 pixel feature grids, progressively pooling through non-overlapping windows at each hierarchical level.

## Method

COCA-Net stacks multiple COCA layers in a bottom-up hierarchy, where each layer performs five steps: (1) refining node features via ViT-22B-style self-attention within spatial neighborhoods, (2) partitioning refined nodes into non-overlapping windows, (3) computing pairwise affinity masks from projected feature distances with soft-argmin normalization, (4) scoring each affinity mask's compactness using a mass-normalized moment-of-inertia formula that compares the mask's inertia to that of an equivalent-area circle, and (5) iteratively selecting the highest-compactness mask as the next cluster via a Stick-Breaking Clustering concealing strategy. The key insight is that moment of inertia is minimized at a shape's centroid, so the compactness score naturally peaks at object centers, providing a spatial inductive bias that breaks slot symmetries without random initialization. Skip connections merge cluster masks between consecutive layers to enable unsupervised feature learning, and the final layer's clusters serve as object slots fed to an SBD decoder trained with MSE reconstruction loss.

## Results

On the standard decoder-foreground evaluation, COCA-Net achieves the highest ARI on 3 of 4 multi-object datasets: 0.91 on ShapeStacks, 0.97 on CLEVR6, and 0.76 on CLEVRTex, while tying INV-SA at 0.88 on ObjectsRoom. For background-included segmentation, COCA-Net shows its largest margins, reaching 0.95 ARI / 0.87 mSC on ObjectsRoom (vs. 0.66/0.68 for INV-SA) and 0.88/0.85 on CLEVR6 (vs. 0.74/0.87 for INV-SA). On the encoder-side dendrogram evaluation, COCA-Net surpasses BOQ-SA by ~30% on ShapeStacks (0.82 vs. 0.49 ARI). Reconstruction MSE is competitive, with COCA-Net matching or approaching the best baselines (0.001–0.006 range).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GEN-v2 [18], COCA-Net wins decisively on every dataset and metric; the gap is most dramatic on CLEVR6 (0.97 vs. 0.43 ARI) and CLEVRTex (0.76 vs. 0.22 ARI), where GEN-v2's random-pixel anchor selection fails to segment textured or multi-object scenes. Against INV-SA [5], COCA-Net matches or exceeds on ARI across all four datasets and wins on mSC for ObjectsRoom (0.82 vs. 0.80) and ShapeStacks (0.85 vs. 0.64); INV-SA retains a narrow mSC edge on CLEVR6 (0.87 vs. 0.82), but COCA-Net dominates on background-included metrics (e.g., 0.95 vs. 0.66 ARI on ObjectsRoom). Against BOQ-SA [28], COCA-Net wins on ARI for all four datasets and on mSC for ShapeStacks (0.85 vs. 0.80) and CLEVRTex (0.54 vs. 0.44); BOQ-SA edges ahead on ObjectsRoom mSC (0.83 vs. 0.82) and CLEVR6 ARI is close (0.86 vs. 0.97 in COCA-Net's favor). COCA-Net also exhibits substantially lower variance across seeds (±0.01–0.02) compared to INV-SA (±0.12–0.13 on ShapeStacks) and BOQ-SA (±0.09–0.21 on CLEVR6), confirming the paper's robustness claim.

## Relation to Prior Work

COCA-Net departs from the dominant Slot Attention paradigm (which the authors frame as a soft K-Means variant suffering from routing problems, fixed slot counts, and poor background handling) by adopting a hierarchical agglomerative clustering strategy within a neural network. The sequential object discovery mechanism is a direct adaptation of Stick Breaking Clustering from GEN-v2/MONET, while the compactness scoring is borrowed from the geography and physics literature on mass-normalized moment of inertia (Massam 1971; Li et al. 2013, 2014). Feature refinement leverages ViT-22B-style self-attention, and reconstruction uses the standard Spatial Broadcast Decoder, making the core novelty the integration of HAC with a physical compactness inductive bias in a bottom-up neural architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COCA-Net demonstrates that hierarchical agglomerative clustering—a largely neglected approach in the OCL community—can match or exceed the state-of-the-art Slot Attention variants on standard synthetic benchmarks, while offering practical advantages: dynamic slot allocation, robust background segmentation, and high-quality encoder-side masks that could serve as a standalone unsupervised segmentation feature extractor. The main open question is generalization to real-world images, where all current OCL methods (including COCA-Net) still struggle; the paper is evaluated exclusively on synthetic datasets. The authors also identify the lack of top-down feedback and the heavy memory cost of the pairwise moment-of-inertia computation as key limitations to address in future work.
