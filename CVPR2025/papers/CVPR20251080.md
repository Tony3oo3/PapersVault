## Problem

Multi-modal inputs such as 3D human pose and text captions substantially improve pedestrian trajectory forecasting, but acquiring text at inference time requires a vision-language model that is computationally infeasible for resource-constrained mobile systems. The paper asks how to transfer the comprehensive motion-intent knowledge encoded by a full-modality teacher into a student that operates on trajectory alone or trajectory plus pose, without needing text or pose at deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251080/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The teacher consumes 2D trajectory sequences, SMPL theta parameters for 3D pose, and text captions (agent behavior and inter-agent interaction) per frame; the student receives only 2D trajectory or trajectory plus 3D pose. On ETH/UCY, pose is replaced by CLIP image features from cropped agent images and text is generated by a rule-based map-context template. All models predict F=6 future trajectory proposals over 12 frames at 2.5 FPS, evaluated under full (8-frame), 2-frame, and 1-frame (instantaneous) observation settings where earlier frames are zero-padded.

## Method

The framework is a two-stage knowledge distillation pipeline: a teacher trained end-to-end on all three modalities is frozen, then a modality-limited student is trained from scratch with regression loss plus KL-divergence (or cosine-similarity for HiVT) distillation losses applied at two levels—the local encoder output Q (intra-agent multi-modal fusion) and the global encoder output H (inter-agent interaction). Both teacher and student share an identical architecture differing only in input modality count: modality-specific embedders (MLP for trajectory/pose, TinyBERT for text) feed into a local encoder (cross-attention transformer for MART, per-frame graph network with rotation-invariant neighbor encoding for HiVT) and a global encoder (group-prediction transformer or graph over agent pairs with interaction-text edge features), followed by an MLP decoder. The key insight is that text acts as a semantic bridge between noisy pose and trajectory, and distilling at both the intra-agent and inter-agent latent spaces allows the student to internalize language-driven motion-intent reasoning without ever seeing text at inference.

## Results

On the JRDB dataset with trajectory-only (X) input, HiVT+KD achieves ADE 0.220 and FDE 0.438, while MART+KD reaches ADE 0.259 and FDE 0.495, both outperforming the LED (ADE 0.358) and ST (ADE 0.324) baselines. On the smaller SIT dataset with X+P input, HiVT+KD delivers the largest gain of +13.03% average improvement (ADE 0.414 vs. 0.518 without KD). On ETH/UCY (BEV-view), HiVT+KD achieves ADE 0.304 and FDE 0.698, surpassing LED (ADE 0.312) and ST (ADE 0.319). Instantaneous prediction (1-frame input) sees the most dramatic gains, with ADE₁ improving up to 27% for MART when moving from X to X+P+S teacher knowledge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiVT+KD dominates all baselines on both ego-view datasets: on JRDB it achieves ADE 0.220 (X) and 0.232 (X+P), well below LED (0.358/0.340) and ST (0.324/0.345); on SIT it reaches ADE 0.404 (X) and 0.414 (X+P), outperforming LED (0.479/0.469) and ST (0.531/0.541). MART+KD also consistently beats LED and ST on all metrics across both datasets, with the largest gap on JRDB (ADE 0.259 vs. 0.358 for LED). On the BEV-view ETH/UCY benchmark, HiVT+KD (ADE 0.304) edges out LED (0.312) and ST (0.319), while MART+KD (0.347) trails both. The KD gain is most pronounced on the smaller SIT dataset (+13.03% for HiVT X+P), where the base model struggles to exploit pose without textual guidance, and on instantaneous (1-frame) prediction where ADE₁ drops by 5–10% relative to the non-KD counterpart.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the knowledge distillation paradigm (Hinton et al.) to the multi-modal trajectory forecasting setting, where a student model with limited input modalities (trajectory or trajectory+pose) is distilled from a teacher trained on trajectory, 3D human pose, and text. It builds directly on two SOTA trajectory predictors—HiVT (graph-based) and MART (transformer-based)—re-implementing both to accept multi-modal inputs via cross-attention and graph operations. The paper departs from prior multi-modal trajectory work (e.g., Social-TransMotion, VisionTrap) by demonstrating that text is the critical bridge enabling effective fusion of noisy pose with trajectory, and from prior KD-for-trajectory work (Monti et al., Xu et al.) by distilling across modalities rather than across observation lengths.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical path for resource-constrained deployment (mobile robots, in-vehicle systems) to inherit multi-modal reasoning without running a VLM at inference time, which is a real bottleneck. The finding that text captions—particularly past-behavior descriptions—serve as the semantic glue between trajectory and pose has broader implications for multi-modal fusion design beyond trajectory forecasting. Limitations include reliance on VLM-generated or annotated text during training, validation only on pedestrian (not vehicle) forecasting, and the fact that the student's gains are most pronounced in data-scarce or instantaneous settings rather than full-observation regimes where strong baselines already perform well.
