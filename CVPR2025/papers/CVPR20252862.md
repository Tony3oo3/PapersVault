## Problem

Existing multimodal communication datasets (Iconary, Co-Draw) rely on turn-based interactions, discrete clip-art icons, and lack natural feedback signals, making them unsuitable for studying the asynchronous, goal-driven dynamics of real human communication. No prior work captures authentic freehand sketching with iconic feedback gestures (correct/incorrect/question) and the temporal overlap of actions between a Drawer and Guesser. The paper also identifies that current VLMs and LLM-based agents operate on rigid turn-taking paradigms, failing to model the continuous, parallel decision-making that characterizes natural cooperative communication.

## Input / Output

DRAWBOT receives a target word, the current canvas rendered as a 224×224 image (strokes thickened 3×), the full guess history CLIP-encoded with timestamps into a 512×2×n_g tensor, and a scalar game-time signal; it outputs a residual stroke image that is vectorized to SVG via virtual sketching and composited onto the canvas. GUESSBOT receives the current canvas embedding (CLIP ViT-B/32) and outputs a single guess word with its timestamp, retrieved from historical sessions. The ActionDecider wraps both agents, consuming aggregated multimodal game-state vectors and emitting one of three discrete actions per 3-second tick: Wake Up, Generate Feedback, or Do Nothing (no-op), enabling true asynchronous pacing.

## Method

The architecture is a two-level hierarchy: a lightweight ActionDecider (CLIP text/vision encoders + 1×1 conv aggregation + 3-layer MLP response head) governs *when* and *what* each agent does, delegating to role-specific modules. DRAWBOT extends Stable Diffusion 1.5 with a ControlNet edge-detection branch for canvas continuity and three modified T2I Adapters (Canvas State, Guess History, Time) that inject non-visual conditioning into the U-Net; it is fine-tuned with LoRA in three stages (ControlNet → adapters → joint) to emit incremental residual strokes rather than full images. GUESSBOT is retrieval-based: it encodes the live canvas with CLIP, queries an Annoy ANN index of historical session embeddings, filters top-10 candidates within a ±6 s time window, and selects the highest cosine-similarity match's guess, naturally inheriting human-like spelling errors. The key design insight is that the ActionDecider's no-op option and 3-second polling cadence let agents act in parallel without turn-taking, and the paper introduces three novel metrics (Feedback Responsiveness Score, Asynchronous Action Overlap, Multimodal Action Timing Similarity via Jensen-Shannon divergence of KDEs) to quantify this asynchronous behavior.

## Results

On the 800-session evaluation set, GUESSBOT achieves a win rate of 81.7% (soft win 86.0%) compared to 85.5% (93.4%) for humans, while DRAWBOT lags significantly at 51.6% (52.4%) win rate, with particularly poor performance on adjectives (30%) and verbs (56%) versus nouns (74%). GUESSBOT's Feedback Responsiveness Score (46%) is comparable to humans (40%), and its Asynchronous Action Overlap (0.17) and MATS (0.27) are closer to human values (0.29 and 0.00) than DRAWBOT's (0.21 and 0.39), indicating more human-like asynchronous pacing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external agent baselines; instead, it benchmarks DRAWBOT and GUESSBOT directly against human performance on the same 800-session evaluation set. GUESSBOT nearly matches human win rate (81.7% vs. 85.5%) and even slightly exceeds humans on FRS (46% vs. 40%), suggesting comparable feedback adaptability. DRAWBOT, however, trails humans by over 33 percentage points in win rate, primarily due to its inability to depict abstract adjectives and verbs that lack a single concrete visual referent. The authors also tested VLMs (e.g., LLaVA, MiniCPM-V) as guessers but found them unsuitable: they produce verbose, unhelpful outputs and operate in a synchronous turn-taking paradigm, making them non-viable for the asynchronous setting. Overall, the agents demonstrate that retrieval-based guessing and diffusion-based sketching can approximate human-like asynchronous multimodal communication, though generative drawing of abstract concepts remains a significant gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Pictionary-like cooperative communication line (Iconary, Co-Draw, SketchGuess) by replacing discrete icons and turn-based interaction with freehand sketches, open-ended guesses, and iconic feedback in an asynchronous setting. For the drawing agent, it builds on Stable Diffusion with ControlNet-style conditioning and a modified T2I-Adapter to handle non-visual inputs (guess history, time), while the guessing agent uses CLIP-based retrieval over a vector database rather than a generative model. The key conceptual departure from prior cooperative drawing work is the full-duplex, asynchronous communication paradigm where both agents continuously monitor and act in parallel, rather than alternating in fixed turns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Sketchtopia is the first large-scale dataset capturing asynchronous multimodal communication with rich feedback signals (iconic gestures, erases, intermediate guesses), filling a gap between static sketch recognition datasets and turn-based cooperative dialogue benchmarks. The novel metrics (AAO, FRS, MATS) provide a framework for evaluating asynchronous interaction quality that is absent from prior Pictionary-style evaluations. Limitations include the small target vocabulary (200 words), predefined feedback icons, and demographic homogeneity, leaving open whether the findings generalize to richer feedback modalities, diverse populations, and more complex multi-turn strategies. The observation that state-of-the-art VLMs fail as asynchronous guessers—producing verbose, unhelpful outputs—raises important questions about the gap between turn-based multimodal reasoning and real-time, contextually responsive communication.
