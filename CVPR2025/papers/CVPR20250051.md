## Problem

Existing visual localization methods cannot simultaneously achieve novel-scene generalization, real-time inference, and high pose accuracy: structure-based pipelines are accurate but slow, absolute pose regression is fast but scene-specific, and relative pose regression generalizes but lags significantly in accuracy. The paper addresses this by demonstrating that a simple symmetric architecture trained at scale on diverse image pairs can close the accuracy gap while retaining generalization and speed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250051/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a query RGB image and a database of posed RGB images; the query is paired with the top-10 retrieved database images via NetVLAD, and each pair is center-cropped and resized to a width of 512 (or 224) pixels. The network outputs a relative pose per pair consisting of a 3×3 rotation matrix (from a 9D representation via SVD orthogonalization) and a unit translation direction vector—deliberately without metric scale. A parameter-free motion averaging module then aggregates these relative estimates across all K pairs to produce the final absolute 6-DoF metric pose (rotation + translation) in the database-defined world frame.

## Method

Reloc3r uses a fully symmetric two-branch Vision Transformer (24 encoder blocks, 12 decoder blocks with cross-attention, RoPE positional embeddings) with shared weights across branches, initialized from DUSt3R's pre-trained 512-DPT weights. Unlike DUSt3R's asymmetric coordinate-alignment design, the symmetric architecture eliminates image-ordering bias and enables weight sharing, while the regression head predicts only translation direction (not metric scale), allowing both rotation and translation to be supervised as angular losses without cross-dataset scale balancing. The network is trained on ~8 million image pairs spanning object-centric, indoor, and outdoor scenes from seven public datasets. A minimalist motion averaging module—comprising quaternion-median rotation averaging and least-squares camera-center triangulation via SVD—converts the relative pose estimates into an absolute metric pose without any robust estimation or trainable parameters. This combination of architectural parsimony, direction-only supervision, and large-scale diverse training is the key to achieving generalization, speed (24 FPS at 512 width), and accuracy simultaneously.

## Results

On the pair-wise relative pose task, Reloc3r-512 achieves AUC@5/10/20 of 34.79/58.37/75.56 on ScanNet1500, 66.70/80.20/88.39 on RealEstate10K, and 38.18/56.39/70.34 on ACID, outperforming all pose regression (PR) methods by a large margin and matching or exceeding the best non-PR methods (e.g., NoPoSplat, MASt3R) on ScanNet1500. On the full visual localization task (7 Scenes), Reloc3r-512 achieves a median error of 0.04 m / 1.02° without any scene-specific training, surpassing all RPR methods including those trained on the same dataset. On Cambridge Landmarks, Reloc3r-512 achieves 0.38 m / 0.52° average error, roughly halving the error of the previous best RPR method (ImageNet+NCM at 0.83 m / 1.36°). Inference runs at 25 ms per pair on an RTX 4090, over 50× faster than the best non-PR method (NoPoSplat, >2000 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Reloc3r-512 dominates all prior pose regression (PR) methods by a wide margin: on ScanNet1500 it achieves AUC@5 of 34.79 versus 2.30 for the best PR baseline (ExReNet-SN), a >15× improvement. Against the strongest non-PR methods, Reloc3r-512 outperforms NoPoSplat (31.80) and MASt3R (28.01) on ScanNet1500 AUC@5, and matches or slightly trails NoPoSplat on RealEstate10K (66.70 vs 69.10) and ACID (38.18 vs 48.60), while being 80× faster (25 ms vs >2000 ms). On the 7 Scenes localization benchmark, Reloc3r-512 (0.04 m / 1.02°) beats all RPR methods including scene-trained ones like CamNet (0.04 m / 1.69°) and Relpose-GNN (0.16 m / 5.20°), and is competitive with APR methods like Marepo (0.03 m / 1.54°) without requiring per-scene training. On Cambridge Landmarks, Reloc3r-512 (0.38 m / 0.52°) halves the average error of the previous best unseen RPR method ImageNet+NCM (0.83 m / 1.36°) and outperforms all APR methods in average rotation error. The CO3Dv2 multi-view evaluation confirms Reloc3r-512's superiority with RRA@15 of 95.8% and mAA@30 of 82.9%, surpassing MASt3R (94.6% / 81.1%) and DUSt3R (94.3% / 77.2%). The key trade-off is that on ACID (aerial outdoor), NoPoSplat retains an AUC@5 advantage of ~10 points, but at 80× the inference cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

Reloc3r directly builds on DUSt3R's ViT encoder-decoder architecture with cross-attention, repurposing it from 3D pointmap prediction to relative pose regression. It departs from DUSt3R in two key ways: adopting a fully symmetric (weight-shared) architecture instead of asymmetric branches, and regressing only rotation and translation *direction* rather than metric 3D coordinates, with a parameter-free motion averaging module recovering absolute scale. Conceptually, it positions itself at the intersection of the foundation-model scaling paradigm and the relative pose regression (RPR) line of work, arguing that prior RPR methods underperformed not due to architectural limitations but due to insufficient training data diversity and scale (~8M pairs here vs. ~523K for Map-free).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Reloc3r is the first pose regression method trained on a diverse mixture of object-centric, indoor, and outdoor data at ~8M-pair scale, and it simultaneously achieves real-time inference (24 FPS at 512px), strong generalization to unseen scenes, and accuracy competitive with or exceeding much slower structure-based methods. The finding that a well-trained patch-level regression network can match or surpass pixel-level feature matching challenges the prior assumption (from EssNet [129]) that pose regression inaccuracies stem from coarse feature localization. A noted limitation is the collinear degeneracy: when all retrieved database images are perfectly collinear with the query, the metric scale becomes unsolvable via motion averaging. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video-based localization, handles dynamic scenes, or benefits from further architectural scaling.
