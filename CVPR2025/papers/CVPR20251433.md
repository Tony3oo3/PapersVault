## Problem

Existing RGB-D pre-training methods either require segmentation labels during pre-training (MultiMAE), fail to learn cross-modal relationships beyond 3D priors (Mask3D), or are limited to small-scale datasets without effective knowledge transfer between stages (CoMAE). Additionally, no prior work has explored denoising—central to diffusion model success—as a representation learning objective for RGB-D data, leaving high-frequency components underexploited.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251433/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB-D image pairs, where depth for ImageNet is extracted via an off-the-shelf AdaBins model and native depth is used for ScanNet (2.5M frames sampled every 25th) and SUN RGB-D (10,335 images). Inputs are split into p×p patches and processed by modality-specific ViT encoders; the output is pre-trained encoder weights that transfer to downstream tasks via a ConvNeXt decoder (segmentation), DPT head (depth), or Mask R-CNN (instance segmentation), with fine-tuning using only the RGB modality.

## Method

The approach is a two-stage progressive pre-training pipeline. Stage 1 applies patch-level InfoNCE contrastive learning to align RGB and depth patch embeddings, capturing local cross-modal context. Stage 2 initializes encoders with stage-1 weights and jointly optimizes three objectives: (1) masked autoencoding that reconstructs masked depth patches using an asymmetric encoder-decoder following MAE, (2) a denoising loss that adds isotropic Gaussian noise to unmasked depth patches and trains the model to predict the noise, with the noise level σ encoded via a 1D sinusoidal embedding passed through an MLP and added to encoded tokens as a positional signal to the decoder, and (3) a smooth L1 feature distillation loss that aligns stage-2 max-pooled global embeddings to frozen stage-1 embeddings. The MAE loss operates on masked patches while the denoising loss operates on unmasked patches, ensuring the decoder is fully utilized without additional computational overhead.

## Results

On ScanNet 2D semantic segmentation, the proposed method achieves 67.5 mIoU with a ViT-B backbone, outperforming Mask3D (66.2 mIoU) by +1.3 and MultiMAE (65.1 mIoU) by +2.4. On SUN RGB-D semantic segmentation, it reaches 48.7 mIoU versus 47.4 for Mask3D. For NYUv2 depth estimation, the method attains 87.1 δ₁, surpassing Mask3D (85.4) and CroCo (85.6) by +1.7 and +1.5 respectively. On ScanNet 2D instance segmentation, it achieves 23.7 AP, exceeding Mask3D (22.8) and MultiMAE (22.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method consistently outperforms all eight baselines across tasks. Against Mask3D [45], the closest competitor, it gains +1.3 mIoU on ScanNet segmentation, +1.3 mIoU on SUN RGB-D, +1.7 δ₁ on NYUv2 depth, and +0.9 AP on instance segmentation. MultiMAE [6] is surpassed by +2.4 mIoU on ScanNet and +1.6 δ₁ on NYUv2 depth. MAE [38] pre-trained on ScanNet RGB is beaten by +2.7 mIoU, confirming the benefit of incorporating depth and contrastive learning. Pri3D [44] and DINO [12], which rely on contrastive or multi-view strategies without depth reconstruction, trail by +7.3 and +9.4 mIoU respectively. CroCo [80], a cross-view completion method, is outperformed by +1.5 δ₁ on depth estimation. CoMAE [84], a single-model hybrid pre-training approach for small-scale RGB-D, is exceeded by +1.1 accs on SUN RGB-D. MoCov2 [86] is surpassed by +5.4 AP on ScanNet instance segmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MAE pre-training paradigm for RGB-D data (as in Mask3D and MultiMAE) by introducing a two-stage progressive strategy: patch-level contrastive learning for cross-modal alignment in stage 1, followed by masked autoencoding with a novel denoising objective and feature distillation in stage 2. It departs from Mask3D by explicitly learning cross-modal relationships rather than relying solely on 3D geometric priors, and from CoMAE by using a multi-stage design that distills stage-1 global embeddings rather than continuing with a single low-level reconstruction task. The denoising component is directly inspired by diffusion models (Ho et al., 2020), repurposing the noise-level encoding mechanism as a self-supervised signal for extracting high-frequency features during pre-training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, label-free pre-training recipe for RGB-D datasets that avoids the segmentation-label dependency of MultiMAE and the modality-lock-in of CoMAE, making it more flexible for downstream fine-tuning with RGB alone. The data-efficiency results in low-label regimes suggest the learned representations are more transferable, which is valuable for resource-constrained settings. However, the absolute gains are modest (+1.3 mIoU over Mask3D, +0.6 mIoU from denoising alone), and the approach still requires access to depth data during pre-training. Open questions include whether the two-stage design is strictly necessary versus a well-tuned single-stage objective, and whether the denoising component generalizes beyond depth to other modalities or larger-scale datasets.
