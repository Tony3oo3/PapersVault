## Problem

Existing normal estimation methods for LiDAR-scanned point clouds rely solely on local geometric features from Euclidean coordinates, which are unreliable due to the anisotropic point distribution (uniform in angular space but non-uniform in Euclidean space) and scanning noise. This limitation is particularly severe for oriented normal estimation, where noise can cause points to appear on the wrong side of a surface, leading to incorrect normal flipping and reconstruction artifacts in complex or thin-plate structures.

## Input / Output

The method consumes LiDAR-scanned point clouds where each point carries six attributes: Euclidean coordinates (x, y, z), ray spherical angles (θ, φ), and a LiDAR station index s. Points are grouped into patches of 768 via k-NN in Euclidean space, and the network outputs a unit oriented normal vector per point. The ray direction and station index are the key distinguishing inputs compared to standard point cloud methods, enabling the network to exploit the uniform angular sampling property of LiDAR and the fact that rays only illuminate exterior surfaces.

## Method

The HAE framework is a three-stage supervised network built on DGCNN edge convolutions. Stage 1 extracts local and global geometric features from xyz coordinates to produce an initial unoriented normal guess. Stage 2 (angular distance encoding) selects the dominant LiDAR station within each patch as the 'main LiDAR,' extracts multi-scale features from the combined (x, y, z, θ, φ) coordinates of those points, and fuses them with full-patch features via skip connections to refine the unoriented normals. Stage 3 (ray feature fusion) concatenates point coordinates, the refined unoriented normal, the ray direction, and their dot product, then performs binary classification to decide whether to invert the normal, leveraging the physical constraint that rays only hit exterior surfaces. Training uses a composite loss of sin loss (unoriented normals), BCE loss (orientation classification), and MSE loss (final oriented normals), with data generated by a virtual LiDAR scanner that replicates real angular sampling and noise profiles on FamousShape and ScanNet meshes.

## Results

On the FamousShape dataset, the proposed HAE method achieves an average oriented normal RMSE of 20.23°, substantially outperforming the next-best baseline CMG-Net (29.41°) by over 9°. On ScanNet, HAE achieves 17.32° average oriented RMSE, again the best among all methods (next best: CMG-Net at 18.27°). For unoriented normals, HAE is competitive, achieving 16.83° (FamousShape) and 14.12° (ScanNet), matching or slightly beating CMG-Net. The advantage is most pronounced under high noise, where HAE records 33.29° vs. 53.41° (CMG-Net) on FamousShape oriented normals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HAE decisively outperforms all six baselines on oriented normal estimation (the primary task) across both datasets and all noise levels. On FamousShape, the gap is largest: HAE's 20.23° average oriented RMSE beats CMG-Net (29.41°) by 9.2° and SHS-Net (35.80°) by 15.6°, with the advantage widening under high noise (33.29° vs. 53.41° for CMG-Net). On ScanNet, the margin is smaller but consistent—HAE leads CMG-Net by 0.95° on average and by 2.36° under high noise. For unoriented normals, HAE is competitive but not uniformly best: it edges out CMG-Net on FamousShape (16.83° vs. 17.89°) and ties on ScanNet (14.12° vs. 14.14°), while GraphFit and Du et al. are slightly ahead on ScanNet unoriented (14.65°/14.50° vs. 14.12°). SHS-Net, the only other end-to-end oriented normal method, degrades severely on ScanNet (39.29° average), suggesting its signed hyper-surface approach struggles with real-scanned indoor scenes. Traditional methods (PCA, N-jet) are consistently the weakest, confirming that learning-based approaches are necessary for noisy, non-uniform LiDAR data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep learning-based normal estimation lineage (PCPNet → GraphFit → SHS-Net → CMG-Net) by introducing a fundamentally different input representation: in addition to xyz coordinates, it encodes LiDAR ray directions (θ, φ) and station indices, exploiting the fact that LiDAR-scanned points are uniformly distributed in angular space but non-uniformly in Euclidean space. The geometric feature extraction backbone follows the DGCNN/edge-convolution paradigm, but the angular distance encoding module and ray feature fusion module are novel components that repurpose scanner-specific metadata previously ignored by all prior normal estimation methods. The virtual scanning pipeline for dataset generation also departs from prior work that relied on uniformly sampled points from CAD models, better matching real LiDAR acquisition distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in LiDAR-based 3D reconstruction: existing normal estimation methods treat all point clouds identically, ignoring the structured non-uniformity inherent to LiDAR scanning, which degrades performance especially on noisy or thin-plate structures. The virtual scanning framework provides a reusable tool for generating training data with realistic scan distributions and noise profiles. However, the method is inherently tied to LiDAR-acquired data (requiring ray directions and station indices), limiting its applicability to MVS, photogrammetry, or other point cloud sources — a limitation the authors explicitly acknowledge. Open questions include whether the angular encoding principle generalizes to other structured acquisition modalities and whether the approach scales to very large outdoor scenes with many scan stations.
