## Problem

Vision language models are increasingly deployed in safety-critical applications, yet their sensitivity to prompt variations remains largely unexplored compared to LLMs. Prior VLM prompt sensitivity studies rely on unrealistic noisy or corrupted prompts, and existing evaluation metrics (accuracy, certainty, consistency) lack direct comparability across datasets and prompt variations due to differing expected random performance. This paper asks: which prompt variations are VLMs most sensitive to, and which VLM families are most agnostic to prompt changes?

## Input / Output

PARC operates on Multiple-Choice Visual Question Answering (MC-VQA) tasks, where each prompt consists of an image (or pair of images for comparative questions) and a text question with discrete answer options. The framework applies 11 prompt variations—three language reformulations, three language semantic changes, three vision reformulations, and two vision semantic changes—then evaluates 22 VLMs across 7 datasets. Outputs are calibrated reliability, accuracy, certainty, and consistency scores in [-1, 1], where 0 represents random performance, enabling direct cross-dataset and cross-prompt comparisons despite varying numbers of answer options (e.g., 2 for comparative datasets vs. 3.7 average for MMBench).

## Method

PARC introduces three technical components. First, a comprehensive set of 11 realistic prompt variations spanning both language (instruction rephrasing, conciseness, verbosity, negation, antonyms, more/less swaps) and vision (blur, lighting, rotation, image swap, image exchange), split into reformulations that preserve the correct answer and semantic changes that alter it. Second, a novel reliability score defined as rel = (2·acc − 1)·cert, which maps accuracy and certainty to a single interpretable value in [-1, 1] with two explicit guarantees: |rel| bounds the certainty, and the sign and magnitude of rel bound the calibrated accuracy improvement or reduction over random. Third, a principled calibration step that rescales all four metrics (accuracy, certainty, consistency, reliability) relative to their expected random performance using s_calib = (s − s_rand)/(1 − s_rand) for s ≥ s_rand and (s − s_rand)/s_rand for s < s_rand, uniquely enabling fair comparisons across datasets with different answer counts and across prompt variations with different numbers of correct answers. Certainty is computed via conformal prediction on softmax scores with a 10% error rate, treating multiple correct answers as a single class for semantically varied prompts.

## Results

Across 22 VLMs evaluated on 7 datasets with 11 prompt variations, InternVL2-40B achieves the highest average calibrated reliability (0.40) and consistency (0.71), while Qwen-VL (reliability 0.06) and CogVLM GG (reliability −0.12) perform worst. Semantic prompt variations are most destructive: image exchange (VS-E) reduces average consistency to 0.00 and language antonyms (LS-A) to 0.09, both near random performance. Reformulations are substantially better tolerated, with language instruction reformulation (LR-I) maintaining 0.78 average consistency versus 0.35 for negation (LS-N). The InternVL2 family dominates across all model sizes, with reliability increasing monotonically from 1B (0.09) to 40B (0.40).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PARC does not propose a new VLM architecture but rather a sensitivity analysis framework, so the 'baselines' are the 22 evaluated VLMs themselves. The InternVL2 family is the clear winner: InternVL2-40B achieves 0.40 average reliability and 0.71 consistency, outperforming the next-best Cambrian-34B (0.30 / 0.64) by 33% in reliability. Within each family, larger models consistently outperform smaller ones (e.g., LLaVA-1.6 34B at 0.33 vs. 7B at 0.18–0.22; InternVL2 40B at 0.40 vs. 1B at 0.09). The Qwen-VL family and CogVLM GG are the most prompt-sensitive, with CogVLM GG showing negative reliability (−0.12), meaning it performs worse than random guessing on average. The paper attributes the InternVL2 advantage primarily to training data quality and scale rather than architecture or image resolution, noting that the reliability ordering across families (InternVL2 > Cambrian > LLaVA-1.6 > LLaVA-1.5) aligns with reported training data size and curation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

PARC extends the LLM prompt sensitivity literature (reformulations, negations, antonyms) to the VLM setting by mirroring language variation classes in the vision domain (blur, lighting, rotation, swap, exchange), a pairing not previously explored. For scoring, it builds on the conformal-prediction-based certainty of Kostumov et al. [28] and the LAC scoring function of Sadinle et al. [49], but departs by wrapping accuracy and certainty into a single reliability score with two explicit, at-a-glance guarantees rather than reporting them separately. The calibration step, which rescales all metrics relative to expected random performance, is novel for VLM evaluation and addresses a comparability gap that prior accuracy/certainty/consistency scores leave unresolved across datasets with differing numbers of answer options.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PARC provides the first systematic, cross-dataset and cross-prompt framework for quantifying VLM prompt sensitivity, which is critical as VLMs are deployed in safety-critical settings (autonomous navigation, medical screening) where inconsistent responses to innocuous prompt rephrasings are unacceptable. The finding that model family (and by extension training data curation) predicts prompt sensitivity more strongly than model size offers a concrete, actionable direction for model developers. Key limitations include the restriction to white-box MC-VQA tasks (precluding generative tasks and black-box APIs), the limited number of semantic vision variations due to dataset-creation cost, and the correlational (not causal) evidence linking training data quality to robustness. Open questions include whether the calibration and reliability framework transfers to open-ended VQA, and whether the InternVL2 advantage persists as the field evolves.
