## Problem

Fine-tuning CLIP for open-vocabulary semantic segmentation suffers from three coupled issues: high computational cost of full fine-tuning, misalignment between the image and text modalities when only one encoder is updated (the dominant asymmetric strategy), and degraded generalization on unseen categories. Existing parameter-efficient methods either freeze the text encoder entirely (causing modality misalignment) or lack a principled mechanism to preserve CLIP's intrinsic semantic structure while acquiring pixel-level prediction ability.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and arbitrary text category descriptions as input, producing a per-pixel semantic segmentation mask that assigns each pixel to one of the text-described categories. It is built on CLIP ViT-B/16 or ViT-L/14 backbones, trained on COCO-Stuff (171 categories, ~118K images), and evaluated on ADE20K (847/150 classes), PASCAL-Context (459/59 classes), and PASCAL VOC (20 classes). The segmentation decoder follows the cost-based approach from CAT-Seg, and the method also generalizes to open-vocabulary object detection on COCO.

## Method

H-CLIP introduces a symmetric parameter-efficient fine-tuning strategy in hyperspherical space with two core components. First, Partial Orthogonal Parameterization (POP) pairs each pre-trained CLIP weight matrix with a block-diagonal learnable transformation matrix (block size q=128); for the text encoder, these matrices are constrained to be strictly orthogonal via Cayley parameterization, which preserves the hyperspherical energy of the pre-trained parameter space and thus its generalization capability, while the image encoder's matrices are left unconstrained to learn segmentation. Second, Dual Cross-Relation Communication (DCRC) organizes all block-diagonal matrices across both modalities and all Transformer layers into a 4-order tensor and applies learnable relation matrices (implemented as 2-layer MLPs) along the modality and layer dimensions via tensor-product operations, explicitly encouraging cross-modal and cross-layer alignment with minimal overhead. Together, these components update only ~4% of CLIP's total parameters (5.6M for ViT-B/16) while achieving state-of-the-art results across all benchmarks.

## Results

H-CLIP achieves state-of-the-art open-vocabulary semantic segmentation across all six test splits (A-847, PC-459, A-150, PC-59, PAS-20, PAS-20b) on ADE20K, PASCAL-Context, and PASCAL VOC, using only ~4% of CLIP's total parameters (5.6M learnable params). With CLIP ViT-B/16, H-CLIP reaches 12.5 mIoU on A-847, 19.4 on PC-459, 32.4 on A-150, 57.9 on PC-59, 95.2 on PAS-20, and 78.2 on PAS-20b. With CLIP ViT-L/14, it further improves to 16.5, 24.2, 38.4, 64.1, 97.7, and 83.2 respectively, outperforming the strongest full fine-tuning baseline CAT-Seg by 0.5–0.8 mIoU on each split.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest full fine-tuning baseline CAT-Seg [7], H-CLIP wins on every split with both backbones: +0.5 mIoU on A-847, +0.4 on PC-459, +0.6 on A-150, +0.4 on PC-59, +0.6 on PAS-20, and +0.9 on PAS-20b (ViT-B/16); the margins are similar for ViT-L/14. Against the PEFT baseline SAN [65], H-CLIP achieves substantially larger gains, e.g., +6.8 mIoU on PC-459 and +4.1 on A-150 with ViT-B/16, and +8.5 on PC-459 and +6.3 on A-150 with ViT-L/14, while using fewer learnable parameters (5.6M vs. 8.4M). Compared to other PEFT methods in the ablation (LoRA, OFT, VPT, Adapter, LST, SSF), H-CLIP consistently leads on all six benchmarks, with the largest advantage on the harder A-847 and PC-459 splits where generalization to unseen categories matters most. On the secondary open-vocabulary object detection task (COCO), H-CLIP also outperforms LoRA (25.1 vs. 24.4 AP50-Base, 42.9 vs. 41.5 AP50-Novel) and OFT (25.1 vs. 24.1, 42.9 vs. 41.7), confirming that the hyperspherical-space PEFT strategy generalizes beyond segmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

H-CLIP extends the orthogonal fine-tuning (OFT) principle from text-to-image diffusion to the CLIP-based open-vocabulary segmentation setting, repurposing the hyperspherical energy preservation mechanism to protect the text encoder's generalization while allowing the image encoder to learn pixel-level predictions. It departs from prior asymmetric PEFT approaches (e.g., SAN, CAT-Seg) that freeze the text encoder and fine-tune only the image encoder, instead applying a symmetric parameter-efficient strategy to both modalities. The dual cross-relation communication module is a novel architectural contribution built on tensor-tensor product theory [22], introducing cross-modal and cross-layer interactions among all learnable block-diagonal matrices—a communication mechanism absent from standard PEFT methods like LoRA or adapters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hyperspherical energy framing provides a principled, theoretically grounded alternative to ad-hoc strategies (small learning rates, selective layer freezing) for balancing generalization preservation and task adaptation in CLIP fine-tuning, and the ~4% parameter budget makes the approach practical for resource-constrained settings. The method's demonstrated transferability to open-vocabulary object detection suggests the POP+DCRC framework could generalize beyond segmentation to other dense prediction tasks. Open questions include whether the tensor-product-based DCRC is truly necessary (ablations show POP alone captures most gains), whether the approach extends to non-CLIP vision-language models, and whether the block-diagonal approximation of orthogonality can be tightened without prohibitive cost.
