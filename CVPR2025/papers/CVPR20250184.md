## Problem

Existing 3D generative models are locked into specific representations (meshes, 3D Gaussians, Radiance Fields), each excelling at either geometry or appearance but not both, and none offer a unified latent space from which diverse output formats can be decoded. This fragmentation prevents a standardized 3D generation paradigm analogous to the unified latent-space approach that has become standard in 2D image generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250184/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts text prompts (generated by GPT-4o) or single RGB image prompts (generated by DALL-E 3) and produces 3D assets in user-selectable formats: 3D Gaussians, Radiance Fields, or meshes. Internally, the intermediate representation is a sparse 64³ grid with ~20K active voxels, each carrying an 8-dimensional latent vector (SLAT). Generation completes in roughly 10 seconds at inference, and the same SLAT can be decoded into any supported format without retraining, enabling format-agnostic output selection and local region editing.

## Method

The core contribution is SLAT, a structured latent that anchors dense DINOv2 multiview image features onto a sparse 3D voxel grid, so that active voxels encode coarse geometry while per-voxel latents capture fine appearance and shape details. A sparse transformer VAE (with shifted window attention for locality) encodes 3D assets into SLAT and decodes them via format-specific heads (Gaussian parameters, Strivec CP-decomposition for Radiance Fields, or FlexiCubes parameters for meshes). Generation uses a two-stage rectified flow pipeline: a first transformer denoises a compressed dense grid to recover the sparse structure, and a second transformer (with sparse-convolution packing and skip connections) generates the per-voxel latents conditioned on the structure. Both stages use conditional flow matching with adaLN timestep injection and cross-attention for text (CLIP) or image (DINOv2) conditioning, trained up to 2B parameters on 500K assets with classifier-free guidance.

## Results

On Toys4k text-to-3D, TRELLIS-XL achieves a CLIP score of 26.70, FD_inception of 20.48, and FD_DINOv2 of 237.48, substantially outperforming all baselines (next best: GaussianCube at FD_inception 27.35, FD_DINOv2 460.07). For image-to-3D, TRELLIS-Latt achieves CLIP 85.77, FD_inception 9.35, and FD_DINOv2 67.21, again leading all competitors by wide margins. In reconstruction fidelity (Table 1), SLAT attains PSNR 32.74, LPIPS 0.025, Chamfer Distance 0.0083, and F-score 0.9999, surpassing even shape-only methods like CLAY (CD 0.0124, F-score 0.9976).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRELLIS outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric in both text-to-3D and image-to-3D settings. The most dramatic gains appear in FD_DINOv2: for text-to-3D, TRELLIS-XL scores 237.48 versus 460.07 (GaussianCube), 478.92 (InstantMesh), and 976.40 (Ln3Diff); for image-to-3D, it reaches 67.21 versus 264.36 (InstantMesh) and 465.74 (Shap-E). CLIP scores (prompt alignment) are also consistently higher, e.g., 26.70 vs. 25.56 (InstantMesh) for text-to-3D and 85.77 vs. 84.43 (InstantMesh) for image-to-3D. Among 3D-native methods, 3DTopia-XL and Ln3Diff lag furthest behind, reflecting the limited reconstruction fidelity of their latent representations. The 2D-assisted methods (InstantMesh, LGM) suffer from multiview inconsistency, while GaussianCube and LGM cannot produce plausible geometry due to their 3D Gaussian output format. In reconstruction (Table 1), SLAT also beats CLAY (a shape-only model) on Chamfer Distance (0.0083 vs. 0.0124) and F-score (0.9999 vs. 0.9976), demonstrating that the unified representation captures geometry at least as well as dedicated shape encoders while additionally encoding appearance.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRELLIS builds on the rectified flow paradigm (Lipman et al., ICLR 2023) and DiT-style transformer architectures (Peebles & Xie, ICCV 2023) as its generative backbone, while drawing on sparse voxel and structured 3D reconstruction techniques (Strivec, FlexiCubes) for its decoders. Its key departure from prior 3D generation work (LN3Diff, 3DTopia, CLAY, GaussianCube) is the introduction of SLAT—a unified structured latent that anchors dense DINOv2 image features onto a sparse 3D grid—eliminating the need for dedicated 3D encoders or costly pre-fitting of assets to specific representations. The two-stage generation (sparse structure → local latents) and the Repaint-based editing strategy further distinguish it from single-stage or 2D-assisted approaches like DreamFusion, LGM, and InstantMesh.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single latent space can faithfully decode into Gaussians, Radiance Fields, and meshes with high fidelity, SLAT addresses a long-standing fragmentation in 3D generation where each representation required its own pipeline. The fitting-free training on 500K assets and the demonstrated editing capabilities (region-specific, detail variation) open practical pathways for production 3D workflows. Open questions include whether the approach extends to full scenes (beyond single objects), whether the DINOv2 features capture all necessary 3D properties at higher resolutions, and how the method scales to interactive or real-time generation settings.
