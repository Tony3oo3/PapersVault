## Problem

Transformer-based semantic occupancy predictors suffer from quadratic computational complexity that becomes prohibitive when processing the millions of voxels in large-scale driving scenes (e.g., 512×512×40 grids in OpenOccupancy). While Mamba's selective state space models offer linear-time global modeling, they are designed for 1D sequences, and naively flattening 3D voxel grids destroys the spatial adjacency that Mamba relies on for contextual reasoning. No prior work had adapted Mamba to outdoor semantic occupancy prediction with an effective 3D-to-1D reordering strategy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250693/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-frame LiDAR point clouds (10 frames spanning ±51.2 m in X/Y and −2 to 6 m in Z for OpenOccupancy; single-frame for SemanticKITTI/SemanticPOSS) and multi-view camera images (6 surround-view or forward stereo), producing a dense 3D occupancy grid with per-voxel semantic labels at 0.2 m resolution. Unlike projection-based methods that compress features into BEV or tri-perspective views, OccMamba processes the full uncompressed voxel volume directly, with spatial dimensions matching the ground-truth grid (512×512×40 for OpenOccupancy, 256×256×32 for SemanticKITTI).

## Method

OccMamba replaces M-CONet's CNN occupancy encoder with a hierarchical Mamba encoder-decoder (four groups of two Mamba blocks each, with downsampling/upsampling and skip connections) plus a lightweight local context processor that scans XY-plane patches at window sizes [3, 5, 7] with dedicated two-layer Mamba blocks. The critical enabler is a height-prioritized 2D Hilbert expansion reordering: voxels are first sequenced along the z-axis (exploiting height as a categorical prior), then the resulting vertical columns are ordered by a 2D Hilbert curve on the XY plane, preserving both vertical and horizontal spatial proximity in the 1D sequence fed to Mamba. Each Mamba block applies LayerNorm → Linear → 1D Conv → SiLU → Selective SSM → gated Linear output, with the reordering applied before and after every block group. Training is supervised with a composite loss of cross-entropy, Lovász-softmax, geometric/semantic affinity, and depth supervision, all weighted equally.

## Results

On OpenOccupancy (512×512×40 voxel grid), OccMamba-384 achieves 35.7% IoU and 26.2% mIoU, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art Co-Occ by 5.1% IoU and 4.3% mIoU. On SemanticKITTI (256×256×32 grid), OccMamba-128 reaches 24.6% mIoU, edging out Co-Occ (24.4%) by 0.2%. On SemanticPOSS, OccMamba-128 achieves 23.4% mIoU with LiDAR-only input, outperforming JS3C-Net (22.7%) by 0.7%. The lighter OccMamba-128 variant also reduces inference memory by ~44% and inference time by ~35% compared to M-CONet while still exceeding its accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OccMamba consistently outperforms all 15 baselines across the three benchmarks. On OpenOccupancy, it surpasses the strongest multi-modal baseline Co-Occ [31] by 5.1% IoU / 4.3% mIoU (OccMamba-384) and by 4.1% / 3.3% (OccMamba-128), and beats the CNN-based M-CONet [45] by 5.2% / 6.1% IoU / mIoU. Among camera-only methods, OccMamba exceeds TPVFormer [14] and SparseOcc [39] by over 13% mIoU, and camera+depth methods like 3DSketch [5] and AICNet [15] by over 14% mIoU. On SemanticKITTI, OccMamba-128 (24.6% mIoU) narrowly beats Co-Occ (24.4%) and substantially outperforms LiDAR-only methods such as JS3C-Net [49] (23.8%) and SSC-RS [29] (24.2%), as well as camera-only methods like OccFormer [52] (12.3%) and RenderOcc [32] (12.8%). On SemanticPOSS, OccMamba-128 with LiDAR-only input (23.4% mIoU) beats JS3C-Net [49] (22.7%) and MotionSC [47] (17.6%), demonstrating that the Mamba encoder generalizes even without camera input.

## Relation to Prior Work

OccMamba is built directly on the M-CONet multi-modal pipeline (sparse-convolution LiDAR encoder, ResNet-50 + FPN image encoder, BEV view transformer, and coarse-to-fine occupancy head), replacing only the CNN-based occupancy encoder with a hierarchical Mamba encoder-decoder. Conceptually, it departs from the transformer-based occupancy predictors (OccFormer, OccNet) that dominate recent work, motivated by their quadratic complexity on the millions of voxels typical of driving scenes. The reordering scheme draws on the 3D Hilbert curve idea from PointMamba for point cloud analysis but adapts it to the flat, height-stratified geometry of outdoor driving scenes via a height-prioritized 2D Hilbert expansion, a design choice not present in prior Mamba-for-3D work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a linear-complexity SSM can match or exceed transformer-based occupancy predictors while substantially reducing memory and inference time, OccMamba offers a practical path toward deploying dense 3D occupancy perception on resource-constrained platforms. The height-prioritized reordering is a simple, reusable technique that could transfer to other 3D voxel or grid-based tasks where spatial structure is anisotropic. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to higher-resolution or larger-range scenes beyond the current benchmarks, whether the CNN front-end encoders can also be replaced by SSMs, and how the method scales to real-time autonomous driving latency budgets.
