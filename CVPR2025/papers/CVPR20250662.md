## Problem

Video Large Language Models (Vid-LLMs) excel at recognizing what happens in a video but fail to localize when events occur, often hallucinating nonsensical temporal boundaries (e.g., 'from frame 000 to 580' in a 10-frame video). This gap arises because these models are trained to align visual content with language descriptions but lack a direct mechanism to interpret temporal boundaries, and existing solutions require additional tokens, specialized embeddings, or architectural modifications that limit transferability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250662/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video sampled at 336×336 resolution where each frame has a red frame number (font size 40) overlaid in the bottom-right corner, along with a natural-language event query and a prepended instruction ('The red numbers on each frame represent the frame number'). For moment retrieval, the output is a pair of start and end frame numbers formatted as 'From x to y'; for highlight detection, it produces ranked relevance scores over video clips. The temporal information is entirely encoded as visually readable text on the frames themselves rather than as separate input tokens or learned embeddings.

## Method

NumPro overlays unique numerical identifiers on each video frame, transforming temporal grounding into an OCR-based visual alignment task that Vid-LLMs can perform using their built-in text-recognition capabilities without any architectural changes. In the training-free setting, this requires only adding the numbers and a one-sentence instruction; in the fine-tuned setting (NumPro-FT), the model is trained on 220k NumPro-augmented samples from DiDeMo, ActivityNet Caption, and VTimeLLM using LoRA (r=64, α=128) on the LLM while freezing the visual encoder. The optimal visual design (red color, font size 40, bottom-right position) is determined via CLIP-based experiments on MSCOCO that balance Number Accuracy (recognizability of the overlaid digits) against Caption Accuracy (preservation of original visual content). This approach avoids introducing new tokens, modifying vocabulary, or adding temporal embeddings, making it a zero-cost plug-in for any existing Vid-LLM.

## Results

NumPro-FT (fine-tuned LongVA-7B-DPO) achieves 41.4 mIoU on Charades-STA and 38.8 mIoU on ActivityNet for moment retrieval, surpassing the previous SOTA (HawkEye: 33.7 and 32.7 mIoU) by 7.7% and 6.1% respectively. For highlight detection on QVHighlights, NumPro-FT reaches 25.0 mAP and 37.2 HIT@1, exceeding the prior best (VTG-LLM: 16.5 mAP) by 8.5%. In the training-free setting, NumPro applied to GPT-4o yields 37.6 mIoU on Charades-STA and 33.6 mIoU on ActivityNet, while Qwen2-VL-7B with NumPro jumps from 7.9 to 38.5 mIoU on Charades-STA (+24.7% absolute).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NumPro-FT (LongVA-7B-DPO) decisively outperforms all VTG-tuned baselines: it surpasses HawkEye (33.7 mIoU) by 7.7% on Charades-STA and by 6.1% on ActivityNet (38.8 vs. 32.7), and beats VTimeLLM (31.2/30.4 mIoU) by 10.2%/8.4% on the two datasets. On highlight detection, NumPro-FT's 25.0 mAP exceeds VTG-LLM (16.5) by 8.5 points and TimeChat (14.5) by 10.5 points. In the training-free setting, NumPro applied to GPT-4o improves moment retrieval mIoU from 35.4 to 37.6 on Charades-STA and from 23.7 to 33.6 on ActivityNet, while also boosting QVHighlights mAP from 39.5 to 40.5. For weaker open-source models, the gains are dramatic: Qwen2-VL-7B jumps from 7.9 to 38.5 mIoU on Charades-STA (+30.6), LLaVA-Video-7B from 9.8 to 34.6 (+24.8), and Qwen2-VL-72B from 0.2 to 17.4 (+17.2), indicating that the base models' visual understanding was previously bottlenecked by the lack of temporal cues rather than by visual recognition quality. NumPro-FT also consistently outperforms standard fine-tuning without NumPro augmentation (+FT), achieving 41.4 vs. 40.2 mIoU on Charades-STA and 38.8 vs. 29.0 on ActivityNet, confirming that the frame-number overlay provides a meaningful inductive bias for temporal grounding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the visual prompting paradigm (circles, bounding boxes, semantic masks used for spatial grounding in VLMs) for a fundamentally different axis: temporal grounding in video. Prior VTG methods for Vid-LLMs (TimeChat, VTimeLLM, VTG-LLM, HawkEye) introduce additional temporal tokens, specialized embeddings, or architectural modifications to convey timing information. NumPro departs from all of these by exploiting the OCR capability already present in Vid-LLMs, overlaying frame numbers as a purely visual cue that requires no new tokens, no vocabulary changes, and no architectural modifications — effectively converting a temporal reasoning problem into a visual reading task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a zero-cost, plug-and-play technique that consistently improves VTG across both closed-source (GPT-4o) and open-source (Qwen2-VL, LLaVA, LongVA) models, with the fine-tuned variant setting new SOTA on standard benchmarks. The minimal impact on general video-QA (Table 4) suggests the approach can be deployed without degrading broader video understanding, making it attractive for end-to-end video dialogue systems. Open questions include scalability to very long videos where frame numbers become large, robustness when the bottom-right corner contains semantically important content, and whether the approach generalizes to more complex temporal reasoning (e.g., multi-event sequences, causal ordering) beyond simple start-end boundary detection.
