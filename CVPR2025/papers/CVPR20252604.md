## Problem

Prior scaling analyses for Scene Text Recognition concluded that increasing decoder depth degrades performance, leading to an overemphasis on encoder scaling. Additionally, real-world STR datasets contain substantial label noise and inconsistencies that limit model accuracy, and existing decoder architectures struggle to focus attention on relevant context. The paper asks: what are the true bottlenecks limiting STR, and how can both accuracy and efficiency be improved simultaneously?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252604/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a scene text image resized to 224×224 pixels, split into 16×16 patches for the ViT encoder, and outputs a recognized text sequence of up to 25 characters. Training uses a 94-character vocabulary (mixed-case alphanumeric plus punctuation) while inference restricts to 36 lowercase alphanumeric characters. The decoder operates via Permutation Language Modeling during training (using causal, anti-causal, and 4 random permutations) and standard left-to-right greedy decoding at inference.

## Method

The paper makes three contributions. First, an empirical scaling analysis demonstrates that decoder scaling (PLD-T→PLD-L) yields +1.56% AVG₁₁ with only 26.3M extra parameters, outperforming encoder scaling (ViT-T→ViT-B) which gives +1.16% at 80.3M extra parameters, contradicting prior findings. Second, Cloze Self-Distillation (CSD) mitigates label noise: a teacher model trained on noisy data generates pseudolabels and full-context cloze soft predictions (each character re-predicted given all other characters), then an identical student is trained with NLL on pseudolabels plus a KD loss minimizing KL divergence between the teacher's full-context cloze outputs and the student's limited-context PLM outputs (α=0.1, τ=2.0). Third, the decoder replaces standard Multi-Head Cross-Attention with Differential Cross-Attention (two softmax attention maps subtracted to cancel common noise modes) and SwiGLU activations, adding only 0.1 GFLOPs while improving focus on relevant context.

## Results

The best model CSD-D (ViT-Base + PLD-Diff, 110.2M params) achieves 92.7% AVG₁₁ and 97.4% wAVG₆ on the Real dataset, and 93.3% AVG₁₁ and 97.6% wAVG₆ on RBU, surpassing CLIP4STR-L (446M params) which scores 91.7% AVG₁₁ and 97.0% wAVG₆ on Real. The method achieves state-of-the-art on 10 out of 11 benchmarks using only real data, while requiring only 24.7% of the parameters and 23.9% of the GFLOPs of CLIP4STR-L. Even the smaller CSD-S model (40.8M params) reaches 92.5% AVG₁₁ on Real, outperforming all prior methods with fewer than 50M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLIP4STR-L (446M params, the strongest prior method on Real), CSD-D (110.2M) wins on 10 of 11 benchmarks, achieving 92.7% AVG₁₁ vs. 91.7% while using only 24.7% of the parameters. The smaller CSD-S (40.8M) already surpasses CLIP4STR-B (158M) on AVG₁₁ (92.5% vs. 91.1%) and leads on occluded-text benchmarks (HOST: 83.5% vs. 77.5%; WOST: 90.9% vs. 87.5%). Compared to PARSeq (22.5M), CSD-S improves AVG₁₁ by 2.6 points (92.5% vs. 89.9%) with roughly 1.8× more parameters, while CSD-D gains 2.8 points. On the larger RBU dataset, CSD-D (93.3% AVG₁₁) matches CLIP4STR-H (1B params, 92.8%) and exceeds CLIP4STR-L on RBU (92.7%), using only ~11% of the parameters of the 1B model. The only benchmark where CSD-D does not lead on Real is SVTP, where CLIP4STR-L scores 97.4% vs. CSD-D's 98.1%—actually CSD-D wins there too; the single loss is on C80 where CLIP4STR-L (91.3%) slightly trails CSD-D (92.4%), so CSD-D wins all 11 on Real except IIIT5K where it ties at 99.3–99.5%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the PARSeq architecture and its Permutation Language Modeling (PLM) training objective, extending the Permutation Language Decoder with a Differential Cross-Attention mechanism adapted from the Differential Transformer. It departs from the scaling analysis of Rang et al. (CLIP4STR), which concluded that increasing decoder depth degrades STR performance and that encoder scaling is the primary lever; here, the authors demonstrate the opposite under a modified decoder design that avoids updating context and vision tokens across blocks. The Cloze Self-Distillation technique is novel, combining PLM's bidirectional context with knowledge distillation to combat label noise, a problem acknowledged but not previously addressed with a dedicated training procedure in the STR literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that decoder scaling is more parameter- and compute-efficient than encoder scaling directly contradicts prior guidance and provides practitioners with a concrete, well-evidenced recipe for building larger STR models without resorting to CLIP pre-training or cross-modal branches. CSD offers a practical remedy for the pervasive label noise in real-world STR datasets, yielding consistent gains across data scales. Open questions include whether the decoder-scaling advantage persists at much larger scales (beyond ViT-Base/PLD-Large), whether CSD transfers to other recognition tasks (e.g., handwriting, document OCR), and whether the differential attention benefit generalizes to encoder-side or other cross-modal settings.
