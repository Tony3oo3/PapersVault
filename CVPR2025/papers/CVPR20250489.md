## Problem

Prior unsupervised panoptic segmentation (U2Seg) depends on object-centric pseudo-labeling via MaskCut, which fails on scene-centric imagery, and relies on low-resolution semantic predictions that degrade on high-resolution data. No existing method can directly train a panoptic network on complex scene-centric datasets without object-centric training data or manual annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250489/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Pseudo-label generation consumes consecutive stereo video frame pairs (left-right images at two timesteps) to extract scene flow and depth, while the final panoptic network operates on single monocular RGB images at inference. The output is a full panoptic segmentation with 27 pseudo-semantic classes partitioned into 'thing' and 'stuff' categories, plus per-instance masks for moving objects. The depth and motion cues are used exclusively during the offline pseudo-labeling stage and are not required at test time.

## Method

CUPS generates high-resolution panoptic pseudo labels by fusing three unsupervised signals: (1) instance masks from ensembled SF2SE3 motion segmentation on scene flow (computed via SMURF optical flow and disparity), with consistency filtering and NMS to remove spurious masks; (2) semantic pseudo labels from a DepthG-based model distilling DINO features, enhanced by depth-guided inference that blends low-resolution and high-resolution (sliding-window) predictions weighted by inverse depth; and (3) instance-semantic fusion that assigns consistent class IDs within each mask and separates 'thing' from 'stuff' via a frequency-ratio threshold. The panoptic network (Panoptic Cascade Mask R-CNN with DINO ResNet-50) is then bootstrapped using DropLoss (which only supervises instances overlapping pseudo masks, allowing expansion to static objects) and self-enhanced copy-paste augmentation, followed by self-training with an EMA momentum network, multi-scale augmentation, and class-dependent confidence thresholding to produce refined self-labels.

## Results

On Cityscapes val, CUPS achieves a panoptic quality (PQ) of 27.8%, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art U2Seg (18.4%) by 9.4 percentage points, with gains of +12.5 in recognition quality (RQ) and +10.8 in PQ for 'stuff' classes. CUPS also sets new state-of-the-art results on the sub-tasks: 26.8% mIoU for unsupervised semantic segmentation (vs. 23.1% for DepthG) and 30.5% AP50 for class-agnostic instance segmentation on Waymo (vs. 25.1% for MOD-UV). Across generalization benchmarks (KITTI, BDD, MUSES, Waymo, MOTS), CUPS consistently outperforms U2Seg, with the largest margin of +17.1 PQ on the out-of-domain MOTS dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CUPS decisively outperforms U2Seg [55], the only prior unsupervised panoptic method, by +9.4 PQ on Cityscapes and by 4–17 PQ across all five generalization datasets, while using only 27 pseudo classes versus U2Seg's 827. On unsupervised semantic segmentation (Table 3), CUPS (83.2% Acc, 26.8% mIoU) surpasses DepthG [62] (81.6%, 23.1%), EAGLE [38] (79.4%, 22.1%), STEGO [30] (73.2%, 21.0%), and all other baselines including PiCIE [19], DiffSeg [72], HP [60], and PriMaPs-EM [29]. For class-agnostic instance segmentation on Waymo (Table 4), CUPS (AP50 30.5%, AP 12.4%) beats MOD-UV [69] (25.1%, 11.1%), CutLER [78] (9.1%, 5.2%), and HASSOD [9] (3.9%, 2.0%), though it trails MOD-UV slightly on small-object AP (2.6% vs. 4.5%). The supervised Panoptic FPN [39] upper bound (62.3% PQ) remains well above CUPS, but the label-efficient fine-tuning experiment shows that just 60 annotated images (2% of Cityscapes) recover 70% of the supervised PQ, highlighting the practical value of CUPS as a pre-training stage.

## Relation to Prior Work

CUPS extends the unsupervised panoptic segmentation line initiated by U2Seg [55], which combined CutLER's MaskCut (object-centric instance discovery) with STEGO's semantic distillation. The key departure is replacing MaskCut's visual-only, object-centric pseudo-labeling with a scene-centric pipeline that fuses SSL 3D motion segmentation (SF2SE3 on scene flow from SMURF) for instances and depth-guided DINO feature inference (building on DepthG) for semantics. The training side introduces self-enhanced copy-paste augmentation and a momentum-network self-training loop adapted from the student-teacher literature to the panoptic setting, neither of which appeared in prior unsupervised panoptic work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the dependence on object-centric training data, CUPS opens the door to unsupervised panoptic pre-training on the rich scene-centric imagery that dominates real-world applications (autonomous driving, robotics). The label-efficient results—60 annotated images recovering 70% of supervised PQ—suggest a practical pathway for label-scarce deployments. However, the absolute gap to supervised panoptic segmentation remains large (27.8% vs. 62.3% PQ on Cityscapes), and the pseudo-labeling stage requires stereo video, limiting applicability to monocular-only settings. Open questions include whether the motion cue generalizes to static scenes or single-image inputs, and whether the 27-pseudo-class constraint is a fundamental bottleneck or an artifact of the evaluation protocol.
