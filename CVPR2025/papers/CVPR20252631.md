## Problem

Most existing shadow removal methods rely on binary (hard) shadow masks to indicate shadow regions, which cannot represent the penumbra area where illumination transitions gradually. This limitation causes visible boundary artifacts in soft shadow images, where the transition between lit and shaded regions is blurred rather than sharp. The paper asks whether a continuous grayscale mask, informed by the physical model of shadow formation, can better guide shadow removal in these transition zones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252631/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a single RGB shadow image (256×256) as input and produces both a shadow-free image and an intermediate soft shadow mask (grayscale, where 0 = lit area, 1 = umbra, and values in [0,1] = penumbra). No external mask input is required; the soft mask is generated internally by the SAM-based detector and fed to the removal network. Ground truth soft masks for training are derived by dividing the Y-channel of the shadow-free image by the Y-channel of the shadow image, followed by low-pass filtering and thresholding.

## Method

SoftShadow integrates a pretrained SAM (ViT-H backbone) with LoRA (rank 8) as a soft shadow mask detector, coupled with ShadowDiffusion as the removal backbone, all trained end-to-end. Three losses jointly optimize the system: a shadow removal L2 loss (L_rem) between predicted and ground-truth shadow-free images, a mask reconstruction loss (L_mask) between predicted and derived ground-truth soft masks, and a novel penumbra formation constraint loss (L_pen) that regularizes the gradient direction and magnitude within the penumbra region based on the physical shadow formation geometry. The penumbra constraint defines the penumbra area via two intensity thresholds, computes a unit vector from the shadow center to each penumbra pixel, and penalizes gradients that conflict with the expected outward direction using a ReLU-gated dot product. This physical prior encourages the SAM to produce spatially varied, smooth soft masks rather than binary outputs, providing more accurate positional guidance to the removal network and reducing boundary artifacts.

## Results

On the SRD dataset, SoftShadow achieves 35.57 dB PSNR, 0.975 SSIM, and 3.11 MAE on all pixels, outperforming the best mask-based method HomoFormer (35.37 dB PSNR) and the best mask-free method DeS3 (34.11 dB PSNR). On ISTD+, SoftShadow reaches 35.00 dB PSNR and 2.85 MAE without requiring any input mask, surpassing ShadowDiffusion with FDRNet masks (34.08 dB) and DeS3 (31.38 dB). In the penumbra region specifically, SoftShadow achieves 41.46 dB PSNR and 4.40 MAE, improving over HomoFormer (41.11 dB, 5.91 MAE). For generalizability, zero-shot transfer to LRSS and UIUC yields 23.32 dB and 28.85 dB PSNR respectively, outperforming all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SoftShadow outperforms all 12 compared baselines on both SRD and ISTD+ without requiring any external mask input. On SRD, it surpasses the strongest mask-based method HomoFormer (DHAN mask) by 0.20 dB PSNR (35.57 vs. 35.37) and the strongest mask-free method DeS3 by 1.46 dB (35.57 vs. 34.11). On ISTD+, SoftShadow beats ShadowDiffusion with FDRNet masks by 0.92 dB (35.00 vs. 34.08) and DeS3 by 3.62 dB (35.00 vs. 31.38), while remaining competitive with methods that use ground-truth masks (ShadowDiffusion GT: 35.67 dB). In the penumbra region specifically, SoftShadow improves PSNR by 0.35 dB and reduces MAE by 1.51 over HomoFormer. For zero-shot generalizability on LRSS and UIUC, SoftShadow leads all methods by 1.19 dB (LRSS) and 1.29 dB (UIUC) in PSNR over the next-best baselines (BCDiff and G2R-ShadowNet). The mask sensitivity analysis (Table 4) further shows that SoftShadow's performance is invariant to mask quality, whereas ShadowDiffusion and HomoFormer degrade by up to 7.2 dB when using pretrained SAM masks instead of GT masks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SAM (Segment Anything Model) architecture, adapting it via LoRA to serve as a soft shadow mask predictor rather than a binary segmentation tool, and pairs it with ShadowDiffusion as the removal backbone. It departs from the dominant paradigm of using externally generated binary (hard) shadow masks—produced by detectors like DHAN or FDRNet—by introducing continuous grayscale soft masks that explicitly encode penumbra intensity. The penumbra formation constraint loss, inspired by the physical geometry of shadow formation (umbra/penumbra/lit regions), is a novel regularization not present in prior shadow removal or detection work, and the joint end-to-end training of detector and remover without external mask inputs distinguishes it from both mask-guided and mask-free baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The soft mask formulation addresses a concrete and visually apparent failure mode of binary-mask-guided methods—boundary artifacts in penumbra regions—offering a physically grounded alternative that practitioners can adopt with existing shadow removal backbones. The mask sensitivity analysis (Table 4) is practically useful, showing that methods relying on external detectors degrade significantly with imperfect masks while SoftShadow remains stable. Open questions include whether the simplified degradation model (Eq. 1) generalizes to complex multi-shadow or self-shadow scenes, whether the penumbra constraint scales to higher resolutions, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D shadow removal where temporal/spatial consistency of soft masks becomes critical.
