## Problem

Current multimodal LLMs lack the long-context understanding capacity needed for multi-page document comprehension, and existing retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) workarounds introduce fragmented retrieval contexts, multi-stage error accumulation, and additional inference latency. This gap is compounded by the scarcity of high-quality, document-level QA datasets with genuine cross-page dependencies, making it difficult to train native models that handle long documents without external retrieval.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250906/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts documents in two interchangeable formats—interleaved text-image sequences (extracted via MinerU) or rendered page images (one image per page)—along with a natural-language question, and produces a free-form text answer. Documents can span up to 32K tokens with a maximum of 24 image tiles, and the training data averages 11,245 text tokens and 6,178 image tokens per sample, supporting both single-turn and multi-turn (87K multi-turn questions) interactions.

## Method

Docopilot is built on the InternVL2 ViT-MLP-LLM architecture and fine-tuned via supervised fine-tuning on Doc-750K (758K QA pairs from Sci-Hub, Arxiv, and OpenReview, with 31.6% real QA pairs and 9 proxy tasks) mixed with complementary open-source datasets. The key training innovations are: (1) a multimodal data-packing strategy that uses a priority queue to concatenate variable-length samples up to image and token thresholds, minimizing padding waste; (2) Ring Attention to partition long sequences across devices and enable 32K-token context without exceeding GPU memory; and (3) Liger Kernel for fused, in-place operations that reduce memory consumption. Unlike RAG-based approaches, Docopilot processes the full document natively, directly modeling cross-page dependencies without any retrieval stage, achieving the same inference latency as the base model while substantially improving accuracy on multi-page and long-context benchmarks.

## Results

Docopilot-8B achieves 61.8 overall accuracy on MM-NIAH, surpassing InternVL2-8B (42.9) by 19.9 points and InternVL2-26B (52.8) while using less than 31% of the inference latency. On MMLongbench-Doc, Docopilot-8B reaches 28.8% accuracy (F1: 23.0), outperforming Gemini-1.5-Pro (28.2% Acc) and all open-source baselines, though trailing GPT-4o (42.8% Acc). On DocGenome multi-page QA, Docopilot-8B achieves 51.9% MP accuracy, a 12.6-point improvement over InternVL2-8B (46.1%). Docopilot-2B achieves 49.2% on MM-NIAH overall with fewer than 10% of the parameters of InternVL2-26B, demonstrating strong parameter efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Docopilot-8B outperforms InternVL2-8B by 19.9 points on MM-NIAH (61.8 vs. 42.9) and by 12.6 points on DocGenome MP Acc (51.9 vs. 46.1), while matching its inference latency (81.0 ms). Against the larger InternVL2-26B, Docopilot-8B gains 9.0 points on MM-NIAH (61.8 vs. 52.8) with less than 31% of the latency. Compared to RAG-based methods, Docopilot-8B surpasses InternVL2-8B+RAG by 13.4 points on MM-NIAH (61.8 vs. 48.4) and VisRAG-8B by 26.0 points (61.8 vs. 35.8), while also eliminating the extra retrieval latency. On MMLongbench-Doc, Docopilot-8B (28.8% Acc) edges out Gemini-1.5-Pro (28.2%) but remains well below GPT-4o (42.8%), indicating proprietary models still lead on this benchmark. Docopilot-2B achieves 49.2% on MM-NIAH, outperforming InternVL2-2B (26.4%) by 110% and even surpassing InternVL2-2B+RAG (33.8%) without any retrieval overhead. On single-page VQA (DocVQA, ChartQA, InfoVQA), Docopilot-8B matches or slightly exceeds InternVL2-8B, confirming that long-context training does not degrade short-document performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the InternVL2 ViT-MLP-LLM architecture and extends it to document-level understanding by introducing a large-scale training corpus (Doc-750K) and engineering optimizations (Ring Attention, multimodal data packing, Liger Kernel) that enable 32K-token context processing. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant RAG-based paradigm for long-document QA (VisRAG, M3DocRAG, InternVL+RAG) by arguing that a natively trained long-context MLLM can handle cross-page dependencies without external retrieval, thereby avoiding fragmented contexts and multi-stage error accumulation. The dataset construction pipeline, which leverages MinerU for content extraction and GPT-4o for QA generation, positions Doc-750K as a higher-quality alternative to existing document datasets (Docmatix, DUDE) that rely on synthetic or single-page questions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release of Doc-750K (758K QA pairs, 5.2B tokens, 3.1M images) fills a critical gap in high-quality, multi-page document-level training data, and the demonstrated superiority of a native long-context model over RAG methods offers a practical, lower-latency alternative for real-time document QA systems. The finding that a 2B model can approach the performance of a 26B model on document tasks suggests that targeted document-level training data may be more effective than raw parameter scaling for this domain. Open questions include whether the 32K context window is sufficient for very long documents (e.g., 100+ page reports), how the approach generalizes to non-academic document types, and whether the engineering techniques (Ring Attention, data packing) scale efficiently to even longer contexts without prohibitive compute costs.
