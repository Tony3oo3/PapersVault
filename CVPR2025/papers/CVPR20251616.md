## Problem

Concept-based models (CBMs) trained on a single visual domain suffer significant OOD performance degradation because domain-specific concepts (e.g., 'red color', 'waxy texture') exhibit large distributional shifts across domains, biasing the final classifier toward domain-dependent features. Existing remedies—manual concept filtering, multi-domain training, or black-box domain generalization—either scale poorly, require exhaustive domain coverage, or sacrifice the interpretability that makes CBMs valuable. The paper asks whether domain shifts can be identified and neutralized automatically at the concept level using language.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image, encodes it with a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 image encoder, and computes concept activations as cosine similarities against a fixed set of concept text embeddings (human-written, ConceptNet-derived, or LLM-generated). The output is a class label from a learnable linear layer W_F over the concept activation vector, plus the interpretable concept activations themselves. During training, the DDO regularizer additionally consumes 200 LLM-generated domain descriptor phrases (short phrases like 'clipart illustrations', '3D renders') pre-encoded by the CLIP text encoder, but these are independent of the current training sample and act purely as a regularization signal on W_F.

## Method

The key insight is that CLIP's shared embedding space aligns visual domain shifts with textual descriptions of those shifts: the difference between text embeddings of 'A sketch of an apple' and 'A photo of an apple' approximates the visual domain gap. Building on this, the paper introduces a Domain Descriptor Orthogonality (DDO) loss that decomposes concept activations into domain-specific and domain-shared components, then penalizes the final linear classifier W_F for having non-zero projection onto the simulated domain-specific activations (Eq. 13: L_DDO = E[|W_F · a_sp|]). Domain-specific activations are synthesized by computing the dot product between concept embeddings and the language-guided domain shift vector for each (domain descriptor, class) pair. The DDO loss is a plug-in regularizer added to the standard cross-entropy objective with a fixed weight λ=1, requiring no architectural changes or additional training data. Domain descriptors are generated by prompting GPT-3.5-turbo to list 200 possible visual domains, making the approach scalable to unseen domains without manual annotation.

## Results

On four single-unseen-domain benchmarks, the DDO regularizer consistently improves OOD accuracy by an average of ~3 points across all three CBM baselines while preserving or slightly improving ID accuracy. The strongest gains appear on the newly introduced benchmarks: LaBO+DDO improves OOD accuracy by 5.4 points on LADA-Sculpture (80.00 vs. 74.56) and 4.8 points on LADV-3D (68.01 vs. 63.17). On the standard CUB-Painting benchmark, LaBO+DDO achieves 59.60% OOD accuracy, surpassing the black-box CLIP-ZS (52.77%) and approaching CLIP-LP (61.40%) while retaining full concept-level interpretability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the black-box CLIP-ZS baseline, LaBO+DDO wins on OOD accuracy for CUB-Painting (59.60 vs. 52.77) and LADV-3D (68.01 vs. 66.29) but trails on AwA2-clipart (87.66 vs. 90.26) and LADA-Sculpture (80.00 vs. 82.05), indicating that zero-shot CLIP still has an edge on some style-shift tasks. Compared to CLIP-LP, LaBO+DDO surpasses it on three of four OOD benchmarks (AwA2-clipart 87.66 vs. 86.75, LADA-Sculpture 80.00 vs. 74.40, LADV-3D 68.01 vs. 63.81) while falling slightly short on CUB-Painting (59.60 vs. 61.40). Against the three CBM baselines (CLIP-CBM, PCBM, LaBO), DDO consistently improves OOD accuracy by 2–5.4 points across all benchmarks without degrading ID accuracy, confirming that the plug-in regularizer is model-agnostic. The largest relative gains occur on the new 2D→3D benchmarks (LADA-Sculpture, LADV-3D), where domain-specific texture concepts cause the most severe distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends CLIP-based Concept Bottleneck Models (CBMs) such as LaBO and PCBM by introducing a language-guided regularization that explicitly targets domain-specific concepts. It departs from standard domain generalization methods (which operate on opaque feature spaces) by providing a concept-level, interpretable mechanism for erasing domain bias, and from prior CBM work by showing that CLIP's vision-language alignment can be repurposed to synthesize domain shifts purely in text space. The plug-in DDO loss is agnostic to the CBM architecture, positioning it as an orthogonal add-on to existing concept-based pipelines rather than a replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-in DDO regularizer offers a practical, architecture-agnostic way to improve OOD generalization of interpretable models without additional training data or model modifications, which is valuable for high-stakes applications where both interpretability and robustness are required. The three new benchmarks (photo→clipart, real→sculpture, real→3D-render) fill a gap in the domain generalization literature, which has been dominated by artistic style shifts. However, the ~3-point OOD gains are modest, 2D→3D generalization remains severely limited across all methods, and the approach is currently restricted to CBMs with a linear head, leaving open whether the DDO principle transfers to deeper or non-linear concept-based architectures.
