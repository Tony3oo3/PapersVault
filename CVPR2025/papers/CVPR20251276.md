## Problem

LoRA-based fine-tuning of Multimodal Large Language Models introduces substantial harmful redundant parameters that simultaneously erode pretrained general knowledge and fail to fully serve the downstream task. Existing post-pruning approaches (e.g., Model Tailor) risk degrading downstream performance, and regularization methods do not address the underlying redundancy or the optimization conflict between task-specific updates and critical pretrained weights.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251276/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images paired with text prompts for VQA (IconQA) or images alone for captioning (COCO-Caption), producing text answers or captions via the LLaVA-1.5 framework. Training uses 10k randomly sampled examples per dataset with LoRA applied to all LLM layers (ranks 16/32/64) and full fine-tuning on the connector. Evaluation measures both downstream task accuracy (Target) and general knowledge retention (Source) across four upstream benchmarks (OKVQA, OCRVQA, GQA, TextVQA) to quantify the forgetting trade-off.

## Method

LoRASculpt introduces two synergistic components into LoRA training. First, Sparsifying for Redundancy Reduction (SRR) applies magnitude-based one-shot pruning to the low-rank matrices A and B after a warm-up phase, with formal theorems proving that the product BA retains sparsity with high probability under low rank and high sparsity conditions (resolving the 'LoRA Sparsity Dilemma'). Second, Regularizing for Knowledge Harmonization (RKH) constructs a Magnitude-Guided Retention Mask from pretrained weights (via log-compression and tanh rescaling) and applies a Conflict Mitigation Regularizer — the Frobenius norm of the Hadamard product between this mask and BA — to steer LoRA updates away from critical pretrained parameter regions. For the MLLM connector, the method substitutes the Frobenius norm with an L1 norm to encourage soft sparsity rather than hard pruning, preventing performance degradation in this parameter-scarce module.

## Results

At LoRA rank=32 on IconQA, LoRASculpt achieves Source=56.10 and Target=85.26 (Avg=70.68), surpassing LoRA (Source=44.79, Target=82.52, Avg=63.65) by +11.31 on Source and +2.74 on Target. At rank=64, where LoRA suffers catastrophic forgetting (Source collapses to 5.14), LoRASculpt maintains Source=55.34 and Target=84.74 (Avg=70.04). On COCO-Caption at rank=32, LoRASculpt reaches Target=120.35 vs. LoRA's 110.27, while also improving Source from 49.86 to 54.25. The method consistently achieves the best Avg score across all three ranks (16, 32, 64) and both tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoRASculpt outperforms all six baselines on both Source and Target metrics at rank=32 across both tasks. Against LoRA, it gains +11.31 Source and +2.74 Target on IconQA, and +4.39 Source and +10.08 Target on COCO-Caption. Model Tailor is the closest competitor on Source (50.97 vs. 56.10 on IconQA), but LoRASculpt surpasses it on Target by +3.50 (85.26 vs. 81.76), confirming that post-pruning sacrifices downstream performance. L2-Reg and DARE degrade severely at rank=32 (Source drops to 22.65 and 43.71 respectively on IconQA), while LoRASculpt remains stable. At rank=64, the gap widens dramatically: LoRA and DoRA suffer catastrophic forgetting (Source ≈ 0–5), whereas LoRASculpt retains Source=55.34, demonstrating robustness to over-parameterization that no other baseline achieves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LoRA (Hu et al., ICLR 2022) by introducing structured sparsity directly into the training loop, departing from post-hoc pruning methods like Model Tailor and LoRA-Prune that risk degrading downstream performance. It also moves beyond generic regularization baselines (L2, OrthReg) by proposing a magnitude-guided retention mask that encodes pretrained knowledge importance into the loss, and provides theoretical guarantees (expected sparsity of the product of two sparse low-rank matrices) that prior LoRA-sparsity work lacked. The approach sits at the intersection of parameter-efficient fine-tuning and continual learning, but is specifically tailored to the MLLM setting where cross-modal architecture and connector modules introduce additional forgetting risks not captured by LLM-only analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that retaining only 1% of LoRA parameters can simultaneously improve both general and task-specific performance suggests that standard LoRA training wastes substantial capacity on harmful redundancy, a practical insight for anyone deploying MLLMs across multiple tasks. The discovery that catastrophic forgetting is largely driven by the connector module (far fewer parameters than the LLM) redirects attention to a component often overlooked in forgetting analyses. However, the evaluation is limited to a single architecture (LLaVA-1.5), two downstream tasks, and 10k training samples, leaving open whether the sparsity and regularization strategy generalizes to larger models, more diverse task distributions, or longer training regimes where the one-shot pruning assumption may break down.
