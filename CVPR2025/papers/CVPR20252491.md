## Problem

Existing text-guided inpainting models suffer from three interrelated failures: semantic misalignment (because training pairs whole-image captions with masks rather than mask-specific descriptions), structural distortion in generated objects, and style inconsistency between the inpainted region and the surrounding image. A key enabling gap is the absence of a large-scale dataset that aligns individual object masks with detailed content-and-style text descriptions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252491/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB image, a binary inpainting mask, and a text prompt, and outputs a fully inpainted image. It is trained to handle two mask regimes simultaneously: object-specific masks paired with detailed per-object descriptions (from MTADataset) and random masks paired with whole-image captions, unifying both within a single network. Training images are drawn from LAION with aesthetic score > 5.8 and resolution > 1024, each annotated with approximately 5 masks and their LLaVA-generated descriptions.

## Method

MTADiffusion adopts a dual-branch diffusion architecture (UNet + brush branch) where the brush branch replaces BrushNet's residual blocks with multi-resolution self-attention blocks to capture global style context, connected to the UNet via zero convolutions. A multi-task training strategy jointly optimizes noise prediction (inpainting) and Sobel-based edge-map prediction, with the edge branch extending the brush branch output by one channel to guide structural reconstruction. For style consistency, a VGG network (input channels expanded from 3 to 4 for latent space) extracts multi-layer style features from both the predicted and ground-truth denoised latents, and a Gram-matrix MSE loss enforces stylistic alignment. The final end-to-end loss combines all three terms (L = γ·L_noise + δ·L_style + η·L_structure with γ=1, δ=100, η=0.1), training only the attention branch and VGG extractor while keeping the VAE and UNet frozen.

## Results

On BrushBench, MTADiffusion achieves the best scores across all seven metrics: Image Reward 12.69×10, Aesthetic Score 6.50, PSNR 31.87, LPIPS 18.94×10⁻³, MSE 0.80×10⁻³, CLIP Similarity 26.52, and VQA Score 68.97×10⁻². On EditBench (random masks), it similarly leads with IR 4.82×10, AS 5.85, LPIPS 10.10×10⁻³, and CLIP Sim 29.12, though BrushNet marginally edges it on PSNR (33.66 vs 33.31) and MSE (0.63 vs 0.65). The user study confirms these gains, with MTADiffusion receiving 66% of votes for semantic alignment, 60% for structural stability, and 54% for style consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MTADiffusion outperforms all four baselines on BrushBench across every metric. Against the closest competitor BrushNet, gains are consistent but modest: +0.17 on IR, +0.13 on AS, +0.05 on PSNR, and +0.75 on VQA Score, reflecting incremental improvements from the mask-text aligned dataset and style-consistency loss. Against the older baselines (SDI, PowerPaint, CNI), the advantage is dramatic on reconstruction quality—PSNR jumps from 21.4–22.7 to 31.87 and LPIPS drops from 43–48 to 18.94—because those methods lack pixel-to-pixel mask conditioning. On EditBench (random masks), MTADiffusion leads on IR, AS, LPIPS, and CLIP Sim, but BrushNet retains a slight edge on PSNR (33.66 vs 33.31) and MSE (0.63 vs 0.65), suggesting the dual-branch architecture's pixel-level blending still gives BrushNet a small advantage in exact region preservation under random masking. The user study (30 participants, 1800 votes per question) strongly corroborates the quantitative results, with MTADiffusion receiving 66% of semantic-alignment votes versus 15% for BrushNet and 10% for PowerPaint.

## Relation to Prior Work

MTADiffusion extends the BrushNet dual-branch inpainting architecture (UNet + brush branch with zero-convolution connections) by replacing the brush branch's residual blocks with multi-resolution self-attention blocks for global style perception. Its most significant departure from prior inpainting work (SDI, SmartBrush, PowerPaint, BrushNet) is the training data: rather than pairing masks with whole-image captions or simple object labels, the MTAPipeline generates mask-specific text descriptions with style details via Grounded-SAM + LLaVA, directly addressing the semantic misalignment caused by caption-mask mismatch. The multi-task edge prediction objective and the VGG Gram-matrix style loss in latent space are borrowed from style-transfer and structural-control literature (ControlNet, PHDiffusion) and adapted to the inpainting setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MTADataset (5M images, ~25M mask-text pairs with detailed style annotations) is the paper's most broadly useful contribution, offering the community a resource that prior inpainting models lacked. However, the architectural and loss-function improvements over BrushNet are modest (e.g., CLIP Sim 26.52 vs. 26.32 on BrushBench), and the ablation in Table 4 reveals that LLaVA style captions barely outperform original LAION image captions (26.36 vs. 26.31), suggesting the dataset's value lies more in mask-region specificity than in style-description richness. Open questions include whether the latent-space VGG style loss is optimal compared to pixel-space alternatives, whether the approach transfers to video or 3D inpainting, and how caption-model errors (noted by the authors for color accuracy) propagate to generation quality at scale.
