## Problem

Existing RGB-D polyp segmentation methods require depth data at inference time due to complex feature fusion in the encoder, severely limiting their clinical deployment where depth sensors are unavailable. This paper addresses how to exploit the complementary spatial information in depth images during training while producing a model that operates on RGB images alone at inference, within a semi-supervised setting with limited labeled data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250517/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the framework consumes paired RGB and depth images (both resized to 320×320); depth maps are generated offline via the Depth Anything monocular depth estimation model and color-mapped to three channels. At inference, only the RGB image is fed to the main student network (Model_R1), producing a pixel-wise binary polyp segmentation mask via a Sigmoid output. No depth input or feature fusion is required at test time.

## Method

RD-Net extends the Mean Teacher framework with three UNet-ResNet34 networks: a teacher (Model_R2) and main student (Model_R1) both taking RGB input, plus an auxiliary student (Model_D) taking depth input. The core novelty is a depth-guided cross-modal mutual learning strategy with three components: (1) consistency learning aligning encoder features and predictions across modalities via MSE, (2) a contrastive loss using the Dice coefficient as a regional similarity measure to pull cross-modal positive pairs together and push negative pairs apart at the output level, and (3) an adaptive pseudo-label refinement where high-confidence predictions from Model_D replace corresponding teacher predictions when they are more confident. Additionally, a depth-guided patch augmentation (DPA) identifies difficult regions by computing local depth variance rather than model prediction entropy, then applies CutMix-style patch swapping on those regions to strengthen learning in challenging areas. The two student networks train independently with constraints only at the loss level, avoiding any architectural feature fusion between modalities.

## Results

On Kvasir-SEG with 10% labeled data, RD-Net achieves 89.07% Dice, 84.21% IoU, and 97.07% Acc, outperforming the best baseline (BCP) by 1.77% Dice. With only 2.5% labeled data, it reaches 86.35% Dice on Kvasir-SEG and 82.60% Dice on CVC-ClinicDB, surpassing the previous best (CML) by 3.14% and 5.00% Dice respectively. On the unseen ETIS-Larib dataset (generalization test), RD-Net achieves 71.47% Dice, a 28.17% improvement over supervised-only training and 5.18% over the best semi-supervised baseline (AD-MT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RD-Net outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across both label ratios and both primary datasets. At 2.5% labeled data, the margin is largest: RD-Net beats CML (the previous best) by 3.14% Dice on Kvasir-SEG and 5.00% Dice on CVC-ClinicDB, and surpasses PH-Net by 3.23% and 9.23% Dice respectively. At 10% labeled data, RD-Net leads BCP (the closest competitor) by 1.77% Dice on Kvasir-SEG and 2.25% Dice on CVC-ClinicDB, while also exceeding PH-Net and CML by similar margins. On the generalization test (Table 2, trained on Kvasir-SEG + CVC-ClinicDB, tested on unseen datasets), RD-Net achieves the highest Dice on all three held-out datasets, with the most dramatic gain on ETIS-Larib (71.47% vs. 66.29% for AD-MT, a 5.18% improvement) and CVC-ColonDB (74.29% vs. 71.46% for AD-MT). The supervised-only U-Net baseline is consistently the weakest semi-supervised competitor, confirming that the depth-guided mutual learning and patch augmentation strategies provide substantial gains over standard mean-teacher consistency training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mean Teacher semi-supervised framework by introducing an auxiliary depth-input student network alongside the standard RGB student-teacher pair, creating a three-network architecture. Rather than performing feature-level fusion of RGB and depth modalities (as in ACNet, CMX, or MaskMentor), it keeps the two modalities in separate networks and transfers complementary knowledge through output-level contrastive learning and consistency constraints. The depth-guided patch augmentation directly adapts PH-Net's adaptive patch augmentation mechanism, replacing model-prediction-based difficulty scoring with depth-variance-based scoring to avoid the failure mode where low-confidence predictions misidentify hard regions as easy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in decoupling depth from inference: by using monocular depth estimation (Depth Anything) to generate training-time depth maps, the method leverages geometric cues without requiring depth sensors in clinical deployment, a common barrier for RGB-D methods. The approach is validated across five polyp datasets with consistent gains, and the generalization experiments (training on Kvasir-SEG + CVC-ClinicDB, testing on three unseen datasets) strengthen the claim of robustness. Open questions include whether the depth-guided strategy transfers to other medical segmentation tasks (e.g., 3D volumetric data), whether real multi-sensor depth would yield further gains, and whether the monocular depth estimation errors introduce systematic biases in the augmentation or mutual learning signals.
