## Problem

Current text-to-video diffusion models rely on encoder-based text features (e.g., T5, CLIP) that provide limited text comprehension, struggling with precise understanding of quantities, spatial relationships, and temporal sequences in prompts. While decoder-only LLMs offer superior text understanding and reasoning, their feature distributions are fundamentally misaligned with encoder-based text features, causing training collapse when naively integrated into established video diffusion architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251467/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text prompt as input and generates a video clip. The prompt is processed in parallel by a T5 text encoder and a Phi-3.5 mini-instruct decoder-only LLM (which also receives four attribute-specific instruction prompts). The base diffusion backbone is CogVideoX-5B operating in latent space via a 3D causal VAE with temporal compression ratio 4 and spatial ratio 8, trained on 500,000 high-quality video clips.

## Method

Mimir introduces a Token Fuser that harmonizes heterogeneous encoder and decoder-only text tokens through two mechanisms. First, Non-Destructive Fusion applies a normalization layer with learnable scale to align feature magnitudes, then uses Zero-Convolution layers on both branches so that the decoder-only contribution starts at zero and the encoder contribution starts at its original value, enabling gradual, non-destructive integration during training. Second, a Semantic Stabilizer concatenates four learnable tokens (added to instruction tokens from the LLM) to the fused sequence, actively constraining the feature fluctuations inherent in next-token prediction (e.g., varying descriptions of the same object) while preserving the LLM's reasoning capacity. The final fused text tokens are concatenated with video tokens and fed into the diffusion transformer, trained end-to-end with the standard denoising objective.

## Results

On VBench, Mimir achieves the best score across all eight evaluated metrics, with particularly strong gains in Multiple Objects (85.29% vs. 65.70% for CogVideoX-5B) and Spatial Relationship (78.67% vs. 64.86%), demonstrating superior text comprehension for complex prompts. In a blind user study with 10 participants, Mimir is preferred 82.00% of the time for Instruction Following, 83.65% for Physics Simulation, and 89.65% for Visual Quality, substantially outperforming the next-best method CogVideoX-5B (72.15%, 57.30%, 63.25% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mimir outperforms all four baselines on every VBench metric. The most dramatic gains are over CogVideoX-5B (the base model Mimir extends): Multiple Objects improves by ~19.6 points (85.29% vs. 65.70%) and Spatial Relationship by ~13.8 points (78.67% vs. 64.86%), directly reflecting the LLM's superior handling of quantities and spatial descriptions. Against OpenSora, Mimir leads on Aesthetic Quality (+4.35 pts), Multiple Objects (+20.48 pts), and Spatial Relationship (+2.04 pts), while OpenSora retains a slight edge on Imaging Quality (63.38% vs. 63.91% — actually Mimir still wins by 0.53 pts). OpenSoraPlan is the weakest competitor, trailing Mimir by over 25 points on Multiple Objects and 40 points on Spatial Relationship. In the user study, Mimir dominates CogVideoX-5B by 9.85 points on Instruction Following, 26.35 points on Physics Simulation, and 26.40 points on Visual Quality, confirming that the token fuser's integration of Phi-3.5 decoder-only tokens translates into perceptually meaningful improvements beyond what the T5 encoder alone can provide.

## Relation to Prior Work

Mimir extends the latent diffusion transformer paradigm (Rombach et al., CogVideoX) by introducing a dual-branch text conditioning mechanism that combines a ViT-style encoder (T5) with a decoder-only LLM (Phi-3.5), bridged by a Token Fuser comprising Zero-Conv layers and a Semantic Stabilizer. This departs from prior LLM-for-diffusion work (LiDiT, SANA, ParaDiffusion, LaViBridge) that either fine-tunes decoder-only models into encoders or uses simple adapters for image generation, arguing that such strategies constrain the LLM's next-token-prediction reasoning. The paper positions itself as the first to integrate a decoder-only LLM into a video diffusion framework, addressing the feature distribution gap that makes direct use of LLM embeddings in established T2V models infeasible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Token Fuser's Zero-Conv mechanism offers a lightweight, generalizable recipe for integrating heterogeneous text representations into pre-trained diffusion models without destroying learned video priors, a technique likely transferable to other multimodal generation settings. However, the evaluation is limited to 500K training clips and VBench metrics, and the user study (10 participants, 20 prompts) provides only modest statistical confidence. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger LLMs or longer video durations, whether the Semantic Stabilizer's four instruction tokens generalize beyond the tested attribute categories, and whether the computational overhead of running both encoder and decoder-only branches is acceptable at production scale.
