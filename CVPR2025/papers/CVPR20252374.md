## Problem

Existing RGBE tracking methods rely on overly complex cross-modal fusion (e.g., FENet uses 8 self-attention and 4 cross-domain attention operations), inflating computational cost and training difficulty, while simultaneously neglecting historical temporal information that is critical for capturing target appearance changes and motion trends. The paper asks whether Mamba's linear-complexity, long-range sequence modeling can simultaneously simplify multimodal fusion and enable effective historical cue propagation in a unified framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252374/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tracker receives a single RGB template image plus multiple RGB search frames and their corresponding event template and search frames, all processed through a shared-weight dual-branch encoder with 4×4 patch embedding and gradual downsampling (8 MLP layers + 2 merging layers) to produce 512-dim tokens. The historical decoder additionally consumes N=4 learnable autoregressive queries that encode prior-frame target states. Output is a center-based bounding box (center offset, size) and a classification score map, trained with focal loss (classification), GIoU loss (λ=2), and L1 loss (λ=5).

## Method

MamTrack inserts a Fusion Mamba (FM) module at the 12th, 15th, and 18th layers of a pretrained HiViT-Base encoder. FM contains two sub-modules: Target-Aware Scan (TAS), which applies bidirectional SSM over concatenated template+search tokens to model global template–search relevance, and Cross-Modality Scan (CMS), which uses modality-specific SiLU gating signals to adaptively weight RGB vs. event search features, suppressing the degraded modality. A separate Historical Decoder (3 layers) processes N learnable queries through a Mamba-based Historical State Aware (HSA) module that propagates hidden states autoregressively across frames, followed by a Feature-Sequence Attention (FSA) module where the temporal queries attend to the fused spatial features via 8-head multi-head attention. The final prediction multiplies the enriched spatial features element-wise with the temporal query output before feeding a center-based prediction head, achieving SOTA SR/PR on VisEvent (61.6/79.2), FELT (48.9/60.8), and FE108 (66.4/94.2) at 18.1 FPS.

## Results

On the FE108 benchmark, MamTrack achieves 66.4% SR and 94.2% PR, outperforming the previous best tracker ViPT (65.8% SR) by 0.6% in SR. On VisEvent, the method reaches 61.6% SR and 79.2% PR, surpassing AQATrack by 2.9% SR and SDSTrack by 1.9% SR. On the long-term FELT benchmark, MamTrack attains 48.9% SR and 60.8% PR. On RGB-T tracking, the method achieves 54.2% SR on LasHeR and 62.4% SR on RGBT234, both setting new state-of-the-art results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MamTrack outperforms all nine compared baselines across every benchmark. On FE108, it beats the strongest prior method ViPT [48] by 0.6% SR (66.4 vs 65.8) and 0.4% PR, and surpasses FENet [41] by 3.0% SR. On VisEvent, the method exceeds the Transformer-based temporal tracker AQATrack [36] by 2.9% SR and the multimodal SDSTrack [13] by 1.9% SR. On the long-term FELT benchmark, MamTrack's Mamba-based historical decoder provides a clear advantage over Transformer-based autoregressive schemes. For RGB-T tracking, MamTrack outperforms ViPT [48] by 1.7% SR on LasHeR (54.2 vs 52.5) and by 0.7% SR on RGBT234 (62.4 vs 61.7). The older CNN-based trackers (ATOM [5], DiMP [1], PrDiMP [6], MixFormer [4], OSTrack [27]) are all surpassed by a wide margin (7–14% SR) on FE108, confirming the benefit of the Mamba-based multimodal architecture over conventional fusion designs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two active research threads: RGBE multimodal tracking (which has relied on increasingly complex cross-attention fusion, e.g., FENet's 8 self-attention + 4 cross-domain attention operations) and temporal tracking with autoregressive queries (e.g., AQATrack, which uses Transformer-based sequential decoding). It departs from both by replacing the intricate attention-based fusion with a simple gate-based Mamba mechanism (Target-Aware Scan + Cross-Modality Scan) and by substituting Transformer autoregressive decoding with Mamba's linear-complexity SSM for historical state modeling. Architecturally, it follows AQATrack's gradual-downsampling encoder (HiViT-Base) and borrows the bidirectional SSM design from Vision Mamba, but is the first to integrate Mamba into the RGBE tracking pipeline for both fusion and temporal history.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in showing that Mamba's linear complexity can handle long-sequence historical modeling in tracking faster than Transformer autoregressive schemes (18.1 vs. 16.9 FPS at 3 decoder layers) while matching or exceeding accuracy, which could make long-term RGBE tracking more deployable. The generalization to RGB-T tracking (outperforming ViPT on both LasHeR and RGBT234) suggests the Mamba-based fusion and historical decoder are modality-agnostic. However, the authors acknowledge a key limitation: the method does not exploit event data's high temporal resolution, leaving a clear avenue for future work. The modest SOTA margins (1–3% SR) and the fact that the optimal history length is only 4 frames temper the broader impact.
