## Problem

Existing surrogate refinement methods for Vision Transformers are limited to modifying backward propagation (i.e., gradient information), leaving the potential of refining forward propagation unexplored. This paper investigates whether modifying internal features during the forward pass—specifically attention maps and token embeddings—can further improve adversarial transferability beyond what gradient-level refinements achieve.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250963/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean RGB image (224×224, from ILSVRC2012 validation set) and its ground-truth class label, and produces a single adversarial example with an L∞-bounded perturbation (ε=16). The adversarial example is crafted on a surrogate ViT over 10 MIM iterations and then evaluated for transferability against 13 unseen target models (7 ViTs and 6 CNNs), both with and without defenses. The method requires no retraining of the surrogate model and operates entirely at inference time via PyTorch hooks.

## Method

FPR comprises two complementary forward-pass modifications applied during adversarial example generation. Attention Map Diversification (AMD) multiplies selected attention maps by a random diversity matrix with elements drawn from U(1−d, 1+d) followed by row-wise softmax, introducing controlled randomness that simulates unknown target models and implicitly induces fine-grained gradient vanishing at the row level during backpropagation. Momentum Token Embedding (MTE) stabilizes token embeddings across iterations by maintaining a moving average: Ê_t = η·Ê_{t−1} + s·E_t, applied to the outputs of all Attention and MLP blocks, thereby reducing the impact of local-optimum noise in the surrogate's internal representations. AMD is categorized as 'external refinement' (introducing extraneous perturbation, applied selectively via an index set I), while MTE is 'internal refinement' (leveraging self-history, applied to all blocks), and their complementary nature yields stronger transferability than either alone. The overall optimization loop follows MIM's momentum gradient update, with FPR's modifications injected via forward-pass hooks before gradient computation.

## Results

On 1000 ILSVRC2012 validation images, FPR achieves an average attack success rate (ASR) of 84.3% across 13 target models (7 ViTs + 6 CNNs) when using ViT-B as the surrogate, surpassing the strongest backward-refinement baseline GNS (77.3%) by 7.0% and the vanilla MIM baseline (50.9%) by 33.4%. Against five defenses (AT, HGD, NRP, JPEG, Bit-depth reduction), FPR maintains a 70.8% average ASR on ViT-B, outperforming GNS (65.1%) by 5.7%. FPR also improves all four tested complementary transfer methods (SIA, BSR, VTM, GRA) by 11–24 percentage points when combined.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FPR consistently outperforms all four backward-propagation surrogate refinements across every surrogate model. Against the strongest baseline GNS [51], FPR gains 7.0% average ASR (ViT-B surrogate), 2.8% (CaiT-S), 9.4% (PiT-T), and 4.8% (DeiT-B); against TGR [45] the margins are 7.4%, 3.6%, 6.3%, and 3.0% respectively; against PNAPO [38] the gaps are even larger (15.5%, 23.7%, 8.0%, 13.8%). The vanilla MIM [4] baseline is surpassed by 33.4% on ViT-B and 18.7% on DeiT-B. In the compatibility experiments (Table 4), FPR is combined with four non-surrogate-refinement transfer methods: it boosts VTM [34] from 57.3% to 81.7% on CNNs (+24.4%), BSR [32] from 66.9% to 89.9% on CNNs (+23.0%), SIA [36] from 69.1% to 90.4% on CNNs (+21.3%), and GRA [49] from 68.3% to 85.3% on CNNs (+17.0%), confirming that forward-propagation refinement is orthogonal to and complementary with gradient-correction and input-augmentation strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the surrogate refinement line of work for adversarial transferability on ViTs by shifting the refinement target from backward propagation (as in PNAPO, TGR, GNS) to forward propagation. It builds on MIM as the base optimization loop and borrows the momentum distillation idea from the vision-language pretraining literature (Li et al., 2021) to design its Momentum Token Embedding component. Conceptually, AMD is positioned as a moderate alternative to the aggressive attention-map dropout used in PNAPO, while the theoretical analysis shows that AMD's forward modification implicitly reproduces the gradient-vanishing effect that PNAPO achieves by directly truncating attention gradients during backpropagation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FPR provides the first systematic framework for refining ViT surrogates during forward propagation, offering a complementary axis (external vs. internal refinement) to the backward-only approaches that dominate the literature. The 7% average improvement over the strongest backward refinement (GNS) across 13 target models and five defenses is practically meaningful for red-teaming pipelines. However, the method requires per-surrogate hyperparameter tuning (index set, diversity factor, scale/attenuation factors), and the evaluation is limited to ImageNet-1000 with four surrogate ViTs, leaving open whether the forward-propagation perspective generalizes to other architectures (e.g., Mamba-based or hybrid models) or to targeted attacks.
