## Problem

3D graphics editing by VLM systems lacks a comprehensive, quantitative benchmark: prior evaluations rely on narrow task-specific sample sets, expensive human judgment, or unreliable AI-as-judge paradigms, making cross-system comparison impossible. Additionally, how inference-time compute should be allocated between generation and verification in graphics editing pipelines remains unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252220/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The VLM system receives two rendered camera views (at least one comprehensive) of the start and goal scenes plus the Python script that generates the start scene, and produces an edited Blender Python script that reconstructs the goal scene. Evaluation is fully quantitative using photometric loss, negative CLIP similarity, and Chamfer distance computed across all manually selected camera views (≥3 per instance), eliminating any human or AI-judge involvement. The pipeline operates in a d×b generator-verifier tree (d=3, b=4 by default), where each iteration prunes suboptimal edits and propagates the winner forward.

## Method

BlenderGym introduces 245 handcrafted start-goal Blender scene pairs spanning five editing tasks (object placement, lighting, procedural material, blend shapes, procedural geometry) and evaluates VLM systems through a multi-agent generator-verifier pipeline adapted from BlenderAlchemy. The generator comprises a brainstormer that identifies visual differences and proposes edit instructions, and a code editor that implements them in Blender Python API/Infinigen code; the verifier performs iterative pairwise selection among rendered candidates. The key empirical contribution is demonstrating that scaling the verifier via a reselection parameter k (repeated shuffled pairwise selection followed by a final round) improves selection quality, that scaled open-source verifiers (InternVL2-8B) can outperform unscaled closed-source ones (GPT-4o, Claude-3.5-Sonnet), and that the optimal fraction of compute allocated to verification (VeriRatio) increases with total compute budget—shifting from generation-dominated to verification-dominated regimes.

## Results

On BlenderGym, even the strongest VLM (GPT-4o) achieves a photometric loss (PL) of 2.410×10⁻² and N-CLIP of 2.398×10⁻² on lighting, compared to human baselines of 1.239×10⁻² and 1.632×10⁻², revealing a substantial performance gap. On blend shape editing, GPT-4o scores PL 9.140×10⁻³, N-CLIP 20.47×10⁻³, and CD 0.904, while humans achieve PL 0.934×10⁻³, N-CLIP 9.12×10⁻³, and CD 0.399. Open-source models (e.g., InternVL2-8B, Qwen2-VL-7B) generally underperform closed-source models, and several open-source VLMs fail to produce executable code for >75% of geometry and material instances. The verifier scaling experiment shows that scaled InternVL2-8B outperforms unscaled GPT-4o and Claude-3.5-Sonnet on all three metrics for blend shape selection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o is the strongest VLM across most tasks, achieving the best PL on lighting (2.410×10⁻²) and material (3.653×10⁻²), and the best N-CLIP on blend shape (20.47×10⁻³) and geometry (8.561×10⁻²). However, it is outperformed by Gemini-1.5-Flash on placement PL (10.94 vs. 11.89) and CD (8.324 vs. 11.22), and by MiniCPM-V-2.6 on geometry PL (7.127 vs. 6.747) and N-CLIP (8.542 vs. 8.561). Claude-3.5-Sonnet trails GPT-4o on nearly all metrics, with particularly large gaps on placement N-CLIP (51.76 vs. 30.38) and geometry PL (10.81 vs. 6.747). Open-source models (Qwen2-VL, InternVL2, Phi-3.5) generally lag closed-source models by 20–50% on PL and N-CLIP, and several fail entirely on geometry/material due to code generation failures. The most striking comparison is against the human baseline: humans outperform the best VLM by roughly 5–10× on PL and 2–3× on N-CLIP across all five tasks, confirming that 3D graphics editing remains far from solved for VLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on BlenderAlchemy [20], which first demonstrated VLM-based 3D graphics editing through a generator-verifier multi-agent pipeline, and extends it from a single-method evaluation into a comprehensive, task-agnostic benchmark. It departs from prior graphics editing evaluations (SceneCraft, 3D-GPT, LLPlace) that relied on method-specific sample sets, human judgment, or AI-as-judge by introducing fixed start-goal scene pairs with quantitative image and 3D metrics (photometric loss, CLIP, Chamfer distance). The verifier scaling experiments connect to the inference-time compute scaling literature (Brown et al. [6], Snell et al. [34]) but shift the focus from scaling generation to scaling the verification step that prunes candidate edits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BlenderGym provides the first standardized, quantitative benchmark for evaluating VLM systems on 3D graphics editing, enabling cross-comparison that was previously impossible due to method-specific evaluation protocols. The finding that even SOTA closed-source VLMs (GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet) fall far short of human Blender users on tasks that are straightforward for domain experts underscores that graphics editing remains an open challenge. The verifier scaling result—that a scaled open-source model can outperform unscaled closed-source models as a verifier, and that optimal compute allocation shifts toward verification at higher budgets—opens a practical direction for improving VLM editing pipelines without retraining. Limitations include the modest scale (245 instances, 10 human annotators), restriction to Blender, and the verifier scaling evidence being drawn from only 16 blend-shape instances.
