## Problem

Monolithic MLLMs that integrate visual encoding and language decoding into a single LLM face a fundamental tension: continuous pre-training on visual data causes catastrophic forgetting of the pre-trained language knowledge, while native pre-training from scratch is prohibitively expensive and unstable. The shared architecture for joint vision-language modeling means that visual optimization inevitably degrades language capabilities, making it difficult to scale visual pre-training without undermining the model's core linguistic strengths.

## Input / Output

Mono-InternVL takes RGB images (up to 8 million pixels, i.e., 10,240 patches of 28×28) and text prompts as input, producing free-form text responses including captions, VQA answers, and detection/OCR outputs. Images are patchified directly by a lightweight patch-embedding module with stride 28 plus an MLP projection into the LLM's embedding space, with an additional thumbnail token providing global visual context. A dynamic high-resolution strategy selects the optimal resolution per image, and the model processes up to 6,400 image patches during instruction tuning to handle high-resolution scenarios.

## Method

Mono-InternVL embeds a set of visual FFN experts into a pre-trained LLM (InternLM2-1.8B) via a static multimodal mixture-of-experts (MMoE) routing: visual tokens are routed to visual experts while textual tokens use the original language FFNs, with visual experts initialized from the LLM's own FFNs to inherit pre-trained representations. The key insight is formulating visual pre-training as delta tuning—freezing the entire LLM's language parameters and optimizing only the patch embedding and visual experts—so that visual learning never corrupts language knowledge. Endogenous Visual Pre-training (EViP) is a progressive three-stage curriculum: concept learning on 922M noisy image-text pairs, semantic learning on 258M synthetic captions from InternVL-8B, and alignment learning on 143M task-specific data (captioning, detection, OCR) where multi-head attention layers are additionally unfrozen. After EViP, the full model undergoes supervised instruction tuning on 5M bilingual instructions, with the maximum patch count progressively increasing from 1,280 to 6,400 across stages.

## Results

Mono-InternVL-2B (1.8B activated parameters) achieves an average of 55.2 on 9 MLLM benchmarks and 70.1 on 8 VQA benchmarks, outperforming the modular baseline InternVL-1.5-2B by +0.8% on Avg_MM while using a smaller model. On OCRBench, Mono-InternVL scores 767, surpassing Emu3 (687) by +80 points and InternVL-1.5-2B (654) by +113 points. On MathVista, it reaches 45.7, exceeding InternVL-1.5-2B (41.1) and EVE-7B HD (34.2). Inference efficiency is substantially improved: first-token latency is reduced by up to 67% and throughput increased by up to 31% compared to InternVL-1.5-2B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the modular baseline InternVL-1.5-2B (same LLM backbone), Mono-InternVL-2B achieves +0.8% on Avg_MM (55.2 vs 54.4) and +113 points on OCRBench (767 vs 654), but trails on MMBench (65.5 vs 70.9) and InfoVQA (43.0 vs 55.4, a −12.4% gap attributed to shallower model depth). Versus the SOTA monolithic model Emu3 (8B params), Mono-InternVL wins on 9 of 12 benchmarks with only 1.8B activated parameters, including +80 on OCRBench and +15.4% average on VQA tasks. Compared to EVE-7B HD, Mono-InternVL gains +16.3 on Avg_MM and +15.5 on Avg_QA. On NLP benchmarks, Mono-InternVL retains most of InternLM2-Chat's language ability (MMLU 45.1 vs 47.1; CMMLU 44.0 vs 46.1), whereas EVE-7B drops to 43.9/33.4 and Chameleon-7B to 52.1/–, confirming that delta tuning effectively prevents catastrophic forgetting. In inference, Mono-InternVL reduces time-to-first-token by 59–67% and increases throughput by 14–31% over InternVL-1.5-2B across all tested input lengths.

## Relation to Prior Work

Mono-InternVL extends the monolithic MLLM line (Chameleon, EVE, Emu3, SOLO) by addressing the catastrophic forgetting problem that plagues continuous pre-training of LLMs for vision. Rather than training from scratch or fine-tuning the full LLM, it introduces a multimodal mixture-of-experts structure where visual experts are initialized from the LLM's own FFNs, enabling delta tuning that freezes the language backbone during visual pre-training. This design is conceptually aligned with modular MLLMs (Qwen-VL, InternVL-1.5) that keep visual parameters outside the LLM, but achieves the same separation within a single decoder. The progressive Endogenous Visual Pre-training (EViP) strategy draws on the coarse-to-fine data curriculum used in InternVL-1.5's pre-training pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a monolithic MLLM can match a leading modular MLLM (InternVL-1.5-2B) on average multimodal performance while cutting first-token latency by up to 67%, making the monolithic design practically competitive for deployment. The delta-tuning + MoE recipe provides a general, low-risk pathway for extending any pre-trained LLM to multimodality without degrading its language capabilities. Key limitations include inferior performance on high-resolution benchmarks (e.g., −12.4% on InfoVQA), attributed to the shallow 1.8B model depth, and the results are validated at a single small scale, leaving open whether the approach scales to 7B+ models. Future work could explore whether the MoE visual experts can be further scaled or whether the progressive pre-training recipe transfers to other base LLMs.
