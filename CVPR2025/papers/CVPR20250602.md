## Problem

Current video-language models rely on heavyweight pretrained image or video encoders (300M–1.4B parameters) to extract visual features before aligning them with a language model, creating substantial computational overhead especially for multi-frame video processing. While encoder-free approaches have emerged for image-language understanding (e.g., EVE, Fuyu-8B), no encoder-free video-language model existed that could directly process video inputs without a pretrained vision encoder while maintaining competitive accuracy on open-ended video question answering.

## Input / Output

The model takes 8 uniformly sampled frames from a video (max spatial dimension 448 pixels) and a text question as input, producing a free-form text answer. Each frame is divided into non-overlapping patches, and the resulting visual tokens are structured with <row> delimiter tokens to preserve 2D spatial layout before being prepended with a conditioned frame-level context token. The LLM backbone is Vicuna-7B, and a LanguageBind teacher model (ViT-L/14, 224×224 input) provides reference visual tokens for distillation during training.

## Method

The core contribution is the Spatio-Temporal Alignment Block (STAB), a 45M-parameter module that directly processes video patches without any pretrained encoder. STAB first applies Local Spatio-Temporal Encoding (LSTE) using stacked 3D convolutions (1×1×1, 3×1×1, 1×1×1) plus a 3×3×3 depthwise dynamic positional encoding to capture local spatio-temporal context. A Local Spatial Downsampling (LSD) step then uses learnable window-based attention over 2×2 spatial patches to halve spatial resolution. Two complementary aggregators follow: the Frame-wise Spatial Relationship Aggregator (FSRA) uses a frame-specific query to summarize each frame's spatial content, while the Global Spatio-Temporal Relationship Aggregator (GSTRA) uses a single global query over all tokens to capture video-wide context, which is then fused with frame summaries via a learned weighted combination. Training proceeds in three stages—initial alignment (STAB only, frozen LLM, 351K WebVid pairs), end-to-end visual-language integration (702K pairs), and instruction tuning (100K VideoChatGPT samples)—using a negative cosine similarity distillation loss against LanguageBind teacher tokens alongside standard cross-entropy on text generation.

## Results

Video-Panda achieves 64.7% accuracy (3.8 score) on MSVD-QA, 54.8% (3.4) on MSRVTT-QA, 42.9% (3.2) on TGIF-QA, and 40.0% (3.3) on ActivityNet-QA, using only 45M visual parameters. On fine-grained evaluation (ActivityNet-200), it scores 2.74 on correctness, 3.01 on context, and 2.26 on temporal understanding (scale 1–5). The visual component requires 105.5G FLOPs and 41 ms latency, approximately 4× faster than Video-ChatGPT (171 ms) and 3× faster than Video-LLaVA (125 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Video-ChatGPT (307M visual params, 100 frames), Video-Panda (45M, 8 frames) wins on MSRVTT-QA (54.8 vs 49.3), TGIF-QA (42.9 vs 40.7), ActivityNet-QA (40.0 vs 35.2), and MSVD-QA score (3.8 vs 3.3), while trailing by only 0.2% on MSVD-QA accuracy (64.7 vs 64.9). Against Video-LLaVA (425M), Video-Panda wins on TGIF-QA (42.9 vs 41.7) but trails on MSRVTT-QA (54.8 vs 58.3) and MSVD-QA accuracy (64.7 vs 64.8). On fine-grained metrics, Video-Panda outperforms Video-ChatGPT on correctness (2.74 vs 2.40), context (3.01 vs 2.62), and temporal understanding (2.26 vs 1.98), and beats Video-LLaVA* on all five dimensions. The encoder-free EVE baseline (extended to video) is consistently outperformed by 4–6% accuracy across all four benchmarks, confirming the importance of the video-specific STAB module. In terms of efficiency, Video-Panda uses 6.5× fewer visual parameters than Video-ChatGPT and 9.4× fewer than Video-LLaVA, with 41 ms inference latency versus 171 ms and 125 ms respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the encoder-free vision-language paradigm introduced by EVE (Diao et al., NeurIPS 2024) from images to video, introducing the Spatio-Temporal Alignment Block (STAB) to handle the temporal dimension without a pretrained video encoder. It departs from the dominant encoder-based video-language architecture (Video-ChatGPT, Video-LLaVA, VideoChat) that relies on 300M–1.4B parameter vision encoders plus alignment modules, replacing them with a 45M-parameter block combining 3D convolutions, learned attention-based downsampling, and separate frame-level and video-level aggregators. The dynamic positional encoding borrows from UniFormer (Li et al., ICLR 2022), and the distillation-based training recipe follows EVE's approach of aligning to a frozen teacher (here LanguageBind).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6.5× parameter reduction and 3–4× inference speedup for the visual component make video-language models substantially more deployable, particularly for longer videos or resource-constrained settings. However, the approach still depends on a pretrained teacher (LanguageBind) for distillation and evaluates on only 8 frames, leaving open whether the encoder-free paradigm scales to hour-long videos or can be trained without a teacher. The fine-grained evaluation also shows Video-Panda still trails the strongest encoder-based models (ST-LLM, VideoChat2) on context and consistency, suggesting the spatio-temporal modeling, while effective, has not yet fully closed the gap with heavyweight encoders on nuanced reasoning.
