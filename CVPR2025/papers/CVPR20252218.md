## Problem

Stereo video synthesis from monocular input suffers from two fundamental gaps: the absence of large-scale high-quality paired stereo video data for supervised training, and the difficulty of simultaneously preserving geometric consistency between the two views and temporal consistency across frames. Existing approaches that apply single-image or multi-view novel view synthesis techniques to video sequences produce flickering, jittering, and spatially inconsistent results, particularly in dynamic scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252218/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video sequence, a stereoscopic pose describing the target viewpoint, and an estimated depth map (e.g., from MiDaS) as input, and produces the corresponding target-view video as a stereo pair. Training operates on 8-frame clips at 1024×1024 resolution, where the left and right views serve as target and reference respectively for both training and inference. The DVG module internally renders occluded target views and refines occlusion masks using inter-frame optical flow before feeding them to the diffusion model.

## Method

SpatialDreamer builds on Stable Video Diffusion's latent denoising U-Net and introduces three key components. First, a Depth-based Video Generation (DVG) module performs forward-backward rendering with optical-flow-guided occlusion mask refinement to produce temporally consistent paired training videos without annotation. Second, RefinerNet—a spatial-only U-Net initialized from SD2.1—extracts reference-view features that are merged with the denoising U-Net's features via a spatial attention layer, preserving the pretrained feature space while enabling cross-view information transfer. Third, a consistency control module combines a stereo deviation strength metric (the L1 latent difference between views, injected as a positional embedding alongside the timestep) with a Temporal Interaction Learning (TIL) module that blends self-attention with cross-view attention over N_r adjacent reference frames to enforce temporal coherence. Training proceeds in two stages: individual-frame training with frozen temporal layers, followed by joint training of temporal layers and TIL on 8-frame video clips, supervised by a combined v-prediction loss and stereo-aware disparity loss.

## Results

On RealEstate10K (image-level, t=5), SpatialDreamer achieves SSIM 0.916, PSNR 32.26 dB, and LPIPS 0.038, outperforming the strongest baseline SinMPI (SSIM 0.898, PSNR 28.79, LPIPS 0.040) by a clear margin. On the video-level benchmark, the method attains FVD 67.09 and flow warping error E_warp 3.374×10⁻³, beating the next-best method Webui-depthmap (FVD 83.18, E_warp 3.486×10⁻³) and Apple's AVP 3D converter (FVD 99.92, E_warp 3.446×10⁻³) on both metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpatialDreamer outperforms all 10 image-level baselines on LPIPS at both t=5 and t=10, with the largest gaps over 3D-photography (0.038 vs 0.116) and P-NVS (0.038 vs 0.241). On SSIM and PSNR at t=5, the proposed method leads all baselines; the closest competitor is SinMPI (SSIM 0.898, PSNR 28.79) which trails by 0.018 SSIM and 3.47 dB PSNR. At t=10, MVSplat slightly edges out the proposed method on SSIM (0.863 vs 0.857) and PSNR (25.89 vs 24.86), but the proposed method still dominates on LPIPS (0.049 vs 0.132). For video synthesis, SpatialDreamer achieves the best FVD (67.09) and E_warp (3.374×10⁻³), beating Webui-depthmap by ~19% in FVD and AVP 3D converter by ~33% in FVD. The largest video-level gaps are against P-NVS (FVD 420.2) and MVSplat (FVD 1543), which struggle severely with temporal consistency in dynamic scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpatialDreamer sits at the intersection of two research lineages: depth-based novel view synthesis (3D Photography, AdaMPI, SinMPI) and video diffusion models (SVD, Video LDM). It inherits the forward-backward rendering paradigm from AdaMPI and Wang et al. for constructing training pairs, but extends it from single images to video sequences by incorporating optical-flow-guided occlusion mask refinement across frames. The RefinerNet architecture departs from the more common ControlNet-style conditioning by using a parallel U-Net with spatial attention to fuse reference-view features, while the TIL module and stereo deviation strength metric are novel consistency-control mechanisms not present in prior NVS or video diffusion work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: generating temporally consistent stereo video from monocular input for spatial computing applications (e.g., Apple Vision Pro), where existing NVS methods produce flickering and geometrically inconsistent results. The self-supervised DVG pipeline eliminates the need for expensive paired stereo video capture, making the approach broadly applicable. Key limitations include dependence on depth map quality (MiDaS, DepthAnything, etc.) and the large model size that precludes real-time inference. Open questions include whether implicit depth estimation can replace explicit depth maps, whether the approach generalizes to larger camera baselines or more complex dynamic scenes, and whether the two-stage training strategy can be unified.
