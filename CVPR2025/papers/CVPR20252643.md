## Problem

Despite synthetic data achieving state-of-the-art results for face recognition and body pose estimation, 3D hand pose estimation still exhibits a large synthetic-to-real gap that has not been systematically decomposed. Existing synthetic hand datasets differ from real data across multiple confounded dimensions (pose, background, texture, occlusion), making it impossible to isolate which specific components drive the performance gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252643/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The synthesis pipeline takes NIMBLE hand model parameters (pose θ, shape β, texture α), HDRI background scenes, and optionally segmented arm/object crops from real images, then renders 4K-resolution RGB images via Blender Cycles ray-tracing (~1s per image on an RTX A5000). The downstream estimation models consume single RGB images and output 3D hand joint positions and mesh vertices, evaluated via PA-MPJPE and PA-MPVPE after Procrustes alignment. A key design choice is that the pipeline supports controlled decomposition: arms and interacting objects can be independently added, removed, or randomized to isolate each component's contribution.

## Method

The paper proposes a high-quality synthesis pipeline built on the NIMBLE hand model (5,990 vertices, 9,984 faces vs. MANO's 778/1,538) with 38 photo-realistic textures and 669 HDRI backgrounds, rendered via Blender Cycles. The core analytical technique is a decomposition-and-composition strategy: arms and interacting objects are segmented from real images using Grounding DINO for detection and SAM for masking, then composited into synthetic renders via element-wise mask blending (Eq. 2), enabling controlled ablation of each visual component. Amplitude spectrum augmentation (PASTA) is applied to close the frequency-domain gap between synthetic and real images, and a VAE-based object occlusion prior (encoder-decoder with KL divergence and 3D joint loss) refines predicted joints at high occlusion levels. Systematic ablations across forearm presence, frequency statistics, texture/background diversity, pose distribution coverage, occlusion levels, and skeleton topology (NIMBLE vs. MANO) reveal that the arm is critical for wrist localization, amplitude augmentation is essential for domain robustness, and only ~5% of real poses are needed to reach 80% of final performance.

## Results

On the FreiHAND test set, models trained solely on the paper's SynFrei synthetic data achieve 97%, 91%, 88%, 91%, and 84% of the performance of models trained on real FreiHAND for S²HAND, CMR, METRO, MeshGraphormer, and simpleHand respectively, with the synthetic-to-real gap being approximately 0.1 cm (e.g., S²HAND: 1.02 vs. 0.99 PA-MPJPE). On Dex-YCB, SynDex achieves 0.87 cm PA-MPJPE versus 0.86 cm for real Dex-YCB training. Cross-dataset evaluation (Table 6) shows that mixing real and synthetic data outperforms real-only training across all test sets, e.g., Dex-YCB+SynDex achieves 1.18 cm on MOW versus 1.24 cm for Dex-YCB alone.

| Method | Real FreiHAND | SynFrei (Ours) | Syn→Real Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| S²HAND | 0.99 / 1.02 | 1.02 / 1.05 | 97% |
| CMR | 0.77 / 0.78 | 0.85 / 0.88 | 91% |
| METRO | 0.69 / 0.71 | 0.78 / 0.79 | 88% |

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DARTset (800K samples, static backgrounds, no dynamic lighting), the paper's SynFrei (325K samples) dramatically outperforms: CMR trained on SynFrei achieves 0.85 cm PA-MPJPE versus DARTset's 2.56 cm, and METRO achieves 0.78 cm versus 1.77 cm (Table 3). Against RenderIH (1M samples, 1K HDR backgrounds), SynFrei achieves better cross-dataset generalization on HO3D-v2 at 1.41 cm versus 1.76 cm PA-MPJPE, attributed to RenderIH's low-resolution blurry backgrounds and limited hand-object interaction expressiveness. The paper also shows that mixing real and synthetic data consistently outperforms real-only training across all five cross-dataset test sets (FreiHAND, Dex-YCB, HO3D-v2, RHD, MOW), with the largest gains on MOW (1.18 vs. 1.24 cm) and HO3D-v2 (1.15 vs. 1.25 cm for Dex-YCB+SynDex). Notably, the synthetic-to-real gap is smallest for S²HAND (~0.03 cm) and largest for simpleHand (~0.12 cm), suggesting stronger backbones overfit more easily to synthetic data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two established lines of work: (1) the success of synthetic-only training in face recognition (Digiface-1M, Fake it till you make it) and body pose estimation (BEDLAM), where the synthetic-to-real gap has been effectively closed, and (2) the growing but fragmented literature on synthetic hand datasets (RHD, ObMan, DARTset, RenderIH, Re:InterHand) that have been used for pre-training but never systematically decomposed. Rather than proposing a new estimation architecture, the paper repurposes PASTA's amplitude spectrum augmentation from semantic segmentation into the hand pose domain and introduces a controllable NIMBLE-based rendering pipeline specifically designed to isolate and ablate individual image components (forearm, frequency statistics, pose distribution, object occlusion, skeleton topology). It departs from prior synthetic hand dataset work by treating the rendering pipeline as an experimental instrument rather than a data product, enabling causal attribution of the gap to specific visual components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: the open-source synthesis pipeline and the identified component checklist give practitioners a concrete recipe for closing the synthetic-to-real gap in hand pose estimation, potentially eliminating the need for expensive multi-camera annotation. The finding that only 5% of real hand poses yield 80% of final performance (and 40% yield 97%) has direct implications for data efficiency in future hand pose research. Open questions remain: whether the results generalize to two-hand estimation and different model architectures (stronger backbones showed worse synthetic-to-real transfer, suggesting overfitting), whether the object occlusion prior approach scales to open-world object categories beyond YCB, and whether the ~0.1 cm residual gap can be fully eliminated. The paper also highlights a practical but underappreciated issue—skeleton topology mismatches between synthetic and real datasets introducing measurable error—that the broader community should address in benchmarking.
