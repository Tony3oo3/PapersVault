## Problem

Stereo depth estimation degrades in scenes whose dynamic range exceeds the camera's native DR, producing over- or under-exposed regions that break correspondence. Existing auto-exposure control (AEC) methods adjust a single exposure per frame without expanding the camera's DR, while exposure bracketing methods rely on fixed, non-adaptive exposure settings. No prior approach combines adaptive exposure control with bracketing specifically for stereo disparity estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251029/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes four images per stereo pair: two left and two right frames captured at alternating dual exposures (e.g., 1440×928 resolution, 24-bit linear compressed to 8-bit). The ADEC module automatically selects the two exposure values per frame pair based on histogram statistics of the left-camera image. The output is a single disparity map for the first frame, with the second frame's contribution warped into the first frame's coordinate system via estimated optical flow.

## Method

The pipeline has two coupled components. First, the ADEC module computes per-frame histogram skewness and extreme-pixel ratios (L_i, H_i) to decide whether the scene DR exceeds the camera DR; if so, it diverges the dual exposures proportionally to the clipped-pixel fractions (capped at Δe=2.5), otherwise it converges them toward zero skewness. Second, for disparity estimation, features are extracted from each exposure frame using a pretrained RAFT-Stereo feature extractor, the second-frame features are warped to the first-frame view via RapidFlow optical flow, and the two feature maps are fused with intensity-based trapezoidal weight maps that up-weight well-exposed pixels. The fused features feed into a standard correlation-volume disparity estimator (RAFT-Stereo backbone), finetuned end-to-end on the CARLA synthetic dataset. Ablations confirm that feature-level fusion with motion compensation outperforms exposure-level or disparity-level fusion strategies.

## Results

On the synthetic CARLA dataset, ADEC achieves a disparity MAE of 1.3837 px, outperforming the best baseline NeuralAE (1.7198 px) by ~19%. On the real-world dataset, ADEC achieves a depth MAE of 1.6518 m, beating NeuralAE (1.7565 m) and the other baselines (2.67–2.71 m). In terms of speed, ADEC runs at 124.58 FPS for the exposure control module, supporting real-time use, while NeuralAE operates at only 0.25 FPS and GradientAE at 42.10 FPS. The method also demonstrates effective DR expansion up to 160% without significant depth accuracy degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADEC outperforms all three baselines on both disparity MAE (synthetic) and depth MAE (real-world). Against AverageAE [1] (ARM Mali-C71 ISP), ADEC reduces disparity MAE from 2.2958 to 1.3837 px (−40%) and depth MAE from 2.7089 to 1.6518 m (−39%), though AverageAE is much faster at 616 FPS. Against GradientAE [40] (Shim et al., IEEE TCSVT 2018), ADEC improves disparity MAE from 2.4680 to 1.3837 px (−44%) and depth MAE from 2.6677 to 1.6518 m (−38%), while also being ~3× faster (124.58 vs 42.10 FPS). Against NeuralAE [32] (Onzon et al., CVPR 2021), the closest competitor in accuracy, ADEC achieves a 19% lower disparity MAE (1.3837 vs 1.7198 px) and a 6% lower depth MAE (1.6518 vs 1.7565 m), while running 500× faster (124.58 vs 0.25 FPS), making it the only method that combines competitive accuracy with real-time exposure control.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes two well-studied ideas—auto-exposure control (AEC) and exposure bracketing—into a unified dual-exposure pipeline specifically designed for stereo depth estimation. Rather than building on a single prior architecture, it combines a pretrained RAFT-Stereo backbone with RapidFlow-based optical flow warping and a histogram-driven exposure controller (ADEC) that dynamically diverges or converges dual exposures based on scene DR. The key conceptual departure from prior HDR stereo work is that exposure settings are not predetermined but adapt frame-by-frame, and the fusion happens at the feature level with motion compensation rather than at the pixel or disparity level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For autonomous systems operating in real-world lighting (tunnels, high beams, dusk transitions), the ability to expand effective camera DR by 160% while maintaining >120 FPS makes this practically deployable in a way that prior neural AEC methods (0.25 FPS) are not. The introduced real-world and synthetic datasets with LiDAR ground truth fill a gap in evaluation resources for wide-DR stereo. Open questions include generalization to more than two exposure levels, performance in photon-starved conditions (fog, rain) mentioned only as future work, and whether the temporal dependency on consecutive frames degrades under high-speed motion or occlusion.
