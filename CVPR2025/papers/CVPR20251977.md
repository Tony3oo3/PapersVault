## Problem

Existing GCD methods rely on the global CLS token of DINO, creating an inherent trade-off: making similar fine-grained categories discriminable at the global level also differentiates their local semantics, blocking knowledge transfer, while enforcing generalization (e.g., via superclasses) blurs the fine-grained cues needed to separate novel from similar known classes. No prior method can simultaneously highlight discriminative parts for separation and share other parts for transfer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251977/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images processed by a frozen DINO ViT backbone, extracting both patch token features (N×C) and the CLS token. It produces T part feature vectors per image (each C-dimensional), which are pooled by averaging to form a single representation that replaces the CLS token for both classification and contrastive learning. No part masks, object masks, or additional annotations are required; the number of parts T is a fixed hyperparameter shared across all images.

## Method

APL introduces a set of T shared learnable part queries that are adapted per-image via cross-attention with DINO's multi-head attention-derived part priors, then patches are hard-assigned to queries using a Gumbel-Softmax approximation, yielding consistent and corresponding parts across images without any supervision. The key training signal is an all-min contrastive loss: for positive pairs, all T corresponding parts are pulled together, but for negative pairs only the single least-similar corresponding part is pushed apart, leaving other parts free to remain similar and thus shareable across categories. A diversity loss enforces orthogonality among part features within each image to prevent query collapse. The entire module is plug-and-play: it simply replaces the CLS token feature with the pooled part features in any DINO-based GCD framework (SimGCD, SPTNet, CMS) without modifying the backbone or classification head.

## Results

On the four fine-grained GCD benchmarks (CUB-200, Stanford Cars, FGVC-Aircraft, Herbarium-19), APL integrated into SimGCD achieves 64.5%, 60.1%, 56.6%, and 44.9% overall clustering accuracy, improving over SimGCD by +4.2, +6.3, +2.4, and +1.9 points respectively. When plugged into SPTNet, the best overall results reach 68.5% (CUB), 62.3% (Cars), 60.9% (Aircraft), and 47.3% (Herbarium-19), surpassing the prior SOTA SPTNet by +2.7, +3.3, +1.6, and +3.9 points. On generic datasets, APL+SimGCD attains 97.1% on CIFAR-10, 80.9% on CIFAR-100, and 83.2% on ImageNet-100, with novel-class accuracy of 98.2%, 79.5%, and 78.5% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

APL consistently outperforms all 15 baselines on the fine-grained GCD benchmarks. Against the three plug-in frameworks it is integrated into, APL yields the largest gains: +4.2–6.3% over SimGCD, +2.7–3.9% over SPTNet, and +2.6–6.0% over CMS† in overall accuracy, with novel-class improvements of up to +6.2% (Cars vs. SimGCD) and +5.5% (Cars vs. CMS†). Compared to the prior SOTA SPTNet, APL achieves the best overall accuracy on all four fine-grained datasets (68.5% CUB, 62.3% Cars, 60.9% Aircraft, 47.3% Herbarium-19). On the generic datasets (Table 2), APL+SimGCD surpasses InfoSieve by +2.3% (CIFAR-10) and +2.7% (ImageNet-100) in overall accuracy, and beats SelEx on CIFAR-10 (+1.2%) and ImageNet-100 (+0.1%), though SelEx retains a slight edge on CIFAR-100 overall (82.3% vs. 80.9%) while APL leads on novel-class accuracy (79.5% vs. 76.3%). Older baselines (k-means, RS+, UNO+, ORCA, GCD, GPC) are outperformed by 10–50+ points, reflecting the substantial progress of DINO-based GCD methods over clustering-only approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DINO self-supervised ViT backbone that underpins all modern GCD methods (SimGCD, SPTNet, CMS), but departs from their reliance on the global CLS token by introducing a part-level representation learned via shared learnable queries guided by DINO's multi-head attention as priors. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate lines of work: unsupervised object part discovery (ProtoPNet, DINO-based part segmentation) and GCD, unifying them through a plug-and-play module that replaces the CLS feature with pooled part features. The all-min contrastive loss is a novel departure from standard contrastive objectives, selectively constraining only the least-similar corresponding part in negative pairs rather than all parts, which is the key mechanism enabling simultaneous discriminability and generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design makes APL immediately applicable to any DINO-based GCD framework without architectural changes, which has practical value for the GCD community. However, the current integration simply averages part features into a single vector for classification, leaving open whether more explicit part-level supervision or attention-weighted aggregation could yield further gains. The approach is also limited to fine-grained settings where part-level semantics are meaningful; on generic datasets (CIFAR-10, ImageNet-100) improvements shrink to 0.7–3.4%, suggesting the part decomposition may be less informative when categories are visually distinct. An open question is whether the fixed number of shared queries T generalizes well across domains with varying part complexity, and whether the Gumbel-Softmax hard assignment introduces training instability at scale.
