## Problem

Existing egocentric video datasets are either large-scale but sparsely annotated (Ego4D, EPIC-KITCHENS) or richly annotated but collected in controlled or synthetic settings, leaving a gap for unscripted in-the-wild footage with dense, multi-tiered annotations including 3D grounding, nutrition, and gaze. No prior dataset simultaneously provides recipe steps, fine-grained action narrations with how/why, ingredient nutrition tracking, labelled 3D digital twins, object motion in 3D, and gaze priming on the same set of recordings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250908/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 41.3 hours of egocentric RGB video at 1408×1408 and 30 FPS, alongside 60 FPS eye-tracking, 30 FPS SLAM trajectories, and 7-channel audio from Project Aria glasses. The VQA benchmark consumes video clips ranging from single frames to over 7 hours plus a text question, producing a 5-way multiple-choice answer; action and sound recognition consume short video/audio clips and output verb/noun/action labels or one of 44 sound classes; the VOS benchmark takes initial object/hand masks and produces per-frame segmentation masks over 1000 sequences.

## Method

The core contribution is a multi-tiered annotation pipeline over unscripted 3-day kitchen recordings. Recipe steps are paired with 'prep' segments capturing all preparatory actions; narrations are parsed for verbs, nouns, hands, and how/why clauses; ingredients are weighed on-camera and tracked nutritionally via MyFitnessPal. A 3D digital twin of each kitchen is built in Blender from multi-video SLAM point clouds, with 413 labelled fixtures (cupboards, drawers, counters, appliances) that anchor object take/place events. Object movements are annotated as 2D bounding boxes at motion onset/end, lifted to 3D via dense depth estimation (Depth Anything) and RANSAC-fitted affine depth correction, then associated with the nearest fixture. Gaze priming is computed by finding when eye-gaze intersects an object's 3D location before pick-up or put-down. The VQA benchmark is constructed from 30 question prototypes sampling hard negatives directly from annotations (e.g., four different 'how' descriptions of the same action), yielding 26,650 questions across 7 categories without LLM-generated distractors.

## Results

On the HD-EPIC VQA benchmark (26,650 questions across 7 categories), the best model Gemini Pro (video-language) achieves only 37.6% average accuracy, compared to a 90.3% human baseline. Language-only models (Llama 3.2 at 26.5%, Gemini Pro blind at 26.7%) perform only ~6.7% above random, confirming the benchmark cannot be solved without visual input. For action recognition, the best model (TIM, audio+video) reaches 51.3% verb / 36.1% noun / 23.4% action accuracy, a large drop from its 77.1%/67.2%/57.5% on EPIC-KITCHENS-100. Sound recognition shows a similar gap: TIM drops from 58.3% Top-1 on EPIC-Sounds to 31.9% on HD-EPIC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gemini Pro (video-language) is the strongest model overall at 37.6% average VQA accuracy, benefiting most from external knowledge on Recipe (60.5%) and Ingredient (46.2%), but it still trails the human baseline by over 52 points. Among open-source video models, LLaVA-Video leads at 32.4% (strongest on Action at 43.0%), while LongVA (29.3%) and VideoLlama 2 (27.4%) are competitive but weaker; all three outperform their language-only counterparts by 5–11 points, confirming the need for visual input. Language-only models (Llama 3.2, Gemini Pro blind) cluster near random (26.5–26.7%), validating that the benchmark is not solvable by text priors alone. For action recognition, TIM (A+V) is best at 51.3% verb accuracy but drops ~26 points from its EPIC-KITCHENS-100 score, and all video-only models (SlowFast, Omnivore, MotionFormer, VideoMAE) fall below 50% on verbs. Sound recognition shows a uniform ~26-point degradation for all three audio models (SSAST, ASF, TIM) relative to EPIC-Sounds, indicating poor generalisation to new kitchens and devices. In long-term VOS, SAM2 outperforms Cutie on hands (J&F 89.1 vs 77.2) but underperforms on objects (27.0 vs 34.6), with both far above the static-mask baseline (9.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

HD-EPIC directly extends the EPIC-KITCHENS data collection protocol (3-day unscripted kitchen recordings) but departs fundamentally from all prior egocentric datasets by being the first to unify recipe steps, ingredient nutrition, fine-grained action narrations, gaze priming, audio events, object masks, and a 3D digital twin on the same in-the-wild footage. Where prior richly-annotated datasets (Assembly101, HOT3D, Aria Digital Twin) rely on controlled or scripted settings, and large-scale datasets (Ego4D, EPIC-KITCHENS-100) are sparsely annotated, HD-EPIC bridges this gap with 263 annotations per minute of unscripted video. The VQA benchmark extends prior video VQA efforts (EgoSchema, TemporalBench, MMBench-Video) by incorporating 3D-grounded spatial reasoning, multi-video recipe tracking, and gaze-based anticipation questions that no prior benchmark addresses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 52.7-point gap between the best model (Gemini Pro at 37.6%) and the human baseline (90.3%) on the VQA benchmark, combined with the finding that language-only models sit barely above random (26.5%), establishes a clear and challenging target for video understanding research. The action recognition drop from 77% (EPIC-KITCHENS-100) to 51% verb accuracy on HD-EPIC, and the ~26-point sound recognition gap, reveal that current models lack robustness to new kitchens, objects, and devices. Open questions include whether 3D grounding via digital twins can be automated at scale, how models can integrate gaze signals for anticipation, and whether the multi-hop object itinerary reasoning requires fundamentally new architectures. The dataset's zero-shot nature (new kitchens, new participants) makes it a true generalization test rather than a memorization benchmark.
