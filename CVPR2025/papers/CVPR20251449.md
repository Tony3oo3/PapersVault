## Problem

NeRF-based multi-view 3D object detectors suffer from two critical deficiencies: 2D image features lack the 3D positional information needed for precise object localization, and NeRF's scene-level rendering focus neglects object-level geometric details, producing imprecise opacity predictions that degrade detection accuracy. Prior methods like NeRF-Det address neither issue directly, leaving voxel representations noisy and geometry-awareness insufficient for reliable 3D detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251449/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes N multi-view RGB images along with their camera intrinsic and extrinsic parameters, processed through a pre-trained ResNet-50/101 backbone to extract 2D feature maps. Voxel centers are projected onto these feature maps at a 40×40×16 grid (substantially smaller than competing methods like CN-RMA's 192×192×80), and the network outputs 3D bounding boxes parameterized by center (x,y,z), dimensions (w,h,l), and orientation θ, plus per-voxel class predictions. Depth maps are optionally available for rendering supervision during training.

## Method

GO-N3RDet introduces three synergistic modules within an end-to-end NeRF-enhanced detection pipeline. The Positional information Embedded voxel Optimization Module (PEOM) predicts per-pixel offsets to dynamically adjust voxel projections, fuses multi-view features via max pooling (reducing noise vs. average pooling), and encodes the selected 3D coordinates into voxel features to inject explicit positional information. The Double Importance Sampling (DIS) module constructs a CDF from a weighted combination of NeRF-predicted density and voxel-grid proximity density, then inverse-samples to concentrate ray-marching points on foreground regions. The Opacity Optimization Module (OOM) enforces cross-view opacity consistency through a variance-minimization loss and computes a ray-distance-weighted average density to suppress cumulative errors from distant samples. The full network is trained end-to-end with a composite loss combining classification, localization, photometric, depth-rendering, and opacity-consistency terms, achieving SOTA mAP@0.25 of 58.6 on ScanNet with ~14× fewer training epochs than CN-RMA.

## Results

On ScanNet, GO-N3RDet-R101* achieves 58.6 mAP@0.25, surpassing the previous best NeRF-based method NeRF-Dets* (57.5) by 1.1 points and the foundational ImVoxelNet (48.4) by 10.2 points. On ARKITScenes, GO-N3RDet-R50 reaches 44.7 mAP@0.25 and 21.9 mAP@0.5, substantially outperforming NeRF-Det (26.7 / 14.4). Notably, GO-N3RDet matches CN-RMA's 58.6 mAP@0.25 on ScanNet while using only 14 training epochs versus CN-RMA's 192, and a grid size of [40,40,16] versus [192,192,80].

## Baselines & Comparisons

GO-N3RDet-R101* (58.6 mAP@0.25) outperforms all NeRF-based baselines on ScanNet: NeRF-Det-R101* (53.3, +5.3), NeRF-Det+++ (53.3, +5.3), NeRF-Dets* (57.5, +1.1), and MVSDet* (56.2, +2.4). It also surpasses the non-NeRF ImGeoNet (54.8, +3.8) and the foundational ImVoxelNet (48.4, +10.2). Against CN-RMA, GO-N3RDet achieves an identical 58.6 mAP@0.25 but with roughly 14× fewer training epochs (14 vs. 192) and a much smaller voxel grid, while avoiding CN-RMA's reliance on ground-truth TSDF supervision. On ARKITScenes, GO-N3RDet-R50 (44.7 mAP@0.25) exceeds NeRF-Det (26.7) by 18.0 points, though no other competing methods report results on this dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

GO-N3RDet extends the NeRF-Det lineage (which itself builds on ImVoxelNet's multi-view voxel projection) by addressing two identified gaps: the absence of 3D positional information in voxel features and the insufficiency of scene geometry perception in the NeRF branch. Where ImVoxelNet and NeRF-Det rely on average pooling to fuse multi-view features and uniform sampling along rays, this paper introduces PEOM (dynamic offset prediction + max pooling + positional encoding), Double Importance Sampling (combining NeRF density and voxel-proximity density for CDF-based resampling), and an Opacity Optimization Module enforcing cross-view consistency with ray-distance weighting. The method also positions itself against the non-NeRF ray-matching approach CN-RMA, arguing that comparable accuracy can be achieved without ground-truth TSDF supervision and with far fewer training epochs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the efficiency result: matching CN-RMA's 58.6 mAP@0.25 with roughly 14× fewer training epochs and a much coarser grid (40×40×16 vs. 192×192×80) makes NeRF-based multi-view detection substantially more accessible. The ablation revealing that NeRF-Det's opacity branch contributes only 0.2% mAP (vs. 3.4–4.3% for GO-N3RDet) suggests prior NeRF-based detectors were not effectively leveraging their geometry branch, an insight that may redirect future work. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to outdoor or large-scale scenes, whether the pre-trained 2D backbone can be replaced by a learned encoder, and whether the opacity consistency loss transfers to other NeRF-based tasks beyond detection.
