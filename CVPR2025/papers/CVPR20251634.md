## Problem

Existing 3D manipulation policies either train 3D encoders from scratch (constrained by the scarcity of large-scale robotic 3D data) or perform modality transformations such as projecting point clouds into multi-view images or lifting 2D features into 3D space, both of which inevitably lose spatial geometry. The paper asks whether a 3D policy can simultaneously leverage large-scale pretrained 2D knowledge and consume complete 3D point cloud input without such information loss.

## Input / Output

At inference, Lift3D takes a single-view point cloud of 1,024 points (derived from RGBD via camera intrinsics/extrinsics) concatenated with robot state (end-effector pose, joint positions, velocities) and outputs a 7-DoF end-effector pose: 3-DoF translation, 3-DoF rotation, and 1-DoF gripper open/close. The point cloud is first tokenized into 128 tokens of dimension 768 by a lightweight 3D tokenizer (FPS + kNN + linear layers), and the policy head is a simple 3-layer MLP. During Stage 1 pretraining, the input is a 224×224 masked image with depth as the reconstruction target; during Stage 2, the 2D foundation model is frozen and only the tokenizer, LoRA adapter, and policy head are updated.

## Method

Lift3D is a two-stage framework that progressively endows a 2D foundation model (DINOv2 or CLIP ViT-base) with 3D manipulation capability. Stage 1 (implicit 3D representation) introduces a task-aware MAE: CLIP attention maps extracted from task text prompts are back-projected onto the image to guide masking of task-relevant affordance patches, and the decoder reconstructs depth (rather than RGB) for masked tokens, with a distillation loss against the frozen pretrained model to prevent catastrophic forgetting. Stage 2 (explicit 3D representation) proposes a 2D model-lifting strategy: 3D point cloud tokens are projected onto 6 virtual cube faces to establish a positional correspondence with the 2D model's pretrained positional embeddings; the per-plane 2D PEs are averaged into a unified 3D PE that is added to the 3D tokens before feeding them into the frozen 2D transformer. This avoids creating new 3D PEs (which would introduce semantic mismatch with pretrained knowledge) and eliminates modality transformation, so the 2D model encodes point clouds directly using its original PEs while preserving large-scale pretrained spatial and semantic features.

## Results

On the MetaWorld benchmark (15 tasks), Lift3D(CLIP) achieves a mean success rate of 83.9%, outperforming the best 2D representation method (R3M, 75.1%) by 8.8 points and the previous SOTA 3D policy (DP3, 65.3%) by 18.6 points. On the Adroit dexterous hand benchmark (3 tasks), Lift3D(CLIP) reaches 88.0% mean success rate versus 66.7% for DP3. In real-world experiments across 10 tasks on a Franka Research 3 arm, Lift3D achieves 62.5% mean success rate, a 17-point improvement over DP3 (45.5%) and a 42-point improvement over VC-1 (20.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Lift3D(CLIP) dominates all 8 baselines on MetaWorld, achieving 83.9% mean success rate versus R3M (75.1%, best 2D rep.), SPA (69.5%, best 3D rep.), and DP3 (65.3%, best 3D policy). The gap is most pronounced on medium-difficulty tasks (78.8% vs. 77.4% for SPA) and very-hard tasks (42.0% vs. 18.0% for DP3), where 3D spatial reasoning is most critical. On Adroit dexterous hand tasks, Lift3D(CLIP) achieves 88.0% mean, surpassing DP3 (66.7%) by 21.3 points, with the largest margin on the pen task (64% vs. 12%). In real-world experiments, Lift3D leads all baselines on 8 out of 10 tasks, with the largest wins on Pick-and-Place (85% vs. 40% for DP3) and Place Bottle (90% vs. 50% for DP3). The only task where Lift3D ties a baseline is Open Drawer (60% for both Lift3D and DP3). Notably, Lift3D uses only a single-view point cloud and a simple 3-layer MLP policy head, while DP3 employs a diffusion-based decoder, suggesting the representation quality rather than the policy head drives the performance gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

Lift3D builds on the masked autoencoder framework (He et al., CVPR 2022) and the pretrained 2D foundation models CLIP and DINOv2, but departs from prior robotic MAE work (VC-1, 3D-MVP, MVP) by replacing random masking with CLIP-guided affordance masking and RGB reconstruction with depth reconstruction. Its most distinctive departure from the 3D manipulation literature (RVT, Act3D, DP3) is the 2D model-lifting strategy: rather than projecting point clouds into multi-view images or lifting 2D features into 3D space (both of which incur spatial information loss), Lift3D projects 3D tokens onto virtual planes solely to establish a positional correspondence with the 2D model's pretrained positional embeddings, then feeds the 3D tokens directly into the frozen 2D transformer. This avoids modality transformation entirely while preserving the large-scale pretrained knowledge of the 2D backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PE-lifting mechanism offers a computationally efficient and conceptually clean way to transfer 2D foundation model knowledge to 3D manipulation without the spatial degradation inherent in prior modality-transformation approaches, and the strong real-world results (62.5% vs. 45.5% for DP3 with only 30 training episodes) suggest practical viability. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that Lift3D cannot process language instructions, leaving open the question of whether the lifted 3D encoder can be integrated with a language model to form a 3D Vision-Language-Action system. The scalability experiments (ViT-base → large → giant yielding 28→58→68 on a very-hard task) hint that larger 2D backbones could further close the gap on the most challenging manipulation tasks, but the framework's dependence on accurate depth estimation and single-view point clouds remains a practical constraint in cluttered or occluded real-world scenes.
