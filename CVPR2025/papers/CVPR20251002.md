## Problem

Diffusion models can generate concrete out-of-distribution concepts like 'a blue banana' but fail to understand the abstract semantics of 'creative,' producing separate objects rather than cohesive combinatorial fusions (e.g., a lettuce-mantis hybrid). Existing creative generation methods (ConceptLab, BASS, MagicMix) require per-concept retraining or reference images, incurring high computational costs (40–120 s per image) that limit practical deployment.

## Input / Output

At inference, the user provides a natural-language prompt in which the word 'creative' is replaced by the learned <CreTok> token (e.g., 'a photo of a <CreTok> mixture that resembles both a lettuce and a mantis'), optionally combined with style descriptors such as 'watercolor painting' or 'in the style of Monet.' The output is a single RGB image generated by Stable Diffusion 3 in ~4 s with no additional training or reference images. During training, the method consumes text pairs (200 pairs from the CangJie dataset) and optimizes only the two CLIP text encoders (CLIP L/14 and OpenCLIP bigG/14) while keeping all diffusion-model parameters frozen.

## Method

CreTok redefines 'creative' as a universal trainable token <CreTok> by iteratively maximizing the cosine similarity between a restrictive prompt (e.g., 'a lettuce mantis') and an adaptive prompt (e.g., 'a photo of a <CreTok> mixture') in CLIP embedding space. A loss threshold θ = 0.5 caps the similarity to prevent overfitting to a single concept, and the order of the two concept names is alternated each step to enforce balanced fusion. Rather than converging on one pair, the training loop continuously samples fresh text pairs from the 200-pair CangJie dataset (10 K steps, batch size 1, gradient accumulation over 16 pairs, ~30 min on a single 4090 GPU), so <CreTok> absorbs a generalized 'how to be creative' representation instead of encoding any specific concept. At inference, <CreTok> plugs into any prompt as a universal adjective, enabling zero-shot combinatorial generation across unseen concept pairs and diverse artistic styles without further optimization.

## Results

On the CangJie TP2O benchmark, CreTok achieves the best VQAScore (0.835), PickScore (21.775), and ImageReward (1.065) among all compared methods, outperforming SD3.5 (0.805, 21.766, 0.881) and BASS (0.710, 20.799, 0.481). In GPT-4o creativity evaluation, CreTok scores highest on Integration (9.5±0.4), Originality (9.3±0.4), Aesthetics (9.6±0.3), and Composition (9.6±0.3). In a 50-participant user study, CreTok achieves an average rank of 1.9±1.1, substantially better than SD3.5 (3.1±1.1) and BASS (3.1±1.3). Generation time is 4 seconds per image, a 30× speedup over ConceptLab (120s) and 10× over BASS (40s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CreTok outperforms all quantitative baselines on every metric in Table 1: it beats SD3.5 by +0.030 VQAScore, +0.009 PickScore, and +0.184 ImageReward; it surpasses BASS by +0.125 VQAScore, +0.976 PickScore, and +0.584 ImageReward; and it leads Kandinsky 3 by +0.064 VQAScore and +0.431 ImageReward. In the GPT-4o creativity evaluation (Table 2), CreTok achieves the highest scores on Integration (9.5 vs. 9.1 for SD3.5), Originality (9.3 vs. 9.1), Aesthetics (9.6 vs. 9.4), and Composition (9.6 vs. 9.4), while matching SD3.5 on Alignment (9.9). In the user study, CreTok's average rank of 1.9 is roughly 1.2 positions better than the next-best methods (SD3.5 and BASS at 3.1). For visual-only comparisons, CreTok produces more coherent combinatorial fusions than MagicMix and Black-Scholes (which rely on reference images and degrade under large visual disparities), and more realistic integrated objects than DALL-E 3 and Midjourney (which tend toward artistic styles). On the CT2I multi-concept task, CreTok preserves individual concept features more effectively than ConceptLab, which struggles when combining three or more concepts.

## Relation to Prior Work

CreTok builds directly on ConceptLab's token-based concept fusion objective (maximizing cosine similarity between text embeddings) and the textual inversion paradigm of optimizing a single embedding token, but departs fundamentally by making the token universal rather than concept-specific. Where ConceptLab assigns a unique token per novel concept and BASS relies on rule-based search over candidate images, CreTok iteratively refines a single <CreTok> across diverse text pairs from the CangJie dataset, distilling a generalized 'how to be creative' representation rather than a 'what to generate' one. This positions the work at the intersection of personalization (textual inversion, DreamBooth) and creative generation (TP2O, CT2I), reframing the problem from per-instance synthesis to meta-creativity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that an abstract adjective like 'creative' can be redefined as a universal token analogous to 'blue' in 'a blue banana'—offers a potentially generalizable recipe for handling other ambiguous descriptors in T2I models, and the 30× speedup over ConceptLab makes combinatorial creativity practical for interactive applications. However, the evidence is somewhat narrow: training uses only 200 text pairs on a single base model (SD3), the CangJie dataset is small, and generalization to other abstract adjectives or different architectures remains untested. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more complex creative tasks (e.g., narrative coherence, multi-step reasoning) and whether the universal token degrades when the concept space becomes highly heterogeneous.
