## Problem

Local aggregation networks (LANets) for point cloud analysis have seen performance saturation over recent years, as increasingly sophisticated local representation designs yield diminishing returns. The field has largely ignored the alternative path proven successful in 2D vision—constructing much deeper networks—because existing LANet blocks are too computationally expensive to stack and very deep architectures are difficult to optimize on the complex geometric patterns of point clouds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250015/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw 3D point clouds (coordinates plus per-point features) without any intermediate voxelization or projection, using KNN grouping with K=24 to form local neighborhoods at each stage. For semantic segmentation (S3DIS, ScanNet v2) it outputs per-point class labels; for object classification (ScanObjectNN) a single scene-level label; and for part segmentation (ShapeNetPart) per-point part labels. All experiments are conducted on a single RTX 3090 (24 GB) without voting, pre-trained weights, or test-time augmentation.

## Method

DeepLA-Net is built from a lightweight ResLFE block that cuts FLOPs by ~10x through three mechanisms: (1) stage-level position embedding, where relative-position features are pre-computed once per stage and cached across all layers in that stage; (2) a vector feature representation (VFR) module that performs linear abstraction before grouping (front-linear), uses feature differences f_i - f_i^k instead of concatenation, and adds the position embedding via simple addition; and (3) a modernization structure with pre-norm, dropout, and a same-dimension FFN (no 4x inverted bottleneck). To make very deep training feasible, a Hybrid Deep Supervision (HDS) strategy attaches two auxiliary losses at every encoder stage—weighted cross-entropy on semantic predictions and MSE on spatial-distribution targets—whose weights decay exponentially with epoch number, guiding early convergence while fading out to avoid interfering with the primary segmentation loss. This combination enables networks of up to 120 ResLFE blocks (DeepLA-120) to be trained on a single GPU, achieving SOTA mIoU on S3DIS Area5 (75.7%), ScanNet test (77.2%), ScanObjectNN OA (90.6%), and ShapeNetPart cls.mIoU (86.5%).

## Results

On S3DIS Area5, DeepLA-120 achieves 75.7% mIoU, surpassing the 75% threshold for the first time and outperforming the previous SOTA KPConvX (73.5%) by +2.2%. On the 6-fold split, DeepLA-120 reaches 79.8% mIoU (+1.4% over prior best). On ScanNet v2 test, DeepLA-120 achieves 77.2% mIoU (+1.6% over OA-CNN's 75.6%). For object classification on ScanObjectNN, DeepLA-24 reaches 90.6% OA (+2.1%), and for part segmentation on ShapeNetPart, it achieves 86.5% Cls.mIoU (+1.0%), all without voting or test-time augmentation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On S3DIS Area5, DeepLA-120 (75.7%) decisively outperforms all prior methods, beating KPConvX (73.5%) by +2.2%, Point Transformer v3 (73.4%) by +2.3%, and PDNet/PointVector (72.3%) by +3.4%. Even the lightweight DeepLA-24 (73.2%) matches or exceeds most 2024 methods while using only 9.2G FLOPs versus 58.5G for PointVector-XL. On ScanNet v2 test, DeepLA-120 (77.2%) surpasses OA-CNN (75.6%) and ConDaFormer (75.5%) by +1.6% and +1.7% respectively. On ScanObjectNN, DeepLA-24 (90.6% OA) breaks the long-standing ~88% plateau, outperforming PDNet (88.5%), NLGAT (88.4%), and PointNeXt (88.1%) by 2.1–2.5% OA. On ShapeNetPart, DeepLA-24 (86.5% Cls.mIoU) leads Interpretable3D (85.6%) and PDNet (85.4%) by +0.9% and +1.1% respectively. The paper does not report losses to any baseline on any of the four benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on PointNet++ as the foundational local aggregation network (LANet), performing a step-by-step 'modernization' of its position embedding and feature representation phases into the lightweight ResLFE block. It deliberately departs from the dominant trend in point cloud research—designing increasingly sophisticated local representations (Point Transformer, PointVector, ConDaFormer, PointNeXt)—by drawing an analogy to 2D CNNs where residual connections and depth, rather than convolutional kernel complexity, drove the major performance gains. The hybrid deep supervision (HDS) strategy is a new training technique not previously applied to point cloud networks, combining semantic and spatial distribution losses with exponential decay to stabilize optimization in very deep architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the field's saturation around 73% mIoU on S3DIS Area5 was not a fundamental limit of local aggregation but a consequence of computational overhead preventing sufficient depth, and that a 10× FLOPs reduction in the local block unlocks a new regime where 120-block networks break the 75% threshold for the first time. Practically, the entire DeepLA-120 trains on a single RTX 3090, making very deep point cloud networks accessible without multi-GPU clusters. Open questions include whether further depth (beyond 120 blocks) continues to yield gains, whether the HDS strategy transfers to other modalities or self-supervised settings, and whether the depth-over-complexity finding generalizes to outdoor/large-scale scenes where local patterns may be more varied.
