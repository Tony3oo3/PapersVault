## Problem

Existing diffusion synchronization methods (e.g., SyncTweedies, MultiDiffusion) rely on naive heuristics like averaging intermediate signals to correlate multiple diffusion trajectories, without explaining why these heuristics work or when they fail. This lack of theoretical grounding forces practitioners to empirically test dozens of strategies per task (SyncTweedies tests 60), and a heuristic optimal for one task often produces suboptimal or blurry results when blindly applied to another.

## Input / Output

The method is training-free and operates on pretrained diffusion backbones (Stable Diffusion, DeepFloyd-IF, ControlNet, MDM) at their native resolutions. Depending on the task, it consumes one or more text prompts, an optional binary or soft mask, a source image (for editing), or a 3D mesh with depth maps, and produces a synchronized composite: a standard image, an edited image preserving background, a 2048×512 wide image from overlapping patches, an ambiguous image supporting multiple visual interpretations, a UV texture map from 10 synchronized views, or a long-horizon motion sequence from overlapping 120-frame segments. All trajectories share the same DDIM timestep schedule and classifier-free guidance scale.

## Method

SyncSDE formulates multi-trajectory diffusion synchronization as a conditional score estimation problem, decomposing the reverse SDE update into the standard pretrained denoising step plus an explicit correlation term ∇log p(X̃_t^i | y_t^i) that models how the i-th trajectory should align with previously generated trajectories. By applying Bayes' rule to the conditional score, the paper shows this correlation term can be parameterized as a Gaussian with a task-specific precision matrix (e.g., a diagonal mask for background/foreground separation, or an identity matrix for ambiguous images) scaled by a single hyperparameter λ. The key insight is that λ controls the variance of the conditional distribution—small λ tightly couples trajectories (preserving earlier semantics), while large λ allows independence—so the optimal synchronization strategy is determined by choosing the correct precision matrix structure per task rather than searching over dozens of averaging heuristics. This reduces the empirical search space and provides a principled explanation for why simple averaging works in some settings but fails in others.

## Results

On mask-based T2I generation (Table 1), SyncSDE with tuned λ achieves KID 34.86, FID 72.12, and CLIP-S 0.331, substantially outperforming SyncTweedies (KID 117.36, FID 149.47) and MultiDiffusion (KID 47.69, FID 84.23). On text-driven real image editing (Table 2), SyncSDE attains the best LPIPS (0.254) and BG-LPIPS (0.222) among all methods, while maintaining competitive CLIP-S (0.313). Across wide image generation, ambiguous image generation, and 3D mesh texturing, SyncSDE consistently achieves the lowest KID and FID scores, with the largest margin over SyncTweedies on wide images (KID 16.87 vs. 51.02).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SyncSDE outperforms SyncTweedies [16] across all five evaluated tasks (mask-based T2I, image editing, wide image, ambiguous image, 3D texturing) on KID and FID, with the most dramatic gap on mask-based T2I (KID 34.86 vs. 117.36) and wide image generation (KID 16.87 vs. 51.02). Against MultiDiffusion [28] on mask-based T2I, SyncSDE wins on KID (34.86 vs. 47.69) and FID (72.12 vs. 84.23) while matching CLIP-S (0.331 vs. 0.330), and does so without MultiDiffusion's additional bootstrapping module. On text-driven image editing, SyncSDE achieves the best LPIPS (0.254) and BG-LPIPS (0.222), beating MasaCtrl [5] (0.290/0.341), SDEdit [26] (0.407/0.369), PTI [8] (0.409/0.379), and DDIB [39] (0.379/0.350), though PTI edges it slightly on CLIP-S (0.322 vs. 0.313). For ambiguous image generation, SyncSDE surpasses both Visual Anagrams [7] (KID 174.90 vs. 195.29) and SyncTweedies (KID 174.90 vs. 215.12) on KID and FID, despite Visual Anagrams being purpose-built for this task. On 3D mesh texturing, SyncSDE marginally beats SyncMVD [25] on KID (184.70 vs. 196.34) and FID (183.18 vs. 189.27), with comparable CLIP-S (0.311 vs. 0.314).

## Relation to Prior Work

SyncSDE builds directly on the diffusion synchronization line of work initiated by SyncTweedies, MultiDiffusion, Visual Anagrams, and SyncMVD, all of which synchronize multiple diffusion trajectories via naive heuristics (typically averaging) without theoretical justification. The paper departs from this empirical tradition by grounding synchronization in a probabilistic framework derived from conditional score guidance (CSG) and SDE-based diffusion theory, reframing the problem as modeling the conditional probability p(X̃_t^i | y_t^i) between trajectories. This positions SyncSDE as the first work to explain *why* synchronization works and *where* task-specific heuristics should be focused, rather than exhaustively searching over strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing the synchronization design space to a single tunable hyperparameter λ within a principled probabilistic model, SyncSDE eliminates the need for the extensive empirical strategy searches (e.g., 60 strategies in SyncTweedies) that have limited the scalability of multi-trajectory generation. The consistent outperformance across six diverse tasks (mask-based T2I, image editing, wide images, ambiguous images, 3D texturing, long-horizon motion) suggests the framework generalizes well, though the single-hyperparameter assumption may be insufficient for tasks with highly heterogeneous correlation structures. Open questions include whether richer correlation models (beyond Gaussian conditionals) could further improve results, and whether the framework extends naturally to more than a handful of synchronized trajectories.
