## Problem

Existing end-to-end autonomous driving methods aggregate historical information either through dense BEV features (confined to perception, ignoring motion planning) or via sparse memory banks (performing rough, instance-level interactions that misalign with the multi-step nature of trajectory prediction and planning). This paper argues that neither paradigm adequately leverages temporal history at the step level, where each future time step requires its own historical context to produce coherent multi-step predictions.

## Input / Output

The model consumes six synchronized camera images per keyframe (256×704 for BridgeAD-S, 512×1408 for BridgeAD-B) plus a discrete driving command (left, right, straight), and notably does NOT use ego vehicle status as input to avoid the ego-status leakage issue identified by Li et al. Outputs include 3D detection boxes with tracking IDs, a vectorized HD map, multi-modal future trajectories for surrounding agents over 6 seconds (12 time steps), and the ego vehicle's planned trajectory over 3 seconds (6 time steps), all produced in a single forward pass at ~5 FPS on an RTX 3090.

## Method

BridgeAD's core contribution is reformulating motion and planning queries as multi-step tensors (Q_mot ∈ R^{N_a×M_mot×T_mot×C}, Q_plan ∈ R^{M_plan×T_plan×C}) so that each future time step is individually addressable. A FIFO memory queue caches K=3 past frames of these queries, from which step-specific historical subsets are extracted: the current-frame step feeds a Historical Mot2Det Fusion module (cross-attention into object queries for perception), while future steps feed History-Enhanced Motion Prediction and Planning modules (cross-attention plus step-level and mode-level self-attention to propagate information across all steps). A Step-Level Mot2Plan Interaction module then cross-attends the highest-probability motion queries with planning queries at corresponding time steps to enforce consistency between predicted agent behavior and ego planning. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with L1 regression and Focal classification losses under a winner-takes-all strategy for multi-modal outputs.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set (open-loop), BridgeAD-S achieves an average L2 displacement error of 0.59 m and a collision rate of 0.09%, outperforming SparseDrive (0.61 m / 0.10%) and VAD (0.72 m / 0.21%). BridgeAD-B further improves to 0.58 m L2 and 0.08% collision rate. In closed-loop NeuroNCAP simulation, BridgeAD-S scores 1.52 (vs. SparseDrive 0.92, UniAD 0.73) without post-processing, and BridgeAD-B reaches 3.06 with post-processing, reducing collision rate to 44.3% (vs. VAD 50.7%, UniAD 68.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BridgeAD outperforms all nine compared baselines on open-loop planning. Against the closest competitor SparseDrive, BridgeAD-S reduces average L2 by 0.02 m (0.59 vs. 0.61) and collision rate by 0.01% (0.09% vs. 0.10%), while BridgeAD-B extends the gap to 0.03 m and 0.02%. The advantage over VAD is more pronounced: 0.13 m lower L2 and 0.12% lower collision rate for BridgeAD-S. In closed-loop NeuroNCAP, BridgeAD-S achieves a 65% higher score than SparseDrive (1.52 vs. 0.92) and a 12.4-point lower collision rate than UniAD (76.2% vs. 88.6%) without trajectory post-processing. On perception, BridgeAD-S (R50) surpasses SparseDrive (R50) by 0.005 mAP and 0.009 NDS, and BridgeAD-B (R101) leads UniAD (R101) by 0.127 mAP and 0.096 NDS. For motion prediction, BridgeAD-B achieves the best ADE (0.60/0.70 m for car/pedestrian) and EPA (0.52/0.45), edging out SparseDrive (0.62/0.72, 0.48/0.41) and UniAD (0.71/0.78, 0.46/0.35).

## Relation to Prior Work

BridgeAD builds directly on the unified end-to-end driving pipeline established by UniAD, VAD, and SparseDrive, adopting their sparse-query architecture for perception and vectorized map representation. The key conceptual departure is in temporal information handling: prior methods inherit detection-era paradigms (dense BEV feature aggregation or flat memory-bank queries) that treat history as a monolithic signal applied only to perception. BridgeAD instead decomposes motion and planning queries into per-time-step representations, enabling step-specific cross-attention with cached historical queries from past frames, thereby extending temporal reasoning from perception into prediction and planning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a genuine gap in end-to-end driving: historical information was previously leveraged almost exclusively in perception, leaving prediction and planning to operate without temporal continuity. The multi-step query decomposition is a clean, modular design choice that other end-to-end driving systems could adopt without architectural overhaul. However, evaluation is confined to nuScenes and the NeuroNCAP simulator, leaving open whether the step-level historical interaction generalizes to longer driving horizons, more diverse datasets, or real-world deployment. The closed-loop gains are encouraging but rest on a single photorealistic benchmark, and the approach's reliance on a fixed-size FIFO memory queue (K=3 frames) raises questions about scalability to longer temporal contexts.
