## Problem

Existing controllable video generation methods (e.g., MotionCtrl, CameraCtrl) model only camera motion (translation and rotation) while ignoring critical optical parameters—focal length, lens distortion, aperture, and focus point. This omission causes ambiguities (e.g., zoom confused with forward translation), prevents cinematic effects like fisheye or bokeh, and limits the level of control available to users for producing optically coherent, film-quality video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250294/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a text prompt (or a conditioning image for I2V mode) plus a full set of per-frame camera parameters: extrinsic pose (SE(3)), focal length, radial distortion coefficients, aperture value, and focus point coordinates. These are encoded into a 9-channel per-pixel camera map (Plücker direction, Plücker moment, and aperture map) matching the video frame dimensions, which is fed to a lightweight camera adapter. The output is a video sequence where camera motion and all optical effects (zoom, distortion, bokeh) are independently and simultaneously controllable.

## Method

AKiRa introduces an extended distorted-pinhole camera model with aperture, represented per-pixel via Plücker coordinates (encoding focal length and distortion through ray geometry) augmented with a dedicated aperture map (encoding in/out-of-focus regions via a sigmoid-scaled distance to the focus point). Since no existing dataset contains videos with varying optical parameters, the paper proposes a data augmentation kit that simulates zoom (center crop + resize), lens distortion (radial warping with compensatory cropping), and bokeh (depth-estimated virtual defocus rendering) on training frames, jointly augmenting both the image and its corresponding camera parameters. A camera adapter is then trained on these augmented pairs atop a frozen video diffusion backbone (AnimateDiff or SVD), with spline-interpolated parameter sampling and augmentation dropout to ensure temporal smoothness and generalization. This disentangles focal length from translational motion and enables compositional effects such as the dolly zoom, which prior methods cannot reproduce.

## Results

On the WebVid dataset, AKiRa achieves the best FVD (332.3 on AnimateDiff, 162.8 on SVD) and CD-FVD (328.7, 398.3) among all camera-controlled methods, outperforming CameraCtrl (384.5/355.2 and 173.8/424.9) and MotionCtrl (822.9/449.7 and 217.3/460.8). For camera motion fidelity, AKiRa attains the highest FlowSim scores (70.97 on AnimateDiff, 80.11 on SVD) and lowest relative pose errors (RPE-R: 0.438°/0.295°, RPE-t: 1.480 cm/1.321 cm). On dynamic consistency (VBench), AKiRa leads with Consistency 0.969/0.981, Smoothness 0.974/0.993, and Flickering 0.957/0.975 across the two backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CameraCtrl [20], the closest competitor, AKiRa wins on FVD (332.3 vs 384.5 on AnimateDiff; 162.8 vs 173.8 on SVD), CD-FVD (328.7 vs 355.2; 398.3 vs 424.9), FlowSim (70.97 vs 53.74; 80.11 vs 79.64), and both RPE metrics, while CameraCtrl edges out AKiRa by 0.26 FID on SVD (32.32 vs 32.58). Against MotionCtrl [55], AKiRa dominates across nearly all metrics: FVD improves by 490.6 (AnimateDiff) and 54.5 (SVD), FlowSim jumps from 58.50 to 70.97 and from 11.96 to 80.11, and CD-FVD drops from 449.7 to 328.7 and from 460.8 to 398.3. Compared to the uncontrolled AnimateDiff [17] and SVD [5] baselines, AKiRa improves FVD by 502.9 and 188.2 respectively, while raising FlowSim from near-zero to 70.97 and 80.11, confirming that the camera adapter adds meaningful controllability without degrading video quality. In the user study, AKiRa is ranked 1st by 44.2% of participants on average (vs 29.2% for CameraCtrl and 18.1% for MotionCtrl), with the strongest margins on text-video consistency (55.2%) and video quality (39.6%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the camera-controlled video generation line initiated by MotionCtrl (raw pose adapter) and CameraCtrl (Plücker-coordinate ray representation) by augmenting the camera model with optical parameters—focal length, radial lens distortion, and aperture/focus point. The key methodological departure is the AKiRa data augmentation framework, which simulates these optical effects on training frames and their corresponding Plücker maps, circumventing the absence of any existing dataset with rich optical annotations. Rather than modifying the backbone, it trains a lightweight camera adapter on a frozen video diffusion model (AnimateDiff or SVD), following the conditional-generation-with-auxiliary-module paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AKiRa is the first framework to jointly control camera motion and optical parameters (zoom, fisheye distortion, bokeh) in video generation, enabling cinematic effects like the dolly zoom that were previously impossible because prior methods conflated focal-length changes with translational motion. The data-augmentation approach is practical and backbone-agnostic, making it a reusable recipe for adding optical controllability to any ray-based video model. Open questions include whether the depth-estimation-dependent bokeh augmentation generalizes to scenes with complex geometry, whether the effects compose well over longer video sequences, and whether the augmentation strategy can be extended to more exotic optics (chromatic aberration, anamorphic lenses).
