## Problem

Generating targeted universal adversarial perturbations for multi-label recognition requires learning a separate perturbation for each of the 2^|C| possible label combinations, incurring exponential time and space complexity. Existing universal perturbation methods are designed for multi-class recognition and cannot be directly adapted to multi-label settings where multiple labels co-exist and the adversary targets specific subsets. No efficient principled method existed to compose label-wise perturbations into effective multi-label attacks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251767/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method takes a trained multi-label classifier (e.g., ASL or ML-Decoder), a dataset of labeled images, and a norm budget ε (ℓ∞, set to 0.05 for images in [0,1]), and learns a matrix U of |C| mutually orthogonal label-wise perturbation vectors. At inference, given any target label set Ω, the universal perturbation u_Ω is produced in constant time by summing the corresponding columns of U. The output perturbation, when added to any image containing the target labels, flips those labels' predictions while minimally affecting non-target labels.

## Method

The key insight is that a universal perturbation for a target set Ω can be decomposed as the sum of atomic label-wise perturbations (u_Ω = Σ_{i∈Ω} u_i) under an orthogonality constraint, reducing the problem to learning |C| vectors rather than 2^|C|. This decomposition is interpreted geometrically as learning affine convex cones for each class, where the attack vector for a combination lies at the intersection of the relevant cones. The CMLU_β formulation maximizes the binary cross-entropy attack loss over points sampled from these cones (via random continuous λ vectors) while penalizing changes to non-target labels, solved with projected gradient updates on U. Training complexity is O(E|C|) versus O(E·2^|C|) for the oracle, and inference for any target combination is O(|Ω|) vector additions. A variant CMLU_α solves a minimax lower bound by iteratively optimizing the discrete composition vector λ, trading a small performance gap for potentially tighter guarantees.

## Results

On OpenImages (100 classes, ASL model), CMLU_β achieves 97.3% fooling success rate (FR) at target size |Ω|=1 and maintains 95.2% at |Ω|=20, closely matching the Oracle (97.6% → 96.8%) while using only linear O(E|C|) training complexity versus the Oracle's exponential O(E2^|C|). Baselines Or-S and Or-C collapse to 0% FR by |Ω|=10, and SGA drops to 0.15% at |Ω|=10. On NUS-WIDE and MS-COCO (target sizes 1–5), CMLU_β achieves FR within 1–2% of Oracle across both ASL and ML-Decoder models, while maintaining non-target flip rates (NTR) only slightly above Oracle (e.g., 7.96% vs 6.17% on NUS-WIDE/ASL at |Ω|=1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SGA [59] (gradient aggregation for UAP generalization), CMLU_β wins decisively at all target sizes beyond |Ω|=1: on OpenImages, SGA's FR collapses from 98.9% (|Ω|=1) to 0.15% (|Ω|=10) and 0% (|Ω|=20), while CMLU_β maintains 94.8–95.2% across the same range. On NUS-WIDE/MS-COCO, SGA also degrades sharply (e.g., 7.69%→– on NUS-WIDE/ASL at |Ω|=5), whereas CMLU_β stays within ~1% of Oracle. Against NAG [68] (GAN-based universal perturbation generation), CMLU_β achieves higher FR at large target sizes on OpenImages (95.2% vs 84.2% at |Ω|=20) and lower NTR (1.67 vs 1.84), though NAG is competitive at smaller sizes (89.7% vs 95.2% at |Ω|=5). On NUS-WIDE/MS-COCO, NAG's NTR is substantially higher than CMLU_β's (e.g., 13.7% vs 10.5% on MS-COCO/ML-Decoder at |Ω|=5), indicating NAG perturbs non-target labels more aggressively. Overall, CMLU_β is the only baseline-comparable method that scales gracefully to large target sets while keeping NTR close to the exponential-complexity Oracle.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends universal adversarial perturbations (UAPs) from the well-studied multi-class recognition setting to multi-label learning, and further extends multi-label adversarial attacks from the instance-specific regime to the universal (instance-agnostic) regime. The key conceptual departure is the compositional assumption: rather than learning an exponential number of perturbations (one per label combination) as an oracle would, the authors assume label-wise perturbations can be summed and derive a formulation that learns affine convex cones per label. This builds directly on the multi-label attack loss formulation from Mahmood & Elhamifar (CVPR 2024), which addressed negative gradient correlation in instance-specific attacks, and contrasts with generative approaches like NAG that condition a GAN on class vectors but do not guarantee generalization to unseen label combinations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reduction from exponential to linear training complexity (and constant-time inference for any target set) makes targeted universal attacks on multi-label models practically feasible at scale, which is important given MLL's role in safety-critical applications like autonomous driving and medical imaging. The transferability results (Table 4) suggest the learned convex cone regions are partially model-agnostic, opening a path toward black-box multi-label universal attacks that the authors explicitly flag as future work. A key open question is how well the orthogonality/independence assumption on label-wise perturbations holds when labels are strongly correlated, and whether the framework degrades gracefully in such regimes.
