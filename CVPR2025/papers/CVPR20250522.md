## Problem

Dense correspondences from modern detector-free matchers significantly improve two-view relative pose accuracy over sparse keypoint matching, but the large number of matches (typically 10,000+) makes the subsequent RANSAC robust estimation step prohibitively slow, as scoring and refinement scale linearly with match count. The paper asks whether the geometric constraints in dense matches are sufficiently redundant to allow aggressive summarization without meaningful accuracy loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250522/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of N dense 2D correspondences (e.g., 10,000 from DKM, RoMA, or ASpanFormer) between a calibrated image pair and produces K summarized 'metamatches' (K≈128), each consisting of a single representative 2D point pair plus a 9×9 symmetric matrix that encodes the aggregate epipolar constraint of all matches in that cluster. The final output is the estimated essential matrix (or fundamental matrix in the uncalibrated WxBS setting). The 9×9 matrix representation means each cluster's contribution to the cost function is evaluated at constant O(81) cost regardless of how many original matches it contains.

## Method

The approach has two stages. First, dense matches are clustered into K groups using K-means on 4D match vectors (concatenated coordinates from both images), and one representative match per cluster is selected as the closest point to the centroid. Second, the sum of Sampson residuals within each cluster is approximated by replacing the per-match denominator with the representative match's denominator, reducing the numerator to ‖Ae‖² where A is the n×9 constraint matrix for the cluster; this is then compressed to ‖Me‖² via Cholesky factorization AᵀA = MᵀM, yielding a fixed 9×9 matrix M per cluster. The resulting approximate cost function evaluates K terms of the form min{‖Mₖe‖²/α(E;Cₖ), |Cₖ|τ²} instead of N individual Sampson residuals, giving a 10–100× speedup in RANSAC scoring and refinement with only a marginal drop in pose accuracy (e.g., AUC@5° drops from 67.4 to 66.7 on MegaDepth-1500 with a 45× speedup).

## Results

On MegaDepth-1500 with DKM matches, the CCA variant achieves 66.7 AUC@5° (vs. 67.4 dense) at 1.5 ms runtime, a 45.2× speedup; CCC achieves 66.0 AUC@5° at 1.2 ms (55.0× speedup). With RoMA, CCA matches the dense baseline exactly (70.1 AUC@5°) while running 42.2× faster. On the WxBS wide-baseline benchmark, CCA with RoMA achieves 85.1 recall@10px (vs. 86.3 dense) at 43.3× speedup, confirming the approach generalizes to fundamental matrix estimation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against the sparse SuperPoint + LightGlue (SP+LG) pipeline as the primary external baseline. On MegaDepth-1500, SP+LG achieves 63.7 AUC@5° at 5.0 ms, while the summarized dense methods (CCA/CCC with DKM) reach 66.7/66.0 AUC@5° at 1.5/1.2 ms — meaning the summarized dense matches are both more accurate (+3.0 to +3.3 AUC@5°) and faster (3–4×) than the state-of-the-art sparse matcher. On ScanNet-1500 the gap widens: SP+LG scores 21.8 AUC@5° versus 30.9/30.4 for CCA/CCC, a difference of over 8 points. The dense baselines (full RANSAC on all matches) are the internal reference; CCA loses only 0.0–0.7 AUC@5° relative to dense across all three matchers, while CCC loses 0.7–2.2 AUC@5°, confirming that the summarization preserves nearly all geometric information at a fraction of the cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active research threads: detector-free dense matching (LoFTR, DKM, RoMA, ASpanFormer) and RANSAC-based robust estimation. It departs from prior RANSAC speedup work (SPRT, PROSAC, GroupSAC, Space-Partitioning RANSAC), which targets scoring or iteration reduction, by instead reducing the number of residuals that must be evaluated. The core compression mechanism repurposes the reduced measurement matrix from Rodríguez et al. [34] (originally for incremental SfM) in a new clustering-based context, and the resulting 9×9 per-cluster constraint is conceptually related to affine correspondences but without the local planarity assumption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for pipelines that already use dense matchers: a 10–100× speedup in the robust estimation step with less than 1° average accuracy loss, and the method is matcher-agnostic. The key limitation is that it only helps when the input match count is very large (the authors note it 'only makes sense if the original number of matches is very large'), and it does not address the dominant runtime cost of the dense matcher itself. An open question is whether the same summarization principle extends to other geometric estimation problems (e.g., PnP, homography) or to multi-view settings where the redundancy structure may differ.
