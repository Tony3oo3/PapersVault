## Problem

Standard video encoders optimize for human perceptual quality, but in many modern applications (autonomous driving, security) the compressed video feeds downstream AI tasks like object detection. The challenge is to adapt an existing, highly optimized codec (e.g., x264) to prioritize task-relevant regions without requiring the downstream model, ground-truth labels, or heavy pre-encoding computation at inference time — a gap left by prior work that either relied on semantic maps, per-frame optimization ignoring inter-frame dependencies, or entirely new deep encoders.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes per-patch encoder statistics (readily available from x264's look-ahead, requiring no extra computation) for each 480×320 frame partitioned into 16×16 pixel macro-blocks. It outputs a 30×20 QP-delta map (coarsened then upsampled via interpolation) that modulates the frame-level QP per block, which is fed back into x264 to produce the compressed stream. Critically, neither the downstream task model nor any ground-truth annotation is needed at inference, making the policy deployable on edge devices at 250 FPS.

## Method

RL-RC-DoT formulates per-frame encoding as an MDP (one timestep per frame) and trains a PPO policy to select all macro-block QP deltas simultaneously, balancing a task-performance reward against a bit-rate compliance penalty. The task reward is self-supervised: the downstream model's output on the raw frame serves as pseudo-ground-truth, and the reward measures how well the model performs on the reconstructed frame relative to that reference. A key architectural addition is a block-level auxiliary prediction loss that forces the network to predict per-block sub-scores contributing to the aggregate task metric, providing a denser and more localized learning signal. The high-dimensional action space is made tractable by operating on a coarsened grid and interpolating to full resolution. The resulting policy requires only encoder statistics at inference, runs in 0.004 s per frame, and generalizes across different detector models and closely related tasks without retraining.

## Results

On the BDD100K dataset, RL-RC-DoT achieves a BD-rate reduction of 24.7% (±1.57%) in car detection precision over standard x264, with only a 1.19% (±0.46%) PSNR deterioration, indicating task-aware compression with minimal perceptual quality loss. For ROI (saliency) encoding, the method achieves a BD-rate of −25.64% (±0.99%) in saliency-weighted PSNR while also slightly improving standard PSNR (−5.26% BD-rate). The method demonstrates task robustness: when tested with a different detector (SSD) or a related task (segmentation), it still yields BD-rate improvements of −26.2% (precision) and −14.6% (segmentation IoU) respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the standard x264 encoder (medium preset, 50–200 kbps target bit-rates). RL-RC-DoT substantially outperforms x264 on car detection: at low bit-rate, precision improves from 0.22 to 0.36 and recall from 0.45 to 0.63; at high bit-rate, precision rises from 0.66 to 0.71 and recall from 0.81 to 0.83. In BD-rate terms, this corresponds to a 24.7% bitrate saving for equivalent detection precision, at the cost of only a 1.19% PSNR BD-rate increase—meaning the reconstructed video remains visually comparable to the x264 baseline. For ROI encoding, RL-RC-DoT again wins, achieving a 25.64% BD-rate improvement in saliency-weighted PSNR while also slightly beating x264 on standard PSNR (−5.26% BD-rate). The method also generalizes: when the trained policy is evaluated with a different detector (SSD) or a segmentation model (DeepLab), it still yields BD-rate gains of −26.2% and −14.6% respectively, confirming that the learned QP allocation captures task-relevant structure rather than overfitting to a single model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of task-aware video compression work (e.g., Xie et al. [39], Li et al. [17]) by formulating per-macro-block QP control as a sequential RL problem over consecutive frames rather than per-frame or limited-GOP optimization, and by eliminating the need for ground-truth segmentation maps or downstream task outputs during inference. It builds on the x264 standardized encoder as a fixed backbone and adapts the reward-prediction auxiliary loss concept from Ye et al. [40] (originally for Atari) to exploit the compositional, block-aggregated structure of task scores. The key departure from prior work is that the policy consumes only lightweight encoder statistics (available in x264's look-ahead) and outputs a full QP-delta map per frame, making it deployable in real-time streaming without any task-specific computation on the encoding side.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling task-aware compression on resource-constrained edge devices (vehicles, drones) for data collection pipelines, where ground-truth labels are unavailable and computational budgets are tight. The demonstrated robustness—improving SSD detection and DeepLab segmentation without retraining a policy trained on YOLOv5-nano—suggests the learned QP allocation captures general task-relevant visual structure rather than model-specific artifacts, which is crucial for long-lived deployment where downstream models evolve. Open questions include generalization across resolutions (which changes action-space dimensionality), extension to H.265/H.266 with CTU-level structures, and whether unsupervised quality metrics in the reward could further improve cross-task transfer. The training cost (encoding + running the downstream model per frame) remains a practical bottleneck for complex tasks.
