## Problem

Open Vocabulary Video Anomaly Detection (OVVAD) faces two unexplored challenges for novel anomalies: detection ambiguity, where models lack sufficient information to score unfamiliar anomalies accurately, and categorization confusion, where novel anomalies are misclassified as visually similar base categories. Existing methods rely on training or fine-tuning that cannot adapt to open-world variability, and use pre-trained text encoders without task-specific guidance for label encoding, limiting multimodal alignment for unseen categories.

## Input / Output

The method takes video sequences (up to 256 frames) encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 image encoder, along with a full anomaly label set (base + novel) that is processed by GPT-4 to produce group descriptions and a concept library of anomaly-relevant nouns. It outputs per-frame binary anomaly scores in [0,1] and a video-level Top-1 label from the complete label set, where the video-level prediction is computed by averaging the top M frame probabilities per label (M = total frames / 16) before applying softmax.

## Method

Anomize introduces two mechanisms: (1) a Text-Augmented Dual Stream where a dynamic stream (lightweight LSTM temporal features augmented by GPT-4-generated label descriptions) and a static stream (original CLIP features augmented by a concept library of anomaly-related nouns) each pass through a unified multi-head-attention augmenter before a shared detector head, with outputs weighted by a tunable β; and (2) a Group-Guided Text Encoding that uses GPT-4 to cluster labels by visual similarity and generate group-aware descriptions, which are encoded by the frozen CLIP text encoder to position novel label embeddings near visually similar base labels. Training is segmented into two stages: first the LSTM is trained for categorization with cross-entropy plus a separation loss, then the dual streams are trained for detection with a weighted MIL loss, while all CLIP encoders remain frozen throughout.

## Results

On XD-VIOLENCE, Anomize achieves 69.31% AP overall and 84.24% AP on novel categories, surpassing the prior best OVVAD method (Wu et al., CVPR 2024) by 2.78% and 8.21% respectively. On UCF-CRIME, it reaches 84.49% AUC, competitive with the state-of-the-art (86.40%). For anomaly categorization, Anomize attains 90.29% Top-1 accuracy on XD-VIOLENCE (vs. 64.68% for the prior method) and 47.14% on UCF-CRIME (vs. 41.43%), with particularly large gains on novel categories (87.43% vs. 30.90% on XD-VIOLENCE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest OVVAD baseline (Wu et al., CVPR 2024), Anomize wins decisively on XD-VIOLENCE detection (69.31% vs. 66.53% AP; 84.24% vs. 76.03% AP on novel) and categorization (90.29% vs. 64.68% Top-1; 87.43% vs. 30.90% on novel), but trails slightly on UCF-CRIME detection (84.49% vs. 86.40% AUC), which the authors attribute to the lightweight LSTM and segmented training limiting detection-branch optimization. Compared to traditional WSVAD methods (RTFM, Sultani et al., Wu et al. ECCV 2020), Anomize leads on both datasets across all detection metrics, with the largest margins on novel categories. Against Zhu et al. (open-set VAD with evidential deep learning), Anomize achieves higher AP (69.31% vs. 64.40%), though the comparison is noted as using different category divisions. The categorization advantage is most striking: the group-guided text encoding mechanism yields a 56.53-point improvement on novel XD-VIOLENCE categories over the prior OVVAD method, demonstrating that label-grouping guidance is the key differentiator for open-vocabulary classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Wu et al. (CVPR 2024), which first formulated the OVVAD task using CLIP, and on VadCLIP (AAAI 2024) for the MIL-based detection loss. It departs from prior OVVAD methods in two key ways: (1) replacing the single-stream detection pipeline with a Text-Augmented Dual Stream that separately models temporal dynamics and static scene context, each augmented by different textual signals (label descriptions vs. a concept library), and (2) introducing a Group-Guided Text Encoding mechanism that uses GPT-4 to generate group-aware label descriptions, rather than relying solely on pre-trained text encoders with fixed templates. The deliberate choice of a lightweight LSTM over heavier temporal encoders is positioned as a countermeasure against overfitting on novel cases, a concern the authors identify as underexplored in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The substantial gains on novel-case categorization (56.53% on XD-VIOLENCE) suggest that the text-encoding bottleneck, not the visual encoder, is the primary limiter of zero-shot generalization in OVVAD — a useful diagnostic for the field. However, the categorization comparison rests on a single baseline, and the detection results on UCF-CRIME are competitive rather than dominant, leaving open whether the dual-stream design generalizes beyond these two benchmarks. The reliance on GPT-4 for generating group descriptions and concept nouns introduces a practical dependency and raises questions about reproducibility and cost at scale. Cross-dataset transfer results are encouraging but limited to two closely related surveillance/violence datasets, so broader applicability to diverse anomaly taxonomies remains untested.
