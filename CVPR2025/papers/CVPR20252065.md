## Problem

Diffusion-based audio-driven talking avatar methods produce high-fidelity, expressive videos but suffer from slow inference due to many denoising steps combined with multi-CFG (three forward passes per step) to maintain audio-reference correlation. Naive application of existing diffusion distillation techniques to this task degrades robustness on open-set inputs and weakens audio-video alignment, making direct distillation insufficient.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252065/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 reference portrait image and a driving audio signal as input, along with motion frames from the previous clip for temporal continuity. It outputs a talking avatar video at 512×512 resolution. Inference requires only 6 denoising steps (18 NFE-D for the balanced setting, 6 NFE-D for the fast setting with CFG distillation) compared to 75 NFE-D for the 25-step teacher with 3 CFG runs.

## Method

FADA builds on a dual-UNet architecture (derived from Loopy) with audio and temporal attention layers, distilled via PeRFlow piecewise rectified flow. Two key innovations address distillation-specific failures: (1) an adaptive mixed-supervised loss that combines teacher-supervised distillation with a ground-truth loss, where the ground-truth weight is modulated by the ratio R = L_gt/L_teacher using a piecewise function with peak and dead thresholds, allowing the student to exploit a larger moderate-quality dataset (1300 h vs. 160 h for the teacher) without degrading on low-quality samples; (2) a multi-CFG distillation mechanism that replaces the three separate forward passes with learnable tokens γ_a, γ_r, γ_b whose linear combination (mirroring the multi-CFG formula) is injected via cross-attention CFG layers after each audio layer, enabling the student to internalize the CFG reasoning in a single pass.

## Results

On the CelebV-HQ test set, Ours-Balanced (18 NFE) achieves the best IQA (3.588), FVD (50.36), FID (33.67), and E-FID (2.202) among all methods, while Ours-Fast (6 NFE) attains the best Sync-D (8.746) with competitive quality. On HDTF, Ours-Balanced leads in FVD (13.32) and FID (18.39), and Ours-Fast maintains comparable performance across most metrics. Both variants achieve 4.17×–12.5× NFE speedup over the 75-NFE teacher while matching or exceeding the quality of undistilled baselines that require 50–80 NFE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Hallo (80 NFE), FADA-Balanced wins on IQA (3.588 vs 3.505), FVD (50.36 vs 53.99), FID (33.67 vs 35.96), and E-FID (2.202 vs 2.426) on CelebV-HQ, while using only 22.5% of the NFE. Against EchoMimic (60 NFE), FADA-Balanced leads on IQA (3.588 vs 3.307), Sync-D (8.998 vs 10.37), FVD (50.36 vs 54.71), and E-FID (2.202 vs 3.018) on CelebV-HQ. Against V-Express (50 NFE), FADA dominates on every metric on both datasets, with the largest gap in FVD (50.36 vs 117.8 on CelebV-HQ; 13.32 vs 48.03 on HDTF). Against the GAN-based SadTalker, FADA-Balanced achieves substantially better FVD (50.36 vs 171.8) and FID (33.67 vs 36.64) on CelebV-HQ, though SadTalker retains a slight edge in E-FID on HDTF (1.559 vs 1.362). The fastest variant (Ours-Fast, 6 NFE) trades a small quality drop (e.g., E-FID 2.604 vs 2.202 on CelebV-HQ) for a 12.5× speedup over the teacher, remaining competitive with all baselines on IQA and Sync-D.

## Relation to Prior Work

FADA builds directly on the dual-UNet diffusion architecture popularized by Loopy, EchoMimic, and Hallo for audio-driven talking avatar synthesis, using Stable Diffusion 1.5 as the base. For the distillation backbone, it adopts PeRFlow (a rectified-flow variant) after empirically ruling out TCD and RFlow, but departs from all prior diffusion distillation work (text-to-image, text-to-video) by addressing the unique challenge of multi-condition CFG: the student must internalize the three-pass CFG computation over audio and reference-image conditions rather than simply matching a single conditional output. The mixed-supervised loss and learnable-token CFG embedding are new contributions not present in any prior distillation framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first diffusion distillation framework purpose-built for audio-driven talking avatars, and it demonstrates that generic distillation recipes (TCD, RFlow, PeRFlow) fail in this multi-condition setting, losing both robustness to open-set inputs and audio-video correlation. The practical impact is significant: a 12.5× NFE reduction (75→6) while matching or exceeding undistilled baselines on FVD and FID makes real-time or near-real-time talking avatar generation feasible on consumer hardware. Open questions include whether the learnable-token CFG distillation generalizes to other multi-condition generation tasks (e.g., multi-prompt video), whether the adaptive mixed-supervised weighting transfers cleanly to text-to-video, and how the approach scales to longer sequences or higher resolutions beyond 512×512.
