## Problem

Existing CLIP-based weakly supervised semantic segmentation methods rely solely on global image-text alignment for CAM generation, leaving CLIP's dense patch-text alignment capability unexplored. This creates two specific deficiencies: textual prompts (e.g., 'a photo of [CLASS]') are semantically sparse and lack localization knowledge, while CLIP's q-k self-attention produces overly uniform attention maps that homogenize tokens and lose fine-grained spatial details.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252661/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images and image-level class labels, with CLIP ViT-B (frozen) as the visual encoder. On the text side, GPT-4 generates 20 detailed descriptions per class, which are encoded by CLIP's text encoder and clustered into an implicit attribute space (112 centroids for VOC, 224 for COCO). The pipeline produces class activation maps in both a training-free mode (2.9 GB GPU, no training) and an efficient-learning mode (3.2 GB GPU, 90 minutes), which are then refined into pixel-level pseudo labels to supervise a lightweight Transformer segmentation head.

## Method

ExCEL generates CAMs via a patch-text alignment paradigm, computing patch-wise cosine similarity between enhanced text embeddings and calibrated visual features. Text Semantic Enrichment (TSE) clusters GPT-4-generated class descriptions into implicit attributes via K-means, then hunts for the top-K most relevant attributes to the global class embedding, aggregating them with softmax-weighted coefficients to produce richer text representations. Static Visual Calibration (SVC) replaces CLIP's suboptimal q-k self-attention with an Intra-correlation operation that computes attention within each of the q, k, v spaces across the last 5 intermediate layers, preserving fine-grained spatial details without any retraining. Learnable Visual Calibration (LVC) adds a lightweight adapter that learns dynamic token relations guided by SVC's static CAMs, applying a distribution shift to push frozen features toward spatially diverse representations. Training optimizes only the adapter and segmentation head with a diversity loss (supervising token correlations via static pseudo labels) plus cross-entropy segmentation loss, requiring just 6% of the training time of comparable methods.

## Results

ExCEL achieves 78.4% mIoU on PASCAL VOC 2012 val and 78.5% on test, outperforming the previous single-stage SOTA WeCLIP (76.4%/77.2%) by 2.0%/1.3% and all multi-stage methods by at least 3.9%. On MS COCO 2014 val, ExCEL reaches 50.3% mIoU, a 3.2% improvement over WeCLIP (47.1%). In a training-free setting (ExCEL*), the method already achieves 74.6% mIoU on VOC train for CAM quality, surpassing CLIP-ES (70.8%) and matching most training-required methods. ExCEL requires only 90 minutes of training and 3.2 GB GPU memory, compared to 270 minutes and 6.2 GB for WeCLIP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ExCEL outperforms the closest single-stage competitor WeCLIP [43] by 2.0% mIoU on VOC val (78.4 vs 76.4) and 3.2% on COCO val (50.3 vs 47.1), while also reducing training time from 270 to 90 minutes and GPU memory from 6.2 to 3.2 GB. Against other single-stage methods, ExCEL leads SeCo [39] by 4.4% on VOC val and DIAL [13] by 3.9%, and surpasses DuPL [35] by 5.1%. Compared to multi-stage CLIP-based methods, ExCEL exceeds CPAL [31] by 3.9% and PSDPM [45] by 4.3% on VOC val, and outperforms CLIP-ES [20] by 6.2%. In the training-free CAM generation setting (Table 2), ExCEL* achieves 74.6% mIoU on VOC train, beating CLIP-ES [20] by 3.8% and matching or exceeding most training-required methods like MCTformer+ [38] (68.8%) and CTI [41] (69.5%). ExCEL reaches 96.1% of the fully-supervised WeCLIP-Full performance (78.4% vs 81.6%), the highest ratio among all WSSS methods compared.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant CLIP-based WSSS paradigm (CLIMS, CLIP-ES, WeCLIP) that relies on global image-text alignment for CAM generation, instead proposing a patch-text alignment paradigm that directly computes per-patch cosine similarity between text embeddings and individual visual tokens. It builds on WeCLIP's single-stage frozen-CLIP architecture and MaskCLIP's observation that q-k attention homogenizes fine-grained details, but goes further by replacing q-k attention with a non-parametric Intra-correlation operation across intermediate layers and adding a lightweight learnable adapter for dynamic calibration. The Text Semantic Enrichment module introduces a novel use of LLMs (GPT-4) to construct a dataset-wide knowledge base and K-means clustering to form an implicit attribute space, treating text prompting as an attribute-hunting process rather than explicit template concatenation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: ExCEL achieves SOTA WSSS performance (78.4% mIoU on VOC, 50.3% on COCO) with only 90 minutes of training and 3.2 GB GPU memory, roughly 6% of the training time of multi-stage methods and 33% of WeCLIP. The finding that CLIP's q-k self-attention is actively counterproductive for dense prediction—producing overly uniform maps that suppress fine-grained localization—challenges the assumption that CLIP's architecture is universally well-suited for downstream dense tasks. Open questions include whether the patch-text alignment paradigm and LLM-based text enrichment generalize to more complex or open-vocabulary segmentation settings, and whether the GPT-4 dependency for description generation limits reproducibility and scalability to very large class taxonomies.
