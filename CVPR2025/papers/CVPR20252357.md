## Problem

Generic object detectors suffer severe performance degradation on tiny objects (2–32 pixels) because successive downsampling suppresses their already limited pixel representations to near-background levels. Existing remedies—scale-aware feature fusion and heuristic attention maps—fail to address this fundamental information loss, as attention derived from sparse, weakly activated regions is unreliable and fusion cannot recover what has already been lost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252357/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and operates on the bottom-level FPN feature P2 (H/4 × W/4 × C) as its primary enhancement target, while also consuming P3 and P4 for multi-scale distribution map prediction. It is a plug-and-play module that outputs an enhanced feature map P2′ to replace P2 in any FPN-like detector's detection head, producing standard bounding boxes with class labels. Internally it also generates an unsupervised information map σ and a supervised position Gaussian distribution map M_pd2, both at P2 resolution, which jointly modulate the features before CBAM-based attention and element-wise fusion.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage feature enhancement pipeline. First, Pixels Feature Information Modeling (PFIM) applies GDN-style differentiable quantization to P2, then fits a per-pixel Gaussian density (mean μ and scale σ predicted by a CNN) and minimizes the Information Entropy loss (sum of negative log-likelihoods) in an unsupervised manner; the resulting scale map σ highlights salient regions with higher encoding cost and is used to modulate P2 via y₁ = y ⊗ (1+σ). Second, Position Gaussian Distribution Prediction (PGDP) constructs a ground-truth map from a Gaussian Mixture where each component's covariance is scaled by a size-dependent factor α (4/6/8/10 for very-tiny/tiny/small/general objects), ensuring tiny objects receive higher peak intensities; this map is predicted from multi-scale features [P4+¼σ, P3+½σ, P2+σ] under weighted MSE supervision with deep supervision across three branches. The two enhanced features y₁ and y₂ are each passed through CBAM and fused by element-wise addition to produce P2′. Training uses a composite loss L = L_det + 0.01·L_IE + 1.0·L_pred, combining the original detection loss with the unsupervised entropy loss and the supervised distribution prediction loss.

## Results

On VisDrone2019, the plug-and-play module combined with RFLA achieves the best overall performance: AP 29.0, AP₀.₅ 50.7, APᵥₜ 7.4, APₜ 14.9, and APₛ 26.5, outperforming all baselines by at least 0.6 AP. On AI-TOD, DetectoRS w/ ours reaches AP 24.3 and APₜ 24.9 (gains of +9.7 and +13.9 over the DetectoRS baseline). On AI-TODv2, DetectoRS w/ ours achieves AP 25.5, AP₀.₅ 58.2, and APₜ 25.7, with gains of +9.4 and +13.1 over its baseline respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is plug-and-play and consistently improves every baseline it is attached to. On VisDrone2019, RFLA w/ ours surpasses Salience DETR (AP 28.4) by 0.6 AP and SR-TOD (AP 27.3) by 1.7 AP, while achieving the highest APᵥₜ of 7.4 (vs. 5.4 for Salience DETR and 2.3 for SR-TOD). The largest absolute gains appear on the tiny-object metrics: +5.8 APₜ over Faster R-CNN and +5.1 APₜ over DetectoRS, confirming the method's targeted benefit for extremely small objects. On AI-TOD and AI-TODv2, DetectoRS w/ ours leads on AP, AP₀.₅, AP₀.₇₅, APₜ, and APₛ, ranking second only on APᵥₜ (8.5/8.6 vs. 8.4/8.6 for Cascade R-CNN w/ ours). Compared to SR-TOD, which also targets information-loss regions via a difference map, the proposed method achieves higher AP on all three datasets (e.g., 29.0 vs. 27.3 on VisDrone2019), attributed to directly identifying loss regions at the feature-map level rather than relying on image-restoration quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the density estimation framework from learned image compression (Ballé et al.'s GDN quantization and Minnen et al.'s Gaussian-uniform convolution) to identify information-loss regions in feature maps, a conceptual transplant from compression to detection. Within tiny object detection, it departs from the dominant scale-aware fusion (FPN, DetectoRS) and heuristic attention (SCRDet, KB-RANN) paradigms by grounding feature enhancement in Shannon information theory, and contrasts with the recent SR-TOD difference-map approach by operating directly at the feature level rather than relying on a restored image. The Position Gaussian Distribution Map, with instance-size-dependent covariance scaling, is a novel supervised signal that explicitly encodes the foreground-background and tiny-vs-general distinction, complementing the unsupervised information map.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design (applicable to any FPN-based detector with consistent gains across Faster R-CNN, Cascade R-CNN, DetectoRS, and RFLA) makes the technique practically adoptable without architectural redesign. However, all experiments are confined to aerial/drone imagery (VisDrone, AI-TOD, AI-TODv2) with a single ResNet-50 backbone, leaving open whether the information-entropy signal generalizes to ground-level or indoor tiny objects and to larger or transformer-based backbones. The added computational cost of quantization, density estimation, and multi-scale distribution prediction is not quantified, and the method's interaction with other recent tiny-object strategies (e.g., NWD-based label assignment) beyond simple stacking remains unexplored.
