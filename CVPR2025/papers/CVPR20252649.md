## Problem

Existing 3D QA methods predominantly rely on point clouds as the sole visual input, overlooking the rich local texture details in multi-view images that are critical for identifying flat or small objects (e.g., TVs, pictures, carpets). Moreover, naive back-projection of multi-view features into 3D space suffers from two inherent limitations: fixed per-view aggregation weights that ignore question context, and unavoidable feature degradation caused by camera pose noise and occlusions.

## Input / Output

The network takes a 40,000-point RGB-colored point cloud, 20 multi-view images at 224×224 resolution, and a question (optionally concatenated with a situation description for 3D SQA) as input. The point cloud is encoded by a pre-trained PointNet++ backbone (discarding VoteNet's detection head) to produce seed-point features, images by a frozen Swin Transformer, and text by a pre-trained Sentence-BERT. The output is a predicted answer vector over N_α candidate answers, scored via an MCAN head.

## Method

DSPNet introduces three modules that progressively integrate dual-vision information. The Text-guided Multi-view Fusion (TGMF) module back-projects multi-view image features into 3D point coordinates, then learns question-conditioned importance weights via cross-attention between globally pooled image and text features, replacing the fixed 1/n averaging. The Adaptive Dual-vision Perception (ADVP) module applies a SENet-inspired point-wise and channel-wise gating mechanism to the concatenated back-projected and point cloud features, filtering high-confidence channels while suppressing degraded ones before projecting to a unified visual representation. The Multimodal Context-guided Reasoning (MCGR) module performs L=4 layers of sparse-to-dense cross-attention (FPS-sampled candidates attend to all dense visual features) followed by a transformer sub-layer that fuses the updated visual tokens with text tokens, preserving spatial fidelity without the computational cost of full cross-modal attention on dense features.

## Results

On the SQA3D dataset, DSPNet achieves 50.4% average answer accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline 3DGraphQA (49.2%) and pre-trained 3D-VisTA (48.5%). On the ScanQA dataset, DSPNet attains EM@1 of 26.5/23.8 (test w/ object / w/o object), EM@10 of 58.8/56.1, and CIDEr of 78.1/69.6, surpassing all baselines on every reported metric. Notably, DSPNet requires no external pre-training yet matches or exceeds 3D-VisTA (pre-trained on a large-scale 3D-Text dataset) on EM@1 and significantly outperforms it on CIDEr (78.1 vs. 76.6) and ROUGE (39.3 vs. 38.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSPNet outperforms all seven baselines on both benchmarks. On SQA3D, it leads 3DGraphQA (49.2%) by 1.2 points in average accuracy and pre-trained 3D-VisTA (48.5%) by 1.9 points, while the gap over ScanQA (45.3%) and SQA3D (47.2%) is 5.1 and 3.2 points respectively. On ScanQA, DSPNet beats the closest competitor 3D-VisTA (pre-trained) on EM@1 by 0.5/0.8 points and on CIDEr by 1.5/7.0 points, and surpasses 3DGraphQA on EM@1 by 0.9/1.5 points and CIDEr by 3.5/6.7 points. The advantage is most pronounced on sentence-level metrics (CIDEr, ROUGE, METEOR), where DSPNet's dual-vision perception yields answers with closer semantic and structural alignment to references. Importantly, DSPNet achieves these gains without any external pre-training, whereas 3D-VisTA and Multi-CLIP rely on large-scale 3D-Text paired data, suggesting that the adaptive multi-view fusion and context-guided reasoning contribute more than pre-training alone for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

DSPNet extends the 3D QA line of work (ScanQA, SQA3D, 3DGraphQA) that has relied almost exclusively on point cloud features, by incorporating multi-view image features through back-projection—a technique borrowed from 3D detection (3DMV, BEVFusion). However, it departs from both the 3D QA and 3D detection literatures in two key ways: it introduces text-conditioned weighting of multi-view features (TGMF) rather than uniform aggregation, and it applies SENet-inspired channel-wise gating (ADVP) to mitigate the feature degradation inherent in back-projection, a problem largely overlooked in prior multi-modal 3D fusion work. The final reasoning stage builds on MCAN's modular co-attention but augments it with a cross-attention mechanism over dense point-level features to preserve spatial granularity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that multi-view images provide complementary texture and color information that point clouds inherently lack, particularly for flat or small objects (TVs, pictures, carpets), and that naive back-projection fusion is insufficient without adaptive, text-guided weighting. However, the improvements over strong baselines are modest (≈1–1.5 points), the evaluation is confined to ScanNet-based indoor scenes, and the approach requires known camera poses and multi-view image availability, limiting its applicability to settings where such data is accessible. Open questions include whether the dual-vision paradigm generalizes to outdoor or large-scale scenes, whether the text-guided fusion transfers to open-ended 3D generation or navigation tasks, and whether the modest gains justify the additional computational cost of encoding and fusing 20 multi-view images.
