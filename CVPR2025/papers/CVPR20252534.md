## Problem

Multi-task learning can degrade generalization when task labels are spuriously correlated, causing per-task predictors to rely on unrelated features from other tasks. Existing mitigations frame the problem as distributional invariance under unknown covariate shifts, but this formulation lacks theoretical guarantees and its effectiveness is highly sensitive to how the validation distribution is constructed. The paper asks whether spurious correlations between tasks can be directly identified from label statistics and then explicitly removed from task-specific predictors.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a frozen pre-trained shared encoder that maps images (RGB or depth) to a shared latent representation. For each task, a learnable projector and predictor are appended to produce task-specific outputs (class labels, segmentation masks, or depth estimates). The identification step consumes only the training-set label vectors across tasks—no image features are needed—computing Pearson correlation coefficients on the original and class-wise resampled label distributions to flag spurious task pairs.

## Method

The framework has two stages. First, for every pair of tasks (i, j), it computes the Pearson correlation coefficient of their labels on the full training set and again after down-sampling one class of task j's labels (resampling constant ξ=0.1); if the absolute difference exceeds a threshold δ, task i is added to task j's spurious set Z^j. Theorem 1 proves this difference is lower-bounded by a function of the marginal probability shift, guaranteeing detection even under label imbalance, while Remark 1 shows the difference vanishes when no spurious correlation exists. Second, debiased adversarial training fine-tunes only the per-task projector h^j and predictor g^j (with the shared encoder frozen) to minimize task j's loss while simultaneously maximizing the loss of its identified spurious tasks' frozen predictors evaluated on the projected representation, effectively forcing h^j to discard information that would help those spurious tasks.

## Results

The paper reports relative improvements over vanilla MTL across six benchmark datasets. On CelebA, the proposed method achieves +5.7% (Gender), +4.6% (Smiling), +3.9% (Age), and +2.4% (Attractiveness) accuracy gains. On Multi-MNIST, improvements reach +9.3% (left digit) and +9.6% (right digit). On NYU-V2, the method gains +11.3% mean IoU on segmentation and +10.2% on depth estimation, while on Taskonomy it achieves +12.7% on object classification and +9.9% on scene classification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four baselines on every task across all reported datasets. MT-CRL [14] (distributional invariance via disentangled modules) shows the weakest performance on CelebA (+0.1% to +2.6%) and MetaShift (+1.6% to +3.2%), consistent with the paper's argument that covariate-shift-based formulations are sensitive to validation set construction. Meta-learning [11] (bi-level optimization with disentangled representations) performs marginally better than vanilla MTL on several tasks, e.g., +3.4% on CelebA Smiling but only +0.1% on MetaShift Scene. GMTL [27] (generative modeling of joint label distributions) is competitive on Multi-MNIST (+5.9%, +7.2%) but is infeasible on high-dimensional segmentation/depth tasks (Taskonomy, NYU-V2, CityScape) and underperforms on CelebA Attractiveness (+1.2%). SubSel [21] (subset selection) provides moderate gains but is consistently outperformed by the proposed method by 1–4 percentage points on CelebA and by 2–5 points on Multi-MNIST.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant distributional invariance formulation for spurious correlations in MTL (MT-CRL [14], Meta-learning [11]) and the generative joint-distribution modeling of GMTL [27], arguing that both lack theoretical guarantees and are sensitive to validation set construction. Instead, it repurposes the classical label-dependency view of spurious correlation (Pearson coefficient) into a multi-task setting, introducing class-wise resampling to make the identification robust to label imbalance. The mitigation step draws on adversarial training but applies it only to task-specific projectors and predictors, leaving the shared encoder frozen—a lightweight alternative to the full-network regularizations in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded, label-based diagnostic for between-task spurious correlation that avoids the intractability of causal feature recovery and the fragility of distributional invariance to validation-set bias. Its practical advantage is that mitigation requires only fine-tuning per-task projectors and predictors, making it cheap to apply post-hoc to any pre-trained MTL model. Open questions include how the identification threshold δ behaves under very weak or multi-way spurious correlations, whether the approach extends to continuous-valued or high-dimensional labels (e.g., dense prediction masks), and whether the adversarial objective can be integrated into end-to-end training rather than a separate fine-tuning phase.
