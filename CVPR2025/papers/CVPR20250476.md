## Problem

Conventional 3D scene representation methods (NeRF, 3DGS) assume sharp input images, but real-world capture with finite depth of field inevitably produces defocused images. Existing blur-handling approaches either rely on learned kernels without photographic grounding (Deblur-NeRF, BAGS) or use slow implicit representations (DoF-NeRF), leaving a gap for a rasterization-based method that accurately models defocus via physically grounded Circle of Confusion (CoC) principles while maintaining real-time rendering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250476/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes defocused multi-view RGB images (not sharp ones), camera poses from COLMAP, and an SfM point cloud for Gaussian initialization. It produces a 3D Gaussian scene representation that can render sharp novel views by excluding CoC Gaussians, or customizable defocused images by adjusting the learned aperture parameter K and per-image focus plane d_F at render time. Each view is rendered as M+1 images (one per CoC Gaussian set plus the base set) that are combined via learned pixel-wise weights into a single defocused output.

## Method

CoCoGaussian models defocus by generating M CoC Gaussians per base 3D Gaussian, positioned to form the Circle of Confusion shape. The CoC diameter is computed from the photographic formula σ ≈ K|1/d − 1/d_F| using the Gaussian depth, a learnable aperture scalar K = f×D, and a per-image learnable focus plane d_F. A lightweight MLP h_θ takes the camera position and base Gaussian parameters (μ_B, s_B, q_B) and outputs the aperture K, M direction vectors d_m, M adaptive scaling factors β_m ∈ (0,1], and scaling/rotation adjustments for the CoC Gaussians. The β_m factors ensure CoC Gaussians remain within the CoC boundary and adaptively shrink the diameter when depth is unreliable (e.g., reflective or refractive surfaces). A shallow CNN computes pixel-wise softmax weights over the M+1 rendered images, which are combined via weighted sum to produce the final defocused image, optimized with L1 + D-SSIM loss against ground truth defocused inputs.

## Results

On the Deblur-NeRF synthetic dataset, CoCoGaussian achieves the best performance across all metrics: PSNR 30.84, SSIM 0.9212, and LPIPS 0.0478, outperforming the closest competitor BAGS (30.65 / 0.9128 / 0.0631). On the DoF-NeRF real-world dataset, CoCoGaussian scores PSNR 30.14, SSIM 0.9127, and LPIPS 0.0701, again leading all baselines. On the Deblur-NeRF real-world dataset, CoCoGaussian achieves the best SSIM (0.7531) and LPIPS (0.0825), though PDRF-10 edges it slightly on PSNR (23.85 vs. 23.70) due to illumination differences between sharp and defocused captures. On the all-in-focus NeRF-LLFF dataset, CoCoGaussian also surpasses vanilla 3DGS (PSNR 27.76 vs. 27.10, LPIPS 0.0372 vs. 0.0569), demonstrating generalization beyond defocused inputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoCoGaussian outperforms all seven baselines on the Deblur-NeRF synthetic dataset across every metric, with the largest margin over BAGS (the closest competitor) of +0.19 PSNR and −0.0153 LPIPS. On the DoF-NeRF real-world dataset, CoCoGaussian leads BAGS by +0.27 PSNR and −0.0399 LPIPS, and surpasses Deblurring 3DGS by +3.56 PSNR, indicating that the physically grounded CoC modeling provides a substantial advantage over learned-kernel approaches at higher resolutions. The only metric where CoCoGaussian does not rank first is PSNR on the Deblur-NeRF real-world set, where PDRF-10 scores 23.85 vs. 23.70; the authors attribute this to illumination inconsistencies between sharp and defocused captures in that dataset. Compared to the ray-tracing baselines (Deblur-NeRF, PDRF, DP-NeRF), CoCoGaussian achieves comparable or better quality while benefiting from the real-time rendering speed of the 3DGS rasterization pipeline. On the all-in-focus NeRF-LLFF dataset, CoCoGaussian improves over vanilla 3DGS by +0.66 PSNR and −0.0197 LPIPS, suggesting that even tiny CoC modeling at small apertures contributes to sharper reconstructions.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoCoGaussian sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) 3D scene reconstruction from degraded images, initiated by Deblur-NeRF and extended by rasterization-based methods like Deblurring 3DGS and BAGS, and (2) physically-grounded depth-of-field modeling, previously explored only in the implicit NeRF setting by DoF-NeRF. The key departure is transplanting the Circle of Confusion formulation from DoF-NeRF into the 3DGS rasterization pipeline, generating multiple Gaussians per base Gaussian to explicitly shape the CoC, while borrowing the adaptive scaling/rotation mechanism from Deblurring 3DGS and the weighted-sum image combination from Deblur-NeRF. This is, to the authors' framing, the first method to combine photographic defocus physics with real-time Gaussian splatting rendering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is twofold: it enables real-time (rasterization-based) rendering of depth-of-field effects, which DoF-NeRF could not achieve due to ray tracing, and it provides post-hoc customization of aperture and focus plane without retraining—useful for AR/VR applications. The main limitation acknowledged is that the adaptive CoC scaling factor β only handles overestimated CoC diameters (reflective/refractive surfaces), not underestimation, leaving a gap for complex optical scenes. An open question is whether the learned aperture parameter K generalizes across different camera hardware or is tightly coupled to the training dataset's optical characteristics.
