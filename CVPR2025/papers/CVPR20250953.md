## Problem

CLIP-style vision encoders used in most MLLMs produce a single high-level image representation that captures overall scene semantics but misses pixel- and region-level details critical for tasks like OCR, grounding, and fine-grained perception. The paper asks whether a single generative vision model can produce multiple task-specific visual features, and how to effectively fuse and align these diverse features with a pretrained LLM.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250953/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (processed by DaViT into visual tokens) and three task-specific text prompts (detailed caption, OCR, dense region caption) as input. Florence-2's encoder-decoder processes each prompt-image pair to produce task-conditioned visual features, which are channel-concatenated with lower-level DaViT features and projected via an MLP into the LLM's embedding space as 576 visual tokens. The LLM (Phi-3.5 or Llama-3) then generates a free-form text response such as a caption, VQA answer, or OCR transcription.

## Method

Florence-VL replaces the standard CLIP vision encoder with Florence-2, a generative vision foundation model whose encoder-decoder architecture can produce task-conditioned visual features via different prompts. The core mechanism is Depth-Breadth Fusion (DBFusion): 'Depth' combines lower-level DaViT features (V) with higher-level encoder-decoder features (V′) to capture multiple granularity levels, while 'Breadth' extracts three distinct visual representations using prompts for detailed captioning, OCR, and dense region grounding. All four feature sets [V, V′_t1, V′_t2, V′_t3] are fused via simple channel concatenation (chosen over token concatenation and average pooling based on ablation), then projected to LLM space by an MLP. Training follows a two-stage recipe: end-to-end pretraining on large-scale detailed caption data (CC12M, RedCaps, CommonPool with PixelProse captions) to align vision and language, followed by finetuning of the projection layer and LLM on curated instruction-tuning data (Cambrian-7M, Vision-Flan, ShareGPT4V, Docmatix).

## Results

Florence-VL 8B (576 visual tokens) achieves 76.2 on MMBench(EN), 89.9 on POPE, 56.3 on MM-Vet, and 74.2 on TextVQA, outperforming all 8B baselines on the majority of 25 benchmarks. On OCR & Chart tasks, Florence-VL 8B scores 63.4 on OCRBench, 74.7 on ChartQA, and 84.9 on DocVQA, surpassing Cambrian 8B (62.4, 73.3, 77.8) and LLaVA Next 8B (55.2, 69.3, 78.2). The 3B variant achieves 71.6 on MMBench(EN) and 88.3 on POPE, remaining competitive with Phi 3.5 Vision despite using far less training data (open-source vs. 500B proprietary tokens). Florence-VL uses only 576 visual tokens, compared to 2880 for LLaVA Next and Mini-Gemini-HD, while matching or exceeding their performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Cambrian 8B (the strongest 8B baseline using multiple vision encoders), Florence-VL 8B wins on 10 of 12 reported benchmarks, with the largest margins on MMVP (73.3 vs. 51.3), MathVista (55.5 vs. 49.0), and DocVQA (84.9 vs. 77.8), while achieving this with a single vision encoder versus Cambrian's multi-encoder design. Compared to LLaVA Next 8B (2880 visual tokens), Florence-VL 8B (576 tokens) surpasses it on every reported benchmark, e.g., MMVP 73.3 vs. 38.7, MathVista 55.5 vs. 37.4, and TextVQA 74.2 vs. 65.4, using roughly 5× fewer visual tokens. Against Mini-Gemini-HD 8B, Florence-VL 8B leads on all 12 benchmarks, with particularly large gaps on MMVP (73.3 vs. 18.7) and OCRBench (63.4 vs. 47.7). In the 3B comparison, Florence-VL 3B outperforms Phi 3.5 Vision on 12 of 24 tasks (e.g., TextVQA 69.1 vs. 61.1, DocVQA 82.1 vs. 75.9) despite Phi 3.5 Vision being trained on 500B proprietary tokens. The controlled comparison with LLaVA 1.5 (identical training data, same LLM backbones) confirms the architecture advantage: Florence-VL 8B improves over LLaVA 1.5 8B by +4.5 on TextVQA (68.0 vs. 45.4), +18.8 on OCRBench (63.4 vs. 44.6), and +15.8 on ChartQA (74.7 vs. 58.9), demonstrating that Florence-2's OCR and grounding features are critical for text-centric image understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant LLaVA-style paradigm of pairing a contrastively-pretrained vision transformer (CLIP/SigLIP) with an LLM, instead repurposing the generative vision foundation model Florence-2 as a prompt-conditioned encoder that yields multiple task-specific visual features (caption, OCR, grounding) from a single backbone. Conceptually, it sits between the single-encoder MLLM line (LLaVA, Phi-3.5-Vision) and the multi-encoder mixture line (Cambrian, Brave, MouSi), arguing that one generative model with diverse prompts can replicate the breadth that multiple specialized encoders provide. The DBFusion mechanism—channel concatenation of features from different encoder depths (DaViT low-level vs. encoder-decoder high-level) and different prompt breadths—builds on standard multi-scale feature fusion ideas but applies them in the novel context of prompt-conditioned generative vision features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that MLLM builders can replace a CLIP encoder with a generative vision model and a simple channel-concatenation fusion to gain meaningful improvements across OCR, chart, and perception benchmarks without the compute overhead of multiple vision encoders. The cross-modal alignment loss diagnostic (Section 4) offers a lightweight, training-free way to compare vision encoders before committing to full MLLM training. Open questions include whether the fixed three-prompt breadth can be made task-adaptive, whether the approach extends naturally to high-resolution tiling (which the authors note is compatible but untested), and whether the alignment advantage of Florence-2 persists at larger model scales or with different LLM backbones beyond Phi-3.5 and Llama-3.
