## Problem

Existing instruction-based image editing methods face two key limitations: (1) training datasets are low-resolution, lack background consistency, and use overly simplistic template-based instructions that fail to challenge complex reasoning; (2) current approaches condition primarily on text embeddings while underexploiting the rich visual semantics of the source image, resulting in poor performance on complex instruction following and background preservation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251868/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a high-resolution source image (originally ~2K from Pexels, processed at 1024×1024) and a free-form text editing instruction that may include reasoning elements (e.g., 'What's hanging up in the image? Please remove it.') or spatial/color/number references. It produces a single edited target image where only the specified region is modified while the background remains consistent with the source. Instructions span three task types—removal, addition, and replacement—and can be either simple (synonym-level) or advanced (requiring multi-step reasoning about object identity and scene context).

## Method

InsightEdit employs a three-module architecture: a comprehension module (LLaVA-7B with r special [MM] tokens trained via LoRA to jointly encode image and instruction), a two-stream bridging module, and a diffusion-based generation module. The bridging module's textual branch uses a Q-Former to extract reasoned text features from [MM] hidden states, followed by a BIM module for bidirectional image-text interaction; the novel image branch uses an Image Alignment Adapter (IAA) that maps [MM] hidden states via an MLP to a 768-d embedding supervised by CLIP features of the target image, then expands it to N=4 tokens for cross-attention. In the generation module, a decoupled cross-attention mechanism (inspired by IP-Adapter) separately injects text and image conditions into the UNet, with a tunable weight λ on the image branch at inference. The approach is trained on 2.5M+ pairs from the AdvancedEdit dataset, constructed via an automated pipeline that uses GPT-4o for captioning and instruction recaptioning, GroundedSAM for mask extraction, and mask-based editors (BrushNet, Power-Paint) for high-fidelity target generation.

## Results

On AdvancedEdit-Eval, InsightEdit achieves a VIEScore of 0.738, CLIPScore of 20.395, and LPIPS of 0.112, outperforming all five baselines on instruction-following and perceptual quality metrics. When further trained on the AdvancedEdit dataset, the model improves to VIEScore 0.831, CLIPScore 21.002, PSNR 22.871, SSIM 0.716, and LPIPS 0.071, achieving the best score on every metric. On the Reason-Edit benchmark, InsightEdit with AdvancedEdit reaches VIEScore 0.934 (understanding) and 0.947 (reasoning), surpassing SmartEdit-7B (0.866 / 0.835) by a substantial margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InsightEdit outperforms all five baselines on VIEScore (0.738 vs. 0.682 for the strongest competitor SmartEdit-7B) and CLIPScore (20.395 vs. 20.114), confirming superior instruction following. On background-consistency metrics, MagicBrush retains a slight edge in PSNR (22.636 vs. 21.267) and SSIM (0.743 vs. 0.675) over the base InsightEdit model, but InsightEdit trained on AdvancedEdit surpasses MagicBrush on both (PSNR 22.871, SSIM 0.716) while also achieving the lowest LPIPS (0.071 vs. 0.132). InstructDiffusion and InstructPix2Pix trail significantly on all metrics, with VIEScores below 0.35. On the Reason-Edit benchmark, InsightEdit with AdvancedEdit leads on VIEScore in both understanding (0.934 vs. 0.866) and reasoning (0.947 vs. 0.835) scenarios, though SmartEdit-7B edges it out by a narrow margin on CLIPScore in reasoning tasks (20.950 vs. 20.867 for base InsightEdit). Overall, the two-stream bridging mechanism and the AdvancedEdit dataset together yield consistent gains across every evaluated metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

InsightEdit extends the SmartEdit line of work, which first introduced MLLM-reasoned text embeddings as conditioning for diffusion-based image editing, by adding a parallel image-feature stream (the IAA module) that injects MLLM-reasoned visual semantics into the denoising process via decoupled cross-attention, a design borrowed from IP-Adapter. On the data side, it departs from prior datasets (InstructPix2Pix, HIVE, UltraEdit) that relied on Prompt2Prompt or low-resolution generation by using a mask-based editing pipeline (BrushNet, Power-Paint) to produce high-fidelity 1024² pairs with complex, reasoning-oriented instructions. The overall architecture thus sits at the intersection of the MLLM-conditioned editing paradigm (SmartEdit, MGIE) and the image-prompt conditioning paradigm (IP-Adapter), unifying both as complementary streams.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The AdvancedEdit dataset (2.5M+ pairs at 1024² with complex instructions and verified background consistency) is a substantial practical resource that addresses a widely acknowledged gap in instruction-based editing benchmarks. The two-stream design offers a clean, modular recipe—MLLM reasons over both text and image, and the diffusion model consumes both—making it straightforward to swap in stronger MLLMs or additional conditioning signals. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-step or compositional editing instructions, whether the fixed IAA supervision from the target image limits flexibility at inference, and whether the gains over SmartEdit persist as MLLM comprehension improves further.
