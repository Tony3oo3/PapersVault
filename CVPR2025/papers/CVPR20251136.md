## Problem

Existing loss-based and gradient-based multi-task learning methods still exhibit significant task imbalance on standard benchmarks, with some tasks consistently under-optimized relative to single-task baselines. The paper identifies that performance-level information—specifically the variance of per-task performance drops—has been overlooked as a fairness indicator, despite showing a striking positive correlation with overall MTL performance. This motivates incorporating performance variance as an explicit optimization target to achieve fairer gradient aggregation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-task gradients computed from diverse modalities (RGB images for scene understanding, molecular graphs for QM9, robot observations for MetaWorld) along with STL baseline metrics that serve as a reference for computing per-task performance drops Δm_i. These performance drops are evaluated on a validation set (or training set when no validation split exists) and updated periodically during training to refresh the dynamic weights. The output is a single aggregated gradient direction d for updating shared parameters, which is then scaled by a step size derived from the KKT solution.

## Method

PIVRG formulates gradient aggregation as minimizing the mean of inverse weighted utilities (1/k)Σ ω_i/(g_i^T d), where the weights ω are computed via a temperature-scaled softmax of per-task performance drops Δm_i, ensuring they are positively correlated with task difficulty, sum to k, and remain bounded. The temperature parameter τ controls weight smoothness and is chosen to satisfy the boundedness constraint. The optimal update direction d is derived analytically via KKT conditions, reducing to a nonlinear least-squares problem (G^T G α)² = ω/α solved with scipy. The paper proves convergence to a Pareto stationary point under standard smoothness and linear-independence assumptions, with a step size η_t = Σ√(ω_i/α_i) / (kL Σ√(ω_i α_i)). The approach is orthogonal to existing methods and can be plugged into loss-based or gradient-based frameworks to reduce performance variance while improving overall performance.

## Results

On NYUv2 (3-task), PIVRG achieves a Mean Rank of 2.33 and an average performance drop of −6.50%, outperforming the previous best FairGrad (MR 3.44, Δm −4.66%) and Nash-MTL (MR 3.67, Δm −4.04%). On CityScapes (2-task), PIVRG is the only method to achieve a negative Δm (−0.54%) with MR 1.50, versus FairGrad's MR 2.25 and Δm +5.18%. On the high-task-count benchmarks, PIVRG reaches MR 3.15 / Δm −0.96% on CelebA (40-task) and MR 3.00 / Δm 33.6% on QM9 (11-task), both substantially ahead of FairGrad (MR 6.38 / 0.37% and MR 4.73 / 57.9% respectively). On the MT10 reinforcement learning benchmark (10-task), PIVRG attains a 96% success rate, surpassing Nash-MTL (91%) and FairGrad (84%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PIVRG outperforms all 13 baselines on every benchmark for both Mean Rank and Δm. Against the strongest gradient-based competitors, PIVRG improves MR from 3.44 to 2.33 and Δm from −4.66% to −6.50% over FairGrad on NYUv2, and from 2.25 to 1.50 (MR) and +5.18% to −0.54% (Δm) over FairGrad on CityScapes. Nash-MTL, the closest competitor on NYUv2 (MR 3.67, Δm −4.04%), is surpassed by PIVRG by 1.34 MR and 2.46% Δm. On the 40-task CelebA benchmark, PIVRG nearly halves the MR of the next-best method (3.15 vs. 5.70 for IMTL-G) and is the first to achieve a negative Δm (−0.96%). On QM9, where loss-scale imbalance is most severe, PIVRG reduces Δm from 57.9% (FairGrad) to 33.6%, a 42% relative improvement. In the MT10 RL setting, PIVRG reaches 96% success rate, exceeding Nash-MTL (91%) and FairGrad (84%) by 5 and 12 percentage points respectively. Loss-based methods (LS, DWA, UW, RLW) consistently underperform gradient-based ones, with Δm values ranging from +3.57% to +7.78% on NYUv2, confirming the paper's motivation for gradient-level aggregation.

## Relation to Prior Work

PIVRG is positioned as a gradient-based MTL method whose core optimization objective (minimizing the mean of inverse task utilities) is a specific case of the FairGrad framework [4], while its utility formulation deliberately departs from Nash-MTL [30] by minimizing the mean of inverse utilities rather than maximizing the sum of log-utilities. The key conceptual departure from both loss-based and gradient-based prior work is the introduction of performance-level information—specifically cross-task performance variance—as an explicit fairness indicator, which neither loss scales nor gradient magnitudes capture. The paper further distinguishes itself from prior performance-aware methods [16, 41] by incorporating gradient-level information and enforcing cross-task weight constraints that directly target variance reduction, rather than merely using per-task accuracy to set loss weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstrated positive correlation between cross-task performance variance and overall performance drop provides a practical diagnostic for MTL practitioners: methods that reduce variance tend to achieve better aggregate performance, a connection previously overlooked. The orthogonality of the dynamic weighting strategy—showing it can be plugged into both loss-based (LS, DWA, UW) and gradient-based (MGDA, Nash-MTL) methods to improve them—suggests broad applicability beyond PIVRG itself. Open questions include whether performance variance is the optimal fairness indicator (the authors acknowledge alternatives exist), whether the method's reliance on STL baselines for computing Δm_i limits its use in settings where strong single-task baselines are unavailable, and whether the temperature-scaled softmax weighting generalizes to non-convex or highly heterogeneous task sets beyond the tested benchmarks.
