## Problem

Existing VLM-based autonomous driving systems (e.g., DriveVLM) integrate vision-language models with end-to-end planners only at the trajectory level via post-processing, preventing feature-level knowledge sharing: the E2E model cannot exploit the VLM's high-level reasoning about critical objects, and the VLM cannot leverage the E2E model's 3D perception for precise scene comprehension. Additionally, VLMs are too computationally expensive for real-time inference, and they struggle to directly generate fine-grained trajectories in an auto-regressive manner.

## Input / Output

The system consumes 6 surround-view camera images (encoded by EVA02-L), ego vehicle status (velocity, acceleration), historical trajectory, and a discrete navigation command (forward, left-turn, right-turn). The VLM branch outputs a refined future trajectory via a two-stage T-CoT process (selection from a k-means-clustered trajectory bank of 36 candidates per command, then waypoint refinement), while the E2E branch outputs a real-time trajectory. VLM trajectories are stored in a memory buffer and asynchronously retrieved by the E2E planner as initialization priors with a temporal offset, decoupling the high-latency VLM from the real-time E2E loop.

## Method

SOLVE introduces a Sequential Q-Former (SQ-Former) that sequentially compresses three groups of visual cues—static scene context, dynamic objects (900 queries), and dynamic lane maps (300 queries)—into 384 shared collector tokens via interleaved self-attention and cross-attention decoders with a temporal memory bank, enabling feature-level knowledge sharing between the VLM (LLaVA-7B with LoRA) and the E2E planner. For trajectory prediction, a Trajectory Chain-of-Thought (T-CoT) mechanism replaces direct auto-regressive waypoint generation: a trajectory adapter encodes candidate trajectories from a k-means bank into single tokens for a selection step, then encodes the selected reference trajectory's waypoints into tokens for a refinement step, reducing numerical prediction uncertainty. A temporal decoupling strategy stores VLM-predicted long-horizon trajectories in memory, which the E2E planner asynchronously retrieves as additional planning-query initializations, maintaining real-time operation while benefiting from VLM quality. Training proceeds in five stages: QA pre-training with LoRA, trajectory adapter training, E2E head training (frozen SQ-Former), joint VLM+E2E optimization with T-CoT, and finally async E2E refinement using historical VLM trajectories.

## Results

On the nuScenes open-loop planning benchmark, SOLVE-VLM achieves the best average L2 error of 0.28 m and the lowest average collision rate of 0.20%, outperforming all VLM-based and E2E baselines. SOLVE-E2E (the end-to-end branch alone) reaches 0.31 m L2 and 0.30% collision, matching DriveVLM-Dual's L2 while adding collision-rate reporting. The async variant (SOLVE-E2E Async), which uses VLM trajectories from prior frames as initialization priors, further reduces L2 to 0.30 m and collision to 0.29%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among E2E baselines, SOLVE-E2E reduces average L2 error by 0.15 m over UniAD (0.46→0.31), 0.06 m over VAD-Base (0.37→0.31), and 0.04 m over BEV-Planner++ (0.35→0.31), while also lowering collision rates by 0.07%, 0.03%, and 0.07% respectively. SOLVE-E2E ties DriveVLM-Dual on L2 (both 0.31 m) but additionally reports collision metrics, and the paper attributes this parity to the shared SQ-Former encoder providing richer scene understanding than detection-only supervision. Among VLM-based methods, SOLVE-VLM surpasses OmniDrive by 0.05 m in L2 (0.33→0.28) and by 0.10% in collision rate (0.30→0.20), and beats DriveVLM by 0.03 m in L2 (0.31→0.28), all using comparable 7B-parameter language models (LLaVA-1.5-7B vs. QwenVL-7.7B). The temporal-decoupling strategy (SOLVE-E2E Async) yields a further 0.01 m L2 improvement over SOLVE-E2E alone, confirming that asynchronous VLM trajectory priors benefit the real-time E2E planner without adding inference latency.

## Relation to Prior Work

SOLVE extends the VLM-plus-E2E paradigm initiated by DriveVLM, which first coupled a VLM with an end-to-end planner at the trajectory level only. The key departure is introducing feature-level synergy through a shared Sequential Q-Former (SQ-Former) that compresses multi-view images into unified visual tokens consumed by both the VLM (LLaVA-7B) and the E2E planner, enabling joint training and cross-branch knowledge transfer. The Trajectory Chain-of-Thought (T-CoT) mechanism refines the broader CoT-for-driving line (DriveCoT, DriveLM) by restricting reasoning to the trajectory level with a k-means trajectory bank, reducing output length. The temporal decoupling strategy builds on asynchronous VLM-E2E cooperation but operates at the trajectory level rather than the feature level used in prior work [9].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SOLVE offers a practical recipe for integrating VLMs into real-time driving stacks: a shared encoder avoids redundant feature extraction, T-CoT tames the auto-regressive cost of trajectory generation, and temporal decoupling lets a slow VLM asynchronously seed a fast E2E planner. However, all results are open-loop on a single dataset (nuScenes), with no closed-loop or multi-dataset validation, leaving open whether the synergy generalizes to distribution shifts or long-horizon driving. The modest margins over OmniDrive and DriveVLM-Dual (0.03–0.05 m L2) suggest the shared-encoder benefit, while interesting, is still small relative to the overall error budget, and the approach inherits the interpretability and safety limitations of open-loop evaluation.
