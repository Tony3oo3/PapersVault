## Problem

Existing 3D-VLMs rely on task-agnostic object segmentation to represent scenes, which introduces redundant information and loses fine-grained details in task-relevant areas when facing large, high-density 3D scenes. Additionally, current benchmarks are confined to single-room scenarios (~29 m²), leaving cross-room and large-scale scene understanding largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251922/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a dense 3D point cloud (40k points randomly sampled per scene) and a natural language instruction as input. The point cloud is first encoded into dense features, then downsampled to sparse vision tokens for coarse understanding; dense tokens are selectively extracted only from identified regions of interest. Outputs are autoregressive text: question answers, room-level scene captions, or hierarchical planning subtasks, evaluated with CIDEr, METEOR, ROUGE, and accuracy.

## Method

LSceneLLM introduces a two-level adaptive framework built on Llama2-7b. A coarse scene understanding module (OpenScene encoder + PointNet++ SA module) converts downsampled point clouds into sparse vision tokens. The key novelty is a plug-and-play scene magnifier module that replaces the last N_MA self-attention layers of the LLM: a dense token selector reads the LLM's own attention map over sparse vision tokens, activates the top 10–20% most relevant tokens, and retrieves dense point cloud features from those spatial regions; an adaptive self-attention mechanism then fuses these dense tokens with the hidden state by concatenating their K/V projections with those of the text and sparse tokens, while an attention mask restricts each text token to attend only to its selected dense tokens. This design lets the LLM's reasoning drive region selection without external query modules like QFormer, and the magnifier can be dropped into existing 3D-VLMs (Leo, LL3DA) by simply swapping their self-attention layers.

## Results

On the introduced XR-Scene benchmark (average scene area 132 m²), LSceneLLM achieves CIDEr 117.21 / METEOR 38.18 / ROUGE 19.30 on XR-QA, CIDEr 4.59 / METEOR 23.43 / ROUGE 16.16 on XR-SceneCaption, and CIDEr 63.08 / METEOR 22.97 / ROUGE 36.96 on XR-EmbodiedPlanning, outperforming the strongest baseline (LL3DA) by 4.4 CIDEr on XR-QA and 27.1 CIDEr on XR-EmbodiedPlanning. On the single-room ScanQA benchmark, LSceneLLM reaches CIDEr 88.24 / METEOR 17.95 / ROUGE 40.82, surpassing the previous best (Leo, CIDEr 80.20) by 8.0 CIDEr. On the outdoor Nuscenes-QA benchmark, LSceneLLM attains 56.4% overall accuracy, exceeding the best generative method (OccLLaMA, 53.4%) by 3.0 points without requiring multi-view image input.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LSceneLLM outperforms all three main 3D-VLM baselines (Chat-Scene, Leo, LL3DA) across every metric on the XR-Scene benchmark. The largest margin is on XR-EmbodiedPlanning, where LSceneLLM's CIDEr of 63.08 exceeds LL3DA (35.96) by 27.1 and Chat-Scene (46.18) by 16.9, reflecting the advantage of adaptive region-level understanding over object-level modeling for cross-room planning. On XR-QA, the gap is smaller (117.21 vs. 114.10 for Chat-Scene) but consistent across all three metrics. On the single-room ScanQA benchmark, LSceneLLM (CIDEr 88.24) beats Leo (80.20) and LL3DA-Llama2 (79.08) by 8+ CIDEr, confirming the method generalizes beyond large scenes. On the outdoor Nuscenes-QA benchmark, LSceneLLM (56.4% Acc) surpasses all generative baselines including OccLLaMA (53.4%) and LidarLLM (48.6%), though it remains below the task-specific NuscenesQA specialist (58.1%). The plug-and-play scene magnifier module also improves LL3DA by 4.44 CIDEr on XR-QA and 11.09 on the fine-grained XR-QA-S subset, validating its portability to existing 3D-VLM architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D-VLM lineage from object-level understanding (PointLLM, MiniGPT-3D, Point-Bind) to scene-level understanding, departing from the dominant object-centric paradigm (Chat-Scene, Leo) that segments all objects and feeds task-agnostic features to the LLM. While LL3DA introduced QFormer-based task-relevant feature extraction, it operates outside the LLM and relies on shallow query mechanisms. LSceneLLM instead leverages the LLM's own attention maps to identify visual preferences and introduces a plug-and-play scene magnifier module that zooms into task-relevant regions, repurposing the LLM's internal reasoning as a spatial attention mechanism rather than an external selector.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of XR-Scene (average 132 m² vs. 29 m² in ScanQA) addresses a critical gap in 3D-VLM evaluation, as cross-room and large-scale scene understanding has been largely unbenchmarked. The plug-and-play scene magnifier module offers a practical path to improve existing 3D-VLMs without architectural redesign, with particularly notable gains on fine-grained object queries (XR-QA-S). Open questions include whether the attention-based region selection generalizes to even larger or outdoor scenes, whether full LLM fine-tuning is necessary, and how the approach scales when the number of rooms or objects grows substantially beyond the HM3D-based evaluation.
