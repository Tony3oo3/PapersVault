## Problem

Existing attention-based transformers for multi-view 3D human pose estimation overfit to specific training camera arrangements and struggle to model the spatial arrangement of keypoints, especially under occlusion. No prior work has integrated state space models into multi-view 3D geometry modeling, leaving a gap in how to efficiently capture joint spatial sequences across views without the quadratic cost of full cross-attention over all image tokens.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250388/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes T synchronized RGB images (one per camera) along with known camera projection matrices, and produces per-person 3D keypoint coordinates via differentiable algebraic triangulation. Internally, each person is represented as a joint token combining a visual feature term (J×L, decomposed into person-level and keypoint-level sub-tokens) and a geometry term (J×3). Each PSS layer additionally outputs per-view 2D residual offsets and confidence scores that iteratively refine the triangulated 3D positions through progressive regression.

## Method

MV-SSM stacks Projective State Space (PSS) blocks, each combining projective attention (which samples local context around projected keypoint anchors in each view) with a state space model (SS2D) that scans along the joint dimension to capture inter-joint spatial relationships. The key architectural innovation is Grid Token-guided Bidirectional Scanning (GTBS), which restricts the Mamba scan to only the sampled projection tokens from each view rather than all image patches, making the scan both computationally efficient and semantically aligned with the joint spatial sequence. A ResNet-50 backbone extracts multi-scale features, and M stacked PSS blocks progressively refine keypoint estimates via residual 2D offsets and confidence-weighted differentiable algebraic triangulation. Token filtering uses a learned classifier with anchor-based matching to distinguish positive (real person) from negative (empty) tokens, with NMS applied layer-wise.

## Results

On CMU Panoptic (in-domain), MV-SSM achieves 93.5 AP₂₅ and 15.7 mm MPJPE, outperforming the prior SOTA MVGFormer (92.3 AP₂₅, 16.0 mm MPJPE) and MvP (92.3 AP₂₅, 15.8 mm MPJPE). In cross-camera evaluation with only 3 cameras, MV-SSM gains +10.8 AP₂₅ over MVGFormer (55.4 vs. 44.6), and in cross-arrangement testing it achieves +7.0 AP₂₅ on the challenging CMU3 setting. Cross-dataset transfer to Campus (no fine-tuning) yields +15.3 PCP on Actor 1 and +9.2 average PCP over MVGFormer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MV-SSM outperforms all seven baselines on in-domain CMU Panoptic, achieving 93.5 AP₂₅ vs. 92.3 for both MVGFormer and MvP, and 15.7 mm MPJPE (best among all methods). The most dramatic gains appear in generalization settings: on the 3-camera cross-camera test, MV-SSM scores 55.4 AP₂₅ versus 44.6 for MVGFormer (+10.8) and 12.3 for MvP, while MvP collapses to 0.0 AP₂₅ on 6- and 7-camera configurations due to overfitting. In cross-arrangement evaluation, MV-SSM leads MVGFormer by +7.0 AP₂₅ on CMU3 and +3.9 on average, whereas MvP and VoxelPose degrade severely (MvP scores 0.0 across all cross-arrangement splits). On cross-dataset Campus, MV-SSM achieves 67.3 average PCP vs. 58.1 for MVGFormer and 11.2 for VoxelPose; on Shelf the gap narrows (88.0 vs. 87.9) as scores saturate. Geometry-based Dong et al. [12] remains competitive on raw PCP for Campus/Shelf but fails on cross-camera AP₂₅ (≤0.2) due to brittle cross-view matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

MV-SSM sits at the intersection of two research threads: attention-based multi-view pose estimators (MvP, MVGFormer) that model cross-view keypoint relationships via projective attention, and visual state space models (Mamba, VMamba/SS2D) that capture long-range spatial dependencies with linear-time scanning. The paper's core departure is repurposing SSMs—originally designed for 1D temporal sequences or 2D image patches—to model the *joint spatial sequence* of a human skeleton across multiple static views, a use case neither Mamba nor VMamba addressed. It inherits projective attention and the hierarchical token scheme from MvP, and differentiable algebraic triangulation from Iskakov et al., but replaces the attention-only fusion with a hybrid PSS block that combines projective attention with a modified SS2D scanning mechanism (GTBS) tailored to keypoint-level tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in generalization: existing transformer-based methods (MvP, MVGFormer) degrade sharply when camera count or arrangement changes, limiting deployment in real-world settings where camera setups vary. MV-SSM's +10.8 AP25 gain on the 3-camera setting and +15.3 PCP on cross-dataset Campus A1 demonstrate that SSM-based joint-sequence modeling can mitigate this overfitting without fine-tuning. Key limitations remain: the model fails when a person is visible in only one camera, and the initial keypoint positions are sampled from a T-pose prior rather than learned. Open questions include whether the GTBS scanning strategy transfers to other articulated-body tasks (e.g., hand or object pose) and whether generative or semi-supervised approaches can fill in occluded keypoints more robustly than the current normal-distribution initialization.
