## Problem

Self-supervised 3D scene models have failed to produce off-the-shelf features comparable to supervised models, unlike their 2D counterparts (e.g., DINOv2). The paper identifies two root causes: the absence of an evaluation protocol that properly captures the hierarchical nature of 3D scene models, and the lack of a masked prediction objective tailored to hierarchical 3D architectures that avoids information leakage from geometric cues.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250515/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud scene, voxelizes it into sparse occupied voxels, and applies two augmented views with random cropping and consistent multi-level masking. The output is a set of hierarchical point features obtained by trilinearly interpolating and concatenating feature maps from every decoder level, rather than using only the final layer. These frozen features are then used directly (without fine-tuning) for semantic segmentation, instance segmentation via PointGroup displacement vectors, and 3D visual grounding via cross-attention with text embeddings.

## Method

Masked Scene Modeling performs bottom-up hierarchical masked feature reconstruction: masked voxels are removed from the sparse encoder input but reinserted at each decoder level with level-specific learnable tokens, preventing geometric information leakage from neighboring voxels. The model reconstructs deep features (not raw coordinates or colors) of masked patches using per-level predictor MLPs, supervised by an EMA-updated teacher that sees the full unmasked scene. Cross-view reconstruction (view 1 predicts view 2's targets and vice versa) enforces view invariance. The architecture (HUNet) is a sparse-convolution UNet with Multi-Head Attention blocks in the last two encoder/decoder levels, and consistent masking across hierarchy levels with patch size fixed to the coarsest voxel resolution ensures stable training across masking ratios.

## Results

On semantic segmentation (mIoU, Linear protocol), the proposed Masked Scene Modeling with HUNet achieves 68.7 on ScanNet, 26.8 on ScanNet200, and 59.5 on S3DIS, surpassing the best self-supervised baseline (MSC with HUNet) by +10.5, +6.5, and +1.8 points respectively. Under the Nearest-Neighbor protocol, the gains are even larger: 65.7 vs. 39.9 on ScanNet (+25.8). On 3D visual grounding (ScanRefer), the method reaches 55.7% overall accuracy, outperforming the supervised SR-UNet (44.3%) and all self-supervised baselines by at least +4.7 points. On instance segmentation (mAP@50), it achieves 44.4 on ScanNet, more than doubling the best baseline (MSC HUNet at 24.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four 3D self-supervised baselines (CSC, MSC, MM3D, OESSL), the proposed method wins decisively on all datasets and both evaluation protocols. On ScanNet semantic segmentation (Linear), it outperforms OESSL (35.4) by +33.3, CSC (27.3) by +41.4, and MM3D (26.7) by +42.0 mIoU. Even when the best baseline MSC is retrained with the same HUNet architecture (58.2), the proposed objective still yields +10.5 mIoU, confirming the gain comes from the Masked Scene Modeling loss rather than architecture alone. On 3D visual grounding, the method surpasses MSC-HUNet (51.0 overall) by +4.7 and even exceeds the supervised SR-UNet (44.3) by +11.4. Against 2D foundation models (DINOv2, LSeg, CLIP, Stable Diffusion), the 3D-native model outperforms all on semantic segmentation (68.7 vs. 62.8 for DINOv2) and matches or exceeds them on visual grounding (52.7 overall vs. 52.0 for DINOv2), though LSeg edges it slightly on the Unique split (88.1 vs. 87.1). The method narrows the gap to fully supervised HUNet (77.0) to within 8.3 mIoU on ScanNet, whereas the best prior self-supervised method (MSC-HUNet) trails by 18.8.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Masked Image Modeling paradigm from 2D vision (MAE, DINOv2, SimMIM) to 3D scene understanding, but departs from existing 3D MIM methods (MSC, MM3D) in two critical ways: it reconstructs deep teacher features rather than shallow input attributes (color, normals, coordinates), and it performs bottom-up hierarchical reconstruction across all decoder levels rather than only at the final resolution. The evaluation protocol also breaks from the standard fine-tuning paradigm used in prior 3D self-supervised work (CSC, OESSL, MM3D) by introducing hierarchical feature extraction via trilinear interpolation, drawing on the linear-probing and nearest-neighbor evaluation conventions established in 2D self-supervised learning (DINOv2, iBOT). The architecture (HUNet) combines sparse convolutional UNet blocks with multi-head attention in the deepest levels, borrowing serialization strategies from PTv3 and window attention from Stable Diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first 3D self-supervised model whose off-the-shelf features match or exceed supervised models on semantic segmentation, instance segmentation, and visual grounding, shifting the practical use of 3D self-supervised pre-training from a weight-initialization step to a viable feature-extraction alternative. The hierarchical evaluation protocol is itself a field-level contribution: the pilot study reveals that prior methods' features were systematically under-evaluated by last-layer-only extraction, meaning the true gap between supervised and self-supervised 3D models may be smaller than previously reported. Key limitations include training on a single dataset (ScanNet only), which constrains generalization, and the supervised HUNet baseline for visual grounding failed to converge, leaving that comparison incomplete. Scaling to larger multi-dataset pre-training is the natural next step to close the remaining gap with 2D foundation models.
