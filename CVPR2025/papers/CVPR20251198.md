## Problem

Recovering structured 3D building representations from sparse, noisy, and incomplete point clouds is difficult because traditional plane-detection pipelines (RANSAC, region growing) fail under low density, and direct neural mapping from points to meshes is unstable due to the coupling of geometry and connectivity. No prior program-based or neurosymbolic method had addressed structured abstraction from such degraded architectural point clouds, with existing inverse procedural approaches relying on brittle optimization or template instantiation.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input an extremely sparse point cloud of roughly 200–2000 points per building (as few as ~300 in the teaser), which may be non-uniform, noisy, and incomplete, and produces a tokenized architectural program that is deterministically compiled into a clean, low-face-count 3D mesh. The program encodes a hierarchical tree of layers, each specified by a height and a 2D polygonal contour, serialized via breadth-first traversal into a bijective token sequence. A finite state machine constrains token generation at inference to guarantee syntactic validity, and a post-processing geometry refinement step snaps child contours to parent edges for visual clarity.

## Method

ArcPro introduces a domain-specific language (DSL) that represents buildings as hierarchical architectural trees of layers (height + 2D contour), bridging feedforward and inverse procedural modeling by using a feedforward procedural generator to synthesize large-scale program–point-cloud training pairs and then learning the reverse mapping. The network pairs a 3D sparse convolutional encoder (ResNet-style, 128³→4³ voxel space, 512-d features) with a 12-layer transformer decoder that autoregressively predicts program tokens via next-token prediction loss, with point-cloud features injected through cross-attention. A finite state machine masks invalid tokens during inference to enforce syntactic correctness, and a learning-free geometry compiler translates the predicted program into a mesh. Training data is generated online by sampling root contours from 872k Bing Maps footprints and recursively spawning child layers via edge contraction or planar bisection, with data augmentation introducing sparsity, non-uniformity, incompleteness, and Perlin noise to match real-world degradation.

## Results

On the SfM point cloud dataset (270 buildings), ArcPro achieves the lowest Hausdorff Distance (HD = 0.0154) and Light Field Distance (LFD = 3873) among all methods, while producing the simplest meshes (64 vertices, 36 faces, 14 planes) at a 100% success rate. On the sparse sampling dataset (1,038 buildings from UrbanBIS), ArcPro again attains the best HD (0.0219) and LFD (4932) with the fewest geometric elements (27 vertices, 32 faces, 15 planes). In a user study with 110 participants, ArcPro receives a 76.7% preference rating, far exceeding the next-best method (ProxyRecon at 21.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ArcPro outperforms all five baselines on both HD and LFD across both evaluation datasets. Against traditional proxy reconstruction methods, PolyFit and KSR suffer severe success-rate drops (40% and 78% on SfM; 15% and 54% on sparse) due to plane-detection failures under sparsity, while ProxyRecon maintains 100% success but still trails ArcPro in HD (0.0243 vs. 0.0154 on SfM) and produces meshes with 3× more faces. Among learning-based methods, CA (cuboid primitives) and BSP-Net (plane primitives) both achieve 100% success but yield substantially higher HD (0.0363 and 0.0431 on SfM) and far more complex meshes (180 and 96 faces respectively vs. ArcPro's 36). In the user study, ArcPro's 76.7% preference dwarfs ProxyRecon's 21.0%, confirming that its structured program representation produces abstractions that are both geometrically accurate and aesthetically preferred over all alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

ArcPro draws its DSL design from classical procedural and grammar-based building modeling (Müller et al. 2006; Parish & Müller 2001) and its program-as-intermediate-representation paradigm from neurosymbolic 3D shape modeling (Ritchie et al. 2023; Jones et al. 2020, 2023). It departs from prior inverse procedural methods (Mathias et al. 2011; Toshev et al. 2010), which rely on hand-crafted optimization or template matching and fail under sparsity, by instead learning the inverse mapping end-to-end with a 3D sparse convolutional encoder and autoregressive transformer decoder. The key conceptual bridge is using feedforward procedural generation to synthesize training data, enabling the network to learn the reverse (points → program) direction without explicit geometric losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ArcPro demonstrates that a compact, architecturally-motivated program space can serve as an effective regularizer against noise and sparsity, producing clean structured abstractions from as few as ~200 points where traditional primitive-detection pipelines and flexible learning-based methods (BSP-Net, CA) degrade. The practical efficiency (1,090 buildings in ~37 s on a single GPU) makes it relevant for urban-scale digital twin and autonomous navigation pipelines. Open questions include whether the single-solution top-1 inference can be extended to capture structural ambiguity, whether the DSL can scale to curved or sloped geometries, and whether the framework generalizes to image or multi-modal inputs without point cloud segmentation.
