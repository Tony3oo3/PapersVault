## Problem

Existing diffusion-based data augmentation methods for image classification cannot simultaneously achieve faithfulness (preserving fine-grained category characteristics) and diversity (producing varied contexts). Methods that improve faithfulness (e.g., Da-Fusion) yield repetitive synthetic samples, while those that improve diversity (e.g., Diff-Mix) introduce label ambiguity by mixing across categories. This fundamental trade-off limits the generalization gains of downstream classifiers in data-scarce scenarios such as few-shot, long-tailed, and OOD classification.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small labeled training set (5 or 10 images per category on fine-grained benchmarks like CUB, Aircraft, Cars, Pet at 224×224 or 384×384 resolution) along with category labels. It first learns n concept token embeddings per category and shared LoRA matrices, then produces a synthetic image set (typically 5× expansion) that is combined with the original set for classifier training. Contextual suffixes for the two-stage denoising are extracted by captioning all training images with BLIP and summarizing them into a small set of descriptions via GPT-4.

## Method

Diff-II generates synthetic images through three stages. First, category concepts are learned by fine-tuning n token embeddings per category plus shared LoRA matrices in a pretrained diffusion U-Net via reconstruction of noised training images. Second, each training image is mapped to its DDIM inversion conditioned on the learned concept, and random pairs from the same category undergo circle interpolation (a unified formulation of spherical interpolation and extrapolation) to produce initial noise that remains in a Gaussian distribution while spanning a larger interpolation space than linear mixing. Third, a two-stage denoising process splits the reverse diffusion at ratio s: the early stage uses a suffixed prompt (learned concept + a randomly sampled context suffix) to inject diverse layouts and gestures, while the late stage switches to a plain prompt (concept only) to refine fine-grained category details. The split ratio s directly controls the faithfulness–diversity trade-off.

## Results

On few-shot fine-grained classification (5-shot, ResNet-50), Diff-II achieves 64.61% average accuracy across CUB, Aircraft, Pet, and Cars, improving over the original training set by +10.05% and outperforming the strongest baseline Diff-AUG (63.04%) by 1.57%. With ViT-B/16, the 5-shot average reaches 72.59% (+8.52% over original). On long-tail CUB-LT (IF=100), Diff-II achieves 51.21% accuracy, exceeding the previous best Diff-Mix (50.35%) by 0.86%. On OOD Waterbird classification, Diff-II attains 46.09% average accuracy, a +11.39% gain over the unaugmented baseline and +1.86% over the best baseline Diff-AUG (44.23%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Diff-II outperforms all seven baseline families across every experimental setting. Against the strongest diffusion-based competitor Diff-AUG [42], Diff-II leads by 1.57% on 5-shot ResNet-50 (64.61 vs. 63.04), 1.07% on 5-shot ViT-B/16 (72.59 vs. 71.52), and 0.93% on 10-shot ResNet-50 (75.78 vs. 74.85). Da-Fusion [39], which focuses on faithfulness via learned concepts, trails Diff-II by 5.88% on 5-shot ResNet-50 (58.73 vs. 64.61), confirming that faithfulness alone is insufficient. The inter-category methods Real-Mix and Diff-Mix [42] underperform the original training set on several datasets (e.g., Real-Mix drops to 53.56% on 5-shot ResNet-50 vs. 54.56% original), illustrating the faithfulness-diversity trade-off the paper identifies. On long-tail CUB-LT (IF=100), Diff-II achieves 51.21%, beating Diff-Mix (50.35%) by 0.86% and CMO-DRW [3] (32.57%) by a large margin. On OOD Waterbird, Diff-II's 46.09% average exceeds Diff-AUG (44.23%) by 1.86% and Da-Fusion (40.37%) by 5.72%, with the largest gap in the water-land group (51.01% vs. 48.71%). Traditional augmentation methods (Mixup, CutMix) provide only marginal gains (+0.15–0.16% on 5-shot ResNet-50) or even degrade performance, highlighting the advantage of diffusion-based generation for fine-grained data-scarce settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

Diff-II positions itself against the dominant 'image editing' paradigm in diffusion-based data augmentation (noising a reference image then denoising with a category prompt), which prior works like Real-Guidance [12], Da-Fusion [39], and Diff-Mix [42] all follow. The key departure is operating in the DDIM inversion space rather than the pixel/latent editing space: by computing inversions for all training images and performing circle interpolation between pairs from the same category, the method sidesteps the faithfulness-diversity trade-off that plagues intra-category (faithful but repetitive) and inter-category (diverse but semantically ambiguous) denoising. The learned category concepts (via LoRA + textual inversion) and the two-stage denoising with LLM-summarized suffixes are borrowed from and extend the Da-Fusion and Diff-Mix lines, but the interpolation-in-inversion-space step is the novel architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean conceptual framework (faithfulness vs. diversity as two orthogonal axes) that explains why prior diffusion-based DA methods plateau, and offers a practical recipe—circle interpolation in DDIM inversion space plus two-stage denoising—that addresses both simultaneously. The consistent gains across few-shot, long-tail, and OOD settings (up to +10% in 5-shot, +11.4% in OOD) make this a useful tool for practitioners working with fine-grained, data-scarce benchmarks. However, the method fundamentally requires at least two samples per category, limiting its applicability in extreme long-tail or zero-shot regimes; the authors acknowledge this and suggest future extensions to detection, segmentation, and video, as well as removing the VLM captioner dependency in favor of pure LLM-based prompt diversification.
