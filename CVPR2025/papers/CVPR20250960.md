## Problem

Open-vocabulary semantic segmentation methods typically depend on a combination of image-level vision-language models (e.g., CLIP), ground truth mask annotations, custom grouping encoders, and the Segment Anything Model to learn pixel-level image-text alignment. This paper asks whether a simple model can achieve competitive performance without any of these components, directly training for pixel-level feature and language alignment from noisy web image-text pairs alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250960/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, S-Seg takes an RGB image (processed at 448×448) and a set of candidate category names as text, producing a pixel-level semantic segmentation map where each pixel is assigned one of the provided labels. The model predicts N=64 mask-feature pairs via a MaskFormer decoder; background is obtained by thresholding the maximum class probability. During training, the model consumes only image-text pairs (up to 26M from CC3M, CC12M, and RedCaps) with no dense annotations or class labels.

## Method

S-Seg builds on MaskFormer (6-layer transformer decoder, 64 queries, 256-dim features) but removes the fixed-class classifier, instead outputting raw mask features supervised by a 12-layer text transformer via image-text contrastive loss. Mask shape is supervised by a pseudo-mask generator that extracts DINO ViT-S/8 features from the input and applies K-Means clustering to produce class-agnostic segmentation maps, matched to predicted masks via bipartite matching with combined dice and focal loss. The key design choice is decoupling mask supervision (pseudo-masks) from semantic supervision (language), enabling training entirely from noisy web captions without CLIP, ground truth masks, custom grouping encoders, or SAM. An optional self-training step (S-Seg+) re-trains the model on its own predictions over unlabeled training images, yielding a consistent +5.5% mIoU improvement across benchmarks.

## Results

On the standard unsupervised open-vocabulary segmentation protocol (background included), S-Seg achieves 53.2 / 27.9 / 30.3 mIoU on Pascal VOC / Pascal Context / COCO (37.1% 3-avg), outperforming GroupViT (50.8 / 23.7 / 27.5) on all three datasets without using CLIP, SAM, or any ground-truth masks. With the optional self-training step, S-Seg+ reaches 62.0 / 30.2 / 35.7 mIoU (42.6% 3-avg), a 5.5% absolute mIoU gain over S-Seg and surpassing SAM-CLIP (60.6 / 29.2 / 31.5) on all benchmarks. The pseudo-mask generator alone achieves 78.8 / 66.3 oracle mIoU on P.VOC / P.Context, outperforming GroupViT's grouping (73.7 / 54.6) while running at the same 0.002 s amortised speed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S-Seg (53.2 / 27.9 / 30.3) beats GroupViT (50.8 / 23.7 / 27.5) on all three datasets despite not using a pretrained CLIP model or a custom grouping encoder, and also surpasses SegCLIP (52.6 / 24.7 / 26.5) and OVSegmentor (53.8 / 20.4 / 25.1) on P.VOC and COCO. S-Seg+ (62.0 / 30.2 / 35.7) outperforms SAM-CLIP (60.6 / 29.2 / 31.5) on all three benchmarks and TCL (55.0 / 30.4 / 31.6) on P.VOC and COCO, while using none of the components (SAM, CLIP, grouping encoder) that those methods rely on. Against the simple baselines the authors construct (Pseudo-mask+CLIP at 6.6% 3-avg and Pseudo-mask ViT at 14.9%), S-Seg's 37.1% 3-avg demonstrates that jointly training mask prediction and language alignment is essential rather than naively combining a segmenter with a classifier. In the background-excluded protocol (Table 4), S-Seg reaches 81.8 / 27.2 / 42.4 mIoU, exceeding GroupViT (77.2 / 23.0 / 37.5) on all datasets and approaching TCL (83.2 / 33.9 / –) on P.VOC, while S-Seg+ (84.7 / 31.6 / 53.0) surpasses TCL on P.VOC and COCO. The gap to fully-supervised MaskFormer (81.2 / 50.0 / 62.1) remains substantial, particularly on P.Context, indicating that the absence of dense annotations is still the primary bottleneck.

## Relation to Prior Work

S-Seg builds directly on MaskFormer's query-based segmentation architecture but removes the fixed-class classifier head, replacing it with raw mask features supervised by image-text contrastive loss. It deliberately departs from the four dominant strategies in open-vocabulary segmentation—adapting CLIP-style VL models, training on ground-truth masks, using custom grouping encoders (GroupViT, OVSegmentor), and leveraging SAM—by relying solely on DINO-based pseudo-masks and noisy web captions. Conceptually, the paper positions itself as a minimal intersection of these approaches: a CLIP-like contrastive objective applied at the mask level, a ZegFormer-style decoupling of grouping and classification, and a GroupViT-style text-supervised grouping, each stripped of its most expensive component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central message—that a simple MaskFormer trained on pseudo-masks and web text can match or exceed methods requiring CLIP, SAM, or annotated masks—lowers the barrier to entry for open-vocabulary segmentation research and provides a clean, reproducible baseline. The consistent 5.5% mIoU gain from self-training and the demonstrated data scalability (12M→26M pairs) suggest a clear path for further improvement without architectural changes. Open questions include whether the DINO+K-Means pseudo-mask generator is the right ceiling for mask supervision, whether the approach extends to panoptic or instance-level tasks, and how much of the remaining gap to fully-supervised MaskFormer (e.g., 81.2 vs. 62.0 on P.VOC) is attributable to the pseudo-mask quality versus the weak text supervision.
