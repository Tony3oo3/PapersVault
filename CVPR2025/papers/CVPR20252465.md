## Problem

Existing 3D scene generation methods are offline, requiring tens of minutes to hours per scene due to progressive multi-view synthesis and lengthy geometry optimization, making them unsuitable for interactive use cases like game prototyping, VR exploration, and iterative world design. Additionally, generated scenes exhibit strong geometric distortion at boundaries when connecting multiple scenes, creating visible seams.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252465/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a single RGB image as the starting scene, plus runtime user controls: camera movement (specifying where to generate) and text prompts (specifying what content to generate). It produces a set of coherently connected 3D scenes represented as FLAGS (Fast LAyered Gaussian Surfels), each generated in under 10 seconds on a single A6000 GPU and rendered in real time. Each scene is a union of three surfel layers (foreground, background, sky) with view-independent RGB colors, enabling seamless alpha-blended rendering across the entire world.

## Method

The core representation, FLAGS, is a variant of 3D Gaussian Splatting where each Gaussian is flattened to a surfel (z-axis scale set to a tiny ε) and view-dependent colors are removed, organized into three depth-ordered layers. A single-view layer generation pipeline discovers foreground via depth-edge and segmentation masks, inpaints occluded background and sky regions with a text-guided diffusion model, then initializes each surfel's position, orientation, and scale directly from estimated monocular depth and normals—making optimization a fast fine-tuning step (100 Adam iterations, <1 s per layer) rather than training from scratch. To mitigate geometric distortion at scene boundaries, a guided depth diffusion modifies a latent depth diffusion model's denoiser with a gradient-based guidance term that enforces consistency between the newly generated depth and the depth rendered from existing scene geometry, producing smooth extrapolation without additional training.

## Results

WonderWorld generates a single 3D scene in 9.5 seconds on an A6000 GPU, roughly 80× faster than the fastest baseline (WonderJourney at 749.5 s). On novel-view rendering quality, WonderWorld achieves the best scores across all five metrics: CLIP Score 29.47, CLIP Consistency 0.9948, CLIP-IQA+ 0.6512, Q-Align 3.6411, and CLIP Aesthetic 5.9543. In a 2AFC human study on bird-eye view renderings, WonderWorld is preferred over WonderJourney (98.5%), LucidDreamer (98.6%), and Text2Room (98.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WonderWorld outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric. Against WonderJourney [67] (point-cloud representation, 749.5 s/scene), WonderWorld is ~79× faster and leads on CLIP Score by 2.13 points and Q-Align by 0.92 points, while achieving near-perfect CLIP Consistency (0.9948 vs 0.9544). Against LucidDreamer [8] (3DGS, 798.1 s/scene), the speedup is ~84× and the CLIP-IQA+ gap is the largest (0.6512 vs 0.5260), indicating substantially better rendered image quality. Against Text2Room [20] (mesh-based, 766.9 s/scene), WonderWorld leads on all metrics, with the most notable gap in CLIP Score (29.47 vs 24.50), reflecting Text2Room's limited generalization to outdoor scenes. The paper attributes the baselines' geometric distortion at scene boundaries to their reliance on global depth alignment heuristics or indoor-trained depth inpainting, whereas WonderWorld's guided depth diffusion directly conditions on visible existing geometry to produce coherent seams.

## Relation to Prior Work

WonderWorld builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting [28] by introducing FLAGS, a layered surfel variant that shrinks the z-axis to near-zero and removes view-dependent colors, enabling pixel-aligned geometry-based initialization that reduces per-layer optimization to under one second. For depth estimation, it extends the Marigold latent depth diffusion model [27] with a training-free guidance term that conditions on partially visible existing geometry, addressing the seam problem that plagues prior offline methods like WonderJourney [67] and LucidDreamer [8]. The layered foreground/background/sky decomposition draws on classical layered depth image ideas, while the LLM-driven prompt structuring follows WonderJourney's approach to content diversity. The key conceptual departure from all prior scene generation work is the shift from offline multi-view synthesis and lengthy optimization to single-view generation with geometry-initialized fine-tuning, enabling sub-10-second interactive generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 80× speedup over prior methods (9.5s vs. 700+s per scene) transforms 3D scene generation from a batch process into an interactive creative tool, with immediate implications for game prototyping, VR world-building, and iterative design workflows. The geometry-based initialization insight—that the right representation (surfels with well-defined normals) combined with pixel-aligned depth/normal estimates makes optimization a trivial fine-tuning step—is a broadly applicable principle for accelerating 3DGS-based pipelines. However, significant limitations remain: generated scenes only have frontal-facing surfaces (no back-side geometry), detailed objects like trees produce holes and floaters, and the view synthesis range is restricted to a small area around the camera. The authors position WonderWorld as a prototyping tool rather than an end-to-end solution, suggesting a future pipeline where interactive coarse generation is followed by slower, higher-fidelity refinement (e.g., video diffusion or 3D object generation).
