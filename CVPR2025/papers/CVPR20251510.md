## Problem

In cross-domain federated learning, clients' data from different domains cause domain shifts that degrade aggregated model performance by preventing consistent representation learning. Existing approaches either maximize client consensus or personalize models, but integrating both remains unexplored because personalization typically hinders consensus. The paper asks how to use personalized parameters to erase domain-specific skew while simultaneously enhancing consensus on domain-agnostic knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251510/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client provides RGB images from a single domain (e.g., one of the domains in Office-Caltech10, DomainNet, or PACS), and the model outputs a class label per image. The AlexNet backbone is decomposed at every convolutional layer into a DFE submodule (reduced output channels T/G) and a DSE submodule (expanding each DFE channel back to G channels via cheap linear operations), so the forward pass alternates between feature extraction and domain-skew erasing at every depth. The DFE parameters are globally shared across all clients while DSE parameters are personalized per client, and a consistency regularization term is added to the standard classification loss during local training.

## Method

FDSE decomposes each convolutional layer into a Domain-agnostic Feature Extractor (DFE) that learns shared core features and a Domain-specific Skew Eraser (DSE) that performs client-specific deskewing, following a GhostNet-style split where DFE reduces channels by a factor G and DSE cheaply expands them back. A consistency regularization term pulls the running mean and variance of each DSE's output toward the global statistics of the subsequent DFE's batch-normalization layer, with depth-increasing weights (controlled by β) to enforce progressive deskewing. For aggregation, DFE modules are combined via a consensus-maximization strategy inspired by multi-gradient descent that minimizes the L2-norm of the per-layer aggregated update, while DSE modules are aggregated per client using a self-attention mechanism over normalized client parameters (temperature τ controls personalization strength). This decoupled design lets the model learn domain-agnostic representations through fair shared aggregation while collaboratively erasing heterogeneous domain skew through similarity-aware personalization.

## Results

FDSE achieves the best testing accuracy across all three cross-domain FL benchmarks. On DomainNet, it reaches 76.77% (ALL) and 74.50% (AVG); on Office-Caltech10, 87.15% (ALL) and 91.58% (AVG); and on PACS, 83.81% (ALL) and 82.17% (AVG). These results are averaged over 5 trials with different random seeds, and FDSE outperforms the strongest baseline (Ditto) by 1.59% (ALL) on DomainNet, 3.03% (ALL) on Office-Caltech10, and 1.79% (ALL) on PACS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FDSE outperforms all 11 baselines on every dataset and both evaluation metrics (ALL and AVG). Against the strongest personalized baselines, FDSE gains +1.59% ALL accuracy over Ditto on DomainNet (76.77 vs. 75.18), +3.03% over Ditto on Office-Caltech10 (87.15 vs. 84.12), and +1.79% over Ditto on PACS (83.81 vs. 82.02). Compared to the best consensus-based methods (FedHeal, FedFA), FDSE leads by 7.34% on DomainNet ALL and 8.37% on Office-Caltech10 ALL, confirming that consensus alone is insufficient without domain-specific personalization. Against the best personalization-only methods (PartialFed, FedBN), FDSE still leads by 0.85–4.03% across datasets, showing that integrating consensus maximization on top of personalization yields further gains. The paper also reports that FDSE uses only 0.65×10⁷ parameters and 24.87M communication per round—roughly half of FedBN/Ditto—while maintaining competitive training time (4.27s vs. 4.03s for FedBN).

## Relation to Prior Work

FDSE positions itself at the intersection of two established lines of cross-domain FL research: consensus-based methods (FedHeal, FedFA, FedProto) that align clients' representations, and personalization-based methods (FedBN, PartialFed, Ditto) that adapt to local domain skew. The paper's key conceptual departure is arguing these are not competing strategies but complementary ones, and that personalization can actively *enhance* consensus rather than undermine it. Architecturally, the DFE/DSE layer decomposition repurposes the GhostNet-style channel-splitting trick [9] for knowledge decoupling, while the layer-wise consensus maximization in Eq. (8) adapts the multi-gradient-descent algorithm [7, 10] to the federated setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hybrid framing provides a principled recipe for deciding *what* to personalize (domain-specific skew erasure) versus *what* to share (domain-agnostic feature extraction), a distinction that prior cross-domain FL methods handled ad hoc. The label-free adaptation to unseen domains via DSE fine-tuning on the consistency loss is a practical deployment advantage. However, the evaluation is limited to AlexNet on three image classification benchmarks, leaving open whether the layer-decomposition strategy generalizes to transformers, segmentation, or other modalities. The authors themselves note plans to extend to more architectures and applications.
