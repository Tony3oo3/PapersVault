## Problem

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods like LoRA constrain weight updates to fixed low-rank subspaces, forcing multiple tasks to compete within the same subspace and causing task interference that degrades performance below single-task baselines. The paper identifies that these methods directly modify the singular vectors of pre-trained weight matrices—directions that encode essential visual features—rather than preserving them and adapting only the singular values.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250233/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images and produces dense prediction outputs across four tasks simultaneously: semantic segmentation (21-class), human part segmentation (7-class), saliency detection, and surface normal estimation (with depth estimation on NYUD). The Swin Transformer backbone processes images through progressive patch merging across stages, and an HRNet decoder (6% of total parameters) produces pixel-level predictions. All tasks share a single encoder with joint inference, requiring only 1.61M trainable encoder parameters for the full multi-task setup.

## Method

DITASK decomposes each pre-trained weight matrix via SVD (W = UΣV^T), freezes the singular vectors U and V, and applies a learned neural diffeomorphism f^θ to the singular values to produce the adapted matrix W_A = U diag(f^θ(σ_1),...,f^θ(σ_p)) V^T. The diffeomorphism is parameterized by a Continuous Piecewise Affine-Based (CPAB) velocity field with only N_P ≤ 128 parameters per weight matrix, ensuring smooth, invertible, and monotone transformations that preserve the ordering of singular values. Two sets of transformations are learned per layer: a joint (task-agnostic) set for capturing task synergies and task-specific sets for individual adaptations, applied in a staged manner across the Swin Transformer. This design achieves full-rank updates during optimization while requiring only ~8.5K total adaptation parameters—500× fewer than MTLoRA—enabling 22% faster batch processing and 75% fewer trainable parameters than existing multi-task methods.

## Results

On PASCAL MTL (4 dense prediction tasks), MTL-DITASK achieves a +3.22% relative improvement (Δm) over single-task fine-tuning using only 1.61M trainable encoder parameters, outperforming full fine-tuning (+2.23%, 28.12M params) and the strongest low-rank baseline MTLoRA (r=64, +2.55%, 6.40M params) by 26.27% in Δm with 4× fewer parameters. On NYUD, DITASK reaches 43.85 MIOU for semantic segmentation and 0.606 RMSE for depth estimation (+20.65% Δm), surpassing MTLoRA (37.18 MIOU, 0.635 RMSE, +8.42% Δm). DITASK also reduces batch runtime by ~22% (27.31s vs 35.20s) and uses 500× fewer adaptation parameters (8.5K vs 4.86M) compared to MTLoRA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DITASK outperforms all 12 baselines on the key Δm metric (relative improvement over single-task tuning), achieving +3.22% versus the next-best MTLoRA (r=64) at +2.55% and HyperFormer at +2.64%. Against the closest multi-task low-rank competitor MTLoRA [1], DITASK wins on Δm by 26.27% while using 4× fewer trainable parameters (1.61M vs 6.40M) and 500× fewer adaptation parameters (8.5K vs 4.86M). Among adapter-based methods, DITASK surpasses Polyhistor (+2.34%) and VL-Adapter (−1.83%) on Δm, though HyperFormer achieves slightly higher per-task MIOU (71.43 vs 69.66 on SemSeg) at the cost of 70.83M parameters—44× more than DITASK. All fixed-rank methods except MTLoRA yield negative Δm, confirming the paper's hypothesis that shared low-rank subspaces cause task interference; DITASK avoids this by preserving full-rank structure through singular vector retention. On NYUD, DITASK extends its lead over MTLoRA with +20.65% Δm versus +8.42%, while using 3.9× fewer parameters (1.61M vs 6.26M).

## Relation to Prior Work

DITASK repurposes CPAB diffeomorphisms (Freifeld et al., 2015/2017), originally developed for 1D image alignment and temporal registration, as a mechanism for adapting pre-trained weight matrices in multi-task learning. It departs fundamentally from the LoRA/MTLoRA lineage, which constrains weight updates to fixed low-rank subspaces, by instead preserving the singular vectors of pre-trained weights and applying a learned monotone warp to the singular values. The motivation draws on the incremental rank-growth phenomenon observed in transformer training dynamics (Boix-Adsera et al., 2023), arguing that fixed-rank constraints are misaligned with how networks naturally adapt.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a remarkably compact intervention—32 trainable parameters per layer via CPAB velocity fields—can match or exceed methods requiring millions of adaptation parameters, which is practically significant for deploying multi-task ViTs under tight memory budgets. However, evaluation is confined to dense prediction tasks on PASCAL MTL and NYUD with Swin Transformers, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to classification, detection, or non-Transformer backbones. A key open question is whether the diffeomorphic (monotone, invertible) constraint is essential or whether any smooth monotone reparameterization of singular values would suffice, which would simplify implementation considerably.
