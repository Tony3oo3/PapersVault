## Problem

Weakly-supervised video anomaly detection (WSVAD) methods rely almost exclusively on RGB spatio-temporal features, which are insufficient to distinguish visually similar but semantically distinct anomalies such as shoplifting from normal shopping. While multi-modal cues (pose, depth, optical flow, panoptic masks, text) could resolve this ambiguity, adapting them to WSVAD is hindered by sparse training data, semantic disparity across modalities, and the prohibitive inference cost of running multiple modality backbones simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252641/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method consumes only RGB video (encoded by a pre-trained I3D backbone into 16-frame snippet features), producing frame-level anomaly scores via a standard MIL scoring head. During training, five additional modalities—pose (YOLOv7-pose), depth (DepthAnythingV2), panoptic masks (SAM), optical flow (RAFT), and text (ViFiCLIP)—are extracted by frozen pre-trained backbones and used solely to supervise the pseudo-modality generation and alignment objectives. The architecture is plug-in: the Poly-modal Inductor can be inserted at any block of the teacher-student backbone, and the paper places it at both the first and last blocks to capture low- and high-level multi-modal cues.

## Method

PI-VAD introduces a Poly-modal Inductor (PI) with two plug-in modules that augment a frozen UR-DMU teacher's RGB features into a student network. The Pseudo Modality Generation (PMG) module is an encoder-decoder (one shared 1D-conv encoder, five parallel linear+1D-conv decoders) that synthesizes modality-specific embeddings directly from the student's intermediate RGB features, eliminating the need for modality backbones at inference. The Cross Modal Induction (CMI) module then aligns each pseudo-modality to RGB via a snippet-level bi-directional InfoNCE contrastive loss, passes the concatenated embeddings through transformer blocks that encode cross-modal correlations while re-injecting RGB at each layer, and distills the final multi-modal output toward the teacher's pre-trained features via an L2 distillation loss. Training proceeds in two stages: a warm-up phase optimizing PMG reconstruction, alignment, and distillation losses jointly, followed by full WSVAD training with the UR-DMU MIL loss plus weighted alignment and distillation terms. At inference, only the student network runs (≈19.9 GFLOPs, 30 FPS), achieving 90.33% AUC on UCF-Crime without any modality backbone overhead.

## Results

On UCF-Crime, π-VAD achieves 90.33% AUC and 77.77% AUC_A, surpassing the best multi-modal method VadCLIP (88.02% AUC, 70.23% AUC_A) by +2.31% and +7.54% respectively, and the best RGB-only method UR-DMU (86.97% AUC, 70.81% AUC_A) by +2.75% and +6.96%. On XD-Violence, π-VAD reaches 85.37% AP and 85.79% AP_A, outperforming VadCLIP (84.15% AP) by +1.22% and UR-DMU (81.66% AP, 83.94% AP_A) by +3.20% and +1.85%. On the more diverse MSAD dataset, π-VAD attains 88.68% AUC and 71.25% AUC_A, exceeding the best prior SoTA (TEVAD, 86.82% AUC) by +1.65% and the UR-DMU re-implementation (85.78% AUC, 67.95% AUC_A) by +2.90% and +3.30%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

π-VAD outperforms all 18 baselines on every reported metric across all three datasets. Against the strongest multi-modal competitor VadCLIP [32] (CLIP-based, uses text at inference), π-VAD gains +2.31% AUC and +7.54% AUC_A on UCF-Crime, and +1.22% AP on XD-Violence, while requiring only RGB at inference. Against the strongest RGB-only baseline UR-DMU [40] (I3D encoder, also used as the teacher), π-VAD improves by +2.75% AUC / +6.96% AUC_A on UCF-Crime and +3.20% AP / +1.85% AP_A on XD-Violence. On MSAD, π-VAD beats TEVAD [3] (86.82% AUC) by +1.86% AUC and the UR-DMU re-implementation by +2.90% AUC / +3.30% AUC_A. Notably, methods that use additional modalities at inference (e.g., MACIL-SD with audio, PEMIL with text) are outperformed by π-VAD, which uses only RGB at test time, demonstrating that the training-time multi-modal induction transfers effectively without inference-time modality overhead. The largest relative gains appear on the AUC_A metric (abnormal-event detection), where π-VAD improves over UR-DMU by nearly 7 points on UCF-Crime, indicating that multi-modal cues are particularly beneficial for localizing short, subtle anomalous segments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the UR-DMU teacher-student WSVAD framework [40], using it as both the base architecture and the frozen teacher for knowledge distillation. It departs from the long line of single-modality (RGB-only) WSVAD methods by introducing five auxiliary modalities (pose, depth, panoptic masks, optical flow, text) during training, drawing on multi-modal representation learning techniques (contrastive InfoNCE alignment, knowledge distillation) that have been applied to action recognition but not previously to WSVAD. The key conceptual departure from multi-modal foundation models like CLIP and ImageBind is the Pseudo-Modality Generation module, which synthesizes modality-specific embeddings from RGB features alone, thereby eliminating the need for five separate modality backbones at inference while retaining their training-time supervision signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PI-VAD addresses a practical gap: multi-modal foundation models require massive paired data and all modalities at inference, making them infeasible for sparse WSVAD datasets (e.g., 810 videos in UCF-Crime) and real-time CCTV deployment. The pseudo-modality approach achieves near-equivalent accuracy to using full modality backbones (90.33 vs. 90.58 AUC) while reducing inference compute by ~130× (19.88 vs. 2561.40 GFLOPs), making multi-modal WSVAD practically deployable. Open questions include whether the pseudo-modality paradigm generalizes beyond VAD to other weakly-supervised video tasks, how it scales to additional modalities or longer videos, and whether the small performance gap to full-backbone inference can be closed with better PMG architectures. The reliance on five pre-trained modality backbones during training (SAM, YOLOv7-pose, DepthAnythingV2, RAFT, ViFiCLIP) remains a non-trivial setup cost.
