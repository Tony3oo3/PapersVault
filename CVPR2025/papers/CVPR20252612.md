## Problem

Hierarchical embodied agents that use language as the communication protocol between VLM reasoners and low-level policies fail to convey precise spatial information, making it impossible to distinguish among multiple similar objects in a scene. Existing vision-based alternatives (future-image prompting, latent codes) introduce world-model hallucinations or interpretive ambiguity. The paper asks whether a segmentation-based, temporally-grounded interface can unlock VLMs' spatial reasoning for open-world interaction.

## Input / Output

ROCKET-1 consumes a sequence of 640×360 RGB frames (resized to 224×224) concatenated pixel-wise with binary segmentation masks to form 4-channel inputs, alongside categorical interaction-type labels; the high-level reasoner receives language task descriptions and outputs (x,y) point prompts. The policy outputs low-level mouse/keyboard actions causally over up to 128-frame context windows. Between high-level reasoner invocations (which run at ~1.5 Hz), SAM-2 provides continuous object tracking at the environment's 20 Hz frequency, bridging the temporal gap.

## Method

The core contribution is visual-temporal context prompting, a protocol where VLMs highlight regions of interest via object segmentation masks and convey interaction intent through discrete skill primitives, replacing ambiguous language instructions. ROCKET-1 fuses each observation with its segmentation mask into a 4-channel image processed by an EfficientNet-B0 backbone, pools features via self-attention, and feeds them into a TransformerXL that models temporal dependencies and incorporates interaction-type embeddings to predict the next action. A backward trajectory relabeling technique automatically generates segmentation annotations by running SAM-2 in reverse temporal order from detected interaction events, enabling fully automated dataset construction from 1.6B frames of Minecraft gameplay. During training, segmentations and interaction types are randomly dropped (p=0.75) to force the policy to infer intent from visual-temporal context rather than overfitting to the current mask. The full hierarchical pipeline chains GPT-4o (task decomposition) → Molmo (object localization) → SAM-2 (segmentation and tracking) → ROCKET-1 (action execution), with the first two running at low frequency and the latter two at environment frequency.

## Results

On the Minecraft Interaction Benchmark (12 tasks across 6 interaction categories), ROCKET-1 with Molmo prompting achieves an overall success rate of 82%, compared to 7% for VPT-bc, 19% for STEVE-1, and 9% for GROOT-1 — a 76% absolute improvement over the strongest baseline. With a human oracle reasoner, ROCKET-1 reaches 95% overall. On long-horizon tasks (Table 3), the proposed visual-temporal context protocol achieves 70% success on the wool-dyeing task where all baselines (DEPS, MineDreamer, OmniJarvis) score 0%, and 75% on the obsidian task versus 0% for all prior methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ROCKET-1 with Molmo prompting (82% overall) decisively outperforms all three baselines on the Minecraft Interaction Benchmark: VPT-bc (7%), STEVE-1 (19%), and GROOT-1 (9%). The gap is most dramatic on spatially demanding tasks like 'place oak door on diamond block' (91% vs. 0% for all baselines) and 'hunt sheep in right fence' (91% vs. 0–13%). On long-horizon tasks, the visual-temporal context protocol (ROCKET-1) achieves 100% on wooden pickaxe and furnace tasks (matching DEPS and MineDreamer), but substantially outperforms them on steak (75% vs. 15–40%), obsidian (70% vs. 0%), and wool dyeing (70% vs. 0%). OmniJarvis (latent-code protocol) is the closest competitor on long-horizon tasks but still trails by 25–70 percentage points on the hardest tasks. The human-oracle ROCKET-1 (95%) establishes the upper bound, showing that the Molmo-based reasoner closes most of the gap to human-level prompting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hierarchical agent paradigm (DEPS, Voyager, Jarvis-1, OmniJarvis) where a VLM serves as a high-level reasoner and a separate policy executes low-level actions, but departs from prior work by replacing the communication protocol—language instructions, future goal images, or latent behavior codes—with object segmentations and interaction-type cues derived from past observations. It builds on VPT's large-scale Minecraft pretraining and hindsight relabeling, but introduces backward trajectory relabeling with SAM-2 to automate segmentation annotation in reverse temporal order. The visual backbone design (pixel-wise concatenation of observation and mask, inspired by ControlNet-style conditioning) and the TransformerXL temporal model are standard components repurposed for a segmentation-conditioned policy, making the core novelty the prompting protocol rather than the architecture itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that spatial information conveyed via segmentation masks is a more effective communication channel between VLMs and low-level policies than language or imagined futures, directly addressing a recognized bottleneck in hierarchical embodied agents. The fully automated backward trajectory relabeling pipeline is a practical contribution that lowers the barrier to training segmentation-conditioned policies. However, all experiments are confined to Minecraft, the Molmo-based prompting still relies on task-specific predefined prompts, and the baselines (VPT-bc, STEVE-1, GROOT-1) are relatively weak, leaving open whether the protocol generalizes to real-world robotics or more diverse 3D environments. A key open question is whether the segmentation interface can be made fully autonomous without human or hand-crafted prompting at the VLM level.
