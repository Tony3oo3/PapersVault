## Problem

Visual Language Models require thousands of visual tokens for inference, but many of these tokens are either irrelevant or visually redundant, inflating compute and memory costs. Existing token reduction methods address only one of these two issues (irrelevance or redundancy), and many rely on attention scores or [CLS] tokens, making them incompatible with FlashAttention and modern VLM architectures that lack a [CLS] token.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251420/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

PACT operates at inference time on an early layer L of the language model, consuming the hidden states H ∈ R^{n×d}, key and query matrices K, Q ∈ R^{n×n_h×d_h}, and position IDs of the visual tokens at that layer. It outputs a reduced set of merged hidden states H' and corresponding position IDs P', where each output token is the mean of its cluster members and inherits the position ID of its cluster center. The method acts solely on visual tokens without accessing text tokens, making it independent of the prompt and suitable for multi-turn conversations.

## Method

PACT combines two complementary modules applied at a single early layer of the language model. First, EUTI identifies unimportant tokens using a novel importance score that multiplies a softmax of key–global-query dot products (averaged over attention heads) by the L2 norm of each token's hidden state, avoiding attention scores entirely and thus remaining FlashAttention-compatible. Second, DBDPC (Distance Bounded Density Peak Clustering) clusters the remaining important tokens using key vectors with cosine distance, selecting cluster centers greedily by descending local density while enforcing a maximum intra-cluster distance d_c, guaranteeing that no two elements in the same cluster are farther apart than the threshold. Pruned tokens within α·d_c of a cluster center are reincorporated to recover mislabeled information, and each cluster's hidden states are averaged into a single representative token. Proportional attention is applied to compensate for the reduced influence of merged tokens in subsequent attention layers.

## Results

At a 98.6% Approach-to-Reference Metric Ratio on LLaVA-OneVision-7B, PACT achieves a 71.3% visual token reduction with a 225% throughput speedup and 31% GPU memory reduction (27.4 GB → 19.05 GB), substantially outperforming FastV (50% reduction, 165% throughput, 30.4 GB), VTW (25% reduction, 160% throughput), and ToME (40% reduction, 137% throughput). At a 71.3% reduction ratio, PACT incurs only a 1.4% average performance drop, whereas the best prior method shows at least a 4.4% drop at the same ratio. Across 24 benchmarks spanning single-image, multi-image, and video tasks, PACT matches or exceeds the no-reduction baseline on most datasets (e.g., 57.7 vs. 58.5 on VideoMME, 1571 vs. 1579 on MME, 85.4 vs. 87.2 on DocVQA) while processing each element in roughly 0.3–0.5 s versus 0.8–1.1 s without reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PACT consistently outperforms all compared baselines at both equal reduction ratios and equal throughput across four VLMs (LLaVA-OneVision-7B, LLaVA-1.6-Mistral-7B, Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct, InternVL2-8B). Against FastV (attention-score pruning), PACT achieves higher quality at the same reduction ratio and avoids the GPU memory overhead of computing full attention scores, which is incompatible with FlashAttention. Against VTW (deeper-layer token withdrawal), PACT achieves far greater reduction (71.3% vs. 25% at equal quality) because VTW's performance collapses above 40% reduction. Against ToME (iterative ViT-level merging), PACT reaches 71.3% reduction versus ToME's 40% at the same quality level, and ToME cannot exceed 50% reduction with its fixed per-layer merging schedule. Against PruMerge and HiRED (both limited to LLaVA-1.6 with [CLS]-token architectures), PACT shows less degradation even at low reduction ratios and is applicable to architectures without a [CLS] token. For clustering, DBDPC outperforms DPC, DBSCAN, k-means, and agglomerative clustering at all reduction ratios, confirming that bounding intra-cluster distance is critical for preserving visual information during token merging.

## Relation to Prior Work

PACT extends the Density Peaks Clustering (DPC) algorithm by introducing a distance-bound constraint (DBDPC) to prevent merging of dissimilar visual tokens, and adopts proportional attention from ToME to preserve the influence of merged tokens. It departs from prior visual token reduction methods (FastV, PruMerge, HiRED) by avoiding reliance on attention scores or [CLS] tokens, making it compatible with FlashAttention and modern VLM architectures that lack a [CLS] token. Unlike previous approaches that address either irrelevance (pruning) or redundancy (merging) in isolation, PACT combines both at an early language-model layer, and unlike VTW which removes tokens only in deeper layers, it achieves the reduction in a single early layer for maximum computational gain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 71.3% visual token reduction with only 1.4% performance degradation (versus 4.4% for prior SOTA at the same ratio) and 225% throughput improvement make PACT a practically significant efficiency tool for deploying VLMs in resource-constrained settings. Its plug-and-play, training-free nature and FlashAttention compatibility broaden applicability across current VLM architectures. Open questions include whether the fixed early-layer strategy remains optimal for very different model depths or token counts, whether the method degrades gracefully for extremely high-resolution inputs with tens of thousands of tokens, and whether the distance-bound parameter d_c can be made adaptive rather than manually tuned per model.
