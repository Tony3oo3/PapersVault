## Problem

Existing MLLMs for GUI understanding treat screenshots as natural images, relying solely on global visual features from a vision backbone without explicit spatial structure modeling. Since GUI elements are artificially designed and spatially arranged to convey specific semantic meanings, this leads to inaccurate feature representation and misunderstanding of GUI structure. Compounding this, high-quality spatial structure data (e.g., View Hierarchy) is difficult to obtain due to privacy constraints and noise, making purely visual spatial perception essential.

## Input / Output

The model takes a GUI screenshot (split into 448×448 sub-images via Dynamic High-Resolution) and a text question/prompt. Internally, it produces two parallel visual clue streams: standard dense image tokens from the vision backbone and GUI-tailored visual clues from the TGS-PFM, which are concatenated with the question embeddings as LLM input. Outputs are task-dependent: bounding box coordinates for grounding, free-text answers for QA, captions for widget/screen description, and action sequences (click, slide, type) for navigation.

## Method

MP-GUI introduces a TGS-Perception Fusion Module (TGS-PFM) with three lightweight MLP perceivers—Textual (TxP), Graphical (GaP), and Spatial (SaP)—that each extract modality-specific signals from the vision backbone's dense embeddings. A Fusion Gate, inspired by MoE routing, uses cross-attention between the question semantics and the concatenated perceiver outputs to dynamically weight and fuse the three signals into task-oriented features. Training follows a Multi-stage Strategy: each perceiver is first trained on specialized data (text-only grounding, graphics-only grounding, and a novel Spatial Relationship Prediction task with four relationship types including negative samples), then the FG is trained on mixed multi-task data. A data synthesis pipeline using Qwen2-VL-72B generates Single- and Multi-Perceiver Enhanced QA pairs to support FG training. The entire TGS-PFM is trained from scratch on top of InternVL2-8B (InternViT-300M + InternLM2.5-7B) using LoRA, with only 0.68M GUI-specific training samples.

## Results

On the basic GUI understanding benchmark (Table 3), MP-GUI (8B) achieves 151.0 CIDEr on Widget Captioning (+5.7% over the second-best InternVL2-P), 83.0 Acc@IoU=0.1 on RefExp grounding (+13.2% over CogAgent), and 84.3 SQuAD F1 on Complex ScreenQA (+2.3% over InternVL2). On zero-shot ScreenSpot grounding, MP-GUI reaches 64.1% average accuracy, surpassing SeeClick (53.4%) and CogAgent (47.4%). For screen navigation, MP-GUI achieves 69.2% overall success on AITW (vs. ShowUI's 68.3%) and leads on Mind2Web with 42.1% element accuracy and 89.0% operation F1 in the cross-task setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MP-GUI (8B) outperforms all general-purpose MLLMs (Qwen-VL, MiniCPM-V, Qwen2-VL, Llama 3.2-V, CogAgent, InternVL2) on 6 of 8 basic GUI understanding tasks, with the largest margins on RefExp grounding (+13.2% over CogAgent's 73.3) and Widget Captioning (+5.7% over InternVL2-P's 142.8). It slightly trails CogAgent on Taperception (88.2 vs. 88.4) and Llama 3.2-V on ScreenQA Short (90.5 vs. 91.6), but leads on all other metrics. Against GUI-specific methods (Table 4), MP-GUI matches ScreenAI (156.5 vs. 156.4 CIDEr on WC) while using only 0.68M training samples versus ScreenAI's 383.5M, and surpasses Ferret-UI, Spotlight, and Pix2Struct on most tasks. On ScreenSpot zero-shot grounding, MP-GUI (64.1% avg) beats SeeClick (53.4%), CogAgent (47.4%), and GPT-4V (16.2%), with particularly strong gains on icon/widget grounding (65.9% mobile vs. 52.0% for SeeClick). For navigation, MP-GUI edges out ShowUI on AITW (69.2% vs. 68.3% overall) and Mind2Web cross-task (42.1% Ele.Acc vs. 39.7%), while GPT-4 remains competitive on Mind2Web cross-domain Step.SR (26.4% vs. 33.7% for MP-GUI, where MP-GUI actually leads).

## Relation to Prior Work

MP-GUI builds directly on the InternVL2-8B architecture (InternViT-300M vision backbone + InternLM2.5-7B LLM) and departs from prior GUI-specific MLLMs (SeeClick, ShowUI, Ferret-UI, ScreenAI, Pix2Struct) that treat screenshots as natural images and rely on unified end-to-end instruction-tuning to implicitly learn GUI knowledge. The key architectural departure is the TGS-PFM module, which introduces three modality-specific perceivers (textual, graphical, spatial) and a task-adaptive Fusion Gate inspired by MoE routing, providing the LLM with structured GUI-tailored visual clues alongside the standard global visual features. The multi-stage training strategy and the novel Spatial Relationship Prediction task represent a shift from the implicit, data-hungry pretraining paradigm (e.g., ScreenAI's 383.5M samples) toward explicit, stage-wise modality learning with far fewer samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit spatial structure modeling and modality-specific perception can substantially improve GUI understanding with only 0.68M training samples, challenging the assumption that GUI MLLMs require massive domain-specific pretraining corpora. The finding that domain instruction-tuning (InternVL2-P) can actually degrade generic reasoning abilities (CQA, QAS) while the dual-visual-clues architecture preserves them highlights a practical tension in domain adaptation that the field has not fully addressed. Open questions include whether the three-perceiver decomposition generalizes to other structured visual domains (e.g., infographics, scientific figures), whether the multi-stage training can be collapsed into a single phase without performance loss, and how the approach scales to larger model sizes or more diverse GUI paradigms (e.g., AR/VR interfaces).
