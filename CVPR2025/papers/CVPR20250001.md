## Problem

Deterministic image-to-image translation (e.g., super-resolution, label-to-photo) requires that each input produce a single, faithful output matching the ground truth. Existing SDE-based bridge and diffusion samplers introduce randomness at every step, yielding diverse but inconsistent outputs, while PF-ODE-based samplers are deterministic but produce blurry, low-fidelity images. No prior generative model simultaneously achieves SDE-level image quality and near-zero output variance for deterministic I2I tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250001/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the model consumes paired (source, target) images at 256×256 (Cityscapes, DF2K, Urban100, BSD100) or 64×64 (Edges2Handbags) resolution. At inference, a single source image (segmentation map, edge map, or low-resolution image) is fed through the reverse diffusion process to produce one deterministic target image. The only stochastic element is a single random draw at the first reverse step scaled by 1/√T, making output variance negligible (PSNR std ≈ 0.01 at 3 steps).

## Method

Dual-approx Bridge builds on the Brownian bridge SDE (dX = −(X−Y)/(1−t)dt + g(t)dW) but replaces the two random quantities needed at each reverse step with dedicated neural networks. A forward approximator ε_θ(X_t, t) is trained to predict the initial state X_0 from the noisy interpolation X_t, while a reverse approximator Z_φ(X_t, t) is trained independently to predict the white-noise increment z that would otherwise be sampled from N(0,I). This dual-approximator design is the key architectural novelty: a single network cannot simultaneously estimate both the score-related initial state and the noise increment, so two networks trained on separate objectives (‖X_t − X_0 − ε_θ‖ and ‖z − Z_φ‖) are required. The resulting iterative sampling formula (Eq. 12) is fully deterministic except for one negligible random term at t=T, and achieves peak faithfulness (PSNR 16.99, SSIM 57.4%) with only 3 sampling steps, outperforming both SDE-based (high variance) and PF-ODE-based (low quality) baselines on Cityscapes, Edges2Handbags, and super-resolution benchmarks.

## Results

On the Edges2Handbags dataset, Dual-approx Bridge achieves an FID of 1.36, ranking second only to A-Bridge (1.07) and substantially outperforming all other baselines including DDBM (1.83), I2SB (7.43), and Pix2Pix (74.8). On the deterministic Cityscapes label-to-photo task, the method achieves FID 48.70, PSNR 15.70, and SSIM 53.26%, surpassing the best GAN baseline (UNIT) by 35% in FID and ~50% in SSIM. For super-resolution on BSD100, it attains the best LPIPS (0.1267) and PSNR (26.56), outperforming SRDiff (0.2046 / 24.17) and ESRGAN (0.1579 / 23.65), while on Urban100 it achieves the best LPIPS (0.1207) but trails SRDiff slightly on PSNR (23.23 vs. 23.88).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dual-approx Bridge outperforms all GAN-based baselines (Pix2Pix, CycleGAN, UNIT, MUNIT, DRIT, CUT, FastCUT, DCLGAN, SimDCL, CGGAN, DistanceGAN) on both FID and faithfulness metrics across Cityscapes and Edges2Handbags, with the closest GAN competitor DCLGAN (FID 49.4) still trailing by a narrow margin. Among diffusion/bridge models, it beats I2SB (FID 7.43), Rectified Flow (25.3), SDEdit (85.8), and DDIB (186.84) decisively on Edges2Handbags, and surpasses DDBM (1.83) by a small margin. The only method it loses to on FID is A-Bridge (1.07 vs. 1.36), but A-Bridge uses an SDE-based sampler with 50 steps and exhibits substantially higher output variance (std 0.0903 vs. 0.0084 for Dual-approx Bridge). In super-resolution, it dominates ESRGAN and RankSRGAN on BSD100 (PSNR 26.56 vs. 23.65/23.80) and matches or beats SRDiff on LPIPS, though SRDiff retains a slight PSNR edge on Urban100 (23.88 vs. 23.23). Against the Brownian bridge SDE and PF-ODE samplers, Dual-approx Bridge improves PSNR by ~12% over PF-ODE (16.99 vs. 15.23) and SSIM by ~12% (57.4% vs. 51.2%) while maintaining near-zero output variance, unlike the SDE variant which shows PSNR std of 0.21.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Brownian bridge diffusion framework (Liu et al., ICCV 2023; Wang et al., ACM MM 2024) by introducing a dual-approximator design: one network estimates the initial state X₀ in the forward process, while a second network approximates the white noise increment in the reverse process. This departs from standard score-based diffusion and bridge models, which rely on a single approximator trained in the forward process and either sample random noise (SDE) or discard it entirely (PF-ODE) during generation. The reverse SDE structure follows Anderson's (1982) formulation, and the forward training objective is explicitly stated to be identical to that of the Brownian bridge model in [47], positioning the contribution as a sampling/inference innovation rather than a new generative objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in deterministic I2I translation (super-resolution, denoising, inpainting) where SDE-based samplers produce diverse but unfaithful outputs and PF-ODE-based samplers produce consistent but blurry ones. The dual-approximator design is a clean conceptual solution, and the finding that only 3 sampling steps suffice for peak faithfulness is practically appealing for efficiency. However, experiments are limited to small-scale datasets (64×64 and 256×256), the model uses only the VP SDE formulation, and a small residual randomness remains at the first sampling step, leaving open questions about scalability to high-resolution generation and whether combining VP and VE SDEs (noted as future work) would further improve performance.
