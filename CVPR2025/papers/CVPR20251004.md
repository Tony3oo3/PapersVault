## Problem

Conventional mean teacher (MT) semi-supervised methods fix the teacher-student roles, but in 3D vessel segmentation the teacher does not always outperform the student due to the complex, variable tubular topology of vessels. This static supervision causes cognitive bias: incorrect pseudo-labels propagate and amplify through EMA updates, degrading performance. The paper also notes that existing vessel-specific architectures (deformable convolutions, snake convolutions) are complex, lack local detail capture, and do not generalize well under semi-supervised settings with only 5% labeled data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D vessel volumes (CT angiography at ~512×512×206–275 for coronary arteries, MRA for cerebral arteries, CT for pulmonary arteries) center-cropped to 96×96×96 with batch size 2. The transformer branch receives a reorganized 5-view input (1 global + 4 local sub-volumes at half spatial resolution) concatenated along the batch dimension, while the CNN branch receives the standard single volume. Output is a per-voxel 3D vessel segmentation mask; at inference, a sliding-window strategy produces the full-volume prediction from the VNet branch.

## Method

DiCo pairs a VNet (CNN) and a UNETR (transformer) sub-network that dynamically swap teacher-student roles at every iteration: whichever model achieves lower supervised loss on the current labeled batch becomes the teacher, and its unlabeled prediction serves as the pseudo-label for the other model, eliminating the fixed-role error amplification of standard MT. A multi-view integration module feeds the transformer a 2×2 spatial split of the volume plus a downsampled global view, then recomposes the four local feature maps with boundary-smoothing convolutions before fusing with the upsampled global feature. An MIP-based adversarial supervision projects both labeled and unlabeled 3D images/masks into 2D along the z-axis via maximum-intensity projection, fuses each projected mask with its image, and trains a discriminator to distinguish ground-truth from predicted mask projections, enforcing shape-style consistency without requiring pixel-wise correspondence. The total loss combines Dice+CE supervised losses for both models, the unsupervised consistency loss for the student, and the adversarial loss.

## Results

On the CAS2023 cerebral artery dataset, DiCo achieves 86.05% DSC, 74.35% NSD, and 1.49 voxels ASD, surpassing the previous best semi-supervised method MagicNet by 2.28% in DSC. On the large-scale ImageCAS coronary artery dataset (1,000 volumes), DiCo reaches 73.79% DSC and 58.59% NSD, outperforming all compared semi-supervised baselines. On Parse2022 pulmonary artery segmentation, DiCo attains 70.93% DSC and 55.26% NSD, leading all semi-supervised methods by 1.74% in DSC over MagicNet. Notably, DiCo uses only 5% labeled data (5 out of 90 training volumes) yet matches or exceeds fully supervised methods like DSCNet on CAS2023.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among semi-supervised baselines, DiCo outperforms all eight compared methods (MT, UA-MT, SASSNet, SLCNet, MagicNet, CAML, CauSSL, GuidedNet) on DSC and NSD across all three datasets. The largest margin is over MagicNet on CAS2023 (+2.28% DSC, +1.62% NSD, −0.43 voxels ASD), and over GuidedNet on Parse2022 (+3.10% DSC, +6.68% NSD). On Parse2022, DiCo trails MagicNet by 0.21 voxels on ASD (5.74 vs 5.53), the only metric where it does not lead among semi-supervised methods. Against fully supervised vessel-specific methods, DiCo (using only 5% labels) surpasses DSCNet on CAS2023 (86.05% vs 83.14% DSC) and ImageCAS (73.79% vs 73.49%), but falls short of ERNet on Parse2022 (70.93% vs 76.39%) and CTNet on ImageCAS (73.79% vs 79.71%), indicating that the advantage over fully supervised baselines is dataset-dependent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mean Teacher (MT) consistency-regularization paradigm by replacing the fixed teacher-student hierarchy with a dynamic, performance-based role switch between a CNN (VNet) and a ViT (UNETR) sub-network. It departs from prior vessel-specific architectures (DUNet, DSCNet, MICNet) that rely on specialized deformable or snake convolutions, instead proposing a simpler multi-view input reorganization to capture local and global context. The MIP-based adversarial supervision draws on GAN-style shape constraints but introduces a 2D projection step to avoid pixel-level correspondence between labeled and unlabeled masks, a design choice not seen in prior semi-supervised vessel work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dynamic role-switching mechanism offers a principled remedy for error propagation in static MT frameworks, and the ~7% average DSC gain over MT across three vessel benchmarks validates this. The finding that 2D-projected adversarial supervision substantially outperforms its 3D counterpart (up to 17% DSC on ImageCAS) suggests that dimensionality reduction can be a useful regularizer for shape-prior enforcement in 3D segmentation. However, the approach is validated only on vessel data, and the assumption that labeled-data loss reliably predicts unlabeled-data performance may not hold in more heterogeneous clinical settings. Open questions include whether dynamic collaboration generalizes to other organ types and whether the CNN+ViT pairing is essential or merely convenient.
