## Problem

While MLLMs have been adapted to individual domains like robotics, games, or UI control, it remains unclear whether a single unified model can be proficient across all of these simultaneously, and what training data and algorithmic choices are necessary to achieve this. The paper tackles the gap of building a Generalist Embodied Agent (GEA) that generalizes to unseen tasks across manipulation, navigation, video games, UI control, and planning, and empirically isolates the contributions of cross-domain data and online RL to this generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252307/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each timestep, GEA receives an embodiment-specific prompt, a natural language task instruction, and up to c=3 interleaved previous RGB observations and actions, then autoregressively generates a sequence of action tokens from the LLM vocabulary. Discrete actions (e.g., 'pick apple', 'right') are represented as standard text tokens, while continuous actions are encoded as sequences of learned RVQ tokens (2 codebooks × 512 entries, 1024-dim vectors) that are decoded and truncated to the specific embodiment's action dimensionality. Constrained decoding ensures the output stays within the valid action space for the current environment.

## Method

GEA builds on LLaVA-OneVision and introduces a unified multi-embodiment action tokenizer: a single Residual VQ-VAE trained across all continuous action spaces (end-effector, joint velocity, joint position, base velocity) by padding to the maximum dimension, replacing the most infrequent LLM vocabulary tokens with the learned codebook entries. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) full-model SFT on 2.2M successful trajectories spanning 12+ datasets and diverse embodiments (human demos, RL experts, motion planners) plus vision-language instruction data, and (2) online PPO with joint SFT (λ=0.1) on a subset of interactive simulators (Habitat Pick, LangR, Procgen) using LoRA, PopArt return normalization, and a separate MLP critic initialized from scratch. The key empirical findings are that cross-domain SFT data yields a consistent performance boost over per-domain training, and that online RL on top of the SFT model substantially outperforms both offline RL (IQL) and iterative success-SFT, while the pretrained MLLM (especially its visual encoder) is critical for visual generalization.

## Results

GEA achieves strong zero-shot generalization across 11 benchmarks spanning manipulation, video games, navigation, UI control, and planning. Headline results include 94.7% success on Meta-World (vs. 87.0% for Gato), 90.0% on CALVIN with unseen instructions and backgrounds, 82.5% on Habitat Pick in unseen scenes (outperforming an RL policy trained with ground-truth simulator state at 81.0%), and 44.0% of expert score on Procgen (vs. 25% for the prior specialist). In UI control, GEA reaches 57.3% success, surpassing GPT-4o with Set-of-Marks (45%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Gato [69], the closest generalist baseline, GEA wins on Meta-World (94.7 vs. 87.0) and Atari (32.7 vs. 31) but trails slightly on BabyAI (91.1 vs. 93.2). On CALVIN, GEA (90.0) outperforms the MLLM+IL specialist [48] (82.4) by 7.6 points and closely matches the 3D Diffuser Actor [35] (92.2), which relies on a depth camera and manipulation-specific action representation. In Habitat Pick, GEA (82.5) surpasses the RL+sim-state specialist [78] (81.0) despite using only RGB observations, but underperforms on Habitat Place (93.5 vs. 95.5) and Habitat Nav (62.5 vs. 72). For Procgen, GEA (44.0) nearly doubles the prior specialist [59] (25), while on Atari it is far behind Multi-Game DT [41] (32.7 vs. 85) which leverages offline RL. In UI control, GEA (57.3) beats GPT-4o with Set-of-Marks [93] (45) by 12 points, and on LangR planning it ties the MLLM+RL specialist [81] (50.0 vs. 51). GEA's main losses are in Maniskill (13.6 vs. 47.8 for IL+PPO) and Atari, where it lacks any RL training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLLM-as-agent paradigm (RT-2, OpenVLA) from single-domain robotic control to a truly generalist setting spanning manipulation, navigation, video games, UI control, and planning. It builds directly on Szot et al.'s action tokenization approach for grounding MLLMs in continuous actions, but generalizes the Residual VQ-VAE tokenizer to a single unified model across all embodiments rather than per-domain tokenizers. The key departure from prior generalist agents like Gato is the use of a pretrained MLLM backbone, a learned multi-embodiment action tokenizer, and a second stage of online RL (PPO) rather than pure imitation learning, positioning GEA as a hybrid supervised + RL adaptation of a vision-language foundation model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, reproducible recipe for turning a pretrained MLLM into a competitive generalist embodied agent, with clear evidence that cross-domain SFT data yields transfer benefits and that online RL is substantially more effective than offline RL or iterative SFT for closing the gap to specialist performance. However, GEA remains far from a true decision-making foundation model: it cannot handle arbitrary embodiments zero-shot, underperforms on several benchmarks (Maniskill, Atari, AndroidControl), and relies on a limited 3-observation context that constrains partially observable settings. Open questions include whether extending RL to all domains, scaling to longer contexts, and incorporating more diverse embodiments would close the remaining gaps to specialist systems.
