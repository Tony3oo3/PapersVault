## Problem

Existing adaptive compressive sensing (ACS) methods for unknown scenes lack accurate and robust feedback mechanisms for adaptive sampling allocation (ASA). Measurement-domain methods suffer from the inherent ill-posedness of under-sampled data, while image-domain methods create positive feedback loops that concentrate sampling in initially allocated regions, making it difficult to correct ASA errors across multi-stage sampling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251192/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images divided into 32×32 pixel blocks, acquiring measurements in multiple stages (initial uniform sampling followed by S=4 adaptive stages) at a total sampling rate (e.g., 0.10–0.50). The output is a full-resolution reconstructed image where each block has a different number of acquired samples determined by its innovation score, rather than a fixed uniform count. The reconstruction network PCCD-Net takes the concatenated adaptive sampling values and their corresponding sampling matrix as input.

## Method

SIB-ACS introduces a sampling innovation (SI) criterion that measures the decrease in reconstruction error attributable to a small sampling increment, serving as a relative (not absolute) indicator that naturally decays as principal components are restored, creating a negative feedback mechanism. A multi-stage AS framework iteratively performs Innovation Sampling (uniform probing), Innovation Estimation (lightweight reconstruction before/after IS to compute SI per block), and Adaptive Sampling (proportional allocation of remaining samples by normalized SI), progressively correcting ASA errors across stages. For reconstruction, PCCD-Net unfolds K=24 phases of proximal gradient descent split into two parallel paths: a PCPGD path that compresses the feature domain into a single-channel principal component image for cheap gradient computation, and a CDPGD path that operates in a low-dimensional (L=4) compressed domain to recover complementary details, with both paths merged additively each phase.

## Results

On BSD68 and Urban100 at sampling rates of 0.10 and 0.25, SIB-ACS achieves an average PSNR of 32.19 dB, exceeding the second-best method (CPP-Net, 30.63 dB) by 1.56 dB. The paper reports that SIB-ACS surpasses the second-best model by 1.10 dB (3.17%) in PSNR and 0.0101 (1.10%) in SSIM on BSD68, and by 1.48 dB (4.25%) in PSNR and 0.0028 (0.30%) in SSIM on Urban100. In terms of efficiency, PCCD-Net (the UCS component) runs in 0.060 s with 2.32 M parameters, comparable to the lightweight OCTUF (0.061 s, 0.29 M) while achieving 0.27 dB higher PSNR. The full SIB-ACS pipeline runs in 0.160 s with 4.73 M parameters, well below CPP-Net (0.193 s, 12.47 M) and CASNet (0.106 s, 15.85 M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIB-ACS outperforms all 14 compared baselines in Table 1 across all tested sampling rates (0.10–0.50) on both BSD68 and Urban100, with the largest margins at lower sampling rates. Against the strongest UCS baseline CPP-Net [24] (30.63 dB), SIB-ACS gains 1.56 dB in average PSNR while using 62% fewer parameters (4.73 M vs. 12.47 M) and running 17% faster (0.160 s vs. 0.193 s). Compared to the lightweight OCTUF [55] (30.31 dB, 0.29 M params), PCCD-Net alone achieves 0.27 dB higher PSNR at the same runtime (0.060 s vs. 0.061 s) with only 8× more parameters. Against the transformer-based CASNet [41] (30.09 dB, 15.85 M), SIB-ACS improves PSNR by 2.10 dB with one-third the parameters. The adaptive sampling module contributes an additional 1.06–2.04 dB over uniform sampling (e.g., 34.35 vs. 32.15 dB on BSD68 at SR=0.25), confirming that the innovation-guided allocation is the primary source of the performance gain over UCS methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of multi-stage adaptive compressive sensing (ACS) frameworks and deep unfolding networks for image reconstruction. It departs from prior ASA strategies—measurement-error-based [28, 43], saliency-based [2, 71, 76], and cosine-similarity-based [63]—by introducing an 'innovation criterion' that measures the decrease in reconstruction error attributable to sampling increments, thereby creating an image-domain negative feedback loop rather than the positive feedback inherent in saliency methods or the ill-posedness of measurement-domain indicators. For reconstruction, PCCD-Net extends the deep unfolding paradigm by splitting PGD into a principal-component path (low-dimensional) and a compressed-domain path (supplementary features), a design motivated by the variable and potentially large sampling matrices in ACS that make full feature-domain gradient descent prohibitively expensive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The negative feedback mechanism addresses a recognized weakness in multi-stage ACS where initial allocation errors compound across stages, and the PCCD-Net's dimensionality compression offers a practical path to deploying ACS in bandwidth-constrained settings (single-pixel imaging, underwater sensing, medical imaging) where per-block sampling rates vary widely. Limitations include evaluation only on natural-image benchmarks (BSD68, Urban100) at 256×256 resolution, a fixed block size of 32, and the need for lightweight reconstruction networks at every AS stage, which adds inference overhead during the sampling phase. Open questions include whether the innovation criterion generalizes to video, hyperspectral, or medical imaging modalities, and whether the multi-stage overhead can be reduced for real-time acquisition.
