## Problem

Existing self-supervised single image denoising methods (Noise2Noise and Noise2Void paradigms) rely on information-lossy operations—downsampling, masking, or adding extra noise—that discard information from the original noisy image, inevitably causing residual noise, aliasing, or texture loss. The paper asks whether a new paradigm can be constructed that avoids all such lossy operations while still providing valid self-supervision from a single noisy image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252742/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single noisy RGB image (e.g., 256×256×3) and outputs a denoised image in one forward pass at inference. The CNN backbone (~0.99M params, 11.4 GFLOPs) is initialized with weights pre-trained on DIV2K with Gaussian noise, and training requires only <100 iterations per image. No paired clean images, noisy datasets, or external noise models are needed.

## Method

Positive2Negative consists of two steps. Renoised Data Construction (RDC) first runs the network on the noisy image to obtain a predicted clean image x̂ and predicted noise n̂ = y − x̂, then multiplies n̂ by positive and negative scale factors sampled from N(1, σ) to synthesize multi-scale Positive and Negative noisy images (ŷ_p = x̂ + σ_p·n̂, ŷ_n = x̂ − σ_n·n̂), preserving all original image information. Denoised Consistency Supervision (DCS) then minimizes the distance between the network's denoised outputs on ŷ_p and ŷ_n using a gradually varying ‖·‖_{2→1.5} norm, driving the network to become noise-insensitive. The key insight is that real noise is approximately zero-mean and symmetrical, so the negated noise −n follows the same distribution, enabling valid renoised observations without any lossy operation. A Taylor-expansion analysis proves that minimizing the consistency loss drives ∂F_θ(x)/∂x · n → 0, recovering the clean signal.

## Results

Positive2Negative achieves state-of-the-art self-supervised single image denoising across all four benchmarks: 35.24 dB PSNR / 0.889 SSIM on SIDD val, 35.80 / 0.875 on SIDD benchmark, 37.92 / 0.954 on CC, 38.20 / 0.961 on PolyU, and 34.00 / 0.887 on FMDD. It outperforms the strongest single-image baseline (MASH) by 0.18–0.83 dB PSNR and the dataset-based self-supervised methods (LUD-VAE, CVF-SID) on SIDD. Notably, P2N also surpasses the supervised CLIPDenoising method on CC (+1.62 dB) and PolyU (+0.66 dB), while requiring only a single noisy image and fewer than 100 training iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Positive2Negative outperforms all 16 compared baselines on every dataset where results are available, with the sole exception of a 0.02 dB PSNR gap to the supervised CLIPDenoising on SIDD benchmark (35.80 vs 35.82). Among single-image self-supervised methods, P2N leads MASH by 0.18 dB on SIDD val, 1.00 dB on SIDD ben, 6.75 dB on CC, 0.58 dB on PolyU, and 0.29 dB on FMDD; it leads ScoreDVI by 0.49 dB on SIDD val, 0.41 dB on SIDD ben, 0.83 dB on CC, 0.43 dB on PolyU, and 0.90 dB on FMDD. Against dataset-based self-supervised methods, P2N surpasses LUD-VAE (the strongest) by 0.33 dB on SIDD val and 0.31 dB on SIDD ben, and exceeds CVF-SID by 0.43 dB on SIDD val. On CC and PolyU, P2N also beats the supervised CLIPDenoising by 1.62 and 0.66 dB respectively, demonstrating that the information-lossy-free paradigm can match or exceed methods that require paired clean-noisy training data. The efficiency advantage is substantial: P2N converges in fewer than 100 iterations versus 450,000 for Self2Self, 8,000 for R2R, and 2,400 for MASH, while using the same 0.99 M parameter CNN backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both the Noise2Noise and Noise2Void paradigms by identifying their shared reliance on information-lossy operations (noise addition, downsampling, masking) as a fundamental barrier to denoising quality. Rather than building on a specific prior method, it repurposes the zero-mean symmetry property of noise—long assumed in N2N/N2V—to construct training data by renoising the network's own predicted denoised image with its predicted noise (RDC), paired with a consistency loss between denoised outputs (DCS) rather than between denoised and noisy observations. The network architecture and initialization follow Noise2Noise/MASH conventions, but the training data construction and supervision strategy constitute a genuinely new paradigm the authors term Positive2Negative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: Positive2Negative converges in under 100 iterations (vs. 450,000 for Self2Self or 8,000 for R2R), making per-image self-supervised denoising far more tractable in real-world deployment. The conceptual reframing—showing that the information-lossy barrier, not the lack of clean data, is the core bottleneck—opens a clear path for future work, particularly extending the paradigm to noisy-dataset-based self-supervised denoising where no pre-trained Gaussian denoiser is available. The main limitation is the dependence on a pre-trained model to avoid trivial solutions, which constrains applicability to settings where such initialization is feasible.
