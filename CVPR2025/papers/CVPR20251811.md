## Problem

CLIP-based vision-language models are highly vulnerable to adversarial perturbations, yet existing defenses rely on adversarial fine-tuning that requires labeled data and lacks flexibility across downstream tasks. Existing test-time adaptation methods for VLMs focus on improving clean-sample accuracy and overlook the adversarial threat, leaving a gap for a training-free, task-agnostic defense at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251811/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single test image (any resolution compatible with the CLIP backbone) and a set of class-name text prompts, applying AugMix to generate 64 augmented views at inference time. A 4-token learnable textual prompt (initialized as 'a photo of a') is the only parameter optimized, via a single Adam step. The output is a class label obtained by reliability-weighted ensembling of softmax predictions across all augmented views; no training set or labeled data is required at any stage.

## Method

R-TPT decomposes the standard marginal entropy objective into a pointwise entropy term and a KL-divergence term, then discards the KL term because under adversarial perturbation the mean prediction is distorted and enforcing consistency toward it introduces optimization conflicts. The remaining pointwise entropy minimization is used to tune the textual prompt on a low-entropy batch of augmented views. A reliability-based weighted ensembling strategy then aggregates predictions: each augmented view's reliability is computed as the average cosine similarity to its K nearest neighbors in the image-feature space, and a softmax over these reliabilities yields ensemble weights that down-weight outliers (adversarial or noisy views) while up-weighting reliable ones. The entire procedure is training-free, requires no labeled data, and operates in a single inference pass per test sample.

## Results

On ImageNet with CLIP-ResNet50 (ε=1.0), R-TPT achieves 47.7% adversarial accuracy, a 47.6-point improvement over vanilla CLIP (0.1%) and 7.6 points above the second-best method Ensemble (40.1%), while maintaining 60.9% clean accuracy comparable to TPT (60.7%). Across eight fine-grained classification datasets, R-TPT reaches 45.8% average adversarial accuracy versus 2.4% for CLIP and 41.3% for Ensemble, with 53.7% clean accuracy. On the CLIP-ViT-B/16 backbone (ε=4.0), R-TPT achieves 41.2% average adversarial accuracy on fine-grained datasets, outperforming all baselines including MTA (30.0%) and Ensemble (33.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

R-TPT decisively outperforms all test-time adaptation baselines on adversarial robustness. Against TPT and C-TPT, which achieve near-zero adversarial accuracy (0.7% and 0.4% on ImageNet avg.), R-TPT reaches 35.4%, a gap of over 34 points, while matching their clean accuracy (47.1% vs. 47.2% for TPT). Compared to MTA (the strongest ensembling-based baseline), R-TPT gains 15.1 points in adversarial accuracy (35.4% vs. 20.3%) and 0.0 points in clean accuracy on ImageNet-OOD. Against simple Ensemble, R-TPT wins on both metrics: +4.4 points clean (47.1% vs. 42.7%) and +5.9 points robust (35.4% vs. 29.5%), showing that reliability-based weighting avoids the negative transfer that plain averaging introduces on clean samples. On the ViT backbone (Table 2), R-TPT extends its lead to 41.2% adversarial accuracy vs. MTA's 30.0% and Ensemble's 33.7%. When combined with the training-time defense TeCoA (Table 4), R-TPT further boosts adversarial accuracy from 13.1% to 21.7%, confirming compatibility with existing robust pre-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

R-TPT builds directly on the test-time prompt tuning (TPT) framework [37] and its marginal entropy objective inherited from MEMO [57], but departs from both by decomposing marginal entropy into a pointwise entropy term and a KL divergence term, then discarding the latter on the grounds that it introduces optimization conflicts under adversarial perturbations. The reliability-based weighted ensembling strategy extends the simple view-averaging used in MTA [53] by introducing a similarity-based reliability score that downweights outlier views. The paper positions itself as the first to apply a test-time paradigm specifically for adversarial defense in CLIP, complementing rather than replacing training-time methods like TeCoA [26] and APT [21].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Because CLIP is the most widely deployed VLM and is selected from a small pool of open-source checkpoints, it faces a realistic adversarial threat that training-time defenses (requiring labeled data and task-specific fine-tuning) cannot address flexibly. R-TPT's test-time formulation is practical: it needs no annotations, works across downstream tasks, and can be stacked on top of robust pre-training. Limitations include a non-trivial per-image inference cost (~0.58 s with 64 views), weaker gains on already-robust pretrained models, and the fact that the ablation reveals ensembling—not the entropy modification—is the primary driver of robustness. Open questions include whether the reliability metric generalizes to other VLM architectures, whether adaptive attacks that account for the augmentation pipeline can bypass the defense, and whether the approach extends to generative or open-vocabulary settings.
