## Problem

Current text-to-image diffusion models lack precise, continuous control over camera parameters, producing flat horizons and limiting artistic expression. Existing alternatives—prompt engineering, predefined shot tags, or 3D/multi-view representations—are either imprecise, restricted to a small set of presets, or require unavailable geometry and multi-view data. The paper addresses this gap by enabling arbitrary, fine-grained control over both intrinsic and extrinsic camera parameters using only single-view conditioning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt and four user-specified camera parameters (roll, pitch, vertical FoV, and distortion ξ) provided via sliders. These parameters are analytically converted into a 2D PF-US map—a per-pixel encoding of up-vectors and latitude angles—rather than being fed as raw scalars. The output is a single RGB image whose camera view matches the specified parameters while its content is determined entirely by the prompt; the model also supports per-frame conditioning for video sequences.

## Method

The core insight is to bypass 3D scene representation entirely by encoding the per-pixel visual effects of camera parameters using the Perspective Field (PF) representation combined with the Unified Spherical (US) camera model, yielding a compact 2D PF-US map that captures up-vector direction and horizon distance for every pixel. A ControlNet-based module, initialized from SDXL's encoder and middle block weights, is trained to condition the frozen SDXL diffusion model on these PF-US maps. At inference, only the bottleneck (middle block) residual is injected into SDXL rather than all decoder skip connections, a choice shown via ablation to preserve image quality while maintaining camera adherence. Training uses a novel 57,380-image dataset generated by sampling camera parameter quartets from 360° panoramic sources, with prompts auto-generated by BLIP-2; the conditioning module learns camera view independently of prompt content, effectively disentangling the two.

## Results

PreciseCam achieves comparable prompt adherence to the SDXL baseline, with a CLIP score of 0.543 (±0.039) vs. 0.557 (±0.039) and a BLIP score of 0.292 (±0.026) vs. 0.299 (±0.025), evaluated over 2,940 images per method. A user study with 34 participants confirms that PreciseCam significantly outperforms both SDXL and Adobe Firefly in aligning generated images with the desired camera view. The paper notes the absence of reliable automated metrics for evaluating camera parameters in generated images, so camera control is assessed primarily through qualitative examples and the user study.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SDXL with prompt engineering, PreciseCam trades a small drop in prompt adherence (CLIP 0.543 vs. 0.557; BLIP 0.292 vs. 0.299) for precise, continuous camera control that SDXL simply cannot provide — SDXL consistently fails to interpret complex camera-related prompts such as tilted views from below. Against Adobe Firefly, which offers only a handful of discrete preset tags (close-up, shot from above, shot from below, wide angle), PreciseCam wins decisively: Firefly succeeds on the 'shot from below' tag in realistic style but fails on the same tag in artistic style, and has no tag at all for more complex perspectives like a tilted view from below, producing invalid results even with elaborate prompt engineering. PreciseCam, by contrast, achieves reliable camera control across both realistic and artistic styles and across all four continuous parameters (roll, pitch, vFoV, distortion ξ), as confirmed by the 34-participant user study.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on ControlNet's framework for spatially-conditioned diffusion generation and repurposes the Perspective Field (PF) representation—originally designed for single-image camera calibration—into a generative conditioning signal. It departs from prior camera-control methods (NeRF-based multi-view approaches, 3D word embeddings, and preset shot tags) by eliminating the need for 3D scene representations or multi-view data, instead encoding the per-pixel effects of four continuous camera parameters (roll, pitch, vFoV, distortion ξ) via the Unified Spherical camera model. The key conceptual move is treating camera parameters as a 2D per-pixel field (up-vectors and latitude angles) rather than as a 3D geometric specification, making the conditioning lightweight and compatible with any UNet-based diffusion backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PreciseCam provides the first general, continuous-parameter camera control for text-to-image generation that works across both photographic and artistic styles without requiring 3D assets or multi-view data, filling a gap that prompt engineering and preset tags cannot address. The 57K-image dataset with ground-truth PF-US parameters is a useful resource for the controllable generation community. Open questions include whether the approach extends to more complex multi-object scenes with conflicting depth cues, how it behaves under extreme parameter combinations beyond the training distribution, and whether the PF-US conditioning can be integrated natively into video diffusion models rather than applied frame-by-frame. The paper also acknowledges that prompt-camera conflicts can produce incoherent outputs, suggesting a need for better arbitration between semantic and geometric conditioning.
