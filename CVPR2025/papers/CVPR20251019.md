## Problem

Interactive medical image segmentation (IMIS) is hindered by the absence of large-scale, diverse, and densely annotated datasets that capture complex clinical environments. Existing public datasets are tailored to specific modalities or tasks, contain sparse annotations (e.g., COSMOS averages only 5.7 masks per image versus SA-1B's 100), and lack a unified evaluation framework, limiting both model generalization and fair cross-method comparison.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251019/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 1024×1024×3 medical images alongside interactive prompts: click points (uniformly sampled from foreground regions), bounding boxes (smallest enclosing box with a 5-pixel offset to simulate user bias), and text prompts following the template 'A segmentation area of a [category]' covering 200+ organ/lesion classes. The output is a 256×256 segmentation mask upsampled to input resolution via bilinear interpolation. During training, the image encoder processes the image once while K=8 successive interaction steps iteratively refine predictions, with each step receiving the previous low-resolution mask as an additional cue.

## Method

The paper contributes two main components: the IMed-361M dataset and the IMIS-Net baseline. IMed-361M integrates 6.4 million images from 110+ public and private sources across 14 modalities and 204 targets, generating 273.4 million interactive masks via SAM's automatic mask generation (32×32 point grid, confidence threshold >0.85, NMS at IoU>0.7, background removal >80%) refined against original ground truth through bounding-box overlap matching and morphological cleanup. IMIS-Net adopts a SAM-like architecture with a ViT-base image encoder, a prompt encoder that fuses positional encodings with learned embeddings for points/boxes and CLIP text embeddings for language prompts, and a 768-dimensional Transformer mask decoder. Training simulates K=8 consecutive interaction rounds using a combined Focal+Dice loss (20:1 ratio) with Adam at 2×10⁻⁵ over 12 epochs on 72 A4090 GPUs, where only the prompt encoder and decoder are updated after the initial image encoding. The ablation shows that expanding the decoder dimension from 256 to 768 yields a 5.63-point Dice gain with only 24M additional trainable parameters, offering a scalable path to further performance improvements.

## Results

On the ISLES dataset (ischemic stroke lesion), IMIS-Net achieves 71.78% Dice, outperforming the second-best SAM-Med2D (68.22%) by 3.56 points. On SegThor (thoracic organs), IMIS-Net reaches 89.27% average Dice, leading SAM-Med2D (86.43%) and SAM-2 (85.86%). On TotalSegmentator MRI (12 abdominal organs), IMIS-Net attains 79.06% average Dice, surpassing SAM-2 (77.62%) and SAM-Med2D (75.92%), and outperforms all baselines in 10 of 12 individual organs. MedSAM is consistently the weakest baseline across all three external datasets (59.52–60.55% average Dice).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IMIS-Net wins on 2 of 3 dataset averages (ISLES and TotalSegmentator MRI) and on the SegThor average, but loses on individual SegThor organs Heart (81.27 vs. SAM-Med2D's 94.18) and Trachea (90.04 vs. Huang et al.'s 94.60). Against SAM-Med2D, the closest medical-specific competitor, IMIS-Net leads by 3.56 points on ISLES, 2.84 on SegThor average, and 3.14 on TotalSegmentator MRI average. MedSAM is a clear outlier, trailing all other methods by 15–25 Dice points on every dataset, likely due to its limited pretraining data. SAM and SAM-2 (natural-image foundation models) occupy the mid-range, confirming that medical-domain pretraining is necessary but insufficient without dense interactive masks. Huang et al. [24] is competitive on SegThor (84.73 avg) but lags on TotalSegmentator MRI (75.47), suggesting weaker generalization to MRI modalities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SAM-to-medical-imaging adaptation line (MedSAM, SAM-Med2D, Huang et al.) by addressing what the authors identify as the core bottleneck: the extreme sparsity of interactive masks in existing medical datasets (~5.7 masks/image in COSMOS vs. ~100 in SA-1B). Rather than proposing a new architecture, IMIS-Net follows the standard ViT-encoder + prompt-encoder + mask-decoder design, but is trained on IMed-361M, a dataset where SAM's own automatic mask generation is bootstrapped to produce 56 dense masks per image across 14 modalities and 204 targets. The key methodological departure from prior medical SAM fine-tuning work is the use of SAM-generated interactive masks as additional supervision alongside hand-annotated GT, combined with a quality-control pipeline that reconciles auto-generated masks against original annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IMed-361M (361M masks, 14.4× MedTrinity-25M) is likely to become the de facto benchmark for evaluating interactive medical segmentation, filling a gap that has limited fair cross-model comparison. The paper's ablations confirm that mask density, not merely image count, drives IMIS performance, which reframes how the community should think about dataset construction for interactive tasks. Open questions remain around semantic labeling of auto-generated masks, extension to 3D volumetric segmentation, and whether the 18 datasets with quality issues (identified by radiologist review) introduce systematic biases that could affect downstream clinical deployment.
