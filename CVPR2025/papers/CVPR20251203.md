## Problem

Deepfake detectors over-rely on specific frequency bands (termed spectral bias), a previously unexplored form of model bias in the frequency domain that restricts generalization to unseen forgery types. While prior work has addressed human-perceptible biases like identity or background, the imperceptible over-dependence on dominant frequency components—where detectors ignore broader forgery artifacts spread across the spectrum—remains unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251203/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 256×256 RGB facial image frames and produces a binary real/forgery classification with a probability score. During training, the Fo-Mixup augmentation operates on the FFT amplitude and phase spectra of forgery images, selectively modulating only the identified dominant frequency bands while preserving the original phase to retain spatial structure of tampered regions. A confidence sampling step filters augmented samples by Shannon entropy, retaining only the top λ=50% highest-confidence predictions to avoid injecting noisy training signals.

## Method

FreqDebias combines two complementary strategies to mitigate spectral bias. First, Fo-Mixup partitions the amplitude spectrum into angular segments, clusters them via K-means, and uses OHEM with a pre-trained detector to identify the top-t dominant frequency bands (those whose exclusion maximizes classification loss); it then selectively interpolates amplitude spectra between forgery images only within those bands while preserving phase, exposing the detector to diversified frequency content. Second, a dual consistency regularization enforces local consistency by matching class-wise normalized CAMs between synthesized and original images via Jensen-Shannon Divergence, and global consistency by modeling facial features with a two-component vMF mixture on a unit hypersphere and maximizing a Distribution Matching Score between synthesized and training domains. The backbone (ResNet-34) is augmented with feature alignment modules at each stage to produce hierarchical feature maps for the vMF classifier, and all auxiliary classifiers are removed at inference incurring no extra cost.

## Results

Trained on FF++ (HQ), FreqDebias achieves 97.5% frame-level AUC in-domain and a cross-domain average (C-Avg) of 82.88%, outperforming the strongest baseline LSDA (82.56%) by 0.32 points. On individual cross-domain test sets, FreqDebias leads on CDFv1 (87.5%), CDFv2 (83.6%), DFDCP (82.4%), and DFDC (74.1%), though it trails LSDA on DFD (86.8% vs. 88.0%). In the robustness evaluation (Table 3), FreqDebias attains 97.6% average video-level AUC across six corruption types, surpassing CADDM (97.4%) and RealForensics (95.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreqDebias outperforms the strongest cross-domain baseline LSDA [65] on four of five cross-domain test sets (CDFv1 +0.8, CDFv2 +0.6, DFDCP +0.9, DFDC +0.5 AUC) but trails it on DFD (86.8 vs. 88.0). Against earlier SOTA methods such as SPSL [36] (C-Avg 76.74) and SLADD [6] (76.42), FreqDebias gains over 6 points in cross-domain average AUC. In the cross-manipulation evaluation (Table 2), FreqDebias consistently beats GFF [38], DCL [52], IID [24], and SFDG [59] across all four unseen forgery types, with the largest margin of +3.85 AUC on FaceSwap. For robustness against corruptions (Table 3), FreqDebias achieves 97.6% average AUC, edging out CADDM [13] (97.4%) and substantially exceeding RealForensics [21] (95.2%) and LipForensics [20] (92.1%). In-domain, FreqDebias (97.5) marginally surpasses IID [24] (97.4) and SLADD [6] (96.9) on FF++.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by identifying 'spectral bias'—over-reliance on specific frequency bands—as a previously unexplored form of model bias in deepfake detection, distinct from the identity, background, and forgery-specific biases addressed by prior debiasing work (IID, UCF, LSDA). It repurposes consistency regularization (building on CORE's representation-consistency idea) but extends it with a dual local-global formulation: CAM-based attention consistency for spatial focus and a vMF-distribution-based hyperspherical consistency for geometric embedding structure. The Fo-Mixup augmentation departs from prior frequency-domain augmentations (amplitude swap/mix from FSDR) by explicitly targeting only dominant frequency components identified via OHEM-guided clustering, rather than indiscriminately modifying all frequency bands.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The spectral bias identification provides a concrete, measurable explanation for why deepfake detectors fail to generalize across forgery types, and the Fo-Mixup + dual CR framework offers a practical recipe to mitigate it without architectural changes. However, the approach relies on a pre-trained detector for OHEM-based frequency band selection, introducing a dependency that may not transfer cleanly to new domains, and the vMF global consistency component's marginal contribution over simpler alternatives (e.g., cosine similarity) is not fully isolated in ablations. Open questions include whether spectral bias is specific to face forgery or generalizes to other media forensics tasks, and whether the framework extends naturally to video-level detection where temporal consistency adds another dimension of generalization.
