## Problem

Batch Normalization in Federated Learning suffers from two compounding issues: heterogeneous client data produces inconsistent BN statistics that drive client models to diverge, and the small batch sizes required by resource-limited clients make mini-batch mean/variance estimates unreliable. Additionally, when large batch sizes are available (e.g., cross-silo FL), BN's generalization benefit degrades because the stochastic uncertainty inherent in mini-batch statistics is lost.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, 32×32) fed into CNN backbones (VGG-16, ResNet-18, ResNet-50) and outputs class labels. The key operational detail is that normalization layers consume learned scalar parameters (per-neuron mean μ and standard deviation γ) rather than computing statistics from the current mini-batch, enabling training with batch sizes as small as 1. In the FL setting, these learned statistics are aggregated on the server alongside all other model weights via FedAvg, producing a single off-the-shelf global model.

## Method

Population Normalization (PN) replaces BN's mini-batch estimated statistics with per-neuron population mean μ and standard deviation γ that are learned as trainable parameters during training, subject to two constraints derived from the definitions of mean and variance (enforced via stochastic Lagrangian multipliers). Because μ and γ are ordinary parameters, they are updated locally and aggregated on the server like any other weight, ensuring normalization layers remain consistent across clients regardless of data heterogeneity and eliminating dependence on batch size. For large-batch scenarios where PN underperforms BN, Noisy Population Normalization (NPN) multiplicatively injects data-independent Gaussian noise (centered at 1) into the learned μ and γ during training, restoring the stochastic uncertainty that BN naturally obtains from small-batch estimation and thereby improving generalization.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with VGG-16 in a federated setting (80 clients, α=0.04, batch size 8), PN achieves 78.59% top-1 accuracy, outperforming BN (70.22%), FedBN (71.49%), and GN (71.05%). At the extreme of batch size 1 where BN and FedBN are inapplicable, PN still achieves 64.38% (α=0.04) and 86.69% (α=1.00) on CIFAR-10. For large-batch training (batch 2048, 4 participants), NPN reaches 93.66% top-1 on CIFAR-10 and 72.74% on CIFAR-100, surpassing BN (93.13% / 72.21%) and PN (93.10% / 72.15%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PN consistently outperforms BN across all batch sizes and heterogeneity levels; the gap is largest at small batch sizes (e.g., 78.59% vs 70.22% on CIFAR-10 at batch 8, α=0.04) and becomes absolute at batch size 1 where BN is inapplicable. FedBN, which keeps BN layers local to avoid aggregation, is also outperformed by PN (e.g., 78.59% vs 71.49% at batch 8, α=0.04) and shares the same batch-size-1 limitation. Group Normalization is beaten by PN by 7–14 points under severe heterogeneity (α=0.04), while Instance Normalization degrades catastrophically at small batch sizes (35.47% vs PN's 64.38% at batch 1, α=0.04). In the large-batch regime (batch 2048), plain PN is slightly outperformed by BN (93.10% vs 93.13% on CIFAR-10), but NPN recovers and surpasses BN (93.66%), confirming that the injected noise compensates for the missing stochastic uncertainty. The noise-injection idea also generalizes: adding noise to Batch Renormalization improves its CIFAR-10 top-1 from 93.21% to 93.60% (Table 2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard paradigm of estimating normalization statistics from mini-batches (BN) or using moving averages (BRN) by instead learning the population mean and variance as trainable parameters constrained by their mathematical definitions. It directly addresses the limitations of FedBN, which keeps BN layers local and thus cannot produce a deployable global model, and of other batch-independent normalizations (GN, IN, LN) that underperform BN in generalization. The NPN extension builds on prior analyses of BN's implicit regularization (Luo et al., 2018; Wu et al., 2019) by explicitly isolating the stochastic uncertainty in batch statistics as a separable, injectable component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PN offers a practical solution for resource-constrained FL clients where batch sizes of 1–4 are common, and it produces a single global model without the cold-start problem of FedBN. The noise-injection idea in NPN is general and demonstrated to also improve BRN, suggesting a broader regularization principle. However, experiments are confined to CIFAR-10/100 with VGG-16 and ResNet, and the paper explicitly leaves open whether NPN inherits BN's theoretical properties (e.g., learning-rate auto-tuning via scale invariance), limiting confidence in its applicability to larger-scale or more diverse FL deployments.
