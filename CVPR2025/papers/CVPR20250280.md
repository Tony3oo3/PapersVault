## Problem

Auxiliary-free human video matting is under-defined without a clear target, causing methods like RVM to fail on ambiguous backgrounds with similar objects. Existing mask-guided approaches that refine VOS segmentation priors into alpha mattes are hampered by the poor quality, small scale, and synthetic nature of available video matting data, which disrupts the very segmentation prior they aim to leverage, resulting in unstable core regions and insufficient boundary detail.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250280/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of RGB video frames at arbitrary resolution (evaluated at 512×288 and 1920×1080) together with a single first-frame segmentation mask that assigns the target object, obtainable from off-the-shelf tools like SAM. It sequentially produces a per-frame alpha matte (continuous values in [0,1]) for the assigned target, processing frames one at a time in a memory-driven manner. Unlike methods requiring per-frame masks or trimaps, only the first-frame mask is needed, and an optional recurrent warm-up on the first frame refines its matte before propagation begins.

## Method

MatAnyone builds on the STCN memory-matching paradigm but stores full alpha mattes (rather than binary masks or trimaps) in an alpha memory bank, and introduces a region-adaptive memory fusion module that predicts per-token alpha-change probability via a lightweight three-convolution boundary predictor; the final pixel readout is a soft merge between the current memory query and the previous frame's propagated values, weighted by this probability, so core regions retain stable memory while boundary regions adapt to new detail. For training, the paper directly supervises the matting head with large-scale real segmentation data (COCO, SPD, YouTubeVIS) using an L1 loss on core regions and a novel scaled DDC loss on boundaries—correcting the original DDC assumption by factoring in the foreground-background color difference—rather than using a separate parallel head. The model is trained in three stages (VM800 matting data, segmentation core supervision, image matting fine-tuning on D646/AIM) and evaluated on the newly introduced VM800 training set (2× VideoMatte240K in size and quality) and YouTubeMatte benchmark.

## Results

On the real-world benchmark, MatAnyone achieves MAD 0.14, MSE 0.10, and dtSSD 0.89, substantially outperforming the strongest auxiliary-free baseline RVM-Large (MAD 0.95, MSE 0.50, dtSSD 1.30) and the strongest mask-guided baseline MaGGIe (MAD 1.94, MSE 1.53, dtSSD 1.63). On the synthetic VideoMatte benchmark at 1920×1080, MatAnyone attains the best MAD (4.24), MSE (0.33), Grad (4.00), and dtSSD (1.19) among all methods. On the new YouTubeMatte benchmark at 1920×1080, it achieves the best MAD (1.99), MSE (0.71), and dtSSD (1.65), though MaGGIe slightly edges it on Grad (7.69 vs. 8.91).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MatAnyone dominates all five baselines on the real-world benchmark, reducing MAD by 85% over RVM-Large (0.14 vs. 0.95) and by 93% over MaGGIe (0.14 vs. 1.94), indicating far superior semantic stability in core regions. On synthetic benchmarks, it achieves the best MAD and dtSSD across all four test sets (VideoMatte and YouTubeMatte at both resolutions), confirming both spatial accuracy and temporal consistency advantages. The closest competitor is MaGGIe, which benefits from per-frame instance masks as guidance; MatAnyone still outperforms it on MAD, MSE, and dtSSD at 1080p, though MaGGIe achieves a slightly lower Grad on YouTubeMatte 1080p (7.69 vs. 8.91), suggesting marginally sharper boundary gradients in that setting. Among auxiliary-free methods, RVM-Large is the strongest but remains confused by ambiguous backgrounds, while MODNet is consistently the weakest across all metrics. The ablation (Table 3) confirms that the new training scheme (core-area supervision via segmentation) contributes the largest single gain, dropping MAD from 1.85 to 0.42 on the real benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

MatAnyone adapts the memory-based VOS paradigm (STCN, XMem, Cutie) to video matting by storing alpha mattes rather than binary segmentation masks in the memory bank, and introduces a region-adaptive memory fusion mechanism that treats core and boundary tokens differently during propagation. It departs from the standard parallel-head training strategy used by RVM and MaGGIe by directly supervising the matting head with real segmentation data, using a scaled DDC loss for boundary regions where no alpha ground truth exists. The paper also positions itself against auxiliary-free video matting (MODNet, RVM) by arguing that setting is under-defined, and instead adopts the semi-supervised target-assigned formulation already well-studied in VOS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The target-assigned video matting formulation is significantly more practical for real-world applications (e.g., video editing, AR) than auxiliary-free matting, and MatAnyone demonstrates that a single first-frame mask suffices for stable tracking even in complex multi-person scenes. The introduction of VM800 (2× larger, higher quality than VideoMatte240K) and the YouTubeMatte benchmark addresses a critical infrastructure gap in the field. The finding that directly supervising the matting head with segmentation data (via the scaled DDC loss) dramatically outperforms the parallel-head approach challenges a widely adopted training recipe. Open questions include generalization beyond human subjects, scalability to very long videos, and whether the region-adaptive fusion principle transfers to other memory-based video tasks.
