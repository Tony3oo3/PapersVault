## Problem

CLIP's zero-shot logits suffer from serious inter-class confusion in downstream few-shot classification tasks because CLIP is pre-trained via contrastive learning rather than directly optimizing classification boundaries. This ambiguity between category predictions degrades accuracy, particularly when inter-class similarity is high or domain shift exists between pre-training and downstream data, and existing CLIP-based FSL methods (prompt tuning, adapters, fine-tuning) do not explicitly model or remove this confusion pattern.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224 RGB images and a set of C natural-language text prompts (one per category) as input, with K labeled images per class (K ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8, 16}) for training. It produces a final classification logit vector of dimension C via adaptive fusion of two intermediate logit streams (MAF logits from visual features alone and ICD logits from deconfused zero-shot logits), from which the predicted class label is obtained by argmax. The CLIP image and text encoders remain frozen; only lightweight adapter parameters in MAF, ICD, and the α Generator are trained.

## Method

LDC introduces three learnable modules on top of frozen CLIP. (1) Multi-level Adapter Fusion (MAF) extracts features from four levels of the image encoder, transforms each through a side adapter, fuses them via either preset weighted averaging or a learnable channel-concatenation adapter, and projects through a frozen projector to yield an enhanced feature z^e. (2) Inter-Class Deconfusion (ICD) models the inter-class confusion as a learnable noise term Δs(x) computed by three sequential adapters that jointly process the zero-shot logits and the enhanced feature z^e, then subtracts this term from the original zero-shot logits via a residual connection to produce cleaner ICD logits. (3) Adaptive Logits Fusion (ALF) combines MAF logits and ICD logits with a per-sample weight α generated from z^e. Training jointly minimizes three cross-entropy losses (on MAF, ICD, and ALF logits) plus two L1 similarity losses that anchor the modified logits to the original zero-shot logits to prevent over-deconfusion.

## Results

On the average of 11 classification benchmarks, LDC achieves 65.71% (1-shot), 67.92% (2-shot), 71.17% (4-shot), 75.79% (8-shot), and 79.78% (16-shot) top-1 accuracy, outperforming all baselines at every shot setting. On ImageNet alone (ResNet-50), LDC reaches 66.63% at 16-shot, surpassing the previous best (FAR, 66.39%) by 0.24%. Under OOD evaluation (trained on ImageNet, tested on ImageNet-V2), LDC achieves 58.03% with ResNet-50 and 66.10% with ViT-B/16, both leading all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LDC outperforms all 16 compared baselines on the 11-dataset average at every shot setting, with the largest margin over Proto-CLIP-F (76.18% → 79.78%, +3.60%) at 16-shot and over APE (73.36% → 79.78%, +6.42%) at 16-shot. On ImageNet specifically (Table 1), LDC leads at 8-shot (64.44% vs. FAR's 64.30%) and 16-shot (66.63% vs. FAR's 66.39%), but trails slightly at 1-shot (60.48% vs. APE's 62.04%) and 2-shot (61.25% vs. CALIP-FS's 62.03%). Under OOD transfer to ImageNet-V2, LDC achieves the best accuracy with both ResNet-50 (58.03%) and ViT-B/16 (66.10%), beating FAR (57.91%) and RPO (65.13%) respectively. However, on ImageNet-Sketch, LDC underperforms the best baselines (35.52% vs. FAR's 36.69% with ResNet-50; 48.85% vs. RPO's 49.27% with ViT-B/16), indicating limited robustness to large domain shifts. The ICD module alone contributes the largest single gain (+19.07% over ZS-CLIP on 11-dataset average), while MAF adds +11.96% and ALF provides a further +0.96%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CLIP's zero-shot logits as a starting point and positions itself within the growing family of CLIP-based few-shot adaptation methods (CoOp, Tip-Adapter, FAR, CALIP-FS). Its key conceptual departure is reframing the adaptation problem as explicit logit deconfusion: rather than learning new prompts, fine-tuning the full encoder, or training a separate classifier, it models the inter-class confusion pattern as a learnable noise term and subtracts it via a residual structure. The multi-level adapter fusion (MAF) module draws on the broader adapter and feature-pyramid literature, while the adaptive logits fusion (ALF) module is a lightweight mechanism for combining the deconfused and multi-level pathways.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, interpretable diagnosis of why CLIP's zero-shot logits degrade in few-shot settings (contrastive pre-training does not optimize classification boundaries) and offers a lightweight, plug-in remedy that requires only adapter-sized parameters. However, the method's weakness on ImageNet-Sketch (worse than several baselines) reveals that learned deconfusion patterns do not transfer well under large domain shifts, leaving open the question of whether the approach can be made more robust to distributional change. The modest SOTA margins (0.24% on ImageNet 16-shot, 3.60% on the 11-dataset average) suggest the technique is a useful component rather than a paradigm shift, and its practical value may lie in being combined with stronger domain-adaptation strategies.
