## Problem

Recent 3D instance segmentation methods deploy hundreds of instance candidates (kernels or queries) but fail to fully exploit them: multiple candidates often encode different fragments of the same instance (e.g., sofa backrest vs. armrest) without being aggregated, and inherent noises from sparse, unordered point clouds cause models to misinterpret the spatial extent and appearance of instances. The paper addresses both the fragmentation problem and the noise-induced spatial ambiguity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252773/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a colored 3D point cloud (N_p × 6: xyz + RGB) as input, voxelizes it for sparse convolutional processing, and produces per-point instance masks along with semantic class labels. Internally, it operates on hundreds of instance candidate features (e.g., 256 in the kernel-based ISBNet baseline, 400 in the transformer-based MAFT baseline) that are sampled via two-stage Farthest Point Sampling from the backbone feature map, and these candidates are refined through the proposed IKA and ISG modules before being decoded into final masks via dynamic convolution.

## Method

IKNE introduces two complementary modules that enhance instance candidate features. First, Instance-wise Knowledge Aggregation (IKA) computes a pairwise correlation matrix among all candidates, identifies highly correlated pairs (correlation > τ) as fragments of the same instance, and applies a correlation regularization loss that pulls same-instance candidates closer in latent space while pushing unrelated candidates apart. Second, Instance-wise Structural Guidance (ISG) applies truncated singular value decomposition (SVD) to the first-stage instance features, retaining only the top t singular vectors to produce a noise-reduced representation, then uses a cross-correlation regularization loss to transfer structural and spatial cues from the denoised features back to the original candidates. Both modules are architecture-agnostic and plug into either kernel-based (ISBNet) or transformer-based (MAFT) backbones, with the total loss combining standard mask (BCE + Dice) and classification losses with the IKA and ISG regularization terms.

## Results

On ScanNetV2 (18 classes), IKNE achieves 62.9 mAP / 81.8 mAP50 / 88.7 mAP25 on validation and 61.7 / 81.2 / 88.0 on the hidden test set, setting new state-of-the-art across all metrics. On S3DIS, it reaches 61.1 AP / 73.0 AP50 (Area 5) and 65.8 / 76.9 (6-fold CV). On the fine-grained ScanNet200 (200 classes), IKNE scores 29.9 mAP / 38.9 mAP50, and on the outdoor STPLS3D dataset it achieves 64.9 mAP / 81.2 mAP50 / 87.6 mAP25, surpassing all prior methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IKNE outperforms all 18 compared baselines across all four datasets. On ScanNetV2, it edges out the closest competitor SphMask [41] by +0.6 mAP and +1.9 mAP50 (62.9 vs. 62.3; 81.8 vs. 79.9), and surpasses the kernel-based ISBNet [32] by +6.1 mAP and the transformer-based MAFT [29] by +4.5 mAP. On S3DIS Area 5, IKNE leads SphMask by +0.6 AP and +0.7 AP50, and EASE [37] by +2.1 AP. On ScanNet200 (200 fine-grained classes), IKNE beats MAFT [29] by +0.7 mAP and +0.7 mAP50, and Q-Former [31] by +1.8 mAP. On the outdoor STPLS3D dataset, IKNE surpasses Mask3D [39] by +1.5 mAP, +2.0 mAP50, and +2.0 mAP25, confirming generalization beyond indoor scenes. The gains are consistent across both kernel-based and transformer-based backbones, indicating the IKA and ISG modules are architecture-agnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

IKNE builds directly on two dominant 3DIS paradigms: kernel-based architectures (ISBNet) that decode instance masks via dynamic convolution with instance-aware kernels, and transformer-based architectures (MAFT) that use learned instance queries with attention. Rather than proposing a new backbone or decoding strategy, the paper introduces two plug-and-play modules—Instance-wise Knowledge Aggregation (IKA) and Instance-wise Structural Guidance (ISG)—that operate on the hundreds of candidate features these frameworks already produce. IKA draws on correlation regularization ideas from self-supervised learning (VICReg, Barlow Twins) to encourage candidates representing the same instance to cluster in latent space, while ISG repurposes truncated SVD, a classical linear-algebra denoising tool, to 3D point cloud features—a setting where SVD has received limited attention per the authors' survey.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical underutilization: modern 3DIS methods generate 256–400 instance candidates but treat them largely independently, leaving inter-candidate structure untapped. The ablation showing IKA alone yields +5.9 mAP (kernel) / +0.7 mAP (transformer) over baseline suggests that correlation-aware candidate grouping is a meaningful, previously overlooked signal. However, the overall SOTA margins are modest (+0.6 mAP over SphMask on ScanNetV2), and the method introduces additional hyperparameters (threshold τ, SVD truncation ratio t, loss weights) that require careful tuning. Open questions include whether the correlation-based instance grouping could be learned end-to-end rather than thresholded, and whether SVD is truly the optimal low-rank denoising operator for unordered point cloud features versus learned alternatives.
