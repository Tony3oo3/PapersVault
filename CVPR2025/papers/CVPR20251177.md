## Problem

Existing parameter-based augmentation methods for adversarial attacks on face recognition (e.g., LGV) initialize surrogate models solely with pre-trained parameters, limiting parameter diversity and thus transferability. Additionally, FR head models are frequently not released as open-source, making it impractical to apply traditional parameter augmentation that requires access to both backbone and head weights. This paper addresses both limitations by diversifying parameter initializations and eliminating the dependency on a pre-trained FR head.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251177/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source face image x^s and a target face image x^t (both RGB) and produces a single adversarial face example x^adv that visually resembles the source but causes a victim FR model to embed it close to the target. The perturbation is constrained by an L∞ norm bound of ε=10 (out of 255), and the attack is iterative with up to 200 FGSM-style steps. The surrogate model is trained on BUPT-BalancedFace during the DPO stage, while evaluation is performed on LFW and CelebA-HQ against 10+ victim models including adversarially robust variants.

## Method

DPA consists of two stages. In Diverse Parameters Optimization (DPO), the surrogate model's backbone and head parameters are initialized with both pre-trained values and random noise; the model is then trained for c epochs on BUPT-BalancedFace using an ArcFace-style loss, and intermediate parameter snapshots are saved at intervals of κ=⌊√c⌋ to form a diverse surrogate model set V_c^q. In Hard Model Aggregation (HMA), beneficial perturbations—computed as sign(∇_ω L̃_{t-1}) with step size η—are added to pre-defined intermediate feature maps of each surrogate model, transforming them into hard models that increase loss for the current adversarial example. The final adversarial update is then computed by averaging the loss across all hard models and applying an FGSM step with clipping to the ε-ball around the source image. This dual mechanism—diverse parameter initialization expanding the surrogate model space, and hard-model feature perturbation increasing optimization difficulty—jointly boosts black-box transferability without requiring access to the victim's FR head.

## Results

Using MobileFace as the surrogate model on LFW, DPA achieves black-box ASR of 67.7% on IR152, 98.2% on IRSE50, and 90.8% on FaceNet, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline DI (18.4%, 97.3%, 32.9%). On adversarially robust models, DPA reaches 55.4% (IR152^adv), 66.4% (IRSE50^adv), 42.6% (FaceNet^adv), and 71.6% (MobileFace^adv), compared to at most 18.2% for DI. The ablation confirms both stages contribute: DPO alone lifts IR152 ASR from 5.3% (vanilla) to 35.9%, and adding HMA further raises it to 67.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPA dominates all nine baselines across every victim model and both normal and adversarially robust settings. The largest margins appear on IR152 and FaceNet: DPA reaches 67.7% and 90.8% ASR versus 18.4% and 32.9% for the strongest input-based baseline DI [59], a gap of ~49 and ~58 percentage points respectively. Among feature-based methods, BPFA [71] (15.8% on IR152) and SIA [52] (15.7%) are the closest competitors but still trail DPA by over 50 points. The parameter-based baseline LGV [12] (Table 2) scores only 4.8% on IR152 and 15.7% on FaceNet, confirming that static pre-trained initialization severely limits transferability. On adversarially robust models, DPA maintains 55.4–71.6% ASR while the best baseline (DI) peaks at 38.6%, demonstrating that the diverse-parameter strategy generalizes even against trained defenses. Under JPEG compression (Figure 5), DPA consistently outperforms baselines across all quality factors on both LFW and CelebA-HQ.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parameter-based augmentation line of work initiated by LGV (Gubri et al., ECCV 2022) and Wu et al. (CVPR 2024), which collect multiple surrogate model parameter sets through additional training to improve adversarial transferability. The key departure is introducing diverse initializations—both pre-trained and randomly initialized parameters—rather than relying solely on pre-trained starting points, and incorporating intermediate-epoch checkpoints to broaden the surrogate model set. The HMA stage builds directly on BPFA (Zhou et al., 2024) and the beneficial perturbations framework (Wen & Itti, 2020), applying hard-model augmentation on top of the diversified parameter set. The paper also addresses a practical gap: most FR heads are not publicly released, making traditional parameter augmentation infeasible for many open-source FR backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical observation that FR heads are typically unavailable makes the random-initialization component of DPO a genuinely useful workaround for black-box attackers targeting real-world FR systems. The very large ASR gains (e.g., ~68% vs. ~5–16% on IR152) suggest that parameter diversity from random initializations and intermediate epochs is a substantially underexploited source of transferability. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond FR to other recognition or classification tasks, whether the gains persist against stronger defenses (e.g., adversarially trained ensembles), and whether the compute cost of training multiple random-init models scales acceptably. The paper does not report wall-clock training time for the DPO stage, which is a practical concern for adoption.
