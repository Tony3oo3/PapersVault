## Problem

Existing desnowing models are trained almost exclusively on synthetic snowfall datasets, creating a significant domain gap that impairs generalization to real-world snowy images. Compounding this, no large-scale real-world snow dataset or dedicated snowfall-specific evaluation metric exists, making it difficult to incorporate real snowy images into training or to reliably assess restoration quality without ground truth.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250042/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The desnowing model (NAFNet-based) takes a single snowy RGB image and outputs a restored image. The evaluation component (MMPO-fine-tuned Q-Instruct) takes the same image and produces four discrete quality scores—snowflake amount, object obscurity, texture detail, and background clarity—each computed as a probability-weighted average over five multiple-choice option tokens (values 1–5) derived from the model's output logits. The full pipeline operates on real-world images with no ground-truth reference, relying entirely on the MLLM's scores to gate pseudo-label updates.

## Method

SnowMaster is a two-phase framework. In phase 1, Multi-Model Preference Optimization (MMPO) fine-tunes Q-Instruct-13B: outputs from four MLLMs (Q-Instruct, LLaVA-1.5, LLaVA-1.6, MiniCPM-V) on 1,500 real snow images are ranked by human experts to form 36K preference pairs, and a DPO-style loss aligns Q-Instruct to human snowfall perception while preserving its low-level visual assessment ability. In phase 2, the fine-tuned Q-Instruct serves as an unsupervised scorer within a mean-teacher semi-supervised loop: a pseudo-label database is updated only when at least three of the four evaluation dimensions show the teacher score exceeding both the student score and the stored database score, and the NAFNet student is trained with a PSNR supervised loss on Snow100K for the first 20k steps before an L1 pseudo-label loss on 6,406 real images is added. The key insight is that no single MLLM dominates all four snowfall evaluation dimensions, so aggregating multi-model preferences into one model via MMPO yields a more reliable and comprehensive evaluator than any individual model.

## Results

On the RealSnow10K test set, SnowMaster achieves the best scores across all four non-reference metrics: BRISQUE 17.546, NIQE 3.058, PIQE 29.742, and PAQ2-PIQ 70.304. On Snow100K-realistic, it similarly leads with BRISQUE 16.305, NIQE 2.797, and PIQE 30.162, while tying T3-DiffWeather on PAQ2-PIQ (70.042). The MMPO-enhanced Q-Instruct also improves snowfall evaluation accuracy over the base Q-Instruct by +5.83% (snowflake amount) and +7.64% (object obscure), while also gaining +6.59% and +5.16% on texture and background metrics that were not explicitly targeted.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the five desnowing baselines, SnowMaster outperforms all on both datasets. On RealSnow10K, it beats the closest competitor TransWeather by 1.018 on BRISQUE (17.546 vs. 18.564) and 0.346 on NIQE (3.058 vs. 3.404), while T3-DiffWeather, the strongest diffusion-based method, is surpassed by 2.665 on BRISQUE and 0.079 on NIQE. On Snow100K-realistic, SnowMaster leads TransWeather by 0.239 on BRISQUE and 0.228 on NIQE, and edges out T3-DiffWeather by 1.439 on BRISQUE and 0.071 on NIQE. For the MLLM evaluation comparison (Table 1), MMPO-enhanced Q-Instruct surpasses LLaVA-v1.6-13b on object obscure (80.90% vs. 79.56%) and MiniCPM-V on all four metrics, though LLaVA-v1.6 retains a slight edge on snowflake amount (86.34% vs. 82.14%), which the authors attribute to the preference data being sourced from those same models. NAFNet and SnowFormer, the CNN and Transformer baselines respectively, show the weakest performance on RealSnow10K (BRISQUE 20.425 and 23.702), highlighting the domain gap that SnowMaster's semi-supervised real-data training addresses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLLM-based image quality assessment line (Q-Instruct, Q-Align) into the weather-degradation domain by introducing Multi-Model Preference Optimization (MMPO), which generalizes DPO's single-model preference pairs to aggregate ranked outputs from multiple MLLMs (LLaVA, MiniCPM-V, Q-Instruct) into a unified evaluator. For the restoration side, it builds on NAFNet as a CNN backbone and adapts the mean-teacher semi-supervised framework with a pseudo-label database, but replaces conventional non-reference IQA metrics with MLLM-generated multi-dimensional snowfall scores. The key departure from prior desnowing work is the explicit use of real-world data (RealSnow10K) and a learned perceptual evaluator to bridge the synthetic-to-real domain gap, rather than relying solely on synthetic Snow100K training pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RealSnow10K dataset (10K+ real snowy images with multi-dimensional annotations) fills a notable gap in the desnowing literature, where prior work has been dominated by synthetic data. The MMPO framework offers a reusable recipe for adapting any MLLM evaluator to a new degradation domain by collecting multi-model preference pairs, which could transfer to rain, fog, or haze. However, the desnowing gains over strong baselines (e.g., TransWeather, T3-DiffWeather) are modest, the approach is computationally heavy due to multi-angle MLLM scoring at each pseudo-label update, and the method has only been validated on a single CNN backbone (NAFNet), leaving open whether the MLLM-guided semi-supervised paradigm generalizes to Transformer or diffusion-based restorers.
