## Problem

Existing unsupervised 3D semantic segmentation methods (GrowSP, PointDC) rely on per-point local features and simple K-means grouping, which cannot capture richer semantic priors beyond local appearance. GrowSP's randomly initialized backbone produces weak initial features, while PointDC's supervoxel clustering is error-prone and yields inaccurate pseudo-labels. The paper asks whether global structural patterns in the frequency domain can be exploited to generate more discriminative semantic pseudo-labels without any human annotations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D point clouds (xyz + rgb, N×6 per scene) together with K calibrated 2D RGB/D images per scene, which are used only during training to distill DINOv2 features into 3D. At test time, only the point cloud is needed and no superpoints are constructed. The output is a per-point semantic class label from a user-specified number of categories C (e.g., 20 for ScanNet, 12 for S3DIS, 16 for nuScenes), with Hungarian matching applied to align predicted and ground-truth label orderings during evaluation.

## Method

LogoSP combines three modules: (1) a 2D-to-3D distillation step that projects DINOv2 pixel features onto 3D points and trains a SparseConv to regress them, yielding per-point features with semantic priors; (2) a bottom-up superpoint growing loop that iteratively merges superpoints via K-means on mean features over T rounds; and (3) a top-down pseudo-label generator that builds a global superpoint graph across all H training scenes, computes its normalized Laplacian, and decomposes it via eigendecomposition to obtain S global frequency-domain patterns. The superpoint features are transformed to the frequency domain via Graph Fourier Transform, the S frequency vectors are clustered into S' groups by K-means, and the corresponding eigenvectors are merged to form S' semantically richer global patterns. Finally, K-means on the row vectors of the merged pattern matrix assigns C semantic pseudo-labels to all superpoints, which supervise the SparseConv for the next round of growth. This local-global grouping in the frequency domain is the key mechanism that captures global structural semantics (e.g., flat planes, curved surfaces, edges) that purely local feature similarity cannot distinguish.

## Results

On ScanNet validation (20 classes), LogoSP achieves 35.8% mIoU, 50.8% mAcc, and 64.7% OA, outperforming the strongest unsupervised baseline PointDC-DINOv2 (29.6% mIoU) by 6.2 points and GrowSP (25.4% mIoU) by 10.4 points. On S3DIS 6-fold cross-validation (12 classes), LogoSP reaches 46.3% mIoU, surpassing GrowSP (44.6%) and PointDC-DINOv2 (41.3%). On the outdoor nuScenes dataset (16 classes), LogoSP achieves 20.1% mIoU, the best among all unsupervised methods, though the gap over PointDC-DINOv2 (18.2%) is smaller.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LogoSP decisively outperforms all unsupervised baselines across all three datasets. On ScanNet, the 6.2-point mIoU gain over PointDC-DINOv2 (35.8% vs 29.6%) is the most meaningful comparison since both use DINOv2 features, isolating the contribution of the local-global superpoint grouping. Against GrowSP, the 10.4-point mIoU improvement (35.8% vs 25.4%) reflects both the benefit of DINOv2-distilled features and the frequency-domain grouping. On S3DIS 6-fold, LogoSP leads GrowSP by 1.7 points (46.3% vs 44.6%) and PointDC-DINOv2 by 5.0 points (46.3% vs 41.3%), though the margin over GrowSP is modest due to sparse 2D imagery in S3DIS. On nuScenes, LogoSP wins by 1.9 points over PointDC-DINOv2 (20.1% vs 18.2%) and by 9.9 points over GrowSP (20.1% vs 10.2%), successfully identifying minor categories like person and truck that all baselines miss. The 2D-adapted baselines (IIC, PiCIE) perform poorly in 3D, confirming the domain gap highlighted in prior work.

## Relation to Prior Work

LogoSP sits at the intersection of two recent unsupervised 3D segmentation lines: GrowSP's bottom-up superpoint growing with K-means grouping, and PointDC's 2D-to-3D feature distillation from self-supervised pretrained models. It departs from GrowSP by replacing randomly initialized features with DINOv2-distilled features and by substituting the simplistic K-means grouping with a top-down Graph Fourier Transform (GFT) on a global superpoint graph, which captures frequency-domain patterns that encode richer semantic structure. The 2D-to-3D projection strategy follows OpenScene, and the overall iterative grow-cluster-train loop is inherited from GrowSP, making the paper a principled synthesis of these threads with the GFT-based global grouping as its core novelty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a clear new SOTA for unsupervised 3D semantic segmentation with consistent 5–10 mIoU gains over the strongest baselines across indoor and outdoor datasets, and the visualization of learned global patterns provides rare interpretability evidence that frequency-domain superpoint features genuinely encode semantics without human labels. However, the outdoor (nuScenes) results are notably weaker, revealing that DINOv2's 2D semantic priors do not transfer well to LiDAR driving scenes, which limits the method's practical reach. The authors identify extending to sequential point clouds as a natural next step, and the cross-dataset generalization from S3DIS to ScanNet remains poor due to S3DIS's sparse 2D imagery, leaving open whether the approach can work in data-scarce regimes.
