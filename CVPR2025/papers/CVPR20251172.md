## Problem

Off-the-shelf video diffusion models trained on short clips (<24 frames) produce low-quality long videos with broken temporal consistency and missing visual details. The paper identifies two root causes via theoretical analysis: temporal position ambiguity (the model cannot distinguish among the large number of relative frame positions in long sequences) and information dilution (per-frame information content decreases as sequence length grows). Existing training-free workarounds (sliding windows, frequency-domain blending) remain suboptimal.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt and produces a 128-frame video at 320×512 resolution in one forward denoising pass, with no sliding windows or autoregressive chunking. It operates on pretrained 16-frame short-video models (LaVie, VideoCrafter-512) by simply extending the initial noise sequence length; no parameter updates or additional training data are required. The only architectural change is to the temporal transformer layers, while spatial transformer layers remain untouched.

## Method

LongDiff introduces two training-free modifications to the temporal self-attention of short-video diffusion models. Position Mapping (PM) first applies a GROUP operation that collapses 2N−1 relative positions into 2G−1 groups, then a SHIFT operation that applies M=S−1 successive shifts to the grouped position matrix and averages the resulting softmax attentions, recovering fine-grained position distinguishability within each group. Informative Frame Selection (IFS) constructs a pseudo-video from intermediate hidden states via channel pooling, detects key frames using image entropy and frame-differencing scores, and inserts an attention mask that restricts each query frame to attend only to its L nearest neighbors plus the selected key frames. Together, PM addresses temporal position ambiguity and IFS addresses information dilution, enabling high-quality 128-frame generation in a single denoising pass with only a minor inference-speed overhead.

## Results

On VBench (200 prompts, 128-frame generation), LongDiff achieves the best scores across all six metrics on both tested models. With LaVie, LongDiff reaches SC=98.10, BC=98.23, MS=97.46, TF=96.84, IQ=68.83, and OC=25.24. With VideoCrafter-512, it achieves SC=93.69, BC=95.59, MS=94.59, TF=93.35, IQ=70.03, and OC=23.17. The largest gains over the Direct baseline (which simply extends the noise sequence) are in Subject Consistency (+4.15 for LaVie) and Overall Consistency (+2.90 for LaVie), confirming that the method addresses both temporal coherence and visual detail degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LongDiff outperforms both FreeNoise and FreeLong on all six VBench metrics for both VideoCrafter-512 and LaVie. Against FreeNoise (sliding-window attention + noise rescheduling), LongDiff gains the most on Subject Consistency (+5.80 for LaVie, +2.26 for VideoCrafter-512) and Overall Consistency (+0.82 and +0.48 respectively), indicating superior global temporal coherence. Against FreeLong (frequency-domain spectral blending), LongDiff leads by +2.94 SC and +0.68 OC on LaVie, and by a wider margin on VideoCrafter-512 (+5.48 MS, +4.89 TF, +3.41 IQ), suggesting FreeLong's frequency-domain approach is less effective at preserving motion smoothness and temporal flickering on that architecture. The Sliding baseline (temporal sliding windows) is consistently weaker than both FreeNoise and FreeLong, confirming that local-window attention alone is insufficient for long-range consistency. The Direct baseline (no modification) shows the largest degradation, particularly in Temporal Flickering (78.53 for VideoCrafter-512) and Subject Consistency (88.62), validating the paper's diagnosis of temporal position ambiguity and information dilution.

## Relation to Prior Work

LongDiff builds directly on short-video diffusion models (LaVie, VideoCrafter) that employ temporal transformers with relative positional encodings (RoPE or Shaw-style RPE), and draws theoretical motivation from LM-Infinite's pseudo-dimension analysis of long-sequence generalization in LLMs. It departs from prior training-free long-video methods (FreeNoise, FreeLong, Gen-LVideo) that rely on sliding windows or frequency-domain blending by instead making minimal, targeted modifications to the temporal self-attention mechanism itself—specifically, a position-mapping scheme (GROUP + SHIFT) and an informative-frame-selection mask. The conceptual framing of the short-to-long failure mode as two distinct, theoretically-grounded challenges (temporal position ambiguity and information dilution) is the paper's primary departure from the empirically-driven designs of earlier training-free approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, one-go formulation is practically attractive because it requires no long-video datasets, no retraining, and no sliding-window bookkeeping, making it easy to plug into any off-the-shelf short-video model with temporal transformers. However, the approach is validated only at 128 frames on two base models, and the key-frame detection heuristic (image entropy + SAD on pseudo-frames) is ad hoc and may not generalize to very long sequences or diverse content. Open questions include whether the GROUP+SHIFT position mapping degrades gracefully beyond 128 frames, whether the IFS mask's fixed neighbor-window hyperparameter L needs to scale with video length, and whether the theoretical bounds (Theorems 1 and 2) can be tightened to predict the optimal G and L for a given model.
