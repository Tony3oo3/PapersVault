## Problem

Existing AI-generated image detectors exhibit inconsistent and biased behavior because their training data contains spurious correlations in format, resolution, and semantic content, causing them to learn dataset-specific shortcuts rather than true generation artifacts. This leads to poor generalization to unseen generators and significant performance degradation on real-world images shared online. The paper argues that careful training data design, rather than algorithmic novelty, is the key bottleneck for robust forensic detection.

## Input / Output

The detector consumes RGB images as 504×504 pixel crops without any resizing operation, preserving subtle low-level forensic cues that would be washed out by resampling. At inference, larger images are split into multiple 504×504 crops whose predictions are averaged. The output is a calibrated probability score for the fake class (not merely a hard label), enabling evaluation via Expected Calibration Error and balanced Negative Log-Likelihood alongside standard accuracy metrics.

## Method

B-Free constructs a bias-free training set by generating fake images through self-conditioned reconstruction: each real COCO image is fed into Stable Diffusion 2.1's inpainting model with an empty mask, forcing the full diffusion denoising pipeline to regenerate the same content while embedding generator-specific artifacts at multiple frequency bands (including lower frequencies absent in simple autoencoder reconstructions). Content augmentation is then applied via inpainting that replaces objects with same- or different-category substitutes, optionally restoring the original background, combined with standard operations (cutmix, mixup, scaling, cut-out, noise, jittering). The detector is a DINOv2 ViT with four registers, fine-tuned end-to-end (not linear probing) on the 51k real / 309k fake dataset, with class-balanced sampling during training. The key insight is that the full diffusion pass introduces forensic inconsistencies across a broader frequency range than autoencoder-only reconstruction, and aggressive content augmentation is essential for calibration and robustness under compression, resizing, and repeated online re-posting.

## Results

On the primary evaluation across 10 generators (Midjourney, SDXL, DALL-E 2/3, Firefly, FLUX, SD 3.5, and three WildRF social-media subsets), B-Free achieves an average balanced accuracy of 96.3% and NLL of 0.10, outperforming the second-best method (CoDE, 67.3% bAcc) by +20.7%. On the additional in-the-wild dataset SynthWildX, B-Free reaches 95.6% bAcc with NLL 0.14, while most competitors fall below 75%. The method also maintains >92% average accuracy on viral images shared online even after 100 days of repeated reposting, whereas most SoTA detectors drop below 70% after one day.

## Baselines & Comparisons

B-Free decisively outperforms all 15 baselines on every generator and dataset. The closest competitor is CoDE (contrastive learning, ECCV 2024) at 67.3% avg bAcc, trailing B-Free by 29 percentage points; DeFake (72.3%) and DMID (75.6%) are next but still far behind. Several methods (CNNDetect, DIRE, NPR, LaDeDa) perform at or near chance level (~50% bAcc) on most generators, indicating they fail to capture generalizable forensic artifacts. Notably, DMID achieves near-perfect scores on Midjourney and SDXL (its training generators) but collapses to ~50% on DALL-E 2/3, illustrating severe overfitting to known architectures. On the in-the-wild SynthWildX dataset, B-Free reaches 95.6% bAcc while the best competitor (CoDE) manages only 72.3%, and on viral social-media images B-Free sustains ~92% accuracy over 100 days of reposting versus <70% for all other methods. The ablation in Table 4 further shows that swapping the training dataset (ProGAN or LDM) into RINE or CLIP architectures yields substantially lower performance than using the B-Free dataset, confirming that the training paradigm itself is the primary driver of the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in AI-generated image detection by shifting focus from algorithmic innovation to training data construction. It builds on the observation from Wang et al. [43] that training set composition drives generalization, and on concurrent work by Sundara Rajan et al. [35] that used autoencoder reconstruction for semantic alignment, but departs by performing the full self-conditioned diffusion pass (rather than a single autoencoder encode-decode) and adding inpainting-based content augmentation. The detection backbone (DINOv2 with registers, fine-tuned end-to-end) is standard; the contribution is entirely in the bias-free data pipeline that ensures real/fake pairs share semantic content while preserving generation-specific low-level artifacts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that careful dataset curation can outperform algorithmic complexity by a wide margin (≈20% bAcc over the second-best method), and that SOTA detectors' published results may be substantially inflated by dataset biases (format, content, resolution). The calibration results (ECE, NLL) and the viral-image robustness experiment (92% accuracy after many days of social-media reposting) are directly relevant to real-world deployment. Open questions include whether the paradigm generalizes to future generators with fundamentally different synthesis processes, and how to defend against adversarial attacks on the detector.
