## Problem

Additive PEFT methods (LoRA, AdaptFormer, Adapter) outperform non-additive alternatives for long-tailed and cross-domain adaptation, but their performance is extremely sensitive to hyperparameters—specifically intra-block insertion position, block depth, and scaling factor—that exhibit complex non-monotonic interactions. Manual or exhaustive search over this mixed discrete-continuous configuration space is computationally infeasible (O(L|S|N_α) configurations), and gradient-based optimization is blocked by the discrete nature of position choices.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pretrained ViT (ViT-B/16 or ViT-L/16) and fine-tunes it on RS images (optical DOTA at 188K samples, SAR FUSRS v2 at 5,261 samples) or natural images (CIFAR-100-IR100, Places-LT, iNaturalist-2018), producing a class label per image. The trainable parameters consist of the standard PEFT module (e.g., LoRA low-rank matrices) plus a set of per-position modulator scalars (fewer than 800 additional parameters for LoRA on ViT-B/16), while all pretrained weights remain frozen. The modulator is initialized to 1.0 and constrained non-negative via softplus activation.

## Method

MetaPEFT unifies the discrete positional indicator (1_p) and continuous scaling factor (α) of additive PEFT into a single differentiable modulator γ per position, so that γ≈0 deactivates the module and γ>0 controls adaptation strength, converting the intractable MINLP problem into a continuous optimization. The modulator is learned via bi-level optimization: an inner loop (SGD, lr=1e-2) trains PEFT parameters with γ frozen, while an outer loop (Adam) updates γ on a randomly sampled 20% stratified validation subset drawn from the training set, alternating every K inner steps. This dynamic sampling acts as implicit regularization that particularly benefits tail classes (3.5× higher accuracy improvement for tail vs. head classes in the SatMAE→SAR setting). The approach is plug-and-play, requiring no architectural changes to the underlying PEFT method (LoRA, Adapter, or AdaptFormer) and no additional training data beyond the standard train/validation split.

## Results

On the three transfer scenarios (IN21K→iNat2018, IN21K→DOTA, SatMAE→SAR), MetaPEFT combined with LoRA achieves the best average tail-class accuracy of 81.63% and overall average of 77.91%, outperforming all non-additive and additive baselines. The most significant gain appears in the cross-spectral SatMAE→SAR scenario, where LoRA+MetaPEFT improves tail-class accuracy from 72.1% to 74.2% (+2.1 pp) and head accuracy from 40.0% to 40.6%. On DOTA (IN21K→DOTA), LoRA+MetaPEFT reaches 91.4% tail accuracy vs. 90.7% for vanilla LoRA, and 95.1% medium accuracy. The method requires fewer than 800 additional parameters (0.0008M) on ViT-B/16 for the modulator.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetaPEFT consistently improves all three additive baselines (Adapter, AdaptFormer, LoRA) but with varying magnitudes. LoRA benefits the most: MetaPEFT adds +1.13 pp overall accuracy and +1.2 pp on SAR tail classes, yielding the best Avg_tail of 81.63% vs. 80.43% for vanilla LoRA. Adapter gains a modest +0.14 pp Avg_tail (81.37→81.60), while AdaptFormer shows a slight decrease in Avg (76.70→76.60) despite a small tail improvement on iNat2018 (78.0→78.2), suggesting the modulator's benefit is most pronounced for low-rank methods. Against non-additive baselines, the gap is substantial: VPT-Shallow trails by 9.4 pp in Avg_tail (72.23 vs. 81.63), and BitFit, while competitive on DOTA (89.1% tail), collapses on SAR (74.7% tail) and iNat2018 (68.4% tail), confirming that additive methods with MetaPEFT dominate across all three transfer scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the PEFT literature (LoRA, AdaptFormer, Adapter, BitFit, VPT) by identifying that their hyperparameters—specifically intra-block insertion position, block depth, and scaling factor—form a mixed discrete-continuous optimization problem that resists both manual tuning and standard gradient-based methods. It draws on meta-learning frameworks (MAML, Meta-SGD) and differentiable architecture search (DARTS) to propose a unified differentiable modulator that relaxes the discrete positional choices into continuous scalars, optimized via bi-level optimization. The long-tailed learning literature (LDAM, cRT, BBN) informs the evaluation protocol, but the paper's core contribution is orthogonal: it treats PEFT hyperparameter selection as a learnable, end-to-end optimization problem rather than a fixed design choice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that individually optimal PEFT hyperparameters (e.g., FFN position + deepest block) combine to produce performance degradation is practically important, as it undermines the common heuristic of selecting each hyperparameter independently. MetaPEFT's modulator adds negligible overhead (<800 parameters) and is generic across additive PEFT methods, making it a drop-in improvement for practitioners adapting foundation models to data-scarce or long-tailed domains. Open questions include whether the learned modulator transfers across tasks or requires re-optimization per dataset, whether the approach extends to non-ViT backbones (e.g., convolutional or hybrid architectures), and whether the modest 1–2% gains scale to larger models or more extreme domain gaps.
