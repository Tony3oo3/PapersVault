## Problem

Reconstruction-based 3D point cloud anomaly detection methods assign equal loss weights to normal and pseudo-abnormal points, diluting the model's focus on the anomalous deviations that are critical for detection. This issue is compounded by the inherently disordered and sparse nature of point cloud data, making it difficult for the network to effectively distill normal representations under the anomaly-free training setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud (N×3 coordinates) as input, which is voxelized (voxel size 0.03) and normalized to [-1, 1] before processing. During training, a pseudo-anomaly is generated from the normal sample via Norm-AS, and the ground-truth offset (pseudo-anomaly minus normal) serves as the supervision label. At inference, the network outputs per-point 3D offset predictions, from which point-level anomaly scores are computed as the L1 norm of predicted offsets and object-level scores as the mean over all points, directly yielding anomaly maps without any post-hoc comparison step.

## Method

PO3AD replaces the standard reconstruction objective with point offset prediction: a MinkUNet sparse-convolutional backbone extracts voxelized features, and a 3-layer MLP predicts the displacement vector (magnitude and direction) of each point relative to its normal counterpart. The training loss combines an L1 term for offset distance and a negative cosine term for offset direction, where the direction loss applies only to pseudo-abnormal points (normal points have zero ground-truth offset), naturally forcing the network to concentrate capacity on anomalous regions. Pseudo-anomalies are generated by Norm-AS, which moves a randomly selected patch of points along or opposite to their surface normal vectors with a distance-weighted falloff, producing geometrically credible bulge/concavity deformations that avoid the overlap artifacts of direction-agnostic augmentation. During inference, the predicted offset magnitudes directly serve as anomaly scores, eliminating the need for input-output comparison metrics used by reconstruction-based baselines.

## Results

On Anomaly-ShapeNet, PO3AD achieves an average object-level AUC-ROC of 83.9% and point-level AUC-ROC of 89.8%, surpassing the second-best method R3D-AD (74.9% / 74.9%) by 9.0% and 23.0% respectively, and attaining the best mean rank (1.3 vs. 2.2). On Real3D-AD, PO3AD reaches 76.5% object-level AUC-ROC with a mean rank of 3.2, edging out Group3AD (75.1%, rank 3.6) by 1.4%. The method also demonstrates robustness to Gaussian noise, with performance degradation remaining below the clean-data performance of all competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PO3AD outperforms all eight baselines on both datasets. On Anomaly-ShapeNet, the gap is most pronounced against reconstruction-based R3D-AD (74.9% vs. 83.9%, +9.0%) and IMRNet (66.1%, +17.8%), while memory-bank methods (Reg3D-AD 57.2%, PatchCore 56.8%) and classical-feature methods (BTF 49.3–52.8%, M3DM 55.2%) trail by 25–35 points. On Real3D-AD, where the large train-test scanning gap favors template-registration-based methods, PO3AD still leads Group3AD (75.1%) by 1.4% and R3D-AD (73.4%) by 3.1%, while Reg3D-AD (70.4%) and IMRNet (72.5%) remain competitive but inferior. The mean-rank metric confirms PO3AD's consistent superiority: rank 1.3 on Anomaly-ShapeNet (vs. 2.2 for R3D-AD) and rank 3.2 on Real3D-AD (vs. 3.6 for Group3AD), indicating fewer categories where it underperforms. Notably, PO3AD achieves near-perfect scores (≥95%) on several Anomaly-ShapeNet categories (e.g., ashtray0 100%, bowl4 98.1%, eraser0 99.5%), whereas no baseline exceeds 90% on any single category.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant reconstruction-based paradigm for 3D point cloud anomaly detection (IMRNet, R3D-AD), which trains models to restore normal samples from masked or pseudo-abnormal variants. Instead, it repurposes point offset prediction—a technique well-established in 3D instance segmentation and object detection—into the anomaly detection setting, reframing the learning objective from coordinate restoration to displacement estimation. The Norm-AS augmentation extends R3D-AD's pseudo-anomaly generation by incorporating normal-vector guidance to control point displacement direction, addressing the overlap problem between pseudo-abnormal and normal regions. The backbone (MinkUNet) and overall pipeline structure follow standard sparse convolutional architectures, so the novelty is primarily in the task formulation and augmentation strategy rather than architectural design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean conceptual argument—that reconstruction-based methods inherently dilute model attention between normal and pseudo-abnormal points, whereas offset prediction naturally focuses learning on anomalous regions—provides a principled alternative to the reconstruction paradigm that could influence future 3D anomaly detection research. However, the one-model-per-category training paradigm remains a significant practical limitation, as each of the 40+ categories requires a separately trained network, making deployment in real industrial settings costly. The modest 1.4% improvement on Real3D-AD (versus 9.0% on the synthetic Anomaly-ShapeNet) suggests the advantage may be less pronounced under the large train-test distribution gap of real scanned data. Open questions include whether the offset prediction framework can be adapted to a one-model-all-category setting and whether it generalizes to anomaly types beyond the bulge/concavity categories that Norm-AS can simulate.
