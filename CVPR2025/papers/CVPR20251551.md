## Problem

Existing diffusion bias mitigation methods either retrain models or apply external guidance to the generation process, but none investigate the intrinsic neuron-level mechanisms that causally drive biased outputs. The polysemanticity of neurons in diffusion models further complicates fine-grained mechanistic analysis, leaving the specific internal features responsible for social bias (gender, race, age) unidentified and uncontrollable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251551/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on the bottleneck-layer hidden activations (n-dimensional vectors) of the U-Net during diffusion generation. For unconditional models (P2 on CelebA-HQ), the input is random noise; for text-conditional models (Stable Diffusion on LAION), it is a text prompt plus random noise. The k-SAE projects these n-dimensional activations into a much higher-dimensional (m >> n) sparse space where only k features are active per sample, and the final output is a generated image whose bias level is controlled by scaling or shifting the identified bias features, alongside the set of bias-specific feature indices.

## Method

DIFFLENS is a three-stage mechanistic interpretability framework. First, a k-Sparse Autoencoder (k-SAE) disentangles polysemantic neuron activations into a sparse monosemantic feature space, where each dimension corresponds to a distinct semantic concept. Second, a gradient-based attribution method (integrated gradients, Eq. 6) scores each sparse feature by integrating the gradient of a lightweight bias classifier along the path from a baseline to the full feature activation, selecting the top-τ features as bias-specific. Third, intervention is applied exclusively to these identified features via scalar multiplication or additive bias (Eq. 7), then the modified sparse vector is decoded back to the original latent space for continued denoising. This targeted intervention in the sparse space avoids the collateral damage of editing the full polysemantic latent vector, enabling precise bias control while preserving non-target attributes like facial expression and background coherence.

## Results

On the unconditional P2 model (CelebA-HQ), DIFFLENS achieves near-perfect gender balance (FD=0.002) with the best FID (31.93) and highest CLIP-I (0.9479), outperforming all baselines across gender, age, and race. On the text-to-image Stable Diffusion model (LAION), DIFFLENS attains the lowest FD for gender (0.046) and age (0.049) while also achieving the best FID (112.83 and 99.17 respectively) and highest CLIP-I scores, demonstrating simultaneous bias mitigation and quality preservation. The method also achieves the best race FD (0.401) and CLIP-I (0.9096) on Stable Diffusion, where Finetuning and Latent Direction struggle with multi-class attributes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIFFLENS outperforms all five baselines on the primary bias metric (FD) for gender and age on Stable Diffusion, achieving FD=0.046 vs. 0.050 (Finetuning) and 0.049 vs. 0.052 (Latent Direction) respectively, while also maintaining the best FID (112.83 vs. 161.47 for Finetuning, 113.81 for Latent Direction). Latent Editing [32] degrades FID severely (166–201) and fails to reduce age/race bias meaningfully. H-Distribution [43] achieves moderate FD reduction but at the cost of higher FID (147–152) and lower CLIP-I. Latent Direction [33] is competitive on age (FD=0.052) and race (FD=0.175) but overcorrects, dropping CLIP-I to 0.8151 and CLIP-T to 0.6067. Finetuning [54] excels on gender (FD=0.050) but catastrophically fails on age (FD=0.746, worse than the original 0.752) and incurs the highest FID (161.47) due to full model retraining. On the unconditional P2 model, DIFFLENS achieves near-zero gender FD (0.002) with the best FID (31.93), while Activation [6] barely reduces bias (FD=0.190) and degrades quality (FID=34.27).

## Relation to Prior Work

DIFFLENS transplants two established mechanistic-interpretability tools—k-Sparse Autoencoders (Makhzani & Frey, ICLR 2014) for disentangling polysemantic activations and integrated gradients (Sundararajan et al., ICML 2017) for feature attribution—into the diffusion-model setting, a domain where prior bias-mitigation work has relied on prompt engineering, fine-tuning, or coarse latent-space editing (Latent Editing, H-Distribution, Latent Direction). The key conceptual departure is treating bias as a property of identifiable, monosemantic features inside the U-Net rather than as a distributional shift to be corrected externally, thereby enabling targeted intervention on a minimal set of features instead of the entire latent space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that a small set of sparse-semantic features causally drives social bias in diffusion models, the paper offers a principled, interpretable handle for fine-grained bias control (from balanced to highly skewed ratios) while preserving non-target attributes and image quality—something prior guidance-based methods struggle with at extreme ratios. Limitations include quality degradation under aggressive edits, difficulty isolating racial features, and evaluation restricted to the U-Net bottleneck layer and face-centric datasets. Open questions include whether the same SAE-plus-attribution pipeline transfers to Diffusion Transformers, whether identified features remain stable across model scales, and how to handle multi-attribute interactions without sequential intervention.
