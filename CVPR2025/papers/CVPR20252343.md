## Problem

Existing HDR novel view synthesis methods face a fundamental trade-off: 3D tone mapping decouples HDR rendering from LDR supervision, causing unstable HDR reconstruction, while 2D tone mapping couples them but degrades LDR fitting due to less robust HDR ray accumulation. Additionally, all prior methods employ a global tone mapper that enforces uniform tone-mapping characteristics across the entire scene, ignoring fine-grained spatial variations in the camera response function.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view, multi-exposure LDR images (5 exposure levels per view, with 3 used for training) along with calibrated camera poses and exposure times from COLMAP. It outputs a 3D Gaussian scene parameterized by HDR irradiances (unbounded, [0, +∞)) that can render both novel HDR views and novel LDR views at any user-specified exposure. For Scaffold-GS, the per-anchor feature is projected via a linear layer into a 4-dimensional context feature; for vanilla 3DGS, a new context feature attribute is learned per Gaussian.

## Method

GaussHDR unifies 3D and 2D local tone mapping by assigning each 3D Gaussian a learnable context feature that is rendered to the image plane alongside HDR irradiance, providing spatially-varying conditioning for a shared residual local tone mapper (an MLP that adds a local correction to a global tone-mapping baseline). This single mechanism enables both 3D local tone mapping (tone-mapping each Gaussian before rasterization) and 2D local tone mapping (tone-mapping the rendered HDR image with the rendered feature map). The dual LDR results are combined at the loss level using uncertainty learning: a separate uncertainty MLP predicts per-Gaussian variances that are rendered to uncertainty maps, which adaptively weight the 3D and 2D reconstruction losses without manual hyperparameter tuning. Training proceeds in two stages—first fitting the global tone mapper alone, then enabling the residual MLP and uncertainty predictor jointly—while gradient stopping ensures the uncertainty model does not interfere with scene optimization.

## Results

On the HDR-NeRF synthetic dataset (Exp-3 setting), GaussHDR with Scaffold-GS achieves 43.78 PSNR / 0.990 SSIM / 0.003 LPIPS on LDR-OE, 43.00 PSNR on LDR-NE, and 39.02 PSNR / 0.976 SSIM on HDR, setting new state-of-the-art across all tracks. On real scenes (HDR-NeRF and HDR-Plenoxels), it reaches 36.77 PSNR (LDR-OE) and 33.92 PSNR (LDR-NE) on HDR-NeRF, and 32.85 / 29.72 PSNR on HDR-Plenoxels. The most striking gain is in HDR reconstruction: 39.02 PSNR vs. 29.22 for the HDR-Scaffold-GS baseline, a nearly 10 dB improvement, while also improving LDR fidelity by ~0.6 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HDR-NeRF (re-implemented under Exp-3), GaussHDR (Scaffold-GS) wins on every metric: +3.62 dB PSNR on LDR-OE, +3.71 dB on LDR-NE, and +0.92 dB on HDR. Against HDR-GS (re-implemented without its HDR-GT supervision), the gains are even larger on LDR tracks (+2.65 dB LDR-OE, +3.22 dB LDR-NE) and dramatically larger on HDR (+12.04 dB), confirming that the dual 3D+2D local tone mapping with uncertainty resolves the unstable HDR reconstruction that plagues 3D-only tone mapping. Compared to the closest baseline HDR-Scaffold-GS, GaussHDR improves LDR-OE by +0.62 dB, LDR-NE by +0.65 dB, and HDR by +9.80 dB, showing that the residual local tone mapper and uncertainty-based joint optimization are the primary sources of the gain. Notably, HDR-GS in its original form uses HDR ground-truth supervision on synthetic data, yet GaussHDR without this extra supervision still surpasses it on HDR PSNR (39.02 vs. 38.31 in the original HDR-GS numbers), underscoring the effectiveness of the unified 3D+2D learning framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the HDR novel view synthesis line initiated by HDR-NeRF and accelerated by HDR-GS, which established the 3DGS-based pipeline of predicting HDR irradiances and applying a tone mapper to model the camera response function. The key departure is twofold: (1) replacing the global tone mapper with a residual local tone mapper that accepts per-Gaussian context features (inspired by language-embedded scene representations) to capture spatially varying CRFs, and (2) unifying 3D and 2D tone mapping at the loss level with uncertainty-based adaptive weighting, rather than committing to one or the other as prior methods do. The uncertainty mechanism draws on Kendall & Gal's heteroscedastic regression framework, and the feature-based rendering of context features parallels Scaffold-GS's per-anchor feature design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated tension in HDR NVS: 3D tone mapping yields good LDR fitting but unstable HDR reconstruction, while 2D tone mapping stabilizes HDR at the cost of LDR quality. By showing that a residual local tone mapper with uncertainty-based modulation can simultaneously improve both, the work provides a practical recipe for HDR Gaussian Splatting practitioners. Limitations include the requirement for calibrated multi-exposure multi-view inputs, the added complexity of dual rendering paths and uncertainty networks, and the absence of evaluation on dynamic scenes or RAW-based HDR. Open questions include whether the local tone mapping idea transfers to dynamic HDR settings (e.g., extending HDR-HexPlane) and whether the uncertainty mechanism generalizes beyond the relatively small HDR-NeRF and HDR-Plenoxels benchmarks.
