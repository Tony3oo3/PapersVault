## Problem

Existing priors for neural implicit surface reconstruction from multi-view images either require large-scale pre-training on specific datasets (data-driven priors like depth or normal maps) that fail to generalize across scene types, or rely on overfitting-based priors (e.g., sparse MVS point clouds) that provide only geometric cues without photometric information. This limits both the accuracy and the generality of reconstructed surfaces, particularly in textureless indoor regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252029/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of posed multi-view RGB images of a single scene (no additional datasets or ground-truth geometry required). It first trains a grid-based NeRF prior (TensoRF architecture, 30k iterations, ~30 min) on the same images, then learns an SDF network (NeuS-style MLP with 8 hidden layers) for 200k iterations. The final output is a signed distance field extracted into a watertight mesh via marching cubes, with the multi-view consistency constraint activated after 100k iterations and the depth consistency loss after 150k to avoid early-stage instability.

## Method

NeRFPrior trains a grid-based NeRF on the target scene and uses its density and view-dependent color fields as supervision for a separate SDF network trained via volume rendering. The key mechanism is a multi-view consistency constraint: for each ray-surface intersection found by root-finding, the method performs a local-prior volume rendering in a small interval around the intersection to check visibility from source views (comparing rendered color against projected pixel color with a threshold), and only includes visible rays in the rendering loss. For textureless regions where photometric consistency is weak, a depth consistency loss with dual confidence weights (color variance on the image and density variance on the tangent plane) constrains neighboring surface points to maintain planar depth. The overall loss combines RGB rendering error, prior density/color L1 supervision (with a threshold to filter noisy prior densities), Eikonal regularization, and the weighted depth consistency term.

## Results

On the real-world ScanNet dataset, NeRFPrior achieves an F1-score of 0.433 without any data-driven priors, substantially outperforming the best prior-free baselines (NeuS, Geo-NeuS, NeuralAngelo at ~0.29 F1). When augmented with monocular cues, it reaches F1 0.782, surpassing MonoSDF* (0.732) and NeuRIS (0.705); with ground-truth depth it reaches F1 0.930, beating Go-Surf (0.887). On synthetic datasets, NeRFPrior achieves F1 0.621 on BlendSwap (vs. 0.484 for UNISURF) and F1 0.813 on Replica (vs. 0.665 for NeuS), with the lowest Chamfer Distance in both cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against prior-free neural implicit methods (NeuS, Geo-NeuS, NeuralAngelo, PermutoSDF, MonoSDF without cues), NeRFPrior wins decisively on ScanNet with F1 0.433 vs. 0.285–0.310, and on BlendSwap/Replica with F1 0.621/0.813 vs. 0.483–0.496/0.632–0.665. Against data-driven prior methods (Manhattan-SDF, NeuRIS, MonoSDF*), the paper's method with monocular cues achieves F1 0.782, beating MonoSDF* (0.732) by 5 points and NeuRIS (0.705) by 7.7 points. Against the ground-truth-depth baseline Go-Surf, NeRFPrior with depth supervision reaches F1 0.930 vs. 0.887, a 4.3-point gain. On BlendSwap, NeRFPrior outperforms the classic MVS baseline COLMAP (F1 0.387) by a wide margin (0.621) and UNISURF (0.484) by 13.7 points. The total training pipeline is also faster: 4.7 hours for NeRFPrior (37 min prior + 4.2 h training) versus 7.2 h for NeuS, 9.0 h for Geo-NeuS, and 10.6 h for MonoSDF.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on NeuS's SDF-to-density volume rendering formulation and Eikonal regularization, and on UNISURF's root-finding technique for locating ray-surface intersections. Its key conceptual departure is from the two dominant prior paradigms in neural implicit reconstruction: data-driven priors (MonoSDF, NeuRIS, Manhattan) that require large-scale pretraining and suffer from poor cross-domain generalization, and overfitting priors (Geo-NeuS, COLMAP sparse depth) that provide geometry but lack photometric information. NeRFPrior instead trains a grid-based NeRF (TensoRF architecture) on the target scene itself in ~30 minutes, yielding a scene-specific prior that supplies both density (geometry) and view-dependent color, which is then used to supervise the SDF network and to perform visibility-aware multi-view consistency checking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, self-contained pipeline for indoor scene reconstruction that avoids the generalization pitfalls of data-driven priors and the sparsity of MVS-based overfitting priors, while remaining competitive with methods that use ground-truth depth. The confidence-weighted depth consistency loss for textureless regions (walls, floors) addresses a persistent weakness of neural implicit methods in indoor scenes. Open questions include whether the two-stage pipeline (NeRF prior → SDF) can be unified into a single optimization, how the approach scales to very large or outdoor scenes, and whether the grid-based NeRF prior can be replaced by faster representations (e.g., 3DGS) without losing the continuous density needed for SDF supervision.
