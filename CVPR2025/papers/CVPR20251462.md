## Problem

Existing diffusion-based driving world models produce high-fidelity videos but suffer from limited predictive duration and poor zero-shot generalization to unseen scenes. A fundamental open question is whether iterative de-noising diffusion loss alone is sufficient for learning the visual semantics needed for generalizable world modeling, since the de-noising objective does not directly encourage contextual reasoning over scene structure and object motion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251462/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a sequence of past reference frames (always at diffusion timestep τ=0) along with action commands (angle, speed, trajectory, goal point) encoded as Fourier embeddings injected into cross-attention, plus a text prompt. It outputs future driving video in latent space at 512×288 resolution: 25 frames for the single-view MaskGWM-long variant and 8 frames across multiple camera views for MaskGWM-mview. During training, a binary mask randomly drops 25% of tokens, and the model must simultaneously predict the denoised video and reconstruct the masked patches.

## Method

MaskGWM builds on a 2B-parameter SD3 DiT backbone and introduces mask reconstruction as a complementary self-supervised task alongside Rectified Flow v-prediction. The key architectural insight is a diffusion-related mask token m_τ = (1−τ)·f_m(ε) + τ·p that blends a noise-encoding network with a learnable parameter, explicitly bridging the gap between the z₀-prediction objective of mask reconstruction and the v-prediction (z₀−ε) objective of diffusion. A two-branch reconstruction module handles spatial context (temporally-shared mask reconstructed by a spatial transformer) and temporal context (frame-specific mask reconstructed by a temporal transformer using a novel row-wise shifted self-attention that drops masked tokens per row and connects visible tokens, enabling FlashAttention compatibility). For multi-view generation, a cross-view row-wise self-attention concatenates features horizontally across views on the same row, leveraging the row-wise mask as implicit data augmentation. Training proceeds in three stages: pre-training on 1740h OpenDV-2K, single-view fine-tuning on nuScenes+OpenDV-2K, and multi-view fine-tuning on nuScenes.

## Results

On nuScenes single-view evaluation, MaskGWM-long achieves FID 5.6 and FVD 92.5, improving FID over VISTA (6.9) while FVD is comparable (92.5 vs. 89.4). For multi-view generation without future layout, MaskGWM-mview attains FID 8.9 and FVD 65.4, outperforming Drive-WM (FID 15.8, FVD 122.7) and DriveDreamer-2 (FID 11.2) on FID. On zero-shot Waymo validation, MaskGWM-long reduces FVD from 176.56 (VISTA) to 118.83 while maintaining comparable FID (9.55 vs. 9.76). In long-horizon rollout on OpenDV-2K, MaskGWM exhibits a significantly lower FVD growth rate than VISTA, indicating more stable extended prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In single-view generation on nuScenes, MaskGWM-long achieves the best FID (5.6) among all compared methods, surpassing VISTA (6.9) by 1.3 points, though VISTA retains a slight FVD edge (89.4 vs. 92.5). Against GenAD, MaskGWM-long improves FID by 9.8 points and FVD by 91.5 points, reflecting the benefit of the DiT backbone and mask reconstruction over the U-Net-based approach. In multi-view generation without future layout, MaskGWM-mview achieves the lowest FID (8.9) but DriveDreamer-2 reports a lower FVD (55.7 vs. 65.4), though DriveDreamer-2 relies on an LLM-enhanced pipeline. MaskGWM-mview substantially outperforms Drive-WM on both FID (8.9 vs. 15.8) and FVD (65.4 vs. 122.7). On zero-shot Waymo evaluation, MaskGWM-long reduces FVD by 33% over VISTA (118.83 vs. 176.56) with marginally better FID (9.55 vs. 9.76), demonstrating stronger cross-dataset generalization. In long-horizon rollout on OpenDV-2K, MaskGWM's FVD degrades more slowly than VISTA's, confirming improved temporal stability over extended prediction horizons.

## Relation to Prior Work

MaskGWM builds on Stable Diffusion 3's DiT backbone and VISTA's action-conditioned training protocol, but departs from the prevailing diffusion-only paradigm for driving world models by introducing MAE-style mask reconstruction as a complementary training objective. It extends the line of masked diffusion work (MaskDiT, SD-DiT) from image generation to the video domain, and specifically to driving, where the authors argue that spatial and temporal contexts require distinct masking strategies. The row-wise temporal mask with shifted self-attention is a novel design that replaces the standard masked self-attention of MAE, enabling token dropping (and thus FlashAttention compatibility) while preserving temporal continuity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical recipe—DiT backbone + web-scale pre-training + dual-branch mask reconstruction—that meaningfully improves both fidelity (FID) and zero-shot generalization (Waymo FVD reduced by ~33%) over prior driving world models, addressing a key limitation of diffusion-only approaches. The finding that learnable mask parameters and noise-embedded mask tokens serve complementary roles at high and low noise levels, respectively, offers a principled way to reconcile representation learning with iterative denoising. Open questions include whether the mask reconstruction benefit persists at even larger scales, whether the row-wise mask strategy generalizes beyond driving to other video prediction tasks, and whether the approach can be extended to closed-loop planning without the iterative diffusion cost.
