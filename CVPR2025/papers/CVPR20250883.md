## Problem

Existing physical adversarial attacks on face recognition rely on occlusive artifacts (3D-printed masks, glasses, stickers) that are user-specific, costly to fabricate, and fail liveness detection because they obstruct facial features. No prior method provides a non-intrusive, configurable attack that can impersonate multiple targets in real time while simultaneously bypassing liveness detection mechanisms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250883/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an attacker's RGB face image and a victim's RGB face image as input. It produces a light-based adversarial 3D texture that is projected onto the attacker's face via a projector, yielding a composite adversarial facial image captured by a camera. The 3D mask is derived from 3DMM coefficients regressed from the victim image, defining the spatial projection region, while the adversarial pattern is a per-pixel RGB perturbation optimized to survive both the projector-to-skin and camera-capture domain transitions.

## Method

ProjAttacker jointly optimizes a 3D mask and a light-based adversarial pattern through two key simulation modules that bridge the digital-to-physical gap. A Light Reflection Function (LRF) models the spectral interaction between projected light, skin reflectance, and camera photodetector sensitivity using CIE XYZ color-space integrals, accounting for specular/diffuse reflection and linear superposition of ambient and projected illumination. A differentiable U-Net-based Camera ISP proxy network simulates real-world image capture (denoising, color correction, tone mapping) via conditional inputs, trained on 2,270 COCO image pairs generated with an undifferentiable ISP simulator. The overall optimization is framed as a reinforcement learning problem where an agent interacts with an ensemble of white-box face recognition models, receiving reward from cosine similarity scores, and dynamically adjusts ensemble weights to generalize to black-box targets.

## Results

On the CelebA-HQ dataset, ProjAttacker achieves an average Attack Success Rate (ASR) of 85.36% across six target models (ArcFace, FaceNet, MobileNet, ResNet, CosFace, and a commercial API), with per-model ASRs ranging from 44.03% (API) to 98.13% (MobileNet). On LFW, the average ASR is 68.72%, with the highest per-model score of 82.98% on ResNet. The ensemble-based black-box attack (Table 1) reaches up to 90.50% ASR on CelebA-HQ and 75.28% on LFW when the target model is excluded from the surrogate ensemble. In the physical world, the full pipeline (3D mask + LRF + ISP) achieves 68% ASR on ArcFace, compared to 56% without the 3D mask and 38% without LRF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProjAttacker outperforms all three query-based baselines (AdvSticker, SOPP) and the transfer-based TAP on both LFW and CelebA-HQ by a wide margin: on CelebA-HQ it achieves 85.36% average ASR versus 48.35% (AdvSticker), 50.19% (SOPP), and 49.13% (TAP). Against the strongest transfer-based baseline AT3D, ProjAttacker edges ahead on CelebA-HQ (85.36% vs. 84.05%) but trails on LFW (68.72% vs. 74.50%), where AT3D's 3D-printed mask approach retains an advantage. On individual models, ProjAttacker leads AT3D on ArcFace (95.15% vs. 92.50%), FaceNet (84.47% vs. N/A), and CosFace (93.20% vs. 87.50%) on CelebA-HQ, while AT3D remains stronger on MobileNet (96.25% vs. 98.13% — actually ProjAttacker wins here too) and ResNet (93.75% vs. 97.20%). The commercial API remains the hardest target for all methods, with ProjAttacker reaching only 44.03% on CelebA-HQ and 41.43% on LFW, still above AT3D's 50.25%/45.25% on those respective datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of physical adversarial attacks on face recognition that rely on tangible artifacts (hats, glasses, stickers, 3D-printed masks) by using a projector to cast light-based adversarial patterns directly onto a face. It builds on the light-based attack line (infrared projection by Zhou et al., 2D RGB projection by Nguyen et al.) but introduces two key modeling components absent from prior work: a Light Reflection Function grounded in CIE XYZ colorimetry to simulate projector-skin-camera optical interactions, and a differentiable U-Net-based Camera ISP proxy to model the physical-to-digital capture transition. The 3DMM-based mask generation and reinforcement-learning-driven ensemble weighting extend the digital-to-physical robustness literature (NPS, D2P) by addressing both domain transitions simultaneously rather than only the digital-to-physical one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical security implication is significant: a non-intrusive, reconfigurable projector attack that bypasses all four liveness detection categories (transform, depth, motion, multi-bio) with 100% success on two commercial APIs exposes a vulnerability class that physical-artifact attacks cannot reach, since no facial occlusion is introduced. However, the physical evaluation is limited to 50 attacker-victim pairs under controlled conditions, and the notably lower API attack success rates (32–38%) suggest commercial systems retain some robustness. Open questions include how the attack degrades with projector distance, ambient lighting variability, and whether the ISP proxy generalizes to unseen camera hardware; more broadly, the findings underscore the need for defense mechanisms that account for projected-light adversarial perturbations rather than only printed or worn artifacts.
