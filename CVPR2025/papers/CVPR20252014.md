## Problem

CLIP-based domain generalization suffers from two intertwined limitations: its pre-training aligns images with textual descriptions that inevitably encode domain-specific visual details, and fine-tuning on a small number of source domains biases the model toward those domains' limited feature diversity. Existing adaptation strategies (weight ensembling, knowledge distillation, prompt learning) do not directly address how to suppress domain-sensitive features while simultaneously diversifying the learned representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252014/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and outputs a class label determined by cosine similarity between the image embedding and an ensembled text prompt (the mean of all 80 template-generated prompts for that class). During training, the image is processed by a fine-tuned CLIP ViT-B/16 image encoder, while 80 diverse text templates (e.g., 'a painting of a {class}', 'a {class} in a video game') are encoded by the frozen CLIP text encoder to generate domain-imitating prompts. Entropy-based filtering selects the m=40 most confident prompts per sample for the regularization losses.

## Method

The core idea is to use diverse text prompts as proxies for multiple domains, guiding the image encoder to learn domain-invariant features through three coupled mechanisms. First, Domain-sensitive Channel Suppression (DCS) uses gradient attribution to identify feature channels whose activation is disproportionately driven by a specific prompt's domain context (high z-score across prompts), masks those channels, and applies L1 regularization to suppress them. Second, Multi-domain Consistency Constraint (MCC) enforces KL-divergence consistency between the predicted class distributions obtained under different filtered prompts, making the encoder robust to domain variation. Third, Orthogonal Uniformity Regularization (OUR) decomposes each image feature into a component parallel to its ensembled prompt and an orthogonal residual, then applies a pairwise uniformity loss only on the orthogonal component—diversifying features in the hyperspherical space without disturbing the classification-relevant direction. A knowledge distillation baseline (aligning image features to both CLIP's image and text encoders) provides the primary training signal, and all three regularizers are added to the total loss.

## Results

The proposed method achieves an average leave-one-domain-out accuracy of 79.17% across five DomainBed datasets (Office-Home, Terra Incognita, VLCS, PACS, DomainNet) using ViT-B/16 as the backbone, surpassing the previous SOTA (VL2V-SD, 77.73%) by 1.4%. The most notable gain is on Terra Incognita, where the method reaches 63.27% versus 59.30% for the best prior fine-tuning method (MIRO), a 4.7% improvement. On DomainNet, the method achieves 63.11%, the highest among all compared approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 17 baselines on average (79.17% vs. 77.73% for the closest competitor VL2V-SD) and wins on 4 of 5 individual datasets. The largest margin is on Terra Incognita (+3.97% over MIRO, the best fine-tuning method there), attributed to the method's ability to suppress irrelevant contextual features in wildlife photography. On PACS, the method (97.03%) narrowly trails DPL (97.30%) by 0.27%, the only per-dataset loss, though DPL freezes CLIP parameters while the proposed method fine-tunes them. Among fine-tuning baselines, the gap over WiSE-FT (75.80%) and MIRO (76.66%) is 2.4–3.4% on average. Among prompt-learning methods (DPL, SPG, CoOp, CoCoOp, MaPLe), the proposed template-based approach surpasses the best (DPL, 75.60%) by 3.6% on average, demonstrating that fixed diverse templates outperform learnable prompts for domain generalization. The method also exceeds the parameter-efficient fine-tuning baseline LPD (76.11%) by 3.06% on average.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on VL2V-SD [1], which distills CLIP's image and text features into a DG model, but departs by replacing the single text prompt with an ensemble of 80 diverse templates to imitate multiple domains. Conceptually, it unifies ideas from DomainDrop (channel suppression), prompt-ensembling for robustness, and alignment-uniformity regularization into a single framework where diverse text prompts serve as the mechanism for all three operations on the visual encoder. Unlike prior prompt-based DG methods (DPL, SPG, CoOp) that optimize learnable prompts in the text encoder, this work keeps prompts fixed and instead uses them to directly regularize the image encoder's feature space through gradient attribution, KL consistency, and orthogonal uniformity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The approach offers a practical recipe for improving CLIP-based DG without architectural changes or additional training data, and the consistent outperformance of fixed template prompts over learnable prompts (Table 3) challenges the prevailing assumption that adaptive prompts are strictly superior for generalization. Limitations include evaluation only on ViT-B/16 and DomainBed-scale datasets, leaving open whether the method scales to larger backbones or more diverse domain shifts. A key open question is whether the specific 80 CLIP templates are optimal or whether domain-specific template selection could further improve performance, and whether the three-component loss can be simplified without sacrificing gains.
