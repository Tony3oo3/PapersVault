## Problem

Training object detectors directly on RAW sensor data for edge deployment is hindered by the scarcity of large labeled RAW datasets, while existing RGB-to-RAW conversion methods fail to faithfully reconstruct bright regions and nighttime scenes. This paper addresses both gaps by proposing a high-fidelity reverse ISP that can convert abundant labeled RGB datasets into sensor-specific RAW, enabling effective RAW-based model pretraining without requiring large ground-truth RAW collections.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252150/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a W×H×3 RGB patch alongside the full source RGB image (downscaled to 128×128 and randomly cropped to 90% area per sample) and outputs a W/2×H/2×4 packed RGGB RAW patch, which is black-level subtracted and max-normalized. The network is applied patch-by-patch (66×66 RGB → 32×32 RAW) to reconstruct full-resolution RAW images. The full RGB image provides global context that modulates the local patch conversion, making the output dependent on scene-level color and luminosity characteristics.

## Method

ReRAW employs a four-module CNN architecture: a Color Reconstruction Network (depth-wise conv + 8 residual point-wise layers) produces a local latent representation; a Global Context Encoder (ResNet18) extracts scene-level color/luminosity features that multiply the CRN output; a Multi-head Gamma Predictor with 10 parallel heads (each 8 residual point-wise conv layers) generates 10 gamma-corrected RAW patch candidates at distinct gamma values (0.1–1.0); and a Gamma Scaling Encoder (ResNet18) predicts softmax-normalized weights to linearly combine the de-gammaed candidates into the final RAW patch. Training uses a custom hard-log loss (L_hln = −ln(1−|ê−e|+ε)) that heavily penalizes misreconstructed bright pixels while converging to L1 for low-error regions, combined with a stratified sampling strategy that bins training patches by per-channel average brightness and uniformly selects across bins to counteract the natural skew toward dark pixel values in RAW data.

## Results

ReRAW-S (stratified sampling variant) achieves state-of-the-art PSNR across four of five datasets: 41.00 dB on NOD-Nikon, 40.07 dB on NOD-Sony, 30.45 dB on FiveK-Canon, and 38.88 dB on PASCALRAW, with SSIM values above 0.91 in all cases. The closest competitor, RAW-Diffusion, trails by 1.2–3.5 dB in PSNR depending on the dataset. For object detection, pretraining on ReRAW-S synthetic RAW data (heuristic d) yields the highest mAP across all three detectors on both daytime (PASCALRAW) and nighttime (NOD-Nikon) benchmarks, e.g., YOLOX-s reaches 65.85 mAP on PASCALRAW and 29.03 mAP on NOD-Nikon, surpassing the RGB-pretrained baseline (65.36 and 27.14 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReRAW outperforms all six baselines on PSNR and SSIM across all five datasets. The closest competitor is RAW-Diffusion [51], which ReRAW-S surpasses by 1.18 dB on NOD-Nikon (41.00 vs 39.82), 1.85 dB on NOD-Sony (40.07 vs 38.22), and a substantial 3.54 dB on PASCALRAW (38.88 vs 35.34). The CycleGAN-based CycleR2R [31] and the UNet [10] baseline are significantly outperformed, with gaps exceeding 6 dB on most datasets. SRISP [49] and the oracle variants InvISP+ [61] and ISPLess+ [39] (which use ground-truth RGB as additional input) are also consistently beaten by ReRAW-S, with the largest margins on PASCALRAW (38.88 vs 31.79 for SRISP, 38.88 vs 31.07 for InvISP+). Notably, ReRAW-S achieves SSIM above 0.99 on both NOD nighttime datasets, indicating near-perfect structural preservation in the most challenging low-light conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reverse ISP / RGB-to-RAW reconstruction line of work, which has progressed from calibration-based radiometric models through CycleGAN (CycleR2R), invertible ISP networks (InvISP, ISPLess), self-supervised approaches (SRISP), and recently diffusion-based generation (RAW-Diffusion). ReRAW departs from these by predicting multiple gamma-corrected RAW candidates in parallel heads and dynamically weighting them via a learned scaling vector, rather than producing a single reconstruction. The stratified sampling heuristic and hard-log loss are novel training-side contributions that address the well-known skew toward dark pixels in RAW distributions, a problem prior methods handled only implicitly through loss design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for edge deployment: by converting large labeled RGB datasets (e.g., BDD100K) into sensor-specific synthetic RAW, practitioners can pretrain compact detectors without the costly and scarce labeled RAW data, then fine-tune on a small real RAW set. The consistent outperformance of the synthetic-RAW pretraining recipe over the standard RGB pipeline across three detectors and both day/night settings makes this a viable alternative to ISP-based pipelines on resource-constrained hardware. An open question the authors flag is the imperfect correlation between PSNR of synthetic RAW and downstream detection accuracy (RTMDet on NOD-Nikon prefers the lower-fidelity variant), suggesting that reconstruction fidelity metrics may not fully capture what matters for high-level tasks. Future work on RAW-tailored detector architectures and alternative sampling strategies (high-DR, high-entropy filtering) is indicated.
