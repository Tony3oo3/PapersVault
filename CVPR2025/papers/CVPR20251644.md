## Problem

Existing multiview diffusion models for human generation are limited to 256×256 or at most 512×512 resolution, which is insufficient for capturing fine human details such as facial features, hands, and clothing textures. The fundamental barrier is the prohibitive memory and compute cost of cross-view attention mechanisms (dense or epipolar) when scaled to megapixel resolutions, making it impractical to train consistent multiview human generators at the 1024×1024 level where VAE reconstruction quality becomes adequate.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251644/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single in-the-wild frontal human image as input, from which it extracts a clothed human mesh and SMPL-X parameters via ECON under an optimized frontal perspective camera. It outputs 16 dense, view-consistent human renderings at 1024×1024 resolution, with cameras orbiting the subject at fixed elevation and distance. The generated multiview images are designed to be directly applicable to downstream 2D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction.

## Method

MEAT introduces mesh attention, which uses a monocular-reconstructed clothed human mesh as a central coarse geometric representation to establish direct cross-view pixel correspondences via rasterization and projection, reducing multiview attention complexity from O(N²S⁴) (dense) to O(N²S²d) where d is a small grid-sampling constant. The architecture is a U-Net-based diffusion model following Zero-1-to-3's all-view-at-once paradigm, with mesh attention blocks inserted into up-sampling layers to fuse features across all 16 views simultaneously. Each mesh attention block performs per-pixel cross-attention using projected 3D coordinates to retrieve corresponding features from other views, augmented with harmonic-embedded camera pose tokens and masked skip connections. The model additionally incorporates skeleton keypoint conditioning per view, multi-scale VAE latent features from the reference view, and a linear noise schedule, and is trained on the DNA-Rendering multiview human motion video dataset adapted through mesh alignment and object-centric cropping.

## Results

On DNA-Rendering Part 1 at 1024×1024 resolution, MEAT achieves PSNR 18.91, SSIM 0.9271, LPIPS 0.0751, and Patch-FID 10.60, substantially outperforming all baselines on perceptual and generation-quality metrics. At 256×256, MEAT records PSNR 19.41, LPIPS 0.0791, FID 16.56, and PPLC 0.0991. The most striking gap is in Patch-FID, where MEAT (10.60) beats the next-best retrained baseline (Stable Zero123 +kpts at 63.39) by over 5×, and in LPIPS where MEAT (0.0751) leads Wonder3D +kpts (0.1239) by ~40%. On cross-view consistency (PPLC at 256), Wonder3D +kpts (0.0895) slightly edges out MEAT (0.0991).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MEAT decisively outperforms all five baselines on perceptual quality (LPIPS) and generation fidelity (Patch-FID / FID) at both resolutions. At 1024, MEAT's LPIPS of 0.0751 beats the best retrained baseline (Stable Zero123 +kpts, 0.1234) by 39%, and its Patch-FID of 10.60 is roughly 6× lower than the next-best (63.39). At 256, MEAT's FID of 16.56 is more than 2× better than Stable Zero123 +kpts (33.64) and 3× better than Wonder3D +kpts (51.75). On PSNR/SSIM, MEAT is competitive but slightly trails Wonder3D +kpts (19.41 vs 19.51 PSNR at 256), which benefits from its cross-domain attention for geometric consistency. On cross-view consistency (PPLC at 256), Wonder3D +kpts (0.0895) narrowly beats MEAT (0.0991), though the paper notes Wonder3D's pre-trained checkpoint may produce duplicate back views that artificially lower PPLC. MagicMan, a concurrent human-specific method, is compared only qualitatively and shows visible artifacts and incomplete limbs on side views, whereas MEAT produces clean, detail-rich renderings across all 16 views.

## Relation to Prior Work

MEAT extends the all-view-at-once multiview diffusion paradigm established by Zero-1-to-3, MVDream, and Wonder3D, but departs from their cross-view attention mechanisms (dense, epipolar, or row-wise) by introducing mesh attention, which uses a monocular-reconstructed clothed human mesh to establish direct pixel correspondences via rasterization and projection. It also builds on monocular human reconstruction methods (ECON, PIFuHD) to obtain the centric mesh, and adapts the DNA-Rendering multiview human motion video dataset as a training source—a data pipeline not previously applied to multiview diffusion. The framework incorporates SDXL VAE, keypoint conditioning, and a linear noise schedule (from Zero123++) to support the 1024² resolution regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MEAT is the first multiview diffusion model to generate human images at 1024² resolution, a threshold the paper demonstrates is necessary for capturing facial, hand, and clothing detail that lower resolutions fundamentally cannot preserve through VAE reconstruction. The mesh attention mechanism's 3× memory advantage over dense attention at 1024² (68 GB vs. 186 GB) while simultaneously improving quality suggests a broadly applicable strategy for any task where a coarse 3D proxy can establish cross-view correspondences. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other articulated objects (animals, robots), whether the dependence on monocular reconstruction quality (ECON/PIFuHD) limits robustness on in-the-wild inputs, and whether the keypoint conditioning can be replaced by learned pose representations to reduce the pipeline's multi-stage fragility.
