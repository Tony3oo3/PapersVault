## Problem

Denoising diffusion models require many sampling steps to produce high-quality images, creating a fundamental trade-off: existing fast samplers (DDIM, DPM-Solver, DEIS) degrade generation quality when steps are reduced below ~10, while distillation-based approaches are complex and task-specific. This paper asks whether the reverse diffusion path itself can be optimized to minimize reconstruction error, enabling few-step generation without quality loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250584/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a random Gaussian noise vector as input and produces a synthetic RGB image after a configurable number of reverse diffusion steps (tested at 1–50 NFE). Experiments span three resolutions: CIFAR-10 at 32×32, CelebA at 64×64, and LSUN Churches at 256×256. For fair comparison, the same noise seed is used for both DDIM and ShortDF, so generated samples are directly comparable along the same sampling path.

## Method

ShortDF reframes the reverse diffusion process as a shortest-path problem on a step-reverse graph, where each node is a timestep and edge weights quantify the residual error introduced by using a noisy estimate x̂₀ instead of the true x₀ when transitioning between steps. A Dijkstra-like relaxation condition (dist(x_t,t) > dist(x_k,k) + edge(k,t)) iteratively compresses multi-step paths into direct shortcuts, effectively teaching the network to collapse x₀→x_k→x_t into x₀→x_t. Training uses a multi-state strategy with three synchronized model instances: a Base model for noise prediction, an EMA model for stable error estimation at intermediate steps, and a Graph model (updated at intervals) for computing edge weights. The total loss combines the standard DDIM noise-prediction loss with a relaxation loss that penalizes deviation from the shortest-path condition, enabling end-to-end optimization of both the generator and the sampler while preserving task-agnostic sampling.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, ShortDF achieves an FID of 9.08 at just 2 steps, outperforming DDIM's 11.14 at 10 steps (a 5× speedup with 18.5% FID improvement). At 10 steps, ShortDF reaches FID 3.75, beating the best baseline gDDIM(DDPM) at 4.17. On CelebA (64×64), ShortDF achieves FID 5.00 at 10 steps versus DDIM's 10.59, and FID 2.78 at 50 steps versus DDIM's 4.66. On LSUN Churches (256×256), ShortDF attains FID 11.78 at 10 steps compared to DDIM's 21.52, and FID 6.95 at 50 steps versus DDIM's 7.65.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ShortDF outperforms all 10 baselines at every comparable step count across all three datasets. On CIFAR-10 at 10 steps, ShortDF (FID 3.75) beats DPM-Solver (5.09), gDDIM-DDPM (4.17), DEIS3 (4.17), DPM-Solver++ (7.83), D-ODE (8.00), iPNDM (9.36), DDIM (11.14), gDDIM-CLD (13.41), and ResFusion (28.82). The most dramatic gains appear at very low step counts: at 2 steps on CIFAR-10, ShortDF achieves FID 9.08 while DDIM and D-ODE exceed 100, and at 4 steps ShortDF (6.59) surpasses DPM-Solver (33.29) and DPM-Solver++ (30.46) by a wide margin. On CelebA at 10 steps, ShortDF (5.00) nearly halves DDIM's FID (10.59) and outperforms DPM-Solver (8.44), DPM-Solver++ (8.31), iPNDM (7.78), and DEIS3 (6.95). On LSUN Churches at 10 steps, ShortDF (11.78) matches PNDM (11.80) while substantially outperforming DDIM (21.52) and DPM-Solver++ (38.89). No baseline is competitive with ShortDF at the 1–5 step regime, where most methods either fail to produce usable images (FID > 40) or are not reported.

## Relation to Prior Work

ShortDF builds directly on DDPM and DDIM, positioning itself as a third acceleration strategy alongside distillation (PD, InstaFlow, CD, TRACT) and faster ODE solvers (DPM-Solver, DEIS, PNDM, gDDIM). Its conceptual departure is framing the reverse diffusion path as a shortest-path problem in a step-reverse graph, where edge weights quantify residual differences between estimate-based and ground-truth-based transformations. This graph-theoretic lens, combined with a multi-state training strategy (Base, EMA, Graph models), distinguishes it from both distillation methods that collapse steps into a single network and numerical solvers that approximate the ODE trajectory. The residual-propagation analysis (Sec. 3.2) is explicitly positioned as the first systematic treatment of how estimation errors accumulate and propagate across reverse steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5× speedup with an 18.5% FID improvement on CIFAR-10 (2 steps vs. 10) is a practically meaningful result for latency-sensitive applications, and the method's task-agnostic sampler design suggests portability to text-to-image and other domains. However, all experiments are confined to small-scale datasets (32×32 to 256×256) with a single A100 GPU, and the paper lacks ablations isolating the graph-modeling component from the modified loss, making it unclear how much of the gain comes from the shortest-path framing versus the multi-state training trick. Open questions include whether the approach scales to large pretrained models (e.g., Stable Diffusion), whether the graph construction overhead becomes prohibitive at higher resolutions, and whether the claimed end-to-end generator-sampler optimization delivers the promised cross-domain robustness.
