## Problem

Making 3D characters animatable requires rigging (placing bones) and skinning (assigning blend weights), which is traditionally manual or handled by slow, limited automatic tools. Existing auto-rigging methods demand manual joint annotations, support only fixed skeleton topologies, and struggle with non-standard poses or exaggerated character shapes, while template-based generators are constrained to realistic human proportions. No prior method achieves fast, template-free, pose-agnostic rigging that generalizes to arbitrary shapes and supports both mesh and 3D Gaussian splat inputs.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a 3D character in any pose as either a mesh or 3D Gaussian splats, which is consumed as a re-centered, normalized point cloud (sampled via FPS). It outputs three animation assets: per-joint head-and-tail positions (K×6), per-joint pose-to-rest rigid transformations (K×6, internally represented as 8D dual quaternions), and per-vertex blend weights (K×N_q) for all query points. The skeleton topology is user-defined (default 52-bone human skeleton, extensible to extra bones for ears, tails, etc.), and the entire inference completes in under one second regardless of input particle count.

## Method

The framework is a particle-based shape autoencoder built on the 3DShape2VecSet encoding pattern (FPS downsampling + cross-attention to produce M latent vectors), extended with a geometry-aware attention branch that adaptively fuses per-point normals to preserve surface geometry lost during sampling. A coarse-to-fine strategy first uses a lite model to roughly localize joints, then applies canonical transformation and hierarchical sampling (denser in hands/fingers) to refine the shape representation. For bone attributes, learnable discrete semantic queries (one per bone) replace spatial queries, and a structure-aware transformer performs next-child-bone prediction via masked causal attention over the kinematic tree, so each bone's embedding incorporates its parent's predicted attributes autoregressively. Training is end-to-end supervised with L1 losses on ground-truth weights, positions, and poses, plus body prior losses enforcing bone connectivity, left-right symmetry, and limb parallelism; the shape encoder is frozen (pretrained on ShapeNet) while the decoder and structure-aware modules are trained on 95 Mixamo characters with 2,453 motion sequences.

## Results

On the bipedal humanoid subset of the ModelsResource-RigNetv1 test set, the proposed method achieves 82.50% IoU, 81.07% Precision, and 90.31% Recall for skeleton prediction, substantially outperforming RigNet (53.50% IoU, 47.27% Precision, 89.30% Recall). Chamfer distance metrics also improve significantly: CD-J2J drops from 6.63% to 4.49%, CD-J2B from 4.97% to 3.32%, and CD-B2B from 2.88% to 1.58%. Inference speed is approximately 0.5 seconds per character, compared to ~10 minutes for RigNet, ~3 minutes for Meshy/Tripo, and ~2 minutes for Mixamo.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RigNet, the proposed method wins decisively on all six quantitative metrics, with the largest gap in IoU (+29 percentage points) and Precision (+34 percentage points), while also halving the bone-to-bone Chamfer distance. Against the commercial tools Meshy and Tripo, the comparison is qualitative: both baselines produce incorrect rest poses for complex shapes, fail to predict finger bones, and generate overly diffuse blend weights, whereas the proposed method yields complete, accurate skeletons with spatially smooth weights and natural animations. Against the template-based generators TADA and HumanGaussian, the proposed method achieves comparable or superior rigging quality while being template-free, supporting arbitrary character shapes beyond realistic human proportions, and handling non-standard skeleton topologies (e.g., animal ears, tails) that the SMPL-based baselines cannot accommodate. Across all comparisons, the ~0.5-second inference time represents a 100–1000× speedup over existing methods, making real-time or interactive rigging feasible.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the 3DShape2VecSet encoding architecture (FPS + cross-attention) as its shape autoencoder backbone, repurposing a shape-representation tool for the downstream task of rigging and skinning. It departs from prior auto-rigging methods (RigNet, TARig, Pinocchio) in three key ways: (1) a particle-based formulation that natively supports both meshes and 3D Gaussian splats, (2) a structure-aware transformer that applies LLM-style causal/autoregressive attention over the kinematic tree for bone prediction, and (3) dual-quaternion pose representation with body-prior losses for connectivity, symmetry, and parallelism. Unlike template-based avatar generators (TADA, HumanGaussian) that are locked to SMPL body proportions, this framework is template-free and handles arbitrary shapes, poses, and non-standard skeleton topologies (e.g., ears, tails, fingers).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sub-second inference time (vs. minutes for commercial tools like Mixamo and Meshy) makes automatic rigging practical for real-time pipelines, interactive creation, and large-scale content generation. Supporting 3D Gaussian splatting as input is timely given the rapid adoption of 3DGS in the graphics community. Key limitations include the requirement for a fixed kinematic tree topology, the empirical (non-adaptive) hierarchical sampling strategy, and restriction to bipedal humanoid characters. Open questions include whether the structure-aware causal attention generalizes to non-bipedal or multi-limb characters, and whether the frozen ShapeNet-pretrained encoder can be replaced by a more task-specific representation without sacrificing the speed advantage.
