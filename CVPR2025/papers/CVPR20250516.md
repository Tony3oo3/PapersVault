## Problem

Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) encode rich multimodal representations but are too computationally expensive for edge deployment, while lightweight networks lack the representational capacity for fine-grained classification. Traditional knowledge distillation requires a two-stage process—pre-training a task-specific teacher then distilling to a student—which is resource-intensive and risks propagating teacher biases. The paper asks whether VLM knowledge can be transferred directly to lightweight models in a single training step without any additional teacher fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250516/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single RGB image and outputs a class label via the lightweight student network alone (no VLM needed). During training, the frozen VLM (ConvNeXt-XXLarge from OpenCLIP, 1024-dim output) processes the same image, while class labels are converted into text prompts (e.g., 'A photo of a [class name]') and fed through the frozen VLM text encoder. Two randomly initialized 2-layer MLP condensation layers project the VLM's high-dimensional image and text features down to the student's lower-dimensional space, so the student never directly sees the full VLM feature vectors.

## Method

VL2Lite trains a lightweight student (ResNet-18, MobileNet-V2, Swin-Tiny, etc.) with a composite loss of three terms: (1) standard cross-entropy classification loss, (2) a visual KD loss that aligns pairwise Euclidean distances in the student's feature space with those in the condensed VLM image feature space using smooth L1 (an RKD-style relational objective), and (3) a linguistic KD loss that minimizes KL divergence between temperature-scaled softmax distributions of cosine similarities between the student's image features and the condensed VLM text-prompt features. The VLM is entirely frozen; only the student backbone and the two condensation MLPs receive gradients. A dynamic weighting schedule starts with λ_cls = 0.01 (emphasizing distillation) and gradually increases it, shifting focus toward task-specific accuracy as training progresses. This single-stage, same-domain alignment strategy avoids the cross-domain feature matching used by prior VLM distillation methods like RISE and eliminates the need for any teacher fine-tuning on the target dataset.

## Results

VL2Lite achieves up to 8.77% accuracy improvement over the no-KD baseline (e.g., ShuffleNet-V2 on Stanford Cars: 69.34% → 78.11%) and consistently outperforms all compared KD methods across 9 fine-grained and general classification datasets. On CUB-200-2011 with ResNet-18, VL2Lite reaches 71.38% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the strongest baseline KD(T:CLIP) at 70.95% and RISE at 69.69%. Gains are most pronounced on fine-grained datasets (CUB-200, FGVC-Aircraft, Stanford Cars) and for smaller backbones (ResNet-18, MobileNet-V2), while even the strongest student (Swin-Tiny) still benefits by 3.15–4.60%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VL2Lite outperforms all compared baselines on every dataset for both ResNet-18 and MobileNet-V2. Against the strongest CLIP-based KD baseline (KD T:CLIP), VL2Lite gains +0.43% on CUB-200 (71.38% vs 70.95%) and +3.02% on Stanford Cars (77.09% vs 74.99%) for ResNet-18; for MobileNet-V2 the margin widens to +3.02% on CUB-200 (71.02% vs 68.00%). Compared to RISE (the closest VLM-distillation competitor), VL2Lite leads by +1.69% on CUB-200 and +4.64% on Stanford Cars for ResNet-18, and by +3.51% and +2.32% respectively for MobileNet-V2. BorLan, which also distills from VLMs, trails VL2Lite by 4.59% on CUB-200 (66.79% vs 71.38%) and 6.38% on Stanford Cars (70.71% vs 77.09%) for ResNet-18. Traditional KD and RKD with a ResNet-152 teacher are consistently the weakest baselines, confirming that the VLM's multimodal representations provide substantially richer supervision than a same-modality CNN teacher.

## Relation to Prior Work

VL2Lite extends the knowledge distillation paradigm (Hinton et al.) by replacing the conventional two-stage teacher-training-then-distill pipeline with a single-step process that uses a frozen, pre-trained VLM as a fixed teacher. The visual distillation loss adapts Relational Knowledge Distillation (Park et al., CVPR 2019) to align pairwise feature distances, while the linguistic loss follows CLIP's contrastive image-text alignment framework, a combination not previously unified in a single lightweight-model training objective. The paper explicitly departs from RISE (Huang et al., ICCV 2023), which minimizes cross-domain embedding distances, by arguing that within-domain relational alignment better preserves the contrastive structure learned by VLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-step, frozen-teacher design removes the need to pre-train or fine-tune a large model on the target dataset, substantially reducing the computational and time overhead of deploying accurate classifiers on edge devices. The consistent 3–7% gains across six lightweight architectures (CNNs and Transformers) and nine fine-grained datasets, plus the >10% improvement under 30% data, make this a practically attractive recipe for resource-constrained settings. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to dense prediction tasks (detection, segmentation), how sensitive it is to the choice of VLM backbone, and whether the condensation layers can be further compressed or eliminated for truly minimal-footprint deployment.
