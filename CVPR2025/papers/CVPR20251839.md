## Problem

Vision-centric 3D detectors in autonomous driving suffer significant performance degradation under out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios such as adverse weather, yet existing controllable text-to-image diffusion methods for training data augmentation either require substantial training data to learn auxiliary control modules (e.g., ControlNet) or rely on coarse low-resolution self-attention features that fail to preserve all annotated 3D objects with precise geometry (e.g., FreeControl, PnP). The paper addresses the gap of achieving training-free, object-preserving OOD scenario generation specifically suited for 3D detection where even tiny objects must retain accurate position, orientation, and scale.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB training images paired with their 3D bounding box annotations (layouts), auto-generates text prompts from object class names via a template, and accepts a target OOD scenario label (e.g., Snow, Fog, Rain). It outputs augmented RGB images at the same resolution as the input, rendered in the specified OOD condition while preserving all annotated objects' 3D geometry so that original labels remain valid for detector retraining. The pipeline operates on Stable Diffusion 1.5 with 200 DDIM inversion and 200 DDIM sampling steps, requiring no additional diffusion model training.

## Method

DriveGEN is a two-stage training-free pipeline. Stage 1 (Self-Prototype Extraction) runs DDIM inversion on each training image, applies PCA to the self-attention keys of the first U-Net decoder layer to obtain principal components, then re-weights object regions using a Gaussian peak function centered on layout-derived object centers, producing self-prototypes that encode precise 3D object geometry at the feature level. Stage 2 (Prototype-Guided Diffusion) guides the denoising process with two complementary alignment losses: a semantic-aware feature alignment that minimizes the masked L2 distance between current PCA principal components and the stored self-prototypes, and a shallow feature alignment that constrains the noisy latent directly at the layout-pixel level to preserve tiny objects (e.g., a 20×5 px cyclist) that vanish after 32× downsampling. The overall denoising step combines classifier-free guidance with both alignment terms, requiring only hyper-parameters s, σ, and τ with no gradient updates to the diffusion model.

## Results

On KITTI-C (severity 1, Mean AP_3D|R_40), DriveGEN with a single Snow augmentation improves Monoflex from 26.45 to 34.07 mAP (+7.62) and MonoGround from 26.36 to 33.38 mAP (+7.02) across 13 OOD scenarios. With 6-scenario augmentation, Monoflex reaches 37.98 mAP and MonoGround reaches 37.21 mAP. On nuScenes-C, DriveGEN (3k Snow) improves BEVFormer-tiny by +1.53 mAP (20.04→21.57) and +2.04 NDS (31.31→33.35), and on real-world Night/Rainy scenarios by +2.61 mAP (18.04→20.65) and +2.52 NDS (27.64→30.16).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DriveGEN decisively outperforms both training-based and training-free controllable diffusion baselines. ControlNet [59], despite being provided with accurate segmentation masks and rich prompts, collapses to 11.50 mAP (Snow only) and 2.00 mAP (3 scenarios) on Monoflex, far below the source-only 26.45, indicating severe object omission corrupts training data. FreeControl [25], the closest training-free competitor, achieves 22.44 mAP (Snow only) vs. DriveGEN's 34.07, a 11.63-point gap, and degrades further with more scenarios (19.58 at 3 scenarios) while DriveGEN improves monotonically. Against traditional augmentation (Color Jitter: 24.19, Brightness: 20.05), DriveGEN gains 9.88 and 14.02 mAP respectively with Snow-only augmentation. On the multi-view nuScenes setting, DriveGEN improves BEVFormer-tiny [20] by +1.53 mAP on synthetic corruptions and +2.61 mAP on real-world Night/Rainy scenarios, confirming generalizability beyond monocular detection. MonoCD [54] is used as an additional base detector in supplementary comparisons (Figure 4), where DriveGEN similarly outperforms ControlNet and FreeControl.

## Relation to Prior Work

DriveGEN extends the training-free controllable T2I diffusion line initiated by PnP [46] and FreeControl [25], which inject self-attention features to guide denoising, by introducing layout-based self-prototype extraction and a two-tier feature alignment (semantic-aware + shallow) specifically designed to preserve 3D object geometry. It deliberately departs from training-based spatial control methods like ControlNet [59] and T2i-adapter [27], which the authors show fail catastrophically when auxiliary modules are trained on the limited data available in autonomous driving. The method is positioned as the first application of training-free controllable diffusion to the OOD robustness problem in vision-centric 3D detection, bridging the gap between general-purpose image editing and the strict geometric fidelity required for 3D bounding box annotation reuse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is strong: a single Snow augmentation yields an average 7.6 mAP gain across 13 OOD scenarios on KITTI without any diffusion model retraining, and the gains transfer to unseen corruptions (Fog, Defocus) and real-world nuScenes Night/Rainy splits, suggesting the augmentation induces broader invariance rather than memorizing one corruption. However, the approach still requires 200-step DDIM inversion plus 200-step sampling per training image, which is computationally expensive for large-scale training sets, and the evaluation is confined to KITTI and nuScenes with Stable Diffusion 1.5. Open questions include whether the self-prototype mechanism generalizes to denser urban scenes with hundreds of objects, whether it extends to LiDAR-camera fusion settings, and whether the peak-function re-weighting remains effective when object annotations are noisy or partially occluded.
