## Problem

Gaussian splatting's inherent radial symmetry and smoothness constraints limit its ability to represent complex shapes (rectangles, triangles, ellipses) and sharp boundaries, often requiring thousands of primitives to approximate detailed geometry. The paper asks whether the Gaussian kernel is truly optimal for 3D scene representation and proposes a more general, flexible planar primitive that can adapt to diverse scene geometries with far fewer primitives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252043/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images with COLMAP-estimated camera poses and produces a set of 2D planar DRK primitives, each parameterized by a 3D center, rotation quaternion, K radial basis lengths and angles, a curvature weight η, a sharpness coefficient τ, opacity, and spherical harmonic coefficients. Rendering is performed via differentiable α-blending with ray-primitive intersection for depth sorting, producing novel view images at real-time framerates (77–235 FPS depending on primitive density).

## Method

DRK generalizes 2D Gaussian splatting by replacing the fixed elliptical kernel with K learnable radial basis control points in polar coordinates, smoothly interpolated via cosine weighting. A learnable L1–L2 norm blending weight η allows the kernel boundary to transition between straight-line (L1) and conic (L2) contours, while a piecewise linear sharpening function Ψ(g) controlled by τ decouples edge sharpness from spatial extent—something Gaussian variance inherently couples. For rasterization, the paper introduces polygon-based tile culling using calibrated radial endpoints, a view-dependent low-pass filter scaled by the cosine of the view angle, and a cache-sorting strategy that uses ray-primitive intersection distances rather than center depth to prevent popping artifacts. The formulation is backward-compatible: setting K=4 with orthogonal bases and η=0, τ=0 recovers the standard 2D Gaussian kernel.

## Results

On the proposed DiverseScenes benchmark, DRK achieves an average PSNR of 37.58, LPIPS of 0.0564, and SSIM of 0.9752, outperforming the best baseline 3D-HGS (35.68 PSNR) by ~2 dB. The sparser variant DRK(S2) uses only 42K primitives (vs. 330K+ for all baselines) while still achieving 35.03 PSNR, comparable to 3D-GS at 34.41. On Mip-NeRF360, DRK attains the best perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.2364, SSIM 0.7871) but trails 3D-GS(L) slightly on PSNR (26.76 vs. 26.94), with the gap narrowing on masked foreground (M-PSNR 32.81 vs. 32.75).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRK outperforms all four baselines (2D-GS, 3D-GS, 3D-HGS, GES) on DiverseScenes across every metric, with the largest gains on the 'Large' scene category (35.28 vs. 32.19 PSNR for 3D-GS) and 'Geometry' category (31.93 vs. 29.14 PSNR). The sparser DRK(S2) variant, using only 42K primitives (~8× fewer than baselines), matches or exceeds 3D-GS and GES in quality while reducing model size to 12.3 MB and maintaining 234.9 FPS. On Mip-NeRF360, DRK wins decisively on perceptual metrics (LPIPS 0.2364 vs. 0.2688–0.3047 for baselines; SSIM 0.7871 vs. 0.7537–0.7799) but is marginally behind 3D-GS(L) on raw PSNR (26.76 vs. 26.94), which the authors attribute to overfitting in distant, poorly-supervised regions. In terms of rendering speed, full DRK is slower (77.5 FPS) than 3D-GS (247.1 FPS) due to its more complex kernel evaluation, though DRK(S2) recovers competitive throughput at 234.9 FPS.

## Relation to Prior Work

DRK extends the 2D Gaussian Splatting (2D-GS) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) frameworks by replacing the fixed Gaussian kernel with a deformable radial basis formulation that generalizes 2D Gaussians as a special case (K=4, orthogonal bases). It departs from prior kernel-modification approaches—GES (exponent tuning), DisC-GS (curve cutting), and 3D-HGS (hemispheric truncation)—which all remain constrained by the underlying Gaussian smoothness, by instead redefining the kernel's geometric foundation through learnable radial lengths, angles, L1/L2 norm blending, and a piecewise sharpening function. The polygon-based tile culling and cache-sorting strategies are tailored to DRK's non-elliptical shapes, and the mesh-to-DRK conversion capability has no direct analogue in prior splatting methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~8× primitive count reduction (42K vs. 330K+ for DRK(S2) vs. baselines) with comparable quality is practically significant for memory-constrained deployment, and the training-free mesh-to-DRK conversion offers a concrete bridge between the millions of existing polygon assets and splatting-based rendering pipelines. However, the rendering speed drops substantially (77.5 FPS vs. 247.1 FPS for 3D-GS), and on unbounded Mip-NeRF360 scenes the PSNR advantage largely vanishes, suggesting DRK may overfit distant, weakly-supervised regions. Open questions include whether the approach scales to dynamic or very large scenes, whether the speed gap can be closed with further GPU optimization, and whether the planar-primitive assumption limits applicability to volumetric or highly transparent content.
