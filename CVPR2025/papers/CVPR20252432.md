## Problem

Existing online label shift (OnLS) methods adapt offline BBSE-based techniques to streaming settings, but the resulting confusion-matrix inversion produces negative label distribution estimates that violate the convexity assumption required by Online Convex Optimization, undermining both optimization and theoretical guarantees. Additionally, prior work provides only static or dynamic regret bounds, neither of which is well-suited: static regret is too optimistic for non-stationary environments, while dynamic regret is too pessimistic as per-round optimal comparators overfit to noise.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of unlabeled samples (10 per round in experiments) arriving over T rounds, along with a fixed labeled validation set used to compute per-class empirical risks and the confusion matrix. The feature extractor (ResNet-18 or MLP) is frozen after initial supervised training; only the linear classifier weights are updated online. The output at each round is a convex risk estimator constructed from the likelihood-based label distribution and the validation-set risks, which drives the model update and produces class predictions on the current unlabeled batch.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-part pipeline. First, OSCM-L replaces BBSE's confusion-matrix inversion with a likelihood maximization over the multinomial distribution of classifier predictions, solved by projected gradient ascent onto the probability simplex; this guarantees non-negative label distribution estimates and hence a convex risk estimator. Second, LASU uses this convex risk to drive multiple Optimistic Online Mirror Descent base learners, each with a different step size from a geometric pool, combined via an Optimistic Hedge ensemble that adaptively weights learners by cumulative loss plus an optimism term. The ensemble eliminates the need to know the path-length P_T or label-shift intensity V_T in advance, yielding a universal dynamic regret bound of O(√((T+V_T)(1+P_T))) that is minimax optimal and subsumes both static and dynamic regret as special cases.

## Results

The paper reports average error (%) over 1000 online rounds across 6 datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10, Fashion-MNIST, EuroSAT, CINIC-10, SHL) under 4 label shift types. LASU achieves the lowest error in nearly all 24 dataset-shift combinations, e.g., 9.68% on CIFAR-10 (Bernoulli shift) vs. 10.20% for the best baseline (UOGD), and 4.42% on EuroSAT (Bernoulli) vs. 6.08% for UOGD. On the most challenging dataset CINIC-10, LASU reaches 18.43% (Sine shift) compared to 18.75% for UOGD, while the Oracle upper bound is 11.31%. The gap to Oracle narrows as online sample size increases, confirming that more accurate label distribution estimation drives the gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LASU outperforms all three baseline families across nearly every dataset-shift combination. Against Wu et al.'s methods (FTH, FTFWH, ROGD [36]), LASU achieves 1.5–3.5 percentage points lower error on CIFAR-10 and EuroSAT, and a particularly large ~4.7-point margin on Fashion-MNIST (Bernoulli: 7.86% vs. 9.18% for FTH). Against Bai et al.'s ATLAS/UOGD [4], LASU wins on 22 of 24 settings, with the largest gaps on EuroSAT (Bernoulli: 4.42% vs. 6.08%) and Fashion (Bernoulli: 7.86% vs. 10.74%); the only near-tie is CIFAR-10 Linear where LASU (10.58%) slightly trails UOGD (10.34%). Against Baby et al.'s FLH-FTL [3], LASU is consistently better, e.g., 9.68% vs. 10.61% on CIFAR-10 Bernoulli and 4.42% vs. 7.02% on EuroSAT Bernoulli. The Oracle (true label margins) remains substantially better than all methods, indicating room for improvement, but LASU closes the gap more effectively than any baseline, especially as online sample size grows.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Online Label Shift (OnLS) line initiated by Wu et al. (2021) and Bai et al. (2022), which adapted the offline BBSE confusion-matrix framework to streaming settings. The key departure is replacing BBSE's matrix inversion with a likelihood-based estimator (OSCM-L) that guarantees non-negative label distributions and preserves convexity of the risk, a property violated by prior methods when online samples are scarce. For optimization, the paper upgrades from standard OGD to Optimistic OMD (Chiang et al., 2012) and wraps multiple step-size variants in an Optimistic Hedge ensemble (Syrgkanis et al., 2015), yielding a universal dynamic regret guarantee that subsumes both static and dynamic regret as special cases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The convexity fix is practically important: it enables stable optimization and clean theoretical analysis within the OCO framework, removing a hidden failure mode in prior OnLS methods that can produce negative class probabilities. The minimax-optimal universal dynamic regret provides a more honest performance measure than static regret (too optimistic) or per-round dynamic regret (too pessimistic). However, the method still assumes strict conditional-distribution invariance (pure label shift), and the authors acknowledge that covariate shift, feature drift, and reducing the dependence on T remain open. The substantial gap to the Oracle model (e.g., ~4% on CIFAR-10) suggests room for improvement in label distribution estimation accuracy.
