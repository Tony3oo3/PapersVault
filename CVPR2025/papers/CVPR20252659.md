## Problem

Multi-image Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) exhibit a previously uncharacterized position bias: simply shuffling the order of input images can change predictions by up to 30% across established benchmarks. While position bias (e.g., 'lost in the middle') is well-documented in NLP, its manifestation in multi-image LVLMs—where open-source models show recency bias and proprietary models show U-shaped performance curves—had not been systematically identified or addressed.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard multi-image inputs (5 to 100 RGB images) paired with a text question, producing a text answer. For the proposed PQA evaluation task, the output is structured as a position-wise list (e.g., [3, 2, 0, ...]) with one answer per image, enabling per-position accuracy measurement. SoFA itself is a training-free inference-time modification: it requires no retraining, no additional parameters, and only alters the attention mask computation within the existing language decoder, with the interpolation parameter σ tuned per task on a small 32-shot validation set.

## Method

SoFA (SoFt Attention) mitigates position bias by linearly interpolating the inter-image attention mask between its causal and bidirectional forms: M_soft = (1−σ)·1_causal + σ·1_bidirectional, where σ controls the degree of bidirectional information flow. The key mechanistic insight is that standard inter-image causal attention creates an asymmetry—later images can attend to earlier ones while earlier images remain isolated—making image hidden states heavily dependent on relative position. SoFA is applied every two layers (rather than all layers) to stay close to the original training distribution, and only modifies inter-image attention while preserving causal attention between text segments. This design balances the trade-off between eliminating positional dependence (full bidirectional) and maintaining training-time consistency (full causal), yielding 2–3% average accuracy gains across multi-image benchmarks while reducing prediction inconsistency from ~30% to ~10%.

## Results

SoFA, a training-free attention modification, yields average accuracy improvements of 2–3% across six multi-image benchmarks (BLINK, Mantis-Eval, MuirBench, MIRB, NLVR2, MVBench) for all five evaluated LVLMs. The largest single-benchmark gains include InternVL2 on BLINK (+4.31%, 38.95→43.26) and LLaVA-NeXT on MIRB (+3.52%, 57.15→60.67). On the position-robustness evaluation (Table 1), SoFA reduces prediction inconsistency by 50–70% (e.g., Idefics2 on BLINK drops from 41.55% to 12.36%), confirming that the method effectively eliminates position-dependent blind spots. In long-context settings (100 images on Multi-modal Haystack), SoFA achieves the largest relative gain, improving accuracy from 49.19% to 55.11%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SoFA is a training-free plug-in applied on top of each baseline model, so every comparison is 'model' vs. 'model+SoFA' under identical evaluation protocols. SoFA consistently outperforms all five baselines: Idefics2 gains +2.53% on BLINK and +4.52% on MuirBench; InternVL2 gains +4.31% on BLINK and +2.61% on MuirBench; VILA gains +4.07% on MuirBench; Mantis gains +2.38% on Mantis-Eval and +3.36% on MuirBench; and LLaVA-NeXT gains +2.58% on BLINK and +3.52% on MIRB. The only exception is VILA on Mantis-Eval (52.64→52.13, −0.51%) and Mantis on NLVR2 (90.26→89.98, −0.28%), where SoFA causes a negligible drop, likely because these tasks already have minimal position sensitivity. The largest absolute improvements occur on benchmarks with strong position-agnostic tasks (BLINK, MuirBench, MIRB), while gains on NLVR2 (image-pair comparison) and MVBench (video) are smaller, consistent with the paper's observation that tasks requiring temporal ordering benefit less from bidirectional attention.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the well-studied position bias phenomenon from NLP (Liu et al.'s 'lost in the middle,' Peysakhovich & Lerer's attention sorting) into the multi-image LVLM setting, where it had not been systematically investigated. Rather than building on a specific prior LVLM architecture, it identifies inter-image causal attention as the mechanical source of the bias and proposes SoFA—a training-free linear interpolation between causal and bidirectional attention masks—as a lightweight mitigation. The PQA diagnostic task is introduced to fill a gap: existing multi-image benchmarks assess holistic comprehension but cannot isolate per-position reasoning quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that merely shuffling image order causes ~30% prediction inconsistency in open-source LVLMs (and ~25% in GPT-4o) exposes a significant robustness gap that undermines reliability in production multi-image applications. SoFA's training-free nature makes it immediately deployable, and the 2–3% accuracy gains plus large inconsistency reductions are practically meaningful. Open questions remain: whether the bias is inherited from LLM backbones or arises from vision-specific training, whether it persists under better interleaved pre-training, and whether the per-task σ tuning generalizes or can be learned automatically. The approach also raises the question of whether similar attention modifications could address related positional artifacts in video understanding or long-context multimodal RAG.
