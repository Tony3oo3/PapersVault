## Problem

Existing layout-guided text-to-image methods treat all objects as residing on a single layer, ignoring their visibility (stacking) order. This causes overlapping objects to be rendered with incorrect or ambiguous occlusion relationships, since neither text prompts nor bounding boxes alone specify which object should appear in front of another in shared regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250588/cropped_page0_idx17.webp)

## Input / Output

Beyond the standard text prompt and per-object bounding boxes, VODiff accepts an explicit visibility order (a numerical stacking rank) for each object, which is the key new input that disambiguates occlusion in overlapping regions. The method is training-free and operates entirely within a pre-trained Stable Diffusion pipeline, producing a single photorealistic RGB image where all objects respect both their spatial layout and their relative front-to-back ordering. The visibility order is consumed at two levels: it determines the sequential generation schedule (bottom-to-top) and defines region-specific attention constraints during denoising.

## Method

VODiff introduces two visibility-aware mechanisms into the denoising loop. First, a Sequential Denoising Process (SDP) inspired by the painter's algorithm stratifies objects into layers by visibility order and divides the T-step denoising into N sub-stages, each primarily generating one object from bottom to top; a smoothly transitioning visual guidance scale (λ) is assigned per object so that the active object receives full guidance while others receive half, with linear interpolation at stage boundaries to prevent structural collapse. Second, a Visibility-Order-Aware (VOA) loss splits each object's mask into overlapping, visible, and background sub-regions based on the visibility order, then optimizes the cross-attention maps via gradient descent on the noisy latent at every denoising step to enforce that upper-layer objects dominate attention in overlap regions, the target object is reinforced in its visible region, and background attention is suppressed. The two components are complementary: SDP provides coarse sequential structure while VOA corrects fine-grained spatial shifts introduced by convolutional layers that would otherwise cause position drift and false occlusions.

## Results

On VOBench, VODiff achieves the best CLIP-Score (29.73), Layout Alignment (55.11), and Occlusion Alignment (82.50) among all 13 compared methods, while also attaining the best user-study rankings across quality (1.50), layout (1.73), and occlusion (1.26). Its FID of 10.03 is competitive with the best training-based method MIGC (9.82) and superior to all training-free baselines (best: RPG at 11.66). The most striking gain is in Occlusion Alignment, where VODiff scores 82.50% versus 69.50% for the next-best method (AnyControl) and 51.50% for the best training-free method (RPG).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VODiff outperforms all 7 training-free baselines on every metric, with the largest margins in Occlusion Alignment (82.50 vs. 51.50 for RPG, the strongest training-free method) and Layout Alignment (55.11 vs. 51.15 for RPG). Against training-based methods, VODiff wins on CLIP-Score (29.73 vs. 29.63 AnyControl), LA (55.11 vs. 54.81 SmartControl), and OA (82.50 vs. 69.50 AnyControl), but trails MIGC on FID (10.03 vs. 9.82) and AnyControl on AR-Q (1.50 vs. 3.05 is actually a win for VODiff; AnyControl's AR-O of 2.05 is close to VODiff's 1.26). The occlusion advantage is the paper's central claim: VODiff's 82.50% OA exceeds the best training-based method (AnyControl, 69.50%) by 13 points and the best training-free method (RPG, 51.50%) by 31 points, confirming that explicit visibility-order modeling is the key differentiator. In the user study, VODiff ranks first on all three subjective criteria (quality, layout, occlusion), with AR-O of 1.26 versus 2.05 for AnyControl and 3.18 for MIGC.

## Relation to Prior Work

VODiff extends the layout-guided text-to-image generation line (GLIGEN, DenseDiffusion, MIGC, BoxDiff, Attention Refocusing) by introducing an explicit visibility-order constraint that prior methods entirely ignore. Conceptually, it repurposes the painter's algorithm from computer graphics—rendering objects from back to front—as a sequential denoising strategy, and builds on attention-map optimization techniques (R&B, Attention Refocusing) by adding occlusion-aware region-specific losses. Unlike sampling-based compositional methods (MultiDiffusion, LRDiffusion) that fuse multiple denoising paths for spatial consistency, VODiff operates within a single denoising trajectory but stratifies it into per-object sub-stages with dynamically varying guidance scales.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to treat object visibility order as an explicit, user-controllable input in T2I generation, filling a gap that layout-only methods leave open for overlapping objects. The training-free, plug-and-play nature means it can enhance existing methods (demonstrated with GLIGEN, InstanceDiffusion, ControlNet, MIGC) without retraining, which has practical appeal. However, the OA metric relies on GPT-4V as a judge, introducing evaluation uncertainty, and the method struggles with very high object counts. Open questions include whether the layer-by-layer strategy generalizes to 3D scenes or video generation, and whether the attention-level occlusion constraints can be made more robust to the local shifts that motivate them.
