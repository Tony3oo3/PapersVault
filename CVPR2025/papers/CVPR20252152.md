## Problem

In monocular 3D object detection, the 2D bounding box height frequently exceeds the true projected central height due to visual surface irregularities, causing geometric depth to deviate from ground truth. Existing remedies either abandon 2D geometric priors through direct depth prediction or rely on complex multi-depth fusion branches that fail to correct systematic errors when all predicted depths cluster on the same side of the truth. The paper asks whether a single, perspective-invariant geometry error can replace multi-depth prediction while preserving the interpretability of the projection formula.

## Input / Output

The network consumes a single-view RGB image (KITTI resolution) and outputs per-object 3D bounding boxes (center, dimensions, orientation), category labels, and 2D bounding boxes. Depth is not predicted directly but decomposed into a geometric depth computed from the focal length, 3D height, and 2D bbox height, plus a learned depth error term; the final depth is their sum. A lightweight depth map and multi-scale region segmentation probabilities are also produced as intermediate supervision signals.

## Method

MonoDGP builds on the DETR framework with a ResNet-50 backbone feeding two parallel transformer encoders (visual and depth). The key depth mechanism replaces raw depth prediction with a perspective-invariant geometry error Z_err added to the geometric depth Z_geo = f·H/h_bbox, yielding a symmetric, tightly distributed target that avoids the long-tail problem of raw depth and the skew of height-error alternatives. A decoupled 2D visual decoder independently initializes object queries and reference points from visual embeddings before they enter the 3D depth-guided decoder, preventing 3D supervision from destabilizing early 2D convergence. A Region Segmentation Head (U-Net with SEBlocks) predicts per-pixel target probabilities to enhance foreground features via element-wise multiplication and generates BERT-style segment embeddings that distinguish foreground from background tokens in the encoder.

## Results

On the KITTI benchmark (Car category, R40), MonoDGP achieves 22.34% moderate AP_3D on the validation set and 18.72% on the test set, setting a new state-of-the-art among monocular detectors without extra data. On the test set, it reaches 25.23% moderate AP_BEV and 26.35% easy AP_3D. The method improves moderate AP_3D by +1.55% (test) and +1.73% (val) over its baseline MonoDETR, and by +2.97% over MonoCD (CVPR 2024) on the validation set. The full model runs at 42 ms per frame on a single RTX 3090 with 38.9M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MonoDGP outperforms all monocular baselines without extra data on the KITTI moderate AP_3D ranking metric: it beats MonoDETR (its direct baseline) by +1.55% on test and +1.73% on val, MonoCD by +2.13% (test) and +2.97% (val), and FD3D by +1.60% (test) and +2.11% (val). It also surpasses methods that use LiDAR or depth maps as auxiliary supervision, including OccupancyM3D (18.72% vs 17.02% test Mod AP_3D) and MonoDTR (18.72% vs 15.39%), despite using only a single RGB image. The only metric where MonoDGP trails a LiDAR-based method is test Easy AP_BEV (35.24% vs 35.38% for OccupancyM3D), a negligible 0.14% gap. Among pure monocular methods, MonoDGP achieves the best AP_3D across all three difficulty levels on both test and val splits, and the best AP_BEV on val across all difficulties. The improvement is most pronounced on the Hard split (+1.80% over baseline), suggesting the geometry-error prior is particularly effective for distant or partially occluded objects where depth uncertainty is greatest.

## Relation to Prior Work

MonoDGP builds directly on MonoDETR's depth-guided transformer decoder but departs from the prevailing multi-depth prediction paradigm (MonoDDE, MonoCD, MonoDETR) by replacing weighted fusion of direct, map, and geometric depths with a single perspective-invariant geometry error correction. The perspective projection formula itself traces back to earlier geometric-constraint methods (Deep3DBox, Shift R-CNN, GUPNet), but MonoDGP reframes the residual between geometric and true depth as a learnable, viewpoint-invariant quantity rather than treating geometric depth as a final estimate. The decoupled 2D/3D decoder design and segment embeddings draw on DETR's query mechanism and BERT's token-type embeddings, while the RSH module repurposes U-Net and SEBlock architectures for feature enhancement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a clean conceptual alternative to the increasingly complex multi-branch depth prediction strategies that have dominated monocular 3D detection, showing that a single scalar error correction can match or exceed weighted fusion without additional branches. The systematic analysis of prediction difficulty (data distribution compactness, gradient stability, symmetry) provides a useful framework for choosing what to predict in geometrically-constrained problems. However, evaluation is limited to KITTI's car category, and the approach's generalizability to other datasets, object classes, or non-rigid objects remains untested. The authors note potential extension to dense depth map prediction, but the broader question of whether geometry-error priors transfer to novel sensor configurations or urban scenes with diverse object geometries is left open.
