## Problem

Existing 3D indoor scene synthesis methods face a controllability trade-off: text-conditioned diffusion models (e.g., DiffuScene) offer convenient but only rough alignment between prompts and generated scenes, while graph-based approaches (e.g., InstructScene) provide fine-grained control but require users to manually design detailed scene graphs. No prior work unifies free-form multimodal inputs (text, photos, sketches, top-view diagrams) with precise graph-level controllability in a single generative model.

## Input / Output

The system accepts free-form user inputs—text descriptions, realistic photographs, line-drawing sketches, or top-view diagrams, in any combination—which a VLM-based Graph Designer (GPT-4o with one-shot CoT prompting) parses into a partial graph of object categories and spatial relationship edges. The output is a complete 3D indoor scene layout specifying per-object category, position, orientation, size, and fVQ-VAE feature indices, from which concrete 3D-FUTURE mesh models are retrieved via OpenCLIP feature matching. The same MG-DiT model supports text-to-scene, graph-to-scene, rearrangement, completion, and stylization through different constrained-sampling configurations without retraining.

## Method

FreeScene comprises two components: a VLM-based Graph Designer that uses a one-shot Chain-of-Thought prompt with DFS traversal to hierarchically extract objects and pairwise spatial relationships from multimodal inputs, and MG-DiT, a Mixed Graph Diffusion Transformer that jointly denoises continuous attributes (size, position, orientation) and discrete attributes (category, fVQ-VAE feature indices, relationship edges) in a single diffusion process. MG-DiT embeds noisy node and edge features into a DiT backbone with FiLM-based node-edge interaction and cross-attention for text conditioning, training with a factored loss combining MSE on continuous noise and KL divergence on discrete posterior distributions. At inference, constrained sampling fixes user-specified graph variables (categories, edges, or both) at every denoising step while generating the remaining attributes, enabling a single trained model to handle text-to-scene, zero-shot graph-to-scene, rearrangement, completion, and stylization tasks.

## Results

On 3D-FRONT, FreeScene (Ours+GD) achieves the best generation quality across all three room types: FID of 108.0 (bedroom), 108.22 (living room), and 125.32 (dining room), with correspondingly best KID scores of 0.21×10⁻³, 3.87×10⁻³, and 10.68×10⁻³. For controllability, Ours+GD reaches iRecall of 81.40% (bedroom), 71.81% (living room), and 75.01% (dining room), substantially outperforming all baselines. The SCA scores (53.16%, 54.05%, 55.59%) are closest to the ideal 50%, indicating the most realistic generated scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeScene outperforms all three baselines across every metric and room type in the text-to-scene task. Against ATISS (autoregressive transformer), FreeScene+GD improves FID by 14–20 points and iRecall by 30–43 percentage points, reflecting ATISS's inability to integrate global text descriptions. Against DiffuScene (U-Net diffusion), the gap is even larger: FID improves by 17–34 points and KID by 1.5–25×10⁻³, attributed to DiffuScene's 1D convolution limiting global feature capture. Against the closest competitor InstructScene (dual text-to-graph + graph-to-scene model), FreeScene (without GD) already wins on FID, FID-CLIP, KID, SCA, and iRecall in all three room types; adding the Graph Designer extends the iRecall lead from ~1–2% to 9–14% (e.g., 81.40% vs. 72.71% on bedrooms). In graph-to-scene generation (Table 4), FreeScene also edges out InstructScene on FID (e.g., 98.31 vs. 101.86 for bedrooms) and iRecall (89.37% vs. 88.84%). In the multi-application table (Table 5), FreeScene wins on re-arrangement, completion, and unconditional generation FID/iRecall, while matching InstructScene on stylization (Δ≈0.31 vs. 0.29 for living rooms).

## Relation to Prior Work

FreeScene sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: the mixed (continuous + discrete) diffusion formulation from MiDiffusion [18] and the graph-prior-based controllability of InstructScene [28], from which it adopts fVQ-VAE feature quantization and constrained sampling. It departs from InstructScene's two-stage pipeline (text-to-graph model followed by graph-to-scene model) by collapsing both stages into a single Mixed Graph Diffusion Transformer (MG-DiT) that jointly denoises discrete graph tokens and continuous bounding-box attributes. The key architectural departure is the VLM-based Graph Designer, which uses GPT-4o with chain-of-thought prompting to convert free-form multimodal inputs (text, photographs, sketches, top-view diagrams) into partial scene graphs, replacing the need for users to manually specify object categories and spatial relations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework lowers the barrier to controllable 3D scene synthesis by accepting natural, heterogeneous user inputs rather than requiring users to hand-craft scene graphs or write highly structured prompts. Unifying text-to-scene, graph-to-scene, rearrangement, completion, and stylization in a single model via constrained sampling is practically appealing for interactive design tools. However, the authors acknowledge that the VLM-based graph extraction degrades with scene complexity, the model cannot precisely control exact object positions and orientations (limiting real-world usability), and the 3D-FRONT dataset's scale remains a bottleneck. Open questions include whether RAG-augmented generation can overcome data limitations and whether additional physical constraints (affordance, collision) can be integrated into the diffusion process.
