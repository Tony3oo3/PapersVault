## Problem

Existing CLIP-based compositional zero-shot learning methods treat attribute and object recognition as independent classification tasks, ignoring rich semantic relationships among primitive concepts: hierarchical structure among object classes, high inter-class similarity (e.g., dog vs. wolf), and the fact that the same attribute can have different visual meaning when paired with different objects. This paper addresses the gap by explicitly modeling these relationships through logic-induced learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250445/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resized to 224×224 with horizontal flip and random crop augmentation) and produces three outputs: a composition label (attribute-object pair), an attribute label, and an object label. At inference, the final prediction combines the composition score with the product of attribute and object scores; in open-world setting, a compatibility matrix further filters predictions. The framework is designed as a plug-and-play module that adds no computational overhead at inference time.

## Method

LOGICZSL constructs three primitive concept trees using GPT-4: an object-induction tree (grouping similar objects under superclasses), an object-extension tree (generating discriminative feature descriptions per object), and an attribute-extension tree (generating semantically similar texts per attribute). Three first-order logic rules are then defined over these trees—oi-logic enforces hierarchical containment, oe-logic enforces that an object implies its descriptive features, and ae-logic enforces that an attribute implies its semantically similar descriptions. These logical formulae are relaxed via fuzzy logic operators (product for AND, max for OR, complement for NOT) into differentiable loss functions that penalize score inconsistencies between parent/child or concept/description nodes. The resulting logic-induced losses are added to standard CZSL classification losses with a small weight (β=0.01), making the framework complementary to and compatible with any existing CLIP-based CZSL method, as demonstrated by plugging it into both CSP and Troika.

## Results

On CGQA (close world), Logic-Troika achieves 15.3 AUC and 33.3 HM, surpassing the previous best CAILA (14.8 AUC, 32.7 HM) by 0.5 AUC. On UT-Zappos50K (close world), Logic-Troika reaches 45.8 AUC and 57.8 HM, improving over Troika by 4.1 AUC and over CAILA by 1.7 AUC. On MIT-States (close world), Logic-Troika attains 23.4 AUC and 40.5 HM, edging out CAILA (23.4 AUC, 39.9 HM) on HM. Under open world settings, Logic-Troika similarly leads, e.g., 3.4 AUC on CGQA vs. 3.1 for CAILA, and 36.2 AUC on UT-Zappos50K vs. 32.8 for CAILA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LOGICZSL is applied as a plug-in to two CLIP-based base models (CSP and Troika), and consistently outperforms both: Logic-CSP gains +1.5 AUC on CGQA, +5.2 AUC on UT-Zappos50K, and +1.2 AUC on MIT-States over CSP; Logic-Troika gains +2.9, +4.1, and +1.3 AUC respectively over Troika. Against the strongest prior method CAILA, Logic-Troika wins on CGQA (15.3 vs. 14.8 AUC) and UT-Zappos50K (45.8 vs. 44.1 AUC), and ties on MIT-States AUC (23.4) while winning on HM (40.5 vs. 39.9). All other baselines (CoOp, Prod, HPL, PCVL, DFSP, PLID, CDS-CZSL) are substantially outperformed by Logic-Troika across every dataset and metric. Under open world settings, the same pattern holds: Logic-Troika leads CAILA by 0.3 AUC on CGQA, 3.4 AUC on UT-Zappos50K, and 0.5 AUC on MIT-States. The gains are most pronounced on UT-Zappos50K, where the fine-grained shoe categories benefit most from the object-extension and attribute-extension logic rules.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends CLIP-based CZSL methods (CSP, Troika) by adding logic-induced losses that encode semantic relationships among primitive concepts, drawing on the neural symbolic computing (NSC) paradigm previously applied to semantic segmentation (LogicSeg) and HOI detection (NeuralLogic). It departs from prior CZSL work that models only attribute-object conditional relationships by also capturing object-object hierarchical structure and attribute polysemy. The use of LLMs (GPT-4) to automatically construct the relational knowledge (primitive concept trees) distinguishes it from earlier NSC approaches where logical rules were manually specified, and from prior LLM-for-vision work that uses generated text purely as supervision signals rather than as structured logical constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LOGICZSL offers a plug-and-play framework that can be grafted onto any existing CLIP-based CZSL method without architectural modifications, making it broadly applicable. The neurosymbolic framing opens a path toward more interpretable and knowledge-grounded composition recognition, though the approach's reliance on LLM-generated descriptions introduces a potential error propagation risk if the generated relational knowledge is inaccurate. Open questions include whether the logic-induced losses scale to much larger composition spaces (e.g., open-vocabulary settings), whether the tree depth limitation (depth 2) is a fundamental constraint, and whether the framework generalizes to other zero-shot recognition tasks beyond CZSL.
