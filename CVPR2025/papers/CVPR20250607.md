## Problem

Existing camouflaged object detection methods focus on isolating the object from its highly similar environment, but this direct approach fails under strong camouflage because inter-feature similarity cannot reliably separate foreground from background. Supervised solutions require dense pixel-level annotations, while prompt-based methods suffer from LMM hallucinations. The paper asks: instead of finding the glass ball in water, why not pour off the water?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250607/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resized to 476×476 for DINOv2 feature extraction) and produces a binary segmentation mask of the camouflaged object, entirely training-free and without any annotation. The pipeline relies on a pre-built offline environment prototype library (generated once via LMMs and diffusion models) and optionally integrates with SAM or HQ-SAM for boundary refinement, in which case the EASE mask is converted to a bounding-box prompt. No post-processing such as CRF is applied.

## Method

EASE inverts the detection paradigm by identifying the salient environment and subtracting it to reveal the camouflaged object. The environment prototype library is constructed by DiffPro: LLaVA-1.5 labels environment categories from dataset images, Stable Diffusion generates environment-only images per category (avoiding object contamination), and DINOv2 extracts both global (average-pooled) and local (pixel-level) feature vectors. Retrieval proceeds in three stages: (1) KDE-AT sets an adaptive per-image threshold from the bimodal similarity distribution via kernel density estimation, eliminating fixed hyperparameters; (2) G2L first retrieves top-K globally similar prototype images, then performs pixel-level retrieval on their local features for fine-grained environment masks; (3) SR uses the G2L mask to compute foreground and background prototypes via mask-averaged pooling, then refines the environment mask by contrasting each pixel's similarity to these two self-derived prototypes. The final object mask is the complement of the averaged environment mask.

## Results

On the four standard COD benchmarks, EASE with DINOv2-ViT-L/14 achieves Sα of 0.819 (CHAMELEON), 0.749 (CAMO), 0.773 (COD10K), and 0.800 (NC4K), with corresponding Fβω scores of 0.741, 0.684, 0.656, and 0.735. This represents an average improvement of over 10% in Sα and Fβω across all datasets compared to the best prior unsupervised method (VoteCut/CuVLER ensemble). When integrated with HQ-SAM for prompt-based segmentation, EASE reaches Sα of 0.866 on COD10K and 0.866 on NC4K, performing competitively with fully-supervised SOTA methods such as HitNet (0.875) and FSPNet (0.879) on NC4K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EASE (DINOv2-ViT-L/14) outperforms all eight unsupervised baselines across every dataset and metric. The closest competitor, VoteCut with DINO(v2) ensemble [1], achieves Sα of 0.690 on COD10K versus EASE's 0.773, a gap of 8.3 points; on NC4K the gap widens to 6.1 points (0.739 vs. 0.800). DiffCut [10] and FOUND [65] trail by even larger margins (e.g., DiffCut Sα=0.591 vs. EASE 0.773 on COD10K). In the prompt-based setting (Table 2), EASE+HQSAM surpasses all four LMM-based prompt methods: WS-SAM [23] (Sα=0.829 on NC4K), GenSAM [28] (0.810), ProMaC [29] (not reported on NC4K), and MMCPF [70] (0.767). Against fully-supervised references, EASE+HQSAM (Sα=0.866 on NC4K) is within 1–3 points of the best supervised methods (HitNet 0.875, FSPNet 0.879, PopNet 0.861), while clearly exceeding FEDER [22] (0.847) and FDNet [82] (0.832). On the marine animal generalization task (Table 5), EASE leads all unsupervised baselines by a wide margin, e.g., Sα=0.780 vs. TokenCut's 0.714 on MAS3K.

## Relation to Prior Work

EASE departs from the dominant COD paradigm of directly detecting camouflaged objects within their environment by inverting the problem: it first identifies and removes the salient environment, then inverts the result to reveal the object. Conceptually, it is closest to FOUND [65], which also discovers objects by observing the background, but replaces FOUND's self-attention-based background estimation with a retrieval mechanism against a pre-built environment prototype library. The prototype construction pipeline (DiffPro) repurposes LMMs for environment categorization and diffusion models for environment-only image generation, drawing on retrieval-augmented generation ideas from NLP and retrieval-based segmentation from vision, while the retrieval schemes (G2L, SR, KDE-AT) build on the DINO/DINOv2 feature space established by unsupervised segmentation methods like TokenCut, MaskCut, and CuVLER.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, annotation-free nature of EASE is practically significant for COD, where dense pixel-level annotations are expensive and the task's difficulty (objects blending into backgrounds) makes even weak supervision challenging. Achieving over 10% average gain on COD10K over prior unsupervised SOTA and competitive performance with fully-supervised methods when paired with SAM demonstrates that the 'remove the environment' framing is not merely a conceptual reframe but yields substantial practical gains. Open questions include whether the static prototype library scales to truly open-world environments, how sensitive performance is to the quality and coverage of generated environment images, and whether the approach extends naturally to video or 3D camouflage settings where temporal or geometric cues matter.
