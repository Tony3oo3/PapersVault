## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods for vision-language models, such as prompt tuning and adapter tuning, rely on prior knowledge to manually designate which model components to adapt, often introducing extra parameter overhead. Automatically identifying and fine-tuning the most critical parameters in a VLM without adding new modules remains unexplored, leaving a gap between the need for task-specific adaptation and the desire to preserve pretrained knowledge efficiently.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (randomly cropped and resized to 224×224) and class name text prompts formatted with a template (e.g., 'a photo of [CLASS]'). Images are processed through patch embedding into token sequences, while text prompts are tokenized and embedded separately; both pass through their respective ViT-B/16 transformer encoders. The output is a per-class probability distribution computed via cosine similarity between the projected visual and text embeddings, divided by a learnable temperature, yielding a classification label and confidence score.

## Method

CLIP-AST operates in two stages. First, a brief transformer fine-tuning phase (1 epoch) runs the full AdamW optimizer over the CLIP transformer blocks to populate the second-moment estimates (v̂) for every parameter. Second, the adaptive selective fine-tuning stage computes a scalar importance score per sub-layer as the average of 1/√v̂, ranks sub-layers within each transformer layer, and trains only the top-K most important sub-layers while freezing all others. The key insight is that AdamW's adaptive learning rate already encodes parameter importance: parameters with low second-moment estimates receive large effective updates and thus capture task-relevant features. A self-consistency loss (L1 on embeddings plus KL divergence on logits against a frozen CLIP copy) regularizes the selective updates to prevent catastrophic forgetting, and the total loss combines cross-entropy with weighted SCL terms. No extra parameters are introduced, so inference speed matches the original CLIP model.

## Results

On ImageNet few-shot learning, CLIP-AST achieves 73.91% top-1 accuracy at 16-shot, surpassing the previous best (GraphAdapter at 73.40%) and the zero-shot CLIP baseline (66.92%) by 7.0 points. Across 11 datasets in the base-to-novel setting, CLIP-AST attains a harmonic mean of 81.06%, outperforming PromptSRC (79.97%) and MMA (79.87%). In the out-of-distribution setting, CLIP-AST reaches 62.87% average accuracy over ImageNet, ImageNet-Sketch, and ImageNet-V2, a 1.15-point gain over the next-best method (PromptSRC at 61.72%). The method also matches the original CLIP's inference speed (1323 FPS) while requiring only 100 seconds of training on StanfordCars.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLIP-AST consistently outperforms all seven compared baselines across every experimental setting. In few-shot learning on ImageNet, it beats GraphAdapter (73.40%) and PromptSRC (73.17%) by 0.51 and 0.74 points at 16-shot, with the gap widening at lower shot counts (e.g., +3.96 over CoOp at 1-shot). In the base-to-novel setting across 11 datasets, CLIP-AST achieves the highest harmonic mean (81.06%), surpassing PromptSRC (79.97%) by 1.09 points and MMA (79.87%) by 1.19 points, with particularly large gains on EuroSAT (+4.37% HM over MMA) and FGVC Aircraft (+2.78% over PromptSRC). In the out-of-distribution setting, CLIP-AST leads by 1.15 points in average accuracy over PromptSRC (61.72%) and 1.19 over Tip-Adapter (61.68%), while also achieving the highest in-distribution ImageNet accuracy (73.87%). Notably, CLIP-AST matches the original CLIP's inference speed (1323 FPS) and requires less training time (100 s) than prompt-based methods (CoCoOp: 2280 s; PromptSRC: 630 s), while adapter methods (Tip-Adapter: 30 s) are faster to train but achieve lower accuracy (83.09% vs. 85.64% on StanfordCars).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) for vision-language models and selective fine-tuning. It departs from prompt tuning (CoOp, CoCoOp, PromptSRC) and adapter tuning (Tip-Adapter, MMA, GraphAdapter), which all rely on prior knowledge to manually designate where to insert learnable modules, by instead using the AdamW optimizer's second-moment gradient estimates as automatic importance scores to select which existing sub-layer parameters to fine-tune. It also extends the selective fine-tuning line (BitFit's fixed bias selection, BSR's layer-output comparison, SPT's sensitivity-based selection) by grounding the selection criterion in an optimizer-internal signal rather than external heuristics, and by operating at sub-layer granularity within each transformer layer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is that CLIP-AST introduces zero extra parameters and preserves the original CLIP inference speed, making it a drop-in replacement for any CLIP-based pipeline without architectural modification. However, the approach is currently validated only on ViT-B/16 CLIP with a single optimizer (AdamW), leaving open whether the second-moment heuristic generalizes to other architectures (e.g., SigLIP, EVA-CLIP), other optimizers, or non-contrastive VLMs. The paper also does not deeply analyze *why* lower second-moment estimates correlate with task-critical parameters, so the theoretical grounding remains heuristic. Future work on extending the selection criterion beyond a single optimizer's internal state and on multi-task or continual settings would strengthen the contribution.
