## Problem

Existing one-step diffusion distillation methods for real-world image super-resolution (e.g., OSEDiff with VSD loss) suffer from two critical issues: the teacher model provides unreliable gradient directions when the student's initial outputs are suboptimal, and uniform timestep sampling fails to adequately optimize high-frequency detail recovery. This paper addresses both limitations to achieve a one-step model that matches or exceeds multi-step diffusion methods in restoration quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251351/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-quality RGB image (128×128 in real-world benchmarks) and produces a 512×512 super-resolved image in one forward pass. The student model operates in the latent space of a frozen VAE decoder, directly denoising from the LQ latent without requiring a separate reconstruction network. A fixed default text prompt is used for the student, while HQ-extracted prompts condition the teacher and LoRA models during training.

## Method

TSD-SR distills a pre-trained SD3 text-to-image diffusion model into a one-step student via two novel components. Target Score Distillation (TSD) blends a new Target Score Matching (TSM) loss—which aligns the teacher model's noise predictions on synthetic latents with those on ground-truth HQ latents—with the original VSD loss, providing a reliable optimization trajectory even when student outputs are poor. The Distribution-Aware Sampling Module (DASM) accumulates gradients along the diffusion sampling trajectory within a single training iteration by iteratively denoising with the LoRA model, effectively concentrating optimization on early timesteps where high-frequency detail recovery is most challenging. The student is trained with LPIPS reconstruction loss plus TSD regularization, while the LoRA replica is alternately updated with a standard diffusion denoising loss.

## Results

On DRealSR, TSD-SR (1 step) achieves the best LPIPS (0.2967), DISTS (0.2136), FID (134.98), NIQE (5.9131), MUSIQ (66.62), and CLIPIQA (0.7344) among all compared methods, while trailing only ResShift on PSNR (27.77 vs. 28.69) and SSIM (0.7559 vs. 0.7874). On RealSR, it similarly leads in LPIPS (0.2743), DISTS (0.2104), FID (114.45), NIQE (5.1298), MUSIQ (71.19), and CLIPIQA (0.7160). Inference time is 0.136 s on A100, making it 40× faster than SeeSR (5.82 s) and 120× faster than SUPIR (16.87 s), and the fastest among all one-step baselines (vs. OSEDiff 0.165 s, SinSR 0.142 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against one-step baselines, TSD-SR outperforms OSEDiff-1s on LPIPS (0.2967 vs. 0.2968, marginal), DISTS (0.2136 vs. 0.2162), FID (134.98 vs. 135.51), NIQE (5.91 vs. 6.45), MUSIQ (66.62 vs. 64.69), and CLIPIQA (0.7344 vs. 0.6958) on DRealSR, while AddSR-1s and SinSR-1s are clearly inferior across nearly all perceptual and no-reference metrics. Versus multi-step methods, TSD-SR achieves the best perceptual quality (LPIPS, DISTS) and distributional fidelity (FID) across all three datasets, surpassing even 50-step SeeSR and 200-step StableSR. The main trade-off is on reconstruction metrics: ResShift-15s attains higher PSNR (28.69) and SSIM (0.7874) on DRealSR, and AddSR-4s edges out on MANIQA (0.6257 vs. 0.5874), which the authors attribute to the known perception–distortion trade-off where richer detail recovery lowers pixel-level fidelity scores. In inference speed, TSD-SR (0.136 s) is the fastest of all compared methods, beating the next-fastest one-step model SinSR (0.142 s) and being 40× faster than SeeSR and 120× faster than SUPIR.

## Relation to Prior Work

TSD-SR directly extends the one-step diffusion distillation line initiated by OSEDiff, which first brought Variational Score Distillation (VSD) from text-to-3D generation (ProlificDreamer) into Real-ISR. The paper identifies two concrete failure modes of VSD in the restoration setting—unreliable teacher gradients on low-quality synthetic latents and suboptimal uniform timestep sampling for detail recovery—and addresses them with Target Score Matching (TSM) and the Distribution-Aware Sampling Module (DASM), respectively. Conceptually, it sits between the GAN-based one-step methods (Real-ESRGAN, BSRGAN) and the multi-step diffusion-based methods (SeeSR, SUPIR, PASD), aiming to retain diffusion priors while matching GAN-level inference speed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving competitive or superior quality to multi-step diffusion models (20–200 steps) in a single forward pass, TSD-SR makes high-quality Real-ISR practical for latency-sensitive applications such as mobile photography pipelines. The TSD loss and DASM are modular contributions that could transfer to other restoration or generation tasks where score distillation from a pretrained diffusion prior is used. The main open limitation is model size: the approach still relies on a full SD3 backbone, and the authors acknowledge that pruning or quantization is needed to match the parameter efficiency of GAN-based methods. Whether the DASM trajectory-accumulation strategy generalizes beyond the flow-matching scheduler used here remains an open question.
