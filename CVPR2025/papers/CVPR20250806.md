## Problem

MLLMs encode images as large numbers of vision tokens whose count grows quadratically with resolution, causing prohibitive inference costs. Existing prompt-aware token reduction methods (FastV, VTW, PDrop) rely on handcrafted layer-specific rules that do not generalize across different MLLMs or benchmarks, and they only address cost reduction without optimizing how to allocate a fixed computational budget for maximum performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250806/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pretrained MLLM (e.g., LLaVA-1.5-7B, InternVL2-1B/2B/4B/8B) that receives an RGB image and a text instruction prompt. The key output is a per-layer keeping rate sequence (one rate per LLM layer from layer 3 to L) that determines how many of the original N vision tokens to retain at each layer; at inference, a plug-and-play Sort & Reduce module applies these rates before each LLM layer. The rates are searched offline via Bayesian Optimization on a small holdout dataset, making the entire procedure training-free and model-specific.

## Method

The core insight is that the ranking of vision tokens by their attention scores to instruction tokens remains highly correlated (Kendall's Tau ≥ 0.7) across consecutive layers after the first, implying the number of essential top tokens does not increase with depth. For Scenario I (cost reduction with minimal accuracy drop), G-Search greedily determines the minimum keeping rate at each layer from shallow to deep using Bayesian Optimization over a target function balancing effectiveness and efficiency, and the authors prove this greedy procedure yields the globally optimal keeping rate sequence. For Scenario II (performance maximization under a fixed budget), P-Sigmoid parameterizes the keeping rate curve as r̂(i) = 2b / (1 + e^{k(i−α)}), where b encodes the token budget and α is the midpoint; only the steepness parameter k is searched via Bayesian Optimization, since the integral of the curve over the layer domain is always (L−2)·b regardless of k, guaranteeing the budget constraint is met.

## Results

For Scenario I (efficiency without performance loss), G-Search reduces TFLOPs by 16.9%–49.2% across InternVL2-1B/2B/4B/8B while maintaining average accuracy within 0.2–0.7% of the full model (e.g., 70.10 vs 70.83 on InternVL2-8B, 48.77 vs 48.97 on LLaVA-1.5-7B). For Scenario II (better performance under a fixed budget), P-Sigmoid outperforms FastV by +3.38% average accuracy on LLaVA-1.5-7B and +7.69% on InternVL2-8B when 87.5% of vision tokens are removed, with the largest gains on OCR & Chart benchmarks (e.g., ChartQA: 61.8 vs 35.0 on InternVL2-8B). Overall, G-Search achieves up to 2.3× speedup in prefilling time with negligible accuracy drops.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three prompt-aware baselines (VTW, PDrop, FastV), G-Search consistently achieves the lowest TFLOPs while matching or exceeding their accuracy. On LLaVA-1.5-7B, G-Search reaches 48.77 avg accuracy at 3.95 TFLOPs, versus FastV's 48.70 at 5.49 TFLOPs and PDrop's 48.70 at 5.47 TFLOPs; VTW degrades severely to 44.32. The gap widens on InternVL2 models: VTW drops to 52.51 on InternVL2-8B (−18.3% from full), while G-Search retains 70.10 (−0.7%), showing that handcrafted reduction strategies do not generalize across architectures. For Scenario II, P-Sigmoid beats FastV by +3.38% (LLaVA-1.5-7B) to +7.69% (InternVL2-8B) average accuracy at matched ~87.5% token budgets, with the largest per-benchmark gains on ChartQA (+26.8 on InternVL2-8B) and DocVQA (+20.3). G-Search also stacks on top of prompt-agnostic methods (TokenPacker, DeCo), further reducing TFLOPs by 33.9–35.3% with <0.26% accuracy loss, confirming that instruction-aware pruning removes tokens that prompt-agnostic compression retains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prompt-aware vision token reduction line (FastV, VTW, PDrop) by replacing handcrafted per-layer reduction rules with an automatic search (G-Search) grounded in a new empirical finding: attention-score rankings of vision tokens are highly correlated (Kendall's Tau ≥ 0.7) across consecutive LLM layers after the first. It also draws on the attention-sink and KV-cache compression ideas from StreamingLLM and SnapKV, but applies them to vision tokens in MLLMs rather than text tokens in LLMs. The P-Sigmoid formulation for Scenario II (maximizing performance under a fixed token budget) departs from all prior reduction work, which only targeted cost reduction with minimal accuracy loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2×+ inference speedup with <0.2% accuracy drop is practically significant for deploying MLLMs on edge devices and in latency-sensitive applications, and the training-free, plug-and-play nature lowers adoption barriers. The demonstration that handcrafted reduction strategies fail to generalize across MLLMs (Table 4) is a useful caution against one-size-fits-all pruning heuristics. Open questions include whether the layer-correlation finding and the sigmoid-shaped keeping-rate profile hold for video MLLMs, for models with different attention mechanisms (e.g., Mamba-based), and whether the Bayesian optimization search overhead becomes prohibitive for very deep models. The finding that training with reductions yields no benefit (Table 5) suggests the redundancy is architectural rather than learned, but this was tested only on LLaVA-1.5-7B.
