## Problem

Existing continual learning methods focus on mitigating catastrophic forgetting but treat the decision-making process as a black box, leaving the evolving rationale behind predictions unexplained. As new tasks are introduced, understanding what the model has learned and how it retains prior knowledge becomes critical for trust and robustness, yet interpretability in continual learning remains largely unexplored. This paper addresses the dual challenge of maintaining both high accuracy and transparent, human-understandable reasoning across sequential tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (processed at 224×224 through a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 encoder) and class names (encoded via CLIP text encoder) as input. For each new task, ChatGPT generates candidate concepts per class, which are filtered by a learning-to-search module into a task-specific concept bottleneck. The output is a predicted class label over all seen classes plus per-concept contribution scores (positive or negative) that decompose the prediction into human-readable semantic factors, enabling post-hoc inspection of which visual attributes drove the decision.

## Method

The framework builds a Concept Bottleneck Layer (CBL) whose neurons correspond to CLIP text embeddings of LLM-generated concepts, projecting frozen CLIP image features into a cumulative concept space that expands with each new task. A concept alignment loss enforces cosine similarity between the CBL's concept score matrix and the CLIP concept activation matrix (with E³ sharpening), anchoring learned representations to stable semantic knowledge. To combat forgetting without exemplars, a semantic-guided prototype augmentation module identifies the most semantically similar new class to each old class via text-embedding cosine similarity, then transfers the feature discrepancy to generate pseudo-features for previously learned classes. The final classifier is regularized with an elastic-net sparsity penalty to encourage sparse, interpretable concept-to-class mappings. Only the CBL and classifier are trained (with CLIP encoders frozen), making the approach architecture-agnostic and parameter-efficient.

## Results

On ImageNet-subset B-10 Inc-10, the method achieves 86.83% average incremental accuracy (Ā) and 78.97% final accuracy (A_last), outperforming the best baseline EASE (84.80% / 70.82%) by 2.03% and 8.15% respectively. On CIFAR-100 B-50 Inc-5, it reaches 79.07% Ā and 75.91% A_last, surpassing EASE (76.72% / 70.50%). On fine-grained CUB-200 B-100 Inc-10, the method achieves 82.20% Ā, exceeding the next-best SLCA (76.85%) by 5.35%. Overall, the method attains the best or second-best performance across all seven datasets and both split strategies, with a maximum improvement of 3.06% in A_last on ImageNet-subset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all nine baselines on the majority of dataset-split combinations. Against the strongest non-interpretability baselines, EASE leads on CIFAR-100 B-10 Inc-10 (85.07 vs. 84.49 Ā) and Tiny-ImageNet B-10 Inc-10 (79.88 vs. 79.28 Ā), but the proposed method wins decisively on ImageNet-subset B-10 Inc-10 (86.83 vs. 84.80 Ā, 78.97 vs. 70.82 A_last) and Tiny-ImageNet B-100 Inc-10 (75.64 vs. 70.42 Ā). CPP is competitive on ImageNet-subset B-50 Inc-5 (79.74 vs. 81.85 Ā) but is outperformed by 2–5% on all other settings. Continual-CLIP and SLCA are consistently surpassed, with gaps of 3–10% on Ā across datasets. Prompt-based methods (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt) and LAE trail by 5–15% on most benchmarks. Against the interpretable baseline ICICLE, the method achieves substantially higher accuracy (e.g., 66.21 vs. 39.31 on CUB-200 B-50 Inc-50; 75.26 vs. 24.22 on Stanford-cars B-14 Inc-14) while requiring fewer trainable parameters and supporting both ViT and CNN backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate threads: Concept Bottleneck Models (Koh et al., 2020; Yan et al., 2023) for interpretable classification, and exemplar-free class-incremental learning with pre-trained models (L2P, DualPrompt, EASE, etc.). It directly builds on the CLIP-based CBM framework of Yan et al. (2023) for concept generation and selection, and on the concept-alignment loss of Oikarinen et al. (2023), but repurposes both within a continual learning loop where the bottleneck layer and classifier must expand across tasks. The only prior interpretable-CL method, ICICLE (Rymarczyk et al., 2023), relies on prototypical parts in a CNN and imposes interpretability regularization that the authors argue limits plasticity; this paper departs from that by using language-guided concepts and a frozen CLIP backbone, making the approach architecture-agnostic and substantially more performant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that interpretability constraints (concept alignment, sparse classifier) need not come at a performance cost in continual learning, and in fact the semantic structure of concepts aids retention across tasks. This opens a path toward CL systems whose decisions remain transparent as the class space grows, which is important for safety-critical or regulated deployments. Key limitations remain: concept quality is bottlenecked by LLM-generated descriptions that sometimes include non-visual attributes, and the concept-selection module adds training overhead. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very long task sequences, whether pre-defined (non-learned) concepts suffice, and how the framework behaves under domain shift rather than pure class increment.
