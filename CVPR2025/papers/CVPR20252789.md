## Problem

Controlling text-to-image generation with desired properties (aesthetic quality, user intention) via prompt adaptation is challenging because RL-based methods like Promptist converge to deterministic policies that generate similar postfixes, reducing the approach to simple heuristics. Naively applying GFlowNets to this task also suffers from mode collapse, which the paper traces to a previously overlooked progressive loss of neural plasticity (increasing dormant neurons) compounded by inefficient credit assignment in sequential token generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252789/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a short initial text prompt (e.g., a 5-word ChatGPT-generated description or a COCO caption) and produces a single adapted prompt via a GPT-2 language model acting as a GFlowNet forward policy. The adapted prompt is then fed to a frozen text-to-image diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v1.4 by default) to generate images. The reward function combines aesthetic improvement (LAION aesthetic predictor difference) and CLIP-based relevance between the generated image and the original prompt, with a KL regularization term keeping adapted prompts close to natural language.

## Method

PAG reformulates prompt adaptation as probabilistic inference: the optimal policy p*(y|x) ∝ p_ref(y|x)·exp(r(x,y)/β) defines an unnormalized density that a GFlowNet learns to sample from, shifting from reward maximization to distribution matching. To combat mode collapse, PAG introduces three complementary mechanisms: (1) flow reactivation, which periodically resets only the last layer of the flow function every M steps to revive dormant neurons while preserving the forward policy; (2) reward-prioritized sampling, which draws training examples from a replay buffer with probability proportional to exp(R) to maintain access to high-reward regions after resets; and (3) progressive reward decomposition, which extends the reward to intermediate states via R(x, y_{0:t}) = p_ref(y_{0:t}|x) for t < T, enabling token-level credit assignment through a forward-looking detailed balance (FL-DB) objective that sums per-step losses across the generation sequence.

## Results

On the COCO evaluation set with the default reward function, PAG achieves a reward of 0.88±0.02 and prompt diversity of 0.32±0.00, outperforming vanilla GFlowNets (0.81±0.15 reward, 0.20 diversity) and Promptist (0.70±0.05 reward, 0.02 diversity). Under the ImageReward function, PAG scores 0.83±0.01 reward with 0.29±0.00 diversity, while under HPSv2 it achieves 0.02±0.00 reward with 0.37±0.00 diversity. In zero-shot transfer to SD3, PAG reaches 0.95±0.05 reward, substantially exceeding all baselines (SFT 0.77, Promptist 0.73, GFlowNets 0.75).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Promptist (the PPO-based RL approach), PAG wins decisively on both reward and diversity: on ImageReward, PAG scores 0.83 vs. 0.70 for Promptist, while diversity jumps from 0.02 to 0.29, indicating Promptist's reward-maximizing policy collapses to near-deterministic postfix generation. Against vanilla GFlowNets (TB objective), PAG improves reward from 0.81 to 0.83 (with much lower variance: ±0.01 vs. ±0.15) and nearly doubles diversity from 0.20 to 0.29, confirming that the flow reactivation and reward decomposition components are critical for preventing mode collapse. In the zero-shot transfer setting (Table 2), PAG outperforms all baselines across four different text-to-image models, with the largest gap on SD3 (0.95 vs. 0.75–0.77 for baselines), suggesting that diverse prompt generation provides robustness that reward-maximizing methods lack. Compared to DDPO (direct diffusion fine-tuning), PAG achieves competitive aesthetic quality while producing visibly more diverse outputs, positioning prompt adaptation as a complementary zero-shot alternative to parameter-level fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the RL-based prompt adaptation paradigm established by Promptist (Hao et al., NeurIPS 2024) by reformulating the problem as probabilistic inference via GFlowNets, shifting from reward maximization to sampling from an unnormalized reward-weighted distribution. It builds on the GFlowNet training objectives (Trajectory Balance, Detailed Balance, Forward-Looking) from Bengio et al. and Malkin et al., and draws on the dormant neuron / capacity loss literature from deep RL (Sokar et al., Lyle et al.) to diagnose a previously overlooked mode collapse mechanism in GFlowNet fine-tuning. The method also positions itself as a lightweight alternative to direct diffusion model fine-tuning approaches (DDPO, DPO-Diff) that require parameter access, enabling zero-shot transfer across closed-source text-to-image models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Prompt adaptation is a practically important alternative to fine-tuning diffusion models, especially for closed-source systems where parameter access is unavailable, and this paper demonstrates that GFlowNets-based prompt adaptation can match or exceed RL-based methods while preserving diversity. The identification of progressive neural plasticity loss as the root cause of mode collapse in GFlowNet fine-tuning is a useful diagnostic that may generalize to other sequential generation tasks. However, the evaluation is limited to GPT-2-scale language models and a small set of reward functions, and it remains open whether the plasticity loss phenomenon and the flow reactivation remedy scale to larger LLMs or more complex compositional generation tasks. The zero-shot transferability to SD3 is promising but the gap over baselines there may partly reflect SD3's sensitivity to prompt quality rather than a fundamental advantage of the method.
