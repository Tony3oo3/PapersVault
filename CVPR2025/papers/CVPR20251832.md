## Problem

Existing multi-agent SLAM systems either produce sparse, low-fidelity maps (ORB-SLAM3, CCM-SLAM, Swarm-SLAM) or suffer from prohibitive latency in dense reconstruction (CP-SLAM requires >10 s per frame with NeRF-based representations). No prior system achieves real-time, scalable, photorealistic 3D reconstruction across multiple agents with globally consistent maps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251832/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent ingests a sequential stream of RGB images and depth maps (e.g., 2,500 frames per agent on Multi-agent Replica, 500–1,000 frames on 7-Scenes). The system outputs per-agent SE(3) pose trajectories, a shared 3D Gaussian splat map stored in shared memory accessible by all agents, and rendered RGB/depth images from arbitrary viewpoints. Inter-agent overlap detection uses a pre-trained feature embedding model (dot-product similarity with threshold 0.8) on vectorized 2D images, and G-ICP with a 0.02 m distance threshold handles point-level alignment.

## Method

MAC-Ego3D introduces a Multi-Agent Gaussian Consensus mechanism that operates at two levels using 3D Gaussian splats as a unified map representation. Intra-Agent Consensus lets each agent independently refine its local map through four steps—FOV-based Gaussian selection, spatial-proximity association, Mahalanobis-distance pose alignment, and photometric-loss optimization via differentiable rendering. Inter-Agent Consensus periodically aligns overlapping local maps by detecting overlaps through image-embedding similarity, associating co-visible Gaussians, computing a relative SE(3) transform that minimizes weighted Mahalanobis discrepancies (Eq. 6), and jointly optimizing a combined photometric (L1 + SSIM) and depth (L1) loss with a pose-graph term (Eq. 8). The key architectural insight is that Gaussian splats enable both fast rasterization (0.005 s/frame) for rapid inter-agent association and gradient-based joint optimization across agents, while asynchronous multiprocessing of intra- and inter-agent threads preserves real-time performance (0.02 s tracking, 0.05 s mapping per frame).

## Results

On the Multi-agent Replica dataset, MAC-Ego3D achieves an average ATE RMSE of 0.14 cm, nearly an order of magnitude better than the previous SOTA CP-SLAM (1.23 cm) and substantially outperforming sparse methods (ORB-SLAM3: 1.99 cm, Swarm-SLAM: 3.60 cm). For dense mapping, MAC-Ego3D reaches 40.04 dB PSNR versus CP-SLAM's 29.33 dB (a 10.7 dB gain), with SSIM of 0.977 vs. 0.843 and depth L1 error halved to 0.74 mm vs. 1.40 mm. On the real-world 7-Scenes dataset, MAC-Ego3D improves PSNR by ~4 dB (21.02 vs. 17.20) and SSIM by 31% (0.774 vs. 0.592) over CP-SLAM while avoiding the tracking failures CP-SLAM suffers on sequences like 'Chess' and 'Stairs'. In runtime, MAC-Ego3D tracks at 0.02 s/frame and maps at 0.05 s/frame, roughly 15× faster than CP-SLAM's 10.10 s/frame mapping time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAC-Ego3D decisively outperforms all five baselines on tracking accuracy across both datasets. Against the closest dense-mapping competitor CP-SLAM [8] (NeurIPS 2023), MAC-Ego3D reduces average ATE RMSE from 1.23 cm to 0.14 cm on Multi-agent Replica (≈8.8× improvement) and improves PSNR by 10.7 dB (40.04 vs. 29.33) and 3.8 dB on 7-Scenes (21.02 vs. 17.20). Against sparse classical methods, MAC-Ego3D beats ORB-SLAM3 [1] by 14× in ATE (0.14 vs. 1.99 cm) and Swarm-SLAM [14] by 26× (0.14 vs. 3.60 cm), while CCM-SLAM [25] fails entirely on the multi-room Apartment-0 sequence. On runtime, MAC-Ego3D (0.02 s track / 0.05 s map per frame) is ~15× faster in mapping than CP-SLAM (10.10 s) and ~6× faster than Vox-Fusion [34] (0.83 s), while also achieving lower ATE than NICE-SLAM [38] (0.15 vs. 2.12 cm) and ORB-SLAM3 (0.15 vs. 0.56 cm) on loop sequences. The only scenario where a baseline is competitive is ORB-SLAM3's raw tracking speed (0.03 s vs. 0.02 s), but ORB-SLAM3 produces only sparse keypoint maps and cannot match MAC-Ego3D's photorealistic reconstruction quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) and single-agent Gaussian-splat SLAM (SplatAM, Keetha et al., 2024) into the multi-agent collaborative setting, introducing a 'Gaussian Consensus' mechanism that unifies intra-agent temporal coherence with inter-agent spatial alignment. It departs from prior multi-agent SLAM systems (CCM-SLAM, Swarm-SLAM, ORB-SLAM3) that produce only sparse keypoint maps, and from CP-SLAM, which achieves dense collaborative mapping via NeRF-inspired implicit representations but at >10 s per frame. The key conceptual shift is treating Gaussian splats as a shared, differentiable, and efficiently renderable primitive that enables both photometric and geometric consensus across agents in real time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MAC-Ego3D is the first real-time full-cycle multi-agent dense SLAM system, bridging the long-standing gap between sparse multi-agent localization and slow dense collaborative reconstruction. The 15× speedup over CP-SLAM and order-of-magnitude ATE reduction make photorealistic collaborative mapping practically viable for robotic swarms and AR. However, the authors acknowledge limitations: no communication bandwidth constraints are imposed, collaboration is sequential rather than parallel, and evaluation is confined to single-room indoor scenes, leaving large-scale outdoor mapping, memory compression for growing splat counts, and bandwidth-limited settings as open problems.
