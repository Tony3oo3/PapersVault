## Problem

Existing Continual Test-Time Adaptation (CTTA) methods fail to maintain stable inter-class topological structures in the feature space during continuous domain shifts. Unsupervised self-training causes progressive distortion of class centroid relationships and intra-class uniformity, leading to erroneous pseudo-labels that compound into severe error accumulation over time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251598/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB test images in sequential batches (sizes from 1 to 300) from a continuously shifting target domain, with no access to source training data. Each image is randomly augmented to construct class centroid proxies, and the model (WideResNet, ResNeXt-29, or ResNet-50 backbone with a linear head) outputs a class label prediction. Adaptation is fully online: the inter-class topological graph and centroid estimates are updated incrementally batch-by-batch via exponential moving averages, so the method handles both balanced and highly imbalanced class distributions within each batch.

## Method

TCA builds on a mean-teacher self-training framework and introduces a class topological consistency constraint that jointly enforces inter-class uniformity and intra-class compactness. Inter-class uniformity is measured as the log of the average Gaussian potential over all centroid pairs in a dynamically maintained topological graph (centroids updated via EMA across batches), pushing class centroids apart to prevent topology collapse. Intra-class compactness applies pairwise uniformization within each class to keep features tight without blurring boundaries. A batch imbalance topology weighting (BTW) matrix, computed from per-class frequencies and smoothed via EMA, reweights the inter-class uniformity loss to account for uneven class representation in each batch. The total training objective combines symmetric cross-entropy (self-training), an alignment loss on augmented positives, and the topology loss, with the BTW-weighted inter-class term replacing the unweighted version after the first batch.

## Results

TCA achieves mean classification error rates of 14.7% on CIFAR-10-C, 29.7% on CIFAR-100-C, and 59.3% on ImageNet-C (severity level 5, online evaluation), outperforming all baselines on every dataset. On the long-sequence CCC-benchmark, TCA attains average accuracy of 32.9% (49.1% Easy, 39.5% Medium, 10.1% Hard), surpassing RDumb (32.6%) and EATA (30.8%). The method also remains effective at batch size 1, reducing CIFAR-10-C error from 81.6% (CoTTA) to 61.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TCA outperforms all nine baselines on all three primary corruption benchmarks. On CIFAR-10-C, TCA (14.7% error) beats the closest competitor DSS (16.0%) by 1.3 points and CoTTA (16.2%) by 1.5 points. On CIFAR-100-C, TCA (29.7%) leads RMT (30.4%) by 0.7 points and DSS (30.9%) by 1.2 points, with a particularly large margin over TENT (60.9%) which suffers severe error accumulation. On ImageNet-C, TCA (59.3%) edges out RMT and DSS (both 60.2%) by ~0.9 points and CoTTA (62.7%) by 3.4 points. On the long-sequence CCC-benchmark, TCA (32.9% avg accuracy) narrowly beats RDumb (32.6%) and clearly surpasses EATA (30.8%), SANTA (29.9%), and CoTTA (7.9%), confirming its robustness over extended domain-shift sequences.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the CoTTA mean-teacher framework for continual test-time adaptation and adopts the symmetric cross-entropy self-training loss from RMT, while drawing on the alignment-uniformity decomposition from contrastive representation learning (Wang et al., 2021). Its key departure is reframing the CTTA problem as one of maintaining the geometric topology of the feature space: rather than focusing solely on pseudo-label quality or entropy minimization (as in TENT, CoTTA, RMT, DSS, BeCoTTA), TCA explicitly constrains inter-class centroid distances and intra-class compactness to prevent the progressive topological collapse that prior methods leave unaddressed. The batch imbalance topology weighting further distinguishes it by incorporating per-batch class frequency into the topology loss, a mechanism absent from prior CTTA work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Identifying inter-class topology collapse as a distinct failure mode in CTTA provides a useful diagnostic lens for the field, and the proposed constraint is lightweight and composable with existing mean-teacher pipelines. However, the performance gains, while consistent across CIFAR-10-C, CIFAR-100-C, and ImageNet-C, are moderate (roughly 1–3 percentage points over the strongest baselines), and on the long-sequence CCC-benchmark TCA only marginally outperforms the simple RDumb baseline (32.9% vs 32.6%), suggesting that topology maintenance alone may not be sufficient for very long adaptation streams. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-CNN backbones (e.g., vision transformers), whether the topology constraint interacts well with parameter-efficient adaptation methods, and how it behaves under more extreme or non-stationary domain shifts beyond the corruption-based benchmarks used here.
