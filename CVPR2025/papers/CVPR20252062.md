## Problem

While two-view homography cannot constrain focal length (any homography is realizable with arbitrary intrinsics), three-view homographies of a planar scene do provide such constraints. However, the existing solver by Heikkilä (2017) requires expensive 82×82 or 176×176 eigenvalue computations and returns many spurious solutions (up to 152), making it impractical for real-time or robust estimation pipelines. This paper derives new explicit constraints and efficient solvers that reduce the problem to univariate polynomials or small eigenvalue problems.

## Input / Output

The method requires at least 4 coplanar point triplets across three images, from which two 2D homographies G₂ and G₃ (relative to a common reference view) are estimated via DLT. Cameras are assumed to have square pixels and centered principal points, so the only unknown intrinsic is the focal length. The output is one or two focal length values (depending on the camera configuration case) plus the relative poses of all three cameras, obtained by decomposing the Euclidean homography and registering the third camera via P3P.

## Method

The core insight is that both Euclidean homographies H₂ and H₃ encode the same plane normal n, yielding the constraint [n]×H_j^T H_j [n]×^T ∝ [n]×[n]×^T. Substituting the polynomial expressions H_j ∝ K_j^{-1}G_jK₁ produces 12 equations in the normal vector components, scale factors, and focal lengths. Applying the elimination ideal technique (via Macaulay2) removes the normal and scale unknowns, leaving 7 polynomial constraints purely in the focal length parameters. For one unknown focal length (Cases I–II), a single univariate polynomial of degree 9 or 6 is solved via Sturm sequences; for two unknowns (Cases III–IV), the hidden variable technique converts the bivariate system into a polynomial eigenvalue problem on an 18×18 or 12×12 matrix. Robust estimation wraps the minimal solver in LO-RANSAC, scoring hypotheses with pairwise Sampson error after P3P-based registration of the third camera, followed by Levenberg-Marquardt refinement.

## Results

On the proposed Planar Scenes Dataset (1870 images, 14 cameras, 6 scenes), the H_fff+P3P solver achieves a median relative focal length error (ξ_f) of 0.0439 and mAA_f(0.1) of 51.49 in Case I (three equal unknown focal lengths), outperforming the closest triplet-based baseline fEf+P3P (ξ_f = 0.0503, mAA_f(0.1) = 47.83). In Case II (one known, two equal unknown focal lengths), H_ff+P3P achieves ξ_f = 0.0611 and mAA_f(0.1) = 43.11. Two-view solvers (fEf, fEf+PP, Ef) fail catastrophically on planar scenes with median ξ_f > 0.46, while the Heikkilä [23] solver is ~20× slower (1102 ms vs. 49 ms) with slightly worse accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed H_fff+P3P (Case I) and H_ff+P3P (Case II) solvers consistently outperform all baselines on the planar scenes dataset. Against the closest triplet-based competitor fEf+P3P [43], the proposed methods achieve ~13% lower median ξ_f in Case I (0.0439 vs. 0.0503) and ~13% lower in Case II (0.0611 vs. 0.0692), with correspondingly higher mAA_f(0.1) scores. The Heikkilä [23] solver, while using the same 4-triplet input, is ~22× slower (1102 ms vs. 49 ms) and slightly less accurate (ξ_f = 0.0480 vs. 0.0439), attributed to worse numerical stability from its 82×82 eigenvalue formulation. Two-view solvers fEf [27], fEf+PP [42], and Ef [5] fail dramatically on planar scenes (median ξ_f > 0.46), confirming that two-view geometry alone cannot recover focal lengths from homographies. The fEf+PP solver [42] (4+1 plane+parallax within DEGENSAC) performs best among two-view methods but still lags far behind all triplet-based approaches, highlighting the fundamental advantage of leveraging three-view consistency for focal length recovery.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Heikkilä's 2017 formulation of focal length recovery from three-view homographies, which derived constraints from the shared plane normal vector but required solving high-degree polynomial systems (82×82 and 176×176 eigenvalue problems). The key departure is a new elimination-ideal-based derivation that yields only seven degree-6 polynomials in the homography entries, enabling reduction to a degree-9 univariate (Case I) or 18×18 eigenvalue problem (Case III) via Sturm sequences and the hidden variable technique. The robust estimation pipeline also draws on the LO-RANSAC framework and P3P solver, positioning the work within the broader minimal-solver tradition (5-point, 6-point, 7-point algorithms) for partially calibrated cameras.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first extensive benchmark of focal length self-calibration from planar three-view scenes, introducing a public dataset of 1870 images across 14 cameras and 6 scenes. The ~20× speedup in the full RANSAC pipeline (and ~80× for the raw solver) makes three-view focal length recovery practical for real-time applications, whereas prior solvers were too slow. Key limitations remain: pure translation is degenerate for Cases I and III, and Cases III/IV cannot distinguish focal lengths without off-plane points during scoring. Open questions include whether the planar-scene assumption can be relaxed and whether the elimination-ideal constraints generalize to other partially calibrated multi-view problems.
