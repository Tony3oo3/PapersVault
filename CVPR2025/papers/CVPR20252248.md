## Problem

Detecting mistakes in egocentric videos of skilled human activities has traditionally required either fully supervised mistake labels (which are domain-specific and expensive to collect) or costly temporal action annotations. This paper asks whether gaze signals alone can serve as an unsupervised indicator of mistakes, exploiting the observation that eye-movement patterns deviate from expected attention sequences when a user makes an error during hand-eye coordinated tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252248/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each timestep t, the method consumes the last F RGB frames of the egocentric video (stride 1, batch of 4 clips × 8 frames) together with a partial 2D gaze trajectory covering only the first F/2 frames, obtained from a dedicated gaze tracker on the wearable device. The model outputs a predicted gaze heatmap for the remaining F/2 frames, from which a peak-finding operation recovers the predicted trajectory; a scoring function then compares this prediction against the ground-truth gaze from the tracker to produce a per-timestep mistake confidence score s_t (high score = mistake). The gaze input is encoded as a stack of 2D Gaussian heatmaps centered on fixation points, and the model is trained with a Kullback-Leibler divergence loss on the predicted heatmaps.

## Method

The core contribution is a novel 'gaze completion' task in which a model predicts the continuation of a partial gaze trajectory conditioned on both the video and the observed initial trajectory, reducing the inherent uncertainty of free-form gaze prediction. The architecture is an encoder-decoder transformer (built on GLC) that fuses visual tokens with a single gaze trajectory token via two complementary strategies: channel fusion, which appends gaze heatmaps as an extra input channel, and a dedicated Gaze-Frame Correlation module that computes cross-attention between the gaze token and each local visual token at an intermediate level, explicitly modeling gaze-visual correlations. Mistake detection is then performed by scoring the discrepancy between the predicted and observed gaze trajectories using a heatmap-likelihood function that evaluates the probability of each ground-truth fixation under the predicted heatmap. The approach operates without any mistake labels: in one-class mode it is trained only on verified-correct executions, and in fully unsupervised mode it is trained on a natural mix of correct and mistaken clips, with the gaze signal providing the implicit supervisory structure.

## Results

On EPIC-Tent, the proposed gaze completion method achieves an AUC of 0.69 and F1 of 0.51 in the unsupervised setting, matching the AUC of the best fully supervised method (C2F, 0.72) when late-fused with MoCoDAD(H) (AUC 0.72, F1 0.54) without using any mistake labels. On HoloAssist, the method reaches AUC 0.59 and F1 0.18 (unsupervised), yielding +9% AUC and +80% F1 relative gains over the GLC gaze-prediction baseline. On IndustReal, one-class results show AUC 0.63 and F1 0.24, with +5% AUC and +14% F1 improvements over GLC. The method is ranked first on the HoloAssist Mistake Detection challenge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed gaze completion method outperforms all five baselines across all three datasets and supervision levels. Against the fully supervised C2F [35] (AUC 0.72, F1 0.58 on EPIC-Tent), the unsupervised Ours+MoCoDAD(H)* matches the AUC (0.72) and approaches the F1 (0.54 vs 0.58) without any mistake labels. Compared to the gaze-prediction baseline GLC [19], the method consistently wins: on EPIC-Tent it gains +8% AUC (0.69 vs 0.61) and +16% F1 (0.51 vs 0.44) in the unsupervised setting; on HoloAssist it achieves +9% AUC and +80% F1; on IndustReal it gains +5% AUC and +14% F1 in one-class. Against the skeleton-based anomaly detectors TrajREC [39] and MoCoDAD [8], the proposed method substantially outperforms their gaze-only (G) and hand-only (H) variants in F1 and AUC, though late fusion with MoCoDAD(H) provides additional gains on EPIC-Tent and HoloAssist (e.g., AUC 0.72 vs 0.69), indicating complementary signals from hand keypoints and gaze. TimeSformer [2] as a fully supervised classifier is outperformed by the unsupervised proposed method in AUC (0.69 vs 0.67) on EPIC-Tent, demonstrating that gaze-based unsupervised detection can rival label-dependent approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the GLC transformer architecture (Lai et al., BMVC 2022) for egocentric gaze estimation, extending it with a novel 'gaze completion' task that conditions prediction on a partial observed trajectory rather than predicting gaze from video alone. It departs from prior supervised mistake detection (C2F, TimeSformer) and one-class video anomaly detection (TrajREC, MoCoDAD) by requiring no mistake labels at all, instead using gaze prediction error as an unsupervised signal for behavioral deviation. The Gaze-Frame Correlation module introduces an explicit attention mechanism between a single gaze token and all visual tokens, going beyond the global-local fusion of GLC by operating at both encoder and mid-level stages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is removing the need for expensive, domain-specific mistake annotations, making mistake detection potentially deployable in new AR-assistant scenarios without expert labeling. However, the approach still requires a dedicated gaze tracker, limiting its applicability to devices with eye-tracking hardware, and absolute performance remains modest (F1 ~0.52 on EPIC-Tent, ~0.22 on HoloAssist). Open questions include whether the method generalizes to activities with less hand-eye coordination, whether the negative correlation with user confidence (more effective for novices) limits its utility for expert users, and whether the gaze completion formulation can be extended to longer temporal contexts or multi-task settings.
