## Problem

State-of-the-art vision-language models excel at environmental perception but generate inaccurate or impractical responses in embodied tasks because they lack understanding of a robot's physical reachability. Two key challenges remain: (1) robots vary widely in size, joint types, and kinematic structure, making it difficult for a single model to learn robot-specific reachability, and (2) introducing reachability as a new modality risks degrading the model's general vision-language capabilities.

## Input / Output

The model consumes three inputs: an egocentric RGB image, a text instruction, and an S-P Map — a 2D image derived from the robot's DH parameters, joint limits, extrinsic calibration, and egocentric depth map, where physically unreachable regions are rendered as a gray mask with outlined boundaries over the original depth. The S-P Map is robot-agnostic by design, encoding only which spatial regions are reachable rather than which specific robot is present. The output is a free-form text response (e.g., a yes/no reachability judgment, a spatial reasoning answer, or a multi-step task plan) that is consistent with both the visual scene and the robot's physical constraints.

## Method

PhysVLM introduces a dual-branch encoder–decoder architecture: a vision branch (SigLip-400M → MaxPool → 2-layer MLP) processes the RGB image, while a separate physical-reachability branch (SigLip-400M → MaxPool → fusion layer → 2-layer MLP) processes the S-P Map; both produce token sequences that are concatenated with language tokens and fed to a Qwen-2.5 Instruct-3B decoder. The S-P Map is generated by sampling joint configurations within each joint's motion range, computing end-effector positions via forward kinematics (Denavit-Hartenberg transformations), discretizing the reachable workspace into a voxel grid, intersecting it with the camera point cloud transformed into the robot frame, and projecting the valid points back to the image plane to mask unreachable regions. Training follows a two-stage supervised procedure: stage 1 aligns multimodal features by training only the projection layers on LLaVA-Pretrain and OpenX-Embodiment data, and stage 2 unfreezes all parameters and fine-tunes on Phys100K (100K multi-robot samples), ShareGPT4V, and RoboVQA. The independent reachability encoder (rather than sharing weights with the vision encoder) is critical, as ablations show that weight sharing degrades both reachability reasoning and general visual QA performance.

## Results

On the EQA-phys benchmark (the paper's primary evaluation), PhysVLM-3B achieves an overall score of 71.0%, outperforming GPT-4o (57.0%) by 14 percentage points and Claude-3.5 (56.4%) by 14.6 points. On RoboVQA-val, PhysVLM-3B attains BLEU-4 of 43.5, surpassing RoboMamba (36.3) by 7.2 points. On OpenEQA, PhysVLM-3B scores 57.4 overall, outperforming all embodied VLMs (SpatialBot 49.1, SpatialVLM 43.8) and GPT-4V (55.3), though trailing GPT-4o (66.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhysVLM-3B decisively outperforms all embodied VLM baselines: it beats SpatialVLM by 16.9 points on EQA-phys (71.0 vs 54.1), by 42.3 BLEU-4 on RoboVQA-val (43.5 vs 1.2), and by 13.6 points on OpenEQA (57.4 vs 43.8); it similarly dominates SpatialBot (71.0 vs 51.1 on EQA-phys, 43.5 vs 7.2 BLEU-4 on RoboVQA-val). Against API-based VLMs, PhysVLM surpasses GPT-4o by 14 points on EQA-phys (71.0 vs 57.0) and Claude-3.5 by 14.6 points (71.0 vs 56.4), though GPT-4o still leads on OpenEQA (66.7 vs 57.4) and on task planning when all objects are in range (75.9 vs 69.2). On the partial-reach task planning condition, PhysVLM (48.4) substantially outperforms GPT-4o (35.8), Claude-3.5 (32.1), and all embodied VLMs, demonstrating its core advantage in physical reachability reasoning. On RoboVQA-val, PhysVLM also exceeds 3D-VLA (43.5 vs 26.8 BLEU-4) and RoboMamba (43.5 vs 36.3 BLEU-4), confirming its superiority in embodied visual reasoning without sacrificing general VQA capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of embodied VLMs (RoboVQA, SpatialVLM, 3D-VLA) and robotic workspace analysis (reachability maps, occupancy grids, REMP), but departs from both lines by introducing a unified, robot-agnostic spatial representation (the S-P Map) that bridges the gap between kinematic constraint modeling and visual-language reasoning. Prior embodied VLMs assume all objects are within the robot's operational area, while prior reachability methods operate in purely geometric or planning contexts without integrating into multimodal reasoning. Architecturally, PhysVLM extends the standard dual-encoder VLM paradigm (ViT + LLM) by adding a third modality branch with its own feature encoder for the S-P Map, rather than repurposing or fine-tuning an existing embodied VLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The S-P Map offers a practical, plug-and-play mechanism for injecting physical reachability awareness into any VLM (demonstrated with a 7.1% gain on GPT-4o-mini without architectural changes), which could accelerate adoption in robotic task planning pipelines. The EQA-phys benchmark and Phys100K dataset fill a clear gap in evaluation infrastructure for reachability-aware reasoning. Key limitations include the domain gap between simulation and real-world zero-shot performance (63–64% vs. 71–78%), the restriction to six robot types, and the 3B model scale; open questions include whether the S-P Map generalizes to mobile manipulators, dexterous hands, or multi-robot settings, and how it integrates with vision-language-action (VLA) models for closed-loop control.
