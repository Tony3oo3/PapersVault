## Problem

Existing diffusion-based image editing methods impose a fixed or heuristic weight on the edit direction (the shift from source to target concept), but the appropriate magnitude varies per image depending on how strongly the source concept is already present. Over-editing overwhelms the image with the target concept while under-editing fails to remove the source, and searching for the right strength via trial-and-error is costly. The paper asks: can we estimate the actual presence of each concept in a given image to determine the correct edit magnitude automatically?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251698/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB image, its source caption, and a target editing prompt (e.g., 'replace cat with dog', 'add rusty', 'remove fresh') and produces a single edited image. It is zero-shot and plug-and-play, operating as a drop-in module within existing diffusion backbones (P2P, P2P-Zero, InfEdit, MasaCtrl) in either the CLIP text embedding space or the diffusion score space. A vision-language model parses the image-prompt tuple at inference to select task-relevant concepts, and the sparse decomposition step adds only ~0.15 s (0.4% of total editing time for P2P-Zero), making the overhead negligible relative to the diffusion sampling cost.

## Method

CoLan introduces a curated concept dataset (CoLan-150K: 5,078 visual concepts with 152,971 diverse textual stimuli) from which concept vectors are extracted via the diffusion model's text encoder and a RepRead procedure (arithmetic average or PCA over stimulus embeddings). At inference, a VLM identifies the task-relevant concepts for the given image-prompt pair, and their vectors form a dictionary matrix D. The source latent vector (text embedding or score prediction) is then decomposed via Elastic Net sparse coding (v = Dw* + r), yielding per-concept coefficients that quantify how much each concept is present. Concept transplant is performed by replacing the source concept's column in D with the target concept's column and recomputing v' = D'w* + r, which naturally sets the edit magnitude from the solved coefficient rather than a fixed weight. This scheme generalizes to concept addition (VLM suggests a counterpart source concept) and removal (target set to a null-concept vector), and the entire pipeline is backbone- and space-agnostic.

## Results

On PIE-Bench, CoLan-equipped backbones achieve state-of-the-art performance across both consistency preservation and edit effectiveness. The best configuration, InfEdit with CoLan in the score space (VI inversion), attains StruDist of 13.97×10⁻³, PSNR of 23.42, LPIPS of 110.3×10⁻³, SSIM of 75.51%, Target Image of 24.94%, and Target Concept of 22.45%. On the P2P-Zero backbone, CoLan reduces StruDist by ~70% (53.04→15.91) and LPIPS by ~56% (273.8→120.3) compared to VecAdd, while improving PSNR by ~31% (17.65→23.08) and SSIM by ~23% (61.78→75.82). Sparse decomposition adds only 0.15 s (0.4%) to P2P-Zero's 38.7 s editing time, confirming the plug-and-play efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against P2P-Zero (VecAdd) [40], CoLan on the same backbone dramatically improves consistency: StruDrop drops from 53.04 to 15.91 (×10⁻³) and LPIPS from 273.8 to 120.3 (×10⁻³), while PSNR rises from 17.65 to 23.08 and SSIM from 61.78% to 75.82%, with only a marginal 0.7-point drop in Target Image score. Against InfEdit (VecAdd) [65], CoLan in the score space (InfEdit(S)) wins on every consistency metric (StruDist 13.97 vs 27.18, LPIPS 110.3 vs 136.6, PSNR 23.42 vs 21.83, SSIM 75.51% vs 71.70%) and also edges out on edit effectiveness (Target Concept 22.45% vs 22.04%). Against P2P [11] with DI inversion (no representation manipulation), CoLan-equipped InfEdit(S) achieves comparable StruDist (13.97 vs 11.02) but substantially better LPIPS (110.3 vs 114.1) and higher edit effectiveness (Target Concept 22.45% vs 22.07%). Against MasaCtrl [4], CoLan-equipped methods dominate on all four consistency metrics and both edit effectiveness scores, confirming that sparse concept decomposition outperforms attention-map swapping for representation-level editing.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoLan extends the representation-manipulation paradigm established by P2P-Zero (VecAdd in text embedding space) and InfEdit (score-space steering), both of which impose a fixed, user-chosen weight w along an edit direction. The key departure is replacing this arbitrary scalar with coefficients estimated via Elastic Net sparse decomposition of the source latent vector against a curated concept dictionary, drawing on the representation-engineering (RepRead) literature from LLMs and the compositional-structure observations in diffusion latent spaces. The paper also introduces CoLan-150K, a concept-stimulus dataset that scales up prior small concept collections (fewer than 50 vectors in earlier works) to over 5,000 concepts with diverse multi-word phrases and contextual stimuli.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design—applicable across multiple inversion strategies (DDIM, DI, VI) and backbones (P2P, P2P-Zero, InfEdit, MasaCtrl) in both text-embedding and score spaces—makes the sparse-decomposition approach broadly adoptable without retraining. The ~50% reduction in structural distance and LPIPS on P2P-Zero demonstrates that image-specific edit magnitude estimation is a meaningful and previously under-addressed factor in diffusion editing quality. Open questions include whether the linear-sparse decomposition assumption holds for highly compositional or multi-object edits, how the method behaves when the VLM misidentifies relevant concepts, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D editing where the latent geometry is less well understood.
