## Problem

Existing driving scene generation methods capture at most two of the four essential modalities (ego-action, map, agent, image), limiting their ability to produce comprehensive, controllable scenes for closed-loop evaluation of autonomous driving systems. Methods that generate ego-action and agent motion (e.g., GUMP, TrafficGen) lack dynamic map progression, while video-based approaches (e.g., GAIA-1, DriveDreamer) cannot forecast agent behaviors, precluding user-specific scene generation.

## Input / Output

The model takes T past multimodal scene frames as input, where each frame contains ego-action (x/y displacement and angular change), a raster traffic map, N_a agent states (position, size, velocity, heading, category), and a camera image. Optionally, user-specified ego-vehicle actions or agent velocities can override the model's own predictions to steer generation. Output is the next scene's four modalities, generated autoregressively frame-by-frame up to 60-second sequences, with tokens arranged in a fixed causal order (ego-action → map → agent → image) reflecting the physical dependency chain.

## Method

UMGen decomposes scene generation into two autoregressive stages to handle the high token count from four modalities: a Temporal AutoRegressive (TAR) module applies causal self-attention only to tokens at the same position across frames, reducing temporal complexity from O((TN)²) to O(T²), followed by an Ordered AutoRegressive (OAR) module that performs GPT-like sequential decoding within each frame in the fixed modality order to enforce cross-modal consistency. An Action-aware Map Alignment (AMA) module applies an affine transformation (rotation + translation) parameterized by the predicted ego-action to prior map features, preserving spatial coherence between ego-motion and the evolving map. Ego-action is predicted first via two cross-attention blocks (over historical ego actions, then over the current environment state) and serves as a conditioning prior for all subsequent modality generation. Map and image modalities are tokenized with pretrained VQ-GAN codebooks while ego-action and agent attributes use discretization; the model is trained with combined cross-entropy losses on both TAR and OAR token predictions.

## Results

On the Waymo Open Motion Dataset, UMGen achieves MMD scores of 0.0730 (Position), 0.0550 (Heading), 0.0533 (Size), and 0.1502 (Velocity), substantially outperforming the best baseline UniGen (0.1208, 0.1104, 0.0815, 0.1591) across all four agent-attribute metrics. On nuPlan, UMGen similarly leads with MMD values of 0.0828, 0.0682, 0.0674, and 0.0760 versus TrafficGen's 0.0926, 0.0799, 0.0856, and 0.0988. The ablation study further shows that removing the OAR module (UMGen-T) increases the average agent collision rate from 5.1% to 5.6% and degrades MMD scores, confirming the importance of intra-frame autoregressive decoding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UMGen outperforms all four baselines on every MMD metric on the Waymo Open Motion Dataset. The closest competitor, UniGen, is beaten by a wide margin on Position (0.0730 vs. 0.1208, ~40% lower MMD), Heading (0.0550 vs. 0.1104, ~50% lower), and Size (0.0533 vs. 0.0815, ~35% lower), with a smaller but still clear advantage on Velocity (0.1502 vs. 0.1591). TrafficGen, LCTGen, and SceneGen trail even further behind, with Position MMDs ranging from 0.1319 to 0.1451. On nuPlan, UMGen also dominates TrafficGen (reproduced under identical settings) across all four attributes, with the largest gap on Velocity (0.0760 vs. 0.0988, ~23% lower). Notably, UMGen is the only method that generates all four modalities (ego-action, map, agent, image) simultaneously, whereas the baselines produce only a subset, making the MMD comparison on agent attributes a conservative measure of UMGen's overall advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

UMGen extends the autoregressive scene-generation paradigm established by GAIA-1 and GUMP, which each handle at most two to three modalities (e.g., ego-action + image, or ego-action + agent on a static map), by unifying all four modalities—ego-action, map, agent, and image—within a single tokenized sequence. Architecturally, it repurposes the GPT-style next-token prediction and VQ-GAN tokenization from NLP and image synthesis, but introduces a two-stage TAR+OAR decomposition to avoid the O((TN)²) cost of vanilla AR over concatenated multi-frame tokens. The AMA module is a novel geometric prior that has no direct analogue in prior driving-generation work, drawing instead on the affine-warping intuition from optical-flow and feature-alignment literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By generating all four modalities in a single pass, UMGen provides a practical path toward closed-loop, user-controllable AD simulation that prior two-modality systems (GUMP, TrafficGen) or image-only systems (GAIA-1, DriveDreamer) cannot offer, since agent and map dynamics are essential for testing planning and perception stacks. The paper acknowledges that image fidelity remains a bottleneck and suggests coupling the generated multimodal tokens as conditions for a diffusion model as a natural next step. Open questions include whether the fixed modality ordering in OAR generalizes to additional modalities (e.g., LiDAR, HD-map semantics), how the approach scales to multi-camera or multi-ego settings, and whether the 60-second generation horizon is sufficient for long-tail scenario testing.
