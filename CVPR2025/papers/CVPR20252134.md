## Problem

Directly combining active learning (AL) and semi-supervised learning (SSL) has not yielded the expected performance gains because existing SSAL methods design AL strategies tailored to a specific SSL algorithm rather than genuinely integrating both fields. The paper identifies two fundamental mismatches: diversity-based AL relies on clustering assumptions that fail when SSL provides only 1–2 labeled samples per class, and uncertainty-based AL requires strong-augmentation views whose large feature variability makes reliable uncertainty estimation difficult.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252134/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small labeled set (1–2 samples per class, e.g., 100 or 200 total for CIFAR-100) and a large unlabeled pool of RGB images, and in each AL round produces both a trained classifier (ViT backbone) and a subset of unlabeled samples selected for annotation. Sample selection is performed under weak augmentations rather than the strong augmentations that the theoretical analysis would ideally require, which is precisely the gap the feature re-alignment module is designed to close. The framework is agnostic to the specific SSL algorithm (FixMatch, FreeMatch) and AL strategy (CoreSet, AlphaMix, ActiveFT, Noise) plugged in.

## Method

The paper first decomposes the SSL risk upper bound into a pseudo error (linked to diversity-based selection) and a margin error (linked to uncertainty-based selection), providing a theoretical bridge between AL objectives and the pseudo-label SSL framework. To address the two practical mismatches, it proposes a feature re-alignment module with two auxiliary losses added to the standard SSL training: (1) a similarity loss L_s that maintains feature queues for strong and weak augmentations of labeled data, finds each unlabeled sample's nearest labeled neighbor under strong augmentation, and then pulls their weak-augmented features together to improve class clustering for representativeness-based selection; and (2) a contrastive loss L_c (inspired by Barlow Twins) that constrains per-dimension feature agreement between strong and weak views of the same sample, so that uncertainty computed under weak augmentation reliably approximates the margin error that would be measured under strong augmentation. The module is plug-and-play: it can be combined with any strong-weak SSL algorithm and any AL sampling strategy without modifying either.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with 100 initial labeled samples, FixMatch + Feature Alignment (FA) achieves 46.48% average accuracy across four AL strategies, improving over FixMatch alone (45.27%) and supervised learning (37.77%) by +1.21% and +8.71% respectively. On Tiny-ImageNet with 400 labeled samples, FreeMatch + FA reaches 52.79% average accuracy, surpassing FreeMatch (52.06%) and supervised (42.63%) by +0.73% and +10.16%. The FA module consistently improves all AL strategies, with the largest gains observed for ActFT (+1.97% on CIFAR-100 #100) and Noise (+2.29% on CIFAR-100 #100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its Feature Alignment (FA) module against six baselines: two SSL methods (FixMatch, FreeMatch) and four AL strategies (CoreSet, AlphaMix, ActFT, Noise). Adding FA consistently improves every SSL+AL combination: FixMatch+FA outperforms FixMatch by +1.21% (CIFAR-100 #100), +1.95% (CIFAR-100 #200), +1.53% (Tiny-ImageNet #200), and +0.72% (Tiny-ImageNet #400) in average accuracy. FreeMatch+FA improves over FreeMatch by +0.73% on Tiny-ImageNet #400. Among AL strategies, ActFT benefits most from FA (+1.97% on CIFAR-100 #100, from 49.61% to 51.58%), while Noise also sees a notable gain (+2.29%). A critical finding is that without FA, some AL methods (AlphaMix at 51.43%, Noise at 52.28%) actually underperform random selection (52.71%) under FreeMatch on CIFAR-100 #100, but with FA all AL strategies surpass random, confirming that the module resolves the integration gap between AL and SSL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the pseudo-label-based SSL paradigm (FixMatch, FreeMatch) and standard AL selection strategies (CoreSet, AlphaMix, ActiveFT, Noise), but departs from prior SSAL work (Consit, IDEAL, ASCENT) that designed AL criteria tailored to a specific SSL algorithm. Its theoretical contribution reframes the SSL risk decomposition into pseudo error and margin error, mapping them onto diversity-based and uncertainty-based AL objectives respectively, providing a principled bridge that prior SSAL methods lacked. The feature re-alignment module draws on unsupervised contrastive learning insights (Wang & Isola) for the similarity loss and Barlow Twins-style invariant feature constraints for the contrastive loss, repurposing these ideas to address the specific clustering and augmentation-variability gaps in SSAL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of the feature re-alignment module is practically useful: it allows practitioners to combine their preferred SOTA SSL and AL algorithms without redesigning either, addressing a real integration gap. However, the experimental scope is narrow—only one AL iteration, two small datasets (CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet), a single ViT backbone, and four AL methods—leaving open whether the gains persist over multiple rounds, at ImageNet scale, or with CNN backbones. A key open question is whether the theoretical risk decomposition generalizes beyond the pseudo-label SSL family to other SSL paradigms (e.g., consistency-only or contrastive SSL), and whether the module's benefits compound over many AL rounds or diminish as labeled data grows.
