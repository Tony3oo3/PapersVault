## Problem

Existing GUI agent datasets suffer from two critical gaps: they ignore developer-driven structural variations across apps, preventing knowledge transfer to unfamiliar software, and they focus almost exclusively on basic navigation/automation tasks, failing to capture the full spectrum of user interactions. This limits both cross-app generalization and cross-task versatility of GUI agents.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250777/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-recorded exploration video (~20 minutes, covering ~200 pages per app) alongside a task query, and produces a five-way multiple-choice answer. The video is unstructured and task-agnostic, capturing all interactive pages and transitions during exploration. The five downstream tasks span two levels of granularity: Environment Understanding (Application Overview, Page Analysis, Application Usage) and Operational Behavior Understanding (Action Recall, Action Sequence Verification), all standardized as multiple-choice questions for unified evaluation.

## Method

Xplore-Agent is a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 (Action-aware GUI Modeling) detects action boundaries via luminance difference (Y-Diff) in YUV color space to extract keyframes (reducing ~629 frames to ~116 per app), then generates simplified View Hierarchies using a QwenVL-7B model fine-tuned with Pix2Struct's pre-training objective, and produces action sequences from adjacent keyframe pairs. Stage 2 (Graph-Guided Environment Reasoning) employs an LVLM-based GUI clustering module that sequentially processes keyframes to group functionally similar screens into nodes, constructing a GUI Transition Graph where edges encode action transitions between screen clusters. This graph's node and edge lists are then composed into a prompt for GPT to perform the five downstream Q&A tasks, enabling reasoning over both local interactions and global app structure.

## Results

On the Cross-App Task Automation test set (500 operations, 20 unseen apps), Xplore-Agent achieves 30.73% Element Accuracy, 84.63% Operation Accuracy, and 30.39% Step Success Rate, surpassing the best prior GUI agent (CogAgent: 17.18%, 73.54%, 15.80%) by approximately 10–15 percentage points across all three metrics. On the Cross-Task benchmark spanning five hierarchical downstream tasks, Xplore-Agent attains a 64.24% average accuracy, outperforming GPT-4 (59.39%), VideoTree (57.97%), and VideoLLaMA2 (53.02%). The largest gains appear on Action Sequence Verification (36.54% vs. 28.85% for GPT-4), while the smallest gap is on Application Overview (99.25% vs. 96.88%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Xplore-Agent outperforms all eight baselines on both evaluation axes. In the Cross-App experiment, it surpasses CogAgent (the strongest prior GUI agent) by 13.55 pp on Element Accuracy and 14.59 pp on StepSR, and dominates GPT-4 (5.06%), AUTO-UI (7.40%), and SeeClick (6.64%) by wide margins on Element Accuracy. In the Cross-Task benchmark, Xplore-Agent leads GPT-4 by 4.85 pp average, VideoTree by 6.27 pp, and open-source video models (CogVLM2-Video, VideoChat2, VideoLLaMA2) by 11–22 pp. The most pronounced advantage is on Action Sequence Verification (36.54% vs. 28.85% for GPT-4), where the GUI Transition Graph provides structural context that flat video inputs lack. Notably, even the strongest baseline (GPT-4) scores below 25% on Action Recall, indicating that temporal operational reasoning remains a significant open challenge across all methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both major paradigms in GUI agent benchmarks: pre-trained frameworks (Mind2Web, AndroidInTheWild) that learn generalized GUI knowledge from fixed interaction data, and interactive environment frameworks (WebArena, LLaMATouch) that require real-time state feedback. Instead, it introduces an 'exploration-then-reasoning' paradigm where pre-recorded exploration videos serve as app-specific priors, drawing on DroidBot's automated exploration and Pix2Struct's screenshot-to-VH pretraining objective. The Xplore-Agent baseline extends DroidBot's rule-based GUI Transition Graph with LVLM-based page clustering (QwenVL-7B) and action-aware keyframe extraction, positioning itself as a lightweight alternative to purpose-built GUI agents like CogAgent and SeeClick that rely on extensive automation-specific pretraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset (312 apps, 115 hours of video, 32K QA pairs) and the exploration-video-as-prior framing offer a practical path toward cross-app generalization without requiring real-time interaction or per-app fine-tuning. The stark performance gap between Environment Understanding tasks (80–99%) and Operational Behavior Understanding tasks (6–36%) across all tested models reveals that current LVLMs can recognize individual GUI elements but remain weak at reasoning about temporal sequences and inter-page structural relationships—a key bottleneck for truly generalizable agents. However, the paper acknowledges that Xplore-Agent only outputs textual answers rather than executable actions, and the 10% cross-app improvement, while notable, is measured against baselines not designed for the exploration-video input format, leaving open whether the gap narrows under fairer comparison.
