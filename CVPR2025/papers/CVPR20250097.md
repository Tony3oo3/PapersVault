## Problem

Existing spike-based transformers (Spikformer, Spike-driven Transformer, SpikingReformer, QKFormer) compute self-attention only within each timestep, ignoring the temporal dependencies inherent in spike-based processing. The paper demonstrates through entropy analysis that spatial-only attention yields dispersed, less informative feature representations, while combining spatial and temporal correlations produces more focused patterns and higher accuracy. The challenge is to incorporate temporal attention without incurring the quadratic O(T²N²D) complexity of full spatial-temporal attention or excessive memory for storing cross-timestep information.

## Input / Output

The method accepts RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet, 64×64 for DVS) or DVS event streams, converted to binary spikes via direct coding. For static datasets, T=4 timesteps are used; for neuromorphic datasets, T=16; and for the sequential CIFAR task, images are split column-wise into 32 timesteps of 1-D inputs. The output is a single class label per input, with the model trained from scratch using surrogate gradients for backpropagation through LIF neurons.

## Method

STAtten partitions the temporal dimension into blocks of size B (B=2 for T=4, B=4 for T=16) and computes attention jointly over spatial tokens and timesteps within each block: STAtten(X[b]) = LIF(Q[b]K[b]ᵀV[b]·α). The key enabling insight is that binary Q, K, V eliminate the need for Softmax, allowing the computation to be reordered as Q(KᵀV) rather than (QKᵀ)V, which reduces intermediate memory from O(TND²) to O(D²) per block and enables a 1.6× larger batch size. This maintains the same O(TND²) asymptotic complexity as spatial-only attention while capturing local temporal correlations. The module is plug-and-play: it replaces the spatial-only attention in existing backbones (Spikformer, SDT, SpikingReformer, QKFormer) while preserving each architecture's residual connection strategy (Spike Element-Wise or Membrane-Shortcut), requiring no other structural modifications.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, STAtten integrated with QKFormer achieves 80.20% top-1 accuracy, and with Spike-driven Transformer achieves 79.85%, consistently outperforming their spatial-only baselines by 0.4–1.5%. On ImageNet at 288×288 resolution, STAtten+SDT-V2 reaches 80.67% top-1 accuracy with only 55.4M parameters, surpassing SpikingReformer (79.40%) and Spikformer (74.81%) while using ~20% fewer parameters. On the sequential CIFAR-100 task, STAtten+SDT achieves 64.30%, a 3.22% improvement over the spatial-only baseline, demonstrating strong temporal dependency capture. On neuromorphic datasets, STAtten+QKFormer achieves state-of-the-art 83.90% on CIFAR10-DVS and 84.25% on N-Caltech101.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STAtten consistently outperforms its spatial-only backbone across all tested architectures: +0.37%/+0.79% over Spikformer, +0.43%/+1.45% over Spike-driven Transformer, +0.23%/+0.74% over SpikingReformer, and +0.23%/+0.41% over QKFormer on CIFAR-10/CIFAR-100 respectively. On ImageNet, STAtten+SDT-V2 (80.67% at 288×288) surpasses SpikingReformer (79.40%) and Spikformer (74.81%) while using ~20% fewer parameters than SpikingReformer, though it remains below QKFormer (84.22%) which uses a larger 6-block architecture. On the sequential CIFAR-100 task where temporal dependencies are critical, STAtten+SDT achieves 64.30% versus 61.08% for the spatial-only baseline, a 3.22% gain that far exceeds the ~1% improvement seen on static images. On neuromorphic datasets, STAtten+QKFormer sets new state-of-the-art at 83.90% (CIFAR10-DVS) and 84.25% (N-Caltech101), beating the previous best QKFormer by 1.0% and 0.67% respectively. Energy analysis (Table 6) confirms that STAtten introduces negligible overhead: STAtten+SDT consumes 12.36 mJ versus 12.42 mJ for the baseline, and STAtten+SDT-V2 uses 52.38 mJ versus 52.40 mJ, validating that spatial-temporal attention is achieved at the same O(TND²) complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the lineage of spike-based transformer architectures (Spikformer, Spike-driven Transformer, SpikingReformer, QKFormer) by identifying and addressing a shared design limitation: all prior works employ spatial-only attention that processes each timestep independently, ignoring the temporal dynamics inherent in spike-based computation. The key departure is the introduction of a block-wise spatial-temporal attention mechanism (STAtten) that captures local temporal correlations while maintaining the O(TND²) complexity of spatial-only approaches, enabled by the non-softmax, binary QKV formulation established by Spikformer. The entropy-based analysis providing theoretical motivation for why spatial-temporal attention yields more structured representations is a new analytical contribution not present in prior spike-based transformer work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that spatial-only attention is systematically suboptimal for spike-based transformers challenges a design assumption shared by all prior spike-based transformer architectures, and the plug-and-play nature of STAtten means existing implementations can be immediately improved without architectural redesign. The maintenance of energy efficiency (no additional overhead) is practically important for neuromorphic deployment. However, the paper acknowledges that hardware deployment on current neuromorphic chips (TrueNorth, Loihi) remains challenging due to the block-wise temporal processing requirement, pointing toward layer-by-layer neuromorphic architectures as a future implementation path. Open questions include whether larger block sizes or adaptive block partitioning could further improve performance, and whether the approach generalizes to other spike-based architectures beyond transformers.
