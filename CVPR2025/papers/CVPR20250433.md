## Problem

Existing CZSL research focuses almost exclusively on image classification, and the authors demonstrate that performance on image-based benchmarks correlates only weakly with performance on video-based compositional tasks, suggesting models may be over-fitting to a single modality rather than achieving true compositional generalization. Additionally, CZSL faces two compounding challenges: a domain shift between seen and unseen compositions, and significant visual/semantic variability of the same primitive across different compositional contexts (conditional variance).

## Input / Output

The method accepts either a video clip (T frames of 224×224×3) or a single RGB image, encoded frame-by-frame by a pretrained TSM-18 or ResNet visual encoder into per-frame embeddings of dimension n. A temporal compression module maps these to a unified time-invariant representation: for images the temporal-variation component is identically zero, so the architecture processes both modalities without structural changes. The output is a compositional label consisting of a condition primitive (verb or attribute) and an object primitive, scored via a combined inference formula that averages condition-first and object-first contextual scores with a small regularization term on the independent primitive scores.

## Method

The DHD framework runs two parallel decoding branches inspired by human observation strategies: a condition-first branch (CPD) that decodes the condition primitive before the object, and an object-first branch (OPD) that does the reverse. Each branch first applies temporal compression (average pooling for stable features plus a temporal convolution on frame-difference residuals for variation), then uses multi-head cross-attention with pretrained text embeddings as key-value to extract the target primitive. A Contextual Dependency Encoder feeds the first-pass decoded representation back into the opposite branch's decoder via an MLP with layer normalization and residual connection, producing second-pass contextual scores (s_{o|c} and s_{c|o}). To counteract conditional variance, a Copula-based orthogonal decoding loss applies rank transformation to both branch outputs, estimates the joint and marginal densities via Gaussian KDE, and minimizes the negative log copula density, effectively decoupling marginal primitive features from their nonlinear contextual coupling. The total loss combines independent primitive losses, contextual decoding losses, and the orthogonality term with balancing hyperparameters.

## Results

On the proposed C-EgoExo video benchmark, DHD achieves the best performance across all four metrics: 32.4% seen accuracy, 17.8% unseen accuracy, 18.3% harmonic mean, and 4.8% AUC, outperforming the strongest baseline C2C (30.8/16.8/17.3/4.4). On the image-based UT-Zappos dataset, DHD attains the highest unseen accuracy (71.8%) and AUC (36.4%), though its harmonic mean (48.1%) trails ADE (51.1%). On CGQA, DHD leads in unseen accuracy (29.6%), harmonic mean (25.3%), and AUC (9.5%), surpassing the previous best C2C by 6.0 points in U and 1.3 points in AUC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DHD achieves the best AUC on all three benchmarks, with the largest margin on CGQA (9.5 vs. C2C's 8.2, a 15.9% relative gain) and C-EgoExo (4.8 vs. C2C's 4.4, an 9.1% relative gain). On UT-Zappos, DHD leads in unseen accuracy (71.8% vs. CGE's 71.3%) and AUC (36.4% vs. CGE's 36.3%), but its harmonic mean (48.1%) is notably below ADE (51.1%) and CGE (49.7%), indicating a seen-class bias. On CGQA, DHD's unseen accuracy of 29.6% substantially exceeds C2C (23.6%) and OADis (19.8%), while its seen accuracy (38.1%) is slightly below C2C (39.4%) and OADis (38.3%). On the new C-EgoExo video benchmark, DHD dominates all four metrics, with the closest competitor C2C trailing by 1.6 points in S, 1.0 in U, 1.0 in HM, and 0.4 in AUC. The paper highlights that image-tuned methods like CGE drop sharply on video (AUC from 36.3 on UT-Zappos to 2.0 on C-EgoExo), whereas DHD maintains consistent performance across modalities, validating its dual-branch design for cross-modal compositional generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the de-composed CZSL paradigm (learning condition and object primitives independently) by introducing a dual-branch architecture inspired by two human cognitive observation modes: condition-first and object-first. It repurposes cross-attention decoding mechanisms similar to ADE [9] but restructures them into parallel branches with a temporal compression module that unifies image and video processing. The copula-entropy-based orthogonal decoding loss is a novel contribution that draws on probability integral transformation and kernel density estimation to enforce independence between primitive decoders, departing from the standard cross-entropy losses used in prior CZSL work. The C-EgoExo benchmark builds on the EgoExo-4D dataset and the Sth-com split protocol, extending CZSL evaluation from attribute-object image classification to verb-object video recognition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key empirical finding—that performance on image-based CZSL benchmarks shows only weak correlation with video-based performance—challenges the assumption that current methods capture general compositional reasoning rather than task-specific image classification heuristics. The C-EgoExo benchmark (387K clips, 10,112 compositions) provides a much larger and more diverse evaluation resource than existing image datasets, though absolute performance remains very low (best AUC ~4.8), indicating the video CZSL task is far from solved. A significant open question is whether the S:U imbalance on video (1.82 vs. 0.94 on UT-Zappos) reflects a fundamental difficulty in temporal compositional generalization or an artifact of the annotation granularity. The cross-domain transfer result (only 62.7% of in-domain performance) suggests that domain adaptation for compositional video understanding remains a major unsolved challenge.
