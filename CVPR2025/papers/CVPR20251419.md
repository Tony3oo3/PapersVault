## Problem

High-quality, large-scale underwater datasets with dense annotations (depth maps, semantic masks) remain scarce due to the complex underwater environment and prohibitive collection costs. Existing synthesis methods like Atlantis generate only a single annotation type (depth) and rely on terrestrial depth maps as conditions, which may not align with natural underwater scenes. No prior method can simultaneously generate realistic underwater images and multiple types of highly consistent dense annotations from text alone.

## Input / Output

The method takes only free-form text prompts (e.g., scene captions) as input and simultaneously produces a triple: a photorealistic underwater RGB image, a corresponding depth map, and a semantic segmentation mask, all mutually consistent. Training data consists of ~14K quadruples {image, depth, mask, caption} assembled from SUIM, UIIS, and USIS10K segmentation datasets, with depth maps obtained via Depth Anything and captions via BLIP-2. The resulting synthetic dataset SynTIDE contains ~50K samples (5K non-redundant captions × 10 generations each) used to fine-tune downstream dense prediction models.

## Method

TIDE builds on PixArt-α (a latent diffusion transformer) for text-to-image generation, augmented with two fine-tuned mini-transformers (initialized from the first 10 layers of PixArt-α) for text-to-depth and text-to-mask generation, all operating in parallel. The key alignment mechanism is Implicit Layout Sharing (ILS): the cross-attention map M_i = softmax(Q_i K_i^T/√c) computed in the text-to-image branch is directly substituted into the text-to-depth and text-to-mask cross-attention layers, replacing their own QK computations so all three branches share identical spatial layout. A second mechanism, Time Adaptive Normalization (TAN), enables cross-modal feature interaction by mapping features from other branches (depth↔mask, and both→image) through MLPs to modulation parameters (γ, β), then applying a time-adaptive confidence α (from timestep embeddings via sigmoid) to control the normalization strength: x* = α·γx + α·β + x. Training uses LoRA fine-tuning (ranks 32/64/64) with equal-weighted MSE denoising losses across all three branches, requiring only ~200K iterations on 4×NVIDIA 4090 GPUs.

## Results

On the D3 and D5 subsets of Sea-thru, fine-tuning NewCRFs with SynTIDE reduces SI_log from 37.10 to 22.37 (−14.73) and improves δ1 from 0.48 to 0.84 (+36%) compared to Atlantis. On MIM, SI_log drops from 37.01 to 22.49 (−14.52) and δ1 rises from 0.56 to 0.85 (+29%). For underwater semantic segmentation, pre-training with SynTIDE followed by fine-tuning on real data yields up to +5.2 mIoU on UIIS (Segformer: 70.2→75.4) and +2.1 mIoU on USIS10K (ViT-Adapter: 74.6→76.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole direct baseline is Atlantis [41] (CVPR 2024), the previous state-of-the-art underwater data synthesis method that uses terrestrial depth maps as ControlNet conditions. SynTIDE outperforms Atlantis across nearly all depth estimation metrics on both Sea-thru and SQUID for all four evaluated models (AdaBins, NewCRFs, PixelFormer, MIM). The most dramatic gains appear on Sea-thru D3/D5: SI_log improves by 11–15 points and δ1 by 7–36 percentage points depending on the model. On SQUID, improvements are more modest (SI_log −0.5 to −3.9) but still consistent. The one exception is NewCRFs on SQUID where SI_log slightly worsens (25.19→25.55, +0.36), though other metrics remain comparable. For semantic segmentation, the comparison is against real-data-only training rather than Atlantis; SynTIDE pre-training consistently adds 1.0–5.2 mIoU over real-data-only baselines across Segformer, Mask2former, and ViT-Adapter.

## Relation to Prior Work

TIDE extends the controllable data synthesis line (Atlantis, Diffumask, Freemask, SegGen) by unifying text-to-image and text-to-dense-annotation generation into a single parallel denoising framework, departing from prior methods that either require dense conditions (e.g., Atlantis uses terrestrial depth maps) or generate only a single annotation type. The Implicit Layout Sharing mechanism repurposes the cross-attention map—already known to control layout in text-to-image models—as a shared spatial prior across parallel branches, while Time Adaptive Normalization introduces a novel cross-modal modulation layer. Architecturally, the model is built on PixArt-α with LoRA fine-tuning, inheriting the latent diffusion paradigm of Stable Diffusion but adapting it for multi-branch, multi-annotation generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TIDE offers a practical pathway to alleviate dense-annotation scarcity in underwater scenes (and potentially other domains) by generating images, depth maps, and semantic masks from text alone, removing the need for expensive manual annotation or proxy conditions. The SynTIDE dataset and the demonstrated improvements (e.g., 14.73 SI_log reduction, 36% δ1 gain on Sea-thru) validate the approach for depth estimation and segmentation. However, the method cannot yet produce instance-level masks, and its generalization beyond underwater scenes remains untested; whether the ILS/TAN consistency mechanisms transfer to other multi-annotation domains is an open question.
