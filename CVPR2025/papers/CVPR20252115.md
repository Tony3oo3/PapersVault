## Problem

Existing video-to-music generation methods are limited to narrow scenarios (e.g., dance videos) or produce symbolic MIDI output, restricting musical diversity and generalization across genres. No large-scale, multi-genre dataset with a unified benchmark exists to systematically evaluate such systems. The paper asks whether high-fidelity, diverse music can be generated end-to-end from visual input alone for arbitrary video genres.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252115/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a variable-length video as input, sampling frames at 2 fps within 30-second segments for short-term modeling and 32 uniformly sampled frames across the full video for long-term modeling. It produces a 32 kHz monophonic music audio signal, generated autoregressively in 30-second sliding windows with 0.5-second overlap, where each window's music tokens are decoded by EnCodec into waveform output.

## Method

VidMuse employs a Long-Short-Term Visual Module (LSTV-Module) that processes video features from a pretrained visual encoder (CLIP by default) through two parallel branches: a short-term module capturing fine-grained local dependencies at the segment level and a long-term module modeling global video-level context. An LST-Fusion step applies cross-attention with short-term features as queries and long-term features as keys/values, enabling each local segment to attend to the full video context before music generation. A transformer-based Music Token Decoder then autoregressively predicts EnCodec tokens conditioned on the fused visual features via cross-attention, trained with standard cross-entropy next-token prediction loss. The pipeline is pretrained on 360K video-music pairs (V2M-360K) and fine-tuned on a 20K subset selected by audio-visual alignment ranking, with the key design insight being that local cues drive musical diversity while global cues ensure coherence across the full video.

## Results

On V2M-bench, VidMuse achieves the best scores across all six metrics: KL divergence 0.734, FAD 2.459, FD 29.946, density 1.250, coverage 0.730, and ImageBind alignment 0.202. The closest competitor, VM-NET (retrieval-based), trails VidMuse on every metric (KL 0.899, FAD 6.252, coverage 0.383, ImageBind 0.147). On cross-benchmark generalization (AIST++, LORIS, TikTok), VidMuse attains the highest F1 scores of 98.2, 95.9, and 87.7 respectively, and the best beats-hit score (BHS) of 97.3 on TikTok.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VidMuse outperforms all eight baselines on every metric in the main V2M-bench comparison. Against the MIDI-based methods Video2Music [33] and CMT [13], VidMuse shows dramatic gains in diversity (density 1.250 vs. 0.103 and 0.080; coverage 0.730 vs. 0.023 and 0.070), confirming the advantage of directly generating audio signals over predicting symbolic notes. Against the strongest generative baseline M²UGen [32] (and its retrained variant M²UGen*), VidMuse reduces FAD from 5.10 to 2.46 and improves coverage from 0.43 to 0.73, while also achieving a higher ImageBind score (0.202 vs. 0.181), indicating better video-music semantic alignment. Against the retrieval-based VM-NET [29], VidMuse surpasses it on all six metrics, with the largest gap in coverage (0.730 vs. 0.383) and ImageBind (0.202 vs. 0.147), showing that learned generation produces more diverse and semantically aligned music than database retrieval. On the cross-benchmark generalization tests (AIST++, LORIS, TikTok), VidMuse achieves the best F1 on AIST++ (98.2 vs. 91.5 for V2Meow [65]) and LORIS (95.9 vs. 94.5 for LORIS [80]), and the best BHS on TikTok (97.3 vs. 86.3 for CDCD [88]), though its BCS on TikTok (79.8) is lower than D2M-GAN [87] (87.1) and CDCD [88] (91.8), suggesting some trade-off in beat-coverage for non-dance content.

## Relation to Prior Work

VidMuse carves new ground by repurposing the autoregressive next-token prediction paradigm from NLP and the neural audio codec (EnCodec) from speech compression into a unified video-to-music pipeline, departing from prior MIDI-based approaches (CMT, Video2Music) and LLM-mediated multi-modal systems (M2UGen, V2Meow) that rely on symbolic intermediates or text bridges. The Long-Short-Term Visual Module draws on cross-attention mechanisms from video transformers and the SlowFast architecture, but restructures them into a segment-level/local and video-level/global hierarchy specifically designed to condition music generation. The paper also introduces the V2M dataset (360K pairs), which extends the limited-genre, retrieval-oriented HIMV-200K and dance-specific benchmarks into a broad, signal-level training resource.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The V2M dataset and V2M-bench provide the field with the first large-scale, multi-genre resource for video-to-music generation, addressing a gap where prior datasets were restricted to dance videos, MIDI output, or retrieval tasks. The finding that a simple autoregressive signal-level model outperforms LLM-based multi-modal pipelines (M2UGen) suggests that direct video-to-audio modeling may be more effective than routing through language for this task. Key limitations remain: reliance on EnCodec introduces reconstruction artifacts, the system lacks text or style conditioning, and generalization to very long or structurally complex videos is untested. Open questions include whether better codecs (e.g., HiFi-Codec) would close the quality gap, and how to incorporate user-controllable parameters (tempo, mood, genre) without sacrificing the end-to-end simplicity.
