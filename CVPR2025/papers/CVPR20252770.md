## Problem

Transferring image-level CLIP to video-text retrieval introduces three key discrepancies—vision (temporal dimension), language (caption granularity), and alignment (spatio-temporal complexity)—but existing parameter-efficient methods primarily address only the vision discrepancy through temporal modeling, leaving language and alignment gaps unexplored. No prior method simultaneously mitigates all three discrepancies while maintaining parameter efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252770/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip (uniformly sampled into F frames) and a text query (video caption) as input, producing a video-level similarity score for retrieval ranking. During training only, LLaVA-NeXT generates pseudo image captions for each sampled frame guided by the video caption; these are omitted at inference to maintain fair comparison and avoid MLLM overhead. The backbone is frozen CLIP (ViT-B/16 or ViT-B/32) with only ~0.56M trainable parameters (LoRA rank 8 + adapter dimension 8).

## Method

DiscoVLA introduces three complementary modules: (1) IVFusion, which extends Image-Level Attention with a cross-image CLS-token attention branch and merges the two feature streams via a lightweight adapter, reducing complexity from O((FN)²) to O(F(N)²+(F)²N) while capturing both spatial and spatio-temporal information; (2) PImgAlign, which uses LLaVA-NeXT to generate video-caption-guided pseudo image captions and computes fine-grained image-level alignment via max-similarity matching across all image-caption pairs; and (3) AlignDistill, which minimizes KL divergence between image-level and video-level similarity distributions to transfer pretrained image-level alignment knowledge to the video level. The overall training objective combines video-level contrastive loss, image-level contrastive loss (weighted by α=0.3), and KL distillation loss (weighted by β=1.0), with PImgAlign and AlignDistill contributing no additional inference parameters.

## Results

On MSRVTT with CLIP (ViT-B/16), DiscoVLA achieves 50.5% R@1 and 209.9% R@sum for text-to-video, and 49.2% R@1 and 209.9% R@sum for video-to-text, using only 0.56M trainable parameters. With CLIP (ViT-B/32), it reaches 47.0% R@1 (t2v) and 47.7% R@1 (v2t), outperforming the best prior parameter-efficient method (RAP) by 2.2% R@1 and 7.5% R@sum. The method also achieves state-of-the-art results on LSMDC (23.6% R@1 t2v), ActivityNet (41.2% R@1 t2v), and DiDeMo (48.4% R@1 t2v), all with CLIP (ViT-B/32).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiscoVLA outperforms all nine compared baselines across all four benchmarks. On MSRVTT (ViT-B/16), the closest competitor is TempMe [44] (0.50M params), which DiscoVLA surpasses by 1.5% R@1 (50.5 vs 49.0) and 3.2% R@sum (209.9 vs 206.7) for t2v, and by 1.6% R@1 and 1.6% R@sum for v2t. Against DGL [58] (0.83M params), the gap widens to 2.2% R@1 and 9.2% R@sum (t2v). Full fine-tuning CLIP4Clip [37] (123.54M params) is exceeded by DiscoVLA on every metric while using over 200× fewer trainable parameters. Among parameter-efficient methods, VoP [22] (both F+P and F+C variants) and RAP [5] trail DiscoVLA by 2.8–3.4% R@1 (t2v) on ViT-B/16. On the smaller ViT-B/32 backbone, DiscoVLA leads RAP [5] by 2.2% R@1 and 5.1% R@sum (t2v), and outperforms VoP F+P [22] by 3.5% R@1. The method also dominates on LSMDC (23.6% vs 21.4% R@1 over DGL), ActivityNet (41.2% vs 40.8% over RAP), and DiDeMo (48.4% vs 46.4% over VoP F+C), confirming consistent gains across datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parameter-efficient adaptation of CLIP to video-text retrieval, building on the temporal modeling approaches of VoP, DGL, and MV-Adapter, which primarily address vision discrepancies through prompt tuning or shared adapters. It departs from this line by arguing that language and alignment discrepancies are equally critical and previously neglected, introducing pseudo image caption generation via LLaVA-NeXT and image-to-video alignment distillation as complementary mechanisms. The IVFusion module repurposes the LoRA and Adapter paradigms from NLP and image tasks into a unified spatio-temporal attention design that operates at both image and video granularity within a single encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The three-discrepancy framing (vision, language, alignment) provides a useful diagnostic lens for understanding why image-level CLIP transfers imperfectly to video, and the ablations confirm that addressing all three simultaneously yields gains that no single-discrepancy method captures. However, PImgAlign is training-only and depends on an external MLLM for pseudo caption generation, introducing a non-trivial computational and data-quality dependency that limits practical deployment. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer or untrimmed videos, whether the MLLM dependency can be replaced by lighter captioning models, and whether the alignment distillation signal remains informative as the video-level model matures.
