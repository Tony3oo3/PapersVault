## Problem

Applying radiance field methods like 3DGS to in-the-wild drone imagery faces two coupled challenges: dynamic distractors (e.g., moving vehicles) violate the static-scene assumption, and limited viewpoint coverage in a single drone flight causes overfitting and geometric artifacts in novel views. Existing distractor-removal methods rely on fixed thresholds or predefined semantic categories that fail in uncontrolled scenes, while sparse-view approaches either lack optimization constraints or underutilize geometric priors from multi-view stereo.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of posed drone images (poses from COLMAP/SfM) as input and produces a 3D Gaussian Splatting model that renders distractor-free novel views. Internally, DUSt3R predicts dense point clouds at 512 resolution for geometric initialization, and SAM 2 provides per-object segmentation masks for adaptive masking. The final mask at each training iteration is a union of per-frame local masks and a globally tracked set of distractor objects, applied to both L1 and D-SSIM losses during 7,000-iteration training on an A100 GPU.

## Method

DroneSplat introduces two key mechanisms. First, Adaptive Local-Global Masking computes object-wise average residuals (normalized D-SSIM + L1) over SAM 2 segments and sets a per-frame threshold as mean + variance of pixel residuals, with a relaxation factor that decays over training to accommodate early-stage convergence variance; objects exceeding a higher global threshold are tracked across frames via SAM 2's video segmentation to catch temporarily stationary distractors. Second, Voxel-guided Gaussian Splatting initializes Gaussians from DUSt3R dense points sampled by a score combining confidence and FPFH geometric features within adaptive voxels, then constrains subsequent Gaussian splitting/cloning to remain within voxel bounds (with exponential gradient decay beyond a τ·voxel-length limit) and prunes voxels with insufficient Gaussians or low average opacity, preventing the unconstrained optimization that degrades sparse-view reconstruction.

## Results

On the DroneSplat(dynamic) dataset, DroneSplat achieves the best PSNR across all six scenes (17.79–24.53 dB), outperforming the strongest baseline GS-W by 0.4–2.4 dB depending on scene. For limited-view reconstruction on DroneSplat(static), DroneSplat reaches 18.09 dB (Exhibition Hall) and 18.17 dB (Plaza) PSNR, surpassing the closest competitor InstantSplat (17.37 / 16.61 dB) by ~0.7–1.6 dB. On UrbanScene3D, DroneSplat attains 15.62 dB (PolyTech) and 20.47 dB (ArtSci) PSNR, edging out FSGS (14.82 / 20.32 dB) and significantly outperforming NeRF-based methods (FreeNeRF, DiffusioNeRF) by 3–9 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DroneSplat outperforms all 13 baselines on both distractor elimination and limited-view reconstruction tasks. On distractor elimination (DroneSplat dynamic + NeRF On-the-go), GS-W and WildGaussians produce visible artifacts and floaters because their uncertainty-based masking fails on small drone-scale distractors, while RobustNeRF, NeRF-HuGS, and NeRF On-the-go remove distractors but lose fine static details; DroneSplat achieves the highest PSNR in every scene (e.g., 24.53 vs. 23.29 for GS-W on Cultural Center; 22.60 vs. 22.27 for WildGaussians on Patio-High). On limited-view reconstruction, InstantSplat is the closest competitor but still trails DroneSplat by 0.7–1.6 dB PSNR (17.37 vs. 18.09 on Exhibition Hall), attributed to InstantSplat's lack of voxel-guided optimization that lets Gaussians drift from the DUSt3R geometric prior. FSGS and DNGaussian, which rely on depth priors with hand-crafted heuristics, underperform DroneSplat by 0.8–5.5 dB, while NeRF-based methods (FreeNeRF, DiffusioNeRF) are 3–9 dB behind due to their inability to recover accurate geometry under sparse views. The ablation variant Ours(COLMAP), which replaces DUSt3R initialization with COLMAP sparse points, performs nearly identically on dynamic scenes (within 0.1 dB) but drops more noticeably on static limited-view scenes, confirming that the voxel-guided 3DGS module is the key differentiator for geometry accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

DroneSplat builds directly on 3DGS as its rendering representation and on DUSt3R for dense geometric priors, but departs from InstantSplat by adding a voxel-guided optimization strategy that constrains Gaussian primitives to respect the stereo-predicted geometry rather than letting vanilla 3DGS optimization erode it. For distractor elimination, it moves beyond both segmentation-based methods (which require predefined object classes) and uncertainty-based methods (which struggle with small objects in drone imagery) by combining SAM 2's video tracking with an adaptive statistical threshold that adjusts per-frame and per-iteration. The paper positions itself at the intersection of two previously separate sub-problems—dynamic object removal and sparse-view reconstruction—unified under the specific constraints of in-the-wild drone capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DroneSplat dataset (24 sequences with graded dynamic difficulty) fills a gap in evaluation for drone-based reconstruction with realistic distractors, and the adaptive local-global masking offers a practical, class-agnostic alternative to hand-tuned thresholds that prior methods require per scene. However, the method's complexity (SAM 2 video tracking, DUSt3R inference, FPFH computation, and voxel bookkeeping) raises questions about scalability to very large urban scenes and real-time deployment. An open question is whether the voxel-guided optimization generalizes beyond the 6-view sparse setting tested here, and whether the adaptive thresholding strategy transfers to other capture modalities (e.g., handheld or satellite imagery) without re-tuning.
