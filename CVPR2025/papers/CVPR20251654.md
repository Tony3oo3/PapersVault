## Problem

Existing AVQA methods assume a static training environment and cannot adapt to new question types, audio-visual categories, or answer spaces over time. No benchmark exists for studying AVQA under continual learning, where models must sequentially acquire new multimodal knowledge without catastrophic forgetting of previously learned spatial-temporal reasoning and question semantics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251654/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes three modalities per sample: an audio spectrogram (encoded offline by a frozen VGGish), a fixed number of video frames (encoded by a frozen ResNet-18), and a natural-language question (encoded by an LSTM). The output is a single answer label drawn from an open-ended candidate set of 2,135 options (Split-AVQA) or 40 options (Split-MUSIC-AVQA). In the continual setting, tasks are organized hierarchically by question type (4 or 5 top-level tasks) and further subdivided into subtasks by object/sound category groups, with a small exemplar memory buffer (5,000 or 700 samples) from prior tasks available at each incremental step.

## Method

The proposed method combines three modules on top of frozen VGGish/ResNet-18/LSTM encoders. First, Question-guided Cross-modal Information Fusion (QCIF) uses the question embedding as a query in multi-head attention to selectively attend to spatial-temporal regions in visual features and temporal segments in audio features, then fuses the attended modalities via element-wise multiplication with the question vector. Second, Task-specific Knowledge Distillation with Spatial-Temporal Feature Constraints (TKD-STFC) minimizes KL divergence between old and new model outputs while additionally penalizing differences in feature deltas along temporal-audio, temporal-visual, and spatial-visual dimensions between the current and previous model, preserving spatial-temporal reasoning structure. Third, a Question Semantic Consistency Constraint (QSCC) enforces cosine-similarity alignment of question feature vectors between consecutive tasks to prevent semantic drift. The total loss is the sum of the TKD-STFC loss and a cross-entropy loss augmented by the QSCC term, with dataset-specific weighting coefficients.

## Results

On Split-AVQA, the proposed method achieves a Mean Accuracy (MA) of 32.05% and Average Forgetting (AF) of 2.47%, outperforming the strongest baseline SS-IL (MA 30.50%, AF 2.68%) by 1.55 and 0.21 points respectively. On Split-MUSIC-AVQA, it reaches MA 33.64% and AF 27.08%, improving over SS-IL (MA 32.16%, AF 28.05%) by 1.48 and 0.97 points. The Upper Bound (joint training on all data) sits at MA 55.42% (Split-AVQA) and 65.57% (Split-MUSIC-AVQA), indicating substantial headroom but also confirming the method's competitive gap-closing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines on both MA and AF across both datasets. On Split-AVQA, it beats the closest competitor SS-IL by 1.55 MA points (32.05 vs. 30.50) and 0.21 AF points (2.47 vs. 2.68), while AV-CIL trails by 4.56 MA and 0.44 AF. Parameter-regularization methods (LwF, EWC, MAS) and the replay-based iCaRL are substantially weaker, with EWC and Vanilla showing severe forgetting (AF > 30). On Split-MUSIC-AVQA, the margin over SS-IL narrows to 1.48 MA and 0.97 AF, and AV-CIL is 2.42 MA behind. Notably, MAS achieves the lowest AF among non-replay methods on Split-AVQA (8.91) but still lags the proposed method by 23.14 MA points, highlighting that regularization alone is insufficient for this multimodal continual setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: audio-visual question answering (AVQA) and continual learning. It directly builds on AV-CIL [30], which introduced task-specific knowledge distillation for audio-visual class-incremental learning, and on VQACL [43], which formulated visual question answering under a continual learning setting with evolving questions. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of question-guided cross-modal attention (QCIF) to address modality-specific distraction, combined with spatial-temporal feature constraints (STFC) to protect temporal reasoning knowledge — a combination not present in either prior line. The method repurposes standard continual learning baselines (LwF, EWC, MAS, iCaRL, SS-IL) within a unified multimodal architecture using frozen VGGish and ResNet-18 encoders with an LSTM question encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The AVQACL benchmark is the first to formalize continual learning specifically for audio-visual question answering, providing a structured evaluation protocol (Split-AVQA, Split-MUSIC-AVQA) that the field lacked. However, the large remaining gap to the upper bound (32.05 vs. 55.42 MA on Split-AVQA) indicates the problem is far from solved, and the authors explicitly acknowledge that large multimodal models (LMMs) have not been explored for this setting, leaving a significant open question about whether LLM-based approaches could close this gap. The finding that memory replay on Split-MUSIC-AVQA leads to overfitting rather than sustained gains also raises practical questions about the scalability of exemplar-based methods in long-video, multi-instrument scenarios.
