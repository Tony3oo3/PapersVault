## Problem

Streaming dynamic scene reconstruction via 3D Gaussian Splatting currently requires per-frame optimization, resulting in 10s+ per-frame latency and progressive error accumulation that degrades quality over longer sequences. No existing method achieves both fast per-frame reconstruction (under 3s) and stable quality across extended video sequences in a streaming fashion.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view image pairs (current and previous frame, 4 views at 128×128 resolution) along with camera intrinsics/extrinsics, and produces deformed 3D Gaussian primitives (updated positions and quaternions) for the target frame. The streaming pipeline starts from a key frame's Gaussian set and propagates forward frame-by-frame via a single network inference, with key frames every w=5 frames undergoing bounded refinement. Rendering output supports 204 FPS novel view synthesis at 1352×1014 resolution with ~7.9 MB storage per frame.

## Method

The core contribution is AGM-Net, a generalized network that eliminates per-frame optimization by computing Gaussian motion in a single forward pass. It extracts 2D motion features from optical flow (GM-Flow + Swin-Transformer) between frame pairs, lifts them to 3D via projection-aware feature lift onto 8192 anchor points sampled by Farthest Point Sampling, and refines them with a 4-layer self-attention Transformer. Each Gaussian interpolates its motion feature from K nearest anchors (distance-weighted) and decodes it into position offset (dμ) and rotation delta (drot) via a linear head. A Key-frame-guided Streaming strategy refines every w-th frame with max-points-bounded densification/pruning, resetting the deformation chain to prevent error accumulation while keeping point counts bounded. The network is trained offline across scenes with L1 + D-SSIM loss, then deployed for streaming inference with optional online key-frame refinement.

## Results

On the N3DV dataset, IGS-s achieves 33.89 dB PSNR with a per-frame reconstruction time of 2.67 s and 204 FPS rendering, while IGS-l reaches 34.15 dB PSNR at 3.35 s per frame. This represents a ~6× reduction in per-frame training time compared to the closest streaming baseline 3DGStream (16.93 s) while improving PSNR by 1.14 dB. On the cross-domain Meeting Room dataset, IGS-s achieves 29.24 dB PSNR at 2.77 s per frame with only 1.26 MB storage, outperforming 3DGStream (28.36 dB, 11.51 s, 7.59 MB) on all three metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest streaming baseline 3DGStream [51], IGS-s improves PSNR by 1.14 dB (33.89 vs. 32.75) while reducing per-frame reconstruction time from 16.93 s to 2.67 s (~6× speedup), with comparable rendering speed (204 FPS) and storage (7.90 vs. 7.69 MB). Against StreamRF [29], IGS-s gains 1.80 dB PSNR, reduces training time from 15 s to 2.67 s, and improves rendering from 8.3 FPS to 204 FPS while using 4× less storage. Among offline methods, IGS-l (34.15 dB) surpasses the best offline baseline Saro-GS (33.90 dB) and Spacetime-GS (33.71 dB) in PSNR while offering streaming capability with only 3.35 s per frame versus 48 s total training for Spacetime-GS. In the cross-domain Meeting Room evaluation, IGS-s outperforms 3DGStream by 0.88 dB PSNR, 4.2× faster training (2.77 s vs. 11.51 s), and 6× less storage (1.26 vs. 7.59 MB), demonstrating strong generalization without per-frame optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the streaming dynamic scene reconstruction paradigm established by 3DGStream and StreamRF by replacing their per-frame optimization with a generalized feedforward network (AGM-Net) that predicts Gaussian motion in a single inference pass. The architectural design draws from generalizable 3D reconstruction work (PixelSplat, MVSplat) that uses transformers to decode scene attributes from multi-view features, but repurposes this for inter-frame motion estimation via anchor-point sampling and projection-aware 3D feature lifting. The key-frame-guided streaming strategy with max-points-bounded refinement is a practical addition absent from prior streaming methods, addressing error accumulation and unbounded point growth that plague frame-by-frame optimization approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6× reduction in per-frame reconstruction time (from ~16s to ~2.7s) makes streaming free-viewpoint video practically viable for interactive applications like live broadcasting and virtual meetings, while cross-domain generalization (N3DV-trained model evaluated on Meeting Room) demonstrates the approach is not scene-specific. However, the acknowledged jitter between adjacent frames due to the absence of temporal dependencies limits visual quality for applications requiring temporal smoothness, and the method still requires per-key-frame optimization (4–7.5s), which constrains end-to-end latency. Open questions include whether temporal modeling can be integrated without sacrificing the feedforward speed advantage, and whether the approach scales to longer sequences or more complex non-rigid deformations beyond the 300-frame, 21-camera setups tested here.
