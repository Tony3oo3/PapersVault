## Problem

Vision-centric perception systems in autonomous driving fail under real-world adverse weather because degradations are coupled, unknown, and compound (e.g., rain + blur + noise simultaneously). Task-specific restoration methods require prior knowledge of the degradation type, while all-in-one methods trained on synthetic data suffer a significant domain gap on real images. The paper asks whether a VLM can serve as an autonomous controller that dynamically plans and schedules multiple expert restoration models without requiring paired clean-degraded supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252301/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a degraded RGB image (captured under rain, night, snow, or fog) together with a text instruction that enumerates available restoration tasks and candidate models as single-choice options. The VLM outputs a chain-of-thought degradation assessment followed by an ordered task sequence with a specific model assigned to each sub-task; the actual restored image is then produced by sequentially executing the selected expert models (e.g., SCUnet for denoising, StableSR-turbo for super-resolution, IDT for deraining, RIDCP for dehazing, LightenDiff for low-light enhancement, Snowformer for desnowing).

## Method

JarvisIR builds on Llava-Llama3-8B as a VLM agent that performs four steps at inference: task planning, model selection, task execution via external expert models, and response generation. Training is two-stage: first, supervised fine-tuning on 150K synthetic instruction-response pairs from CleanBench (generated via self-instruct with GPT-4V and DepictQA-Wild for quality reasoning) teaches the model to follow instructions and perceive degradation; second, a novel MRRHF alignment stage fine-tunes on 80K unlabeled real images from CleanBench-Real. MRRHF extends RRHF with (i) a hybrid sample generation strategy that combines offline diverse beam search from the SFT model with online sampling from the policy model to balance exploration and stability, (ii) an entropy regularization term to maintain response diversity, and (iii) a unified reward model that z-score-normalizes and sums scores from three VLM-based IQA models (Q-instruct, MUSIQ, MANIQA). The final loss is a weighted combination of a PRO-inspired ranking loss, cross-entropy on the highest-reward response, and the entropy term, enabling unsupervised alignment on real-world data without paired ground truth.

## Results

On CleanBench-Real, JarvisIR-MRRHF achieves the best scores across all four IQA metrics (MUSIQ, MANIQA, CLIP-IQA+, LIQE) in all four adverse weather scenes (Night, Rain, Fog, Snow). In Night Scenes, JarvisIR-MRRHF reaches MUSIQ 67.25 and MANIQA 0.5876, surpassing the strongest baseline AutoDIR (47.30 / 0.1885) by 42.2% and 210% respectively. In Snow Scenes, it attains MUSIQ 73.87 and MANIQA 0.8014, outperforming the best baseline InstructIR (67.98 / 0.4038) by 8.7% and 98%. Overall, JarvisIR achieves a 50% improvement in the average of all perception metrics compared to existing all-in-one methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JarvisIR-MRRHF dominates all seven all-in-one baselines across every metric and every weather scene. The largest margins appear in Night Scenes, where JarvisIR-MRRHF's MUSIQ of 67.25 exceeds AutoDIR (47.30) by 42.2% and DA-CLIP (45.86) by 46.6%, while its MANIQA of 0.5876 is roughly 3× higher than DA-CLIP (0.2010) and MiOIR (0.2013). In Rain Scenes, JarvisIR-MRRHF leads AutoDIR by 10.0% in MUSIQ (70.38 vs. 63.93) and by 75% in MANIQA (0.7004 vs. 0.4002). In Fog and Snow Scenes, the gaps narrow slightly but remain substantial: JarvisIR-MRRHF outperforms the best baselines (MiOIR for Fog, InstructIR for Snow) by 14.3% and 8.7% in MUSIQ respectively, and by 89% and 98% in MANIQA. Notably, even the SFT-only version (JarvisIR-SFT) already surpasses all baselines, confirming that the VLM-agent paradigm itself provides a structural advantage over fixed-pipeline all-in-one methods, while MRRHF alignment further amplifies the lead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RRHF alignment framework to the multimodal, tool-calling setting, proposing MRRHF with hybrid (offline + online) sample generation and entropy regularization to train a VLM agent for image restoration. It builds on the tool-augmented LLM paradigm (Gorilla, ToolLLM, HuggingGPT) and the VLM-as-controller idea explored by contemporaneous works like RestoreAgent and AgenticIR, but deliberately departs from them by targeting real-world coupled degradations rather than synthetic conditions, and by training on large-scale unlabeled real data via human feedback alignment rather than supervised fine-tuning on paired synthetic data. The system also draws on the self-instruct strategy for dataset construction and integrates multiple VLM-based IQA models as a unified reward signal, a design choice not present in prior restoration agents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

JarvisIR and CleanBench provide the first large-scale benchmark and training pipeline for autonomous multi-expert image restoration under real-world coupled weather degradations, a setting where prior all-in-one and task-specific methods struggle. The MRRHF algorithm offers a practical recipe for aligning VLM agents on unlabeled real data without the memory overhead of full RLHF, which could transfer to other tool-use domains. However, evaluation is confined to the authors' own CleanBench-Real benchmark, the system relies on lightweight (non-SOTA) restoration models, and the comparison to human experts uses a single composite metric, leaving open questions about scalability to truly novel degradation combinations and the computational cost of the multi-model pipeline in latency-sensitive autonomous driving.
