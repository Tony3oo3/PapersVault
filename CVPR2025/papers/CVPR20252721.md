## Problem

Current large multimodal models (LMMs) lack 3D spatial reasoning capabilities, struggling with tasks like predicting object interactions or understanding physical events that require understanding 3D distances, orientations, and their combinations. This limitation stems from both the scarcity of high-quality 3D spatial relationship training data and the absence of a systematic study into when and where in the LMM training pipeline 3D-awareness should be injected. Prior spatial reasoning works focused almost exclusively on 3D distance relationships, overlooking the crucial role of 3D object orientation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252721/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single raw RGB image and a natural-language question about 3D spatial relationships (e.g., 'Is the car facing the same direction as the truck?') and produces a free-form text answer. Unlike SpatialRGPT, SpatialLLM requires no auxiliary inputs such as depth maps, segmentation masks, or bounding box coordinates at inference time. The SpatialVQA benchmark used for evaluation contains 1,323 questions spanning three difficulty levels: distance-only, orientation-only, and complex spatial reasoning combining both, all built on urban and indoor scenes from Omni3D.

## Method

SpatialLLM introduces a compound 3D-informed design that simultaneously optimizes data, architecture, and training stages. On the data front, two types of 3D-informed datasets are created: probing data (3DI-Pb-IN166K from human-annotated ImageNet3D, and 3DI-Pb-OI1M from semi-automated annotation of OpenImages) capturing object-level 3D location and orientation, and conversation data (3DI-Ft1M) for complex multi-object spatial relationships—making this the first work to incorporate 3D orientation VQA on real images. Architecturally, the vision encoder mixes CLIP and DINOv2 features to combine language-supervised and self-supervised representations with stronger 3D awareness. The key training innovation is injecting 3D-informed data at multiple stages: 3DI-Pb-IN166K during multimodal alignment (Stage 1) to align visual features with 3D semantics, and 3DI-Ft1M during instruction tuning (Stage 2) to teach complex spatial reasoning. The LLM backbone is upgraded from Vicuna-7B to Llama3-8B. Empirically, 3D-informed data across training stages contributes 13.7% improvement versus only 1.3% from architectural changes, yielding 62.7% accuracy on SpatialVQA—surpassing GPT-4o by 8.7%.

## Results

On the SpatialVQA benchmark, SpatialLLM achieves 62.7% average accuracy, surpassing GPT-4o (54.0%) by 8.7% and the best open-source model Cambrian-8B (51.7%) by 10.5%. The model excels particularly in 3D distance reasoning (86.3% vs. 64.3% for SpatialVLM-13B), while 3D orientation (52.9%) and complex 3D spatial relationship (50.8%) performance are more competitive but still lead or match GPT-4o. The ablation in Table 2 shows that 3D-informed data contributes 13.7% of the total gain over the LLaVA-v1.5 baseline, while architectural upgrades (mixed encoder + Llama3) contribute 1.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpatialLLM outperforms all seven baselines on average accuracy. Against proprietary models, it beats GPT-4o (54.0%) by 8.7%, GPT-4o Mini (45.4%) by 17.3%, and Claude 3.5 Sonnet (49.3%) by 13.4%. Among open-source models, it surpasses LLaVA-v1.5-7B (47.7%) by 15.0%, LLaVA-NeXT-8B (50.6%) by 12.1%, and Cambrian-8B (51.7%) by 11.0%. The most direct spatial-reasoning comparison is with SpatialVLM-13B (52.2%), which SpatialLLM exceeds by 10.5% overall; the gap is especially large on 3D distance (86.3% vs. 64.3%), while 3D orientation is closer (52.9% vs. 48.5%). Notably, SpatialVLM actually underperforms LLaVA-v1.5 on 3D orientation (48.5% vs. 46.4% is a small gain, but the paper notes it falls short due to lack of orientation-specific training data), highlighting that distance-only 3D data is insufficient. On 3D spatial relationship, SpatialLLM ties GPT-4o at 50.8% and slightly trails Claude 3.5 Sonnet (51.5%), indicating this sub-task remains the hardest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LLaVA-v1.5 architecture and three-stage training pipeline (pre-pretraining, multimodal alignment, instruction tuning), extending it with a compound 3D-informed design that injects spatial data at multiple stages rather than only at instruction tuning as in SpatialVLM. It departs from prior spatial-reasoning work (SpatialVLM, SpatialRGPT) in two key ways: it is the first to incorporate 3D object orientation relationships (leveraging ImageNet3D 6D pose annotations) alongside distance, and it systematically ablates *where* in the training pipeline and *which* architectural component benefits from 3D-informed data. The mixed vision encoder design (CLIP + DINOv2) draws on recent work showing self-supervised features complement language-supervised ones, but the paper's contribution is the empirical mapping of this choice to spatial reasoning performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, reproducible roadmap for building spatially-intelligent LMMs, with the key finding that 3D-informed data (13.7% gain) dwarfs architectural changes (1.3% gain) in driving spatial reasoning performance — a useful signal for practitioners deciding where to invest effort. The negative result that 3D-informed pre-pretraining of the vision encoder (Stage 0) actually degrades performance is a cautionary finding that challenges the intuition that more 3D data at every stage is always beneficial. Limitations include evaluation solely on the authors' own 1,323-question SpatialVQA benchmark, reliance on ImageNet3D for orientation data (which may not generalize to all object categories), and the absence of comparison with models that receive explicit 3D inputs (depth maps, bounding boxes). Open questions include whether the compound design transfers to video or multi-view settings, and whether the orientation data bottleneck can be addressed with better 6D pose estimators.
