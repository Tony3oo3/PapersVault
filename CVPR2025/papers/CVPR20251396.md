## Problem

Text-to-image generation, even with spatial controls like ControlNet, remains a stochastic 'dice roll' where no single output reliably contains every element a user wants. Existing image blending methods operate in pixel space and struggle to preserve local appearances while producing seamless composites, and trial-and-error re-rolling offers no fine-grained control. The paper asks whether users can treat a stack of generated images as intermediate outputs and selectively composite desired regions across them to form a final image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251396/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a stack of N spatially aligned ControlNet-generated images (produced from the same edge/sketch/depth condition and text prompt with different random seeds) along with sparse user brush strokes that designate which region from which image to include. The user also designates one image as the base (typically the one whose background is selected). The output is a single composited RGB image at the original resolution, produced by a final denoising pass in ControlNet, plus an intermediate feature-space label assignment map at 1/8 image resolution that encodes which source image contributes to each spatial location.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage pipeline: (1) a multi-label graph cut performed in diffusion feature space rather than pixel space, and (2) a self-attention feature injection scheme for compositing. For segmentation, the energy function combines a unary term (hard penalty C=10⁶ where user strokes conflict with the assigned label) and a pairwise term that places seams where key features K are dissimilar across all images in the stack, using top-10 PCA components of K at 1/8 resolution and the final denoising time step. For compositing, the label assignment produces binary masks that form composite Q, K, V features per self-attention layer: K and V are masked sums over all images, while Q uses the model's own features for the base image (Q^model) and stored features for others, allowing the U-Net to adapt structure near seams during the final denoising pass. This design preserves user-selected local appearances (via injected K, V) while letting the model harmonize boundaries (via free Q in the base mask).

## Results

On 20 test examples, Generative Photomontage achieves the highest PSNR (22.46) among all baselines, indicating the best preservation of local image appearance, along with the second-best masked LPIPS (0.123) and third-best masked SSIM (0.815). Its seam gradient score (0.339) falls within the natural range of the image stack (0.255–0.425) and is nearly identical to the stack average (0.340), confirming seamless blending. In user surveys across 12 scenes, the method is rated best for blending quality by a wide margin and is comparable to MasaCtrl+ControlNet in perceived realism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Generative Photomontage outperforms all baselines on PSNR (22.46 vs. next-best CollageDiffusion at 20.57), confirming superior preservation of user-selected local regions. It trails only IDP on masked LPIPS (0.123 vs. 0.104) and masked SSIM (0.815 vs. 0.888), but IDP's gradient-domain blending causes visible color shifts that lower its PSNR to 20.13. Against the diffusion-based baselines, the method substantially outperforms BLD (PSNR 22.46 vs. 20.27; LPIPS 0.123 vs. 0.222) and MasaCtrl+ControlNet (PSNR 22.46 vs. 18.34; SSIM 0.815 vs. 0.680), which both introduce structural or appearance changes in blended regions. GP-GAN, CDC, and CollageDiffusion all produce seam gradient scores outside the natural image range (0.220, 0.456, 0.559 respectively), revealing significant seam artifacts, whereas Ours (0.339) sits almost exactly at the stack average. In the user survey, Ours is rated best for blending quality by a wide margin over all three closest competitors (IDP, BLD, MasaCtrl+ControlNet) and is statistically comparable to MasaCtrl+ControlNet on realism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classic Interactive Digital Photomontage pipeline (pixel-space graph cut + gradient-domain blending) by performing both segmentation and compositing in the self-attention feature space of a diffusion model, specifically ControlNet. Rather than treating generated images as final outputs, it repurposes them as intermediate candidates whose internal Q/K/V features are stored and later re-injected during a second denoising pass. The approach is complementary to controllable generation (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter) and distinct from attention-based editing methods like MasaCtrl or StyleAligned, which transfer high-level style rather than preserving local appearance across a spatially aligned stack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces a practical user workflow—generate many, select parts, composite—that combinatorially improves the odds of achieving a desired result over repeated re-rolling, and the feature-space graph cut + self-attention injection pipeline is a reusable technique for any spatially aligned image stack. Limitations include the requirement for a spatially aligned stack (same input condition), dependence on at least one 'correct' candidate per region, and evaluation on only 20 test examples with a single diffusion backbone (Stable Diffusion 1.5). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-aligned stacks, to newer architectures (SDXL, DiT-based models), and to more than a handful of images in the stack.
