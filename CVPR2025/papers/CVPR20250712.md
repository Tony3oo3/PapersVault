## Problem

Existing visual token compression methods for LVLMs face two unresolved dilemmas: (1) target token identification must balance importance against redundancy, but either selecting purely by importance (FastV) or tuning a fixed hyperparameter (Turbo) fails to robustly avoid redundant selections; (2) token compression must choose between pruning (which discards non-target token information) and merging (which disrupts target tokens when dissimilar tokens are combined). No prior method simultaneously addresses both trade-offs in a training-free, plug-in manner.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a training-free plug-in inside a pre-trained LVLM (e.g., LLaVA-1.5-7B/13B, LLaVA-NeXT-8B, VideoLLaMA-2, Qwen2-VL). It consumes the visual token sequence (N×D) produced by the frozen visual encoder and projector, along with the output attention scores (1×N_all) computed during response decoding, and produces a compressed visual token sequence of length N_t = (1−R)·N per compression layer. The compression is applied hierarchically at multiple LLM layers (e.g., layers 7, 15, 23 for a 32-layer model), progressively reducing token count from N to N·R^K. A hybrid attention mechanism computes only the 1×N_t output-to-visual attention scores, preserving flash-attention 2 compatibility with negligible overhead.

## Method

Libra-Merging consists of two core modules applied at each compression layer. First, position-driven token identification divides the visual token sequence into N_t contiguous spatial intervals of length 1/(1−R) and selects the single highest-importance token from each interval as a target token, thereby disrupting spatial continuity to avoid redundant selections without any tunable hyperparameter. Second, importance-informed grouped merging computes cosine similarities between each non-target token and all target tokens, then partitions non-target tokens into a positive set (s^max > τ, merged into their most similar target token using Softmax-weighted importance as merging weights) and a negative set (s^max ≤ τ, condensed into a single importance-weighted information compensation token appended to the sequence). This prevents dissimilar tokens from disrupting target tokens while preserving their important information. A hierarchical merging strategy applies these two operations at multiple layers for progressive compression, and a hybrid attention mechanism enables compatibility with flash-attention 2 by computing only the output-token-to-visual-token attention scores needed for the importance metric.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B, Libra-Merging at 47% Flops (1.78T) achieves an average score of 62.1 across six benchmarks, matching the vanilla model (62.5 at 100% Flops) while outperforming FastV (61.8) and Turbo (61.8) at 56% Flops. On LLaVA-NeXT-8B, the 47%-Flops Libra-Merging even surpasses the vanilla model (71.7 vs. 71.6 average), while FastV and Turbo drop to 71.5 and 70.0 respectively. On Video-MME, Libra-Merging achieves 48.8 overall (52.9 with subsampling) versus 46.5/51.4 for FastV and 47.9/52.1 for Turbo at the same 34% Flops ratio. Actual runtime on A800 shows Libra-Merging at R=80% achieves 58.81 GQA score with 0.086s latency/example, versus FastV's 56.57 at 0.085s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Libra-Merging consistently outperforms both FastV (Chen et al., ECCV 2025) and Turbo (Ju et al., ECCV 2025) across all three image-text models while operating at a lower Flops budget (47% vs. 55–56%). On LLaVA-1.5-7B, Libra-Merging achieves 62.1 average at 47% Flops versus 61.8 for both FastV and Turbo at 56% Flops. The gap widens substantially on LLaVA-NeXT-8B, where Libra-Merging scores 71.7 (even exceeding the vanilla 71.6) while Turbo drops to 70.0 and FastV to 71.5. On the video benchmark Video-MME, Libra-Merging achieves 48.8 overall (52.9 with subsampling) compared to FastV's 46.5 (51.4) and Turbo's 47.9 (52.1) at the same 34% Flops ratio. In terms of actual runtime on A800, Libra-Merging matches FastV's latency (0.086s vs. 0.085s per example at R=80%) while delivering a 2.2-point GQA improvement (58.81 vs. 56.57). The paper also demonstrates that Libra-Merging is compatible with Flash-Attention 2 via a hybrid attention mechanism, whereas FastV without this mechanism loses the Flash-Attention speedup (12:25 vs. 08:12 on Qwen2-VL-7B MME).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the visual token compression line initiated by FastV (output-attention-based pruning) and Turbo (information-degree-based merging) for LVLMs, explicitly framing its contributions as resolving two dilemmas those methods leave open: the importance-redundancy trade-off in target token selection and the pruning-merging trade-off in how non-target tokens are handled. The position-driven interval-based selection draws on the well-known spatial redundancy property of ViT tokens, while the importance-informed grouped merging extends the similarity-based merging paradigm (ToMe, K-Medoids) with an importance-weighted Softmax and a compensation-token mechanism reminiscent of EViT's fused token. The hierarchical multi-layer compression and hybrid attention for flash-attention compatibility are practical extensions not present in prior LVLM compression work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LVLMs grow in size and visual token count (e.g., LLaVA-NeXT's 576 tokens, Qwen2-VL's dynamic resolution), inference cost becomes a primary deployment bottleneck, making training-free, plug-in compression methods like Libra-Merging practically important. The finding that stronger models paradoxically benefit more from compression (LLaVA-NeXT-8B at 47% tokens slightly outperforming 100%) hints at a scaling interaction worth further study, though the margin is minimal. Open questions include whether the fixed similarity threshold τ and the interval-based redundancy assumption generalize to non-grid token layouts (e.g., dynamic-resolution models), and whether the compensation token's single-vector bottleneck limits performance at very high compression ratios (>80%).
