## Problem

Existing 3D Gaussian head avatar methods face a trilemma: capturing expression-dependent dynamic textures (wrinkles, skin folds) either requires storing per-blendshape Gaussian sets with gigabyte-scale storage, or relies on large MLPs with super-resolution that preclude real-time rendering. No prior method simultaneously achieves high-fidelity dynamic appearance, real-time inference speed, and minimal per-subject storage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251930/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view face videos (16 viewpoints at 802×550) along with pre-tracked FLAME parameters (shape, pose, expression, vertex offset) and camera intrinsics/extrinsics from the Nersemble dataset. It produces a compact animatable avatar (~10 MB per subject) that, given arbitrary expression coefficients and jaw rotation, renders photorealistic face images with dynamic textures at 300 FPS on an RTX 4090. The avatar is evaluated on novel view synthesis, self-reenactment with unseen expressions, and cross-identity reenactment.

## Method

The key insight is that neighboring Gaussian splats share similar static and dynamic appearance, enabling compact tensorial storage instead of per-splat attributes. Static view-dependent color is encoded in a triplane (128×128, asymmetric feature dimensions) in canonical space, decoded with a 2-layer MLP and view direction to replace the 48 SH coefficients per Gaussian. Dynamic texture changes are captured as opacity offsets relative to the neutral face, stored in lightweight 1D feature lines (length 64, dim 32) for each of 80 PCA expression bases and 16 jaw rotation bases, interpolated by tracked coefficients and decoded by a second 2-layer MLP. Two training strategies improve generalization: an adaptive truncated opacity penalty that zeros offsets on mesh triangles with minimal displacement, and class-balanced sampling via spectral clustering (16 clusters) to prevent overfitting to predominantly neutral expressions.

## Results

On the Nersemble dataset, the proposed method achieves the best PSNR (32.97) and SSIM (0.9506) on novel view synthesis, and the best PSNR (28.07) and SSIM (0.9259) on self-reenactment, outperforming all three baselines. However, it trails GA and GHA on LPIPS (0.0594 vs. 0.0514 and 0.0491 for NVS), suggesting slightly less perceptual fidelity in fine texture details. The method requires only 10 MB of storage per subject (vs. 21 MB for GA, 120 MB for GHA, and 2 GB for GBS) while maintaining 300 FPS real-time rendering on an RTX 4090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three 3DGS-based head avatar methods: GA (GaussianAvatars), GHA (Gaussian Head Avatar), and GBS (3D Gaussian Blendshapes). The proposed method wins on PSNR and SSIM across both novel view synthesis and self-reenactment tasks, with the largest margin over GHA (e.g., +5.98 dB PSNR on self-reenactment) and a clear advantage over GBS (+4.09 dB PSNR on self-reenactment). However, it loses on LPIPS to both GA and GHA, indicating that the compact triplane representation slightly blurs high-frequency texture details compared to per-splat spherical harmonics (GA) or super-resolution-enhanced rendering (GHA). In terms of efficiency, the method uses 2× less storage than GA (10 MB vs. 21 MB), 12× less than GHA, and 200× less than GBS (2 GB), while GHA's 20 FPS rendering speed makes it impractical for real-time use. GBS achieves the highest FPS (370) but at the cost of 2 GB storage and inferior rendering quality due to linear interpolation artifacts in large expressions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3DGS-based head avatar line initiated by Gaussian Avatars (GA), which binds splats to a FLAME mesh for geometry motion, by replacing per-splat spherical harmonics with a compact triplane for canonical appearance and introducing 1D feature lines per blendshape for dynamic opacity offsets. It departs from GBS, which stores a full set of Gaussians per blendshape (2 GB storage), and from GHA, which uses large MLPs with super-resolution for dynamic details (20 FPS), by showing that lightweight 1D tensorial features suffice to capture expression-dependent texture changes. The jaw rotation handling borrows the farthest-point-sampling basis extraction from PoseVocab, and the mesh-binding formulation follows GA directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10 MB per-subject storage and 300 FPS rendering make high-fidelity dynamic head avatars practical for bandwidth-constrained and mobile deployment (e.g., real-time video conferencing), a regime where prior methods with 120 MB–2 GB footprints are infeasible. The finding that 1D feature lines adequately encode dynamic texture offsets challenges the implicit assumption that per-blendshape 2D/3D representations are necessary, opening the door to even more compact dynamic scene representations. Limitations remain: reliance on a tracked mesh precludes complex hairstyles and intra-oral topology changes, and the absence of material-lighting decoupling precludes relighting, both flagged as future work.
