## Problem

Existing first-order meta-learning methods for domain generalization (e.g., Fish, Reptile) use uniform gradient weights in the outer loop, which the authors prove is just one of infinitely many directions that satisfy gradient matching. These methods overlook a second critical requirement: the balanced parameters should lie near the centroid of each source domain's optimal parameters, not merely align gradient directions. The paper shows that the specific update direction within the gradient-matching manifold significantly affects generalization to unseen domains.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images from multiple source domains (e.g., PACS with 7 classes, Office-Home with 65 classes, DomainNet with 345 classes) in domain-specific batches of size 32, and outputs a single class label for each image from an unseen target domain. Training uses a ResNet-50 backbone pretrained on ImageNet, with 20% of each source domain reserved for validation. The method is modality-agnostic in principle but is evaluated on both natural/office images (DomainBed) and medical/pathology images (CAMELYON17 on WILDS).

## Method

Arithmetic meta-learning replaces the uniform gradient weights of Fish/Reptile with an arithmetically decreasing sequence (n+1-i)/(n+ε) for the n inner-loop gradients, which is algebraically equivalent to averaging all intermediate models produced during the inner loop. This averaging estimates the centroid of domain-specific optimal parameters, achieving a more precise balance across source domains while still satisfying the gradient-matching condition (proven via the general form Θ ← Θ − Σ εᵢgᵢ). To ensure each inner-loop gradient faithfully represents its domain, the method uses SGD without momentum in the inner loop and Adam only in the outer loop, preventing momentum from blending cross-domain gradients. The entire modification requires changing one line in the standard Fish algorithm, and it composes additively with global weight-averaging techniques like SWAD for further gains.

## Results

On the DomainBed benchmark, Arithmetic Meta-Learning (Arith) achieves 65.0% average accuracy across five datasets (PACS, VLCS, Office-Home, TerraIncognita, DomainNet), outperforming the second-best method Fishr (64.0%) by 1.0%. Arith attains the best per-dataset accuracy on four of five datasets: PACS (86.5%), VLCS (79.4%), Office-Home (69.4%), and TerraIncognita (48.1%), while trailing Fish (42.7%) on DomainNet (41.5%). On the WILDS CAMELYON17 benchmark, Arith reaches 76.6% average accuracy, surpassing Fish (74.8%) and ERM (73.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Arith outperforms all gradient-based meta-learning baselines on average: it beats Fish (63.9%) by 1.1%, Fishr (64.0%) by 1.0%, MLDG (63.6%) by 1.4%, HGP (63.0%) by 2.0%, and Hutchinson (63.4%) by 1.6%. Against invariance-based methods, Arith leads ERM (63.3%) by 1.7%, IRM (61.6%) by 3.4%, VREx (61.9%) by 3.1%, RSC (62.7%) by 2.3%, ARM (61.7%) by 3.3%, AND-mask (62.0%) by 3.0%, and SAND-mask (60.6%) by 4.4%. The closest competitor is CORAL (64.5%), which Arith surpasses by 0.5% on average; however, CORAL edges out Arith on DomainNet (41.5 vs. 41.5, tied) and Fish leads on DomainNet (42.7 vs. 41.5). On CAMELYON17, Arith (76.6%) beats Fish (74.8%) by 1.8% and ERM (73.1%) by 3.5%. The paper attributes its gains to two factors: estimating the centroid of domain-optimal parameters via arithmetic gradient weights, and removing momentum from the inner loop to preserve domain-specific gradient matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends first-order meta-learning for domain generalization (Reptile [40], Fish [48]) by showing that the uniform gradient weights used in existing methods represent just one of infinitely many valid gradient-matching directions. It departs from prior work by introducing arithmetic-progression gradient weights that simultaneously satisfy the gradient-matching principle and approximate the centroid of domain-optimal parameters, thereby bridging meta-learning with weight averaging [24, 9] in a principled, localized manner. The method also refines optimizer usage (SGD without momentum in the inner loop, Adam in the outer loop) to preserve domain-specific gradient signals that momentum would otherwise blur.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-line modification to gradient weights offers a simple, drop-in improvement for practitioners already using Fish or MLDG, and the theoretical connection between arithmetic weights and model averaging provides a clearer geometric interpretation of what balanced meta-learning should achieve. However, the practical gains are modest (0.3–0.7% over the best baselines on DomainBed), and the fundamental limitation remains that balance across source domains does not guarantee alignment with an arbitrary unseen target. Open questions include whether the arithmetic weighting scheme generalizes to non-uniform domain sizes, multi-step inner loops with varying step counts per domain, or settings where domain-optimal parameters are highly separated in parameter space.
