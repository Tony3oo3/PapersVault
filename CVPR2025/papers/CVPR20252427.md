## Problem

Existing reward-based fine-tuning methods for text-to-image models (e.g., DRaFT) optimize global image-level rewards, which can degrade other quality aspects (prompt alignment, introduce new artifacts), cause catastrophic forgetting, or lead to reward hacking where the model finds entirely different solutions rather than fixing the intended issue. No prior method constrains fine-tuning to correct only the specific problematic regions of a generated image while preserving the rest.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252427/cropped_page0_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

During fine-tuning, the method takes a text prompt, a reference image from the pretrained model, and a binary region mask (derived from heatmap prediction models or gradient-based saliency maps on classifier reward models) identifying problematic areas. At inference, only the text prompt is needed—a standard forward pass of the fine-tuned LoRA-augmented diffusion model produces the corrected image with no additional inputs or computation. Experiments use SD v1.4 (512×512) and SDXL as base models, with LoRA rank 64 and backpropagation truncated to the last 2 denoising steps.

## Method

Focus-N-Fix extends DRaFT's direct reward fine-tuning objective by adding a regional constraint term: the model maximizes reward r(Î, c) minus β·‖(1−M(Î₀))⊙(Î₀−Î)‖_F, where M(Î₀) is a binary mask of problematic regions in the pretrained model's reference image and the Frobenius norm penalizes pixel changes outside that mask. This forces the model to improve only within the identified regions while keeping the rest of the image compositionally and stylistically similar to the original. The mask is obtained either from dedicated heatmap-prediction models (e.g., for artifacts) or by applying gradient-based saliency maps (Grad-CAM) to simple CNN classifiers (e.g., for over-sexualization), with thresholding and dilation to produce binary masks. Only LoRA parameters are updated during training, and the mask function M(·) is used solely to produce the region mask without contributing gradients. After fine-tuning, inference requires no mask or heatmap—just a standard diffusion forward pass with the updated weights.

## Results

On the over-sexualization (safety) task, Focus-N-Fix achieves the highest human preference safety score of 0.479 (vs. 0.439 for SLD and 0.361 for DRaFT) while maintaining near-zero degradation in artifacts (0.042) and T2I alignment (0.004). On the artifact reduction task, Focus-N-Fix achieves the best artifact score of 0.294 with a positive T2I alignment score of 0.100, outperforming DRaFT (0.207 / 0.012). On SDXL, Focus-N-Fix preserves image fidelity far better than DRaFT (PSNR 20.92 vs. 12.40, SSIM 0.76 vs. 0.44, LPIPS 0.33 vs. 0.66) while still improving safety reward (Δ0.21 vs. Δ0.33) and maintaining positive VNLI alignment (+0.013 vs. −0.031).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DRaFT [Clark et al., ICLR 2024], Focus-N-Fix wins on every metric in both tasks: safety preference improves from 0.361 to 0.479, artifact degradation flips from −0.097 to +0.042, and T2I alignment goes from −0.146 to +0.004 (safety task); for the artifact task, artifact score rises from 0.207 to 0.294 and T2I alignment from 0.012 to 0.100. Against Safe Latent Diffusion [Schramowski et al., CVPR 2023], Focus-N-Fix achieves a higher safety score (0.479 vs. 0.439) and substantially better T2I alignment (0.004 vs. −0.081) and combined Min score (−0.085 vs. −0.149), though SLD has a marginally higher artifact score (0.092 vs. 0.042). Against Reward Guidance [Bansal et al., CVPR 2023] and its region-constrained variant (RG+RC), Focus-N-Fix dominates across all reported metrics in both the safety and artifact settings, with the largest gap on T2I alignment (0.004 vs. −0.058/−0.072) and the combined Min score (−0.085 vs. −0.187/−0.155). On SDXL, Focus-N-Fix trades a slightly lower safety reward gain (Δ0.21 vs. Δ0.33 for DRaFT) for dramatically better image preservation (PSNR 20.92 vs. 12.40) and positive VNLI alignment (+0.013 vs. −0.031), confirming that the region-aware constraint prevents the global image drift seen in unconstrained fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

Focus-N-Fix extends DRaFT (Clark et al., ICLR 2024), which fine-tunes diffusion models by backpropagating through differentiable reward functions, by adding a spatial constraint term (Frobenius norm of masked pixel differences) that restricts model changes to problematic regions identified by a localization function. This departs from prior reward-based alignment methods—DRaFT, Reward Guidance (Bansal et al., CVPR 2023), and Safe Latent Diffusion (Schramowski et al., CVPR 2023)—which optimize a global scalar reward and can produce entirely different images or engage in reward hacking. The localization component draws on rich human feedback heatmap models (Liang et al., CVPR 2024) and gradient-based saliency maps (Grad-CAM), while the region-preserving philosophy is conceptually related to targeted concept erasure (Gandikota et al., 2024) but operates at the pixel level rather than the concept level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical practical bottleneck in deploying reward-fine-tuned T2I models: global reward optimization causes collateral damage (degraded alignment, new artifacts, catastrophic forgetting of spatial/counting abilities) that often makes fine-tuned models unusable in production. By showing that a simple spatial constraint during training—requiring no extra inputs at inference—can isolate improvements to deficient regions, the work provides a practical recipe for iterative, multi-aspect model improvement (e.g., fix safety, then fix artifacts) without compounding degradation. Key limitations include applicability only to localizable quality aspects (aesthetics, style require reverting to global optimization), dependence on the quality of the localization mask, and evaluation primarily on SD v1.4 with limited architectural diversity. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video generation, non-differentiable rewards, and whether sequential multi-region fine-tuning accumulates errors.
