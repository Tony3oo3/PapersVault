## Problem

Memory-based trackers like SAM2 concatenate recent frames into a memory buffer for target localization, but this design implicitly encodes distractors alongside the target, causing drift when visually similar regions appear. The paper argues that a single undifferentiated memory cannot simultaneously serve segmentation accuracy (requiring recent appearances) and tracking robustness (requiring frames that discriminate the target from critical distractors), and that existing memory management protocols do not explicitly address this dual need.

## Input / Output

The method takes a video sequence of RGB frames and a user-provided segmentation mask on the first frame as initialization. SAM2.1 internally predicts three candidate masks per frame with associated IoU scores; the highest-IoU mask is selected as the tracking output. For bounding-box benchmarks, axis-aligned boxes are derived by applying min-max to the predicted mask. The method is entirely training-free, operating at 11 fps (vs. 13.3 fps for baseline SAM2.1) with fixed hyperparameters across all benchmarks.

## Method

DAM4SAM restructures SAM2's memory into two functionally distinct components: a Recent Appearance Memory (RAM) with 3 FIFO slots updated every 5 frames (with temporal encoding) to ensure segmentation accuracy, and a Distractor Resolving Memory (DRM) with 3 anchor slots plus the initialization frame (without temporal encoding) to serve as a time-less prior for distractor discrimination. The key mechanism is an introspection-based DRM update rule that exploits SAM2's multi-hypothesis output: when the bounding-box area ratio between the selected mask and the union of the selected mask plus the largest connected component in an alternative mask drops below 0.7, the frame is flagged as containing a critical distractor. DRM is updated only under this divergence condition AND during stable tracking (predicted IoU > 0.8, mask area within 0.2 of the 10-frame median), preventing memory corruption from erroneous segmentations. Additionally, RAM is not updated when the predicted mask is empty, avoiding memory degradation during occlusions. The paper also introduces the DiDi dataset (180 sequences, 274,882 frames), semi-automatically distilled from six benchmarks using DINO2 feature-space cosine distances to retain only sequences where at least one-third of frames contain non-negligible distractors.

## Results

On the proposed DiDi dataset, DAM4SAM achieves a tracking quality of 0.694 (accuracy 0.727, robustness 0.944), outperforming the SAM2.1 baseline (0.649) by 7% and the best distractor-aware tracker ODTrack (0.608) by 14%. On VOT2020 and VOT2022, DAM4SAM sets new state-of-the-art EAO scores of 0.729 and 0.753 respectively, surpassing the VOT2020 winner RPT by 37.5% and the VOT2022 winner MS_AOT by 12%. On VOTS2024, DAM4SAM achieves quality 0.711, placing second behind the challenge-tuned S3-Track (0.722) while outperforming all published trackers. On bounding-box benchmarks, DAM4SAM reaches 75.1 AUC on LaSOT, 60.9 AUC on LaSoT_ext, and 81.1 AO on GoT-10k, all state-of-the-art or tied.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAM4SAM outperforms the SAM2.1 baseline on all seven evaluated benchmarks, with the largest gains in robustness (e.g., +6% on DiDi, +2.1% on VOT2020, +3% on VOT2022), confirming that the distractor-aware memory primarily improves tracking persistence. Against SAM2-based extensions, DAM4SAM beats SAMURAI by 2% quality on DiDi and SAM2Long by 7%, with SAM2Long performing on par with the unmodified SAM2.1 baseline. Among explicit distractor-handling trackers, DAM4SAM surpasses ODTrack by 14% and Cutie by 21% on DiDi, while on VOT2020 it exceeds the challenge winner RPT by 37.5% EAO and on VOT2022 the winner MS_AOT by 12% EAO. On VOTS2024, DAM4SAM takes second place (quality 0.711) behind the unpublished, multi-backbone S3-Track (0.722) but ahead of all published methods including LORAT (0.536) and Cutie-SAM (0.607). On bounding-box benchmarks, DAM4SAM ties LORAT on LaSOT (75.1 AUC) but clearly outperforms it on LaSoT_ext (60.9 vs. 56.6 AUC) and GoT-10k (81.1 vs. 78.2 AO), indicating stronger generalization to unseen categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the memory-based video object segmentation lineage (XMem, AOT, RMem) and specifically builds on SAM2's restricted memory bank of six recent frames plus an initialization frame. The key conceptual departure is splitting the memory into two functionally distinct sub-memories—Recent Appearance Memory (RAM) for segmentation accuracy and Distractor-Resolving Memory (DRM) for robustness—rather than treating all slots uniformly as prior FIFO or restricted-memory schemes do. It also departs from explicit distractor-handling architectures (KeepTrack, ODTrack, Cutie) by addressing distractors purely through selective memory management and by exploiting SAM2's previously-ignored alternative mask predictions as a distractor-detection signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a training-free, architecture-agnostic memory management change yields 7–9% EAO improvements over SAM2.1 across VOT benchmarks, making it an immediately adoptable upgrade for anyone using SAM2-based trackers. The DiDi dataset is a useful contribution that exposes benchmark saturation on standard benchmarks (e.g., GoT-10k where >99% of frames are tracked) and reveals that distractor handling remains a major unsolved challenge. Open questions include whether learnable memory management policies could further improve over the hand-crafted thresholds, whether the DRM concept generalizes beyond SAM2 to other memory-based trackers, and whether the 20% speed reduction is acceptable in real-time deployment.
