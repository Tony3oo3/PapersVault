## Problem

While Multimodal Large Language Models have demonstrated strong reasoning in various vision tasks, the detection and reasoning of visual anomalies remains largely unexplored due to the absence of dedicated instruction-tuning datasets and benchmarks. Existing zero-shot anomaly detection methods only output a likelihood score without providing interpretable rationales, and even advanced MLLMs like GPT-4o fail to accurately detect and describe fine-grained anomalous details in images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251340/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a high-resolution RGB image (e.g., 1152×1152, split into multiple crops via the AnyRes strategy yielding up to 7290 visual tokens) along with a text instruction in multi-round conversation format. It produces a scalar anomaly score (sigmoid of aggregated expert features), a significance map highlighting suspicious regions, and free-form text responses covering both low-level defect descriptions and complex reasoning about potential causes and improvement suggestions. The text output is conditioned on an indication prompt whose intensity word (highly/moderately/slightly) is determined by the anomaly score relative to predefined thresholds.

## Method

Anomaly-OV builds on LLaVA-OneVision and introduces a specialist anomaly expert (visual encoder only, no text encoder) that learns class-awareness abnormality embeddings. The Look-Twice Feature Matching (LTFM) mechanism uses global object features from a look-back path merged with learnable positive/negative embeddings to compute per-token significance maps via cosine similarity and softmax across four ViT levels, mimicking the two-step human inspection of first identifying then closely examining suspicious regions. A Visual Token Selector then multiplies visual tokens by these significance maps, applies spatial pooling, and uses a Q-Former to aggregate the most suspicious tokens, which are injected into the LLM alongside the original features with an intensity-indicating prompt. Training proceeds in two stages: Stage 1 trains the anomaly expert with balanced BCE loss on labeled anomaly data, and Stage 2 freezes the expert and visual encoder while instruction-tuning the projector and LLM on Anomaly-Instruct-125k plus ~350k general samples to preserve generalization.

## Results

On the ZSAD benchmark (Table 2), Anomaly-OV's anomaly expert achieves an average image-level AUROC of 88.6 across nine datasets, outperforming the best prior method AdaCLIP (85.3) by 3.3 points and AnomalyCLIP (84.5) by 4.1 points. On the VisA-D&R benchmark (Table 4), Anomaly-OV-7B achieves 0.79 accuracy and 0.83 F1-score in text-based anomaly detection, surpassing GPT-4o (0.70 accuracy, 0.68 F1) and all open-source MLLMs. For reasoning, Anomaly-OV-7B attains a GPT-Score of 4.26 (low-level) and 6.34 (complex), approaching GPT-4o's 4.84 and 6.89 respectively, while significantly outperforming LLaVA-OV-7B (3.57 and 5.44).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Anomaly-OV outperforms all seven ZSAD baselines in Table 2, with the largest margins over CLIP (+25.5 avg AUROC) and CoOp (+17.7), and the most competitive gap against AdaCLIP (+3.3) and AnomalyCLIP (+4.1). Notably, Anomaly-OV achieves the best score on 7 of 9 individual benchmarks, losing only on AITEX (72.0 vs. WinCLIP's 73.0) and Br35H (95.5 vs. AdaCLIP's 97.7). In the MLLM comparison (Table 4), Anomaly-OV-7B surpasses GPT-4o on detection accuracy (0.79 vs. 0.70) and F1-score (0.83 vs. 0.68), but GPT-4o retains a slight edge on complex reasoning GPT-Score (6.89 vs. 6.34) and low-level GPT-Score (4.84 vs. 4.26). Against open-source MLLMs (Qwen2-VL, InternVL-2, IXC-2.5, LLaVA-OV), Anomaly-OV-7B leads on detection F1-score (0.83 vs. 0.63–0.76) and low-level reasoning ROUGE-L (0.34 vs. 0.21–0.25), while the base LLaVA-OV-7B shows high precision (0.95) but poor recall (0.56), indicating insensitivity to anomalies that Anomaly-OV corrects. The fine-tuned LLaVA-OV-0.5B* (trained on Anomaly-Instruct-125k alone) already reaches 0.76 F1, demonstrating the dataset's value, and adding the anomaly expert further lifts it to 0.82 F1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on LLaVA-OneVision's three-component MLLM architecture (visual encoder, projector, LLM) and AnomalyCLIP's multi-level ViT feature extraction, but departs fundamentally from all prior ZSAD methods (WinCLIP, AnomalyCLIP, AdaCLIP, AnoVL) by eliminating the CLIP text encoder entirely. Instead of constructing positive/negative text prompts to score anomaly likelihood, Anomaly-OV learns class-aware abnormality embeddings directly in visual feature space using two learnable vectors and a Look-Twice Feature Matching mechanism. The specialist-expert design—where a frozen anomaly expert selects and emphasizes suspicious visual tokens for a generalist LLM—repurposes the Q-Former and spatial pooling in a novel way, positioning the anomaly expert as a domain-specific guide rather than a standalone detector.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing the first large-scale instruction-tuning dataset (Anomaly-Instruct-125k) and evaluation benchmark (VisA-D&R) for anomaly detection and reasoning, this paper fills a critical data gap that has held back the field and provides a standard reference for future work. The demonstration that even GPT-4o fails at fine-grained anomaly description validates the need for domain-specialized models, while the specialist-expert paradigm offers a practical recipe for injecting domain knowledge into generalist MLLMs without full retraining. Key open questions include whether the anomaly expert can be strengthened without restricting the LLM's detection performance (a limitation the authors acknowledge), and whether the approach generalizes beyond the industrial and medical domains tested. The extension to 3D point clouds and medical imaging, while promising, remains preliminary.
