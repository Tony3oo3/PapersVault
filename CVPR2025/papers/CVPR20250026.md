## Problem

Current image generation models, despite producing high-quality images, fall short of the nuanced, professional demands of designers who require precise control over visual details, multi-image consistency, and complex multimodal instructions. Existing benchmarks are narrow in scope, focusing on isolated academic tasks with short prompts (avg. 8–10 words) and lack comprehensive criteria to evaluate the multifaceted capabilities needed for professional-grade image design.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250026/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark spans five task categories with escalating modality complexity: text-only prompts, single image + text, multi-image + text, text-only to multi-image output, and image(s) + text to multi-image output. Prompts average 138.68 words—roughly 10× longer than prior benchmarks—and often embed detailed visual specifications, style constraints, and in-image text requirements. Outputs range from a single generated image to variable-length image sequences (e.g., storybook pages, multi-view renders, movie shots) that must maintain identity, style, and content consistency across the set.

## Method

IDEA-Bench comprises 100 professional tasks and 275 cases organized into five categories, each evaluated with six binary (0/1) questions arranged in three hierarchical levels (basic comprehension → task completion → detail/aesthetics), where failure at a lower level automatically zeros all higher-level scores. For automated evaluation, a refined 18-task subset (IDEA-Bench-mini) uses Gemini 1.5 Pro as an MLLM judge, with per-case evaluation questions manually tuned to mitigate known MLLM sensitivities (e.g., image ordering bias) and scored three times for stability. To enable multi-image generation with models lacking native multi-output capability, GPT-4o rephrases the original prompt into per-image instructions, approximating the multi-image task with standard T2I models. The benchmark is evaluated across nine models spanning diffusion-based T2I (FLUX-1, DALL-E 3, SD3, PixArt), instruction-based editing (InstructPix2Pix, MagicBrush), unified generative (Emu2, OmniGen), and interleaved autoregressive (Anole) architectures.

## Results

On IDEA-Bench (100 tasks, 275 cases, 1,650 binary scoring items), FLUX-1 with GPT-4o prompt rephrasing achieves the highest average score of 22.48 across all five task categories, significantly outperforming all other models. Without MLLM assistance, no model surpasses an average score of 10, with Emu2 (6.81) and OmniGen (6.47) leading among general-purpose models. Specialized image editing models (MagicBrush: 19.07, InstructPix2Pix: 17.58) dominate the Image-to-Image category, while Emu2 leads in Images-to-Image (8.98). The only model capable of unrestricted multi-image input/output, Anole, scores a mere 0.48 on average, highlighting a critical gap in current generative model capabilities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLUX-1 (with MLLM rephrasing) achieves the best overall score (22.48) and dominates Text-to-Image (46.06), outperforming DALL-E 3 (24.34) and SD3 (24.04) by a wide margin. In Image-to-Image, specialized editing models MagicBrush (19.07) and InstructPix2Pix (17.58) substantially outperform general-purpose models like FLUX-1 (12.13) and DALL-E 3 (6.95), confirming their advantage in local editing tasks. For Images-to-Image, Emu2 leads (8.98) over FLUX-1 (4.89) and DALL-E 3 (5.27), demonstrating superior in-context learning for complex multi-condition tasks. OmniGen (with MLLM) achieves the second-highest overall score (15.45) and matches Pixart in I(s)2Is (21.39), but trails FLUX-1 in T2I (21.41 vs. 46.06). Anole, despite being the only model supporting unrestricted multi-image input/output, scores near zero across all categories (avg. 0.48), indicating that current interleaved generation models are far from professional-grade. The consistent finding across all comparisons is that MLLM-assisted prompt rephrasing (†) boosts scores by 5–10 points for general-purpose models, yet even the best configuration (FLUX-1†) remains far below professional design standards.

## Relation to Prior Work

IDEA-Bench extends the line of T2I evaluation benchmarks (DrawBench, GenEval, TIFA, ImagenHub, DALL-EVAL) by dramatically increasing task count (100 vs. 3–30), prompt complexity (avg. 138.68 words vs. 7–10), and modality diversity, adding multi-image input and multi-image generation dimensions absent from all prior benchmarks. It departs from traditional evaluation metrics (FID, CLIPScore) by adopting MLLM-based hierarchical binary scoring, building on the LLM-as-judge paradigm from MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena but applying it to visual generation. The benchmark also positions itself against universal generative models (Emu2, OmniGen, Anole) and specialized editing models (InstructPix2Pix, MagicBrush), explicitly targeting the gap between academic task evaluation and professional design workflows.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a stark gap: no model exceeds a score of 10/100 without MLLM prompt rephrasing, and even the best configuration (FLUX-1 + GPT-4o) reaches only 22.46, quantifying how far current generative models are from professional-grade design. A notable open question is whether MLLM-based evaluation (Gemini 1.5 Pro) can reliably replace human assessment at scale, as the paper shows MLLM scores tend to be more lenient than human scores. The finding that Anole—the only model natively supporting arbitrary multi-image input/output—scores near zero across all categories raises questions about whether current architectures for interleaved generation are fundamentally misaligned with professional multi-image tasks. Future work will need to determine whether task unification and professional quality can coexist in a single model.
