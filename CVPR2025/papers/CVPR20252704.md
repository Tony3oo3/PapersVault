## Problem

LLM-based video understanding faces a fundamental tension: long videos require representing many consecutive frames, which demands an excessive token budget and memory footprint. Existing compression methods (pooling, masking, memory banks) apply a static encoding to all frames, either discarding temporal clues by sampling fewer frames or losing fine visual details by uniform compression. The paper identifies that redundancy (repeated and answer-irrelevant frames) and correspondence (different questions require different key frames) vary per query, making a dynamic, question-aware encoding strategy necessary.

## Input / Output

The model takes T video frames at 224×224 resolution (encoded by ViT-G/14 from EVA-CLIP) and a text instruction/question (encoded by a Q-Former from InstructBLIP), producing a free-form language response via Vicuna-7B. The key I/O detail is that the token count per frame is dynamic: meaningful frames are encoded with ~40 tokens (event prototype + multi-grained spatial prototypes), while redundant frames are compressed to just 2 tokens (a text-guided token and a global content token), yielding a total token budget far below the 256 tokens/frame of uncompressed ViT features. For long-video evaluation, the model processes up to 200 input frames.

## Method

DynFocus consists of two cooperating modules. The Dynamic Event Prototype Estimation (DPE) module first applies DPC-KNN clustering temporally across T frame features to obtain L event prototypes, then uses an MLP to score each prototype's answer-relevance; the top-K selection is made differentiable via the perturbed-maximum method (solving a linear program with Gaussian perturbations), enabling end-to-end training with the LLM's autoregressive loss. The Compact Cooperative Encoding (CCE) module then applies a rod/cone-inspired split: frames flagged as meaningful (b_t=1) are encoded with 'Cones' — a concatenation of the event prototype and multi-grained spatial object prototypes (obtained by iterative DPC-KNN clustering within each frame) passed through an MLP, preserving fine visual detail; frames flagged as redundant (b_t=0) are encoded with 'Rods' — a text-grounded cross-attention over spatial prototypes condensed by average pooling to a single token plus a global content token, capturing coarse temporal dynamics. Training follows a two-stage scheme: Stage 1 aligns the visual and language spaces (freezing ViT and LLM, training only the projector MLPs), and Stage 2 fully fine-tunes the LLM and all DPE/CCE parameters on a composite instruction-tuning dataset.

## Results

On MLVU-Bench (long video), DynFocus with 32 input frames achieves 49.6% M-Avg and 4.38 G-Avg, surpassing all open-source 7B models (Video-LLaVA: 47.3/3.84; VideoChat2: 44.5/3.81; LLaMA-VID: 33.2/4.22) while using only 32 frames vs. 1 fps or 2048 frames for competitors. On VideoMME, DynFocus (16 frames) reaches 44.1% overall accuracy without subtitles, outperforming ST-LLM (37.9%) and VideoChat2 (39.5%) by 6.2 and 4.6 points respectively. On short-video MSVD-QA, DynFocus achieves 74.8% accuracy (4.0 score), matching or slightly exceeding ST-LLM (74.6%/3.9). On LV-Bench (1-hour+ videos), DynFocus attains 32.9% overall, the best among all open-source 7B models and surpassing the 34B PLLaVA (26.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MLVU-Bench, DynFocus (32 frames) achieves 49.6% M-Avg, beating Video-LLaVA (47.3%, 8 frames) by 2.3 points and VideoChat2 (44.5%, 16 frames) by 5.1 points, while using fewer input frames than most competitors; it trails only the proprietary GPT-4o (64.6%). On VideoMME, DynFocus (16 frames, 7B) reaches 44.1% overall without subtitles, surpassing ST-LLM (37.9%) by 6.2 points and VideoChat2 (39.5%) by 4.6 points, though it remains below the 34B LLaVA-NeXT (52.0%) and VILA-1.5 (59.0%). On short-video MSVD-QA, DynFocus (74.8%) marginally outperforms ST-LLM (74.6%) and LLaMA-VID (69.7%), while on ANet-QA it slightly trails ST-LLM (50.3% vs. 50.9%) but uses ~25% fewer tokens. On LV-Bench (1-hour+ videos), DynFocus (32.9%) outperforms all open-source 7B baselines including LWM (25.5%) and even the 34B PLLaVA (26.1%), approaching Gemini 1.5 Pro (33.1%). The ablation in Table 6 confirms that removing either Cones (fine-grained) or Rods (coarse) tokens causes 1.7–4.6% accuracy drops, validating the cooperative design.

## Relation to Prior Work

DynFocus extends the line of video token compression work (VideoChatGPT, Chat-UniVi, LLaMA-VID) by introducing a question-aware, dynamic encoding strategy rather than static spatial/temporal pooling. It borrows DPC-KNN clustering from Chat-UniVi for temporal event segmentation, applies the perturbed-maximum method for differentiable top-K selection to make the frame selector end-to-end trainable, and adopts LLaMA-VID's two-stage training protocol. The key conceptual departure is the cooperative dual-pathway design (Cones for fine-grained detail on selected frames, Rods for coarse temporal context on the rest), inspired by primate retinal biology, which prior video LLMs have not explicitly modeled.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a core practical bottleneck in video LLMs—the exponential token growth with video length—by showing that question-relevant frames can be dynamically identified and encoded at higher resolution while redundant frames are compressed to just 2 tokens, achieving competitive accuracy with substantially fewer tokens. Limitations include struggles with ego-centric tasks (ER on MLVU) and the added complexity of the differentiable top-K mechanism. Open questions include whether the dynamic selection generalizes to streaming/real-time video, how the method scales beyond 200 frames without further architectural changes, and whether the rod/cone analogy can be extended to audio-visual or multi-modal cooperative encoding.
