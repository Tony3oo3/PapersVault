## Problem

Single-sensor industrial anomaly detection is inherently limited: RGB cameras miss subsurface defects, infrared sensors lack geometric precision, and point clouds cannot capture color or texture anomalies. No existing dataset or method unifies all three modalities, leaving a critical gap in detecting the full spectrum of real-world industrial defects (surface, internal, and 3D geometric) in a single framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251813/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three aligned per-object inputs: a top-view RGB image (1280×960, 8-bit), a lock-in infrared grayscale image (640×480, 16-bit, 7.5–14 μm band), and a high-precision 3D point cloud (0.03 mm accuracy, 20k–100k points, acquired via dual-scan with ICP alignment). Training uses only anomaly-free samples (unsupervised setting); at test time the model outputs a single object-level binary anomaly label and a continuous anomaly score, while per-modality pixel/point-level anomaly masks are available for localization evaluation.

## Method

MulSen-TripleAD is a decision-level (late) fusion pipeline with three components. First, pretrained DINO encoders extract features from RGB and infrared images, while a pretrained PointMAE encoder processes the point cloud, yielding modality-specific feature sets. Second, a separate memory bank is constructed per modality from normal training samples following the PatchCore nearest-neighbor scoring scheme, where each test feature's anomaly score is its L2 distance to the closest memory-bank feature. Third, a Decision Gating Unit—adapted from M3DM's learnable OCSVM—fuses the three per-modality scores into a final object-level anomaly score. The key design choice is late fusion: each sensor is processed independently to avoid cross-modal noise propagation, and only the calibrated anomaly scores are combined, which the ablation in Table 5 shows yields 96.1% mean AUROC versus 91.1% (RGB alone) and 66.8% (point cloud alone).

## Results

MulSen-TripleAD achieves a mean object-level AUROC of 96.1% on the MulSen-AD dataset, substantially outperforming the best single-modality baselines: InvAD on RGB (89.2%), InvAD on Infrared (83.2%), and PatchCore(FPFH) on Point Cloud (78.4%). The method reaches perfect 1.000 AUROC on three categories (Screw, Piggy, Button Cell) and shows the largest gains on challenging categories like Screen (93.8% vs. 33.4% for the best RGB method) and Light (97.2% vs. 82.5% for the best IR method). For anomaly localization, RGB achieves the highest mean pixel-AUROC (0.982), followed by Infrared (0.970), while Point Cloud lags significantly (0.632).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MulSen-TripleAD (96.1% mean AUROC) decisively outperforms all 11 single-modality baselines across all three sensor types. Among RGB-based methods, InvAD leads at 89.2%, still trailing MulSen-TripleAD by 6.9 points; the gap widens to 12.9 points over the best IR method (InvAD, 83.2%) and 17.7 points over the best Point Cloud method (PatchCore-FPFH, 78.4%). The advantage is most dramatic on categories where a single modality is blind: on Screen, MulSen-TripleAD scores 93.8% while the best RGB method (DeSTSeg) reaches only 92.0% and the best IR method (CFLOW-AD) only 57.2%; on Light, it achieves 97.2% versus 82.5% for the best IR baseline. MulSen-TripleAD attains perfect 1.000 AUROC on Screw, Piggy, and Button Cell, matching or exceeding the best single-modality scores on those categories (e.g., PatchCore RGB already hits 1.000 on Screw and Nut, but MulSen-TripleAD generalizes this across more categories). No baseline matches MulSen-TripleAD on more than two categories, confirming that no single sensor modality is sufficient for comprehensive industrial anomaly detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-sensor industrial anomaly detection paradigm (MVTec-AD, PVEL-AD, Real3D-AD) to a multi-sensor setting by introducing the first dataset that simultaneously provides RGB, lock-in infrared, and high-precision point cloud data for the same objects. Methodologically, MulSen-TripleAD is a straightforward composition of two existing components: the PatchCore memory-bank scoring function for per-modality anomaly scores and the M3DM learnable OCSVM as a decision-level gating unit to fuse those scores. The feature extractors (DINO for images, PointMAE for point clouds) are used off-the-shelf, so the architectural novelty is limited to the multi-sensor pipeline and the gating mechanism rather than any new learning primitive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset fills a clear gap: no prior industrial AD benchmark covers all three of appearance, thermal, and geometric modalities for the same objects, making it a valuable resource for evaluating multi-sensor fusion strategies. The 96.1% AUROC over the best single sensor (~91%) confirms the complementary value of the modalities, but the relatively small gap between RGB+IR (94.8%) and the full triple fusion (96.1%) suggests that point cloud contributes limited marginal value under the current decision-level fusion scheme. Key open questions include whether feature-level or modality-level fusion could better exploit cross-modal interactions, whether the approach generalizes to few-shot or zero-shot settings, and how to achieve real-time inference for deployment on resource-constrained factory hardware.
