## Problem

Existing indirect trajectory prediction attacks (e.g., SP-Attack) rely on single-point perturbations at only the current frame, making them transient, easily filtered by the prediction model, and unable to sustain influence over earlier historical frames. Additionally, finding optimal 3D adversarial object locations in a vast continuous space is computationally challenging, and the attack is highly sensitive to small deviations from the target point and to the physical object's size and orientation.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes the victim AV's LiDAR point clouds over L_h historical frames, the tracked historical trajectory states of a parked adversarial vehicle, and candidate 3D object locations as input. It outputs a set of M optimized 3D coordinates (for physical objects like circular cardboards of ~0.1 m radius) and a sequence of n-frame target state perturbations (δx, δy, δh per frame). The output locations are designed so that when objects are physically placed, the full perception→tracking→prediction pipeline produces the desired multi-frame perturbations without requiring the victim AV to hit a precise attack point.

## Method

OMP-Attack operates in two phases. In the attack phase, it sequentially applies single-point PGD-based attacks across n consecutive historical trajectories (stepping back one frame at a time from the current frame), clipping perturbations via a 3-σ rule from sampled Gaussian distributions, and concatenating results into a multi-frame target perturbation sequence. In the location search phase, it employs Particle Swarm Optimization guided by three composite loss functions—pose loss (Euclidean distance between detected and target perturbations), heading loss (cosine similarity for directional alignment), and shape loss (Dynamic Time Warping distance for overall trajectory shape)—to efficiently navigate the 3D location space. A 'Precise Attack, Vague Optimization' strategy enhances robustness: the attack phase models the full object geometry (4 corner points), while the optimization phase treats the object as a single center point, decoupling the search from object size and orientation and tolerating up to 1 m lateral/longitudinal deviation from the nominal attack point.

## Results

On the nuScenes dataset (50 selected scenes, L_h=5, L_f=6), OMP-Attack achieves an ATD of 6.439 m, PRE of 2.393 m, and CR of 64%, substantially outperforming SP-Attack (7.212 m, 1.191 m, 42%) and Brute-Force search (8.196 m, 0.998 m, 27%). The attack endurance is maintained across 4 preceding frames with an average ATD of 6.551 m and PRE of 2.938 m, while SP-Attack degrades to 9.877 m ATD and 1.86 m PRE. In black-box transferability to AgentFormer, OMP-Attack still reduces ATD by 3.386 m and increases PRE by 1.705 m, compared to SP-Attack's 2.685 m and 1.443 m respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is SP-Attack [16], the only prior state-of-the-art indirect (LiDAR-induced) trajectory prediction attack. OMP-Attack outperforms SP-Attack on all three metrics: ATD is reduced by 47% (6.439 m vs. 7.212 m), PRE is doubled (2.393 m vs. 1.191 m), and CR is improved by 52% (64% vs. 42%). A secondary Brute-Force baseline (random location sampling) is also dominated, with OMP-Attack achieving 21% lower ATD and 140% higher PRE. In the black-box transferability evaluation against AgentFormer, OMP-Attack again surpasses SP-Attack (ATD 7.697 m vs. 8.398 m; PRE 1.705 m vs. 1.443 m), while Brute-Force becomes nearly ineffective (CR drops from 27% to 14%). The paper also shows that replacing PSO with SP-Attack's manual matching in OMP-Attack degrades performance (ATD rises to 7.588 m, PRE drops to 0.813 m), confirming that the multi-frame uncorrelated perturbations specifically benefit from swarm-intelligence optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends SP-Attack (Lou et al., USENIX Security 2024), the first physical-world trajectory prediction attack that places LiDAR-reflective objects near a parked vehicle to deceive the perception module. The key conceptual departure is replacing SP-Attack's single-frame perturbation with a sequential multi-frame strategy that applies single-point attacks across consecutive historical trajectories, and substituting the manual location-matching procedure with Particle Swarm Optimization guided by three specialized loss functions (pose, heading, shape). The 'Precise Attack, Vague Optimization' strategy—simulating full object geometry during the attack phase but reducing it to a single center point during location search—is a novel robustness mechanism not present in prior LiDAR-induced attack work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that a physical-world trajectory prediction attack can be both enduring (sustained across multiple frames rather than a single transient perturbation) and robust (insensitive to object size, orientation, and up to 1 m positional deviation), which addresses the main criticisms of prior indirect attacks. However, the evaluation is confined to 50 scenes on nuScenes with a specific AD stack, and the white-box assumption (full model access for PGD) limits immediate real-world applicability despite the transferability results. Open questions include whether multi-frame perturbations can be detected by temporal consistency filters in production tracking pipelines, and whether the approach generalizes to camera-based or multi-sensor-fusion perception systems.
