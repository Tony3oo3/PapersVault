## Problem

Zero-shot video editing methods that leverage pre-trained image diffusion models impose strict temporal consistency constraints (DDIM inversion, attention injection, optical flow), which inadvertently suppress semantic fidelity and visual quality, causing generated videos to poorly follow text editing prompts. The paper addresses how to incorporate human preference signals (reward fine-tuning) into the video editing pipeline to improve text alignment and aesthetics without degrading inter-frame coherence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250523/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source video (a sequence of n RGB frames) and a single text editing prompt as input, and produces an edited video of the same frame count. It operates at the image level using Stable Diffusion 1.5 with DDIM inversion, so each frame is processed individually through the latent diffusion pipeline. The reward tuning phase only requires a single anchor frame from the video, and the full edited video is reconstructed via feature propagation, meaning the optimization cost is independent of video length.

## Method

Align-A-Video has two core components. First, deterministic reward tuning: rather than optimizing over the expected noise distribution (which causes gradient instability and training collapse), the method fixes a single deterministic noise vector obtained via DDIM inversion of a selected anchor frame, reducing the reward loss to a simple -R(x, P) without expectation. Inversion features from the last k denoising steps are reused in the first k generation steps to lock in rough structure, while a randomized gradient truncation point l ~ Uniform(K) over the remaining steps prevents short-term dependency bias. Only the attention projection matrices are updated via LoRA, completing tuning in minutes. Second, anchor frame feature propagation: the optimized anchor frame's features are transferred to a set of keyframes through a cross-frame attention mechanism (query from the current frame, key and value from the anchor frame), and the remaining frames are filled in via distance-weighted interpolation between adjacent keyframes, preserving motion consistency from the source video.

## Results

On V2VBench, Align-A-Video achieves the best Dover score (0.761 vs. 0.708 for ControlVideo), PickScore (21.847 vs. 21.039), CLIP score (0.284), and ViCLIP score (0.265) among all compared methods. It also maintains competitive temporal consistency, with CLIP Consistency of 0.955 and EPE of −1.874, close to the best-performing TokenFlow (0.957 and −1.762). The method slightly trails ControlVideo on Aesthetic score (5.512 vs. 5.520) and TokenFlow on DINO Consistency (0.944 vs. 0.956), but no single baseline dominates across all three evaluation dimensions simultaneously.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Align-A-Video outperforms all five baselines on Dover (0.761 vs. best 0.708), PickScore (21.847 vs. 21.039), CLIP score (0.284 vs. 0.282), and ViCLIP score (0.265 vs. 0.263), demonstrating superior visual quality and semantic fidelity. Against TokenFlow and Flatten (attention feature injection methods), it trades a small margin in temporal consistency (EPE −1.874 vs. −1.762; DINO C. 0.944 vs. 0.956) for substantially better prompt alignment and visual quality. Compared to Tune-A-Video, it achieves similar semantic fidelity (CLIP 0.284 vs. 0.282) but dramatically better temporal consistency (EPE −1.874 vs. −13.667; DINO C. 0.944 vs. 0.824). Versus ControlVideo, it matches Aesthetic score (5.512 vs. 5.520) while improving Dover by 7.5% and EPE by 80% (−1.874 vs. −9.309). Text2Video-Zero is the weakest baseline overall, suffering from both low visual quality (Dover 0.596) and poor motion alignment (EPE −15.611) due to its use of random initial noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of text-guided video-to-video editing and image reward fine-tuning. It builds on PnP-diffusion for structural feature injection during denoising, adapts Tune-A-Video's spatial-temporal attention into a cross-frame mechanism for anchor-to-keyframe propagation, and borrows TokenFlow's feature correspondence for fusing remaining frames. The key departure from prior reward-based video alignment work (InstructVideo, VADER) is the shift from optimizing the full video or sparsely sampled frames to a single deterministic anchor frame, combined with a fixed-noise training strategy that contrasts with the stochastic noise sampling used in DDPO, DPOK, and AlignProp for image generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-recognized trilemma in zero-shot video editing—temporal consistency, semantic fidelity, and visual quality are typically traded off against each other—and shows that a few minutes of single-frame reward tuning with LoRA can improve all three simultaneously. Practical limitations include reliance on Stable Diffusion 1.5, evaluation on only 50 V2VBench videos, and an anchor-frame selection strategy that is not deeply analyzed. Open questions include whether the deterministic-noise trick generalizes to longer videos or more complex multi-object edits, and whether the approach transfers to newer diffusion backbones (SDXL, video-native models) where the PnP feature injection paradigm may be less applicable.
