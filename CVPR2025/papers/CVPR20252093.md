## Problem

Existing active learning methods either annotate all selected instances at a single supervision level or, in the case of hybrid full/weak approaches like APFWA, set a global proportion of weak vs. full annotations without considering instance-specific characteristics. No prior method dynamically assigns the optimal supervision level (exact class label vs. superclass label) to each individual instance based on its unique value-to-cost trade-off and diversity contribution within a fixed annotation budget.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252093/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unlabeled RGB image pool, pre-specified per-instance annotation costs (C_f for full supervision, C_w for weak supervision, e.g., C_f=1, C_w=1/2), and a per-round budget B. It outputs a trained ResNet-18 classifier with two heads (one for exact classes, one for superclasses) and, at each round, a batch of instances each tagged with its assigned supervision level. The supervision-level assignment is the key output that differs from standard AL: each instance in the batch is individually designated as full or weak, and the resulting labeled data is split into two pools (D_f, D_w) used in a two-stage training procedure.

## Method

ISO computes a Value-to-Cost Ratio (VCR) for each instance–supervision pair as v(x) = M·u(x)/C, where M is the expected per-instance model improvement estimated by incrementally adding K subsets of labeled data with a recency-weighted average, u(x) is margin-based uncertainty normalized to percentile scores, and C is the annotation cost. Batch selection then treats each instance as a vector v(x)·f̃(x) (VCR scaled by the L2-normalized feature) and greedily samples vectors with probability proportional to the squared distance to the nearest already-selected vector (a k-means++-style approximation to k-DPP), deducting the corresponding C_f or C_w from the budget until it is exhausted. This simultaneously optimizes supervision-level assignment and instance selection by maximizing the enclosed area (determinant) of the chosen vectors under the budget constraint. The classifier uses a shared ResNet-18 backbone with separate linear heads for full and weak classification, trained in two stages (weak data first, then full data) with cross-entropy loss.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, ISO achieves over 10% higher classification accuracy at round 5 compared to conventional full-supervision AL methods (which reach ~30% accuracy after 5 rounds / budget of 5000). ISO reaches the same or higher accuracy in just 3 rounds, using only three-fifths of the total budget. On CUB-200, ISO similarly outperforms all baselines across all 5 rounds. When weak supervision cost is reduced from C_w=1/2 to C_w=1/8, accuracy further improves on both datasets, confirming that cheaper weak labels amplify ISO's advantage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ISO outperforms all six baselines across every round on both CIFAR-100 and CUB-200. Against uncertainty-based methods (Margin, MaxConf, Entropy) and diversity-based methods (Coreset), ISO gains over 10% accuracy at round 5 on CIFAR-100 by leveraging weak supervision within the same budget. Against BADGE, which already combines uncertainty and diversity, ISO still leads because it additionally allocates budget between full and weak annotations per instance. The most direct comparison is with APFWA [22], the only other method using both full and weak supervision; ISO surpasses APFWA because APFWA sets a global proportion of full vs. weak labels and then applies random sampling, whereas ISO makes instance-level decisions based on per-instance VCR and diversity. Ablation studies confirm that both the instance-wise uncertainty component and the diversity-aware batch selection contribute meaningfully, with the uncertainty component being especially impactful on CUB-200 and diversity being especially impactful on CIFAR-100.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new sub-problem at the intersection of active learning and weakly supervised learning: instance-wise supervision-level optimization under a fixed annotation budget. It builds conceptually on uncertainty-based AL (Margin, MaxConf, Entropy), diversity-based batch selection (Coreset, BADGE's k-means++-inspired DPP sampling), and the single prior method combining full and weak supervision in AL (APFWA [22]). The key departure from APFWA is that rather than setting a global proportion of full vs. weak annotations per batch, ISO assigns the supervision level per instance using a value-to-cost ratio, and adapts the k-means++ seeding mechanism to a budget-constrained (rather than fixed-k) selection problem with mixed supervision vectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: in real annotation pipelines, the decision of which instances to fully label versus weakly label is currently made heuristically or via a fixed ratio, and this paper shows that instance-level optimization yields meaningful cost savings (achieving the same accuracy with ~60% of the budget when weak labels cost half). However, the validation is limited to two classification datasets with ResNet-18 and only two supervision levels (exact class vs. superclass), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to segmentation (bounding boxes vs. masks), more than two supervision levels, or larger-scale settings. The finding that the optimal full/weak ratio shifts substantially with the relative cost of weak supervision (from 60/40 to 20/80) underscores that a one-size-fits-all proportion is suboptimal, but the method's sensitivity to the VCR estimation (which requires K model retraining runs per round) could be a practical bottleneck.
