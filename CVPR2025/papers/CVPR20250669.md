## Problem

Weakly supervised spatio-temporal video grounding (WSTVG) requires localizing a subject in both space and time from only a textual caption, without frame-level annotations. While vision-language foundation models like Grounding DINO excel at zero-shot image grounding, naively extending them to video fails because they lack compositional action understanding and cannot handle dense multi-actor scenes. Prior weakly supervised STVG methods are complex, rely on additional modalities, or restrict object categories, leaving a gap for a streamlined, free-form-text approach.

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrimmed video (L frames) and a free-form descriptive caption specifying the target subject and activity, and outputs a spatio-temporal tubelet: a tracked bounding box across frames plus start and end timestamps delimiting the action. G-DINO performs per-frame detection every 5th frame at a 0.4 confidence threshold, BoTSORT links detections into tubelets, and I3D extracts clip-level video features. The final prediction is the tubelet with highest SRM attention score within the TRM-predicted temporal window.

## Method

STPro builds on Tubelet Referral Grounding (TRG), which compresses video into tubelet-level representations and uses a two-stage pipeline: a Temporal Referral Module (TRM) with reconstruction-based loss to predict action boundaries, and a Spatial Referral Module (SRM) with contrastive loss to select the correct tubelet among temporally-filtered candidates. To overcome TRG's weaknesses, STPro introduces two curriculum strategies: (1) Sub-Action Temporal Curriculum Learning (SA-TCL), which uses GPT-3.5 to decompose complex queries into ordered sub-action combinations and trains progressively from single actions to full sequences over four stages; and (2) Congestion-Guided Spatial Curriculum Learning (CG-SCL), which combines Soft-Label Filtering (using G-DINO's zero-shot labels to restrict candidates to the caption's subject type) with Congestion-Guided Sampling (ordering training samples by decreasing pairwise tubelet temporal IoU, from simple single-actor scenes to complex multi-actor scenes). This is the first application of curriculum learning to WSTVG, addressing temporal compositionality and spatial complexity as orthogonal challenges.

## Results

On HCSTVG-v1, STPro achieves 17.56 m_vIoU, 26.98 vIoU@0.3, and 12.93 vIoU@0.5, surpassing the prior state-of-the-art VCMA by 2.92%, 8.38%, and 6.81% respectively. On HCSTVG-v2, STPro reaches 19.99 m_vIoU and 14.55 vIoU@0.5, improving over its own W-GDINO baseline by 10.14% and 8.92%. On VidSTG-Declarative, STPro attains 15.52 m_vIoU and 12.69 vIoU@0.5, outperforming VCMA by 1.07% and 3.93% respectively, though it trails VCMA by 0.69% on VidSTG-Interrogative m_vIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STPro outperforms all two-stage pipelines (Grounder+LCNet, MATN+LCNet, Grounder+CPL, RAIR+CPL) by large margins on HCSTVG-v1, exceeding the best two-stage method (RAIR+CPL) by 10.68% in m_vIoU. Among single-stage methods, STPro surpasses the prior SOTA VCMA by 2.92% m_vIoU and 6.81% vIoU@0.5 on HCSTVG-v1, and by 1.07% m_vIoU and 3.93% vIoU@0.5 on VidSTG-Declarative. On VidSTG-Interrogative, STPro achieves competitive but slightly lower m_vIoU (12.56 vs. VCMA's 13.25) and vIoU@0.3 (14.95 vs. 16.74), though it still leads on vIoU@0.5 (9.29 vs. 7.66). Against its own W-GDINO baseline (naive adaptation of Grounding DINO to video), STPro gains 8.52% m_vIoU on HCSTVG-v1 and 10.14% on HCSTVG-v2, confirming that the progressive curriculum modules are essential for exploiting foundation model features in the weakly supervised setting. Older single-stage methods (WSSTG, AWGU, Vis-CTX) are outperformed by 5–8% in m_vIoU on HCSTVG-v1, highlighting the gap between earlier weakly supervised approaches and the current method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends image-based vision foundation models (specifically Grounding DINO) into the video domain for weakly supervised spatio-temporal grounding, bridging the gap between zero-shot image referral and spatio-temporal video localization. It builds on the temporal grounding loss from CNM [63] and the spatial contrastive formulation from RefCLIP [15], wrapping both in a tubelet-based pipeline (TRG) that compresses video into tracked object proposals. The key departure from prior WSTVG methods (WINNER, VCMA, Vis-CTX) is the use of a pretrained VFM as the backbone combined with curriculum learning strategies—sub-action temporal curriculum and congestion-guided spatial curriculum—that are, to the authors' knowledge, the first application of curriculum learning to WSTVG.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that foundation model features, when properly adapted with tubelet-level contrastive training and progressive difficulty scheduling, can substantially outperform purpose-built weakly supervised pipelines on spatio-temporal grounding, reducing reliance on labor-intensive frame-level annotations. However, the approach introduces practical dependencies (GPT-3.5 for query decomposition, BoTSORT for tracking, I3D for video features) and the gains on VidSTG interrogative sentences are actually negative (−0.69% m_vIoU), suggesting the curriculum strategies may not generalize uniformly across query types. Open questions include whether the congestion-based spatial curriculum transfers to other dense-scene tasks, whether the LLM-based sub-action decomposition can be replaced by learned decompositions, and how the method scales to longer or higher-resolution videos.
