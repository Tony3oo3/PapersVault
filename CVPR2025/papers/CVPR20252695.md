## Problem

Traditional rigging representations for deformable objects (skeletons, skinning weights) require extensive category-specific expert knowledge, making them impractical to design for every object category of interest. Existing automated approaches still rely on some level of category-specific prior knowledge, limiting their applicability to generic categories. This paper asks whether a low-dimensional, interpretable pose space can be discovered entirely from data for any deformable object category without any prior structural assumptions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252695/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a point cloud (n_p × 3) of a posed 3D shape as input and produces a sparse set of 8–24 feature-embedded anisotropic spheres (blobs), each parameterized by a 3D center, rotation quaternion, radius, opacity, and a d-dimensional feature vector. The pose-related parameters (position, rotation) can be directly edited by a user to re-pose the object, while identity-related parameters (radius, opacity, feature) are preserved from the source shape. The edited blobs are decoded into a re-posed 3D mesh via an occupancy field and Marching Cubes at an 8×8×8 voxel resolution.

## Method

The encoder uses a PointTransformer to extract point-wise features, then performs cross-attention between these features and a shared learnable codebook of n_b query tokens to aggregate spatially grounded blob parameters, with pose and identity parameters regressed from separate feature pathways. The decoder differentially voxelizes the blobs into a feature volume using a Gaussian kernel weighted by blob opacity and radius, augments each grid feature with point-specific embeddings to recover fine details, refines the volume through self-attention layers conditioned on the original point features, and predicts occupancy via cross-attention at queried coordinates. Training is fully unsupervised: two frames from the same deformation sequence are sampled, with identity parameters predicted from one frame and pose parameters from the other, so the decoder learns to reconstruct the target pose while preserving source identity. A binary cross-entropy occupancy loss combined with a voxelization weight regularization drives end-to-end optimization, and a near-surface sampling schedule (ramping from 0 to 0.8) stabilizes blob initialization before capturing high-frequency surface details.

## Results

CANOR achieves the best IoU on 4 of 5 datasets: 0.937 on DeformingThings4D, 0.960 on FaMoS, 0.860 on Fish, and 0.903 on Refrigerator, while NeuralDeformationGraph edges ahead on Eyeglasses (0.791 vs. 0.770). CANOR also attains the lowest Chamfer L1 and L2 distances across all five categories, e.g., CD1 of 0.017 and CD2 of 0.011 on DeformingThings4D. The largest margin over the strongest baseline (NeuralDeformationGraph) appears on Fish, where CANOR improves IoU from 0.686 to 0.860 (+0.174).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CANOR outperforms all three baselines on Chamfer L1 and L2 across every dataset, and leads on IoU for 4 of 5 categories. Against NeuralDeformationGraph (the strongest baseline), CANOR gains +0.062 IoU on DeformingThings4D (0.937 vs. 0.875), +0.160 on Fish (0.860 vs. 0.686), and +0.034 on Refrigerator (0.903 vs. 0.869), but trails by −0.021 on Eyeglasses (0.770 vs. 0.791). Against SkeRig, the advantage is most pronounced on Eyeglasses (+0.226 IoU) and Refrigerator (+0.100 IoU), where SkeRig's skeleton optimization produces over-simplified or overly complex structures. KeypointDeformer is consistently the weakest baseline, with IoU as low as 0.452 on Eyeglasses and 0.499 on Fish, because its cage-based deformation cannot handle topological changes. The paper attributes NDG's weaker non-rigid performance to overfitting training shapes and lacking learned deformation priors, while CANOR's data-driven decoder generalizes more naturally.

## Relation to Prior Work

CANOR carves new ground by repurposing the mid-level 'blob' concept from 2D scene representation (BlobGAN) into 3D feature-embedded anisotropic spheres, combined with a PointTransformer encoder and a cross-attention codebook mechanism for sparse pose encoding. It departs from prior neural rigging work (KeypointDeformer, NeuralDeformationGraph, SkeRig) by eliminating all category-specific priors—no skeleton topology, no hand-defined keypoints, no dense correspondences—relying instead on a fully data-driven, unsupervised discovery of the pose space. The training strategy of cross-pose identity/pose disentanglement (predicting identity from one frame, pose from another) is a practical trick borrowed from disentanglement literature but applied here to make the representation editable without paired edit data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The category-agnostic framing lowers the barrier to rigging arbitrary object categories, demonstrated practically with iPhone-scanned clay monsters requiring no 3D modeling expertise. However, the method still requires multiple animated sequences per category (at least 5 held out for testing), the number of blobs must be manually tuned (8–24), and training takes ~7 days on 2 GPUs, limiting immediate scalability. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to categories with significant topological changes, whether real-time inference is feasible, and how performance degrades with very few training sequences. The near-negligible ablation of anisotropic blobs (IoU 0.937→0.934) versus the critical role of identity conditioning (0.937→0.853) suggests the spatial encoding of pose is more important than the geometric shape of individual blobs, a finding that could inform simpler future representations.
