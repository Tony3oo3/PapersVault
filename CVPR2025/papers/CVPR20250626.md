## Problem

Existing sparse geometric solvers for event camera egomotion estimation assume rotational displacements are known (typically from an IMU) and can only recover translational parameters. Recovering full-DoF egomotion—both angular and linear velocities—using a sparse geometric solver without extra sensor measurements or motion priors has not been investigated.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250626/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes asynchronous event streams (normalized image coordinates and timestamps) grouped into line clusters via Hough transform, along with normal flow computed from time-surface maps. The minimum configuration requires 8 events per line (versus 5 when rotation is known) to constrain the 3 additional rotational DoF. Outputs are the 3-DoF angular velocity, the 3-DoF linear velocity (recovered up to an unknown scale but with correct sign), and Plücker-coordinate line structure parameters, all under a locally constant velocity assumption over short intervals (~0.3–0.5 s).

## Method

The paper proposes two dual geometric formulations for full-DoF estimation: an incidence relation on eventail manifolds (line-line intersection) and a novel coplanarity relation among event-induced plane normals derived from normal flow. Rotation is decoupled from translation by minimizing the smallest eigenvalue of a matrix (6×6 for incidence, 3×3 for coplanarity) that depends only on the unknown angular velocity; translation is then recovered via a linear N-point solver. For optimization, the Adam optimizer is employed with a cascade of two rotation parameterizations: a first-order (small-angle) approximation with a data-compression trick for fast initialization, followed by refinement using the exact Rodrigues rotation. The paper also provides a theoretical analysis of pure-rotation degeneracies and shows how to identify and handle them.

## Results

On noise-free synthetic data (M=5 lines, N=100 events/line), the cascade rotation parametrization achieves the best accuracy: IncBat+cascad yields median ε_ang = 1.6×10⁻⁴ and median ε_lin = 1.8×10⁻³ with 98.9% success rate at threshold 0.01, while CopBat+cascad achieves ε_ang = 9.3×10⁻⁵ and ε_lin = 1.1×10⁻³ at 17.1 ms runtime. On real-world VECtor sequences, median angular velocity errors range from 0.195 to 0.236 and linear velocity direction errors from 17.5° to 25.1° across three sequences. The first-order approximated rotation alone is significantly less accurate (ε_ang ~1.4–1.9×10⁻²) but runs in ~17 ms, while exact rotation is more accurate but slower (33–49 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not include a direct head-to-head comparison against prior methods, as it is the first to estimate full-DoF egomotion (both angular and linear velocities) from events alone without an IMU. Prior geometric solvers [12, 13] recover only linear velocity under the assumption that rotation is provided by an IMU, making direct numerical comparison inapplicable. The internal comparisons show that the incidence formulation (IncBat) outperforms the coplanarity formulation (CopBat) at low event counts (<100 events) and low noise levels, while the two become comparable at higher event counts or larger noise. Among rotation parametrizations, the cascade (approx → exact) consistently achieves the best accuracy–efficiency trade-off: it matches or exceeds the exact-only method in accuracy while the CopBat+cascad variant runs in 17.1 ms versus 32.7 ms for CopBat+exact. The first-order approximation alone is insufficient for high-accuracy recovery (SR1 drops to 22–32%), confirming that the exact rotation step in the cascade is essential for final precision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the eventail manifold geometry introduced by Gao et al. [12, 13], which provided sparse solvers for linear velocity estimation under the assumption that angular velocity is known (typically from an IMU). The key departure is treating rotation as an unknown, which increases the minimal configuration from 5 to 8 events and requires a new optimization formulation. The authors also introduce a novel coplanarity relation based on normal flow that is dual to the incidence relation, and they repurpose rotation-translation decoupling and eigenvalue minimization techniques from frame-based camera work [26, 19] into the event-camera setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first sparse geometric solver to recover full-DoF egomotion (both angular and linear velocity) from events alone, removing the IMU dependency that has been a standard assumption in prior line-based event solvers. The practical impact is enabling event-only visual odometry in scenarios where IMU is unavailable or unreliable, though the method still requires identifiable line structures and a locally constant velocity assumption. Open questions include how the approach generalizes to scenes without dominant line structures, whether the scale ambiguity in translation can be resolved without additional sensors, and how the constant-velocity assumption degrades under more dynamic motion.
