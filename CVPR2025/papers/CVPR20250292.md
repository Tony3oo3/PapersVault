## Problem

Concept-bottleneck visual recognition systems rely on visual concepts to improve interpretability and accuracy, but existing approaches either use labor-intensive manual concept definitions or one-shot LLM generation that produces concepts lacking discriminative power and failing to capture interactions between classes. No prior method iteratively refines a concept library using feedback from a vision-language model to resolve specific class confusions over time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250292/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ESCHER takes as input a set of open-vocabulary class categories, a dataset of RGB images (labels optional), a pretrained VLM (e.g., CLIP ViT-L/14), and an optional adapter weight matrix. It outputs an evolved library of natural-language concept descriptions per class and the corresponding adapter weights after T iterations. The concept library is a growing set of textual descriptors (e.g., 'metallic green head and neck') that the VLM scores against each image, and the adapter is a linear projection from concept scores to class logits.

## Method

ESCHER performs alternating maximization over adapter weights and concept library: it first fits a concept-bottleneck model (zero-shot LLM-instantiated or learned linear adapter) on the current concept set, then identifies confused class pairs via heuristics (top-k confusion, Pearson correlation, or agglomerative clustering) on the VLM's score matrix. A VLM critic computes contrastive similarity scores between images and their associated concepts, and a history bank stores past evolutions and confusion trajectories for each class pair. The LLM is prompted with this history (analogous to execution traces in program synthesis) to generate new discriminative concepts targeting only the top-K most confused pairs, with an exponential decay penalty (γ) to avoid repeatedly targeting the same pair. This loop requires no human annotations and is agnostic to the underlying CBM architecture, plugging into zero-shot, few-shot, or fine-tuned baselines.

## Results

In the fine-tuned setting, LM4CV+ESCHER achieves top-1 accuracy improvements over LM4CV on all seven datasets, with the largest gains on CUB-200-2011 (63.26% → 83.17%, +19.91 pts) and Stanford Cars (86.84% → 93.76%, +6.92 pts). In the zero-shot setting, CbD+ESCHER consistently outperforms both CbD and raw CLIP (ViT-L/14), e.g., reaching 81.37% on Oxford Flowers vs. 79.41% (CbD) and 74.51% (CLIP). In the few-shot setting, results are mixed: LaBO+ESCHER shows modest gains on Stanford Cars (+0.92 pts at 8-shot, +1.08 pts at 16-shot) and CUB, but slight drops on CIFAR-100 and Food-101.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LM4CV [30] (fine-tuned adapter), ESCHER wins on all seven datasets, with the most dramatic improvement on CUB-200-2011 (+19.91 pts) and Stanford Cars (+6.92 pts), while gains on already-high-accuracy datasets like Food-101 are marginal (+0.13 pts). Against LaBO [31] (few-shot adapter), results are mixed: ESCHER provides modest gains on Stanford Cars (+0.92 at 8-shot, +1.08 at 16-shot) and CUB, but slightly underperforms on CIFAR-100 (−0.61 at 8-shot) and Food-101 (−0.92 at 8-shot), which the authors attribute to poor calibration and overfitting in the few-shot regime. Against CbD [19] (zero-shot adapter), ESCHER improves top-1 accuracy on all six evaluated datasets, with the largest relative gains on Oxford Flowers (+1.96 pts) and Stanford Cars (+1.49 pts). Against raw CLIP (ViT-L/14) [23] in the zero-shot setting, CbD+ESCHER outperforms CLIP on five of six datasets (e.g., 77.80% vs. 73.30% on CIFAR-100, 81.37% vs. 74.51% on Oxford Flowers), though CLIP retains a slight edge on CUB-200-2011 (64.83% vs. 63.33%).

## Relation to Prior Work

ESCHER extends the concept-bottleneck visual recognition line of work (Menon & Vondrick 2023, Yan et al. 2023, Yang et al. 2023), where an LLM generates a static set of visual concepts and a VLM scores them for classification. The key departure is borrowing the library learning paradigm from program synthesis (DreamCoder, Grayeli et al. 2024) to make concept discovery iterative and feedback-driven: a VLM acts as a critic providing contrastive similarity scores, and the LLM uses history-conditioned in-context learning to propose increasingly discriminative concepts. Unlike prior mutation-based approaches (Chiquier et al. 2024) that evolve concepts per-class in isolation, ESCHER reasons jointly over all classes and focuses only on underperforming pairs, making it scalable to larger label sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ESCHER provides a plug-and-play, annotation-free meta-algorithm that can be layered on top of existing concept-bottleneck classifiers across zero-shot, few-shot, and fine-tuned regimes, which is practically valuable as the CBM ecosystem continues to grow. The ablation confirming that the VLM critic's feedback is essential (rather than simply generating more concepts) validates the library learning framing and suggests that structured, feedback-guided concept search is a meaningful axis of improvement. Open questions include whether the approach can be extended to more complex visual reasoning tasks, how to incorporate limited labeled data into the evolution loop for few-shot settings (where results are currently mixed), and whether the method scales to very large label spaces where the O(|Y|²) confusion computation becomes prohibitive.
