## Problem

Existing VQA benchmarks for large multimodal models predominantly evaluate 2D spatial relationships or limited 2.5D depth cues, leaving a critical gap in assessing full 6D spatial reasoning (3D position + 3D orientation). Real-world image datasets exacerbate this gap because over 70% of objects cluster into a single predominant orientation, making it impossible to comprehensively diagnose 6D reasoning capabilities. No prior benchmark systematically evaluates LMMs across the full spectrum from basic object recognition to complex 6D spatial relationships and collision prediction.

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs a single rendered scene image (synthetic, realistic-style with HDRI environment maps and natural lighting) with a text question. The model is prompted to output both a final answer and intermediate reasoning steps, following the Vlmevalkit evaluation format. The test set comprises 1,000 images with approximately 28,000 questions distributed across 7 types (single-object, multi-object, 2D spatial, occlusion, 3D pose, collision, 6D spatial), and a separate real-world subset (L4-Pose-Real) uses SUN-RGBD scenes with red bounding boxes around target objects to reduce localization ambiguity.

## Method

Spatial457 is a synthetic benchmark built on the CLEVR/Super-CLEVR generation pipeline, extended with 21 textured 3D mesh objects across 5 categories rendered in 224 outdoor HDRI scenes to ensure realistic appearance while maintaining full 6D ground-truth annotations (2D location, 3D world coordinates, and pose direction). The key design principle is unbiased attribute distribution: all 3D orientations are uniformly sampled, unlike real datasets where poses are heavily skewed. The benchmark defines four core capabilities (multi-object, 2D location, 3D location, 3D orientation) and composes them into five difficulty levels with seven question types, introducing new 6D spatial relationship questions (using a 3D Relate operation from a target object's perspective) and collision prediction questions (requiring motion-based inference from orientation and position). A novel Relative Performance Dropping Rate (RPDR) metric quantifies the accuracy decline when each new spatial factor is introduced, enabling per-capability diagnosis. The unbiased design also enables systematic bias analysis via Coefficient of Variation on predicted attribute distributions, revealing that models over-predict certain colors and poses despite balanced ground truth.

## Results

On Spatial457, GPT-4o achieves the highest accuracy among LMMs, scoring 74.46% on single-object (L1) but dropping to 37.01% on 6D spatial (L5) questions — a 50% relative decline. GeminiPro-1.5 follows closely (73.26% → 39.36%), while open-source models like LLaVA-v1.5-7B remain near random (44.87% → 23.86%). The 3D-aware neural symbolic model PO3D-VQA substantially outperforms all LMMs (86.46% → 71.06%), and human performance (89.97% → 79.94%) reveals a widening gap as task complexity increases, with the L5 6D spatial gap reaching ~43 points between humans and the best LMM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among API-based LMMs, GPT-4o leads on L1–L4 tasks (e.g., 74.46% vs. 73.26% for GeminiPro-1.5 on L1; 48.40% vs. 47.65% on L4-Occlusion), while GeminiPro-1.5 edges ahead on L5 collision (41.19% vs. 38.41%) and 6D spatial (39.36% vs. 37.01%) questions. Claude 3.5 Sonnet shows the steepest drop on occlusion (30.84%, well below GPT-4o's 48.40%), indicating particular weakness in 3D location reasoning. Among open-source models, InternVL2-8B is the strongest on 2D spatial (57.40%, slightly above GPT-4o's 56.14%) but trails significantly on 3D tasks; Qwen2-VL-7B is competitive on L1–L3 but drops sharply on L4-Occlusion (27.87%). The 3D-aware neural symbolic model PO3D-VQA outperforms all LMMs by 20–35 points across levels, establishing an upper bound that highlights how far current LMMs are from structured 3D reasoning. On the real-world L4-Pose-Real extension (SUN-RGBD), GeminiPro-1.5 achieves the highest accuracy (40.50%) but all models remain far below human performance (92.64%), with Claude 3.5 Sonnet showing the lowest prediction bias (CV = 0.374).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the synthetic VQA benchmark lineage from CLEVR and Super-CLEVR (2D spatial reasoning) and Super-CLEVR-3D/PO3D-VQA (3D location or 3D orientation in isolation) to a unified 6D spatial reasoning framework that requires models to jointly reason about 3D position AND 3D orientation. It reuses the scene generation pipeline and object attribute system from Super-CLEVR but introduces new 6D spatial relationship and collision prediction question types that have no direct predecessor. The cascading 5-level difficulty structure and the RPDR metric are novel diagnostic tools that go beyond the flat accuracy reporting of prior benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first comprehensive 6D spatial reasoning benchmark for LMMs, Spatial457 provides a structured diagnostic tool that will likely become a standard evaluation for spatial reasoning capabilities in vision-language models. The finding that even GPT-4o drops from ~74% to ~37% accuracy across difficulty levels, with a widening gap to human performance (~80%), quantifies a critical limitation for applications in robotics, AR, and autonomous navigation. The bias analysis revealing systematic over-prediction of 'front' pose and 'yellow' color despite balanced ground truth raises concerns about model reliability in real-world deployment. A key limitation is that the real-world extension is restricted to L4-Pose questions due to annotation constraints, leaving the full 6D evaluation confined to synthetic scenes.
