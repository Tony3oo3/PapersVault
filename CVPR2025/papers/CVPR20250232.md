## Problem

State-of-the-art 3D generation methods either rely on slow iterative optimization (e.g., DreamFusion) or require large-scale 3D datasets that are scarce and low-quality (e.g., ~70% of Objaverse-XL has missing textures or poor aesthetics). The paper asks whether the powerful 3D priors already embedded in pretrained 2D diffusion models can be directly repurposed to produce complete, textured 3D assets without extensive 3D-specific training data or architectural modifications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250232/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts text prompts, single RGB images, or existing 3D meshes as input and produces textured 3D meshes as output. The critical intermediate representation is the '3D Bundle Image': a 2×4 tiled grid containing four orthographic views (90° apart in azimuth, 5° elevation) at 512×512 resolution, where each view contributes both an RGB image and a corresponding normal map. For enhancement and editing tasks, an input mesh is first rendered into this bundle format, which then serves as a ControlNet conditioning signal alongside a text prompt.

## Method

Kiss3DGen fine-tunes the Flux DiT model via LoRA (rank 128) on 147k curated Objaverse objects to generate 3D Bundle Images from text captions produced by GPT-4V. The key design insight is that the bundle is a standard 2D image, so the pretrained diffusion model's generative priors are preserved without any architectural changes, and the DiT's attention blocks naturally capture the long-range dependencies between views and between RGB/normal modalities. At inference, the generated bundle is fed to ISOMER for mesh optimization (optionally initialized via InstantMesh/LRM for robustness). A ControlNet extension (Kiss3DGen-ControlNet) adds conditional branches (Tile, Canny, Normal) with tunable strength λ₁ and active-step fraction λ₂, enabling 3D enhancement, text-guided editing, and image-to-3D refinement by treating the rendered bundle of a coarse mesh as the control signal.

## Results

On the text-to-3D task (GSO dataset, 100 objects), Kiss3DGen-Base achieves a CLIP score of 0.837, Quality of 2.700, and Aesthetic of 1.800, outperforming all baselines. On image-to-3D (200 objects), it attains the best Chamfer Distance (0.149), F-Score (0.769), PSNR (20.348), SSIM (0.902), and LPIPS (0.116). Notably, even a reduced 50K-sample model (Ours-50K) remains competitive, achieving CLIP 0.804 and Quality 2.716 on text-to-3D, demonstrating strong data efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Kiss3DGen-Base outperforms all seven baselines across every reported metric. Against MVDream (text-to-multi-view), it improves CLIP from 0.809 to 0.844 and Quality from 2.509 to 3.248, even surpassing real rendered data on Quality (3.248 vs. 3.138) and Aesthetic (1.94 vs. 1.911). In text-to-3D, it beats 3DTopia by +0.143 CLIP and +0.555 Quality, Direct2.5 by +0.064 CLIP and +0.542 Quality, and Hunyuan3D-1.0 by +0.045 CLIP and +0.183 Quality. In image-to-3D, it achieves the lowest Chamfer Distance (0.149 vs. 0.153 for Hunyuan3D-1.0, 0.178 for CraftsMan, 0.217 for Unique3D) and highest PSNR (20.348 vs. 16.652 for Hunyuan3D-1.0). In qualitative 3D editing/enhancement comparisons, Kiss3DGen-ControlNet maintains better geometric consistency with the input mesh and aligns more closely with text instructions than MVEdit.

## Relation to Prior Work

Kiss3DGen positions itself at the intersection of multi-view diffusion generation and feedforward 3D reconstruction, departing from both optimization-based SDS methods (DreamFusion, ProlificDreamer) and direct 3D latent diffusion approaches (Craftsman, Direct3D) that require large-scale 3D training data. Its core conceptual move is the '3D Bundle Image'—a tiled composite of multi-view RGB and normal maps treated as a single 2D image—allowing a pretrained DiT (Flux) to be fine-tuned via LoRA without architectural modification, in contrast to prior multi-view methods (MVDream, Wonder3D) that introduce switchers or modified attention to handle separate RGB/normal modalities. The reconstruction stage leverages existing LRM/ISOMER pipelines, and the ControlNet extension for editing and enhancement follows the standard conditional-diffusion recipe, making the overall framework a deliberate composition of off-the-shelf components rather than a new generative architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that high-quality 3D asset generation (text-to-3D, image-to-3D, editing, enhancement) can be achieved with a single LoRA fine-tune on a 2D diffusion model, requiring only ~147K curated objects (and still performing well at 50K) versus the 320K–500K+ datasets used by competing direct-3D methods. This lowers the barrier to 3D generation and makes the pipeline composable with the broader diffusion ecosystem (ControlNet variants, SDEdit, etc.). Open questions remain around the geometry representation (normal maps at 512×512, four fixed views), the dependence on ISOMER's optimization-based refinement for final mesh quality, and whether the approach scales to higher-resolution or more complex scene-level generation.
