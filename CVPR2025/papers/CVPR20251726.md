## Problem

Diffusion and flow-matching models rely on predetermined noise schedules that are identical for all prompts, regardless of the complexity of the target image. This one-size-fits-all approach forces simple images to undergo unnecessary denoising steps while potentially under-allocating steps to complex scenes. The paper asks whether both the number of denoising steps and the noise level at each step can be adaptively determined per instance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251726/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a Gaussian noise sample as input, and produces a generated RGB image along with a dynamically determined noise schedule specifying the diffusion time at each step. Unlike standard diffusion inference where the step count is fixed in advance (e.g., 28 steps for SD3), TPDM terminates the denoising process adaptively, typically using around 13–16 steps instead of 28. The TPM is a plug-and-play module that requires no modification to the base DiT architecture, making it compatible with any flow-matching model.

## Method

TPDM adds a lightweight Time Prediction Module (TPM) to a frozen diffusion model. At each denoising step, the TPM concatenates latent features from the first and last DiT layers, applies FiLM-style adaptive normalization for current-time awareness, and predicts a decay rate r as a Beta distribution (with α, β > 1 enforced via reparameterization to ensure unimodality). The next diffusion time is computed as t_n = r_n · t_{n-1}, monotonically decreasing toward zero. Training uses PPO with the entire schedule treated as a single action (following RLOO), where the reward is the ImageReward score of the final image, discounted by γ^{N−n} and averaged over N steps to penalize excessive denoising. Only the TPM parameters are updated (~200 training steps, batch 256), preserving the base model's generative capacity while learning an instance-specific schedule.

## Results

TPDM achieves approximately 50% fewer denoising steps on average (15.28 vs. 28 for SD3-Medium, 15.22 vs. 28 for SD3.5-Large, 13.57 vs. 28 for FLUX.1-dev) while matching or exceeding baseline quality. On SD3-Medium, TPDM reaches an HPS v2.1 of 29.59 and Aesthetic Score of 5.445 with only 15.28 steps, surpassing the 28-step baseline (29.12 HPS, 5.433 Aesthetic). In a user study, TPDM-SD3-Medium achieves a 47.25% win rate versus 26.58% for SD3-Medium at 28 steps and 16.40% at 15 steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SD3-Medium, TPDM wins on all human-preference metrics (HPS +0.47, Aesthetic +0.012, Pick Score +0.21) while using only 15.28 steps versus 28, though FID is slightly worse (25.26 vs. 25.00). Against SD3.5-Large, TPDM improves Aesthetic Score by +0.038 and Pick Score by +0.19 over the 15-step baseline, and matches the 28-step Pick Score (22.81), but trails the 28-step HPS by 0.21 (30.64 vs. 30.85) and FID by 1.19. Against FLUX.1-dev, TPDM achieves the best FID (28.98 vs. 29.09) and CLIP-T (0.314 vs. 0.308) with fewer steps, and the highest Aesthetic Score (5.685 vs. 5.622), but loses on HPS v2.1 (30.77 vs. 31.94) and Pick Score (22.94 vs. 23.03) compared to the 28-step baseline. Overall, TPDM consistently improves human-preference-aligned metrics and efficiency, with the largest gains observed on the SD3-Medium architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on flow-matching diffusion models (Lipman et al., 2023) and the RLOO formulation (Ahmadian et al., 2024) for treating an entire sequence of decisions as a single action in policy optimization. It departs from prior noise-schedule optimization work (Xia et al. 2023, Sabour et al. 2024, Wang et al. 2023), which either fine-tunes a fixed schedule offline or searches for one universal schedule, by instead predicting the next diffusion time per-instance at each step via a lightweight plug-and-play module. Unlike prior RLHF-for-diffusion methods (DDPO, DPOK, Diffusion-DPO) that treat latents as actions, TPDM treats the diffusion time itself as the action, making the approach orthogonal to and composable with latent-space alignment methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a ~50% reduction in denoising steps with equal or slightly better human-preference scores (HPS, Pick Score, Aesthetic) across SD3-Medium, SD3.5-Large, and FLUX.1-dev makes this directly useful for deployment where inference cost matters. The plug-and-play design (freeze base model, train only a small TPM in ~200 steps) lowers the barrier to adoption. Open questions include whether jointly updating the base model with the TPM would yield further gains, whether the Beta-distribution parameterization is optimal, and whether the approach transfers cleanly to video or 3D generation where the step budget is even more critical.
