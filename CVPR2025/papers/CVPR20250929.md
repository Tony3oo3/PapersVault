## Problem

Existing weakly supervised 3D instance segmentation methods that use bounding box supervision assume accurate, compact boxes, but in practice annotated boxes inevitably contain scaling, translation, and rotation errors that cause significant performance degradation. This paper is the first to study 3D instance segmentation under deliberately inaccurate 'sketchy' bounding boxes, addressing the gap between idealized annotation assumptions and real-world annotation noise.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250929/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud (xyz coordinates, RGB, surface normals) and sketchy bounding boxes generated by perturbing ground-truth boxes with 5% scaling, 5% translation, and 5° rotation (individually or combined into four increasing levels of sketchiness S1–S4). It outputs per-point instance masks, semantic class predictions, and predicted instance bounding boxes (plus auxiliary core boxes). Features are extracted at superpoint granularity via a 3D U-Net backbone before being consumed by both the pseudo labeler and the segmentator.

## Method

Sketchy-3DIS jointly trains two components: (1) an adaptive box-to-point pseudo labeler that converts rough sketchy boxes into point-level instance labels by filtering background points via a combined cosine-feature and exponential-coordinate similarity score, and resolving points in overlapping box regions with an MLP that learns point-to-box assignment from reliable (non-overlapping) points; and (2) a coarse-to-fine query-based instance segmentator where learnable instance queries first attend globally to scene features, then hierarchically refine through Multi-level Attention Blocks that interact with reliable regions (defined by IoU between predicted and mask-derived boxes) and core regions (scaled-down predicted boxes). The two components are trained jointly via bilateral Hungarian matching between generated pseudo labels and predicted instances, with losses on classification, mask (BCE + Dice), box (L1), and core-box (MSE) predictions.

## Results

On ScanNetV2 validation with accurate bounding boxes (S0), Sketchy-3DIS achieves AP50=68.8 and AP25=83.6, matching or exceeding all prior box-supervised methods on AP25 (vs. BSNet+SPFormer 83.4, GaPro+SPFormer 79.9). Under sketchy bounding boxes (S1), the method retains AP50=65.8 and AP25=83.1, while GaPro+SPFormer drops to 53.5/72.2 and Box2Mask to 52.4/67.5. On S3DIS Area 5 with S0 supervision, Sketchy-3DIS reaches AP50=69.1, surpassing the fully supervised ISBNet baseline (65.8) by +3.3 and the best box-supervised competitor BSNet+ISBNet (64.3) by +4.8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under accurate boxes (S0) on ScanNetV2, Sketchy-3DIS (AP50=68.8, AP25=83.6) trails BSNet+SPFormer (72.7/83.4) on AP50 by 3.9 points but edges it out on AP25 (83.6 vs. 83.4); it also slightly underperforms GaPro+SPFormer on AP50 (68.8 vs. 70.4) while leading on AP25 (83.6 vs. 79.9). The decisive advantage appears under sketchy boxes (S1): Sketchy-3DIS (65.8/83.1) outperforms GaPro+SPFormer (53.5/72.2) by +12.3 AP50 and +10.9 AP25, and Box2Mask (52.4/67.5) by +13.4 AP50 and +15.6 AP25, demonstrating far greater robustness to annotation noise. On S3DIS Area 5 with S0, Sketchy-3DIS (AP50=69.1) beats BSNet+ISBNet (64.3) by +4.8, GaPro+ISBNet (61.2) by +7.9, and even exceeds the fully supervised ISBNet (65.8) by +3.3. Under S1 on S3DIS, Sketchy-3DIS retains AP50=64.6, still above the fully supervised ISBNet. The method does not require multi-modal 2D priors (unlike CIP-WPIS) or synthesized overlapping scenes (unlike BSNet), yet matches or surpasses them on the primary metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the bounding-box supervised 3D instance segmentation line (Box2Mask, WISGP, GaPro, BSNet, CIP-WPIS) by introducing the 'sketchy bounding box' setting, where annotations are deliberately perturbed with scaling, translation, and rotation to mimic real-world annotation noise. Architecturally, it builds directly on SPFormer and ISBNet as query-based segmentation backbones, adding a 3D U-Net feature extractor, an adaptive box-to-point pseudo labeler that resolves overlapping-box ambiguity via learned point-to-box similarity, and a coarse-to-fine segmentator with a Multi-level Attention Block that hierarchically attends to scene-level, coarse-region, and core-region features. The key conceptual departure from prior box-supervised methods is the joint training of the pseudo labeler and segmentator, rather than treating pseudo label generation as a fixed preprocessing step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: real-world 3D bounding box annotations are rarely perfectly tight, and prior methods (especially GaPro) degrade sharply under even modest perturbations, as the paper demonstrates. By showing that a weakly supervised pipeline with noisy boxes can match or exceed fully supervised baselines (e.g., +3.3 AP50 over ISBNet on S3DIS), the work lowers the annotation-quality bar for deploying 3D instance segmentation in practice. The main open question, acknowledged by the authors, is what happens under 'immensely inaccurate' boxes where performance degrades severely; extending robustness to much larger perturbations or to fully unstructured annotations remains unresolved.
