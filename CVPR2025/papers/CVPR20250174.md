## Problem

Reconstructing dynamic 3D scenes from TV show videos is uniquely difficult because of actor occlusions, cluttered stages, small-baseline frontal cameras, and abrupt shot changes that break temporal continuity. Existing 4D reconstruction methods (template-based, template-free, or feed-forward) fail to produce consistent human-scene alignment or coherent actor deformation under these conditions, and no prior method handles the full pipeline of stage plus multiple actors from shot-changing monocular video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250174/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes a shot-changing TV show video clip (monocular, with abrupt camera cuts) plus additional background images gathered from other episodes of the same sitcom to fill in unobserved stage regions. It produces a compositional 3D Gaussian scene where the stage and each actor are stored as separate Gaussian sets, enabling independent editing (relocation, insertion, deletion, pose manipulation) and novel-viewpoint rendering. Camera poses are recovered via GLOMAP SfM on actor-masked frames, and monocular depth from Depth Pro is aligned to the SfM coordinate system to guide both stage reconstruction and actor placement.

## Method

ShowMak3r represents both the stage and each actor with 3D Gaussian Splatting, compositing them as a union of Gaussian sets. The 3DLocator module places SMPL-parameterized actors onto the reconstructed stage by optimizing a global scale and translation that minimize Huber loss between projected SMPL vertex depths and aligned monocular depth, avoiding assumptions about foot-ground contact or actor identity across shots. ShotMatcher then associates actors across shot boundaries via pairwise Euclidean distance matching with a threshold, and fills occluded or absent frames through linear interpolation and bidirectional low-pass filtering of SMPL parameters. For actor appearance, a foreground mask is derived by comparing rendered stage and actor depths, and an SDS loss with person-specific textual inversion hallucinates unobserved back regions. A face-fitting network (MLP with positional encoding on Gaussian center and time) predicts per-Gaussian color and opacity residuals to capture dynamic facial expressions without moving the underlying Gaussians.

## Results

On the Sitcoms3D dataset (TBBT living room, 165 training / 10 test images), ShowMak3r achieves the best stage reconstruction with PSNR 19.65, SSIM 0.66, and LPIPS 0.49, outperforming 3DGS (19.21), GS-W (19.35), FSGS (19.34), and the original Sitcoms3D/NeRF-W baseline (18.81). For facial expression reconstruction, the face-fitting network reaches PSNR 24.34 and SSIM 0.84, substantially exceeding ExAvatar (PSNR 20.17, SSIM 0.64), though with a slightly higher LPIPS (0.28 vs. 0.25). Qualitative comparisons against HUGS, Shape-of-Motion, and MonST3R show ShowMak3r produces more accurate actor positioning and more robust multi-person reconstruction under shot changes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For stage reconstruction, ShowMak3r outperforms all four baselines on PSNR and SSIM: it gains +0.30 dB over GS-W (19.35), +0.31 dB over FSGS (19.34), +0.44 dB over vanilla 3DGS (19.21), and +0.84 dB over the original Sitcoms3D NeRF-W pipeline (18.81), while matching the best LPIPS (0.49) shared with 3DGS and FSGS. For face reconstruction, ShowMak3r's face-fitting network dramatically outperforms ExAvatar in PSNR (+4.17 dB, 24.34 vs. 20.17) and SSIM (+0.20, 0.84 vs. 0.64), though ExAvatar achieves a marginally lower LPIPS (0.25 vs. 0.28), suggesting slightly better perceptual similarity in some cases. In qualitative comparisons, HUGS fails to correctly position actors on the stage due to its foot-contact assumption, while Shape-of-Motion and MonST3R struggle to deform dynamic actors under moving cameras even on single-shot clips; ShowMak3r handles both multi-person scenes and shot changes more robustly. The 3DLocator ablation (Table 3) confirms that removing the depth-guided positioning increases MTED from 1.19 to 1.99 and MPED from 0.10 to 0.24, validating the module's contribution to cross-shot actor consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

ShowMak3r builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting as its core scene representation, a Gaussian deformation network for dynamic facial expression fitting, and SDS loss with textual inversion for hallucinating unobserved actor regions. It departs from prior 4D reconstruction methods (NeRF-based, multi-view synchronized) and human radiance field approaches (HUGS, NeuMan) that assume continuous viewpoints, foot-ground contact, or single-person scenarios. The closest prior work, Sitcoms3D, reconstructed TV show stages with NeRF-W and optimized SMPL parameters across adjacent shots, but lacked per-actor texture, required identical humans in neighboring shots, and did not handle shot changes. ShowMak3r's key conceptual departure is treating shot boundaries as a first-class problem (via ShotMatcher) and using monocular depth guidance to position actors without ground-plane assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first pipeline to comprehensively reconstruct both the stage and multiple dynamic actors from shot-changing TV show videos, enabling practical editing applications (actor relocation, insertion, deletion, pose manipulation) that mirror production control room workflows. The compositional design—separate Gaussian sets per actor plus stage—makes the scene inherently editable, which is a practical advantage over monolithic 4D representations. Limitations include reliance on off-the-shelf pose estimation that degrades under severe occlusion, and depth estimation that struggles with close-up shots. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond sitcoms to other shot-heavy content (news, sports) and how to enable explicit, user-driven actor editing beyond SMPL parameter manipulation.
