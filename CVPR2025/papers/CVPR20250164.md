## Problem

Existing backdoor watermark-based dataset ownership verification (BW-DOV) methods implant targeted backdoors that alter prediction results of watermarked models, compromising dataset functionality and enabling malicious manipulation. Even recent 'harmless' variants (UBW, Domain-Watermark) still produce prediction inconsistencies between watermarked and benign models. The paper asks whether a backdoor watermark can be designed that preserves all original prediction outputs while still enabling reliable ownership verification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250164/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During watermarking, the method takes a subset of RGB images from the original dataset (e.g., CIFAR-10 at 32×32, Sub-ImageNet-12) along with a patch pattern, and produces a watermarked dataset where selected samples carry nearly imperceptible modifications while retaining their original labels. During verification, the protector queries a suspicious model (black-box) for prediction probability vectors on m benign samples and their patched counterparts, then outputs a p-value from a pairwise T-test and a confidence score ΔE = E_b − E_w indicating whether the model was trained on the protected dataset.

## Method

EntropyMark introduces an entropy-based constraint that increases prediction confidence (reduces entropy) on watermarked samples without changing the predicted class, formalized as a dispersion loss L_d = L_ce + β·H(S_θ(x)). The watermarking uses an iterative clean-label framework: a watermark generator is pre-trained via steganography, then fine-tuned over R rounds where each round trains a surrogate model on the current watermarked dataset, fine-tunes the generator using gradient matching (aligning gradients of a verification loss on patched samples with a training loss on modified samples to avoid backpropagating through the surrogate's training loop), and adaptively selects 'decisive' samples with the smallest cross-entropy gradient norm. Verification exploits the resulting entropy inconsistency: a pairwise T-test compares entropy of predictions on benign versus patched samples, rejecting the null hypothesis (no entropy difference) to confirm ownership. This design makes the backdoor sample-specific and resistant to fine-tuning and pruning defenses.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, EntropyMark achieves a watermark success rate (WSR) of 87.00% and benign accuracy (BA) of 87.79%, while attaining prediction consistency indices C_b = 0.8936 and C_w = 0.8695 — the highest among all backdoor watermark methods. On Sub-ImageNet-12, it reaches WSR = 69.62%, BA = 71.35%, C_b = 0.7510, and C_w = 0.7471. The key advantage is harmlessness: all other methods exhibit C_w < 0.25 (severe prediction inconsistency on watermarked samples), whereas EntropyMark preserves prediction results with C_w > 0.74 on both datasets. For ownership verification, EntropyMark achieves a p-value of 0.0017 on watermarked models versus 1.0000 on benign models, confirming reliable detection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EntropyMark's primary advantage over all six baselines is harmlessness: it achieves C_w = 0.8695 on CIFAR-10 and 0.7471 on Sub-ImageNet-12, whereas every other method has C_w ≤ 0.7821 (Sleeper Agent) and most fall below 0.25, indicating severe prediction inconsistency on watermarked samples. Among clean-label methods, UBW-C [24] is the closest competitor with C_w = 0.7639 on CIFAR-10, but EntropyMark still leads by 0.106 in C_w and 0.018 in C_b. On Sub-ImageNet-12, Domain-Watermark [10] collapses dramatically (BA drops to 47.69%, C_b = 0.4750), while EntropyMark maintains C_b = 0.7510 and C_w = 0.7471. In terms of WSR, dirty-label methods (BadNets, Blend) achieve 100% but at the cost of extreme harm (C_w ≈ 0.04–0.11); EntropyMark's 87.00% WSR on CIFAR-10 is competitive with other clean-label baselines (86.32–94.45%) while being the only method that preserves prediction consistency. For ownership verification, EntropyMark yields a p-value of 0.0017 on watermarked models versus 1.0000 on benign models, a gap that no other method's verification protocol explicitly quantifies in the same framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the backdoor watermark-based dataset ownership verification (BW-DOV) framework established by Li et al. [26], which first proposed implanting backdoors into open-source datasets for copyright tracing. It departs from prior BW-DOV methods (UBW [24], Domain-Watermark [10]) that alter prediction results—either to wrong labels or random outputs—by introducing an entropy-based constraint that preserves the correct prediction while increasing confidence. The iterative clean-label watermarking framework borrows the gradient-matching optimization from Sleeper Agent [33] and the steganography-style generator pre-training from StegaStamp [35], repurposing them for a result-preserving backdoor objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The entropy-based formulation offers a principled way to protect open-source dataset copyrights without degrading dataset functionality, addressing a practical gap where prior methods inevitably altered model predictions. However, the approach remains constrained by the clean-label setting (WSR is bounded by BA), and verification reliability degrades with excessive iteration rounds or high watermarking rates, suggesting a narrow operational window. Open questions include whether the entropy signal remains detectable at scale (e.g., full ImageNet-1000), against more sophisticated defenses beyond fine-tuning and pruning, and whether the pairwise T-test generalizes to non-i.i.d. verification sets.
