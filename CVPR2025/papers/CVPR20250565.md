## Problem

Existing continual test-time adaptive object detection (CTTA-OD) methods prioritize adaptation effectiveness while largely ignoring computational efficiency, which is critical for resource-constrained deployment scenarios such as autonomous driving and UAVs. The paper identifies that indiscriminate adaptation of all source-learned features is wasteful: certain domain-sensitive feature channels actually degrade target-domain performance, yet current methods allocate equal computational resources to every channel during online adaptation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250565/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled target images in mini-batches of 4, streamed sequentially across 10 adaptation rounds to simulate continual domain shift, along with pre-computed source-domain feature statistics (per-layer mean feature maps and average RoI features). Only the learnable BN scaling factors (γ) are updated during adaptation while all other detector parameters remain frozen. The output is standard Faster R-CNN detections (bounding boxes with category labels) evaluated via mAP@50, with FLOPs tracked separately for forward and backward passes to quantify the efficiency gain from pruning.

## Method

The core mechanism is sensitivity-guided channel pruning operating on BN scaling factors: each channel's sensitivity to domain shift is quantified at both the image level (L1 discrepancy between target feature maps and pre-computed source statistics) and the instance level (L1 discrepancy of RoI-aligned features for predicted foreground objects), yielding a per-channel weight ω. A weighted L1 sparsity loss (ω·γ) is applied to selectively suppress high-sensitivity channels, and channels with γ below a threshold t are hard-pruned from the computation graph, yielding quadratic FLOPs reduction in intermediate convolutions. To prevent irreversible early pruning, a stochastic reactivation mechanism samples each pruned channel from a Bernoulli(r) distribution and resets its γ to the pretrained value when triggered. The total loss combines KL-divergence-based feature distribution alignment (image-level and per-class instance-level) with the weighted sparsity term, gated by a pruning-ratio threshold p to prevent over-pruning.

## Results

On Cityscapes→Cityscapes-C, the method achieves 11.4% average mAP with 449.6G total FLOPs, outperforming the SOTA WHW (9.1% mAP, 508.2G FLOPs) by +2.3 mAP while reducing FLOPs by 11.6%. On UAVDT→UAVDT-C, it reaches 14.3% mAP with 442.4G FLOPs (+2.3 mAP, −12.9% FLOPs vs. WHW). On Cityscapes→ACDC, it achieves 24.2% mAP with 452.8G FLOPs (+0.6 mAP, −10.9% FLOPs vs. WHW). Compared to the direct-test baseline, the method boosts average mAP by 6.9, 7.9, and 2.2 points across the three benchmarks while incurring the lowest additional FLOPs among all CTTA-OD methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all five CTTA-OD baselines on all three benchmarks in both mAP and FLOPs. Against the most recent SOTA WHW [42], it gains +2.3 mAP on Cityscapes-C (11.4 vs. 9.1), +2.3 on UAVDT-C (14.3 vs. 12.0), and +0.6 on ACDC (24.2 vs. 23.6), while reducing total FLOPs by 11.6%, 12.9%, and 10.9% respectively. ActMAD [31] achieves the second-best mAP but requires 752.2G FLOPs (67% more than Ours) due to its full-parameter backward pass. STFAR [4] and TeST [35] incur the highest computational costs (1002.9G and 1964.2G FLOPs) because their mean-teacher frameworks require inference and updates over a larger parameter set. DUA [30] matches the direct-test FLOPs (250.7G) since it only updates BN statistics, but suffers from severe BN disruption in multi-round adaptation, yielding only marginal gains over the baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of continual test-time adaptive object detection (CTTA-OD) and structured network pruning. Prior CTTA-OD methods (DUA, STFAR, ActMAD, WHW, TeST) focus exclusively on improving adaptation effectiveness through feature alignment, self-training, or normalization updates, while treating the full source-pretrained network as a fixed substrate. The paper departs from this by introducing a model-structure perspective: it borrows the BN-scaling-factor mechanism from Network Slimming [29] and the structured channel pruning paradigm, but makes pruning domain-sensitivity-aware (via image-level and instance-level discrepancy weights) and dynamic (via stochastic reactivation), rather than applying a static, domain-agnostic sparsity criterion. The adaptation loss itself (KL-divergence-based distribution alignment) is inherited directly from ActMAD and WHW, so the novelty is orthogonal to the adaptation objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is clear: in resource-constrained deployment (autonomous driving, UAV), reducing inference and adaptation FLOPs by ~12% while simultaneously improving mAP over SOTA is a meaningful engineering win. However, the method is tightly coupled to BN-based CNN backbones (ResNet-18 + Faster R-CNN), and the authors themselves flag that extension to YOLO, DETR, or BN-free architectures remains open. A more subtle, class- or region-aware pruning strategy is also identified as future work, since the current sensitivity metric treats all channels uniformly within a layer. The quadratic FLOPs savings from joint input/output channel pruning in intermediate conv layers is a known property of structured pruning, so the efficiency mechanism is not itself novel, but its integration into an online adaptation loop with dynamic reactivation is a useful contribution.
