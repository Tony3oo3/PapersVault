## Problem

Existing diffusion-based SR methods freeze the pre-trained diffusion model and rely solely on enhancing the encoder to extract degradation-robust features from LQ inputs. However, any errors in these extracted features are misinterpreted by the frozen diffusion model as image structures, leading to unfaithful restorations. The paper addresses this by arguing that the diffusion prior itself must be 'unleashed' (fine-tuned) to learn to identify useful information from degraded inputs, and that the encoder and diffusion model must be jointly optimized to benefit from their interplay.

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-quality RGB image and a natural-language image description (generated by LLAVA during training) as inputs, and produces a high-quality super-resolved image. LQ features are extracted from the penultimate layer of the SDXL VAE encoder (512 channels) rather than the final 8-channel bottleneck, preserving more structural detail. At inference, the model uses 20 Euler sampling steps with a classifier-free guidance scale of 5, and the text description provides auxiliary structural guidance through cross-attention.

## Method

FaithDiff jointly fine-tunes three components of an SDXL-based pipeline: the VAE encoder (as LQ feature extractor), a lightweight alignment module, and the diffusion model. The alignment module applies 3×3 convolutions to both the LQ features and the noisy latent, concatenates them, passes through two Transformer blocks for cross-interaction, and produces aligned features via a linear layer with a residual connection to the noisy latent. Training proceeds in two stages: first pre-training the alignment module alone (6,000 iterations), then end-to-end joint fine-tuning of all three modules (40,000 iterations) with an L1 noise-prediction loss. This unified optimization eliminates the need for ControlNet-style adapters, reducing inference time to 2.55 s per 1024×1024 image versus 7–11 s for competing methods, while the jointly trained encoder learns to extract features that coincide with the progressive diffusion process.

## Results

On DIV2K-Val with severe degradation (Level-III), FaithDiff achieves the best perceptual quality among all methods: MUSIQ 66.28, CLIPIQA+ 0.5275, PaQ-2-PiQ 75.05, and MANIQA 0.5686, while GAN-based methods (Real-ESRGAN, BSRGAN) lead in PSNR/SSIM but trail significantly on perceptual metrics. On the real-world RealPhoto60 benchmark, FaithDiff attains MUSIQ 72.74, CLIPIQA+ 0.5932, PaQ-2-PiQ 75.27, and MANIQA 0.6527, outperforming the next-best method (SeeSR) by at least 1.44 in MUSIQ. For faithfulness, OCR recognition on ICDAR 2024 Occluded RoadText reaches 36.45% precision and 46.74% recall, surpassing SUPIR (31.78%/41.57%) by ~5 points. Inference takes only 2.55 s per 1024×1024 image with 20 diffusion steps, roughly 3–4× faster than competing diffusion-based SR methods that require 50 steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FaithDiff consistently outperforms all diffusion-based baselines (DiffBIR, PASD, SeeSR, DreamClear, CCSR, OSEDiff, SUPIR, StableSR) on the perceptual metrics MUSIQ, CLIPIQA+, PaQ-2-PiQ, and MANIQA across all three degradation levels on both DIV2K-Val and LSDIR-Val, with the largest margins under severe degradation (e.g., +0.82 MUSIQ over SeeSR and +0.82 over SUPIR on DIV2K Level-III). Against GAN-based methods (Real-ESRGAN, BSRGAN), FaithDiff trades PSNR/SSIM (losing by ~1–2 dB) for substantially better perceptual quality (e.g., +6.17 MUSIQ over Real-ESRGAN on Level-III). On the real-world RealPhoto60 benchmark, FaithDiff leads in MUSIQ (72.74 vs. 70.80 for SeeSR), CLIPIQA+ (0.5932 vs. 0.5720 for OSEDiff), and MANIQA (0.6527 vs. 0.6166 for DreamClear), though SUPIR achieves a slightly lower NIQE (3.37 vs. 4.07). For structural faithfulness, FaithDiff's OCR precision (36.45%) and recall (46.74%) exceed SUPIR (31.78%/41.57%) and SeeSR (30.07%/33.09%) by 4–13 percentage points. In runtime, FaithDiff (2.55 s, 20 steps) is 3–4× faster than DiffBIR (7.93 s), PASD (7.31 s), SeeSR (10.31 s), and SUPIR (11.44 s), all of which use 50 steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on SDXL's latent diffusion architecture but departs fundamentally from prior diffusion-based SR methods (DiffBIR, SUPIR, PASD, SeeSR) that freeze the pre-trained diffusion model and rely solely on enhancing the encoder to extract degradation-robust features. The key conceptual shift is 'unleashing' the diffusion prior by jointly fine-tuning the VAE encoder and diffusion model within a unified optimization framework, rather than treating them as independent modules. It also eliminates the need for ControlNet-style adapters by introducing a lightweight alignment module that directly incorporates LQ features into the noisy latent, enabling full-step diffusion training without one-step constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete failure mode in diffusion-based SR: errors in extracted LQ features are misinterpreted by frozen diffusion models as genuine image structures, corrupting the restoration. The joint optimization strategy and alignment module offer a principled solution that could inform future work on any task where a generative prior must be conditioned on degraded inputs. Limitations include the reliance on LLaVA-generated text descriptions as auxiliary conditioning (adding a dependency and potential failure point), and the method still trails GAN-based approaches on pixel-fidelity metrics (PSNR/SSIM), suggesting a trade-off between perceptual quality and structural accuracy that remains unresolved. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video SR or other restoration tasks, and whether the text conditioning can be made optional.
