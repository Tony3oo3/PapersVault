## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting and its variants assume pinhole-camera, all-in-focus inputs, so they degrade or fail on real-world images with shallow depth-of-field (bokeh blur). Existing 3D-GS deblurring methods (BAGS, Deblurring 3DGS) model blur kernels but cannot render controllable DoF effects, while NeRF-based lens models (DoF-NeRF, LensNeRF) suffer from slow training and rendering. The paper addresses the gap of an efficient 3D-GS framework that both reconstructs sharp geometry from defocused inputs and enables interactive, controllable DoF rendering.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images with shallow DoF and their COLMAP SfM camera poses as input. It produces a 3D-GS scene representation whose per-image learnable lens parameters (aperture size A and focus distance F) can be adjusted at inference time to render novel views with arbitrary DoF, modify the CoC shape (circular, pentagonal, hexagonal), or set A=0 for fully sharp all-in-focus output. A per-scene fine-tuned monocular depth network also produces dense depth priors used during training, and the final scene yields depth maps via RaDe-GS rasterization.

## Method

DoF-Gaussian replaces the standard pinhole projection with a geometric-optics lens model where the circle-of-confusion diameter is r(d)=A|1/F−1/d|, with A and F as per-image learnable scalars. A custom CUDA kernel (Algorithm 1) simulates defocus by redistributing each rendered pixel's color over its neighborhood, weighted by a differentiable tanh-based confuse function that smoothly transitions between in-focus and out-of-focus contributions. To recover accurate geometry from bokeh-blurred inputs, the method fine-tunes a monocular depth network per scene using sparse COLMAP depth with a silog loss, then regularizes the Gaussian-rendered depth map against these per-scene depth priors. A defocus-to-focus adaptation stage (triggered after 10k iterations) reweights the reconstruction and depth losses via a sigmoid step function of |1/F−1/d| and pixel-wise reweights the aperture, shifting emphasis to sharp focal regions to compensate for the gap between idealized and real CoC. The full training objective combines L1+D-SSIM reconstruction, depth L2, and normal-consistency losses, built on Mip-Splatting with RaDe-GS depth rasterization.

## Results

On the Deblur-NeRF dataset (10 scenes), DoF-Gaussian achieves an average PSNR of 23.97 dB, SSIM of 0.755, and LPIPS of 0.093, outperforming the strongest 3D-GS baseline BAGS (23.95 dB / 0.754 / 0.094) and all NeRF-based methods. On the authors' synthetic refocusing dataset, DoF-Gaussian reaches 28.70 dB PSNR and 0.095 LPIPS, substantially surpassing DoF-NeRF (25.59 dB / 0.207 LPIPS), while also learning more accurate aperture (δ_A = 0.126 vs 0.196) and focus distance (δ_F = 0.079 vs 0.256). On all-in-focus inputs, DoF-Gaussian matches or slightly exceeds Mip-Splatting (27.81 vs 27.05 dB PSNR), confirming the lens model does not degrade general reconstruction quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DoF-Gaussian wins on 8/10 scenes in PSNR against Deblurring 3DGS (losing only on Cake and Cups by <0.3 dB) and on 9/10 against Deblur-NeRF (losing only on Cups). Against the closest 3D-GS competitor BAGS, DoF-Gaussian wins on 6/10 scenes in PSNR but trails on Cake (26.83 vs 27.21), Coral (20.37 vs 20.53), Cups (26.01 vs 26.27), and Tools (28.29 vs 28.60); however, DoF-Gaussian consistently achieves lower LPIPS (better perceptual quality) across all 10 scenes. Against the NeRF-based baselines (DoF-NeRF, DP-NeRF, Deblur-NeRF), DoF-Gaussian dominates on every scene in PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, with the largest margin over DoF-NeRF (~1.2 dB average PSNR gain). On the synthetic refocusing dataset, DoF-Gaussian outperforms DoF-NeRF by 3.11 dB in PSNR and halves the LPIPS (0.095 vs 0.207), while also reducing parameter estimation errors (δ_A: 0.126 vs 0.196; δ_F: 0.079 vs 0.256). On all-in-focus inputs, DoF-Gaussian slightly edges out Mip-Splatting in PSNR (27.81 vs 27.05) and SSIM (0.902 vs 0.893) but is marginally worse in LPIPS (0.117 vs 0.115), confirming no quality regression under standard conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting paradigm (built on Mip-Splatting) by replacing the standard pinhole camera model with a physically grounded lens-based imaging model, drawing on geometric optics principles for the circle-of-confusion formulation. The closest prior work is DoF-NeRF, which achieves controllable DoF in NeRF but suffers from slow training and rendering; BAGS and Deblurring 3DGS handle blur in 3D-GS but lack a lens model and thus cannot offer controllable refocusing. The differentiable confuse function is borrowed from StereFo, and robust depth rendering leverages RaDe-GS, while the per-scene depth prior strategy is inspired by NeRFingMVS's use of sparse SfM supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By bringing controllable DoF to the real-time 3D-GS setting, this work unlocks interactive applications (cinematic camera moves, dynamic refocusing, CoC shape modification) that were previously limited to slower NeRF-based pipelines. The per-scene depth prior adjustment and defocus-to-focus adaptation address a practical gap: shallow DoF inputs corrupt geometry estimation, and the idealized CoC model diverges from real camera optics. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to dynamic scenes, whether the single-scene training assumption can be relaxed, and how it interacts with other non-ideal conditions (sparse views, varied lighting) that the authors flag as future work.
