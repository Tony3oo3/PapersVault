## Problem

Existing graph learning benchmarks evaluate GNNs on text-attributed or unimodal graphs but systematically overlook visual information, despite its critical role in real-world applications like recommendation and information retrieval. No standardized benchmark exists that integrates high-resolution visual node features alongside textual features and graph structure to assess how well graph learning algorithms exploit multimodal semantics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251523/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each dataset provides a graph where every node carries a text feature (product title, book description, or Wikipedia entity description) and a high-resolution visual feature (product photo, book cover, or entity image), with edges encoding co-purchase, co-reading, or knowledge graph relations. For link prediction, each positive edge is ranked against 150 hard negatives (generated via HeaRT for Amazon datasets, random sampling for Goodreads-LP); for KGC, the 1vsAll scoring protocol is used for VISTA while hard negatives from CoDEx are used for MoSE. Validation and test edges are explicitly removed from the graph to prevent leakage.

## Method

MM-GRAPH standardizes an evaluation framework combining five GNN architectures (GCN, SAGE, MMGCN, MGAT, BUDDY), two multimodal KGEs (MoSE, VISTA), and four feature encoder pairings (CLIP, T5+ViT, ImageBind, T5+DINOv2) across seven datasets spanning link prediction, node classification, and knowledge graph completion. The key empirical finding is that conventional GNNs like SAGE consistently outperform purpose-built multimodal GNNs (MMGCN, MGAT), which perform modality-specific message passing and only fuse at the prediction layer, suggesting insufficient cross-modal integration. Aligned encoders (CLIP, ImageBind) that map modalities into a shared embedding space outperform unaligned combinations (T5+ViT, T5+DINOv2) across most datasets, though T5+DINOv2 excels on Goodreads-LP due to its OCR capability for text-heavy book covers. Multimodal features yield over 6% improvement over text-only or image-only baselines, confirming the value of visual information in graph representations.

## Results

On link prediction, SAGE with CLIP achieves the best MRR on Amazon-Sports (33.83) and Amazon-Cloth (24.58), while MGAT with DINOv2+T5 dominates Goodreads-LP (MRR 74.89). On node classification, SAGE+CLIP reaches 87.10% accuracy on Ele-Fashion and 83.30% on Goodreads-NC, though MLP+ImageBind slightly edges out on Ele-Fashion (88.73%). For knowledge graph completion, MoSE with T5+DINOv2 achieves the best MRR on MM-CoDEx-s (40.07), while VISTA with ImageBind leads on MM-CoDEx-m (MRR 23.20). Across all tasks, aligned encoders (CLIP, ImageBind) consistently outperform unaligned combinations (T5+ViT, T5+DINOv2), and multimodal features yield >6% improvement over text-only baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAGE [13] emerges as the strongest conventional GNN, achieving the best MRR on Amazon-Sports (33.83 with CLIP) and Amazon-Cloth (24.58 with CLIP), and outperforming all multimodal GNNs on these datasets. MMGCN [43] and MGAT [39], the two multimodal GNNs adapted from recommendation systems, consistently underperform SAGE on Amazon-Sports and Amazon-Cloth (e.g., MMGCN+CLIP: 31.96 vs. SAGE+CLIP: 33.83 MRR on Amazon-Sports), though MGAT dramatically dominates Goodreads-LP (MRR ~74–75) where no other method exceeds 45. GCN [19] trails SAGE by 1–3 MRR points across all three link prediction datasets. BUDDY [2] is competitive with SAGE on Goodreads-LP (43.25 vs. 44.10 MRR with CLIP) but lags on the Amazon datasets. For KGC, MoSE [49] achieves the best MRR on MM-CoDEx-s (40.07 with T5+DINOv2) but struggles on MM-CoDEx-m (6.97 best), while VISTA [20] is more consistent across both KGC datasets (23.20 MRR on MM-CoDEx-m with ImageBind). The MLP baseline confirms that graph structure is essential: it scores 11.03 MRR on Goodreads-LP versus 44.10 for SAGE, a 4x gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-attributed graph benchmarking line (CS-TAG, GL-Bench) by adding a visual modality, and builds directly on CoDEx for its knowledge graph completion datasets by augmenting entities with crawled Wikipedia images. It departs from existing multimodal knowledge graph datasets (VTKG, MMKG) which the authors identify as suffering from outdated image URLs and poor quality, instead curating fresh visual features from open-source sources. The evaluation framework standardizes both conventional GNNs (GCN, SAGE, BUDDY) and multimodal-specific architectures (MMGCN, MGAT) alongside KGEs (MoSE, VISTA), with feature encoders spanning aligned (CLIP, ImageBind) and unaligned (T5+ViT, T5+DINOv2) strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MM-GRAPH fills a clear gap as the first standardized benchmark explicitly incorporating visual node features across link prediction, node classification, and KGC, providing a common evaluation ground for a rapidly growing but fragmented area. The finding that multimodal GNNs with late-fusion architectures underperform simple feature-concatenation GNNs raises important open questions about where and how to fuse modalities in graph message passing. The paper also highlights that feature alignment (CLIP/ImageBind) is more impactful than GNN architecture choice, and that visual features yield >6% gains over text alone, motivating further research into audio/video integration and more sophisticated mid-level fusion strategies.
