## Problem

Multi-dataset training for pose estimation faces two unresolved challenges: keypoint heterogeneity across datasets (different skeleton types, non-overlapping keypoints, and domain gaps even for identically named joints) and label sparsity (only one skeleton type is annotated per image, leaving others unsupervised). Existing MDT methods rely on shared backbones or multi-head architectures that do not address skeleton heterogeneity or the resulting supervision deficit, leading to degraded performance on underrepresented or anatomically distant domains.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image at 256×192 resolution and simultaneously produces 2D keypoint heatmaps for all six training datasets (COCO, AIC, MPII, AP-10K, APT-36K, COCO-WholeBody) with their respective keypoint counts. During training, a shared embedding module also outputs a normalized keypoint embedding map (F×H×W) that is matched against nonparametric prototypes via cosine similarity to generate prototype-based keypoint predictions; this embedding pathway is discarded at inference, so the model's computational cost matches the multi-head baseline.

## Method

PoseBH replaces dataset-specific keypoint heads with a unified embedding space: a shared embedding module regresses a normalized keypoint embedding map, and keypoint predictions are obtained by cosine-similarity matching against nonparametric prototypes (J×M×F) that are updated via momentum with Sinkhorn-Knopp online clustering for in-class prototype assignment. A pixel-prototype contrastive loss (PPC + PPD) with cross-dataset K-means negatives trains the embedding to be discriminative across skeleton types. To address label sparsity, a cross-type self-supervision mechanism aligns the two prediction modalities (keypoint head vs. prototype head): predictions passing dual confidence and RMS-distance thresholds are combined via weighted average to produce reliable pseudo-heatmaps for unlabeled keypoints, eliminating the need for teacher-student distillation or input augmentation. Training follows a three-phase progressive schedule (freeze heads → train heads+embedding → full training with CSS loss) to stabilize prototype learning.

## Results

On general human pose benchmarks (COCO, AIC, MPII), PoseBH achieves 77.3 AP, 32.1 AP, and 93.2 PCKh respectively (avg 67.5), surpassing ViTPose++ (avg 67.2) and UniHCP (avg 66.8). On whole-body and animal domains, the gains are substantially larger: 75.0 AP on AP-10K, 87.2 AP on APT-36K, and 57.9 AP on COCO-WholeBody (avg 73.4), outperforming ViTPose++ by 4.3 points on average. With the ViT-H backbone, PoseBH reaches 79.5 AP on COCO validation, matching or exceeding single-dataset SOTA methods like PCT (79.3 AP). Transfer to InterHand2.6M and 3DPW yields 98.6 PCK and 83.6 AP respectively, surpassing the ViTPose++ baseline by 0.9 AUC and 1.9 AP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against UniHCP (shared-backbone, task-query design), PoseBH wins decisively on all evaluated datasets: +0.7 AP on COCO, +0.5 AP on AIC, +0.0 PCKh on MPII in Table 1, and a massive +18.5 AP on APT-36K and +37.8 AP on COCO-WholeBody in Table 2, confirming that UniHCP's parameter-sharing strategy fails to handle skeleton heterogeneity. Against ViTPose++ (multi-head baseline), PoseBH achieves consistent but modest gains on standard human pose (+0.3 AP COCO, +0.5 AP AIC, +0.1 PCKh MPII) while delivering large improvements on discrepant domains (+0.9 AP AP-10K, +11.2 AP APT-36K, +0.8 AP COCO-WholeBody). On the SOTA comparison (Table 3), PoseBH with ViT-H matches PCT-Swin-H (79.3 AP) and slightly exceeds it (79.5 AP) on COCO, while with ViT-B it trails PCT-Swin-B (77.7 AP) by 0.4 AP but surpasses all other single-dataset methods. On transfer tasks (Table 4), PoseBH outperforms both UniHCP and ViTPose++ on InterHand2.6M (98.6 PCK, 87.1 AUC, 3.70 EPE) and 3DPW (83.6 AP, 87.1 AR), with the largest margin on 3DPW (+1.9 AP over ViTPose++), demonstrating that the learned keypoint embeddings generalize effectively to unseen skeleton types.

## Relation to Prior Work

PoseBH builds directly on ViTPose++'s multi-head ViT architecture as its backbone and head structure, and adapts the nonparametric prototype learning framework from ProtoSeg (Zhou et al., CVPR 2022) from dense semantic segmentation to the sparse keypoint prediction setting. It departs from prior MDT pose methods (UniHCP's parameter sharing, ACAE's dataset-dependent autoencoders, category-agnostic relabeling) by explicitly modeling skeleton heterogeneity through a unified embedding space with per-keypoint prototypes, rather than forcing all datasets into a shared output dimension. The cross-type self-supervision mechanism is novel in using the dot-product relationship between embeddings and prototypes as a second prediction modality for mutual filtering, avoiding the teacher-student or augmentation overhead typical of semi-supervised pose estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in deploying pose estimators across diverse domains (human, animal, hand, whole-body) without retraining from scratch, and the nonparametric prototype design means new skeleton types can be added with only few-shot adaptation rather than architectural changes. The 11.2 AP gain on APT-36K and the successful transfer to 3DPW and InterHand2.6M suggest the learned embedding space captures genuinely transferable geometric structure. Key limitations include the requirement for at least few-shot samples per new skeleton (ruling out zero-shot), and the method is currently restricted to 2D; extending to 3D manifolds (e.g., axis-angle representations) is identified as an open direction.
