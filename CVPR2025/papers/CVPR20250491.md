## Problem

Existing VideoQA models uniformly sample a fixed number of frames, missing critical evidence that is distributed nonuniformly across long videos, and they lack the ability to temporally localize such evidence within the full video context. This makes them unable to perform multi-step reasoning over temporally scattered visual cues needed for complex questions (e.g., causal or temporal ordering). The paper addresses this by proposing a framework to automatically synthesize temporally grounded evidence chains and distill them into a VLM.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250491/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a long-form video (processed as 96 frames for TimeChat-based or variable frames for LLaVA-Video-based variants) and a text question as input. It outputs a structured temporal evidence chain—multiple time-interval segments (e.g., 'From 4.7 to 6.25 seconds, the girl waves at the dog') with textual descriptions of question-relevant visual content—followed by the final answer. At inference, the model can be prompted to generate either the full evidence chain plus answer or just the answer directly.

## Method

ViTED converts standard QA (Q→A) into evidence-based reasoning (Q→Evidence+A) through a three-stage pipeline. First, a hierarchical evidence pool is generated by non-uniformly segmenting the video across 5 granularity levels (from 1/16-length clips to the full video) and prompting LLaMA-3.2-Vision-11B to describe question-relevant content in each segment. Second, a beam-search algorithm using LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct searches over the pool to find the optimal sequence of evidence segments that maximizes the likelihood of the correct answer, then summarizes the chain into a coherent chain-of-thought with explicit temporal references. Third, the model is trained via curriculum learning: Stage-1 performs standard instruction tuning (Q→A), and Stage-2 distills the evidence chains by training the VLM to predict both evidence tokens and answer tokens, using LoRA fine-tuning on TimeChat or LLaVA-Video backbones.

## Results

ViTED built on LLaVA-Video (7B) achieves SOTA on 4 of 6 VideoQA benchmarks: 84.13% on NExT-QA, 68.23% on STAR, 60.95% on MVBench, and 56.39% on CinePile, surpassing LLaVA-OneVision (7B) by +4.73, +1.99, +5.04, and +9.97 points respectively. On the temporal grounding benchmark NExT-GQA, ViTED achieves IoP@0.5 of 41.2 and Acc@GQA of 27.6, outperforming the GPT-4-driven agent LLoVi (38.0 / 26.8) and all other baselines. The TimeChat-based variant shows the largest relative gains over its base model, improving NExT-GQA from 0.00 to 27.61 and CinePile from 13.67 to 58.98, demonstrating that temporal evidence distillation is particularly impactful for models lacking native grounding capability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest SOTA Video LLM, LLaVA-OneVision (7B), ViTED-LLaVA-Video wins on all five overlapping benchmarks: CinePile 56.39 vs 46.42, PerceptionTest 67.50 vs 57.1, NExT-QA 84.13 vs 79.4, STAR 68.23 vs 66.24, and MVBench 60.95 vs 55.91. On NExT-GQA, ViTED-TimeChat (IoP@0.5 = 41.2, Acc@GQA = 27.6) surpasses the GPT-4-driven agent LLoVi (38.0 / 26.8), SeViLA (22.9 / 16.6), FrozenBiLM (23.7 / 17.5), and LLaMA-3.2V (19.8 / 11.6), establishing a new SOTA for evidence grounding. Compared to its own base models, ViTED-LLaVA-Video improves over LLaVA-Video by +2.62% on CinePile, +1.35% on STAR, and +2.35% on MVBench, while ViTED-TimeChat shows dramatic gains over TimeChat (e.g., +51.71% on NExT-QA, +49.96% on STAR), indicating the evidence distillation is most beneficial for models without native temporal grounding. InternVideo2 (6B) remains stronger on NExT-QA (78.6 vs 73.42 for ViTED-TimeChat) and MVBench (67.2 vs 50.46), but ViTED-LLaVA-Video outperforms it on PerceptionTest (67.50 vs 63.4) and NExT-QA (84.13 vs 78.6). Naive chain-of-thought prompting provides only marginal or negative gains (e.g., TimeChat CoT drops STAR from 21.03 to 17.54), confirming that explicit temporal evidence generation is non-trivial for current VLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViTED sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) VLM-based VideoQA models (TimeChat, LLaVA-Video) that handle questions but sample frames uniformly and lack temporal grounding; (2) temporal grounding models (LITA, UniVTG) that can localize text queries but require the evidence text in advance; and (3) chain-of-thought reasoning in VLMs, which has been explored for video prediction and saliency but not for temporally-grounded evidence generation. The paper's key departure is consolidating all three into a single model via a data synthesis pipeline that converts standard Q→A pairs into Q→Evidence+A, treating visual evidence as a chain of temporally-localized descriptions rather than a single retrieval window. It builds directly on TimeChat and LLaVA-Video as backbones and uses LLaMA-3.2-Vision and LLaMA-3.1-8B as off-the-shelf tools for evidence pool generation and beam-search refinement, respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most impactful finding is that temporal evidence grounding emerges as a byproduct of evidence-chain training without any explicit grounding module or grounding-specific labels, allowing a 7B model to surpass GPT-4-driven agent approaches on NExT-GQA. This suggests that the grounding capability is not a separate skill but is implicitly learned when a model is trained to articulate *where* and *when* evidence appears. Limitations include dependence on the base VLM's captioning quality (hallucinations and vague descriptions propagate through the evidence chain), and the text-only LLM can only reach 72% accuracy on evidence chains, indicating the pipeline's ceiling is bounded by upstream generation quality. Open questions include whether the approach scales to hour-long videos, whether it generalizes beyond multiple-choice formats to open-ended QA, and whether the hierarchical segmentation and beam-search parameters transfer across domains without re-tuning.
