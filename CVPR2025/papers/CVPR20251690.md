## Problem

Existing depth pruning methods for diffusion transformers minimize calibration loss (post-pruning error) to identify redundant layers, but the paper demonstrates through 100,000 random-pruning experiments that low calibration loss does not predict good post-fine-tuning performance. The gap is the absence of a principled, learnable objective that directly optimizes the recoverability of pruned diffusion transformers rather than their immediate loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251690/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained diffusion transformer (e.g., DiT-XL/2 with 28 layers, 675M parameters) and a target pruning ratio (e.g., 50%), and produces a shallow pruned model (e.g., 14 layers, 340M parameters) along with a learned local N:M mask specifying which layers to retain in each block. The pruned model is then fine-tuned (optionally with MaskedKD) on ImageNet 256×256 to recover generation quality, ultimately producing class-conditional images at up to 2× inference speedup over the original.

## Method

TinyFusion formulates depth pruning as a differentiable sampling problem over local N:M blocks: the model is divided into K non-overlapping blocks, and a categorical distribution p(m_k) over valid masks within each block is learned via Gumbel-Softmax with a straight-through estimator. The key innovation is co-optimizing this distribution with a LoRA-based weight update ΔΦ that simulates future fine-tuning, so the training objective directly minimizes post-fine-tuning loss (recoverability) rather than immediate calibration loss. After a short training phase (1 epoch), the highest-probability local masks are retained and ΔΦ is discarded. A MaskedKD variant further improves recovery by thresholding massive activations (|x−μ|>kσ) in hidden states during representation distillation, preventing training instability from outlier values.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, TinyFusion prunes DiT-XL/2 (28 layers) to 14 layers (TinyDiT-D14) and, with MaskedKD fine-tuning at 500K steps (7% of pretraining cost), achieves FID 2.86, IS 234.50, and 13.54 it/s sampling speed. This outperforms a DiT-L/2 trained from scratch for 1M steps (FID 3.73, 458M params, 9.73 it/s) on both FID and inference speed, while using only 340M parameters. At just 100K fine-tuning steps with KD, TinyDiT-D14 already reaches FID 3.73, matching the from-scratch DiT-L/2 with a quarter of the training budget. The method also generalizes to MARs (TinyMAR-D16: FID 2.28, beating MAR-Base's 2.31) and SiTs (TinySiT-D14: FID 3.02 at 7% training cost).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among depth-pruning baselines at 50% compression (28→14 layers), TinyFusion decisively outperforms all importance-based and heuristic methods: ShortGPT (FID 22.28), Flux-Lite (FID 25.92), and Sensitivity Analysis (FID 21.15) all suffer severe quality degradation, while the best heuristic baseline Oracle/BK-SDM reaches FID 7.43. TinyDiT-D14 with KD at 500K steps (FID 2.86) beats even the from-scratch DiT-L/2 (FID 3.73, 458M params, 9.73 it/s) on both FID and sampling speed (13.54 vs. 9.73 it/s), using only 340M parameters and 7% of the original pretraining budget. Against alternative architectures, TinyDiT-D14(KD) at FID 2.86 is competitive with U-ViT-L (FID 3.44, 287M) and U-DiT-L (FID 3.37, 204M) while offering a simpler compression pipeline. The method also generalizes: TinyMAR-D16 (FID 2.28) surpasses the larger MAR-Base (FID 2.31) at 10% training cost, and TinySiT-D14 (FID 3.02) achieves competitive quality at 7% of SiT-XL/2's training cost. Notably, the paper demonstrates that minimizing calibration loss (the principle behind ShortGPT, Flux-Lite, and Sensitivity Analysis) is a poor proxy for post-fine-tuning quality in diffusion transformers, as models with the lowest calibration loss (0.20) actually yield the worst FID (20.69–22.28) after fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant loss-minimization paradigm in depth pruning (ShortGPT, Flux-Lite, Sensitivity Analysis, BK-SDM), which selects layers to minimize immediate calibration error, and instead formulates pruning as optimizing post-fine-tuning recoverability. Mechanically, it repurposes Gumbel-Softmax differentiable sampling to make discrete layer-mask selection learnable, and borrows LoRA as a lightweight proxy for the fine-tuning step that would otherwise be intractable to co-optimize. The N:M local-block pruning scheme constrains the combinatorial search space, and the MaskedKD variant extends Hinton-style knowledge distillation with a thresholding trick to handle massive activations in transformer hidden states.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical demonstration that calibration loss is a poor proxy for post-fine-tuning quality in diffusion transformers challenges a widely assumed principle in model compression and suggests that future pruning methods should explicitly model the recovery process rather than the immediate damage. Practically, the 2× speedup at FID 2.86 with under 7% of pretraining cost is a compelling efficiency result for deployment, and the generalization across DiTs, MARs, and SiTs indicates the approach is not architecture-specific. Open questions include whether the method transfers to text-to-image diffusion transformers (all experiments are class-conditional ImageNet), whether the N:M block size and LoRA rank are sensitive hyperparameters at scale, and whether the recoverability objective can be extended to other compression axes (width, sparsity) or to multi-step sampling efficiency.
