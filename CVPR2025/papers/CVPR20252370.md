## Problem

Existing low-rank gradient projection methods (GaLore, Flora) reduce optimizer memory but suffer from two critical flaws: they lack inter-projection correlation, causing abrupt shifts in the optimization trajectory when the projection subspace changes, and they incur high computational overhead from expensive SVD or per-iteration random matrix generation. This makes them either suboptimal in training dynamics or impractically slow for large-scale models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252370/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard model weight matrices W ∈ R^{m×n}, their gradients G_t, and momentum-based optimizer states (first and second moments), projecting the latter into a low-rank subspace of rank r = min(m,n)/c for storage. Weight updates are performed in the low-rank space and restored to full-rank via multiplication with P_t^T, so the output is a full-rank updated weight matrix identical in shape to the input. The method is optimizer-agnostic (Adam, Adafactor) and composable with 8-bit quantization, activation checkpointing, and LOMO.

## Method

COAP replaces the SVD-based or random projection update with a two-part strategy. First, it computes the new projection matrix P_t by solving a non-convex optimization (Eqn. 6) via SGD that jointly minimizes gradient reconstruction error (MSE between G_t and its low-rank estimate) and a direction-consistency term (1 − CosSim between the restored first-order moment and the current gradient), thereby incorporating the previous projection P_{t-1} and momentum M_{t-1} to maintain smooth optimization trajectories. Second, to prevent SGD from converging to local optima, COAP periodically recalibrates P_t using a low-cost SVD (Eqn. 7) that first applies a reduced QR decomposition to G_t P_{t-1}, then performs SVD on the much smaller Q_red^T G_t matrix, reducing complexity from O(mn²) to O(mr²) and achieving over 20× speedup over GaLore's full SVD. The overall training loop updates P_t every T_u steps via the correlation-aware SGD and every λ×T_u steps via the low-cost SVD, while gradients and moments are projected, accumulated, and restored within each interval.

## Results

For LLaMA-1B pre-training on C4, COAP achieves PPL 15.56 (identical to AdamW) while reducing optimizer memory by 61% (4.99→1.94 GB) with only 2% additional training time, compared to GaLore's 17% overhead. For LLaVA-v1.5-7B fine-tuning on ScienceQA, 8-bit COAP reduces optimizer memory by 81% (26.4→4.9 GB), achieves 92.0% image accuracy, and runs 4× faster than GaLore (7.8h vs 30.0h). On LDM pre-training (ImageNet-1K), COAP+AdamW achieves FID 16.2 (vs. 18.0 for AdamW) with 40% less optimizer memory and 13% less extra time than GaLore. On ControlNet-SDXL, 8-bit COAP reduces optimizer memory by 90% while converging to mAP 69.9, whereas GaLore fails to converge at the same compression rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COAP matches AdamW's PPL exactly (15.56) on LLaMA-1B while cutting optimizer memory by 61% and adding only 2% training time, whereas GaLore incurs 17% overhead and a 0.08 PPL penalty. On LLaVA-v1.5-7B fine-tuning, COAP (92.3%) ties LoRA in accuracy but is 1.4× faster and avoids the 30% model-size increase; it also outperforms GaLore by 1.2% accuracy with 4× speedup, and Flora by 25.7% accuracy (92.3% vs 66.6%). For LLaMA-7B with 8-bit optimizers, COAP beats 8-bit Adam by 0.11 PPL (15.28 vs 15.39) with 2% less training time, while 8-bit GaLore is 0.12 PPL worse and 19% slower. On LDM pre-training, COAP+AdamW achieves FID 16.2, improving over both AdamW (18.0) and GaLore (17.8), with 40% less optimizer memory. On ControlNet-SDXL, COAP converges (mAP 73.4 at 80K steps) while GaLore and Flora fail to converge at the same rank ratio, and 8-bit COAP achieves 90% memory reduction with only 13% extra time versus GaLore's 45%.

## Relation to Prior Work

COAP extends the low-rank gradient projection paradigm established by GaLore (SVD-based periodic projection) and Flora (random per-iteration projection) by addressing two specific gaps: the absence of inter-projection correlation in how the projection matrix P_t is updated, and the prohibitive SVD cost. Rather than deriving P_t from a single gradient snapshot or random sampling, COAP formulates the update as a non-convex optimization problem (Eqn. 6) that jointly minimizes gradient reconstruction error and direction inconsistency with the first-order moment, solved via lightweight SGD. It further introduces an occasional low-cost SVD (via QR decomposition, Eqn. 7) to prevent local-optima trapping, reducing the SVD complexity from O(mn²) to O(mr²). The method is positioned as complementary to, rather than a replacement for, parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA, ReLoRA), since it preserves full-rank weight updates while compressing only optimizer states.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COAP delivers a practically significant efficiency win: 61% optimizer memory reduction with only 2% time overhead on LLaMA-1B, and 81% reduction with 4× speedup over GaLore on LLaVA-7B fine-tuning, all while matching or exceeding baseline accuracy. The method composes cleanly with 8-bit quantization, activation checkpointing, and LOMO, making it a drop-in component for memory-constrained training pipelines. Open questions include sensitivity at very high compression rates (r=64 showed hyperparameter fragility), whether the correlation-aware update generalizes beyond Adam-family optimizers, and whether the occasional-SVD recalibration frequency can be made adaptive rather than a fixed hyperparameter. The CEU analysis (Fig. 3) also raises an interesting but underexplored question: why does low-rank projection sometimes *improve* over full-rank Adam, suggesting the projection acts as a noise filter whose theoretical characterization remains open.
