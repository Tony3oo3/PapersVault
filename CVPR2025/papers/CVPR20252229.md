## Problem

Generating continuous sign language videos from discrete word-level segments requires smooth transition frames between signs, but these transitions are inherently unsupervised—no ground-truth transition data exists for training. Direct concatenation of isolated signs produces abrupt, unnatural breaks that disrupt video coherence and obscure meaning, and existing sign language production methods focus on generating poses from text rather than bridging temporal gaps between segments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252229/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes discrete sign pose segments as input, where each frame contains N 3D joint coordinates, and produces smooth transition pose frames that bridge the gap between the last frame of a preceding segment and the first frame of a following segment. During training, complete long-duration sign videos (F frames × N joints × 3D) are randomly masked to simulate missing transitions; during inference, only the boundary poses of adjacent segments are provided, and the model generates the intermediate transition frames in a latent space (dimension W) before decoding back to pose space. The output is a continuous pose sequence that, when combined with the observed segments, forms a fluid sign language video.

## Method

Sign-D2C converts the unsupervised transition generation problem into a supervised task by randomly masking segments within long sign videos during training, forcing a conditional diffusion model to reconstruct the masked frames from surrounding observable context. The architecture comprises a pre-trained encoder (2 self-attention layers) that maps pose sequences to a latent space, a denoiser (2 blocks of self-attention + cross-attention + feed-forward) that iteratively denoises the latent representation conditioned on observed frames via cross-attention, and a pre-trained decoder (3 self-attention layers) that reconstructs poses. At inference, a linearly interpolating padding strategy initializes missing frames by interpolating between the boundary poses of adjacent segments (using a harmonic-series weighting function), providing a stable starting point that the diffusion model then refines through iterative denoising. The encoder and decoder are frozen during diffusion training, with the denoiser optimized via an L1 loss on latent representations, and the optimal masking ratio (r=0.3) balances semantic preservation against transition smoothness.

## Results

On PHOENIX14T (20 observed frames → 10 predicted frames), Sign-D2C achieves BLEU-1 of 22.46 (DEV) / 22.36 (TEST) and DTW of 0.62 / 0.61, substantially outperforming G2P-DDM (BLEU-1: 8.25/9.52, DTW: 12.71/12.57) and VQ-GCDM (BLEU-1: 9.31/9.85, DTW: 12.11/11.98). On USTC-CSL100 under the same setting, the method nearly matches ground-truth semantic scores (BLEU-1: 69.64 vs. GT 69.10; BLEU-4: 58.98 vs. GT 59.12) while achieving DTW of 1.92 and MPJPE of 77.04, compared to DTW 7.00/5.10 and MPJPE 258.43/91.10 for the two baselines. Under the harder 10-observed → 20-predicted setting, Sign-D2C still leads with BLEU-1 of 19.32/19.59 on PHOENIX14T and 67.95/56.61 (BLEU-1/BLEU-4) on USTC-CSL100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sign-D2C decisively outperforms both baselines across all evaluated datasets and settings. On PHOENIX14T (20 obs → 10 pred), it achieves a BLEU-1 of 22.46 vs. 8.25 (G2P-DDM) and 9.31 (VQ-GCDM), a roughly 2.4× improvement in semantic accuracy, while reducing DTW from ~12.1–12.7 to 0.62 — an order-of-magnitude gain in temporal coherence. On USTC-CSL100, the advantage is even more pronounced: BLEU-1 reaches 69.64 (nearly matching ground truth at 69.10) compared to 13.74 and 30.71 for the baselines, and MPJPE drops to 77.04 versus 258.43 (G2P-DDM) and 91.10 (VQ-GCDM). Under the more challenging 10-observed → 20-predicted setting, Sign-D2C maintains its lead (BLEU-1: 19.32 vs. 8.98/8.64 on PHOENIX14T; 67.95 vs. 13.43/18.77 on USTC-CSL100), confirming robustness when fewer context frames are available. Both baselines are designed for gloss-to-pose generation rather than transition synthesis, so their large gaps partly reflect task mismatch, but the consistent dominance across all metrics underscores the effectiveness of the conditional diffusion + random masking formulation for this specific problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the conditional diffusion framework of Ho et al. (2020), repurposing the forward-noise/reverse-denoising process for a specific application: generating missing transition frames between discrete sign language segments. It departs from prior Sign Language Production work (G2P-DDM, VQ-GCDM), which generates poses from gloss/text inputs, by instead conditioning on surrounding observed pose frames to synthesize temporal transitions. The key conceptual contribution is the random masking strategy that converts an inherently unsupervised transition-generation problem into a supervised reconstruction task, combined with a linear interpolation padding initialization at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical bottleneck in sign language technology: the inability to assemble word-level sign data into coherent sentence-level videos without hand-crafted transitions. The near-ground-truth semantic scores on USTC-CSL100 suggest the approach can faithfully preserve meaning while filling gaps, which could accelerate dataset scaling for continuous sign language. However, the evaluation relies on indirect NSLT back-translation for semantic scoring, the baselines are adapted from a different task (gloss-to-pose) rather than being purpose-built for transition generation, and the paper does not address real-time inference or linguistic validity of the generated transitions beyond pose-level metrics. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer gaps, multi-signer scenarios, or facial expression channels.
