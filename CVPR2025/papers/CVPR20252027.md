## Problem

Existing audio-visual segmentation methods struggle with two forms of ambiguous audio-visual correspondence: visually similar but acoustically different objects in close proximity cause global attention to misallocate audio cues to silent objects, and rapid shifts in an object's sounding state over time lead to over-smoothed predictions that over-segment silent frames. Prior work either relies on ground-truth masks to filter silent regions (preventing the model from learning to distinguish them) or smooths temporal predictions, both of which fail to robustly handle these challenging scenarios.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a sequence of T video frames (one per second, at H×W resolution) paired with corresponding one-second audio segments (represented as Mel spectrograms of T×F filter banks) and produces pixel-wise segmentation masks for audible objects along with a per-pixel uncertainty map. The final prediction is computed by dividing the softmax (or sigmoid) mask probabilities by the normalized Dirichlet-derived uncertainty, effectively suppressing confidence in regions where the model is uncertain about sounding state. Multi-scale visual features at 1/4, 1/8, and 1/16 spatial resolutions are used for the alignment process.

## Method

The proposed RAVS framework introduces two key modules. First, the Audio-Guided Modality Alignment (AMA) module groups visual tokens via DPC-KNN density clustering, then performs sound-guided semantic merging within each group using audio-responsiveness-weighted averaging to produce compact representations that amplify sounding regions and suppress silent ones; an InfoNCE contrastive loss further separates high-responsiveness (positive) from low-responsiveness (negative) compact features in the embedding space. Second, the Uncertainty Estimation (UE) module applies a Dirichlet distribution over class logits to compute pixel-wise prediction uncertainty, which is then used to divide mask probabilities, adaptively lowering confidence in regions affected by rapid sound-state transitions. The overall training objective combines standard segmentation loss (cross-entropy, Dice, IoU) with the contrastive alignment loss at a 1:0.1 weighting ratio.

## Results

On AVS-Semantic, RAVS with MIT-B5 + HTSAT achieves 65.7% J&F_β, surpassing the previous best (AVSSstone, 61.5%) by 4.2 points. On AVS-Objects-MS3, RAVS reaches 76.4% J&F_β, outperforming CAVP (72.7%) and AVSSstone (72.5%). On the challenging VPO-MSMI benchmark, RAVS attains 69.30% J&F_β versus 58.76% for AVSStone and 68.07% for CPM*, demonstrating particularly strong gains in scenarios with visually similar but acoustically distinct objects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAVS outperforms all 17 compared baselines across every reported dataset and metric. The closest competitors are CAVP [5] (90.5 / 72.7 / 55.3 J&F_β on S4/MS3/Semantic), AVSSstone [24] (87.3 / 72.5 / 61.5), and CPM* [6] on VPO (73.49 / 72.91 / 68.07). On AVS-Semantic, RAVS (65.7) leads AVSSstone (61.5) by 4.2 points and CAVP (55.3) by 10.4 points, indicating that the audio-guided alignment and uncertainty estimation modules are especially effective for semantic-level segmentation. On VPO-MSMI—the subset with the most visually similar but acoustically distinct objects—RAVS (69.30) surpasses AVSStone (58.76) by 10.5 points and CPM* (68.07) by 1.2 points, confirming the method's advantage in disambiguating sounding vs. silent objects. On AVS-Objects-S4, RAVS (93.5) edges out CAVP (90.5) by 3.0 points, while on AVS-Objects-MS3 the gap over CAVP narrows to 3.7 points (76.4 vs. 72.7). Older or less specialized methods (TPAVI, CATR, AVSegFormer, COMBO, etc.) trail by 5–30+ points on AVS-Semantic, underscoring the difficulty of semantic-level audio-visual segmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the audio-visual segmentation (AVS) line of work by departing from the dominant global cross-attention paradigm (TPAVI, CATR, AVSegFormer) and the ground-truth-mask filtering strategy (AVSSstone, CAVP). It repurposes Density Peaks Clustering (DPC-KNN, Zeng et al. CVPR 2022) for local visual feature grouping and combines it with InfoNCE contrastive learning to create an audio-guided modality alignment that distinguishes sounding from silent objects without relying on GT masks. The uncertainty estimation component draws on evidential deep learning with Dirichlet distributions (Sensoy et al. NeurIPS 2018), adapted here to handle temporal sound-state transitions in segmentation rather than classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses two concrete failure modes in AVS—visually similar objects with differing sound states and rapid sound-state transitions—that prior methods handle poorly, and the 11.5% J&Fβ gain on VPO-MSMI (a benchmark specifically stress-testing the first failure mode) validates the approach. However, the uncertainty estimation is a post-hoc confidence scaling rather than a principled fix for temporal modeling, and the DPC-KNN grouping introduces a non-differentiable, dataset-dependent hyperparameter (group count) that may limit generalization. Open questions include whether the audio-guided grouping strategy transfers to real-world multi-source scenes beyond the controlled AVS/VPO benchmarks, and whether the contrastive alignment can be extended to handle overlapping or partially occluded sounding objects.
