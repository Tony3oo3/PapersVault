## Problem

Online HD maps used in deployed autonomous driving systems suffer from perception errors (due to sensor limitations and occlusions) and inherent feature redundancy (requiring numerous elements to describe road details), both of which degrade trajectory prediction performance. While SD maps like OpenStreetMap provide accurate road-level skeletons with global coverage, no prior work has effectively integrated them into existing HD map-based trajectory prediction models to address both problems simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250592/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes vectorized SD map polylines (road skeletons sampled at 1 m intervals from OpenStreetMap), online HD maps (vectorized lane-level elements constructed in real-time from 6 surround-view RGB images via MapTRv2 or HRMapNet), and agent historical trajectories (2 s at 2 Hz for nuScenes, 5 s at 10 Hz for Argoverse2). It outputs 6 predicted future trajectories per agent (3 s horizon on nuScenes, 6 s on Argoverse2) and, in the end-to-end setting, a single ego planning trajectory with associated collision risk. Gaussian noise is added to the ego-vehicle pose during SD map retrieval to simulate real-world GPS/IMU positioning errors.

## Method

SATP introduces an SD-HD fusion framework that abstracts the HD map utilization pipeline of any baseline model into two steps—map encoding and feature extraction—then creates downscaled SD equivalents (reduced layers and channels) to ensure feature consistency while keeping parameter overhead minimal. A novel AlignNet, built on a shared lightweight 2D Point Transformer, extracts structural features from both maps via KNN self-attention and local max pooling, then predicts a relative rotation and translation to align the SD map with the HD map; this alignment is learned end-to-end through the trajectory prediction loss rather than direct pose supervision. The HD-informed and SD-informed queries are combined via a sigmoid-gated adaptive fusion (Eq. 1), allowing the model to weight each map source per query. The framework is demonstrated on three representative baselines (HiVT, DenseTNT, QCNet) covering agent-query, learnable-query, and hybrid extraction paradigms, and achieves up to 25% MR improvement with <10% latency increase.

## Results

On nuScenes, SATP achieves up to 25% improvement in Miss Rate (QCNet + HRMapNet: MR 0.083 → 0.062) and up to 3% improvement even with ground-truth HD maps (QCNet: FDE 0.632 → 0.615). Across all combinations of map inputs and baseline models, SATP yields average improvements of 9% in ADE, 13% in FDE, and 17% in MR. On Argoverse2, QCNet with simulated online HD maps (GT HD + noise) plus SATP reaches ADE 0.752 / FDE 1.306 / MR 0.164, matching the performance of using ground-truth HD maps directly (0.741 / 1.293 / 0.162). The efficiency overhead is minimal: parameter increase ≤ 0.4M and latency increase ≤ 8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SATP consistently outperforms all three trajectory prediction baselines (HiVT, DenseTNT, QCNet) when augmented with SD maps, with the largest gains observed under the noisier MapTRv2 online HD map (e.g., QCNet MR drops from 0.087 to 0.071, a 18% reduction). Against the stronger HRMapNet online HD map, improvements remain substantial (QCNet MR: 0.083 → 0.062, a 25% reduction), and SATP even surpasses the ground-truth HD map baseline on several metrics (e.g., QCNet ADE: 0.358 vs. 0.360 with GT HD). In the end-to-end setting, VAD + SATP reduces average L2 displacement from 0.78 m to 0.65 m and collision rate from 0.38% to 0.26%, with larger gains at the 3-second horizon. Compared to the alternative of using SD maps to enhance HD map construction (P-MapNet + MapTRv2), SATP achieves better trajectory prediction (HiVT ADE 0.371 vs. 0.395) because direct SD-HD fusion avoids information loss and also addresses feature redundancy. On Argoverse2, SATP closes the gap between simulated online HD maps and ground-truth HD maps, confirming generalizability beyond nuScenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the vectorized HD map-based trajectory prediction paradigm (HiVT, DenseTNT, QCNet) and the online HD map construction pipeline (MapTRv2, HRMapNet), positioning SD maps as a complementary signal rather than a replacement. It departs from prior SD map work in trajectory prediction [22], which used SD maps as the sole map input and relied on expensive ground-truth HD maps for distillation, by instead fusing SD maps alongside imperfect online HD maps through a model-downscaling strategy that mirrors each baseline's own HD encoding/extraction architecture. The AlignNet component draws on 2D Point Transformer for structural map feature extraction, while the adaptive query fusion mechanism is distinct from the sequential or attention-based fusion patterns found in multi-modal trajectory prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SATP addresses a practical deployment gap: real-world autonomous driving systems rely on online HD maps that inevitably contain perception errors and redundant features, and this paper shows that freely available SD maps can close much of the performance gap to ground-truth HD maps with minimal overhead (<10% latency). The finding that SD maps still yield improvements even when paired with perfect ground-truth HD maps (by alleviating feature redundancy) suggests a broader principle about information density in map representations. Open questions include handling delayed SD map updates, generalization to urban environments with complex topology not well captured by road skeletons, and whether the approach extends to other map-augmented tasks like planning or perception.
