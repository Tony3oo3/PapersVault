## Problem

Semi-supervised text spotting (SSTS) must simultaneously generate reliable pseudo labels for both text detection and recognition, but these two tasks are inherently inconsistent: a correctly located region may still yield a wrong transcription, and slight positional deviations between teacher and student cause recognition results to shift. Existing semi-supervised methods designed for general object detection or single OCR sub-tasks (detection-only or recognition-only) do not address this dual-task inconsistency, leading to noisy supervision and sub-optimal student learning.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes scene text images in two splits: a small labeled set (as few as 0.5% of the training set, i.e., 5 images) providing ground-truth polygon coordinates and transcriptions, and a larger unlabeled set used for pseudo-label generation. The teacher processes weakly augmented versions while the student receives strongly augmented versions (flipping and polarization are excluded to avoid transcription ambiguity). Outputs are arbitrary-shaped text region polygons represented as ordered point sequences and their corresponding character-level transcriptions, evaluated via detection F1 and end-to-end H-mean with or without a lexicon.

## Method

SemiETS follows a Mean-Teacher-style architecture built on a DETR-based text spotter (DeepSolo) and introduces two modules. The Progressive Sample Assignment (PSA) module first applies a joint constraint filter (classification score threshold and non-void decoded text), then performs cost-based bipartite matching via the Hungarian algorithm with a composite cost combining focal loss, recognition loss, and coordinate L1 distance, augmented by hybrid matching for early-iteration efficiency; a subsequent recognition filter (confidence threshold plus teacher-over-student confidence comparison) hierarchically splits pseudo labels into Det-only and E2E categories to suppress noisy recognition supervision. The Mutual Mining Strategy (MMS) exploits detection–recognition complementarity in two bidirectional flows: Spatial-aware Consistency Integration (SCI) computes a polygon DIoU between teacher and student regions to produce an adaptive factor that down-weights ambiguous recognition losses, while Content-aware Region Calibration (CRC) measures Levenshtein distance between teacher and student transcriptions to up-weight detection regression losses for regions whose misalignment causes recognition errors. The overall loss combines supervised losses on labeled data with semi-supervised classification, regression (modulated by β), and recognition (modulated by α) losses on unlabeled data, and the teacher is updated via EMA.

## Results

On Total-Text under the Partially Labeled Data setting, SemiETS achieves 72.0% H-mean (without lexicon) with only 0.5% labeled data, outperforming the previous SOTA Semi-DETR (59.4%) by 8.7%. At 1% and 2% labeled data, it reaches 72.8% and 73.4% respectively, surpassing Semi-DETR by 5.6% and 2.6%. On CTW1500 with 0.5% labeled data, SemiETS achieves 53.5% H-mean versus 33.1% for Semi-DETR (+20.4%). Under the Fully Labeled Data setting, SemiETS further improves upon the fully supervised DeepSolo baseline by 2.0% H-mean on Total-Text (81.7% vs. 79.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SemiETS consistently outperforms all five SSL baselines (STAC, Mean-Teacher, Soft Teacher, Unbiased Teacher v2, and Semi-DETR) across all three datasets and all data proportions. The margin is largest at 0.5% labeled data: +8.7% over Semi-DETR on Total-Text and +20.4% on CTW1500, while at 2% the gap narrows to +2.6% on Total-Text. On ICDAR 2015 (Table 2), SemiETS leads Semi-DETR by 2.0–2.5% H-mean (strong lexicon) across 0.5%–2% settings. Notably, several EMA-based methods (Mean-Teacher, Soft Teacher, Unbiased Teacher v2) actually degrade below the supervised baseline at 0.5% on Total-Text, whereas SemiETS improves it by 13.2%. In the Fully Labeled Data setting (Table 3), SemiETS surpasses the fully supervised DeepSolo by 2.0% H-mean on Total-Text, while other SSL methods (Mean-Teacher, Soft Teacher, Semi-DETR) fail to match or even degrade the supervised baseline. The method also generalizes across architectures, improving ABCNet (RoI-based) by 8.1–13.7% over its SSL baseline on Total-Text (Table 4), and achieves +17.3% detection F1 and +14.4% E2E H-mean in domain adaptation from Total-Text to ICDAR 2015 (Table 5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mean-Teacher EMA framework and DETR-based text spotting (DeepSolo) to the semi-supervised setting, introducing task-specific designs that prior SSOD methods (STAC, Semi-DETR, Unbiased Teacher v2) do not address. It departs from the separate lines of semi-supervised text detection (WeText, SemiText) and semi-supervised text recognition (Baek et al., Seq-UPS) by treating the joint spotting task holistically, recognizing that detection and recognition pseudo labels are not independently reliable. The hybrid matching strategy from Semi-DETR and the DIoU metric from oriented object detection are repurposed for polygon-based text regions, while the core novelty lies in the Progressive Sample Assignment and Mutual Mining Strategy modules that exploit the bidirectional complementarity between localization and transcription.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of semi-supervised text spotting as a unified task, and the 8.7% H-mean gain at 0.5% labeled data (and a 2% boost over a fully supervised model) demonstrates that unlabeled scene text images carry substantial exploitable signal. The domain adaptation results (17.3% detection F1 gain) suggest practical value for deploying text spotters across new domains without re-annotation. However, the method is limited to Latin text and relies on a specific DETR-based backbone (though ABCNet experiments show some generality), leaving multilingual SSTS and broader architectural compatibility as open questions. The observation that VLLMs (InternVL2, Qwen2-VL) score near zero on text spotting underscores that precise spatial-textual grounding remains unsolved by general-purpose vision-language models.
