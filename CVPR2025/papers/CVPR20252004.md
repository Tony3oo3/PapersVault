## Problem

3D subtomogram alignment in cryo-ET requires estimating 6D rigid transformations from extremely noisy (SNR ~0.01) volumes with scarce labeled data, yet existing CNN-based methods still produce high parameter errors under low SNR. Vision Transformers, which could capture global structural dependencies better, have not been applied to this task because they lack inherent shift and rotation equivariance, making them poorly suited for orientation estimation without architectural modification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252004/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a batch of 3D cryo-ET subtomogram volumes (B×C×D×H×W grayscale tensors) and predicts the 6D rigid transformation (three rotation angles and three translation offsets) that maps the input back to a reference orientation. Training is entirely self-supervised: known geometric transformations are applied to subtomograms and the network must recover them, with a two-phase curriculum (coarse: ±180° rotation, 40% volume translation; fine: ±30° rotation, 30% translation). Evaluation is performed at four realistic low-SNR conditions (0.1, 0.05, 0.03, 0.01) on five asymmetric macromolecular complexes.

## Method

BOE-ViT is the first Vision Transformer for 3D subtomogram alignment, built around two equivariance-enhancing modules. The Polyshift module achieves shift-equivariant feature extraction by decomposing the input into polyphase components, anchoring on the component with the highest Lp norm to stabilize multi-scale strided convolution, and replacing standard positional embeddings with circularly-padded depthwise convolutions over normalized coordinate grids. The MARE (Multi-Axis Rotation Encoding) module enhances rotation sensitivity in self-attention by computing axis-specific rotation matrices via the exponential map of learnable skew-symmetric matrices (Rodrigues formula) and applying them independently to query and key vectors along the depth, height, and width axes, with attention outputs summed across axes. The full pipeline uses a standard ViT backbone with self-attention, a prediction head for the 6D parameters, and is trained self-supervised in two phases (coarse then fine) on synthetic transformations of cryo-ET subtomograms.

## Results

At the most challenging SNR 0.01 setting, BOE-ViT achieves a rotation error of 0.34°±0.15 and a translation error of 2.54°±0.91, representing a 77.3% reduction in rotation error and a 62.5% reduction in translation error compared to the best CNN baseline (Gum-Net: 1.50°±0.78 rotation, 6.78°±4.22 translation). Remarkably, BOE-ViT's performance is nearly invariant to noise level, maintaining ~0.33–0.34° rotation error and ~2.2–2.6° translation error across all SNR conditions (0.1 to 0.01), while all baselines degrade substantially as SNR decreases. The standard deviation of BOE-ViT's predictions (±0.15° rotation) is also an order of magnitude lower than CNN baselines (±0.77–0.78°), indicating far more consistent alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BOE-ViT decisively outperforms all four baselines across every SNR level and every macromolecular complex tested. Against the traditional exhaustive-search methods (H-T align and F&A align), BOE-ViT reduces rotation error from ~2.4° to ~0.34° (a ~7× improvement) and translation error from ~11° to ~2.5° at SNR 0.01. Against the state-of-the-art CNN baselines, Gum-Net and Jim-Net, BOE-ViT still achieves roughly 4.4× lower rotation error (0.34° vs. 1.50–1.58°) and 2.7× lower translation error (2.54° vs. 6.78°) at SNR 0.01. A striking finding is the noise robustness: while Gum-Net's rotation error nearly triples from SNR 0.1 to 0.01 (0.62°→1.50°) and its translation error nearly triples (2.41°→6.78°), BOE-ViT's errors remain essentially flat across all noise levels. The ablation (Table 3) further confirms that both Polyshift and MARE are necessary—removing either module degrades performance, and self-attention with both modules (BOE-ViT) outperforms cross-attention (BOE-CrossViT) on translation error.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of three research threads: (1) the established line of CNN-based subtomogram alignment (Gum-Net, Jim-Net) that it departs from by introducing the first ViT-based architecture for this task; (2) the group-equivariant neural network literature (G-CNNs, shift-equivariant ViTs like CPE and RevViT) from which it borrows polyphase decomposition and circularly-padded depthwise convolution to restore shift-equivariance in a ViT backbone; and (3) rotary position encodings (RoPE), which it generalizes to a 3D multi-axis formulation (MARE) using axis-specific skew-symmetric matrices and the exponential map. The self-supervised training paradigm (predicting known geometric transformations) follows the unsupervised/self-supervised approach of Zeng et al., but the equivariant architectural design is the key conceptual departure from prior alignment networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a ViT with equivariant modules can achieve near-constant error across SNR levels (0.33–0.34° rotation error from SNR 0.1 to 0.01) while all CNN baselines degrade substantially, this work suggests that equivariance is not merely a theoretical nicety but a practical necessity for orientation estimation under extreme noise. The Polyshift and MARE modules are modular and could transfer to other 3D orientation-estimation or registration tasks beyond cryo-ET. Key limitations include evaluation on simulated data with only five protein structures, training times exceeding two days on a single V100, and the open question of whether these gains persist on real cryo-ET tomograms with missing-wedge artifacts and heterogeneous cellular backgrounds.
