## Problem

Existing N-shot NAS methods train all subnets within a supernet using a static strategy (identical LR scheduler and shared optimizer), ignoring that subnets have distinct complexities and structures. This causes two failures: (1) an unfairness problem where high-complexity subnets are undertrained relative to low-complexity ones, biasing the supernet's performance predictions, and (2) a noisy momentum problem where a single momentum buffer accumulates heterogeneous gradients from diverse subnets, destabilizing optimization.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a drop-in modification to the supernet training loop of any sampling-based N-shot NAS method (SPOS, FairNAS, FSNAS), requiring only the search space definition to compute per-subnet complexity scores (parameter counts) and structural cluster assignments (operation at a chosen edge). It produces the same outputs as the base NAS method—trained supernet weights used as a performance predictor and a retrieved architecture under a FLOPs/latency budget—but with improved ranking consistency. The additional memory cost is limited to a small number of extra momentum buffers (one per cluster, typically 5), and the extra GPU time is under 2% over the baseline.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary techniques for dynamic supernet training. First, CaLR (Complexity-aware LR Scheduler) replaces the fixed polynomial LR decay with a per-subnet decay ratio γ(α) computed as a log-affine function of the subnet's parameter count, so that high-complexity subnets decay LR slowly (γ < 1, more parameter updates) while low-complexity subnets decay faster (γ > 1). Second, MS (Momentum Separation) clusters subnets by their operation at a single randomly chosen edge in the supernet and maintains a separate momentum buffer per cluster, so that the running gradient mean reflects structurally similar subnets rather than the full heterogeneous search space. Both techniques modify only the optimizer state updates during supernet training, leaving the search algorithm, sampling strategy, and weight-sharing scheme unchanged, and can be applied in a plug-and-play fashion to one-shot and few-shot NAS methods alike.

## Results

On NAS-Bench-201, the method consistently improves Kendall's Tau ranking consistency across all three baselines and datasets: SPOS+Ours reaches 0.814/0.81/0.743 (CIFAR-10/CIFAR-100/ImageNet16-120) vs. 0.751/0.787/0.718 for SPOS alone, while FairNAS+Ours achieves the best overall at 0.828/0.857/0.776. Top-1 accuracy gains are substantial on CIFAR-100 (e.g., FairNAS+Ours: 70.91% vs. 67.72% for FairNAS) and ImageNet16-120 (44.63% vs. 39.81%). On the MobileNet space (ImageNet), the method improves top-1 accuracy by 0.3–0.5% across FLOPs constraints, with the largest gains at the 475M FLOPs budget (e.g., FSNAS-L+Ours: 77.2% vs. 76.8%). Additional memory and GPU-hour overheads are negligible (e.g., 1901→1903 MB, 5.13→5.15 GPU hours for FairNAS on CIFAR-10).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is applied as a plug-and-play dynamic training strategy on top of three N-shot NAS baselines: SPOS (single-subnet sampling), FairNAS (multi-subnet sampling), and FSNAS (few-shot sub-supernet partitioning). All three baselines are consistently improved in both ranking consistency (Kendall's Tau) and final top-1 accuracy across every dataset and FLOPs constraint tested. The largest relative gains appear for FairNAS on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet16-120, where adding CaLR+MS lifts top-1 accuracy by 3.19 and 4.82 percentage points respectively (67.72→70.91, 39.81→44.63), and for FSNAS on ImageNet16-120 (42.97→44.68, +1.71 pp). On the MobileNet space, gains are more modest (0.3–0.5 pp) but still consistent, with the highest-complexity (475M FLOPs) subnets benefiting most (e.g., FSNAS-L: 76.8→77.2%). The method never degrades any baseline, and the additional cost is under 1% in both memory and GPU hours, confirming the plug-and-play nature of the approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a training-strategy improvement for existing N-shot NAS methods (SPOS, FairNAS, FSNAS) rather than a new search algorithm. It identifies two specific pathologies of the static training paradigm used by all prior sampling-based one-shot and few-shot NAS methods — complexity-induced unfairness in LR scheduling and cross-subnet momentum noise — and addresses them with a complexity-aware LR scheduler (CaLR) and operation-based momentum separation (MS). The approach is explicitly orthogonal to recent sampling-strategy improvements (GreedyNAS, PA&DA, ShiftNAS), which modify *which* subnets are sampled, whereas this work modifies *how* each sampled subnet is optimized. Conceptually, it extends the weight-sharing supernet framework by treating subnets as having heterogeneous optimization needs, a distinction prior methods implicitly ignored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of CaLR and MS (negligible memory and time overhead) makes them immediately adoptable by practitioners using SPOS, FairNAS, or FSNAS pipelines, and the introduced diagnostics (Complexity Bias, C³) give the community concrete metrics to audit supernet training fairness. A key limitation is that the framework applies only to sampling-based NAS and cannot be extended to differentiable methods, which jointly optimize architecture coefficients without explicit per-subnet sampling. Open questions include whether more sophisticated clustering (e.g., multi-edge or learned groupings) could further reduce momentum noise, and whether the approach transfers to non-CNN search spaces or video/3D architectures where complexity heterogeneity may be even more pronounced.
