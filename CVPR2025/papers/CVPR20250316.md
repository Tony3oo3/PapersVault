## Problem

Existing information bottleneck-based attribution methods (e.g., IBA) compute relevant information in a single targeted layer, neglecting the distributed evidence of the decision-making process across layers. No single layer dominantly provides the most appropriate relevant information, and there is no theoretical guarantee that low-scored attributions are unnecessary for the decision. This leads to inconsistent, layer-dependent explanations that require heuristic layer selection.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image (224×224) fed through a pre-trained ViT, DeiT, or Swin Transformer and produces a pixel-level attribution map. Internally, the bottleneck operates on patch-level token representations (P patches), and the universal damping ratio is a per-token vector of shape P×1, meaning each token receives a single scalar perturbation strength applied uniformly across all channel dimensions. The bottleneck is inserted into a contiguous sequence of layers (e.g., layers 4–12 for ViT-B), and the final attribution is aggregated from all targeted layers into a single map.

## Method

CoIBA inserts bottleneck layers into a sequence of targeted layers and shares a single learnable universal damping ratio λ (via sigmoid of a trainable parameter α) across all of them, replacing IBA's layer-specific ratios. Within each bottleneck layer, the intermediate representation is perturbed as Z_l = λR_l' + (1−λ)ε_l, where ε_l is Gaussian noise matched to the activation statistics, and the perturbation is applied uniformly across all channels of a token to concentrate on token-level importance. The objective maximizes I[Z_L; Y] − β·I[R_1; Z_1], where a variational upper bound (derived from the data processing inequality and the monotonic decrease of mutual information under iterated noise injection) replaces the sum of L layer-wise mutual informations with just the first layer's term, collapsing L trade-off hyperparameters into a single β. This upper bound guarantees that the discarded activations are unnecessary in every targeted layer, and the shared damping ratio compensates for over-compressed information in individual layers by distributing relevant information across the sequence.

## Results

On insertion/deletion (lower insertion and higher deletion are better), CoIBA achieves 13.01/62.58 on ViT-B-16/224, 11.79/53.96 on DeiT-B-16/224, and 15.40/61.14 on ViT-L-16/224, consistently outperforming all baselines by 2–6 points in insertion and 2–7 points in deletion. On the ROAD benchmark (MoRF↓/LeRF↑), CoIBA reaches 16.63/73.68 on ViT-B-16/224 and 11.59/67.12 on DeiT-B-16/224, again leading all competitors. The gains are particularly pronounced on difficult (low-confidence) samples and on out-of-distribution datasets (ImageNet-A, ImageNet-R), where CoIBA's advantage over the next-best method widens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoIBA outperforms all six baselines on every model and metric reported. Against the closest information-bottleneck competitor IBA, CoIBA reduces insertion scores by 2–5 points (e.g., 13.01 vs. 17.23 on ViT-B, 11.79 vs. 13.95 on DeiT-B) while increasing deletion scores by 3–7 points, and does so in a single forward pass rather than requiring iterative per-layer runs. Against propagation-based methods (Chefer-LRP, Generic, IIA, Beyond), CoIBA's advantage is even larger: on ViT-B-16/224, CoIBA's insertion of 13.01 beats Beyond (15.84), IIA (16.56), and Chefer-LRP (17.67) by 2.8–4.7 points. On the ROAD benchmark, CoIBA achieves the best MoRF (16.63) and LeRF (73.68) on ViT-B-16/224, surpassing the next-best Beyond (17.93/68.44) by 1.3 and 5.2 points respectively. The gap widens on low-confidence and out-of-distribution samples (ImageNet-A, ImageNet-R), where CoIBA's multi-layer information sharing provides a more faithful attribution than any single-layer or gradient-based alternative.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoIBA directly extends IBA (Schulz et al., ICLR 2020), which applies the information bottleneck principle to a single targeted layer to produce attribution maps with theoretical guarantees. The key conceptual departure is replacing IBA's layer-specific damping ratio with a universal damping ratio shared across multiple bottleneck-inserted layers, thereby capturing distributed relevant information that any single layer omits. The variational upper bound (Eq. 7–8) draws on the Deep Variational Information Bottleneck (Alemi et al., ICLR 2017) to collapse the multi-layer compression objective into a single hyperparameter, eliminating the heuristic layer search that IBA* (iterating IBA per layer) requires. Positioning-wise, CoIBA contrasts with propagation-based ViT explainers (Chefer-LRP, Generic, IIA, ViT-CX, Beyond) by offering a formal guarantee that suppressed activations are unnecessary in every targeted layer, not merely a heuristic relevance score.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in removing the need to select or search over a single 'best' attribution layer—a problem the paper documents empirically (Fig. 1b shows no dominant layer)—and in providing a theoretical guarantee that holds simultaneously across all targeted layers. However, the bottleneck layer range (s, e) still requires manual specification, and the variational approximation means the guarantee is an upper bound rather than an exact statement. Open questions include whether the optimal layer window can be learned automatically, whether the approach transfers to decoder-only or multimodal transformers, and whether the uniform channel perturbation assumption holds for architectures with heterogeneous channel semantics (e.g., Swin's multi-scale patches).
