## Problem

Current image generation models are task-specific: each new capability (editing, subject-driven generation, controllable generation, CV tasks) requires a separate module, fine-tuning, and a multi-step pipeline with external detectors and encoders. No single model can handle diverse image generation tasks end-to-end through natural language instructions, unlike how LLMs unify language tasks. This paper asks whether a unified, plugin-free architecture can match or exceed specialized models across all these tasks simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The model accepts free-form interleaved sequences of text tokens (via Phi-3 tokenizer) and image tokens (VAE latents flattened at patch size 2, wrapped in <img>/</img> special tokens), with a timestep embedding appended at the end—no task-specific tokens are needed. During inference, a Gaussian noise latent is iteratively denoised over 50 flow-matching steps with classifier-free guidance (text scale 2.5, image scale 1.6), and the final latent is decoded by the VAE into a single RGB image. Training resolutions range from 256×256 up to 2240×2240, and the model handles arbitrary aspect ratios following the SD3 approach.

## Method

OmniGen consists of only two components: a frozen SDXL VAE for visual encoding/decoding and a 3.8B-parameter transformer initialized from Phi-3 that jointly models text and image tokens, eliminating all auxiliary encoders (CLIP text encoder, image encoders, cross-attention adapters). The key architectural modification is a hybrid attention mask: causal attention across the full sequence but bidirectional attention within each image token block, allowing patches to attend to all other patches in the same image while preserving left-to-right causality between modalities. Training uses rectified flow (linear interpolation between data and Gaussian noise) with an MSE velocity-prediction objective, and for editing tasks a spatially weighted loss amplifies gradients in changed regions to prevent the model from learning a copy-the-input shortcut. The model is trained on the newly constructed X2I dataset (~0.1B images) in five stages of increasing resolution (256→2240), unifying text-to-image, editing, subject-driven, controllable, and CV tasks into a single interleaved input-output format. Inference is accelerated via KV-cache over the conditioning prefix, analogous to LLM autoregressive decoding.

## Results

On the GenEval benchmark, OmniGen (3.8B parameters) achieves an overall score of 0.70, surpassing SD3 (0.68, 12.7B total params), DALL-E 3 (0.67), and IF-XL (0.61), while excelling particularly on attribute binding (0.55) and single-object generation (0.99). On image editing (EMU-Edit), OmniGen matches the best CLIP-T score (0.231) with a CLIP-I of 0.829, and on subject-driven generation (DreamBench) it achieves the highest CLIP-T (0.315). For controllable generation, OmniGen reaches 40.06 mIoU on segmentation masks and 0.8332 SSIM on HED edges, competitive with ControlNet++ (43.64 mIoU) while requiring no external detectors or condition modules.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On GenEval, OmniGen outperforms all compared text-to-image models on overall score (0.70 vs. SD3's 0.68 and DALL-E 3's 0.67) with only 3.8B parameters versus SD3's 12.7B total, and leads on attribute binding (0.55 vs. 0.43 for SD3) and colors (0.85 vs. 0.74 for SD3), though it trails on position (0.31 vs. 0.43 for DALL-E 3) and counting (0.64 vs. 0.66 for SD3/IF-XL). On EMU-Edit, OmniGen ties EMU-Edit on CLIP-T (0.231) but falls slightly behind on CLIP-I (0.829 vs. 0.859), while clearly surpassing InstructPix2Pix (0.219/0.834), MagicBrush (0.222/0.838), and Null-Text Inversion (0.236/0.761). On DreamBench, OmniGen achieves the best CLIP-T (0.315) among all subject-driven methods, beating DreamBooth (0.305), SuTI (0.304), and Kosmos-G (0.287), though its CLIP-I (0.801) is lower than Kosmos-G (0.847) and SuTI (0.819). For controllable generation, OmniGen surpasses ControlNet on all four metrics (e.g., 40.06 vs. 32.55 mIoU on segmentation) and beats ControlNet++ on Canny F1 (38.96 vs. 37.04) and HED SSIM (0.8332 vs. 0.8097), but trails ControlNet++ on segmentation mIoU (40.06 vs. 43.64) and depth RMSE (31.71 vs. 28.32). Notably, OmniGen achieves these results without any external detectors, condition encoders, or task-specific plugins, whereas baselines like ControlNet require separate pose/depth/canny extractors and DreamBooth requires per-subject fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniGen builds on rectified flow (Liu et al., 2022) for its training objective and SDXL's VAE for visual encoding, but departs fundamentally from the standard diffusion model paradigm by replacing the U-Net + separate encoder stack with a single transformer (initialized from Phi-3) that natively processes interleaved text and image tokens. Unlike prior work that extends diffusion models task-by-task via plugins (ControlNet for spatial control, InstructPix2Pix for editing, InstantID for identity preservation), OmniGen unifies all these capabilities through a single multi-task training objective on the newly constructed X2I dataset. The modified attention mechanism—causal across the sequence but bidirectional within each image block—is a key architectural departure from both standard LLMs and existing diffusion transformers, enabling the model to treat images as holistic units while maintaining autoregressive ordering of conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper establishes a concrete blueprint for the 'LLM moment' in image generation: a single model that replaces multi-model pipelines with end-to-end instruction following, potentially simplifying the workflow from dozens of steps to one. The demonstrated knowledge transfer (e.g., handling ControlNet-style spatial conditioning without explicit training on that task) suggests that unified training may unlock capabilities beyond the sum of individual task training, though this remains preliminary. Key limitations include weak text rendering, anatomical artifacts, and inability to handle previously unseen image types (e.g., surface normal maps). The chain-of-thought exploration for step-by-step image generation is an intriguing but early direction, with current results showing quality degradation compared to direct generation, leaving open whether process supervision can eventually outperform outcome supervision for visual synthesis.
