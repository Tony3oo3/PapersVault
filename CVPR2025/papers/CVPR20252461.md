## Problem

Existing methods for animatable 3D clothed human avatars either produce static reconstructions unsuitable for animation or require hours of per-subject test-time optimization to learn personalized skinning weights and canonical shapes. No prior work achieves instant, feed-forward generation of personalized skinned avatars that generalize zero-shot to casually taken phone photos while preserving fine-grained geometric details and producing artifact-free animation across diverse body shapes and cloth types.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252461/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N frames (typically 5) of clothed human images at 512×512 resolution, requiring both front and back views per frame, along with estimated 3D body poses (joint angles and person-specific bone scales compatible with a pre-defined rigged template). It outputs a personalized canonical mesh with arbitrary topology (extracted via Marching Tetrahedra from a DMTet grid), a per-vertex skinning weight matrix (V×J), and a pose-dependent vertex displacement field, which together enable animation to any target pose via LBS plus learned correction. Inference completes in under 20 seconds on a single A100 GPU.

## Method

FRESA learns a universal clothed human model from 1100+ subjects that jointly decodes personalized canonical geometry, skinning weights, and pose-dependent deformation from a single bi-plane feature. A 3D canonicalization pipeline first estimates normals and segmentation, lifts them to surface meshes via normal integration, unposes them using arbitrary (nearest-template) skinning weights, and re-renders to produce pixel-aligned canonical references that simplify feature extraction. A multi-frame encoder then averages high-resolution (shallow CNN) and low-resolution (DeepLabV3) features across all input frames to fuse a robust bi-plane representation that preserves person-specific identity while discarding pose-specific artifacts. Three separate MLP decoders sample from this bi-plane: one predicts SDF values and vertex displacements on a DMTet tetrahedral grid for geometry, one predicts per-vertex skinning weights with a Softmax output, and one predicts pose-dependent vertex corrections conditioned on rendered target-pose position maps. Training uses a two-stage schedule—first canonical-space supervision with pseudo ground truths to initialize geometry, then posed-space supervision with LBS rendering, perceptual loss, skinning-weight regularization toward template weights, and an edge-length penalty to prevent over-stretched triangles.

## Results

On the Dome Data test set (35 unseen subjects), FRESA (Full Model) achieves Normal error 0.026, P2S 0.37 cm, and CD 0.43 cm, substantially outperforming all baselines. On RenderPeople (130 synthetic subjects for zero-shot generalization), it reaches Normal 0.024, P2S 0.30 cm, and CD 0.34 cm. Inference takes only 18 seconds per subject, compared to 8 hours for Vid2Avatar and 3 hours for PuzzleAvatar. The pose-dependent deformation module provides a small but consistent improvement over LBS-only animation (e.g., P2S 0.37 vs. 0.43 cm on Dome Data).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FRESA outperforms all three baselines by a large margin on every geometry metric across both datasets. Against the feed-forward baseline ARCH++ [14], FRESA reduces P2S from 4.52 cm to 0.37 cm (12×) and CD from 5.07 cm to 0.43 cm (12×) on Dome Data, while also being faster (18 s vs. 26 s). Against the optimization-based Vid2Avatar [12], FRESA achieves roughly 2.6× lower P2S (0.37 vs. 0.98 cm) and 2.6× lower CD (0.43 vs. 1.12 cm) on Dome Data, while running in 18 seconds versus 8 hours. Against the diffusion-based PuzzleAvatar [55], FRESA reduces P2S from 1.47 cm to 0.37 cm (4×) on Dome Data and from 1.79 cm to 0.30 cm (6×) on RenderPeople, again with a 60× speedup (18 s vs. 3 h). Qualitatively, the fixed skinning weights used by all baselines produce visible over-stretched triangles in challenging poses, whereas FRESA's personalized skinning weights and pose-dependent deformation module generate smoother, more realistic animation with plausible garment wrinkles.

## Relation to Prior Work

FRESA sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: feed-forward animatable avatar reconstruction (ARCH, ARCH++) that uses fixed template skinning weights, and per-subject optimization methods (SCANimate, SNARF) that jointly fit personalized canonical shapes and skinning weights but require hours of test-time fitting. The paper departs from both by learning a universal prior over 1,100+ clothed subjects that jointly decodes personalized geometry, skinning weights, and pose-dependent deformation in a single feed-forward pass, using DMTet for geometry representation and bi-plane features for identity encoding. A key conceptual departure is the explicit 3D canonicalization step that uses arbitrary (nearest-template) skinning weights for unposing, accepting noisy initial conditions that the network learns to correct, rather than requiring expensive cyclic-consistency optimization as in SCANimate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: 18-second inference with zero-shot generalization to casually taken phone photos makes personalized animatable avatars accessible for XR, virtual try-on, and telepresence applications that previously required controlled capture or hours of optimization. However, the geometric fidelity is bounded by the tetrahedral grid resolution, and the method explicitly omits body-cloth interaction dynamics, long-hair motion, and extremely loose garment behavior that are not purely pose-dependent. Open questions include whether the universal prior can be extended to handle these complex dynamics, whether fewer than 5 input frames suffice in the wild, and whether the approach generalizes to non-human characters or multi-person scenes.
