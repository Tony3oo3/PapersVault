## Problem

Existing C-STVSR methods (VideoINR, MoTIF) rely on simple coordinate concatenation and pre-trained optical flow networks for motion representation, yet surprisingly, adding standard position encoding degrades rather than improves performance in this setting. This limits the model's ability to capture high-frequency spatial details and complex temporal motion, resulting in lower-quality interpolated frames.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252257/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two low-resolution RGB frames (I_0^L, I_1^L ∈ R^{3×H×W}) and produces a high-resolution intermediate frame I_t^H ∈ R^{3×sH×sW} at any continuous time t ∈ [0,1] and any spatial scale s, without requiring a fixed scaling factor at inference. The encoder (ZoomingSlowMo) produces three latent feature maps, and the B-spline and Fourier Mappers operate on nearest-neighbor sampled latent vectors at query coordinates, enabling truly arbitrary-scale and arbitrary-time interpolation. Training uses 720P Adobe240 videos with nine sequential frames, where the 1st and 9th serve as inputs and three intermediate frames as targets.

## Method

BF-STVSR introduces two axis-specific position encoding modules that replace the external optical flow network used by MoTIF: a B-spline Mapper that predicts B-spline coefficients, knots, and dilation from encoded video features to model smooth temporal motion, and a Fourier Mapper that predicts dominant spatial frequencies and their amplitudes to capture fine-grained detail. Both mappers use three-layer SIREN networks as estimators with hidden dimensions of 64, and the B-spline representation is evaluated via a custom CUDA kernel for efficiency. The predicted motion vectors drive forward warping with softmax splatting to propagate high-resolution spatial features to the target time, and the warped features are decoded with a nearest-neighbor upsampled template feature. A key finding is that removing the pre-trained RAFT optical flow network and its supervision loss (L_RAFT) actually improves performance, as the axis-specific encoders extract richer motion and spatial information directly from the encoded features, simplifying the training objective to a single Charbonnier loss on the output frame.

## Results

On the fixed-scale STVSR benchmark (×4 spatial, ×8 temporal), BF-STVSR achieves 31.17 dB PSNR / 0.8898 SSIM on GoPro-Center and 30.22 / 0.8802 on GoPro-Average, outperforming MoTIF (31.04 / 0.8877 and 30.04 / 0.8773) by ~0.13 dB PSNR. On Adobe240-Average, BF-STVSR reaches 30.12 / 0.8808, surpassing MoTIF by 0.30 dB. In video quality metrics (Table 3), BF-STVSR leads on VFIPS (83.01 vs. 81.89 for MoTIF on GoPro), tOF (0.480 vs. 0.517), and VMAF (61.06 vs. 59.82), confirming superior perceptual and temporal quality. On out-of-distribution scales (Table 2), BF-STVSR maintains the best PSNR across nearly all temporal/spatial scale combinations, with the sole exception at ×16 temporal / ×4 spatial.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BF-STVSR outperforms all two-stage Fixed-STVSR baselines (SuperSloMo/QVI/DAIN combined with Bicubic, EDVR, or BasicVSR) by a large margin of 2–4 dB PSNR on GoPro and Adobe240, confirming the advantage of end-to-end continuous modeling over cascaded VFI+VSR pipelines. Against the one-stage Fixed-STVSR method ZoomingSlowMo, BF-STVSR wins on GoPro-Center (+0.48 dB) and Adobe-Center (+0.57 dB) but trails on Vid4 (25.85 vs. 25.96), which the authors attribute to ZoomingSlowMo's training on the similar Vimeo90K dataset. TMNet, the strongest one-stage Fixed-STVSR baseline, is outperformed by BF-STVSR on GoPro-Average (+1.39 dB) and Adobe-Average (+1.82 dB) but again wins on Vid4. Among C-STVSR methods, BF-STVSR surpasses VideoINR by 0.81 dB on GoPro-Average and 0.85 dB on Adobe-Average, and MoTIF by 0.18 dB and 0.30 dB respectively, while also achieving lower tOF (0.480 vs. 0.517) and higher VMAF (61.06 vs. 59.82) on GoPro. On out-of-distribution scales (Table 2), BF-STVSR consistently beats the two-stage C-STVSR combinations (RIFE/EMA-VFI + LIIF/LTE) by 0.5–2.5 dB across all tested temporal and spatial scale combinations, demonstrating superior generalization to unseen interpolation factors.

## Relation to Prior Work

BF-STVSR builds directly on the C-STVSR pipeline established by MoTIF (ICCV 2023) and VideoINR (CVPR 2022), which use implicit neural representations with simple coordinate concatenation and a pre-trained optical flow network (RAFT) for motion. The key departure is replacing both of these components with axis-specific position encodings: B-spline basis functions for smooth temporal motion modeling (inspired by BTC for screen content SR) and Fourier basis functions for spatial feature interpolation (inspired by LTE for arbitrary-scale image SR). By learning motion directly from encoded video features rather than relying on an external flow estimator, the framework eliminates the optical flow supervision term from the training objective, simplifying the overall architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a self-contained C-STVSR model that removes the dependency on a pre-trained optical flow network, reducing both training complexity and inference cost while matching or exceeding prior performance. The empirical finding that standard position encoding degrades (rather than improves) C-STVSR performance when paired with optical flow guidance is a useful cautionary result for the INR-for-video community. However, the method still struggles with large-motion scenes, and the training process remains computationally expensive, leaving open questions about scalability to longer sequences and more challenging motion regimes.
