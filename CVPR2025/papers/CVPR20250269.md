## Problem

Vision-based State Space Models (SSMs) such as VMamba rely on predefined scanning directions (horizontal, vertical) to traverse image patches, making them unable to capture relationships between conceptually related but spatially non-adjacent patches. More critically, these fixed scanning routes are tied to the original image orientation, so rotating an image drastically changes the patch-processing sequence and degrades performance by up to 30%.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250269/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224 RGB images, which are first processed by the RFN module at four canonical rotations (0°, 90°, 180°, 270°) before being patchified into 16×16 patches (yielding a 14×14 token grid). The output is a class label over 100 miniImageNet categories, evaluated via top-1 and top-5 accuracy. Unlike standard SSMs that process 56×56 tokens, Spectral VMamba operates on the reduced 14×14 token set after its spectral reordering, contributing to lower FLOPs.

## Method

Spectral VMamba replaces VMamba's four-way SS2D scanning with a Spectral Traversal Scan (STS) that orders patches by the eigenvectors of the symmetric normalized graph Laplacian built from patch feature similarities (Gaussian-weighted k-NN adjacency, k=5). The first m=4 smallest eigenvectors are computed via the Arnoldi algorithm, and patches are traversed in both increasing and decreasing eigenvector-value order through parallel S6 selective scan blocks before being merged back to spatial layout. A Rotational Feature Normalizer (RFN) module rotates the input at four canonical angles, extracts features through a shared stem, unrotates each feature map, and aggregates via element-wise max, producing rotation-invariant patch features that feed the Laplacian construction. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 3.1) shows that because the Laplacian eigen-spectrum is invariant under isometric permutations, the traversal order—and thus the model's output—remains consistent regardless of image rotation.

## Results

On miniImageNet (224×224), Spectral VMamba achieves 87.86% top-1 accuracy (Tiny, 3.9G FLOPs), 88.09% (Small, 6.3G), and 88.17% (Base, 6.3G), outperforming all SSM-based and ViT-based baselines in Table 1. Compared to the closest competitor VMamba, the method improves top-1 accuracy by 1.61% (Tiny/Small) and 1.00% (Base) while using fewer FLOPs (e.g., 3.9G vs. 4.9G for Tiny). The Base model also surpasses the strongest ViT baseline XCiT-M24 (86.80%) by 1.37% and Swin-B (82.77%) by 5.40% in top-1 accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Spectral VMamba outperforms all seven compared baselines on miniImageNet top-1 accuracy. Against the closest SSM competitor VMamba, it gains +1.61% (Tiny: 87.86 vs. 86.25) and +1.00% (Base: 88.17 vs. 87.17) while using fewer FLOPs (3.9G vs. 4.9G for Tiny; 6.3G vs. 8.7G for Base). It also surpasses MSVMamba-T (86.48%) by 1.38%, LocalVim-T (82.12%) by 5.74%, and Vim-S (79.70%) by 8.39%. Among ViT baselines, the Base model exceeds XCiT-M24 (86.80%) by 1.37% and Swin-B (82.77%) by 5.40%. In the rotation-robustness ablation (Fig. 4), Spectral VMamba maintains ~87% accuracy at canonical 90°/180°/270° rotations, whereas VMamba drops by ~30% at 90° and ~20% at 180°, confirming the practical advantage of the spectral traversal and RFN module.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VMamba/SS2D lineage of vision-based State Space Models by replacing the fixed four-way raster scanning with a content-driven traversal order derived from the eigenvectors of a graph Laplacian over image patches. The conceptual departure is significant: rather than encoding spatial adjacency as the sole basis for sequence ordering (as in VMamba, ViM, MSVMamba, and LocalMamba), Spectral VMamba uses spectral graph analysis to cluster semantically related patches regardless of their spatial proximity. The Rotational Feature Normalizer (RFN) module, which aggregates features across canonical rotations before patchification, is a new component not present in prior SSM-for-vision work, and the paper positions its approach within the learned-canonicalization family of rotation-invariance methods rather than group-equivariant or symmetrization strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and previously unaddressed vulnerability of vision SSMs: their sensitivity to image rotation, which causes VMamba to lose ~30% accuracy at 90° rotation. The spectral traversal mechanism offers a principled, theoretically grounded (isometric invariance of the Laplacian spectrum) solution that could be adopted by other SSM-based vision architectures. However, the evaluation is limited to miniImageNet (100 classes), the RFN module restricts invariance to quarter-turn rotations, and the authors explicitly note that disconnected graph components (common in medical imaging) remain unhandled. Whether the spectral traversal idea generalizes to higher-resolution or multi-modal settings, and whether the Arnoldi-based eigensolver scales efficiently for very large patch counts, are open questions.
