## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting suffers from severe degradation (blur, floaters) under few-shot conditions, and while regularization-based approaches have been explored, the paper identifies that inadequate pointcloud initialization is a critical yet overlooked factor. Traditional SfM pipelines like COLMAP require at least 3 views for triangulation, discarding valuable two-view points that are especially important in texture-poor and peripheral regions, leading to sparse and unevenly distributed initialization points.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251013/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of sparse RGB images (3 views for LLFF, 12 views for Mip-NeRF360) with known camera poses, downsampled to 4× resolution, and produces a 3D Gaussian scene representation for real-time novel view synthesis. The intermediate output is a fine pointcloud that combines the standard multi-view triangulated points with additional two-view triangulated points restricted to attention regions, which serves as the initialization for the 3DGS optimization stage.

## Method

EAP-GS introduces an Attentional Pointcloud Augmentation (APA) technique in the initialization stage. It first projects the SfM-generated coarse pointcloud onto each view and uses DBSCAN clustering to delineate attention regions where point density is sparse (e.g., texture-poor or peripheral areas). Within these regions only, the view requirement for triangulation is relaxed from ≥3 to ≥2, generating additional 3D points from two-view tracks that would otherwise be discarded. These augmented points are combined with the original multi-view pointcloud to form a denser, more evenly distributed initialization. The entire pipeline is implemented on top of DetectorfreeSfM for improved feature extraction in texture-poor scenarios, and the resulting pointcloud feeds into standard 3DGS optimization, making APA a modular plug-in with minimal overhead that can be attached to any existing 3DGS-based method.

## Results

On the LLFF dataset (3 training views), EAP-GS achieves the best SSIM (0.6399) and LPIPS (0.1792) while matching FSGS closely on PSNR (18.93 vs. 19.10), all in just 1.75 minutes of training time. On the Mip-NeRF360 dataset (12 training views), EAP-GS achieves the best scores across all three metrics: PSNR 18.08, SSIM 0.4998, and LPIPS 0.3696, while requiring only 2.06 minutes of training—roughly 24× faster than CoR-GS (49.07 min) and 3× faster than FSGS (6.51 min). The method also uses fewer Gaussian kernels than 3DGS (125k vs. 379k on LLFF) while producing higher quality reconstructions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FSGS (the strongest few-shot 3DGS baseline), EAP-GS trades a marginal 0.17 dB PSNR drop on LLFF (18.93 vs. 19.10) for superior perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.1792 vs. 0.1888) and a 14.6× speedup (1.75 min vs. 25.49 min); on Mip-NeRF360, EAP-GS clearly wins on all three metrics (PSNR +0.15, SSIM +0.0196, LPIPS −0.0725) while training 3× faster. Against CoR-GS, EAP-GS outperforms on every metric on both datasets (e.g., PSNR 18.93 vs. 18.73 on LLFF; 18.08 vs. 17.93 on Mip-NeRF360) and is 8.5× faster on LLFF and 23.8× faster on Mip-NeRF360. Against DRGS, EAP-GS achieves substantially higher PSNR (+1.45 on LLFF, +0.97 on Mip-NeRF360) and better LPIPS, though DRGS is slightly faster in training time. Against vanilla 3DGS, EAP-GS improves PSNR by 4.3 dB on LLFF and 2.0 dB on Mip-NeRF360 while using fewer Gaussians, confirming that the initialization bottleneck is the primary cause of few-shot degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) as the reconstruction backbone and DetectorfreeSfM for feature extraction, but departs conceptually from the dominant line of few-shot 3DGS work (FSGS, CoR-GS, DRGS) that focuses on regularization and densification strategies during optimization. Instead, EAP-GS identifies the SfM-based initialization stage as the underexplored bottleneck, arguing that COLMAP's requirement of ≥3 views per track discards valuable two-view correspondences that are critical when only a handful of images are available. The Attentional Pointcloud Augmentation (APA) technique is positioned as a modular SfM extension that can be grafted onto any existing 3DGS-based optimizer with minimal overhead, rather than a new optimization pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's core claim—that inadequate pointcloud initialization, not insufficient regularization, is a primary cause of few-shot 3DGS degradation—reframes where practitioners should invest effort, and the modular APA design makes the fix broadly applicable. However, the authors acknowledge that improvements remain limited for extrapolation views due to fundamental data incompleteness, and that two-view tracks can introduce subtle triangulation errors without a principled error-bounding mechanism. Open questions include whether incorporating generative priors or depth models can further close the gap in unseen regions, and whether the attention-region delineation (DBSCAN-based) generalizes to highly unbounded or dynamic scenes.
