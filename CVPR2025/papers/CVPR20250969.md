## Problem

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have excelled at text-image comprehension and generation, yet real-world data often forms multimodal attributed graphs (MMAGs) where nodes carry both text and image attributes connected by relational edges. It remains underexplored how MLLMs can incorporate the graph structure alongside node-level semantics to perform multimodal content generation, facing challenges of graph size explosion, non-Euclidean topology, hierarchical modality dependency, and inference ordering.

## Input / Output

Given a target node in an MMAG, the method first selects K most relevant neighbors via personalized PageRank, then encodes each neighbor's text (tokenized) and image (CLIP-encoded) into graph tokens that are interleaved with word and image tokens in the MLLM's autoregressive sequence. The model outputs both a generated text description and a generated image for the target node, with the image decoded from learned image tokens via a Stable Diffusion decoder conditioned on the full text-image-graph token sequence.

## Method

GRAPHGPT-O extends DreamLLM by injecting graph tokens into the autoregressive MLLM sequence, trained jointly with a Stable Diffusion image decoder. A personalized PageRank-based sampling strategy selects the top-K most relevant neighbors to avoid exponential subgraph growth. The key architectural contribution is a hierarchical aligner: a Node Feature Q-Former fuses each neighbor's text and image tokens via self-attention layers followed by cross-attention with learnable queries to produce fixed-size node representations, and a Graph Structure Q-Former then aggregates these node representations across the local subgraph via additional self-attention and cross-attention layers to yield compact graph tokens. The paper also systematically compares linear tokenization (simple concatenation of neighbor text/image tokens) against this hierarchical encoding, and explores sequential (text-then-image or image-then-text) versus parallel inference strategies to manage cross-modal generation dependency.

## Results

On the three MMAG benchmarks, GRAPHGPT-O (Soft) achieves the best image-text alignment (CLIP-IT) across all datasets: 25.63 on ART500K, 27.38 on Amazon-Beauty, and 31.14 on Amazon-Baby. It also attains the lowest KL divergence on ART500K (0.43) and Amazon-Baby (0.23), and the lowest text perplexity on ART500K (59.8) and Amazon-Baby (103.2). The linearization variant GRAPHGPT-O (Hard) achieves the highest image-to-image CLIP score on ART500K (77.62), indicating stronger visual fidelity in that setting. Overall, the hierarchical aligner (Soft) provides the most balanced improvements across all four evaluation metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRAPHGPT-O (Soft) outperforms its backbone DreamLLM on all four metrics across all three datasets, with the most dramatic gains in text perplexity (e.g., 555.7→59.8 on ART500K, a ~9× reduction) and KL divergence on Amazon-Baby (1.92→0.23). Against Chameleon, GRAPHGPT-O (Soft) wins on 11 of 12 metric-dataset combinations, losing only on Amazon-Beauty perplexity (285.0 vs. 180.9). Compared to Emu3 and Janus, GRAPHGPT-O (Soft) dominates on CLIP-IT and KL-DV across all datasets, though Janus achieves a lower KL-DV on Amazon-Beauty (1.45 vs. 5.82). The linearization variant GRAPHGPT-O (Hard) excels in image fidelity (CLIP-I2 of 77.62 on ART500K) but is less consistent on text quality and KL divergence, confirming that the hierarchical aligner provides a more balanced multimodal generation capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two largely separate research threads: (1) MLLMs capable of joint text-and-image generation (DreamLLM, Emu3, Chameleon) and (2) LLMs operating on graph-structured data (GraphGPT, LLaGA, InstructGraph). Prior graph+LLM work has focused almost exclusively on text-attributed graphs, while prior MLLMs ignore relational structure entirely. GRAPHGPT-O departs from both by introducing a hierarchical Q-Former aligner (inspired by BLIP-2's Q-Former) that encodes multimodal node features and local graph topology into fixed-length tokens, and by using Personalized PageRank sampling to control context length — a combination not previously explored for multimodal generation on graphs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper formalizes and benchmarks a task (joint text+image generation for a node given its MMAG neighborhood) that had no prior formulation, providing three cross-domain datasets (art, e-commerce) that the community can use. The hierarchical aligner architecture — node-level modality fusion followed by subgraph-level structure aggregation — is a practical and modular design that could be adapted to other graph+MLLM settings. However, the evaluation is limited to three datasets and a single generation task; the authors themselves note that discriminative tasks on MMAGs and heterogeneous edge relations remain open. The PPR sampling, while effective, is a heuristic whose optimality is not theoretically grounded.
