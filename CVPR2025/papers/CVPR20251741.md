## Problem

3D fluorescence confocal microscopy suffers from spatially varying noise and anisotropic resolution (axial lags lateral by up to 4.5×), yet paired high-resolution, low-noise ground truth is inaccessible because laser power must remain low to preserve cell viability during long-term live-cell imaging. Existing unsupervised SR and denoising methods (CycleGAN, CinCGAN, deep image prior) fail to exploit the unique intra-volume imaging priors inherent to 3D cell volumes, leaving both noise suppression and axial super-resolution unsolved simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251741/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-resolution, high-noise 3D cell volume (512×712×94, mCherry-labeled cell membrane fluorescence from live-cell embryos) where the XY plane is high-resolution but the Z-axis is low-resolution, and produces a denoised, isotropically super-resolved 3D volume. No paired ground truth is required; the model mines priors directly from the input volume itself. At inference, the two trained generators are applied sequentially—first the denoiser, then the SR module—on the raw volume to yield the final output.

## Method

VTCD frames both denoising and super-resolution as two conditional diffusion processes (CDDPs) within a cycle-in-cycle GAN framework with two generators and two discriminators. The Spatially Iso-Distributed Denoiser (SID-Denoiser) models the forward diffusion stage as continuous Z-axis slicing (exploiting the fact that noise increases along Z in confocal imaging) and guides the reverse trajectory via hyperplane-based semantic editing of the latent encoding, progressively reducing spatially varying noise slice-by-slice. The Cross-Plane Global-Propagation SR Module (CPGP-SRM) projects 3D grid coordinates onto XY-plane slices, extracts 2D features with a fixed ResNet32, and uses an Accumulator MLP to aggregate neighboring spatial features, thereby propagating high-resolution XY-plane structural details into the XZ and YZ planes. The two CDDPs are coupled through dual cycle-consistent losses that enforce structural consistency across planes, enabling the model to learn intra-volume priors entirely without paired ground truth.

## Results

On the full-reference benchmark, VTCD achieves PSNR of 32.57 / 40.79 / 34.93 dB and SSIM of 0.636 / 0.987 / 0.814 on FullrefXY-1/2/3, outperforming the strongest baselines (VTCD+IPG, CycleGAN, DASR) by 0.5–1.9 dB in PSNR. On the no-reference axial SR benchmark, VTCD attains the best PIQE (34.70 / 46.57 / 53.26) and NRQM (4.05 / 5.74 / 6.36) on NorefZ-1/2/3, with a 9.8% PIQE improvement over VTCD+IPG on NorefZ-1. The axial resolution is enhanced from ~430 nm to ~90 nm, a roughly 4.5× improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VTCD outperforms all four primary baselines (CycleGAN, CinCGAN, DASR, Neuroclear) on PSNR and SSIM across all three full-reference datasets, with the largest margin on FullrefXY-2 where it leads DASR by ~2.3 dB in PSNR and Neuroclear by ~4.0 dB. Among the diffusion-based variants, VTCD edges out VTCD+IPG on PSNR/SSIM (e.g., 40.79 vs 37.92 on FullrefXY-2) and on PIQE across all no-reference datasets (34.70 vs 38.55 on NorefZ-1, a 9.8% improvement), while VTCD+IPG retains a slight NIQE advantage on NorefZ-1 (15.75 vs 15.92) and NorefZ-2 (12.87 vs 13.42). On NRQM, VTCD achieves the best scores on NorefZ-2 (5.74) and NorefZ-3 (6.36), surpassing VTCD+IPG by 0.07 and 1.49 respectively. CycleGAN and CinCGAN consistently produce the worst full-reference fidelity (lowest PSNR/SSIM) and the highest NIQE/PIQE, confirming that standard cycle-consistency without diffusion-based priors is insufficient for 3D fluorescence volumes. Overall, VTCD wins or ties on the majority of metrics, with the clearest advantages in PSNR, SSIM, PIQE, and NRQM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cycle-in-cycle GAN framework for volumetric fluorescence microscopy (Park et al., 2022) by replacing the GAN generators with conditional diffusion models, creating a dual cycle-consistent diffusion (VTCD) architecture. The spatially iso-distributed denoiser borrows the hyperplane-based latent editing technique from Shen et al. (2020) to guide reverse diffusion trajectories using semantic content from adjacent slices, while the cross-plane SR module adapts the 3D coordinate projection strategy from Holodiffusion (Karnewar et al., 2023) to propagate XY-plane high-resolution features into XZ/YZ planes. The key departure from prior work is the explicit modeling of intra-volume imaging priors—noise distribution consistency along Z and structural consistency across planes—as the forward process of two coupled conditional diffusion models, enabling simultaneous denoising and super-resolution without any paired ground truth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a fundamental practical bottleneck in live-cell 3D imaging: the 4.5× anisotropy between axial and lateral resolution combined with spatially varying noise, which together make volumetric structural analysis unreliable. The unsupervised formulation is essential because maintaining cell viability during long-term observation precludes acquiring high-laser-power reference volumes, and the released 600-volume 4D dataset (with both full-reference and no-reference splits) provides a much-needed benchmark for the community. However, generalizability remains an open question: all experiments use mCherry-stained C. elegans embryos on two specific confocal systems, and it is unclear whether the intra-volume priors (noise consistency along Z, structural continuity across planes) hold for other cell types, fluorescent labels, or microscopy modalities (e.g., light-sheet, STED). The moderate per-component ablation gains (0.4–1.2% PSNR) also suggest that the architecture's individual modules contribute incrementally rather than synergistically.
