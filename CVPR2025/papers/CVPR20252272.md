## Problem

Existing video diffusion models rely on a single text prompt and cannot reliably generate sequences of multiple events with correct ordering or precise timing control. When events are concatenated into one prompt, state-of-the-art models ignore some events or produce them in the wrong order, and autoregressive or clip-concatenation approaches suffer from quality degradation, stagnation, or abrupt scene cuts. The core gap is the absence of explicit temporal binding between event descriptions and their corresponding time ranges in the generated video.

## Input / Output

The model takes a global caption describing scene content (background, subject appearance), a sequence of temporal captions each specifying an event description with explicit start and end timestamps, optional scene cut timestamps, and optionally an initial RGB image for the I2V setting. It produces a 512×288 video up to 12 seconds long where each event occurs within its designated time window with smooth transitions. Unlike autoregressive methods that generate fixed-length clips sequentially, MinT processes all event prompts simultaneously in a single diffusion pass, enabling flexible per-event duration control.

## Method

MinT fine-tunes a pre-trained latent Diffusion Transformer (DiT) by inserting a temporal cross-attention layer in each DiT block, where text embeddings of all event captions are concatenated and cross-attended with video tokens. The key mechanism is Rescaled RoPE (ReRoPE), a positional encoding that rescales each event's time span to a uniform length L before applying rotary rotations, ensuring video tokens attend most strongly to the current event's caption, peak at the event midpoint, and attend equally to adjacent events at boundaries. Scene cuts are handled as special zero-duration events with a learnable embedding, allowing the model to either suppress or explicitly place shot transitions. The model is trained on ~200k manually annotated videos with temporal event labels using the rectified flow objective, and an LLM-based prompt enhancer (GPT-4) can automatically expand short user prompts into the required global and temporal caption format.

## Results

In the I2V setting on HoldOut, MinT achieves FID 22.04 and FVD 218.21, substantially outperforming MEVG (FID 57.57, FVD 495.75), while also improving text-to-video alignment (TA 3.00 vs 2.72) and reducing scene cuts from 0.108 to 0.025 per video. On StoryBench, the gap widens further: MinT scores FID 21.85 vs MEVG's 56.51, and TA 3.37 vs 2.83. In the T2V setting, MinT generates all prompted events with smooth transitions and consistent subjects, whereas Concat baselines (on CogVideoX, Mochi, and the base model) typically produce only a subset of events, and MEVG suffers from abrupt transitions and identity drift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Concat baselines on CogVideoX and Mochi, MinT wins decisively on event completeness and temporal structure: the SOTA open-source models generate only a subset of prompted events when all descriptions are concatenated into a single prompt, while MinT produces all events in the correct order with smooth transitions. Against MEVG (the state-of-the-art multi-event method), MinT achieves roughly 2.6× lower FID (22.04 vs 57.57 on HoldOut) and 2.3× lower FVD (218.21 vs 495.75), indicating substantially better visual fidelity. MEVG also produces 4–10× more scene cuts (0.108 vs 0.025 on HoldOut; 0.150 vs 0.014 on StoryBench), reflecting its tendency toward abrupt transitions between events. On text alignment, MinT leads MEVG by 0.28 in TA on HoldOut (3.00 vs 2.72) and 0.54 on StoryBench (3.37 vs 2.83), confirming tighter event-caption correspondence. In the T2V setting, MinT also outperforms the autoregressive AutoReg baseline, which suffers from video stagnation and fails to generate later events, and the MEVG baseline, which loses subject identity across events with dissimilar captions.

## Relation to Prior Work

MinT builds on the latent Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture and the rectified-flow training objective, adding a dedicated temporal cross-attention layer to the standard self-attention + cross-attention + MLP block structure. Its core positional encoding, ReRoPE, is a direct modification of Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE), rescaling event timestamps to a uniform length so that attention bias correctly localizes each event. The paper departs from autoregressive multi-event generation (Phenaki, MEVG, FreeNoise), which generates events sequentially and cannot control individual event durations, and from personalized video generation pipelines that concatenate independently generated clips, producing abrupt scene cuts. By processing all event captions jointly in a single forward pass with temporally grounded cross-attention, MinT unifies multi-event synthesis with fine-grained timing control in one model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first video generator offering explicit, per-event temporal control, addressing a clear and previously unaddressed limitation of all current text-to-video systems. The ReRoPE mechanism is a clean, lightweight addition (one extra cross-attention layer per DiT block) that could be adopted by other video diffusion architectures seeking temporal conditioning. Open questions include scalability beyond 12-second videos, handling of overlapping or nested events, and whether the approach generalizes to more complex narrative structures (e.g., branching timelines). The reliance on ~200k manually annotated temporal events and a GPT-4 prompt enhancer also raises questions about data efficiency and the quality ceiling of LLM-generated temporal plans.
