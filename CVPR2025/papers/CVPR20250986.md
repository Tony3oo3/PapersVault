## Problem

The 4p3v problem—estimating the relative pose of three calibrated cameras from four point correspondences—is notoriously difficult, with existing solutions either relying on unbounded epipole sweeps or homotopy continuation with a ~26% failure rate. No efficient, numerically stable, and practical solver existed for this configuration, leaving a gap between the theoretical uniqueness of the solution and its reliable computation in RANSAC-based pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250986/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes four 2D point correspondences visible in all three calibrated views (the (4,4,4) configuration, which is over-constrained with 12 constraints for 11 DoF) along with known camera intrinsics. It outputs the full relative pose (rotation and translation) between all three cameras. The solvers are designed to run as hypothesis generators inside RANSAC frameworks (PoseLib, GC-RANSAC), where each 4-point sample produces one or a few pose hypotheses that are subsequently scored and refined on inliers.

## Method

The core idea decomposes the three-view problem into estimating an approximate geometry of the first two views from four correspondences, then registering the third camera via P3P. Two variants are proposed: 4p3v(A) estimates an affine fundamental matrix from the four points, while 4p3v(M) generates a fifth approximate correspondence as the mean of three input points (exact under paraperspective projection) and feeds it to the 5-point essential matrix solver. Practical enhancements include the 4p3v(M±δ) variant that perturbs the mean point along the longest triangle dimension to call the 5pt solver three times, early non-minimal refitting (ENM) of the two-view geometry on detected inliers, filtering geometrically infeasible solutions using the fourth point in the third view (+F), and a few iterations of Levenberg-Marquardt refinement on the original four points in three views (+R). The approach is entirely built from existing efficient solvers (4pt affine F, 5pt, P3P), making it simple to implement and fast to run.

## Results

On PhotoTourism, the best proposed solver 4p3v(M±δ)+R+F+ENM achieves an average pose error of 4.66° and AUC@10° of 77.14%, outperforming the state-of-the-art 4p3v(HC) solver (6.37° AVG, 73.53% AUC@10°) by ~1.7° in mean error. On Cambridge Landmarks, the same solver reaches 6.65° AVG and 66.95% AUC@10° versus 8.73° and 64.58% for 4p3v(HC). On Aachen Day-Night, it achieves 9.96° AVG and 57.42% AUC@10° versus 12.43° and 55.20% for 4p3v(HC). The best solver runs at 112.6 ms (PhotoTourism), roughly 1.8× slower than 4p3v(HC) at 64.1 ms, but the lighter 4p3v(M)+R+F variant runs at 30.9 ms while still beating 4p3v(HC) in accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against the state-of-the-art 4p3v(HC) solver [21], which uses homotopy continuation with an MLP-based anchor selection. All proposed solvers with the suggested modifications (+R, +F, +ENM) outperform 4p3v(HC) in pose accuracy across all three datasets: on PhotoTourism, 4p3v(M±δ)+R+F+ENM reduces the average error from 6.37° to 4.66° (−1.7°) and improves AUC@10° from 73.53% to 77.14%; on Cambridge Landmarks, from 8.73° to 6.65°; on Aachen Day-Night, from 12.43° to 9.96°. The trade-off is runtime: the best solver is ~1.8× slower than 4p3v(HC) on PhotoTourism (112.6 ms vs 64.1 ms), though the lighter 4p3v(M)+R+F variant (30.9 ms) still beats 4p3v(HC) in accuracy while being ~2× faster. The 5pt+P3P baseline, which requires five correspondences in the first two views (vs. four for the proposed methods), achieves 5.40° AVG on PhotoTourism without modifications; with +R+F+ENM it reaches 4.99°, slightly below the proposed 4p3v(M±δ)+R+F+ENM (4.66°) but at a lower runtime (56.5 ms vs 112.6 ms). The paper notes that 4p3v(HC) has a reported 26.3% success rate on noise-free data and cannot benefit from ENM refitting since it estimates all three cameras jointly, whereas the proposed decomposed solvers can refit the two-view geometry early, which is critical for the affine-based 4p3v(A) variant to be competitive.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the well-established decomposition strategy of estimating two-view relative pose and then registering a third camera via P3P, leveraging the efficient 5-point essential matrix solver (Nistér, 2004) and the P3P solver (Persson & Nordberg, 2018) as core building blocks. It departs from the state-of-the-art homotopy continuation approach (Hruby et al., CVPR 2022), which requires an MLP-based classifier to select starting anchors and still achieves only 26.3% success on noiseless data, by instead introducing a simple mean-point correspondence that approximates a 5th point from three existing correspondences. The affine-camera variant (4p3v(A)) draws on the paraperspective approximation framework of Pritts et al. (2013), while the mean-point variant (4p3v(M)) is a novel geometric observation grounded in the property that under affine projection, the mean of 3D points projects to the mean of their 2D projections.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4p3v problem has long been considered practically unsolved, with existing methods either too slow (epipole sweeping requiring ~1000 candidates) or too unreliable (HC with 26.3% success rate). The finding that a trivially simple mean-point strategy, combined with standard non-minimal refitting and a few LM iterations, outperforms the complex HC solver on real data shifts the practical landscape toward simpler, more implementable solutions. Limitations include scene-dependent accuracy without ENM refitting and the fact that the 4p3v(A) variant remains significantly less accurate than 4p3v(M). Open questions include whether the mean-point correspondence idea generalizes to other minimal multi-view configurations and whether the approach extends to uncalibrated or generalized camera models.
