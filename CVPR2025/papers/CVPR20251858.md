## Problem

Two-stage open-vocabulary segmentation methods that pair a mask proposal generator (e.g., SAM) with CLIP incur high computational costs and introduce domain gaps from cropped regions, while one-stage correlation-based methods are efficient but produce less accurate masks due to CLIP's limited spatial localization at low-resolution feature levels. The paper asks how to harness SAM's powerful class-agnostic spatial aggregation capability within an efficient one-stage inference pipeline without running the full SAM encoder-decoder pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251858/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image resized to 336×336 and a set of N_c text descriptions (one per candidate class) as input, processed separately through CLIP's vision and language encoders. It outputs a pixel-level segmentation mask at the original image resolution, produced by a U-Net-style upsampling decoder that uses CLIP image encoder features as skip connections. The model operates at CLIP's low-resolution feature grid (e.g., 21×21 for ViT-B/16), and all per-class operations (pseudo prompt generation, SAM block processing, VLF) are batch-parallelized over N_c classes to maintain efficiency.

## Method

ESC-Net is a one-stage architecture that inserts N=4 pre-trained SAM transformer decoder blocks between CLIP's encoders and a mask decoder. The key mechanism is a Pseudo Prompt Generator (PPG) that converts the CLIP image-text cosine similarity correlation map into SAM-compatible prompts: it applies softmax and thresholding to obtain binary object regions, clusters them via k-means into N_o=5 regions per class, and extracts pseudo points (highest-probability pixels) and pseudo masks (region boundaries) as sparse and dense prompt embeddings. These prompts are fed into the SAM blocks, which perform self-attention on prompts followed by bidirectional cross-attention with CLIP image features, aggregating spatial context in a class-agnostic manner. A Vision-Language Fusion (VLF) module then refines the correlation map using Swin Transformer blocks (image guidance) and a linear attention mechanism (text guidance, order-agnostic over variable N_c). The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end on COCO-Stuff with fine-tuned CLIP attention layers, requiring no SAM image encoder and achieving inference costs comparable to CAT-Seg while outperforming it by 2–4 mIoU points.

## Results

ESC-Net achieves state-of-the-art mIoU across all six open-vocabulary segmentation benchmarks. With CLIP ViT-L/14, it reaches 18.1 on ADE20K-A847, 27.0 on PASCAL-Context-PC459, 41.8 on ADE20K-A150, 65.6 on PC-59, 98.3 on PAS-20, and 86.3 on PAS-20b, outperforming the previous best (CAT-Seg) by 1.1–3.9 mIoU points. With CLIP ViT-B/16, it similarly leads with 13.3 (A-847), 21.1 (PC-459), 35.6 (A-150), 59.0 (PC-59), 97.3 (PAS-20), and 80.1 (PAS-20b). The model also matches CAT-Seg's computational efficiency (451.3M params, 0.76 s inference, 2,203.5 GFLOPs vs. 433.7M, 0.78 s, 2,121.1 GFLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ESC-Net outperforms all 19 compared baselines on every reported benchmark in both CLIP ViT-B/16 and CLIP ViT-L/14 settings. The closest competitor is CAT-Seg [5], the prior one-stage SOTA, which ESC-Net surpasses by +2.1 mIoU on A-847, +3.2 on PC-459, +3.9 on A-150, +2.3 on PC-59, +1.1 on PAS-20, and +3.8 on PAS-20b (ViT-L/14). Among two-stage methods, EBSeg [30] (which uses SAM ViT-B as an additional backbone) is the strongest on PC-459 (21.0) and PC-59 (60.2) with ViT-L/14, but ESC-Net exceeds it by 6.0 and 5.4 mIoU respectively while using no additional backbone. SED [33] (ConvNeXt-L) leads on PC-459 (22.6) and PC-59 (60.6) among non-CLIP methods, yet ESC-Net still surpasses it by 4.4 and 5.0 mIoU. On the smaller ViT-B/16 setting, ESC-Net beats CAT-Seg by +1.3 (A-847), +2.1 (PC-459), +3.8 (A-150), +0.6 (PC-59), +0.3 (PAS-20), and +2.8 (PAS-20b), confirming consistent gains across model scales. Notably, ESC-Net achieves these improvements with comparable inference cost to CAT-Seg (0.76 s vs. 0.78 s on a single RTX A6000), whereas two-stage methods like ZegFormer and OVSeg require 3–4× longer inference times.

## Relation to Prior Work

ESC-Net occupies a middle ground between two dominant paradigms in open-vocabulary semantic segmentation: two-stage methods (ZegFormer, ZSseg, OpenSeg) that chain a full SAM or MaskFormer proposal generator with CLIP classification, and one-stage correlation-based methods (CAT-Seg, SAN, ZegCLIP) that model image-text similarity directly at the pixel level. The key architectural departure is extracting only SAM's transformer decoder blocks—bypassing SAM's image encoder entirely—and feeding them pseudo-prompts (points and masks) derived from CLIP's image-text correlation map, thereby importing SAM's learned spatial aggregation into a one-stage pipeline. The fine-tuning strategy for CLIP's attention layers follows CAT-Seg, and the Swin-Transformer-based VLF module draws on the same design choices, positioning ESC-Net as a targeted enhancement of the CAT-Seg framework rather than a wholly new formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that ESC-Net achieves state-of-the-art mIoU across ADE20K, PASCAL-VOC, and PASCAL-Context while maintaining inference cost comparable to the strongest one-stage baseline (CAT-Seg), making SAM's spatial aggregation accessible without the 4× overhead of a full two-stage SAM pipeline. The ablation showing that pretrained SAM blocks contribute a ~12-point jump over random initialization on A-847 underscores that the value lies in SAM's learned spatial priors, not merely in added transformer capacity. However, the method still operates on low-resolution CLIP features, limiting boundary precision, and memory scales linearly with the number of candidate classes—both constraints that remain open for very large vocabulary settings or high-resolution deployment.
