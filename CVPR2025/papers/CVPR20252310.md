## Problem

In exemplar-free continual learning, feature representations of previously learned classes drift as new tasks are trained, causing progressive catastrophic forgetting. Existing interference-free subspace methods (e.g., InfLoRA, Adam-NSCL) rely on static statistics or features stored from old tasks to define protected subspaces, but these become increasingly outdated as the task sequence grows, leading to noticeable performance degradation over long sequences. The paper asks whether one can construct a drift-resistant training space that adapts dynamically to the evolving feature geometry without storing any old-task data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224 RGB images (normalized to [0,1]) in a sequential class-incremental stream where each task introduces non-overlapping classes and no exemplars from prior tasks are retained. The frozen ViT-B/16-IN21K backbone with LoRA adapters on key/value attention projections produces class-label predictions. A key operational detail is that before each new task, the model's weights are temporarily modified via LoRA subtraction to compute the DRS, after which the original accumulated weights are restored for actual training.

## Method

The core mechanism is LoRA Subtraction (LoRA⁻): before training task t, the cumulative LoRA task vector (Σ BⱼAⱼ for j < t) is subtracted from the pretrained weights W₀, yielding a modified weight matrix W̃ that has effectively 'unlearned' all prior tasks. New-task data is forwarded through W̃ to compute per-layer input feature covariance, whose SVD yields a projection matrix P defining the Drift-Resistant Space (DRS). During training, gradient updates to the new LoRA branch are projected into DRS at every step (Δw = P Pᵀ g), restricting updates to directions that minimally disturb old-task representations. To counteract the plasticity loss from gradient projection, an Augmented Triplet Loss (ATL) is added to the cross-entropy objective, using both intra-batch hard pairs and stored class prototypes from previous tasks to enforce inter-class separation in the feature space.

## Results

On ImageNet-R with 50 incremental tasks, LoRA⁻DRS achieves 77.94% average accuracy (ACC̄₅₀) and 72.12% final-task accuracy (ACC₅₀), outperforming the runner-up EASE by 2.17% and 3.58% respectively. On CIFAR-100 with 50 tasks, the method reaches 91.29% ACC̄₅₀ and 87.29% ACC₅₀, a 3.64% margin over EASE. Backward transfer (BWT) scores of −3.38 (CIFAR-100) and −3.90 (ImageNet-R) at 25 tasks are the best among all compared methods, confirming superior forgetting resistance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoRA⁻DRS outperforms all seven baselines on both ImageNet-R and CIFAR-100 across all task counts (10, 20, 25, 50). The closest competitor is EASE [49], which leads at 10 tasks on ImageNet-R (81.67 vs 81.16 ACC̄) but is surpassed by LoRA⁻DRS at 25 tasks (80.06 vs 79.65) and by a widening margin at 50 tasks (77.94 vs 75.77 ACC̄, a 2.17% gap). InfLoRA [22], the strongest PEFT-based drift-control method, trails by 3.19% in ACC̄₅₀ on ImageNet-R (69.95 vs 77.94) and by 20.99% on CIFAR-100 (70.29 vs 91.29), revealing severe degradation over long sequences. Adam-NSCL [37] and CODA-Prompt [33] show the most dramatic collapse at 50 tasks (58.49% and 55.59% ACC̄₅₀ on ImageNet-R respectively), while prompt-based methods L2P [40] and DualPrompt [39] remain competitive only at 10 tasks. In BWT at 25 tasks, LoRA⁻DRS achieves the best scores (−3.38 CIFAR-100, −3.90 ImageNet-R), narrowly edging Adam-NSCL (−4.39, −3.91) and substantially outperforming InfLoRA (−3.70, −7.56).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) for continual learning and feature-drift mitigation in exemplar-free class-incremental learning (EFCIL). It builds directly on the LoRA framework [14] and the task-arithmetic insight that negating a task vector can 'unlearn' specific knowledge [15], but departs from prior drift-control methods (InfLoRA, Adam-NSCL, EASE) that rely on stored static statistics of old tasks to define interference-free or null subspaces. Instead, LoRA Subtraction dynamically constructs a Drift-Resistant Space by subtracting cumulative LoRA weights from the pretrained backbone before each new task, eliminating the need for any stored old-task data. The Augmented Triplet Loss further extends standard metric-learning objectives by incorporating prototypes from all previous tasks as negative anchors, a design enabled by the stability that DRS provides.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and increasingly important limitation in EFCIL: as task sequences grow longer, static statistics from early tasks become progressively outdated, causing cumulative performance degradation that existing subspace methods cannot fully prevent. The simplicity of the LoRA subtraction operation (a single weight subtraction per layer) makes the approach easy to adopt and integrate into existing PEFT pipelines. However, the method still incurs incremental parameter overhead from accumulating LoRA branches across tasks, and the authors acknowledge this as a limitation. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond ViT backbones, whether the SVD-based DRS projection can be approximated more cheaply for very long sequences, and whether the task-arithmetic subtraction remains well-conditioned when many LoRA branches are accumulated.
