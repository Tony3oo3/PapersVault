## Problem

Existing real-world image dehazing methods either fine-tune pre-trained models or adapt inference procedures, making them heavily dependent on the quality of pre-training data, which is typically synthetic and suffers from a domain gap with real haze. Additionally, diffusion models—capable of recovering heavily distorted information—remain underutilized for dehazing due to their lengthy sampling processes. The paper addresses both gaps by generating realistic hazy training data at scale and designing an accelerated diffusion sampling strategy.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a single hazy RGB image as input (no depth map, atmospheric light estimate, or paired clean image required) and produces a dehazed RGB image at the same resolution. The two-stage system first uses HazeGen to synthesize 100,000 realistic hazy-clean image pairs for training, then DiffDehaze performs inference on a single hazy image via a 50-step accelerated diffusion sampling process that skips intermediate timesteps between τ=800 and ω=600.

## Method

The pipeline comprises HazeGen and DiffDehaze, both built on a fixed Stable Diffusion v2-1 model with IRControlNet for conditional injection. HazeGen preserves generative realism through hybrid training (70% unconditional loss on real hazy images, 30% conditional loss on synthetic pairs) and blended sampling (85% conditional + 15% unconditional noise predictions at each step), preventing the model from overfitting to the physical scattering model. DiffDehaze's key innovation is AccSamp: the Tiled Statistical Alignment Operation (AlignOp) aligns local patch mean and variance between the hazy input and a blurry early diffusion prediction to produce a faithful dehazing estimate, enabling the sampler to jump directly from timestep τ to ω. A haze density-aware fidelity guidance mechanism then refines the result using MSSIM loss weighted by a DCP-derived transmission map, applying stronger fidelity constraints in lightly hazy regions while allowing more generative freedom in densely hazy areas.

## Results

On the RTTS dataset, DiffDehaze achieves the best performance on 6 out of 7 no-reference quality metrics, with a standout 23.8% improvement on Q-Align (2.834 vs. 2.289 for the next-best PTTD) and substantial gains on LIQE (3.069 vs. 2.353), CLIPIQA (0.426 vs. 0.370), and MUSIQ (65.09 vs. 62.11). The only metric where DiffDehaze underperforms is FADE (1.138 vs. 0.712 for PTTD), which the authors attribute to FADE's insensitivity to color-distorted haze residuals. On BRISQUE, DiffDehaze achieves the lowest score (16.49), indicating the least perceived distortion among all methods tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffDehaze outperforms all five baselines on Q-Align, LIQE, CLIPIQA, ManIQA, MUSIQ, and BRISQUE, with the largest margins over PTTD (the strongest competitor) on Q-Align (+0.545) and LIQE (+0.716). Against RIDCP, DiffDehaze leads by +0.350 on Q-Align and +0.518 on LIQE, while also improving BRISQUE from 17.29 to 16.49. The gap over older methods is even wider: DAD, PSD, and D4 trail by more than 0.75 on Q-Align and 1.0+ on LIQE. The sole exception is FADE, where PTTD (0.712) and PSD (0.920) score lower than DiffDehaze (1.138); the authors demonstrate with failure-case examples that FADE incorrectly rewards outputs that merely shift haze color toward blue without effective removal. Qualitatively, DiffDehaze recovers vivid details (e.g., tree textures) that all five baselines fail to produce, and consistently removes haze from heavily distorted regions where DAD, PSD, D4, RIDCP, and PTTD leave visible residual haze.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DiffBIR architecture (Stable Diffusion + IRControlNet) and repurposes the generative prior of a pre-trained text-to-image diffusion model for a novel hazing-dehazing pipeline. It departs from prior real-world dehazing methods (PSD, PTTD, RIDCP) that fine-tune or adapt inference of pre-trained dehazing networks, instead generating realistic hazy training data from scratch via hybrid conditional/unconditional training and blended sampling. The AccSamp sampling process draws on adaptive instance normalization and image-level normalization ideas, applying them to diffusion-based dehazing for the first time to skip intermediate denoising steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hazing-dehazing pipeline addresses a fundamental bottleneck in real-world dehazing: the near-impossibility of collecting large-scale paired hazy-clean data. By showing that a text-to-image diffusion model's generative prior already encodes realistic haze statistics, the paper offers a scalable path to training data that prior physical-scattering-model-based synthesis could not match. Open questions remain around the reliability of no-reference evaluation metrics (the authors themselves demonstrate FADE's failure modes) and whether the fidelity gains from AccSamp can be pushed further without additional sampling steps. The approach also raises the broader question of whether diffusion-based hazing generation can be extended to other atmospheric degradations (rain, fog, glare).
