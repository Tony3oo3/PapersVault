## Problem

Click-based interactive segmentation methods become inefficient and user-unfriendly when targets contain narrow, fine structures, because users must precisely locate and click on regions only a few pixels wide. Additionally, transformer-based methods are forced to downsample high-resolution images to a fixed size (e.g., 1024²) due to the quadratic complexity of global self-attention, irretrievably losing the fine details needed for accurate masks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250864/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image plus three types of user clicks—foreground, background, and a novel noise-tolerant click that only needs to fall in the approximate vicinity of fine structures without precise localization. All clicks are encoded as disk maps and concatenated with the RGB channels. The pipeline produces an intermediate three-class FBU map (foreground/background/uncertain) at 448² resolution from the ECP stage, followed by a final binary segmentation mask at up to 4096² resolution from the HRR stage.

## Method

NTClick introduces a two-stage workflow: an Explicit Coarse Perception (ECP) network (plain ViT with window and global attention, feature pyramid decoder) predicts a three-class FBU map at low resolution, where intricate regions are labeled 'uncertain' rather than forced into foreground or background. A High Resolution Refinement (HRR) network then upsamples the FBU map to high resolution, concatenates it with the RGB image, and classifies all uncertain pixels. To enable inference at up to 4096² without quadratic memory blowup, HRR replaces global attention with grid attention (interval K=8, reducing complexity from O((wh)²C) to O((wh)²C/K²)) and replaces window attention with neighborhood attention to restore direct local dependencies between adjacent patches that grid attention alone cannot establish. The ECP is trained with morphology-processed ground truth FBU maps (erosion/dilation of fine-grained masks), while HRR is supervised directly by ground truth binary masks.

## Results

NTClick achieves state-of-the-art performance across all seven test sets in Table 1, using both NoC@90 (lower is better) and 5-mIoU (higher is better) as metrics. On the most challenging DIS5K-TE4 (IPQ=443), NTClick reaches 9.15 NoC@90 and 87.98 5-mIoU, improving over the previous best (SegNext) by 5.46 in NoC@90 and 8.04 in 5-mIoU. On DIS5K-VD, NTClick achieves 7.23 NoC@90 and 89.23 5-mIoU, and on ThinObject-5K it reaches 2.76 NoC@90 and 95.20 5-mIoU. The method also supports dynamic resolution (1024²–4096²), with 4096² inference achievable on a single RTX 3090 at 0.81 s latency per click.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NTClick outperforms all nine compared baselines on every one of the seven test sets, with the largest margins on the most complex scenes. On DIS5K-TE4 (highest IPQ=443), NTClick reduces NoC@90 from 14.61 (SegNext) to 9.15 and lifts 5-mIoU from 79.94 to 87.98, a gain of 5.46 clicks and 8.04 mIoU points. On DIS5K-VD, the improvement over SegNext is 3.12 in NoC@90 (10.35→7.23) and 3.54 in 5-mIoU (85.69→89.23). Against the scribble-based Slim Scissors [6], NTClick achieves 89.23 vs. 82.57 5-mIoU on DIS5K-VD and 95.20 vs. 91.10 on ThinObject-5K while using only clicks (no scribbles). The advantage grows with scene complexity: on simpler HRSOD (IPQ=6) the NoC@90 gap over SegNext is 1.40, whereas on DIS5K-TE4 it widens to 5.46. NTClick also requires fewer total clicks per image (Fig. 6), and its noise-tolerant clicks eliminate the need for precise localization on fine regions, making the interaction strictly more user-friendly than standard foreground/background clicks.

## Relation to Prior Work

NTClick extends the click-based interactive segmentation lineage (RITM, SimpleClick, SegNext) by introducing a third interaction type—noise-tolerant click—that borrows the localization tolerance of scribble-based methods (Slim Scissors) while retaining click efficiency. The two-stage ECP→HRR workflow is conceptually inspired by the trimap paradigm in image matting, where a coarse three-class map guides a high-resolution refinement stage. Architecturally, the ECP follows the plain ViT + FPN decoder of SimpleClick/SegNext, while the HRR departs from standard ViT by substituting global attention with grid attention (MobileViT) and window attention with neighborhood attention (NAT) to enable ultra-high-resolution inference without quadratic complexity blowup.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The noise-tolerant click addresses a genuine usability bottleneck in interactive segmentation: precisely clicking on thin or intricate regions is impractical, and the proposed interaction form offers a practical middle ground between precise clicks and coarse scribbles. The ability to refine at 4096² resolution on a consumer GPU (vs. 1024² for SAM-HQ/SegNext) opens the door to high-fidelity annotation workflows for fine-grained datasets. However, the paper acknowledges a persistent gap to ground truth that additional clicks cannot close, and the diminishing returns after ~10 clicks remain an open challenge for all interactive methods. The approach's practical value for data annotation pipelines and large-vision-model training is significant, though the individual architectural components are borrowed rather than novel.
