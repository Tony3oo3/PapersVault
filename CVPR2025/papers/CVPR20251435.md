## Problem

Existing 3D hand pose estimation methods operate in the camera frame on single images, making them unsuitable for egocentric video where both hands and camera are continuously in motion. World-space hand motion reconstruction is further complicated by frequent hand occlusions, hands leaving the field of view, and the scale ambiguity inherent to monocular SLAM, for which no dedicated method has previously been proposed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251435/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single egocentric RGB video as input and produces, for each hand, a complete per-frame sequence of MANO parameters (pose, shape, global orientation, root translation) expressed in world coordinates, along with the corresponding camera trajectory in world space. A key design choice is that the output is a full T-frame sequence even when hands are invisible in many frames; the motion infiller network synthesizes the missing frames so the output is always temporally complete. Inference runs at 40 ms per frame in a single forward pass.

## Method

HaWoR decomposes world-space hand motion into three modules. First, a hand motion estimation network extends WiLoR's ViT backbone with an Image Attention Module (IAM) that fuses appearance features across adjacent frames to handle boundary truncation, and a Pose Attention Module (PAM) that applies temporal self-attention directly to MANO pose parameters to learn motion priors. Second, an adaptive egocentric SLAM module modifies DROID-SLAM by projecting reconstructed 3D hands into the image plane to create a dual mask that excludes hand pixels from both feature extraction and dense bundle adjustment, then recovers metric scale via Metric3D depth with an adaptive sampling strategy (AdaSM) that restricts scale optimization to a reliable intermediate depth range. Third, a transformer-encoder motion infiller network operates in a canonical space (first frame aligned to zero translation/rotation) to predict missing MANO tokens for frames where hands are out of view, initialized with SLERP/linear interpolation to reduce the completion burden. The three modules are trained with supervised losses on HOT3D and DexYCB, and combined at inference by transforming camera-frame hand motions into world space using the estimated camera trajectory.

## Results

On the HOT3D dataset, HaWoR achieves a W-MPJPE of 33.20 mm, dramatically outperforming the best baseline WiLoR-SLAM (151.67 mm) by over 4×, and the optimization-based HMP-SLAM (119.41 mm) by 3.6×. In camera-frame evaluation on DexYCB, HaWoR attains 4.76 mm PA-MPJPE and 90.5 AUC overall, with the largest gap under severe occlusion (75%–100%): 5.07 mm vs. WiLoR's 5.68 mm. For camera trajectory estimation, the adaptive SLAM reduces ATE from 3.80 mm (DROID-SLAM) to 3.36 mm, and ATE-S from 21.07 mm (DROID+Metric3DV2) to 14.61 mm, a 31% improvement in metric-scale accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HaWoR decisively outperforms all four world-frame baselines on every metric in Table 3. Against the strongest regression-based baseline WiLoR-SLAM [30], HaWoR reduces W-MPJPE from 151.67 to 33.20 mm (78% reduction) and RTE from 2.99 to 0.78 mm (74% reduction). Against the optimization-based HMP-SLAM [11], which uses hand motion priors to align poses with SLAM trajectories, HaWoR still achieves a 3.6× lower W-MPJPE (33.20 vs. 119.41) while running 4× faster (40 ms vs. 160 ms per frame). In camera-frame evaluation on DexYCB (Table 1), HaWoR beats the best image-based method HandOccNet [27] (5.80 mm) and the best temporal method WiLoR [30] (5.01 mm) on overall PA-MPJPE, with the advantage widening under 75%–100% occlusion (5.07 vs. 5.68 for WiLoR). For camera trajectory (Table 2), the adaptive SLAM with Metric3DV2 [44] and AdaSM sampling reduces ATE-S from 21.07 mm (naive DROID+Metric3DV2) to 14.61 mm, and further outperforms DROID+DepthAnythingV2 [42] (27.49 mm) and DROID+ZoeDepth [7] (43.58 mm) by large margins, confirming that hand-region masking in bundle adjustment is critical for egocentric settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper decomposes world-space hand motion reconstruction into three sub-problems, each building on a distinct prior line: (1) camera-frame hand motion estimation extends WiLoR's ViT backbone with temporal Image and Pose Attention Modules to capture spatio-temporal priors; (2) camera trajectory estimation adapts DROID-SLAM with a dual-masking strategy (following TRAM) to exclude dynamic hand regions from bundle adjustment, combined with Metric3D for metric scale; and (3) a transformer-encoder motion infiller (following conditional motion inbetweening) fills out-of-view frames. The key departure from prior world-space reconstruction work (GLAMR, WHAM, SLAHMR, TRAM) is the shift from full-body to hand-level reconstruction in egocentric settings, where hands are smaller, more frequently occluded, and routinely leave the view frustum—challenges that make body-level priors inapplicable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first method to produce world-coordinate 3D hand trajectories from egocentric video, HaWoR opens a path toward AR/VR interaction understanding and egocentric human behavior analysis that camera-frame-only methods cannot support. The decomposition into camera-frame hand motion, adaptive SLAM, and motion infilling is a practical engineering recipe that other egocentric body-part tasks (e.g., feet, tools) could adopt. However, the 40 ms/frame runtime is still far from real-time, and the approach relies on a multi-stage pipeline (detection → hand pose → SLAM → metric scaling → infilling) where errors compound; the authors themselves suggest that foundational models could eventually replace the SLAM+metric stage with a direct world-space trajectory prediction. A key open question is whether the motion infiller generalizes beyond the HOT3D training distribution to truly novel, long-duration out-of-view segments.
