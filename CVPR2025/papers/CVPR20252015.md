## Problem

Existing single-view panoramic 3D scene reconstruction methods suffer from two critical flaws: global texture inconsistencies that cause visual discontinuities across the scene, and foreground-background occlusions that produce voids in the reconstructed 3D space. These issues prevent users from freely navigating the scene, as prior approaches like DreamScene360 restrict viewing to fixed perspectives and iterative inpainting methods like LucidDreamer lack global coherence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252015/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single equirectangular panoramic image (resampled to 2048×1024) and optional text prompts specifying spatiotemporal conditions (e.g., season, time of day) as input. It produces a 3D Gaussian Splatting scene free of dynamic objects (pedestrians, vehicles) that supports unrestricted camera navigation, along with intermediate layered panoramic images and per-layer depth maps. The 3DGS is trained on 512×512 perspective views sampled from the panorama, with layer-specific iteration counts (3000/4000/3000 for sky/background/foreground) and no splitting or pruning.

## Method

Scene4U decomposes the panorama into four semantic layers (sky, background, foreground, dynamic) using Semantic Segment Anything for instance segmentation followed by an LLM (Qwen-Plus) for hierarchical classification. Occluded regions are then restored layer-by-layer via FLUX-inpainting with a 'no objects present' prompt, ensuring each layer's inpainting is conditioned on the already-repaired deeper layer. Depth is estimated for the foreground via 360MonoDepth (projecting the panorama into 20 overlapping perspective patches with ZoeDepth) and completed for background/sky layers using depth inpainting. The layered panoramas are converted to point clouds via equirectangular projection and initialized as 3DGS, then optimized in back-to-front layer order with a masked L1 + D-SSIM loss that isolates each layer's gradients, preventing cross-layer interference and resolving occlusion inconsistencies.

## Results

On the WorldVista3D dataset, Scene4U achieves the best PSNR (32.778), LPIPS (0.025), NIQE (3.605), and BRISQUE (27.793) among all compared methods, while LucidDreamer retains a slightly higher SSIM (0.985 vs. 0.959). Scene4U improves LPIPS by 24.24% and BRISQUE by 24.40% over the strongest baseline, and also achieves the fastest training time at 11 min 13 s (vs. 12 min 37 s for LucidDreamer and 18 min 23 s for DreamScene360). The ablation in Table 2 further shows that the layered panorama construction strategy yields 5.93% and 3.35% improvements in NIQE and BRISQUE respectively after 0.3 m of in-scene navigation, confirming robustness under free exploration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Scene4U is compared against two baselines: LucidDreamer (single-image progressive inpainting for 360° scene generation) and DreamScene360 (text-to-3D panoramic Gaussian splatting). Against DreamScene360, Scene4U wins on every metric—PSNR (+3.23 dB), LPIPS (−0.022), NIQE (−0.84), BRISQUE (−6.78), and training time (6 min 10 s faster). Against LucidDreamer, Scene4U achieves higher PSNR (+2.37 dB, a 7.79% relative gain), lower LPIPS (−0.008, a 24.24% relative gain), lower NIQE (−1.70), and lower BRISQUE (−22.72), but LucidDreamer retains a marginally higher SSIM (0.985 vs. 0.959), likely reflecting its different rendering geometry. Scene4U also trains roughly 1.5× faster than DreamScene360 and about 10% faster than LucidDreamer, attributed to the lightweight per-layer 3DGS optimization with splitting and pruning disabled. The ablation (Table 2) confirms that the layered panorama construction specifically improves robustness during in-scene navigation, with gains widening from 0.1 m to 0.3 m of movement.

## Relation to Prior Work

Scene4U extends the panoramic 3D reconstruction line initiated by DreamScene360 and Omni-NeRF, but departs fundamentally from the iterative camera-movement paradigm used by LucidDreamer and similar methods. Instead of progressively inpainting novel views, it introduces a layered decomposition strategy that leverages an LLM (Qwen) to classify open-vocabulary segmentation masks into hierarchical depth layers (sky, background, foreground, dynamic), then performs layer-by-layer FLUX-inpainting and depth completion before initializing a 3DGS representation. The Climate Controller, built on InstructPix2Pix, adds a text-guided temporal/weather conditioning step not present in prior panoramic reconstruction work, while the layered 3DGS optimization (back-to-front with per-layer masking) is a novel training strategy on top of the standard 3DGS pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to remove dynamic objects (pedestrians, vehicles) and enable unrestricted navigation from a single panorama addresses a practical gap in VR and immersive experience applications where fixed-viewpoint or obstructed scenes degrade user experience. The WorldVista3D dataset (120 global landmark panoramas) provides a useful benchmark for this specific setting. However, the evaluation is limited to only two baselines and a single dataset, and the reliance on a cloud LLM for segmentation classification introduces latency and reproducibility concerns. Open questions include whether the layered approach generalizes to indoor scenes with complex geometry, whether the 4-layer hierarchy is sufficient for scenes with many depth planes, and how the method scales to higher-resolution panoramas or real-time applications.
