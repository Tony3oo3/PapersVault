## Problem

Existing pose-free generalizable 3D reconstruction methods either rely on pretrained 3D prior models, require per-scene fine-tuning, or depend on error-prone optical flow for pose estimation, making them impractical for uncalibrated in-the-wild images. The paper tackles the inherently ill-posed problem of jointly estimating camera poses, depth, and 3D scene geometry from unposed multi-view images in a single forward pass without any ground-truth supervision or scene-specific optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250958/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a triplet of unposed RGB images (two context views and one target view) at 256×256 or 256×448 resolution, along with known camera intrinsics from sensor metadata. It produces per-pixel depth maps, relative SE(3) camera poses between views, pixel-aligned 3D Gaussian attributes (position offsets, covariance, opacity, spherical harmonics), and rendered novel views. Notably, the target view is excluded from Gaussian generation during training to encourage generalization to unseen viewpoints, and the final 3D scene is constructed by transforming context-view Gaussians into a unified coordinate frame via predicted poses.

## Method

SelfSplat unifies self-supervised depth/pose estimation with 3D Gaussian Splatting in a single feed-forward architecture, exploiting their reciprocal benefits: geometric consistency from reprojection guides Gaussian positioning, while 3D-GS rendering gradients improve pose accuracy beyond what 2D features alone can achieve. The pipeline combines a multi-view encoder (ResNet + Swin Transformer with cross-view attention) and a monocular CroCo ViT encoder, fused via a DPT module that predicts depth and Gaussian attributes per pixel. A matching-aware pose network (2D U-Net with cross-attention blocks) incorporates cross-view features from all three images to estimate relative poses, and a depth refinement module uses Plücker ray embeddings of the estimated poses to correct inter-view depth inconsistencies. Training uses a combined loss of self-supervised reprojection error (L1 + SSIM on warped images) and differentiable 3D-GS rendering loss, with gradients flowing through the Gaussian rasterizer back to both the pose and Gaussian attribute networks.

## Results

On RE10k, SelfSplat achieves 24.22 PSNR, 0.813 SSIM, and 0.188 LPIPS (average), outperforming the strongest baseline FlowCAM (22.29 PSNR, 0.711 SSIM, 0.313 LPIPS) by ~2 dB in PSNR and 0.10 in SSIM. On ACID, the gap widens to 26.71 PSNR vs. 25.59 for FlowCAM. For pose estimation on RE10k, SelfSplat achieves 0.750° average rotation error and 9.095° translation error, beating CoPoNeRF (0.839°, 9.175°) and FlowCAM (1.438°, 22.524°). Cross-dataset generalization (RE10k→ACID) yields 26.60 PSNR, surpassing FlowCAM (25.31) and CoPoNeRF (23.56) without any fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SelfSplat outperforms all four baselines across every dataset and metric. Against FlowCAM [41], the closest competitor on RE10k, SelfSplat gains +1.93 dB PSNR and −0.125 LPIPS (average), with the advantage growing to +2.67 dB on large-overlap pairs. Against CoPoNeRF [21], which requires ground-truth pose supervision during training, SelfSplat leads by +3.19 dB PSNR on RE10k and +3.41 dB on ACID while operating fully pose-free. DBARF [6] is substantially weaker (12.57 PSNR on RE10k) due to its reliance on per-scene fine-tuning and NeRF-based volumetric rendering. VAE [26] does not predict depth or poses, limiting it to 20.65 PSNR on RE10k. In pose estimation, SelfSplat achieves the lowest median rotation error (0.362° on RE10k, 0.199° on ACID) and the lowest median translation error (4.438° on RE10k, 4.535° on ACID), confirming that the matching-aware pose network and depth refinement module yield more geometrically consistent estimates than flow-based (FlowCAM) or correspondence-based (CoPoNeRF) approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

SelfSplat sits at the intersection of two lines of work: feed-forward pixel-aligned 3D Gaussian Splatting (PixelSplat, Splatter Image) and self-supervised monocular depth estimation (Zhou et al., Godard et al.). It departs from prior pose-free generalizable methods (DBARF, FlowCAM, CoPoNeRF) by eliminating both the reliance on pretrained 3D prior models and the need for per-scene fine-tuning, while replacing NeRF-based volumetric rendering with the faster 3D-GS rasterizer. The matching-aware pose network and pose-conditioned depth refinement module are novel architectural contributions that address the specific challenge of 3D-GS's sensitivity to pose errors, a problem less acute in implicit NeRF representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the 3D prior and fine-tuning requirements, SelfSplat makes pose-free 3D reconstruction more accessible for in-the-wild video where calibrated poses and large-scale 3D supervision are unavailable. The reciprocal improvement between self-supervised depth estimation and 3D-GS rendering (where rendering loss gradients stabilize pose estimation, and geometric consistency from depth warping improves Gaussian placement) offers a useful design principle for future unified 3D vision architectures. Limitations include the assumption of known camera intrinsics, reliance on CroCo's self-supervised pretraining (which, while not a 3D prior, is still a large-scale pretrained backbone), and evaluation limited to relatively short video sequences; scalability to longer sequences, dynamic scenes, and truly uncontrolled capture conditions remains open.
