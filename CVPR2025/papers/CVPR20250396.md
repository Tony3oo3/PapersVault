## Problem

Current multi-task image fusion methods rely on high-level vision tasks (object detection, semantic segmentation) for supervisory signals, creating a semantic gap with the pixel-level fusion objective, requiring complex bridging modules or large pre-trained models, and failing to generalise across different fusion tasks without task-specific retraining. This paper asks whether low-level digital photography fusion tasks can serve as a more natural and efficient alternative for cross-task interaction in image fusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250396/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the model consumes aligned infrared-visible pairs from LLVIP plus synthetically generated multi-focus pairs (created by partially blurring the RGB images from the same scenes) to form an RGB-based joint dataset. At inference, only one image pair is required per fusion task, and the model is applied directly to seen (IVIF, MFIF) and unseen (MEIF, NIR-VIS, pansharpening, medical) tasks without any fine-tuning. A unique capability is single-modality enhancement, where both inputs are set to the same RGB image (F(X, X)), producing an enhanced output that can boost downstream classification accuracy.

## Method

GIFNet employs a three-branch architecture: a Multi-Modal (MM) branch, a Digital Photography (DP) branch, and a Reconstruction (REC) branch with dense connections that extracts task-agnostic features targeting the shared RGB modality. The core Cross-Fusion Gating Mechanism (CFGM), built on SwinTransformer blocks, alternately trains the MM and DP branches (freezing one while updating the other) using self-attention plus a learnable-weighted cross-attention (λ) confined to odd layers to avoid disrupting window shifts. Training uses a combined loss: a public loss (SSIM + MSE against the RGB reference) shared across branches, and a private loss that is information-weighted MSE for the MM branch (weights derived from Grad-CAM on a DenseNet121) and supervised MSE for the DP branch. The RGB-based joint dataset and the REC branch jointly mitigate the domain gap between multi-modal and digital photography tasks, enabling a single model to handle diverse fusion scenarios and single-modality enhancement.

## Results

On the primary IVIF task (LLVIP), GIFNet achieves EI=62.46, SCD=1.61, and AG=6.70, outperforming all dedicated and generalised baselines on edge intensity and gradient metrics, though VIF (0.73) trails Text-IF (0.93) and CDDFuse (0.79) due to their 'choose-max' pixel strategy. On unseen tasks, GIFNet delivers substantial gains: VIF +46.7% and AG +37.8% on multi-exposure fusion (DSCIE), and VIF +25% on multi-focus fusion (Lytro). For single-modality enhancement, GIFNet is the only method to surpass the original CIFAR-100 training baseline, reaching 56.18% top-1 and 84.95% top-5 accuracy (vs. 54.11%/83.03% original). The model achieves all results with only 3.30 MB parameters and 9.96 GFLOPs, a >96% reduction in computational cost versus the next-lightest multi-task method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the primary IVIF task (LLVIP), GIFNet leads on EI (62.46 vs. 61.30 for Text-IF) and AG (6.70 vs. 6.33), but trails Text-IF (0.93) and CDDFuse (0.79) on VIF, which the authors attribute to those methods' 'choose-max' pixel strategy that inflates fidelity at the cost of ignoring one modality. On MFIF (Lytro and MFI-WHU), GIFNet dominates all four metrics over every baseline, achieving VIF=1.74/1.94 versus 1.39/1.50 for U2Fusion and 1.34/1.36 for MUFusion. On the unseen MEIF task (DSCIE), GIFNet outperforms the best dedicated method SPD-MEF by +46.7% in VIF (2.52 vs. 1.72) and +37.8% in AG (12.00 vs. 8.12), and surpasses the generalised U2Fusion by +49.2% in VIF. For remote sensing (QuickBird) and medical (Harvard) fusion, GIFNet also leads on VIF and AG over task-specific methods like P2Sharpen, ZeroSharpen, and CoCoNet. In the single-modality CIFAR-100 enhancement experiment, GIFNet is the only method to exceed the original unenhanced training baseline (56.18% vs. 54.11% top-1), while U2Fusion (51.58%) and Text-IF (52.57%) both underperform the original, confirming that GIFNet's cross-task interaction produces genuinely useful pixel-level enhancement rather than task-specific artefacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in advanced image fusion that couples multi-modal fusion with high-level semantic tasks (object detection, segmentation, text prompts) for cross-task supervision, arguing instead that low-level digital photography fusion tasks (multi-focus, multi-exposure) provide more natural pixel-level guidance. It also extends the line of generalised/unified fusion methods (U2Fusion, MUFusion, SDNet) by introducing a structured three-branch architecture with a Cross-Fusion Gating Mechanism and a shared reconstruction branch, rather than relying on continual learning or task-specific rule designs. The SwinTransformer-based gating and DenseNet-based information-weighted loss are repurposed from their original contexts (general vision, classification) to serve the specific role of adaptive cross-task feature mixing and modality-aware loss weighting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 96%+ reduction in GFLOPs and the 3.3 MB model size make task-agnostic fusion practical on resource-constrained devices, addressing a real deployment bottleneck that high-level-task methods cannot. The demonstration that a single model handles seen and unseen fusion subtasks (IVIF, MFIF, MEIF, NIR-VIS, pansharpening, medical) without fine-tuning, plus the unexpected single-modality enhancement capability, broadens the utility of fusion models beyond their traditional multi-modal scope. Open questions include whether the two-task interaction design generalises to more than two concurrent fusion tasks, whether the RGB-based joint dataset strategy transfers to non-RGB modalities, and whether the approach scales to higher-resolution or video fusion settings.
