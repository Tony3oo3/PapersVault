## Problem

Online zero-shot 3D instance segmentation requires efficiently determining spatial overlap between 2D masks from sequentially captured frames, but existing methods either perform this query offline at O(n²) cost or avoid explicit spatial associations entirely (e.g., learning a merging network offline), which degrades performance on incomplete, incrementally reconstructed scenes. No prior method achieves real-time, zero-shot 3D instance segmentation with explicit multi-view spatial consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252574/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of posed RGB-D frames (color image C_t ∈ R^{H×W×3}, depth image D_t ∈ R^{H×W}, and 4×4 camera pose T_t) processed sequentially, with segmentation applied only to keyframes sampled at fixed intervals (every 10 or 20 frames). It outputs the reconstructed scene point cloud S, a set of 3D instance masks over S, and per-instance open-vocabulary semantic features (CLIP embeddings) that enable text-based instance retrieval. The entire pipeline is zero-shot: no 3D training data is used, and the only learned components are the pre-trained VFM (CropFormer), CLIP, and FCGF feature extractors.

## Method

The core mechanism is a hashed voxel volume (VoxelHashing) where each voxel entry stores both a TSDF value and an append-only list of 2D mask IDs that backproject to it, enabling O(m) spatial overlap queries for a mask with m voxels instead of O(n²) pairwise comparisons. To avoid costly hash-table rewrites during mask merging, the method maintains a separate mapping table that remaps original mask IDs to current global IDs; merging only updates this small table (number of masks ≪ number of voxels), keeping the hash table append-only. Mask merging is decided by a zero-shot criterion combining four signals: a symmetric overlap ratio computed via hash-table queries, CLIP-based semantic similarity, FCGF-based geometric similarity, and a third-view consensus count (adapted from MaskClustering) that counts how many other masks simultaneously overlap both candidates. Two masks are merged if their overall similarity exceeds a threshold or if enough third-view supporters exist, and the process runs every 5 keyframes to maintain real-time performance at 15 FPS.

## Results

On ScanNet200, OnlineAnySeg achieves AP=18.6, AP50=36.1, AP25=53.5, running at 15 FPS — the highest efficiency among all compared methods. On SceneNN, it reaches AP=18.1, AP50=35.3, AP25=59.5, outperforming the SOTA offline zero-shot method MaskClustering (AP=16.3) and the online zero-shot method SAM3D (AP=9.1). On intermediate (partially scanned) sequences at 25% completion, the method achieves AP=18.0, substantially ahead of EmbodiedSAM (AP=12.1) and SAM3D (AP=9.7), demonstrating robustness to incomplete reconstruction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the supervised online method EmbodiedSAM [40], OnlineAnySeg trails on ScanNet200 (AP 18.6 vs. 28.8), which is expected since EmbodiedSAM is trained on that dataset; however, on the unseen SceneNN benchmark the gap narrows to 18.1 vs. 20.1, and on intermediate (25% scanned) sequences OnlineAnySeg leads by ~6 AP points (18.0 vs. 12.1), showing that the learned merging strategy does not generalize well to incomplete data. Compared to the SOTA offline zero-shot method MaskClustering [42], OnlineAnySeg is competitive on ScanNet200 (18.6 vs. 19.7 AP) and clearly superior on SceneNN (18.1 vs. 16.3 AP, +1.8 AP; AP25 59.5 vs. 46.2, +13.3). Against the online zero-shot SAM3D [43], OnlineAnySeg gains approximately +9 AP on ScanNet200 (18.6 vs. 9.6) and +9 AP on SceneNN (18.1 vs. 9.1), while also running nearly 2× faster (15 vs. 8 FPS). The method also surpasses the offline zero-shot OVIR-3D [21] by +4.2 AP on ScanNet200 and +5.8 AP on SceneNN, while additionally providing real-time online capability that OVIR-3D lacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on VoxelHashing [27] for efficient O(1) 3D scene queries and CropFormer [31] for 2D entity-level mask generation, adapting the 'view consensus' concept from MaskClustering [42] to an online setting. It departs from EmbodiedSAM [40], which learns mask merging via a supervised transformer (requiring training on ScanNet200), and from SAM3D [43], which performs bottom-up merging without explicit spatial association tracking. The key methodological departure is using a hashed voxel volume with an append-only mapping table to maintain inter-mask spatial associations in O(n) rather than O(n²), enabling real-time zero-shot merging without any learned merging module.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to achieve real-time (15 FPS) online zero-shot 3D instance segmentation using explicit spatial associations, making it directly applicable to embodied AI and robotic exploration where training data is unavailable and scenes are incrementally reconstructed. The ablation revealing that spatial overlap criteria dominate over semantic/geometric feature similarity (≈50% AP drop when features are used alone) challenges the assumption that VFM-derived features are the primary signal for 3D instance grouping. Limitations include reduced robustness to under-segmentation in heavily occluded scenes and difficulty scaling to floor-level environments due to the space-time tradeoff of the hashing-based representation.
