## Problem

Existing video-to-audio generation systems produce sounds that closely replicate real-life audio, offering little creative control for sound designers who often need to diverge artistically (e.g., using a cat meow for a lion roar). These methods also suffer from limited conditioning options (typically only video or basic text), low audio quality (16kHz), and poor synchronization. The paper addresses the need for a unified framework that provides fine-grained multimodal control while generating professional-quality, full-bandwidth audio.

## Input / Output

The model takes a silent 8-second video (encoded at 8 FPS via CAVP into 64×512 features), optional text prompts (positive and negative, encoded by T5-base into 768-dim embeddings), and optional reference audio-visual pairs (3-second clips from SFX libraries or the video itself). It outputs a synchronized 48kHz audio waveform decoded from 40Hz latent features (64 channels) via a DAC-VAE. The conditioning is flexible: any subset of text, reference audio, and reference video can be provided, enabling applications ranging from pure video-to-audio reproduction to text-driven semantic replacement or audio-visual style transfer.

## Method

MultiFoley uses a 12-layer Diffusion Transformer (DiT) as the denoiser operating in a latent space defined by a frozen DAC-VAE that compresses 48kHz audio to 40Hz, 64-channel latents. Video features are concatenated with noisy audio latents along the channel dimension to enforce temporal alignment, while text embeddings are injected via cross-attention. The key training innovation is joint supervision on VGGSound (168K in-the-wild samples tagged 'low quality') and a proprietary HQ-SFX dataset (400K professional 48kHz recordings tagged 'high quality'), with random dropout of quality tags to let the model disentangle audio quality from semantics. Audio-visual conditioning is implemented via masked denoising: conditional latents are prepended to the noisy sequence, and the loss is applied only to the unconditioned portion. At inference, DDIM sampling (100 steps) with classifier-free guidance (scale 3–5) supports negative prompting for semantic exclusion and quality steering, and a 50K-step fine-tuning on a high-correspondence VGGSound subset (ImageBind score > 0.3) further improves synchronization.

## Results

On the VGGSound test set (8,702 videos), MultiFoley achieves the best CLAP score (34.4%), best AV-Sync (0.80 s), best KLD (1.43), and tied-best FAD@AUD (4.62) among all baselines, while being the only method generating at 48 kHz. It trails FoleyCrafter on ImageBind (28.0 vs. 30.2) and V2A-mapper on FAD@VGG (2.92 vs. 1.13). In the text-controlled Foley task (Table 2), MultiFoley achieves AV-Sync of 0.81 s (w/o negative prompting) versus 1.29 s for FoleyCrafter, and in the human study it wins on all four criteria (semantic 85.8%, sync 94.5%, quality 86.5%, overall 90.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MultiFoley outperforms all seven baselines on CLAP (34.4% vs. best 25.3% for FoleyCrafter), AV-Sync (0.80 s vs. best 0.87 s for Frieren), and KLD (1.43 vs. best 2.07 for FoleyCrafter), and ties FoleyGen on FAD@AUD (4.62). Against FoleyCrafter, the closest text-controlled competitor, MultiFoley improves AV-Sync by 0.44 s (0.80 vs. 1.24) and FAD@AUD by 2.27 (4.62 vs. 6.89), but trails on ImageBind (28.0 vs. 30.2) and FAD@VGG (2.92 vs. 2.74). Against Frieren (rectified flow matching), MultiFoley wins on CLAP by 9.7 points and KLD by 1.07, but Frieren achieves a lower FAD@VGG (1.54 vs. 2.92). V2A-mapper achieves the best FAD@VGG (1.13) but is far behind on CLAP (24.5 vs. 34.4) and AV-Sync (1.24 vs. 0.80). In the text-controlled Foley task, MultiFoley's AV-Sync (0.81 s) is substantially better than FoleyCrafter's (1.29 s), though FoleyCrafter edges ahead on CLAP accuracy (94.2% vs. 93.2% with negative prompting). The human study confirms MultiFoley's advantage, with 94.5% win rate on synchronization and 90.2% overall preference over FoleyCrafter.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the video-to-audio generation line (Diff-Foley, FoleyGen, Frieren, FoleyCrafter) by unifying text, audio, and video conditioning within a single DiT-based latent diffusion model, rather than treating these as separate pipelines. It builds on the conditional Foley analogy idea from Du et al. [24] and the two-stage text-to-audio-with-video-adapter approach (FoleyCrafter, SonicVisionLM) but departs from them by performing joint training across low-quality internet video (VGGSound) and high-quality SFX recordings (HQ-SFX) with quality tags, enabling 48kHz full-bandwidth output. The architecture follows the standard DiT + VAE latent diffusion recipe, with CAVP for visual features and T5 for text, while the novel contribution is the multi-conditional training strategy and the masked denoising formulation for audio extension.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MultiFoley provides sound designers with a practical, unified tool for synchronized Foley generation with fine-grained multimodal control (text prompts, reference audio, partial video), addressing a real workflow gap where prior systems offered either synchronization or control but not both. The joint-training strategy for bridging low-quality and high-quality audio distributions is a reusable technique for any audio generation task facing data quality heterogeneity. Key limitations include reliance on the relatively small VGGSound dataset (168K samples), difficulty handling multiple simultaneous sound events, and the risk of generating realistic but misleading audio. Scaling to larger, more diverse audio-visual corpora and improving multi-event temporal reasoning are the most pressing open questions.
