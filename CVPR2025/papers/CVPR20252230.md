## Problem

Federated learning for whole slide image (WSI) classification faces non-i.i.d. feature shifts across institutions, but existing cross-client style transfer methods are inadequate for WSIs because (1) a single WSI contains multiple morphological structures with distinct statistical properties, yet prior methods represent each image with only one style, and (2) style transfer can displace the small, randomly distributed regions of interest (RoIs) that drive WSI classification. These issues remain largely unexplored in the federated pathology setting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes WSI patch features (224×224 patches encoded by a frozen SSL-ViT backbone into d-dimensional vectors) and K=5 compact pseudo bag styles per WSI received from other clients via the server. It outputs a slide-level class label (binary for HER2, multi-class for RCC/Camelyon17) along with per-patch attention weights that identify RoI contributions. Crucially, style transfer operates entirely in the feature space via AdaIN rather than reconstructing stylized pixel images, and only K=5 styles per WSI are transmitted instead of thousands of per-patch styles, reducing communication overhead by roughly half compared to CCST.

## Method

HistoFS introduces two key mechanisms. First, Pseudo Bag Styles Construction partitions the per-patch style distributions (mean and standard deviation of each patch feature) within a WSI into K clusters using K-means with 2-Wasserstein distance as the metric, yielding K compact pseudo styles that capture multiple morphological structures in a single WSI; these are shared across clients via the server in a non-overlapping fashion. Second, an Authenticity Module preserves RoIs by computing the cosine similarity between attention weights of the original and style-augmented WSI, then scaling the augmented attention weights by the authenticity score to realign RoI localization. The augmented and original WSIs are jointly fed into an attention-gated MIL aggregator, and the entire pipeline is trained under a standard federated averaging objective with Gaussian noise for privacy.

## Results

On the RCC dataset, HistoFS (with pseudo bag styles + authenticity module) achieves an average AUC of 99.46% across three institutions, with the lowest entropy score of 0.056, indicating the most equitable performance distribution. On the HER2 dataset, HistoFS reaches an average AUC of 86.07% with an entropy of 0.383, again the best among all compared methods. On the unseen-client evaluation (Camelyon17), HistoFS achieves 84.01% average bACC and 84.67% average AUC, outperforming all baselines. Communication overhead is also reduced: HistoFS requires only 196.77 MB per round on RCC versus 420 MB for CCST, with an average training time of 760 s vs. 1485 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HistoFS outperforms all seven baselines on both the RCC and HER2 datasets. Against the MIL baselines, HistoFS improves average AUC by +2.75% over HistoFL (99.46 vs. 96.71) and +2.47% over FRMIL (99.46 vs. 96.99) on RCC, and by +11.86% over HistoFL (86.07 vs. 74.21) and +10.77% over FRMIL (86.07 vs. 75.30) on HER2. Among local style transfer methods, HistoFS surpasses MixStyle by +2.24% (RCC) and +5.15% (HER2), and DSU by +2.65% (RCC) and +3.76% (HER2). Among federated style transfer methods, HistoFS beats CCST by +2.20% (RCC) and +4.89% (HER2), and DACS by +2.45% (RCC) and +8.81% (HER2). Notably, HistoFS also achieves the lowest entropy score on both datasets (0.056 for RCC, 0.383 for HER2), indicating the most balanced per-institution performance, whereas baselines like HistoFL show the highest divergence (0.096 and 0.655). On the unseen-client Camelyon17 evaluation, HistoFS with the authenticity module leads in bACC (84.01%), F1 (85.78%), and AUC (84.67%) over all baselines, with the best being CCST at 82.08% F1 and 82.30% AUC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of federated learning for whole slide image classification (HistoFL, PointTransformerFL) and cross-client style transfer for domain generalization (CCST, StableFDG, DACS). It departs from prior federated WSI work by explicitly addressing non-i.i.d. feature shifts across institutions—a gap the authors note was largely overlooked—and from prior style-transfer-in-FL methods by recognizing that a single WSI contains multiple morphological styles rather than one. The pseudo-bag concept from DTFD-MIL, PseMix, and PAMIL is repurposed here: instead of random or prototype-based partitioning, bags are split by style information using K-means with 2-Wasserstein distance, and AdaIN is used for the actual transfer. The authenticity module draws on attention-weight alignment ideas from DSMIL and FRMIL but applies them to preserve RoIs across style-augmented bags rather than to recalibrate within a single bag.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a concrete gap: federated WSI classification methods previously assumed or ignored non-i.i.d. feature shifts, leading to sub-optimal and inequitable performance across institutions. The practical impact is notable—transmitting only K=5 pseudo styles per WSI cuts communication by roughly 50% compared to CCST while improving AUC and reducing inter-institution performance divergence (lowest entropy scores). Open questions the authors flag include whether privacy-preserving mechanisms (beyond the small Gaussian noise on weights) can be integrated, and whether large foundation models for pathology will render these per-institution style-shift concerns moot. The evaluation is limited to three institutions and two primary datasets, so generalizability to larger, more heterogeneous federations remains to be tested.
