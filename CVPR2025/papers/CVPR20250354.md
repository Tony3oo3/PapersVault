## Problem

Transferring human bimanual manipulation to dexterous robotic hands remains difficult: direct pose retargeting fails due to morphological mismatches, RL from scratch is sample-inefficient and requires task-specific reward engineering, and teleoperation is costly and embodiment-specific. Most prior work is limited to single-hand grasping, leaving complex bimanual tasks like pen capping and bottle unscrewing largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250354/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes human MoCap trajectories (wrist 6-DoF SE(3) pose with linear/angular velocities, MANO finger joint positions and velocities) and object reference trajectories (6-DoF pose with velocities), with all translations normalized relative to the dexterous hand wrist. The dexterous hand proprioception (joint angles, wrist pose, and their velocities) is also part of the state. The output is a per-step action vector comprising target joint positions for PD control and a 6-DoF wrist force command; the final action is the element-wise sum of the imitation action and a residual correction, clipped to joint limits.

## Method

MANIPTRANS decouples the transfer into two PPO-trained stages. Stage 1 pre-trains a generalist hand trajectory imitator (π_I) on large-scale hand-only MoCap data (no object interaction required), with a reward combining wrist tracking, weighted exponential finger-keypoint imitation (emphasizing thumb/index/middle fingertips), and a smoothness penalty; curriculum learning progressively tightens the finger-deviation termination threshold. Stage 2 fine-tunes a residual module (π_R) that expands the state with object pose/velocity, BPS shape encoding, hand-object distance, and simulated contact force, then outputs a small correction Δa_R added element-wise to the frozen imitation action. The residual is initialized as a zero-mean Gaussian with a warm-up schedule to prevent collapse, and training uses a relaxation mechanism (zero gravity, high friction initially) that gradually restores physical realism. This decoupling reduces action-space complexity by separating morphological adaptation from physics-based interaction, enabling ~15-minute training per unseen trajectory versus ~40 hours for optimization-based baselines.

## Results

On the OakInk-V2 validation set (~80 episodes, half bimanual), MANIPTRANS achieves the lowest errors across all metrics: 8.60° object rotation, 0.49 cm object translation, 2.15 cm per-joint, and 1.36 cm per-fingertip. The headline success rate is 58.1% for single-hand and 39.5% for bimanual tasks, substantially outperforming the next-best baseline (Retarget+Residual at 47.8%/13.9%). For an unseen 60-frame single-hand trajectory, MANIPTRANS requires only ~15 minutes of training on a single RTX 4090, compared to ~40 hours of optimization for QuasiSim.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MANIPTRANS decisively outperforms all three RL-combined baselines on every metric. Against Retarget-Only (which is nearly infeasible at 4.6%/0.0% SR), the gap is trivial. Against RL-Only (trained from scratch with PPO, based on D-Grasp [27]), MANIPTRANS improves bimanual SR from 12.1% to 39.5% and reduces fingertip error from 2.38 cm to 1.36 cm. Against Retarget+Residual (residual learning on keypoint-aligned poses from AnyTeleop [92]), MANIPTRANS nearly triples bimanual success (39.5% vs 13.9%) while also achieving lower rotation error (8.60° vs 11.58°), indicating the pre-trained imitation stage provides a substantially better initialization than raw retargeting. Against the optimization-based QuasiSim [70], a direct quantitative comparison is not possible (no public code or validation set), but qualitatively MANIPTRANS produces smoother, more stable contacts on the Shadow Hand and is ~160× faster in training time (~15 min vs ~40 h). On the DEXMANIPNET policy-learning benchmark (Table 2), all four imitation learning methods (IBC, BET, DP-UNet, DP-Trans) perform suboptimally (4.7–18.4% SR), underscoring the difficulty of the task rather than a weakness of the dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

MANIPTRANS builds directly on the residual policy learning paradigm (Silver et al., 2018), where a learned correction is added to a base action, but departs from prior dexterous-hand residual methods (DexH2R, GraspGF, TransIC) by decoupling the base into a separately pre-trained generalist hand-motion imitator rather than applying residuals to retargeted poses. This two-stage decomposition—first mimicking finger kinematics in isolation, then refining for physics and bimanual coordination—contrasts with QuasiSim's optimization-over-custom-simulators approach and with RL-only methods (D-Grasp, UniDexGrasp) that require task-specific reward engineering. The paper also extends the data pipeline by leveraging existing MoCap datasets (OakInk-V2, FAVOR) rather than collecting new teleoperation data, positioning itself as an offline transfer alternative to labor-intensive human-in-the-loop collection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DEXMANIPNET (3.3K episodes, 1.34M frames, 61 tasks including previously unexplored bimanual activities like pen capping and bottle unscrewing) fills a notable gap in large-scale, physically plausible dexterous manipulation data, and its extensibility makes it a practical resource for future policy learning. The ~15-minute training time per unseen trajectory versus QuasiSim's ~40 hours is a substantial practical efficiency gain for sim-to-real pipelines. However, absolute success rates remain moderate (58% single-hand, 39.5% bimanual), and the authors acknowledge that noisy MoCap sequences and inaccurate articulated-object models limit transferability—open questions include whether the two-stage decomposition scales to deformable objects and whether the residual formulation can be extended to online adaptation during real-world execution.
