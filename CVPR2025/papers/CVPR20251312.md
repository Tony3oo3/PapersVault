## Problem

Text-to-video diffusion models face a persistent trade-off between temporal consistency and imaging quality: existing training-free methods like FreeInit improve frame-to-frame coherence but degrade image fidelity and incur high computational cost through iterative noise refinement. The paper addresses the need for a training-free framework that enhances temporal consistency of pretrained video diffusion models without sacrificing image quality or motion smoothness.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and operates on any pretrained latent video diffusion model (e.g., AnimateDiff, LaVie) as the sampling model, optionally paired with a second pretrained VDM (e.g., VideoCrafter2) as an external guide. It produces a 16-frame video clip in latent space, decoded via the model's VAE. The guidance is applied only during the first I=5 DDIM steps out of 50 total, with τ=10 additional denoising steps performed by the teacher per guidance step, keeping the overall inference overhead modest (×1.8–×3.0 speedup over FreeInit).

## Method

VideoGuide recasts video temporal consistency as a guidance optimization problem: a loss measuring how well a denoised sample is reconstructed by a teacher VDM is integrated into the DDIM reverse sampling step. This loss simplifies algebraically to a linear interpolation between the student's denoised estimate z_{0|t} and the teacher's denoised estimate z_{0|t-τ} (obtained by renoising the student's sample and running τ additional denoising steps through the teacher). A Butterworth low-pass/high-pass filter decomposition is then applied to the interpolated latent, preserving the student's high-frequency spatial details while distilling the teacher's low-frequency temporal structure. The entire procedure is training-free, plug-and-play, and requires only the first few inference steps, with the teacher model running alternately with the student to keep GPU memory overhead minimal.

## Results

On VBench, VideoGuide with VideoCrafter2 guidance applied to AnimateDiff achieves subject consistency 0.9614 and background consistency 0.9664, substantially outperforming the AnimateDiff baseline (0.9183 / 0.9437) and FreeInit (0.9487 / 0.9604), while preserving imaging quality at 0.6671 (vs. 0.6173 for FreeInit). For LaVie, the VC-guided variant reaches subject consistency 0.9635 and imaging quality 0.6796, surpassing both the LaVie baseline and FreeInit. Motion smoothness improves to 0.9772 (AnimateDiff) and 0.9723 (LaVie), though dynamic degree decreases modestly (16.78 vs. 26.67 for AnimateDiff), reflecting the expected consistency–motion trade-off.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the AnimateDiff baseline, VideoGuide (VC-guided) improves subject consistency by +0.0431 (0.9614 vs. 0.9183) and background consistency by +0.0227, while maintaining imaging quality (0.6671 vs. 0.6647). Compared to FreeInit, VideoGuide achieves higher subject consistency (0.9614 vs. 0.9487) and dramatically better imaging quality (0.6671 vs. 0.6173), indicating FreeInit's iterative refinement degrades texture detail. For LaVie, VideoGuide (VC-guided) outperforms FreeInit on subject consistency (0.9635 vs. 0.9625) and imaging quality (0.6796 vs. 0.6533), while also improving dynamic degree (12.63 vs. 10.69). The only metric where VideoGuide trails the base model is dynamic degree (16.78 vs. 26.67 for AnimateDiff), a modest reduction the authors attribute to the inherent consistency–motion trade-off. VideoCrafter2 as a standalone model achieves the highest subject consistency (0.9697) but the lowest dynamic degree (7.91), confirming that VideoGuide's role is to transfer temporal stability to models with richer motion or personalization capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

VideoGuide recasts video temporal consistency as an optimization problem in the reverse diffusion process, directly extending the manifold-constrained guidance framework from DPS [6] and its decomposed variant DDS [8]—originally designed for inverse problems—into the video generation domain. The key conceptual departure is replacing explicit gradient computation with a simple interpolation between the student model's denoised estimate and a teacher VDM's multi-step denoised sample, effectively approximating the PF-ODE endpoint without training a dedicated consistency model. This positions the work as a training-free, plug-and-play alternative to prior temporal-consistency methods like FreeInit (iterative noise reinitialization) and attention-injection approaches, which either degrade image quality or require lengthy pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play nature makes VideoGuide immediately deployable on any existing VDM pair, which is practically valuable as the ecosystem of open-source video models grows rapidly. The prior distillation finding—that interpolating a superior model's denoised samples transfers its data prior to a weaker model without retraining—opens an interesting direction for model composition, though it is only lightly explored. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to 16-frame clips, a modest dynamic-degree trade-off, and dependence on two models being available (albeit running alternately). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to flow-matching or rectified-flow samplers, scales to longer videos, and whether the low-pass filter strategy remains effective at higher resolutions or frame counts.
