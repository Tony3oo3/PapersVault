## Problem

Existing Precise Event Spotting (PES) methods for sports videos rely on frozen pre-trained features and transformer-based temporal modules that struggle with long-range dependencies in long videos, and they largely ignore the inherent class imbalance in sports event distributions (e.g., red cards are far rarer than ball-out-of-play events), leading to poor performance on underrepresented classes especially under tight tolerance settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250824/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes video in fixed-length clips of 128 frames (C×T_s×H×W), processed with a sliding window at half overlap during evaluation. It outputs per-frame classification scores (T_s×K) for K event classes, which are aggregated across overlapping clips via non-maximal suppression to yield sparse (timestamp, class) predictions. A prediction is scored correct only if it falls within δ frames of the ground-truth event AND identifies the correct class, with δ varying by dataset (0–2 frames for Tennis/FS/FineGym; 1–4 s tight and 5–60 s loose for SoccerNet V2).

## Method

The framework is an end-to-end trainable network built on RegNetY-200MF with the proposed Adaptive Spatio-Temporal Refinement Module (ASTRM) inserted after the first convolution in every bottleneck block. ASTRM refines features through three sub-blocks: a local spatial attention (channel-pooled features → 7×7 conv → sigmoid gating), a local temporal attention (3×1×1 and 1×1×1 3D convolutions → sigmoid), and a global temporal block that adaptively generates convolution kernels via global average pooling → two FC layers → softmax, applied as a dynamic convolution to fuse local and global temporal cues. Spatially pooled features are then fed to a Bidirectional GRU to capture long-range temporal dependencies, followed by a linear classifier. Training uses a combined loss of Binary Cross-Entropy plus the proposed Soft-IC loss (a mixup-compatible variant of Instance Contrastive loss that weights contrastive terms by soft-label coefficients to promote intra-class compactness and inter-class separability), optimized with AdamW under Adaptive Sharpness-Aware Minimization (ASAM) for improved generalization.

## Results

On SoccerNet V2, the proposed method achieves 73.74 mAP (tight) and 79.11 mAP (loose), outperforming the previous best COMEDIAN (71.33 tight, 77.07 loose) by 2.41% and 2.04% respectively, while using only 6.46M parameters vs. COMEDIAN's 70.12M. On Tennis (δ=0), the method reaches 61.01 mAP, a 15.67% improvement over E2E-Spot (45.34) and 17.52% over T-DEED (43.49). On FS-Comp (δ=0), it achieves 37.54 mAP vs. 28.92 for T-DEED, and on FineGym (δ=0) it reaches 15.24 vs. 13.54 for T-DEED. The method consistently outperforms all baselines across all four datasets and all tolerance settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 12 baselines on every dataset and tolerance setting tested. On SoccerNet V2 (tight), it beats COMEDIAN (71.33) by 2.41%, ASTRA (66.63) by 7.11%, Spivak (65.10) by 8.64%, UGLF (63.51) by 10.23%, E2E-Spot Y800MF (61.82) by 11.92%, and Baidu/TF (47.05) by 26.69%, while using far fewer parameters than COMEDIAN (6.46M vs. 70.12M) and lower FLOPs than Spivak (60.25 vs. 461.89 GFLOPs). T-DEED, despite being a recent SOTA, shows substantially lower scores (39.43 tight) when evaluated with author-provided checkpoints, suggesting sensitivity to implementation details. On Tennis (δ=0), the method surpasses T-DEED (43.49) by 17.52% and E2E-Spot (45.34) by 15.67%; on FS-Comp (δ=0) it leads T-DEED (28.92) by 8.62% and 2D-VDP+MS-TCN (83.50 at δ=1) by 3.17%. On FineGym (δ=0), it edges out T-DEED (13.54) by 1.70% and UGLF (50.20 at δ=1) by 2.11% at δ=1. The per-class analysis (Figure 4) confirms consistent gains across all event classes, with particularly large improvements on underrepresented classes, validating the Soft-IC loss contribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the E2E-Spot [14] architecture (CNN backbone + GRU temporal module) by replacing the Gated Shift Module with the proposed ASTRM and introducing the Soft-IC loss, which adapts the Instance Contrastive loss [11] to handle soft labels produced by mixup augmentation. Conceptually, it departs from the prevailing trend in PES of relying on large pre-trained frozen feature extractors (Baidu TF, ASTRA, COMEDIAN) combined with transformer-based temporal modules, instead arguing that an end-to-end trainable CNN with a simple Bi-GRU can be both more efficient and more effective. The ASTRM module draws on CBAM-style spatial attention [28] and temporal adaptive modules [19], while the training strategy incorporates ASAM [15] for improved generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses class imbalance in PES, a problem largely overlooked in prior work, showing that minority classes (e.g., Red card, Yellow→Red card) suffer disproportionately under existing methods. The finding that a lightweight Bi-GRU consistently outperforms transformers, Bi-LSTMs, and deformable attention as the temporal module challenges the field's assumption that more complex sequence models are necessary for long-range dependency in this setting. Open questions remain around whether the approach scales to higher spatial/temporal resolutions, whether the Soft-IC loss generalizes beyond sports domains, and whether the efficiency advantage (6.46M params vs. 70M for COMEDIAN) holds when the backbone is scaled up.
