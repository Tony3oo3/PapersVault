## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary 3D scene understanding methods rely on 2D vision-language models for supervision, which inherently lack 3D spatial and geometric context, making them struggle with entity disambiguation and spatial reasoning. Meanwhile, unsupervised 3D representation learning methods (masked modeling, point-level contrastive learning) capture local geometry but lack an object-level concept necessary for language alignment. The paper addresses this gap by learning 3D representations that simultaneously preserve entity-specific geometric structure and align with flexible language descriptions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud (spatial coordinates and RGB colors), K entity mask proposals generated by an off-the-shelf 3D instance segmentation model, and text descriptions (object captions and spatial referrals) produced by 2D foundation models as input. It outputs per-point 3D features in a language-aligned space via a 3D U-Net encoder (SPUNet) and a two-layer MLP VL-adapter, which are then matched against frozen CLIP text embeddings to yield per-point semantic class predictions. Training is fully contrastive, requiring no ground-truth segmentation labels.

## Method

MPEC introduces a two-stage entity-level contrastive framework. First, two randomly grid-masked augmented views of the scene are encoded by a shared 3D U-Net, and a point-to-entity contrastive loss enforces that points belonging to the same entity across views are similar while points from different entities are dissimilar, using entity masks to structure the similarity computation. Second, point features from both views are merged (mean of matched points), projected through a VL-adapter into CLIP's text space, and aligned via an entity-to-language contrastive loss where text-to-entity uses cross-entropy and entity-to-text uses binary cross-entropy to handle multiple descriptions per entity. The key design choice is operating at the entity (object) level rather than point or region level, which captures the concept of discrete objects while retaining 3D geometric structure. Ablations show that the cross-view masked augmentation is critical: without it, the model overemphasizes within-view entity uniqueness and degrades performance.

## Results

On ScanNet open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation, MPEC (SPUNet32) achieves 66.0% foreground mIoU and 81.3% foreground mAcc, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art OV3D by 3.0% f-mIoU and 6.5% f-mAcc. In zero-shot transfer to SceneVerse-val, MPEC reaches 45.0% f-mIoU and 63.6% f-mAcc, outperforming OpenScene by 3.7% f-mIoU. After fine-tuning, MPEC achieves 75.8% mIoU on ScanNet semantic segmentation and 31.8% mIoU on ScanNet200, and sets new state-of-the-art on 3D visual grounding (e.g., 51.8% Acc@0.5 on ScanRefer, 80.0% Acc on Sr3D).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MPEC outperforms all 14 baselines across the evaluated tasks. On the primary ScanNet OV-SemSeg benchmark, MPEC (66.0% f-mIoU) beats the closest competitor OV3D (64.0%) by 2.0% and RegionPLC (59.6%) by 6.4%, while achieving a 10.5% f-mIoU gain over OpenScene-3D (57.8%). On zero-shot Matterport3D, MPEC (47.7% f-mIoU, 69.8% f-mAcc) surpasses RegionPLC by ~20% on both metrics and matches OV3D's f-mIoU (50.4%) while exceeding its f-mAcc by 4.1%. For fine-tuned perception tasks, MPEC edges out GroupContrast (GC) on ScanNet200 semantic segmentation (31.8% vs 30.0% mIoU) and instance segmentation (31.6% vs 27.5% mAP@0.5). On high-level reasoning, MPEC+PQ3D sets new state-of-the-art on ScanRefer (51.8% vs 51.2% for PQ3D), Sr3D (80.0% vs 79.7%), Multi3DRefer (50.8% vs 50.1%), and SQA3D (47.5% vs 47.1%), while matching PQ3D on Nr3D (66.7%) and slightly trailing on Scan2Cap CIDEr (80.2 vs 80.3).

## Relation to Prior Work

MPEC bridges two previously separate research threads: open-vocabulary 3D scene understanding (OpenScene, OV3D, RegionPLC), which relies on 2D foundation models for language supervision but loses 3D spatial structure, and unsupervised 3D point cloud contrastive learning (PointContrast, MSC, GroupContrast), which captures geometric correlations but lacks object-level semantics needed for language alignment. The key conceptual departure is introducing entity-level contrastive pairs across augmented views, so that the model learns to distinguish individual 3D objects while preserving cross-view consistency — a granularity that prior unsupervised methods (region-level, neighborhood-level) and prior open-vocabulary methods (category-level 2D distillation) both miss. The framework reuses SPUNet as the 3D encoder and CLIP as the frozen text encoder, with the SceneVerse data pipeline providing entity masks and text descriptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MPEC demonstrates that incorporating explicit 3D entity structure into contrastive pre-training yields a 3D encoder that generalizes broadly — from open-vocabulary segmentation to visual grounding, scene captioning, and situated QA — without task-specific architectural changes, positioning it as a practical backbone for 3D-VL pipelines. The data-efficiency result (30.7% → 40.8% mIoU on ScanNet DEB with only 1% of scenes) is practically significant for settings where labeled 3D data is scarce. A key limitation is reliance on the fixed CLIP text encoder, which constrains performance on long, spatially detailed descriptions; the authors note this as a bottleneck for precise visual grounding. Open questions include whether the approach benefits from stronger or trainable text encoders, and whether the entity-contrast mechanism scales to outdoor or large-scale scenes where object boundaries are less well-defined.
