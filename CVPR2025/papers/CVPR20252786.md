## Problem

Dequantization-based Diffusion Image Compression (DDIC) codecs apply a latent diffusion model to remove quantization artifacts, but suffer from three critical gaps: the noise type gap (quantization error is uniform while diffusion models assume Gaussian noise), the noise level gap (mismatch between the actual SNR of quantized data and the SNR expected at a given diffusion timestep), and the discretization gap (passing discrete data to a continuous diffusion model). These gaps cause the quantized data to fall outside the diffusion model's learned distribution, producing color shifts, flat textures, and generative artifacts that severely degrade reconstruction quality, especially at very low bitrates.

## Input / Output

The codec takes an RGB image and a user-specified timestep parameter τ as input. The image is encoded to the latent space of a frozen Stable Diffusion v2.1 VAE, quantized with a bin width determined by τ, and entropy-coded (mean-scale hyperprior + ANS) into a bitstream. At decode time, the discrete latent is denoised by the uniform-noise diffusion model over t ∈ {τ, …, 1, 0} using DDIM sampling (up to 50 steps) and decoded back to image space. The single parameter τ simultaneously controls the quantization granularity, the number of denoising steps, and thus the target bitrate, enabling multi-rate compression with one model.

## Method

The core contribution is a quantization-based forward diffusion process that replaces the standard Gaussian noising step with universal quantization (dithered hard quantization), producing a discrete variable for entropy coding while the added uniform dither keeps the output continuous for the diffusion model. A derived quantization schedule Δ_t = √(12(1−α_t)) exactly matches the SNR of the quantized data to the standard diffusion variance schedule at every timestep, closing the noise level gap. The noise type gap is closed by fine-tuning a pretrained Gaussian diffusion model to sample ε ~ U(−√3, √3) instead of N(0,I) during training, while keeping the variance schedule unchanged. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) fine-tune the Stable Diffusion v2.1 U-Net for uniform noise (100k steps, LAION 6.5+, batch 8, lr 1e-5, with input perturbation), then (2) freeze all image transforms and train only the entropy model on Vimeo90k at 256² to model the quantized latent distribution across multiple timesteps. This yields a codec that operates at a wider range of target bitrates than prior DDIC methods while producing consistently realistic reconstructions at very low rates.

## Results

On MS-COCO 30k, the proposed method achieves the best rate-realism (FID) performance below 0.1 bpp and remains competitive with Relic et al. at higher rates. On the larger-resolution CLIC2020 dataset, it achieves the best results among all diffusion-based methods and matches MS-ILLM over a wide bitrate range. With LPIPS and MS-SSIM, the method achieves the best performance below 0.08 bpp, though MS-SSIM degrades above this rate (consistent with the rate-distortion-perception tradeoff). The key advantage is at very low bitrates, where reconstructions maintain high perceptual quality and realism while competing methods introduce color shifts, over-saturation, and flat textures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest competitor Relic et al. [31] (also DDIC), the proposed method wins decisively at low bitrates: Relic et al. introduce gray color into skies and over-saturate textures (e.g., wood grain, water), while the proposed method maintains accurate colors and realistic detail down to extremely low rates. Against HFD [19] (CDIC paradigm), the proposed method produces more detailed and accurate textures, particularly visible in fine structures like red doors and flowers. Against the GAN-based baselines MR [5], MS-ILLM [26], and HiFiC [24], the method is competitive on CLIC2020 over a wide bitrate range, with MS-ILLM being the closest GAN-based competitor. Against CDC [36], only quantitative comparison is made since CDC operates at significantly higher bitrates, making fair qualitative comparison infeasible. Against VTM 19.2 [1] (traditional codec), comparison is qualitative only, showing the generative method produces more realistic textures at equivalent bitrates. The ablation (Fig. 5) confirms that both the uniform noise diffusion model and universal quantization contribute to the gains, with the largest improvements at low bitrates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Dequantization-based Diffusion Image Compression (DDIC) paradigm introduced by Relic et al. [31], which applies latent diffusion models to remove quantization artifacts. The key departure is a systematic diagnosis of three specific mismatches (noise type, noise level, discretization) between quantization error and standard Gaussian diffusion assumptions, and a unified solution combining universal quantization [38,40], a derived quantization schedule, and a uniform-noise diffusion model. Architecturally, it builds directly on Stable Diffusion v2.1 [32] as a frozen VAE backbone with a fine-tuned diffusion decoder, and draws on the theoretical equivalence between uniform and Gaussian diffusion at high t [3] to justify its fine-tuning strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper is the first to demonstrate that uniform noise diffusion models function in the latent domain at practical image resolutions and can be efficiently obtained by fine-tuning a pretrained Gaussian model, validating prior theoretical results that were limited to toy settings. This opens a path for DDIC codecs to operate reliably at very low bitrates where prior methods degrade significantly. However, the approach is tied to a single architecture (Stable Diffusion v2.1), the entropy model is trained on a narrow range of timesteps, and the authors acknowledge that misgeneration of structural content at low bitrates remains an open problem. Broader questions include whether the uniform-noise fine-tuning strategy generalizes to other foundation models or modalities beyond RGB images.
