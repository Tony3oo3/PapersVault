## Problem

Graph-based multi-view clustering (GMVC) methods construct kNN graphs from high-dimensional autoencoder embeddings, but the resulting hubness problem—where a few points dominate nearest-neighbor lists—degrades graph quality and GNN message passing, ultimately harming clustering performance. This issue had been entirely overlooked in the MVC literature, which focused on novel fusion mechanisms rather than the quality of the underlying representations. Additionally, conventional autoencoders do not explicitly preserve local neighborhood topology, producing sub-optimal embeddings for clustering-oriented graph construction.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view data samples (e.g., text bag-of-words features, image pixel features, or tabular attributes) of varying dimensions per view and produces final cluster assignments via K-means on average-fused view-specific embeddings. A key design choice is that hubREP eliminates the need for view-specific decoders entirely, reducing trainable parameters by roughly 30% compared to autoencoder-based baselines (e.g., 7.94M vs. 11.57M on BBCSport). The intermediate outputs—hub-aware embeddings on a hypersphere and view-specific kNN graphs—can be directly plugged into existing GMVC frameworks like DMCAG without modifying their fusion modules.

## Method

hubREP replaces the standard encoder-decoder autoencoder with a parametric hub-aware encoder trained via a t-SNE-inspired objective: the KL divergence between input-space pairwise cosine similarities (with per-sample bandwidth κ_i) and embedding-space similarities is decomposed into a neighborhood preservation term (L_np, pulling similar input-space pairs together in embedding space) and a hubness reduction term (L_hr, negative entropy on the hypersphere that encourages uniform spreading). For cross-view consistency, a hub-aware pairing loss (L_c) aligns view-specific structure matrices weighted by per-sample hubness scores (k-occurrence counts), giving hubs more influence in the alignment. The resulting embeddings feed into a graph autoencoder (graph encoder + feature/structure decoders) for further structural refinement, with the total loss combining reconstruction, the hub-aware embedding loss (weighted by α), and the cross-view pairing loss (weighted by β).

## Results

hubREP achieves state-of-the-art clustering performance on 7 out of 8 benchmark datasets. The most dramatic improvement is on 3Sources, where hubREP reaches 92.90% ACC / 82.46% NMI / 85.19% ARI, surpassing the previous best (SURER) by 21.30% in ACC. On Reuters, hubREP improves ACC from 59.08% (DMCAG) to 65.33%, and on Noisymnist from 64.07% (AECoDDC) to 68.15%. The only dataset where hubREP is not the top performer is MNIST-USPS, where MFLVC achieves 99.36% ACC versus hubREP's 95.72%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

hubREP outperforms all 10 baselines on 7 of 8 datasets in terms of ACC. The largest margin is on 3Sources, where hubREP (92.90% ACC) exceeds the runner-up SURER (71.60%) by 21.3 percentage points, and on Reuters where it beats DMCAG (59.08%) by 6.25 points. Among graph-based baselines, hubREP surpasses DMCAG (the most relevant latent-graph method) on every dataset except MNIST-USPS, and outperforms DFP-GNN on 6 of 8 datasets. The sole loss is on MNIST-USPS, where the subspace-based MFLVC achieves 99.36% ACC versus hubREP's 95.72%, though hubREP still ranks second. On the large-scale Animal dataset (11,673 samples, 20 clusters), hubREP is the only method that avoids OOM while achieving the best ACC (29.66% vs. DMCAG's 28.64%). The transferability analysis (Table 4) further shows that plugging hubREP into DMCAG reduces hubness (SK) by 39–85% and improves ACC by 1.5–19.5 points across five datasets, while also reducing trainable parameters by 20–35%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the hyperspherical embedding theory from Trosten et al. (CVPR 2023), which addressed hubness in transductive few-shot learning via a non-parametric nohub projection, into a parametric encoder suitable for multi-view clustering where a learnable input-to-latent mapping is required. It also builds on the graph autoencoder architecture from Fan et al. (WWW 2020) for structural information aggregation. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant GMVC paradigm (DMCAG, SURER, DFP-GNN) that focuses on designing novel cross-view fusion mechanisms, instead arguing that the root cause of suboptimal performance lies in the quality of the view-specific embeddings from which kNN graphs are constructed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying hubness as a previously overlooked fundamental issue in GMVC and demonstrating a strong negative correlation between skewness and clustering metrics across eight datasets, the paper shifts attention from fusion design to representation quality. The plug-and-play nature of the hub-aware encoder (no decoder required, fewer parameters) makes it practically attractive for integration into existing GMVC pipelines. Open questions include whether the t-SNE-inspired pairwise loss scales efficiently to very large datasets (the largest here is 15K samples), whether hubness-aware embeddings transfer to other graph-based tasks beyond clustering, and whether the approach generalizes to incomplete multi-view settings where cross-view pairing is ill-defined.
