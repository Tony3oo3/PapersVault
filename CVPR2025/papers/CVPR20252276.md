## Problem

Existing audio-driven human animation methods are largely limited to head-region generation, neglecting synchronization of audio with shoulder-below body movements. Extending to half-body animation typically requires stacking multiple control conditions (full-body pose, movement maps, face/hand crop injection modules), which causes unstable training from multi-condition incoordination and elevated inference latency. The paper asks whether striking half-body animation can be achieved while simplifying these redundant conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252276/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference image (768×768, cropped half-body), an audio clip, and a hand pose sequence as input, producing a temporally coherent half-body animation video. Notably, the only pose condition required at inference is the hand keypoints—full-body pose, movement maps, and auxiliary crop-injection modules are all unnecessary. The reference image can be a FLUX-generated character, and the method is designed specifically for cropped half-body inputs rather than full-body or uncropped images.

## Method

EchoMimicV2 builds on a ReferenceNet-based Latent Diffusion Model backbone (with Wav2Vec audio encoder and Temporal Attention) and introduces the Audio-Pose Dynamic Harmonization (APDH) training strategy, which progressively shifts control from pose to audio in a waltz-like manner: Pose Sampling removes keypoints in the order lips→head→body (leaving only hands), while Audio Diffusion simultaneously expands the audio cross-attention mask from lips-only to head to global space. A Phase-specific Denoising Loss (PhD Loss) replaces the need for full pose supervision by applying pose-MSE, Canny-edge-MSE, and LPIPS losses in the early, middle, and late denoising timesteps respectively. Head Partial Attention enables seamless incorporation of headshot data for facial expression augmentation without additional modules, and the entire training follows a curriculum from full-pose pretraining through iterative pose dropout to hands-only pose with global audio control.

## Results

On the EMTD benchmark, EchoMimicV2 achieves FID 49.33, FVD 598.45, SSIM 0.738, and PSNR 21.986, outperforming both pose-driven baselines across all quality metrics. For audio-visual synchronization, EchoMimicV2 attains Sync-C 7.219 and Sync-D 7.021, substantially surpassing AnimateAnyone (Sync-C 0.987, Sync-D 13.887) and MimicMotion (Sync-C 1.495, Sync-D 7.958). On hand animation quality, EchoMimicV2 sets new SOTA with HKC 0.923 and HKV 25.28, and leads in identity consistency (CSIM 0.558) and expression fidelity (E-FID 2.218).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EchoMimicV2 outperforms both pose-driven baselines on every reported metric. Against AnimateAnyone [16], it reduces FID by 16.5% (49.33 vs 58.98), FVD by 41.1% (598.45 vs 1016.47), and improves Sync-C by over 7× (7.219 vs 0.987), reflecting far superior audio-lip synchronization. Against MimicMotion [46], it achieves lower FID (49.33 vs 53.47), lower FVD (598.45 vs 622.62), higher PSNR (21.986 vs 19.278), and a 4.8× improvement in Sync-C (7.219 vs 1.495). For the two audio-driven half-body methods, Vlogger [48] and CyberHost [20], only qualitative comparisons are provided since neither is open-sourced; the paper reports that EchoMimicV2 surpasses both in generated image quality and movement naturalness, while requiring fewer conditions (no body movement maps, no face/hand crop injection modules, no full-body pose guidance). Notably, EchoMimicV2 achieves these gains with only a reference image, audio clip, and hand pose sequence as inputs, whereas CyberHost additionally requires body movement sequences and multiple region-specific injection modules.

## Relation to Prior Work

EchoMimicV2 extends the EchoMimic portrait animation framework from head-region to half-body animation, departing from the multi-condition paradigm of prior audio-driven half-body methods (CyberHost, Vlogger) that require body movement maps, face/hand crop injection modules, and full-body pose guidance. Conceptually, it draws on the curriculum-learning intuition of progressive condition dropout (analogous to V-Express's conditional dropout) but applies it in a spatially structured manner—progressively removing pose keypoints from lips to head to body while simultaneously diffusing audio attention outward. The Phase-specific Denoising Loss is a novel training objective that replaces the need for auxiliary pose encoders and VAE decoders during training, and the Head Partial Attention mechanism repurposes headshot data as a free augmentation for half-body training without additional architectural components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical gap between academic head-only talking-face research and industrial demand for half-body avatar animation, demonstrating that condition simplification (audio + reference image + hand pose only) can match or exceed methods with multiple injection modules. The EMTD benchmark fills a notable evaluation gap for half-body animation. Key open questions remain: the method still requires predefined hand pose sequences (limiting end-to-end deployment), performance degrades on non-cropped full-body images, and whether the APDH curriculum generalizes to other condition-simplification scenarios (e.g., removing audio entirely for pose-only driving) is unexplored.
