## Problem

Existing facial identity representations such as ArcFace and CLIP are trained with discriminative or contrastive objectives on single images, causing them to discard subtle identity nuances (e.g., nose shape, beard, head geometry) that are critical for high-fidelity generative tasks. Moreover, single-image encodings fundamentally lack holistic information about how a person appears across diverse poses and expressions, limiting their utility in generative applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251818/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder accepts a variable number of unstructured face images of the same individual (any count, at varying poses, expressions, and lighting; faces filtered to >448 px resolution in MFHQ) and produces a fixed-size structured representation of 256 tokens × 1280 dimensions, where each token corresponds to a specific global or local identity feature. Downstream, this representation is injected via IP-Adapter into a frozen FLUX flow model to generate photorealistic face images in unseen poses or scene-level personalized text-to-image outputs.

## Method

Omni-ID uses a transformer encoder with 256 learnable queries that cross-attends over concatenated CLIP-H image features from all input images, followed by self-attention, to produce a fixed-size identity code. Training employs a few-to-many identity reconstruction paradigm: a small random subset of images is encoded, and the model must reconstruct a larger set of target images of the same person in different poses and expressions, with targets corrupted (masked or noised) to prevent autoencoding. A multi-decoder objective combines two complementary decoders: a Masked Transformer Decoder (95% token masking) that learns wide-coverage identity features, and a conditional Flow-Matching decoder (FLUX + IP-Adapter) that captures fine-grained details across noise levels. The entire system is trained on the newly introduced MFHQ dataset (134K identities × 8 video-sourced images, >448 px), which ensures intra-identity consistency and high resolution unlike face-recognition datasets.

## Results

On controllable face generation (IP-Adapter + ControlNet with frozen FLUX), Omni-ID achieves 0.708 ID Similarity with 1 input and 0.742 with 7 inputs on the MFHQ test set, substantially outperforming ArcFace (0.515/0.535), CLIP (0.648/0.682), and ArcFace+CLIP (0.638/0.664). On the WebFace test set, Omni-ID reaches 0.774–0.785 ID Similarity across 3–16 inputs, compared to 0.370–0.379 for ArcFace and 0.695–0.696 for CLIP. Pose Error is competitive across all methods (≈2.0–2.7°), confirming that the ID Similarity gains are not at the expense of pose accuracy. Notably, Omni-ID's ID Similarity improves monotonically with more input images, while baselines plateau after 1–3 inputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Omni-ID consistently outperforms all three baselines on ID Similarity across both test sets and all input counts. Against ArcFace, the gap is largest: Omni-ID achieves 0.708 vs. 0.515 (1 input, MFHQ) and 0.774 vs. 0.379 (3 inputs, WebFace), reflecting ArcFace's discriminative training that discards fine-grained identity details. Against CLIP, Omni-ID gains 0.060 ID Similarity with 1 input on MFHQ (0.708 vs. 0.648) and 0.079 with 3 inputs on WebFace (0.774 vs. 0.695), and the advantage grows with more inputs since CLIP's single-instance encoding cannot integrate complementary views. The combined ArcFace+CLIP baseline (following FaceIDPlus) is intermediate but still trails Omni-ID by 0.070 (MFHQ, 1 input) and 0.122 (WebFace, 3 inputs). On Pose Error, all methods are within 0.1–0.4° of each other, indicating comparable pose adherence; Omni-ID is marginally best on MFHQ (2.0°) but slightly worse than ArcFace on WebFace (2.6° vs. 2.4°), a minor trade-off for the substantial identity gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of training face identity representations with discriminative (ArcFace) or contrastive (CLIP, FaRL) objectives, arguing instead that a generative training objective preserves the fine-grained identity details critical for downstream generation. Architecturally, it builds on IP-Adapter's decoupled attention mechanism for injecting identity features into FLUX, and on ControlNet for pose conditioning, but replaces the single-image encoder with a transformer that consolidates multiple input images via cross-attention over learnable queries. The few-to-many reconstruction training and the multi-decoder strategy (combining masked autoencoding for broad coverage with flow matching for fine details) are novel training designs not present in prior face representation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Omni-ID offers a drop-in replacement for ArcFace/CLIP embeddings in existing face generation pipelines (IP-Adapter, ControlNet), with the practical advantage that quality scales with the number of input images rather than being capped by a single view. The MFHQ dataset (134K identities × 8 high-quality multi-pose frames) addresses a real gap in available training data for generative face representations. Open limitations include the inability to represent non-intrinsic attributes like hair, sensitivity to lighting and skin tone, and the fixed 256-token representation size; extending to more diverse contexts, additional decoder types, and larger-scale data collection are identified as future directions.
