## Problem

Domain adaptive person search requires jointly localizing and identifying persons across domains, but existing unified frameworks (e.g., DAPS) suffer from gradient conflict because person detection demands commonness across all persons while ReID demands uniqueness per identity, and these contradictory objectives compete for shared parameters. This conflict is exacerbated in cross-domain settings where the model must simultaneously learn domain-invariant and identity-discriminative representations without target-domain labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251443/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a cropped query person image and uncropped gallery scene images (resized to 1550×900) as input, operating in an unsupervised domain adaptation setting where only the source domain provides bounding box and identity annotations. It produces person bounding boxes with detection confidence scores (from the magnitude of the instance vector) and identity embeddings for ReID (from the direction of the instance vector), with the instance vector decomposed via a norm-aware embedding head followed by global average pooling.

## Method

MoS decomposes the norm-aware embedding head into multiple submodules by dropping convolutional blocks, so each submodule uses a subset of the main model's parameters. A conditional routing policy then dynamically selects Top-K submodules based on both task (detection vs. ReID, encoded via one-hot vectors passed through an MLP) and domain (source vs. target), using an outer product between the instance feature and task embedding as the routing input. For identity-aware domain alignment, the method introduces counterpart domain sample generation: it denormalizes DSBN-normalized features using the counterpart domain's mean and standard deviation to synthesize augmented samples with consistent identity across domains. These synthesized samples form positive pairs with their originals, while cross-domain samples of different identities serve as negatives in a contrastive domain alignment loss, enabling the model to learn domain-invariant yet identity-discriminative ReID representations without requiring GAN-based synthesis.

## Results

On the CUHK-SYSU → PRW domain adaptive person search benchmark, MoS achieves 37.1% mAP and 81.9% top-1 accuracy for ReID, improving over the DAPS baseline by 2.4% mAP and 1.3% top-1. On the reverse direction (PRW → CUHK-SYSU), MoS reaches 80.1% mAP and 81.5% top-1, outperforming DAPS by 2.5% mAP and 1.9% top-1. Notably, MoS under unsupervised domain adaptation surpasses several supervised learning methods (e.g., DPM, MGTS, OIM, IAN, NPSM) on the PRW target, though it remains below the strongest supervised one-step methods such as SeqNet (46.7% mAP) and AlignPS (45.9% mAP) on PRW.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary direct competitor is DAPS [48], the only prior domain adaptive person search method. MoS consistently outperforms DAPS in both transfer directions: +2.4% mAP / +1.3% top-1 on CUHK-SYSU→PRW and +2.5% mAP / +1.9% top-1 on PRW→CUHK-SYSU. Against supervised learning baselines, MoS (trained without any target-domain labels) surpasses DPM (20.5% mAP), MGTS (32.6%), OIM (21.3%), IAN (23.0%), and NPSM (24.2%) on the PRW target, demonstrating that the domain adaptive formulation with MoS can exceed methods that rely on full target annotations. However, MoS still trails the strongest supervised one-step methods on PRW—SeqNet (46.7% mAP), AlignPS (45.9%), BINet (45.3%), and NAE (44.0%)—by roughly 7–10% mAP, reflecting the inherent gap between unsupervised adaptation and fully supervised training. On the CUHK-SYSU target, MoS (80.1% mAP) improves over DAPS (77.6%) but remains well below supervised methods like TCTS (93.9%) and SeqNet (93.8%), indicating that the larger, more diverse CUHK-SYSU domain is harder to adapt to without labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DAPS [48], the first method to formalize domain adaptive person search, by replacing its unified framework with a mixture of submodules (MoS) architecture that routes features through task- and domain-conditional submodules to mitigate gradient conflict between detection and ReID. The architectural foundation draws on the norm-aware embedding head from [5], which decomposes instance vectors into magnitude (detection) and direction (ReID), and the counterpart domain sample generation leverages domain-specific batch normalization [1] to synthesize cross-domain augmented samples without requiring a GAN. The paper is the first to apply sparse mixture-of-experts routing to the domain adaptive person search setting, combining task-conditioned and domain-conditioned routing in a single framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MoS framework offers a practical, lightweight solution to the gradient conflict problem inherent in jointly optimizing detection (commonness) and ReID (uniqueness) under domain shift, without the computational overhead of parallel expert networks. The denormalization-based sample generation is particularly appealing as a GAN-free alternative for cross-domain augmentation. However, the experimental scope is limited to two datasets (CUHK-SYSU and PRW) and a single backbone (ResNet-50), and the improvements over DAPS are modest (2.4–2.5% mAP). Open questions include whether the approach scales to more than two domains, whether it generalizes to other multi-task domain adaptation problems (e.g., detection + segmentation), and whether the routing policy remains effective with deeper or transformer-based backbones.
