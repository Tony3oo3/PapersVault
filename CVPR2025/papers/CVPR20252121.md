## Problem

Traditional point cloud representations suffer from two inherent ambiguities in 3D classification: (1) discrete sampling makes wire-like and flat surfaces appear geometrically similar, and (2) the hard-existence assumption (every point is fully opaque) prevents distinguishing transparent or reflective objects with identical shapes. No prior work has exploited the continuous geometric and material information available in Gaussian Splatting representations to resolve these ambiguities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252121/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a GS point cloud where each point carries four attributes: 3D position, a scalar opacity in [0,1], a 3D scale vector (standard deviations), and a 4D rotation quaternion, yielding 11 input channels per point. Spherical harmonics (view-dependent color) are deliberately excluded to maintain a fair comparison with position-only baselines. The output is a standard k-way probability distribution over object categories, and the only architectural modification to existing classifiers is widening the first layer from 3 to 11 input channels.

## Method

The core idea is to replace the position-only point cloud input of any existing 3D classifier with a GS point cloud, where each point is a 3D Gaussian ellipsoid whose scale and rotation encode local surface geometry and whose opacity encodes material transparency. Wire-like surfaces are naturally represented by multiple slender, oriented ellipsoids, while flat surfaces use fewer broad, flat ellipsoids; transparent objects exhibit low opacity values. The authors construct the first real-world GS point cloud dataset (4,000 objects, 20 categories, ~30 views each) by running official 3DGS optimization on manually curated multi-view images from MVIImageNet, requiring over 600 GPU-hours on a single A100. Experiments across six representative classifiers (PointNet through PTv3) show consistent accuracy gains, with the largest improvements (+7.3% OA) on the simplest architectures and on the most ambiguous object pairs (e.g., Loudspeaker vs. Can, Mug vs. Ashcan), confirming that the benefit is representation-driven rather than architecture-specific.

## Results

On the proposed GS Point Cloud Dataset (4,000 objects, 20 categories), incorporating GS coefficients (position, scale, rotation, opacity) into the input consistently improves all six baseline classifiers. The largest gain is observed for PointNet, which jumps from 73.56% to 80.87% overall accuracy (+7.31 pp) and from 73.77% to 81.46% mean class accuracy (+7.69 pp). State-of-the-art methods also benefit, with PointMLP reaching 90.21% OA (+2.30 pp) and DeLA reaching 90.36% OA (+1.58 pp), while the strongest baseline PTv3 improves by 1.15 pp to 89.93% OA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates six representative point-cloud classifiers (PointNet, PointNet++, PointNeXt, PointMLP, DeLA, PTv3) under an identical protocol where only the input is changed from 3-channel position to 11-channel GS point cloud (position + opacity + scale + rotation). Every method improves with GS coefficients, confirming the gain is representation-driven rather than architecture-specific. PointNet benefits the most (+7.31 pp OA), suggesting its simple shared-MLP design was most bottlenecked by the information-poor position-only input. Among SOTA methods, PointMLP and DeLA lead in absolute OA (90.21% and 90.36% respectively) with GS input, while PTv3 shows the smallest relative gain (+1.15 pp OA), likely because its transformer-based attention already captures richer local context from position alone. The consistent positive delta across all six baselines demonstrates that GS coefficients provide complementary geometric and material information that no single architecture can fully exploit from raw coordinates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023), originally designed for novel view synthesis, as an input representation for point cloud classification. Rather than proposing a new network architecture, it augments the input channel of existing classifiers (PointNet through PTv3) with GS coefficients—scale, rotation, and opacity—while keeping all downstream layers unchanged. The conceptual departure is that GS was engineered to fill rendering holes and model view-dependent appearance, yet the same coefficients (ellipsoid geometry and soft opacity) turn out to encode local surface structure and material transparency that pure position-based point clouds cannot capture. The dataset is built on MVIImageNet multi-view images, extending that resource into the GS domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first real-world GS point cloud dataset (4,000 objects, 20 categories) and a clear empirical demonstration that GS coefficients reduce classification ambiguity for wire-like vs. flat surfaces and transparent vs. reflective objects. However, the practical impact is tempered by the fact that GS point cloud acquisition requires multi-view RGB cameras and per-object optimization (600+ GPU hours), making it far less accessible than LiDAR or structured-light point clouds. Open questions include whether the representation extends to other 3D tasks (segmentation, detection), whether the gains persist at larger scale, and whether lightweight GS estimation from single or few views could democratize access.
