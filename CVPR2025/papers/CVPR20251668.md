## Problem

Existing event-based object detectors are constrained by CNNs' limited local receptive fields, Transformers' O(N²) computational cost, or SNNs' inferior accuracy, and the field lacks a large-scale, diverse benchmark dataset for fair comparison. This paper addresses both gaps by proposing a physics-inspired MoE heat conduction backbone that balances accuracy and efficiency, and by introducing EvDET200K, a 200K-annotation benchmark captured with a high-definition event camera.

## Input / Output

The method consumes asynchronous event streams from a Prophesee EVK4-HD camera, which are first stacked into event frames at 640×640 px resolution; each sample spans 2–5 seconds. The detector outputs a set of bounding boxes with class labels across 10 categories (people, cars, bicycles, electric bicycles, basketball, ping pong, goose, cats, birds, UAVs) using a DETR-style set-prediction head with Hungarian matching, avoiding NMS.

## Method

MvHeat-DET replaces the standard Transformer attention block with a Mixture-of-Experts Heat Conduction Operator (MHCO). Each MHCO layer uses a policy network with Gumbel-Softmax to route features to one of three frequency-domain experts—DFT-IDFT, DCT-IDCT, or HT-IHT—motivated by their complementary strengths (DCT for small targets, HT for complex scenes, DFT for general applicability). Randomly initialized Frequency Embeddings are passed through a linear layer to predict a content-adaptive thermal diffusivity k, which modulates the transformed features via the kernel e^{−k(vx²+vy²)t} before inverse transformation back to the spatial domain. An IoU-based query selection module then extracts the top-K encoder tokens for a DETR-style detection head, with the classification loss augmented by an IoU regression term to align selection with localization quality. The backbone is organized in four stages with progressive downsampling, and the full model is trained end-to-end under supervised learning on the EvDET200K dataset.

## Results

On the newly proposed EvDET200K dataset, MvHeat-DET achieves 52.9 mAP@50:95, 80.4 mAP@50, and 55.9 mAP@75, outperforming the strongest baseline DetectoRS (49.1 mAP@50:95) by 3.8 points while using fewer parameters (47.5M vs. 123.2M) and lower FLOPs (56.4G vs. 117.2G). On N-Caltech101, MvHeat-DET reaches 55.7 mAP, surpassing the previous best EAS-SNN (53.8) by 1.9 points. The model runs at 58 FPS with 47.5M parameters, offering a favorable accuracy–efficiency trade-off compared to heavier Transformer-based detectors like Swin-T (160M params, 1043G FLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MvHeat-DET outperforms all 19 compared baselines on both evaluation datasets. On EvDET200K, it leads the closest competitors DetectoRS (49.1) and Swin-T (49.0) by 3.8 and 3.9 mAP@50:95 respectively, while using roughly 2.6× fewer parameters than DetectoRS and 3.4× fewer than Swin-T. Among SNN-based detectors, it surpasses SpikeYOLO (41.2) by 11.7 points and EMS-YOLO (32.1) by 20.8 points, confirming the persistent accuracy gap of spiking architectures. Among Transformer-based event detectors, it beats S5-ViT (42.9) by 10.0, RVT (40.7) by 12.2, and SAST (27.4) by 25.5 mAP@50:95. On N-Caltech101, MvHeat-DET (55.7 mAP) edges out EAS-SNN (53.8) by 1.9 and Jeziorek et al. (53.4) by 2.3, while substantially outperforming YOLE (39.8) and NvS (34.6). The vHeat† baseline (vHeat encoder + Transformer decoder) achieves 50.3 mAP@50:95, and the MoE expert mechanism adds a further 2.6 points, validating the multi-expert heat conduction design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the vHeat backbone [42], which modeled image patches as heat sources and used a single DCT-IDCT pair to approximate the Heat Conduction Operator, by replacing that fixed transform with a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) layer that routes each feature map through one of three frequency-domain experts (DFT, DCT, or Haar Transform) via a Gumbel-Softmax policy network. The detection head follows the DETR [3] paradigm with learnable queries, augmented by the IoU-based query selection strategy from DETRs-beat-YOLOs [48]. Conceptually, the paper departs from both the CNN-based event detectors (limited local receptive fields) and the SNN-based ones (lower accuracy) by proposing a physics-inspired, frequency-domain backbone that sits between the two in complexity and interpretability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of EvDET200K (10,054 samples, 200K bounding boxes, 10 classes, captured with a Prophesee EVK4-HD camera) fills a notable gap in event-based detection benchmarks, which previously relied on smaller or less diverse datasets like Gen1 and 1Mpx. The MoE heat-conduction backbone offers a practical middle ground: it outperforms vHeat by ~2.6 mAP while using fewer parameters and FLOPs than Swin-T or DetectoRS, suggesting frequency-domain feature mixing can rival attention at lower cost. Open questions include whether the heat-conduction framing generalizes beyond detection (e.g., segmentation, tracking), whether the Gumbel-Softmax routing remains stable at scale, and whether the modest gains over vHeat persist on larger or more diverse event datasets.
