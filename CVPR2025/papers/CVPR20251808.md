## Problem

Existing robotic manipulation policies face a fundamental trade-off: explicit action value maps provide spatial grounding but require exponential discretization for continuous 6-DoF actions, while implicit diffusion-based policies model continuous multimodal distributions but rely on large denoising networks and iterative sampling, resulting in slow inference. No prior method simultaneously achieves spatial awareness in 3D observation space, continuous action modeling, and efficient inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251808/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB-D images (4 cameras at 128×128: front, left/right shoulder, wrist) which are converted to 3D point clouds with learned point features, a natural language instruction encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder, and robot proprioception. The output is a 6-DoF end-effector pose in SE(3) sampled via iterative reverse diffusion, plus binary gripper open and collision-avoidance states. Unusually, the action space is explicitly anchored to 3D observation coordinates: the denoising network is conditioned on a latent vector aggregated from the triplane at the noisy action's spatial location, rather than on a global observation embedding.

## Method

PDFactor projects 3D point cloud features onto three orthogonal 2D planes (top-down, left-right, front-back), patchifies them into tokens, and processes them with a tri-perspective view transformer that also ingests CLIP instruction tokens and robot proprioception (via adaptive layer norm), using 3D rotary position embeddings to learn cross-view correspondences. The transformer outputs three 2D feature planes forming a latent triplane; for any 3D location, a latent vector is obtained by bilinearly interpolating and summing features from the three planes. A small MLP denoising network (3 residual blocks) predicts the noise component of a noisy 6-DoF action conditioned on this location-specific latent vector, trained with a score matching loss derived from DDPM. This MLP serves a dual role: as a parameterized loss function measuring triplane feature quality during training, and as an action gradient decoder during inference where reverse DDPM sampling re-queries the triplane at each noisy action's 3D location. The hybrid explicit-implicit design yields ~10× faster inference than prior diffusion-based methods while outperforming them in accuracy on RLBench.

## Results

On RLBench (18 tasks, 25 episodes each, 5 seeds), PDFactor-2* achieves an average success rate of 87.3% with an average rank of 1.6, a 5.9% absolute improvement over the previous state-of-the-art RVT-2 (81.4%). Even the smallest variant PDFactor-T (16M params) reaches 77.9%, while the base model PDFactor-B (83M params) attains 84.0%. In the real world across 6 tasks with only 15 demonstrations each, PDFactor achieves 47/60 (78.3%) success, outperforming RVT-2 (38/60) and 3D Diffuser Actor (31/60).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDFactor-2* (87.3%) outperforms all six baselines on RLBench, with the largest margins over C2F-ARM-BC (+67.2%), PerAct (+37.9%), and RVT (+24.4%). Against the two strongest competitors, PDFactor-2* beats RVT-2 (81.4%) by 5.9% and 3D Diffuser Actor (81.3%) by 6.0% in average success rate. Notably, even the tiny PDFactor-T (16M params, 77.9%) is competitive with RVT-2, while PDFactor-B (83M, 84.0%) already surpasses both RVT-2 and 3D Diffuser Actor without the two-stage zoom-in trick. In the real-world evaluation (6 tasks, 15 demos each), PDFactor achieves 47/60 success versus 38/60 for RVT-2 and 31/60 for 3D Diffuser Actor, with the largest gap on Stack Cups (4/10 vs. 1/10 vs. 0/10) and Sort Cylinder (7/10 vs. 3/10 vs. 4/10). PDFactor also achieves substantially faster inference (~3.8 FPS for PDFactor-B) compared to 3D Diffuser Actor's iterative diffusion, while maintaining higher accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

PDFactor synthesizes three established threads: the triplane representation from 3D generative modeling (EG3D), the multi-view projection strategy from RVT for robotic manipulation, and the DDPM score-matching framework. The key departure from prior diffusion-based manipulation policies (Diffusion Policy, 3D Diffuser Actor) is replacing the large observation-conditioned denoising network with a tiny MLP that decodes from a learned 3D triplane field, effectively using the denoiser as a parameterized loss function during training and a lightweight score-gradient decoder at inference. This positions the work at the intersection of hybrid explicit-implicit neural field representations and diffusion-based action modeling, borrowing the spatial grounding of explicit affordance maps while retaining the continuous, multimodal expressiveness of implicit diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× inference speedup over diffusion-based policies while simultaneously improving accuracy challenges the prevailing assumption that diffusion-based manipulation must sacrifice real-time performance. This makes diffusion-style multimodal action modeling practically viable for closed-loop robot control, a regime where inference latency is critical. The paper acknowledges that higher precision still requires more diffusion steps, and the real-world evaluation uses only 15 demonstrations per task on a single camera, leaving open questions about robustness to sensor noise, multi-camera setups, and long-horizon tasks in unstructured environments. Future work on distilling or accelerating the diffusion sampling (e.g., consistency models, few-step samplers) could close the remaining efficiency gap.
