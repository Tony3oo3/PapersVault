## Problem

For panoramic WSI tasks like survival prediction, models must integrate intratumoral tissue heterogeneity and inter-tissue interactions, yet existing approaches (PANTHER, HEAT) rely on fixed priors from external classifiers or clustering, adding inference complexity and constraining the model's ability to discover novel prognostic factors. The paper asks whether tissue heterogeneity can be recognized and exploited end-to-end within the MIL aggregation step without additional workflow design.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251164/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes patch embeddings (dimension d) extracted by a pre-trained encoder (UNI) from gigapixel WSIs segmented into N > 10,000 non-overlapping patches. During training, a 10% random subset of patches is additionally classified by CONCH to derive tissue prototypes for expert supervision; at inference, no external classifier is needed. The module outputs a filtered, expert-weighted patch representation (discarding patches no expert selects) that feeds into the downstream prediction head, producing a slide-level survival estimate (C-index) and, for interpretability, per-expert patch assignment heatmaps.

## Method

PAMoE replaces the feed-forward layer in a MIL backbone with a Mixture-of-Experts layer using Expert Choice Routing: rather than assigning each patch to an expert, each of m experts independently selects its top-k patches (k = n·c/m), and patches chosen by no expert are discarded, naturally filtering task-irrelevant background. Experts are split into 4 Prior Supervised Experts and 2 Free Experts; the supervised experts' routing probabilities are regularized via a cross-entropy loss against cosine-similarity selection probabilities computed from tissue prototypes (tumor, stroma, necrosis, infiltration) pre-extracted with CONCH, while free experts learn unconstrained preferences. The total training objective is L = L_task + α·L_PAMoE (α = 0.1), making the prior a soft constraint rather than a hard assignment. The module is plug-and-play: it is inserted by swapping the fully connected layer in TransMIL, LongViT, or PatchGCN, and the paper hypothesizes that its benefit is strongest in transformer backbones because the expanded latent space from diverse expert mappings synergizes with global self-attention to capture long-range tissue interactions.

## Results

On the survival prediction task across five TCGA cancer datasets (COAD, LGG, LUAD, PAAD, BRCA), PAMoE consistently improves transformer-based baselines: TransMIL+PAMoE achieves C-index of 0.688/0.779/0.668/0.655/0.694, and LongViT+PAMoE achieves 0.689/0.780/0.644/0.639/0.673, outperforming their respective baselines by 0.002–0.034 in C-index. The best single result is PatchGCN+PAMoE on LGG (0.793) and COAD (0.715), though PatchGCN+PAMoE shows inconsistent gains versus its baseline. Overall, PAMoE-integrated models match or exceed prior-knowledge-based methods (PANTHER, HEAT) on 3–4 of 5 datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAMoE is integrated into three base architectures (TransMIL, LongViT, PatchGCN) and compared against eight baselines. Against transformer-based baselines, PAMoE delivers consistent gains: TransMIL+PAMoE improves over TransMIL on all five datasets (e.g., LUAD 0.668 vs 0.644, +0.024), and LongViT+PAMoE improves over LongViT on all five (e.g., COAD 0.689 vs 0.655, +0.034). Against prior-knowledge methods, TransMIL+PAMoE beats PANTHER on LGG (0.779 vs 0.763), LUAD (0.668 vs 0.656), and BRCA (0.694 vs 0.691), but trails on PAAD (0.655 vs 0.666); it beats HEAT on LGG (0.779 vs 0.743), LUAD (0.668 vs 0.646), and BRCA (0.694 vs 0.686), but trails on COAD (0.688 vs 0.692) and PAAD (0.655 vs 0.664). For PatchGCN, PAMoE yields only marginal or negative changes (e.g., LUAD 0.651 vs 0.657, BRCA 0.679 vs 0.692), which the authors attribute to PatchGCN's local-only graph interactions limiting the benefit of global expert routing. Classic MIL baselines (ABMIL, AttnMISL, CaMIL) are uniformly outperformed by all PAMoE variants across most datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

PAMoE sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) pathology-aware WSI aggregation methods like PANTHER (prototype-guided clustering) and HEAT (heterogeneous graph with pre-classified tissue categories), which require additional inference steps and specialized workflows, and (2) Mixture-of-Experts architectures, particularly the expert-choice routing variant of Zhou et al. that inverts the standard token-to-expert assignment. The paper's key departure is repurposing expert-choice routing so that each expert acts as a tissue specialist that *selects* patches of interest (discarding irrelevant ones), while a prior-supervision loss (computed from CONCH-classified prototypes) gently aligns expert preferences with known intratumoral tissue categories—achieving pathology awareness end-to-end without the extra classifier or clustering pipeline that PANTHER and HEAT require at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design means PAMoE can be dropped into existing TransMIL, LongViT, or similar transformer-based WSI pipelines with minimal architectural changes, lowering the barrier for incorporating tissue-heterogeneity awareness. The observation that PAMoE benefits transformer-based models but not PatchGCN suggests the gain comes from expanding the latent space for global self-attention interactions rather than from local tissue routing alone—a useful mechanistic insight for future WSI architecture design. However, the evaluation is limited to survival prediction on five TCGA cancer types, hardware constraints prevented scaling experiments, and the impact on non-transformer or larger-scale architectures remains untested, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to broader clinical tasks or foundation-model-scale settings.
