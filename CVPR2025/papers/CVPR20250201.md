## Problem

Existing data-efficient GAN (DE-GAN) training strategies—data augmentation, noise injection, and pre-trained models—focus exclusively on increasing the number of training samples while ignoring sample quality. This oversight allows the discriminator to become overly confident in distinguishing real from fake samples, causing overfitting that degrades generation quality under limited-data settings. No prior method incorporates sample-quality-aware mechanisms directly into the DE-GAN training loop.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250201/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The generator takes a standard noise vector and produces 256×256 RGB synthetic images, operating as a drop-in plugin on existing DE-GAN backbones (StyleGAN2, FastGAN, Diffusion-Projected GAN, InsGen). During training, the discriminator's scalar outputs (critic scores) on each minibatch of real and fake samples are sorted to identify which samples to reject; batch sizes range from 4 (low-shot datasets) to 64 (FFHQ), and the rejection budget is bounded by λ×batchsize with λ=0.25.

## Method

QADDRS introduces two coupled steps into the discriminator update: (1) a sample-quality-aware step that sorts real and fake samples in the minibatch by their discriminator critic scores, and (2) a dynamic rejection step that removes the N highest-scoring real samples and N lowest-scoring fake samples before computing the D loss, while all fake samples are retained for the G update. The rejection number N is adapted online via an overfitting heuristic η (the expected sign of the discriminator's real-sample logits): N increments with probability p (derived from η) and decrements with probability 1−p, bounded in [0, λ×batchsize]. Theoretically, QADDRS re-weights the real and fake data distributions without altering their supports, so convergence to the same KL/JS or reverse-KL objectives is preserved for both non-saturating and hinge losses. Empirically, the method acts as a general plugin that improves FID across five DE-GAN architectures on low-shot and FFHQ benchmarks with negligible computational overhead (~0.25 s per 1K images).

## Results

On the 256×256 low-shot datasets, Diffusion-Projected GAN (FastGAN) + QADDRS achieves the best FID scores across all five benchmarks: 9.93 (Obama), 14.72 (Grumpy Cat), 3.08 (Panda), 16.94 (AF-Cat), and 16.57 (AF-Dog). On FFHQ, Diffusion-Projected GAN (FastGAN Lite) + QADDRS delivers the strongest FID of 4.69 on the 5K subset, 5.18 on 2K, 6.57 on 1K, and 19.89 on 100. QADDRS consistently reduces FID by 1–5 points over its base method across all tested DE-GANs (StyleGAN2, Diff-Augment, ADA, InsGen, FastGAN), confirming it as a general plug-in training strategy. The added computational cost is negligible: only 0.25 seconds per 1K images (17.25 s vs. 17 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

QADDRS consistently improves FID over every base DE-GAN it is applied to: StyleGAN2* drops from 65.57 to 53.54 on Obama (−12.03), StyleGAN2+Diff-Augment from 46.87 to 41.27 (−5.60), StyleGAN2+ADA from 45.69 to 40.83 (−4.86), InsGen+ADA from 32.42 to 25.41 (−7.01), and Diffusion-Projected GAN (FastGAN) from 10.54 to 9.93 on Obama (−0.61). On FFHQ-5K, Diffusion-Projected GAN (FastGAN Lite) + QADDRS achieves FID 4.69, outperforming Projected GAN (6.85), FakeCLR (7.25), and InsGen (7.83), establishing a new state-of-the-art among GAN-based methods. GAN-based methods with QADDRS clearly outperform diffusion-based baselines (EDM, Patch Diffusion) on low-shot datasets, where EDM+DA scores 37.10 on Obama versus 9.93 for FastGAN+QADDRS. Against the original DRS post-training method [5], QADDRS yields substantially larger gains (e.g., 41.27 vs. 45.22 on Obama), confirming that integrating rejection sampling into training is more effective than applying it after training. A fixed rejection number (N = 0.25 × batchsize) underperforms the dynamic N (44.12 vs. 41.27 on Obama), validating the adaptive mechanism. The hyperparameter λ = 0.25 is optimal, with λ = 0.50 and 0.75 degrading performance (42.40 and 43.32 on Obama).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes discriminator rejection sampling (DRS), originally a post-training technique for selecting high-quality generated samples, and adapts it into the training loop of data-efficient GANs. The dynamic rejection mechanism directly inherits the overfitting heuristic η and probability-based adjustment from ADA and APA, which were designed for adaptive data augmentation. The key conceptual departure is shifting the focus from increasing sample *quantity* (via augmentation, noise injection, or pre-trained models) to filtering by sample *quality* during training, which the authors argue has been overlooked in prior DE-GAN work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QADDRS offers a lightweight, architecture-agnostic plugin that consistently improves FID across StyleGAN2, FastGAN, and Diffusion-Projected GAN backbones with negligible computational overhead, making it practically attractive for practitioners working in low-data regimes. The theoretical analysis confirming that re-weighting does not alter distribution support and thus preserves convergence is reassuring but follows from established results. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond face-image domains, whether the simple ±1 random walk for N is optimal, and whether the modest 2–5 FID improvements compound meaningfully at larger scales or in non-face settings.
