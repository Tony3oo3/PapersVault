## Problem

Event cameras are overwhelmingly deployed on ground vehicles, with 12 of 15 public perception datasets collected exclusively from this platform. Adapting event-based dense perception to drones and quadrupeds introduces unique motion dynamics, viewpoints, and activation patterns that conventional frame-based domain adaptation methods cannot handle due to the spatiotemporal nature of event streams. This paper is the first to systematically address cross-platform adaptation across vehicle, drone, and quadruped event camera domains.

## Input / Output

The method consumes event voxel grids of shape T×H×W (polarity-sensitive, with positive and negative events in separate temporal bins) from both a labeled source platform and an unlabeled target platform, along with source semantic labels over 11 classes. EventBlend constructs hybrid voxel grids by selectively copying entire temporal sequences at each spatial location from either source or target based on a binary mask derived from density-map similarity. The output is a per-pixel semantic segmentation mask; the EXPo benchmark comprises 89,228 frames across 21 sequences spanning city, urban, suburban, and rural scenes.

## Method

EventFly combines three components built around the Event Activation Prior (EAP), which identifies high-activation regions in the target domain and minimizes prediction entropy there via a MAP regularization derived from maximum entropy principles. EventBlend creates hybrid voxel grids by computing per-sample source density maps and comparing them against a pre-computed aggregated target density map; a thresholded similarity map yields a binary mask that dictates whether each spatial location's temporal sequence is copied from source (high similarity) or target (low similarity), with labels combined from source ground truth and target pseudo-labels (via mean teacher). EventMatch employs two fully convolutional discriminators: σ1 adversarially aligns blended features with source features to preserve supervised stability, while σ2 softly adapts blended features toward target features specifically in EAP-identified high-activation regions. The backbone (E2VID) is trained via min-max optimization against both discriminators, with the target-domain network updated via EMA for cross-domain consistency.

## Results

Across six cross-platform adaptation settings (vehicle, drone, quadruped as source/target), EventFly achieves an average of 71.25% Acc and 36.08% mIoU, outperforming the best baseline PLSR (68.72% Acc, 34.06% mIoU) and the source-only model (57.52% Acc, 20.37% mIoU) by 2.5% Acc / 2.0% mIoU and 13.7% Acc / 15.7% mIoU respectively. The drone domain is the hardest to adapt to, with EventFly reaching 69.17% Acc / 32.67% mIoU (vehicle→drone) versus 73.42% Acc / 40.05% mIoU (vehicle→quadruped). The supervised target upper bound sits at 81.90% Acc / 49.56% mIoU on average, indicating remaining headroom.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EventFly outperforms all five frame-based adaptation baselines (AdaptSegNet, DACS, MIC, PLSR, CBST) across all six cross-platform settings on both Acc and mIoU. The closest competitor is PLSR, which trails EventFly by 2.5% Acc and 2.0% mIoU on average; the gap is largest in the vehicle→drone setting (4.6% Acc, 3.0% mIoU) where the domain gap is most severe. AdaptSegNet, the weakest baseline, lags EventFly by 8.4% Acc and 7.9% mIoU on average, confirming that generic adversarial training is poorly suited to event data. In the domain-mixing ablation (Fig. 4), EventBlend also surpasses heuristic mixing (Cutout [19]) and prior domain-mixing methods (DACS [77], French et al. [23]), validating that EAP-guided blending is critical. The drone domain consistently yields the lowest scores across all methods, attributed to its unique high-altitude perspective and sparse ground details, making it the most challenging adaptation target.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes three well-established domain adaptation techniques—entropy minimization (Grandvalet & Bengio, 2004), adversarial feature alignment (AdaptSegNet-style GANs), and cross-domain data mixing (DACS, Cutout, Mixup)—but tailors them to the unique spatiotemporal structure of event camera voxel grids. Prior event-camera domain adaptation work (Ev-Transfer, ESS, HPL-ESS) focused on RGB-to-event transfer, while this paper addresses the orthogonal problem of platform-to-platform transfer within event data itself. The EAP component introduces a platform-specific activation prior that has no direct analogue in frame-based DA, leveraging the fact that different robot platforms produce structurally distinct event density patterns (e.g., ground-level vs. aerial activation).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The EXPo benchmark (~90k samples, 3 platforms, 11 classes) fills a critical gap: prior event camera datasets are overwhelmingly vehicle-centric (12 of 15 public datasets per the cited survey), making cross-platform evaluation impossible. The 77.1% average mIoU gain over source-only training quantifies how severe the cross-platform domain gap is, while the remaining gap to the supervised target upper bound (e.g., 32.67% vs. 42.90% mIoU for vehicle→drone) indicates substantial room for improvement. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond semantic segmentation to other dense tasks (depth, detection), whether it scales to more than three platforms, and whether the dual-discriminator overhead is justified in latency-sensitive robotic deployment.
