## Problem

Wildlife behavior monitoring via camera traps generates vast video data, but the field lacks publicly available, curated fieldwork datasets with dense individual-level annotations and hierarchical behavior structure. No existing benchmark addresses multi-view, long-term ecological event understanding, where multiple cameras capture the same scene over extended durations and the goal is to summarize species, activities, group sizes, and environmental conditions rather than per-frame recognition.

## Input / Output

For B1 (clip-level), inputs are 380×380 animal-centered video tracklets (16 frames sampled from 5-second windows), corresponding audio spectrograms, and reference-scene segmentation map clips aligned to the animal's position; outputs are species (5 classes), activity (11 classes), and up to two non-exclusive actions (19 classes, multi-label). For B2 (event-level), inputs are raw multi-view videos from up to three co-located cameras spanning 1 second to 12 minutes per event; outputs are multi-label species and activity sets, a 4-category group size, and a 4-class meteorological condition. The segmentation maps are single fixed-scene annotations per camera, not per-frame masks, and are cropped into tracklet-aligned clips to provide environmental context.

## Method

For B1, the authors adapt VideoMAE to jointly process video frames, audio spectrograms (tokenized following AudioMAE), and one-hot encoded segmentation map clips through a shared transformer, with four output heads for species, activity, and two action slots. For B2, they propose an offline token-merging strategy inspired by ToME that condenses frame patch tokens from a pretrained ViT-MAE encoder first spatially (reduction factor r) then temporally in fixed-duration blocks, so longer events yield proportionally more tokens. Three cosine positional embeddings are added to each condensed token: camera identity, elapsed time from event start, and source frame/patch identity to preserve spatio-temporal provenance. The condensed tokens are fed to a ViT-Base transformer with four classification heads, padded to a maximum context length (8192 tokens) with masked tokens for shorter events. The hierarchical annotation scheme (one activity + one or two actions per frame) enables joint prediction, and the multi-view, long-duration setting forces the model to integrate information across cameras and time to resolve individual counts.

## Results

On B1 (multimodal species and behavior recognition), the best configuration combining video and audio (V+A) achieves an overall mAP of 0.472 for joint task prediction, outperforming video-only (0.453) by +0.019 mAP. On B2 (multi-view long-term event understanding), the best model with ToME reduction factor r=11 and context length 8192 achieves an overall mAP of 0.498, with per-task mAPs of 0.439 (species), 0.450 (activities), 0.634 (meteorological conditions), and 0.593 (individual count). Joint task prediction consistently outperforms single-task prediction across both benchmarks, suggesting that the hierarchical and multi-objective structure aids learning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a dataset paper, so comparisons are internal (modality ablations and model configurations) rather than against external methods. For B1, adding audio to video improves overall mAP from 0.453 to 0.472 (+0.019), with the largest per-class gains on sound-associated behaviors like marking (+0.20 F1) and vocalizing (+0.09 F1). Adding segmentation maps to video actually degrades performance (0.453 → 0.394), though segmentation maps do help the audio-only model (+0.059 mAP), indicating they carry complementary scene information. For B2, increasing the token budget (r=11, context 8192) over the more compressed setting (r=14, context 4096) improves overall mAP from 0.476 to 0.498, with the largest gain on individual counting (0.478 → 0.593). Positional embeddings (camera ID, event time, source frame/patch) further boost individual counting by up to +0.109 mAP in joint prediction, confirming that spatio-temporal provenance information is critical for multi-view identity reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on VideoMAE as a pre-trained video backbone and adapts ToME's token merging algorithm from an online, spatial-only operation to a fully offline, spatio-temporal compression scheme for long-form multi-view video. In the wildlife monitoring literature, it departs from prior datasets (MammalNet, PanAf20k, LoTE, ChimpACT) in three key ways: introducing a hierarchical behavior taxonomy (activities composed of actions) rather than flat behavior labels, providing multi-modal inputs (video + audio + reference scene segmentation maps) for behavior recognition, and recording events from up to three synchronized camera viewpoints. The data processing pipeline leverages MegaDetector and ByteTrack for automated tracklet generation, following the standard camera-trap analysis workflow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MammAlps fills a notable gap as the first publicly available, curated fieldwork camera-trap dataset with hierarchical behavior annotations, multi-modal inputs, and multi-view coverage, bridging the domain gap between internet-scraped datasets and real-world monitoring data. The multi-view long-term event understanding benchmark (B2) is explicitly framed as the first of its kind, addressing a practical need for ecologists to summarize extended ecological events. Key limitations include the narrow species set (5 species, one national park, ~6 weeks), only 86 test events for B2, and the modest absolute performance (mAP ~0.50) suggesting the tasks remain far from solved. Open questions include how to better exploit the hierarchical activity-action structure in model architecture, how to handle temporal drift across unsynchronized cameras, and whether the findings generalize beyond the European Alps.
