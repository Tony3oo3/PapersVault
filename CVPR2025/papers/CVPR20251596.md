## Problem

Chain-of-Thought reasoning strategies—test-time verification with reward models and preference alignment—have proven effective for LLM and LMM understanding tasks, but whether they can be adapted to autoregressive image generation remains unexplored. The paper asks whether step-by-step decoding of images can be verified and reinforced using the same techniques that improve multi-step reasoning in language models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251596/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a generated RGB image via autoregressive token-by-token decoding (using Show-o as the base model). During inference, N diverse decoding paths are sampled at high temperature, and reward models evaluate intermediate partial images at various decoding steps. The reward model (PARM) consumes partially decoded images alongside the text prompt and outputs binary judgments (clarity, potential) plus a final selection score, ultimately returning a single best-of-N' image.

## Method

The paper systematically adapts three CoT reasoning strategies to autoregressive image generation: (1) test-time verification via best-of-N selection using Outcome or Process Reward Models fine-tuned on curated ranking data, (2) DPO preference alignment with iterative re-annotation to reinforce the decoding policy, and (3) their combination where the reward model guides DPO training and also performs test-time selection. The key architectural contribution is PARM, a specialized reward model that overcomes the limitations of both ORM (no step-wise granularity) and PRM (early images too blurry, late images too similar) through three progressive tasks: a clarity judgment gate that skips unevaluable early steps, a potential assessment that prunes paths unlikely to yield high-quality finals, and a final best-of-N' selection among surviving paths. PARM is trained on 400K curated instances split across the three tasks, and when combined with iterative DPO for both reward guidance and test-time verification, it improves the Show-o baseline by +24% on GenEval, surpassing Stable Diffusion 3 by +15%.

## Results

On the GenEval benchmark, the best configuration (Show-o + PARM + iterative DPO with PARM reward guidance) achieves 77% overall score, improving the Show-o baseline (53%) by +24% and surpassing Stable Diffusion 3 (62%) by +15%. The individual strategies contribute incrementally: fine-tuned ORM verification yields 63%, iterative DPO yields 65%, and PARM alone yields 67%. The full integration of PARM with iterative DPO in both post-training and test-time achieves the highest score of 77%, with particularly strong gains on Position (0.11→0.66) and Attribute Binding (0.28→0.64).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method (Show-o + PARM + iterative DPO with PARM guidance) achieves 77% on GenEval, outperforming all compared baselines. Against the strongest diffusion baseline, Stable Diffusion 3 (62%), it leads by +15%, with particularly large margins on Position (+32 pts: 0.66 vs 0.34) and Attribute Binding (+28 pts: 0.64 vs 0.36). Compared to SDXL (55%), DALL-E 2 (52%), and SD v2.1 (50%), the gains are +22%, +25%, and +27% respectively. Among autoregressive baselines, the method surpasses LlamaGen (32%) by +45%, Chameleon (39%) by +38%, LWM (47%) by +30%, and SEED-X (49%) by +28%. Even the Show-o baseline without any reasoning strategies (53%) is improved by +24%, confirming that the CoT-inspired verification and reinforcement pipeline is the primary source of the performance gain rather than the base model architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transfers the CoT reasoning toolkit from LLM/LMM understanding (test-time verification with reward models, DPO preference alignment, iterative refinement) to autoregressive image generation, using Show-o as the base model and LLaVA-OneVision as the reward model backbone. It departs from prior work on process reward models (PRM) by identifying a fundamental mismatch: the step-by-step verification that succeeds in math reasoning fails for image generation because early-stage images are too blurry and late-stage images converge, motivating the novel PARM design with adaptive clarity judgment and potential assessment. The iterative DPO framework follows Pang et al.'s approach but is the first application of this loop to discrete autoregressive image decoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study establishing that CoT reasoning strategies can be adapted to autoregressive image generation, opening a new research direction at the intersection of reasoning and generation. The PARM design—adaptively deciding *when* to evaluate rather than evaluating every step—offers a principled solution to the reward model problem in step-by-step generation that could generalize to other autoregressive tasks (video, 3D). However, the evaluation is confined to GenEval (a narrow object-composition benchmark) and a single base model (Show-o), leaving open whether these findings hold for more complex compositional scenes, continuous diffusion models, or larger-scale autoregressive generators. The reliance on LLaVA-OneVision as the reward model also raises questions about the ceiling of this approach.
