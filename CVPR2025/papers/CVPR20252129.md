## Problem

Existing set-embeddings-based vision-language alignment methods impose diversity constraints on entire sub-representation spaces, ignoring the finding that specific semantics are primarily expressed by a small subset of local dimensions within each subspace. Additionally, the set-embeddings paradigm inherently requires storing and computing k separate embeddings per sample at inference, sacrificing computational and storage efficiency compared to single-embedding methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252129/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text sentence as input, encoding each into k=3 sub-embeddings of dimension d (1024 for ResNet/Faster R-CNN backbones, 512 for Swin/ViT backbones) via shared visual extractors and independent textual extractors. At training, it computes a set-level similarity across all k subspaces; at inference, it produces a single hybrid embedding vector per modality (O(d) storage) by linearly combining key dimensions across subspaces, enabling efficient large-scale retrieval without storing k separate vectors per sample.

## Method

DH-Set introduces three key mechanisms. First, an adaptive sparse dimensional bottleneck inserts a learnable sparse matrix W^i between cross-modal embeddings and the similarity output in each subspace, where the top-m sparse vectors (m=90) identify the local dimensions most important for expressing a given semantic. Second, a fine-grained diversity constraint computes KL divergence between dimension-importance probability vectors (derived from the bottleneck activations) across different subspaces, replacing coarse whole-subspace diversity penalties. Third, by sharing visual embedding parameters across all subspaces, the set similarity satisfies the associative law, allowing all k text embeddings to be collapsed into a single hybrid embedding ū = Σᵢ(uᵢ ⊙ Δᵢ) at inference, reducing complexity from O(kd) to O(d) while preserving training-time set similarity accuracy. A hardest-triplet discriminability loss is applied both across all subspaces jointly and within each individual subspace to ensure aligned pairs are distinguished from misaligned ones.

## Results

On the strongest backbone (Swin-384+BERT), DH-Set achieves rSum of 559.4 (Flickr30K) and 483.8 (MS-COCO 5K), outperforming the best prior method LAPS (545.3 / 470.1) by 2.6% and 2.8% respectively. Across all backbones, DH-Set delivers 2.3%–14.7% relative rSum improvements over state-of-the-art methods. The largest gains appear on the ResNet152+BiGRU backbone, where DH-Set (rSum 456.5 on Flickr30K) surpasses the previous best SEB method SET (442.6) by 3.1%. The hybrid inference reduces both computational and storage complexity from O(kd) to O(d) while maintaining equivalent accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DH-Set consistently outperforms all 21 compared baselines across every backbone and dataset. Against the closest set-embeddings competitor SET [21], DH-Set gains 3.1% rSum on Flickr30K (ResNet152) and 2.6% on Flickr30K (Swin-384), confirming that fine-grained local-dimension diversity constraints are more effective than whole-subspace constraints. Against the strongest one-embedding method LAPS [12], DH-Set achieves 559.4 vs 545.3 rSum on Flickr30K (Swin-384) and 483.8 vs 470.1 on MS-COCO 5K, a 2.6–2.8% margin. On the Faster R-CNN+BERT backbone, DH-Set (rSum 533.3) surpasses NUIF-d [55] (529.4), DIAS [53] (524.5), and CHAN [40] (518.5). On VLP backbones, DH-Set improves over CLIP zero-shot [43] by 11.6 rSum points on Flickr30K (ViT-large-384) and slightly edges BLIP-2 [27] (97.0 vs 96.9 I2T R@1 on Flickr30K), demonstrating plug-and-play compatibility with pretrained encoders.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the set-embeddings-based (SEB) paradigm for vision-language alignment, most directly building on SET (Kim et al., CVPR 2023), which encodes each input as k diverse sub-embeddings and constrains whole-subspace diversity. DH-Set departs from this in two key ways: it replaces coarse whole-subspace diversity constraints with a fine-grained KL-divergence constraint on key local dimensions identified via a learnable sparse bottleneck, and it introduces a hybrid inference mechanism that collapses k embeddings into a single vector through visual sharing and linear transformation, reducing inference complexity from O(kd) to O(d). The discriminative loss also extends prior work by adding per-subspace hardest-triplet constraints alongside the all-subspace constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(d) inference complexity is practically significant for large-scale retrieval systems where storing and computing k embeddings per sample is prohibitive, and the method achieves this without sacrificing accuracy relative to full set-similarity training. The finding that visual semantics already carry distributed diversity from input variance (making visual sharing viable while textual sharing is not) offers a useful insight into the asymmetry of cross-modal ambiguity. Open questions include whether the sparse bottleneck's dimension-dissecting property generalizes to other set-based representation learning tasks beyond retrieval, and whether the k=3, m=90 hyperparameter regime transfers across substantially different feature dimensions or modalities.
