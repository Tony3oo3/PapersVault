## Problem

Existing Contrastive Decoding (CD) methods for LVLM hallucination mitigation apply a single fixed perturbation strategy uniformly across all samples and generative steps. The paper demonstrates through systematic experiments that hallucination causes are hybrid—different samples and even different tokens within the same response are driven by distinct factors (language priors, visual information loss, attention bias)—making any one-size-fits-all CD strategy sub-optimal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252490/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a textual instruction (e.g., 'Please describe this image in detail' for generative tasks, or a yes/no question for discriminative tasks) and produces a free-form text description or a one-word answer. At each autoregressive step, the Octopus module outputs a discrete action selecting which CD strategy (or none) to apply, yielding a per-token contrastive decoding workflow. The underlying LVLM (LLaVA-1.5-7B or InstructBLIP) remains entirely frozen; only the lightweight Octopus decision module is trained.

## Method

Octopus introduces a learnable 'eye' token that is concatenated with the LVLM's hidden states at each generative step and processed by a small transformer block, producing a decision vector that an MLP maps to one of k candidate CD strategies (VCD, M3ID, AVISC, or a null action). Each selected strategy acts as a 'tentacle' performing its specific contrastive logit computation (e.g., Gaussian-noise-masked image for VCD, masked text with independent visual input for M3ID, blind-token visual input for AVISC). Training uses DPO with preference data constructed by randomly sampling 10 action sequences per image and splitting them into positive/negative pairs based on CHAIR score, so the model learns which workflow best suppresses hallucination without explicit labels. The framework is extensible: new CD strategies can be added as additional tentacles without architectural changes, and the optimization can be swapped for PPO or Monte-Carlo sampling.

## Results

On the generative task with LLaVA-1.5-7B, Octopus achieves CHAIR 4.8 (vs. 8.0 baseline, ~40% reduction), Cover 49.2, Hal 23.4, and Cog 1.2 on AMBER, outperforming all single-strategy CD methods (VCD: 6.7, M3ID: 6.0, AVISC: 6.3). On MMHalBench, the GPT-4V score improves from 1.59 (base) to 2.61, and on Object-HalBench, CHAIRs drops from 25.0 to 20.8. For the discriminative task, Octopus boosts AMBER accuracy from 67.00 to 76.70 and POPE-ALL accuracy from 82.04 to 85.79, with F1 gains of 11.6 and 3.02 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Octopus outperforms all three single-strategy CD baselines (VCD, M3ID, AVISC) across every metric on both LLaVA-1.5-7B and InstructBLIP. On AMBER CHAIR, Octopus (4.8) beats M3ID (6.0), AVISC (6.3), and VCD (6.7) by 1.2–1.9 points; on MMHalBench score it leads by 0.42–0.65 over the best single CD method. Against retraining-based methods listed as referenced results (HACL, POVID, EOS, HA-DPO, HALVA), Octopus still achieves superior CHAIR (4.8 vs. 5.1–6.7) and MMHalBench score (2.61 vs. 1.97–2.25). On the discriminative task, Octopus surpasses AVISC (the strongest single CD baseline) by 6.0 accuracy points on AMBER (76.70 vs. 70.70) and 2.40 on POPE-ALL (85.79 vs. 83.39). LCD and ICD, which only report partial results, show substantially worse Object-HalBench CHAIRs (61.0 and 47.4 vs. Octopus's 20.8), indicating they are less effective for this model. OPERA, which reports only MMHalBench and Object-HalBench, is outperformed by Octopus on both (2.61 vs. 2.15 score; 20.8 vs. 45.1 CHAIRs).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Contrastive Decoding (CD) line of work for LVLM hallucination mitigation, building directly on three specific CD strategies—VCD (visual noise masking), M3ID (visual information grounding), and AVISC (attention bias correction)—as its action space. The key conceptual departure from prior CD methods is the rejection of the 'one-size-fits-all' paradigm: rather than applying a single fixed perturbation uniformly, Octopus introduces a lightweight transformer-based decision module that dynamically selects which CD strategy (or none) to apply at each generative step. The optimization leverages DPO (Rafailov et al.) to train this selection policy without retraining the underlying LVLM, positioning the work as a plug-and-play post-hoc correction layer rather than a retraining-based approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical diagnosis that hallucination causes are hybrid and vary across tokens within a single sentence provides a useful conceptual framework for understanding why no single CD method dominates. The modular 'tentacle' design offers practical extensibility—new CD strategies can be added without architectural changes—which is valuable as the CD literature continues to grow. However, the approach is limited to three CD strategies and two 7B models, and the DPO training pipeline (generating 10 random action sequences per sample) introduces non-trivial computational overhead. Open questions include whether the dynamic selection generalizes to fundamentally new hallucination types not covered by existing tentacles, and whether the framework scales to larger models or longer generation sequences where the combinatorial action space grows.
