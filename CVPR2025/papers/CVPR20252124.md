## Problem

Existing building wireframe reconstruction methods rely on either vertex detection (suffering from error accumulation across stages) or fixed-query edge regression (lacking distributional flexibility). The paper identifies that diffusion models, which excel at distribution regression in 2D vision tasks, have not been explored for 3D wireframe reconstruction, and proposes a novel noise-to-wireframe generative paradigm to fill this gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252124/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an input point cloud P ∈ R^{N×7} containing 3D coordinates, RGB values, and reflection intensity per point, and produces a set of M parameterized edges W ∈ R^{M×6}, where each edge is encoded as a 6D vector of midpoint coordinates (x_c, y_c, z_c) and offset-to-endpoint (Δx_c, Δy_c, Δz_c). A key design choice is the midpoint-offset parameterization over endpoint representation, which decouples localization from direction/length. At inference, the number of output edges N_eval can differ from the training count N_train without retraining, enabling dynamic adaptation to buildings of varying complexity.

## Method

EdgeDiff reformulates wireframe reconstruction as a conditional diffusion process: ground truth edges are progressively noised into Gaussian during training, and a neural network f_θ learns to reverse this process conditioned on point cloud features. The architecture comprises three components: (1) a point transformer encoder that extracts global structural features F_enc, (2) an edge attention generation module (lightweight MLP) that predicts point-wise attention weights A ∈ (0,1) highlighting edge-relevant points, supervised by GT-derived weights from Algorithm 1, and (3) stacked edge-aware denoising layers where self-attention processes noisy edge queries and cross-attention computes K,V as weighted combinations of F_enc and A to guide denoising toward edge-relevant features. Inference uses DDIM sampling with flexible single- or multi-step configurations, starting from random Gaussian noise and iteratively refining to the final wireframe. The training loss combines geometric losses from PBWR with an MSE loss for the edge attention prediction, and a signal scaling factor of 2.0 is applied to GT wireframes to optimize the signal-to-noise ratio for edge denoising.

## Results

On the Entry-level subset of Building3D, EdgeDiff achieves CF1=0.91 and EF1=0.88, with edge precision of 0.96 and corner precision of 0.99, outperforming the prior state-of-the-art edge regression method PBWR (EF1=0.86, EP=0.91). On the more challenging Tallinn subset, EdgeDiff attains CF1=0.84 and EF1=0.79, surpassing PBWR (CF1=0.80, EF1=0.76) by 4% and 3% respectively. Vertex detection methods degrade severely on Tallinn, with Point2Roof dropping to ER=0.08 and EF1=0.14, while EdgeDiff maintains robust performance (ER=0.70, EF1=0.79).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EdgeDiff consistently outperforms the strongest edge regression baseline PBWR [14] on both Building3D subsets: on Entry-level it gains +0.02 in EP (0.96 vs 0.91) and +0.02 in EF1 (0.88 vs 0.86), while on Tallinn it improves CF1 by 4 points (0.84 vs 0.80) and EF1 by 3 points (0.79 vs 0.76). Against vertex detection methods, the advantage is overwhelming—Point2Roof [23] achieves only EF1=0.38 on Entry-level and collapses to EF1=0.14 on Tallinn, whereas EdgeDiff maintains EF1 above 0.79 in both settings. PC2WF [26] performs worst among all deep learning baselines, with EF1 of 0.04 (Entry-level) and 0.01 (Tallinn), confirming the limitations of the two-stage vertex-then-edge pipeline. In qualitative comparisons, EdgeDiff produces topologically coherent wireframes, while EC-Net [51] and PIE-Net [46] extract only partial edge points, and traditional mesh methods (PolyFit [34], Topology Aware [1], 2.5D Dual Contouring [54]) introduce redundant facets that complicate building structure. EdgeDiff ties PBWR on ACO (0.22 m) and CR (Entry-level) but leads on all other metrics, establishing the diffusion-based noise-to-wireframe paradigm as a clear improvement over direct edge regression.

## Relation to Prior Work

EdgeDiff departs from the two dominant wireframe reconstruction paradigms—vertex detection (PC2WF, Point2Roof) and direct edge regression (PBWR, NERVE)—by recasting the problem as a conditional diffusion generative process, where noisy edge parameters are iteratively denoised into a wireframe. It builds directly on DDIM for the inference sampling procedure and borrows geometric loss terms and post-processing (NMS, DBSCAN) from PBWR. The edge attention generation module draws on point-cloud edge detection literature (Jiang et al., Wu et al.) to provide geometric conditioning, while the point encoder follows standard point-transformer architectures. Conceptually, the paper extends the diffusion-for-regression line (DiffusionDet, DDP, DiffusionTrack) from 2D detection/dense prediction into structured 3D geometric output.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The noise-to-wireframe paradigm offers a practical advantage over fixed-query regression: dynamic inference with arbitrary edge counts without retraining, which is valuable for buildings of varying complexity. However, the method still relies on post-processing (NMS, DBSCAN) for topological completeness, and evaluation is limited to a single dataset (Building3D), leaving open questions about generalization to other building styles or sensor modalities. The authors note future work on eliminating post-processing entirely, and the modest performance gains over PBWR (e.g., EF1 0.88 vs. 0.86 on Entry-level) suggest the diffusion formulation's full potential may not yet be realized. Whether diffusion-based generative modeling provides a fundamentally better inductive bias for structured 3D outputs—beyond what a well-tuned transformer regression head achieves—remains an open question.
