## Problem

Existing meteorological downscaling methods either rely on spatial interpolation that loses fine-scale detail or directly model the low-to-high resolution mapping without exploiting the coupling between ERA5 reanalysis data and the satellite observations from which it is derived. This omission introduces systematic biases and detail loss when generating maps at small scales (e.g., 6.25 km), particularly for variables like temperature and sea-level pressure that are strongly influenced by satellite brightness temperature measurements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252525/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three inputs: low-resolution ERA5 maps (720×1440, 4 channels: U10, V10, T2M, MSL at 25 km scale), GridSat satellite observation maps (2000×5143, 3 channels: infrared window brightness temperature, VZA-adjusted brightness temperature, and integrated water vapor path), and station-level observations from the Weather5k dataset (5,672 global stations). The output is a high-resolution ERA5 map at 6.25 km (or arbitrary resolution via the patch-based strategy) containing the same four meteorological variables. Notably, the GridSat and ERA5 inputs operate at different native resolutions, and the method supports generating maps at any target resolution by adjusting the patch stride and convolutional kernel.

## Method

SGD is a conditional DDPM pre-trained on ERA5 maps with GridSat satellite observations injected via a pre-trained convolutional encoder and a cross-attention fusion module, so the denoising UNet learns to generate atmospheric states consistent with actual satellite-derived conditions. During sampling, rather than directly modeling the downscaling mapping, SGD inverts the process: an optimizable 9×9 convolutional kernel up-scales the instantaneous clean estimate x̃₀ at each reverse step, and a distance function (MSE against the low-res ERA5 map, optionally augmented with MAE against station observations) measures the discrepancy. Gradients of this distance function simultaneously update the sampling mean (guiding the generated map toward the low-res target) and the convolutional kernel parameters (refining the simulated upscaling operator in real time). A patch-based strategy partitions the high-resolution output into overlapping sub-regions, computing per-patch gradients that are averaged to support arbitrary target resolutions beyond the pre-trained model's native scale.

## Results

On station-level downscaling evaluation (first 6 months of 2023), SGD achieves the best MSE and MAE across U10, T2M, and MSL variables, with particularly large gains on T2M (MSE 187.69 vs. 216.27 for ERA5 1°) and MSL (MSE 374.39 vs. 470.06 for ERA5 1°). On V10, SGD is competitive (MSE 39.82, MAE 5.05) but does not surpass simple bilinear/bicubic interpolation or SwinRDM. Overall, SGD outperforms all diffusion-based baselines (DGP, GDP, DDNM) and reference-based SR methods (Ref-SR, C2-Matching, HyperDS) on every variable and metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGD achieves the best MSE and MAE on U10, T2M, and MSL, outperforming all 10 baselines. The largest relative improvements are on T2M (13.2% MSE reduction over ERA5 1°) and MSL (20.4% MSE reduction over ERA5 1°), attributed to the GridSat satellite conditioning. Among diffusion-based methods, DGP and GDP perform worst (U10 MSE >94), while DDNM is closer but still trails SGD on T2M and MSL. On V10, SGD (MSE 39.82) is slightly behind bicubic interpolation (37.88) and SwinRDM (38.51), indicating that wind components are harder to improve via satellite conditioning. HyperDS [27], the closest meteorological-specific baseline, is outperformed by SGD on all four variables in both MSE and MAE, with the gap most pronounced on MSL (374.39 vs. 384.72 MSE).

## Relation to Prior Work

SGD builds on the conditional diffusion model (DDPM) framework, extending it from class/text conditioning to satellite-observation conditioning via a cross-attention mechanism that fuses GridSat features with ERA5 maps. It departs from prior meteorological downscaling work (SwinRDM, GDP, DDNM, HyperDS) by explicitly leveraging the physical coupling between satellite brightness temperature and reanalysis data, rather than modeling the LR-to-HR mapping in isolation. The zero-shot guided sampling with optimizable convolutional kernels draws on the generative diffusion prior literature (GDP, DDNM) but repurposes the gradient-guidance mechanism for resolution transformation rather than image restoration, and introduces a patch-based strategy to handle arbitrary output resolutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: existing downscaling methods ignore the satellite observations that physically constrain ERA5, leading to systematic biases at fine scales. By conditioning on GridSat and incorporating station-level observations during sampling, SGD achieves consistent (if moderate) improvements across all four tested variables, with the largest gains in T2M and MSL. Limitations include evaluation on only four surface variables, a single 4× resolution target (25 km → 6.25 km), and a relatively short test period (6 months). Open questions include whether the satellite-conditioning benefit persists for upper-atmosphere variables, whether the optimizable kernel approach scales to higher resolution ratios, and how the method performs in data-sparse regions where station guidance is unavailable.
