## Problem

Existing RAFT-like event-based optical flow methods rely on temporally dense 4D cost volumes for pixel matching, incurring O(TN²C) computational complexity that limits scalability to higher resolutions where accuracy gains are known to exist. The paper identifies that event streams typically involve small displacements over short time windows, making global 2D searches within dense cost volumes redundant, and asks whether temporally dense feature differences between adjacent event frames can complement a low-resolution cost volume to enable efficient high-resolution flow estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250470/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two consecutive event sequences (E_{t-dt→t} as reference and E_{t→t+1} as target), each encoded as a voxel grid with B temporal bins via polarity-weighted temporal binning. The target sequence is further divided into g=5 sub-windows of size dt, yielding g+1 feature maps at both 1/4 and 1/8 resolutions. The output is a dense per-pixel optical flow field at 1/4 resolution (twice the 1/8 resolution used by most RAFT-like event-based methods), refined over K=6 iterative GRU updates.

## Method

EDCFlow iteratively fuses two complementary motion representations: (1) correlation motion features from a 1/8-resolution all-pairs cost volume (looked up, upsampled to 1/4, and encoded via convolutions) providing robust long-range correspondences, and (2) difference motion features computed at 1/4 resolution by warping consecutive-frame features with the current flow and taking multi-scale temporal differences at sampling strides s∈{1,2,5}, aggregated with depth-wise separable 3D convolutions and a global attention mechanism. A channel attention (SE) module adaptively fuses the two motion features, which are then decoded by a GRU to produce residual flow updates. The key efficiency gain comes from replacing expensive high-resolution cost volumes (O(TN²C)) with cheap feature differences (O(TNC)) at 1/4 resolution while retaining a single low-resolution cost volume for global matching. The architecture is designed as a plug-and-play refinement module that can be cascaded after existing RAFT-like networks to improve flow details at motion boundaries with minimal additional parameters.

## Results

On DSEC, EDCFlow achieves the best EPE of 0.72 (tied with IDNet-4), the lowest angular error of 2.65°, and the best 1PE/2PE of 10.0%/3.6%, while using only 2.5M parameters and 247G MACs with a 39 ms runtime on an RTX 4090. This represents a 9% EPE improvement over E-RAFT (0.79) and a 3% improvement over TMA (0.74), at roughly half the MACs of TMA (344G) and one-fifth the MACs of IDNet-4 (1200G). On MVSEC at dt=4, EDCFlow achieves SOTA EPE of 0.67 on outdoor_day1 and 2.59 on indoor_flying1, outperforming all RAFT-like baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EDCFlow outperforms all RAFT-like event-based methods (E-RAFT, MultiFlow, TMA, E-FlowFormer) on every DSEC metric, achieving 9% lower EPE than E-RAFT (0.72 vs. 0.79) and 3% lower than TMA (0.72 vs. 0.74), while also reducing MACs by 4% vs. E-RAFT and 28% vs. TMA. Against IDNet-4, which matches EDCFlow's EPE of 0.72, EDCFlow achieves a lower AE (2.65° vs. 2.72°) and uses only 247G MACs versus IDNet-4's 1200G (a 4.9× reduction) with a 68% faster runtime (39 ms vs. 120 ms). On MVSEC at dt=4, EDCFlow achieves the best EPE across all four sequences (e.g., 0.67 on outdoor_day1 vs. 0.70 for TMA and 0.72 for E-RAFT), and at dt=1 it matches DCEIFlow on outdoor_day1 (0.23 EPE) while surpassing E-RAFT and TMA on indoor sequences. In the sim-to-real generalization test (trained on Blinkflow, tested on DSEC), EDCFlow shows the smallest accuracy drop (−0.53 EPE) compared to E-RAFT (−0.61), TMA (−0.72), IDNet-4 (−0.77), and E-FlowFormer (−0.57), confirming superior transferability. As a plug-and-play refinement module, EDCFlow improves TMA from 0.76 to 0.72 EPE and E-RAFT from 0.80 to 0.77 EPE at 1/4 resolution, outperforming simply doubling iterations at 1/8 resolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

EDCFlow extends the RAFT iterative refinement paradigm to the event domain, building directly on E-RAFT's event-based adaptation and TMA's feature extraction architecture. The key conceptual departure is from the cost-volume-centric approach of E-RAFT, TMA, and MultiFlow, which encode intermediate motion via temporally dense 4D cost volumes at O(TN²C) complexity. Instead, EDCFlow replaces high-resolution cost volumes with O(TNC) temporally dense feature differences between adjacent warped frames, retaining only a low-resolution (1/8) cost volume for long-range correspondences. This shifts the design philosophy from 'expensive global matching everywhere' to 'cheap local differences at high resolution + cheap global matching at low resolution,' a complementarity argument that is well-motivated but not entirely new in the broader optical flow literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play refinement capability—cascading EDCFlow after any RAFT-like event-based network to upgrade from 1/8 to 1/4 resolution with marginal overhead—offers a practical path for existing methods to improve accuracy without architectural redesign. The sim-to-real generalization result (Blinkflow-trained model showing the smallest accuracy drop on DSEC) suggests the feature-difference representation may be more transferable than cost-volume-based ones, potentially reducing reliance on large annotated event datasets. Open questions include whether the approach extends to full-resolution estimation, how it behaves with very sparse or very dense event streams, and whether the multi-scale difference strategy generalizes to other event-based tasks beyond flow.
