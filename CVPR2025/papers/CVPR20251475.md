## Problem

Existing Riemannian Batch Normalization (RBN) methods on the SPD manifold rely on metrics (AIM, LEM, LCM) with quadratic dependence on the base point, which causes numerical instability when processing ill-conditioned SPD matrices (ICSM) that are prevalent in real covariance data. No prior RBN method exploited the Bures-Wasserstein Metric (BWM), whose linear dependence on the base point offers superior conditioning, nor did any incorporate the Generalized BWM (GBWM) with its learnable SPD parameter to adapt to evolving batch geometry.

## Input / Output

The GBWBN layer consumes a batch of N d×d SPD covariance matrices (derived from EEG channel covariance or flattened 3D skeleton joint coordinates) and outputs a normalized batch of SPD matrices ready for downstream BiMap classification layers. During training, normalization statistics (Fréchet mean and variance) are computed per-batch via one step of fixed-point iteration; during inference, running statistics are used. The layer introduces three learnable parameters: an SPD matrix M (metric parameter), a scalar power θ (metric deformation), and a target SPD matrix G (bias point), plus a scalar scale s.

## Method

GBWBN performs Riemannian batch normalization under the power-deformed Generalized Bures-Wasserstein Metric (θ-GBWM), which interpolates between GBWM (θ=1) and a LEM-like metric (θ→0). The key computational insight (Theorem 3.4) is that GBWBN reduces to standard BWM-based RBN via a Riemannian isometry: data is mapped into BWM space via f⁻¹(X) = M^{-1/2} X^{θ} M^{-1/2}, normalized using BWM operators (Fréchet mean via fixed-point iteration, parallel transport for centering and biasing, tangent-space scaling), then mapped back. The BWM's linear dependence on the base point (vs. AIM's quadratic dependence) ensures stable computation on ill-conditioned matrices, while the learnable M and θ allow the normalization geometry to adapt to batch-specific structure. The method is plug-and-play, requiring only replacement of the existing RBN layer in SPDNet or RResNet backbones.

## Results

On the MAMEM-SSVEP-II EEG classification task, SPDNet-GBWBN (θ=1) achieves 65.47±3.33% accuracy, outperforming the vanilla SPDNet backbone by 3.17% and the best Euclidean baseline (SCCNet, 62.11%) by 3.36%. On skeleton-based action recognition, RResNet-GBWBN with power deformation (θ=0.5) reaches 69.10±0.83% on HDM05 and 59.72±0.31% on NTU RGB+D, improving over the vanilla RResNet by 8.01% and 7.18% respectively. The method also surpasses the strongest prior RBN baseline (LieBN) by 5.32% on MAMEM-SSVEP-II and by 2.67%/4.98% on HDM05/NTU RGB+D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GBWBN outperforms all 13 baselines across both tasks. On MAMEM-SSVEP-II, SPDNet-GBWBN (65.47%) beats the vanilla SPDNet (62.30%) by 3.17%, the AIM-based RBN of Brooks et al. (62.76%) by 2.71%, the variance-controlled BN of Kobler et al. (60.60%) by 4.87%, ManifoldNorm (62.08%) by 3.39%, and LieBN (60.15%) by 5.32%. Among Euclidean EEG baselines, the closest competitor is SCCNet (62.11%), still 3.36% below GBWBN, while TCNet-Fusion (45.00%) and FBCNet (53.09%) trail by over 12%. On HDM05, RResNet-GBWBN (θ=0.5) achieves 69.10%, surpassing LieBN (66.43%) by 2.67% and the vanilla RResNet (61.09%) by 8.01%; on NTU RGB+D it reaches 59.72% versus LieBN's 54.74% (+4.98%) and RResNet's 52.54% (+7.18%). The power-deformed variant (θ=0.5) consistently outperforms the un-deformed (θ=1) on HDM05 (69.10% vs. 62.01%), confirming the benefit of metric nonlinearity, though on NTU RGB+D the gap is smaller (59.72% vs. 59.45%). Training time is modestly higher (8.21 s/epoch vs. 3.6–6.7 s for other RBNs on HDM05) due to additional SVD operations, but the accuracy gains substantially outweigh this cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Riemannian Batch Normalization (RBN) line initiated by Brooks et al. (NeurIPS 2019) and subsequent works (Kobler et al., Chakraborty, Chen et al.), all of which operate under the Affine Invariant Metric (AIM) or Log-Euclidean Metric (LEM). The key departure is a geometric switch: the authors replace AIM with the Bures-Wasserstein Metric (BWM) and its Generalized BWM (GBWM) variant, motivated by BWM's linear (vs. AIM's quadratic) dependence on SPD matrices, which better handles ill-conditioned inputs. The learnable SPD parameter M in GBWM and the power-deformed metric (θ-GBWM) are borrowed from Han et al. (GSI 2023) and Thanwerdas & Pennec (2022) respectively, and the isometry between BWM and GBWM (Han et al., 2021) is leveraged to derive GBWBN directly from the simpler BWM-based RBN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: existing RBN methods degrade on ill-conditioned SPD matrices, which are pervasive in real covariance-based features (100% of samples on HDM05 and NTU RGB+D have κ > 10³). By grounding normalization in BWM/GBWM geometry with a learnable metric parameter and power deformation, the method offers a principled and flexible countermeasure. Limitations include evaluation on only three datasets and two backbone architectures (SPDNet, RResNet), slightly higher training cost due to additional SVD operations, and the fact that the parallel transport under BWM requires commuting matrices, restricting applicability. Open questions include whether the learnable M converges to interpretable geometric parameters, whether the approach extends to other Riemannian manifolds, and whether the power-deformation benefit persists at larger network depths.
