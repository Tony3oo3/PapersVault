## Problem

No prior work has explored 3D singing facial animation driven by mixed audio from multiple singers with background music. Existing audio-driven animation methods are designed for single-speaker speech or solo singing, ignoring the inter-singer emotional interaction and rhythmic synchronization that are essential for realistic chorus performance. The absence of a dedicated 3D multi-singer singing dataset further compounds this gap.

## Input / Output

The method takes a mixed choral audio track containing two (or more) singer vocals plus background music, which is pre-separated via Spleeter (vocal/BGM split) and MossFormer2 (per-singer split). For each singer, the output is a per-frame 56-dimensional FLAME coefficient vector (53 expression + 3 pose) forming a continuous 3D head animation sequence at 30 FPS. The framework is formulated for two singers but extends to three or more by mixing all non-host partner vocals into a single track.

## Method

PaChorus employs a two-branch architecture trained end-to-end: a Host Animator (Wav2Vec2.0 encoder + transformer decoder with a FLAME lip mask) predicts all 56 FLAME coefficients from the host's own vocal, while a Partner-aware Motion Prior branch (a conditional VAE built on transformer layers) predicts only the 3 pose coefficients, conditioned on the partner's vocal and background music embeddings. The key design insight is that the host vocal drives the full animation, whereas partner vocals and BGM serve solely as emotional/rhythmic priors influencing head movement. The CVAE encodes head poses into a latent Gaussian space and decodes residual poses relative to the initial frame for temporal stability. Training combines MSE loss on coefficients, KL-divergence regularization on the latent space, and a velocity loss for temporal consistency, with MossFormer2 and Wav2Vec TCN parameters frozen.

## Results

On the ChorusHead dataset, PaChorus achieves LVE 1.1922, FVE 1.3083, and FDD 1.2314, outperforming all finetuned baselines on every metric. The closest competitor, DiffSpeaker, records LVE 1.5833 and FDD 2.2121, while SelfTalk achieves the best FDD among baselines at 1.9458—still 37% higher than PaChorus. In a user study with 30 participants, PaChorus wins 78.6–93.3% of pairwise comparisons against the five baselines on lip-sync accuracy and head-motion naturalness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PaChorus outperforms all five baselines on all three metrics (LVE, FVE, FDD) when both are finetuned on ChorusHead. The largest margin is on FDD: PaChorus (1.2314) beats the best baseline SelfTalk (1.9458) by 37%, indicating substantially better upper-face dynamic fidelity. On LVE, PaChorus (1.1922) improves over the closest competitor DiffSpeaker (1.5833) by 25%. Pretrained (unfinetuned) models perform markedly worse, confirming a domain gap between speech and singing that fine-tuning alone cannot fully close. In the user study, PaChorus achieves the highest win rate against Imitator (93.3%) and the lowest against DiffSpeaker (78.6%), suggesting DiffSpeaker is the strongest single-singer baseline but still lacks the partner-aware interaction modeling that PaChorus provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the well-established line of speech-driven 3D facial animation (FaceFormer, CodeTalker, SelfTalk, Imitator) and the nascent song-driven solo animation work (VOCAL, Song2Face, MusicFace, SingingHead) by introducing multi-singer chorus animation as a distinct problem. Rather than extending a single-singer pipeline to multiple independent tracks, PaChorus explicitly models inter-singer interaction through a CVAE-based partner-aware motion prior conditioned on both the partner's vocal and background music, a formulation absent from all prior methods. The Host Animator branch follows standard Wav2Vec + Transformer practice for lip/face generation, while the VAE-based residual pose prediction draws on stochastic generation ideas from diffusion-based animation work (DiffPoseTalk, FaceDiffuser) but applies them specifically to head pose in a multi-agent setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PaChorus establishes the first benchmark (ChorusHead, 8h) and method for 3D multi-singer singing animation, opening a research direction relevant to virtual group performances, digital avatars, and immersive entertainment. The key limitation is the restriction to duets due to MossFormer2's separation quality, leaving the 3+ singer case as a simulation rather than a true end-to-end solution; the authors acknowledge this as the primary open problem. A broader open question is whether the partner-aware interaction modeling generalizes beyond two singers or requires fundamentally different architectures for larger ensembles, and whether the 8-hour dataset is sufficient to capture the full diversity of choral expressiveness.
