## Problem

Existing video semantic segmentation methods face a fundamental trade-off: optical flow approaches are computationally expensive and error-prone in dynamic scenes, RNN-based methods suffer from sequential bottlenecks and training instability on long sequences, and transformer-based methods incur quadratic attention costs that limit scalability. The paper addresses the need for an architecture that efficiently shares temporal state across frames without a memory-heavy feature pool, enabling both parallel computation and long-range temporal coherence.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB video frames (480×480 on VSPW, 512×1024 on Cityscapes) and produces pixel-wise segmentation masks over 124 or 19 classes respectively. During training, frames are sampled at fixed temporal offsets {t−9, t−6, t−3, t}; during inference, frames are processed sequentially with hidden states carried forward across the entire video, so each frame receives context from all preceding frames. No post-processing (e.g., CRF) is applied to the raw network output.

## Method

TV3S partitions each encoded feature map into non-overlapping w×w spatial patches (w=20) that are processed independently and in parallel through N=4 TV3S blocks, each containing two TSS modules built on Mamba's S6 selective state space model. A shifted-window mechanism (shift s=10, inspired by Swin Transformer) re-partitions edge patches into sub-blocks so that boundary regions receive cross-patch spatial context, with the second TSS module in each block handling the shifted representation. Temporal coherence is achieved by propagating per-patch hidden states H across frames: at inference, each new frame updates H using the previous frame's stored states, enabling the model to integrate arbitrarily long temporal context with constant per-frame memory. Training employs a dual cross-entropy loss that supervises both the final frame's temporal aggregation output and intermediate encoder features (weighted λ=0.5), balancing spatial and temporal learning.

## Results

On the VSPW dataset, TV3S with MiT-B1 backbone achieves 40.0 mIoU, 90.7 mVC8, and 87.0 mVC16, outperforming CFFM (38.5 mIoU) and MRCFA (38.9 mIoU) while using fewer GFLOPs (36.9 vs. 143.2 and 127.9 for CFFM/MRCFA with MiT-B2). With the Swin-S backbone, TV3S reaches 50.6 mIoU, surpassing Mask2Former (42.1 mIoU) by 8.5 points and MPVSS (40.4 mIoU) by 10.2 points. On Cityscapes, TV3S (MiT-B1) achieves 75.6 mIoU at 83.6 GFLOPs and 25.1 FPS, beating CFFM (75.1 mIoU at 158.7 GFLOPs) and MRCFA (75.1 mIoU at 145.0 GFLOPs) with substantially lower computational cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TV3S consistently outperforms all video-specific baselines (CFFM, MRCFA, MPVSS) in mIoU across every backbone variant on VSPW, with the largest margin of +10.2 mIoU over MPVSS (Swin-S: 50.6 vs. 40.4) and +8.5 mIoU over Mask2Former (Swin-S: 50.6 vs. 42.1). Against the strongest video baselines CFFM and MRCFA, TV3S gains 1.1–1.5 mIoU with MiT-B1/B2 while reducing GFLOPs by roughly 50–60% (e.g., 53.9 vs. 143.2/127.9 for MiT-B2). On the larger MiT-B5 backbone, TV3S matches MRCFA's mIoU (49.8 vs. 49.9) but at less than one-third the FLOPs (137.0 vs. 373.0) and nearly 3× the FPS (14.0 vs. 5.0). On Cityscapes, TV3S (MiT-B1) achieves 75.6 mIoU, edging out CFFM and MRCFA (both 75.1) while using 47–48% fewer GFLOPs (83.6 vs. 158.7/145.0) and running at 25.1 FPS versus 11.7/13.0 FPS. Frame-by-frame baselines (SegFormer, Mask2Former, DeepLabv3+, PSPNet, OCRNet, UperNet) are uniformly outperformed in mIoU, confirming the benefit of explicit temporal modeling over independent per-frame inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

TV3S builds directly on Mamba's selective state space model (S6) as its temporal processing core and repurposes Swin Transformer's shifted-window mechanism for spatial context sharing across patches. It departs from prior SSM-based vision models (Vision Mamba, VMamba, VideoMamba) that process sequences sequentially in a hierarchical fashion, instead parallelizing computation across spatial patches while sharing hidden states temporally across frames. The paper positions itself against three prior VSS paradigms—optical-flow alignment, RNN-based feature aggregation (ConvLSTM), and transformer-based cross-frame attention—arguing that all suffer from either estimation errors, sequential bottlenecks, or quadratic complexity, and that a patch-parallel SSM with selective gating avoids these limitations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TV3S offers a practical path toward scalable long-video segmentation by enabling parallel computation in both training and inference, reducing the sequential delay inherent in SSMs and the memory overhead of feature pools in prior methods. The temporal context saturating around 16–32 frames suggests diminishing returns from longer histories, an open question for whether richer state representations could extend useful context. The approach is currently validated on two datasets (VSPW, Cityscapes) with a limited set of backbones, and its transfer to other video tasks (detection, action recognition) or to streaming/online settings remains unexplored. The finding that reintroducing spatial features after temporal modeling degrades performance hints at a tension between spatial refinement and temporal coherence that warrants further investigation.
