## Problem

Existing gradient-based OOD detection methods like ODIN degrade performance on near-OOD data because their perturbation preprocessing increases false confidence for OOD inputs rather than reducing it. The paper identifies an unexploited asymmetry: OOD detection scores are more susceptible to reduction under perturbation than IND scores, and this perturbation robustness difference has not been systematically leveraged for OOD detection, particularly in conjunction with adversarially trained models.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet) and produces a scalar OOD detection score that is thresholded to classify the input as IND or OOD. During inference, the input image is iteratively perturbed via K steps of projected gradient descent (with step size ε), and the minimum score across all K+1 intermediate images (including the original) is returned as the final detection score. The method is post-hoc and IND-free, requiring no in-distribution data or model modification at test time.

## Method

PRO (Perturbation Rectified OOD detection) defines an adversarial score function g*(x) = min_{||δ||∞ ≤ ε} g(x+δ) that searches for the local minimum of a given softmax-based OOD score (MSP, Entropy, Temperature Scaling, or GEN) within an ε-ball around the input. This is solved via multi-step FGSM (fast gradient sign method), where the image is updated as x_{t+1} = x_t − ε·sign(∇g(x_t)) for K steps, and the minimum score across all intermediate images is taken to prevent perturbation from boosting false OOD confidence. The key insight is that adversarially trained models provide a theoretical lower bound on perturbed IND scores (via bounded adversarial training loss and Jensen's inequality), while OOD scores lack such protection, thereby widening the IND–OOD score gap. PRO is applied as a preprocessing step to any existing softmax-based score without modifying the underlying model architecture, and its effectiveness is amplified when combined with adversarially robust backbones from RobustBench.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with an adversarially robust model (LRR), PRO-GEN achieves an average FPR@95 of 19.82 and AUROC of 95.00, outperforming the best baseline KNN (30.04/93.27) by over 10% in FPR@95. On the default (non-robust) CIFAR-10 model, PRO-GEN reaches 29.60/91.79, still surpassing KNN (27.52/92.19) in FPR@95 while matching its AUROC. For near-OOD detection specifically (CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet), PRO-GEN on the robust model achieves 29.56/91.85 and 21.96/94.48 respectively, the best among all post-hoc methods. On ImageNet-1K, PRO's gains diminish with model scale, and activation-modification methods (Scale, ASH, ReAct) remain the strongest baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CIFAR-10 with the robust LRR model, PRO-GEN (19.82 FPR@95 / 95.00 AUROC) decisively outperforms KNN (30.04/93.27), VIM (54.90/88.09), and ODIN (68.73/78.77), reducing FPR@95 by over 10 points versus KNN and nearly 50 points versus ODIN. On the default (non-robust) CIFAR-10 model, PRO-GEN (29.60/91.79) edges out KNN (27.52/92.19) in FPR@95 while matching its AUROC, and substantially outperforms ODIN (63.81/86.26) and MDS (38.11/87.88). For near-OOD detection (CIFAR-100, TIN), PRO variants consistently achieve the best FPR@95 among all post-hoc methods on both model types, e.g., PRO-GEN on the robust model reaches 29.56/91.85 on CIFAR-100 and 21.96/94.48 on TIN. On CIFAR-100 (Table 2), PRO-GEN on the robust model achieves 52.38/82.50 near-OOD AUROC, slightly surpassing RMDS (51.13/82.08) and GEN (52.96/81.88). However, on ImageNet-1K, PRO's advantage diminishes: activation-modification methods (Scale, ASH, ReAct) remain the strongest, and PRO only provides modest AUROC gains in near-OOD detection, with far-OOD performance roughly on par with or slightly below the base softmax scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRO repurposes the gradient-based perturbation idea from ODIN and G-ODIN but inverts its objective: rather than boosting IND confidence (as ODIN does), PRO searches for the *minimum* OOD score in an ε-neighborhood, exploiting the observation that OOD scores are more fragile under perturbation. It also explicitly bridges two previously separate research threads—adversarial robustness (RobustBench, LRR-trained models) and OOD detection (OpenOOD benchmark)—by showing that adversarially trained backbones provide a more favorable substrate for perturbation-based score separation. The method is a post-hoc wrapper around existing softmax scores (MSP, Entropy, Temperature Scaling, GEN), requiring no architectural changes or retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a concrete, empirically validated link between adversarial robustness and OOD detection, suggesting that investing in robust training pays dividends for safety-critical deployment beyond just adversarial attack resistance. A key limitation is that PRO's gains attenuate with model scale—ImageNet results show only modest AUROC improvements and the method is outperformed by activation-modification approaches (Scale, ASH, ReAct) at that scale. The finding that larger class counts make IND scores *less* robust to perturbation (Fig. 7) raises an open question about whether the perturbation-robustness gap can be preserved or restored in large-scale models, which would be necessary for PRO to be broadly applicable.
