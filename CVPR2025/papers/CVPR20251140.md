## Problem

Aerial-ground video-based person ReID faces extreme viewpoint, scale, and resolution differences between drone and ground-level footage, yet existing benchmarks are limited in identity count, altitude range (≤60m), platform diversity, and environmental variety. No prior dataset simultaneously captures the full operational altitude spectrum (up to 120m), multiple camera platforms (drone, CCTV, wearable), and large-scale identity variation needed to stress-test robustness in realistic cross-platform surveillance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251140/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video tracklet — a sequence of T person-cropped frames from one of three platforms (drone at 15/30/80/120m, CCTV at 704×480 or 1280×720, or GoPro at up to 3840×2160) — and produces a single identity feature vector for cross-platform matching. Evaluation is performed in both aerial-to-ground and ground-to-aerial directions, with the ground-to-aerial test set augmented by 3,619 additional distractor identities. Performance is measured via CMC Rank-1/5/10 and mAP.

## Method

AG-VPReID-Net is a three-stream end-to-end framework. Stream 1 (Adapted Temporal-Spatial) extracts frame features with a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 encoder, models temporal dynamics via a GRU that regresses SMPL 3D shape parameters, enhances features by fusing appearance and shape (TFE), and refines the representation through an identity memory bank with Sequence-Specific Prompts. Stream 2 (Normalized Appearance) generates per-frame UV texture maps via PhysPT pose estimation and Texformer, applies brightness normalization, histogram matching, and gamma correction, then performs visibility-mask-weighted aggregation across frames before feeding into a DGC Omni-scale Module. Stream 3 (Multi-Scale Attention) uses a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 to collect tokens from multiple encoder layers, applies temporal modulation to build a spatiotemporal feature volume, and processes it through a lightweight Transformer decoder with a learnable video-level query token. The three stream outputs are concatenated to form a unified viewpoint-invariant whole-body representation for retrieval.

## Results

AG-VPReID-Net achieves state-of-the-art results across all five evaluated datasets: 91.5% mAP / 93.2% Rank-1 on MARS, 87.3% mAP / 93.2% Rank-1 on LS-VID, 96.3% Rank-1 on iLIDS-VID, 81.3% mAP / 73.1% Rank-1 on G2A-VReID, and 64.0% mAP / 71.9% Rank-1 (Aerial→Ground) and 58.0% mAP / 75.6% Rank-1 (Ground→Aerial) on the new AG-VPReID benchmark. The largest margins are on LS-VID (+4.9% mAP over LSTRL) and AG-VPReID G2A (+8.8% Rank-1 over CLIP-ReID). Notably, even the best method achieves only ~72–76% Rank-1 on AG-VPReID, underscoring the dataset's difficulty compared to >90% on ground-based benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AG-VPReID-Net outperforms all 19 baselines on every dataset and metric reported. On ground-to-ground benchmarks, the gains are moderate but consistent: +3.4% mAP over CLIP-ReID on MARS, +4.9% mAP over LSTRL on LS-VID, and +3.0% Rank-1 over MFA on iLIDS-VID. On the cross-platform G2A-VReID dataset, the advantage widens to +4.6% mAP and +3.2% Rank-1 over the previous best (MGH), reflecting the benefit of the multi-scale attention stream for aerial-ground scale variation. On the new AG-VPReID benchmark, the largest margin is +8.8% Rank-1 over CLIP-ReID in the Ground→Aerial setting (75.6% vs. 66.8%), while the Aerial→Ground gap is smaller (+0.3% Rank-1, +1.7% mAP over CLIP-ReID), indicating that aerial-to-ground matching remains the harder direction. Among the 19 baselines, CLIP-ReID and LSTRL are the strongest competitors on AG-VPReID, followed by DCCT and CAVIT, all of which trail by 1–9% depending on the direction and metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own prior image-based aerial-ground ReID work (AG-ReID [31, 32]) and the G2A-VReID video benchmark [47] into a substantially larger and more diverse video setting, adding wearable cameras and altitudes up to 120m. Architecturally, the three-stream design composes components from recent video ReID literature: the Adapted Temporal-Spatial Stream adapts the GRU-based 3D shape modeling of Nguyen et al. [34] and the CLIP-based temporal feature enhancement of TF-CLIP [45], while the Multi-Scale Attention Stream follows the frozen-CLIP-plus-Transformer-decoder paradigm established by Lin et al. [23] and Ye et al. [44]. The Normalized Appearance Stream introduces a physics-informed UV-texture aggregation pipeline (PhysPT + Texformer) that is novel in its application to cross-view ReID, though each individual component draws on established 3D human reconstruction tools.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AG-VPReID is likely to become the standard large-scale benchmark for aerial-ground video ReID, given its scale (6,632 identities, 32,321 tracklets, 9.6M frames), three-platform diversity, and altitude range up to 120m—far exceeding prior aerial-ground datasets. The consistent ~20-point Rank-1 drop across all methods relative to ground-based datasets (e.g., 75.6% vs. 93.2% on MARS) confirms that viewpoint and scale invariance remain genuinely unsolved, providing a clear research target. An open question is whether the marginal gain from the full three-stream architecture over the temporal-spatial stream alone (~0.4% A2G Rank-1) justifies the substantial additional compute from PhysPT/Texformer UV-map generation, and whether simpler multi-scale or contrastive approaches could close the gap without the 3D mesh pipeline.
