## Problem

Extracting geometric edges from thin-walled structures in unstructured point clouds is particularly challenging because local neighborhoods around thin edges are inherently unreliable: larger neighborhoods blur boundary information by mixing points from both surfaces and side-end faces, while smaller neighborhoods suffer from noise and sparse sampling. Both traditional geometric methods and existing learning-based approaches depend heavily on sufficient, well-sampled local context, making them degrade significantly on these common real-world structures.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unstructured 3D point cloud (approximately 20,000 to 15 million points) of thin-walled structures with thickness 2–3 units, using KNN with k=26 for neighborhood queries. It produces three outputs: binary edge/non-edge classification per point, refined edge point positions projected onto the true surface intersection, and per-point normal vectors estimated from the spherical curve. The method is robust to Gaussian noise up to σ=0.03 and operates across resolutions from 0.3 to 0.8, where at the coarsest resolution only ~4 points may sample a side-end face.

## Method

STAR-Edge introduces a local spherical curve representation: each point's KNN neighborhood is projected onto a unit sphere, where co-planar neighbors align along great circle arcs; a closed curve is then fitted via PCA projection, convex hull vertex selection, and spline interpolation. This curve is encoded into a rotation-invariant descriptor by computing spherical harmonic coefficients (bandwidth B=10) and taking the ℓ₂ norm of coefficients at each degree, yielding a compact feature vector fed to a lightweight MLP for edge classification. The same spherical curve enables structure-aware normal estimation via RANSAC fitting of the dominant great circle arc, and an optimization function (Eq. 9) projects initially identified edge points onto the intersection of neighboring tangent planes using the estimated normals, with a regularization term (μ=0.1) preventing excessive displacement.

## Results

On the thin-walled structure dataset, STAR-Edge achieves an Edge Chamfer Distance (ECD) of 0.0587, substantially outperforming EC-Net (0.2936), MFLE (0.4839), RFEPS (1.4392), and PIE-Net (52.1204). In terms of classification, STAR-Edge attains 99.52% Recall, 54.18% Precision, 70.13% F1-score, and 99.31% Accuracy. On the ABC dataset, STAR-Edge achieves the lowest ECD of 0.0024, edging out RFEPS and NerVE (both 0.0026) and EC-Net (0.0037).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STAR-Edge dominates all five baselines on the thin-walled dataset. Against EC-Net, STAR-Edge reduces ECD by 5× (0.0587 vs. 0.2936); EC-Net does not report classification metrics, and its up-sampling strategy produces redundant points around true edges. PIE-Net fails catastrophically (ECD 52.12, F1 0.037) because it requires dense sampling that the thin-wall geometry cannot provide. MFLE, the strongest traditional baseline, is outperformed by STAR-Edge on every metric—ECD drops from 0.4839 to 0.0587, Precision nearly doubles (0.3028 → 0.5418), and F1 improves from 0.4621 to 0.7013. RFEPS achieves the highest Recall (0.9997) but at the cost of very low Precision (0.1512) and ECD (1.4392), indicating it flags many non-edge points; STAR-Edge trades a negligible 0.45% Recall for a 3.6× Precision gain. On the ABC dataset, STAR-Edge achieves the best ECD (0.0024) over NerVE and RFEPS (0.0026) and EC-Net (0.0037), confirming generalizability beyond thin-walled geometries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both traditional geometric edge extraction (covariance eigenvalues, curvature, normal-based methods) and recent learning-based approaches (EC-Net, PIE-Net, NerVE) by identifying their shared vulnerability: reliance on reliable local neighborhoods, which breaks down in thin-walled structures where upper/lower surfaces and side faces intermingle. Rather than building on a specific prior architecture, STAR-Edge introduces the local spherical curve as a new structure-aware neighborhood representation, repurposing spherical harmonics (from shape descriptor literature) and kernel density estimation to encode this curve into a rotation-invariant descriptor. The pipeline then combines this descriptor with a lightweight MLP for classification and RANSAC-based great-circle fitting for normal estimation, drawing on tools from signal processing and robust geometry in a novel combination for edge detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Thin-walled structures (aircraft panels, mechanical parts, furniture) are ubiquitous in manufacturing yet remain poorly served by existing edge extraction methods, making this a practically important gap. The method's robustness to noise and sparse sampling (maintaining low ECD at resolution 0.8 where only ~4 points sample the side face) suggests it could be deployed on real measurement data where perfect sampling is unrealistic. However, the 214-second runtime (vs. 1.14 s for MFLE) limits real-time applicability, and the evaluation is confined to a 42-model thin-walled dataset plus an ABC subset, leaving open whether the spherical curve representation generalizes to non-thin-walled or highly curved geometries. A broader ablation (e.g., isolating the contribution of the spherical curve vs. the SH encoding vs. the MLP) would strengthen the claims.
