## Problem

Video content production has mature techniques for visual curation (viewpoint selection, post-editing) but lacks equivalent methods for audio, leading to a disconnect between visual and acoustic saliency. This paper introduces visually-guided acoustic highlighting as a new task: using the curated visual stream as guidance to transform poorly mixed audio into a well-balanced mix with appropriate source-level adjustments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252080/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a poorly mixed mono audio waveform (44 kHz, derived from stereo by channel averaging) and a video stream sampled at 1 fps, with optional per-frame text captions as additional context. The audio is processed in both time-domain (waveform) and frequency-domain (magnitude spectrogram via STFT with 4096 window / 1024 hop) simultaneously. The output is a highlighted audio waveform that preserves the original content while rebalancing the relative levels of speech, music, and sound effects to match the visual narrative.

## Method

VisAH employs a dual U-Net backbone (adapted from HybridDemucs) with parallel spectrogram and waveform branches that produce a unified latent audio embedding. A latent highlighting transformer then integrates contextual information: video frames encoded by CLIP ViT-L/14 and text captions (generated by InternVL2-8B, embedded via T5-XXL) are each processed by a temporal transformer encoder, then fed into a cross-attention transformer decoder that produces an offset added to the original audio features through a zero-initialized 1×1 convolution. The refined latent features are decoded back to waveform via both U-Net branches, with the spectrogram branch outputting a ratio mask applied to the input magnitude. Training uses a simple MR-STFT loss (window sizes 2048/1024/512) on THE MUDDY MIX DATASET, where poorly mixed inputs are synthesized from movie audio via imperfect three-stem separation, random loudness adjustment (±6/9/12 dB), and linear remixing.

## Results

On THE MUDDY MIX DATASET, VisAH achieves the best score across all five evaluation metrics: MAG 10.08, ENV 3.43, KLD 11.01, ΔIB 0.80, and W-dis 0.79 (all lower is better). Compared to the poorly mixed input, VisAH yields 56% improvement in magnitude distance, 46% in envelope distance, 47% in KL divergence, 47% in audio-video semantic alignment (ΔIB), and 59% in Wasserstein distance. In subjective evaluation, VisAH achieves a 77% top-2 ranking rate, surpassing the ground truth movie audio for 34% of test videos.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VisAH outperforms all four baselines on every metric. The DnRv3+CDX empirical baseline, which remixes separated stems using fixed loudness distributions from the Cinematic Sound Demixing Challenge, actually degrades waveform quality (MAG 26.32 vs. 22.69 input) and time alignment (W-dis 2.84 vs. 1.94), only improving KLD by 23% due to its generic distribution matching. Learn2Remix, an audio-only end-to-end remixing model, improves waveform distance (MAG 19.07) but catastrophically fails on semantic alignment (KLD 61.76, ΔIB 8.27), confirming that visual context is essential for correct source rebalancing. LCE-SepReformer, a text-guided sound editor, performs better than Learn2Remix on semantic metrics (KLD 30.99, ΔIB 1.88) but still trails VisAH by 2.8× on KLD and 2.4× on ΔIB, as it lacks the temporal reasoning needed for coherent highlighting. VisAH's advantage is most pronounced on W-dis (0.79 vs. 1.20–2.84 for baselines), indicating superior temporal alignment of highlighted sources with the video.

## Relation to Prior Work

VisAH builds directly on the HybridDemucs dual-branch U-Net architecture [7] for its audio backbone, unifying spectrogram and waveform processing. Conceptually, it departs from audio-visual separation [10, 11, 18, 68], which isolates a target source and suppresses others to zero, by instead producing a balanced multi-source mix guided by visual context. It also extends the music remixing literature [26, 36, 66] from instrument-level rebalancing to general cinematic audio (speech, music, sound effects) and introduces video as the guiding signal rather than a target mixing style. The pseudo-data generation pipeline (separate → adjust → remix) repurposes the Cinematic Sound Demixing Challenge [8] and DnR v3 [63] separation models to synthesize training pairs from well-mixed movie audio.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'free supervision from movies' framing is an elegant dataset construction strategy that could generalize to other audio-visual alignment tasks where ground-truth curation is expensive. However, the current scope is narrow: only action-genre clips, 10-second segments, 1 fps video, and a single synthetic corruption process, leaving open whether the approach transfers to real-world poorly mixed recordings or longer, more diverse content. The demonstrated application to refining MovieGen video-to-audio outputs hints at a broader role as a post-processing stage for generative audio, but this remains preliminary. Key open questions include generalization across genres, handling of longer temporal dependencies, and whether the simple MR-STFT loss is sufficient for perceptual quality at scale.
