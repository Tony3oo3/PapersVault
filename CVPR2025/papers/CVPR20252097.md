## Problem

Inferring material properties from motion observations has been treated either as a system identification problem requiring user-specified material type (e.g., elastic vs. fluid) or as a per-scene learning task requiring a separate network for each object. No prior approach provides a single unified model that can capture the constitutive behavior of diverse materials—elastic, plasticine, sand, Newtonian, and non-Newtonian fluids—without any a priori knowledge of the material category.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252097/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D particle trajectory (positions over T timesteps for P particles) along with the initial object geometry as input, and produces a 32-dimensional latent vector that encodes the material's constitutive behavior. During inference, the latent is initialized from K-means cluster centers of the learned latent space and optimized via differentiable MPM simulation, with the loss computed as L2 distance between simulated and observed particle positions. The resulting latent can be reused to re-simulate the same material under novel initial conditions, velocities, or geometries.

## Method

UniPhy consists of two latent-conditioned MLPs: a deformation gradient projection network g_φ (5 layers, hidden dim 32) and a constitutive law network f_θ (5 layers, hidden dim 128), both conditioned on a shared 32-dimensional latent z. During training, each trajectory in a dataset of 200 simulated MPM trajectories per material is assigned its own random latent, and both networks are jointly optimized with L2 losses on projected deformation gradients and stress tensors, while the latents are regularized with an L2 penalty. At inference, the network weights are frozen and only the latent is optimized by embedding both networks inside a differentiable MPM simulator and minimizing the L2 loss between predicted and ground-truth particle positions over the trajectory. This unified latent-conditioned design replaces the need for user-specified parametric constitutive models or per-scene networks, enabling a single model to span all material types.

## Results

UniPhy reports reconstruction error (lower is better) across four material types. On the primary evaluation trajectory, UniPhy achieves 5.2e-6 (Elastic), 1.5e-5 (Sand), 3.9e-5 (Plasticine), and 1.1e-6 (Newtonian), outperforming the closest baseline NCLaw by 46×, 1.7×, 1.7×, and 18× respectively. In generalization settings (extended time, unseen velocity, different geometry), UniPhy maintains the best reconstruction error across all four materials and three perturbation types, with errors typically in the 1e-5 to 1e-4 range.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniPhy outperforms all four baselines on every material type in the main reconstruction task. Against the closest competitor NCLaw [39], UniPhy reduces reconstruction error by 46× on Elastic (5.2e-6 vs 2.4e-4) and 18× on Newtonian (1.1e-6 vs 2.0e-5), with more modest 1.7× improvements on Sand and Plasticine; the authors attribute NCLaw's weaker performance to its per-scene training and reliance on teacher forcing. Against GNN [47], UniPhy is 1–3 orders of magnitude better across all materials (e.g., 5.2e-6 vs 2.1e-2 on Elastic). The neural constitutive baseline [55] is competitive on Elastic (1.2e-5) but degrades severely on Plasticine (1.9e-1) and Newtonian (2.9e-2), while the spline baseline [59] is uniformly the weakest, with errors around 0.2–0.3 across all materials. Without teacher forcing, UniPhy still outperforms NCLaw by 10–100× on all materials, whereas NCLaw (w/o TF) becomes unstable on Elastic (7.1e-3 vs UniPhy's 7.5e-4).

## Relation to Prior Work

UniPhy builds on the differentiable MPM simulation framework (DiffTaichi) and the neural constitutive modeling line initiated by NCLaw, which learned per-instance networks to approximate constitutive laws from motion observations. It departs from NCLaw's instance-specific architecture by introducing a shared latent-conditioned network that encodes all materials in a common latent space, and from system-identification approaches (e.g., PAC-NeRF) that require the user to specify a parametric material model beforehand. The paper explicitly draws an analogy to the broader CV trend of moving from category-specific to unified models, positioning latent-conditioned constitutive modeling as the physics analogue of that shift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the need for user-specified material type, UniPhy opens the door to truly open-ended material inference from motion, which is a prerequisite for physical reasoning in unstructured scenes. However, the current formulation still requires known initial geometry and full 3D particle trajectories, and cannot handle non-homogeneous (multi-material) scenes, both of which are significant barriers to in-the-wild deployment. The latent-space formulation also raises open questions about interpretability (what do individual latent dimensions correspond to physically?) and whether the learned latent space generalizes to materials outside the training distribution.
