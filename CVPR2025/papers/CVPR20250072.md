## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting relies on low-order spherical harmonics for view-dependent appearance, making it unable to capture the high-frequency, mirror-like reflections characteristic of highly specular surfaces. Existing PBR-based extensions either ignore secondary effects, bake inter-reflections into low-order SH (losing high-frequency detail and hindering editing), or use screen-space ray tracing that produces incomplete reflections. The core challenge is efficiently querying local incident lighting in 3D space to render complete, high-frequency inter-reflections of nearby objects on specular surfaces.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images (synthetic scenes: ~300 frames at 1024×1024; real-world: 138–139 frames at 1440×1080) along with a binary mask identifying highly specular regions (obtained via SAM) and monocular normal priors from StableNormal. It produces a 3D Gaussian point cloud carrying explicit per-Gaussian geometry (depth, surface normal), material parameters (diffuse albedo, 3-channel specular reflectance F0), and an optimizable environment map, enabling not only novel view synthesis but also relighting, object insertion/deletion, and material editing without re-baking.

## Method

SpecTRe-GS separately models highly specular surfaces via a PBR rendering equation with ideal mirror reflection (Schlick's Fresnel approximation) and rough surfaces via per-Gaussian SH color, combined through a binary mask. To compute the incident radiance for specular surfaces, it implements a hardware-accelerated ray tracer (OptiX) within the 3DGS framework using stretched icosahedron proxy meshes as a geometry acceleration structure, tracing secondary rays to obtain both direct (environment map) and indirect (scene geometry) lighting. A key training strategy enhances geometry reconstruction: monocular normal priors provide initial constraints, while joint geometry optimization differentiates the ray-traced incident radiance with respect to surface normals using a depth-aware finite-difference perturbation, and progressive learning gradually decays the normal prior weight while suppressing residual color in reflective regions to enforce physically-based reflection modeling.

## Results

On synthetic scenes, SpecTRe-GS achieves 29.41 PSNR (entire image) and 23.28 PSNR (reflective region), outperforming the best baseline 3iGS by 0.83 dB and 1.30 dB respectively. On real-world scenes, it reaches 25.11 PSNR (entire) and 22.77 PSNR (reflective), surpassing the strongest competitor GOF by 0.25 dB and 1.52 dB. The gains are most pronounced in reflective regions, where SpecTRe-GS also achieves the best SSIM (0.949 synthetic, 0.967 real-world) and lowest LPIPS (0.062 synthetic, 0.035 real-world) across all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpecTRe-GS outperforms all five baselines on every metric across both synthetic and real-world scenes, with the largest margins in reflective regions. Against 3DGS [15], it gains 1.80 dB PSNR on synthetic reflective regions (23.28 vs. 21.51) and 1.46 dB on real-world reflective regions (22.77 vs. 21.31). Against GOF [42], the closest competitor on entire-image metrics, it improves reflective-region PSNR by 1.90 dB (synthetic) and 1.52 dB (real-world), while also achieving the best LPIPS (0.062 vs. 0.076 synthetic; 0.035 vs. 0.047 real-world). Against the PBR-based methods GShader [13] and 3DGS-DR [39], the gains are even larger in reflective regions (2.49–2.54 dB PSNR on synthetic; 2.44–2.59 dB on real-world), confirming that ray-traced incident radiance is critical for high-frequency specular inter-reflections. Notably, adding normal prior guidance to GOF actually degrades its performance (GOF* drops 0.71 dB on synthetic reflective PSNR), whereas SpecTRe-GS benefits from the same guidance as part of its progressive training strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpecTRe-GS builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting and its geometry-focused variant GOF (for the intersection-point depth/normal formulation and distortion/depth-normal losses), and on 3DGRT's OptiX-based ray tracing infrastructure for 3D Gaussians. It departs from prior PBR-based 3DGS methods (GShader, 3DGS-DR, GI-GS) that either ignore secondary effects, bake inter-reflections into low-order SH, or use incomplete screen-space ray tracing, by performing full 3D-space ray tracing for incident radiance restricted to highly specular surfaces. The key conceptual departure is the explicit separation of highly specular and rough surfaces—ray-tracing only the former while retaining SH color for the latter—which dramatically reduces the number of secondary rays needed while preserving rendering speed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a fundamental gap in 3DGS: the inability to faithfully render high-frequency inter-reflections of nearby objects on mirror-like surfaces, which is critical for photorealistic reconstruction and physically meaningful scene editing (relighting, object insertion/deletion, material swapping). The explicit decomposition of geometry, material, and incident lighting enables editing without re-baking, a practical advantage over SH-baked approaches. Limitations remain: only single-bounce specular inter-reflections from rough surfaces are modeled (no multi-bounce path tracing), the ideal-mirror approximation uses one secondary ray per pixel, and the monocular normal prior can fail under strong semantic ambiguity (e.g., mirrors with hidden borders). Open questions include extending to non-convex reflectors, multi-bounce effects, and whether the geometry-enhancing training strategy generalizes to scenes without clear specular/rough boundaries.
