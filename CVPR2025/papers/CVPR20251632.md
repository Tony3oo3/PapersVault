## Problem

Existing 2D diffusion-based character animation methods struggle with flexible multi-attribute control, generalization to novel 3D articulated motions, and natural human-object interactions in complex scenes, while 3D neural representation approaches require expensive multi-view captures for per-case training. The root cause is that prior methods operate entirely in 2D feature space, disregarding the inherent 3D spatial structure of video content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251632/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single reference image for character identity, a pose sequence (from motion databases like AMASS/Mixamo or extracted from in-the-wild driving videos) for motion, and a scene video or image for environment, or alternatively a single driving video from which all attributes are automatically decomposed. Training uses 24-frame video clips from the HUD-7K dataset (5K real + 2K synthetic videos), and the output is a temporally consistent animated character video at the resolution of the input frames. No per-character training or multi-view data is required, enabling zero-shot animation of arbitrary characters.

## Method

MIMO introduces spatial decomposed modeling: it lifts 2D frame pixels into 3D via monocular depth estimation and decomposes each video into three hierarchical spatial layers (main human, underlying scene, floating occlusion) based on depth ordering. The human layer is further disentangled into an identity code (obtained by warping the posed frame to canonical A-pose via a pretrained human repose model, then encoding with CLIP + ReferenceNet) and a structured motion code (6,890 latent codes anchored to SMPL vertices, deformed by estimated pose, and projected to 2D via differentiable rasterization to establish dense body-surface correspondences). Scene and occlusion layers are encoded with a shared frozen VAE and concatenated into a full scene code. These three codes condition a diffusion decoder (Stable Diffusion U-Net with AnimateDiff temporal layers) through 3D convolution fusion, self-attention, and cross-attention, trained end-to-end with standard noise-prediction loss.

## Results

On the 100 in-the-wild test set, MIMO achieves PSNR 25.21, SSIM 0.883, LPIPS 0.125, and FVD 221.4, outperforming all three baselines on every metric. The headline gains are a +4.16 PSNR improvement over the best baseline (Champ, 21.04) and a +0.152 SSIM improvement over the best baseline (Mimic-Motion, 0.731). In terms of video-level quality, MIMO reduces FVD by 23% over Mimic-Motion (289.2) and by 46% over Champ (412.5), indicating substantially better temporal coherence and perceptual realism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIMO dominates all three baselines across every metric. Against Animate Anyone [15] (PSNR 21.00, SSIM 0.722, LPIPS 0.264, FVD 304.3), MIMO gains +4.21 PSNR, +0.161 SSIM, −0.139 LPIPS, and −82.9 FVD. Against Mimic-Motion [60] (PSNR 20.69, SSIM 0.731, LPIPS 0.343, FVD 289.2), the margins are +4.52 PSNR, +0.152 SSIM, −0.218 LPIPS, and −67.8 FVD. Against Champ [63] (PSNR 21.04, SSIM 0.724, LPIPS 0.312, FVD 412.5), MIMO leads by +4.17 PSNR, +0.159 SSIM, −0.187 LPIPS, and −191.1 FVD. The largest relative advantage is in FVD, where Champ's 412.5 is nearly double MIMO's 221.4, reflecting that prior methods struggle most with temporal consistency under complex 3D motions and scene interactions. Qualitatively, the paper notes that all three baselines fail on extreme articulated motions with self-occlusions, human-object interactions, and large camera movements, whereas MIMO handles these cases with coherent structure and texture detail.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based character animation line (Animate Anyone, MimicMotion, Champ) by replacing their 2D feature-space conditioning with a 3D-aware spatial decomposition that lifts video pixels into depth-ordered layers (human, scene, occlusion). It builds on pre-trained Stable Diffusion and AnimateDiff as the generative backbone, borrows SMPL-based structured motion representation from 3D human modeling (En3D, HOSNeRF), and uses monocular depth estimation (Depth Anything) and video tracking (SAM 2) as auxiliary perception tools. The key departure from prior character animation methods is the explicit hierarchical layering and the disentangled encoding of identity (via canonical appearance transfer) and motion (via SMPL-anchored latent codes), which prior 2D methods handle only implicitly through single-frame appearance and 2D skeleton guidance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MIMO demonstrates that 3D-aware spatial decomposition can substantially close the gap between 2D diffusion-based character animation and the quality expected from per-case 3D methods, while retaining the scalability of single-image inputs. The unified controllability over character, motion, and scene opens practical editing applications (character replacement, motion transfer, scene insertion) that were previously fragmented across separate pipelines. Open questions include whether the monocular depth estimation bottleneck limits robustness in depth-ambiguous scenes, whether the approach generalizes to multi-character interactions, and whether the 2D video output can be extended to true 3D-consistent novel-view synthesis without per-case fitting.
