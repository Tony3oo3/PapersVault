## Problem

Existing deep multi-view clustering methods suffer from two interrelated issues: direct fusion of views with divergent feature distributions introduces inter-view conflicts that degrade clustering quality, and individual view features are not selectively enhanced before fusion, causing loss of discriminative information. The paper tackles both by proposing a framework that first strengthens the most informative feature channels within each view and then fuses views in a conflict-aware, progressive manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252163/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes V heterogeneous feature matrices X^(v) ∈ R^(n×d^(v)) with potentially different dimensions per view (e.g., 4659/4633/4665/4684 for BBC, 216/76/64/6/240/47 for HandWritten) and produces discrete cluster assignments via k-means on a single shared representation Z ∈ R^(n×d_k). The number of clusters k is a user-specified hyperparameter, and the framework is fully unsupervised—no ground-truth labels are used during training. The model is trained end-to-end with a combined reconstruction, intra-view contrastive, and cross-view alignment loss, then k-means is applied to the final fused embedding.

## Method

EPFMVC comprises four modules operating in sequence. First, a Feature Channel Attention Encoder (FCAencoder) applies a Squeeze-and-Excitation block to reweight feature channels per view, followed by multi-head self-attention with residual connections and an FFN to produce view-specific latent representations Z^(v). Second, a View Graph-based Progressive Fusion mechanism computes pairwise Optimal Transport distances between all view representations to build a V×V view graph, then iteratively fuses the most similar view pair via cross-view attention (query from one view, key/value from its nearest neighbor), progressively aggregating information to yield a shared representation Z—avoiding the conflicts that arise from simultaneous all-view fusion. Third, an Intra-View Similar Feature Enhancement module uses a contrastive loss over KNN-defined positive pairs to tighten intra-cluster geometry within each view. Fourth, a Cross-View Feature Alignment module applies a contrastive objective pulling each view's representation toward the shared Z. The total loss is L_rec + γ·L_sfe + ε·L_cfa, optimized jointly with Adam.

## Results

EPFMVC achieves the best ACC across all 7 datasets and all 4 metrics (ACC, NMI, Purity, F-score), outperforming 9 baselines. Headline improvements include 87.01% ACC on BBC (+11.97% over the second-best BMVC at 75.04%), 95.25% ACC on UCI-Digit (+3.25% over MFLVC at 92.00%), and 36.94% ACC on the large-scale CIFAR10-All (+4.5% over MVCAN at 32.44%). On Hdigit, EPFMVC reaches 99.88% ACC and 99.60% NMI, marginally surpassing SEM (99.83%/99.42%). The method also handles the 60,000-sample CIFAR10-All dataset where several deep baselines (LMVSC, AdaGAE, SDMVC, MFLVC) fail with out-of-memory errors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EPFMVC wins on every dataset and every metric against all 9 baselines. The largest margin is on BBC, where EPFMVC (87.01% ACC) surpasses the second-best conventional method BMVC (75.04%) by 11.97 points, and on UCI-Digit where it beats the strongest deep baseline MFLVC (92.00%) by 3.25 points. On the large-scale CIFAR10-All (60K samples, 7 views), EPFMVC (36.94% ACC) outperforms MVCAN (32.44%) by 4.5 points while four deep baselines (LMVSC, AdaGAE, SDMVC, MFLVC) fail entirely with out-of-memory errors. On Hdigit, the gap is narrow: EPFMVC (99.88%) edges out SEM (99.83%) by only 0.05 points, suggesting near-saturation on this 2-view dataset. On Wiki, EPFMVC (63.19%) beats DSMVC (62.53%) by a modest 0.66 points, indicating that the progressive fusion mechanism provides smaller gains when views are fewer (2) and more heterogeneous (128-dim vs. 10-dim). The FCAencoder ablation (Table 4) further confirms that the feature channel attention mechanism contributes the largest single gain, e.g., 47.8% ACC improvement over a plain autoencoder on NGS.

## Relation to Prior Work

EPFMVC carves new ground by combining three previously separate ideas into a unified multi-view clustering pipeline: Squeeze-and-Excitation channel attention (Hu et al., 2018) for per-view feature enhancement, Optimal Transport distance for constructing a view-level similarity graph, and multi-head attention for progressive cross-view fusion. Most prior deep multi-view clustering methods (reconstruction-based, graph-based, subspace-based) either fuse all views simultaneously or rely on learned weights, without explicitly addressing the ordering of fusion or enhancing individual view channels before fusion. The progressive fusion strategy—fusing the most OT-similar view pair first and iterating—is the paper's key architectural departure from the direct-concatenation or weighted-averaging fusion used in methods like MFLVC, SEM, and MVCAN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two practical pain points in multi-view clustering—inter-view feature conflicts and under-enhanced individual view representations—with a modular pipeline that is easy to interpret and ablate. However, the approach remains limited to complete multi-view settings with known sample correspondence, and the OT-based view graph construction may not scale efficiently to very large numbers of views. Open questions include whether the progressive fusion ordering generalizes to incomplete or noisy-view scenarios, whether the nearest-view heuristic is optimal compared to learned fusion schedules, and whether the gains persist when views are highly heterogeneous in dimensionality or modality (e.g., text + image + tabular).
