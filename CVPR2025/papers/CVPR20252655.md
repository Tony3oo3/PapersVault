## Problem

Existing distortion-perception (DP) tradeoff algorithms either sacrifice fidelity for perceptual quality or minimize MSE for faithful restoration, and adapting to different operating points on the DP plane requires retraining or redesigning the model. The paper asks whether a single pre-trained score-based generative model can flexibly and provably optimally traverse the entire DP tradeoff at inference time for general inverse problems.

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy measurement y = A(X) + N (where A is a known measurement operator such as Gaussian blur or 8× downsampling, and N is Gaussian noise with known variance σ_n²), a pre-trained score network s_θ(x,k), and a user-chosen scaling parameter λ ∈ [0,1]. It outputs a single reconstructed image x_0 sampled from the variance-scaled posterior. Notably, the same score network trained on clean FFHQ 256×256 images handles all measurement scenarios and noise levels without any retraining, and λ continuously interpolates between the MMSE point (λ=0) and full posterior sampling (λ=1).

## Method

The core contribution is a variance-scaled reverse diffusion process where the posterior covariance C_k at each reverse step is multiplied by a scaling factor λ ∈ [0,1], while the mean μ_k(x_{k+1}, y) remains unchanged. Theorem 1 characterizes the resulting marginal distribution, showing that the mean converges to the MMSE estimator and the variance scales as λ·Cov_{p(x_0|y)}[X_0] as T→∞. Theorem 2 proves that for conditional Gaussian distributions, this variance-scaled process achieves the optimal DP tradeoff under MSE and Wasserstein-2 distance, with the optimal distortion D(P) explicitly characterized in closed form. In practice, the conditional score ∇_{x_k} log p(x_k|y) is approximated via DPS using Tweedie's formula to estimate x̂_0(x_k) and an analytic gradient of the measurement likelihood, requiring only the pre-trained unconditional score network s_θ(x_k,k) plus a single hyperparameter ζ_{k,λ} to weight the data-fidelity term.

## Results

On the mixture Gaussian example, the MSE at λ=1 is approximately 2× the MSE at λ=0, matching the theoretical prediction in Theorem 2 for the conditional Gaussian case. On the FFHQ Gaussian deblur task (σ_n=0.3), PSCGAN requires averaging 64 sampled images to match the MSE achieved by the proposed method at λ=0.9, and its performance deteriorates significantly (much larger MSE and excessively high FID) when tested at σ_n=0.5. On the 8× super-resolution task, DiffPIR performs close to the λ=1 case, achieving a better FID score but larger distortion than the proposed method. The variance-scaled reverse diffusion process achieves a more complete empirical DP tradeoff curve and better MSE than both baselines across all tested inverse problems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PSCGAN [15], the proposed method wins decisively on both the range and quality of the DP tradeoff: it covers a much larger range of MSE–perception points with a single score network, whereas PSCGAN's tradeoff is suboptimal and limited, requiring up to 64 averaged samples to match the MSE at λ=0.9. PSCGAN also requires retraining for each noise level and measurement scenario, and its performance collapses when tested at σ_n=0.5 (trained at σ_n=0.3), showing much larger MSE and excessively high FID. Against DiffPIR [29], the proposed method achieves better distortion (lower MSE) across the full DP curve, while DiffPIR achieves a slightly better FID score at its operating point (close to λ=1). Under severe degradation conditions (σ_n=0.3/0.5 for deblurring, 8× downscaling for SR), DiffPIR's reduced diffusion steps lead to fidelity issues with generated details less faithful than the proposed λ=1 case. Overall, the proposed variance-scaled reverse diffusion dominates PSCGAN on all metrics and outperforms DiffPIR on distortion while being competitive on perception.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: the distortion-perception (DP) tradeoff framework introduced by Blau & Michaeli [2] and the score-based diffusion model approach to inverse problems (DPS [6], ΠGDM [19], score-based variational inference [28]). It builds directly on [28]'s result that propagating the reverse mean converges to the MMSE estimator, and on DPS's conditional score decomposition, but introduces a variance-scaled reverse process as a principled interpolation between the two extremes (MMSE at λ=0 and full posterior sampling at λ=1). Unlike PSCGAN [15], which traverses the DP curve via multi-sample averaging or generator noise injection and requires retraining per noise level, and unlike the linear-combination approach in Wasserstein space [7], this method uses a single pre-trained score network and a single scalar parameter to navigate the tradeoff at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: one score network handles arbitrary noise levels and measurement operators without retraining, and a single λ parameter gives a continuous, complete DP curve rather than discrete points from sample averaging. The theoretical optimality for conditional Gaussian distributions provides a clean benchmark, though the restriction to Gaussian posteriors limits the generality of the guarantee. Open questions include whether the optimality extends to non-Gaussian posteriors (the empirical results on mixture Gaussians and images suggest it works well but without proof), whether DDIM-style deterministic sampling can accelerate traversal, and how the heuristic ζ_{k,λ} weighting of the data-fidelity term interacts with the variance scaling in practice.
