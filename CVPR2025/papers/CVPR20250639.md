## Problem

Video-language models are computationally constrained to process only a small number of uniformly sampled frames, which either includes redundant/irrelevant content or misses critical events needed for reasoning. Prior adaptive selection methods either rely on expensive LLM/VLM API calls for iterative reasoning or select a fixed number of frames regardless of the specific (video, question) complexity, leaving an efficiency-accuracy gap unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The FFS model takes pre-computed visual features from n frames (32 for NExT-QA/IntentQA, 64 for EgoSchema) encoded by CLIP-ViT-B/32, and text features from the question plus m answer candidates encoded by CLIP BERT-Base. It outputs a variable-size binary selection mask over the n frames, where the number of selected frames s is adaptively determined within user-specified bounds [k_low, k_high] (e.g., s ∈ [1,4] or [4,32]). The selected frames are then passed to a downstream VLM (EVA-V-L or OpenLLoVi) for the final multiple-choice answer.

## Method

FFS is a lightweight (~0.2B param) multimodal transformer that encodes visual, language, and c learnable [CTRL] register tokens jointly, then applies a fixed top-k selection over the n+c resulting representations. The key mechanism is that [CTRL] tokens act as a learnable threshold: when the model selects a [CTRL] token in the top-k, that slot is discarded, so the actual number of output frames s ranges from k−c to k, encoding the [k_low, k_high] bounds. Differentiability is achieved via a Gumbel-top-k straight-through estimator, allowing end-to-end training supervised by the downstream VLM's cross-entropy loss without requiring frame-level annotations. The [CTRL] tokens are initialized with mean visual embeddings plus a learnable offset, and auxiliary fixed-k policy losses (k ∈ {1,2,4,8}) guide training and improve generalization.

## Results

On NExT-QA, FFS with EVA-V-L achieves 62.2% overall accuracy using only 2.3 frames on average (from a pool of 32), surpassing the 32-frame uniform baseline (61.6%) and the 4-frame baseline (60.2%). With the OpenLLoVi downstream model, FFS reaches 66.7% using 8.1 frames on average, matching the 32-frame baseline (66.5%) while using 4x fewer frames. On IntentQA, FFS achieves 64.2% overall with 2.4 average frames, outperforming the 32-frame baseline (63.6%). On the long-video EgoSchema benchmark, FFS achieves 55.6% with 16.8 average frames, beating the 180-frame OpenLLoVi baseline (55.4%) and VideoAgent with GPT-4 (54.1%) while processing ~10x fewer frames.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FFS consistently outperforms all open-source and fixed-k selection baselines across all three benchmarks. On NExT-QA, FFS with EVA-V-L (62.2%) beats SeViLa [85] (63.6% with 36 frames), CoVGT [80] (60.0%), MIST-CLIP [18] (57.1%), and VGT [79] (55.0%) while using far fewer frames; with OpenLLoVi it reaches 66.7%, approaching LLoVi with GPT-4 [87] (67.7%) but falling short of VideoAgent with GPT-4 [70] (71.3%). On IntentQA, FFS (64.2%) surpasses CaVIR* [33] (57.6%), VGT [79] (51.3%), and matches LLoVi with GPT-4 [87] (64.0%). On EgoSchema, FFS (55.6%) beats VideoAgent with GPT-4 [70] (54.1%), MoReVQA with PaLM2 [45] (51.7%), Vamos with GPT-4 [67] (48.2%), and MC-ViT-L [50] (44.4%) while processing only 16.8 frames versus 180–256 for those methods. The key efficiency advantage is that FFS adds only 0.2B parameters (40x smaller than the downstream model) and requires no expensive LLM/VLM API calls, unlike VideoAgent [70] and JCEF [45] which rely on GPT-4 or PaLM2 for iterative selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of multimodal frame selection for video QA (Buch et al. 2022, Yu et al. 2023, Gao et al. 2023) by replacing the rigid fixed-k top-k selection with a flexible, variable-size selection mechanism. It builds on the Gumbel-top-k straight-through estimator (Jang et al. 2016, Kool et al. 2019, Maddison et al. 2014) for differentiable discrete selection, and re-implements the LLoVi long-video QA framework (Zhang et al. 2024) as an open-source baseline. The key departure from prior adaptive selection work (VideoAgent, MoReVQA) is that FFS achieves adaptivity through a lightweight 0.2G-parameter [CTRL] register token rather than expensive iterative LLM/VLM API calls, making it end-to-end trainable and 40x smaller than the downstream model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deployment: a 13.5x reduction in downstream-processed frames (32→2.3) with comparable or slightly improved accuracy on NExT-QA and IntentQA, and competitive results on the long-video EgoSchema benchmark. The [CTRL] register token is an elegant, minimal architectural addition that converts inflexible top-k machinery into a controllable flexible selector without sacrificing differentiability or adding model cost. Open questions include whether this approach generalizes to open-ended video generation tasks, very long videos beyond 64 frames, and whether the auxiliary fixed-k supervision signals remain necessary at scale. The method is also limited to discriminative (multiple-choice) VideoQA in its current evaluation.
