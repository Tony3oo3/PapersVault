## Problem

Current vision-language models are fundamentally confined to Euclidean vector spaces in both their patch embeddings and attention/FFN backbones, making them unable to represent the non-Euclidean geometric structures (spherical orbits, hyperbolic gravitational fields) that govern galaxy-scale phenomena. This architectural limitation causes general-purpose VLMs to achieve R² scores below 0.12 on galaxy property estimation and F1 scores of only 0.4–0.7 on morphology classification, far below domain-specific models.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-band galaxy images (DESI-LS DR9), 1D galaxy spectra (DESI EDR), and geometric graph tokens derived from three KNN-based multi-relational graphs (105,172 nodes, 1,024-dim features each) constructed in Euclidean, hyperbolic, and spherical spaces, all aligned to the VLM token space via modality-specific projection layers. Outputs are produced through two parallel heads: a Numeric Head for continuous regression (M*, Z_MW, t_age, sSFR) and the original LM Head for 10-class morphology classification, with tasks formulated as question-answering prompts following AstroCLIP's methodology.

## Method

Galaxy Walker augments Qwen2-VL-2B with two geometry-aware modules. The Geometry Prompt generates geometric tokens by running Riemannian GraphSAGE layers (with exponential/logarithmic maps between manifolds) on multi-scale physical graphs in Euclidean, spherical, and hyperbolic spaces, injecting diverse geometric priors at the input level. The Geometry Adapter replaces standard FFN layers every 4 transformer blocks with a mixture-of-experts network containing three specialized experts: a standard Euclidean FFN, a spherical expert that normalizes outputs onto the unit sphere (capturing angular relationships), and a hyperbolic expert that operates in the Poincaré ball via exponential/logarithmic maps (capturing hierarchical structures), with a learnable gating network routing each token to the appropriate expert. Training proceeds in two stages: first the Geometry Prompt is trained independently on property estimation, then the Geometry Adapter is trained with all attention blocks frozen, using a combined LM loss and smooth-L1 regression loss.

## Results

Galaxy Walker achieves state-of-the-art R² scores across all four galaxy property estimation tasks: M* = 0.91, Z_MW = 0.69, t_age = 0.52, and sSFR = 0.84, surpassing the best domain-specific baseline AstroCLIP (Fine-tuned) by up to +0.15 on sSFR. On morphology classification, it reaches F1 scores of 0.97 (DEO), 0.96 (SPR), 0.87 (EOB), and 0.83 (BLG), with the most notable gains of +0.17 F1 on BAR and SAC over AstroCLIP. General-purpose VLMs (GPT-4o, Claude-3.5, Llama-3.2) perform poorly, with R² scores below 0.12 on most property estimation tasks and F1 scores generally below 0.65 on morphology.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest domain-specific baseline AstroCLIP (Fine-tuned), Galaxy Walker wins on all four property estimation tasks (M*: 0.91 vs 0.88; sSFR: 0.84 vs 0.69, a +0.15 gap) and on 7 of 10 morphology classes, with the largest margins on BAR (+0.17) and SAC (+0.17 F1); it ties on DEO (0.97) and SPR (0.96) and loses only on SMH (0.76 vs 0.83, −0.07). General-purpose VLMs are decisively outperformed: GPT-4o achieves R² = 0.05 on M* and −0.48 on t_age, while Llama-3.2-90B even produces negative R² (−2.99 on M*, −5.46 on Z_MW), confirming that Euclidean-only architectures cannot model galaxy-scale geometric structure. The classical MLP baseline (Photometry) is limited to property estimation only and trails Galaxy Walker by 0.24 R² on M*. Stein, a self-supervised model for morphology, is outperformed on 8 of 10 classes, with the largest gap on BAR (0.71 vs 0.37). Overall, Galaxy Walker sets new SOTA on 12 of 14 evaluated metrics, with the only regression being the SMH morphology class.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Qwen2-VL-2B VLM backbone by injecting non-Euclidean geometric priors through two novel components: a Geometry Prompt that generates tokens via Riemannian GraphSAGE random walks on multi-scale physical graphs in Euclidean, spherical, and hyperbolic spaces, and a Geometry Adapter that replaces standard FFN layers with a mixture-of-experts spanning three geometric spaces. Conceptually, it bridges geometric deep learning (Poincaré ball embeddings, spherical manifolds, Riemannian graph operators) with the VLM paradigm, departing from the assumption that patch embeddings and attention operate purely in Euclidean vector space. It positions itself against both domain-specific astronomical models (AstroCLIP, Stein) and general-purpose VLMs (GPT-4o, Claude-3.5, Llama-3.2), arguing that the latter's Euclidean inductive bias is a fundamental limitation for galaxy-scale reasoning rather than merely a data-coverage gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that general-purpose VLMs are structurally ill-suited for astronomical analysis (R² scores below 0.12, sometimes negative), providing a concrete motivation for geometry-aware architectures in scientific imaging. The +0.17 F1 gains on structurally complex morphology classes (BAR, SAC) and +0.15 R² on sSFR suggest that non-Euclidean inductive biases capture physical regularities invisible to flat-space models. However, the evaluation is confined to a single 2B-parameter base model and one dataset suite, leaving open whether the geometric MoE approach scales to larger VLMs or generalizes beyond galaxy morphology. The expert specialization analysis (hyperbolic dominance for hierarchical structures, spherical for projected 3D geometry) offers an interpretable decomposition that could guide future domain-specific VLM design, but the limited expert count and single-seed results temper the strength of these conclusions.
