## Problem

Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) training data is bottlenecked by manual curation in simulators, limiting scene diversity and real-world generalization. Existing data-centric approaches (AirBERT, ScaleVLN, YTB-VLN, NaVid) each fail to simultaneously achieve scalability in scene diversity, openness in object variety, and comprehensive geometric/spatial awareness in their spatial representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252428/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline ingests first-person room tour videos (hand-held camera, continuous walk-through) and produces two trajectory types: ~100K description-enriched trajectories with ~200K open-vocabulary instructions, and ~17K action-enriched trajectories with navigable actions from 1847 reconstructed 3D environments. Intermediate products include per-frame object tags, bounding boxes, depth maps, room-type labels, and 3D camera poses. For the downstream navigation model, input consists of a sequence of historical observation tokens (<hist>), candidate view tokens (<cand>) from spatially distinct frames, and a natural-language instruction; output is the selected next action (a specific candidate view or <STOP>).

## Method

The core contribution is an automatic, extendable pipeline that converts raw room tour videos into geometry-aware navigation training data. Frames are sampled at 1 frame/2 s to match human walking speed, then annotated with BLIP-2 (room type via discriminative VQA over 16 categories), RAM (open-vocabulary object tags), Grounding-DINO (bounding boxes), and Depth Anything (relative object-camera distances). COLMAP reconstructs 3D scenes from 3 fps clips (100 s segments with 10 s overlap, merged via a graph-based DFS procedure), yielding camera poses and scene geometry. Decision-making frames are sampled at maximal yaw-rotation points and every ~1.5 m; navigable actions are extracted via cosine-similarity thresholding, non-maximum suppression, and DBSCAN clustering of spatially close frames with different views. GPT-4-Turbo generates controllable, open-vocabulary instructions using a task-instruction + in-context-examples prompt that embeds frame captions with spatial relations. The resulting data trains NaviLLM through two stages: a summarization pretraining task (next-token prediction over trajectory descriptions) and an action-selection finetuning task (selecting the correct candidate view given history and instruction), enabling both supervised multi-task VLN and zero-shot navigation.

## Results

On the unified multi-task setting, NaviLLM+RT3D Action achieves SOON Val-U SR 31.7 / SPL 27.8, R2R Val-U SR 62.4 / SPL 62.2, and REVERIE Val-U SR 37.4 / SPL 36.4, setting new state-of-the-art on all three benchmarks. The description-enriched variant (RT3D Desc) reaches CVDN Val-U GP 6.96, the highest among all unified models. In the zero-shot setting on R2R Val Unseen, NaviLLM+RT3D attains SR 14.33 / SPL 10.86, surpassing all open-source zero-shot methods and matching NavGPT with GPT-3.5 (SR 13.89 / SPL 9.12).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the reproduced NaviLLM baseline (SR 28.0 / SPL 26.3 on SOON Val-U; SR 56.7 / SPL 60 on R2R Val-U; SR 31.4 / SPL 32.3 on REVERIE Val-U), NaviLLM+RT3D Action improves SOON SR by 3.7 points, R2R SR by 5.7 points, and REVERIE SR by 6.0 points, with consistent SPL gains across all three tasks. On CVDN, the description-enriched variant (RT3D Desc) achieves the best GP of 6.96, surpassing the original NaviLLM (6.16) and all single-task CVDN methods (best prior: VLN-PETL at 6.13). Among single-task R2R models, BEV-BERT leads with SR 64, slightly above NaviLLM+RT3D Action (62.4), but the latter is the only unified model to approach that level while simultaneously handling three other tasks. In the zero-shot R2R setting, NaviLLM+RT3D (SR 14.33) outperforms NavCoT (7.78), LangNav (0), and DuET (1) by large margins, and edges out NavGPT with GPT-3.5 (13.89) while remaining far below GPT-4-based commercial systems (MapGPT 43.7, DiscussNav 43). The paper attributes the R2R gains primarily to proximity/depth data improving spatial awareness, and the REVERIE gains to open-vocabulary object tags combined with room-type annotations that encourage layout inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on NaviLLM [63], an LLM-based generalist navigation model, as the backbone for all experiments, adapting its training paradigm (summarization pretraining + action-selection finetuning) to video-derived data. It departs from prior data-centric VLN approaches—AirBERT (web image-caption pairs), ScaleVLN (manually curated 3D scenes), and YTB-VLN (YouTube videos without explicit geometry)—by combining web room-tour videos with COLMAP 3D reconstruction to recover trajectory geometry, and by using a multi-model annotation pipeline (RAM, Grounding-DINO, Depth Anything, BLIP-2) plus GPT-4 to generate open-vocabulary, spatially-aware instructions. The key conceptual departure from YTB-VLN is the explicit recovery of 3D camera poses and the sampling of navigable actions at turning points, moving beyond template-based panoramic node instructions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RoomTour3D addresses the fundamental data-scalability bottleneck in VLN by demonstrating that web room-tour videos, augmented with 3D reconstruction and LLM-generated instructions, can serve as a large-scale training signal that improves performance across four established benchmarks simultaneously. The zero-shot result—achieving 14.33% SR on R2R without any simulator action data, matching GPT-3.5-based NavGPT—is conceptually significant as it suggests video-derived geometric trajectories can substitute for simulator-annotated actions. Open questions include the sensitivity of results to COLMAP reconstruction quality (acknowledged as variable), whether the 16-room-type taxonomy limits open-world generalization, and whether the pipeline scales to non-residential or outdoor environments. The dataset's practical value is high for the VLN community, but the supervised-task gains (5–6%) are incremental rather than transformative.
