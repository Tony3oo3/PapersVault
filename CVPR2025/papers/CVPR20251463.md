## Problem

Existing visual pretraining approaches for robotics (e.g., MAE on egocentric videos) learn only 2D representations, while state-of-the-art manipulation policies like RVT rely on explicit 3D multi-view scene understanding. This creates a gap: 2D-pretrained encoders cannot be directly transferred to 3D manipulation architectures, and training 3D policies from scratch limits generalization to novel objects and scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251463/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a point cloud of the robot workspace (captured from four RGB-D cameras at 128×128) and a language instruction. The point cloud is rendered into five orthogonal virtual RGB-D views, each with 10 channels (RGB, depth, 3D world-frame coordinates, 3D camera-frame coordinates), which are tokenized into 10×10 patches. The output is a 6-DoF end-effector pose (3D position and rotation) plus a binary gripper open/close command.

## Method

3D-MVP splits RVT's end-to-end multi-view transformer into a visual encoder E and a lightweight action decoder D, then pretrains E via masked autoencoding on large-scale 3D object datasets (Objaverse, 200K models). During pretraining, 3D objects are rendered from the same five orthogonal virtual cameras, 75% of visual tokens are randomly masked, and a separate 2-layer MAE decoder reconstructs the full multi-view RGB-D images from the encoder's latent embedding using pixel-wise L2 loss. The key insight is that jointly reconstructing all five views with varying masking patterns forces the encoder to reason across perspectives and learn 3D-aware features robust to occlusion and viewpoint change. After pretraining, the MAE decoder is discarded and the encoder is finetuned end-to-end with RVT's action decoder on manipulation demonstrations, yielding policies that generalize better to unseen objects, textures, sizes, and lighting conditions.

## Results

On RLBench (18 manipulation tasks), 3D-MVP achieves a 67.5% average success rate, outperforming RVT trained from scratch (62.9%), PerAct (49.4%), C2F-ARM-BC (20.1%), and Image-BC (1.3%). The gains are most pronounced on medium-difficulty tasks such as Insert Peg (20.0% vs. 11.2%), Put in Cupboard (60.0% vs. 49.6%), and Stack Blocks (40.0% vs. 28.8%). On COLOSSEUM, 3D-MVP is more robust than RVT across most of the 12 environmental perturbations (color, texture, size, lighting changes) and significantly outperforms 2D pretraining baselines (MVP, R3M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

3D-MVP outperforms RVT (the same architecture trained from scratch) by 4.6 points in average success rate on RLBench (67.5% vs. 62.9%), confirming that the gain comes from 3D pretraining rather than architectural changes. Against PerAct, 3D-MVP leads by 18.1 points (67.5% vs. 49.4%), and against C2F-ARM-BC and Image-BC the margin is even larger (47.4 and 66.2 points respectively). On COLOSSEUM, 3D-MVP is more robust than RVT across most of the 12 environmental perturbations, particularly for texture and size changes of the receiver object and manipulation object, as well as light color and table color variations. Both 2D pretraining baselines (MVP and R3M) perform substantially worse than the 3D methods, indicating that 2D visual pretraining is insufficient for complex 3D manipulation. The paper notes that pretraining provides limited benefit on tasks that are already near-saturated (>90% success) or completely unsolvable by the base architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the masked autoencoder (MAE) pretraining paradigm from 2D single-view images to 3D multi-view scenes, and applies it to a 3D manipulation architecture (RVT) rather than the 2D encoders (ResNet, ViT) used in prior robotics pretraining work like MVP and R3M. The key architectural departure is splitting RVT's end-to-end multi-view transformer into a visual encoder and action decoder, then pretraining the encoder on large-scale 3D object datasets (Objaverse) rendered as orthogonal RGB-D views. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate lines of work: self-supervised visual pretraining for robotics and explicit 3D scene representation for manipulation, arguing that 2D pretraining is fundamentally mismatched to 3D manipulation policies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that 3D-aware pretraining yields both higher absolute performance on RLBench and, more importantly, substantially better robustness to environmental perturbations (texture, size, lighting) on COLOSSEUM compared to 2D pretraining or training from scratch. This suggests a path toward more generalizable manipulation policies without requiring massive task-specific demonstrations. Key limitations include the fixed set of five orthogonal viewpoints (no arbitrary camera reasoning), the quasi-static scene assumption, and the continued need for labeled demonstrations per downstream task. Open questions include whether scaling to Objaverse-XL (10M+ objects) further improves performance, whether the approach extends to dynamic interactions, and whether zero-shot generalization to entirely novel tasks is achievable.
