## Problem

Existing ADAS perception systems treat driver state recognition (emotion, behavior) and traffic context recognition (scene, vehicle behavior) as isolated tasks, missing the causal interactions between them (e.g., congestion inducing anxiety that alters driving). Current multi-task learning approaches for driving further suffer from negative transfer between weakly related tasks and rely on single-modal inputs, severely limiting their ability to capture the full driving context.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250290/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 8 synchronized streams: six multi-view video sequences (front, right, left, inside cabin, driver face, driver body) and two skeletal joint streams (gesture, posture), each provided as 16 consecutive frames at 16 fps. Face and body crops are extracted from inside-view images via bounding boxes. The network simultaneously outputs four class labels — driver emotion, driver behavior, traffic context, and vehicle behavior — with a single shared loss (sum of cross-entropies) rather than per-task weighted losses.

## Method

MMTL-UniAD combines two key components. First, the Multi-axis Region Attention Network (MARNet) processes each multi-view image through a horizontal-vertical attention (sequential vertical then horizontal self-attention with 1×1 conv residual) to capture directional global context, followed by a region attention that partitions the feature map into t×t blocks, selects the top-k most similar regions per block via dot-product similarity, and applies local attention within those neighborhoods to isolate task-relevant features while suppressing irrelevant content (e.g., roadside billboards). Second, the Dual-Branch Multimodal Embedding splits the concatenated 8-modality feature vector into a task-shared branch (which groups features by source — scene, driver, joint — applies multi-scale 1×1/3×3 convolutions, and fuses groups via sigmoid-gated weighting) and a task-specific branch (which applies 1D convolutions followed by multi-head self-attention over channels to produce per-task channel-wise gating). A 3D-CNN encodes the joint streams. The two branches are dynamically fused per task via a learnable sigmoid weight that balances shared versus specific features, enabling adaptive soft parameter sharing that mitigates negative transfer while preserving cross-task knowledge transfer.

## Results

On the AIDE dataset, MMTL-UniAD achieves a mean accuracy (mAcc) of 82.30% across four tasks, outperforming all 28 baselines by 4.10%–12.09%. Per-task accuracies are 76.67% (Driver Emotion Recognition), 73.61% (Driver Behavior Recognition), 93.91% (Traffic Context Recognition), and 85.00% (Vehicle Behavior Recognition). The largest gains appear in DBR (+4.64% over the best baseline) and VBR (+3.62%), demonstrating the benefit of joint multimodal multi-task learning over single-task or single-modal approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMTL-UniAD (82.30% mAcc) outperforms the strongest 2D baseline CMT (78.16%) by 4.14%, the best 2D+Timing baseline Res18+TransE (77.17%) by 5.13%, and the best 3D baseline ShuffleNet-V1 (78.20%) by 4.10%. On individual tasks, the gains are most pronounced for Driver Behavior Recognition (73.61% vs. CMT's 68.75%, a +4.86% margin) and Vehicle Behavior Recognition (85.00% vs. 3D-Res18's 81.21%, a +3.79% margin). For Traffic Context Recognition, MMTL-UniAD edges out CMT (93.91% vs. 93.75%) and Video Swin Transformer (93.91% vs. 93.75%), while for Driver Emotion Recognition it surpasses TimeSFormer (76.67% vs. 74.87%) by 1.80%. The paper attributes these consistent wins to MARNet's multi-axis attention filtering task-irrelevant features and the dual-branch embedding's adaptive balancing of shared vs. task-specific parameters, which together mitigate the negative transfer that plagues single-modal or single-task baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends multi-task learning for ADAS by jointly optimizing driver-state tasks (emotion, behavior) and traffic-context tasks (context, vehicle behavior) within a single multimodal framework, whereas prior MTL work addresses either driver-related tasks or traffic-context tasks in isolation. The MARNet repurposes axial attention (from Axial-DeepLab) and region attention (from BiFormer, originally for segmentation) for multi-view driving perception, while the dual-branch embedding adapts soft parameter sharing concepts from MMoE to balance task-shared and task-specific feature extraction. The AIDE dataset provides the first benchmark combining all four task types with multimodal inputs, filling a gap where prior MTL-for-ADAS models relied on single-modal data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical gap in ADAS: real driving safety depends on the interplay between driver state and traffic context, yet prior systems treat them as separate problems. However, the evaluation is limited to a single small dataset (2,898 samples), and the baselines are standard backbones without MTL-specific optimization, making it unclear whether the gains would persist against MTL-tuned competitors or on larger, more diverse datasets. Open questions include whether the multi-task synergy generalizes to different task combinations, whether the architecture scales to real-time deployment on embedded hardware, and whether the negative-transfer mitigation strategy transfers to other multimodal MTL domains beyond driving.
