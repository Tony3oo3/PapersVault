## Problem

Reflective and textureless surfaces defeat standard multi-view 3D reconstruction pipelines because cross-view feature matching for pose calibration (SfM) and photometric consistency for shape recovery both fail. Existing pose-free methods rely on RGB-based correspondences or monocular depth priors that are unreliable for specular, textureless objects, creating a chicken-and-egg problem where poses are needed to reconstruct shape but shape cues are needed to estimate poses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252546/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view surface normal maps in camera space (obtainable from photometric stereo or single-image normal estimators), per-view foreground masks, and known camera intrinsics as input—no RGB images or initial pose estimates are required. It outputs a 3D surface as the zero-level set of a neural SDF network plus per-view camera extrinsics (rotation and translation). Camera poses are initialized in a circular distribution around the object rather than from any calibration procedure, and the SDF is represented by a hash-encoded MLP with volume rendering.

## Method

PMNI jointly optimizes a hash-encoded SDF network and camera poses by exploiting the fact that surface normals remain geometrically consistent across views even when RGB photometric consistency breaks down for reflective surfaces. The training loss combines five terms: a world-to-camera normal alignment loss, a depth regularization from single-view normal integration (BiNI) that anchors the SDF with per-view relative depth, a multi-view normal consistency loss that projects sampled surface points into other views and enforces agreement of camera-space normals rotated to a common world frame, a silhouette mask loss, and an Eikonal loss. The multi-view consistency term is computed dynamically at each iteration using the current pose estimates, creating a self-reinforcing loop where improving poses yield better correspondences that further refine both shape and poses. This avoids the need for reliable feature matching or learned depth priors that fail on reflective, textureless surfaces.

## Results

On the RT3D dataset (6 reflective/textureless objects, 20 views each), PMNI achieves average RPEr of 0.354° and RPEt of 0.022 for camera pose recovery, and a relative depth error of 0.014 for shape estimation — substantially outperforming all pose-free baselines. On DiLiGenT-MV with GT normals, PMNI achieves CD of 0.153 and F1-score of 0.990, comparable to SuperNormal (CD 0.142, F1 0.993) which uses calibrated poses, while being robust to pose noise that degrades SuperNormal to CD 0.775 and F1 0.410.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PMNI outperforms all four pose-free baselines by a large margin on every metric. On pose estimation (RT3D), PMNI's average RPEr of 0.354° is 8.2× lower than DUSt3R (2.920°), 24× lower than Nope-NeRF (8.463°), 10.4× lower than SPARF (3.693°), and 46× lower than CF-3DGS (16.313°); RPEt follows the same pattern (0.022 vs. 0.304/0.661/0.181/0.972). On shape (relative depth error), PMNI (0.014) beats DUSt3R (0.072) by 5×, SPARF (0.067) by 4.8×, Nope-NeRF (0.278) by 20×, and CF-3DGS (0.444) by 32×. On DiLiGenT-MV, PMNI matches SuperNormal (which uses calibrated poses) within ~8% CD and ~0.3% F1-score, while SuperNormal degrades catastrophically under small pose noise (CD jumps from 0.142 to 0.775). The gap is most dramatic for CF-3DGS and Nope-NeRF, which rely on temporal continuity or monocular depth priors that fail on reflective/textureless surfaces, whereas DUSt3R and SPARF, which use learned correspondences, are the closest competitors but still lag by an order of magnitude in pose accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

PMNI builds on the NeuS [24] SDF + volume rendering framework and the BiNI [4] bilateral normal integration technique, but departs fundamentally from both RGB-based reflective reconstruction methods (NeRO, Ref-NeuS) and existing pose-free approaches (DUSt3R, SPARF, Nope-NeRF, CF-3DGS). The key conceptual shift is replacing RGB images—which suffer from view-dependent reflectance on specular surfaces—with multi-view surface normal maps, which remain geometrically consistent at corresponding 3D points regardless of lighting or reflectance. This connects the photometric stereo literature (SDM-UniPS) as a front-end for generating the input normals, while the joint pose+shape optimization borrows the dynamic correspondence idea from pose-free NeRF methods but applies it to normals rather than radiance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PMNI removes the checkerboard/ArUco calibration requirement that has limited reflective surface reconstruction to controlled lab settings, making casual capture of reflective objects (metallic sculptures, jewelry, polished artifacts) feasible. However, the practical capture pipeline still demands 11 images per view under varying illumination for photometric stereo, which is a non-trivial setup that limits true 'casual' use. The RT3D dataset, while small (6 objects), provides the first ground-truth benchmark specifically for pose-free reflective reconstruction. Open questions include whether the approach extends to larger scenes, whether fewer light positions suffice, and whether the method can handle non-reflective textureless objects where photometric stereo is less informative.
