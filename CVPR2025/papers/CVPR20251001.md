## Problem

Membership inference attacks on image captioning models have only been explored in simulated settings using computationally expensive shadow models (Hu et al., 2022), with no practical, shadow-free attack demonstrated on real-world, large-scale operational models. Existing shadow-free MIAs target CLIP-style contrastive models rather than generative captioning models, and none operate under the realistic constraint that the attacker has access only to images without ground-truth captions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251001/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The attacker supplies only images as input to the target captioning model in a black-box, query-only setting; no ground-truth captions or model internals are available. For each image, the target model generates n captions (n=40 in experiments) via top-p sampling, which are then encoded by a pre-trained text encoder into embedding vectors. The final output is a binary membership prediction (member vs. non-member of the training set), evaluated via AUC, accuracy, and TPR at 1% FPR.

## Method

The core insight is that images in the training set produce more consistent (lower-variance) generated captions because the model has memorized them. The paper introduces the means-of-variance (MV) metric, which computes per-feature variance across n caption embeddings for each image and then averages these variances into a single scalar. Two attacks are built on this metric: MVTA (zero-knowledge) classifies an image as a member if its MV score falls below a threshold τ derived from the non-member distribution; C-WSA (partial-knowledge) uses the MV metric to flag pseudo-members from a candidate pool, then trains an image-feature classifier on the combined non-member and pseudo-member set. C-WSA introduces a confidence-threshold training step where, after a warm-up phase, pseudo-member samples are included in each training batch only if the model's predicted membership confidence exceeds a set threshold, thereby reducing the negative impact of mislabeled pseudo-members on classifier accuracy.

## Results

In the zero-knowledge setting, MVTA outperforms the CSA baseline across all models, achieving AUC of 0.66 (BLIP), 0.77 (ViT-GPT2), and 0.76 (GIT) on MSCOCO, compared to CSA's 0.54, 0.72, and 0.59 respectively. In the partial-knowledge setting, C-WSA achieves AUC of 0.91 (BLIP), 0.93 (ViT-GPT2), and 0.93 (GIT) on MSCOCO, surpassing WSA's 0.84, 0.89, and 0.86. On TPR at FPR=1%, C-WSA reaches 0.38–0.43 across models versus WSA's 0.31–0.38, representing roughly 20–30% relative improvement. On TextCaps [GIT], C-WSA still leads with AUC 0.897 vs. WSA's 0.817, though MVTA underperforms CSA on TPR@FPR=1% (0.008 vs. 0.011).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines from Ko et al. (ICCV 2023): the Cosine Similarity Attack (CSA) in the zero-knowledge setting and the Weakly Supervised Attack (WSA) in the partial-knowledge setting. MVTA consistently beats CSA on AUC across all four model-dataset combinations (e.g., 0.66 vs. 0.54 on BLIP/MSCOCO, 0.76 vs. 0.59 on GIT/MSCOCO), with the largest gap on GIT (+0.17 AUC). The sole exception is TextCaps [GIT] TPR@FPR=1%, where CSA slightly edges out MVTA (0.011 vs. 0.008). C-WSA dominates WSA on every metric and every configuration: AUC improvements range from +0.06 (BLIP/MSCOCO) to +0.08 (GIT/MSCOCO and TextCaps/GIT), while TPR@FPR=1% gains are +0.07 to +0.04. The confidence-threshold training step in C-WSA is the key differentiator, reducing mislabeling noise in the pseudo-member set and yielding the most consistent gains on high-precision metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the shadow-free MIA line initiated by Ko et al. (WSA/CSA for CLIP models) to the image captioning domain, replacing the cosine-similarity metric with a novel means-of-variance (MV) metric that captures output diversity across multiple generated captions. It departs from the only prior MIA work on image captioning (Hu et al., M4I) by eliminating the need for computationally expensive shadow models and by requiring only image inputs rather than ground-truth captions. The confidence-threshold training step in C-WSA is a new adaptation of the weakly-supervised paradigm, addressing the label-noise problem inherent in pseudo-member sets that prior WSA formulations ignored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that widely deployed, large-scale image captioning models (BLIP, ViT-GPT2, GIT) are practically vulnerable to membership inference using only image queries, raising concrete privacy concerns for organizations deploying such models on proprietary data. The MV metric is presented as a general-purpose tool for any embedding-based setting, suggesting broader applicability beyond captioning. Open questions include developing effective mitigations against these attacks and extending the variance-based approach to other generative modalities (e.g., diffusion models), which the authors note would only require swapping encoders. A limitation is that the attacks still require multiple queries per image (n=40 captions), which may be impractical in strictly rate-limited API settings.
