## Problem

Existing face security methods for deepfake detection and face anti-spoofing are task-specific, rely on expensive supervised annotations or synthetic forgery data, and initialize backbones from natural-image (ImageNet) pretraining that lacks facial representations. This paper asks whether a single self-supervised pretraining framework on unlabeled real faces can learn a universal representation that transfers well across multiple face security tasks with superior cross-dataset generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250391/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Pretraining consumes 224×224 real face images cropped via DLIB and segmented by the FACER face parser into predefined regions (eyes, nose, mouth, hair, skin, background, etc.), with no data augmentation applied. The CRFR-P strategy outputs both a binary image mask M and a facial region mask M_fr that fully covers one randomly selected non-skin/background region. After pretraining, the vanilla ViT-B/16 encoder E_o produces a facial representation embedding; a lightweight task-specific head is appended for downstream real/fake or live/spoof classification on video frames or single images.

## Method

FSFM jointly trains a masked autoencoder (MIM) and a BYOL-style self-distillation network (ID) on unlabeled real faces using a novel CRFR-P masking strategy that covers one random facial region entirely and proportionally masks patches across all remaining regions, simultaneously enforcing intra-region Consistency and inter-region Coherency. The MIM objective combines a standard MSE reconstruction loss on all masked patches with an additional weighted loss exclusively on the fully-masked facial region to prevent trivial within-region shortcuts. The ID network uses an asymmetric design: the online branch processes the CRFR-P masked view while the target branch (updated via EMA) processes the full unmasked view, with 2-layer ViT representation decoders in both branches creating a disentangled feature space aligned by an asymmetric negative cosine similarity loss. These three objectives (3C: Consistency, Coherency, Correspondence) are combined into a single pretraining loss, and after 400 epochs the online encoder alone serves as a universal backbone for cross-dataset deepfake detection, cross-domain face anti-spoofing, and unseen diffusion forgery detection.

## Results

On cross-dataset deepfake detection (trained on FF++, tested on 4 unseen datasets), FSFM achieves video-level AUC of 91.44/83.47/89.71/86.96 on CDFV2/DFDC/DFDCP/WDF, outperforming all base models by 6.9–21.9 points on average. On cross-domain face anti-spoofing (LOO protocol over 4 datasets), FSFM attains average HTER of 4.81% and AUC of 98.68%, surpassing the best SOTA FAS method (GAC-FAS: 6.52% HTER, 97.19% AUC). On the DiFF diffusion forgery benchmark, FSFM achieves 70.99% average AUC (excluding FF++), dramatically exceeding the next-best baseline DINO (48.35%) and all other pretrained models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FSFM outperforms all five base-model initializations (ViT Scratch, ViT Sup(IN), MAE, DINO, MCF) on every DfD test dataset, with the largest gap over ViT Scratch (21.9 pts avg) and a consistent 6.9–8.8 pt margin over the strongest baselines (ViT Sup(IN), MAE, DINO, MCF). Notably, FSFM is pretrained on only 3.3M VGGFace2 images yet surpasses MCF, which uses 20M facial images and also combines MIM+ID, indicating that facial security representations differ fundamentally from general face analysis. Against SOTA specialized DfD methods, FSFM wins on DFDC, DFDCP, and WDF but trails LAA-Net/SBI on CDFV2 (91.44 vs 95.40), where synthetic blending artifacts align with that method's training distribution. On FAS, FSFM (4.81% avg HTER) beats all 12 SOTA domain-generalization methods, including GAC-FAS (6.52%) and UDG-FAS (8.15%), using only a standard cross-entropy loss without any DG technique. On the DiFF benchmark, FSFM's 70.99% average AUC exceeds DINO (48.35%) by over 22 points, while all other pretrained models (MAE, MCF, ViT Sup(IN)) perform near or below random, confirming that generic visual or face-analysis representations fail on unseen diffusion forgeries.

## Relation to Prior Work

FSFM builds directly on the MAE architecture for masked image modeling and the BYOL-style asymmetric self-distillation for instance discrimination, combining them in the spirit of recent natural-image SSL that shows MIM and ID are complementary. It departs from prior facial SSL (MCF, FaRL, MARLIN) that target normal face analysis (expression, attributes, alignment) by explicitly learning 'realness' representations sensitive to forgery and spoof anomalies, and from task-specific DfD/FAS methods that rely on supervised training or hand-crafted forgery/spoof cues. The CRFR-P masking strategy is a face-domain adaptation of the Fasking idea from MARLIN, but extends it with proportional masking to enforce both intra-region consistency and inter-region coherency simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a single self-supervised pretrained ViT can serve as a universal backbone across three distinct face security tasks (cross-dataset DfD, cross-domain FAS, and unseen diffusion forgery detection) without task-specific architectures, outperforming methods that employ depth maps, adversarial training, or forgery-specific augmentations. The 'free lunch' scaling result (0.1M → 0.6M → 3.3M images yielding large gains) suggests that collecting more unlabeled real faces is a practical path to improved security. Open questions include whether the external face-parser dependency can be eliminated, whether the approach scales to larger ViT variants or 3D face attacks, and whether the still-modest DiFF AUCs (61–79%) can be closed with more diverse pretraining data or task-aware fine-tuning.
