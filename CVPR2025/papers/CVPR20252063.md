## Problem

Existing point cloud adversarial attacks rely on surrogate classifiers (white-box gradients or black-box queries) for iterative perturbation optimization, causing overfitting to specific model parameters and poor cross-network transferability. The paper asks whether highly transferable adversarial point clouds can be generated in a no-box setting—without any classifier access or model queries—and without iterative optimization, by instead exploiting intrinsic geometric properties of the data distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252063/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a point cloud dataset (e.g., ModelNet40 or ShapeNetPart) and a pre-trained autoencoder (PointFlow or PointDiffusion) providing both encoder and decoder. It produces adversarial point clouds in a single forward pass: the encoder maps each point cloud to a latent feature vector, the OT-based attack modifies the feature, and the decoder reconstructs the adversarial point cloud. No surrogate classifier, gradient queries, or iterative refinement are required at any stage; the entire pipeline is end-to-end and classifier-free.

## Method

NoPain formulates the attack as a generative task grounded in semi-discrete optimal transport (SDOT). It solves for the Brenier height vector h by minimizing a convex energy function via Monte-Carlo sampling and Adam updates (Algorithm 1), yielding a set of hyperplanes whose upper envelope defines the Brenier potential over the noise domain. Singular boundaries—where the potential is non-differentiable because multiple hyperplanes tie for the maximum—are identified by computing dihedral angles between neighboring hyperplanes and flagging pairs exceeding a threshold τ. Adversarial features are then generated in one step by taking a distance-weighted convex combination of the two features straddling each singular boundary (Eq. 8), effectively sampling in the mode-mixed region. The resulting features are decoded by the pre-trained autoencoder decoder to produce the final adversarial point clouds, requiring no classifier supervision or iterative optimization.

## Results

On ModelNet40, NoPain-PD achieves 100% ASR on PointNet, 82.8% on PointConv, 88.7% on DGCNN, and 85.7% on PCT, with Chamfer Distance (CD) around 0.0022–0.0027. On ShapeNetPart, NoPain-PD reaches 71.1% ASR on PointNet and 75.0% on DGCNN. The average generation time (AGT) is 0.026 s per sample for NoPain-PD and 0.028 s for NoPain-PF, compared to 6.2–51.5 s for baselines, representing a 200–2000× speedup. Perturbation magnitudes (CD) remain comparable to baselines (~0.002–0.003), confirming that the high ASR is not achieved at the cost of large distortions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NoPain-PD dominates on ModelNet40, achieving 100% ASR on PointNet versus 54.5% for the strongest baseline SI-ADV, and 88.7% on DGCNN versus 67.3%. The one notable exception is PCT on ModelNet40, where SI-ADV reaches 91.3% ASR compared to NoPain-PD's 85.7%, likely because PCT's transformer architecture shares structural similarities with the surrogate PointNet++. On ShapeNetPart, NoPain-PD leads on PointNet (71.1% vs. 19.1% for SI-ADV) and DGCNN (75.0% vs. 18.9%), though SI-ADV retains an edge on PointConv (77.2% vs. 63.3%). All baselines require 6–51 s per sample, while NoPain completes in under 0.03 s—a 200–2000× speedup—without sacrificing perturbation magnitude (CD stays in the 0.002–0.003 range). The white-box HiT-ADV shows the worst transferability (9.2–50.2% ASR on ModelNet40) and the largest CD (0.006–0.033), confirming that model-specific optimization harms both transferability and imperceptibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes semi-discrete optimal transport (from An et al., ICLR 2020) and the geometric hyperplane/polygon decomposition of data manifolds (from Lei et al., 2020) in a fundamentally new way: rather than using OT as a distribution-alignment tool (as in prior OT-based image attacks by Han et al. and Labarbarie et al.), it uses the OT mapping's non-differentiability to locate singular boundaries of the data manifold itself. This departs from the dominant adversarial attack paradigm—whether white-box gradient-based or black-box iterative optimization—by eliminating any dependence on a surrogate classifier. The 'no-box' attack setting has been explored for images and skeletons (Li et al., Sun et al., Lu et al.), but this is the first application to 3D point clouds, leveraging the geometric structure of the latent feature space via pre-trained autoencoders (PointFlow, PointDiffusion).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: NoPain generates adversarial samples in ~0.03 seconds per sample (vs. 6–51 s for baselines) while achieving 97–100% ASR across five diverse target architectures, all without any model access or iterative optimization. This opens a path toward adversarial testing of 3D perception systems in settings where model weights are proprietary or inaccessible. Key limitations include the dependence on a well-trained autoencoder whose latent space quality directly affects boundary detection, and the fact that the 'no-box' guarantee is qualified by this autoencoder requirement. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other modalities (meshes, voxel grids), how sensitive it is to autoencoder architecture choice, and whether the singular-boundary formulation can be extended to multi-class or open-set scenarios.
