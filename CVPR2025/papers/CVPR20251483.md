## Problem

Existing diffusion-based video restoration methods rely on full-attention U-Net architectures that impose strict resolution constraints and require expensive patch-based sampling with large spatial-temporal overlaps, making them impractically slow for long, high-resolution videos. No prior work has demonstrated a scalable diffusion transformer capable of handling arbitrary video lengths and resolutions in a single forward pass without tiled inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251483/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

SeedVR accepts a low-quality video clip of arbitrary length and resolution (not required to be multiples of the window size) along with a text prompt, and outputs a temporally consistent high-quality restored video. The input is first compressed by a causal video VAE with 4× temporal and 8× spatial compression into a 16-channel latent, and the text is encoded by three frozen text encoders (CLIP, T5, and mT5). The model is evaluated at 720p on both synthetic benchmarks (SPMCS, UDM10, REDS30, YouHQ40) and real-world/AIGC data (VideoLQ, AIGC38).

## Method

The core architecture is Swin-MMDiT, which replaces the full self-attention in SD3's MMDiT block with a shifted window attention mechanism using a large 5×64×64 window in the 8×-compressed latent space, combined with variable-sized windows near spatial and temporal boundaries to accommodate arbitrary input dimensions. A 3D rotary position embedding (RoPE) is applied within each window to eliminate resolution bias, and the NaViT flattening scheme enables Flash Attention computation without complex 3D masking. A from-scratch causal video VAE (250.6M params) with 4× temporal compression and 16 latent channels provides efficient encoding while achieving the lowest rFVD among compared VAEs. Training starts from SD3-Medium (2.2B params) and uses a progressive strategy (5 frames @ 256² → 9 @ 512² → 21 @ 768²), mixed image-video data (10M images + 5M videos), precomputed latents, and noise injection into the LQ condition to bridge the synthetic-to-real degradation gap.

## Results

SeedVR achieves the best DOVER score on all six benchmarks, reaching 10.508 on SPMCS, 10.537 on UDM10, 6.673 on REDS30, 12.788 on YouHQ40, 8.009 on VideoLQ, and 13.424 on AIGC38. On perceptual quality metrics, SeedVR attains the lowest LPIPS (0.341 on SPMCS, 0.231 on UDM10, 0.298 on YouHQ40) and DISTS (0.141, 0.116, 0.138, 0.118) across the four synthetic benchmarks, and the best MUSIQ (67.45) and CLIP-IQA (0.635) on YouHQ40. On pixel-fidelity metrics (PSNR, SSIM), SeedVR trails RealViFormer by 1–3 dB, consistent with the known trade-off of diffusion-based methods favoring perceptual over pixel-level quality. Despite 2.48B parameters, SeedVR is over 2× faster than VEnhancer and Upscale-A-Video in inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SeedVR dominates on perceptual and video-quality metrics: it achieves the best DOVER on all six benchmarks and the best LPIPS/DISTS on four of four synthetic sets (SPMCS, UDM10, YouHQ40, and AIGC38), outperforming the strongest diffusion-based baselines VEnhancer (DOVER 7.807 on SPMCS) and Upscale-A-Video (DOVER 9.238 on UDM10) by 1.3–2.3 points. On pixel-fidelity metrics (PSNR, SSIM), RealViFormer [77] consistently leads (e.g., 24.19 PSNR on SPMCS vs. SeedVR's 22.37), reflecting the known trade-off where diffusion-based methods prioritize perceptual realism over pixel accuracy. On REDS30, MGLD-VSR [64] and RealViFormer are particularly strong (trained on the parent REDS dataset), yet SeedVR still secures the best DOVER (6.673 vs. 6.544). On the real-world VideoLQ benchmark, results are more mixed: VEnhancer edges out SeedVR on DOVER (8.719 vs. 8.009) and Real-ESRGAN leads on MUSIQ (60.45 vs. 54.41), suggesting room for improvement on in-the-wild degradations. In the VAE comparison (Table 2), SeedVR's causal video VAE achieves the lowest rFVD (1.85), 69.5% lower than the second-best (CV-VAE/SD3 at 6.50), and the best LPIPS (0.0517), confirming superior reconstruction quality over SD2.1, VEnhancer, Cosmos, and CogVideoX VAEs.

## Relation to Prior Work

SeedVR departs from the dominant U-Net + full-attention paradigm in diffusion-based video restoration (VEnhancer, Upscale-A-Video, MGLD-VSR) by adopting a DiT backbone (SD3's MMDiT) with shifted window attention borrowed from Swin Transformer, combined with NaViT-style flattening to handle arbitrary resolutions. The causal video VAE builds on the CVVAE design from Yu et al., and the training strategy (mixed image-video data, progressive resolution scaling, noise-injected conditions) follows practices established in SD3 and Upscale-A-Video. The key conceptual departure is replacing full attention with a 64×64 latent-space window (equivalent to 512×512 pixels) plus 3D RoPE and variable-sized boundary windows, eliminating the need for the expensive overlapping patch-based sampling that plagues prior diffusion VR methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SeedVR addresses the two most practical bottlenecks in diffusion-based video restoration—resolution constraints and inference speed—by showing that a 2.48B-parameter window-attention DiT can be 2× faster than smaller U-Net-based methods while matching or exceeding their perceptual quality. This opens the door to real-time or near-real-time restoration of long, high-resolution videos that were previously infeasible. The authors acknowledge remaining limitations in sampling efficiency and robustness, and the consistent PSNR/SSIM gap versus non-diffusion baselines (RealViFormer, MGLD-VSR) highlights the ongoing fidelity-perception trade-off in generative restoration. As the largest diffusion transformer for generic VR, it sets a new architectural template that smaller labs can potentially adopt for their own restoration pipelines.
