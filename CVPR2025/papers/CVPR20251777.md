## Problem

Monocular geometry estimation from a single image is highly ill-posed, and existing approaches either decouple depth and camera intrinsics estimation (causing error propagation) or use scale-invariant point maps that suffer from focal-distance ambiguity during training. Critically, the design of training supervision for point map estimation has been largely neglected: global alignment solvers are either sensitive to outliers or rely on coarse approximations, and local, region-specific geometry supervision is entirely absent.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image (trained on aspect ratios 1:2 to 2:1, 250K–500K pixels) and directly outputs an affine-invariant 3D point map of shape H×W×3, where each pixel is mapped to a free 3D point agnostic to global scale and 3D shift. From this point map, a scale-invariant depth map, camera focal length (or FOV), and a valid-region mask (excluding sky/infinity regions) are derived via a fast iterative projection solver (~3 ms, ≤10 iterations). The X/Y axes of the point map coordinate frame are aligned with the image u/v axes, and the principal point is assumed at image center with square pixels.

## Method

MoGe uses a DINOv2-pretrained ViT-Large encoder with a lightweight CNN upsampler decoder to predict an affine-invariant point map, where the key representation choice (over DUSt3R's scale-invariant maps) eliminates focal-distance ambiguity by allowing both a global scale and a Z-axis shift to absorb inconsistencies between prediction and ground truth. Training supervision is the paper's central contribution: a Robust, Optimal, Efficient (ROE) global alignment solver computes the optimal L1 scale-and-shift parameters in O(N² log N) time via parallelized 1-D subproblems (exploiting the fact that the optimum occurs at a breakpoint), with residual truncation for outlier robustness. A multi-scale local geometry loss applies independent ROE alignment to spherical neighborhoods at three scales (1/4, 1/16, 1/64 of the image diagonal) to resolve ambiguous relative distances between objects. Additional losses supervise surface normals (angle difference) and a binary valid-region mask. The model is trained on ~9 million frames from 21 diverse datasets with tailored loss combinations per data source.

## Results

On the primary point map estimation task (affine-invariant), MoGe achieves an average Rel^p of 6.07% and δ1^p of 94.7% across eight diverse datasets, outperforming the next-best method UniDepth (9.43% / 90.5%) by ~36% in relative error. For local geometry (object-region point maps), MoGe reduces average Rel^p to 5.50% vs. 7.97% for DUSt3R, a ~30% improvement. On depth estimation (affine-invariant depth), MoGe achieves 4.72% Rel^d and 95.8% δ1^d, and on camera FOV estimation it reaches a mean error of 2.91° and median of 2.21°, beating all prior methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoGe outperforms all 12 baselines across every task and metric. Against the closest point-map competitors, UniDepth [38] achieves 9.43% average Rel^p (affine-invariant) vs. MoGe's 6.07%, and DUSt3R [52] reaches 11.4% vs. 6.07%; LeReS [64] trails most significantly at 18.1%. On depth estimation, MoGe (4.72% Rel^d, affine-invariant) beats Marigold [27] (8.41%), GeoWizard [18] (8.44%), Metric3D V2 [24] (6.33%), DA V2 [61] (9.09%), and MiDaS V3.1 [5] (8.13% disparity). For camera FOV, MoGe (2.91° mean) surpasses DUSt3R (4.06°), Perspective Fields [26] (9.86°), and WildCam [70] (7.00°), with UniDepth (10.1°) and LeReS (15.4°) far behind. The paper reports >35% error reduction over the previous best point-cloud method, 20–30% over prior MDE methods, and >20% over prior FOV estimators.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DUSt3R's paradigm of directly predicting free 3D point maps from images (bypassing explicit camera parameter estimation) by shifting from scale-invariant to affine-invariant point maps, which resolves the focal-distance ambiguity that degrades training. It departs from the dominant depth-then-unproject pipeline (LeReS, UniDepth) and from prior monocular depth estimation methods that rely on coarse median-based or range-normalization alignment for supervision. The core methodological contribution lies in training supervision rather than architecture: a robust, optimal, and efficient (ROE) alignment solver and a multi-scale local geometry loss, both applied on top of a standard DINOv2-pretrained ViT encoder with a CNN decoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying point map, depth, and camera intrinsics prediction in a single model with strong zero-shot generalization across eight diverse domains, MoGe positions itself as a practical foundational model for downstream 3D tasks (novel view synthesis, 3D-aware editing, scene understanding). The key open question is whether the affine-invariant formulation can be extended to recover metric scale without additional sensors, and whether the supervision techniques transfer to multi-view or video settings. A limitation is that the model still cannot resolve absolute scale, and the focal-distance ambiguity, while mitigated by the affine-invariant representation, is not fully eliminated for extreme focal lengths.
