## Problem

Extremely high-density crowds (≥5 p/m²) exhibit violently chaotic, active-matter-like dynamics that are under-studied in computer vision. Existing methods either rely on individual trajectory data unavailable in noisy CCTV footage, or are black-box data-driven models lacking interpretability and the ability to simulate counterfactual scenarios. No learnable physics model exists that can capture the stochastic, self-propelled behavior of dense crowds directly from in-the-wild video.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single optical flow frame (estimated via DenseFlow from consecutive video frames) as input, treating each flow vector as a noisy observation of the underlying continuum velocity field. It produces a predicted velocity field and corresponding optical flow at a future time step, with prediction horizons ranging from 1.6 s (Hellfest) to 8.2 s (evacuation drills) depending on dataset dynamics. Because the model is a continuous-time PDE solver, it requires only one input frame rather than a sequence, and the output can be evaluated at arbitrary time offsets.

## Method

The core contribution is a neural stochastic differential equation system that models high-density crowds as an active-matter continuum solved via a modified Material Point Method (CrowdMPM), where Lagrangian particles represent individuals rather than mere collocation points. A new 'crowd material' stress combines a weakly-compressible Cauchy stress (elastic asymmetry), a logarithmic inter-particle repulsive force within a comfort distance (exponential resistance), and a compression-dominance term that decouples compression from shear/rotation. Active self-propulsion is modeled via the Toner-Tu equation, with the alignment parameter α learned by a neural network and the remaining stochastic terms parameterized by a Conditional VAE decoder. All material parameters (Young's modulus ε, repulsion constant k, α, and the active-force decoder) are learned end-to-end via CConv+FC networks conditioned on local particle neighborhoods, trained with Adam to minimize the MSE between predicted and observed optical flows.

## Results

The proposed CrowdMPM model achieves the best Err_vel (velocity field MSE) on 5 out of 6 datasets: Drill1 (0.528), Drill2 (1.072), Drill3 (1.646), Hellfest (3.046), and Marathon (1.493). On Drill2, it delivers up to 18.69% and 12.11% improvement in Err_vel and Err_flow respectively over the next-best method. The only exception is Hajj, where SimVP slightly outperforms the proposed method (0.621 vs. 0.659 Err_vel), attributed to the relatively simple, slow circular motion in that scene. The model also requires only a single optical flow frame as input, unlike sequence-based baselines that need multiple frames.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines on Err_vel for Drill1, Drill2, Drill3, Hellfest, and Marathon. Against the closest competitor TAU, it wins on Drill2 (1.072 vs. 2.869), Drill3 (1.646 vs. 2.655), Hellfest (3.046 vs. 5.389), and Marathon (1.493 vs. 1.902), while TAU edges it slightly on Drill1 (0.533 vs. 0.528). The physics-informed HINN baseline is competitive on Drill1 and Drill2 but degrades sharply on Hellfest (7.143 vs. 3.046) and Marathon (4.349 vs. 1.493), likely because it lacks the active-matter self-propulsion modeling. SimVP, a pure video-prediction method, is the only baseline that beats the proposed method, but only on Hajj (0.621 vs. 0.659 Err_vel), a scene with simple, slow circular motion where data-driven sequence prediction suffices. PredFlow, designed for scene parsing, fails catastrophically on Hajj (2246.6 Err_vel) and Marathon (122.86), confirming its unsuitability for high-density crowd dynamics. The standard MPM physics baseline (Baseline I) is consistently outperformed, validating that the learned crowd material stress and active forces are necessary beyond a fixed-elasticity continuum model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three distinct research threads: the Material Point Method from computational graphics (where particles serve as collocation points), the Toner-Tu equation from active matter physics (modeling self-propelled particles with stochastic forces), and differentiable physics / physics-informed neural networks (learning PDE parameters via backpropagation). The key departure is reinterpreting MPM particles as actual individuals rather than integration points, and introducing a novel 'crowd material' stress model with elastic asymmetry, exponential resistance, and compression dominance—properties absent from standard continuum mechanics. Unlike prior hybrid crowd models (social-force + NN, hydrodynamics-informed NNs) designed for low-density trajectories, this work formulates the problem entirely in the Eulerian velocity-field space learned from optical flow, making individual tracking unnecessary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first learnable physics model specifically targeting extremely high-density crowds (≥5 p/m²) from in-the-wild video, filling a gap where neither empirical models (unlearnable) nor data-driven methods (uninterpretable, no simulation capability) adequately operate. The ability to simulate 'what-if' scenarios (wider exits, added obstacles) with a trained model has direct practical value for public safety planning. However, significant limitations remain: single-view input restricts generalization, complex social behaviors (queuing, grouping, lane formation) are unmodeled, and the model is explicitly unsuitable for low-density crowds—no unified density-spanning model exists. The cross-behavior generalization degrades sharply with competitiveness, suggesting the learned active-force distribution may not transfer across qualitatively different crowd states.
