## Problem

Generalizable NeRF methods that synthesize novel views from unknown scenes degrade significantly when given only 1–3 input views, producing over-smoothed textures and artifacts in occluded or reflective regions. Existing approaches rely almost exclusively on local geometric cues along epipolar lines, which lack a global perceptual framework to resolve depth ambiguities and maintain cross-view consistency under sparse observations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251288/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes N posed RGB source views (N ∈ {1, 2, 3, 10}) with their camera intrinsics and extrinsics, extracting multi-scale features at 1/4, 1/8, and 1/16 resolutions via a shared-weight FPN. It outputs a synthesized novel view image by predicting per-ray colors from arbitrary camera positions and directions. During inference, 128 uniform samples plus 64 adaptively sampled points are cast per ray, and the final pixel color is decoded by a small MLP from the aggregated ray representation.

## Method

GoLF-NRT is a coarse-to-fine neural rendering transformer with two complementary stages. First, a 3D transformer with near-linear sparse attention (intra-view block, intra-view grid, and inter-view attention, adapted from MaxViT) encodes 1/8-resolution features from all source views into a scene representation, which a decoder transformer then queries per target ray to produce a global context feature. Second, a GNT-style view transformer aggregates 1/4-resolution features along epipolar lines, but uses the global context feature as its initial query (replacing max pooling) so that source-view contributions are assessed in a scene-aware manner; the resulting local geometric feature is concatenated with the global feature and passed through 8 alternating view-ray transformer blocks. A key sampling innovation transforms ray-transformer attention weights into a smooth probability density function via Gaussian kernel regression, then uses inverse-transform sampling to concentrate refined samples near likely object surfaces, bridging the gap between feature-similarity weights and physically plausible volume-rendering PDFs.

## Results

On the 3-view setting, GoLF-NRT achieves 24.20 dB PSNR / 0.821 SSIM / 0.148 LPIPS on LLFF, 24.30 / 0.916 / 0.097 on Blender, and 25.06 / 0.765 / 0.207 on Shiny, outperforming the strongest baselines (CaesarNeRF, GNT, EVE-NeRF) on most metrics. In the extreme 1-view setting, GoLF-NRT reaches 18.96 dB PSNR on Shiny, surpassing CaesarNeRF by 1.39 dB and GNT by 2.97 dB. Under the 10-view many-shot setting, GoLF-NRT attains 29.74 dB PSNR on Blender and 28.01 dB on Shiny, ranking first or second across all three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GoLF-NRT consistently outperforms all baselines in the 2-view and 1-view settings across all three datasets, with the largest margins on Shiny (e.g., +1.39 dB PSNR over CaesarNeRF and +2.97 dB over GNT at 1 view). In the 3-view setting, GoLF-NRT leads on LLFF (24.20 vs. 24.07 MVSGaussian) and Shiny (25.06 vs. 24.11 EVE-NeRF), but GNT retains a PSNR advantage on Blender (25.80 vs. 24.30), though GoLF-NRT still wins on SSIM and LPIPS there. Against the closest few-shot competitor CaesarNeRF, GoLF-NRT gains 0.75 dB PSNR and 0.027 SSIM on LLFF at 3 views, and the gap widens to 1.39 dB on Shiny at 1 view. In the 10-view many-shot regime (Table 2), GoLF-NRT achieves the best PSNR on Blender (29.74) and Shiny (28.01) but trails EVE-NeRF on LLFF PSNR (26.42 vs. 27.16), while still leading on LPIPS (0.109 vs. 0.141). Overall, the global-context-guided aggregation and kernel-regression adaptive sampling yield the most consistent gains in the few-shot regime, which is the paper's primary target.

## Relation to Prior Work

GoLF-NRT builds directly on GNT's fully attention-based view/ray transformer architecture for epipolar feature aggregation, and on MaxViT's multi-axis sparse attention for efficient 3D encoding. It departs from the local-geometry-only paradigm of GNT and EVE-NeRF by introducing a global context module (a 3D transformer with near-linear sparse attention) that provides a coarse scene-level representation, and from CaesarNeRF's uniform semantic conditioning by using the global context as a per-ray query that guides local epipolar aggregation. The adaptive sampling strategy, which converts attention weights into a regularized PDF via kernel regression, is a novel bridge between transformer attention and classical volume rendering that has no direct precedent in the generalizable NeRF literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Few-shot view synthesis remains a critical bottleneck for real-world deployment of neural rendering, and GoLF-NRT demonstrates that combining global scene understanding with local geometric reasoning yields consistent gains across 1–10 input views on both synthetic and real datasets. The 6× FLOPs reduction from the sparse 3D transformer makes high-resolution generalizable rendering more practical. Open questions include whether the approach extends to unbounded or dynamic scenes, whether the kernel regression bandwidth can be learned end-to-end, and whether the global context module can be further compressed for real-time inference on mobile devices.
