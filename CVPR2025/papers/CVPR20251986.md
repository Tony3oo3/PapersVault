## Problem

Existing debiasing techniques for text-to-image diffusion models rely on additional training (fine-tuning, prompt tuning, concept editing), imposing high computational costs and risking degradation of core image generation quality. This paper asks whether training is truly necessary, hypothesizing that pre-trained Stable Diffusion already contains structured 'minority regions' in its noise space that can be exploited for training-free bias reduction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251986/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt (e.g., 'A photo of a CEO') and produces a single RGB image at the native resolution of the underlying SD version (512×512 for SD1.5/SD2, 1024×1024 for SDXL/SD3). At inference time, a target attribute is sampled uniformly from a pre-defined set (e.g., male/female, or racial categories), and the weak attribute guidance is injected only during the initial τ denoising steps before alternating back to the original text condition for the remainder of the diffusion process.

## Method

The key insight, validated by a proposed 'mode test' (adding Gaussian noise to minority-attribute images and denoising with a neutral prompt), is that minority-attribute noises cluster into coherent regions rather than being sparsely scattered. The method constructs a weak text embedding by computing an attribute direction a_k = φ(k) − φ(∅) via the CLIP text encoder and adding it only to embedding positions after the [EOS] token (mask m_i = 1 for i ≥ [EOS]), preserving the original prompt's semantics while subtly steering the diffusion toward the target attribute. This modified embedding is used exclusively for the first τ denoising steps to establish the attribute's influence, after which the original and modified embeddings alternate for the remaining steps. The approach is entirely training-free, requires no architectural changes, and is validated across SD1.5, SD2, SDXL, and SD3.

## Results

On SD1.5 across eight professions, the proposed method achieves an average absolute deviation from the 0.5 target ratio (Avg. Δ) of 0.167, matching FairDiffusion and substantially outperforming UCE (0.400), FTDiff (0.264), and SelfDisc (0.244). On MS-COCO-2014-30k, the method preserves image quality with a CLIP score of 26.56 and CMMD of 0.509, slightly exceeding vanilla SD (26.42 / 0.532), while all training-based baselines degrade significantly (e.g., UCE drops to CLIP 18.10, CMMD 1.240). The method generalizes across SD versions, reducing Avg. Δ from 0.444→0.362 (SD2), 0.363→0.242 (SDXL), and 0.481→0.349 (SD3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method ties FairDiffusion on Avg. Δ (both 0.167) but decisively wins on image quality: on MS-COCO-30k it achieves CLIP 26.56 / CMMD 0.509 versus FairDiffusion's 26.03 / 0.586, and it correctly respects explicitly specified attributes (e.g., 'a photo of a male doctor') where FairDiffusion produces nearly balanced gender ratios. Against the three training-based baselines, the method dominates on both de-biasing and quality: UCE achieves Avg. Δ 0.400 with a severely degraded CLIP score of 18.10, FTDiff reaches 0.264 with CLIP 25.61, and SelfDisc reaches 0.244 with CLIP 24.97, all substantially worse than the proposed method's 0.167 / 26.56. Notably, the proposed method is the only approach that simultaneously matches the best de-biasing performance and preserves (or slightly improves) vanilla SD's image generation quality, whereas every baseline trades off at least one of the two objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant training-based debiasing paradigm (fine-tuning, LoRA, prompt tuning) and the closest training-free competitor FairDiffusion (which uses SEGA to modify diffusion direction) by proposing a weak text-embedding perturbation that adds attribute directions only after the EOS token. Conceptually, it builds on SDEdit's forward-noising idea for the mode test and on Condition-Annealed Sampling's text-condition perturbation, but refines both into a targeted, semantics-preserving mechanism. The key positioning is that prior work assumed minority representations were too sparse in noise space to exploit without retraining, whereas this paper demonstrates they form structured regions accessible through mild guidance adjustments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: a training-free, drop-in debiasing method that works across SD1.5, SD2, SDXL, and SD3 without degrading image-text alignment or fidelity addresses a major adoption barrier for commercial T2I services. However, the debiasing performance itself is comparable to FairDiffusion (same average Δ of 0.167), with the primary advantage being preservation of generation quality. Open questions include whether the approach extends to intersectional or more complex biases beyond binary gender/race, whether the minority-region structure holds for other demographic axes, and how the method interacts with safety filters or content moderation in production systems.
