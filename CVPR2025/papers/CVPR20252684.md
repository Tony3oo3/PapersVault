## Problem

Existing image vectorization methods either stack numerous shapes to approximate textures (making precise editing infeasible) or embed texture features only along shape boundaries, which limits them to simple artistic images and causes severe texture distortion when shapes are deformed. Additionally, most approaches lack a semantic layer concept, so editing one visual component disrupts overlapping shapes and prevents decoupled shape/texture manipulation of individual targets.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and leverages SAM for region segmentation and Depth Anything for depth-based layer ordering (k-means clustering into foreground/background layers). The output is a compact parameterized representation: per-layer adaptive Bezier boundary shapes, multi-scale proxy triangle meshes, distributed texture feature vectors (16-dim) at geometric control points, and a shared 2-layer MLP decoder (82→128→3). This representation renders at arbitrary resolution and supports direct manipulation of Bezier control points for shape editing or cross-layer feature remapping for texture transfer.

## Method

The framework decomposes each image into semantic layers via SAM + Depth Anything, then fits each layer boundary with a single adaptive Bezier shape whose segments are recursively subdivided based on per-segment Chamfer distance error. Within each shape, Triwild generates a hierarchy-1 triangulation constrained by the Bezier curves, and Sobel-gradient-driven refinement produces multi-scale proxy triangles (2 levels for background, 3 for foreground) that concentrate resolution in high-detail regions. Texture features are distributed at two scales: global features at one point per Bezier segment (interpolated via inverse distance weighting) and local features at all multi-scale triangle vertices (interpolated via barycentric coordinates). A lightweight 2-layer MLP decodes the concatenated interpolated features and position-encoded local triangle-relative coordinates into RGB, optimized per-scene for 2000 epochs. This multi-layer multi-scale embedding provides a stable coordinate-to-texture support domain that preserves texture consistency under shape deformation and enables decoupled per-layer editing.

## Results

On the ImageNet benchmark, the proposed method achieves MSE 0.37×10⁻³, PSNR 34.32 dB, LPIPS 0.1082, and SSIM 0.9333, substantially outperforming all baselines. On Clipart, it reaches MSE 0.17×10⁻³ and PSNR 37.70 dB. Compared to the closest implicit-representation baseline (Chen et al. 512-shape), the method improves PSNR by 6.25 dB on ImageNet while using fewer parameters (48,919 vs. 113,411) and fewer feature codes (2,106 vs. 6,144). The full pipeline runs in ~5 minutes per ImageNet image on an RTX 3090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all seven baselines across every metric on both Clipart and ImageNet. Against the strongest vectorization baseline Chen et al. (CVPR 2024), it improves PSNR by 5.11 dB on Clipart (37.70 vs. 32.59) and 6.25 dB on ImageNet (34.32 vs. 28.07), while also reducing optimization time from 29.2 to 4.3 min (Clipart) and 34.3 to 5.2 min (ImageNet). Compared to the feedforward SuperSVG (S-SVG), which uses 4000 shapes and a ViT-based predictor, the method achieves 4.36 dB higher PSNR on ImageNet with a per-image optimization approach. Against shape-stacking baselines (DVG, LIVE, O&R), the gains are even larger (up to 8.5 dB PSNR on ImageNet), attributed to the multi-scale internal texture embedding rather than relying on dense shape overlays. In the implicit-representation comparison (Table 3), the method surpasses SIREN-5 by 6.62 dB PSNR and LIIF-/4 by 9.23 dB PSNR on ImageNet, while using fewer total parameters (48,919) than both (83,331 and 264,707 respectively). The method also uses fewer feature codes (2,106) than Chen et al. 512-shape (6,144), demonstrating that the layer-wise single-shape strategy is more parameter-efficient than stacking hundreds of shapes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the shape-anchored distributed texture representation of Chen et al. (CVPR 2024), which embeds implicit texture features only along Bezier contour points, by distributing texture features additionally onto a multi-scale internal triangulation (via Triwild) within each shape. It further departs from prior vectorization methods (LIVE, O&R, SuperSVG) that rely on stacking hundreds of overlapping shapes by fitting each semantic layer as a single adaptively-refined Bezier shape, and introduces semantic layer decomposition via SAM and Depth Anything to enable decoupled per-component editing. The coordinate-to-rgb decoding framework follows LIIF, but the key architectural departure is the hierarchical fusion of global (boundary) and local (interior triangle) texture features through barycentric and inverse-distance interpolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that high-fidelity vectorization of complex natural images (not just simple icons or clipart) is achievable with fewer parameters and faster optimization than prior methods, while simultaneously enabling practical shape and texture editing that was previously infeasible for such images. This bridges a significant gap between the quality of neural implicit representations and the editability of vector graphics. Limitations include the per-image optimization cost (~5 min), dependence on SAM/Depth Anything segmentation quality, and the fact that editing is still constrained to per-layer geometric transforms and texture transfers rather than arbitrary semantic manipulation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very complex multi-object scenes, whether the per-scene optimization can be replaced by a learned feedforward model, and how robust the method is to adversarial or highly textured inputs.
