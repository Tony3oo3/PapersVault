## Problem

Existing indoor scene generation methods produce physically implausible layouts with significant object collisions, partly because they represent meshes as single bounding boxes whose intersections do not align with actual mesh overlaps, and partly because training data like 3D-FRONT contains numerous intersecting objects. The paper asks how to generate compact, intersection-free scenes by modeling objects at a finer geometric granularity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250689/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a floor plan (encoded via ResNet18 into a 64-dimensional start token) and, for scene completion, a partial set of retained objects. It outputs an autoregressive token sequence where each object is represented by an entity token (class, translation, size, sin/cos rotation) followed by multiple cuboid tokens sorted by height, with cuboids decomposed from meshes at N=64 voxel resolution. At inference, generated cuboid sequences are matched to 3D-FUTURE models via 3D IoU retrieval rather than bounding-box nearest-neighbor search.

## Method

CASAGPT represents each 3D object as an assembly of cuboids produced by voxelizing the mesh, coarsening into maximal contiguous cuboids along x/z axes, and iteratively merging pairs whose bounding-cuboid volume ratio falls below a dynamic threshold. A Llama-3-based autoregressive transformer decoder (with RMSNorm, SwiGLU, and learned position encoding replacing RoPE) sequentially predicts entity and cuboid tokens, trained with teacher forcing using cross-entropy for class labels and mixture-of-logistic-distribution NLL for continuous attributes. A key training innovation is iterative rejection sampling: K candidate scenes are generated, those exceeding a 3D cuboid-IoU intersection threshold are discarded, and the model is fine-tuned on the surviving samples for three rounds. The authors also curate 3DFRONT-NC by iteratively adjusting object translations via IoU-gradient descent to eliminate mesh-level intersections while leaving non-intersecting arrangements (e.g., table-chair groups) undisturbed.

## Results

On the 3DFRONT-NC dataset, CASAGPT achieves a non-intersection rate (NIRate) of 90.6% for bedrooms, 81.4% for dining rooms, 89.0% for living rooms, and 87.1% for libraries, substantially outperforming all baselines. The Cuboid Intersection-over-Union (CIoU, lower is better) drops to 0.77 (bedroom), 0.92 (dining), 0.62 (living), and 0.79 (library), while FID reaches 24.7, 31.4, 28.4, and 43.0 respectively. On the original 3D-FRONT dataset, the method still leads in CIoU and NIRate across all room types (e.g., 80.6% NIRate and 1.08 CIoU for bedrooms), demonstrating robustness even without the refined training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CASAGPT outperforms all three baselines on the proposed collision metrics (CIoU and NIRate) across every room category where comparisons are available. On the 3DFRONT-NC bedroom benchmark, CASAGPT achieves 90.6% NIRate versus 80.2% (ATISS), 79.1% (DiffuScene), and 58.1% (LayoutGPT) — a 10.4-point margin over the strongest baseline. In CIoU (lower is better), CASAGPT scores 0.77 compared to 1.15 (ATISS) and 1.32 (DiffuScene), indicating roughly 33–42% less cuboid intersection volume. On FID, CASAGPT leads with 24.7 (bedroom) and 28.4 (living room), while DiffuScene trails at 28.0 and 35.7 respectively. The one metric where CASAGPT does not dominate is CKL: DiffuScene achieves 2.08 vs. CASAGPT's 1.68 on bedroom, but the authors note this comparison is not fully fair since DiffuScene does not condition on the floor plan, simplifying its training objective. LayoutGPT, being a training-free GPT-4-based method, is only evaluated on bedroom and living room and consistently lags on all metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

CASAGPT extends the autoregressive indoor scene synthesis line (ATISS, SceneFormer, COFS, FOREST2SEQ) by replacing the standard bounding-box object representation with assemblies of fine-grained cuboid primitives, drawing on shape-abstraction work (Tulsiani et al., DPF-Net, ShapeAssembly) to decompose meshes into compact cuboid sets. It also borrows the rejection-sampling paradigm from LLM alignment (RS-DPO, statistical rejection sampling) and applies it iteratively to 3D layout generation, a cross-domain transfer not previously explored in scene synthesis. The model architecture is a Llama-3-based transformer decoder, positioning it within the broader trend of repurposing LLM backbones for structured generation tasks, while the 3DFRONT-NC dataset curation directly addresses a data-quality gap that prior methods (DiffuScene, ATISS, LayoutGPT) inherited from the noisy 3D-FRONT benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Object intersection remains a persistent and visually jarring failure mode in generated indoor scenes; CASAGPT's cuboid-based representation offers a simple, interpretable mechanism to detect and minimize collisions at the mesh level rather than the coarser bounding-box level, achieving a 90.6% non-intersection rate for bedrooms. The 3DFRONT-NC dataset and the iterative rejection-sampling fine-tuning loop are practically reusable components for any scene-synthesis pipeline. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger, more complex scenes without objects drifting off the floor plane, and whether the autoregressive formulation can be replaced by diffusion or RL-based objectives (as the authors themselves suggest) to further improve generation quality and diversity.
