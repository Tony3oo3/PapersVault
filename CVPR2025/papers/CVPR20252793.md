## Problem

Monolithic vision-language models, which avoid modality-specific encoders and process images and text as a unified whole, consistently underperform compositional VLMs. Existing approaches either train from scratch at enormous computational cost or continually pre-train LLMs to acquire visual abilities, which degrades their language proficiency. The root cause is the absence of a dedicated embedding module that can project both visual and textual inputs into a shared space compatible with a frozen LLM.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252793/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images and text queries as input. Images are processed via a dynamic high-resolution strategy (DynProcess) that divides them into 448×448 tiles plus a global thumbnail, then converted to patch tokens with stride 28 (or stride 14 with pixel shuffle for the HD variant). Text is tokenized with a 92,553-vocabulary tokenizer. Both image and text tokens are concatenated into a single sequence processed by causal attention. The model produces a text response via standard next-token prediction from the LLM backbone (InternLM2-1.8B).

## Method

HoVLE introduces a holistic embedding module composed of 8 causal Transformer layers (hidden size 2048, 16 heads) with the same architecture as the LLM, which jointly processes image patch tokens and text tokens to project them into a unified embedding space before feeding them to a frozen LLM. A three-stage training procedure empowers this module: (1) a distillation stage trains the embedding module to match visual features from InternViT-300M and text embeddings from InternLM2-1.8B using 500M unpaired random images from LAION-2B combined with random text tokens, requiring no image-text pairs; (2) an alignment stage combines the embedding module with the frozen LLM for next-token prediction on 45M multi-modal samples; (3) an instruction tuning stage fine-tunes the entire model on 5M instruction-following data. The key insight is that by matching the embedding module's architecture to the LLM's Transformer layers and using causal attention for both modalities, the LLM can interpret images identically to text without any continual pre-training, preserving its language capabilities while gaining strong visual understanding.

## Results

HoVLE (2.6B) achieves 71.9 on MMBench, 1864 on MME, 44.3 on MM-Vet, and 46.2 on MathVista, surpassing the previous best monolithic VLM (Emu3, 8B) by ~13 points on MMBench and ~7 points on MM-Vet. The high-resolution variant HoVLE(HD) further improves to 73.3 on MMBench and 49.2 on MathVista, matching or exceeding the compositional counterpart InternVL2 (2.2B) on MMBench (73.3 vs. 73.2) and MathVista (49.2 vs. 46.4). On OCR-focused benchmarks, HoVLE(HD) reaches 740 on OCRBench and 86.1 on DocVQA, competitive with compositional models of similar size. Overall, HoVLE narrows the gap to state-of-the-art compositional VLMs (e.g., Qwen2-VL-2B at 74.9 MMB, Phi-3 Vision at 80.5 MMB) while using only 2.6B parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HoVLE (2.6B) decisively outperforms all prior monolithic VLMs: it surpasses Emu3 (8B) by 13.4 points on MMBench (71.9 vs. 58.5), 6.1 on MM-Vet (44.3 vs. 37.2), and 2.5 on POPE (87.6 vs. 85.2), while using roughly 3× fewer parameters. Against EVE-7B(HD), HoVLE leads by 19.6 points on MMBench (71.9 vs. 52.3) and 18.6 on MM-Vet (44.3 vs. 25.7). Compared to its compositional counterpart InternVL2 (2.2B, same LLM and data), HoVLE trails slightly on MMBench (71.9 vs. 73.2) and MME (1864 vs. 1877) but wins on CCB (75.3 vs. 74.7) and HallB (39.6 vs. 37.9); HoVLE(HD) closes the MMBench gap to 73.3 vs. 73.2 and leads on MathVista (49.2 vs. 46.4). Against the stronger compositional models Qwen2-VL-2B and Phi-3 Vision, HoVLE still lags on MMBench (71.9/73.3 vs. 74.9/80.5) and MMMU (33.7 vs. 41.1/40.4), confirming that the monolithic–compositional gap persists at the frontier but is substantially narrowed. On VQA benchmarks (Table 2), HoVLE(HD) matches or exceeds InternVL2 on GQA (64.9 vs. 61.0), SQA-I (94.8 vs. 84.9), and DocVQA (86.1 vs. 85.9), while remaining competitive with Qwen2-VL-2B on TextVQA (70.9 vs. 79.7) and OCRBench (740 vs. 794).

## Relation to Prior Work

HoVLE departs from both dominant VLM paradigms: compositional models (e.g., InternVL, Qwen2-VL) that pair a separate vision encoder with an LLM via a connector, and prior monolithic models (SOLO, EVE, Chameleon, Fuyu) that either train from scratch or require continual pre-training of the LLM to acquire visual abilities. The key conceptual move is to treat the LLM's success in compositional settings—interpreting images when embeddings are aligned—as evidence that a dedicated holistic embedding module, built from the same causal Transformer layers as the LLM, can project both modalities into a shared space without touching the LLM's weights. The multi-stage training pipeline repurposes knowledge distillation (from a frozen vision encoder and the LLM's own text embeddings) with unpaired random data, a data strategy not seen in prior monolithic VLM training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By closing most of the performance gap between monolithic and compositional VLMs at the 2B scale (e.g., ~15 points over the previous best monolithic on MMBench, and matching InternVL2 2.2B on several benchmarks), HoVLE demonstrates that the monolithic design need not sacrifice language proficiency or require expensive LLM retraining. The attention-map analysis providing a mechanistic explanation—sparse text-to-image attention indicating well-aligned visual features—gives a diagnostic tool for future monolithic architectures. However, all experiments are confined to 2B parameters, leaving open whether the holistic embedding module scales effectively to 7B+ models where compositional VLMs have a larger head start, and whether the approach can eventually match or surpass the best compositional systems (Qwen2-VL, Phi-3 Vision) that still lead by a margin.
