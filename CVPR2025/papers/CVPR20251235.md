## Problem

Existing adversarially robust UDA methods rely heavily on pseudo-labels without explicit distributional alignment, leading to confirmation bias and poor generalization to source and unseen domains. No prior work provides a theoretical generalization bound for robust risk on the target domain that justifies the design choices of robust UDA algorithms. This paper derives such a bound using a novel robust margin disparity discrepancy and shows that pseudo-labeling alone is insufficient—domain alignment must be jointly optimized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251235/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled source-domain RGB images, unlabeled target-domain RGB images, and a robustly pre-trained (Robust-PT) feature extractor as initialization. A fixed teacher model (trained via non-robust MDD) generates pseudo-labels for target images, which are used to construct PGD adversarial examples during training. The perturbation budget ε is dataset-dependent (16/255 for Office-31/Office-Home, 8/255 for VisDA2017, 4/255 for DomainNet), and the output is a standard class label predicted by the final π∘ψ network evaluated under AutoAttack.

## Method

TAROT minimizes a three-term objective derived from a new generalization bound: (1) standard margin risk on the source domain (no adversarial examples needed, keeping computation low), (2) a robust margin disparity discrepancy between source and target feature distributions optimized via a GRL-augmented auxiliary head π′, and (3) adversarial cross-entropy on target images using pseudo-labels from a fixed non-robust teacher model to reduce the local Lipschitz constant. The feature extractor ψ is initialized from a Robust-PT checkpoint, which the theory identifies as critical because it bounds the local Lipschitz term in the generalization bound. Adversarial examples generated for the Lipschitz-reduction term are reused in the discrepancy computation, avoiding redundant PGD iterations. The key architectural distinction from MDD is that the discrepancy term compares a robust disparity (on target) against a standard disparity (on source), rather than two standard disparities, directly encoding the adversarial robustness requirement into the domain-alignment signal.

## Results

On Office-31 (ε=16/255), TAROT achieves 89.31% standard accuracy and 83.27% robust accuracy (AutoAttack) on average across all domain pairs, outperforming the strongest baseline PL (88.03%/82.95%). On the more challenging DomainNet (ε=4/255), TAROT reaches 34.50%/20.94% versus PL's 28.34%/18.66%, showing the largest margin of improvement. On Office-Home (ε=16/255), TAROT attains 68.29%/46.80% compared to PL's 66.00%/44.38%. On VisDA2017 (ε=8/255), TAROT achieves 67.12%/37.77% on Syn→Real and 85.18%/51.21% on the synthetic domain, substantially exceeding PL (66.71%/37.83% and 40.98%/24.09% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAROT consistently outperforms all three prior robust UDA methods (ARTUDA, RFA, SRoUDA) across every dataset and domain pair. The closest competitor is PL (pseudo-labeling with a fixed teacher), which TAROT surpasses by 1.28% standard and 0.32% robust accuracy on Office-31, by 2.29%/2.42% on Office-Home, and by 6.16%/2.28% on DomainNet — the gap widening on harder datasets. RFA and ARTUDA exhibit near-zero robust accuracy on multiple tasks (e.g., RFA drops to 1.21% robust on A→D in Office-31; ARTUDA reaches only 10.73% standard on VisDA2017 Syn→Real), indicating they fail to maintain adversarial robustness under the larger perturbation budgets used here. SRoUDA performs moderately but still trails TAROT by 14.69% standard and 39.12% robust on Office-31. Notably, on the domain-invariant robustness evaluation (source and unseen domains), TAROT achieves 68.18%/46.32% average versus PL's 45.39%/29.34%, demonstrating that TAROT learns genuinely domain-invariant robust features rather than merely overfitting to the target domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Margin Disparity Discrepancy (MDD) framework to the adversarially robust setting, introducing a robust margin disparity discrepancy that replaces the standard margin disparity on the target domain with an adversarial one. It departs from prior robust UDA methods (ARTUDA, RFA, SRoUDA) that rely solely on pseudo-labeling by integrating explicit distributional alignment via a GRL-based adversarial head, and it builds on the Robust-PT initialization insight from RFA. The theoretical contribution—a Rademacher-complexity-based generalization bound for robust target risk—fills a gap that none of the existing robust UDA algorithms address, as they lack formal justification for their loss functions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical grounding for robust UDA, showing that pseudo-labeling alone is insufficient for scalability and that combining it with domain alignment yields essentially domain-invariant robust features that generalize to source and unseen domains. A key open question is the sensitivity of the trade-off parameter α, which must be manually tuned per task, and whether the approach extends beyond CNN-based image classification to other architectures or modalities. The modest target-domain gains over a strong pseudo-labeling baseline (e.g., ~0.3% on Office-31) suggest the primary value lies in the theoretical framework and the domain-invariance property rather than raw target accuracy.
