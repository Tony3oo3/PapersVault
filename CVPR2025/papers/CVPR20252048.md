## Problem

Existing portrait relighting methods either depend on light stage captures (limited by physical constraints on light source density and intensity) or rely on inverse graphics to estimate intrinsic scene representations, which fails to capture complex illumination effects such as inter-reflections, occlusion shadows, and subsurface scattering. The paper asks whether a diffusion model can learn to directly re-render a portrait under new lighting from synthetic 3D data alone, without light stage supervision or explicit inverse rendering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252048/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 RGB portrait image, a panoramic HDR environment map (tonemapped to LDR before encoding), and a text prompt as input, and produces a relit 512×512 portrait. The input portrait and LDR environment map are encoded by Stable Diffusion's VAE and concatenated channel-wise with the noised target latent, expanding the U-Net's first convolutional layer following the Palette formulation. At inference, the environment map can be rotated to explore different lighting directions while the portrait and text prompt remain fixed.

## Method

SynthLight frames relighting as a re-rendering problem, training a latent diffusion model to directly map (input portrait, target environment map) → relit portrait, bypassing inverse graphics entirely. Training data consists of ~1.26M images rendered in Blender Cycles from 350 rigged 3D head assets with PBR materials under 10 HDR maps rotated 36 times each. Two key techniques bridge the synthetic-to-real domain gap: (1) multi-task training that jointly optimizes the relighting objective and a text-to-portrait generation task on real LAION images (0.7/0.3 sampling ratio) to prevent distributional drift, and (2) an inference-time dual-conditioning classifier-free guidance scheme with separate text (λ_T) and portrait (λ_I) guidance weights, where λ_I ∈ [2,3] balances identity preservation against relighting strength. The model is initialized from the IC-Light checkpoint and trained for 40K steps on 32 A100 GPUs.

## Results

On the synthetic test set (500 held-out images), SynthLight achieves the best scores across all four metrics: LPIPS 0.063, SSIM 0.945, PSNR 29.57 dB, and FaceNet distance 0.165, outperforming the next-best method (SwitchLight) by a wide margin on PSNR (29.57 vs. 21.43 dB) and FaceNet (0.165 vs. 0.198). On the light stage test set (300 rendered pairs), SynthLight is competitive with SwitchLight, trailing slightly on pixel-level metrics (PSNR 19.70 vs. 20.30 dB; SSIM 0.813 vs. 0.853) but achieving the best LPIPS among diffusion-based methods. In a user study with 482 responses, SynthLight is preferred over all baselines on lighting, image quality, and identity preservation, with the strongest margin being 92% preference over IC-Light for lighting accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the synthetic test set, SynthLight clearly dominates all four baselines, achieving the best LPIPS (0.063 vs. 0.088–0.128), SSIM (0.945 vs. 0.860–0.911), PSNR (29.57 vs. 20.28–25.33 dB), and FaceNet distance (0.165 vs. 0.198–0.357). On the light stage test set, SwitchLight [21]—a commercial method trained directly on light stage data—edges out SynthLight on all four pixel-level metrics (e.g., PSNR 20.30 vs. 19.70 dB; SSIM 0.853 vs. 0.813), reflecting its advantage from domain-matched training data. However, SynthLight substantially outperforms IC-Light [60], DiLightNet [58], and Neural Gaffer [18] on the light stage set, with DiLightNet showing the largest gap (FaceNet 0.576 vs. 0.173). In the user study, SynthLight is preferred over SwitchLight on lighting (56%), quality (64%), and identity (70%), and over IC-Light on lighting (92%), quality (57%), and identity (52%), indicating that perceptual relighting quality favors SynthLight even where pixel-level metrics slightly favor SwitchLight. Total Relighting [28] is included only in qualitative comparisons (Fig. 7), where it produces soft shadows and altered skin tones under strong sunlight, unlike SynthLight's sharper, more physically plausible illumination.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant inverse-graphics paradigm in portrait relighting (Total Relighting, SwitchLight, Lumos) that explicitly estimates lighting-invariant intrinsics (depth, normals, albedo) before re-rendering. Instead, it frames relighting as learning a direct re-rendering function f_re(I, L) via a diffusion model trained on paired PBR renderings from Blender Cycles, bypassing inverse rendering entirely. It builds on Stable Diffusion's latent diffusion architecture (following Palette's channel-expansion conditioning) and adapts classifier-free guidance from InstructPix2Pix for image-conditioned inference. The multi-task training strategy (jointly training on synthetic relighting pairs and real text-to-portrait generation) draws on domain-adaptation ideas from inpainting and prior relighting work (SwitchLight's pre-training, Relightful Harmonization's bootstrapping), but applies them in a simpler, more unified form.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that high-quality portrait relighting no longer requires expensive light stage captures or per-subject 3D reconstruction—synthetic PBR renderings suffice, and the model generalizes to half-body shots, multi-person photos, and even non-human subjects despite training only on simple headshots. The demonstrated illumination effects (catch lights in eyes, subsurface scattering in ears, inter-reflections with clothing) represent a qualitative leap over prior methods that struggle with these complex light transport phenomena. Limitations include residual artifacts under very strong directional lighting (oversaturation, failure to remove input shadows), and the method remains a single-image, offline system without video or real-time support. Open questions include whether the synthetic-data-only recipe extends to full-body or scene-level relighting, and whether the multi-task/inference-adaptation recipe generalizes to other editing tasks beyond relighting.
