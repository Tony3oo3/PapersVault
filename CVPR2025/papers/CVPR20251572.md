## Problem

Existing DeepFake detectors are designed around face-centric manipulations (face-swapping, lip-syncing) and typically require a detectable human face to perform inference, making them ineffective against background alterations and fully AI-generated T2V/I2V videos that may contain no human subjects at all. The rapid proliferation of text-to-video and image-to-video models has expanded the threat landscape well beyond facial forgeries, yet no single detector generalizes across face manipulations, background edits, and fully synthetic content.

## Input / Output

The model consumes full video frames (no face cropping) sampled at every other frame, forming 64-frame segments where each frame is resized to 384×384×3 and encoded by a frozen SigLIP-So400m encoder into a 729-token × 1152-dim sequence. Output is a binary real/fake prediction or, in fine-grained mode, a three-class label distinguishing real, partially manipulated (foreground or background), and fully synthetic videos. The architecture processes the entire frame spatially, so detection works even when no human face is present.

## Method

UNITE extracts domain-agnostic features from each frame using a frozen SigLIP-So400m ViT encoder, concatenates the 64 frame embeddings with sine-cosine positional encodings, and feeds them into a 4-block, 12-head multi-head self-attention transformer for classification. The key innovation is an Attention-Diversity (AD) loss, inspired by Fisher Discriminant Analysis, that computes pooled feature vectors per attention head and enforces both within-class compactness (pooled features stay near dynamically updated class centers) and between-class separation (centers of different classes are pushed apart), thereby preventing all attention heads from collapsing onto the face region. Training combines standard cross-entropy with the AD loss (equal weighting λ₁=λ₂=0.5) and incorporates the fully synthetic GTA-V dataset alongside FaceForensics++ to simulate AI-generated content. This combination enables the model to detect temporal inconsistencies across the full frame rather than relying solely on facial artifacts.

## Results

On face-manipulated datasets, UNITE (trained on FF++ + GTA-V) achieves 99.96% accuracy on FF++, 95.11% on CelebDF, 99.62% on DeeperForensics, and 97.01% on UADFV, outperforming or matching all 14 compared baselines. On the DeMamba synthetic video benchmark (validation split, where UNITE was *not* trained), UNITE achieves 92.76% average precision and 89.60% average recall, surpassing the best trained baseline FTCN-CLIP-B (92.21% P / 86.18% R). On background-manipulated AVID videos, adding GTA-V synthetic data to training lifts accuracy from 41.67% to 100%, and on in-the-wild NYTimes DeepFake quiz videos, accuracy jumps from 50% to 80%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UNITE outperforms all face-centric baselines on every dataset where they overlap: on CelebDF it leads TALL (90.79%) by 4.3 points and FakeCatcher (91.50%) by 3.6 points, while essentially tying PUDD (95.10%); on UADFV it beats Li et al. Res-152 (93.80%) by 3.2 points and CViT (93.75%) by 3.3 points. On FF++ it edges out TI2Net (99.95%) by 0.01 points and Concas et al. (99.49%) by 0.47 points. On the DeMamba synthetic-video benchmark, UNITE—trained only on FF++ and GTA-V without any DeMamba data—achieves 92.76% average precision, beating FTCN-CLIP-B (92.21%) and MINTIME-CLIP-B (91.55%), both of which were trained on the DeMamba train split, making the comparison favorable to UNITE. The largest margin appears on CelebDF, where UNITE's 95.11% accuracy exceeds the next-best PUDD by a narrow margin but surpasses TI2Net (68.22%) and LRNet (53.20%) by 27 and 42 points respectively, highlighting the advantage of full-frame processing over face-cropped approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant face-centric DeepFake detection paradigm (TALL, ISTVT, TI2Net, etc.) that requires face detection and cropping, and from the separate synthetic video detection line (DeMamba) that handles T2V/I2V content but ignores face manipulations. UNITE unifies both by processing full frames through a frozen SigLIP-So400m encoder feeding a learnable multi-head attention transformer, repurposing a vision-language foundation model for video forensics. The key methodological departure is the attention-diversity loss, inspired by Fisher Discriminant Analysis, which explicitly counteracts the transformer's tendency to collapse attention onto facial regions—a failure mode the authors identify as the root cause of poor generalization to background and fully synthetic content.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As T2V/I2V generators (Sora, Runway, ModelScope) produce increasingly convincing full-scene content, the field needs detectors that do not presuppose a human face is present; UNITE is one of the first to demonstrate a single model handling face swaps, background inpainting, and fully synthetic video. The finding that non-AI synthetic data (GTA-V) substantially improves AI-generated video detection is a practical workaround for the scarcity of open-source T2V/I2V training data, though it remains unclear whether this proxy effect holds for truly novel generators. Limitations include the small AVID evaluation set, the cross-domain (unfair) DeMamba comparison, and the 10-video NYTimes quiz, leaving open questions about robustness to unseen manipulation types and audio-visual consistency cues.
