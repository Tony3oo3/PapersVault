## Problem

Semi-supervised breast ultrasound segmentation is hindered by ambiguous lesion boundaries, large scale/shape variation, and scarce annotations. Existing mean-teacher frameworks only model semantic correlations within a single image and are vulnerable to noise and error accumulation between teacher and student, particularly in low-label regimes. Cross-image semantic correlations that could stabilize predictions and refine edges remain largely unexploited.

## Input / Output

The method consumes breast ultrasound images (BUSI at ~500×500, UDIAT at ~760×570) cropped to 513×513 patches, with both labeled and unlabeled samples fed into a single network. For unlabeled images, a confidence-based augmentation selectively masks easy regions to produce a masked variant x^m, which is supervised by the network's own prediction on the original x^u. The output is a binary per-pixel segmentation mask distinguishing lesion foreground from background, evaluated via Dice and IoU under 1/2, 1/4, and 1/8 labeled-data splits.

## Method

CSC-PA replaces the mean-teacher architecture with a single network that integrates two prototype attentions. Foreground Prototype Attention (FPA) derives channel prototypes from encoder features via softmax-weighted self-correlation, then uses a cross-attention mechanism to transfer complementary foreground features between labeled and unlabeled images, capturing cross-image semantic correlations. Edge Prototype Attention (EPA) maintains an adaptive edge container that filters and updates global edge features through a linear layer (rather than first-in-first-out memory banks), generates an edge prototype by averaging stored features, and amplifies edge responses via cosine-similarity-based correlation maps. A Pixel Affinity Loss (PAL) further enforces KL-divergence consistency between affinity matrices computed from predictions and from ground truth or pseudo-labels, exploiting contextual pixel correlations that standard cross-entropy ignores. Training proceeds in two stages: stage 1 trains on labeled data with cross-entropy and PAL, while stage 2 adds unlabeled supervision via the masked image and its pseudo-label.

## Results

CSC-PA achieves state-of-the-art performance on both BUSI and UDIAT datasets across all label ratios. On BUSI (1/4 split, 129 labeled), it reaches 76.93% Dice and 68.20% IoU, surpassing the previous best (PH-Net: 76.21% Dice, 67.67% IoU). On UDIAT (1/4 split, 33 labeled), it achieves 81.26% Dice and 71.65% IoU, outperforming PH-Net (80.52% Dice, 70.87% IoU). The advantage grows with fewer labels: on UDIAT 1/8 (16 labeled), CSC-PA gains +8.30% IoU over the supervised-only baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSC-PA outperforms all 10 baselines across every dataset and label ratio. The closest competitor is PH-Net (CVPR 2024), which CSC-PA surpasses by +0.72% Dice and +0.53% IoU on BUSI 1/4, and by +0.74% Dice and +0.78% IoU on UDIAT 1/4. Among mean-teacher-based methods, ABD (CVPR 2024) is the strongest, but CSC-PA still leads by +0.77% Dice on BUSI 1/4 and +0.86% Dice on UDIAT 1/4. Single-network methods UniMatch and CorrMatch trail CSC-PA by 1.85% and 1.10% Dice respectively on BUSI 1/4. The fully-supervised MFMSNet, despite using all labels, is outperformed by CSC-PA under the 1/4 protocol (76.93% vs. 72.77% Dice on BUSI), highlighting the effectiveness of the semi-supervised framework. On the smaller UDIAT dataset, the gap widens with fewer labels: CSC-PA gains +3.53% Dice over PH-Net at 1/8 (78.70% vs. 77.93%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant mean-teacher (MT) paradigm in semi-supervised segmentation by proposing a single-network framework that explicitly models cross-image semantic correlations via prototype attentions. While CISC-R [30] introduced cross-image contrastive learning and UniMatch/CorrMatch explored single-network designs, none combined channel-prototype-based foreground transfer with an adaptive edge container for boundary refinement. The FPA mechanism draws on channel-wise knowledge distillation [21], and the PAL builds on affinity-matrix formulations from spatial propagation networks [14], but their integration into a unified semi-supervised BUS segmentation pipeline is novel. The adaptive edge container deliberately replaces the FIFO memory bank convention, using a linear-layer mapping to filter and update stored edge features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical clinical bottleneck: BUS annotations are expensive and scarce, yet lesion boundaries are often ambiguous due to speckle noise. The consistent gains across 1/2, 1/4, and 1/8 label ratios (up to +8.3% IoU over supervised-only on UDIAT) suggest the cross-image prototype mechanism is particularly valuable in low-label regimes, which is the realistic clinical scenario. However, the authors acknowledge that extremely low-contrast lesions and severe artifacts remain failure cases, and the method is validated only on two relatively small BUS datasets (647 and 163 images), leaving open whether the prototype-attention approach generalizes to other ultrasound modalities or larger-scale segmentation tasks.
