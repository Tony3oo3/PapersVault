## Problem

Existing re-ranking methods for person ReID (k-reciprocal encoding, ECN, GCR) all rely on k-nearest neighbor search to select similar images, which has O(N² log N) time complexity and is inherently sequential, making it the dominant computational bottleneck that limits real-world deployment. This paper asks whether the k-NN step can be replaced by a cheaper, fully parallelizable neighbor selection strategy without sacrificing retrieval accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted feature vectors (e.g., 512-d from AGW or 256-d from TransReID) for all query and gallery images, requiring no additional model training. It outputs refined feature vectors after T=3 iterations of parameter-free graph convolution, from which new similarity scores are computed to produce the final re-ranked list. The entire pipeline operates on an N×N adjacency matrix (N = total query + gallery images), with all operations being element-wise or matrix-multiplication, enabling full GPU parallelization.

## Method

Cheb-GR replaces k-NN with an adaptive threshold-based edge selection: for each image i, it computes the mean μ_i and standard deviation σ_i of its distances to all other images, then sets a per-node threshold T_i = μ_i − λσ_i, justified by Chebyshev's inequality to separate positive (same-identity) from negative (different-identity) samples. Edges exceeding T_i are removed (set to infinity), and remaining distances are converted to similarities via exp(−d/γ) to form the final adjacency matrix. Feature refinement is then performed by T iterations of parameter-free GCN propagation (D^{−1/2} Â D^{−1/2} X), which requires no learned weight matrix. The entire pipeline runs in O(N²) time with all operations parallelizable on GPU, achieving ~50× speedup over k-reciprocal re-ranking while maintaining competitive mAP and Rank-1 accuracy across Market-1501, DukeMTMC, and CUHK03.

## Results

On Market-1501 with the TransReID backbone, Cheb-GR achieves 93.2% mAP and 95.2% Rank-1 in 1.5 s, outperforming GCR (92.5% mAP, 55.5 s) and approaching KR/ECN (94.0% mAP, 73–148 s) at roughly 1/50th the runtime. On DukeMTMC, Cheb-GR reaches 90.4% mAP and 93.0% Rank-1 in 1.5 s, and on CUHK03 it attains 85.4% mAP and 83.9% Rank-1 in 0.5 s. Across all three datasets, Cheb-GR is the fastest re-ranking method tested, running in under 2 s versus 43–161 s for the next-fastest competitors (GCR, KR, ECN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Cheb-GR is compared against five re-ranking baselines: QE, LBR, KR (k-reciprocal), ECN, and GCR. In terms of speed, Cheb-GR is the clear winner, running in 0.2–1.5 s versus 43–161 s for GCR/KR/ECN (roughly 50× faster than KR on Market-1501). On accuracy, Cheb-GR consistently outperforms QE and LBR across all three datasets and both backbones. Against GCR, Cheb-GR wins on Market-1501 (93.2 vs 92.5 mAP with TransReID) and CUHK03 (85.4 vs 84.0 mAP) while trailing slightly on DukeMTMC (90.4 vs 90.7 mAP). Against the strongest baselines KR and ECN, Cheb-GR sacrifices 0.8–1.0 mAP on Market-1501 and 1.0–1.6 mAP on DukeMTMC/CUHK03, but does so at approximately 1/50th the computation time, making it the best accuracy-to-speed trade-off among all tested methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends GCR [38], which constructs a graph via k-nearest neighbor search and applies parameter-free GCN propagation for re-ranking in person ReID. The key departure is replacing the sequential, O(N² log N) k-NN search with an adaptive threshold-based neighbor selection derived from Chebyshev's Inequality, reducing the graph construction to O(N²) and enabling full GPU parallelization. It also positions itself against the broader re-ranking literature (k-reciprocal encoding, ECN, QE, LBR), all of which share the same k-NN bottleneck, and provides a theoretical justification for why a simple distance threshold can substitute for explicit neighbor ranking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~50× speedup over k-reciprocal re-ranking (0.9 s vs. 89.8 s on Market-1501) makes graph-based re-ranking practically viable for large-scale or real-time surveillance deployments where prior methods were prohibitively slow. However, the method trades a small amount of accuracy (e.g., 93.0 vs. 94.9 mAP on Market with AGW) for speed, and its sensitivity to the λ hyperparameter across models and datasets limits plug-and-play usability. Open questions include whether the quadratic memory cost of the full adjacency matrix can be mitigated for very large galleries, and whether the Chebyshev-guided threshold generalizes to other similarity-based retrieval tasks beyond ReID.
