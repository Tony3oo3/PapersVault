## Problem

Structure-based visual localization fails when the query view has a large viewpoint change relative to the reference keyframe database, as is common in corridors or large indoor spaces. Existing novel view synthesis methods either produce geometrically inaccurate results under large viewpoint shifts (generative models like ZeroNVS) or yield noisy, blurry outputs when extrapolating beyond training views (feed-forward methods like MVSplat), making them unsuitable for the feature matching that localization requires.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of posed reference keyframes with limited viewpoint diversity and a 3D point cloud as offline input, and a single query image as online input. Offline, it produces a scene mesh (via TSDF fusion at 0.01 m voxel size) and a database of K virtual keyframes (K = 10× the number of reference keyframes) rendered with diffuse color and depth at eye-level camera placements with three pitch angles each. Online, the pipeline outputs a 6-DoF camera pose (R, t) for the query image via retrieval, feature matching, and PnP+RANSAC.

## Method

The core contribution is a confidence-aware geometric prior injected into 2D Gaussian Splatting training. Two regularization terms are added to the standard photometric and normal-consistency losses: (1) a confidence-aware normal loss derived from the von Mises-Fisher distribution output of DSINE, which weights each pixel's normal supervision by its concentration parameter κ, and (2) a confidence-aware depth loss using per-pixel confidence weights from Dust3r MVS depth, with a geometric weight-decay schedule (η_t = η_0·α^t) that reduces the depth prior's influence in later training stages to mitigate scale inconsistencies. After training, a mesh is extracted via TSDF fusion of back-projected rendered images and depths at reference poses, and virtual keyframes are rendered from this mesh using diffuse shading. Virtual keyframe placement is performed on a gravity-aligned 2D occupancy map with adaptive spatial resolution, filtering by wall proximity and co-visibility ratio, and three pitch angles per location to cover upward and downward views.

## Results

On the proposed 6-scene dataset emphasizing large viewpoint changes, GPVK-VL achieves the highest localization success rates across all scenes, with particularly large gains in corridor environments: 59.4% vs. 25.7% (HLoc) on Scene001 and 57.7% vs. 31.8% (HLoc) on Scene004 at the (2°, 5 cm) threshold. On the standard 7Scenes and Cambridge Landmarks benchmarks, GPVK-VL remains competitive with HLoc, achieving median errors of 0.01–0.03 m / 0.7°–1.0° on 7Scenes and 0.06–0.25 m / 0.2°–0.4° on Cambridge, slightly behind HLoc's 0.01–0.05 m / 0.2°–1.4° and 0.04–0.14 m / 0.2°–0.3° respectively. In the virtual keyframe synthesis evaluation on ScanNet, GPVK-VL achieves the best Feature Descriptor Similarity (FDS = 1.20) and Geometrically-Correct Feature Matching Ratio (GC-FMR = 71.1/74.8/80.3 at 4/6/8 px), substantially outperforming 2DGS-Mesh (FDS = 1.60, GC-FMR = 52.5/57.8/62.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPVK-VL decisively outperforms all baselines on the proposed large-viewpoint-change dataset. Against the structure-based HLoc pipeline, GPVK-VL improves success rates by 33.7 points on Scene001 (59.4% vs. 25.7%) and 25.9 points on Scene004 (57.7% vs. 31.8%) at the strictest threshold, demonstrating the critical benefit of viewpoint-covering virtual keyframes in corridor scenes. Among virtual keyframe synthesis baselines, Colmap+VK (36.6% on Scene001), DRGS-Mesh+VK (22.7%), 2DGS-Mesh+VK (27.7%), and MVSplat+VK (11.9%) all fall well short, confirming that existing NVS methods fail to preserve geometry under large viewpoint shifts. The regression-based DFNET performs worst overall (10.8% on Scene001), highlighting the fundamental limitation of pose regression without viewpoint-diverse training data. On standard benchmarks (7Scenes, Cambridge), HLoc retains a slight edge in median error (e.g., 0.02 m/0.8° vs. 0.01 m/0.7° on Chess), but GPVK-VL is within 0.01–0.02 m and 0.1–0.2°, confirming no degradation in standard settings. InLoc, which uses rendered views only for verification rather than retrieval, achieves intermediate results (34.6% on Scene001), underscoring that integrating virtual keyframes into the retrieval and matching stages is essential for the full benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 2D Gaussian Splatting (Huang et al., SIGGRAPH 2024) as its rendering backbone, extending it with confidence-aware depth (from Dust3r) and normal (from DSINE) priors to improve geometric fidelity under large viewpoint changes. It departs from prior geometric regularization approaches (DRGS's aligned monocular depth, 2DGS's normal consistency loss) by incorporating per-pixel confidence weights that down-weight unreliable regions, and from generative novel view synthesis methods (ZeroNVS, diffusion-based approaches) that sacrifice geometric consistency for photorealism. In the localization pipeline, the paper positions itself against structure-based methods (HLoc, InLoc) that rely on a fixed reference database with limited viewpoint coverage, and against regression-based methods (DFNet, LENS) that struggle with domain shift, by synthesizing a comprehensive virtual keyframe database that bridges the viewpoint gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and recurring failure mode in structure-based localization: databases built from one direction (e.g., a corridor) cannot support queries from the opposite direction. The confidence-aware geometric prior approach is a clean, modular addition to 2DGS that could be adopted by other view synthesis or localization pipelines. However, scalability remains a significant open question—mesh extraction via TSDF fusion limits the method to relatively small scenes, and virtual keyframe placement is restricted to a 2D eye-level plane, precluding aerial or multi-floor applications. The evaluation, while thorough on the authors' own dataset, is limited to 6 indoor scenes for the primary large-viewpoint-change benchmark, and the method's advantage on standard benchmarks (7Scenes, Cambridge) is modest, suggesting the gains are most pronounced in the specific large-viewpoint-change regime the paper targets.
