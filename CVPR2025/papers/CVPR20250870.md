## Problem

Closed-loop traffic simulation for autonomous driving must simultaneously satisfy user-defined controllability objectives (e.g., generating safety-critical collision scenarios) and realism constraints (matching real-world driving distributions), yet existing generative methods face a fundamental gradient conflict between these two objectives that is amplified in safety-critical contexts. Prior approaches either reweight the objectives or apply uniform classifier guidance across all agents, which degrades performance when individual agents' realism and controllability gradients oppose each other.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 3 seconds of per-agent history trajectories (position, velocity, relative distance, and time-to-collision features) along with a rasterized map encoded by ResNet-18, and generates 10 seconds of future multi-agent trajectories in a closed-loop fashion at 2 Hz (0.5 s intervals). At each diffusion step, the model receives the current noisy state-action trajectory, the map context, and the diffusion step index k; the number of controllable agents K and the generation horizon T are tunable at inference. The output is a full scene trajectory (state-action pairs for all N agents) that is fed back into the next simulation step, making the generation autoregressive over time.

## Method

CCDiff formulates closed-loop scenario generation as a Constrained Factored MDP with a Decision Causal Graph (DCG) that encodes which agents causally influence each other's actions. A transformer-based scene encoder with temporal attention and spatial cross-attention discovers the DCG by combining learned attention weights with a kinematic memory mask based on time-to-collision thresholds, then a graph-based community detector ranks agents by causal influence to identify the top-K most interactive agents. At inference, CCDiff applies a two-part guidance: (1) a causal classifier-free guidance that geometrically interpolates between unconditional ego-only encoding and causal-interventional encoding conditioned on the DCG parents, and (2) a masked classifier-based guidance that applies reward gradients (collision, off-road) only to the top-ranked agents, explicitly suppressing gradients from agents whose realism and controllability objectives conflict. Training uses standard DDPM with classifier-free guidance, where counterfactual conditions are introduced by randomly replacing the DCG with a diagonal matrix so the model learns both causal and non-causal encodings.

## Results

On the nuScenes dataset with 5 controllable agents (K=5), CCDiff achieves a Controllability Score (CS) of 0.56 and Realism Score (RS) of 0.88, outperforming the closest competitor CTG (CS=0.41, RS=0.84). At the full agent set (K=Full), CCDiff reaches CS=0.74 and RS=0.76, while CTG achieves CS=0.56 and RS=0.77. In long-horizon generation (T=4s, 5 controllable agents), CCDiff attains CS=0.89 and RS=0.54, surpassing CTG (CS=0.78, RS=0.53) and all other baselines in the multi-objective Pareto proximity (GD/IGD). CCDiff achieves best or second-best performance in 10 out of 12 metrics across the multi-agent comparison.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCDiff most directly competes with CTG (Guided Conditional Diffusion), which is the closest baseline: CCDiff wins on CS at K≥3 (e.g., 0.74 vs 0.56 at K=Full; 0.67 vs 0.48 at K=10) while CTG marginally edges out on RS at K=10 (0.82 vs 0.80) and K=Full (0.77 vs 0.76). Against STRIVE, CCDiff trades off: STRIVE achieves much higher CS (0.93 vs 0.67 at K=10) but suffers severe realism degradation (RS=0.19 vs 0.80), confirming that CCDiff's causal masking resolves the controllability–realism conflict that STRIVE cannot. SimNet and BITS are dominated by CCDiff on both CS and RS across nearly all K values (e.g., at K=Full: CCDiff CS=0.74/RS=0.76 vs SimNet CS=0.59/RS=0.61 and BITS CS=0.37/RS=0.65). TrafficSim is the only baseline that beats CCDiff on CS at K=4 (0.48 vs 0.44), but CCDiff leads on RS by a wide margin (0.91 vs 0.57) at that setting. In long-horizon generation (Table 2), CCDiff outperforms CTG on CS at T=4s (0.89 vs 0.78) and T=5s (0.78 vs 0.67) while maintaining comparable RS, and dominates STRIVE and BITS on both metrics as the horizon extends.

## Relation to Prior Work

CCDiff extends the diffusion-based traffic simulation line (CTG, SceneDiffuser) by replacing the standard classifier-based guidance with a causally-structured variant. It builds on the scene-transformer architecture from language-guided traffic simulation and the constrained optimization formulation from CTG/BITS, but departs from prior work by automatically discovering a Decision Causal Graph (DCG) via factorized attention and TTC-based masking, then using that graph to (i) derive a causal form of classifier-free guidance and (ii) mask out agents whose gradients conflict with the realism objective. This contrasts with earlier approaches that inject explicit rules (STL, LLM prompts, hand-specified causal graphs) as external constraints, and with reweighting strategies that trade off controllability against realism at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent tension in AV safety evaluation: generating scenarios that are both user-controllable (e.g., collision-inducing) and physically realistic, without one objective degrading the other. The CFMDP formulation and causal-masking mechanism offer a principled way to resolve gradient conflicts rather than heuristically balancing them. However, the causal reasoning pipeline depends on hand-tuned hyperparameters (TTC threshold, sparsity constraint, community-detection parameters) that are difficult to validate independently, and the authors themselves note the need for a dedicated traffic-reasoning benchmark. Broader impact hinges on whether the causal-structure-in-diffusion paradigm generalizes beyond the nuScenes/tbsim setting to more diverse or higher-fidelity simulators.
