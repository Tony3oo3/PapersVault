## Problem

Object-level foundation models like Grounding DINO and Florence-2 fuse visual and textual features, making it difficult to interpret which image regions drive a specific detection. Gradient-based attribution methods lose visual localization precision due to this multimodal fusion, while perturbation-based methods (e.g., D-RISE) produce noisy saliency maps that obscure fine-grained interpretability. No existing approach can precisely identify the minimal set of critical sub-regions whose removal would invalidate a detection.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a target bounding box with category label (the specific detection to explain) as input. The image is sparsified into approximately 100 sub-regions via SLICO superpixel segmentation, and the method performs only forward passes (black-box, gradient-free). The output is an ordered ranking of all sub-regions by importance and a normalized pixel-level saliency map. For models without confidence scores (e.g., Florence-2), the search relies solely on IoU between predicted and target boxes.

## Method

Visual Precision Search frames saliency map generation as a submodular subset selection problem over superpixel sub-regions. A novel submodular function combines two scores: a clue score (max IoU × confidence over all predicted boxes matching the target) that identifies regions supporting accurate detection, and a collaboration score (1 minus the best matching score when the region is removed) that captures regions with strong combinatorial effects. Greedy submodular optimization iteratively selects sub-regions, with attribution scores computed from marginal effects between consecutive selections. The paper proves the function satisfies diminishing returns and monotonic non-negativity, guaranteeing a (1−1/e) approximation bound. This gradient-free design bypasses the localization errors introduced by backpropagation through vision-text fusion layers.

## Results

On Grounding DINO, Visual Precision Search achieves Insertion AUC of 0.5459 (MS COCO), 0.7419 (RefCOCO), and 0.3695 (LVIS rare), surpassing the strongest baseline D-RISE by 23.7%, 20.1%, and 31.6% respectively. On Florence-2, the method improves Deletion AUC by 50.7% (MS COCO) and 66.9% (RefCOCO) over D-RISE, while also achieving the best Point Game (0.9583) and Energy Point Game (0.2519) on MS COCO. The method also outperforms all baselines on failure-interpretation tasks, with ESR reaching 69.73% for misclassified MS COCO samples versus 50.26% for D-RISE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D-RISE [33] is the strongest baseline across all settings, but VPS outperforms it on every faithfulness and location metric for both Grounding DINO and Florence-2, with the largest gaps on Deletion (50.7–66.9% for Florence-2) and Insertion (23.7–31.6% for Grounding DINO). D-HSIC [31] is consistently weaker than D-RISE, particularly on Insertion (e.g., 0.3776 vs. 0.4412 on MS COCO for Grounding DINO) and Point Game (0.7328 vs. 0.9497). Among gradient-based methods, ODAM [54] is the most competitive, occasionally matching or exceeding VPS on Energy Point Game for LVIS rare (0.2050 vs. 0.1785) and RefCOCO (0.3529 vs. 0.3566), but it lags substantially on Insertion and Deletion due to visual-textual gradient fusion issues. Grad-CAM [37] and SSGrad-CAM++ [49] perform worst overall, with Insertion scores below 0.25 on MS COCO detection and Deletion scores above 0.15, confirming that gradient-based attribution is poorly suited to multimodal foundation models. VPS achieves the best or near-best score on every metric in every table, with the only exception being Energy Point Game on LVIS rare where ODAM edges ahead by 0.027.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both gradient-based (Grad-CAM, ODAM, SSGrad-CAM++) and perturbation-based (D-RISE, D-HSIC) attribution methods for object detection, arguing that multimodal feature fusion in foundation models like Grounding DINO and Florence-2 breaks the assumptions underlying both families. Rather than backpropagating through fused vision-text representations or sampling random perturbations, it repurposes submodular optimization from combinatorial optimization (Edmonds, Fujishige) as a structured search over superpixel sub-regions, guided by a novel objective combining a clue score (IoU-weighted confidence) and a collaboration score (sensitivity to region removal). The approach also extends prior failure-explanation work, which relied on qualitative visualizations, into a quantitative framework with an Explaining Successful Rate metric.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As object-level foundation models are deployed in safety-critical settings like autonomous driving, the inability to explain *why* a model detected or missed an object is a practical liability; this paper provides the first submodular-optimization-based attribution method with theoretical approximation guarantees tailored to such models. Key limitations include the linear scaling of inference time with sub-region count (making fine-grained search expensive), the dependence on ground-truth boxes for evaluation, and the reduced effectiveness on MLLM-based detectors that do not output per-class confidence scores. Open questions include whether the submodular framework can be adapted to confidence-free MLLM outputs and whether the superpixel granularity can be increased without prohibitive compute cost.
