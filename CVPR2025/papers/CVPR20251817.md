## Problem

Existing image-based planar 3D reconstruction methods rely on 2D/3D plane detection, cross-view plane matching/tracking, and large-scale plane annotations, producing coarse results that lose geometric detail. These approaches treat planes as special visual features in keypoint-based pipelines rather than directly optimizing 3D plane primitives, failing to fully exploit the structural expressiveness of planar representations for indoor scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251817/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes posed multi-view RGB images of indoor scenes and produces a set of 3D rectangular planar primitives (each parameterized by center, quaternion rotation, and four double-direction radii) that are merged into a final planar surface reconstruction. Monocular depth and normal maps from pretrained foundation models (Metric3Dv2 or Omnidata) serve as pseudo-labels for supervision, eliminating the need for any plane annotations. The optimized primitives can also be sampled to initialize Gaussian Splatting methods for novel view synthesis.

## Method

PlanarSplatting directly optimizes ~2,000 explicit 3D rectangular plane primitives by differentiable splatting them into 2.5D depth and normal maps, supervised by monocular geometry cues from foundation models. The key technical contribution is a novel rectangle-based plane splatting function that replaces the standard anisotropic Gaussian kernel: it computes sigmoid-based weights along the X and Y axes of each plane using double-direction radii, with a hyperparameter λ that increases exponentially during optimization to progressively sharpen the rectangular boundary. A plane splitting operation (triggered when radii gradients exceed a threshold) refines geometry during training, and a CUDA implementation of the forward/backward splatting pipeline enables full scene reconstruction in approximately 3 minutes. After optimization, adjacent primitives with normal angle error < 25° and offset distance < 0.1 m are greedily merged into final plane instances.

## Results

On ScanNetV2 (100 scenes), PlanarSplatting with Metric3Dv2 supervision achieves the best geometry quality with a Chamfer Distance of 4.23 and F-score of 73.76, outperforming the strongest annotation-based baseline AirPlanes (Chamfer 5.30, F-score 64.92) by ~20% in Chamfer. It also attains the best Planar Fidelity (5.72) and Planar Chamfer (8.19) among all methods. On ScanNet++ (30 scenes), the same variant leads in geometry (Chamfer 9.01, F-score 50.68) and Planar Fidelity (17.17), while remaining competitive on segmentation metrics. Notably, PlanarSplatting requires no 2D/3D plane annotations during training, unlike PlanarRecon and AirPlanes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest annotation-based method AirPlanes, PlanarSplatting (Metric3Dv2) wins on geometry (Chamfer 4.23 vs 5.30; F-score 73.76 vs 64.92) and Planar Fidelity (5.72 vs 8.76), but AirPlanes retains a slight edge on segmentation VOI (2.268 vs 2.310) and SC (0.568 vs 0.560), as well as Planar Accuracy (7.98 vs 10.65). Compared to the geometry-only baselines (SR+RANSAC, Metric3Dv2+RANSAC, 2DGS+RANSAC), PlanarSplatting dominates across nearly all metrics: it improves Chamfer by 22% over SR+RANSAC (4.23 vs 5.40) and by 70% over 2DGS+RANSAC (4.23 vs 14.15), while also achieving substantially better Planar Fidelity (5.72 vs 9.42 and 40.02 respectively). On ScanNet++, the same pattern holds: PlanarSplatting leads in Chamfer (9.01 vs 13.75 for AirPlanes) and F-score (50.68 vs 32.58), confirming generalization beyond the training distribution. The key practical advantage is that PlanarSplatting achieves these gains without any plane annotations, whereas PlanarRecon and AirPlanes both require 2D/3D plane labels during training. In the NVS integration (Table 4), initializing 3DGS/2DGS from PlanarSplatting planes improves PSNR by ~1 dB (25.47 vs 24.42 for 3DGS) while reducing total optimization time from 12.2 min to 5.6 min and Gaussian count from 1.27M to 0.37M.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant pipeline in indoor planar reconstruction—detect 2D/3D planes per image, match/track across views, then merge—by directly optimizing explicit 3D rectangular plane primitives via differentiable splatting, eliminating all intermediate detection and matching steps. Conceptually, it repurposes the splatting paradigm from Gaussian Splatting (3DGS, 2DGS) but replaces Gaussian primitives with shape-aware rectangular planes and uses monocular foundation models (Metric3Dv2, Omnidata) as supervision instead of plane annotations. It is positioned against PlanarRecon (end-to-end 3D volume learning) and AirPlanes (dense mesh + plane embeddings), both of which require plane annotations, and against the concurrent AlphaTablets which uses alpha-mapped planar tablets but at ~2 hours per scene versus PlanarSplatting's 3 minutes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3-minute per-scene reconstruction speed (enabled by CUDA) makes large-scale planar surface curation practical for the first time, and the annotation-free training paradigm removes a major bottleneck that has limited prior learning-based methods. The seamless initialization of Gaussian Splatting from reconstructed planes—yielding better NVS quality with fewer points and less training time—suggests a broader pattern where fast structural priors can replace expensive densification in radiance field methods. The stated limitation (inability to handle curved surfaces) and the open question of extending to more flexible geometric primitives remain important gaps for real-world indoor scenes that contain significant non-planar geometry.
