## Problem

Existing text-to-video diffusion models are limited to short clips (16–384 frames), and naively extending them autoregressively by conditioning on the last frame(s) of the preceding chunk produces hard scene cuts, temporal inconsistencies, progressive quality degradation, and severe video stagnation. No prior method simultaneously maintains smooth chunk transitions, preserves long-term scene/object identity, and sustains rich motion over multi-minute videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250838/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt as input and produces a temporally consistent long video of up to 1200+ frames (~2 minutes) at 720×720 resolution. Generation proceeds in two stages: a 256×256 base video is first synthesized autoregressively in 16-frame chunks, then enhanced to 720×720 via a high-resolution refiner operating on overlapping 24-frame chunks. The first 16-frame chunk is produced by an off-the-shelf text-to-video model (e.g., Modelscope), and all subsequent chunks are generated conditioned on the preceding chunk's features.

## Method

StreamingT2V augments a pre-trained text-to-video diffusion model (Modelscope) with two memory modules. The Conditional Attention Module (CAM) provides short-term memory by encoding the last 8 frames of the preceding chunk with a frame-wise encoder and injecting features into the UNet's skip connections via temporal multi-head attention, enabling smooth transitions without restricting motion. The Appearance Preservation Module (APM) provides long-term memory by expanding the CLIP image token of a fixed anchor frame to 16 tokens, concatenating with text tokens, and injecting the mixed representation into every cross-attention layer with learnable per-layer weights, preventing drift in object and scene identity. For the enhancement stage, the paper applies SDEdit on a high-resolution refiner (MS-Vid2Vid-XL) to overlapping 24-frame chunks (8-frame overlap), using shared noise across the overlap and a randomized blending step that probabilistically merges latent codes from consecutive chunks at a randomly sampled boundary, eliminating visible seams without additional training.

## Results

On a 50-prompt test set with 240-frame autoregressive video generation, StreamingT2V achieves the best MAWE score of 52.3 (nearly 30% lower than the second-best OpenSoraPlan at 72.9) and the highest CLIP text-alignment score of 31.73. It also attains the second-lowest SCuts score of 0.04, behind only FreeNoise (0.0), which produces near-static videos. Metrics remain stable over time, with MAWE ranging from 43.25 to 46.92 and CLIP from 32.45 to 31.79 across 20-frame windows from frame 120 to 220.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StreamingT2V outperforms all eight baselines on MAWE, with a 28.6% improvement over the closest competitor OpenSoraPlan (52.3 vs. 72.9) and a 99.1% improvement over DynamiCrafter-XL (52.3 vs. 176.7). On CLIP text-alignment, StreamingT2V leads all methods (31.73), with the nearest competitors FreeNoise (31.55) and OpenSora (31.54) trailing by less than 0.2 points, while CLIP-image-encoder-based methods like SVD (23.95) and I2VGen-XL (27.28) show significant misalignment. On SCuts, StreamingT2V (0.04) is second only to FreeNoise (0.0), but the authors note FreeNoise produces near-static videos, making its zero-cut score uninformative; in contrast, SparseCtrl produces 137× more scene cuts (5.48) and OpenSoraPlan 6× more (0.24). Qualitatively, all competing methods exhibit video stagnation (frozen background, minimal object motion) or severe quality degradation over 240 frames, whereas StreamingT2V maintains smooth motion and consistent appearance throughout.

## Relation to Prior Work

StreamingT2V builds directly on pre-trained text-to-video diffusion models (Modelscope/UNet-based) and the autoregressive extension paradigm used by SVD, I2VGen-XL, SEINE, and DynamiCrafter, which condition on the last frame(s) of a preceding chunk. It departs from this lineage by replacing frame concatenation and CLIP-image-embedding conditioning with an attention-based Conditional Attention Module (CAM) that naturally handles mismatched frame counts without zero-padding, and by adding a lightweight Appearance Preservation Module (APM) for long-term scene/object memory. The video enhancement stage repurposes SDEdit on a high-resolution model with a novel randomized blending scheme for seamless chunk merging, drawing on ControlNet-style feature injection for the CAM architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that all existing autoregressive video generation methods suffer from either temporal inconsistency (scene cuts) or motion stagnation when extended beyond their training length, establishing a clear gap that StreamingT2V addresses with its dual short/long-term memory design. Practical impact is significant for content creation pipelines requiring multi-minute coherent videos, and the CAM module offers a generalizable architectural pattern for conditioning on variable-length context. Limitations include dependence on base model quality (256×256 generation before upscaling), evaluation on only 50 prompts and a single base architecture for quantitative results, and the DiT generalization (OpenSora) being supported only by visual inspection rather than rigorous benchmarks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to even longer videos without quality drift, and whether the attention-based conditioning advantage persists as base models improve.
