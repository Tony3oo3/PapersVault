## Problem

No comprehensive pipeline exists for bimanual category-agnostic hand-object interaction reconstruction from a single monocular RGB video. Prior methods either rely on pre-scanned object templates, handle only single-hand interactions, or require multi-view inputs. The bimanual setting introduces severe and complex occlusions between two hands and an unknown object that degrade both hand pose and object shape reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251239/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular RGB video (T frames) and corresponding camera parameters (obtained via HLoc) as input, and produces 3D Gaussian splats for both hands and the object in canonical space, which can then be posed to render novel-view images with arbitrary hand, object, and camera configurations. Hand Gaussians are stored as a single canonical set shared between left and right hands (flipped along the x-axis), while object Gaussians are stored as a separate canonical set. Foreground masks for hands and objects are extracted via SAM 2 to guide per-region rendering losses.

## Method

BIGS decomposes optimization into two stages: a single-subject step that optimizes hand and object Gaussians independently, and an interacting-subjects step that jointly refines hand translations to enforce 3D contact alignment. For hands, a single canonical Gaussian set is shared across both hands via x-axis flipping, with MANO LBS weights predicted by a deformation MLP and geometry/appearance parameters from TriplaneNet-conditioned MLPs; temporal smoothness and LBS regularization are applied. For the unknown object, the key novelty is incorporating a Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) loss from a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model (conditioned via textual inversion and PiDi-boundary ControlNet) to hallucinate occluded surfaces from random virtual viewpoints around the canonical object. A contact regularization term (L_contact) in the second stage minimizes the distance between hand and object translations to correct 3D misalignment caused by limited viewpoints.

## Results

On the ARCTIC bimanual dataset, BIGS achieves CDo=1.28 cm² and F10=83.93% for object reconstruction, MPJPEl=24.63 mm and MPJPEr=24.35 mm for hand pose, and CDl=46.11 / CDr=31.28 cm² for contact reconstruction, substantially outperforming HOLD (CDo=2.07, F10=63.92, MPJPEl=27.13, CDl=105.92, CDr=123.54). On HO3Dv3 (one-hand, full view), BIGS achieves CDr=11.1 cm², CDo=0.3 cm², F10=96.7%, and MPJPEr=23.9 mm, marginally surpassing HOLD (11.3, 0.4, 96.5%, 24.2). Rendering quality is dramatically improved: PSNR jumps from 12.83 (HOLD) to 24.87 dB and LPIPS drops from 0.32 to 0.05 in the two-hand setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HOLD [17] (NeRF-based, single-hand), BIGS wins decisively on every metric in the bimanual ARCTIC setting: contact error drops by 56–75% (CD_l 46.11 vs 105.92, CD_r 31.28 vs 123.54), object F10 improves by 20 points (83.93 vs 63.92), and rendering PSNR more than doubles (24.87 vs 12.83 dB). Against HaMeR [47] (Transformer hand pose), BIGS improves left-hand MPJPE from 27.23 to 24.63 mm while matching right-hand accuracy (24.35 vs 24.37 mm). Against HLoc [55] (SfM-based object), BIGS reduces object Chamfer distance from 4.24 to 1.28 cm² and nearly doubles F10 (83.93 vs 40.57%). On HO3Dv3 one-hand, BIGS marginally beats HOLD (CD_r 11.1 vs 11.3, F10 96.7 vs 96.5) but dramatically outperforms the older baselines HOMan [24] (CD_r 78.2, MPJPE_r 32.0), iHOI [76] (CD_r 75.8, MPJPE_r 38.4), and DiffHOI [77] (CD_r 68.8, MPJPE_r 32.3), confirming that 3DGS-based optimization with diffusion priors is far more effective than SDF or diffusion-rendering approaches for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

BIGS extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework [29] to the bimanual hand-object interaction domain, departing from the NeRF-based HOLD [17] which was the first category-agnostic monocular HOI method but limited to single-hand scenarios. The key conceptual departure is a two-stage optimization (single-subject then interacting-subjects) combined with a symmetry-based hand Gaussian sharing scheme that exploits the left-right mirror relationship to accumulate 3D information under severe occlusions. For object reconstruction, the paper repurposes score distillation sampling [50] with Stable Diffusion and ControlNet boundary conditioning to hallucinate unseen surfaces, while relying on MANO priors for hands—effectively splitting the prior-knowledge burden between the two subjects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first pipeline to jointly address bimanual, category-agnostic, and monocular constraints in HOI reconstruction, filling a gap where prior methods required either pre-scanned templates, multi-view inputs, or single-hand assumptions. The practical speed advantage of 3DGS over NeRF-based alternatives makes real-time or near-real-time bimanual interaction capture more feasible for VR/AR and robot teleoperation applications. However, the method is limited to rigid objects and hands with known LBS weights, leaving articulated unknown objects (e.g., deformable tools, cloth) as an open problem. The reliance on a shared canonical Gaussian across both hands also assumes a single person's hands, which may not generalize to multi-person or highly asymmetric hand shapes.
