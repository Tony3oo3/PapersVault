## Problem

Autonomous UAVs require temporally stable semantic segmentation for reliable navigation, yet existing video segmentation methods either sacrifice inference speed through expensive temporal context modeling or lose accuracy through key-frame reuse, and none explicitly enforce prediction consistency in their architecture while remaining lightweight enough for onboard real-time processing. Additionally, publicly available aerial datasets are sparsely labeled, making semi-supervised training essential but underexplored for video segmentation in this domain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251439/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB video frames at 736×1280 resolution along with a 3×3 global registration homography (derived from UAV pose in deployment, or estimated via feature-based keypoint matching in experiments) to align the previous frame's prediction to the current frame. It outputs per-pixel semantic segmentation logits for each frame, propagated sequentially through the entire video so that each frame's prediction is a weighted blend of the current image-model estimate and the temporally aligned prior prediction. The base model is Hiera-S + UPerNet (43.17M parameters), and SSP adds only 0.7% additional parameters for the similarity layer.

## Method

SSP propagates predictions as logits through a video by linearly interpolating the current frame's image-model output with the previous frame's aligned prediction, where per-pixel interpolation weights are computed by a learnable convolutional similarity layer operating on concatenated stride-4 feature maps of both frames. A global projective homography aligns the past prediction to the current frame to compensate for camera motion, which is significantly cheaper than optical flow and naturally suited to aerial scenes dominated by ego-motion. For semi-supervised training on sparsely labeled datasets, a temporally consistent knowledge distillation procedure uses a larger frozen teacher (Hiera-B+ UPerNet at 1440×2560) to supervise all frames via KL-divergence on logits, with the teacher's predictions post-processed by bidirectional optical flow warping and occlusion masking to enforce temporal consistency before distillation. A consistency loss (optical-flow-warped L2 between consecutive predictions with an occlusion mask) is jointly optimized with the distillation loss to further regularize the student's temporal behavior.

## Results

On UAVid, SSP achieves 79.75 mIoU and 92.10 TC, improving temporal consistency by 12.5% over the base image model (79.02 TC) while gaining 0.5% mIoU. On RuralScapes, SSP reaches 64.00 mIoU and 94.06 TC, a 6.7% TC increase over the base model. With knowledge distillation, KD-SSP further improves to 80.63 mIoU and 91.53 TC on UAVid, and 64.56 mIoU and 94.00 TC on RuralScapes, achieving the highest mIoU among all compared methods. Inference speed is 95 FPS on A100 and 29.3 FPS on Jetson Orin, only marginally slower than the base image model (104/31.4 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSP outperforms all four video baselines on both mIoU and TC across both datasets. Against DFF [55], SSP gains +2.55 mIoU and +8.82 TC on UAVid (79.75 vs 77.20; 92.10 vs 83.28) while running 4× faster (95 vs 23 FPS). Against NetWarp [12], SSP improves TC by +9.91 on UAVid (92.10 vs 82.19) with comparable mIoU, and is 6× faster (95 vs 15 FPS). Against TCB [33] (both ppm and ocr variants), SSP achieves +10.75 to +10.75 TC on UAVid (92.10 vs 81.35/82.22) with slightly higher mIoU, while being 5× faster (95 vs 18–19 FPS). The key advantage is that SSP's global registration alignment avoids the expensive optical flow computation that limits DFF, NetWarp, and TCB, enabling near-image-model inference speed while delivering the highest temporal consistency among all compared methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends TFC's [26] prediction propagation via linear interpolation from key-frame pairs to full-video sequences, replacing the fixed cosine similarity with a learnable convolutional similarity layer that can capture local motion patterns. It adapts ETC's [28] knowledge distillation framework for temporal consistency but repurposes it for semi-supervised training on sparsely labeled UAV datasets, using a larger teacher model to supervise all frames rather than jointly training teacher and student. A key departure from prior temporal-consistency methods (Miksik et al. [35], STGRU [36]) is the use of global projective registration (homography) instead of per-pixel optical flow for aligning past predictions, motivated by the camera-motion-dominated nature of aerial footage and the availability of UAV pose data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in autonomous UAV perception: achieving high temporal consistency without the computational overhead of feature-enhancement or optical-flow-based video methods, making it suitable for real-time onboard inference on edge hardware like Jetson Orin. The global registration assumption of a planar ground is a clear limitation in complex 3D scenes, though the authors demonstrate robustness through the adaptive similarity weights. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-aerial video with more complex motion, whether the KD teacher could be further improved with semi-supervised or pseudo-label methods, and whether the method's gains hold at higher resolutions or with more semantic classes.
