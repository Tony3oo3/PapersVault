## Problem

Existing multi-view stereo methods struggle to generalize across diverse scene domains, require a known depth range to construct cost volumes, and are often tied to a fixed number of source views. No prior system simultaneously addresses domain generalization, arbitrary depth ranges, variable source frame counts, and 3D-consistent depth prediction in a single zero-shot model.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 640×480 reference RGB image plus a variable number N of source RGB images, each with known relative camera poses and intrinsics. It outputs a dense depth map at the same 640×480 resolution in metric scale aligned with the input pose coordinate system. Notably, no depth range is provided at test time; the model infers valid depth bounds from camera geometry, and the number of source frames can vary per reference image without architectural changes.

## Method

MVSA combines a ResNet18 feature extractor with a transformer-based pipeline. A Cost Volume Patchifier tokenizes the |D|×H/4×W/4 cost volume via two strided convolutions, each concatenated with monocular features from the first two blocks of a Depth Anything V2 ViT-Base reference image encoder, avoiding information loss from naive patchification. A Mono/Multi Cue Combiner ViT-Base (initialized from DINOv2) processes these tokens with multi-level monocular feature injection at blocks 2, 5, 9, and 11 to fuse single-view and multi-view cues. For view-count and scale agnosticism, each source frame's geometric metadata is processed by a per-view MLP predicting a score and weight, aggregated via softmax-weighted sum, while relative pose and depth-hypothesis values are normalized by scene-level maxima. A cascaded cost volume strategy infers initial log-spaced depth bins from camera geometry, makes a coarse prediction, then rebuilds the cost volume with refined bins for the final depth estimate, enabling operation over arbitrary depth ranges without test-time range specification.

## Results

On the Robust Multi-View Depth Benchmark (RMVDB), MVSA achieves an average absolute relative depth (rel) of 2.7% and inlier percentage (τ) of 77.0% across KITTI, ETH3D, DTU, and Tanks & Temples, without requiring per-image depth range information. This outperforms the closest multi-view baseline MAST3R with triangulation (rel 3.4%, τ 70.1%) and the Robust MVD Baseline (rel 6.3%, τ 56.0%). MVSA also surpasses the best single-view method UniDepthV2 (rel 3.3%, τ 68.9%) and the best method with per-image range MVSFormer++ regression (rel 3.6%, τ 69.8%), despite not being given the depth range at test time. On the ScanNet Mesh Evaluation, MVSA (not trained on ScanNet) achieves a Chamfer distance of 5.66 and F-Score of 0.652, outperforming several ScanNet-trained methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVSA outperforms all baselines in its operating setting (d: GT poses, no per-image depth range). Against MAST3R with triangulation—the strongest competitor—MVSA improves average rel from 3.4% to 2.7% and τ from 70.1% to 77.0%, with particularly large gains on Tanks & Temples (rel 1.3 vs 3.4, τ 95.0 vs 67.3). Against the Robust MVD Baseline, MVSA more than halves the average rel (2.7 vs 6.3) and nearly doubles τ (77.0 vs 56.0). MVSA also beats the best method that *is* given per-image depth range (MVSFormer++ regression, rel 3.6, τ 69.8) despite not receiving that information, and surpasses the best single-view method UniDepthV2 (rel 3.3, τ 68.9). On the ScanNet Mesh Evaluation, MVSA (untrained on ScanNet) achieves Chamfer 5.66 and F-Score 0.652, outperforming ScanNet-trained methods like ATLAS (Chamfer 7.38, F-Score 0.636) and 3DVNet (Chamfer 7.22, F-Score 0.621), though it trails VoRTX (Chamfer 5.77, F-Score 0.703) on F-Score. The only setting where MVSA does not dominate is on ETH3D τ (62.9), where MAST3R triangulation achieves 63.0—essentially a tie—while MVSA leads on all other per-dataset metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

MVSA builds directly on SimpleRecon's cost-volume-based MVS architecture, extending it with a transformer-based backbone and a view-count-agnostic metadata mechanism that replaces SimpleRecon's fixed eight-source-frame design. The cascaded cost volume strategy is adapted from stereo matching work (Gu et al., 2020) but repurposed here to handle unknown depth ranges at test time rather than merely refining resolution. A key departure from both prior MVS and monocular depth work is the Mono/Multi Cue Combiner, which fuses features from a pretrained monocular ViT (Depth Anything V2) with the multi-view cost volume via a novel Cost Volume Patchifier, bridging the two traditionally separate lines of research.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first MVS system to simultaneously address domain generalization, arbitrary depth ranges, variable view counts, and 3D consistency, making it a practical drop-in for applications like AR and robotics where scene scale and camera setup are unpredictable. The limitation that known camera intrinsics and poses are still required (unlike emerging pose-free methods like MASt3R-SLAM) is a clear open direction, as is the absence of temporal consistency enforcement. The finding that a model trained exclusively on synthetic data outperforms domain-specific models on held-out real benchmarks suggests that diverse synthetic training may be a viable path to general-purpose 3D perception without expensive real-world annotation.
