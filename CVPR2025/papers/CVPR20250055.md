## Problem

Camera-based 3D human walking pose estimation (HWPE) degrades severely under self-occlusion and at long distances due to per-pixel information loss, while existing mmWave radar pose estimators are restricted to controlled, radial-direction movements because of the inherent directionality of Doppler and range measurements. This paper is the first comprehensive comparison of mmWave versus camera for HWPE and addresses mmWave's directionality limitation to enable robust pose estimation across arbitrary walking trajectories and distances.

## Input / Output

The model consumes two types of mmWave images per sensor: a time-range (positional) image formed by time-windowing the FMCW signal magnitude, and a time-Doppler (motional) image obtained via short-time Fourier transform along the time axis, both uniformly resized. In the multi-view setup, these are acquired from two cross-view AWR1843 3TX-4RX radars placed 5 m from a 10 m x 10 m region of interest. The output is a sequence of 3D joint coordinates (p in R^{3 x T_p x J}) for a predefined set of body joints over T_p temporal frames, produced by averaging the fused multi-modal, multi-view representations and applying a linear projection.

## Method

MVDoppler-Pose encodes each mmWave image with a MobileViT backbone and performs a dual-stage, multi-layered fusion at the token level within the backbone's iterative blocks: a Cross-Modal Fusion (CMF) module first merges positional and motional tokens from the same sensor via cross-attention, followed by a Cross-View Fusion (CVF) module that integrates the corresponding modality tokens across sensors. Sparse attention (top-k over n patches) is applied to limit computation and suppress noise in the inherently sparse mmWave images. Training is guided by a cross-domain loss that augments standard MPJPE with a motional distance metric computed as a cross-product of joint positions at offset time intervals, evaluated via Pearson correlation specifically on limb joints, encouraging the network to learn both spatial accuracy and temporal coherence of the sinusoidal gait patterns.

## Results

On the MVDoppler-Pose dataset under self-occluded walking scenarios, the proposed multi-view multi-modal MVDoppler-Pose achieves MPJPE of 63.27 mm and temporal correlation of 0.53 at y<8.5 m, improving to 58.24 mm and 0.54 at y>11.5 m, demonstrating near-distance-invariant performance. In contrast, the best vision baseline (Wei et al.) degrades from 88.16 mm to 133.80 mm MPJPE over the same distance range. The single-view multi-modal variant already surpasses all vision baselines beyond 8.5 m (88.32 mm vs. 133.80 mm at y>11.5 m), while the multi-view setup further reduces MPJPE by ~25 mm across all distance levels compared to single-view.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four vision baselines (Sun, Wei, Li, Zhao), the multi-view multi-modal MVDoppler-Pose achieves substantially lower MPJPE at all distance levels (63.27–58.24 mm vs. 88.16–141.01 mm) and higher temporal correlation (0.53–0.54 vs. 0.17–0.35), with the gap widening dramatically beyond 8.5 m where vision models degrade most severely. Against the mmWave positional baseline (Wang et al., Person-in-WiFi), the multi-view multi-modal model reduces MPJPE by ~48 mm at close range (63.27 vs. 111.69) and ~51 mm at long range (58.24 vs. 109.02). Against the mmWave motion-only baselines (Zhou et al. and Tang et al.), the multi-view multi-modal model improves MPJPE by ~44–58 mm and correlation by 0.24–0.25 across all distances. The single-view multi-modal variant already outperforms all vision baselines beyond 8.5 m (88.32 mm vs. 133.80 mm for Wei et al.), confirming mmWave's inherent distance resilience. The multi-view setup provides an additional ~25–28 mm MPJPE improvement over single-view, effectively resolving the directionality limitation that causes single-view mmWave to degrade at ±90° walking angles.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MVDoppler dataset [8] (originally for gait classification) into a 3D pose estimation benchmark and builds on two parallel lines of mmWave sensing: positional approaches (point-cloud or raw-tensor processing) and motional/Doppler-based approaches. It repurposes cross-modal fusion architectures from autonomous driving (DeepFusion, MFT, PointAugmenting) and the MobileViT backbone, but introduces a dual-stage (cross-modal then cross-view) sparse cross-attention pipeline specifically tailored to the four-way input structure of multi-modal, multi-view mmWave signals. The cross-domain loss draws on motion-guided pose estimation [34] but adapts it to the mmWave context by applying Pearson correlation to limb-joint velocity patterns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first systematic, controlled comparison showing mmWave can outperform vision for self-occluded walking pose estimation beyond ~8.5 m, and that multi-view mmWave fuses to achieve near-uniform accuracy across all walking directions—something neither single-view mmWave nor vision can do. However, validation is limited to a single subject in a minimally cluttered 10×10 m area with a narrow set of walking activities, leaving open critical questions about multi-person scenes, real-world clutter, and generalization to diverse body types. The released MVDoppler-Pose dataset and the multi-view fusion recipe provide a concrete starting point for the still-nascent mmWave HWPE community.
