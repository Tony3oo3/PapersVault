## Problem

Adam is the de facto optimizer for training neural fields, yet its preference over simpler alternatives has not been rigorously justified, and second-order methods like L-BFGS are impractical in stochastic settings. This paper asks whether curvature-aware diagonal preconditioners can accelerate stochastic training of neural fields, and under what architectural conditions they are effective versus counterproductive.

## Input / Output

The neural fields consume low-dimensional spatial coordinates as input—2D pixel coordinates for image regression, 3D point coordinates for binary occupancy, and 3D coordinates plus viewing direction for NeRF—and produce continuous signal values (RGB, occupancy probability, or volume density plus directional color). Training proceeds via mini-batches of sampled coordinates (e.g., 512 for images, 50,000 for 3D occupancy), with the network regressing the target signal at each coordinate rather than processing a fixed-size tensor.

## Method

The paper develops a theoretical framework linking Adam's second-moment estimate to a diagonal preconditioner derived from the Gauss-Newton matrix (Diag(J^T J)), then proves two key theorems: sine, Gaussian, wavelet, and sinc-activated neural fields yield dense Hessian-vector products (making curvature-aware preconditioners like ESGD's equilibrated preconditioner D^E effective), while ReLU/ReLU(PE)-activated fields yield sparse Hessian-vector products (rendering such preconditioners largely inert). ESGD, which applies the row 2-norm of the Hessian as a diagonal preconditioner recomputed every N iterations, is the primary algorithm validated. Empirically, ESGD consistently outperforms Adam for Gaussian, sine, and wavelet activations across 2D image reconstruction (DIV2K), 3D binary occupancy (Stanford 3D Scanning), and NeRF (LLFF), achieving comparable or better PSNR/SSIM with fewer iterations, while Adam remains superior for ReLU-PE networks as predicted by the sparsity theorem.

## Results

On the NeRF task (LLFF dataset), ESGD achieves comparable or slightly better test PSNR than Adam while requiring fewer iterations and less optimization time: e.g., on 'fern', ESGD reaches PSNR 24.41 in 120K iterations (270.81s) versus Adam's 24.38 in 200K iterations (368.43s). On 2D image reconstruction (DIV2K, lion instance), a wavelet-based neural field trained with ESGD achieves 29.36 PSNR at the 10th epoch, outperforming Adam's 26.61 PSNR. For 3D binary occupancy (armadillo), ESGD produces visibly improved mesh reconstruction at the 1000th epoch compared to Adam, and for NeRF (Gaussian activation), ESGD achieves lower reconstruction error (0.0695) versus Adam (0.0743).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Adam (the de facto standard for neural field training), ESGD achieves comparable or marginally better PSNR with roughly 40% fewer iterations on most LLFF NeRF scenes (120K vs. 200K), translating to ~30% less optimization time; however, Adam still wins on a few scenes (horns, room, fortress, leaves) where it converges to slightly higher PSNR in fewer iterations. Against AdaHessian (both E and J variants), ESGD shows clearly superior convergence in both 2D image reconstruction (Fig. 5) and 3D binary occupancy (Fig. 6), with the gap widening for higher-mode signals where AdaHessian's per-iteration Hessian updates become noisy. Against Shampoo, ESGD is both faster to converge and more computationally efficient per iteration, since Shampoo requires expensive Kronecker-product computations; Shampoo also degrades on the 3D occupancy task. Against K-FAC, ESGD similarly outperforms in convergence speed while maintaining comparable per-iteration cost. Overall, ESGD is the strongest curvature-aware preconditioner in stochastic settings for non-traditional activations (Gaussian, sine, wavelet), while Adam remains preferable for ReLU-PE networks due to their sparse Hessian-vector products.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the preconditioning literature (originating in numerical linear algebra) to the specific domain of neural field training, building directly on ESGD (Dauphin et al., 2015) and connecting it to the broader family of curvature-aware optimizers including AdaHessian, Shampoo, and K-FAC. Its key conceptual departure is a theoretical framework that links Adam's second-moment estimate to the diagonal of the Gauss-Newton matrix, then uses Hessian-vector product sparsity to explain why curvature-aware preconditioners help sine/Gaussian/wavelet activations but not ReLU-PE. This positions the work at the intersection of optimization theory and the neural fields literature (NeRF, SIREN, Gaussian INR, wavelet INR), providing a principled explanation for the empirical observation that Adam is the de facto choice while second-order methods like L-BFGS fail in stochastic settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper gives practitioners a clear, theory-backed rule: use ESGD (or similar curvature-aware diagonal preconditioners) for sine, Gaussian, or wavelet-activated neural fields, and stick with Adam for ReLU-PE. The Hessian-vector product sparsity theorems (dense for non-ReLU, sparse for ReLU-PE) are a clean mechanistic explanation that could guide future optimizer design. A notable limitation is that the authors do not explore whether alternative preconditioners beyond the Gauss-Newton diagonal could improve ReLU-PE training, leaving a practical gap since ReLU-PE remains widely deployed. Open questions include whether the sparsity-based theory extends to deeper architectures, different loss functions, or mixed-activation networks.
