## Problem

Partial-to-partial shape matching requires not only finding correspondences between two partially observed shapes but also determining which points actually have a counterpart on the other shape. Existing methods—DPFM (learning-based) and SM-COMB/GC-PPSM (axiomatic)—struggle with accurate overlap prediction, are slow, or depend heavily on good feature initialisation and spatial alignment, leaving this realistic setting under-explored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251580/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two partial 3D mesh shapes and per-vertex features extracted either from spatial coordinates or from DINOv2 image features (aggregated over 100 rendered views via the Diff3f pipeline with backface culling). It produces a row-stochastic functional map encoding soft point-to-point correspondences in the spectral domain, plus a per-vertex overlap prediction mask in [0,1] indicating whether each vertex lies in the shared region. During training, ground-truth overlap masks are used to prune features on one shape before computing the functional map, but at test time the overlap predictor relies solely on learned features.

## Method

EchoMatch introduces correspondence reflection: it models the X→Y and Y→X matchings separately as row-stochastic soft point maps (softmax of feature dot products), then composes them (P_X = Π_XY × Π_YX) so that overlapping points return to their spatial neighbourhood while non-overlapping points scatter. The neighbourhood-aggregated reflection scores are refined by a Diffusion Net that learns to smooth symmetry artefacts and spatial inconsistencies. The full pipeline embeds this overlap predictor within a functional map framework with feature pruning (restricting F_Y to the ground-truth overlap region during training) and is trained with three losses: a functional map L2 loss, a PointInfoNCE contrastive loss on features, and a weighted binary cross-entropy overlap loss. The Siamese feature extractor and the use of DINOv2 image features (rather than spatial coordinates) make the method robust to unaligned and non-isometric shapes.

## Results

EchoMatch achieves state-of-the-art mean IoU (mIoU) on all three partial-to-partial shape matching benchmarks. With DINOv2 image features, it reaches 84.72% on CP2P24, 84.75% on PSMAL, and 64.68% on the most challenging BeCoS dataset, outperforming the previous best (DPFM) by 10.57, 11.08, and 13.66 points respectively. With spatial coordinates (XYZ), EchoMatch still leads on CP2P24 (80.10%) and PSMAL (72.71%), while on BeCoS with XYZ features all methods degrade substantially due to the dataset's unaligned, unnormalised, non-isometric nature. The paper also reports geodesic error curves (Figure 5) where EchoMatch achieves the best AUC across all datasets with DINOv2 features.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EchoMatch outperforms all three baselines (DPFM, GC-PPSM, SM-COMB) on every dataset with both XYZ and DINOv2 features. Against DPFM (the only other learning-based method), EchoMatch gains the largest margin on BeCoS with DINOv2 features (64.68 vs. 51.02, +13.66 mIoU), where DPFM's overlap predictions are particularly scattered. The two axiomatic methods, GC-PPSM and SM-COMB, perform reasonably with XYZ features but degrade severely with DINOv2 features (e.g., GC-PPSM drops from 69.29 to 49.66 on CP2P24), because they rely on integer linear programming in low dimension and are sensitive to feature quality. EchoMatch's advantage is most pronounced on the non-isometric, unaligned BeCoS dataset, where the correspondence reflection mechanism provides a principled overlap signal that the baselines lack. On CP2P24 with XYZ features, EchoMatch leads DPFM by 16.24 points (80.10 vs. 63.86), demonstrating that the reflection-based overlap predictor is effective even without image-based features.

## Relation to Prior Work

EchoMatch extends the DPFM [3] functional map framework for partial shape matching by introducing a correspondence reflection mechanism: composing forward and backward soft point maps to predict overlap regions, a concept inspired by mutual nearest neighbors and cycle-consistent matching but generalized with neighbourhood aggregation and DiffusionNet smoothing. It departs from prior partial-to-partial methods (SM-COMB, GC-PPSM) that rely on integer linear programming and geometric consistency constraints, offering a faster, more scalable learning-based alternative. The paper also follows Diff3f [20] in projecting DINOv2 image features onto 3D meshes, showing these foundation-model features substantially outperform spatial coordinates for alignment-robust matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Partial-to-partial shape matching is the most realistic yet least explored setting in 3D correspondence, with only three prior methods; EchoMatch sets a clear new SOTA and provides a principled, interpretable overlap predictor (correspondence reflection) that could transfer to other partial-data problems in visual computing. The finding that DINOv2 features dramatically outperform spatial coordinates on unaligned shapes (e.g., 64.68 vs. 52.40 mIoU on BeCoS) signals a broader shift toward foundation-model features for geometry tasks. Open questions include handling points invisible in all rendered views, full discretization invariance, and whether the reflection principle generalizes beyond pairwise matching to multi-shape or temporal correspondence.
