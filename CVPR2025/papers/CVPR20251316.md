## Problem

Existing lightweight 3D occupancy prediction methods rely on the Lift-Splat-Shoot pipeline, which produces sparse BEV features (less than 50% of grids receive valid image features) and inaccurate depth estimation. Meanwhile, multimodal fusion approaches that jointly process LiDAR and camera data impose heavy computational costs, precluding real-time inference. The paper addresses how to exploit LiDAR geometric and semantic priors to improve view transformation accuracy while maintaining or exceeding real-time speeds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251316/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes T-frame multi-view RGB images at 256×704 resolution from surround-view cameras and LiDAR point clouds (with 3+d features per point) as joint input. It produces a 3D semantic occupancy voxel map where each voxel is classified into one of 17 categories (16 object classes plus 'empty'). On Occ3D-nuScenes the prediction volume spans −40 m to 40 m in X/Y and −1 m to 5.4 m in Z at 0.4 m voxel resolution; on SurroundOcc-nuScenes it extends to ±50 m in X/Y at 0.5 m resolution. The image backbone is ResNet-50 with FPN aggregation at 1/16 scale, and the LiDAR branch uses SPVCNN for sparse voxel encoding.

## Method

SDG-OCC introduces two key modules. First, the Semantic and Depth-Guided (SDG) view transformation replaces LSS's uniform depth sampling: a multi-task head produces per-pixel semantic masks and depth distributions, which are combined with LiDAR-projected depth maps via same-class local diffusion and bidirectional bilinear discretization to generate a small set of high-precision virtual point seeds, reducing redundant features and improving depth accuracy. Second, the Fusion-to-Occupancy-Driven Active Distillation (FOAD) module fuses camera and LiDAR BEV features through dynamic neighborhood attention with gated attention to handle cross-modal misalignment, then unidirectionally distills multimodal knowledge into image features only in LiDAR-identified occupied regions (active vs. inactive), with adaptive scaling to balance region sizes. Two variants are offered: SDG-Fusion (fusion only, 133 ms, mIoU 51.66) for maximum accuracy and SDG-KL (fusion + distillation, 83 ms, mIoU 50.16) for real-time inference, both using a channel-to-height occupancy head for efficient 3D output.

## Results

On Occ3D-nuScenes, SDG-Fusion achieves a SOTA mIoU of 51.66% with an inference time of 133 ms, outperforming the previous best multimodal method RadOcc-LC (49.38% mIoU, 3333 ms) by 2.28 mIoU while being ~25× faster. The lighter SDG-KL variant reaches 50.16% mIoU at 83 ms, the fastest inference among all compared methods. On the more challenging SurroundOcc-nuScenes validation set, SDG-Fusion* achieves 52.2% mIoU, surpassing the prior best multimodal method DAOcc (30.5%) by over 21 mIoU, using only a ResNet-50 backbone at 256×704 resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDG-Fusion (51.66 mIoU) surpasses the strongest prior multimodal method RadOcc-LC (49.38 mIoU) by 2.28 mIoU on Occ3D-nuScenes, while running 25× faster (133 ms vs. 3333 ms) and using a lighter ResNet-50 backbone instead of Swin-B. SDG-KL (50.16 mIoU, 83 ms) also beats RadOcc-LC by 0.78 mIoU at the fastest inference speed among all compared methods, making it the only real-time multimodal approach. Against the best camera-only method COTR (46.2 mIoU, Swin-B), SDG-Fusion gains 5.46 mIoU with a smaller backbone. On SurroundOcc-nuScenes, SDG-Fusion* (52.2 mIoU) dramatically outperforms the previous best multimodal baseline DAOcc (30.5 mIoU) by 21.7 mIoU, and even exceeds the camera-only FlashOcc* (44.1 mIoU) by 8.1 mIoU. The ablation confirms that SDG view transformation contributes +10.67 mIoU over the FlashOcc baseline, while the fusion module adds a further +7.08 mIoU.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Lift-Splat-Shoot (LSS) pipeline for 2D-to-3D view transformation, replacing its uniform per-pixel depth distribution with a semantic- and depth-guided virtual point generation strategy anchored by sparse LiDAR co-points. It adopts FlashOcc's channel-to-height transformation for the occupancy head and borrows neighborhood attention from the Neighborhood Attention Transformer for cross-modal feature fusion. Conceptually, it departs from prior multimodal occupancy methods (OccFusion, Co-Occ, OccGen) that perform late-stage feature concatenation or adaptive weighting, instead injecting LiDAR geometric and semantic priors early in the pipeline to reshape the view transformation itself, and further introduces a unidirectional occupancy-driven distillation that selectively transfers fused knowledge back to camera features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving SOTA mIoU (51.66) at 83 ms inference on Occ3D-nuScenes, roughly 40× faster than the prior best multimodal method (RadOcc-LC at 3333 ms), making real-time multimodal occupancy prediction viable for deployment. The core idea—using sparse LiDAR depth and semantic masks as anchors to replace dense per-pixel depth distributions—offers a general recipe for reducing view-transformation cost in any LSS-based pipeline. Open questions include how the method degrades when LiDAR is unavailable or severely degraded (e.g., heavy rain), whether the semantic-guided diffusion generalizes to other sensor pairs (radar, event cameras), and whether the distillation gap between SDG-Fusion and SDG-KL can be closed without sacrificing the speed advantage.
