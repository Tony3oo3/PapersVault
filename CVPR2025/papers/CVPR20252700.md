## Problem

Existing self-supervised visual correspondence methods are limited to small, continuous transformations (e.g., high-frame-rate video tracking or high-overlap pixel-to-pixel mapping), making them ineffective for extreme viewpoint changes such as ego-to-exo camera switches or large temporal gaps in video. State-of-the-art predictive approaches like SiamMAE and DINO entangle semantic object identity with spatial cues in their learned representations, causing failures against semantically similar distractor objects in challenging real-world settings like robot imitation learning.

## Input / Output

The correspondence model takes a source RGB image, a binary object segmentation mask in the source view (produced by SAM ViT-H), and a target RGB image, and outputs a binary segmentation mask for the corresponding object in the target view or a no-correspondence signal. During the pseudolabeling phase, the grayscale colorization model consumes a grayscale target image and a colorful source image (or a color-augmented variant) and reconstructs the full RGB target. The correspondence model uses a ViT-B/16 backbone with a linear projection that patchifies the source mask and adds it to the encoded source features before cross-attention decoding.

## Method

Predictive Cycle Consistency (PCC) combines a grayscale colorization pretext task with object-level cycle consistency to bootstrap self-supervised correspondence. First, a two-stage ViT (shared encoder + cross-attention decoder) is trained to reconstruct a target image from its grayscale version conditioned on a colorful source view. Correspondence is then extracted in a black-box manner: augmenting an object's color in the source view and measuring the resulting color-change heatmap in the target output localizes the corresponding object. Cycle consistency is enforced by running this extraction in both directions and retaining only mutually agreeing object pairs, which filters out spurious matches caused by lighting or semantic confounds. These cycle-consistent pseudolabels train a dedicated correspondence model (ViT with mask projection and cross-attention decoder, Dice + BCE loss), which is then used to generate higher-quality pseudolabels in iterative rounds (3 iterations saturate). The key distinction from prior work is operating cycle consistency at the object-mask level rather than on feature patches, and using the colorization model purely as a spatial probe rather than relying on internal embeddings for KNN matching.

## Results

On the EgoExo4D correspondence benchmark, PCC Iter 3 achieves 41.45 IoU (Exo Query) and 29.89 IoU (Ego Query), surpassing the prior SoTA self-supervised method DINOv2+Registers+SAM (34.78 / 28.92 IoU) by +6.7 and +1.0 IoU respectively, and outperforming all prior supervised approaches (best: XView-XMem+XSegTx at 19.39 / 22.5 IoU). On DAVIS-17 with a 20-frame temporal gap, PCC Iter 3 reaches 70.2 J&Fm, beating DINO ViT-B/8 (66.4) and SiamMAE (60.7). On LVOS, PCC achieves state-of-the-art J&Fm for all temporal gaps above 10 frames, while SiamMAE retains the edge at 5 and 10 frames.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On EgoExo4D, PCC Iter 3 (41.45 Exo IoU) decisively outperforms the strongest self-supervised baseline DINOv2+Registers+SAM (34.78 Exo IoU) by +6.7 IoU, and surpasses all prior supervised methods (best: XView-XMem+XSegTx at 19.39 Exo IoU) by over +22 IoU. SiamMAE (14.18 Exo IoU) and CroCo v2 (9.56 Exo IoU) trail PCC by 27+ and 32+ IoU respectively, confirming that representational approaches struggle with extreme viewpoint changes. On DAVIS-17 (20-frame gap), PCC Iter 3 (70.2 J&Fm) beats DINO ViT-B/8 (66.4) by +3.8 and SiamMAE (60.7) by +9.5. On LVOS, PCC achieves SoTA J&Fm for all temporal gaps above 10 frames, while SiamMAE retains a narrow edge at 5 and 10 frames where precise segmentation matters more than semantic robustness. Notably, PCC's Location Score (0.071 Exo) is substantially better than all baselines, indicating superior spatial localization even when IoU is comparable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repositions grayscale colorization (Vondrick et al., 2018), a technique largely superseded by masked autoencoders like SiamMAE and CroCo v2, as the core predictive task for correspondence extraction. It departs from prior cycle-consistency methods (Wang et al., 2019; Zhou et al., 2016) that operated on dense features or required continuous video by applying cycle consistency at the discrete object-mask level, enabling it to handle extreme view discontinuities. The iterative pseudolabeling loop—where a trained correspondence model replaces the grayscale model to generate cleaner labels—extends the self-training paradigm to correspondence, building on the ViT-B/16 architecture initialized from CroCo v2.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first self-supervised method to achieve competitive (and in fact superior) correspondence under the extreme viewpoint changes of EgoExo4D, a setting critical for robot imitation learning and egocentric scene understanding where camera pose and depth are unavailable. The ablation revealing that grayscale pretraining actually degrades supervised performance (Table 3) clarifies that the gains come from the cycle-consistency extraction pipeline rather than improved representations, a useful diagnostic for the field. Open questions include whether the pipeline can be made fully unsupervised by replacing SAM with self-supervised segmentation, whether it scales to more than two views, and whether the iterative refinement can be made more sample-efficient.
