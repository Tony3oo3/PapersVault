## Problem

Existing remote sensing multimodal benchmarks suffer from three critical limitations: image sizes far below real-world RS resolutions (typically 512×512 vs. 10,000×10,000), annotation quality compromised by GPT-based generation without rigorous human verification, and evaluation dimensions that omit complex reasoning, spatiotemporal change detection, and interactive perception tasks essential for real-world RS decision-making.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250841/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes ultra-high-resolution RS images averaging 8,500×8,500 pixels (with 840 images at 10,000×10,000) paired with text queries, and produces three output formats: multiple-choice VQA answers (2–4 options per question), detailed image captions averaging 379 words in English and 663 in Chinese, and bounding boxes for visual grounding evaluated at IoU thresholds of 0.5 and 0.7. A unique spatiotemporal sub-task requires the model to process two co-registered images of the same location at different times and report object count changes. The benchmark is fully bilingual, with all long-text content translated and cross-checked by six bilingual experts rather than machine translation.

## Method

XLRS-Bench is constructed through a multi-stage pipeline: 1,400 real-world RS images are sourced from detection (DOTA-v2, ITCVD), segmentation (MiniFrance, Toronto, Potsdam), and change detection (HRSCD) datasets, then annotated by 45 domain experts across 16 sub-tasks spanning 10 perception and 6 reasoning dimensions. For image captioning, a semi-automated pipeline divides each image into nine sub-images plus a compressed full image, feeds all ten views to GPT-4o with structured prompts to generate general-to-specific descriptions, and then requires human annotators to correct object counts, refine scene types, and manually identify anomalies. Quality control employs three 15-member annotation groups that cross-validate each other's VQA and grounding annotations, plus a 10-expert MLLM review team that resolves discrepancies and validates question design to prevent GPT-induced linguistic bias. The final benchmark contains 45,942 annotations (32,389 VQA pairs, 12,619 grounding instances, 934 captions) evaluated against 10 MLLMs including GPT-4o, Qwen2-VL, LLaVA variants, and the RS-specific GeoChat in a zero-shot setting.

## Results

On XLRS-Bench VQA tasks (average image size 8,500×8,500), the best-performing model CogVLM2 (Llama3-8B) achieves 39.80% average accuracy across 8 L-2 dimensions in English, while Qwen2-VL (Qwen2-7B) reaches 38.99% in English and 41.10% in Chinese. GPT-4o, despite being a proprietary frontier model, scores only 32.15% (en) and 30.40% (zh), with particularly weak spatiotemporal reasoning (<25%). On visual grounding, even GPT-4o achieves only 46% Acc@0.5 and 5% Acc@0.7, while most open-source models fall below 20% Acc@0.5, highlighting the extreme difficulty of localizing objects as small as 5–10 pixels in ultra-high-resolution imagery.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the 10 evaluated MLLMs, Qwen2-VL and CogVLM2 lead in overall VQA accuracy (38.99% and 39.80% in English), outperforming GPT-4o (32.15%) by roughly 7–8 percentage points, largely due to GPT-4o's weakness on object properties (24.78%) and spatiotemporal reasoning (21.85%). Models supporting higher-resolution inputs (Qwen2-VL, LLaVA-OneVision) consistently outperform CLIP-based encoders like LLaVA-1.5 (22.81%) and the RS-specific GeoChat (22.03%), confirming that resolution capacity is a critical bottleneck. On visual grounding, GPT-4o achieves the best Acc@0.5 of 46% on XLRS-Bench-EN, yet this is still far below the 51% it achieves on the much smaller 512×512 VRSBench, and all other models drop below 20%. For captioning, GPT-4o-mini leads in English BLEU-1 (38.29) while LLaVA-OneVision edges ahead (41.12), and GPT-4o dominates Chinese ROUGE_L (36.41). The RS-specific GeoChat performs at the bottom across all three task types, suggesting that domain-specific training on small images does not transfer to ultra-high-resolution scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of general multimodal benchmarks (MME, MMBench, MME-Realworld) and RS-specific benchmarks (VRS-Bench, LHRS-Bench, RSIEval), which it identifies as insufficient in three dimensions: image size (existing RS benchmarks use 512×512 slices vs. real-world 10,000×10,000), annotation quality (prior work relies heavily on GPT-generated labels), and evaluation breadth (prior RS benchmarks focus on basic perception, omitting complex reasoning, planning, and spatiotemporal change detection). Rather than building on a specific method, XLRS-Bench repurposes the VQA, captioning, and visual grounding paradigms from general MLLM evaluation and extends them to an ultra-high-resolution RS setting with 16 sub-tasks spanning 10 perception and 6 reasoning dimensions. The semi-automated captioning pipeline (GPT-4o pre-annotation + human verification) draws on the trend of LLM-assisted data construction but adds stringent manual calibration to avoid the linguistic biases documented in MME-Realworld.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

XLRS-Bench reveals that current MLLMs are far from ready for real-world RS applications: visual grounding accuracy drops to 0.5% at IoU 0.7 even for GPT-4o, and spatiotemporal reasoning (change detection) remains below 25% for all models. The finding that GPT-4o underperforms open-source models like Qwen2-VL on RS-specific reasoning tasks challenges the assumption that frontier proprietary models dominate all multimodal domains. Key open questions include how to develop MLLMs that can process 10,000×10,000 imagery without compressing 5–10 pixel objects to a single pixel, and whether domain-specific pretraining on RS spatiotemporal data can close the gap. The benchmark's bilingual support and 45,942 human-verified annotations make it a practical resource for the RS+MLLM community.
