## Problem

Existing LLM-based long video understanding methods suffer from two critical limitations: they struggle to accurately identify question-related frames (relying on sparse sampling or CLIP image-text similarity with limited context), and they lose essential visual information when converting frames to brief captions, forcing the LLM to make biased guesses. This paper addresses how to systematically locate and enrich question-relevant visual information from videos lasting minutes to over an hour.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250118/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a long video (sampled at 0.2–0.5 FPS depending on benchmark) and a text question, with optional subtitles for the Video-MME benchmark. The video is first converted entirely into a text document via per-frame VLM captions, after which all subsequent processing occurs in language space. The output is a final answer (multiple-choice or open-ended) accompanied by a confidence score and a chain-of-thought reasoning trace, enabling transparency in the decision process.

## Method

DrVideo reframes long video understanding as a long-document retrieval and augmentation task. It first densely samples frames and generates per-frame captions with a VLM (LaViLa or LLaVA-NeXT) to form an initial video document, then retrieves top-K key frames via OpenAI embedding cosine similarity between the question and document. A multi-stage agent interaction loop follows: a planning agent judges whether the current document suffices for answering, and if not, an interaction agent identifies additional missing frames and selects an augmentation type per frame (detailed caption vs. question-specific VQA), which the document augmentation module executes via LLaVA-NeXT with tailored prompts. This loop iterates until the planning agent confirms sufficiency or a maximum iteration count is reached, after which a zero-shot chain-of-thought answering module produces the final prediction. The entire framework is training-free and operates in a single language space, avoiding the token-length and long-range reasoning bottlenecks of direct visual-token concatenation.

## Results

On EgoSchema (3-min videos), DrVideo with GPT-4 achieves 66.4% accuracy on the 500-question subset and 61.0% on the full set, outperforming the previous LLM-based SOTA (VideoAgent [14] at 62.8%/60.2%). On MovieChat-1K (10-min videos), DrVideo reaches 93.1% global accuracy and 56.4% breakpoint accuracy, surpassing MovieChat+ (71.2%/49.6%) by 21.9% and 6.8% respectively. On the Video-MME long split (avg. 44-min videos), DrVideo achieves 51.7% without subtitles and 71.7% with subtitles, the latter outperforming Gemini 1.5 Flash (68.8%), GPT-4o mini (63.4%), and GPT-4V (56.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest LLM-based baselines (LLoVi and VideoAgent), DrVideo wins decisively across all three benchmarks: +5.2%/+6.2% on EgoSchema (GPT-4), +8.3%/+24.8% on MovieChat-1K global/breakpoint, and +6.3%/+4.0% on Video-MME w/o subs / w/ subs. On Video-MME with subtitles, DrVideo (71.7%) surpasses Gemini 1.5 Flash (68.8%), GPT-4o mini (63.4%), and GPT-4V (56.9%), but trails GPT-4o (72.1%) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (77.4%). Without subtitles, DrVideo (51.7%) edges out Claude 3.5 Sonnet (51.2%) and all open-source MLLMs except Qwen2-VL-72B (62.2%), but falls short of GPT-4V (53.5%) and above. On MovieChat-1K, DrVideo's 93.1% global accuracy is a substantial 21.9% improvement over the previous best open-source MLLM (MovieChat+, 71.2%) and 27.7% over LLoVi (58.3%), though it is below the proprietary agent Sullam Jeoung et al. (84.8%). The ablation in Table 4 confirms that the retrieval module contributes +5.2% and the multi-stage agent loop adds a further +2.0% over the LLoVi-style baseline (57.4% → 62.6% on EgoSchema with GPT-3.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

DrVideo extends the LLoVi paradigm of converting long videos into text descriptions for LLM-based reasoning, and the VideoAgent approach of using an LLM agent to iteratively locate question-relevant frames. Its key conceptual departure is reframing long video understanding as a document retrieval and augmentation problem: rather than sparse frame sampling followed by CLIP-based image-text similarity search (as in VideoAgent), DrVideo performs dense sampling to build a full text document, retrieves key frames via embedding similarity, and then uses a multi-stage agent loop to dynamically identify and augment missing information in language space. This positions the work at the intersection of retrieval-augmented generation and agent-based systems, leveraging LLMs' proven strengths in long-document comprehension rather than pushing visual token sequences through a fixed-length context window.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DrVideo provides a training-free, researcher-friendly recipe for long video understanding that outperforms heavily-engineered proprietary MLLMs (e.g., Gemini 1.5 Flash, GPT-4o mini) on the Video-MME long split when subtitles are available, suggesting that structured document retrieval can be a competitive alternative to scaling visual encoders. The ablation finding that increasing the number of retrieved key frames (K=5 → 20) degrades performance highlights an underexplored tension between information coverage and noise in LLM-based video reasoning. Open questions include whether the approach scales to hour-long or multi-hour videos without iterative degradation, how to reduce dependence on proprietary LLMs (the GPT-4 vs. Mistral gap is substantial), and whether the document-retrieval abstraction can be extended to other long-sequence multimodal tasks beyond video.
