## Problem

Federated class-incremental learning (FCIL) must simultaneously handle spatial heterogeneity (non-IID data across clients), temporal heterogeneity (sequential arrival of new classes), and resource heterogeneity (diverse client capabilities), yet existing methods address these challenges in isolation. This leads to interference between client/task updates, catastrophic forgetting, or communication costs that scale with the number of clients. The paper identifies the absence of a unified, interference-free aggregation mechanism that preserves both efficiency and accuracy under all three heterogeneity dimensions.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet) as input, processed by a frozen DINO-pretrained ViT-B/16 backbone, and outputs a class label from the unified label space spanning all incremental tasks. Rather than communicating full parameter matrices, each client uploads only its sampled Fourier coefficients (|Ω| scalars, e.g., 3000 out of d²=589,824 for d=768), and the server reconstructs the global update via inverse 2D-DFT. The classifier output is structured as a product of task-specific softmax scores and a prototype-based routing probability, enabling automatic task assignment at inference without explicit task labels.

## Method

pFedMxF replaces standard LoRA low-rank factorization with a frequency-domain parameterization: each client-task pair (κ,t) is assigned a disjoint set of 2D-DFT frequency coordinates Ω_κ^t sampled without replacement from the full frequency grid, and only the corresponding Fourier coefficients are trained via backpropagation. This guarantees natural dual orthogonality—spatial (across clients) and temporal (across tasks)—so that updates from different client-task pairs reside in orthogonal subspaces and aggregate without interference. The server performs Mixture of Frequency aggregation by simply summing the frequency components from all participating clients, achieving perfect reconstruction of the global update with O(1) memory regardless of client count. An Auto-task Agnostic Classifier (AAC) decomposes the joint prediction into task-specific classifiers φ_t(·) and a prototype-based router r(·) that estimates p(t|x) via Gaussian similarity to task prototypes, preventing decision-boundary interference between tasks. The entire trainable parameter budget is O(|Ω|) per client (e.g., 3000 parameters at 3.2% sparsity), comparable to LoRA rank r=2, while matching or exceeding the accuracy of rank r=16 baselines.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (10 tasks, 10 classes/task) under quantity-based label imbalance (α=6), pFedMxF achieves 71.3% last-task accuracy and 80.7% average accuracy, outperforming the strongest baselines InfLoRA (70.5%/78.4%) and PILoRA (69.3%/78.5%). Under severe heterogeneity (α=2), pFedMxF maintains 57.0%/64.9% while L2P collapses to 2.6%/5.6% and GLFC drops to 13.1%/37.7%. On TinyImageNet (α=6), pFedMxF reaches 76.3%/82.7%, again leading all baselines. The method uses only |Ω|=3000 frequency parameters (≈3.2% sparsity of the full parameter space), comparable to LoRA rank r=2, yet matches or exceeds the performance of rank r=16 baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

pFedMxF outperforms all 11 baselines across both CIFAR-100 and TinyImageNet under all tested heterogeneity levels. Against the closest competitors InfLoRA (CVPR 2024) and PILoRA (ECCV 2024), pFedMxF gains +0.8%/+2.2% (last/avg) on CIFAR-100 at α=6, with the gap widening to +0.7%/+2.4% at α=2 where both LoRA-based methods degrade more sharply. Against FCIL-specific baselines, pFedMxF leads LGA by +6.8%/+7.1% and TARGET by +10.4%/+9.4% on CIFAR-100 (α=6), with the advantage growing substantially under severe non-IID (α=2: +35.4%/+24.0% over LGA). Classical CIL methods (EWC, LwF, iCaRL) are outperformed by 13–35 points in average accuracy, and L2P fails catastrophically under severe heterogeneity (2.6% last accuracy at α=2 vs. pFedMxF's 57.0%). In the aggregation ablation (Table 6), pFedMxF surpasses FedAvg by +2.9%/+2.9% and FedStack by +2.1%/+1.8% average accuracy on CIFAR-100 (α=6), while maintaining O(1) memory versus FedStack's O(K) scaling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning for federated class-incremental learning (FCIL), departing from prior approaches that address spatial, temporal, and resource heterogeneity in isolation. Where InfLoRA and PILoRA enforce orthogonality through explicit constraints on LoRA matrices or prototype-guided module selection, pFedMxF instead assigns orthogonal frequency components in the 2D DFT domain, achieving interference-free aggregation without explicit orthogonal projection. The Auto-task Agnostic Classifier (AAC) also departs from similarity-based routing used in prompt/adapter-based FCIL methods, replacing inference-time similarity computation with a lightweight Gaussian-kernel router over task prototypes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The constant-memory property (O(1) regardless of client count K) is practically significant for large-scale federated deployments where FedStack's O(K) storage becomes prohibitive, and the frequency-domain framing provides a clean theoretical guarantee of interference-free updates. However, the empirical gains over the strongest baselines (InfLoRA, PILoRA) are modest (1–3% average accuracy), and the method is validated only on ViT-B/16 with 10 clients and 10 tasks, leaving open questions about scalability to larger client pools, more tasks, or non-square parameter matrices where 2D DFT does not directly apply. The random frequency assignment strategy also raises the question of whether learned or structured allocation could further improve performance.
