## Problem

Existing keypoint localization methods learn shape and semantic priors purely from narrow-domain training data, resulting in poor generalization to unseen domains, objects, or in few-shot and zero-shot settings. This paper asks whether the rich structural and semantic knowledge embedded in large-scale pre-trained generative models (specifically Latent Diffusion Models) can be repurposed for keypoint localization without requiring large task-specific datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250340/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB object image as input, which is encoded by a frozen VAE encoder into a latent feature map. It produces one heatmap per keypoint type (e.g., 17 channels for human or animal keypoints), from which coordinates are decoded via argmax. The keypoint type information is encoded as learnable condition embeddings (dimension 768 for SD1.5) that interact with the latent features through modified cross-attention layers, rather than being fed as a separate input modality.

## Method

GenLoc exploits the cross-attention mechanism inside a frozen Stable Diffusion U-Net to extract keypoint heatmaps, avoiding the infeasibility of fine-tuning the U-Net to output multi-channel heatmaps through a fixed VAE decoder. The core CKHE module reverses the softmax direction in cross-attention: instead of softmax over condition tokens (as in standard LDM), it applies softmax over spatial latent tokens, making each keypoint condition an independent query that produces a localized spatial distribution while keeping adjacent keypoints decoupled. Training optimizes only the keypoint condition embeddings and LoRA adapters (r=16) in the U-Net up-block cross-attention layers, preserving the generative prior. The proposed Heatmap Expectation Loss (HEL) replaces MSE by computing the negative log of the integral of the ground-truth heatmap over the predicted distribution, yielding larger gradients on hard (low-confidence) positive samples and leveraging the normalization property of the correlation map for more effective optimization.

## Results

On AP-10K (animal keypoint localization), GenLoc achieves 80.1% AP, outperforming the previous best CLAMP-L (77.8%) by 2.3% and HRFormer-B (73.5%) by 6.6%. On COCO Keypoint (human), GenLoc reaches 77.6% AP, slightly exceeding ViTPose (77.4%) and HRNet (76.8%). In the cross-domain Human-Art setting (trained on COCO, tested on Human-Art), GenLoc achieves 67.0% AP, surpassing LocLLM (64.8%) by 2.2%. In few-shot (5 images/species) and zero-shot settings, GenLoc shows the largest margins, e.g., 62.8% AP in 5-shot (vs. ScarceNet 53.3%) and up to 76.0% AP in zero-shot (vs. CLAMP 46.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenLoc outperforms all 16 compared baselines across every evaluation setting. On AP-10K, it beats the strongest prior method CLAMP-L (77.8% AP) by 2.3% and HRFormer-B (73.5%) by 6.6%; conventional heatmap methods like HRNet (72.8%), RTMPose (72.2%), and SimplePose (69.9%) trail by 7–11%. On COCO Keypoint, GenLoc (77.6% AP) edges out ViTPose (77.4%) and HRNet (76.8%), and substantially surpasses regression-based RLE (74.0%) and DeepPose (53.8%). In the cross-domain Human-Art test, GenLoc (67.0% AP) leads LocLLM (64.8%) by 2.2% and ViTPose (53.8%) by 13.2%, demonstrating that generative priors transfer better across visual domains than task-specific training. In few-shot (5 images/species) AP-10K, GenLoc (62.8% AP) exceeds ScarceNet (53.3%) by 9.5% and semi-supervised methods like FixMatch (54.4%) and FlexMatch (59.6%) by 8.4–3.2%, while using only labeled data. In zero-shot cross-species transfer, GenLoc achieves 50.2–76.0% AP versus CLAMP's 22.0–48.4%, with the largest gap (35.6% AP) when training and testing species belong to different animal orders (Canidae→Felidae).

## Relation to Prior Work

GenLoc builds on Latent Diffusion Models (Stable Diffusion v1.5) as a frozen pre-trained backbone, departing from prior diffusion-for-perception work (Marigold, DMP, GED) that fine-tunes the U-Net to directly output task-specific predictions. It also extends the line of condition-optimization methods (SLiMe, Diff-Tracker, Hedlin et al.) that exploit cross-attention maps in diffusion models, but introduces two key departures: the CKHE module reverses the softmax direction so that keypoint conditions act as queries over spatial tokens (modeling keypoint distribution rather than aggregating condition information), and the Heatmap Expectation Loss replaces element-wise MSE with an expectation-based overlap objective. The use of LoRA for lightweight adaptation of attention layers bridges the gap between pure condition optimization and full fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that rich generative priors from large-scale pre-trained diffusion models can be effectively repurposed for fine-grained spatial localization without requiring large task-specific training sets, yielding strong few-shot (62.8% AP with 5 images/category) and zero-shot (20%+ AP over CLAMP) generalization. This suggests a broader principle: the spatial-semantic knowledge encoded in generative models' attention mechanisms is transferable to dense prediction tasks through targeted architectural modifications rather than output-head retraining. Open questions include the computational cost of running a full diffusion U-Net at inference time, whether the approach extends to 3D keypoints or multi-object scenes, and whether the gains persist as the number of keypoint types grows substantially beyond 17.
