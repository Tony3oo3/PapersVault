## Problem

Adapting 3D Gaussian Splatting from finite-scale objects to unbounded 3D city generation is non-trivial: representing a 10 km² city scene requires billions of Gaussians, demanding hundreds of gigabytes of VRAM and incurring prohibitive storage overhead. Existing NeRF-based city generators (CityDreamer, InfiniCity) are computationally inefficient, while all prior 3D-GS generators are limited to small-scale assets and cannot scale to unbounded scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251441/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a local patch of BEV maps (height field H, semantic map S, binary density map D) along with camera intrinsic K and extrinsic T matrices, and produces a single rendered city image (evaluated at 960×540) in one feed-forward pass. Notably, the Gaussian attributes predicted are RGB values only (C_A=3), with scale, rotation, and opacity set to fixed defaults, and the number of active points is reduced from ~20M to ~31.7K via ray-intersection visibility filtering, keeping VRAM constant regardless of scene extent.

## Method

GaussianCity introduces BEV-Point, a compact intermediate representation that decouples Gaussian attributes into position-related components (stored as BEV maps) and style-related components (a per-instance style lookup table), retaining only ray-visible points so VRAM stays constant as scene scale grows. The BEV-Point Decoder converts unstructured BEV points into Gaussian attributes via a Point Serializer (a grid-based integer ordering that restores spatial locality lost relative to NeRF's ray-sampled points), a Point Transformer that captures structural and contextual relationships among serialized points, and Modulated MLPs that condition on per-instance style codes and instance labels. Training uses a GAN objective combining L1, VGG perceptual, and adversarial losses, optimized with Adam on 8 V100 GPUs for 200K iterations, achieving 10.72 FPS inference on an A100—approximately 60× faster than CityDreamer.

## Results

On GoogleEarth, GaussianCity achieves the best scores across all five metrics: FID 86.94, KID 0.090, depth error 0.136, camera error 0.057, and a runtime of 93 ms per 960×540 frame on an A100. On KITTI-360, it attains FID 29.5 and KID 0.017, outperforming all street-view baselines. The headline efficiency gain is a 60× speedup over the closest NeRF-based competitor CityDreamer (93 ms vs. 5580 ms), while also improving generation quality on every metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On GoogleEarth, GaussianCity wins on every metric against all five baselines. The most striking margin is runtime: 93 ms versus 5580 ms for CityDreamer (60× faster), 620 ms for SceneDreamer, and 193/167 ms for SGAM/PersistentNature, while simultaneously achieving the lowest FID (86.94 vs. 97.38 for CityDreamer) and KID (0.090 vs. 0.096). CityDreamer is the closest quality competitor but suffers from seam artifacts due to its two-field compositing, whereas GaussianCity avoids these via a single Gaussian rasterizer. On KITTI-360, GaussianCity outperforms StyleGAN2 (FID 29.5 vs. 31.9; KID 0.017 vs. 0.021) and UrbanGIRAFFE (FID 29.5 vs. 39.6; KID 0.017 vs. 0.036), with GSN and GIRAFFE trailing by a wide margin (FID 160.0 and 112.1 respectively). InfiniCity is discussed qualitatively only (code unavailable) and is noted to suffer from structural distortions because all buildings share the same semantic label.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) for efficient explicit scene representation, and unbounded 3D city generation via BEV-based compositional models (CityDreamer, InfiniCity, PersistentNature). Prior 3D-GS generators (DreamGaussian, HumanGaussian, LucidDreamer) are limited to finite-scale objects or small scenes, while prior unbounded city generators rely on NeRF-based rendering. GaussianCity is the first to bridge these by introducing the BEV-Point representation, which decouples position-related attributes (condensed into BEV maps) from style-related attributes (compressed into a lookup table), ensuring constant VRAM regardless of scene scale. The Point Serializer + Point Transformer decoder specifically addresses the loss of spatial correlation that occurs when moving from NeRF's ray-ordered sampling to unstructured BEV points.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 60× speedup over CityDreamer (10.72 vs 0.18 FPS) makes real-time unbounded city generation practically viable for applications in gaming, film, and VR, and the constant-VRAM BEV-Point representation removes the fundamental scalability barrier that previously made 3D-GS infeasible for city-scale scenes. However, two significant limitations remain: the Manhattan assumption in BEV-Point initialization prevents representation of hollow or overhanging structures, and the decoder only predicts RGB rather than the full Gaussian attribute set (scale, rotation, opacity), constraining expressiveness. Open questions include whether full attribute prediction can be stabilized, whether non-Manhattan geometries can be handled, and whether the approach extends to 4D (dynamic) city generation.
