## Problem

Latent diffusion models face an optimization dilemma: increasing the per-token feature dimension in visual tokenizers improves reconstruction fidelity but significantly degrades generation performance, requiring substantially larger diffusion models and more training iterations to compensate. Existing work either scales up model parameters at prohibitive compute cost or deliberately limits tokenizer capacity, capping the upper bound of generation quality. The paper identifies the root cause as the difficulty of learning well-structured, unconstrained high-dimensional latent spaces from scratch.

## Input / Output

The system takes a single class label as conditioning input and generates 256×256 RGB images. The visual tokenizer (VA-VAE) uses f16 downsampling with variable latent dimensions (d16, d32, d64), and the DiT operates with patch size 1 so that all spatial compression is handled entirely by the VAE. The latent space is continuous (KL-regularized VAE, not discrete VQ), and the diffusion model is trained on pre-extracted latent features rather than raw pixels.

## Method

The core contribution is VA-VAE, which introduces a Vision Foundation model alignment Loss (VF Loss) during tokenizer training to regularize the latent space using features from pretrained models (DINOv2, MAE). The VF Loss comprises two components: a marginal cosine similarity loss enforcing element-wise alignment between VAE latents and projected foundation model features, and a marginal distance matrix similarity loss preserving pair-wise relative structure, both with margins to avoid over-regularization. An adaptive weighting mechanism (gradient-ratio based) balances the VF loss against the reconstruction loss. Paired with LightningDiT—an enhanced DiT incorporating Rectified Flow, SwiGLU FFN, RMSNorm, ROPE, logit normal sampling, and velocity direction loss—the integrated system achieves FID 1.35 on ImageNet 256×256 with a 21.8× convergence speedup over the original DiT.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, LightningDiT with VA-VAE (VF DINOv2) achieves a state-of-the-art FID of 1.35 (with CFG) at 800 epochs, with a tokenizer reconstruction FID (rFID) of 0.28. At just 64 epochs, the system reaches FID 2.11 (with CFG), representing a 21.8× convergence speedup over the original DiT (which requires 1400 epochs to reach FID 2.27). The method also substantially improves reconstruction quality over the standard SD-VAE (rFID 0.28 vs. 0.61) while maintaining superior generative performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LightningDiT with VA-VAE achieves the best FID (1.35 with CFG) among all compared methods, outperforming the closest latent diffusion baselines REPA (1.42) and MDTv2 (1.58) by 0.07 and 0.23 FID respectively, while also surpassing AR methods MAR (1.55) and MagViT-v2 (1.78). In terms of convergence speed, the system reaches FID 2.11 in only 64 epochs, beating DiT (2.27 at 1400 epochs) and SiT (2.06 at 1400 epochs) by a 21.8× margin, and outperforming FasterDiT (2.03 at 400 epochs) with 6× fewer epochs. The method also achieves substantially better reconstruction (rFID 0.28) compared to all SD-VAE-based baselines (rFID 0.61) and the LDM-based MAR (rFID 0.53), while simultaneously delivering superior generation quality. Against the AR baselines, LightningDiT uses far fewer parameters (675M vs. 2.0–3.1B for VAR and LlamaGen) and fewer training epochs (800 vs. 300–1080) to achieve competitive or better FID scores. The only metric where the method trails is IS (295.3 vs. 365.4 for VAR and 319.4 for MagViT-v2), though this is a less commonly used metric in recent literature.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent diffusion framework (LDM) and Diffusion Transformer (DiT) by addressing a specific optimization dilemma: higher-dimensional visual tokenizers improve reconstruction but degrade generation quality. The VF Loss draws a conceptual parallel to the codebook utilization problem in discrete VQGAN tokenizers and to representation alignment in REPA, but applies foundation-model alignment to the tokenizer's latent space rather than the diffusion model's intermediate features. LightningDiT assembles known DiT improvements (Rectified Flow, SwiGLU, RMSNorm, ROPE, logit-normal sampling) into a unified training recipe, positioning itself as a practical baseline rather than a novel architecture. The key departure from prior work is that the alignment target is the VAE encoder's output during tokenizer training, making it a plug-and-play module decoupled from the generative model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The VF Loss offers a simple, architecture-agnostic mechanism to resolve the reconstruction-generation trade-off in latent diffusion systems, with the practical implication that high-dimensional tokenizers no longer require prohibitively large diffusion models or extended training. The mechanistic explanation—linking latent space uniformity (measured via Gini coefficient and KDE) to generation quality—provides a diagnostic tool for future tokenizer design. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to text-to-image and video generation, whether the specific choice of foundation model (DINOv2 vs. MAE) matters at scale, and whether the LightningDiT training recipe generalizes beyond ImageNet class-conditional generation. The paper is limited to ImageNet 256×256 and does not evaluate on open-domain or higher-resolution settings.
