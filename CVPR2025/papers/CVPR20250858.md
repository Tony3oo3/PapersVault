## Problem

CLIP's last-layer self-attention is spatially invariant, causing poor localization for dense prediction tasks like open-vocabulary semantic segmentation. Existing training-free fixes (SCLIP, NACLIP, ClearCLIP) replace the last layer's query-key attention with self-correlation attention (query-query or key-key) but overlook the cross-correlation (query-key) properties that capture rich spatial correspondence. The paper reveals that cross-correlation attention in CLIP's intermediate layers already exhibits class-specific localization, yet this signal has never been exploited to repair the last layer.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 336 px shorter side, 560 px for Cityscapes) and a set of text class-name prompts (standard ImageNet prompts, no additional prompting strategies) as input, and produces a pixel-level semantic segmentation map as output. Inference uses a sliding 224×224 window with stride 112 over the ViT-B/16 or ViT-L/14 backbone, and the entire pipeline is training-free—no retraining, fine-tuning, or post-processing (e.g., PAMR, DenseCRF) is applied.

## Method

ResCLIP introduces two plug-and-play modules that remold the last-layer attention of CLIP. The Residual Cross-correlation Self-attention (RCS) module averages the query-key attention maps from a range of intermediate layers (e.g., layers 6–9) and blends them with the self-correlation attention (SCSA) used by prior methods via a weighted residual: A_rcs = (1−λ_rcs)·A_s + λ_rcs·A_c, thereby injecting cross-feature spatial correspondence into the final block. The Semantic Feedback Refinement (SFR) module then uses the initial segmentation map as feedback: for each patch it builds a binary same-class mask, applies a Chebyshev-distance-based exponential decay to preserve locality, smooths with a 1-D Gaussian kernel, and blends the refined scores back into the attention. The final attention is A_ResCLIP = (1−λ_rcs)·A_sfr + λ_rcs·A_c, combining SCSA spatial covariance, intermediate-layer cross-correlation, and semantic-consistency refinement. With optimal λ_rcs = 0.5 and λ_sfr = 0.7, ResCLIP integrates seamlessly into SCLIP, ClearCLIP, and NACLIP, yielding 1.7–13.1 % mIoU gains across eight benchmarks.

## Results

On the five datasets without a background class (VOC20, Context59, Stuff, Cityscapes, ADE20k), ResCLIP as a plug-and-play module improves SCLIP from 37.1% to 39.3% mIoU (+2.2), ClearCLIP from 37.5% to 40.0% (+2.5), and NACLIP from 38.2% to 40.3% (+2.1) on ViT-B/16. The most dramatic gain appears on ViT-L/14, where SCLIP+ResCLIP jumps from 23.6% to 36.7% mIoU (+13.1), substantially mitigating the backbone-scaling degradation. On datasets with a background class (VOC21, Context60, Object), NACLIP+ResCLIP achieves 43.2% average mIoU, surpassing all weakly-supervised methods including GroupViT (32.2%) and TCL (35.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ResCLIP consistently outperforms all training-free baselines across every dataset. Against SCLIP (ECCV 2024), ResCLIP adds +2.2 mIoU on ViT-B/16 and a striking +13.1 on ViT-L/14, where SCLIP alone suffers severe backbone-scaling degradation. Against ClearCLIP (ECCV 2024), gains are +2.5 (ViT-B/16) and +3.9 (ViT-L/14). Against NACLIP (WACV 2025), gains are +2.1 (ViT-B/16) and +2.9 (ViT-L/14), with NACLIP+ResCLIP achieving the overall best 40.3% average mIoU. Compared to earlier training-free methods, ResCLIP+NACLIP surpasses MaskCLIP (28.0%) by 12.3 mIoU, ReCo (25.4%) by 14.9, and CLIPSurgery (partial results) on overlapping datasets. Against weakly-supervised methods, NACLIP+ResCLIP (40.3%) exceeds GroupViT (27.7%), TCL (33.1%), and CoCu, demonstrating that a training-free approach can now match or exceed methods requiring pixel-level or image-text training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free open-vocabulary segmentation line initiated by MaskCLIP and refined by SCLIP, NACLIP, and ClearCLIP, all of which modify the last-layer self-attention using self-correlation (query-query or key-key) to recover spatial covariance. ResCLIP departs from this paradigm by discovering that the cross-correlation (query-key) attention in *intermediate* CLIP layers already encodes class-specific localization, and uses a residual connection to inject that signal into the final block. The SFR module further differentiates from NACLIP's isotropic Gaussian neighbor prior by leveraging a first-pass semantic segmentation map to create class-aware, anisotropic attention refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a plug-and-play module that consistently improves SCLIP, ClearCLIP, and NACLIP across eight benchmarks without any retraining, ResCLIP offers a low-cost path to better dense prediction from frozen CLIP, preserving its open-vocabulary generalization. The intermediate-layer localization discovery suggests that CLIP's dense-prediction deficit is not solely a last-layer problem, opening questions about whether other VLMs (e.g., DINOv2, SigLIP) share similar mid-layer structure. Limitations include the iterative SFR dependency on a first-pass prediction, sensitivity to the layer-window choice, and the fact that gains, while consistent, remain bounded by CLIP's original contrastive training objective.
