## Problem

State-of-the-art video depth and normal estimation methods (e.g., DepthCrafter, ChronoDepth) require large-scale paired video-geometry datasets for training, which are expensive to annotate and difficult to scale. This paper asks whether existing single-image geometry models can be upgraded to produce temporally consistent video predictions without any paired video supervision.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of K RGB video frames (resolutions 252×420 to 308×546, up to 110 frames for depth and 32 for normals) and outputs per-frame depth maps (K×H×W) and surface normal maps (K×H×W×3) in camera coordinates. Training requires only unpaired RGB video clips (~200K clips of 128 frames); a frozen optical flow network (GMFlow) and a frozen image geometry model are queried just-in-time during training to provide supervision signals, so no ground-truth geometry is ever needed.

## Method

The core idea is to fine-tune a strong image geometry backbone (Depth Anything V2 for depth, Marigold-E2E-FT for normals) by injecting lightweight temporal attention blocks (structured like AnimateDiff, with zero-initialized projections so the model initially behaves identically to the image model) while freezing the backbone. Training uses a hybrid loss with no paired video data: (1) a per-frame regularization loss that pulls predictions toward the frozen image model's output (affine-invariant relative loss for depth, L2 on latent codes for normals), computed on one randomly sampled frame per iteration; and (2) an optical-flow-based stabilization loss that enforces consistency between adjacent frames via GMFlow correspondences, with cycle-validation and Canny-edge masking to discard unreliable flow estimates. For the normal model, deferred back-propagation over 4-frame chunks handles memory constraints. This zero-shot strategy yields video predictions that match or surpass methods trained on large-scale paired video datasets.

## Results

On video depth estimation, Buffer Anytime (built on Depth Anything V2) achieves AbsRel of 0.123, 0.119, and 0.102 on ScanNet, KITTI, and Bonn respectively, with δ₁ of 0.853, 0.865, and 0.925, and OPW of 0.076, 0.038, and 0.028. These results match or exceed DepthCrafter (the SOTA video model trained on paired video data) on ScanNet and KITTI while using no paired video-geometry supervision. For video normal estimation, the method reduces temporal inconsistency (OPW) from 0.152 to 0.065 on Sintel and from 0.092 to 0.069 on ScanNet relative to its Marigold-E2E-FT backbone, while maintaining comparable per-frame angular accuracy (mean 33.4° vs 32.9° on Sintel).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its own backbone Depth Anything V2, Buffer Anytime improves AbsRel by 0.012–0.017 and OPW by 0.043–0.083 across all three depth datasets, confirming that the temporal blocks and optical-flow loss add meaningful consistency without sacrificing per-frame accuracy. Compared to DepthCrafter (the strongest video baseline trained on paired video data), the method achieves a slightly better AbsRel on ScanNet (0.123 vs 0.125) and a much lower OPW on KITTI (0.038 vs 0.111), but is outperformed on Bonn (AbsRel 0.102 vs 0.075, OPW 0.028 vs 0.029), where dynamic objects challenge the optical-flow-based supervision. Against ChronoDepth and NVDS, the method wins on every metric across all datasets, with particularly large OPW reductions (e.g., 0.038 vs 0.050 on KITTI vs ChronoDepth). For normal estimation, the method dramatically reduces temporal inconsistency—OPW drops from 0.152 to 0.065 on Sintel and 0.092 to 0.069 on ScanNet relative to Marigold-E2E-FT—while per-frame mean angular error remains within 0.6° of the backbone. Against DSINE and Lotus on normals, the method achieves the best OPW on both datasets (0.065/0.069) while remaining competitive on per-frame accuracy, though DSINE and Lotus have slightly lower mean angular error on Sintel (35.7°/34.5° vs 33.4°).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two distinct lines of work: state-of-the-art single-image geometry estimators (Depth Anything V2 for depth, Marigold-E2E-FT for normals) and video diffusion temporal modules (AnimateDiff-style temporal attention blocks). It departs from the dominant paradigm of video geometry estimation—where methods like DepthCrafter, ChronoDepth, and NVDS are trained on large-scale paired video-geometry datasets—by proposing a zero-shot fine-tuning strategy that requires only unpaired RGB video, using optical flow smoothness as the sole temporal supervision signal. The optical flow stabilization loss with cycle-validation and Canny-edge masking extends earlier stabilization ideas (NVDS, masked-frame modeling) into a training objective rather than a post-hoc correction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: it removes the need for expensive paired video-geometry annotations, making video depth/normal estimation accessible to any lab with a pretrained image model and unpaired video. The authors acknowledge two key limitations—failure when the image backbone itself is wrong, and the inability of adjacent-frame optical flow to handle objects that leave and re-enter the scene—pointing toward hybrid training with limited video supervision or 3D-space losses as natural next steps. More broadly, the result suggests that for geometry estimation (unlike generative tasks), the structural information in RGB frames is rich enough that temporal consistency can be imposed through simple smoothness constraints rather than learned video priors.
