## Problem

Existing image safety judgment systems rely on human-annotated datasets for fine-tuning, which is expensive, hard to scale, and brittle when safety rules are updated. Simply querying pre-trained MLLMs with a safety constitution in a zero-shot setting fails due to three challenges: subjective/ambiguous rules, the inability to reason over complex lengthy constitutions, and inherent biases in MLLM token probabilities from language priors and non-centric image regions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and a safety constitution (a set of textual rules, e.g., 14 objectified rules in Table 1) as input. It produces a binary safety label (safe/unsafe) plus a list of specific violated rules with explanations. The constitution is pre-processed offline into objectified rules and precondition chains, so at inference time only the image and the stored rule/precondition structures are needed. For small centric objects (e.g., a mouth in a kissing rule), the system can crop the detected region and feed the cropped image to the MLLM for improved detection.

## Method

CLUE (Constitutional MLLM JUdgE) is a multi-stage zero-shot pipeline that avoids any fine-tuning. First, an LLM-as-an-Optimizer loop rewrites subjective safety rules into objective, actionable ones (targeting a 9/10 objectiveness score). Second, CLIP cosine similarity between the image embedding and each rule's text embedding filters out irrelevant rules (threshold 0.22), reducing the number of MLLM queries by ~67%. Third, each remaining rule is decomposed offline into a logically complete precondition chain (e.g., [people OR animals visible] AND [bloody injuries] AND [imminent death]). Fourth, each precondition is checked via debiased token-probability scoring: the 'Yes' token probability is compared against a no-image baseline (to cancel language-prior bias) and against a centric-region-removed image score (to cancel non-centric spatial bias), with OWLv2 used to locate the centric region. Fifth, when token-probability confidence is insufficient, a cascaded chain-of-thought reasoning step (free-form CoT followed by a structured JSON summary) provides a slower but more reliable fallback judgment.

## Results

On the OS Bench dataset, CLUE achieves 95.9% recall, 94.8% accuracy, and 0.949 F-1 score with InternVL2-76B, substantially outperforming the best zero-shot baseline (Inputting Entire Constitution + Direct Answer, 79.7% recall / 85.5% accuracy / 0.846 F-1 with the same model). Even with smaller models, CLUE maintains strong performance: 91.2% recall and 0.879 F-1 with InternVL2-8B-AWQ, and 88.9% recall and 0.866 F-1 with Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct. Against fine-tuning-based baselines (Q16, SD Safety Checker, NSFW Detector, LLaVA Guard), CLUE with LLaVA-v1.6-34B achieves 93.6% recall and 0.871 F-1, exceeding the best fine-tuned baseline (NSFW Detector at 41.6% recall / 0.515 F-1) by over 50 percentage points in recall.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLUE decisively outperforms all four fine-tuning-based baselines on OS Bench. Q16 (CLIP ViT L/14) achieves only 29.7% recall and 0.441 F-1, SD Safety Checker reaches 26.4% recall and 0.410 F-1, NSFW Detector (CLIP ViT B/32) attains 41.6% recall and 0.515 F-1, and LLaVA Guard (default prompt) scores 26.1% recall and 0.401 F-1 — all far below CLUE's 93.6% recall and 0.871 F-1 with LLaVA-v1.6-34B. This gap highlights that fine-tuned detectors lack generalizability beyond the specific safety rules used during training. Among zero-shot baselines, the strongest is Inputting Entire Constitution + Direct Answer with InternVL2-76B (79.7% recall, 0.846 F-1), which CLUE surpasses by 16.2 percentage points in recall and 0.103 in F-1. Notably, CLUE with the smaller InternVL2-8B-AWQ (91.2% recall, 0.879 F-1) already exceeds the best zero-shot baseline using the much larger InternVL2-76B, demonstrating that the methodological contributions (objectification, relevance scanning, debiased token probability) matter more than raw model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of fine-tuning MLLMs on human-labeled safety datasets (Q16, SD Safety Checker, NSFW Detector, LLaVA Guard) by proposing a fully zero-shot, constitution-driven pipeline. It repurposes several existing components—CLIP contrastive embeddings for rule-image relevance filtering, OWLv2 open-vocabulary detection for centric-region isolation, LLM-as-an-Optimizer for rule objectification, and token-probability debiasing techniques from Lin et al. and Zhang et al.—into a unified multi-stage framework (CLUE) that has not been previously assembled for image safety judgment. The work also extends the MLLM-as-a-Judge literature (primarily studied for text) to the visual domain with a structured, rule-by-rule evaluation protocol rather than a single holistic query.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for platform moderation: safety rules can be updated without retraining or re-annotating, and the zero-shot setting removes the expensive human-labeling bottleneck. However, the evaluation is confined to a self-constructed benchmark (OS Bench) of AI-generated images with objectively defined rules, leaving open how well the approach generalizes to real-world user-generated content, subjective or evolving safety policies, and multilingual contexts. The multi-component pipeline (relevance scanning → precondition extraction → debiased token probability → cascaded CoT) introduces several hyperparameters and dependencies (CLIP threshold, OWLv2 confidence, token-probability thresholds) whose interactions on diverse, real-world image distributions remain untested.
