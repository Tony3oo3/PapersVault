## Problem

Existing text-to-3D methods, whether feed-forward or optimization-based, struggle to generate multiple 3D objects with correct attribute assignment, spatial relationships, and 3D consistency from a single compositional prompt. Feed-forward approaches are limited by single-object training data, while SDS-based optimization with implicit 2D diffusion priors fails to disentangle attributes across objects, producing multi-faced artifacts, semantic drift, and incorrect interactions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251032/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single compositional text prompt (e.g., 'an owl perches on a branch near a pinecone') and produces a multi-entity 3D Gaussian Splatting scene. The prompt is first decomposed by an LLM into entity-level sub-prompts, which drive segmentation of a DALL-E-generated composed image; each segmented entity image is then lifted to a coarse mesh via TripoSR to initialize Gaussians. The system also supports progressive 3D adding, where MagicBrush edits a rendered front view to introduce new entities into an already-trained scene.

## Method

COMPGS represents the scene as explicit 3D Gaussians and introduces two core designs. First, it transfers 2D compositionality to initialize Gaussian parameters entity-by-entity: a composed image is segmented per entity, each segment is converted to a coarse mesh, and Gaussian centers/colors are sampled from mesh vertices while preserving 2D spatial layout. Second, it employs a dynamic SDS optimization strategy with two components: (1) Decomposed Optimization alternates between entity-level optimization (using MVDream multi-view priors with gradient masking to isolate each entity's bounding box) and composition-level optimization (jointly using 2D Stable Diffusion and 3D MVDream priors to refine inter-entity interactions); and (2) Volume-adaptive Optimization dynamically rescales each entity's bounding box to a standardized volume during training and rescales back after each iteration, enabling fine-grained detail capture in small objects that would otherwise be under-optimized in the original 3D space.

## Results

On T³ Bench (Multiple Objects track), COMPGS achieves a Quality score of 54.2 and a Text-3D Alignment score of 37.9, yielding an average of 46.1, outperforming the strongest baseline VP3D (49.1 / 31.5 / 40.3) by +5.1, +6.4, and +5.8 respectively. In the user study, COMPGS receives the highest ranking score of 7.23, ahead of VP3D (6.71) and DreamFusion (4.54). COMPGS also substantially outperforms feed-forward methods (OpenLRM: 20.4 avg, TripoSR: 22.7 avg) and standard optimization-based methods (DreamFusion: 16.1 avg, SJC: 11.7 avg, Fantasia3D: 18.5 avg).

## Baselines & Comparisons

COMPGS outperforms all 10 baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest compositional-generation competitor VP3D [11], COMPGS gains +5.1 in Quality, +6.4 in Alignment, and +0.52 in user-study ranking, attributed to explicit 2D compositionality transfer and dynamic SDS optimization versus VP3D's implicit visual-feature guidance. Among optimization-based single-object methods, ProlificDreamer [71] (45.7 Quality) and Magic3D [41] (26.6 Quality) are the strongest, yet COMPGS still leads by +8.5 and +27.6 in Quality respectively, demonstrating that decomposed entity-level and composition-level optimization is critical for multi-object scenes. Feed-forward methods (OpenLRM [29], TripoSR [67]) lag significantly in Quality (15.2, 16.7) due to limited single-object training data, though TripoSR's Alignment (28.6) is competitive because it is used as COMPGS's own initialization prior. Set-the-Scene [15] achieves the second-best Alignment (29.9) among non-COMPGS methods but suffers from blurred rendering due to its implicit NeRF representation, resulting in a lower Quality score (20.8). In the user study, COMPGS ranks first (7.23) while all optimization-based baselines cluster between 2.43 and 4.54, confirming that standard SDS-based methods struggle with compositional 3D generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

COMPGS builds on the SDS optimization framework from DreamFusion and the 3D Gaussian Splatting representation, but departs from prior single-object optimization methods by introducing structured, entity-by-entity initialization from 2D compositional images. It extends the line of compositional 3D generation work (Set-the-Scene, VP3D, GraphDreamer, GALA3D) by replacing implicit NeRF-based or feature-guided approaches with explicit Gaussian parameters that can be independently optimized per entity. The key conceptual departure is the decomposed optimization strategy—alternating between entity-level and composition-level SDS—which has no direct precedent in prior SDS-based text-to-3D methods that optimize all parameters jointly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Compositional 3D generation is a natural and practically important next step beyond single-object text-to-3D, and COMPGS provides a concrete, modular pipeline (2D composition → segmentation → per-entity 3D init → dynamic SDS) that practitioners can adopt. The volume-adaptive optimization insight—that small objects in a scene are systematically under-optimized in standard SDS—addresses a previously unacknowledged failure mode. Open questions include scalability beyond 4–5 entities, handling dynamic/physical interactions (not just spatial), and whether the reliance on DALL-E for 2D compositionality introduces a bottleneck for novel or abstract prompts.
