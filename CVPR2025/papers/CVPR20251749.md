## Problem

Multi-view diffusion models that underpin current 3D generation pipelines produce low-resolution, view-inconsistent images, which directly bottleneck the quality of downstream 3D reconstructions. Existing enhancement approaches—image super-resolution and video super-resolution—either ignore cross-view consistency or fail to correct texture errors under large viewpoint changes, leaving no dedicated framework for multi-view 3D enhancement that simultaneously refines textures and enforces geometric coherence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251749/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 4 low-quality multi-view RGB images at 512×512 (azimuth 0°–360°, elevation −5°–30°) along with their camera poses encoded as Plücker coordinates, an optional text prompt, and a user-controllable noise level that governs enhancement strength. It outputs enhanced multi-view images with corrected textures and cross-view consistency, which can either be fed directly into a feed-forward 3D reconstruction model (e.g., LGM) or used as supervision targets to iteratively optimize a coarse 3D representation (NeRF or 3DGS) via L1 + LPIPS loss.

## Method

3DEnhancer adapts a PixArt-Σ DiT-based latent diffusion model into a multi-view enhancer with two key architectural additions. A pose-aware encoder concatenates Plücker ray coordinates with RGB channels and encodes the result into latent space, injecting camera conditioning into the pre-trained DiT via a learnable copy. Within the DiT blocks, a multi-view row attention layer exploits the epipolar constraint (correspondences lie on horizontal rows for near-level cameras) to enable efficient cross-view token exchange, while a near-view epipolar aggregation module explicitly propagates features between adjacent views using fundamental-matrix-constrained cosine-distance matching (jᵀFi = 0), with gradients passed through a straight-through estimator. Training uses a comprehensive degradation pipeline (downsampling, blur, JPEG, grid distortion, camera jitter, color shift, semi-transparent masking, and controllable noise) on 400K Objaverse G-buffer objects to bridge the domain gap between synthetic training data and real coarse 3D inputs.

## Results

On the Objaverse synthetic benchmark, 3DEnhancer achieves PSNR 27.53, SSIM 0.9265, and LPIPS 0.0626, outperforming the strongest baseline RealBasicVSR (PSNR 26.21, SSIM 0.9212, LPIPS 0.0888) by a clear margin. On the in-the-wild dataset, 3DEnhancer attains the best FID (108.40) and Inception Score (7.93) among all methods, while RealBasicVSR edges it out slightly on MUSIQ (74.07 vs. 73.32). For 3D reconstruction enhancement, 3DEnhancer achieves the best FID (71.78) and IS (9.96), surpassing all baselines including Upscale-A-Video (FID 74.12, IS 9.77).

## Baselines & Comparisons

3DEnhancer outperforms all four baselines on the primary Objaverse synthetic benchmark (Table 1), achieving the best PSNR (27.53), SSIM (0.9265), and LPIPS (0.0626). Against the image-based baselines Real-ESRGAN and StableSR, the gains are consistent across all metrics, with the largest improvement over StableSR (PSNR +2.41, LPIPS −0.0504), confirming that per-view enhancement without cross-view modeling fails to maintain multi-view consistency. Against the video-based baselines RealBasicVSR and Upscale-A-Video, 3DEnhancer wins on all three reference metrics in Table 1, with the most notable gap over RealBasicVSR in LPIPS (0.0626 vs. 0.0888). On the in-the-wild dataset (Table 2), 3DEnhancer achieves the best FID (108.40 vs. 114.25–128.30) and IS (7.93 vs. 7.09–7.75), but RealBasicVSR narrowly leads on MUSIQ (74.07 vs. 73.32), though the paper notes RealBasicVSR produces visually implausible outputs. For 3D reconstruction enhancement (Table 3), 3DEnhancer achieves the best FID (71.78) and IS (9.96), beating Upscale-A-Video (FID 74.12, IS 9.77) and StableSR (FID 72.98, IS 9.51), demonstrating superior multi-view consistency in the final 3D output.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the PixArt-Σ DiT backbone and the latent diffusion denoising framework from Rombach et al. into a multi-view enhancement setting, incorporating the multi-view row attention mechanism from Era3D for efficient cross-view information exchange. It departs from video-based 3D enhancement approaches (SuperGaussian, 3DGS-Enhancer) that rely on temporal attention and per-instance optimization, instead formulating enhancement as a standalone multi-view diffusion module that can be inserted between multi-view generation and 3D reconstruction stages. The key architectural departure is the addition of near-view epipolar aggregation—an explicit, geometry-constrained token propagation step complementing the implicit consistency learned by row attention—drawing on ideas from TokenFlow and DGE but making them trainable and epipolar-constrained. The data augmentation pipeline borrows degradation strategies from Real-ESRGAN and extends them with 3D-specific distortions (grid warping, camera jitter, color shift, ghosting masks).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical bottleneck in the dominant two-stage 3D generation pipeline (multi-view diffusion → feed-forward reconstruction), where low-resolution and view-inconsistent intermediate images directly limit final 3D quality. By providing a plug-in enhancement module compatible with existing frameworks (e.g., MVDream → 3DEnhancer → LGM), it offers a path to improving 3D output quality without retraining the full pipeline. Limitations include the assumption of approximately horizontal viewing angles for the row-attention epipolar approximation, the 512×512 resolution ceiling, and the need for posed multi-view inputs. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions, handles arbitrary camera configurations (e.g., large elevation changes), and can be made faster for interactive use.
