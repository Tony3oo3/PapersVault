## Problem

Existing IP protection methods for deep models rely solely on visual backbones, lack sufficient semantic richness, and require extensive training or fine-tuning that is particularly costly for large VLMs like CLIP. Moreover, methods that generate supplementary data to constrain generalization often lack adequate controllability. This paper addresses the need for a lightweight, plug-and-play IP protection strategy that restricts a CLIP-based model's performance to an authorized domain while degrading it on unauthorized domains, without full fine-tuning of pre-trained parameters.

## Input / Output

During training, RGB images from both the authorized domain and the unauthorized domain are fed in parallel into CLIP's frozen visual encoder; during inference, a single image is processed and a class label is predicted. The key design constraint is that the model must produce accurate predictions for authorized-domain images while deliberately yielding incorrect results for unauthorized-domain images. The prompts are not hand-crafted text but are dynamically constructed from visual features (domain tokens derived from batch-wise style statistics and image tokens from multi-scale encoder responses) and fed into CLIP's frozen text encoder.

## Method

IP-CLIP introduces a lightweight IP-Prompt module that extracts multi-scale features from CLIP's frozen visual encoder and converts them into two token types: domain-specific tokens (T_a/T_u) computed from first- and second-order batch-wise feature statistics to capture style, and image tokens [V_1,...,V_L] capturing content. These tokens are concatenated with a [CLS] token and fed into CLIP's frozen text encoder, so the model simultaneously identifies both the domain authorization status and the image category. A style-enhancement branch (STAM) uses frozen N-way K-shot feature banks for both domains, applying learnable cross-attention with residual connections to inject self-enhanced and cross-domain features. Training combines contrastive image-text alignment losses for both domains, MSE loss to maximize domain-token separation, KL divergence to separate text representations, and an entropy penalty on unauthorized-domain text features, with only the IP Projector and STAM parameters updated.

## Results

On the target-specified IP protection task, IP-CLIP achieves a mean weighted drop (W_ua) of 74.84% on Office-31, 55.10% on Office-Home-65, and 54.68% on Mini-DomainNet, consistently outperforming all baselines. The method also achieves near-zero authorized-domain degradation (D_a = 0.00% on Office-31, 0.20% on Office-Home-65, 0.33% on Mini-DomainNet) while dropping unauthorized-domain accuracy by 83.12%, 64.98%, and 65.48% respectively. In the ownership verification scenario, IP-CLIP attains a mean O_ua of 71.3%, surpassing CUTI† (65.7%) and NTL† (52.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IP-CLIP outperforms both CNN-based baselines (NTL and CUTI) and their CLIP-adapted counterparts (NTL† and CUTI†) on W_ua across all three datasets. On Office-31, IP-CLIP leads CUTI† by 2.36 points (74.84 vs. 72.48) and NTL† by 19.86 points (74.84 vs. 54.98); on Office-Home-65 the margins widen to 4.81 over CUTI† and 22.34 over NTL†; on Mini-DomainNet IP-CLIP exceeds CUTI† by 3.93 and NTL† by 13.09. A critical advantage is that IP-CLIP achieves D_a = 0.00% on Office-31 (no authorized-domain degradation), whereas CUTI† incurs 1.27% and NTL† 2.07%. In the ownership verification setting, IP-CLIP's O_ua of 71.3% surpasses CUTI† (65.7%) by 5.6 points and NTL† (52.6%) by 18.7 points, with statistically significant differences (p < 0.05). The CLIP-based variants (NTL†, CUTI†) generally outperform their original CNN versions, confirming that the stronger feature extraction of CLIP benefits IP protection, but IP-CLIP's prompt-based approach still yields the best overall balance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes prompt-tuning techniques (CoOp, MaPLe) from their original role of improving downstream performance into a mechanism for IP protection, a task previously addressed by methods like NTL and CUTI that require full fine-tuning or GAN-based data synthesis. It builds directly on CLIP's dual-encoder architecture, using the frozen visual backbone to extract multi-scale style and content features that are projected into learnable domain tokens and image tokens fed to the text encoder. The key departure from prior IP protection work is the shift from visual-backbone-only constraints to a prompt-based formulation that simultaneously encodes domain identity and class-specific visual cues, combined with a style-enhancement branch (STAM) that uses frozen feature banks to amplify authorized/unauthorized separation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As VLMs become commercially critical and increasingly expensive to train, a lightweight, plug-and-play IP protection mechanism that avoids retraining the backbone is practically valuable for model providers. However, the evaluation is confined to domain-adaptation classification benchmarks (Office-31, Office-Home-65, Mini-DomainNet) with only two prior baselines (NTL, CUTI), leaving open questions about robustness against adversarial prompt removal, applicability to generative or detection tasks, and behavior under distribution shifts beyond style differences. The authors acknowledge extension to detection and image description as future work, and the target-free setting shows substantially weaker protection (W_ua drops from ~75% to ~53% on Office-31), suggesting the method's effectiveness is sensitive to having access to unauthorized domain data.
