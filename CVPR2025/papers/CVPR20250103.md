## Problem

Data-free universal adversarial perturbation (UAP) methods generate a single image-agnostic perturbation without any target-domain data, but they rely on random priors (Gaussian noise, jigsaw patterns) that lack semantic content, severely limiting black-box transferability. A key consequence is that input transformations—proven effective for image-specific adversarial attacks—cannot be leveraged in data-free UAP because random noise provides no meaningful semantic structure to transform.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250103/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes only random noise (Gaussian or jigsaw patterns) as input during training, with no access to any target-domain dataset. It produces a single universal adversarial perturbation δ constrained to ‖δ‖∞ ≤ ε (ε = 10/255), which when added to any RGB image causes misclassification across diverse CNN architectures. During training, the UAP itself is combined with random noise to form a pseudo-semantic prior, from which N=10 semantic samples are drawn via random crop-and-resize operations at the original UAP resolution.

## Method

The core insight is that a trained UAP encodes diverse semantic features across its spatial regions (verified via Grad-CAM heatmaps and per-region classification), enabling it to serve as its own semantic data source. At each iteration, the current UAP δ_t is added to random noise to form a pseudo-semantic prior, and N semantic samples are extracted by randomly cropping and resizing regions of this composite. Input transformations (rotation, scaling, or block shuffling, following L2T) are then applied to both clean and adversarial versions of each sample to increase diversity. A sample reweighting scheme computes the reciprocal of the KL-divergence between the surrogate model's temperature-scaled softmax outputs on the transformed clean and adversarial samples, assigning higher weight to harder-to-fool examples. The final loss is a weighted sum of log-activations across multiple CNN layers, optimized iteratively with a saturation check and curriculum learning strategy inherited from TRM-UAP.

## Results

On the ImageNet validation set, PSP-UAP achieves an average fooling rate (FR) of 89.95% across five CNN models (AlexNet, VGG16, VGG19, ResNet152, GoogleNet), outperforming the next-best data-free UAP method AT-UAP-U (87.95%) by 2.0 percentage points. The most substantial gain is on ResNet152, where PSP-UAP reaches 85.65% FR versus 73.15% for AT-UAP-U and 67.46% for TRM-UAP. In black-box transferability (Table 2), PSP-UAP achieves a 70.1% average FR across strictly black-box settings, slightly exceeding TRM-UAP's 69.6%, and even surpasses data-dependent UAPs (SGA-UAP, AT-UAP-S) in average black-box FR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PSP-UAP achieves the highest average white-box FR (89.95%) among all data-free UAP methods, beating AT-UAP-U (87.95%), TRM-UAP (86.39%), Cosine-UAP (84.05%), AAA (73.89%), and GD-UAP (70.88%). The largest per-model gains are on ResNet152 (+12.5 pp over AT-UAP-U, +18.2 pp over TRM-UAP) and VGG16/VGG19, where PSP-UAP leads all baselines. PSP-UAP trails AT-UAP-U on AlexNet (91.77 vs. 96.66) and Cosine-UAP on GoogleNet (81.43 vs. 87.57), but these are offset by dominant performance on deeper architectures. In black-box transferability (Table 2), PSP-UAP outperforms TRM-UAP in 14 out of 20 surrogate-to-target settings and achieves a higher strictly black-box average FR (70.1% vs. 69.6%). Against data-dependent UAPs (Table 4), PSP-UAP surpasses both SGA-UAP and AT-UAP-S in average black-box FR for UAPs crafted on ResNet152 (79.28% vs. 74.09% and 72.16%) and VGG16 (75.65% vs. 69.27% and 67.08%), demonstrating that the pseudo-semantic prior effectively compensates for the absence of target-domain data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data-free UAP line of work (FFF, GD-UAP, TRM-UAP, Cosine-UAP) by replacing the standard random-noise prior with a pseudo-semantic prior constructed from the UAP itself, exploiting the observation that generated UAPs encode diverse semantic content across their spatial regions. It builds directly on GD-UAP's activation-maximizing loss and TRM-UAP's curriculum learning and saturation-check strategy, while introducing input transformations (rotation, scaling, shuffling) borrowed from image-specific attack literature (L2T) into the data-free UAP framework for the first time. The key conceptual departure is treating the evolving UAP as a self-contained semantic source rather than relying on external data or surrogate-model-generated samples, which prior data-free methods (AT-UAP, AAA) used at the cost of surrogate overfitting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a purely data-free UAP outperforms data-dependent UAPs (SGA-UAP, AT-UAP-S) in black-box transferability is practically significant, as it removes the need for target-domain data while matching or exceeding methods that require it. The pseudo-semantic prior is a lightweight, generalizable mechanism that could be plugged into other data-free UAP pipelines (as demonstrated in the ablation with TRM-UAP and GD-UAP), suggesting a reusable design pattern. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-CNN architectures (ViTs, diffusion models), whether the semantic richness of the UAP degrades at very small perturbation budgets, and how the method behaves under adaptive defenses or multi-target settings.
