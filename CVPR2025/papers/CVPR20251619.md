## Problem

In cross-modal retrieval, a small subset of samples (hubs) disproportionately dominate nearest-neighbor rankings, causing biased representations and degraded accuracy. Existing mitigations either apply post-hoc normalization that requires knowledge of test or training distributions, or indiscriminately penalize all hubs without distinguishing semantically relevant (good) hubs from irrelevant (bad) ones. This paper addresses the gap of how to selectively balance hub centrality during training while preserving beneficial hubs and also addressing overlooked anti-hubs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes video clips (sampled as 12 or 64 frames depending on dataset) and text descriptions (24 or 64 words), encoding them via CLIP ViT-B/32 into 512-dimensional representations. At the fine-grained level, visual patches and text words are aligned via a Weighted Token-wise Interaction module; at the coarse level, DPC-KNN density-peaks clustering merges tokens into single representations. The output is a cross-modal similarity score used to rank gallery items against a query, with retrieval evaluated via R@K, MdR, MnR, and Rsum metrics.

## Method

NeighborRetr introduces three complementary losses operating within a two-level hierarchy framework. First, a Centrality Weighting Loss (L_Wti) computes intra-modal centrality scores via a memory bank queue and exponentially weights the contrastive loss to emphasize hub samples. Second, a Neighbor Adjusting Loss (L_Nbi) defines a de-centrality similarity (cross-modal similarity minus inter-modal centrality) and adaptively reweights neighborhood contributions so that good hubs are promoted while bad hubs are penalized, with the gradient direction determined by the gap between the neighbor probability and the centrality-adjusted weight. Third, a Uniform Regularization Loss (L_Opt) solves an optimal transport problem to enforce uniform marginal retrieval probabilities, ensuring anti-hubs are not systematically overlooked. A KL divergence loss stabilizes optimization by aligning the fine-grained and coarse-grained similarity distributions.

## Results

On MSR-VTT, NeighborRetr achieves 49.5% R@1 and 207.7 Rsum for text-to-video retrieval, and 48.7% R@1 and 207.5 Rsum for video-to-text retrieval, setting new state-of-the-art on both directions. On ActivityNet Captions, it reaches 46.0% R@1 (a 3.8-point gain over the previous best HBI at 42.2%), and on ECCV Caption it attains 92.1% R@1 for text-to-image and 82.1% R@1 for image-to-text. Hubness metrics also improve substantially: skewness drops 46% and hub occurrence drops 28% relative to the baseline, confirming that the retrieval gains are driven by a more balanced embedding space rather than overfitting to a few dominant samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeighborRetr outperforms all 18 compared baselines on the primary MSR-VTT benchmark, beating the closest competitor DiffusionRet [26] by 0.5% on T2V R@1 (49.5 vs 49.0) and by 1.0% on V2T R@1 (48.7 vs 47.7), while also achieving the highest Rsum in both directions (207.7 and 207.5). Against the fine-grained three-level method HBI [24], NeighborRetr gains 0.9% on T2V R@1 and 1.9% on V2T R@1 using only a two-level framework. On the longer-video ActivityNet Captions dataset, the margin widens to 3.8% R@1 over HBI (46.0 vs 42.2), and on DiDeMo it leads by 1.3% over HBI (48.2 vs 46.9). For text-image retrieval on ECCV Caption, NeighborRetr surpasses PCME++ [7] by 1.3% R@1 (92.1 vs 90.8) and 0.8% R@1 on image-to-text (82.1 vs 81.3). Notably, DiCoSA [25] relies on the post-hoc QB-Norm inference trick and is excluded from fair comparison; without such test-time normalization, NeighborRetr's training-time hubness mitigation yields superior results across all seven evaluated datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeighborRetr builds on the two-level fine-grained matching framework of HBI [24] (KL divergence between low- and high-level similarity distributions) and the Weighted Token-wise Interaction module [48] for computing cross-modal similarity. Its key departure from prior hubness-mitigation work (HAL [35], Dual Softmax [5], QB-Norm [3], DB-Norm [50]) is shifting the intervention from inference-time score normalization to training-time loss design, and further distinguishing semantically relevant (good) hubs from irrelevant (bad) hubs via intra- and inter-modal centrality scores rather than treating all hubs uniformly. The uniform regularization loss draws on optimal transport / Sinkhorn-style marginal constraints [11] to address the under-retrieved anti-hub problem that prior methods overlook.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By addressing hubness during training rather than relying on test-set or training-set priors at inference, NeighborRetr offers a more practical solution for real-world deployments where the test distribution is unknown or shifted, as demonstrated by the MSR-VTT→ActivityNet cross-domain experiment. The conceptual distinction between good and bad hubs, and the finding that subtracting centrality from similarity (Simi-Cent) substantially outperforms adding them (Simi+Cent), provides a useful design principle for future contrastive learning objectives. Open questions include whether the memory-bank-based centrality estimation scales efficiently to very large corpora, whether the approach transfers to non-CLIP backbones or generative retrieval paradigms, and whether the modest 1–2% R@1 gains persist at larger model scales.
