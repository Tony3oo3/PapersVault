## Problem

DNNs trained with cross-entropy loss are overconfident, and existing calibration techniques either sacrifice accuracy (regularization-based) or require extra validation data (post-hoc). Deep Ensembles improve both accuracy and calibration but incur 3× inference cost. Deep Mutual Learning (DML) offers an implicit ensemble with single-model inference, yet standard DML causes models to converge to near-identical predictions during training, destroying the output diversity that ensemble-based calibration depends on.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) and outputs a K-dimensional calibrated probability vector from which both the class label (argmax) and a confidence score (max) are derived. During training, a primary ResNet34/50 model and one or two auxiliary ResNet18 models are jointly optimized; at inference, only the primary model is deployed, so the computational cost matches a single network. The auxiliary models are discarded after training, making the approach drop-in compatible with single-model deployment pipelines.

## Method

Diff-DML reinterprets DML's KL-divergence loss as continuous alignment of each model toward a weighted ensemble distribution (Proposition 1), then introduces two diversity-preserving modifications. First, the Differentiated Training Strategy (DTS) assigns the primary model a standard decaying LR schedule while the auxiliary model trains at a fixed LR of 0.01, preventing premature convergence to identical parameters. Second, the Diversity-Preserving Learning Objective (DPLO) removes the cross-entropy term from the auxiliary model's loss, so it is supervised only by KL divergence from the primary model rather than by one-hot labels, avoiding the overconfidence that label-driven training induces. The primary model retains the full DML loss (CE + KL from auxiliary), while the auxiliary model's loss reduces to a single KL term. This asymmetry lets the auxiliary model develop a differentiated prediction distribution that enriches the implicit ensemble signal without collapsing into the primary model's output.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, Diff-DML with ResNet34 achieves 78.20% accuracy with 0.65% ECE and 1.43×10⁻³ cw-ECE, while ResNet50 reaches 79.05% accuracy with 0.86% ECE and 1.40×10⁻³ cw-ECE. Compared to the previous state-of-the-art FL+MDCA, Diff-DML yields absolute accuracy gains of 1.3% (ResNet34) and 3.1% (ResNet50), with relative ECE reductions of 49.6% and 43.8%, respectively. Inference cost is identical to a single model, unlike Deep Ensembles which require 3× inference time for DE-3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Diff-DML outperforms all single-model calibration baselines on CIFAR-100. Against the previous SOTA FL+MDCA [10], Diff-DML improves accuracy by 1.3% (ResNet34) and 3.1% (ResNet50) while reducing ECE by 49.6% and 43.8% respectively. Post-hoc methods (CE+TS, CE+VS, CE+DS [8,15]) achieve moderate ECE (~3.1–3.8%) but Diff-DML reduces ECE to 0.65–0.86%, a 3–5× improvement, without sacrificing accuracy. DCA [19] and MMCE [16] both fail to improve ECE over the CE baseline (DCA actually worsens it to 10.10–11.69%), while FL [21] achieves low ECE (1.21%) at the cost of ~1.3% accuracy drop. Compared to standard DML [29], Diff-DML improves accuracy by 0.84–1.16% and reduces ECE by 81–81% (3.43→0.65 for ResNet34; 4.42→0.86 for ResNet50). Against DE-3 [5,17], Diff-DML achieves lower ECE (0.65 vs 2.95 for ResNet34) at the cost of ~1.1% accuracy, but with 3× less inference time since only the primary model is used at test time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Deep Mutual Learning (DML), a collaborative training technique originally designed for knowledge distillation, and reframes it as an implicit form of Deep Ensembles through a gradient-equivalence proposition. It departs from prior DML by identifying that standard mutual learning erodes the prediction diversity essential for calibration, and introduces two targeted modifications: differentiated learning-rate schedules (DTS) and removal of cross-entropy supervision from the auxiliary model (DPLO). The work also positions itself against the broader landscape of train-time calibration regularizers (FL, MDCA, MMCE, DTDP) and post-hoc methods (TS, VS, DS), arguing that ensemble-based approaches inherently avoid the accuracy-calibration trade-off that plagues single-model regularizers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Diff-DML offers a practical path to well-calibrated, accurate predictions at single-model inference cost, which is valuable for safety-critical deployment where Deep Ensembles' multi-model inference overhead is prohibitive. The ~50% relative ECE reduction while simultaneously improving accuracy over the prior SOTA (FL+MDCA) is a meaningful step for practitioners who need both metrics. However, the method's reliance on an auxiliary model of comparable capacity to the primary model limits scalability to very large architectures, and all experiments are confined to CIFAR-scale datasets with ResNet backbones, leaving open whether the approach transfers to ImageNet-scale or transformer-based settings.
