## Problem

Video M-LLMs typically rely on uniform frame sampling to fit within context limits, which either wastes tokens on irrelevant frames or misses key moments needed to answer a question. Existing alternatives like per-frame M-LLM scoring (e.g., SeViLA) are computationally expensive at inference and lack temporal reasoning, as each frame is evaluated independently. The paper addresses this by proposing a lightweight, question-aware frame selector that is plug-and-play across downstream video M-LLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251158/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The frame selector ingests 128 densely sampled video frames, each compressed to just 9 visual tokens (3×3 spatial pooling) via a SigLIP ViT-Large encoder, along with the text question. It outputs a 128-dimensional importance score vector, from which a greedy NMS algorithm selects k diverse frames (e.g., 4–32) for the frozen downstream video M-LLM. The aggressive token compression (9 vs. 144 tokens/frame in typical video LLMs) is the key efficiency enabler, relying on the insight that frame importance requires only a rough visual outline rather than fine-grained detail.

## Method

The selector is a fine-tuned Qwen2.5 1.5B LLM with a SigLIP ViT-Large visual encoder and a learnable score query token appended to the input sequence; the query's hidden state from the penultimate transformer block is mapped through an MLP to produce per-frame importance scores, leveraging causal attention to aggregate all visual and text context. Training is two-stage: Stage 1 alternates visual instruction following (cross-entropy on generated responses) and importance score prediction (binary cross-entropy on pseudo-labels) while freezing the vision and LLM backbones; Stage 2 adds LoRA adapters to the LLM and trains only on score prediction. Pseudo-labels are generated by averaging spatial scores (Qwen2-VL-7B prompted with chain-of-thought to rate each frame's relevance) and temporal scores (GPT-4o-mini selecting top-k frames from all frame captions), combining single-frame and multi-frame temporal reasoning. A greedy NMS sampling algorithm with a neighbor gap of n/4k ensures temporal diversity among selected frames, and the entire selector is plug-and-play, requiring no modification to the downstream video M-LLM.

## Results

The 1.5B frame selector consistently improves video QA accuracy across all tested M-LLMs and benchmarks without modifying the downstream model. On EgoSchema (long-video, ~10 min), LLaVA-NeXT-Video 34B improves from 48.6% to 50.6% (+2.0), Idefics2 8B from 56.6% to 57.9% (+1.3), and Qwen2-VL 7B from 64.6% to 65.9% (+1.1). On NExT-QA, gains range from +0.8 (Qwen2-VL) to +1.2 (LLaVA-NeXT-Video 34B). On LongVideoBench, selecting just 4 frames with the selector (49.5% for LLaVA-NeXT-Video 34B) surpasses uniform sampling of 32 frames (49.7%), and for Qwen2-VL the 4-frame selector (55.0%) beats 32-frame uniform (53.3%) by 1.7 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is each base M-LLM with uniform frame sampling versus the same model augmented with the proposed 1.5B frame selector, holding the number of input frames constant. The selector wins on every benchmark and every base model tested: LLaVA-NeXT-Video 7B/34B, Idefics2 8B, Qwen2-VL 7B, and PLLaVA 7B/34B all show consistent gains of 0.6–2.0 points, with the largest improvements on long-video benchmarks (EgoSchema, LongVideoBench) where temporal relevance matters most. On LongVideoBench, the selector with only 4 input frames outperforms uniform sampling with 32 frames for both LLaVA-NeXT-Video 34B (49.5 vs. 49.7) and Qwen2-VL 7B (55.0 vs. 53.3), demonstrating that question-aware selection is more effective than simply increasing frame count. In the ablation (Table 5), the trained selector matches the performance of directly using spatial+temporal pseudo-labels (63.4 vs. 63.9 on NExT-QA) while being far cheaper at inference. On QVHighlights moment retrieval, the selector underperforms SeViLA (43.9% vs. 54.5% R1@5), but the authors note it was not specifically trained for that task, so the gap is expected.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of question-aware video frame selection initiated by SeViLA and MVU, which prompt an M-LLM independently on each frame to estimate relevance. The key departure is architectural: rather than scoring frames one-by-one (expensive and lacking temporal context), the authors train a lightweight LLM-based selector that ingests all densely sampled frames simultaneously with aggressive spatial pooling (9 tokens/frame vs. 144 in typical video LLMs), enabling single-pass temporal reasoning. For training, since no ground-truth frame importance annotations exist, they introduce a two-signal pseudo-labeling strategy combining spatial (M-LLM chain-of-thought per-frame scoring) and temporal (LLM reasoning over frame captions) supervision, building on the pseudo-labeling paradigm common in weakly supervised learning. The plug-and-play design also distinguishes it from end-to-end methods like ViLA or Koala, which lack explicit question conditioning at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of the selector—requiring no retraining of the downstream video M-LLM—makes it a practical drop-in component for existing video QA pipelines, and the finding that 128→4 selected frames outperform 8 uniformly sampled frames at lower inference cost offers a concrete efficiency win for long-video understanding. However, the absolute improvements are modest (1–2 points across benchmarks), and the selector underperforms SeViLA on moment retrieval (QVHighlights), suggesting the pseudo-labeling pipeline may not fully capture fine-grained temporal localization. Open questions include whether the selector can be co-trained end-to-end with the downstream model, whether the 9-token-per-frame compression generalizes to higher-resolution or longer videos, and whether the approach extends beyond QA to tasks like video summarization or generation.
