## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary 3D affordance grounding methods align object geometry with instruction modalities but remain trapped in a limited training semantic space, failing to exploit invariant geometric structures shared across objects with the same affordance and potential correlations among distinct interactions of the same object. When confronted with unseen affordances (e.g., 'pour' absent from training), these models collapse to the nearest seen category (e.g., 'grasp') rather than reasoning analogically about the interaction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251515/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud (N×4: xyz coordinates plus per-point affordance label) and a 2D interaction image (3×H×W) depicting a human-object interaction. Notably, images and point clouds are not required to be one-to-one paired; they are sampled from different instances to encourage generalization. The output is a per-point affordance heatmap (N×1) produced by a sigmoid head, supervised directly by focal and dice losses without any affordance category classification constraint, enabling open-vocabulary grounding.

## Method

GREAT fine-tunes InternVL with LoRA adapters using a Multi-Head Affordance Chain-of-Thought (MHACoT) prompting strategy: an Object-Head identifies the interacting part and reasons about its geometric structure, while an Affordance-Head describes the full interaction and brainstorms two additional plausible interactions for the same object. The resulting textual descriptions are encoded by RoBERTa and cross-attended to produce an object geometric knowledge feature T̄_o and an affordance intention feature T̄_a. A Cross-Modal Adaptive Fusion Module (CMAFM) then injects T̄_o into the deepest PointNet++ encoder layer via cross-attention (query from point features, key/value from geometric knowledge) and concatenates T̄_a with ResNet-18 image features. A shared decoder fuses the enriched point and image representations to regress the per-point affordance heatmap, trained end-to-end with focal + dice loss over 65 epochs.

## Results

On the PIADv2 benchmark, GREAT achieves AUC 91.99, aIOU 38.03, SIM 0.676, and MAE 0.067 on the Seen partition, outperforming the strongest baseline LASO (AUC 90.34, aIOU 34.88). The advantage is most pronounced in open-vocabulary settings: on Unseen Object, GREAT reaches AUC 79.57 and aIOU 20.16 (vs. LASO's AUC 73.32), and on Unseen Affordance it attains AUC 69.81, SIM 0.290, and MAE 0.127 (vs. LASO's AUC 64.07, SIM 0.228, MAE 0.140). GREAT is the only method that consistently leads on all four metrics across all three partitions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GREAT outperforms all four baselines on every metric in all three partitions (Seen, Unseen Object, Unseen Affordance). Against the strongest 3D affordance baseline LASO [25], GREAT gains +1.65 AUC and +3.15 aIOU on Seen, +6.25 AUC and +11.66 aIOU on Unseen Object, and +5.74 AUC and +0.62 SIM on Unseen Affordance. The cross-modal baselines FRCNN [54] and XMF [1] (originally designed for shape completion, not affordance) lag further behind, with XMF achieving only 6.6 aIOU on Unseen Object versus GREAT's 20.16. IAG [56], the prior state-of-the-art for image-guided 3D affordance grounding, is surpassed by GREAT by +2.96 AUC on Seen and +7.52 AUC on Unseen Affordance. The largest relative gains appear in the Unseen Object partition, where GREAT's aIOU more than doubles the best baseline, confirming that the MHACoT reasoning and CMAFM fusion are critical for generalizing to novel object categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

GREAT departs from prior open-vocabulary 3D affordance grounding methods (IAGNet, LASO, OpenAD, OpenKD) that directly align geometric features with textual or visual instruction modalities, which the authors argue overfits to the training semantic space. Instead, it repurposes MLLM chain-of-thought reasoning (previously applied to 2D detection and robot manipulation) to extract invariant geometric attributes and analogical interaction intentions from interaction images, treating them as transferable 'knowledge dictionaries' rather than category-specific labels. The cross-modal fusion design draws on image-point cloud fusion literature (FusionRCNN, XMF) but injects the MLLM-derived knowledge through a dedicated adaptive module (CMAFM) at the deepest PointNet++ encoder layer, a structural choice not present in prior affordance grounding pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The open-vocabulary affordance grounding task is a critical bottleneck for general-purpose robot manipulation, and GREAT's demonstrated ability to ground unseen affordances (e.g., 'pour' on a kettle) without category-specific training signals points toward more flexible embodied perception. The PIADv2 dataset (15K images, 38K point clouds, 43 objects, 24 affordances) provides a substantially larger and more diverse benchmark than prior 3D affordance datasets, enabling more rigorous evaluation of generalization. However, the high computational cost of multi-step MLLM reasoning limits real-time deployment, and the authors acknowledge the need for distillation into specialized models. Open questions include whether the CoT-extracted geometric attributes remain valid under significant geometric deformation and whether the approach scales to multi-object interaction scenes.
