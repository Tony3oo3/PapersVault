## Problem

Video large multimodal models (LMMs) perform poorly on out-of-distribution videos (e.g., crime, surgery, sports) due to limited OOD representation in training data, and fine-tuning for each new domain is computationally impractical. While in-context learning (ICL) offers a training-free alternative for text and image tasks, it is infeasible for video because each video example consumes roughly 5.5K tokens, allowing only ~4 examples within a typical 32K context window—far fewer than the many-shot regimes explored in text ICL.

## Input / Output

The method takes a test query consisting of a video clip (sampled at 1 fps, capped at 32 frames) and a text prompt, along with a pre-encoded pool of task-specific video-text example pairs stored in a vector database. It produces task-appropriate outputs: multiple-choice answers, open-ended text responses, class labels, or video captions. The key constraint is that only m=2 video examples (out of k=8 total) can be fed to the model per inference call due to the ~5.5K-token cost per 30-second video at 384×384 resolution, forcing the iterative design.

## Method

VideoICL is a training-free framework with two core components. First, similarity-based example selection ranks k candidate demonstrations by a weighted cosine similarity between SentenceBERT text embeddings and InternVideo2 video embeddings of the query versus each example, retrieving the top-k most relevant pairs from a pre-encoded database at negligible inference cost. Second, confidence-based iterative inference feeds the model m examples at a time (starting from the most similar), computes a confidence score as the minimum token probability across the generated response, and stops iterating once confidence exceeds a threshold c_th (0.7 for multiple-choice, 0.5 otherwise); the final answer is the one with the highest confidence across all iterations. A theoretical analysis (Proposition 1) shows that as the number of iterations grows, expected accuracy converges to a bound determined by the confidence estimator's TPR/FPR and the per-iteration base accuracy, guaranteeing improvement over single-shot ICL when the estimator is sufficiently accurate.

## Results

VideoICL with LLaVA-Video-7B achieves an average improvement of +25.6%p over zero-shot baselines across six OOD benchmarks, with the largest gains in open-ended QA (+54.6%p on PitVQA, +22.1%p on Sports-QA) and video classification (+33.2%p on Drive&Act, +14.0%p on UCF-Crime). On video captioning (CapERA), it gains +0.143 BLEU-4 and +0.104 METEOR over zero-shot. Notably, the 7B VideoICL model surpasses the 72B LLaVA-Video zero-shot model on Animal Kingdom (72.3 vs. 69.7) and PitVQA (61.3 vs. 5.7), and outperforms LoRA fine-tuned counterparts on UCF-Crime (53.3 vs. 51.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoICL (LLaVA-Video-7B) outperforms GPT-4o by an average of +17.8%p and Gemini-1.5 Pro by +14.2%p across the six benchmarks, despite using a 7B backbone versus much larger proprietary models. Against Otter-7B (a video ICL model pre-trained with in-context instruction tuning), VideoICL achieves substantially higher scores on every shared benchmark, e.g., 72.3% vs. 19.4% on Animal Kingdom and 61.3% vs. 6.8% on PitVQA, highlighting the advantage of a training-free approach. Compared to MMICES (an image-ICL example-selection method extended to video), VideoICL wins on all five tasks where both are reported, with the largest margin on PitVQA (61.3 vs. 46.4). Against the ablation baselines SIMRANKVOTE (similarity-ranked examples with majority voting, no confidence) and RANDEXVOTE (random examples with voting), VideoICL's confidence-based iterative inference consistently yields better results, confirming that the confidence mechanism—rather than merely using more examples—drives the gains. On UCF-Crime, VideoICL (53.3%) also surpasses the LoRA fine-tuned LLaVA-Video-7B (51.9%), suggesting that for small OOD datasets, ICL can match or exceed task-specific fine-tuning without any training cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends in-context learning from text and image-language domains to video-language tasks, where the long token sequences of video examples make standard ICL infeasible within limited context windows. It departs from prior video ICL work (Otter, which requires ICL-specific fine-tuning, and MMICES, which selects a fixed small set of examples) by introducing a training-free iterative mechanism inspired by text-only iterative ICL methods (IDS, Se²) and confidence estimation techniques from the LLM literature. The key conceptual move is repurposing token-probability-based confidence as a stopping criterion to sequentially consume a ranked pool of examples, effectively extending the usable context without exceeding the model's token budget.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature of VideoICL makes it a practical, low-cost alternative to fine-tuning for adapting video LMMs to new domains, which is critical given the combinatorial explosion of specialized video types in real-world deployment. However, the approach assumes access to a curated pool of relevant examples for each target task, limiting its applicability to truly novel domains with no available demonstrations. Open questions include how the method scales to longer videos, whether the confidence threshold can be made task-agnostic, and whether the iterative overhead (up to 4× inference cost) is acceptable in latency-sensitive settings.
