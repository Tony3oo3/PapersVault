## Problem

Prior work assesses cross-modal alignment between unimodal vision and language models using proxy metrics (e.g., mutual nearest-neighbor) that do not directly measure the cross-modal distances used during VLM inference. Additionally, building strong vision-language models like CLIP requires training from scratch on hundreds of millions of image-text pairs, leaving open whether pretrained unimodal models can be efficiently aligned with far less data and compute.

## Input / Output

SAIL takes RGB images at 224×224 resolution and text prompts/captions as input. Both the DINOv2 vision encoder and the language encoder (GTE-en-large-v1.5 or NV-Embed-2) are frozen; only a lightweight GLU alignment layer (output dim 1024) is trained. During training, images and text are pre-encoded into embeddings once, so only these embeddings plus the alignment layer are loaded onto the GPU, enabling batch sizes up to 32,768 on a single A100. Outputs include aligned image-text embeddings for retrieval, zero-shot class predictions, and patch-level segmentation masks via cosine similarity between patch tokens and sentence embeddings.

## Method

SAIL aligns frozen pretrained unimodal encoders through three optimized components: (1) a non-linear Gated Linear Unit (GLU) alignment layer with 8× intermediate expansion, which outperforms both linear layers and MLPs; (2) a sigmoid-based contrastive loss that averages over all B² pairs (rather than just B positive pairs) to balance positive and negative contributions, replacing CLIP's InfoNCE; and (3) multi-positive caption training that combines short web captions with long ShareGPT4V synthetic captions as additional positives in the same batch. The paper also introduces alignment probing—a linear-probing-style assessment that freezes both backbones and trains only a linear alignment layer on 2.2M CC3M pairs to directly measure cross-modal retrieval performance. This reveals that SSL clustering quality (k-NN accuracy) correlates more strongly with alignment than linear separability, and that language understanding capability (MTEB score) is the dominant factor for complex vision-language reasoning.

## Results

SAIL-L-NV2, trained on only 23M image-text pairs (~6% of CLIP's 400M), achieves 73.4% zero-shot top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1k, surpassing CLIP-L (72.7%). On COCO retrieval, SAIL-L-NV2 reaches 48.6% T2I R@1 and 62.4% I2T R@1, outperforming CLIP-L (43.0% and 59.7%). On the complex reasoning Winoground benchmark, SAIL-L-NV2 scores 40.25/18.75/15.0 (Text/Image/Group) versus CLIP-L's 30.5/11.5/8.75, and on MMVP it achieves 28.9 vs. CLIP-L's 20.0. For open-vocabulary segmentation on ADE20K, SAIL(NV2) reaches 14.2 mIOU compared to CLIP's 1.2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAIL-L-NV2 (23M pairs) outperforms CLIP-L (400M pairs) on ImageNet-1k (73.4% vs. 72.7%), COCO T2I retrieval (48.6% vs. 43.0%), I2T retrieval (62.4% vs. 59.7%), Winoground (40.25 vs. 30.5 Text score), MMVP (28.9 vs. 20.0), and ADE20K segmentation (14.2 vs. 1.2 mIOU), winning on 6 of 10 classification datasets. Against DreamLIP, LiT, and ShareLock (all using the same DINOv2 backbone), SAIL consistently achieves higher scores on both classification and retrieval; for example, SAIL-B-NV2 reaches 68.1% IN-1K vs. ShareLock's 59.1% and LiT's 56.2% on CC12M. On open-vocabulary segmentation, SAIL(NV2) surpasses MaskCLIP (14.2 vs. 6.9 mIOU on ADE20K) and SCLIP (14.2 vs. 7.1), though SCLIP retains a lead on VOC20 (60.3 vs. 66.1 is actually SAIL winning; SCLIP 60.3 vs SAIL 66.1). When integrated into LLaVA-1.5, SAIL's vision encoder surpasses CLIP-L/14 in 5 out of 7 MLLM benchmark tasks (e.g., GQA 50.00 vs. 49.93, SEED-IMG 62.63 vs. 61.54), while both underperform on OCR-heavy tasks (TextVQA, MMBench) due to DINOv2's inherent OCR limitations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the efficient VLM alignment line initiated by LiT (freeze vision, train language) and ShareLock (add a tunable head over frozen language), but departs by freezing both unimodal backbones and training only a lightweight non-linear GLU alignment layer, making it the first framework to simultaneously preserve the strengths of both pretrained encoders. It also introduces a direct alignment probing methodology inspired by SSL linear probing, contrasting with prior proxy-based assessments (mutual nearest-neighbor, centered kernel distance) that do not measure cross-modal distances the way inference actually does. The sigmoid contrastive loss and multi-positive caption strategy build on recent improvements to CLIP-style training (Zhai et al. 2023; DreamLIP), while the use of DINOv2 and NV-Embed-2 as frozen backbones positions the work at the intersection of SSL vision and large-scale language embedding research.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a single A100 GPU and ~5 hours of training with 6% of CLIP's paired data yields a model that outperforms CLIP-L on ImageNet, retrieval, and complex reasoning, dramatically lowering the barrier for academic groups to build competitive VLMs. The finding that clustering quality (k-NN) of SSL representations predicts alignment more strongly than linear separability, and that language model quality (MTEB) is the dominant factor for complex reasoning, provides actionable guidance for selecting backbones. A key limitation is that DINOv2's inherent OCR weakness persists through SAIL alignment, capping performance on text-centric benchmarks like TextVQA and MMBench. Open questions include whether the alignment probing framework generalizes to other modality pairs (audio, video) and whether the GLU alignment layer can be further compressed without performance loss.
