## Problem

Existing training-free alignment methods for text-to-image diffusion models were designed for U-Net architectures and rely on optimizing the latent space using resolution-specific cross-attention maps. When directly transferred to Transformer-based models (e.g., PixArt-α, DiT), these methods show limited effectiveness because Transformer cross-attention maps maintain a uniform resolution and more evenly distributed semantic information, making it difficult to identify a core map for guidance. This paper addresses the resulting gap in achieving precise coarse-grained, fine-grained, and style attribute binding in Transformer-based TGDMs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language text prompt (e.g., 'an apple with blue flesh and an orange stem') and produces a 512×512 RGB image via PixArt-α. It is entirely training-free, operating during the 50-step denoising process by modifying cross-attention maps in-place. For fine-grained attribute binding, an LLM is queried at inference time to infer spatial proportions of object parts (e.g., flesh vs. stem ratio), which guides mask extraction without any additional training data.

## Method

Self-Coherence Guidance (SCG) directly optimizes cross-attention maps during generation rather than the latent space, exploiting the fact that Transformer architectures preserve a constant spatial resolution across all layers. At each denoising step t, the method extracts a spatial mask from the previous step's (t+1) concept-token attention map (averaged across layers), then selectively amplifies the current step's attribute/style-token attention at positions where the concept mask is active, using a multiplicative factor c. For coarse-grained and style binding, the mask is obtained via K-means clustering of the averaged attention map into two regions; for fine-grained binding, an LLM infers the approximate area ratio of object parts, and the mask is extracted by thresholding the attention map at the corresponding quantile. This bypasses the intermediate loss-computation-and-latent-optimization pipeline used by U-Net-based methods, eliminating the semantic gap introduced by that indirect route.

## Results

On the BLIP-VQA metric, Self-Coherence Guidance achieves 0.679 (coarse-grained), 0.623 (fine-grained), and 0.799 (style) binding scores, substantially outperforming the base PIXART-α model (0.293, 0.398, 0.676) and all U-Net-based SOTA methods. In text-text similarity, the method reaches 0.852, 0.827, and 0.689 across the three subtasks, surpassing CONFORM(SD) (0.834, 0.774, 0.642) and D&B(SD) (0.798, 0.742, 0.660). In a 50-participant user study, the method's outputs are preferred 81%, 90.6%, and 92.6% of the time for coarse-grained, fine-grained, and style binding respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Self-Coherence Guidance against four baselines: the base PIXART-α Transformer model, and three U-Net-based SOTA methods (D&B and CONFORM, both evaluated on Stable Diffusion v1.5). On BLIP-VQA, the method wins decisively across all three subtasks: it improves over PIXART-α by 131% on coarse-grained (0.679 vs 0.293), 56% on fine-grained (0.623 vs 0.398), and 18% on style binding (0.799 vs 0.676). Against CONFORM(SD), the strongest U-Net baseline, the method leads on coarse-grained (0.679 vs 0.667) and fine-grained (0.623 vs 0.522) but trails slightly on style (0.799 vs 0.498 is actually a win — correction: 0.799 vs 0.498). In text-text similarity, the method outperforms CONFORM(SD) on coarse-grained (0.852 vs 0.834) and fine-grained (0.827 vs 0.774) but is nearly tied on style (0.689 vs 0.642). The user study confirms these quantitative findings, with 81–92.6% preference rates for the proposed method versus 0.4–13.2% for all baselines combined.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the cross-attention editing paradigm established by Prompt-to-Prompt [12], which demonstrated that cross-attention maps encode rich spatial-semantic information and can be directly manipulated. However, it departs from both Prompt-to-Prompt (which requires a reference image for editing) and the U-Net-based alignment methods (D&B, CONFORM, Attend-and-Excite) that optimize the latent space through loss functions. The key conceptual shift is recognizing that Transformer architectures, lacking downsampling/upsampling modules, produce uniformly-shaped attention maps with more evenly distributed semantic information, making latent-space optimization less effective and motivating direct attention-map refinement via self-coherence from prior denoising steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As Transformer-based diffusion models (DiT, PixArt-α) increasingly replace U-Net architectures, the finding that established alignment methods do not transfer is practically critical for the community. The extension from coarse-grained to fine-grained attribute and style binding addresses a real gap in compositional generation. Limitations include evaluation on a single model at a single resolution (PixArt-α-512), reliance on LLMs for fine-grained proportion estimation, and modest gains in style binding. Open questions include whether the self-coherence mechanism generalizes to video diffusion, larger resolutions, or other Transformer variants, and whether the LLM-based proportion estimation can be replaced by learned alternatives.
