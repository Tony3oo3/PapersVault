## Problem

Existing semi-supervised medical image segmentation methods train all model components indiscriminately with both labeled and unlabeled data, but pseudo labels from unlabeled data degrade decoder performance since decoders depend heavily on accurate annotations. This issue is exacerbated in class-imbalanced multi-organ segmentation, where small organs are under-segmented or entirely ignored. Additionally, prior methods treat labeled and unlabeled data as independent streams, missing the opportunity for mutual semantic enrichment.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D abdominal CT volumes resampled to 80×160×160 (Synapse) or equivalent (AMOS), randomly cropped to 64×128×128 patches during training with a batch of 2 labeled + 2 unlabeled samples. At inference, the unlabeled decoder produces per-voxel multi-organ segmentation masks (13 foreground classes for Synapse, 15 for AMOS) via a sliding window with stride 32×32×16. Evaluation uses Dice Score and Average Surface Distance (ASD) in voxels.

## Method

SKCDF decouples the data flow so the shared VNet encoder learns from both labeled and unlabeled data, while two separate decoders (labeled and unlabeled) are trained on their respective streams, preventing noisy pseudo labels from corrupting the labeled decoder. A semantic knowledge complementarity module performs local (patch-level) and global (volume-level) channel-wise cross-attention between labeled and unlabeled features, enabling unlabeled data to enrich labeled feature representations while labeled data guides higher-quality pseudo-label generation. An auxiliary balanced segmentation head, inspired by ABC, applies Bernoulli-distribution masks inversely proportional to class voxel counts to up-weight minority organ losses without reweighting the main heads. EMA-based knowledge transfer is applied between decoders and between auxiliary and main segmentation heads in a Head→Decoder→Head sequence to propagate balanced knowledge back to the main prediction path.

## Results

On the 20% labeled Synapse dataset, SKCDF achieves the best average Dice of 64.27% and average ASD of 1.45 voxels, outperforming the strongest baseline GenericSSL (60.88% Dice, 2.52 ASD) by 3.39% in Dice. On the 5% labeled AMOS dataset, SKCDF achieves 53.81% average Dice, a 3.78% improvement over GenericSSL (50.03%). The gains are most pronounced on small and complex organs: on Synapse, the method improves stomach (+9.6%), pancreas (+7.5%), and left adrenal gland (+10.6%) over the next-best method, and on AMOS, esophagus (+5.5%), stomach (+2.6%), and right adrenal gland (+2.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SKCDF outperforms all 13 baselines on average Dice for both the 20% labeled Synapse and 5% labeled AMOS datasets. The closest competitor is GenericSSL [32], which SKCDF surpasses by 3.39% Dice on Synapse (64.27% vs. 60.88%) and 3.78% on AMOS (53.81% vs. 50.03%); however, GenericSSL achieves a slightly lower ASD on AMOS (5.21 vs. 5.97 voxels). Among imbalance-aware methods, DHC [31] is the strongest, but SKCDF leads it by 15.66% Dice on Synapse and 4.28% on AMOS. General SSL methods (UA-MT, URPC, CPS, SS-Net, DST, DePL) all fail to segment several small organs (gallbladder, esophagus, adrenal glands) with near-zero Dice, whereas SKCDF achieves 59.8–60.7% on these classes. The auxiliary balanced segmentation head is particularly effective: gallbladder Dice jumps from 0.0% (most baselines) to 59.8%, and left adrenal gland from 0.0–10.6% to 37.8%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the semi-supervised medical image segmentation literature by introducing a data flow decoupling framework that separates encoder training (on all data) from decoder training (on labeled vs. unlabeled data separately), building conceptually on GenericSSL's decoupling idea but reframing it around the distinct responsibilities of encoder (feature extraction) versus decoder (segmentation decision-making). The semantic knowledge complementarity module draws on cross-attention mechanisms from AllSpark and GuidedNet but inverts the information flow: labeled data guides pseudo-label generation while unlabeled data enriches labeled features, rather than the reverse. The auxiliary balanced segmentation head is a direct adaptation of the ABC classifier (Lee et al., NeurIPS 2021) from classification to 3D segmentation, using Bernoulli-sampled masks weighted inversely to class frequency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling framework offers a clean, interpretable principle for semi-supervised segmentation: unlabeled data should inform feature learning but not directly supervise the segmentation head, which addresses a practical failure mode (small organs being ignored) that prior methods handle only through reweighting or resampling. The auxiliary balanced head's ability to lift gallbladder Dice from 0% to 62% without degrading majority-class performance is practically significant for clinical multi-organ segmentation. However, the method's complexity (two decoders, four segmentation heads, a specific knowledge-transfer sequence H→D→H) raises questions about generalizability to transformer backbones, other modalities beyond abdominal CT, and whether the decoupling principle holds when the encoder and decoder are more tightly coupled (e.g., in U-Net skip connections).
