## Problem

Deploying DR grading models in clinical settings faces a triple constraint: the pre-trained source model must remain inaccessible (to prevent model-targeted attacks like membership inference), target patient data arrives sequentially in a flowing manner, and source data must stay private. Existing adaptation methods (UDA, SFDA, TTA) each require at least one of model access, offline target data, or source data, making them inapplicable to this combined scenario. The paper introduces the Online Model-aGnostic Domain Adaptation (OMG-DA) setting that enforces all three constraints simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled fundus images from the target domain arriving in an online, batch-by-batch fashion (batch sizes from 2 to 64). It produces generative unadversarial examples — the original image plus an individually generated perturbation of the same spatial dimensions — which are then fed to a frozen DeiT-base source model or a trainable TTA model for 5-class DR grading. The VAE itself is trained entirely on target data using fine-grained saliency maps as reconstruction targets, requiring no labels, no source data, and no access to the source model's parameters or outputs.

## Method

GUES theoretically reformulates iterative unadversarial perturbation optimization (Theorem 1) into a generative form δ_k = δ_0 + V·F_Φ(∂δ_0/∂x), where the perturbation is produced by a learned function F_Φ parameterized by a latent variable ∂δ_0/∂x. In practice, a VAE instantiates this: the encoder with reparameterization trick approximates the latent input from the input image, and the decoder generates the individual perturbation. A bypass path adds the perturbation to the original image to form the unadversarial example. Training is driven by two losses: a KL divergence term aligning the latent space to N(0,I) (linking random noise to the latent variable) and an MSE reconstruction loss against the fine-grained saliency map, which Theorem 2 proves provides a valid upper bound on ∂δ_0/∂x, justifying its role as pseudo-perturbation supervision. This design eliminates dependence on model parameters, labels, and offline data while producing image-specific (rather than class-specific) perturbations that stabilize performance at small batch sizes.

## Results

Across 12 cross-domain transfer tasks on four DR benchmarks (APTOS, DDR, DeepDR, Messidor-2), GUES+Source (frozen model) achieves average ACC of 58.4%, QWK of 62.9%, and AVG of 60.7%, improving over the unadapted source model by +4.5% ACC, +2.8% QWK, and +3.7% AVG. When combined with trainable TTA methods, GUES+SHOT-IM reaches 62.0% ACC, 66.9% QWK, and 64.5% AVG, while GUES+TENT reaches 61.0% ACC, 63.3% QWK, and 62.2% AVG. GUES also demonstrates robustness to small batch sizes, where SHOT-IM drops ~16% ACC and TENT drops ~34% QWK at batch size 2, but GUES-augmented variants show no significant degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GUES+SHOT-IM outperforms all nine baselines on average: it surpasses the best SFDA method (TPDS, 59.2% ACC) by 2.8% ACC and the best TTA method (TENT, 59.3% ACC) by 2.7% ACC, while also leading in QWK (66.9% vs. SHOT's 65.0%) and AVG (64.5% vs. SHOT-IM's 61.9%). Compared to its own backbone SHOT-IM, GUES+SHOT-IM gains +3.0% ACC, +2.2% QWK, and +2.6% AVG. GUES+TENT improves over TENT by +1.7% ACC, +4.1% QWK, and +2.9% AVG, with the QWK gain being particularly notable given the severe class imbalance in DR datasets. In the no-training setting, GUES+Source (58.4% ACC) already exceeds NRC (53.1%), CoWA (56.9%), PLUE (55.5%), and SAR (56.3%), and matches SHOT (58.4%) while requiring no model access. The key differentiator is that GUES operates under the OMG-DA constraint (no model weights, flowing data, source privacy), whereas all SFDA baselines require full model access and offline target data, and TTA baselines require model access.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on unadversarial learning (Salman et al., NeurIPS 2021), which modifies input distributions to improve model generalization, but departs fundamentally by reformulating the iterative perturbation optimization into a generative function (Theorem 1) that can be learned without access to the model, loss, or labels. It positions itself against SFDA methods (SHOT, NRC, CoWA, PLUE, TPDS) that require full model access and offline target data, and TTA methods (SHOT-IM, TENT, SAR) that require model access and training, by introducing the OMG-DA setting where neither the model nor source data are available and target data arrives in a stream. The VAE-based instantiation with saliency-map supervision is a novel combination: VAEs are repurposed not for representation learning but for generating individualized perturbations, and saliency maps—previously used only as attention guides in medical imaging—are repurposed as pseudo-perturbation labels with a theoretical upper-bound justification (Theorem 2).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The OMG-DA setting addresses a real clinical deployment gap: hospitals cannot share model weights (privacy/attack risk) or accumulate patient data offline (streaming arrivals, storage), yet must adapt to new imaging equipment or patient populations. The demonstrated robustness to small batch sizes (2 vs. 64) is practically significant for streaming clinical pipelines where batch accumulation is infeasible. However, the authors acknowledge a key limitation: saliency maps work well for the relatively monolithic structure of fundus images but fail for natural images with rich, task-irrelevant semantics, where the bound constant U in Theorem 2 becomes too relaxed. Open questions include whether task-specific saliency alternatives or learned pseudo-labels could extend the approach beyond medical imaging, and whether the generative perturbation framework generalizes to other model-agnostic adaptation scenarios.
