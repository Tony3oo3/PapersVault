## Problem

LiDAR semantic segmentation models suffer significant accuracy degradation under adverse weather (fog, rain, snow), and existing remedies rely on complex weather simulation or universal data augmentation. This paper identifies that adverse weather induces heterogeneous domain shifts: reflectance intensity is drastically altered while geometric structure remains relatively stable, yet prior work treats both as a unified input. The key question is how to explicitly exploit this asymmetry to extract robust, generalizable features without augmentation or simulation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw LiDAR scan with per-point 3D coordinates (x, y, z) and reflectance intensity r, and produces a per-point semantic label. The input is consumed in two distinct forms: coordinates are voxelized into a sparse 3D grid for the geometric branch, while reflectance is spherically projected into a 2D range-view image (H×W) for the reflectance branch. Training uses only clear-weather source data (SemanticKITTI or SynLiDAR), and the model must generalize to unseen adverse-weather targets (SemanticSTF with 19 categories, SemanticKITTI-C with 8 corruption types at 3 severity levels).

## Method

GRCNet employs a dual-branch encoder: a sparse 3D convolutional network (MinkNet) processes voxelized geometry, while a 2D depthwise-separable convolutional network with Instance Normalization processes the range-view reflectance image. A Complementarity-aware Information Constraint (CIC) projects each branch's features into Gaussian distributions via reparameterization, then minimizes KL divergence to a standard Gaussian prior (discarding domain-specific noise) and between the two branches (enforcing complementarity). Fusion occurs at two levels: locally, a dynamic weighted combination uses per-branch standard deviations as reliability indicators; globally, a two-stage cross-attention with learnable query tokens efficiently aggregates range-view global context into the voxel domain without direct large-scale attention. The total loss is cross-entropy plus the CIC objective, and the entire framework is trained solely on source-domain data with no weather simulation or augmentation.

## Results

On the primary SemanticKITTI→SemanticSTF benchmark, GRCNet achieves 42.5 mIoU overall, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art RDA (39.5) by +3.0 mIoU and the MinkNet baseline (24.4) by +18.1 mIoU. Per-weather gains over RDA are +2.1 (dense fog), +2.6 (light fog), +0.5 (rain), and +1.0 (snow). On the harder SynLiDAR→SemanticSTF setting, GRCNet reaches 23.5 mIoU, slightly edging out UniMix (23.4) while substantially outperforming RDA (20.5). On SemanticKITTI-C with MinkNet18/32 backbone, the method achieves 60.3 mIoU vs. 58.6 for RDA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRCNet outperforms all eight baselines on the primary SemanticKITTI→SemanticSTF task. Against the MinkNet backbone (24.4 mIoU), the gain is +18.1 mIoU; against data-augmentation-based methods PolarMix (26.0), MMD (26.9), PCL (26.4), and PointDR (28.6), gains range from +13.9 to +16.5 mIoU. The closest competitor is RDA [30], which uses reinforcement-learning-driven augmentation and requires four A6000 GPUs; GRCNet surpasses it by +3.0 mIoU while training on a single RTX 4090. UniMix [61], which relies on weather simulation plus EMA ensembling, is outperformed by +11.1 mIoU on SemanticKITTI→SemanticSTF and by +0.1 on the harder SynLiDAR→SemanticSTF setting. With the stronger MinkNet18/32 backbone (Table 3), GRCNet extends its lead over RDA to +4.2 mIoU on SemanticSTF (43.7 vs. 39.5) and +1.7 on SemanticKITTI-C (60.3 vs. 58.6), and also beats SPVCNN+RDA by +2.4 mIoU on SemanticSTF.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of addressing adverse-weather LiDAR segmentation through data-centric strategies—weather simulation (Hahner et al., UniMix) or universal augmentation (PolarMix, RDA)—by instead proposing an architectural solution that explicitly decouples geometric and reflectance feature extraction. While multi-branch point cloud networks exist (Alnaggar et al., RpvNet), none address the domain-generalization setting or the specific insight that geometry and reflectance undergo heterogeneous domain shifts under adverse weather. The Complementarity-aware Information Constraint repurposes variational information bottleneck ideas from the OOD generalization literature (Ahuja et al., Alemi et al.) into a paired-feature setting, and the two-stage cross-attention with learnable query tokens is a novel fusion design tailored to the 3D-voxel/2D-range-view geometry mismatch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that simply discarding reflectance improves generalization by +12.9 mIoU challenges the common assumption that all input channels are beneficial, and the subsequent recovery-and-exceedance via the GRC framework demonstrates that corrupted reflectance still carries recoverable semantic signal. Practically, the method trains on a single RTX 4090 versus four A6000 GPUs for the prior SOTA (RDA), making robust LiDAR segmentation more accessible. Open questions include whether the geometry-reflectance separation principle generalizes to other sensor modalities (e.g., radar, multi-spectral), whether it transfers to downstream tasks like 3D detection or tracking, and whether the information-theoretic constraint can be extended to handle more than two feature streams.
