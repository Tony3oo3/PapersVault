## Problem

Existing text-guided video inpainting methods rely on pre-trained text-to-video diffusion backbones that suffer from limited visual quality and weak text-following due to scarce video training data, making sophisticated creative manipulations (e.g., transforming a head into a balloon) impractical. The core gap is that state-of-the-art image inpainting models offer far superior quality and controllability, yet no effective mechanism exists to leverage them within a video inpainting pipeline while preserving temporal coherence.

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip (14 or 25 frames at up to 576×1024), a mask sequence defining regions to inpaint (ranging from precise segmentation to rough user sketches), and an optionally inpainted keyframe produced by any external image-level tool (e.g., Photoshop generative fill or FLUX ControlNet). The output is a temporally coherent video where masked regions are filled with content consistent with both the inpainted keyframe and unmasked surrounding pixels. The model supports arbitrary keyframe positions (not just the first frame) and arbitrary mask geometries without requiring segmentation preprocessing at inference time.

## Method

VideoRepainter is a two-stage framework: the user first inpaints a keyframe with any image-level technique, then a repurposed image-to-video (I2V) diffusion model (SVD) propagates the edit across remaining frames. The key architectural contribution is a symmetric mask conditioning mechanism that encodes two VAE variants of the masked image (black-filled and white-filled mask regions) and concatenates both latents as UNet input, resolving the spatial ambiguity introduced by conventional mask downsampling to latent resolution. For efficient adaptation, only LoRA adapters on spatial and temporal attention layers plus a new input convolution are trained (<2% of total parameters, 27.9M of 1525.9M), preserving pre-trained I2V priors. Training uses content-agnostic spatiotemporal mask augmentation (full, grid, square, scribble masks with Bezier trajectories and temporal shape variants) on ~300K in-the-wild videos, eliminating the need for segmentation-based preprocessing. For sequences exceeding the training length, a coarse-to-fine strategy samples sparse anchor frames first, then applies MultiDiffusion on subsequences conditioned on those anchors.

## Results

On the video editing benchmark (20 in-the-wild samples), VideoRepainter achieves 97.31 CLIP consistency, 32.29 PSNR (background), 3.77×10⁻⁴ MSE, and 5.49 aesthetic score, outperforming all four baselines on every metric. On the unconditioned video inpainting benchmark (50 DAVIS videos), it reaches 96.01 consistency, 31.46 PSNR, 8.85×10⁻⁴ MSE, and 4.47 aesthetic score, again leading all baselines. The method uses only 27.9M trainable parameters (out of 1525.9M total) via LoRA on the SVD-xt backbone, trained on ~300K self-collected videos with 16 A100 GPUs for 50K iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoRepainter outperforms all four baselines on every metric in the video editing evaluation. Against CoCoCo (text-guided inpainting), it gains +1.15 in consistency, +0.47 in PSNR, and nearly halves the background MSE (3.77 vs 6.66), while also improving aesthetic score by 0.25. Against I2VGen-XL (image-to-video), the gains are similar in consistency (+1.33) and MSE (3.77 vs 7.61), with a notable aesthetic advantage (5.49 vs 4.68). Against ModelScope (text-to-video), the margins are large across all metrics, with PSNR improving by 6.52 points and MSE dropping from 28.28 to 3.77. Against AnyV2V (DDIM-inversion-based editing), VideoRepainter achieves dramatically better background fidelity (PSNR 32.29 vs 18.38, MSE 3.77 vs 173.96), indicating that DDIM inversion severely degrades unmasked regions, while also matching or exceeding AnyV2V on consistency and aesthetic score.

## Relation to Prior Work

VideoRepainter departs from the dominant text-guided video inpainting paradigm (AVID, CoCoCo) by decoupling content generation from temporal propagation: it first inpaints a keyframe using SOTA image-level tools (FLUX ControlNet, Photoshop generative fill), then repurposes a pre-trained image-to-video diffusion model (SVD) to propagate the edit across frames. The key architectural contribution is a symmetric mask conditioning mechanism that resolves spatial ambiguity introduced by downsampling binary masks to VAE latent resolution, replacing the conventional single-channel mask concatenation used in prior diffusion-based inpainting. For model adaptation, the paper applies LoRA to spatial and temporal attention layers, updating less than 2% of SVD's parameters, and introduces content-agnostic spatiotemporal mask augmentation to train on in-the-wild videos without segmentation preprocessing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-stage design makes creative video inpainting practically accessible by offloading the hardest part (high-quality content synthesis) to mature image models, while the I2V backbone handles only temporal coherence. This significantly lowers the barrier compared to training dedicated video inpainting models from scratch or relying on text prompts with weak text-following. However, the approach is bounded by the I2V backbone's domain capacity (e.g., adding an anime character to a realistic scene yields static results) and still requires a user-provided mask rather than instruction-based editing. Open questions include whether instruction-driven mask generation via MLLMs can close this gap, and whether the symmetric mask trick generalizes to higher-resolution or longer-sequence video models.
