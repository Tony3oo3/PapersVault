## Problem

Zero-shot image restoration via plug-and-play methods can employ either deterministic regression (high data fidelity but smearing artifacts) or stochastic sampling with diffusion models (high perceptual quality but poor fidelity), yet these strategies have not been integrated in a unified zero-shot framework. Prior attempts to combine discriminative and generative priors relied on cascaded networks, introducing detail mismatches, extra computational cost, and overfitting. The paper asks whether a single pre-trained diffusion model can simultaneously serve as both a stochastic sampler and a deterministic denoiser within one iterative restoration loop.

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded RGB image y (256×256), a known forward degradation operator A (bicubic downsampling for 2×/4×/8× super-resolution, or Gaussian/motion blur kernels), additive Gaussian noise level σ_n, and a single pre-trained diffusion model ε_θ. It outputs a restored clean image x_0 after T=100 iterative steps. A single hyperparameter τ ∈ [0,1] controls the distortion-perception tradeoff: τ→0 yields pure deterministic regression (RED-like), while τ→1 yields pure stochastic sampling (DiffPIR-like), with intermediate values blending both.

## Method

RDMD formulates restoration via half-quadratic splitting with an auxiliary variable z, placing the regularizer in both sub-problems with complementary roles controlled by τ. In the x sub-problem, one step of DDIM reverse diffusion (Eq. 12) performs stochastic sampling, with renoising (Eq. 14) using effective noise ε̂_t and a stochasticity parameter ζ to maintain alignment with the diffusion trajectory. In the z sub-problem, the deterministic regularizer follows the RED formulation R_θ^D(z_t) = ½ z_t^T(z_t − f_θ(z_t; t')), whose gradient ∇R = z_t − f_θ(z_t; t') is computed explicitly from the same diffusion model at a matched noise level t', enabling a gradient-descent update that enforces data consistency and deterministic denoising simultaneously. The key insight is that the diffusion model's training objective is equivalent to training a non-blind Gaussian denoiser, so the same ε_θ can be repurposed as a discriminative regularizer without any additional network. This yields a unified zero-shot framework where a single model provides both generative diversity and regression fidelity, outperforming both its deterministic (RED) and stochastic (DiffPIR) special cases across deblurring and super-resolution tasks on FFHQ and ImageNet.

## Results

On FFHQ 256×256 (σ_n=0.05), RDMD achieves the best PSNR across all five restoration tasks: 28.29 dB (Gaussian deblur), 27.05 dB (motion deblur), 31.03 dB (2× SR), 27.84 dB (4× SR), and 23.31 dB (8× SR), surpassing the best competing method by up to +2.23 dB on 8× SR. On ImageNet 256×256, RDMD leads in PSNR for Gaussian deblur (23.30 dB), 2× SR (25.94 dB), 4× SR (23.28 dB), and 8× SR (20.83 dB), though DiffPIR retains a slight LPIPS edge on several tasks. The method also achieves the best SSIM on nearly all FFHQ tasks (e.g., 0.8715 on 2× SR) and competitive LPIPS, demonstrating a favorable distortion–perception balance controlled by the single parameter τ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RDMD consistently outperforms all four external baselines (MCG, DPS, Resample, BIRD) on PSNR and SSIM across both FFHQ and ImageNet. MCG and Resample are particularly weak on noisy deblurring tasks (PSNR ≈ 11–19 dB), as their hard data-consistency projections overfit to measurement noise; RDMD avoids this by incorporating a deterministic regularizer that suppresses noise while preserving perceptual detail. DPS achieves moderate results (e.g., 23.78 dB on Gaussian deblur, FFHQ) but is outperformed by RDMD by 4.5 dB on the same task. BIRD is the strongest external competitor, reaching 26.14 dB on Gaussian deblur (FFHQ), yet RDMD still leads by 2.15 dB. Against the two internal special cases—RED (τ=0, pure deterministic) and DiffPIR (τ=1, pure stochastic)—RDMD with intermediate τ achieves higher PSNR than both on most tasks (e.g., +0.45 dB over RED and +0.99 dB over DiffPIR on 4× SR, FFHQ) while retaining LPIPS competitive with DiffPIR, confirming that the reconciliation of the two strategies yields a genuine Pareto improvement over either extreme.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of zero-shot image restoration: the deterministic PnP/RED paradigm (using a denoiser as a regularizer via gradient descent) and the stochastic diffusion-sampling paradigm (using a pre-trained diffusion model to guide the reverse process). Rather than cascading separate discriminative and generative networks as in DiffBIR or ResDiff, RDMD repurposes a single pre-trained diffusion model to play both roles simultaneously within one HQS-style iterative loop, with a weighting parameter τ controlling the relative contribution of each. The stochastic branch follows DiffPIR's effective-noise renoising, while the deterministic branch adopts RED's explicit regularizer gradient, making both prior methods special cases (τ=1 and τ=0 respectively) of the proposed framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in offering a single hyperparameter (τ) to navigate the well-known distortion-perception tradeoff without retraining or swapping models, which is directly useful for practitioners who need to balance fidelity and perceptual quality across different deployment contexts. A notable limitation is the reliance on RED's symmetric-Jacobian assumption for the deterministic regularizer's gradient, which may not hold perfectly for all diffusion architectures, and the method is validated only on 256×256 images with known degradation operators. Open questions include whether the dual-strategy formulation extends to video restoration, unknown/compound degradations, or larger-resolution settings where the iterative cost (T=100 steps) becomes prohibitive.
