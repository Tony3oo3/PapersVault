## Problem

Hybrid visual foraging—searching for multiple instances of multiple target types with varying values and prevalence—is a common real-world task, yet existing AI visual search models assume either a single target type or equal values across targets, and there is a notable lack of studies examining how eye movements reflect the underlying decision-making process in such tasks. This paper addresses both gaps by studying human eye-tracking behavior in hybrid foraging and proposing an AI model that learns value- and prevalence-guided foraging without any human data.

## Input / Output

At each fixation step, the model receives a search image (a 16×16 grid of objects), N target images (N ∈ {1,2,4}), and their scalar values, all processed through foveated vision where receptive field size grows with eccentricity from the current fixation. It outputs two actions: a next fixation location discretized to the 16×16 grid (sampled from a softmax attention map) and a binary click probability (sigmoid) for the currently fixated item. Time costs of 776 ms per click and 336 ms per fixation are imposed to match human motor delays for fair comparison.

## Method

VF comprises three modules: (1) a VGG16 backbone with eccentricity-dependent pooling (replacing standard max-pooling) to mimic foveated vision, whose features are convolved with target image features to produce N similarity maps; (2) a value encoder (two FC layers) whose embedding is added element-wise to a CNN-encoded version of the similarity maps, yielding fixation-dependent value-modulated feature maps; and (3) a ViT-based actor-critic policy network that reshapes these maps into patches, appends a click token and a state-value token, and outputs fixation and click distributions. Training uses PPO with a two-stage curriculum: stage 1 trains only the fixation policy without foveation and with infinite inhibition-of-return, then stage 2 freezes those weights and fine-tunes positional embeddings and the click policy under foveated vision with a finite IOR (exponential decay η=0.8). A channel augmentation that shuffles target image/value pair order during training enables generalization to unseen value combinations and target sets.

## Results

Under three foraging conditions (Uneven Value/Equal Prevalence, Uneven Value/Unequal Prevalence, Even Value/Unequal Prevalence), human participants achieve Normalized Scores of 87.4%, 84.1%, and 93.1%, while the VF model achieves 72.6%, 67.1%, and 81.6%, respectively—surpassing all six baseline models in every condition. VF's mean saccade size of 4.06° closely matches the human mean of 4.05°, despite zero training on human eye-movement data. In out-of-distribution generalization tests (unseen targets, unseen values, varying set sizes), VF consistently outperforms the best baseline (FeatOnly) but still lags behind human performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VF outperforms all six internal baselines (Chance, FeatOnly, MaxVal, AvgVal, DQN, UpperBound) across all three in-domain and OOD conditions, with FeatOnly being the strongest baseline but still trailing VF. Against DQN [92] (a 2D-CNN-based deep-Q network trained end-to-end), VF's transformer-based actor-critic architecture with foveated vision and value modulation yields substantially higher cumulative rewards, demonstrating the benefit of the modular design. Compared to IVSN [145] (a zero-shot visual search model using target-feature convolution), VF's additional value modulation and RL-trained click policy produce superior foraging performance, particularly when target values are uneven. Gazeformer [93], a scanpath prediction model fine-tuned on in-domain data, also underperforms VF, though the comparison is noted as not entirely equitable since Gazeformer does not use foveated vision. Notably, VF achieves these results without any training on human eye-tracking data, whereas Gazeformer and IVSN variants rely on human data for supervised or behavioral-cloning training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the foveated visual search line of work (Gupta et al. [51] for eccentricity-dependent pooling; Zhang et al. [145] for target-feature attentional modulation) by adding a value-based modulation channel and an RL-trained decision-making head that jointly outputs fixation and click actions. Unlike prior scanpath-prediction models (GazeFormer, OAT, IRL) that are supervised on human eye-tracking data and assume single-target or equal-value settings, VF is trained end-to-end with PPO on procedurally generated foraging environments and explicitly handles multiple target types with heterogeneous values and prevalence. The two-stage curriculum (fixation-only → full foraging with finite IOR) departs from the infinite-IOR assumption common in prior visual search models, enabling strategic revisits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a rare joint benchmark where a purely RL-trained AI model is compared against human eye-tracking data on the same foraging task, revealing that value-driven attention and foveated processing together approximate human saccade sizes (4.06° vs 4.05°) and click biases without any human-data supervision. However, VF still lags humans by ~15 points in normalized score and operates in a simplified 16×16 grid world, leaving open whether these findings transfer to naturalistic scenes with continuous spatial layouts. The OOD generalization via value-channel augmentation is promising but tested only on five controlled perturbations, and the cognitive interpretability of the transformer's internal representations remains unexplored.
