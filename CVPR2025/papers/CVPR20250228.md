## Problem

Existing Human Trajectory Prediction methods leverage pose cues only implicitly through data-driven learning, producing trajectories that are physically inconsistent with the observed body pose. No prior work explicitly evaluates whether a predicted trajectory is physically walkable by a humanoid under the laws of physics, a gap that is especially critical in momentary observation settings where only a few frames of past trajectory are available.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250228/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The HTP network consumes past trajectories of the target and surrounding pedestrians, 3D human poses (SMPL-style), and 2D/3D bounding boxes, predicting 12 future frames at 2.5 fps. The LocoVal function, which serves as the plausibility evaluator, takes only the predicted future trajectory, the initial 3D pose j_0, and the root joint velocity v_root,0 — a deliberately minimal subset of the full humanoid state required by the physics simulator. At inference, the LocoVal filter outputs a binary keep/reject decision per candidate trajectory based on a plausibility threshold λ.

## Method

The Locomotion Embodiment framework operates in two training stages. In stage one, a physics-based locomotion generator (PACER) trained via reinforcement learning in Isaac Gym produces discounted cumulative rewards for pose-trajectory pairs; a differentiable MLP (LocoVal) is then trained to predict these rewards from only the trajectory, initial pose, and root velocity, serving as a differentiable surrogate for the non-differentiable simulator. In stage two, the HTP network (Social-Transmotion) is trained with a combined objective: the standard MSE or minMSE loss for trajectory accuracy plus the EmLoco loss, which negates the LocoVal plausibility score of each predicted trajectory, thereby penalizing physically implausible outputs while preserving stochastic diversity across multiple heads. At inference, the LocoVal filter can be applied in a plug-and-play manner to any pre-trained stochastic HTP network, discarding trajectories whose plausibility falls below a threshold.

## Results

Under the standard 9-frame observation setting, the proposed method achieves ADE 0.97 m and FDE 1.91 m on JTA, and ADE 0.37 m and FDE 0.72 m on JRDB, outperforming the strongest baseline Social-Transmotion (1.11/2.26 on JTA; 0.40/0.76 on JRDB) by 12.6% and 15.5% in ADE/FDE on JTA respectively. In the stochastic multi-head setting (Table 2), the method also improves χ² distances for all four physics primitives (velocity, acceleration, angular velocity, angular acceleration), confirming that predicted trajectories are more physically plausible. With only 2 momentary observation frames, the method still leads on all metrics, e.g., reducing FDE from 5.07 m (Social-Trans, 20 heads) to 4.47 m on JTA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all eight baselines on both JTA and JRDB across ADE and FDE. Against the closest competitor Social-Transmotion [47] (the base architecture), the EmLoco loss yields a 12.6% ADE reduction (1.11→0.97) and 15.5% FDE reduction (2.26→1.91) on JTA, and a 7.5% ADE reduction (0.40→0.37) on JRDB. Compared to EqMotion [63] (1.13/2.39 on JTA) and Trajectron++ [49] (1.18/2.53 on JTA), the gains are even larger, with FDE improvements of 20.1% and 24.9% respectively on JTA. The older LSTM-based baselines (Social-LSTM [2], Directional-LSTM [23]) and generative baselines (Social-GAN [18], Autobots [12]) show substantially larger gaps, with ADE differences of 0.24–0.69 m on JTA. Notably, the JRDB improvements are smaller in absolute terms (0.03 m ADE) because JRDB uses estimated 3D poses rather than ground-truth motion-capture poses, yet the method still achieves the best result, suggesting robustness to pose estimation noise in real-world settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends pose-conditioned HTP methods (Social-Transmotion, HST) that treat 3D poses as an additional input feature, by instead using poses to enforce physical consistency through a differentiable plausibility score. The LocoVal function is trained as a surrogate for the non-differentiable PACER locomotion generator in Isaac Gym, bridging physics-based character control and data-driven trajectory prediction. The EmLoco loss is positioned as a physics-informed regularization term complementing standard MSE/minMSE objectives, and the LocoVal filter repurposes the same plausibility function as a plug-and-play inference-time trajectory selector applicable to any pre-trained HTP network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical safety gap: momentary-observation HTP where only 1–2 frames are available and pose cues become critical, yet prior methods produce physically implausible trajectories (e.g., sharp turns inconsistent with body orientation). The plug-and-play LocoVal filter is particularly impactful because it can improve any existing stochastic HTP network without retraining, and the zero-shot transfer to ETH/UCY (without poses, using only root velocity) suggests the plausibility signal generalizes beyond the training distribution. Key limitations include the dependence on accurate 3D pose estimates (JRDB improvements are smaller than JTA) and the assumption that daily pedestrian locomotion is the relevant plausibility target, leaving open how to handle infeasible-but-observed trajectories or pose uncertainty.
