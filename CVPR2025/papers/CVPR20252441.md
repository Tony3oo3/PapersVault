## Problem

One-Shot Federated Learning (OSFL) requires only a single round of client-server communication, but existing methods struggle with feature space heterogeneity (non-identically distributed client data) and severe data scarcity per client. Directly applying a pretrained Latent Diffusion Model to synthesize client-specific training data produces significant distribution shifts, particularly in underrepresented domains like medical or satellite imaging, leading to degraded classification performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252441/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client locally encodes its 16-shot-per-class images into noised latent vectors z(T) via the LDM's VAE encoder (with same-class interpolation for privacy) and optimizes small concept vectors (domain S ∈ R^{n_s×d_w}, category V ∈ R^{C×n_v×d_w}) through 50 epochs of prompt-level fine-tuning. The client uploads only these compact vectors—not raw images or model weights—to the server, which then generates synthetic images (2×, 5×, or 10× the original dataset size) using the frozen pretrained Stable Diffusion v1.4 pipeline. The final output is a ResNet-18 classifier trained on the aggregated synthetic set, producing C-way class labels.

## Method

FedBiP personalizes a pretrained LDM at two complementary levels without modifying its weights. At the instance level, each client computes z(T) by VAE-encoding a local image, interpolating it with a same-class neighbor (γ ~ N(0.5, 0.1²)), and applying T-steps of forward diffusion noise—requiring no gradient optimization. At the concept level, the client optimizes a domain concept vector S and per-category vectors V by minimizing the standard LDM denoising loss with a templated prompt (e.g., 'A [S] style of a [V_y]'), effectively fine-tuning only the textual conditioning. The server asynchronously integrates all clients' z(T) vectors and concept vectors (with a small Gaussian perturbation added to S for diversity), feeds them into the frozen LDM to generate synthetic images, and trains the target classifier via cross-entropy loss. This bi-level design simultaneously addresses fidelity to client distributions (instance) and generation diversity (concept) while transmitting only noised latents and small vectors, preserving privacy.

## Results

On three OSFL benchmarks with feature space heterogeneity (DomainNet, PACS, OfficeHome), FedBiP-L achieves average accuracies of 67.82%, 62.67%, and 61.52% respectively, outperforming the strongest baseline FedDEO (62.52%, 59.78%, 56.96%) by up to 5.96% on DomainNet. On real-world medical (DermaMNIST) and satellite (UCM) datasets with label space heterogeneity, FedBiP-L reaches 56.10% and 65.59% under IID splits, surpassing FedAvg by 4.16% and 1.77% respectively. Notably, the lightweight FedBiP-S variant (generating only 2× local data) matches FGL's performance while FGL requires 3500 synthetic images per class per domain versus FedBiP-S's 16.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedBiP-L outperforms all five baselines across all three feature-space-heterogeneity benchmarks. Against FedAvg (multi-round, not OSFL-compliant), FedBiP-L gains +6.33% on DomainNet, +3.04% on PACS, and +1.52% on OfficeHome. Against the closest OSFL competitor FedDEO (description-enhanced diffusion), FedBiP-L leads by +5.30% on DomainNet, +2.89% on PACS, and +4.56% on OfficeHome. FGL (caption-guided LDM) is the strongest single-generation baseline at 61.69% on DomainNet, but FedBiP-L surpasses it by 6.13%; notably, FedBiP-S matches FGL's DomainNet performance (61.51% vs 61.69%) while generating only 16 images per class per domain versus FGL's 3500. DENSE and FedD3 (model/distillation-based) trail significantly, with FedBiP-L exceeding them by 9.56% and 11.56% on DomainNet respectively. On real-world DermaMNIST and UCM, FedBiP-L consistently leads all baselines under both IID and Dirichlet (β=0.5, 0.01) splits, with the largest margin over FGL (+15.28% on DermaMNIST IID), confirming that bi-level personalization is critical in domains poorly represented in LDM pretraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the One-Shot Federated Learning (OSFL) line of work, which previously relied on transmitting model weights (DENSE), distilled datasets (FedD3), or text captions extracted by vision-language models (FGL, FedDEO). It departs from these approaches by repurposing a pretrained Latent Diffusion Model as a server-side generator, but critically personalizes it at two levels: instance-level (noised VAE latents from client data) and concept-level (learned domain and category prompt vectors). The bi-level design is novel relative to prior diffusion-based OSFL methods, which only optimized textual descriptions without anchoring generation to client-specific latent distributions, and relative to centralized data-augmentation-with-diffusion work that assumes a single homogeneous data source.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedBiP is the first OSFL framework to explicitly address feature space heterogeneity via generative model personalization, and it validates on challenging real-world domains (dermatology, satellite imagery) where LDM pretraining data is sparse. A notable limitation is that personalization can reduce generation diversity in domains that overlap heavily with LDM's pretraining corpus (e.g., 'Real' in DomainNet), suggesting a trade-off between fidelity to client distribution and generative breadth. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video or 3D medical imaging, whether categorical prototypes (mentioned as future work) can further strengthen privacy guarantees, and how the method scales when the number of categories or domains grows substantially.
