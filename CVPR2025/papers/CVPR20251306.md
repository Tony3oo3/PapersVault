## Problem

Existing semantic segmentation datasets (COCO-Stuff, ADE20K) operate below 1000×1000 resolution with coarse boundary annotations, while high-resolution applications like bokeh imaging, AR/VR, and image editing demand matting-level mask precision. Binary segmentation datasets (DIS, matting benchmarks) achieve fine boundaries but are class-agnostic, leaving a gap: no dataset combines 4K resolution, multi-class semantic categories, and matting-level annotation quality simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251306/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes full 4K-resolution RGB images (average diagonal ~5144 px) at test time, using 1024×1024 crops during training. It produces a 7-class semantic segmentation mask (human, vegetation, ground, sky, water, building, others) at native resolution, where 'others' is not a background class but a collection of precisely segmented unnamed objects. An auxiliary edge prediction map (1-channel) is also generated to supervise boundary-aware feature learning.

## Method

MaSSFormer builds on Mask2Former's encoder–decoder structure but replaces the standard FPN pixel decoder with a dual-branch design. The Global Semantic Branch uses a Cross Semantic Transmission (CST) module that applies cross-attention to transfer detail from S3 into a globally-pooled D4 feature, followed by windowed self-attention and deformable convolution, then a Receptive Field Broaden (RFB) module with four parallel deformable convolutions (kernels 1, 3, 5, 7) to capture multi-scale structure. The Local Structure Branch (LSE) upsamples S1 to H/2×W/2, concatenates with the downsampled input image, and applies spatial attention to extract edge-aware low-level features. An Edge Guided Fusion (EGF) module predicts an edge map from the fused features, uses it to re-weight the semantic branch output via sigmoid gating, and produces the final high-resolution feature D1. Training uses a weighted BCE loss that up-weights edge pixels (via morphological difference of ground-truth masks), plus Dice loss, classification loss, and an auxiliary edge-detection BCE loss. For novel classes, pseudo-labels from SAM/Grounded-SAM are mixed with MaSS13K's precise labels under a label re-weighting scheme that suppresses unreliable pseudo-label edges while preserving edge supervision from ground-truth annotations.

## Results

On the MaSS13K validation set (500 images at 4K resolution), MaSSFormer with ResNet-50 backbone achieves the best mIoU of 88.97%, BIoU of 48.97%, and boundary F1 of 0.5639, outperforming the second-best Mask2Former by +0.69% mIoU and +1.57% BIoU while using fewer parameters (37.42M vs 44.01M) and lower FLOPs (2036G vs 3123G). The lightweight MaSSFormer-Lite variant with ResNet-18 backbone achieves 87.11% mIoU and 45.35% BIoU with only 15.07M parameters and 771G FLOPs, surpassing several ResNet-50-based competitors such as DeepLabv3+ (86.66% mIoU) and MaskFormer (83.27% mIoU). On the test set (1,500 images), MaSSFormer maintains its lead with 88.21% mIoU and 48.39% BIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaSSFormer outperforms all 14 baselines on mIoU, BIoU, and BF1 simultaneously. Against the strongest Transformer-based competitor Mask2Former (88.28% mIoU, 47.40% BIoU), MaSSFormer gains +0.69% mIoU and +1.57% BIoU while reducing FLOPs from 3123G to 2036G. Compared to MPFormer (87.76% mIoU, 47.81% BIoU), the improvement is +1.21% mIoU and +1.16% BIoU with roughly half the FLOPs (2036G vs 4155G). The boundary-quality gap is most pronounced against CNN-based methods: SegNeXt achieves similar mIoU (87.71%) but lags by 9.04% in BIoU, and OCRNet trails by 16.29% in BIoU despite comparable mIoU. The lightweight MaSSFormer-Lite (R18, 15.07M params, 771G FLOPs) surpasses DeepLabv3+ (86.66% mIoU, 8008G FLOPs), UperNet (82.03% mIoU, 11373G FLOPs), MaskFormer (83.27% mIoU, 2396G FLOPs), and PEM (83.41% mIoU, 1859G FLOPs) on mIoU, demonstrating that the architectural design compensates for the smaller backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

MaSSFormer is architecturally built on Mask2Former's transformer-decoder framework, but replaces the standard FPN-based pixel decoder with a purpose-built high-resolution decoder comprising four novel modules (CST, RFB, LSE, EGF) that explicitly separate global semantic aggregation from local edge-aware detail extraction. Conceptually, the paper positions itself at the intersection of two previously disjoint dataset lineages: multi-class semantic segmentation datasets (COCO-Stuff, ADE20K, EntitySeg) that cover full scenes but suffer from low resolution and coarse boundaries, and high-quality binary segmentation/matting datasets (DIS, BIG) that achieve matting-level precision but are class-agnostic. The novel-class transfer pipeline, which combines precise MaSS13K annotations with pseudo-labels from pre-trained models under a label re-weighting scheme, extends the standard supervised training paradigm without requiring additional human annotation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4K-resolution, matting-level MaSS13K dataset fills a critical gap for practical applications in mobile photography (bokeh), AR/VR, and image editing where boundary precision directly affects user experience, and its 20–50× higher mask complexity (mIPQ) compared to existing semantic segmentation benchmarks makes it a substantially harder evaluation target. The comprehensive 14-method benchmark reveals that current SOTA models achieve acceptable mIoU but degrade significantly on boundary quality at high resolution, highlighting that mIoU alone is an insufficient metric for this regime. Open questions include whether the 7-class taxonomy (with the ambiguous 'others' category) limits generalization, whether the pseudo-label transfer pipeline scales to many new classes simultaneously, and whether the edge-guided fusion strategy generalizes to other high-resolution tasks beyond semantic segmentation.
