## Problem

Conventional frame-based active stereo cameras are limited to 30–90 FPS, making them ineffective for high-speed depth sensing where motion blur degrades depth accuracy. While event cameras offer microsecond-level temporal resolution, existing event-based stereo systems are passive and fail in texture-less or low-light regions, and no prior work combines binocular event cameras with infrared structured light. Furthermore, state-of-the-art event-based stereo matching networks are computationally heavy, contradicting the low-latency, energy-efficient goals of neuromorphic sensing.

## Input / Output

The method consumes stereo event streams from two DAVIS346 cameras (346×260) illuminated by an 850 nm infrared 2D pattern projector; events are binned into event images (temporal bins) as the network input. The output is a dense disparity map with maximum disparity 192, accompanied by a void mask for visualization of unreliable regions. The full pipeline achieves up to 150 FPS inference on an NVIDIA 3090 GPU, with a runtime of 6.5 ms per frame for the network itself.

## Method

ActiveEventNet is a lightweight CNN that replaces standard convolutions with MobileNet v2 inverted-residual blocks (depthwise + pointwise convolutions) in both the feature-extraction backbone and the encoder-decoder, reducing inference time by ~6× with only a 0.025 EPE increase. The key architectural novelty is a dynamic-interaction 3D cost volume: instead of simple concatenation or correlation, channels whose batch-normalization scaling factor γ falls below a threshold θ_γ are replaced by the corresponding channel from the opposite view, and the resulting interacted left and right feature maps are concatenated along the channel dimension to form the cost volume. This mechanism adaptively exchanges stereo information where one view is less informative, improving matching accuracy at comparable computational cost. The encoder-decoder then regresses the dense disparity map, and the network is trained end-to-end with an L1 loss for 50 epochs using Adam (lr=1e-3) on the proposed Active-Event-Stereo and RealSense-Event-Sim datasets.

## Results

On the real-world Active-Event-Stereo dataset, ActiveEventNet achieves an EPE of 1.995, RMSE of 7.821, and D1-all of 0.083 with only 2.23M parameters and a 6.5 ms inference time. This represents a 12× speedup over the best-accuracy baseline UniMatch (EPE 1.902, 87.6 ms) while remaining within 0.09 EPE. On the synthetic RealSense-Event-Sim dataset, ActiveEventNet reaches EPE 1.223 and D1-all 0.070 in 21.9 ms, again outperforming all frame-based baselines (PSMNet, DeepPruner-Fast, AANet) on both accuracy and speed. The method also outperforms passive RGB-frame stereo by 0.243 EPE and 0.076 D1-all on average, with particularly large gains in low-light sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ActiveEventNet outperforms all five frame-based baselines (SGM, PSMNet, DeepPruner-Fast, AANet, DDES) on every accuracy metric while also being the fastest (6.5 ms vs. 15.6–87.6 ms) and smallest (2.23 M params). Against the strongest baseline UniMatch (Transformer-based, EPE 1.902), ActiveEventNet trades a small 0.093 EPE increase for a 13.5× reduction in inference time (6.5 ms vs. 87.6 ms) and fewer parameters (2.23 M vs. 4.70 M). Compared to the event-based DDES baseline, ActiveEventNet improves EPE by 0.648 (2.643→1.995) and D1-all by 0.039 while running 3× faster. On the synthetic RealSense-Event-Sim dataset, the same pattern holds: ActiveEventNet (EPE 1.223, 21.9 ms) beats AANet (1.397, 28.5 ms), PSMNet (2.786, 20.6 ms), and DDES (1.696, 36.4 ms) on both accuracy and speed, though UniMatch still leads on raw EPE (1.108) at 14× the runtime. The paper also shows that incorporating structured light reduces EPE from 2.614 to 1.993 and D1-all from 0.197 to 0.083 on static/slow-motion sequences, confirming the benefit of the active illumination component.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by combining two previously separate lines of work: passive event-based stereo matching (DDES, EDS, etc.) and monocular event-based structured light (Muglikar, Huang, et al.), into a unified binocular active event-based stereo system. No prior work had integrated binocular event cameras with an infrared pattern projector for stereo depth estimation. Methodologically, ActiveEventNet repurposes MobileNet v2 depthwise-separable convolutions from mobile vision into the event-based stereo matching pipeline and introduces a dynamic channel-exchange cost volume, departing from the heavier 3D/4D cost volumes used by prior event-based stereo methods that prioritized accuracy over efficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes the first active event-based stereo dataset (real + synthetic) and demonstrates that structured light enables event cameras to generate dynamic events even in static or dark scenes, overcoming a fundamental limitation of passive event-based stereo. The 150 FPS inference on a single GPU and the finding that sparse events with structured light can outperform dense RGB frames suggest a viable path toward high-speed depth sensing for agile robots. However, the system is constrained to 346×260 resolution with 15 Hz ground-truth labels from a single RealSense D455, and the method's generality beyond this specific hardware configuration remains untested. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher-resolution event sensors and whether the structured-light benefit persists in outdoor scenes with strong ambient infrared interference.
