## Problem

Controlling procedural content generation (PCG) to produce a desired 3D shape requires cumbersome manual tuning of dozens of parameters. Existing inverse PCG methods either rely on MCMC sampling that needs hundreds to thousands of expensive forward evaluations, or use neural networks with limited condition types, poor generalization to real-world images, and category-specific designs. The paper addresses the need for an efficient, generalizable inverse PCG method that works from arbitrary image conditions with any procedural generator.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 256×256 condition image (RGB photo or sketch) and a specific procedural generator (e.g., from Infinigen) as input. The image is processed by a frozen DINOv2 ViT-B/14 to extract spatial patch features, which serve as cross-attention conditions. The output is a vector of normalized procedural parameters in [-1,1]^N (where N is the number of generator parameters, typically dozens), which is projected back to original parameter ranges and fed into the generator to produce a watertight 3D mesh. Discrete parameters are canonicalized by uniformly partitioning [-1,1] into equal pieces, one per choice.

## Method

DI-PCG directly treats the procedural generator's parameter vector as the denoising target in a lightweight Diffusion Transformer (12 layers, 6 heads, 192 hidden dim, 7.6M parameters), with DINOv2 image patch features injected via cross-attention at each layer. Training is fully self-contained: 20,000 image-parameter pairs are generated per generator by random parameter sampling, multi-view rendering (3 azimuths × 2 elevations × 2 distances), and augmentation including color jitter, flipping, cropping, binary mask substitution, and Canny edge maps. The model is trained for ~30 GPU hours on a single V100 using standard denoising score-matching (MSE on predicted vs. ground-truth noise). At inference, the diffusion model samples parameters in seconds from a Gaussian prior conditioned on the image, bypassing the iterative search of MCMC. The key architectural choice is that the parameter vector is compact (dozens of tokens), enabling a tiny generator-specific model rather than a large general-purpose 3D generator.

## Results

On the DI-PCG test split (procedurally generated chairs), DI-PCG achieves CD=0.033, EMD=0.028, and F-Score=0.896, substantially outperforming all baselines (next best: 3DShape2VecSet* at F-Score 0.426). On the external ShapeNet chair subset (218 models), DI-PCG achieves CD=0.093, EMD=0.108, and F-Score=0.452, competitive with strong reconstruction-based methods like InstantMesh (F-Score 0.473) but trailing the category-specific 3DShape2VecSet* trained directly on ShapeNet chairs (F-Score 0.593). The method requires only 7.6M parameters and ~30 GPU hours to train per generator, with inference in seconds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the DI-PCG test split, DI-PCG dominates all eight baselines by a wide margin, achieving F-Score 0.896 versus 0.426 for the next-best 3DShape2VecSet* and 0.416 for InstantMesh, reflecting its ability to precisely recover the ground-truth procedural parameters. On the external ShapeNet chair subset, DI-PCG (F-Score 0.452) beats all 3D-native diffusion methods (Shap-E 0.285, SDFusion 0.178, Michelangelo 0.407, CraftsMan 0.280, LION 0.257) and IM-NET (0.439), but is slightly behind InstantMesh (0.473) and notably behind 3DShape2VecSet* (0.593), which was specifically retrained on ShapeNet chairs. In terms of Chamfer Distance on ShapeNet, DI-PCG (0.093) is nearly on par with InstantMesh (0.095) and Michelangelo (0.111), while 3DShape2VecSet* achieves the best CD (0.070). The paper also compares against MCMC (Metropolis-Hastings with DINOv2 scoring) in an ablation: DI-PCG achieves F-Score 0.452 in seconds versus MCMC's 0.236 after hours of sampling, and random parameter sampling at 0.155. Overall, DI-PCG's advantage is most pronounced on in-domain data and in efficiency, while general-purpose reconstruction models retain an edge on out-of-domain ShapeNet chairs.

## Relation to Prior Work

DI-PCG builds on the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture for its denoising backbone and borrows DINOv2 visual foundation model features as image conditions, but departs fundamentally from both prior inverse PCG methods and 3D diffusion models. Compared to MCMC-based inverse PCG (which requires thousands of forward evaluations) and feedforward neural-guided procedural models (limited to sketches or specific categories), DI-PCG treats procedural parameters as the denoising target of a diffusion model, learning the full posterior distribution in a self-contained manner. Compared to 3D-native diffusion models (Shap-E, SDFusion, Michelangelo, CLAY) that model geometry directly in point clouds, voxels, or latent shapes, DI-PCG instead models the *construction rules* of a parametric generator, yielding inherently editable and mesh-clean outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DI-PCG demonstrates that a tiny 7.6M-parameter model trained for 30 GPU hours can outperform billion-parameter 3D generation models on category-specific tasks, suggesting that modeling *how to build* an asset via procedural rules is a viable and efficient alternative to modeling geometry directly. The explicit parameter representation enables trivial editing (adjusting leg height, back type) that is impossible with implicit or mesh-based 3D representations. Key limitations include strict dependence on available procedural generators (no out-of-domain generation), support for image conditions only (no text), and demonstration only at the object level rather than scenes. Open questions include whether this paradigm scales to scene-level procedural generation, whether text conditions can be integrated, and whether procedural generators themselves can be automatically designed by LLMs to close the loop.
