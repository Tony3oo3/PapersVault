## Problem

Existing diffusion-based dexterous grasp generators produce suboptimal poses with hand-object penetration or insufficient contact because they lack explicit physical constraints during both training and sampling. Additionally, available dexterous grasping datasets suffer from narrow distributions, limited object categories, and scalability issues, hindering the development of truly universal grasp generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250509/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D object point cloud (downsampled to 2048 points) and a text object label as input. The point cloud is encoded by a Point Transformer, while the label prompts an LLM to generate a shape description that is encoded by BERT-large-uncased; the two feature vectors are concatenated and injected into the diffusion backbone via cross-attention. The output is a diverse set of ShadowHand grasping poses h = (θ, R, t) ∈ R³³, where θ ∈ R²⁴ encodes 24 joint articulations, R ∈ SO(3) is the global rotation, and t ∈ R³ is the global translation, sampled from the conditional distribution P(h|O).

## Method

DexGrasp Anything integrates three tailored physical constraints—Surface Pulling Force (pulling inner finger surfaces toward the object), External-penetration Repulsion Force (preventing hand-object intersection via signed distances), and Self-Penetration Repulsion Force (enforcing minimum inter-finger distances)—into a UNet-based diffusion model at two stages. During training, a Physics-Aware Training objective adds weighted physical-constraint losses computed on the estimated clean sample ĥ₀ to the standard MSE loss, propagating gradients back through the diffusion corruption. During sampling, a Physics-Guided Sampler applies a classifier-guidance-style offset to the posterior mean at each denoising step, using a Spherical Gaussian Constraint with weighted gradient direction to reduce estimation bias and iteratively refine the grasp toward a physically feasible configuration. An LLM-enhanced representation module supplements geometric features with semantic priors extracted from LLM-generated object descriptions, and the authors release a 3.4M-pose, 15k-object dataset built via a model-in-the-loop generation-and-filtering pipeline over Objaverse meshes.

## Results

DexGrasp Anything (w/ LLM) achieves state-of-the-art performance across five dexterous grasping benchmarks. On DexGraspNet, it reaches 57.5% six-direction success rate (Suc.6), 90.6% single-direction success (Suc.1), 17.8 mm maximum penetration, and 0.23 diversity—surpassing the previous best (UGG: 46.9% Suc.6). On MultiDex, it attains 79.1% Suc.6 and 98.1% Suc.1, and on RealDex it achieves 44.8% Suc.6 with 27.7 mm penetration. The diversity metric (Div.) is the highest or near-highest across all five datasets, confirming that physics-aware constraints improve both quality and pose variety simultaneously.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DexGrasp Anything (w/ LLM) outperforms all four baselines on the majority of metrics across all five datasets. Against UGG [21] (the strongest prior method), it gains +10.6 points on DexGraspNet Suc.6 (57.5 vs 46.9), +23.8 on MultiDex Suc.6 (79.1 vs 55.3), and +12.1 on RealDex Suc.6 (44.8 vs 32.7), while also reducing penetration by 7–11 mm on most benchmarks. The only metric where a baseline edges out Ours (w/ LLM) is MultiDex penetration, where UGG achieves 10.3 mm vs. 11.4 mm; however, Ours without LLM achieves 9.6 mm there. SceneDiffuser [10] shows higher diversity on MultiDex (0.27 vs 0.22) but is substantially worse on success rates (69.8 vs 79.1 Suc.6). Against UniDexGrasp [47] and GraspTTA [11], the gap is even larger—Ours (w/ LLM) exceeds them by 20–40 points on Suc.6 across all datasets, confirming that physics-aware diffusion training and sampling yield a clear and consistent advantage over both regression-based and earlier generative approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on diffusion-based dexterous grasping generators (SceneDiffuser, UGG, DexDiffuser) that model grasp pose distributions via iterative denoising, and on physics-aware constraints previously used only as post-hoc filters or training regularizers in UniDexGrasp and SceneDiffuser. The key departure is integrating three physical constraints (surface pulling, external-penetration repulsion, self-penetration repulsion) into both the training objective (via the estimated clean sample) and the sampling process (via a physics-guided sampler with spherical Gaussian constraint), rather than treating them as separate pipeline stages. An additional novelty is the LLM-enhanced object representation, where BERT-encoded LLM descriptions of object shape and category are concatenated with Point Transformer geometric features, and the 'model-in-the-loop' data generation strategy inspired by SAM for dataset scaling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3.4M-pose, 15K-object DGA dataset is the largest dexterous grasping dataset to date, and cross-dataset experiments show it substantially improves the diversity and success rates of multiple existing methods (SceneDiffuser, UGG, GraspTTA), making it a practically valuable community resource. The physics-aware training-plus-sampling framework is a generalizable recipe that could be adapted to other diffusion-based manipulation tasks. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other hand morphologies beyond ShadowHand, whether the model-in-the-loop generation introduces subtle distributional biases that limit true universality, and how the method performs in real-time closed-loop settings with dynamic or deformable objects.
