## Problem

Grounding abstract, high-level instructions (e.g., 'prepare for dinner') to 3D scenes remains unsolved because the task breakdown is environment-dependent: the correct subtasks depend on what objects and tools are present, while the scene representation needed to support execution depends on the task hierarchy itself. Prior work either requires instructions to explicitly name objects, relies on closed-set semantic labels, or sidesteps the grounding problem entirely, leaving no framework that jointly produces a grounded task hierarchy and a task-aligned 3D scene graph.

## Input / Output

ASHiTA consumes RGB-D image streams from a mobile robot (segmented via EfficientViT and encoded with MobileCLIP), camera poses from RTABMap, and one or more high-level natural-language tasks. It produces a hierarchical task decomposition (task → subtasks → items) where each entity is grounded to a 3D scene graph node carrying a bounding box, centroid, and spatial radius. The scene graph is organized in layers mirroring the task hierarchy, with the bottom primitives layer consisting of class-agnostic 3D bounding boxes with visual feature embeddings, and inter-layer edges encoding soft parent-child assignments.

## Method

ASHiTA alternates two steps in an iterative loop: a bottom-up Scene Hierarchy Update that builds a 3D scene graph from the primitives layer using a novel Hierarchical Information Bottleneck (H-IB) algorithm, and a top-down Task Update that refines the task hierarchy via LLM queries informed by the grounded scene graph. H-IB generalizes the standard Information Bottleneck to multi-resolution tasks by introducing a chain of compressed representations S₁…Sₙ corresponding to task-hierarchy levels, solved via iterative multi-layer update steps under Markov chain assumptions, with a weighted KL-divergence objective across all task levels. The scene graph is constructed bottom-up (primitives → items → subtasks → tasks) using argmax of H-IB conditional probabilities, then pruned top-down by confidence to remove irrelevant nodes. The Task Update grounds task entities spatially (centroid and radius from aligned scene graph nodes) and queries GPT-4o-mini to incorporate pruned primitives as new items or subtasks, with spatially-informed conditional probabilities fed back into the next H-IB round.

## Results

On the Hierarchical Task Analysis benchmark (205 high-level tasks from SG3D/HM3DSem), ASHiTA achieves 10.39% subtask recall, 20.6% subtask precision, and 9.27% task accuracy, substantially outperforming Hydra+GPT (9.43%/15.51%/4.88%) and HOV-SG+GPT (4.55%/4.87%/1.95%). With ground-truth positions, ASHiTA(GTPos) improves to 15.12%/34.47%/16.59%, and with both GT positions and text embeddings reaches 38.71%/34.39%/36.1%. For grounding evaluation with GT 3D instance segmentation (Table 1), ASHiTA achieves 28.71% subtask-accuracy and 12.13% task-accuracy, surpassing zero-shot baselines 3D-VisTA (25.3%/10.3%) and PQ3D (24.4%/9.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASHiTA outperforms all baselines on the primary Hierarchical Task Analysis task. Against Hydra+GPT, ASHiTA gains +0.96% subtask recall, +5.09% subtask precision, and +4.39% task accuracy, with the largest gap in task accuracy (9.27% vs. 4.88%), indicating ASHiTA produces fewer incorrect subtasks. Against HOV-SG+GPT, the margin is even larger: ASHiTA nearly doubles subtask recall (10.39% vs. 4.55%) and quadruples task accuracy (9.27% vs. 1.95%), as HOV-SG's rigid hierarchical query format is poorly suited to parsing free-form high-level tasks. In the grounding evaluation (Table 1), ASHiTA (28.71% s-acc) beats both 3D-VisTA (25.3%) and PQ3D (24.4%) by 3–4 points, and in the incremental scene-graph setting (Table 2), ASHiTA (21.7% s-acc) more than doubles Hydra+GPT (8.18%) and HOV-SG (8.98%), even surpassing Hydra with ground-truth segmentation (14.2%). The GPT-powered baselines tend to generate many contextually relevant but redundant subtasks, hurting precision, while ASHiTA's information-bottleneck-based pruning yields more focused task decompositions.

## Relation to Prior Work

ASHiTA extends the Information Bottleneck (IB) principle used in Clio [30] for task-driven 3D scene graph construction into a hierarchical generalization (H-IB) that compresses primitives across multiple abstraction levels corresponding to a task hierarchy. It departs from prior scene graph approaches (Hydra, HOV-SG, ConceptGraph) that rely on closed-set labels or manually defined hierarchical layers, and from LLM-based task planners (SayCan, LLM+P, AutoTAMP) that sidestep the visual grounding problem entirely. The key conceptual departure is the iterative coupling: the task hierarchy informs the scene graph construction and the scene graph in turn refines the task hierarchy, rather than treating decomposition and grounding as sequential or independent steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to automatically co-determine a task decomposition and its spatial grounding in a 3D scene, addressing a gap where prior methods either decompose tasks without scene awareness or ground simple instructions without hierarchical reasoning. However, absolute performance remains low (single-digit to low-double-digit accuracy on most metrics), and the lack of spatial relations in the scene graph, the word-bank-limited granularity, and the tree structure preventing object sharing across subtasks are significant open limitations. Future work incorporating classical planning verification, relational edges, and richer open-vocabulary representations could substantially improve the framework's practical utility for embodied agents.
