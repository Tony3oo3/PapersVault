## Problem

Existing visual hallucination (VH) mitigation methods for large vision-language models (LVLMs) rely on secondary decoding, rollback mechanisms, or external tools, which substantially reduce inference speed. While attention in LVLM decoders is empirically sparse, naively applying vision-agnostic token pruning worsens hallucinations because low-attention image tokens containing crucial visual information are preferentially discarded. The paper addresses how to exploit token sparsity for efficient VH mitigation while preserving visual fidelity, without additional training or post-processing.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a plug-and-play decoding strategy on top of pretrained LVLMs (LLaVA-1.5, MiniGPT-4, mPLUG-Owl2), taking an RGB image and a text prompt (e.g., 'Please describe this image in detail') and producing an autoregressively generated text description. It modifies attention scores and output logits at each decoding step without altering model weights. The sparsity rate is set to 0.9L (retaining 90% of tokens), the image masking rate for contrastive decoding is 0.5, and decoding uses beam search with beam size 3 (L_max=64) or 2 (L_max=512).

## Method

VASparse unifies token sparsification and visual awareness into a constrained optimization problem (Def. 1) that minimizes attention recall error subject to a sparsity budget and visual saliency constraint, and proves the resulting greedy selection strategy is globally optimal (Theorem 1). The visual-aware token selection scores each token by δ_i = (⟨q, K_i⟩)² + λP_i, where P_i is a visual saliency score computed from the last attention head's weights toward image tokens; pruned tokens are then aggregated via k-nearest-neighbor density peak clustering. A sparse-based visual contrastive decoding (SVCD) step contrasts the logit distribution from visual-aware sparsification against one obtained by feeding randomly masked visual token embeddings directly into the LLM's language head (bypassing the full decoder), thereby avoiding the secondary decoding overhead of prior contrastive methods. Finally, a sinking attention penalty applies softmax-normalized cumulative attention weights to penalize low-semantic tokens (e.g., <.>, <s>) that attract disproportionate attention, recalibrating the attention scores as (1+β)qKᵀ − βW⊙qKᵀ during decoding.

## Results

On the CHAIR benchmark (MSCOCO), VASparse achieves the lowest instance-level hallucination rate (CHAIR_i) across all three LVLM families: 5.82 on LLaVA-1.5, 11.35 on MiniGPT-4, and 7.36 on mPLUG-Owl2, outperforming the best decoding baseline HALC (6.27, 11.69, 7.71) and post-hoc methods (Woodpecker: 6.72, 12.09, 8.99). VASparse also attains the lowest sentence-level CHAIR_s (18.51, 30.19, 22.03) while maintaining competitive decoding speed (27.73, 30.87, 18.18 TPS), which is 12.9× faster than HALC (2.15 TPS) and 6.4× faster than OPERA (4.36 TPS) on LLaVA-1.5. On the offline POPE benchmark, VASparse achieves the highest F1-score in most settings (e.g., 62.13 Random / 60.93 Popular / 59.20 Adversarial on LLaVA-1.5), and on MME it leads in all object- and attribute-level sub-metrics (e.g., 180.00 Existence, 132.67 Count on LLaVA-1.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VASparse outperforms all nine baselines on CHAIR_i and CHAIR_s across all three LVLM families. Against the strongest decoding baseline HALC, VASparse reduces CHAIR_i by 0.45 (LLaVA-1.5), 0.34 (MiniGPT-4), and 0.35 (mPLUG-Owl2) while being 12.9×, 8.0×, and 12.0× faster in decoding speed, respectively. Compared to OPERA, VASparse improves CHAIR_i by 1.22 on LLaVA-1.5 (5.82 vs. 7.04) and 1.71 on mPLUG-Owl2 (7.36 vs. 9.07), with 6.4× and 5.1× speedups. Against VCD and DoLa, VASparse achieves lower CHAIR_i (5.82 vs. 7.02/6.44 on LLaVA-1.5) at comparable or higher TPS (27.73 vs. 17.58/23.61). The post-hoc methods Woodpecker and LURE achieve competitive CHAIR_i (6.72/6.67 on LLaVA-1.5) but require external LLMs (ChatGPT/GPT-4) and report no TPS. Vision-agnostic sparsity baselines FastV and SparseVLM substantially worsen hallucination (CHAIR_i 8.53/8.44 vs. 5.82 for VASparse on LLaVA-1.5), confirming that naive token pruning without visual awareness is counterproductive.

## Relation to Prior Work

VASparse carves new ground by unifying two previously separate threads—token sparsification for efficient inference (FastV, SparseVLM) and contrastive decoding for hallucination mitigation (VCD, HALC, OPERA)—into a single constrained optimization framework. It departs from prior contrastive decoding methods by computing the 'negative' logit distribution from raw embeddings fed directly to the language head rather than running a full secondary decode, and it extends the attention-sinking phenomenon documented in LLMs (Xiao et al., 2024) to LVLMs, showing that sink tokens concentrate on low-semantic text tokens even when vastly outnumbered by image tokens. The paper also explicitly demonstrates that vision-agnostic sparsification, previously studied only as an efficiency tool, actively exacerbates hallucination, reframing token pruning as a fidelity-sensitive operation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners deploying LVLMs in latency-sensitive or resource-constrained settings, VASparse offers a plug-and-play decoding strategy that simultaneously reduces hallucination and accelerates inference (up to 12.9× faster than HALC) without any retraining or external models, making it immediately applicable to existing LLaVA-1.5, MiniGPT-4, and mPLUG-Owl2 checkpoints. Open questions include whether the fixed hyperparameters (λ=0.1, α=0.1, β=0.1, sparsity rate 0.9L) transfer to substantially different architectures (e.g., InternVL, Qwen-VL) or to video LVLMs where temporal attention patterns differ. The theoretical optimality guarantee (Theorem 1) is limited to the specific quadratic objective and may not extend to more complex attention formulations in newer model families.
