## Problem

Existing knowledge distillation methods for vision-language models either target single-modal LLMs or merely use teachers to generate training data, leaving the most critical aspect of VLMs—cross-modal alignment—undistilled. As VLMs are compressed for mobile deployment, performance degrades sharply, and no prior method transfers the alignment knowledge that enables a small model to properly fuse vision and text embeddings in a shared high-dimensional space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20251696/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The student model (MobileVLM V2 1.7B) receives an RGB image and a text prompt, producing a text response, while the teacher (MobileVLM V2 7B) processes the same inputs to supply distillation signals. Training is conducted on two subsets differing in maximum prompt length (512 vs. 2048 embedded tokens), with overlong VQA samples dropped rather than truncated, creating resource-limited scenarios. The distillation signals are extracted from the teacher's first-layer attention matrices and vision projector output embeddings, not from intermediate hidden states across all layers.

## Method

Align-KD distills cross-modal alignment knowledge from two complementary sources. First, it applies an MSE loss between the teacher's and student's first-layer text-query-vision attention sub-matrices (A_{1,t-v}), after dimension alignment via a 1×1 convolution, capturing how text tokens attend to vision tokens during initial cross-modal projection. Second, it uses the teacher's A_{1,t-v} to compute per-vision-token attention scores, selects the top-K most-focused tokens, and injects the teacher's vision embeddings for those tokens (weighted by λ=0.1) alongside a lower-weighted all-token vision embedding distillation, preventing suppression of less-attended tokens. A reverse Kullback-Leibler divergence loss on output distributions (following MiniLLM) provides mean-seeking output alignment. The key empirical finding motivating this design is that the first and last Transformer layers exhibit the largest feature shifts, with the first layer specifically responsible for projecting heterogeneous vision and text embeddings into a shared high-dimensional space.

## Results

On the Short subset (max 512 tokens), Align-KD improves MobileVLM V2 1.7B from an average of 62.4 to 64.4 across 6 benchmarks, with the largest gains on GQA (+3.8) and SQA (+3.4). On the Long subset (max 2048 tokens), the average improves from 63.7 to 65.1, with SQA gaining +3.2 and MMBench +1.6. The distilled 1.7B model (65.1 avg, Long) narrows the gap to the 3B MobileVLM V2 (68.1) and approaches the 7B teacher (72.2) while remaining deployable on mobile hardware.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Align-KD consistently improves MobileVLM V2 1.7B over its no-distillation counterpart on all 6 benchmarks under both Short and Long settings, with the most substantial gains on SQA (+3.4 Short, +3.2 Long) and GQA (+3.8 Short, +1.1 Long). Under the resource-constrained Short setting, the distilled 1.7B model (64.4 avg) surpasses MoE-LLaVA-1.6B×4 (63.3) and approaches MoE-LLaVA-2.7B×4 (66.7), despite using a single 1.7B LLM rather than a 4-expert MoE. Under the Long setting, the distilled model (65.1) still trails MobileVLM V2 3B (68.1) by 3.0 points, but the gap narrows from 5.7 (no KD) to 3.0, indicating that distillation recovers a meaningful fraction of the capability lost by shrinking from 3B to 1.7B. The 7B teacher (72.2) remains substantially stronger, confirming that the student–teacher gap is not fully closed but is significantly reduced. Notably, on the hallucination-sensitive POPE benchmark, Align-KD yields a +1.2 improvement (Short) and +0.9 (Long), suggesting the cross-modal alignment knowledge also mitigates object hallucination.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends knowledge distillation from single-modal LLMs (MiniLLM, Hsieh et al.) to the vision-language setting, but departs from prior VLM distillation work (Fang et al. 2021, Wang et al. 2023, LLaVA-MoD) by targeting a specific and previously neglected component: cross-modal alignment at the first transformer layer. Rather than distilling full attention matrices across multiple layers or relying on vision proposals, Align-KD isolates the text-query-vision attention of the first layer as the carrier of alignment knowledge, and pairs it with selective vision-token enhancement guided by the teacher's attention scores. The reverse KLD output loss follows MiniLLM, while the architectural substrate is MobileVLM V2's lightweight vision projector and MobileLLaMA/Vicuna backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Align-KD offers a practical, low-overhead path to improve mobile VLMs without enlarging model size or data volume, which is critical for edge deployment where compute and memory are tightly constrained. The ablation showing that distilling vision-query-vision attention is actively harmful (43.7 vs. 64.4 average) provides useful guidance for future VLM distillation design. However, the evaluation is limited to the MobileVLM V2 family (1.7B student, 7B teacher) and two prompt-length subsets, leaving open whether the first-layer alignment hypothesis and the text-query-vision-only strategy generalize to other VLM architectures with different projector designs or deeper LLM backbones. The modest ~2-point average improvement also raises the question of whether further gains require richer alignment signals beyond a single attention sub-matrix.
