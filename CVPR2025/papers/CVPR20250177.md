## Problem

Existing figure skating datasets are designed for single tasks (action recognition, temporal segmentation, or scoring) and lack comprehensive annotations that connect individual technical elements to overall artistic performance quality. Moreover, current sports understanding benchmarks are dominated by ball sports and ignore the artistry dimensions—rhythm, emotional expression, movement fluidity—that are central to evaluating figure skating. This limits the ability of multimodal LLMs to perform multi-task, human-like understanding of artistic sports.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes identity-free motion sequences (extracted via 4DHumans) and skeleton data (via HRNet) to prevent MLLMs from leveraging prior knowledge of athlete identities, alongside original RGB video links and audio commentary transcribed with Whisper. FSBench-Text takes text-only multiple-choice questions (4,200+ on rules and event information), while FSBench-Motion takes multimodal motion/skeleton inputs paired with open-ended QA prompts spanning six task types from single-action recognition to full-performance commentary generation. Outputs range from discrete labels (action class, GOE sign) to structured multi-dimensional scores (TES, PCS across five components) and free-text professional commentary.

## Method

The primary contribution is the FSAnno dataset and FSBench benchmark, constructed from 783 performances across 11 ISU Grand Prix and Junior Grand Prix competitions, with annotations sourced from official judges' reports, video segmentation, and audio commentary. For the model component, SkateLLM is built by instruction-tuning MotionGPT using a two-phase strategy: first pre-training a cross-modal projector to align motion embeddings with LLM text embeddings, then fine-tuning the projector and LLM backbone with LoRA for one epoch to mitigate catastrophic forgetting. Instruction-tuning data is generated by GPT-4 using manually crafted templates that encode technical parameters (take-off edge, rotation count, air position, landing control) and condition artistic commentary on GOE scores, blending general motion datasets (Motion-X, KIT) with figure skating-specific captions from FSAnno. Evaluation employs AutoDQ for open-ended description tasks, which extracts key technical events from both ground truth and model output to compute precision and recall, rather than relying on surface-level metrics like BLEU.

## Results

On the figure skating element description task evaluated via AutoDQ, SkateLLM (instruction-tuned on MotionGPT with FSAnno) achieves an F1 of 38.0, Recall of 58.33, and Precision of 61.62, substantially outperforming the un-tuned MotionGPT baseline (F1: 7.1, Recall: 3.77, Precision: 27.46). On the FSBench prior knowledge test, GPT-4 reaches 73.0% on event information and 78.0% on rules, while GPT-3.5-turbo scores 59.0% and 64.8% respectively, and LLaVA-13B lags at 47.0% and 51.4%. The authors note that SkateLLM's higher precision but lower recall reflects difficulty distinguishing elements that differ only by rotation count due to rapid spin speeds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SkateLLM, built by instruction-tuning MotionGPT [15] on FSAnno, dramatically outperforms the un-tuned MotionGPT baseline on all three AutoDQ metrics: F1 improves from 7.1 to 38.0 (5.4×), Recall from 3.77 to 58.33 (15.5×), and Precision from 27.46 to 61.62 (2.2×). This confirms that domain-specific instruction tuning with fine-grained figure skating annotations is critical for motion understanding in artistic sports. On the prior knowledge test (Table 3), GPT-4 [1] leads all tested LLMs (73.0% event info, 78.0% rules, 87.9% quiz), reaching only the 'All-Star Striker' level on a public figure skating quiz, while GPT-3.5-turbo and LLaVA-13B score substantially lower. The paper does not directly compare SkateLLM against GPT-4 on the motion description task, but notes in Figure 4 that GPT-4's generated captions are less focused on technical movement details and artistic evaluation compared to SkateLLM. Overall, the results highlight that even the strongest general-purpose LLMs remain limited in understanding the technical and artistic nuances of figure skating.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the figure skating CV literature (FSD-10, MCFS, FS1000, OlympicFS) by unifying previously isolated single-task datasets into a multi-level, multi-modal framework (FSAnno) that integrates technical scoring, artistic commentary, and action segmentation under one annotation scheme. Methodologically, SkateLLM builds directly on MotionGPT's architecture, applying LoRA-based instruction fine-tuning with domain-specific templates generated via GPT-4. The paper also departs from sports QA benchmarks (SportQA, SPORTU, SportsQA) that focus on ball-sport tactics and strategy, instead targeting the artistic and expressive dimensions of figure skating that those benchmarks ignore.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FSBench is the first benchmark to systematically evaluate LLMs on artistic-sport understanding across multiple levels (prior knowledge → single action → full performance), and its initial results expose a substantial gap: even GPT-4 reaches only 'All-Star Striker' on a figure skating knowledge quiz, and motion-based LLMs score poorly on element description. However, the experimental validation is limited to the motion-captioning subtask, leaving the full benchmark largely untested by existing methods. Open questions include whether a fully trained MLLM on FSAnno can close the artistic-evaluation gap, whether the identity-free motion/skeleton modality generalizes to other artistic sports, and whether the AutoDQ-based evaluation adequately captures subjective artistic quality.
