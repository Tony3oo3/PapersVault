## Problem

Existing retrieval augmentation strategies for few-shot image classification rely on nearest-neighbor similarity scoring, which independently ranks auxiliary samples and thus retrieves highly redundant sets that fail to cover the full diversity of the target task. The paper asks whether incorporating diversity into the data selection objective can improve retrieval effectiveness, and shows that all prior similarity-based methods are in fact instances of a restricted class of Combinatorial Mutual Information (CMI) functions that lack any diversity term.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20250541/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small target dataset (1–16 labeled images per class from benchmarks like ImageNet, Flowers102, FGVC-Aircraft, DTD, Food101) and a large auxiliary image-caption pool (LAION-2B, pre-filtered by string-matching class names in captions). A sparse k-NN similarity matrix is computed via CLIP ViT-B/16 encoders and FAISS, and the retrieval budget is fixed at approximately 16 images per class. The output is the retrieved subset, which is concatenated with the target data to fine-tune a frozen CLIP backbone with a lightweight adapter (Tip-Adapter-F, Clip-Adapter, CaFo, or linear probe).

## Method

COBRA formulates retrieval as maximizing a submodular Combinatorial Mutual Information objective built on the Facility Location function (FLMI), which simultaneously rewards samples that are relevant to the target set and diverse among themselves. The full objective adds a soft class-balancing term (log of per-class counts) and an optional quality score, all weighted by hyperparameters μ and λ. The paper proves that standard Sim-Score and CLIP-Score retrieval reduce to Graph Cut Mutual Information, a degenerate CMI instance with no diversity incentive, motivating FLMI as a strictly richer alternative. Because the objective is submodular and monotone, it is optimized with a greedy algorithm yielding a (1−e^{−1}) approximation guarantee, and the sparse k-NN similarity matrix keeps the one-time retrieval cost to roughly 10 minutes on a single A100 GPU.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k (16-shot, ViT-B/16, Tip-Adapter-F), COBRA achieves 72.78% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline Sim-Score (72.35%) by 0.43 points and CLIP-Score (72.20%) by 0.58 points. Across six target datasets (ImageNet, ImageNet-V2, Flowers102, FGVC-Aircraft, DTD, Food101) and multiple shot levels (1–16), COBRA attains the best average rank in over 90 experimental settings. With a larger ViT-L/14 backbone, COBRA reaches 78.48% on ImageNet versus 78.15% for Sim-Score, confirming gains scale with model capacity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COBRA consistently outperforms all five primary baselines (Sim-Score, CLIP-Score, Random, SDXL-Aug, No Retrieve) across all six target datasets and all few-shot adaptation techniques (Linear Probe, Clip-Adapter, Tip-Adapter-F, CaFo). The closest competitor is Sim-Score (72.35%), which COBRA beats by 0.43 points on ImageNet; adding a soft class-balancing term to Sim-Score narrows the gap to 0.32 points (72.46%) but COBRA still leads. Among diversity-aware baselines, MMR with its best λ (0.50) reaches 72.41%, still 0.37 points below COBRA, while log det-MI (DPP-based CMI) underperforms significantly at 71.28%, likely due to the positive-definiteness constraint on the similarity matrix. Synthetic image generation via SDXL-Aug (72.33%) never surpasses COBRA on any task, and the authors attribute this to unrealistic artifacts and low diversity in generated images. On Flowers102, COBRA and Sim-Score are statistically indistinguishable, which the authors attribute to the dataset's low intra-class diversity diminishing the benefit of diversity-aware retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Combinatorial Mutual Information (CMI) framework from Iyer et al. [38] and the broader submodular optimization literature, repurposing it for retrieval augmentation in vision-language models. The key conceptual contribution is showing that all prior nearest-neighbor retrieval strategies (Sim-Score, CLIP-Score) used in retrieval-augmented few-shot adaptation [58, 94, 100, 37] are special cases of Graph Cut Mutual Information (GCMI), a restricted CMI instantiation that ignores diversity. COBRA departs from this lineage by proposing Facility Location Mutual Information (FLMI) as a richer CMI instantiation that jointly captures relevance and diversity, drawing on the facility location submodular function and connecting to classical information retrieval diversity methods like MMR [15] and DPP-based selection [52].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that diversity-aware retrieval is a meaningful and previously overlooked dimension in retrieval-augmented few-shot adaptation, with the CMI framework providing a principled, efficiently optimizable (greedy with (1-1/e) guarantee) way to incorporate it. The finding that even the strongest available text-to-image model (SDXL) fails to match simple diversity-aware retrieval from real data is a useful cautionary result for the synthetic-data-for-training literature. Open questions include whether the CMI-based retrieval framework extends to generative RAG pipelines, whether gains persist at much larger auxiliary pool scales, and whether the submodular structure can be exploited for streaming or online retrieval settings.
