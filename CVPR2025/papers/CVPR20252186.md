## Problem

Transformer-based 3D instance segmentation methods primarily model external relationships between scene features and query features via mask attention, but neglect internal relationships among scene features (superpoints) and between query features. Superpoint features exhibit insufficient intra-instance consistency and inter-instance differentiation, while conventional self-attention between queries relies on imprecise position embeddings that do not accurately reflect the spatial locations of predicted masks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud where each point carries position (x,y,z), color (r,g,b), and surface normal (nx,ny,nz), voxelized at 0.02 m resolution. It produces per-instance binary masks with semantic class labels and 3D bounding boxes, using 400 instance queries (500 for ScanNet++/ScanNet200) and NMS as a post-processing step on the final decoder layer output.

## Method

Relation3D introduces three modules on top of a Sparse UNet backbone and Maft-style transformer decoder. (1) The Adaptive Superpoint Aggregation Module (ASAM) replaces naive pooling by computing max- and mean-pooled superpoint features, deriving per-point weights via two MLPs on the residual between superpoint and point features, applying softmax within each superpoint, and concatenating the two weighted branches. (2) The Contrastive Learning-guided Superpoint Refinement Module (CLSR) inverts the usual cross-attention direction—superpoint features serve as Q while instance queries serve as K/V—creating a dual-path update, and a BCE contrastive loss between the superpoint similarity matrix and a ground-truth instance-relation matrix enforces intra-instance consistency and inter-instance separation; this refinement runs every 3 decoder layers. (3) Relation-aware Self-Attention (RSA) computes explicit positional (log-normalized center offsets) and geometric (log scale ratios) relationships from each query's predicted bounding box, encodes them with sine-cosine positional encoding, and adds the resulting bias to the attention logits, integrating explicit spatial-geometric priors with implicit content-based attention.

## Results

On ScanNetV2 validation, Relation3D achieves mAP 62.5, AP@50 80.2, and AP@25 87.0, surpassing the strongest transformer-based baseline Maft‡ (mAP 59.9, AP@50 76.5) by +2.6 mAP and +3.7 AP@50. On the ScanNetV2 hidden test set, it reaches mAP 62.2 and AP@25 90.1. The method also leads on ScanNet++ (mAP 28.2 vs. Maft's 23.1), ScanNet200 (mAP 31.6 vs. Maft's 29.2), and S3DIS Area 5 (AP@50 72.5 vs. Spherical Mask's 72.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Relation3D outperforms all 16 compared baselines across all four benchmarks. Among transformer-based methods, it leads Maft (the closest competitor) by +4.1 mAP on ScanNetV2 validation (62.5 vs. 58.4), +5.1 mAP on ScanNet++ (28.2 vs. 23.1), +2.4 mAP on ScanNet200 (31.6 vs. 29.2), and +3.4 AP@50 on S3DIS (72.5 vs. 69.1). Against the strongest non-transformer method, Spherical Mask, Relation3D edges ahead on ScanNetV2 validation mAP (62.5 vs. 62.3) and S3DIS AP@50 (72.5 vs. 72.3), while Spherical Mask slightly leads on ScanNetV2 AP@25 (88.2 vs. 87.0). On the ScanNetV2 hidden test set, Relation3D achieves the best AP@25 of 90.1, exceeding Spherical Mask (87.5) by 2.6 points. The gains are most pronounced on ScanNet++ (+5.1 mAP over Maft), suggesting the relation modeling benefits are amplified in denser, higher-resolution scenes with more instance categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

Relation3D builds directly on Maft (Mask-attention-free transformer) as its architectural baseline, inheriting its Sparse UNet backbone, transformer decoder, and loss formulation, while introducing three targeted modules to address what the authors identify as a gap in prior transformer-based 3D instance segmentation: the lack of explicit internal relationship modeling among scene features and between query features. The adaptive superpoint aggregation module replaces the simple max/mean pooling used in SPFormer and Maft with learned per-point weights, and the relation-aware self-attention draws conceptual inspiration from 2D relation modeling work (Relation-DETR, CORE) by injecting positional and geometric biases into the attention computation. The contrastive learning-guided refinement module introduces a dual-path decoder where superpoint features and instance queries update each other bidirectionally, a design not present in prior 3D instance segmentation transformers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly modeling internal relationships—both among superpoint features (via contrastive supervision) and among query features (via geometric/positional attention biases)—yields consistent and meaningful gains across four diverse benchmarks, suggesting that prior methods underexploited relational structure in 3D scenes. The dual-path query-superpoint interaction and the relation-aware attention mechanism are modular components that could be adopted by other transformer-based 3D segmentation or detection methods. Open questions include whether the contrastive loss, which requires ground-truth instance annotations, can be relaxed to a self-supervised or pseudo-label formulation, and whether the superpoint-based formulation generalizes well to large-scale outdoor or LiDAR scenes where superpoint segmentation quality degrades.
