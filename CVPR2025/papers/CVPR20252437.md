## Problem

Real-time hair simulation for avatars is constrained by the high computational cost and instability of physics-based solvers, while existing learned simulators require large pre-computed simulation datasets that are expensive to generate and store. No prior method provides a self-supervised neural approach to quasi-static hair draping that generalizes across body poses, body shapes, and diverse hairstyles without any simulated training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20252437/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 107-dimensional conditioning vector (16-dim groom latent code from a frozen autoencoder, 10-dim body shape coefficients, and 81-dim skeleton pose parameters) and produces a 64×64×24×3 deformation texture map, where each pixel encodes 3D displacements for 24 vertices along one hair strand. The final drape is the sum of a rigidly posed groom (from the pose-based transformation module) and the predicted deformation field, yielding a quasi-static result in ~2.86 ms per groom on a single GPU.

## Method

Quaffure decomposes hair draping into a rigid pose-based transformation (linear blend skinning of scalp-attached strands) plus a learned deformation decoder (2D CNN) that predicts physics-dependent corrections. The decoder is trained entirely self-supervised with a composite loss comprising a modified Cosserat elastic potential (optimizing position offsets only, with an explicit Hookean stretch term to avoid over-rigidity), gravity, vertex-triangle body collision, SPH-density-based self-collision, and a temporal pose regularizer. The key architectural insight is replacing the full Cosserat rod model (which jointly optimizes position and orientation quaternions) with a position-only formulation that is orders of magnitude faster to train while still preserving curliness and strand shape. A separate autoencoder stage encodes rest-shape grooms into 16-dim latent codes, enabling a single network to handle multiple hairstyles without per-groom fitting.

## Results

Quaffure predicts a single groom in 2.86 ms and 1000 grooms in 0.3 s on an NVIDIA RTX A6000, making it ~60,000× faster than Adam/L-BFGS optimization and ~22,000× faster than GPU XPBD simulation. On quality metrics, the method achieves 0.26% body intersection, 175.42 length preservation error, 286.13 orientation preservation error, and 3399.95 gravity potential — substantially outperforming GroomGen on all four quality metrics while matching its inference speed (~2.9 ms vs ~2.5 ms). Compared to direct energy minimization (Adam, L-BFGS), Quaffure trades a small increase in body intersection (0.26% vs 0.22%) for dramatically better length and orientation preservation (175/286 vs 104/76 and 90/70).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GroomGen [51], the closest neural baseline, Quaffure wins on every quality metric: body intersection drops from 0.39% to 0.26%, length preservation error improves from 1319.74 to 175.42 (7.5×), orientation preservation error improves from 1281.96 to 286.13 (4.5×), and gravity potential decreases from 3889.78 to 3399.95. Inference time is comparable (2.86 ms vs 2.49 ms), and Quaffure additionally generalizes to arbitrary body poses and shapes, whereas GroomGen was only demonstrated for varying gravity on a single fixed pose. Against XPBD [25], the GPU physics simulation achieves the best quality (0.01% intersection, 57.96 length, 18.10 orientation) but at 63.26 s per groom — roughly 22,000× slower than Quaffure. Against direct energy minimization with Adam and L-BFGS, Quaffure is 60,000–100,000× faster and achieves better length and orientation preservation, at the cost of a marginal 0.04% increase in body intersection. The paper positions Quaffure as the best speed–quality trade-off, with the added advantage of requiring no pre-computed simulation data for training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of physics-based self-supervised learning for simulation (previously applied to cloth draping and rigid-body kinematics) to the domain of hair, where prior neural approaches like GroomGen relied on supervised training with expensive pre-computed simulation data. It departs from the full Cosserat rod formulation [21] by proposing a position-only modified Cosserat energy that is far more tractable for neural network training, and repurposes the SPH smoothing kernel from Bando et al. [1] for differentiable self-collision. The key conceptual departure is from supervised learned simulators: by formulating the physics energy directly as a loss, the method eliminates the need for any simulated training data, a first for hair simulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The elimination of simulation data generation is practically significant for the real-time graphics and virtual production pipelines, where producing training data requires expert knowledge, specialized tools, and substantial storage. The method's fixed inference cost (independent of strand count or collision count) and linear scaling to 1000 grooms in 0.3 s make it viable for multi-avatar telepresence and large-scale character generation. Open questions include conditioning on per-groom material parameters for artistic control, improving the rigid pose-based initialization that can place strands inside the body, and whether the approach extends to dynamic (non-quasi-static) hair motion.
