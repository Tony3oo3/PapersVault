## Problem

Existing cooperative object detection (COOD) methods predominantly rely on dense BEV feature maps, whose computational cost grows quadratically with perception range and whose sharing demands large communication bandwidth. Fully sparse alternatives are rarely explored, and when they are, they suffer from Center Feature Missing (CFM) and Isolated Convolution Field (ICF) pathologies that prevent them from matching dense-baseline performance, especially for long-range and occluded objects.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes sequential LiDAR point clouds from the ego agent and up to N−1 cooperative agents, where cooperative frames may carry timestamps earlier than the ego frame due to communication latency (up to 200 ms in experiments). It outputs 3D bounding boxes (center offset, dimensions, and orientation) with detection scores, and for the time-aligned COOD variant, predictions are referenced to a global scan-end timestamp rather than the per-point acquisition times. Communication is limited to sharing top-K=1024 object query features (each a single vector), reducing CPM size to under 1.3 MB versus 48–127 MB for dense BEV-map methods.

## Method

SparseAlign builds a fully sparse 3D UNet (SUNet) that replaces standard sparse convolutions at the two deepest levels with Coordinate-Expandable Convolution (CEC) layers, which enlarge the receptive field to bridge isolated voxel blobs (ICF) and, via 2D CEC on BEV features, guarantee coverage of object centers (CFM). On top of SUNet, a TempAlign Module (TAM) uses a memory queue of top-256 queries from the previous 4 frames with hybrid attention to learn temporal context and compensate for sensor asynchrony and communication delay. A PoseAlign Module (PAM) embeds pose-agnostic relative neighborhood geometry (orientation, edge direction, dimensions, distance) of each detected BBox, matches ego and cooperative BBoxes via Hungarian assignment, and refines the relative transform with pose-graph optimization. A SpatialAlign Module (SAM) rotates cooperative query features into the ego frame and fuses them with ego queries by aggregating k=8 nearest neighbors on a discretized grid. Finally, a CompassRose encoding regresses BBox orientation as offsets to four fixed anchor angles, outperforming direct-angle, second-style, and sin-cos encodings.

## Results

On OPV2V, SparseAlign achieves AP@0.5 of 0.930 and AP@0.7 of 0.892 using only LiDAR, approaching the LiDAR+Camera V2VFormer++ (0.935/0.895) while consuming <1.3 MB of communication bandwidth versus 72.08 MB for dense-BEV methods. On DAIR-V2X, it reaches AP@0.5 of 0.845 and AP@0.7 of 0.685, outperforming the previous best DI-V2X (0.788/0.662) by 5.7% and 2.3% respectively. For the more challenging time-aligned COOD (TA-COOD) task, SparseAlign achieves AP@0.7 of 0.818 on OPV2Vt and 0.548 on DAIR-V2Xt, surpassing the StreamLTS baseline by 9.7% and 14.4% at IoU 0.7.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SparseAlign outperforms all LiDAR-only baselines on both COOD benchmarks: on OPV2V it beats V2VNet (0.917/0.822) by 1.3%/7.0% and CoBEVT (0.913/0.861) by 1.7%/3.1% in AP@0.5/AP@0.7, while on DAIR-V2X it surpasses DI-V2X (0.788/0.662) by 5.7%/2.3%. The only method exceeding SparseAlign on OPV2V is V2VFormer++ (0.935/0.895), which additionally uses camera data. For TA-COOD, SparseAlign dominates StreamLTS by large margins (9.7% AP@0.7 on OPV2Vt, 14.4% on DAIR-V2Xt), and also outperforms Fcooper+SA, FPVRCNN+SA, and OPV2V+SA when the same alignment modules are attached to those weaker backbones. A key differentiator is communication efficiency: SparseAlign transmits <1.3 MB per frame versus 72.08 MB for dense-BEV methods (Fcooper, V2VNet, CoAlign, CoBEVT, V2VAM) and 126.8 MB for OPV2V, representing up to 98% bandwidth reduction. The method also demonstrates superior robustness to communication latency (only 2.6% AP@0.5 drop at 200 ms vs. 6.6% for StreamLTS) and to pose errors (12% AP drop vs. 27% for CoAlign at 1 m/1° error).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in cooperative object detection (COOD), where prior methods (Fcooper, V2VNet, OPV2V, CoAlign, DI-V2X) all encode point clouds into dense BEV feature maps for sharing and fusion. It builds on the MinkowskiEngine sparse convolution framework [5] and the query-based temporal modeling of StreamLTS [40], but introduces a fully sparse pipeline by solving two backbone-specific pathologies—Center Feature Missing and Isolated Convolution Field—via Coordinate-Expandable Convolution (CEC) layers. The pose alignment module extends CoAlign's [17] PGO-based approach with pose-agnostic neighborhood graph features for more robust matching, and the spatial fusion (SAM) is specifically designed for sparse query merging rather than dense grid interpolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 98% reduction in communication bandwidth while matching or exceeding dense-BEV SOTA is practically significant for real-world V2X deployment where bandwidth is a hard constraint. The fully sparse architecture also scales linearly with point count rather than quadratically with perception range, making long-range cooperative detection more tractable. However, pose alignment still degrades by ~10% AP under large localization errors, and the framework has only been validated on two vehicle-vehicle and two vehicle-infrastructure benchmarks; extension to roadside-only or multi-infrastructure scenarios (HoloVIC, RCooper) remains open. The CEC technique for resolving sparse connectivity issues could generalize beyond COOD to other sparse 3D perception tasks.
