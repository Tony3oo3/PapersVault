## Problem

Existing Evolving Domain Generalization (EDG) methods are limited by the small number of available timestamps (typically ≤30 domains), which causes overfitting to sparse temporal observations and prevents accurate capture of the underlying evolving distribution pattern. Moreover, these methods rely on discrete transition functions that inherently represent time in fixed steps, making them unable to model the continuous temporal dynamics needed for robust extrapolation to unseen future domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241745/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled data samples (RGB images at 320×320 for Caltran/Portraits/OcularDisease, 28×28 MNIST digits, 2D coordinate points, or scalar time-series features) at M discrete source timestamps, along with their class labels, and produces a class label for any unseen future timestamp. Inputs are first encoded into a latent space via a feature extractor (MLP, ResNet-18, or ConvNet depending on modality), and the SDE operates entirely in this latent space. At inference, predictions are made by assigning the class of the nearest Evolving Centroid generated by the SDE at the target timestamp, rather than through a learned classifier head.

## Method

SDE-EDG constructs an Infinitely Fined-Grid Evolving Trajectory (IFGET) by first identifying sample-to-sample correspondence between consecutive domains via nearest-neighbor matching in the latent space (within each class), then generating continuous-interpolated samples using linear interpolation with a Beta-distributed rate λ∈(0,1) to fill temporal gaps. A class-conditional Neural SDE (with separate drift and diffusion functions per class) models the continuous latent trajectory, and a path alignment regularizer (J_mle) fits the SDE to IFGET by maximizing the likelihood of observed and interpolated samples under the SDE's transition density. The final objective jointly optimizes a classification loss (using Parzen Window non-parametric density for D(z|y,t) and a neural network for the time-dependent label prior D(y|t)) with the path alignment loss weighted by α. Theoretically, the paper shows J_mle decomposes into a path alignment term and a stochastic uncertainty term, and proves that SDE-EDG achieves a lower generalization bound on the target path space, with the bound tightening as the effective temporal interval Δt decreases.

## Results

SDE-EDG achieves an average accuracy of 75.4% across eight datasets, substantially outperforming the best traditional DG method (MTL at 62.8%) and the best prior EDG method (LSSAE at 65.4%). The largest gains appear on Rotated Gaussian (97.7% vs. 76.3% for AndMask) and Circle (81.5% vs. 73.8% for LSSAE). On the challenging real-world Ocular Disease dataset, SDE-EDG reaches 62.6% accuracy, surpassing the next-best method (CORAL at 60.1%) by 2.5 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDE-EDG outperforms all 15 baselines on average accuracy. Against the four EDG-specific baselines, the margins are most pronounced: SDE-EDG beats LSSAE (65.4%) by 10.0 points, DRAIN (64.4%) by 11.0 points, DDA (63.8%) by 11.6 points, and GI (which could not complete Ocular Disease) by a wide margin on the remaining datasets. Among traditional DG methods, the closest competitor is MTL (62.8%), which SDE-EDG surpasses by 12.6 points on average; notably, IRM and RSC perform poorly on evolving tasks (58.4% and 58.0% respectively), confirming that invariant-feature approaches struggle with temporal distribution shift. On individual datasets, SDE-EDG achieves near-perfect accuracy on Rotated Gaussian (97.7%) and strong results on Circle (81.5%), while the gap narrows on Portraits (89.6% vs. 89.4% for DRAIN) and Caltran (71.3% vs. 70.7% for GI), suggesting that for some real-world tasks with subtler temporal shifts, the advantage is more modest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Evolving Domain Generalization (EDG) line of work (Bai et al. 2023, Qin et al. 2022, Zeng et al. 2023, Nasery et al. 2021) by addressing its core limitation: the small number of discrete timestamps that causes overfitting. It departs from prior EDG methods that rely on discrete transition functions by introducing Neural SDEs (building on Kidger et al. 2021, Kong et al. 2020) to model continuous-time latent trajectories, and proposes IFGET—a sample-correspondence-based interpolation scheme—to densify the temporal grid. The path alignment loss (maximum likelihood fitting of SDE trajectories to IFGET) is a new regularization mechanism not present in prior EDG or domain generalization work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded framework for handling the sparse-timestamp problem in EDG, with a generalization bound showing that smaller temporal intervals reduce error. However, performance degrades noticeably as the extrapolation horizon grows (visible in Figures 4b–c), and the linear interpolation in IFGET is a simplification that may not capture non-linear evolving dynamics. Open questions include whether richer interpolation (e.g., spline or learned) would help, whether the sample-to-sample correspondence assumption holds in settings without natural identity tracking, and whether the approach scales to very long prediction horizons or high-dimensional real-world tasks beyond the modest real-world gains observed here.
