## Problem

Standard RLHF with PPO tends to exploit shortcuts on easy, high-reward data while neglecting challenging samples, leading to inconsistent performance across data groups, training instability, and poor generalization to unseen domains. Existing mitigations such as fixed KL penalties are calibrated to the most likely outliers and cannot adapt to varying difficulty across data subsets. The paper addresses how to learn a policy that performs consistently across diverse data groups without requiring costly manual group annotations.

## Input / Output

The method operates on text-based RLHF trajectories where the agent receives a user query and dialogue history as state, generates tokens sequentially as actions, and receives scalar reward from a Bradley-Terry-trained reward model. During training, the group inference classifier processes entire trajectories to produce soft group assignments, while the adaptive KL penalty is computed per (query, response) pair using the classifier's probability of the high-performing group. The final output is an aligned text response evaluated via win/tie/lose comparisons against baselines under both GPT-4 and human judgment.

## Method

The core contribution is a unified adversarial framework with two jointly trained components that eliminates the need for manual group annotations. First, a group label inference classifier φ (embedded in the critic model's final layer) is trained to maximize the variance of log-likelihood weighted returns across groups, automatically discovering data subsets with distinct performance characteristics. Second, the standard PPO policy gradient is augmented with a variance minimization regularizer that penalizes performance disparities between groups, encouraging the policy to learn invariant features that generalize uniformly. Additionally, the fixed KL penalty coefficient is replaced with an adaptive term η·p_φ(g_high|x,y) that applies stronger constraints to high-performing data (preventing reward hacking) while relaxing constraints on challenging data (expanding exploration space). The classifier and policy are updated in alternating fashion, creating an adversarial dynamic where the classifier seeks the most disparate groups while the policy seeks uniform performance across them.

## Results

Under GPT-4 evaluation, the proposed method achieves win rates of 40.4%, 29.5%, and 34.1% against PPO w/KL on Anthropic-Harmful, Anthropic-Helpful, and OpenAI-Summary respectively, with lose rates of only 25.9%, 6.7%, and 17.7%. Against plain PPO (without KL), the method wins 58.2% on Harmful and 46.3% on Summary. On out-of-distribution data (PKU-SafeRLHF and CNN Dailymail), the method's win rate further increases relative to in-distribution results, and the lose rate against PPO on summarization drops from 32.2% to 12.8%. Chi-squared tests confirm statistical significance (p < 0.05) against all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PPO (without KL), the method wins decisively on Harmful (58.2% vs 16.5% lose) and Summary (46.3% vs 32.2% lose), confirming that the group invariant constraint prevents the reward hacking and instability that plague unregularized PPO. Against PPO w/KL, the method maintains a consistent win advantage across all three tasks (40.4%, 29.5%, 34.1%), with the largest gap on Harmful where the fixed KL penalty is most restrictive. Against DPO, the method wins on all three tasks (29.6%, 33.2%, 30.4%) with lower lose rates, indicating that the RL-based approach with group invariance captures preference signals that DPO's offline formulation misses. Against SFT, the method shows the largest margin on Summary (77.8% win), demonstrating that RL alignment with group invariance substantially improves over supervised fine-tuning alone. In the robust optimization comparison (Table 6), the method outperforms Group DRO (31.0% vs 40.4% win on Harmful) and JTT (32.1% vs 40.4%), while the Macro average loss approach underperforms even PPO, validating that variance minimization is the correct objective over mean-based alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the group invariant learning framework from supervised classification (Arjovsky et al., 2019; Creager et al., 2021) into the RLHF pipeline, where the state-action distribution shifts continuously during training. Unlike prior invariant learning methods that require pre-defined group labels or a two-stage train-then-infer procedure, the authors propose a unified iterative framework that jointly performs adversarial group label inference (via a classifier on the critic) and policy invariant learning, using variance maximization of group returns (Krueger et al., 2021) as the inference objective. The adaptive KL penalty (Eq. 9) further departs from the fixed KL constraint in standard RLHF (Ouyang et al., 2022) by scaling the penalty according to the inferred group membership, allocating more exploration capacity to harder data. The authors claim this is the first application of group invariant learning to RL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RLHF generalization is a critical bottleneck for deploying LLMs as universal assistants, and reward hacking remains a persistent failure mode; this paper offers a principled, annotation-free mechanism to address both. However, the evidence is limited to a single 7B model (Llama 2) and two task settings, and the main experiments use only binary groups, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger models, more diverse domains, or finer-grained group structures. The added complexity (adversarial training of a group classifier on top of already fragile PPO) raises practical adoption concerns, and the sensitivity analysis shows performance degrades sharply when β_policy exceeds 0.1, suggesting the method may be difficult to tune in practice.
