## Problem

Existing reward models for text-to-image evaluation (ImageReward, HPS v2, PickScore) rely on small annotator pools and focus on image fidelity and text alignment, failing to capture community-level creative editing preference. The paper demonstrates that these models achieve only 59–63% pairwise accuracy on a million-user-scale implicit feedback dataset, revealing a significant gap in modeling what makes AI-generated art popular in a creative social network.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240309/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The Social Reward model takes a text prompt and an RGB image as input and outputs a real-valued score computed as the cosine similarity between fine-tuned CLIP text and image embeddings. The training data consists of (prompt, positive image, negative image) triplets where labels are derived from remix counts (the number of times community members reuse an image for editing) rather than explicit ratings. When used for fine-tuning Stable Diffusion 2.1-base, the model selects the highest- and lowest-scoring images per prompt from 1M user-generated images, with a special negative-prompt token appended to low-scoring examples.

## Method

The paper introduces Social Reward, a scoring model obtained by fine-tuning CLIP's last two text-encoder and last three visual-encoder residual blocks with a triplet loss (margin-based distance between prompt, positive image, and negative image embeddings) on the newly curated Picsart Image-Social dataset (1.7M images, 3M pairs, 1.5M users). The dataset is built from implicit community editing behavior on Picsart, using remix frequency and influencer remixes as positive signals, with biases mitigated via prompt conditioning, exposure-time matching, and a shared public profile. The Social Reward score is then used to fine-tune Stable Diffusion 2.1-base (U-Net and text encoder at lr 1e-5) by selecting best/worst images per prompt and adding 625K LAION-5B images for regularization, yielding a 330K-image training set. This approach achieves 69.7% pairwise accuracy (vs. 62.6% for PickScore) and improves fine-tuned model scores across HPS v2, ImageReward, PickScore, and LAION aesthetic metrics while only slightly reducing CLIP alignment.

## Results

On the Picsart Image-Social test set, Social Reward achieves 69.7% pairwise accuracy, outperforming PickScore (62.6%), ImageReward (60.48%), and HPS v2 (59.4%). In a user study with popular Picsart creators, Social Reward also outperforms PickScore in selecting the image more likely to gain social popularity. When used to fine-tune Stable Diffusion 2.1-base, the resulting model improves Social Reward score from -0.095 to -0.062 (general prompts) and LAION aesthetic from 5.902 to 6.011, while CLIP alignment slightly decreases (0.280 → 0.278).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Social Reward outperforms all three baselines on the Picsart Image-Social test set: it beats PickScore by 7.1 percentage points (69.7% vs. 62.6%), ImageReward by 9.2 points (69.7% vs. 60.48%), and HPS v2 by 10.3 points (69.7% vs. 59.4%). The gap over PickScore is notable because PickScore is the only baseline trained on real user feedback (albeit from a web app with 6.4K users), whereas ImageReward and HPS v2 rely on professional annotators focused on image quality and text alignment rather than creative editing intent. In the user study (Figure 5), Social Reward also wins against PickScore on prompts sampled from PickScore's own test set, confirming generalizability beyond its training distribution. For the fine-tuning experiment, the Social Reward–fine-tuned SD 2.1-base model wins on 75.6% of prompts for Social Reward, 73.8% for LAION aesthetic, 73.4% for HPS v2, 70.6% for ImageReward, and 63.2–67.8% for PickScore, but loses on CLIP alignment (43–43.8% win-rate), suggesting a trade-off between text-image alignment and community creative preference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the human-preference reward modeling line (ImageReward, HPS v2, PickScore) by shifting the annotation criterion from image quality and text-image alignment to social popularity for creative editing, operationalized as the frequency of community remixes. Methodologically, it fine-tunes CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) with a triplet loss to produce a social reward score, and adapts the reward-guided fine-tuning procedure from Wu et al. (2023b) to align Stable Diffusion with community creative preferences. Conceptually, it also draws on the social-network popularity prediction literature (Flickr, Instagram) but repurposes it for synthetic images in an editing-centric creative community, where the popularity signal (remix count) reflects active creative reuse rather than passive engagement like likes or views.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Picsart Image-Social dataset (1.7M images, 1.5M users) is the first million-user-scale collection of implicit human preferences specifically for synthetic images in a creative editing context, filling a gap that smaller, quality-focused annotation efforts (ImageReward, HPD v2) cannot address. A notable open question is generalizability: the dataset and model are tied to Picsart's specific community dynamics and remix-based signal, and it remains unclear whether social reward transfers to other platforms or to non-editing creative contexts. The finding that CLIP alignment slightly *decreases* after Social Reward fine-tuning suggests that creative-editing popularity is a genuinely distinct axis from text-image fidelity, but the modest absolute accuracy (~70%) and single-platform scope limit how broadly the conclusions can be extrapolated.
