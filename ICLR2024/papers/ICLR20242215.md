## Problem

Prior physics-based motion representations for humanoid control (e.g., ASE, CALM, PhysicsVAE) are trained on small, specialized datasets and produce latent spaces that only cover narrow movement styles like locomotion or boxing, making them inapplicable to complex tasks such as free-form VR controller tracking or complex terrain traversal. The core challenge is learning a single latent space that captures the full spectrum of human motor skills from a large, unstructured MoCap dataset while remaining a useful, navigable action space for downstream RL.

## Input / Output

The system takes proprioception (3D joint rotations and positions of 24 SMPL joints plus angular and linear velocities) and a task-specific goal (e.g., target speed, 3D reach point, 2D trajectory with height map, or three 6DOF VR controller poses) as input. The high-level policy outputs a 32-d latent code per frame at 30 Hz, which is decoded by a frozen network into 23×3 PD joint targets driving the physics simulation. The learnable prior can also generate latent codes from proprioception alone, enabling random motion generation without any task goal.

## Method

PULSE first trains PHC+, an improved Perpetual Humanoid Controller that achieves 100% imitation success on the full AMASS dataset via progressive hard-negative mining, SiLU activations, and a three-primitive composer. It then distills PHC+ into a variational information bottleneck (encoder-decoder with a learnable prior R(z|s^p) conditioned on proprioception) through online distillation: the student policy explores the environment while the frozen teacher annotates collected states with target actions, optimizing an action-matching loss plus KL divergence and a temporal smoothness regularizer. The key insight is that direct online distillation from a pretrained imitator is essential for scaling to large datasets—training the variational bottleneck from scratch with RL alone fails to converge. For downstream tasks, a high-level PPO policy generates latent codes in the 32-d space, and actions are computed as a residual over the prior's mean (a_t = D(z_t^task + μ_t^p)), which provides a physically grounded starting point for exploration and yields human-like behavior without adversarial rewards.

## Results

On the VR controller tracking task (AMASS-Test), PULSE achieves 93.4% success rate with 88.6 mm global MPJPE and 67.7 mm root-relative MPJPE, matching the from-scratch baseline (93.4% Succ, 80.4 mm E_g-mpjpe) while significantly outperforming ASE-30Hz (37.6% Succ) and CALM-30Hz (10.1% Succ). On the real-world QuestSim dataset, PULSE successfully tracks all 14 sequences (14/14) with 68.4 mm controller position error, compared to ASE-30Hz (7/14, 99.0 mm) and CALM-30Hz (2/14, 206.9 mm). For motion imitation on AMASS-Test, PULSE retains 97.1% success rate after distillation from PHC+ (99.2%), with 54.1 mm E_g-mpjpe versus PHC+'s 36.1 mm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PULSE dramatically outperforms both ASE and CALM on VR controller tracking. On AMASS-Test, PULSE achieves 93.4% success rate versus ASE-30Hz's 37.6% and CALM-30Hz's 10.1%, with E_g-mpjpe of 88.6 mm vs. 120.5 mm (ASE) and 122.4 mm (CALM). On the real-world QuestSim dataset, the gap is even starker: PULSE tracks all 14 sequences while ASE-30Hz manages only 7/14 and CALM-30Hz only 2/14. Compared to PHC (the teacher imitator), PULSE retains 97.1% imitation success rate on AMASS-Test (vs. PHC's 97.1%) at the cost of slightly higher tracking error (54.1 vs. 36.1 mm E_g-mpjpe), confirming the variational bottleneck introduces mild lossy compression. Notably, PULSE matches the from-scratch baseline on success rate (93.4%) but with slightly higher MPJPE (88.6 vs. 80.4 mm), suggesting the latent space does not handicap exploration but trades a small amount of precision for the benefit of faster convergence and human-like behavior in generative tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

PULSE extends the line of physics-based motion latent spaces (ASE, CALM, ControlVAE, PhysicsVAE, NCP) by addressing their core limitation: narrow coverage due to small, specialized training datasets. Rather than training the latent space from scratch with adversarial or RL objectives, PULSE distills motor skills from a pretrained motion imitator (PHC+) trained on the full AMASS dataset via online supervised distillation with a variational information bottleneck. The learnable prior conditioned on proprioception draws from HuMoR's kinematic prior but operates in a physics-governed action space, and the residual-action formulation for downstream tasks is a practical departure from prior hierarchical RL approaches that sample directly from the latent distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PULSE provides a practical 'foundation model for control' that a single trained decoder can serve across locomotion, terrain traversal, striking, reaching, and free-form VR tracking without task-specific adaptation layers or adversarial rewards, reducing the engineering burden of building new humanoid controllers. Key limitations include lossy compression (99.8% vs. 100% imitation success), occasional stagnation in fallen/standing states, and slightly worse tracking precision than a from-scratch policy on VR tasks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to human-object interaction, articulated fingers, and multi-agent settings, and whether the variational bottleneck can be made lossless while retaining the probabilistic sampling benefits.
