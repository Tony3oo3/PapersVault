## Problem

Existing online mapping methods for autonomous driving are split into two disconnected paradigms: map element detection captures laneline geometry but omits topology, while centerline perception captures lane connectivity but lacks physical geometry. Both ignore the intrinsic relationship that lanelines bind centerlines, and simply predicting both in separate branches is empirically redundant and mutually exclusive in learning objective. The paper introduces lane segment as a unified representation that seamlessly incorporates geometry, semantics, and topology in a single end-to-end formulation.

## Input / Output

The network takes surrounding multi-view RGB images as input and produces vectorized lane segments within a ±50 m × ±25 m BEV range, where each segment is defined by 10 ordered 3D points for the centerline plus a symmetric offset for left/right boundaries, a class label (lane segment or pedestrian crossing), laneline type annotations (dashed, solid, non-visible) for each boundary, a weighted adjacency matrix encoding directed topology among all segments, and a per-instance drivable-area mask. Pedestrian crossings are treated as a special transverse lane segment with a fixed principal-axis orientation to avoid directional ambiguity during training.

## Method

LaneSegNet comprises a ResNet-50 + FPN encoder with BEVFormer's PV-to-BEV transformation, a 6-layer decoder built on Deformable DETR, and a multi-branch MLP predictor. The core novelty is a lane attention module that replaces standard deformable cross-attention with a heads-to-regions mechanism: four reference points are uniformly distributed along the predicted lane segment, and each of the 8 attention heads is assigned to attend only to 32 sampling locations around one reference point, enabling long-range context gathering along the elongated lane shape while preserving local detail for pivot-point identification. A second key design is an identical initialization strategy where all heads in the first decoder layer share a single reference point derived from the positional query, rather than the conventional distributed initialization, which the authors show stabilizes convergence and improves mAP by 3.5 points. Training follows the DETR paradigm with Hungarian bipartite matching and a composite loss combining L1 geometric regression, focal classification, cross-entropy laneline-type prediction, focal topology loss, and CE+dice mask supervision.

## Results

On the OpenLane-V2 dataset, LaneSegNet achieves 32.6 mAP on the newly proposed lane segment perception task, outperforming the strongest baseline MapTRv2 (28.5 mAP) by +4.1 mAP and TopoNet (23.0 mAP) by +9.6 mAP. On map element detection, LaneSegNet reaches 27.5 mAP (vs. MapTR's 22.7), and on centerline perception it attains 29.7 OLS (vs. TopoNet's 20.0 and STSU's 13.2). The model runs at 14.7 FPS with a 31.4% decoder latency reduction over the multi-branch baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaneSegNet wins decisively across all three evaluation tasks. On lane segment perception (Table 1), it beats MapTRv2 by +4.1 mAP and TopoNet by +9.6 mAP, while also achieving the best topology score (TOP_ls_ls 8.1 vs. 1.0 for TopoNet) and lowest distance error (0.673 vs. 0.769). On map element detection (Table 2), LaneSegNet's reshaped predictions yield 27.5 mAP, surpassing MapTR (22.7) by +4.8 mAP and VectorMapNet (8.3) by a wide margin. On centerline perception (Table 3), LaneSegNet achieves 29.7 OLS, outperforming TopoNet (20.0) by +9.7 and STSU (13.2) by +16.5, with a particularly large gap in topology reasoning (TOP_ll 7.6 vs. 2.3 for TopoNet). The lightweight LaneSegNet-tiny variant matches MapTRv2's mAP (28.5) while running faster (16.2 vs. 13.6 FPS), demonstrating that the lane segment formulation is effective even with a smaller model.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaneSegNet builds on the BEVFormer encoder for multi-view-to-BEV feature extraction and the Deformable DETR decoder architecture, but departs from both the map element detection line (MapTR, VectorMapNet) and the centerline perception line (TopoNet, STSU) by introducing 'lane segment' as a unified representation that simultaneously encodes geometric boundaries, semantic line types, and topological connectivity. The lane attention module with its heads-to-regions mechanism is a targeted modification of deformable attention, designed to handle the elongated, long-range geometry of lanes rather than compact objects. The topology branch and topological loss follow TopoNet's graph-based formulation, while instance mask prediction is adapted from Mask2Former.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying geometry and topology in a single representation, lane segment perception eliminates the need for separate post-processing pipelines to recover missing details, which is critical for real-time deployment in production autonomous driving systems. The 31.4% latency reduction and 37.7% parameter reduction over multi-branch baselines make the unified approach not just more complete but also more efficient. Key limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (OpenLane-V2), lack of lane segment annotations on other popular benchmarks (nuScenes, Waymo), and no exploration of downstream planning benefits. Open questions include whether the lane segment formulation generalizes to 3D multi-sensor settings and whether the identical initialization strategy transfers to other deformable-attention-based tasks.
