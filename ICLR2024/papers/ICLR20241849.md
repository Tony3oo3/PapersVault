## Problem

In Continual Federated Learning, the global model suffers from Global Catastrophic Forgetting when trained on sequentially arriving tasks, and existing CFL solutions either require clients to store old-task data (infeasible on edge devices), assume server-side representative datasets (violating FL privacy), or rely on generative models (adding heavy computation and communication overhead). A fundamental challenge specific to the federated setting is that local continual-learning methods applied per-client do not guarantee global forgetting mitigation, since clients only hold local information about old tasks and aggregation of locally-optimized updates can still disrupt global performance, especially under non-IID data.

## Input / Output

Clients receive the global model and orthogonal subspace vectors from the server, perform standard SGD on their local image data (28×28 for Permuted MNIST, 32×32 for CIFAR-100, 84×84 for Mini-Imagenet), and send only model updates. At the end of each task, an additional GPSE round requires clients to compute layer activations, project them onto the orthogonal complement of the current subspace, multiply by a random Gaussian matrix, and securely aggregate the resulting d^ℓ × s^ℓ matrices at the server. The server outputs an updated global model and an expanded orthonormal basis set {O^ℓ} that accumulates the principal subspaces of all completed tasks.

## Method

FOT enforces a relaxed orthogonality constraint: instead of requiring ΔW^ℓ X_t^ℓ = 0 (which would block all learning), it projects aggregated updates onto the orthogonal complement of the low-rank Global Principal Subspace (GPS) of each layer's inputs from prior tasks, via the FedProject aggregation rule δ_global^ℓ* = δ_global^ℓ − O_{t-1}^ℓ (O_{t-1}^ℓ)^T δ_global^ℓ. The GPS is extracted privacy-preservingly through GPSE: each client multiplies its projected layer activations by a random Gaussian matrix, the server securely aggregates these products, and applies SVD to recover the principal subspace up to a data-driven rank threshold (Eq. 12), then extends the orthonormal basis via Gram-Schmidt. This design requires no client-side storage, no modification to local training, and is compatible with secure aggregation, while the global nature of the subspace extraction makes the method robust to data heterogeneity across clients.

## Results

FOT achieves the highest average accuracy and lowest forgetting across all four benchmarks in both IID and non-IID settings. On IID data, FOT reaches 90.35% ACC / 1.75% FGT on Permuted MNIST, 71.90% / 0.87% on Split-CIFAR100, 85.21% / 1.11% on 5-Datasets, and 69.07% / 0.19% on Split Mini-Imagenet. Under non-IID data, FOT maintains strong performance (e.g., 66.31% ACC / 0.60% FGT on CIFAR100) while baselines degrade substantially (e.g., GPM+FL drops to 34.15% ACC / 34.60% FGT on CIFAR100). The paper reports an average accuracy gain of up to 15% and 27% lower forgetting compared to the best baselines, with no additional client-side storage or computation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FOT outperforms all eight baselines on every benchmark in both IID and non-IID settings. Against the strongest direct CFL baselines, FOT beats TARGET by 1.12% ACC and 1.38% FGT on PMNIST (IID), and by 6.62% ACC and 24.93% FGT on Mini-Imagenet (IID). Against FedCIL (generative replay), FOT gains 11.64% ACC and 28.71% FGT on Mini-Imagenet (IID) while requiring no client-side storage or extra computation. Against the FL-adapted CL methods (EWC, ER, RGO, GPM), FOT achieves 1.5–6.5% higher ACC and 3–17% lower FGT depending on the dataset. The advantage is most pronounced under non-IID data: on CIFAR100, FOT achieves 66.31% ACC / 0.60% FGT while GPM+FL collapses to 34.15% / 34.60%, and RGO+FL drops to 40.83% / 31.57%. FOT is the only method that requires no edge storage and no extra local computation (Table 2), unlike all other baselines which store old-task data, Fisher matrices, or projection matrices on clients.

## Relation to Prior Work

FOT adapts the centralized Gradient Projection Memory (GPM, Saha et al., 2021) to the federated setting, but with a critical departure: rather than projecting local gradients against locally-computed subspaces (which diverge across clients under non-IID data), FOT extracts a *global* principal activation subspace per layer via a privacy-preserving randomized SVD procedure (GPSE) and projects the *aggregated* update (FedProject) into its orthogonal complement. This distinguishes FOT from prior CFL methods that rely on client-side memory (ER, GLFC), server-side representative data (knowledge distillation), or generative replay (FedCIL, TARGET), all of which impose storage, computation, or privacy costs that FOT avoids. The base aggregation follows FedAvg, and the convergence guarantee is inherited from the equivalence of FedProject to ProximalSGD on a convex constraint set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FOT is the first CFL method that simultaneously requires zero client-side storage, negligible extra local computation, and full compatibility with secure aggregation, making it the most deployable solution for edge-device continual learning to date. The near-zero forgetting (≤0.2% on Mini-Imagenet with 20 tasks) and the threshold-based capacity/forgetting trade-off provide a practical knob for system designers. Key limitations include the task-incremental assumption (known task boundaries), the subspace capacity ceiling (Table 3 shows 89.94% of layer space consumed on Mini-Imagenet), and the fact that the method is restricted to task-separated settings. Open questions include extending to task-free continual learning, handling overlapping task arrivals, and understanding how the threshold should be set adaptively rather than via grid search.
