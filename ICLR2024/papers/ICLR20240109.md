## Problem

Deployed robots often must operate with limited, low-cost sensors (e.g., a single RGB camera or proprioception alone), yet additional expensive or fragile sensors (motion capture, tactile arrays, multi-view cameras) could dramatically aid learning if available only during training. Prior privileged-information RL methods exploit only a single training component (critic, policy, world model, or representation) and often assume the privileged observations are predictable from target observations, causing them to fail when the information gap is large. This paper formalizes the 'sensory scaffolding' setting and asks how to comprehensively exploit privileged, training-time-only sensors across all auxiliary components of an RL algorithm to improve the target policy.

## Input / Output

At test time, the target policy consumes only impoverished observations (proprioception, binary touch, a single occluded or wrist-mounted RGB camera, or joint angles) and outputs continuous robot actions (gripper velocities, joint torques, or finger positions). At training time, the system additionally receives privileged observations that vary by task: multi-view RGB cameras, object pose, dense tactile sensing, true simulator state, future piano notes, or active wrist-camera images. The privileged observations can be high-dimensional (RGB images) or low-dimensional (pose vectors), and may be noisy; they are strictly more informative than target observations but need not be recoverable from them.

## Method

Scaffolder builds on DreamerV3's RSSM-based model-based RL and trains a second 'scaffolded' world model on the combined observations o+ = [o-, o^p] alongside the original target world model on o-. A key mechanism, Nested Latent Imagination (NLI), rolls out the target policy inside the scaffolded world model: the target policy selects actions from z-, the scaffolded dynamics generate z+, and a learned target-embedding predictor translates z+ back into the target posterior to recover z- for the next step. The scaffolded world model provides more accurate dynamics, the scaffolded reward and critic (conditioned on both z- and z+) improve credit assignment, a scaffolded exploration policy collects task-relevant data faster, and an auxiliary decoder from z- to o+ enriches the target representation. Together these components yield a more accurate TD(λ) return estimate, which the paper empirically verifies reduces mean absolute error against ground-truth Monte-Carlo returns, directly improving the policy gradient signal at every update.

## Results

Scaffolder achieves the highest aggregate median performance across all ten S3 tasks, bridging 79% of the performance gap between target-only (o⁻) and fully privileged (o⁺) DreamerV3. It learns faster in 8 out of 10 tasks and, with a sample budget of only 250K–5M environment steps, outperforms or matches AAC and RMA+ (both trained for 100M steps) in 9 out of 10 tasks. The unprivileged DreamerV3 baseline proves surprisingly competitive, even outperforming some privileged methods like AAC on several tasks, highlighting that the gap between o⁻ and o⁺ lies more in learnability than in behavioral support.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Scaffolder outperforms all five baselines in aggregate median score across the 10 S3 tasks. Against the unprivileged DreamerV3 baseline, Scaffolder consistently improves, with the gap being largest in information-poor settings like Blind Pick and Blind Locomotion. Against AAC (privileged critic, 100M steps), Scaffolder wins in 9/10 tasks despite using 20–400× fewer samples; AAC exhibits high variance and is sometimes worse than unprivileged DreamerV3. Against RMA+ (privileged representation, 100M steps), Scaffolder wins broadly, while RMA+ excels only in Noisy Monkey (where noisy proprioception is easily regressed to true state) and completely fails in Blind Pick. Against DreamerV3+BC (privileged teacher with BC loss), Scaffolder wins in tasks with large imitation gaps (Blind Pick, Blind Locomotion, Wrist/Occluded Pick-Place) where the privileged teacher's optimal behavior is fundamentally different from what the blind student can execute. Against Informed Dreamer (privileged world model decoding), Scaffolder wins in tasks where target observations are not predictive of privileged observations, as Informed Dreamer's decoding objective fails under large information gaps. Against Guided Observability (progressive privileged dropout), Scaffolder consistently outperforms, benefiting from its multi-component scaffolding rather than a single dropout schedule.

## Relation to Prior Work

Scaffolder builds directly on DreamerV3's RSSM-based model-based RL framework, extending it by training a parallel 'scaffolded' world model on privileged observations alongside the target world model. It departs from prior privileged-RL work (AAC, Informed Dreamer, RMA, teacher-student methods) which each exploit privileged information through a single route—critic, world model, representation, or policy imitation—by integrating privileged sensing simultaneously across world modelling, credit assignment, exploration, and representation learning. The paper also distinguishes itself from privileged imitation learning by training both target and scaffolded policies from scratch rather than assuming a pre-existing expert, and from domain adaptation by framing the problem as a POMDP with a strict superset observation space at training time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sensory scaffolding framing is practically well-motivated for robotics deployment (expensive sensors for training, cheap sensors for deployment), and the S3 benchmark of 10 diverse tasks with varied sensor configurations provides a valuable evaluation suite for future work. Key limitations include the compute overhead of maintaining two world models and an additional exploration policy, evaluation restricted to simulation, and the lack of theoretical guarantees on how privileged information improves the RL objective. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to real robots with noisy privileged sensors, whether the representation learning objective can be improved beyond reconstruction, and whether a single shared model could replace the dual world model architecture.
