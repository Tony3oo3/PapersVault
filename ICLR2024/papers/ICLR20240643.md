## Problem

General-purpose LLMs are static, expensive to retrain, and struggle with hallucination, long-tail facts, and temporal knowledge updates. Existing augmentation approaches (retrieval-augmented LMs, generated knowledge prompting) rely on a single fixed knowledge source and do not reflect the modular, collaborative nature of real-world knowledge, making targeted updates and community-driven contributions impractical for black-box LLMs.

## Input / Output

The system takes a text query and a pool of small specialized LMs (knowledge cards, each ~1.3B parameters fine-tuned on a specific domain corpus) as inputs. At inference time, knowledge cards generate background documents that pass through three sequential selectors (relevance filtering via MPNet cosine similarity, pruning via Pegasus summarization, and factuality scoring via FactKB + VitaminC with top-k sampling) before being concatenated into the LLM prompt as a 'Knowledge:' prefix. The output is a text answer for QA tasks or a classification label for misinformation detection, with the top-down variant additionally producing iterative yes/no decisions about whether external knowledge is needed.

## Method

KNOWLEDGE CARD is a modular framework that plugs small, domain-specialized language models (trained from OPT-1.3B checkpoints via causal LM fine-tuning on 25 diverse corpora) into black-box LLMs at the natural language level, avoiding any parameter access. Three knowledge selectors control quality: a relevance selector retains top-k documents by encoder-based cosine similarity to the query, a pruning selector condenses documents via abstractive summarization to fit context limits, and a factuality selector scores documents using both summarization faithfulness and retrieval-augmented fact-checking, then samples from the top-k scored documents to balance strictness with flexibility for novel knowledge. Two integration strategies are proposed: bottom-up activates all knowledge cards simultaneously and concatenates selected documents into the prompt (enabling multi-domain synthesis), while top-down iteratively asks the LLM whether it needs external knowledge, then selectively activates one relevant card per round (via automatic description matching or explicit selection) until the LLM is confident enough to answer. This design makes knowledge updates plug-and-play: new domain cards can be trained independently and added without retraining the base LLM.

## Results

On MMLU (5-shot), KNOWLEDGE CARD Top-Down with Explicit Selection achieves 72.8% overall accuracy, outperforming vanilla Codex (68.3%) and even the ~3× larger Flan-PaLM (72.2%). On the LUN misinformation detection task (16-shot), the Bottom-Up approach reaches 89.8% balanced accuracy (2-way) and 70.6% (4-way), surpassing all baselines by at least 15.8% and 10.0% BAcc respectively. On the newly curated MidtermQA benchmark (5-shot), Bottom-Up achieves 83.6% exact match in the open-book setting, outperforming vanilla Codex (55.1%) by 28.5 points using a single 1.3B-parameter knowledge card to update the 175B Codex model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KNOWLEDGE CARD outperforms all nine baselines across all three evaluation tasks. On MMLU, Top-Down Exp (72.8%) beats the strongest retrieval baseline RePlug LSR (71.8%) by 1.0 points and the strongest generated-prompting baseline Recitation (71.9%) by 0.9 points, while also surpassing the much larger Flan-PaLM (72.2%) by 0.6 points; ATLAS (47.9%) is far behind all methods. On misinformation detection, Bottom-Up (89.8% BAcc 2-way) dominates RePlug LSR (78.8%) by 11.0 points and Recitation (65.0%) by 24.8 points, with the multi-domain synthesis advantage of activating all knowledge cards simultaneously. On MidtermQA, Bottom-Up (83.6% EM open-book) outperforms Si et al. (52.1% F1) which uses the same midterm news as a retrieval corpus, and all generated-prompting baselines (GKP 45.0%, Recitation 44.4%, GRTR 55.6% F1) actually underperform vanilla Codex, confirming that probing outdated parametric knowledge is counterproductive for temporal updates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both retrieval-augmented generation (which relies on a single fixed non-parametric corpus) and generated knowledge prompting (which extracts knowledge from the LLM's own parameters) by proposing small, domain-specialized LMs as plug-in knowledge repositories for black-box LLMs. Conceptually, it repurposes the modularity idea from Mixture-of-Experts and adapter-based approaches but operates at the natural language level rather than the parameter level, making it compatible with API-only models. The three knowledge selectors (relevance, pruning, factuality) draw on encoder-based similarity, abstractive summarization, and retrieval-augmented fact-checking, respectively, but their composition into a pipeline for multi-domain knowledge synthesis is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical path for community-driven knowledge updates to black-box LLMs without retraining, and the MidtermQA benchmark provides a concrete test of temporal knowledge update. Key limitations include the factuality selector's bias toward well-documented domains (newer knowledge may score low), imperfect LLM self-assessment via yes/no questions, and the reliance on relatively small (1.3B) knowledge cards that can still hallucinate. Open questions include designing generalizable factuality evaluation, better abstention/calibration strategies, and combining bottom-up and top-down approaches more effectively.
