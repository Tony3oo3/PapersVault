## Problem

Existing retrieval benchmarks for LLMs are either saturated or measure only single-fact queries, leaving a gap in understanding how models handle multi-constraint information retrieval queries that require generating longer, structured outputs. The paper asks how constraint type, information popularity, and constrainedness jointly affect a model's ability to satisfy all specified constraints simultaneously, and whether providing complete context (simulating retrieval-augmented settings) resolves these failures.

## Input / Output

Each query specifies an author (name + birth year) plus one or two book constraints drawn from lexical (title starts/ends with a letter, word count), temporal (publication year range), or named-entity (human/city name present or absent in title) types. The model must output a numbered list of book titles, optionally preceded by a 1–2 sentence justification per title (chain-of-thought). Evaluation is deliberately lenient: titles are matched via bidirectional substring checks or 80% Levenshtein similarity, word-count constraints tolerate ±1 word, and the resulting metrics (irrelevance, satisfaction, completeness, all-correctness) are computed over clustered output items rather than raw strings.

## Method

KITAB is built by sampling 611 authors from WikiData (filtered by birth year, work count, and Open Library cross-reference), collecting their English-language books with publication years and entity annotations (human names via Azure NLP + GPT-4, city names via Azure NLP + Geonames), then generating 12,989 constraint-satisfaction queries with controlled constrainedness and popularity distributions. The evaluation framework defines four experimental conditions—ALL-BOOKS, NO-CONTEXT, WITH-CONTEXT, and SELF-CONTEXT—that systematically decouple parametric recall from constraint verification and from self-retrieval. A key design choice is the dynamic data collection pipeline, which can be re-run on disjoint author lists to produce fresh test data and mitigate benchmark contamination. The paper's central empirical finding is that even with complete in-context book lists, GPT-4 and GPT-3.5 still fail to satisfy constraints at high rates, and that self-retrieval (SELF-CONTEXT) paradoxically increases hallucinated titles as models fabricate books to meet constraints.

## Results

On KITAB (13,000+ book-retrieval queries across 611 authors), GPT-4 achieves a satisfaction rate of 0.51 (NO-CONTEXT) and 0.78 (WITH-CONTEXT), but all-correctness (perfect match with ground truth) remains below 0.31 even with full context. GPT-3.5 is consistently worse, with all-correctness peaking at only 0.15 under WITH-CONTEXT. Irrelevant (potentially hallucinated) information ranges from 20–44% under NO-CONTEXT and SELF-CONTEXT, dropping to 0% only when the full book list is provided as context. Self-retrieval (SELF-CONTEXT) paradoxically *increases* irrelevance (0.33 for GPT-4 vs. 0.26 for NO-CONTEXT) as the model fabricates titles to satisfy constraints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between GPT-4 and GPT-3.5 on the same KITAB benchmark. GPT-4 outperforms GPT-3.5 on every metric across all three conditions: satisfaction improves from 0.44→0.51 (NO-CONTEXT) and 0.68→0.78 (WITH-CONTEXT), completeness from 0.16→0.24 (NO-CONTEXT) and 0.47→0.70 (WITH-CONTEXT), and all-correctness from 0.07→0.08 (NO-CONTEXT) and 0.15→0.31 (WITH-CONTEXT). However, the authors emphasize that the gap is 'not as dramatic,' suggesting that scaling alone does not resolve the constraint-satisfaction bottleneck. Notably, GPT-3.5's SELF-CONTEXT condition is particularly degraded (satisfaction drops to 0.26, all-correctness to 0.02), indicating that the weaker model is more susceptible to self-retrieval failures. Even the best configuration (GPT-4 + WITH-CONTEXT) leaves 21% of relevant books unsatisfying the constraint and only 31% of queries fully correct, establishing that constraint verification remains a fundamental open problem for current LLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operationalizes the constraint-satisfaction lens proposed by Yuksekgonul et al. (2023) for mechanistic analysis of factual errors in open-source models, repurposing it as a controlled evaluation framework for proprietary LLMs (GPT-4, GPT-3.5). It departs from prior factual QA benchmarks (SQuAD, MMLU, Natural Questions, TruthfulQA) that focus on single-constraint, single-output queries and have largely saturated, by introducing multi-constraint, list-output queries with controlled constrainedness and popularity. The WITH-CONTEXT condition explicitly simulates retrieval-augmented generation (Lewis et al., 2020; Nakano et al., 2021) by providing complete ground-truth context, isolating the model's constraint-verification ability from its retrieval ability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KITAB provides a non-saturated, dynamically extensible benchmark for a task that is central to LLM-powered search engines (e.g., BingChat), where constraint satisfaction failures directly erode user trust. The finding that complete context eliminates irrelevance yet leaves constraint satisfaction and all-correctness below 35% challenges the assumption that RAG resolves factual reliability, pointing to a fundamental filtering bottleneck. Open questions include the unexplained 'phase transition' in popularity, the bimodal constrainedness effect for certain constraint types, and whether the dynamic data collection workflow generalizes beyond the literature domain to other retrieval settings.
