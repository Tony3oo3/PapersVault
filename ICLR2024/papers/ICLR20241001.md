## Problem

Existing AI advice systems assume humans will perfectly follow recommendations, producing suboptimal policies when adherence is partial, and they provide advice at every step regardless of necessity. No prior work formulates a sequential decision-making model that simultaneously accounts for stochastic human adherence and a defer option, nor provides learning algorithms with tighter sample complexity than generic problem-agnostic RL for this structured setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241001/cropped_page9_idx7.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on an episodic tabular MDP with |S| states, |A| actions, and horizon H. In Environment 1, the transition kernel, reward, and human policy π^H are known while the adherence level θ: S×A→[0,1] is unknown; in Environment 2, all components are unknown. The machine outputs either an advice action a^M ∈ A or a defer action at each step, and the human executes the advice with probability θ(s,a^M) or falls back to π^H. The final output is a near-optimal advice policy, and in E2 a family of pertinent advice policies {π̂_β} parameterized by a penalty β (equivalently, a CMDP constraint D bounding the expected number of non-defer steps).

## Method

Two tailored algorithms exploit the structure of the adherence model. UCB-AD (for E1) maintains an optimistic estimate θ̄ of the adherence level and leverages a monotonicity property—higher θ entry-wise implies higher optimal value—to reduce the UCB confidence analysis to the θ-space, achieving O(H²S²A/ε²) PAC sample complexity versus O(H³S²A/ε²) for directly applying Dann & Brunskill's model-based UCB. RFE-β (for E2) performs reward-free exploration to estimate the full transition kernel, then plans simultaneously for all penalized reward functions r_β = r^M − β·𝕀{a≠defer}, yielding a family of ε-optimal pertinent advice policies with O(H⁵SA/ε²) episodes. The β-penalized formulation is shown to be equivalent (via primal-dual) to a constrained MDP where the constraint bounds the expected number of advice steps, enabling a single exploration phase to produce near-optimal policies for all advice budgets D ∈ [0,H).

## Results

Theoretically, UCB-AD achieves a PAC sample complexity of Õ(H²S²A/ε²) for the partially-known environment E₁, improving over the O(H³S²A/ε²) bound from model-based RL (Dann & Brunskill) and O(H⁴SA/ε²) from model-free RL. For the fully-unknown environment E₂, RFE-β requires O(H⁵SA/ε²) episodes to output near-optimal policies uniformly for all penalty parameters β. Empirically on Flappy Bird, UCB-AD achieves the lowest cumulative regret among all tested algorithms for both Policy Greedy and Policy Safe, and RFE-CMDP generalizes to unseen advice budgets (D=2,3,4,5) after training on D=1, while UC-CFH fails to do so.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: EULER (Zanette & Brunskill, 2019), a problem-agnostic model-free RL algorithm achieving minimax-optimal regret, and UC-CFH (Kalagarla et al., 2021), a provably convergent CMDP algorithm. On the Flappy Bird environment (E₁), UCB-AD consistently outperforms both RFE-AD and EULER in cumulative regret for both Policy Greedy and Policy Safe (Figure 2a, 2b), demonstrating that exploiting the adherence structure yields better sample efficiency than generic RL. For pertinent advice under E₂, RFE-CMDP shows a marginal convergence-rate advantage over UC-CFH when the advice budget is D=1 (Figure 3b). More critically, RFE-CMDP generalizes: after learning with D=1, it produces near-optimal policies for D=2,3,4,5 without additional exploration, whereas UC-CFH fails to explore the transition kernel sufficiently and can only output near-optimal policies for the original D=1 instance (Figure 3c). Additionally, RFE-CMDP is more sample-efficient with respect to the advice budget since it avoids re-running exploration for each D value.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adherence-aware recommendation model of Grand-Clément and Pauphilet (2022) by introducing a defer action (analogous to the lazy-MDP of Jacq et al., 2022) and, crucially, provides the first learning algorithms with theoretical guarantees for this combined formulation. The UCB-AD algorithm is a customized adaptation of the model-based UCB framework of Dann and Brunskill (2015), while RFE-β builds directly on the reward-free exploration paradigm of Jin et al. (2020) and the RF-Express algorithm of Ménard et al. (2021). The key methodological departure from prior work is exploiting the monotonicity of the value function in the adherence parameter θ to reduce the non-stationary MDP analysis to a stationary parameter space, yielding tighter sample complexity than generic problem-agnostic RL methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first theoretical foundation for learning when and what to advise in human-AI interactions where the human may ignore recommendations, a practically critical gap as AI systems increasingly operate in advisory rather than autonomous roles. The connection between the penalty-based pertinent advice formulation and constrained MDPs offers a principled way for users to control advice frequency without specifying a Lagrange multiplier. However, empirical validation is limited to simple grid-world environments (Flappy Bird, Car Driving) with a single scalar adherence probability per state-action pair, leaving open how these methods perform with richer, non-stationary, or context-dependent human behavior models and in real-world advisory settings.
