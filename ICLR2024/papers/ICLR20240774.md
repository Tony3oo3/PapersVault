## Problem

Spatio-temporal prediction models are highly sensitive to data sparsity, uneven sensor deployment, and spurious correlations, leading to poor generalization in extreme scenarios and cross-domain transfer. Existing ST architectures are task-specific and lack an interpretable causal mechanism to distinguish genuine predictive signals from environmental confounders, making them brittle under distribution shift.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240774/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of past ST observation frames (e.g., 10 frames of 128×128 or 384×384 radar/traffic/pollution maps) and produces K-step-ahead future frames at the same or higher spatial resolution. Internally, each frame is patchified into non-overlapping tokens for the ViT-based upstream; the output of the Discovery stage is a binary saliency mask over patches (causal vs. environmental), and the Update stage produces augmented sequences stored in a spatio-temporal bank that are retrieved via temporal Gaussian decay sampling for downstream training.

## Method

NuwaDynamics is a two-stage causal framework. In the Discovery stage, an upstream self-supervised ViT reconstruction task computes column-wise attention scores (averaged across heads) to rank patch importance; the least-attended patches are designated as environmental (spurious) patches, while the rest are causal patches. In the Update stage, a DistriXplorer module performs causal intervention on environmental patches via an ST mixup that blends spatially neighboring and temporally historical patches with a geometric decay coefficient, generating multiple deformed copies that simulate broader test distributions. This intervention is formally justified as backdoor adjustment under a Structural Causal Model (X → C̃ → Y ← S), where do-calculus rules eliminate the confounding path S→Y. Downstream, a temporal Gaussian decay sampler retrieves the most influential augmented sequences from the ST bank, enabling both transformer (parameter transfer) and non-transformer (data transfer) backbones to inherit causal perception without architectural modification.

## Results

Across six ST benchmarks and eight backbones, NuwaDynamics consistently reduces MSE by 0.01–6.54 (Transformers) and 0.04–5.51 (non-Transformers), with the largest gains on the PD (pollutant diffusion) dataset where MSE drops from 8.99 to 2.45 (ViT) and from 10.98 to 5.47 (ConvLSTM). On SEVIR, CSI-M improves by ~7.40 for Transformer backbones (e.g., ViT: 37.07→46.88) and ~3.28 for non-Transformers. On KTH, MAE reductions reach 22.45 (ViT) and 36.97 (ConvLSTM), and on the long-range super-resolution task (6→12 frames at 128×128), SSIM gains of 0.56–1.44 are observed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's comparison is ablation-style: each of the eight ST backbones (ViT, SwinT, Rainformer, Earthformer, ConvLSTM, PredRNN-V2, E3D-LSTM, SimVP) is evaluated both without and with NuwaDynamics on the same six datasets. NuwaDynamics wins on every reported metric for every backbone–dataset pair, with no regressions observed. The most dramatic gains appear on PD (MSE reduction of 4.88–6.54 for Transformers, 2.24–5.51 for non-Transformers) and KTH (MAE reduction up to 37.24 for Rainformer). On SEVIR, CSI-M improves by ~7.40 for Transformer backbones (ViT: 37.07→46.88; Rainformer: 36.68→46.88) and ~3.28 for non-Transformers (ConvLSTM: 41.93→44.88). In the transfer-learning setting (RainNet↔SEVIR), SSIM improves by 1.34–7.75, with PredRNN-V2 showing the largest gain (S→R: 64.37→72.12, Δ=7.75). Earthformer consistently benefits most in transfer and super-resolution scenarios, while SimVP maintains the lowest absolute error across most settings both before and after augmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the backdoor adjustment mechanism from causal inference (Pearl, 2009; Arjovsky et al., 2019; Sagawa et al., 2019) into spatio-temporal prediction, where prior causal learning work has focused on class-level or graph-level interventions rather than patch-level ST data. It repurposes Vision Transformer attention maps (Dosovitskiy et al., 2020; Selvaraju et al., 2017) not for token pruning (as in DynamicViT, IARED-2) but for identifying 'causal' versus 'environmental' patches in a self-supervised reconstruction task. The DistriXplorer augmentation via ST mixup and the temporal Gaussian decay sampling for downstream training are novel engineering choices that bridge the gap between causal theory and practical ST forecasting pipelines built on diverse backbones (ConvLSTM, PredRNN, SimVP, Earthformer).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing an interpretable causal lens on ST prediction, NuwaDynamics addresses a persistent pain point in earth sciences: sensor imbalance and data scarcity that cause models to overfit spurious correlations and fail on extreme events (hurricanes, turbulence). The consistent gains across 8 architectures and 6 diverse benchmarks (traffic, human motion, weather, fire, pollution) suggest the causal patch identification is capturing something transferable rather than dataset-specific. Open questions include whether the attention-based 'causal' identification truly isolates causal structure or merely highlights high-salience regions, whether the Mixup-based intervention scales to more complex environmental confounders, and how the framework extends to graph-structured ST data where patch-level interventions are less natural. The authors themselves flag efficiency as a limitation and point toward generative augmentation and LLM-guided descriptions as future directions.
