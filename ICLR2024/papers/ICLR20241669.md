## Problem

Prior thin-shell manipulation research is limited to specific tasks (cloth unfolding, paper flipping) using heuristic policies or real-world demonstrations, while existing simulation benchmarks either ignore bending stiffness and frictional contact or model only cloth-like materials without end-effectors. No differentiable simulation platform existed that supports diverse thin-shell materials with varying bending stiffness and plasticity, coupled with volumetric objects, enabling gradient-based skill learning and evaluation across a broad task range.

## Input / Output

The system consumes 6-DoF or 7-DoF manipulator action sequences (position changes per timestep, bounded to 1–2 mm), material parameters (bending stiffness, friction coefficients, Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio), and scene configurations (vertex positions, rest configurations, manipulator poses). For sim-to-real calibration, it ingests real-world force and tactile sensor readings (dot-matrix displacement, contact force) to back-propagate gradients into material parameters. Outputs include optimized open-loop trajectories, calibrated physical parameters, and per-timestep vertex positions/velocities from the simulation, with observations downsampled to N_d×6 position-velocity pairs plus manipulator poses for RL agents.

## Method

ThinShellLab is a fully differentiable physics engine built in Taichi that simulates thin-shell materials via Kirchhoff-Love shell theory (stretching + bending energy with a yield criterion for bending plasticity), volumetric materials via Stable Neo-Hookean energy, and frictional contact via a smooth IPC-based penalty potential, all integrated through implicit time-stepping solved by Newton's method. Gradients are computed via implicit function differentiation (following DiffPD), back-propagating through the Newton solve at each timestep to yield analytic gradients with respect to manipulator trajectories and material parameters. The key learning insight is a hybrid optimization scheme: CMA-ES (sampling-based) first searches for feasible initial contact configurations to escape the non-smooth landscape caused by rapidly changing contact pairs, then gradient-based optimization (Adam) refines the trajectory for fine-grained control. This hybrid outperforms either method alone across seven manipulation tasks and three inverse design tasks, and the differentiable engine also enables real-to-sim system identification by matching simulated force/tactile outputs to real sensor data via gradient descent on material parameters.

## Results

On the 7 manipulation tasks, the hybrid CMA-ES + GD method achieves the highest maximum reward on 6 out of 7 tasks, with standout scores of 8.24 on Separating, 4.74 on Pick-Folding, 4.68 on Folding-L, and 3.63 on Forming. Pure gradient-based optimization (GD) wins only on Lifting (4.15 vs. 2.51 for CMA-ES+GD), where the complex three-end-effector interaction causes the hybrid to converge to a similar local optimum. Among RL baselines, SAC is the strongest, reaching 7.73 on Separating and 3.49 on Folding-L, but trails the hybrid method on all tasks except Separating where the gap is narrow (7.73 vs. 8.24). On the 3 inverse design tasks, GD achieves comparable or slightly better scores than CMA-ES (e.g., 0.75 vs. 0.62 on Bouncing) with lower variance over time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PPO, CMA-ES+GD dominates on every task: e.g., 4.74 vs. 0.17 on Pick-Folding, 3.81 vs. 1.13 on Folding-U, and 3.43 vs. −0.53 on Following. Against SAC, the hybrid still leads on 6/7 tasks (e.g., 4.68 vs. 3.49 on Folding-L, 4.74 vs. 0.85 on Pick-Folding), with SAC remaining competitive only on Separating (7.73 vs. 8.24). Compared to CMA-ES alone, the hybrid improves substantially on tasks requiring fine-grained control: Pick-Folding jumps from 2.14 to 4.74, Folding-U from 3.22 to 3.81, and Folding-L from 3.93 to 4.68, while CMA-ES retains a slight edge on Separating (8.08 vs. 8.24 is actually a hybrid win) and Forming (3.51 vs. 3.63, hybrid wins). Against pure GD, the hybrid wins on 6/7 tasks (e.g., 3.81 vs. −0.03 on Folding-U, 4.74 vs. 0.03 on Pick-Folding), but GD uniquely solves Lifting (4.15 vs. 2.51) because the hybrid's CMA-ES initialization fails to find a good contact configuration for that task's three-end-effector coordination.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes several established differentiable simulation components—implicit time integration with gradient back-propagation from DiffPD (Du et al., 2021), smooth frictional contact from IPC (Li et al., 2020), Stable Neo-Hookean volumetric energy (Smith et al., 2018), and Kirchhoff-Love shell stretching/bending energies (Grinspun et al., 2003; Tamstorf & Grinspun, 2013)—into the first fully differentiable simulation engine that supports end-effector manipulation of thin-shell materials with bending plasticity. It departs from prior thin-shell simulators (SoftGym, DaxBench) that model only cloths without bending stiffness or frictional contact, and from prior differentiable simulators (DiffCloth, DiffPD) that lack end-effectors and manipulation tasks. The hybrid CMA-ES + gradient-based trajectory optimization scheme is a methodological contribution that addresses the non-smooth optimization landscape caused by frequent contact-pair changes, a challenge not previously characterized in the thin-shell manipulation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ThinShellLab fills a concrete gap by providing the first differentiable benchmark for diverse thin-shell manipulation (papers, cards, sheets with varying bending stiffness and plasticity), enabling gradient-based skill learning that was previously infeasible for these materials. The empirical finding that neither RL nor pure gradient-based optimization suffices—due to frictional dependence, extreme action sensitivity, and contact-pair-induced local optima—provides a useful diagnostic for the deformable manipulation community. However, the sim-to-real transfer remains limited to open-loop trajectory deployment with only tactile/force sensor calibration, and the residual sim-to-real gap from the penalty-based contact model and real-world instability is acknowledged but not resolved. Open questions include whether closed-loop control, visual observations, and learned generalization policies can close this gap, and whether the hybrid optimization paradigm extends to more complex multi-object or long-horizon thin-shell tasks.
