## Problem

The prevailing query-embedding approach for complex query answering on knowledge graphs has been operating under a restricted Tree-Form (TF) query family that is not even a subset of the intended EFO1 (existential first-order, single free variable) family. The paper formally characterizes this gap, showing that TF queries cannot represent EFO1 formulas with existential leaves, multi-edges, or cycles, and that the previously studied queries are actually tractable in O(nk²) time rather than exhibiting the exponential complexity commonly claimed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a knowledge graph of factual triples, an EFO1 query in first-order logic, and a pretrained neural link predictor whose scores are calibrated into |R| truth-value matrices P_r ∈ [0,1]^{|E|×|E|} via softmax with a sparsity-aware scaling factor. The output is an answer vector A[φ(y)] ∈ [0,1]^{|E|} assigning a fuzzy truth value to every entity, which is ranked to compute MRR. Unlike prior methods that only handle operator-tree-structured queries, FIT accepts arbitrary EFO1 formulas including those with cycles and multi-edges, and the neural matrices are fine-tuned end-to-end on complex-query answers rather than one-hop triples.

## Method

FIT (Fuzzy Inference with Truth values) converts each EFO1 query to DNF and represents each conjunctive disjunct as a query graph, then performs a differentiable, step-by-step graph reduction: removing self-loops, constant entities, and leaf nodes while updating per-node fuzzy vectors C_u ∈ [0,1]^{|E|} via t-norm-weighted matrix operations (Gödel t-norm for existential quantification, product t-norm for conjunction). Cyclic subgraphs are handled by enumerating top-M candidates for a chosen node and recursing. Because every operation is differentiable, the entire pipeline back-propagates a cross-entropy loss to fine-tune the pretrained neural link predictor on complex-query data. The paper proves two theoretical guarantees: perfectness (exact answers when matrices are perfect) and faithfulness (all deductible answers receive truth value 1 under a consistency assumption), and shows FIT subsumes QTO as a special case on TF∩EFO1 queries.

## Results

On the newly proposed real EFO1 dataset, FIT achieves average MRR of 23.9% on FB15k-237, 64.8% on FB15k, and 37.3% on NELL, outperforming the strongest baseline QTO (21.8%, 53.4%, 35.3%) by 2.1, 11.4, and 2.0 points respectively. The largest gains appear on multi-graph and cyclic queries (e.g., 3cm: 65.4% vs 54.9% on FB15k; 2m: 73.5% vs 64.6% on FB15k), where previous operator-tree methods cannot represent the correct semantics. On the existing Tree-Form BetaE dataset, FIT also surpasses CQD and LMPNN across all query types, with particularly large improvements on longer-path queries (3p: 56.9% vs 29.7% CQD on FB15k).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FIT outperforms all six baselines (BetaE, LogicE, ConE, QTO, CQD, LMPNN) on every query type and every knowledge graph in the new real EFO1 dataset. The most significant gap is on FB15k where FIT achieves 64.8% average MRR versus QTO's 53.4% (the next best), a 11.4-point margin driven by multi-graph queries (2m: 73.5% vs 64.6%) and cyclic queries (3cm: 65.4% vs 54.9%). On the existing Tree-Form BetaE dataset (Table 2), FIT also surpasses CQD and LMPNN on all positive and negative query types, with the largest advantage on 3p queries (56.9% vs 29.7% CQD on FB15k) and negative queries (35.5% vs 20.9% LMPNN on FB15k). CQD cannot handle negation queries at all, limiting its evaluation to positive queries only. The paper further demonstrates that FIT is a strict superset of QTO: when configured with Godel t-conorm and product t-norm on TF∩EFO1 queries, FIT reproduces QTO's results exactly (Table 4 shows 0% difference), confirming QTO as a special case. On faithfulness (deductible answers), FIT achieves 100% MRR on all positive query types, validating its theoretical guarantee (Theorem 24).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the query-embedding line of work (BetaE, ConE, LogicE, QTO, CQD, LMPNN) that represents logical queries as operator trees with set operations, but fundamentally departs from it by showing that this formulation only captures a restricted family (Tree-Form queries) that is not even a subset of the intended EFO1 target. It draws on classical database theory (Yannakakis, 1981) and constraint satisfaction (Dechter & Pearl, 1987) for the acyclic query evaluation strategy, and on fuzzy logic (t-norms/t-conorms) to provide a principled, differentiable truth-value framework that unifies the neural link predictor with symbolic inference. The paper positions FIT as a strict generalization of QTO, proving they coincide on TF∩EFO1 queries while FIT extends to the full EFO1 family via query graphs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals that the widely-used BetaE benchmark's 'pni' query, when properly converted to DNF, actually contains a universal quantifier—meaning the entire field has been evaluating models on a different (and in some cases logically invalid) formula than intended. This reframes the evaluation landscape and motivates the new 10-type EFO1 dataset covering existential leaves, multi-graphs, and cyclic structures never before tested. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger KGs, how to handle universal quantifiers and more complex query families (EFOk), and whether the fine-tuning signal from complex queries transfers to other downstream tasks. The complexity correction (TF queries are O(nk²), not exponential) also recalibrates expectations for what is tractable.
