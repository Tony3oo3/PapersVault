## Problem

Humanoid control in high-dimensional (56-DOF) action spaces is notoriously difficult due to bipedal instability and the computational cost of learning from scratch, which often yields non-human-like behaviours. Existing approaches leveraging motion-capture data either require costly online interactions for each task or produce task-specific policies with limited transferability. H-GAP addresses the gap of a single, task-agnostic generative model that can be deployed zero-shot across diverse downstream control tasks without any online learning.

## Input / Output

The model takes an initial egocentric proprioceptive state (joint angles, velocities, angular velocities) as its sole conditioning signal and generates complete state-action trajectories of configurable horizon T. Actions are desired joint angles for all 56 degrees of freedom, which are converted to joint torques via pre-defined PD controllers in the MuJoCo simulator. During MPC deployment, a task-specific reward function (e.g., forward velocity, body height) is applied post-hoc to score sampled trajectories, meaning the generative model itself is reward-agnostic and the same weights serve all downstream tasks.

## Method

H-GAP combines a VQ-VAE that discretizes continuous state-action sequences into latent codes (using M=16 codes per L=4-step chunk for fine-grained temporal resolution) with a causal Transformer prior that autoregressively models p(z_i | z_{<i}, s_1), conditioned on the initial state. Unlike TAP, which models state, action, reward, and return, H-GAP models only state-action dynamics, placing primary emphasis on accurate state reconstruction rather than value estimation. At inference, MPC planning samples N=256 trajectories via top-p (ρ=0.99) sampling with elevated temperature (Υ=4) to balance diversity and in-distribution reliability, decodes them through the VQ-VAE, and selects the trajectory maximizing a simple cumulative reward objective. The entire system is trained offline on the 67M-transition MoCapAct dataset with no online interactions, enabling zero-shot transfer to novel tasks by simply swapping the reward function.

## Results

On six downstream humanoid control tasks (speed, rotate y, jump, forward, shift left, backward), H-GAP achieves a mean return of 40.39 (46.18 with reward discounting), outperforming all model-free offline RL baselines (CRR: 7.82, IQL: 15.72, TD3-BC: 32.70) and conventional MPC (MPPI: 12.50, MPPI_μ,σ: 11.43). H-GAP matches or exceeds TAP (43.8) on most tasks, with particularly large margins on speed (83.4 vs 65.5) and rotate y (89.5 vs 44.0), though TAP leads on forward (121.2 vs 51.1) and shift left (27.1 vs 13.2). On the imitation evaluation, H-GAP achieves 46.02±1.57 return and 69.11±2.62 episode length, substantially surpassing an MLP behaviour-cloning baseline (29.38±2.43 return, 49.34±3.91 length).

## Baselines & Comparisons

H-GAP decisively outperforms all model-free offline RL specialists: CRR (mean 7.82), IQL (15.72), and TD3-BC (32.70), with the largest gaps on out-of-distribution tasks like backward and rotate y where these methods struggle. Against the model-based offline RL method TAP (mean 43.8), H-GAP (40.39) is slightly lower on average but wins by wide margins on speed (+17.9) and rotate y (+45.5), while losing on forward (−70.1) and shift left (−13.9); with reward discounting (H-GAP*, 46.18), it surpasses TAP overall. H-GAP also substantially outperforms conventional MPPI (12.50) and its action-prior variant MPPI_μ,σ (11.43) despite MPPI having access to the ground-truth simulator dynamics, attributing the gap to H-GAP's learned latent action space and robust action prior. The MPPI_μ,σ variant, which parameterises planning noise from MoCapAct action statistics, improves over vanilla MPPI on well-represented tasks (speed, forward, jump) but degrades on others, confirming that a single Gaussian prior cannot capture the complex multimodal action distribution of humanoid control.

## Relation to Prior Work

H-GAP extends the trajectory autoencoding planner (TAP) framework by repurposing the VQ-VAE + autoregressive Transformer architecture from a task-specific offline RL method into a task-agnostic generalist model for 56-DOF humanoid control. It draws on the broader line of work discretizing continuous action spaces (Janner et al., 2021; Reed et al., 2022; Brohan et al., 2023) and the MoCapAct dataset (Wagener et al., 2022), but departs from both TAP and conventional MPC (MPPI) by learning a rich latent action prior from diverse MoCap data rather than relying on a ground-truth dynamics model or task-specific reward signals. The paper positions itself as an early foundation-model attempt for humanoid control, analogous to how large language models generalize across NLP tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

H-GAP demonstrates that a single offline-trained generative model can zero-shot adapt to multiple novel humanoid control tasks via MPC, outperforming both task-specific offline RL methods and MPC with access to the true simulator dynamics. The scaling study reveals a non-trivial finding: larger models improve imitation and validation metrics but degrade downstream control performance due to reduced latent-code diversity, suggesting data scale and diversity matter more than parameter count for controllability. Open questions include whether the approach extends to sparse-reward or long-horizon planning, partially observable environments, and whether hierarchical planning or value integration can recover the steerability lost at scale.
