## Problem

Existing multimodal LLMs for medical imaging rely on adapter networks (e.g., Q-former, linear mapping layers) to bridge frozen image encoders and frozen LLMs, creating an information bottleneck that limits vision-language alignment. This is especially problematic for medical images like chest X-rays, where distinguishing subtle findings (e.g., pneumonia vs. pulmonary edema) demands a more intimate mapping between visual and textual features than natural image tasks require. The paper asks whether an LLM can be taught to directly process and generate image tokens within its own architecture, without any separate adapter, to achieve stronger alignment.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 256×256 CXR images that are tokenized into 256 discrete VQ-GAN tokens (a 16×16 grid flattened), which are fed directly into the LLM's expanded embedding table alongside text tokens. Text inputs include free-form radiology reports and natural-language questions, all structured within an Alpaca-style Instruction/Input/Response template. Outputs are either a text radiology report, a sequence of 256 image tokens decoded back into a 256×256 CXR via the frozen VQ-GAN decoder, or a free-text answer to a VQA question — all produced autoregressively by the same LLM.

## Method

The core mechanism is to expand the LLM's token embedding table from K_text to K_text + K_img entries (50,821 → 51,845 for dolly-v2-3b), placing VQ-GAN image tokens in the same embedding space as text tokens so the LLM processes both modalities natively with no adapter network or structural modification. A frozen VQ-GAN (trained with an added 1024-dim clinical information-preserving L2 loss from TorchXRayVision's DenseNet-121 features) handles all image encoding/decoding. The LLM is instruction-fine-tuned on four task types — natural-language instruction-following, report-to-CXR generation, CXR-to-report generation, and CXR-VQA — using a conditional loss applied only to Response tokens, which the authors argue mitigates overfitting compared to the joint loss used in non-LLM VLP transformers. Training follows a two-stage schedule: Stage 1 trains on the full MIMIC-CXR distribution (76k steps) to learn the image-token space, then Stage 2 refines alignment on a pruned subset of frontal-view, first-study impression sections (9k steps). Synthetic VQA pairs generated by LLaMA-2 (~127k question-answer pairs) augment the training data to strengthen vision-language alignment and preserve language capabilities.

## Results

On CXR-to-report generation (MIMIC-CXR-JPG, 256×256), LLM-CXR achieves a Micro AUROC of 0.628 and Weighted F1 of 0.350, outperforming XrayGPT (0.617 AUROC, 0.330 F1) and UniXGen-256 (0.575 AUROC, 0.243 F1) at the same resolution, while remaining competitive with higher-resolution models UniXGen-512 (0.668 AUROC) and RadFM (0.638 AUROC). On CXR-VQA, LLM-CXR reaches 44.8% overall accuracy, substantially exceeding XrayGPT (25.2%) and RadFM (32.7%), though trailing ELIXR (54.8%) which uses PaLM-2. For report-to-CXR generation, LLM-CXR achieves the best FID (22.75 Inception / 0.714 TXV) and highest Weighted AUROC (0.799) and F1 (0.805) among all compared methods, surpassing UniXGen (FID 78.19) and RoentGen (FID 42.38).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLM-CXR (3B parameters) outperforms XrayGPT (based on Vicuna-7B) on CXR-to-report generation at comparable resolution, achieving 0.628 vs. 0.617 Micro AUROC and 0.350 vs. 0.330 Weighted F1, while also surpassing UniXGen-256 (0.575 AUROC) and the non-LLM baselines IFCC and R2Gen by a wide margin. On CXR-VQA, LLM-CXR (44.8%) more than doubles XrayGPT's accuracy (25.2%) and substantially exceeds RadFM (32.7%), though it remains below ELIXR (54.8%) which leverages the much larger PaLM-2 backbone. For report-to-CXR generation, LLM-CXR dominates both UniXGen and RoentGen across all metrics: it achieves 0.799 Weighted AUROC vs. 0.752 (UniXGen) and 0.706 (RoentGen), 0.805 Weighted F1 vs. 0.708 and 0.630, and a dramatically lower FID of 22.75 (Inception) / 0.714 (TXV) vs. 78.19/7.894 (UniXGen) and 42.38/6.039 (RoentGen). The paper notes that LLM-CXR is the only model capable of performing all three tasks (CXR-to-report, report-to-CXR, and CXR-VQA) within a single architecture, whereas each baseline is designed for only a subset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant adapter-network paradigm for multimodal LLMs (BLIP-2's Q-former, Flamingo's cross-attention, XrayGPT's linear mapping layer) by instead directly expanding the LLM's token embedding space to include VQ-GAN image tokens, treating them identically to text tokens. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate lines of work: the non-LLM vision-language pretraining approaches that tokenize images with VQ-GAN and generate joint image-text sequences (UniXGen, Unified-IO, OFA, ERNIE-ViLG), and the instruction-tuning methodology for LLMs (Alpaca, Dolly). The key departure is that rather than training a transformer from scratch on joint image-text data, the paper applies instruction fine-tuning to an already-trained instruction-following LLM (dolly-v2-3b), adding bidirectional multimodal capability without any structural modification or additional network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 3B-parameter model can match or exceed substantially larger multimodal LLMs (7B XrayGPT, PaLM-2-based ELIXR) on CXR understanding and generation tasks, suggesting that the adapter-network bottleneck may be a limiting factor in vision-language alignment for medical imaging. However, significant limitations remain: generated reports still contain false positives and missed diagnoses, inference latency is 30–60 seconds for image generation, and the 256×256 resolution constrains clinical utility. Open questions include whether the instruction-tuning-with-expanded-token-space approach generalizes to other medical imaging modalities or larger LLMs, whether dynamic tokenization can address the latency problem, and whether longitudinal patient data can reduce hallucinations.
