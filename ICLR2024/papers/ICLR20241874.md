## Problem

Codebook-based DNN memory compression suffers from two key limitations: (1) a granularity problem where a single codebook per tensor limits expressiveness, while naive multi-codebook schemes incur prohibitive mapping overhead, and (2) standard gradient descent on the weight-to-codebook mapping favors jumps toward extreme codebook values rather than proximal ones, making joint optimization of codebooks and mappings infeasible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241874/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained DNN's fp16 weight tensors and, in the data-driven variant, a small calibration set from the original training distribution. The single hyperparameter α (compression goal) determines codebook size, number of codebooks or scale factors, and the resulting bit-width per weight. The output is a compressed model where each weight is stored as a compact index into a codebook, with the codebook assignment implicitly determined by the (reordered) row index of the weight, eliminating any per-weight codebook-index storage overhead.

## Method

JLCM first reorders neurons within each layer using hierarchical clustering on their weight distributions, so that similarly-distributed neurons become contiguous in memory; this enables assigning distinct codebooks (for dense/transformer layers) or per-group scale factors (for convolutional layers) to contiguous row blocks without any extra index bits. From this initialization, the method jointly optimizes codebook values C and soft mapping indices I via sequential, layer-wise gradient descent on a self-distillation loss (mimicking AdaRound's GPTQ framework), regularized by a weight-reconstruction term and a hard-assignment sharpness penalty. The critical technical contribution is a custom gradient update for the mapping I, inspired by straight-through estimation: instead of the standard chain-rule gradient (which scales updates by codebook values and thus favors extreme entries), JLCM uses a proximity-based function D that assigns larger gradient magnitudes to codebook entries close to the currently assigned value, enabling a proximal search that makes joint codebook-mapping optimization stable and effective.

## Results

On ImageNet, JLCM achieves 62.94% top-1 accuracy on ResNet-18 at α=5.33 (W3/A16 equivalent), outperforming the previous best data-driven method BrecQ (52.94%) by 10 points, and 80.56% on ViT-B/16 at the same compression rate (vs. NUPES 77.23%). At the more aggressive α=7.5, JLCM retains 41.78% on ResNet-18 and 69.99% on ViT-B/16. On Llama 7B common-sense reasoning (original score 56.14), JLCM at α=5.33 scores 55.94, surpassing OPTQ (49.94) and SqueezeLLM (48.17) at comparable or higher compression rates, and JLCM+REx at α=6.95 recovers 56.00 (99.7% of original).

## Baselines & Comparisons

JLCM decisively outperforms all data-free baselines (DFQ, SQuant, PowerQuant, RED++) on both ResNet-18 and ViT-B/16, with gaps of 10–60+ points at α=5.33. Against data-driven GPTQ methods, JLCM beats AdaRound by 43.3 points on ResNet-18 and 19.4 points on ViT-B/16 at α=5.33. Among the strongest competitors, JLCM surpasses BrecQ by 10.0 points (ResNet-18) and 7.4 points (ViT-B/16), and NUPES by 9.9 and 3.3 points respectively. On Llama 7B (Table 6b), JLCM at α=5.33 scores 55.94 vs. OPTQ's 49.94 (α=5.33), AWQ's 47.68 (α=5.8), and SqueezeLLM's 48.17 (α=5.8), achieving higher accuracy at equal or greater compression. JLCM+REx at α=6.95 recovers 56.00 (99.7% of the original 56.14), matching full-precision performance at a 31% higher compression rate than OPTQ.

## Relation to Prior Work

JLCM builds directly on the sequential, layer-by-layer optimization framework of AdaRound (Nagel et al., 2020) for gradient-based post-training quantization, but repurposes it to jointly optimize codebook values and weight mappings rather than rounding decisions. It departs from prior codebook-based compression methods (Chen et al., 2015; Han et al., 2015; Eban et al., 2020) by solving the granularity problem through neuron reordering that eliminates per-weight codebook index overhead, and by introducing a straight-through-estimation-inspired gradient term that replaces the standard chain-rule update for mapping indices. The method thus sits at the intersection of clustering-based compression, GPTQ-style calibration, and STE, combining elements that had not previously been unified for memory-footprint-focused compression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: compressing Llama 7B to ~2 GB while retaining 95% of common-sense reasoning performance opens the door to running frontier LLMs on commodity smartphones (iPhone 8, Galaxy A10), a deployment scenario previously infeasible. The ablation revealing that standard gradient updates actively degrade joint codebook-mapping learning (Table 4) is a useful diagnostic for anyone attempting similar joint optimization. Open questions include whether the neuron-reordering assumption (similarly distributed neurons cluster contiguously) holds universally across architectures, how codebook-based inference integrates with existing hardware accelerators, and whether the method extends to activation compression or mixed-precision settings without the current fp16 activation assumption.
