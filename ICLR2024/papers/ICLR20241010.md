## Problem

Existing data poisoning attacks for DNNs optimize poisoning samples against a single re-trained model, but the re-training process introduces uncertainty (different initializations, architectures, and algorithms) that can cause the model to converge to a very different point where the poisoning effect is lost. Prior solutions like Ensemble and Re-initialization (E&R) mitigate this by averaging over multiple models but are computationally expensive and cannot fully cover the re-training space. The paper asks whether a principled, lightweight strategy can make poisoning robust to re-training uncertainty without ensembling.

## Input / Output

The attacker provides a clean training set (CIFAR-10/100, SVHN at 32×32 RGB), a pre-trained surrogate model, a victim set (one or more target images for targeted attacks, a victim class for backdoor attacks, or the full training set for untargeted attacks), and constraints specifying the poisoning ratio (0.2%–100%) and an l∞ perturbation budget (4/255, 8/255, or 16/255). The output is a set of imperceptibly perturbed training images that, when the victim model is retrained from scratch on the poisoned dataset, cause misclassification of the victim set (targeted/backdoor) or a drop in overall test accuracy (untargeted).

## Method

SAPA replaces the standard adversarial objective Q(θ*; D_T) in existing poisoning attacks with a sharpness-aware adversarial objective max_{||v||_p≤ρ} Q(θ*+v; D_T), which approximates the worst poisoning effect within a ball of radius ρ around the ERM solution. The worst direction v̂ is computed via a first-order approximation (v̂ = ρ·sign(∇Q)·|∇Q|^{q-1}·||∇Q||_q^{q/p}, with p=2), and the gradient of the sharpness-aware objective is evaluated at θ*+v̂. This drop-in substitution is applied to Gradient Matching (for targeted and backdoor attacks via Sleeper Agent) and to Error-Min/Error-Max (for untargeted attacks), where it adds a single 'find worst direction' step between model updates and perturbation updates. The approach requires no additional attacker knowledge beyond what the backbone attack already uses, and with ρ=0.05 it consistently improves success rates across re-training algorithms (SGD, ADAM, Cut-Out, Mix-Up, SAM), architectures (ResNet, VGG, MobileNetV2, ViT), and schedules, while remaining far more efficient than E&R.

## Results

On targeted attacks (ResNet18, CIFAR-10, 16/255, ε=1%), SAPA+Grad-Match achieves 80.1% success rate, outperforming Grad-Match (72.9%) and Meta-Poison (42.5%). On backdoor attacks (CIFAR-10, ResNet18, 16/255), SAPA+Sleeper Agent reaches 98.1% ASR versus 90.9% for Sleeper Agent alone. For un-targeted attacks (CIFAR-10, ResNet18, 100% poisoning), Error-Min+SAPA reduces test accuracy to 10.9% compared to 21.2% for Error-Min alone, approaching the 11.8% of Autoregressive Perturbation. The advantage is most pronounced under constrained budgets (8/255 or 4/255) and smaller poisoning ratios (0.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAPA consistently outperforms all 11 baselines across targeted, backdoor, and un-targeted attacks. Against the strongest targeted-attack baseline Grad-Match, SAPA gains +7.2 pp on CIFAR-10 (80.1% vs 72.9%) and +24.7 pp on CIFAR-100 at 8/255 (86.8% vs 62.1%), with the gap widening under tighter budgets (4/255: 16.7% vs 10.3% on CIFAR-10). Feature-collision methods (Bullseye, Poison-Frog) are essentially ineffective in the end-to-end setting (≤4% success rate), confirming their reliance on transfer-learning assumptions. For backdoor attacks, SAPA+Sleeper Agent beats Sleeper Agent by +7.2 pp on CIFAR-10 (98.1% vs 90.9%) and +15.1 pp on CIFAR-100 at 8/255 (41.8% vs 26.7%), while Hidden-Trigger and Clean-Label remain below 5% due to their design for transfer learning or controlled training. In un-targeted attacks, Error-Min+SAPA halves the test accuracy of Error-Min (10.9% vs 21.2% on CIFAR-10 at 100% poisoning) and matches Autoregressive Perturbation (11.8%), a method specifically designed for CNN vulnerabilities. SAPA also maintains its advantage under re-training variations (Mix-Up, SAM, different architectures like ViT and MobileNetV2) and outperforms the Ensemble & Re-initialization strategy in both effectiveness and computational efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAPA repurposes the loss landscape sharpness concept from Sharpness-Aware Minimization (Foret et al., 2020) — previously used to improve generalization — as a mechanism for data poisoning attacks, creating a conceptual bridge between two seemingly unrelated areas. It is built directly on Gradient Matching (Geiping et al., 2020) for targeted/backdoor attacks and on Error-Min/Error-Max (Huang et al., 2021; Fowl et al., 2021) for untargeted attacks, replacing their adversarial objectives with a sharpness-aware variant that approximates the worst-case model in the poisoned model space. This departs from the Ensemble-and-Re-initialization strategy (Huang et al., 2020), which handles re-training uncertainty by averaging over multiple surrogate models, by instead using a single local perturbation to capture the 'worst' re-trained model more efficiently.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAPA provides a general, lightweight plug-in strategy that consistently improves multiple existing poisoning attacks under re-training uncertainty (different architectures, optimizers, schedules) without requiring additional attacker knowledge, making it practically attractive for threat modeling. However, the formulation only locally searches within the same architecture as the surrogate model, and the paper acknowledges that extension to other domains (text, graphs, self-supervised learning) remains open. The efficiency advantage over ensemble-based methods is clear but the absolute improvements over already-strong baselines like Grad-Match and Sleeper Agent are moderate, suggesting the approach is a useful refinement rather than a fundamental breakthrough in poisoning attack design.
