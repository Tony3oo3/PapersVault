## Problem

In diffusion-based image editing, DDIM inversion introduces perturbations that degrade both content preservation and edit fidelity. Prior optimization-based inversion methods (e.g., Null-Text Inversion, StyleDiffusion) attempt to correct this by learning a single latent shared across both the source and target diffusion branches, but this is slow (148–383 s per image), suboptimal due to limited optimization iterations, and disrupts the pretrained model's input distribution. The paper challenges the prevailing assumption that such optimization is necessary, showing that the source and target branches should instead be disentangled so each can fulfill its distinct role.

## Input / Output

The method is a plug-and-play correction inserted into the forward diffusion loop of any existing editing pipeline (e.g., Prompt-to-Prompt, MasaCtrl, Pix2Pix-Zero, Plug-and-Play), requiring only 3 additional lines of code. It operates in the latent space of Stable Diffusion (v1.4/v1.5) at 50 diffusion steps, taking a source image, a source prompt, and a target prompt as input. The output is a pair: a reconstruction image from the source branch (used to verify content preservation) and an edited image from the target branch, with the correction applied exclusively to the source branch's latent at every denoising step.

## Method

PnP Inversion disentangles the source and target diffusion branches, assigning each a distinct responsibility: the source branch handles essential content preservation while the target branch handles edit fidelity. At each forward diffusion step, the method computes a correction term d_t = z_t^{I_p} − z_{t−1}^{F_sg} (the difference between the DDIM Inversion latent and the DDIM forward latent conditioned on the source prompt) and adds it only to the source branch's latent, directly rectifying the deviation path introduced by DDIM inversion and classifier-free guidance. The target branch is left completely unaltered, preserving the diffusion model's full generative capacity for the target prompt. This eliminates the need for any per-image optimization, reduces inference time to 28.17 s (vs. 148.48 s for Null-Text Inversion), and avoids distorting the model's input distribution. The paper also introduces PIE-Bench, a 700-image benchmark with 10 editing types and hand-annotated editing masks, to systematically evaluate both content preservation and edit fidelity.

## Results

On PIE-Bench (700 images, 10 editing types), PnP Inversion combined with Prompt-to-Prompt achieves a Structure Distance of 11.65×10⁻³ (83% improvement over DDIM Inversion's 69.43×10⁻³), background PSNR of 27.22 dB, and Edit Region CLIP Similarity of 22.10, outperforming Null-Text Inversion (13.44, 27.03, 21.86) on all metrics. PnP Inversion also improves MasaCtrl (Structure Distance 24.70 vs 28.38), Pix2Pix-Zero (49.22 vs 61.68), and Plug-and-Play (24.29 vs 28.22) over their DDIM Inversion baselines. Inference time is 28.17s per image, compared to 148.48s for Null-Text Inversion and 382.98s for StyleDiffusion — nearly an order-of-magnitude speedup over optimization-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PnP Inversion outperforms DDIM Inversion on every metric across all four editing methods (P2P, MasaCtrl, P2P-Zero, PnP), with the largest gains in Structure Distance (up to 83% reduction) and background preservation (up to 85% MSE reduction). Against Null-Text Inversion (NT), PnP Inversion wins on Structure Distance (11.65 vs 13.44), all four background preservation metrics, and both CLIP similarity scores, while also being 5.3× faster (28.17s vs 148.48s). Compared to StyleDiffusion, PnP Inversion achieves better background preservation (PSNR 27.22 vs 26.05, LPIPS 54.55 vs 66.10) and CLIP similarity, at 13.6× lower inference cost. Against content-preservation-focused methods (Proximal Guidance, Edit Friendly DDPM, EDICT), PnP Inversion is the only method that simultaneously improves both background preservation and edit fidelity (CLIP similarity), whereas all others sacrifice editability (e.g., EDICT drops Edit CLIP from 21.86 to 20.32). Versus model-based editing (InstructPix2Pix, InstructDiffusion), PnP Inversion + P2P achieves substantially better Structure Distance (11.65 vs 57.91/75.44) and background PSNR (27.22 vs 20.82/20.28). Blended Latent Diffusion, which uses ground-truth masks, achieves higher background PSNR (29.13) and CLIP (25.72/23.56) but has a much worse Structure Distance (81.42) due to forced editing boundaries, and requires explicit mask input unavailable in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DDIM inversion framework (Song et al., 2020) and the two-branch (source/target) architecture used by diffusion-based editing methods like Prompt-to-Prompt, MasaCtrl, and Pix2Pix-Zero. It departs from the prevailing optimization-based inversion paradigm (Null-Text Inversion, StyleDiffusion) by arguing that the learned latent's forced assignment to both branches is fundamentally suboptimal, and instead proposes a non-optimization correction that disentangles the two branches. The conceptual lineage also connects to non-optimization alternatives like Negative-Prompt Inversion and Edit Friendly DDPM, which the paper shows sacrifice edit fidelity, positioning PnP Inversion as a middle ground that preserves both.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The disentanglement insight—that the source branch should be corrected for preservation while the target branch should remain untouched for edit fidelity—provides a clean conceptual framework that simplifies a previously optimization-heavy pipeline into three lines of code with a 5–13× speedup over Null-Text Inversion and StyleDiffusion. PIE-Bench (700 images, 10 editing types, hand-annotated masks) fills a real gap in standardized evaluation for diffusion-based editing. However, the method inherits the instability and limited success rate of underlying editing models, and the performance gains over the best optimization-based baselines (Null-Text Inversion) are modest in several metrics, leaving open whether more fundamental improvements to editing algorithms are needed.
