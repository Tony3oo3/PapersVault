## Problem

Editing real images with text-guided diffusion models suffers from reconstruction-path divergence caused by classifier-free guidance, which degrades editing fidelity. Existing fixes like Null-text Inversion (NTI) correct this via per-timestep optimization of a 1D null-text embedding, but this is computationally expensive and cannot capture the spatial context of the input image. Optimization-free alternatives (NPI, ProxNPI) reduce cost but still fail to preserve spatial structure, limiting their effectiveness for spatially demanding edits.

## Input / Output

The method takes a real RGB image and a text prompt describing the desired edit, operating entirely in the latent space of Stable Diffusion with T=50 diffusion steps. It produces a single edited image that preserves the spatial layout of the input while applying the textual modification. NMG is designed as a drop-in replacement for the reconstruction path within existing inversion-based editing frameworks (Prompt-to-Prompt, MasaCtrl, pix2pix-zero), so the output format and editing semantics are determined by the paired editing method rather than by NMG itself.

## Method

NMG exploits the noise maps—the latent variables {z*_t} obtained from DDIM inversion—as a spatially rich conditioning signal, since they are noisy versions of the input image and retain its spatial structure. To incorporate this conditioning without optimization, the paper reformulates the reverse process using energy guidance (EGSDE), defining an energy function E(z_t, c, t) = ||z'_{t-1} - z*_{t-1}||_1 that measures the L1 divergence between the current denoising step and the corresponding noise map. The reverse process is then executed in two sequential stages: first, a noise-map-conditioned denoising step (with guidance scale s_N) pulls the latent toward the inversion trajectory, and second, a text-conditioned denoising step (with guidance scale s_T) applies the semantic edit. A gradient scale s_g modulates how strongly the energy gradient corrects the trajectory. The entire procedure is training-free and requires no per-timestep optimization, achieving ~20× speedup over NTI while matching or exceeding its reconstruction and editing quality.

## Results

NMG achieves the highest CLIPScore across all three editing tasks: 0.3007 (P2P Local), 0.3221 (P2P Global), and 0.2955 (MasaCtrl), outperforming all baselines. On TIFA, NMG leads in P2P Global (0.8991) and MasaCtrl (0.8548) but trails NTI (0.9125) on P2P Local (0.8955). In a user study with 50 participants, NMG is preferred 72.5% of the time, far exceeding NTI (10.0%), NPI (5.0%), and ProxNPI (12.5%). For reconstruction, NMG matches NTI's quality (MSE 0.0124 vs 0.0127) while running ~17× faster (5.97s vs 104.85s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NMG outperforms all four baselines on CLIPScore for every editing task, with the largest margins on P2P Global (+0.0115 over NTI) and MasaCtrl (+0.0052 over ProxNPI). On TIFA, NMG wins decisively on P2P Global (0.8991 vs. 0.8463 for ProxNPI, the next best) and MasaCtrl (0.8548 vs. 0.8277 for NTI), but NTI retains a narrow edge on P2P Local TIFA (0.9125 vs. 0.8955). In the user study, NMG is overwhelmingly preferred at 72.5%, compared to 10–12.5% for NTI and ProxNPI and only 5% for NPI, indicating that automated metrics understate NMG's perceptual advantage. On reconstruction (Table 2), NMG matches NTI's MSE (0.0124 vs. 0.0127) and SSIM (0.7296 vs. 0.7292) while being roughly 17× faster (5.97s vs. 104.85s), confirming that the optimization-free design does not sacrifice quality. ProxNPI, which also uses noise maps, achieves competitive reconstruction (MSE 0.012) but its proximal guidance applied to the editing path constrains editing flexibility, resulting in lower CLIP scores and limited style-transfer capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

NMG sits at the intersection of two lineages: DDIM inversion (Song et al., 2020) for encoding real images into latent noise, and energy guidance (Zhao et al., 2022) for flexible conditioning of diffusion reverse processes. It departs from the NTI→NPI→ProxNPI progression, which all operate on a 1D null-text embedding to correct reconstruction divergence, by instead using the full spatial noise maps (the DDIM inversion latents themselves) as the conditioning signal. This reframes the problem from 'optimize a scalar vector to align paths' to 'condition the reverse process directly on the spatially-structured inversion trajectory,' eliminating per-timestep optimization entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20× speedup over NTI while matching reconstruction quality makes high-fidelity real-image editing substantially more practical, and the spatial-context preservation addresses a concrete failure mode (loss of layout/structure) that plagues existing inversion methods. However, NMG inherits the limitations of inversion-based editing: it cannot perform precise spatial operations (e.g., removing a specific individual or placing an object at an exact location), and it is incompatible with non-inversion editing paradigms like SGC-Net. Open questions include whether the noise-map conditioning can be extended to video or 3D editing, and whether the sequential dual-conditioning (noise map then text) can be made more principled rather than empirically tuned.
