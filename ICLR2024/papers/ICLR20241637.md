## Problem

Explicit NeRF models (using voxel grids or neural points) balloon to 100+ MB, making storage and transmission impractical, yet prior compression work treated this as a model-compression problem (pruning, vector quantization) while overlooking the spatial redundancy inherent in the 3D geometry structure and textured latent features. The paper reformulates explicit NeRF compression as a 3D data compression task and also highlights that existing methods neglect encoding-time budgets critical for real-world deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241637/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained explicit NeRF model (DVGO with two voxel grids or PointNeRF with neural points) and produces a compressed bitstream containing octree-encoded positions, RAHT-transformed and entropy-coded features, importance masks, a learned entropy model, the NeRF MLPs, and metadata. Decoding reconstructs the full explicit NeRF model on the receiver side, with decoding overhead under 1 second for the inverse RAHT and entropy decoding steps.

## Method

ACRF compresses the explicit 3D representation through three stages: (1) view-independent pruning that scores voxels by the maximum absolute value of their feature tensor multiplied by sampled-point importance, avoiding the need for training camera poses; (2) importance-guided feature encoding that splits points into independent sets via importance-threshold masks, applies RAHT (Region Adaptive Hierarchical Transform) to each set with set-specific quantization factors, and transmits the masks via zip compression; (3) importance-prioritized entropy modeling that learns independent deep entropy models per importance group to minimize KL divergence between the true coefficient distribution and the estimated one, followed by arithmetic coding. Two variants are provided: ACRF jointly fine-tunes the NeRF and entropy model with a combined rendering + cross-entropy loss for best compression, while ACRF-F fixes the NeRF and only fits the entropy model to the coefficient distribution, achieving compression in under 4 seconds.

## Results

On the Synthetic-NeRF dataset (voxel-based, DVGO), ACRF achieves a better rate-distortion curve than VQRF, and ACRF-F outperforms VQRF-F, with all methods reaching compression ratios up to ~100×. On Tanks&Temples (point-based, PointNeRF), ACRF achieves a 10× compression ratio with negligible PSNR degradation. In terms of encoding speed, ACRF-F compresses a NeRF model in ~3.9 s (both Synthetic-NeRF and Tanks&Temples), compared to 4 min 28 s for VQRF and 5 min 10 s for VQRF-F on Synthetic-NeRF, and 9 min 14 s for VQRF on Tanks&Temples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is VQRF (Li et al., CVPR 2023), a volumetric NeRF compression method combining voxel pruning, vector quantization, and fine-tuning. ACRF outperforms VQRF on both Synthetic-NeRF (voxel-based) and Tanks&Temples (point-based) in rate-distortion performance, attributed to the importance-guided RAHT feature encoding and the importance-prioritized entropy model replacing VQRF's vector quantization. ACRF also completes the full compression pipeline faster than VQRF (3 min 55 s vs. 4 min 28 s on Synthetic-NeRF), because RAHT transform takes only 0.5 s versus 40 s for vector quantization, and the joint entropy-minimization learning converges faster than VQRF's fine-tuning. The fast variant ACRF-F dramatically outperforms VQRF-F in encoding time (3.9 s vs. 1 min 6 s on Synthetic-NeRF, 3.9 s vs. 5 min 10 s on Tanks&Temples) while delivering competitive compression quality, thanks to the view-independent pruning strategy that eliminates the need for training camera poses. The key advantage of ACRF-F over VQRF-F is the view-independent pruning (2.2 s vs. 26 s on Synthetic, 2.3 s vs. 4 min 31 s on Tanks&Temples), which removes the dependency on rendered transmittance and blending weights.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the VQRF line of work (Li et al., 2023) for volumetric NeRF compression, which introduced voxel pruning and vector quantization, and on the RAHT wavelet transform (De Queiroz & Chou, 2016) from the 3D point cloud compression literature. The key conceptual departure is reformulating explicit NeRF compression as a 3D data compression problem rather than a model compression problem: instead of pruning voxels and quantizing features in-place, the paper converts the explicit representation into a point cloud and applies the full 3D data compression pipeline (octree position coding, RAHT feature transform, and learned entropy coding). The view-independent pruning strategy and importance-guided feature encoding are novel contributions that address practical limitations of prior view-dependent approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Explicit NeRF representations (DVGO, PointNeRF) are becoming the standard for fast rendering, but their large model sizes (100+ MB) are a significant barrier to deployment and transmission. The ACRF-F variant achieving competitive compression in under 4 seconds (vs. ~9 minutes for VQRF) is a practical contribution that makes NeRF compression viable for real-world workflows. Open questions include extending the framework to dynamic scenes, streaming compression, and whether the importance-based splitting strategy generalizes to other explicit representations beyond voxel grids and point clouds. The paper is limited to two datasets and offline compression, leaving scalability to larger, more complex scenes untested.
