## Problem

Existing imbalanced learning methods are designed for categorical label spaces and do not extend to continuous regression labels, where minority sample features collapse into majority neighbours in feature space. Prior regression-specific approaches either capture only local label dependencies (LDS, FDS) or assume a linear order structure (RankSim) that fails on high-dimensional or non-ordinal label spaces such as depth maps. The paper asks whether inter-label relationships of arbitrary complexity can be faithfully transferred to the feature space without such restrictive assumptions.

## Input / Output

ConR consumes standard RGB images (face crops for age and gaze estimation, indoor scene images for depth estimation) and produces continuous regression targets: a scalar age in years, a 114×152 depth map, or a 2D gaze direction vector. Each input is augmented (random crop, flip, rotation, colour jitter) to produce two views per sample, and the method operates on the encoder features of these augmented pairs. The label space is continuous and potentially high-dimensional, with empirically imbalanced bin densities ranging from 1 to over 7,000 samples per bin.

## Method

ConR is a contrastive regularizer built on infoNCE but adapted for multi-positive pairs in continuous label space. For each augmented sample, it dynamically selects anchors—those with at least one negative pair, where a negative pair has a dissimilar label but a similar prediction—thereby targeting regions of feature-space collapse. Positive pairs (similar labels) are pulled together, while negative pairs are pushed away with a weight S_{j,q} that is proportional to the anchor's label-density weight η_j (so minority anchors push harder) and inversely proportional to the label similarity between anchor and negative sample. The total loss is a weighted sum of a standard regression loss (MAE or RMSE) and the ConR contrastive term, and the regularizer is orthogonal to existing imbalanced-learning baselines (LDS, FDS, RankSim, Balanced MSE). The paper further derives an upper bound showing that minimizing the ConR loss reduces the probability of minority-sample collapse, weighted by the severity of the mislabelling.

## Results

On AgeDB-DIR, the full combination LDS+FDS+RankSim+ConR achieves the best MAE of 6.81 (All) and 9.21 (Few), outperforming the same combination without ConR (7.03 / 9.92). On NYUD2-DIR (high-dimensional depth maps), Balanced MSE+LDS+ConR reaches RMSE 1.265 (All) and 1.667 (Few), while RankSim is inapplicable. On MPIIGaze-DIR (2D gaze), Balanced MSE+ConR achieves the lowest Mean Angle Error of 5.63° (All) and 5.21° (Few). ConR adds only ~1–2 s of training overhead on AgeDB-DIR and ~0.2 min on NYUD2-DIR relative to vanilla regression, making it comparable in cost to LDS and RankSim.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConR consistently improves every baseline it is combined with across all four benchmarks. Against LDS (Yang et al., 2021), adding ConR reduces MAE by 2.58–6.55% on AgeDB-DIR and 3.67–1.21% on IMDB-WIKI-DIR, with the largest gains on the few-shot region (10.78% GM improvement on AgeDB-DIR). Against FDS (Yang et al., 2021), ConR yields 3.07–5.47% MAE reduction on AgeDB-DIR and 9.92–11.37% RMSE reduction on NYUD2-DIR, where FDS is the strongest baseline. Against RankSim (Gong et al., 2022), ConR provides 1.62–3.49% MAE improvement on AgeDB-DIR; notably, RankSim is inapplicable to depth and gaze estimation due to its reliance on linear order, whereas ConR handles these high-dimensional label spaces seamlessly. Against Balanced MSE (Ren et al., 2022), ConR reduces RMSE by 2.52–18.42% on NYUD2-DIR and Mean Angle Error by 1.75–5.05% on MPIIGaze-DIR. Against MoCo V1/V2 (He et al., 2020; Chen et al., 2020) used as unsupervised contrastive regularizers, ConR outperforms both on the few-shot region (11.10% MAE improvement over MoCo V1 on AgeDB-DIR few-shot), confirming that incorporating label-aware supervision into the contrastive objective is critical for imbalanced regression.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConR adapts the infoNCE contrastive objective (Oord et al., 2018) and its multi-positive extension from supervised contrastive learning (Khosla et al., 2020b) to the continuous-label regression setting, where predefined class anchors and prototype-based negatives are infeasible. It departs from prior imbalanced regression methods (LDS, FDS, RankSim, Balanced MSE) by not relying on label-space binning or order assumptions, instead using dynamic anchor selection driven by prediction-label disagreement and relative pushing weighted by label similarity and density. The method is explicitly positioned as orthogonal to these baselines, functioning as a regularizer that can be stacked on top of any existing DIR technique.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ConR fills a practical gap for imbalanced regression on high-dimensional or non-ordinal label spaces (e.g., depth maps, 2D gaze vectors) where order-based methods like RankSim are inapplicable, and where binning-based local-smoothing methods (LDS, FDS) require domain-specific heuristics. The theoretical upper bound linking the contrastive loss to the probability of minority-sample collapse provides a principled justification beyond empirical gains. Open questions include sensitivity to the similarity threshold ω and pushing power η (which require per-task tuning), whether the method extends to truly unstructured label spaces beyond the four benchmarks tested, and how it interacts with newer probabilistic or uncertainty-based imbalanced regression approaches.
