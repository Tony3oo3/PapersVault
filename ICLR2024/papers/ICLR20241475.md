## Problem

Scientific knowledge stored in PDFs loses critical semantic information, particularly for mathematical expressions where relative character positions (superscripts, subscripts, fractions) are essential. Existing OCR engines process text line-by-line and cannot capture these spatial relationships, while tools like GROBID store equations only as Unicode strings without recoverable LaTeX structure. This leaves millions of academic papers effectively inaccessible to machine processing.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single rasterized document page image at 96 DPI, resized and padded to a fixed 896×672 resolution (aspect ratio between US Letter and A4), with no OCR text, bounding boxes, or embedded PDF text as auxiliary input. It produces a lightweight markup language supporting headings, bold/italic text, algorithms, LaTeX inline and display math, and LaTeX tables, generated auto-regressively up to 4096 tokens per page. Because the input is purely visual, the method generalizes to scanned documents and books, not just digital-born PDFs.

## Method

Nougat is an end-to-end encoder-decoder transformer built on the Donut architecture, requiring no external OCR module. The encoder is a Swin Transformer that splits the page image into non-overlapping windows and aggregates spatial information via hierarchical self-attention, producing patch embeddings; the decoder is a 10-layer mBART transformer with cross-attention that generates markup tokens auto-regressively, using the Galactica tokenizer specialized for scientific text. The full model has 350M parameters (base) or 250M (small variant with 4 decoder layers). Training uses AdamW over 3 epochs with a decaying learning rate schedule, and data augmentation simulates scanning imperfections (erosion, Gaussian noise, blur, compression, elastic distortion) to improve robustness on out-of-domain documents. A key practical contribution is an anti-repetition training strategy that randomly perturbs ground-truth tokens, combined with a logit-variance-based repetition detector at inference that identifies and halts degenerate generation loops.

## Results

On the arXiv test set, Nougat base (350M) achieves a Character Error Rate of 7.1%, BLEU of 89.1, METEOR of 93.0, and F1 of 93.1 across all modalities. The smaller Nougat model (250M) performs nearly identically (CER 7.3%, F1 92.9). Mathematical expressions remain the hardest modality (CER 12.8% for Nougat base), but plain text is recognized with very high accuracy (CER 5.8%, F1 95.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Nougat base outperforms the GROBID + LaTeX-OCR pipeline by a wide margin on every metric: CER drops from 31.2% to 7.1%, BLEU rises from 55.6 to 89.1, and F1 improves from 73.0 to 93.1 on the overall 'All' modality. The most dramatic gain is on mathematical expressions, where GROBID + LaTeX-OCR achieves a CER of 72.7% and an F1 of only 9.7%, while Nougat base reduces CER to 12.8% and F1 to 76.5 — a roughly 8× improvement in F1. Nougat also surpasses the raw embedded PDF text (CER 25.5%, F1 79.2), which benefits from having the correct characters but lacks any structural or mathematical formatting. The 250M-parameter Nougat small model is essentially on par with the 350M base model (CER 7.3% vs 7.1%), suggesting the architecture is efficient and the smaller variant is a practical choice for deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

Nougat builds directly on the Donut (Kim et al., 2022) OCR-free document understanding architecture, adopting its Swin Transformer encoder and mBART decoder with cross-attention, but repurposes it for a full-page academic document-to-markup task rather than information extraction. It departs from traditional line-by-line OCR engines (Tesseract) and rule-based parsers (GROBID, pdf2htmlEX) by treating the entire page image as input and generating structured output with LaTeX math in a single end-to-end pass. The paper positions itself at the intersection of OCR, mathematical expression recognition, and Visual Document Understanding, unifying these previously separate subfields into one model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Nougat addresses a concrete gap: millions of scientific papers are locked in PDF format with mathematical expressions rendered as unparseable glyphs, and no prior tool recovers their semantic structure from a page image alone. The released 8.5M-page dataset and pre-trained models lower the barrier for future work on scientific text recognition. Key open questions include the persistent repetition-loop failure mode (1.5% in-domain, much higher out-of-domain), the single-page processing that causes cross-document inconsistencies, and the model's inability to handle non-Latin scripts. Generation speed (~19.5s per batch of 6 pages) remains a practical bottleneck compared to classical parsers.
