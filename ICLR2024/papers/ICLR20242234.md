## Problem

Self-supervised learning methods incur prohibitive training costs—BYOL, for instance, demands 23× the computation of supervised learning—yet the efficiency of SSL training remains severely underexplored. Existing efficiency approaches (rotation prediction, dual gating) add architectural complexity on top of the SSL paradigm rather than exploiting the inherent structural property that the two augmented views in a siamese encoder are partially similar. The paper asks whether this inter-branch similarity can be leveraged in a generic, plug-and-play manner to cut computation without sacrificing accuracy.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a pair of augmented RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet/Stanford Cars/FGVC/CUB, 32×32 for CIFAR) derived from the same source image, which are divided into 30×30 (or 5×5) blocks for similarity matching. It produces a trained encoder (ResNet-18, ResNet-50, or DeiT-S) with up to 54% fewer training FLOPs and ~24% shorter wall-clock training time, while maintaining or slightly improving linear-evaluation accuracy. The framework is agnostic to the underlying SSL objective (SimCLR, SimSiam, MoCo, DINO) and operates on both CNN and ViT backbones.

## Method

SIMWNW exploits the inherent similarity between the two augmented views in a siamese SSL encoder through a two-stage removal strategy. First, it identifies similar blocks between the paired augmented images via PSNR-based block matching, constrained to a semantically related neighborhood (4× block size) and accelerated with diamond search, then removes those blocks so convolution kernels skip them entirely. Second, to counteract the 'waning' effect—where removed regions shrink and vanish as they pass through successive 3×3 convolutions—it performs a 'waxing' step on intermediate feature maps: starting from the boundary of each removed region, it iteratively checks concentric rings of pixels for channel-wise similarity between the two branches and expands the removed region outward until similarity drops below a threshold (PSNR = 20). This coarse-grained, block-level sparsity is hardware-friendly (compatible with GPU sparse libraries and FPGA tiling) and, by eliminating irrelevant features, also accelerates model convergence by >30% in epoch count, compounding the FLOPs savings.

## Results

On training-from-scratch tasks (ResNet50/ImageNet, ResNet18/CIFAR), SIMWNW reduces training FLOPs by up to 54% (CIFAR-10) and 19% (ImageNet) while matching or slightly exceeding baseline accuracy (e.g., 91.17% vs. 90.80% on CIFAR-10 with SimSiam). On transfer learning tasks (ResNet50 pre-trained on ImageNet), SIMWNW cuts training FLOPs by up to 51% (Stanford Cars) and 49% (CUB-200) with no accuracy loss. Average training time is reduced by 24%, and model convergence is accelerated by over 30% fewer epochs compared to SimCLR and SimSiam baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SimCLR and SimSiam baselines, SIMWNW matches or slightly exceeds accuracy (e.g., 71.28% vs. 71.12% on ImageNet with SimSiam) while cutting training FLOPs by 19–54%, and at equal FLOPs the baselines lose 3.5% accuracy on average. Compared to Back Razor (activation gradient pruning), SIMWNW saves an additional 18% (training from scratch) and 14% (transfer learning) in FLOPs at comparable accuracy, because Back Razor keeps dense activations during forward propagation. Against SOTA efficient SSL methods (Addepalli et al. 2022, Koçyiğit et al. 2023), SIMWNW achieves 2–11% greater training time reduction at similar accuracy (e.g., 65% vs. 75–81% training time on CIFAR-10 with SimSiam). Versus the sparse SSL method CDG (Meng et al. 2022), SIMWNW saves 8–12% more FLOPs at matched accuracy on CIFAR. SIMWNW is also compatible with Fast-MoCo and S3L, adding a further 23% and 30% training time reduction respectively without accuracy loss, and on ViT (DINO + DeiT-S) it reduces FLOPs by 43% while maintaining 58.83% accuracy vs. 58.95% baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the efficient self-supervised learning line, departing from prior approaches that add auxiliary tasks (rotation prediction in Addepalli et al.), modify training schedules (Koçyiğit et al.), or introduce gating mechanisms (Contrastive Dual Gating) to reduce SSL costs. Instead of adding complexity on top of the siamese SSL paradigm, SIMWNW exploits the inherent redundancy between two augmented views of the same image—a property unique to siamese architectures—to skip computation on similar regions. It also relates to sparse training and pruning methods (Back Razor, CDG) but operates at the spatial/input level rather than the weight or gradient level, and its coarse-grained block removal is more hardware-friendly than unstructured sparsity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SSL's 23× computational overhead over supervised learning is a major barrier to deployment on resource-constrained platforms, and SIMWNW addresses this with a generic, plug-and-play framework that works across multiple SSL methods (SimCLR, SimSiam, MoCo) and architectures (CNN, ViT). The dual mechanism of savings—skipping convolution on removed regions plus accelerating convergence by eliminating irrelevant features—makes the approach more impactful than pure FLOPs reduction. Open questions include how the method interacts with non-siamese SSL architectures, whether the PSNR-based similarity metric generalizes to non-visual modalities, and how the block size and similarity threshold should be tuned for very high-resolution or video inputs.
