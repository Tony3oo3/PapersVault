## Problem

FlashAttention already achieves 2–4× speedup over standard attention by exploiting the GPU memory hierarchy, yet it still reaches only 25–40% of the theoretical maximum FLOPs/s on A100 GPUs, far below the 80–90% achieved by optimized GEMM kernels. Profiling reveals the bottleneck is suboptimal work partitioning between thread blocks and warps, causing either low GPU occupancy (when batch size and head count are small relative to the 108 SMs) or unnecessary shared-memory reads/writes within each thread block.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241395/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes Q, K, V matrices of shape N×d (sequence length × head dimension, typically N = 512–16k and d = 64 or 128) stored in HBM, and produces the attention output O (N×d) plus a row-wise logsumexp vector L (N) needed for the backward pass. In the backward pass it additionally takes dO and produces dQ, dK, dV. The algorithm tiles inputs into blocks of size {64,128}×{64,128} to fit in on-chip SRAM (192 KB per SM on A100), and supports causal masking, multi-query attention, and grouped-query attention without duplicating KV heads.

## Method

FlashAttention-2 makes three targeted changes to FlashAttention's tiling + online-softmax algorithm. First, it reduces non-matmul FLOPs (which are 16× more expensive than matmul FLOPs on Tensor Cores) by maintaining an un-scaled running output and only storing the logsumexp L = m + log(ℓ) instead of both the row-max and row-sum. Second, it parallelizes the outer loop over sequence-length blocks across separate thread blocks (in addition to batch and head dimensions), using atomic adds in the backward pass to accumulate dQ contributions, which raises occupancy when batch size is small. Third, within each thread block it splits Q across warps rather than K/V, so each warp independently computes its slice of QKᵀ and multiplies by the shared V without any inter-warp shared-memory synchronization. Together these changes yield ~2× speedup over FlashAttention, reaching 50–73% of peak A100 throughput in the forward pass and up to 225 TFLOPs/s (72% model FLOPs utilization) in end-to-end GPT training.

## Results

FlashAttention-2 achieves up to 225 TFLOPs/s per A100 GPU (72% model FLOPs utilization) when training GPT-style models end-to-end, a 1.3× speedup over FlashAttention and 2.8× over a baseline without FlashAttention. In isolated attention benchmarks, FlashAttention-2 reaches up to 230 TFLOPs/s (73% of theoretical maximum on A100) in the forward pass and up to 63% in the backward pass, roughly 2× faster than FlashAttention. During LLM inference (decoding), the FlashAttention-2 kernel is up to 7× faster than FasterTransformer's attention kernel and 28× faster than a naive PyTorch implementation.

|---|---|---|---|

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlashAttention-2 consistently outperforms all compared baselines. Against FlashAttention (the direct predecessor), it achieves ~2× speedup in the attention kernel (1.7–3.0× depending on sequence length and head dimension) and 1.3× speedup in end-to-end GPT training, with the largest gains at longer sequence lengths (8k context: 220–225 vs. 170–175 TFLOPs/s). Against FlashAttention implemented in Triton, FlashAttention-2 is 1.3–1.5× faster in the forward pass and ~2× faster in the backward pass. Against the xformers/CUTLASS implementation, FlashAttention-2 is approximately 2× faster. Against a standard PyTorch attention implementation, FlashAttention-2 is 3–10× faster. For inference decoding with multi-query attention, FlashAttention-2 is up to 7× faster than FasterTransformer's kernel, thanks to splitting KV cache loading across multiple thread blocks to saturate HBM bandwidth.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlashAttention-2 is a direct systems-level refinement of FlashAttention (Dao et al., 2022), which first introduced IO-aware tiling and online softmax to achieve exact attention in O(N) memory. Rather than proposing a new algorithmic formulation, it identifies that FlashAttention's remaining inefficiency stems from suboptimal GPU work partitioning—specifically, low occupancy from not parallelizing along sequence length and excessive shared-memory traffic from a 'split-K' warp scheme. The paper also positions itself against the broader family of approximate attention methods (Reformer, Linformer, Longformer, Performer), explicitly maintaining exactness while closing the gap with optimized GEMM kernels in raw throughput.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reaching 50–73% of theoretical peak FLOPs/s (versus 25–40% for FlashAttention) and 72% model FLOPs utilization end-to-end, FlashAttention-2 makes long-context training (16k+) economically viable at the same cost as 8k, directly enabling applications in long-document understanding, high-resolution image processing, and video generation. The 7× decoding speedup over FasterTransformer also has immediate practical impact on inference cost. Open questions include porting to H100 (TMA, 4th-gen Tensor Cores, FP8), AMD GPUs, and auto-tuning block sizes; the authors also note that combining these low-level optimizations with algorithmic sparsity (local, dilated, block-sparse attention) could unlock substantially longer contexts.
