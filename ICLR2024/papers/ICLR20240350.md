## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary object detectors align region embeddings only with categorical labels (e.g., 'bicycle'), ignoring VLMs' superior ability to align visual features with fine-grained descriptors of object parts and attributes (e.g., 'pedals', 'bells'). This under-utilization of VLM knowledge results in significantly weaker zero-shot detection performance compared to VLMs' classification accuracy, as the fine-grained alignment knowledge that VLMs possess is never distilled into the detector.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a set of fine-grained textual descriptors per category (e.g., 'hair', 'two eyes', 'face' for 'person') as input, with both categorical labels and descriptors encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder. It outputs bounding boxes with category predictions, operating as a plug-in module on the classification branch of existing two-stage detectors (Faster R-CNN, CenterNet2) without modifying the localization/RPN branch. The descriptors are dynamically generated and updated during training via LLM interaction, so the text input evolves over the course of training rather than being fixed.

## Method

DVDet introduces two coupled mechanisms. First, the Conditional Context Prompt (CCP) expands each region proposal by a fixed margin to capture surrounding background, extracts features from this enlarged region, and passes them through a lightweight convolutional meta-network to produce a region-conditional visual prompt that is added to the original ROI embedding, transforming it into an image-like representation compatible with CLIP's image-text alignment. Second, a hierarchical descriptor generation mechanism iteratively interacts with LLMs during training: it prompts LLMs using two templates (one listing high-frequency descriptors, another listing confusing categories), records descriptor usage frequency via a semantic selection function, preserves high-frequency descriptors while discarding low-frequency ones, and merges semantically similar descriptors across categories using cosine-similarity thresholding with momentum-based embedding averaging. This iterative loop refines the descriptor pool to be visually relevant and category-specific without any grounding annotations.

## Results

On OV-COCO, DVDet as a plugin consistently improves all base detectors: BARON+DVDet achieves the best overall mAP of 51.5 (vs. 49.1 for BARON alone), with Novel AP of 35.8 and Base AP of 57.0. VLDet+DVDet reaches 48.0 overall mAP (Novel 34.6, Base 52.8), surpassing the standalone VLDet (45.8) by 2.2 points. On OV-LVIS with Swin-B backbone, VLDet+DVDet achieves 40.2 mAP_all^mask (Novel 27.5), outperforming all other methods tested. The method also transfers to PASCAL VOC and LVIS without retraining, gaining 2.3% and 2.1% mAP respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DVDet is designed as a general plugin and is evaluated on top of five existing OVOD methods. It improves BARON by +2.4 overall mAP (49.1→51.5) and +2.7 Novel AP (33.1→35.8), yielding the best result on OV-COCO. VLDet gains +2.2 overall mAP (45.8→48.0) and +2.6 Novel AP (32.0→34.6), making VLDet+DVDet competitive with BARON on novel classes. RegionCLIP sees a +1.7 overall mAP gain (47.5→49.2) with +1.6 Novel AP improvement. Detic benefits most in relative terms with +2.4 overall mAP (44.9→47.3), though it remains the weakest base. On OV-LVIS, DVDet consistently adds 1.3–2.1 mAP across all base methods (e.g., VLDet RN50: 30.1→31.2 mAP_all^mask; Detic Swin-B: 38.4→40.4), confirming its generality across architectures and datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

DVDet extends the open-vocabulary detection line (ViLD, Detic, VLDet, RegionCLIP, BARON) by identifying that these methods under-utilize VLMs' fine-grained alignment capability, focusing only on categorical label matching. It adapts visual prompt tuning (Jia et al., 2022) into a region-conditional form (CCP) that transforms ROI features into image-like representations, and extends LLM-assisted descriptor generation (CuPL, Menon & Vondrick, 2023) from a one-shot static lookup to an iterative hierarchical interaction during training. The paper positions itself as a general plugin rather than a standalone architecture, deliberately preserving the localization branch while enhancing only the classification head.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The observation that VLMs excel at aligning fine-grained object-part descriptors (e.g., 'pedals', 'bells') rather than just categorical labels provides a concrete mechanism for closing the gap between VLM zero-shot classification and OVOD performance. The plugin design makes the technique broadly applicable across detector backbones and datasets, and the transfer experiments (COCO→VOC, COCO→LVIS without retraining) suggest the learned descriptors capture transferable visual semantics. Open questions include whether the iterative LLM interaction scales to very large vocabularies (LVIS has 1203 classes), whether descriptor quality bottlenecks at the LLM level, and whether the approach extends naturally to other dense prediction tasks like segmentation or tracking.
