## Problem

Classical experimental design for multi-channel imaging (Fisher-matrix, sequential Bayesian) requires a priori model specification, is limited to model-parameter estimation tasks, and becomes computationally intractable at the high dimensions encountered in real-world imaging (e.g., C up to 1344 channels). This paper introduces a task-driven paradigm that replaces continuous design optimization with a supervised feature-selection (subsampling) problem, enabling arbitrary user-specified tasks including model-free ones like reconstruction.

## Input / Output

The method takes densely-sampled multi-channel image data X_D̄ ∈ R^{n×C̄} (pixel/voxel-level, with C̄ channels acquired from a small number of subjects or simulations), corresponding target labels Y, and a user-specified sequence of decreasing channel subset sizes {C_1 > C_2 > ... > C_T}. It outputs, at each step t, a binary mask selecting C_t channels (the experimental design D_t ⊂ D̄) and a trained task network T_t that maps the subsampled, score-weighted data to Y. The stepwise procedure also yields a family of optimized designs at multiple sizes, supporting post-hoc trade-off between acquisition cost and task performance.

## Method

TADRED couples a scoring network S_t and a task network T_t within a recursive feature elimination (RFE) outer loop that progressively reduces the channel set from C̄ to C_T. At each step, a four-phase inner-loop training procedure (inspired by progressive GAN training) stabilizes optimization: Phase 1 initializes from the previous step and refines sample-dependent scores; Phase 2 linearly interpolates a mix parameter α to shift from sample-dependent to sample-independent (averaged) scores; Phase 3 gradually drives the mask elements of the lowest-scored features to zero; Phase 4 performs final task-network refinement with fixed scores and features. Unlike SSEFS and FIRDL, which search for a small discriminative subset among mostly uninformative features, TADRED is tailored to the imaging setting where most channels are individually informative and the goal is a compact complementary combination. Information is passed across outer-loop steps via network weights and scores, and removed features are filled with a data-median hyperparameter to preserve tensor shape throughout training.

## Results

On VERDICT-MRI parameter estimation (Table 1), TADRED achieves MSE×10² of 1.03 at C=110 and 2.64 at C=14, outperforming SSEFS (1.06, 4.58) and FIRDL (2.22, 4.05) at all subset sizes. On the MUDI reconstruction challenge (Table 2), TADRED reduces MSE from 0.49 to 0.22 at C=500 and from 3.48 to 2.88 at C=10 compared to the best published result (PROSUB). On AVIRIS Indian Pine remote sensing (Table 3), TADRED achieves MSE of 0.87 at C=110 versus 2.03 (SSEFS) and 8.10 (FIRDL), and on HCP downstream MRI metrics (Figure 2), TADRED outperforms baselines on 17/18 comparisons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TADRED outperforms SSEFS (Lee et al., ICLR 2022) on every comparison across all datasets: on VERDICT-MRI at C=14, TADRED achieves MSE×10² of 2.64 versus SSEFS's 4.58 (a 42% reduction), and on AVIRIS at C=110, TADRED's 0.87 beats SSEFS's 2.03 by more than 2×. Against FIRDL (Wojtas & Chen, NeurIPS 2020), TADRED wins decisively, e.g. on AVIRIS at C=110 (0.87 vs 8.10) and on HCP downstream metrics where FIRDL's MSE for AD is 77.7 versus TADRED's 13.4. On the MUDI challenge, TADRED surpasses PROSUB (Blumberg et al., 2022), the previous best published result specifically designed for this data, reducing MSE from 0.49 to 0.22 at C=500 and from 3.48 to 2.88 at C=10. For tissue oxygen saturation estimation, TADRED outperforms the Waterhouse & Stoyanov (2022) baseline by ~38% RMSE at C=6 (2.80 vs 4.54 for HbO₂/Hb) and ~38% at C=6 for SO₂ (2.76 vs 4.45). Finally, on NODDI parameter estimation (Table 7), TADRED at C=99 achieves MSE×10² of 4.51 versus 8.00 for the classical Fisher-matrix design from Zhang et al. (2012), a 44% improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

TADRED reframes experimental design for multi-channel imaging as a supervised feature selection problem, departing from both classical Fisher-matrix approaches (which require a priori model specification and optimize over a continuous design space) and sequential Bayesian experimental design (which faces intractability at the scale of real imaging problems). It adopts the dual scoring-network + task-network architecture from state-of-the-art feature selection methods (SSEFS, FIRDL) but replaces their search-for-discriminative-subset optimization with a recursive feature elimination (RFE) / backward-selection procedure, motivated by the observation that in imaging most channels are individually informative rather than mostly uninformative. The method also generalizes PROSUB, a prior qMRI-specific approach that used neural architecture search at each RFE step, by replacing that with a fixed four-phase progressive training procedure that transfers network weights across steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paradigm enables task-driven experimental design without requiring a specific generative model, opening the door to 'model-free' tasks like sparse-to-dense reconstruction that classical and Bayesian methods cannot address. The substantial improvements over Fisher-matrix designs (e.g., ~7× lower MSE for VERDICT parameter estimation) and over SOTA feature selection baselines across qMRI and hyperspectral imaging suggest practical impact for shorter clinical scan times and cheaper deployable sensors. Key limitations include the RFE-style nested constraint (optimal subsets for different sizes may not be nested), the assumption of equal per-channel cost, and the restriction to per-pixel/voxel tasks using fully-connected networks; extending to spatially-correlated tasks (CNNs) and heterogeneous acquisition costs are natural next steps.
