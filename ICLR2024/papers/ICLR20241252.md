## Problem

Existing conformal prediction methods for time series forecasting either guarantee coverage only for individual time steps (not the full horizon jointly) or apply worst-case corrections like Bonferroni that produce confidence regions far too large to be practically useful, especially in multivariate or long-horizon settings. The temporal dependency structure among future time steps is ignored, making it impossible to simultaneously achieve validity and efficiency across the entire forecast window.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241252/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a past multivariate time series segment of t steps (each step in R^d) and produces k confidence regions — one d-dimensional ball per future time step — such that the joint probability of all k true values falling within their respective regions is at least 1-α. The output is a 'cone of uncertainty' covering the full forecast horizon simultaneously rather than per-step intervals, and the regions are centered at the point forecast of an arbitrary underlying forecaster (LSTM, LaneGCN, etc.).

## Method

CopulaCPTS performs a two-step calibration to model the joint distribution of nonconformity scores across future time steps. First, conformal predictive distributions (CDFs) are estimated per time step on a calibration subset D_cal-1, transforming each score into a uniform marginal. Second, an empirical copula is fitted on the transformed k-dimensional score vectors from a second calibration subset D_cal-2, capturing the dependency structure between time steps. The algorithm then solves a constrained optimization (minimizing the sum of marginal quantiles subject to the copula CDF exceeding 1-α) to find the tightest joint confidence region, implemented via gradient descent with a sigmoid-approximated indicator. A finite-sample validity guarantee is proven by showing the empirical copula is a rank statistic under exchangeability, reducing the proof to the standard inductive conformal prediction argument. This approach is strictly more efficient than Bonferroni correction (which corresponds to the Fréchet-Hoeffding lower bound on copulas) and more valid than prior copula-based methods that lacked a two-step calibration.

## Results

At 90% target confidence, CopulaCPTS achieves near-exact calibration (90.0–91.3% coverage) across all four datasets while producing the most efficient confidence regions among valid methods. On Argoverse trajectory prediction, CopulaCPTS reduces the confidence area to 126.8 (vs. 396.9 for CF-RNN and 880.8 for BJRNN), a 68% reduction over the best conformal baseline. On COVID-19 daily cases, CopulaCPTS achieves 90.5% coverage with area 408.6, a 33% reduction over CF-RNN (610.2). The advantage grows with prediction horizon: at 25 steps on the particle dataset, CopulaCPTS achieves over 50% area reduction compared to CF-RNN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CopulaCPTS is the only method that simultaneously achieves valid (≥90%) and well-calibrated coverage across all four datasets. MC-dropout (Gal & Ghahramani, 2016) is severely underconfident on harder tasks—coverage drops to 46.1% on particle σ=.05, 19.1% on COVID-19, and 27.9% on Argoverse—making it invalid. BJRNN (Alaa & Van Der Schaar, 2020) is overconfident, producing excessively wide regions (e.g., 100% coverage with area 12.13 on particle σ=.05; area 880.8 on Argoverse vs. CopulaCPTS's 126.8). CF-RNN (Stankevičiūtė et al., 2021) also over-covers due to its Bonferroni correction (97.3–98.8% coverage), yielding areas 3–7× larger than CopulaCPTS on Argoverse and COVID-19. The single-step Copula baseline (Messoudi et al., 2021) without two-step calibration is consistently underconfident (86.9–89.7% coverage), confirming that the two-stage calibration in CopulaCPTS is essential for validity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of conformal prediction for time series (Stankeviciutė et al., 2021; Gibbs & Candes, 2021; Xu & Xie, 2021) and copula-based multi-target conformal prediction (Messoudi et al., 2021). It departs from Stankeviciutė et al. by replacing the Bonferroni correction (which corresponds to the Fréchet-Hoeffding lower bound) with an empirical copula estimate, yielding guaranteed efficiency improvements, and from Messoudi et al. by introducing a two-step calibration procedure that provides a finite-sample validity proof over the full forecast horizon—something the prior copula work lacked. The method is model-agnostic and builds directly on inductive conformal prediction (Vovk et al., 2005) and conformal predictive distributions (Vovk et al., 2017).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CopulaCPTS provides the first provably valid, distribution-free confidence regions for the full multi-step, multivariate time series horizon, filling a gap where prior methods either covered only individual steps or produced uninformatively wide regions. The 30–50% area reductions over long horizons make the method practically relevant for high-stakes forecasting (vehicle trajectories, pandemic modeling). Key limitations include the need for abundant calibration data (split into two subsets) and the validity proof being restricted to the empirical copula, leaving open whether learnable parametric copulas can preserve the guarantee. Future directions noted by the authors include online adaptation and improving the autoregressive extension for non-stationary series.
