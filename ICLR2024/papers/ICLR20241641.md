## Problem

Existing OOD detection evaluation datasets conflate semantic shift (novel classes) with covariate shift (visual or domain changes to known classes), making it impossible to determine whether modern OOD detectors are genuinely detecting new classes or merely reacting to spurious visual differences. Under failure detection benchmarks, no modern OOD detector surpasses the simple maximum softmax probability (MSP) baseline, yet the underlying reason for this discrepancy remains unexplained. The paper asks: what are modern OOD detectors actually detecting?

## Input / Output

The OOD detection algorithms under evaluation take 224×224 RGB images and a trained ImageNet-1K classifier as input, producing a scalar OOD score per image that is thresholded into an ID/OOD binary decision. The introduced ImageNet-OOD dataset contains 31,807 images across 637 classes, all sourced from ImageNet-21K to minimize covariate shift relative to the ImageNet-1K ID set. Detection performance is measured via AUROC (ID as positive, OOD as negative) under two distinct protocols: new-class detection, where all training-class images including covariate-shifted ones count as ID, and failure detection, where only correctly classified images count as ID.

## Method

The core contribution is a carefully curated semantic-shift dataset (ImageNet-OOD) constructed from ImageNet-21K with systematic removal of three ambiguity sources: hypernyms and hyponyms of ImageNet-1K classes in the WordNet hierarchy, the 'Organism' subtree where biological categorization creates inconsistent hierarchical relations, and visually ambiguous classes identified through 20 hours of manual inspection of sister classes. The empirical analysis partitions ImageNet-R and ImageNet-OOD into subsets of matched PASS feature distance to ImageNet-1K, revealing via linear regression that modern detectors (Energy, ViM, ReAct) respond significantly more strongly to covariate shift than semantic shift at equal visual similarity. A sanity check with randomly initialized ResNet-50 models confirms that OOD detectors exhibit systematic bias toward specific covariate shifts (blur, noise, renditions) even without any learned representation, while ImageNet-OOD remains undetectable at chance level. Finally, evaluation of nine OOD detection algorithms across 13 CNN architectures shows that the best modern method achieves only 0.7% AUROC improvement over MSP on ImageNet-OOD (versus 3–5% on OpenImage-O), and that under failure detection MSP consistently outperforms all modern methods because their gains come from re-ranking incorrect ID examples rather than better separating semantic OOD from ID.

## Results

On the new ImageNet-OOD dataset (clean semantic shift with minimal covariate shift), the best modern OOD detector (Max-Cosine) achieves only 80.7% AUROC for new-class detection versus MSP's 79.8%, a mere 0.9% gap. Under failure detection, MSP (86.9%) outperforms all modern methods, with the best being Max-Cosine at 84.8%. Critically, the 3.3–5.5% AUROC improvements that Max-Logit, ViM, and ASH-B show over MSP on OpenImage-O vanish entirely on ImageNet-OOD (shrinking to 0.7%, 0%, and −0.3% respectively). When covariate-shifted data (ImageNet-R, ImageNet-Sketch) is pitted against ImageNet-OOD, all detectors score below 50% AUROC, confirming they are more sensitive to covariate shift than semantic shift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates nine OOD detection algorithms (MSP, Max-Logit, Energy, Mahalanobis, ViM, KNN, Max-Cosine, ASH-B, ReAct) plus gradient-based methods (ODIN, GradNorm, OpenMax) across 13 architectures. On the clean semantic-shift dataset ImageNet-OOD, MSP is essentially tied with the best modern method (Max-Cosine: 80.7 vs 79.8 AUROC for new-class detection), while on OpenImage-O the same Max-Cosine gains only 1.2% over MSP (85.8 vs 84.6) compared to ASH-B's 5.5% gain (90.1 vs 84.6), revealing that prior improvements were driven by uncontrolled covariate shift. Under failure detection, MSP dominates all methods on every dataset pair, outperforming Max-Logit by 2.8% on IN-1K vs IN-OOD despite Max-Logit's 0.7% new-class advantage. Feature-based methods (Mahalanobis, KNN, ReAct) perform worst, with KNN dropping to 59.3% AUROC on IN-1K vs IN-OOD. The sanity check with random untrained models confirms that modern detectors (especially ViM, Energy, ReAct) exhibit strong covariate-shift bias (AUROC deviating from 0.5 on ImageNet-C/R) while MSP remains near random chance, further validating that their apparent gains are artifacts of covariate shift rather than genuine semantic-shift detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not propose a new OOD detection algorithm but instead introduces a carefully curated evaluation dataset (ImageNet-OOD) and a systematic empirical analysis of existing detectors. It departs from prior OOD benchmarks (ImageNet-O, OpenImage-O, NINCO, C-OOD) by explicitly addressing their contamination from ID classes and uncontrolled covariate shifts, which the authors argue confound the measurement of semantic shift detection. Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of the new-class detection and failure detection literatures, using the PASS/MoCo-v2 self-supervised feature space to quantify visual similarity and the WordNet hierarchy to enforce semantic disjointness between ID and OOD classes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central finding—that modern OOD detectors (ViM, Energy, ReAct, etc.) show negligible improvement over the MSP baseline once covariate shift is controlled—challenges the field's assumption that more sophisticated scoring functions are inherently better at detecting semantic novelty. This has direct implications for how OOD detection should be evaluated and for the design of future detectors that must be robust to covariate shift while remaining sensitive to semantic shift. Limitations include the ImageNet-centric scope (637 OOD classes, CNN architectures only) and the reliance on the WordNet hierarchy, which may not capture all real-world semantic ambiguities. An open question is whether any detector can simultaneously achieve strong semantic shift detection without being confounded by even modest covariate shifts.
