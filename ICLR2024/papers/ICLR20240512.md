## Problem

With hundreds of pretrained models available in hubs like timm, practitioners must decide which model to finetune and how to set its hyperparameters for a new dataset, but no prior work jointly optimizes both choices. The paper demonstrates empirically that no single model dominates across datasets even with per-dataset HPO, making the joint model-and-hyperparameter search problem both necessary and non-trivial under tight time budgets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240512/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a new image classification dataset (characterized by meta-features: number of samples, image resolution, channels, and classes), a pool of 24 Pareto-optimal pretrained models from timm, and a wall-clock time budget (1, 4, or 16 hours in experiments). It outputs the best (model, hyperparameter) pipeline and the corresponding finetuned classifier. The search space spans 24 models crossed with ~30 hyperparameters covering finetuning strategies (layer freezing, linear probing, stochastic norm, DELTA/BSS/SP regularization, co-tuning), regularization (MixUp, CutMix, dropout, label smoothing), data augmentation, optimizer/scheduler choices, and batch size, with conditional dependencies between groups.

## Method

Quick-Tune performs Combined Algorithm Selection and Hyperparameter Optimization (CASH) via Bayesian Optimization with three key components: (1) gray-box evaluation that advances each pipeline by only Δt=1 epoch per iteration, discarding poor configurations early; (2) a meta-learned deep-kernel Gaussian Process performance predictor and MLP cost predictor, pre-trained on a 20k+ configuration meta-dataset spanning 24 models × 87 Meta-Album datasets, which provides a strong prior for new datasets; (3) a cost-sensitive Expected Improvement acquisition function that divides the expected improvement in validation loss by the predicted incremental finetuning cost, trading off exploration speed against computational expense. The pipeline encoding concatenates raw hyperparameters, a linear embedding of the one-hot model identity, and a 2-layer CNN embedding of the observed learning curve, all jointly updated during both meta-training and online BO. This design lets the method transfer knowledge about model-hyperparameter interactions across datasets while adapting to dataset-specific characteristics through meta-features.

## Results

Quick-Tune achieves the lowest normalized regret across all Meta-Album splits: 0.153±0.054 (Micro), 0.139±0.112 (Mini), and 0.052±0.031 (Extended), substantially outperforming the best default-HP baseline (XCiT at 0.199±0.057 on Extended). Against DINOv2 (1B params) with LoRA or linear probing at 4 hours, Quick-Tune attains 0.072±0.024 (Micro), 0.039±0.014 (Mini), and 0.042±0.016 (Extended) normalized regret, beating both DINOv2 variants on every split. The method also achieves the best any-time performance versus state-of-the-art gray-box HPO optimizers (ASHA, BOHB, DEHB, DyHPO) and two-stage model-selection-then-HPO pipelines (LogME, LEEP, NCE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Quick-Tune outperforms all 12 baselines on every evaluated split. Against default-hyperparameter finetuning of individual models (BEiT, XCiT, DLA), Quick-Tune reduces normalized regret by 30–77% (e.g., 0.052 vs. 0.199 on Extended for XCiT). Versus state-of-the-art gray-box HPO methods (ASHA, BOHB, DEHB, DyHPO, Random Search), Quick-Tune achieves the best any-time normalized regret curves on all three Meta-Album versions, with the gap widening on larger datasets where cost-awareness matters most. Against DINOv2 (1B parameters) with LoRA or linear probing at 4 hours, Quick-Tune wins on all splits (e.g., 0.039 vs. 0.049 on Mini for LoRA; 0.042 vs. 0.081 on Extended for linear probing). Finally, Quick-Tune surpasses two-stage pipelines that first select a model via LogME, LEEP, or NCE and then optimize hyperparameters, confirming that joint CASH search is superior to decoupled model selection followed by HPO.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CASH (Combined Algorithm Selection and Hyperparameter Optimization) framework of Thornton et al. (2013) from classical ML to the deep learning finetuning setting, where the 'algorithms' are pretrained models from a hub. It builds directly on the gray-box Bayesian optimization framework of Wistuba et al. (2022, DyHPO) for learning-curve evaluation and on deep-kernel Gaussian Process surrogates (Wistuba & Grabocka, 2021) for the performance predictor, adding a cost-sensitive EI acquisition function and meta-learned initialization from a large cross-dataset meta-dataset. The key departure from prior model-hub work (LogME, LEEP, NCE) is that those methods select a model first and then tune hyperparameters separately, whereas Quick-Tune searches over the joint (model, hyperparameters) space in a single optimization loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With hundreds of pretrained models available in hubs like timm, the combinatorial challenge of choosing both the right model and its finetuning hyperparameters is a practical bottleneck for ML practitioners; Quick-Tune provides a principled, time-efficient solution backed by a large open-source meta-dataset (20k+ configurations across 87 datasets). The finding that no single model dominates across datasets, even with per-dataset HPO, reinforces that model selection is genuinely dataset-dependent and cannot be reduced to a fixed 'best' architecture. Limitations include the focus on image classification with a single-GPU budget, a specific model hub (timm), and the Meta-Album benchmark; open questions include whether the meta-learned surrogates transfer across modalities, whether the approach scales to much larger model pools (e.g., LLMs), and how the method behaves when the target dataset is highly out-of-distribution relative to the meta-training set.
