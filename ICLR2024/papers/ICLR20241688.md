## Problem

While it is well known that object-boundary pixels are harder to segment, no prior work has quantitatively characterized *why* they fail or linked their errors to a specific signal-processing phenomenon. This paper identifies frequency aliasing during downsampling as the root cause, categorizes boundary errors into three distinct types (false responses, merging mistakes, displacements), and shows that existing anti-aliasing filters either empirically set their cutoff or underestimate the true Nyquist frequency by ignoring kernel size and channel expansion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241688/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (including noisy low-light images) at resolutions ranging from 512×512 crops (VOC, ADE20K) to 768×768 crops (Cityscapes, originally 2048×1024) and produces per-pixel semantic class labels or instance-level masks. The DAF and FreqMix modules are parameter-light, plug-and-play insertions into existing encoder backbones (ResNet-50, Swin Transformer, ConvNeXt, InternImage) and segmentation heads (UPerNet, PointRend, Mask2Former), adding at most ~0.7M parameters and ~0.7 GFLOPs over the baseline.

## Method

The paper introduces an Equivalent Sampling Rate (ESR) that incorporates both the downsampling kernel size and the channel expansion ratio (via min(K_down, √(C_out/C_in)) × spatial_downsample_ratio) to compute a corrected Nyquist frequency, rather than relying solely on stride. An aliasing score is then defined as the ratio of Fourier power above this Nyquist frequency to total spectral power, providing a quantitative per-pixel measure of aliasing severity. Two modules exploit this: (1) the De-Aliasing Filter (DAF) is a parameter-free operation that FFTs the feature map before each downsampling layer and zeros all frequency bins above the equivalent Nyquist frequency, precisely removing only the aliased components; (2) the Frequency Mixing (FreqMix) module, inserted after convolutional layers in encoder blocks, decomposes features into sub-Nyquist and super-Nyquist components and applies learned channel-wise (global pooling + FC) and spatial-wise (convolution) sigmoid weights to dynamically balance low- and high-frequency content, preserving boundary-relevant high frequencies while suppressing aliasing-prone ones.

## Results

On Cityscapes with UPerNet-R50, the full method (DAF + FreqMix) achieves 79.7 mIoU, 63.2 BIoU, and 76.2 BAcc, improving over the UPerNet baseline (78.1 mIoU) by +1.6 mIoU and reducing all three boundary error types (FErr 25.2, MErr 23.3, DErr 24.8). On PASCAL VOC and ADE20K, the method gains +1.8 mIoU and +1.5 mIoU respectively over UPerNet. For low-light instance segmentation on LIS, DAF+FreqMix improves AP^mask by +1.2 (PointRend) and +1.1 (Mask2Former) over the prior end-to-end state of the art.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed DAF+FreqMix outperforms all three prior anti-aliasing baselines on Cityscapes: it beats Blur (Zhang 2019) by +0.9 mIoU, AdaBlur (Zou 2020) by +0.8 mIoU, and FLC (Grabinski 2022) by +1.1 mIoU, while also achieving the lowest values on all three boundary error metrics (FErr, MErr, DErr). Notably, DAF alone (parameter-free, zero extra FLOPs) already surpasses AdaBlur by +0.4 mIoU and FLC by +0.7 mIoU, and FreqMix adds only 0.71 GFLOPs and 0.38M parameters for an additional +0.4 mIoU. On PASCAL VOC, the method improves over UPerNet by +1.8 mIoU (76.1 vs 74.3) and +1.7 BIoU; on ADE20K, by +1.5 mIoU (40.4 vs 38.9) and +2.8 BAcc. For low-light instance segmentation on LIS, the method surpasses the end-to-end pipeline of Chen et al. (2023) by +1.2 AP^mask with PointRend and +1.1 AP^mask with Mask2Former, without any additional preprocessing. When combined with SegFix boundary refinement, the method further adds +0.6 mIoU (81.1 vs 80.5), confirming complementarity with post-hoc boundary correction approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: anti-aliasing in neural networks (Zhang 2019, Zou 2020, Grabinski 2022) and hard-pixel analysis in semantic segmentation (Li 2017, Gu 2020, Deng 2022). It departs from prior anti-aliasing work by correcting the Nyquist frequency calculation to account for kernel size and channel expansion (introducing the equivalent sampling rate), and departs from hard-pixel work by providing a physical, frequency-domain explanation (aliasing) rather than merely an empirical easy/hard binary split. The DAF extends Grabinski et al.'s Fourier-domain low-pass pooling with a corrected cutoff, while FreqMix introduces a novel frequency-domain attention mechanism within encoder blocks that has no direct predecessor in the segmentation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The aliasing score provides a quantitative diagnostic tool that connects a well-understood signal-processing phenomenon to pixel-level segmentation errors, offering a principled (rather than empirical) basis for designing anti-aliasing interventions. The DAF is parameter-free and adds zero FLOPs, making it trivially adoptable in any segmentation pipeline, while FreqMix adds only ~0.7M parameters. Open questions include how the equivalent sampling rate should be refined when downsampling filters are non-orthogonal (the paper acknowledges this assumption), how aliasing manifests specifically in transformer attention operations, and whether the three-error-type taxonomy generalizes to other dense prediction tasks like depth estimation or surface normal prediction.
