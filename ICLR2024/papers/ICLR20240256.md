## Problem

Contrastive learning (CL) produces powerful but opaque representations whose features lack sparsity, disentanglement, and semantic consistency, largely due to the rotation symmetry inherent in pairwise-distance-based objectives. The paper asks whether one can directly learn interpretable, part-based features while preserving the simplicity, generality, and downstream performance of deep contrastive representation learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240256/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet-100) through a ResNet-18 backbone followed by a two-layer MLP projector, producing a non-negative feature vector (256-dim for CIFAR, 2048-dim for ImageNet-100) at the projector output. The non-negativity constraint (applied via ReLU) makes the output structurally sparse—roughly 90% of dimensions are zero per sample—enabling direct feature selection by Expected Activation without additional training. For downstream classification, the backbone (pre-projector) output is used with a linear head, and the supervised variant (NCE) additionally applies ReLU to the class-embedding weights in the final linear layer.

## Method

NCL is derived by recognizing that the spectral contrastive loss is mathematically equivalent to matrix factorization of the normalized co-occurrence matrix, then replacing MF with symmetric NMF to obtain a non-negative spectral contrastive loss. In practice, this reduces to a single reparameterization: applying a non-negative activation (ReLU) to the projector output before computing the contrastive objective, which breaks the rotation symmetry of standard CL and forces features to align with coordinate axes. Theoretically, the paper proves that under mild latent-class assumptions, the optimal NCL features recover the posterior class-membership vector (up to permutation and scaling), guaranteeing identifiability, sparsity, orthogonality, and Bayes-optimal linear classification. The approach extends naturally to supervised learning via a Non-negative Cross Entropy (NCE) loss that applies the same reparameterization to both representations and class embeddings, and to multi-modal settings via asymmetric NMF.

## Results

On ImageNet-100, NCL achieves 69.4% linear probing accuracy and 79.2% fine-tuning accuracy, outperforming CL (SimCLR) by 0.7% and 1.9% respectively. On CIFAR-100, NCL reaches 59.7% LP and 73.0% FT versus CL's 58.6% and 72.6%. For out-of-distribution generalization, NCL improves by +1.4% on average across Stylized ImageNet (21.2 vs 19.6), ImageNet-C (36.1 vs 34.5), and ImageNet-Sketch (28.0 vs 27.1). In feature selection, NCL with EA-based 512/2048 dimension selection is nearly lossless (68.9% LP vs 68.9% full), while CL drops 0.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NCL consistently outperforms CL (SimCLR) across all evaluated tasks and datasets: +0.6% average LP gain, +0.9% average FT gain in-distribution, and +1.4% average gain on OOD benchmarks. The advantage is most pronounced on ImageNet-100 fine-tuning (+1.9%) and OOD transfer, suggesting NCL's disentangled features are more robust to domain shift. Against the spectral contrastive loss (SCL) baseline, NCL achieves 88.6% vs 88.0% linear accuracy on CIFAR-10 while providing 69.65% feature sparsity versus SCL's 0.00%, and also outperforms SCL with ℓ₁ sparsity regularization (88.6% vs 87.2% accuracy, 69.65% vs 0.03% sparsity). In feature disentanglement (SEPIN@1 on ImageNet-100), NCL scores 7.43×10² versus CL's 0.88×10², an ~8.4× improvement. For supervised learning, NCE loss improves over CE by 2.5% from scratch and 3.1% with MLP projector on ImageNet-100.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws a direct analogy between the established equivalence of contrastive learning (CL) and matrix factorization (MF) (HaoChen et al., 2021) and the classical equivalence of non-negative matrix factorization (NMF) as the interpretable counterpart of MF (Lee & Seung, 1999). By imposing non-negativity on CL features via a simple ReLU reparameterization of the SimCLR projector output, NCL inherits NMF's part-based interpretability while retaining CL's sampling-based training. The theoretical analysis builds on the spectral contrastive loss framework and the latent-class assumptions from Saunshi et al. (2019), extending them to prove identifiability and Bayes-optimal linear classification guarantees that are unavailable for standard CL due to its rotation symmetry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NCL offers a nearly zero-cost modification (one ReLU layer) to any existing CL pipeline that yields dramatically more interpretable, sparse, and disentangled features, enabling practical applications like lossless 4× feature compression for retrieval. The identifiability guarantee under mild assumptions (one unique sample per latent class) is a meaningful theoretical advance over CL, where rotation symmetry fundamentally prevents axis-aligned disentanglement. Open questions include the dead neuron problem (up to 86/512 dimensions inactive), whether the theoretical assumptions hold on large-scale real data, and systematic extension to multi-modal (CLIP-style) and supervised settings, which the paper only sketches. The modest downstream gains (+0.6–0.9% in-distribution, +1.4% OOD) suggest the primary value is interpretability and feature selection rather than raw accuracy.
