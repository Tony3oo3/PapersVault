## Problem

Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) assumes i.i.d. training data, but under domain shifts the inconsistent convergence degrees across domains cause SAM's perturbation to be biased toward less-converged domains, actually degrading generalization below ERM. No existing SAM variant accounts for this domain-level convergence disparity when generating its sharpness-estimation perturbation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242140/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images from multiple source domains (e.g., PACS, OfficeHome, DomainNet) with known domain labels, which are required to compute per-domain loss and its variance during each training step. It produces standard class-label predictions on an unseen test domain. The approach is plug-and-play on top of any SAM-based optimizer (SAM, GSAM, SAGM) and works with both CNN (ResNet50) and ViT (CLIP ViT-B/16) backbones, adding only ~0.01s overhead per step independent of model size.

## Method

DISAM modifies SAM's perturbation direction by incorporating a domain loss variance minimization constraint into the gradient used for the ascent step. Concretely, the perturbation gradient becomes ∇L_SAM minus a correction term (2λ/M)(L_i(w) − mean_j L_j(w))·∇L_i(w) for each domain i, which automatically weakens perturbation for domains with above-average loss (less converged) and strengthens it for below-average domains (more converged). The variance term uses a stop-gradient on w so it only shapes the perturbation location without altering the main loss optimization. Theoretically, this domain-aware reweighting yields a smaller Lipschitz constant Γ in the convergence bound (Γ ≤ L vs. Γ = L for SAM), enabling faster convergence and permitting a larger perturbation amplitude ρ for improved generalization. The method requires no extra storage and is compatible with existing SAM variants and domain-invariant regularizers like CORAL, V-REx, and Fishr.

## Results

On the DomainBed benchmark with ResNet50, DISAM consistently improves out-of-domain accuracy across all five datasets: SAM+DISAM achieves 66.4% average OOD accuracy (vs. 64.5% for SAM alone, +1.9%), GSAM+DISAM reaches 66.8% (vs. 65.1%, +1.7%), and SAGM+DISAM attains 67.0% (vs. 66.1%, +1.9%). The strongest configuration, SAGM+DISAM+CORAL, sets a new state-of-the-art at 67.9% average OOD accuracy. In-domain accuracy also improves, with SAGM+DISAM reaching 87.0% average (vs. 85.9% for SAGM). On CLIP-based fine-tuning, DISAM lifts CLIPOOD from 76.9% to 78.8% average OOD accuracy and CoOp from 74.2% to 74.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DISAM improves over SAM by +1.9% average OOD accuracy (66.4% vs. 64.5%), with the largest gain on TerraIncognita (+4.6%, 47.9% vs. 43.3%), where SAM actually underperforms ERM due to inconsistent domain convergence. Over GSAM, DISAM adds +1.7% (66.8% vs. 65.1%), and over SAGM it adds +1.9% (67.0% vs. 66.1%). Combined with CORAL, SAGM+DISAM+CORAL reaches 67.9% average, surpassing the previous SOTA CORAL alone (64.5%) by 3.4%. On CLIP-based fine-tuning, DISAM improves CLIPOOD by +1.9% (78.8% vs. 76.9%) and CoOp by +0.6% (74.8% vs. 74.2%), with the largest gains on TerraIncognita (+1.5% for CoOp, +1.1% for CLIPOOD). In the open-class setting, DISAM improves CoOp by +1.7% total accuracy (72.2% vs. 70.5%) and CLIPOOD by +1.2% (72.6% vs. 71.4%), with particularly strong gains on new classes (+3.3% for CoOp, +2.8% for CLIPOOD). DISAM also complements convergence-consistency methods: V-REx+DISAM improves over V-REx by +2.7% (64.6% vs. 61.9%), and Fishr+DISAM improves over Fishr by +1.5% (67.2% vs. 65.7%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) lineage (Foret et al., 2021; GSAM; SAGM) by identifying that SAM's perturbation generation is ill-posed under domain shifts due to inconsistent convergence degrees across domains, and introduces a domain loss variance constraint into the perturbation direction. Conceptually, it is orthogonal to convergence-consistency methods like V-REx and Fishr, which modify the optimization objective on w directly, whereas DISAM modifies only the perturbation estimation step. The approach is composable with existing SAM variants (GSAM, SAGM) and with domain-alignment methods (CORAL), positioning it as a plug-in correction rather than a replacement for the broader DG toolkit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear diagnostic for why SAM can underperform ERM in multi-domain settings (inconsistent convergence biasing the perturbation toward less-optimized domains), which is practically important for anyone applying SAM-based training to domain generalization. The theoretical result that DISAM achieves a smaller Γ (and thus permits larger ρ for better generalization at the same convergence rate) gives practitioners a principled reason to prefer it. Limitations include the method being specific to SAM-family optimizers, the variance constraint requiring domain labels at training time, and the theoretical proof relying on a correlation assumption between domain loss magnitude and gradient magnitude that is not rigorously verified. Open questions include whether the domain-level granularity is optimal (vs. sample-level or task-level balancing) and whether the approach transfers to non-SAM flatness-seeking methods like SWA/SWAD.
