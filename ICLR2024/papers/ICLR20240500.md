## Problem

Full fine-tuning of pre-trained Vision Transformers for deep metric learning is computationally expensive and prone to catastrophic forgetting, yet parameter-efficient fine-tuning strategies have not been systematically evaluated for DML tasks. Additionally, conventional proxy-based DML losses rely on randomly initialized proxies that lack semantic information, creating a large distribution gap that slows convergence and biases early training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240500/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images resized to 224×224 pixels, split into 16×16 patches, and produces L2-normalized embedding vectors for each sample via the [CLS] token of a frozen ViT. For each class, it additionally generates a class-level semantic proxy by encoding all images of that class through a separate ViT tower augmented with class-specific learnable prompts, then accumulating them into a single vector. Retrieval is performed via cosine similarity between sample embeddings and the final blended proxy representations.

## Method

The framework freezes all ViT parameters except a linear head and BitFit biases, and introduces learnable visual prompts (VPT) as the sole tunable encoder parameters. For semantic proxy generation, a set of m class-specific prompts is appended to the ViT at each layer, so that each image's encoding through this augmented tower yields a proxy candidate already grounded in the pre-trained model's semantic space. These per-image candidates are accumulated into a single class proxy using either exponential moving average or a modified GRU (replacing Tanh with ReLU to preserve vector scale), with no gradient flow across iterations. The final proxy blends the accumulated semantic proxy with the original randomly-initialized proxy at a fixed ratio α, and training uses the Proxy-Anchor loss comparing sample embeddings against these blended proxies. Only ~5.2% of total parameters (1.56M of 30M) are tuned, yet the method matches or exceeds full fine-tuning SOTA on CUB200 and CARS196.

## Results

On CUB200 with ViT-S/16, VPTSP-G achieves R@1 of 86.6 and MAP@R of 52.7, surpassing the full fine-tuning baseline (85.5 / 51.1) while tuning only 5.2% of parameters (1.56M out of 30.2M) and using 2.6 GB memory vs. 6.0 GB. Scaling to ViT-B/16, the method reaches R@1 of 88.5 on CUB200 and 91.2 on CARS196, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art RS@K (89.5 on CARS196) with a batch size of 64 versus RS@K's >200. On iNaturalist with ViT-B/16, VPTSP-G achieves R@1 of 84.5 and MAP@R of 48.1, exceeding Recall@K (83.9) under identical pre-training and architecture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the VPT baseline (Jia et al., 2022), VPTSP-G(B) improves R@1 by 0.9 pp (86.6 vs. 85.7) and MAP@R by 1.1 pp (52.7 vs. 51.6) on CUB200, confirming that semantic proxies add meaningful representational capacity beyond plain visual prompts. Compared to the Adapter (Houlsby et al., 2019 / Chen et al., 2022a), VPTSP-G(B) outperforms Adapter(B) by 0.8 pp R@1 (86.6 vs. 85.8) and 3.0 pp MAP@R (52.7 vs. 49.7), while using comparable parameter budgets (~5%). BitFit (Zaken et al., 2021) acts as a consistent plug-and-play boost: adding it to VPT yields +0.2 pp R@1, and to Adapter yields +2.7 pp R@1, validating its inclusion in all settings. Against the full fine-tuning Proxy-Anchor baseline (Kim et al., 2020), VPTSP-G(B) exceeds it by 1.1 pp R@1 and 1.6 pp MAP@R on CUB200 while tuning only 5.2% of parameters. Versus the state-of-the-art Hyp-ViT (Ermolov et al., 2022), VPTSP-G wins on CUB200 (86.6 vs. 85.6) and CARS196 (87.7 vs. 86.5) with ViT-S, but trails on SOP (84.4 vs. 85.9) and InShop (91.2 vs. 92.5) due to the larger class count. Against RS@K (Patel et al., 2022) with ViT-B, VPTSP-G surpasses it on CARS196 (91.2 vs. 89.5) and InShop (92.5 vs. –) but falls short on SOP (86.8 vs. 88.0), a gap the authors attribute to the difficulty of optimizing per-class proxies across 22,634 classes. Older CNN-based baselines (ProxyNCA++, NIR, XBM+PA, vanilla PA from Table 6) are substantially outperformed, with R@1 gaps of 15–21 pp on CUB200, reflecting the architectural advantage of ViT backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) for Vision Transformers and proxy-based deep metric learning. It builds directly on Visual Prompt Tuning (Jia et al., 2022) as the PEFT backbone and Proxy-Anchor (Kim et al., 2020) as the DML loss, but departs from both by repurposing class-specific visual prompts to generate semantically informed proxies rather than randomly initialized ones. The GRU-based proxy accumulation mechanism (adapted from Chung et al., 2014) is a novel integration strategy not previously applied to DML, and the systematic comparison of PEFT strategies (Linear Probing, BitFit, Adapter, VPT) specifically for metric learning tasks fills a gap left by prior PEFT work that targeted classification or detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for practitioners with limited compute: matching or exceeding full fine-tuning SOTA on CUB200 and CARS196 while tuning only ~5% of parameters on a single GPU addresses a real deployment bottleneck in image retrieval. However, the method does not outperform full fine-tuning SOTA on large-class datasets (SOP, InShop), revealing that the per-class prompt overhead and proxy update frequency become limiting factors at scale. Open questions include whether the semantic proxy idea generalizes to open-vocabulary or few-shot retrieval settings, and whether the GRU accumulation can be replaced by more scalable mechanisms when class counts reach tens of thousands.
