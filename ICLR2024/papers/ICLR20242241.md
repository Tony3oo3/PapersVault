## Problem

Preference-based reinforcement learning (PbRL) learns reward functions from trajectory-level preference feedback but lacks a credit assignment strategy to determine which states within a trajectory most contributed to a preference decision. Without such a strategy, many reward functions can explain the same preference labels, leading to arbitrary reward selection, data-intensive training, and reward functions that fail to generalize to unseen trajectories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242241/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes trajectory segments of state-action pairs (length 50 by default, tested up to 200) along with Bradley-Terry preference labels comparing trajectory pairs, and produces a learned dense reward function over state-action pairs, a trained SAC policy, and per-state importance scores derived from attention weights. The world model operates on 1-D internal state vectors (not images) with continuous action spaces, and the attention maps are extracted autoregressively by feeding the full trajectory through the Transformer XL latent state predictor to obtain the attention distribution over all past state-action pairs at the final timestep.

## Method

Hindsight PRIOR augments the standard PbRL cross-entropy loss with an auxiliary return-redistribution objective guided by state importance estimated from a Transformer-based World Model (TWM). The TWM, adapted for continuous control by removing reward/discount heads and using 1-D state encoders, is trained on the policy's replay buffer to predict next latent states; its multi-layer, multi-head attention weights over past state-action pairs serve as a proxy for which states a human evaluator would attend to when judging a trajectory. For each preference-labeled trajectory, the predicted return is redistributed across state-action pairs proportionally to their mean attention weight, producing per-state reward targets. The reward model is then trained to match these targets via an MSE auxiliary loss (weighted by λ) alongside the standard Bradley-Terry cross-entropy loss, effectively constraining the reward function to assign higher values to states that are most predictive of the trajectory's future. The world model's observation encoder is frozen after pre-training while the dynamics model is updated every 2000 policy steps from the shared replay buffer.

## Results

On MetaWorld (6 manipulation tasks), Hindsight PRIOR achieves a mean normalized success rate of 0.66±0.002, outperforming all baselines (PEBBLE 0.56, SURF 0.56, RUNE 0.53, MRN 0.55). On DMC (3 locomotion tasks), PRIOR achieves a mean normalized return of 0.59±0.003 versus PEBBLE (0.48), SURF (0.55), RUNE (0.49), and MRN (0.53). The paper reports that PRIOR recovers on average 20% more reward on MetaWorld and 15% more on DMC (both p < 0.05), and achieves ≥80% success rate with as little as half the preference feedback of baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hindsight PRIOR outperforms all four PbRL baselines on both MetaWorld and DMC with statistically significant margins (two-tailed paired t-test, p < 0.05 for all comparisons). Against PEBBLE (the underlying algorithm), PRIOR gains +0.10 on MetaWorld (0.66 vs 0.56) and +0.11 on DMC (0.59 vs 0.48). Against SURF, the closest competitor on DMC (0.55), PRIOR still leads by +0.04, while on MetaWorld the gap is +0.10. RUNE and MRN trail by larger margins (0.53 and 0.55 on MetaWorld; 0.49 and 0.53 on DMC). The one exception is quadruped-walk on DMC, where PEBBLE (0.66) marginally edges out PRIOR (0.65), but PRIOR dominates on the other two DMC tasks (walker-walk: 0.60 vs 0.46; cheetah-run: 0.51 vs 0.33). Additionally, PRIOR is more robust to noisy preference labels: with 20% labelling errors, PRIOR outperforms PEBBLE trained with perfect labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PEBBLE framework for preference-based RL by introducing a credit assignment mechanism that prior PbRL methods (PEBBLE, SURF, RUNE, MRN) do not explicitly address. It repurposes the attention weights of a Transformer-based World Model (TWM) as a proxy for state importance, adapting the return redistribution concept from sparse-reward RL (RUDDER) to the dense-reward PbRL setting. Unlike Kim et al. (2023), which also tackles credit assignment in PbRL, PRIOR decouples the credit assignment mechanism from the reward architecture, keeping the world model as an independent auxiliary component rather than embedding it into the reward function itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The credit assignment gap in PbRL is a fundamental bottleneck: without it, many reward functions fit the preference data equally well, forcing reliance on large amounts of human feedback. Showing that a simple attention-based prior significantly reduces feedback requirements (≥80% success with half the labels) and improves robustness to noisy human labels (20% error outperforms PEBBLE with 0% error) has direct practical implications for human-in-the-loop deployment. Key open questions include whether the world model's notion of state importance generalizes across different human evaluators (personalization), and the paper's own negative result (Q6) confirms that the prior alone cannot replace preference feedback, bounding the method's ceiling.
