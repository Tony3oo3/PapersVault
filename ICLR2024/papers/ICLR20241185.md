## Problem

Standard RL agents struggle with multi-object manipulation from pixels due to the curse of dimensionality, and existing structured approaches either require ground-truth state access or assume goals can be decomposed into independent per-object subtasks (as in SMORL), overlooking critical object-object interactions. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a goal-conditioned RL framework that learns multi-object manipulation entirely from images while explicitly modeling interactions between entities, and provides a theoretical foundation for why the chosen architecture generalizes to unseen numbers of objects.

## Input / Output

The method consumes K multi-view RGB images (128×128) of the current state and K corresponding goal images, each processed independently by a pretrained DLP encoder into a set of 24 latent particles with attributes (position, scale, depth, transparency, visual features). The output is a 3D end-effector delta action (Δx, Δy, Δz) for the policy and a scalar Q-value for the critic; in the Q-function, the action is projected into particle space and appended as an additional entity token. A novel image-based Chamfer reward (GDAC distance between state and goal particle sets, filtered by a lightweight classifier) enables training without any ground-truth state access.

## Method

The framework pairs a lean object-centric representation (pretrained DLPv2) with an expressive Entity Interaction Transformer (EIT) that handles all dynamics and task-relevant reasoning. The EIT processes state and goal particles from multiple views through a sequence of Transformer blocks (SA→CA→SA→AA), where cross-attention between state (queries) and goal (keys/values) particles provides goal-conditioning without requiring explicit entity matching across views or between state and goal. The action is treated as a separate entity particle in the Q-function, and a learned single-particle aggregation (AA) produces a permutation-invariant output. The paper proves that self-attention Q-functions admit compositional generalization: if the optimal Q decomposes as a mean of per-entity attention terms, then an ε-optimal approximation for M objects has sub-optimality growing at most linearly in k for M+k objects. Training uses TD3 with Hindsight Experience Replay, and the DLP is pretrained unsupervised on random-policy rollouts.

## Results

On the Adjacent-Goals task (requiring object interactions), the proposed method with state input achieves 96.3% success rate, substantially outperforming SMORL with state input (71.6%). With image input, the method reaches 71.0% success rate, matching SMORL's state-based performance. On standard 3-Cubes, the method achieves 97.8% (state) and 91.9% (image) success rate. For compositional generalization, an agent trained on 3 cubes generalizes to 6 cubes with 39.8% success rate, and the decay in average return is approximately linear in the number of objects, consistent with the paper's Theorem 2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SMORL (Zadaianchuk et al., 2020), the proposed method wins decisively on tasks requiring object interactions: on Adjacent-Goals, Ours(State) achieves 96.3% success vs. SMORL(State) at 71.6%, and Ours(Image) at 71.0% matches SMORL's state-based performance, demonstrating that SMORL's sequential single-object decomposition is fundamentally limited. On standard N-Cubes (2 and 3 cubes), both methods reach similar success rates (99.1% vs. 98.0% for 2-Cubes; 97.8% vs. 93.2% for 3-Cubes with state input), with SMORL being slightly more sample-efficient. Against the unstructured VAE+MLP baseline (corresponding to Nair et al., 2018), the proposed method wins overwhelmingly: the unstructured image-based baseline fails to learn at all with more than one object, while the unstructured state-based baseline is significantly outperformed by both structured methods. The paper also shows that with image-based Chamfer reward, the method still surpasses SMORL on 3-Cubes (58.0% vs. 50.9% success rate), though both suffer from occlusion-related reward noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Deep Latent Particles (DLP) object-centric image representation and adapts the Particle Interaction Transformer (PINT) from DDLP into a new Entity Interaction Transformer (EIT) for goal-conditioned RL. It departs from SMORL, which decomposes multi-object tasks into sequential single-object sub-goals and cannot model entity-entity interactions, and from Zhou et al. (2022), which requires explicit entity matching between state and goal. The key conceptual shift is a 'lean OCR + expressive EIT' design: rather than demanding the image representation understand dynamics, the Transformer architecture learns object interactions, multi-view consistency, and goal dependencies end-to-end, while a formal compositional generalization theorem provides theoretical grounding for the self-attention architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates the first pixel-based multi-object manipulation with goals requiring inter-object dependencies (e.g., ordered push) and shows zero-shot compositional generalization from 3 to 6+ objects (and 12 in a sorting task), with performance decay matching the theoretical bound. However, the Chamfer reward (needed for real-robot deployment without ground-truth state) underperforms the GT reward substantially, and the approach is validated only in simulation. Open questions include whether the method extends to articulated or interlocking objects, how to integrate multi-modal goal specification (language, depth), and whether the DLP pretraining requirement can be relaxed for more complex or real-world scenes.
