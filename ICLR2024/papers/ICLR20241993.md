## Problem

Standard information bottleneck (IB) regularization for time series imputation forces the encoder to converge toward a unit Gaussian prior, causing it to rely on observed features at a single time point rather than learning temporal dependencies from other time steps. This overly strict constraint leads to significant loss of temporal context, degrading imputation performance especially when entire time steps are missing. The paper identifies this limitation theoretically and proposes a conditional IB that relaxes the regularization by conditioning on the remaining observed time steps.

## Input / Output

The method takes a partially observed multivariate time series x_{1:T}^o (with arbitrary missing patterns per feature per time step) and a binary missing mask, and produces multiple stochastic imputations of the missing features x_t^m along with associated uncertainty. The encoder is a 2-layer bidirectional LSTM (preceded by a CNN for image-sequence data) that maps the full observed sequence to per-time-step latent Gaussians; the decoder is an isotropic Gaussian that reconstructs each time step. At inference, a single point estimate is obtained from the latent mean, while multiple samples from the latent posterior enable uncertainty quantification for downstream classification or forecasting tasks.

## Method

TimeCIB replaces the standard IB regularization I(Z_t; X_{1:T}^o) with a conditional version I(Z_t; X_t^o | X_{\t}^o), which via the chain rule decomposes into the usual KL-to-prior term minus a temporal-context term I(Z_t; X_{\t}^o) that must be maximized. This second term is approximated by a novel temporal kernel-enhanced contrastive loss: positive pairs are latent representations from the same time series with the target time step zeroed out (acting as an augmentation), while negative pairs are latents from other time series in the mini-batch, with cosine similarity weighted by a Cauchy or periodic kernel to encode inductive bias about temporal dynamics. The reconstruction term is the standard ELBO computed only over observed features. The full objective is β·L¹ + L² + γ·L³, where L¹ is the negative log-likelihood, L² is the KL divergence, and L³ is the contrastive loss, jointly optimized end-to-end.

## Results

TimeCIB achieves state-of-the-art imputation and prediction performance across all five datasets. On HealingMNIST (MNAR, 60% missing), the Cauchy-kernel variant achieves NLL 0.202 and MSE 0.088, outperforming GP-VAE (0.261/0.114) by ~23% in NLL. On Physionet2012 (80% missing), it reaches AUROC 0.744 for ICU mortality prediction, surpassing GP-VAE (0.730) and HI-VAE (0.696). On weather forecasting, the periodic-kernel variant achieves ForecastMSE of 0.443 (Beijing) and 0.327 (US Local), outperforming all baselines including CSDI (0.423/0.347) and SAITS (0.450/0.350) on imputation MSE while also leading on downstream prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TimeCIB consistently outperforms all VAE-based baselines (VAE, HI-VAE, GP-VAE) across every dataset and metric. Against GP-VAE (the strongest VAE baseline), TimeCIB improves NLL by 5–23% and MSE by 10–39% depending on the dataset, with the largest gains on Physionet2012 (MSE 0.521 vs. 0.616) and RotatedMNIST extrapolation. Against predictive methods (BRITS, SAITS), TimeCIB achieves comparable or better imputation MSE while additionally providing multiple imputations for uncertainty quantification, which predictive methods cannot offer. Against generative baselines mTANs and CSDI, TimeCIB matches or exceeds imputation MSE on Beijing (0.283 vs. 0.287/0.270) and US Local (0.260 vs. 0.268/0.340), while achieving the best downstream ForecastMSE (0.443/0.327 vs. 0.436/0.337 and 0.423/0.347). The periodic kernel variant provides the largest gains on weather data (e.g., ForecastMSE drops from 0.445 to 0.443 on Beijing and 0.351 to 0.327 on US Local), confirming the value of incorporating domain-specific temporal inductive bias.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Information Bottleneck (IB) principle (Tishby & Zaslavsky, 2015) to a Conditional Information Bottleneck (CIB) by replacing the unconditional regularization I(Z_t; X_1:T^o) with a conditional version I(Z_t; X_t^o | X_\t^o), arguing that the standard IB's strict regularization forces the encoder to ignore temporal context. The variational decomposition follows Voloshynovskiy et al. (2019), while the contrastive objective for maximizing I(Z_t; X_\t^o) draws on InfoNCE (Oord et al., 2018) and the temporal kernel weighting borrows from conditional alignment (Dufumier et al., 2021). Conceptually, it aligns with conditional regularization approaches in supervised IB (Fischer, 2020; Tezuka & Namekawa, 2021) but redirects the conditioning from label information to temporal context, which is the key departure from prior conditional IB work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first information-theoretic formulation specifically for time series imputation, identifying a concrete failure mode of the IB principle in temporal settings and offering a principled fix. The temporal kernel mechanism (Cauchy, periodic) gives practitioners a clean way to inject domain knowledge about seasonality or smoothness without architectural changes. Limitations include reliance on the InfoNCE approximation (which may be loose for small batch sizes), the kernel choice remaining a manual hyperparameter, and evaluation primarily on moderate-length sequences (T≤168). Open questions include whether the CIB framework extends naturally to irregularly sampled or multi-scale time series, and whether the contrastive objective can be replaced with tighter mutual information estimators.
