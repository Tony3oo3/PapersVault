## Problem

Path gradient estimators for normalizing flows offer lower variance than standard gradient estimators, but existing implementations are 2–5× more expensive computationally and cannot be applied to maximum likelihood (forward KL) training in a scalable manner. The only prior fast path gradient estimator was restricted to continuous normalizing flows, leaving coupling-based architectures—which dominate practical applications in physics and chemistry—without an efficient solution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241926/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For reverse KL training, the method consumes base samples x₀ ~ q₀ (e.g., standard Gaussian) and the target energy E(x) in closed form, producing an unbiased path gradient estimate of the KL divergence. For forward KL training, it additionally requires samples x ~ p from the target distribution (e.g., from MCMC) and computes the gradient directly on those samples without importance reweighting. The output is a vector-Jacobian product ∇_θ log q_θ(T_θ(x₀)) that can be plugged into any standard optimizer, with the notable property that the target energy E(x) appears explicitly in the forward-KL path gradient, providing a built-in regularization signal.

## Method

The key insight is a recursive relation (Proposition 3.2/3.3) that computes the score ∂_x log q_θ(x) layer-by-layer during the forward sampling pass, eliminating the need to evaluate the flow in the inverse direction T_θ⁻¹. For coupling flows, this recursion has O(d) complexity and requires only the Jacobian of the element-wise transformation f_θ, which is diagonal and trivially invertible—making it directly applicable to implicitly invertible flows without costly numerical inversion or backpropagation through it. A second key result (Proposition 4.1) shows that the forward KL divergence in data space is exactly a reverse KL divergence in base space (via the pullback density p_{θ,0}), so the same fast path gradient machinery applies to maximum likelihood training without the variance-prohibitive reweighting used in prior work. The resulting Algorithm 1 requires roughly 1.4–2.3× the runtime of the standard gradient estimator (versus 2.1–17.5× for prior baselines), operates at constant memory, and exhibits the 'sticking the landing' property where estimator variance vanishes at the optimum.

## Results

Path gradient training consistently outperforms standard gradient training across all three physics/ML benchmarks. On the Gaussian Mixture Model (64 modes), forward KL path gradients achieve 91.8% ESS vs. 79.1% for standard gradients, and reverse KL path gradients reach 97.4% vs. 92.2%. On the φ⁴ lattice model, path gradients improve ESS from 85.1–85.6% to 95.6–96.0% for both forward and reverse KL. In terms of runtime, the proposed fast estimator is 1.4–1.6× the standard gradient cost for explicitly invertible flows (vs. 2.1–2.2× for the prior method) and 2.0–2.3× for implicitly invertible flows (vs. 8.2–17.5× for the prior method combined with implicit differentiation).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two prior path gradient methods. Against Vaitl et al. (2022a) (the previous state-of-the-art path gradient estimator for explicitly invertible flows), the proposed fast estimator achieves the same or better ESS while reducing runtime from 2.1–2.2× to 1.4–1.6× the standard gradient cost (Table 2). Against the implicitly invertible flow baseline (Vaitl et al. 2022a combined with implicit differentiation from Köhler et al. 2021), the speedup is dramatic: 2.0–2.3× vs. 8.2–17.5× runtime overhead, because the recursive formulation avoids costly backpropagation through numerical inversion entirely. On the U(1) gauge theory benchmark (which uses only implicitly invertible flows), path gradients yield a modest but consistent ESS improvement (41.1% vs. 40.1%) and slightly better ELBO. The forward KL path gradient's advantage is most pronounced on the GMM, where it gains 12.7 percentage points in ESS over standard maximum likelihood, attributed to the regularizing effect of incorporating the known energy function.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and accelerates the line of path gradient estimators for normalizing flows that began with Roeder et al. (2017) for VAEs, was extended to explicitly invertible NFs by Vaitl et al. (2022a), and was made fast for continuous NFs by Vaitl et al. (2022b). The key departure is a recursive formulation that computes the score ∂_x log q_θ(x) alongside the forward sampling pass, eliminating the need for a separate reverse pass or numerical inversion, and a change-of-perspective argument (forward KL in data space = reverse KL in base space) that extends path gradients to maximum likelihood training without the variance-prohibitive reweighting used previously. The paper also shows that the GDReG estimator of Bauer & Mnih (2021) is a special case of the derived forward KL path gradient, but only the present formulation enables the fast recursive computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing the runtime overhead of path gradients from 2–17× to 1.4–2.3× relative to standard gradients, this work makes the lower-variance estimators practically viable for the high-dimensional physics applications (lattice gauge theory, φ⁴ field theory) where they were previously too expensive. The forward KL path gradient's ability to incorporate the known target energy function as a natural regularizer against overfitting to small MCMC sample sets is a practically important insight for the neural-sampler community. Limitations include the exclusion of autoregressive flows (which lack efficient bidirectional evaluation) and the requirement that the target energy be known in closed form for the forward KL path gradient, precluding application to tasks like image generation where only samples are available.
