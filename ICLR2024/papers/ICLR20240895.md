## Problem

Applying NeRF to multi-camera autonomous driving systems suffers from severe quality degradation due to under-calibration: per-camera ISP differences cause spatially varying color inconsistencies, and driving vibrations introduce pose errors that existing global latent codes and photometric pose refinement cannot adequately correct. The limited overlap between side-view and front-view cameras further exacerbates both problems, as global appearance codes overfit and photometric consistency across cameras is violated.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240895/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-camera RGB images at 1920×1280 (6 cameras in Waymo/NuScenes), initial camera poses from sensor-fusion SLAM decomposed into ego pose and temporally fixed relative camera transforms, camera intrinsics, and sky segmentation masks from a pretrained model. It additionally requires MVS-estimated depth maps (with geometric consistency filtering) to drive virtual warping. Outputs include photorealistic novel-view renderings (panoramic at 5760×1280), a two-layer NeRF scene representation (foreground via Zip-NeRF, sky via vanilla NeRF), and depth maps usable for downstream monocular depth estimation.

## Method

UC-NeRF introduces three coupled modules. First, layer-based color correction splits the scene into foreground and sky NeRFs, each receiving its own per-image affine color transformation [A,x] or [C,y] decoded from a 4-dim latent code via a 3-layer MLP, with a binary cross-entropy sky loss enforcing proper layer separation. Second, virtual warping uses MVS depth maps (filtered by geometric consistency across 6 target views) to project each real image onto 9 randomly perturbed virtual poses (±20° rotation, 0–1 m translation), producing viewpoint-diverse yet color-consistent training views that strengthen latent code constraints and expand NeRF's angular coverage. Third, spatiotemporally constrained pose refinement models each camera pose as ego pose × fixed relative transform, then optimizes via bundle adjustment on explicit Superpoint feature correspondences across cameras and time (reprojection error), rather than relying on photometric loss. Training proceeds in two stages: pose refinement + depth estimation, followed by end-to-end NeRF optimization with loss L = L_ph + λL_sky + γL_reg, sampling rays from real and virtual views at a 4:1 ratio.

## Results

On Waymo, UC-NeRF achieves 28.13 PSNR, 0.842 SSIM, and 0.356 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest baseline Zip-NeRF (26.21 PSNR, 0.815 SSIM, 0.389 LPIPS) by 1.92 dB in PSNR. On NuScenes, UC-NeRF reaches 30.20 PSNR, 0.876 SSIM, and 0.374 LPIPS, surpassing Zip-NeRF (27.06 PSNR, 0.831 SSIM, 0.435 LPIPS) by 3.14 dB in PSNR and 0.061 in LPIPS. The method also demonstrates robustness under adverse conditions, achieving 30.03 PSNR in rainy scenes and 31.32 PSNR at night, consistently outperforming Zip-NeRF in both settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UC-NeRF outperforms all five main baselines on every metric across both Waymo and NuScenes. The closest competitor, Zip-NeRF (the base architecture UC-NeRF builds upon), is surpassed by 1.92 dB PSNR on Waymo and 3.14 dB on NuScenes, with a notable 0.061 LPIPS improvement on NuScenes indicating substantially better perceptual quality. S-NeRF, which models dynamic objects with semantic priors, trails UC-NeRF by 3.24 dB PSNR on Waymo and 4.18 dB on NuScenes. Mip-NeRF 360 and Instant-NGP, which lack explicit color correction or pose refinement, show the largest gaps (5.67 dB and 4.29 dB PSNR on Waymo, respectively). In the color-correction ablation (Table 5), UC-NeRF's layer-based approach beats Urban-NeRF (27.89→28.15 PSNR) and NeRF in the Wild (25.59 PSNR), confirming that separate foreground/sky affine transforms are more effective than a single global latent code. For pose refinement (Table 6), the spatiotemporally constrained strategy achieves 29.14 PSNR versus 28.48 for NeRF--, a 238% relative improvement in the gap over the no-refinement baseline, demonstrating that explicit cross-camera geometric constraints are critical in multi-camera setups.

## Relation to Prior Work

UC-NeRF extends the NeRF/Zip-NeRF rendering pipeline to the multi-camera autonomous driving setting, where prior appearance-modeling approaches (NeRF in the Wild, Urban-NeRF) rely on a single global latent code per image to absorb color inconsistency. The key departure is a layer-based (foreground/sky) decomposition of color correction, motivated by the observation that ISP effects differ systematically between bright sky and darker foreground regions. For pose refinement, the paper moves beyond photometric-loss-based joint optimization (NeRF--, BARF, SiNeRF) to explicit pixel-correspondence bundle adjustment with a spatiotemporal constraint that exploits the fixed inter-camera geometry, a structure that contemporaneous MC-NeRF does not leverage. The virtual warping module, which uses MVS depth to synthesize color-consistent pseudo-views, is a novel data-augmentation strategy not seen in prior NeRF-for-driving work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-camera under-calibration is a practical bottleneck for deploying NeRF-based rendering in real autonomous driving stacks, and UC-NeRF provides a complete, well-ablated recipe that addresses color, geometry, and pose errors simultaneously. The demonstrated downstream benefit for monocular depth estimation (VA-DepthNet) suggests the rendered novel views can serve as a data-augmentation pipeline for perception models, which is economically attractive for the industry. Open questions include whether the foreground/sky binary split generalizes to scenes with complex illumination (e.g., strong reflections, mixed indoor-outdoor), whether the MVS depth dependency introduces a hard failure mode in textureless or dynamic regions, and how the approach transfers to emerging representations like 3D Gaussian Splatting, which the authors note as future work.
