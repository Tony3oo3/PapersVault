## Problem

Existing LLM watermarking methods, such as the K-gram watermark of Kirchenbauer et al. (2023), lack formal theoretical guarantees for robustness against post-processing attacks like token edits and paraphrasing. This paper is the first to provide provable robustness guarantees for LLM watermarks against arbitrary edits, along with rigorous bounds on generation quality and detection error rates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241707/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The watermarking algorithm takes a language model M and a prompt x, and produces a watermarked model M̂ that generates text with a fixed green-list bias; the detection algorithm takes a suspect text y and a watermark key k (which determines the green/red vocabulary partition) and outputs a binary decision via a z-score threshold test. The watermark key is a random seed that partitions the vocabulary into a green list of size γN and a red list, and the detection requires no access to the original model or prompt—only the key and the suspect text.

## Method

UNIGRAM-WATERMARK simplifies the K-gram watermark of Kirchenbauer et al. (2023) by fixing K=1, meaning a single consistent green/red vocabulary split is used for every token position rather than a context-dependent split. During generation, the logits of green-list tokens are uniformly increased by a strength parameter δ, subtly biasing the model toward green tokens. Detection counts the number of green tokens in the suspect text and computes a normalized z-score, comparing it against a calibrated threshold. The key theoretical insight is that the fixed split (K=1) makes the watermark provably twice as robust to edits as K≥2 variants, since editing a token does not change its list membership. The paper further establishes Rényi-divergence quality bounds (showing the watermarked model is (δ,0)-indistinguishable from the original per token), exponential decay of Type I/II errors with text length, and a formal security guarantee bounding the z-score drop under bounded edit distance.

## Results

UNIGRAM-WATERMARK achieves perfect detection (TPR=1.000, F1=0.995 at 1% FPR) with zero false positives on both OpenGen and LFQA under no-attack conditions. Under paraphrasing attacks, it consistently outperforms the KGW+23 baseline: against ChatGPT paraphrasing on OpenGen at 1% FPR, UNIGRAM-WATERMARK reaches TPR=0.866 (F1=0.910) versus KGW+23's TPR=0.565 (F1=0.704). Watermarked text quality is well preserved, with human evaluation scores of 3.665/5 (watermarked) vs. 3.660/5 (un-watermarked) and z-scores of ~11.1 for watermarked text vs. ~−2.4 for un-watermarked text, confirming a clear detection margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is KGW+23 (Kirchenbauer et al., 2023), the K-gram soft watermarking method with a prefix-dependent green/red split. UNIGRAM-WATERMARK outperforms KGW+23 across every paraphrasing attack scenario on both datasets. On OpenGen at 1% FPR, TPR gains are +0.30 (ChatGPT), +0.34 (DIPPER-1), +0.29 (DIPPER-2), and +0.25 (BART); on LFQA at 1% FPR, gains are +0.12 (ChatGPT), +0.27 (DIPPER-1), +0.26 (DIPPER-2), and +0.20 (BART). Under no-attack conditions both methods achieve identical perfect detection (TPR=1.000, F1=0.995). The paper further proves theoretically that UNIGRAM-WATERMARK is twice as robust to arbitrary edits as KGW+23 (Theorem 3.7 vs. Appendix D), and that its z-score degrades only linearly in edit distance η/√n, enabling robustness up to O(n) edits when δ is constant.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper simplifies the K-gram statistical watermark family introduced by Kirchenbauer et al. (2023) by fixing K=1, yielding a single consistent green-red vocabulary split rather than context-dependent splits. The key conceptual departure is proving that this simplest variant is provably twice as robust to arbitrary edits as the K≥2 baseline, while also providing the first rigorous theoretical framework (quality, Type I/II error, security) for LLM watermarks using tools from differential privacy (Rényi divergence, bounded-range analysis) and concentration inequalities for dependent random variables. The paper positions itself against both passive detection methods (GLTR, DetectGPT) and concurrent cryptographic watermarking approaches (Aaronson 2023, Christ et al. 2023), arguing that statistical watermarks with provable edit-robustness fill a gap that cryptographic constructions do not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide provable robustness guarantees for LLM watermarks against arbitrary edits, giving practitioners a formal security parameter (edit distance η) that quantifies how much modification an adversary must perform to evade detection. The counterintuitive finding that the simplest K=1 scheme outperforms more complex K≥2 variants on robustness opens the question of a fundamental robustness-learnability tradeoff: a fixed green list is easier for an adversary to estimate, yet the paper's white-box experiments suggest this is practically difficult. Open questions include constructing unlearnable watermarks, unifying cryptographic and statistical watermarking paradigms, and whether the 'homophily' and 'high-entropy' assumptions needed for Type II error bounds hold broadly across real LLMs and prompts.
