## Problem

The role of general function approximation in offline RL remains poorly understood: different types of function assumptions (realizability-type vs. completeness-type) are often conflated in the literature, and it is unclear what the fundamental limitations of learnability are under various combinations of function-class and data-coverage assumptions. In particular, no prior work establishes general-purpose minimax lower bounds for offline RL with general function approximation, leaving open whether one can learn good policies without approximating targets well for every policy in a policy class.

## Input / Output

The theoretical framework takes as input an offline dataset of N i.i.d. (s,a,r,s') tuples drawn from a data distribution d^D, a policy class Π, function classes (state-action value class Q, state transition probability class P_S, density ratio class W), a reward function R, initial state distribution μ_0, and discount rate γ. The output is a policy π̂, and the paper's contribution is to characterize the worst-case sub-optimality gap J(ξ(M)) − E[J(π̂)] that any algorithm must incur under specified realizability-type or completeness-type assumptions, rather than proposing a new algorithm.

## Method

The paper introduces a principle for lower-bound construction: model-realizability (the assumption that the true MDP belongs to a known class M) is the strongest structural assumption, and lower bounds established for model-realizability can be extended to any function class via a function-transferring argument (Proposition 1). Building on this, the authors construct a family of counterexample MDPs (M_1 and M_2) with a specific state-space structure involving intermediate states partitioned into subsets I and Ī, where the only difference between MDPs is which subset determines the transition kernel. Theorem 1 shows that under strong over-coverage data assumptions, even with realizability-type assumptions on value functions, density ratios, and state transition kernels (all with only 2–4 elements), no algorithm can beat the covered policy by more than γ²/8. Theorem 2 extends this to Q*-realizability under weaker partial coverage, showing a γ²/16 gap. The proofs rely on reducing the minimax problem to a hypothesis-testing problem and bounding the total variation distance between dataset distributions via χ²-divergence and hypergeometric tail bounds.

## Results

The paper establishes two main lower bounds for offline RL with general function approximation. Theorem 1 shows that under strong data-coverage assumptions (over-coverage with concentration coefficient ≤ 16) and realizability-type assumptions on the value function and density ratio for a specific policy, any algorithm must suffer a sub-optimality gap of at least γ²/8. Theorem 2 strengthens this by incorporating Q*-realizability, showing a sub-optimality gap of at least γ²/16 even when the optimal value function is in the function class, provided only partial coverage (concentration coefficient ≤ 32) of a non-optimal policy. Corollaries 1–3 further demonstrate that adding realizability of the density ratio, any S-related function, or any π-related function (each with only 2–4 elements) does not improve learnability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines. The results are positioned against prior theoretical work: Foster et al. (2021b) proved impossibility without Bellman-completeness but only for value functions, while this paper extends the impossibility to density ratios and arbitrary π-related functions via the model-realizability transfer principle (Proposition 1). Xie & Jiang (2020a) showed that Q*-realizability combined with strong data assumptions is sufficient for near-optimal learning; Theorem 2 shows this sufficiency breaks down when data coverage is weakened to partial coverage of a non-optimal policy. The paper's key contribution over prior lower bounds is the generic construction principle (Section 4) that allows lower bounds established for model-realizability to be extended to any function class, making the results broadly applicable beyond value functions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the emerging taxonomy of realizability-type versus completeness-type function assumptions in offline RL (Chen & Jiang 2022; Zhan et al. 2022; Ozdaglar et al. 2022; Mao 2023) and the lower bound construction techniques from Foster et al. (2021b), who showed impossibility without Bellman-completeness but only for value functions. It departs from prior work by introducing a general principle (Proposition 1) that lower bounds established under model-realizability can be transferred to arbitrary function classes, thereby unifying the lower bound landscape. The counterexample construction draws on the 'over-coverage' concept from Xie & Jiang (2020a) and the chi-squared divergence bounding from Foster et al. (2021b), but applies them to a broader set of function types (density ratios, state transition kernels, and arbitrary π-related functions).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing ambiguity in the offline RL literature by showing that realizability-type assumptions on individual functions (value functions, density ratios, transition kernels) are fundamentally insufficient for policy improvement, even under very strong data coverage—completeness-type assumptions (approximating targets for all policies in the class) are necessary. This clarifies why many practical algorithms implicitly require Bellman-completeness or similar structural assumptions. A key open question remains: Theorem 2's lower bound under Q*-realizability uses partial coverage that excludes the optimal policy, leaving the exact boundary between learnable and unlearnable regimes under Q*-realizability with full coverage unresolved. The model-realizability transfer principle also opens a pathway for establishing lower bounds in settings previously thought intractable.
