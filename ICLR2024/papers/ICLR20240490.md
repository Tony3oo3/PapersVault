## Problem

Batch normalization prevents rank collapse in deep networks by progressively orthogonalizing representations, but provably causes gradient explosion in depth in an activation-independent manner (Yang et al., 2019). This creates a fundamental tension: the orthogonalization mechanism that makes BN essential for signal propagation simultaneously makes arbitrarily deep networks numerically unstable. The paper asks whether a BN-based architecture can retain optimal signal propagation while provably avoiding gradient explosion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240490/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a batch of n=d linearly independent samples in R^d as input, with each layer's weight matrix drawn from the Haar measure over the orthogonal group O_d. The BN operator is simplified relative to standard practice (omitting mean reduction and the 1/n scaling factor), normalizing each row to unit norm. The theoretical outputs are a depth-independent bound on the expected log-norm of per-layer gradients (O(d^5)) and an exponentially decaying isometry gap, while the practical output is class predictions on image benchmarks (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, MNIST, Fashion-MNIST).

## Method

The core construction is an MLP with linear activations, simplified BN layers, and orthogonal random weight matrices under the constraint n=d. Using Weingarten calculus over the orthogonal group, the paper proves two non-asymptotic results: the isometry gap of representations decays exponentially in depth (achieving perfect orthogonality in the infinite-depth limit, stronger than the O(width^{-1/2}) bound of Daneshmand et al., 2021), and the expected log-norm of gradients at any layer is bounded by O(d^5) independent of depth. The gradient bound follows from showing the BN Jacobian's log-norm is upper-bounded by the isometry gap of pre-normalization matrices, reducing the total gradient log-norm to a convergent geometric series. For non-linear activations (tanh, sin), the paper introduces an activation shaping scheme that tunes per-layer pre-activation gain α_ℓ toward zero, making the non-linearities approximate identity and preventing the isometry perturbation that would otherwise re-introduce gradient explosion.

## Results

The paper establishes two key theoretical guarantees for MLPs with orthogonal random weights and batch normalization: (1) the isometry gap decays exponentially in depth, E[φ(X_ℓ)] ≤ φ(X_0)·e^{-ℓ/k} with k = O(d²(1+d·φ(X_0))), and (2) the log-norm of per-layer gradients is bounded by O(d⁵(φ(X_0)³+1)), independent of depth. Empirically, on CIFAR-10 (width 100, batch size 100, lr 0.001), MLPs with orthogonal weights and shaped tanh/sin activations achieve largely depth-independent training accuracy, while identity activations perform significantly worse, confirming the networks operate beyond the linear regime. Gradient norms remain bounded by a constant across depths up to 1000 layers, in stark contrast to the exponential growth observed with Gaussian weights.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against the mean-field theory of batch normalization by Yang et al. (2019, ICLR), which proves that MLPs with Gaussian weights and BN suffer gradient explosion at an exponential rate in depth (Theorem 3.9 of that paper). Figure 3 directly contrasts the two: with Gaussian weights, the log-gradient norm of the first layer grows linearly in depth (i.e., gradients grow exponentially), while with orthogonal weights, the log-gradient norm remains flat and bounded by a constant depending only on width. This validates Theorem 5 of the present paper, which provides the first non-asymptotic gradient bound for finite-width networks with BN. The paper also compares against Daneshmand et al. (2021), who show BN orthogonalizes representations but only up to an O(width^{-1/2}) ball around orthogonality; the present work proves perfect orthogonalization in the infinite-depth limit. For non-linear activations, the paper compares shaped tanh and shaped sin against identity activation on CIFAR-10 (Figure 4), finding that the non-linear variants achieve substantially higher accuracy, confirming they are not operating in the linear regime while still maintaining bounded gradients.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: (1) the theory of batch normalization's orthogonalization bias (Daneshmand et al., 2021; Joudaki et al., 2023b), which shows BN avoids rank collapse, and (2) the mean-field theory of BN (Yang et al., 2019), which proves gradient explosion is activation-independent for Gaussian weights. The key departure is combining orthogonal (Haar-distributed) weight initialization — previously used for linear networks without normalization (Saxe et al., 2013a) — with BN, and using Weingarten calculus to derive non-asymptotic bounds. For non-linear activations, the paper adapts activation shaping ideas from Li et al. (2022) and Martens et al. (2021), but repurposes them in conjunction with BN rather than as a replacement for normalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central insight — that gradient explosion in BN networks is a property of the weight distribution rather than an inherent consequence of normalization — challenges a widely held assumption in the field and opens a path toward arbitrarily deep BN networks. However, the theory is restricted to linear activations and a simplified BN operator (no mean subtraction), and the non-linear activation results rely on empirical activation shaping without theoretical guarantees. The observed implicit orthogonality bias of SGD in middle layers of very deep networks is an intriguing open question that could reshape how we think about optimization geometry in deep learning. Extending the non-asymptotic theory to standard BN with mean reduction and to non-linear activations remains a significant open problem.
