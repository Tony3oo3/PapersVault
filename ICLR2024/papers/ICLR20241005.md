## Problem

Conventional Mixture-of-Experts architectures require storing all experts in memory, making full fine-tuning computationally infeasible for modern LLMs, and prior work has focused almost exclusively on MoE as a pretraining strategy rather than a fine-tuning tool. This paper asks whether MoE can be pushed to an extreme degree of parameter efficiency by replacing dense experts with lightweight PEFT adapters, enabling instruction tuning that updates less than 1% of an 11B model's parameters while matching full fine-tuning performance on unseen tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text instruction prompts formatted according to P3 templates (62 datasets spanning 12 task categories) with a maximum input sequence length of 1024 tokens, and produces generated text responses with a 256-token target length in the T5 text-to-text paradigm. Evaluation is strictly zero-shot on 8 held-out datasets from task categories not seen during training (ANLI, CB, RTE, WSC, WIC, Copa, WNG, HS), plus a generative summarization task (SAMSum) measured by ROUGE scores.

## Method

The core architecture replaces dense feed-forward layers in a pretrained T5 model with MoE layers whose experts are lightweight PEFT adapters—either (IA)³ rescaling vectors (MoV) or LoRA low-rank matrices (MoLORA)—while a router network (a single dense layer with softmax) takes intermediate token embeddings and produces gating scores. For MoV, a key efficiency insight is 'soft merging': because (IA)³ vectors act as linear rescaling functions, the weighted average of expert vectors is computed first and then applied as a single combined transformation, reducing FLOPs without information loss. For MoLORA, the weighted average is instead taken across the outputs of each LoRA adapter to preserve the memory advantage of low-rank projection. Only the expert adapters and router are trained from scratch (using Adafactor at lr 3e-4, batch size 32, 500K steps), while all pretrained weights remain frozen; ablations show that soft merging of all experts substantially outperforms discrete top-k routing, and token-level routing outperforms sentence-embedding routing across all model scales from 770M to 11B.

## Results

On the P3 zero-shot evaluation (8 unseen tasks) with T5-3B, MoV-30 achieves a 60.61 average median score while updating only 0.68% of parameters, outperforming the (IA)³ baseline (52.90) by 14.57% and matching the fully fine-tuned T0-3B replication (60.06). MoLORA-15 (4.69% params) reaches 60.79, slightly exceeding full fine-tuning. At 11B scale, MoV-60 (0.86% params) achieves 64.08, approaching the full fine-tuning score of 65.03, while (IA)³ lags at 59.12.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against full fine-tuning (T0-3B replication, 60.06 average), MoV-10 updates only 0.32% of parameters yet achieves 59.93, essentially matching full fine-tuning; MoV-30 (60.61) and MoLORA-15 (60.79) both slightly exceed it. Against (IA)³ (52.90), MoV-30 delivers a 14.57% relative improvement while updating only 38× more parameters (0.68% vs 0.018%). Against LoRA at comparable parameter budgets, MoV-30 (0.68%, 60.61) outperforms LoRA rank 8 (0.6%, 58.89) by 1.72 points and LoRA rank 16 (1.2%, 59.54) by 1.07 points, demonstrating that the mixture-of-experts routing provides a meaningful advantage over simply increasing LoRA rank. At 11B scale, MoV-60 (0.86% params, 64.08) narrows the gap to full fine-tuning (65.03) to just ~1 point, while (IA)³ remains 5.91 points behind full fine-tuning (59.12 vs 65.03).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Mixture of Experts architecture—previously dominant in pretraining with full dense feed-forward experts (Fedus et al., 2022; Shazeer et al., 2017)—for the parameter-efficient fine-tuning regime by replacing heavy experts with lightweight (IA)³ vectors or LoRA adapters. It builds directly on the soft-merging mechanism from Muqeeth et al. (2023) and the router design from Switch Transformers, while departing from prior PEFT-MoE work (Wang et al., 2022; Ponti et al., 2022; Caccia et al., 2023) by eliminating task identifiers and scaling to 11B-parameter models. The conceptual move is to treat the MoE's conditional computation as a way to specialize tiny adapters across heterogeneous instruction-tuning data, rather than as a capacity-scaling mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: updating 0.32% of an 11B model's parameters to match full fine-tuning on unseen tasks is a compelling efficiency result for practitioners who cannot afford full fine-tuning. The finding that soft merging (activating all experts) outperforms standard top-k routing in this PEFT setting challenges the default MoE design assumption and suggests that sparsity is less beneficial when experts are extremely small. Key limitations include evaluation only on T5 encoder-decoder models and instruction tuning (not pretraining or decoder-only LLMs), leaving open whether the approach transfers to the dominant decoder-only architecture or to the pretraining phase where MoE was originally designed.
