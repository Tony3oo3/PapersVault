## Problem

In standard knowledge distillation, temperature scaling is applied to both teacher and student logits, but the role of temperature and why it must appear on both sides has remained poorly understood. This paper asks whether dropping temperature entirely on the student side is not only valid but actually beneficial, and provides a systematic theoretical and empirical treatment of the resulting variant.

## Input / Output

The method operates on the logit vectors (K-dimensional) produced by a pre-trained teacher network and a student network in response to RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, standard 224×224 for ImageNet). The teacher's logits are power-transformed (equivalent to temperature-scaled softmax) to produce a smoothed target distribution, while the student's logits are used directly without any temperature scaling. The final output is a class label prediction from the student network, with the distillation loss computed purely in logit/probability space.

## Method

The paper introduces Transformed Teacher Matching (TTM), which removes temperature scaling from the student side of the standard KD loss, yielding L_TTM = H(y,q) + β·D(p_T^t || q). By reinterpreting temperature scaling as a power transform of probability distributions, the authors prove that TTM decomposes into standard KD plus an inherent Rényi entropy regularizer of order 1/T, which penalizes overconfident student outputs and improves generalization. To further enhance matching, they propose Weighted TTM (WTTM), which multiplies the KL divergence by a sample-adaptive weight U_{1/T}(p^t) — the power sum of the teacher's raw probabilities — so that smoother (more uncertain) teacher targets receive larger distillation weight while near one-hot targets receive smaller weight. The approach requires no architectural changes, has the same computational complexity as vanilla KD, and is validated across 13+ teacher-student pairs on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet using CNNs (ResNet, VGG, WideResNet, MobileNet, ShuffleNet) and a ViT (DeiT).

## Results

On ImageNet, WTTM achieves 72.19% top-1 accuracy for ResNet-18 distilled from ResNet-34 and 73.09% for MobileNet distilled from ResNet-50, outperforming all 12 compared distillation baselines. On CIFAR-100 (same-architecture pairs), WTTM reaches up to 76.37% (WRN-40-2→WRN-16-2), and when combined with ITRD, WTTM+ITRD achieves up to 77.36% (ResNet32x4→ResNet8x4), setting new state-of-the-art. TTM consistently outperforms standard KD across all 13 CIFAR-100 teacher-student pairs, confirming the theoretical Rényi entropy regularization benefit.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WTTM outperforms standard KD by 1.53% on ImageNet (72.19% vs. 70.66% for ResNet-34→ResNet-18) and 2.59% for ResNet-50→MobileNet (73.09% vs. 70.50%). Among recent logits-based methods, WTTM edges out KD-Zero (72.17%), DIST (72.07%), and DKD (71.70%) on the ResNet-34→ResNet-18 pair, and surpasses DIST (73.24%) by a narrow margin on ResNet-50→MobileNet. On CIFAR-100, TTM consistently beats KD across all 13 teacher-student pairs (e.g., 76.23% vs. 74.92% for WRN-40-2→WRN-16-2), validating the theoretical Rényi entropy regularization. WTTM+ITRD achieves the best overall accuracy on CIFAR-100, reaching 77.36% (ResNet32x4→ResNet8x4) and 75.34% (WRN-40-2→WRN-40-1), surpassing feature-based CRD (75.51% and 74.14% respectively) and all other logits-based methods. Notably, WTTM is a simple logits-based method with nearly the same computational cost as KD, yet it outperforms complex feature-based distillation methods like CRD, PKT, and AT on most settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly modifies the standard KD loss of Hinton et al. (2015) by dropping temperature scaling on the student side, a change that prior works (Zhang & Sabuncu, 2020; Menon et al., 2021) made 'for convenience' without justification. The key conceptual contribution is showing that this modification is equivalent to adding a Rényi entropy regularizer to KD, connecting it to the confidence penalty framework of Pereyra et al. (2017) and the label smoothing perspective of Yuan et al. (2020). The statistical perspective of Menon et al. (2021) and the cross-entropy upper bound of Yang et al. (2023a) provide complementary justifications for why the teacher's power-transformed distribution serves as an estimate of the true conditional probability, but this paper offers a more direct theoretical decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical explanation for why a seemingly arbitrary modification (dropping T on the student) improves generalization, which should guide future distillation design. WTTM's sample-adaptive weighting is extremely simple yet achieves SOTA accuracy with near-zero additional compute, making it practically attractive. Open questions include: whether the Rényi entropy order can be decoupled from T (currently tied at 1/T), whether other transforms beyond the power function yield better smoothing, and what the optimal sample-adaptive weight is. The modest but consistent gains over the best logits-based baselines (DIST, DKD) suggest the remaining gap to feature-based methods may require fundamentally different information channels.
