## Problem

Neural Implicit Representation (NIR) methods for video compression assume a one-to-one mapping between data and model, making them unable to sequentially encode multiple videos without catastrophic forgetting of previously learned representations. Existing continual learning approaches (regularization, rehearsal, or architecture-based) are ill-suited for high-dimensional video data due to memory costs or insufficient parameter discretization, leaving a gap in how to progressively accumulate lossless neural video representations as new videos arrive.

## Input / Output

The model takes only two scalar indices — a video session identifier s and a frame index t — which are expanded into 160-dimensional positional encodings via sine/cosine functions (b=1.25, l=80). The output is a full RGB frame at 1280×720 resolution, decoded through a multi-head architecture where each video session has a dedicated head layer. No actual video frames are fed as input during inference; the entire video is reconstructed purely from the (s, t) index pair and the session-specific binary subnetwork mask.

## Method

PFNR builds on a NeRV backbone (sparse MLP + five NeRV blocks with PixelShuffle upscaling) and inserts a Fourier Subnetwork Operator (FSO) at a chosen NeRV layer, which performs element-wise multiplication in Fourier space using complex-valued tensors parameterized separately as real and imaginary components. A learnable weight-score vector ρ is jointly trained with the network weights via a straight-through estimator; at each session, the top-c% scored weights per layer form a binary mask that selects the active subnetwork. After a session completes, the selected weights are frozen by accumulating the binary mask (union operation), guaranteeing zero backward transfer while allowing new sessions to overlap and reuse previously frozen weights for forward transfer. This joint weight-and-structure training in Fourier space avoids the iterative prune-retrain cycles of the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis and eliminates the need for replay buffers or pre-trained models.

## Results

On the UVG17 benchmark (17 sequential video sessions), PFNR with f-NeRV3 at c=30.0% achieves an average PSNR of 31.72 and MS-SSIM of 0.94, surpassing the Multi-Task Learning upper bound (29.42 PSNR, 0.90 MS-SSIM) while maintaining zero backward transfer (BWT = 0.0). On UVG8, PFNR (f-NeRV3, c=50.0%) reaches 32.12 average PSNR and 0.93 MS-SSIM, again exceeding MTL (30.78 PSNR, 0.91 MS-SSIM). On the large-scale DAVIS50 dataset (50 sessions), PFNR (f-NeRV3) achieves 27.57 average PSNR versus MTL's 24.57, confirming consistent gains at scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PFNR (f-NeRV3, c=30%) outperforms all five baselines on both UVG8 and UVG17 in average PSNR and MS-SSIM. Against the strongest architecture-based baseline WSN (c=30%), PFNR gains +4.92 dB PSNR on UVG17 (31.72 vs. 26.80) and +4.46 dB on UVG8 (32.12 vs. 27.66 at c=50%), while both maintain BWT = 0.0. Rehearsal-based methods (iCaRL, ESMER) and the regularization-based EWC all suffer severe catastrophic forgetting, with BWT values of −6.23, −15.23, and −16.13 respectively on UVG17, and their average PSNRs (21.67, 20.95, 11.38) fall far below PFNR. Notably, PFNR exceeds the MTL upper bound (which trains on all sessions simultaneously) by +2.30 dB on UVG17 and +1.34 dB on UVG8, suggesting that Fourier-space subnetwork selection generalizes better than joint multi-task training. On DAVIS50, PFNR (f-NeRV3) leads MTL by +3.00 dB (27.57 vs. 24.57) and WSN by +6.01 dB (27.57 vs. 21.56), though absolute PSNR drops due to the larger number of sessions and higher resolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

PFNR extends the NeRV architecture for neural video representation by introducing a Fourier Subnetwork Operator (FSO) that adapts the Fourier Neural Operator framework (Li et al., 2020a,b) to the NIR setting, replacing standard convolutional layers with frequency-domain operations. It departs from WSN (Kang et al., 2022a), which applies the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis in standard weight space, by performing sparse subnetwork selection in Fourier space where real and imaginary components are masked independently. Unlike rehearsal-based continual learning methods (iCaRL, ESMER) that require storing high-dimensional video frames, PFNR achieves forget-free sequential compilation purely through architectural sparsity and frozen weight accumulation, drawing on the progressive neural networks paradigm but with a fundamentally different sparsification mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PFNR provides the first replay-free, forget-free method for sequential video neural representation, eliminating the substantial memory overhead of storing video frames for rehearsal. However, the FSO's parameter count grows with layer depth and feature map size, and the DAVIS50 results (27.57 avg PSNR vs. 31.41 for STL) reveal that the approach struggles to scale to 50 sequential sessions, suggesting the current architecture lacks sufficient capacity for large-scale video compilation. Open questions include designing more parameter-efficient FSO variants, whether the Fourier-space sparsity principle generalizes to other NIR tasks (3D, audio), and whether the decoding speed penalty (~31 FPS vs. ~57 FPS for NeRV) can be mitigated for practical deployment.
