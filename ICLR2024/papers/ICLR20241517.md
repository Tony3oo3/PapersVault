## Problem

In-context learning for LLMs requires large volumes of annotated examples as prompts, but obtaining them incurs substantial annotation costs. The prior selective-annotation method Vote-k is not end-to-end (it requires LLM inference on unlabeled data), demands a delicate balance between diversity and representativeness, and lacks theoretical guarantees. This paper seeks an unsupervised, end-to-end selection method with provable approximation bounds that minimizes annotation cost while maximizing in-context learning performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pool of unlabeled text instances (typically 3k randomly subsampled from a training set) and an integer annotation budget m (e.g., 18 or 100). It outputs a subset of exactly m instances to be manually annotated, which then serve as the candidate pool for similarity-based prompt retrieval at inference time. The selection process is entirely label-free and agnostic to the downstream prompt retrieval strategy, so it can be paired with any retrieval mechanism.

## Method

IDEAL constructs a directed graph over unlabeled instances using Sentence-BERT embeddings, where each vertex connects to its k nearest successors with edge weights normalized from cosine similarity. Subset influence is quantified via an independent-cascade diffusion simulation (Algorithm 1): starting from the candidate subset, information propagates along edges with probability equal to the edge weight, and influence equals the number of activated vertices (averaged over 10 runs). A greedy submodular maximization algorithm (Algorithm 2) then iteratively adds the vertex yielding the maximum marginal gain in influence until the budget m is met. Under a submodularity condition on the influence function, the paper proves the greedy solution achieves at least (1−(1−1/m)^m) ≈ 1−1/e of the optimal subset's influence, providing the first theoretical guarantee for selective annotation in ICL.

## Results

IDEAL achieves the best performance in 17 out of 18 evaluation scenarios across 9 datasets (classification, multi-choice, dialogue, and generation) under two annotation budgets (100 and 18), outperforming both Vote-k and Random selection. At budget 100, IDEAL scores 66.4 on MRPC, 51.4 on SST-5, 41.0 on MNLI, 90.6 on DBpedia, 58.9 on RTE, 68.6 on HellaSwag, 52.2 on MWoZ, 58.2 on GeoQuery, and 19.9 ROUGE-L on Xsum. At the tighter budget of 18, IDEAL still leads in 9/9 cases, e.g., 63.0 on MRPC vs. 61.1 (Vote-k) and 57.4 (Random). Additionally, IDEAL requires only 13% of the time consumed by Vote-k during subset selection, achieving a 7.8× average speedup under identical hardware.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Vote-k (Su et al., 2023), the primary selective-annotation baseline, IDEAL wins 17 out of 18 cases across all 9 datasets and both budgets; the sole loss is GeoQuery at budget 100 (58.2 vs. 58.8). IDEAL also reduces subset-selection time by 7.8× on average because it is fully unsupervised and avoids the expensive LLM inference step that Vote-k requires for confidence-based selection. Against K-Means (Lloyd, 1982), IDEAL achieves substantially higher accuracy on all three coreset-selection benchmarks: 63.0 vs. 57.4 on MRPC, 40.0 vs. 35.8 on MNLI, and 67.1 vs. 65.4 on HellaSwag (budget 18). Against Maximizing Facility Location (Lin & Bilmes, 2009), IDEAL similarly leads on all three datasets (63.0 vs. 58.2, 40.0 vs. 38.8, 67.1 vs. 65.2), confirming that graph-based influence maximization captures inter-example similarity relationships more effectively than non-graph coreset methods. Random selection occasionally outperforms Vote-k (3/18 cases), but IDEAL consistently surpasses both, suggesting that influence-driven selection provides a more stable and representative subset than either heuristic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Vote-k (Su et al., 2023), the prior state-of-the-art for selective annotations in in-context learning, which selects diverse and representative subsets from unlabeled pools using confidence-based scoring. The key departure is replacing Vote-k's multi-stage, prediction-dependent pipeline with an end-to-end graph-based influence diffusion process inspired by the independent-cascade model from social network influence maximization (Kempe et al., 2003). By framing subset selection as a submodular maximization problem over a directed similarity graph, the method inherits the classical (1−1/e) approximation guarantee while eliminating the need for explicit diversity/representativeness trade-offs that plague Vote-k.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 7.8× speedup over Vote-k during subset selection is practically significant for LLM-as-a-service settings where inference costs dominate, and the theoretical guarantee provides a principled stopping criterion absent in prior selective annotation work. The Auto-IDEAL extension—using the diffusion process itself to propagate labels automatically—suggests a path toward near-zero manual annotation, though it introduces its own inference costs and failure modes when embedding similarity diverges from task relevance. Open questions include whether the influence metric generalizes to multi-turn or tool-augmented ICL settings, and whether the submodularity assumption (Condition 1) holds robustly across all graph constructions or is dataset-dependent.
