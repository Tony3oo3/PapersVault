## Problem

Modern Vision Transformers (DINOv2, DeiT-III, OpenCLIP) exhibit high-norm outlier tokens in their output feature maps that appear in low-informative background regions, where the model repurposes patch tokens to store and process global image information. This behavior degrades local feature quality, produces uninterpretable attention maps, and breaks downstream tasks like unsupervised object discovery (LOST), even though the earlier DINO model did not suffer from this issue.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240110/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images processed through a Vision Transformer patch embedding, with N additional learnable register tokens appended to the token sequence after the patch embedding layer. At inference, only the [CLS] token and patch tokens are retained as outputs (feature maps, attention maps, or object detections); the register token outputs are discarded. The registers carry no input information and are not used for any downstream prediction—they serve purely as internal computation buffers during the forward pass.

## Method

The paper first characterizes the artifacts: they are ~2% of tokens with roughly 10× higher norm, emerging around middle layers of sufficiently large (≥ViT-L) models after extended training, located in patches that are highly similar to their neighbors (redundant background). Linear probing confirms these outlier tokens discard local position and pixel information while retaining strong global image classification signal. The proposed fix appends N learnable register tokens (analogous to [CLS]) to the input sequence, giving the model dedicated slots for internal global computation so it no longer needs to hijack patch tokens. At the output, registers are simply dropped, leaving clean patch and [CLS] representations. The approach is validated across three training paradigms—supervised (DeiT-III on ImageNet-22k), text-supervised (OpenCLIP), and self-supervised (DINOv2 on ImageNet-22k)—with 4 registers used by default, showing artifact elimination, improved dense prediction (ADE20k, NYUd), and substantially better object discovery (LOST) without any performance regression.

## Results

On linear probing with frozen features, DINOv2+reg achieves 84.8% ImageNet Top-1 (vs. 84.3% without registers), 47.9% ADE20k mIoU (vs. 46.6%), and 0.366 NYUd RMSE (vs. 0.378). DeiT-III+reg matches the baseline on ImageNet (84.7%) and slightly improves ADE20k (39.1% vs. 38.9%). For unsupervised object discovery via LOST, DINOv2+reg jumps from 35.3 to 55.4 corloc on VOC 2007 and from 40.2 to 60.0 on VOC 2012, while DeiT-III+reg improves from 11.7 to 27.1 on VOC 2007.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three training paradigms (supervised DeiT-III, text-supervised OpenCLIP, self-supervised DINOv2) each with and without register tokens. On dense prediction tasks (Table 2a), DINOv2+reg improves over DINOv2 on all three metrics (+0.5 ImageNet, +1.3 ADE20k mIoU, −0.012 NYUd RMSE), while DeiT-III+reg and OpenCLIP+reg essentially match their baselines with marginal gains. The most dramatic gains appear in unsupervised object discovery (Table 3): DINOv2+reg gains +20.1 corloc on VOC 2007 (35.3→55.4) and +19.8 on VOC 2012 (40.2→60.0), and DeiT-III+reg gains +15.4 on VOC 2007 (11.7→27.1). OpenCLIP+reg is the one case where registers slightly hurt object discovery (−1.7 corloc on VOC 2007), which the authors attribute to the value projection layer already filtering out outliers in that model. Overall, registers never degrade linear probing performance and substantially improve feature map smoothness and object discovery viability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DINO/DINOv2 line of self-supervised ViT pretraining and extends the analysis to supervised (DeiT-III) and text-supervised (OpenCLIP) ViTs, revealing that high-norm outlier tokens are a universal phenomenon rather than a DINOv2-specific quirk. The proposed fix—appending learnable register tokens to the input sequence—draws directly from the Memory Transformer (Burtsev et al., 2020) in NLP, but the paper's key conceptual departure is showing that this mechanism is not an external addition but an isolation of behavior that already emerges naturally in large ViTs. The work also repositions DINO (Caron et al., 2021) from being a special case with clean attention maps to being the exception, with DINOv2 and all other modern ViTs matching the 'baseline' artifact-prone behavior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a handful of learnable tokens (adding <2% FLOPs) eliminates feature-map artifacts across all training paradigms and improves dense prediction, object discovery, and interpretability makes this a broadly adoptable architectural change for any ViT-based pipeline. Open questions remain about why artifacts only emerge in sufficiently large and long-trained models, why DINO avoids them, and why register norms appear quantized. The paper also leaves unresolved the mechanism by which OpenCLIP's value projection naturally filters outliers, and the specialization behavior of individual registers (some attending to borders, others to centers) is noted but not regularized or exploited.
