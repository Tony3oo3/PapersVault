## Problem

Pre-trained molecular language models face three critical limitations: SMILES-based representations produce syntactically invalid molecules, most models are restricted to synthetic compounds while neglecting structurally complex natural products, and generated molecules often suffer from 'molecular hallucinations'—structurally valid molecules that fail to exhibit the anticipated chemical properties or biological activity in practice.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242110/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes molecules encoded as SELFIES strings (a 100%-robust molecular language with a compact 185-token vocabulary) and, for optimization tasks, a property target (p-logP, QED, or binding affinity to a specific protein target) plus a Tanimoto similarity constraint δ. It outputs generated or optimized molecules as SELFIES strings that are guaranteed chemically valid by construction. For constrained optimization, the input molecule is modified while maintaining a user-specified structural similarity threshold; for docking, the output is a ligand with improved binding affinity to a specified protein pocket (ESR1 or ACAA1).

## Method

MOLGEN employs a two-stage domain-agnostic pre-training: first, a BART-based denoising objective reconstructs 100M corrupted SELFIES strings to internalize molecular grammar and semantics; second, domain-agnostic molecular prefix tuning prepends shared tunable prefix vectors to the keys and values of multi-head attention, acting as domain instructors that influence the entire model's parameters across synthetic and natural product domains simultaneously. The key innovation is a chemical feedback paradigm that uses a margin-based rank loss to align the model's sequence-level probability rankings with property-score rankings of its own generated candidates, combined with token-level cross-entropy (with label smoothing) to preserve generative diversity. This self-reflective loop—generate candidates, score them, and penalize probability inversions—steers the model away from molecular hallucinations without requiring external annotated data or retrieval databases.

## Results

On molecular distribution learning, MOLGEN achieves 100% validity on both synthetic and natural product domains without external valency checks, while also attaining the best SNN (0.9996 synthetic, 0.8148 NP), lowest FCD (0.0015 synthetic, 0.6519 NP), and highest Novelty (1.000 synthetic, 0.9987 NP). For targeted molecule discovery, MOLGEN reaches a penalized logP of 80.30 (no length limit), nearly doubling the next-best MARS (44.99), and achieves binding affinities of 0.13 (ESR1) and 3.36 (ACAA1), substantially outperforming LIMO (0.72 and 37 respectively). In constrained optimization at δ=0.6, MOLGEN yields a mean p-logP improvement of 12.08, more than 2× the next-best GRAPHAF (4.98).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOLGEN outperforms all 14 baselines across every main evaluation. Against graph-based VAEs (JT-VAE, AAE, LATENTGAN) and RL methods (GCPN, MOLDQN, MARS, GRAPHAF, GRAPHDF), MOLGEN achieves dramatically higher p-logP scores (80.30 vs. MARS's 44.99) and binding affinities (0.13 ESR1 vs. LIMO's 0.72; 3.36 ACAA1 vs. LIMO's 37). Among SELFIES-based models, LIMO also guarantees 100% validity but collapses on scaffold similarity (0.0005 on NP) and FCD (31.84 on NP), whereas MOLGEN maintains strong structural fidelity. The closest SMILES-based PLM competitor, Chemformer, achieves competitive FCD on synthetic molecules (0.0061) but falls well short on natural products (SNN 0.5875 vs. 0.8148; FCD 0.8346 vs. 0.6519) and cannot match MOLGEN's property optimization gains. In constrained optimization, MOLGEN (12.08 at δ=0.6) surpasses retrieval-augmented RETMOL (3.78) and the SELFIES-based regression transformer RT (2.21) by a wide margin, confirming that the chemical feedback paradigm provides a more effective optimization signal than external databases or property predictors.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOLGEN builds on the BART denoising seq2seq pre-training paradigm (Lewis et al., 2020) but replaces SMILES with SELFIES as the molecular representation, making it the first work to pre-train a molecular language model on SELFIES. It adapts prefix-tuning (Li & Liang, 2021) into a domain-agnostic mechanism that influences full model parameters rather than only prefix matrices, enabling knowledge transfer across synthetic and natural product domains. The chemical feedback paradigm draws on the BRIO rank loss (Liu et al., 2022) to align model-generated probability rankings with property-score rankings, distinguishing it from prior approaches that rely on reinforcement learning, external retrieval databases, or supervised fine-tuning on ideal samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper formalizes 'molecular hallucination'—structurally valid but functionally useless molecules—as a distinct failure mode and offers a practical, data-efficient remedy (chemical feedback via rank loss) that does not require external annotated databases or reward functions. Extending evaluation to natural products, which exhibit far greater scaffold diversity and structural complexity than synthetic molecules, broadens the applicability of molecular generation beyond the drug-like domain that dominates prior benchmarks. Key limitations include single-target optimization only (multi-objective ranking ambiguity), 2D-only generation, and computational cost of training on 100M+ molecules; open questions include whether the chemical feedback paradigm generalizes to multi-objective settings and whether 3D conformational information can be incorporated.
