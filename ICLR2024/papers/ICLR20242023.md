## Problem

Vector quantization in VQ-VAEs requires complex auxiliary machinery—commitment losses, codebook EMA updates, entropy penalties, codebook reseeding, and code splitting—to achieve reasonable codebook utilization, and still suffers from codebook collapse at larger sizes. This paper asks whether a far simpler quantization scheme can serve as a drop-in replacement for VQ without any of these tricks while maintaining competitive performance across generation and dense prediction tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242023/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates in two stages: a VAE (convolutional or transformer-based) is first trained to encode inputs into a low-dimensional representation, then a transformer (masked or autoregressive) is trained on the frozen discrete codes. For image generation, a class label produces a 256×256 ImageNet image; for dense prediction tasks, an RGB or grayscale image produces a depth map, colorized image, or panoptic segmentation mask. The key structural detail is that the encoder's final layer projects to very few dimensions (d < 10, versus d ≥ 512 in VQ), and each dimension is independently quantized to L discrete levels, yielding an implicit codebook of size L^d with no learnable parameters.

## Method

Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) replaces VQ's nearest-neighbor lookup in a learned codebook with a fixed, parameter-free operation: the encoder output z ∈ R^d is bounded per-channel via f(z_i) = ⌊L_i/2⌋·tanh(z_i) and then rounded to the nearest integer, producing a quantized code from a product codebook of size ∏L_i. Gradients flow through the non-differentiable rounding via the straight-through estimator, and no auxiliary losses (commitment, codebook, entropy) or tricks (EMA, reseeding, splitting) are needed. The core insight is that VAEs have sufficient encoder/decoder capacity to absorb the non-linearity that VQ's learned Voronoi partition provides, so a simple fixed grid in a much lower-dimensional space achieves comparable representational complexity. The paper validates FSQ as a drop-in replacement in two architecturally distinct frameworks—MaskGIT (convolutional VQ-GAN + masked transformer) and UViM (transformer VQ-VAE + encoder-decoder transformer)—across image generation, depth estimation, colorization, and panoptic segmentation, achieving within 0.5–3% of VQ baselines while reaching ~100% codebook utilization without any auxiliary mechanisms.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 image generation with MaskGIT, FSQ achieves a Sampling FID of 4.534 (Precision 0.864, Recall 0.453) with 100% codebook usage, compared to 4.509 for VQ (81% usage) and 4.59 for the diffusion-based ADM baseline. On UViM dense prediction tasks, FSQ achieves RMSE 0.473 on NYU Depth v2 (vs. 0.468 for VQ), PQ 43.2 on COCO Panoptic (vs. 43.4 for VQ), and FID-5k 17.55 on ImageNet Colorization (vs. 16.90 for VQ), with 99–100% codebook usage across all tasks without any auxiliary losses or codebook splitting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the official MaskGIT(VQ) GitHub checkpoint, FSQ improves Sampling FID from 4.916 to 4.534 and codebook usage from 81% to 100%, while also achieving higher Precision (0.864 vs. 0.836). Compared to the diffusion-based ADM baseline, FSQ achieves a slightly better FID (4.534 vs. 4.59) with comparable Precision (0.864 vs. 0.83) but lower Recall (0.453 vs. 0.52). On UViM tasks, FSQ is marginally worse than VQ on all three metrics (RMSE +0.005, PQ −0.2, FID-5k +0.65) but achieves 99–100% codebook usage without codebook splitting, whereas VQ without splitting collapses to 0.78% usage. Against external baselines, FSQ outperforms ColTran on colorization (17.55 vs. 19.37 FID-5k) but is slightly behind DenseDepth on depth estimation (0.473 vs. 0.465 RMSE) and DETR-R101 on panoptic segmentation (43.2 vs. 45.1 PQ). The key finding is that FSQ matches VQ performance within 0.5–3% across all tasks while eliminating all auxiliary losses, EMA updates, and codebook splitting mechanisms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes bounded scalar quantization from the neural image compression literature (Mentzer et al., 2018; Tschannen et al., 2018; Agustsson et al., 2019) as a drop-in replacement for vector quantization in VQ-VAE-based representation learning. The key conceptual move is recognizing that by projecting to a small number of dimensions (d < 10) and bounding each to L discrete levels, the product structure yields an implicit codebook of size L^d that matches VQ codebook sizes, while eliminating all auxiliary losses, learnable codebook parameters, and the associated optimization tricks (commitment losses, codebook reseeding, code splitting, entropy penalties). The paper positions FSQ as a simplification of the VQ-VAE formulation (Van Den Oord et al., 2017) that trades the learnable high-dimensional Voronoi partition for a fixed low-dimensional grid, arguing that the VAE's encoder/decoder capacity can absorb the non-linearity that VQ provides.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FSQ offers a substantially simpler and more robust quantization mechanism that achieves ~100% codebook utilization by design, sidestepping the well-documented codebook collapse problem that has motivated a large body of VQ improvement work. The finding that VQ performance degrades for large codebooks while FSQ continues to improve suggests the field has been over-engineering the quantizer. Open questions include whether the low-dimensional, fixed-grid structure of FSQ limits expressiveness in settings where VQ's learnable partition is genuinely advantageous (the paper notes VQ marginally outperforms FSQ at small codebook sizes), and whether FSQ extends to other modalities (audio, video) and architectures beyond the two tested here. The modest 0.5–3% performance gap across diverse tasks and architectures makes FSQ a practical, low-risk alternative for practitioners already using VQ-VAE pipelines.
