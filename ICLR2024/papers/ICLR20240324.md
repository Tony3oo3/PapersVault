## Problem

Existing positional encoding schemes for transformers (absolute and relative PE) were designed for 1D NLP sequences and treat camera poses as flat vectors added to tokens, failing to respect the non-commutative Euclidean geometry underlying multi-view images. This makes it difficult for transformer-based novel view synthesis models to learn strong 3D geometric priors, especially in sparse wide-baseline settings where the model must infer unobserved scene regions from limited context views.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of context RGB images (e.g., 2–5 views at 128×128 or 256×256 resolution) along with their 4×4 camera extrinsic matrices and 3×3 intrinsic matrices, plus the target view's camera parameters. Each image is split into patches (e.g., 8×8 or 16×16), and every patch token is associated with a geometric attribute g = (c_i, θ_h, θ_w) ∈ SE(3) × SO(2) × SO(2) combining the camera pose and the patch's 2D angular position. The output is a rendered RGB pixel or patch for the target viewpoint, produced by a cross-attention decoder that queries the encoded scene representation.

## Method

GTA replaces standard attention with a geometry-aware variant that applies relative group transformations directly to Q, K, and V vectors: O = P_g ⊙ Attn(P_g^T ⊙ Q, P_g^{-1} ⊙ K, P_g^{-1} ⊙ V). The representation matrix ρ_g is a block-diagonal composition of four irreducible components: the SE(3) homogeneous camera transformation (4×4), SO(3) rotation via Wigner-D matrices of degrees 1 and 2, and two SO(2) 2D rotation matrices (for horizontal and vertical image position) with multiple frequencies analogous to Fourier features. This transforms all QKV features into a shared coordinate space before computing dot-product attention, then maps the output back to each token's local frame, requiring no additional learned parameters. The approach is invariant to the choice of global reference coordinate system and, unlike RoPE, also transforms value vectors to maintain coordinate consistency between keys and values.

## Results

On CLEVR-TR, GTA achieves 39.63 PSNR, outperforming RePAST (37.27) and SRT (33.51) by 2.36 and 6.12 dB respectively. On MSN-Hard, SRT+GTA reaches 25.72 PSNR / 0.289 LPIPS / 0.798 SSIM, surpassing RePAST (24.48 / 0.348 / 0.751) and SRT (24.27 / 0.368 / 0.741). On real-world datasets, Du et al.+GTA improves over Du et al. by 1.20 dB on RealEstate10k (22.85 vs 21.65 PSNR) and 0.75 dB on ACID (24.10 vs 23.35 PSNR). On ImageNet 256×256 generative modeling, DiT+GTA achieves 158.2 IS and 5.87 FID-50K, beating DiT+2D-RoPE (151.8 / 6.26) and vanilla DiT (145.3 / 7.02).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GTA consistently outperforms all compared baselines across every dataset and metric. On the synthetic NVS benchmarks, GTA beats the strongest transformer baseline RePAST by 2.36 dB on CLEVR-TR and 1.24 dB on MSN-Hard, while also improving LPIPS by 0.059 and SSIM by 0.047 over RePAST on MSN-Hard. Against the APE-based SRT, the gains are even larger (6.12 dB on CLEVR-TR, 1.45 dB on MSN-Hard). On real-world datasets (RealEstate10k, ACID), GTA improves the state-of-the-art Du et al. (2023) model by 1.20 and 0.75 dB in PSNR respectively, with corresponding LPIPS reductions. In the PE-scheme comparison on CLEVR-TR (Table 4), GTA (38.99) surpasses RoPE+FTL (38.18), Frustum Embedding (37.23), GBT (35.63), ElemMul (34.74), SRT (33.21), and MLN (32.48). On the 2D ImageNet generative task, GTA as a positional encoding for DiT-B/2 outperforms both vanilla DiT and DiT+2D-RoPE in both IS and FID, confirming that the geometric transform mechanism generalizes beyond 3D vision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of encoding geometric information via additive positional embeddings (APE) or attention biases (RPE) by instead applying group representations directly as transformations on QKV features within the attention mechanism. Conceptually, it extends the philosophy of transforming autoencoders and equivariant networks into the transformer attention context, while building practically on SRT and Du et al.'s NVS architectures by simply replacing their positional encodings with GTA. The closest prior method is RePAST, which applies relative camera transformations to ray embeddings before Fourier encoding, but GTA applies the transformation to the feature vectors themselves rather than to auxiliary geometric descriptors, and additionally transforms value vectors (unlike RoPE, which only rotates queries and keys).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GTA provides a parameter-free, group-theoretic framework for making transformers geometrically aware that is invariant to the choice of reference coordinate system—a property that existing PE schemes lack and that matters for generalization across scenes. The finding that transforming value vectors (not just queries and keys) is critical for NVS, and that the mechanism transfers to 2D image generation with DiT, suggests the approach has broader applicability beyond 3D vision. A key limitation is the reliance on known or estimated camera poses, and the authors identify joint learning of geometry and features as an important open direction. The moderate but consistent gains (1–2 dB PSNR) over strong baselines like RePAST, combined with improved learning efficiency (reaching RePAST-level performance in 1/6 of training steps on MSN-Hard), make this a practical drop-in improvement for transformer-based NVS pipelines.
