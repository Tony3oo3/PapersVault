## Problem

When adapting a pre-trained model to a new target distribution with very limited labeled data (as few as 4–256 points), standard linear probing is not the most sample-efficient strategy because pre-trained embeddings contain redundant, non-predictive, and noisy dimensions that inflate variance without reducing bias. The paper asks: what is the most data-efficient way to select and combine features from a frozen embedding space so that a small target dataset can reliably identify the correct decision boundary under distribution shift?

## Input / Output

The method consumes cached feature embeddings from a frozen pre-trained backbone (e.g., 1024-dim vectors from CLIP-ViT-L/16) along with a large source labeled dataset and a very small target labeled dataset (2–128 images per label). It outputs a linear classifier over a reduced d-dimensional projected feature space (where d can be as small as 1), producing class predictions. The entire adaptation pipeline involves at most two linear layers trained on cached vectors, requiring no GPU and completing ~30k experimental runs in 24 hours on four CPUs.

## Method

PRO² is a two-step framework. In the Project step, it learns a linear projection Π: ℝ^D → ℝ^d by sequentially optimizing each basis vector to minimize source classification loss subject to orthogonality against all previously learned vectors (enforced via QR decomposition after each gradient step), yielding a diverse, predictive, and non-redundant feature basis. In the Probe step, it fits a linear classifier on top of the projected embeddings using only the small target dataset, effectively interpolating among the orthogonal features. The key theoretical insight is that this procedure induces a favorable bias-variance tradeoff: variance scales as O(d/M) rather than O(D/M), while bias is governed by the nullspace norm ‖(I_D − M_d) w_T*‖, which decreases slowly under severe shift but rapidly under mild shift. Consequently, a small d suffices for mild distribution shifts while larger d is needed for severe ones, and the orthogonality constraint ensures the learned basis covers both shortcut and robust features that different target distributions may require.

## Results

PRO² achieves 5–15% higher target accuracy than standard linear probing (DFR) in the few-shot regime (2–128 samples per label) across all six evaluated datasets. On CelebA-Balanced with only 2 samples per label, PRO² reaches 94.1% accuracy versus 82.4% for DFR and 82.9% for Random Projection. On the more challenging Living17 and FMoW benchmarks (Table 1), PRO² leads at 1 sample per label with 91.2% (Living17) and 20.2% (FMoW), outperforming DFR by 4.1 and 2.7 points respectively. The method is best or tied for best across every dataset and every target data size tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRO² substantially outperforms DFR (standard linear probing, Kirichenko et al. 2022) in the low-data regime across all six datasets; for example, on CelebA-Balanced with 2 samples/label, PRO² achieves 94.1% vs. DFR's 82.4% (+11.7 points). Against Teney et al. (2021), which learns diverse features via gradient alignment rather than explicit orthogonality, PRO² wins on 3 of 4 main datasets on average and pulls ahead more clearly as target data increases (e.g., CelebA-Balanced at 128/label: 81.8% vs. DFR's 78.4%), indicating the orthogonality constraint provides better feature coverage under severe shift. On the additional Living17 and FMoW benchmarks, PRO² leads both Random Projection and DFR at every data size. In comparisons with D-BAT (Pagliardini et al. 2022) and DivDis (Lee et al. 2022b), which additionally require unlabeled target data, PRO² matches or slightly exceeds their accuracy (e.g., Waterbirds-Balanced at 2/label: 91.8% vs. D-BAT 90.1% and DivDis 89.5%) while using no unlabeled data, making it more practical in settings where only a handful of labeled target points are available.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRO² repurposes the idea of learning diverse, orthogonal features (explored in diverse-classifier literature like Teney et al. 2021 and Pagliardini et al. 2022) and combines it with the linear probing paradigm that has become the default for distribution-shift adaptation (Kirichenko et al. 2022, Mehta et al. 2022, Rosenfeld et al. 2022). Unlike prior diversification methods that use soft penalty terms or require unlabeled target data, PRO² hard-enforces orthogonality via QR decomposition during projected gradient descent, and provides a theoretical bias-variance analysis connecting the projection dimension to shift severity. The method is also conceptually related to LDA and PCA, but is supervised, enforces orthogonality, and explicitly targets the few-shot adaptation regime where M ≪ D.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the widely-adopted default of standard linear probing for few-shot adaptation, showing that a simple orthogonal projection step on source data yields 5–15% gains with very limited target data (as few as 4 samples per class). The theoretical bias-variance tradeoff (variance scales as O(d/M), bias as O(√(D−d)·KL(p_S‖p_T))) gives practitioners a principled way to choose the projection dimension based on expected shift severity. Limitations include diminishing benefits with larger target datasets and the need for more features (and thus more data) under severe shifts. Open directions include unsupervised feature selection without labeled target data and extension to active learning settings.
