## Problem

Existing 3D generative models require large, diverse datasets, but high-quality 3D data with fine geometry and texture is orders of magnitude scarcer than 2D images, and many artistically designed models have unique structures with only a single instance available. This paper addresses the gap of learning a generative model from just one 3D textured shape to synthesize diverse, high-quality variations that locally resemble the input.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241494/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single textured 3D mesh as input, which is voxelized into a 3D grid of up to 256³ with 4 channels (truncated signed distance + RGB) or 9 channels for PBR materials (base color, metallic, roughness, normal). At inference, it produces new 3D textured meshes extracted via Marching Cubes at 256 resolution, with UV-mapped textures rendered at 2048×2048 via xatlas. A distinctive capability is retargeting: by changing the spatial dimensions of the initial Gaussian noise, the model generates outputs at different sizes and aspect ratios while preserving local content.

## Method

Sin3DM operates in two stages: first, a triplane autoencoder compresses the 3D grid into three axis-aligned 2D feature maps (128 spatial resolution, 12 channels) that implicitly encode the SDF and texture fields; second, a DDPM is trained on these triplane latents to learn their distribution. The denoising network is a shallow fully-convolutional U-Net with only one level of depth, giving it a receptive field of roughly 40% of the feature map size to capture local patch statistics while avoiding overfitting to the single input. Each ResBlock uses a TriplaneConv module that aggregates cross-plane features via axis-aligned average pooling, enabling coherent generation across the three planes. The model predicts the clean latent h₀ directly rather than the added noise ε, which is simpler in the single-example setting where h₀ is fixed. At inference, sampled triplane latents are decoded into an SDF grid, meshed via Marching Cubes, and textured by querying the decoder at barycentric-interpolated 3D positions on the UV-unwrapped surface.

## Results

Across 10 test shapes, Sin3DM achieves the best geometry quality (G-Qual. = 0.156 SSFID) and texture quality (T-Qual. = 0.028 SIFID), substantially outperforming SSG (0.238 / 0.099) and Sin3DGen (3.390 / 0.036). On diversity metrics, Sin3DM scores 0.224 (G-Div.) and 0.133 (T-Div.), which are competitive but slightly below Sin3DGen on geometry diversity (0.261) and SSG on texture diversity (0.150). The ablation study confirms that removing the triplane-aware convolution, the encoder, or switching to ε-prediction all degrade quality scores significantly (e.g., G-Qual. rises from 0.156 to 0.347, 0.608, and 0.925 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sin3DM is compared against two single-instance 3D generation baselines: SSG (Wu & Zheng, 2022), a multi-scale GAN on voxel pyramids, and Sin3DGen (Li et al., 2023), a patch-matching method on Plenoxels radiance fields. On geometry quality (SSFID), Sin3DM achieves 0.156, beating SSG (0.238) by ~34% and Sin3DGen (3.390) by over 20×; on texture quality (SIFID), it scores 0.028 versus 0.099 for SSG and 0.036 for Sin3DGen. On diversity, Sin3DM is competitive but not always best: it slightly trails Sin3DGen on geometry diversity (0.224 vs. 0.261) and SSG on texture diversity (0.133 vs. 0.150), while beating both on the other diversity metric. Visually, SSG produces noisy geometry and blurry textures due to its voxel-resolution ceiling, and Sin3DGen often yields broken mesh surfaces and distorted textures from its radiance-field-to-mesh conversion, whereas Sin3DM generates clean high-resolution geometry with fine texture detail.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-instance generation paradigm—well established in 2D (SinGAN, SinDiffusion)—to 3D textured shapes, and simultaneously brings diffusion models (DDPM) into the single-example 3D setting where prior work relied on GANs (SSG) or patch matching (Sin3DGen). The triplane latent representation from Convolutional Occupancy Networks provides the compact 3D encoding, while the triplane-aware convolution block borrows cross-plane interaction from Rodin. The key conceptual departure is from large-dataset 3D diffusion models (3DGen, Lion, etc.) by showing that a limited-receptive-field U-Net over triplane features suffices to capture patch-level statistics from a single shape, analogous to how SinGAN's patch discriminators constrain 2D generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work makes single-asset 3D variation generation practical for artists and studios who possess unique models without large training sets, and the triplane-latent + limited-receptive-field recipe is a reusable pattern for other single-instance 3D tasks. However, the paper acknowledges that generated variations are primarily axis-aligned, the quality-diversity trade-off is controlled only by receptive field size, and the method cannot leverage external priors—limiting its applicability to shapes with locally repetitive structure. An open direction the authors highlight is combining single-instance learning with large pretrained diffusion models (e.g., via fine-tuning à la DreamBooth) to produce more semantically diverse variations.
