## Problem

Existing deep clustering methods are designed for nominal data and fail on orderable data (e.g., facial age, medical severity) where adjacent classes are inherently hard to distinguish and objects possess an intrinsic ordering. No prior method can simultaneously discover the underlying clusters and their meaningful order relationship without any supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241658/cropped_page1_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled instances (RGB/grayscale images, text sentences, or speech utterances) through a pretrained encoder (VGG16, BERT, or Wav2Vec2) and produces k ordered clusters whose indices encode a discovered sequence, a learned embedding space where instances are sorted compactly along a chain of line segments defined by cluster centroids, and a continuous rank estimate for any unseen test instance obtained via K-NN averaging over cluster indices. The number of clusters k is specified a priori, and no labels are used at any stage.

## Method

The core contribution is an ordered k-means that extends ordinary k-means by penalizing each instance's deviation from three consecutive centroids (its own plus the two adjacent ones) rather than just its assigned centroid, causing clusters to lay out sequentially in the embedding space. The full UOL algorithm alternates between this ordered k-means (with centroid, NCC, and permutation rules) and fine-tuning the encoder with two losses: a straight-line loss that forces each instance to lie collinearly with its nearest consecutive centroids, and a deviation loss that suppresses the distance to those centroids beyond a threshold. Centroids are treated as learnable parameters during fine-tuning to guide cluster regions. Rank estimation for unseen instances is then performed by a simple K-NN search in the final embedding space, averaging the cluster indices of the K nearest training neighbors.

## Results

On MORPH II (setting A, k=6), UOL achieves an SRCC of 0.632 and MAE of 1.122, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline MiCE (SRCC 0.260, MAE 1.568). On the medical assessment datasets DR and RetinaMNIST (k=5), UOL reaches SRCCs of 0.333 and 0.567 respectively, again leading all baselines. For unsupervised rank estimation, UOL achieves MAE of 1.20 on MORPH II (setting A), approaching the supervised OL upper bound of 0.65 while far exceeding random guess (1.94). On language and speech data (French sentiment, Speechocean762), UOL attains SRCCs of 0.448 and 0.672 at k=5, roughly doubling the best conventional baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UOL outperforms all six conventional deep clustering baselines (DAC, DeepCluster, IIC, MiCE, IDFD, ProPos) on every dataset and every k value tested. The strongest baseline is MiCE, which achieves SRCC 0.260 on MORPH II (A, k=6); UOL more than doubles this to 0.632. DeepCluster and IDFD are particularly weak on ordered clustering, often yielding near-zero SRCC (e.g., DeepCluster 0.051 on MORPH II B, k=6), because they use ordinary k-means that ignores cluster ordering. On the medical datasets (DR, RetinaMNIST), UOL's advantage is most pronounced: it reaches SRCC 0.567 on RetinaMNIST versus 0.495 for MiCE and 0.066 for IDFD. For unsupervised rank estimation, UOL (MAE 1.20 on MORPH II A) substantially beats the best conventional method ProPos (MAE 1.43) and approaches the supervised OL upper bound (MAE 0.65), demonstrating that the learned ordered embedding carries meaningful rank information without any labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends ordinary k-means (Gersho & Gray, 1991) into an ordered k-means that penalizes deviation from adjacent cluster centroids, and wraps it in an alternating deep clustering loop similar to DeepCluster (Caron et al., 2018) and IDFD (Tao et al., 2021). However, it departs fundamentally from those methods by treating cluster indices as carrying ordinal meaning rather than arbitrary labels, and from prior order learning work (Lim et al., 2020; Lee & Kim, 2021) by requiring no ground-truth ranks at all. The straight-line loss and deviation loss are novel objectives designed specifically to enforce the order desideratum that index differences should be proportional to embedding-space distances.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithm to discover both cluster structure and their hidden ordering in orderable data without any supervision, opening a path toward reducing annotation costs in domains like medical severity grading and facial age estimation where adjacent classes are inherently ambiguous. The authors acknowledge a key limitation: the method assumes a total order over classes, whereas many real-world orderable datasets (e.g., facial expressions) may only admit a partial order, which they leave for future work. Performance also degrades on highly diverse environments (CLAP2015) and subjective criteria (aesthetic scoring), suggesting the approach is most reliable when the underlying order is physically grounded (age, disease severity).
