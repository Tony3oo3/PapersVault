## Problem

Existing human-scene interaction methods either require separate policy networks per task (limiting versatility) or rely on kinematic models that produce physically implausible motions. No prior system supports unified, long-horizon, multi-object interaction control driven by natural language commands, and training typically demands labor-intensive motion capture annotations for each interaction type.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240970/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural language command (e.g., 'lie down on the bed'), a scene described by object meshes with fine-grained part annotations and point clouds, and a fixed set of 15 whole-body humanoid joint names. The LLM Planner outputs a structured Chain of Contacts plan — an ordered list of contact steps, each containing tuples of {object, object part, joint, contact type (contact/not contact/not care), relative direction (up/down/front/back/left/right)}. The Unified Controller then produces a physically simulated whole-body motion sequence that executes the plan step by step, with step completion detected by distance and direction thresholds.

## Method

The core contribution is a unified interaction representation called Chain of Contacts (CoC), which decomposes any interaction into ordered sequences of human joint–object part contact pairs, enabling a single controller to handle diverse tasks. A GPT-4-based LLM Planner uses prompt engineering (combining language commands, scene object/part layouts, and joint names) to generate CoC plans, making training interaction-annotation-free. The Unified Controller builds on AMP (adversarial motion priors + goal-conditioned RL in Isaac Gym), with a TaskParser that converts each CoC step into uniform task observations (joint-to-nearest-point vectors) and contact rewards (exponential distance + directional alignment terms). Two key design choices enable versatility: adaptive contact weights that dynamically up-weight hard-to-optimize contact pairs during training, and an ego-centric heightmap for scene-aware navigation and collision avoidance in dense real-world scans.

## Results

On the ScenePlan benchmark, UniHSI achieves 94.3% success rate on Sit, 81.5% on Lie Down, and 97.5% on Reach, with contact errors of 0.032, 0.061, and 0.016 respectively. On the broader ScenePlan evaluation (Table 2), UniHSI reaches 91.1% success on simple PartNet tasks, 63.2% on medium, and 39.7% on hard multi-object plans, with contact errors ranging from 0.038 to 0.101. Generalization to real ScanNet scenes yields 76.1% success on simple tasks and 32.2% on hard tasks, confirming robustness to unseen scanned environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniHSI outperforms all task-specific baselines on the Sit task (94.3% vs. InterPhys 93.7%, AMP 77.3%, NSM/SAMP 75.0%) and achieves the lowest contact error (0.032 vs. SAMP 0.06, AMP 0.090). On the challenging Lie Down task, UniHSI reaches 81.5% success, surpassing InterPhys (80.0%), SAMP (50.0%), and AMP (21.3%), though SAMP reports a marginally lower contact error (0.05 vs. 0.061). On Reach, AMP slightly edges out UniHSI in success rate (98.1% vs. 97.5%) while both achieve identical contact error (0.016). The vanilla combination baseline (AMP-VC), which naively merges multiple tasks into one model, degrades substantially (62.5%/20.1%/90.3%), demonstrating that UniHSI's unified CoC representation and TaskParser are critical for effective multi-task learning without interference.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniHSI builds directly on AMP (Peng et al., 2021) for its physics-based controller with adversarial motion priors, but departs fundamentally from prior physics-based HSI methods (InterPhys, PADL) that train separate policies per task by introducing a unified Chain-of-Contacts representation and a TaskParser that converts diverse interactions into a single observation/reward format. It also extends the language-conditioned HSI line (HUMANISE, ScenDiffuser, PADL) by replacing rule-based or BERT-based task selection with an LLM planner that generates structured multi-step contact plans, and by grounding execution in physical simulation rather than kinematic synthesis. The interaction annotation-free training paradigm, leveraging LLM world knowledge to generate the ScenePlan dataset, is a notable departure from prior methods that require paired motion-object datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UniHSI provides the first unified physical HSI framework controllable via natural language, significantly reducing the annotation burden that has limited the scalability of prior HSI systems. The Chain-of-Contacts abstraction is a practical and extensible interface that could be adopted by downstream applications in embodied AI and VR. However, performance degrades substantially on complex multi-object tasks (39.7% success on hard PartNet plans), LLM planning correctness remains imperfect (71.9% with GPT-4), and generalization to real scanned scenes still shows a notable gap. Open questions include whether the CoC formulation scales to open-world scenes with unstructured objects, whether the LLM planner can be replaced by learned planners for lower latency, and whether the framework can support real-time interactive control.
