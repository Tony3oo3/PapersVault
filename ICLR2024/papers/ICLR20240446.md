## Problem

Pre-trained diffusion models have been shown to contain semantically enriched intermediate representations, but producing fine-grained pixel-level segmentation maps from them has always required additional knowledge such as mask annotations or hand-crafted boundary priors. It remains unclear to what extent a pre-trained diffusion model alone encodes accurate pixel-level semantic relations, particularly given that semantically meaningful feature maps reside only in spatially low-dimensional layers (e.g., 16×16) far below image resolution.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single real RGB image (resized to approximately 512×512 with dimensions divisible by 64) and produces K class-agnostic segmentation masks at full image resolution (e.g., 512×512, default K=30). No annotations, text prompts, or additional training are required; the entire pipeline operates on the pre-trained Stable Diffusion v1.4 checkpoint. The output masks are well-delineated and capture moderately small objects, though they occasionally fail on extremely fine details like animal legs or facial parts.

## Method

The framework first inverts the input image into the DDPM denoising trajectory, then extracts query vectors from the first cross-attention layer of the 16×16 upward U-Net blocks at timestep t_f=1 and applies k-means to obtain low-resolution (16×16) segmentation masks. The core insight is that perturbing a sub-region of these low-resolution feature maps during denoising selectively alters only the pixels semantically related to that sub-region in the generated image. Concretely, for each low-res mask, the method runs two modulated denoising passes (adding +λ and −λ offsets to the masked region of the third cross-attention layer at timestep t_m=281) while injecting original attention maps to preserve structure, then computes a difference map whose magnitude encodes the strength of semantic correspondence between each image pixel and that mask. Each pixel is assigned to the mask with the strongest correspondence, yielding a full-resolution segmentation map without any additional training or hand-crafted priors.

## Results

Under the modified evaluation protocol, EmerDiff achieves mIoU of 33.1 on ADE20K (AD150), 45.7 on PASCAL-Context (PC59), 45.8 on COCO-Stuff (CS27), and 37.1 on Cityscapes (City19), consistently outperforming all baselines across all six dataset splits. Compared to naively upsampled Stable Diffusion masks (which share the same mask embeddings), EmerDiff improves mIoU by 4.0 points on ADE20K (33.1 vs. 29.1) and 3.7 points on COCO-Stuff-27 (45.8 vs. 42.1), validating the effectiveness of the modulated denoising pipeline. In annotation-free open-vocabulary segmentation, combining EmerDiff with MaskCLIP yields a substantial gain (11.5→15.9 mIoU on ADE20K), while TCL+Ours improves from 14.6 to 17.4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EmerDiff outperforms all DINO-based baselines by a wide margin: on ADE20K it achieves 33.1 mIoU versus 19.1 for DINO and 24.0 for DINOSAUR, and on COCO-Stuff-27 it reaches 45.8 versus 36.6 for STEGO. Against text-aligned CLIP-based encoders, the gains are equally clear: 33.1 vs. 22.0 (CLIP), 20.8 (TCL), and 24.0 (CLIPpy) on ADE20K, confirming that the improvement is not merely due to text-image pre-training. The most direct comparison is against naively upsampled SD masks (same backbone, same mask embeddings, differing only in mask shape), where EmerDiff consistently wins by 3–5 mIoU points across all datasets, isolating the contribution of the modulated denoising pipeline. In open-vocabulary segmentation, MaskCLIP+Ours improves over MaskCLIP by 4.4 mIoU on ADE20K (15.9 vs. 11.5) and 7.8 on PASCAL-Context (33.2 vs. 25.4), while TCL+Ours gains 2.8 on ADE20K (17.4 vs. 14.6). The one exception is CLIPpy+Ours, which shows only marginal improvement (12.9 vs. 12.6 on ADE20K), attributed to CLIPpy's over-smoothed pixel embeddings that limit the benefit of finer mask boundaries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work showing that k-means on diffusion model intermediate feature maps yields semantically aligned clusters (Baranchuk et al., 2022; Karazija et al., 2023; Xu et al., 2023a), but departs from prior approaches that relied on hand-crafted boundary refinement priors or additional training to obtain fine-grained masks. Instead of naively upsampled low-resolution clusters or externally imposed spatial priors, EmerDiff exploits the diffusion model's own generation dynamics—specifically, modulating cross-attention feature maps and observing which pixels change—to discover pixel-level semantic correspondences endogenously. The framework is built on Stable Diffusion's U-Net architecture and DDPM-based inversion for processing real images, with attention injection (Tumanyan et al., 2023) used to preserve structure during the modulated denoising process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides evidence that pre-trained diffusion models encode highly accurate pixel-level semantic knowledge that can be extracted without any additional training, mask annotations, or hand-crafted priors—a finding with implications for understanding what these models actually learn. The modulated denoising approach offers a general principle for extracting fine-grained correspondences from any generative model that operates through iterative refinement. Key limitations include difficulty distinguishing extremely small objects (animal legs, face parts) due to compression in low-dimensional layers, over-segmentation of spatially homogeneous regions (sky, ground), and high computational cost since the modulated denoising must run independently per mask. Open questions include whether the modulation principle transfers to other generative architectures and whether the produced masks can serve as effective pseudo-labels in weakly-supervised pipelines.
