## Problem

Existing meta-learning-based knowledge distillation (MetaKD) methods lack incentives for the teacher to improve itself, distill knowledge for different tasks in isolation without capturing cross-task commonalities, and position the teacher as the sole role model without encouraging the student to surpass it. This paper asks whether a collaborative-competitive framework can simultaneously enhance the teacher's distillation capability and empower the student to outperform the teacher.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241515/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained teacher model (e.g., BERT-base 111M, DeBERTa-v2-xxlarge 1.4B) and a smaller pre-trained student model (e.g., BERT-6L 66M, DeBERTa-12 547M) along with task-specific training data from NLU benchmarks. The meta-teacher is a lightweight feed-forward network (only 0.001% of teacher parameters) that consumes hidden state representations from both teacher and student to produce final outputs. The curriculum model takes a projection vector on R^768 derived from the student's pooling layer as its state and outputs a task selection (action) from the set of available NLU tasks.

## Method

MPDistil operates in four steps: (i) fine-tuning the teacher on task T, (ii) distilling the teacher into the student using a convex combination of task-specific loss, logit-level L2 loss, and hidden-representation MSE loss, (iii) meta-teacher learning where a tiny feed-forward meta-teacher is trained on a quiz set with either a collaborative loss (jointly optimizing teacher and student hidden representations) or a competitive loss (Wasserstein/EM-distance-inspired margin between teacher and student), and (iv) student curriculum learning where a policy network trained via REINFORCE selects a sequence of tasks for the student to self-train on, maximizing a binary or real-valued reward that measures the student's advantage over the meta-teacher. The key architectural choice is replacing the full-model meta-teacher of MetaDistil with a minimal feed-forward discriminator, making the approach computationally feasible for large pre-trained models. Proposition 1 proves that lower collaborative loss than competitive loss guarantees a stronger student, and Proposition 2 shows binary reward always yields higher expected reward than real reward for classification tasks.

## Results

On SuperGLUE dev with BERT-base (teacher) and BERT-6L (student), MPDistil achieves an average Δ Margin of +1.5%, meaning the distilled student outperforms the teacher on average — a first among the 20 baselines evaluated. The distilled 6-layer BERT surpasses the 12-layer BERT teacher on 5 out of 6 SuperGLUE tasks (COPA +7.0%, WiC +2.5%, WSC +1.0%, CB 0.0%, RTE +0.4%), with a maximum advantage of +7.0% on COPA. On the DeBERTa-v2-xxlarge → DeBERTa-12L setting, MPDistil narrows the student-teacher gap to −4.6% average, compared to −7.9% for the next-best method (CKD) and −6.0% for MetaDistil. On GLUE dev, the MPDistil-distilled student outperforms the BERT-base teacher on 4 of 8 tasks (MRPC, QQP, RTE, SST-2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MPDistil is the only method among 20 baselines to achieve a positive average Δ Margin (+1.5%) on SuperGLUE dev with BERT, meaning the 6-layer student outperforms the 12-layer teacher on average. The closest competitor, MetaDistil (Zhou et al., 2021), achieves an average Δ Margin of −1.0% and only beats the teacher on WSC (+0.9%); TinyBERT (Jiao et al., 2019) reaches −0.5% average, winning only on COPA (+4.3%) and WiC (+1.7%). Traditional KD methods (Hinton et al., 2015; DistilBERT; MiniLMv2) show large negative margins (−13 to −15% on WSC), confirming that unidirectional distillation cannot close the teacher-student gap. On the DeBERTa-v2-xxlarge → DeBERTa-12L setting, MPDistil's average Δ Margin of −4.6% substantially outperforms MetaDistil (−6.0%), CKD (−9.8%), and PKD (−11.9%), with the student exceeding the teacher on WiC (+3.3%) and WSC (+2.9%). On GLUE test, MPDistil is the only method achieving positive Δ Margin on four tasks (MRPC +0.3%, QQP +0.3%, RTE +1.3%, SST-2 −0.6% on dev but +1.1% on dev), while MetaDistil achieves positive margins on only RTE (+0.8%) and SST-2 (0.0%). The ablation without curriculum learning drops the average Δ Margin from +1.5% to −0.5%, confirming that the RL-based curriculum is critical for the student to surpass the teacher.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends MetaDistil (Zhou et al., 2021), which first introduced meta-learning-based knowledge distillation where the teacher is fine-tuned with awareness of the student's performance. MPDistil departs from MetaDistil in three key ways: (1) it replaces the computationally expensive full-model meta-teacher with a lightweight feed-forward network (0.001% of teacher parameters), (2) it introduces both collaborative and competitive loss formulations (the latter inspired by Wasserstein/EM distance from WGAN), and (3) it adds a REINFORCE-based curriculum learning step where the student actively selects tasks to outperform the teacher. The curriculum learning component draws on Bengio et al. (2009), while the distillation loss follows the standard Hinton et al. (2015) paradigm with L2 logit matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a 6-layer BERT student can outperform a 12-layer BERT teacher on 5/6 SuperGLUE dev tasks challenges the assumption that KD is inherently a one-directional knowledge transfer where the student can never surpass the teacher. The lightweight meta-teacher design makes the approach practically viable for distilling from >1B parameter models (demonstrated with DeBERTa-v2-xxlarge), which prior MetaKD methods could not handle due to the cost of cloning and fine-tuning the full teacher. Open questions include whether the competitive curriculum framework generalizes to generation tasks, whether the student's advantage on dev sets holds robustly on test sets (it does on only 3/6 SuperGLUE test tasks), and how the approach scales to even larger teacher-student gaps or architecturally heterogeneous model pairs.
