## Problem

Prior linear stability analyses of SGD (e.g., Wu et al., 2022) were restricted to mean-squared error loss and relied on weaker stability notions, leaving open how the geometric arrangement of per-sample Hessians affects SGD's implicit regularization toward flat minima. This paper asks: what are the precise necessary and sufficient conditions under which linearized SGD diverges from a stationary point, expressed in terms of SGD hyperparameters and a geometric property of the loss Hessian that applies to any additively decomposable loss function?

## Input / Output

The analysis takes as input the set of per-sample Hessian matrices {H_i} at a stationary point w*, the learning rate η, batch size B, and number of samples n, and produces a sufficient divergence condition of the form λ_1(H) > σ/η · (n/B − 1)^{−1/2}, where σ is a newly defined Hessian coherence measure. The coherence measure σ = λ_1(S) / max_i λ_1(H_i) is computed from the entry-wise square root of the Gram matrix of {H_i} under the trace inner product, reducing to a measure of how close S is to being diagonally dominant in the rank-one Hessian case. The condition is shown to be nearly optimal: Theorem 2 constructs explicit Hessian sets where the dynamics remain bounded whenever λ_1(H) falls below a constant-factor relaxation of the Theorem 1 threshold.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a Loewner-order inductive bound on the k-step product of random SGD Jacobians, yielding both a divergence condition (Theorem 1) and a convergence condition (Lemma 4.1) that together provide a near-complete characterization. The key insight is a multiplicative decoupling: the effect of batch size and learning rate on instability factors as (η²/nB − η²/n²)^k, while the Hessian geometry enters through the sum of Frobenius norms of products ‖H_{y_k}…H_{y_1}‖_F², allowing the two effects to be analyzed separately. The Hessian coherence measure σ is introduced to bound the geometric term from below using the trace of H^k, connecting it to the spectral properties of the Gram matrix of per-sample Hessians. Unlike prior work that relied on norm inequalities, the proof tracks the alignment of unstable eigenvectors across random Jacobian draws via the Loewner partial order, which is essential for capturing how Hessian alignment amplifies or suppresses stochastic instability. The result applies to any additively decomposable loss (including cross-entropy), and experiments on synthetic quadratic problems confirm the predicted divergence boundaries as functions of σ, B, and η.

## Results

The paper's central result (Theorem 1) establishes that linearized SGD diverges when λ₁(H) > 2/η or λ₁(H) > (σ/η)·(n/B − 1)^(−1/2), where σ is a novel Hessian coherence measure. Theorem 2 proves this condition is near-optimal: for σ, n/B = O(1), no sufficient condition relying only on η, σ, B, λ₁(H), and n can improve the threshold by more than a constant factor. Empirically, on synthetic quadratic problems (n=100), all (σ, B) tuples below Theorem 1's boundary diverge and all above Theorem 2's boundary converge, with the gap between the two bounds narrowing as B increases. The experiments confirm a 'squared-scaling' law: increasing batch size proportionally to η² does not improve stability, and the coherence measure σ causes instability even at small η when B is small relative to n.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary theoretical comparison is to Theorem 3.3 of Wu et al. (2022), which guarantees instability when ‖H‖_F > (1/η)√(B/μ₀) for MSE loss. This paper's condition is strictly more general (applies to any additively decomposable loss, including cross-entropy, answering an open question raised by Agarwala & Dauphin 2023), guarantees a stronger form of divergence (E‖w_k‖₂ → ∞ rather than merely bounded expected loss), and is easier to satisfy in practice because it uses λ₁(H) instead of ‖H‖_F — a weaker requirement when the Hessian has low stable rank, as is typical in overparameterized networks. Specifically, ignoring alignment factors, Wu et al. require ‖H‖_F > (1/η)√B while this paper requires only λ₁(H) > (1/η)√(B/n), a gap of √n in the stable-rank regime. Additionally, Wu et al.'s loss-scaled alignment factor μ₀ is undefined at the optimum for quadratic losses where L(w*)=0, rendering their condition inapplicable to the synthetic validation problems used here.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the linear stability analysis of SGD initiated by Wu et al. (2018, 2022) and Ma & Ying (2021), which characterized how SGD's stochastic dynamics select flat minima in overparameterized networks. The key departure is the introduction of a Hessian coherence measure σ that captures the geometric alignment among individual sample Hessians, enabling the analysis to generalize from MSE loss to any additively decomposable loss function (including cross-entropy), while simultaneously providing a stronger divergence guarantee and a condition that is easier to satisfy in practice. The squared-scaling relationship between batch size and learning rate (B ∝ η²) and the multiplicative decoupling between hyperparameter effects and Hessian geometry are new structural insights not present in prior formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a near-optimal, interpretable sufficient condition for SGD instability that depends only on λ₁(H), η, B, n, and the new coherence measure σ, making it a practical diagnostic tool for understanding when SGD will diverge from a given optimum. However, the gap between the sufficient divergence condition (Theorem 1) and the near-optimality bound (Theorem 2) remains open, and σ has not yet been measured or approximated in real neural networks, limiting immediate practical utility. The authors identify extending the framework to sharpness-aware minimization (SAM), momentum, and adaptive methods as natural next steps, as well as deriving fine-grained local convergence rates from the Lemma 4.1 convergence analysis.
