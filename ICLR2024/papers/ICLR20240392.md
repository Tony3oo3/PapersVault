## Problem

Existing multi-scale representation learning for semantic segmentation—whether via receptive-field-variable kernels (ASPP, PSP) or hierarchical backbone multi-level outputs (Swin, ConvNeXt, SegFormer)—suffers from two systematic defects identified through effective receptive field (ERF) visualization: scale inadequacy (missing global, local, or intermediate scales) and field inactivation (dead sub-areas within the nominal receptive field). No prior relational multi-scale learner (e.g., ISANet, ANN) resolves both issues simultaneously while preserving the efficiency of local window attention.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240392/cropped_page1_idx8.webp)

## Input / Output

VWFormer consumes multi-level feature maps (specifically the last three stages, e.g., F₈, F₁₆, F₃₂) from a hierarchical backbone such as Swin Transformer, ConvNeXt, or MiT, and produces a single dense semantic segmentation mask at the resolution of the second-stage feature map. Input images are standard RGB at 512×512 (ADE20K), 640×640 or 768×769 (Cityscapes), or 512×512 (COCOStuff-164K). The decoder operates on the aggregated feature at 1/8 spatial resolution, with local window size P set to H/8 × W/8 of that feature map.

## Method

Varying Window Attention (VWA) disentangles local window attention into a fixed P×P query window and an enlarged RP×P context window (varying ratio R), letting each query pixel attend to a receptive field R times larger than the local window. To eliminate the R²-fold cost blowup, the paper introduces a pre-scaling principle: before unfolding the large context, DOPE (densely overlapping patch embedding, a Conv2d with kernel R, stride 1, reducing channels from C to C/R²) shrinks the feature dimensionality, and a subsequent PE (Conv2d with kernel R, stride R) downsamples the spatial context back to P×P while restoring channels to C—adding only one extra linear-mapping's worth of FLOPs. Copy-Shift Padding (CSP) replaces zero-padding to prevent attention collapse at image borders. The full VWFormer decoder runs three parallel VWA branches (R=2, 4, 8) plus a 1×1 short path for the very-local scale, concatenates their outputs, applies a two-layer MLP with low-level enhancement (fusing a downsampled F₄ feature), and outputs a 256-channel segmentation feature.

## Results

On ADE20K, VWFormer with Swin-B backbone achieves 52.5/53.5 mIoU (single/multi-scale), outperforming UperNet (50.8/52.4) by 1.7% while using only 120G FLOPs versus UperNet's 306G (a 61% reduction). With ConvNeXt-B, VWFormer reaches 53.3/54.1 mIoU at 107G FLOPs, surpassing UperNet's 52.1/52.7 at 293G FLOPs. On Cityscapes, VWFormer with Swin-B achieves 82.7/83.3 mIoU, and when paired with Mask2Former, VWFormer improves over Deformable Attention by 0.7%–1.4% mIoU at lower FLOPs. Across all MiT backbone variants (B0–B5), VWFormer consistently outperforms SegFormer's MLP-decoder by 0.5%–1.5% mIoU on ADE20K while reducing FLOPs by up to 45%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VWFormer consistently outperforms UperNet across all backbone variants (Swin-B/L, ConvNeXt-B/L/XL) on both ADE20K and Cityscapes, achieving 1.0%–2.1% higher mIoU while reducing FLOPs by 55%–63% (e.g., 120G vs. 306G with Swin-B). Against SegFormer's MLP-decoder, VWFormer improves mIoU by 0.5%–1.5% across all MiT variants (B0–B5) on ADE20K while also reducing FLOPs (e.g., 5.8G vs. 8.4G for MiT-B0). In the MaskFormer setting, VWFormer surpasses FPN by 0.8%–1.7% mIoU at comparable or lower FLOPs, and in Mask2Former it outperforms Deformable Attention by 0.7%–1.4% mIoU with fewer FLOPs (e.g., 129G vs. 142G for Swin-B). Against classical multi-scale learners (PSPNet, DeepLabV3+, DenseASPP, APCNet, DMNet, ANN, ISANet) on ResNet50/101, VWFormer achieves the best mIoU on both ADE20K and Cityscapes with the lowest FLOPs (203.2G) and fewest parameters (50.4M). VWFormer also improves upon SOTA methods when swapped in as the decoder: HRViT gains 1.1%–1.4% mIoU, SegViT-V2 gains 0.6%, and ViT-Adapter gains 1.0%–0.8% mIoU on ADE20K.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends local window attention (LWA) from Swin Transformer by disentangling the query and context windows, allowing the context to be enlarged by a ratio R while the query remains local, thereby creating a variable receptive field at near-LWA cost. It departs from two established paradigms for multi-scale learning: (1) variable-kernel filters (ASPP, PSP) and (2) hierarchical backbone multi-level feature maps fused by MSDs (FPN, UperNet, MLP-decoder), both of which the authors diagnose via ERF visualization as suffering from scale inadequacy and field inactivation. The DOPE (densely overlapping patch embedding) and CSP (copy-shift padding) mechanisms are novel engineering contributions that eliminate the R² cost overhead of context enlargement, positioning VWA as a drop-in replacement for LWA within existing hierarchical transformer architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ERF-based diagnosis of systematic scale gaps in widely-used backbones (Swin, ConvNeXt, SegFormer) and decoders (ASPP, PSP) provides a useful diagnostic framework for the community. VWFormer's practical value lies in being a lightweight, drop-in MSD that consistently outperforms FPN, UperNet, and MLP-decoder while matching their computational budget, making it immediately applicable to existing segmentation pipelines. Open questions include whether the VWA mechanism generalizes beyond window-based attention to other attention variants, whether the 25% overhead over pure LWA becomes problematic at very large resolutions, and whether the fixed R values (2, 4, 8) are optimal across all input sizes or could be made adaptive.
