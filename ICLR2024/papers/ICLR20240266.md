## Problem

Current diffusion models parameterize the backward denoising step as a single Gaussian, which cannot capture the multimodal structure of the true posterior q(x_{t-1}|x_t) when the data distribution is multimodal (e.g., Gaussian mixtures). The paper proves this expressive bottleneck yields unbounded local and global denoising errors, demonstrating that the bounded score estimation error assumption underlying existing theoretical guarantees is too strong.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240266/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard Gaussian noise vector x_T and iteratively denoises it through T backward steps to produce a synthetic RGB image at resolutions from 32×32 (CIFAR-10) to 256×256 (CelebA-HQ, LSUN-Church). A key practical detail is that SMD is most beneficial at low iteration counts: it achieves competitive FID with only 100 backward steps where vanilla LDM would need roughly 8× more. At each denoising step, the U-Net backbone is additionally conditioned on a sampled latent variable z_t generated from a separate noise vector η, so the network's effective parameters change per sample.

## Method

SMD replaces the single-Gaussian backward probability with a continuously relaxed Gaussian mixture by introducing a latent variable z_t that modulates the denoising network's parameters through a hypernetwork f_φ(z_t, t), so the backward probability becomes an integral over z_t of Gaussians with z_t-dependent means and covariances. A neural network g_ξ maps standard Gaussian noise η to z_t, making the mixture structure learnable without specifying a fixed number of components K. The paper proves SMD achieves zero denoising error for any Gaussian mixture data distribution (Theorem 4.1), resolving the unbounded-error results for vanilla diffusion models. For optimization, the authors derive a tractable upper bound on the negative log-likelihood that reduces to a modified noise-prediction loss with an additional expectation over η, approximated by Monte Carlo sampling at training time. This adds only minor computational overhead over vanilla DDPM while enabling the model to represent arbitrarily complex multimodal posteriors.

## Results

SMD consistently reduces FID scores across all tested diffusion model architectures and datasets. On CIFAR-10 (32×32), DDPM w/SMD achieves FID 3.13 vs. 3.78 for vanilla DDPM, and ADM w/SMD achieves 2.55 vs. 2.98 for vanilla ADM. On high-resolution CelebA-HQ (256×256), LDM w/SMD reaches FID 5.48 vs. 6.13 for vanilla LDM. The gains are most pronounced at fewer backward iterations: SMD achieves the same FID as vanilla LDM with only 100 steps, whereas vanilla LDM requires ~800 steps (8× more) to match.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMD outperforms all four baselines across every dataset and resolution tested. Against DDPM, SMD reduces FID by 15–17% (e.g., 3.78→3.13 on CIFAR-10, 3.65→3.17 on LSUN-Church). Against ADM, the improvement is similarly consistent at 15–17% (e.g., 2.98→2.55 on CIFAR-10, 3.41→2.98 on LSUN-Church). Against LDM on high-resolution 256×256 images, SMD reduces FID from 6.13 to 5.48 on CelebA-HQ and from 5.86 to 5.21 on LSUN-Church (~11% improvement). Against DDIM (a fast deterministic sampler), the paper shows in Fig. 3 that SMD's advantage is consistent across samplers, with the largest gains at low iteration counts (e.g., T=200), confirming that SMD addresses a fundamental expressiveness limitation rather than merely a sampling-efficiency issue.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020), inheriting its forward diffusion process, reparameterized noise-prediction loss, and U-Net architecture, while departing from the core assumption that a single Gaussian suffices for the backward denoising step. It explicitly challenges the growing body of theoretical guarantees (Lee et al., 2022a,b; Chen et al., 2023) that prove diffusion models are universal approximators under bounded score estimation error, showing that assumption is too strong. The proposed SMD method draws on hypernetworks (Ha et al., 2017) and normalizing flows (Rezende & Mohamed, 2015) to introduce a continuous latent variable that relaxes a discrete Gaussian mixture into a tractable, optimizable backward distribution, positioning itself as complementary to fast samplers like DDIM and DPM-Solver rather than a replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical result that standard diffusion models have unbounded denoising errors for common multimodal distributions (e.g., Gaussian mixtures) is a meaningful correction to the field's theoretical narrative, undermining guarantees that had been accumulating confidence in diffusion models' expressiveness. Practically, SMD's ability to reduce backward iterations by up to 8× while maintaining or improving FID has direct cost implications for large-scale generation, and the method is architecture-agnostic (applied to DDPM, ADM, LDM). Open questions include whether the theoretical zero-error guarantee holds with finite-capacity networks, whether the hypernetwork overhead becomes prohibitive at very large scales, and whether the approach extends cleanly to conditional generation (text-to-image) and video.
