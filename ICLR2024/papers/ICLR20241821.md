## Problem

Neural surface reconstruction is highly sensitive to camera pose noise, even when initialized with state-of-the-art estimators like COLMAP or ARKit. Existing joint pose-NeRF optimization methods (e.g., BARF, SPARF) fail to meaningfully improve pose accuracy in real-world scenes because they optimize per-frame pose parameters independently (ignoring global sequence information) and rely on ambiguous color rendering loss that creates many false local minima.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241821/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images, initial 6D camera poses (3D axis-angle rotation + 3D translation) from COLMAP or ARKit, pre-computed 2D feature correspondences (SIFT from COLMAP by default, or LoFTR for dense matches), and known camera intrinsics. The PoRF MLP consumes a 7D input vector (1D normalized frame index + 6D initial pose) per frame and outputs a 6D pose residual. The final output is a set of refined camera poses used to train Voxurf for SDF-based surface reconstruction and mesh extraction.

## Method

The core contribution is the Pose Residual Field (PoRF), a 2-layer MLP (256 nodes, ELU activation) that regresses a 6D pose residual from the frame index and initial pose, with parameters shared across all frames to capture global trajectory information. A fixed small scaling factor α multiplies the MLP output so the refined pose starts near the initial pose and residuals are learned gradually. For supervision, the paper introduces an epipolar geometry loss that computes Sampson distance between pre-computed 2D correspondences using the fundamental matrix derived from the learned poses, weighted by the squared inlier rate of each image pair. This loss depends only on poses and correspondences (not on rendered depths), avoiding the inaccuracy of NeRF-rendered geometry and allowing an arbitrary number of matches per iteration. The total loss combines the standard NeuS color + Eikonal regularization loss (backpropagating to both PoRF and the SDF/rendering network) with the epipolar geometry loss (backpropagating to PoRF only), enabling joint optimization of pose refinement and surface reconstruction in a single 50k-iteration training run.

## Results

On the DTU dataset, PoRF reduces the COLMAP rotation error by 78% (from 0.65° to 0.14°) and decreases the reconstruction Chamfer distance from 3.48 mm (COLMAP pose) to 0.85 mm, approaching the 0.72 mm achieved with ground-truth poses. On the MobileBrick dataset, the method refines ARKit poses to achieve a reconstruction F1 score of 75.67 (σ=2.5 mm), surpassing the dataset's provided ground-truth pose (75.13) and all baselines. The method also consistently improves novel view rendering in the Nerfstudio library, raising mean PSNR from 20.32 to 21.54 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DTU, PoRF dramatically outperforms all pose-refinement baselines: BARF (3.53 mm) and SPARF (2.89 mm) both fail to meaningfully reduce the COLMAP Chamfer distance of 3.48 mm, while L2G diverges on several scenes (mean rotation error 5.29°). PoRF achieves 0.85 mm, within 0.13 mm of the GT-pose Voxurf result (0.72 mm). On MobileBrick, PoRF improves the ARKit F1 score from 69.18 to 75.67, beating both BARF (73.96) and SPARF (72.99), and slightly surpassing the dataset's own ground-truth pose (75.13). In the Nerfstudio generalisation experiment, replacing Nerfacto's pose optimisation with the PoRF MLP yields consistent PSNR gains across all 10 scenes (mean 21.54 vs. 20.32 dB) without using the proposed epipolar geometry loss, confirming the transferability of the pose residual field representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the joint pose-and-NeRF optimization line (NeRF--, BARF, SPARF, L2G) by replacing per-frame independent pose parameters with a shared MLP that regresses pose residuals — a direct analogy to how NeRF's shared MLP encodes all 3D points. It builds on NeuS for the SDF-based surface reconstruction backbone and adopts Mip-NeRF 360's coordinate contraction for unbounded scenes. The key departure from SPARF is the use of a pure epipolar geometry (Sampson distance) loss on pre-computed correspondences, avoiding SPARF's reliance on NeRF-rendered depths that can be inaccurate and limit pose accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 78% rotation error reduction on DTU and the MobileBrick result of surpassing the (admittedly imperfect) GT pose in reconstruction F1 demonstrate that pose refinement via a shared MLP is a practical, low-overhead strategy for real-world pipelines using COLMAP or ARKit. The finding that sparse SIFT matches (488/pair) perform comparably to dense LoFTR matches (5394/pair) makes the method deployable without expensive deep matching. Open questions include whether the approach can be accelerated (currently 2.5 hours on an A40) via hash-grid or Instant-NGP representations, and whether it extends to sparse-view or severely noisy initialization regimes where it currently requires near-dense coverage.
