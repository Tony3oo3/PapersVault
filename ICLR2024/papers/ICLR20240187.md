## Problem

State-of-the-art model-based reinforcement learning methods rely on flat, non-hierarchical world models that are restricted to predictions on a single, predefined time scale, limiting their ability to perform long-horizon planning. The central challenge is learning higher-level temporal abstractions that are neither tied to particular tasks nor to fixed nested time scales, so that an agent can flexibly reason across multiple time scales.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pixel-based observations (ego-centric ±2 grid-cell views in MiniHack, robot end-effector camera views in MultiWorld, pad-activation sequences in VisualPinPad) together with discrete or continuous actions. It produces a two-level hierarchical prediction: the low level predicts the next stochastic latent state, next-frame image, reward, and discount; the high level predicts the stochastic state and action immediately preceding the next context change, the elapsed time Δτ, the accumulated discounted reward over that interval, and a categorical high-level action A_t that disambiguates which context transition is anticipated. These outputs feed directly into MBRL (value estimates and policy actions) or MPC (subgoal-conditioned action sequences via CEM and MCTS).

## Method

THICK builds a two-level hierarchy on top of a modified RSSM called C-RSSM, which splits the latent state into a stochastic component z_t, a continuously updated GRU hidden state h_t, and a low-dimensional context c_t that changes only sparsely in time via a GateL0RD cell whose update gate is L0-regularized. A coarse prediction pathway (independent of h_t) forces the context to encode prediction-relevant information and enables the high level to make predictions without the full deterministic state. The high-level network W_θ is trained on input-target pairs where inputs are (c_t, z_t) and targets are the states, actions, elapsed time, and accumulated reward at the next context-change time τ(t), with self-organizing categorical high-level actions A_t (learned via a posterior-prior KL objective) disambiguating different anticipated outcomes. During inference, the high level predicts a pre-transition scenario, which is fed into the coarse pathway of C-RSSM to compute the new context and a coarse prior, enabling iterative temporal-abstract roll-outs. Downstream, THICK Dreamer fuses short-horizon λ-returns with long-horizon value estimates from temporal-abstract predictions to accelerate credit assignment, while THICK PlaNet uses MCTS over high-level actions to set subgoals that guide low-level CEM planning.

## Results

THICK Dreamer matches or outperforms DreamerV2 across all MiniHack tasks (KeyRoom, WaterCrossing-Ring, WandOfDeath-Advanced, EscapeRoom), with the largest gains in multi-subgoal tasks like EscapeRoom (five sequential subgoals). In VisualPinPad, THICK Dreamer is slightly more sample-efficient on the harder PinPadFour and PinPadFive tasks while matching DreamerV2 on PinPadThree. In zero-shot MPC on Multiworld, THICK PlaNet achieves higher returns than PlaNet on sparse-reward (Pusher-Sparse) and long-horizon (Door, PickUp) tasks, while showing no notable difference on the short-horizon dense-reward Pusher-Dense task. Systematic variation of corridor length in KeyCorridor confirms that THICK Dreamer's advantage grows with task horizon until both methods fail to discover rewards.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DreamerV2 (Hafner et al., 2020), THICK Dreamer achieves comparable or slightly better sample efficiency and final success rates across all MiniHack and VisualPinPad tasks, with the gap widening as task horizon increases (e.g., EscapeRoom with 5 subgoals vs. KeyRoom with 2). Against Director (Hafner et al., 2022), a hierarchical RL method based on Dreamer, THICK Dreamer is more robust: Director outperforms both methods on KeyRoom but fails to learn the other MiniHack tasks (WaterCrossing-Ring, WandOfDeath, EscapeRoom) and is less sample-efficient on VisualPinPad. Against PlaNet (Hafner et al., 2019b) in zero-shot MPC on Multiworld, THICK PlaNet clearly outperforms on Pusher-Sparse, Door, and PickUp (long-horizon or sparse-reward settings) but shows no significant difference on Pusher-Dense, indicating the hierarchical advantage is specifically tied to task horizon and reward sparsity rather than being a universal improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

THICK extends the Dreamer/PlaNet RSSM framework by introducing a sparsely updating context code (via GateL0RD) that segments sequences into invariant periods, then trains a high-level model to predict the states immediately preceding context transitions. This departs from prior hierarchical world models that rely on fixed time scales (Clockwork VAEs, Director) or prediction-error-based segmentation, instead deriving the temporal abstraction entirely from learned sparsity in latent dynamics. The high-level planning component borrows MCTS from MuZero and CEM from PlaNet, but applies them at the adaptive context-change timescale rather than at fixed intervals. Conceptually, the work connects to Schmidhuber's 1992 hierarchical RNNs (where higher levels activate on lower-level prediction failure) but replaces error-triggered activation with a learned, L0-regularized sparsity objective over a continuous latent context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a fully self-supervised, task-agnostic hierarchical world model can improve long-horizon MBRL and zero-shot MPC without requiring manually specified subgoals or fixed temporal abstractions, addressing a key limitation of flat world models. The KeyCorridor experiment cleanly isolates task horizon as the driver of improvement, and the interpretable context changes (≈90–97% at task-relevant events) provide mechanistic transparency. However, the approach still depends on the base Dreamer discovering any solution (if Dreamer fails, THICK cannot decompose the task), requires per-suite tuning of the sparsity hyperparameter β^sparse, and is limited to a two-level hierarchy. Open questions include whether recursive multi-level THICK hierarchies scale, whether the coarse pathway can be further exploited for direct context-to-context prediction, and how the method behaves in highly dynamic or partially observable environments where context changes may be less discrete.
