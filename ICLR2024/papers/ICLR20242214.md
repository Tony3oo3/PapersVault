## Problem

Evaluating the quality of representations learned by Joint Embedding (JE) self-supervised architectures without downstream task access remains an open problem. Existing covariance-rank-based metrics like RankMe are susceptible to spurious inflation from regularization terms in SSL objectives, which can produce full-rank covariance matrices from uninformative features rather than genuine semantic structure, leading to poor correlation with actual downstream performance.

## Input / Output

LiDAR consumes embeddings from a pretrained SSL model (evaluated on either the student or teacher branch) along with n clean input samples treated as surrogate classes, each with q augmented views generated by the method's own data augmentation or masking pipeline. The output is a single scalar effective rank score computed from the eigenvalues of the LDA matrix, which can be used to rank checkpoints, select hyperparameters, or compare architectures. In practice, n=1k and q=50 suffice for transformer-based methods (I-JEPA, data2vec, DINO), while ResNet-based methods (SimCLR, VICReg) require larger n (5k–10k) due to higher-dimensional features.

## Method

LiDAR replaces the raw feature covariance matrix used by RankMe with the Linear Discriminant Analysis matrix Σ_lidar = Σ_w^{-1/2} Σ_b Σ_w^{-1/2}, where Σ_b captures between-class variance (spread of per-class embedding means) and Σ_w captures within-class variance (spread of augmented views around their class mean, regularized by δI). The effective rank is then computed as the smooth entropy-based measure exp(−Σ p_i log p_i) over the normalized eigenvalues of Σ_lidar. The key insight is that the Σ_w^{-1/2} whitening operation removes directions of variability that are irrelevant to discriminating between surrogate classes—precisely the directions inflated by implicit or explicit regularization in JE objectives—so the resulting rank reflects only information content pertinent to the SSL task. A theoretical proposition shows that appending independent noise dimensions to the embedding cannot increase the LiDAR score, a property that covariance-rank metrics lack.

## Results

LiDAR consistently outperforms RankMe across all five SSL methods tested (I-JEPA, data2vec, SimCLR, DINO, VICReg). For I-JEPA (ViT-Base, grid search), LiDAR on the student branch achieves a Kendall's τ of 0.8159 overall versus 0.5830 for vanilla RankMe and 0.7513 for augmented RankMe. The gap is most dramatic for VICReg (ResNet-50), where LiDAR reaches a Spearman rank correlation of 0.9161 with linear probe accuracy compared to only 0.3174 for RankMe. In hyperparameter selection, LiDAR recovers 67.87% linear probe accuracy for I-JEPA (matching augmented RankMe) versus 56.76% for vanilla RankMe, and 58.93% for SimCLR versus 56.46% for RankMe.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is RankMe (Garrido et al., 2022), the prior state-of-the-art label-free representation quality metric based on the effective rank of the feature covariance matrix. LiDAR wins decisively on every SSL method and every hyperparameter tested: for I-JEPA, LiDAR (student) achieves Kendall's τ of 0.8159 vs. 0.5830 (RankMe) and 0.7513 (augmented RankMe); for data2vec, 0.5531 vs. 0.2238; for SimCLR, 0.8167 vs. 0.4982; for VICReg, Spearman 0.9161 vs. 0.3174; and for DINO, Kendall's τ 0.7157 vs. 0.6288. The advantage is largest for VICReg and I-JEPA, where regularization and masking hyperparameters can inflate raw covariance rank without improving downstream performance — precisely the failure mode LiDAR's LDA-based whitening addresses. In hyperparameter selection (top-1 model recovery), LiDAR matches or exceeds augmented RankMe on I-JEPA (67.87% vs. 67.87%) and SimCLR (58.93% vs. 57.83%), while vanilla RankMe lags by 11+ points. The one notable exception is I-JEPA's target mask scale, where LiDAR (teacher) drops to 0.2927, below even vanilla RankMe (0.2867), suggesting the teacher branch is less reliable for this particular hyperparameter.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on RankMe (Garrido et al., 2022), which demonstrated that the effective rank of the feature covariance matrix correlates with downstream linear probing performance in JE-SSL architectures. The key departure is the observation that SSL regularization terms (variance, covariance penalties) can arbitrarily inflate the covariance spectrum without improving semantic content, rendering raw covariance rank an unreliable signal. LiDAR repurposes classical Linear Discriminant Analysis (Bishop, 2007) to the multi-view SSL setting, replacing the raw covariance with a between-class/within-class scatter ratio that inherently filters out directions of variability driven by regularization rather than the SSL objective. The smooth effective rank formulation is inherited from Roy & Vetterli (2007), the same measure RankMe employs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LiDAR provides a practical, label-free criterion for hyperparameter selection and early stopping in JE-SSL training, reducing the need for expensive multi-task downstream evaluation. The consistent improvement over RankMe across contrastive (SimCLR), regularized (VICReg), self-distillation (DINO), and masking-based (I-JEPA, data2vec) architectures suggests broad applicability. However, limitations remain: the method exhibits negative correlation in some high-dimensional embedding settings (VICReg), incurs computational overhead from matrix inversion that may preclude use as a per-iteration loss signal, and its effectiveness is contingent on the positive-pair sampling strategy specific to each SSL method, complicating cross-method comparisons. Open questions include whether LiDAR extends to nonlinear probing protocols and whether a differentiable formulation could integrate it into the training loop.
