## Problem

Classic optimal transport enforces strict mass conservation, making neural Monge map estimators vulnerable to outliers and class imbalances between source and target distributions. While unbalanced OT (UOT) addresses this in the discrete setting, its integration into neural Monge map estimators has been limited to specific architectures (e.g., ICNN-OT) and adversarial training schemes, with no general, theoretically grounded framework applicable to arbitrary estimators.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240018/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes paired source and target batches (e.g., 256×256 CelebA images compressed to 32×32×4 latent codes via a pretrained Stable Diffusion VAE, 28×28 EMNIST digits/letters, or single-cell RNA-seq embeddings) along with an unbalancedness parameter τ ∈ (0,1] that controls the degree of mass deviation. Training operates on rescaled empirical measures derived from the UOT coupling, while evaluation is performed on the original balanced distributions. The output is a learned transport map (Monge map or continuous normalizing flow) that maps source samples to target samples, producing translated images, predicted cell trajectories, or predicted perturbation responses.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution (Proposition 3.1) shows that the UOT coupling π_UOT between μ and ν is itself a balanced OT coupling between its own marginals μ̃ = p₁#π_UOT and ν̃ = p₂#π_UOT, which are re-weighted versions of the original measures with equal total mass. Practically, this means one can compute a discrete UOT coupling batch-wise via entropic regularization (generalized Sinkhorn), sample from the rescaled marginals, and then train any existing balanced Monge map estimator (OT-ICNN, Monge Gap, or OT-FM) on those rescaled samples without architectural modification. For OT-FM specifically, the UOT coupling directly replaces the balanced OT coupling in the flow matching loss, so UOT-FM incurs zero additional computational overhead over OT-FM. The approach is validated across three tasks—single-cell trajectory inference (UOT-ICNN), perturbation response prediction (UOT-MG), and unpaired image translation (UOT-FM)—demonstrating consistent improvements in biological plausibility, predictive accuracy, and FID/transport cost.

## Results

On CelebA 256×256, UOT-FM achieves FID scores of 13.9 (Male→Female), 14.3 (Female→Male), 18.5 (Remove Glasses), and 18.3 (Add Glasses), outperforming CycleGAN (15.2/22.2/24.2/19.8) and OT-FM (14.6/14.8/20.9/20.1) while approaching the SOTA UVCGAN (9.6/13.9/14.4/13.6). On EMNIST digits→letters, UOT-FM reduces average FID from 36.9 (OT-FM) to 23.0, with the 8→B mapping improving from 83.1 to 42.6. For single-cell trajectory inference, UOT-ICNN improves correct Ngn3 EP cell transitions from 7% (OT-ICNN) to 69%, and on CIFAR-10 generation, UOT-FM (τ=0.97) achieves FID 3.42 vs. OT-FM's 3.59.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UOT-FM outperforms CycleGAN on all four CelebA 256×256 tasks (e.g., 13.9 vs. 15.2 FID on Male→Female) and OT-FM on all tasks with both FID and transport cost (e.g., 13.9 vs. 14.6 FID and 56.8 vs. 59.3 T-Cost on Male→Female), while remaining competitive with the SOTA UVCGAN (9.6 FID). On EMNIST, UOT-FM dramatically reduces average FID from 36.9 (OT-FM) to 23.0, with the most striking gain on the 8→B mapping (42.6 vs. 83.1), demonstrating that unbalancedness resolves class-imbalance-induced misassignments. In single-cell trajectory inference, UOT-ICNN surpasses OT-ICNN, TrajectoryNet, scVelo, and WOT on the EB and NEB branches (57% and 85% correct transitions) and achieves 69% on Ngn3 EP cells (vs. 7% for OT-ICNN and 50% for WOT). For perturbation response prediction, UOT-MG improves over OT-MG on nearly all 35 drugs under both Sinkhorn divergence and L2 drug signature metrics. On CIFAR-10 generation, UOT-FM (τ=0.97) marginally improves FID from 3.59 to 3.42 over OT-FM while reducing transport cost from 104.53 to 102.98. In the sciPlex benchmark against competing unbalancedness methods, UOT-ICNN (25.95 Sinkhorn divergence at τ=0.95) outperforms both Lubeck et al. (26.39) and Yang & Uhler (37.40), with Lubeck et al. diverging severely at lower τ values.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of unbalanced optimal transport (Chizat et al., 2018) and neural Monge map estimation (OT-ICNN, Monge Gap, OT-FM). Prior work on unbalanced neural Monge maps (Lubeck et al., 2022; Yang & Uhler, 2019) was restricted to specific estimators and relied on adversarial training or explicit inverse map parameterization. The key departure here is a universal re-scaling scheme grounded in Proposition 3.1, which shows that the UOT coupling's marginals define rescaled measures between which a standard balanced Monge map can be computed — making the approach plug-and-play for any existing estimator without architectural modifications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that distribution shifts (class imbalance, outliers, batch-level discrepancies) are ubiquitous in real-world domain translation, and this paper provides a single, theoretically justified mechanism to handle them across heterogeneous tasks — from single-cell biology to image translation. The main limitation is the non-trivial choice of the unbalancedness parameter τ, which requires a grid search and whose optimal value is application-dependent. An open direction noted by the authors is extending beyond squared Euclidean cost to geometry-aware costs tailored to the data manifold, which could further improve performance in structured domains.
