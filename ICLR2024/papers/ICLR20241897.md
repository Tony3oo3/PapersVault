## Problem

Existing learning-based rigid protein-protein docking methods either reduce the task to local point cloud registration (which is ill-posed when registered points degenerate to lower-dimensional manifolds) or rely on slow generative/diffusion processes. The paper addresses the need for a fast, equivariant docking method that avoids steric clashes and generalizes to arbitrary input poses without requiring inter-protein coordinate-based edges (which are unavailable for unbound structures).

## Input / Output

The method takes two unbound protein structures (ligand and receptor) represented as residue-level graphs with 3D Cα coordinates and k-NN edges, and produces a single SE(3) transformation (rotation matrix Q ∈ SO(3) and translation vector t ∈ ℝ³) that maps the ligand onto the fixed receptor. Internally, the model also predicts a pair of elliptic paraboloid interfaces (each parameterized by a 3×3 matrix A, a 3-vector b, and a scalar c) that are constrained to share the same standard-form shape, encoding the binding geometry.

## Method

ElliDock uses a pairwise-independent SE(3)-equivariant GNN called EPIT, built on a PAINN backbone augmented with a Graph Transformer Layer for intra-protein message passing, and a novel inter-protein interaction module that passes only invariant features through dense cross-protein edges (since unbound proteins lack meaningful coordinate-based inter-protein edges). The key insight is representing the docking interface as an elliptic paraboloid rather than a point cloud: the model predicts a shared standard-form paraboloid (via invariant graph-level outputs) and per-protein SE(3) transformations from standard to general form (via equivariant outputs), then computes the docking transformation by making the two paraboloids coincide. A rotation refinement step resolves residual x-y ambiguity in the standard frame. Training combines four losses: a fitness loss pulling interface pockets onto the predicted paraboloid and its tangent plane, an overlap loss enforcing spatial separation of ligand and receptor on opposite sides of the interface to prevent steric clashes, a refinement loss aligning x-y pocket angles via Kabsch, and a direct dock loss on the predicted transformation.

## Results

On the RAbD antibody-antigen test set, ElliDock achieves a median CRMSD of 11.541 Å and IRMSD of 11.319 Å, outperforming EquiDock (14.301/12.700) and DiffDock-PP (11.764/12.207) while running ~90× faster than DiffDock-PP (91.2 s vs. 8308.7 s). On the DB5.5 benchmark, ElliDock attains a median IRMSD of 11.134 Å and DockQ of 0.037, beating both EquiDock and DiffDock-PP on all three metrics with the fastest inference time among learning-based methods (36.7 s). The pre-trained HDock and AlphaFold-Multimer baselines achieve substantially lower CRMSD/IRMSD (e.g., HDock: 0.323/0.262 on RAbD) but require 37,000–197,000 s per prediction, making them impractical for high-throughput screening.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EquiDock (the closest regression-based competitor), ElliDock wins on both CRMSD and IRMSD on both DB5.5 (12.995 vs. 14.136; 11.134 vs. 11.971) and RAbD (11.541 vs. 14.301; 11.319 vs. 12.700), while also being 2–3× faster (36.7 s vs. 60.1 s on DB5.5). Against DiffDock-PP, ElliDock achieves lower IRMSD and higher DockQ on both datasets and is roughly 90× faster (91.2 s vs. 8,308.7 s on RAbD); on RAbD it also edges out DiffDock-PP on CRMSD (11.541 vs. 11.764). The pre-trained HDock and AlphaFold-Multimer baselines dominate in absolute accuracy (HDock CRMSD ≈ 0.32 vs. ElliDock ≈ 11.5), but their inference times of 37,000–197,000 s per complex make them infeasible for large-scale screening; the authors also flag a potential data-leakage concern for HDock. On DB5.5, AlphaFold-Multimer achieves a CRMSD of 1.987 (vs. ElliDock's 12.995), but on the more challenging RAbD set the gap narrows considerably (13.598 vs. 11.541), where ElliDock actually outperforms Multimer on CRMSD and IRMSD.

## Relation to Prior Work

ElliDock builds directly on EquiDock's (Ganea et al., 2021) independent SE(3)-equivariant framework and PAINN's equivariant message passing backbone, but departs from the point-cloud-registration paradigm by replacing keypoint alignment with parametric elliptic paraboloid interface fitting. The EPIT architecture introduces a novel pairwise dense-edge inter-protein interaction module that operates on invariant features only, addressing the challenge that unbound proteins cannot form meaningful local 3D edges. Conceptually, the paper reframes rigid docking as a geometric surface-fitting problem rather than a point-correspondence or generative sampling problem, positioning itself between the deterministic regression of EquiDock and the iterative diffusion of DiffDock-PP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The primary practical contribution is a ~50–1500× speedup over competing learning-based methods (36.7 s vs. 2103 s for DiffDock-PP and 56,762 s for Multimer on DB5.5), making large-scale virtual screening of protein pairs feasible. The overlap loss that spatially separates receptor and ligand on opposite sides of the predicted interface is a clean mechanism to avoid steric clashes without post-hoc filtering. Key limitations include the rigid-body assumption (no conformational changes), reliance on soft constraints that may not fully enforce geometric properties, and the fact that absolute docking accuracy on DB5.5 remains far below template-based methods like HDock, suggesting the paraboloid approximation has a ceiling for diverse interface shapes. Open questions include whether the interface-fitting paradigm extends to flexible docking and whether hard architectural constraints could replace the current soft losses.
