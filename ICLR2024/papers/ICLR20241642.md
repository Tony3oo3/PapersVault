## Problem

Existing MetaBBO methods are either constrained by the inherent limitations of a hand-crafted backbone optimizer (auto-configuration approaches) or suffer from overfitting and poor generalization when using neural networks to directly propose candidate solutions. No prior framework can autonomously discover novel, interpretable, closed-form optimization rules that generalize zero-shot across unseen tasks with varying dimensions, population sizes, and optimization horizons.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241642/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each optimization step, the SEG receives the current candidate population, a set of nine task-agnostic Fitness Landscape Analysis features (population dispersion, objective-value statistics, and temporal stagnation indicators), and the partially constructed symbolic expression tree encoded as a 124-bit Vectorized Tree Embedding. It outputs a closed-form update rule τ^(t) (e.g., 0.18×(x*−xr)+0.42×(xi*−xr)) composed from a 10-symbol basis set, which is applied as x^(t+1)=x^(t)+τ^(t) to produce the next population. Constants within the rule are inferred on-the-fly as mantissa×10^exponent pairs from a discrete grid, and the expression tree depth is bounded between 2 and 5.

## Method

The Symbolic Equation Generator (SEG) is an LSTM that autoregressively constructs a pre-order traversal of a symbolic expression tree, selecting tokens from a basis symbol set {+, −, ×, x, x*, x⁻, xi*, Δx, xr, c} at each step, with token masking to enforce syntactic validity. FLA features are embedded as the LSTM's initial cell state, while the VTE of the half-built tree serves as the recurrent input, enabling context-aware rule generation. Three PPO-based training strategies meta-learn the SEG: SYMBOL-E rewards progress toward the known optimum from scratch; SYMBOL-G rewards closeness (negated Earth Mover's Distance) to a teacher optimizer's population for behavior cloning; and SYMBOL-S synergizes both rewards with a surrogate global optimum to reduce reliance on ground-truth optima. The key architectural insight is that generating interpretable closed-form equations—rather than neural-network-predicted coordinates—yields optimizers that are both flexible (able to compose novel rules beyond hand-crafted templates) and generalizable (task-agnostic, dimension-agnostic features enable zero-shot transfer to HPO and protein-docking tasks).

## Results

SYMBOL-S achieves the best meta-test performance on CEC 2021 (0.972 ± 0.011, rank 1), surpassing ipop-CMA-ES (0.970) and LDE (0.970). On zero-shot generalization to HPO-B, SYMBOL-S (0.963 ± 0.006) ranks second only to SMAC (0.979 ± 0.005), while on Protein-docking it achieves 0.999 ± 0.000, edging out SMAC (0.998). Critically, SYMBOL-S solves each HPO-B and Protein-docking instance in under 1 second, compared to SMAC's 42 s and 227 s respectively, while three MetaBBO baselines (LDE, MELBA, RNN-Opt) fail entirely to generalize to these unseen tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SYMBOL-S outperforms all traditional BBO baselines on the in-distribution CEC-2021 meta test (0.972 vs. ipop-CMA-ES 0.970, MadDE 0.940, SMAC 0.937) and leads on Protein-docking (0.999 vs. SMAC 0.998, ipop-CMA-ES 0.996). On HPO-B, SMAC retains a narrow edge (0.979 vs. 0.963), but SYMBOL-S is ~60× faster per instance (0.7 s vs. 42 s). Among MetaBBO baselines, SYMBOL-S dominates: it beats LDE (0.970) and DEDDQN (0.959) on meta test, and is the only MetaBBO method alongside Meta-ES that generalizes to both HPO-B and Protein-docking—whereas LDE, MELBA, and RNN-Opt fail entirely. Notably, SYMBOL-S also surpasses its own teacher MadDE (0.940) by 3.2 points on meta test, and SYMBOL-G (0.964) already exceeds MadDE, confirming the framework's ability to go beyond the teacher's fixed rules. The speed advantage is stark: SYMBOL-S completes each HPO-B instance in 0.7 s and each Protein-docking instance in 0.7 s, versus SMAC's 42 s and 227 s.

## Relation to Prior Work

SYMBOL sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: symbolic regression via autoregressive RNN+RL (Petersen et al., 2021; Biggio et al., 2021) and meta-learning for black-box optimization (MetaBBO). Prior MetaBBO methods either fine-tune hyperparameters of a fixed backbone optimizer (DEDDQN, LDE, Meta-ES) or directly propose candidate solutions via neural networks (RNN-Opt, MELBA), both of which inherit limitations of hand-crafted rules or suffer from poor generalization. SYMBOL departs from both branches by generating closed-form symbolic equations as the update rule itself, repurposing the symbolic regression paradigm (token-by-token tree construction with constant inference) to the BBO domain, and training the generator via PPO with three distinct reward formulations (exploration, guided, synergized).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is that SYMBOL's generated optimizers are interpretable (closed-form equations), generalize zero-shot across unseen dimensions, population sizes, and horizons where most MetaBBO baselines fail, and run in under a second versus SMAC's 42–227 seconds on realistic tasks. Open questions include the sample efficiency of the teacher-free SYMBOL-E strategy, the sensitivity to basis symbol set design (adding non-linear operators like sin/cos actually hurts performance), and whether the approach scales to higher-dimensional or mixed-integer BBO problems. The paper also acknowledges that teacher selection remains non-trivial for novel task domains, and that LLM-based generators represent a promising but unexplored direction.
