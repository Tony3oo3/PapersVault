## Problem

Offline RL algorithms are highly vulnerable to data corruption in real-world datasets, where states, actions, rewards, and dynamics can be randomly noisy or adversarially attacked. Prior robust offline RL work focuses on testing-time robustness or distributional robustness, leaving the training-time robustness to diverse corruption across all four dataset elements largely unexplored. This paper systematically investigates how existing offline RL algorithms degrade under such corruption and proposes a provably robust alternative.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240621/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a static offline dataset of N transitions (s, a, r, s') where any subset of the four elements may be corrupted at a rate c with scale ε, and produces a deterministic policy π(a|s) (or optionally a diagonal Gaussian policy). Corrupted data is not filtered or identified; the algorithm must learn directly from the mixed clean-and-corrupted dataset. Evaluation is performed by rolling out the learned policy in a clean environment, with performance measured as normalized return on D4RL continuous control benchmarks (HalfCheetah, Walker2d, Hopper).

## Method

RIQL builds on IQL's weighted imitation learning framework, which the paper proves is inherently robust under corruption (error decays as O(√(ζ/N)) where ζ is cumulative corruption level). Three modifications address IQL's remaining vulnerability to dynamics corruption: (1) observation normalization, which computes mean and variance over all states and next-states in the dataset to prevent large-scale features from dominating; (2) Huber loss replacing MSE in the Q-function regression, which provably recovers near-unbiased value estimates under heavy-tailed targets (requiring only a (1+ν)-th moment condition); and (3) quantile Q estimators that generalize the Clipped Double Q-learning minimum operator to an ensemble of K Q functions with an α-quantile selection, allowing flexible control over the degree of penalization for high-uncertainty (corrupted) data points while avoiding the instability of the raw minimum operator under heavy-tailedness.

## Results

Under random data corruption (30% rate, scale 1.0) on D4RL medium-replay-v2, RIQL achieves an average normalized score of 60.0, outperforming IQL (43.6), CQL (35.5), SQL (33.8), EDAC (23.5), and MSG (17.0), with only 17.0% average degradation from clean-data performance. Under adversarial corruption, RIQL scores 56.2 (vs. IQL's 33.1) with just 22.0% degradation, while all other baselines degrade by 46–71%. RIQL achieves the highest score in 9 out of 12 random-corruption settings, with the largest margins in dynamics corruption (e.g., Walker2d: 78.2 vs. IQL's 17.7; Hopper: 51.5 vs. IQL's 1.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RIQL consistently outperforms all five baselines across both random and adversarial corruption settings. Against IQL (the closest competitor), RIQL achieves a 37.6% average score improvement under random corruption and nearly 70% under adversarial corruption, with the largest gaps in dynamics attacks (e.g., Hopper dynamics: 51.5 vs. 1.3). EDAC and MSG, the leading pessimism-based ensemble methods, suffer catastrophic degradation (68.5% and 61.5% respectively under random corruption), dropping to near-zero scores on most dynamics and observation attacks. CQL and SQL, the ensemble-free baselines, show moderate degradation (42.3% and 45.0%) but still trail RIQL by 24.5 and 26.2 points in average score. Behavior Cloning exhibits the smallest degradation (0.4%) due to its purely supervised nature, but its absolute score (23.7) is far below RIQL's, confirming that RIQL successfully retains the performance benefits of return-aware learning while achieving near-BC-level robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on IQL (Kostrikov et al., 2021), modifying its Q-learning objective with Huber loss, replacing the Clipped Double Q-learning minimum operator with a quantile-based ensemble estimator (extending Fujimoto et al., 2018), and adding observation normalization. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant pessimism-based offline RL paradigm (EDAC, MSG, CQL) by showing that IQL's supervised policy learning scheme is inherently more robust to data corruption than uncertainty-based penalization of OOD actions. It also distinguishes itself from prior robust offline RL work (Zhang et al., 2022; Ye et al., 2023b; Wu et al., 2022) by addressing training-time corruption across all four data elements simultaneously, rather than focusing on testing-time distributional robustness or a single corruption type.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: real-world offline RL datasets (RLHF annotations, sensor logs) are routinely noisy or adversarially corrupted, yet SOTA algorithms like EDAC and MSG degrade by over 60% under modest corruption. RIQL's simple modifications (Huber loss, quantile Q, normalization) yield a 37.6% average improvement over IQL under random corruption and ~70% under adversarial attacks, with a clean theoretical guarantee under mild assumptions (ζ = o(N)). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond D4RL locomotion tasks to high-dimensional or language-based settings, and the authors note that observation corruption remains the hardest case for RIQL, suggesting further work is needed on handling distributional shifts in the state space itself.
