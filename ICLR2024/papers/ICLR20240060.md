## Problem

Existing image completion methods that incorporate auxiliary guidance are restricted to a single modality (e.g., segmentation or sketch), and extending them to multiple simultaneous modalities requires cumbersome joint re-training of all conditional networks. This limits scalability and flexibility, making it impractical to add new guidance types or combine more than two modalities for plausible completion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240060/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a masked RGB image (x_m) with a binary mask (m) plus any arbitrary subset of guidance modalities—text prompt, Canny edge map, sketch map, segmentation map, depth map, and pose map—and produces a completed RGB image at the original resolution. Guidance maps can be user-drawn (e.g., scribbles, pose) or estimated by off-the-shelf tools (MiDaS for depth, PiDiNet for sketch, Canny algorithm for edges). The framework operates in the latent space of Stable Diffusion Inpainting-2.1, where spatial dimensions are reduced by a factor of 8, and supports inpainting, outpainting, and local editing tasks.

## Method

MaGIC has two core components. First, a Modality-Specific Conditional U-Net (MCU-Net) pairs a frozen SD U-Net denoiser with a lightweight encoding network (τ_c) that extracts multi-scale guidance features from a single modality and injects them additively into the encoder's intermediate feature maps; each modality's MCU-Net is fine-tuned independently using classifier-free guidance loss. Second, the Consistent Modality Blending (CMB) algorithm integrates any combination of independently trained MCU-Nets in a training-free manner: at each denoising step (for the last P steps), it computes a weighted Euclidean distance between the original U-Net's intermediate features and the guided features from all active MCU-Nets, then performs Q steps of gradient descent on the denoised latent to pull it toward the multi-modal guidance distribution. This avoids joint re-training, preserves the original SD feature distribution, and allows plug-and-play addition or removal of modalities at inference time.

## Results

On COCO, MaGIC with multi-modality guidance (segmentation, canny edge, sketch, depth, and text) achieves FID 37.65±0.22 and PickScore 49.57±0.17%, outperforming the best inpainting baseline MAT (FID 45.51, PickScore 27.10%) by 21% in FID and 61% in PickScore over its own guidance-free variant. On Places2, the single-modality MCU-Net with edge guidance reaches FID 8.32±0.02, U-IDS 26.23±0.07%, and P-IDS 10.96±0.33%, substantially surpassing prior edge-based methods (e.g., ZITS: FID 18.96, U-IDS 18.75%). The PickScore of 49.57% indicates that, from a learned human-preference perspective, completed images are more faithful to the ground-truth caption than the original images themselves.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaGIC outperforms all eight baselines across every reported metric. Against traditional inpainting methods, MaGIC (multi-modality) reduces FID from 45.51 (MAT) and 48.63 (LAMA) to 37.65 on COCO, while its PickScore of 49.57% nearly doubles MAT's 27.10%. The guidance-free MaGIC† (FID 47.70, PickScore 30.79%) already matches or exceeds LAMA and MAT, confirming the strength of the SD-inpainting backbone. Among edge-based inpainting baselines, MaGIC with edge guidance (FID 41.15 on COCO, 8.32 on Places2) dominates EC (FID 76.64/25.08), CTSDG (FID 97.05/42.81), and ZITS (FID 61.27/18.96) by wide margins. In the controllable-generation comparison (Appendix Table 4), MaGIC with multi-modality guidance achieves FID 37.65 and PickScore 49.57% on COCO, beating T2I-Adapter (FID 39.08, PickScore 34.26%) by 44.68% in PickScore, and ControlNet (FID 37.17, PickScore 37.30%) by 32.9% in PickScore; on Places2, MaGIC improves FID by 37.07%, U-IDS by 71.40%, and P-IDS by 230.30% over T2I-Adapter. The only scenario where a baseline edges out MaGIC is the joint multi-modality re-training upper bound (FID 35.42 vs. 37.65), which requires paired multi-modal training data and is not practical for new modality addition.

## Relation to Prior Work

MaGIC builds directly on the Stable Diffusion latent-space U-Net architecture (Rombach et al., 2022) and borrows the lightweight adapter design from T2I-Adapter (Mou et al., 2023) for its MCU-Net encoding network. Conceptually, it departs from the joint re-training paradigm of ControlNet and T2I-Adapter by introducing CMB, a training-free blending strategy inspired by classifier-guidance diffusion (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021) but operating at the intermediate feature-map level rather than the pixel or logit level. The paper positions itself against both single-modality inpainting methods (LAMA, MAT, ZITS) and controllable generation methods (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter), arguing that neither handles arbitrary multi-modality guidance for completion without cumbersome re-training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MaGIC provides a practical, plug-and-play recipe for combining arbitrary numbers of guidance modalities (text, edge, sketch, segmentation, depth, pose) in image completion without joint re-training, which lowers the barrier for users who want to add new control signals. The training-free CMB also generalizes to image generation backbones (Anything-4.0, SD-1.5), suggesting broader applicability beyond inpainting. Key limitations remain: inference cost scales linearly with the number of modalities, high-frequency detail fidelity is bounded by the backbone, and conflicting or low-correlation modalities can produce artifacts (as shown in the failure cases). Open questions include whether CMB can be made more efficient (e.g., fewer gradient steps) and how to automatically balance modality weights δ_c without manual tuning.
