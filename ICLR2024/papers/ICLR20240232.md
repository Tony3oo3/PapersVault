## Problem

Existing structure-based drug design (SBDD) generative models learn only the target-ligand distribution, making them unable to produce molecules with desired properties (e.g., high binding affinity, synthesizability) when training data does not align with those targets. Additionally, most methods are limited to de novo design, while practically important controllable generation tasks such as R-group optimization and scaffold hopping lack a unified framework and are addressed by task-specific approaches.

## Input / Output

The model takes a protein pocket (3D atom positions and types) and a set of reference arms (substructures that locally interact with corresponding subpockets within 10 Å) as conditions, and generates a complete 3D ligand molecule (atom positions, atom types, and bond types). The key structural detail is that the ligand is decomposed into arms and a scaffold: only the arms are conditioned (via SE(3)-invariant features from an EGNN encoder), while the scaffold is left unconditioned to preserve generative diversity. During optimization, the input arms are iteratively updated from ordered arm lists (OALs) per subpocket, and the output molecules are re-decomposed to extract new arm candidates for the next iteration.

## Method

DECOMPOPT combines a controllable and decomposed diffusion model with iterative optimization in a decomposed drug space. The condition encoder uses an SE(3)-equivariant EGNN to encode each reference arm together with its surrounding subpocket, producing per-atom and global SE(3)-invariant features that are injected into the diffusion decoder only for arm atoms (scaffold atoms remain unconditioned). The forward noising process is independent of arm conditions; conditions enter exclusively in the reverse denoising process, enabling fine-grained per-arm control while preserving scaffold diversity. The optimization loop maintains ordered arm lists (OALs) per subpocket: each iteration samples arms from OALs, generates a batch of ligands via the diffusion model, decomposes them into new arms, evaluates each arm with a multi-objective score (QED, SA, Vina Min via Z-score aggregation), and replaces the worst arms in the OALs. This generative mutation strategy is more efficient than rule-based perturbation used in traditional evolutionary methods, and the decomposition enables arm-level optimization that outperforms molecule-level selection.

## Results

On the CrossDocked2020 benchmark, DECOMPOPT achieves a mean Vina Dock score of −8.98 kcal/mol and a Success Rate of 52.5% (molecules passing QED > 0.25, SA > 0.59, and Vina Dock < −8.18 simultaneously), setting a new state-of-the-art for structure-based molecular optimization. It also attains a High Affinity rate of 73.5% (percentage of generated molecules binding better than the reference per protein). In single-objective ablations, DECOMPOPT improves QED by +8.3%, SA by +10.5%, and Vina Min by +11.8% over the DecompDiff generation baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DECOMPOPT outperforms all pure generation baselines (LiGAN, GraphBP, AR, Pocket2Mol, TargetDiff, DecompDiff) on Vina Dock, High Affinity, and Success Rate, with the largest gap over DecompDiff (−8.98 vs. −8.39 Vina Dock; 52.5% vs. 24.5% Success Rate). Against the optimization-only baseline RGA, DECOMPOPT achieves a better Vina Dock (−8.98 vs. −8.01) and higher Success Rate (52.5% vs. 46.2%) while maintaining substantially higher diversity (0.60 vs. 0.41), avoiding the local-solution trap typical of rule-based mutation. Compared to TargetDiff w/ Opt. (which adds whole-molecule conditions to TargetDiff), DECOMPOPT wins on Vina Dock (−8.98 vs. −8.30) and Success Rate (52.5% vs. 25.8%) but trails on QED (0.48 vs. 0.66) and SA (0.65 vs. 0.68), reflecting a trade-off between binding affinity and drug-likeness. The only metric where DECOMPOPT underperforms the best generation baseline is Diversity (0.60 vs. 0.68 for DecompDiff), a consequence of the optimization process concentrating on high-scoring arms. In controllable generation tasks (R-group design, scaffold hopping), DECOMPOPT also surpasses DecompDiff+Inpainting on completeness (93.0% vs. 89.2%) and achieves lower scaffold similarity (0.35 vs. 0.40), indicating better exploration of novel chemical space while preserving pivotal groups.

## Relation to Prior Work

DECOMPOPT builds directly on DecompDiff (Guan et al., 2023b), adopting its decomposed prior distribution, SE(3)-equivariant architecture, and training loss, while introducing a condition encoder that allows fine-grained control over individual arms. Conceptually, it departs from the pure generative modeling paradigm by coupling the diffusion model with an iterative optimization loop over ordered arm lists, drawing on evolutionary algorithm ideas (cf. RGA, AutoGrow4) but applying them in a decomposed 3D space where generative sampling serves as a more efficient mutation operator than rule-based perturbation. It also unifies previously separate controllable generation tasks (R-group design, scaffold hopping) under a single 3D framework, extending 2D approaches (Imrie et al., Huang et al.) that were task-specific.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in SBDD: generative models learn the training distribution but cannot reliably produce molecules with desired properties (high affinity, drug-likeness, synthesizability) when training data don't align with those targets, while pure optimization methods suffer from low diversity and local optima. The unified decomposed framework is practically valuable because it covers both de novo design and the more common industrial workflows (R-group optimization, scaffold hopping) in a single model. Limitations include reliance on Vina scores as a proxy for true binding affinity, evaluation on a single dataset (CrossDocked2020), and the authors' own acknowledgment that multi-objective optimization remains underexplored. Open questions include whether the arm-level decomposition generalizes to targets with very different pocket geometries and whether the optimization loop scales to more complex multi-objective criteria.
