## Problem

Existing vision-language models can only understand images holistically, lacking the ability to recognize and comprehend individual objects at the instance level as humans do. This limitation is compounded by the scarcity of large-scale open-world region-text data: prior datasets either lack spatial location (Laion-5B), lack semantic detail (SA-1B), or are too small and closed-set (Visual Genome, COCO) to support panoptic open-world understanding.

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB image together with an optional bounding box (x1, y1, x2, y2) and a natural-language prompt. When no box is provided, the bounding box defaults to the full image, unifying region-level and image-level processing. For generative tasks the model outputs autoregressive text (region captions, VQA answers); for discriminative tasks it outputs a similarity score computed from a learned <align> token embedding, enabling region-text retrieval and zero-shot recognition.

## Method

ASM combines a location-aware image tokenizer with an LLM-based decoder (Husky-7B) under a unified training objective (generation loss + contrastive loss). The tokenizer extends BLIP-2's Q-Former by first encoding the image with ViT-g/14, extracting region features via RoIAlign conditioned on the input bounding box, and concatenating them with learned query tokens before Q-Former cross-attention, so that spatial location is baked into the visual representation. A trainable task prompt at the sequence head switches between generative and discriminative modes, and a learnable <align> token aggregates the holistic representation for contrastive matching. The model is trained on the newly introduced AS-1B dataset (1.2B regions, 3.5M open-world concepts, 132.2B tokens), which is produced by a semi-automatic 'data-human-model' loop: SAM, InternImage, EVA-02, and GLIP generate dense region proposals; LLMs (Vicuna, MiniGPT-4) and VLLMs (BLIP, Husky) generate semantic tags, QA pairs, and captions; CLIP/CLIPSeg match tags to regions; and human annotators verify a sampled subset to iteratively fine-tune the models, raising annotation accuracy from 54.8% to 83.5% over three loops.

## Results

On zero-shot region recognition, ASM achieves 69.6 mAP on COCO and 60.5 mAP on LVIS, outperforming CLIP by 10.7 and 13.4 mAP respectively. For image-level captioning, ASM-FT reaches 88.0 CIDEr on Flickr30K and 116.9 CIDEr on NoCaps, surpassing all concurrent VLLMs including InstructBLIP (82.8) and Shikra-13B (73.9). On region-level captioning, ASM-FT sets a new record on RefCOCOg with 21.8 Meteor and 107.8 CIDEr, exceeding GRiT (15.2/71.6) and Kosmos-2 (12.2/60.3) by large margins.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASM outperforms CLIP by 10.7 mAP on COCO (69.6 vs. 58.9) and 13.4 mAP on LVIS (60.5 vs. 47.1) in zero-shot region recognition, and beats OpenCLIP by 6.3 and 11.4 mAP respectively. For image-level captioning, ASM-FT surpasses InstructBLIP (88.0 vs. 82.8 CIDEr on Flickr30K), Shikra-13B (88.0 vs. 73.9), Kosmos-1 (88.0 vs. 67.1), and Flamingo-9B (88.0 vs. 61.5), though it trails BLIP-2 on NoCaps CIDEr (116.9 vs. 121.6). On region-level captioning, ASM-FT substantially outperforms GRiT (21.8 vs. 15.2 Meteor, 107.8 vs. 71.6 CIDEr on RefCOCOg) and SLR (21.8 vs. 15.4 Meteor, 107.8 vs. 59.2 CIDEr), while in zero-shot it edges out Kosmos-2 on Meteor (15.0 vs. 12.2) but falls short on CIDEr (48.8 vs. 60.3) before fine-tuning closes the gap decisively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the BLIP-2 Q-Former architecture by conditioning its queries on bounding-box location information, creating a location-aware image tokenizer that unifies region-level and image-level feature extraction. It departs from prior LLM-based VLMs (MiniGPT-4, InstructBLIP, Kosmos-2) that operate only at the whole-image level, and from closed-set region recognition models (RegionCLIP, GRiT) that lack open-world semantics, by proposing a single framework that handles both discriminative (region-text alignment) and generative (captioning, VQA) tasks with shared weights. The data engine draws on an ensemble of existing perception models (SAM, GLIP, InternImage, EVA-02) and LLMs (Vicuna, MiniGPT-4, BLIP) in a semi-automatic 'data-human-model' loop, building on the SA-1B image collection but adding the missing open-world semantic layer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AS-1B (1.2B regions, 3.5M concepts, 132.2B tokens) fills a critical gap: no prior dataset simultaneously provides open-world location, open-world semantics, and detailed region-level descriptions at this scale (159× more concepts than ImageNet-22K, 33× more regions than BigDetection). The practical data engine methodology—iteratively refining model-generated annotations with limited human verification—offers a replicable recipe for scaling region-level annotation beyond what pure human labeling can afford. Key limitations include reliance on ground-truth boxes for evaluation (no end-to-end detection), the 7B parameter size, and the fact that LLM-generated semantic tags still require filtering (only ~26% of LLM/VLLM tags survive matching). Open questions include whether the iterative data engine converges beyond 3–4 loops, how the approach extends to video or 3D, and whether the unified discriminative-generative architecture scales to larger LLM backbones without degrading contrastive alignment quality.
