## Problem

Gaussian processes with standard Matérn or RBF kernels are almost exclusively used as surrogate models in Bayesian optimization, despite BNNs offering natural advantages such as non-stationary function modeling, learned similarity metrics for high-dimensional inputs, and multi-output handling. No prior work has systematically compared the full spectrum of BNN surrogate types—varying in stochasticity, width, and inference procedure—against GPs across diverse optimization problems, leaving it unclear whether and when BNNs are a viable alternative.

## Input / Output

The surrogate models consume a small set of noisy objective function evaluations (typically 10–3000 queries) over input spaces that can be continuous, discrete, or mixed, with dimensions ranging from 2 to 6,320. The output is a posterior predictive distribution over the objective at all candidate points, which feeds into a Monte-Carlo Expected Improvement acquisition function to select the next batch of query points. For multi-objective problems, the surrogate independently models each objective and the acquisition function optimizes hypervolume on the Pareto front.

## Method

The paper conducts a comprehensive empirical comparison of seven surrogate model families—standard GP (Matérn 5/2), fully stochastic finite-width BNNs with three inference procedures (full-batch HMC, SGHMC, deep ensembles), infinite-width BNNs (equivalent to GPs with neural-network-derived non-stationary kernels), linearized Laplace approximation, and deep kernel learning—across 15 benchmarks spanning synthetic, real-world, and high-dimensional settings. All surrogates use Monte-Carlo Expected Improvement as the acquisition function, which only requires posterior samples and thus removes the need for closed-form predictive distributions. The study isolates the roles of stochasticity, representation learning, and strong architectural priors by comparing models that differ along exactly one axis (e.g., DKL vs. HMC isolates full vs. partial stochasticity; I-BNN vs. finite-width BNN isolates representation learning). Key findings include that HMC is the best approximate inference for finite-width BNNs, deep ensembles perform surprisingly poorly due to insufficient model diversity in the low-data regime, and infinite-width BNNs dominate in high-dimensional settings because their fixed non-Euclidean, non-stationary kernel provides a strong prior without requiring data-hungry representation learning.

## Results

The paper evaluates seven surrogate models (GP, HMC-BNN, SGHMC-BNN, Deep Ensemble, DKL, LLA, I-BNN) across 15 benchmarks spanning 2–6,320 input dimensions. The headline finding is that no single method dominates: HMC-BNN is the best finite-width BNN inference procedure, I-BNNs clearly outperform all other surrogates in high-dimensional settings (e.g., Knowledge Distillation at 6,320 dims), and standard GPs with Matérn-5/2 kernels remain competitive on low-dimensional synthetic benchmarks. Deep ensembles consistently plateau at lower objective values than all other surrogates, and SGHMC/LLA show high variance across problems. On the marginal log-likelihood metric (Table A.1), I-BNNs achieve substantially higher MLL than GPs in high dimensions (e.g., −2,457 vs. −39,198 on 100-dim NN Draw; −3,060 vs. −2,126,659 on 6,230-dim Knowledge Distillation).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard GP (Matérn-5/2) baseline [Snoek et al., 2012], HMC-BNN is broadly comparable on low-dimensional synthetic benchmarks (Branin, Hartmann) but outperforms GP on 10-dim Ackley; I-BNN slightly underperforms GP on these same low-dimensional problems but decisively wins in high-dimensional settings (Polynomial, NN Draw, Knowledge Distillation). Among finite-width BNN inference methods, full-batch HMC [Izmailov et al., 2021] consistently outperforms SGHMC [Chen et al., 2014], which shows high variance and often fails to approach the optimum, and deep ensembles [Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017], which plateau at noticeably lower rewards (e.g., on BraninCurrin and DTLZ1). DKL [Wilson et al., 2016] is relatively competitive, matching GP on Branin and BraninCurrin but underperforming on highly non-stationary problems like Ackley. LLA [Kristiadi et al., 2023] has poor and inconsistent performance across single-objective problems. In the high-dimensional regime, I-BNN [Lee et al., 2017] outperforms all other surrogates including GP, with the gap widening as dimensionality increases (e.g., GP MLL drops to −2.1M on 6,230-dim Knowledge Distillation while I-BNN stays at −3,060).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the long tradition of GP surrogates in Bayesian optimization by systematically evaluating a wide range of neural network-based alternatives, building on recent advances that made BNNs practical (Izmailov et al. 2021; Wilson and Izmailov 2020) and Monte-Carlo acquisition functions that removed the need for closed-form predictive distributions (Balandat et al. 2020). Prior attempts at BNN surrogates (Springenberg et al. 2016; Kim et al. 2021; Foldager et al. 2023) were limited to specific inference methods or specialized settings, while this work is the first to compare fully stochastic finite-width BNNs (HMC, SGHMC, deep ensembles), infinite-width BNNs, deep kernel learning, and linearized Laplace approximations under a unified protocol. It also departs from prior work by deliberately avoiding a 'horse in the race,' including standard GPs as a strong baseline and reporting where they remain competitive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that no single surrogate dominates across problems, and that the ranking is highly problem-dependent, suggests that surrogate selection should be tailored to the objective rather than defaulting to GPs. The practical implication that infinite-width BNNs—which require no approximate inference, no hyperparameter learning, and provide a non-Euclidean non-stationary prior—may serve as a strong default for high-dimensional BO is a concrete, actionable recommendation. Open questions include whether I-BNNs can be further adapted (e.g., with learned hyperparameters or architecture search) to close the gap on lower-dimensional problems where GPs remain competitive, and whether the poor performance of deep ensembles in the low-data BO regime generalizes to other sequential decision-making settings.
