## Problem

Fixed robustness benchmarks (e.g., ImageNet-C) inevitably lead to selection bias as methods overfit to specific test sets, while perturbation-based evaluations are constrained to well-defined, limited distortion spaces that may not expose real-world weaknesses. The paper asks: can we dynamically generate evaluation images that are sufficiently different from the original test set yet provably preserve the underlying image-label structure, and can we define a robustness metric that is comparable across different dynamically generated test sets?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240361/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean image (x, y) at 224×224 resolution, the model θ to evaluate, and a zoo of foundation models (CLIP, ConvNeXt-T-CvSt, CoCa) as the surrogate oracle. It produces a perturbed image x̂ that the foundation model ensemble still classifies as class y, along with a scalar robustness score FMR = PA/SA × 100%. The generation is iterative with a fixed computation budget B=50 and per-step perturbation size of 0.001 in VQGAN latent space; if any iteration causes the foundation models to disagree on the label, the process reverts to the last valid image and stops.

## Method

The core mechanism is counterfactual generation in VQGAN's encoder space, guided by the evaluated model's classification loss as the scoring function: the system iteratively maximizes l(θ(x̂), y) (pushing the model toward misclassification) subject to the hard constraint that a majority-vote ensemble of foundation models still predicts the correct class y. This creates a dynamic, model-specific attack that is unbounded in pixel space but bounded in semantic space by the oracle. The FMR metric normalizes perturbed accuracy (PA) by standard accuracy (SA) to disentangle the two, yielding a single robustness score interpretable as the fraction of the model's clean performance that survives under oracle-validated perturbations. A sparse VQGAN variant (retaining only ~0.69% of latent dimensions identified via LASSO) reduces runtime by up to 28.5% while preserving evaluation conclusions, making the protocol feasible on a single GPU.

## Results

Under the proposed FMR (Foundation Model-oriented Robustness) metric, standard models show significant robustness gaps: on ImageNet, ResNet50 achieves only 40.11% FMR (vs. 76.26% SA), while Swin Transformer leads among standard architectures at 67.26% FMR. Among OOD-robust methods, DAT (Discrete Adversarial Training) achieves the best FMR of 68.66%, substantially outperforming SIN (40.07%) and ANT (40.14%), which perform at or below vanilla ResNet50. The results reveal that methods well-ranked on fixed benchmarks like ImageNet-C (e.g., ANT+SIN at 52.60% SA*) do not necessarily transfer to dynamic evaluation, validating the need for the proposed protocol.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 12 baselines under the new FMR metric. Among robustness methods, DAT (Mao et al., 2022) achieves the best FMR of 68.66%, far exceeding DeepAug+AM (55.56%), AugMix (50.68%), DeepAug (43.59%), ANT+SIN (40.77%), ANT (40.14%), and SIN (40.07%); notably, SIN and ANT perform at or below vanilla ResNet50 (40.11%) despite strong ImageNet-C scores, exposing selection bias in fixed benchmarks. Among standard architectures on ImageNet, Swin Transformer (Liu et al., 2021) achieves the highest FMR (67.26%), followed by Twins (57.71%), Visformer (57.22%), ConvNeXt (55.38%), DeiT (52.41%), and ViT (48.02%), all outperforming ResNet50 (40.11%). The paper attributes the transformer advantage to strong data augmentation (Mixup, CutMix, Random Erasing) used in their training, as ConvNeXt with similar augmentation closes the gap. Across all experiments, a large robustness gap persists between evaluated models and the foundation model zoo, with FMR values typically 30–60 percentage points below SA, indicating that current models remain far from foundation-model-level robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of robustness evaluation: perturbation-based protocols (adversarial attacks, texture/frequency shifts) that preserve image-label structure but limit perturbation variety, and fixed-dataset protocols (ImageNet-C, ImageNet-A, WILDS) that offer diverse samples but suffer from selection bias once published. It departs from both by introducing a dynamic counterfactual generation process that uses VQGAN's latent space, guided by the evaluated model's own classification loss, and constrained by a majority-vote ensemble of foundation models (CLIP, ConvNeXt-T-CvSt, CoCa) to maintain semantic integrity. The FMR metric further departs from prior work by measuring robustness as a ratio relative to a foundation-model oracle rather than comparing models against each other on a shared fixed test set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The protocol offers a practical path toward mitigating the well-known 'model selection with test set' problem: because perturbed images are generated dynamically conditioned on the model under test, methods cannot overfit to a static benchmark. The finding that top ImageNet-C methods (SIN, ANT) perform at or below vanilla ResNet50 under dynamic evaluation is a cautionary result for the community's reliance on fixed corruption benchmarks. Key limitations include dependence on foundation model quality (zero-shot errors introduce label noise), computational cost of iterative VQGAN optimization, and the fact that the 'oracle' is still an imperfect proxy for human perception. Open questions include whether the FMR metric transfers to other modalities, how to handle domain-specific settings where general foundation models underperform, and whether the dynamic protocol can be made cheap enough for routine use in model development loops.
