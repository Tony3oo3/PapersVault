## Problem

Convolution-based models have been losing ground in time series analysis because their limited effective receptive fields (ERFs) prevent them from capturing long-term temporal dependencies, while Transformer and MLP models with global ERFs dominate. Previous attempts to revive convolution (MICN, SCINet) added sophisticated auxiliary structures but still suffered from small ERFs. The open question is how to modernize the convolution operator itself for time series, rather than merely wrapping it in extra modules.

## Input / Output

The model takes multivariate time series of M variables and length L (or univariate series for M4 and univariate ETT tasks), which are patchified via a 1D convolution stem (kernel P, stride S) into N D-dimensional tokens while preserving the variable dimension. For imputation, the input is element-wise multiplied by a binary mask to represent missing values, and the output is the fully imputed series. For anomaly detection, the output is a reconstruction whose MSE serves as the anomaly score; for classification, a flattened representation is projected to a class label via softmax.

## Method

ModernTCN redesigns the 1D convolution block following the ConvNeXt paradigm: a depth-wise convolution (DWConv) handles temporal mixing per variable and per feature independently with a large kernel (size 51), while two decoupled grouped point-wise convolution FFNs (ConvFFN1 and ConvFFN2) handle feature mixing per variable and cross-variable mixing per feature, respectively. This three-way decoupling ensures each sub-module mixes information along exactly one axis (temporal, feature, or variable), reducing optimization difficulty. The key insight is that enlarging the kernel size grows the ERF linearly (O(ks·√nl)) versus sub-linearly with depth, so a single large-kernel DWConv in one block outperforms stacking many small-kernel layers. Structural re-parameterization fuses a parallel small-kernel branch into the large kernel at inference for stable training. A patchify variable-independent embedding via a 1D conv stem replaces the variable-mixing embedding used in CV, preserving the variable dimension for downstream cross-variable modeling.

## Results

On long-term forecasting across 9 benchmarks, ModernTCN achieves the best or near-best MSE on 8 out of 9 datasets (e.g., ETTh1: 0.404, ETTm2: 0.253, Electricity: 0.156, Weather: 0.224), surpassing the best Transformer (PatchTST) and all MLP-based baselines. On short-term forecasting (M4), ModernTCN achieves the best SMAPE (11.698), MASE (1.556), and OWA (0.838). For imputation, it achieves 22.5% MSE reduction and 12.9% MAE reduction over the previous SOTA (TimesNet). In classification, ModernTCN reaches 74.2% average accuracy across 10 UEA datasets, and in anomaly detection it matches TimesNet's F1 while saving 57.3% training time per epoch.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest Transformer baseline PatchTST, ModernTCN matches or slightly beats it on 7 of 9 long-term forecasting datasets (e.g., ETTh1: 0.404 vs 0.413 MSE; ETTm2: 0.253 vs 0.255), while PatchTST edges ahead only on Traffic (0.391 vs 0.396 MSE). Compared to the best prior convolution-based model TimesNet, ModernTCN achieves a 27.4% average MSE reduction and 15.3% MAE reduction across all 9 datasets, with the largest gap on ILI (1.440 vs 2.139 MSE) and Electricity (0.156 vs 0.192). Against MLP-based baselines (DLinear, MTS-Mixer, RLinear), ModernTCN outperforms all of them on 8 of 9 datasets, confirming that the decoupled DWConv + ConvFFN1 + ConvFFN2 design provides stronger representation than lightweight MLP backbones. Among other convolution-based methods, ModernTCN substantially outperforms MICN (e.g., ETTh1: 0.404 vs 0.433) and SCINet (0.404 vs 0.460), which the paper attributes to their limited effective receptive fields. In imputation, ModernTCN achieves 22.5% MSE reduction over TimesNet, and in classification it reaches 74.2% accuracy vs TimesNet's lower score while saving 55.1% training time per epoch. In anomaly detection, ModernTCN matches TimesNet's F1 (e.g., SMD: 85.81 vs 85.81) while training 57.3% faster per epoch.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the 'modern convolution' paradigm from computer vision (ConvNeXt, RepLKNet, SLaK) into time series analysis, redesigning the 1D convolution block with depth-wise convolution, inverted-bottleneck ConvFFN, and large kernels to mimic the Transformer block structure. It departs from prior time-series convolution efforts (MICN, SCINet, TimesNet) that augmented traditional small-kernel convolution with multi-scale or 2D-transform structures, instead arguing that the convolution operator itself should be modernized. The patchify variable-independent embedding borrows the patching idea from PatchTST but implements it as a fully-convolutional stem to preserve the variable dimension, and the decoupled ConvFFN1/ConvFFN2 design is a time-series-specific modification not present in the CV original.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a compelling efficiency-performance argument: a pure convolution model with large kernels can match or beat state-of-the-art Transformer and MLP models across five diverse time series tasks while using less memory and training faster, which is practically significant for deployment-constrained settings. The ERF analysis (ERF ∝ ks × √nl) offers a clear design principle—enlarge kernel size rather than stack layers—that could guide future convolution-based time series architectures. Open questions include whether the approach scales to extremely long sequences or very high-dimensional variable spaces (the Traffic bottleneck workaround hints at limitations), and whether the structural re-parameterization trick generalizes beyond the tested kernel sizes.
