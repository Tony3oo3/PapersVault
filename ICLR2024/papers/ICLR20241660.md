## Problem

LLMs are limited to processing plain text and cannot natively interpret user-specified emphasis markers (e.g., bold, italics) to direct attention to critical parts of a prompt. Even when such markers are added, state-of-the-art LLMs struggle to discern these weak signals, leading to poor instruction following, failure to capture key contextual details, and inability to resolve knowledge conflicts within contexts.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt containing user-specified emphasis spans (delimited by markers like asterisks, which need not be a continuous span) and read-only access to attention scores from selected heads during inference. It produces steered text generation where the model's attention is biased toward the highlighted tokens. No model parameters are modified; the intervention is purely at inference time and requires only the attention score tensors, not gradients or weight access.

## Method

PASTA consists of two components: (1) post-hoc attention steering, which scales down the attention scores of non-highlighted tokens by a coefficient α (0 ≤ α < 1) at selected heads and renormalizes, effectively upweighting the user-specified tokens by 1/α while preserving relative attention magnitudes among highlighted tokens; and (2) multi-task model profiling, which identifies the effective subset of heads to steer by evaluating each head's steering performance on small (1000-sample) training sets from multiple tasks and taking the intersection of top-k performing heads across tasks. The key insight is that different attention heads encode distinct semantic/syntactic information, so steering all heads degrades performance while steering a carefully selected subset (typically 50–150 heads) substantially improves task performance. The profiling is performed once per model and the resulting head set generalizes to unseen tasks without re-profiling.

## Results

On LLAMA-7B, PASTA (multi-task) achieves an average accuracy of 95.46% across four tasks, a 22% improvement over the strongest baseline (few-shot prompting at 73.45%). Notable per-task gains include 99.60% Efficacy Score on CounterFact (vs. 87.45% for few-shot), 96.64% format accuracy on JSON Formatting (vs. 84.85%), and 95.28% accuracy on BiasBios (vs. 88.79%). On GPT-J-6B, PASTA (multi-task) reaches 85.22% average, outperforming few-shot (59.65%) by 25.6%. The task-agnostic variant (no labeled data for the target task) still achieves 85.89% on LLAMA-7B, exceeding few-shot by 12.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PASTA outperforms all three prompting baselines across every task and both models. Few-shot prompting is the strongest baseline (73.45% avg on LLAMA-7B), yet PASTA multi-task surpasses it by 22 percentage points on average; the gap is largest on CounterFact (99.60% ES vs. 87.45%) and JSON format accuracy (96.64% vs. 84.85%). Marked prompting (adding asterisks or quotes around the target span) actually degrades performance severely—dropping to 49.38% and 42.15% average on LLAMA-7B—confirming that LLMs cannot reliably interpret textual emphasis markers from pre-training. Zero-shot prompting (67.29%) is intermediate but still far below PASTA. The task-agnostic variant of PASTA (which excludes the evaluation task from profiling) already beats few-shot on three of four tasks, demonstrating that the selected attention heads generalize without task-specific labeled data.

## Relation to Prior Work

PASTA sits at the intersection of mechanistic interpretability of attention heads (Tenney et al., 2019; Voita et al., 2019; Deb et al., 2023) and inference-time intervention methods. It departs from model editing approaches (ROME, MEMIT, MEND) by requiring no weight modification, and from prompting strategies by operating directly on attention scores rather than relying on the model to parse textual emphasis markers. The multi-task profiling step draws on the observation that attention heads encode distinct semantic/syntactic roles, while the steering mechanism itself is conceptually related to TOAST (Shi et al., 2023a), which also reweights attention for transfer learning but targets a different objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PASTA offers a parameter-free, one-time-profiling mechanism that could be integrated into user-facing LLM interfaces to let users highlight critical instructions or facts, addressing a practical gap in current interaction paradigms. Key limitations include the requirement for direct access to attention scores (not universally exposed by all model APIs), evaluation restricted to 6–7B models, and the need for a small labeled profiling set per model. Open questions include whether the technique scales to 70B+ models, how it interacts with KV-cache-based serving systems, and whether the selected head sets remain stable across fine-tuning or continued pre-training.
