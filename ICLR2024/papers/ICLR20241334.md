## Problem

Existing autoregressive 3D molecular generators (G-SchNet, G-SphereNet) rely on rotationally invariant features, which cannot capture the orientations of geometric motifs around a single atom. This forces them to use triangulation across multiple atoms or unphysical auxiliary tokens to resolve positional degeneracy, making predictions non-smooth and sensitive to atom ordering. The paper asks whether higher-degree E(3)-equivariant features can enable accurate, single-atom-centered position prediction without these workarounds.

## Input / Output

Symphony takes a random seed (or a partial molecular fragment for the completion task) and iteratively outputs atom types and 3D coordinates, building a full molecule up to 35 atoms. The position distribution is parameterized over 64 uniformly spaced radial shells (0.9–2.0 Å) with angular distributions expressed as spherical harmonic coefficients up to l_max=5, evaluated on a 180×359 spherical Gauss-Legendre quadrature grid. A global STOP token terminates generation, and the model can also be conditioned on a fixed fragment to predict only the remaining atoms.

## Method

Symphony is an autoregressive model that constructs molecules via a minimum spanning tree fragment sequence, predicting at each step a focus atom, target species, and target position. The key architectural contribution is parameterizing the 3D position distribution as spherical harmonic projections on radial shells: the unnormalized logits f(r,θ,φ) are expressed as a sum over l of coefficients c_l(r) dotted with Y_l(θ,φ), where c_l(r) is a linear map of the tensor product between the focus node's degree-l equivariant features and the target atom-type embedding. Two separate E(3)-equivariant embedders are used—E3SchNet (l_max=2) for focus/species and NequIP (l_max=5) for position—allowing the position prediction to capture higher angular detail. To bypass the angular frequency bottleneck of finite l_max, the model uses 2 channels of spherical harmonics combined via a log-sum-exp, effectively increasing representational capacity. Training uses teacher forcing with KL divergence to empirically computed target distributions, where the true position target is a smoothed Dirac delta approximated in the spherical harmonic basis.

## Results

On QM9, Symphony achieves 83.50% validity and 97.98% uniqueness (via xyz2mol), outperforming G-SchNet (74.97%/96.73%) and G-SphereNet (26.92%/21.69%) but slightly trailing EDM (86.74%/99.16%). On the bispectra MMD metric for local environment geometry, Symphony attains the lowest score for several environments (e.g., C:C3,H1: 0.1111, N:C2,H1: 0.0953), beating EDM on 4 out of 10 tested environments. Symphony's inference speed is 0.293 sec/molecule, approximately 3× faster than EDM (0.930 sec/mol) but slower than G-SchNet (0.011 sec/mol). On valid completion rate for unseen fragments, Symphony (98.66%) slightly outperforms G-SchNet (98.15%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EDM (the strongest diffusion baseline), Symphony trades a small validity gap (83.50% vs 86.74% via xyz2mol) for a ~3× speedup in inference (0.293 vs 0.930 sec/mol) and wins on 4 of 10 bispectra MMD scores for local environment geometry (e.g., C:C3,H1: 0.1111 vs 0.2254; N:C2,H1: 0.0953 vs 0.2339), indicating better angular accuracy in those motifs. Against G-SchNet, Symphony clearly wins on validity (83.50% vs 74.97%), uniqueness (97.98% vs 96.73%), and most bispectra MMD scores, though G-SchNet is ~27× faster at inference (0.011 vs 0.293 sec/mol). Against G-SphereNet, Symphony dominates across every metric: validity improves from 26.92% to 83.50%, uniqueness from 21.69% to 97.98%, and bispectra MMD drops by an order of magnitude in most environments (e.g., C:C1,H3: 1.2004 → 0.2668). On the valid completion rate task for unseen fragments, Symphony (98.66%) marginally outperforms G-SchNet (98.15%), and both generalize well with minimal train-test gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

Symphony extends the autoregressive 3D molecule generation line (G-SchNet, G-SphereNet) by replacing rotationally invariant features with higher-degree E(3)-equivariant features from NequIP and a generalized E3SchNet. The key architectural departure is using spherical harmonic projections to parameterize the position distribution from a single focus atom, eliminating the triangulation procedure and auxiliary symmetry-breaking tokens required by prior autoregressive models. The spherical harmonic parameterization draws on Cohen & Welling (2015) for harmonic exponential families, while the equivariant message-passing backbone follows the NequIP/E(3)-GNN framework of Batzner et al. (2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Symphony demonstrates that higher-degree equivariant features enable a clean, single-atom-centered parametrization of 3D position distributions, offering a practical middle ground between the speed of autoregressive models and the quality of diffusion models. The bispectrum-based angular accuracy metric and the multi-channel spherical harmonic trick to bypass angular frequency limits are reusable tools. However, the coarse radial discretization (64 bins over 0.9–2.0 Å) remains a clear limitation for bond length accuracy, and the increased tensor-product cost limits scalability. Open directions include replacing discretization with normalizing flows for the radial component and placing entire local environment motifs simultaneously to speed up generation.
