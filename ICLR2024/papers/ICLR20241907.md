## Problem

Pipeline parallelism (PP) is essential for training large models across many GPUs, but pipeline bubbles—idle time caused by inter-stage dependencies—have been considered unavoidable under synchronous training semantics. Existing schedules like 1F1B and interleaved 1F1B reduce but never eliminate these bubbles, leaving significant GPU idle time that limits throughput. This paper asks whether zero pipeline bubbles can be achieved while preserving exact synchronous optimization semantics.

## Input / Output

The scheduling algorithm consumes profiled per-pass execution times (T_F, T_B, T_W, T_comm) measured on the target hardware, along with the pipeline stage count p, microbatch count m, and an activation memory budget M_limit. It outputs a concrete per-stage execution plan specifying the exact ordering and timing of every F, B, and W pass across all microbatches. The plan is validated for bit-to-bit numerical equivalence with standard 1F1B training, ensuring the schedule changes do not alter the optimization trajectory.

## Method

The key insight is splitting the backward pass into two independent computations: B (input gradient) and W (parameter gradient), which breaks the sequential dependency that previously forced B at stage i−1 to wait for W at stage i. Handcrafted schedules (ZB-H1 for memory-matched, ZB-H2 for zero-bubble) exploit this freedom to place W passes into previously idle slots, reducing bubble size to a third or eliminating it entirely. For realistic settings where T_F ≠ T_B ≠ T_W and communication is non-negligible, a heuristic algorithm (with optional ILP refinement) automatically searches the schedule space under the memory constraint. To truly achieve zero bubble, a post-validation strategy replaces the all-reduce synchronization at the optimizer step: each stage proceeds optimistically using a partially reduced global state, and a rollback is issued only if the fully reduced state (propagated back during the next iteration's warm-up) reveals a violation such as NaN or gradient-norm clipping.

## Results

ZB-2p (zero-bubble schedule with 2pM_B memory) achieves the highest throughput across all model sizes, reaching 14.9 samples/GPU/s on 1.5B (8 GPUs, 64 microbatches) and 1.00 on 28.3B (32 GPUs, 256 microbatches), outperforming 1F1B by 9–14% and 1F1B-I by 5–11%. ZB-1p (memory-matched to 1F1B at pM_B) delivers 4–9% throughput gains over 1F1B at comparable memory. Bubble rates drop from 11–26% (1F1B) to below 1% for ZB-2p (e.g., 0.26% at 1.5B/64 microbatches, 0.18% at 28.3B/256 microbatches), confirming near-zero-bubble execution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 1F1B (the standard synchronous pipeline schedule from Megatron-LM), ZB-2p wins on throughput in every tested configuration, with gains ranging from ~9% (1.5B, 64 microbatches: 14.9 vs 13.6) to ~14% (28.3B, 256 microbatches: 1.00 vs 0.88), at roughly 2× the activation memory. ZB-1p, which matches 1F1B's peak memory (pM_B), still achieves 4–9% throughput improvement, confirming that the backward-splitting alone reduces bubbles even without extra memory. Against interleaved 1F1B (1F1B-I), ZB-2p wins by 5–11% across all settings, and ZB-1p is comparable to 1F1B-I on single-node (8 GPU) setups but clearly outperforms it on multi-node (16/32 GPU) configurations where inter-node communication bandwidth is the bottleneck. The paper further shows (Appendix F) that under identical memory budgets, ZB-2p with half the microbatch size still beats 1F1B with full microbatch size by 18–22%, and under small-microbatch settings (m ≤ p, Appendix H) the advantage extends to 20–33%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 1F1B pipeline schedule lineage (PipeDream → Megatron-LM interleaved) by introducing a finer-grained decomposition of the backward pass into input-gradient (B) and parameter-gradient (W) computations, which breaks the sequential dependency that previously made bubbles appear unavoidable under synchronous semantics. It departs from GPipe's activation-recomputation approach and from asynchronous methods (PipeDream, PipeMare) that sacrifice exact optimization semantics, instead achieving zero bubbles while preserving synchronous training. The automatic scheduling algorithm (heuristic + ILP) generalizes the handcrafted ZB-H1/ZB-H2 schedules to realistic, non-uniform execution times and communication costs, building on the Megatron-LM implementation as its engineering substrate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving near-zero pipeline bubbles under synchronous semantics removes what has been considered the fundamental efficiency ceiling of PP, yielding 15–30% throughput gains that are orthogonal to and composable with DP, TP, and ZeRO. The practical caveat is that the zero-bubble schedule (ZB-2p) requires roughly 2× the activation memory of 1F1B, which may be prohibitive on memory-constrained hardware; the paper argues ZeRO/TP can absorb this overhead, but this interaction is not exhaustively validated. Open questions include how the post-validation optimizer mechanism behaves under adversarial or numerically unstable training regimes, and whether the ILP formulation scales beyond the problem sizes tested (up to 32 stages × 256 microbatches).
