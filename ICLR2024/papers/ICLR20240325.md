## Problem

Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DMs) implicitly capture modular generative attributes for faithful image synthesis, but their formulation lacks explicit encoders to extract these attributes as compact, disentangled feature vectors. Existing diffusion-based representation methods (PDAE, Diff-AE) learn a single time-step-independent feature that entangles all attributes, failing to exploit the natural correspondence between diffusion time-steps and attribute granularity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240325/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean RGB image (no noise) as input and produces a 512-dimensional feature vector partitioned into k disjoint subsets (k ≤ T, where T=1000 diffusion steps). Each subset z_i is designed to encode the modular attribute lost at diffusion time-step i. For counterfactual generation, only a specific subset is interpolated or manipulated while the complement is held fixed, yielding an edited image that changes one attribute without affecting others.

## Method

DiTi exploits a theoretical result showing that in the forward diffusion process, fine-grained attributes (small pixel-level changes) are lost at earlier time-steps while coarse-grained attributes are lost later, with the loss degree monotonically increasing as the overlapping coefficient of noisy sample distributions grows. The encoder f maps x_0 to a d-dimensional feature z, which is partitioned into T disjoint subsets {z_i}. At each time-step t, a masked feature z̄_t = [z_1,...,z_t, 0,...,0] is fed to a decoder g that compensates for the frozen pre-trained DM U-Net's reconstruction error, so that z_t is forced to capture only the attribute newly lost at step t. This inductive bias—unlike PDAE which uses the full z at all time-steps—prevents information leakage across time-steps and enforces modularity, yielding a disentangled representation validated by improved attribute classification and artifact-free counterfactual generation.

## Results

On CelebA, DiTi achieves 62.3% AP for attribute classification, 0.617 Pearson's r, and 0.392 MSE for LFW attribute regression, outperforming the closest baseline PDAE (60.2% AP, 0.596 r, 0.410 MSE) by 2.1 AP points. On FFHQ, DiTi reaches 61.4% AP, 0.622 Pearson's r, and 0.384 MSE, again leading all baselines. DiTi also enables faithful counterfactual generation by interpolating only a single feature subset (e.g., 16 of 512 dimensions) without affecting other attributes, a property that none of the baselines exhibit.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiTi outperforms all five baselines on every metric across both CelebA and FFHQ. Against the closest competitor PDAE (Zhang et al., NeurIPS 2022), DiTi gains +2.1 AP on CelebA and +1.7 AP on FFHQ, with the paper attributing the gap to PDAE's use of a time-step-independent feature that leaks information across time-steps and encourages spurious correlations. Diff-AE (Preechakul et al., CVPR 2022) is similarly outperformed by +2.0 AP on CelebA and +0.9 AP on FFHQ. SimCLR (Chen et al., ICML 2020) trails DiTi by +2.6 AP on CelebA and +0.6 AP on FFHQ, largely because its augmentation-invariance objective discards attributes like hair and skin color. The traditional disentanglement methods β-TCVAE (Chen et al., NeurIPS 2018) and IB-GAN (Jeon et al., AAAI 2021) are far behind, achieving only 45.0% and 44.2% AP on CelebA respectively, confirming that information-bottleneck approaches without explicit inductive bias struggle on real-world data.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiTi extends the diffusion-based representation learning line initiated by Diff-AE and PDAE, which learn a single time-step-independent feature to compensate for a frozen DM's reconstruction error. Its key departure is a theoretical argument that the forward diffusion process loses fine-grained attributes (e.g., texture) at early time-steps and coarse-grained ones (e.g., shape) at later time-steps, motivating a time-step-specific partition of the feature vector where only the first t subsets are active at time-step t. This inductive bias replaces the information-bottleneck objective used by classical disentanglement methods (β-TCVAE, IB-GAN) and the unstructured full-feature compensation used by PDAE, grounding modularity in the diffusion schedule itself rather than in a learned regularizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, provable link between diffusion time-steps and attribute granularity that can serve as a general inductive bias for unsupervised disentanglement, and the counterfactual generation results (editing one attribute subset without disturbing others) offer strong qualitative evidence of modularity. However, the numerical gains over PDAE are modest (~2 AP points), some fine-grained attributes (e.g., earrings) remain poorly disentangled, and the method inherits the quality ceiling of the frozen DM. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more expressive backbones (e.g., Stable Diffusion), whether text prompts can provide an additional disentangled template, and whether the current optimization (detaching prior subsets) can be made to converge faster without sacrificing modularity.
