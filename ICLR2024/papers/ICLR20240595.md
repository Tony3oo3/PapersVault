## Problem

Most existing 3D generative models are optimized for a single category or a small number of categories, making them unable to generalize across the vast diversity of real-world objects. Large-vocabulary 3D generation faces three compounding challenges: the need for an expressive yet efficient 3D representation, the wide geometric and textural diversity across hundreds of categories, and the complex appearances of real-world objects that are difficult to capture with category-specific models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240595/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a random noise tensor and a category label as input and produces a 3D object with both geometry and photorealistic RGB texture. The generation is a two-step feed-forward pipeline: a trained diffusion model denoises the latent noise into triplane features (three axis-aligned orthogonal planes at 128×128 resolution), and a shared category-independent triplane decoder then renders the final 3D content via volume rendering. The triplane fitting stage uses foreground-only sampling with a foreground-background mask to accelerate convergence while preserving background accuracy.

## Method

DiffTF introduces a 3D-aware transformer that explicitly separates generalized 3D prior knowledge from specialized category-specific features. A 3D-aware encoder/decoder first applies cross-plane attention (using the target plane as query and the full triplane as key/value) with shared weights to extract plane-independent 3D relations before the main transformer. The 3D-aware transformer then operates in two branches: a self-attention branch that builds interdependencies within individual planes to capture specialized features, and a cross-plane attention branch (again with shared weights) that aggregates generalized 3D knowledge across all three planes. The triplane fitting is further stabilized with total variation regularization, L2 normalization, and foreground-biased sampling to keep triplane values in a consistent range across diverse categories, enabling a single shared decoder to handle over 200 object types.

## Results

On OmniObject3D (216 real-world categories), DiffTF achieves FID 25.36, KID 0.8%, COV 43.57%, and MMD 6.64‰, outperforming all baselines by a large margin on every metric. On ShapeNet single-category generation, DiffTF achieves FID 36.68 (Car), 14.46 (Plane), and 35.16 (Chair), again leading all competitors. The largest gains are in FID (e.g., 25.36 vs. 122.40 for NFD) and MMD (6.64 vs. 10.92 for NFD), indicating substantially better geometric fidelity and diversity coverage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffTF dominates all four baselines on every metric in both OmniObject3D and ShapeNet evaluations. Against the GAN-based EG3D, DiffTF reduces FID from 41.56 to 25.36 (−39%) and MMD from 28.21 to 6.64 (−76%) on OmniObject3D, while also improving COV from 14.14% to 43.57%. Against GET3D, the FID gap is even wider (49.41 → 25.36), and DiffTF nearly doubles COV (24.42% → 43.57%). Against the diffusion-based NFD w/ texture, DiffTF cuts FID by nearly half (122.40 → 25.36) and MMD by 39% (10.92 → 6.64), demonstrating that the 3D-aware transformer's global cross-plane attention substantially outperforms NFD's 2D CNN diffusion. Against DiffRF (voxel-based 3D CNN diffusion), DiffTF achieves a 5.8× lower FID (147.59 → 25.36), attributed to the higher-resolution triplane representation. On ShapeNet single-category tasks, DiffTF wins on all three categories (Car, Plane, Chair), with the most dramatic gain on Plane (FID 14.46 vs. 26.80 for GET3D) and a narrow but consistent lead on Chair (35.16 vs. 35.33).

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffTF builds directly on EG3D's triplane representation as its 3D encoding, but departs from the GAN-based generation paradigm by adopting a diffusion model as the generative backbone. It extends NFD, which applied 2D CNN diffusion to flattened triplane features, by introducing a 3D-aware transformer with shared cross-plane attention that explicitly models inter-plane dependencies. The key conceptual departure from prior work (including Rodin's local 3D CNN awareness) is the decomposition of 3D object features into generalized 3D prior knowledge (extracted globally across planes via cross-plane attention) and specialized per-plane features (captured by self-attention), enabling a single model to handle 200+ diverse categories rather than a handful.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is one of the first single-model frameworks to achieve competitive 3D generation across 200+ real-world categories, demonstrating that a unified architecture can handle the geometric and textural diversity of objects like pine cones, conches, and dinosaurs without per-category tuning. The generalized/specialized feature decomposition offers a reusable architectural principle for any multi-category generative task. However, the triplane fitting bottleneck (~3 minutes per object) limits practical scaling to million-object datasets, and the authors acknowledge that the noise scheduling for triplane diffusion remains underexplored, leaving room for further quality improvements especially in fine geometric detail.
