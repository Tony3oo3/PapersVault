## Problem

Diffusion-based offline RL methods achieve strong performance by modeling heterogeneous behavior distributions, but their inference requires 5–100 iterative denoising steps per action, making evaluation slow and impractical for computation-sensitive domains like robotics. The paper asks whether the generative power of diffusion models can be fully exploited for behavior regularization without ever sampling actions from the diffusion process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240404/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a static offline dataset of (s, a, r, s') tuples and, at evaluation time, takes only a single environment state to produce one deterministic action via a 2-layer MLP. No iterative denoising or parallel candidate generation is needed at inference; the entire diffusion model is used solely as a frozen score estimator during training. The policy network is a simple Dirac (deterministic) actor, in contrast to the stochastic diffusion policies used by competing methods.

## Method

SRPO's key insight is that the gradient of the reverse-KL behavior-regularization term in policy optimization equals the score function ∇_a log μ(a|s) of the behavior distribution, which a pretrained diffusion model approximates via its noise-prediction network ε_ψ(a_t|s,t)/σ_t. Rather than sampling fake actions from the behavior model to estimate policy–behavior divergence, SRPO directly injects this score into the policy gradient, regularizing at the gradient level instead of the loss level. A surrogate objective ensembles score estimates across multiple diffusion times t∈(0.02,0.98) with a weighting ω(t)=σ_t² (inspired by DreamFusion's score distillation), and subtracts the noise ε as a variance-reducing baseline. The full pipeline combines IQL expectile-regression critics, a continuous-time conditional diffusion behavior model trained for 2M steps, and a deterministic MLP policy trained for 1M steps, yielding a 25–1000× speedup in action sampling over diffusion-based baselines while matching their D4RL performance.

## Results

On D4RL Locomotion tasks, SRPO achieves an average normalized score of 87.1, competitive with the best diffusion-based method Diffusion-QL (88.0) while using a simple deterministic (Dirac) inference policy. On AntMaze tasks, SRPO scores 73.6, trailing IDQL (79.1) and QGPO (78.3) but surpassing Diffusion-QL (69.6). The headline efficiency gain is a 25–1000× speedup in action sampling and less than 1% of the computational FLOPS required by diffusion-based baselines during evaluation, since SRPO entirely avoids iterative diffusion sampling at inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Gaussian/Dirac-policy baselines (BEAR, TD3+BC, IQL), SRPO wins by large margins on nearly every task: e.g., on Medium Hopper it scores 95.5 vs. IQL's 66.3 and TD3+BC's 59.3, and on Medium-Replay Hopper it reaches 101.2 vs. IQL's 94.7. Among diffusion-based methods, SRPO is most competitive with Diffusion-QL, trailing by only 0.9 points on the Locomotion average (87.1 vs. 88.0) while being 25–1000× faster at inference; on AntMaze, SRPO (73.6) actually beats Diffusion-QL (69.6). SRPO outperforms IDQL on Locomotion (87.1 vs. 82.1) despite sharing an identical critic and behavior-model architecture, but falls short on AntMaze (73.6 vs. 79.1), where IDQL's select-from-behavior-candidates strategy proves more effective. Compared to QGPO, SRPO edges ahead on Locomotion (87.1 vs. 86.6) but lags on AntMaze (73.6 vs. 78.3). Overall, SRPO matches or approaches state-of-the-art diffusion-based performance while using a single forward pass through a small MLP at evaluation, making it uniquely suited for high-frequency control and robotics deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

SRPO sits at the intersection of behavior-regularized offline RL (Wu et al., 2019; Kumar et al., 2019) and score-based generative modeling (Song et al., 2021), but departs from the dominant paradigm of using diffusion models as the policy itself (Diffusion-QL, IDQL, QGPO). Instead of sampling actions from a diffusion policy, SRPO repurposes a pretrained diffusion behavior model purely as a score function estimator that regularizes the gradient of a simple deterministic actor, drawing conceptual inspiration from score distillation in text-to-3D (DreamFusion, Poole et al., 2023). The critic training pipeline is borrowed directly from IQL (Kostrikov et al., 2022), and the reverse-KL policy extraction objective follows the behavior-regularized optimization framework of Wu et al. (2019), but the key novelty is performing regularization at the gradient level rather than the loss level, eliminating the need for iterative diffusion sampling entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 25×–1000× inference speedup makes diffusion-quality behavior regularization practical for computation-sensitive domains like robotics and high-frequency control, where iterative diffusion sampling is prohibitive. The conceptual insight—that a pretrained diffusion model's score function can substitute for explicit sampling in policy regularization—opens a path to combining expressive generative models with lightweight deterministic policies. However, performance on AntMaze tasks remains noticeably below diffusion-based methods (e.g., 73.6 vs. 79.1 average), and the per-task sensitivity of the temperature coefficient β limits plug-and-play applicability. Open questions include whether the deterministic policy assumption is always appropriate for highly multimodal environments and whether the approach extends to discrete or hierarchical action spaces.
