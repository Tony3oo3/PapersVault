## Problem

Existing non-transferable learning (NTL) methods rely on relaxing statistical dependence between source and target domain representations, but they cannot distinguish between content and style factors in the data. This causes them to inadvertently fit spurious correlations between styles and labels in the source domain and model fake independence between contents and labels in the target domain, making learned representations fragile to natural distribution shifts and vulnerable to adversarial interventions such as domain masquerade attacks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241082/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images from both source and target domains at 32×32 or 64×64 resolution (up to 224×224 for high-resolution experiments) and outputs class labels. The key constraint is that the model must simultaneously maintain high accuracy on the source domain while deliberately degrading to near-random performance on the target domain. For source-only NTL, the target domain is synthesized by applying style augmentations (e.g., blurring, solarize, color shifts) to source images, effectively performing do(S) interventions that change style without altering content.

## Method

H-NTL introduces a causal model that decomposes each instance into a content factor C (the intrinsic cause of the label Y) and a style factor S (the cause of domain D), with their co-occurrence modeled as statistical dependence from latent confounders. A variational inference framework (disentanglement VAE) is trained by maximizing the ELBO of P(X,Y,D) to separately infer C and S from observable data, using dedicated encoders, a decoder, and classifiers for label and domain prediction. The NTL student network is then trained via dual-path knowledge distillation: the source-domain path constrains representations to fit content factors (preserving classification ability), while the target-domain path forces representations to fit style factors (which barely predict labels), achieving optimal untransferability. For source-only NTL, style augmentations from RandAugment serve as causal interventions on S, replacing the GAN-based synthesis used in prior work.

## Results

On target-specified NTL (Table 1), H-NTL maintains source domain accuracy within 0–1.2% of supervised learning across all 8 dataset/resolution settings, while degrading target domain accuracy by 14–89% relative drop. On source-only NTL (Table 2), H-NTL similarly preserves source performance (≤3.1% drop) and achieves 14–86% target domain degradation. On the most challenging low-resolution settings (C10→S10 and OP→OC at 32×32), tNTL fails to degrade target performance meaningfully (only 19–33% drop), whereas H-NTL still achieves 54–77% target degradation while keeping source accuracy near SL levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is tNTL/sNTL from Wang et al. (2022b), which relaxes statistical dependence via KL divergence and MMD terms. H-NTL wins on the key metric of source–target performance difference across all 8 target-specified and 8 source-only settings, consistently achieving a larger gap between maintained source accuracy and degraded target accuracy. On source domain maintenance, H-NTL is superior: its relative source drop is ≤1.2% (often negative, i.e., slightly above SL) versus tNTL's 0.05–8.89%. On target domain degradation, tNTL occasionally achieves a slightly larger drop (e.g., 91.31% vs. 89.84% on MM→MT), but this comes at the cost of a 6.33% source accuracy loss. Critically, on the hardest low-resolution settings (C10→S10 and OP→OC at 32×32), tNTL's target degradation collapses to 19–33% while H-NTL still achieves 54–77%, demonstrating that the causal disentanglement approach is far more robust when content and style are tightly entangled.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the non-transferable learning (NTL) framework introduced by Wang et al. (2022), which relied on statistical dependence relaxation (KL divergence and MMD) to resist knowledge transfer. H-NTL departs from this statistical paradigm by introducing a causal model that explicitly separates content (the intrinsic cause of labels) from style (the cause of domain), then uses a VAE-based disentanglement framework and dual-path knowledge distillation to guide the NTL student network. The causal representation learning literature (invariant causal mechanisms, partial disentanglement for domain adaptation) provides the broader intellectual context, but H-NTL inverts the usual goal: instead of learning invariant representations across domains, it learns representations that are deliberately variant between source and target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete failure mode of statistical NTL methods—fitting spurious style-label correlations and fake content-label independence—that makes them fragile under distribution shifts and vulnerable to domain masquerade attacks. The causal framing provides a principled definition of 'optimal untransferability' (N_t ⊥ N_s and N_t ⊥ Y) that goes beyond ad-hoc regularization. Open questions include whether the VAE-based disentanglement generalizes to more complex entanglement structures, whether the weak-relation assumption between content and style holds in highly correlated domains, and whether the approach extends beyond image classification to other modalities. The practical impact is primarily in IP protection (ownership verification, applicability authorization), a niche but commercially relevant area.
