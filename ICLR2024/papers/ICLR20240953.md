## Problem

Despite strong empirical performance on irregular time series, Neural Controlled Differential Equations (NCDEs) lacked any statistical analysis of their learning performance. Specifically, no generalization bound, no decomposition of approximation error, and no quantification of how sampling coarseness affects prediction quality had been established for this class of models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multivariate time series x^D ∈ R^{d×(K+1)} sampled on an arbitrary but fixed grid D = {t_0=0, ..., t_K=1} shared across all individuals, with paths assumed L_x-Lipschitz. Discrete observations are embedded into continuous paths via fill-forward (piecewise-constant) interpolation, which drives the CDE. The output is a scalar prediction obtained by a linear readout Φ^⊤ z_1 of the terminal latent state, suitable for both classification and regression.

## Method

The paper derives a sampling-dependent generalization bound for NCDEs by proving Lipschitz continuity of the predictor with respect to all parameters (vector field weights, biases, initialization, and readout), then bounding the covering number and Rademacher complexity of the function class. The key technical tool is a continuity-of-flow theorem for CDEs, which decomposes the difference between two CDE terminal states into contributions from differing vector fields, initial conditions, and driving paths. This decomposition yields a precise total-risk bound split into a worst-case generalization term, a discretization bias proportional to the maximal sampling gap |D|, and an approximation bias that separates into vector-field approximation error and initial-condition approximation error—allowing classical feed-forward neural network approximation results to transfer directly to NCDEs.

## Results

The paper's main contribution is a generalization bound (Theorem 4.1) showing that with probability at least 1−δ, the generalization error of an NCDE is upper bounded by a term scaling as O(1/√n) times a complexity factor depending on hidden dimension p, depth q, and discretization-dependent constants, plus a confidence term M_ℓ√(log(1/δ)/2n). The total risk bound (Theorem 5.4) further decomposes the error into a worst-case Rademacher term, a discretization bias linear in the sampling gap |D|, and an approximation bias that separates into vector-field and initial-condition approximation errors. Empirically, on a fractional Brownian motion classification task (H=0.4 vs H=0.6), the generalization error (binary cross-entropy) is shown to correlate clearly with both the sampling gap |D| and the average maximal path variation, validating the theoretical dependence on discretization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper that does not benchmark NCDEs against competing architectures (RNNs, LSTMs, GRU-ODE, etc.) on standard datasets. Instead, it positions its contributions relative to prior theoretical work. Compared to Bartlett et al. (2017), Chen et al. (2020a), and Marion (2023), this paper is the first to derive a generalization bound specifically for NCDEs (sequential data with irregular sampling) and the first to provide a non-vacuous approximation error decomposition. Unlike Fermanian et al. (2021), which requires restrictive regularity conditions enabling linearization in signature space, the present bound applies to general Lipschitz paths. The discretization bias term (linear in |D|) is a novel contribution absent from all prior works listed in Table 1, which either ignore sampling effects (static data) or do not decompose the approximation error. The empirical validation (Figure 4) confirms the predicted |D|-dependence but does not provide head-to-head numerical comparisons with other time-series models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Lipschitz-based generalization bound framework of Bartlett et al. (2017) and Chen et al. (2020a) from static and recurrent architectures to Neural Controlled Differential Equations, a model class introduced by Kidger et al. (2020) for irregular time series. It departs from Fermanian et al. (2021), which obtained NCDE generalization bounds only under restrictive regularity conditions enabling linearization in signature space, and from Marion (2023), which analyzed neural ODEs and ResNets but not the CDE-driven setting. The key technical novelty is combining the continuity of the CDE flow (a tool from rough path theory) with covering-number/Rademacher complexity arguments to obtain both a generalization bound and a decomposition of the approximation bias into vector-field and initial-condition terms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first statistical learning theory for NCDEs, providing practitioners with a theoretical handle on how sampling coarseness (the mesh size |D|) degrades both generalization and approximation. The linear-in-|D| discretization bias and the decomposition of approximation error into tractable neural network approximation sub-problems open a path to transferring classical NN approximation results (e.g., Berner et al., 2021) to the NCDE setting, which the authors leave as future work. Limitations include the reliance on bounded parameter spaces, the use of fill-forward interpolation (rather than splines as in practice), and validation only on synthetic fractional Brownian motion data, leaving open whether the theoretical scaling predictions hold on real-world irregular time series.
