## Problem

Bilevel optimization with nonconvex and/or constrained lower-level problems lacks both a clear landscape characterization and tractable first-order algorithms. While penalty methods convert bilevel problems into single-level formulations, the relationship between the penalty hyper-objective ψ_σ(x) and the true hyper-objective ψ(x) has remained elusive when the lower-level solution set is non-unique or the feasible set is constrained. Moreover, no first-order algorithm was known to reach a stationary point of ψ(x) under Polyak-Łojasiewicz-type conditions alone, as opposed to the stronger strong-convexity assumptions used in prior work.

## Input / Output

The method consumes smooth objectives f(x,y) and g(x,y) over closed convex sets X⊆R^{d_x} and Y⊆R^{d_y} (with Y bounded and expressible via at most m₁ convex inequality and m₂ linear equality constraints), accessed only through unbiased stochastic gradient oracles with bounded variance. It outputs a saddle point (x*, y*, z*) of the proximal-smoothed formulation, where y* and z* are proximal solutions of the perturbed and original lower-level problems respectively, and x* is an ε-stationary point of ψ_σ(x) with σ=O(ε). Under additional local regularity (LICQ, strict complementarity, Hessian-Lipschitzness along the solution path), x* is also an O(ε)-stationary point of the original hyper-objective ψ(x).

## Method

The paper introduces a proximal error-bound (EB) condition on the σ-perturbed lower-level objective h_σ(x,y)=σf(x,y)+g(x,y), which guarantees Lipschitz continuity of solution sets in a neighborhood of the optimum and serves as the key regularity assumption. Under this condition, the authors prove that |ψ_σ(x)−ψ(x)|=O(σ/μ) and ‖∇ψ_σ(x)−∇ψ(x)‖=O(σ/μ³), establishing the penalty formulation as a valid O(σ)-approximation of the original problem. To optimize ψ_σ(x) without explicit implicit gradients, they reformulate it as a saddle-point problem and apply Moreau envelope smoothing (with parameter ρ≪1/l_{g,1}) to make the inner minimization strongly convex, enabling stable proximal-operation chasing via gradient steps. The double-loop algorithm uses T_k inner iterations and M_k batch sizes that grow polynomially in k, with σ_k=k^{−1/3} decreasing gradually, achieving O(ε^{−7}) oracle complexity. A fully single-loop variant leverages momentum-assisted gradient estimators under a mean-squared smoothness condition on the stochastic oracles, reducing complexity to O(ε^{−5}) with only O(1) samples per iteration.

## Results

The paper establishes that under the proximal error-bound (EB) condition, the penalty hyper-objective ψ_σ(x) is O(σ/μ)-close in value and O(σ/μ³)-close in gradient to the original hyper-objective ψ(x). For the double-loop algorithm (Algorithm 1), the oracle complexity to find an ε-stationary point is O(ε⁻⁷) (fully stochastic), O(ε⁻⁵) (upper-level stochastic only), and O(ε⁻³) (deterministic). The fully single-loop momentum-assisted algorithm (Algorithm 2) improves the fully-stochastic complexity to O(ε⁻⁵) with O(1) samples per iteration, achieving a convergence rate of ‖∇ψ_ε(x_R)‖ ≍ log(K)/K^{1/5}.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are made against prior complexity bounds in the literature. A direct plug-in of existing min-max optimization methods (e.g., Yang et al. 2022) under the PL condition yields an oracle complexity of O(σ⁻⁴ε⁻⁴) = O(ε⁻⁸) when σ = O(ε); this paper improves that to O(ε⁻⁷) (double-loop) and O(ε⁻⁵) (single-loop) by exploiting the specific saddle-point structure of the penalty formulation and the local nature of the proximal-EB condition. In the special case of unconstrained strongly-convex lower-level problems with deterministic oracles, Chen et al. (2023a) achieves O(ε⁻²), which is tighter than this paper's O(ε⁻³) deterministic bound, but that result does not extend to nonconvex or constrained lower-level problems. The paper's key advantage over prior penalty-method analyses (Shen & Chen 2023; Lu & Mei 2023) is that it provides a global O(σ) landscape connection between ψ_σ and ψ (not just local neighborhoods of minimizers) and delivers explicit stochastic oracle-complexity bounds for both double-loop and single-loop implementations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the penalty-method framework for bilevel optimization from classical asymptotic analyses (White & Anandalingam 1993; Ye et al. 1997) and the closely related deterministic double-loop study of Shen & Chen (2023) to a global, non-asymptotic, stochastic setting. It departs from the dominant implicit-gradient-descent line (Ghadimi & Wang 2018; Chen et al. 2021; Hong et al. 2023), which requires strong convexity and unconstrained lower-level problems, by replacing the PL condition with a more general proximal error-bound (EB) assumption suited to constrained and nonconvex lower-level objectives. The saddle-point reformulation draws on the min-max optimization literature (Yang et al. 2022), but the paper's key algorithmic novelty lies in the proximal-envelope smoothing and momentum-assisted single-loop design that avoid the O(σ⁻⁴) overhead of naive plug-in min-max solvers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first explicit formula for ∇ψ(x) when the lower-level problem admits multiple solutions under mild regularity (LICQ + strict complementarity), filling a gap that had only conjectures in the literature. The global O(σ) landscape connection between the penalty and original hyper-objectives under proximal-EB (rather than just local neighborhoods or PL) gives a principled justification for penalty-based BO algorithms in the nonconvex constrained regime. Open questions include whether the O(ε⁻⁷)/O(ε⁻⁵) oracle complexities are tight, how to handle x-dependent lower-level constraints (which require estimating Lagrangian multipliers), and whether the analysis extends to merely convex (not strongly convex) lower-level objectives or nonsmooth settings.
