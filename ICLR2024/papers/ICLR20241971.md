## Problem

Existing methods for clustering neural spiking activity either assume static linear dynamics over the entire recording (e.g., mixPLDS, mixDPFA) or model temporal state changes without simultaneously discovering spatial neuron populations. No prior framework jointly infers both the spatial (which neurons form a functional population) and temporal (which periods share similar dynamics) clustering structures while allowing for non-stationary, interacting latent dynamics across populations over elongated recordings.

## Input / Output

The method takes binned spiking counts (non-negative integers) from N neurons across multiple anatomical regions over T time bins, with over-dispersion handled via a negative-binomial observation model. It produces two sets of cluster assignments—spatial (subject) labels for neurons and temporal (state) labels for time bins—along with per-cluster latent trajectories and per-state linear dynamics parameters (transition matrix, bias, noise covariance). All neurons are assumed to switch states synchronously, so the state sequence is shared across populations, and the latent dimension p is currently fixed across clusters.

## Method

The model defines each neural population with private low-dimensional latent trajectories (MFMM prior on the number of populations) while stacking all populations' latent factors into a joint vector that evolves under state-dependent linear dynamics (sticky-HDP-HMM prior on the number of states), thereby capturing inter-population interactions and non-stationary dynamics. The negative-binomial observation equation is handled via Pólya-Gamma data augmentation, which transforms the counting likelihood into a Gaussian form amenable to forward-filtering-backward-sampling (FFBS) for latent factor updates. Subject cluster assignments are sampled with a partition-based algorithm using a Laplace-approximated marginalized likelihood (integrating out per-neuron loadings) to ensure good mixing in high-dimensional settings, while state assignments use a weak-limit Gibbs sampler for the sticky-HDP-HMM. The entire posterior is explored with a four-step MCMC cycle: latent factors → subject-specific observation parameters → states and dynamics → subject cluster labels.

## Results

In simulation (N=30 neurons, T=500, p=2), the NB bi-clustering model successfully recovers the true 3 subject clusters and 2 state clusters, with high posterior similarity within true groups and accurate latent trajectory estimation. On the Allen Institute Neuropixels application (78 neurons from CA1, LP, and VISp across 3 visual-stimulus epochs, T=720 bins), the NB bi-clustering model detects approximately 10 subject clusters and 13 state clusters. The largest maxPEAR subject cluster contains 21 neurons (9 from CA1, 7 from LP, 5 from VISp), revealing cross-regional functional interactions not captured by anatomy alone. The Poisson variant and mixDPFA both produce noisier, less anatomically consistent clustering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three approaches on the Neuropixels multi-regional data: the proposed NB bi-clustering model, a Poisson bi-clustering variant (same architecture but assuming equi-dispersion), and mixDPFA (Wei et al., 2023, which assumes Poisson spikes and static linear dynamics with no state switching). The NB bi-clustering model produces the most anatomically consistent subject clusters, with the largest cluster predominantly CA1 neurons and clear groupings by brain region, while also revealing cross-regional functional interactions (e.g., CA1–VISp and LP–VISp co-clustering). The Poisson bi-clustering model shows more mismatches between detected clusters and anatomical labels, suggesting spurious interactions arise from ignoring over-dispersion. mixDPFA performs worst, producing noisier similarity matrices that can hardly distinguish between VISp and LP neurons, consistent with the paper's argument that static dynamics assumptions bias population detection under changing experimental conditions. In simulation, the Poisson variant also recovers the true clustering structure (since over-dispersion is mild with r_i=10) but shows bias in latent trajectory estimation, whereas the NB model provides unbiased estimates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work on neural spiking data: spatial clustering of neurons via private latent trajectories (mixPLDS, mixDPFA) and temporal state detection via switching linear dynamical systems (SLDS, RSLDS). It builds on the multi-population interacting dynamics framework of Glaser et al. (2020b) and wraps both clustering dimensions in nonparametric priors—MFMM for the finite-but-unknown number of neural populations and sticky-HDP-HMM for the potentially unbounded number of temporal states. The key departure from mixDPFA is the simultaneous, joint inference of both spatial and temporal cluster structure, along with a negative-binomial observation model (via Pólya-Gamma augmentation) to handle over-dispersion that Poisson-based methods ignore.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that ignoring either the spatial or temporal clustering dimension leads to biased latent trajectory estimates and spurious cluster assignments, providing a principled argument for joint bi-clustering in large-scale neural recordings. Open questions include the geometric state-duration limitation of sticky-HDP-HMM (states switch too rapidly in practice), the fixed latent dimension assumption, and scalability—MCMC is computationally expensive, and the authors note variational inference or HDP-HSMM variants as natural next steps. The method is framed as potentially applicable beyond neuroscience to any large-scale counting time series with elongated recording periods, though no such application is demonstrated.
