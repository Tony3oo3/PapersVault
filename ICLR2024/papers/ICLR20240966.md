## Problem

While over-parameterization is widely used in practice because the true rank is unknown, its effect on gradient descent convergence for matrix sensing was only empirically observed and upper-bounded, with no rigorous lower bound establishing the slowdown. This paper proves that symmetric over-parameterization (k > r) causes an exponential-to-polynomial degradation in convergence rate (from exp(-Ω(T)) to Ω(1/T²)), and identifies the mechanism—redundant space in the factor matrix that decays sublinearly—along with a surprising remedy via asymmetric parameterization.

## Input / Output

The method takes m linear measurements y_i = ⟨A_i, M*⟩ of an unknown rank-r matrix M* (with measurement matrices satisfying RIP of order 2k+1) and a random Gaussian initialization of factor matrices at scale α. It produces the recovered matrix (XX^T or FG^T) with explicit convergence rate guarantees: the rate is Ω(1/T²) for symmetric over-parameterization, exp(-Ω(T)) for asymmetric exact-parameterization, and exp(-Ω(α²T)) for asymmetric over-parameterization, where the initialization scale α directly controls the linear convergence constant in the over-parameterized case.

## Method

The paper establishes an Ω(1/T²) lower bound for symmetric over-parameterized matrix factorization by constructing a potential function on the redundant (zero-eigenvalue) rows of the factor matrix and showing it decays at most as 1/T. For the asymmetric setting, the key technical insight is that the imbalance matrix Δ_t = F_t^T F_t - G_t^T G_t drives linear convergence: when Δ_t is positive definite, the redundant component K_t shrinks linearly, yielding a rate of exp(-Ω(α²T)) where α is the initialization scale. The paper then proposes a simple one-step algorithm that, upon reaching a neighborhood of the optimum, applies a singular-value-based transformation (scaling F by Σ_inv and G by Σ_inv^{-1}) to amplify the F-G imbalance from O(α) to O(σ_r), recovering an α-independent linear convergence rate exp(-Ω(T)) without changing the GD update rule.

## Results

The paper establishes four main convergence rate guarantees for gradient descent in matrix sensing. For symmetric over-parameterization (k>r), it proves an Ω(1/T²) lower bound (Theorem 3.1), confirming that the previously known O(1/T²) upper bound is tight. For asymmetric exact-parameterization (k=r), it achieves a linear convergence rate of exp(−Ω(T)) with random initialization (Theorem 4.3), independent of the initialization scale α. For asymmetric over-parameterization (k>r), it proves the first global exact convergence result with rate exp(−Ω(α²T)) (Theorem 4.2), where the rate depends on the initialization scale α. A simple one-step modification (Theorem 5.1) removes the α² dependency, recovering the exp(−Ω(T)) rate even in the over-parameterized regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Stöger & Soltanolkotabi (2021) and Soltanolkotabi et al. (2023), which only guarantee convergence to a small error dependent on the initialization scale (no exact convergence), this paper proves exact convergence (loss → 0 as T → ∞) for both symmetric and asymmetric settings with random initialization. Against Tu et al. (2016) and Ma et al. (2021), which require local (spectral) initialization to achieve exp(−Ω(T)) linear convergence in the exact-parameterization case, this paper achieves the same linear rate with purely random Gaussian initialization, removing the need for a spectral initialization step. For the over-parameterization case (k>r), Zhuo et al. (2021) provides an O(1/T²) upper bound under local initialization; this paper proves a matching Ω(1/T²) lower bound for the symmetric case (Theorem 3.1), establishing that the sublinear rate is fundamental to symmetric over-parameterization. The key practical advantage is that switching from symmetric to asymmetric parameterization in the over-parameterized regime upgrades the rate from Ω(1/T²) to exp(−Ω(α²T)) — an exponential speedup — without changing the GD algorithm itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the line of work studying gradient descent dynamics for low-rank matrix recovery, particularly the small-random-initialization results of Stöger & Soltanolkotabi (2021) and the over-parameterization convergence analysis of Zhuo et al. (2021) for the symmetric case, and the implicit balancing framework of Soltanolkotabi et al. (2023) for the asymmetric case. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) it provides the first rigorous Ω(1/T²) lower bound proving that the empirically observed sublinear convergence in symmetric over-parameterization is fundamental rather than an artifact of analysis, and (2) it identifies imbalance between factor matrices (rather than the balance emphasized by Tu et al. 2016 and Ma et al. 2021) as the key mechanism enabling linear convergence in the over-parameterized asymmetric setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a clean theoretical separation between symmetric and asymmetric parameterizations for the same underlying problem, showing that the choice of parameterization (not just the algorithm) can change convergence from polynomial to exponential — a finding with implications for how over-parameterized models are structured in practice. The one-step imbalance amplification trick (Theorem 5.1) offers a practical recipe to recover initialization-independent linear convergence without changing the optimization algorithm. Open questions include whether the imbalance mechanism generalizes to deeper factorizations (e.g., multi-layer matrix factorization or deep linear networks, which the authors mention as future work) and whether similar parameterization-dependent slowdowns appear in other over-parameterized non-convex problems beyond matrix sensing.
