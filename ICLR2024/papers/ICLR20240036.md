## Problem

Large language models have been widely adopted in vision-language frameworks, but always in roles that depend on the language modality—either as text encoders, token decoders, or cross-attention partners. Whether a single frozen transformer block from a text-only LLM can improve purely visual representation learning, with no language prompts, inputs, or outputs at all, remained an unexplored question. This paper demonstrates that it can, and that the benefit is a general phenomenon across LLM families and block depths.

## Input / Output

The method is modality-agnostic: it accepts any tokenized visual representation (16×16 image patches, unordered 3D point sets, spatiotemporal video cubes, agent/lane polyline embeddings, or fused vision-language features) and passes it through the frozen LLM block before the original decoder. The only trainable parameters are two linear projection layers that bridge the dimensionality gap between the visual encoder and the LLM block; no language tokens are ever concatenated or attended to. Outputs remain task-specific (class scores, trajectory waypoints, answer tokens, retrieval scores) and are produced by the unchanged downstream decoder.

## Method

The architecture inserts a single frozen transformer block from a pretrained LLM (default: the last block of LLaMA-7B) between the visual encoder F_E and decoder F_D, flanked by two trainable linear layers F_L^1 and F_L^2 for dimension alignment. Two critical modifications are made to the LLM block: the autoregressive causal mask is replaced with a padding-only mask (since visual tokens arrive simultaneously), and the rotary positional embedding is removed to stay consistent with the visual backbone. During training, the LLM block remains frozen while all other modules are optimized; ablations show that fine-tuning the LLM block actually hurts performance due to overfitting, and that the gains are not merely from added capacity (a matched-parameter MLP baseline recovers only ~half the improvement). The authors propose an information filtering hypothesis to explain the mechanism: the frozen LLM transformer acts as a filter that identifies informative visual tokens and amplifies their contribution to the latent representation, evidenced by emergent object-segmentation patterns in feature activations and by the observation that attention scores remain noisy while token features become sharply focused on relevant regions.

## Results

On ImageNet, adding a single frozen LLaMA-7B transformer block to ViT-B improves top-1 accuracy from 80.6% to 81.7%, with consistent gains on robustness benchmarks (ImageNet-C: 60.5→62.1, ImageNet-A: 23.4→26.9, ImageNet-SK: 31.9→33.2, ImageNet-R: 43.5→44.3). The improvement generalizes across modalities: point cloud classification (ScanObjectNN T50: 83.1→83.8), action recognition (SSv2 Acc1: 64.97→66.03 for ViT-B), motion forecasting (Argoverse FDE: 1.10→1.08 for mmTransformer), and vision-language tasks (VQAv2 overall: 69.60→70.23; SQA3D EM@1: 47.20→48.09). Ablations confirm the gains stem from the pre-trained LLM weights rather than added capacity, and that freezing (not fine-tuning) the LLM block is optimal for sufficient training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against nine baselines across six task categories, and the frozen LLaMA block wins in every comparison. On image classification, ViT-B+LLaMA (81.7%) outperforms the DeiT-trained ViT-B baseline (80.6%) by 1.1 points, with larger relative gains on adversarial (ImageNet-A: +3.5) and OOD (ImageNet-SK: +1.3) benchmarks. For point clouds, Point-BERT+LLaMA improves ScanObjectNN T50 from 83.1% to 83.8%, while on the more challenging BG split the gain is 0.6 points. In action recognition, ViT-B+LLaMA (66.03% Acc1) beats the VideoMAE-initialized ViT-B (64.97%) by over 1 point on SSv2. For motion forecasting, mmTransformer+LLaMA reduces FDE from 1.10 to 1.08 and MR from 10.7 to 10.5 on Argoverse. In vision-language tasks, METER+LLaMA improves VQAv2 overall accuracy from 69.60 to 70.23 and Flickr30k IR@1 from 49.66 to 50.22, while SQA3D-LLaMA (EM@1 48.09) surpasses both the SQA3D baseline (47.20) and Multi-CLIP (48.02) on 3D VQA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes pre-trained LLM transformer blocks in a fundamentally different role than prior vision-language models: rather than using LLMs as decoders that produce tokenized text outputs (BLIP-2, Flamingo, FROMAGE) or as text encoders in multimodal pipelines, it extracts a single frozen transformer block and inserts it as a standalone visual encoder layer that processes visual tokens without any language input, prompt, or output. It also departs from the requirement for pre-trained visual backbones like CLIP, supporting training-from-scratch. The information filtering hypothesis draws on interpretability literature (Grad-CAM, network dissection, knowledge neurons in FFN layers) but applies it to a new scenario: explaining why text-only-trained transformers benefit purely visual encoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a frozen, text-only-trained transformer block consistently improves visual encoding across 2D, 3D, video, and non-semantic tasks challenges the assumption that modality-specific training is necessary for effective representation learning, and the information filtering hypothesis offers a testable mechanistic account (amplification of informative tokens) that could guide future architecture design. Key open questions include how training dynamics facilitate cooperation between visual features and the frozen language transformer, how to quantify the distinct functions of individual LLM layers, and whether the effect extends to other non-visual modalities. The scale requirement (≥1.3B parameters) and the modest but consistent gains (typically 0.5–1.5%) temper immediate practical impact, but the simplicity of the technique (one frozen block + two linear layers) makes it an accessible probe for understanding LLM internal representations.
