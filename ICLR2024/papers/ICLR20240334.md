## Problem

Existing open-set 3D scene segmentation methods either operate on explicit 3D representations (point clouds, meshes) that have lower resolution than images and misalign with pixel-aligned VLM features, or embed global CLIP features into NeRFs that are poorly localized in 3D space, requiring complex multi-resolution patches and DINO regularization. No prior work combines pixel-aligned VLM features with NeRF's novel view synthesis to improve open-set segmentation of under-observed scene regions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 200 posed RGB(-D) frames at 640×360 resolution (every 10th frame from 2000-frame Nice-SLAM sequences) plus pre-computed OpenSeg pixel-aligned feature maps (640×360×768, stored in float16). At query time, a text prompt is encoded via the CLIP ViT-L14@336 text encoder and matched against rendered open-set features to produce per-point 3D semantic labels. The 3D point cloud is used only for evaluation, not for building the NeRF, making the representation independent of any explicit 3D reconstruction.

## Method

OpenNeRF extends Mip-NeRF with an additional MLP head that predicts a 768-dimensional open-set feature vector per 3D point, supervised by cosine similarity against pixel-aligned OpenSeg feature maps (rather than global CLIP features as in LERF), eliminating the need for multi-resolution patches and DINO regularization. A key innovation is a confidence-based novel view synthesis pipeline: per-point uncertainty is estimated as the generalized variance (determinant of covariance) of projected open-set features across multiple views, and high-uncertainty points are selected as targets for generating new camera poses (with density sampling to avoid geometry collisions). Rendered novel views from these poses yield additional open-set features that are fused into the representation, improving segmentation of under-observed regions. Training uses a combined loss of RGB L2, open-set cosine similarity, and optional depth Huber loss, with no gradient flow from the open-set branch to the density branch to handle multi-view inconsistency.

## Results

On the Replica dataset (8 scenes, 640×360 resolution), OpenNeRF achieves 20.4 mIoU and 31.7 mAcc for zero-shot 3D semantic segmentation across all 51 classes, outperforming LERF (10.5 mIoU) and OpenScene Ensemble (15.9 mIoU). The gains are most pronounced on tail classes (5.8 mIoU vs. 2.3 for LERF and 1.5 for OpenScene), while head-class performance is 35.4 mIoU. Ablation studies show that rendering novel views contributes +1.0 mIoU over depth-supervised rendering-and-projection (19.4 → 20.4), and that the render-and-project evaluation strategy outperforms direct NeRF sampling by +2.0 mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OpenNeRF outperforms both baselines across all class subsets. Against LERF, the improvement is +9.9 mIoU overall (20.4 vs. 10.5), with particularly large gains on tail classes (+3.5 mIoU) and common classes (+10.0 mIoU); LERF's reliance on global CLIP-image features and multi-resolution patches with DINO regularization leads to less localized masks. Against OpenScene (the only prior method reporting open-set 3D segmentation scores), OpenNeRF achieves +4.5 mIoU over the Ensemble variant (20.4 vs. 15.9) and +5.6 mIoU over the Distilled variant. The gap is smallest on head classes (+3.7 mIoU over OpenScene Ensemble), which the authors attribute to OpenScene's benefit from large-scale Matterport pre-training on common geometric structures. Notably, OpenNeRF detects several long-tail categories (wall-plug, clock, tissue-paper, tablet, cloth) that OpenScene fails to segment at all, while both methods still miss numerous other tail classes, underscoring the difficulty of the task.

## Relation to Prior Work

OpenNeRF builds on the Mip-NeRF volumetric rendering backbone and extends it with an additional MLP head that predicts pixel-aligned open-set features (from OpenSeg) at each 3D point, departing from LERF's use of global CLIP-image features that required multi-resolution patches and DINO regularization. Conceptually, it positions itself between the explicit 3D convolutional approach of OpenScene (which operates on point clouds/meshes) and the implicit NeRF-based approach of LERF, arguing that NeRFs' unlimited resolution and novel-view synthesis capability are better suited to open-set segmentation. The key methodological departure is the direct distillation of pixel-wise VLM features into the NeRF, which simplifies the architecture while improving localization, combined with an uncertainty-driven mechanism for selecting informative novel camera poses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper establishes the first systematic evaluation protocol for open-set 3D semantic segmentation, directly comparing explicit (mesh/point cloud) and implicit (NeRF) representations, which is valuable for the community as the field matures. The finding that NeRF-based representations outperform mesh-based ones specifically on small, long-tail objects (e.g., wall plugs, switches) has practical implications for robotic and AR/VR applications where fine-grained scene understanding is critical. However, the evaluation is limited to 8 Replica scenes with absolute mIoU still below 21%, and many long-tail classes remain undetected by all methods, indicating substantial room for improvement. Open questions include scalability to larger outdoor scenes, real-time inference, and whether the uncertainty-based novel-view selection generalizes beyond the controlled indoor setting.
