## Problem

Existing early-exit dynamic neural networks (EEDNs) suffer from two fundamental shortcomings: (1) gating mechanisms and intermediate inference modules are trained in a decoupled manner, creating a train-test distribution mismatch; and (2) threshold-based gating introduces a positive bias into predicted probabilities, making reliable uncertainty characterization (calibration, conformal intervals) difficult. No prior architecture-agnostic method jointly optimizes both components while providing well-calibrated uncertainty outputs.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100/100-LT and SVHN, 224×224 for ImageNet) and produces a class label, a predicted probability vector, and a conformal prediction set whose size adapts to the computational budget. Unusually, the output is sample-dependent: each input exits at a different depth determined by learned gate probabilities, so the inference cost and the associated conformal threshold are both per-sample. Conformal intervals are constructed using gate-specific validation subsets, with a fallback to a global threshold when a gate's validation set is too small.

## Method

JEI-DNN augments a frozen pretrained backbone (e.g., T2T-ViT) with lightweight single-layer inference modules and gates at every layer, then jointly trains them via a bi-level optimization. The key modeling choice is to parameterize the exit probability P(G=l|x) sequentially using a min operator over cumulative gate outputs, which avoids the vanishing/saturation issues of modeling independent gate probabilities as a product and guarantees a valid probability distribution. The outer level optimizes gate parameters by training each gate as a binary classifier whose target is determined by the argmin of the combined accuracy-plus-cost objective across all gates; the inner level optimizes IM parameters via a weighted cross-entropy loss where the weights are the learned exit probabilities, directly closing the train-test gap. A warm-up phase first trains all IMs on the full dataset before bi-level optimization begins, and the added computation from gates and IMs is less than 0.01% of the backbone's Mul-Adds.

## Results

JEI-DNN is evaluated as accuracy-vs-inference-cost (Mul-Add) curves rather than fixed operating points, since the cost parameter λ is set at training time. In the region exceeding 80% of the full network's end accuracy, JEI-DNN significantly outperforms all baselines (BoostedNet, L2W-DEN, MSDNet, RANet) on CIFAR-100, SVHN, and CIFAR-100-LT, with notably tighter 95% confidence intervals. On uncertainty metrics, JEI-DNN achieves substantially lower expected calibration error (ECE) and tighter conformal prediction intervals (lower inefficiency |C̄|) at comparable or lower inference cost. When applied to the Dynamic Perceiver backbone, JEI-DNN yields significant accuracy improvements on both CIFAR-100 and ImageNet over the unmodified Dynamic Perceiver.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two architecture-agnostic baselines, BoostedNet (Yu et al., 2023) and L2W-DEN (Han et al., 2022b), JEI-DNN achieves a better accuracy-vs-Mul-Add trade-off across all tested datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, CIFAR-100-LT, SVHN, ImageNet), with the gap most pronounced in the high-accuracy region (>80% of full-model accuracy) and on the imbalanced CIFAR-100-LT where baselines show much wider confidence intervals. Against the EEDN-tailored architectures MSDNet (Huang et al., 2018) and RANet (Yang et al., 2020), JEI-DNN also outperforms, benefiting from its ability to avoid poorly calibrated early IMs that threshold-based gates cannot skip. When layered on top of the Dynamic Perceiver (Han et al., 2023b), JEI-DNN produces significant accuracy gains on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet (MobileNetV3 backbone), demonstrating that the joint training procedure can enhance even specialized early-exit architectures. The additional baseline DiffBranch (Scardapane et al., 2020) is significantly outperformed on SVHN, though the authors note implementation uncertainty due to missing code. JEI-DNN adds negligible overhead: fewer than 0.01% additional Mul-Adds and only ~60 extra parameters over the backbone, at the cost of slightly longer training time (e.g., 37 min vs. 22–24 min for T2T-ViT-14 on CIFAR-100).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two threads in the early-exit dynamic neural network (EEDN) literature: architecture-agnostic training procedures (BoostedNet, L2W-DEN) that attempt to close the train-test mismatch between gates and inference modules, and learnable-gate approaches (Bolukbasi et al., EPNet, PTEENet) that decouple gate training from IM training. JEI-DNN departs from both by formulating the joint optimization of gates and IMs as a bi-level problem with a novel sequential parameterization of exit probabilities P(G=l|x) that avoids the vanishing-product instability of modeling individual gate events. The conformal prediction framework for EEDNs, while drawing on standard conformal scoring, is adapted to the multi-exit setting by computing per-gate thresholds, a detail not previously addressed in the EEDN literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that jointly training gates and IMs not only improves the accuracy-cost trade-off but also yields substantially better calibration and tighter conformal intervals than threshold-based baselines—addressing a practical need for reliable uncertainty in adaptive-inference systems. A key limitation is that the inference-cost parameter λ must be fixed at training time, preventing post-deployment adjustment of the accuracy/cost operating point, which threshold-based methods allow. Open questions include whether the bi-level optimization scales to very deep or very wide backbones, whether the approach transfers cleanly to non-classification tasks (e.g., language modeling, detection), and whether the lightweight single-layer IMs remain sufficient for more complex intermediate representations.
