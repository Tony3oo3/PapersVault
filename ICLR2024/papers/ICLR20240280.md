## Problem

Monocular 3D lane detection methods suffer from depth ambiguity and sensitivity to adverse lighting, while existing multi-modal approaches (e.g., M²-3DLaneNet) fuse camera and LiDAR features into a single BEV space, discarding modality-specific information that is naturally expressed in the perspective view. This paper asks whether retaining features in both PV and BEV spaces simultaneously, with bidirectional cross-modal fusion, can yield substantially more accurate 3D lane localization than single-space fusion.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 720×960 RGB image, a 64-beam LiDAR point cloud sampled at 10 Hz, and camera extrinsics/intrinsics. Points are voxelized at (0.2 m, 0.4 m) resolution for the BEV branch. Output is structured as 40 lane queries, each expanded into M point queries (via learnable point embeddings); for every point the network regresses x and z coordinates at predefined y positions, a visibility flag, and a lane category, so each group of M points constitutes one complete 3D lane.

## Method

DV-3DLane maintains a symmetric dual-branch backbone (ResNet for PV, PillarNet for BEV) and introduces three key modules. (1) Bidirectional Feature Fusion projects 3D points onto the image plane to scatter point features into pixel grids and simultaneously grid-samples image features at projected point locations, then concatenates each cross-modal feature with its original before feeding the remaining backbone stages, so both F_pv and F_bev carry multi-modal information. (2) A Unified Query Generator produces lane-aware queries in each view via Instance Activation Maps, then merges them through a lane-centric k-means cross-attention (with Gumbel-Softmax) supervised by an InfoNCE contrastive loss that pulls queries targeting the same GT lane together across views. (3) A 3D Dual-view Deformable Attention predicts 3D reference points and offsets from the unified queries, forms deformed 3D sample locations, and projects them consistently onto both the PV and BEV planes for bilinear feature sampling, ensuring that features from the same 3D location in both views are aggregated into each query. Training combines the 3D lane regression/classification loss with auxiliary 2D instance-segmentation losses on both branches and a depth-estimation loss on the PV branch.

## Results

On OpenLane-1K with a strict 0.5 m distance threshold, DV-3DLane-Large (ResNet-50 + PillarNet-50, C+L) achieves an F1 score of 65.2 and category accuracy of 93.4%, reducing X-axis localization error to 0.082 m (near) / 0.101 m (far) and Z-axis error to 0.028 m (near) / 0.048 m (far). This represents an 11.2-point F1 gain and a 53.5% reduction in localization errors over the previous state-of-the-art (LATR). Under the more lenient 1.5 m threshold, DV-3DLane-Large reaches F1 = 66.8 and Acc = 93.3%, outperforming all prior methods by at least 4.9 points in F1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DV-3DLane-Large (F1 = 65.2 at 0.5 m) decisively outperforms the previous camera-only SOTA, LATR (F1 = 54.0), by 11.2 points, and also surpasses the naive multi-modal baseline LATR + LiDAR (F1 = 57.4) by 7.8 points, demonstrating that its dual-view fusion strategy extracts substantially more information than simply appending LiDAR features to a camera backbone. Against the earlier multi-modal method M²-3DLaneNet (F1 = 48.2), DV-3DLane gains 17.0 F1 points, confirming that bidirectional feature fusion and unified query generation are far more effective than the naive BEV-only fusion used in M²-3DLaneNet. Among camera-only methods, DV-3DLane also beats PersFormer (F1 = 43.2, ResNet-50) and Anchor3DLane (F1 = 34.9, EffNet-B3) by large margins. In the ablation-style comparisons (Table 3, 0.5 m), DV-3DLane's 3D dual-view deformable attention (F1 = 70.7) edges out FUTR3D's modality-agnostic sampling (F1 = 70.2) by 0.5 points and DeepInteraction's sequential fusion (F1 = 68.7) by 2.0 points, validating the benefit of consistent 3D sampling locations across both view spaces.

## Relation to Prior Work

DV-3DLane builds directly on the query-based 3D lane detection paradigm established by LATR (Luo et al., 2023), adopting its refined point-query scheme and overall Transformer decoder structure, while extending it from monocular to multi-modal input. For query generation, it repurposes the Instance Activation Map mechanism from sparse instance segmentation (Cheng et al., 2022) to produce lane-aware queries in both PV and BEV spaces, and adapts the k-means cross-attention clustering from kMax-DeepLab (Yu et al., 2022) to unify dual-view queries into a single set. The key conceptual departure from prior multi-modal 3D lane work (notably M²-3DLaneNet) is the refusal to collapse all features into a single BEV space; instead, the paper maintains symmetric PV and BEV branches with bidirectional cross-modal fusion, arguing that modality-specific information is best preserved in its native view.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 11.2-point F1 gain at the stricter 0.5 m threshold and the consistent superiority even at 0.1 m demonstrate that dual-view multi-modal representation is not merely additive but qualitatively improves 3D localization precision, which is critical for safety-critical autonomous driving. The ablation revealing that single-space lane-aware queries underperform random initialization is a useful cautionary result: it suggests that forcing lane semantics into one geometric view discards complementary information that the other view retains. Open questions include whether the dual-view framework generalizes to multi-camera or multi-LiDAR rigs, how it behaves under severe sensor degradation (heavy rain, LiDAR dropout), and whether the bidirectional fusion overhead can be reduced for real-time deployment on embedded platforms.
