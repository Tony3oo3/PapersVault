## Problem

Generalizable NeRF methods condition on image features from reference views but lack an effective mechanism for exploiting correlations among different sampled points along rays and across views, limiting their ability to learn precise global 3D scene representations. This paper identifies that mask-based modeling, analogous to MLM in NLP and MIM in vision, can serve as a self-supervised pretraining signal to capture these point-to-point and view-to-view correlations as 3D scene prior knowledge.

## Input / Output

The method takes S reference RGB images with known camera poses and renders a novel view by casting rays from the target viewpoint. Along each ray, 64 points are sampled at the coarse stage and 32 additional points at the fine stage; each point is projected onto all S reference image planes to collect pixel-aligned RGB and CNN feature tokens. The output is a rendered image via volume ray-marching, with the key distinction that during pretraining, a fraction of the fine-stage feature tokens are replaced by a shared learnable mask token before entering the network.

## Method

MRVM-NeRF introduces a two-level masking strategy: at the ray level, a preset ratio η (default 50%) of fine-stage points are randomly masked; at the view level, for each masked point, 1–S of its feature tokens from the S reference views are additionally masked. The unmasked coarse branch acts as the target network while the masked fine branch acts as the online network (BYOL-style), with the target's projector updated via exponential moving average (τ=0.99). The pretraining loss L_mrvm aligns the projected latent representations of identically sampled points between the two branches using a cosine-similarity objective, and is jointly optimized with the standard NeRF rendering loss (weighted by λ=0.1). After pretraining, the projectors and predictor are discarded and the model is finetuned with rendering loss only. The approach is model-agnostic, demonstrated on both transformer-based (NeRFormer) and MLP-based (NeuRay) backbones.

## Results

On the category-agnostic ShapeNet-all setting, NeRFormer+MRVM achieves 29.25 PSNR / 0.942 SSIM / 0.060 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest baseline VisionNeRF (28.76 PSNR) by 0.49 dB and the unmasked NeRFormer baseline by 1.67 dB. On the more challenging ShapeNet-unseen setting (trained on 3 categories, tested on 10 unseen), NeRFormer+MRVM reaches 24.08 PSNR, surpassing PixelNeRF (22.71) by 1.37 dB. On realistic datasets, NeuRay+MRVM gains approximately 3.0 dB PSNR over NeuRay on DTU cross-scene generalization (29.48 vs. 26.47) and 2.2 dB on DTU per-scene finetuning (31.98 vs. 29.79), while also improving on NeRF Synthetic and LLFF across both settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeRFormer+MRVM consistently outperforms all 13 baselines across every evaluated setting. On ShapeNet-all, it beats the closest competitor VisionNeRF by 0.49 dB PSNR and 0.009 SSIM, and surpasses SRT by 1.38 dB PSNR. On ShapeNet-unseen, the gap widens: NeRFormer+MRVM leads PixelNeRF by 1.37 dB and FE-NVS by 2.18 dB in PSNR, with LPIPS dropping from 0.182 to 0.117. On realistic datasets (Table 3), NeuRay+MRVM dominates the cross-scene generalization setting, beating IBRNet by 3.72 dB on DTU (29.48 vs. 25.76) and MVSNeRF by 5.65 dB, while in per-scene finetuning it exceeds the original NeRF by 3.87 dB on DTU (31.98 vs. 28.11). In few-shot scenarios (Table 5), the advantage grows as reference views decrease: NeuRay+MRVM gains 1.10 dB at 50→5 views but 1.84 dB at 10→3 views, confirming that the mask-learned prior is most valuable under data scarcity. The method also transfers to the GNT backbone (Table 8), where GNT+MRVM beats GNT-MOVE by 0.31 dB PSNR on NeRF Synthetic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the mask-then-predict paradigm from NLP (BERT/MLM) and CV (MAE/SimMIM) into the 3D NeRF domain, adapting it to the unique structure of radiance fields where information is organized along rays and across reference views. The self-supervised objective borrows the BYOL-style target/online network design (Grill et al., 2020), repurposing the coarse-to-fine sampling hierarchy of NeRF: the unmasked coarse branch serves as the target while the masked fine branch is the online network. The method is applied on top of existing generalizable NeRF backbones (NeRFormer for Transformer, NeuRay for MLP), positioning it as a model-agnostic pretraining layer rather than a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic demonstration that masked pretraining improves generalizable NeRF, and the consistency of gains across two backbone families (MLP and Transformer), four datasets (synthetic and real), and both cross-scene and per-scene settings makes it a broadly applicable technique. The few-shot results suggest the learned 3D scene prior can partially compensate for limited reference views, which is practically important for real-world capture scenarios. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic or large-scale scenes, whether the specific two-level masking (ray + view) is optimal versus other masking granularities, and whether the BYOL-style target network is necessary or simpler alternatives suffice.
