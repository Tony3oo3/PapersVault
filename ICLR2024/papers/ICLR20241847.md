## Problem

Existing crystal structure generation methods rely on explicit graph representations whose inter-atomic relationship modeling scales quadratically with atom count, limiting them to small or simple chemical systems. Additionally, standard generative model evaluation metrics (reconstruction error, learned energy networks) do not reliably predict whether generated materials are physically stable, creating a disconnect between generation quality and the downstream goal of discovering novel stable materials.

## Input / Output

The model takes Gaussian noise as input (unconditional) or Gaussian noise concatenated with a chemical composition vector (conditional, specifying element types and optionally exact atom counts per element). The output is a 4D tensor of shape L×9×18×3 (max atoms per element × periods × groups × xyz coordinates) plus six unit cell parameters (a, b, c, α, β, γ), where absent atoms are encoded at a null location (−1,−1,−1) and filtered out post-generation. This periodic-table-indexed tensor format replaces explicit atom-to-atom edges, enabling arbitrary crystal sizes up to the maximum L without quadratic overhead.

## Method

UniMat represents any crystal as a 4D tensor indexed by the periodic table (periods × groups × max atoms per element × 3D coordinates), embedding chemical prior knowledge (e.g., same-group similarity) as spatial inductive bias. A denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM) is trained with a repurposed 3D U-Net architecture that applies interleaved 3D convolution and self-attention layers across the L, H, W dimensions, capturing inter-atom relationships without explicit graph edges. The denoising process iteratively moves atoms from random positions to their target unit-cell locations while pushing absent atoms to a null coordinate, enabling generation of crystals with arbitrary atom counts. For conditional generation, the composition vector is concatenated along the channel dimension, and classifier-free guidance is used to condition on auxiliary properties like energy. The model is trained with a cosine noise schedule, ε-prediction, and EMA, scaling to over 2 million structures (GNoME) on 32 TPU-v4 chips.

## Results

On DFT-verified stability metrics, UniMat discovers 414 stable (E_d < 0) and 2,157 metastable (E_d < 25 meV/atom) materials against the Materials Project 2021 convex hull, compared to 56 and 90 for CDVAE — a 7× and 24× improvement respectively. Against the more challenging GNoME convex hull (2.2M structures), UniMat finds 32 stable materials versus 1 for CDVAE. In per-composition formation energy comparisons, UniMat-generated structures have an average ΔE_f of −0.216 eV/atom lower than CDVAE, with 86.3% of overlapping compositions showing lower energy. For conditional generation on unseen compositions, UniMat achieves a 0.81 DFT convergence rate versus 0.55 for AIRSS, and produces structures with ΔE_f = −0.68 eV/atom lower than AIRSS (80% reduction rate).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CDVAE (Xie et al., 2021), the primary graph-based baseline, UniMat wins decisively on all DFT-based metrics: 414 vs. 56 stable materials (7.4×), 2,157 vs. 90 metastable materials (24×), and 32 vs. 1 stable materials against the GNoME hull (32×). UniMat also outperforms CDVAE on per-composition formation energy (ΔE_f = −0.216 eV/atom, 86.3% reduction rate) and on proxy metrics for the complex MP-20 dataset (best coverage, property statistics, and composition validity). Against the diffusion+VAE baseline DP-CDVAE (Pakornchote et al., 2023) and the language model baseline (Flam-Shepherd & Aspuru-Guzik, 2023), UniMat achieves superior property statistics and coverage on MP-20, though all methods perform similarly on the simpler Perov-5 dataset. In the conditional generation setting, UniMat outperforms AIRSS (Pickard & Needs, 2011) — the current leading non-learning structure search method — with a 0.81 vs. 0.55 DFT convergence rate and ΔE_f = −0.68 eV/atom lower formation energy (80% reduction rate), demonstrating zero-shot generalization to compositions absent from the 2.2M-structure GNoME training set.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant graph-based paradigm for crystal generation (CDVAE, Xie et al. 2021) by replacing explicit inter-atom structure with a periodic-table-organized 4D tensor (UniMat) processed by interleaved 3D convolution and attention, borrowing the architectural recipe from Imagen (Saharia et al., 2022). It also moves beyond learned proxy metrics for evaluation, instead advocating DFT-based per-composition formation energy and convex-hull decomposition energy as more reliable criteria. The conditional generation setup extends the diffusion framework with classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022) to condition on compositions, and positions UniMat as a scalable alternative to Ab initio Random Structure Search (AIRSS) for proposing initial structures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is that UniMat scales to over 2 million structures (GNoME) and outperforms AIRSS in both DFT convergence rate (0.81 vs 0.55) and per-composition formation energy, suggesting generative models can now serve as practical initializers for structure search at scale. The proposed DFT-based evaluation metrics (per-composition ΔE_f, decomposition energy against convex hulls) address a real gap where learned energy predictors fail to capture relative stability within a chemical system. Limitations include <100% structural validity on complex datasets, computational overhead from sparse representations for small chemical systems, and the fact that generated 'stable' materials still require further experimental verification. Extending UniMat to amorphous or non-crystalline materials and to property-conditioned generation beyond composition remain open directions.
