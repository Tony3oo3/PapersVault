## Problem

Deep neural networks have successfully guided Large Neighborhood Search (LNS) for combinatorial problems with graphical structure (integer programming, vehicle routing), but MAPF presents a unique two-fold challenge: agent interactions are spatiotemporal (requiring convolutional representations rather than graph convolutions), and the fast runtime of MAPF solvers demands each neural decision in under 0.05s. Prior work resorted to a linear model with handcrafted features, leaving the potential of deep architectures for MAPF-LNS unexplored.

## Input / Output

The network consumes three H×W×T one-hot encoded tensors (current solution paths, shortest paths, and static obstacles) where T is a time cutoff scaled to map size (48–216 timesteps). It simultaneously scores J=100 candidate agent subsets and outputs the highest-scoring subproblem for reoptimization. Pre-applied spatial and temporal pooling (up to 4×) reduces tensor dimensions for larger maps to keep inference overhead below ~25ms on a single V100 GPU.

## Method

The Multi-Subset architecture interleaves 3D convolution blocks (capturing local spatiotemporal agent-obstacle and agent-agent interactions) with intra-path attention blocks (enabling trajectory-level information flow along each agent's full path), so that any two agents become two-hop neighbors if their paths interact with a common agent. Crucially, the convolution-attention backbone is shared across all J subsets, amortizing heavy computation; only lightweight per-subset Transformer heads produce individual scores. Training uses a pairwise hinge classification loss that encourages the predicted score ordering to match the ground-truth solver improvement ordering across all subset pairs within a single LNS iteration, rather than regressing absolute improvement values.

## Results

On the five MAPF benchmark floor maps, the proposed Multi-Subset architecture achieves the lowest average gap across all settings: 31% on empty (32×32), 5% on random (32×32), 12% on warehouse (161×63), 22% on ost003d (194×194), and 29% on den520d (256×257). The largest improvements over the Unguided LNS baseline are on ost003d (22% vs. 43% average gap) and empty (31% vs. 42%), with Win/Loss of 200/0 on both ost003d and den520d. Model inference overhead is kept low at 0.012–0.025 s per LNS step, roughly 2× less than the Per-Subset architecture and negligible relative to solver runtime (0.06–0.21 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Unguided LNS baseline (MAPF-LNS, Li et al. 2021), Multi-Subset wins on all five floor maps in average gap, with the most dramatic gains on ost003d (22% vs. 43%, a 21-point reduction) and empty (31% vs. 42%), achieving a 200/0 Win/Loss on both ost003d and den520d. Against the Linear baseline (MAPF-ML-LNS, Huang et al. 2022), Multi-Subset clearly outperforms on empty (31% vs. 39%), warehouse (12% vs. 15%), and ost003d (22% vs. 36%), while only marginally beating it on random (5.0% vs. 5.4%) and den520d (29% vs. 30%). The Per-Subset architecture, which encodes each subproblem independently with 3D convolutions, is consistently worse than Unguided on most settings (e.g., 46% vs. 42% on empty, 20% vs. 14% on warehouse), highlighting the importance of the shared convolution-attention design. In absolute sum-of-delays comparisons (Appendix A.7), Multi-Subset also outperforms the previously reported MAPF-ML-LNS numbers on all five settings, e.g., 1760 vs. 6584 on ost003d and 2229 vs. >11535 on den520d.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MAPF-LNS framework (Li et al., 2021a) by replacing its heuristic subset selection with a deep neural network, and departs from the closest prior work MAPF-ML-LNS (Huang et al., 2022) by substituting a linear model with handcrafted features for a convolution-attention architecture that natively encodes spatiotemporal agent interactions. The broader lineage draws from learning-guided LNS for integer programming and vehicle routing, but the authors identify two MAPF-specific challenges—spatiotemporal locality and sub-50ms inference budgets—that prior graph-based or attention-based LNS guidance methods do not address. The intra-path attention mechanism, interleaved with 3D convolutions, is the key architectural departure, enabling trajectory-level information flow that pure convolution cannot capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a deep architecture can practically guide LNS for MAPF within the tight inference constraints of iterative path planning, opening the door to learning-based acceleration of state-of-the-art MAPF solvers at warehouse scale. The convolution-attention blocks for representing path-wise spatiotemporal interactions may generalize beyond MAPF to other multi-path planning problems (circuit routing, pipe routing). Key limitations include: the method is only validated on synthetic floor maps, does not handle very dense settings where the solver runtime is too short for the model overhead to be worthwhile, and does not predict solver runtime alongside improvement. Open questions include deployment in real robotic warehouses, extension to stochastic solvers, and whether the Multi-Subset architecture can directly construct subsets rather than merely ranking heuristically-generated ones.
