## Problem

No existing deep learning model can simultaneously perform music generation (synthesizing a coherent mixture) and source separation (decomposing a mixture into individual stems). Generation models learn p(y) over mixtures, collapsing the per-source information needed for separation, while separation models either architecturally condition on the mixture (preventing unconditional generation) or model independent source distributions (losing inter-source context). This paper bridges the gap by learning the joint prior p(x_1, …, x_N) over contextual sources with a single model.

## Input / Output

The model operates on raw waveforms at 22 kHz, mono, with ~12-second context length, using four stems (Bass, Drums, Guitar, Piano) from Slakh2100. For total generation, no input is required and the model outputs all N stems which are summed to form the mixture. For partial generation (source imputation), a subset of stems is provided as conditioning and the remaining stems are generated. For source separation, a single mixture waveform is the input and individual stem waveforms are the output. The score network takes an N×D tensor (N sources, D waveform samples) and returns the same shape, with the noise level σ(t) as an additional scalar input.

## Method

The Multi-Source Diffusion Model (MSDM) learns the joint prior p(x_1, …, x_N) via denoising score-matching with a U-Net score network (based on the Mousai architecture) that processes N stacked mono channels simultaneously, capturing inter-source dependencies. The key novelty for source separation is a Dirac delta likelihood p(y(t)|x(t)) = 1_{y(t)=Σx_n(t)} that exploits the exact additive relationship between sources and mixture, constraining one source as x_N(t) = y(0) − Σ_{n=1}^{N-1} x_n(t) and computing the posterior score for each free source m as S_m^θ(z,σ) − S_N^θ(z,σ), where z is the constrained N-tuple. This is the γ→0 limit of the Gaussian likelihood used in NCSN-BASIS, yielding tighter conditioning on the mixture. The sampler uses a first-order Euler ODE integrator with S_churn stochasticity (set to 20–40) and optional correction steps, following the Karras et al. (2022) design space. A weakly-supervised variant (ISDM) factorizes the joint prior into independent per-source score networks, trading contextual generation capability for slightly better separation quality.

## Results

On Slakh2100 source separation, the best configuration (ISDM Dirac with correction) achieves 17.27 dB average SI-SDR_I, approaching the state-of-the-art Demucs+Gibbs(512 steps) at 17.73 dB. The contextual MSDM Dirac with correction reaches 16.48 dB average SI-SDR_I, surpassing standard Demucs (16.11 dB) while retaining the ability to perform total and partial generation. For total generation, MSDM achieves a FAD of 6.55 (vs. 6.67 for a mixture-trained model) and higher subjective quality (6.51 vs. 6.15) and coherence (6.35 vs. 5.67) scores. The Dirac likelihood consistently outperforms the Gaussian likelihood by 2–3 dB across all configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Demucs (Défossez et al., 2019), both the weakly-supervised ISDM Dirac (correction, 17.27 dB) and the contextual MSDM Dirac (correction, 16.48 dB) outperform the 16.11 dB average, with ISDM Dirac gaining +1.16 dB and MSDM Dirac gaining +0.37 dB. Against the stronger Demucs+Gibbs(512 steps) baseline (Manilow et al., 2022) at 17.73 dB, ISDM Dirac (correction) is close at 17.27 dB (−0.46 dB) and actually surpasses it on Bass (19.36 vs. 17.16 dB) and Drums (20.90 vs. 19.61 dB), while MSDM Dirac (correction) trails at 16.48 dB. The key distinction is that MSDM uniquely supports total generation, partial generation (source imputation), and separation from a single model, whereas both Demucs baselines are separation-only. The Dirac likelihood ablation confirms a consistent 2–3 dB improvement over the Gaussian likelihood of Jayaram & Thickstun (2020) across all model variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work—generative music synthesis (which models p(y) over mixtures) and generative source separation (which models individual p_n(x_n) and conditions on the mixture at inference, as in NCSN-BASIS)—by learning the joint prior p(x_1,...,x_N) over contextual sources via denoising score-matching. The training framework follows the EDM formulation (Karras et al., 2022) with its S_churn stochasticity and Euler ODE integrator, while the partial generation (imputation) procedure adapts the inpainting method from Song et al. (2021). The key methodological departure from NCSN-BASIS is the replacement of its Gaussian likelihood with a Dirac delta likelihood that enforces the exact additive constraint y = Σx_n, and the shift from independent per-source priors to a single joint contextual prior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first single model that handles total generation, partial generation (source imputation), and source separation, opening a path toward general-purpose audio models where a composer can generate, edit, and decompose music within one framework. The source imputation task (e.g., generating a piano track that fits given drums) is genuinely new and practically useful for music production. However, significant limitations remain: inference is ~40× slower than Demucs (4.6s vs 0.111s per 12s chunk), separation quality does not surpass the Demucs+Gibbs SOTA (16.48 vs 17.73 SI-SDR_I), and the model is tested on only 4 instruments from a single synthetic dataset. Open questions include scaling to more instruments, real (non-synthesized) recordings, non-additive source relationships, and incorporating symbolic (MIDI) conditioning for finer control.
