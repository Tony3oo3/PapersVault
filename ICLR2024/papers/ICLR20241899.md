## Problem

Existing dataset distillation methods synthesize a single small set of images by matching training dynamics only over early epochs (4–15 out of 200), yet deep networks' learning dynamics shift dramatically as training progresses from simple to complex functions. This mismatch between the captured early dynamics and the full training trajectory creates a substantial performance gap between models trained on distilled data versus the original dataset.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241899/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a full training dataset (32×32 CIFAR-10/100 or 64×64 Tiny-ImageNet images with class labels) and a pluggable base distillation algorithm (e.g., MTT or IDC) as input. It produces P synthetic image subsets of fixed per-stage IPC (e.g., 10 images per class per stage), where the total synthetic budget is P × per-stage IPC. At evaluation time, a randomly initialized network is trained progressively on cumulative unions of subsets (S₁, then S₁∪S₂, …, S₁∪…∪S_P) rather than on all images simultaneously, and the total number of training iterations matches single-stage baselines.

## Method

PDD generates a sequence of synthetic subsets S₁,…,S_P where each S_i is synthesized by a base distillation algorithm conditioned on all previously generated subsets, ensuring S₁∪…∪S_i captures the full-data training dynamics at stage i without redundancy. Two key mechanisms enable this: (1) a transition step that trains a model on S₁∪…∪S_{i-1} to obtain starting parameters θ_i, so that S_i is distilled from the correct point in the training trajectory; and (2) a progressive evaluation protocol where the model is trained on expanding unions of subsets to prevent catastrophic forgetting. The framework is architecture-agnostic and composable with any gradient-matching or trajectory-matching distillation method. Additionally, PDD can discard low-forgetting-score (easy) examples at later stages, focusing distillation on increasingly difficult samples and reducing computational cost without performance loss.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (IPC=50), PDD+IDC achieves 76.5% ConvNet test accuracy and PDD+MTT achieves 74.2%, compared to 88.1% for full-data training. On CIFAR-100 (IPC=50), PDD+IDC reaches 54.4% and PDD+MTT reaches 52.0%, versus 56.2% full-data accuracy. On Tiny-ImageNet (IPC=50), PDD+MTT achieves 29.2% versus 37.6% full-data. PDD consistently improves its base methods by 1.6–4.3% over single-stage distillation across all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDD+MTT outperforms MTT by 1.6%/2.3% on CIFAR-10 (IPC 10/50), 3.5%/4.3% on CIFAR-100, and 4.1%/1.2% on Tiny-ImageNet, confirming that progressive multi-stage distillation captures longer training dynamics. PDD+IDC improves over IDC by 0.4%/2.0% on CIFAR-10 and 0.7%/1.9% on CIFAR-100. Against data-selection baselines (Herding, K-Center, Forgetting), PDD+MTT leads by 35+ points on CIFAR-10 IPC=10, demonstrating that gradient/trajectory-matching distillation far exceeds simple selection. PDD+DC and PDD+DSA improve over their single-stage counterparts by 2.4% and 0.7% respectively on CIFAR-10 IPC=50. PDD+DM yields a modest 0.4% gain over DM (63.4% vs 63.0%), expected since DM does not capture training dynamics. Finally, PDD+DREAM surpasses DREAM by 0.6% (IPC=10) and 1.9% (IPC=50) on CIFAR-10, showing PDD can further boost even the latest representative-matching methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

PDD builds directly on two state-of-the-art dataset distillation methods—MTT (trajectory matching) and IDC (gradient matching on full-data trajectories)—by wrapping them in a multi-stage progressive pipeline. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the argument that a single synthetic subset matching only early training dynamics (first 4–15 epochs) is fundamentally insufficient, because SGD learns functions of increasing complexity over training (Kalimeris et al., 2019; Hu et al., 2020). PDD addresses this by sequentially generating conditioned subsets, each capturing a later training interval, drawing on observations that deep models benefit from progressively harder examples (Pooladzandi et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2023). The framework is modular and method-agnostic, applicable to any base distillation algorithm including DC, DSA, DM, and DREAM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PDD is the first dataset distillation approach that enables scaling synthetic datasets well beyond IPC=50 (reaching IPC=250 on CIFAR-10), considerably narrowing the gap to full-data training (90% accuracy with 5% of data). This opens the door to practical deployment where moderate synthetic budgets are acceptable but single-stage methods plateau. Open questions include whether the progressive conditioning mechanism scales efficiently to very large datasets (e.g., ImageNet), whether the approach transfers to non-image modalities or generative tasks, and how it interacts with further condensation techniques where the image budget is dynamic. The paper also notes that updating previously frozen subsets degrades performance, suggesting the sequential dependency is a structural constraint rather than a design choice.
