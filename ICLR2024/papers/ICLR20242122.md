## Problem

LLMs frequently fail at simple decision-making tasks in embodied environments due to two misalignment issues: they generate invalid actions (e.g., using ingredients not present) and misestimate environment-specific dynamics and constraints. Conversely, RL agents are perfectly aligned with environments but start from random policies, suffering poor sample efficiency. The paper addresses how to combine LLM prior knowledge with RL-based environment alignment in a single online framework that requires no prepared datasets or prior knowledge of the environment.

## Input / Output

At each decision step, the method takes a textual observation prompt (a scripted description of the agent's current state, e.g., 'You notice a tomato on the table. Currently you don't have anything in hand. To serve the dish of a bowl only containing chopped tomato, you should first') and a fixed set of valid action prompts (e.g., 'pick up the tomato', 'take the bowl'). Rather than generating an action autoregressively, the LLM is queried for the token-level log-probability of each action prompt conditioned on the observation, and these scores are normalized via softmax to produce a discrete action probability distribution. The trained alignment is fully encoded in LoRA parameters (~4.2M for LLaMA-7B), which serve as a plug-and-play module transferable across environments.

## Method

TWOSOME deploys a frozen LLaMA-7B as both actor and critic under PPO. The actor generates valid policies by computing the joint token probability of each pre-defined action prompt (Eq. 1), then applying word normalization—dividing the log-probability by the number of words rather than tokens—to correct the bias where longer actions are systematically penalized. The key insight is that sub-word tokens after the first (e.g., 'op' after 'ch' in 'chop') carry near-100% probability, so word-level averaging is more principled than token-level. The critic is a 3-layer MLP (1024-512-1) attached to the last transformer block, while the actor is updated via LoRA adapters; only these lightweight parameters are trained, with a larger learning rate for the randomly-initialized critic and a smaller one for the zero-initialized LoRA. The framework also contributes four prompt design principles (cohesive concatenation, article sensitivity, repetition of preferred nouns, context-dependent action phrasing) that shape the initial policy before RL fine-tuning begins.

## Results

TWOSOME with word normalization achieves a 100% success rate across all four tasks (Tomato Salad, Tomato-Lettuce Salad, Food Preparation, and Entertainment), outperforming PPO (which drops to 33% on Entertainment) and the non-finetuned SayCan-like baseline (0–81%). On the NLP benchmarks (ARC_C, HellaSwag, PIQA, MMLU), the finetuned TWOSOME models show no significant degradation from the base LLaMA-7B (e.g., ARC_C: 0.43 vs. 0.42; PIQA: 0.79 vs. 0.79). In the eight unseen generalization tasks, TWOSOME maintains 97–100% success rate versus 4–85% for the non-finetuned baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PPO (adapted from CleanRL), TWOSOME with word normalization ties on the three easier tasks (100% each) but decisively wins on the Entertainment task (100% vs. 33%), where PPO also required action masking to learn anything in VirtualHome. Against the SayCan-like baseline (TWOSOME without finetuning), TWOSOME-WN wins on every task, with the most dramatic gap on Tomato-Lettuce Salad (100% vs. 0%) and Entertainment (100% vs. 45%), confirming that RL alignment is necessary for complex multi-step tasks. Against the GLAM-like baseline (TWOSOME without normalization), TWOSOME-WN wins on Tomato Salad (100% vs. 67%) and Entertainment (100% vs. 67%) while tying on the remaining two tasks, demonstrating that word normalization is critical for stability in environments with variable-length action prompts. On the NLP benchmarks, both TWOSOME variants (Food Preparation and Entertainment) match or slightly exceed base LLaMA-7B scores, confirming that online PPO finetuning does not erode the LLM's general language abilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

TWOSOME sits at the intersection of LLM-based planning (SayCan, Brohan et al. 2023) and online RL (PPO, Schulman et al. 2017), combining the LLM's prior knowledge as a policy initializer with PPO's environment-grounded updates. It departs from SayCan by adding RL fine-tuning rather than relying solely on prompt engineering, and from GLAM (Carta et al. 2023) by operating on semantically rich macro-actions rather than primitive actions, which exposes and motivates the word-normalization correction for variable-length action prompts. The parameter-efficient architecture—sharing a frozen LLaMA-7B between actor (via LoRA) and critic (via MLP heads)—builds on LoRA (Hu et al. 2022) and the RLHF actor-critic framing (Ouyang et al. 2022) but applies them to embodied decision-making rather than preference alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 7B LLM can be aligned with embodied environments via online PPO without degrading its general language capabilities, and that the resulting LoRA weights enable open-vocabulary generalization to unseen tasks—something traditional RL agents cannot do. The word-normalization insight (averaging log-probabilities by word count rather than token count) is a practical, easily adoptable fix for a subtle but important bias in LLM-based action selection. Key limitations include the high per-step computational cost of scoring all valid actions through the LLM, the restriction to simple macro-action environments, and the reliance on hand-crafted observation prompts; scaling to continuous or high-dimensional action spaces and reducing inference overhead remain open challenges.
