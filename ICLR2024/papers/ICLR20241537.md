## Problem

Prior work on best arm identification (BAI) in linear bandits either addresses the fixed-budget regime without privacy constraints or the fixed-confidence regime with differential privacy, leaving the fixed-budget BAI problem under an ε-DP constraint entirely unexplored. This paper fills that gap by constructing a differentially private policy for fixed-budget BAI and establishing the first minimax lower bound on error probability that explicitly depends on the privacy parameter ε.

## Input / Output

The policy takes as input the privacy parameter ε > 0, a fixed budget T, the number of arms K, and known d-dimensional feature vectors {a_i} for each arm, where arm rewards are i.i.d. and supported on [0,1] with means given by a_i^T θ* for an unknown θ* ∈ R^d. The output is a single arm index Î_T identifying the best arm after exactly T sequential pulls. The policy must satisfy ε-DP in the sense that changing any single reward observation in the full reward table can shift the output distribution by at most a factor of e^ε.

## Method

DP-BAI operates over M = Θ(log d) successive-elimination phases, reducing the active arm set each phase. In each phase, it performs dimensionality reduction on the active arm vectors, then either pulls all active arms uniformly or constructs a MAX-DET collection (a subset of d_p arms maximizing the absolute determinant) and pulls only those arms, using the linear coordinates of non-pulled arms to impute their private means. The key privacy mechanism adds independent Laplacian noise with scale 1/(ε · n_i) to each arm's empirical mean, where n_i is the number of pulls; the MAX-DET principle ensures all coordinate coefficients are bounded by 1 (via Cramer's rule), so noise does not amplify when imputing means for non-pulled arms. The paper proves the policy satisfies ε-DP, derives an upper bound exp(−Ω(T/(H log d))) on error probability where H = H_BAI + H_pri decomposes into standard BAI hardness and a privacy-dependent term (1/ε)·max i/Δ_(i), and establishes a matching minimax lower bound showing none of the log d, H_BAI, or H_pri factors can be improved in general.

## Results

The DP-BAI policy achieves an upper bound on error probability of exp(−Ω(T/(H log d))), where H = H_BAI + H_pri is a composite hardness parameter combining the standard fixed-budget BAI complexity (H_BAI = max i/Δ_(i)²) and the privacy-induced complexity (H_pri = (1/ε)·max i/Δ_(i)). A matching minimax lower bound of the form exp(−T/(c(log d)^β₁(H_BAI^β₂ + H_pri^β₃))) is established, confirming order-wise tightness of the exponent in T, log d, H_BAI, and H_pri. In the numerical study (K=30, d=2), DP-BAI significantly outperforms BASELINE (no MAX-DET) and DP-OD across all tested ε values, and approaches the non-private OD-LINBAI performance as ε→∞.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DP-BAI is compared against three baselines in the numerical study (K=30, d=2, 1000 trials). Against BASELINE (which follows DP-BAI but omits the MAX-DET collection), DP-BAI significantly outperforms across all ε values, demonstrating the utility of exploiting the linear structure via MAX-DET. Against OD-LINBAI (Yang and Tan, 2022), the state-of-the-art non-private fixed-budget BAI algorithm, DP-BAI approaches its performance as ε→∞ (privacy vanishes), confirming that the privacy constraint is the sole source of degradation. Against DP-OD (a DP variant of OD-LINBAI using Laplacian noise on the OLS moment matrix), DP-BAI consistently outperforms, and the gap widens as the condition number of the arm vectors (x∨y)/(x∧y) increases — a known weakness of DP-OD whose error bound explicitly depends on this ratio, whereas DP-BAI's bound is independent of the arm vectors. The paper also compares DP-BAI (Laplacian noise, ε-DP) with DP-BAI-GAUSS (Gaussian noise, (ε,δ)-DP): DP-BAI is strictly better for small δ (and vacuous for DP-BAI-GAUSS at δ=0), while DP-BAI-GAUSS can be tighter for large δ and T.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fixed-budget best-arm identification (BAI) framework for linear bandits (Carpentier and Locatelli, 2016; Yang and Tan, 2022) by imposing an ε-differential privacy constraint on the sampling policy. It builds directly on the dimensionality-reduction machinery of OD-LINBAI (Yang and Tan, 2022) but departs from its G-optimal design allocation strategy, which the authors show does not extend naturally to the DP setting because the resulting error bound acquires an uncontrolled dependence on arm-vector geometry. The DP formulation follows the sequential-policy definition of Basu et al. (2019) and Nikolakakis et al. (2021), who studied BAI with DP only in the fixed-confidence regime without deriving lower bounds as functions of ε.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide matching-order upper and lower bounds on the error probability for fixed-budget BAI under ε-DP, with a hardness parameter that cleanly decomposes into a standard BAI term and a privacy term. The MAX-DET allocation principle is a novel tool that avoids the arm-vector sensitivity of G-optimal designs under DP, and the early-stopping policy technique (Lemmas 4.3, 4.5) is flagged as potentially reusable for lower bounds in other bandit problems. Open questions include whether a policy exists whose error bound matches a problem-dependent lower bound for all instances (not just hard ones), and whether the framework extends to generalized linear or neural contextual bandits.
