## Problem

Applying classifier-guided diffusion to pixel-based sketch generation with a fixed gradient scale produces either overly sparse sketches (insufficient recognizability) or over-sketched outputs with repetitive, overlapping strokes, and no single scale works across all object categories. This paper addresses the lack of a mechanism to dynamically balance sketch complexity and recognizability during the denoising process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241992/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single class label (from QuickDraw's 345 categories) as conditioning and produces a 64×64 RGB pixel sketch. A U-Net noise predictor is trained on 10k sketches per class, and a separate classifier is trained on noisy intermediate sketches x_t to provide guidance gradients. Generation uses DDIM sampling over 250 steps with a batch of 128 sketches sharing a common optimized scale per timestep to reduce computation.

## Method

The core mechanism is a scale-adaptive classifier-guided sampling strategy that dynamically optimizes the gradient scale s at each denoising timestep. A scaling indicator ζ(x_t) combines stroke complexity (fraction of non-zero pixels in the estimated final sketch x_{0|t}) and recognizability (classifier probability of the target class) to signal how much guidance is needed. A residual sketch x_rs measures the per-pixel difference between x_{0|t} before and after applying classifier guidance, and the scale is optimized via SGD to minimize the MSE between ζ(x_t) and the global average of x_rs. The full pipeline uses a three-phase sampling strategy: (1) warm-up unconditional sampling until the top-1 class probability exceeds the second by a margin η, (2) scale-adaptive classifier-guided sampling until recognizability exceeds threshold ξ, and (3) end-up unconditional denoising to remove background clutter. An asymmetric reverse process is used to compute x_{t-1} from both pre- and post-guidance noise predictions.

## Results

On the QuickDraw dataset (30 random categories), the proposed scale-adaptive diffusion model achieves the best FID of 3.08, CLIP-Score of 0.68, CLIP-Fine of 55.54%, and Recall of 0.35, while matching the second-best Precision of 0.68 (behind DDIM's 0.71). On the full 345-category setting, it further improves to FID 2.51 and Recall 0.39, outperforming all baselines on fidelity and mode coverage. The method also runs at 1.90 s per sketch with 250 DDIM steps, and a shortcut variant (67 steps) achieves FID 3.30 in 0.98 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all eight baselines on FID, CLIP-Score, CLIP-Fine, and Recall across both the 30-category and 345-category settings. Against the strongest pixel-based baselines, it improves FID from 3.75 (CFGD) to 3.08 and Recall from 0.32 to 0.35 on 30 categories, and from 2.64 to 2.51 FID with Recall 0.36→0.39 on 345 categories. The only metric where it trails is Precision: DDIM achieves 0.71 vs. 0.68 for the proposed method on 30 categories, though the gap narrows to 0.74 vs. 0.72 on 345 categories. All vector-based methods (SketchRNN, SketchHealer, SketchAA, SketchKnitter, ChiroDiff) are substantially outperformed, with FID gaps of 1.7–5.1 points, confirming that pixel-level diffusion modeling handles complex sketches more effectively than coordinate-sequence approaches. The CLIP-Fine score (55.54%) indicates the generated sketches capture richer fine-grained visual details than any baseline, with the closest competitor (CFGD) at 54.86%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classifier-guided diffusion pipeline (ADM, Dhariwal & Nichol 2021) from natural image generation to pixel-based sketch synthesis, departing from the dominant vector-based sketch generation paradigm (SketchRNN, SketchKnitter, ChiroDiff) that relies on autoregressive sequence decoders over stroke coordinates. The key methodological departure is replacing the fixed classifier guidance scale with a dynamically optimized one, driven by a scaling indicator (combining stroke complexity and recognizability) and a residual sketch that measures per-pixel guidance impact. The three-phase sampling strategy—unconditional warm-up, scale-adaptive guided sampling, and unconditional end-up denoising—adapts the DDIM sampling procedure specifically to the structure of sketch generation, where early steps establish global shape and later steps refine local detail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that pixel-based diffusion models can outperform vector-based methods on complex sketches, suggesting that the field's reliance on sequential vector representations may be unnecessarily restrictive. The identification of 'over-sketching'—where higher guidance scales produce repetitive, overlapping strokes rather than improved fidelity—challenges the standard assumption from photo generation that more guidance is always better, and the dynamic scale optimization offers a practical remedy. Limitations include the 64×64 resolution, evaluation on a single dataset (QuickDraw), and the use of a shared scale across the batch rather than per-sample optimization, which constrains the generality of the approach. Open questions include whether the scale-adaptive strategy transfers to higher-resolution sketches, diverse artistic styles, or other line-art domains such as architectural drawings or musical notation.
