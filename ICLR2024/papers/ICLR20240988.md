## Problem

Standard knowledge distillation assumes transfer from a trained teacher to an untrained student, but in practice one often has two already-trained models where the teacher may be weaker, equiperformant, or stronger than the student. This paper asks whether complementary knowledge (data context held by one model but not the other) can be transferred between arbitrary pretrained model pairs without performance degradation and without access to external rankings such as separate test sets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240988/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained teacher model, a pretrained student model (both trained on the same dataset, e.g. ImageNet), and the training data samples; ground-truth labels are optional since an unsupervised confidence-based variant matches supervised performance. The output is an updated student model after 20 epochs of transfer. Notably, the approach is architecture-agnostic (works across CNN, Transformer, and MLP families), requires no pairing-specific hyperparameters, and maintains the student's original inference cost unlike ensembling methods.

## Method

The paper frames knowledge transfer between pretrained models as a continual learning problem and proposes KL-Dist + DP (Data Partitioning): training samples are split into two subsets via a confidence-based mask that assigns each sample to the model (teacher or frozen initial student) with the higher softmax probability for the ground-truth class. For teacher-assigned samples, the student is distilled from the teacher via KL divergence on soft targets; for student-assigned samples, the student is distilled from its own frozen initial weights to retain existing knowledge. This data-level regularization avoids the over-constraint of weight-level methods like momentum weight interpolation (MCL), which struggle when teachers have niche areas of expertise. The approach requires no additional hyperparameters beyond standard KL distillation (temperature, learning rate), and the supervised mask can be replaced by a fully unsupervised one using maximum prediction confidence, enabling label-free transfer. Across 400 teacher-student pairs, this raises the transfer success rate from ~32.5% (standard KL distillation) to 92.5%, with consistent positive gains even from much weaker teachers.

## Results

On full ImageNet, the proposed KL+DP transfer achieves a 92.5% success rate (positive transfer delta) across 400 teacher-student pairs, compared to 32.5% for standard KL distillation and ~80% for weight-interpolation (MCL) regularization. In the full ImageNet evaluation (Table 1), KL+DP yields positive transfer deltas for all 8 student models (ranging from +0.15% to +0.65% mean top-1 accuracy), while standard KL distillation produces negative deltas for 4 of 8 students (e.g., ConvNeXt: −0.51%, Mixer-B16: −0.29%). The unsupervised variant of KL+DP matches or exceeds the supervised version (e.g., PiT-B: +0.95% vs +0.55%), and sequential multi-teacher transfer achieves a 59% average gain over the best single-teacher transfer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard KL distillation (Hinton et al., 2015), KL+DP wins decisively: the transfer success rate jumps from 32.5% to 92.5%, and on full ImageNet, KL+DP converts 4 negative transfer deltas (ConvNeXt −0.51%, Mixer-B16 −0.29%, ResNetV2 −0.09%, SeNet154 −0.05%) into positive ones while also improving the already-positive cases. Against contrastive representation distillation (CRD, Tian et al., 2020), the paper shows in Figure 6 that CRD achieves similar knowledge-gain levels but incurs far stronger overwriting of the student's prior knowledge, resulting in net-negative transfer for most teacher-student pairs; KL+DP avoids this by partitioning data so the student retains its own predictions on samples where the teacher is less confident. Against label-smoothing-regularized distillation (Yuan et al., 2020b), which reaches only 54% success rate with consistently negative transfer from weaker teachers, KL+DP is the only method that enables reliably positive transfer from weaker, equiperformant, and stronger teachers alike without pairing-specific hyperparameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends standard knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015) and contrastive distillation (Tian et al., 2020) to the setting of transfer between two already-pretrained models, a regime where the student already encodes substantial knowledge that must be preserved. It draws on continual learning regularization—particularly momentum weight interpolation (Stojanovski et al., 2022) and model soups (Wortsman et al., 2022a)—but departs from weight-level constraints by proposing a confidence-based data partitioning that selectively distills from the teacher only where the teacher is more confident. The measurement of complementary knowledge via positive prediction flips follows Lopes et al. (2022), and the multi-teacher extension builds on sequential distillation and weight-averaging ideas from the model-soups literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is that any public model repository (timm, HuggingFace) becomes an unrestricted auxiliary resource: one can improve a model by distilling from weaker, same-performance, or stronger peers without needing a held-out test set for model selection. Key open questions include whether gains compound meaningfully across many sequential teachers (the paper shows diminishing returns), whether the approach transfers to non-classification tasks or cross-domain settings beyond the mild DomainNet shift tested, and why CNNs with strong inductive biases remain resistant to knowledge reception. The modest absolute gains (~0.3–0.65% top-1) suggest the method captures only a fraction of available complementary knowledge, leaving room for more sophisticated retention mechanisms.
