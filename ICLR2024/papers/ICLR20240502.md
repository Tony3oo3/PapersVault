## Problem

Traveling waves of neural activity are widely observed in the brain and correlated with memory tasks, yet no simple trainable RNN architecture has been shown to exhibit such waves and demonstrate their computational role in sequence memory. Existing models either lack trainability (large-scale spiking networks) or conflate wave effects with oscillation effects (Neural Wave Machine), leaving the hypothesis that cortical wave propagation serves as an invertible short-term memory mechanism untested in a controlled computational setting.

## Input / Output

The wRNN consumes inputs one timestep at a time: individual MNIST pixels (784 timesteps), flattened CIFAR-10 rows (96-dim vectors over 32 timesteps, padded to 1000 with noise), categorical one-hot tokens (copy task), or paired numeric/indicator sequences (addition task). The output is read from the final hidden state via a linear map (or 2-layer MLP variant), producing class labels, a scalar sum, or a reproduced token sequence. The hidden state is structured as c circular channels of n neurons each, with the wave dynamics operating independently within each channel.

## Method

The Wave-RNN replaces the fully-connected recurrent matrix U of a simple RNN with a 1D circular convolution kernel [0, 1−ν, ν], which discretizes the one-way wave equation ∂h/∂t = ν ∂h/∂x and causes activity to propagate as traveling waves across the hidden state. The hidden state is organized into c separate circular channels (e.g., 6 channels × 100 neurons), and the input encoder V is initialized as a sparse identity mapping to a single hidden unit per channel, while the convolution kernel is initialized with ν=1 (pure shift). This combination supports a separate traveling wave in each channel driven by the input at a single source location, preventing overwriting of past inputs and enabling invertible encoding of both timing and position. The model uses ReLU activation and is trained with standard backpropagation through time, compared directly against an identity-initialized RNN (iRNN) that produces static decaying bumps rather than propagating waves.

## Results

On the copy task, the wRNN (n=100, c=6) achieves more than 5 orders of magnitude lower MSE than iRNNs with comparable parameters (n=100) or activations (n=625), and can perfectly copy sequences up to T=480 while the iRNN degrades after T=10. On the long-sequence addition task, the wRNN solves sequences up to T=1000 (MSE ≈ 6×10⁻⁵) in as few as 300 iterations, while the iRNN fails entirely at T≥700. On sequential image classification, the wRNN (16 channels, 256 units, 47k params) reaches 97.6% on sMNIST and 96.7% on psMNIST, and 55.0% on noisy sequential CIFAR-10, outperforming standard gated architectures (GRU: 43.8%, LSTM: 11.6% on nsCIFAR10) while using far fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On psMNIST, the wRNN (96.7%) outperforms uRNN (91.4%), LSTM (92.9%), GRU (94.1%), NWM (94.8%), IndRNN (96.0%), and coRNN (96.6%), and ties with LEM (96.6%) and wRNN+MLP matches URLSTM (97.6%) while using 10× fewer parameters. On sMNIST, the wRNN (97.6%) trails most gated and structured baselines (LSTM 98.8%, GRU 99.1%, Lip. RNN 99.4%, FlexTCN 99.6%) but still exceeds uRNN (95.1%) and iRNN (98.5% with 68k params vs. 47k). On noisy sequential CIFAR-10, the wRNN (55.0%) beats GRU (43.8%), LSTM (11.6%), anti-symmetric RNN (48.3%), Gated anti-symmetric RNN (54.7%), and Incremental RNN (54.5%), though it remains below Lipschitz RNN (57.4%), coRNN (59.0%), and LEM (60.5%). The key differentiator is parameter efficiency: the wRNN achieves competitive psMNIST accuracy with only 47k parameters versus 201k–267k for GRU/LSTM, and its convolutional recurrent structure provides a natural inductive bias for sequence-order invariance that the iRNN baseline lacks (92.5% vs. 96.7% on psMNIST).

## Relation to Prior Work

The Wave-RNN is a deliberate reduction of the Neural Wave Machine (Keller & Welling, 2023) to its essential traveling-wave component, stripping away the coupled-oscillator structure that previously confounded the memory contributions of oscillations with those of spatial propagation. Architecturally, it replaces the fully-connected recurrent matrix of the iRNN (Le et al., 2015) with a weight-shared convolutional layer initialized as a discrete shift operator derived from the 1-D wave equation, creating a locally connected ring of neurons. The paper also positions itself against the broader long-sequence RNN literature (LSTM, GRU, coRNN, Lipschitz RNN, state space models) and notes a structural kinship with the shift-SSM component of H3 (Fu et al., 2023), though the wRNN predates and differs from the HiPPO initialization used in S4/Mamba.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides some of the first clean computational evidence that traveling waves in a recurrent hidden state serve as an invertible short-term memory mechanism, directly supporting long-standing neuroscience hypotheses (King & Wyart, 2021; Muller et al., 2018) that had previously lacked a trainable model to test them. The wave-field's 'register' or 'tape' interpretation offers a concrete mechanistic account of why waves prevent overwriting of sequential inputs, and the permutation-invariance result on psMNIST suggests a broader inductive bias beyond mere speed. Key limitations include the small model scale (single layer, linear I/O), the high parameter count on nsCIFAR10 driven by the input encoder, and the absence of comparison with modern deep architectures (S4, Mamba, Transformers). Future work on integrating wRNN principles into language-modeling-scale systems and on the theoretical connection to state space models is the most promising direction.
