## Problem

Existing incentivized federated bandit protocols (e.g., Inc-FedUCB) naively assume clients truthfully report their participation costs, making them vulnerable to strategic misreporting that can paralyze the learning system. No prior work simultaneously guarantees incentive compatibility (truthful reporting), near-optimal regret, low communication cost, and bounded social cost in federated bandit learning.

## Input / Output

At each communication round, the server receives each client's reported participation cost D̂_{i,t} (which can be any data valuation function f(ΔV_{i,t}), not restricted to a constant) and the local sufficient statistics ΔV_{i,t}. The mechanism outputs a selected participant set S_t and a critical-value payment for each selected client that depends only on the other clients' reported costs, decoupled from the client's own claim. The server then aggregates uploaded statistics to update the global ridge regression estimator used for UCB arm selection.

## Method

TRUTH-FEDBAN reformulates the incentivized client selection problem as a submodular set cover (minimizing total reported cost subject to a log-determinant ratio constraint) and solves it via a budget-increasing greedy search (Algorithm 1) that achieves a [1+ε, 1−e^{−1}] bi-criteria approximation. Truthfulness is guaranteed by the Mu'Alem–Nisan characterization: the selection rule is proven monotone (Proposition 5, a non-trivial result showing the greedy output's objective is non-decreasing in budget), and payments use the critical value computed from all other clients' reports. This decouples each client's incentive from its own claimed cost, eliminating any benefit from misreporting while preserving the O(d√T log T) regret and O(N²d³ log T) communication cost of the underlying DisLinUCB protocol. A hyperparameter constraint β ≤ (1+tL²/λd)^{−d} eliminates the infinite-payment issue for monopoly clients without requiring a monopoly-free environment assumption.

## Results

TRUTH-FEDBAN achieves O(d√T log T) cumulative regret and O(N²d³ log T) communication cost, matching the near-optimal bounds of prior federated bandit methods. For social cost, it provides a [1+ε, 1−e⁻¹] bi-criteria approximation (Theorem 9), meaning the total true participation cost is within a (1+ε) factor of optimal while the constraint is relaxed by only a constant (1−e⁻¹) factor. Crucially, these guarantees hold simultaneously with incentive compatibility (truthfulness) and individual rationality — a combination no prior method achieves. Empirically, with d=5, N=25, T=6250, TRUTH-FEDBAN shows sub-linear regret and communication cost comparable to DisLinUCB, while incurring lower incentive and social costs than the vanilla greedy baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DisLinUCB (Wang et al., 2020), TRUTH-FEDBAN matches the regret and communication cost bounds exactly (both O(d√T log T) and O(N²d³ log T)), but adds all three economic guarantees (IR, IC, SC) that DisLinUCB completely lacks, since it assumes altruistic clients. Against Inc-FedUCB (Wei et al., 2023), TRUTH-FEDBAN again matches regret and communication cost, but critically adds incentive compatibility (IC) and social cost near-optimality (SC) — Inc-FedUCB only guarantees individual rationality and is vulnerable to strategic misreporting since it pays clients their claimed cost directly. Against the vanilla greedy baseline (Algorithm 3), which is also truthful via monotonicity, TRUTH-FEDBAN provides a provable constant-factor [1+ε, 1−e⁻¹] bi-criteria approximation for social cost, whereas the vanilla greedy method admits no constant-factor approximation guarantee (its approximation ratio is problem-instance-specific per Wolsey, 1982). Empirically, TRUTH-FEDBAN achieves lower incentive and social costs than the vanilla greedy method while maintaining comparable regret and communication cost, and both methods successfully prevent individual clients from benefiting via misreporting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Wei et al. (2023)'s incentivized communication framework for federated bandits, which assumed clients truthfully report their participation costs, by introducing the first incentive-compatible (truthful) mechanism. The core technical tool is the Mu'Alem & Nisan (2008) characterization of truthful mechanisms as monotone selection rules combined with critical-value payments, here applied for the first time to federated bandit learning. The client selection subproblem is reformulated as a submodular set cover (inspired by Iyer & Bilmes, 2013), enabling a constant-factor bi-criteria approximation that simultaneously controls social cost and maintains the determinant-ratio communication threshold inherited from Wang et al. (2020).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to demonstrate that incentive compatibility and near-optimal regret (O(d√T log T)) can be achieved simultaneously in federated bandit learning, removing the critical vulnerability of prior incentivized protocols to strategic cost misreporting. The monotonicity proof for the budget-increasing greedy algorithm (Proposition 5) is noted as absent from prior submodular optimization literature, suggesting broader applicability. Open questions include vulnerability to collusion (empirically observed at macro-level), long-term strategic planning by clients, and robustness to malicious (not merely self-interested) adversaries — all flagged by the authors as future directions.
