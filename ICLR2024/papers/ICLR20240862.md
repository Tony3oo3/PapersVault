## Problem

Temporal activity boundaries in video are inherently ambiguous because actions are continuous in time with no clear-cut transitions, and human annotation of start/end points is subjective. This boundary uncertainty makes it difficult for localization models to learn precise temporal spans, as existing methods either rely on exhaustive proposal matching or direct regression without explicitly addressing the noise inherent in boundary annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240862/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes pre-extracted video snippet features (e.g., from the official MAD or QVHighlights feature releases) and a tokenized natural language query (GloVe embeddings), both projected into a joint space. It outputs M=30 temporal spans as normalized (start, end) pairs in [0,1] with confidence scores, sorted by relevance. Because a set-based loss with Hungarian matching is used during training, no NMS is needed at inference, and the model operates on only 30 proposals rather than thousands.

## Method

DenoiseLoc is a transformer encoder-decoder where the encoder (8 layers of multi-head self-attention) fuses video and text features into a memory representation, and the decoder (8 layers) progressively refines learnable proposal spans. The key innovation is boundary-denoising training: during training, Gaussian noise is injected into ground-truth spans to create noisy proposals that the decoder must denoise, serving as a temporal augmentation that accelerates convergence. Each decoder layer extracts explicit proposal features via temporal RoI alignment (Align1D) from the memory, then interacts them with implicit proposal embeddings through DynamicConvolution (from Sparse R-CNN) rather than standard cross-attention, before MLPs predict refined start/end offsets. The noisy spans are disabled at inference, where only the 30 learnable proposals are used, and a single denoising step (not a full diffusion schedule) suffices to outperform multi-step diffusion baselines.

## Results

On QVHighlights, DenoiseLoc achieves 42.96% Average mAP (mAP@0.5: 61.30, mAP@0.7: 43.07), surpassing the prior best DiffusionVMR (39.92% AmAP) and QD-DETR (39.86% AmAP) by over 3 points in AmAP. On the long-form MAD dataset (with a guidance model), DenoiseLoc achieves 11.59% R@1 at IoU=0.1, outperforming CONE (8.90%) and all other baselines by large margins (9.6–34.4% relative improvements). Notably, DenoiseLoc uses only 30 queries per video, achieving competitive performance without NMS or thousands of proposals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On QVHighlights, DenoiseLoc achieves the best Average mAP (42.96%) by a clear margin over DiffusionVMR (39.92%) and QD-DETR (39.86%), with the largest gap at mAP@0.7 (43.07 vs. 40.17 and 39.88 respectively). However, DenoiseLoc trails QD-DETR (62.40%) and DiffusionVMR (61.61%) on R@1@0.5 (59.27%), while narrowly winning R@1@0.7 (45.07% vs. 44.98% and 44.49%). On the MAD dataset with a guidance model, DenoiseLoc dominates all baselines at every IoU threshold, achieving 11.59% R@1 at IoU=0.1 versus CONE's 8.90% and Moment-DETR's 5.07%, with the advantage widening at stricter IoU (5.63% vs. 4.10% at IoU=0.5). Without the guidance model, proposal-based methods (Zero-shot CLIP, VLG-Net) substantially outperform proposal-free methods including DenoiseLoc, confirming that long-form grounding remains challenging for regression-based approaches. The paper also shows that a single denoising step suffices, outperforming the multi-step DiffusionVMR without additional diffusion iterations.

## Relation to Prior Work

DenoiseLoc adapts the encoder-decoder architecture and Hungarian matching from DETR to the video domain via Moment-DETR, and transplants the noisy-proposal injection training strategy from DN-DETR (object detection) to temporal span localization. The decoder replaces standard cross-attention with DynamicConv (from Sparse R-CNN) and extracts explicit proposal features via Align1D (from G-TAD), creating a hybrid that combines implicit query embeddings with explicit boundary-sensitive features. Conceptually, the paper positions boundary denoising as a single-step training augmentation rather than a full diffusion process, explicitly contrasting with DiffusionDet, DiffTAD, and DiffusionVMR, and showing that iterative multi-step denoising provides no benefit for the lower-dimensional detection output.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical efficiency gain is notable: DenoiseLoc achieves SOTA on MAD with only 30 queries and no NMS, compared to thousands of proposals in competing methods, making it attractive for deployment. The acknowledged limitation of the Hungarian matcher for high-recall metrics (R@5, R@10) remains an open problem for set-prediction approaches in video localization. The negative result on diffusion (no gain from 1 to 512 steps) challenges the assumption that iterative denoising is universally beneficial and suggests the field should be cautious about porting diffusion paradigms to structured, low-dimensional prediction tasks. Future work could explore whether the denoising-as-regularizer perspective generalizes to other temporal prediction problems beyond moment retrieval.
