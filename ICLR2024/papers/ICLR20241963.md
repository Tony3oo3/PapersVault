## Problem

Existing Federated Long-Tailed Learning (Fed-LT) methods focus exclusively on improving the generic global model under long-tailed data while neglecting local personalization, and conventional Personalized Federated Learning (pFL) techniques assume a balanced global distribution. No prior work simultaneously trains a strong generic global model and tailored local personalized models under the combined challenges of global long-tailed imbalance and local data heterogeneity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241963/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images from K decentralized clients whose local datasets follow heterogeneous Dirichlet-partitioned distributions with a globally long-tailed class distribution (controlled by an Imbalance Factor). It produces two types of outputs: a single generic global model (shared backbone θ + global classifier ψ) evaluated on a balanced global test set, and K personalized local models (shared backbone θ + client-specific classifier φ_k) evaluated on local test sets matching each client's training distribution. The backbone is shared across all models; only the classifier heads differ between the global and personalized variants.

## Method

FedLoGe operates in three stages. First, it constructs a Static Sparse Equiangular Tight Frame Classifier (SSE-C) by randomly zeroing a fraction β of an ETF matrix and then optimizing equal-norm and maximal-angle losses to restore ETF geometry, which prunes noisy small-mean features (addressing 'feature degeneration' observed under long-tailed training) while preserving expressive dominant features. The shared backbone θ is then trained in federated rounds with SSE-C frozen as the classifier, while an auxiliary global classifier ψ and K local classifiers φ_k are updated alternately. Second, Global Adaptive Feature Realignment (GA-FR) normalizes each global classifier vector to unit ℓ₂ norm, effectively rebalancing the long-tailed norm distribution for balanced evaluation. Third, Local Adaptive Feature Realignment (LA-FR) constructs each personalized classifier by taking the global classifier direction scaled by the corresponding local classifier norm, thereby transferring global feature structure while respecting each client's learned class-importance weighting, followed by optional local fine-tuning.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-LT (α=0.5, IF=100), FedLoGe achieves 76.72% global model (GM) accuracy and 90.96% personalized model (PM) accuracy, outperforming the best baselines FedRep (72.18% GM) and FedROD (89.41% PM). On CIFAR-100-LT (α=0.5, IF=100), FedLoGe reaches 42.33% GM and 72.85% PM, surpassing FedBABU (39.21% GM) and FedPer (70.07% PM). On ImageNet-LT, FedLoGe attains 35.6% GM and 72.6% PM, with particularly strong gains on tail classes (28.5% few-class accuracy vs. 18.9% for Ratio Loss). On iNaturalist-160k, FedLoGe achieves 50.3% GM and 75.9% PM, leading all baselines by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedLoGe consistently outperforms all 10 baselines across every dataset and setting. Against the strongest pFL baselines, FedLoGe surpasses FedROD by 5.35% GM (76.72% vs. 71.37%) and 1.55% PM (90.96% vs. 89.41%) on CIFAR-10-LT (α=0.5, IF=100), and by 3.14% GM (42.33% vs. 39.19%) and 3.66% PM (72.85% vs. 69.19%) on CIFAR-100-LT under the same setting. Against the strongest Fed-LT baselines, FedLoGe leads FedBABU by 3.51% GM on CIFAR-10-LT (76.72% vs. 72.80% at IF=100, α=0.5) and Ratio Loss by 3.82% GM (76.72% vs. 72.90%). FedETF, which uses a dense fixed ETF classifier, shows the weakest PM performance (73.23% on CIFAR-10-LT IF=100) due to feature degeneration, while FedLoGe's sparse SSE-C design avoids this issue. On ImageNet-LT, FedLoGe achieves 72.6% PM vs. FedROD's 70.33% and 35.6% GM vs. Ratio Loss's 35.1%, with the largest gap on tail classes (28.5% few vs. 18.9%). On iNaturalist, the advantage is most pronounced: FedLoGe reaches 75.9% PM vs. FedETF's 71.3% and 50.3% GM vs. Ratio Loss's 45.2%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural collapse framework (Papyan et al., 2020) and the fixed-ETF classifier paradigm (Yang et al., 2022) into the federated long-tailed learning setting, where prior work had treated global imbalance correction (Fed-LT) and local personalization (pFL) as separate problems. The key conceptual departure is the identification of 'feature degeneration'—a breakdown of the ETF collapse property under long-tailed data—motivating the sparsified SSE-C classifier, which borrows the angle-maximization loss from MMA regularization (Wang et al., 2020) to maintain geometric structure while pruning noisy features. The GLA-FR module then repurposes the well-known classifier-norm-to-cardinality correspondence from long-tail recognition (Kang et al., 2019; Kim & Kim, 2020) as a lightweight realignment mechanism for both global and personalized heads, avoiding the heavier projection layers and dot-regression losses that FedETF (Li et al., 2023) requires.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedLoGe is the first framework to jointly optimize a strong generic global model and personalized local models under federated long-tailed data, addressing a practical gap where existing Fed-LT methods ignore client heterogeneity and existing pFL methods assume balanced global data. The feature degeneration diagnosis and the sparsity-based fix offer a reusable diagnostic for practitioners deploying fixed-classifier or neural-collapse-inspired methods in imbalanced settings. Limitations include the need for manual tuning of the sparsity ratio β and norm γ (which differ substantially across datasets), evaluation restricted to ResNet backbones on image classification, and the fact that the local finetuning step, while helpful, adds per-client compute. Open questions include whether adaptive sparsity can eliminate the manual tuning, whether the approach generalizes to non-vision tasks, and how the method behaves under more extreme heterogeneity or privacy constraints.
