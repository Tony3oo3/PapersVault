## Problem

Standard message-passing GNNs are limited to 1-WL expressivity and cannot capture higher-order structures like triangles, while existing equivariant simplicial networks (EMPSN) rely on manually computed geometric quantities for higher-dimensional simplex initialization and use scalarization-based EGNN layers that restrict vector updates to scaling. The paper asks whether one can combine the topological expressivity of simplicial message passing with the full steerable expressivity of Clifford algebra equivariance in a unified, efficient architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241221/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes geometric graphs or point clouds where each node carries scalar features (e.g., mass, charge) and vector features (e.g., position, velocity) in R^d. These are lifted to simplicial complexes via Vietoris-Rips, clique, or manual lifts (typically capped at dimension 2), and simplex features are initialized as geometric products of vertex Clifford algebra elements. Outputs are task-dependent: an E(n)-invariant scalar (convex hull volume) or E(n)-equivariant vector predictions (future atom positions, player trajectories, body joint positions).

## Method

CSMPN embeds each simplex's feature as the geometric product of its constituent vertices' Clifford algebra elements, so edges naturally carry bivector-valued features and triangles carry trivector-valued features, encoding oriented areas and volumes without manual computation. The key architectural innovation is shared simplicial message passing: a single message function φ^m handles all four adjacency types (boundary, coboundary, upper, lower) by conditioning on the source and target simplex dimensions, and the final message is restricted to a single aggregation over all incoming messages, enabling use of existing parallelized graph reduction kernels. All message and update functions are Clifford group equivariant neural networks (compositions of equivariant linear, bilinear, and nonlinear layers), guaranteeing E(n) equivariance of the full pipeline. This contrasts with prior simplicial methods that instantiate a separate neural network per adjacency type and with EGNN-based methods that cannot multiplicatively combine three or more positions to extract covariant geometric information.

## Results

On the 5D Convex Hulls task, CSMPN achieves MSE 0.002, outperforming EMPSN (0.007), CGENN (0.013), and EGNN (0.011). On CMU Motion Capture, CSMPN reaches MSE 7.55 (×10⁻²), beating CGENN (9.41), EMPSN (15.1), and EGNN (31.7). On MD17 atomic motion prediction, CSMPN achieves the best or near-best ADE/FDE across all four molecules (e.g., 3.88/5.94 on malonaldehyde vs. CGENN's 4.20/6.55). On NBA trajectory prediction, CSMPN attains the lowest ADE/FDE in both Attack (8.88/14.06) and Defense (6.44/9.22) settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSMPN consistently outperforms all non-equivariant and scalarization-based baselines (MPNN, GVP-GNN, VN, TFN, SE(3)-Tr, Radial Field) by large margins across every experiment. Against the key equivariant baseline EGNN, CSMPN improves MSE from 0.011 to 0.002 on Convex Hulls (5.5×) and from 31.7 to 7.55 on CMU (4.2×). Against the simplicial baseline EMPSN, CSMPN wins on all four tasks, with the largest gap on Convex Hulls (0.002 vs. 0.007) and CMU (7.55 vs. 15.1). Against the closest competitor CGENN (Clifford equivariant GNN without simplicial structure), CSMPN wins clearly on Convex Hulls (0.002 vs. 0.013), CMU (7.55 vs. 9.41), and NBA (8.88/14.06 vs. 9.17/14.51 Attack), while on MD17 the two methods are nearly tied—CSMPN is slightly better on ethanol and malonaldehyde but marginally worse on aspirin (3.82 vs. 3.70 ADE). The paper notes that EMPSN's restricted expressivity (scalarization-based) can act as a useful inductive bias for rigid, planar molecules like benzene, where CGENN and CSMPN perform comparably.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: Clifford group equivariant neural networks (CGENNs, Ruhe et al. 2023a), which provide steerable equivariant layers operating on geometric algebra multivectors, and simplicial message passing networks (MPSNs, Bodnar et al. 2021), which exploit topological structure beyond pairwise graph edges. It directly addresses two limitations of the closest prior work, EMPSN (Eijkelboom et al. 2023): the need for manually computed geometric quantities to initialize higher-order simplices, and the reliance on EGNN's scalarization-based architecture. The key departures are (1) initializing simplex features via geometric products of vertex multivectors, which naturally encodes areas and volumes, and (2) sharing a single message-passing network across all simplex-dimension communication types by conditioning on source/target dimensionality, reducing the problem to standard hypergraph message passing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The combination of Clifford algebra equivariance with simplicial topology offers a principled way to capture both geometric symmetries and higher-order relational structure in a single framework, with the shared message passing providing a practical efficiency gain over per-dimension networks. The consistent (if moderate) outperformance across four diverse geometric tasks—convex hull volume, human motion, molecular dynamics, and sports trajectories—suggests the approach generalizes well. However, the paper acknowledges increased computational cost from simplicial message passing, and the benzene result (where the less expressive EMPSN outperforms CSMPN) hints that the added expressivity is not always beneficial. Open questions include identifying which data regimes most benefit from simplicial geometric message passing and further reducing the computational overhead.
