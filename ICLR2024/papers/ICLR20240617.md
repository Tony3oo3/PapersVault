## Problem

Low-bitwidth (4-bit) post-training quantization of LLMs suffers severe performance degradation because activation outliers in specific channels inflate the quantization range, causing most normal values to be quantized imprecisely. Existing smoothing approaches (SmoothQuant, OmniQuant) either provide only limited relief for extreme outliers or introduce unstable gradients, while QAT is impractical due to prohibitive training costs on billion-parameter models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240617/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained LLM (LLaMA-1 or LLaMA-2 family, 7B–70B) and a small calibration set of 128 randomly sampled 2048-token sequences from WikiText2. It produces a fully quantized model using per-channel weight quantization and per-token activation quantization at W4A4, W4A8, or W6A6 bitwidths. The low-rank correction weights are merged into the frozen weights and re-quantized (quant(W+AB)), so the final model incurs zero additional inference overhead beyond the small channel reassembly operations.

## Method

QLLM combines a gradient-free adaptive channel reassembly with an efficient low-rank error correction. Channel reassembly first disassembles outlier channels into T sub-channels (dividing their magnitude by T, where T = ⌈max|x_M|/θ⌉), then merges T−1 similar channels back via bipartite soft matching to restore the original channel count; an adaptive grid-search strategy selects the optimal threshold θ per layer by minimizing the Frobenius-norm reconstruction error between original and reassembled outputs. For error correction, QLLM freezes the quantized model and introduces two low-rank matrices A∈R^{M×r} and B∈R^{r×N} (r=4) into each projection layer, training only these parameters by minimizing block-wise reconstruction error over 4 consecutive Attention-FFN blocks. The reduced parameter count enables multi-block reconstruction on a single A100-80G GPU, and after training the low-rank weights are absorbed via quant(W+AB) with sequential reconstruction to limit error accumulation.

## Results

On the headline W4A4 quantization of LLaMA-2-70B, QLLM achieves 58.62% average zero-shot accuracy (across PIQA, ARC-e, ARC-c, HellaSwag, WinoGrande) and 7.95 average perplexity (WikiText2/C4), outperforming the previous SOTA OmniQuant by 7.89% in accuracy. For LLaMA-1-65B W4A4, QLLM reaches 59.83% average accuracy vs. 56.41% for OmniQuant (+3.42%). QLLM quantizes the 4-bit LLaMA-2-70B model within 10 hours on a single A100-80G GPU, and its inference throughput (6385 tokens/s for 7B) incurs only ~4% overhead over a standard W4A4 baseline while achieving a 1.96× speedup over FP16.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SmoothQuant (SQ), QLLM achieves a large margin on W4A4: for LLaMA-2-70B, QLLM scores 58.62% average accuracy vs. SQ's 48.64% (+9.98%), and for LLaMA-1-65B, 59.83% vs. 46.21% (+13.62%). Against OmniQuant, QLLM leads by 3.42% on LLaMA-1-65B W4A4 (59.83% vs. 56.41%) and by a dramatic 19.84% on LLaMA-2-70B W4A4 (58.62% vs. 38.78%), though the latter comparison is partially confounded by OmniQuant's incompatibility with grouped-query attention. Against Outlier Suppression+ (OS+), QLLM gains 7.89% on LLaMA-2-70B W4A4 (58.62% vs. 50.73%). Notably, QLLM also surpasses the QAT method LLM-QAT+SQ by 8.6% on LLaMA-1-7B W4A4 (51.84% vs. 43.24%), demonstrating that the PTQ approach can outperform full quantization-aware training in the extreme low-bit regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

QLLM departs from the dominant outlier-handling paradigm in LLM quantization (SmoothQuant, Outlier Suppression+, OmniQuant), which shifts activation magnitudes to weights via mathematically equivalent transformations. Instead, QLLM redistributes outlier magnitudes *across* channels through a novel channel disassembly-assembly pipeline, repurposing the bipartite soft matching and feature-averaging mechanism from token merging (Bolya et al., ICLR 2023) to the quantization setting. For error correction, it adapts the LoRA low-rank parameterization (Hu et al., ICLR 2022) but applies it to frozen quantized weights with a key distinction: the learned low-rank factors are absorbed back into the quantized weights post-training, incurring zero inference overhead—unlike QLoRA or INT2.1 which retain full-precision residuals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QLLM addresses the central bottleneck in deploying LLMs at 4-bit precision: activation outliers that cause catastrophic quantization error. The channel reassembly approach is gradient-free and the low-rank error correction reduces GPU memory enough to quantize a 70B model on a single A100, making low-bit deployment practically accessible. A key limitation is the additional runtime inference cost from disassembly/assembly operations (though only ~4% over W4A4), which the authors acknowledge could be mitigated via kernel fusion. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-Transformer architectures, whether the adaptive threshold search scales to even larger models, and whether the channel reassembly can be made fully kernel-fused to eliminate the residual overhead.
