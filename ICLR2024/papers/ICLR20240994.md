## Problem

Despite extensive work on calibration via loss functions and training frameworks, the calibration properties of neural network architectures themselves remain poorly understood, with prior studies limited to a handful of hand-designed models. This paper asks whether calibration can be generalized across datasets, whether robustness metrics proxy calibration, how reliable different calibration metrics are, and which architectural designs favor calibration—questions that require evaluating a large, diverse architecture space rather than a few isolated models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240994/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The study consumes 117,702 pre-trained CNN architectures from NATS-Bench (6,466 topology + 32,768 size variants) and 11 Vision Transformers, all trained on 32×32 RGB images from CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet-16-120. For each model it produces 102 calibration measurements: 90 bin-based metrics (ECE, ECE_em, cwCE, cwCE_em) across 9 bin sizes (5–500) before and after temperature scaling, plus 12 additional metrics (KSCE, MMCE, Brier score, NLL, OoD AUC on CIFAR-10-C and SVHN, adversarial robustness). The resulting dataset is released as a benchmark for NAS calibration research.

## Method

The paper leverages NATS-Bench's exhaustive search space—where each cell is a 4-node, 6-edge DAG with operations {1×1 conv, 3×3 conv, 3×3 avg-pool, skip, zero} and channel widths from 8 to 64—to systematically evaluate calibration across 117,702 unique architectures without retraining. Calibration is assessed using Kendall ranking correlation matrices to probe cross-dataset generalizability, metric reliability, robustness-as-calibration, and post-hoc temperature scaling effects. A key analytical tool is the proposed Harmonic Calibration Score (HCS_β), which balances accuracy and ECE via a harmonic-mean formulation to identify well-calibrated architectures without sacrificing prediction quality. Architectural analysis of top-HCS models reveals preferences for 1×1 or 3×3 convolutions on specific edges and a unanimous skip connection on edge 4, while width experiments show ECE surging from 3% to 8% on ImageNet as channel counts increase from 8 to 64.

## Results

Across 117,702 NATS-Bench architectures evaluated on 102 calibration measurements, the study finds that calibration rankings do not generalize across datasets: Kendall correlation between ECE on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 is near zero, and even negative for top-1000 models on ImageNet. Post-hoc temperature scaling does not uniformly improve calibration—the Kendall correlation between pre-ECE and post-ECE is nearly zero, meaning well-calibrated models can lose their ranking after scaling. Among high-accuracy models (>90%), a positive accuracy–calibration correlation emerges, but this disappears across all TSS models. Wider models (64 channels) show ECE rising from ~3% to ~8% on ImageNet, while the proposed Harmonic Calibration Score (HCS) identifies architectures preferring 1×1/3×3 convolutions on edges 1, 5, 6 and skip connections on edge 4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an empirical benchmark study rather than a method paper, so there are no external method baselines to compare against. Internally, the paper compares 90 bin-based and 12 additional calibration metrics (ECE, ECE_em, KSCE, MMCE, NLL, Brier score, cwCE, cwCE_em, KDECE, etc.) and finds that most metrics show consistent Kendall ranking correlations, with the notable exception of cwCE_em, which is negatively correlated with other widely accepted metrics on ImageNet. Robustness metrics (PGD adversarial accuracy, corruption accuracy, OoD AUC) correlate strongly with ECE only among top-ranked models but lose correlation as lower-accuracy models are included. The study also compares calibration before and after temperature scaling across bin sizes (5–500), showing that post-ECE is far more sensitive to bin size (quintupling at 500 bins) than pre-ECE. Among the 11 Vision Transformers included for generality, similar cross-dataset non-generalization and post-hoc non-uniformity patterns are observed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the NATS-Bench search space (Dong et al., 2021) — originally designed for NAS algorithm benchmarking — as a systematic substrate for calibration property exploration, a use case the original authors did not envision. It departs from prior calibration studies (Guo et al., 2017; Minderer et al., 2021) that examined only a handful of hand-designed architectures (ResNet variants, MLP-Mixer, ViT) by scaling to 117,702 unique CNNs plus 11 Vision Transformers, enabling statistical conclusions that were previously intractable. The post-hoc calibration protocol relies on temperature scaling from Guo et al. (2017), and the metric suite draws on the full landscape of bin-based (ECE, ECE_em, cwCE), kernel-based (MMCE), and statistics-based (KSCE) measures from the calibration literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset (117,702 networks × 102 measurements across three datasets) fills a critical gap: no prior work has provided a large-scale, reproducible benchmark for studying calibration as a function of architecture, dataset, and metric choice. The finding that calibration rankings do not transfer across datasets (even CIFAR-10 vs. CIFAR-100) and that temperature scaling reshuffles model rankings nearly completely challenges the common practice of selecting or comparing models based on a single calibration score. Limitations include the restriction to image classification, the exclusive use of temperature scaling as the post-hoc method, and the purely empirical (non-theoretical) nature of the conclusions, leaving open questions about whether these patterns hold for other post-hoc techniques, other modalities, or under different training regimes.
