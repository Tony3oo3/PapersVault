## Problem

Open-source text-to-image diffusion models like Stable Diffusion lag behind black-box generators (e.g., Midjourney) in visual quality, resolution handling, and compositional fidelity. Previous Stable Diffusion versions suffered from fixed square output sizes, cropping artifacts from random training crops, and a relatively small UNet backbone that limited representational capacity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240076/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt encoded by two text encoders (CLIP ViT-L and OpenCLIP ViT-bigG) plus scalar conditioning signals for original image size, crop offset, and target aspect ratio, all injected via Fourier feature embeddings added to the UNet timestep embedding. It produces images at approximately 1024² pixel area in multiple aspect ratios (multiples of 64 px), with an optional second pass through a refinement model that re-noises and denoises the base latents to sharpen local details. In multimodal mode, a CLIP image embedding replaces the pooled text embedding, enabling text-controlled image editing from a single input image.

## Method

SDXL scales the UNet to 2.6B parameters with a heterogeneous transformer block distribution [0, 2, 10] that shifts computation to lower-resolution feature levels, and conditions on dual text encoders whose penultimate outputs are concatenated along the channel axis (2048-dim context). Two novel micro-conditioning signals—original image dimensions and random crop coordinates—are embedded via Fourier features and added to the timestep embedding, eliminating the need to discard small training images or suffer cropping artifacts. The model is trained in three stages (256², 512², then multi-aspect ~1024² with offset noise), followed by an optional SDEdit-style refinement model specialized on the first 200 noise scales that re-noises and denoises base-model latents to improve high-frequency detail. A from-scratch autoencoder trained at batch size 256 with EMA further improves latent-space reconstruction fidelity over prior Stable Diffusion VAEs.

## Results

In a user preference study, SDXL with the refinement stage achieves a 48.44% win rate, substantially outperforming SDXL base (36.93%), Stable Diffusion 1.5 (7.91%), and Stable Diffusion 2.1 (6.71%). On class-conditional ImageNet at 512², the proposed size-conditioning reduces FID-5k from 39.76 (no conditioning) to 36.53 and improves IS-5k from 211.50 to 215.34. The improved autoencoder achieves PNSR 24.7, SSIM 0.73, LPIPS 0.88, and rFID 4.4 on COCO2017, outperforming both SD 1.x (PNSR 23.4, rFID 5.0) and SD 2.x (PNSR 24.5, rFID 4.7) autoencoders.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baselines are Stable Diffusion 1.5 and 2.1, the two preceding versions of the same model family. In the user preference study, SDXL with refinement dominates both: 48.44% vs. 7.91% (SD 1.5) and 6.71% (SD 2.1) win rates, a roughly 6× and 7× margin respectively. Even without the refinement stage, SDXL base (36.93%) still outperforms both prior versions by a wide margin. On the quantitative ImageNet size-conditioning experiment, the proposed conditioning technique improves FID-5k by 3.23 points (39.76 → 36.53) and IS-5k by 3.84 points (211.50 → 215.34) over the no-conditioning baseline, while the data-discarding baseline (CIN-512-only) degrades severely (FID 43.84, IS 110.64) due to overfitting on only 70k images. The authors note that standard text-to-image metrics (FID, CLIP score) do not reflect SDXL's improvements over prior Stable Diffusion versions, consistent with findings by Kirstain et al. (2023), making the user study the most informative comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

SDXL is a direct architectural extension of the Stable Diffusion latent diffusion model (Rombach et al., 2021), scaling the UNet backbone from ~860M to 2.6B parameters by redistributing transformer blocks to lower feature levels following Hoogeboom et al. (2023). The refinement stage adapts SDEdit (Meng et al., 2021) and the eDiff-I expert-denoiser design (Balaji et al., 2022), while the pooled text-embedding conditioning is borrowed from GLIDE (Nichol et al., 2021). The paper's most original contributions are the micro-conditioning techniques (size and crop parameters via Fourier feature encodings) and multi-aspect-ratio finetuning, which are lightweight additions that require no extra supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SDXL demonstrated that an open-source model could match the perceptual quality of closed systems like Midjourney, significantly lowering the barrier to high-quality text-to-image generation and enabling a large ecosystem of downstream applications. The micro-conditioning and multi-aspect training techniques are simple, reusable, and have been widely adopted in subsequent diffusion models. Key open questions remain: the two-stage base-plus-refiner pipeline doubles memory and inference cost, text rendering is still weak, and the authors note that transformer-dominated architectures (DiT, UViT) showed no immediate benefit at this scale, leaving the optimal architecture for very large diffusion models unresolved.
