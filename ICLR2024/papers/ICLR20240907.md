## Problem

Existing RLHF-based language models require complete retraining whenever new human preferences or queries are introduced, which is impractical due to computational cost and data privacy constraints. The paper addresses the stability-plasticity dilemma in continual RLHF: how to acquire new preference knowledge while retaining previously learned alignment, without the inefficiency and instability that vanilla PPO exhibits on shifting data distributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240907/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (e.g., a Reddit post or SHP question) and generates a text response (summary or answer) that is scored by a continually updated reward model. In the continual setting, the policy is initialized from the previous task's model and trained only on the current task's prompts and reward model, with historical data assumed inaccessible. The reward model itself is continually trained via MAS regularization, and a separate reference PM (trained on all preference data) provides an external alignment score for evaluation.

## Method

CPPO extends PPO by introducing sample-wise balance weights α(x) and β(x) that modulate the clipped policy gradient and a knowledge retention penalty, respectively. Rollout samples are classified into five types (high-performance, overfitting, high-variance, noisy, normal) based on whether their generation probability and reward score fall inside or outside a discriminant interval defined by mean ± k·std. The knowledge retention penalty replaces the full-vocabulary KL divergence with an L2 distance on log generation probabilities of the sampled tokens, avoiding the need to store the entire vocabulary distribution. Two weight-determination strategies are provided: a heuristic version using linear gain/decay formulas, and a learnable version that optimizes 2N per-batch parameters via SGD to satisfy the inequality constraints derived from the weighting strategy. The theoretical objective is derived from a principled formulation that maximizes reward on high-probability generations while penalizing divergence from the old policy only on high-reward samples.

## Results

On the Reddit TL;DR continual summarization task (2-task DIL setting), CPPO (Learn) achieves the best reference PM score of 3.180 on Task-1 and 3.085 on Task-2, with a final rPMS of 3.207 across the entire test set. Notably, CPPO is the only method that exhibits negative Score Forgetting Ratio (SFR = −0.161), indicating backward transfer where learning Task-2 actually improves performance on Task-1. The best CL baseline (HN-PPO) reaches rPMS1=2.859 and final rPMS=2.846, while vanilla PPO achieves only rPMS1=2.629 and final rPMS=2.612.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPPO (Learn) outperforms all eight baselines on both rPMS1 and rPMS2. Against the strongest CL baseline HN-PPO (rPMS1=2.859, rPMS2=2.868, final rPMS=2.846), CPPO (Learn) gains +0.32 on rPMS1, +0.22 on rPMS2, and +0.36 on final rPMS. PPO+EWC (rPMS1=2.833, final rPMS=2.801) and PPO+TFCL (rPMS1=2.867, final rPMS=2.842) are the next-best regularization-based baselines, both trailing CPPO by 0.3–0.4 in rPMS. The critical differentiator is SFR: all baselines show positive SFR (0.15–0.21, i.e., forgetting), while both CPPO variants achieve negative SFR (−0.16), demonstrating backward transfer. NLPO (rPMS1=2.784) and Policy Consolidation (rPMS1=2.692) are notably weaker, with NLPO's token-masking strategy and PC's behavioral distillation failing to match CPPO's sample-adaptive weighting. On the Stanford Human Preferences 3-task benchmark (Llama-7B), CPPO (Learn) similarly leads with AA=0.890 and AIA=0.894, outperforming PPO (AA=0.874) and approaching Iterated RLHF (AA=0.879) at far lower computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends PPO-based RLHF (Stiennon et al., 2020; Ouyang et al., 2022) into a continual learning setting, drawing on regularization-based CL methods (EWC, MAS, LwF, TFCL) and policy consolidation (Kaplanis et al., 2019) as baselines. The key conceptual departure is replacing the uniform KL-divergence knowledge retention penalty with a sample-wise weighting strategy that categorizes rollouts into five types based on joint generation probability and reward, assigning different policy-learning (α) and knowledge-retention (β) weights to each. Theoretically, the paper derives the weighted objective as a generalization of an indicator-function objective over high-probability/high-reward sample subsets, and replaces the computationally expensive full-vocabulary KL divergence with a lightweight L2 distance on log-probabilities of generated tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CPPO addresses a practical gap: real-world LMs must adapt to shifting human preferences (e.g., evolving policy stances, domain-specific norms) without the prohibitive cost and privacy risks of full retraining. The method is task- and model-agnostic, adds negligible compute overhead (weight learning is ~1/50th of rollout time), and achieves backward transfer (negative SFR), meaning learning a new domain can actually improve performance on old ones. Limitations include evaluation primarily on summarization with small models (gpt2-xl, gptj-6B), only 2–3 task sequences, and an acknowledged over-optimization risk against the reward model. Open questions include whether the sample-wise weighting strategy transfers to larger LLMs (70B+), online (non-offline) continual settings, and whether the five-category taxonomy generalizes beyond text generation.
