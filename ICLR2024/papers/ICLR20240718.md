## Problem

Synaptic delays are known to greatly increase the expressivity of spiking neural networks and are natively supported on neuromorphic hardware, yet efficient algorithms to learn these delays jointly with weights in deep feedforward SNNs have been lacking. Prior approaches either restricted themselves to single-layer networks or relied on imprecise finite-difference gradient estimates for delay parameters, making it unclear whether learned delays genuinely outperform fixed random ones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240718/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes spatio-temporally binned spike trains (700→140 input neurons, Δt=10 ms) derived from audio via artificial cochlear models for SHD and SSC, or Mel spectrograms with 140 frequency bins and ~100 timesteps for the non-spiking GSC benchmark. Output is a class label over 20 spoken digits (SHD) or 35 speech commands (SSC/GSC), computed by summing softmax-normalized membrane potentials of infinite-threshold readout LIF neurons across all time steps. A key structural detail is that each synapse between consecutive layers is represented as a 1D temporal convolution kernel of size T_d (250 or 300 ms) containing exactly one non-zero weight at the learned delay position.

## Method

The core insight is a mathematical equivalence between a synaptic delay d_ij and a 1D temporal convolution kernel with a single non-zero element at position T_d − d_ij − 1, allowing delays to be learned via standard backpropagation. To make the discrete delay position differentiable, each kernel is replaced during training by a Gaussian centered at the delay position with a shared standard deviation σ that is exponentially decreased from T_d/2 to 0.5 over training, transitioning from capturing long-range temporal dependencies to refining precise delay and weight values. At inference, the Gaussian is converted back to a sparse discrete kernel (one non-zero per synapse), compatible with neuromorphic hardware. The architecture is a simple feedforward SNN with 2–3 fully connected hidden layers of LIF neurons (surrogate arctangent gradient), batch normalization, and dropout, trained with Adam using separate learning rates for weights (0.001) and delays (0.1) under a one-cycle/cosine-annealing schedule. The method is implemented in PyTorch/SpikingJelly and leverages automatic differentiation end-to-end.

## Results

On the Spiking Heidelberg Dataset (SHD), DCLS-Delays (2L-1KC) achieves 95.07±0.24% top-1 accuracy with only 0.2M parameters and no recurrent connections, outperforming the previous best (RadLIF, 94.62% with 3.9M params). On Spiking Speech Commands (SSC), the 3L-1KC variant reaches 80.29±0.06% with 1.2M params, surpassing RadLIF (77.40%, 3.9M params). On the harder GSC-35 benchmark (35 classes), the 3L-2KC model achieves 95.35±0.04% with 2.5M params, beating RadLIF (94.51%, 1.2M params) and the dense convolution delay baseline (93.19%, 19M params).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCLS-Delays outperforms all 13 compared baselines across all three benchmarks. On SHD, it beats the closest competitor RadLIF (Bittar & Garner, 2022) by 0.45% (95.07% vs. 94.62%) while using 19.5× fewer parameters (0.2M vs. 3.9M) and no recurrent connections; it also surpasses the two delay-learning methods from Sun et al. (2023a,b) by ~2.5% and the multi-delay approach of Patiño-Saucedo et al. (2023) by ~4.6%. On SSC, the advantage over RadLIF widens to 2.89% (80.29% vs. 77.40%), and the method exceeds the recurrent baselines of Cramer et al. (2022) and Perez-Nieves et al. (2021) by roughly 30 and 23 percentage points respectively. On GSC-35, DCLS-Delays (3L-2KC) edges out RadLIF by 0.84% (95.35% vs. 94.51%) despite using 2.1× more parameters, and surpasses the non-spiking MSAT baseline (He et al., 2023) by 8.02%. Notably, the paper's own dense convolution delay ablation (19M params) underperforms DCLS-Delays (1.2M params) on both SSC and GSC-35, confirming that the sparse learnable-spacing formulation is more effective than exhaustive delay enumeration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Dilated Convolution with Learnable Spacings (DCLS), originally proposed for increasing receptive fields in 2D vision CNNs, to learn synaptic delay positions in deep feedforward SNNs. The key conceptual bridge is the mathematical equivalence between 1D temporal convolutions and connection delays, which allows standard backpropagation to compute exact gradients with respect to delays—unlike prior deep-SNN delay learning (SLAYER-based methods by Sun et al.) that relied on finite-difference approximations. Earlier delay-learning work (Wang et al. 2019; Grimaldi & Perrinet 2022/2023; Zhang et al. 2020) was restricted to single-layer or shallow SNNs, while Patiño-Saucedo et al. (2023) used fixed multi-delay connections with pruning rather than continuous delay optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on three temporal benchmarks (SHD, SSC, GSC-35) with fewer parameters and no recurrent connections, producing sparse discrete kernels at inference that are directly compatible with programmable-delay neuromorphic hardware (Loihi, TrueNorth, SpiNNaker, SENeCA). The ablation revealing that fixed random delays nearly match learned delays in fully connected networks—but not under sparse connectivity—provides an important practical guideline: delay learning matters most when the number of synapses is limited relative to the temporal range. Open questions include whether the approach extends to convolutional or recurrent SNN architectures, whether alternative kernel functions (beyond Gaussian) improve performance, and how the method behaves in online or continual learning settings where the decreasing-σ schedule may not apply.
