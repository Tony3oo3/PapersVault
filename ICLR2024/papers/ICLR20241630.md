## Problem

Prior self-rationalization work relies on very large LMs (≥100B parameters) to produce quality rationales and evaluates them solely by downstream task accuracy, ignoring whether the rationales themselves are plausible, consistent, and diverse. This paper asks whether small LMs (~200× smaller than GPT-3) can be trained to generate rationales that are simultaneously better on task performance and on intrinsic quality properties, without requiring human-written rationale labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241630/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a multiple-choice question (from StrategyQA, QuaRel, OpenBookQA, NumerSense, or QASC) concatenated with a sequence of control tokens, each encoding a binned reward level for a specific property (plausibility, diversity, consistency, task correctness). It produces a free-text rationale followed by the predicted answer in I-RO format. At inference, the highest-reward control tokens are prepended to elicit the best-quality generation; the control tokens are the key mechanism that makes the output quality controllable at generation time.

## Method

MARIO extends QUARK's single-reward control-token framework to a multi-reward setting. A small T5-LARGE model is first initialized via supervised fine-tuning on GPT-3-generated silver rationales (filtered for correct answers), then iteratively trained with a multi-reward objective: for each of four properties (plausibility via VERA, consistency via a trained M_QR/M_Q pair, diversity via n-gram uniqueness, and task correctness), generated samples are independently scored, binned into 5 (or 2 for accuracy) reward levels, and assigned a distinct control token. Two variants are explored—CLASSIC (all property tokens present simultaneously) and ADDITIVE (properties introduced sequentially in order of SFT weakness). The training objective combines cross-entropy on the binned data with a KL-divergence penalty against the SFT reference model, preventing degenerate generations. This allows the small LM to learn to optimize all quality dimensions jointly at inference by conditioning on the top-reward token for each property.

## Results

On five QA datasets (StrategyQA, QuaRel, OpenBookQA, NumerSense, QASC), MARIO achieves the best average Normalized Relative Gain (NRG) across all four metrics (Task Accuracy, Plausibility, Diversity, Consistency) compared to all baselines. The largest gains are on StrategyQA where ADDITIVE MARIO reaches 65.07% accuracy (vs. 57.64% SFT) and 63.27 Avg. NRG (vs. 58.66 SFT), and on NumerSense where CLASSIC MARIO reaches 55.28% accuracy (vs. 46.23% SFT) and 69.95 Avg. NRG (vs. 66.18 SFT). Rationale quality metrics also improve: e.g., Consistency on StrategyQA goes from −0.02 (SFT) to 0.04 (ADDITIVE MARIO), and Plausibility on QASC goes from 0.44 (SFT) to 0.47 (CLASSIC MARIO). Human evaluation confirms MARIO rationales are preferred over SFT rationales for 66.6%–83.15% of instances across datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MARIO (both CLASSIC and ADDITIVE variants) outperforms all four baselines—SFT, PRODUCT, FILT-ACC (a STAR variant), and FILT-ALL—on average NRG across all five datasets, with the largest margin on StrategyQA (63.27 vs. 60.34 for the best baseline) and NumerSense (69.95 vs. 68.32). Against the much larger reference LMs, the 0.7B MARIO model beats FLAN-T5-L (same size but instruction-finetuned) on most metrics across all datasets, and beats FLAN-T5-XL and LLAMA-7B on several datasets (e.g., MARIO achieves 79.89% accuracy on QuaRel vs. 76.99% for FLAN-T5-XL and 56.70% for LLAMA-7B). However, MARIO still trails GPT-3 (175B), FLAN-T5-XXL, and LLAMA-65B on task accuracy and plausibility, particularly on StrategyQA (65.07% vs. 69.0% GPT-3) and OpenBookQA (66.06% vs. 85.94% GPT-3). The paper also shows that optimizing solely for task accuracy (FILT-ACC) does not naturally improve rationale quality, motivating the multi-reward approach: human annotators prefer MARIO rationales over FILT-ACC rationales for 69.65% of OpenBookQA questions.

## Relation to Prior Work

MARIO extends QUARK (Lu et al., 2022), a reinforcement-learning-like framework that uses control tokens to condition generation on a single reward, into a multi-reward setting where multiple quality properties (plausibility, diversity, consistency, task correctness) are optimized concurrently. It sits at the intersection of self-rationalization (Wiegreffe et al., 2021; Narang et al., 2020), chain-of-thought prompting (Wei et al., 2022), and knowledge distillation from large LMs, but departs from prior work by treating rationale quality as an explicit multi-objective optimization target rather than a byproduct of downstream accuracy. The silver-rationale distillation from GPT-3 provides a warm start, while the iterative QUARK-style sampling-and-binning loop refines the small model along each reward axis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 0.7B T5-LARGE can produce rationales that are human-preferred over SFT baselines and competitive with models 10–250× larger, suggesting that rationale quality need not scale with parameter count when multi-reward optimization is applied. However, the method's dependence on GPT-3 silver rationales for initialization, the limited set of three well-automated reward properties, and the observed reward-hacking phenomena (repetitive or incomplete rationales when rewards are weak or incompatible) leave open the question of how to extend this framework to harder-to-score properties like factuality and completeness. The findings on reward hacking and the insufficiency of task accuracy as a proxy for rationale quality are practically important cautions for anyone building rationale-generation pipelines.
