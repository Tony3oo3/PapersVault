## Problem

Existing diffeomorphic mesh deformation methods for cortical surface reconstruction rely on Chamfer distance (CD) as the optimization objective, which suffers from pseudo-metricity, quadratic computational complexity, no theoretical guarantee for the number of sampled points, and a tendency to get trapped in local minima. This paper addresses these limitations by reformulating mesh comparison through the lens of optimal transport on probability measures, replacing CD with sliced Wasserstein distance (SWD).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241364/cropped_page1_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D brain MRI volume as input and produces a high-resolution 3D triangle mesh of the white matter cortical surface. The pipeline first extracts an initial surface via a U-Net segmentation mask and Marching Cubes, then deforms it via a neural ODE. For the oriented varifold representation used in the loss, each mesh face is encoded as a 6D vector (face barycenter concatenated with unit normal), so the SWD comparison operates in R^6 rather than R^3.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-part framework: (1) representing triangle meshes as probability measures in three forms—continuous (area-weighted over faces), empirical (random point sampling), and discrete via oriented varifold (area-weighted Dirac masses at face barycenters with normals); and (2) using sliced Wasserstein distance (SWD) as the deformation loss, which has O(m log m) time complexity versus O(m²) for CD and is a valid metric with a proven m^{-1/2} convergence rate that is independent of dimension (Theorem 1). The deformation is modeled by a neural ODE where an MLP takes concatenated point features and multi-scale spatial cube features sampled from the MRI volume to predict velocity fields, with L=100 random projections and p=2 for the SWD. The oriented varifold representation empirically outperforms both empirical point sampling and CD-based optimization, achieving near-zero self-intersection (<10^{-4}%) while maintaining superior geometric accuracy across ADNI and OASIS datasets.

## Results

On the ADNI dataset, DDOT achieves the best geometric accuracy across all metrics: EMD of 0.728 mm (Left WM) and 0.702 mm (Right WM), SWD of 0.420 mm and 0.365 mm, and ASSD of 0.202 mm and 0.205 mm, outperforming the previous SOTA CortexODE (EMD 0.803/0.782 mm). On OASIS, DDOT similarly leads with EMD 0.418/0.429 mm, SWD 0.779/0.770 mm, and ASSD 0.161/0.160 mm. Most notably, DDOT achieves a self-intersection face ratio below 10⁻⁴%, which is 100× better than CortexODE (0.004–0.032%) and orders of magnitude better than Vox2Cortex (0.72–1.02%). On the TRT consistency test, DDOT outperforms Vox2Cortex and CortexODE in EMD and ASSD, with only FreeSurfer achieving a slightly better SWD score.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDOT outperforms all four learned baselines (DeepCSR, Vox2Cortex, CFPP, CortexODE) on every geometric accuracy metric (EMD, SWD, ASSD, CN) across both ADNI and OASIS datasets. Against the closest competitor CortexODE, DDOT improves EMD by ~9% on ADNI (0.728 vs. 0.803 mm Left WM) and ~2% on OASIS (0.418 vs. 0.425 mm), while reducing self-intersection from 0.004–0.032% to below 10⁻⁴% — a 100× improvement. Vox2Cortex, which uses GNN-based deformation without diffeomorphic guarantees, shows the highest self-intersection rates (0.72–1.02%) and is consistently outperformed on all metrics. DeepCSR, an implicit-surface method, avoids self-intersections via Marching Cubes but lags significantly on geometric accuracy (e.g., EMD 1.368 vs. 0.728 mm on ADNI Left WM). On the TRT consistency test, DDOT beats Vox2Cortex and CortexODE in EMD and ASSD, though FreeSurfer achieves a marginally better SWD (0.358 vs. 0.403 mm), indicating that the learned method's consistency is competitive with the classical pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: diffeomorphic mesh deformation via neural ODEs (CortexODE, CorticalFlow), efficient optimal transport metrics (sliced Wasserstein distance), and geometric measure theory (oriented varifolds). All prior diffeomorphic deformation methods for cortical surface reconstruction rely on Chamfer distance as their optimization objective, which the authors identify as prone to local minima and lacking theoretical guarantees on sample count. The key departure is reframing mesh comparison as a problem over probability measures—first as continuous surface measures, then as empirical point-cloud measures, and finally as discrete oriented varifold measures—and replacing Chamfer distance with sliced Wasserstein distance, which admits a closed-form 1D solution and linear computational complexity. The authors claim to be the first to exploit oriented varifolds as discrete probability measures within a learning-based deformation framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in mesh deformation learning: Chamfer distance's quadratic complexity and tendency to produce suboptimal point assignments, which manifests as self-intersecting faces and non-uniform surface coverage. The theoretical contribution (Theorem 1) showing that SWD on empirical measures converges at rate m^{-1/2} independent of dimension is a meaningful improvement over Wasserstein's m^{-1/d} curse of dimensionality, making the approach scalable to high-dimensional vertex features. However, the method is currently validated only on cortical surface reconstruction, and the authors acknowledge that the deterministic support count tied to mesh resolution introduces stochastic memory during training. Broader applicability to other 3D deformation tasks (e.g., shape correspondence, generative modeling) remains an open question that the authors explicitly flag for future work.
