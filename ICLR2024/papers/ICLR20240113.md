## Problem

Regularization-based continual learning methods rely on fixed Hessian approximations computed at the end of each task, which become increasingly inaccurate as parameters drift. Meta-CL methods implicitly approximate the Hessian online via hypergradient computation, enabling timely adaptation, but suffer from high variance due to random sampling from a limited memory buffer. This paper bridges these two families for the first time, showing that Meta-CL is a Hessian approximation technique, and then addresses its variance problem to achieve both timely and accurate second-order information.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet) as a single-pass sequential stream in mini-batches of size 32 (ResNet-18) or 10 (PcCNN), with an equal-sized replay batch drawn from a memory buffer of configurable size (200–1000 samples). At each iteration, the model produces updated parameters via a momentum-corrected hypergradient, and at test time outputs class labels under either online CL (task index known) or class-incremental (task index unknown) evaluation protocols.

## Method

VR-MCL augments the standard Meta-CL hypergradient update with a momentum-based variance reduction term: ĝ_b = g_b + r(ĝ_{b−1} − g_{b−1}), where g_b and g_{b−1} are hypergradients computed at the current and previous parameters using the same memory buffer sample ε_b, and r is a momentum ratio (set to 0.25). The key theoretical result (Proposition 3) shows this is equivalent to replacing the raw online Hessian H_M with a regularized version H_VR whose inverse eigenvalues are v_b^{-1} + r(v̂_{b−1}^{-1} − v̄_{b−1}^{-1}), which suppresses excessive updates along directions where the Hessian was erroneously estimated as low-curvature. The paper further proves a near-optimal Õ(√T) regret bound under standard smoothness and boundedness assumptions, and demonstrates that the momentum-based approach outperforms alternatives like simply increasing replay batch size or adding naive SGD momentum.

## Results

On Seq-CIFAR10 (5 tasks, 2 classes each), VR-MCL achieves 66.97% AAA and 56.48% final accuracy with a reduced ResNet-18 backbone and |M|=1000, outperforming the strongest baselines DER++ (64.22/52.29) and ER-OBC (65.82/54.85) by ~2.7% and ~1.6% in AAA/Acc respectively. On the longer Seq-CIFAR100 (10 tasks, 10 classes each), VR-MCL reaches 27.01% AAA and 19.49% Acc, surpassing CLSER (25.46/17.88) and ER-OBC (25.54/17.21). On Seq-TinyImageNet (20 tasks, 10 classes each), VR-MCL attains 21.26% AAA and 13.27% Acc, leading ER-OBC (20.11/11.51) and CLSER (19.43/11.09). VR-MCL also substantially outperforms the Meta-CL baselines La-MAML (42.98/33.43 on CIFAR10) and MER (50.99/36.92), confirming that variance reduction in the hypergradient is the key improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VR-MCL consistently outperforms all 14 baselines across all three datasets on both AAA and Acc metrics. Against the strongest non-meta baselines, VR-MCL leads DER++ by 2.75% AAA and 4.19% Acc on Seq-CIFAR10, and ER-OBC by 1.15% AAA and 1.63% Acc on Seq-CIFAR100. Among Meta-CL methods, VR-MCL improves over La-MAML by 23.99% AAA and 23.05% Acc on Seq-CIFAR10, and over MER by 15.98% AAA and 19.56% Acc, demonstrating that the momentum-based variance reduction is the critical factor. Against regularization-based methods (On-EWC, IS), VR-MCL gains over 28% AAA on Seq-CIFAR10, confirming that online Hessian estimation with variance reduction substantially outperforms fixed Hessian approximations. In the imbalanced CL setting (Table 4), VR-MCL also surpasses methods specifically designed for imbalance (CBRS, Coresets) by 4–6% Acc, and on the smaller PcCNN backbone (Table 5) it leads the best baseline CLSER by 2.85% AAA and 4.03% Acc.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate strands of continual learning—regularization-based methods (EWC, On-EWC, KFLA, IS) and Meta-CL methods (MER, La-MAML)—under a common Hessian approximation framework, showing that Meta-CL's hypergradient computation is equivalent to an implicit online Hessian estimate. Building directly on La-MAML's bi-level optimization formulation and the momentum-based variance reduction technique of Cutkosky & Orabona (2019), the authors adapt the latter to the online CL setting where full-batch gradients are unavailable, yielding VR-MCL. The key departure from prior work is the recognition that Meta-CL's random memory buffer sampling introduces high variance into its implicit Hessian, and that a simple momentum correction can be interpreted as a regularizing penalty on that Hessian.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conceptual bridge between Meta-CL and regularization-based methods provides a clearer design principle for future CL algorithms: the quality of the implicit or explicit Hessian approximation directly governs the forgetting–plasticity trade-off. Practically, VR-MCL offers a drop-in variance reduction that requires no full-batch access, making it applicable to truly online settings where SAG/SVRG-style methods fail. Open questions include whether the approach extends to larger-scale benchmarks (e.g., ImageNet-scale CL), whether the fixed momentum ratio r=0.25 is optimal across all regimes, and how the method interacts with parameter-efficient or architecture-adaptive CL strategies.
