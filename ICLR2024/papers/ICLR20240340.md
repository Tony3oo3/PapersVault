## Problem

Standard adversarial training (AT) forces models to fit one-hot labels on adversarially perturbed inputs, but these hard labels are noisy because they fail to reflect the shifted data distribution, causing robust overfitting and poor generalization. Existing remedies—label smoothing, consistency regularization, and knowledge distillation—either assume uniform label noise, require temporal output consistency, or depend on a separately pre-trained robust teacher model. The paper asks whether one can design a well-calibrated, data-driven training target that mimics realistic annotation noise without any pre-trained resources.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet-200) and produces a class prediction over C classes. The key distinction is that the training target is not the original one-hot vector but a rectified soft probability distribution P(x_i) that assigns non-zero mass to non-true classes, reflecting inter-class similarity. This soft target is used both to guide the PGD adversarial perturbation (replacing the hard label in the inner max) and to compute the cross-entropy loss in the outer min, so the model is trained to match a calibrated distribution rather than a single class.

## Method

ADR maintains an exponential moving average (EMA) copy of the student weights (Mean Teacher scheme) and uses its output as a self-distillation signal. The EMA logits are passed through a softmax with a temperature τ that is cosine-annealed from a high value (producing near-uniform outputs early in training) to a low value (producing sharper, more informative distributions later). This softened EMA distribution is then interpolated with the one-hot ground truth using a factor λ that is cosine-annealed from low to high, progressively trusting the EMA more as training proceeds. A per-sample correction clips λ_i downward whenever the EMA's top-predicted class differs from the true class, guaranteeing the true class always retains the highest probability in the rectified target. The resulting soft label P(x_i) replaces the hard label in both the PGD attack step and the training loss, requiring only one extra forward pass per iteration and no pre-trained model.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, AT+ADR achieves 50.38% AutoAttack robust accuracy (vs. 48.81% for PGD-AT) and TRADES+ADR reaches 51.02% (vs. 50.10% for TRADES). On the more challenging CIFAR-100, AT+WA+AWP+ADR attains 28.50% robust accuracy, surpassing all prior methods without extra data. On TinyImageNet-200, TRADES+ADR achieves 19.17% robust accuracy, a 1.82% gain over TRADES alone. ADR also reduces the robust overfitting gap (Best vs. Final checkpoint difference) from 5.62% to 3.20% on CIFAR-10 and from 5.29% to 2.64% on CIFAR-100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADR consistently outperforms PGD-AT (Madry et al., 2018) by 1.4–1.9% robust accuracy across all three datasets, and improves upon TRADES (Zhang et al., 2019) by 0.5–1.8%. Against consistency-regularization baselines (Zhang et al., 2022; Dong et al., 2022b), ADR achieves higher robust accuracy on CIFAR-100 (28.50% vs. 26.03% and 26.30% with ResNet-18) without requiring a pre-trained teacher. On CIFAR-100 with WRN-34-10, AT+ADR reaches 31.60% AutoAttack, surpassing LTD (Chen & Lee, 2021; 30.59%), LAS-AT (Jia et al., 2022; 30.77%), and Cui et al. (2021; 31.20%) without extra data, and 32.19% with DDPM data, exceeding Sehwag et al. (2022; 31.15%). In the knowledge distillation comparison (Table 8), ADR (28.50%) outperforms ARD (Goldblum et al., 2020; 25.65%), RSLAD (Zi et al., 2021; 26.70%), and IAD (Zhu et al., 2022; 25.84%) on CIFAR-100. The only case where ADR does not clearly dominate is TRADES+ADR combined with WA+AWP on CIFAR-10, where the robustness gain saturates (50.59% vs. 51.25% for TRADES+WA+AWP), attributed to TRADES already using soft targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on PGD-AT (Madry et al., 2018) as the base adversarial training framework and repurposes the Mean Teacher EMA mechanism (Tarvainen & Valpola, 2017) from semi-supervised learning as a self-distillation signal for generating soft training labels. It departs from prior label-rectification approaches—label smoothing (uniform noise), consistency regularization (Temporal Ensembling, Mean Teacher), and knowledge distillation (requiring a pre-trained robust teacher)—by introducing annealed softmax temperature and a per-sample interpolation factor that progressively trusts the EMA model, eliminating the need for any external teacher. The empirical analysis of robust model output distributions (calibration on OOD data, uncertainty on misclassified examples, clean-vs-adversarial consistency) provides the conceptual motivation that unifies these design choices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ADR offers a minimal-overhead (~2–4% extra compute), plug-and-play label-softening technique that consistently narrows the robust overfitting gap and achieves state-of-the-art robust accuracy on CIFAR-100 and TinyImageNet-200 without requiring additional data or pre-trained models. A key open question is the dataset-dependent tuning of temperature τ and interpolation λ, which the authors acknowledge limits generalizability; automatic parameter scheduling is identified as future work. The observation that ADR's gains saturate when combined with TRADES on smaller-class datasets (CIFAR-10) suggests diminishing returns when multiple smoothing mechanisms are stacked, an important practical caveat for practitioners.
