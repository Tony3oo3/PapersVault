## Problem

Offline RL methods face out-of-distribution extrapolation errors that are particularly severe in sparse-reward and scarce-data settings. Pessimism-based methods (e.g., CQL) suffer from compounded Bellman bootstrapping errors under sparse rewards, while DICE-based methods (e.g., OptiDICE) produce arbitrarily large importance ratios when the dataset's state-action support is narrow, causing unstable training. No existing approach explicitly bounds the stationary distribution density in unseen regions while retaining the behavior-agnostic, low-variance estimation of marginal importance sampling.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-collected offline dataset of (s, a, r, s') transitions and an initial state distribution ρ₀, and produces a tanh-squashed Gaussian policy π_θ(a|s). A key structural detail is that the OOD region is defined per-state by a radius Δa (estimated from the behavioral cloning policy's standard deviation) around in-distribution actions, and the method samples n=5 OOD actions per state from a uniform distribution over this region. Policy extraction occurs only after value function convergence (warm-up of 20k–40k steps), decoupling the two optimization phases to avoid compounded errors from interleaved actor-critic updates.

## Method

CDE formulates offline policy optimization as an f-divergence-regularized stationary distribution problem with an explicit density constraint d^π(s,a) ≤ εμ(s,a) on OOD state-actions, where μ is a uniform distribution over unseen actions. To resolve the support mismatch inherent in marginal importance sampling, it introduces a mixed proposal distribution d̂^D = ζd^D + (1−ζ)μ, guaranteeing the proposal covers the target distribution. The inner maximization over importance ratios admits a closed-form solution w* = (f′)⁻¹(Ã/α) using soft-chi² divergence (making (f′)⁻¹ = ELU+1), and the OOD constraint yields a provable upper bound w* ≤ ε̃ on the concentrability coefficient without assuming bounded concentrability. Training proceeds in two decoupled phases: (1) convex optimization of V-value v_φ and regularized advantage Ã_φ via weighted regression with OOD advantages clamped to αf′(ε̃), and (2) policy extraction by minimizing KL divergence between the learned policy and a mixed behavior policy, weighted by the optimal importance ratios.

## Results

On D4RL sparse-reward tasks, CDE achieves the highest normalized score on 9 out of 11 tasks, with particularly large margins in the Maze2D domain (e.g., 210.0 on maze2d-large vs. 155.7 for OptiDICE and 58.0 for IQL). On sparse-MuJoCo success rates, CDE reaches 97.0% on walker2d-medium-expert and 95.2% on halfcheetah-medium-expert, matching or exceeding all baselines. In the scarce-data setting (1% of trajectories), CDE maintains high rewards while OptiDICE and other baselines undergo sharp performance drops, demonstrating robustness to data scarcity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDE decisively outperforms AlgaeDICE on all 11 tasks (e.g., 210.0 vs. -0.1 on maze2d-large), attributed to AlgaeDICE's high-variance policy gradient updates. Against OptiDICE, CDE wins on 9/11 tasks with the largest gap on maze2d-large (210.0 vs. 155.7), and the advantage widens dramatically in scarce-data settings where OptiDICE's importance ratios become unbounded. CDE beats CQL on most tasks but loses on door-human (7.7 vs. 9.9) and halfcheetah-medium success rate (82.0% vs. 97.6%), where CQL's value regularization is more effective. Against IQL, CDE wins on 8/11 tasks; IQL is competitive on expert Adroit tasks (relocate-expert: 102.7 vs. 102.6) and halfcheetah-medium-expert (95.4% vs. 95.2%). BCQ outperforms CDE only on pen-expert (114.9 vs. 105.0), while TD3+BC is consistently outperformed across all domains. On goal-conditioned tasks, CDE achieves the highest reward on 3/5 tasks (FetchReach: 29.1, FetchPick: 27.7, HandReach: 17.0) compared to GoFAR, GCSL, and WGCSL.

## Relation to Prior Work

CDE unifies two previously separate lines of offline RL: pessimism-based value regularization (CQL, BCQ, IQL) and DICE-style stationary distribution correction via marginal importance sampling (AlgaeDICE, OptiDICE, GenDICE). Its key conceptual departure is applying the pessimism mechanism in the stationary distribution space rather than the Q-value space, using a mixed proposal distribution (data + OOD) to resolve the support mismatch that causes unbounded importance ratios in standard DICE. The method inherits the behavior-agnostic, closed-form structure of DICE while adding an explicit density constraint on OOD state-actions that yields an automatically bounded concentrability coefficient, something prior DICE methods could only assume.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CDE provides a principled, theoretically grounded way to handle the OOD extrapolation problem that plagues offline RL in sparse-reward and data-scarce settings, with the notable practical result of maintaining high performance at 1% data where baselines collapse. The automatic bounding of the concentrability coefficient without assuming it is a useful theoretical contribution for the broader offline RL literature. However, the method requires strict alignment of initial state distributions between training data and deployment, limiting its applicability to settings like antmaze where this fails. Extending the framework to goal-conditioned RL or distribution-shifted initial states remains an open direction.
