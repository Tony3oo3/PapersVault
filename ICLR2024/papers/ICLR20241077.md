## Problem

Protein complex modelling (PCM) for multi-chain assemblies faces two critical challenges: the combinatorial optimization space of N^(N-2) assembly actions makes exhaustive search intractable, and the scarcity of training data for large complexes (fewer than 100 PDB entries beyond 10 chains) causes severe distribution shift that limits generalization. Existing assembly-based methods like MoLPC rely on non-deep-learning tree search that lacks generalization, while end-to-end predictors like AlphaFold-Multimer degrade sharply beyond 9 chains.

## Input / Output

GAPN takes as input the amino acid sequences of all N chains (encoded via ESM pre-trained residue embeddings averaged to chain-level vectors) and pre-computed dimeric 3D structures for every chain pair (either ground-truth or AlphaFold-Multimer predicted). It outputs a sequential assembly action set—a sequence of N−1 docking pairs (A_k^1, A_k^2)—which, combined with the rotation-translation transformations extracted from the dimer structures, yields the final assembled 3D complex structure. The method is not limited by chain count and can handle up to 60 chains in under 2 minutes.

## Method

GAPN formulates PCM as a Markov Decision Process where a policy network (GAPN) sequentially selects docking pairs via an attention mechanism: a context vector g_t derived from docked-chain embeddings and the full complex embedding is inner-producted with undocked-chain embeddings to produce a softmax action distribution. The key innovation is an adversarial reward function built on a GCN-based discriminator trained in a GAN framework to distinguish ground-truth assembly graphs from policy-generated ones; the discriminator's output (−R(π_θ, D_φ)) is added to the domain-specific reward (negative RMSD) to guide the policy toward globally consistent assembly patterns across varied chain numbers. Training uses PPO with a learned MLP value network, alternating between discriminator updates (Adam on graph classification) and policy updates (clipped surrogate objective). The adversarial reward specifically addresses the data-scarcity generalization problem by encoding global assembly rules from all chain-number regimes into a single discriminator, enabling the policy to transfer structural knowledge across scales.

## Results

GAPN achieves state-of-the-art performance across all chain number ranges (3–30) on both TM-Score and RMSD. For small-scale multimers (3≤N≤10), GAPN improves mean TM-Score by 8.7% and mean RMSD by 11.0% over the second-best baseline. For large-scale multimers (11≤N≤30) with ground-truth dimers, GAPN achieves ~31% improvement in mean TM-Score and ~53% in median TM-Score over MoLPC. Inference efficiency is dramatically improved: GAPN requires only 0.85 sec/chain versus 540 sec/chain for MoLPC (~600× speed-up), and can predict up to 60-chain complexes in under 2 minutes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAPN outperforms all five baselines across every chain-number range where comparisons are possible. Against the strongest assembly-based baseline MoLPC, GAPN achieves up to 27% higher TM-Score (e.g., 0.95 vs 0.89 at N=3; 0.62 vs 0.45 at N=11–15) while being ~600× faster in inference (0.85 vs 540 sec/chain). Against the end-to-end deep learning baselines AF-Multimer and ESMFold, GAPN leads by a wide margin on large complexes (N≥11) where AF-Multimer degrades sharply (TM-Score drops to 0.39 at N=11–15) and ESMFold is not applicable. The older assembly methods Multi-LZerD and RL-MLZerD are limited to N≤10 and are consistently outperformed by GAPN by 10–40+ points in TM-Score. Notably, GAPN is the only method that handles all chain numbers from 3 to 30 (and up to 60 in practice) without requiring exhaustive search or plDDT guidance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the assembly-based protein complex modelling (PCM) setting introduced by MoLPC (Bryant et al., 2022b), which used Monte Carlo tree search to predict docking paths, by replacing the non-deep-learning search with a deep reinforcement learning agent (PPO-based policy network). It draws on the broader RL-for-combinatorial-optimization literature (pointer networks, graph RL) and repurposes the GAN framework (Ho et al., 2016) as an adversarial reward mechanism, where a GCN-based discriminator encodes global assembly rules across chain numbers. The key conceptual departure from prior PCM work is the explicit treatment of the N^(N-2) combinatorial space as an MDP with an adversarial generalization signal, rather than relying on exhaustive or heuristic search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 600× inference speedup over MoLPC and the ability to handle up to 60 chains make large-complex assembly practically feasible for routine structural biology workflows, where prior methods were prohibitively slow. The adversarial reward's demonstrated role in bridging the data-scarcity gap for large complexes (ablation in Fig. 4) suggests a general recipe for RL agents facing distribution shift across problem scales. Open questions include whether the approach extends beyond 60 chains, whether the GCN discriminator can be replaced by more expressive graph transformers, and whether the adversarial reward transfers to other sequential assembly problems (e.g., nucleic acid complexes).
