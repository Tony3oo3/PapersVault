## Problem

Existing value-based RL algorithms treat Q-values as deterministic functions and focus on point estimates, neglecting the inherent stochasticity in agent-environment interactions and the need for uncertainty quantification. While the Kalman Temporal Difference (KTD) framework addresses this by modeling parameters as random variables, its reliance on Extended or Unscented Kalman Filters introduces O(np²) computational complexity, O(p²) memory overhead for covariance matrices, and accuracy degradation from linearizing nonlinear value functions.

## Input / Output

At each time step, the algorithm consumes a minibatch of n transition tuples (state, action, reward, next state, next action) generated by the current policy, optionally drawn from a replay buffer of finite capacity R. The deep neural network parameters are initialized from a mixture Gaussian prior with a sparsity-inducing component, and the pseudo-population size N serves as a tempering hyperparameter controlling the optimization-to-sampling trade-off. Outputs include posterior samples of network parameters from which Q-value point estimates (sample averages) and calibrated 95% prediction intervals are derived, along with the induced policy for action selection.

## Method

The paper reformulates RL as a state-space model where the state evolution equation incorporates a prior density π(θ) via a Langevin drift term, and applies the variance splitting technique to convert the nonlinear Bellman residual measurement equation into a linear one. The Langevinized Ensemble Kalman Filter (LEnKF) then performs forecast-analysis updates in a single chain with K inner iterations per time step, achieving O(np) per-iteration complexity by exploiting the special structure of the observation matrix H_t. A key innovation is the pseudo-population size N, which scales the stochastic gradient noise to prevent the invariant distribution from degenerating to a delta function, thereby framing RL as a sampling problem rather than pure optimization. The paper proves W₂-convergence of the posterior samples to a stationary distribution under mild dissipativity and Lipschitz conditions, and extends the theory to off-policy settings with replay buffers, showing that the gradient bias from stale transitions vanishes asymptotically.

## Results

On the Indoor Escape Environment, LKTD (N=10000) achieves a Q-value MSE of 0.00020 (East) and 0.00022 (North), outperforming DQN (0.109/0.086), QR-DQN (0.0064/0.0058), and KOVA (0.0058/0.0053) by 2–3 orders of magnitude. For uncertainty quantification, LKTD's 95% prediction interval coverage rate reaches 94.6%, closely matching the nominal level, while DQN achieves only 41.1%, BootDQN 38.0%, and KOVA 24.1%. Computationally, LKTD requires ~1.3 ms per parameter update (comparable to DQN's ~1.8 ms), whereas KOVA requires 44–251 ms due to Jacobian computation and matrix inversion. On OpenAI Gym benchmarks (CartPole-v1, Acrobot-v1, LunarLander-v2, MountainCar-v0), LKTD consistently achieves higher training and evaluation rewards than both DQN and QR-DQN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DQN, LKTD reduces Q-value MSE by roughly 500× (0.0002 vs. 0.086–0.109) and improves 95% prediction-interval coverage from 41% to 94.6%, while also exploring multiple optimal policies (equal probability for N and E actions) where DQN locks onto a single policy. Against BootDQN, the MSE improvement is similar (~500×) and coverage rises from 38% to 94.6%. Against QR-DQN, LKTD achieves ~30× lower MSE (0.0002 vs. 0.006) and a substantially higher coverage rate (94.6% vs. 85.7%), indicating that QR-DQN's quantile-based distributional approximation does not yield valid frequentist intervals. Against KOVA (the EKF-based KTD baseline), LKTD reduces MSE by ~30×, improves coverage from 24% to 94.6%, and is approximately 33× faster per iteration (1.3 ms vs. 44 ms at batch size 100), since LKTD avoids Jacobian computation and O(p²) covariance storage. On the four OpenAI Gym environments, LKTD outperforms DQN in both training and evaluation reward across all 100 replicates, and slightly exceeds QR-DQN, with the gap most pronounced in CartPole-v1 where a single misstep terminates the episode.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the Kalman Temporal Difference (KTD) framework (Geist & Pietquin, 2010; Shashua & Mannor, 2020) with Stochastic Gradient MCMC (SGLD, Welling & Teh, 2011) via the Langevinized Ensemble Kalman Filter (LEnKF, Zhang et al., 2023). Prior KTD methods (KOVA, UKF-based KTD) rely on EKF/UKF linearization, incurring O(np²) compute and O(p²) memory for the covariance matrix; LKTD replaces this with a particle-based forecast-analysis loop at O(np) per iteration. The introduction of a pseudo-population size N to prevent degeneration of the invariant distribution is a novel reformulation that bridges the optimization and sampling perspectives of RL, and the variance-splitting state augmentation converts the nonlinear measurement equation into a linear one without explicit Jacobian computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LKTD provides the first KTD-based algorithm with provable Wasserstein convergence to the correct posterior for deep Q-networks, yielding prediction intervals that actually achieve nominal coverage (≈95%)—a property that DQN, BootDQN, and KOVA all fail to deliver. The O(np) complexity and particle-based memory footprint make Bayesian value tracking feasible at deep-network scale, and the replay-buffer extension (Theorem 2) shows asymptotic consistency is preserved off-policy. Open questions include whether the framework scales to high-dimensional continuous-action spaces (e.g., MuJoCo, Atari), how sensitive performance is to the pseudo-population size N in practice, and whether the dissipativity assumptions (C4) hold for arbitrarily deep architectures without careful prior design.
