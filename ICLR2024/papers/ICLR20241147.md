## Problem

In federated learning, each client must train on its full local dataset before uploading updates to the server, which is computationally expensive and communication-heavy. While Bayesian coresets can approximate full-data posterior inference from a small weighted subset in centralized settings, and personalized FL methods exist for client-specific adaptation, no prior work integrates Bayesian coreset selection into a personalized federated learning pipeline. This paper addresses how to select representative coreset data points per client such that the resulting personalized model generalizes nearly as well as full-data training, with provable guarantees.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a local dataset of images (28×28 grayscale for MNIST/Fashion-MNIST, 32×32 RGB for CIFAR-10, and X-ray/retinal images for medical datasets) partitioned in a non-IID fashion across N clients. The method additionally learns a sparse coreset weight vector w_i per client with L0-norm constraint ‖w_i‖₀ ≤ n_k (e.g., n_k = 50% of n), where non-zero weights identify representative data points. Outputs include per-client variational distributions q^i(θ) over neural network parameters (Gaussian mean-field), a global variational distribution z(θ), and class predictions for evaluation.

## Method

CORESET-PFEDBAYES embeds Bayesian coreset optimization within the PFEDBAYES personalized federated learning framework by introducing a novel joint objective (Eq. 8) that simultaneously minimizes the KL divergence between the coreset-weighted client variational distribution q^i(θ;w) and the full-data client distribution q^i(θ), and the L²(π̂) norm difference between the coreset-weighted data likelihood and the full-data likelihood, subject to sparsity constraints on w. The sparse coreset weight optimization is solved via Accelerated Iterative Hard Thresholding (A-IHT), while the variational distributions are optimized using reparameterized stochastic gradient descent with minibatch Monte Carlo sampling. The server aggregates client updates using a momentum-based weighted average with coefficient β, and the overall system is formulated as a bi-level optimization (client-side variational inference nested within server-side aggregation). Theoretical analysis proves the average generalization error is minimax optimal up to logarithmic factors in the coreset size n_k, with the excess error over full-data PFEDBAYES bounded by a closed-form function 𝓘(w, n_k) of the coreset weights and size.

## Results

On MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and CIFAR-10 with 10 clients in a non-i.i.d. setting, CORESET-PFEDBAYES (k=50%) achieves 96.3% global accuracy on MNIST, 92.7% on Fashion-MNIST, and 71.5% on CIFAR-10, outperforming PFEDBAYES trained on randomly sampled 50% data by +3.97%, +10.37%, and +10.66% respectively on global model accuracy. Notably, CORESET-PFEDBAYES with only 50% of the data surpasses PFEDBAYES trained on the full dataset on CIFAR-10 (71.5% vs. 64.40% global accuracy). On medical imaging datasets (COVID-19, APTOS, OCTMNIST) with 2 clients, CORESET-PFEDBAYES (50%) achieves classwise accuracies of 0.932/0.919/0.871 on COVID-19 and 0.921/0.894/0.886 on APTOS, outperforming all submodular-based subset selection baselines (LogDet, DispSum, DispMin) on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PFEDBAYES (Zhang et al., 2022) with 50% random sampling, CORESET-PFEDBAYES wins decisively on global accuracy: +3.97% on MNIST (96.3% vs. 92.33%), +10.37% on Fashion-MNIST (92.7% vs. 82.33%), and +10.66% on CIFAR-10 (71.5% vs. 60.84%). Against pFedMe (Dinh et al., 2020) trained on full data, CORESET-PFEDBAYES with only 50% data achieves higher global accuracy on Fashion-MNIST (92.7% vs. 84.41%) and CIFAR-10 (71.5% vs. 70.86%), though it trails on personal accuracy. Against FedAvg (McMahan et al., 2017) full-data, CORESET-PFEDBAYES matches or exceeds global accuracy on MNIST (96.3% vs. 92.39%) and Fashion-MNIST (92.7% vs. 85.42%) while using half the data. Against BNFed (Yurochkin et al., 2019), CORESET-PFEDBAYES dominates across all three datasets by large margins (e.g., 96.3% vs. 82.95% on MNIST global). Against perFedAvg (Fallah et al., 2020), CORESET-PFEDBAYES achieves competitive personal accuracy on Fashion-MNIST (89.55% vs. 88.51% at 50%) and CIFAR-10 (69.66% vs. 69.61% at 50%), while also providing a global model that perFedAvg does not report.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes two previously separate research threads: Bayesian coreset construction (Campbell & Broderick, 2018; Campbell & Beronov, 2019; Zhang et al., 2021) and personalized federated learning via variational Bayesian inference (PFEDBAYES, Zhang et al., 2022b). While prior coreset work operates in centralized settings where the full dataset is available, and prior FL work (including client-selection approaches like Balakrishnan et al., 2021) operates at the client granularity, this paper introduces data-level coreset weights within each client's local training loop. The A-IHT solver from Zhang et al. (2021) is adopted for the non-convex sparse regression subproblem, and the Gaussian mean-field variational framework from PFEDBAYES provides the closed-form KL divergence structure that makes the joint objective tractable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that intelligent per-client data selection can reduce communication rounds by roughly 50% while maintaining or improving accuracy over random subsampling, which is relevant for bandwidth-constrained FL deployments. However, the evaluation is limited to small-scale benchmarks (MNIST, FashionMNIST, CIFAR-10) with simple fully-connected architectures and at most 10 clients, and the medical experiments use only 2 clients, leaving open whether the gains persist at scale or with deeper models. The authors themselves flag robustness to skewed client distributions, adversarial attacks, and the interplay with differential privacy as important open directions. The theoretical minimax-optimality-up-to-log-bounds result, while clean, inherits its assumptions (equal-width networks, bounded parameters, Hölder smoothness) from the PFEDBAYES analysis and may not transfer directly to modern architectures.
