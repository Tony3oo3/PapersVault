## Problem

Masked autoencoders require a target representation (teacher network) to guide reconstruction, and prior methods each adopt different teachers (DALL-E, HOG, CLIP, pixels, EMA-updated networks) without systematic justification. This paper asks whether the careful design of the target representation is actually necessary, or whether multi-stage distillation renders the teacher choice irrelevant.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241333/cropped_page4_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 RGB images, splits them into non-overlapping 16×16 (or 14×14 for ViT-H) patch tokens, and masks 75% of patches for the student while the teacher receives the full image. The student (asymmetric encoder-decoder) outputs patch representations projected to match the teacher's embedding dimension, and the reconstruction target is the teacher's output on the unmasked patches. Training uses Smooth L1 loss; for distillation from larger teachers, images are resized to 196×196 to align output lengths.

## Method

dBOT performs masked knowledge distillation in multiple stages (2 for classification, 3 for dense prediction), where at each breakpoint the student's weights are copied to the teacher (momentum m=0) and the student is re-initialized (m=1 otherwise). The key empirical finding is that after 2–3 stages, students distilled from fundamentally different teachers (supervised DeiT, contrastive DINO, autoregressive DALL-E, autoencoding MAE, or a randomly initialized network) converge to nearly identical downstream performance, with variance dropping from 2.24 to 0.04 on classification. This eliminates the need for an extra pre-training stage to obtain a teacher. The architecture follows MAE's asymmetric encoder-decoder for the student and a vanilla ViT with intact input for the teacher, with the target being raw patch tokens without Layer Normalization.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K classification, dBOT achieves 84.5%, 86.6%, 87.4%, and 88.0% top-1 fine-tuning accuracy with ViT-B/16, ViT-L/16, ViT-H/14, and ViT-H/14 (448px) respectively, outperforming MAE by 0.9%, 0.7%, 0.5%, and 0.2% at each scale. For dense prediction, dBOT reaches 52.7 and 56.0 AP^box on COCO object detection, and 49.5 and 54.5 mIoU on ADE20K semantic segmentation with ViT-B and ViT-L, surpassing all prior self-supervised methods by nontrivial margins. In semi-supervised settings, dBOT outperforms MAE by 1.7 and 1.4 points at 1% and 10% label fractions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

dBOT consistently outperforms all compared self-supervised baselines across all model scales and downstream tasks. Against the strongest prior method MAE, dBOT gains +0.9% (ViT-B) to +0.5% (ViT-H) on ImageNet-1K classification, +2.1 AP^box (ViT-B) and +2.0 AP^box (ViT-L) on COCO detection, and +1.4 mIoU (ViT-B) and +0.9 mIoU (ViT-L) on ADE20K segmentation. Compared to data2vec (84.2%, 86.2%), dBOT leads by +0.3% and +0.4% on classification. Against iBOT (84.0%, 85.2%), the gap widens to +0.5% and +1.4% at ViT-B and ViT-L. BEiT (83.2%, 85.2%) and DINO (83.6%) trail dBOT by 1.3–1.4% and 0.9% respectively on ViT-B. The largest margin is over the supervised baseline (82.3%, 82.6%, 83.1%), where dBOT leads by 2.2–4.3% depending on model size, confirming that the bootstrapped-teacher distillation strategy with random initialization surpasses both supervised and prior self-supervised pre-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the masked autoencoder (MAE) framework and the broader masked image modeling (MIM) paradigm, specifically the subcategory the authors term Masked Knowledge Distillation (MKD), where a parameterized teacher network generates the reconstruction target. It departs from prior MKD methods (BEiT, MaskFeat, iBOT, data2vec) by demonstrating that the teacher's identity—whether it comes from supervised, contrastive, autoregressive, or autoencoding pipelines—is irrelevant after multi-stage distillation, and that a randomly initialized teacher suffices. The proposed dBOT pipeline unifies the offline-teacher (m=1) and online-teacher (m∈(0,1)) paradigms into a discrete multi-stage bootstrapping schedule (m=0 at breakpoints, m=1 otherwise), eliminating the need for an extra pre-training stage to produce the teacher.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that target representation design is unnecessary under multi-stage distillation simplifies the MIM pre-training recipe and removes a major hyperparameter search dimension, potentially lowering the barrier to entry for practitioners. However, the paper acknowledges a tension: when the teacher is trained on substantially richer data (e.g., CLIP with 400M image-text pairs), multi-stage distillation on ImageNet-1K alone underperforms single-stage distillation from that teacher, suggesting the 'teacher doesn't matter' conclusion is bounded by data availability. Open questions include whether the convergence behavior holds for non-ViT architectures, for tasks beyond ImageNet-scale pre-training, and whether the multi-stage schedule can be further compressed without sacrificing the convergence guarantee.
