## Problem

Existing inverse design methods that backpropagate through learned surrogate models suffer from over-optimization into adversarial modes, producing design parameters that perform poorly under ground-truth simulation. Additionally, these methods cannot generalize to design spaces more complex than training data—such as longer trajectories, more interacting bodies, or multi-part boundary shapes—because the surrogate has no inherent measure of data likelihood to prevent out-of-distribution drift.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241176/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-trained diffusion models (U-Net backbone) over subsets of the joint design variable z = U_{[0,T]} ⊕ γ, a differentiable design objective J, and Gaussian noise as initialization. For the airfoil task, the boundary γ is a 64×64×3 tensor (12,288 dimensions) encoding a binary mask and relative offsets, while the fluid state U_t is a 64×64×3 grid of velocity and pressure. The output is a jointly optimized boundary shape and initial state (no autoregressive rollout is needed), which is then validated by a ground-truth solver (Lily-Pad for airfoils, PyMunk for N-body) at higher resolution (128×128) than training.

## Method

CinDM reformulates inverse design as energy optimization: a diffusion model trained with standard denoising loss on the joint distribution of trajectories and boundaries implicitly defines an energy function E_θ whose gradient is the denoising network ε_θ. At inference, Langevin dynamics jointly minimizes E_θ (enforcing physical consistency and preventing adversarial drift) and the design objective J, eliminating the need for autoregressive surrogate rollout. The key compositional mechanism composes N pre-trained energy functions over overlapping subsets z_i ⊂ z at test time—overlapping time windows for longer trajectories (Eq. 7), pairwise body interactions for more objects (Eq. 8), or spatial parts for multi-component boundaries (Eq. 9)—with the sampling step averaging denoising gradients across all N models. This allows a single 24-step, 2-body, single-airfoil model to generalize to 54-step, 8-body, and multi-airfoil designs without retraining, exploiting shared local physical structure while permitting globally novel configurations.

## Results

On the N-body time composition task (2-body, 24–54 steps), CinDM achieves the best MAE across all settings (0.0126, 0.0092, 0.0096, 0.0106), outperforming the strongest baseline (Backprop, U-Net) by up to 36.0% in MAE. On the object composition task (4-body and 8-body), CinDM improves design objective by 15.6% and MAE by 53.4% over the best baseline on average. On the 2D multi-airfoil task, CinDM achieves a lift-to-drag ratio of 2.177 (1 airfoil) and 1.422 (2 airfoils), substantially exceeding all baselines (best baseline: 1.434 and 1.057 respectively), and discovers formation flying that reduces drag by 53.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CinDM is compared against four families of baselines: CEM (cross-entropy method with GNS or U-Net), Backprop (backpropagation through surrogate models, Allen et al. 2022), NABL (neural adjoint with boundary loss), and cINN (conditional invertible neural network). On the N-body time composition task, CinDM outperforms all baselines in MAE by 15.8% on average and matches or beats the best design objective (Backprop, U-Net) at 24 steps (0.1160 vs. 0.1228) while remaining competitive at longer horizons. On the object composition task (4-body, 8-body), CinDM dominates all baselines by a wide margin, achieving 15.6% lower design objective and 53.4% lower MAE than the best baseline (Backprop, GNS). On the 2D airfoil task, CinDM achieves a lift-to-drag ratio of 2.177 for 1 airfoil (vs. best baseline 1.434 for CEM, LE-PDE) and 1.422 for 2 airfoils (vs. best baseline 1.057 for CEM, LE-PDE), a 66.1% improvement in the compositional case. The NABL and cINN baselines (Appendix H) also underperform CinDM significantly, with cINN being unable to handle the compositional 2-airfoil setting at all due to its fixed input dimensionality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant inverse design paradigm of backpropagating through learned surrogate/forward models (Allen et al., 2022; Wu et al., 2022a), which suffers from adversarial over-optimization because the surrogate lacks a data-likelihood signal. Instead, it reframes inverse design as joint energy optimization using a diffusion model trained on the joint distribution of trajectories and boundaries, where the denoising network simultaneously approximates dynamics and provides an implicit out-of-distribution penalty. The compositional mechanism draws directly from the product-of-experts and composable diffusion literature (Du et al., 2020b, 2023; Liu et al., 2022), but is the first application of such compositional generative models to inverse design, enabling test-time generalization to longer trajectories, more interacting bodies, and multi-part boundaries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The adversarial over-optimization problem in surrogate-based inverse design is a recognized practical bottleneck, and the energy-based diffusion formulation offers an elegant fix that also eliminates the costly autoregressive rollout inner loop. The compositional generalization (2→8 bodies, 24→54 steps, single→multi airfoil) is practically important for engineering design where test-time complexity routinely exceeds training complexity. However, the physical systems tested (bouncing balls, 2D incompressible flow) are relatively simple, and it remains open whether the approach scales to 3D Navier-Stokes, compressible flows, or truly novel physics. The emergent discovery of formation flying is compelling but demonstrated only in a 2D, low-Reynolds-number setting.
