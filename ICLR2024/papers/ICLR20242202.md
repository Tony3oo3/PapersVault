## Problem

Existing meta-learning methods parameterize the task-invariant prior either as a black-box neural network (e.g., RNNs) or as a preselected probability density function (e.g., Gaussian), both of which suffer from limited expressiveness and poor interpretability. Gaussian priors are inherently unimodal, symmetric, and log-concave, making them ill-suited for tasks with multimodal or asymmetric parametric distributions, while RNN-based priors offer no analytical guarantee of what has been learned. The paper asks whether one can learn a more flexible, data-driven prior that remains interpretable from an optimization perspective.

## Input / Output

For each task, the method consumes a small set of labeled RGB images (1 or 5 shots per class, e.g., 84×84 from miniImageNet) as training data and a single test image for prediction. The learned prior is encoded as a set of piecewise-linear-function (PLF) control points (ζ, ψ) shared across all tasks, operating on the 1-dimensional model parameter vector θ_t ∈ ℝ^d rather than on the image pixels directly. The output is a class label produced by a 4-layer CNN whose weights are adapted per task via K unrolled PGD steps using the learned PLFs.

## Method

MetaProxNet unrolls K iterations of proximal gradient descent into a multi-block neural network where each block consists of a data-consistency module (a GD step on the task loss) followed by a learnable PLF that approximates the proximal operator of an unknown optimal regularizer. Under the assumption that the regularizer factorizes across parameter dimensions, the d-dimensional proximal operator decomposes into d independent 1-D PLFs, each defined by C+1 learnable control points on a fixed uniform grid over [-A, A]. The PLF parameters are optimized via bilevel ERM: the inner loop runs K unrolled PGD steps per task to produce θ̂_t, and the outer loop updates the shared PLF parameters by descending the validation loss across a mini-batch of tasks. Theoretical analysis establishes tight O(1/C²) and O(1/C) approximation error bounds for smooth and continuous proximal operators respectively, providing a quantitative criterion for choosing the number of PLF pieces C.

## Results

On 5-class 1-shot miniImageNet, MetaProxNet+MC achieves 55.94% accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline MC (54.08%) by 1.86 points. On 5-class 5-shot miniImageNet, MetaProxNet+MC reaches 71.97%, surpassing MeTAL (70.52%) by 1.45 points. On TieredImageNet, MetaProxNet+MC attains 57.34% (1-shot) and 73.38% (5-shot), exceeding the best baselines KML (54.67%) and L2F (73.34%) respectively. MetaProxNet+MAML also consistently outperforms its MAML backbone by 2.9–7.0 points across all four settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetaProxNet+MC achieves the best accuracy across all four evaluation settings, beating the strongest per-setting baselines: MC (54.08%) on 1-shot miniImageNet by +1.86 pts, MeTAL (70.52%) on 5-shot miniImageNet by +1.45 pts, KML (54.67%) on 1-shot TieredImageNet by +2.67 pts, and L2F (73.34%) on 5-shot TieredImageNet by a marginal +0.04 pts. MetaProxNet+MAML outperforms its MAML backbone by 5.0 pts (1-shot miniImageNet), 7.0 pts (5-shot miniImageNet), 2.9 pts (1-shot TieredImageNet), and 1.5 pts (5-shot TieredImageNet), confirming that the unrolling-based prior is the key driver of improvement over the implicit Gaussian prior. Against RNN-based LSTM (Ravi & Larochelle, 2017), MetaProxNet+MC gains 12.5 pts on 1-shot miniImageNet. The ablation (Table 2) further isolates the contribution: replacing MAML's GD with PGD alone (PGD-Gaussian) yields negligible change, while adding the learnable PLF prior (MetaProxNet+MAML) produces a 4.9–5.0 pt jump, confirming that prior expressiveness—not the optimizer swap—drives the gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes algorithm unrolling (Gregor & LeCun, 2010; Monga et al., 2021), previously applied to signal reconstruction and learned optimizers, for a new purpose: learning task-invariant priors in meta-learning. It departs from two dominant paradigms—RNN-based meta-learners (Ravi & Larochelle, 2017) that lack interpretability, and pdf-based priors (Gaussian in MAML, sparse in ProtoNets) that are limited in expressiveness—by unrolling proximal gradient descent and replacing the fixed proximal operator with learnable piecewise linear functions. The bilevel optimization formulation follows the MAML lineage (Finn et al., 2017; Grant et al., 2018), but the key novelty is that the regularizer R is no longer tied to a preselected distribution; instead, its proximal operator is directly parameterized and learned, with tight approximation error bounds established for both smooth and non-smooth cases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MetaProxNet offers a principled middle ground between the black-box flexibility of RNN meta-learners and the limited expressiveness of parametric priors, with the added benefit of visualizable, optimization-grounded interpretability. The finding that learned PLFs naturally produce sparse-like behavior in shallow layers and linear-like behavior in deep layers—mirroring the practical 'gradual unfreezing' strategy—suggests the framework could inform fine-tuning protocols beyond few-shot classification. Open questions include whether the factorability assumption (Assumption 3.1) is sufficient for highly correlated parameter spaces, whether convergence guarantees for the bilevel problem can be established, and whether the approach extends to optimizers beyond PGD or to non-convex deep architectures with tied weights across unrolling steps.
