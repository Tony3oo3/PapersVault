## Problem

Local graph clustering methods rely solely on graph connectivity, ignoring additional node information such as attributes, text, or partial labels that are commonly available in practice. While global methods (e.g., GCNs, semi-supervised learning) exploit such information, they require processing the entire graph and are unsuitable for local clustering. This paper asks how noisy binary node labels—serving as an abstract proxy for any additional node information—can be incorporated into local diffusion-based clustering to improve target cluster recovery without accessing the full graph.

## Input / Output

The method takes an undirected graph G=(V,E), a set of seed nodes from the target cluster, noisy binary labels ỹᵢ∈{0,1} for each node (where 1 indicates target cluster membership with some fraction flipped), and a source mass parameter θ. It outputs a local cluster C = supp(x†(θ†)), the set of nodes with positive values in the flow diffusion solution. The procedure is strictly local: it only accesses nodes in the support of the solution and their neighbors, with running time O(d̄θ) independent of |V|, where d̄ is the maximum degree within the support. The label accuracy parameters a₀ and a₁ (fraction of correctly labeled nodes outside and inside K, respectively) must each be at least 1/2 for the theoretical guarantees to hold.

## Method

The core mechanism is a simple label-based edge weighting scheme: construct a weighted graph G^w where each edge (i,j) receives weight 1 if ỹᵢ = ỹⱼ and a small weight ε∈[0,1) if ỹᵢ ≠ ỹⱼ, then solve the ℓ₂-norm flow diffusion problem (minimizing the sum of squared edge flows subject to capacity constraints) on G^w rather than on G. The key insight is that this weighting attenuates boundary edges (connecting the target cluster to the rest of the graph) more than internal edges, because boundary edges are more likely to be cross-label edges, thereby reducing mass leakage during diffusion and yielding a tighter recovered cluster. Theoretically, under a localized stochastic block model, the paper proves that when label accuracy satisfies a₀ ≥ 1/2 and a₁ ≥ 1/2, flow diffusion on G^w with an appropriately chosen source mass θ† achieves a strictly higher F1 score than flow diffusion on the original graph G with any source mass. In practice, the noisy labels can be obtained by training a lightweight classifier (e.g., logistic regression) on node attributes using a small number of ground-truth samples, or heuristically from the top/bottom values of a preliminary diffusion solution, making the method applicable in both supervised and unsupervised settings.

## Results

On 6 real-world attributed graph datasets, Label-based Flow Diffusion (LFD) achieves an average F1 score of 62.5, outperforming the best baseline (FD multi-seed at 55.9) by +5.3 F1 points (+9.3% relative improvement). The largest gain is on Coauthor CS where LFD reaches 63.1 F1 versus 50.5 for FD multi-seed (+24.9% relative). Even with very noisy labels (classifier F1 as low as 40%), LFD still improves over standard flow diffusion by up to 13% F1. On synthetic data, LFD already outperforms FD at label accuracy a₀=a₁=0.7, where the labels alone achieve an F1 of only 0.2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LFD consistently outperforms all three baselines across all 6 datasets. Against standard Flow Diffusion (FD, Fountoulakis et al. 2020), LFD improves F1 by +5.3 on average (e.g., 63.1 vs. 50.5 on Coauthor CS, 72.9 vs. 62.8 on Coauthor Physics). Against Weighted Flow Diffusion (WFD, Yang & Fountoulakis 2023), which uses node attributes directly under a strong separability assumption, LFD achieves +5.3 average F1 improvement (62.5 vs. 49.1 single-seed, 62.5 vs. 54.1 multi-seed). Against ℓ₁-regularized PageRank (PR, Fountoulakis et al. 2017), the analogous Label-based PageRank (LPR) method achieves 64.7 average F1 versus 58.3 for PR single-seed (+8.2% relative). The gains are largest on datasets where the graph structure alone is less informative (Coauthor CS, Coauthor Physics) and smallest on Cora and Pubmed where the graph structure already provides strong cluster signal. Notably, LFD also outperforms a two-layer GCN (46.9 F1) by a wide margin (73.0 F1) while running 28× faster, highlighting the advantage of local methods in low-label regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the flow diffusion framework for local graph clustering (Fountoulakis et al., 2020; Chen et al., 2022) by incorporating noisy binary node labels as a general abstraction of all additional node information. It departs from the closest prior work, Yang & Fountoulakis (2023), which required strong separability assumptions on node attributes and a specific weighted-graph construction; here, the authors replace structured attributes with a single noisy label per node, making the method agnostic to the source and format of auxiliary information. The local random graph model and the ℓ₂-norm flow diffusion formulation are inherited directly from these two lines of work, while the label-based edge-weighting scheme (Eq. 4) is a new, minimal intervention on top of the existing diffusion machinery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical characterization of when noisy labels provably improve local clustering over the label-free graph, giving practitioners a concrete criterion (Theorem 3.4) tied to label accuracy and cluster connectivity. The practical finding that even a weak classifier (F1 ≈ 40%) yields up to 13% F1 improvement over standard diffusion across six real-world datasets makes the approach broadly applicable to any attributed graph where a cheap binary predictor can be trained locally. Open questions include determining an optimal ε (the gap between Conjectures 1 and 2 remains unresolved), extending the analysis to multi-class or soft labels, and understanding how the method behaves on graphs with heterogeneous degree distributions outside the target cluster.
