## Problem

Latent spaces produced by different neural networks are related by unknown, complex transformations, and prior work assumed a single class of invariance (e.g., angle-preserving/cosine) suffices to align them. This paper empirically demonstrates that the optimal transformation varies across architectures, datasets, seeds, and modalities, so no single projection function can reliably enable zero-shot model stitching. The gap addressed is how to construct a representation that captures multiple, simultaneously acting invariances without knowing a priori which transformation relates two given latent spaces.

## Input / Output

The method consumes latent representations from any encoder (pretrained foundation models such as ViT, BERT, ALBERT, CLIP, RexNet, or trained-from-scratch AEs/VAEs/GCNs) together with a fixed set of anchor points uniformly sampled from the training set. It produces a product space of invariant components—each a Relative Representation computed via a distinct similarity function (Cosine, Euclidean, L1, L∞)—whose total dimensionality remains constant regardless of the number of subspaces when using sum aggregation. This product representation is then passed to a frozen decoder for zero-shot classification or image reconstruction, with no additional training or fine-tuning at stitching time.

## Method

The core mechanism generalizes the Relative Representation (RR) framework by computing, for each sample, its similarity to a set of fixed anchors under multiple distance metrics simultaneously, yielding one invariant subspace per metric. Each subspace encodes a different invariance: Cosine to isotropic scaling and orthogonal transformations, Euclidean to orthogonal and translation, L1 to translation and permutation, and L∞ to translation and permutation. These subspaces are aggregated via a learnable function φ—most effectively an independent compact MLP (LayerNorm + Linear + Tanh) per subspace followed by element-wise summation—which preserves dimensionality while blending complementary invariances. The key insight is that the unknown transformation relating two latent spaces is too complex for any single similarity function, but the product of several invariant components approximates the underlying manifold where representations coincide. This enables zero-shot stitching across vision, text, and graph modalities with consistent accuracy gains over any single projection, validated across twelve pretrained models, nine benchmarks, and multiple architectures trained from scratch.

## Results

On zero-shot image classification stitching, the product of invariances (Cosine + Euclidean + L1 + L∞ with MLP+Sum aggregation) achieves 0.58 accuracy on CIFAR-100 with CViT-B/32 encoder, outperforming the best single projection (0.53 for Euclidean/L1) by 5 points. With RViT-B/16, the product reaches 0.81 on CIFAR-100 and 0.95 on CIFAR-10, again surpassing individual projections. On graph classification (CORA, GCN), the product achieves 0.77 accuracy with a stitching index of 1.00, meaning zero performance drop relative to end-to-end training, compared to 0.53 for Cosine alone (stitching index 0.71).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the product-of-invariances approach against four individual projection functions (Cosine, Euclidean, L1, L∞) that each enforce a single class of invariance. No single projection consistently dominates: on CIFAR-100 with CViT-B/32, Euclidean and L1 lead (0.53) while L∞ collapses to 0.27; on CORA (graph), Cosine leads (0.53) while Euclidean and L1 drop to ~0.27. The product of all four projections consistently achieves the highest or near-highest accuracy across all modalities and datasets, with the largest gains on CIFAR-100 (+5 points over best single) and CORA (+24 points over best single). The stitching index for the product on CORA reaches 1.00 (no performance drop vs. end-to-end), whereas individual projections range from 0.58 to 1.00. On text (TREC), L1 and the product tie at 0.65, showing the product does not sacrifice performance where a single invariance already suffices.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Relative Representation (RR) framework of Moschella et al. (2023), which projected latent spaces into a relative coordinate frame using cosine similarity to enforce invariance to angle-preserving transformations. The key departure is the observation that no single invariance class universally connects latent spaces across architectures, seeds, and datasets, motivating a generalization to a product space where each component uses a different similarity function (Cosine, Euclidean, L1, L∞) to capture distinct invariances. The aggregation of these invariant subspaces via learned or fixed merging strategies (MLP+Sum, Self-Attention, Concatenation) is novel relative to the single-projection RR formulation. The work also situates itself within the broader representation-similarity literature (CCA, SVCCA, CKA) and invariance-learning literature, but its specific contribution is the product-space construction applied to zero-shot stitching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the transformation class relating latent spaces depends on dataset, architecture, and training dynamics challenges the implicit assumption in prior RR work that angle-preserving transformations are the primary correlating factor, and provides a practical, training-free solution (product space of invariances) that works without knowing the optimal transformation a priori. The stitching index metric and the demonstration of zero performance drop in graph stitching are useful for the model-reuse community. Limitations include the requirement that similarity functions be analytically expressible (learned metrics are left as future work) and the observation that a smaller subset of invariant spaces may suffice, suggesting room for better space-selection strategies. The approach is broadly applicable across modalities but has been validated primarily on classification and reconstruction tasks.
