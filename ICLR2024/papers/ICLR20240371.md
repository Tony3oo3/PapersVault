## Problem

Recent offline RL and IL algorithms (XQL, CQL, IQLearn, OPOLO, SMODICE, etc.) were derived from disparate mathematical perspectives—Gumbel regression, pessimistic value learning, change of variables, contrastive losses—without a shared theoretical lens, making it difficult to diagnose their shared limitations. Specifically, offline IL methods rely on a restrictive coverage assumption and a discriminator to estimate a density ratio between expert and suboptimal data, while XQL's exponential (Gumbel) loss causes well-known training instability. The paper asks whether a unified framework can expose these root causes and yield principled fixes.

## Input / Output

For ReCOIL (offline IL), the input is a pair of datasets: expert demonstrations (state-action trajectories) and suboptimal transitions from a behavior policy, with no environment reward required; the output is a policy, a Q-function serving as a Bellman-consistent score, and a recovered reward function r(s,a) = Q*(s,a) − T₀^π Q*(s,a) that captures expert intent. For f-DVL (offline RL), the input is a standard offline dataset of (s, a, r, s') transitions; the output is a policy extracted via advantage-weighted regression from learned Q and V functions. Both methods operate purely on logged data with no online interaction, and ReCOIL notably requires no discriminator or density-ratio network, consuming only raw transitions from both expert and suboptimal sources.

## Method

The paper casts the regularized RL objective max_π E_{d^π}[r] − α D_f(d^π‖d^O) as a convex program over visitation distributions with Bellman-flow constraints, then applies Lagrangian duality to obtain unconstrained dual-Q (min-max over Q and π) and dual-V (single minimization over V) formulations. This unification reveals that XQL is a dual-V instance with reverse-KL (exponential) conjugate, CQL is a dual-Q instance with χ² conjugate, and IQLearn/OPOLO/SMODICE are dual-Q or dual-V instances for imitation. From this, ReCOIL replaces the discriminator-based pseudo-reward with a mixture-distribution matching objective: it minimizes D_f(βd + (1−β)d^S ‖ βd^E + (1−β)d^S), whose dual-Q uses the zero-reward Bellman operator T₀ and requires only samples from the offline mixture—eliminating both the coverage assumption and the discriminator. f-DVL extends XQL by substituting the exponential reverse-KL conjugate with polynomial surrogates from χ² or total-variation divergences (e.g., max(y²/4 + y, 0)), yielding stable implicit value maximizers that provably converge to sup_x x as the temperature λ→1.

## Results

On offline IL (D4RL), ReCOIL achieves near-expert performance across locomotion and manipulation tasks, e.g., 108.18 on Hopper (random+expert, expert=111.33) and 95.04 on Pen (cloned+expert, expert=106.42), while the strongest baseline SMODICE scores 101.61 and −3.36 respectively. In the harder random+few-expert setting, ReCOIL reaches 97.85 on Hopper vs. SMODICE's 60.11 and ORIL's 42.04. On offline RL (D4RL), f-DVL (TV) achieves 98.0 on hopper-medium-replay (vs. XQL 95.1, IQL 94.7), 87.7 on antmaze-umaze (vs. IQL 87.5, CQL 74.0), and 70.0 on kitchen-partial (vs. IQL 46.3, CQL 49.8). f-DVL also resolves the training instability of XQL by replacing the exponential Gumbel loss with low-order polynomial surrogates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For offline IL, ReCOIL outperforms SMODICE on most tasks, with the largest gaps when expert data is scarce (random+few-expert: Hopper 97.85 vs. 60.11, HalfCheetah 76.92 vs. 2.28) or in high-dimensional manipulation (Pen 95.04 vs. −3.36, Door 102.75 vs. 0.25), where SMODICE's discriminator-based density ratio fails. ReCOIL also dominates RCE, ORIL, IQLearn, and behavior cloning by wide margins across all suboptimal dataset configurations. For offline RL, f-DVL (TV) beats XQL(r) on hopper-medium-replay (98.0 vs. 95.1), antmaze-umaze (87.7 vs. 47.7), antmaze-medium-play (71.0 vs. 31.2), and kitchen-partial (70.0 vs. 48.6), while f-DVL (χ²) wins on hopper-medium-expert (105.8 vs. 94.0). f-DVL matches or slightly exceeds IQL on antmaze tasks (87.7 vs. 87.5 on umaze; 71.0 vs. 71.2 on medium-play) and substantially outperforms CQL on long-horizon antmaze and kitchen tasks. The paper notes that XQL's originally reported numbers used best-during-training evaluation rather than the standard last-iterate protocol, which inflates XQL's apparent performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dual RL framework of Nachum and Dai (2020) by showing, via Lagrangian duality, that a wide range of previously disparate offline RL and IL algorithms—XQL, CQL, IQL, IQLearn, OPOLO, SMODICE, and others—are all instantiations of the same dual formulation with different choices of f-divergence, update rule, and constraints. The key conceptual departure is identifying the coverage assumption (suboptimal data must cover expert visitations) as the root cause of failure in prior off-policy IL methods, and showing that a simple mixture-distribution matching objective eliminates this need. For offline RL, the paper reframes XQL's Gumbel regression as the convex conjugate of reverse KL divergence, revealing that its training instability is an artifact of the exponential loss rather than a fundamental limitation of implicit policy improvement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification provides a principled diagnostic tool: by mapping any algorithm to its dual-RL coordinates (Q vs. V, full vs. semi-gradient, f-divergence choice), researchers can identify why a method fails and what modification would fix it. ReCOIL's discriminator-free approach and f-DVL's polynomial losses address two concrete pain points (coverage assumption fragility and exponential-loss instability) with clear empirical gains on D4RL. Limitations include restriction to the regularized RL setting (α > 0), a static regularization distribution, and an assumption of high-quality expert demonstrations; extending to dynamic regularization (as in PPO/TRPO) or handling noisy demonstrations remains open.
