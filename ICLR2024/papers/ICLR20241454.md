## Problem

Contrastive and triplet losses for audio-text matching treat all negative samples equally and minimize a fixed metric (cosine or Euclidean) per anchor, ignoring the global geometric structure of the joint embedding space. This leads to suboptimal relative distances between aligned and misaligned pairs, and contrastive learning in particular preserves a modality gap that hinders transfer to downstream tasks. Additionally, real-world audio-text datasets contain misaligned pairs from data collection, and no prior method for audio-text matching addresses this noise in a principled way.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 10-second audio clips (AudioCaps) or 15–30-second clips (Clotho) converted to 64-bin log mel-spectrograms (window 1024, hop 320), paired with free-form text captions. Audio is encoded by a pretrained ResNet-38 or HTSAT backbone and text by BERT; both outputs are projected into a shared 1024-dimensional embedding space via linear layers. At retrieval time, the model produces a full pairwise optimal-transport plan between all test audios and captions, from which top-k rankings are extracted; the learned 1024×1024 Mahalanobis matrix M is also part of the output and is used to compute the ground cost during both training and inference.

## Method

The m-LTM framework formulates cross-modal matching as an inverse optimal transport problem solved on mini-batches: given b paired audio-text samples, it computes the entropic optimal transport plan (via the differentiable Sinkhorn algorithm) under a Mahalanobis ground metric c(x,y)=√((f(x)−g(y))ᵀM(f(x)−g(y))) and minimizes the KL divergence between the identity matching matrix and this plan. The Mahalanobis matrix M is updated with projected gradient descent to maintain positive-definiteness, while the audio and text encoders use standard SGD. A POT variant relaxes the marginal constraints of the transport plan (allowing total mass s < 1) so that misaligned pairs in noisy training data are effectively discarded rather than forced into the coupling, solved via Bregman projection. This soft-matching objective captures the full geometric structure of the mini-batch point cloud rather than pushing individual negatives, and the Mahalanobis metric adaptively rescales embedding dimensions to close the modality gap.

## Results

On AudioCaps, m-LTM achieves R@1 of 39.10 (Text→Audio) and 49.94 (Audio→Text), surpassing the strongest baseline CLAP (Wu et al., 2022b) by 2.4 and 4.6 points respectively. On Clotho, m-LTM reaches R@1 of 16.65 (Text→Audio) and 22.1 (Audio→Text), outperforming the best prior method (Deshmukh et al., 2022) by 0.86 and 4.68 points. In zero-shot sound event detection on ESC-50, m-LTM attains 81.0% R@1 (accuracy) and 87.57 mAP, exceeding contrastive loss (72.25% R@1, 80.84 mAP) by 8.75 and 6.73 points. The modality gap is reduced to 0.117 on AudioCaps and 0.224 on ESC-50, compared to 0.181 and 0.922 for contrastive loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

m-LTM outperforms all four SOTA retrieval baselines on both AudioCaps and Clotho across every R@k metric. Against the strongest retrieval baseline CLAP (Wu et al., 2022b), m-LTM gains +2.4 R@1 on AudioCaps Text→Audio (39.10 vs 36.7) and +4.6 R@1 on Audio→Text (49.94 vs 45.3); on Clotho the margins are +4.65 R@1 (Text→Audio) and +6.4 R@1 (Audio→Text). Against Deshmukh et al. (2022, WavText5K/CLAP), m-LTM leads by +6.03 R@1 on Clotho Audio→Text (22.1 vs 17.42). Against Mei et al. (2022) and Oncescu et al. (2021), the gaps are even larger (e.g., +11.04 R@1 on Clotho Audio→Text vs Oncescu). In the loss-function ablation (Table 2), m-LTM beats both triplet loss (Wei et al., 2021) and contrastive loss (Radford et al., 2021/CLIP) with the same ResNet-38 encoder: 39.10 vs 33.9 (contrastive) vs 32.2 (triplet) R@1 on AudioCaps Text→Audio, and 49.94 vs 39.4 vs 36.1 on Audio→Text. On zero-shot ESC-50 sound event detection, m-LTM (81.0% R@1) surpasses contrastive (72.25%) and triplet (71.25%) by 8.75 and 9.75 points respectively, confirming superior transferability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Learning-to-Match (LTM) framework from Li et al. (2018), originally designed for small-scale tabular inverse optimal transport, to the deep learning regime via mini-batch subsampling, making it scalable to large audio-text datasets. It departs from the standard contrastive and triplet loss paradigms (CLIP-style, Wei et al.) by formulating cross-modal matching as an entropic optimal transport problem with a Mahalanobis-enhanced ground metric, which is introduced for audio-text matching for the first time. The noisy-correspondence variant further draws on Partial Optimal Transport (Chapel & Alaya, 2020) to relax the full-mass constraint, a technique previously applied to positive-unlabeled learning but not to cross-modal retrieval.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The m-LTM framework addresses two practical pain points in cross-modal retrieval: the modality gap that hinders zero-shot transfer (reduced from 0.922 to 0.224 on ESC-50) and sensitivity to misaligned training pairs (maintaining ~28% R@1 at 60% noise where triplet loss collapses to 0.1%). However, the approach is limited to audio-text matching and relies on a 1024×1024 Mahalanobis matrix whose computational cost and generalizability to other modality pairs (e.g., image-text) remain untested. An open question is whether the modality-gap reduction generalizes to larger-scale pretraining settings where contrastive methods have shown strong scaling behavior.
