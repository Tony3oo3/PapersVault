## Problem

Existing Gaussian process formulations for vector fields on Riemannian manifolds require the underlying manifold to be known and analytically tractable (e.g., spheres, tori), limiting their applicability to real-world data where the manifold is only implicitly defined by local similarities. This paper addresses the gap by constructing a GP that models vector fields over arbitrary latent manifolds estimated purely from data, without assuming a parametric manifold form.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241511/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes pairs (x_i, v_i) where x_i are samples from an unknown manifold and v_i are vectors in the tangent bundle, with the manifold dimension m estimated from the data via singular value cutoffs on local tangent frames. In the EEG application, input is a 64-channel low-density recording and output is a 256-channel high-density reconstruction expressed as a vector field (spatial gradient of voltage), from which divergence and curl fields are extracted for singularity detection and disease classification. The GP output is a matrix-valued kernel prediction in ambient R^d space, not per-channel scalar predictions.

## Method

RVGP constructs a matrix-valued Matérn kernel using positional encoding from eigenvectors of the connection Laplacian, a block operator built on a proximity graph where off-diagonal blocks are weighted parallel transport maps (Lévy-Civita rotations) between locally estimated tangent space frames. Unlike prior work that treats vector components as independent scalar channels or requires an isometric embedding of the tangent bundle into Euclidean space, the kernel is intrinsically defined on the tangent bundle and accounts for alignment of the vector field to principal curvature directions via the Weitzenbock identity. The manifold and tangent bundle are both unknown: the manifold is approximated by a K-nearest-neighbour graph, and tangent spaces are estimated via SVD of local neighbour displacement vectors. Training proceeds via standard Bayesian linear regression with inducing-point approximation for scalability, and the resulting GP provides out-of-sample predictions with controllable smoothness through the Matérn hyperparameters.

## Results

On the EEG superresolution task (64→256 channels, n=61 subjects), RVGP achieves significantly lower angular error and curl-divergence MAE than all baselines (linear, spline, and RBF kernel interpolation), with the advantage most pronounced in preserving vector field singularities (sources, sinks, vortices). For Alzheimer's disease classification using a linear SVM on reconstructed divergence and vorticity fields, RVGP yields classification accuracy approaching that of the ground-truth 256-channel recording, significantly outperforming all interpolation baselines. On synthetic superresolution (Stanford Bunny, Torus), RVGP maintains accurate vector alignment with only 10 connection Laplacian eigenvectors across a broad range of sampling densities, whereas the channel-wise RBF benchmark requires k=50 eigenvectors for comparable accuracy and degrades at lower data densities. At 32-channel density, all methods fail to produce practically useful reconstructions, establishing 64 channels as an empirical limit.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares RVGP against three interpolation baselines: linear interpolation, spherical spline interpolation (Perrin et al., 1989), and a channel-wise RBF kernel GP that treats vector components as independent scalar channels. On the synthetic Stanford Bunny and Torus experiments, RVGP clearly outperforms the RBF channel-wise baseline in both superresolution and inpainting: the RBF kernel produces discontinuities at mask boundaries, vectors protruding off the mesh surface, and fails to converge to zero magnitude at singularities, while RVGP smoothly resolves all features. On the EEG task, where the scalp curvature is relatively homogeneous, the RBF kernel performs most similarly to RVGP (as expected, since curvature effects are reduced), but RVGP still achieves significantly lower angular error and curl-divergence MAE across all 61 subjects. Linear interpolation underfits the vector field, and spline interpolation introduces spurious local structures. For the downstream Alzheimer's classification task, RVGP's reconstructed divergence and vorticity fields yield ROC AUC approaching the ground-truth 256-channel signal, while all three baselines produce substantially lower classification accuracy, demonstrating that the connection Laplacian's encoding of tangent-space geometry is critical for preserving the singularity patterns that serve as disease markers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Matérn GP framework of Borovitskiy et al. (2020, 2021), which used Laplace-Beltrami or graph Laplacian eigenfunctions as positional encoding for scalar signals on known manifolds or graphs, to the vector-valued case on *unknown* latent manifolds. The key departure from Hutchinson et al. (2021), who defined vector-valued GPs on Riemannian manifolds via isometric projection into Euclidean space, is that RVGP requires no explicit manifold parametrisation: it constructs a matrix-valued kernel from connection Laplacian eigenvectors and parallel-transport maps derived entirely from a proximity graph. The scalar case (m=1) is recovered as a special case, and the connection Laplacian's spectral convergence to the continuous operator (Singer & Wu, 2017) provides the theoretical grounding for the discrete-to-continuous bridge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RVGP removes the assumption that the underlying manifold must be known and analytically tractable, which is the primary practical barrier to applying geometric GPs to real-world data. The EEG application demonstrates that preserving vector field singularities (sources, sinks, vortices) through manifold-aware interpolation translates directly into improved Alzheimer's classification accuracy from low-density recordings, suggesting a path toward cheaper, more accessible clinical diagnostics. Limitations include the need for sufficient sampling density (32-channel EEG fails for all methods), diminishing advantage over channel-wise kernels on homogeneous-curvature manifolds, and the assumption of uniform tangent-space dimension. Open questions include extending to non-tangent vector fields, sheaf Laplacians for variable-dimensional tangent spaces, and high-dimensional latent manifolds where proximity graph construction becomes ill-conditioned.
