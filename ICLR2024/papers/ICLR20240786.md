## Problem

Predictive coding (PC) networks, while biologically plausible and grounded in variational inference, suffer from three critical training limitations: they require an external control signal to switch between the inference and learning phases, the inference phase is slow (needing dozens of iterations to converge), and convergence is highly sensitive to hyperparameter choices. These issues have made PC impractical for large-scale tasks where backpropagation (BP) remains the default.

## Input / Output

For image classification, the method takes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/FashionMNIST/SVHN, 28×28 for MNIST) and produces class labels; for generation, it takes latent vectors and produces images; for language modeling, it takes token sequences (8001-token BPE vocabulary) and produces reconstructed masked tokens or autoregressively generated tokens. A notable structural detail is that in supervised mode, the input data point is clamped at the top layer (x^(L) = y_in) while the target label is clamped at the bottom layer (x^(0) = y_out), inverting the standard feedforward direction.

## Method

Incremental predictive coding (iPC) modifies the temporal scheduling of the original PC algorithm by performing the inference update (Eq. 6, updating value nodes x) and the weight update (Eq. 7, updating parameters θ) simultaneously at every time step, rather than waiting for inference to converge before updating weights. This is formally derived by applying incremental expectation-maximization (iEM) to the variational free energy of a hierarchical Gaussian generative model, inheriting iEM's proven convergence guarantees to a minimum of the global marginal likelihood. The key efficiency gain is that iPC requires only 2 simultaneous matrix multiplications (SMMs) per weight update (all layers updated in parallel) versus 2L−1 SMMs for BP (sequential forward and backward passes), yielding O(1) vs O(L) time complexity per update in full-batch training. The algorithm is fully automatic—no external control signal is needed to trigger weight updates—and empirically converges to better local minima than original PC, achieving test accuracies comparable to BP across image classification and language modeling benchmarks.

## Results

On image classification, iPC consistently outperforms original PC across all benchmarks: 89.13% on FashionMNIST (vs. 85.12% PC), 96.45% on SVHN (vs. 94.53% PC), 72.54% on CIFAR-10 with a CNN (vs. 70.84% PC), and 72.42% on CIFAR-10 with AlexNet (vs. 64.63% PC). iPC is competitive with backpropagation, matching or exceeding BP on 4 of 6 image classification settings. On language models (1B Word Benchmark), iPC achieves 106.19 ppl on masked LM (vs. 120.02 BP, 523.08 PC) with all 10 seeds converging, and 142.54 ppl on conditional LM (vs. 113.32 BP, 206.34 PC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

iPC decisively outperforms original predictive coding (PC) on every non-trivial benchmark: the gap widens with task complexity, from a negligible 0.01% on MNIST to a 7.79% gap on AlexNet/CIFAR-10 (72.42% vs. 64.63%) and a 416.89 ppl gap on masked language models (106.19 vs. 523.08). Against backpropagation (BP), iPC is competitive: it matches or exceeds BP on FashionMNIST (89.13% vs. 88.54%), SVHN (96.45% vs. 95.35%), and CNN/CIFAR-10 (72.54% vs. 69.34%), but trails BP on AlexNet/CIFAR-10 (72.42% vs. 75.64%) and conditional language models (142.54 vs. 113.32 ppl). In terms of convergence robustness, iPC converged in 88/96 hyperparameter combinations on AlexNet versus only 26/96 for PC, and all 10 seeds converged for iPC on masked LM versus only 3/10 for PC. iPC also maintains significantly better calibration under distribution shift (average AdaECE of 0.05 vs. 0.12 for BP on in-distribution data), and is more parameter-efficient, achieving comparable accuracy with fewer network parameters before performance degrades.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the original predictive coding (PC) formulation of Rao & Ballard (1999) and the variational free energy framework of Friston (2005), where learning alternates between an inference phase (updating hidden activities) and a learning phase (updating weights). The key departure is a simple temporal scheduling change: instead of running inference to convergence before updating weights, iPC performs both updates simultaneously at every time step, mirroring the incremental EM (iEM) algorithm of Neal & Hinton (1998). This places iPC conceptually between standard PC (which waits for full convergence) and Z-IL (Song et al., 2020), which updates weights at specific inference moments to exactly replicate backpropagation, and is also related to equilibrium propagation with continual weight updates (Ernoult et al., 2020). The convergence guarantee is inherited from the iEM literature rather than derived from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses the two main practical barriers to adopting predictive coding for large-scale learning—slow training and hyperparameter sensitivity—by showing that a minimal scheduling change yields convergence guarantees, better calibration under distribution shift, and competitive accuracy with backpropagation across image classification and language modeling benchmarks. However, the weight transport problem (requiring transposed weights for error propagation) remains unresolved, and the empirical evidence is limited to relatively small architectures (AlexNet on CIFAR-10, 2-block BERT with 128 hidden dimensions), leaving open whether the efficiency and stability advantages persist at the scale of modern vision or language models. The observed performance degradation under overparametrization (Table 2) also suggests iPC may require more careful capacity tuning than BP, a practical concern for scaling.
