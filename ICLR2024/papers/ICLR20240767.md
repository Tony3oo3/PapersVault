## Problem

Retrieval-augmented language models (RALMs) improve access to long-tail and up-to-date knowledge, but existing approaches face a trade-off: end-to-end pre-training (REALM, RETRO, ATLAS) is expensive, while post-hoc fusion of off-the-shelf LLMs with retrievers (REPLUG) is suboptimal because the LLM is not trained to incorporate retrieved content. The paper asks whether lightweight instruction tuning alone can close this gap and retrofit any pre-trained LLM with effective retrieval capabilities.

## Input / Output

The system takes a text prompt (question, instruction, or dialogue turn) and retrieves top-k text chunks (k=10 at inference) from a 399M-chunk corpus (Wikipedia + CommonCrawl, max 200 words per chunk). Each retrieved chunk is prepended to the prompt as a 'Background' field, and predictions from all k augmented prompts are computed in parallel and ensembled via retriever-score-weighted averaging. For multi-choice tasks the output is the highest-probability option; for generation tasks it is the highest-probability unique answer across chunks, scored by negative log-likelihood variants.

## Method

RA-DIT performs two decoupled fine-tuning steps. First, retrieval-augmented LM fine-tuning (LM-ft) uses the label-loss objective over 20 instruction-tuning datasets, where each example is expanded into k̃=3 instances by prepending retrieved chunks as a background field, teaching the LLM to exploit relevant passages and ignore noisy ones. Second, retriever fine-tuning (R-ft) updates only the query encoder of a DRAGON+ dual-encoder retriever by minimizing KL-divergence between retriever scores and LM-Supervised Retrieval (LSR) scores computed by the LLM, using a 95% corpus / 5% multi-task instruction data mixture. The key insight is that optimizing the LLM and retriever independently and then fusing them through instruction tuning achieves state-of-the-art zero- and few-shot performance on knowledge-intensive benchmarks, outperforming both un-tuned in-context RALMs (REPLUG) and jointly pre-trained RALMs (ATLAS) without requiring extensive continuous pre-training.

## Results

RA-DIT 65B achieves state-of-the-art performance on knowledge-intensive benchmarks, reaching 50.5% average accuracy (over MMLU, NQ, TQA, ELI5) in the 0-shot setting and 55.2% in the 5-shot setting, outperforming the REPLUG baseline by +8.9% and +1.4% respectively. On individual tasks, RA-DIT 65B scores 64.6% on MMLU (0-shot), 35.2% on Natural Questions (0-shot), and 75.4% on TriviaQA (0-shot). In the 64-shot fine-tuning comparison against ATLAS, RA-DIT 65B achieves 60.9% average across 8 KILT tasks, surpassing ATLAS by +4.1 points while using a single unified model rather than task-specific models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base LLAMA 65B model, RA-DIT 65B improves the 0-shot average from 22.8% to 50.5% and the 5-shot average from 45.0% to 55.2%, demonstrating the substantial benefit of retrieval augmentation combined with dual instruction tuning. Compared to REPLUG (LLAMA 65B + DRAGON+ without fine-tuning), RA-DIT wins on 9 out of 10 knowledge-intensive tasks in the 0-shot setting, with the largest gains on zsRE (+22.9 points), T-REx (+16.8 points), and NQ (+6.4 points), while only trailing slightly on WoW (16.4 vs 16.1). In the 5-shot setting, RA-DIT outperforms REPLUG on 7 of 10 tasks, with notable advantages on AIDA (+9.4), zsRE (+12.0), and NQ (+1.6). Against ATLAS (which jointly pre-trains the LM and retriever), RA-DIT 65B achieves 60.9% vs 56.8% average in the 64-shot fine-tuning setting, winning on 6 of 8 tasks with the largest margin on AIDA (+14.0 points) and T-REx (+13.9 points). For retriever comparisons, DRAGON+ outperforms Contriever by 5.9 points and Contriever-MSMARCO by 1.6 points on the 5-shot average, confirming the choice of DRAGON+ as the base retriever.

## Relation to Prior Work

RA-DIT positions itself as a lightweight middle ground between two existing paradigms for building retrieval-augmented language models: (1) expensive end-to-end pre-training that integrates retrieval from the outset (REALM, RETRO, ATLAS), and (2) post-hoc fusion of off-the-shelf LLMs with general-purpose retrievers via in-context learning (REPLUG, In-Context RALM). The method draws on instruction tuning (Chung et al., 2022b; Iyer et al., 2022) for the LM side and LM-Supervised Retrieval (LSR, Shi et al., 2023b) for the retriever side, but decouples these into two independent fine-tuning steps rather than jointly optimizing them. It also builds on the parallel in-context retrieval-augmentation framework of REPLUG and the DRAGON+ dense retriever, extending LSR from unlabeled corpus data to multi-task instruction data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that RA-DIT provides a simple, low-cost recipe for retrofitting retrieval capabilities onto any pre-trained LLM without expensive pre-training, and the resulting model competes with extensively pre-trained RALMs like ATLAS. The finding that the LM can learn to ignore noisy retrieval and fall back on parametric knowledge is important for robustness in deployment. Open questions include whether iterative dual tuning (alternating LM-ft and R-ft) can yield further gains, whether chunk concatenation with longer context windows might outperform parallel retrieval-augmentation, and how the approach scales to even larger models or different retriever architectures.
