## Problem

Asynchronous federated learning methods such as FedBuff improve training efficiency by letting clients send updates without waiting, but the resulting asynchronous delay degrades convergence. The paper provides a theoretical analysis showing that this degradation is multiplicatively amplified by data heterogeneity (non-i.i.d. distributions), producing a joint effect term O(Kτ_max τ_avg σ_g²/T) that dominates the convergence rate under realistic heterogeneous settings. No prior asynchronous FL method addresses this joint delay–heterogeneity interaction.

## Input / Output

The server receives local model update differences (Δ_t^i) from asynchronously participating clients and maintains a per-client cached variable h_t^i representing each client's most recent update, along with a global cached average h_t = (1/N)Σ h_t^i. The output is a calibrated global update vector v_t = h_t + (1/|S_t|)Σ_{i∈S_t}(Δ_t^i − h_t^i), which is applied to the global model. Unlike standard FedBuff that simply averages raw updates, CA²FL subtracts the stale cached update before accumulation, effectively removing the component of each update already reflected in the global model. A memory-friendly variant (MF-CA²FL) quantizes the cached updates to 8-bit or 4-bit to reduce server memory overhead.

## Method

CA²FL extends the FedBuff asynchronous aggregation framework by introducing server-side cached updates for cross-client variance reduction. When client i sends its update Δ_t^i, the server accumulates the *difference* (Δ_t^i − h_t^i) rather than the raw update, where h_t^i is the previously cached update for that client; for clients not participating in the current round, their cached variables remain unchanged. After M updates are accumulated, the global model is updated using v_t = h_t + (1/|S_t|)Σ(Δ_t^i − h_t^i), a linear combination of the global cached average and the mean of new update differences. This design is analogous to SAGA-style variance reduction applied across clients rather than data samples, but does not rely on SAGA's unbiased incremental gradient properties, making the theoretical analysis non-trivial. The key theoretical result is that the problematic joint delay–heterogeneity term O(Kτ_max τ_avg σ_g²/T) is entirely eliminated from the convergence bound, reducing the delay's impact to only the stochastic noise term O((τ_max + ζ_max)σ²/T), while incurring no additional communication or computation overhead on clients.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18 under Dir(0.1) heterogeneity, CA²FL achieves 68.37% test accuracy, outperforming FedBuff (63.03%) by over 5% and FedAsync (40.58%) by nearly 28%. On CIFAR-100 with severe heterogeneity (Dir(0.01)), CA²FL reaches 37.30% accuracy versus 32.49% for FedBuff, while FedAsync fails to converge. On the GLUE benchmark (Dir(0.6)), CA²FL achieves 90.76% on SST-2, 65.63% on RTE, and 56.10% on CoLA, consistently surpassing both asynchronous baselines except on MRPC where FedAsync leads (82.86% vs. 79.26%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CA²FL consistently outperforms FedBuff across all evaluated tasks: on CIFAR-10 ResNet-18 Dir(0.1) it gains +5.34% (68.37% vs. 63.03%), on CIFAR-100 Dir(0.01) it gains +4.81% (37.30% vs. 32.49%), and on GLUE SST-2 it gains +4.70% (90.76% vs. 86.06%). Against FedAsync, CA²FL wins on CIFAR-10 (both models and heterogeneity levels), CIFAR-100 Dir(0.01), SST-2, RTE, and CoLA, but loses on MRPC (79.26% vs. 82.86%) and CIFAR-100 Dir(0.1) (59.50% vs. 62.91%). Notably, FedAsync fails to converge entirely on CIFAR-10 CNN Dir(0.1) and CIFAR-100 Dir(0.01), where CA²FL still achieves 57.62% and 37.30% respectively. Compared to synchronous FedAvg, CA²FL achieves comparable or better accuracy while being significantly faster in wall-clock time on image classification (e.g., 214.16 vs. 388.64 units on CIFAR-10), though FedAvg remains faster for the CoLA fine-tuning task (0.79 vs. 34.75 units). Overall, CA²FL is the most robust method under high data heterogeneity, with the smallest standard deviations across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on FedBuff (Nguyen et al., 2022), a buffered asynchronous federated learning algorithm, by adding a server-side cached update calibration mechanism to its global aggregation step. Conceptually, the calibration design draws inspiration from SAGA-style variance reduction (storing and reusing previously computed gradients), though the authors explicitly note their method does not satisfy SAGA's unbiased incremental gradient property, making the theoretical analysis non-trivial. The work also positions itself against the broader line of heterogeneity-mitigation methods in FL (FedProx, FedDyn, Scaffold, MIMIC) by achieving variance reduction without introducing client-side control variables or extra communication rounds, instead leveraging server-side memory that is already available in asynchronous settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean theoretical result—that a simple server-side caching mechanism eliminates the dominant joint delay-heterogeneity term O(Kτ_max τ_avg σ_g²/T) from the convergence bound—provides a principled explanation for why asynchronous FL degrades under non-i.i.d. data and offers a lightweight fix requiring zero additional client-side computation or communication. The memory-friendly variant (MF-CA²FL) with 4-bit quantization shows the approach is practical for large models. Open questions include whether the method's advantage persists under concept drift (only briefly explored), how it scales to very large model sizes where even quantized caching becomes expensive, and why the efficiency gap versus synchronous FedAvg remains significant for fine-tuning pre-trained language models.
