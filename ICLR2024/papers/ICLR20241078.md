## Problem

Existing text-image inconsistency detection methods rely on binary classification, which outputs a label without explainable evidence for why a pair is inconsistent. Humans naturally identify inconsistencies by pointing to specific image regions and words that conflict, but manual inspection does not scale. The paper addresses the gap of automatically localizing inconsistencies at both the pixel and word level while providing interpretable justification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241078/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a real-world news image (resolutions ranging from 256×396 to 3744×3744) and its accompanying text caption as input. It produces three outputs: a binary pixel-level mask highlighting semantically inconsistent image regions, the specific words in the text that are inconsistent (identified via CLIP cosine similarity with the edited image), and a scalar consistency score in [0, 100] computed from the CLIP similarity between the masked image and the original text embedding.

## Method

D-TIIL leverages a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model as a knowledge-rich 'omniscient' agent through a four-step iterative alignment pipeline. Step 1 optimizes a text embedding E_aln via gradient descent on the diffusion model to match the input image, constrained by a Frobenius-norm bound to the original CLIP embedding E_0, thereby filtering text-only semantics. Step 2 computes the spatial difference between noise estimates conditioned on E_0 and E_aln to produce a coarse mask M', then uses RePaint inpainting guided by E_0 to generate an edited image I_edit that materializes the text in the visual domain. Step 3 performs a second alignment (image-regulated text denoising) to obtain E_dnt from I_edit, removing image-only implicit content. Step 4 generates the final inconsistency mask M from the noise difference between E_aln and E_dnt, identifies inconsistent words via CLIP token-image similarity, and computes the consistency score from the masked image's CLIP embedding against E_0. The key insight is that the diffusion model's broad training data provides background knowledge (e.g., knowing dolphins are mammals) that neither the text nor image explicitly contains, enabling detection of subtle semantic mismatches.

## Results

On the TIIL dataset, D-TIIL achieves 47.12% mIoU for pixel-level inconsistency localization, substantially outperforming the DetCLIP baseline (14.24%) and GAE (27.35%). For binary inconsistency detection, D-TIIL reaches 87.00% AUC and 79.46% accuracy, surpassing CLIP (84.18% AUC, 76.77% accuracy), fine-tuned CLIP* (78.58% AUC, 71.87% accuracy), and CCN (82.80% AUC, 74.10% accuracy). The method also shows robustness to data manipulation, maintaining 79.15% accuracy on manipulated image/text subsets where CCN drops to 68.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For localization, D-TIIL (47.12% mIoU) nearly doubles the performance of GAE (27.35%) and more than triples DetCLIP (14.24%), which relies on a generic object detector (Zhou et al., ECCV 2022) plus CLIP similarity to identify the most dissimilar region. For detection, D-TIIL outperforms CLIP (Radford et al., ICML 2021) by 2.82 AUC points and 2.69 accuracy points, and outperforms CCN (Abdelnabi et al., CVPR 2022) by 4.20 AUC points and 5.36 accuracy points. Notably, CCN depends on reverse image search for external evidence, causing its accuracy to drop from 80.12% on original web-sourced pairs to 68.0% on manipulated pairs, whereas D-TIIL remains stable (82.79% vs. 79.15%) since it requires no external sources. The fine-tuned CLIP* variant actually underperforms the base CLIP, suggesting that the NewsCLIPpings training data introduces noise that degrades generalization to the more carefully curated TIIL dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends text-image inconsistency detection from binary classification (MAIM, COSMOS, NewsCLIPpings, CCN) to a fine-grained localization task that outputs pixel-level masks and word-level annotations. Conceptually, it repurposes pre-trained text-to-image diffusion models (Stable Diffusion) not for generation but as 'omniscient' semantic alignment agents, leveraging their broad world knowledge to filter irrelevant information and expose inconsistencies. The method builds directly on Stable Diffusion's UNet architecture and CLIP embeddings, but introduces a novel two-step iterative alignment procedure (image-regulated text denoising) that has no direct precedent in prior inconsistency detection work. The accompanying TIIL dataset departs from prior datasets (MAIM, NewsCLIPpings, COSMOS) that create inconsistencies via random caption swapping, instead using diffusion-model-based image editing with manual curation to produce more realistic and interpretable mismatches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from opaque binary labels to interpretable, localized evidence (which specific words and image regions are inconsistent) addresses a key practical gap in misinformation detection, where explainability is essential for human verification and trust. The TIIL dataset, as the first to provide both pixel-level and word-level inconsistency annotations, enables a new class of evaluation that prior datasets cannot support. However, the method's reliance on a general-purpose diffusion model limits its ability to handle domain-specific inconsistencies (e.g., fashion brands, medical terminology), and the authors acknowledge that domain-specialized diffusion models would be needed. Open questions include whether the iterative alignment approach scales to longer texts or video, and whether the localization quality (47% mIoU) is sufficient for real-world deployment at scale.
