## Problem

Existing assembly-based multimer structure prediction methods score docking events using independent PPI (I-PPI), ignoring how already-docked chains alter the docking probability of new chain pairs (conditional PPI, C-PPI). Moreover, C-PPI knowledge exhibits significant distribution shifts across multimer scales, and experimental structures for large-scale multimers (N>10) are extremely scarce, making direct generalization from small to large scales difficult.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241803/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes residue sequences of N chains (converted to 13-dimensional chain-level embeddings via a Siamese Residual RCNN-based function) and pre-computed dimer structures (from ground-truth, AFM, or ESMFold) as input. It outputs an assembly graph (a UCA graph representing the docking path) that, combined with the dimer structures via SE(3) transformations, yields the final assembled multimer 3D structure. Inference requires N−1 sequential steps, each scoring all remaining chain pairs and selecting the highest-probability link.

## Method

PromptMSP follows a pre-training + prompt tuning paradigm. A GIN encoder with a regression task head is pre-trained on a graph-level source task (predicting assembly correctness/TM-Score for UCA graphs of 3–5 chain multimers). During prompt tuning, a learnable prompt model with cross-attention layers takes the GIN embeddings of a docked chain v_d and an undocked chain v_u, and produces two virtual node embeddings that, together with v_d and v_u, form a fixed 4-node path graph. This 4-node prompt graph is fed to the frozen pre-trained GIN, whose output serves as the C-PPI linking probability — reformulating arbitrary-scale link prediction as fixed-scale graph regression. The 4-node path design is grounded in the ℓ=3 PPI rule from network biology. A MAML-based meta-learning strategy further learns a reliable prompt initialization from small-scale data, enabling rapid adaptation to scarce large-scale multimer data.

## Results

On small-scale multimers (3≤N≤10), PromptMSP achieves the best average TM-Score across all three dimer types: 0.67 (GT Dimer), 0.55 (AFM Dimer), and 0.45 (ESMFold Dimer), outperforming the strongest baseline MoLPC by 0.15, 0.12, and 0.14 respectively. Under GT Dimer, the median TM-Score reaches 0.71 with a median RMSD of 11.74 Å. For large-scale multimers (11≤N≤30), PromptMSP also surpasses MoLPC on all metrics and achieves perfect predictions (TM-Score = 1.0) on certain samples. In terms of efficiency, PromptMSP's docking-path prediction takes only 0.01–0.04 min per multimer, compared to 11.64 min for MoLPC, yielding a total inference time of 0.36 min (ESMFold Dimer, 3≤N≤10) versus 365.87 min for MoLPC (11≤N≤30).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PromptMSP consistently outperforms all four baselines across every dimer type and metric. Against MoLPC (the strongest assembly-based baseline), PromptMSP improves average TM-Score by 0.15 under GT Dimer (0.67 vs. 0.52), 0.12 under AFM Dimer (0.55 vs. 0.43), and 0.14 under ESMFold Dimer (0.45 vs. 0.31), while also reducing median RMSD substantially (e.g., 11.74 vs. 18.08 Å under GT Dimer). Compared to AFM† (the best non-assembly baseline), PromptMSP gains 0.08 TM-Score under GT Dimer and 0.15 under ESMFold Dimer, and is dramatically faster (0.01 min path prediction vs. 155.72 min total for AFM). Against RL-MLZerD† and Multi-LZerD†, PromptMSP leads by 0.16 and 0.13 TM-Score respectively under GT Dimer, with even larger margins under predicted dimers. Notably, PromptMSP achieves these gains without requiring additional plDDT scores or coarse interaction information that MoLPC relies on, and its per-step inference time remains constant regardless of multimer scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the step-wise assembly paradigm for multimer structure prediction (Multi-LZerD, RL-MLZerD, MoLPC) by replacing independent PPI (I-PPI) scoring with conditional PPI (C-PPI) modeling, arguing that third-party chain occupancy fundamentally alters docking probabilities. It repurposes prompt learning from NLP to bridge the gap between a graph-level regression source task and an edge-level link prediction target task, using the biologically-motivated ℓ=3 path rule to construct a fixed 4-node prompt graph. The GIN encoder (Xu et al., 2018) serves as the pre-trained backbone, while MAML (Finn et al., 2017) provides the meta-learning strategy for prompt initialization under data scarcity. The chain-level embedding from Chen et al. (2019) is used to construct node attributes for all assembly graphs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical bottleneck: large-scale multimers (N>10) have extremely limited experimental structures, and existing assembly-based methods like MoLPC are slow and rely on I-PPI that the authors show causes negative transfer. The 1000× speedup in docking path prediction (0.01 min vs. 11.64 min) makes large-scale assembly feasible in practice. However, the evaluation is constrained by a small test set (180 samples) and the only large-scale (N>10) comparison is against MoLPC; AFM and other DL baselines cannot handle that scale. Open questions include whether the fixed 4-node prompt scale is optimal, whether the approach generalizes to non-PDB or de novo sequences, and whether the C-PPI formulation can be integrated into end-to-end structure prediction without requiring pre-computed dimers.
