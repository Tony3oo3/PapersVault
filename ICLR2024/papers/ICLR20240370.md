## Problem

Direct Advantage Estimation (DAE) is a powerful on-policy multi-step learning method, but it cannot leverage off-policy data because its loss and constraint share the same policy. This limits DAE to less sample-efficient on-policy settings. The paper asks whether the advantage function's causal-effect interpretation can be extended to decompose returns into agent-caused (skill) and environment-caused (luck) components, thereby enabling a principled off-policy generalization that avoids importance sampling or action truncation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n-step trajectories (sequences of state-action-reward-next-state tuples) sampled from an arbitrary behavior policy and stored in a replay buffer; states are binary images in the MinAtar environments. It outputs four quantities from a shared convolutional backbone: a policy π_θ(a|s), a value function V_θ(s), an advantage function A_θ(s,a), and a luck function B_θ(s,a,s′) parameterized through a discrete latent space of size 16. The CVAE component separately learns a conditional posterior q(z|s,a,s′) and prior p(z|s,a) over the latent space, which are used to enforce the B-centering constraint without requiring knowledge of the true transition probabilities.

## Method

The key theoretical insight is that the return of any trajectory decomposes as G = V^π(s₀) + ΣγᵗA^π(sₜ,aₜ) + Σγᵗ⁺¹B^π(sₜ,aₜ,sₜ₊₁), where A is the agent's advantage (skill) and B is the advantage of an imaginary 'nature' agent whose actions are the stochastic transitions (luck). Off-policy DAE minimizes the squared error of this decomposition under two centering constraints: A is zero-mean under the target policy π, and B is zero-mean under the transition probability p(s′|s,a). To enforce the B constraint without knowing p, the paper trains a Conditional VAE that encodes transitions into a small discrete latent space Z, allowing the centering sum to be approximated as a difference of expectations over q(z|s,a,s′) and p(z|s,a). The resulting method requires no knowledge of the behavior policy, uses full trajectories without truncating off-policy actions, and is shown to be a strict generalization of both DAE (B≡0) and the uncorrected multi-step estimator (A≡B≡0).

## Results

On the MinAtar suite (5 environments, backup lengths 8/16/32, 20 seeds), Off-policy DAE achieves the best scores in all three stochastic environments: Seaquest (1225.3 at n=32 vs. 821.6 for DAE and 4.0 for Uncorrected), Asterix (268.9 vs. 215.4 and 2.0), and Freeway (62.9 vs. 58.8 and 8.2). In deterministic environments (Breakout, Space Invaders), DAE and Off-policy DAE perform comparably, both substantially outperforming Uncorrected (e.g., Space Invaders n=32: 14970.3/12560.4 vs. 307.7). Tree Backup is competitive in deterministic settings but degrades sharply in stochastic ones (e.g., Seaquest n=32: 266.2 vs. 1225.3 for Off-policy DAE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Uncorrected baseline (multi-step target without off-policy correction), Off-policy DAE wins by orders of magnitude in stochastic environments (e.g., Seaquest n=32: 1225.3 vs. 4.0; Asterix n=32: 268.9 vs. 2.0) and by large margins in deterministic ones (Space Invaders n=32: 14970.3 vs. 307.7), confirming that the advantage correction Â is essential when actions have significant consequences. Against DAE (on-policy advantage estimation applied off-policy), Off-policy DAE consistently wins in stochastic environments (Seaquest: 1225.3 vs. 821.6; Asterix: 268.9 vs. 215.4) due to the luck correction B̂, while the two methods are comparable in deterministic settings where B≡0. Against Tree Backup (a true off-policy method that truncates off-policy actions), Off-policy DAE dominates in stochastic environments (Seaquest n=32: 1225.3 vs. 266.2; Asterix n=32: 268.9 vs. 39.0) but is slightly behind in deterministic ones (Breakout n=32: 7372.1 vs. 10139.8; Space Invaders n=32: 14970.3 vs. 17374.2), suggesting Tree Backup's truncation strategy is less harmful when transitions are deterministic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Direct Advantage Estimation (DAE) from Pan et al. (2022), which established the advantage function as the causal effect of an action on the return, by introducing a second 'nature' agent whose advantage function B captures environment stochasticity. The two-step transition decomposition (agent action → nature's transition) echoes the afterstates concept from Sutton et al. (1998) and the 'move by nature' framing from game theory, but the paper is the first to formalize this as a constrained optimization problem yielding Off-policy DAE. The method is positioned as a generalization of Monte-Carlo methods with richer trajectory features, and as a principled alternative to importance sampling and Tree Backup for off-policy multi-step learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Off-policy DAE offers a theoretically grounded way to perform multi-step off-policy learning without the high variance of importance sampling or the data waste of truncation, which is practically significant for sample-efficient RL. However, the CVAE-based enforcement of the B constraint introduces substantial computational overhead and additional hyperparameters, limiting scalability to high-dimensional domains; the authors themselves flag learning a value-equivalent model as a promising but unexplored direction. The experiments are confined to MinAtar (small, low-dimensional environments), so whether the skill/luck decomposition and the CVAE approach transfer to large-scale settings like full Atari or continuous control remains an open question.
