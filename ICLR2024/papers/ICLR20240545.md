## Problem

Current safety alignment and evaluation of open-source LLMs is conducted under a single fixed decoding configuration (e.g., top-p=0.9, τ=0.1, with a system prompt), creating a critical blind spot. This paper shows that this fixed-configuration assumption is catastrophically fragile: simply manipulating decoding hyperparameters—without any adversarial prompt engineering—can break alignment across 11 open-source models, exposing a major failure in existing red-teaming and safety evaluation procedures.

## Input / Output

The attack consumes a malicious text instruction and a set of 49 decoding configurations (combinations of temperature, top-k, top-p, and system prompt presence/absence), generating one response per configuration. A scorer trained on HH-RLHF then selects the single most misaligned response as the final output. Evaluation uses a BERT-based classifier (96% validation accuracy, 92% human agreement) to compute attack success rate, supplemented by human annotation for harmfulness percentage among misaligned outputs.

## Method

The generation exploitation attack bypasses alignment by removing the system prompt and sweeping decoding hyperparameters (τ from 0.05 to 1 in steps of 0.05, K ∈ {1,2,5,10,20,50,100,200,500}, p from 0.05 to 1 in steps of 0.05), generating 49 candidate responses per prompt and selecting the most misaligned via a scorer. For safety-aligned models like LLAMA2-CHAT, the attack is further strengthened by sampling multiple times per configuration and imposing decoding constraints (length penalty, banned tokens like 'sorry'/'unethical', and forced tokens like 'sure'/'ok'), pushing ASR above 95% with ~30× less compute than GCG. The proposed defense, generation-aware alignment, proactively collects outputs from diverse decoding strategies and fine-tunes using a chain-of-hindsight contrastive objective that penalizes misaligned responses while reinforcing aligned ones, reducing ASR from 95% to 69%.

## Results

The generation exploitation attack raises the Attack Success Rate (ASR) from 0% (default decoding with system prompt) to ≥95% for 9 out of 11 open-source LLMs simply by varying decoding hyperparameters (temperature, top-K, top-p) and removing the system prompt. For the two safety-aligned LLAMA2-CHAT models, combining multiple sampling runs with decoding constraints (length penalty, bad-word blocking, forced words) pushes ASR to 95–100%. The attack requires ~3 minutes per prompt on a single A100 GPU, roughly 30× less compute than the GCG baseline. On the proprietary gpt-3.5-turbo, the same attack achieves only 7% ASR, highlighting a large safety gap between open-source and proprietary models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its generation exploitation attack against GCG (Zou et al., 2023), the prior state-of-the-art adversarial-suffix optimization attack. On LLAMA2-7B-CHAT, the proposed method achieves 97% ASR (substring) / 87% (classifier) on AdvBench and 99% / 95% on MaliciousInstruct, versus GCG's 47% / 36% and 50% / 42% respectively — a roughly 2× improvement. On LLAMA2-13B-CHAT, the gap widens further: the proposed method reaches 98% / 89% on AdvBench and 100% / 96% on MaliciousInstruct, while GCG manages only 38% / 31% and 24% / 21%. In addition to the large accuracy gap, the proposed attack is approximately 30× faster per prompt (~3 min vs. ~1.5 h on a single A100), since it requires no gradient-based optimization. The authors note that GCG retains an advantage in transferability: an optimized suffix can be copy-pasted across users without GPU access, whereas the generation exploitation attack must be re-run per model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of jailbreak research (GCG by Zou et al., 2023; Carlini et al., 2023) that focuses on optimizing adversarial input prompts, instead targeting the generation/decoding process itself. Conceptually, it builds on the chain-of-hindsight fine-tuning objective from Liu et al. (2023a) for its proposed generation-aware alignment defense, and draws on the broader RLHF/PPO alignment literature (Bai et al., 2022a; Touvron et al., 2023b) to demonstrate that safety tuning conducted under a single default decoding configuration leaves models catastrophically vulnerable. The paper also introduces MaliciousInstruct as a new benchmark and a classifier-based evaluation metric (trained on HH-RLHF) to replace the brittle substring-matching approach used in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that merely varying top-p from 0.9 to 0.75 can break RLHF-trained safety alignment across 11 open-source models exposes a fundamental gap in how the field evaluates and certifies model safety, with immediate practical implications for anyone deploying open-source LLMs. The generation-aware alignment defense reduces ASR from 95% to 69% but does not eliminate the vulnerability, leaving open the question of whether complete robustness to arbitrary decoding configurations is achievable with current alignment paradigms. The stark disparity between open-source (95% ASR) and proprietary (7% ASR) models also raises questions about whether the open-source ecosystem can ever match proprietary safety guarantees, and whether the attack transfers to newer or multimodal architectures.
