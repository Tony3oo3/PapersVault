## Problem

Existing 3D visual grounding methods directly predict the target object in a single step, offering no explanation of how or why the network arrives at its decision, and they are not data-efficient. The paper asks whether an interpretable 3D visual grounding framework can be designed that mimics human perception by decomposing the referring task into a sequence of intermediate reasoning steps (a chain of anchors) before localizing the final target.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240467/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D scene (N points with XYZ+RGB), pre-extracted object proposals (up to L=52 bounding boxes with class labels), and a natural language utterance (max length W=24) as input. Rather than outputting a single bounding box, it produces a sequence of 3D bounding boxes: a chain of anchor objects in a logical causal order followed by the target object, with the sequence length M=8 for Nr3D and M=3 for Sr3D. The anchor identities, their logical ordering, and their 3D localizations are all generated automatically via pseudo-labels (scene graph parsing, GPT-3.5 in-context learning, and rule-based geometry matching) rather than requiring human annotation.

## Method

CoT3DRef reformulates 3D visual grounding as a sequence-to-sequence task. A Pathway module (one transformer encoder layer) takes the BERT-encoded utterance and predicted object names to produce a logically ordered chain of anchors. A parallel referring head then localizes the target and all anchors simultaneously from multi-modal features, and a CoT decoder (one transformer decoder layer with masked self-attention) refines these predictions autoregressively, where each anchor can only attend to previously located objects, enforcing a one-directional causal chain. Pseudo-labels for anchor identities, logical order, and 3D positions are generated without human effort using a scene graph parser with SBERT class matching, GPT-3.5 in-context learning for ordering, and a rule-based localization algorithm that resolves spatial relations. The training objective combines extended language classification (target + anchors), parallel referring loss, CoT referring loss, and an auxiliary distractor binary classification loss, all optimized end-to-end. The framework is architecture-agnostic and integrates into any existing 3D grounding backbone (MVT, SAT, LAR, ViL) by replacing the referring head with the CoT decoder.

## Results

On Nr3D, integrating CoT3DRef into MVT with pseudo labels achieves 60.4% overall referring accuracy (vs. 55.1% MVT baseline, +5.3%), and with GT anchor labels reaches 64.4% (+9.3%). On Sr3D, MVT+CoT3DRef with GT labels achieves 73.2% (vs. 64.5% baseline, +8.7%), and ViL3DRef+CoT3DRef reaches 73.6%. On ScanRefer, MVT+CoT3DRef achieves 64.2% at 100% data (vs. 57.4% MVT, +6.8%) and 48.6% at only 10% data (vs. 36.4% MVT at 10%, +12.2%), matching full-data SOTA with just 10% of training samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoT3DRef is integrated into four baselines (LAR, SAT, MVT, ViL3DRef) and consistently outperforms each: on Nr3D the gains are +3.6%, +4%, +5%, and +0.5% respectively, and on Sr3D they are +10%, +11%, +9%, and +1%. Against the strongest prior SOTA without manual anchor annotations (MVT at 55.1% Nr3D / 64.5% Sr3D), MVT+CoT3DRef with GT labels reaches 64.4% / 73.2%, surpassing even methods that rely on expensive manual annotations such as PhraseRefer (59.0% Nr3D, 68.0% Sr3D) and ScanEnts (59.3% Nr3D). On ScanRefer, MVT+CoT3DRef outperforms MVT by 6.8% at 100% data and by 12.2% at 10% data, while SAT+CoT3DRef beats SAT by 6.5% at 100% data. The largest absolute gains appear on Sr3D (structured descriptions) where pseudo-label quality is higher, while on the more ambiguous Nr3D the pseudo-label module limits the gain to ~5% without GT anchors. The ViL3DRef baseline shows only marginal improvement (+0.5% Nr3D, +1% Sr3D), which the authors attribute to ViL already being near the dataset's annotation-error ceiling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the chain-of-thought (CoT) paradigm from NLP (Wei et al., 2022) into 3D visual grounding, formulating the referring task as a sequence-to-sequence problem where the model predicts a causal chain of anchor objects before the target. It is conceptually related to PhraseRefer and ScanEnts, which also leverage anchor objects, but departs from them by (1) introducing a Seq2Seq decoder with masked self-attention to enforce causal reasoning, (2) adding a Pathway module that learns logical ordering of anchors, and (3) eliminating the need for manual anchor annotations via an automatic pseudo-label pipeline (scene-graph parsing + GPT-3.5 in-context learning + geometric disambiguation). The framework is designed as a plug-in module demonstrated across four distinct backbones (MVT, SAT, LAR, ViL), positioning it as an architecture-agnostic enhancement rather than a standalone model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The data-efficiency result—matching full-data SOTA on Sr3D with only 10% of training data across multiple architectures—is practically significant for domains where 3D annotation is prohibitively expensive (autonomous driving, robotics, federated indoor navigation). The interpretability angle (visualizing which anchor was mislocalized to diagnose failures) is a step toward trustworthy deployment in safety-critical settings. Key limitations remain: pseudo-label quality caps gains on the harder Nr3D benchmark (77% anchor localization accuracy vs. human), the Pathway module cannot handle multi-path reasoning, and the anchor localization heuristic is tailored to ScanNet. Open questions include whether improved pseudo-label generation (e.g., with stronger LLMs or learned parsers) can close the gap to human-annotated performance, and whether the CoT formulation generalizes beyond indoor scenes.
