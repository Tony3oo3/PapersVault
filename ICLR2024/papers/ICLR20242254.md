## Problem

Existing query-based membership inference attacks on diffusion models, such as SecMI, require 60+2 queries to iteratively compute deterministic intermediate points, making them computationally expensive as models scale. Additionally, prior MIA research on diffusion models has exclusively targeted image generation, leaving the vulnerability of audio generation models (e.g., text-to-speech) entirely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242254/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The attack takes a single sample (image or audio) and requires exactly two queries to the target diffusion model: one at t=0 to obtain the noise prediction ε_θ(x_0, 0), and one at a chosen time t to obtain ε_θ(x_t, t). The output is a binary membership decision produced by comparing the attack metric R_{t,p} against a threshold τ, which can be calibrated from a surrogate model. The method applies to both discrete-time models (DDPM, Stable Diffusion) and continuous-time models (Grad-TTS), with the metric formula adapted accordingly.

## Method

PIA exploits a key property of DDIM (Theorem 1): when σ_t=0, knowing x_0 and any single point x_k on the trajectory determines all other points. The method uses the model's own output at t=0, ε_θ(x_0, 0), as a proxy for the true noise ε_0, which is valid because α̅_0 ≈ 1 makes the approximation tight. This single query establishes a 'groundtruth trajectory,' and a second query at time t yields the model's predicted noise. The attack metric R_{t,p} is the ℓ_p-norm between the t=0 noise prediction and the t-time noise prediction; training samples, which the model fits better, produce smaller R_{t,p}. For continuous-time diffusion models, the metric is derived from the ODE form of the reverse SDE, replacing the discrete DDIM update with the drift term f_t(x_t) − ½g_t²s_θ(x_t,t). A normalized variant (PIAN) rescales the t=0 output to approximate a standard normal distribution, improving TPR@1% FPR on some models.

## Results

On Grad-TTS (continuous-time diffusion), PIA achieves 99.6% AUC and 94.2% TPR@1% FPR on LJSpeech, 87.8% AUC and 20.6% TPR@1% FPR on VCTK, and 95.4% AUC and 30.0% TPR@1% FPR on LibriTTS, using only 2 queries (1+1). On DDPM (discrete-time diffusion), PIA reaches 88.5% AUC and 13.7% TPR@1% FPR on CIFAR-10, 89.6% AUC and 17.1% TPR@1% FPR on TinyImageNet, and 89.4% AUC and 19.6% TPR@1% FPR on CIFAR-100. PIA requires only 2 queries versus SecMI's 12–62 queries, yielding a 5–10× speedup while matching or exceeding SecMI's AUC and substantially outperforming it on TPR@1% FPR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PIA is compared against two baselines: the Naive Attack (Matsumoto et al., 2023), which directly uses the training loss, and SecMI (Duan et al., 2023), which iteratively computes the t-error over multiple steps. Against the Naive Attack, PIA wins decisively on every dataset and metric — for example, on VCTK, PIA achieves 87.8% AUC vs. 83.4% and 20.6% TPR@1% FPR vs. 6.1%. Against SecMI, PIA matches or slightly exceeds AUC (e.g., 99.6% vs. 99.5% on LJSpeech; 88.5% vs. 88.1% on CIFAR-10) while substantially outperforming on TPR@1% FPR (e.g., 30.0% vs. 19.7% on LibriTTS; 13.7% vs. 9.11% on CIFAR-10), with an average improvement of 5.4–17.8% depending on the model. The key efficiency gain is that PIA requires only 2 queries (one at t=0, one at time t) compared to SecMI's 12–62 queries, reducing computational cost to 3.2–17% of SecMI. PIAN (the normalized variant) further boosts TPR@1% FPR on some models (e.g., 44.7% vs. 30.0% on LibriTTS) but underperforms PIA on Stable Diffusion, where the normalization assumption breaks down.

## Relation to Prior Work

PIA builds directly on DDIM's deterministic trajectory property (σ_t=0) to prove that knowing x_0 and any single intermediate point x_k determines the entire diffusion path (Theorem 1). It departs from SecMI (Duan et al., 2023), which iteratively computes forward-backward errors over many timesteps (60+2 queries), by using the model's own output at t=0 as the initial noise, collapsing the attack to just 2 queries. The Naive Attack (Matsumoto et al., 2023) is subsumed as a special case that ignores the stochasticity of the training loss. The paper also extends the MIA-for-diffusion-models literature, previously confined to image generation, into the text-to-speech domain for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6× query reduction with matching or superior AUC/TPR@1% FPR makes MIA on large diffusion models practically feasible, which matters as models scale. The finding that mel-spectrogram-output TTS models are highly vulnerable (AUC ≈ 99.6) while audio-output models are near-random (AUC ≈ 52–54) provides a concrete design guideline: audio-native outputs appear to be a natural defense against membership inference. Open questions include why image-like representations are inherently more memorized, whether this robustness gap persists at larger scale, and whether the intermediate-output access requirement (not fully black-box) limits real-world applicability. PIAN's failure on Stable Diffusion also suggests the normalization assumption is fragile for large pretrained models.
