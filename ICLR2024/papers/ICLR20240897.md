## Problem

Diffusion models generate I.I.D. samples, which is suboptimal when a finite sample budget must cover a diverse distribution: redundant draws waste the budget on already-discovered modes while leaving others uncaptured. The paper asks how to enforce diversity across a finite set of samples while preserving the finite-time sampling guarantee that makes diffusion models superior to MCMC-based alternatives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240897/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is plug-and-play on top of any pre-trained diffusion score model (no retraining). For text-to-image, it consumes a text prompt and produces a batch of 4 images at 512×512 via Stable Diffusion v1.5, with the guidance kernel operating in DINO feature space or downsampled VAE latent pixels. For molecular conformer generation, it consumes a molecular graph and produces an ensemble of 3D conformers via torsional diffusion, with the kernel defined over dihedral-angle differences on the torsional manifold and made invariant to atom-permutation symmetries.

## Method

Particle guidance modifies the reverse diffusion SDE by adding the gradient of a time-evolving joint potential Φ_t(x₁,…,xₙ) that is permutation-invariant over all n particles, so each particle's drift combines the standard score (pulling toward data) with the potential gradient (pushing away from other particles). Two instantiations are provided: fixed-potential PG, where Φ_t is a handcrafted negative sum of pairwise RBF similarity kernels requiring zero additional training, and learned-potential PG, where Φ_t is trained via a Doob h-transform regression objective to provably sample from the maximum-entropy-optimal joint distribution under a diversity constraint, optionally preserving marginals. Theoretically, the paper derives via Eynman-Kac the exact joint distribution generated by any arbitrary guidance potential, and shows the connection to SVGD, metadynamics, and Poisson flow generative models. Empirically, the method improves in-batch diversity at matched CLIP/Aesthetic scores on Stable Diffusion and reduces median AMR by 19% (precision) and 8% (recall) on GEOM-DRUGS with torsional diffusion.

## Results

On the GEOM-DRUGS molecular conformer generation benchmark, applying particle guidance to Torsional Diffusion (without retraining) reduces the median Average Minimum RMSD by 8% for recall (0.565 → 0.520 Å) and 19% for precision (0.729 → 0.594 Å), while simultaneously improving median recall coverage from 80.0% to 82.6% and median precision coverage from 56.9% to 78.1%. For text-to-image generation with Stable Diffusion v1.5 on COCO 2014, particle guidance consistently achieves lower in-batch similarity (i.e., greater diversity) at the same CLIP and Aesthetic quality scores across classifier-free guidance scales of 6–10, pushing the diversity–quality Pareto frontier outward relative to I.I.D. sampling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Particle guidance applied to Torsional Diffusion outperforms all five baselines on every metric in Table 1. Against the strongest baseline, Torsional Diffusion itself, it improves median recall coverage by 2.6 points (80.0% → 82.6%), reduces median recall AMR by 0.045 Å (0.565 → 0.520), raises median precision coverage by 21.2 points (56.9% → 78.1%), and cuts median precision AMR by 0.135 Å (0.729 → 0.594). Against OMEGA (the best non-diffusion baseline), TD w/ PG more than doubles median precision coverage (33.3% → 78.1%) and reduces median precision AMR by 0.260 Å. GeoMol and GeoDiff trail far behind on both coverage and AMR, with GeoDiff's median precision coverage of only 14.5% and precision AMR of 1.090 Å being the weakest of all methods. In the text-to-image setting, particle guidance dominates I.I.D. sampling on the diversity–quality trade-off: at any fixed CLIP or Aesthetic score, PG yields a lower in-batch DINO similarity, and at any fixed in-batch similarity, PG achieves a higher CLIP/Aesthetic score, with the DINO feature-space kernel outperforming the pixel-space kernel.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classifier guidance mechanism of diffusion models (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021) from a single-sample classifier score to a joint-particle potential that enforces diversity across a batch of samples simultaneously. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate lines of work: the finite-time sampling guarantee of score-based diffusion models (Song et al., 2021) and the diversity-enforcing potentials long used in molecular simulations (metadynamics, coupled replicas) and variational inference (SVGD). The key departure is that prior methods in those fields rely on MCMC or iterative particle updates that lack finite-time guarantees, whereas particle guidance inherits the diffusion model's ability to sample in a fixed number of steps independent of data dimension. The paper also establishes a formal equivalence showing that under specific kernel and drift choices, particle guidance reduces to a time-dependent variant of SVGD, clarifying why diffusion-based sampling avoids SVGD's known mode-collapse pathology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that particle guidance is a drop-in modification to any existing trained diffusion model requiring no retraining, making it immediately applicable to production systems like Stable Diffusion or Torsional Diffusion. The theoretical contributions (exact joint distribution via Feynman-Kac, optimal potential learning via Doob h-transform, marginal preservation) provide a principled foundation that goes beyond the heuristic 'add a repulsive kernel' approach. Open questions include: how to choose the time-evolving potential schedule optimally without grid search, whether the quadratic scaling in particle count limits applicability to very large ensembles (e.g., thousands of conformers), and whether the learned-potential approach generalizes beyond the synthetic experiments shown. The paper also notes that overly aggressive guidance weights degrade sample quality, suggesting a fundamental diversity-quality tradeoff that remains to be better characterized.
