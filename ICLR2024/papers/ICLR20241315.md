## Problem

While Geerts (2020) established that the 3-WL test is equivalent to the algebraic language fragment ML(L3) over the adjacency matrix, no systematic procedure existed to derive a GNN architecture from an arbitrary language fragment with provable expressiveness guarantees. Prior models like GNNML3 were only shown to exceed 1-WL expressiveness without formal 3-WL equivalence, and PPGN's 3-WL power relied on MLP universality rather than a direct algebraic derivation. This paper fills that gap by proposing a generic framework that translates reduced Context-Free Grammars into GNN layer architectures with provable WL-equivalent expressiveness.

## Input / Output

The model takes as input the graph adjacency matrix A, a node feature matrix H^(0) of size n×(f_n+1) (features concatenated with a column of ones), and an edge feature tensor C^(0) of size n×n×(f_e+1) (adjacency stacked with edge features along the third dimension). Internally, the model maintains a 3D tensor C^(l) for edge/matrix embeddings and a 2D matrix H^(l) for node/vector embeddings, both with a fixed stacking dimension S^(l) per layer. Outputs are produced via permutation-equivariant readout functions (sum over node embeddings, sum over diagonal and off-diagonal components of the edge tensor) followed by task-specific fully connected layers yielding scalar regression values, class logits, or per-node predictions.

## Method

The framework proceeds in three steps: (1) constructing an exhaustive CFG G_{L3} whose variables (M, Vc, Vr, S) and rules enumerate all operations in ML(L3); (2) reducing this grammar to r-G_{L3} with only two variables (Vc, M) and four rules (Vc→MVc|1, M→M⊙M|MM|diag(Vc)|A) by exploiting algebraic identities (e.g., M⊙(Vc·Vr)=diag(Vc)M·diag(Vr)) and symmetry arguments; (3) translating the reduced CFG into a GNN layer where each rule is computed in parallel using learnable linear combinations L_i that select inputs from the memory tensors, and MLPs perform rule selection, dimension compression, and pointwise nonlinearities. The key architectural insight is that the grammar's production rules directly map to the layer's update equations (Eqs. 3–4), and the skip connection on C^(l) guarantees memory persistence across layers, ensuring that G²N² can produce any matrix or vector in L(r-G_{L3}) after l iterations, making it provably 3-WL expressive.

## Results

On QM9 regression (12 targets), G²N² achieves the best MAE on every target, with the most dramatic improvement on R² (0.342 vs. 3.78 for PPGN, a 10× reduction) and ZPVE (0.0000951 vs. 0.000399). On the TUD classification benchmark, G²N² ranks 1st on MUTAG (92.5%), PTC (72.3%), and Proteins (80.1%), and 2nd–3rd on the remaining three datasets. On spectral filtering, G²N² matches or slightly exceeds CHEBNET and GNNML3 on all three filter types (low-pass 0.9996, high-pass 0.9994, band-pass 0.8206 R²), while PPGN collapses on band-pass (0.1041).

## Baselines & Comparisons

G²N² outperforms PPGN (the strongest 3-WL baseline) on all 12 QM9 targets, with the largest gap on R² (0.342 vs. 3.78 MAE) and ZPVE (0.0000951 vs. 0.000399), while also training ~25% faster (98s vs. 129s per epoch). Against subgraph-based GNNs (GSNs, I²-GNN, NGNN, DeepLRP), G²N² achieves substantially lower MAE on QM9 (e.g., 0.0162 vs. 0.206 for I²-GNN on U) and wins on 4 of 6 TUD classification datasets (MUTAG 92.5% vs. 92.2%, PTC 72.3% vs. 68.2%, Proteins 80.1% vs. 77.4%, IMDB-M 54.0% vs. 54.3%—a near-tie). On TUD, G²N² trails GSNs slightly on NCI1 (82.8% vs. 83.5%) and IMDB-B (76.8% vs. 77.8%), but these gaps are within one standard deviation. For spectral filtering, G²N² matches CHEBNET and GNNML3 on all three filter types (R² ≥ 0.82 for band-pass), whereas PPGN fails catastrophically on band-pass (0.1041 R²), confirming that G²N²'s explicit grammar-derived operations avoid the MLP-width bottleneck that limits PPGN's spectral expressiveness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Geerts (2020), who reformulated the 1-WL and 3-WL tests as fragments of the matrix language MATLANG (Brijder et al., 2019), and on Balcilar et al. (2021), who proposed GNNML1 and GNNML3 as GNNs inspired by these fragments. The key departure is the introduction of a generic framework that uses Context-Free Grammars (CFGs) to systematically translate algebraic language fragments into GNN architectures, with a grammar reduction scheme that removes redundant rules while preserving expressiveness. Unlike PPGN (Maron et al., 2019a), whose 3-WL power relies on mimicking 2-FWL colouring/hashing via MLP universality, G²N² inherits 3-WL expressiveness directly from the reduced CFG, avoiding the need for deep/wide MLPs to approximate missing operations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, provable pathway from algebraic language fragments to GNN architectures, which the authors argue can be extended to higher-order WL tests (beyond 3-WL) by operating on higher-order tensors. The grammar reduction scheme offers a practical tool for balancing expressiveness against computational cost, as demonstrated by the ablation study. Open questions include whether the framework scales to 4-WL or higher, whether the CFG-to-GNN translation remains tractable for richer operation sets, and whether the approach generalizes beyond GNNs to other learning paradigms as the authors speculate. A limitation is that the current instantiation is still bounded by 3-WL, and the spectral filtering advantage over PPGN may partly reflect PPGN's architectural constraints rather than a fundamental expressiveness gap.
