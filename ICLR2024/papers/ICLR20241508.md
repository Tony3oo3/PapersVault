## Problem

Conventional MLP-based PINNs treat each spatiotemporal point independently, neglecting the temporal dependencies inherent in hyperbolic and parabolic PDEs where the state at time t+Δt depends on the state at time t. This point-to-point formulation fails to propagate initial condition constraints globally, leading to characteristic failure modes where approximations are accurate near t=0 but degrade into overly smooth solutions elsewhere.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241508/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single vectorized spatiotemporal point [x, t] ∈ ℝ^d as input and internally expands it into a pseudo-sequence of k points { [x, t], [x, t+Δt], …, [x, t+(k−1)Δt] } via a Pseudo Sequence Generator. The output is a sequential approximation of length k, where only the first element corresponds to the desired solution û(x,t); the remaining elements serve as auxiliary temporal context for the attention mechanism. The modified loss regularizes all k outputs for residual and boundary points but only the first output for initial conditions, since only t=0 matches the initial constraint.

## Method

PINNsFormer replaces the point-to-point MLP with a Transformer encoder-decoder that operates on generated pseudo-sequences, using multi-head self-attention in the encoder and encoder-decoder cross-attention in the decoder to explicitly model temporal dependencies across discrete time steps. A Spatio-Temporal Mixer (fully-connected linear projection) embeds low-dimensional inputs into a higher-dimensional space before attention, and a novel Wavelet activation (ω₁sin(x) + ω₂cos(x) with learnable ω₁, ω₂) replaces ReLU, providing a universal approximator grounded in the Real Fourier Transform. The training objective is a sequential PINNs loss that separately regularizes residual, boundary, and initial condition terms over the pseudo-sequence, with initial conditions applied only to the first element. The architecture relies solely on linear layers and non-linear activations (no convolutions or RNNs), keeping computational overhead approximately linear in the pseudo-sequence length k.

## Results

On the convection PDE (a known PINNs failure mode), PINNsFormer achieves rMAE 0.023 and rRMSE 0.027, compared to rMAE 0.778 and rRMSE 0.840 for standard PINNs — a ~34× reduction in rMAE. On the 1D-reaction PDE, PINNsFormer reaches rMAE 0.015 and rRMSE 0.030 versus rMAE 0.982 and rRMSE 0.981 for PINNs. On the high-dimensional 2D Navier-Stokes PDE, PINNsFormer achieves rMAE 0.384 and rRMSE 0.280, outperforming PINNs (rMAE 13.08, rRMSE 9.08) by over 30×. When combined with the NTK learning scheme on the 1D-wave PDE, PINNsFormer+NTK reaches rMAE 0.054 and rRMSE 0.058, nearly 3× better than PINNs+NTK (rMAE 0.140, rRMSE 0.149).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PINNsFormer decisively outperforms all four baselines across every PDE evaluated. Against standard PINNs (Raissi et al., 2019), the improvement is dramatic: on the convection PDE, PINNsFormer reduces rMAE from 0.778 to 0.023 (~34×), and on the 1D-reaction PDE from 0.982 to 0.015 (~65×), while all MLP-based baselines remain stuck in failure modes with near-unity errors. QRes (Bu & Karpatne, 2021) and FLS (Wong et al., 2022) show only marginal improvements over vanilla PINNs on the failure-mode PDEs (e.g., FLS rMAE 0.674 vs. PINNs 0.778 on convection) and are similarly outperformed on Navier-Stokes (FLS rMAE 3.98 vs. PINNsFormer 0.384). When the NTK optimization scheme (Wang et al., 2022a) is applied, PINNsFormer+NTK still achieves roughly 2.6× lower rMAE than PINNs+NTK on the 1D-wave PDE (0.054 vs. 0.140), demonstrating that the architectural advantage is orthogonal to and composable with optimization-level improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PINNs framework (Raissi et al., 2019) by replacing the point-to-point MLP architecture with a Transformer-based encoder-decoder that explicitly models temporal dependencies through pseudo-sequences generated from single spatiotemporal inputs. It departs from prior PINNs variants (QRes, FLS, NTK-based methods) that address failure modes through architectural tweaks or optimization schemes, arguing instead that the root cause is the neglect of temporal propagation inherent in hyperbolic and parabolic PDEs. The Wavelet activation function draws on Fourier integral theory to provide a universal approximator with continuous derivatives, positioning it alongside ReLU and Sigmoid in the classical universal approximation literature while being specifically motivated by PDE solution structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete architectural limitation of PINNs (absence of temporal dependency modeling) and shows that a relatively simple Transformer adaptation with pseudo-sequences can dramatically reduce failure-mode errors, suggesting that many previously attributed 'optimization' failures may actually be architectural. The Wavelet activation, with its Fourier-decomposition-based universal approximation proof, has potential applicability beyond PINNs to any task where smooth, frequency-aware activations are beneficial. However, the experimental scope is limited to four relatively simple PDEs (mostly 1D), the baselines exclude more recent and stronger PINNs variants, and the ~3x computational overhead may become prohibitive for high-dimensional or long-time-horizon problems. Open questions include whether the pseudo-sequence abstraction generalizes to genuinely high-dimensional PDEs and whether the approach scales to complex multi-physics systems.
