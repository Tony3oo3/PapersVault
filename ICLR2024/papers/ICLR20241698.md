## Problem

Retrieval-augmented language models (RALMs) prepend full retrieved documents (often hundreds of words) to the input at inference time, which substantially increases computational cost, causes models to miss information in long contexts, and can degrade performance when irrelevant documents are included. No prior method efficiently compresses retrieved evidence into a concise form while optimizing for the downstream LM's task performance, nor does it provide a mechanism to selectively skip augmentation when retrieved documents are unhelpful.

## Input / Output

The method takes an input query x and a set of N retrieved documents D (each ~100 words, retrieved via BM25 or Contriever) and produces a compressed textual summary s that is prepended to x before being fed to a frozen target LM. The summary is natural-language text (not soft prompts or embeddings), making it interpretable and transferable across different LMs. Crucially, s can be an empty string when the retrieved documents are irrelevant or unhelpful, implementing selective augmentation. The extractive compressor outputs a concatenation of top-k selected sentences, while the abstractive compressor generates a free-form summary of variable length.

## Method

RECOMP introduces two compressors trained to optimize end-task performance of a target LM rather than human-readable summarization quality. The extractive compressor is a 110M-parameter dual-encoder (initialized from Contriever) trained with a contrastive loss: for each query, the sentence yielding the highest log-likelihood (or correct answer) when prepended is the positive, and up to five sentences with lower scores are hard negatives selected by their Contriever similarity to the query. The abstractive compressor is a 775M-parameter T5-large encoder-decoder trained via symbolic knowledge distillation from GPT-3.5: multiple summarization prompts generate candidate summaries, the one producing the best end-task score is selected as the target, and if no summary improves over no-retrieval, the target is set to an empty string. This end-task filtering during data generation is the key mechanism enabling both effectiveness and selective augmentation, distinguishing RECOMP from standard query-focused summarization.

## Results

On open-domain QA with Flan-UL2 (20B), RECOMP's abstractive compressor achieves 5% compression on NQ (36 tokens vs. 660 for top-5 documents) with only 2.35 EM drop (37.04 vs. 39.39), and 5% compression on TQA (32 tokens vs. 677) with 3.69 EM drop (58.68 vs. 62.37). On HotpotQA, the extractive compressor achieves 11% compression (75 tokens vs. 684) with 2.4 EM drop (30.40 vs. 32.80). On WikiText-103 language modeling, the abstractive compressor achieves 15 tokens (vs. 512 for top-5 docs) with minimal perplexity increase (33.64 vs. 32.90 for GPT-2; 10.66 vs. 10.57 for GPT-J), and the extractive compressor achieves 25% compression ratio with comparable performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to RALM without compression (Ram et al., 2023; Shi et al., 2023b), RECOMP achieves 94–95% token reduction on QA tasks with only 2–4 EM drop, and 97% reduction on language modeling with <1 PPL increase. Among extractive baselines, RECOMP's trained compressor outperforms BM25 (25.82→36.57 EM on NQ) and Contriever (30.06→36.57 EM) by 10.7 and 6.5 EM respectively, though DPR (34.32 EM) remains competitive on in-domain NQ but degrades out-of-domain. For abstractive compression, RECOMP's T5-based compressor substantially outperforms off-the-shelf T5 (25.90→37.04 EM on NQ, 55.18→58.68 on TQA) while using 3–5x more tokens, and matches GPT-3.5 (37.12 EM) on NQ with fewer tokens (36 vs. 56). Heuristic phrase-level methods (BoW, NE) consistently underperform both full-document prepending and RECOMP, confirming that structured compression is necessary. The extractive oracle (60.22 EM on NQ) far exceeds all baselines, indicating substantial headroom, while RECOMP's trained compressors close much of this gap at 5–11% compression rates.

## Relation to Prior Work

RECOMP sits at the intersection of retrieval-augmented language models (RALMs) and query-focused multi-document summarization, but departs from both in its training objective: rather than optimizing for human-readable summaries or retrieval relevance, the compressors are trained to maximize a downstream LM's end-task performance when the summary is prepended. It contrasts with soft-prompt compression (Wingate et al., Chevalier et al.) and context distillation (Snell et al.) by producing interpretable textual summaries that transfer across LMs without gradient updates, and with decision-focused summarization (Hsu & Tan) by operating on multi-document retrieval sets and enabling selective augmentation (empty output). The abstractive compressor is built via symbolic knowledge distillation (West et al.) from GPT-3.5, but filtered by end-task performance rather than intrinsic summarization metrics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RECOMP offers a practical, plug-and-play efficiency gain for RAG pipelines: 5–25% token compression with minimal performance loss and 2× inference speedup, applicable to black-box LMs without fine-tuning. However, the paper reveals important limitations: compressors trained for one LM transfer well for language modeling but degrade substantially for QA tasks on different LMs, and the selective augmentation mechanism (deciding when to output an empty summary) achieves very low F1 (0.10–0.20), indicating the compressor often fails to recognize when retrieval is unhelpful. Open questions include building model-agnostic compressors for complex multi-hop tasks like HotpotQA and improving the reliability of selective augmentation.
