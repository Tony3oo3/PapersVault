## Problem

Grokking—the phenomenon where a model generalizes long after fitting training data—has been attributed to complex mechanisms like representation learning, loss slingshots, or random walks among solutions, but no solvable model had captured its dynamics analytically. This paper asks whether grokking can arise in the simplest possible setting (linear networks on linear tasks) and, if so, whether it reflects a genuine transition from memorization to understanding or is merely an artifact of how accuracy is measured.

## Input / Output

The model consumes iid Gaussian input vectors x_i ∈ R^{d_in} and a fixed teacher weight matrix T, with the student initialized from N(0, 1/(2d_in·d_out)). The key output is a closed-form analytical expression for the grokking time Δt_grok = t_gen* − t_tr*, where each loss crosses the threshold ε/4, rather than a numerical trajectory. Unusually, accuracy is an explicit deterministic function of loss (Erf(√(ε/2L)) for scalar output, or an incomplete-gamma expression for d_out > 1), meaning the entire generalization curve is determined by a single scalar—the loss—rather than by the full weight configuration.

## Method

The paper solves the full-batch gradient-flow dynamics of a linear teacher-student model exactly: D(t) = exp(−2η₀Σ_tr t) D₀, where Σ_tr is the empirical training covariance. The eigenvalue spectrum of Σ_tr is characterized by the Marchenko-Pastur distribution, yielding closed-form expressions for training loss L_tr ≈ E_{ν~MP}[ν e^{−4η₀νt}] and generalization loss L_gen ≈ E_{ν~MP}[e^{−4η₀νt}]. The central insight is that grokking arises purely because gradients are set by the training covariance (which has a non-trivial MP spectrum), causing L_gen to decay slower than L_tr, while the apparent 'sharp transition' in accuracy is an artifact of the Erf mapping from loss to accuracy. The grokking time is derived in closed form as Δt_grok ≈ −log(1−√λ) / [2η₀(1−√λ)²], depending primarily on the aspect ratio λ = d_in/N_tr. Extensions to d_out > 1, weight decay, two-layer networks, and nonlinear activations are handled by rescaling the effective learning rate and modifying the accuracy-loss mapping, with the same qualitative mechanism preserved.

## Results

The paper derives an exact closed-form expression for the grokking time in a linear teacher-student model: Δt_grok ≈ −log(1−√λ)/(2η₀(1−√λ)²), which diverges quadratically as λ→1 (i.e., d_in/N_tr → 1) and vanishes for λ→0. For the overparameterized regime (λ>1), the generalization loss plateaus at 1−1/λ, preventing perfect generalization without regularization. All analytical predictions (training/generalization loss, accuracy, and grokking time as functions of λ, d_out, γ, and initialization) match numerical experiments with zero fitting parameters. The key finding is that grokking is purely an artifact of the accuracy threshold: the loss dynamics are oblivious to the ε/4 threshold, and no qualitative transition in generalization ability occurs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary empirical comparison is with the modular addition task analyzed by Gromov (2023), presented in Appendix G (Figure 8). The paper shows that the grokking time difference in modular addition (p=48²) as a function of the effective ratio λ = d_in/N_tr exhibits a ~(λ−c)^{−2} divergence, qualitatively matching the (1−√λ)^{−2} scaling predicted by the linear model. Additionally, the accuracy-vs-training-loss curves for both the linear teacher-student model and the modular addition task follow similar trends as grokking begins, suggesting the linear model's mechanism may generalize. However, this comparison is explicitly preliminary and qualitative; the paper does not claim the linear model quantitatively reproduces modular addition dynamics, noting that real grokking scenarios can feature non-monotonic loss evolution and special weight structures absent in the linear setup.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the teacher-student framework of Seung et al. (1992) and the grokking construction of Liu et al. (2023) by applying random matrix theory (Marchenko-Pastur spectral analysis) to derive exact closed-form solutions for training and generalization dynamics in linear estimators. It departs from prior mechanistic explanations of grokking (slingshots, oscillations, representation learning) by showing that in the simplest solvable setting, grokking requires none of these ingredients—it emerges purely from the mismatch between the training covariance and the identity generalization covariance, combined with a threshold-based accuracy definition. The analytical tools draw on the statistical physics literature on gradient-flow dynamics (Sompolinsky, Advani, Saxe, Loureiro) but apply them specifically to the grokking phenomenon, which prior RMT-based analyses had not addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first exact analytical predictions for grokking time as a function of input dimension, sample size, output dimension, regularization, and initialization, all verified numerically with no free parameters. Its central conceptual claim—that grokking in linear models is a metric artifact rather than a qualitative phase transition from memorization to understanding—reframes how the phenomenon should be interpreted, even if the specific closed-form solutions do not transfer directly. The authors acknowledge the model does not capture non-monotonic loss or special weight structures observed in real grokking, and the preliminary modular-addition results (Appendix G) are suggestive but not conclusive. Open questions include whether the core mechanism (accuracy as a rapidly-varying function of loss with a data-dependent threshold) persists under realistic data covariances, finite learning rates, and adaptive optimizers.
