## Problem

Visual prompting (VP) design choices—prompt size, source model, and label-mapping strategy—have been studied in isolation, with no systematic framework to jointly optimize them and no unified benchmark for fair cross-method comparison. Existing VP papers evaluate on arbitrarily selected datasets, making it difficult to determine which configurations actually matter for downstream performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241991/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

AutoVP takes standard RGB images, resizes them to a tunable scale (0.5×, 1.0×, or 1.5× of the source model's input resolution, e.g., 128×128 within a 224×224 canvas), and pads the remaining border with a trainable frame-shaped visual prompt. The frozen pre-trained model produces source-domain logits (or CLIP cosine similarities against text-encoder embeddings of target class names), which are then mapped to target class labels via one of four output-mapping strategies. The framework also extends to pixel-wise segmentation masks (via DeepLabV3) and detection boxes (via OWL-ViT), though classification is the primary focus.

## Method

AutoVP is a modular, end-to-end framework with four jointly optimized components: (1) Input Scaling, which uses differentiable image resizing (Kornia) to learn the optimal prompt-to-image ratio; (2) Visual Prompt, a universal pixel-level frame perturbation δ passed through a sigmoid and masked to the border region; (3) Pre-trained Classifier selection among ResNet18, ResNeXt101-IG, Swin-T, and CLIP (all frozen); and (4) Output Label Mapping, offering nonparametric (FreqMap, SemanticMap) and trainable (IterMap, FullyMap) strategies. Hyperparameter tuning over 222 configurations is performed with Ray Tune and an ASHA early-stopping scheduler, running 2–5 epoch trials to select the best setting before full 200-epoch training. A key innovation is weight initialization for FullyMap with CLIP, which sets the linear layer's diagonal to 1 (connecting each target class to its corresponding text-prompt template) so the mapping starts from a semantically meaningful state rather than random weights, enabling FullyMap to compete with IterMap/FreqMap during the short tuning phase.

## Results

AutoVP achieves 81.02% average accuracy across 12 downstream image-classification tasks when allowed to select the optimal pre-trained model, outperforming the best prior VP method (ILM-VP) by 2.1% and LP by 3.4%. With CLIP fixed as the backbone, AutoVP reaches 80.6% average accuracy, beating ILM-VP (78.5%) by up to 6.7% on 9 of 12 datasets and surpassing LP (79.9%) by as much as 27.5% on SVHN. With ResNet18, AutoVP's 70.02% average exceeds ILM-VP's 45.2% by 24.8%. In data-limited settings (10% and 1% training data), AutoVP consistently outperforms LP across all 12 datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLIP-VP (Bahng et al., 2022), AutoVP achieves a 4.6% average accuracy gain (80.6% vs. 76.0%) with CLIP, with the largest margin on Flowers102 (+20.1%) and UCF101 (+7.0%). Against ILM-VP (Chen et al., 2023b), AutoVP leads by 2.1% on average with CLIP (80.6% vs. 78.5%) and by a dramatic 24.8% with ResNet18 (70.02% vs. 45.2%), winning on 9 of 12 datasets in the CLIP setting. Against CLIP-TP zero-shot inference (Radford et al., 2021), AutoVP's 81.02% average exceeds the 49.54% baseline by over 31 points, confirming that learned visual prompts plus label mapping far surpass fixed text prompts. Against BlackVIP (Oh et al., 2023), AutoVP achieves a 16% average improvement (80.4% vs. 64.6%), though the authors note the comparison is somewhat unfair due to differing objectives (white-box vs. black-box). AutoVP also surpasses LP by 0.7% on average (Table 1) and by up to 27.5% on SVHN, while maintaining greater robustness on CIFAR-10-C corruption benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the visual prompting / model reprogramming line of work initiated by Tsai et al. (2020) and Chen (2022), and specifically extends CLIP-VP (Bahng et al., 2022) and ILM-VP (Chen et al., 2023b), which each optimized a single design axis (prompt size or label mapping) in isolation. AutoVP departs from this component-by-component tradition by formulating VP design as a joint optimization problem over input scaling, prompt parameters, source model selection, and output label mapping, solved via grid search with ASHA early stopping. It also introduces two novel components absent from prior VP work: automated input-image scaling as a differentiable optimization axis, and semantic weight initialization for the FullyMap layer when paired with CLIP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 12-dataset benchmark addresses a real gap in the VP literature, where prior methods were evaluated on arbitrarily chosen subsets, making cross-method comparison unreliable. The finding that visual prompts can be counterproductive with smaller backbones (ResNet18) and that OOD datasets benefit disproportionately from VP provides actionable guidance for practitioners choosing when and how to deploy VP. Key limitations include the exclusion of learning rate and weight decay from the tuning space, the focus on classification (with only preliminary segmentation/detection results), and the reliance on a fixed set of four pre-trained models. Open questions include whether the framework extends to generative tasks, whether the OOD-benefit pattern holds for newer foundation models, and whether the 222-configuration search space can be further pruned for efficiency.
