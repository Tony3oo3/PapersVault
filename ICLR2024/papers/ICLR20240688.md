## Problem

Virtual sensing methods for inferring signals at unmonitored locations typically rely on spatial proximity to interpolate from neighboring sensors, but this breaks down when sensor coverage is sparse due to cost or ecological constraints. In such sparse settings, no nearby observations exist to support interpolation, and existing approaches either ignore the multivariate structure of the data or cannot exploit correlations between the target variable and available covariates at the same location. This paper addresses the gap by formulating multivariate virtual sensing as a problem of learning both inter-location and intra-location dependencies in a data-driven manner, without relying on pre-defined spatial relationships.

## Input / Output

The method takes a collection of N multivariate time series (each with D channels and T time steps) where individual channels may be entirely missing at certain locations, along with spatial coordinates for all locations. A binary mask m^d[n] indicates channel availability, and the model must reconstruct the missing channels at target locations. The output is a full time series for each missing channel, accompanied by uncertainty estimates derived from a three-quantile (pinball) loss. The model is inherently transductive: it learns a static embedding per location, so adding a new sensor requires fine-tuning its embedding rather than retraining from scratch.

## Method

GgNet propagates information through a nested graph structure: an inter-location graph G (N nodes with a learned N×N adjacency matrix from node embeddings) models T→Y dependencies across spatially distant but functionally similar locations, while a shared intra-location graph g (D nodes with a learned D×D adjacency) models C→Y dependencies among channels at each location. The architecture stacks temporal convolutions (per-channel, with dilated filters for multi-scale dynamics), G-convolutions (spatial message passing), and g-convolutions (channel-wise message passing) in alternating blocks, conditioned on both location and channel node embeddings. The adjacency matrices are learned end-to-end via MLPs over the embeddings rather than derived from geographic distance, enabling the model to capture functional similarities invisible to spatial proximity. Training uses a weighted reconstruction loss with higher weight on masked-out channels plus a three-quantile pinball loss for uncertainty, and a channel-masking strategy during training to explicitly bias the model toward the virtual sensing task rather than generic imputation.

## Results

On the daily climatic dataset (N=235 locations, D=10 channels, T=10958 steps), GgNet achieves a mean relative error (MRE) of 13.4±0.2%, outperforming the second-best method SAITS (15.5±0.5%) by 13.5% relative improvement. On the hourly climatic dataset (D=7, T=8760), GgNet reaches 20.4±0.6% MRE versus SAITS at 22.2±0.5%. On the photovoltaic energy production task, GgNet's advantage over SAITS grows with sensor density, from 3.0% at N=10 locations to 14.2% at N=200 locations (MAE 357±16 vs. 416±14 W·h).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GgNet outperforms all three baselines across every dataset and metric. Against SAITS (the strongest non-graph baseline, which models intra-location covariates via self-attention but ignores spatial relations), GgNet achieves a 13.5% relative MRE improvement on daily climatic data and 8.1% on hourly data; the gap is largest for channels poorly correlated with local covariates (e.g., wind speed: 23.9% vs. 26.9% MRE). Against GRIN (graph-based imputation relying on a fixed spatial-proximity adjacency matrix), GgNet's advantage is even more pronounced (19.8% vs. 13.4% daily MRE), particularly at geographically isolated locations where no nearby sensor exists. Against BRITS (bidirectional RNN imputation), GgNet reduces daily MRE from 20.5% to 13.4%. On the photovoltaic dataset, GgNet's lead over SAITS scales with the number of available locations (3.0% at N=10 to 14.2% at N=200), indicating that the learned inter-location graph becomes increasingly valuable as more spatial information is available to exploit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of GNN-based spatiotemporal imputation (GRIN, SPIN) and virtual sensing (IGNNK, INCREASE) by introducing a nested graph architecture that simultaneously models inter-location and intra-location dependencies. It adapts the learnable node embedding approach from Cini et al. (2023a) for the inter-location graph and the observation-plus-embedding encoding from Marisca et al. (2022) for the input layer, but departs fundamentally from prior work by learning the graph topology from data rather than relying on spatial proximity, and by adding a second (intra-location) graph to capture channel-to-channel correlations. This positions GgNet as the first architecture explicitly designed for multivariate virtual sensing in sparse settings where geographic distance is uninformative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: when sensor networks are too sparse for spatial interpolation to work, existing methods fail entirely, yet correlated covariates (e.g., satellite weather data) are often available at target locations. The nested graph design provides a clean, interpretable solution, and the t-SNE visualization of learned embeddings clustering by climate zone (without access to coordinates or labels) offers qualitative evidence that the model captures meaningful functional similarities. Key limitations include the transductive setting (new locations require retraining embeddings), O(N²) scaling of the inter-location graph, and the assumption of synchronous time series. Extending to inductive learning and asynchronous data (e.g., healthcare) are natural next steps that would broaden applicability significantly.
