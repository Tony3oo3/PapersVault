## Problem

Existing model-based optimization (MBO) methods for protein design struggle when high-fitness training samples are both rare (imbalance) and located far from the dominant low-fitness region in sequence space (separation). While imbalance has been studied, the separation challenge—where the desired optimum is poorly represented and distal to well-represented regions—remains largely unexplored, and prior weighted-MLE approaches degrade significantly as separation increases.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242196/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a training set of (x, y) pairs where x is a discrete protein sequence (one-hot encoded) or continuous design vector and y is a scalar property value (e.g., fitness, viability, solution quality). At each MBO iteration, it generates K candidate samples from the VAE decoder, evaluates them with a ground-truth property oracle, and retrains. The output is a set of generated design samples prioritized toward high-fitness regions, with performance measured by the relative improvement ΔY_max over the best training sample.

## Method

PPGVAE augments a standard VAE with a relationship loss that restructures the latent space geometry: it enforces log(Pr(μ_i)) − log(Pr(μ_j)) = τ(y_i − y_j) for all sample pairs, where τ is a temperature hyperparameter. Minimizing this loss is equivalent to minimizing the variance of log(Pr(μ_i)) − τy_i, which places samples with the same property on the same sphere in latent space, with higher-property spheres closer to the origin. Since the VAE prior is N(0, I), samples near the origin have higher generation probability, so high-fitness samples are naturally prioritized without explicit weighting. This avoids the reduced effective sample size problem of weighted MLE (where N_eff can drop to 1 under extreme imbalance), as all samples contribute fully to training. The final objective combines the standard VAE ELBO with the relationship loss scaled by λ_r/τ², and the method is trained iteratively over MBO rounds with a fixed temperature τ=5.

## Results

The primary metric is ΔY_max (relative improvement of the highest property found over the highest property in the initial train set), evaluated across five benchmarks (GMM, AAV, GB1, PhoQ, PINN) under varying imbalance ratios (ρ from 0.0125 to 1) and separation levels. PPGVAE achieves consistently higher ΔY_max than all baselines and, critically, maintains robust performance regardless of imbalance ratio or separation degree, whereas all other methods degrade as separation increases or high-fitness samples become rarer. PPGVAE also converges in fewer MBO steps and is less sensitive to reduced per-step sampling budget (N_s) compared to the strongest baseline CEM-PI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PPGVAE outperforms all five baselines on ΔY_max across every benchmark. CEM-PI (Snoek et al., 2012) is the most competitive weighting-based method, matching PPGVAE at low separation levels but degrading significantly as separation increases, particularly at low imbalance ratios. CbAS (Brookes et al., 2019) and RWR (Peters & Schaal, 2007) are consistently weaker than CEM-PI, with RWR showing the most extreme weighting and thus the lowest effective sample size. AdaLead (Sinai et al., 2020), an evolutionary greedy search, exhibits high variability due to its dependence on the starting sequence and generally underperforms the generative methods. Gómez-Bombarelli et al. (2018), compared on the toy MNIST latent space, barely generates samples from the top two highest-property classes, whereas PPGVAE generates them and their interpolations most frequently under the VAE prior. The key differentiator is that PPGVAE avoids weighted MLE entirely, maintaining N_eff = K and thus robustness to the severe imbalance (e.g., ρ = 0.0125) that causes other methods to fail.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on VAE-based generative models for design (Gómez-Bombarelli et al., 2018) and the broader model-based optimization (MBO) framework for protein sequence design, but departs fundamentally from the dominant weighting-based paradigm (CbAS, RWR, CEM-PI) that updates the search model via weighted MLE. Instead of assigning higher weights to high-fitness samples, PPGVAE restructures the latent space geometry through a novel relationship loss that maps higher-property samples closer to the origin, thereby increasing their generation probability under the standard VAE prior N(0,I). The paper also contrasts with methods that condition generation on property values or encode properties as separate latent variables, arguing that neither approach can achieve the desired prioritization without discarding or down-weighting samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The robustness to both imbalance and separation is practically critical for real protein design scenarios (e.g., engineering enzymes for unnatural substrates), where functional variants are rare and distal to the wild-type neighborhood. By avoiding weighted MLE entirely, PPGVAE sidesteps the effective sample size degradation that plagues weighting-based methods under severe imbalance, a point the paper supports with a clean variance analysis. Open questions include whether the structural constraint becomes overly restrictive in some design problems, how the method behaves under oracle uncertainty (the paper uses ground-truth oracles throughout), and whether additional exploratory mechanisms could further strengthen the approach. The generality across discrete (protein) and continuous (PINN) design spaces suggests the latent-space restructuring principle may transfer to other MBO settings.
