## Problem

Real-time recurrent learning (RTRL) offers conceptual advantages over BPTT—no activation caching, no gradient truncation, and native online learning—but its O(N⁴) time and O(N³) space complexity for fully recurrent networks make it impractical. Prior work has focused on approximation methods evaluated in diagnostic settings, leaving the true empirical promise of exact RTRL on challenging real-world tasks largely unexplored. This paper asks whether RTRL can be made tractable without approximation by exploiting architectural structure, and whether it delivers measurable benefits at scale.

## Input / Output

The system consumes raw RGB observation frames from first-person 3D game environments (DMLab-30, ProcGen, Atari 2600) in a POMDP setting, with action repetition of 4 for DMLab/Atari and no repetition for ProcGen. A frozen (pre-trained) convolutional vision stem encodes frames into feature vectors that feed the recurrent layer; the eLSTM then produces a discrete action (policy head) and a value estimate (critic head). Only the recurrent layer and heads above it are trained with RTRL, while the vision stem is trained separately with TBPTT since its sensitivity matrices would be intractable.

## Method

The paper introduces eLSTM, an LSTM variant where the recurrent connections use element-wise (scalar) weights w^f and w^z instead of full matrices, which introduces sparsity in the temporal Jacobian and reduces RTRL's sensitivity-matrix update to a diagonal scaling plus outer product—yielding O(N²) space and O(N²) per-step time with exact (non-approximate) gradients. Built on this architecture, the R2AC algorithm is an actor-critic method that combines RTRL for the recurrent policy with recurrent policy gradients, structured within the IMPALA distributed RL framework where the hyperparameter M controls update frequency and M-step TD learning but does not truncate gradients. The key derivation shows that for element-wise recurrence, each sensitivity entry ∂c_i(t)/∂F_{i,j} evolves via a simple scalar recursion (Eq. 10), and the full gradient reduces to a single outer product per parameter matrix (Eq. 14), implementable as standard PyTorch operations. The paper also demonstrates that stacking two such layers reintroduces full recurrence and intractable complexity, and that RTRL for certain linear Transformers/Fast Weight Programmers is similarly tractable in the one-layer case.

## Results

On DMLab-30 rooms_watermaze (mean episode length ~1000 steps), RTRL with eLSTM achieves 54.8±4.3 (M=100) and 52.3±1.9 (M=50) using fewer than 1.2B environmental frames, outperforming IMPALA (26.9 at 1B frames; 47.0 at 10B frames) and R2D2+ (49.0 at 10B frames). On rooms_select_nonmatching_object (~100 steps), RTRL (62.2±0.3) matches or slightly exceeds R2D2+ (63.6) while using ~8x fewer frames. In the architecture/algorithm comparison (Table 2, M=100), exact RTRL on eLSTM (54.8 on watermaze) substantially outperforms approximate RTRL (SnAp-1) on feLSTM (27.7), confirming that exact gradients matter for long-horizon credit assignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against IMPALA (Espeholt et al., 2018), RTRL with eLSTM achieves 52.3 on watermaze with <1.2B frames, surpassing IMPALA's 26.9 at 1B frames and even IMPALA's 47.0 at 10B frames — a ~10-point improvement with ~8x fewer environmental steps. Against R2D2/R2D2+ (Kapturowski et al., 2019), RTRL (M=50) scores 52.3 on watermaze versus R2D2's 45.9 and R2D2+'s 49.0 (both at 10B frames); on select_nonmatch, RTRL (62.0) is within 1.6 points of R2D2+ (63.6) while using far fewer frames. Against SnAp-1 (Menick et al., 2021), the paper's exact RTRL on eLSTM (62.2/54.8) clearly outperforms SnAp-1 applied to the closely related feLSTM architecture (53.6/27.7), demonstrating that approximation quality is a significant bottleneck for approximate RTRL methods on long-horizon tasks. On ProcGen Chaser and Atari (Breakout, Gravitar, Q*bert, Seaquest), RTRL matches or slightly exceeds TBPTT, with the exception of a slight degradation on MsPacman.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the classic RTRL algorithm (Williams & Zipser, 1989) and the IMPALA actor-critic framework (Espeholt et al., 2018), but departs from the dominant line of RTRL research that focuses on approximation methods (SnAp-1, low-rank decompositions, sparsity) tested on diagnostic tasks. Instead, it identifies that existing element-wise recurrent architectures (Quasi-RNN, Simple Recurrent Units, and a new eLSTM variant) make RTRL exactly tractable without approximation, a property traceable to Mozer (1989) and Gori et al. (1989) but never connected to modern architectures or evaluated at scale. The method also incorporates recurrent policy gradients (Wierstra et al., 2010) to enable untruncated credit assignment in POMDPs, and the paper explicitly contrasts its exact RTRL against approximate RTRL (SnAp-1) under matched architectural conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that RTRL's principled advantage (untruncated gradients) translates into a concrete ~8× compute efficiency gain on a challenging long-memory task (DMLab watermaze), outperforming IMPALA and R2D2 baselines trained on 10B frames with fewer than 1.2B frames. However, it also surfaces critical, previously under-discussed limitations: the multi-layer case reintroduces the original quartic/cubic complexity bottleneck, and for most practical tasks TBPTT with a sufficiently large span already suffices, raising the question of whether RTRL's principled elegance justifies its engineering complexity. The one-layer restriction and the need to train vision stems with TBPTT limit immediate practical adoption, leaving open the question of whether approximation-free RTRL can scale to the deep, multi-layer architectures that dominate modern deep learning.
