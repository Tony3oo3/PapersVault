## Problem

CLIP's strong open-vocabulary generalization comes from image-text pretraining, but it lacks temporal modeling for video. Fine-tuning CLIP on action datasets improves closed-set accuracy yet degrades performance on unseen categories, especially those semantically distant from the training set (e.g., K-600 vs. K-400). The paper addresses how to make a CLIP-based video model simultaneously video-specific and generalizable without sacrificing either property.

## Input / Output

The model takes a video clip (8 frames sampled uniformly, 3 clips at test time with 1 crop each, ensembled by averaging) and text prompts formed by embedding action category names in a template ("A video of []"). Text inputs are augmented at training time with GPT-3.5-generated descriptions of each action to alleviate text scarcity. Output is a class label determined by cosine similarity between the frame-averaged [CLS] visual feature and the text encoder's output for each candidate action.

## Method

FROSTER fine-tunes a CLIP-based video model (e.g., VCLIP, ActionCLIP, or adapter variants) with a standard cross-entropy classification loss for video-specific learning, while simultaneously distilling features from a frozen CLIP teacher to preserve generalizability. The key mechanism is a Residual Feature Distillation (RFD) module: a two-layer MLP with GELU activation is applied to the student features in a residual form, ẑ_g = z_g + α·W₂(σ(W₁(z_g))), where α=0.1 and W₂ is zero-initialized. The identity path lets the frozen CLIP features directly supervise the student for generalizability, while the small projected term permits the student to deviate and fit video-specific patterns. The total loss is L = L_CE + β·L_FD (β=2), where L_FD is an L2 distance between frozen CLIP features and the RFD-transformed student features, applied to both vision and text encoders. This design is architecture-agnostic and compatible with both fully-tuned and adapter-based CLIP video models.

## Results

Under the cross-dataset setting (trained on K-400, evaluated on unseen datasets), FROSTER achieves 85.0% top-1 accuracy on UCF-101, 54.5% on HMDB-51, and 74.8% on K-600 (220 novel categories). Under the base-to-novel setting, FROSTER attains harmonic means of 70.4% (K-400), 65.1% (HMDB-51), 87.0% (UCF-101), and 14.6% (SSv2), setting new state-of-the-art on all four datasets. The largest gains over the closest competitor (Open-VCLIP) appear on K-600 (+1.8%), the dataset most demanding of generalizability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FROSTER outperforms all 12 compared baselines on every dataset under both evaluation settings. The closest competitor is Open-VCLIP (weight interpolation), which FROSTER surpasses by 1.5% on UCF-101 (85.0 vs 83.5), 1.3% on HMDB-51 (54.5 vs 53.2), and 1.8% on K-600 (74.8 vs 73.0). Among full fine-tuning methods, ViFi-CLIP is the strongest (71.2% on K-600) but still trails FROSTER by 3.6 points; ActionCLIP lags further behind at 62.5% on K-600. Adapter-based methods (AIM at 66.7%, ST-Adapter at 60.2%, X-CLIP at 65.2%, AdapterFormer at 67.0%) and prompting methods (VPT at 55.8%, Vita-CLIP at 67.4%) all underperform FROSTER by 3–19 points on K-600. Notably, frozen CLIP (68.1% on K-600) outperforms most adapted models on this generalization-demanding benchmark, but FROSTER recovers and exceeds it by 6.7 points while also dominating on UCF-101 (85.0 vs 74.2) and HMDB-51 (54.5 vs 46.3), demonstrating that FROSTER successfully combines video-specific adaptation with CLIP's generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

FROSTER sits at the intersection of CLIP-based video adaptation and knowledge distillation. It builds directly on CLIP's pretrained encoders (Radford et al., 2021) as a frozen teacher and on feature-based distillation (Romero et al., 2014) for the L2 consistency loss, but departs from prior distillation work by having two *competing* objectives (video-specific fitting vs. generalizable preservation) rather than transferring a single knowledge source. The residual connection in the distillation loss is inspired by ResNet's identity mapping (He et al., 2016), and the approach is positioned as an architecture-agnostic alternative to Open-VCLIP's weight interpolation (Weng et al., 2023), which requires identical weight dimensions between teacher and student.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and quantifies a concrete failure mode of CLIP fine-tuning for video: generalization degrades precisely on semantically distant test sets, which is the regime where open-vocabulary recognition is most needed. The residual feature distillation is a lightweight, plug-and-play technique compatible with both fully-finetuned and adapter-based CLIP variants, making it broadly applicable. Limitations include the reliance on simple average pooling for temporal aggregation (no explicit temporal modeling), modest gains on fine-grained datasets like SSv2, and evaluation restricted to ViT-B/16. Open questions include whether the approach extends to longer videos, other backbone architectures, or multi-modal video understanding tasks.
