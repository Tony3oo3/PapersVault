## Problem

Training continuous normalizing flows on Riemannian manifolds has been limited by methods that require expensive simulation during training even on simple geometries like hyperspheres, rely on biased approximations of score functions or divergences, or fail to scale to high dimensions. No prior approach could handle general geometries with non-trivial curvature and boundaries while remaining simulation-free where possible and avoiding stochastic estimation. This paper addresses the gap by providing a unified framework that works across all manifold types without these limitations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a noise sample x0 from a base distribution p, a training example x1 from the data distribution q, and the manifold geometry—either closed-form exponential/logarithm maps for simple manifolds (sphere, torus, hyperbolic space, SPD matrices) or a fixed set of k Laplace-Beltrami eigenfunctions for general meshes. For simple geometries, the intermediate point x_t is computed in closed form via x_t = exp_{x1}(κ(t) log_{x1}(x0)) with no simulation; for general geometries, x_t requires forward ODE integration of the conditional vector field (300 Euler steps with projection) but no backpropagation through the solver. The output is a parametric vector field v_t that generates samples on the manifold, with log-likelihood computed via the instantaneous change of variables formula.

## Method

Riemannian Flow Matching (RFM) extends Conditional Flow Matching to Riemannian manifolds by introducing a 'premetric' d(x,y)—a non-negative, positive, non-degenerate function measuring point closeness—that defines the target vector field in closed form: u_t(x|x1) = (d log κ(t)/dt) · d(x,x1) · ∇d(x,x1)/||∇d(x,x1)||²_g. The key theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) shows this vector field is the minimal-norm solution that monotonically decreases the premetric, guaranteeing all mass concentrates at x1 at t=1. For simple geometries, choosing the geodesic distance as premetric recovers constant-speed geodesic paths and yields a fully simulation-free training objective identical in form to Euclidean Flow Matching. For general geometries (triangular meshes with curvature and boundaries), spectral distances (biharmonic or diffusion) computed from a one-time eigenfunction decomposition serve as the premetric, requiring only forward ODE simulation during training. The training loss is a simple L2 regression ||v_t(x_t) − ẋ_t||²_g with no divergence computation, no score approximation, and no stochastic estimation, making it scalable to high dimensions and general manifolds alike.

## Results

On the 2D sphere (earth/climate datasets), Riemannian Flow Matching with geodesic distance achieves the best NLL on Volcano (-7.93±1.67) and Fire (-1.86±0.11), outperforming the strongest baselines by 1.3 and 0.46 NLL respectively. On the 7D torus (RNA protein dataset), RFM achieves -5.20±0.067 NLL, substantially outperforming Riemannian Diffusion Models (-3.70±0.592) by 1.5 NLL. On general triangular mesh manifolds (Stanford Bunny, Spot the Cow), RFM with biharmonic spectral distance achieves competitive NLL (e.g., 1.49±0.01 on Bunny with k=100 eigenfunctions), demonstrating the first tractable training of continuous-time generative models on non-trivial geometries with boundaries.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RFM outperforms all five baselines on the Volcano dataset (-7.93 vs. best -6.61 for Riemannian Diffusion) and Fire dataset (-1.86 vs. best -1.40 for CNF Matching), where highly concentrated regions demand high fidelity. On the 7D torus RNA dataset, RFM achieves -5.20 NLL versus -3.70 for Riemannian Diffusion Models, a 1.5 NLL gap that highlights the advantage of simulation-free training at higher dimensions. RFM is slightly outperformed by CNF Matching on Earthquake (-0.28 vs. -0.38) and by Riemannian Diffusion on Proline 2D (0.15 vs. 0.12), though these gaps are within or near standard deviation. Against Riemannian Score-Based methods (De Bortoli et al., 2022), RFM consistently matches or exceeds performance across all sphere and torus datasets while being simulation-free, whereas the score-based approach requires iterative SDE simulation and biased heat-kernel score approximations. The key structural advantage is that RFM's closed-form target vector field eliminates the need for divergence estimation (required by Moser Flow and ISM) and avoids the biased conditional score approximations inherent to denoising score matching on manifolds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Flow Matching / Conditional Flow Matching framework (Lipman et al., 2023; Albergo & Vanden-Eijnden, 2023) from Euclidean space to general Riemannian manifolds, building on the Continuous Normalizing Flow architecture (Chen et al., 2018). The key conceptual departure from prior Riemannian generative modeling work (Riemannian CNFs, Riemannian diffusion models) is the introduction of a 'premetric' — a function satisfying only non-negativity, positivity, and non-degeneracy — that unifies geodesic distances (for simple manifolds) and spectral distances (for general manifolds) as a single mechanism for constructing conditional vector fields. This bypasses the simulation, divergence estimation, and biased score approximations that plague existing Riemannian diffusion approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration of tractable continuous-time deep generative modeling on general manifolds with non-trivial curvature and boundaries (triangular meshes, mazes), a setting where all prior methods either fail to scale or require expensive simulation. The premetric framework is notably flexible: it does not require a full metric, and spectral distances with as few as k=3 eigenfunctions (roughly the manifold dimension) already satisfy all required properties, in stark contrast to Riemannian diffusion models where even hundreds of eigenfunctions yield biased score approximations. Open questions include whether the ODE simulation required on general manifolds can be made parallel or simulation-free, and whether neural eigenfunction approximations can replace the one-time Laplace-Beltrami eigenvalue solve for very complex geometries.
