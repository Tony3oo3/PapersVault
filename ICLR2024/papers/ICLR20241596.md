## Problem

Existing SE(3) manipulation policies (PerAct, RVT, C2FARM) require 100+ expert demonstrations to solve basic pick-place tasks, largely because SO(3) is non-Euclidean and standard convolutions or coarse discretization cannot capture the continuous rotation structure. Moreover, the dual SE(d)×SE(d) bi-equivariant symmetry inherent in pick-place problems—where independent rotations of the picked object and the placement target transform the action as a' = g₂ a g₁⁻¹—has not been exploited in a general 3D policy learning setting.

## Input / Output

In 3D, the observation is a 72×96×56 voxel grid (each voxel ≈ 0.94 cm³) reconstructed from four RGB-D cameras; in 2D, it is a 320×160 top-down image. The network outputs a full probability distribution over SE(d) for both pick and place, parameterized as Fourier coefficients of Wigner D-matrices (3D) or complex exponentials (2D) at every voxel/pixel. A coarse-to-fine decoding strategy first samples 384 rotations globally, then refines to 26,244 rotations at the argmax location to balance angular resolution against cubic memory growth in voxel grids.

## Method

FOURTRAN is an open-loop behavior cloning architecture with two equivariant U-Net branches: an SE(d)-equivariant pick network f_pick that outputs SO(d) Fourier coefficients per voxel, and an SE(d)×SE(d)-equivariant place network f_place that performs template matching via cross-correlation. The key mechanism is a fiber-space Fourier transform: the crop c is encoded by ψ, lifted by a finite rotation set (384 SO(3) rotations in 3D), and then Fourier-transformed along the channel axis to produce a dynamic steerable kernel κ(c) whose output type is ⊕(2ℓ+1)D^ℓ. Cross-correlation between κ(c) and the observation features φ(o_t) is executed entirely in Fourier space, avoiding the combinatorial explosion of independently correlating each rotated crop. Theoretical propositions prove that this construction exactly satisfies the bi-equivariance constraint, and the use of O(24) regular representations in hidden layers keeps the parameter count (~4.7M) comparable to C2FARM while achieving 6–200% higher success rates with only 5–10 demonstrations.

## Results

On the RLbench 3D pick-place benchmark, FOURTRAN trained with only 5 demonstrations achieves 76% (stack-blocks), 88% (stack-cups), 100% (stack-wine), 10.6% (place-cups), and 32% (put-plate) success rate, outperforming all single-task baselines trained with 10 demonstrations by large margins. With 10 demonstrations, FOURTRAN reaches 80%, 92%, 100%, 26.6%, and 66.6% respectively, approaching the discrete expert upper bound of 100%, 92%, 100%, 90.6%, and 65.3%. On the 2D Ravens Benchmark, FOURTRAN achieves 100% on block-insertion and 99.8% on sweeping-piles, maintaining superior performance under the tighter 7.5° rotation threshold compared to baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FOURTRAN dominates all three 3D baselines (RVT, PerAct, C2FARM-BC) across every task with only 5–10 demonstrations. The most dramatic gap is on stack-cups, where FOURTRAN achieves 88% with 5 demos while C2FARM-BC and PerAct score 0% and RVT scores 13.3% with 10 demos — a margin exceeding 200%. Even against multi-task baselines trained on 100 demonstrations per task, FOURTRAN with just 5 demos outperforms RVT-Multi on stack-blocks (76 vs 28.8), stack-cups (88 vs 26.4), and place-cups (10.6 vs 4.0), and matches it on stack-wine (100 vs 91.0). On the 2D Ravens Benchmark, FOURTRAN slightly outperforms Equivariant Transporter (its closest 2D competitor) on assembling-kits at the 7.5° threshold (78.0 vs 76.0) and matches it on block-insertion (100 vs 98.0), while both substantially outperform the non-equivariant Transporter Net (64.0 on assembling-kits at 7.5°). The key takeaway is that FOURTRAN's bi-equivariant Fourier-space architecture converts a 10× reduction in demonstrations into a 5–100× improvement in success rate over prior SE(3) methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

FOURTRAN extends the Transporter Networks framework (Zeng et al., 2021) and its 2D equivariant variant (Huang et al., 2022) to the full SE(3) action space by introducing a fiber-space Fourier transform over Wigner D-matrices to parameterize SO(3) rotation distributions. This departs from prior SO(3)-equivariant manipulation work (NDF, TAX-Pose, Equivariant Descriptor Fields), which relied on point descriptors or energy-based models requiring optimization at inference, by encoding bi-equivariance directly inside a general-purpose convolutional policy network. The equivariant convolution layers follow the e(n)-equivariant steerable CNN framework of Cesa et al. (2021), but the key architectural innovation is performing cross-correlation in Fourier space to avoid the combinatorial explosion of 3D rotation sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic sample-efficiency gap (5 demonstrations outperforming 100-demonstration baselines by up to 200% on high-precision tasks) suggests that properly encoding the geometric symmetry of pick-place is more important than data volume for 3D manipulation. The fiber-space Fourier representation offers a general recipe for handling high-dimensional rotation spaces in equivariant networks beyond robotics, with the authors noting applicability to protein-ligand docking and point cloud registration. Key limitations include the open-loop formulation (no collision awareness or path planning), single-task restriction, and the ablation revealing that the equivariance constraint on the crop encoder ψ contributes little, raising questions about which symmetry constraints are truly load-bearing. Extending to multi-task, language-conditioned, closed-loop settings remains an important open direction.
