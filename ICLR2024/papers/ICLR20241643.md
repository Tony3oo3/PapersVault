## Problem

The Language of Thought Hypothesis posits that cognition operates on structured, compositional, discrete mental representations, yet it remains unclear how to learn such LoT-like structure from non-linguistic observations like images. Existing methods each satisfy only a subset of the required properties: patch-based VQ-VAE provides discrete abstraction but lacks semantic scene structure, while object-centric models like Slot Attention and SysBinder offer compositional representations but lack discrete concept tokens and probabilistic compositional generation. NLoTM is the first model to simultaneously achieve compositional semantic decomposition, symbol-like discrete abstraction, and probabilistic compositional generation.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB scene image (128×128 for CLEVR variants, 64×64 for 2D Sprites) and produces a hierarchical discrete latent: N slots (one per object) each split into M blocks (one per factor such as color, shape, position), where each block is a discrete code index from a factor-specific codebook shared across all objects. The autoregressive prior generates these NM codes sequentially (one block at a time, independent of image resolution), and the transformer decoder reconstructs or generates a full image from the code sequence. Downstream, the codebook prototype vectors or indices serve as fixed-dimensional representations for supervised tasks like odd-one-out identification and property comparison.

## Method

NLoTM comprises two components. The Semantic Vector-Quantized (SVQ) Variational Autoencoder uses Slot Attention as its encoder, then splits each d_s-dimensional slot into M equally-sized blocks and applies block-level GRUs and residual MLPs with parameters shared across blocks of the same factor index (inspired by SysBinder's binding mechanism). Each block is then vector-quantized against its own codebook (K prototype vectors of dimension d_c = d_s/M), with codebooks shared across all slots so that each block specializes in a particular factor (e.g., color, shape, position). The Autoregressive LoT Prior (ALP) is a transformer decoder trained to predict the next block's discrete code given all preceding codes, with positional encodings distinguishing slot and block positions; sampling from this prior generates new scenes one object at a time based on their properties, analogous to a language model generating text tokens. Training uses reconstruction loss plus VQ commitment loss with EMA codebook updates and random restarts to prevent collapse.

## Results

On the CLEVR datasets, NLoTM achieves the lowest FID across all three settings: 32.50 on CLEVR-Easy, 43.12 on CLEVR-Hard, and 84.52 on CLEVR-Tex, outperforming dVAE (40.30/65.89/112.80) and VQ-VAE (57.06/73.33/178.59). On the 2D Sprites generation task, NLoTM achieves 75.00% generation accuracy on the 3-object setting and 42.19% on the 4-object-with-background setting, compared to 19.53% for VQ-VAE. On the downstream odd-one-out OOD task, NLoTM Codebook reaches 99.1% accuracy versus 67.6% for SysBinder and 26.7% for dVAE Discrete. On CLEVR-Hard property comparison, NLoTM Codebook achieves 71.15% OOD accuracy, slightly exceeding SysBinder's 70.09%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NLoTM consistently outperforms all patch-based discrete baselines (VQ-VAE and dVAE) on FID across every dataset, with the largest margins on CLEVR-Tex (84.52 vs. 112.80 for dVAE and 178.59 for VQ-VAE). Against the continuous object-centric baseline GENESIS-v2, NLoTM achieves substantially lower FID (e.g., 43.12 vs. 93.01 on CLEVR-Hard), and GENESIS-v2 completely fails on CLEVR-Tex. Compared to SysBinder (continuous factor-level representations), NLoTM Codebook achieves 99.1% OOD accuracy on the odd-one-out task versus SysBinder's 67.6%, demonstrating that the discrete codebook vectors provide a more compact and generalizable representation for property comparison. On the CLEVR-Hard property comparison task, NLoTM Codebook (71.15% OOD) slightly edges out SysBinder (70.09%), while both far exceed the patch-based methods (~20%). On FG-ARI segmentation, NLoTM matches SysBinder on CLEVR-Easy (91.37 vs. 92.58) and CLEVR-Hard (90.48 vs. 90.43) but underperforms on CLEVR-Tex (70.93 vs. 78.12), while still exceeding vanilla Slot Attention on all three datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

NLoTM synthesizes three previously separate lines of work: object-centric scene decomposition via Slot Attention, factor-level disentanglement within slots as in SysBinder, and discrete vector-quantized representation learning as in VQ-VAE/dVAE. The key architectural departure is performing vector quantization at the factor/block level within each slot (rather than at the patch level as in VQ-VAE or at the slot level, which would be combinatorially intractable), and then training an autoregressive transformer prior over these semantic concept tokens analogous to a language model over text. The paper positions itself as the first model simultaneously satisfying three desiderata—compositional semantic decomposition, discrete symbol-like abstraction, and probabilistic compositional generation—that no prior model (VQ-VAE, Slot Attention, SysBinder) achieves together.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work provides a concrete neural instantiation of the Language of Thought Hypothesis, showing that discrete, factor-aligned representations can support both high-fidelity generation and strong out-of-distribution generalization (99.1% OOD accuracy on odd-one-out vs. 67.6% for SysBinder's continuous latents). The finding that fixed codebook prototypes outperform continuous representations for OOD tasks—because they prevent spurious correlations in property comparison—is a useful conceptual insight for representation learning. However, the method is validated only on synthetic datasets (2D Sprites, CLEVR variants), and the authors acknowledge that all latents being discrete is a limitation for continuous factors like position and pose. Scaling to realistic scenes and combining continuous and discrete factors remain important open questions.
