## Problem

Semantic part segmentation at arbitrary granularity typically demands large annotated datasets or category-specific generative models (e.g., GANs trained per class). The paper asks whether a single annotated image with its segmentation mask is sufficient to segment unseen images at the same level of detail, without training a class-specific model or curating a large dataset.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241590/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During optimization, the method takes one RGB image (H×W×3) and its pixel-wise segmentation mask with K classes, and optimizes the first K text embeddings of Stable Diffusion (up to 77 classes, matching SD's token limit). At inference, it takes an unseen RGB image and produces a segmentation mask by argmax over K WAS-attention maps, each resized to the input dimensions. Attention maps are computed at 64×64 resolution (H''×W'') and upscaled via bilinear interpolation; for 512×512 CelebAMask-HQ images, the input is split into four 400-pixel patches whose maps are aggregated.

## Method

SLiMe frames part segmentation as a one-shot optimization of Stable Diffusion's text embeddings, guided by two attention-based losses and an SD denoising regularization. The key architectural contribution is the Weighted Accumulated Self-attention (WAS) map: cross-attention maps from UNet layers 8–12 are averaged and resized, then used as per-channel weights to compute a weighted sum of self-attention channels (from the last three UNet layers), yielding a higher-resolution map with sharper semantic boundaries. The optimization objective combines cross-entropy between the cross-attention map and the ground-truth mask, MSE between the WAS-attention map and the ground-truth mask, and the standard LDM noise-prediction loss (weighted by β=0.005) to keep embeddings within SD's learned distribution. Text embeddings are optimized with Adam (lr=0.1) for 200 epochs, with t_opt sampled uniformly from [5,100] each step. At inference, the frozen optimized embeddings are paired with the encoded unseen image to extract attention maps and produce the segmentation mask, requiring no further optimization.

## Results

On PASCAL-Part horse (10-sample), SLiMe achieves 63.3% mIoU, outperforming ReGAN (54.3%) and SegDDPM (58.7%). In the 1-sample setting, SLiMe reaches 56.4% mIoU on horse, surpassing SegGPT (40.3%) by ~16% and SegDDPM (43.0%) by ~13%. On CelebAMask-HQ face segmentation (1-sample), SLiMe attains 69.6% mIoU versus SegGPT's 57.1% and SegDDPM's 60.1%. On PASCAL-Part car (10-sample), SLiMe scores 68.3% mIoU compared to ReGAN's 52.15%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLiMe consistently outperforms ReGAN across all tested categories: by ~9% mIoU on horse (63.3 vs 54.3, 10-sample) and ~16% on car (68.3 vs 52.15, 10-sample), despite ReGAN leveraging GAN-generated pseudo-labels alongside 10 annotated samples. Against SegDDPM, SLiMe wins by ~4.6% on horse 10-sample (63.3 vs 58.7) and by a larger margin in the 1-sample regime (~13% on horse, ~9.5% on CelebAMask-HQ). In the 1-sample setting, SLiMe surpasses SegGPT by ~16% on horse (56.4 vs 40.3), ~12% on car (65.6 vs 53.3), and ~12.5% on CelebAMask-HQ (69.6 vs 57.1), even though SegGPT is a fully supervised method trained on a large segmentation dataset. The one notable exception is CelebAMask-HQ 10-sample, where SegDDPM (78.0%) edges out SLiMe (75.7%) on average, though SLiMe still wins on most individual parts. Importantly, SLiMe requires no category-specific pre-trained generative model or large annotated dataset, unlike ReGAN and SegDDPM which need class-specific GAN/DDPM pre-training, and unlike SegGPT which demands extensive supervised training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

SLiMe repurposes Stable Diffusion's cross-attention and self-attention mechanisms—previously leveraged for image editing (Hertz et al., 2022), correspondence (Hedlin et al., 2023), and shape variation (Patashnik et al., 2023)—for semantic part segmentation by optimizing text embeddings in a one-shot fashion. It departs from prior few-shot part segmentation methods (ReGAN, SegDDPM) that require training a category-specific generative model, and from SegGPT which demands a large supervised segmentation dataset, by instead exploiting the general-purpose semantic knowledge already embedded in SD's pre-trained weights. The novel WAS-attention map, which fuses higher-resolution self-attention with cross-attention to recover boundary detail lost in low-resolution cross-attention maps, is the key architectural contribution that distinguishes SLiMe from naive attention-based segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SLiMe offers a practical path to customizable, granularity-controllable segmentation without curating category-specific datasets or training generative models, which is valuable for interactive applications like visual effects where users define segmentation targets ad hoc. However, the method is limited to 2D images, struggles with tiny target regions due to the resolution gap between attention maps and input images, and is capped at 77 classes by SD's token limit. Extending the approach to 3D and video segmentation, and resolving the small-object failure mode, remain open directions that would broaden its applicability.
