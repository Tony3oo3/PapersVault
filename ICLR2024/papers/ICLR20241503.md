## Problem

Standard language models generate text by predicting individual tokens from a fixed vocabulary, limiting interpretability and factuality. Existing retrieval-augmented approaches like CoG still rely on a two-stage pipeline (document retrieval followed by phrase extraction), making performance bottlenecked by the quality of the first-stage retriever. This paper asks whether text generation can be performed entirely through direct phrase retrieval from a large supporting corpus, eliminating the document retrieval stage altogether.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prefix (token sequence) and retrieves the next phrase from a pre-built phrase table of ~137M context-aware phrases extracted from Wikipedia documents (each phrase is a syntactic constituent of 2–10 words, encoded with its surrounding document context). For open-ended generation, it outputs a 128-token continuation assembled from retrieved phrases and standalone tokens; for QA tasks, it computes likelihoods over candidate answers by summing over all valid phrase-retrieval paths via dynamic programming. The phrase table is plug-and-play: it can be enlarged, swapped for a domain-specific index (e.g., 3M medical phrases), or pruned without any retraining.

## Method

CoG-2 reformulates text generation as sequential next-phrase retrieval using a balanced dual-encoder architecture: a causal Transformer encodes the prefix, and a bidirectional Transformer (DensePhrases) encodes each phrase with its document context, with matching via dot product and efficient MIPS (FAISS) at inference. The central technical challenge is constructing training oracles (prefix → phrase → source-document triplets), addressed by a two-fold strategy: (1) linguistics-motivated heuristics that extract syntactic constituents via Stanford Parser, filter by length and IDF, and select the best-matching source copy using BM25 plus semantic similarity; and (2) iterative self-reinforcement where the model periodically retrieves its own top-k candidates and updates targets to the highest-scoring valid phrase, gradually aligning oracles with learned preferences. Training uses an InfoNCE contrastive loss with in-batch and hard negatives (from the self-reinforcement pool), combined with a standard next-token prediction loss to preserve token-level generation capability. Only the prefix encoder is fine-tuned (initialized from GPT-2), while phrase embeddings remain frozen for efficiency.

## Results

On knowledge-intensive tasks, CoG-2 achieves the best accuracy across all five datasets, with headline improvements of +12.8% on OpenbookQA (23.47% → 36.27%) and +4.5% on TruthfulQA (29.73% → 34.27%) over the base GPT-2 LM. On open-ended text generation (MiniPile), CoG-2 attains a MAUVE score of 81.58, nearly doubling the base LM's 42.61, while maintaining the lowest latency (1.29×) among all retrieval-augmented baselines. The model also benefits from a plug-and-play phrase index: swapping in a 3× enlarged index boosts TruthfulQA accuracy to 39.59%, and a domain-specific medical index (3M phrases) improves Med-USMILE to 26.38% without any retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoG-2 outperforms all four baselines on every knowledge-intensive dataset. Against the base GPT-2 LM, the largest gain is on OpenbookQA (+12.8 pp), while gains on ARC-Challenge (+4.35 pp) and MedMCQA (+1.11 pp) are more modest. Compared to kNN-LM, CoG-2 leads by 4.0 pp on TruthfulQA and 13.3 pp on OpenbookQA; kNN-LM actually underperforms the base LM on OpenbookQA (22.93 vs. 23.47). RETRO is the weakest retrieval-augmented baseline, trailing CoG-2 by 6.7 pp on TruthfulQA and 10.6 pp on MedMCQA. The closest competitor is CoG (Lan et al., 2023), which CoG-2 edges out by 0.16 pp on TruthfulQA, 0.80 pp on OpenbookQA, 1.03 pp on ARC-Challenge, 0.37 pp on MedMCQA, and 0.62 pp on Med-USMILE. On open-ended generation (Table 4), CoG-2 achieves the highest MAUVE (81.58 vs. 52.27 for CoG, 62.39 for RETRO, 42.61 for base LM) and the lowest latency among retrieval-augmented methods (1.29× vs. 4.40× for CoG and 6.29× for kNN-LM), though CoG attains a slightly better coherence score (2.08 vs. 3.25) at the cost of severely reduced diversity (55.04% vs. 76.26%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends CoG (Lan et al., 2023), which introduced phrase retrieval from a two-stage pipeline of document retrieval followed by grounded phrase extraction. CoG-2 eliminates the document retrieval stage entirely, replacing it with a pre-built, context-aware phrase index searched via MIPS, thereby reframing generation as direct phrase retrieval rather than token prediction. The dual-encoder formulation is inherited from standard LMs but rebalanced: both the prefix encoder and the phrase encoder are multi-layer Transformers, in contrast to the heavily unbalanced design of standard LMs where the target side is merely an embedding lookup. The training oracle construction departs from CoG's forward maximum matching by combining linguistics-motivated heuristics (syntactic constituency, IDF filtering, semantic similarity) with iterative self-reinforcement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a retrieval-based generation paradigm can simultaneously improve quality (MAUVE 42.61→81.58, OpenbookQA 23.47→36.27) and reduce inference latency (1.29× vs. 4.40× for CoG) compared to both standard LMs and other retrieval-augmented baselines, suggesting a practical path toward more interpretable and efficient language models. The plug-and-play property of the phrase index—swapping to an enlarged or domain-specific index without retraining—opens a route to domain adaptation that is orthogonal to fine-tuning. Key open questions include scalability beyond the Wikipedia-scale corpus, integration with alignment/RLHF techniques, and whether the paradigm generalizes to multilingual or multimodal settings. The authors explicitly frame the current results as a proof-of-concept, leaving substantial room for future work.
