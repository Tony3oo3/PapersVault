## Problem

Existing adversarial robustness benchmarks for LLMs (AdvGLUE, AdvGLUE++) rely on transferable adversarial samples generated against BERT-based models or smaller open-source LLMs, which neither genuinely measures a black-box victim LLM's robustness nor scales efficiently. Constructing these datasets also demands substantial GPU resources (up to 105 GB), making them impractical for rapid auditing of proprietary models accessed only via API.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240121/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single data point from a GLUE task (original text sample plus its ground-truth label) along with a task description, and produces an adversarial text variant that causes the victim LLM to output an incorrect label. The attack prompt is assembled from three templated components (original input, attack objective, attack guidance) and the generated adversarial sample passes through a fidelity filter enforcing a word-modification-ratio threshold (τ₁) and a BERTScore threshold (τ₂); if fidelity is insufficient, the original sample is returned unmodified. The ensemble variant generates nine adversarial candidates (one per perturbation instruction) and selects the one that both fools the LLM and maximizes BERTScore.

## Method

PromptAttack converts adversarial textual attacks into a natural-language prompt that instructs the victim LLM to generate its own failure mode. The attack prompt comprises: (1) an original input stating the sample and its true label, (2) an attack objective requiring the LLM to produce a semantically equivalent sentence that the LLM itself will misclassify, and (3) an attack guidance specifying one of nine perturbation instructions spanning character-level (typos, letter changes, extraneous characters), word-level (synonym replacement, deletion, neutral-word insertion), and sentence-level (meaningless handles, paraphrasing, syntactic restructuring) modifications. A fidelity filter based on word modification ratio and BERTScore discards low-quality adversarial samples whose semantics have drifted. Two enhancement strategies boost attack power: a few-shot strategy that embeds five example perturbations in the guidance to improve the LLM's comprehension of the task, and an ensemble strategy that searches across all nine perturbation levels and selects the highest-fidelity sample that successfully fools the LLM. The entire pipeline requires only a few black-box API queries without access to model parameters or gradient computation.

## Results

PromptAttack-FS-EN achieves an average ASR of 48.34% against GPT-3.5 across all six GLUE tasks, compared to 25.51% for AdvGLUE and 6.64% for AdvGLUE++. On the SST-2 task specifically, PromptAttack-FS-EN raises the ASR against GPT-3.5 from 33.04% (AdvGLUE) to 75.23%, a 42.18 percentage-point improvement. Against Llama2-13B, PromptAttack-EN reaches 63.20% average ASR versus 40.76% (AdvGLUE) and 10.36% (AdvGLUE++). The attack requires only ~2 seconds per data point via black-box API queries, compared to 50 s (AdvGLUE) and 330 s (AdvGLUE++) with 16–105 GB GPU memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PromptAttack consistently and substantially outperforms both AdvGLUE and AdvGLUE++ across all six GLUE tasks and all three victim LLMs. Against GPT-3.5, PromptAttack-FS-EN achieves 48.34% average ASR, more than doubling AdvGLUE (25.51%) and exceeding AdvGLUE++ (6.64%) by over 7×. Against Llama2-13B, PromptAttack-EN reaches 63.20% average ASR versus 40.76% (AdvGLUE) and 10.36% (AdvGLUE++). The gap is most dramatic on GPT-3.5, where AdvGLUE++ is nearly ineffective (e.g., 5.24% on SST-2) while PromptAttack-FS-EN reaches 75.23%. An interesting nuance is that the few-shot strategy (FS) helps GPT-3.5 but slightly hurts Llama2, likely because smaller models misinterpret the few-shot examples and generate low-quality perturbations that are filtered out. Overall, PromptAttack wins on every task–model combination, confirming that adaptive, prompt-based attacks are far more effective than transferable adversarial samples generated against different model families.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the perturbation strategies from classical adversarial textual attacks (TextBugger, TextFooler, BERT-ATTACK, CheckList, SCPN) by converting them from algorithmic procedures into natural-language instructions embedded in a prompt, asking the victim LLM to generate its own adversarial sample. It departs from AdvGLUE and AdvGLUE++, which rely on transferable adversarial samples generated against BERT-based models or open-source LLMs, by making the attack adaptive to the specific victim LLM through black-box prompting. The few-shot and ensemble enhancement strategies draw directly from few-shot inference (Logan IV et al., 2021) and ensemble attacks (Croce & Hein, 2020), respectively, but apply them in the novel context of self-fooling via prompt construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PromptAttack provides a practical, computationally efficient (2 seconds per data point vs. 50–330 seconds) black-box tool for auditing LLM adversarial robustness without requiring model access, and the released AdvGLUE-GPT dataset offers a new benchmark for the field. However, the evaluation is limited to GLUE tasks and two model families (Llama2, GPT-3.5), and the few-shot strategy paradoxically degrades attack quality on smaller models, suggesting the approach's effectiveness is tightly coupled to the LLM's instruction-following capability. Open questions include whether PromptAttack-generated samples can serve as adversarial training data for robustifying LLMs and whether the findings generalize beyond classification tasks to generation or reasoning.
