## Problem

Existing deep learning models for mathematics specialize in either symbolic or numeric domains and are trained in a supervised, task-specific manner, neglecting the mutual benefits of a task-agnostic multi-modal understanding between symbolic equations and their numeric counterparts. No pre-training framework existed that could jointly learn representations bridging these two modalities, limiting cross-modal transfer and data efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241657/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The symbolic encoder consumes mathematical expressions tokenized in prefix-order tree traversal (with BOS/EOS delimiters and base-10 floating-point encoding for constants), while the numeric encoder consumes (x, y) data points tokenized as sign-mantissa-exponent triplets rounded to four significant digits, with input dimensionality constrained to D ≤ 10. Pre-training uses ~60M synthetic paired examples; downstream outputs include a shared latent embedding (for contrastive alignment or property prediction via an MLP head) and generated symbolic expressions (via a Transformer decoder for symbolic regression).

## Method

SNIP employs dual Transformer encoders—one for symbolic expressions (with positional embeddings) and one for numeric data points (without positional embeddings, exploiting permutation invariance)—each followed by attention-based pooling to produce fixed-size latent vectors. The encoders are jointly pre-trained with a symmetric InfoNCE contrastive loss (CLIP-style) that aligns matched symbolic-numeric pairs while distancing mismatched ones within a batch. For symbolic regression, a learnable mapping network (inspired by ClipCap) bridges SNIP's numeric embeddings to E2E's pre-trained expression decoder, and a Latent Space Optimization (LSO) procedure performs stochastic gradient-free search over the interpolatable latent space, with BFGS refining equation constants, transforming combinatorial equation search into continuous optimization. The key insight is that the task-agnostic contrastive pre-training imprints the latent space with integrated symbolic-numeric semantics, making it both interpolatable (linear interpolation between function embeddings yields semantically intermediate functions) and rich enough to support zero-shot property prediction and competitive SR performance, especially in low-data regimes.

## Results

On the cross-modal property prediction task (10K training equations), SNIP (frozen) reduces NMSE from 0.5299 (supervised) to 0.0731 for Non-Convexity Ratio and from 0.5356 to 0.0540 for Upwardness, with Acc₀.₁ rising from ~0.56 to ~0.85. Fine-tuning further improves to NMSE 0.0683 / 0.0400 and Acc₀.₁ 0.921 / 0.901. On SRBench, SNIP achieves top-tier R² of 0.928 on Strogatz datasets, lower equation complexity than Operon on both Black-box (47.52 vs. 64.95) and Feynman (31.63 vs. 69.87) datasets, and higher accuracy than AI Feynman on Feynman (0.882 vs. 0.798), placing on the first Pareto-front across all three dataset groups.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the supervised baseline (same dual-encoder architecture, random initialization) on property prediction, SNIP (frozen) reduces NMSE by roughly 7× (0.0731 vs. 0.5299 for NCR) and boosts Acc₀.₁ from 0.565 to 0.861, with the gap widening in low-data regimes (at 100 training samples, supervised R² drops to 0.292 while SNIP stays above 0.745). On SRBench, SNIP outperforms the E2E transformer (Kamienny et al., 2022) by achieving a stronger accuracy–complexity tradeoff, placing on the first Pareto-front across all three dataset groups where E2E does not consistently dominate. Compared to the Operon GP baseline, SNIP achieves substantially lower equation complexity on both Black-box (47.52 vs. 64.95 nodes) and Feynman (31.63 vs. 69.87 nodes) while maintaining competitive or superior R². On Feynman datasets, SNIP also surpasses AI Feynman (Udrescu & Tegmark, 2020) in accuracy (R² 0.882 vs. 0.798) while simultaneously producing simpler expressions. Overall, SNIP wins or ties on the Pareto-front in every SRBench dataset group and dominates the supervised baseline on property prediction by a wide margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

SNIP transplants the CLIP-style dual-encoder contrastive pre-training paradigm (Radford et al., 2021) from the vision-language pair to the symbolic-numeric mathematical domain, a modality combination that had not been jointly pre-trained before. Its data generation, numeric tokenization, and symbolic prefix-order tokenization follow E2E (Kamienny et al., 2022) and Lample & Charton (2020) respectively, while the mapping network that bridges SNIP's numeric encoder to E2E's pre-trained SR decoder borrows the ClipCap (Mokady et al., 2021) strategy. The key conceptual departure from prior SR work (which is supervised and task-specific) is the task-agnostic InfoNCE objective that learns a shared embedding space without any downstream task labels, and the subsequent use of that interpolatable latent space for continuous optimization rather than discrete expression search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SNIP establishes that a single task-agnostic pre-training objective can produce representations simultaneously useful for both symbolic-to-numeric property prediction and numeric-to-symbolic equation discovery, with particularly strong gains in low-data regimes (R² > 0.745 vs. 0.292 at 100 samples). The interpolatable latent space offers a principled alternative to GP-style combinatorial search for SR, though the paper acknowledges limitations: input dimensionality is capped at D ≤ 10, the model assumes closed-form expressibility, and the operator vocabulary is fixed by the data generation protocol. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher-dimensional or non-closed-form phenomena, and whether the learned symbolic-numeric proximity metric can serve as a general-purpose evaluation or data-valuation tool.
