## Problem

While parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods like LoRA have made billion-scale model adaptation accessible, it remains unclear whether low-rank update strategies can be extended to pre-training large language models from scratch. The paper asks whether a sequence of individually low-rank updates can collectively produce a high-rank network update comparable to full-rank training, and whether this approach can reduce the prohibitive computational costs of training overparameterized models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241927/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes text tokens from the C4 dataset in a single-epoch, compute-optimal training regime (sized per Chinchilla scaling laws, from 1.2B to 23.1B tokens depending on model size). It produces full-rank transformer language model weights (60M to 1.3B parameters, LLaMA-style architecture with SwiGLU, RMSNorm, and rotary embeddings) where all low-rank LoRA updates have been merged back into the base weights. The trainable parameter count at any point is roughly 1/5 of the full model (e.g., ~250M trainable out of 1.3B total), with embeddings and normalization layers always updated full-rank.

## Method

ReLoRA exploits the subadditivity of matrix rank (rank(A+B) ≤ rank(A)+rank(B)) to accumulate a high-rank weight update from a sequence of low-rank LoRA updates. The training procedure has two phases: a full-rank warm start (~25% of total steps) followed by ReLoRA training where LoRA adapters (rank 128) are periodically merged into the base weights and reinitialized (every 5K steps). To prevent the Adam optimizer's accumulated gradient moments from steering newly initialized parameters back into the same subspace, ReLoRA performs a partial optimizer state reset via 99% magnitude pruning and a jagged learning rate schedule that drops LR to zero and warms it back up over 50–100 steps after each restart. This combination of restarts, optimizer resets, jagged scheduling, and warm start enables ReLoRA to explore different subspaces at each cycle, achieving perplexity within 0.44 of full-rank training at 1.3B scale while saving 5.5 GB of GPU RAM and 9–40% wall-clock time depending on hardware.

## Results

On C4 pre-training, ReLoRA achieves perplexity within 0.44 of full-rank training at 1.3B scale (17.27 vs. 16.83) while training only ~250M parameters out of 1.3B total. At 350M scale, ReLoRA reaches 19.32 ppl versus 18.66 for full training, and at 250M it achieves 22.48 vs. 22.39. ReLoRA also saves 5.5 GB of GPU RAM per device and yields a 9–40% wall-clock speedup depending on hardware (9% on 8×A100, 33% on 6×A6000 Ada, 51% on 2×RTX 3090).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA (Hu et al., 2022), ReLoRA wins decisively in pre-training: at 130M scale ReLoRA achieves 25.04 ppl versus LoRA's 34.17, and at 350M the gap widens to 19.32 vs. 57.11, confirming that periodic merge-and-reinit restarts are essential to escape the low-rank subspace. In the fine-tuning comparison (Appendix Table 8, T5-Large on GLUE), ReLoRA (avg 87.47) outperforms Ladder Side-Tuning (84.1), Prompt Tuning (72.2), Adapters (85.3), Compacter (85.8), and KronA (86.1), though it slightly trails LoRA (87.99) on the same model, indicating ReLoRA's advantage is primarily in pre-training rather than fine-tuning. The Control baseline (full-rank training with the same number of trainable parameters) is also consistently beaten by ReLoRA (e.g., 17.27 vs. 21.73 at 1.3B), showing that the low-rank restart mechanism itself—rather than simply reducing parameter count—drives the performance gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReLoRA extends LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) from a fine-tuning technique to a pre-training method by exploiting the subadditivity of matrix rank: multiple sequential low-rank updates, when merged into the base weights, can collectively approximate a high-rank update. The warm-start phase draws on the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis with rewinding (Frankle et al., 2019), while the periodic merge-and-reinit with partial optimizer resets and a jagged learning rate schedule are novel engineering contributions not present in prior low-rank training methods (Schotthofer et al., 2022; Sui et al., 2023; Kamalakara et al., 2022), which typically replace rather than augment the base weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ReLoRA demonstrates that parameter-efficient techniques, previously confined to fine-tuning, can be adapted for pre-training with only modest perplexity degradation (0.44 at 1.3B scale) while saving 5.5 GB of GPU RAM and 9–40% wall-clock time depending on hardware. The observation that efficiency gains grow with model size positions ReLoRA as increasingly relevant for multi-billion-parameter training on budget hardware. Key limitations include the mandatory full-rank warm start (~25% of total steps), the finding that ReLoRA does not outperform plain LoRA for fine-tuning, and the negative result showing that more frequent resets (Online ReLoRA) hurt rather than help performance. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 10B+ models, whether it can be applied to continued pre-training of existing checkpoints, and whether the warm-start requirement can be eliminated.
