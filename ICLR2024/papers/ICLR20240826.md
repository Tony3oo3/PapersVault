## Problem

Existing LLM alignment methods (RLHF, RLAIF, DPO, etc.) all require finetuning, human-annotated or prompt data, and substantial computational resources to maintain multiple models. This paper asks whether a frozen LLM can align its own outputs with human preferences entirely at inference time, without any parameter updates, training data, or gradient computation.

## Input / Output

RAIN takes a text prompt and a frozen LLM as input, with the alignment objective (e.g., harmlessness, truthfulness, positive sentiment) communicated solely through a self-evaluation prompt template. It produces a single aligned text response generated token-by-token through the outer loop, where each token set is committed immutably before proceeding. The method requires no external datasets, reward models, or reference models—only the LLM itself and a Sentence-BERT encoder for computing token-set embeddings used in similarity-based attribute updates.

## Method

RAIN performs a tree search over token sets (sequences of 1–10 tokens) during inference, alternating between an inner loop (search and evaluation) and an outer loop (committing the next token set). In the inner loop, the search direction is determined by a PUCT-style formula balancing exploitation (recorded value v) and exploration (visit count n weighted by model probability), and at each leaf the LLM self-evaluates the generated text via a binary-choice prompt to obtain a score. This score propagates back up the tree to update node values, while a similarity-based mechanism (using Sentence-BERT embeddings) also updates sibling nodes not on the search path, and dynamic node addition expands the tree when child embeddings are too similar. The outer loop then normalizes visit counts of the root's children into a probability distribution to select the next committed token set, making RAIN appear identical to vanilla auto-regressive inference from the outside. The entire process is learning-free: no gradients, no parameter updates, no external data—only the frozen LLM's own generation and self-evaluation capabilities drive the alignment.

## Results

On the HH dataset, RAIN improves the harmlessness rate of LLaMA 30B from 82.2% (vanilla auto-regressive) to 97.0%, while maintaining helpfulness. On TruthfulQA, RAIN boosts the truthfulness of the already-aligned LLaMA-2-chat 13B from 69.2% to 74.1% (+5%). Under GCG adversarial attacks on AdvBench, RAIN reduces white-box attack success rates by up to 75% (Vicuna 33B: 94%→19%). The method incurs approximately 4× time overhead relative to vanilla inference, with the ratio decreasing for larger, safer models (4.36× for LLaMA 30B, 3.78× for LLaMA-2 70B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAIN is compared against RLHF and RLAIF on LLaMA 30B (HH dataset, Figure 4). Both RLHF and RLAIF require additional data (human annotations or prompt datasets) and involve multi-stage training (SFT + RM + RL), yet RAIN achieves comparable or superior harmlessness rates without any training, data, or parameter updates. Against the natural inference-time baseline of reject sampling (cherry-pick sampling with 500 trials), RAIN substantially outperforms: on LLaMA 30B, RAIN reaches 97.0% harmlessness versus 88.1% for 500 repeated samples, and on LLaMA 7B the gap is even starker (69.3% vs. 64.4%), demonstrating that RAIN's guided search is far more sample-efficient than brute-force regeneration. Against vanilla auto-regressive inference, RAIN consistently improves harmlessness across all tested model sizes (7B through 70B) and also enhances adversarial robustness, truthfulness, and controlled sentiment generation without degrading helpfulness on adequately large models.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAIN repurposes the self-evaluation capability that RLAIF and Constitutional AI use for generating training data or reward models, instead deploying it directly during inference to guide a PUCT-based tree search over token sequences. The method draws on the superficial alignment hypothesis (Zhou et al., 2023) — that alignment is about selecting a sub-distribution of formats rather than changing parameters — and operationalizes this via a rewind mechanism that lets the model 'un-commit' to tokens. It is conceptually adjacent to Tree of Thoughts (Yao et al., 2023) in using lookahead and backtracking, but targets safety alignment rather than problem-solving, and differs in using self-evaluation scores (rather than LLM-judged intermediate steps) to drive the search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RAIN demonstrates that a frozen LLM's own self-evaluation, combined with a lightweight tree search, can replace the entire RLHF/RLAIF pipeline for safety alignment, with no data, no gradients, and no extra memory beyond vanilla inference. The 4× time overhead and the observation that the method is an emergent capability (ineffective on GPT-neo 1.3B/2.7B) are significant practical limitations, and the single-turn, single-objective evaluation leaves open questions about multi-turn dialogue, competing objectives (helpfulness vs. harmlessness trade-offs at scale), and whether the approach generalizes to non-safety alignment tasks. The finding that 500 rounds of reject sampling barely move the needle while RAIN's guided search achieves large gains suggests that the search structure, not just the evaluation signal, is the critical ingredient.
