## Problem

Bayesian Neural Networks provide well-calibrated uncertainty estimates, but scaling them to modern overparameterized deep networks forces severe approximations—Gaussian posteriors with diagonal or low-rank covariance, or restricting stochasticity to only the last layer—that dramatically reduce predictive strength. The key gap is how to induce stochasticity over the full set of network weights while keeping the inference problem tractable, without sacrificing the expressiveness of the posterior.

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard inputs (32×32 RGB images for CIFAR-10/100, 28×28 for MNIST, or tabular feature vectors for UCI regression) and produces full predictive distributions: class probabilities with calibrated uncertainty for classification, or Gaussian predictive means and variances for regression. Unusually, the stochasticity is not placed directly on network weights but is generated at inference time by evaluating a low-dimensional SIREN hypernetwork (as few as 160–260 parameters) that outputs multiplicative noise factors ξ for every weight in the primary network, which are then propagated through Monte Carlo sampling or closed-form linearization to yield the predictive.

## Method

Each weight w in the primary network is factored as w∘ξ, where ξ is a multiplicative nuisance factor of the same dimensionality as w. The ξ factors are parameterized by a shared SIREN INR hypernetwork f_{w_INR} that maps 5-dimensional tensor coordinates (channel, filter, kernel position, layer) to a scalar noise value, compressing the full weight-space stochasticity into a tiny parameter vector w_INR (e.g., 260 parameters for ResNet-50). A prior is placed on w_INR, and approximate inference—Laplace Approximation with full covariance, SWAG, or a RealNVP normalizing flow—is performed exclusively in this low-dimensional space, while the primary weights w are held at their MAP estimate. Predictive distributions are then obtained either by Monte Carlo sampling (draw w_INR from the posterior, evaluate ξ = f_{w_INR}(·), scale by w, and propagate through the network) or by a closed-form linearized Laplace predictive that exploits the Gaussianity of w_INR. This design achieves full-network stochasticity at a fraction of the inference cost of full-weight BNNs, while the non-Gaussian, structured ξ distributions produced by the INR yield better-calibrated uncertainty than Gaussian or rank-1 alternatives.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18, INR-SWAG achieves the best log-likelihood among low-dimensional spaces (−2.09 standard, −4.18 corrupted) with ECE of 0.22/0.36, outperforming Rank-1 (−2.29/−4.77) and matching or exceeding Subnetwork and Partially Stochastic baselines. On UCI regression, INR combined with RealNVP or Laplace achieves better test log-likelihood than BbH and Last-Layer Laplace across 8 datasets, surpassing Deep Ensembles in 5 out of 8. On Corrupted CIFAR-100 (ResNet-50), INR-RealNVP outperforms all non-INR methods in both log-likelihood and ECE, while the INR hypernetwork uses only 260 trainable parameters (0.001% of the ξ parameters).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Rank-1 multiplicative noise (Dusenberry et al., 2020), INR-SWAG achieves substantially better log-likelihood on CIFAR-100 (−2.09 vs. −2.29 standard, −4.18 vs. −4.77 corrupted) and lower Brier score (0.50 vs. 0.55), while also being 2.5× faster at inference (0.11s vs. 0.28s). Against Subnetwork inference (Daxberger et al., 2021b) and Partially Stochastic ResNets (Sharma et al., 2023), INR-SWAG trades a small ECE gap (0.22 vs. 0.20) for better log-likelihood (−2.09 vs. −2.14) and significantly faster inference (0.11s vs. 0.28–0.29s). On UCI regression, INR+RealNVP and INR+Laplace both outperform BbH (Pawlowski et al., 2017) and Last-Layer Laplace (Kristiadi et al., 2020) in test log-likelihood, and surpass Deep Ensembles (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017) in 5 of 8 datasets while remaining competitive overall. MC Dropout (Gal & Ghahramani, 2016) is outperformed by both INR variants on corrupted CIFAR-100 in ECE and LL, with INR-RealNVP showing the largest margin. GGN Laplace (Immer et al., 2021) is outperformed by INR+RealNVP on UCI regression log-likelihood. The INR approach is notably more efficient: the hypernetwork uses only 260 parameters for ResNet-50 (0.001% of ξ), enabling full-covariance Gaussian posteriors without KFAC or low-rank approximations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of Bayesian Neural Networks, Implicit Neural Representations (SIREN), and hypernetworks. It departs from prior low-dimensional Bayesian inference approaches (rank-1 factors, subnetwork selection, last-layer Laplace) that restrict stochasticity to a subset of weights, by instead making all weights stochastic while compressing the stochastic component through a tiny INR hypernetwork that generates multiplicative noise factors ξ. The approximate inference machinery (Laplace, SWAG, RealNVP) is then applied in the low-dimensional INR parameter space rather than the full weight space, enabling full-covariance posteriors without KFAC or low-rank approximations. Conceptually, this is distinct from Bayesian hypernetworks (Krueger et al., Pawlowski et al.) where the hypernetwork itself is stochastic; here the INR is deterministic and the prior is placed over its parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is that a 260-parameter INR (0.001% of the noise factors for ResNet-50) suffices to induce full-network stochasticity with competitive or superior calibration compared to Deep Ensembles, MC Dropout, and last-layer Laplace, while enabling full-covariance inference that would be intractable in the original weight space. This opens a path to more expressive posterior approximations (e.g., normalizing flows, HMC) at negligible additional cost since they operate in a tiny space. Limitations include validation only on CIFAR/MNIST/UCI-scale problems, a ~20% training overhead from hypernetwork evaluation, and the open question of whether the approach scales to ImageNet-sized models. The authors note future directions including multiplicative filter networks and Hamiltonian Monte Carlo integration.
