## Problem

Existing fairness research in sequential decision-making either assumes known MDP parameters, operates in bandit settings, or lacks regret guarantees. The central challenge is that optimizing a fairness function over individual agent value functions (rather than their sum) breaks the classical Bellman equation, rendering standard value-iteration and policy-gradient approaches inapplicable. This paper asks whether one can achieve sub-linear regret for generic α-fairness in episodic MDPs under bandit feedback, and whether a provably-efficient offline fair RL algorithm exists.

## Input / Output

In the online setting, the algorithm receives bandit-feedback observations: at each step it only sees the rewards of all N agents for the encountered state-action pair, and must maintain empirical estimates of transition probabilities and rewards across K episodes of horizon H. In the offline setting, it receives a fixed trajectory dataset collected by a behavior policy. The output is a fair policy π_h(a|s) recovered from the optimal occupancy measure, along with the fairness value V_1^F(s_1). A key constraint is that all immediate rewards must be bounded below by ε/H to ensure the fairness functions (log, power) remain finite and Lipschitz.

## Method

The core insight is to parameterize the problem via state-action (or state-action-next-state) occupancy measures, which makes each agent's return a linear function of the measure, so the fairness objective becomes a concave function over a polytope of linear constraints — a convex optimization solvable in polynomial time. For online learning, the algorithm maintains empirical estimates of rewards and transition probabilities with confidence intervals, then solves an optimistic convex program where rewards are inflated by β^r and transition constraints are relaxed to [p̄−β^p, p̄+β^p], encouraging exploration. Since Bellman's equation fails for non-additive objectives, the regret proof uses a two-step bounding: first applying the Lipschitz property of F to reduce the fairness gap to individual value-function gaps (scaled by C_F), then bounding each gap via the value-difference lemma and Azuma-Hoeffding. For offline learning, a pessimistic reward r̲ = r̄ − b^r − H·Σb^p is constructed so that the value under (r̲, p̄) lower-bounds the true value with high probability, and the same convex program is solved on the empirical MDP. A policy-gradient variant is also provided for large state spaces, computing ∇_θ F by the chain rule over per-agent value gradients.

## Results

The paper's primary contributions are theoretical guarantees rather than empirical benchmarks. The online algorithm (Algorithm 1) achieves a sub-linear regret bound of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(C_F(H^2NS\sqrt{AK}))$ (Theorem 1), where $C_F$ depends on the fairness type ($\epsilon^{-\alpha}$ for $\alpha$-fairness, $\epsilon^{-1}$ for proportional fairness, $1/N$ for max-min fairness). The PAC guarantee (Theorem 2) requires $C = C_F \max\{\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(H^5N^2S^2A/\varepsilon^2),\; \tilde{\mathcal{O}}(H^3N^4S^3A^2/\varepsilon^2),\; \tilde{\mathcal{O}}(H^3N^2S^4A^2/\varepsilon^2)\}$ samples. For the offline setting, the suboptimality gap is bounded by $2NC_F \cdot \mathbb{E}_{\pi^*}[\text{Intrinsic-Uncertainty}]$ (Theorem 3). Numerical experiments on randomly generated MDPs are provided in Appendix G but no main comparison table is included in the body.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not include a direct empirical comparison table against competing methods; the `compares_to` set is empty. The closest prior work is Mandal & Gan (2022), which provides regret bounds for Nash social welfare and max-min fairness in MDPs. The key theoretical distinction is in scaling: Mandal & Gan's bound scales as $O(H^N)$ (exponential in the number of agents), whereas this paper's bound scales as $O(NH^2)$ (polynomial in N). For max-min fairness specifically, the regret bound matches that of Mandal & Gan (2022), but the proposed algorithm is computationally more efficient since it requires solving a finite-variable convex optimization rather than an infinite-variable one. The paper also notes that when $\alpha=0$, the result recovers the standard single-agent episodic MDP regret (scaled by N), providing a consistency check. No head-to-head numerical comparison against prior algorithms is reported in the main text.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the occupancy-measure-based RL framework of Efroni et al. (2020) and the socially fair RL approach of Mandal & Gan (2022) to the generic α-fairness objective, which encompasses both proportional fairness (α=1) and max-min fairness (α→∞) as special cases. The key conceptual departure is that the non-linearity of the fairness function in individual value functions renders the classical Bellman equation inapplicable, forcing a shift from value-iteration-based methods to a convex optimization over state-action occupancy measures with confidence-bound relaxation. For the offline setting, the paper adapts the pessimistic reward construction from Jin et al. (2021b) but requires a fundamentally different proof technique since the standard Bellman-consistency argument for pessimism does not carry over to non-linear fairness objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide sub-linear regret bounds for generic α-fairness in episodic MDPs and the first theoretical guarantee for offline fair RL, filling a notable gap between the well-studied resource-allocation fairness literature and the RL community. The regret bound scaling as Õ(H²NS√(AK)) is substantially tighter than the O(H^N) dependence in the closest prior work (Mandal & Gan, 2022) for Nash social welfare, making the approach more practical for multi-agent settings. Key limitations include the tabular finite-horizon setup, central-controller assumption, and bandit-feedback model; the paper identifies decentralized fair MARL, function-approximation-based policy gradients with convergence guarantees, and non-tabular extensions as important open directions.
