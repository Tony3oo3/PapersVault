## Problem

Most vision-language models (VLMs) are designed around single-image prompts and struggle with complex multi-modal inputs containing multiple interleaved images and text. Specifically, they fail to resolve text-to-image references, understand spatial/temporal/logical relationships among multiple images, and leverage in-context multi-modal demonstrations, because their pre-training data (web-crawled, loosely related image-text pairs) does not match the structure of real-world user queries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240598/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an arbitrary number of images interleaved with text in any order, where each image is preceded by a natural-language declaration (e.g., 'image 0 is [IMG0]') that assigns a unique proxy token for explicit referencing throughout the prompt. In-context demonstrations (few-shot exemplars with their own image declarations) are prepended to the query. The output is free-form text: answers to visual questions, image/video captions, classification labels, or reasoning responses. Video inputs are handled by extracting eight frames per video as separate image tokens.

## Method

MMICL treats image and text embeddings symmetrically by interleaving them in a single sequence fed to the LLM, rather than injecting visual features via separate cross-attention layers (as in Flamingo). A Q-Former extracts fixed-size visual embeddings from a ViT encoder, a projection layer aligns them to the LLM's text embedding dimension, and the query/value projection weights in the LLM's self-attention are made learnable to adapt to multi-image contexts. The key architectural innovation is the image declaration mechanism: each image receives a unique [IMG_j] proxy token plus a natural-language reference, enabling the model to disambiguate which image a textual mention points to. Training is two-stage: Stage I aligns visual-text embeddings (freezing the vision encoder and LLM, training only the Q-Former and projection), and Stage II performs multi-modal in-context tuning on the 5.8M-sample MIC dataset (freezing the vision encoder, Q-Former, and LLM while jointly training the projection layer and Q/V vectors). The MIC dataset is automatically constructed from 16 public datasets using three strategies: adding image declarations, generating interconnected multi-image data from video frames and VCR bounding-box crops, and assembling unified in-context formats with diverse ChatGPT-generated instruction templates.

## Results

On the MME benchmark, MMICL (12.1B, FLAN-T5-XXL) achieves a total average score of 129.33, surpassing the next-best models BLIVA (119.23) and LRV-Instruction (116.29). On the Winoground compositionality benchmark, MMICL achieves a group score of 43.00, a 13-point improvement over GPT-4V (39.25) and 19.5 points over BLIP-2 (23.50). On the RAVEN nonverbal reasoning test, MMICL reaches 34.00% accuracy, a 12-point gain over KOSMOS-1 and Otter (both 22.00%). On ScienceQA-IMG, MMICL achieves 82.10% average accuracy with only a 0.90-point gap between visual and non-visual questions, compared to a 21.30-point gap for InstructBLIP, demonstrating effective mitigation of language bias.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMICL achieves the best overall MME score (129.33), outperforming BLIVA (119.23) by 10.1 points and LRV-Instruction (116.29) by 13.0 points, with particularly strong gains in Text Recognition (132.50 vs. 77.50 for BLIVA) and Numerical Calculation (82.50 vs. 57.50). On Winoground, MMICL (43.00 group score) surpasses GPT-4V (39.25) by 3.75 points and BLIP-2 (23.50) by 19.5 points, demonstrating superior text-to-image reference understanding. On RAVEN, MMICL (34.00%) leads KOSMOS-1 and Otter (both 22.00%) by 12 points and InstructBLIP (10.00%) by 24 points in nonverbal visual reasoning. For multi-modal ICL, MMICL (Instruct-FLAN-T5-XXL) with 4-shot examples achieves 50.28 on VizWiz, exceeding Flamingo-9B (34.90) by 15.38 points and KOSMOS-1 (35.30) by 14.98 points, while also leading on VQAv2 (70.56 vs. 56.30 for Flamingo-9B) and Hateful Memes (64.60 vs. 62.70). On ScienceQA-IMG, MMICL (82.10%) outperforms InstructBLIP (71.30%) by 10.8 points while reducing the language bias gap from 21.30 to just 0.90 points, indicating far more balanced use of visual and textual information.

## Relation to Prior Work

MMICL builds directly on the BLIP-2/InstructBLIP architecture (frozen ViT + Q-Former + frozen FLAN-T5 LLM) but departs from the single-image paradigm by treating image and text embeddings as equally interleaved tokens rather than placing a single image at the top of the prompt. It extends the multi-image capability introduced by Flamingo and KOSMOS-1 by adding an explicit image-declaration mechanism (proxy tokens like [IMG_j] with natural-language references) and by constructing training data with spatially, temporally, and logically interconnected images. The unified multi-modal in-context format generalizes the LLM in-context learning paradigm to the vision-language setting, where prior VLMs either lacked multi-image support (BLIP-2, LLaVA) or lacked explicit text-to-image references in their pre-training data (Flamingo, KOSMOS-1).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MIC dataset (5.8M samples across 16 training and 18 test datasets) provides a practical resource for training VLMs that handle complex multi-modal prompts, addressing a gap where prior pre-training data (web-crawled interleaved image-text) lacked explicit referential structure. The image-declaration design is simple and modular, making it potentially adoptable by other VLM architectures. However, the authors note that only ~10% of the data was used due to compute constraints, leaving open whether full-data training or larger backbones would yield substantially larger gains. A notable limitation is that ICL benefits are task-dependent (Flickr30K captioning actually degrades with more exemplars), suggesting the in-context mechanism is not uniformly beneficial across all vision-language tasks.
