## Problem

Pretrained vision-language models like CLIP perform poorly on low-level vision tasks such as image restoration because they cannot capture fine-grained differences between degradation types (e.g., 'blurry' vs. 'noisy'), causing image features to misalign with degradation texts on corrupted inputs. Existing unified restoration methods handle only a few degradation types and do not leverage the semantic knowledge embedded in large-scale VLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240649/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-quality corrupted RGB image (resized to 224×224, normalized to [0,1]) and produces two outputs: a high-quality content embedding aligned with clean captions, and a degradation embedding that identifies the corruption type. These embeddings are then injected into a downstream restoration network (diffusion-based IR-SDE or MSE-based NAFNet) via cross-attention and a prompt-learning module, respectively, to yield the final restored image. The degradation embedding also serves as a natural classifier, achieving near-perfect accuracy across ten degradation types.

## Method

DA-CLIP freezes both the CLIP text encoder and ViT-B-32 image encoder, and adds a trainable image controller (a copy of the ViT) with zero-initialized dense connections between transformer blocks, following the ControlNet paradigm. The controller outputs a degradation embedding and hidden controls that are added to the frozen encoder's block outputs, gradually adapting it during training without overfitting. Training uses a joint contrastive objective that simultaneously aligns the controller's content embedding with clean BLIP-generated captions and its degradation embedding with the true degradation text. For restoration, the content embedding is injected into the U-Net via cross-attention in the bottom blocks, while the degradation embedding drives a prompt-learning module for unified multi-degradation restoration. The approach is trained on a mixed dataset of ten degradation types with synthetic captions and can be plugged into both diffusion-based and direct MSE-based restoration architectures.

## Results

On the unified image restoration task (10 degradation types), DA-CLIP achieves the best perceptual quality with LPIPS 0.127 and FID 34.89, outperforming PromptIR (LPIPS 0.147, FID 48.26) and IR-SDE (LPIPS 0.167, FID 49.18), though it trails NAFNet+DA-CLIP on distortion metrics (PSNR 27.01 vs. 27.22, SSIM 0.794 vs. 0.861). On degradation-specific tasks, the method sets a new state-of-the-art on deraining (PSNR 33.91, FID 11.79) and achieves the best perceptual scores on dehazing (LPIPS 0.030, FID 5.52) and deblurring (LPIPS 0.058, FID 6.15). On the out-of-distribution Rain100L dataset, DA-CLIP surpasses all unified baselines by a large margin (PSNR 36.61, FID 11.98 vs. PromptIR's 32.77 and 43.31).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the unified restoration benchmark, DA-CLIP (built on IR-SDE) achieves the best perceptual metrics (LPIPS 0.127, FID 34.89) but sacrifices distortion fidelity compared to MSE-based baselines: NAFNet+DA-CLIP leads on PSNR (27.22) and SSIM (0.861), while PromptIR is close on PSNR (27.14). Against the diffusion baseline IR-SDE, DA-CLIP improves all four metrics substantially (e.g., FID drops from 49.18 to 34.89). On degradation-specific tasks, the method dominates deraining (PSNR 33.91 vs. MAXIM's 30.81 and IR-SDE's 31.65) and wins on perceptual metrics for dehazing (FID 5.52 vs. DehazeFormer's 7.58) and deblurring (FID 6.15 vs. MAXIM's 11.57), though MAXIM retains an edge on PSNR/SSIM for deblurring (32.86 vs. 30.88). For low-light enhancement, DA-CLIP outperforms IR-SDE on all metrics (PSNR 23.77 vs. 20.45) and beats MAXIM on PSNR and FID but trails on SSIM (0.830 vs. 0.863). On the OOD Rain100L test, DA-CLIP generalizes far better than AirNet (PSNR 36.61 vs. 30.07) and PromptIR (36.61 vs. 32.77), confirming robustness to unseen degradation levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected lines of work: large-scale vision-language models (CLIP) and low-level image restoration. It adapts the ControlNet-style zero-initialized controller to wrap a frozen CLIP ViT image encoder, enabling it to produce degradation-free content embeddings and degradation-type embeddings from corrupted inputs. For the restoration side, it builds on IR-SDE (a U-Net-based diffusion model) and integrates the VLM embeddings via cross-attention and prompt learning, extending the unified image restoration paradigm of AirNet and PromptIR by grounding degradation classification in pretrained VLM knowledge rather than learning it from scratch. The training data pipeline leverages BLIP's bootstrapped caption generation to construct image-text-degradation tuples without manual annotation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a lightweight, zero-initialized controller is sufficient to repurpose a frozen VLM for low-level vision, offering a practical recipe for transferring web-scale semantic knowledge to pixel-level restoration tasks. The mixed degradation dataset with ten corruption types and synthetic captions provides a useful benchmark for unified restoration. Key limitations include the inability to handle multiple simultaneous degradations in a single image, reliance on a single VLM backbone (ViT-B-32), and increased memory footprint. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to real-world mixed degradations, whether other VLM backbones yield better representations, and whether instruction-based restoration via the text encoder can be explored.
