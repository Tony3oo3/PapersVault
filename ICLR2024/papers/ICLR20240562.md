## Problem

Protein diffusion methods represent structures using backbone residue frames rather than explicit atoms, rendering most existing structure encoders (which rely on interatomic distances and angles) inapplicable. The only encoder designed for frame modeling, IPA (from AlphaFold2), suffers from an 'atom representation bottleneck': it extracts geometric features via a single scalar (sum of pairwise virtual-atom distances) with no learnable weights, severely limiting expressiveness. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a more expressive frame-based encoder that overcomes this bottleneck.

## Input / Output

For protein diffusion (VFN-Diff), the input is a set of residue backbone frames T_i (4×4 rigid transformations per residue) and the output is a sampled protein backbone structure. For inverse folding (VFN-IF), the input additionally includes backbone atom coordinates, which are used to initialize a subset of frame-anchored virtual atoms, enabling hierarchical frame-and-atom modeling; the output is a 20-category amino acid assignment per residue. In VFN-IFE, the VFN-IF probability output is further refined by a LoRA-fine-tuned ESM language model that accepts soft (probability-weighted) token embeddings rather than hard labels.

## Method

The core contribution is the Vector Field Operator, a vector-specific analogue of a linear layer: instead of scalar inputs, each input channel receives a 3D virtual-atom coordinate, and learnable scalar weights w^a, w^b are applied to produce multiple output feature vectors h_k = Σ_l w^a_{k,l} q_l + Σ_l w^b_{k,l} k_l, where k_l is the j-th residue's virtual atoms transformed into the i-th residue's local frame. To handle the large numerical range of these vectors, each h_k is decomposed into a unit direction vector and an RBF-encoded length, concatenated into a geometric feature g_{i,j}. These features feed into MLP-based multi-head attention (node interactions) and edge interactions to update residue and edge representations, after which virtual atom coordinates are refreshed via either a linear projection of node features or an attention-weighted coordinate aggregation (V-MLP). SE(3) invariance is guaranteed because all vector computations occur within local residue frames, eliminating the need for the distance-pooling constraint that limits IPA. In VFN-Diff, the operator replaces IPA's point attention inside FrameDiff; in VFN-IF, it replaces PiGNN inside the PiFold framework, with real backbone atoms treated as additional virtual atoms for hierarchical modeling.

## Results

On inverse folding (CATH 4.2), VFN-IF achieves 54.74% sequence recovery (All) and 4.17 perplexity, outperforming PiFold (51.66%, 4.55) and ProteinMPNN (45.96%, 4.61). With the ESM knowledge base, VFN-IFE reaches 62.67% recovery and 3.36 perplexity, substantially surpassing LM-Design (55.65%, 4.52). On protein diffusion, VFN-Diff achieves 67.04% designability (scTM>0.5) and 66.54% diversity, compared to FrameDiff's 53.58% and 51.98%, respectively, with a similar parameter count (18.3M vs. 17.4M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VFN-IF outperforms all non-ESM baselines on CATH 4.2: it beats PiFold (54.74% vs. 51.66% recovery), ProteinMPNN (54.74% vs. 45.96%), GVP (54.74% vs. 39.47%), AlphaDesign (54.74% vs. 41.31%), GCA (54.74% vs. 37.64%), StructGNN (54.74% vs. 35.91%), and GraphTrans (54.74% vs. 35.82%), with the largest margin over ProteinMPNN (+8.78%). In the ESM-equipped setting, VFN-IFE surpasses LM-Design by 7.02% (62.67% vs. 55.65%) and ESM-IF by 11.07% (62.67% vs. 51.60%), while also achieving the lowest perplexity (3.36 vs. 4.52 and 4.01). On protein diffusion, VFN-Diff dominates FrameDiff across all noise scales and step counts: at the standard setting (noise 1.0, 500 steps, 8 sequences), designability improves from 53.58% to 67.04% (+13.46%) and diversity from 51.98% to 66.54% (+14.56%). The speed-accuracy trade-off analysis shows VFN-IF with only 5 layers (52.68% recovery) already exceeds 10-layer PiFold (51.66%) while being faster, and VFN-IF does not saturate at 10 layers unlike PiFold. On TS50/TS500, VFN-IFE achieves 73.30%/72.49% recovery, far exceeding LM-Design (57.89%/67.78%) and PiFold (58.72%/60.42%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the protein diffusion paradigm (FrameDiff, RFDiffusion) and the inverse folding task (PiFold, ProteinMPNN), identifying a specific architectural bottleneck in the Invariant Point Attention (IPA) encoder from AlphaFold2: its reliance on a single pooled distance scalar between frame-anchored virtual atoms, which limits geometric expressiveness. VFN departs from IPA by performing learnable vector operations (analogous to a linear layer) on virtual atom coordinates within local frames, producing multiple feature vectors rather than one scalar, thereby circumventing the SE(3)-invariance constraint that forced IPA into distance pooling. The approach also extends naturally to atom-level modeling (inverse folding) by treating real backbone atoms as virtual atoms, positioning VFN as a potential universal encoder across both frame-only and frame+atom settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in the dominant de novo protein design pipeline (diffusion + inverse folding), where the absence of atomic information renders most prior structure encoders inapplicable. The VFN-IFE variant, which fine-tunes a 15B ESM model with LoRA to correct VFN-IF predictions, opens a path for integrating large language model knowledge into structure-based design without full retraining. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very long proteins or multi-chain complexes, whether the vector field operator generalizes to other 3D molecular tasks beyond proteins, and whether the gains persist when the full pipeline (VFN-Diff + VFN-IF) is compared under matched compute budgets rather than the controlled encoder-swap protocol used here.
