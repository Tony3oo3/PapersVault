## Problem

Extensive numerical studies over the past decade have revealed that the Hessian and gradient second-moment (G-) matrices of overparameterized networks exhibit a bulk-outlier spectral structure, and that SGD trajectories rapidly align with the low-dimensional outlier eigenspaces. However, a simultaneous rigorous understanding of high-dimensional SGD dynamics and the evolution of Hessian/G-matrix spectra along the training trajectory had remained largely open, with prior theoretical work focusing on spectra at fixed points (typically initialization) rather than along the full training path.

## Input / Output

The method takes k-component Gaussian mixture data in dimension d (with d → ∞) and a neural network architecture—either a single-layer logistic regression (k classes) or a two-layer ReLU network of width K (4-class XOR structure)—along with SGD hyperparameters (step size δ = O(1/d), ℓ² regularization β). The output is a rigorous characterization of the alignment between the SGD trajectory and the top eigenspaces of the empirical Hessian and G-matrices computed from test data, including the explicit rank of the outlier eigenspaces (k for 1-layer, 2 for first-layer and 4 for second-layer in the XOR case) and how this rank becomes deficient when SGD converges to suboptimal classifiers. The alignment is proven to hold with probability 1 − o_d(1) after O(δ⁻¹) steps, with error O(ε + λ⁻¹) or O(ε + λ⁻¹/²) depending on the architecture.

## Method

The proof proceeds in three key steps. First, the population Hessian and G-matrices are expanded in large signal-to-noise ratio λ using Gaussian integration by parts, revealing that each block decomposes into a finite-rank (rank k or rank 4) outlier part spanned by the class mean vectors plus a bulk of O(1/λ) operator norm. Second, the SGD trajectory is analyzed via limiting effective dynamics: finite families of summary statistics (inner products of parameters with means, pairwise overlaps) are shown to converge to autonomous ODEs in the high-dimensional limit, and the λ-finite trajectory is expanded about its λ = ∞ solution to establish that the SGD lives in the span of the means up to O(1/λ) error. Third, concentration inequalities for uniformly sub-exponential random variables combined with ε-net arguments show that the empirical Hessian and G-matrices concentrate about their population versions in operator norm uniformly over the parameter space. The key insight is that the same low-dimensional subspace (spanned by the class means) simultaneously governs the outlier eigenspaces and the SGD trajectory, and in the two-layer XOR case the second layer's rank-4 outlier eigenspace only emerges dynamically during training—a dynamical analogue of the BBP phase transition—while its rank drops below 4 precisely when SGD converges to suboptimal classifiers.

## Results

The paper proves that for k-class Gaussian mixture classification with a 1-layer network, the SGD trajectory aligns with the top-k eigenspaces of both the empirical Hessian and G-matrix (per class block) up to O(ε + λ⁻¹) error after O(δ⁻¹) steps (Theorem 3.1). In the orthonormal-means special case, the SGD further aligns specifically with the single largest outlier eigenvalue, which separates from the other k−1 outliers during training (Theorem 3.3). For the 2-layer XOR GMM problem, alignment occurs per layer: first-layer rows align with rank-2 outlier subspaces and the second-layer vector aligns with a rank-4 outlier subspace (Theorem 4.1), with the second-layer outliers emerging dynamically from a rank-K bulk via a BBP-type spectral transition. When SGD converges to sub-optimal classifiers, the outlier eigenspaces become rank-deficient (rank < 4 in the XOR case), providing a provable diagnostic for classification failure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical method comparisons or benchmark tables. Rather than competing against baselines, it provides rigorous proofs for phenomena previously observed only numerically by Gur-Ari et al. (2019) and Papyan (2019, 2020). The paper extends the SGD effective dynamics framework of Ben Arous et al. (2022) by deriving new ODE limits for the general k-GMM case (Theorem C.7) and by coupling those dynamics with spectral analysis of the Hessian and G-matrices. It builds on the XOR classification dynamics of Refinetti et al. (2021) and Ben Arous et al. (2022) to show that the previously observed convergence to sub-optimal classifiers manifests as rank-deficient outlier eigenspaces. The concentration results for empirical matrices (Section D) extend the approach of Mei et al. (2018) to the non-differentiable ReLU setting via ε-net arguments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate theoretical threads: high-dimensional SGD limit theorems (Ben Arous et al. 2022, Refinetti et al. 2021) and random-matrix-theoretic analysis of neural network Hessians (Pennington & Worah 2018, Liao & Mahoney 2021). Prior spectral work characterized Hessian and information-matrix spectra at fixed points (typically initialization), while prior SGD analyses tracked low-dimensional summary statistics without connecting them to spectral structure. The paper's core contribution is the first rigorous joint treatment: it proves that the same low-dimensional subspace governing SGD dynamics also generates the outlier eigenspaces of the Hessian and G-matrices, and that this alignment emerges dynamically (a 'dynamical BBP transition' in the second layer) rather than being present at initialization. The empirical observations of Gur-Ari et al. (2019) and Papyan (2019) are thus given a precise mathematical foundation in two canonical high-dimensional classification tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The rank-deficiency result—where sub-optimal classifiers produce lower-rank outlier eigenspaces than the number of hidden classes—provides a concrete, provable diagnostic for detecting when SGD has converged to a bad solution, and connects the lottery ticket hypothesis to a measurable spectral quantity. The dynamical emergence of outliers in the overparametrized second layer (absent at initialization, appearing only as the first layer aligns with data means) gives a rigorous account of the neural-collapse-like crystallization observed empirically. Limitations are significant: results hold only in the high-dimensional limit (d→∞) for two specific Gaussian mixture tasks with fixed k, and the XOR extension requires M ≳ d log d for train-data matrices. Extending the framework to deeper networks, non-Gaussian data, or practical architectures remains open, as does dropping the logarithmic sample-complexity overhead.
