## Problem

Manifold-based self-supervised learning methods like MSimCLR rely on a uniform prior over chart membership, which forces inputs into each chart embedding uniformly. This uniform prior introduces higher prediction uncertainty and causes convergent head embeddings when encoding dimensions grow, limiting MSimCLR to extremely low target dimensions (≤8) where it can outperform SimCLR. The paper asks whether a manifold representation paradigm can scale to high-dimensional encodings (≥256 per head) while maintaining effective, diverse chart outputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241978/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes temporally adjacent RGB image pairs (x_t, x_{t+1}) at 160×210 resolution from Atari 2600 games (AtariARI benchmark) or standard CIFAR-10 images. The encoder (a CNN backbone) produces a shared latent representation that is then projected by n parallel chart heads, each yielding a d-dimensional embedding, alongside a membership probability vector of length n. At inference, only the single argmax head's embedding is retained, while the remaining heads serve as dilated prediction targets during pretraining—functionally analogous to BYOL's momentum network.

## Method

DIM-UA adapts the ST-DIM (Spatiotemporal Deep InfoMax) framework with an Unbalanced Atlas (UA) paradigm: instead of enforcing a uniform prior over chart membership, an MMD-based loss L_Q actively pushes the membership probability distribution away from uniformity, increasing prediction confidence. To prevent convergent head embeddings at high dimensions, the global-local score function uses dilated prediction targets computed as the Minkowski sum (average) of all n head outputs rather than a single head's embedding. Two propositions guarantee that this Minkowski-sum construction preserves all mapped intersection embeddings under a convexity assumption. The total training objective is L_UA = L_GL + L_LL + τ·L_Q, combining the standard infoNCE-based global-local and local-local losses with the unbalanced membership loss. The same UA paradigm is also applied to SimCLR (yielding SimCLR-UA), demonstrating its generality across contrastive and mutual-information-based SSL frameworks.

## Results

On the AtariARI benchmark (19 games), DIM-UA achieves a mean F1 score of 0.75±0.013, outperforming ST-DIM (0.72), ST-DIM* with 16384 units (0.70±0.033), CPC (0.63), and VAE (0.41). DIM-UA matches or exceeds all baselines in every individual game, with particularly large gains on Freeway (0.86 vs. 0.30 for ST-DIM*) and Pong (0.85 vs. 0.78 for ST-DIM*). On CIFAR-10 linear probing, SimCLR-UA reaches 88.6% accuracy (8 heads × 512 units), surpassing SimCLR (88.3%) and MSimCLR (best 87.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIM-UA (4 heads × 4096 units) outperforms all baselines on the AtariARI benchmark, achieving a mean F1 of 0.75 versus 0.72 for ST-DIM (256 units) and 0.70 for ST-DIM* (1 head × 16384 units), with notably lower variance (±0.013 vs. ±0.033). The advantage is most pronounced on Freeway, where ST-DIM* collapses to 0.30±0.355 while DIM-UA reaches 0.86±0.02. On CIFAR-10, SimCLR-UA (8 heads × 512 units) achieves 88.6% linear probing accuracy, a modest 0.3-point gain over SimCLR (88.3% at 512 units) but a clear 0.8-point improvement over the best MSimCLR configuration (87.8% with 2 heads × 512 units). MSimCLR degrades substantially as the number of heads increases (87.8% → 86.4% from 2 to 8 heads), whereas SimCLR-UA maintains or improves performance with more heads, validating the unbalanced atlas paradigm at higher dimensions. VAE and CPC baselines are substantially weaker (0.41 and 0.63 mean F1), confirming that the contrastive/mutual-information objectives remain the strongest framework for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the manifold-based self-supervised learning line initiated by Korman's MSimCLR (2021b), which used a uniform prior over chart membership probabilities, by proposing an 'unbalanced atlas' (UA) paradigm that deliberately trains membership distributions away from uniformity. It adapts the ST-DIM spatiotemporal Deep InfoMax framework (Anand et al., 2019; Hjelm et al., 2018) and also modifies SimCLR (Chen et al., 2020) to demonstrate the paradigm's generality. The key methodological departure is the use of dilated prediction targets via Minkowski sums of chart outputs, grounded in two propositions showing that this preserves coverage of intersected manifold regions while preventing convergent head embeddings at high dimensions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The UA paradigm demonstrates that manifold-based SSL can scale to high encoding dimensions (≥256 per head), whereas prior manifold SSL work (MSimCLR) was only effective at very low dimensions (≤8), potentially unlocking larger backbone architectures for representation learning. However, the practical impact is tempered: the CIFAR-10 improvement over SimCLR is marginal (88.6% vs 88.3%), and the more compelling AtariARI gains (75% vs 70% mean F1) are on a niche benchmark. Open questions include the role of the convexity assumption in Proposition 2, the optimal relationship between number of heads and per-head dimensionality, and whether the UA paradigm generalizes beyond the two tested SSL frameworks.
