## Problem

While it was established that Unique Hard Attention Transformers (UHATs) recognize only languages in AC^0, the precise fragment of AC^0 they can recognize remained unknown, and the extent to which Average Hard Attention Transformers (AHATs) extend this counting capability was uncharacterized. This paper addresses both gaps: showing that UHATs cannot recognize all of AC^0, yet can recognize a rich fragment (FO(Mon)), and that AHATs can recognize a powerful counting logic (LTL(C,+)) that goes beyond AC^0.

## Input / Output

The input is a word over a finite alphabet, where each symbol is embedded as a one-hot vector augmented with a positional encoding that encodes the absolute position i, the reciprocal 1/(i+1), the parity (-1)^i, trigonometric functions of position, and the values of all unary numerical predicates θ_n(i) at that position relative to word length n. The output is a scalar acceptance decision: the inner product of the first output vector with a fixed target vector must be positive for accepted words and negative for rejected words. The positional encoding is critical—it is what enables the attention mechanism to implement positional reasoning (next, until) and counting operations.

## Method

The paper establishes three main results. First, a negative result: combining Ajtai's approximate majority circuit family with a structural property of UHATs from Hahn (2020) shows that an AC^0 language (approximate majority) is not UHAT-recognizable. Second, a positive result for UHATs: rather than directly translating FO(Mon) formulas, the authors invoke Kamp's Theorem to reduce the problem to LTL(Mon), then prove by structural induction that every LTL(Mon) formula is realizable position-wise by a UHAT; the key mechanism is constructing positional encodings with trigonometric components so that the attention score ⟨Av_i, v_j⟩ is uniquely maximized at j=i+1 (for the next operator) or at the first position where a computed predicate holds (for the until operator). Third, a positive result for AHATs: the authors introduce LTL(C,+), which extends LTL(Mon) with left/right counting terms and linear integer inequalities over them, and show that the averaging mechanism of AHAT naturally computes these counts, enabling recognition of languages outside AC^0 such as majority and parity.

## Results

The paper establishes three main theoretical characterizations: (1) UHAT encoders do not accept all AC⁰ languages — specifically, the approximate majority language (an AC⁰ language) is provably not UHAT-recognizable (Proposition 1); (2) UHAT encoders accept all languages definable in FO(Mon), the first-order logic extended with arbitrary unary numerical predicates (Theorem 1), which includes all regular languages in AC⁰ and non-regular languages like {aⁿbⁿ | n>0}; (3) AHAT encoders accept all languages definable in LTL(C,+), a counting extension of LTL(Mon) (Theorem 2), which captures languages outside AC⁰ such as parity and majority, as well as permutation closures of all regular languages (Corollary 2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper positions its results against several prior theoretical characterizations of transformer expressivity. Compared to Hao et al. (2022), which showed UHATs are contained in AC⁰ and that AHATs can recognize majority, this paper provides a precise logical characterization of the UHAT fragment (FO(Mon)) and extends the AHAT result to all of LTL(C,+), newly showing that parity (previously unaddressed) is AHAT-recognizable. Compared to Hahn (2020), which established a key structural property of UHATs used here for the negative result, this paper leverages that property to prove the first separation between UHAT and AC⁰. Compared to Yao et al. (2021), which showed UHATs recognize Dyck languages of bounded nesting depth, the FO(Mon) result strictly subsumes this since those languages are regular and in AC⁰. Compared to Chiang et al. (2023), which embedded a counting logic into soft-attention transformers, this paper works with hard attention and retains access to word order (unlike Chiang et al.'s logic, which cannot express simple FO-definable languages like a*b*). The paper also shows UHATs are strictly more powerful than regular and context-free languages up to letter-permutation (Proposition 5), a result not available from prior work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the circuit-complexity-based characterization of transformer encoder expressiveness (Hao et al., 2022; Hahn, 2020) from coarse upper bounds (UHAT ⊆ AC⁰, AHAT ⊆ TC⁰) to precise logical characterizations. The key technical bridge is Kamp's Theorem (1968), which allows the authors to reduce the FO(Mon) → UHAT proof to a structural induction over LTL(Mon) formulas, sidestepping the arity problem that makes direct FO induction intractable. The negative result (not all AC⁰ languages are in UHAT) combines Ajtai's (1983) approximate-majority circuit family with a structural property of UHATs from Hahn (2020), while the AHAT results build on the average-hard-attention mechanism introduced by Hao et al. (2022) and Pérez et al. (2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first fine-grained logical characterization of what UHAT and AHAT encoders can and cannot recognize, moving beyond the coarse AC⁰/TC⁰ bounds to identify FO(Mon) as a rich lower bound for UHAT and LTL(C,+) for AHAT. The result that AHATs can recognize parity (a canonical non-AC⁰ language) and all permutation closures of regular languages is a concrete demonstration of the counting power gained by averaging over tied attention positions. A key open question is whether FO(Mon) is the exact boundary for UHATs (a contemporaneous result by Angluin et al., 2023, confirms this under positional masking, but the paper notes UHATs with infinite-valued positional encoding can go beyond FO(Mon), e.g., recognizing palindromes). For practitioners, the results clarify that hard-attention transformers are fundamentally limited in counting, which has implications for tasks requiring exact frequency reasoning.
