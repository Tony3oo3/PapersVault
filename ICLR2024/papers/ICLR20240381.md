## Problem

Existing video-language pretraining frameworks are confined to vision and language, and the closest multi-modal extension, ImageBind, relies on images as an intermediary for aligning other modalities (infrared, depth, audio, IMU). This indirect alignment can degrade performance on language-centric downstream tasks and complicates straightforward extension to additional modalities. The paper asks whether using language itself as the central binding modality—given its high semantic density and well-explored encoder—can achieve direct, scalable N-modality alignment.

## Input / Output

Non-language inputs are processed into a unified token format: video clips (8 frames, up to 20 s) and depth/infrared images (224×224, channel-replicated to 3) are patchified at 14×14, while audio is converted to 10-second, 128-mel-bin spectrograms that are repeated and zero-padded to a fixed duration. Text prompts are tokenized with BPE to a maximum of 77 tokens. All modalities are mapped to a single shared embedding space; downstream use produces either a class label (zero-shot classification via text-template matching) or a ranked list of candidates (cross-modal retrieval via cosine similarity).

## Method

LanguageBind freezes the OpenCLIP language encoder (12-layer, 768-dim transformer) and trains separate 24-layer ViT-L/14 encoders (1024-dim, patch size 14) for each non-language modality using a symmetric contrastive (InfoNCE) loss that directly pulls paired modality–text embeddings together and pushes unpaired ones apart. To keep training efficient, the paper applies MAE-style patch masking (50% mask ratio) and LoRA fine-tuning (rank 2 for depth/infrared, rank 16 for video/audio) so that only low-rank adapter matrices are learned while the base OpenCLIP weights remain frozen. The key architectural distinction from ImageBind is that every modality aligns directly to the language embedding space rather than routing through an image encoder, eliminating the indirect-alignment bottleneck. The approach is validated on VIDAL-10M, a 10-million-pair dataset with aligned video, infrared, depth, audio, and multi-view text (title, hashtags, OFA keyframe captions, mPLUG-owl video captions, and ChatGPT-enhanced captions), where infrared and depth frames are synthesized from video keyframes using sRGB-TIR and GLPN generative models.

## Results

On zero-shot X-language classification (Table 4), LanguageBind (ViT-Large/14) achieves 64.0% top-1 on Kinetics-400, 87.2% on LLVIP (infrared), 65.1% on NYU-D (depth), and 91.8% on ESC-50 (audio), outperforming ImageBind (ViT-Huge) by 14.0%, 23.8%, 11.1%, and 23.9% respectively. On zero-shot text-to-video retrieval (Table 2), LanguageBind achieves R@1 of 42.6 on MSR-VTT, 52.2 on MSVD, 37.8 on DiDeMo, and 35.1 on ActivityNet, surpassing the strongest baselines by 1.9–8.8 points. For zero-shot audio-language retrieval, LanguageBind reaches R@1 of 12.1 on Clotho and 12.2 on Audiocaps, exceeding ImageBind by 6.1 and 5.5 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LanguageBind decisively outperforms ImageBind (ViT-Huge) across every modality despite using a smaller ViT-Large backbone: +14.0% on K400, +23.8% on LLVIP, +11.1% on NYU-D, and +23.9% on ESC-50, confirming that direct language alignment is superior to ImageBind's image-mediated indirect alignment. Against OpenCLIP (same ViT-Large size), LanguageBind wins on all shared benchmarks, with the largest gaps on depth (+19.7% on NYU-D) and infrared (+5.0% on LLVIP), while also surpassing it on video (+3.3% on K400). In zero-shot text-to-video retrieval, LanguageBind beats InternVideo by 1.9% on MSR-VTT and 8.8% on MSVD, and outperforms TVTSv2 by 4.4% on MSR-VTT and 3.2% on DiDeMo, all while training on only 3M samples versus 8.5–12.8M for those baselines. For audio-language retrieval, LanguageBind exceeds VALOR by 3.7% R@1 on Clotho and AVFIC by 9.1% R@1, demonstrating that the shared multi-modal space also benefits audio. The dataset ablation (Table 3) further shows that even a 100K subset of VIDAL-10M outperforms a 380K subset of HowTo100M when used with CLIP4Clip, validating data quality over raw volume.

## Relation to Prior Work

LanguageBind directly extends the multi-modal alignment paradigm established by ImageBind (Girdhar et al., 2023), which used images as the intermediary hub for binding modalities like infrared, depth, audio, and IMU. The key conceptual departure is replacing the image-centric alignment with a language-centric one: the language encoder (initialized from OpenCLIP) is frozen, and all other modalities are trained via contrastive learning to align directly to the language embedding space. This builds on the CLIP contrastive learning framework, MAE-style patch masking for efficiency, and LoRA for parameter-efficient fine-tuning, while the VIDAL-10M dataset addresses the data gap by being the first large-scale collection with directly aligned video, infrared, depth, audio, and language pairs rather than relying on separately collected image-paired datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The language-centric alignment offers a more natural and extensible architecture for downstream tasks that are predominantly language-anchored (retrieval, classification with text prompts), and the VIDAL-10M dataset fills a critical gap as the first accessible 5-modal dataset with direct language alignment. However, the depth and infrared modalities are generated via GLPN and sRGB-TIR rather than captured with specialized sensors, introducing a potential domain gap that limits real-world deployment fidelity. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to truly novel modalities (e.g., IMU, point clouds) without the same generative-model workaround, and whether the performance margins over ImageBind persist when both methods are given equivalent training data and compute budgets.
