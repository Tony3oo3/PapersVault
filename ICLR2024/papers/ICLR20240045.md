## Problem

Contrastive vision-text models like CLIP excel at generic concepts but struggle on fine-grained entities (specific car models, bird species) that are rare or absent from pre-training data. The dominant strategy has been to scale and curate pre-training corpora, but this paper asks whether fine-grained knowledge can instead be retrieved from an external memory at inference time, avoiding the need to encode it into model parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240045/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes an RGB image or a text prompt, encodes it with a frozen CLIP or LiT backbone, and queries a large external memory (~1B image-text pairs from WebLI) via approximate k-NN search (~14 ms per query). The output is a refined multi-modal embedding: the original image embedding is augmented with text embeddings retrieved from visually similar images, and the original text embedding is augmented with image embeddings retrieved from semantically similar texts. These refined embeddings can be used directly for zero-shot classification, image-text retrieval, or open-domain entity recognition, and either branch can be independently disabled at inference depending on the task.

## Method

RECO's key insight is to decouple search from fusion: it performs uni-modal search (image-to-image or text-to-text) within the memory to retrieve the k most similar items, then performs cross-modal fusion by augmenting each original embedding with the opposite-modality embeddings of the retrieved items. The fusion is a single-layer multi-head self-attention transformer (3.16M parameters, ~2% of CLIP-B/32) that lets the original embedding attend to all retrieved elements, trained with a combined InfoNCE loss over refined-refined, original-refined, and refined-original embedding pairs. The backbone encoder remains fully frozen throughout training, which is done on Conceptual Captions 12M with retrieval from the WebLI memory. This design enables flexible inference modes (retrieval on image side only, text side only, or both) and allows the memory to be updated post-training without retraining the fusion module.

## Results

RECO improves zero-shot CLIP-B/32 accuracy by +10.9 on Stanford Cars (57.2→68.1), +10.2 on CUB-2011 (52.8→63.0), and +5.8 on Oxford Flowers (62.1→67.9), with smaller but consistent gains on ImageNet-1k (+1.1) and Places365 (+1.6). On the OVEN open-domain entity recognition benchmark, CLIP-L/14+RECO achieves 12.3% harmonic mean (11.5% seen, 13.3% unseen), a +132% relative improvement over CLIP-L/14 alone (5.3%), and outperforms fine-tuned CLIP2CLIP (11.5%) and PaLI-3B (9.3%) while using 2× and 7× fewer parameters respectively. The largest single gain is +20.3 on CUB-2011 when using the LiT-L16L backbone (54.5→74.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RECO substantially outperforms K-Lite on Cars (68.1 vs. 10.0) and ImageNet-1k (64.6 vs. 52.3), though K-Lite edges ahead on Flowers (78.6 vs. 67.9); K-Lite only reports results on three of the six classification benchmarks. Against RA-CLIP, RECO wins decisively on ImageNet-1k (64.6 vs. 53.5) and Dogs (59.7 vs. 26.1). Compared to Align (247M params), CLIP-B/32+RECO (151M params) surpasses it on Cars (68.1 vs. 78.7 is actually Align winning), CUB (63.0 vs. 38.2), and Dogs (59.7 vs. 56.3), while Align leads on Flowers (64.9 vs. 67.9 is RECO winning) and Im1k (67.6 vs. 64.6). On OVEN, RECO (435M params, zero-shot) beats fine-tuned CLIP2CLIP (860M) on harmonic mean (12.3 vs. 11.5) and PaLI-3B (3B) (12.3 vs. 9.3), and approaches PaLI-17B (17B, fine-tuned) at 12.3 vs. 16.1 while being 39× smaller. The key takeaway is that RECO's gains are concentrated on fine-grained tasks (Cars, CUB, Flowers) where all baselines struggle, and it achieves competitive or superior results to much larger models without any task-specific fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

RECO builds directly on frozen dual-encoder vision-text models (CLIP, LiT) and the broader retrieval-augmented generation paradigm established in NLP (REALM, RAG). It departs from prior knowledge-based vision-text work like K-Lite, which augments only the text modality with retrieved text, by performing cross-modal fusion: image embeddings are enriched with retrieved text and vice versa. A key conceptual departure is the use of unimodal search (image-to-image, text-to-text) rather than cross-modal search, motivated by the observation that within-modality matching is more reliable than cross-modal matching in pre-trained CLIP spaces. Unlike REACT, which uses retrieval to generate training data for fine-tuning, RECO operates entirely at inference time with a lightweight single-layer transformer fusion module (3.16M parameters, ~2% of CLIP-B/32).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RECO demonstrates that fine-grained visual knowledge need not be memorized in model parameters but can be retrieved from an external memory at inference, offering a practical path to improving zero-shot recognition without retraining large models. The ability to update the memory without retraining is a significant practical advantage for domains where knowledge evolves rapidly. Key limitations include the requirement for a very large image-text memory (WebLI, 1B pairs) and a ~25% inference-time overhead, though the authors show LAION-400M can serve as a public alternative. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond classification to generation or reasoning tasks, how it interacts with increasingly large pre-training corpora that may reduce the fine-grained gap, and whether the unimodal-search insight transfers to other dual-encoder architectures.
