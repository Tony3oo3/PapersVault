## Problem

Spectral convolutional graph neural networks have traditionally been restricted to undirected graphs because their definition relies on the graph Fourier transform, which requires a self-adjoint (symmetric) characteristic operator. On directed graphs, characteristic operators are generically non-self-adjoint, so no complete orthogonal eigenbasis exists and the standard spectral framework breaks down. Prior directed-graph spectral methods either resorted to spatial convolutions or restricted themselves to special operators that artificially preserve self-adjointness, limiting their expressivity and task adaptability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a directed weighted graph with per-node weights μ_i, asymmetric edge weights w_ij, and F-dimensional node feature vectors. For the FaberNet instantiation (node classification), the output is a per-node class label; for the Dir-ResolvNet instantiation (graph regression), node features are aggregated via a weighted L1-norm Ω(X)_j = Σ_i |X_ij|·μ_i into a single graph-level feature vector used to predict a scalar molecular property (atomization energy). The Dir-ResolvNet variant is specifically designed so that its graph-level output remains stable when edge weights within strongly connected sub-graphs are scaled by a large factor c, effectively discarding fine-grained topological detail.

## Method

The core insight is to replace the graph Fourier transform with the holomorphic functional calculus: a spectral filter g(T) is defined via the Cauchy integral g(T) = -(1/2πi)∮_Γ g(z)(T - z·Id)^{-1} dz, which is well-defined for any operator T (self-adjoint or not) as long as g is holomorphic on a domain containing σ(T). In practice, g is parameterized as a linear combination of precomputed basis functions Ψ_i(T), yielding the efficient update rule X^ℓ = ρ(α Σ Ψ_i^fwd(T)·X^{ℓ-1}·W_i^fwd + (1-α) Σ Ψ_i^bwd(T*)·X^{ℓ-1}·W_i^bwd + B^ℓ), where forward and backward filters on T and its adjoint T* ensure bidirectional information flow. Two filter banks are instantiated: FaberNet uses monomial basis Ψ_n(λ)=λ^n (Faber polynomials on a circular domain) with a zero-diagonal normalized adjacency operator for heterophilic node classification, while Dir-ResolvNet uses resolvent powers Ψ_j(λ)=(λ-y)^{-j} on the in-degree Laplacian L^in, provably suppressing high-frequency spectral content and yielding scale-invariant graph features under two-scale weight decompositions.

## Results

FaberNet achieves new state-of-the-art accuracy on all five heterophilic directed node-classification datasets: 76.71% on Squirrel, 80.33% on Chameleon, 64.43% on Arxiv-year, 75.10% on Snap-patents, and 92.24% on Roman-Empire. On the QM7 molecular regression task, Dir-ResolvNet achieves a mean absolute error of 17.12±0.63 kcal/mol, outperforming the best directed baseline (DiGCN at 39.95±6.23) by a factor of more than two. Additionally, Dir-ResolvNet's graph-level features remain stable under resolution-scale perturbations, with prediction accuracy degrading far less than baselines when inference is performed on coarsened graphs (performance gap widens to a factor of up to 240).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FaberNet outperforms the strongest directed baseline DirGNN (Rossi et al., 2023) on all five heterophilic datasets, with margins of 1.58 (Squirrel), 0.59 (Chameleon), 0.46 (Arxiv-year), 1.15 (Snap-patents), and 0.94 (Roman-Empire) percentage points. Against undirected heterophily-specialized methods, FaberNet beats FSGNN by 2.61 points on Squirrel and 2.06 on Chameleon, and surpasses Gradient Gating by 12.45 points on Roman-Empire and 5.60 on Snap-patents. The spectral directed method MagNet (Zhang et al., 2021), which relies on a self-adjoint operator to retain a graph Fourier transform, is outperformed by FaberNet by 37.70 points on Squirrel and 4.17 on Roman-Empire, suggesting that the holomorphic functional calculus enables more task-adapted non-self-adjoint operators. On QM7 regression, Dir-ResolvNet (17.12 kcal/mol MAE) outperforms DiGCN (39.95), MagNet (45.31), and DirGNN (59.01) by factors of 2.3×, 2.6×, and 3.4× respectively, attributed to its ability to discard fine-structure information via resolvent-based filters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spectral GNN lineage (Bruna et al. 2014, Defferrard et al. 2016, He et al. 2021) by replacing the graph Fourier transform with the holomorphic functional calculus from complex analysis, thereby removing the self-adjointness requirement that had confined spectral convolutions to undirected graphs. The FaberNet instantiation generalizes Chebyshev-based polynomial filters to complex domains via Faber polynomials, while Dir-ResolvNet directly extends the resolvent-based scale-invariant filters of Koke et al. (2023) from undirected to directed settings. The paper positions itself against directed spatial methods (Dir-GNN, MagNet, DiGCN) that had to resort to specialized self-adjoint operators to retain spectral structure, arguing that the holomorphic functional calculus allows use of more task-adapted non-self-adjoint operators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conceptual shift—treating learnable functions on operators as fundamental rather than the graph Fourier transform—opens spectral GNNs to any operator with a well-defined spectrum, not just self-adjoint ones, which is a meaningful broadening of the method's applicability. The proven scale-invariance (Theorem 4.2) with a 240× performance gap under resolution variation on QM7 is a strong practical guarantee for multi-scale graph learning. Open questions include scalability to very large directed graphs (the resolvent-based approach requires matrix inversions), whether the complex-parameter advantage observed on some datasets generalizes, and how the framework extends to other non-self-adjoint settings beyond directed graphs (e.g., signed or hyperbolic operators).
