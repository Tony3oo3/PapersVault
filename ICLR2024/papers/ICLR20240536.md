## Problem

Correspondence-based statistical shape models (SSMs) are essential for population-level anatomical analysis, but traditional optimization-based methods require complete, noise-free meshes or binary volumes, impose biases through predefined templates or metrics, and demand slow simultaneous optimization over the entire cohort. While point cloud deep learning has advanced in other 3D tasks, its application to anatomical SSM construction remains largely unexplored, and existing point cloud networks have not been benchmarked on this task or tested for robustness to clinical challenges such as noise, sparsity, partiality, and limited training data.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unordered point cloud of N=1024 3D coordinates (pre-aligned via ICP to remove global pose) and produces M=1024 ordered correspondence points that reconstruct the full shape surface. Each output point is a convex combination of all input points via a learned attention map, which inherently constrains outputs to the surface. From the cohort of correspondence point sets, PCA yields a statistical shape model (mean shape plus modes of variation) usable for downstream clinical tasks. The method operates on point clouds that may be noisy, sparse (as few as 128 points), or missing up to 20% of regions.

## Method

Point2SSM combines a DGCNN encoder (which learns 128-dim per-point features via edge convolution to capture local topological structure) with a transformer-like attention module built from Self-Feature Augment (SFA) blocks that predict a global attention map via self-attention. The output correspondence points are computed as the matrix product of this attention map and the input point cloud, making each output a weighted combination of all inputs and guaranteeing surface sampling. Training is fully self-supervised: the loss combines Chamfer distance (reconstruction of the full shape) with a pairwise mapping error (ME) regularization computed across all shape pairs in a mini-batch, which encourages neighborhood consistency across the cohort without requiring a reference shape. This design avoids the global bottleneck of autoencoders (which limits expressivity), the reference-shape bias of DPC, and the per-point independence of ISR, while the attention module captures population-level structural statistics in a single forward pass.

## Results

On the L4 vertebrae dataset (160 shapes), Point2SSM achieves the best surface sampling accuracy across all metrics: CD=2.61 mm, EMD=1.48 mm, and P2F=0.304 mm, while also providing the best compactness (34 PCA modes for 95% variance) among point-based methods and competitive statistical metrics (generalization=0.879, specificity=2.06). On the pancreas dataset, Point2SSM achieves CD=2.72 mm, EMD=1.42 mm, P2F=0.283 mm, and compactness of 24 modes. In a downstream tumor-size classification task, Point2SSM's PCA embeddings yield 89.28% random-forest accuracy versus 85.71% for all other methods. Training Point2SSM on the 1096-shape left atrium cohort takes under 8 hours on a single GPU, compared to over 4 days of incremental optimization for PSM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Point2SSM outperforms all five point-cloud baselines (PN-AE, DG-AE, CPAE, ISR, DPC) on surface sampling metrics (CD, EMD, P2F) across all four datasets, with the largest margins over the autoencoder methods (PN-AE, DG-AE) which sacrifice sampling accuracy for compactness. Against the optimization-based PSM baseline, Point2SSM matches or exceeds statistical compactness (e.g., 34 vs. comparable modes on L4 vertebrae) while achieving substantially better surface sampling and eliminating the 4-day optimization bottleneck. CPAE performs worst overall, with compactness of 117 modes and generalization error of 27.4 on L4 vertebrae, indicating its canonical sphere mapping fails on complex anatomical topology. ISR and DPC provide competitive sampling accuracy (since both output weighted combinations of input points) but yield less compact and less specific SSMs than Point2SSM, with DPC's reliance on a fixed reference shape introducing bias. In the downstream pancreas tumor classification task, Point2SSM achieves 89.28% accuracy versus 85.71% for PSM, ISR, and DPC, confirming that its superior correspondence translates to practical clinical utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: optimization-based statistical shape modeling (PSM/ShapeWorks, functional maps, Spharm-PDM) and point cloud deep learning (PointNet, DGCNN, transformer-based encoders). It departs from the former by replacing cohort-wide optimization with a data-driven, per-point feature learning approach, and from the latter by repurposing DGCNN edge convolution and attention-based SFA blocks (originally designed for point cloud completion) toward the correspondence task. The paper also positions itself against recent point correspondence methods (DPC, CPAE, ISR) by being global rather than pairwise, incorporating neighborhood structure rather than treating points independently, and avoiding reliance on a reference shape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By relaxing the input requirement from complete, noise-free meshes to raw point clouds, Point2SSM significantly broadens the clinical applicability of SSM, which has been a powerful but under-adopted tool in medical research. The first systematic benchmark of SOTA point cloud networks on anatomical SSM reveals that methods designed for other tasks (completion, pairwise matching) are insufficiently robust to the noise, sparsity, and data scarcity typical of clinical imaging. Open questions include handling misaligned inputs, incorporating uncertainty quantification for clinical decision-making, and whether multi-anatomy training can be made to improve performance (current results show it neither helps nor hinders).
