## Problem

Test-time adaptation methods that recalibrate batch normalization statistics assume test batches are i.i.d. with respect to true labels, an assumption that breaks down in real-world streaming scenarios (e.g., autonomous driving, video) where temporally correlated samples cause skewed BN statistics and unreliable adaptation. Existing remedies—memory banks, instance-level normalization, or fixed blending hyperparameters—introduce privacy constraints, computational overhead, or limited generality, leaving a gap for a lightweight, plug-and-play normalization layer that handles non-i.i.d. test streams without storing data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a streaming sequence of test batches (images or video frames) where consecutive batches may contain temporally correlated labels, with batch sizes ranging from 1 to 256. At each temporal step it produces per-instance normalized feature maps (B×C×L) that replace the standard BN output, which are then fed to the downstream classifier to yield class labels. The K statistics components (K=16–256 depending on scenario) persist across temporal steps, maintaining a running mixture model of the test distribution without retaining any raw input data.

## Method

UnMix-TNS decomposes the stored BN statistics of a pre-trained network into K Gaussian components, initialized around the source mean with controlled spread via a hyperparameter α. At each time step, it computes instance-wise means and variances (analogous to Instance Normalization), then derives assignment probabilities via softmax over cosine similarities between each instance's mean vector and the K component means. Per-instance normalization statistics are obtained by mixing the current instance statistics with all K components weighted by these probabilities, and features are normalized using the resulting per-instance mean and variance. The K components are then updated via momentum-weighted shifts toward the most similar instances in the batch, so that components tracking the current temporal cluster adapt quickly while others retain statistics from previously seen, less similar features—effectively simulating an i.i.d. mixture. A theoretical analysis (Appendix A.3) shows this mixture-based estimation is unbiased for the true test-domain normalization statistics, whereas standard TBN introduces a time-varying bias proportional to the non-uniformity of the component sampling distribution.

## Results

On the corruption benchmark (CIFAR10-C, CIFAR100-C, ImageNet-C at severity 5), UnMix-TNS achieves a mean online classification error of 45.6% (single domain) and 45.5% (continual domain), outperforming the next-best normalization baselines (IABN at 56.6%, RBN at 56.7%, α-BN at 56.9%) by over 11 percentage points. On DomainNet-126 natural shift, UnMix-TNS reaches 45.5% (single) and 44.5% (continual) mean error, surpassing the second-best method by 11.5–12.5%. When integrated with TTA methods, UnMix-TNS yields dramatic gains: ROID drops from 77.8% to 32.0% mean error on corruption (single domain), and TENT drops from 80.1% to 45.1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UnMix-TNS decisively outperforms all test-time normalization baselines: it reduces mean error by 34.7 pp over TBN (45.6% vs 80.3%), 11.3 pp over α-BN (45.6% vs 56.9%), 11.0 pp over IABN/NOTE (45.6% vs 56.6%), and 11.1 pp over RBN/RoTTA (45.6% vs 56.7%) on the corruption benchmark (single domain). The advantage is even more pronounced in mixed-domain settings, where RBN degrades to 83.6% and TBN to 90.0%, while UnMix-TNS holds at 58.8%. When paired with TTA methods, UnMix-TNS transforms near-failing baselines: ROID improves from 77.8% to 32.0% (−45.8 pp) and TENT from 80.1% to 45.1% (−35.0 pp) on corruption, and on DomainNet-126, ROID+UnMix-TNS reaches 26.5% mean error versus 86.6% for ROID alone. The only case where UnMix-TNS does not improve a TTA method is LAME on mixed-domain CIFAR100-C and ImageNet-C, where LAME already propagates labels within the batch and achieves very low error (28.3%) without it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time BN recalibration line (TBN, α-BN) and the memory-bank approaches (NOTE's IABN, RoTTA's RBN) by replacing the single shared statistics vector with K online-updated components, drawing conceptual inspiration from sequential clustering. It departs from NOTE/RoTTA by eliminating the need for a stored memory bank (addressing privacy constraints) and from α-BN/IABN by avoiding a fixed blending hyperparameter, instead using similarity-weighted assignment probabilities. The momentum parameter design borrows from Momentum BN (Yong et al., 2020), and the theoretical analysis formalizes the bias introduced by non-i.i.d. sampling relative to the i.i.d. TBN case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UnMix-TNS addresses a practically critical gap: real-world deployment streams (autonomous driving, video) are rarely i.i.d., yet most TTA methods silently assume they are, leading to near-total failure. The method's generality (drop-in BN replacement, 0.15ms overhead, works at batch size 1) makes it immediately applicable to existing TTA pipelines, and the newly released corrupted video datasets (ImageNet-VID-C, LaSOT-C) fill a benchmarking gap for video-level non-i.i.d. adaptation. Open questions include handling outlier/diverse instances within a batch, extending to segmentation models, and determining the optimal number of components K per scenario, which the authors acknowledge remains dataset-dependent.
