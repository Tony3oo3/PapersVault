## Problem

Existing regression-based DAG learning methods rely on lasso-type score functions whose sparsity penalty must be retuned whenever the unknown exogenous noise variance changes across problem instances, and they implicitly assume homoscedasticity (equal noise variances across nodes). This paper is the first to bring concomitant scale estimation from sparse linear regression into the linear DAG learning setting, decoupling the sparsity parameter from noise levels and enabling principled handling of heteroscedastic noise profiles.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a d×n observational data matrix X of i.i.d. samples drawn from a linear SEM (X = W†X + Z), where d is the number of variables and n the sample size. It outputs a d×d DAG adjacency matrix W (with zero diagonal) and, simultaneously, either a single noise standard deviation σ (CoLiDE-EV) or a vector of d per-node noise standard deviations Σ (CoLiDE-NV). The noise estimates are obtained in closed form at each BCD iteration, requiring no separate post-hoc computation.

## Method

CoLiDE replaces the standard LS + l1 score with a concomitant lasso formulation: for the equal-variance case, the objective is (1/2nσ)‖X − W†X‖²_F + dσ/2 + λ‖W‖₁, and for the non-equal-variance case it generalizes to a weighted LS with a diagonal noise matrix Σ. The acyclicity constraint is enforced via the log-determinant function H_ldet(W, s) = d log(s) − log det(sI − W∘W), dualized as a regularizer in a sequential μ_k → 0 schedule (following DAGMA). Optimization proceeds by inexact block coordinate descent: W is updated with a single ADAM step on the smooth part of the objective, while σ (or Σ) is updated in closed form via the residual covariance trace. This yields a per-iteration cost of O(d³), on par with state-of-the-art continuous relaxation methods, while the sparsity parameter λ is now independent of the noise scale.

## Results

On 200-node ER4 graphs with homoscedastic Gaussian noise (variance=1.0), CoLiDE-EV achieves the lowest SHD of 87.3±33.7, outperforming DAGMA (100.1±41.8) and GOLEM (468.6±144.0), with TPR of 0.95 and FDR of 0.06. When noise variance increases to 5.0, CoLiDE-EV's advantage widens substantially (SHD 105.6 vs. DAGMA's 194.4), demonstrating its noise-adaptive property without hyperparameter retuning. In the heteroscedastic setting, CoLiDE-NV achieves the best SHD of 390.7±35.6, surpassing SortNRegress (397.8) and DAGMA (470.2). On the real-world Sachs dataset (11 nodes, 17 edges), CoLiDE-NV attains the lowest SHD of 12 and highest TPR of 0.35 among all tested methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoLiDE-EV decisively outperforms GOLEM across all metrics in the homoscedastic setting, reducing SHD from 468.6 to 87.3 (σ²=1) and from 336.6 to 105.6 (σ²=5), with the gap widening as noise increases—confirming that CoLiDE's concomitant noise estimation eliminates the need for penalty retuning. Against DAGMA (the closest continuous-optimization competitor), CoLiDE-EV achieves lower SHD (87.3 vs. 100.1) and FDR (0.06 vs. 0.07) at σ²=1, and a much larger margin at σ²=5 (105.6 vs. 194.4 SHD). In the heteroscedastic setting, CoLiDE-NV leads on SHD (390.7) but SortNRegress is competitive on FDR (0.20 vs. 0.25) and SID (22560 vs. 22734), while CoLiDE-NV still dominates on TPR (0.68 vs. 0.62). On the Sachs dataset, CoLiDE-NV achieves the best SHD (12) and TPR (0.35), edging out SortNRegress and CoLiDE-EV (both SHD=13) and clearly surpassing DAGMA (SHD=16, TPR=0.05), GOLEM-NV (SHD=15), DAGuerreotype (SHD=14), and GES (SHD=13, TPR=0.23). CoLiDE consistently exhibits lower standard deviations than all baselines across metrics, indicating greater estimation stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoLiDE sits at the intersection of two research threads: continuous-relaxation DAG learning (NOTEARS, DAGMA, GOLEM) and concomitant scale estimation in sparse linear regression (smoothed concomitant lasso, generalized concomitant multi-task lasso). It adopts DAGMA's log-determinant acyclicity function and sequential μ_k schedule as its optimization backbone, but replaces the standard lasso-type score with a jointly convex objective that simultaneously estimates the DAG adjacency matrix and the exogenous noise scale(s). The key conceptual departure is that the sparsity penalty λ becomes independent of the unknown noise level, a property inherited from the square-root lasso / concomitant lasso literature but never previously exploited in the DAG learning context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical benefit of decoupling λ from noise variance is significant for practitioners who must apply DAG learners across problem instances with unknown and varying noise profiles, eliminating a tedious and error-prone retuning step. The heteroscedastic variant (CoLiDE-NV) addresses a known identifiability limitation of prior methods that implicitly assume equal noise variances, and the O(d³) per-iteration cost means no computational penalty is incurred. Open questions include extending the framework to nonlinear SEMs (e.g., neural-network-parameterized), interventional data, and fully online/stochastic settings; the authors also note sensitivity to data standardization as a limitation relative to greedy methods like GES.
