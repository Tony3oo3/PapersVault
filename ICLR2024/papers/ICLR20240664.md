## Problem

Existing multi-task linear representation learning methods (e.g., FedRep) assume isotropic and iid covariates, and when these assumptions are violated, the representation gradient incurs biases that do not scale down with the number of tasks, causing sample complexity to be bottlenecked by single-task data size. The paper demonstrates that vanilla alternating minimization-descent fails catastrophically even for iid but mildly non-isotropic data, and provides a unified framework for linear operator recovery from non-iid, non-isotropic covariates that encompasses applications like linear system identification and imitation learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes T task-specific trajectories of length N, where each trajectory consists of covariate vectors x_i^(t) ∈ R^{d_x} and noisy vector observations y_i^(t) = M_★^(t) x_i^(t) + w_i^(t) ∈ R^{d_y}. Covariates may be sequentially dependent (β-mixing processes from dynamical systems) and have task-specific non-identity covariance matrices. The output is a shared representation matrix Φ ∈ R^{r×d_x} with orthonormal rows (r ≤ d_x) and task-specific weight matrices F^(t) ∈ R^{d_y×r}, such that M_★^(t) = F_★^(t) Φ_★. The representation is normalized to orthonormal rows to resolve the non-identifiability of the factorization.

## Method

The paper introduces DFW (De-bias & Feature-Whiten), an adaptation of the alternating minimization-descent (AMD) scheme from Collins et al. (2021) and Nayer & Vaswani (2022). Two algorithmic modifications address the identified failure modes: (1) De-biasing splits each task's data into two disjoint trajectory subsets—one for computing least-squares weights F^(t) and another for computing the representation gradient—eliminating the correlated bias that arises when both are computed on the same batch; (2) Feature-whitening pre-conditions each task's gradient estimate by its inverse sample covariance matrix (Σ̂_x^(t))^{-1}, removing the nuisance term from non-identity second moments. Under β-mixing and subgaussian covariate assumptions, DFW achieves linear convergence with a noise floor scaling as Õ(σ_avg √((d_x + log(T/δ))/(NT))), where the variance term scales jointly in total data size NT rather than being bottlenecked by per-task data, yielding approximate ERM generalization bounds on fixed datasets.

## Results

The main theoretical guarantee (Theorem 3.1) establishes that one DFW iteration achieves dist(Φ̂₊, Φ⋆) ≤ (1 − ηλ_min^F/2)^{1/2} · dist(Φ̂, Φ⋆) + Õ(σ_avg · √((d_x + log(T/δ))/(NT))), where the noise term scales jointly in the number of tasks T and data per task N. Corollary 3.1 shows that after K iterations, the representation error matches oracle ERM: dist(Φ̂_K, Φ⋆)² ≤ Õ(C(ρ) · max_t σ_w^{(t)2}(d_x·r + log(1/δ))/(NT)). Numerically, in the linear regression experiment (d_x=d_y=50, r=7, N=100), DFW with T=25 converges to near-optimality while vanilla AMD (FedRep) plateaus at a highly suboptimal bias; in the neural-net AMD comparison, DFW reaches optimality in roughly two orders of magnitude fewer iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is FedRep (Collins et al., 2021), the vanilla alternating minimization-descent scheme that assumes isotropic, noise-free covariates. In the linear regression experiment with mildly non-isotropic iid data (condition number of Σ_x only slightly above 1), FedRep with T=25 tasks plateaus at a highly suboptimal subspace distance and never recovers the true representation, while DFW with the same T=25 converges to near-optimality with variance that decreases as more tasks are added. In the neural-network representation experiment, AMD on a ReLU network (64 hidden units) plateaus for many iterations before eventually finding a non-linear parameterization that allows descent, taking approximately 100× more iterations than DFW on a linear representation to reach the same validation loss. In the linear system identification experiment (d_x=25, d_u=2, r=6, N=100, sequentially dependent covariates), DFW continues to benefit from multi-task data and converges to a shared representation, whereas FedRep remains suboptimal due to the non-iid, non-isotropic nature of the trajectory data. The paper thus demonstrates that the de-biasing and feature-whitening modifications are not merely theoretical niceties but are essential for any gradient-based representation learning method to exploit the full source dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the alternating minimization-descent (AMD) framework independently proposed by Collins et al. (2021, FedRep) and Nayer and Vaswani (2022, AltGD-Min) for multi-task linear representation learning. The key departure is that all prior AMD analyses assume isotropic, iid covariates and often scalar measurements, whereas this paper handles non-iid (β-mixing) and non-isotropic covariates in the vector measurement setting. The authors identify that under these relaxed assumptions, the vanilla AMD representation gradient acquires bias terms that do not vanish with the number of tasks, and introduce two targeted modifications—de-biasing via data splitting and feature-whitening via inverse covariance pre-conditioning—to restore favorable joint scaling in total data size.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: practitioners using FedRep-style algorithms on real-world data (which is rarely perfectly isotropic or iid) may be silently bottlenecked by single-task data size rather than benefiting from the full multi-task dataset. The DFW fix is lightweight and drop-in, making it immediately applicable to federated learning, system identification, and imitation learning pipelines. Open questions include whether good initialization is necessary in the non-isotropic regime, whether the convergence rate can be further tightened (e.g., via ℓ²-regularized weights), and whether the framework extends to nonlinear representations while preserving joint scaling in tasks and data.
