## Problem

Existing remedies for natural overfitting (NO), robust overfitting (RO), and catastrophic overfitting (CO) are designed separately for each type, typically targeting either natural or adversarial patterns in isolation, with limited transferability across paradigms. This paper asks whether a single shared mechanism underlies all three forms of overfitting and whether a unified intervention can mitigate them simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB image classification datasets (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, SVHN at 32×32, Tiny-ImageNet at 64×64) with paired class labels. A key operational detail is that during adversarial training, DOM identifies over-memorization samples exclusively via the natural training loss of each sample, even though the model only ever receives adversarial perturbations during optimization. The output is a trained classifier (PreactResNet-18, WideResNet-34, or ViT-small) with reduced generalization gap and improved adversarial robustness at the final checkpoint.

## Method

The paper identifies a shared behaviour termed over-memorization: DNNs suddenly become high-confidence on certain training patterns and retain a persistent memory for them (evidenced by barely increasing loss after removal), which degrades generalization. A critical empirical finding is that over-memorization of adversarial patterns co-occurs with high-confidence prediction of the corresponding natural patterns (~90% overlap in top-confidence groups), enabling reliable identification of over-memorization samples by examining only natural training loss regardless of the training paradigm. The proposed Distraction Over-Memorization (DOM) framework uses a fixed loss threshold T after a warm-up epoch K to flag high-confidence natural patterns, then applies one of two interventions: DOM_RE removes these patterns from the training batch (relying on persistent memory to prevent relearning), or DOM_DA applies iterative data augmentation (AugMix or RandAugment) to these patterns until their loss exceeds T, thereby weakening the model's over-reliance. The framework is paradigm-agnostic, requiring only the natural loss as a signal, and is validated across natural training, multi-step PGD adversarial training, and single-step RS-FGSM adversarial training on multiple datasets and architectures.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with PreactResNet-18, DOM_DA (with RandAugment) achieves 4.13% test error at the best checkpoint and 4.24% at the last checkpoint, reducing the generalization gap from -0.14 (baseline) to -0.11. On WideResNet-34, DOM_DA (with RandAugment) reaches 2.98% best and 3.20% last test error on CIFAR-10, outperforming both AUGMIX (3.43%/3.69%) and RandAugment alone (3.20%/3.44%). In multi-step adversarial training (PGD-20, last checkpoint), DOM_DA (with RandAugment) achieves 50.39% robust accuracy on CIFAR-10 versus 45.16% for the baseline and 47.84% for RandAugment alone. For catastrophic overfitting (single-step AT, PGD-20), DOM_DA (with RandAugment) recovers 50.10% robust accuracy on CIFAR-10 where the baseline and all standard augmentation baselines collapse to 0.00%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DOM_DA (with RandAugment) consistently outperforms both AUGMIX and RandAugment applied uniformly across all training patterns. On CIFAR-10 with WideResNet-34, DOM_DA (RandAugment) achieves 2.98% best test error versus 3.43% for AUGMIX and 3.20% for RandAugment alone, while also reducing the generalization gap from −0.20 (AUGMIX) and −0.24 (RandAugment) to −0.22. In multi-step adversarial training on CIFAR-10 (PGD-20, last checkpoint), DOM_DA (RandAugment) reaches 50.39% robust accuracy, surpassing AUGMIX (47.71%) and RandAugment (47.84%) by approximately 2.5–2.7 percentage points. For catastrophic overfitting (single-step AT), DOM_DA (RandAugment) is the only method that prevents the robustness collapse, achieving 50.10% PGD-20 accuracy on CIFAR-10 where AUGMIX, RandAugment, and the baseline all drop to 0.00%. DOM_RE (removal) also improves over the baseline in all settings but is weaker than DOM_DA under Auto Attack, suggesting that removing high-confidence patterns discards some useful information that iterative augmentation preserves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new conceptual ground by proposing a unified lens—over-memorization—across three previously siloed overfitting phenomena (natural, robust, and catastrophic overfitting), each of which prior work (Rice et al. 2020; Wong et al. 2019; Andriushchenko & Flammarion 2020) addressed with paradigm-specific remedies. Methodologically, it repurposes well-known tools (sample removal from noisy-label literature, AugMix/RandAugment) but applies them selectively to high-confidence natural patterns identified via a loss threshold, rather than uniformly. The key departure from prior work is the empirical discovery that natural and adversarial patterns for the same sample share a memory tendency (~90% overlap in high-confidence rankings), enabling a single detection signal (natural loss) to govern interventions across all training paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified over-memorization framing offers a principled, paradigm-agnostic diagnostic for overfitting that practitioners can apply without redesigning their training pipeline for each overfitting type. However, the practical gains are modest and inconsistent: DOM_RE degrades Auto Attack robustness, DOM_DA with AugMix fails to prevent CO on CIFAR-100, and the method's effectiveness is explicitly bounded by the quality of the underlying augmentation. A detailed theoretical account of why over-memorization impairs generalization remains open, and the fixed-threshold approach (though an adaptive variant is shown) still requires per-dataset tuning, limiting plug-and-play applicability.
