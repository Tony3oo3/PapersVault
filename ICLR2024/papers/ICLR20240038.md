## Problem

Diffusion models achieve state-of-the-art image generation but cannot efficiently compute the exact constrained posterior needed for tasks like inpainting, forcing existing methods to approximate it with high bias that produces semantically incoherent results. This paper asks the converse question: what is the benefit of a tractable model that can exactly and efficiently compute the constrained posterior, and can such a model effectively steer a diffusion model's denoising process?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240038/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For inpainting, the method takes a 256×256 RGB image with a binary mask (revealing only 5–20% of pixels in the challenging evaluation settings) and produces a complete inpainted image. For the semantic fusion extension, it takes multiple reference images each paired with a mask and generates a new image that semantically aligns with the unmasked regions of all references. Internally, images are encoded into a 16×16 VQ-GAN latent grid (1024–16384 categories per variable), where the PC operates, and decoded back to pixel space for the final output.

## Method

Tiramisu trains a Probabilistic Circuit (PC) on VQ-GAN latent representations of noise-free images, then at each early denoising step computes the exact constrained posterior p_TPM(x̃₀|x_t, x₀ᵏ) via a forward-backward pass over the PC in O(|p|) time, treating the diffusion noise and inpainting mask as independent soft-evidence constraints. This TPM distribution is combined with the diffusion model's estimate p_DM(x̃₀|x_t, x₀ᵏ) (generated by CoPaint) through a weighted geometric mean of per-variable logits, p ∝ p_DM^α · p_TPM^(1−α), with α following an exponential decay schedule. The PC is initialized via latent variable distillation from a deep generative model and fine-tuned with EM, using a parameter-tied PD structure that exploits spatial invariance. TPM guidance is applied only during the first ~20% of denoising steps (controlled by a cutoff t_cut), since the PC primarily shapes global semantics while the diffusion model refines fine-grained details later, keeping the additional computational overhead to roughly 10%.

## Results

On the average LPIPS metric across 21 dataset-mask configurations (3 datasets × 7 masks), Tiramisu achieves 0.421, outperforming the closest baseline DPS (0.434) and its own base method CoPaint (0.427). Tiramisu attains the best LPIPS in 14 out of 21 settings and beats CoPaint in 18 out of 21, with the largest gains on challenging large-hole masks (e.g., Expand1 on CelebA-HQ: 0.454 vs. 0.468 for CoPaint, 0.504 for RePaint). The TPM guidance adds only ~10% computational overhead (≈10 s extra on CelebA-HQ Expand1) when applied to the first ~20% of denoising steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Tiramisu is built on top of CoPaint and consistently improves upon it, winning 18 out of 21 settings (losing only on CelebA-HQ Left, CelebA-HQ Top, and LSUN-Bedroom Wide by margins of ≤0.004 LPIPS). Against the closest competitor DPS (avg. 0.434), Tiramisu (avg. 0.421) wins 19 out of 21 settings, with the largest gaps on hard masks such as Expand1 on CelebA-HQ (0.454 vs. 0.466) and V-strip on ImageNet (0.646 vs. 0.637, where DPS actually edges ahead). Tiramisu decisively outperforms RePaint (avg. 0.459), DDNM (0.532), DDRM (0.531), and Resampling (0.514) across all 21 settings, with margins of 0.03–0.11 LPIPS depending on mask difficulty. The advantage is most pronounced on large-hole masks (Expand1, Expand2, V-strip, H-strip) where only 5–20% of pixels are visible, confirming that the TPM's semantic guidance is most valuable when the diffusion model has the least pixel-level information to work with.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in diffusion-based inpainting (CoPaint, RePaint, DDNM, DDRM, DPS), where the constrained posterior p(x̃₀|x_t, x₀ᵏ) is approximated via gradient-based relaxation, resampling, or hard pixel replacement. Instead, Tiramisu introduces a fundamentally different mechanism: it trains a Probabilistic Circuit (a Tractable Probabilistic Model) on the image distribution and uses it to compute the constrained posterior exactly, then combines this with the diffusion model's estimate via a weighted geometric mean. The PC component builds directly on recent scaling work (latent variable distillation, PD-structured circuits) and is applied in a VQ-GAN latent space to handle high-resolution images, while the diffusion backbone is CoPaint. The paper is the first to demonstrate TPMs steering the generation of natural, high-resolution images, bridging the tractable-probabilistic-modeling literature with modern generative AI.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core conceptual contribution—that a tractable model can provide exact, efficient guidance to an intractable one, improving output quality with only ~10% overhead—opens a new research direction at the intersection of probabilistic reasoning and deep generative models. However, practical limitations temper the impact: the PC operates in a VQ-GAN latent space (losing full pixel-level tractability), the latent-space soft-evidence computation relies on Monte Carlo approximation, and the PC's expressiveness remains below what would be needed for direct pixel-space modeling of 256×256 images. The demonstrated semantic fusion task suggests broader applicability to controlled generation, but the empirical gains over strong baselines like CoPaint are modest (winning 18/21 settings by small LPIPS margins), and the method requires training and maintaining a separate PC model. Open questions include whether PCs can scale to pixel-space modeling of high-resolution images and whether this tractable-steering paradigm generalizes to other modalities or constraint types.
