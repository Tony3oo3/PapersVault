## Problem

When MLLMs are instruction-tuned on multiple tasks and modalities simultaneously, a severe 'tug-of-war' phenomenon causes task-specific optimizations to cancel each other out, degrading performance across all downstream tasks. This interference is especially acute when combining 2D image and 3D point cloud modalities, yet prior work has largely overlooked this issue in the MLLM setting.

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images (resized to 224×224, encoded into 256 CLIP ViT-L/14 patches), 3D point clouds (1024 points sampled per RoI detected by FCAF3D, with ~50 RoIs per scene filtered by confidence threshold τ=0.3), and a text instruction in instruction-following dialogue format. Outputs are free-form text sequences that encode task-specific answers: normalized bounding box coordinates [x1,y1,x2,y2] for detection, class labels for classification, natural-language captions, or VQA responses. In the joint 2D+3D setup, 3D scene features (16 query vectors) are zero-padded to 256 dimensions with masks to match the image patch count.

## Method

The core contribution is LoRA-MoE, which replaces the single LoRA adapter in each projection layer of a Vicuna-13B LLM with N independent LoRA experts (rank 32) and an instance-based gate that routes each input to a sparse subset of experts (top-2) based on the text question rather than individual tokens. This instance-level routing assigns task-specific knowledge to dedicated experts, preventing the gradient interference that a shared LoRA suffers. For 3D input, the Object-As-Scene encoder first localizes candidate RoIs with FCAF3D, extracts language- and image-aligned instance features via a ULIP-pretrained Point-Bert, then aggregates them into a scene-level representation using 16 learnable queries with multi-head cross-attention and 3D bounding-box positional embeddings. Training uses standard next-token prediction over instruction-following dialogues (Adam, lr=5×10⁻⁴, batch 64, 4 epochs) with no auxiliary load-balancing loss, which the authors show is incompatible with instance-based gating. The framework scales from 4 experts (2D-only) to 6 experts (joint 2D+3D), adding only ~1.6% trainable parameters over the baseline.

## Results

Across all three modality setups (2D image, 3D point cloud, and joint 2D+3D), Octavius with LoRA-MoE achieves approximately 20% average improvement over its non-MoE counterpart. On 2D tasks (Table 1), the MoE variant boosts PASCAL VOC detection Recall@0.5 from 7.61 to 39.04 and Flickr30K CIDEr from 0.21 to 5.66. On 3D tasks (Table 2), ScanQA CIDEr rises from 168.21 to 167.31 (comparable) while ShapeNet classification accuracy improves from 19.75% to 24.85%. In the joint 2D+3D setup (Table 3), the overall average improvement reaches 21.40%, with detection Recall@0.5 jumping from 2.64 to 34.30 and 3D captioning CIDEr from 19.76 to 33.29.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LAMM (the primary 2D baseline), Octavius with LoRA-MoE dramatically outperforms on detection (39.04 vs. 7.61 Recall@0.5, a 5× gain) and captioning (5.66 vs. 0.21 CIDEr), while also improving VQA accuracy from 40.31 to 46.95; the overall average improvement is 20.89%. Against LLaVA-LoRA, Octavius wins on VQA (55.58 vs. 52.35) and classification (41.00 vs. 2.89 Acc@1) but trails on captioning CIDEr (23.08 vs. 30.75), yielding a net 18.36% average gain. On 3D tasks, Octavius substantially outperforms 3D-LLM (Flamingo) on ScanQA VQA (44.24 vs. 30.30 BLEU-1; 167.31 vs. 59.20 CIDEr), while achieving comparable Scan2Cap captioning performance (35.94 vs. 36.10† BLEU-1, noting the test sets differ). In the joint 2D+3D multimodal setup, the MoE variant recovers most of the severe degradation caused by cross-modal interference, achieving 21.40% average improvement and approaching single-modality performance on several tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two well-established lines of work—LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning (Hu et al., 2021) and Mixture-of-Experts routing (Shazeer et al., 2017)—into a novel LoRA-MoE decoder specifically designed for multimodal instruction tuning. It departs from conventional MoE in two key ways: replacing token-level gating with an instance-level gate that routes based on the input instruction/question, and training only LoRA expert modules rather than full expert networks. The point cloud encoder (Object-As-Scene) draws on ULIP-style contrastive pre-training (Xue et al., 2023) and FCAF3D detection, while the overall MLLM pipeline follows LAMM (Yin et al., 2023). The paper positions itself as one of the first to explicitly address task interference in MLLMs via MoE, distinguishing from Adamix (Wang et al., 2022a) which uses random expert selection rather than learned routing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and quantifies a previously underexplored failure mode in MLLMs—severe cross-task and cross-modality interference during joint instruction tuning—providing empirical evidence that simply mixing datasets degrades performance dramatically (e.g., 2D captioning CIDEr drops from 5.66 to 0.04 when 3D data is added). The LoRA-MoE solution offers a practical, extensible mechanism to mitigate this with minimal additional parameters, making it relevant for embodied AI and multi-modal agent systems. However, significant limitations remain: performance still lags behind single-modality training, standard load-balancing losses are incompatible with instance-based gating, and the approach has only been validated on a single LLM backbone (Vicuna-13B) with a limited set of tasks. Open questions include whether the framework scales gracefully to many more modalities and tasks, and how to design effective load-balancing strategies for instance-level routing.
