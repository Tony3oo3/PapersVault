## Problem

Existing multi-output Gaussian Process (MOGP) methods, particularly Linear Model of Coregionalization (LMC) variants, suffer from three key limitations: (i) prohibitive computational and storage costs from the SN×SN correlation matrix, (ii) model mismatch when the underlying likelihood deviates from Gaussian, and (iii) symmetric, covariance-based output correlations that cannot capture asymmetric conditional dependence or provide interpretable graph structures. Moreover, LMC-based models can underperform isolated single-output GPs when sufficient training data exists per output.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-output training data X ∈ R^{N×d} and Y ∈ R^{NS} (S outputs, N samples each) and a test location x_* ∈ R^d, producing per-output predictive means and variances along with learned attention coefficients α_{i,j} that define a directed acyclic graph over the S outputs. The kernel hyperparameters Θ are shared across all outputs (enabling information exchange), while each output maintains its own N×N aggregated kernel k_G^{(i)}(X,X) rather than a joint SN×SN matrix. The model also supports heterotopic data (different input locations per output) and, in the TGMOGP variant, accepts additional flow parameters Φ_i for non-Gaussian likelihoods.

## Method

GMOGP models each output as a single-output GP whose prior mean is a linear combination of its parent outputs weighted by attention coefficients α_{i,j}, and whose covariance aggregates the parent kernels scaled by α_{i,j}² plus its own kernel. The parent set (graph structure) is learned via a modified attention mechanism using an inner-product scoring function with LeakyReLU nonlinearity and per-pair parameters, enabling dynamic (input-dependent) attention and asymmetric dependence (α_{i,j} ≠ α_{ji}); parents with α_{i,j} ≈ 0 are pruned to yield a sparse DAG. Kernel hyperparameters are optimized as a multi-objective problem F(Θ) = [L^(1)(Θ), …, L^(S)(Θ)]^T, solved via the weighted sum method to obtain Pareto optimal solutions, avoiding the high-dimensional joint optimization of classic MOGPs. For non-Gaussian data, the TGMOGP composes K invertible marginal flows (e.g., Sinh-Archsinh) on each target node and trains via sparse variational inference (100 inducing points) by minimizing the negative ELBO. The separable per-output structure (each with its own N×N kernel) enables distributed learning across computing units without block-diagonal approximations or prediction fusion.

## Results

On the synthetic 5-output benchmark (1800 train / 600 test points), TGMOGP achieves the best average RMSE of 0.5343±0.0023 and test NLL of −0.6354±0.0023, outperforming the strongest baseline CMOGP (RMSE 0.5539, NLL 0.4689) and FICM (RMSE 0.5544, NLL 0.4798). On real-world datasets, GMOGP and TGMOGP achieve the lowest RMSE across all six benchmarks (e.g., EEG: 0.082 vs. FICM 0.147; SARCOS₁: 0.558 vs. FICM 0.792; KUKA: 0.02 vs. FICM 0.03). TGMOGP₁₀₀ (100 inducing points) also reduces per-iteration time on SARCOS₂ to 3.43 s (distributed) versus 419.3 s for FICM and >2400 s for SGPRN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GMOGP/TGMOGP consistently outperform all five baselines on every dataset where they are compared. Against V-LMC₁₀₀ (de Wolff et al., 2021), the proposed methods achieve substantially lower RMSE (e.g., 0.082 vs. 0.207 on EEG; 0.558 vs. 1.063 on SARCOS₁) while also being faster per iteration. Against FICM (Bonilla et al., 2007), the gains are most pronounced on EEG (0.082 vs. 0.147, a 44% reduction) and SARCOS₁ (0.558 vs. 0.792, a 30% reduction). On the synthetic benchmark, GPRN (Wilson et al., 2012) and CMOGP (Alvarez & Lawrence, 2011) both achieve RMSE around 0.55–0.58, whereas TGMOGP reaches 0.5343 with a markedly lower NLL (−0.6354 vs. 0.4689–0.5787), indicating better-calibrated uncertainty. SGPRN (Li et al., 2021) is the strongest scalable baseline on real data but is still outperformed by TGMOGP on JURA (0.382 vs. 0.438), ECG (0.217 vs. 0.232), and SARCOS₁ (0.558 vs. 0.844), while requiring >2400 s per iteration on SARCOS₂ compared to 3.43 s for distributed TGMOGP.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate threads: (1) the Linear Model of Coregionalization (LMC) and its variants (FICM, GPRN, CMOGP) for multi-output GP regression, (2) attention-based graph construction from the GAT literature (Veličković et al., 2018), and (3) transformed/normalizing-flow GPs (Rios & Tobar, 2019; Maroñas et al., 2021). The key conceptual departure is replacing the LMC's symmetric covariance over shared latent GPs with a directed acyclic graph whose edges are learned via an asymmetric attention mechanism, reducing the per-output covariance from SN×SN to N×N. The multi-objective (Pareto) formulation for shared kernel hyperparameters and the extension of marginal flows to the graphical multi-output setting are novel combinations not present in any single prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GMOGP framework offers a practical path to scalable multi-output GP regression with interpretable output-dependence structure, addressing the two main barriers to MOGP adoption: computational cost and model mismatch for non-Gaussian data. The learned attention coefficients provide a natural graphical representation that aligns with known correlations (e.g., matching Pearson coefficients on SARCOS), which is valuable for explainability. Open questions include whether the static attention mechanism can capture input-dependent (dynamic) dependence, how the method scales beyond the tested 7-output setting, and whether the Pareto-optimal hyperparameter selection generalizes to heterogeneous or imbalanced output distributions. The paper also acknowledges that interpretation of learned coefficients in the transformed (TGMOGP) setting remains an open XAI problem.
