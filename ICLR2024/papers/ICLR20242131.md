## Problem

Existing evaluations of LLMs' ethical values rely on static discriminative benchmarks (moral judgement, questionnaires) that suffer from data contamination and measure only whether models *know* the correct moral answer rather than whether their *behaviors* conform to values in realistic scenarios. This paper asks: how can we dynamically and generatively probe the intrinsic ethical values of LLMs by eliciting actual value violations in their generated text, and how can we then align those values in-context?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242131/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a value principle (e.g., 'It's wrong to break your word'), an initial scenario prompt, and a target LLM (open-source or black-box) as inputs, and produces (1) a dynamically optimized provocative prompt tailored to that specific model's vulnerabilities, (2) a prompt-specific value instruction for alignment, and (3) three conformity metrics (EVR, APV, MVP) computed via a trained classifier. Prompts are constrained to ≤250 tokens and completions to ≤100 tokens; the classifier p_ω(v|y) is a RoBERTa-large or LLaMA-2-7B model achieving ~90% F1 on value-violation detection.

## Method

DeNEVIL frames prompt generation as a Variational EM problem: the E-step samples K completions that violate the target value (via direct instruction for instruction-tuned LLMs, or classifier-guided controllable decoding à la FUDGE for vanilla LLMs), and the M-step refines the prompt via Simulated Annealing to maximize the probability of inducing those violating completions; for black-box models where log-probabilities are unavailable, an Energy-based Model is trained on the LLM's outputs to approximate them. VILMO then performs in-context alignment by treating value-instruction generation as a generative model-based black-box optimization problem: it iteratively collects (prompt, instruction, conformity-score) triples, augments the empirical distribution, and fine-tunes a Vicuna-13B (LoRA) to minimize KL divergence toward high-conformity instructions, outperforming Self-Critique, APE, and InstructZero baselines on both value conformity and generation quality.

## Results

On the MoralPrompt benchmark (2,397 prompts, 522 value principles across 5 moral foundations), VILMO achieves the best value conformity among all in-context alignment methods: EVR=89.45, MVP=85.84, and APV=57.58, compared to the unaligned ChatGPT baseline (EVR=96.18, MVP=93.58, APV=70.07). This represents a 12.5-point reduction in APV over the baseline. However, VILMO incurs a trade-off in generation quality, with the highest Self-BLEU (74.29, indicating reduced diversity) and second-worst PPL (3.04) among all methods. Across 27 LLMs benchmarked, the paper finds that most models are substantially misaligned with ethical values, with even aligned models like LLaMA2-Chat-70B showing 77.73 APV.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VILMO outperforms all three in-context alignment baselines on every violation metric. Against Self-Critique (Saunders et al., 2022), VILMO reduces APV from 58.28 to 57.58 and EVR from 93.67 to 89.45, with a more substantial gap on MVP (85.84 vs. 90.06). Against InstructZero (Chen et al., 2023a), VILMO achieves a 6.5-point APV improvement (57.58 vs. 64.08) and 4.6-point EVR improvement, which the authors attribute to InstructZero's limited learnable parameters struggling with the task's complexity on only 432 training prompts. Against APE (Zhou et al., 2022), VILMO leads by 2.4 points on APV (57.58 vs. 59.98) and 2.1 points on EVR (89.45 vs. 91.54). The trade-off is that VILMO produces the least diverse completions (SB=74.29 vs. 70.96 for baseline ChatGPT) and slightly worse fluency (PPL=3.04 vs. 2.56), consistent with the paper's observation that overemphasizing conformity hurts generation quality. In the broader evaluation comparison (Fig. 3a), the paper also shows that its generative MoralPrompt evaluation yields substantially lower conformity scores than discriminative Moral Judgement or Moral Questionnaire methods, validating that generative evaluation better captures true value alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of static discriminative evaluation (moral judgement, moral questionnaires) for assessing LLM ethics, arguing that these methods measure 'knowing' rather than 'doing' and are vulnerable to data contamination. DeNEVIL repurposes the EM algorithm (Neal & Hinton, 1998) as an iterative prompt-refinement loop, borrowing controllable decoding from FUDGE (Yang & Klein, 2021) for vanilla LLMs and energy-based models (Nie et al., 2021) for black-box probability estimation. VILMO extends generative model-based black-box optimization (Snoek et al., 2015) to the specific problem of learning prompt-wise value instructions, positioning itself as a lightweight alternative to RLHF and SFT-based alignment. The work is situated within Moral Foundations Theory from social psychology, extending its application from human moral psychology to LLM behavior assessment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that generative evaluation reveals substantially more value violations than discriminative methods challenges the assumption that high moral-judgement scores imply genuine value alignment, suggesting current alignment techniques may primarily teach LLMs to produce 'correct-sounding' answers rather than conform to values in generation. The MoralPrompt dataset and DeNEVIL framework provide a reusable, dynamic evaluation tool applicable to both black-box and open-source models, addressing the data-contamination problem that plagues static benchmarks. Key limitations include reliance on a single value theory (MFT), English-only evaluation, single-principle focus (ignoring moral dilemmas), and the acknowledged risk that the vulnerability-exploitation methodology could be repurposed for adversarial attacks. Open questions include integrating multiple moral frameworks, multilingual evaluation, and whether the knowing-doing gap persists as models scale.
