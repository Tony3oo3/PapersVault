## Problem

Zeroth-order (ZO) optimization, which estimates gradients via finite differences of function values, has been limited to small-scale problems such as single-input adversarial attacks. No prior work has demonstrated that ZO optimization can train deep neural networks from scratch without significant performance degradation compared to first-order (FO) training, as the bias of finite-difference gradient estimates grows with model dimensionality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241956/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For image classification, the method consumes standard 32×32 RGB images (CIFAR-10) and produces 10-class labels; for adversarial defense, it takes perturbed images and outputs denoised images via a learned defense operation; for PDE error correction, it ingests low-fidelity simulation states and outputs corrected states. Critically, the model is accessed exclusively through forward-pass function value queries—no gradient, Hessian, or computational graph access is required—making the approach applicable to truly black-box models including third-party APIs and non-differentiable simulators.

## Method

DeepZero replaces randomized vector-wise gradient estimation (RGE) with deterministic coordinate-wise gradient estimation (CGE), which perturbs one parameter at a time and yields both higher accuracy and lower per-query computation cost. To reduce CGE's O(d) query complexity, the paper introduces ZO-GraSP, a pruning-at-initialization method that estimates GraSP scores using only function queries to identify layer-wise pruning ratios (LPRs); during training, dynamic sparsity patterns are randomly reshuffled subject to these LPRs, yielding a Sparse-CGE that queries only a subset of coordinates while still training a dense model. Two implementation-level accelerations exploit CGE's structure: feature reuse (reusing intermediate activations before the perturbed layer to avoid redundant forward passes, giving ~2× speedup) and forward parallelization (distributing coordinate-wise finite differences across multiple GPUs, achieving linear speedup with GPU count). The full pipeline is formalized in Algorithm 2, which alternates sparsity pattern updates with parallelized sparse-CGE gradient estimation and weight synchronization.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-20 (270K parameters), DeepZero achieves 86.94% test accuracy, the best reported for gradient-free model training and approaching first-order SGD performance. On the 8-layer CNN benchmark (Boopathy & Fiete, 2022), DeepZero at width 64 reaches 64.1% accuracy, surpassing Align-ada at width 512 (58.0%) by 6.1 points. For black-box adversarial defense on ImageNet-10, DeepZero achieves 10–20% higher certified accuracy than ZO-AE-DS across all perturbation radii (e.g., 76.61% vs. 52.80% at r=0.25). For PDE error correction, ZO-SOL via DeepZero outperforms non-interactive training (NON) and narrows the gap with fully differentiable FO-SOL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeepZero outperforms Pattern Search (Chiang et al., 2023) in accuracy and runtime across all dataset sizes from 100 to 32K samples on ResNet-20/CIFAR-10, with the margin widening as data increases. Against Align-ada (Boopathy & Fiete, 2022), DeepZero at width 64 (64.1%) exceeds Align-ada at width 512 (58.0%) by 6.1 points, demonstrating that the gain comes from optimization quality rather than model width. DeepZero also clearly beats Feedback Alignment (46.5%) and Direct Feedback Alignment (49.9%) at the same width of 64. For black-box adversarial defense, DeepZero surpasses ZO-AE-DS (Zhang et al., 2022) by 10–20% certified accuracy at every perturbation radius, while remaining below the white-box FO-DS baseline (Salman et al., 2020) as expected. In the PDE error correction task, ZO-SOL (DeepZero) outperforms both the uncorrected simulation (SRC) and non-interactive training (NON) from Um et al. (2020), and narrows the gap to the fully differentiable FO-SOL baseline despite only having query-based access to the simulator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GraSP pruning-at-initialization framework (Wang et al., 2020) into the zeroth-order regime by replacing first-order and Hessian-gradient products with finite-difference estimates, yielding ZO-GraSP. It departs from prior ZO optimization work (which has been confined to input-space attacks and small-scale problems) by demonstrating that coordinate-wise gradient estimation (CGE), combined with pruning-induced sparsity and forward parallelization, can scale to training DNNs from scratch. The paper also extends the solver-in-the-loop paradigm (Um et al., 2020) to non-differentiable simulators and removes the autoencoder dimensionality-reduction bottleneck from ZO-AE-DS (Zhang et al., 2022c) for black-box adversarial defense.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DeepZero provides the first principled demonstration that ZO optimization can train a DNN from scratch at near-FO accuracy (86.94% on ResNet-20/CIFAR-10), opening a path for gradient-free training in settings where backpropagation is infeasible (black-box APIs, on-chip learning, non-differentiable simulators). The CGE-over-RGE finding and the pruning-sparsity synergy offer concrete, reusable design principles for the ZO optimization community. However, scalability remains the central open question: the main results are on a 270K-parameter model, and the authors explicitly acknowledge that larger models and datasets remain out of reach. The 10–20% improvement in black-box adversarial defense and PDE error correction over prior SOTA suggests practical value, but these applications are relatively niche.
