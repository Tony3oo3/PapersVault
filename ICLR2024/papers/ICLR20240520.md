## Problem

Learning-based image matching methods generalize poorly to in-the-wild images because they are trained on domain-specific datasets (ScanNet for indoor, MegaDepth for outdoor) constructed via inefficient RGBD scans or SfM+MVS pipelines that limit data diversity and scale. No single model can handle unknown scene types, and existing data construction methods are too slow and failure-prone to scale up training on diverse internet imagery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240520/cropped_page0_idx7.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework is architecture-agnostic and accepts a pair of RGB images, producing correspondences whose density depends on the underlying architecture: sparse keypoints (SuperGlue), semi-dense pixel-level matches (LoFTR), or dense kernelized matches (DKM). Training data is generated from 50 hours of YouTube tourism videos (covering 26 countries, 43 cities), with frames sampled every 20 frames and correspondences propagated to pairs up to several hundred frames apart. The final model is evaluated zero-shot on 46K image pairs across 12 diverse domains (8 real, 4 simulated) with overlap ratios from 10% to 50%.

## Method

GIM is a self-training framework that first trains a base model on standard domain-specific datasets, then uses that model combined with complementary hand-crafted methods (RootSIFT, etc.) to generate candidate correspondences on nearby video frames at 20, 40, and 80-frame intervals. Outlier correspondences are filtered via robust fitting (MAGSAC), and the key innovation is label propagation: correspondences are chained across intermediate frames to extend supervision to distant frame pairs, doubling the frame interval iteratively as long as more than 1024 correspondences survive. Strong data augmentations (random perspective transformations beyond standard crops/flips) are applied to counteract the limited camera motion in handheld videos. This avoids the expensive and failure-prone SfM+MVS pipeline entirely, processing 12.5 hours of video per day on 16 A100 GPUs, and consistently improves zero-shot AUC by 6.9–18.1% across three state-of-the-art architectures.

## Results

On the proposed ZEB zero-shot benchmark (46K image pairs across 12 domains), GIM improves the mean AUC@5° of all three architectures: SuperGlue from 31.2% to 34.3% (+9.9% relative), LoFTR from 33.1% to 39.1% (+18.1% relative), and DKM from 46.2% to 49.4% (+6.9% relative), using 50 hours of YouTube videos. Scaling to 100 hours further lifts GIM_DKM to 51.2% AUC. On downstream tasks, GIM_DKM improves indoor visual localization by >5% absolute on the (0.25 m, 10°) metric (57.1% vs. 51.5% on DUC1) and homography estimation by up to 4.7% AUC on HPatches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GIM consistently outperforms all four baseline families on the ZEB zero-shot benchmark. Against the hand-crafted RootSIFT baseline (31.8% mean AUC), GIM_SuperGlue (34.3%), GIM_LoFTR (39.1%), and GIM_DKM (49.4%) all achieve higher scores, and notably GIM closes the gap on domains where RootSIFT previously matched or beat learning-based methods (e.g., GL3, BLE, KIT, GTA). Compared to the in-domain SuperGlue baselines, GIM_SuperGlue improves mean AUC from 31.2% (OUT) to 34.3%, and even surpasses the LoFTR (OUT) baseline (33.1%) despite using a less advanced architecture. Against LoFTR, GIM_LoFTR yields the largest relative gain (+18.1%), lifting mean AUC from 33.1% to 39.1% and achieving the best mean rank (3.5) among semi-dense methods. Against DKM, GIM_DKM improves mean AUC from 46.2% (IN) to 49.4% and mean rank from 2.6 to 1.4, with the largest absolute gains on aerial/wild (GL3: 44.4→58.3) and object (BLE: 37.0→47.8) domains. On downstream tasks, a single GIM model beats domain-specific baselines: GIM_DKM outperforms DKM (IN) on indoor localization (57.1% vs. 51.5% at 0.25 m/10° on DUC1) and GIM_LoFTR outperforms LoFTR (OUT) on homography estimation by 4.7% AUC at 3 px.

## Relation to Prior Work

GIM extends the self-training paradigm (Grandvalet & Bengio, 2004) to image matching, drawing conceptual inspiration from foundation-model data strategies (CLIP's massive web-scale corpus, SAM's data engine, mixed-dataset depth estimation). It departs from the standard data construction pipelines of MegaDepth (COLMAP SfM+MVS) and ScanNet (RGBD scans) by replacing them with a lighter-weight self-training loop that fuses complementary matching methods, robust fitting, and temporal label propagation on internet videos. The framework is architecture-agnostic, applied here to SuperGlue, LoFTR, and DKM, and introduces ZEB as the first zero-shot cross-domain evaluation benchmark, in contrast to the in-domain-only evaluation protocol used by all prior matching methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the 2012 hand-crafted RootSIFT outperforms recent learned matchers on several in-the-wild ZEB domains challenges the implicit assumption that learned methods are universally superior and highlights how domain-specific training actively harms generalization. ZEB, with 46K pairs across 12 diverse domains, fills a critical evaluation gap that in-domain benchmarks (MegaDepth-1500, ScanNet-1500) cannot address. Open questions include whether the self-training signal quality plateaus at larger video scales, whether the tourism-video bias limits applicability to industrial or medical imagery, and whether the framework extends naturally to other geometric tasks (e.g., 3D reconstruction, SLAM) beyond two-view matching.
