## Problem

Multi-sensor fusion is widely regarded as a defense against adversarial attacks, yet it remains untested whether fusion models for 3D object detection can be compromised through the less significant camera modality alone. Prior attacks on fusion models required expensive LiDAR-manipulation equipment or targeted multiple modalities simultaneously, leaving open whether affordable camera-only adversarial patches can effectively subvert state-of-the-art camera-LiDAR fusion detectors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240952/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes multi-view camera images (6 surrounding cameras) and a 360-degree LiDAR point cloud from a target scene, along with white-box access to the target fusion model. It produces two outputs: a sensitivity heatmap (256×704, visualizing which image regions are most vulnerable to adversarial perturbation) and a physically deployable 2D adversarial patch (e.g., 2m×2.5m for scene-oriented or 1m×1m for object-oriented attacks). The patch is optimized with 3D projection functions that simulate its appearance when physically placed on the road or attached to a target object, ensuring digital-to-physical transferability.

## Method

The paper proposes a two-stage optimization-based attack framework. In Stage 1 (Sensitivity Distribution Recognition), an optimizable mask parameterized by a tanh function is jointly optimized with adversarial perturbations to minimize both an adversarial loss (MSE of detection scores toward zero) and a mask loss (penalizing mask magnitude); the dual optimization converges to high perturbation on sensitive regions and low on insensitive ones, yielding a sensitivity heatmap that classifies the model as either object-sensitive or globally sensitive. In Stage 2, the attack strategy is tailored to the identified sensitivity type: globally sensitive models receive scene-oriented attacks (patch on static background like the road, compromising arbitrary nearby objects via ground-plane 3D projection), while object-sensitive models receive object-oriented attacks (patch attached to a target object via bounding-box-based 3D projection, affecting only that object). Both strategies use Expectation of Transformation across training frames and color jitters to enhance physical-world robustness, and the patch is designed to be concealable within natural textures for stealth.

## Results

The proposed two-stage adversarial patch framework successfully compromises all six camera-LiDAR fusion models and one camera-only model on NuScenes. For scene-oriented attacks targeting globally sensitive models, mAP drops from 0.824 to 0.353 (57.2% degradation) on BEVFusion-PKU and from 0.886 to 0.553 (37.6%) on BEVFusion-MIT, while object-sensitive models remain largely unaffected. For object-oriented attacks, the target object's detection score drops from 0.728 to 0.156 on average (78.63% degradation) across all fusion models, while other objects' mAP changes by only 0.17%. Physical-world experiments confirm a 67.76% average reduction in pedestrian detection scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its camera-only adversarial patch attacks against six state-of-the-art camera-LiDAR fusion models (TransFusion, DeepInteraction, UVTR, PointAugmenting, BEVFusion-MIT, BEVFusion-PKU) and one camera-only model (BEVFormer) as a reference baseline. The key finding is that multi-sensor fusion does NOT provide inherent defense: all fusion models are successfully compromised by camera-only patches, with the camera-only BEVFormer actually showing the worst degradation (90.02% score drop vs. 78.63% average for fusion models). In benign performance, fusion models substantially outperform the camera-only baseline (e.g., BEVFusion-MIT mAP 0.886 vs. BEVFormer 0.519), but this advantage vanishes under attack. For scene-oriented attacks, only the two globally sensitive fusion models (BEVFusion-PKU, BEVFusion-MIT) are significantly affected, while the camera-only BEVFormer—being object-sensitive—remains robust, confirming that sensitivity type, not modality count, determines vulnerability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial patch optimization framework from Cheng et al. (ECCV 2022), originally designed for camera-only monocular depth estimation, to the setting of camera-LiDAR fusion models for 3D object detection. It departs from prior fusion-model attacks (Cao et al. 2021, Tu et al. 2021, Hallyburton et al. 2022) that targeted LiDAR or both modalities using expensive equipment, by showing that the 'less significant' camera modality alone suffices. The projection-based physical deployment technique from Cheng et al. (ICLR 2023) is reused to bridge the digital-to-physical gap, while the novel two-stage sensitivity recognition and targeted attack strategy is the core methodological contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work challenges the widely-held security assumption that multi-sensor fusion inherently defends against adversarial attacks, demonstrating that the weakest link in a fusion system is its least-weighted modality—which is also the cheapest for an attacker to exploit. The sensitivity heatmap provides a diagnostic tool for understanding *why* different fusion architectures (alignment-based vs. non-alignment-based, BEV-based vs. cross-attention) exhibit different vulnerability patterns, informing future defense design. Open questions include whether decision-level fusion architectures can resist such single-modal attacks, whether the findings generalize to other sensor combinations (e.g., radar-camera), and what architectural modifications could eliminate global sensitivity without sacrificing detection performance.
