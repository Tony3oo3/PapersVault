## Problem

State-of-the-art motion prediction methods rely on elaborate architectural designs such as anchor-based modeling and proposal-refinement schemes, leading to intricate information processing pipelines. The paper asks whether a simpler, universal transformer architecture can achieve strong scene understanding through a properly designed self-supervised training scheme, analogous to how large language models learn broadly applicable knowledge from masked reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241317/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes A nearest agents' historical trajectories (T timesteps at 10 Hz, each with coordinates, timestamp, and agent type in the target agent's local frame) and a vectorized road network of directed lane centerline segments (≤5 m each, pruned via static path planning to a compact subset S). It outputs N predicted 2D future trajectories, each paired with a normalized confidence score, produced by N learnable queries cross-attending to the scene memory embeddings.

## Method

SEPT uses a two-stage training procedure. In the self-supervised pretraining stage, the encoder is trained on three mask-reconstruction tasks: Masked Trajectory Modeling (randomly masking and reconstructing waypoints to capture kinematic temporal dependencies), Masked Road Modeling (zeroing road attributes except the start point to learn road topology and connectivity), and Tail Prediction (predicting the latter portion of a trajectory from its head plus road context to align temporal and spatial representations). The encoder itself is a compact sequential design: TempoNet (K_T transformer blocks) encodes trajectories along the temporal axis with T5-style relative position embeddings, then SpaNet (K_S transformer blocks) encodes the concatenated agent-road embeddings along the spatial axis, avoiding dedicated sub-modules for agent-agent, road-road, or agent-road relations. In the fine-tuning stage, a Cross Attender decoder with N learnable queries is attached and the full model is trained end-to-end with a combined L1 regression and classification loss. This token-level granularity (individual waypoints and short road vectors as tokens) and the additive TP alignment task distinguish SEPT from prior masked-autoencoder approaches like Traj-MAE and Forecast-MAE, yielding ~9.6 M parameters and 2× faster inference than the strongest baseline QCNet.

## Results

On Argoverse 1, SEPT achieves b-minFDE₆ of 1.682, minADE₆ of 0.728, minFDE₆ of 1.057, and MR₆ of 0.103, ranking 1st on all primary metrics except MR₆ (where HOME's sparse-sampling trick gives it a narrow edge). On Argoverse 2, SEPT ranks 1st across all metrics with b-minFDE₆ of 1.736, minADE₆ of 0.605, minFDE₆ of 1.151, and MR₆ of 0.137. Notably, SEPT uses only ~9.6M parameters (40% of QCNet) and runs at twice the inference speed of the strongest baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEPT outperforms all 10 Argoverse 1 baselines on b-minFDE₆, minADE₆, minFDE₆, minADE₁, minFDE₁, and MR₁, with the sole exception of MR₆ where HOME (0.085) edges out SEPT (0.103) via a sparse-sampling technique that sacrifices other metrics. Against the strongest baseline QCNet, SEPT improves b-minFDE₆ from 1.693 to 1.682 and minFDE₆ from 1.067 to 1.057 while using only 40% of QCNet's parameters and achieving 2× faster inference. On Argoverse 2, SEPT dominates all four listed baselines (MacFormer, Gnet, ProphNet, QCNet) on every metric, with the largest gap over QCNet on minFDE₆ (1.151 vs. 1.191) and MR₆ (0.137 vs. 0.144). Compared to the two closest SSL-based methods, SEPT beats Traj-MAE by 0.082 on minADE₆ and 0.193 on minFDE₆ (Argoverse 1), and beats Forecast-MAE by 0.085 on minADE₆ and 0.187 on minFDE₆ (Argoverse 2), demonstrating that the three-task pretraining design (especially Tail Prediction) yields substantially better scene understanding than single-modality masking.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEPT builds on VectorNet's vectorized map representation and the masked autoencoder paradigm (He et al., 2022), applying it to the joint trajectory-and-road input space of motion prediction. It departs from prior SSL approaches in this domain (Traj-MAE, Forecast-MAE) in two key ways: introducing a cross-modality pretraining task (Tail Prediction) that explicitly aligns trajectory and road representations, and adopting finer-grained waypoint-level tokenization rather than whole-trajectory or whole-lane-segment tokens. Conceptually, SEPT challenges the prevailing trend of increasingly elaborate architectural designs (anchor-based modeling, proposal-refinement, hierarchically stacked factorized attention in QCNet, WayFormer, ProphNet) by demonstrating that a simple sequential encoder (temporal then spatial) combined with well-designed self-supervised objectives is sufficient to reach SOTA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: SEPT achieves SOTA on both Argoverse 1 and 2 while using only 40% of QCNet's parameters and running at twice the inference speed, making it attractive for deployment-constrained autonomous driving systems. The consistent additive improvement from all three pretraining tasks (no combination degrades performance) provides a robust recipe that practitioners can adopt. However, the agent-centric formulation limits SEPT to single-agent prediction, and extending it to multi-agent joint prediction remains an open challenge. The finding that including test-set data in pretraining further improves validation performance, while framed as a diversity benefit, raises questions about the generalizability of the pretraining gains.
