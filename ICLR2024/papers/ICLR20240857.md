## Problem

Traditional binary classification decision boundaries rely solely on model score thresholds, ignoring prediction uncertainty. This paper shows that model score estimation bias (deviation from true positivity) systematically depends on both score and uncertainty, meaning the same score can correspond to different true positivity rates at different uncertainty levels—especially under differential sampling during training. The key question is whether a 2D decision boundary over score and uncertainty can yield significantly better recall at high precision bounds.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a model score s(x) and an uncertainty estimate u(x) (e.g., differential entropy of a Beta posterior from a Posterior Network) for each sample in a hold-out set. These are discretized into a K×L grid of bins (e.g., 500 score bins × 3 uncertainty bins), where each bin stores positive and total sample counts. The output is a vector of K score thresholds b(i), one per uncertainty level, defining the labeling function ψ_b(x) = 1[s(x) ≥ b(u(x))]. The MIST variant additionally produces calibrated class probabilities via per-uncertainty-level isotonic regression.

## Method

The paper first provides a theoretical analysis (Theorem 3.1) using Beta priors and Posterior Networks to show that expected test positivity conditioned on model score is a function of both score and γ(x) (the prior-to-evidence ratio, positively correlated with uncertainty), yielding a closed-form estimation bias expression. It then formulates the 2D Binned Decision Boundary (2D-BDB) problem—maximizing recall subject to a precision bound over a K×L grid—and proves it NP-hard via reduction from subset-sum. For the tractable equi-weight binning case (uncertainty quantiles nested with score quantiles), an optimal O(K²L²) dynamic programming algorithm (EW-DPMT) is provided. A more scalable alternative, MIST, performs 1D isotonic regression independently within each uncertainty level to calibrate scores, then selects a single global threshold on calibrated probabilities, achieving comparable quality at O(KL log(KL)) complexity. Empirically, these 2D boundary methods yield 25–40% recall gains over score-only thresholding at high precision bounds on Criteo, Avazu, and E-Com datasets.

## Results

On Criteo (90% precision, τ=3), EW-DPMT achieves 2.7%±0.3% recall vs. 2.2%±0.2% for the score-only baseline (ST), a 22% relative gain. On Avazu (70% precision, τ=5), the gain is 42% (2.7% vs. 1.92%), and on E-Com (70% precision, τ=5), it is 26% (22.3% vs. 17.6%). The isotonic-regression-based MIST algorithm achieves comparable recall (2.7%, 2.7%, 21.6%) with significantly lower runtime than the DP methods, making it a practical alternative. Gains are largest at high precision bounds and diminish as precision requirements relax.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is ST (Single Threshold), which uses only the model score to set a 1D decision boundary. All proposed uncertainty-aware methods (GMT, MIST, EW-DPMT, VW-DPMT) consistently outperform ST across all three datasets, with statistically significant gains (paired t-test p-values ≤ 0.08). The heuristic recalibration method HR from Pocevivciutė et al. (2022), which assumes positivity monotonically increases with uncertainty for a fixed score, performs substantially worse than ST on all datasets (e.g., 0.8% vs. 2.2% on Criteo Equi-weight), confirming that naive uncertainty incorporation can be harmful. Among the proposed methods, EW-DPMT and MIST achieve the best recall; MIST matches EW-DPMT's quality while running in ~100s regardless of bin count, whereas EW-DPMT's runtime grows quadratically with bins (530s at 1000 score bins vs. 80s for MIST). The variable-weight DP (VW-DPMT) is competitive but computationally expensive, making MIST and EW-DPMT the recommended practical choices.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard 1D decision boundary selection (score-thresholding) to a 2D formulation incorporating both model score and uncertainty, building on Posterior Networks (Charpentier et al., 2020) for uncertainty estimation and isotonic regression (Stout, 2013) for calibration. It departs from prior heuristic 2D approaches in digital pathology (Pocevivciutė et al., 2022) and static uncertainty-threshold methods (Dolezal et al., 2022) by providing the first formal problem formulation, a theoretical analysis linking score estimation bias to uncertainty via Beta-prior Bayes' rule, and an NP-hardness proof via reduction from subset-sum. The work also connects to Bayesian decision-making literature (Troffaes, 2007; Denœux, 2019) but differs by grounding the boundary in empirical hold-out data rather than axiomatic risk criteria.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 25–40% relative recall gain at high precision bounds (90% on Criteo, 70% on Avazu/E-Com) is practically meaningful for high-stakes applications like fraud detection and medical diagnosis where precision constraints are strict. The model-agnostic formulation (works with any blackbox classifier plus any uncertainty estimator) and the practical MIST algorithm (near-optimal quality at O(KL log KL) cost) make the approach broadly deployable. Key limitations include reliance on a Beta-prior generative assumption for the theoretical analysis, evaluation limited to three tabular CTR/e-commerce datasets, and the fact that absolute recall improvements remain small (e.g., 2.2%→2.7% at 90% precision). Open questions include joint optimization of binning configuration and boundary, extension to multi-class and regression settings, and whether the gains persist with other uncertainty estimators beyond Posterior Networks.
