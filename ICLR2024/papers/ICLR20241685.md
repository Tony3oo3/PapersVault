## Problem

Most causal discovery methods assume no latent confounders, and existing approaches that handle latent variables impose strong graphical constraints (e.g., observed variables cannot be adjacent, latent variables must be mutually independent, or observed variables can only be leaf nodes). This paper tackles the more general and practically important setting where latent variables can serve as causes, effects, or mediators for both observed and latent variables, and observed variables can be directly adjacent to each other.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241685/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only i.i.d. samples of the n observed variables (no access to latent variables) from a linear latent causal model. It outputs the Markov equivalence class of the full causal graph over all n+m variables, including the locations and cardinalities of latent variables. In finite-sample settings, rank is estimated via canonical correlations (Anderson, 1984) with a chi-squared test, and the algorithm operates on the covariance matrix structure rather than requiring distributional assumptions beyond linearity.

## Method

RLCD (Rank-based Latent Causal Discovery) is a three-phase algorithm that exploits rank deficiency of cross-covariance matrices to identify t-separations, which are strictly more informative than d-separations when latent variables are present. Phase 1 builds a CI skeleton by replacing PC's conditional independence tests with rank tests (Lemma 10). Phase 2 (FindCausalClusters) searches for combinations of cover sets C and X that induce rank deficiency equal to k, identifying 'atomic covers'—the minimal identifiable units containing latent variables—while a NoCollider check prevents false positives from collider-induced rank deficiency. Phase 3 (RefineCausalClusters) corrects any over-merged clusters by recursively re-running Phase 2 on subgraphs. The key theoretical contribution is establishing that under mild graphical conditions (every latent variable belongs to an atomic cover, and a cardinality constraint on colliders), the rank deficiency property uniquely maps to atomic covers, enabling asymptotic identification of the Markov equivalence class with conditions significantly weaker than prior work.

## Results

On synthetic latent graphs, RLCD achieves the highest F1 score for skeleton recovery among all variables across all three graph types (latent+tree, latent+measurement, latent+general) and all sample sizes (2k, 5k, 10k). The headline result is 0.98 F1 on latent+tree with 10k samples, compared to 0.60 for the strongest baseline (Hier. rank) and 0.37 for PC/FCI. On the most challenging general latent model, RLCD reaches 0.80 F1 at 10k samples versus 0.45 for Hier. rank. On the real-world Big Five Personality dataset (50 indicators, ~20k samples), RLCD recovers a plausible causal graph that reconciles latent dimension and network theories of personality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RLCD outperforms all five baselines on every graph type and sample size for both the full-structure F1 (Table 2) and the observed-only F1 (Table 3). The closest competitor, Hier. rank (Huang et al., 2022), trails by 0.24–0.35 F1 points because it restricts observed variables to leaf nodes and cannot handle direct edges among them. PC and FCI, which do not explicitly model latent structure, plateau around 0.37–0.49 F1 regardless of sample size, reflecting their inability to recover latent edges. GIN and RCD perform comparably to PC/FCI on the full-structure metric (0.33–0.47) and are not applicable to the observed-only metric (Table 3) because they assume observed variables are not directly adjacent. The advantage of RLCD is most pronounced on tree and measurement models (up to 0.98 F1) and remains substantial on the hardest general latent model (0.80 vs. 0.45 for Hier. rank at 10k samples).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the rank-based causal discovery line initiated by Huang et al. (2022) (Hier. rank), which identified latent hierarchical structures but required observed variables to be leaf nodes. It also builds on the foundational rank-t-separation theorem of Sullivant et al. (2010) and the PC algorithm's CI-skeleton stage (Spirtes et al., 2000), replacing CI tests with rank tests for a unified framework. The key departure is the introduction of the 'atomic cover' as a minimal identifiable unit, which allows latent variables to serve as causes, effects, or mediators for both observed and latent variables — a generality not available in FCI, RCD, GIN, or Hier. rank, all of which impose strong graphical constraints (e.g., no edges among observed variables, mutually independent latents, or tree structures).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental practical limitation: nearly all widely-used causal discovery methods assume no latent confounders, an assumption that rarely holds in real-world data. By establishing that rank deficiency (via t-separation) is strictly more informative than CI (d-separation) in the presence of latent variables, and by providing graphical conditions (Condition 1 and 2) that are significantly weaker than prior work, RLCD opens the door to causal structure learning in much more general and realistic settings. Limitations include restriction to linear models and acyclic graphs, with extensions to nonlinear and cyclic settings left as future work. The Big Five personality application demonstrates practical relevance by reconciling latent-dimension and network-theory perspectives on personality structure.
