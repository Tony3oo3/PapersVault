## Problem

Off-policy adversarial imitation learning methods like DAC reuse historical data via a replay buffer but lose the guarantee of matching the expert's state-action distribution, since the buffer represents a uniform mixture of all past policies rather than the current policy. ValueDICE recovers a principled off-policy objective but is restricted to deterministic environment dynamics. This paper seeks a fully off-policy AIL algorithm that is both principled (guaranteeing distribution matching) and compatible with stochastic transitions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes expert demonstrations as state-action pairs, where states are either low-dimensional controller vectors or 3 stacked 84×84 RGB frames (9×84×84) for pixel-based tasks. Rather than outputting a single policy, AILBoost produces a weighted ensemble of Markovian policies; at evaluation, one policy is sampled from the ensemble with probability proportional to its boosting weight and executed for the entire episode without switching. The expert itself may be a single Markovian policy or a mixture of weighted Markovian policies, and the induced state-action distribution of the ensemble is provably richer than that of any single Markovian policy.

## Method

AILBoost performs gradient boosting in the state-action occupancy space: it maintains a weighted ensemble of policies (weak learners) and at each iteration trains a new weak learner to reduce the reverse KL divergence between the ensemble's occupancy measure and the expert's. The discriminator is trained via the variational form of reverse KL using a weighted replay buffer, where data from each past policy π_i is weighted by its current boosting coefficient α_i, providing an unbiased estimate of the ensemble's state-action distribution. New weak learners are trained with an off-policy RL algorithm (SAC) using the discriminator as a reward signal, and weights are updated with a fixed learning rate α: the new policy receives weight α while all existing weights are multiplied by (1−α). This weighting scheme is the key distinction from DAC, which uses uniform weights over the replay buffer, and it makes the entire training loop off-policy while preserving the distribution-matching guarantee.

## Results

On the DeepMind Control Suite (5 tasks spanning easy to hard difficulty), AILBoost outperforms all baselines (DAC, ValueDICE, IQ-Learn, BC) across all aggregate metrics — inter-quartile mean (IQM), mean score, and optimality gap — with 95% confidence intervals over 3 seeds. A key finding is that AILBoost consistently matches expert performance with as few as 1 expert trajectory, where state-of-the-art baselines like IQ-Learn completely fail on harder tasks (e.g., Humanoid Stand). On image-based DMC environments (Walker Walk, Cheetah Run) using DrQ-v2 as the base RL algorithm, AILBoost also outperforms DAC, demonstrating scalability to pixel observations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DAC (Kostrikov et al., 2019), AILBoost wins on both state-based and image-based DMC environments across all aggregate metrics (IQM, mean, optimality gap), though the probability of improvement per task is approximately 50% — the advantage comes from a significantly larger magnitude of improvement. Against ValueDICE (Kostrikov et al., 2020), AILBoost achieves substantially better performance, with the paper noting it 'greatly outperforms' ValueDICE, which is limited to deterministic transition dynamics and suffers from biased mini-batch estimates. Against IQ-Learn (Garg et al., 2021), a state-of-the-art IL method, AILBoost outperforms it on all five DMC tasks, with the gap widening on harder environments; IQ-Learn completely fails on Humanoid Stand with 1 expert trajectory while AILBoost still matches expert performance. Against behavior cloning (BC), AILBoost consistently outperforms, confirming the benefit of distribution-matching over action-matching objectives. The paper also notes that AILBoost's advantage over DAC grows with environment difficulty, suggesting the principled boosting-based weighting of the replay buffer becomes more critical as tasks become harder.

## Relation to Prior Work

AILBoost extends the adversarial imitation learning lineage from GAIL (Ho & Ermon, 2016) through DAC (Kostrikov et al., 2019) by replacing DAC's ad-hoc uniform-weighted replay buffer with a principled weighted ensemble derived from gradient boosting in the state-action occupancy space. It departs from ValueDICE (Kostrikov et al., 2020), which achieves off-policy AIL via a Bellman-operator reformulation that requires deterministic dynamics, by instead maintaining a weighted mixture of Markovian policies (weak learners) whose occupancy measure is directly matched to the expert distribution. The connection to gradient boosting (Mason et al., 1999) and prior work on boosted policy search (Scherrer & Geist, 2014; Hazan et al., 2019) provides the theoretical guarantee that the ensemble update monotonically decreases the reverse KL objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AILBoost offers the first principled (rather than heuristic) off-policy AIL algorithm that works with stochastic dynamics and mini-batch updates, addressing a fundamental gap left by both DAC and ValueDICE. The ability to achieve competitive imitation with a single expert trajectory has practical implications for domains where expert data is expensive to collect. Open questions include extending the framework to discrete action spaces, observation-only settings, and understanding the memory trade-off of maintaining policy ensembles at scale. The ~50% probability of improvement over DAC (though with larger magnitude) suggests the boosting weighting helps most when the replay buffer distribution diverges significantly from the current policy.
