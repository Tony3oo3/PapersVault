## Problem

Existing Concept Bottleneck Models (CBMs) and their variants (CEMs, PCBMs) fail to capture high-order nonlinear interactions among concepts, so correcting one mispredicted concept does not propagate to fix correlated concepts, and they cannot quantify complex conditional dependencies between concepts and class labels. This limits both the accuracy of concept intervention and the depth of interpretability a model can provide.

## Input / Output

The model takes RGB images (299×299 for CUB/AWA2, 64×64 for CelebA) processed through a frozen ResNet101 backbone at inference. Beyond standard class labels and binary concept vectors, ECBMs produce structured conditional probability estimates—such as p(c_k|y), p(c_k|c_k', y), and p(c_{-k}|x, c_k)—computed by composing the three energy functions, enabling both instance-level and class-level probabilistic interpretation of concept interactions.

## Method

ECBMs define a joint energy E_joint(x, c, y) as a weighted sum of three neural-network energy functions: a class energy E(x,y) measuring input–label compatibility, a concept energy E(x,c) measuring input–concept compatibility (using CEM-style positive/negative embeddings per concept), and a global energy E(c,y) capturing interactions among all concepts and between concepts and the class label. Training minimizes the negative log-likelihood under Boltzmann distributions for each energy term. At inference, all parameters are frozen and the optimal concept vector and class label are found by gradient-based optimization over relaxed continuous variables (sigmoid/softmax parameterizations), then rounded to binary/one-hot. The key unifying insight is that prediction, concept correction (Proposition 3.1), and conditional interpretation (Propositions 3.2–3.5) all reduce to computing conditional probabilities from the same joint energy, with the global energy network specifically responsible for modeling concept–concept and concept–class interactions that vanilla CBMs lack.

## Results

On CUB-200-2011, ECBM achieves 81.2% class accuracy and 71.3% overall concept accuracy, substantially outperforming the best baseline CEM (79.6% class, 39.6% overall concept). On CelebA, ECBM reaches 34.3% class accuracy and 47.8% overall concept accuracy, again leading all baselines. On AWA2, ECBM attains 91.2% class accuracy and 85.4% overall concept accuracy. The most striking gain is in overall concept accuracy on CUB, where ECBM more than doubles the best baseline (71.3% vs. 39.6%), demonstrating its ability to capture inter-concept correlations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ECBM outperforms all four baselines (CBM, ProbCBM, PCBM, CEM) on overall concept accuracy across all three datasets, with the largest margin on CUB where it achieves 71.3% versus CEM's 39.6%—nearly double. On class accuracy, ECBM leads CEM by 1.6 points on CUB (81.2% vs. 79.6%), 1.3 points on CelebA (34.3% vs. 33.0%), and 0.4 points on AWA2 (91.2% vs. 90.8%). Concept accuracy is closely matched across methods (within ~1%), with ECBM slightly ahead on CUB (97.3% vs. 96.5%) and CelebA (87.6% vs. 86.7%). In the intervention/correction experiments (Fig. 2), ECBM outperforms baselines on concept and overall concept accuracy at all intervention ratios, though it underperforms CEM with RandInt on class accuracy during intervention because CEM's strict bottleneck architecture benefits more from even a few corrected concepts. The paper positions ECBM as complementary to CEM: ECBM excels at capturing concept interactions and providing conditional interpretations, while CEM with RandInt remains stronger for pure class-accuracy gains under intervention.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Concept Bottleneck Model (CBM) lineage (Koh et al., 2020) and its variants (CEM, PCBM, ProbCBM) by replacing the sequential decomposition p(c|x)·p(y|c) with a joint energy function over (x, c, y) tuples, drawing on the energy-based model framework (LeCun et al., 2006). The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a global energy network E_θ^global(c,y) that explicitly models concept-concept interactions and concept-class dependencies, which prior CBM variants treat as independent. The paper positions itself as the first method to unify concept prediction, test-time concept correction, and conditional dependency quantification (e.g., p(c_k|y, c_k')) as conditional probabilities derived from a single joint energy, rather than treating these as separate ad-hoc procedures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified energy-based formulation provides a principled way to answer 'what-if' questions about a model's concept predictions (e.g., 'if I correct concept c_k, what happens to c_{-k} and y?') that prior CBMs cannot address, potentially improving trust in concept-based interpretations for high-stakes applications. The near-doubling of overall concept accuracy on CUB (71.3% vs. 39.6%) suggests that modeling concept interactions is critical for coherent multi-concept predictions. However, class accuracy gains over strong baselines like CEM are modest, and the method still requires full concept annotations; open questions include scaling to hundreds or thousands of concepts, enabling unsupervised concept discovery within the energy framework, and quantifying uncertainty via Bayesian energy networks.
