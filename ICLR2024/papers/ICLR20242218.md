## Problem

Quality-Diversity (QD) algorithms suffer from low sample efficiency, and while prior work has focused on refining selection and variation operators, the inherently large-scale optimization problem—simultaneously optimizing thousands of high-dimensional policy networks—has been underexplored. This paper identifies that the excessive size of the optimization space is a key bottleneck and asks whether cooperative coevolution can simplify the QD problem by decomposing policy networks into functionally distinct parts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242218/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes environment state vectors (e.g., 11-dim for Hopper, 227-dim for Humanoid) or RGB images (Atari Pong) as input and produces a CVT-ME decision archive of 1024 cells, each containing a complete policy network paired with its associated representation part. The representation archive is deliberately small (20 entries) compared to the decision archive, reflecting the observation that state representations are more shareable than decision-making behaviors. For QDax tasks, policies are 3-layer MLPs (256-256-|A| with tanh activations); for Atari Pong, a DQN with 3 convolutional and 2 fully connected layers is used.

## Method

CCQD decomposes each policy network into a representation part (front layers) and a decision part (remaining layers), maintaining two sub-populations that are coevolved cooperatively. The representation archive is kept small (20) while the decision archive is large (1024), exploiting the insight that representations contain shareable knowledge while decisions must remain diverse. Variation follows PGA-ME's split strategy—half offspring use IsoLineDD for diversity and half use TD3 for quality—but with two critical differences: each representation part maintains its own PeVFA critic (rather than a single shared critic), which alleviates off-policy bias, and PeVFA explicitly conditions on a policy embedding to provide better value generalization across the multiple decision parts that share a given representation. Parent selection pairs each randomly chosen decision part with a randomly selected representation part, and survivor selection uses vanilla QD replacement for the decision archive and a simple replacement-plus-association-check for the representation archive.

## Results

CCQD achieves the best QD-Score AUC (×10¹²) on 7 out of 8 QDax environments, with an average rank of 1.12 versus 3.50 for the next-best methods (QD-PG, PGA-ME, OMG-MEGA). On the most challenging tasks, CCQD yields substantial gains: 96.75 on Hopper Uni (vs. 93.25 for PGA-ME), 2.65 on Humanoid Omni (vs. 1.40 for PGA-ME), and 52.73 on Point Maze (vs. 43.90 for ME). The paper reports an average ~247% sample-efficiency improvement over PGA-ME when reaching equivalent QD-Score, and CCQD also attains the best average rank on Max Fitness (1.88) and QD-Score (1.00).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCQD is compared against five baselines: MAP-Elites (ME), QD-PG, PGA-ME, OMG-MEGA, and PBT-ME. CCQD significantly outperforms ME on all 8 environments (e.g., 96.75 vs. 84.17 on Hopper Uni, 2.65 vs. 0.90 on Humanoid Omni). Against the three strongest baselines (QD-PG, PGA-ME, OMG-MEGA), CCQD wins significantly on 7/8 environments and is statistically equivalent only on Ant Maze (157.03 vs. 164.94 for QD-PG). PBT-ME is the only method that statistically matches CCQD on one environment (HalfCheetah Uni: 425.16 ≈ 432.83) and achieves the highest Max Fitness on Ant Uni and Ant Maze, but its poor Coverage drags its QD-Score below CCQD. On the longer-episode setting (length 1000), CCQD retains the best average rank on QD-Score AUC (1.75) and Max Fitness (1.38), though PBT-ME narrowly edges CCQD on HalfCheetah Uni AUC (1624.75 vs. 1484.76). The framework's versatility is further confirmed on Atari Pong, where CCQD with DQN consistently outperforms DQN-ME across three decomposition configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies cooperative coevolution (Potter & Jong, 2000) to Quality-Diversity optimization for the first time, using a layer-based decomposition of policy networks into a small representation sub-population and a large decision sub-population. It builds directly on the MAP-Elites QD loop and adopts the PGA-ME variation operator (IsoLineDD + TD3), but departs from prior sample-efficiency work—which has focused on refining selection and variation operators—by identifying the inherently large-scale optimization problem (thousands of high-dimensional solutions) as the core bottleneck. The use of PeVFA as a per-representation critic is a natural fit for the coevolutionary structure, where each representation part is combined with multiple decision parts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~200% sample-efficiency improvement over strong baselines across eight QDax environments and Atari Pong makes QD-RL more practical for expensive-evaluation settings (e.g., environment generation, robotics). The framework's generality—compatible with different selection, variation, and survivor-selection operators—positions it as a composable building block rather than a monolithic algorithm. Key open questions include: whether the shared representation parts enable transfer of decision policies to new tasks, whether combining CCQD with archive distillation (QDT) yields both sample and storage efficiency, and the lack of theoretical guarantees for the coevolutionary QD process.
