## Problem

GAN optimization is typically analyzed under the assumption that the discriminator reaches its optimal solution, yet real-world GANs rarely achieve this, and it remains unclear whether the gradients the discriminator provides actually reduce the distance between the generator and target distributions. The paper identifies that most existing GAN losses (hinge, saturating, non-saturating) fail a key condition called direction optimality, meaning the discriminator's learned direction does not correspond to the most distinguishing axis between the two distributions in feature space.

## Input / Output

The generator takes a latent vector z ~ N(0, I) (128-dimensional in experiments) and, for conditional generation, a class label c, producing a generated RGB image (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 128×128 for CelebA, 256×256 for ImageNet). The discriminator's architecture is modified so that its final linear layer is constrained to the unit hypersphere S^{D-1}, decomposing the discriminator into a feature extractor h(x) and a trainable direction vector ω that is updated with a dedicated loss term separate from the feature network parameters.

## Method

SAN introduces two minimal modifications to any existing GAN: (1) the discriminator's last linear layer is replaced by a unit-norm direction vector ω ∈ S^{D-1} that projects the feature h(x) onto a scalar, and (2) the maximization objective is split into two terms — L^h (the original GAN loss, e.g., hinge or saturating) that trains the feature extractor h, and L^ω (a Wasserstein-style term) that trains ω to be the direction most distinguishing the real and fake distributions in feature space. Theoretically, the paper proves that when ω satisfies direction optimality, h is separable and injective, the discriminator induces a true distance (via the functional mean divergence FM* and max-ASW framework), so generator gradients provably reduce distributional dissimilarity without requiring the discriminator to be optimal. The scheme is loss-agnostic: any GAN objective with a positive derivative R' can be converted to SAN by simply adding the L^ω term, and it extends to class-conditional generation by maintaining per-class direction vectors.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, StyleSAN-XL achieves a FID of 1.36, improving over StyleGAN-XL's 1.85. On ImageNet 256×256, StyleSAN-XL reaches FID 2.14 and IS 274.20, surpassing StyleGAN-XL's FID 2.30 and IS 265.12. On DCGAN with CIFAR-10, SAN consistently reduces FID by ~4 points across all three loss variants (e.g., Hinge: 24.07→20.23). On BigGAN, Hinge SAN improves FID from 8.25 to 6.20 on CIFAR-10 and from 10.73 to 8.05 on CIFAR-100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAN outperforms GAN across all tested architectures and loss functions. On DCGAN (CIFAR-10), SAN reduces FID by 3.8–5.0 points over the corresponding GAN for Hinge, Saturating, and Non-saturating losses (e.g., Hinge: 24.07→20.23). On BigGAN, Hinge SAN improves FID from 8.25 to 6.20 on CIFAR-10 and from 10.73 to 8.05 on CIFAR-100, while also slightly improving IS. On StyleGAN-XL, SAN achieves the paper's headline SOTA results: FID 1.36 vs. 1.85 on CIFAR-10 and FID 2.14 vs. 2.30 on ImageNet 256×256. The paper also notes that Wasserstein GAN, while satisfying direction optimality, empirically fails the separability condition, leading to rotational behavior and poor generation quality, whereas SAN satisfies all three metrizable conditions simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges GAN theory and sliced optimal transport by introducing the functional mean divergence (FM*) and max-ASW as analytical tools, departing from the standard optimal-discriminator assumption that underpins most GAN analyses (Goodfellow et al., 2014; Arjovsky et al., 2017). It builds on the augmented sliced Wasserstein distance framework (Chen et al., 2022) and the original sliced Wasserstein concept (Bonneel et al., 2015) to derive three sufficient conditions—direction optimality, separability, and injectivity—under which a GAN discriminator is metrizable. The practical contribution (SAN) is a minimal modification to existing GAN training: normalizing the discriminator's last linear layer onto a hypersphere and adding a direction-optimization term, making it orthogonal to and composable with prior stabilization techniques like spectral normalization and guidance losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a diagnostic lens for GAN training failures (mode collapse, instability) that does not rely on the unrealistic optimal-discriminator assumption, and the finding that standard GAN losses (hinge, saturating, non-saturating) violate direction optimality while WGAN violates separability offers new explanations for known pathologies. The practical impact is notable: the two-line modification improves SOTA FID on StyleGAN-XL for class-conditional generation (1.85→1.36 CIFAR10, 2.30→2.14 ImageNet 256). However, separability remains only empirically verified rather than theoretically characterized, injectivity is acknowledged as hard to control via loss design, and the method does not extend straightforwardly to all GAN variants (e.g., least-squares GAN), leaving open questions about the full scope of the metrizable framework.
