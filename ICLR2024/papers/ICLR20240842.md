## Problem

Machine learning predictors of protein-protein binding affinity changes (ΔΔG) upon mutation suffer from poor generalization due to three compounding issues: training datasets contain severe structural redundancy, standard train-test splits leak near-duplicate interfaces between folds, and model architectures overfit to the limited labeled data. As a result, reported performance overestimates real-world capability on genuinely unseen protein binders.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240842/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 3D protein-protein complex as a coarse-grained k-NN graph over Cα atoms, augmented with one-hot amino acid encodings (type-0 scalars) and virtual beta-carbon orientation vectors (type-1 equivariant features) derived from ideal backbone geometry, deliberately omitting side-chain rotamer angles to model interface flexibility. It outputs a per-residue amino acid probability matrix P ∈ [0,1]^{N×20}; the ΔΔG estimate is then computed as the log-odds ratio between wild-type and mutant residue probabilities at mutated positions, requiring only a single forward pass and inherently satisfying antisymmetry.

## Method

PPIFORMER is an SE(3)-equivariant transformer built from stacked Equiformer graph-attention blocks that process the coarse-grained interface graph via equivariant message passing, producing SE(3)-invariant residue embeddings. It is pre-trained on the newly constructed PPIRef dataset (45,553 structurally non-redundant PPIs deduplicated from 322K filtered entries using the iDist algorithm, a fast SE(3)-invariant message-passing approximation of iAlign that is ~480× faster than exact structural alignment) via masked amino acid modeling with label smoothing and inverse-frequency class weighting to encourage flexibility toward unseen variants. For ΔΔG prediction, the pre-trained model is fine-tuned with an MSE loss on the log-odds ratio of wild-type versus mutant probabilities, a thermodynamically motivated objective that decomposes ΔΔG into log(K_wt) − log(K_mut) and enforces antisymmetry without requiring two forward passes. The coarse-grained representation (single virtual beta-carbon per residue rather than full backbone frames) and the non-redundant pre-training data are both shown via ablation to be critical for generalization beyond the training distribution.

## Results

On 5 non-leaking held-out PPIs from SKEMPI v2.0, PPIFORMER achieves a Spearman correlation of 0.44±0.03, outperforming all machine-learning baselines in 6 of 7 metrics (second-best only in recall at 0.61 vs. RDE-Network's 0.65). This represents a 183% relative improvement in mutation ranking over the prior state-of-the-art supervised RDE-Network (Spearman 0.24). Notably, the traditional force-field simulator FLEX DDG still leads with Spearman 0.55, but is ~5 orders of magnitude slower (1 CPU-hour vs. 73 GPU-ms per mutation). In independent case studies, PPIFORMER achieves P@1=100% on both the SARS-CoV-2 antibody optimization (mean favorable-mutation rank 11.60%) and staphylokinase thrombolytic engineering (P@10%=87.5% for ≥2× activity-enhancing mutations).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FLEX DDG (Rosetta force-field simulation), PPIFORMER loses on every metric (Spearman 0.44 vs. 0.55, ROC AUC 0.78 vs. 0.84), but runs ~5 orders of magnitude faster (73 GPU-ms vs. ~1 CPU-hour per mutation), making it practical for large-scale mutational screening. Against RDE-Network (the prior SOTA supervised deep-learning method), PPIFORMER wins on Spearman (0.44 vs. 0.24), Pearson (0.46 vs. 0.30), Precision (0.60 vs. 0.54), ROC AUC (0.78 vs. 0.67), MAE (1.64 vs. 1.70), and RMSE (1.94 vs. 2.02), but slightly loses on Recall (0.61 vs. 0.65). Against GEMME (evolutionary non-learning method), PPIFORMER wins on Spearman (0.44 vs. 0.38), Pearson, ROC AUC, MAE, and RMSE, ties on Precision (0.60), and wins on Recall (0.61 vs. 0.49). Against MSA Transformer and ESM-IF (sequence-based and inverse-folding baselines respectively), PPIFORMER dominates across all seven metrics, with the largest gaps on MAE (1.64 vs. 6.13 for MSA Transformer) and Spearman (0.44 vs. 0.18 for ESM-IF). The paper also notes that prior PDB-disjoint splits used by RDE-Network and others contain 53–88% data leakage, meaning those baselines' previously reported performance was likely overestimated.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-supervised pre-training paradigm for protein design from sequence-based (ESM, MSA Transformer) and atom-level (RDE-Network, ESM-IF) approaches to a coarse-grained, SE(3)-equivariant transformer operating on residue-level PPI graphs. Architecturally, it builds directly on Equiformer's equivariant graph attention blocks, adapting them to the PPI domain with a virtual-beta-carbon representation that deliberately avoids full-frame side-chain geometry. The fine-tuning loss is a thermodynamically motivated adaptation of the log-odds ratio introduced by Meier et al. (2021) for protein language models, here applied to 3D structure-conditioned amino acid probabilities. A key departure from prior work is the identification of pervasive data leakage in standard SKEMPI v2.0 splits and the construction of PPIRef, a 7× larger non-redundant PPI dataset enabled by the new iDist structural deduplication algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The revelation that 53–88% of test examples in widely used PPI benchmark splits share near-duplicate structures with training data fundamentally undermines prior performance claims in the field and motivates a re-evaluation of how ΔΔG predictors are assessed. PPIRef (322K structures, 46K unique interfaces) and the iDist algorithm (480× faster than iAlign at 99% precision) provide practical infrastructure for large-scale PPI learning that was previously infeasible. However, the paper candidly shows that on non-leaking splits, force-field simulations (flex ddG) still outperform all ML methods in predictive accuracy, and the labeled mutational data remains limited to ~7K annotations across 343 PPIs. Open questions include whether scaling PPIFORMER to foundation-model size with PPIRef can close the gap to physics-based methods, and whether the coarse-grained representation sacrifices accuracy for generalizability in ways that matter for specific design applications.
