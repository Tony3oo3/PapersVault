## Problem

DPSGD with gradient clipping (DPSGD-GC) introduces a constant bias that prevents convergence to the true optimum, and existing convergence guarantees require clipping thresholds tuned to problem-specific parameters (gradient bounds, variance) that are unknown in practice. This paper asks whether one can design a DP-SGD variant that eliminates the clipping bias entirely while allowing an arbitrary, problem-independent clipping threshold.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241556/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a sensitive dataset of N samples, an initial parameter vector, two clipping thresholds (C1 for per-sample gradients, C2 for the accumulated feedback error, with C2 ≥ 3C1 + σ/B), a stepsize, and a DP noise multiplier. It outputs trained model parameters with a formal (ε,δ)-DP guarantee. Unusually, the algorithm maintains a hidden feedback-error state e^t that is never released to the public; only the model parameter x^t is released at each iteration, and the DP noise must be slightly larger than in standard DPSGD to account for the accumulated influence of this hidden state.

## Method

DiceSGD introduces a clipped error-feedback mechanism: at each iteration, the update direction is the sum of the averaged clipped per-sample gradient and the clipped accumulated feedback error, where the feedback error e^{t+1} = e^t + (1/B)Σ∇f(x^t;ξ_i) − v^t records the discrepancy between unclipped and clipped gradients. The critical design choice is that e^t is clipped (with threshold C2) before being added to the update, rather than fed back directly, which preserves the per-iteration sensitivity bound required for DP. The convergence analysis shows that under standard smoothness and bounded-variance assumptions, DiceSGD achieves O(1/√T) gradient-norm convergence with no constant bias term, matching the rate of DPSGD without clipping, for any fixed C1, C2 independent of problem constants. The privacy analysis uses Rényi DP with a novel composition argument that separately accounts for the privatized public state (x^t) and the non-privatized hidden state (e^t), yielding an (ε,δ)-DP guarantee with noise variance O(Ḡ·T·log(1/δ)/(N²ε²)) where Ḡ depends only on the clipping thresholds, not on gradient bounds.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with (2, 10⁻⁵)-DP, DiceSGD achieves 97.4–97.5% test accuracy (clipping C=1.0 or 0.1), outperforming DPSGD-GC (95.2% and 94.5%) by 2.2–3.0 points and approaching the non-private SGD upper bound of 99.0%. On CIFAR-100 under the same privacy budget, DiceSGD reaches 86.3–86.5% versus 78.9–79.0% for DPSGD-GC, a gap of over 7 points. On the E2E NLG Challenge (GPT-2, (8, 8×10⁻⁶)-DP), DiceSGD improves BLEU from 56.8 to 62.6 and ROUGE-L from 65.2 to 66.6 over DPSGD-GC, closing the gap to the non-private SGD baseline (BLEU 70.4) substantially.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiceSGD consistently outperforms DPSGD-GC (Abadi et al., 2016) across all evaluated tasks and clipping thresholds. On CIFAR-10, DiceSGD gains 2.2–3.0 percentage points in accuracy; on CIFAR-100 the margin widens to 7.3–7.6 points, indicating the clipping-bias elimination is more impactful on harder tasks. On the E2E NLG Challenge, DiceSGD improves BLEU by 5.8 points (56.8→62.6) and NIST by 2.22 points (4.83→7.05) over DPSGD-GC. The non-private SGD baseline (Hu et al., 2021) serves as an upper bound: DiceSGD narrows the privacy-utility gap substantially (e.g., 97.4% vs. 99.0% on CIFAR-10; BLEU 62.6 vs. 70.4 on NLP), whereas DPSGD-GC lags further behind (95.2% and 56.8 respectively). Notably, DiceSGD's performance is robust to the clipping threshold choice (C=0.1 vs. C=1.0 yields nearly identical results), while DPSGD-GC degrades with smaller C, confirming the theoretical prediction that DiceSGD eliminates the constant clipping bias.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two previously separate threads: the standard DPSGD-GC framework (Abadi et al., 2016) for differentially private training, and the error-feedback (EF) mechanism originally developed for debiasing gradient compression (Karimireddy et al., 2019). It departs from prior convergence analyses of DPSGD-GC—which all require problem-dependent clipping thresholds and, as shown by Koloskova et al. (2023), inevitably leave a constant bias—by introducing a clipped EF mechanism that accumulates and feeds back the per-sample clipping error. The DP analysis also departs from standard sequential-composition arguments because the feedback error e^t is a non-privatized hidden state, requiring a novel Rényi-DP treatment that accounts for its accumulated influence across iterations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiceSGD addresses a practical pain point in DP training: the need to carefully tune the clipping threshold based on unknown problem constants, and the resulting constant accuracy degradation. By making the clipping threshold problem-independent and eliminating the constant bias, it offers a simpler and more robust alternative to DPSGD-GC. Limitations include a slightly larger DP noise requirement (a constant multiplicative factor over DPSGD-GC), convergence guarantees that still rely on bounded-gradient and bounded-variance assumptions, and empirical validation limited to two image datasets and one NLP task. Open questions include whether the clipped EF mechanism extends naturally to federated DP settings, whether the noise overhead can be tightened, and whether the approach remains effective at much larger scales (e.g., ImageNet, LLM pre-training).
