## Problem

Graph contrastive learning (GCL) suffers from two scalability bottlenecks: message passing in GNN encoders grows exponentially with graph size, and the contrastive loss requires computing O(n²) sample pairs. Existing scalable GNN techniques—graph sampling and diffusion-matrix decoupling—are inapplicable to GCL because sampling degrades positive/negative pair quality and data augmentation prevents precomputing the diffusion matrix. No unified training framework existed for scaling arbitrary GCL models.

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{n×n} and node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×d} as input. During training, these are compressed via a METIS partition matrix P' into X_c = P^T X ∈ R^{n'×d} and A_c = P^T AP, where n' ≪ n is the number of clusters; the encoder operates only on these compressed representations. At inference, the full graph structure and original node features are fed through the GNN encoder (with parameters transferred from the trained MLP) to produce node embeddings Z ∈ R^{n×d'} for all n nodes.

## Method

StructComp replaces GNN message passing during training with a sparse low-rank approximation of the diffusion matrix: a METIS graph partition matrix P' ∈ {0,1}^{n×n'} defines clusters, and nodes within each cluster are merged into a single 'mixed node' via X_c = P^T X. The GNN encoder is swapped for an MLP that processes only the n' compressed nodes, reducing the contrastive loss computation from O(n²) pairs to O(n'²) pairs while eliminating all message-passing cost. For multi-view GCL, a novel DropMember augmentation randomly drops nodes within each cluster to generate perturbed views, replacing inapplicable edge-based augmentations like DropEdge. Theoretically, the paper proves the compressed contrastive loss approximates the original GCL loss within a bound proportional to ‖A − P'P'ᵀ‖_F (Theorem 4.1), and that the augmentation introduces an implicit regularization term that improves encoder robustness (Theorem 4.2). At inference, MLP weights are transferred back to the GNN encoder, which then performs full message passing on the complete graph to produce final node embeddings.

## Results

On small-scale datasets (Cora, Citeseer, Pubmed, Amazon Computers, Amazon Photo), StructComp improves average classification accuracy by +0.4% (SCE), +0.2% (COLES), +2.6% (GRACE), and +1.6% (CCA-SSG) over full graph training, while reducing memory by 5.9×–57.1× and training time by 1.8×–21.1×. On large-scale Ogbn-Arxiv and Ogbn-Products, StructComp achieves the best accuracy across all four GCL models (e.g., 75.2% for SCE, 75.7% for GRACE on Products) while using 2.7–12.0 GB memory and 0.05–4.0 s/epoch, substantially outperforming Cluster-GCN, GraphSAINT, and GraphZoom in both accuracy and resource efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three scalable training baselines on large graphs, StructComp consistently wins: on Ogbn-Products, it achieves 75.2% (SCE) vs. 74.6% (Cluster-GCN), 74.5% (GraphSAINT), and 60.6% (GraphZoom), while using only 2.7 GB memory and 0.05 s/epoch compared to 8.0 GB/196.8 s (Cluster-GCN) and 9.5 GB/18.5 s (GraphSAINT). On Ogbn-Arxiv, StructComp reaches 71.6% (SCE) vs. 70.4–70.6% for the other methods, with 1.8 GB memory and 0.03 s/epoch. Against recent GCL baselines (Table 12), StructComp-trained models outperform BlockGCL (78.1%), SP-GCL (81.4%), Contrast-Reg (79.2%), and GGD (79.9%) on Cora accuracy while using 3–10× less memory and 2–50× less time per epoch. The paper notes these comparisons are somewhat orthogonal since StructComp is a training framework rather than a new GCL model, and it can in principle be applied to SP-GCL, BlockGCL, Contrast-Reg, and GGD as well.

## Relation to Prior Work

StructComp departs from the two dominant paradigms for scalable GNN training—subgraph sampling (GraphSAINT, Cluster-GCN) and diffusion-decoupling (SGCN, APPNP)—both of which the authors argue are inapplicable to GCL due to data augmentation perturbing the adjacency matrix. It also distinguishes itself from graph coarsening methods like GraphZoom, which the paper notes are designed for traditional graph embedding rather than GCL and suffer from information loss in the coarse-to-refine pipeline. Conceptually, StructComp repurposes the sparse low-rank approximation of the diffusion matrix (via METIS graph partitioning) not as a preprocessing step but as a training-time substitute for message passing, reducing the encoder to an MLP on compressed community features. The DropMember augmentation is a novel contribution specifically designed to enable multi-view GCL under this compressed representation, filling a gap left by standard augmentations like DropEdge that require an explicit adjacency matrix.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: on OGBN-Papers100M (111M nodes), StructComp trains in ~0.2s per epoch versus 1.6h for GGD+ClusterGCN, a roughly 30,000× speedup, while matching or exceeding accuracy. The theoretical guarantee that the compressed contrastive loss approximates the original loss (bounded by the partition remainder norm) and the implicit regularization effect provide principled justification beyond empirical observation. Open questions include: how well the framework handles heterophilic graphs (the paper's experiments are preliminary), whether the compression rate hyperparameter can be made adaptive, and whether the approach extends to other self-supervised graph learning paradigms beyond contrastive learning. The reliance on METIS for partitioning, while effective, introduces a preprocessing dependency that may not scale trivially to dynamic or streaming graphs.
