## Problem

Existing multi-agent cooperation research either presupposes a cost-free communication channel or relies on a centralized controller with shared observations, leaving open whether LLMs can drive decentralized embodied agents that must perceive raw sensory input, maintain world state, decide when and what to communicate under time costs, and plan collaboratively over long horizons. This paper formalizes this as a DEC-POMDP-COM problem and investigates whether LLMs possess the reasoning and language capabilities necessary for such distributed embodied cooperation.

## Input / Output

Each agent receives egocentric 512×512 RGB-D images (TDW-MAT) or 256×512 RGB-D images (C-WAH), teammate messages (≤500 characters per message), task predicates with counts (e.g., ON(plate, dinnertable):2), and its own position/rotation. The Perception Module converts raw pixels into a 2D top-down semantic map (L×W×3 channels: semantic class, occupied, explored) via Mask-RCNN segmentation and 3D point-cloud projection. Outputs are primitive environment actions (move, turn, grasp, put-in, drop), natural-language broadcast messages, and high-level plans selected from a compiled Action List; communication is costly since sending a message consumes a timestep just like any other action.

## Method

CoELA is a cognitive-inspired modular architecture with five modules: Perception (Mask-RCNN + 3D point clouds → semantic map), Memory (Semantic for world/task/other-agent state, Episodic for action and dialogue history, Procedural for environment-specific code), Communication (LLM generates a candidate message 'in mind' conditioned on retrieved memory), Planning (LLM with zero-shot chain-of-thought selects from a compiled multiple-choice Action List, including the option to send the pre-generated message), and Execution (A-star navigation + procedural rules convert high-level plans to primitive actions). The key design insight is the two-step communication: the LLM first drafts a message, then the Planning Module decides whether sending it is the best action versus executing a physical plan, making communication an explicit, costly decision rather than a free channel. The framework is instantiated with GPT-4 (temperature 0.7) for the LLM-driven modules, and a fine-tuned CoLLAMA (LLAMA-2-13b-chat with LoRA on 572 human-filtered trajectories) achieves competitive performance, demonstrating the architecture's portability across LLM backbones.

## Results

On TDW-MAT, CoELA driven by GPT-4 achieves a Transport Rate of 0.71 (39% efficiency improvement over RHP alone) when two CoELA agents cooperate, outperforming RHP+RHP (0.61, 29% EI) and RHP+CoELA (0.69, 36% EI). With oracle perception, CoELA+CoELA reaches 0.85 TR (41% EI). On C-WAH, CoELA+CoELA reduces average steps to 57 (49% improvement) under symbolic observation and 92 (34% improvement) under visual observation, compared to MHP+MHP at 75 (33%) and 103 (26%) respectively. The fine-tuned CoLLAMA (LoRA on LLAMA-2-13b) recovers most of GPT-4's performance, reaching 0.70 TR on TDW-MAT (38% EI).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoELA consistently outperforms both planning-based baselines. On TDW-MAT, RHP+CoELA achieves 36% efficiency improvement over RHP alone, compared to only 29% for RHP+RHP, and CoELA+CoELA further boosts this to 39%. On C-WAH, MHP+CoELA achieves 45% improvement (57 steps) versus 33% for MHP+MHP (75 steps) under symbolic observation, with similar gains under visual observation (34% vs 26%). The MARL baseline MAT, which relies on centralized observation and oracle perception, performs poorly (0.15 TR with oracle perception on TDW-MAT), highlighting the difficulty of long-horizon sparse-reward settings for centralized approaches. The fine-tuned CoLLAMA narrows the gap with GPT-4 substantially (0.70 vs 0.71 TR), even surpassing GPT-4 on the Stuff subtask (0.66 vs 0.61), while raw LLAMA-2-13b-chat underperforms significantly (0.53 TR, only 10% EI).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cognitive architecture tradition (Soar, ACT-R) by replacing hand-designed modules with LLM-driven components, and extends prior LLM-based embodied agent work (Huang et al. 2022, Song et al. 2022, Liang et al. 2022) from single-agent planning to decentralized multi-agent cooperation. It departs from prior multi-agent communication research (Lowe et al. 2017, Puig et al. 2021, Wen et al. 2022) by requiring agents to use natural language under costly communication constraints rather than presupposing free channels or centralized observation sharing. The DEC-POMDP-COM formalization and the two-step 'generate message in mind, then decide whether to send' communication protocol are the key methodological departures from both the MARL and LLM-agent literatures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that LLMs can serve as the reasoning backbone for cooperative embodied agents in realistic decentralized settings, and the user study provides early evidence that natural-language communication builds human trust in AI partners (6.3 vs. 4.7 on a 7-point scale). Key limitations remain: LLMs struggle with 3D spatial reasoning, low-level action planning, and stable counting, and the AI-to-AI communication benefit is surprisingly small compared to the AI-to-human benefit. Open questions include whether fine-tuned open LLMs (CoLLAMA) can close the gap with GPT-4 at scale, how to incorporate spatial information into text-based LLMs, and whether the framework generalizes beyond two agents and small indoor environments.
