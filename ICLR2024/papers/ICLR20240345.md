## Problem

Existing distance-based OOD detection methods model each in-distribution class with a single prototype on the hypersphere, which oversimplifies the natural diversity within classes and forces all samples to be compact around one point. This leads to non-compact embeddings and confusion between ID and OOD samples at test time, as diverse sub-patterns within a class cannot be faithfully represented. PALM addresses this gap by modeling each class with a mixture of multiple prototypes to better capture intra-class variation and improve ID-OOD separability.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet-100) and produces a per-sample OOD detection score via Mahalanobis or KNN distance computed on penultimate-layer ResNet embeddings. Internally, a two-layer MLP projector maps the high-dimensional encoder output to a 128-dimensional unit hypersphere where K=6 prototypes per class are maintained as 128-d unit vectors. These prototypes serve as the anchors for the mixture vMF distribution and are the key structural elements distinguishing PALM from single-prototype baselines.

## Method

PALM models each class's hyperspherical embedding space as a mixture of K von Mises-Fisher distributions (one per prototype) rather than a single vMF, capturing multiple sub-clusters within each class. Samples are softly assigned to prototypes within their class by solving an entropy-regularized optimal transport problem via the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm, followed by top-K pruning to remove weakly relevant assignments. Training optimizes two losses: an MLE loss that pulls each sample toward its assigned prototypes, and a prototype-level contrastive loss that enforces intra-class compactness and inter-class separation among all prototypes simultaneously. Prototypes are updated asynchronously via exponential moving average (EMA) to maintain stable cluster assignments across iterations, and the framework extends to unsupervised OOD detection by dropping class labels and adopting a SwAV-style swapped assignment with data augmentation.

## Results

On the standard CIFAR-100 ID / 5 OOD datasets benchmark (ResNet-34), PALM achieves a state-of-the-art average AUROC of 93.82 and average FPR@95 of 28.02, outperforming the closest competitor CIDER (AUROC 89.34, FPR 40.85) by 4.48 AUROC points and 12.83 FPR points. The largest gains appear on SVHN (FPR 3.29 vs. 12.55 for CIDER) and LSUN (FPR 9.86 vs. 30.24). In the unsupervised setting (unlabeled CIFAR-100), PALM reaches an average AUROC of 88.07 and FPR of 43.37, surpassing CSI (AUROC 86.11) and SimCLR+KNN (AUROC 76.56).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PALM outperforms all 12 baselines on average AUROC and FPR in the supervised CIFAR-100 setting. Against the most closely related method CIDER (which also uses vMF-prototype modeling but with a single prototype per class), PALM reduces average FPR by 12.83 points (40.85→28.02) and improves AUROC by 4.48 points (89.34→93.82), with the largest per-dataset gain on SVHN (FPR 12.55→3.29). Compared to NPOS, which synthesizes artificial OOD samples during training, PALM achieves lower FPR (28.02 vs. 32.01) and substantially higher AUROC (93.82 vs. 89.84) without any OOD data generation. Against contrastive-learning-based distance methods SSD+ and kNN+, PALM more than halves the average FPR (28.02 vs. 67.16 and 60.22 respectively) while improving AUROC by ~8 points. In the unsupervised setting, PALM (AUROC 88.07) beats CSI (86.11) and SimCLR+KNN (76.56), and notably matches or exceeds its own supervised counterparts NPOS and CIDER on several OOD datasets despite having no label information.

## Relation to Prior Work

PALM extends the hyperspherical embedding framework for OOD detection established by CIDER (Ming et al., 2023) and SIREN (Du et al., 2022a), which model each class with a single von Mises-Fisher prototype. The key departure is replacing this single-prototype assumption with a mixture of K prototypes per class, using Sinkhorn-Knopp soft assignment (borrowed from SwAV, Caron et al., 2020) to dynamically assign samples to their most relevant prototypes, and adding a prototype-level contrastive loss inspired by SupCon (Khosla et al., 2020). Unlike NPOS (Tao et al., 2023), which synthesizes artificial OOD samples to regularize the decision boundary, PALM achieves comparable or better performance purely through more faithful modeling of intra-class diversity in the embedding space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mixture-of-prototypes formulation addresses a fundamental limitation in distance-based OOD detection: forcing all samples of a class into a single compact cluster ignores natural intra-class diversity, leading to non-compact embeddings and ID-OOD confusion. The practical impact is significant—PALM achieves 93.82 average AUROC on CIFAR-100 with a 12.83% FPR reduction over the closest competitor CIDER, and extends naturally to unsupervised OOD detection without labels. The main open question is the manual selection of the number of prototypes K as a hyperparameter, which the authors acknowledge limits applicability to complex scenarios where different classes may require different numbers of prototypes; they suggest nonparametric Bayesian approaches as future work.
