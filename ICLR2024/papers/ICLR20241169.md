## Problem

Existing visual representation learning for robotics relies on proxy tasks (temporal cycle-consistency, behavior cloning, dynamics prediction) that bypass the end-user's input on what actually matters for the task, leading to spurious correlations and robot behaviors misaligned with user preferences. The paper addresses the gap of how to align a robot's visual representation with the end-user's preferences using only easy-to-provide preference feedback on video demonstrations, without requiring action labels or hand-crafted reward signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241169/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two types of input: (1) video triplets where a human ranks three short video clips by preference (e.g., 150 triplets for representation training), and (2) a set of 10 expert video demonstrations used to construct the OT-based reward. The visual backbone is a ResNet-18 (pre-trained on ImageNet) whose final linear layer is fine-tuned to produce 32-dimensional embeddings. The output is a frozen aligned representation used to compute an entropy-regularized optimal transport reward, which is then fed into Soft Actor-Critic to train a robot policy that maps RGB observations to actions.

## Method

RAPL formalizes visual representation alignment as a triplet-based metric learning problem: the robot's encoder φ_R is trained to minimize divergence from the human's latent representation space S_φH, approximated via a finite set of preference triplets. Human preferences over triplets are modeled with the Bradley-Terry model, where the distance between two video embedding trajectories is computed as the entropy-regularized Wasserstein distance (solved via Sinkhorn) between their empirical frame-level embedding distributions. The key architectural choice is to dedicate all preference feedback exclusively to fine-tuning the representation (only the last linear layer of ResNet-18), while the reward structure is fixed as the negative OT transport cost between the robot's and expert's embedding distributions. This decoupling—representation alignment via preferences, reward via OT feature matching—avoids the sample inefficiency and causal confusion of directly regressing a scalar reward from high-dimensional preference queries, and enables zero-shot cross-embodiment transfer since the aligned representation captures task-relevant features independent of the specific robot morphology.

## Results

In the IsaacGym Franka manipulation task, RAPL achieves ~70% success rate (matching the GT oracle) while all baselines (RLHF, MVP-OT, Fine-Tuned-MVP-OT, R3M-OT, ImageNet-OT, TCC-OT) fail to exceed 10%. In X-Magical, RAPL matches GT at ~80% (avoid) and ~60% (grouping) success with substantially better sample efficiency (reaching 70% in 10 epochs vs. GT's 100). RAPL also achieves ~65% success in a complex cluttered manipulation task with visual distractors, and demonstrates zero-shot cross-embodiment transfer (Franka→Kuka) at comparable success rates to GT while baselines drop to near 0%. With only 25 preference queries, RAPL still achieves 45% success in manipulation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAPL decisively outperforms all five baselines in the IsaacGym manipulation setting (~70% success vs. <10% for all), with the gap most dramatic against RLHF (which achieves near-zero true success despite its learned reward perceiving high performance). In X-Magical, RAPL matches GT and significantly outperforms TCC (Zakka et al., 2022), which incorrectly rewards spatial progress that violates user preferences (e.g., entering off-limits zones). Against the OT-based baselines (MVP-OT, R3M-OT, TCC-OT, ImageNet-OT), RAPL's advantage comes from its preference-aligned representation: the OT coupling matrices show clean diagonal peaks for preferred videos and uniform values for disliked ones under RAPL, while all other representations produce diffused, uninformative couplings. In cross-embodiment transfer (Franka→Kuka), RAPL maintains ~70% success while all baselines collapse to near 0%, confirming that preference-aligned representations capture embodiment-agnostic task features. RAPL also outperforms RLHF by 75% in success rate while using 50% fewer preference queries (150 vs. 300 triplets), and achieves 45% success with as few as 25 queries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) optimal transport-based visual rewards for robot imitation (Haldar et al. 2023a,b; Guzey et al. 2023; Dadashi et al. 2021), which previously required proxy tasks like behavior cloning or temporal cycle-consistency to fine-tune representations; (2) preference-based reward learning (Christiano et al. 2017; Bobu et al. 2023a,b), which typically maps observations directly to scalar rewards; and (3) the cognitive science formulation of representation alignment via triplet similarity (Sucholutsky & Griffiths 2023). RAPL's key departure is using preference-based human feedback exclusively for representation alignment (not reward prediction), then leveraging the aligned representation with an OT-based reward, thereby eliminating the need for action labels or proxy tasks that prior OT-based methods required.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in deploying visual robot rewards: how to ensure the learned representation captures what the end-user actually cares about, rather than spurious correlations. The finding that direct preference-based reward prediction (RLHF) fails in more complex manipulation environments while preference-based representation alignment succeeds provides a mechanistic explanation (attention to irrelevant features) that could inform broader preference-learning research. However, all experiments use a simulated human in simulation, leaving open whether real human preference feedback is sufficiently consistent and informative for this approach, and whether the method extends to online adaptation or multi-user settings.
