## Problem

Existing time series disentanglement methods (β-VAE, TCVAE, FactorVAE) enforce strict statistical independence among latent attributes, an assumption that fails for real-world signals where attributes are correlated (e.g., appliances used simultaneously with overlapping consumption profiles). This independence assumption leads to non-identifiability and poor out-of-distribution generalization. The paper asks whether a weaker condition—Independence-of-Support (IOS) on the prior—combined with contrastive learning is sufficient to provably identify and disentangle correlated time series attributes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a C-variate household electricity time series x ∈ R^{C×T} (C channels of active, reactive, and apparent power sampled at 60 Hz over a fixed T=256-step window) and produces a disentangled latent matrix z ∈ R^{d_z×(M+K)} with d_z=16 per-appliance latent blocks, from which the decoder reconstructs each appliance's power contribution y_m ∈ R^T. Training uses four sequential augmentations (crop/delay, temporal cutout, channel masking, Gaussian noise) applied to both the input and the per-appliance targets, and the model is evaluated under controlled correlation scenarios parameterized by a coupling strength σ between appliance pairs.

## Method

DIOSC replaces the standard independence prior with an Independence-of-Support constraint enforced through a pairwise contrastive objective with two terms: a latent-invariant term that pushes z_m away from negative samples z_m⁻, and a latent-alignment term that pulls z_m toward its augmented version z_m⁺, both measured by cosine distance. This is embedded in a deep l-Variational Autoencoder (L=16 residual levels) where each level's posterior is a Gaussian residual over the prior, and temporal multihead self-attention replaces local convolutions to capture long-range dependencies without temporal locality. The paper proves (Theorem 1) that under IOS and a positivity condition, the learned encoder achieves block-identifiability up to permutation, translation, and scaling. The full training objective balances the DIOSC contrastive loss (weight λ=2.3), reconstruction, and a multi-level KL divergence (weight β=1.5), with SiLU activations and Squeeze-and-Excitation blocks in the residual architecture.

## Results

On the primary Scenario A (train REFIT, test UK-DALE), DIOSC achieves the lowest RMSE across all correlation levels: 19.6 ± 1.95 (no correlation), 22.3 ± 1.79 (pair σ=0.3), 23.97 ± 1.19 (pair σ=0.4), and 22.63 ± 1.49 (random σ=0.8). This represents up to a 61.4% RMSE improvement over the Bert4NILM baseline (56.4) and a 19.3% improvement over the strongest VAE variant (β-TCVAE+HFS at 24.3). On disentanglement metrics, DIOSC achieves the best TDS (0.49 vs. 0.52 for β-TCVAE+HFS) and competitive DCI (63.5 vs. 67.2), with the gap narrowing under correlation where DIOSC's TDS drops to 0.40 while baselines remain at 0.52.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIOSC outperforms all five baselines on RMSE across every correlation scenario and dataset. Against the strongest VAE baseline (β-TCVAE, Chen et al. 2018c), DIOSC reduces RMSE from 43.2 to 19.6 (no correlation) and from 49.2 to 22.3 (pair σ=0.3), a ~55% relative improvement. FactorVAE (Kim & Mnih, 2019) shows similar degradation under correlation (47.7→53.2), while DIOSC degrades only modestly (19.6→22.3). The Hausdorff Factorized Support method (Roth et al., 2023) alone performs poorly (57.2 RMSE), but when combined with β-TCVAE it reaches 24.3—still 19% worse than DIOSC. The non-VAE baselines Bert4NILM and S2S (Chen et al., 2018a) are the weakest, with RMSE around 54–56 in the uncorrelated case and degrading to ~70 under correlation, confirming that methods assuming attribute independence fail most severely when real-world correlations are present.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NVAE/Deep Attentive Variational Inference architecture (Vahdat & Kautz, 2020; Apostolopoulou et al., 2021) by replacing local attention with temporal multihead self-attention and introducing a new contrastive regularizer (DIOSC) that enforces Independence-of-Support rather than full statistical independence of latent factors. It departs from the β-VAE/TCVAE/FactorVAE lineage, which assumes factor independence and suffers non-identifiability on correlated data, and from Roth et al. (2023) HFS, which uses Hausdorff factorized support but remains disconnected from observational data. The contrastive identifiability framework draws on Nonlinear ICA theory (Jutten et al., 2010; Hyvärinen, 2013) and Zimmermann et al. (2022), but is the first to apply it to time series under correlated-attribute conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: real-world time series (e.g., household appliance consumption) exhibit strong inter-attribute correlation that invalidates the independence assumption underlying most disentanglement methods. The theoretical identifiability guarantee under the weaker IOS condition, combined with the practical TDS evaluation metric, provides both a principled framework and a tool for the field. Limitations include the batch-size-dependent empirical relaxation of IOS (Asm. 4.1), the single-encoder/decoder design that may not scale to very large appliance counts, and evaluation confined to the electricity/NILM domain. Future work on causal support factorization for time series and broader domain validation would strengthen the contribution.
