## Problem

Existing hybrid learning methods for Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) combine local (STDP) and global (STBP) learning but do not account for the network conditions under which each mode is most effective, failing to simultaneously achieve high accuracy and low energy consumption. The paper asks how to dynamically and biologically plausibly switch between these two learning regimes based on the network's own connectivity state.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240730/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts either standard RGB images (32×32×3 for CIFAR-10/100) or event streams (128×128×2 with timestamp and polarity for DVS-CIFAR10) and outputs a class label. Internally, inputs are processed by integrate-and-fire neurons over a temporal window T, and the final prediction is the time-averaged output across all T steps. A secondary output is the network sparsity (fraction of zero-valued weights), which EIHL actively controls via a tunable target-sparsity parameter b.

## Method

EIHL introduces an excitation-inhibition mechanism that uses the network's current sparsity as a macroscopic proxy for its connectivity state, dynamically alternating between STBP (excited/global) and STDP (inhibited/local) learning. During the inhibited (STDP) phase, a contraction function H(x) = thresh(b)·x·α progressively shrinks weights below a moving boundary toward zero, with the update direction at the boundary fixed to only decrease, smoothly accelerating LTD-driven disconnection. When the current sparsity reaches the target threshold b, the network is deemed over-inhibited and switches to STBP; conversely, when sparsity stops decreasing during STBP (over-excitation), it reverts to STDP. Each transition into STBP increments b by a fixed step, so the sparsity ceiling ratchets upward over training, yielding a progressively sparser network. This design is validated on Spiking ResNet18 and Spiking VGG11 across three datasets, where EIHL matches or exceeds STBP accuracy while achieving 13–32% weight sparsity that neither pure STDP nor STBP can produce.

## Results

On Spiking ResNet18, EIHL achieves 90.25% accuracy on CIFAR-10 (vs. 89.53% STBP, 76.71% STDP) with 17.17% sparsity, 58.63% on CIFAR-100 (vs. 58.48% STBP, 32.66% STDP) with 31.75% sparsity, and 62.9% on DVS-CIFAR10 (vs. 60.2% STBP, 36.0% STDP) with 11.42% sparsity. On Spiking VGG11, EIHL reaches 62.45% on DVS-CIFAR10, outperforming STBP by 4.35% and STDP by 11.40%, while maintaining 18.74% sparsity. All baselines (STDP, STBP) report 0% sparsity, whereas EIHL consistently achieves 11–32% sparsity depending on dataset and architecture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against STDP (local learning), EIHL wins decisively on accuracy across all six dataset–model combinations (e.g., +25.97% on CIFAR-100 ResNet18, +11.40% on DVS-CIFAR10 VGG11) while also introducing 11–32% sparsity that STDP lacks. Against STBP (global learning), EIHL matches or slightly exceeds accuracy on most settings (e.g., +0.15% on CIFAR-100 ResNet18, +2.7% on DVS-CIFAR10 ResNet18, +4.35% on DVS-CIFAR10 VGG11) and ties on CIFAR-10 VGG11 (85.75% vs. 85.76%), while consistently adding 11–32% sparsity. Against the hybrid method EICIL (Shao et al., NeurIPS 2024), EIHL achieves higher accuracy on both models (90.25% vs. 89.32% GSI / 88.95% GSGI on ResNet18; 85.75% vs. 85.63% GSI / 85.66% GSGI on VGG11) and uniquely provides 13–17% sparsity, whereas EICIL reports 0% sparsity. Overall, EIHL's key differentiator is combining near-STBP accuracy with substantial structural sparsity, a property neither pure local, pure global, nor the EICIL hybrid baseline achieves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hybrid learning line initiated by Wu et al. (2022), who proposed Hybrid Plasticity (HP) using two parallel weight sets for local (STDP) and global (STBP) updates. EIHL departs from HP's additive weight-fusion strategy by instead using a macroscopic excitation-inhibition mechanism—monitoring network sparsity as a proxy for the network's excitation/inhibition state—to dynamically *switch* between STDP and STBP rather than running them simultaneously. The biological grounding draws on three-factor learning rules and cortical E/I balance literature, but the key conceptual move is treating sparsity thresholds as the switching criterion, which is a repurposing of homeostatic plasticity ideas at the network level rather than the synaptic level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is the simultaneous gain in accuracy (comparable to or slightly above STBP) and sparsity (13–31%), which is directly relevant to deploying SNNs on energy-constrained neuromorphic hardware where dense connectivity is costly. However, the experimental scope is narrow: only CIFAR10/100 and DVS-CIFAR10 with two architectures, no statistical significance testing, and the accuracy margins over STBP are small (often <1%) and sometimes negative (e.g., VGG11 on CIFAR10). Open questions include whether the sparsity-accuracy tradeoff holds at ImageNet scale, whether the fixed step-size schedule for the target sparsity generalizes, and whether the excitation-inhibition switching logic transfers to ANN training as the authors speculate.
