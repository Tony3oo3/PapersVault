## Problem

Existing deep generative models for time series assume regularity and uniformity in underlying patterns, making them ineffective for financial time series where recurring dynamics repeat with varying duration and magnitude (scale-invariance) and non-deterministic intervals (irregularity). This gap is critical because financial data is inherently limited by historical availability and high noise, so synthesizing realistic augmentations is essential for training reliable deep learning models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes univariate financial return series (daily closing-price changes) and an initial segment sampled from historical data to bootstrap generation. It produces synthetic return series of arbitrary total length, constructed as a concatenation of variable-length segments (between 10 and 21 time steps) whose shapes correspond to learned scale-invariant patterns, each scaled by a duration factor α and magnitude factor β determined by a Markov transition. Unlike baselines that generate fixed-length subsequences, FTS-Diffusion outputs segments of heterogeneous lengths that are stitched together sequentially.

## Method

FTS-Diffusion decomposes financial time series generation into three stages. First, a Scale-Invariant Subsequence Clustering (SISC) algorithm performs greedy segmentation using dynamic time warping (DTW) to find optimal variable-length segments at each position, then clusters them via K-means with a distance-proportional initialization to identify K scale-invariant pattern centroids. Second, a pattern-conditioned DDPM diffusion network (six residual TCN blocks, N=100 steps) learns to denoise pattern representations conditioned on the learned centroids, jointly trained with a two-layer LSTM/GRU scaling autoencoder that maps between variable-length segments and fixed-length pattern-space representations. Third, a fully-connected pattern evolution network predicts the next (pattern, α, β) state given the current one, modeling the Markov transition between consecutive segments. Generation proceeds iteratively: the evolution network selects the next state, the diffusion network and AE decoder produce the corresponding segment, and the segment is appended until the target series length is reached.

## Results

On distribution fidelity, FTS-Diffusion achieves the highest KS and AD statistics across all three assets (e.g., KS=0.327, AD=0.128 on S&P 500), substantially outperforming CSDI (KS=0.168, AD=0.069), RCGAN (KS=0.189, AD=0.073), and TimeGAN (KS=0.293, AD=0.115). In the downstream TATR experiment, augmenting the training set with 100 years of FTS-Diffusion synthetic data reduces MAPE by 17.9% (S&P 500), 15.3% (GOOG), and 17.4% (ZC=F), while baselines show no improvement or degradation. In the TMTR setting, FTS-Diffusion maintains near-constant predictive accuracy across all synthetic-to-real mixing proportions, whereas all three baselines exhibit increasing MAPE as the real-data fraction decreases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FTS-Diffusion outperforms all three baselines on every distribution-fidelity metric (KS, AD, ES, Wasserstein distance) and every financial summary-statistic discrepancy (ΔSharpe, ΔSortino, ΔCalmar) across all three assets. Against the closest competitor TimeGAN, FTS-Diffusion improves KS by roughly 0.03–0.04 (e.g., 0.327 vs. 0.293 on S&P 500) and AD by about 0.01–0.02. Against CSDI, the gap is even larger: KS is nearly doubled (0.327 vs. 0.168 on S&P 500). In the downstream TATR prediction task, only FTS-Diffusion's synthetic data consistently reduces MAPE (by up to 17.9%), while RCGAN, TimeGAN, and CSDI either increase error or leave it unchanged. In the TMTR task, FTS-Diffusion is the only method whose downstream accuracy remains stable as the proportion of real data drops to 10%, whereas all baselines degrade sharply. The Calmar-ratio discrepancy is particularly telling: FTS-Diffusion achieves ΔCR of 0.29–0.61 versus 12.8–31.2 for the baselines, indicating far better preservation of tail-risk characteristics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM) as its generative backbone and on Markov-chain formulations of financial time series dynamics (Dueker 1997; Bai & Wang 2011), but departs fundamentally from prior time-series generation work (RCGAN, TimeGAN, CSDI, QuantGAN) by rejecting the implicit assumption of regularity and fixed-length segmentation. The key conceptual departure is the identification of irregularity and scale-invariance as first-class properties of financial time series, addressed through a novel SISC algorithm that combines DTW-based variable-length segmentation with K-means clustering, followed by a pattern-conditioned diffusion network and a Markov transition network. No prior generative framework explicitly models the joint distribution over pattern identity, duration scaling, and magnitude scaling as a three-state Markov process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled decomposition of financial time series generation into pattern recognition, pattern generation, and pattern evolution, and demonstrates that augmenting limited real data with FTS-Diffusion synthetic samples reduces downstream prediction error by up to 17.9%—a practically meaningful gain in a domain where data cannot be experimentally generated. Limitations include the univariate, single-asset scope, the relatively high computational cost (171K parameters, 131 min training), and the reliance on hand-tuned segment length bounds. Open questions the authors flag include extending to multivariate settings with cross-asset dependencies, handling distribution shifts in pattern transitions, and providing theoretical guarantees on generation quality.
