## Problem

Posterior sampling (the exponential mechanism) achieves near-optimal privacy-utility tradeoffs under pure DP in theory, but sampling from a general posterior requires MCMC approximation, which degrades the guarantee to (ε,δ)-approximate DP with a potentially unbounded privacy breach. The paper asks whether one can obtain pure DP (or pure Gaussian DP) guarantees with an efficient MCMC algorithm, and answers affirmatively.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a dataset D of n points, individual loss functions ℓ_i that are G-Lipschitz, β-smooth, and α-strongly convex, and a privacy parameter (ε for pure DP or μ for Gaussian DP). It outputs a parameter estimate θ̂ ∈ R^d satisfying 3ε-pure DP or √3μ-Gaussian DP. The method requires the losses to be strongly convex and smooth, and operates within a localized bounded ball Θ = B(θ₀, B) whose radius B is determined by the privacy parameter, dimension, and condition number κ = β/α.

## Method

The ASAP algorithm decomposes an MCMC sample θ̃ as θ* + (θ̃ − θ*) where θ* follows the exact posterior, then adds noise (Laplace for pure DP, Gaussian for GDP) scaled to the Wasserstein-infinity distance Δ between the MCMC distribution and the exact posterior, so the perturbed difference has bounded sensitivity with probability 1. A key technical lemma (Lemma 8) converts standard TV-distance convergence bounds into W_∞ bounds under the condition that the target density is bounded below on a bounded ball. The MCMC sampler is MALA with constraint (Algorithm 1), which runs unconstrained MALA and rejects samples outside the domain Θ, converging in W_∞ distance. A preceding localization step (Algorithm 4, approximate output perturbation) produces an initial point θ₀ and radius B ensuring the true minimizer θ* lies inside Θ, enabling the W_∞ conversion and yielding the first nearly linear-time algorithm achieving optimal DP-ERM rates under pure DP and pure Gaussian DP for strongly convex smooth losses.

## Results

The paper establishes that Localized-ASAP achieves the optimal excess empirical risk of O(d²G²(ln d + ln κ)/(αnε²)) under pure DP and O(G²(d + ln κ)/(αnμ²)) under Gaussian DP, matching the information-theoretic lower bound up to a constant factor. The runtime is nearly linear in n (Õ(n)) when the condition number κ = polylog(n), improving the κ-dependency in the risk bound from O(κ) to O(log κ). Empirically, on the Red Wine Quality (n=1599) and White Wine Quality (n=4898) datasets, the proposed algorithm consistently outperforms output perturbation and DP-GD with gradient clipping under both ε-DP and μ-GDP settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NoisySGD (Bassily et al., 2014), ASAP achieves pure DP (δ=0) rather than (ε,δ)-DP, eliminating the unbounded privacy breach risk, while also reducing computation from n² to nearly linear n/α². Against the Exponential Mechanism (Bassily et al., 2014), ASAP matches the same optimal risk bound d²G²/αnε² under pure DP but reduces runtime from n⁴ to nearly linear time. Against PosteriorSample (Gopi et al., 2022), ASAP achieves true pure Gaussian DP (δ=0) rather than δ-approximate GDP, with comparable or better computation (n/α² vs n/α). In the empirical experiments on both wine quality datasets, the proposed algorithm outperforms DP-GD with per-sample gradient clipping (Bu et al., 2022) and output perturbation across all tested privacy levels under both ε-DP and μ-GDP, confirming the theoretical advantages in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the posterior sampling / exponential mechanism framework for DP-ERM (Wang et al. 2015; Gopi et al. 2022) and the MCMC-based Langevin samplers (MALA) used to approximate the posterior in practice. The key departure is identifying Wasserstein-infinity (W_∞) distance—rather than the standard total variation distance—as the correct convergence metric for maintaining pure DP or pure Gaussian DP guarantees when using approximate MCMC sampling. This contrasts with Seeman et al. (2021), who achieve pure DP via exact posterior sampling at exponential cost, and Bassily et al. (2014), who use MCMC for pure DP but at O(n⁴) runtime. The perturbation-to-strengthen-privacy idea draws on Feldman et al. (2018) and Altschuler & Talwar (2023), but the specific W_∞-based construction and the TV-to-W_∞ conversion lemma (Lemma 8) are new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first nearly linear-time algorithm achieving the information-theoretic optimal rate for DP-ERM under both pure DP and pure Gaussian DP for strongly convex smooth losses, closing a gap where prior methods either required exponential time, O(n⁴) time, or settled for weaker (ε,δ)-DP or suboptimal utility. The W_∞ distance framework and the TV-to-W_∞ conversion lemma are of independent interest for any setting where approximate sampling must preserve the strongest privacy guarantees. However, the method is restricted to strongly convex and smooth losses; extending to general convex or non-smooth settings (where posterior sampling is also known to be optimal) remains an open problem, as do characterizing optimal computational complexity in those regimes.
