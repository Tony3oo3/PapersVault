## Problem

Existing LLM watermarking algorithms face a fundamental trade-off between attack robustness (surviving text modifications) and security robustness (resisting watermark rule inference). In hash-based methods like KGW, the watermark logits for each token depend on a fixed window of k preceding tokens: small k yields strong attack robustness but makes the watermark vulnerable to word-frequency cracking, while large k improves security but degrades robustness. This paper resolves the trade-off by grounding watermark logits in the semantics of all preceding tokens rather than a fixed token window.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and the sequence of already-generated tokens as input. An embedding LLM (Compositional-BERT) encodes the preceding text into a fixed-dimensional semantic vector, which is then passed through a small 4-layer residual network to produce a watermark logit vector over the vocabulary (projected to a fixed 1000-dim space via random mapping to handle varying vocabulary sizes). The output is the watermarked text (LLM logits plus δ-scaled watermark logits) and, at detection time, a z-score computed as the mean of per-token watermark logit values, which is compared against a threshold calibrated to a target false-positive rate.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces the hash-of-k-tokens watermark rule with a learned mapping from semantic embeddings to watermark logits. A Compositional-BERT encoder produces a sentence embedding of all preceding tokens, and a trained 4-layer fully-connected residual network (with ReLU activations) transforms this embedding into a vocabulary-sized logit vector. Training uses two losses: a similarity loss that forces cosine similarity between watermark logits to match tanh-scaled cosine similarity between input embeddings (extending the range to [-1,1] for diversity), and a normalization loss that centers row and column means to zero while constraining absolute values to a target R, ensuring unbiased token preference and balanced ±1 scores. After training, a final tanh(1000·x) scaling makes the logits nearly binary, equivalent to a semantic red-green token assignment. Because semantically invariant modifications (paraphrasing, synonym substitution) produce only small embedding perturbations, the watermark logits remain largely unchanged, yielding attack robustness comparable to the global KGW-1 upper bound, while the high-dimensional semantic space provides security robustness on par with KGW-4.

## Results

Under no-attack conditions, SIR achieves 100% TPR at 1% FPR (F1 = 0.995), matching all baselines. Under GPT-3.5 rewrite attacks, SIR achieves 0.740 TPR (1% FPR) and 0.900 Best F1, substantially outperforming KGW-1 (0.590 TPR), KGW-4 (0.225 TPR), and EXP-Edit (0.435 TPR). Under the more aggressive DIPPER-2 paraphrase, SIR reaches 0.875 TPR versus 0.765 for KGW-1 and 0.150 for KGW-4. On security robustness (watermark decryption accuracy via word-frequency analysis), SIR achieves performance close to KGW-4 while maintaining attack robustness close to KGW-1, resolving the prior trade-off.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against KGW-1 (global fixed watermark logits), SIR wins decisively on all semantic-preserving rewrite attacks: GPT-3.5 (0.740 vs 0.590 TPR), DIPPER-1 (0.765 vs 0.715), and DIPPER-2 (0.875 vs 0.765), while tying on Copy-Paste (0.856 vs 0.854) and Emoji (0.969 vs 0.973). However, KGW-1 suffers from poor security robustness (high watermark decryption accuracy via word-frequency analysis), whereas SIR maintains security comparable to KGW-4. Against KGW-2 and KGW-4, SIR dominates on every rewrite attack by large margins (e.g., DIPPER-2: 0.875 vs 0.470 and 0.150 respectively), and also avoids their catastrophic failure under the Emoji attack (SIR 0.969 vs KGW-2 0.006, KGW-4 0.004). Against EXP-Edit (exponential minimum sampling with edit-distance detection), SIR outperforms on GPT-3.5 (0.740 vs 0.435), DIPPER-1 (0.765 vs 0.630), and DIPPER-2 (0.875 vs 0.485), and additionally supports beam search decoding where EXP-Edit is inapplicable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the KGW watermarking framework (Kirchenbauer et al., 2023a), which adds watermark logits to LLM logits based on a hash of the preceding k tokens. The key departure is replacing the discrete token-ID hash with a continuous semantic embedding from an auxiliary encoder (Compositional-BERT), then training a small residual network to map that embedding to watermark logits via a dual similarity-and-normalization loss. This shifts the watermark's dependency from local token identity to global text semantics, fundamentally changing the robustness profile compared to both KGW-k (which trades off attack vs. security robustness via k) and sampling-based methods like EXP-Edit (Kuditipudi et al., 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a previously acknowledged trade-off in LLM watermarking: small k gives strong attack robustness but weak security (vulnerable to word-frequency cracking), while large k does the opposite. By grounding watermark logits in semantics, SIR achieves near-KGW-1 attack robustness (the theoretical upper bound for logit-based methods) while retaining security robustness close to KGW-4. Practical limitations include dependence on embedding model quality (security degrades on refined domain text like L2 DBpedia classes) and the assumption that semantically invariant modifications are the primary threat model. The authors note that multilingual extension and stronger embedding models are natural next steps, and the method's parallel-computability makes it deployable with negligible latency overhead.
