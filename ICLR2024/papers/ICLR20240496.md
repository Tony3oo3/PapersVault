## Problem

Despite empirical evidence that model-agnostic meta-learning methods like ANIL learn shared representations during pretraining, no theoretical guarantee existed showing they recover the correct low-dimensional structure under architectural misspecification (i.e., when the network width exceeds the true hidden dimension). Prior analyses either assumed the hidden dimension was known a priori (k'=k) or relied on infinite samples per task, neither of which reflects the few-shot, overparametrized regime where these methods are actually deployed.

## Input / Output

The method operates on N linear regression tasks, each with m samples of d-dimensional Gaussian features and noisy labels, where the true task parameters lie in a shared k-dimensional subspace (k < d). The student network is a linear two-layer model with hidden width k' ≥ k (the overparametrized case), producing a representation B ∈ R^{d×k'} and task head w ∈ R^{k'}. At test time on a new task, a single gradient step on w (with B frozen) yields a regression parameter estimate whose excess risk depends only on k, not on d or k'.

## Method

The paper proves that FO-ANIL in the infinite-tasks limit with a linear two-layer network asymptotically learns the correct shared representation despite overparametrization (k' > k). The key mechanism is a dual phenomenon: FO-ANIL both learns the k-dimensional subspace col(B_*) and unlearns its orthogonal complement (B_{*,⊥}^T B_t → 0), with the learned B_∞ having singular values scaling as α^{-1} to enable rapid single-step adaptation. This unlearning of the orthogonal complement is a finite-sample effect that vanishes in the infinite-samples limit, and it is crucial for preventing overfitting during task-specific finetuning. The proof establishes monotonic decay of ‖B_{*,⊥}^T B_t‖₂ and ‖B_t w_t‖₂ alongside monotonic Loewner-order increase of B_*^T B_t B_t^T B_* toward a fixed point Λ_*, using bounding sequences that sandwich the iterates. The result requires only a full-rank initialization (no alignment with B_*), and reveals a polynomial slowdown in convergence rate due to overparametrization, analogous to phenomena observed in supervised learning.

## Results

On a synthetic multi-task linear regression benchmark (d=50, k=5, m=30, N=5000, k'=d), FO-ANIL achieves an excess risk of 0.81±0.01 after a single gradient descent step at test time (m_test=20), approaching the oracle ridge baseline of 0.50±0.01. At m_test=30, FO-ANIL reaches 0.64±0.01 (1-GD) and 0.57±0.02 (ridge on learned representation), while Burer-Monteiro factorization achieves only 1.03±0.02 and the single-task ridge baseline achieves 1.63±0.02. The infinite-samples idealization of FO-ANIL performs far worse (1.78±0.02 at m_test=20), confirming that finite samples are essential for unlearning the orthogonal complement of the shared subspace.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Burer-Monteiro factorization (Tripuraneni et al., 2021), FO-ANIL achieves a substantially lower excess risk: 0.81±0.01 vs. 1.23±0.03 at m_test=20, and 0.64±0.01 vs. 1.03±0.02 at m_test=30 (both with 1-GD). This gap arises because Burer-Monteiro, when misspecified (k'=d), fails to unlearn the orthogonal complement of the shared subspace, leading to overfitting at test time. Against FO-MAML (Finn et al., 2017), FO-ANIL performs essentially identically (0.81±0.01 vs. 0.81±0.01 at m_test=20; 0.64±0.01 vs. 0.63±0.01 at m_test=30), confirming that restricting inner-loop updates to the last layer does not sacrifice adaptation quality in this linear setting. Both model-agnostic methods dramatically outperform the single-task ridge baseline (1.84±0.03 at m_test=20), demonstrating the benefit of learning a shared representation. Notably, the infinite-samples variant of FO-ANIL (1.78±0.02) performs nearly as poorly as the single-task baseline, highlighting that the unlearning of the orthogonal complement—which only occurs with finite samples—is critical for good test-time performance under overparametrization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical analysis of model-agnostic meta-learning (MAML, ANIL) initiated by Finn et al. (2017) and simplified by Raghu et al. (2020) and Nichol et al. (2018). Prior representation-learning guarantees for MAML/ANIL (Collins et al., 2022; Saunshi et al., 2020) required either a well-specified network width matching the hidden dimension, infinite samples per task, or a unidimensional shared structure. This work departs from all three restrictions simultaneously, proving that FO-ANIL with an overparametrized width (k′ > k), finite samples, and no initial alignment still recovers the correct low-dimensional subspace in the infinite-tasks limit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides the first provable guarantee that model-agnostic meta-learning learns shared representations under architectural misspecification, directly supporting the empirical observation that these methods work well in practice despite unknown hidden dimensions. A key conceptual finding is that finite samples are *necessary* for unlearning the orthogonal complement of the shared subspace—a phenomenon absent in the infinite-sample limit that would otherwise hurt test-time performance. Limitations include the infinite-tasks idealization, zero-mean task parameters, identity task covariance, and Gaussian features; extending to finite tasks, anisotropic covariances, and MAML remain open. The revealed polynomial (vs. exponential) convergence slowdown under overparametrization connects meta-learning theory to recent supervised-learning results (Xu & Du, 2023).
