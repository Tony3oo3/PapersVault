## Problem

Global self-attention in Vision Transformers has quadratic computational complexity that severely limits image throughput, especially for high-resolution inputs needed in detection and segmentation. Existing multi-scale approaches like Swin Transformer rely on window-shifting for cross-window interaction, which becomes ineffective at large resolutions and still suffers from limited receptive fields. The paper addresses the gap of achieving a state-of-the-art accuracy-throughput Pareto front for high-resolution CV tasks without sacrificing global context modeling.

## Input / Output

FasterViT consumes standard RGB images (224×224 for classification benchmarks, up to 768×768 for high-resolution fine-tuning) and produces task-specific outputs: class labels for ImageNet classification, bounding boxes and instance masks for MS COCO detection/segmentation, and per-pixel class maps for ADE20K semantic segmentation. The architecture is a 4-stage hierarchical design that progressively downsamples spatial resolution by 2× per stage (from 112×112 to 7×7) while doubling channel dimensions, with the stem using two strided 3×3 convolutions to produce overlapping patch embeddings.

## Method

FasterViT is a hybrid CNN-ViT architecture where stages 1–2 use residual convolutional blocks (dense 3×3 convolutions with BatchNorm) for efficient high-resolution feature extraction, and stages 3–4 employ the proposed Hierarchical Attention (HAT) blocks. HAT introduces learnable carrier tokens (CTs) per local window, initialized via a 3×3 convolution followed by average pooling to L tokens per window. Each HAT block alternates between (i) full self-attention over all CTs to enable cross-window global communication, and (ii) joint self-attention over each window's local tokens concatenated with its dedicated CTs for local-global information exchange. This decomposition reduces the attention complexity from O(H⁴d) for full attention to O(k²H²d + LH²d + H⁴L²d/k⁴), which grows nearly linearly with resolution since the windowed local attention dominates. Positional information is injected via 1D absolute positional bias on tokens plus 2D relative positional bias computed by a 2-layer MLP (from SwinV2), enabling resolution flexibility without fixed positional embeddings.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K (224×224, A100 GPU, batch size 128), FasterViT achieves a SOTA accuracy-throughput Pareto front: FasterViT-0 reaches 82.1% top-1 at 5802 img/s, FasterViT-2 reaches 84.2% at 3161 img/s, and FasterViT-4 reaches 85.4% at 849 img/s. The largest variant, FasterViT-6, achieves 85.8% top-1 accuracy at 352 img/s with 1360M parameters. On MS COCO with Cascade Mask R-CNN, FasterViT-4 achieves 52.9 box AP and 45.8 mask AP at 117 img/s, outperforming ConvNeXt-B and Swin-B while being 15–30% faster. On ADE20K with UPerNet, FasterViT-4 achieves 49.1/50.3 mIoU (single-scale/multi-scale) at 202 img/s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FasterViT consistently dominates the accuracy-throughput Pareto front across all model scales. At the small scale, FasterViT-0 (82.1%, 5802 img/s) outperforms ConvNeXt-T (82.0%, 3196 img/s) and Swin-T (81.3%, 2758 img/s) on both accuracy and throughput. At the medium scale, FasterViT-1 (83.2%, 4188 img/s) matches Swin-S accuracy while being 2.4× faster, and FasterViT-2 (84.2%, 3161 img/s) beats SwinV2-S (83.8%, 1043 img/s) by +0.4% accuracy at 3× the throughput. At the large scale, FasterViT-3 (84.9%, 1780 img/s) surpasses SwinV2-B (84.6%, 535 img/s) and MaxViT-B (84.9%, 507 img/s) with 3–3.5× higher throughput, and FasterViT-4 (85.4%, 849 img/s) exceeds MaxViT-L (85.1%, 376 img/s) and DeiT3-L (84.8%, 535 img/s) on both metrics. On dense prediction, FasterViT-4 with Cascade Mask R-CNN achieves 52.9 box AP / 45.8 mask AP, beating ConvNeXt-B (+0.2/+0.2) and Swin-B (+1.0/+0.8) while being 15% and 30% faster respectively. The paper also demonstrates that HAT as a plug-and-play module improves Swin-T by +0.4% top-1, +0.5 box AP, +0.6 mask AP, and +0.9% mIoU with minimal throughput cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

FasterViT extends the hierarchical multi-scale architecture of Swin Transformer by replacing its window-shifting mechanism with a novel Hierarchical Attention (HAT) module that uses learned carrier tokens to enable cross-window communication at near-linear cost. The carrier token concept draws inspiration from global-token approaches in NLP (BigBird, LongFormer) and vision (EdgeViT, Twins, Focal Transformer), but departs by learning the tokens end-to-end and using them in both a sub-global self-attention pass and a local windowed attention pass. The hybrid CNN-Transformer design (conv blocks in stages 1–2, transformer blocks in stages 3–4) follows the broader trend of hybrid architectures (LeViT, CoaT, CrossViT) but is explicitly guided by hardware-aware design principles that distinguish memory-bound early stages from math-bound later stages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FasterViT establishes a strong SOTA Pareto front on the accuracy-throughput tradeoff across classification, detection, and segmentation, and the HAT module's plug-and-play nature (improving Swin-T by +0.4% Top-1 and +0.9% mIoU with minimal overhead) makes it a practical drop-in for existing ViT backbones. The consistent 2× throughput advantage over SwinV2 at high resolutions addresses a real bottleneck for dense prediction at large input sizes. Open questions include whether the hardware-specific optimizations (pre-computed positional biases, no feature reshaping) transfer cleanly to new architectures, and whether the carrier-token mechanism generalizes to video or 3D settings where the spatial hierarchy differs.
