## Problem

Existing GNN explanation methods either train auxiliary black-box models (making the explanations themselves opaque), rely on gradient back-propagation (suffering from ReLU saturation), or depend on complex hyperparameter tuning. Additionally, prior work largely ignores whether explanations are discriminative across classes and stable across similar samples, leaving a gap in how we assess explanation quality beyond fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241351/cropped_page6_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph G=(V,E) with node feature matrix X∈R^{N×d} and adjacency matrix A∈{0,1}^{N×N}, along with a pretrained GNN (GCN, GraphSAGE, or GIN) whose parameters are fixed. It produces per-edge importance scores and an explanation subgraph S⊆E (a subset of edges), treating edges as the attribution variables while node features are folded into edge contributions. The approach is entirely training-free and analytical, requiring only a forward pass to compute activation patterns.

## Method

GOAt expands the GNN output into a sum of scalar product terms by exploiting the linearity of matrix multiplications and the element-wise nature of ReLU activations, where each term involves entries from the adjacency matrix A, input features X, fixed model parameters W, and activation patterns P (the ratio of post- to pre-activation values). The key theoretical insight is the Equal Contribution principle: in a product term z = c·X₁X₂…Xₘ, each binary variable contributes z/|V(z)| to the output, so the importance of an edge is the sum of its equal share across all scalar products it participates in. Activation pattern contributions are further distributed back to the edges and node features within r hops that influence them. The method satisfies the completeness axiom (attributions sum to the output difference), requires no training or gradient computation, and is demonstrated analytically for GCN, GraphSAGE, and GIN architectures.

## Results

GOAt consistently outperforms all 8 baselines on fidelity across all sparsity levels (0.1–0.9) on BA-2Motifs, Mutagenicity, and NCI1, with RCExplainer being the closest competitor. On discriminability, GOAt achieves higher class separation than the original unmodified graphs at sparsity=0.7 on BA-2Motifs and NCI1, while baselines show significant overlap. On stability, GOAt explains all Class 1 samples on BA-2Motifs using only 3 distinct motifs, whereas baselines require many more. In qualitative motif coverage (Table 1), GOAt achieves 93.4% coverage on Mutagenicity Class 0 versus ≤1.3% for all baselines, and runs in 0.05–0.83 s per sample versus 56.7–541.6 s for SubgraphX.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GOAt outperforms all 8 baselines (GNNExplainer, PGExplainer, PGM-Explainer, SubgraphX, CFGNNExplainer, RCExplainer, RG-Explainer, DEGREE) on all three proposed metrics — fidelity, discriminability, and stability — across all six datasets and all sparsity levels. RCExplainer is the strongest fidelity competitor (it is explicitly designed for fidelity optimization), but GOAt still leads by a clear margin while being training-free and requiring no hyperparameter tuning. On discriminability, GNNExplainer and PGExplainer produce explanations where all class embeddings cluster together with no separation; SubgraphX and DEGREE show partial separation but with significant overlap, whereas GOAt achieves clean class separation with no overlapping points. On stability, GOAt covers all Class 1 samples on BA-2Motifs with just 3 motifs, while baselines require substantially more distinct explanations. In terms of speed, GOAt (0.05–0.83 s/sample) is roughly 2–3× faster than GNNExplainer and DEGREE, and 100–1000× faster than SubgraphX, which relies on Monte Carlo Tree Search.

## Relation to Prior Work

GOAt departs from the dominant paradigm of GNN explanation, which relies on training auxiliary models (GNNExplainer, PGExplainer, SubgraphX) or gradient back-propagation (DEGREE, GNN-LRP), by instead exploiting the algebraic sum-product structure of GNN forward passes to analytically expand outputs into scalar products. The core theoretical tool—the 'equal contribution' principle for multiplicative terms—draws on axiomatic attribution (Sundararajan et al., 2017; Shrikumar et al., 2017) but applies it to the specific structure of message-passing layers with ReLU activations. Unlike prior work that treats explanation as a learned or search-based problem, GOAt frames it as a closed-form decomposition, requiring no hyperparameters or auxiliary training, though it does require hand-crafted expansion formulas per GNN variant (GCN, GraphSAGE, GIN).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of discriminability and stability as evaluation metrics addresses a genuine gap in the GNN explanation literature, where fidelity alone has been the standard. The finding that a training-free analytical method can outperform learned explainers on all three metrics (fidelity, discriminability, stability) suggests that the black-box auxiliary-model paradigm may be unnecessary for local explanations. However, the method's generality is limited: it requires expert-designed expansions for each GNN architecture, assumes ReLU activations, and the theoretical 'large N' assumption, while empirically relaxed, remains a constraint. Open questions include extending the framework to other activation functions (GELU, SiLU), handling attention-based GNNs, and scaling to very large graphs where the number of scalar products explodes.
