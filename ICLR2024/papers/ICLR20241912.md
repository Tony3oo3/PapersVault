## Problem

Standard contrastive self-supervised learning enforces invariance to data augmentations, which erases sensitivity to perceptually important features like color. Prior equivariant approaches address this by learning non-linear (e.g., MLP-based) transformations of embeddings, but these suffer from geometric pathologies such as inconsistency under composition of augmentations. The paper asks whether one can learn equivariance where input augmentations map to simple linear (orthogonal) transformations in embedding space, while remaining compatible with the pairwise contrastive learning paradigm.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR/STL, 224×224 for ImageNet-100) or 3D protein point clouds from the PDB as input and produces unit-norm embeddings on S^{d-1} (128-dim for images via a ResNet-50 backbone with MLP projection head; 16-dim for proteins via a DeepSet). The key structural constraint on the output is that applying any augmentation a to the input rotates the embedding by a single orthogonal matrix R_a independent of the input, so that pairwise angles between embeddings are preserved under augmentation. Downstream, frozen embeddings are classified by a linear probe.

## Method

CARE introduces an equivariance loss L_equi that enforces angle preservation: the inner product between embeddings of two augmented samples must match the inner product between their unaugmented counterparts, i.e., f(a(x'))^T f(a(x)) ≈ f(x)^T f(x'). The paper proves that L_equi = 0 implies the existence of a single orthogonal matrix R_a ∈ O(d) such that f(a(x)) = R_a f(x) for all x, and that compositions of augmentations correspond to compositions of rotations (a group homomorphism). Rather than explicitly learning R_a, CARE sidesteps the non-uniqueness problem by training on pairs of data, making it naturally compatible with contrastive learning. The full objective L_CARE = L_inv + L_unif + λ·L_equi combines the equivariance term with InfoNCE's invariance and uniformity components; the invariance term biases rotations to be small (localized), while the uniformity term prevents trivial collapse. In practice, the batch is split into chunks so that the equivariance loss samples multiple augmentations per batch while InfoNCE retains a large batch size.

## Results

On linear probe evaluation, CARE_SimCLR achieves 91.92% top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-10, 68.05% on CIFAR-100, 84.64% on STL-10, and 76.69% on ImageNet-100, consistently outperforming its SimCLR backbone across all four datasets. The largest gain is on ImageNet-100, where CARE_SimCLR improves over SimCLR by 3.90 percentage points (76.69% vs. 72.79%). CARE_MoCo-v2 similarly improves over MoCo-v2 on CIFAR-10 (+0.24), CIFAR-100 (+0.68), and ImageNet-100 (+0.80). On the protein point cloud task, CARE achieves the lowest error in predicting the first principal component, and exhibits markedly more regular SO(3) rotation trajectories in embedding space than SimCLR or invariance-only baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CARE consistently outperforms SimCLR across all four image classification datasets when used as the backbone: +0.94 pp on CIFAR-10 (91.92 vs. 90.98), +1.28 pp on CIFAR-100 (68.05 vs. 66.77), +0.45 pp on STL-10 (84.64 vs. 84.19), and a substantial +3.90 pp on ImageNet-100 (76.69 vs. 72.79). Against the equivariant baseline EquiMOD (Devillers & Lefort, 2023), CARE_SimCLR wins on all three overlapping datasets: CIFAR-10 (91.92 vs. 91.28), CIFAR-100 (68.05 vs. 67.59), and STL-10 (84.64 vs. 83.67). CARE_MoCo-v2 also beats MoCo-v2 on CIFAR-10 (+0.24), CIFAR-100 (+0.68), and ImageNet-100 (+0.80). Beyond classification accuracy, CARE demonstrates superior geometric structure: it achieves lower Wahba's problem error (indicating more consistent rotational equivariance), lower relative rotational equivariance γ_f (confirming non-trivial equivariance rather than collapse to invariance), and greater sensitivity to color changes in retrieval tasks on Flowers-102, where SimCLR remains largely invariant.

## Relation to Prior Work

CARE extends the equivariant contrastive learning line initiated by Dangovski et al. (2022), Devillers & Lefort (2023), and Garrido et al. (2023), which train a learnable MLP to predict how augmentations transform embeddings. The key departure is constraining the transformation T_a to be orthogonal (linear) rather than an arbitrary non-linear map, which prior approaches could not enforce due to the difficulty of learning a mapping into O(d). CARE sidesteps this by training f to preserve pairwise angles—a property that is naturally compatible with the Siamese/contrastive paradigm—and proves this weaker-looking condition actually implies full orthogonal equivariance. The method also builds on the symmetry regularization framework of Shakerinava et al. (2022) and integrates directly into standard contrastive pipelines (SimCLR, MoCo-v2, Barlow Twins).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean theoretical result (angle preservation ⟹ orthogonal equivariance, with compositionality) provides a principled and provably structured alternative to ad-hoc equivariant modules, and the method's pair-based formulation makes it drop-in compatible with existing contrastive training loops. However, the empirical gains over strong invariance-based baselines are modest (0.2–3.9 points on linear probes), and the paper acknowledges that only approximate equivariance is achieved, with no efficient mechanism to identify which rotation corresponds to which augmentation without solving Wahba's problem per augmentation. Open questions include whether approximate equivariance is sufficient for downstream tasks at scale, how to extend the framework to non-orthogonal groups (Lorentz, symplectic) for hyperbolic or symplectic embedding spaces, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the small-dimension protein point cloud setting where SO(3) structure is most clearly demonstrated.
