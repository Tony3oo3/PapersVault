## Problem

Two-stage robust optimization (2RO) with integer second-stage decisions is computationally intractable: the classical column-and-constraint generation (CCG) algorithm requires solving an increasingly large MILP main problem and a bilevel adversarial problem at each iteration, and existing exact or heuristic methods (branch-and-price, k-adaptability) scale poorly with problem size. Machine learning has been largely unexplored in this setting, with only a handful of prior works applying ML to robust optimization. Neur2RO addresses this gap by learning to predict the intractable second-stage value function and embedding it into CCG to obtain high-quality solutions in seconds rather than hours.

## Input / Output

The neural network takes as input a first-stage decision vector x ∈ {0,1}^n, an uncertainty scenario ξ ∈ Ξ, and per-variable problem coefficients (e.g., nominal profits, costs, repair costs for knapsack; sensitivity matrix entries for capital budgeting). It outputs a scalar prediction of the optimal second-stage objective value. The architecture is size-invariant via set-based (Deep Sets) embeddings, so a single trained model generalizes across instances with different numbers of decision variables. The predicted value is then consumed inside MILP formulations (via big-M ReLU encodings) for the CCG main and adversarial problems, whose final output is the first-stage robust decision x*.

## Method

Neur2RO trains a compact feedforward ReLU network that predicts the optimal second-stage value for a given (x, ξ, P) triple, using a two-stage embedding architecture: separate set-based networks Φ_x and Φ_ξ produce low-dimensional embeddings of the first-stage decision and scenario (invariant to variable ordering and count), which are concatenated and passed through a small Value Network Φ. The key design insight is that the network is deliberately kept small and MILP-representable (via Fischetti–Jo big-M encodings) so it can be optimized over inside the CCG loop. In the main problem, an argmax formulation selects the worst-case scenario from the finite set Ξ′ using the NN prediction, while the exact second-stage cost is still modeled in the objective—this means the NN only needs to correctly identify the worst scenario rather than be pointwise accurate. In the adversarial problem, the inner integer optimization over y is replaced entirely by the NN prediction, reducing the AP to a MILP (or MIQP) over the uncertainty set. Training is supervised: 250,000 (x, ξ, P, label) pairs are generated by solving the tractable deterministic second-stage MILP for fixed x and ξ, and the model is trained for 500 epochs with MSE loss.

## Results

On the two-stage knapsack problem, Neur2RO solves instances in 4–77 seconds (median) versus 0–10,800 seconds for the state-of-the-art branch-and-price (BP) algorithm, achieving a ~100× speedup on the hardest instances (strongly correlated, n=80) while also finding better solutions (0.000% vs. 0.179% median relative error). On capital budgeting, Neur2RO achieves the lowest median RE for the three largest instance sizes (n=20, 40, 50: 0.000%, 0.009%, 0.001%) with solving times of 324–1,032 seconds, compared to 8,702–10,807 seconds for k-adaptability baselines, representing a 10–100× reduction in solution time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For knapsack, Neur2RO outperforms the branch-and-price algorithm of Arslan & Detienne (2022) on all harder instances (n≥50 with weak or strong correlation), achieving lower median RE while being up to 100× faster (e.g., 13 s vs. 10,800 s for strongly correlated n=80, with 0.000% vs. 0.179% RE). On easier instances (uncorrelated, small n), BP converges to optimal solutions within the time limit while Neur2RO retains a median RE of ~1–2%, though still 1–2 orders of magnitude faster. For capital budgeting, Neur2RO outperforms all three k-adaptability variants (k=2, 5, 10) of Subramanyam et al. (2020) on the largest instances (n=20, 40, 50) in both solution quality and speed, with median RE of 0.000%, 0.009%, and 0.001% versus 0.064–0.196% for the best k-adaptability baseline. On n=30 instances, k=2 achieves a slightly lower median RE (0.020% vs. 0.109%), but Neur2RO is still ~18× faster (602 s vs. 10,801 s). Overall, Neur2RO demonstrates a clear advantage as instance difficulty and size increase, with the gap widening substantially for the most challenging configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two established threads: the column-and-constraint generation (CCG) algorithm for two-stage robust optimization (Zeng & Zhao, 2013) and MILP representations of ReLU neural networks (Fischetti & Jo, 2018). Rather than predicting solutions directly—a common strategy in ML-for-optimization—the authors 'neuralize' CCG by replacing its intractable main and adversarial subproblems with compact, MILP-representable neural networks that estimate the second-stage value function. The use of set-based (Deep Sets) architectures for size-invariant generalization across instances, and the argmax formulation that decouples scenario selection from objective estimation, are novel design choices that distinguish this work from prior learning-augmented optimization approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Two-stage robust optimization with integer recourse is notoriously intractable—state-of-the-art exact methods time out on instances with as few as 25 variables—so a method that achieves 10–100× speedups while maintaining or improving solution quality on the hardest instances is practically significant for logistics, finance, and healthcare applications. Key limitations include evaluation on only two benchmark problems, a convergence guarantee that requires a finite first-stage feasible set, and reliance on the MILP-representability of ReLU networks (ruling out more expressive architectures). Open directions the authors identify include leveraging adversarial-attack heuristics for the adversarial problem, predicting constraint infeasibility, and exploring decision-tree surrogates as alternatives.
