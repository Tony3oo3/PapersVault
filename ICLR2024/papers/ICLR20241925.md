## Problem

Standard diffusion models fix the forward diffusion process to a simple Gaussian noising of the original image, limiting the flexibility of the inference model while retaining the scalability benefits of parameter sharing and efficient ELBO computation. This paper asks whether one can introduce a learned, time-dependent encoder into the diffusion process to improve likelihood without increasing sampling cost, and whether the common assumption that forward and backward variances must be equal is truly necessary.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241925/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images at 28×28 (MNIST), 32×32 (CIFAR-10), and 32×32 (ImageNet32). During training, the input image is first passed through a time-dependent encoder (a U-Net with downsampling to 16×16 and 8×8) to produce an encoded representation x_φ(λ_t) that is then noised; during sampling, the encoder is entirely absent and only the generative U-Net (without downsampling) denoises Gaussian noise into an image. The v-prediction network outputs a v-vector from which both the noise and the encoded image prediction are recovered.

## Method

DiffEnc introduces a depth-dependent encoder x_φ(λ_t) = α_t² x + σ_t² y_φ(λ_t) that replaces the identity mapping in the forward diffusion process, adding a mean-shift term α_t(x_t − x_s) to the conditional q(z_t|z_s, x). To prevent this shift from degrading the diffusion loss, the generative mean μ_P is augmented with a counterterm α_s(λ_s − λ_t)σ_t² x̂_θ(λ_t) that approximates the encoder's derivative in the continuous limit. The resulting continuous-time loss (Eq. 18) is trained with v-prediction parameterization, and the encoder is initialized to zero so training starts from the standard VDM. The paper further proves that in the infinite-depth limit the optimal generative variance must equal the diffusion variance (w_t = 1), while for finite T the ELBO corresponds to a time-weighted diffusion loss with closed-form optimal σ_P².

## Results

On CIFAR-10, DiffEnc-32-4 (trainable encoder, 8M steps) achieves 2.62 BPD, improving over the VDMv-32 baseline (2.64 BPD) and the original VDM with ε-parameterization (2.65 BPD at 10M steps). The improvement is statistically significant (p-value 0.03 via t-test over 3 seeds) and is driven primarily by a reduction in the diffusion loss component (2.609 vs. 2.629 BPD). On ImageNet32, DiffEnc-32-8 matches VDMv-32 at 3.46 BPD, while on MNIST and smaller CIFAR-10 models the encoder improves the latent loss without degrading the diffusion loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffEnc-32-4 outperforms the VDM baseline (Kingma et al., 2021) by 0.03 BPD on CIFAR-10 (2.62 vs. 2.65) and by 0.26 BPD on ImageNet32 (3.46 vs. 3.72*), with the CIFAR-10 improvement being statistically significant (p=0.03). It also beats Reflected Diffusion Models (Lou & Ermon, 2023) by 0.06 BPD on CIFAR-10 and ARDM (Hoogeboom et al., 2021) by 0.02 BPD. However, DiffEnc does not surpass Flow Matching TN (Zheng et al., 2023), which achieves 2.60 BPD on CIFAR-10 and 3.45 BPD on ImageNet32, remaining the strongest non-augmented method in the table. On ImageNet32, DiffEnc-32-8 ties VDMv-32 at 3.46 BPD, suggesting the encoder's benefit is more pronounced on the simpler CIFAR-10 dataset. The v-parameterization itself provides a small gain over the original ε-parameterization (2.64 vs. 2.65 BPD on CIFAR-10 at comparable training budgets).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Variational Diffusion Models (VDM; Kingma et al., 2021) by relaxing the assumption that the forward diffusion process is fixed and simple, introducing a learned time-dependent encoder that modifies the mean of the diffusion process. It is positioned as a generalization of the hierarchical VAE view of diffusion models, where the encoder adds flexibility to the inference model while preserving the Markov structure and parameter sharing that make diffusion models scalable. The approach is distinct from blurring diffusion models (which use a fixed linear blurring), latent diffusion (which uses a depth-independent encoder/decoder), and implicit nonlinear diffusion (which requires invertibility), as DiffEnc's encoder is a learned nonlinear function of both data and time that is only needed during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical insight is that the encoder improves training (better likelihood) without increasing sampling cost, since it is not used at inference time. However, the empirical gains are modest (2.62 vs 2.64 BPD on CIFAR-10, p=0.03) and do not extend to ImageNet32 or small models, suggesting the encoder's benefit may require sufficient model capacity and dataset complexity. The theoretical result that the generative and diffusion variances must be equal in the continuous-time limit for the ELBO to be well-defined is a clean constraint that clarifies a commonly made assumption. Open questions include whether the encoder can be combined with latent diffusion, distillation, or classifier-free guidance, and whether the learned time-dependent representations reveal useful structure about the data.
