## Problem

Diffusion-based image editing methods have been developed almost entirely empirically, with ODE samplers (e.g., DDIM) being the default choice due to fast sampling and easy inversion. However, no principled probabilistic analysis exists to explain whether SDE or ODE is preferable when the edited latent variable deviates from the model's marginal distribution — the defining characteristic that distinguishes editing from standard sampling. This paper asks: does the additional stochasticity in SDEs help or hurt when the starting distribution is mismatched?

## Input / Output

The method operates in the latent space of a pretrained text-to-image diffusion model (Stable Diffusion 1.5, 4×4×128×128 latent tensors). The input RGB image is first encoded and perturbed to a noisy latent at a chosen time t₀, then manipulated in a task-specific way (copy-and-paste for dragging, prompt swap for I2I, mask compositing for inpainting), creating a distribution mismatch with the model's marginal. Task-specific controls include source/target point pairs (for dragging), text prompts (for I2I), and optional binary masks (for inpainting and region preservation in dragging). The output is a decoded RGB image at the same resolution as the input.

## Method

The paper introduces a unified two-stage probabilistic formulation for diffusion-based editing: (1) invert or noise the input to a latent x_{t₀}, then manipulate it to produce x̃_{t₀} that deviates from the model's marginal p_{t₀}; (2) sample from x̃_{t₀} back to x̃₀ via an SDE or ODE solver. The key theoretical result (Theorems 3.1–3.2) proves that the KL divergence between the marginal distributions of two SDEs with mismatched priors strictly decreases as time approaches zero (contractive), while for ODEs it remains invariant — meaning SDEs naturally 'heal' the distribution mismatch introduced by editing. Practically, the paper proposes SDE-Drag, which replaces gradient-based latent optimization with a simple copy-and-paste operation (amplifying source-region pixels by factor α and perturbing the source with Gaussian noise by factor β to avoid duplication), followed by Cycle-SDE sampling that reuses memorized noise from the forward pass. SDE counterparts for inpainting and I2I translation are obtained by replacing ODE inversion with noise-adding and ODE sampling with Cycle-SDE, yielding consistent improvements across FID, LPIPS, and CLIP-Score metrics.

## Results

On the Places inpainting benchmark, Inpaint-SDE achieves FID 4.91 and LPIPS 0.215 (small mask, 100 steps) versus 5.86 and 0.227 for Inpaint-ODE, and FID 15.27 and LPIPS 0.343 (large mask, 100 steps) versus 17.50 and 0.361. Notably, SDE at 25 steps (FID 5.18) already surpasses ODE at 100 steps (FID 5.86). In the dragging user study on DragBench (100 open-set images), SDE-Drag achieves significantly higher preference rates than ODE-Drag, DragDiffusion, and DragGAN, with comparable per-image runtime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Inpaint-SDE (DDPM) consistently outperforms Inpaint-ODE (DDIM, i.e., Stable Diffusion's default inpainting) on both FID and LPIPS across all step counts and mask sizes, with the gap widening for larger masks (FID 15.27 vs. 17.50 at 100 steps). DiffEdit-SDE (Cycle-SDE) beats DiffEdit-ODE on FID, LPIPS, and CLIP-Score simultaneously across all tested t0 values (0.3T–0.8T), achieving better image-text alignment without sacrificing fidelity. In the dragging task, SDE-Drag significantly outperforms ODE-Drag (its direct ODE counterpart), DragDiffusion (Shi et al., 2023), and DragGAN (Pan et al., 2023) in a two-alternative forced-choice user study on 100 open-set images; DragGAN additionally cannot handle open-domain images at all (restricted to cats/lions). The SDE counterparts also match or exceed ODE baselines in DDIB image-to-image translation (Appendix G.2). All SDE methods achieve these gains without increasing computational time relative to their ODE counterparts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the score-based SDE/ODE framework (Song et al., 2020b) and the Cycle-SDE reconstruction procedure (Wu & De la Torre, 2022) to provide a unified probabilistic formulation for diffusion-based image editing that encompasses SDEdit, DiffEdit, DDIB, and DragDiffusion under a single two-stage view. The key conceptual departure is from the prevailing practice of using ODE solvers (DDIM) in editing pipelines due to their speed and invertibility; here, the authors prove via the Fokker-Planck equation that SDEs are contractive in KL divergence while ODEs are invariant when the prior distribution is mismatched, inverting the usual sampling-time preference. The theoretical tools draw on Lu et al. (2022a) for the KL/Fisher divergence decomposition and on the log-Sobolev inequality for linear convergence rates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is remarkably simple: replacing DDIM with DDPM (or more generally, an ODE solver with its SDE counterpart) in any diffusion-based editing pipeline yields consistent, substantial improvements across inpainting, image-to-image translation, and point-based dragging, without additional training or architectural changes. The DragBench benchmark (100 open-set images across natural, art, and AI-generated categories) fills a gap in evaluation for open-domain dragging. Open questions include whether the theoretical guarantees extend to finite-approximation and discretization errors, whether quantitative convergence rates can be established for Cycle-SDE, and whether the SDE advantage persists under very few sampling steps or with more aggressive editing operations.
