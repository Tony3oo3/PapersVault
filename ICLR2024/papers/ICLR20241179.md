## Problem

For uniformly ergodic average reward MDPs under a generative model, the best known sample complexity upper bound was \tilde{O}(|S||A|t_{\text{mix}}^2\epsilon^{-2}) while the matching lower bound was \Omega(|S||A|t_{\text{mix}}\epsilon^{-2}), leaving a gap of one factor of t_{\text{mix}}. This paper resolves the open question by providing the first algorithm that achieves the lower bound, establishing \tilde{\Theta}(|S||A|t_{\text{mix}}\epsilon^{-2}) as the minimax-optimal sample complexity.

## Input / Output

The algorithm requires a generative model (a sampler that draws next states independently from p_{s,a} for any state-action pair), an error tolerance \epsilon \in (0,1], and a priori knowledge of the uniform mixing time upper bound t_{\text{mix}} (or equivalently t_{\text{minorize}}) to set the internal discount factor. It outputs a stationary Markov deterministic policy \hat{\pi}_0 that achieves long-run average reward within \epsilon of optimal with probability at least 1-\delta. A notable constraint is the minimum sample size requirement of \tilde{\Omega}(|S||A|(1-\gamma)^{-1}) per state-action pair, which is smaller than the \tilde{\Omega}(|S||A|(1-\gamma)^{-3}) needed by prior variance-reduced Q-learning approaches.

## Method

The method is a two-stage reduction: first, the AMDP is approximated by a DMDP with discount factor 1-\gamma = \epsilon/(19\,t_{\text{minorize}}), following Jin & Sidford (2021); second, the DMDP is solved via the Perturbed Model-Based Planning (PMBP) algorithm of Li et al. (2020), which adds a uniform random perturbation to rewards to ensure a sufficient optimality gap and then solves the empirical Bellman equation. The key technical contribution is a new analysis of PMBP under uniform ergodicity that replaces the worst-case (1-\gamma)^{-3} dependence with the optimal t_{\text{minorize}}(1-\gamma)^{-2} dependence, using decoupling of the data-dependent policy from the empirical transition kernel, Bernstein concentration inequalities over a finite \xi-net of auxiliary rewards, and span semi-norm bounds from Wang et al. (2023). This yields a total sample complexity of \tilde{O}(|S||A|t_{\text{minorize}}\epsilon^{-2}), matching the lower bound, while retaining the small minimum sample size of the model-based approach that prior reduction-based methods (Jin & Sidford 2021; Wang et al. 2022) could not achieve because they relied on the suboptimal (1-\gamma)^{-3} DMDP bound.

## Results

The paper establishes that the sample complexity for learning an ε-optimal policy in uniformly ergodic AMDPs under a generative model is Õ(|S||A|t_mix/ε²), matching the existing lower bound Ω(|S||A|t_mix/ε²) from Jin & Sidford (2021). This closes a gap of a factor t_mix that had persisted in the literature. In numerical experiments on hard MDP instances from Wang et al. (2023), the algorithm achieves a convergence rate with log-log slope of approximately −0.5 (i.e., n^{−1/2}), confirming the optimal Õ(ε^{−2}) dependence, while the prior algorithm from Jin & Sidford (2021) exhibits a slope near −1/3, consistent with its suboptimal Õ(ε^{−3}) rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the reduction-to-DMDP algorithm of Jin & Sidford (2021), which achieved Õ(|S||A|t_mix/ε³) for uniformly ergodic AMDPs. The proposed method improves the ε-dependence from ε^{−3} to ε^{−2} by leveraging the optimal Õ(t_mix(1−γ)^{−2}) sample complexity for uniformly ergodic DMDPs (from Wang et al., 2023) rather than the worst-case Õ((1−γ)^{−3}) bound used by Jin & Sidford (2021). In the numerical experiment (Figure 1a), the proposed algorithm's error decreases with a log-log slope of approximately −0.5, while Jin & Sidford (2021) shows a slope near −1/3, directly confirming the theoretical improvement from ε^{−3} to ε^{−2}. The paper also compares against the primal-dual SMD approach of Jin & Sidford (2020), which had a t_mix² dependence, and the weakly-communicating results of Wang et al. (2022) and Zhang & Xie (2023), which use the span parameter H instead of t_mix but still carry an ε^{−3} or H² factor respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three prior threads: the Perturbed Model-Based Planning (PMBP) algorithm from Li et al. (2020), the AMDP-to-DMDP reduction with discount factor 1−γ = Θ(ε/t_mix) from Jin & Sidford (2021), and the mixing-time-aware concentration analysis from Wang et al. (2023). The key departure from prior AMDP reduction work (Jin & Sidford 2021; Wang et al. 2022) is recognizing that those approaches inherited a suboptimal (1−γ)^{-3} dependence from worst-case DMDP theory, whereas the uniformly ergodic setting admits a (1−γ)^{-2} rate. The paper's technical contribution is proving that PMBP achieves this improved rate while maintaining a minimum sample size of only Ω(|S||A|(1−γ)^{-1}), overcoming the Ω(|S||A|(1−γ)^{-3}) barrier that made Wang et al.'s (2023) algorithm inapplicable to the reduction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This resolves a long-standing open problem by closing the t_mix gap between the best known upper and lower bounds for uniformly ergodic AMDPs under a generative model, establishing the first minimax-optimal sample complexity guarantee in this setting. The DMDP result (Theorem 1) is of independent interest, providing the first optimal analysis of PMBP under mixing assumptions. Key limitations include the need for a priori knowledge of t_mix, the strong uniform ergodicity assumption (which is conservative when suboptimal policies mix slowly), and restriction to finite state/action spaces. The authors identify the H-based complexity measure (span semi-norm of bias) as a more natural and general parameter, and outline future work toward O(|S||A|Hε^{-2}) and general state-space extensions.
