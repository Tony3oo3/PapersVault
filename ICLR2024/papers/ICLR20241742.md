## Problem

While scaling laws for LLM pretraining are well-established, the scaling behavior of LLM finetuning—how model size, pretraining data size, finetuning data size, and parameter-efficient tuning (PET) parameter size jointly affect downstream performance—remains largely unexplored. This paper systematically investigates these scaling relationships in the data-limited regime where finetuning data is much smaller than the LLM, a setting that better reflects practical LLM deployment.

## Input / Output

The method takes pretrained bilingual decoder-only LLMs (1B–16B parameters, trained on 84B–283B tokens) and finetuning corpora of varying sizes (8K–25M examples) as input, and produces fitted multiplicative scaling law parameters (A, E, α, β) that predict token-level perplexity as a function of any scaling factor and finetuning data size. Evaluation uses PPL for scaling law fitting, BLEURT for translation, and RougeL for summarization, with three random runs averaged to reduce noise. The paper also derives critical finetuning data sizes at which different finetuning methods cross over in performance, and reports zero-shot and few-shot generalization metrics on Flores200.

## Method

The core contribution is a multiplicative joint scaling law L̂(X, D_f) = A · X^(−α) · D_f^(−β) + E that models the interaction between finetuning data size D_f and each other scaling factor X (LLM model size, pretraining data size, or PET parameter size). The authors demonstrate this multiplicative formulation generalizes better than the additive alternative from Hoffmann et al. (2022) by comparing held-out fitting errors across all three finetuning methods. Parameters are estimated via Huber loss (δ=0.001) with L-BFGS, using a joint fitting strategy where β and E are shared across scaling factors to reduce overfitting and improve extrapolation. The study covers three finetuning methods—full-model tuning (FMT), prompt tuning (varying prompt length 50–600), and LoRA (varying rank 4–128)—across two bilingual LLM families (En-De, En-Zh) and three downstream tasks (WMT14 En-De, WMT19 En-Zh translation, and MLSum multilingual summarization), revealing that LLM model scaling benefits finetuning more than pretraining data scaling, PET parameter scaling is largely ineffective, and the optimal method is highly task- and data-dependent.

## Results

The proposed multiplicative joint scaling law (Eq. 1) achieves lower average held-out fitting errors than the additive formulation across all three tasks (e.g., 0.0048 vs 0.0079 for LLM model size on WMT14 En-De). The fitted scaling exponents confirm that LLM model size (α_m = 0.10–0.52) has a consistently larger impact on finetuning loss than pretraining data size (α_p = 0.069–0.21) across all tasks and methods. PET parameter scaling is negligible, with |α_t| < 0.005 in all settings, and even shows inverse scaling (α_t = −0.0017) for LoRA on En-De. The finetuning data exponent β is highest for FMT (0.087–0.15), intermediate for LoRA (0.025–0.081), and lowest for Prompt (0.015–0.051), indicating FMT is most data-hungry.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the additive scaling formulation of Hoffmann et al. (2022), the proposed multiplicative law achieves lower average held-out fitting errors in 5 out of 9 task×factor combinations (e.g., 0.0048 vs 0.0079 for LLM model size on En-De; 0.004 vs 0.005 for PET parameter size on En-De), though the additive form occasionally wins on individual factors (e.g., pretraining data on En-De: 0.0068 vs 0.0069). Among finetuning methods, FMT outperforms both PET methods when finetuning data exceeds the critical threshold (estimated via Eq. 3), while Prompt achieves the best performance with only a few thousand examples. LoRA consistently surpasses Prompt at larger finetuning data sizes due to its higher β and better training stability, whereas Prompt degrades with increasing prompt length due to optimization instability. For zero-shot generalization to related translation tasks, PET (especially LoRA) substantially outperforms FMT, particularly with larger base LLMs, because frozen parameters preserve the pretrained knowledge. The critical finetuning data size at which methods cross over varies dramatically across tasks (Table 5), confirming no universal method selection rule exists.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-variable finetuning data scaling law of Hernandez et al. (2021) into a multiplicative joint scaling law that simultaneously captures the interaction between finetuning data size and other factors (LLM model size, pretraining data size, PET parameter size). It builds on the power-law fitting methodology of Hoffmann et al. (2022) but departs from their additive formulation, showing empirically that a multiplicative form generalizes better in the finetuning regime. The systematic comparison of FMT, Prompt, and LoRA under controlled scaling conditions goes beyond prior work that typically evaluates one or two methods in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with a quantitative framework for selecting finetuning methods based on available data: PET (especially LoRA) for thousands of examples, FMT for million-scale data, with the critical crossover point being highly task-dependent. The finding that PET parameter scaling is largely ineffective challenges the common assumption that increasing LoRA rank or prompt length should yield proportional gains. Key limitations include the restriction to closed generation tasks (translation, summarization) without theoretical grounding for the multiplicative form, and the use of intermediate checkpoints as a proxy for pretraining data scaling, leaving open whether conclusions hold for creative/open-ended generation or multi-task finetuning.
