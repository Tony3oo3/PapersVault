## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient tuning (PET) methods for referring image segmentation focus on vision-language modal fusion but neglect adapting the biased feature distributions inherited from pre-trained models. Additionally, they repeatedly fuse local visual features with textual embeddings at every layer without introducing a global prior from the text input to regularize visual features, leading to off-target interference and sub-optimal segmentation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241327/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 416×416 and patch-tokenized) and a natural-language referring expression (tokenized and projected to match the visual feature dimension) as input. It produces a binary segmentation mask at the original image resolution, obtained by computing the element-wise product of a cross-modal feature and a transformed text feature, applying a sigmoid, reshaping to H/4 × W/4, and upsampling 4×. The entire pipeline is end-to-end trainable with only ~2.2M learnable parameters on a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone.

## Method

BarLeRIa combines two PET modules: a Local Intertwined Module (LIM) and a Global Shortcut Tuning (GST) network. The LIM interleaves projected text tokens with visual patch tokens before each frozen transformer block, using Shortcut Normalizing Flow (SNF) to adapt the residual shortcut distributions in both the visual and textual branches, thereby achieving modal fusion through the original self-attention while correcting feature bias. The GST is a parallel 4-module transformer (feature dim 144) that receives learnable query tokens, text embeddings, and image patches; it injects a global prior—computed as the cosine similarity between concatenated [F_l, F_v] features and the language embeddings—into the MHSA value tokens via an additive projection, providing global text-driven regularization without conflicting with local tuning. Training uses a text-to-pixel contrastive loss that aligns text embeddings with target pixels and repels them from background pixels, requiring no additional pre-training datasets.

## Results

BarLeRIa achieves an average IoU of 66.5 across RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, and G-Ref, surpassing the SOTA PET method ETRIS (62.8) by +3.7 IoU on average. Specifically, it gains +2.01 IoU on RefCOCO, +5.19 IoU on RefCOCO+, and +3.74 IoU on G-Ref. With only 2.2M tunable parameters (0.4%–2.5% of the ViT-B backbone), BarLeRIa also outperforms full fine-tuning SOTA methods like WiCo (65.3) and CRIS (63.8) without requiring additional region-level pre-training datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BarLeRIa outperforms all 14 traditional full fine-tuning baselines (MAttNet through WiCo) by a wide margin, with the largest gap over early methods like RRN (+22.7 Avg IoU) and MAttNet (+16.0 Avg IoU), and a more modest but consistent lead over recent methods like WiCo (+1.2) and LAVT (+1.6). Against the SOTA PET method ETRIS, BarLeRIa achieves +3.7 Avg IoU (66.5 vs. 62.8) using comparable tunable parameters (~2.2M vs. 1.4M), with the largest gain on RefCOCO+ (+4.9 IoU). In comparisons with full fine-tuning SOTA methods that use large models or extra pre-training (Table 2), BarLeRIa (69.8 Avg) matches LISA-7B-ft (69.9) and PolyFormer-B† (69.9) without any additional region-level datasets, and with mixed fine-tuning (BarLeRIa-Mixed†, 73.5) surpasses UNINEXT-R50† (71.3) by +2.2 IoU. On the ViT-Large backbone (Table 3), BarLeRIa-L (72.7) already exceeds PolyFormer-L† (71.6) without extra pre-training, and BarLeRIa-L-Mixed† (75.4) surpasses UNINEXT-L† (74.4) by +1.0 IoU.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parameter-efficient tuning (PET) paradigm from recognition tasks to cross-modal dense prediction, specifically referring image segmentation. It builds directly on SNF (Wang et al., 2023) for shortcut feature adaptation and CRIS (Wang et al., 2022) for the text-to-pixel contrastive objective and segmentation head, but departs from the prior PET-for-RIS work (ETRIS, Xu et al., 2023) by identifying two overlooked issues: ETRIS's bridge module performs only local modal fusion without adapting the biased feature distribution of the frozen backbone, and it lacks any global prior from the text to regularize visual features. The proposed 'intertwined' design—concatenating projected text tokens with visual tokens before each frozen transformer layer—repurposes the original self-attention mechanism as a free fusion operator, while a parallel global shortcut tuning network injects text-derived global priors without conflicting with local adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BarLeRIa demonstrates that a 2M-parameter PET framework can match or exceed full fine-tuning SOTA methods (PolyFormer, UNINEXT) that require additional region-level pre-training on external datasets, offering a practical path to high-quality RIS without expensive pre-training pipelines. The ablation revealing that the local intertwined module alone (0.39M params) already surpasses ETRIS (1.39M params) by +2.6 IoU suggests that *how* modalities are fused matters more than the sheer number of tunable parameters, a useful design principle for future PET architectures. Open questions include whether the global shortcut tuning network (1.8M params, the bulk of the budget) can be made more efficient, whether the approach extends to convolutional backbones, and whether it generalizes to open-vocabulary zero-shot referring segmentation as multi-modal LLMs evolve.
