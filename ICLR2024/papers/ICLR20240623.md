## Problem

Few-shot test-time domain adaptation (FSTT-DA) requires extracting domain-specific knowledge from only a handful of unlabeled target images, yet prior methods either store domain knowledge in expensive expert networks or learn source prompts independently from the adaptation process. Critically, none leverage foundation models like CLIP whose frozen features already exhibit strong out-of-distribution generalization, and they lack domain-centric training that jointly learns transferable source knowledge and the adaptation mechanism.

## Input / Output

At test time, the method consumes a small mini-batch of 16 unlabeled RGB images from an unseen target domain to generate a single domain prompt, which is then reused for all subsequent inferences on that domain's images. The CLIP image encoder is entirely frozen and accessed only via pre-computed embeddings (black-box setting), so no gradients flow through the backbone. Outputs are standard class labels for classification tasks (iWildCam, FMoW, Camelyon17, DomainNet) or continuous values for regression (PovertyMap), with the method handling both without architectural changes.

## Method

VDPG builds a shared learnable knowledge bank (KB) across all source domains, regularized by an orthogonality loss to preserve per-vector specialty. A conditional domain prompt generator uses cross-attention with the KB as queries and CLIP embeddings of the few-shot support set as keys/values, condensing transferable knowledge into a domain-specific prompt via average pooling. A bidirectional cross-attention guidance module then mixes this prompt (with a learnable [CLS] token) into the CLIP features of every target image to produce predictions. Training employs episodic meta-learning—each episode samples one domain, generates a prompt from its support set, and evaluates on a disjoint query set—while a domain-aware contrastive loss (soft-nearest-neighbor) enforces intra-domain prompt similarity and inter-domain discrepancy, purifying the prompts of category-specific information. The entire pipeline operates on frozen CLIP features, requiring no backbone access and enabling gradient-free, single-adaptation inference suitable for on-device deployment.

## Results

On WILDS, VDPG achieves 78.8% accuracy and 46.5% Macro F1 on iWildCam, 96.0% accuracy on Camelyon17, and 61.9% average accuracy on FMoW, outperforming the best CNN-based baselines (MABN, Meta-DMoE) and the CLIP-based FLYP on 3 of 4 datasets. On DomainNet, VDPG reaches 59.8% average accuracy, surpassing the previous best CLIP-based method MIRO (54.0%) by 5.8 points and the zero-shot CLIP baseline (57.1%) by 2.7 points. The method also handles the regression task on PovertyMap (0.83 Pearson r) while using only 3 of 8 input channels (38% data utility).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VDPG outperforms all CNN-based baselines (ERM, CORAL, IRM, ARM, Meta-DMoE, MABN) on iWildCam Macro F1 (46.5 vs. 38.3 for MABN) and Camelyon17 accuracy (96.0 vs. 92.4 for MABN). Against the CLIP-based FLYP, VDPG wins on iWildCam F1 (46.5 vs. 41.9) and Camelyon17, but FLYP retains a slight edge on FMoW average accuracy (63.3 vs. 61.9). On DomainNet, VDPG (59.8% avg) beats DoPrompt (48.3%), MIRO (54.0%), and all CNN-based methods (Mixup 39.2%, CORAL 41.5%, MTL 40.6%, SegNet 40.3%, ARM 35.5%, Meta-DMoE 44.2%, MABN 45.5%). Notably, VDPG surpasses the zero-shot CLIP baseline (57.1%) on DomainNet without any finetuning of the backbone, whereas finetuning CLIP (ERM at 44.4%) actually degrades performance below zero-shot. The method also handles the PovertyMap regression task (0.83 Pearson r) where FLYP cannot operate due to its language-vision training paradigm.

## Relation to Prior Work

VDPG builds on the Few-Shot Test-Time Domain Adaptation (FSTT-DA) formulation established by Meta-DMoE (Zhong et al., 2022) and MABN (Wu et al., 2024), but departs from their CNN-based mixture-of-experts or batch-normalization approaches by operating entirely on frozen CLIP visual features. It extends the visual prompt tuning line (DoPrompt, Zheng et al. 2022; VPT, Bahng et al. 2022) by replacing per-domain static prompts with a shared learnable knowledge bank and a conditional cross-attention generator that produces domain-specific prompts from few-shot data. The black-box, gradient-free adaptation setting follows Oh et al. (2023) and Ouali et al. (2023), while the episodic/meta-learning training paradigm draws from few-shot classification literature (Chi et al., 2022) rather than the standard ERM training used by prior FSTT-DA methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling domain adaptation without accessing foundation model weights, requiring only a single forward pass over 16 images per target domain, making it suitable for on-device deployment and API-only model access. A key limitation is that performance is upper-bounded by the foundation model's inherent OOD generalization, and CLIP's 3-channel input constraint reduces data utility on multi-channel benchmarks like PovertyMap. Open questions include whether the knowledge bank and contrastive loss generalize to even larger or more diverse foundation models (e.g., DINOv2, where gains are smaller), and whether the episodic training requirement can be relaxed for settings with very limited source domain diversity.
