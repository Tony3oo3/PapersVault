## Problem

While LLMs demonstrate strong generalization in language and text-based decision-making, no prior work has shown they can serve as vision-language policies trained via online RL for embodied tasks. Existing embodied AI approaches either depend on prohibitively expensive expert demonstration data (PaLM-E, RT-2) or use LLMs only in text-only settings, leaving open whether the world knowledge encoded in LLMs can transfer to visual, multi-task embodied decision-making with generalization to novel instructions and behaviors.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a natural language instruction (embedded via the LLM's own token embeddings) and a sequence of up to 32 egocentric 256×256 RGB frames from a head-mounted camera, along with robot joint positions and base egomotion. Visual frames are encoded by a frozen VisualCortex (VC1) ViT encoder, with the [CLS] token linearly projected to the LLM's embedding dimension. The output is a categorical distribution over 70 parameterized high-level skills (e.g., pick(apple), navigate(fridge), open(cabinet)) plus a scalar value estimate for the PPO update.

## Method

LLaRP strips the input and output layers from a frozen LLaMA-7B model, retaining only the transformer backbone as a sequence processor. Two lightweight learnable adapter modules are added: a visual observation encoder (frozen VC1 ViT + linear projection) that maps each RGB frame to a single D-dimensional embedding, and a 2-layer action output MLP (hidden dim 512, ReLU, LayerNorm) that converts the backbone's hidden states into action distributions and value estimates. The instruction tokens and observation embeddings form a single sequence fed to the frozen LLM backbone, and only the hidden outputs at observation positions are decoded into actions. Training uses DD-PPO with the LLM backbone and visual encoder fully frozen, so only the adapter modules (~tens of millions of parameters) are updated, preserving the LLM's pre-trained language reasoning while learning embodied behavior from sparse environmental rewards. This design yields a 1.7× improvement over the best non-LLM baseline on unseen tasks and substantially better sample efficiency than training a same-architecture transformer from scratch.

## Results

On the Language Rearrangement benchmark (1,000 unseen tasks), LLaRP achieves a 42% aggregate success rate, outperforming the next best baseline LSTM-Flan (25%) by 1.7× and zero-shot ChatGPT (22%) by 1.9×. On Paraphrastic Robustness, LLaRP scores 38% vs. 23% (LSTM-Flan) and 21% (ZS-ChatGPT); on Behavior Generalization, it scores 45% vs. 28% and 23% respectively. On Atari 2600 (55 games), LLaRP achieves 3–4× higher mean, median, and interquartile mean scores than LLaRP-Scratch, and outperforms it on 43 of 55 games. LLaRP also converges ~10× faster than LLaRP-Scratch (50M vs. 500M steps) and is 3× more sample-efficient than LSTM-Flan in continual learning (500k vs. 1.5M steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLaRP outperforms all seven baselines across every evaluation category. Against the closest learned baseline, LSTM-Flan (which uses Flan-T5 to encode instructions into a fixed-length vector fed to an LSTM), LLaRP leads by 17 points overall (42% vs. 25%) and by 17 points on Behavior Generalization (45% vs. 28%), indicating that using the LLM as a full decoder rather than an encoder is critical for generalizing to novel behaviors. Against zero-shot LLM baselines, LLaRP beats ZS-ChatGPT (22%) by 20 points and ZS-LLaMA (instruction-tuned LLaMA-65B, Touvron et al., 2023) by 30 points, showing that RL training on top of a frozen LLM substantially exceeds what prompting alone achieves. The multimodal zero-shot baseline ZS-Flamingo (IDEFICS, an open-source Flamingo reproduction based on LLaMA-65B, Laurençon et al., 2023) performs worst at 6%, suggesting that without task-specific RL training, even large VLMs struggle with embodied rearrangement. LLaRP-Scratch (same architecture but trained from scratch with 2B parameters) scores only 17%, confirming that pre-trained LLM weights are essential. On Atari, LLaRP's 3–4× advantage over LLaRP-Scratch further validates that the frozen LLM backbone transfers useful world knowledge beyond language-conditioned tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

LLaRP sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) LLMs as zero-shot or fine-tuned policies for text-based decision making (Code as Policies, Voyager), (2) VLMs for embodied AI trained on expert demonstrations (PaLM-E, RT-2), and (3) online RL for language-conditioned rearrangement (Habitat, ALFRED). The key departure is that no prior work trains a frozen LLM backbone as a vision-language policy via online RL for generalization; existing embodied LLM approaches rely on large-scale imitation data, while prior online RL agents use smaller non-LLM architectures. The paper also introduces the Language Rearrangement benchmark, which extends the Rearrangement task (Batra et al., 2020) to 150k instructions with complex logical concepts (conditionals, universal quantification) and a dual-axis generalization evaluation (paraphrastic robustness and behavior generalization) absent from prior benchmarks like ALFRED.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that RL outperforms imitation learning at matched data budgets for LLM-based embodied policies challenges the dominant paradigm of scaling expert demonstrations (as in RT-2, PaLM-E) and suggests a more data-efficient path to language-conditioned robot control. The Language Rearrangement benchmark (150k tasks, 18× ALFRED) provides a much-needed stress test for generalization in embodied AI. However, the 42% success rate on unseen tasks and near-zero performance on multi-object rearrangement reveal substantial gaps; the 7B+ parameter requirement and the action decoder that may suppress the LLM's world knowledge are practical limitations. Open questions include whether the LLM's language head can directly output actions without a separate decoder, and whether these results transfer to real-robot settings with continuous control.
