## Problem

Category-level 3D pose estimation methods that perform domain adaptation typically require 3D data (depth maps, point clouds, or CAD models) in the target domain, or access to source data during adaptation. This paper addresses the previously unaddressed setting of source-free, image-only unsupervised domain adaptation, where only unannotated RGB images from a nuisance-ridden target domain are available and no source data or 3D annotations are accessible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241268/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes unannotated RGB images from the target domain and a pre-trained source model consisting of a ResNet-50 CNN backbone and a per-category cuboid neural mesh with learnable vertex features. No depth maps, point clouds, CAD models, or 3D annotations are provided during adaptation or inference. The output is a 3D pose parameterized by azimuth, elevation, and in-plane rotation, evaluated via rotation matrix error against ground truth at π/6 and π/18 thresholds.

## Method

3DUDA exploits two key observations: (1) local object subparts remain robust across OOD shifts, and (2) local pose ambiguity allows updating individual vertex features even when the global pose estimate is incorrect. The method uses Selective Vertex Adaptation (SVA), which independently measures the vMF likelihood similarity between each rendered mesh vertex feature and its corresponding CNN image feature, updating only vertices that exceed a similarity threshold to avoid corrupting the model with OOD noise. Training proceeds in an EM fashion, alternating between selective vertex feature updates (averaging target-domain features for robust vertices) and CNN backbone fine-tuning via a contrastive render-and-compare loss. Multi-pose initialization pre-renders multiple candidate poses to handle the OOD loss landscape's multiple local optima. A theoretical result shows that SVA simulates fine-tuning on a global pseudo-labeled dataset under a piece-wise support overlap assumption, with the standard global pseudo-labeling setup as a special case (K=1 partition).

## Results

On the primary OOD-CV benchmark (Combined nuisance), 3DUDA achieves 94.0% π/6 accuracy and 87.8% π/18 accuracy, dramatically outperforming the best baseline (Res50-General at 51.8% / 18.1%). The method maintains strong performance across individual nuisance types, reaching 97.0% π/6 on texture shift and 95.1% on pose shift. In the extreme UDA setup combining real nuisances, synthetic corruption, and partial occlusion (L2+Speckle), 3DUDA still achieves 60.6% π/6 accuracy while the best baseline (NeMo) drops to 15.1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3DUDA outperforms all five baselines by a wide margin across every nuisance type and metric. Against the closest methodological competitor NeMo (Wang et al., 2021a), 3DUDA improves π/6 accuracy from 48.1% to 94.0% (+45.9 pts) and π/18 from 21.7% to 87.8% (+66.1 pts) on the Combined OOD-CV setting. DMNT (Wang et al., 2023) and P3D (Yang et al., 2023) perform comparably to NeMo (50.0% and 48.2% π/6 respectively), all far below 3DUDA. Interestingly, the simple Res50-General classifier (51.8% π/6) slightly outperforms the more complex NeMo and DMNT models, suggesting that multi-category training provides some inherent OOD robustness, yet it is still nearly doubled by 3DUDA. In the extreme UDA setup (real nuisance + synthetic corruption + occlusion), the gap widens further: 3DUDA achieves 60.6–84.6% π/6 while all baselines collapse to 15–32%, confirming that the selective vertex adaptation mechanism is critical under compounded domain shifts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feature-level neural render-and-compare paradigm established by NeMo (Wang et al., 2021a), DMNT (Wang et al., 2023), and P3D (Yang et al., 2023) from fully supervised category-level 3D pose estimation to a source-free, image-only unsupervised domain adaptation setting. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of Selective Vertex Adaptation (SVA), which exploits the observation that individual mesh vertex features (corresponding to local object parts) remain robust across domain shifts even when the global pose estimate is incorrect. The method also draws on OOD detection literature (Du et al., 2022) for its vMF-based similarity measure and on the EM framework to alternate between vertex feature updates and backbone fine-tuning, providing a theoretical guarantee that SVA asymptotically simulates global pseudo-labeling under a piece-wise support overlap assumption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practically critical gap: deploying category-level 3D pose estimation in new real-world environments where no 3D annotations, depth maps, or point clouds are available for the target domain. The method's reliance on simple cuboid meshes (rather than detailed CAD models) and its ability to handle combined nuisances (real-world OOD + synthetic corruption + occlusion) makes it applicable to robotics and AR deployment scenarios. Limitations include dependence on the source model quality, inability to handle articulated objects, and the need for iterative optimization at inference time. Open questions include whether the concentration parameter κ can serve as a principled uncertainty signal for harder domain shifts, and whether the approach extends to 6D pose estimation or truly unseen object categories.
