## Problem

Existing codebook-based face restoration methods rely on a single encoder pre-trained on high-quality (HQ) data to encode low-quality (LQ) inputs, introducing domain bias and imprecise feature representation due to the significant domain gap between LQ and HQ images. This neglects the LQ domain's inherent visual characteristics and statistical properties, which contain valuable identity and content information that could supplement lost details during restoration.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512×3 RGB low-quality face image (degraded by Gaussian blur, downsampling, additive noise, and JPEG compression) and produces a 512×512×3 high-quality restored face image. Internally, both HQ and LQ encoders produce quantized features of size 16×16×256, which are mapped to a shared codebook of 1,024 code items (256 channels each). The final restoration is generated by a frozen HQ decoder that reconstructs the image from predicted code indices in the HQ codebook.

## Method

DAEFR is a three-stage dual-encoder framework. First, separate HQ and LQ autoencoders with discrete codebooks are trained via self-reconstruction using VQGAN-style losses (L1, perceptual, adversarial, and code-level). Second, a CLIP-inspired association stage flattens encoder outputs into patches, constructs a cosine-similarity matrix, and applies cross-entropy loss to maximize diagonal similarity, aligning the two encoders into a shared feature space while preserving spatial relationships. Third, a multi-head cross-attention module fuses the associated features (LQ features as queries, HQ features as keys and values), and a transformer predicts code indices in the frozen HQ codebook for final image reconstruction. The key design choice is training the LQ encoder exclusively on degraded data to capture domain-specific statistics, then using the association stage to bridge the domain gap rather than forcing one encoder to handle both distributions.

## Results

On the real-world WIDER-Test dataset, DAEFR achieves the best FID of 36.72, outperforming CodeFormer (38.80) and GFP-GAN (39.73). On BRIAR-Test, DAEFR attains the best FID (90.03) and NIQE (4.65), while on LFW-Test it achieves the best NIQE (3.55). On the synthetic CelebA-Test with ground truth, DAEFR is competitive, achieving the second-best FID (52.06), LPIPS (0.388), IDA (1.071), and LMD (5.634), though CodeFormer leads on PSNR (21.45 vs. 19.92) and SSIM (0.575 vs. 0.553).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAEFR achieves the best FID on both WIDER-Test (36.72 vs. CodeFormer's 38.80) and BRIAR-Test (90.03 vs. GPEN's 91.65), and the best NIQE on LFW-Test (3.55 vs. VQFR's 3.589) and BRIAR-Test (4.65 vs. VQFR's 4.97), demonstrating superior perceptual quality on real-world data. On the synthetic CelebA-Test, CodeFormer remains the strongest baseline, leading on PSNR (21.45 vs. 19.92), SSIM (0.575 vs. 0.553), LPIPS (0.365 vs. 0.388), IDA (1.049 vs. 1.071), and LMD (5.381 vs. 5.634), while GFP-GAN achieves the best FID (46.13 vs. 52.06). DAEFR's advantage over CodeFormer is most pronounced under severe real-world degradation, as evidenced by the widening face-recognition verification gap on LFW: at the most severe turbulence level (40,000), DAEFR scores 73.78% vs. CodeFormer's 71.67%. Against diffusion-based DR2, DAEFR substantially outperforms on all real-world FID scores (e.g., 36.72 vs. 45.73 on WIDER-Test) and NIQE (3.55 vs. 5.15 on LFW-Test). Overall, DAEFR trades a small amount of pixel-level fidelity (PSNR/SSIM) on synthetic data for significantly better perceptual quality and identity preservation on real-world, heavily degraded inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAEFR extends the codebook-prior paradigm for blind face restoration established by CodeFormer, RestoreFormer, and VQFR, which all rely on a single encoder pre-trained on HQ data to encode LQ inputs. The key departure is the introduction of a dedicated auxiliary LQ encoder trained exclusively on degraded data, addressing the domain bias inherent in reusing an HQ-trained encoder. The feature association stage borrows the contrastive alignment mechanism from CLIP to bridge the HQ–LQ feature space, and the fusion step uses multi-head cross-attention (as in RestoreFormer) to merge the two domain-specific representations before code prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-encoder + association design offers a principled solution to the domain-gap problem that plagues all single-encoder codebook methods, and the ablations convincingly isolate the contribution of each component. However, the method still depends on the FFHQ-trained HQ codebook and decoder, limiting generalization to out-of-distribution faces (e.g., extreme poses, as acknowledged in the appendix). Open questions include whether the association stage can be replaced by lighter-weight alignment, whether the approach extends to other restoration domains (hands, text), and whether the LQ encoder can be made adaptive to unknown degradation types without retraining.
