## Problem

Quality-diversity (QD) search algorithms require hand-designed measures of quality and diversity, which is infeasible for qualitative domains like creative writing where notions of genre, sentiment, and quality are subjective and hard to specify algorithmically. While LMs can now generate text variation and evaluate quality via AI feedback, no prior work had leveraged LMs to also evaluate qualitative diversity axes, leaving QD search inapplicable to open-ended creative domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240843/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language task prompt (e.g., 'write a short story about a spy and a politician'), a small set of hand-chosen few-shot seed examples spanning the diversity space, and natural-language specifications of diversity axes (e.g., 'genre: romance vs. horror'). It produces a MAP-Elites archive — a grid of bins where each bin corresponds to a region of the diversity space and holds the highest-quality text found for that region. For the 2D Stories domain, the archive is a 20×20 grid; for 1D domains it is a 20-bin 1D grid. Quality and diversity scores are derived from log-probabilities of LM token predictions rather than explicit scalar outputs.

## Method

QDAIF extends MAP-Elites by replacing all three hand-crafted components with LM-based operators: (1) mutation via LMX, which concatenates a few existing archive solutions into a few-shot prompt to elicit new similar candidates; (2) quality evaluation by prompting an LM to answer 'yes' or 'no' to whether the text is high-quality, using the log-probability ratio as the fitness score; and (3) diversity evaluation by prompting the LM to classify the text along a natural-language-defined axis (e.g., sentiment, genre), using the log-probability of the predicted label as the diversity coordinate. A key design choice is non-uniform bin discretization (denser at range ends) to compensate for the non-linear calibration of instruction-tuned LMs on label prediction. For the poetry domain with GPT-4, the mutation operator is replaced by LMX-rewrite, which instructs the model to produce a new poem with a different genre and tone while preserving inspiration from the parent, and diversity is assessed via categorical label selection from a fixed set (e.g., haiku/sonnet/ballad/limerick/hymn).

## Results

QDAIF achieves significantly higher QD scores than all baselines across the Opinions, Stories-Genre, Stories-Ending, and Stories-Genre-and-Ending domains (Figure 3). In the Poetry domain with GPT-4, QDAIF reaches a QD score of 130 (CI: 118–145), compared to 76 (CI: 67–85) for Random-Poems and 99 (CI: 72–117) for Fixed Seed Rewrite. In human evaluation (Table 1), QDAIF attains a Human QD score of 0.772 and a quality rating of 3.90/5, with 83.3% agreement between human and AI diversity labels—competitive with or better than all baselines.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fixed-Few-Shot | 0.767 | 4.133 | 0.800 | 0.867 |
| Shuffling-Few-Shot | 0.696 | 3.500 | 0.700 | 0.667 |

| LMX, Quality-Only | 0.650 | 3.533 | 0.633 | 0.733 |
| **QDAIF (ours)** | **0.772** | 3.900 | **0.833** | 0.800 |

## Baselines & Comparisons

QDAIF outperforms LMX, Quality-Only (Meyerson et al., 2023) in QD score across all four creative-writing domains, demonstrating that single-objective quality optimization alone cannot guide search toward diverse, high-quality texts; in human evaluation, QDAIF's Human QD score (0.772) exceeds LMX Quality-Only (0.650) by 0.122. Against the ROUGE-L-based diversity baseline (inspired by Self-Instruct, Wang et al., 2022), QDAIF achieves substantially higher QD scores, as n-gram-based filtering fails to capture qualitative diversity dimensions like genre or sentiment. Compared to Novelty Search through AI Feedback (NSAIF, based on Lehman & Stanley, 2011), QDAIF consistently achieves higher QD scores and better sample efficiency, though NSAIF is competitive in the 2D Stories-Genre-and-Ending domain. QDAIF also outperforms simpler baselines (Fixed-Few-Shot, Shuffling-Few-Shot, Random-Search) in computed QD score, while matching or exceeding them in human-evaluated quality-diversity, with the notable exception that Fixed-Few-Shot receives a slightly higher average quality rating (4.13 vs. 3.90) but fails to populate diverse niches effectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

QDAIF unifies three previously separate research threads: Evolution through Large Models (ELM), which uses LMs as variation operators; Quality-Diversity (QD) search (MAP-Elites), which maintains diverse high-quality solution archives; and AI feedback, which leverages instruction-tuned LMs to evaluate generated content. The key conceptual departure is using LMs to evaluate *diversity* attributes (e.g., genre, sentiment) in addition to quality, removing the need for hand-crafted diversity measures that have limited QD to domains with algorithmically specifiable metrics. This extends prior single-objective AI-feedback work (self-refine, Constitutional AI) and prior ELM work (which used LMs for variation but relied on external quality signals) into a fully LM-driven QD loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QDAIF provides a practical recipe for open-ended search in qualitative domains (creative writing, and potentially vision-language or code) where diversity and quality are inherently subjective, opening a path toward AI systems that can independently search, diversify, and evaluate. Key limitations remain: reward hacking emerges at high AI-feedback confidence (0.995–1.0), diversity axes must still be manually specified (the system won't autonomously discover that character interaction matters in stories), and the approach inherits sensitivity to few-shot prompt ordering. Open questions include whether ensemble evaluators or RLHF-tuned critics can mitigate reward hacking, whether LMs can suggest their own diversity measures, and how the method scales to multi-modal generation.
