## Problem

Existing audio models map inputs to pre-defined discrete label sets and lack reasoning, explanation, and scene-understanding capabilities, while modern LLMs possess strong reasoning but cannot perceive general (non-speech) audio. The paper asks whether a single model can be built that unifies audio perception with advanced reasoning and comprehension, enabling it to listen to, think about, and understand the sound environment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240744/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 10-second audio waveform (converted to a 1024×128 log-Mel spectrogram, split into 512 patches, and compressed to 32 temporal embeddings at 3.2 Hz) concatenated with a free-form text question. It produces a free-form text answer that can be sound class labels, an audio caption, a step-by-step reasoning chain, a scene interpretation, or an action suggestion. Unlike CLAP-style models, no pre-defined label set is required at inference; the model directly generates label names or descriptions as text.

## Method

LTU integrates an AST audio encoder (pretrained with CAV-MAE, finetuned on AudioSet-2M) with LLaMA-7B (Vicuna instruction-tuned) via a linear projection layer (768→4096 dims), with LoRA adapters (rank=8, α=16) on key/query projections adding only 4.2M trainable parameters while freezing LLaMA to prevent catastrophic forgetting. Training follows a 4-stage perception-to-understanding curriculum: first training only the projection layer on closed-ended classification and acoustic-feature tasks, then progressively unfreezing all non-LLaMA parameters and increasing task openness from closed-ended to open-ended QA. The OpenAQA-5M dataset (1.9M closed-ended + 3.7M open-ended tuples) is constructed from 8 public audio datasets, with open-ended QA generated via Audio Instruction Generation (AIG), which feeds audio meta-information (events, acoustic features, captions, timestamps) as text to GPT-3.5-Turbo to produce diverse, complex question-answer pairs. The curriculum design is critical: closed-ended tasks with objective answers force the model to condition on audio early, mitigating hallucination before open-ended reasoning is introduced.

## Results

On closed-ended audio classification, LTU achieves an average score of 50.3 across 8 benchmarks (ESC50, DCASE, VocalSound, TUT, Beijing Opera, VGGSound, FSD50K, AudioSet), outperforming CLAP (40.7) by 23.6% relative improvement. On audio captioning, LTU scores 17.0 SPICE on AudioCaps and 11.9 on Clotho (14.5 avg), comparable to SOTA specialized models. On open-ended audio question answering, LTU achieves an 82.9% instruction-following and factual-correctness rate on human-generated questions and a 96.9% instruction-following rate (GPT-4 evaluated), with 74.9% of human evaluators preferring LTU answers over human-written answers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLAP (Elizalde et al., 2023), LTU wins on all 8 classification benchmarks with a 23.6% average relative improvement (50.3 vs. 40.7 avg), and additionally does not require a predefined label set at inference. Against AudioCLIP (Guzhov et al., 2022), LTU scores lower on AudioSet mAP (18.7 vs. 25.9), though AudioCLIP only reports a single benchmark. Against Wu et al. (2023b), LTU trails on ESC50 (83.1 vs. 89.1), but Wu et al. uses a 44.1KHz sampling rate versus LTU's 16KHz. Against Pengi (Deshmukh et al., 2023), the two models perform comparably on closed-ended classification (55.6 vs. 55.1 avg across 6 tasks), but LTU dramatically outperforms on open-ended instruction following (96.9% vs. 3.1–28.3% depending on question source), reflecting LTU's design focus on audio understanding rather than perception-only transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

LTU sits at the intersection of two well-developed but previously disconnected lines of work: audio perception models (AST, CLAP, AudioCLIP) that map audio to fixed label sets, and large language models (LLaMA/Vicuna) that possess reasoning but lack sensory input. The architecture directly composes a CAV-MAE-pretrained AST encoder with LLaMA-7B via LoRA adapters, following the soft-prompt integration pattern established by visual LLMs like LLaVA. The key departure from concurrent audio-LLM efforts (WavPrompt, SpeechGPT, Pengi, AudioGPT) is the focus on general non-speech audio and the introduction of a perception-to-understanding training curriculum, which the authors show is critical for preventing the model from ignoring audio input and hallucinating from language priors alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LTU establishes a practical template for building general-audio LLMs and contributes OpenAQA-5M, the largest and most diverse audio QA dataset to date, which will likely become a standard training resource. The perception-to-understanding curriculum and the finding that open-ended training improves closed-ended perception are actionable training insights for the broader multimodal LLM community. Key limitations remain: 16 kHz sampling and 2× temporal downsampling constrain fine-grained temporal reasoning, the model has limited speech understanding, and the 7B LLaMA backbone may underperform relative to larger LLMs. Open questions include whether scaling the language model, increasing temporal resolution, and adding speech content understanding will yield the emerging capabilities the authors anticipate.
