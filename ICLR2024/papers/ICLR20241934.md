## Problem

It remains unclear for practitioners whether extending an LLM's context window or augmenting it with retrieval yields better performance on long-context tasks, and whether the two approaches can be combined for complementary gains. Prior comparisons have been limited to small models (≤7B) or black-box APIs, leaving the behavior of large (40B+) instruction-tuned LLMs underexplored.

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural-language question or query together with a long context document (average lengths range from ~4.9K tokens for Qasper to ~84K tokens for NarrativeQA). For retrieval-augmented variants, the document is chunked into 300-word segments, and the top-N most relevant chunks (N=5, 10, or 20) are concatenated in relevance order and prepended to the prompt before the dialogue template. Outputs are short generated answers (F1-scored), multi-sentence summaries (ROUGE-scored), or a single selected choice (EM-scored), depending on the task.

## Method

The paper extends the 4K context windows of GPT-43B and Llama2-70B to 16K/32K via positional interpolation on RoPE embeddings (fine-tuned on the Pile for 1000 steps), then performs instruction tuning on a 102K-sample blend of dialogue and QA datasets. At inference, a standalone dense retriever (Dragon, Contriever, or OpenAI text-embedding-ada-002) selects the top-N chunks from the full document, which are inserted as context in the prompt. The key conceptual framing is that retrieval-augmented decoding acts as sparse attention whose sparsity pattern is determined by the retriever rather than predefined. The central empirical finding is that retrieval significantly improves performance at every context window size (4K, 16K, 32K), that 4K+retrieval matches 16K without retrieval at a fraction of the compute, and that the best configuration (Llama2-70B-32k + Dragon top-5 retrieval) outperforms GPT-3.5-turbo-16k and Davinci-003 on average across nine long-context tasks.

## Results

The best model, retrieval-augmented Llama2-70B-32k-ret, achieves an average score of 43.6 across seven long-context tasks (QMSum, Qasper, NarrativeQA, QuALITY, MuSiQue, HotpotQA, MultiFieldQA-en), outperforming GPT-3.5-turbo-16k+ret (42.8) and Davinci-003 (39.2). On the four ZeroSCROLLS tasks (Avg-4*), it scores 43.0 vs. 42.4 for GPT-3.5-turbo-16k and 40.8 for Davinci-003. Retrieval also substantially boosts the 4K-context baseline: Llama2-70B-4k improves from 31.61 to 36.02, approaching the 16K-context score of 36.78 while using far less computation at inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-3.5-turbo-16k+ret, Llama2-70B-32k-ret wins on five of seven tasks (QM: 18.5 vs 17.6; NQA: 31.5 vs 28.8; QLTY: 75.6 vs 72.6; HQA: 53.9 vs 51.6; MFQA: 52.9 vs 52.3) but loses on Qasper (46.3 vs 50.5) and roughly ties on MuSiQue (26.7 vs 26.9), yielding a 0.8-point average advantage. Against Davinci-003 (175B), the gap is much larger: Llama2-70B-32k-ret leads by 4.4 points on Avg-7 and 2.2 points on Avg-4*, with particularly large margins on HotpotQA (53.9 vs 41.2) and NarrativeQA (31.5 vs 24.6). Compared to its own non-retrieval baseline Llama2-70B-32k (40.9), adding retrieval yields a 2.7-point average gain, with the largest improvements on MuSiQue (26.7 vs 19.1) and QMSum (18.5 vs 15.6). The paper also notes that retrieval-augmented 4K Llama2-70B (36.02) nearly matches the 16K-context model without retrieval (36.78), suggesting retrieval can substitute for context extension at lower inference cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed research threads: long context LLMs (via positional interpolation of RoPE embeddings, FlashAttention, and continued pretraining) and retrieval-augmented generation (via dense retrievers like Dragon, Contriever, and OpenAI embeddings). It builds directly on Chen et al. (2023) for context window extension and on the established RAG literature (Lewis et al., 2020; Izacard et al., 2022) for the retrieval component, but its core contribution is a systematic empirical study of how these two approaches interact at scale (43B and 70B parameters). It explicitly departs from the concurrent LongBench work (Bai et al., 2023), which concluded retrieval only helps small/short-context models, by demonstrating that retrieval benefits persist even for 32K-context 70B models, attributing the discrepancy to model scale and zero-shot context incorporation ability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This study provides practitioners with concrete, evidence-based guidance on whether to invest in longer context windows, retrieval infrastructure, or both—a decision with significant compute and engineering implications for production systems. The finding that retrieval improves even 32K-context models challenges the implicit assumption that sufficient context length makes retrieval redundant, suggesting the two techniques are complementary rather than competing. Open questions remain around mitigating the 'lost in the middle' phenomenon, whether findings generalize beyond 32K to 64K+ contexts, and how to best integrate retrieval signals into the attention mechanism rather than simply prepending retrieved chunks. The paper's scope is limited to two model families and English-centric tasks, and the comparison to GPT-3.5-turbo-16k is constrained by its black-box nature.
