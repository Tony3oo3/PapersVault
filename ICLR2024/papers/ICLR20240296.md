## Problem

Conventional pair-based metric learning losses (e.g., Contrastive, MultiSimilarity) scale polynomially with batch size, making them expensive to optimize at scale. While proxy-based methods like ProxyAnchor can convert pair-based losses into cheaper classification-style objectives, they only apply to losses with an anchor concept, leaving symmetric (anchor-free) pair-based losses unaddressed. This paper asks whether mean field theory from statistical physics can serve as a complementary, more general tool for reducing the complexity of pair-based metric learning losses.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (with random resized cropping and horizontal flipping during training, center cropping at evaluation) and produces a d-dimensional embedding vector (128D or 512D in experiments) via a pretrained backbone (BNInception or ViT-B16). In addition to the embedding model parameters θ, the method introduces one learnable mean-field vector M_c per class, which is optimized jointly with θ. The final retrieval output is the embedding vector, evaluated by MAP@R, P@1, and R-precision on image-retrieval benchmarks.

## Method

The paper draws an analogy between ferromagnetic spins and data embeddings, then applies the mean field approximation: each pair-based loss is Taylor-expanded around class-specific mean fields M_c, and all cross-product terms of fluctuations are discarded, reducing O(|D|²) pairwise interactions to O(|D|) sample-to-mean-field interactions. Applying this to Contrastive loss yields MFCont., and applying it to a newly proposed symmetric class-wise variant of MultiSimilarity (CWMS) yields MFCWMS. The mean fields are learned jointly with the embedding model, and a self-consistency condition (analogous to the Weiss mean-field equation) guarantees that at the optimum, each M_c equals the class-average embedding, so no explicit center-update step is needed. A soft regularization term enforces the constraint that inter-class mean-field distances exceed the negative margin, preventing unstable terms from the resummation. Unlike the proxy-based approach, this derivation is based on Taylor expansions and therefore applies to any pair-based loss, including those without anchors.

## Results

Under the modern MLRC protocol, MFCWMS achieves the best MAP@R on CUB (22.1±0.1 at 128D, 27.0±0.1 at 512D) and SOP (44.6±0.2 at 128D, 48.3±0.2 at 512D), outperforming all baselines including ProxyAnchor and ArcFace. Under the traditional protocol, MFCont. and MFCWMS surpass ProxyAnchor on CUB (25.3 vs 25.1), SOP (52.9/52.7 vs 51.5), and InShop (67.7/67.5 vs 65.5) in MAP@R, while also converging 2–4× faster (e.g., 4.4 vs 11.5 epochs on CUB). The only dataset where the mean field losses underperform is Cars, where ProxyAnchor and CWMS retain the edge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ProxyAnchor (the strongest single baseline), MFCWMS wins on CUB (22.1 vs 21.7 at 128D; 27.0 vs 26.5 at 512D) and SOP (44.6 vs N/A at 128D; 48.3 vs N/A at 512D, where ProxyAnchor failed to converge), but loses on Cars (18.9 vs 19.4 at 128D; 27.0 vs 26.8 at 512D). Against ArcFace, both mean field losses win on CUB (22.0/22.1 vs 21.5 at 128D) and SOP (43.6/44.6 vs 41.5 at 128D) but lose on Cars (18.1/18.9 vs 18.3 at 128D). Against MultiSimilarity, the mean field losses consistently outperform on CUB and SOP while trailing on Cars. Against the original Contrastive loss, both MFCont. and MFCWMS improve MAP@R on every dataset (e.g., 22.0 vs 21.0 on CUB 128D; 44.6 vs 41.1 on SOP 128D), confirming that the mean field approximation reduces noise from pairwise comparisons. Against ProxyNCA, MFCWMS achieves a clear margin on SOP (44.6 vs 42.7 at 128D) and CUB (22.1 vs 18.8 at 128D). The consistent loss on Cars suggests that within-batch sample interactions (exploited by ProxyAnchor and CWMS) remain important for that particular fine-grained dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws a direct analogy between ferromagnetic spin systems in statistical physics and embedding spaces in deep metric learning, applying the 1907 Weiss mean field approximation (Taylor expansion around a self-consistent order parameter) to convert O(|D|²) pair-based losses into O(|D|) classification-based losses. It positions itself as complementary to the proxy-based method of Movshovitz-Attias et al. (2017) and Kim et al. (2020), which substitutes positive/negative samples with learnable proxies while retaining anchors; the mean field approach instead operates via Taylor expansion and naturally extends to anchor-free losses like Contrastive and the proposed ClassWiseMultiSimilarity. The derivation of MFCont. from Contrastive loss (Hadsell et al., 2006) and MFCWMS from a class-wise variant of MultiSimilarity (Wang et al., 2019) follows a unified resummation procedure (Eq. 15) that also recovers ProxyNCA as a special case when applied to NCA loss with anchors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is a principled, general recipe for reducing training complexity of pair-based metric learning losses without resorting to mining or sampling heuristics, with the added benefit of faster convergence (4–5 epochs vs. 11–40 for ProxyAnchor). A notable practical finding is that the mean field regularization term (λ_MF) is effectively unnecessary—the self-consistency constraint is satisfied within two epochs even at λ_MF=0—simplifying the method to just learning class-mean embeddings alongside the backbone. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-label settings, whether the phase-transition framing can yield a meaningful 'phase diagram' of metric learning, and why the method underperforms on Cars where within-batch interactions appear critical. The cross-disciplinary connection to statistical physics (Hubbard-Stratonovich transformation, self-consistent equations) may inspire further physics-informed loss design beyond metric learning.
