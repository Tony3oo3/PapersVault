## Problem

Large multimodal models for image-to-text and text-to-image generation have achieved remarkable success almost exclusively in English, while non-English languages remain severely underdeveloped due to the extreme scarcity of high-quality, large-scale image-text pairs. This paper asks whether a pretrained multilingual LLM can serve as a linguistic pivot to transfer multimodal alignment from English to other languages in a (quasi)-zero-shot manner, eliminating the need for native-language multimodal data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241693/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For image-to-text (ViSCPM-Chat), the model accepts an RGB image concatenated with a text instruction or question (in Chinese or English) and generates a free-form text response via beam search (beam size 3). For text-to-image (ViSCPM-Paint), the model takes a text prompt in Chinese or English and synthesizes images at 256×256 or 512×512 resolution using a 50-step DDPM scheduler. Critically, both models are trained exclusively on English image-text pairs yet accept and respond to prompts in the target language without any target-language multimodal pretraining data.

## Method

MPM (Multilingual Pivot Multimodal) decomposes non-English multimodal learning into two stages: (1) multilingual alignment, achieved by directly leveraging a pretrained multilingual LLM (CPM-Bee-10B for Chinese, LLaMA for six languages) that already provides close hidden representations for semantically equivalent text across languages; and (2) multimodal alignment, where visual modules are trained solely on English image-text pairs to align visual features with the LLM's English representations. For image-to-text, a Muffin (BEiT-3-based) encoder is pretrained for 180K steps on 100M English pairs with the LLM frozen, then jointly fine-tuned with the LLM for 80K steps on bilingual instruction data. For text-to-image, only the cross-attention layers of a Stable Diffusion UNet and a linear transblock are trained on Laion-2B English pairs for 300K steps, with the LLM frozen. The key mechanism is that the multilingual LLM's cross-lingual representation alignment (f_σ(y^l_t) ≈ f_σ(y^l_p)) causes the visual-to-text mapping learned in English to transfer zero-shot to the target language, and a small amount of translated instruction data (as little as 0.1%) suffices to calibrate the response language.

## Results

On the LLaVA Test Set in Chinese, ViSCPM-Chat achieves an average score of 90.9 (Con 90.0, DD 87.4, CR 95.0), outperforming all bilingual baselines including Qwen-VL-Chat (88.2) and VisualGLM (82.7), despite using no native Chinese image-text pairs during pretraining. On UniMM-Bench in Chinese, ViSCPM-Chat scores 67.0 average, surpassing Qwen-VL-Chat (64.9) and VisualGLM/Ziya-Visual (59.6). For text-to-image generation, ViSCPM-Paint achieves a zero-shot FID of 10.9 on MSCOCO in Chinese, substantially outperforming AltDiffusion (16.1) and TaiyiDiffusion (15.6), while its English FID of 9.5 is competitive with Stable Diffusion (8.6) and UniDiffuser (9.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In Chinese image-to-text, ViSCPM-Chat (trained exclusively on English image-text pairs) outperforms all bilingual baselines on LLaVA Test Set: it beats Qwen-VL-Chat by 2.7 points (90.9 vs 88.2), VisualGLM by 8.2 points (90.9 vs 82.7), Ziya-Visual by 10.1 points, and mPLUG-Owl by 18.9 points. On UniMM-Bench in Chinese, ViSCPM-Chat (67.0) also leads Qwen-VL-Chat (64.9) and the other baselines. In English, ViSCPM-Chat (81.4 AVG) trails LLaVA (85.6) by about 4 points but exceeds InstructBLIP (80.5) and MiniGPT-4 (69.7), and is roughly on par with Ziya-Visual (81.7) and Qwen-VL-Chat (83.8). For text-to-image in Chinese, ViSCPM-Paint achieves FID 10.9, a 5.2-point improvement over AltDiffusion (16.1) and a 4.7-point improvement over TaiyiDiffusion (15.6), while ViSCPM-Paint+ (with additional Chinese data) further reduces FID to 9.6. In English text-to-image, ViSCPM-Paint (FID 9.5) is competitive with Stable Diffusion (8.6) and UniDiffuser (9.7), and substantially outperforms AltDiffusion (17.2). On the IGLUE benchmark, MPM with LLaMA backbone achieves 46.1 on xGQA-CMN, surpassing UC2 (31.2) and M3P (28.7), confirming cross-lingual generalization beyond Chinese.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the two-stage training paradigm of LLaVA (multimodal pretraining + instruction tuning) for image-to-text and the Stable Diffusion UNet architecture for text-to-image, but introduces a key conceptual departure: using a pretrained multilingual LLM (CPM-Bee or LLaMA) as a fixed 'pivot' that already encodes cross-lingual alignment, so that multimodal alignment need only be learned in English. This contrasts with prior multilingual multimodal efforts (PaLI, UC2, M3P) that jointly optimize multilingual and multimodal alignment from scratch, and with Ying-VLM/MultiFusion which observed cross-lingual transfer but without a systematic formulation. The MPM paradigm is explicitly inspired by Bilingual Dual-coding Theory, framing visual semantics as language-agnostic and decoupling the two alignment stages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: researchers in any non-English language can rapidly obtain competitive multimodal models by training only on English image-text data, bypassing the need for scarce native-language multimodal corpora. The finding that zero-shot English-trained models surpass native-Chinese-trained baselines (e.g., FID 10.9 vs. 15.6 for text-to-image) challenges the assumption that native data is necessary, though the evidence is concentrated on Chinese and a handful of European languages. Open questions include how well this generalizes to morphologically complex or very low-resource languages, whether the reliance on a strong multilingual LLM backbone is a fundamental requirement, and whether the 'quasi-zero-shot' image-to-text setting (requiring a small amount of translated instruction data for language calibration) can be made fully zero-shot.
