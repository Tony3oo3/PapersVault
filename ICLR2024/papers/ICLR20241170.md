## Problem

Existing Riemannian batch normalization methods are either restricted to specific manifolds and metrics (e.g., SPD under the affine-invariant metric), fail to provide principled control over both the Riemannian mean and variance simultaneously, or are derived in an ad hoc manner without general theoretical guarantees. No unified framework existed for batch normalization on general Lie groups that provably normalizes both first- and second-order statistics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes batches of SPD covariance matrices (ranging from 20×20 for radar signals to 93×93 for skeleton sequences) or rotation matrices in SO(3), and produces normalized manifold-valued activations that remain on the same Lie group. The normalization is architecture-agnostic and is inserted after transformation layers in existing SPD networks (SPDNet, TSMNet) or before the LogMap layer in LieNet. A key practical detail is that for pullback metrics like LEM and LCM, all Lie group operations reduce to standard Euclidean batch normalization in the codomain (symmetric or lower-triangular matrix space), making computation fast and simple.

## Method

LieBN defines three operations on a Lie group with left-invariant metric: centering via left translation by the group inverse of the Fréchet mean, scaling via the exponential map at the neutral element applied to a variance-normalized logarithm, and biasing via left translation by a learnable parameter B. The paper proves that the Fréchet mean is the MLE of the Riemannian Gaussian, that left translation preserves the sample mean (homogeneity), and that the scaling operation multiplicatively controls dispersion from the neutral element. For SPD manifolds, the authors introduce a deformation parameter θ via the matrix power function, yielding three parameterized metric families—(θ,α,β)-AIM, (α,β)-LEM, and θ-LCM—that interpolate between existing Lie group structures. A key computational theorem shows that when the metric is a pullback, LieBN in the domain equals mapping to the codomain, performing standard Euclidean BN there, and mapping back, which simplifies implementation substantially.

## Results

On the HDM05 dataset, LieBN-LCM-(0.5) achieves 70.84% mean accuracy, improving over vanilla SPDNet (59.13%) by 11.71% and over SPDNetBN (66.72%) by 4.12%. On FPHA, LieBN-AIM-(1.5) reaches 90.39%, a 4.8% gain over SPDNet and 1.06% over SPDNetBN. On the Hinss2021 EEG dataset, DSMLieBN-AIM-(-0.5) achieves 53.97% balanced accuracy in inter-subject transfer, outperforming SPDDSMBN (50.10%) by 3.87% while also reducing training time. On the G3D rotation-matrix dataset, LieNet+LieBN improves over LieNet from 87.91% to 88.88%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SPDNet without normalization, the best LieBN variant improves accuracy by 2.22% (Radar, AIM-(1): 95.47% vs. 93.25%), 11.71% (HDM05, LCM-(0.5): 70.84% vs. 59.13%), and 4.80% (FPHA, AIM-(1.5): 90.39% vs. 85.59%). Compared to SPDNetBN (Brooks et al., 2019b), which only controls the Riemannian mean under AIM, LieBN consistently wins: +0.62% on Radar, +4.12% on HDM05, and +1.06% on FPHA, while also controlling variance. Against SPDDSMBN (Kobler et al., 2022a) on the Hinss2021 EEG dataset, DSMLieBN-AIM achieves +0.98% in inter-session (55.10% vs. 54.12%) and DSMLieBN-AIM-(-0.5) achieves +3.87% in inter-subject transfer (53.97% vs. 50.10%), with LCM-based variants further reducing training time by roughly half. On the G3D rotation-matrix benchmark, LieNet+LieBN outperforms the original LieNet (Huang et al., 2017) by 0.97% (88.88% vs. 87.91%), confirming the framework generalizes beyond SPD manifolds to SO(n) Lie groups.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and generalizes the line of Riemannian batch normalization work that has been developed in an ad hoc manner for specific manifolds and metrics. Brooks et al. (2019b) and Kobler et al. (2022b) introduced RBN for SPD manifolds under the affine-invariant metric, controlling mean and then variance respectively, while Chakraborty (2020) proposed normalization for Riemannian homogeneous spaces and matrix Lie groups but without principled control over both statistics. The present work departs from these by formulating a single Lie group framework (LieBN) that guarantees control of both Riemannian mean and variance on any Lie group with a left-invariant metric, and further introduces a deformation parameter θ that generalizes the three known SPD Lie group structures into continuous families, building on the power-function deformation concept of Thanwerdas & Pennec (2019b) and the Gaussian distribution definition of Chakraborty & Vemuri (2019).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical guarantee that LieBN normalizes both first- and second-order statistics on general Lie groups fills a gap that prior RBN methods left open, providing a principled foundation rather than manifold-specific recipes. The deformation parameter θ adds a practical tuning knob that can rescue underperforming metrics (e.g., LCM on FPHA), and the pullback-metric simplification (Theorem 5.3) makes the implementation computationally efficient. Limitations include the empirical focus on SPD manifolds (with only preliminary SO(n) experiments), the restriction to batch normalization (though the authors note extensibility to other normalization types), and the fact that population variance control after scaling is generally agnostic outside pullback-from-Euclidean settings. Open questions include extension to special Euclidean groups, other normalization variants (LayerNorm, InstanceNorm), and whether the deformation parameter can be learned end-to-end.
