## Problem

CLIP's dominance in vision-language pretraining is largely attributed to its WIT400M dataset, yet the curation methodology behind it was never fully disclosed. Existing replication efforts (LAION, DataComp) rely on CLIP itself as a blackbox filter, introducing unknown biases and obscuring the actual data distribution. This paper aims to reverse-engineer and formalize CLIP's curation process to make it transparent, reproducible, and independent of any external model.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a raw pool of image-text pairs (e.g., 1.6B pairs from CommonCrawl), a metadata set of ~500K entries reconstructed from WordNet synsets, Wikipedia unigrams (count ≥ 100), high-PMI bigrams, and Wikipedia article titles, and a balancing threshold t (default 20K). It outputs a curated subset where each metadata entry contributes at most t pairs, yielding 400M–2.5B balanced image-text pairs. Notably, the curation operates purely on text (no image access required), enabling integration early in a data pipeline before expensive image downloading.

## Method

MetaCLIP formalizes CLIP's curation as a two-stage algorithm: (1) sub-string matching associates each text with metadata entries, automatically filtering low-quality text (retaining ~50% of English pairs) and building a structured text→entry mapping; (2) balancing via independent sampling caps each entry at t pairs, where tail entries (count < t) are kept with probability 1 and head entries are down-sampled with probability t/count. The key insight is that the threshold t=20K corresponds to the tail-to-head transition in the long-tailed count distribution, and flattening this distribution is as critical as the curation itself—training on the full unbalanced 1.6B pool actually hurts accuracy (61.9% vs 65.5% on ImageNet). Instead of building an expensive inverted index mapping entries to all their texts, the algorithm samples each pair independently, reducing global operations to per-entry counting and enabling scalable, modular integration into data pipelines.

## Results

On zero-shot ImageNet classification, MetaCLIP-400M achieves 70.8% (ViT-B/16), 76.2% (ViT-L/14), surpassing CLIP's 68.3% and 75.5% respectively. Scaling to 2.5B data with the same training budget (12.8B seen pairs) pushes accuracy to 79.2% (ViT-L/14), 80.5% (ViT-H/14), and 82.1% (ViT-bigG/14). On the DataComp 38-task benchmark, MetaCLIP-2.5B achieves 65.6% average (ViT-L/14) and 68.3% (ViT-bigG/14), outperforming CLIP-400M by +4.2% and +6.9% respectively. The 2.5B ViT-B/32 model reaches 67.6% on ImageNet, breaking the previously believed saturation point for B/32 architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetaCLIP consistently outperforms both CLIP (WIT400M) and OpenCLIP (LAION-400M) at the same 400M data scale across all model sizes. On ImageNet zero-shot, MetaCLIP-400M beats CLIP by +2.1% (ViT-B/32: 65.5 vs 63.4), +2.5% (ViT-B/16: 70.8 vs 68.3), and +0.7% (ViT-L/14: 76.2 vs 75.5), while beating OpenCLIP by +2.6%, +3.8%, and +3.5% respectively. On the 38-task DataComp benchmark average, MetaCLIP-400M leads CLIP by +2.0% (ViT-B/32) and +0.8% (ViT-L/14), and leads OpenCLIP by +0.8% (ViT-B/32) and +2.5% (ViT-L/14). Scaling MetaCLIP to 2.5B data yields the largest gains: +7.8% over CLIP on ImageNet for ViT-L/14 (79.2 vs 75.5) and +4.2% on the 38-task average (65.6 vs 61.4). Notably, OpenCLIP underperforms CLIP on ViT-L/14 (72.7 vs 75.5 on ImageNet), confirming that CLIP-filtered LAION data introduces biases that hurt larger models, whereas MetaCLIP's metadata-based curation avoids this issue.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly reverse-engineers the data curation process described (but not fully specified) in CLIP (Radford et al., 2021), reconstructing the 500K-query metadata from WordNet and Wikipedia and implementing the substring matching and per-entry balancing (t=20k) that CLIP's paper only briefly mentions. It departs conceptually from LAION (Schuhmann et al., 2021, 2022) and DataComp (Gadre et al., 2023), which use a trained CLIP model as a blackbox filter to curate data post-hoc; MetaCLIP instead uses a purely NLP-based, model-free curation pipeline that operates before any training. The approach also shares the substring-matching philosophy with CiT (Xu et al., 2023), but extends it with the metadata construction and balancing steps that are specific to CLIP's original design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making CLIP's curation recipe transparent and open-source, this paper removes a key barrier to reproducible vision-language pretraining and gives practitioners a model-free alternative to blackbox filtering pipelines. The finding that simple metadata-based curation with balancing outperforms both raw web data and model-filtered datasets (LAION, DataComp) challenges the prevailing assumption that trained-model filtering is necessary for quality. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond English, whether the specific metadata composition (WordNet + Wikipedia) is optimal, and how the balancing threshold t should be tuned at different data scales. A limitation is that CommonCrawl serves as a proxy for CLIP's unknown proprietary data source, so absolute comparisons to WIT400M are approximate.
