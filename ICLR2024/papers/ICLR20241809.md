## Problem

Infinite-horizon average-reward MDPs (AMDPs) lack a unified theoretical framework for sample-efficient exploration under general function approximation. Prior work is fragmented across tabular, linear, and linear-mixture settings with no single complexity measure that encompasses all tractable AMDP models, and no algorithm that flexibly handles both value-based and model-based paradigms in the infinite-horizon regime.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a hypothesis class H (either value-based, parameterized by paired (Q_f, J_f) functions, or model-based, parameterized by (P_f, r_f) transition-reward pairs), an auxiliary function class G, the known span sp(V*) of the optimal bias function, and a time horizon T. At each step it observes state s_t and outputs a greedy action a_t = argmax_a Q_t(s_t, a). The theoretical guarantee is a high-probability regret bound of Õ(sp(V*) · d · √(Tβ)), where d = max{√d_G, κ_G} is derived from the AGEC and β scales with the log-covering number of H ∪ G.

## Method

LOOP (Local-fitted Optimization with OPtimism) is a modified fitted Q-iteration that combines three key mechanisms: (1) a novel confidence set B_t defined by the cumulative squared discrepancy L_{D_{t-1}}(f,f) − inf_g L_{D_{t-1}}(f,g) ≤ β, which simultaneously controls in-sample training error; (2) a lazy policy-update rule that only re-solves the optimistic planning problem when the discrepancy trigger Υ_t exceeds 4β, adaptively partitioning T steps into O(κ_G log T) epochs to bound switching cost; and (3) optimistic planning that selects f_t = argmax_{f∈B_t} J_f to guarantee J_t ≥ J*. The theoretical analysis introduces the Average-reward Generalized Eluder Coefficient (AGEC), a two-part complexity measure (Bellman dominance + transferability) that extends GEC to the infinite-horizon setting, and decomposes regret into a Bellman error term controlled by d_G and a realization/switching term controlled by κ_G, yielding the Õ(√T) rate.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 3) establishes that LOOP achieves a regret bound of Õ(sp(V*) · d · √(Tβ)), where d = max{√(d_G), κ_G} is determined by the AGEC, β = c·log(T·N²_{H∪G}(1/T)/δ)·sp(V*) is the optimistic parameter, and sp(V*) is the span of the optimal state bias function. This yields a Õ(√T) regret rate, matching the best known rates for special cases like linear AMDPs. The AGEC complexity measure is shown to encompass all previously known tractable AMDP models (tabular, linear, linear mixture, eluder dimension) while also capturing newly identified models (kernel AMDPs, linear Q*/V* AMDPs, and AMDPs with low Bellman eluder dimension).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LOOP is the only algorithm in the comparison that handles all seven AMDP model classes (tabular, linear mixture, linear, eluder, ABE, kernel, and AGEC), while each baseline covers only a strict subset. Compared to SIM-TO-REAL (Chen et al., 2022a), LOOP additionally covers linear AMDPs, ABE, kernel, and AGEC models, and operates under a weaker assumption (Bellman optimality vs. communicating AMDP). Against UCRL2-VTR (Wu et al., 2022), LOOP extends coverage to linear AMDPs, eluder, ABE, kernel, and AGEC, and supports both model-based and value-based paradigms rather than model-based only. Versus FOPO (Wei et al., 2021), LOOP adds linear mixture, eluder, ABE, kernel, and AGEC coverage, and also provides a model-based variant. UCRL2 (Jaksch et al., 2010) is the most restricted, handling only tabular AMDPs under the strongest assumption (communicating with finite diameter). The paper notes that when specialized to concrete models, LOOP's regret bounds are comparable to those of the dedicated algorithms for those special cases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Generalized Eluder Coefficient (GEC) framework of Zhong et al. (2022) from finite-horizon MDPs to the infinite-horizon average-reward setting, introducing the Average-reward Generalized Eluder Coefficient (AGEC) with an additional transferability condition that is specific to the infinite-horizon regime. The LOOP algorithm is a modified fitted Q-iteration (Szepesvári, 2010) augmented with optimistic planning and a low-switching lazy update scheme, building on the Bellman eluder dimension (Jin et al., 2021) and the eluder dimension (Russo & Van Roy, 2013) as intermediate complexity measures. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior AMDP work (Wei et al., 2021; Wu et al., 2022; Chen et al., 2022a) by providing a single unifying complexity measure and algorithmic framework that simultaneously covers model-based and value-based paradigms, rather than treating each structural assumption (linear, linear mixture, eluder) in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first comprehensive theoretical framework that unifies nearly all previously known tractable AMDP models (tabular, linear, linear mixture, eluder-dimension) under a single complexity measure, while also identifying new tractable classes (kernel AMDPs, linear Q*/V* AMDPs, AMDPs with Bellman eluder dimension). The transferability condition in AGEC reveals a structural requirement unique to infinite-horizon settings that has no analogue in the finite-horizon GEC, offering insight into why naive extensions of episodic results fail. Limitations include the absence of computational efficiency guarantees for the constrained optimization, the requirement that the span sp(V*) be known, and the purely theoretical nature with no empirical validation. Open questions include whether sharper regret bounds are achievable, whether the transferability condition can be weakened, and whether computationally efficient implementations exist.
