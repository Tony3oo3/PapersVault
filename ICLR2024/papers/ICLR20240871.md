## Problem

While LLMs can adopt diverse personas for personalization and human behavior simulation, the effect of persona assignment on their fundamental reasoning capabilities remained unexplored. This paper asks whether assigning a socio-demographic persona (e.g., 'a physically-disabled person') to an LLM causes measurable performance degradation on reasoning tasks that are tangential to the persona, and whether such degradation reveals deep-rooted stereotypical biases hidden beneath surface-level alignment.

## Input / Output

The system prompt contains one of three validated persona instructions (e.g., 'You are {persona}. Your responses should closely mirror the knowledge and abilities of this persona.') and the user prompt contains a zero-shot reasoning question from MMLU (multiple-choice), Big-Bench-Hard (Yes/No), or MBPP (code generation with unit tests). Responses are parsed via regex to extract option letters, Yes/No labels, or Python code, then scored against gold answers or test suites. All evaluations use greedy decoding (temperature 0, top-p 1, max 1024 tokens) and report accuracy averaged over 3 runs × 3 persona instructions (9 total runs per persona-dataset pair).

## Method

The study systematically evaluates 19 socio-demographic personas (spanning race, gender, religion, disability, and political affiliation) across 24 reasoning datasets and 4 LLMs (ChatGPT-3.5 June/Nov, GPT-4-Turbo, Llama-2-70b-chat), using Wilson's confidence intervals (α=0.05) to identify statistically significant performance drops relative to a 'Human' baseline persona. Error analysis distinguishes two bias manifestations: explicit abstentions (model refuses to answer citing persona-inadequacy, e.g., 58% of Phys. Disabled errors) and implicit reasoning errors (model answers but makes more mistakes on a shared non-abstained question set). The paper further tests 11 task-agnostic de-biasing prompts (ranging from 'try your best' to 'don't make stereotypical assumptions') and a task-dependent expertise-reframing strategy, finding the former largely ineffective and the latter effective but non-generalizable. Compound persona experiments (13 intersectional combinations) reveal that combining high-bias personas can partially mitigate bias, while combining low- and high-bias personas yields intermediate performance.

## Results

Across 24 reasoning datasets and 19 socio-demographic personas, ChatGPT-3.5 (June 2023) exhibits persona-induced bias in 80% of personas, with the Physically Disabled persona suffering a 33% average relative accuracy drop (affecting 96% of datasets) and the Religious persona showing a 69% drop on college chemistry. The bias is pervasive: some personas show 70%+ drops on individual datasets, and 80%+ of datasets show statistically significant degradation for the most affected personas. GPT-4-Turbo shows the least bias (affecting 42% of personas), while the November 2023 ChatGPT-3.5 model shows bias in 100% of evaluated personas. Task-agnostic de-biasing prompts (e.g., 'don't make stereotypical assumptions') have minimal to no effect, while task-dependent expertise reframing (e.g., 'a physically-disabled historian') reduces bias to near-zero but lacks generalizability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares 19 socio-demographic personas against 'Human' and 'Average Human' baselines, finding that most personas (except Man, Woman, and Caucasian) perform statistically significantly worse than the 'Human' baseline, with Phys. Disabled and Religious showing 35%+ drops. Within the Disability group, the Able-Bodied vs. Phys. Disabled pair shows statistically significant differences on 23 out of 24 datasets (the largest disparity of any group). Within Religion, the Jewish persona outperforms Christian on all STEM categories, and Religious performs significantly worse than Atheist on Computer and Natural Sciences. Across LLMs, GPT-4-Turbo shows the smallest bias (5/12 personas with stat. sig. difference vs. 'Human'), Llama-2-70B-Chat shows more gender and race bias than ChatGPT-3.5 (13 datasets with gender disparity), and the November 2023 ChatGPT-3.5 model shows the worst bias (all 12 personas with stat. sig. drops, with relative changes up to 100% for some persona pairs). The Obama Supporter vs. Trump Supporter pair shows a 27% drop on ethics and 39% on college maths, confirming political bias in reasoning performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the persona-assignment paradigm—previously studied for behavior simulation (Park et al., 2023; Aher et al., 2023) and output toxicity (Deshpande et al., 2023; Wan et al., 2023)—to probe a fundamentally different question: whether adopting a socio-demographic persona degrades an LLM's reasoning accuracy. Prior persona-bias work focused on harmful or stereotypical text in generated outputs, while this paper shifts the lens to task performance (math, law, medicine, coding) measured across 24 standard benchmarks. It also builds on the broader LLM bias literature (Bolukbasi et al., 2016; Rudinger et al., 2018; Zhao et al., 2018) but isolates a previously unexamined trigger: the system-prompt persona instruction itself, rather than input-level demographic cues.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Persona assignment is a rapidly growing practice for LLM personalization and human-behavior simulation, yet this work shows it can silently degrade reasoning by 35–70% for certain socio-demographics, with the bias being both explicit (stereotypical abstentions) and implicit (more reasoning errors without overt refusal). The finding that 11 prompt-based de-biasing strategies are largely ineffective is a significant negative result that pushes the field toward training-time or architectural solutions rather than prompt-level fixes. Open questions include whether compound/intersectional personas amplify or mitigate bias (the paper shows both effects), whether the bias generalizes beyond English and the tested task domains, and how to build alignment that addresses persona-conditioned reasoning rather than only surface-level refusals.
