## Problem

Data-driven autonomous driving methods suffer from dataset bias, overfitting, and uninterpretability, limiting their generalization to unseen scenarios. The paper asks how to instill the knowledge-driven decision-making that characterizes human driving—relying on common sense and causal reasoning rather than pattern matching—into autonomous driving systems, and proposes LLMs as the natural vehicle for this capability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241354/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes raw vehicle kinematic data (position, speed, acceleration of ego and surrounding vehicles) from Highway-env at 1 Hz, which a scenario descriptor transcribes into structured natural-language text. This text is embedded via text-embedding-ada-002 to retrieve top-k similar experiences from a Chroma vector database, then combined with a fixed system prompt and few-shot dialogue pairs to form the LLM input. The output is a single discrete meta-action (accelerate, maintain speed, decelerate, lane-change left/right) extracted from a chain-of-thought reasoning trace; a successful episode requires 30 consecutive collision-free decision frames.

## Method

DiLu comprises four modules: Environment, Reasoning, Reflection, and Memory. The Memory module stores scene-description/reasoning-process pairs, initialized with 5 human-crafted examples and grown through interaction; retrieval is performed by embedding similarity search over the Chroma database. The Reasoning module feeds a prompt (system instructions + current scenario text + k few-shot experiences) into GPT-3.5 with chain-of-thought prompting, and an action decoder extracts the final meta-action from the response. The Reflection module, powered by GPT-4, post-processes each driving session: successful sessions contribute sampled key frames directly to memory, while failed sessions trigger an error-analysis prompt that asks the LLM to identify the faulty reasoning step, produce a corrected decision, and suggest preventive strategies, all of which are written back into the Memory module. This closed loop of reason → reflect → memorize enables continuous, gradient-free improvement and domain-agnostic generalization, as the stored knowledge is environment-independent natural language rather than learned weights.

## Results

In the training environment (lane-4-density-2), DiLu with only 40 accumulated experiences achieves a 70% success rate (SR), matching GRAD's 69% SR that required 600,000 RL training episodes. In the most challenging unseen environment (lane-5-density-3), DiLu maintains a 35% SR without any additional optimization, while GRAD suffers an 85% performance drop. The 5-shot setting with 40 memory items achieves a median of 30/30 success steps (full task completion) in the majority of trials, whereas the 0-shot baseline (no memory) fails all tasks with a median below 5 steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is GRAD (Xi & Sukthankar, NeurIPS Workshop 2022), a graph-representation RL method trained for 600,000 episodes on lane-4-density-2. In the training environment, DiLu slightly edges out GRAD (70% vs. 69% SR) using only 40 text-based experiences versus 600K episodes of gradient-based training. The decisive advantage appears in generalization: when both methods are transferred to the unseen lane-5-density-3 environment (5 lanes, density 3.0), DiLu retains 35% SR while GRAD collapses by 85% (to roughly 10% SR). The paper attributes GRAD's failures to an inability to brake in time, reflecting RL's tendency to overfit the training environment rather than encode human-like driving knowledge. DiLu's experiences, stored as natural-language text, are environment-agnostic and can even be sourced from the real-world CitySim dataset, further demonstrating transferability that the RL baseline cannot match.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes the LLM-agent literature—Voyager's skill library and self-verification, ReAct's reasoning-acting loop, and PALM-E's embodied language grounding—into a closed-loop driving framework, but departs from the dominant data-driven paradigm (behavior cloning, RL) that has governed autonomous driving research. Rather than training a policy on large driving datasets, DiLu repurposes retrieval-augmented generation (Chroma vector store + text-embedding-ada-002) and chain-of-thought prompting as the decision-making engine, with a Reflection module that leverages GPT-4's self-repair capability to correct unsafe decisions. The conceptual lineage is closest to Voyager's lifelong-learning agent architecture, transplanted from Minecraft to a traffic simulation, and explicitly contrasts with Wayve's LINGO-1, which remains an open-loop commentator rather than a closed-loop controller.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a compelling narrative that a handful of natural-language experiences can substitute for hundreds of thousands of RL episodes, and the generalization gap (35% SR vs. 85% performance drop for GRAD in a denser environment) is a concrete demonstration that knowledge-based reasoning transfers where fitted policies do not. However, the practical ceiling is constrained: 5–10 s per decision, a five-action discrete space, a single simplified simulator (Highway-env), and residual LLM hallucinations all limit near-term deployment. Open questions include whether the memory-reflection loop scales to multi-modal sensor inputs, urban intersection complexity, and real-time latency budgets, and whether the CitySim-to-Highway-env transfer generalizes beyond trajectory-level knowledge to richer driving semantics.
