## Problem

Equivariant Transformers such as Equiformer are limited to low maximum irreps degrees (L_max ≤ 3) because SO(3) tensor products scale as O(L_max^6), restricting angular resolution critical for force prediction. While eSCN demonstrated that SO(2) linear operations can reduce this to O(L_max^3) and enable L_max up to 8, it retained a SEGNN-like message passing design rather than the more expressive Transformer architecture. The open question is whether equivariant Transformers can be adapted to actually benefit from higher-degree representations, since naively swapping in eSCN convolutions does not improve over the original eSCN baseline.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 3D atomistic graphs with atomic positions and element types, embedded into equivariant irreps features spanning degrees 0 through L_max (up to 6 or 8) with C channels per degree. Outputs are per-atom forces (extracted from degree-1 vectors via an equivariant graph attention block) and total energies (scalar sum over nodes through a feed-forward head). For the AdsorbML application, the model additionally performs iterative structure relaxations using predicted forces, followed by DFT single-point calculations on the top-k relaxed structures to estimate adsorption energies.

## Method

EquiformerV2 replaces the SO(3) convolutions in Equiformer with eSCN convolutions—rotating irreps features by edge-direction rotation matrices and applying SO(2) linear layers—enabling L_max up to 8 at O(L_max^3) cost. Three architectural improvements are then introduced to make higher degrees actually useful: (1) attention re-normalization adds a layer normalization before the non-linear attention weight computation to keep inputs in a stable range as channel counts grow with L_max; (2) separable S² activation splits degree-0 vectors into a SiLU-activated gate portion and a portion that participates in full S² activation with higher-degree vectors, preventing the gradient instability that plain S² activation causes; (3) separable layer normalization (SLN) normalizes degree-0 vectors with standard mean/std while normalizing all higher-degree vectors jointly by their shared RMS, preserving relative magnitudes across degrees. Ablations confirm that each component is necessary: without them, the eSCN-based Equiformer underperforms the original eSCN model despite using the same convolutional backbone.

## Results

On OC20 S2EF-All+MD, EquiformerV2 (λ_E=4, 153M) achieves S2EF test force MAE of 14.2 meV/Å and energy MAE of 219 meV, improving over the previous state-of-the-art eSCN (15.6 meV/Å, 228 meV) by 9% on forces and 4% on energies. On IS2RS, it reaches 54.4% AFbT (vs. 50.3% for eSCN), and on IS2RE it achieves 309 meV energy MAE (vs. 323 meV for eSCN). On OC22, EquiformerV2 trained on only OC22 (8.4M structures) outperforms GemNet-OC trained on both OC20 and OC22 (~138M structures), demonstrating significantly better data efficiency. In the AdsorbML pipeline, EquiformerV2 (153M) at k=2 achieves 88.9% success rate, exceeding SCN-MD-Large at k=5 (87.8%), requiring at least 2× fewer DFT calculations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EquiformerV2 outperforms all 14 baselines on OC20 S2EF-All+MD across every reported metric. Against the closest competitor eSCN (L=6, K=20), EquiformerV2 (153M) reduces S2EF force MAE from 15.6 to 14.2 meV/Å (9% improvement) and energy MAE from 228 to 219 meV (4% improvement), while also improving IS2RS AFbT from 66.7% to 69.4% and IS2RE energy from 323 to 309 meV. The smaller 31M EquiformerV2 variant matches or beats eSCN on S2EF energy (228 vs. 228) and IS2RS AFbT (68.3% vs. 66.7%) while being substantially faster (7.1 vs. 2.9 samples/GPU-sec throughput). On OC22, EquiformerV2 trained solely on OC22 (8.4M structures) beats GemNet-OC trained on OC20+OC22 (~138M structures) on all S2EF-Total and IS2RE-Total metrics, e.g., S2EF-Total test force MAE of 21.58 vs. 26.9 meV/Å (ID) and IS2RE-Total test energy of 1119 vs. 1200 meV (ID). On QM9, EquiformerV2 wins on 9 of 12 molecular property tasks over Equiformer (e.g., Δε: 29 vs. 30 meV, ε_HOMO: 14 vs. 15 meV), though gains are modest due to the small dataset size. In the AdsorbML pipeline, EquiformerV2 (153M) at k=2 (88.9% success) surpasses SCN-MD-Large at k=5 (87.8%), and the 31M variant is 3.7×–9.8× faster per relaxation than GemNet-OC-MD-Large and SCN while maintaining higher success rates at all k values.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes two parallel lines of equivariant GNN research: the Transformer-based Equiformer architecture (Liao & Smidt, 2023) and the efficient SO(2) convolutions from eSCN (Passaro & Zitnick, 2023) that reduce tensor product complexity from O(L_max^6) to O(L_max^3). The key conceptual departure is the finding that simply transplanting eSCN convolutions into Equiformer does not yield gains over the original eSCN baseline, motivating three targeted architectural modifications (attention re-normalization, separable S² activation, separable layer normalization) that are necessary to unlock the benefits of higher-degree representations. The S² activation is adapted from Spherical CNNs (Cohen et al., 2018) but modified into a 'separable' form that isolates degree-0 channels to prevent training instability at higher degrees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EquiformerV2 sets new state-of-the-art on the large-scale OC20 and OC22 benchmarks with substantial force MAE improvements (~9%) and demonstrates that higher-degree equivariant representations are genuinely beneficial when paired with appropriate architectural design, validating a direction that was previously blocked by computational cost. The 2× reduction in DFT calculations for adsorption energy and the data-efficiency result (OC22-only training outperforming OC20+OC22 GemNet-OC) have direct practical implications for catalysis and materials discovery. A noted limitation is that gains diminish on smaller or less complex datasets (e.g., QM9), suggesting the architecture's expressivity is most valuable for large, diverse systems; the authors suggest pre-training on large datasets as a mitigation. Open questions include whether the architectural principles (separable normalization, attention re-normalization) generalize to other equivariant architectures beyond the Transformer design.
