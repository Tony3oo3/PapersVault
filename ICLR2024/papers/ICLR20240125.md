## Problem

Designing generative self-supervised learning for hypergraphs is non-trivial: the appropriate SSL task, its theoretical connection to downstream tasks, and the empirical properties of learned representations remain unresolved. Naive extensions of graph-level generative tasks (e.g., generating hyperedges from scratch) are computationally prohibitive since the number of potential hyperedges grows exponentially with the number of nodes, and existing hypergraph SSL methods are predominantly contrastive rather than generative.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a node feature matrix X ∈ R^{|V|×d} and a hyperedge set E, producing node embeddings Z ∈ R^{|V|×k} via a UniGCNII encoder. During training, the hyperedge filling objective operates at the node level: for each hyperedge e_j and each member v_i ∈ e_j, the model receives the query subset q_ij = e_j \ {v_i} (aggregated via summation) and must predict v_i from among all |V| nodes. The learned embeddings serve as fixed inputs for linear probes (node classification, hyperedge prediction) or as initializations for fine-tuning.

## Method

HYPEBOY formulates a generative SSL task called hyperedge filling: given all but one node of a hyperedge, predict the missing node, reducing the prediction space from O(2^{|V|}) to O(|V|). The training pipeline has three steps: (1) hypergraph augmentation via Bernoulli feature masking and random hyperedge subsampling to prevent over-reliance on local proximity; (2) encoding with a UniGCNII backbone followed by a node projection head (MLP) and a set projection head (sum-then-MLP, inspired by Deep Sets) to prevent dimensional collapse; (3) a softmax-based filling loss over all nodes for each query subset, whose numerator promotes alignment (pulling the correct node toward its query) and denominator promotes uniformity (pushing all nodes apart). A two-stage training scheme first warms up the encoder with masked feature reconstruction (300 epochs, following GraphMAE) before switching to hyperedge filling, preventing the projection heads from dominating optimization. The paper further provides theoretical guarantees (Theorems 1–2) showing that representations refined by hyperedge filling improve node classification accuracy under homophilic hyperedge structures, with the improvement probability bounded below by 0.5 for any affinity parameter.

## Results

Under the fine-tuning protocol, HYPEBOY achieves the best average ranking (1.7) across all 18 methods on 11 hypergraph datasets, outperforming the second-best SSL method TriCL (A.R. 3.4) by up to 5.0 points in node classification accuracy (e.g., 62.3% vs. 60.2% on Cora, 56.7% vs. 51.7% on Citeseer). Compared to its own backbone UniGCNII, HYPEBOY yields gains of up to 12.5 points across 10 of 11 datasets. Under the linear evaluation protocol, HYPEBOY also obtains the best average ranking (1.5) in both node classification and hyperedge prediction, with up to 6.3 points accuracy improvement over TriCL in node classification and up to 3.2 points AUROC improvement in hyperedge prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HYPEBOY outperforms all 16 baselines in average ranking across the 11 datasets. Among hypergraph SSL methods, it beats the closest competitor TriCL by up to 5.0 points (Citeseer: 56.7 vs. 51.7) and HyperGCL by up to 9.7 points (Citeseer: 56.7 vs. 47.0); both TriCL and HyperGCL are contrastive methods, while HYPEBOY is generative. The generative graph SSL methods GraphMAE2 and MaskGAE underperform HYPEBOY by 5.6–15.6 points, confirming that reducing hyperedges to pairwise edges loses critical higher-order structure. Among semi-supervised HNNs, the strongest is PhenomNN (A.R. 3.9), which HYPEBOY surpasses by up to 6.9 points (Cora-CA: 66.3 vs. 60.8); ED-HNN and AllSet trail further behind. The direct hypergraph extension of graph contrastive learning (H-GD) and the generative hypergraph method HyperGRL are both clearly outperformed, with HyperGRL failing to complete training on 20News within 24 hours. In the linear evaluation setting (Table 2), HYPEBOY also dominates: it leads TriCL by up to 6.3 points in node classification (Cora: 63.5 vs. 62.1) and by up to 3.2 points in hyperedge prediction AUROC (Pubmed: 95.1 vs. 91.9), while MaskGAE and HyperGRL again fail to finish on 20News.

## Relation to Prior Work

HYPEBOY sits at the intersection of generative self-supervised learning (as exemplified by GraphMAE/GraphMAE2 and MaskGAE for ordinary graphs) and hypergraph neural networks (UniGCNII, ED-HNN, PhenomNN). It departs from the dominant contrastive SSL paradigm for hypergraphs (TriCL, HyperGCL) by proposing a generative task—hyperedge filling—that is computationally tractable (O(|V|) prediction space vs. O(2^|V|) for full hyperedge generation) and provides richer per-node signals. The closest prior work, HyperGRL, also pursues generative SSL on hypergraphs but with a different task formulation and methodological design; HYPEBOY additionally introduces a two-stage training scheme (feature-reconstruction warm-up) and Deep Sets–based projection heads to address dimensional collapse and over-reliance on projection layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretically grounded generative SSL framework for hypergraphs, with a formal proof linking the hyperedge filling objective to improved node classification accuracy under homophilic assumptions. This gives practitioners a principled pre-training recipe for HNNs that is task-agnostic and empirically validated across 11 diverse datasets. Open questions include scalability to very large hypergraphs (the softmax over all nodes in the filling loss is O(|V|) per query), whether the theoretical guarantees extend beyond the Gaussian/homophily assumptions, and how the method performs on dynamic or temporal hypergraphs where hyperedge sets evolve over time.
