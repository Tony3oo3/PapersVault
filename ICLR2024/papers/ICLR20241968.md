## Problem

Existing few-shot generative domain adaptation methods adapt a pre-trained generator to a single target domain, but real-world applications often require combining attributes from multiple target domains (e.g., sketch style + smile + baby). This hybrid domain adaptation (HDA) task is fundamentally harder because no real images exist from the hybrid domain, making discriminator-based approaches infeasible, and the extreme scarcity of per-domain references causes discriminators to overfit to easily learned attributes of certain domains while ignoring others.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241968/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained StyleGAN2 generator (source domain, e.g., FFHQ at 256×256), 10 reference images from each of N individual target domains, and a noise vector z, and produces a single adapted generator that outputs images integrating attributes from all target domains while preserving source-domain identity and pose. Notably, no real images from the hybrid domain are ever provided; the method relies entirely on the pre-trained image encoders (Swin, DINOv2) to define target subspaces from the individual-domain references. Adaptation completes in ~3 minutes on a single GPU with 300 iterations and batch size 4.

## Method

The core contribution is a discriminator-free framework that uses pre-trained image encoders as implicit domain classifiers, projecting few-shot reference images into well-separable embedding subspaces via SVD-based projection matrices. A novel directional subspace loss guides adaptation with two components: a distance loss that minimizes the squared distance from generated-image embeddings to each target subspace (weighted by domain coefficients α_i for HDA), and a direction loss that enforces the adaptation vector (f_t − f_s) to be collinear with the perpendicular from the generated embedding to the subspace, thereby preserving source-domain characteristics and preventing model collapse. For HDA, the distance loss sums weighted distances to all N subspaces and the direction loss uses the weighted sum of all perpendicular directions. An ensemble of multiple encoders (Swin + DINOv2) further reduces per-encoder bias, and the entire pipeline requires no adversarial training, no inference-time interpolation, and no per-domain model training.

## Results

On the novel 10-shot Hybrid Domain Adaptation (HDA) task, the proposed method achieves the best CLIP-Score across all three hybrid domains: 25.65 (smile-sunglasses), 23.08 (baby-smile), and 24.91 (baby-sketch), outperforming the strongest baseline NADA by 5.4, 2.6, and 2.9 points respectively. It also leads on Inception Score (2.4–2.97) and Identity similarity (0.223–0.373) across all HDA settings. On single-domain DA, the method similarly achieves the highest CLIP-Score (20.38–24.13), IS (2.23–3.71), and ID (0.353–0.587) across baby, smile, sunglasses, and sketch domains, while completing adaptation in ~3 minutes with a 24M-parameter model regardless of the number of target domains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method wins on every metric in every comparison. On HDA, it outperforms Sequential adaptation (which suffers catastrophic forgetting and collapses to the last domain), DoRM (whose latent-code interpolation is unstable and loses domain attributes), and NADA (which exhibits CLIP-encoder bias, e.g., failing to capture the sketch style or sunglasses attribute) by 5–9 CLIP-Score points, 0.5–1.3 IS, and 0.04–0.10 ID. On single-domain DA, it beats CDC, DoRM, RSSA, AdAM, and NADA across all four target domains; the largest margins appear on IS (e.g., 3.71 vs. 3.36 for NADA on baby) and ID (e.g., 0.587 vs. 0.490 for DoRM on smile), confirming superior fidelity and cross-domain consistency. Notably, the method also matches or exceeds NADA's ID on baby-smile HDA (0.34 vs. 0.34) while substantially improving CS and IS, and it does so with a fixed 24M model size and ~3 min training time versus 240–1200 min for interpolation-based baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the few-shot generative domain adaptation line (CDC, RSSA, AdAM, DoRM) by introducing the hybrid domain adaptation (HDA) task, where a single generator must simultaneously acquire attributes from multiple target domains. It departs fundamentally from the discriminator-based paradigm that underpins most prior DA methods, instead repurposing pre-trained image encoders (Swin, DINOv2) as implicit domain classifiers that project reference images into separable embedding subspaces. The closest discriminator-free prior work, NADA (CLIP-guided), is explicitly positioned as insufficient due to CLIP's semantic bias and inability to capture fine-grained visual attributes like sketch style or sunglasses. The directional subspace loss (distance + direction) is a novel geometric formulation that has no direct analogue in prior DA literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HDA addresses a practical gap: in real applications, one often needs to combine multiple visual attributes (e.g., sketch style + smile + baby) without access to hybrid-domain training images, making conventional DA infeasible. The discriminator-free, subspace-geometric approach sidesteps the fundamental impossibility of training a discriminator on non-existent hybrid images, and the constant model size and ~3-minute training time regardless of domain count offer a clear efficiency advantage over sequential or interpolation-based alternatives. Key open questions include how to automatically determine domain weights (α_i) rather than relying on empirical tuning, whether the subspace separability assumption holds for more overlapping or complex domains, and whether the approach generalizes beyond face-centric StyleGAN2 to broader generative architectures like diffusion models.
