## Problem

While MTRL theory has established statistical efficiency gains from shared structure across tasks, the role of exploration has been largely overlooked. Myopic exploration strategies like ε-greedy are known to have exponential sample complexity in single-task RL, yet they succeed widely in practice. This paper asks whether myopic exploration can be provably sample-efficient in MTRL, and shows that a sufficiently diverse task set is the key condition that makes this possible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240046/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a set of episodic MDPs sharing the same state space, action space, and horizon, along with a function class for Q-value approximation and an exploration parameter ε. In each round it collects exactly one episode per task using a single mixture exploration policy (one task's ε-greedy policy randomly selected for the entire episode), and an offline learning oracle (e.g., fitted Q-iteration) maps each task's accumulated dataset to a greedy policy. The output is a near-optimal policy for every task in the set, with sample complexity polynomial in |M|, H, the Bellman-eluder dimension, and 1/β under the diversity condition.

## Method

Algorithm 1 implements policy-sharing MTRL: each round, an offline oracle produces a greedy policy per task, ε-greedy is applied to each, and a mixture policy (randomly selecting one task's ε-greedy policy for the full episode) is used to collect one trajectory on every task. The core insight is that when the task set is diverse, ε-greedy around other tasks' near-optimal policies provides sufficient state-action coverage for the current task, converting what is myopic in single-task RL into effective exploration in MTRL. The paper formalizes this via a Multitask Myopic Exploration Gap (Multitask MEG) and a diversity condition requiring a lower-bounded gap for all β-suboptimal joint functions, yielding a polynomial sample-complexity bound (Theorem 1). For linear MDPs, diversity is concretely characterized as having one-hot reward parameters at each step so that optimal policies induce a full-rank feature covariance matrix (Theorem 2). The approach connects to Hindsight Experience Replay and automatic curriculum learning, and is validated on BipedalWalker where the diverse task subset matches the task selection of ALP-GMM.

## Results

The primary results are theoretical: Theorem 1 establishes a sample-complexity bound of Õ(|M|² H² d_F^BE d_F^cover · ln c̃(β) / α̃²(β) · ln(1/δ)) for Algorithm 1 under the diversity condition. For the linear MDP case, Theorem 2 lower-bounds the multitask myopic exploration gap as α = Ω(√(β²b₁²/(|A||M|H))), yielding a combined bound of Õ(|M|³ H³ d² |A| / (β² b₁²)). Empirically on BipedalWalker, eigenvalues of the feature covariance matrix are up to 10× higher for environments with stump heights in [1.0, 2.3] compared to extreme heights, while spacing has minimal effect. The paper also shows an exponential separation: single-task MEG can be as small as 2^(−H/2) while multitask MEG remains polynomially bounded for diverse task sets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's empirical comparison is with ALP-GMM (Portelas et al., 2020), a state-of-the-art automatic curriculum learning algorithm. On BipedalWalker, the subset of tasks that produces a more spread covariance spectrum (i.e., the theoretically 'diverse' set) closely matches the task selection density of ALP-GMM during 20M-step training, with both showing strong preference for mid-range stump heights and little preference over spacing. This alignment is qualitative rather than a head-to-head performance benchmark. Theoretically, the paper compares against single-task MEG (Dann et al., 2022): Proposition 1 shows Multitask MEG ≥ Single-task MEG / √|M|, while Proposition 2 demonstrates that single-task MEG can be exponentially small in H (e.g., ≤ 2^(−H/2) for sparse-reward hallways), whereas the multitask version stays polynomially bounded for diverse task sets—establishing an exponential separation in exploration efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MTRL theory line (Brunskill & Li, 2013; Zhang & Wang, 2021; Agarwal et al., 2022) that has focused on statistical efficiency through shared structure, by addressing the previously overlooked exploration dimension. It generalizes the single-task Myopic Exploration Gap (MEG) of Dann et al. (2022) to a multitask setting and shows that a simple policy-sharing algorithm with ε-greedy exploration achieves polynomial sample complexity under a diversity condition on the task set. The mixture-policy mechanism is closely related to Hindsight Experience Replay (Andrychowicz et al., 2017) in multi-goal RL, and the diversity condition connects to reward-free exploration (Jin et al., 2020a) and curriculum learning (Portelas et al., 2020), but the paper's contribution is the first formal demonstration that task diversity converts myopic exploration from exponentially to polynomially sample-efficient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first theoretical explanation for the long-standing puzzle of why ε-greedy succeeds in practice despite being provably exponentially sample-inefficient in single-task RL, and it formally links MTRL exploration benefits to curriculum learning. The empirical validation on BipedalWalker showing that the theoretically diverse task set aligns with ALP-GMM's automatic curriculum selection is suggestive but preliminary (single environment, covariance spectrum analysis only). Key open questions include: how to construct diverse task sets for general (non-linear, non-tabular) function classes, whether the suboptimal sample complexity bound (|M|²S³H⁵A²/β² vs. the independent-learning bound |M|SA/β²) can be tightened, and whether the feature coverage assumption can be removed in the linear MDP case.
