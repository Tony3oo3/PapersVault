## Problem

Existing video editing methods, whether training-based or training-free, fail at fine-grained multi-attribute editing: they omit intended changes, modify the wrong elements, mix separate edits together, and fail to preserve regions that should remain unchanged. This stems from the fact that all semantic changes are entangled within a single cross-attention prompt, with no spatially-disentangled layout information to localize each edit independently.

## Input / Output

The method takes a short source video (8 frames at 512×512), a set of per-frame target groundings (bounding box coordinates paired with entity captions, auto-extracted via GLIP then manually refined), a target text prompt, depth maps estimated by ZoeDepth, and optical flow maps from RAFT. It produces temporally consistent edited video frames in a fully zero-shot, training-free manner—no fine-tuning on any video data is required. The groundings serve a dual role: they direct spatially-localized semantic edits and, when bounding boxes share common outer space, generate a binary inpainting mask to protect unedited regions.

## Method

Ground-A-Video integrates two complementary condition types—spatially-continuous (depth maps via an inflated ControlNet) and spatially-discrete (per-frame groundings via a novel Cross-Frame Gated Attention)—into a refactored Stable Diffusion backbone. The Cross-Frame Gated Attention extends GLIGEN's gated self-attention by computing queries from a single frame's latent plus its grounding tokens while computing keys and values from all frames' latents and grounding tokens concatenated, ensuring the same semantic edit is projected consistently across the video. A Modulated Cross-Attention mechanism addresses the temporal inconsistency introduced by per-frame null-text optimization: in the unconditional CFG branch, it concatenates all frames' optimized null embeddings into a shared key-value space so that variant context vectors can interact rather than producing frame-specific appearance drift. Finally, an optical-flow-guided smoothing step (Algorithm 1) uses RAFT magnitude maps thresholded at 0.2 to identify static regions between consecutive frames and copies latent values from the previous frame in those regions, eliminating flicker without requiring any architectural change to the diffusion model.

## Results

On the DAVIS dataset (20 videos, 8 frames at 512×512), Ground-A-Video achieves the best CLIP text-alignment score (0.837) and frame consistency (0.970) among all compared methods. In the user study (28 participants, 1–5 scale), the method scores 4.13 on Edit-Accuracy and 4.24 on Preserve-Accuracy, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline Tune-A-Video (2.99 and 3.13 respectively). The frame consistency user-study score of 4.01 also leads all baselines, with the closest competitor being Tune-A-Video at 3.05.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ground-A-Video outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against Tune-A-Video (the strongest baseline), it gains +0.027 in CLIP text-alignment, +0.011 in CLIP frame consistency, +1.14 in Edit-Accuracy, +1.11 in Preserve-Accuracy, and +0.96 in user-study frame consistency. ControlVideo, the most closely related training-free method also using ControlNet, trails by a wider margin: +0.015 text-alignment, +0.007 frame consistency, and +1.77 Edit-Accuracy. Control-A-Video and Gen-1, both of which require training on video datasets, are outperformed on all metrics as well, with Gen-1 showing the weakest CLIP frame consistency (0.939) despite its dedicated video diffusion architecture. The largest relative gap appears in the user-study accuracy metrics, where Ground-A-Video's grounding-driven approach avoids the neglected-edit, wrong-element, and mixed-edit failures that plague the baselines in multi-attribute scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the grounded text-to-image generation paradigm (GLIGEN) and structural video guidance (ControlNet) into a unified, training-free video editing framework. It departs from prior zero-shot video editing methods (TAV, ControlVideo, FateZero) that rely solely on attention-map manipulation or single-video fine-tuning by introducing spatially-discrete grounding conditions alongside spatially-continuous depth/flow priors. The key architectural departures are the Cross-Frame Gated Attention (refactoring GLIGEN's per-image gated attention for temporal consistency) and Modulated Cross-Attention (resolving inconsistencies from per-frame null-text optimization), neither of which has a direct precedent in the video editing literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that multi-attribute video editing can be achieved in a fully training-free manner with both spatially-continuous and discrete conditions, this work lowers the barrier to precise video editing for researchers without access to large video datasets. The optical flow smoothing module is framework-agnostic and applicable to any DDIM-inversion-based pipeline, offering a broadly useful tool. Key limitations include dependence on accurate (often manually refined) groundings, restriction to short 8-frame clips at 512×512, and the structural rigidity inherent to ControlNet. Open questions include whether automated grounding refinement can eliminate the manual step, whether the approach scales to longer/higher-resolution videos, and whether the continuous+discrete condition framework generalizes beyond depth and bounding boxes.
