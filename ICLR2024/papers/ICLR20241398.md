## Problem

Deep learning knowledge tracing models achieve high predictive accuracy but sacrifice the interpretability of psychologically-inspired models, making it difficult to extract actionable learner traits or prerequisite structures for intelligent tutoring systems. Additionally, existing approaches lack efficient continual-learning mechanisms that allow a model to update its learner representation with each new interaction without costly retraining, and they do not jointly model both individual cognitive dynamics and the shared prerequisite structure of the learning domain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241398/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes irregularly-spaced time series of (KC identifier, timestamp, binary correct/incorrect) triples per learner and predicts the next 10 binary outcomes. Beyond the performance prediction, it outputs a 4-dimensional learner-specific trait vector (forgetting rate α, long-term memory μ, volatility σ, transfer ability γ) and a shared K×K prerequisite probability matrix A derived from low-dimensional KC embeddings. The prerequisite graph is pooled across all learners (collaborative-filtering style), while the trait vector and per-KC latent knowledge states are learner-specific and evolve over time.

## Method

PSI-KT is a three-level hierarchical probabilistic state-space model: Level 0 emits binary performance via Bernoulli(sigmoid(z)); Level 1 models per-KC latent knowledge states as Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes whose mean is shifted by prerequisite-weighted contributions from other KCs (structural coupling), balancing exponential forgetting with long-term memory consolidation; Level 2 introduces a 4D learner-specific trait vector that parameterizes the OU dynamics (rate, mean, volatility, transfer). The prerequisite graph is parameterized via KC embedding vectors with a skew-symmetric factorization (Lippe et al. 2021) that enforces the no-mutual-prerequisites constraint. Inference uses variational inference with an amortized neural inference network to learn per-learner posteriors, and continual learning is achieved through variational continual learning (VCL): the current posterior is propagated forward via the generative transition to form a next-time prior, which is then updated with the new observation in a single ELBO maximization step—avoiding full retraining on the growing history.

## Results

PSI-KT achieves the best within-learner 10-step prediction accuracy on all three datasets: 0.68 on Assist12, 0.63 on Assist17, and 0.83 on Junyi15. For between-learner generalization (100 unseen learners), PSI-KT leads on Assist12 (0.61) and Junyi15 (0.79), and ties for best on Assist17 (0.53). With fine-tuning on 10-point histories from unseen learners, PSI-KT is best on Assist12 (0.62) and Assist17 (0.56), but trails DKTF (0.84) on Junyi15 (0.80). In continual learning, PSI-KT requires the least cumulative retraining time while retaining the highest prediction accuracy across incremental updates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PSI-KT outperforms all eight baselines on within-learner prediction across all three datasets, with the largest margins on Assist17 (+0.05 over QIKT, the nearest competitor) and Assist12 (+0.01 over AKT). On between-learner generalization, PSI-KT ties with GKT on Assist12 (0.61) and Assist17 (0.53) and leads on Junyi15 (0.79 vs. GKT's 0.77). The one clear loss is Junyi15 fine-tuning, where DKTF (0.84) and AKT (0.83) both exceed PSI-KT (0.80), suggesting that with sufficient per-learner data the LSTM-based models can partially compensate for their lack of explicit structure. Psychological regression baselines (HLR, PPE) are consistently 0.05–0.15 below the deep learning methods on within-learner accuracy, confirming the accuracy cost of interpretability in prior work that PSI-KT aims to resolve. In continual learning, PSI-KT requires the least cumulative retraining time while maintaining the highest next-10-step accuracy, a cost-accuracy trade-off no baseline matches.

## Relation to Prior Work

PSI-KT unifies two previously separate lines of knowledge tracing research: psychologically-motivated regression models (HLR, PPE, Bayesian Knowledge Tracing) that offer interpretability but limited accuracy, and deep learning models (DKT, AKT, GKT, QIKT) that achieve high accuracy but produce opaque representations. The closest prior work is HKT (Wang et al., 2021), which models knowledge evolution as a multivariate Hawkes process with KC structure but lacks learner-specific representations; PSI-KT adds a second latent level of cognitive traits (forgetting rate, long-term memory, volatility, transfer ability) governed by an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process, and parameterizes the prerequisite graph via a skew-symmetric embedding factorization from Lippe et al. (2021). The inference framework draws on variational continual learning (Nguyen et al., 2017; Loo et al., 2020) and amortized inference networks, positioning the model as a hierarchical probabilistic state-space model rather than a purely neural architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PSI-KT demonstrates that the interpretability-accuracy trade-off in knowledge tracing can be substantially narrowed: a psychologically grounded generative model with only four interpretable learner traits per person matches or exceeds deep learning baselines while providing representations that map to measurable behavioral patterns (e.g., personalized forgetting rates that reveal exponential decay when used to rescale time). The continual-learning formulation (updating the posterior with a single new interaction without retraining) addresses a practical bottleneck for real-time tutoring systems. Key limitations include restriction to pre-college mathematics datasets, an exponential (rather than power-law) forgetting law that may not capture long-term retention, and the absence of acyclicity constraints on the inferred prerequisite graph. Open questions include whether the approach scales to the extreme-data regime where deep models converge, and whether the OU-process inductive bias generalizes to non-mathematical structured domains.
