## Problem

Online test-time adaptation (OTTA) methods that rely on self-training suffer from catastrophic forgetting and mode collapse, while existing remedies that anchor adaptation to a frozen source model (e.g., ROID, SAR) sacrifice the model's ability to track shifting distributions. The paper addresses the unresolved tension: how to continuously update the source model with noisy target-model information to gain adaptability without losing the prior knowledge that prevents forgetting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242085/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of unlabeled target data (images at standard resolution or 16 kHz audio) and a pre-trained source model, adapting only a small parameter subset (normalization layers for vision models, self-attention bias for speech transformers) in real time without access to any historical or labeled data. At each time step it produces a class prediction or transcribed utterance, and internally maintains an updated 'refined source model' (the Kalman-filter hidden state) that feeds into the next optimization step. The adaptation loop runs one SGD iteration per batch for vision and ten iterations per utterance for speech, with the KF inference adding negligible overhead since it involves only element-wise convex combinations.

## Method

CMF alternates between an SGD-based optimization step that minimizes a target generalization error (DW-SLR + SCE loss) and a Kalman-filter inference step that denoises the resulting target parameters. The KF is built on a linear Gaussian state-space model where the hidden state is a 'refined source model' and the observation is the noisy target model; the transition model is source-conjugated, pulling the hidden state toward the original source parameters at each step to prevent drift. The emission model treats the target parameters as a noisy linear readout of the hidden state, and the Kalman gain (governed by the process noise q) controls how much target information is absorbed. A key simplification replaces all d×d matrices with scalars (α, q, γ), reducing the inference to repeated convex-combination 'Moments' operations that are computationally trivial. This yields a dynamically updated source model that is more flexible than a frozen one yet more stable than the raw target model, closing the adaptability–robustness gap.

## Results

In the temporally-correlated covariate shift (TC-CS) scenario, CMF achieves the lowest average error rate across all four image datasets and three model architectures (ViT, Swin, D2V). On ImageNet-C, CMF reaches 44.8% (ViT), 46.6% (Swin), and 43.5% (D2V), outperforming the strongest baseline ROID by 0.2–1.3 points. On D109, CMF achieves 43.4% (ViT), 43.6% (Swin), and 42.3% (D2V), a 1.6–1.9 point improvement over ROID. For real-world streaming speech recognition, CMF attains 11.8% WER on TED (D2V-Libri) and 18.7% WER on Common Voice (D2V-Vox), surpassing SUTA-episodic by 0.2 and 0.2 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMF consistently outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and model architecture tested. Against the strongest competitor ROID (Marsden et al., 2023), CMF improves by 0.2–1.3 points on ImageNet-C, 1.6–1.9 points on D109, 1.5–2.2 points on Rendition, and 1.6–2.3 points on Sketch in the TC-CS scenario. TENT (Wang et al., 2020) and CoTTA (Wang et al., 2022) suffer severe degradation under temporal correlation (e.g., TENT drops to 83.3% on D109-ViT), while CMF maintains stable performance. EATA (Niu et al., 2022) is highly unstable on D2V (64.4% on ImageNet-C, 78.3% on Sketch), whereas CMF achieves 43.5% and 53.9% respectively. SAR (Niu et al., 2023) and LAME (Boudiaf et al., 2022) show large variance and inconsistent results, particularly under label-shift conditions (Table 3). For speech recognition, CMF (11.8% WER on TED, 29.6% on CV) marginally outperforms SUTA-episodic (Lin et al., 2022) (12.0% and 30.3%), while SUTA in a continual setting catastrophically fails (67.7% and 120.9% WER).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the OTTA lineage from self-training (TENT) through regularization-based forgetting prevention (EATA, SAR) to parameter ensembling with a fixed source model (ROID). The key departure is replacing the frozen source model in ROID's ensemble with a Kalman-filter-dynamically-updated hidden model, creating a feedback loop where noisy target parameters are denoised and fed back to refine the source over time. The source-conjugated transition model (Eq. 5) is the critical architectural choice that anchors the hidden state to the original source while allowing gradual evolution, distinguishing CMF from both pure self-training and static ensembling approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CMF provides a principled Bayesian framework for the adaptability-forgetting tradeoff in OTTA, reducing the problem to just two hyperparameters (α, q) and offering a clear interpretation of when noisy target information is beneficial (high-diversity settings) versus harmful. The consistent outperformance across four architectures, four image benchmarks, and real-world speech streaming suggests broad applicability. However, the reliance on a linear Gaussian model for parameter dynamics is a theoretical limitation, and the sensitivity to γ (the ensemble weight) hints that the linear assumption may break down in more extreme distribution shifts. Open questions include whether non-linear filtering (e.g., particle filters) or confidence-based parameter selection could extend the framework to settings where the linear model fails.
