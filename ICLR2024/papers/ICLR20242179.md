## Problem

Existing coreset selection methods optimize either data diversity (geometry-based) or sample difficulty (score-based) in isolation, but these two factors are complementary: diversity-only selection biases toward easy samples in dense regions, while difficulty-only selection omits the easy samples that deep networks need for stable optimization. No prior framework jointly accounts for both the spatial distribution of samples in embedding space and their individual difficulty scores during coreset selection.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a training dataset with per-sample feature embeddings (e.g., last-layer activations from a ResNet or [CLS] token from RoBERTa) and per-sample difficulty scores (e.g., forgetting count, EL2N, AUM, or variance). It constructs a sparse k-nearest-neighbor graph with RBF-kernel edge weights, then outputs a ranked coreset of the desired size along with updated node importance scores that reflect both local density and difficulty. The two tunable hyperparameters (k and γ_r) control the trade-off between difficulty concentration and diversity in the selected subset.

## Method

D² PRUNING represents the dataset as an undirected graph where nodes are samples (initialized with difficulty scores) and edges connect k-nearest neighbors (weighted by an RBF kernel of Euclidean embedding distance). A single forward message-passing step updates each node's score by aggregating neighbor difficulty scores scaled by edge weights, effectively merging local density information into the difficulty ranking so that an easy sample in a hard region is ranked higher than one in an easy region. Coreset selection then proceeds iteratively: the highest-scoring node is selected, and reverse message passing down-weights its neighbors (with the down-weight magnitude controlled by γ_r) to prevent redundant selections from the same neighborhood. This three-stage pipeline—graph initialization, forward message passing, and selection with reverse message passing—produces a coreset that is simultaneously diverse in embedding space and balanced across the difficulty spectrum, with hyperparameters k and γ_r allowing the user to shift the balance toward difficulty (low k, high γ_r) or toward equitable easy/difficult coverage (high k, low γ_r).

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, D²PRUNING achieves 71.8% top-1 accuracy at 50% pruning and 65.9% at 80% pruning, outperforming the previous best (CCS at 70.5% and 64.5% respectively) by 1.3% and 1.4%. On CIFAR-10, D²PRUNING reaches 93.3% at 70% pruning (vs. 93.0% for CCS) and 91.4% at 80% pruning (vs. 91.0% for CCS). On CIFAR-100, D²PRUNING leads at 30% pruning (78.2% vs. 78.1% for GLISTER) and 50% pruning (75.9% vs. 74.5% for CAL-SDS2). On NLP, D²PRUNING improves ANLI accuracy by 1.7% at 70% pruning (45.3% vs. 43.6% random) and IMDB by 8.8% at 90% pruning (90.3% vs. 81.3% random).

## Baselines & Comparisons

D²PRUNING consistently outperforms all baselines at low-to-medium pruning rates (30%–80%) across all three vision datasets. On ImageNet-1K, it beats the strongest baselines CCS (70.5%→71.8% at 50%) and CAL-SDS2 (70.5%→71.8% at 50%, 64.2%→65.9% at 80%) by 1.0–1.4%. On CIFAR-100, D²PRUNING surpasses GLISTER (78.1%→78.2% at 30%) and CAL-SDS2 (74.5%→75.9% at 50%) by 0.1–1.4%. Score-based methods (Forgetting, EL2N, AUM) collapse dramatically at high pruning rates (e.g., EL2N drops to 11.8% on CIFAR-100 at 90%), while geometry-only methods (BADGE, Moderate) underperform at low pruning rates. On NLP, D²PRUNING improves over CCS by 0.8% on ANLI at 70% pruning (45.3% vs. 44.5%) and by 2.8% on IMDB at 90% pruning (90.3% vs. 87.5%), while INGENIOUS and CAL-SDS2 (both using facility location for diversity) underperform the graph-based approach. At very high pruning rates (90%+), CCS remains competitive and D²PRUNING's advantage narrows, consistent with the paper's claim that difficulty dominates over diversity when the data budget is extremely small.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of coreset selection—geometry-based diversity methods (k-center, facility location, BADGE) and difficulty-score-based methods (forgetting, EL2N, entropy)—by representing the dataset as a k-NN graph and using message passing to propagate both spatial proximity and difficulty information. It builds directly on the message-passing framework of Hamilton et al. (2017) and extends the graph-based density sampling idea of Ebert et al. (2012) by initializing nodes with difficulty scores and adding a forward message-passing step before selection. Unlike CAL-SDS2, which combines difficulty with a global facility-location submodular function, D²PRUNING uses local graph neighborhoods and a reverse message-passing mechanism during iterative selection to enforce diversity, avoiding the computational overhead of submodular optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play message-passing framework allows practitioners to swap in different difficulty metrics, embedding sources, and graph topologies without redesigning the selection algorithm, making it a versatile tool for data curation across modalities. The first systematic evaluation of coreset selection on NLP datasets (ANLI, IMDB) fills a notable gap in the literature. However, the method still requires a model trained on the full dataset for the supervised variant, limiting its utility for creating datasets from scratch; the self-supervised and unsupervised extensions show promise but yield smaller gains. Open questions include whether the framework can operate without full-dataset access and whether the message-passing scheme can incorporate additional factors (e.g., label noise, domain shift) in a principled way.
