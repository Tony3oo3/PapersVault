## Problem

Well-trained RL agents are highly vulnerable to adversarial state perturbations at test time, where a malicious opponent strategically alters observations to collapse cumulative reward. Existing defenses either regularize the policy for smoothness (insufficient against strong attacks) or alternately train agent and attacker policies (computationally prohibitive for large environments). A critical structural flaw in prior work is the train/test mismatch: actions are derived from true states during training but from perturbed states at test time, and historical observations and MDP dynamics are not systematically exploited to reduce uncertainty about the true state.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a perturbed state observation (continuous 2D coordinates in Gridworld or 84×84 raw pixel frames in Atari Pong/Freeway) along with a history of prior perturbed states and actions. For BP-DQN, the PF-RNN belief model processes the full history to produce κ_p=30 candidate true states; for DP-DQN, the diffusion model generates κ_d=4 purified states from the single perturbed observation plus small pixel-wise Gaussian noise. The output is a single discrete action per timestep, selected by maximizing the worst-case Q-value across the belief set, making the policy agnostic to the exact perturbation budget ε.

## Method

The paper formulates robust RL under state perturbation as a two-player Stackelberg Markov game with asymmetric observations and derives a pessimistic Q-learning algorithm where the agent selects actions via a maximin criterion: max_a min_{s̄∈B_ε(s̃)} Q(s̄, a), applied consistently at both training and test stages to eliminate the train/test mismatch. Two complementary uncertainty-reduction mechanisms are integrated: (1) a PF-RNN belief model that approximates the set of possible true states from historical state-action pairs using particle filtering, and (2) a diffusion-based purification module (DDPM or Progressive Distillation) that denoises perturbed pixel observations by running a few reverse diffusion steps from the perturbed state rather than from pure noise. The Q-network loss is modified to target the worst case over the belief set, and the attacker is simulated via projected gradient descent during training. A theoretical result (Theorem 1) bounds the asymptotic Q-value gap from the optimal no-attack policy by O(ε·(l_r + l_p|S|R_max/(1−γ))), characterizing the cost of pessimism under Lipschitz continuity assumptions on rewards and transitions.

## Results

In the continuous Gridworld, BP-DQN achieves 176±16 under PGD (ε=0.5) and 177.2±10.6 under PA-AD (ε=0.5), while all baselines collapse to near-zero or negative rewards (DQN: −53±86; SA-DQN: −100±0; WocaR-DQN: −63.2±88). In Atari Pong, DP-DQN-O maintains a reward of ~19.3–19.9 across all three attack types and all budgets (ε=1/255 to 15/255), whereas vanilla DQN drops to −21 under any PGD attack. In Atari Freeway, DP-DQN-F achieves 31.4±0.8 under PA-AD (ε=15/255) and 30.7±1.2 under PGD (ε=15/255), while DQN drops to 0 and SA-DQN drops to 0 under MinBest (ε=1/255).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Vanilla DQN (Mnih et al., 2015) is completely defeated by any state perturbation: it drops from 21 to −21 in Pong and from 34 to 0 in Freeway under even the smallest PGD budget (ε=1/255), and similarly collapses in the Gridworld. SA-DQN (Zhang et al., 2020) and WocaR-DQN (Liang et al., 2022) survive small PGD perturbations in Atari (maintaining ~21 in Pong and ~30–31 in Freeway at ε=1/255) but fail catastrophically under MinBest and PA-AD attacks (dropping to 0 or near 0 in Freeway) and are entirely ineffective in the continuous Gridworld (rewards of −100 to −63). The key advantage of the proposed methods is robustness under the strongest attacks: DP-DQN-F achieves 31.4±0.8 under PA-AD (ε=15/255) in Freeway versus 12.3±3.2 for WocaR-DQN and 0 for DQN, and BP-DQN achieves 177.2±10.6 under PA-AD (ε=0.5) in Gridworld versus −67.8±78.3 for SA-DQN. The proposed methods trade a small clean-environment reward (e.g., 19.9 vs. 21 in Pong, 28.8 vs. 34 in Freeway) for dramatically improved worst-case robustness, and are agnostic to the attack budget (a single policy trained at ε=1/255 handles all budgets), unlike SA-DQN and WocaR-DQN which require knowing ε in advance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DQN framework (Mnih et al., 2015) and departs from the two dominant lines of defense against adversarial state perturbations: regularization-based smoothing (SA-DQN, WocaR-DQN) and alternating agent-attacker training (ATLA). Its key conceptual departure is a pessimistic maximin action-selection rule applied consistently at both training and test stages, eliminating the train/test state mismatch that plagues prior methods. For uncertainty reduction, it repurposes PF-RNN belief tracking (Ma et al., 2020) from POMDPs and DDPM (Ho et al., 2020) from image generation, adapting both to the adversarial RL setting where the 'noise' is an adaptive attacker rather than a fixed observation function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals that pixel-wise perturbations in Atari-style environments produce structurally 'invalid' states, opening a practical defense avenue via diffusion-based purification that is agnostic to the attack budget ε—a property lacking in all prior regularization-based methods. However, the approach requires a clean environment during training, incurs significant test-time overhead (6.6 FPS for DDPM vs. 500+ FPS for baselines), and is limited to value-based methods. Open questions include extending to policy-based algorithms, offline settings without clean environment access, and combining belief and diffusion for attacks that alter both semantics and pixels.
