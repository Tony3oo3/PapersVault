## Problem

While Lipschitz bounds are well-established for Euclidean networks (MLPs, CNNs), no prior work has derived or applied Lipschitz bounds to non-Euclidean GNNs for fairness. The key gap is the absence of a computationally tractable mechanism to constrain GNN output perturbations induced by implicit biases in graph data, without requiring explicitly labeled sensitive attributes or incurring prohibitive computational costs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×F} and a graph adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{N×N}, and produces standard node class predictions or link prediction scores alongside a scalar Lipschitz bound regularization term. The Lipschitz bound is computed locally per-node via the Jacobian ∂Y_i/∂X_i (not the full N×N cross-node Jacobian), aggregated into an N×F^{out} matrix LB(J), and summarized as its (∞,2)-norm. This local formulation aligns with the nearest-k-neighbor similarity used in rank-based individual fairness and avoids the NP-hard global Lipschitz computation.

## Method

The paper derives a closed-form cumulative Lipschitz bound for L-layer GNNs (Theorem 1) as a product of per-layer Jacobian row norms scaled by output dimensions, assuming 1-Lipschitz ReLU activations. For practical training, this is simplified to Lip(f) = ||LB(J)||_{∞,2}, where LB(J) stacks per-node, per-output-dimension Jacobian norms, computed efficiently via PyTorch autograd. The resulting JacoLip algorithm is a plug-and-play regularization: the Lipschitz bound is added as a weighted term (μ·cum_lip) to the standard cross-entropy loss during supervised training, requiring no architectural changes. Theoretically, bounding Lip(f) ensures that small input perturbations (biases) cannot disproportionately distort the relative ranking of node similarities in the output space, thereby guaranteeing rank-based individual fairness without any labeled sensitive attributes.

## Results

On node classification, JacoLip applied on top of Redress with GCN backbone achieves NDCG@10 of 53.62% on ACM (vs. 49.13% for Redress alone), 68.20% on Co-author-CS (vs. 55.01%), and 49.37% on Co-author-Phy (vs. 43.64%), while maintaining accuracy within ~1% of the vanilla model. On link prediction, JacoLip on Redress with GCN reaches NDCG@10 of 18.02% on BlogCatalog, 14.37% on Flickr, and 30.07% on Facebook, consistently outperforming InFoRM and PFR on both utility and fairness. The method adds only ~4.6% training overhead (0.2972s vs. 0.2841s per step) compared to vanilla GCN, versus ~11.4x overhead for the Shi et al. (2022) bound-propagation approach.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JacoLip consistently outperforms all three fairness baselines (InFoRM, PFR, Redress) on the NDCG@10 fairness metric across all six datasets and both backbone architectures, while preserving accuracy within 1–2% of the vanilla model. InFoRM degrades both accuracy and fairness substantially (e.g., −15.9% NDCG on ACM, −52.7% on structural similarity), and PFR shows the largest fairness drops (−34.1% to −42.5% NDCG), confirming that pre-processing-based and non-GNN-specific fairness methods are poorly suited to graph data. Redress is the strongest baseline, but JacoLip stacked on top of Redress yields further gains of 4–14% NDCG on node classification and 2–10% on link prediction, demonstrating the plug-and-play nature of the Lipschitz regularization. The only non-fairness comparison is with Shi et al. (2022) on computational efficiency: JacoLip adds ~4.6% per-step overhead (0.2972s vs. 0.2841s) while Shi et al.'s bound-propagation method requires ~11.4x the time (3.2407s) and does not support graph structure, making it inapplicable to the GNN fairness setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the rank-based individual fairness framework of Dong et al. (2021, Redress) by introducing a Lipschitz bound regularization computed via the Jacobian matrix, specifically adapted to the non-Euclidean structure of GNNs. It departs from prior Lipschitz bound literature (Virmaux & Scaman 2018; Shi et al. 2022; Dasoulas et al. 2021), which focused on tight bounds for robustness or gradient stability in Euclidean networks, by repurposing the Lipschitz constraint as a fairness regularizer that preserves input-output ranking consistency without requiring explicit sensitive attributes. The method also distinguishes itself from group fairness approaches (Bose & Hamilton 2019; Rahman et al. 2019) by operating on implicit biases in node features and connectivity rather than annotated protected attributes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

JacoLip offers a practical, plug-and-play fairness regularization for GNNs with negligible computational overhead (~4% per-iteration cost), making it attractive for large-scale graph learning where existing fairness methods struggle. However, the evaluation is limited to six relatively small datasets and three backbone architectures (GCN, SGC, GAE), leaving open questions about scalability to million-node graphs and applicability to more expressive architectures like GAT or GraphSAGE. The local (per-node) Lipschitz formulation, while justified by the NP-hardness of global bounds, may under-constrain fairness in highly interconnected graphs where cross-node interactions dominate. Future work could explore tighter per-layer bounds, adaptive regularization schedules, and integration with contrastive or self-supervised GNN training.
