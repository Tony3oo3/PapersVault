## Problem

Multi-class classification from multiple unlabeled datasets (MCMU) requires learning a classifier using only class-prior proportions for each unlabeled set, with no individual labels. The sole prior work (Tang et al., 2022) proposed an unbiased risk estimator that suffers from negative empirical risk, which breaks its theoretical guarantee and leads to suboptimal performance. No existing method simultaneously achieves risk consistency, avoids negative empirical risk, and handles arbitrary numbers of unlabeled sets in the multi-class setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes m unlabeled datasets (each containing n_i image points drawn from a mixture of class-conditional densities) and an m×k class prior matrix θ (full column rank, rows sum to 1) specifying the proportion of each class in each set. It produces a standard multi-class classifier f: X → R^k that predicts individual class labels for unseen instances. The class priors are the sole supervision signal; without them the problem reduces to fully unsupervised learning. The prior matrix can be square (m=k) or non-square (m>k), and may be diagonal-dominated or fully random.

## Method

The paper proposes two consistent methods. CCM (Classifier-Consistent Method) defines a deterministic probability transition function T that maps the model's softmax output η(x)=p(y|x) to surrogate set probabilities p(ȳ|x), then trains a standard multi-class cross-entropy loss on the surrogate set classification problem, guaranteeing classifier consistency via injectivity of T. RCM (Risk-Consistent Method) rewrites the original classification risk as an importance-weighted sum over classes, where the weight p(y=j|ȳ,x) is computed dynamically from the current model's softmax outputs and the known prior matrix, enabling progressive purification of supervision information in an EM-like alternating fashion. RCM intrinsically avoids negative empirical risk, requires no hyperparameters, and achieves risk consistency (the optimized objective is exactly equivalent to the true classification risk). Both methods are supported by Rademacher-complexity-based generalization bounds.

## Results

On the most challenging Random prior matrix setting (m=10), RCM achieves 95.04% on MNIST, 81.11% on Fashion-MNIST, 78.60% on Kuzushiji-MNIST, 50.51% on CIFAR-10, and 78.70% on SVHN, consistently outperforming CCM (91.92%, 78.07%, 72.62%, 42.31%, 72.73%) and all baselines. The gap between RCM and CCM is largest on CIFAR-10 (+8.2%) and SVHN (+6.0%), indicating greater benefit from deeper models. On the Asymmetric matrix, RCM reaches 96.75% (MNIST) and 92.03% (SVHN), approaching the supervised upper bounds of 98.24% and 92.89% respectively. All methods suffer severe degradation on the Random matrix (e.g., Unbiased drops to ~14% on MNIST), while RCM and CCM remain robust.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RCM decisively outperforms all methods from Tang et al. (2022) (Unbiased, U-Stop, U-Correct, U-Flood, U-PRR) across every dataset and matrix setting. On the Random matrix (m=10), RCM achieves 95.04% on MNIST versus 78.95% for the best Tang et al. method (U-Flood) and 26.56% for U-PRR, a gap of 16–69 percentage points. Against Prop (Yu et al., 2014), RCM wins on MNIST (95.04% vs. 89.09%), Kuzushiji-MNIST (78.60% vs. 67.12%), CIFAR-10 (50.51% vs. 37.38%), and SVHN (78.70% vs. 59.56%), though Prop slightly edges RCM on Fashion-MNIST (79.26% vs. 81.11% — actually RCM still wins). CCM also beats all baselines but trails RCM by 3–8% depending on dataset, with the largest gaps on CIFAR-10 and SVHN where deeper ResNet models amplify RCM's importance-weighting advantage. The paper also shows RCM is more robust to noisy prior matrices than CCM, which overfits at high noise rates (dropping to 10.31% at ε=0.3 random noise on MNIST while RCM stays at 95.56%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Lu et al. (2021), which solved binary MCMU via a surrogate set classification problem with a probability transition function, to the multi-class setting (CCM). It also directly addresses the limitations of Tang et al. (2022), the sole prior work on MCMU, whose unbiased risk estimator suffers from negative empirical risk and whose partial-risk regularization breaks unbiasedness. The RCM method departs from both by using importance weighting to rewrite the classification risk into an equivalent form accessible from unlabeled sets, avoiding negative risk while maintaining risk consistency. The paper also distinguishes MCMU from the closely related Learning from Label Proportions (LLP) literature, noting that MCMU operates at the instance level with independent data points across sets, unlike LLP's set-level dependence structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first multi-class solution to MCMU with both classifier-consistency and risk-consistency guarantees, filling a gap left by prior binary-only or theoretically inconsistent methods. The EM interpretation of RCM's iterative training provides a principled understanding of how importance weights and model predictions co-evolve to progressively purify supervision. However, the practical scope is limited: class priors must be known exactly, the prior matrix must be full column rank, and experiments are confined to relatively simple datasets (MNIST variants, CIFAR-10, SVHN). Open questions include robustness to unknown or estimated priors, scalability to larger and more complex datasets, and whether the importance-weighting framework generalizes to other weakly supervised settings where only aggregate statistics are available.
