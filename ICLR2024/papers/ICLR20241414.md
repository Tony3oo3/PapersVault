## Problem

Evaluating and selecting the best policy in robotics is bottlenecked by expensive robot time, and existing offline policy representations (e.g., the 'Actions' vector from Konyushkova et al., 2021) can be misleading because they encode actions at canonical states rather than actual behavioral effects on the environment. Two policies that produce different random actions at each state would appear dissimilar under the Actions representation despite exhibiting nearly identical behavior. The paper seeks a policy representation that captures how a policy evolves the environment, enabling reliable offline prediction of held-out policy performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a black-box policy (queryable for actions but with no access to parameters or architecture), a fixed offline dataset of trajectories from other policies or human demonstrations, and a frozen pretrained visual foundation model (CLIP, ViT, TAP, or random ResNet-50) that maps multi-view RGB images plus proprioception to a feature vector. The output is a fixed-dimensional policy representation vector Ψ_π^φ ∈ R^N, computed entirely offline without executing the policy in the environment. This vector is then fed to a linear regression model to predict the policy's expected return. Notably, the successor feature network outputs a distributional representation (N histograms over B bins) rather than a point estimate, and the final vector is obtained by averaging over a set of canonical states sampled from different trajectories.

## Method

π2vec represents a policy by the expected discounted sum of foundation-model state features along trajectories the policy would generate, formalized through the successor feature framework. Concretely, a policy-specific successor feature network ψ_π^φ(s,a) is trained via Fitted Q Evaluation (FQE) on offline data, where the standard scalar Bellman backup is replaced with vector-valued transition features φ(s,a,s′), and a distributional objective (histograms over B bins) is used for the network output. The policy-agnostic encoder φ is kept frozen throughout; only ψ_π^φ is trained, making the representation transferable across policies. The state-dependent successor features are then averaged over a set of canonical states D_can (sampled uniformly from different trajectories) to yield a state-agnostic policy vector Ψ_π^φ. A linear regression model trained on known (Ψ_π^φ, R_i) pairs then predicts returns for unseen policies, enabling offline policy selection without any online evaluation.

## Results

On RGB Stacking (real robot), π2vec with ViT achieves Regret@1 of 0.036, a 51% relative improvement over the Actions baseline (0.074). On Insert Gear (Real), π2vec with CLIP achieves Regret@1 of 0.267 and Correlation of +0.618, compared to Actions' 0.578 and −0.545, respectively. On Metaworld and Kitchen (averaged over 12 and 15 task-POV settings), selecting the best feature encoder (BEST-φ) yields Regret@1 of 0.153 and 0.070 versus Actions' 0.232 and 0.221. In the fully-offline setting on Metaworld assembly, π2vec outperforms FQE on Regret@1 across all three camera views (e.g., 0.149 vs. 0.609 for the top view).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the Actions representation from Konyushkova et al. (2021), which encodes each policy by concatenating its actions on a fixed set of canonical states. π2vec consistently outperforms Actions on Regret@1 across all five environments: on RGB Stacking, ViT-based π2vec halves the regret (0.036 vs. 0.074); on Insert Gear (Real), CLIP-based π2vec more than doubles the correlation (0.618 vs. −0.545) and nearly halves regret (0.267 vs. 0.578); on Metaworld and Kitchen, BEST-φ achieves Regret@1 of 0.153 and 0.070 versus Actions' 0.232 and 0.221. The second baseline is FQE (Le et al., 2019), compared in the fully-offline setting where no policy performance is known. π2vec outperforms FQE on Regret@1 for all three Metaworld assembly camera views (0.215 vs. 0.424 left, 0.319 vs. 0.504 right, 0.149 vs. 0.609 top) and on Correlation, though FQE achieves lower NMAE, indicating π2vec ranks policies better but predicts absolute returns less accurately. The paper also notes that in low-variability domains (Metaworld, Kitchen), Actions performs competitively on Correlation, but π2vec still wins on NMAE and Regret@1 when the best feature encoder is selected.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines three established threads: successor features (Barreto et al., 2017) for decomposing policy behavior from task reward, Fitted Q Evaluation (Le et al., 2019) for offline value estimation, and the action-based policy representation from Active Offline Policy Selection (Konyushkova et al., 2021). The key departure is replacing the action-based representation with successor features computed over pretrained visual foundation model embeddings (CLIP, ViT, TAP), shifting the policy characterization from 'what actions does it take' to 'how does it change the environment's visual state over time.' The method is explicitly designed as a drop-in replacement for the AOPS representation, maintaining the same canonical-state averaging and linear regression pipeline while swapping the underlying feature space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in robotics RL—expensive real-robot evaluation time—by enabling policy ranking purely from offline data and pretrained visual features. A notable open question is the choice of foundation model φ, which the paper shows is task-dependent (CLIP for hard/high-variance tasks, ViT for easier ones), and no principled selection criterion is provided beyond cross-validation on known policies. The fully-offline variant (Table 3) reveals a significant limitation: while π2vec ranks policies competitively, its absolute return estimates are far less accurate than FQE, suggesting the linear reward assumption is a real constraint. Future directions include end-to-end fine-tuning of φ jointly with ψ, integration into the AOPS active selection loop, and extension to Generalized Policy Improvement in offline settings.
