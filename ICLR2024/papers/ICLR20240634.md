## Problem

Multi-modal models are empirically known to be vulnerable to perturbations on specific modalities, yet no theoretical framework explains which components determine multi-modal robustness or why modality preference creates this vulnerability. This paper provides a certified robustness analysis for joint multi-modal classifiers, identifying uni-modal representation margins and modality integration factors as the essential components, and showing how modality preference degrades both to limit robustness and make attacks on the preferred modality disproportionately effective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240634/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on paired multi-modal inputs: audio clips + video clips (Kinetics Sounds, VGGSound) or optical flow + RGB frames (UCF101), each encoded independently by a ResNet18 backbone. The two uni-modal representations are concatenated and passed through a linear classifier with orthogonal row constraints per modality, producing a K-way class label. The certified robustness bound is computed per-sample using the Lipschitz constants of the uni-modal scores and modality-specific integration weights, yielding a guaranteed perturbation radius the model can defend against.

## Method

The paper introduces an orthogonal-based framework where each uni-modal classifier has unit-orthogonal class vectors, decoupling uni-modal representation learning from modality integration (controlled by per-class weights a^(m)). Building on a derived certified lower bound for the multi-modal perturbation radius, the authors propose a two-step Certifiable Robust Multi-modal Training (CRMT) procedure: Step 1 jointly optimizes encoders and classifiers with cross-entropy plus a LogSumExp-based margin regularization that enlarges the smallest uni-modal margin across modalities, alleviating the imbalance caused by modality preference; Step 2 freezes the encoders and classifiers and updates only the modality-specific weights to maximize the certified bound, rebalancing the vulnerability indicators η^(m) = c_j^(m)·τ_j^(m) across modalities. This procedure is composable with existing robust training strategies (adversarial training, mixup), yielding CRMT-AT and CRMT-Mix variants that further improve certified robustness.

## Results

On the three multi-modal datasets (Kinetics Sounds, UCF101, VGGSound), CRMT-AT achieves the strongest adversarial accuracy across all attack settings. Under ℓ₂-PGD (ε=0.5), CRMT-AT reaches 60.2% on KS, 60.2% on UCF101, and 41.4% on VGGS, outperforming the strongest baseline MMAT (50.7%, 59.8%, 40.9% respectively). Under FGM, CRMT-AT scores 60.8% (KS), 61.4% (UCF101), and 42.2% (VGGS). Clean accuracy is also improved: CRMT-AT achieves 76.2% on KS and 75.9% on UCF101, surpassing the JT baseline (64.3% and 74.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRMT-AT dominates all baselines under ℓ₂-PGD on every dataset: it beats MMAT by 9.5 points on KS (60.2% vs 50.7%), by 0.4 points on UCF101 (60.2% vs 59.8%), and by 0.5 points on VGGS (41.4% vs 40.9%). Under FGM, CRMT-AT surpasses MMAT by 9.4 points on KS (60.8% vs 51.4%) and by 1.5 points on UCF101 (61.4% vs 60.9%), while on VGGS it edges out MMAT by 0.9 points (42.2% vs 41.3%). The imbalance-focused baselines (GB, OGM, PMR) improve clean accuracy over JT but remain far below CRMT-AT under adversarial attacks—for example, GB achieves only 34.4% under ℓ₂-PGD on KS versus CRMT-AT's 60.2%. CRMT-Mix also outperforms the standalone Mixup baseline by 16.4 points under ℓ₂-PGD on KS (49.1% vs 32.7%), confirming that the CRMT procedure can be plugged into existing robust training strategies to yield consistent gains. On clean accuracy, CRMT-JT achieves the highest score on UCF101 (78.9%) and VGGS (52.6%), while CRMT-AT leads on KS (76.2%), showing that the orthogonal framework plus margin regularization also benefits standard performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: multi-modal robustness training (MMAT, Mixup, MSEFM), modality imbalance mitigation (GB, OGM, PMR), and certified robustness (randomized smoothing, interval bound propagation). It departs from prior work primarily by providing a theoretical certified bound for multi-modal models that explicitly decomposes robustness into uni-modal margins and integration factors, rather than treating robustness as a purely empirical property. The orthogonal classifier framework (borrowed from Huang et al., 2018) is repurposed here to decouple uni-modal representation learning from modality integration, enabling a two-step training procedure (CRMT) that prior multi-modal robustness methods did not offer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical explanation for the widely observed but previously unexplained phenomenon that multi-modal models are disproportionately vulnerable to attacks on a specific (preferred) modality, linking it to an imbalance in vulnerability indicators (η). The CRMT procedure is modular and composable with existing robust training strategies (adversarial training, mixup), making it a practical add-on rather than a replacement. Limitations include the theory being restricted to two modalities and a linear classifier, the transformer extension only applying step-1, and experiments limited to ResNet-18 backbones on three datasets. Open questions include whether the orthogonal constraint generalizes to deeper or non-linear fusion architectures and whether the certified bound remains tight under more complex perturbation models.
