## Problem

In continuous-time reinforcement learning (CTRL), the policy evaluation step requires computing an integral of the utility function, which in practice must be approximated by a quadrature rule from discrete state samples. While it was known that computational errors exist, the specific impact of the quadrature rule choice on the convergence behavior of policy iteration and the resulting controller performance had not been quantitatively characterized. This paper bridges that gap by establishing how computational error propagates through IntRL's policy iteration to affect the learned control policy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes evenly-spaced state samples {x(t_i)} collected at discrete time instants within each interval [t, t+ΔT], along with the known control-affine dynamics g(x) and utility function l(x,u). It produces a learned control policy u(x) and value function V(x) approximated as a linear combination of basis functions, plus a theoretical convergence rate bound expressed as a function of the number of samples N per interval. A key constraint is that the internal dynamics f(x) must be unknown, which rules out adaptive ODE solvers and necessitates fixed quadrature rules on sensor-sampled data.

## Method

The paper establishes that IntRL's policy iteration (PEV + PIM) is mathematically equivalent to Newton's method applied to the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation in a Banach space, via Fréchet differentiation of the HJB operator. Under this correspondence, the quadrature error in PEV appears as a bounded extra error term E^(i) in each Newton iteration, whose norm is proportional to the computational error. The computational error is then quantified via the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in the RKHS: Err ≤ ||l||_{H_K} · e(H_K), where e(H_K) is the worst-case error minimized by Bayesian quadrature with the RKHS-inducing kernel. This yields concrete convergence rates: O(N^{-2}) for the trapezoidal rule (equivalent to BQ with Wiener kernel) and O(N^{-b}) for BQ with a Matérn kernel of smoothness parameter b, validated on linear and nonlinear canonical control tasks.

## Results

The paper establishes that the convergence rate of IntRL's policy iteration depends on the quadrature rule used in the PEV step: O(N^{-2}) for the trapezoidal rule and O(N^{-b}) for Bayesian quadrature with a Matérn kernel of smoothness parameter b. These rates are validated experimentally on two canonical control tasks (a third-order LQR system and a nonlinear system from Vrabie & Lewis 2009) with evenly-spaced samples of size 5 ≤ N ≤ 15. For the Matérn kernel with b=4, the learned parameter error ‖ω̂^(∞) − ω*‖₂ converges at O(N^{-4}), roughly 4× faster than the trapezoidal rule's O(N^{-2}), as confirmed in both the linear and nonlinear examples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares two quadrature rules for computing the PEV integral in IntRL: the classical trapezoidal rule and Bayesian quadrature (BQ) with a Matérn kernel (smoothness b=4). BQ with the Matérn kernel consistently outperforms the trapezoidal rule, achieving O(N^{-4}) convergence in the learned value-function parameters versus O(N^{-2}) for the trapezoidal rule, on both the linear LQR system and the nonlinear system. This 2× improvement in convergence exponent translates to substantially lower accumulated cost J relative to the optimal J* at the same sample size N (as shown in Figure 1). The paper further shows that BQ with the RKHS-inducing kernel is the optimal quadrature rule in the sense of minimizing the worst-case error e(H_K), which equals the square root of BQ's posterior variance. No other RL algorithms or quadrature methods (e.g., Simpson's rule, higher-order BQ kernels) are directly benchmarked, so the comparison is limited to these two quadrature choices within the IntRL framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: the IntRL algorithm for continuous-time optimal control (Vrabie & Lewis, 2009), Bayesian quadrature as the optimal quadrature rule in RKHS settings (Briol et al., 2019), and error analysis of Newton's method with bounded perturbations (Urabe, 1956). The key conceptual departure is drawing an explicit parallel between IntRL's policy iteration and Newton's method applied to the HJB equation, thereby reframing quadrature-induced computational error as a bounded perturbation in each Newton iteration. While Yildiz et al. (2021) noted that numerical solver choice affects control performance for known-dynamics CTRL, this paper extends the analysis to the more challenging unknown-dynamics setting where adaptive solvers are infeasible and quadrature rules are the only option.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to quantitatively link the choice of quadrature rule to the convergence rate of IntRL, providing practitioners with a principled criterion (BQ with Matérn kernel) for selecting the PEV computation method. The practical implication is significant for real-world autonomous systems where sensor sampling rates are fixed and uneven, making the quadrature choice a non-trivial design decision. However, the analysis is limited to local convergence, assumes known g(x), bounded state space, and neglects basis-function approximation error; the empirical validation uses only two low-dimensional canonical examples. Open questions include whether these results extend to high-dimensional or partially-observed systems, non-uniform sampling, and whether the posterior variance from BQ can be actively exploited in a 'computation-aware' control policy as the authors briefly suggest.
