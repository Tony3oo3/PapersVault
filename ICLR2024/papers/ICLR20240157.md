## Problem

LLMs struggle with multi-hop knowledge reasoning, hallucination, and lack of explainability, and existing LLM+KG approaches (LLM ⊕ KG) treat the LLM merely as a query translator that does not participate in graph traversal. This paper introduces a tight-coupling LLM ⊗ KG paradigm where the LLM acts as an agent that interactively explores and reasons over the knowledge graph at every step, enabling deep, traceable, and correctable reasoning without any additional training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240157/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language question and a structured KG (Freebase queried via pre-defined SPARQL templates, or Wikidata via nine pre-defined service APIs) as input. It produces a final answer (entity, number, or boolean) together with explicit top-N reasoning paths on the KG that serve as evidence. The beam search width N and maximum depth D_max are configurable hyperparameters (default 3 each), and the entire pipeline is training-free and plug-and-play across different LLMs, KGs, and prompting strategies.

## Method

ToG implements beam search on the KG where the LLM serves as the scoring and decision-making agent at every pruning step. The inference proceeds in three phases: (1) initialization, where the LLM extracts topic entities from the question; (2) iterative exploration, where at each depth the LLM first searches all neighboring relations of the current tail entities, prunes to top-N relations via a scoring prompt, then searches tail entities for those relations and prunes to top-N entities; and (3) reasoning, where the LLM evaluates whether the accumulated paths suffice to answer the question and either generates the answer or triggers another exploration round. A variant, ToG-R, replaces full triple paths with relation chains and substitutes LLM-based entity pruning with random sampling, halving the number of LLM calls (ND+D+1 vs. 2ND+D+1) while reducing sensitivity to unfamiliar intermediate entity names. The key distinction from prior LLM ⊕ KG methods is that the LLM participates in every hop of graph traversal rather than issuing a single query, allowing it to complement missing KG relations with its own parametric knowledge mid-reasoning.

## Results

ToG with GPT-4 achieves overall SOTA on 6 out of 9 datasets without any additional training, outperforming fine-tuned methods that were specifically trained on evaluation data. Headline numbers include 82.6% EM on WebQSP (vs. 82.1% DeCAF), 81.4% on GrailQA (vs. 75.4% Pangu), 54.7% on QALD10-en (vs. 45.4% SPARQL-QA), 88.3% on Zero-Shot RE (vs. 74.6% KILT), and 95.6% on Creak (vs. 88.2% RACo-Large). Even with the smaller ChatGPT backbone, ToG surpasses all prompting-based SOTA methods across every dataset. Notably, ToG with Llama-2-70B (57.6% on CWQ) outperforms CoT with GPT-4 (46.0%), demonstrating that a small LLM augmented with KG reasoning can rival a much larger model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against fine-tuned SOTA methods, ToG with GPT-4 wins on WebQSP (82.6 vs. 82.1 DeCAF), GrailQA (81.4 vs. 75.4 Pangu), QALD10-en (54.7 vs. 45.4 SPARQL-QA), WebQuestions (57.9 vs. 56.3 FiE+PAQ), T-REx (88.3 vs. 87.7 Re2G), Zero-Shot RE (88.3 vs. 74.6 KILT), and Creak (95.6 vs. 88.2 RACo-Large), but trails on CWQ (69.5 vs. 70.4 DeCAF) and Simple Questions (66.7 vs. 85.8 DiFaR). Among prompting-based methods, ToG with even ChatGPT surpasses KB-BINDER (74.4 on WebQSP), StructGPT (72.6), and KD-CoT (50.5 on CWQ) across all datasets. Compared to LLM-only baselines (CoT, SC, IO), ToG yields gains of 18.5–23.5% on CWQ and 11.5–15.3% on WebQSP depending on backbone model size, with the gap widening for stronger LLMs. The paper also shows that ToG with Llama-2-70B (57.6% CWQ, 68.9% WebQSP) outperforms CoT with GPT-4 (46.0%, 67.3%), suggesting KG-augmented small models can substitute expensive large models in vertical domains. Ablations confirm that LLM-based pruning is critical (replacing with BM25/SentenceBERT drops CWQ by ~8.4% and WebQSP by ~15.1%), and that triple-based prompt representation outperforms sequence or sentence formats.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new 'LLM ⊗ KG' paradigm that departs from both LLM-only prompting (CoT, Self-Consistency) and the looser 'LLM ⊕ KG' approach where the LLM merely translates questions into SPARQL queries or receives one-shot retrieved knowledge as prompt augmentation. Conceptually, it builds on beam search over knowledge graphs (Atif et al., 2023; Sun et al., 2023a) and the ReAct-style agent loop (Yao et al., 2022), but repurposes the LLM as an iterative pruning and decision-making agent that walks the graph step-by-step rather than generating a single formal query. The authors note that StructGPT (Jiang et al., 2023) is a special case of their framework, and they position ToG as a generalization that handles multi-hop exploration with explicit path maintenance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play nature of ToG makes it immediately deployable across different LLMs, KGs, and prompting strategies without retraining, which is practically significant for organizations that cannot afford fine-tuning. The finding that Llama-2-70B with ToG outperforms GPT-4 with CoT on multi-hop KBQA suggests a cheaper technical route for vertical-domain deployments where external KGs are available. Key limitations include dependence on KG completeness and correctness (outdated triples propagate errors), higher computational cost (~22 LLM calls per question vs. 1 for CoT), and weaker gains on single-hop tasks. Open questions include whether the framework scales to very large KGs like Wikidata without significant pruning difficulty, and how to automate the knowledge-infusion loop for KG correction at scale.
