## Problem

Existing learning-based crystal generation methods (diffusion models, VAEs, GANs) largely ignore space group symmetry, which is fundamental to crystal geometry and intrinsically linked to properties like topological phases. Directly enforcing space group constraints is nontrivial because the 230 space groups impose diverse and complex geometric restrictions on both lattice shape and atomic positions, making seamless integration into a generative framework challenging.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240269/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as input an atom composition (one-hot vector), a target space group (which determines the crystal family and the set of Wyckoff positions), and the Wyckoff position assignment for each atom. It outputs a full crystal structure: the lattice matrix L (reconstructed from the O(3)-invariant coefficient vector k via polar decomposition), fractional coordinates for all N atoms (expanded from N' basic Wyckoff coordinates via symmetry operations R_si, t_si), and atom types. Notably, the diffusion process only operates on the unconstrained dimensions of k (some of the 6 coefficients are fixed by the crystal family) and on the N' basic Wyckoff coordinates rather than all N atomic positions, reducing the effective degrees of freedom.

## Method

The core insight is to decompose the space group constraint into two tractable components: (1) a basis constraint on the O(3)-invariant logarithmic representation of the lattice matrix, obtained via polar decomposition L = Q exp(S) where the symmetric matrix S is expanded in 6 orthogonal bases with crystal-family-specific zero/fixed-value constraints on the coefficients k_i; and (2) a Wyckoff position constraint that reduces fractional coordinate generation to N' basic coordinates, with all N positions recovered via symmetry transformations. A joint DDPM diffusion process simultaneously denoises the unconstrained k dimensions (standard Gaussian), the basic Wyckoff fractional coordinates F' (wrapped normal for periodicity, with noise projected onto constrained subspaces via pseudo-inverse of R_s0), and the basic atom types A' (standard Gaussian on one-hot). The denoising backbone is an E(3)-invariant message-passing GNN that uses Fourier embeddings of relative fractional coordinates and the invariant k vector (replacing L^T L from DiffCSP), with constrained outputs enforced by masking k, projecting F' onto Wyckoff subspaces, and applying WyckoffMean to ensure symmetry-consistent predictions.

## Results

On the crystal structure prediction (CSP) task, DiffCSP++ with ground-truth space groups achieves 98.44% match rate on Perov-5, 80.27% on MP-20, and 46.29% on MPTS-52, substantially outperforming DiffCSP (52.02%, 51.49%, 12.19%). With the practical CSPML template approach (no GT access), DiffCSP++ still reaches 70.58% MR on MP-20 and 37.17% on MPTS-52, versus 51.49% and 12.19% for DiffCSP. On ab initio generation, DiffCSP++ achieves the best Wasserstein distances for density (d_ρ = 0.0661 on Perov-5, 0.2351 on MP-20) and formation energy (d_E = 0.0405, 0.0574) while maintaining ~100% structural validity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffCSP++ (w/CSPML) decisively outperforms DiffCSP on MP-20 (70.58% vs 51.49% MR) and MPTS-52 (37.17% vs 12.19% MR), with a ~19 and ~25 percentage point gap respectively, while roughly matching DiffCSP on Perov-5 (52.17% vs 52.02%). Against CDVAE, the improvement is even more pronounced: 70.58% vs 33.90% on MP-20 and 37.17% vs 5.34% on MPTS-52. Optimization-based methods (RS, BO, PSO from Cheng et al.) and the autoregressive P-cG-SchNet are far behind, with the best optimization baseline (BO) reaching only 12.68% on MP-20. On ab initio generation, DiffCSP++ achieves the lowest density and formation-energy Wasserstein distances on Perov-5 (d_ρ=0.0661, d_E=0.0405) and MP-20 (d_ρ=0.2351, d_E=0.0574), beating DiffCSP (0.1110, 0.0263) and CDVAE (0.1258, 0.0264) on Perov-5, though it slightly trails DiffCSP on element-count distance for MP-20 (0.3749 vs 0.3398) and formation energy for Carbon-24 (0.0935 vs 0.0820). FTCP and G-SchNet are effectively non-competitive, with near-zero coverage and very poor validity. CSPML (Kusaba et al.) is not a direct generative baseline but serves as the template-retrieval component; the paper notes a remaining gap between CSPML-derived and GT-derived space groups (70.58% vs 80.27% on MP-20), suggesting room for improved template selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DiffCSP (Jiao et al., 2023), which jointly generates lattice matrices and fractional coordinates via a diffusion framework, by incorporating space group symmetry constraints that prior crystal generation methods (CDVAE, MatterGen, GemsDiff) largely overlooked. The key conceptual departure is a mathematical reduction of the 230 space group constraints into two tractable components: crystal-family constraints on the O(3)-invariant logarithmic representation of the lattice (via polar decomposition) and Wyckoff-position constraints on fractional coordinates. This contrasts with PGCGM (Zhao et al., 2023), which embeds space group affine matrices into a GAN but is restricted to ternary systems, and PCVAE (Liu et al., 2023), which constrains lattice parameters via a conditional VAE rather than within a diffusion process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Space group symmetry is intrinsically linked to critical material properties (e.g., topological phases), yet generative models have rarely enforced it, limiting their utility for targeted material design. The demonstrated ability to generate structures under customized space group constraints opens a practical pathway for symmetry-guided discovery. A notable limitation is the performance gap between ground-truth space groups (80.27% MR on MP-20) and those derived from the CSPML template-matching pipeline (70.58%), suggesting that improved template retrieval or learned space group prediction could further close the gap. The method also remains dependent on pre-specified Wyckoff position assignments, which constrains its fully autonomous applicability.
