## Problem

The mainstream LIF neuron model in SNNs suffers from two critical deficiencies: gradient vanishing or exploding along identity-mapping paths in deep residual networks, and an inability to differentiate neurons' current responses based on their distinct historical spike-firing patterns. These limitations restrict the LIF model's representational capacity and prevent effective training of deep SNN architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240193/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet) or neuromorphic event streams (DVS-CIFAR10 resized to 48×48 and integrated into 10 frames) as input, processed over T discrete time-steps (typically 4–10). At each time-step, binary spike signals propagate through the network, and the final output is a class label derived from the average firing rate across all time-steps, making the temporal dimension an internal computation axis rather than an input sequence.

## Method

The LM-H neuron introduces a two-compartment architecture with a dendrite layer (long-term memory) and a soma layer (spike decision), where four learnable scaling factors (μ_D, μ_S, λ_D, λ_S) dynamically regulate the extraction ratio between historical and current information. The key theoretical insight is that LIF is a strict special case of LM-H (μ_D=μ_S=0, λ_D=1), enabling a progressive training strategy that starts from the LIF regime and gradually unlocks the dendrite's historical memory via a scalable-shiftable sigmoid parameterization. The progressive STBP algorithm back-propagates through both spatial (dendrite-level and soma-level) and temporal dimensions, with λ_D specifically stabilizing gradient flow through deep residual identity paths. For efficiency, the paper further proposes hybrid training (QCFS-based ANN-SNN conversion followed by ≤30 epochs of progressive fine-tuning) and time-slicing online training that detaches gradient chains between consecutive k-step slices to bound memory at O(k).

## Results

The LM-H model achieves 96.36% top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-10 (ResNet-19, T=4), 80.31% on CIFAR-100 (ResNet-19, T=4), 59.93% on ImageNet-200 (VGG-13, T=4), and 79.10% on DVS-CIFAR10 (ResNet-19, T=10). On ImageNet-1k (ResNet-34, T=4), the radical version reaches 69.73%, surpassing all prior SNN methods. Notably, the method's 4-time-step results consistently exceed the 6-time-step results of competing approaches on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CIFAR-100 (ResNet-19, T=4), the LM-H model achieves 80.31%, outperforming GLIF (77.05%) by 3.26% and TEBN with improved structure (78.71%) by 1.60%. On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-19, T=4), it reaches 96.36%, exceeding GLIF (94.85%) by 1.51% and TET (94.44%) by 1.92%. On ImageNet-200 (VGG-13), the method achieves 59.93% at only 4 time-steps, surpassing ASGL (56.81% at T=8) by 3.12% while using fewer time-steps, and beating Online-LTL (54.82% at T=16) by 5.11%. On DVS-CIFAR10 (ResNet-19, T=10), the LM-H model leads MPBN (74.40%) by 4.70% and RecDis-SNN (72.42%) by 6.68%. On ImageNet-1k (ResNet-34, T=4), the radical version reaches 69.73%, outperforming GLIF (67.52%) by 2.21% and SEW ResNet (67.04%) by 2.69%. In hybrid training (Table 2), the progressive fine-tuning stage adds 3.49% (VGG-16) and 22.98% (ResNet-20) over QCFS conversion alone on CIFAR-100, demonstrating the critical role of the LM-H parameter optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LIF neuron model by proposing the Learnable Multi-hierarchical (LM-H) model, which introduces separate dendrite and soma layers with learnable scaling factors. It builds directly on the STBP training framework (Wu et al., 2018) and the ANN-SNN conversion paradigm (Bu et al., 2022), while drawing conceptual inspiration from the two-compartment TC-LIF model (Zhang et al., 2023) and the gated GLIF neuron (Yao et al., 2022). The key departure from TC-LIF is a rigorous mathematical demonstration that LIF is a strict special case of LM-H, combined with a progressive training strategy that starts from the LIF regime and gradually introduces dendrite/soma parameters, avoiding the gradient instability issues that plague TC-LIF. The efficient training frameworks (hybrid and time-slicing online) extend prior work by Rathi & Roy (2023) and Xiao et al. (2022) to accommodate the additional learnable membrane parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LM-H model provides a principled, mathematically grounded generalization of LIF that addresses two concrete failure modes (gradient vanishing/exploding in deep residual SNNs and inability to differentiate neurons with identical current states but different histories), making it a more robust building block for deep spiking architectures. The progressive training framework is practically important because it allows practitioners to start from a well-understood LIF initialization and gradually unlock additional representational capacity, reducing training instability. The 'Long-Range Performance Reward' phenomenon—where models trained at T time-steps continue to improve at inference with more steps—suggests the learned membrane parameters encode transferable temporal dynamics, though the paper does not deeply characterize when or why this occurs. Open questions include whether the radical (per-time-step parameter) version's modest gains justify its increased parameter count at scale, and whether the multi-hierarchical structure maps meaningfully to biological dendritic compartmentalization.
