## Problem

Current evaluation of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) relies on task-performance metrics (VQA accuracy, CIDEr) that mask critical alignment flaws such as object hallucinations, inability to abstain, poor compositionality, weak explainability, and failure to follow complex user instructions. This paper asks whether these flaws persist despite scaling, and whether training-free in-context learning (ICL) can efficiently mitigate them without costly fine-tuning or RLHF.

## Input / Output

Each LMM receives an RGB image paired with a text question or instruction, optionally preceded by N in-context demonstrations formatted as (image, question, answer) triplets delimited by end-of-chunk tokens. The output varies by evaluation axis: a free-form caption or answer (hallucination, instruction following), a binary yes/no or a fixed abstention keyword (abstention, compositionality), or a natural-language explanation (explainability). For the proposed X-ICL variants the context is further structured: CoH-ICL appends a positive and a negative response to each demonstration, SC-ICL inserts a secondary answerability question in a second inference pass, and MT-ICL embeds a second task prompt within every demonstration.

## Method

The paper systematically evaluates 10 open-source LMMs (OpenFlamingo and IDEFICS, 3B–80B parameters) on five axes under zero-shot and few-shot ICL, revealing that ICL's effect is highly nuanced: it substantially improves explainability and abstention, marginally helps instruction following, has no measurable effect on compositionality, and actually amplifies object hallucinations beyond 4 shots. Building on these findings, the authors propose three training-free ICL variants that require no weight updates. Chain-of-Hindsight ICL (CoH-ICL) places both a human-annotated positive response and a model-generated negative response in each demonstration, steering the model toward higher-quality explanations. Self-Correcting ICL (SC-ICL) performs a two-step inference: the model first answers the question via standard ICL, then re-evaluates whether the question is answerable from the image and replaces the answer with an abstention keyword if it is not. Multitask ICL (MT-ICL) embeds an auxiliary task (object recognition for hallucination reduction, or answerability classification for abstention) alongside the primary task within each in-context demonstration, leveraging cross-task synergy without any parameter changes.

## Results

On COCO captioning, LMMs suffer severe object hallucinations: zero-shot CHAIR_s scores range from 6.85 (OFv2-3B) to 31.42 (IDEFICS-9B), far worse than smaller SoTA models like OFA (4.36) and UnIVAL (4.44). ICL amplifies hallucinations beyond 4 shots (e.g., OFv2-9B CHAIR_s rises from 5.02 at 4-shot to 9.00 at 32-shot), while ICL significantly improves abstention (OFv2-9B F1: 28.27→56.44 from 4→32 shots) and explainability (OFv2-9B CIDEr: 61.43→80.51). The proposed SC-ICL yields the largest single improvement, boosting abstention F1 by +19.07 points over ICL at 4-shot for OFv2-9B, and CoH-ICL improves explainability CIDEr by +9.33 at 4-shot.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On hallucination (COCO), all LMMs produce substantially more object hallucinations than smaller finetuned baselines: OFA achieves CHAIR_s of 4.36 and UnIVAL 4.44, while the best LMM (OFv2-3B) reaches only 6.85 and IDEFICS-9B reaches 31.42 in zero-shot. On explainability (VQA-X), IDEFICS-80B with ICL surpasses the NLX-GPT SoTA (CIDEr ~90+), while smaller LMMs remain well below it (OFv2-9B: 80.51 CIDEr at 32-shot ICL). The proposed SC-ICL outperforms standard ICL on abstention by up to +19.07 F1 points (OFv2-9B, 4-shot), and CoH-ICL outperforms ICL on explainability by +9.33 CIDEr (OFv2-9B, 4-shot). MT-ICL provides consistent but smaller gains over ICL across both abstention and explainability. Notably, ICL has virtually no effect on compositionality (CREPE scores remain near 50–60% regardless of shot count), and ICL actually worsens hallucination beyond 4 shots, meaning the proposed variants cannot fully remedy all five identified flaws.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Flamingo-family LMM architecture (OpenFlamingo, IDEFICS) by shifting the evaluation paradigm from task performance (VQA accuracy, CIDEr) to alignment-oriented axes: hallucination, abstention, compositionality, explainability, and instruction following. For remediation, it departs from the dominant training-based approaches (instruction tuning, RLHF) and instead adapts three LLM-side ideas—Chain-of-Hindsight, self-correction, and multitask learning—into training-free multimodal ICL variants (CoH-ICL, SC-ICL, MT-ICL). The work is positioned as complementary to prior LMM evaluation benchmarks (MMBench, LLaVA-Bench, SEED-Bench), which focus on task performance, by explicitly targeting alignment flaws that those benchmarks do not capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic finding that ICL amplifies object hallucinations (rather than reducing them) is practically important for anyone deploying LMMs with in-context demonstrations, as it inverts the expected benefit of adding more shots. The paper also reveals that apparent compositionality gains in 80B models are largely dataset-bias artifacts, cautioning against over-interpreting scale. Limitations include evaluation restricted to Flamingo-architecture models up to 80B, modest absolute improvements from the proposed ICL variants, and reliance on GPT-4 for instruction-following scoring. Open questions include whether these findings generalize to other LMM architectures (e.g., LLaVA, BLIP-2) and whether more sophisticated ICL designs or RLHF can close the remaining gaps.
