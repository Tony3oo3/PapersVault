## Problem

Vector Neurons (VN), a popular SO(3)-equivariant network, are confined to 3D feature space, which limits their ability to capture the multi-frequency details present in 3D shapes. Existing high-dimensional equivariant representations (e.g., spherical harmonics in TFN or SE(3)-transformers) rely on quantum-mechanical Wigner-D matrices that are difficult to interpret and lock users into specific architectures incompatible with modern point-processing networks. The paper seeks an intuitive, high-dimensional, frequency-based equivariant feature that can be dropped into standard VN-based architectures.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 3D point cloud (typically 300 surface points with Gaussian noise) and first maps each point through the equivariant feature ψ: R³ → Rⁿ before feeding the augmented (3+n)-dimensional vector into VN-based PointNet or DGCNN encoders. Depending on the downstream task, the network outputs class labels, per-point part labels, per-point normal vectors, per-query-point occupancy values (for neural implicit reconstruction), a shape embedding (for compression), or a rotation matrix (for registration). The feature dimension n is configurable (e.g., 5, 8, or 15), and multi-frequency features at n=3,5,7,9 can be concatenated for richer representation.

## Method

The core contribution is a provably equivariant feature ψ(u) = ||u|| D(R^z(û)) ê, where D: SO(3)→SO(n) is constructed as D(R) = exp(θω̂·J) with three n×n skew-symmetric matrices J₁,J₂,J₃ satisfying cyclic commutator relations [J₁,J₂]=J₃, [J₂,J₃]=J₁, [J₃,J₁]=J₂. The key insight is that D(R) decomposes into sinusoids whose maximum frequency is ⌊(n−1)/2⌋/2π, directly linking feature dimensionality to the highest resolvable spatial frequency. The J matrices are constructed algorithmically: J₃ is sampled as a random skew-symmetric matrix whose eigenvalues are set to {−⌊(n−1)/2⌋i, …, 0, …, ⌊(n−1)/2⌋i}, then J₁ and J₂ are found by solving a linear system for the first three commutator/skew-symmetry constraints and a Cross-Entropy Method optimization for the remaining nonlinear constraint. This feature is plugged into VN-based PointNet and DGCNN with a new magnitude-scaling nonlinearity, achieving state-of-the-art equivariant performance across classification, segmentation, normal estimation, completion, compression, and registration.

## Results

On ModelNet40 classification, FER-VN-DGCNN achieves 90.5% accuracy across all augmentation settings (z/z, z/SO(3), SO(3)/SO(3)), the best among rotation-equivariant methods. On ShapeNet part segmentation, FER-VN-DGCNN reaches 83.5% mean IoU under SO(3)/SO(3), outperforming all other equivariant baselines. For point cloud completion, FER-VN-OccNet achieves 71.9% volumetric mIoU in all settings, surpassing both OccNet (71.4% at canonical, 30.9% under rotation) and VN-OccNet (69.3%). On normal estimation, FER-VN-DGCNN attains the lowest error (0.142 on ShapeNet, 0.079 on ModelNet40 under SO(3)/SO(3)), and on registration it achieves the best Chamfer Distance in the Distinct Sample (0.00347) and Varying Density (0.00714) settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FER-VN-DGCNN outperforms VN-DGCNN (Deng et al., 2021) by +1.0% on ModelNet40 classification (90.5% vs 89.5%) and +2.1% on ShapeNet segmentation (83.5% vs 81.4%), and by a large margin on point cloud completion (71.9% vs 69.3% mIoU), directly addressing the limitation that VN's 3D features cannot capture multi-frequency details. Against SVNet-DGCNN (Su et al., 2022), FER-VN-DGCNN wins on classification (90.5% vs 90.3%) and segmentation (83.5% vs 81.4%). Compared to TFN (Thomas et al., 2018), FER-VN-DGCNN leads by +2.0% on classification and +6.7% on segmentation under SO(3)/SO(3). The only method that slightly outperforms FER-VN-DGCNN is the rotation-invariant PaRINet (Chen & Cong, 2022), which achieves 91.4% on classification and 83.8% on segmentation, but PaRINet is specifically designed for invariance rather than equivariance. On point cloud registration, FER-VN-EquivReg beats VN-EquivReg (Zhu et al., 2022) by 38% in Chamfer Distance on Distinct Sample (0.00347 vs 0.00560) and 34% on Varying Density (0.00714 vs 0.01077), and outperforms DeepGMR (Yuan et al., 2020) by 55% and 55% respectively in those settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Vector Neurons (VN) by Deng et al. (2021), which achieved SO(3)-equivariance by modifying standard NN operations but confined features to 3D space, limiting expressivity. It also positions itself against TFN and SE(3)-Transformers, which use spherical harmonics and Wigner-D matrices from quantum mechanics for high-dimensional equivariant features but are difficult to interpret and require specific architectures. The key conceptual departure is the observation that rotation matrices decompose into sinusoids whose frequencies are determined by eigenvalues, enabling a provably equivariant multi-frequency feature representation that plugs directly into standard PointNet and DGCNN architectures without architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides an intuitive, accessible alternative to quantum-mechanics-based equivariant features, lowering the barrier for practitioners to use high-dimensional equivariant representations in standard 3D vision pipelines. The frequency-based perspective connects equivariant network design to the well-understood Fourier analysis literature (NeRF, Tancik et al.), making hyperparameter choices (feature dimension n) interpretable in terms of maximum representable frequency. Open questions include whether the CEM-based construction of the J matrices scales robustly to very high dimensions, whether the approach extends naturally to SE(3) (translation equivariance), and whether the multi-frequency concatenation strategy (n=3,5,7,9) is optimal or whether learned frequency selection could further improve performance.
