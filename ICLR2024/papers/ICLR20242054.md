## Problem

CLIP ViTs achieve strong zero-shot image classification but their dense feature maps encode global image context rather than local region identity, making them poorly suited for open-vocabulary dense prediction tasks like detection and segmentation. Unlike CLIP CNNs, whose dense features naturally support region recognition, ViT-based CLIPs suffer a domain shift when transferring from full-image to local-region representation, and prior attempts (masked attention, modified output layers) have yielded sub-optimal results.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242054/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

During self-distillation training, the method consumes only unlabeled RGB images (no text or region annotations required), splitting each into a random m×n grid of patches where m,n ∈ {1,…,6}. The Student model processes the full image at high resolution (1024×1024 for ViT-B/16, 896×896 for ViT-L/14) to produce a dense feature map, while the frozen Teacher processes each individual patch crop at standard CLIP resolution (224×224 or 336×336). The output is a fine-tuned CLIP ViT whose dense features can be plugged into downstream detectors (F-ViT), semantic segmentation (Cat-Seg), or panoptic segmentation (ODISE) pipelines to produce bounding boxes, semantic masks, or panoptic masks with open-vocabulary category labels.

## Method

CLIPSelf performs self-distillation by aligning the Student's region-level dense representations to the Teacher's image-level representations of the corresponding image crops, eliminating the need for region-text pairs. Concretely, an image is partitioned into a random m×n grid; the Student (initialized from the Teacher) extracts a dense feature map via a modified last attention block (self-attention removed, projection and FFN retained), and each patch's region embedding is obtained by RoIAlign pooling from this map. The Teacher (frozen original CLIP ViT) independently encodes each patch crop to produce an image-level embedding. The training loss is the mean 1-minus-cosine-similarity between corresponding Student dense embeddings and Teacher image embeddings across all patches. This design leverages the fact that CLIP ViTs already recognize individual regions well at the image level (as shown by crop-based classification), using that capability as a free, noise-free teacher signal to reshape the dense representation toward local region identity.

## Results

On OV-COCO, F-ViT+CLIPSelf (ViT-L/14) achieves 44.3 AP₅₀ⁿᵒᵛᵉˡ, surpassing the previous best ViT-based method CFM-ViT (ViT-L/16) by 10.2 points and the CNN-based CORA+ (RN50x4) by 1.2 points. On OV-LVIS, the same model reaches 34.9 mAPᵣ, outperforming CFM-ViT (33.9) and RO-ViT ViT-H/16 (34.1). For open-vocabulary semantic segmentation, Cat-Seg+CLIPSelf (ViT-L/14) improves ADE-150 mIoU from 31.5 to 34.5 and ADE-847 mIoU from 10.8 to 12.4. On panoptic segmentation, ODISE+CLIPSelf (ViT-L/14) raises COCO Panoptic PQ from 45.3 to 45.7 and ADE20K PQ from 22.9 to 23.7.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On OV-COCO, F-ViT+CLIPSelf (ViT-L/14) at 44.3 AP₅₀ⁿᵒᵛᵉˡ decisively outperforms all prior ViT-based detectors: CFM-ViT (34.1), RO-ViT (33.0), and PromptOVD (30.6), and also surpasses the strongest CNN-based method CORA+ (43.1) by 1.2 points. On OV-LVIS, the same model (34.9 mAPᵣ) edges out CFM-ViT (33.9) and RO-ViT ViT-H/16 (34.1), while substantially exceeding CORA+ (28.1) and F-VLM (32.8). For semantic segmentation, Cat-Seg+CLIPSelf (ViT-L/14) improves over Cat-Seg by +3.0 mIoU on ADE-150 and +1.6 on ADE-847, and over SAN by +2.4 mIoU on ADE-150. On panoptic segmentation, ODISE+CLIPSelf yields modest but consistent gains over ODISE (+0.4 PQ on COCO Panoptic, +0.8 PQ on ADE20K). The ablation in Table 6 confirms that CLIPSelf outperforms the region-text pair approach (RegionCLIP-style) by +2.6 AP₅₀ⁿᵒᵛᵉˡ on OV-COCO with the same ViT-B/16 backbone, validating that self-distillation avoids the noise inherent in pseudo region-text matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on CLIP's vision-language alignment (Radford et al., 2021) and the dense feature extraction procedure from Zhou et al. (2022a), but departs fundamentally from prior region-language alignment approaches that rely on region-text pairs (RegionCLIP, GLIP, Grounding DINO) or architectural modifications to ViTs (CFM-ViT, RO-ViT). Instead, CLIPSelf positions itself between upstream image-text pre-training and downstream dense prediction by using self-distillation: the ViT's own image-level representation of a crop serves as the teacher for its dense feature map, entirely bypassing the text modality during fine-tuning. The detection baseline (F-ViT) follows F-VLM's frozen-backbone paradigm but extends it to ViT backbones, which prior work found challenging.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that CLIP ViT dense features encode global image representations rather than local region representations (confirmed by retrieval experiments and K-Means visualizations) provides a clear diagnostic for why ViTs underperform CNNs in dense prediction despite superior image-level classification. The self-distillation solution is remarkably simple and general, improving detection, semantic segmentation, and panoptic segmentation simultaneously without task-specific modifications. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to Swin-based foundation models (GLIP, Grounding DINO) where region-language grounding is learned separately, and whether the performance ceiling is ultimately bounded by the original CLIP's image-level alignment quality.
