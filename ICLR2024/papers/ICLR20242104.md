## Problem

Game theory has resisted the deep learning paradigm shift seen in other domains, with task-specific solutions (Elo ratings, non-differentiable equilibrium solvers) suffering from fundamental limitations such as inability to capture cyclic dynamics or non-deterministic convergence. The paper asks whether the inherent permutation equivariance of normal-form games—any reordering of strategies yields an equivalent game—can be leveraged for a single, general-purpose representation that serves multiple game-theoretic tasks without loss of generality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a payoff tensor G of shape N×T^N (N players, T actions each) as input, or an incomplete version where unobserved joint-actions are indicated by binary validity masks. It produces a set of action embeddings {a_p^t ∈ R^D} that are equivariant to player and action permutations. Downstream decoders operate at different granularities: per-action (softmax over action logits for mixed-strategy NE), per-joint-action (scalar deviation gain), or per-payoff (reconstructed scalar), all sharing the same encoder. The number of network parameters is independent of game size, enabling a single architecture to handle games of arbitrary dimensions.

## Method

NfgTransformer reverses the generative process from actions to payoffs by representing an NFG as action embeddings, zero-initialised and iteratively refined through K blocks. Each block performs three attention operations: (1) action-to-joint-action self-attention, where each action's embedding concatenated with its payoff under a joint action serves as both query and key-value, producing a 'play' vector; (2) action-to-play cross-attention, where a singleton action embedding queries over its unordered set of plays to aggregate information; and (3) action-to-action self-attention, propagating information across all action embeddings. Equivariance is preserved by construction because attention is order-invariant over key-values and equivariant over queries. For incomplete games, a masked attention variant (Eq. 4) sets invalid positions to −∞ in the attention logits, allowing the model to infer unobserved payoffs. Task-specific decoders (MLP for NE gap minimisation, joint-action MLP for deviation gain, self-attention for payoff reconstruction) are attached to the shared action embeddings, trained end-to-end with task-appropriate losses.

## Results

On synthetic games, the best NfgTransformer variant (D=128, K=8, A=1) achieves NE Gap of 0.0308 (2×16), 0.0502 (2×64), 0.0412 (3×8), and 0.0297 (3×16), substantially outperforming NES (0.0829–0.1140) and MLP baselines (0.1381–0.3905). For maximum deviation-gain estimation, the same variant achieves MSE of 0.0001 (2×16) and 0.0161 (2×64), an order of magnitude below NES (0.0488 and 0.4860). On DISC games with only 10% of joint-actions observed, NfgTransformer recovers the latent Z=1 game near perfectly with error an order of magnitude lower than the second-best method (xElo), despite not being designed for this task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NfgTransformer outperforms all baselines across every game size and task. Against NES (Marris et al., 2022), the best NfgTransformer variant reduces NE Gap by 2.7×–3.9× (e.g., 0.0297 vs. 0.1140 on 3×16 games) and Max-Deviation-Gain MSE by up to 17.8× (0.0001 vs. 0.0488 on 2×16). Against the MLP baseline (Duan et al., 2023), improvements are even more dramatic: NE Gap is reduced by 4.7×–12.8× and deviation-gain MSE by up to 3854×. EquivariantMLP, which reorders actions by average payoff, consistently trails NES in 2-player settings but is outperformed by NfgTransformer by 2.7×–9.1× on NE Gap. On DISC games (Figure 3), NfgTransformer significantly outperforms Elo, mElo (Balduzzi et al., 2018), and xElo (Bertrand et al., 2023) across all observation fractions and latent dimensions, recovering the Z=1 game near perfectly at just 10% observation with error an order of magnitude below xElo — despite baselines being specifically designed for DISC games while NfgTransformer is not.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes Hartford et al. (2016), the first to apply deep representation learning to NFGs, from two-player to N-player general-sum games while satisfying the same desiderata (player/action equivariance, parameter-count independence from game size). It departs from prior neural equilibrium solvers (NES, Marris et al. 2022; Duan et al. 2023) that relied on handcrafted payoff feature vectors or order-invariant pooling, instead repurposing the transformer's self- and cross-attention mechanisms—whose built-in order-invariance over key-values and equivariance over queries—naturally implement the required permutation equivariance without sacrificing representational generality. The masked-attention extension for incomplete games is a straightforward but useful adaptation of the standard attention masking pattern.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing a single equivariant encoder that supports multiple decoding granularities (per-action, per-joint-action, per-player, whole-game), the paper unifies previously fragmented approaches to equilibrium solving, deviation-gain estimation, and game ranking under one architecture, directly addressing the non-differentiability bottleneck that has blocked integration of equilibrium solving into deep multi-agent RL pipelines. The interpretability analysis—showing that learned attention masks spontaneously converge to attending to co-player equilibrium strategies and best-responses—offers a rare window into how neural networks implement game-theoretic reasoning. Open questions include scaling to games with hundreds or thousands of actions, integration into end-to-end RL training loops, and whether the architecture's inductive biases transfer to extensive-form or stochastic games.
