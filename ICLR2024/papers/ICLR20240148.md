## Problem

Pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs) exhibit strong zero-shot generalization but degrade under distribution shifts between training and test data. Existing test-time adaptation (TTA) methods for VLMs, such as test-time prompt tuning (TPT), rely on entropy minimization, which makes the model blindly confident and unable to escape incorrect predictions. This paper asks how to adapt VLMs at test time using feedback that can both encourage correct and discourage incorrect outputs, without any ground-truth labels or task-specific training corpus.

## Input / Output

The method operates in a fully TTA setting where only a single unlabeled test sample is available at inference time. For classification, a single RGB image is augmented into 64 views, and the output is a class label from a fixed vocabulary (e.g., 1000 ImageNet classes). For retrieval, a text query or image query is matched against a candidate pool, producing a ranked list. For captioning, a single image is fed to an LLM-based captioner (CapDec or CLIPCap with OPT-125M), and the output is a generated sentence selected via beam search. In all cases, the VLM's learnable parameters (prompt tokens, encoder weights, or projector) are updated in-place for 3–8 gradient steps per sample before producing the final prediction.

## Method

RLCF frames test-time adaptation as a reinforcement learning problem where CLIP serves as a frozen reward model. Given a single test input, the VLM samples K candidates from its output distribution (top-K for classification/retrieval, beam search for captioning), and the reward is the CLIPScore between the input and each candidate minus the average CLIPScore across all K samples, yielding both positive and negative feedback signals. Parameters are then updated via the REINFORCE policy gradient (log-derivative trick) to maximize expected reward. The framework is task-agnostic in its reward design but task-specific in its pipeline: for classification it inherits TPT's augmentation and low-entropy view selection while supporting both prompt tuning and encoder tuning; for retrieval it updates only the query-side encoder for efficiency; for captioning it tunes only the CLIP-to-LLM projector while keeping the LLM frozen. Additional practical tricks include ensembling multiple CLIP reward models with preference-weighted scores, episodic TTA (resetting parameters after each sample), and a momentum buffer for incremental knowledge accumulation across samples.

## Results

On zero-shot image classification with OOD shifts, RLCF with image encoder tuning on CLIP-ViT-B/16 achieves 72.64% OOD average accuracy (RLCF-S-M), surpassing the zero-shot CLIP-ViT-B/16 baseline (57.20%) by 15.44 points and even exceeding the larger CLIP-ViT-L/14 reward model (69.97%). For prompt tuning, RLCF-S+CoOp reaches 70.93% OOD average, outperforming TPT+CoOp (62.84%) by 8.09 points. In zero-shot retrieval, RLCF-S achieves R@1 of 38.3 on MS-COCO text-to-image (↑5.3 over baseline), surpassing CLIP-ViT-L/14-336 (36.6). For image captioning, CLIPCap+RLCF improves CIDEr by up to 9.2 points on NoCaps out-domain data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RLCF decisively outperforms all entropy-minimization-based TTA methods: with image encoder tuning, RLCF-S-M (72.64% OOD avg) beats TPT (63.63%) by 9.01 points and TPT+CoOp (62.84%) by 9.80 points. Against knowledge distillation baselines, RLCF (71.69%) surpasses KD (66.76%) and ATKD (68.72%) by 4.93 and 2.97 points respectively, demonstrating that the feedback mechanism avoids the teacher-student mimicry problem. Notably, RLCF with the smaller CLIP-ViT-B/16 surpasses the larger CLIP-ViT-L/14 reward model on ImageNet-A (73.71 vs 68.82), ImageNet-V2 (69.77 vs 67.80), and ImageNet-R (86.19 vs 85.40), showing the feedback mechanism combines strengths of both models. In retrieval, RLCF-S outperforms CLIP-ViT-L/14-336 on both MS-COCO (R@1: 38.3 vs 36.6) and Flickr30K (R@1: 68.5 vs 67.1), while KD degrades with more steps (R@1 drops from 37.6 to 34.6). For captioning, RLCF improves CIDEr by 5.3–9.2 points over both CapDec and CLIPCap baselines across NoCaps domains, and outperforms the zero-shot DeCap method (CIDEr 43.9 on out-domain) by 19.9 points with CLIPCap+RLCF (63.8).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time adaptation (TTA) line for vision-language models initiated by TPT (Manli et al., 2022), which relies on entropy minimization to reduce distribution shift. The key departure is replacing entropy minimization with a reinforcement learning objective (REINFORCE) where CLIPScore serves as a standalone reward, building on the RLHF paradigm (Ouyang et al., 2022) and the CLIPScore metric (Hessel et al., 2021). Unlike Cho et al. (2022), who showed CLIPScore requires auxiliary signals (CIDEr, grammar regularization) to function as a reward, this paper demonstrates that a proper sampling strategy and average-baseline subtraction make CLIPScore sufficient on its own. The captioning pipeline is built upon LLM-based methods CapDec and CLIPCap, while the classification and retrieval pipelines inherit data augmentation and confidence selection from TPT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RLCF provides the first unified fully-TTA framework that works across discrimination tasks (classification, retrieval) and generation tasks (captioning) for VLMs, addressing a gap where prior TTA methods were task-specific. The finding that a smaller TTA model (CLIP-ViT-B/16) can surpass a larger reward model (CLIP-ViT-L/14) on OOD benchmarks through feedback—something knowledge distillation cannot achieve—suggests that the feedback mechanism preserves the student's correct predictions while incorporating teacher knowledge. Limitations include the additional compute cost of running a CLIP reward model per sample, the episodic nature of adaptation (parameters reset after each sample), and reliance on CLIP's calibration quality. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger VLMs (e.g., Flamingo, LLaVA), whether non-CLIP reward models work equally well, and whether the momentum buffer heuristic can be replaced with a principled continual-learning mechanism.
