## Problem

Existing equivariant graph neural networks (EGNNs) for molecular dynamics rely on local radius cutoffs that fail to capture non-negligible long-range interactions (electrostatic, van der Waals) in large biomolecules, while fragmentation-based quantum chemistry methods capture long-range effects but lose many-body interactions. Increasing the cutoff or stacking layers to compensate leads to information over-squashing, convergence difficulty, and prohibitive computational cost. The paper seeks a framework that explicitly models long-range interactions while preserving many-body accuracy and computational efficiency.

## Input / Output

The method consumes atomic numbers (Z ∈ ℕ^{n×1}) and 3D Cartesian positions (p ∈ ℝ^{n×3}) as input, and outputs per-atom scalar energy contributions and vectorial force predictions. A key structural detail is that the input molecule is simultaneously processed at two scales: a short-range radius graph (r_short ≈ 4–5 Å) for local many-body geometry, and a bipartite atom-fragment graph (r_long ≈ 9 Å) where fragments are generated via BRICS chemical substructure decomposition. The final prediction uses a late-fusion strategy concatenating short-range and long-range embeddings before a dense output layer.

## Method

LSR-MP introduces a two-level message-passing architecture: (1) a short-range module (e.g., ViSNet) performs standard equivariant message passing on a local radius graph to encode bonds, angles, and dihedrals; (2) a fragmentation module uses BRICS to dissect the molecule into chemically meaningful fragments, learning equivariant fragment embeddings via weighted aggregation of constituent atom embeddings; (3) a long-range module performs bipartite equivariant message passing between atoms and fragments using a distance-dependent multi-headed attention mechanism with size-dependent radial basis function encoding. The framework is proven to alleviate over-squashing by reducing the graph commute time from O(|E|²) to O(|E|) through the fragment virtual nodes. The approach is architecture-agnostic and demonstrated to improve PaiNN, ET, Equiformer, and ViSNet backbones by 15–59% in force MAE on large biomolecules.

## Results

On the Chignolin dataset (166 atoms, ~37 Å diameter), ViSNet-LSRM achieves 0.1267 kcal/mol energy MAE and 0.1278 kcal/mol/Å force MAE, reducing force MAE by ~95% over the ViSNet backbone (2.4355) and ~88% over Equiformer (1.0967). On the largest MD22 molecule AT-AT-CG-CG (118 atoms, ~24 Å), ViSNet-LSRM reaches 0.1335 energy MAE and 0.1135 force MAE, outperforming MACE (0.1578/0.1578) and Equiformer (0.1510/0.1510). The model uses only 1.70M parameters and trains at 19 s/epoch, compared to 3.02M/155 s for Equiformer and 15.11M/818 s for Allegro at comparable force MAE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViSNet-LSRM outperforms all nine baselines on every molecule where they are directly comparable. The most dramatic gains appear on Chignolin (166 atoms), where ViSNet-LSRM (0.1267/0.1278) beats Equiformer (1.0967/1.0967) by ~88%, PaiNN (2.4491/2.5298) by ~95%, and Allegro (3.513 energy) by ~96%. On the 118-atom AT-AT-CG-CG, ViSNet-LSRM (0.1335/0.1135) surpasses MACE (0.1578/0.1578) and Equiformer (0.1510/0.1510) by roughly 15–28% in force MAE. Compared to its own backbone ViSNet, the improvement scales with system size: from ~18% on the 42-atom Ac-Ala3-NHMe to ~95% on Chignolin, confirming that explicit long-range message passing becomes increasingly critical as molecular diameter grows. In terms of efficiency, ViSNet-LSRM uses 44% fewer parameters than Equiformer (1.70M vs 3.02M) and trains 8× faster (19 s vs 155 s per epoch) at comparable force MAE on AT-AT-CG-CG, while Allegro requires 9× more parameters and 43× longer training time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ViSNet as its short-range backbone and generalizes the equivariant graph neural network (EGNN) paradigm by introducing a bipartite atom-fragment message-passing layer for long-range interactions. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate lines of work: quantum-chemistry fragmentation methods (BRICS, divide-and-conquer, MFCC) that capture long-range physics but neglect many-body effects, and EGNNs that model many-body interactions locally but lose information beyond a radius cutoff. The theoretical grounding draws on the over-squashing literature (Alon & Yahav, Topping et al., Di Giovanni et al.), providing a commute-time-based proof that the long-range module alleviates Jacobian obstruction, and the attention mechanism for atom-fragment interaction follows the equivariant transformer lineage (TorchMD-NET, Equiformer).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental and practically important limitation of EGNNs for large biomolecules: the inability to capture non-negligible long-range electrostatic and van der Waals interactions without incurring prohibitive cost or information over-squashing. The demonstrated 40% MAE reduction on MD22 and Chignolin, combined with the framework's general applicability across PaiNN, ET, Equiformer, and ViSNet, makes it a broadly useful architectural component for the ML force-field community. Key open questions remain: BRICS fails on supramolecules (requiring fallback to k-means or spectral clustering), the fragmentation step is non-differentiable and thus not end-to-end learnable, and whether the approach extends to periodic boundary conditions or condensed-phase systems is unaddressed.
