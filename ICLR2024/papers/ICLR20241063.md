## Problem

In-context RL agents using sequence models face three coupled bottlenecks: limited memory (context length), short planning horizons (discount factor), and instability when scaling to long-sequence Transformers with off-policy updates. Prior work showed off-policy learning could stabilize recurrent in-context agents, but extending this to Transformers over full rollouts in parallel remained an unsolved engineering challenge. Additionally, in-context RL had not been extended to goal-conditioned problems with sparse rewards and hard exploration.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes a trajectory sequence of up to l timesteps (each containing observation, action, reward, and done flag) plus a goal/instruction string of up to k subgoal tokens, and produces a single action (discrete or continuous) at the current timestep. The context length l is set equal to the full rollout horizon H (up to 10,000 timesteps in experiments), and the timestep encoder maps heterogeneous observation formats (pixels, texture IDs, depth sensors, coordinates) to a unified fixed-size representation before the shared Transformer processes the sequence. At test time, the agent can select actions corresponding to any of the γ_N discount horizons learned during training.

## Method

AMAGO trains a single long-context Transformer as a shared trajectory encoder for both actor and critic, combining their objectives into one loss optimized by a single optimizer with careful gradient detachment to prevent the critic from minimizing the actor's objective. The Transformer architecture incorporates Normformer's extra LayerNorms, σ-Reparam's bounded query/key/value magnitudes, and Leaky ReLU activations to prevent attention entropy collapse in long-sequence RL. A multi-gamma update jointly optimizes actor-critic losses across multiple discount factors (γ_N values), creating diverse Q-value surfaces that maintain learning signal throughout training, with PopArt normalizing loss scales so hyperparameters transfer across environments. For goal-conditioned domains, a hindsight instruction relabeling scheme extends HER to multi-step goals by sampling alternative subgoals from achieved timesteps and merging them into the original instruction in chronological order, effectively creating automatic exploration plans in open-world settings.

## Results

On the 39-environment POPGym suite, AMAGO achieves an average standardized score of 0.95 (normalized to [0,1] per environment), approaching the upper bound set by an exhaustive grid search over 13 alternative sequence models. In 9 recall-intensive POPGym environments, AMAGO reaches a normalized score of 0.999 versus 0.19 for the best GRU-based baseline. AMAGO stably solves the Passive T-Maze memory task up to context length l=H=10,000 timesteps (limited only by GPU memory), and in Crafter it completes multi-step instructions across procedurally generated worlds, with hindsight instruction relabeling proving essential for advanced goals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMAGO substantially outperforms the GRU-based recurrent baseline [94] on POPGym, most dramatically in 9 recall-intensive environments where it scores 0.999 versus 0.19, demonstrating that Transformers unlock memory capabilities that RNNs cannot match in off-policy RL. Against the efficient Transformer variant [95] and the best per-environment grid search over 13 on-policy sequence models, AMAGO's 0.95 average approaches the upper bound while requiring no per-environment tuning. On Dark Key-To-Door, AMAGO adapts significantly faster than Algorithm Distillation [28], which converges at the speed of its single-task training data, because AMAGO directly optimizes return over the full horizon H. On Half-Cheetah Velocity, AMAGO matches or exceeds variBAD [64], BOReL [109], RL² [11], and the recurrent off-policy agent of Ni et al. [22] without requiring tuning for this easy task. On MazeRunner with randomly permuted action dynamics, AMAGO nearly recovers its standard performance while variBAD [64], HyperX [111], and RL² [11] all fail or show minimal progress. The naive shared-Transformer baseline (same architecture without AMAGO's architectural fixes and multi-gamma update) collapses in 9/39 POPGym environments, confirming that the specific technical contributions are essential rather than incidental.

## Relation to Prior Work

AMAGO sits at the intersection of in-context RL (RL², VariBAD) and off-policy actor-critic methods, building directly on Ni et al.'s demonstration that off-policy updates make recurrent in-context agents competitive. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) it replaces recurrent encoders with long-sequence Transformers trained end-to-end via a redesigned shared actor-critic update (using REDQ, PopArt, and a custom gradient-detachment scheme), and (2) it extends HER from single-goal to multi-step instruction relabeling, enabling goal-conditioned sparse-reward exploration. Unlike Algorithm Distillation and Decision Transformer approaches that reformulate RL as supervised sequence modeling, AMAGO optimizes the true RL objective, which the paper argues matters when the supervised proxy becomes misaligned with the actual goal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AMAGO provides the first stable, open-source recipe for training long-context Transformers with pure off-policy RL, removing the need to reformulate the problem as supervised learning and enabling context lengths up to 10,000 timesteps. The multi-goal hindsight relabeling scheme opens a new direction for in-context RL in open-world, procedurally generated environments (demonstrated on Crafter), where the agent discovers a 'frontier' exploration strategy by following mastered sub-goals toward undiscovered skills. Limitations include reliance on simplified Crafter observations (texture IDs rather than raw pixels in most experiments), the absence of a world-modeling component, and the fact that current benchmarks still do not fully stress-test the 10,000-step memory capacity. Open questions include whether the architectural stabilization tricks (Normformer + σ-Reparam + Leaky ReLU) generalize to even longer sequences or different sequence model families, and whether the multi-gamma update's benefits persist at larger scales.
