## Problem

Modern ConvNets with large kernels still fail to effectively encode expressive middle-order game-theoretic interactions, implicitly biasing toward extreme low-order (local texture) or high-order (global shape) patterns that are less discriminative. This representation bottleneck limits their generalizability and parameter efficiency despite increasing kernel sizes, as revealed by multi-order interaction analysis of existing architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241467/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

MogaNet accepts standard RGB images at 224×224 or 256×256 for classification, and processes multi-scale feature maps (H/4, H/8, H/16, H/32) through a 4-stage hierarchical backbone for dense prediction tasks. For video prediction, it consumes 10 input frames at 64×64 resolution and outputs 10 successive predicted frames. The architecture is a pure ConvNet (no self-attention), making it directly compatible with existing detection, segmentation, and pose estimation pipelines via standard neck modules.

## Method

MogaNet introduces two key modules within a 4-stage modern ConvNet framework. The Spatial Aggregation (SA) block combines a Feature Decomposition (FD) module—which adaptively re-weights the complementary spatial component (Y − GAP(Y)) to suppress trivial interactions—with a multi-order gated aggregation (Moga) module that runs three parallel depth-wise convolutions at dilation ratios {1, 2, 3} over channel-split features to capture low, middle, and high-order interactions simultaneously, then adaptively aggregates them via SiLU-activated gating. The Channel Aggregation (CA) block replaces the conventional large-ratio MLP with a lightweight channel-reducing projection that reallocates channel-wise features, achieving equivalent performance to r=8 MLP at only r=4 cost. Together, these modules force the network to encode the expressive middle-order interactions that are inherently overlooked, yielding 80.0% and 87.8% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K with 5.2M and 181M parameters respectively, while saving 59% FLOPs over ParC-Net and 17M parameters over ConvNeXt-L.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K at 224² resolution, MogaNet-S achieves 83.4% top-1 accuracy with 25M parameters and 5.0G FLOPs, outperforming ConvNeXt-T (82.1%) and Swin-T (81.3%) by 1.3 and 2.1 points respectively. At the large scale, MogaNet-XL reaches 87.8% top-1 at 384² (IN-21K pre-trained) with only 181M parameters, matching ConvNeXt-XL (87.8%) while saving 169M parameters. On COCO detection, Mask R-CNN with MogaNet-S achieves 46.7 AP^b, surpassing Swin-T (42.2) and ConvNeXt-T (44.2) with fewer parameters. On ADE20K segmentation, Semantic FPN with MogaNet-S reaches 47.7 mIoU, exceeding Swin-T (41.5) by 6.2 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MogaNet consistently outperforms same-scale ConvNets: MogaNet-S (83.4%) beats ConvNeXt-T (82.1%), SLaK-T (82.5%), and HorNet-T (82.8%) by 1.3–2.1 points; MogaNet-B (84.3%) surpasses ConvNeXt-S (83.1%) and SLaK-S (83.8%) by 1.2–1.4 points; MogaNet-L (84.7%) exceeds ConvNeXt-B (83.8%) and HorNet-B (84.3%) by 0.9–0.4 points. At the XL scale, MogaNet-XL matches ConvNeXt-XL at 87.8% top-1 while using 181M vs. 350M parameters (48% fewer) and 102G vs. 179G FLOPs. For lightweight models, MogaNet-T (79.0%, 5.2M params) outperforms ParC-Net-S (78.6%, 5.0M) with 2.04G fewer FLOPs, and MogaNet-XT (77.2%, 3.0M) surpasses T2T-ViT-7 (71.7%, 4.3M) by 4.6 points. On downstream tasks, MogaNet-S with Mask R-CNN achieves 46.7 AP^b on COCO, beating Swin-T (42.2) by 4.5 and ConvNeXt-T (44.2) by 2.5; on ADE20K, Semantic FPN with MogaNet-S reaches 47.7 mIoU vs. Swin-T's 41.5 (+6.2). MogaNet also leads in 2D pose estimation (MogaNet-B: 77.3 AP vs. Swin-L: 76.3 AP) and 3D face/hand reconstruction (MogaNet-S: 2.24mm 3DRMSE vs. ConvNeXt-T: 2.34mm).

## Relation to Prior Work

MogaNet is built directly on the modern ConvNet template established by ConvNeXt (Liu et al., 2022) and the MetaFormer SMixer/CMixer decomposition (Yu et al., 2022), adopting the same four-stage hierarchical layout with embedding stems and residual blocks. Its key departure is a principled design motivation drawn from multi-order game-theoretic interaction analysis (Deng et al., 2022; Cheng et al., 2021), which identifies that both ViTs and existing large-kernel ConvNets implicitly encode extreme-order interactions while suppressing the more discriminative middle-order ones. Rather than simply enlarging kernels or inserting self-attention, MogaNet addresses this bottleneck through a compact spatial aggregation block (parallel dilated DWConvs with gated aggregation) and a lightweight channel aggregation module, positioning itself as a pure-ConvNet alternative that matches or exceeds hybrid and Transformer-based backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The game-theoretic interaction lens offers a useful diagnostic for why certain architectural choices succeed or fail, potentially guiding future design beyond ad-hoc kernel-size scaling. The consistent parameter efficiency across six model scales and seven downstream tasks (e.g., 181M vs. 350M parameters at matched ImageNet accuracy) makes MogaNet a practical backbone for resource-constrained deployment. Open questions include whether the specific dilation ratios (d∈{1,2,3}) and channel split (1:3:4) are optimal or task-dependent, whether the game-theoretic framing generalizes to non-visual modalities, and whether the middle-order interaction hypothesis holds under distribution shift or adversarial conditions.
