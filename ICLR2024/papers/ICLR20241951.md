## Problem

Adversarial training is the standard defense for improving model robustness, and the verification phase is where practitioners confirm a model meets robustness thresholds before deployment. However, no prior work examined whether the verification phase itself could be deceived: an adversary could craft subtle input perturbations that make a substandard adversarially trained model exhibit a falsely high adversarial accuracy, misleading evaluators into deploying insecure models in safety-critical settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241951/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained adversarially robust DNN (e.g., ResNet-18, WideResNet-28-10), a clean verification image with its ground-truth label, and a perturbation budget ε (8/255 for ℓ∞ or 0.5 for ℓ2). It produces a single two-faced perturbed example x_tf = x + δ* where ‖δ*‖ ≤ ε, designed so that when standard adversarial verification (PGD, FAB, Square, etc.) applies its own ε-bounded perturbation t on top of x_tf, the model still predicts the correct label. The inflated adversarial accuracy score is then reported as if it were the model's true robustness.

## Method

The core formulation is a bi-level optimization (Eq. 4): the outer loop minimizes cross-entropy over the two-faced perturbation δ, while the inner loop maximizes cross-entropy over the adversarial verification perturbation t, both constrained to the same ε-ball. This is solved by alternating PGD steps (Algorithm 1), where each iteration first computes the worst-case verification perturbation t* given the current δ*, then updates δ* to minimize loss under t*. The paper further introduces a formal two-faced risk (Eq. 5) and proves Theorem 1, establishing that two-faced risk and adversarial risk exhibit contrasting trends—models with lower adversarial risk (i.e., higher robustness) are paradoxically more vulnerable to two-faced attacks. A simple adaptive countermeasure is proposed: training with an enlarged defense budget (e.g., 12/255–20/255 instead of 8/255) during PGD-AT, which reduces two-faced risk at a modest cost to clean accuracy.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, two-faced attacks inflate the adversarial accuracy of a PGD-AT-trained ResNet-18 from 51.72% (actual) to 82.81% (+31.1 pp), and a TRADES-trained model from 52.95% to 80.19% (+27.2 pp). The inflation is even more dramatic for THRM-trained models (23.87% → 75.54%, +51.7 pp). Across all four datasets (CIFAR-10, SVHN, CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet) and all three adversarial training methods, two-faced examples consistently yield the highest false robustness, exceeding both clean and hypocritical verification scores. The attack also transfers across architectures: two-faced examples generated by ResNet-18 raise WideResNet-28-10's apparent robustness from 53.91% to 74.89% on CIFAR-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the hypocritical attack (Tao et al., AAAI 2022), which inflates natural accuracy by making misclassified examples appear correct. Two-faced attacks consistently outperform hypocritical attacks in inflating adversarial robustness: on CIFAR-10 with PGD-AT, two-faced achieves 82.81% vs. hypocritical's 78.58% (+4.2 pp), and with TRADES, 80.19% vs. 77.58% (+2.6 pp). The gap widens substantially for THRM-trained models (75.54% vs. 40.09%, +35.5 pp), indicating that methods with lower inherent robustness are more susceptible to two-faced attacks. Against the ground-truth clean robustness, two-faced attacks inflate scores by 20–52 percentage points depending on the dataset and training method. Among the three adversarial training methods, THRM shows the lowest two-faced attack success rate (Figure 4), suggesting partial resilience, while PGD-AT and TRADES are equally vulnerable. The paper also demonstrates that the inflated robustness is not an artifact of a single verification method: the same two-faced examples raise scores under DI-FGSM, APGD-CE, FAB^T, and the black-box Square attack (Table 4).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of verification-time evasion attacks by introducing two-faced attacks as a robustness-focused counterpart to hypocritical attacks (Tao et al., 2022b), which target natural accuracy. While hypocritical examples make misclassified inputs appear correctly classified, two-faced examples craft perturbations that deceptively inflate adversarial accuracy scores, formulated as a bi-level optimization (Eq. 4) with no direct analogue in prior work. The paper also builds on adversarial training (PGD-AT, TRADES) as the defense paradigm under study and introduces a new theoretical quantity—two-faced risk—whose relationship to adversarial risk is established via Theorem 1, providing a formal lens absent from prior robustness evaluation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that models with higher adversarial robustness are paradoxically more vulnerable to two-faced attacks challenges the assumption that stronger adversarial training provides comprehensive protection against all verification-time threats. This has direct implications for safety-critical deployment pipelines where verification metrics gate model approval. However, the countermeasure (enlarging the training-time defense budget) only partially mitigates the issue, and the paper acknowledges two-faced risk remains non-negligible. Open questions include whether the threat persists under stronger threat models (e.g., unbounded or multi-step perturbations), whether it transfers to non-visual modalities, and whether a principled defense can fully eliminate the vulnerability without sacrificing standard robustness.
