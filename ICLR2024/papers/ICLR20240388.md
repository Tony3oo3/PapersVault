## Problem

In distributed stochastic optimization via token algorithms, the random walk driving gradient access is typically a standard Markov chain whose mixing properties directly limit convergence performance. While the Self-Repellent Random Walk (SRRW) was recently shown to reduce MCMC sampling variance at rate O(1/α), it remained unclear whether this benefit transfers to the optimization context and whether it could be amplified. This paper asks: can SRRW-driven token algorithms achieve near-zero asymptotic variance for the optimization iterates on any general graph?

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates on a graph G(N,E) where each node holds one local data point; the base Markov chain P (e.g., MHRW with uniform target μ) and the self-repellence parameter α define the SRRW kernel. Two polynomially decreasing step sizes β_n=(n+1)^{-b} and γ_n=(n+1)^{-a} govern the optimization iterate and the weighted empirical measure respectively, with their relative rates (a<b, a=b, a>b) defining three distinct timescale regimes. The primary theoretical output is the explicit asymptotic covariance matrix V_θ^(k)(α) for the iterate error θ_n−θ*, which is shown to decrease at O(1/α²) in the best case (i), surpassing the O(1/α) rate of the underlying SRRW sampling.

## Method

The SA-SRRW algorithm replaces the standard Markovian token with a nonlinear Markov chain whose transition kernel K[x] down-weights transitions to over-visited nodes via the factor (x_j/μ_j)^{-α}, making the walker 'self-repellent.' At each step, the token draws its next node from K[x_n], updates the weighted empirical measure x_n with step size γ_n, and updates the optimization iterate θ_n with step size β_n using local gradient information H(θ_n, X_{n+1}). The key technical contribution is proving a central limit theorem for the two-timescale SA with controlled Markov noise (cases i and iii), which had been an open problem; this is achieved by decomposing the Markovian noise into a martingale-difference term plus non-vanishing corrections via repeated Poisson equation applications. The resulting asymptotic covariance V_θ^(1)(α) = ∫₀^∞ e^{t(∇h(θ*)+½I)} U_θ(α) e^{t(∇h(θ*)+½I)^T} dt, where U_θ(α) contains the factor 1/(α(1+λ_i)+1)², yields the O(1/α²) decay rate — a squared amplification over the O(1/α) sampling variance reduction. For case (iii) where θ_n is on the faster timescale, the SRRW provides no asymptotic benefit (V_θ^(3)(α)=V_θ^(3)(0)), making case (i) the strictly superior algorithmic choice.

## Results

The paper's headline theoretical result is that the asymptotic covariance matrix V_θ^(1)(α) for the SA-SRRW algorithm in case (i) (β_n = o(γ_n)) decreases to zero at rate O(1/α²) as the self-repellence parameter α increases, which is a squared improvement over the O(1/α) rate for pure MCMC sampling. Empirically, on the wikiVote graph (889 nodes) with the ijcnn1 dataset, SGD-SRRW and SHB-SRRW with α ≥ 5 achieve lower MSE than i.i.d. sampling even under graph-constrained token traversal. On the smaller Dolphins graph (62 nodes), curve fitting at n = 10⁷ confirms the O(1/α²) MSE decay. Across all three step-size cases, case (i) (a = 0.8) consistently yields the lowest MSE for all tested α ∈ {1, 5, 10}.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SA-SRRW against three baselines: (1) the base Markov chain (α = 0, i.e., standard MHRW-driven token algorithm), (2) i.i.d. sampling (a 'random jumper' that can access any node with probability μ_j in one step), and (3) the other two step-size cases (ii) and (iii). SRRW with α ≥ 5 outperforms i.i.d. sampling on the wikiVote graph for both SGD-SRRW and SHB-SRRW, demonstrating that graph-constrained self-repellent walks can beat unconstrained random access. Case (i) (β_n = o(γ_n), a = 0.8) dominates cases (ii) (a = 0.9) and (iii) (a = 1) across all α values, consistent with the theoretical ordering V_θ^(1)(α) <_L V_θ^(3)(α) = V_θ^(3)(0). The base Markov chain (α = 0) is the worst performer, confirming that the self-repellent mechanism provides meaningful variance reduction. SHB-SRRW (stochastic heavy ball) shows similar ordering to SGD-SRRW but with slightly lower MSE due to momentum acceleration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Self-Repellent Random Walk (SRRW) framework from Doshi et al. (2023), which demonstrated O(1/α) asymptotic variance reduction for MCMC sampling on graphs, to the setting of distributed stochastic optimization via token algorithms. It builds on the efficiency-ordering methodology of Hu et al. (2022) for comparing SGD variants under different stochastic inputs, and generalizes the SRRW step-size analysis to weighted empirical measures with polynomially decreasing rates. The key conceptual departure is replacing the standard linear Markovian token (e.g., Metropolis-Hastings random walk) with a nonlinear Markov chain whose transition probabilities depend on the walker's own visit history, and proving a CLT for the resulting two-timescale SA with controlled Markov noise—a setting for which no prior CLT existed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding that the SRRW's variance-reduction benefit amplifies from O(1/α) in sampling to O(1/α²) in optimization is practically significant: it means that a drop-in replacement of the token's Markov chain with SRRW can yield near-zero asymptotic covariance for the optimization iterate without modifying the optimization iteration itself. The negative result that case (iii) (θ on the faster timescale) gains no asymptotic benefit from SRRW provides clear algorithmic guidance. Limitations include the requirement of a time-reversible base chain, local (not global) Lipschitz conditions that preclude standard finite-time analysis, and empirical validation on relatively small graphs (≤889 nodes) with a single binary classification task. Open questions include extending to non-reversible chains, non-convex objectives at scale, and deriving non-asymptotic bounds that capture the α-dependence.
