## Problem

Pretrained large models are widely available but too resource-intensive for edge and mobile deployment, and the smallest pretrained variants (e.g., ViT-Base at 80M parameters) are often still too large. This paper asks whether the knowledge encoded in a large pretrained model can be transferred to a smaller model purely through weight initialization, without requiring the large model at inference time or additional training compute.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240572/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained teacher model and a smaller student architecture from the same model family (e.g., ViT-S → ViT-T, ConvNeXt-T → ConvNeXt-F), where the student has reduced embedding dimensions and/or fewer layers. It produces a fully initialized weight tensor for the student by selecting subsets of elements from each corresponding teacher tensor. No extra computational cost is incurred at inference, and the student can be trained on any target dataset without access to the teacher's pretraining data.

## Method

Weight selection initializes a smaller model through three steps: (1) layer selection, where the first N layers of the teacher are chosen to match the student's depth; (2) component mapping, where each student layer's components (attention, MLP, normalization, embeddings) are matched one-to-one to the corresponding teacher components; and (3) element selection, where evenly-spaced slices are uniformly sampled from each dimension of the teacher's weight tensor to fill the student's smaller tensor. A critical finding is that consistency—selecting the same spatial positions across all weight tensors within a layer—is essential for performance, as it preserves complete neurons and residual connections. The method outperforms Xavier/Kaiming initialization by large margins (e.g., +9.1% on CIFAR-100 for ViT-T), reduces training time to reach a target accuracy by ~3×, and is compatible with knowledge distillation for further gains.

## Results

On 9 image classification datasets, weight selection consistently improves test accuracy over random initialization for both ViT-T and ConvNeXt-F. Gains are largest on small datasets: ViT-T improves by +43.6 pp on Pets (25.0→68.6), +21.9 pp on STL-10 (61.5→83.4), and +9.1 pp on CIFAR-100 (72.3→81.4), while ConvNeXt-F gains +14.4 pp on Pets (72.9→87.3) and +10.9 pp on STL-10 (81.4→92.3). On the large-scale ImageNet-1K, improvements are more modest (+1.6 pp for ViT-T, +0.3 pp for ConvNeXt-F). Weight selection also reduces training time by 3× to reach the same CIFAR-100 accuracy and is 6.12× faster than pretraining+finetuning on ImageNet-1K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Weight selection substantially outperforms both classic initialization methods on CIFAR-100: ViT-T reaches 81.4% vs. Xavier (72.1%) and Kaiming (73.0%), while ConvNeXt-F reaches 84.4% vs. Xavier (82.8%) and Kaiming (82.5%). Against knowledge distillation (Hinton et al.), weight selection alone already beats vanilla logit-based distillation on ImageNet-1K (75.5% vs. 74.8%) and feature-based distillation on CIFAR-100 (81.4% vs. 78.4%), and combining both yields the best results (76.0% and 83.9% respectively). Compared to pruning-based approaches, weight selection (81.4%/84.4%) clearly beats L1 pruning (79.5%/82.8%) and magnitude pruning (73.8%/81.9%) on CIFAR-100, as pruning breaks the consistency property critical to weight selection. Finally, weight selection dominates mimetic initialization (Trockman & Kolter, 2023) by large margins across all three datasets: 97.0% vs. 93.3% on CIFAR-10, 81.4% vs. 74.7% on CIFAR-100, and 83.4% vs. 67.5% on STL-10, confirming that directly utilizing pretrained parameters is far more effective than mimicking their statistical properties.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the abundance of pretrained models as a source of weight initialization for smaller models, departing from the classical Xavier/Kaiming paradigm designed for training from scratch. It is conceptually adjacent to DistilBERT-style layer selection (Sanh et al., Shleifer & Rush) but removes the requirement that the student share the teacher's width, and to mimetic initialization (Trockman & Kolter) but directly copies parameters rather than matching statistical properties. Unlike knowledge distillation or weight pruning, weight selection incurs no extra training or inference cost and requires no learnable projection layers, positioning it as a purely initialization-time operation that leverages the modular structure of modern architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The technique is immediately deployable in resource-constrained settings (edge devices, mobile) where even the smallest pretrained model (e.g., ViT-Base at 80M params) is too large, and it composes naturally with knowledge distillation for further gains. A key open question is whether the method extends beyond same-family initialization (e.g., ViT-to-ConvNeXt) or to LLMs, where the paper only speculates (LLaMA-7B from LLaMA-30B). The finding that a smaller teacher (ViT-S) outperforms a much larger one (ViT-L) suggests an optimal compression ratio that remains unexplored, and the method's reliance on architectural modularity may limit applicability to non-standard or heterogeneous designs.
