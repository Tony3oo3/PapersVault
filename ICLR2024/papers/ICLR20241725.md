## Problem

Retrieval-augmented language models are slow at inference because the reader must compute cross-attention between a query and dozens of retrieved passages, and existing precomputation approaches (e.g., DeFormer) that cache continuous passage representations require terabytes of storage for Wikipedia-scale corpora. The paper asks whether passage token representations can be compressed to 1-bit vectors without significant accuracy loss, enabling both fast inference and tractable storage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241725/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At training time, the method takes a text query and a set of retrieved passages (up to 40 passages of 320 tokens each) and produces task predictions (answer spans or classification labels). At inference time, the query is encoded on the fly in the lower encoder layers (layers 1–k), while precomputed 1-bit binary token vectors for the passages are looked up from a key-value store, converted back to continuous vectors, and concatenated with the query representations before the upper encoder layers (k+1 to n). The final output is a generated answer or classification label, with the binary representations themselves stored as 1-bit vectors requiring only 127 GB for 3 billion Wikipedia tokens.

## Method

BTR inserts calibrated binarization inside the Transformer encoder layer—after layernorm but before multihead attention—rather than after the full layer as in prior work, and saves per-token variance to recover original scales, avoiding the scale mismatch that causes performance drops in pre-layernorm architectures like T5. A straight-through estimator replaces annealing for differentiable binarization. Two training objectives mitigate accuracy loss: a passage representation recovery loss (MSE between a linear projection of binary vectors and the original continuous representations) and a query-aware passage token distillation loss that only distills the top-r query-salient passage tokens from the non-decomposed teacher model. Offline compression merges similar binary token representations across the corpus using bipartite merging with Hamming distance, while runtime compression merges similar query-passage representations in the upper encoder (intra-passage) and decoder (cross-passage) using cosine distance, reducing token counts by a configurable ratio without additional training.

## Results

On five knowledge-intensive tasks, BTR-Atlas base achieves 92–97% of the original Atlas base performance (e.g., 49.5 EM on NQ vs. 52.1, 66.7 EM on TQA vs. 69.3) while accelerating inference by 2.5–3.1×. BTR-Atlas large reaches 95–97% of Atlas large performance with 2.5–4.1× speedup (e.g., 56.1 EM on NQ vs. 58.3, 75.9 Acc on FEVER vs. 78.2). Storage is reduced by over 100× compared to DeFormer (127 GB vs. 12,804 GB for 3B Wikipedia tokens). The ablation shows binary representations alone cut storage by 100×, offline compression further reduces it by 20%, and runtime compression adds >30% throughput gain with <0.5% accuracy loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BTR-Atlas base outperforms DeFormer on speed (3.1× vs. 2.2× on NQ) while using 100× less storage (127 GB vs. 12,804 GB), at a small accuracy cost (49.5 vs. 51.4 EM on NQ). Compared to Atlas-Q (4-bit GPTQ quantization), BTR achieves 2.5–3.1× speedup versus only 1.1–1.3×, with comparable accuracy (95% vs. 99% relative). DensePhrase is the fastest baseline (4.9–5.8× speedup) but suffers a large accuracy drop (77–81% relative), making it unsuitable for tasks requiring high precision. LLaMA2-7B (LoRA-finetuned) achieves the highest absolute accuracy on TQA (74.3 EM, 108% relative) and MMLU (51.2 Acc, 133% relative) but is ~10× slower than Atlas base (0.1× speedup), highlighting the throughput-accuracy tradeoff. Overall, BTR occupies a favorable position: it matches DeFormer-level accuracy while being 100× more storage-efficient, and it is 2–3× faster than DeFormer with only a 1–2 EM accuracy gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

BTR builds directly on the DeFormer decomposition strategy (Cao et al., 2020), which splits passage and query encoding in the reader encoder to enable precomputation, but departs by replacing the cached continuous float vectors with 1-bit binary token representations. It also draws on the line of binary representation work for retrieval and semantic matching (Yamada et al., 2021; Shen et al., 2019), but extends it from coarse passage-level or image-level codes to fine-grained token-level representations within a reader model. The calibrated binarization technique (inserting binarization after layernorm rather than after the full encoder layer, combined with a straight-through estimator) is a novel architectural modification not present in prior binarization or quantization work, and the two training objectives (query-aware distillation and passage representation recovery) are new losses designed specifically to counteract the information loss from token-level binarization in a multi-passage retrieval setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100x storage reduction (127GB for 3B Wikipedia tokens vs. terabytes for continuous representations) and 2–4x inference speedup make retrieval-augmented language models far more deployable on commodity hardware, addressing a key bottleneck that limits real-world adoption. The finding that token-level 1-bit representations can retain >95% task accuracy challenges the assumption that fine-grained reader computation requires full-precision continuous vectors. Open questions remain around extending BTR to decoder-only readers (where sequential KV-cache dependencies complicate precomputation), handling very long queries where speedup diminishes, and scaling to larger representation dimensions. The technique is orthogonal to model quantization and could combine with 4-bit or 8-bit weight compression for further efficiency gains on resource-constrained platforms.
