## Problem

Text-conditioned video prediction (TVP) requires generating coherent future frames from a few reference frames and a task-level language instruction, but existing approaches suffer from three limitations: they demand large-scale labeled text-video datasets and heavy computation, produce low-fidelity frames that lose background and object detail from the reference, and rely on a single global text embedding that cannot provide fine-grained per-frame guidance for multi-step task-level motions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240143/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes 1–2 reference frames (256×256 RGB) and a short task-level text instruction (e.g., 'tipping a beer can') as input, and outputs 12 or 16 predicted future frames at 256×256 resolution in the latent space of a pretrained Stable Diffusion v1.5 VAE. Unlike text-to-video generation that describes scene content, the text here acts as a motion/task descriptor, and the reference frames anchor the spatial scene so the model must preserve appearance while executing the instructed motion. Sampling uses DDIM with 30 steps and a classifier-free guidance scale of 7.5.

## Method

Seer inflates a pretrained 2D Stable Diffusion U-Net along the temporal axis by expanding 2D convolution kernels to 3D (1×3×3) and inserting Scaled Autoregressive Window Temporal Attention (SAWT-Atten) layers that compute causal self-attention over local spatial windows (m×m) across the full temporal sequence, constraining information flow from future to past frames. A novel Frame Sequential Text (FSText) Decomposer—comprising bidirectional text-sequential attention, cross-attention for semantic inheritance from the global CLIP embedding, and directed temporal attention—splits one global instruction into n frame-specific sub-instruction embeddings, each injected into its corresponding frame via frame-individual cross-attention (fic-attn). All pretrained 2D spatial layers and the VAE are frozen; only the SAWT-Atten layers and the FSText Decomposer (initialized as an identity function of the CLIP embedding) are trained from scratch under the standard diffusion denoising objective, requiring roughly 480 GPU-hours on 4×RTX 3090s versus over 12,480 GPU-hours for CogVideo.

## Results

Seer achieves the best FVD and KVD across all three primary benchmarks: FVD 112.9 / KVD 0.12 on Something-Something V2 (a 31% FVD improvement over the previous SOTA VideoFusion at 163.2), FVD 246.3 / KVD 0.55 on BridgeData, and FVD 271.4 / KVD 1.40 on Epic-Kitchens-100. In human evaluation on text-conditioned video manipulation, Seer receives an 83.7% average preference rate. The method achieves these results with only ~480 GPU-hours of training (4× RTX 3090, 120 hours), compared to over 12,480 GPU-hours for CogVideo.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Seer outperforms all seven baselines on both FVD and KVD across SSv2, BridgeData, and Epic-Kitchens-100. The closest competitor is VideoFusion (FVD 163.2 on SSv2), which Seer improves by 31%; Tune-A-Video (FVD 291.4) and PVDM (FVD 502.4) trail further behind. On BridgeData and Epic100, the gap widens substantially—Seer nearly halves the FVD of the next-best method (VideoFusion at 501.2 and 349.9 respectively). In the human evaluation, Seer achieves >72% preference over every baseline, with ~90% preference over TATS and MCVD. On the UCF-101 class-conditioned benchmark (Appendix), Seer (FVD 260.7) outperforms CogVideo (626) and MagicVideo (699) but falls short of Make-A-Video (81.25), which benefits from unlabelled video pretraining. In zero-shot transfer to EGO4D, Seer (FVD 301.7) more than doubles the performance of VideoFusion (618.6).

## Relation to Prior Work

Seer extends the latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v1.5) along the temporal axis, drawing on I3D-style 3D convolutions and Swin Transformer's window attention to build a computation-efficient 3D U-Net. It departs from prior text-to-video methods (Make-A-Video, MagicVideo, Tune-A-Video) in two key ways: (1) replacing global bi-directional or directed temporal attention with a scaled autoregressive window temporal attention (SAWT-Atten) that respects causal frame ordering, and (2) introducing the FSText Decomposer, which splits a single global text instruction into temporally aligned per-frame sub-instructions via a transformer with sequential, cross, and directed temporal attention. Unlike CogVideo, which trains a large transformer from scratch on massive text-video data, Seer freezes nearly all pretrained 2D weights and trains only the lightweight temporal and text-decomposition layers on small datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 26× reduction in training compute (480 vs. 12,480 GPU-hours) relative to CogVideo while achieving 31% FVD improvement over the prior SOTA makes task-level video prediction accessible to smaller labs and more practical for robot policy learning. The FSText Decomposer's identity-initialization requirement (FVD 367.9→112.9) and the finding that over-fine-tuning cross-attention layers catastrophically degrades performance (FVD 112.9→1807) offer useful practical guidance for adapting pretrained diffusion models. Open questions include scaling to higher resolutions and longer sequences (16-frame videos already show appearance drift), handling scene transitions and camera motion (noted failure cases on Epic-Kitchens-100), and whether the robot policy learning gains observed in Meta-World transfer to real-world manipulation.
