## Problem

Existing methods for combining event cameras with spiking neural networks require converting asynchronous event streams into conventional frames or voxels, which introduces additional data mapping overhead, destroys the inherent sparsity of event data, and loses fine-grained temporal information critical for action recognition. This conversion step fundamentally contradicts the event-driven, sparse-processing design philosophy of both event cameras and SNNs, leaving a gap in how to process raw event data natively within an SNN framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241097/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw asynchronous event streams from DAVIS event cameras (resolutions ranging from 128×128 to 346×260), where each event is a 4-tuple (x, y, t, polarity). Events are clipped into sliding windows (0.5–1.5 s with 0.25–0.5 s overlap), randomly sampled to a fixed 1024 points, and converted into a 3D pseudo-point cloud by normalizing spatial coordinates to [0,1] and mapping timestamp to a z-axis. The output is a single action class label produced by a voting layer over 16 timesteps, evaluated on five datasets spanning 10 to 101 action categories.

## Method

SpikePoint processes event data as a point cloud through a singular-stage SNN architecture (deliberately avoiding the multi-stage hierarchical design of ANN point cloud networks, where spike sparsity and gradient vanishing degrade with depth). Events are grouped via Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) for centroids and KNN for members, then standardized to yield relative positions [Δx, Δy, Δz]; because SNN rate coding cannot handle negative values, the method takes absolute values and compensates by substituting [x_min, y_min, z_min] for the centroid in a parallel branch, fusing the two branches via addition. The local feature extractor uses ResFB units (1D convolutions with bottleneck) on both branches, while the global feature extractor uses ResF units to capture inter-group relationships, all built on LIF neurons with a modified identity-mapping residual that cancels the vanishing surrogate-gradient coefficient during backpropagation. Training uses a stateless Poisson rate encoder with 16 timesteps, arctan surrogate gradients, and the spikingjelly framework, achieving SOTA on five benchmarks with as few as 0.16M parameters.

## Results

SpikePoint achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on five event-based action recognition datasets using only 16 timesteps: 98.74% on DVS128 Gesture (10-class), 97.92% on Daily DVS, 90.6% on DVS Action, 55.6% on HMDB51-DVS, and 68.46% on UCF101-DVS. On Daily DVS, DVS Action, and HMDB51-DVS, SpikePoint surpasses all competing ANN and SNN methods. On DVS128 Gesture and UCF101-DVS, it achieves SOTA among SNNs while approaching or matching the best ANN results, using only 0.16M–1.05M parameters (approximately 0.3% of comparable ANN parameters) and 0.5% of ANN power consumption.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DVS128 Gesture, SpikePoint (98.74%) outperforms all SNN baselines by at least 0.84% (vs. SEW-ResNet at 97.9%) and beats lightweight ANNs VMV-GCN (97.5%) and EV-VGCNN (95.7%), but trails the heavy TBR+I3D ANN (99.6%, 12.25M params) by 0.86%. On Daily DVS, SpikePoint (97.92%) achieves SOTA across all methods, surpassing TANet (96.5%), I3D (96.2%), and the best SNN Motion-based SNN (90.3%) by 7.6%. On DVS Action, SpikePoint (90.6%) leads ST-EVNet (88.7%), PointNet (75.1%), and Motion-based SNN (78.1%) by 1.9–15.5%. On HMDB51-DVS, SpikePoint (55.6%) surpasses RG-CNN (51.5%), I3D (46.6%), and ResNet-34 (43.8%) by 4.1–11.8%. On UCF101-DVS, SpikePoint (68.46%) dominates all SNN baselines (RM-RES-SNN-18 at 58.5%, Res-SNN-18 at 57.8%) by 10+ points and beats I3D (63.5%) and RG-CNN+Incep.3D (63.2%), though it falls 1.74% short of the ANN SOTA ECSNet-SES (70.2%). Across all datasets, SpikePoint uses 0.16M–1.05M parameters, roughly 0.3% of comparable ANN parameter counts, and 0.5% of their power consumption.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpikePoint sits at the intersection of two well-studied lines of work: point cloud networks (PointNet, PointNet++) and spiking neural networks for event-based vision. It departs from prior event-based SNNs (Liu et al., 2021a; Yao et al., 2023) that convert asynchronous events into frames or address-event representations, instead treating raw events directly as a 3D space-time point cloud to preserve sparsity and fine-grained temporal structure. Architecturally, it diverges from the multi-stage hierarchical design standard in PointNet++ by proposing a singular-stage SNN with identity-mapping residual connections (building on Hu et al., 2021 and Fang et al., 2021a) to avoid the gradient vanishing that plagues deep SNNs trained via BPTT. The novel absolute-value encoding with min-coordinate compensation for handling negative relative positions in rate-coded SNN inputs is a practical contribution not addressed in prior point-cloud or SNN literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic efficiency gap—matching or exceeding ANN accuracy with ~0.3% of the parameters and ~0.5% of the power consumption across five diverse datasets—makes this a practically important result for ultra-low-power edge deployment of event-based vision. The singular-stage design validates that shallow SNNs can effectively extract both local and global features from point clouds, challenging the assumption that hierarchical depth is necessary. Open questions remain: background noise in datasets like HMDB51-DVS still limits accuracy, the sliding window length is manually tuned per dataset, and the approach has not yet been extended to other event-based tasks (SLAM, multimodal fusion) that the authors identify as future directions.
