## Problem

Standard self-attention in transformers suffers from quadratic time and memory complexity in sequence length, and existing linearized attention (LA) methods consistently underperform compared to softmax attention. The root cause of this performance gap was not well understood: prior LA work selected feature embedding functions (ELU, ReLU, trigonometric) without analyzing the statistical and concentration properties that make softmax attention effective. This paper asks: what distributional and concentration characteristics of the attention matrix must an LA method replicate to match softmax attention performance?

## Input / Output

The method operates on sequences of N tokens with d-dimensional query, key, and value vectors (text for RoBERTa on GLUE/WikiText-103, or image patches for ViT on Dogs vs Cats) and produces standard classification or language-modeling outputs. The key architectural constraint is that the LLN attention computes a running sum over keys and values in O(N) memory rather than materializing the full N×N attention matrix, while the block-diagonal component applies standard softmax attention within fixed-size local blocks. The hyperparameters α and β are set via a moment-matching procedure that injects Gaussian inputs and linearly interpolates to equate the LLN and SA attention variances.

## Method

The paper first proves that softmax attention matrix entries follow a log-normal distribution (via Fenton's theorem on sums of log-normals) and characterizes attention concentration through two metrics: entropy (biased, monotonically increasing with temperature) and spectral gap (unbiased, tied to the second eigenvalue of the stochastic attention matrix). Building on this, LLN Attention uses exponential feature embeddings Φ_Q(q)=e^(αq) and Φ_K(k)=e^(βk) so that the resulting attention matrix is also log-normal, then performs moment matching to align its variance with that of softmax attention by solving for α and β. A block-diagonal softmax attention is added in parallel and averaged with the LLN output to capture short-range interactions that pure linear attention dilutes. The combined LLN+Diag layer achieves linear O(N) complexity while matching softmax attention's entropy and spectral gap curves during training, yielding competitive GLUE accuracy (86.9% average) and handling sequences up to 16K tokens where standard attention runs out of memory.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark (MNLI, QNLI, QQP, SST-2), LLN+Diag Attention achieves 86.9% average accuracy, matching the Softmax Attention baseline (87.0%) and outperforming Nyströmformer (86.8%), Flash (86.52%), and T1 (86.46%). On the Long Range Arena benchmark, LLN+Diag attains 57.86% average score, slightly exceeding Softmax Attention (57.38%) while using substantially less memory and time. On the Dogs vs Cats image classification task, LLN+Diag reaches 81.72% accuracy, surpassing both Softmax (81.37%) and Linformer (79.89%). In terms of efficiency, LLN+Diag handles sequences of length 16,384 (4× longer than SA's OOM limit at 8,192) with 23 GB memory and 13.3 sec/iteration, faster than Nyströmformer at every tested length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLN+Diag Attention (86.9% avg) essentially matches the Softmax Attention baseline (87.0%) on GLUE, losing by only 0.1 percentage points, while outperforming all other linearized attention methods tested. It beats Nyströmformer (86.8%) by 0.1%, Flash (86.52%) by 0.38%, T1 (86.46%) by 0.44%, CosFormer (85.6%) by 1.3%, and T2 (85.48%) by 1.42%. The gap widens substantially against older or less refined methods: Longformer (83.7%, −3.2%), ELU/Linear Transformer (82.8%, −4.1%), Performer (70.6%, −16.3%), and Reformer (49.8%, −37.1%). On the LRA benchmark, LLN+Diag (57.86%) actually surpasses Softmax Attention (57.38%) and all linear baselines (Reformer 57.67%, Skyformer 56.93%, Performer 55.26%), while requiring the least memory (1,641–3,276 MB vs. 17,108 MB for SA on Text-4k). On Dogs vs Cats image classification, LLN+Diag (81.72%) edges out Softmax (81.37%) and clearly beats Linformer (79.89%). In speed and memory, LLN+Diag is consistently faster than Nyströmformer at every sequence length (e.g., 13.3 vs. 16.7 sec/it at 16,384) and handles sequences 4× longer than SA before OOM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Linearized Attention framework (Katharopoulos et al., 2020), where softmax is decomposed into a product of feature embedding functions, by providing a principled criterion for choosing those functions: the induced attention matrix should be log-normal with matching moments to softmax attention. It also incorporates the hybrid block-diagonal + linear attention architecture from Qin et al. (2022a) to address short-range dilution. Unlike prior LA methods (Performer, Linear Transformers, RFA) that selected kernels (exponential, ELU, trigonometric) without analyzing the resulting attention distribution, this work derives the kernel choice from a formal characterization of softmax attention's statistical and spectral properties.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The log-normal characterization of the attention matrix and the introduction of spectral gap as an 'unbiased concentration' metric (complementing entropy as 'biased concentration') provide a new analytical lens for understanding and designing attention mechanisms. The moment-matching procedure offers a practical recipe for calibrating LA kernels, though it requires empirical estimation of constants a and b. Limitations include evaluation only at RoBERTa-base scale, the need for a block-diagonal component to match SA performance, and the assumption that queries/keys are approximately Gaussian. Open questions include whether the log-normal approximation holds in deeper or larger models, whether the framework extends to multi-head attention without per-head calibration, and whether the approach scales to the 100B+ parameter regime where LA methods are most needed.
