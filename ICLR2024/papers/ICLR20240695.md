## Problem

Supervised local learning methods, which train each layer independently with gradient-isolated auxiliary networks, suffer from a large accuracy gap compared to backpropagation, particularly for deep networks with many independently optimized layers. This gap arises because hidden layers only optimize for their local objectives without any feedback from subsequent layers, leading to short-sighted feature representations that fail to support downstream layers. The paper addresses this scalability bottleneck by strengthening the structural coupling between local layers and their downstream counterparts.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/SVHN, 96×96 for STL-10, 224×224 for ImageNet) and produces class labels, operating within conventional CNN architectures (ResNets, VGGs, MobileNet, EfficientNet, RegNet). A key operational detail is that during training, each hidden layer is paired with a gradient-isolated auxiliary network that maps its hidden representation to the output space, but all auxiliary networks are discarded at inference time, so the deployed model is architecturally identical to a BP-trained network. The method also supports downstream tasks like object detection and instance segmentation via Mask R-CNN with ImageNet-pretrained backbones.

## Method

AugLocal constructs each hidden layer's auxiliary network by uniformly sampling a small subset of layers from its subsequent layers in the primary network (using the formula β_i = ℓ + ⌊(L−ℓ)i/(d^ℓ−1)⌋), rather than relying on handcrafted auxiliary architectures. This structural prior allows the auxiliary network to capture essential transformations of downstream layers, guiding the hidden layer to produce representations that resemble those learned by BP. To control computational cost, a pyramidal depth schedule linearly reduces auxiliary network depth from a maximum d at the bottom to a minimum d_min near the output (governed by decay factor τ), reducing auxiliary FLOPs by ~45% with only ~0.5% accuracy loss. The local loss is standard cross-entropy, and the stop-gradient operator prevents gradient flow between adjacent primary layers, preserving the gradient-isolation property that enables parallel training and reduced memory usage (~40% less GPU memory than BP).

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-110 (55 local layers), AugLocal (d=6) achieves 93.96% top-1 accuracy, approaching BP's 94.61% while all other local learning methods lag by 7–20 points. On ImageNet, AugLocal reaches 73.95% top-1 on ResNet-34 (vs. BP 74.28%) and 76.70% on ResNet-101 (vs. BP 77.34%), demonstrating scalability to tens of independently trained layers. AugLocal also reduces GPU memory by ~40% compared to BP (e.g., 1.72 GB vs. 9.27 GB on ResNet-110/CIFAR-10) and achieves a 45% FLOPs reduction in auxiliary networks via the pyramidal depth strategy with only ~0.5% accuracy loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AugLocal decisively outperforms all three supervised local learning baselines across every dataset and network depth. On ResNet-110/CIFAR-10, AugLocal (d=6) reaches 93.96%, surpassing DGL (85.69%) by 8.3 points, InfoPro (86.95%) by 7.0 points, and PredSim (74.95%) by 19.0 points. Against BP, AugLocal narrows the gap to within 0.65 points on ResNet-110/CIFAR-10 and 0.33 points on ResNet-34/ImageNet, effectively matching end-to-end training accuracy. Even at the minimal auxiliary depth (d=2, one hidden layer + classifier), AugLocal already exceeds DGL and InfoPro by 4–5 points on ResNet-110, indicating that the uniform layer-sampling strategy is more effective than the handcrafted auxiliary networks used by prior methods. On ImageNet, AugLocal achieves 73.95% top-1 on ResNet-34 (vs. BP 74.28%) and 76.70% on ResNet-101 (vs. BP 77.34%), confirming that the accuracy gap to BP remains small even at 34 independently trained layers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the supervised local learning line (DGL, PredSim, InfoPro) where each hidden layer is trained independently with a gradient-isolated auxiliary network. The key departure is replacing handcrafted auxiliary network designs with a principled construction rule: uniformly sampling a small subset of the primary network's subsequent layers to form each auxiliary network, thereby injecting structural information about downstream transformations. The pyramidal depth schedule, which linearly shrinks auxiliary network depth toward the output, is a novel efficiency mechanism not present in prior local learning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AugLocal closes the long-standing accuracy gap between local learning and BP for large-scale networks (up to 55 independently trained layers) while reducing GPU memory by ~40%, making local learning viable on resource-constrained platforms. However, the wall-clock overhead (87–215% relative to BP for d=2–6) means the method is not yet faster in practice without a dedicated parallel implementation, which the authors acknowledge as future work. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-CNN architectures (transformers, diffusion models) and whether gradually freezing auxiliary layers can further mitigate residual update locking within the auxiliary networks themselves.
