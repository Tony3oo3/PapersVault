## Problem

Distributed deep learning training is vulnerable to Byzantine worker failures and dependent noise introduced by data augmentation (e.g., adversarial training, nonlinear transforms), yet existing robust aggregators rely on pairwise distance thresholding with discrete hyperparameters that are suboptimal under correlated or nonlinear noise and hinder convergence. The paper asks whether gradient aggregation can be cast as a principled optimization problem over subspaces that simultaneously handles both failure modes without hand-tuned neighbor counts.

## Input / Output

At each iteration the parameter server receives a gradient matrix G ∈ R^{n×p} (or R^{n×pk} when workers send multiple gradients) from p workers, along with a regularization parameter λ and subspace dimension m = ⌈(p+1)/2⌉. The server computes an orthogonal subspace estimate Ŷ ∈ R^{n×m} via a few IRLS/SVD iterations and outputs a single aggregated gradient direction d_t = (1/p)ŶŶᵀG·1, which is broadcast to all workers for a synchronous SGD update. The orthogonality constraint YᵀY = I is essential: it makes the reconstruction ŶŶᵀG a closed-form projection, avoiding expensive eigendecomposition at every step.

## Method

FA formulates aggregation as Maximum Likelihood Estimation over an orthogonal subspace Y, where each worker's 'value' is its reconstruction ratio v_i = tr(Yᵀg_i g_iᵀY)/‖g_i‖² ∈ [0,1] modeled with a Beta(1, ½) density. A Taylor expansion of the log-likelihood with a large constant a converts the intractable log-optimization into a smooth ℓ_a-norm objective (equation 5), which is then solved by an IRLS procedure whose inner step is a truncated SVD of the Cholesky factor of GDGᵀ + λI. A data-dependent pairwise regularization term (inspired by AggregaThor) allows λ to interpolate between pure subspace estimation and distance-based filtering, unifying Bulyan/Multi-Krum-style behavior. Convergence to second-order stationary points is guaranteed via a smooth SDP relaxation on the Flag Manifold, leveraging results from Bhojanapalli et al. (2018) on factored low-rank SDPs, with the 'Flag' structure providing nested subspace dimensions for finer gradient decomposition.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18 (p=15, batch size 128), FA achieves approximately 20% higher top-1 accuracy than the best baseline across f=1,2,3 Byzantine workers sending uniformly random gradients. On Tiny ImageNet with ResNet-50 (batch size 192), FA converges to higher accuracy in roughly 2× fewer iterations than all competing aggregators. Under 10% packet-loss communication (f=3), FA reaches a given accuracy threshold in fewer total training iterations than any baseline, demonstrating superior time-to-accuracy. Scaling to p=60 workers with up to f=14 Byzantine nodes on MNIST confirms feasibility in large-scale settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FA outperforms all seven primary baselines (Trimmed Mean, Median, MeaMed, Phocas, Multi-Krum, Bulyan) on CIFAR-10 and Tiny ImageNet across every tested noise level (f=1 to f=3). Trimmed Mean suffers catastrophic accuracy degradation even with a single Byzantine worker, while FA maintains stable convergence. Against Multi-Krum and Bulyan, FA achieves higher final accuracy, and the data-dependent regularization parameter λ allows FA to interpolate toward their behavior (verified via cosine similarity of aggregated gradients in Figure 10). In the supplement, FA also outperforms RESAM (Farhadkhani et al., 2022) and CGE (Gupta et al., 2021) under Fall of Empires, sign-flipping, and augmented-data attacks (Figure 13), while requiring no extra worker-side computation for gradient momentums. A PCA baseline (top-m principal components of the gradient matrix) is outperformed by FA's iteratively reweighted variant, confirming that the IRLS refinement over plain SVD is essential for robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Flag Median framework of Mankovich et al. (2022) by revealing it as a Maximum Likelihood Estimator under a Beta(1, 1/2) model on explained-variance ratios, then adding regularization and an IRLS/SVD solver grounded in low-rank matrix recovery theory (Fornasier et al., 2011) and smoothed SDP convergence results (Bhojanapalli et al., 2018). It departs from the dominant pairwise-distance and neighborhood-thresholding paradigm used by Krum, Bulyan, Multi-Krum, and related aggregators, instead formulating aggregation as a continuous subspace estimation problem solved via iteratively reweighted least squares. The paper also connects its left-multiplication update rule to Bulyan's right-multiplication selection matrix through Lagrange multiplier analysis, unifying the two perspectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~20% accuracy gain over state-of-the-art Byzantine-resilient aggregators across CIFAR-10, MNIST, and Tiny ImageNet, combined with robustness to nonlinear data augmentation (Lotka-Volterra, Arnold's Cat Map) and communication loss, positions FA as a practical drop-in replacement for existing aggregators in parameter-server architectures. However, the per-iteration SVD cost (O(n·N_δ·p²)) is a significant practical limitation that the authors acknowledge, and the paper leaves provable worst-case robustness guarantees as future work. Open questions include whether FA can be offloaded to programmable network switches, how it behaves in federated learning settings, and whether the regularization parameter λ can be tuned adaptively rather than fixed.
