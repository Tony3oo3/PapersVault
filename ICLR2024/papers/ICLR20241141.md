## Problem

Existing objectives for approximating the Laplacian representation (GDO and GGDO) suffer from three critical flaws: they depend on hyperparameters that cannot be tuned without already knowing the true eigenvectors, they converge to arbitrary rotations of the desired eigenvectors under gradient descent, and they cannot recover the corresponding eigenvalues. This makes it impossible to guarantee accurate Laplacian representation learning in environments where the eigensystem is unknown a priori.

## Input / Output

The method consumes transition batches (state, action, next state) sampled from a fixed policy, with state inputs provided as either (x,y) grid coordinates or pixel-based representations (each grid tile as a pixel, ranging from 7×9×3 to 41×41×3). A fully-connected network (3 layers × 256 units) or a convolutional variant maps these inputs to a d-dimensional vector of eigenvector components (d=11 in experiments). Additionally, the dual variables of the optimization objective directly yield the d corresponding Laplacian eigenvalues, which prior methods could not estimate.

## Method

The paper introduces ALLO (Augmented Lagrangian Laplacian Objective), a max-min optimization objective that combines three key mechanisms: (1) stop-gradient operators applied to the orthonormality constraints, which create an asymmetric descent direction so that each eigenvector u_i only adjusts to satisfy constraints with previously learned vectors, breaking the rotational symmetry that makes all rotations equilibrium points of GDO/GGDO; (2) dual variables (augmented Lagrangian multipliers) that penalize the linear constraints and whose optimal values are proportional to the Laplacian eigenvalues, enabling simultaneous eigenvector and eigenvalue recovery; and (3) a monotonically increasing barrier coefficient for the quadratic penalty term. Theoretical analysis proves that under gradient ascent-descent dynamics, the only stable equilibrium is the identity-ordered set of d smallest eigenvectors with their eigenvalues, provided the barrier coefficient exceeds a finite threshold (b > 2 suffices), eliminating the need for environment-specific hyperparameter tuning.

## Results

ALLO achieves near-perfect average cosine similarity (≥0.99) with the true Laplacian eigenvectors in 10 out of 12 grid environments, while GGDO fails catastrophically in the two smallest environments (GridMaze-7: 0.30, GridMaze-9: 0.38) and degrades significantly in the two largest (GridMaze-32: 0.80, GridRoom-64: 0.76). ALLO is statistically significantly better (p < 0.01) in 9 of 12 environments. For eigenvalue estimation, ALLO's dual variables yield consistently lower relative error than the Monte Carlo-based method of Wang et al. (2023) across all 12 environments and all eigenvalues in the spectrum.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is GGDO (Wang et al., 2021), which introduces a sequence of decreasing hyperparameters to break eigenvector rotation symmetry. ALLO decisively outperforms GGDO on eigenvector cosine similarity: GGDO collapses to near-random similarity (0.30–0.38) in the two smallest environments (GridMaze-7, GridMaze-9) and degrades to 0.76–0.80 in the two largest (GridRoom-64, GridMaze-32), while ALLO maintains ≥0.99 in all but one environment. The advantage is most dramatic where GGDO's hyperparameters are hardest to tune, confirming the paper's central claim that ALLO eliminates the untunable-hyperparameter dependence. The second baseline is the eigenvalue estimation method of Wang et al. (2023), which uses GGDO eigenvectors combined with Monte Carlo approximations. ALLO's dual variables provide lower relative eigenvalue error across all 12 environments and all eigenvalues in the spectrum (Figure 4B), with the gap widening for larger eigenvalues where GGDO's eigenvector inaccuracy compounds. The only environment where ALLO does not significantly outperform GGDO is GridRoomSym-4 (p=0.281), where both methods struggle due to the symmetric topology.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on approximating Laplacian representations in RL, building directly on the Graph Drawing Objective (GDO) of Wu et al. (2019) and the Generalized Graph Drawing Objective (GGDO) of Wang et al. (2021). It departs from both by replacing the pure quadratic penalty with an augmented Lagrangian formulation and introducing stop-gradient operators to break eigenvector rotation symmetry, yielding the ALLO min-max objective. A key conceptual departure is the demonstration (Corollary 1) that GGDO's unconstrained equilibrium points are actually rotations of the eigenvectors rather than the eigenvectors themselves, despite the constrained problem having a unique solution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Laplacian representation underpins several important RL capabilities—option discovery, reward shaping, and exploration in sparse-reward settings—so a reliable, hyperparameter-free method for recovering it has broad downstream impact. The finding that GGDO, the standard baseline, systematically converges to incorrect rotations is a significant correction for the field. Open questions include how the recovered eigenvalues can be leveraged (e.g., as emphasis vectors or proxies for option duration), whether the barrier coefficient's theoretical lower bound translates to practical guidance, and whether ALLO's advantages persist in continuous or high-dimensional state spaces beyond the grid environments tested here.
