## Problem

Existing theoretical analyses of Transformer training dynamics are limited to single-layer models with strong simplifying assumptions such as fixed MLP weights, linear activations, or local gradient steps at initialization. A unified mathematical framework that characterizes how self-attention and MLP layers jointly evolve during training in multilayer Transformers—particularly how attention sparsity changes over time and how hierarchical features are learned across layers—remains an open problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241427/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework operates on a single Transformer layer's parameters: attention logits z_m ∈ R^{M_C} for query token m, lower MLP weight vectors w_k ∈ R^d for K hidden nodes, and input token frequency vectors x ∈ R^{M_C}. It assumes orthonormal token embeddings (verified empirically in Pythia, LLaMA, and other pre-trained models) and stationary backpropagated gradients across layers. The output is a closed-form invariant linking z_m(t) to the MLP weights v_k(t) = U_C^T w_k(t), from which attention sparsity trajectories, rank dynamics of the MLP projection matrix, and hierarchical feature learning patterns are derived.

## Method

JoMA derives a first integral (Theorem 1) showing that the self-attention logits z_m(t) are a deterministic function of the lower MLP layer weights v_k(t) = U_C^T w_k(t), effectively integrating out the attention layer into a modified MLP-only dynamics. For exp attention, the invariant is z_m(t) = ½Σ_k v_k²(t) + c; for softmax attention, an additional term involving the mean attention vector b̄_m appears. With linear MLP activations, this recovers a winner-take-all dynamic where attention concentrates on the most co-occurring token (consistent with prior Scan&Snap results). With nonlinear activations (e.g., ReLU), the exp(v²/2) modulation from self-attention causes salient components (tokens with high co-occurrence frequency) to converge exponentially faster than non-salient ones, producing a characteristic sparse-then-dense attention trajectory. In multilayer settings with a hierarchical binary latent tree generative model, this dynamics explains how lower layers first bind strongly co-occurring tokens, forming latent features that then serve as inputs to higher layers' attention, thereby implicitly learning the data's hierarchical structure.

## Results

The paper's primary quantitative result is Table 1, which validates the theoretical prediction that MLP hidden nodes learn to represent latent variables in a hierarchical generative model. Normalized correlation between latents and their best-matched hidden nodes reaches 0.99–1.00 at the lowest layer (s=0) and 0.55–0.81 at the next layer (s=1), across various HBLT configurations (C=20–50 classes, Nch=2–3 children). Empirically, training from scratch on Wikitext2/Wikitext103 with lr=1e-4 yields the best validation losses (5.21 and 5.17 respectively), and the resulting attention sparsity pattern (drop-then-bounce) matches the theoretical prediction of Theorem 4. Pre-trained OPT-2.7B and Pythia models show consistent stable-rank dynamics in top layers, further supporting the hierarchical learning mechanism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not propose a new architecture or training method to benchmark against competing approaches; instead, it provides a theoretical framework (JoMA) whose predictions are validated against prior work and empirical observations. For linear MLP activations, JoMA exactly recovers the Scan&Snap result (Tian et al., 2023) that attention becomes sparse over training, confirming consistency with the existing 1-layer analysis. The key new finding is that with nonlinear activations, attention first becomes sparse (learning salient tokens) and then becomes dense (learning less salient tokens), a behavior absent from Scan&Snap which lacks residual connections and nonlinearity. Empirically, the predicted drop-then-bounce attention entropy pattern is observed in models trained from scratch on Wikitext2/Wikitext103, and the learning rate that produces this pattern (lr=1e-4) also achieves the best validation loss (5.21/5.17), while larger learning rates (lr=0.01) produce overly sparse attention and worse performance (6.76). In pre-trained OPT-2.7B and Pythia models, stable rank of the MLP lower layer shows the predicted drop-and-bounce structure in top layers, with bottom layers showing suppressed dynamics due to hierarchical learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Scan&Snap (Tian et al., 2023), which characterized 1-layer Transformer training dynamics under linear activations and without residual connections. JoMA generalizes this by incorporating residual connections, nonlinear MLP activations, and joint (rather than sequential) training of attention and MLP weights, yielding a closed-form invariant that links attention logits to MLP lower-layer weights. The multilayer hierarchical learning analysis builds on the hierarchical binary latent tree model from Tian et al. (2020) and connects to the broader literature on training dynamics of attention-based models (Tarzanagh et al., 2023; Li et al., 2023c; Oymak et al., 2023), which had not previously addressed the interaction between MLP nonlinearity and attention sparsification across layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first unified mathematical account of how multilayer Transformers learn hierarchical feature representations, predicting that attention first sparsifies (capturing salient co-occurring tokens) and then densifies (capturing less salient ones) under nonlinear activations—a non-monotonic behavior absent from prior linear-activation analyses. Key limitations include the orthonormal embedding assumption (though verified as approximately valid in practice), the stationary backpropagated gradient assumption, and the fact that attention logits are treated as independent parameters rather than being parameterized through the embedding matrix. Open questions include extending the analysis to near-orthogonal embeddings, jointly trained embeddings, and making the hierarchical learning explanation quantitative rather than qualitative.
