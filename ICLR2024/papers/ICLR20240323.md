## Problem

Standard diffusion models assume the prior is random noise, making them ill-suited for image-to-image translation where the input is a real image from a data distribution. Existing workarounds—classifier guidance, projected sampling, and Schrödinger bridge methods relying on iterative proportional fitting—are either not theoretically principled, lose cycle consistency, or are computationally expensive. The paper seeks a scalable, principled framework that directly learns transport between two arbitrary paired distributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240323/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the model consumes paired images (x₀, x_T) drawn from a joint data distribution, such as edge maps paired with handbag photos (64×64) or normal maps paired with RGB outdoor scenes (256×256). At inference, a single source image (e.g., an edge map or normal map) is provided as the fixed endpoint x_T = y, and the model generates the corresponding target image by reversing the bridge process. When one endpoint is set to Gaussian noise, the framework reduces to standard unconditional image generation on CIFAR-10 (32×32) and FFHQ (64×64).

## Method

DDBM constructs a diffusion bridge between two arbitrary endpoints using Doob's h-transform, yielding a tractable Gaussian conditional distribution q(x_t | x₀, x_T) whose mean is a linear interpolation between the (scaled) endpoints. The model learns the score ∇log q(x_t | x_T) via a denoising bridge score-matching loss that matches against the closed-form conditional score of this Gaussian bridge, requiring no iterative approximation. A generalized EDM-style pred-x parameterization with derived scaling functions (c_in, c_out, c_skip) accounts for both endpoint variances and their covariance, reducing to standard EDM when one endpoint is noise. Sampling uses a hybrid higher-order ODE + Euler-Maruyama scheme that injects stochasticity between Heun steps to avoid the blurriness of purely deterministic ODE paths, and a guidance parameter w controls the strength of the h-transform drift adjustment.

## Results

On Edges→Handbags (64×64), DDBM(VP) achieves FID 1.83 and LPIPS 0.142, substantially outperforming the best baseline I2SB (FID 7.43, LPIPS 0.244). On DIODE (256×256), DDBM(VP) reaches FID 4.43 and LPIPS 0.244, again beating I2SB (FID 9.34, LPIPS 0.373). For unconditional generation, DDBM matches EDM with FID 2.06 on CIFAR-10 (vs. EDM's 2.04) and slightly improves on FFHQ-64×64 (FID 2.44 vs. 2.53) at the same NFE budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDBM dominates all image-translation baselines on both datasets. Against the closest competitor I2SB, DDBM(VP) improves FID from 7.43 to 1.83 on Edges→Handbags (4× reduction) and from 9.34 to 4.43 on DIODE, while also halving LPIPS (0.244→0.142 and 0.373→0.244) and reducing MSE by an order of magnitude (0.191→0.0402 and 0.145→0.0839). Rectified Flow, an OT-based method, struggles when domains share little low-level similarity, yielding FID 25.3 on Edges→Handbags and 77.18 on DIODE, far behind DDBM. DDIB fails catastrophically (FID 186.84 and 242.3) due to the large pixel-space distribution gap between paired data. SDEdit and Pix2Pix are also clearly outperformed on both generation quality (FID) and translation faithfulness (LPIPS, MSE). For unconditional generation, DDBM essentially matches EDM (FID 2.06 vs. 2.04 on CIFAR-10; 2.44 vs. 2.53 on FFHQ-64×64) at the same NFE, confirming that the bridge formulation does not sacrifice generative quality when one endpoint is Gaussian.

## Relation to Prior Work

DDBM extends the score-based diffusion framework (Song et al., 2020b) by conditioning the forward diffusion process on both endpoints via Doob's h-transform, yielding a tractable Gaussian bridge between arbitrary paired distributions. The denoising bridge score-matching loss generalizes standard denoising score-matching to the two-endpoint setting, while the pred-x parameterization and scaling functions are a strict superset of EDM's (Karras et al., 2022), reducing to EDM when one endpoint is Gaussian. Conceptually, the framework subsumes OT-Flow-Matching and Rectified Flow as a noiseless limit (c→0 of a VE bridge) and avoids the expensive iterative proportional fitting required by Schrödinger bridge methods, positioning itself as a unifying middle ground between stochastic diffusion and deterministic transport.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DDBM provides a principled, single-model solution to image-to-image translation that avoids the ad-hoc guidance and projected-sampling workarounds standard diffusion models require, while retaining the ability to recover standard diffusion performance when one endpoint is Gaussian. The unification of diffusion, flow matching, and Schrödinger bridges under one score-matching objective offers a clean theoretical lens for future work on distribution transport. However, empirical validation is limited to 64×64 and 256×256 pixel-space experiments; the abstract mentions latent-space and larger-scale applications that are not detailed in the main paper, leaving open whether the approach scales to high-resolution or text-conditioned generation. The finding that VP bridges outperform VE bridges in some translation settings, and that both the generalized preconditioning and hybrid stochastic sampler are essential (FID drops from ~14 to ~2 only when both are present), suggests practical design choices matter significantly and warrant further study.
