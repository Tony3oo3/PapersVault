## Problem

Existing high-probability last-iterate convergence guarantees for SGD require either compact domains or almost-surely bounded stochastic noises, and smooth optimization results are largely absent beyond the Euclidean, non-composite setting. This paper asks whether optimal rates (near O(1/√T) for convex, O(1/T) for strongly convex) can be proven without these restrictive assumptions, and whether a single unified framework can simultaneously handle composite objectives, non-Euclidean norms, general domains, (L,M)-smoothness, and (strong) convexity.

## Input / Output

The algorithm (Composite Stochastic Mirror Descent) takes an initial point x¹ in a general closed convex set X (no compactness required), an unbiased stochastic gradient oracle with sub-Gaussian noise, a step-size schedule η_t, a convex regularizer h(x), and a 1-strongly-convex Bregman divergence function ψ defining the geometry. It returns the final iterate x^{T+1} and provides explicit convergence rate bounds on the function value gap F(x^{T+1}) − F(x*) both in expectation and with probability at least 1−δ, where the rates depend on the Bregman distance D_ψ(x*, x¹), the smoothness parameters (L, M), and the noise level σ.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a unified proof framework built around a single master inequality (Lemma 4.1) that bounds F(x^{t+1}) − F(z^t) for a carefully constructed convex combination z^t of x* and previous iterates, rather than directly bounding the function value gap. This technique, inspired by Zamani & Glineur (2023), allows convexity of F to be exploited at the final step to extract last-iterate rates. For high-probability bounds, the authors introduce a non-increasing weight sequence w_t (borrowed from Liu et al., 2023) and construct two supermartingales—one for the squared noise terms and one for the cross terms—using only the basic sub-Gaussian moment property (Lemma 2.1), avoiding the need for compactness or boundedness. The resulting unified lemmas (4.2 for expectation, 4.3 for high probability) are then instantiated with different step-size schedules to recover all theorems for Lipschitz, smooth, and strongly convex cases under composite objectives and non-Euclidean norms simultaneously.

## Results

For general (L,M)-smooth convex functions, the last iterate of CSMD achieves E[F(x^{T+1})−F(x*)] = O(LD_ψ/T + (M+σ)√(D_ψ log T)/√T) in expectation and the same rate with σ replaced by σ√log(1/δ) in high probability, without requiring compact domains or bounded noises. For smooth convex (M=0), this yields Õ(L/T + σ/√T), improving the prior best of O(T^{-1/3}) (Moulines & Bach, 2011). For strongly convex (L,M)-smooth functions, the rate is O(LD_ψ/T + (M²+σ²)log T/(μ_f(T+κ_f))) in expectation, and with known T the first term becomes exponential: O(LD_ψ/exp(T/(3+4κ_f)) + (M²+σ²)log T/(μ_f(T+κ_f))).

**Summary of main convergence rates for the last iterate of CSMD (Theorems 3.1, 3.3, 3.4, 3.5).**

| Setting | In Expectation | High Probability (1−δ) |
|---|---|---|
| Convex, Lipschitz (L=0, T unknown) | Õ((M+σ)√D_ψ / √T) | Õ((M+σ√log(1/δ))√D_ψ / √T) |
| Convex, smooth (M=0, T unknown) | Õ(L/T + σ/√T) | Õ(L/T + σ√log(1/δ)/√T) |
| Strongly convex, Lipschitz (L=0, T unknown) | Õ((M²+σ²)log T / (μ_f T)) | Õ((M²+σ²log(1/δ))log T / (μ_f T)) |
| Strongly convex, smooth (M=0, T known) | Õ(LD_ψ/exp(T/κ_f) + σ²log T/(μ_f T)) | Õ(μ_f D_ψ/exp(T/κ_f) + σ²log(1/δ)log T/(μ_f T)) |

*Here D_ψ = D_ψ(x*,x¹), κ_f = L/μ_f, and Õ hides log T factors. All rates hold for general (non-compact) domains, composite objectives, and non-Euclidean norms.*

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a pure theory paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are against prior convergence guarantees. The paper removes two restrictive assumptions that all prior high-probability last-iterate results (Harvey et al. 2019a; Jain et al. 2021) required: compact domains and almost-surely bounded noises. Orabona (2020) was the first to drop the compact-domain assumption but only for in-expectation rates and non-composite objectives; this paper extends to high probability, composite objectives, and non-Euclidean norms. For smooth convex optimization, the Õ(1/√T) rate improves upon the O(T^{-1/3}) bound of Moulines & Bach (2011), which also required bounded stochastic gradients and a global optimum. For strongly convex smooth problems, the paper provides the first last-iterate convergence results in general domains (prior work by Gower et al. 2021 and Khaled & Richtárik 2023 only covered X = R^d under the PL-condition). The high-probability rates incur an extra O(√log(1/δ)) factor over the in-expectation rates, matching the known lower bound of Ω(log(1/δ)) for the Lipschitz case (Harvey et al. 2019a).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on three key prior threads: (1) the CSMD algorithm of Duchi et al. (2010) as the object of analysis, (2) the convexity-exploiting technique of Zamani & Glineur (2023) that upper-bounds F(x^{t+1}) − F(z^t) for a carefully constructed convex combination z^t to extract last-iterate rates, and (3) the auxiliary sequence w_t from Liu et al. (2023) for clean high-probability arguments. It departs from prior last-iterate convergence work (Harvey et al. 2019a, Jain et al. 2021, Orabona 2020) by simultaneously removing the compact-domain and bounded-noise restrictions, extending from non-composite Euclidean objectives to composite objectives under general norms, and covering smooth functions where prior results were limited to O(T^{-1/3}) (Moulines & Bach 2011).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes several long-standing gaps: it provides the first high-probability last-iterate convergence for SGD under sub-Gaussian noise on general (non-compact) domains, and the first near-optimal rates for smooth convex and smooth strongly convex last-iterate convergence in general domains. The unified analytical framework (three lemmas parameterized by step size and sequences) is a practical tool for researchers studying variants of stochastic gradient methods. Open questions include whether the analysis extends to adaptive methods like AdaGrad, whether the O(log T) factor in the strongly convex case can be removed, and whether the results generalize to non-convex settings.
