## Problem

Previous online nonconvex optimization studies either minimized global regret (infeasible for nonconvex objectives) or minimized local regret of window-smoothed functions, which required multiple oracle queries per time step and could misrepresent performance when functions change rapidly. This paper asks: what are the fundamental lower bounds on the local regret of the original (unsmoothed) objective functions when only a single oracle (exact gradient, stochastic gradient, or function value) is available per iteration, and can standard algorithms achieve these bounds?

## Input / Output

The agent receives a sequence of smooth, bounded, nonconvex functions f_t: R^d → R and at each iteration t queries exactly one oracle (∇f_t, a stochastic gradient g_t, or f_t) before selecting the next decision variable x_{t+1}. The performance metric is the local regret R(T) = Σ‖∇f_t(x_t)‖², which measures proximity to instantaneous stationary points. The problem difficulty is characterized by the function variation V_T = Σ sup_x |f_{t-1}(x) − f_t(x)|, which captures how rapidly the objective landscape shifts over time.

## Method

For exact and stochastic gradient oracles, the paper establishes tight problem-dependent lower bounds (Ω(1+V_T) and Ω(σ_g√(T(1+V_T))) respectively) via block constructions with orthogonal gradient directions, and shows that standard OGD/OSGD with appropriately chosen stepsizes match these bounds, proving optimality within the linear-span algorithm class. For the harder function-value-only setting, the key contribution is a one-point running difference gradient estimator: ∇̂f_t(x_t) = (d/δ)·u_t·(f_t(x_t+δu_t) − f_{t-1}(x_{t-1}+δu_{t-1})), which reuses the previous iteration's function value as a residual to cancel variance. A novel contraction argument (Lemma 1) bounds the estimator's norm in terms of V_T, and the resulting OGD algorithm achieves O(d√((1+V_T)T)) regret—matching both the stochastic-gradient lower bound and the regret of a two-point estimator, despite using only one function value query per step.

## Results

The paper establishes tight lower and upper bounds on local regret for online nonconvex optimization under three single-oracle settings. For SGO, OGD achieves the optimal O(1+V_T) regret matching the Ω(1+V_T) lower bound. For SSGO, OSGD achieves O(σ_g√((1+V_T)T)) matching the Ω(σ_g√(T(1+V_T))) lower bound. For SVO, the proposed one-point running difference estimator achieves O(d√((1+V_T)T)), matching both the two-point estimator's bound and the lower bound achievable by a generic stochastic gradient oracle.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main comparison is between gradient estimators under the SVO setting. The proposed one-point running difference estimator achieves O(d√((1+V_T)T)), which is strictly better than the conventional one-point estimator's O(d(1+V_T)^{1/4}T^{3/4}) (since V_T ≤ O(T), the former is at most O(dT) while the latter is O(dT^{3/4}·T^{1/4}) = O(dT) in the worst case but worse when V_T grows sublinearly). Crucially, the proposed one-point estimator matches the two-point estimator's O(d√((1+V_T)T)) bound while requiring only one function value query per iteration instead of two. For the SGO and SSGO settings, the paper shows that classical OGD and OSGD are already optimal among linear-span algorithms, improving upon prior vacuous Ω(T) lower bounds by providing problem-dependent bounds that capture function variation V_T.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on local regret for online nonconvex optimization (Hazan et al. 2017; Aydore et al. 2019; Hallak et al. 2021; Guan et al. 2023) by removing two key simplifying assumptions: window-smoothing of objective functions and multiple oracle queries per step. The running difference gradient estimator is inspired by the one-point residual estimator of Zhang et al. (2022) for offline optimization, but the authors identify a critical analytical difference: in the online setting, consecutive functions f_t and f_{t-1} can be arbitrarily different, so the analysis must rely on function variation V_T rather than stochastic unbiasedness. The lower bound constructions draw on hard-to-learn nonconvex functions from Carmon et al. (2020) and Arjevani et al. (2022), adapted to the online sequential setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The problem-dependent lower bounds (scaling with function variation V_T rather than the vacuous Ω(T)) provide the first meaningful characterization of when online nonconvex optimization is tractable versus fundamentally hard under single-oracle feedback. The result that a one-point running difference estimator matches the regret of a two-point estimator is practically significant for black-box systems (e.g., recommendation, control) where only one function evaluation is available per step. Open questions include whether the linear-span algorithm class restriction is tight, whether the results extend to higher-order or accelerated methods, and whether the theoretical guarantees hold in finite-dimensional practical settings with non-smooth or constrained objectives.
