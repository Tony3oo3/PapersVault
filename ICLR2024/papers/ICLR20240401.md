## Problem

PnP-ULA combines a physical measurement model with a deep-learning denoiser as an implicit prior to sample from the posterior in imaging inverse problems, but the relationship between the resulting sampling distribution and mismatches in either the forward model or the denoiser has not been theoretically characterized. Prior convergence guarantees (Laumont et al., 2022) required the denoiser to be close to the exact MMSE denoiser in the infinite norm, leaving open how errors propagate when this assumption is violated or when the measurement operator is imprecise.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a degraded observation y = Ax + n (e.g., a 256×256 blurred image with Gaussian noise), a known or assumed measurement operator A (e.g., a Gaussian or uniform blur kernel), and a trained deep denoiser D_ε (DnCNN for grayscale, DRUNet for RGB). It outputs a collection of posterior samples from the PnP-ULA Markov chain (typically 1,000 samples saved every 10–30 steps over 10,000–100,000 iterations), from which the MMSE estimator and uncertainty maps are derived. The theoretical output is an explicit bound on the TV or Wasserstein-1 distance between two sampling distributions parameterized by the posterior-L2 pseudometric of their drifts.

## Method

The paper introduces a posterior-L2 pseudometric d₁(f₁,f₂) = √(E_{X~π¹}‖f₁(X)−f₂(X)‖²) that measures drift differences under the reference sampling distribution, and proves (Theorem 1) that the TV distance between two PnP-ULA invariant measures is bounded by A₀·d₁(b¹,b²) + B₀·δ^{1/4}, with an analogous Wasserstein-1 bound. The proof decomposes the distributional difference via a telescoping sum over Markov kernel iterates, controls the single-step discrepancy using Girsanov's theorem, and leverages exponential ergodicity to sum the geometric series. Three corollaries specialize the bound: (1.1) to denoiser shift, showing the posterior-L2 between denoisers predicts sampling-distribution error more accurately than a prior-L2 metric; (1.2) to a reformulation of PnP-ULA convergence that removes the requirement that the denoiser approximate the exact MMSE denoiser; and (1.3) to forward-model shift, bounding the sampling error by the spectral norm of the operator mismatch. Experiments on 2D GMMs, grayscale deblurring (CBSD68), and color deblurring (CelebA) confirm that the posterior-L2 pseudometric correlates strongly (r > 0.97) with the observed Wasserstein distance between sampling distributions.

## Results

The paper's primary quantitative validation shows that the proposed posterior-L₂ pseudometric strongly correlates with the Wasserstein distance between sampling distributions: r=0.98 in the 2D GMM experiment (vs. r=0.89 for the prior-L₂) and r=0.9909 on average across 15 grayscale deblurring images (with r>0.97 per image). In the forward model shift experiment on color images, the Wasserstein distance decreases monotonically from 640 (σ₁=0) to 341 (σ₁=2.5) as the assumed blur kernel standard deviation approaches the true value σ*=3, confirming the linear dependence predicted by Corollary 1.3. The theoretical bound (Theorem 1) establishes that the TV distance between two sampling distributions is bounded by A₀·d₁(b¹,b²) + B₀·δ^(1/4), where the first term captures the drift mismatch and the second is the discretization error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark PnP-ULA against competing restoration methods (e.g., diffusion-based samplers or flow-matching models); instead, it validates its theoretical sensitivity bounds by comparing PnP-ULA runs under controlled perturbations. In the denoiser shift experiment (Corollary 1.1), 17 DnCNN denoisers with 1–17 layers (10³ to 5×10⁵ weights) are compared against the reference 17-layer denoiser; the 5×10⁵-weight denoiser and the 10⁵-weight (5-layer) denoiser produce nearly indistinguishable sampling distributions, suggesting the smaller model is sufficient. In the GMM experiment (Corollary 1.2), the posterior-L₂ pseudometric outperforms the prior-L₂ as a predictor of Wasserstein distance (r=0.98 vs. r=0.89), demonstrating that the reference sampling distribution is the correct integration measure. For the forward model shift (Corollary 1.3), the Wasserstein distance exhibits the expected near-linear decrease with |σ₁−σ*|, with the reconstruction quality visibly improving as the assumed blur approaches the true kernel. The paper also notes that PnP-ULA's computational cost (hundreds of thousands of steps) exceeds that of diffusion or flow-matching alternatives, positioning its contribution as theoretical rather than algorithmic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PnP-ULA framework established by Laumont et al. (2022), which provided convergence guarantees under the assumption that the denoiser approximates the exact MMSE denoiser. The key departure is a sensitivity analysis using Girsanov theory (the same tool used in diffusion model error bounds) to quantify how the sampling distribution shifts when either the denoiser or the forward model is mismatched, without requiring the denoiser to be close to the exact MMSE solution. The posterior-L2 pseudometric introduced here generalizes the prior-L2 comparison used in earlier PnP-ULA analysis and connects to score-matching error terms in diffusion model literature (Chen et al., 2023; Benton et al., 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

In practice, denoisers are never exact MMSE estimators and forward models are never perfectly calibrated, yet prior theory offered no quantitative bound on how these mismatches affect the sampling distribution. This work provides that bound and identifies the posterior-L2 pseudometric as the correct diagnostic, with empirical correlations (r=0.99) confirming its relevance. Open questions include whether the suboptimal Wasserstein bound can be tightened, whether the analysis extends to annealed or relaxed Langevin dynamics, and how the results generalize to non-Gaussian noise or nonlinear forward models. The practical implication is that practitioners can now assess whether a cheaper denoiser (e.g., 5-layer vs. 17-layer DnCNN) is 'good enough' for posterior sampling.
