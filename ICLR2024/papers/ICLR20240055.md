## Problem

Large language models like LLaMA exhibit gender bias by associating gender-neutral professions with stereotypical genders, but it remains unclear which specific model components store these biased representations and whether they can be surgically removed without degrading general language capabilities. Prior debiasing methods either target a single bias manifestation, rely on proxy tasks, or fail to verify that downstream language understanding is preserved.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the full weight matrices of a pre-trained LLaMA model (7B–65B) along with a curated set of stereotypical profession prompts (from Bolukbasi et al.) that carry gender-association signals. It produces modified output weight matrices for the feed-forward MLPs in a contiguous block of mid-upper layers (e.g., layers 21–29 of 32 in the 7B model), where each matrix is multiplied by a projection matrix of the same dimensionality, leaving the model's architecture, parameter count, and inference cost unchanged. The resulting model generates gender-balanced pronoun predictions while retaining near-original perplexity and downstream task scores.

## Method

DAMA first uses causal tracing (clean vs. corrupted runs with per-layer activation restoration) to identify mid-upper MLP layers as the primary mediators of stereotypical gender bias. It then computes stereotypical key vectors (hidden states at the last token of profession prompts) and gendered value vectors (optimized output representations for 'he', 'she', 'they' via a regularized cross-entropy objective that preserves general LM capability). Partial Least Squares is applied to the key-value pairs to extract the low-dimensional subspace most correlated with gendered outputs, and an orthogonal projection matrix (I − P_c) is constructed to nullify that subspace. The projection is multiplied directly into the feed-forward output matrices of the selected layers, and a theorem proves this yields the least-squares-optimal mapping that renders all key representations orthogonal to the bias subspace while leaving other stored correlations intact.

## Results

On LLaMA 7B, DAMA reduces the stereotypical bias coefficient a_s from 0.235 to −0.005 and the intercept b from 0.072 to −0.006, effectively eliminating the model's gendered pronoun preference in language generation. WinoBias stereotype gap ΔS drops from 40.3% to 31.5%, and StereoSet ss decreases from 71.9 to 69.3 while ICAT improves from 53.7 to 58.5. Performance is largely preserved: perplexity increases only from 26.1 to 28.9, and MMLU actually improves slightly (30.3 → 30.8). Scaling to 65B, DAMA still reduces a_s from 0.249 to 0.185 and ΔS from 35.7% to 27.2% with minimal perplexity change (19.5 → 20.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAMA substantially outperforms MEMIT on all bias metrics: while MEMIT barely moves a_s (0.235 → 0.209) and leaves the intercept b unchanged (0.071), DAMA drives a_s to near zero (−0.005) and b to −0.006, because MEMIT edits specific key-value pairs that do not generalize to unseen professions. Against LoRA fine-tuning, DAMA achieves comparable or better debiasing on a_s (−0.005 vs. 0.144) and WinoBias ΔS (31.5% vs. 34.4%), while LoRA wins on StereoSet ss (62.9 vs. 69.3) and ICAT (66.0 vs. 58.5). However, LoRA fine-tuning severely degrades general language modeling (PPL 51.1 vs. 28.9 for DAMA) and downstream performance (OBQA drops from 57.2 to 45.6, MMLU from 30.3 to 26.6), whereas DAMA preserves or slightly improves MMLU (30.3 → 30.8) and causes only a small PPL increase. MEMIT, by contrast, maintains PPL at 26.1 but provides negligible debiasing benefit. Overall, DAMA offers the best bias–performance trade-off among the three methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAMA synthesizes three established threads: causal mediation analysis (Vig et al., 2020; Meng et al., 2022) for locating bias-carrying components, the key-value memory interpretation of MLPs (Geva et al., 2021) for formulating stereotypical keys and gendered values, and orthogonal concept erasure via projection (Ravfogel et al., 2022) for the actual intervention. Unlike prior debiasing methods that either fine-tune on balanced data (Gira et al., 2022; Lauscher et al., 2021), target a single bias manifestation, or use proxy tasks, DAMA directly edits the weight matrices of causally identified mid-upper feed-forward layers using a PLS-derived projection, preserving architecture and parameter count. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) that the projected mapping is the optimal least-squares solution under the orthogonality constraint formalizes why this intervention is minimally destructive to non-bias correlations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method is practically attractive because it is architecture-neutral, requires no additional parameters or inference overhead, and generalizes across LLaMA scales (7B–65B) with consistent bias reduction and negligible downstream degradation. However, the evaluation is limited to the LLaMA family and gender bias specifically; the authors note open questions about generalization to other bias types, other transformer architectures, and morphologically rich languages where gender marking is more pervasive. The significant drop in R² of the linear bias model after DAMA (e.g., 0.49→0.21 for 7B) confirms reduced stereotypical predictability but also means the diagnostic metric becomes less interpretable post-intervention, leaving open how to best quantify residual bias in a non-linear regime.
