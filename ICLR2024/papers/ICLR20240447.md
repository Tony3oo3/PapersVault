## Problem

Off-policy primal-dual safe RL methods suffer from systematic cost underestimation because the minimization operation breaks the zero-mean noise property of value estimates, and this bias is amplified by the distribution shift inherent in off-policy learning. This underestimation causes the Lagrange multiplier to update in the wrong direction, leading to constraint violations. As a result, most existing primal-dual safe RL methods are on-policy by design, requiring large sample budgets that are impractical in safety-critical deployment.

## Input / Output

The method operates on state vectors in continuous control environments (Safety-Gym, MuJoCo) and a scalar cost constraint threshold d, producing a stochastic action from a parameterized policy π_φ(·|s) and a scalar Lagrange multiplier λ updated via gradient ascent on the dual objective. The cost constraint is a single scalar in the CMDP formulation, and the algorithm maintains E bootstrapped cost Q-networks alongside a double reward Q-network, all trained from a shared replay buffer. In the semi-batch oCPM setting, the state includes user/ads context features, cumulative revenue and pay, and predicted CTR/CVR/GMV, while the action is a scalar bidding weight k ∈ [0.5, 1.5].

## Method

CAL builds on the SAC off-policy backbone and introduces two coupled ingredients. First, conservative policy optimization replaces the point cost estimate in the Lagrangian with an upper confidence bound computed from an ensemble of E bootstrapped Q_c networks (mean + k·std), which encourages cost overestimation and keeps the policy inside the true feasible region. Second, local policy convexification applies the augmented Lagrangian method to the primal-dual objective, adding a quadratic penalty term with coefficient c that convexifies the neighborhood of a locally optimal policy; this acts as gradient rectification on the cost term (replacing λ with λ̃ = max{0, λ − c(d − E[Q_c^UCB])}), stabilizing both policy and multiplier updates near local optima. The joint effect is that ALM-induced stabilization focuses collected samples on the local policy distribution, which dissipates epistemic uncertainty, shrinks the bootstrap disagreement, and gradually expands the conservative boundary toward the true constraint boundary—recovering reward without sacrificing safety.

## Results

On the real-world oCPM advertising bidding task (204.8M records, 47,244 advertisers), CAL achieves 656.31k total advertiser payment in Week 1 versus 433.33k for the best baseline (CUP), a 51% improvement, while maintaining a comparable ROI violation rate (30.55% vs. 31.86%). Across all four deployment weeks, CAL consistently yields the highest payment (e.g., 226.37k in Week 4 vs. 95.80k for CUP) with the lowest violation rate (17.26% vs. 19.50%). On the Safety-Gym and velocity-constrained MuJoCo benchmarks, CAL significantly outperforms all off-policy baselines in the low-data regime and reaches asymptotic reward comparable to state-of-the-art on-policy methods (PDO, RCPO, IPO, CRPO, CUP) while using substantially fewer samples and incurring fewer constraint violations during training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among off-policy baselines, CAL dominates all three: SAC-PIDLag (Stooke et al., 2020) suffers from the most severe constraint violations and lowest reward in the low-data regime; WCSAC (Yang et al., 2021) achieves moderate conservatism via CVaR but still underperforms CAL in both reward and constraint satisfaction; CVPO (Liu et al., 2022) improves sample efficiency over naive off-policy methods but remains inferior to CAL, particularly in constraint violation during training. Against on-policy baselines, CAL matches or approaches the asymptotic reward of the best on-policy method in each task while requiring far fewer environment steps. IPO (Liu et al., 2020) attains slightly higher reward on some Safety-Gym tasks but is clearly inferior on MuJoCo tasks. CUP (Yang et al., 2022) and CRPO (Xu et al., 2021) are matched in asymptotic reward by CAL, but CAL reaches comparable performance with significantly fewer samples and fewer constraint violations. Notably, no on-policy baseline achieves constraint satisfaction on all tested tasks, whereas CAL does. On the oCPM real-world task, CAL outperforms all four baselines (WCSAC, CVPO, CUP, SAC-PIDLag) by a wide margin in payment maximization (e.g., 656k vs. 433k in Week 1) while maintaining the lowest ROI violation rate by Week 4 (17.26% vs. 19.50% for CUP).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SAC off-policy backbone and the augmented Lagrangian method from convex optimization, combining them with a bootstrapped ensemble (inspired by Osband et al.'s bootstrapped DQN) to form a conservative cost estimator. It departs from the dominant on-policy primal-dual safe RL literature (PDO, RCPO, IPO, CRPO, CUP) by identifying cost underestimation under distribution shift as the fundamental failure mode of off-policy variants, and from prior off-policy attempts (SAC-PIDLag, WCSAC, CVPO) by proposing a two-ingredient solution—UCB-based conservatism plus ALM-based local convexification—whose coupling effect is analyzed theoretically. The positioning is that neither ingredient alone suffices: UCB alone shrinks the search space, while ALM alone does not address the underestimation bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in safe RL deployment: on-policy methods require millions of risky environment interactions, while existing off-policy methods fail to satisfy constraints due to cost underestimation. The semi-batch oCPM experiment on 204.8M real bidding records demonstrates that the approach transfers to industrial-scale settings where the policy cannot be updated mid-deployment, a scenario common in advertising, robotics, and autonomous driving. Open questions include extension to fully offline settings (acknowledged by the authors), multi-constraint CMDPs, and whether the UTD-ratio advantage over baselines persists at larger scales. The method's reliance on tuning both k (conservatism) and c (convexity) per task remains a practical limitation.
