## Problem

Personalized text-to-image diffusion models (e.g., DreamBooth, LoRA fine-tunes) produce high-quality static images but cannot generate animations. Existing video generation approaches either retrain all parameters or modify the feature space of the base T2I, making them incompatible with community-shared personalized models, and model-specific fine-tuning is impractical for amateur users in terms of compute and data costs.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a text prompt and an existing personalized T2I model (any LoRA or DreamBooth checkpoint built on the same Stable Diffusion base) and produces a 16-frame animation clip at 512×512 resolution via DDIM sampling with classifier-free guidance. The personalized T2I is inflated to accept 5D video tensors (batch × channels × frames × height × width), and the pre-trained motion module is inserted without any modification to the personalized weights. Optionally, a depth map sequence can be fed through ControlNet for spatially controllable animation, and MotionLoRA weights can be composed linearly to combine multiple shot-type effects.

## Method

AnimateDiff trains a plug-and-play temporal Transformer motion module once on WebVid-10M that can be inserted into any personalized T2I from the same base without further tuning. The training uses a three-stage decoupled strategy: (1) a LoRA-based domain adapter absorbs the visual quality gap between high-quality image pre-training data and lower-quality video frames, so the motion module learns motion rather than pixel-level artifacts; (2) the base T2I is inflated via network inflation (image layers process each frame independently by reshaping the temporal axis into batch), and a zero-initialized temporal self-attention module with sinusoidal position encoding is trained on video clips while keeping the base T2I and domain adapter frozen; (3) MotionLoRA adds low-rank adapters to the motion module's self-attention layers, enabling adaptation to specific camera motions (zoom, pan, roll) with as few as 50 reference videos and ~30M parameters. The domain adapter is dropped (or scaled to zero) at inference, and the motion module's zero-initialization ensures it acts as an identity mapping before training, preventing degradation of the personalized model's visual quality.

## Results

In the user study (Average User Ranking, higher is better), AnimateDiff achieves the best scores on text alignment (2.210) and motion smoothness (2.825), while Text2Video-Zero leads on domain similarity (2.620 vs. 2.280). On CLIP metrics, AnimateDiff attains the highest domain similarity (87.29) and motion smoothness (98.00), but trails Tune-a-Video on text alignment (31.39 vs. 35.98). Overall, AnimateDiff is the only method that simultaneously scores well across all three evaluation axes, whereas each baseline excels on at most one.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnimateDiff is compared against two academic baselines (Text2Video-Zero and Tune-a-Video) and two commercial tools (Gen-2 and Pika Labs, qualitative only). Against Text2Video-Zero, AnimateDiff wins on user-study text alignment (2.210 vs. 1.620), motion smoothness (2.825 vs. 1.560), and CLIP domain similarity (87.29 vs. 84.84), but loses on user-study domain similarity (2.280 vs. 2.620) and CLIP text alignment (31.39 vs. 32.04). Against Tune-a-Video, AnimateDiff wins on user-study domain similarity (2.280 vs. 1.100) and motion smoothness (2.825 vs. 1.615), as well as CLIP domain (87.29 vs. 80.68) and smoothness (98.00 vs. 97.42), but loses on CLIP text alignment (31.39 vs. 35.98). The commercial tools Gen-2 and Pika Labs are compared only qualitatively in Figure 5; the paper reports that AnimateDiff produces more temporally coherent animations that better preserve the personalized domain style. Notably, Tune-a-Video requires per-model fine-tuning on a reference video, while Text2Video-Zero is training-free but produces less diverse motion, making AnimateDiff's plug-and-play motion module the most practical option for the community of personalized T2I models.

## Relation to Prior Work

AnimateDiff builds on Stable Diffusion's latent diffusion framework and the LoRA parameter-efficient fine-tuning paradigm, repurposing LoRA in two distinct roles: a domain adapter to absorb visual distribution gaps during training, and MotionLoRA to adapt the motion module to new patterns. It departs from prior video generation approaches (Tune-a-Video, Text2Video-Zero, Align-Your-Latents) that either require per-model fine-tuning or modify the base model's feature space, by introducing a plug-and-play temporal Transformer module that can be inserted into any personalized T2I without retraining. The network inflation strategy follows the 5D tensor reshaping convention established by Ho et al. (2022b) and Wu et al. (2023), but the key conceptual departure is the separation of motion learning from visual quality learning via the domain adapter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work unlocks animation generation for the entire ecosystem of community-contributed personalized T2I models (Civitai, Hugging Face) without requiring users to bear expensive retraining costs, with MotionLoRA needing only ~50 reference videos and ~30M additional storage. The practical plug-and-play nature makes it immediately adoptable by non-expert users, and its compatibility with ControlNet for controllable generation broadens applicability. Key limitations remain: motion diversity is bounded by WebVid-10M's coverage (struggling with complex motions like dance), text-motion alignment is weak due to coarse motion labels in the training data, and the approach has not been validated on longer or higher-resolution sequences. Open questions include whether the temporal Transformer design scales to longer temporal contexts and whether richer motion-labeled datasets would substantially improve text-motion alignment.
