## Problem

Existing in-processing fairness methods that regularize empirical risk with non-linear divergence measures (MMD, Rényi correlation, χ², KL, etc.) produce objectives that cannot be decomposed into per-sample functions, making them incompatible with stochastic gradient descent and thus infeasible for large-scale training. Moreover, when distribution shift exists between training and test domains, prior distributionally robust fairness approaches are restricted to convex losses, rely on specific shift assumptions, or lack convergence guarantees for stochastic updates.

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular data where each sample consists of a feature vector x_i ∈ R^d, a class label y_i, and one or more sensitive attributes s_i (e.g., gender, race). During training, the sensitive attribute is used to compute joint and marginal probability estimates via the softmax layer outputs, but at inference the model produces only a class prediction via maximum-likelihood decoding. The framework supports arbitrary batch sizes from 1 to full-batch, and the number of sensitive attributes and their cardinalities determine the dimensionality of the auxiliary variable A in the min-max formulation.

## Method

The core contribution is reformulating the f-divergence regularizer in fair ERM via the Legendre-Fenchel (convex conjugate) variational representation, which transforms the non-separable divergence into a min-max problem that is linearly separable over data points. This yields unbiased stochastic gradient estimators for both the model parameters θ (descent) and the auxiliary variable A (ascent), enabling a convergent SGDA algorithm with O(ε^{-8}) iteration complexity for any batch size. For distribution shift, the paper introduces a distributionally robust formulation using ℓ_p norm balls as uncertainty sets around the training distribution; for small shifts, a first-order Taylor approximation reduces the inner maximization to a tractable nonconvex-concave problem, while for large shifts under ℓ_∞ balls, the optimal perturbation has a closed-form extreme-point solution that reduces the problem to a standard (sub)gradient descent.

## Results

The paper presents results primarily as fairness-accuracy trade-off curves rather than single-point metrics. On the Adult dataset (gender + race, batch size 8), KL-divergence f-FERM achieves simultaneous improvements in both demographic parity violation and accuracy at low λ values, a phenomenon not observed for other f-divergences or baselines. Across batch sizes from full-batch down to 2, f-FERM (reverse KL) maintains consistent trade-off performance, while baselines (Mary et al., Baharlouei et al., Cho et al.) degrade significantly at smaller batch sizes. In the distribution-shift setting (New Adult dataset, trained on one state, evaluated across 50 states), f-infinity preserves fairness levels better while f-DRO achieves better accuracy, both outperforming Rezaei et al. and Wang et al.

## Baselines & Comparisons

f-FERM (reverse KL) consistently outperforms Mary et al. (2019), Baharlouei et al. (2020, Rényi fair inference), and Cho et al. (2020, kernel density estimation) on the Adult dataset across all tested batch sizes (full-batch, 64, 8, 2), with the gap widening substantially at smaller batch sizes where baselines' performance drops significantly. Pre-processing (Zemel et al., 2013), post-processing (Hardt et al., 2016), and in-processing methods (Zafar et al., 2017; Donini et al., 2018; Jiang et al., 2020) all showed lower performance and were excluded from the main comparison figure. In the distribution-shift experiments, f-DRO and f-infinity both achieve lower demographic parity violation than Rezaei et al. (2021, robust fairness under covariate shift) and Wang et al. (2020, robust optimization for fairness with noisy protected groups) as the proportion of corrupted sensitive attributes increases from 0% to 20%. On the New Adult dataset (cross-state generalization), f-infinity preserves fairness better while f-DRO offers better accuracy, with both methods dominating the SOTA distribution-shift baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the stochastic fair ERM framework of Lowy et al. (2022), which handles only the χ² divergence, to the full family of f-divergences (KL, reverse KL, Jensen-Shannon, Hellinger, total variation) via a Legendre-Fenchel variational reformulation that yields unbiased stochastic gradient estimators. It departs from concurrent work by Zhong & Tandon (2023), which also uses f-divergences but estimates them offline through neural networks, by solving the variational min-max problem directly with convergence guarantees. For the distributionally robust extension, the paper builds on the small-domain min-max approximation of Ostrovskii et al. (2021a) and extends DRO-based fairness beyond the convex-logistic-regression setting of Taskesen et al. (2020) to general nonconvex losses under ℓ_p norm uncertainty sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a single, theoretically grounded recipe for fair classification that scales from full-batch to batch size 1, addressing a key practical limitation of prior in-processing methods whose nonlinear regularizers break stochastic gradient descent. The distributionally robust extension is notable for not requiring causal graphs or assumptions on the type of shift (demographic, label, covariate), making it applicable to complex real-world shifts. Open questions include: which f-divergence is optimal for a given fairness-accuracy tradeoff (the paper shows no single divergence dominates), whether the ℓ_p norm uncertainty sets can be replaced by richer measures (Wasserstein, MMD) while preserving convergence, and how the framework performs on high-dimensional or non-tabular data.
