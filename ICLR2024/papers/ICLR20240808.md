## Problem

Existing physics-informed neural networks (Lagrangian/Hamiltonian NNs, Neural ODEs, Graph Neural ODEs) are designed exclusively for deterministic dynamics governed by ODEs, leaving a gap for stochastic systems such as Brownian dynamics governed by SDEs. No prior work has attempted to learn the dynamics of Brownian (or more general SDE) systems directly from trajectory data, despite the widespread use of Brownian dynamics in physics, chemistry, and biology.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240808/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes as input the 3D positions of all particles, one-hot encoded particle types, and a directed interaction graph (edges encode relative displacement vectors between connected particles, making them asymmetric). It outputs predicted pairwise inter-particle forces, per-particle friction coefficients (γ_i), and updated positions via Euler-Maruyama integration. Training is performed on single-timestep position transitions with a Gaussian NLL loss, but evaluation is on 100-timestep forward rollouts (0.1 s) from unseen initial conditions, making the task significantly harder than what the model was explicitly trained for.

## Method

BROGNET parameterizes the over-damped Langevin (Brownian) SDE using a message-passing GNN: node and edge embeddings are constructed via MLPs, refined through L layers of sum-pooling message passing, and a final MLP predicts pairwise forces from edge representations. The critical architectural choice is that the net force on each node is computed as the sum of incoming edge forces minus outgoing edge forces (Eq. 8), which enforces Newton's third law and guarantees exact linear momentum conservation (Theorem 1) without any external field. The stochastic diffusion coefficient γ_i is learned independently via a small MLP on one-hot particle type, decoupling it from the graph topology. Training minimizes a Gaussian NLL loss over positions propagated by the Euler-Maruyama integrator, and the graph-based inductive bias (fixed parameter count regardless of particle number) enables zero-shot generalization to system sizes two orders of magnitude larger and to unseen temperatures.

## Results

On the linear 5-spring system, BROGNET and MIBDGNN significantly outperform all other baselines (NN, BNN, BFGN, BDGNN, BNequIP) across trajectory roll-out error, Brownian error, and position error. BROGNET achieves near-zero total force (momentum error) while all other models predict finite net force, confirming exact momentum conservation. The model generalizes zero-shot to system sizes two orders of magnitude larger (5→500 springs) and to temperatures 10× and 100× higher than training, with comparable error metrics. BROGNET trains in 2126 s and runs forward simulation in 0.506 s per 100 timesteps, compared to 27535 s training and 21.319 s inference for BNequIP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BROGNET outperforms BFGN (Sanchez-Gonzalez et al., ICML 2020) on all three metrics (trajectory roll-out, Brownian, and position error) across all three spring systems, with the gap being most pronounced on the linear and non-linear systems. BROGNET also outperforms BNequIP (Batzner et al., Nature Communications 2022) on all metrics, and is substantially faster in both training (2126 s vs. 27535 s) and inference (0.506 s vs. 21.319 s). Against the momentum-conservation ablation BDGNN (identical architecture minus Newton's third law enforcement), BROGNET achieves lower error and near-zero net force, confirming the hard architectural constraint is beneficial. Interestingly, MIBDGNN (BDGNN + soft momentum penalty in the loss) outperforms BROGNET on trajectory and position error, suggesting the soft regularizer aids learning, but BROGNET achieves strictly zero momentum error versus MIBDGNN's small-but-finite residual. On the binary linear 10-spring system, the performance gap between BROGNET and other baselines narrows, though BROGNET still leads.

## Relation to Prior Work

BROGNET extends the Graph Neural ODE paradigm (Poli et al., 2021) from deterministic to stochastic dynamics, replacing the ODE integrator with an Euler-Maruyama SDE integrator and a distributional (Gaussian NLL) loss. It departs from the dominant line of physics-informed learning work (Lagrangian/Hamiltonian NNs, Neural ODEs) that exclusively targets deterministic systems governed by ODEs, and from prior GNN-based particle simulators (Sanchez-Gonzalez et al., 2019, 2020) that do not model stochasticity. The momentum-conserving architecture draws on the broader trend of embedding conservation laws as hard inductive biases (Finzi et al., 2020; Thangamuthu et al., 2022), but applies Newton's third law specifically to the force-prediction head of a GNN to guarantee zero net force by construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to learn Brownian (SDE) dynamics directly from trajectory data, opening a path toward data-driven simulation of stochastic physical processes in chemistry, biology, and soft matter where over-damped Langevin dynamics are standard. The demonstrated zero-shot generalizability to systems 100× larger and to unseen temperatures is practically valuable for scaling simulations. However, the scope is limited to the over-damped limit (zero acceleration), simple spring topologies, and no external fields; extending to full Langevin dynamics, other SDE classes, and real-world experimental data remains open. The finding that a soft momentum constraint (MIBDGNN) outperforms the hard architectural constraint (BROGNET) on accuracy metrics, while the hard constraint wins on momentum conservation, raises an interesting design question about when to prefer soft vs. hard physics constraints.
