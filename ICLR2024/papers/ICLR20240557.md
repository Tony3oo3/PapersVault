## Problem

MLP-based neural fields struggle to model long and complex spatiotemporal signals (e.g., dynamic 3D scenes, long videos) due to limited model capacity. Existing remedies—widening the network, spatial partitioning into local fields, or meta-learning per-frame weights—either slow down inference, sacrifice global reasoning and implicit regularization, or impose prohibitive training costs. The paper asks how to increase capacity in an architecture-agnostic way that preserves the generalization properties of a single global MLP.

## Input / Output

The method consumes time-space coordinate pairs (t, x) where x is a 2D pixel coordinate, a 3D point, or a ray direction, and produces task-specific outputs: RGB colors for video approximation, signed distance values for temporal SDF, color-and-density pairs for radiance field rendering, or 3D offset/SE(3)/DCT vectors for scene flow. A distinctive aspect is that the residual weight coefficients v(t) are stored as a T_i × R_i matrix and linearly interpolated along time, enabling continuous temporal queries without per-frame lookups. The shared spanning set M is global across the entire sequence, so the same MLP backbone serves all frames while the residual modulation adapts per time step.

## Method

ResFields replace selected linear layers of an MLP with residual field layers whose weights are W_i + W_i(t), where W_i(t) is a time-dependent residual. To avoid an unconstrained per-frame weight dictionary, the residual is factorized as W_i(t) = Σ_r v_i(t)[r] · M_i[r], with a small set of R_i time-dependent scalar coefficients v(t) and a large globally shared spanning set M ∈ R^{R_i × N_i × M_i}. This design allocates few independent parameters per time step (in v) and many shared parameters (in M), simultaneously increasing capacity and strengthening generalization by letting the shared weights encode patterns common across the whole sequence. The formulation is architecture-agnostic: it can be inserted into any MLP-based neural field (SIREN, TNeRF, DNeRF, Nerfies, HyperNeRF, NDR, HexPlane) by swapping a subset of hidden layers, without altering the input encoding, activation functions, or training objective. Residual weights are initialized from N(0, 10⁻²) to preserve the base MLP's initialization, and the entire system is trained end-to-end with standard Adam optimization.

## Results

On the Owlii dynamic NeRF benchmark (4 sequences, 4 sparse RGB views), TNeRF+ResFields (i=1,...,7) achieves mean PSNR 27.55, SSIM 95.45, and CD 14.2, outperforming all other baselines in their original form including Tensor4D (PSNR 22.59), HexPlane (PSNR 24.71), and NeuS2 (PSNR 21.73). On 2D video approximation, Siren-512+ResFields reaches 39.21 test PSNR versus 36.37 for Siren-1024 and 34.52 for Instant NGP, while training 2.5× faster and using 30% less GPU memory. On temporal SDF reconstruction, Siren-128+ResFields (rank 40) achieves CD 8.16 and ND 14.19, surpassing the larger Siren-256 (CD 9.04, ND 16.37) at over 2× lower inference cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ResFields consistently improves every baseline method tested, with the most dramatic gains on deformation-based methods: DNeRF improves from PSNR 23.36 to 27.89 (+4.5 dB) and Nerfies from 24.35 to 27.72 (+3.4 dB) on Owlii. The simplest baseline, TNeRF+ResFields (12h training), outperforms all more expensive methods in their original form, including Tensor4D (15h, PSNR 22.59), HexPlane (5h, PSNR 24.71), and NeuS2 (0.5h, PSNR 21.73). On 2D video approximation, Siren-512+ResFields (39.21 PSNR) beats both the larger Siren-1024 (36.37) and the grid-based Instant NGP (34.52), while using 30% less GPU memory. On temporal SDF, Siren-128+ResFields (rank 40) surpasses Siren-256 in both CD (8.16 vs 9.04) and ND (14.19 vs 16.37) at over 2× lower inference cost. In the factorization ablation (Table 7), the proposed low-rank spanning-set factorization achieves the best test PSNR (41.69 at rank 40) compared to CP (33.96), Tucker (39.04), LoE (40.62), and no factorization (38.52), confirming superior generalization. The method does not show advantage on ill-posed monocular reconstruction where the bottleneck is lack of constraints rather than model capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

ResFields repurposes the concept of residual connections (He et al., 2016) in a fundamentally different way: rather than adding residuals to layer outputs, it models time-dependent residuals on the layer weight matrices themselves, parameterized via a low-rank factorization inspired by LoRA and factorized layers (Denil et al., 2013; Ioannou et al., 2015). This positions the work between two existing strategies for increasing MLP capacity in neural fields—spatial partitioning (Instant NGP, Tensor4D, HexPlane) which sacrifices global reasoning, and hypernetworks (Ha et al., 2017) which impose heavy computational overhead—by offering an architecture-agnostic, drop-in module that preserves implicit regularization. The closest prior work is LoE (Hao et al., 2022), which also introduces input-dependent weight composition, but ResFields achieves stronger generalization through its global shared spanning set and per-time-step coefficients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling substantially smaller MLPs (e.g., 128 vs. 256 neurons) to match or exceed larger baselines while being 2–3× faster with lower GPU memory, which is critical for inverse rendering tasks requiring billions of field queries. The drop-in compatibility with seven distinct dynamic NeRF/SDF methods (TNeRF, DNeRF, Nerfies, HyperNeRF, NDR, DyNeRF, HexPlane) makes it a broadly applicable building block for the community. Key limitations remain: the approach does not help when the bottleneck is under-constrained observation (e.g., monocular reconstruction), shared weights run out of capacity for very long sequences, and thin geometric structures are still poorly captured. Open questions include whether the low-rank residual formulation can be extended to non-temporal conditioning (e.g., appearance, identity) and how to handle arbitrarily long sequences without chunking.
