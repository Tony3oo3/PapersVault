## Problem

Mechanistic interpretability research has largely been confined to small toy models or narrow tasks, with little evidence that interpretable components recur across the diverse architectures and scales of practical LLMs. The universality hypothesis—that different models form common internal representations—remains largely untested at scale, leaving open whether insights from small models transfer to the 10B+ parameter systems used in practice.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240368/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on the weights of pre-trained transformer LLMs (Pythia 31M–12B, GPT-2, Llama-2 7B) and consumes token sequences from eight ordinal tasks (numbers 1–200, number words, cardinal words, days, day prefixes, months, month prefixes, letters). The key measurement is the effective OV circuit W_U·W_OV·MLP_0(W_E), a shallow four-component path that bypasses all other layers, producing per-head successor scores (fraction of tokens where the head's output logit on the successor exceeds all other same-task tokens). Downstream analyses produce mod-10 feature vectors, logit heatmaps, and four behavioral categories (successorship, acronym, copying, greater-than) scored over 128 length-512 contexts from The Pile.

## Method

The paper identifies successor heads by testing whether each attention head's effective OV circuit (W_U·W_OV·MLP_0(W_E)) assigns the highest logit to the successor token for more than half of tokens in the succession dataset. To uncover the internal structure these heads operate on, the authors train sparse autoencoders on MLP_0-layer representations of ordinal tokens and discover ten 'mod-10' features f_0…f_9, where f_n activates on all tokens whose ordinal index ≡ n (mod 10) across numbers, months, cardinal words, and other tasks. These features are independently validated via linear probes (94/102 out-of-distribution accuracy) and ablative experiments on individual MLP_0 neurons showing period-10 firing patterns. The authors then perform vector arithmetic (e.g., MLP_0(W_E('fifth')) − k·f_5 + k·f_7) to causally shift a token's ordinal index, confirming compositional structure, and finally analyze the head's behavior on natural-language data via direct-effect mean ablation, revealing interpretable polysemanticity where the same head performs both incrementation and acronym prediction.

## Results

Successor heads are identified across models spanning nearly three orders of magnitude in parameter count (31M to 12B), including GPT-2, Pythia, and Llama-2 architectures. The mod-10 features extracted via sparse autoencoders are validated by a linear probe achieving 0.70764 average cosine similarity with SAE features and generalizing to 94/102 non-numeric tokens. Vector arithmetic using mod-10 features successfully steers successor head behavior on 53% of month tokens and 89% of digit tokens (20–29) when the target residue exceeds the source residue. In natural language (The Pile), the successor head is the winning head in all 64 numbered-listing prompts, and compositionality projections achieve 1.00 top-1 output-space decoding accuracy on held-out token pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a mechanistic interpretability paper without traditional method-vs-baseline comparisons; the 'compares_to' field is empty. Instead, the paper validates its findings through multiple independent methods: (1) SAE-extracted mod-10 features are corroborated by linear probes (0.70764 cosine similarity) and by ablative experiments on individual MLP0 neurons (most common firing period = 10 across the top 16 neurons); (2) the mod-10 structure is shown to be universal across architectures (Pythia, GPT-2, Llama-2) and scales (31M–12B parameters) without any architecture-specific tuning; (3) the effective OV circuit (W_U W_OV MLP_0(W_E)) is compared against the residual-stream-only path W_U MLP_0(W_E), which achieves <1% successor score, confirming the attention head is necessary for incrementation. The vector arithmetic experiments reveal a 'greater-than bias' in the successor head's OV matrix that is absent in the mod-10 features themselves, indicating the features capture only part of the numeric representation. No direct head-to-head comparison against competing interpretability methods (e.g., path patching, activation patching) is performed, though the paper notes its approach differs from prior work by targeting shallow, specific paths rather than deep hidden states.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the transformer circuit framework of Elhage et al. (2021) and the effective OV circuit methodology of McDougall et al. (2023) to identify and interpret a new class of attention heads (successor heads) responsible for ordinal incrementation. It builds on the universality hypothesis (Olah et al., 2020) by providing concrete evidence that the same circuit architecture emerges across Pythia, GPT-2, and Llama-2 at scales from 31M to 12B parameters. The use of sparse autoencoders to isolate mod-10 features draws on the monosemanticity program (Bricken et al., 2023; Cunningham et al., 2023), while the vector arithmetic experiments echo Mikolov et al. (2013) but operate on internal MLP0 representations rather than raw embeddings. Unlike prior circuit work on induction heads (Olsson et al., 2022) or greater-than circuits (Hanna et al., 2023), this paper provides an end-to-end shallow circuit explanation that generalizes across architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the clearest evidence to date for weak universality in LLMs: the same successor head circuit with mod-10 features appears across three orders of magnitude of scale and three distinct architectures, suggesting that findings from small models may transfer to frontier systems. The identification of interpretable polysemanticity (a single head performing both succession and acronym prediction) in a real 1.4B-parameter model is a notable advance over prior polysemanticity observations in toy or vision models. Key limitations include the failure of mod-10 features on days and letters tasks, an unexplained greater-than bias in the OV circuit, and the absence of a proof that successor heads are necessary (rather than sufficient) for all incrementation. Open questions include why mod-10 is privileged over other moduli, whether the compositional index/domain decomposition generalizes beyond ordinal sequences, and how these circuits interact with deeper layers in larger models.
