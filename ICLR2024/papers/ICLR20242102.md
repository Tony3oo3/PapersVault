## Problem

Existing GCD methods focus exclusively on adapting pre-trained model parameters (e.g., fine-tuning the top ViT layer) while keeping the data representation fixed. The paper identifies that naively applying visual prompt tuning (VPT or global border prompts) to GCD actually degrades performance, and that jointly optimizing model and data parameters in an end-to-end fashion leads to instability and sub-optimal solutions for both. The gap is an efficient framework that simultaneously adapts both the model and the input data representation in a stable, alternating manner tailored to the open-world GCD setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes a labelled set of images from seen classes (D_l) and an unlabelled set from both seen and unseen classes (D_u), all as RGB images resized to 224×224 for the ViT-B/16 backbone. Each input image is divided into 196 patches of 16×16 pixels; spatial prompts (a rectangular border of width m=1 around each patch) and a global prompt (border of width m⁺=30 around the full image) are added in pixel space before patchification. The output is a class label for every unlabelled image, with evaluation via clustering accuracy over the optimal permutation of predicted vs. ground-truth labels, reported separately for All, Old (seen), and New (unseen) categories.

## Method

SPTNet introduces a two-stage alternating optimization framework inspired by the EM algorithm: in Stage 1 the ViT backbone and projection head are frozen while only the spatial prompt parameters P_s are updated; in Stage 2 the prompts are frozen and the top transformer block plus projection head are fine-tuned using a contrastive loss where the prompted image serves as a learned augmentation paired with the raw image. The key architectural contribution is Spatial Prompt Tuning (SPT), which attaches instance-agnostic, pixel-level learnable prompts to each 16×16 image patch (6m(h+w−2m) parameters per patch) rather than inserting tokens in hidden model space or wrapping a single border around the whole image, enabling the model to focus on local object parts that transfer between seen and unseen classes. The overall loss combines unsupervised InfoNCE, supervised contrastive, cosine-softmax classification, and an entropy regularizer (following SimGCD), with the prompted version of every image used across all loss terms. The entire method adds only 0.117% extra parameters relative to the ViT-B backbone (105,120 parameters total for SPT + global prompt), and the two stages alternate every k=20 iterations over 1,000 epochs.

## Results

On the Semantic Shift Benchmark (SSB; CUB, Stanford Cars, FGVC-Aircraft), SPTNet achieves an average 'All' accuracy of 61.4%, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art SimGCD (56.1%) by 5.3 points and PromptCAL (55.1%) by 6.3 points. On ImageNet-100, SPTNet reaches 85.4% 'All' accuracy vs. 83.0% for SimGCD and 83.1% for PromptCAL. On the large-scale Herbarium-19 dataset, SPTNet achieves 43.4% 'All' accuracy, outperforming SimGCD (43.0%) and PromptCAL (37.0%). These gains are achieved with only 0.117% additional parameters over the ViT-B/16 backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPTNet outperforms all eight baselines on the SSB fine-grained benchmark. Against the strongest prior method SimGCD, SPTNet gains +5.5 pts on CUB (65.8 vs. 60.3), +5.2 pts on Stanford Cars (59.0 vs. 53.8), and +5.1 pts on FGVC-Aircraft (59.3 vs. 54.2). Against the concurrent PromptCAL, SPTNet leads by +2.9 pts on CUB, +8.8 pts on Stanford Cars, and +7.1 pts on FGVC-Aircraft. DCCL achieves 63.5% on CUB but is not evaluated on the other SSB datasets or Herbarium-19, making a full comparison incomplete. On generic datasets, SPTNet wins on CIFAR-100 (81.3 vs. 81.2 PromptCAL, 80.1 SimGCD) and ImageNet-100 (85.4 vs. 83.1 PromptCAL, 83.0 SimGCD), but is slightly behind PromptCAL on CIFAR-10 (97.3 vs. 97.9), which the authors attribute to the extremely low 32×32 resolution limiting the benefit of spatial prompting. On Herbarium-19, SPTNet edges out SimGCD (43.4 vs. 43.0) and substantially outperforms PromptCAL (43.4 vs. 37.0).

## Relation to Prior Work

SPTNet builds directly on the SimGCD baseline (Wen et al., 2023), adopting its contrastive + parametric classification loss and ViT-B/16 backbone, while introducing a two-stage alternating optimization inspired by the EM algorithm to jointly tune model and data parameters. It departs from prior prompt tuning methods (VPT, Bahng et al.) by operating in pixel space at the patch level rather than in hidden model space or around image borders, and repurposes the learned spatial prompt as a learned data augmentation within the contrastive framework. The global prompt component is borrowed from Bahng et al. (2022), but the per-patch spatial design is novel, motivated by the GCD insight that object parts transfer knowledge between seen and unseen classes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~10% proportional improvement on the SSB benchmark with only 0.117% extra parameters demonstrates that adapting the data representation (via spatial prompts) can be as effective as or more effective than fine-tuning the model, offering a practical and efficient path for GCD. The finding that hidden-space prompts (VPT) actually degrade GCD performance while pixel-space spatial prompts help provides useful guidance for prompt design in open-world settings. Limitations include unsatisfactory cross-domain robustness (DomainNet results show significant room for improvement), reliance on a pre-trained DINO backbone that may inherit its own biases, and the approach is currently validated only on ViT architectures. Open questions include whether the alternating strategy generalizes to other backbone families and whether the spatial prompt concept transfers to video or multi-modal GCD.
