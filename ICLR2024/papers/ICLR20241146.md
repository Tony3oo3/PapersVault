## Problem

In-context learning with LLMs incurs quadratic self-attention costs as demonstrations lengthen the input. Existing distillation approaches (prompt tuning, HyperTuning, HINT) either require task-specific retraining or produce compressed vectors that degrade ICL performance relative to vanilla ICL. The open question is how to distill arbitrary demonstrations into compact vectors that match or exceed vanilla ICL efficacy without per-task retraining.

## Input / Output

MEND takes a concatenated set of K input-output demonstration pairs (D) and a test input x. It appends l learnable placeholder tokens to the demonstration embeddings and passes them through a smaller transformer (e.g., gpt2-small for a gpt2-large LLM), outputting l distilled vectors of dimension d. These vectors replace the full demonstration text (reducing context from ~900 tokens to ~100) and are prepended to the test input embedding before being fed to the frozen LLM, which produces a class label or answer.

## Method

MEND's core mechanism is a knowledge-distillation alignment objective: the LLM conditioned on full demonstrations acts as a teacher, while the same LLM conditioned only on MEND's distilled vectors acts as a student, and the KL divergence between their output token distributions is minimized with gradients backpropagated through the frozen LLM into MEND. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) meta-distillation pretraining on C4 text, where 1024-token sequences are split into a demonstration portion (fraction β) and an input portion, minimizing only the distillation loss; (2) meta-distillation finetuning on ICL tasks, combining a prediction loss (LLM generating the held-out label from distilled vectors + test input) with the distillation loss weighted by λ. This replaces the conditional language modeling objective used by HyperTuning and HINT, directly optimizing for behavioral equivalence between the distilled and full-demonstration LLM rather than next-token prediction from compressed tokens.

## Results

On the MetaICL benchmark (7 task partitions, K=16 demonstrations), MEND achieves an average score of 43.05 with GPT-2-large, 43.12 with GPT-2-XL, and 39.79 with T5-large, consistently outperforming Vanilla ICL (41.93, 42.15, 38.15 respectively) and HyperTuning (41.15, 40.61, 35.09). MEND also reduces FLOPs by up to 75% and accelerates inference by up to 33% compared to Vanilla ICL, while matching or exceeding its accuracy. The method achieves up to 3.5× greater computational efficiency than Vanilla ICL with lower peak GPU memory usage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MEND consistently outperforms both PromptTuning (Lester et al., 2021) and HyperTuning (Phang et al., 2023) across all three LLM architectures and all seven task partitions. Against PromptTuning, MEND gains +4.88 (GPT-2-large), +3.48 (GPT-2-XL), and +5.72 (T5-large) in average score, with the largest gap on T5 where PromptTuning actually underperforms zero-shot. Against HyperTuning, MEND achieves +1.90 (GPT-2-large), +2.51 (GPT-2-XL), and +4.70 (T5-large) average improvements, with the most pronounced advantage on T5 where HyperTuning degrades severely (e.g., 29.06 on non-Class→Class vs. MEND's 38.93). MEND also surpasses Vanilla ICL by +1.12 (GPT-2-large), +0.97 (GPT-2-XL), and +1.64 (T5-large) while using only 100 tokens of context versus 469–848 tokens for full demonstrations. The key differentiator is that MEND's knowledge distillation objective (KL divergence between teacher and student LLM outputs) aligns the distilled vectors with the LLM's internal representations, whereas HyperTuning relies on a conditional language modeling objective that produces suboptimal vectors for the downstream LLM.

## Relation to Prior Work

MEND extends the hypernetwork-based demonstration distillation line initiated by HyperTuning (Phang et al., 2023) and HINT (Ivison et al., 2022), which use conditional language modeling (CLM) to train a small LM to compress demonstrations into soft vectors. The key departure is replacing the CLM objective with a knowledge-distillation loss (KL divergence) that explicitly aligns the LLM's output distribution when conditioned on distilled vectors versus full demonstrations, treating the LLM itself as a frozen teacher. This reframes the distillation problem from 'predict next tokens from a compressed summary' to 'make the LLM behave identically whether it sees full or compressed context,' drawing on the knowledge distillation framework of Hinton et al. (2015) in a novel self-distillation configuration where the same LLM serves as both teacher and student.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MEND resolves a persistent trade-off in efficient ICL: prior distillation methods (HyperTuning, HINT) consistently underperformed Vanilla ICL, forcing practitioners to choose between context-length efficiency and prediction quality. By achieving parity or improvement over Vanilla ICL while reducing FLOPs by up to 75%, MEND makes compact demonstration representations practically viable for deployment. However, the approach requires training a separate MEND for each LLM architecture, relies on backpropagation through the frozen LLM (limiting scalability to very large models), and is constrained by the context window of both MEND and the LLM, leaving open the question of handling demonstrations that exceed these limits (e.g., K > 1000 examples).
