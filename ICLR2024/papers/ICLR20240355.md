## Problem

Existing video-language pre-training methods optimize global instance-level alignment between video clips and captions but neglect fine-grained local structures: region-object correspondences in the spatial dimension and scene/action changes along the temporal dimension. This lack of local discriminative modeling limits performance on downstream tasks requiring temporal localization and semantic reasoning, such as temporal action localization and video question answering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240355/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 32 frames at 224×224 resolution (processed by a 12-layer ViT-base with 2×16×16 temporal-spatial patches) and captions tokenized to 32 tokens (processed by BERT-base), with K=2 noun phrases extracted per caption via spaCy and prompted with 12 handcrafted templates. Videos are pre-processed with a cut-and-paste augmentation that pastes a foreground clip from one video onto a background video, creating an additional positive caption pair. The 256-dimensional video-language embeddings support retrieval, VQA (via a BUTD-style fusion head), action recognition (linear probing or fine-tuning), and temporal action localization (features fed to G-TAD).

## Method

S-ViLM is a dual-encoder framework trained with three simultaneous objectives: inter-clip spatial grounding, intra-clip temporal grouping, and global contrastive learning. For spatial grounding, 32 learnable group tokens are progressively updated by K-means attention grouping blocks inserted at layers 6, 9, and 12 of the video encoder, clustering semantically similar voxels into regions without off-the-shelf detectors; a grounding loss then aligns these region tokens with prompted noun tokens, encouraging each noun to be grounded to one or a few regions. For temporal grouping, the cut-and-paste operation provides a foreground-background mask as ground truth, and clip-level features are assigned to foreground or background cluster centers via cosine similarity, trained with an MSE loss. The cut-and-paste also introduces a secondary positive caption (the background video's caption), handled through weighted contrastive and grounding losses where weights are proportional to the foreground ratio. All three losses are summed with equal weights, and the entire model is pre-trained on VideoCC + ActivityNet (~3.3M pairs) for 10 epochs with batch size 1024 on TPUs.

## Results

On zero-shot text-video retrieval (MSR-VTT), S-ViLM achieves R@1=28.6, R@5=53.6, R@10=65.1, and MedR=5.0, surpassing the strongest baseline MCQ (R@10=56.4) by approximately 9 points. On UCF101 action recognition with linear probing, S-ViLM reaches 94.8% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the multi-modal MMV baseline (91.8%) by 3.0%. For temporal action localization on ActivityNet, S-ViLM achieves mAP@0.5=51.7 and average mAP=35.6, matching or exceeding fully supervised methods like TSP (mAP@0.5=51.3, Avg=35.8). On VQA, S-ViLM attains 43.5% on MSRVTT-QA and 46.4% on MSVD-QA, leading all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S-ViLM consistently outperforms all baselines across four downstream tasks. In zero-shot retrieval, it surpasses MCQ (the strongest prior method using WebVid-2M) by 9.7 points in R@10 (65.1 vs. 56.4) and 2.6 points in R@1 (28.6 vs. 26.0), despite using a smaller pre-training corpus (3.3M vs. 5.5M pairs). In fine-tuning retrieval, S-ViLM leads MCQ by 0.8 R@1 (38.4 vs. 37.6) and All-in-One by 1.3 R@1 (38.4 vs. 37.1). For action recognition, S-ViLM (text-only modality) beats the multi-modal MMV baseline (audio+text) by 3.0% on UCF101 linear probing (94.8 vs. 91.8) and 2.9% on HMDB51 (70.0 vs. 67.1), demonstrating that fine-grained video-language structure learning compensates for the absence of additional modalities. On VQA, S-ViLM edges out ALPRO by 1.4% on MSRVTT-QA (43.5 vs. 42.1) and 0.5% on MSVD-QA (46.4 vs. 45.9). For temporal action localization, S-ViLM achieves mAP@0.5=51.7, marginally exceeding TSP (51.3) and BSP (50.9), though TSP slightly leads on average mAP (35.8 vs. 35.6), indicating near-parity on this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

S-ViLM extends the dual-encoder video-language pre-training paradigm by incorporating two structural inductive biases absent from prior methods: learnable group tokens (borrowed from GroupViT and K-means Mask Transformer) for self-supervised region clustering, and a cut-and-paste augmentation (inspired by CutMix and PAL) to synthesize temporal scene changes in short clips. Unlike ALPRO and DemoVLP, which rely on pre-trained object detectors to extract visual regions, S-ViLM clusters regions entirely in a self-supervised manner via shared group tokens, avoiding detector artifacts. The spatial grounding loss adapts the image-domain grounding objective from OpenSeg to video, while the temporal grouping loss is a novel contribution to the video-language pre-training literature, where prior temporal modeling work (TSP, PAL, BSP) targeted long-form videos with natural scene transitions rather than the short, repetitive clips typical of video-language datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that exploiting fine-grained spatiotemporal structure during pre-training yields consistent gains across both multimodal (retrieval, VQA) and single-modal (action recognition, TAL) tasks, suggesting that structured video-language alignment produces more transferable representations. The data efficiency is notable: S-ViLM trained on 3.3M pairs outperforms methods trained on 120M pairs, implying that structural inductive biases can partially compensate for data scale. Limitations include evaluation on a single ViT-base architecture, a relatively small pre-training corpus, and TAL results that trail the best self-supervised baseline (TSP) by a narrow margin. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models and longer videos, and whether the specific hyperparameters (32 group tokens, 3 grouping blocks, K=2 noun phrases) generalize across domains.
