## Problem

Existing referring expression comprehension (REC) datasets contain only 1–2 manually annotated phrases per image, which is far too sparse to capture the full range of user detection intentions such as describing objects by color, shape, location, relationships, or detecting multiple objects in various combinations. This leaves a practical gap where visual grounding models cannot fulfill diverse, real-world user instructions. The paper addresses this from a data-centric perspective by automatically generating massively diversified instructions using foundation models rather than relying on manual annotation.

## Input / Output

The InstructDET pipeline takes an RGB image with pre-annotated object bounding boxes (from MSCOCO, Flickr30K, or Objects365) and produces 3.6M diversified text instructions across 120.6K images, organized into 6 complexity groups from simple category names to multi-object commonality generalizations. The resulting DROD model accepts an RGB image paired with a free-form text instruction and outputs an arbitrary number of object bounding boxes (0, 1, or many), determined by a confidence threshold on cosine-similarity matching scores rather than a fixed detection count.

## Method

InstructDET generates instructions through two complementary pipelines: a global prompt pipeline that converts images to text via LLaVA and feeds the description plus bbox coordinates into LLaMA with in-context examples, and a local prompt pipeline that sends bbox-marked images directly to a partially finetuned LLaVA (only the Q-Former-to-text linear layer is updated on REC data) to produce object-focused descriptions. Generated expressions are filtered using CLIP-based visual-textual matching with a composite score S_f = S_l − α₁S_g that penalizes global-image descriptions in favor of local-object referring, where visual prompting (red ellipse + Gaussian blur reversion via SAM) emphasizes the target region. For multi-object instructions, concatenated per-object expressions are embedded with BERT, clustered via DBSCAN, and summarized by LLaMA into commonality descriptions. The DROD detection model uses a Deformable DETR encoder-decoder with bidirectional cross-attention for visual-textual fusion, where N learnable queries retrieve instance proposals that are matched to the instruction via cosine similarity, enabling flexible output of any number of bounding boxes.

## Results

On the InDET test set, DROD with ResNet50 achieves 62.24% AP (53.78% on shuffled instructions), substantially outperforming UNINEXT (43.37%/37.61%), Grounding-DINO (35.96%/30.43%), and MDETR (34.86%/31.21%). With a ViT-H backbone, DROD further improves to 66.90% AP. On standard RefCOCO/g/+ benchmarks, DROD (ResNet50) achieves 88.92/90.86/85.57 AP on RefCOCO val/testA/testB, 78.27/83.39/71.04 on RefCOCO+, and 83.01/82.91 on RefCOCOg, surpassing all compared baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DROD (ResNet50) achieves 62.24% AP on InDET, outperforming UNINEXT (43.37%) by 18.87 points, Grounding-DINO (35.96%) by 26.28 points, and MDETR (34.86%) by 27.38 points. The shuffled-instruction experiment confirms DROD genuinely comprehends instruction semantics rather than relying on keywords: its 8.46-point drop (62.24→53.78) is larger than UNINEXT's 5.76-point drop, indicating deeper semantic understanding. On RefCOCO/g/+, DROD (ResNet50) beats UNINEXT by 1.28/0.51/2.08 AP on RefCOCO val/testA/testB, by 0.13/0.17/2.33 on RefCOCO+, and by 2.05/1.05 on RefCOCOg. DROD also surpasses MDETR and Grounding-DINO across all RefCOCO splits, with the largest margin over G-DINO on RefCOCO+ testB (71.04 vs. 62.96, +8.08). The advantage is most pronounced on multi-object groups G5 and G6 (70.15 and 74.22 AP vs. 31.41 and 32.01 for UNINEXT), where the diversified multi-object instructions in InDET provide the strongest benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the referring expression comprehension (REC) and visual grounding literature by reframing the task as diversified referring object detection (ROD) that handles multi-object references and abstract descriptions, going beyond the single-object, discriminative phrasing of RefCOCO-style datasets. Methodologically, it departs from the dominant paradigm of improving model architectures (e.g., DETR variants like MDETR, Grounding-DINO, UNINEXT) and instead pursues a data-centric strategy: leveraging foundation models (LLaVA, LLaMA) with in-context learning and partial fine-tuning to automatically generate human-like detection instructions at scale. The expression filtering via CLIP with visual prompting (red ellipse + Gaussian blur reversion) draws on recent visual language pretraining work, while the multi-object commonality mining via DBSCAN clustering and LLM summarization is a novel pipeline not seen in prior REC dataset construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

InDET (3.6M instructions over 120.6K images) becomes the largest real-world REC dataset, and the demonstrated ~19-point AP gap over the strongest baseline (UNINEXT) on the InDET test set suggests that instruction diversity, not architectural novelty, is the primary bottleneck for practical visual grounding. The data-centric pipeline is scalable to any image with object bounding boxes, offering a reusable recipe for expanding training data with foundation models. However, the most impressive results are on the authors' own benchmark, standard RefCOCO improvements are more modest (~1-2 points), and the approach inherits hallucination risks from LLM/VLM generation that are only partially mitigated by CLIP-based filtering. Open questions include whether this data expansion strategy transfers to open-set or in-the-wild detection, and whether the DROD model can approach the generalization of the very foundation models used to generate its training data.
