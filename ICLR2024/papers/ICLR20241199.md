## Problem

Existing deep RL policies for online game level generation use greedy or unimodal stochastic policies that cannot represent complex, diverse decision distributions, leading to highly similar generated levels that bore players. Moreover, standard reward functions evaluate single actions and cannot express diversity, which is a property of the entire trajectory distribution in the MDP. No prior work has addressed the limited diversity of levels generated online by RL policies.

## Input / Output

The agent observes a state formed by concatenating a fixed number of d-dimensional latent vectors from recently generated level segments (zero-padded when fewer segments exist), yielding a 200-dim state for MarioPuzzle (10 segments) or 100-dim for MultiFacet (5 segments). The policy outputs a continuous d-dimensional latent action vector, which is decoded by a pre-trained GAN into a concrete game level segment. The ensemble policy additionally outputs a mixture weight vector β over m sub-policies, and the final action is sampled from the stochastically selected sub-policy's Gaussian distribution.

## Method

NCERL employs a multimodal ensemble policy consisting of m individual actor MLPs (each parameterising a spherical Gaussian) and a selector MLP (outputting mixture weights), with a novel negative correlation regularisation that maximises the expected 2-Wasserstein distance between pairs of sub-policy decision distributions, weighted by the selector and bounded by a down-clip to prevent degenerate far-apart clusters. The paper derives two theorems—regularised policy iteration and the policy-regularisation gradient (PRG)—that provide the theoretical foundation for optimising this distribution-level regularisation within an MDP, extending soft policy iteration and standard policy gradient to general regularisation. The implementation builds on SAC with separate Q_ρ critics trained via Bellman residuals for the regularisation term, and the actor loss combines the regularisation objective with the regularised soft Q-value. An asynchronous off-policy training framework decouples model updates from time-consuming reward evaluation (which requires simulating a game-playing agent on complete levels), yielding a 22.9% speedup over the prior synchronous framework and a 596% speedup over single-process training.

## Results

On MarioPuzzle, NCERL (m=5, λ=0.2) achieves the best G-mean of 322.7, outperforming PMOE (279.4), SUNRISE (243.8), and SAC (186.1), while also attaining the highest diversity score of 1940 (vs. 1714 for PMOE and 984.8 for SUNRISE). On MultiFacet, NCERL (m=5, λ=0.5) achieves the best G-mean of 199.0 and highest diversity of 1142, compared to PMOE (G-mean 181.7, diversity 973.9) and SUNRISE (G-mean 134.1, diversity 388.6). Across both tasks, NCERL's reward remains competitive (within ~5–10% of the best single-policy baselines) while diversity improves by 2–5× over non-ensemble methods. Varying λ from 0.0 to 0.5 produces a well-spread Pareto front, with diversity increasing monotonically while reward decreases modestly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NCERL outperforms all five baselines on G-mean (the unified reward-diversity metric) on both tasks: on MarioPuzzle, NCERL (λ=0.2, G-mean 322.7) beats PMOE (279.4) by 15%, SUNRISE (243.8) by 32%, DvD (210.6) by 53%, and SAC (186.1) by 73%; on MultiFacet, NCERL (λ=0.5, G-mean 199.0) beats PMOE (181.7) by 10% and SUNRISE (134.1) by 48%. In terms of diversity alone, NCERL achieves the highest score on both tasks (1967 on MarioPuzzle, 1142 on MultiFacet), exceeding PMOE by 14% and 17% respectively, and SUNRISE by 99% and 194%. However, NCERL does not always win on raw reward: SUNRISE achieves the highest reward on MarioPuzzle (60.43 vs. NCERL's 53.26–55.24) and MultiFacet (46.53 vs. NCERL's 35.77–46.39), and also attains the best Avg-rank on MarioPuzzle (4.28 vs. NCERL's 4.88). PMOE is the closest competitor, showing competitive diversity (1714 on MarioPuzzle) but with much higher variance (±11.1 reward std) and lower G-mean. The asynchronous framework (ASAC) matches standard SAC in reward while being 22.9% faster than the synchronous EGSAC framework and 596% faster than single-process SAC, confirming the framework's practical utility without degrading performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the EDRL framework for online level generation by introducing a multimodal ensemble policy (building on PMOE's Gaussian mixture approach) with a novel negative correlation regularisation inspired by classical ensemble learning (Liu & Yao, 1999). The theoretical core generalises soft policy iteration and policy gradient from entropy regularisation (SAC) to arbitrary decision-distribution regularisation, deriving new theorems (regularised policy iteration and the Policy-Regularisation Gradient) that have no direct precedent in the RL literature. The GAN-based decoder for mapping latent actions to level segments follows prior PCG-via-RL work, while the asynchronous off-policy training framework is a practical contribution distinct from existing synchronous or on-policy asynchronous designs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to explicitly address the diversity limitation of online RL-based level generation, providing both a practical method (NCERL) and general theoretical tools (PRG, regularised policy iteration) applicable to any MDP with distribution-level regularisation. The Pareto front of generators parameterised by λ enables explicit reward-diversity trade-offs, which is practically useful for game designers. Limitations include notable training instability across trials (especially for small ensemble sizes), evaluation restricted to the Mario benchmark, and the gap between the Wasserstein-distance regularisation and the actual Hamming-distance diversity metric. Open questions include whether the theoretical framework extends to multi-objective RL for training non-dominated generator sets in a single run, and whether the instability can be mitigated.
