## Problem

Learning near-optimal policies in POMDPs requires exponentially many samples in the worst case, and existing polynomial-sample results rely on strong structural assumptions like linearly independent emission columns (revealing POMDPs). This paper asks whether an enhanced feedback model—where the learner receives k additional observations from each visited latent state after each episode—can enable sample-efficient learning over a strictly broader class of POMDPs, and what the minimal structural condition is for such learning to be possible.

## Input / Output

The learner interacts with a finite-horizon POMDP and, after each episode, receives k−1 additional i.i.d. observations from each latent state visited during that episode (the k-MOMDP feedback), but never observes the latent states themselves. The k-OMLE algorithm takes a model class Θ of POMDPs and outputs a near-optimal policy; the OST algorithm additionally requires a POMDP planning oracle POP and outputs pseudo-state assignments alongside the estimated transition and emission models. Sample counts include all k observations per step per episode, so each round costs kH samples.

## Method

The paper introduces two learnable POMDP subclasses under k-MOMDP feedback: (1) k-MO-revealing POMDPs, where the k-fold tensor of the emission matrix O⊗k admits a well-conditioned left inverse, learned via k-OMLE—an adaptation of optimistic MLE that casts the problem as learning an augmented POMDP with a restricted single-observation policy class; and (2) distinguishable POMDPs, which only require distinct (not linearly independent) emission columns, learned via the OST algorithm. OST uses a closeness-testing subroutine (adapted from Chan et al., 2013) to cluster k-observation sequences into pseudo-states up to permutation, then applies HOMDP-style optimistic model estimation with a planning oracle. Theoretically, any α-distinguishable POMDP is k-MO-revealing for k = Õ(√O·log(SO)/α²), establishing a reverse containment, and OST achieves a sharper sample complexity than the reduction to k-OMLE by explicitly leveraging distribution testing in its design.

## Results

The paper provides theoretical sample complexity guarantees rather than empirical benchmarks. For (α,k)-MO-revealing tabular POMDPs, k-OMLE achieves Õ(poly(H)·kSA(S²A+SO)/(α²ε²)) samples (Theorem 6). For distinguishable POMDPs, the OST algorithm achieves the sharper bound Õ(poly(H)·(SO/ε² + SA√O/(ε²α²) + SAO^{2/3}/(ε²α^{4/3}))) samples (Theorem 11), improving over the reduction-based k-OMLE bound of Õ(poly(H)·SA√O(S²A+SO)/(α²ε²)). A lower bound (Proposition 1) shows that no finite k suffices to make all tabular POMDPs polynomially learnable under k-MOMDP feedback, in contrast to the hindsight observable setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between two approaches for learning distinguishable POMDPs: the reduction to k-MO-revealing (using k-OMLE) versus the direct OST algorithm. OST achieves a strictly sharper sample complexity, with all three terms in its bound being smaller than the S²A·O^{1.5}/(α²ε²) term in the reduction approach (ignoring H factors). The paper also contrasts k-MOMDP with the hindsight observable setting of Lee et al. (2023): while hindsight observability makes *any* tabular POMDP polynomially learnable, Proposition 1 shows that k-MOMDP feedback (for any finite k) still leaves some POMDPs (e.g., the combination lock) requiring exponentially many samples. Compared to standard revealing POMDPs (the k=1 special case), the k-MO-revealing class is a strict superset (Proposition 3), and distinguishable POMDPs only require distinct emission columns rather than linearly independent ones, making them a substantially broader class. The OST algorithm is computationally efficient given a POMDP planning oracle, with all other operations taking poly(H,S,A,O,T,k) time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself between two existing lines of work on tractable POMDP learning: structural conditions (revealing POMDPs, B-stability) and enhanced feedback models (HOMDPs of Lee et al., 2023). The k-MOMDP feedback model is a conceptual weakening of HOMDPs—latent states are not directly revealed but only 'approximately' revealed through multiple noisy observations—while k-MO-revealing POMDPs generalize revealing POMDPs (the k=1 case) by exploiting the k-fold tensor product of emission matrices. The k-OMLE algorithm adapts the OMLE framework of Chen et al. (2022a) to an augmented POMDP, while the OST algorithm explicitly imports closeness-testing subroutines from the distribution testing literature (Chan et al., 2013; Batu et al., 2013) to recover latent state identities up to permutation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that the linearly-independent-columns requirement of revealing POMDPs can be relaxed to mere distinctness of emission distributions (distinguishability) when multiple observations are available, which is a meaningful broadening of the class of efficiently learnable POMDPs. However, Proposition 1 shows k-MOMDP does not fully resolve worst-case hardness (unlike HOMDPs), so structural assumptions remain necessary. Open questions include lower bounds matching the upper bounds, extending distinguishable POMDPs to function approximation, and whether the k-MOMDP model can be further weakened (e.g., partial hindsight) while retaining polynomial sample complexity.
