## Problem

Denoising diffusion models learn complex image-text relationships, but the nature of these relationships is opaque: it is unclear which words actually influence which parts of a generated image, or whether a prompt intervention will have any effect. Existing attention-based interpretability methods require white-box architectural access, are tied to specific network designs, and do not guarantee that attended inputs actually affect the output. The paper asks whether information theory can provide a principled, architecture-agnostic decomposition of the relationships a diffusion model has learned.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v2.1) and an image-text pair, where the image is represented in the autoencoder's latent space and the text prompt is split into a target word y* and remaining context c. It produces three types of quantities: a scalar pointwise mutual information score i(x;y) per image-text pair, a pixel-wise information heatmap i_j(x;y*|c) assigning a non-negative information value to each latent-space pixel for a given word, and a conditional mutual information score that quantifies the additional information a word contributes beyond the rest of the prompt. All quantities are computed by integrating the denoiser's squared error over the noise level α, estimated via importance sampling with a truncated logistic distribution.

## Method

The core insight is that the MMSE denoiser trained by a diffusion model yields an exact expression for the log-density: -log p(x) = ½∫ E[‖ε − ε̂_α(x_α)‖²] dα + const, and the analogous conditional expression with ε̂_α(x_α|y). Taking the difference gives the log-likelihood ratio, which is the pointwise mutual information. Two estimators are derived: a standard variant i^s with higher variance, and an orthogonality-based variant i^o that is always non-negative and lower variance. Because the squared error decomposes additively across variables, the pointwise information decomposes into a sum of per-pixel (per-variable) terms, yielding a natural non-negative pixel-wise information decomposition without requiring the intractable partial information decomposition framework. For conditional mutual information, all denoisers are simply conditioned on the context c. No additional training is needed; the pretrained denoiser is used directly, and the integral over α is evaluated numerically via importance sampling.

## Results

On the ARO benchmark for compositional understanding, the proposed information-based score (Info.) achieves 72.0% accuracy on VG-A, 69.1% on VG-R, 40.1% on COCO, and 49.3% on Flickr30k, outperforming all baselines across every dataset. The largest margin is on VG-R (relation understanding), where Info. scores 69.1% versus 52.3% for the strongest diffusion-based baseline (HardNeg-DiffITM) and 51.4% for OpenCLIP. For prompt intervention prediction, CMI achieves a Pearson correlation of 0.34 with image-level L2 change versus 0.24 for attention, and 0.27 vs 0.21 at the pixel level. For unsupervised object segmentation (mIoU on COCO-IT), CMI alone (33.63% at 200 steps) is slightly below DAAM attention (35.35%), but combining both (Attention+Information) reaches 42.84%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three baselines on the ARO benchmark. Against OpenCLIP (a contrastive VLM using cosine similarity as alignment score), the information-based method wins on all four datasets, with the most dramatic gain on VG-R (69.1% vs 51.4%, a +17.7 pp improvement), suggesting diffusion models' denoising objective captures compositional structure that contrastive pre-training misses. Against DiffITM (Krojer et al., which uses negative MMSE as the alignment score), Info. outperforms by 9.1 pp on VG-A, 19.1 pp on VG-R, 16.6 pp on COCO, and 16.1 pp on Flickr30k, demonstrating that the full information-theoretic estimator is substantially more discriminative than raw denoising error. Against HardNeg-DiffITM (which adds compositional-aware hard negative fine-tuning), Info. still leads on all datasets (72.0 vs 67.6 on VG-A, 69.1 vs 52.3 on VG-R, 40.1 vs 34.4 on COCO, 49.3 vs 48.6 on Flickr30k), indicating that the information decomposition itself provides a stronger signal than architectural fine-tuning for compositional alignment. The authors note that while these gains are significant, they remain smaller than those achieved by contrastive pre-training with composition-aware negatives, suggesting the information estimator is a complementary rather than replacement signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the exact I(X;X_α)–MMSE identity from Guo et al. (2005) and the information-theoretic diffusion framework of Kong et al. (2022) to derive pointwise and pixel-wise mutual information estimators directly from a pretrained denoiser, without solving SDEs or optimizing MI bounds. It departs from the dominant attention-based interpretability paradigm (DAAM, Diffuse-Attend-Segment, etc.) by offering an architecture-agnostic, black-box-compatible measure of text-image dependence, and from prior diffusion-based image-text matching (Krojer et al., 2023, which uses negative MMSE) by showing that the full information-theoretic decomposition yields substantially better alignment scores. The pixel-wise non-negative decomposition is inspired by Williams & Beer's partial information decomposition but sidesteps the intractability of separating unique, redundant, and synergistic components by exploiting the additive structure of squared-error terms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, model-agnostic tool for probing what a diffusion model has actually learned at the level of individual pixels and words, which is valuable for auditing, debugging, and building trust in generative systems. The finding that diffusion models' compositional understanding was significantly underestimated by prior metrics (MMSE, attention aggregation) shifts how the field should evaluate these models. Open questions include whether the decomposition can be extended to explicitly separate redundant and synergistic information, whether it scales to video or 3D generation, and whether CMI can serve as a metric for identifying functional 'circuits' in diffusion architectures as mechanistic interpretability research expands beyond language models.
