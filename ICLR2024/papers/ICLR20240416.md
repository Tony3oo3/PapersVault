## Problem

Existing Transformer-based time series forecasting models operate at limited or fixed temporal scales, making it difficult to capture characteristics spanning multiple scales. Two specific gaps limit effective multi-scale modeling: (1) incompleteness, where prior methods address either temporal resolution (patch size) or temporal distance (local vs. global dependencies) but not both simultaneously, and (2) rigidity, where fixed scale configurations are applied uniformly to all data rather than adapting to the specific temporal dynamics of each input series.

## Input / Output

The model accepts multivariate or univariate time series of length H (H=96 for most datasets, H=36 for ILI) with d feature dimensions, and outputs future values at prediction horizons F ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720} (or F ∈ {24, 48, 96, 192} for Cloud Cluster, F ∈ {24, 36, 48, 60} for ILI). The input is first passed through Instance Normalization to handle distribution shift, then processed through 3 stacked Adaptive Multi-Scale Blocks before a fully connected predictor produces the forecast. Each variable in multivariate inputs is handled independently (channel independence).

## Method

Pathformer introduces a Multi-Scale Transformer Block that divides the input into patches of M different sizes (drawn from a pool of {2,3,6,12,16,24,32}) and applies dual attention: intra-patch attention (cross-attention with a trainable query per patch) captures local details within each patch, while inter-patch attention (self-attention over flattened patch embeddings) captures global correlations across patches. On top of this, Adaptive Pathways implement a Mixture-of-Experts-style routing mechanism: a multi-scale router performs temporal decomposition (DFT-based seasonality extraction plus multi-kernel average pooling for trend) on the input, then generates pathway weights via a softmax with noise to select the top-K most relevant patch sizes for the current sample. A multi-scale aggregator then performs weighted aggregation of the Transformer outputs from only the selected scales, with a transformation function aligning temporal dimensions across scales. This layer-by-layer routing and aggregation enables data-adaptive multi-scale modeling without manual scale tuning.

## Results

On 11 real-world datasets (ETT, Weather, Electricity, Traffic, ILI, and three Cloud Cluster datasets), Pathformer achieves the best MSE and MAE in 81 out of 88 evaluation cases. Compared to the strongest Transformer baseline PatchTST, Pathformer reduces MSE by 8.1% and MAE by 6.4% on average. Against multi-scale baselines (Pyraformer, Scaleformer), the improvement is substantially larger: 36.4% MSE reduction and 19.1% MAE reduction. In transfer learning, Pathformer's part-tuning strategy reduces training time by 52% while maintaining accuracy comparable to full-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pathformer outperforms all eight baselines across the 88 evaluation cases (81 best, 5 second-best). Against PatchTST (the strongest Transformer baseline), Pathformer achieves an 8.1% MSE and 6.4% MAE reduction, with the gap widening on long prediction horizons (e.g., ETTh2 F=720: 0.398 vs. 0.412 MSE). Against the linear model NLinear, Pathformer wins comprehensively, with particularly large margins on high-dimensional datasets like Electricity (0.145 vs. 0.185 MSE at F=96) and Traffic (0.479 vs. 0.645 MSE at F=96). Against multi-scale baselines Scaleformer and Pyraformer, the advantage is most pronounced: 36.4% MSE and 19.1% MAE reduction on average, with Scaleformer struggling severely on Traffic (MSE 2.678 vs. Pathformer's 0.479 at F=96). Against FEDformer and Autoformer, Pathformer consistently leads, with FEDformer showing the largest gaps on Weather (0.156 vs. 0.238 MSE at F=96) and Autoformer on ETTh1 (0.382 vs. 0.449 MSE at F=96). In transfer learning, Pathformer's part-tuning (fine-tuning only the router) matches full-tuning accuracy while reducing training time by 52%, and outperforms full-tuning of PatchTST, FEDformer, and Autoformer on most datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

Pathformer extends PatchTST's single-scale patching to a multi-scale setting by dividing time series with multiple patch sizes and introducing dual attention (intra-patch for local details, inter-patch for global correlations), unifying temporal resolution and temporal distance in a single Transformer block. It departs from prior multi-scale time series methods (N-HiTS, Pyraformer, Scaleformer) that use fixed scales by borrowing the Mixture-of-Experts routing paradigm (Shazeer et al., 2016; Dean, 2021) to adaptively select which patch sizes to activate per sample, with a temporal decomposition module (DFT + multi-kernel moving averages) informing the router. The design also responds to the 'linear models suffice' critique (Zeng et al., 2023) by demonstrating that Transformers with proper multi-scale adaptation can outperform both linear baselines and prior Transformer variants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Pathformer is the first to introduce adaptive multi-scale modeling for time series forecasting, addressing the practical problem that different series require different temporal scales and that manually tuning scales is intractable. The part-tuning transfer learning strategy (fine-tuning only the router) reducing training time by 52% while matching full-tuning accuracy offers a practical pathway for deploying forecasting models across heterogeneous datasets. Open questions include whether the adaptive routing generalizes to extremely long sequences or non-stationary regimes beyond the tested benchmarks, and whether the top-K sparsity constraint is the right inductive bias versus learned continuous mixtures. The reliance on a fixed pool of patch sizes {2,3,6,12,16,24,32} also limits flexibility for series with unusual periodicities.
