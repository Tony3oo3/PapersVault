## Problem

Pre-trained diffusion models such as Stable Diffusion produce severe object repetition and structurally unreasonable images when sampled at resolutions beyond their training size (e.g., 1024² from a 512² model). Existing tuning-free approaches—attention scaling, Multi-Diffusion, and SyncDiffusion—fail to eliminate this repetition. The paper identifies the root cause as the limited receptive field of convolutional kernels in the U-Net, rather than the attention token count previously hypothesized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241476/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a pre-trained Stable Diffusion U-Net (SD 1.5, SD 2.1, or SD XL 1.0) as input, and directly produces images at arbitrary resolutions up to 16× the training resolution (e.g., 4096×4096 from a 1024²-trained model) and arbitrary aspect ratios (e.g., 1:2), all without any training, fine-tuning, or optimization. It also extends to a text-to-video model (LVDM) for 2× higher-resolution video generation. The only additional requirement is a pre-defined dilation schedule specifying which U-Net blocks and timesteps receive re-dilation or dispersed convolution.

## Method

The core insight is that the convolutional receptive field, not attention, governs structural correctness at higher resolutions. The paper proposes three complementary, training-free mechanisms: (1) **Re-dilation**, which dynamically inserts spaces into pre-trained convolution kernels at inference time to scale the perception field proportionally to the resolution increase, with fractional dilation supported by stretching the input feature map via bilinear interpolation before applying an integer dilation; the dilation factor is scheduled to decrease from deep to shallow layers and from noisier to less noisy timesteps. (2) **Dispersed convolution**, which enlarges a pre-trained r×r kernel to r′×r′ via a linear transform R solved by a least-squares objective combining structure-level calibration (output consistency under feature interpolation) and pixel-level calibration (local behavior preservation), eliminating the periodic sub-sampling artifacts of pure dilation at extreme scales. (3) **Noise-damped classifier-free guidance**, which linearly combines a strong-denoising base model ε_θ with a re-dilated/dispersed model ε̃_θ, exploiting the observation that the erroneous noise predictions in ε̃_θ(x_t, y) and ε̃_θ(x_t) are highly correlated and thus cancel in their difference, leaving only useful structural guidance.

## Results

On SD 2.1 at 16× resolution (2048²), ScaleCrafter achieves FIDr of 59.40, reducing the Direct-Inf baseline (104.70) by 43% and Attn-SF (104.34) by 43%. At the more moderate 4× setting (1024²), the method reaches FIDr 20.88 on SD 2.1 versus 29.89 (Direct-Inf) and 28.95 (Attn-SF). In a user preference study on 16× texture definition, ScaleCrafter is preferred over the trained SD-SR upscaler 62% vs 38%, while also being faster (5.6s vs 9.5s per image) and using fewer parameters (1.3B vs 2.2B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Attn-SF (Jin et al., 2023), which scales self-attention features by resolution, ScaleCrafter wins decisively on every metric and setting: Attn-SF barely moves the needle (e.g., 110.10 vs 111.34 FIDr at 16× on SD 1.5), confirming that attention token count is not the bottleneck, whereas re-dilation of convolutional kernels is. Against Direct-Inf (vanilla sampling at higher resolution), the gap widens dramatically with resolution: at 4× the FIDr improvement is ~9 points on SD 2.1, but at 16× it reaches ~45 points, showing the method is most impactful where repetition is worst. Against the trained SD-SR upscaler (Stability AI 4× upscaler), ScaleCrafter has slightly higher FID/KID (20.88 vs 12.59 at 4×) but wins the texture-definition user study 62% to 38% at 16×, runs 41% faster (5.6s vs 9.5s), and uses 41% fewer parameters (1.3B vs 2.2B), all without any additional training data or optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Stable Diffusion U-Net architecture and its latent diffusion framework, but departs sharply from prior higher-resolution generation work in its diagnosis of the failure mode. While Jin et al. (2023) attributed object repetition to attention token quantity and proposed attention scaling, and Multi-Diffusion/SyncDiffusion focused on window-based feature fusion, this paper identifies the limited convolutional receptive field as the root cause through systematic ablation of U-Net components. The proposed re-dilation, dispersed convolution, and noise-damped classifier-free guidance are all inference-time modifications to the convolutional pathway, requiring no retraining or additional data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a pre-trained low-resolution diffusion model can be directly repurposed for 16× higher-resolution generation (4096×4096) without any tuning has immediate practical value for applications demanding 4K+ imagery. The conceptual reframing—convolution receptive field rather than attention as the bottleneck—opens a new diagnostic lens for understanding how spatial inductive biases interact with resolution scaling in generative models. Limitations remain in local structural fidelity (e.g., fingers, thin limbs), which the authors note is inherited from the base model, and the approach has only been validated on U-Net-based diffusion architectures, leaving open whether similar receptive-field interventions apply to transformer-based diffusion models.
