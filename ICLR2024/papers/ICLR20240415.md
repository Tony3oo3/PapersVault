## Problem

Prior work has established where relational knowledge is stored in transformer LMs (enriched subject representations, MLP key-value stores, specialized attention heads), but has not characterized what computation the model performs to decode a relation from a subject representation into an object prediction. This paper asks whether that decoding computation is well-approximated by a simple linear (affine) transformation, and if so, whether such a transformation can be extracted directly from the model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240415/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the hidden state of the last subject token at a chosen intermediate layer ℓ_r (after the model has enriched it with relevant facts) together with a relation-specific prompt context (e.g., 'plays the'). It produces an affine operator (W_r, b_r) that maps any subject representation to the corresponding object representation, which is then decoded via the LM's unembedding head into a token distribution. The attribute lens variant applies this operator at every layer to yield a per-layer object-token distribution for a given relation, revealing when and where the model has resolved the relation internally.

## Method

The core insight is that the transformer's relation-decoding function F(s, c) can be approximated by a first-order Taylor expansion around a reference subject, yielding an affine map LRE(s) = βW_r·s + b_r. The projection W_r is estimated as the mean Jacobian ∂F/∂s over n=8 in-context examples of the same relation, and the bias b_r as the mean residual F(s,c) − W·s; a scalar β>1 corrects for systematic underestimation of slope magnitude caused by layer normalization. The approach is validated with two complementary metrics: faithfulness (does LRE(s) produce the same next-token prediction as the full LM?) and causality (does inverting LRE to compute an edit direction Δs = W_r†(o′−o) and patching s+Δs into the model actually change its prediction to the target object o′?). Across 47 relations in GPT-J, GPT-2-XL, and LLaMA-13B, roughly half exhibit robust LREs with >60% faithfulness, while others (e.g., company CEO) are accurately predicted by the LM but resist any linear approximation, revealing a heterogeneous deployment of linear vs. non-linear decoding strategies.

## Results

Across 47 relations tested on GPT-J, LREs achieve over 60% faithfulness for approximately 48% of relations, with top-performing relations (e.g., adjective comparative, verb past tense, country capital city) reaching 88–98% faithfulness and 94–99% causality. Faithfulness and causality are strongly correlated (R = 0.84) when hyperparameters are selected for best causal influence. The attribute lens application reveals latent knowledge in 54–63% of adversarially distracted prompts (R@1), compared to only 2–3% correct outputs from the LM itself. Results generalize across model sizes: GPT-2-XL (β=2.25, R=0.85) and LLaMA-13B (β=8.0, R=0.83) show consistent faithfulness–causality correlations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares LRE against four alternative linear approximations in Figure 4. LRE (Jacobian-based, applied to enriched subject representations) consistently outperforms all baselines across every relation category. The Logit Lens / Identity baseline (nostalgebraist, 2020), which decodes the subject hidden state directly with the LM decoder head, achieves substantially lower faithfulness than LRE, confirming that a non-trivial projection W is needed. The Translation baseline (s + b, inspired by Merullo et al., 2023 and word2vec-style arithmetic) also underperforms LRE, demonstrating that the linear projection term W is necessary beyond a simple offset. A linear regression model trained on the same n=8 examples is outperformed by the Jacobian-estimated LRE, and applying LRE to the raw subject embedding e_s (before enrichment layers) yields much lower faithfulness, highlighting the importance of the intermediate-layer enrichment process. Notably, for relations like Company CEO (faithfulness ≤6%) and Task done by tool, no linear method—including LRE—can capture the LM's decoding, indicating genuinely non-linear computation for those relations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the linear relational embedding (LRE) scheme from Paccanaro & Hinton (2001) into the transformer LM setting, showing that a first-order Taylor approximation of the LM's internal computation (via the Jacobian) recovers an affine map from subject to object representations. It builds on prior work that located relational knowledge in subject representations and MLP key-value stores (Geva et al., 2020/2023; Li et al., 2021; Meng et al., 2022) but departs from probing classifiers by extracting the linear operator directly from the model's own derivatives rather than training an external probe. The attribute lens application generalizes the Logit Lens (nostalgebraist, 2020) and linear shortcut methods (Belrose et al., 2023; Din et al., 2023) by applying a relation-specific affine transformation before decoding, revealing attribute-specific knowledge rather than just next-token predictions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key finding—that roughly half of tested relations are linearly decodable while others (e.g., Company CEO) are accurately predicted yet resist any linear approximation—reveals a heterogeneous knowledge representation strategy that challenges the assumption that correct prediction implies linearly accessible internal knowledge. The attribute lens provides a practical diagnostic tool for visualizing when and where specific relational knowledge is encoded, and for detecting cases where an LM's output contradicts its own internal representations (as shown with repetition-distracted prompts). Open questions include why certain relations (particularly those with large name-based ranges) resist linear encoding, whether the 47-relation sample generalizes to the full space of factual knowledge, and whether LRE-based interventions can be leveraged for targeted bias mitigation beyond the superficial representation-level edits demonstrated here.
