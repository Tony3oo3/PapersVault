## Problem

Existing meta-RL algorithms rely on model-free RL, making them sample-inefficient, and have been demonstrated primarily on low-dimensional task distributions. The paper asks whether the Dreamer world-model architecture, which excels at POMDPs (a generalization of meta-RL), can be adapted to meta-RL to achieve both higher returns and dramatically better sample efficiency (up to 15×) with minimal hyperparameter tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240743/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives environment observations (continuous state vectors or 84×84×4 RGB images for visual domains) which are augmented with the scalar reward and the current time step index to form the augmented observation o'_t = [o_t, r_t, t]. This augmentation is critical: the reward helps identify the sampled MDP, and the time step allows the RSSM to model the episodic reset every T steps. The output is a single action per time step, produced by a policy conditioned on the RSSM latent state z_t, with no additional planning at test time.

## Method

MAMBA builds on DreamerV3's RSSM world model and latent imagination policy, introducing three modifications tailored to meta-RL. First, observations are augmented with reward and time step. Second, instead of Dreamer's random sub-trajectory sampling (L=64 transitions), MAMBA feeds the entire meta-episode τ into the RSSM to obtain z_{1:H}, preserving all information about MDP identity across sub-episodes; optimization then proceeds by sampling L random indices from the full latent sequence to keep computational cost comparable. Third, a horizon schedule linearly increases the world-model rollout length from a short prefix to the full meta-episode as training progresses, avoiding inaccurate long-horizon rollouts early on. The theoretical contribution shows that for decomposable task distributions (DTD), local single-step reconstruction (as in Dreamer) yields PAC regret bounds that depend exponentially only on the per-subtask DoF D and linearly on the number of subtasks K, versus exponential in D×K for global reconstruction (as in VariBAD), explaining why MAMBA scales to multi-goal environments where VariBAD and HyperX fail.

## Results

MAMBA achieves the highest total return across nearly all meta-RL benchmarks, with up to 15× better sample efficiency than state-of-the-art meta-RL methods (converging in ~2M environment steps vs. ~10–15M for VariBAD/HyperX on Point Robot Navigation). On the decomposable task distributions (Rooms-N, Reacher-N), MAMBA's advantage is most dramatic: on Reacher-3 it reaches 325.0±47.0 while VariBAD and HyperX fail entirely (0.2±0.2 and 0.5±0.2), and on Rooms-5 it achieves 113.1±5.7 versus 0.9±2.5 for VariBAD. MAMBA also outperforms unmodified DreamerV3 (Dreamer-Vanilla) on all sub-task environments, confirming that the algorithmic modifications (full meta-episode encoding, horizon scheduling) are critical.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAMBA decisively outperforms both state-of-the-art meta-RL baselines (VariBAD and HyperX) across all environments, with the gap widening dramatically on decomposable tasks: on Reacher-3, MAMBA achieves 325.0±47.0 while VariBAD and HyperX score near zero (0.2 and 0.5), and on Rooms-5 MAMBA reaches 113.1±5.7 versus 0.9±2.5 (VariBAD) and −15.8±0.4 (HyperX). Against Dreamer-Vanilla (unmodified DreamerV3), MAMBA wins on all sub-task environments (Rooms-3 through 6, Reacher-1 through 4), with the largest margin on Reacher-3 (325.0 vs. 183.6) and Reacher-4 (77.7 vs. 0.4), confirming that the full-meta-episode encoding and horizon scheduling modifications are essential. Against RL² (Duan et al., 2016), MAMBA is superior in all environments except Escape Room where RL² edges ahead slightly (79.9 vs. 73.9) but uses 5× more data. In terms of sample efficiency, MAMBA converges in ~2M steps on PRN and Escape Room versus ~10–20M for VariBAD/HyperX, and in ~6M steps on Rooms-6 versus ~100M for the meta-RL baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAMBA bridges two previously separate lines of work: context-based meta-RL (VariBAD, HyperX) and latent model-based RL (Dreamer). The paper identifies that Dreamer's RSSM world model plays the same structural role as VariBAD's belief encoder, then makes three targeted modifications—full meta-episode encoding, local (single-step) reconstruction, and horizon scheduling—to adapt Dreamer to the episodic, task-identification structure of meta-RL. Theoretically, it extends the PAC bounds of Rimon et al. (2022) to a new class of decomposable task distributions, showing that estimating sub-task distributions individually yields linear (rather than exponential) dependence on the number of sub-tasks. This departs from prior model-based meta-RL (Nagabandi et al., Perez et al., Lin et al.) which use short histories and do not explicitly model task boundaries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MAMBA provides a simple, robust, and highly sample-efficient baseline for meta-RL that outperforms purpose-built algorithms by up to 15× in environment steps while requiring minimal hyperparameter tuning—a practical advantage given that VariBAD and HyperX are shown to be highly sensitive to configuration. The DTD framework and accompanying PAC bounds offer a principled lens for understanding when meta-RL should scale gracefully (decomposable tasks) versus exponentially (entangled DoF), guiding future environment design and algorithm development. Key limitations include the runtime cost of sequentially encoding full meta-episodes and the restriction to episodic resets; open questions include whether chunking or attention-based mechanisms can reduce this cost without losing task-identification information, and whether the approach extends to non-episodic or streaming task distributions.
