## Problem

Exploration bonuses in reinforcement learning—count-based rewards, pseudo-counts, and state-entropy maximization—are non-stationary by definition because they depend on dynamically changing parameters like state-visitation frequencies or ellipsoid matrices. This non-stationarity turns the MDP into a POMDP where optimal Markovian policies may not exist, yet this issue is generally acknowledged but under-explored, and existing solutions like decoupled exploration/exploitation policies (DeRL) add significant complexity while still facing hard optimization problems.

## Input / Output

The method takes the standard environment observation (grid-based entity maps of size 32×32, pixel observations from MiniHack/Procgen, or raycast-based observations in 3D Godot maps) and augments it with the sufficient statistics of the intrinsic reward: state-visitation count vectors N_t for count-based methods, the full 512×512 ellipsoid matrix C_t for E3B, or the Gaussian generative model parameters (μ_t, σ_t²) for S-Max. These statistics are processed through a separate CNN (matching the observation CNN architecture) and concatenated with observation features before the policy head, so the agent explicitly observes the previously hidden dynamics that drive the intrinsic reward.

## Method

SOFE transforms non-stationary exploration objectives into stationary ones by augmenting the MDP state space with the sufficient statistics of the intrinsic reward distribution, converting the implicit POMDP back into a fully observed MDP where the reward becomes a deterministic function of the augmented state. For count-based methods, the state-visitation frequency vector N_t is appended; for E3B, the ellipsoid matrix C_t (or its diagonal) is included; for S-Max, the generative model parameters are passed directly. The key requirement is that the update rule for these statistics must be Markovian (incremental per-step updates), which holds for all three objectives. Implementation-wise, a second CNN with the same architecture as the observation encoder extracts features from the sufficient statistics, and the two feature vectors are concatenated before the policy network. This minimal modification—adding one feature extractor and a concatenation—makes the learning objective stationary without decoupling policies or adding memory, and the paper shows it provides orthogonal gains across count-based rewards, pseudo-counts, and state-entropy maximization.

## Results

On the DeepSea hard-exploration benchmark, SOFE-DQN achieves 0.70, 0.65, and 0.42 average returns on DeepSea-20, DeepSea-24, and DeepSea-30 respectively, substantially outperforming the best DeRL baseline (DeRL-DQN: 0.40, 0.53, 0.10) on the harder variants. On Procgen-Maze, E3B+SOFE is the only method that consistently reaches the goal, while vanilla E3B, ICM, and RND all fail. In reward-free maze exploration, SOFE-augmented agents achieve higher episodic state-visitation coverage than vanilla count-based methods across all three mazes and multiple RL algorithms (A2C, PPO, DQN, SAC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DeRL (Schäfer et al., 2021), which decouples exploration and exploitation policies, SOFE-DQN clearly wins on the harder DeepSea variants: 0.70 vs. 0.40 on DeepSea-20, 0.65 vs. 0.53 on DeepSea-24, and 0.42 vs. 0.10 on DeepSea-30, while DeRL-DQN remains competitive on the easier DeepSea-10 (0.98 vs. 0.97) and DeepSea-14 (0.95 vs. 0.78). Against vanilla E3B (Henaff et al., 2022), SOFE+E3B achieves higher IQM on Procgen-Maze where vanilla E3B can only inconsistently reach goals, and also improves sample efficiency in the Habitat reward-free setting. ICM (Pathak et al., 2017) and RND (Burda et al., 2018) both fail to provide useful exploration signals in procedurally generated environments (MiniHack, Procgen-Maze), with SOFE+E3B substantially outperforming both. In the maze and 3D navigation experiments, SOFE consistently outperforms vanilla count-based rewards (Strehl & Littman, 2008) and vanilla state-entropy maximization (Berseth et al., 2019), achieving higher state coverage and extrinsic returns across A2C, PPO, DQN, and SAC algorithms. Notably, even PPO+LSTM, which has capacity to implicitly model non-stationarity, still benefits from SOFE's explicit state augmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

SOFE builds on the lineage of count-based exploration (Strehl & Littman, 2008), its high-dimensional generalization via elliptical bonuses/E3B (Henaff et al., 2022), and state-entropy maximization (Berseth et al., 2019), all of which define intrinsic rewards that are non-stationary by construction. Prior attempts to address this non-stationarity—most notably DeRL (Schäfer et al., 2021)—decouple exploration and exploitation into separate policies, adding architectural complexity. SOFE departs from this by proposing a minimal state augmentation: simply feeding the sufficient statistics of the intrinsic reward (visit counts, ellipsoid matrix, or generative model parameters) into the agent's observation, thereby converting the implicit POMDP back into a fully observed MDP without altering the reward function itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, general-purpose fix for a long-acknowledged but under-addressed problem in intrinsic-motivation RL, and its orthogonality to the specific exploration objective (counts, pseudo-counts, S-Max) makes it a drop-in improvement for a wide range of existing pipelines. The simplicity of the approach—essentially one additional feature extractor—contrasts favorably with the multi-policy architectures of DeRL, and the broad empirical validation across 2D mazes, 3D continuous navigation, pixel-based observations, and procedurally generated environments strengthens confidence in its generality. Open questions include how the method scales when sufficient statistics become very high-dimensional (e.g., full ellipsoids in large embedding spaces), whether the CNN-based encoding of statistics introduces its own approximation errors that could limit gains, and whether the approach extends to settings where the sufficient statistics are not incrementally updatable (violating the Markovian update assumption).
