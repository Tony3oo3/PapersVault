## Problem

Existing 4D reconstruction methods require synchronized multi-view videos or calibrated monocular videos with known camera trajectories, making data collection tedious and limiting real-world applicability. This paper addresses the gap by generating 360° dynamic objects from a single uncalibrated monocular video, but must overcome the fundamental challenge that image diffusion models provide only time-discrete supervision, which undermines temporal coherence in the resulting 4D representation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242027/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-view uncalibrated video captured by a static camera as input; the foreground object is segmented with SAM and uniformly sampled to 32 frames (videos typically span ~2 seconds). The output is a 4D dynamic object represented as a DyNeRF, from which arbitrary 360° novel views can be rendered as RGB, normal, and depth maps at any timestamp. An optional lightweight video enhancer (GAN-based) can post-process rendered sequences to remove residual artifacts like floaters and uneven color.

## Method

The core architecture is a Cascade DyNeRF built on K-planes, where instead of concatenating multi-scale features, low-resolution planes learn temporally coherent (slightly over-smoothed) dynamics and high-resolution planes learn residuals via additive composition, balancing temporal continuity against image quality. The DyNeRF is optimized via Score Distillation Sampling using Zero123-XL as a 3D-aware image diffusion prior, providing spatially consistent but temporally discrete supervision. A novel Interpolation-driven Consistency Loss (ICL) is introduced, which renders batches of spatially or temporally consecutive frames and penalizes their deviation from frames interpolated by the pre-trained RIFE video interpolation model, thereby enforcing spatiotemporal coherence and also alleviating the multi-face Janus problem. An optional cross-frame video enhancer (pix2pix UNet with cross-frame attention querying adjacent frames) is trained adversarially to refine occasional rendering noise. The full training objective combines SDS, ICL, reconstruction, foreground mask, normal smoothness, and orientation losses with tuned weights.

## Results

On the synthetic dataset (7 animated objects, 4 test views), Consistent4D (s=4) achieves the best video-level FVD of 992.61, substantially outperforming D-NeRF (2327.83), K-planes (2295.68), and Zero123 (1571.60). At the image level, Consistent4D (s=4) attains LPIPS 0.12 and CLIP 0.91, matching or exceeding Zero123 (LPIPS 0.15, CLIP 0.90). The s=2 variant (LPIPS 0.16, CLIP 0.87, FVD 1133.44) already surpasses both dynamic NeRF baselines by a wide margin on all three metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Consistent4D (s=4) outperforms all three baselines on every metric. Against D-NeRF and K-planes, the gains are dramatic: FVD drops from ~2300 to 992.61 (a ~57% reduction), and LPIPS improves from 0.38–0.51 to 0.12, reflecting the advantage of diffusion-based generation over reconstruction-only methods that lack multi-view supervision. Against Zero123 (per-frame 3D generation), Consistent4D wins decisively on FVD (992.61 vs. 1571.60, a 37% improvement), confirming that Zero123's per-frame approach suffers from severe temporal incoherence. On image-level metrics, Consistent4D (s=4) slightly edges out Zero123 (LPIPS 0.12 vs. 0.15; CLIP 0.91 vs. 0.90), while the s=2 variant is marginally behind Zero123 on LPIPS (0.16 vs. 0.15) and CLIP (0.87 vs. 0.90) but still far ahead on FVD. The paper notes that the video enhancer (optional post-processing) provides only a slight additional improvement (FVD 1114.85 vs. 1133.44 for s=2), indicating the core DyNeRF + ICL pipeline already captures most of the quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of 3D generation via Score Distillation Sampling (DreamFusion, Zero123) and dynamic NeRF reconstruction (D-NeRF, K-planes), but departs from both by casting 4D reconstruction as a generative problem supervised by a 3D-aware image diffusion model (Zero123) rather than requiring multi-view calibrated inputs or text prompts. The core technical departure is addressing the temporal discontinuity inherent in using a time-agnostic image diffusion model as supervision for a dynamic scene, solved through a Cascade DyNeRF architecture and a novel Interpolation-driven Consistency Loss that repurposes a pre-trained video interpolation model (RIFE) as a spatiotemporal regularizer. The optional video enhancer follows the pix2pix GAN paradigm with an added cross-frame attention mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single uncalibrated monocular video suffices for 360° dynamic object generation, this work significantly lowers the data acquisition barrier for 4D content creation, making it accessible without complex multi-view rigs or camera calibration. The ICL loss is a simple, broadly applicable regularizer that also alleviates the multi-face Janus problem in text-to-3D generation, suggesting potential utility beyond the 4D setting. Key limitations include dependence on a diffusion model trained primarily on synthetic data (limiting real-world generalization), sensitivity to input video quality, and a ~2.5-hour per-object training cost that hinders practical deployment. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer videos, more complex motions, and whether the reliance on a specific 3D-aware diffusion model (Zero123) can be replaced by more general or video-native diffusion priors.
