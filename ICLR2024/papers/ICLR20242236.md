## Problem

Performing k-NN search with cross-encoders (CEs) is impractical at scale because exact search requires O(|I|) expensive CE forward passes per query. Existing workarounds either use dual-encoder retrieve-and-rerank (which suffers from poor domain generalization and decoupled retrieval/reranking) or CUR-based matrix factorization (which requires computing a dense query-item CE score matrix, scaling linearly with the number of items and taking thousands of GPU-hours for millions of items). The paper addresses how to efficiently index items aligned with a CE using only a sparse subset of CE scores, and how to perform adaptive test-time retrieval that incrementally refines the query representation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a test query, a pre-computed item embedding matrix V (learned offline via sparse matrix factorization), and black-box access to a trained cross-encoder model. At test time, it operates under a fixed budget of B_CE cross-encoder calls distributed over R rounds, producing top-k items ranked by exact CE scores, approximate CE scores for all items in the corpus, and a refined test query embedding. The item embeddings remain fixed throughout inference; only the query embedding is updated iteratively, making the approach suitable for large-scale deployment where re-indexing is expensive.

## Method

The approach has two stages. Offline, a sparse matrix G is constructed by scoring only the top-k_d items (per train query, selected via a base dual-encoder) with the CE, then factorized via transductive MF (free item embedding parameters) or inductive MF (a 2-layer MLP with skip connection trained on top of frozen DE embeddings), minimizing a matrix-completion objective over observed entries. At test time, the proposed AXN algorithm performs multi-round adaptive retrieval: in each round, it solves a linear regression (V_{A_r}^T V_{A_r})^† V_{A_r}^T a_r to compute the test query embedding from CE scores of items retrieved so far, uses the resulting inner-product scores to select the next batch of items, and scores them with the CE. A regularization term blends the linear-regression solution with a parametric model's query embedding to prevent overfitting when fewer items have been scored than the embedding dimension. This yields up to 5% and 54% recall improvement over retrieve-and-rerank for k=1 and k=100, while the sparse indexing achieves up to 100× speedup over CUR-based methods.

## Results

On Hotpot-QA (5M items), AXN combined with inductive matrix factorization (MF_IND, 10K train queries, 100 items/query) achieves Top-1-Recall@100=80.5 and Top-100-Recall@500=42.6 while requiring less than 3 hours of indexing time, compared to ADACUR_DE_SRC which requires 1000+ GPU hours for Top-1-Recall@100=75.9 and Top-100-Recall@500=44.8. On YuGiOh (10K items), AXN_DE_SRC,DE_SRC provides a 5.2% improvement in Top-1-Recall and a 54% improvement in Top-100-Recall over the corresponding retrieve-and-rerank baseline (RNR_DE_SRC) at no additional indexing cost. The proposed sparse-matrix factorization indexing achieves up to 100× speedup over CUR-based (ADACUR) and 5× speedup over dual-encoder distillation-based approaches while matching or improving k-NN search recall.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ADACUR (Yadav et al., 2023), the proposed AXN + MF approach achieves higher Top-1-Recall on Hotpot-QA (80.5 vs. 75.9) while requiring orders of magnitude less indexing compute (<3 hours vs. 1000+ GPU hours), though it trails slightly on Top-100-Recall (42.6 vs. 44.8). The 100× speedup over ADACUR comes from using a sparse matrix (only k_d items per query scored with CE) instead of a dense query-item score matrix. Against TOUR (Sung et al., 2023), a pseudo-relevance-feedback baseline, AXN achieves similar Top-1-Recall at smaller inference cost budgets and marginally better recall at larger budgets; for k=100 retrieval, AXN with λ=0 achieves significantly better recall than both TOUR-CE and TOUR-MSE variants. On downstream task metrics (nDCG@10, recall), AXN and TOUR yield similar performance on Hotpot-QA, while AXN with λ=0 performs best on SciDocs downstream recall.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work by Yadav et al. (2022, 2023) on approximating cross-encoder similarity via matrix factorization for k-NN search. The key departure is replacing the dense CUR-based score matrix (which requires scoring every item against every train query) with a sparse matrix factorization approach that scores only a small subset of query-item pairs, reducing CE calls by orders of magnitude. At test time, the proposed AXN method adapts the multi-round adaptive retrieval of ADACUR by solving a linear regression for the query embedding in each round rather than recomputing a CUR decomposition, and optionally regularizing with a parametric dual-encoder embedding. The inductive MF variant (a shallow MLP on frozen DE embeddings) draws on inductive matrix completion and adapter-style parameter-efficient fine-tuning, positioning itself as a lightweight alternative to full distillation-based DE training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100× indexing speedup over CUR-based methods and 5× over distillation-based DE training make cross-encoder-aligned indexing practical for large-scale deployment (e.g., 5M items in under 3 hours vs. 1000+ GPU hours), which is a significant practical barrier removal for retrieval systems. The 54% recall improvement at k=100 over retrieve-and-rerank demonstrates that coupling retrieval and re-ranking through iterative query embedding refinement is substantially more effective than the decoupled paradigm. An open question the paper highlights is the discrepancy between k-NN search recall and downstream task performance (EXACT brute-force CE search sometimes underperforms RNR on entity linking), suggesting that CE training objectives may not fully align with downstream utility—a gap the authors flag for future work on loss design and negative mining.
