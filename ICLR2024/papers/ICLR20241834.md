## Problem

Model-free RL with sparse rewards struggles to solve complex, high-precision robot manipulation tasks due to severe exploration bottlenecks, and existing learning-from-demonstration methods (RLPD, JSRL, DAPG, Cal-QL) still require many demonstrations or fail entirely on hard tasks with high initial-state randomization. The critical gap is how to overcome exploration with only a handful of demonstrations (≤5) for tasks that are long-horizon, precise, and highly randomized—tasks that prior methods cannot solve at all.

## Input / Output

The method consumes low-dimensional robot state vectors (39–55 dimensional observations depending on the environment) and a small set of demonstration trajectories (1–20, sourced from teleoperation, scripting, or motion planning) as offline data. It outputs continuous robot actions (4–30 dimensional) under a sparse binary reward (+1 on success, 0 otherwise). A key operational detail is that during training the agent is repeatedly reset to states sampled from within the demonstration trajectories rather than from the true initial state distribution, and episode timelimits are dynamically adjusted based on which demonstration state the agent is reset to.

## Method

RFCL is a two-stage curriculum built on top of SAC with a Q-ensemble. Stage 1 (reverse curriculum) generates a per-demonstration curriculum by resetting the agent to states near the end of each successful demonstration and progressively stepping backward toward the demonstration's initial state, using a geometric-distributed offset and a small reverse step size δ; the curriculum advances only when the agent achieves success m consecutive times at the current frontier state. Dynamic episode timelimits scale with the remaining demonstration horizon to avoid wasting interactions. Stage 2 (forward curriculum) continues training without resetting networks, using a PLR-inspired score-based prioritization over a pool of sampled initial states from the true S_init: states where the agent sometimes succeeds (score 3) are prioritized over states where it never succeeds (score 2) or always succeeds (score 1), combined with staleness-aware resampling to keep scores calibrated. The per-demonstration design handles multi-modal demonstration data, and the forward curriculum is what enables generalization from the narrow set of demonstration initial states to the full initial state distribution, yielding extreme demonstration and sample efficiency.

## Results

Across 21 fully-observable manipulation tasks in Adroit, ManiSkill2, and MetaWorld with sparse rewards, RFCL with 5 demonstrations and 1M interaction steps is the only method achieving nonzero success on every environment. RFCL shows significant success-rate improvements over all baselines in ManiSkill2 and MetaWorld, with more modest gains in Adroit where baselines are already competitive. On the hardest tasks (PegInsertionSide, PlugCharger), RFCL is the sole method to achieve reliable success, while all baselines fail completely. In a direct comparison on MetaWorld Stack 3, RFCL solves the task in under 10M samples with 20 demonstrations, whereas Nair et al. (2018) requires over 100M samples with 100 demonstrations to reach only 39% success.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RFCL outperforms all five baselines across every environment suite. Against RLPD (Ball et al., 2023), the strongest baseline and current SOTA on Adroit, RFCL achieves significant gains in ManiSkill2 and MetaWorld while showing only minor improvements on Adroit where RLPD already performs well. Against JSRL (Uchendu et al., 2023), which also uses a reverse curriculum but generates it via an offline guide policy, RFCL outperforms on all three Adroit tasks. Against DAPG (Rajeswaran et al., 2018) and Cal-QL (Nakamoto et al., 2023), RFCL achieves substantially higher success rates across all suites, with the gap widening on harder tasks. Against the uniform state-reset strategy of Nair et al. (2018), RFCL's per-demonstration reverse curriculum reduces the steps to reach ~100% success from the demonstration initial states from >100M (uniform, which fails within 1M) to 24.1M (per-demo + dynamic timelimit), and on Stack 3 solves the task in <10M samples with 20 demos versus >100M samples with 100 demos for only 39% success.

## Relation to Prior Work

RFCL sits at the intersection of three established lines of work: state-reset-based exploration (Nair et al., 2018; Resnick et al., 2018), reverse curricula generated from demonstrations (Florensa et al., 2017; JSRL/Uchendu et al., 2023), and forward curricula that prioritize frontier states (PLR/Jiang et al., 2021). The key departure is the per-demonstration reverse curriculum, which handles multi-modal demonstration distributions that prior global or uniform curricula cannot, and the explicit coupling of a reverse stage (narrow initial-state distribution) with a forward stage (full initial-state distribution) in a single training pipeline. The underlying RL algorithm is standard SAC with a Q-ensemble, and the offline data mixing strategy follows RLPD (Ball et al., 2023), so the contribution is architectural in the curriculum design rather than in the policy optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RFCL demonstrates that previously unsolvable sparse-reward manipulation tasks (PegInsertionSide, PlugCharger in ManiSkill2) can be solved with as few as 5 demonstrations, dramatically reducing the demonstration collection burden that is the primary bottleneck in real-world robotics. The main limitation is the reliance on state reset, which restricts the method to simulation and requires a separate sim2real pipeline for deployment. Open questions include whether the reverse-forward curriculum structure transfers to model-based RL (the authors note MoDem as a natural extension), how to handle tasks where demonstration initial states are far from the true initial state distribution, and whether the per-demo curriculum generalizes to very large or highly heterogeneous demonstration sets.
