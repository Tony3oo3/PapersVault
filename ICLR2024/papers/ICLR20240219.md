## Problem

Existing diffusion-based foundation models like Stable Diffusion are trained on natural web images and cannot handle the unique properties of satellite imagery: multi-spectral bands, irregular temporal sampling, and the absence of text captions. While discriminative foundation models for remote sensing have emerged, they cannot solve the inverse problems (super-resolution, temporal prediction, inpainting) that are central to practical satellite applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240219/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For single-image generation, the model takes a text caption plus 7 numerical metadata fields (longitude, latitude, GSD, cloud cover, year, month, day) and produces a 512×512 or 256×256 RGB satellite image. For conditional tasks, it additionally accepts a sequence of T satellite images (each with its own metadata), where the conditioning images may have different channel counts (e.g., 13-band Sentinel-2) and resolutions than the target output. Notably, the model is invariant to the ordering of conditioning images since each image's temporal position is determined solely by its timestamp metadata rather than its position in the sequence.

## Method

DiffusionSat builds on the Stable Diffusion 2.1 latent diffusion architecture (VAE encoder/decoder + denoising UNet) with two key modifications. First, numerical metadata is encoded via sinusoidal projections (identical to the diffusion timestep embedding) followed by per-metadata MLPs, whose outputs are summed and added to the timestep embedding—avoiding the discretization and numerical-encoding weaknesses of text-based conditioning. Second, a novel 3D ControlNet extension replaces 2D zero-convolutions with 3D zero-convolutions between SD blocks, adds temporal attention layers, and introduces a learned mixing parameter α_i per block to gradually incorporate temporal conditioning without disrupting pretrained weights. Training initializes all components from SD 2.1 but updates only the UNet and metadata embeddings, with metadata randomly zeroed at 10% probability to support unconditional generation. The 3D ControlNet reshapes batch and temporal dimensions before processing, applies a temporal pixel-wise transformer, and allows each conditioning image to carry its own metadata embedding.

## Results

On single-image 512×512 generation (fMoW), DiffusionSat achieves FID 15.80, substantially outperforming raw SD 2.1 (FID 117.74) and even SD 2.1 finetuned with metadata in the caption (FID 24.23). For multi-spectral super-resolution on fMoW-Sentinel, DiffusionSat attains the best LPIPS (0.6221) and SSIM (0.1703) among all baselines. On Texas Housing temporal super-resolution, DiffusionSat+3D ControlNet achieves LPIPS 0.3247 (future→past) and 0.3534 (past→future), outperforming STSR(EA64) (0.3764/0.4342) and all other baselines on the perceptual metric. On fMoW temporal generation, DiffusionSat+3D ControlNet reaches LPIPS 0.4304 (t'>t) and 0.3937 (t'<t), beating STSR and MCVD by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffusionSat decisively outperforms raw SD 2.1 on single-image generation, reducing FID from 117.74 to 15.80 (an ~7.4× improvement), demonstrating that metadata conditioning via sinusoidal embeddings is far more effective than the unconditioned prior. For super-resolution, DiffusionSat beats Pix2Pix (LPIPS 0.6221 vs 0.6895), DBPN (0.6221 vs 0.6826), and SRGAN (0.6221 vs 0.5261 on fMoW; 0.3247 vs 0.5261 on Texas Housing) on the perceptual LPIPS metric, though DBPN and STSR retain a slight edge on PSNR/SSIM (which favor blurriness). Against the strongest dedicated satellite super-resolution baseline STSR(EA64), DiffusionSat+3D ControlNet achieves lower LPIPS (0.3247 vs 0.3764 for t'>t; 0.3534 vs 0.4342 for t'<t) at the cost of ~1.5 dB PSNR, indicating sharper but less pixel-accurate reconstructions. On fMoW temporal generation, DiffusionSat+3D ControlNet surpasses MCVD (LPIPS 0.4304 vs 0.6058) and STSR (0.4304 vs 0.4898) by a large margin, and also outperforms SD+3D ControlNet without pretraining (0.4304 vs 0.5523), confirming the value of the single-image pretraining stage. The ablation comparing Ours+ControlNet vs Ours+3D ControlNet shows that the 3D temporal architecture consistently improves LPIPS by ~0.06–0.08 over the 2D channel-stacking variant across both temporal directions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Stable Diffusion latent diffusion architecture (Rombach et al., 2022) to the remote sensing domain, replacing text-only conditioning with a hybrid of CLIP text embeddings and sinusoidal-encoded numerical metadata (latitude, longitude, GSD, timestamp). For conditional generation tasks, it extends ControlNet (Zhang & Agrawala, 2023) into a 3D variant with temporal attention layers, drawing on video diffusion designs (Blattmann et al., 2023) but adapting them to the irregular temporal spacing and multi-spectral nature of satellite sequences. This departs from prior satellite image generation work (STSR, GAN-based super-resolution) that used task-specific architectures, instead positioning a single pretrained LDM as a universal prior that can be flexibly conditioned via lightweight ControlNet modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiffusionSat is the first generative foundation model for satellite imagery, unifying single-image generation, multi-spectral super-resolution, temporal prediction, and disaster-damage inpainting under one architecture with metadata-aware conditioning. This could accelerate downstream applications in disaster response, crop-yield prediction, and environmental monitoring by providing a shared generative prior rather than task-specific models. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger and more geographically diverse datasets, whether synthetic data generation can augment discriminative methods, and how to enable faster sampling for resource-constrained deployment. The paper also acknowledges that geographical bias along axes beyond location (building types, crop varieties, seasonal understanding) remains unexamined.
