## Problem

Diffusion-based single-image novel view synthesizers such as Zero 1-to-3 achieve high-fidelity results but require approximately 10 days of training on 8 A100 GPUs, making rapid research iteration impractical. The paper asks whether the training cost can be drastically reduced without sacrificing generalizability or output quality, without simply scaling up hardware.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241747/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single 256×256 RGB image as the conditional reference together with a target camera pose (relative rotation R and translation T) and produces a photorealistic novel-view image of the same object from the specified angle. During training, each sample is a pair of 256×256 rendered views from the same Objaverse object, with one view serving as the condition and the other as the denoising target, plus the associated relative pose transformation.

## Method

Efficient-3DiM finetunes a pretrained Stable Diffusion UNet with three key modifications. First, it replaces uniform timestep sampling with a Gaussian distribution (mean=1000, std=200), motivated by the observation that 3D geometry is established in early denoising steps while texture is refined in later (noisier) steps, so finetuning should bias updates toward high-timestep regions. Second, it swaps the CLIP image encoder for a DINO-v2 ViT-L/14, extracting multi-scale spatial features from the 5th, 12th, 18th, and final transformer layers, concatenating them into a 16×16×640 map, and injecting this additively into the 4th, 6th, and 8th UNet encoder stages alongside a cross-attention connection with the [CLS] token. Third, it applies engineering enhancements: 16-bit mixed-precision training stabilized by an extra LayerNorm on DINO-v2 features, pre-computed and disk-cached encoder outputs to bypass backpropagation through DINO-v2, and a cosine-decayed learning rate schedule. Together these yield a 14× training speedup (10 days → <1 day on 8 A100s) while matching or exceeding Zero 1-to-3 quality at equal iteration budgets.

## Results

On the Objaverse validation set at 20k training steps, Efficient-3DiM achieves 0.171 LPIPS and 0.128 MSE, substantially outperforming the Zero 1-to-3 baseline (0.279 LPIPS, 0.212 MSE) under the same step budget. On the real-world OmniObject3D benchmark, Efficient-3DiM at 20k steps reaches 0.248 average LPIPS, surpassing Zero 1-to-3's 0.276 average LPIPS even after 105k steps. The total training time is reduced from 10 days to under 1 day (14× speedup) on 8× A100 GPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is Zero 1-to-3 (Liu et al., 2023b), the original 10-day Stable Diffusion finetuning pipeline. Efficient-3DiM decisively outperforms it at every matched training budget: at 10k steps the average LPIPS drops from 0.327 to 0.253, and at 20k steps from 0.290 to 0.248 on OmniObject3D. Most strikingly, Efficient-3DiM at just 20k steps (≈1 day of training) beats Zero 1-to-3 at its full 105k-step checkpoint (0.248 vs. 0.276 average LPIPS), meaning the proposed Gaussian timestep sampling, DINO-v2 feature amalgamation, and mixed-precision/cosine-schedule enhancements collectively yield both a 14× wall-clock speedup and a quality improvement over the fully trained baseline. On the Objaverse validation set the gap is even wider (0.171 vs. 0.279 LPIPS at 20k steps). The paper does not report any setting where the baseline matches or exceeds the proposed method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct efficiency refinement of the Zero 1-to-3 pipeline (Liu et al., 2023b), which itself extends the 3DiM conditional diffusion framework (Watson et al., 2022) by finetuning a pretrained Stable Diffusion model on synthetic 3D objects. The key conceptual departures are threefold: replacing uniform timestep sampling with a Gaussian distribution biased toward high-noise timesteps (motivated by observing that 3D structure emerges early in the denoising process), substituting the CLIP image encoder with DINO-v2 for superior dense 3D feature extraction, and applying standard engineering optimizations (mixed precision, cached encoder outputs, cosine LR decay). The paper positions itself squarely within the diffusion-based single-image novel view synthesis lineage, contributing training-efficiency strategies rather than a new generative formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 14× reduction in training time (10 days → 1 day on 8×A100) makes diffusion-based novel view synthesis far more accessible for rapid research iteration, lowering the barrier for groups without large compute budgets. The observation that geometric structure is established at high diffusion timesteps while texture is refined at low timesteps provides a useful inductive principle for finetuning diffusion models on 3D tasks more broadly. However, the method still requires 8 A100 GPUs and relies on synthetic Objaverse data, and the authors acknowledge that multi-view consistency remains an open challenge. Whether these efficiency tricks transfer cleanly to other diffusion-based 3D generation tasks (e.g., text-to-3D, video) is an open question.
