## Problem

While prior work established that supervised contrastive loss (SCL) and cross-entropy (CE) both yield symmetric simplex-ETF training representations under balanced data, no study had characterized the SCL embedding geometry under class imbalances, where symmetry is known to break. This paper asks whether a simple architectural modification can restore geometric symmetry under imbalance, and how mini-batch selection strategies influence the learned representation geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242183/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes labeled RGB images (MNIST at 28×28, CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, Tiny-ImageNet at 64×64) and produces unit-norm feature embeddings of dimension d=512 from the last hidden layer, constrained to the non-negative orthant by a ReLU applied before normalization. The primary output of interest is the geometry of class-mean embeddings (their Gram matrix), not just a class prediction; for generalization evaluation, a 2-layer MLP projection head is appended and a Nearest Center Classifier is used on the projected features.

## Method

The paper introduces a ReLU activation at the final layer before the normalization step, which constrains all embeddings to the non-negative orthant. Theoretically, this is captured by a refined Unconstrained Features Model (UFM+) that minimizes SCL over non-negative, unit-norm embeddings; the authors prove via successive Jensen's inequality applications that all global minimizers satisfy Neural Collapse and form an orthogonal frame (OF) regardless of the class-imbalance ratio. For mini-batch training, they define a Batch Interaction Graph and prove necessary and sufficient conditions (connectedness within each class and at least one cross-class edge per pair) under which the OF is the unique global minimizer. To guarantee these conditions hold for any arbitrary partition, they propose batch-binding: appending k binding examples (one per class) to every mini-batch, which adds only O(k) overhead while provably ensuring convergence to a unique OF geometry.

## Results

The paper's central empirical finding is that SCL with a ReLU activation at the final layer consistently converges to an Orthogonal Frame (OF) geometry (class-mean embeddings mutually orthogonal with equal norms) regardless of the imbalance ratio R (tested at R=1, 10, 100) and imbalance pattern (STEP or Long-Tailed), as measured by the distance metric Δ_G_M → 0. On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18 and NCC classification, adding ReLU does not compromise test accuracy: at R=100 (LT), SCL+ReLU achieves 39.71% vs. 37.19% for SCL without ReLU, and at R=10 (Step), 58.16% vs. 56.58%. The full-batch SCL loss empirically converges to the theoretical lower bound from Theorem 1, confirming the OF prediction. Batch-binding (adding k binding examples to every mini-batch) accelerates convergence to OF even without per-epoch reshuffling, and also improves convergence to ETF under balanced training without ReLU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three geometric baselines: (1) vanilla SCL without ReLU, (2) Cross-Entropy (CE) loss, and (3) SCL with ReLU (the paper's focus). Under class imbalance, both vanilla SCL and CE produce distorted, imbalance-sensitive embedding geometries (deviating from symmetric structures), whereas SCL+ReLU maintains a consistent OF geometry across all imbalance levels. In terms of accuracy, SCL+ReLU matches or slightly exceeds vanilla SCL across all imbalance ratios on CIFAR-100 (e.g., +2.52 points at R=100 LT), confirming that the ReLU-induced geometric symmetry does not hurt generalization. For batching, the paper compares three strategies: fixed partition (no shuffling), random reshuffling per epoch, and no-shuffling + batch-binding. Fixed partition fails to converge to OF under high imbalance, random reshuffling converges but with epoch-to-epoch fluctuations, and batch-binding achieves the fastest and most consistent convergence to OF even without reshuffling. The paper also notes that the UFM (without ReLU constraints) predicts an ETF for balanced data, which is consistent with the centered version of the OF found here, but the UFM fails to predict the true geometry under imbalance, unlike UFM+.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural-collapse / implicit-geometry line of work from cross-entropy (Papyan et al., 2020; Thrampoulidis et al., 2022) to supervised contrastive learning (SCL), and specifically extends Graf et al. (2021) from balanced to imbalanced data. The key methodological departure is the introduction of UFM+, a refined unconstrained-features model that imposes entry-wise non-negativity to account for ReLU activations, replacing the unconstrained UFM used in prior SCL and CE analyses. The batch-binding strategy is a novel practical contribution that leverages the paper's necessary-and-sufficient conditions on batch interaction graphs to guarantee a unique orthogonal-frame minimizer, going beyond the restrictive all-combinations batching assumed by Graf et al. (2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first explicit geometric characterization of SCL under class imbalance, filling a gap left by prior work that only covered balanced data. The finding that a trivial architectural choice (ReLU at the final layer) provably restores symmetry regardless of imbalance ratio is practically useful for practitioners training contrastive models on long-tailed data, and the batch-binding scheme offers a low-overhead way to guarantee convergence to the predicted geometry. Open questions include characterizing SCL geometry without ReLU under imbalance (which the paper acknowledges as future work), extending to d < k settings, and understanding how the OF geometry interacts with generalization under imbalance beyond the NCC evaluation performed here.
