## Problem

Existing diffusion-based sampling methods (PIS, DIS, DDS, Schrödinger bridge) all minimize a reverse KL divergence on path space, which is prone to mode collapse, high Monte Carlo estimator variance, and requires differentiating through SDE solvers. No unifying framework connected these methods, and no principled way existed to substitute alternative divergences that might avoid these pathologies. The paper asks whether a path-space perspective on time-reversed controlled diffusions can both unify these approaches and enable divergences with superior numerical properties.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only an analytical (unnormalized) target density ρ(x) and a prior distribution p_prior (e.g., δ_{x_0} for PIS or N(0,ν²I) for DIS), with no access to target samples. The gradient ∇log ρ is optional and used only for control parametrization in PIS/DIS; the log-variance loss itself never requires derivatives of ρ. Outputs are samples from p_target = ρ/Z together with an estimate of log Z (via the ELBO lower bound) and importance weights in path space used to compute the normalized effective sample size (ESS) and Sinkhorn distance for evaluation.

## Method

The paper reformulates diffusion-based sampling as a time-reversal problem between two controlled SDEs (X^u from p_prior to p_target and Y^v from p_target to p_prior) and derives the Radon-Nikodym derivative dP_{X^u}/dP_{Y^v} via Girsanov's theorem and the HJB equation, yielding a general path-space loss. The key novelty is replacing the standard KL divergence with the log-variance divergence D_LV^{P_{X^w}}(P_{X^u}, P_{Y^v}) = V[(R_f + S_{u+v} + B)(X^w)], where the reference process X^w can be detached from the computational graph (w = u with stop-gradient), eliminating the need to differentiate through the SDE solver or compute ∇ρ. This loss exhibits the 'sticking-the-landing' property (zero gradient variance at the optimum), allows an exploration-exploitation tradeoff via the choice of w, and reduces to a control-variate version of the KL gradient when w = u. The framework recovers PIS, DIS, DDS, and the Schrödinger bridge as special cases by fixing v, r, and p_prior, and the log-variance variant of each is derived in closed form (Table 1), showing consistent improvements in Δlog Z, ESS, and mode coverage across GMM, Funnel, and double-well benchmarks.

## Results

On the GMM (d=2) benchmark, the log-variance (LV) loss reduces the PIS Δlog Z error from 1.094 (KL) to 0.046 and boosts ESS from 0.0051 to 0.9093, a ~178× improvement in effective sample size. On the challenging double-well problem (d=5, m=5, δ=4), PIS with LV achieves Δlog Z = 0.214 vs. 3.567 for KL, and ESS jumps from 0.0004 to 0.6744. For DIS on the same DW problem, LV improves Δstd from 0.431 to 0.009. On the 50-dimensional DW (δ=2), PIS with LV achieves near-perfect ESS (0.8453 vs. 0.8172) and Δstd = 0.000 vs. 0.001 for KL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed log-variance (LV) loss against the standard reverse KL loss for two diffusion-based sampling methods: PIS (Zhang & Chen, 2022) and DIS (Berner et al., 2024). For PIS, the LV loss delivers dramatic wins on the GMM (d=2) and DW (d=5, δ=4) problems: Δlog Z drops from 1.094→0.046 and 3.567→0.214 respectively, while ESS improves by over two orders of magnitude (0.0051→0.9093 and 0.0004→0.6744). On the Funnel (d=10) and high-dimensional DW (d=50) problems, PIS with LV shows modest but consistent gains in Δlog Z and ESS. For DIS, the LV loss improves ESS and Δstd substantially on the DW problems (e.g., ESS 0.0012→0.0225, Δstd 0.431→0.009 on d=5) and Δlog Z on GMM (1.551→0.433), though W_γ² can be worse in some settings. The paper notes that in the few cases where KL performs better, the difference is insignificant, and the LV loss also eliminates mode collapse visible in the KDE plots (Figures 2–3), a qualitative advantage not captured by the tabular metrics alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies previously separate diffusion-based sampling methods—Schrödinger Bridge (SB), Time-Reversed Diffusion Sampler (DIS), Path Integral Sampler (PIS), and Denoising Diffusion Sampler (DDS)—under a single path-space framework based on time-reversed controlled diffusions, building on the variational bridge formulation of Chen et al. (2021a) and the optimal control perspective of Berner et al. (2024). The key departure is the introduction of a variational formulation over path-space measures that allows arbitrary divergences as objectives, rather than being restricted to the reverse KL divergence used by all prior methods. The log-variance divergence, originally developed by Nusken & Richter (2021) for HJB PDEs and variational inference, is extended here to the two-process bridge setting, yielding a loss that avoids differentiation through SDE solvers and provably has zero gradient variance at the optimum.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unifying path-space perspective provides a clean theoretical lens for understanding and extending diffusion-based samplers, while the log-variance loss offers a drop-in replacement for the KL loss that eliminates mode collapse and removes the need for backpropagation through SDE solvers—two practical pain points that have limited the competitiveness of diffusion-based methods for the classical sampling problem. The paper demonstrates large empirical gains (e.g., ESS improving from 0.005 to 0.91 on a 2D GMM) without hyperparameter retuning, suggesting the KL divergence was a significant bottleneck. Open questions include whether the general bridge framework can be stabilized in high dimensions (where computing divergences of learned controls is costly), whether problem-specific tuning of the log-variance reference measure w can yield further gains, and whether diffusion-based samplers with these improvements can finally match or surpass MCMC and normalizing-flow baselines on the hardest targets.
