## Problem

Recurrent switching linear dynamical systems (rSLDS) are widely used to segment neural activity into discrete dynamical regimes, but they suffer from a fixed number of states across all trials, no geometric structure over states (permutation invariance), and purely Markovian switching that cannot capture semi-Markovian temporal dependencies. These limitations hinder interpretation of trial-varying neural dynamics and prevent the model from adapting state cardinality to the data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241174/cropped_page3_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes time-stamped neural spike train recordings (Neuropixels probes), preprocessed via Gaussian kernel smoothing (100 ms for whole trials, 30 ms for open-loop) and PCA projection (50 PCs per-trial, 10 PCs per-session) into firing-rate vectors. Task event timestamps (stimulus onset, reward, first movement) are available for post-hoc analysis but are NOT fed to the model. Outputs include the most-likely discrete state sequence, a continuous interpolated state trajectory on the latent geometry (unique to irSLDS due to its state geometry), continuous latent state trajectories, and time-varying transition matrices governed by their own recurrent dynamics.

## Method

The irSLDS replaces the rSLDS HMM transition matrix with a heat-equation PDE prior over a sufficient statistic w(j,t) derived from the distance-dependent Chinese Restaurant Process. The PDE ∂w/∂t = γ ∂²w/∂j² + κ·1_{z_n=j} + Rx_n + r simultaneously provides (i) exponential temporal decay matching the dist-CRP, (ii) spatial diffusion that induces a continuous geometry over discrete states, and (iii) semi-Markovian dynamics with flexible state cardinality per trajectory. Recurrence is preserved: the continuous latent state x_n drives the transition matrix through the R·x_n + r term, maintaining the key rSLDS property. Inference uses variational Laplace-EM, and the linear transition-matrix dynamics are parallelized via an associative scan operation, reducing complexity to O(K³(T/L + log L)) over L processors. Only three scalar hyperparameters {α, γ, κ} govern the discrete state process, contrasting with the K×K transition matrix in the rSLDS.

## Results

On the synthetic NASCAR task (K=4, the true number of states), irSLDS achieves a test log-likelihood of 1656.10, outperforming rSLDS (1628.57) and SLDS (1331.35), while matching rSLDS on dynamical MSE (0.015 vs. 0.010). On the non-stationary Extended NASCAR task (K=6), irSLDS achieves the best test log-likelihood (8219.41) and lowest dynamical MSE (0.24), surpassing rSLDS (8015.93, 0.35) and SLDS (7353.22, 316.75). On IBL neural data (per-trial, K=8), irSLDS attains a slightly higher held-out test log-likelihood (−1392.51) than rSLDS (−1399.03), while both models achieve comparable session-level ELBO (−6.69×10³ vs. −6.67×10³). Notably, irSLDS uses fewer parameters than rSLDS (e.g., 88 vs. 100 for K=4 NASCAR; 112 vs. 142 for Extended NASCAR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares irSLDS against two baselines: the standard SLDS (no recurrence) and the rSLDS (recurrence with a fixed K×K HMM transition matrix). Against SLDS, irSLDS clearly wins on all tasks: on NASCAR K=4, irSLDS improves test LL by ~325 nats (1656 vs. 1331) and reduces dynamical MSE by ~60× (0.015 vs. 0.930); on Extended NASCAR, the gap widens to ~866 nats in test LL and a >1000× reduction in dynamical MSE. Against rSLDS, irSLDS wins on the stationary NASCAR task at the true K=4 (test LL 1656 vs. 1629) with a negligible dynamical MSE cost (0.015 vs. 0.010), and decisively wins on the non-stationary Extended NASCAR task (test LL 8219 vs. 8016, dynamical MSE 0.24 vs. 0.35), where rSLDS fails to identify the two additional states introduced mid-trajectory. On IBL neural data, irSLDS achieves a marginally better per-trial test LL (−1392.5 vs. −1399.0) and reveals larger, more structured fluctuations in the number of active states per trial (spline R²=0.46 vs. 0.16 for rSLDS), while the two models are essentially tied on session-level ELBO. Overall, irSLDS matches or outperforms rSLDS across all settings while using fewer parameters and providing an interpretable geometric structure over discrete states.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the recurrent switching linear dynamical system (rSLDS) of Linderman et al. (2016) by replacing its fixed-cardinality HMM transition matrix with a heat-equation PDE prior derived from the distance-dependent Chinese Restaurant Process (Blei & Frazier, 2011). It departs from the HDP-HMM line of work (Fox et al., 2008; Beal et al., 2001), which achieved flexible state cardinality but could not support the recurrent (x→z) dependencies central to rSLDS. The PDE formulation simultaneously recovers the self-reinforcing, non-exchangeable structure of the dist-CRP, induces a continuous geometry over discrete states, and yields semi-Markovian dynamics, all while reducing the transition model from a K×K matrix to three scalar hyperparameters {α, γ, κ}. Inference leverages the variational Laplace-EM framework of Zoltowski et al. (2020) and a parallel scan operation for the linear transition-matrix dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The irSLDS addresses a practical bottleneck in neural state-space modeling: the need to pre-specify and cross-validate the number of discrete dynamical regimes, and the inability of HMM-based priors to represent geometric relationships between states. The demonstration that a 3-parameter PDE prior matches or outperforms a full transition matrix while enabling interpolated state trajectories and revealing systematic trial-to-trial fluctuations in active-state count (e.g., more states mid-session) opens a path toward interpretable, non-stationary analyses of large-scale electrophysiology. Key limitations include evaluation on a single IBL session and probe location, the scan-based parallelization not supporting automatic differentiation (requiring a sequential fallback for final training), and the absence of comparisons to other flexible-cardinality baselines such as HDP-HMM or sticky HDP-HMM. Scaling to the full IBL brain-wide dataset and other data modalities is identified as important future work.
