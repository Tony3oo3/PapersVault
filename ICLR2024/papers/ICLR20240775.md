## Problem

Schrödinger Bridge (SB) methods can in principle translate between arbitrary distributions, unlike diffusion models constrained by Gaussian priors, making them attractive for unpaired image-to-image translation. However, no existing SB method has successfully performed unpaired translation between high-resolution images, as the curse of dimensionality causes finite samples to fail to capture the image manifold, biasing the learned optimal transport map. This paper identifies this failure mode and proposes a solution that makes SB-based translation scalable to 256×256 and 512×512 images.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 256×256 (or 512×512) RGB image from the source domain and produces a translated image in the target domain. Generation uses N=5 function evaluations (NFE=5), where at each step the model predicts the target-domain image from the current intermediate sample, then interpolates with Gaussian noise to advance to the next timestep. The output is stochastic, producing diverse translations for the same input, consistent with the one-to-many nature of the underlying SB.

## Method

UNSB exploits the self-similarity property of SBs (an SB restricted to a subinterval is itself an SB) to decompose the full bridge into a composition of N generators, each learned via adversarial training. The training objective at each timestep combines three terms: an adversarial loss using a Markovian (patch-level) discriminator to match the predicted target distribution, an entropy-regularized transport cost loss (estimated via mutual information neural estimation), and a CUT-style contrastive regularization that enforces structural consistency between source and predicted target. A single time-conditional DNN (with positional embedding and AdaIN layers) serves as the shared generator across all timesteps, predicting the target image from any intermediate sample. This formulation allows incorporating advanced discriminators and task-specific regularization to overcome the curse of dimensionality, which is the key mechanism by which UNSB succeeds where prior SB methods fail.

## Results

On four 256×256 unpaired image-to-image translation benchmarks, UNSB (NFE=5, 0.045s/image) achieves FID scores of 35.7 (Horse2Zebra), 73.9 (Summer2Winter), 53.2 (Label2Cityscape), and 47.6 (Map2Satellite), outperforming all baselines. The closest competitor, CUT (NFE=1), achieves FID 45.5, 84.3, 56.4, and 56.1 respectively. UNSB also achieves the best KID scores across all four datasets (0.587, 0.421, 1.191, 2.013), while requiring only 5 function evaluations versus 30–50 for diffusion-based methods like SDEdit and P2P.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UNSB outperforms all 14 baselines on every dataset where comparisons are available. Against the strongest GAN-based competitor CUT, UNSB improves FID by 9.8 (Horse2Zebra: 35.7 vs 45.5), 10.4 (Summer2Winter: 73.9 vs 84.3), 3.2 (Label2Cityscape: 53.2 vs 56.4), and 8.5 (Map2Satellite: 47.6 vs 56.1). Against OT-based NOT, UNSB achieves dramatically lower FID (e.g., 35.7 vs 104.3 on Horse2Zebra), confirming that NOT fails to overcome the curse of dimensionality. Diffusion-based SDEdit and P2P, despite using 30–50 NFEs, still underperform UNSB (e.g., SDEdit FID 97.3 vs UNSB 35.7 on Horse2Zebra), and are not applicable to Label2Cityscape or Map2Satellite due to LDM's natural-image training. Among earlier GAN methods (CycleGAN, MUNIT, DistanceGAN, GcGAN), UNSB consistently achieves 20–150 FID points lower. On the additional Male2Female task (CelebA-HQ-256), UNSB (FID 37.87, KID 1.54) also beats EGSDE (FID 45.2), StarGAN v2 (FID 48.55), and NOT (FID 51.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

UNSB sits at the intersection of Schrödinger Bridge (entropy-regularized optimal transport) theory and adversarial generative modeling. It builds directly on the SB static formulation (connecting SB to Sinkhorn-Knopp-style OT) and the stochastic control formulation, but departs from all prior SB solvers (SBCFM, DSB, SB-FBSDE, NOT) by recasting the SB problem as a sequence of adversarial learning subproblems rather than learning a drift field or score function. Architecturally, it borrows from DDGAN (positional embeddings, AdaIN) and uses CUT's patch-wise contrastive loss as its regularization term, making N=1 UNSB nearly equivalent to CUT while the multi-step SB structure generalizes it. The paper explicitly positions UNSB as a generalization of both GAN-based one-step translators and diffusion-based methods like SDEdit, which use suboptimal SDEs with fixed Gaussian priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that Schrödinger Bridges can be trained successfully for high-resolution (256×256) unpaired image-to-image translation, a setting where all prior SB and OT methods fail. The identification of curse of dimensionality as the specific failure mechanism—empirically verified on a two-sphere toy problem—provides a clear diagnostic that the field lacked. Limitations include artifacts at higher NFE values, preliminary (under-tuned) results on the 64×64 entropic OT benchmark, and reliance on task-specific regularization choices. Open questions include whether the adversarial-SB formulation scales to video or 3D translation, whether the number of timesteps can be reduced further without quality loss, and how to automate the choice of regularization for new domain pairs.
