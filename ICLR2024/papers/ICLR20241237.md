## Problem

Existing OOD generalization bounds measure distribution shift via hypothesis-class-dependent distances (e.g., FΔF divergence) but ignore the optimization properties of the learned model. While empirical work shows that flat minima improve OOD generalization, no rigorous theoretical link connects sharpness (a geometric property of the loss landscape) to robustness (tolerance to distributional shift), leaving the mechanism behind this observation unexplained.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241237/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a training set S of size n from a source distribution, the learned model parameters (specifically the trainable weights w of a one-hidden-layer ReLU random feature network with m random directions a_i), and an unlabeled target empirical distribution of size n. It outputs a high-probability upper bound on the target risk that decomposes into empirical source risk, a partition-based total variation distance d_(ε,K), a robustness term 2ε(S), and a concentration term of order √(K log K / n). Crucially, the bound is data-dependent (K is estimated from the data via ε-covering) and does not scale with the hypothesis class pseudo-dimension, making it meaningful in the overparameterized regime where m ≫ d.

## Method

The paper proposes a two-part theoretical framework. First, it derives a robust OOD generalization bound (Theorem 3.1) by partitioning the input space into K disjoint subspaces where the loss difference within each subspace is bounded by ε(S), replacing the standard pointwise FΔF distance with a coarser partition-level total variation distance that can be zero for small shifts confined within a single partition. Second, it establishes a rigorous upper bound on the robustness constant ε(S,A) in terms of the sharpness κ(ŵ,S,A) = ||ŵ||²·tr(H) of the learned model (Theorem 3.4), under L-Hessian Lipschitz continuity within each partition for ReLU random feature networks. This connection allows the intractable robustness term (which requires knowledge of the target distribution) to be replaced by a sharpness quantity computable from training data alone. The resulting sharpness-based OOD bound shows that flatter minima yield tighter generalization guarantees, and the bound's concentration term depends only on K (data-dependent) rather than the model size, avoiding the vacuous bounds that plague VC-dimension-based approaches in overparameterized settings.

## Results

On the ridge regression distributional shift experiment, increasing the L2 regularization β from small to large values monotonically reduces both the sharpness κ(θ̂,S) and the OOD test loss along the shifting angle α, confirming the theoretical prediction that flatter minima yield lower OOD generalization error. On RotatedMNIST (4-layer MLP, DomainBed protocol), a clear negative correlation is observed between model sharpness and OOD test accuracy across ERM, SWAD, Mixup, and GroupDRO (6 seeds each), with SWAD consistently finding flatter minima and achieving higher OOD accuracy than ERM. In the spurious-correlation synthetic experiment, the proposed robust OOD bound (scaling as √K, independent of model size) remains tight and bounded as model size increases beyond 500 (overparameterized regime), whereas the Zhao et al. (2018) baseline bound grows unboundedly with model size. On PACS and Wilds-Camelyon17, the sharpness–OOD accuracy correlation persists at scale (60 and 18 ERM models respectively), validating the theory on real-world datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the Zhao et al. (2018) FΔF-based OOD bound (Proposition 2.1), which the proposed robust bound strictly improves upon: in the overparameterized regime (model size > 500), the baseline's concentration error term grows with model size while the proposed √K term remains constant, and the proposed distribution distance d_(ε,K) is tighter than ½d_FΔF when shifts are localized within partitions. Among the OOD algorithms compared on RotatedMNIST (ERM, SWAD, Mixup, GroupDRO), SWAD (Cha et al., 2021) consistently produces the flattest minima and highest OOD accuracy, while ERM produces the sharpest minima and lowest OOD accuracy; Mixup and GroupDRO fall in between. The PAC-Bayes domain adaptation bound (Germain et al., 2016) is compared in Appendix G and is also shown to be looser than the proposed robust bound in the synthetic spurious-correlation setting. The paper does not claim a single algorithm outperforms all others; rather, it provides the theoretical justification for why flat-minima-seeking methods (like SWAD) should generalize better OOD, which is empirically confirmed across all four algorithms and three datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate theoretical threads: the (K, ε)-robustness framework for generalization bounds from Xu & Mannor (2012) and the sharpness/relative flatness measure from Petzka et al. (2021). It departs from canonical OOD bounds (Blitzer et al., 2007; Zhao et al., 2018) that rely on hypothesis-class-size-dependent FΔF distances by instead partitioning the input space via robustness, yielding a data-dependent bound that remains meaningful in the overparameterized regime. The work also provides the first rigorous theoretical grounding for the empirical observation by Cha et al. (2021) that flat minima improve OOD generalization, which previously lacked a formal connection to optimization geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sharpness-based OOD bound offers a practical diagnostic: since sharpness is computable from training data alone (unlike the intractable robustness constant), it gives practitioners a concrete optimization target for improving domain generalization. However, the core sharpness-robustness theorem is proven only for one-hidden-layer ReLU networks with random features, and the case studies (ridge regression, 2-layer diagonal networks) extend the result but do not yet yield a unified theorem for general deep architectures. A key open question is whether the connection between sharpness and robustness holds in the rich (non-kernel) regime of deep networks, and whether the partition-based framework can be made fully data-driven without requiring a priori specification of K.
