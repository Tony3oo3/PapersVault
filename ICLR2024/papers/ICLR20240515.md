## Problem

Existing bilevel optimization algorithms require the upper-level function to have a bounded (Lipschitz) smoothness parameter, but important neural network architectures such as RNNs, LSTMs, and Transformers exhibit unbounded smoothness where the Hessian can grow linearly with the gradient norm. This renders standard bilevel methods inapplicable to these architectures, and the interaction between unbounded smoothness and the bilevel structure introduces two new challenges: hypergradient estimation error is amplified by inaccurate lower-level solutions, and the resulting bias is statistically dependent on the hypergradient itself.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes stochastic gradient and stochastic Hessian/Jacobian-vector product oracles with O(1) batch size, along with initial upper-level variable x₀ and a rough lower-level variable y₀'. It outputs an ε-stationary point of the bilevel problem in Õ(ε⁻⁴) oracle calls. A distinguishing feature is that the lower-level variable is only updated every I iterations (where I scales as σ²_{g,1}K₀²/(μ²ε²)) rather than at every step, and the upper-level update uses a normalized momentum direction (m_{k+1}/‖m_{k+1}‖) rather than a standard gradient step, making the per-iteration step size constant regardless of gradient magnitude.

## Method

BO-REP combines three mechanisms to handle unbounded smoothness in bilevel optimization. First, the upper-level variable x is updated via normalized momentum (a moving average of hypergradient estimates divided by its own norm), which controls both stochastic noise and potentially unbounded gradient magnitudes while ensuring x moves slowly. Second, an initialization refinement subroutine (Epoch-SGD with shrinking projection balls) is invoked at the start to obtain y₀ within ε/(8K₀) of y*(x₀) with high probability. Third, the lower-level variable y is updated only periodically—every I iterations—using N steps of projected stochastic gradient descent with a Polyak-averaged output, exploiting the fact that slow movement of x implies slow movement of y*(x) via its C_{gxy}/μ-Lipschitz continuity. This periodic schedule avoids the amplification of hypergradient bias that would occur if y were updated at every iteration under unbounded smoothness, and the proof establishes Õ(ε⁻⁴) oracle complexity matching state-of-the-art bounded-smoothness results up to logarithmic factors.

## Results

The main theoretical result is that BO-REP achieves $\tilde{O}(\epsilon^{-4})$ oracle complexity to find an $\epsilon$-stationary point for nonconvex, unbounded-smooth upper-level problems with strongly convex lower-level problems, using $O(1)$ batch size. This matches the state-of-the-art complexity under bounded smoothness (e.g., SOBA, MA-SOBA) up to logarithmic factors, while being the first result to handle unbounded smoothness. In experiments on hyper-representation learning, hyperparameter optimization, and data hyper-cleaning for text classification, BO-REP consistently achieves the fastest convergence rate and lowest final loss compared to all baselines across the Amazon Review and Sentiment140 datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BO-REP is the first bilevel optimization algorithm to handle unbounded smoothness in the upper-level function, whereas all baselines (BSA, StocBio, AmIGO, TTSA, ALSET, F²SA, SOBA, MA-SOBA) require the upper-level gradient to be Lipschitz (bounded smoothness). In terms of oracle complexity, BO-REP matches the best existing results ($\tilde{O}(\epsilon^{-4})$) of StocBio, AmIGO, ALSET, SOBA, and MA-SOBA, while using only $O(1)$ batch size (StocBio requires $\tilde{O}(\epsilon^{-2})$ and AmIGO requires $O(\epsilon^{-2})$). It also improves upon BSA ($\tilde{O}(\epsilon^{-6})$), TTSA ($\tilde{O}(\epsilon^{-5})$), and F²SA ($O(\epsilon^{-7})$). In all three experimental tasks (hyper-representation learning, hyperparameter optimization, and data hyper-cleaning), BO-REP achieves the lowest training and test loss and fastest convergence compared to StocBio, TTSA, F²SA, SOBA, MA-SOBA, and (for meta-learning) MAML and ANIL, demonstrating consistent empirical superiority across the Amazon Review and Sentiment140 datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research lines: stochastic bilevel optimization (Ghadimi & Wang 2018; Ji et al. 2021; Hong et al. 2023; Dagréou et al. 2022; Chen et al. 2023b), which universally assumes Lipschitz gradients (bounded smoothness) for the upper-level function, and single-level optimization under relaxed/unbounded smoothness (Zhang et al. 2020b; Jin et al. 2021; Crawshaw et al. 2022; Cutkosky & Mehta 2020), which handles gradient explosion but has never been applied to bilevel problems. BO-REP adapts the normalized momentum update from the single-level unbounded smoothness literature to the bilevel setting and introduces two novel lower-level update mechanisms—initialization refinement via epoch-SGD and periodic updates—that are specifically designed to control the amplified hypergradient bias caused by unbounded smoothness. The (L_{x,0}, L_{x,1}, L_{y,0}, L_{y,1})-smoothness assumption is a natural bilevel generalization of the standard relaxed smoothness condition, with separate constants for the upper and lower-level variables.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work removes a key theoretical barrier for applying bilevel optimization to RNNs, LSTMs, and Transformers, which are known to exhibit unbounded smoothness and for which all prior bilevel algorithms lack convergence guarantees. The fact that the Õ(ε⁻⁴) oracle complexity matches the state-of-the-art under bounded smoothness (up to logarithmic factors) is reassuring: it suggests that unbounded smoothness does not fundamentally degrade the optimization rate, provided the right algorithmic ingredients (normalized momentum, periodic lower-level updates) are in place. Open questions include whether single-loop and Hessian-free variants can be designed, whether the periodic update schedule can be made adaptive, and whether the results extend to lower-level problems without strong convexity. The experimental validation is limited to text classification tasks on relatively small datasets (Amazon Review, Sentiment140), leaving open the question of performance on larger-scale or non-text bilevel problems.
