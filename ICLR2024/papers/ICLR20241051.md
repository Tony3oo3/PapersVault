## Problem

Large multi-agent datasets contain mixed-quality behaviors, but desirability scores are typically assigned only to collective trajectories (e.g., number of incidents in a driving episode), not to individual agents. This makes it impossible to selectively imitate only desirable agents using standard behavior cloning or simple trajectory filtering, since a single trajectory may contain both good and bad agents. The credit assignment problem is further complicated in practice by fixed group sizes (ruling out Shapley Values), incomplete observations, and fully anonymized agent IDs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of multi-agent trajectories where each trajectory involves a fixed-size group of agents, a Desired Value Function (DVF) that assigns a single scalar score per collective trajectory (no per-step reward), and optionally low-level behavioral features (action frequencies, TF-IDF vectors) extracted from the trajectories. It outputs a single-agent imitation policy (replicated across all agents at evaluation), per-agent Exchange Values quantifying each agent's contribution to the DVF, and cluster assignments used to stabilize EV estimation under data scarcity. The DVF is the only signal of desirability—no dense reward or per-agent label is provided.

## Method

The paper introduces Exchange Values (EVs), a modification of Shapley Values that measures an agent's contribution as the expected change in DVF when substituting that agent with a randomly selected other agent, comparing only groups of the same size. This makes EVs applicable to constrained characteristic function games where only specific group sizes are permitted (e.g., 11-player football). To estimate EVs from incomplete or fully anonymized datasets, the paper proposes EV-Clustering, which assigns agents to clusters by maximizing the variance in EVs across agents, shown to be equivalent under an additive value function approximation to clustering by unobserved individual contributions. For degenerate (fully anonymized) data, behavior embeddings (PCA-reduced action distributions or TF-IDF vectors) are combined with EV-Clustering via a penalized SLSQP optimization. Finally, EV2BC modifies the standard behavior cloning loss by weighting each agent's contribution with an indicator that its estimated EV exceeds a tunable threshold, so the policy imitates only agents with positive contribution to the DVF while still learning from the full interaction context.

## Results

EV2BC consistently outperforms all baselines across three domains. On StarCraft (2s3z, 3s_vs_5z, 6h_vs_8z), EV2BC achieves DVF scores of 17.38, 20.31, and 10.0, compared to 12.14, 13.10, and 8.56 for standard BC. On Overcooked with human-generated data (D^human), EV2BC reaches 170.89 soups vs. 153.34 for BC and 163.34 for Group-BC. On the 120-agent Tragedy of the Commons, EV2BC achieves 8123.4 (v_final), 270.0 (v_total), and 33.1 (v_min), substantially exceeding BC's 2028.8, 38.9, and 1.8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EV2BC outperforms standard Behavior Cloning (Pomerleau, 1991) across all environments and datasets. On StarCraft, EV2BC improves DVF scores by 43–54% over BC (e.g., 20.31 vs. 13.10 on 3s_vs_5z). On the 120-agent Tragedy of the Commons, EV2BC achieves roughly 4× the v_final score of BC (8123.4 vs. 2028.8) and 7× the v_total score (270.0 vs. 38.9). Against OMAR (Pan et al., 2022), EV2BC wins decisively on Overcooked (e.g., 114.89 vs. 12.4 on D^human) and was omitted on StarCraft because OMAR performed significantly worse than BC. Group-BC, which selectively imitates only high-scoring collective trajectories, is also outperformed by EV2BC in every setting, confirming that individual-level credit assignment via Exchange Values is more effective than filtering by group-level outcomes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces Exchange Values (EVs) as a practical alternative to Shapley Values for credit assignment in multi-agent settings, motivated by the observation that Shapley Values require comparing groups of different sizes — infeasible in real-world scenarios with fixed group sizes (e.g., football always has 11 players). The EV2BC method modifies the standard Behavior Cloning loss (Pomerleau, 1991) by gating imitation on individual agents' estimated EVs, and EV-Clustering provides a variance-maximizing clustering objective to estimate EVs from incomplete or fully-anonymized datasets. The paper explicitly positions itself as the first work to address selective imitation from multi-agent datasets containing mixed-quality behaviors while aligning with a collective Desired Value Function, departing from prior multi-agent imitation learning (which assumes optimal agents and simulator access) and offline multi-agent RL (which assumes dense per-step rewards).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: large-scale human interaction datasets inevitably contain undesirable behaviors, and simply filtering by collective trajectory scores cannot identify which individual agents within a trajectory were responsible. The EV framework provides a principled, game-theoretic tool for this credit assignment that works under realistic constraints (fixed group sizes, incomplete observations, anonymized IDs). Key limitations include the assumption that individual agents behave consistently across trajectories, the need for a pre-specified DVF, and the acknowledged risk of misuse for individual discrimination. Open questions include extending to non-stationary agent behavior, leveraging undesired trajectories more constructively (e.g., as negative constraints), and validating on truly large-scale real-world multi-agent datasets beyond the simulated benchmarks used here.
