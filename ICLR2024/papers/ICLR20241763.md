## Problem

Existing ML-based MILP solving frameworks rely on large-scale optimizers and large training datasets, and perform problem reduction only at the variable level, overlooking constraint reduction. This creates severe performance bottlenecks when tackling large-scale MILPs with millions of variables and complex constraints, as the computational cost of both training and inference scales linearly with problem size.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241763/cropped_page3_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a large-scale MILP formulation (up to 2 million variables and constraints) and converts it into a bipartite graph that is partitioned into subgraphs via FENNEL. Training requires only small-scale instances (tens of thousands of variables) with homogeneous structure, while inference generalizes to problems 10-100× larger. The output is a near-optimal feasible solution produced by a lightweight optimizer constrained to solve subproblems with at most αn variables (α = 30% or 50%), iteratively refined through neighborhood search and crossover.

## Method

Light-MILPopt is a four-stage pipeline: (1) FENNEL graph partitioning divides the bipartite graph into low-correlation subgraphs to decouple the large problem into parallel small-scale subproblems; (2) an EGAT with multi-layer half-convolutions (combining edge-aggregated attention with alternating variable-to-constraint and constraint-to-variable message passing) predicts initial solutions, trained with Focal Loss on small-scale data and using improved features (random features for foldable MILPs, one-hot encoding for categorical types); (3) a generalized confidence threshold fixes high-confidence variables while a KNN strategy identifies active constraints by measuring distance from the predicted solution to constraint hyperplanes, achieving simultaneous variable and constraint reduction; (4) iterative optimization uses random-cluster-based neighborhood grouping and progressive KNN constraint updating (K shrinks by factor η each iteration) to guide parallel neighborhood search and hierarchical individual crossover, with a REPAIR algorithm maintaining feasibility as the active constraint set changes.

## Results

On four large-scale benchmark MILPs (SC, MVC, MIS, MIKS) and a real-world case study, Light-MILPopt with only 30% variable proportion (α=30%) outperforms both SCIP and Gurobi on nearly all problems. For example, on SC₁ (minimize, 2000s), Ours-30%S achieves 17,121.5 vs. SCIP's 25,191.2 and Gurobi's 17,934.5; on the Case Study (maximize, 1000s), Ours-30%G reaches 979,797.8 vs. SCIP's 924,954.5. The GNN&GBDT baseline fails entirely on MIKS and the Case Study, while Light-MILPopt solves all nine problem instances. In terms of efficiency, Light-MILPopt achieves the same target objective in as little as 0.5% of the time required by SCIP/Gurobi on benchmark MILPs, and saves over 90% of solution time compared to GNN&GBDT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SCIP, Light-MILPopt (α=30%) wins on 8 of 9 problem instances; the sole exception is MIS₂ where SCIP achieves 9,104.3 vs. Ours-30%S's 227,074.5 (both maximize), though this appears to be a SCIP anomaly given its performance on other instances. Against Gurobi, Light-MILPopt (α=30%) outperforms on all comparable instances, e.g., SC₁: 17,121.5 vs. 17,934.5 (minimize), MIKS₁: 35,067.8 vs. 32,960.0 (maximize), and the Case Study: 979,797.8 (Ours-30%G) where Gurobi finds no feasible solution. Against the GNN&GBDT framework (Ye et al., 2023), Light-MILPopt dominates on every solvable instance—on SC₂ (minimize), Ours-30%S achieves 166,756.0 vs. GBDT-30%S's 261,174.0, a ~36% improvement—and critically, GNN&GBDT fails to produce any feasible solution on MIKS₁, MIKS₂, and the Case Study, whereas Light-MILPopt solves all of them. The advantage is most pronounced on Set Covering, where constraint reduction cuts per-iteration optimization time to 1/5 of the original, and on the Case Study where Gurobi itself cannot find a feasible solution within the time budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ML-based MILP solving lineage initiated by Gasse et al. (2019) and Nair et al. (2020), which introduced bipartite graph representations and Neural Diving for initial solution prediction. It directly builds on the GNN&GBDT framework of Ye et al. (2023b) but departs from it in three key ways: (1) replacing the whole-graph GNN with FENNEL-based graph partitioning to enable small-scale training, (2) upgrading the GNN backbone from standard GCN to EGAT with half-convolutions to better exploit edge features, and (3) introducing KNN-based constraint reduction alongside the existing variable reduction. The EGAT component draws from Gong & Cheng (2019), and the confidence threshold generalization extends Yoon (2022) from binary to multi-bit integer variables.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a lightweight optimizer (solver restricted to 30–50% of variables) combined with small-scale training data (1% of problem size) can outperform full-scale commercial solvers (SCIP, Gurobi) on large-scale MILPs, which challenges the assumption that solving large MILPs requires proportionally large computational resources. The constraint reduction via KNN is a practical addition absent from prior ML-based frameworks and yields a 5× per-iteration speedup on constraint-heavy problems like Set Covering. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-homogeneous problem structures, multi-objective or nonlinear MILPs, and whether the fixed reduction ratio α can be learned automatically rather than set empirically.
