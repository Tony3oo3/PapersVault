## Problem

Posterior Sampling for Reinforcement Learning (PSRL) requires the prior over environment dynamics to be specified as a parametric distribution, which is cumbersome for domain experts who naturally express knowledge as causal relationships (e.g., a physician listing known dependencies between biometric variables). This paper addresses the gap by enabling PSRL to accept a partial causal graph as the prior, making Bayesian RL accessible to practitioners who lack expertise in specifying parametric distributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240419/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a partial causal graph prior (a bipartite DAG over state-action variables X and state variables Y, specifying known edges z₀ ⊆ z*), a degree of sparseness Z bounding the number of parents per state variable, and then interacts with the true environment to collect state-action-reward sequences. Beyond the per-episode optimal policy, C-PSRL produces as a byproduct a Z-sparse super-graph of the true causal graph (weak causal discovery), meaning it recovers all true causal edges plus possibly some spurious ones. The bipartite structure (no edges within Y or from Y to X) is a key constraint that enables independent parent sampling per variable.

## Method

C-PSRL implements a two-level hierarchical posterior sampling procedure. At the top level, a hyper-prior (categorical distribution) is maintained over the set Z of all Z-sparse factorizations consistent with the partial causal graph prior; at the bottom level, a Dirichlet prior over transition probabilities is maintained for each factorization. Each episode, the algorithm samples a factorization z from the hyper-posterior, samples a factored transition model p from the conditional posterior P(·|z), computes the optimal policy via exact planning in the resulting FMDP, collects one episode of data, and updates both the Dirichlet parameters (closed-form) and the categorical hyper-posterior (via marginalization over the Dirichlet). The key structural insight is that the bipartite causal graph allows independent sampling of parents z_j for each state variable Y_j, reducing the hyper-prior from a single distribution over the super-exponential set of all factorizations to d_Y independent categorical distributions, which both reduces parameter count and yields a regret bound of Õ(√(K/2^η)) where η is the minimum number of known parents per variable.

## Results

In the Random FMDP domain (d_X=9, d_Y=6, Z=5, N=2, H=100, η=2, 500 episodes), C-PSRL achieves a Bayesian regret significantly smaller than PSRL with an uninformative prior, while remaining surprisingly close to F-PSRL with the oracle causal graph prior. In the Taxi 5×5 domain (d_X=5, d_Y=4, Z=5, H=15), PSRL still exhibits linear regret after 400 episodes, whereas C-PSRL (η=2) converges to a good policy efficiently. The theoretical regret bound Õ((H^{5/2} N^{1+Z/2} d_Y + √(H·2^{d_X−η}))√K) explicitly captures how the degree of prior knowledge η reduces the regret rate, yielding Õ(√(K/2^η)) for the factorization-learning component.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares C-PSRL against two baselines: PSRL (Strens, 2000) with an uninformative prior that does not model factorizations, and F-PSRL (Osband & Van Roy, 2014) which assumes the full causal graph is known (oracle prior). In the Random FMDP domain, C-PSRL (η=2) clearly outperforms PSRL in both regret and model estimation error (ℓ₁ distance to the true transition model), while being only slightly inferior to F-PSRL. In the Taxi 3×3 domain, both PSRL and C-PSRL eventually converge to a good policy, but C-PSRL does so faster. In the more challenging Taxi 5×5 domain, the gap widens dramatically: PSRL still suffers linear regret after 400 episodes, while C-PSRL finds a near-optimal policy efficiently, demonstrating that even a partial causal graph prior (η=2) provides a substantial practical advantage over an uninformative prior. F-PSRL is omitted in the Taxi experiments since the oracle prior is not available in that setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the posterior sampling for RL (PSRL) line of work by replacing the standard parametric prior with a partial causal graph prior over the environment's variables. It builds directly on F-PSRL (Osband & Van Roy, 2014), which assumes the full factorization is known, and on hierarchical Thompson sampling with mixture priors (Hong et al., 2022b), which handles latent states in tabular MDPs. The key departure is formulating the unknown factorization as a latent variable in a hierarchical Bayesian hierarchy, where the causal graph prior constrains the hypothesis space, and then deriving a regret bound that explicitly depends on the degree of prior knowledge η rather than the full state space size.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in adopting PSRL: domain experts can naturally specify causal relationships but struggle to encode them as parametric distributions. The regret bound Ō(√(K/2^η)) provides a clean quantitative link between prior causal knowledge and sample efficiency, and the weak causal discovery corollary shows that C-PSRL incidentally recovers a super-graph of the true causal structure. However, the experiments are limited to small domains (d_X ≤ 9, small Taxi grids), exact planning in FMDPs remains computationally intractable in general, and the causal discovery result only yields a super-graph rather than the minimal graph. Open questions include tighter regret analyses, scaling to complex domains, and extending to model-free settings.
