## Problem

Current text-to-3D methods inherit the compositional limitations of 2D diffusion priors, producing object missing, attribute mismatching, and quality degradation when prompts describe multiple interacted objects with distinct attributes. No existing framework specifically addresses this semantic complexity gap in 3D content creation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242076/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each local editing step takes a pre-trained source 3D representation (NeRF, SDF, or DMTet), a source prompt, a target prompt, and a user-defined 3D region prompt (bounding box, custom mesh, or segmentation result). The region prompt is projected into per-view 2D masks that gate where edits may occur. The output is a target 3D representation initialized from the source, modified only within the specified region to match the target prompt. Multiple such steps are chained to build up complex scenes progressively.

## Method

Progressive3D decomposes complex 3D generation into a sequence of local editing steps, each constrained to a user-defined 3D region. Two 2D-mask-based losses enforce locality: a content consistency loss keeps colors and opacity in non-editable regions identical to the source, and a content initialization loss (applied only in early iterations) encourages opacity growth in empty editable regions to stabilize geometry creation from scratch. The key technical contribution is Overlapped Semantic Component Suppression (OSCS), which decomposes the target prompt's denoising residual into a projection component (overlapped semantics shared with the source prompt) and a perpendicular component (new semantics), then suppresses the projection by a factor W>1 while preserving the perpendicular component at full strength. This modified guidance is plugged into a standard Score Distillation Sampling loss, redirecting optimization toward the semantic difference rather than re-attending to already-realized attributes.

## Results

On the CSP-100 benchmark, Progressive3D achieves a CLIP score of 0.292, outperforming DreamTime (0.289), DreamTime+CEBM (0.275), and DreamTime+A&E (0.281). On fine-grained semantic metrics, the full Progressive3D framework (with L_consist, L_initial, and OSCS) reaches BLIP-VQA of 0.474 and mGPT-CoT of 0.609, compared to 0.255 and 0.567 for the baseline with only the consistency constraint. In human preference studies, 83.2% of users prefer Progressive3D outputs over DreamTime alone (16.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Progressive3D is compared against DreamTime (NeRF-based), DreamTime+CEBM (composable energy-based model for 2D composition), and DreamTime+A&E (attention-based semantic guidance) on the CSP-100 benchmark. Progressive3D wins on all metrics: it achieves the highest CLIP score (0.292 vs. 0.289 for DreamTime, 0.275 for CEBM, and 0.281 for A&E), and the fine-grained BLIP-VQA and mGPT-CoT scores also improve substantially over the DreamTime baseline. Notably, both CEBM and A&E actually degrade CLIP performance below the DreamTime baseline (0.275 and 0.281 vs. 0.289), indicating that 2D compositional guidance does not transfer well to 3D generation. In human evaluation, Progressive3D is preferred in 83.2% of cases over DreamTime, confirming that the progressive local editing strategy produces more semantically consistent 3D content for complex prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

Progressive3D builds directly on DreamFusion's Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) framework, modifying the SDS loss to incorporate an Overlapped Semantic Component Suppression (OSCS) term that decomposes the denoising prediction into projection and perpendicular components relative to the source prompt. It departs from prior text-to-3D methods (DreamTime, TextMesh, Fantasia3D, MVDream) that focus on quality improvements through better optimization strategies or 3D representations but ignore semantic consistency for compositional prompts. In the 3D editing literature, it differs from methods like DreamEditor and FocalDreamer by emphasizing the semantic difference between source and target prompts rather than relying solely on spatial attention-based localization, and by using 2D mask-based constraints to bridge heterogeneous 3D representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a concrete and practically important gap: current text-to-3D methods produce object missing, attribute mismatching, and quality degradation when prompts describe multiple interacted objects with distinct attributes, a common real-world scenario. The framework's generality across NeRF, SDF, and DMTet representations makes it a plug-in improvement for existing pipelines. Key limitations include the need for human-defined 3D region prompts at each step (increasing time cost and intervention), and the fact that final quality is bounded by the base method's generative capacity. Open questions include whether automated 3D layout generation could eliminate manual region specification, and how the approach adapts as 2D diffusion priors improve.
