## Problem

Masked modeling is a promising domain-agnostic framework for self-supervised learning because it avoids domain-specific augmentations, but its mask sampling procedure remains domain-specific (e.g., contiguous word spans for text, contiguous pixel patches for images). This reliance on hand-designed masks or domain-specific tokenizers makes it non-trivial to extend self-supervised learning to new domains without specialized knowledge of the input structure.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw data sequences reshaped to n×d_raw with no tokenization: discrete modalities (text, protein, SMILES) use a simple UTF-8 character-to-learned-vector mapping, while continuous modalities (images, particle physics) use a linear projection. For long sequences (protein at 1024, images at 36864), a cross-attention/Perceiver IO architecture with 256–512 latents is used; for shorter sequences (chemistry at 256, particle physics at 28), standard self-attention suffices. The model outputs reconstructed raw values at masked positions, trained with MSE for continuous inputs or cross-entropy for discrete inputs.

## Method

SMA generates masks from the model's own first-layer attention map, exploiting the observation that attention queries attend to semantically coherent regions of the input. Concretely, a random subset of query indices is selected, their attention rows are summed (across queries and across heads after softmax), and the top-k positions in this summed vector are masked by setting their pre-softmax values to −∞, preventing any information flow from masked tokens through subsequent layers. This single top-k operation approximates the more expensive iterative per-query top-k procedure while achieving the target masking ratio in O(n+mk) rather than O(m(n+k)) time. The masked input is then encoded through repeated Transformer layers, upsampled back to input dimensionality via cross-attention with positional-embedding-derived queries, and trained to reconstruct the original raw values at masked positions. Because the mask is derived from the model's own learned representations, it adapts to the data's structure without any domain-specific priors, tokenizers, or hand-designed masking heuristics.

## Results

On the TAPE protein benchmark, SMA achieves 0.23 accuracy on Remote Homology, 0.68 Spearman on Fluorescence, and 0.80 Spearman on Stability, outperforming ProteinBERT (0.22/0.66/0.76) while remaining fully domain-agnostic. On MoleculeNet, SMA pre-trained on only 1.6M molecules (an order of magnitude less than competitors) outperforms all six ChemBERTa-2 configurations on the three shared tasks and beats Uni-Mol on 3 of 6 tasks. On the HIGGS particle physics benchmark, SMA achieves 74.8% accuracy, surpassing the previous SOTA Transformer-PLR (73.0%) by 1.9%. On GLUE, SMA reaches 76.7 average score, outperforming word-level masking (75.6) by ~1.5% and random masking (73.4) by 3.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMA outperforms ProteinBERT on all three TAPE protein tasks (Remote Homology 0.23 vs 0.22, Fluorescence 0.68 vs 0.66, Stability 0.80 vs 0.76) without using any domain-specific architecture or sequence labels. On MoleculeNet, SMA-1.6M beats all six ChemBERTa-2 configurations (MLM and MTR at 5M/10M/77M) on the three shared ROC tasks and surpasses Uni-Mol on BBBP (0.750 vs 0.729), ESOL (0.623 vs 0.788), and FreeSolv (1.09 vs 1.48), though Uni-Mol retains an edge on BACE (0.857 vs 0.843) and HIV (0.808 vs 0.789). On HIGGS, SMA (74.8%) outperforms all 12 supervised tabular baselines including Transformer-PLR (73.0%), XGBoost (72.7%), CatBoost (72.6%), and TabNet (71.9%), with the gap over the best supervised method being 1.9%. On GLUE, SMA (76.7 avg) outperforms the Perceiver IO word-masking baseline (75.6) by ~1.5%, random masking (73.4) by 3.3%, and no pre-training (48.5) by 28.2%. On ImageNet-100, SMA (86.1% with augmentation, 78.5% without) outperforms random masking (85.6%/77.6%) in both settings, while patch masking (84.3%/79.6%) is competitive only with augmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

SMA extends the masked autoencoder paradigm (MAE, BERT) by replacing domain-specific mask sampling strategies with masks derived from the model's own first-layer attention maps, building on the Perceiver/Perceiver IO architecture's cross-attention mechanism and its formalization of domain-agnostic processing. While prior masked modeling methods require hand-designed tokenizers and contiguous-span or patch-based masks tailored to each modality, SMA learns to identify semantically coherent groups of tokens purely from attention statistics during training. The approach also connects to learned-augmentation literature (AutoAugment, adversarial masking) but differs by operating entirely within the masked prediction objective without external augmentation pipelines or contrastive losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that much of the domain-specific knowledge (tokenizers, mask shapes, inductive biases) previously considered necessary for effective self-supervised learning can be recovered from unlabeled data alone, offering a practical path to applying representation learning to new scientific domains without domain expertise. However, the method still requires some minimal domain knowledge (e.g., image resolution choice), the learned masks show a positional bias that may limit their utility in value-correlated domains, and the empirical gains in well-studied domains (text, images) are modest (~1.5% on GLUE). Open questions include whether the approach scales to very long sequences where the first-layer attention may not capture fine-grained semantic structure, and whether the positional bias in masks becomes a bottleneck for tasks requiring value-level reasoning.
