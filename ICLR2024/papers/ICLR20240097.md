## Problem

Instruction tuning has been extensively explored for decoder-only and encoder-decoder architectures, but the BERT family of encoder-only models—once the dominant pre-trained paradigm—has never been investigated as instruction followers. This paper asks whether encoder-only models can be made into competitive multitask instruction followers and zero-shot learners, and what their fundamental limitations are compared to autoregressive counterparts.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt (instruction) concatenated with a fully masked target sequence of a predetermined length as input, and produces the unmasked target text as output. The target length must be specified before generation begins—either via a learned length-prediction module (for flexible-length tasks like translation) or a fixed value (for determined-length tasks like classification)—which is a key constraint absent in autoregressive models. During inference, the Mask-Predict algorithm iteratively unmaskes tokens over T steps, progressively replacing low-confidence predictions.

## Method

The core architecture repurposes a single XLM-R encoder to handle both source encoding and target decoding via a dynamic mixed-attention mechanism: at each layer l, the target hidden state attends to the concatenation of the source representation from layer l-1 and the target representation from layer l-1, eliminating the need for a separate decoder and enabling full parameter sharing. Training uses a conditional MLM objective where the source and masked target are concatenated into one sequence, with attention masking preventing source tokens from attending to target tokens. Inference follows the Mask-Predict algorithm, starting from a fully masked target and iteratively unmasking the highest-confidence tokens over T iterations. The model is instruction-tuned on a 1/80 subsample of the multilingual xP3 dataset (~0.6B tokens), and a length-prediction head is added to estimate target sequence length for open-ended generation tasks.

## Results

On three held-out zero-shot tasks, Instruct-XMLR_XL (3.5B params) achieves 0.646 accuracy on XCOPA, 0.518 on XNLI, and 0.582 on XWinograd, outperforming BLOOMZ-3B on all three tasks and matching or exceeding mT0-3.7B on XCOPA and XWinograd. On WMT'14 machine translation, Instruct-XMLR_XL scores 5.72 BLEU (EN→X) and 13.51 BLEU (X→EN), trailing mT0-3.7B (9.41 / 22.82) but achieving a 3.1× decoding speedup over autoregressive baselines. The model was fine-tuned on only 0.6B tokens versus 15B for both baselines, yet still demonstrates strong task and language generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BLOOMZ-3B (decoder-only, 3B params), Instruct-XMLR_XL wins on every evaluated task: XCOPA 0.646 vs 0.519 (+12.7 pts), XNLI 0.518 vs 0.482 (+3.6 pts), XWinograd 0.582 vs 0.523 (+5.9 pts), and machine translation EN→X 5.72 vs 4.15 BLEU. Against mT0-3.7B (encoder-decoder, 3.7B params), Instruct-XMLR_XL wins on XCOPA (0.646 vs 0.627) and essentially ties on XWinograd (0.582 vs 0.584), but underperforms on XNLI (0.518 vs 0.528) and machine translation (5.72 vs 9.41 EN→X; 13.51 vs 22.82 X→EN). The authors attribute the XNLI gap to mT0's encoder-decoder architecture being more suited to NLI and to mT0 having 2× more pre-training tokens (1T vs 0.5T) and 25× more fine-tuning tokens (15B vs 0.6B). On language generalization (unseen languages), Instruct-XMLR still beats BLOOMZ but falls behind mT0, particularly on XNLI (0.466 vs 0.533). The key differentiator is inference speed: Instruct-XMLR achieves ~3× speedup over both autoregressive baselines due to its non-autoregressive Mask-Predict decoding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the instruction tuning paradigm—well established for decoder-only (BLOOMZ, LLaMA) and encoder-decoder (mT0, FLAN-T5) models—to the encoder-only BERT family for the first time. Architecturally, it builds on the Markov Random Field formulation of BERT (Wang & Cho, 2019), the Mask-Predict/CMLM non-autoregressive decoding algorithm (Ghazvininejad et al., 2019), and the dynamic mixed-attention mechanism (Liang et al., 2023b; He et al., 2018) to enable sequence-to-sequence generation from a single shared encoder. The key conceptual departure is that prior non-autoregressive BERT generation work followed the traditional pre-train-then-task-specific-fine-tune paradigm, whereas this paper introduces multi-task instruction tuning as the post-training objective, bridging the gap between BERT's masked language model pre-training and the instruction-following framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study showing that encoder-only models can serve as competitive instruction followers, achieving comparable zero-shot task generalization to encoder-decoder models of similar scale while offering ~3× decoding speedup. However, the paper also reveals critical limitations: unreliable length prediction for tasks with variable-length targets, significant quality degradation on long-generation tasks (attributed to the MLM pre-training paradigm rather than the non-autoregressive manner), and a complete failure of few-shot in-context learning. These findings suggest that BERT family models may require a generative post-training stage before instruction tuning to close the gap with autoregressive LLMs, and that the bidirectional architecture fundamentally limits in-context learning capabilities—open questions that will guide future work on encoder-only generative models.
