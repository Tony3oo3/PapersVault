## Problem

Deep reinforcement learning struggles with sparse-reward, goal-directed hierarchical tasks because exploration is intractable without dense signals, while classical planning excels at such tasks but requires pre-defined subtask structures and symbolic action models. Existing hybrid approaches either assume pre-defined subtasks, rely on object-centric representations needing fully observable environments, or do not learn the underlying symbolic rules from data. This paper addresses the gap by automatically inducing task substructures (effect and precondition rules) from a small number of expert demonstrations, then using them to guide DRL training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of expert demonstrations (20 for SWITCH/DOORKEY, 64 multi-task for MINECRAFT) solely for symbolic induction, plus a goal specification and 8×8 gridworld observations encoded as 3-channel images (object, color, status) during training. It produces two complementary outputs: a trained PPO actor-critic policy for fine-grained execution, and a symbolic knowledge base consisting of critical action schemata (precondition conjunctions and arithmetic effect rules) organized into a critical action network (an in-tree of subtask dependencies). The demonstrations are never used for direct policy imitation; they only seed the genetic programming population and decision-tree classifier.

## Method

The framework is two-stage. In the induction stage, it first identifies action–effect variable linkages via mutual information with two-center clustering, then induces effect rules as arithmetic programs (over {+,-,×,÷,inc,dec}) using genetic programming (gplearn, population 2000, two-phase explore/prune), and finally derives precondition rules by training a CART decision tree on whether the induced effect holds given the state. In the training stage, it constructs a critical action network by backward-chaining from the goal through effect–precondition dependencies, then trains a PPO agent (CNN for grid + FC for PDDL state) with an intrinsic reward of +1 whenever the agent executes an action satisfying a critical effect in the network, on top of the sparse extrinsic reward. The key conceptual bridge is the 'critical action': an MDP action whose execution causes a transition in the abstract planning state space, formalized via a proper abstraction mapping between the MDP and a SAS⁺ planning task.

## Results

On the generalization benchmark (Table 1), the proposed framework achieves 96%±1% on 4-SWITCHES-(N+1), 90%±2% on 8-SWITCHES-(N+1), 95%±2% on 4-DISTRACTORS-(N+1), and 95%±1% on 4-DISTRACTORS-(2N+1), substantially outperforming GAIL (10–30%) and BC-PPO (0–97%). On the main task-performance evaluation (Figure 4, 5M training steps), the framework outperforms all seven baselines on challenging tasks (8-SWITCHES, 4-DISTRACTORS, ENHANCETABLE, MULTIPLE) where all baselines completely fail, and is the most sample-efficient method on DOORKEY. The framework also requires only 4 additional demonstrations to adapt to the 4-DISTRACTORS-(2N+1) variant via re-induction of genetic programming populations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against seven baselines: DQN, PPO, BC, GAIL, DQN-RBS, BC-PPO, and RIDE. On the hardest tasks (8-SWITCHES, 4-DISTRACTORS, ENHANCETABLE, MULTIPLE), all seven baselines fail entirely while the proposed framework succeeds, as shown in Figure 4 training curves. BC and GAIL fail across all tasks due to insufficient demonstrations and lack of exploration. DQN-RBS and BC-PPO, which leverage both rewards and demonstrations, still fail on advanced tasks requiring long-term planning. RIDE, an exploration-based method, also fails on these advanced tasks. In the generalization setting (Table 1), BC-PPO drops from 97% to 0% on 8-SWITCHES-(N+1) and to 33–41% on distractor variants, while GAIL drops from 30% to 10–11%; the proposed framework maintains 90–95% across all variant domains, demonstrating superior rule-level and task-level generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the abstraction mapping between MDPs and planning domains established by Lee et al. (2021) and employs Koza's genetic programming as a symbolic regression engine to automatically induce action schemata (preconditions and effects) from expert demonstrations. It departs from prior planning-DRL integration work (Konidaris et al., 2018; Mao et al., 2022; Silver et al., 2022) that either requires pre-defined subtasks, object-centric representations with fully observable predicates, or hand-crafted skill libraries. The key conceptual departure is treating the induction of symbolic action models as a search problem over expression trees (via GP) rather than a supervised learning or template-matching problem, enabling the framework to discover novel effect rules in variant domains without re-annotation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled way to inject deductive planning structure into model-free DRL without requiring domain experts to hand-specify subtasks, which is a practical bottleneck in real-world hierarchical tasks. The compositional critical action network enables both task-level generalization (recombining known substructures for new goals) and rule-level generalization (evolving GP populations to adapt effect rules), demonstrated by 90–95% success on variant domains where BC-PPO and GAIL collapse to 0–41%. However, the evaluation is confined to deterministic, fully observable 8×8 gridworlds with integer state variables; extending to stochastic, partially observable, or continuous-state environments remains open, and the authors acknowledge that induction can fail entirely in such settings, degrading the framework to vanilla PPO.
