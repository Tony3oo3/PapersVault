## Problem

The best-known convergence guarantee for the EF21 error feedback method with contractive compressors (e.g., TopK) depends on the quadratic mean L_QM of per-client smoothness constants, which can be orders of magnitude larger than the arithmetic mean L_AM in heterogeneous data regimes. This means the theory provides no communication-complexity improvement over uncompressed gradient descent in the general smooth nonconvex setting, despite EF21's strong empirical performance. The paper closes this gap by replacing L_QM with L_AM in the convergence rate.

## Input / Output

The method operates on n clients each holding a local L_i-smooth loss f_i, with the global objective f = (1/n)Σf_i. It takes an initial model x^0, a contractive compressor C_i^t ∈ C(α) (e.g., TopK with α=K/d), and a stepsize γ bounded by 1/(L + L_AM·ξ(α)), and outputs a uniformly random iterate x̂^T from the trajectory {x^0, …, x^{T−1}} satisfying E‖∇f(x̂^T)‖² ≤ ε. The key practical difference from vanilla EF21 is that the admissible stepsize is governed by L_AM rather than L_QM, permitting substantially larger steps when smoothness constants are heterogeneous.

## Method

The paper develops the L_AM improvement through a three-step discovery. First, a client-cloning reformulation shows that duplicating client i ⌈L_i/L_AM⌉ times and rescaling its loss reduces the quadratic mean of the new smoothness constants to at most √2·L_AM, yielding the improved rate via existing EF21 theory. Second, this cloning idea is translated into a new algorithm EF21-W that uses smoothness-proportional weights w_i = L_i/Σ_j L_j in both the gradient aggregation (g^t = Σ w_i g_i^t) and the local error-feedback update (g_i^{t+1} = g_i^t + C_i^t((1/(nw_i))∇f_i(x^{t+1}) − g_i^t)), achieving the L_AM rate on the original n clients without any cloning. Third, a new weighted Lyapunov analysis of the original unweighted EF21—defining the distortion as G^t = (1/n²)Σ (1/w_i)‖g_i^t − ∇f_i(x^t)‖²—shows that vanilla EF21 itself inherits the L_AM convergence rate, since the critical sum Σ L_i²/w_i collapses to (Σ L_i)²/n² = L_AM². The technique extends to stochastic-gradient (EF21-W-SGD) and partial-participation (EF21-W-PP) variants, and yields linear convergence under Polyak-Łojasiewicz.

## Results

The paper's headline theoretical result improves the communication complexity of EF21 from O((L + L_QM·ξ(α))·ε⁻¹) to O((L + L_AM·ξ(α))·ε⁻¹), where L_QM is the quadratic mean and L_AM is the arithmetic mean of per-client smoothness constants (L_AM ≤ L_QM always, with strict inequality under heterogeneity). Empirically, on non-convex logistic regression with Top1 compression and n=1,000 clients, EF21-W with the improved stepsize consistently converges faster than original EF21, with the gap most pronounced on AUSTRALIAN (L_var ≈ 10¹⁶) and W1A (L_var ≈ 3.28). On synthetic non-convex linear problems (n=2,000, L=50), EF21-W matches or surpasses EF21 across all four settings of L_var ranging from ~4.4×10⁶ down to ~5.4×10³, with the advantage diminishing as L_QM and L_AM converge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the original EF21 method (Richtárik et al., 2021), which uses a stepsize bounded by 1/(L + L_QM·ξ(α)). EF21-W, with its improved stepsize 1/(L + L_AM·ξ(α)), consistently achieves faster convergence in all six LIBSVM experiments (Figure 1) and all four synthetic experiments (Figure 3), with the advantage scaling with the magnitude of L_var = L_QM² − L_AM². On the most heterogeneous dataset (AUSTRALIAN, L_var ≈ 10¹⁶), the gap is dramatic; on the least heterogeneous (PHISHING, L_var ≈ 9×10⁻⁴), the two methods are nearly indistinguishable, as expected since L_QM ≈ L_AM. The second baseline is EF21-SGD and EF21-PP (Fatkhullin et al., 2021), the stochastic-gradient and partial-participation variants of vanilla EF21. The weighted counterparts EF21-W-SGD and EF21-W-PP (Figure 2) demonstrate faster convergence on both W1A and AUSTRALIAN, though the larger permitted stepsize in the weighted variants introduces more pronounced oscillations in the stochastic setting (Figure 2c). Notably, the paper also shows that the original unweighted EF21 itself inherits the L_AM-based rate (Theorem 4) via a new smoothness-weighted analysis, meaning the practical algorithm need not change—only the theoretical guarantee and the admissible stepsize improve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on EF21 (Richtárik et al., 2021), the current state-of-the-art theory for distributed optimization with contractive (biased) compressors such as TopK and RankK, and on its practical extensions EF21-SGD and EF21-PP (Fatkhullin et al., 2021). The key departure is a conceptual re-analysis: by first exploring a 'client cloning' reformulation that replaces the quadratic mean of smoothness constants with the arithmetic mean, the authors discover that the original EF21 algorithm already enjoys this tighter rate, requiring only a smoothness-weighted Lyapunov analysis rather than any algorithmic modification. The weighted variant EF21-W is an intermediate step in this discovery, and the final result (Theorem 4) shows the weights can be pushed entirely into the proof.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The improvement from L_QM to L_AM is most impactful in heterogeneous data regimes where smoothness constants vary widely across clients (e.g., federated learning with non-IID data), where the gap can be orders of magnitude. The elegant finding that no algorithmic change is needed—only a better analysis—means practitioners using EF21 with TopK or RankK already benefit from the tighter stepsize bound. However, the paper itself notes that even with this improvement, the best theory for contractive compressors still merely matches the communication complexity of uncompressed methods in the general smooth nonconvex regime, leaving a substantial gap between theory and the strong empirical performance of biased compressors. Open questions include whether the analysis can be pushed further (e.g., to sublinear dependence on smoothness heterogeneity) and whether the PL-condition linear convergence results extend to more realistic settings.
