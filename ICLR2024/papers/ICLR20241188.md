## Problem

Existing multimodal fusion methods treat all modalities symmetrically, combining them simultaneously in neural networks, which deviates from the brain's hierarchical and sequential processing of multimodal information. This paper addresses the gap by asking: can a neuro-inspired hierarchical architecture, where one modality serves as the primary input and others act as information detectors, produce more compact and effective multimodal representations than conventional fusion approaches?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241188/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes pre-extracted embeddings from three modalities—text (768-dim from BERT/DeBERTa), audio (283-dim MFCC/Mel features from Librosa for MUStARD, 74-dim for MOSI), and video (2048-dim ResNet-152 pool5 for MUStARD, 47-dim for MOSI)—but only the designated primary modality (X₀) is fed as the actual network input. The remaining two modalities serve as reconstruction targets ('detectors') during training and are entirely inactive at inference time. The output is a compact latent state (e.g., 64-dim B₁) passed to a task-specific head producing either a binary sarcasm label or a continuous sentiment intensity score.

## Method

ITHP constructs a two-level (generalizable to N-level) information bottleneck hierarchy where a stochastic encoder compresses the primary modality X₀ into latent state B₀ while an output MLP reconstructs X₁ from B₀, and a second encoder compresses B₀ into B₁ while another MLP reconstructs X₂ from B₁. The training loss at each level balances KL divergence (minimizing mutual information between input and latent state for compression) against negative log-likelihood (maximizing mutual information between latent state and the detector modality for relevance), weighted by Lagrange multipliers β and γ. The overall loss combines both IB levels with a task-related loss on the final latent state B₁, scaled by α. When integrated with DeBERTa for text feature extraction, the model is trained end-to-end, and the hierarchical structure ensures that only the most relevant, non-redundant information from each modality is progressively distilled into an increasingly compact representation.

## Results

On CMU-MOSI, ITHP-DeBERTa achieves 88.7% Binary Accuracy, 88.6% F1, 0.643 MAE, and 0.852 Pearson Correlation, surpassing the human-level benchmark (85.7% BA, 87.5% F1, 0.710 MAE, 0.820 Corr) on all four metrics. On CMU-MOSEI, ITHP reaches 87.3% BA and 87.4% F1, outperforming the best DeBERTa-based baseline (MAGd: 85.8% BA, 85.9% F1). On MUStARD sarcasm detection, ITHP achieves 75.2% F-Score with the V→T→A modality order, improving over the MSDM baseline (71.5% F-Score) by 3.7 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ITHP-DeBERTa outperforms all 14 baselines on CMU-MOSI across every metric. Against the strongest DeBERTa-based competitor MAGd (86.1% BA, 0.690 MAE, 0.831 Corr), ITHP gains +2.6 BA, −0.047 MAE, and +0.021 Corr. The BERT-based baselines (Self-MMb, MMIMb, MAGb) cluster around 84% BA, while ITHP's DeBERTa integration pushes performance well above this range. On CMU-MOSEI, ITHP (87.3% BA, 0.813 Corr) beats MMIMb (86.0% BA, 0.772 Corr) and MAGd (85.8% BA, 0.800 Corr), though MMIMb retains a slight MAE edge (0.526 vs. 0.564). On MUStARD, ITHP (75.2% F-Score) surpasses both MSDM (71.5%) and HFM (68.9%), with the largest gap appearing in the V-A two-modal combination where MSDM underperforms its unimodal baseline while ITHP still improves. Notably, Self-MM degrades catastrophically under DeBERTa (55.1% BA vs. 84.0% under BERT), highlighting ITHP's robustness to backbone swaps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Information Bottleneck (IB) principle from Tishby et al. (2000) and its deep variational formulation (Lee & van der Schaar, 2021) into a novel hierarchical multimodal fusion architecture. Unlike prior multimodal fusion methods (TFN, MFN, MulT, MAG) that treat all modalities symmetrically through concatenation, tensor products, or cross-attention, ITHP introduces an asymmetric structure where a designated 'prime' modality serves as the sole input and remaining modalities act as detectors in a sequential information pathway. The neuro-inspired framing—drawing on hierarchical brain connectivity research (Sepulcre et al., 2012; Raut et al., 2020)—motivates the ordered, incremental distillation of information across modalities rather than simultaneous fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ITHP provides a principled, information-theoretic alternative to ad-hoc multimodal fusion, with the appealing property that it can be plugged into existing pre-trained LLM backbones (DeBERTa) as a modular fusion layer. The claim of surpassing human-level benchmarks on all four CMU-MOSI metrics is notable, though the margin over the prior SOTA (MAG-DeBERTa) is modest (~2.6% BA). A key open question is the reliance on preset modality ordering: without domain knowledge to select the prime modality and rank others, the approach becomes combinatorially expensive, and the paper acknowledges this as a significant limitation. Future work on learned or automatic modality ordering would substantially broaden applicability.
