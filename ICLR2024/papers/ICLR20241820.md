## Problem

In federated learning, clients with heterogeneous device resources (memory, compute, bandwidth) require differently sized local models, but existing subnetwork-training approaches still transmit the full pruned model structure back to the server, leaking architectural information and incurring unnecessary communication costs. The paper identifies that the privacy implications of continuously sending complete model structures under model heterogeneity remain unexplored, and that no framework simultaneously addresses personalized layer assignment, dual-level pruning, and privacy-preserving client-to-server communication.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241820/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client receives a pruned subset of global model weights (determined by a server-side pruning mask P_i) and is assigned a specific subset of layers L_i to train locally on its private image data (32×32 RGB for CIFAR-10/100 and FashionMNIST, 28×28 grayscale for EMNIST-L). Critically, the client only transmits back the weights of its assigned trained layers to the server, not the full model, so the server never observes the complete local architecture. The server outputs an updated global model via layer-wise aggregation, while each client retains a personalized local model sized to its resource constraints.

## Method

FedP3 introduces a dual-pruning federated framework with three coupled mechanisms: (1) server-to-client global pruning (mask P_i) shrinks the model before deployment to match each client's memory budget; (2) client-specific local pruning (mask Q_i, supporting fixed, uniform, or ordered-dropout strategies) further sparsifies the non-trained layers during local SGD steps; and (3) layer-wise personalization assigns each client a subset of layers L_i to update, with only those layers' weights sent back to the server for aggregation. The server aggregates received layer subsets using simple, weighted (proportional to |L_i|), or learned attention averaging. Theoretically, the paper formalizes global pruning as a biased diagonal sketch and personalized aggregation as a permutation-based sketch, proving O(d/ε²) communication complexity for convergence to an ε-stationary point, and extends the analysis to a locally differentially private variant (LDP-FedP3) with (ε,δ)-LDP guarantees via Gaussian perturbation.

## Results

On ResNet18 with CIFAR-10/100 under class-wise non-iid data, the partial pruning variant -B3(part) achieves 73.47% and 62.39% top-1 accuracy respectively, matching or exceeding the full model (73.25% / 63.33%) while reducing communication by 6.25%. In the layer-overlap experiments across four datasets, OPU3 (3 layers trained per client) matches FedAvg accuracy with only 80% of parameters communicated (20% communication reduction), and OPU2 achieves 40% reduction. Against the personalized FL baseline FedCR, FedCR-OPU3 incurs only a 2.56% accuracy drop (S1) and 3.20% drop (S2) across all four datasets while saving 20% communication. The LDP-FedP3 variant achieves (ε,δ)-LDP with communication complexity O(mε√(dLΔ₀)/(C√log(1/δ)) + m²ε²/log(1/δ)), notably independent of the number of clients n.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FedAvg, FedP3's OPU3 configuration (3 layers per client) achieves comparable top-1 accuracy across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, EMNIST-L, and FashionMNIST while communicating only 80% of parameters; OPU2 and LowerB push reductions to 40% and 60% respectively at modest accuracy cost. Against FedCR (the state-of-the-art personalized FL method), FedCR-OPU3 shows only a 2.56% average accuracy drop under class-wise non-iid (S1) and 3.20% under Dirichlet non-iid (S2) across all four datasets, while saving 20% communication. Theoretically, FedP3's communication cost of O(d/ε²) improves over Distributed Gradient Descent's O(nd/ε) by a factor of O(n/ε), and over DGD under the same smoothness bound by a factor of O(d/n). In the LDP setting (Table 5), LDP-FedP3's communication complexity is independent of the number of clients n, whereas Q-DPSGD-1, LDP SVRG/SPIDER, SDM-DSGD, and CDP-SGD all scale with n (e.g., n^{3/2} or n^{7/2} factors), making FedP3 particularly advantageous in large-scale deployments. On ResNet18, the -B3(part) variant actually surpasses the full model on CIFAR-10 (73.47% vs 73.25%), demonstrating that selective layer pruning can sometimes enhance rather than degrade performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedP3 extends Independent Subnetwork Training (IST) by Yuan et al. (2022) with two key departures: personalized layer assignment (each client trains a different subset of layers based on its resource constraints) and privacy-friendly communication (clients transmit only their trained layers rather than the full pruned model). The theoretical analysis builds on the sketch-based framework of Shulgin & Richtárik (2023) and the permutation compressor of Szlendak et al. (2021), while the LDP variant reformulates the privacy guarantees from SoteriaFL (Li et al., 2022). The paper also positions itself against federated network pruning methods (FjORD, FedRolex, Flado) by offering a unified framework that accommodates multiple local pruning strategies rather than prescribing a single one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical FL bottleneck—model heterogeneity across clients with varying compute, memory, and bandwidth—while simultaneously reducing communication costs by O(n/ε) over standard distributed gradient descent and achieving n-independent communication in the LDP variant. This is particularly relevant as FL is applied to increasingly large models (including LLMs) where full-model transmission is infeasible. Open questions include whether the theoretical gains hold at LLM scale, whether tighter gradient bounds can further reduce communication, and how the layer-wise aggregation strategies (especially attention-based) perform with deeper, more heterogeneous architectures. The experiments, while thorough across four datasets, are limited to relatively small networks (ResNet18 at most), leaving scalability to billion-parameter models untested.
