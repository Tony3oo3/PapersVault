## Problem

Subspace activation patching methods such as DAS optimize for end-to-end causal effect, but this objective is insufficient to guarantee that the found direction faithfully represents a feature in the model's computation. The paper identifies a specific failure mode — an 'interpretability illusion' — where a direction achieves the intended causal effect not by modulating an existing computational pathway, but by activating a dormant parallel pathway through a component that is causally disconnected from the model output. This undermines the interpretability claims of prior work that relies solely on end-to-end patching success as evidence of feature localization.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pre-trained transformer at a specific token position (last token for IOI, last subject token for factual recall) and a specific component (MLP hidden activations or residual stream). The key diagnostic output is not just the found direction but its orthogonal decomposition into the nullspace of the downstream projection matrix W_out (the causally disconnected component) and its rowspace (the causally relevant component), revealing which part actually drives the observed causal effect. Metrics include fractional logit difference decrease (FLDD) and interchange accuracy, with the critical test being whether removing the nullspace component eliminates the effect.

## Method

The paper formalizes the illusion as a decomposition v = v_disconnected + v_dormant, where v_disconnected lies in the nullspace of all downstream components (correlated with the input feature but causally inert) and v_dormant is causally relevant but not naturally activated by the input variation; patching along their sum transfers the disconnected component's input-dependent variation into the dormant component, fabricating a causal effect. Empirically, DAS finds a 1D direction v_MLP in GPT-2 Small's layer-8 MLP that appears to encode name position in the IOI task, but decomposing it shows the nullspace component (cosine similarity ~0.9 with input variation) drives the effect while the rowspace component is dormant. In contrast, the analogous directions v_resid (residual stream) and v_grad (gradient of name-mover attention scores) pass the same nullspace/rowspace tests and are validated as faithful through additional checks (S-inhibition heads write to v_resid, the direction generalizes to OpenWebText prompts). The paper further proves that any 1D activation patch along v in an MLP layer implies an equivalent rank-1 ROME weight edit (Lemma D.2), providing a mechanistic explanation for why rank-1 fact editing succeeds even on layers not storing the fact — the edit creates a dormant pathway rather than modifying an existing one.

## Results

On the IOI task (GPT-2 Small), the illusory MLP direction v_MLP achieves 46.7% FLDD and 4.2% interchange accuracy, but this effect drops to 13.5% FLDD when only the causally relevant (rowspace) component is patched, and to 0% when only the causally disconnected (nullspace) component is patched. In contrast, the faithful residual stream direction v_resid achieves 140.7% FLDD and 74.8% interchange accuracy, with the rowspace component alone retaining 127.5% FLDD and 63.1% interchange accuracy. For factual recall (GPT-2 XL, COUNTERFACT), DAS-found 1D patches strongly change the recalled object, but this effect vanishes when the nullspace component of W_out is ablated, while full MLP patching has negligible effect.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not propose a new method to benchmark against baselines; instead, it contrasts illusory vs. faithful subspace patches to demonstrate an interpretability illusion. The key contrast is between v_MLP (found in MLP8 hidden activations) and v_resid (found in the layer-8 residual stream): v_MLP appears to mediate position information (46.7% FLDD) but its effect is driven entirely by the causally disconnected nullspace component (rowspace alone gives only 13.5% FLDD), whereas v_resid's effect is robust to removing the nullspace component (127.5% vs. 140.7% FLDD). Full MLP patching actually increases confidence in the correct answer (−8% FLDD), confirming that the MLP layer is not part of the IOI circuit. In the factual recall setting, the DAS-found direction successfully changes the recalled object, but ablating the nullspace component eliminates the effect entirely, while patching the full MLP activation has negligible impact—mirroring the IOI illusion. The paper further shows that the illusory 1D fact patch is mathematically equivalent to a rank-1 ROME edit, providing a mechanistic explanation for why rank-1 editing works even on layers not storing the fact (Hase et al., 2023).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the activation patching framework (Vig et al., 2020; Geiger et al., 2020) from full-component interventions to 1-dimensional subspace patches, a generalization recently proposed by DAS (Geiger et al., 2023b) and related work. Its central contribution is identifying a fundamental failure mode of this subspace variant: a direction can exhibit strong end-to-end causal effect while being composed of a causally disconnected component (correlated with the feature) and a dormant causal component (not naturally activated by the data). The paper also draws a formal equivalence between 1D subspace patches and rank-1 ROME edits (Meng et al., 2022a), providing a mechanistic explanation for the localization-editing discrepancy observed by Hase et al. (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result challenges a core working assumption in mechanistic interpretability—that a direction with a strong causal effect via patching faithfully represents the feature the model actually uses—and provides a concrete recipe (check the nullspace/rowspace decomposition of the direction relative to downstream weights) for detecting the illusion. Practical recommendations include preferring residual-stream bottlenecks over MLP activations for subspace search and requiring validations beyond end-to-end causal effect. Open questions include how prevalent the illusion is in larger, more complex models, whether it persists with higher-dimensional subspaces in practice, and what additional criteria (beyond the nullspace test) are needed to certify faithfulness of a patched direction.
