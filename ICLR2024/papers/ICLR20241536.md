## Problem

While gradient clipping is essential for differential privacy in federated learning, there has been no rigorous theoretical comparison of the two dominant strategies—per-sample and per-update clipping—on FedAvg convergence under arbitrary clipping thresholds. Prior analyses either required specific threshold values, relied on strong bounded-gradient or uniformly-bounded-noise assumptions that fail for deep networks, or did not account for data heterogeneity via gradient dissimilarity.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes n workers each holding local data distribution D_i, a clipping threshold c (any positive value), and stepsizes (η for per-sample; η_l, η_g for per-update), and produces a convergence bound on min_t E[‖∇f(x̄_t)‖] that explicitly separates the effect of stochastic noise σ, gradient dissimilarity ζ, and the threshold c. The key structural detail is that the guarantee holds for any c without requiring c to be tuned to a specific value, and the neighborhood size for per-sample clipping is O(min(σ+ζ, (σ²+ζ²)/c)), while per-update clipping can drive the error to zero by shrinking η_l.

## Method

The paper proves convergence of FedAvg under a distributed (L₀,L₁)-smoothness assumption, bounded variance σ², and gradient dissimilarity ζ², for both per-sample clipping (clipping each mini-batch gradient at every local step) and per-update clipping (clipping the accumulated local update after τ steps). The central finding is an asymmetry: per-sample clipping is stuck at a neighborhood whose radius is Ω(min(σ+ζ, (σ²+ζ²)/c)) and cannot be eliminated without increasing c, whereas per-update clipping can reach any target accuracy by choosing η_l ≤ O(c/(√τ·σ + τ·ζ)), because the inner stepsize controls the norm of the update before clipping. The authors prove tightness via explicit counterexamples and extend both results to differentially private variants with Gaussian noise, showing that per-update clipping's privacy-utility trade-off is no better than per-sample's due to the noise scaling with the number of rounds R.

## Results

The paper provides tight convergence guarantees for two clipping strategies in FedAvg under bounded variance σ² and gradient dissimilarity ζ² assumptions. Per-sample clipping converges to a neighborhood of size O(min(σ+ζ, (σ²+ζ²)/c)), which is shown to be tight via explicit counterexamples; reaching any target accuracy ε requires c ≥ Θ((σ²+ζ²)/ε). Per-update clipping, in contrast, can converge to any accuracy for an arbitrary clipping threshold c by choosing the inner stepsize η_l ≤ O(c/(√τ·σ + τ·ζ)), at the cost of more communication rounds. Experiments on MNIST with varying data heterogeneity confirm that per-sample clipping plateaus at a neighborhood under high heterogeneity (e.g., single-class workers) unless c is increased, while per-update clipping eventually reaches the target accuracy ε=0.18.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Koloskova et al. (2023) on clipped mini-batch SGD (single worker), the paper's per-sample result extends the analysis to the federated multi-worker setting with gradient dissimilarity ζ, showing the neighborhood size becomes O(min(σ+ζ, (σ²+ζ²)/c)) rather than O(min(σ, σ²/c)). Against Liu et al. (2022) on clipped LocalSGD, the paper handles heterogeneous data (ζ>0) and uses the weaker bounded-variance assumption instead of uniformly bounded noise, yielding a tighter characterization of the clipping bias. Versus Zhang et al. (2022) on per-update clipped FedAvg, the paper avoids the bounded-gradient assumption and provides a cleaner separation between the FedAvg convergence term and the clipping bias term, showing that the standard FedAvg rate is recovered as c→∞. Compared to Karimireddy et al. (2019) on unclipped FedAvg, the per-update result adds explicit clipping terms F₀/(cη_g R) + (η_l τ/c)(σ²/τ+ζ²) that vanish as c grows, while the per-sample result introduces an irreducible bias term min(σ+ζ, (σ²+ζ²)/c) absent in the unclipped case. Against Yang et al. (2022) on FAT clipping with β-moment assumptions, the paper works under the more standard bounded-variance assumption and provides guarantees for arbitrary c rather than requiring specific c values tied to the β-moment bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-worker clipped SGD convergence analysis of Koloskova et al. (2023) to the multi-worker federated setting (FedAvg), and unifies the previously separate analyses of per-sample clipping (Liu et al., 2022) and per-update clipping (Zhang et al., 2022) under a common set of assumptions: bounded variance (allowing heavy-tailed noise), gradient dissimilarity (capturing data heterogeneity), and a distributed (L0,L1)-smoothness condition adapted from Zhang et al. (2020b). Unlike prior work that required bounded gradients or uniformly bounded stochastic noise, the analysis here works under weaker moment conditions, and unlike prior federated clipping analyses that treated each clipping strategy in isolation, this paper provides the first side-by-side theoretical comparison of both methods under identical assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with a clear theoretical distinction: per-sample clipping is fundamentally limited to converging to a neighborhood whose size depends on the clipping threshold, while per-update clipping can reach any accuracy by tuning the inner stepsize at the cost of more communication rounds. This has direct implications for DP-FedAvg design, where the choice between example-level and user-level privacy dictates which clipping method is used, and the analysis reveals that the privacy-utility trade-off is no better for per-update clipping despite its superior convergence properties. Open questions include whether incorporating an inner stepsize into per-sample clipping (as the authors suggest) can close the gap, and whether the stepsize dependence on maximum gradient norm (required under (L0,L1)-smoothness with L1>0) can be avoided in practice.
