## Problem

Pre-trained LLMs cannot natively process time series data, and existing approaches either require expensive fine-tuning that degrades language ability or naively serialize TS as text, which fails for multivariate sequences. The paper tackles the underexplored TS-for-LLM paradigm: how to create LLM-compatible TS embeddings without modifying the frozen LLM, bridging the gap between unconstrained contrastive TS representations and the LLM's text embedding space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241482/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts univariate or multivariate time series of arbitrary length, which are tokenized via sliding window into subsequences. A causal TCN encoder maps each token to a vector matching the LLM's embedding dimension (e.g., 768 for GPT-2 Small, 4096 for LLaMA-2), and these TS embeddings are injected directly into the LLM, skipping its embedding layer. Task-specific soft prompts are prepended to the embedding sequence, and the frozen LLM produces class labels, forecasted values, or discriminative representations depending on the downstream task.

## Method

TEST trains a causal TCN encoder (10 dilated convolution blocks) via a three-level contrastive objective: (1) instance-wise contrast (SimCLR-style) pulls augmented views of the same token together while pushing other batch members apart; (2) feature-wise contrast treats columns of the batch embedding matrix as soft feature labels, aligning same-feature columns across positive pairs and differentiating different-feature columns to prevent representation collapse; (3) text-prototype-aligned contrast selects P representative text embeddings from the LLM's vocabulary as coordinate axes, projecting TS embeddings onto them so the TS embedding space is constrained to the LLM's text embedding geometry. A separate p-tuning soft prompt is then trained against the frozen LLM's output loss, and the paper proves this soft-prompt objective is approximately equivalent to supervised fine-tuning when TS and word embeddings are aligned, providing theoretical justification for the approach.

## Results

On long-term forecasting (GPT2-Medium backbone), TEST achieves average MSE of 0.353 on ETTm1, 0.414 on ETTh1, 0.331 on ETTh2, 0.162 on Electricity, 0.430 on Traffic, 0.229 on Weather, and 2.195 on ILI, matching or exceeding SOTA TS models like TimesNet and DLinear. On multivariate classification (UEA), TEST achieves an average rank of 4.012 and 24/30 datasets in Top-5, outperforming all 13 baselines. In few-shot forecasting (10% training data), TEST achieves a 23.5% relative average MSE reduction over baselines. In zero-shot forecasting, TEST attains the best average score of 15.93 across M4, M3, TOURISM, and ELECTR, surpassing N-BEATS (15.19) and GPT4TS (16.38).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TEST (GPT2-Medium) achieves comparable or superior MSE to GPT4TS on 6 of 7 long-term forecasting datasets, with the only exception being ILI where GPT4TS edges ahead (1.925 vs 2.195). Against SOTA TS transformers, TEST matches or beats TimesNet on 5/7 datasets and DLinear on 4/7, while dramatically outperforming Informer (e.g., 0.331 vs 4.431 MSE on ETTh2) and TCN (0.331 vs 3.095 on ETTh2). In multivariate classification (UEA), TEST achieves the best average rank (4.012) and most Top-5 placements (24/30), surpassing SVP-T (rank 4.032), MiniRocket (5.013), and all other baselines including T-Loss, TS2Vec, and CoST. In zero-shot forecasting, TEST achieves the best average (15.93) and ties N-BEATS for most first-place dataset counts, outperforming GPT4TS (16.38), PatchTST (17.67), and all other baselines. The LLM-QA baselines (PromptCast, Health Learner) and SFT method LLM4TS are substantially outperformed, confirming that TEST's embedding alignment strategy is far more effective than direct numerical prompting or full fine-tuning for TS tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

TEST positions itself as the first systematic instantiation of the TS-for-LLM paradigm (data-centric, modify the input rather than the model), in contrast to the dominant LLM-for-TS approaches (GPT4TS, LLM4TS) that fine-tune or retrain the LLM. Methodologically, it builds on instance-wise contrastive learning (SimCLR, MoCo) and prototype-level contrast (SwAV, Contrastive Clustering) for time series representation, but introduces a novel text-prototype-aligned contrast that maps TS embeddings into the LLM's pre-trained text embedding space using selected text tokens as coordinate axes. The soft prompt component draws directly from p-tuning (Lester et al., 2021), and the paper provides a theoretical argument (Equation 5) that soft prompt tuning is approximately equivalent to supervised fine-tuning when the TS embedding space covers the text embedding space — a connection not previously established in the TS+LLM literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a completely frozen LLM, given properly aligned TS embeddings and a short soft prompt, can match or exceed dedicated SOTA TS models on classification, forecasting, and zero-shot transfer — without sacrificing the LLM's language capabilities. This is practically significant because TS-for-LLM requires far less data and compute than training a TS foundation model, and preserves the LLM's ability to handle mixed text+TS queries. However, the paper itself flags 'weak robust' as a limitation, and the alignment is acknowledged as somewhat forced at the machine level; how to achieve human-perceptual alignment between objective TS data and subjective text remains open. The finding that nearest-neighbor word matching reveals sentiment-like adjectives rather than numerical descriptors raises interesting questions about what LLMs actually compute over TS patterns.
