## Problem

Online training of spiking neural networks avoids the high memory cost of BPTT by processing time-steps sequentially, but this lack of access to future time-step information has prevented the incorporation of batch normalization, a key module for stabilizing training. Without BN, online SNN training suffers from temporal covariate shift and underperforms its BPTT counterparts. The paper asks whether normalization can be applied consistently across all time-steps in an online setting without requiring future data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242088/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts either RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet) or event streams (48×48 for DVS-Gesture, CIFAR10-DVS) and produces a single class label per sample. Inputs are unrolled into T discrete time-steps (T=4 for CIFAR/ImageNet, T=10 for CIFAR10-DVS, T=20 for DVS-Gesture), where each time-step is processed sequentially in both forward and backward passes. The output is determined by accumulated spikes across all time-steps, with a TET loss that provides per-time-step feedback rather than a single final-time-step loss.

## Method

The paper introduces two modules within an online SNN training framework. Online Spiking Renormalization (OSR) normalizes input currents using a 'double transformation': it first applies standard batch normalization with current time-step statistics (preserving gradient flow), then linearly re-transforms the result using running mean and variance from previous batches, ensuring identical normalization parameters between training and inference across all time-steps. An online accumulation scheme computes all-time mean and variance from per-time-step statistics without storing intermediate data. Online Threshold Stabilizer (OTS) dynamically adjusts the LIF neuron firing threshold at each time-step to maintain a constant firing rate, using a Gaussian assumption on membrane potential to map the desired quantile back to a threshold value. The backward pass removes temporal dependencies through membrane potential decay and reset, making gradients at each time-step independent. A theoretical analysis proves that stabilizing firing rates (via OTS) reduces the variance of input means across time-steps (stabilizing OSR) while leaving within-time-step variance unchanged.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the proposed method (OSR+OTS) achieves 95.20% top-1 accuracy with ResNet-19 at T=4 time steps, outperforming the online baseline OTTT (93.58%, T=6) by 1.62%. On ImageNet, it reaches 67.54% with NF-ResNet-34 at T=4, surpassing OTTT (65.15%, T=6) by 2.39% while using fewer time steps. Among online SNN training algorithms, the method achieves state-of-the-art performance on all five evaluated datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, CIFAR10-DVS, DVS-Gesture, ImageNet) with constant memory usage regardless of the number of time steps, unlike BPTT methods whose memory scales linearly with T.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among online SNN training algorithms, the proposed method (OSR+OTS) outperforms OTTT on all five datasets with fewer time steps (T=4 vs. T=6): CIFAR-10 (95.20% vs. 93.58%), CIFAR-100 (76.48% vs. 71.11%), DVS-Gesture (97.57% vs. 96.88%), CIFAR10-DVS (82.40% vs. 76.30%), and ImageNet (67.54% vs. 65.15%). Against BPTT-based baselines, the method is competitive but slightly behind the best BPTT methods: on CIFAR-10 it reaches 95.20% vs. TEBN's 95.58%, on CIFAR-100 it achieves 76.48% vs. TEBN's 78.71%, and on ImageNet it scores 67.54% vs. TEBN's 68.28%. However, the method uses fewer time steps (T=4 vs. T=4–6) and maintains constant memory usage regardless of T, whereas BPTT methods scale memory linearly with time steps. On CIFAR10-DVS, the method (82.40%) surpasses tdBN (67.80%) by a large margin and approaches TET (83.17%) and TEBN (84.90%). The vanilla BN baseline on ImageNet (60.48%) confirms the necessity of the proposed OSR mechanism, which adds approximately 4% accuracy over per-time-step normalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two SNN research threads: online training (RTRL, OTTT, Zenke & Ganguli) and temporal normalization (tdBN, TEBN, BNTT). Prior online SNN methods deliberately avoided normalization modules because they require all-time statistics unavailable in a forward-only pass, while BPTT-based normalization methods (tdBN, TEBN) achieve strong results but sacrifice the memory efficiency and biological plausibility of online learning. The paper's key departure is the 'double transformation' in OSR—first normalizing with per-time-step statistics (preserving gradient flow) then applying a linear rescaling with running statistics (unifying the transformation across time-steps)—which had no direct precedent in either the RNN online learning or SNN normalization literature. The OTS mechanism, which adjusts firing thresholds via a Gaussian quantile argument, is also novel in the SNN training context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work makes batch normalization practically viable in online SNN training, closing a significant gap that had kept online methods behind their BPTT counterparts. The theoretical results (Theorems 4.2–4.3) provide a clean mechanistic explanation for why stabilizing firing rates reduces temporal covariate shift in the normalization statistics, giving practitioners a principled design rationale rather than a heuristic. However, the method still trails BPTT-based approaches by a few points, and the authors acknowledge that the missing inter-layer and inner-layer temporal dependencies in backpropagation are the primary bottleneck. Open questions include whether eligibility traces or other local learning mechanisms can recover some of this gap while preserving the online property, and whether the approach extends to event-driven (asynchronous) SNN training.
