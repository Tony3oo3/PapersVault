## Problem

LLMs exhibit strong in-context learning but suffer from poor OOD generalization and hallucination in NLU and QA tasks, while supervised task-specific language models (SLMs) capture task knowledge but lack general multi-task capacity. Despite their complementary strengths, prior work has largely treated LLMs and SLMs as independent paradigms, with little systematic exploration of how SLMs can augment LLM inference to improve reliability on out-of-distribution data and reduce hallucinations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text input (sentence or question), the SLM's predicted label and confidence score (inserted as a 'receipt' between question-answer pairs in the prompt), and optionally 16 in-domain few-shot examples also annotated with SLM predictions. For NLU tasks it outputs a class label; for QA (SQuAD 2.0) it outputs a JSON-formatted answer span or the string '?' for unanswerable questions. An optional interpreter prompt additionally elicits the index of influential in-context examples and a sentence-level rationale. Prompt length is capped at 4,096 tokens for Llama2-7B-chat, and the supervised knowledge occupies only a short span (~30× shorter than 16-shot examples alone).

## Method

SuperContext is a plug-in inference-time framework that inserts a fine-tuned discriminative SLM's prediction and confidence score as a 'receipt' r_i between each question-answer pair in the LLM's prompt, formalized as p_LLM(r_i, y_j | {x_i, r_i, y_i}_{i∈S_j}, x_j, s_i). The SLM (ELECTRA-large for NLU, RoBERTa-large for QA) is fine-tuned on in-domain data and then used zero-shot on OOD test sets, so its confidence reflects genuine generalization ability rather than memorization. The confidence score acts as a calibration channel: when the SLM is highly confident, the LLM tends to follow its prediction; when confidence is low, the LLM leverages its own domain knowledge, exploiting the complementarity between task-specific and pretraining knowledge. Unlike contrastive decoding (which requires joint training) or retrieval-augmented methods (which query external knowledge bases), SuperContext requires no additional training of the LLM and uses the SLM itself as the consulting agent. An optional interpreter prompt s_i further asks the LLM to identify which in-context example influenced its decision and what rationale it used, enabling post-hoc analysis of the mechanism.

## Results

On the GLUE-X OOD benchmark (8 NLU tasks, 15 OOD datasets), SuperContext with ChatGPT achieves 80.05% average accuracy in zero-shot, surpassing ELECTRA-large (79.86%), ChatGPT zero-shot (66.67%), and ChatGPT +16-shot ICL (72.28%). With Llama2-7B-chat, SuperContext (16-shot) reaches 79.08% average, dramatically outperforming Llama2-chat zero-shot (55.92%) and 16-shot ICL (58.54%). On SQuAD 2.0 QA, SuperContext (zero-shot) improves exact match from 55.82% (ChatGPT) to 57.68% and no-answer accuracy from 32.35% to 54.65%, demonstrating significant hallucination reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ELECTRA-large (the 334M-parameter SLM baseline), SuperContext with ChatGPT achieves a marginal average improvement (80.05% vs. 79.86%), while SuperContext with Llama2-7B-chat substantially outperforms ELECTRA-large (79.08% vs. 76.84%), particularly on MRPC (54.63% vs. 37.59%) and CoLA (45.47% vs. 45.73%). Against ChatGPT zero-shot, SuperContext yields a large 13.38-point average gain (80.05% vs. 66.67%), with the most dramatic improvement on MNLI (87.24% vs. 41.54%) and QQP (78.65% vs. 43.23%). Against Llama2-7B-chat, SuperContext (16-shot) delivers a 23.16-point average improvement (79.08% vs. 55.92%), effectively closing the gap with the proprietary ChatGPT model. On SQuAD 2.0, SuperContext outperforms ChatGPT on no-answer accuracy (54.65% vs. 32.35%) and exact match (57.68% vs. 55.82%), while ChatGPT retains an edge on has-answer accuracy (79.35% vs. 60.71%), indicating that the SLM's discriminative signal is most valuable for detecting unanswerable questions.

## Relation to Prior Work

SuperContext sits at the intersection of in-context learning (ICL) and model-augmentation literature. It departs from contrastive decoding (Li et al., 2023b), which requires training-time contrastive mapping between expert and amateur models, by instead inserting a fine-tuned SLM's prediction and confidence directly into the LLM's prompt at inference time—no retraining of the LLM is needed. Conceptually, it is closest to SuperICL (Xu et al., 2023a) and HuggingGPT (Shen et al., 2023) in treating a smaller model as a plug-in, but distinguishes itself by targeting out-of-distribution generalization (via GLUE-X) and hallucination reduction (via SQuAD 2.0), and by adding an optional interpretation prompt that asks the LLM to identify which in-context example influenced its decision. Unlike retrieval-augmented approaches that consult external knowledge bases or search engines, SuperContext's 'consulting agent' is a task-specific discriminative model, shifting the goal from knowledge acquisition to reliable task adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 334M-parameter ELECTRA-large, used purely as a prompt-level hint, can lift Llama2-7B-chat's OOD NLU average from 58.54% to 79.08%, surpassing ChatGPT's 16-shot ICL (72.28%)—a practical, low-cost recipe for improving LLM reliability without fine-tuning the LLM itself. The dramatic QA result (no-answer accuracy jumping from 32.35% to 54.65% with SuperContext, while 16-shot ICL *degrades* it to 13.22%) highlights a concrete hallucination-reduction pathway. Key limitations temper the impact: the method requires a well-trained SLM per task, the optional interpreter component yields negligible gains, and evaluation is confined to two LLMs and one SLM architecture. Open questions include whether the SLM–LLM complementarity stems from pre-training data or few-shot demonstrations, and whether the approach extends to open-ended generation tasks beyond classification and extractive QA.
