## Problem

Large language models confidently generate factually incorrect claims in long-form text, yet existing approaches to improving factuality either require expensive human fact-checking labels, target only short-form question answering, or rely on inference-time interventions that add system complexity. This paper asks whether models can be fine-tuned to reduce factual errors in open-ended, long-form generation using entirely automated preference signals, without any human annotation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241749/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes open-ended text prompts (e.g., 'Write a biography of [person]' or 'What are the common symptoms of [condition]?') and produces a fine-tuned autoregressive language model that generates more factual long-form paragraphs. During training, multiple candidate responses are sampled per prompt and scored for truthfulness; at inference time, the model generates text in the standard autoregressive manner with no additional retrieval or scoring overhead. The two evaluation domains are biographies of well-known individuals and medical condition question answering, each with Wikipedia-grounded ground truth.

## Method

The paper constructs factuality preference pairs through two complementary truthfulness estimators: (1) a reference-based approach using FactScore, which extracts atomic claims via GPT-3.5 and checks each against Wikipedia with a fine-tuned NLI model, and (2) a reference-free approach that converts each atomic claim into a minimally ambiguous question, resamples answers 20 times from a base model, and computes expected or max confidence as a truthfulness proxy. For each prompt, all pairwise combinations of sampled responses are ranked by their average truthfulness score, and the higher-scoring response is labeled preferred. The model is then fine-tuned using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), which optimizes a simple classification loss over preference pairs without requiring an explicit reward model or online sampling. This pipeline is applied to Llama-1-7b and Llama-2-7b (including the RLHF Chat variant), and the resulting FactTune-FS model achieves ~50% reduction in factual error rate on biographies and ~25% on medical QA compared to the base chat model.

## Results

On Llama-2-7b, FactTune-FS (reference-based factuality tuning) achieves 88.0% correct on Biographies and 80.9% correct on Medical QA, compared to 77.4% and 61.9% for the RLHF Chat baseline. This corresponds to a 53% reduction in factual error rate on biographies and a 50% reduction on medical questions relative to Llama-2-Chat. The reference-free variant FactTune-MC also outperforms RLHF on both tasks (78.3% vs. 77.4% on Biographies; 70.6% vs. 61.9% on Medical QA) without requiring any external knowledge base. Factuality tuning composes with DOLA decoding, further improving accuracy (e.g., 83.0% on Medical QA for Llama-2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FactTune-FS consistently outperforms all three baselines on both tasks and both model scales. Against ITI (Li et al., 2023), FactTune-FS achieves 88.0% vs. 71.2% on Llama-2 Biographies and 80.9% vs. 69.0% on Medical QA — a 17–12 percentage point gap. Against DOLA (Chuang et al., 2023), the advantage is even larger: 88.0% vs. 68.4% on Biographies and 80.9% vs. 62.7% on Medical QA for Llama-2. Against the RLHF Chat model (Llama-2-Chat, Touvron et al., 2023b), FactTune-FS improves from 77.4% to 88.0% on Biographies and from 61.9% to 80.9% on Medical QA, while also reducing the absolute number of incorrect facts (from 6.30 to 2.76 and from 6.69 to 2.97 respectively). The reference-free FactTune-MC variant also beats RLHF on both tasks (78.3% vs. 77.4% on Biographies; 70.6% vs. 61.9% on Medical QA) without any external reference, though it generates fewer total facts. Notably, FactTune-FS and DOLA are complementary: stacking DOLA on top of FactTune-FS further improves Medical QA accuracy from 80.9% to 83.0% on Llama-2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two recent NLP threads: preference-based fine-tuning via DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) and automated factuality estimation (FactScore via Min et al., 2023; calibration-based confidence via Kadavath et al., 2022). It departs from standard RLHF by replacing human preference labels with automatically generated factuality rankings, and from decoding-time interventions (DOLA, ITI) by modifying model weights rather than inference behavior. The key conceptual move is treating model calibration as a self-supervised signal for truthfulness, eliminating the need for external reference texts during preference construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pipeline offers a practical, low-cost path to reducing hallucinations in deployed LLMs without expensive human fact-checking, and the reference-free variant removes the dependency on curated knowledge bases. However, the evaluation is limited to 7B-scale models and two narrow domains (biographies, medical QA), leaving open whether the approach scales to larger models or generalizes to open-ended domains without reliable Wikipedia articles. The paper also leaves unresolved how best to integrate factuality preferences with standard RLHF reward signals, and whether the terser outputs observed post-tuning represent an acceptable trade-off for downstream applications.
