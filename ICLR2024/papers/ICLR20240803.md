## Problem

In-context learning (ICL) allows LLMs to incorporate private data as exemplars without fine-tuning, but the resulting prompts are vulnerable to prompt-leaking attacks where malicious users extract the sensitive in-context information. No principled framework existed for providing formal differential privacy guarantees to ICL-based systems, leaving a critical gap for real-world deployments involving healthcare records, financial data, or other sensitive domains.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query, a private exemplar dataset of query-answer pairs, and black-box access to an LLM API (GPT-3 Babbage/Davinci, GPT-4, or OpenLLaMA-13B). The private exemplar set is Poisson-subsampled and partitioned into N disjoint subsets, each concatenated with the query to form a separate prompt. For text classification, the output is a single class label; for language generation (document QA, dialog summarization), the output is a reconstructed sentence or passage. The entire pipeline is parameterized by a privacy budget (ε, δ) and must handle a bounded number of queries (up to 100,000 in experiments).

## Method

DP-ICL follows a 'sample-and-aggregate' paradigm: private exemplars are partitioned into disjoint subsets, each subset is paired with the query to form an independent prompt, the LLM is queried for each prompt, and the resulting responses are aggregated through a differentially private mechanism. For text classification, a voting histogram over class predictions is released via Report-Noisy-Max with Gaussian noise (RNM-Gaussian). For language generation, two aggregation strategies are proposed: Embedding Space Aggregation (ESA) projects each output sentence into a 1563-dimensional embedding via text-embedding-ada-002, privately estimates the mean embedding with the Gaussian mechanism, and reconstructs a sentence by selecting the zero-shot candidate with maximum cosine similarity; Keyword Space Aggregation (KSA) builds a token-frequency histogram across all outputs and privately selects top-K keywords using either the joint exponential mechanism (for moderately large domains) or the propose-test-release paradigm (for very large or infinite domains), then re-queries the LLM with the selected keywords to generate the final answer. Privacy amplification by Poisson subsampling and tight privacy accounting via PRV (for Gaussian-based methods) or Rényi DP (for KSA) complete the framework.

## Results

On SST-2 with GPT-3 Davinci, DP-ICL achieves 95.80% accuracy at ε=3, matching or exceeding the non-private 4-shot ICL (95.49%) and non-private aggregation (95.73%). On AGNews, DP-ICL at ε=1 reaches 75.49% accuracy, a 22.89-point improvement over zero-shot (52.60%). For document QA (DocVQA), keyword space aggregation by PTR at ε=1 achieves ROUGE-1 of 59.92 and BLEU of 23.32, surpassing non-private 1-shot ICL (ROUGE-1 50.68). For dialog summarization (SAMSum), keyword by joint EM at ε=1 yields ROUGE-1 of 40.02, only ~1% below non-private aggregation (42.40).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DP-ICL outperforms all four DP-SGD baselines on SST-2 at ε=3: Li et al. (ICLR 2022) achieves 93.04%, Yu et al. (ICLR 2022) achieves 95.30% (at a looser ε=6.7), Bu et al. (2023) achieves 94.60%, and He et al. (ICLR 2023) achieves 94.23%, while DP-ICL reaches 95.80% — a 1.2–2.8% absolute improvement over each. Notably, DP-ICL's ε=3 result also exceeds the non-private 4-shot ICL upper bound (95.49%), indicating that the ensemble aggregation provides a utility boost beyond what a single 4-shot prompt achieves. For language generation tasks, DP-ICL's keyword space aggregation by PTR on DocVQA at ε=1 (ROUGE-1 59.92) surpasses both the non-private 1-shot ICL (50.68) and the non-private aggregation (62.42 is the ε=∞ Agg, so DP-ICL is within 3% of it). The paper also notes that DP-ICL is compatible with black-box LLM APIs and requires no hyperparameter tuning, unlike DP-SGD which demands per-example gradient clipping and extensive tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the classic 'sample-and-aggregate' paradigm (Nissim et al., 2007) from private data analysis to the LLM in-context learning setting, where the 'aggregation' step must handle either discrete class votes or open-ended text outputs. It departs from the dominant DP-SGD line of work (Li et al., 2022; Yu et al., 2022; Bu et al., 2023) by targeting private *prediction* rather than private *training*, making it compatible with black-box LLM APIs that do not expose weights. Concurrent DP-ICL efforts (Duan et al., 2023a; Tang et al., 2023) are restricted to small label spaces; this paper extends the problem to language generation tasks (DocVQA, SAMSum) by introducing two novel aggregation techniques—Embedding Space Aggregation and Keyword Space Aggregation—that operate in lower-dimensional semantic or keyword spaces to avoid the intractable vocabulary^length output space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, drop-in privacy layer for organizations deploying ICL with sensitive data (healthcare, finance) under regulations like GDPR, and the right-to-be-forgotten is trivially satisfied by removing exemplars from the database. Key limitations include a finite query budget (up to ~100,000 queries before the privacy budget is exhausted), the computational overhead of N parallel API calls per query, and the dependence of ESA on the quality of embedding-to-text reconstruction—an active research area. Open questions include tighter privacy accounting for the PTR and Exponential Mechanism components to extend the query budget, the efficiency-utility tradeoff in choosing the number of disjoint exemplar subsets, and whether advanced embedding-to-text models can close the gap between ESA and KSA.
