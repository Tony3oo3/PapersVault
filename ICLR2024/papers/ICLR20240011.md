## Problem

Existing NAS benchmarks (e.g., NAS-Bench-201) evaluate architectures only under standard training, leaving no look-up table for robust NAS algorithms that require adversarially trained accuracy scores. Moreover, the architecture ranking shifts when adversarial training is involved (as shown by low Spearman correlations between standard-trained and adversarially-trained robustness scores), and no NTK-based generalization theory exists for the multi-objective setting that jointly optimizes clean and adversarial losses.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes the 6,466 non-isomorphic cell-based architectures from the NAS-Bench-201 search space (6 edges × 5 operators: 3×3 Conv, 1×1 Conv, Zeroize, Skip, 1×1 AvgPool) and produces per-architecture clean accuracy plus robust accuracy under FGSM, PGD (at ρ=3/255 and 8/255), and AutoAttack on CIFAR-10/100 (32×32) and ImageNet-16-120 (16×16). The theoretical component takes residual FCNNs or CNNs parameterized by activation function choice, skip-connection flags, and (for CNNs) filter size κ, and outputs high-probability generalization bounds on both clean 0-1 loss and robust 0-1 loss governed by the minimum eigenvalues of joint NTK matrices.

## Method

Empirically, the authors adversarially train all 6,466 architectures for 50 epochs using mini-batch SGD with 7-step PGD (step-size 2/255, radius 8/255) and a one-cycle LR schedule, repeated over 3 seeds and 3 datasets (~107k GPU hours), then evaluate under multiple attacks to build NAS-RobBench-201. Theoretically, they define two joint NTKs: K_all = (1−β)²K + β(1−β)(K̄_ρ + K̄_ρᵀ) + β²K̂_ρ for clean accuracy, and K̃_all = (1−β)²K̂_ρ + β(1−β)(K̄_{2ρ} + K̄_{2ρ}ᵀ) + β²K̂_{2ρ} for robust accuracy, where K̂_{2ρ} is constructed from a 'twice-perturbed' adversary A_ρ*(A_ρ*(x,W),W) that seeks perturbations outside the original ρ-ball. The key result (Theorem 1) shows that under multi-objective adversarial training with balance parameter β, clean generalization error is controlled by λ_min(K_all) while robust generalization error is controlled by λ_min(K̃_all), recovering standard-training bounds at β=0 and pure adversarial-training bounds at β=1. The framework extends to CNNs (Corollary 1) where filter size κ modulates the required over-parameterization and step-size, and empirical NTK-score correlations on the benchmark confirm that robust NTKs (especially at larger ρ) predict robust accuracy more strongly than clean NTKs.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the optimal architecture in NAS-RobBench-201 achieves 79.4% clean accuracy, 69.8% FGSM(3/255) robust accuracy, and 48.2% PGD(8/255) robust accuracy. Among the three NAS algorithms tested with 150 queries, Local Search searching by PGD(8/255) achieves the best robust accuracy (53.5% FGSM(8/255), 48.1% PGD(8/8/255)), closely matching the optimal architecture. Searching by robust metrics (FGSM or PGD) consistently yields more robust architectures than searching by clean accuracy, with Local Search outperforming Regularized Evolution and Random Search across all robust metrics. The benchmark spans 6,466 architectures on three datasets (CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet-16-120) requiring ~107k GPU hours to construct.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Local Search (White et al., 2021) consistently outperforms Regularized Evolution (Real et al., 2019) and Random Search (Li & Talwalkar, 2020) across all robust accuracy metrics when searching by PGD(8/255), achieving 53.5% FGSM(8/255) vs. 53.1% (RE) and 52.0% (RS), and 48.1% PGD(8/255) vs. 47.8% (RE) and 47.1% (RS). Searching by robust metrics (FGSM or PGD) yields architectures with higher robust accuracy than searching by clean accuracy for all three algorithms, confirming that robustness-aware search is beneficial. Compared to the Jung et al. (2023) benchmark (which evaluates architectures under standard training), the Spearman correlation between PGD(8/255) accuracy under adversarial training (this paper) and PGD(8/255) accuracy under standard training (Jung et al.) is only 0.313–0.382, demonstrating that architectures ranked as robust under standard training are not reliably robust under adversarial training. In contrast, clean accuracy correlations between the two benchmarks remain high (0.970–0.977), underscoring that adversarial training fundamentally changes the robustness landscape.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NAS-Bench-201 search space (Dong & Yang, 2020) by adversarially training all 6,466 non-isomorphic architectures, filling a gap left by prior benchmarks (NAS-Bench-101, TransNAS-Bench-101, Jung et al. 2023) that only evaluate standard-trained networks. Theoretically, it generalizes the NTK-based generalization framework of Cao & Gu (2019) and Zhu et al. (2022a) from single-objective standard training to a multi-objective setting combining clean and adversarial losses, introducing joint NTKs that mix clean, robust, and 'twice-perturbed' kernels. The key departure from Jung et al. (2023) is that architectures are trained adversarially rather than standard-trained and then evaluated under attack, which the paper shows leads to materially different architecture rankings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NAS-RobBench-201 (107k GPU hours, 3 datasets) provides the first look-up table for adversarially-trained robust NAS, enabling rapid evaluation of new search algorithms without retraining. The theoretical result that clean accuracy is governed by a joint NTK (not purely the clean NTK) and robust accuracy by a robust NTK plus its 'twice-perturbed' version offers a principled basis for train-free robust architecture selection. Limitations include the restriction to the NAS-Bench-201 cell-based space (no Transformers), the NTK analysis being confined to the linear/infinite-width regime, and the benchmark covering only 3 small-to-medium datasets. Open questions include whether the joint-NTK framework extends to attention-based architectures and whether the 'twice-perturbation' construction generalizes beyond the ℓ∞ setting.
