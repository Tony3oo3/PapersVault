## Problem

Anomaly detection in tabular data is typically cast as one-class classification where only normal samples are available, requiring the model to capture characteristic patterns of normal data. While masked image/language modeling (MIM/MLM) has proven highly effective in vision and NLP, no prior work has adapted this paradigm to tabular anomaly detection, leaving a gap between the most powerful SSL pretext task and the tabular AD setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes a tabular data sample (a row vector of heterogeneous features, dimensions ranging from 6 to 500 across datasets) and produces an anomaly score computed as the average L2 reconstruction error across K=15 masked versions and all features. Beyond the scalar score, MCM provides two interpretability signals: per-mask contribution (identifying which learned feature correlation a sample violates) and per-feature contribution (identifying which individual feature deviates most from the normal marginal distribution).

## Method

MCM consists of three modules: a mask generator (MLP + sigmoid) that produces K=15 data-dependent soft masking matrices with values in [0,1], a 3-layer MLP encoder, and a symmetric 3-layer MLP decoder. The key architectural novelty is the learnable masking strategy: rather than using random or fixed masks, the generator learns to assign different masks to different samples, so each mask captures a specific correlation structure relevant to that particular normal instance. A diversity loss (log-sum-exp of pairwise inner products between mask matrices, scaled by temperature τ=0.1) prevents the ensemble from collapsing into redundant masks, forcing each mask to capture a distinct correlation. Training is end-to-end with a combined reconstruction loss (averaged L2 norm over all masks and samples) and the diversity loss, and at test time the average reconstruction error across all K masked reconstructions serves as the anomaly score.

## Results

MCM achieves the best AUC-PR on 13 out of 20 tabular anomaly detection datasets, with an average AUC-PR of 0.7486 and average AUC-ROC of 0.9044. This represents an absolute gain of at least 11.7% over the next-best method (ICL, avg AUC-PR 0.6027) and 8.2% over NeuTralAD (avg AUC-PR 0.5997). Notable margins include NSL-KDD (0.9085 vs. 0.8385 for NeuTralAD), Optdigits (0.8885 vs. 0.4363 for LOF), and Pendigits (0.8258 vs. 0.693 for NeuTralAD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCM outperforms all nine baselines on average AUC-PR (0.7486 vs. 0.6315 for LOF, the strongest classical method, and 0.6027 for ICL, the strongest SSL method), with the largest margins on Optdigits (+45.2% over LOF), NSL-KDD (+7.0% over NeuTralAD), and Pendigits (+13.3% over NeuTralAD). Among the SSL-based competitors, MCM beats NeuTralAD on 14/20 datasets and ICL on 15/20 datasets. MCM trails ICL on Glass (0.1905 vs. 0.2573) and Fraud (0.5141 vs. 0.5960), and falls slightly behind NeuTralAD on Arrhythmia (0.6107 vs. 0.6237) and Shuttle (0.9479 vs. 0.9971). Classical methods (IForest, LOF, OCSVM, ECOD) are consistently outperformed, with MCM exceeding the best classical method (LOF) by 11.7% on average AUC-PR. DeepSVDD and DAGMM, while competitive on some datasets (e.g., DeepSVDD on Breastw at 0.9924), suffer severe degradation on others (DAGMM drops to 0.0099 on Fraud), whereas MCM maintains more consistent performance across all 20 datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

MCM transplants the masked image/language modeling (MIM/MLM) paradigm—mask tokens/patches, reconstruct from context—into the tabular anomaly detection setting, a domain where prior SSL-based methods (GOAD, NeuTralAD, ICL) relied on transformation-based or contrastive pretext tasks rather than reconstruction. The key departure is the learnable, data-dependent masking strategy: unlike MAE's random patch masking or BERT's random token masking, MCM's mask generator produces soft, input-specific masks optimized to expose feature correlations in normal data. The diversity loss for ensemble masks is a novel addition not present in standard MIM/MLM, addressing the risk of redundant masks that would arise from simply replicating the encoder-decoder pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MCM provides the first principled bridge between the highly successful MIM/MLM family and tabular AD, opening a path for further masked-modeling techniques (e.g., variable masking ratios, discrete tokenization) to be adapted to structured data. The interpretability via per-mask and per-feature contributions is practically valuable in domains like medical diagnosis, where understanding *which* correlations are violated matters as much as the anomaly score itself. Open questions include scalability to very high-dimensional or mixed-type tabular data, whether the fixed ensemble size of 15 masks is optimal across regimes, and whether the approach extends naturally to streaming or online anomaly detection settings.
