## Problem

Existing Transformer Architecture Search (TAS) methods perform both supernet training and architecture search under fully supervised paradigms, requiring large amounts of labeled data that are expensive to obtain and limit generalization across domains. No prior work had explored self-supervised architecture search specifically for vision transformers, leaving a gap in scalable, label-free automated ViT design.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240610/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB images at 224×224 resolution, which are randomly masked at a 90% ratio to produce visible and masked patch sets. The primary output is a searched transformer architecture (with inherited supernet weights) that can be fine-tuned for downstream classification or semantic segmentation; the architecture search itself is evaluated via an unsupervised teacher-student feature consistency metric rather than labeled accuracy, eliminating any need for ground-truth labels during the search phase.

## Method

MaskTAS builds a siamesed teacher-student supernet where a pre-trained MAE teacher (frozen) provides stable supervision to a student encoder supernet containing weight-sharing subnets of varying patch dimensions, attention heads, MLP ratios, and depths. Student training combines two self-supervised objectives: pixel-level reconstruction of masked patches and feature-level distillation toward the teacher's latent representations via a projection network, which prevents the co-training of diverse subnets from diverging. For architecture search, an evolutionary algorithm (crossover and mutation over depth and block configurations) ranks candidates using a novel unsupervised metric that measures the relative pairwise feature relation between teacher and student outputs, computed as a cross-entropy over softmax-normalized dot-product similarities. The searched architecture inherits supernet weights and is fine-tuned on the target dataset for final deployment.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, MaskTAS achieves 75.6%, 82.5%, and 83.8% top-1 accuracy for the tiny, small, and base configurations respectively, all without using any manual labels during the search process. The base model (53.7M params, 11G FLOPs) outperforms the supervised AutoFormer-base (pre-trained for 800 epochs) by 1.4% top-1 and 0.7% top-5, while converging in only 100 epochs versus 500+ for AutoFormer. On ADE20K semantic segmentation, the searched MaskTAS small and base models outperform the second-best supervised method by 0.4% and 1.4% mIoU respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the supervised TAS method AutoFormer, MaskTAS wins across all three size groups: +0.9% top-1 (tiny), +0.8% (small), and +1.4% (base), with the base comparison particularly notable since AutoFormer-base was pre-trained for 800 epochs versus MaskTAS's 100. Compared to ViTAS-Twins, MaskTAS achieves comparable or better top-1 accuracy (75.6 vs 79.4 tiny, 82.5 vs 82.0 small, 83.8 vs 83.5 base) while using substantially fewer parameters (5.8M vs 13.8M tiny; 22.1M vs 30.5M small; 53.7M vs 124.8M base). Against hand-crafted transformers, MaskTAS-small (82.5%) surpasses DeiT-S (79.9%), ViT-S/16 (78.8%), and T2T-ViT-14 (81.7%), and MaskTAS-base (83.8%) exceeds DeiT-B (81.8%) and ViT-B/16 (79.7%). Among CNN baselines, MaskTAS-small matches or approaches EfficientNet-B4 (82.9%) and RegNetY-4GF (80.0%) at similar FLOPs, while MaskTAS-base trails EfficientNet-B7 (84.3%) by 0.5% but at a fraction of the compute (11G vs 37G FLOPs). On ADE20K semantic segmentation, MaskTAS small and base outperform the second-best supervised method (ViTAS-Twins) by 0.4% and 1.4% mIoU respectively, demonstrating transferability to dense prediction tasks without labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the one-shot NAS framework for vision transformers established by AutoFormer, ViTAS, and NASformer by replacing the fully supervised training and evaluation paradigm with a masked image modeling (MIM) based self-supervised one. It builds directly on MAE's masked autoencoder formulation for the reconstruction objective and on knowledge distillation (Hinton et al.) to transfer features from a pre-trained teacher into the student supernet, addressing the subnet divergence problem that arises when co-training diverse architectures without strong supervision. The evolutionary search algorithm is adapted from Guo et al. (2020) but re-scored with a novel unsupervised teacher-student feature consistency metric rather than labeled validation accuracy. Conceptually, the paper positions itself as the first self-supervised TAS specifically for ViTs, filling a gap where prior self-supervised NAS work targeted only CNN modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for labeled data during both supernet training and architecture search, MaskTAS substantially lowers the practical barrier to deploying automated ViT design in domains where annotation is expensive or unavailable. The siamesed teacher-student distillation strategy is a reusable technique for stabilizing one-shot NAS under weak supervision, potentially applicable beyond MIM. However, the method still depends on a pre-trained MAE teacher, and the unsupervised evaluation metric (feature similarity with the teacher) has not been rigorously validated as a proxy for downstream task performance across diverse domains. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger search spaces, whether the 90% masking ratio generalizes beyond ImageNet-scale pre-training, and how the searched architectures perform when the teacher and student operate in substantially different capacity regimes.
