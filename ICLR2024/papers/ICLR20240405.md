## Problem

While generated data from diffusion models is widely assumed to improve contrastive learning by increasing data diversity, the paper shows that naively inflating a dataset with 1M generated images can actually degrade linear probing accuracy. The underlying causes—spanning both the data inflation strategy (mixing ratio, generation quality) and the data augmentation protocol—had not been identified or theoretically explained.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240405/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unlabeled real image dataset (e.g., CIFAR-10 at 32×32, 50k images) and 1M synthetic images produced by an unconditional diffusion model (STF or DDPM) trained on that same real data, with no external data or labels. The output is a pretrained visual encoder (ResNet-18 backbone) evaluated exclusively via linear probing on the original raw dataset, so the generated data influences only the contrastive pretraining phase and never appears at test time.

## Method

AdaInf (Adaptive Inflation) is a purely data-centric strategy that adjusts two knobs when inflating contrastive learning data: (1) data reweighting, which replicates real samples 10× relative to generated samples to reduce the TV distribution gap between training and test distributions, and (2) weak augmentation, which relaxes the most aggressive transforms (RRC min scale raised from 0.08 to 0.2, ColorJitter probability lowered from 0.8 to 0.4, ColorJitter strength halved). The theoretical justification derives a generalization bound for inflated contrastive learning of the form 8α/λ_{k+1} + 16α + 2(1−β)·D_TV(P_d, P_g), where α is the augmentation-induced labeling error and λ_{k+1} is the (k+1)-th Laplacian eigenvalue of the augmentation graph. The key insight is that data inflation monotonically improves graph connectivity (larger λ_{k+1}) without affecting labeling error, whereas stronger augmentation improves connectivity but simultaneously increases labeling error—making the two factors complementary. Consequently, once inflation supplies sufficient connectivity, weaker augmentation becomes optimal to suppress α, explaining why the optimal augmentation strength shifts downward as the inflated dataset grows.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, AdaInf achieves 93.42% linear probing accuracy with SimCLR, 94.19% with MoCo V2, 92.87% with BYOL, and 93.64% with Barlow Twins, consistently outperforming both the no-inflation baseline and vanilla inflation. Across datasets, AdaInf with SimCLR reaches 93.42% on CIFAR-10, 69.6% on CIFAR-100, and 48.36% on Tiny ImageNet. Notably, vanilla inflation (equal mixing of real and generated data with standard augmentations) actually degrades performance on several methods (e.g., SimCLR drops from 91.56% to 91.38%, Tiny ImageNet drops from 47.21% to 41.03%), while AdaInf's adaptive reweighting and weak augmentation strategy yields consistent gains of 1–2.5% over the no-inflation baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaInf consistently outperforms the no-inflation baseline across all four contrastive methods on CIFAR-10, with the largest gains on Barlow Twins (+2.40%) and SimCLR (+1.86%). Compared to vanilla inflation (equal mixing + standard augmentation), AdaInf achieves over 1% improvement on SimCLR (+2.04%), MoCo V2 (+1.68%), and Barlow Twins (+1.55%), but performs comparably on BYOL (92.87% vs. 92.90%), likely because BYOL's augmentation pipeline requires a different weakening strategy. A critical finding is that vanilla inflation actively harms performance on several baselines: SimCLR drops from 91.56% to 91.38%, MoCo V2 from 92.75% to 92.51%, and Tiny ImageNet (SimCLR) from 47.21% to 41.03%, confirming the paper's central claim that generated data do not always help contrastive learning. On CIFAR-100, AdaInf improves over no-inflation by +2.79% (69.60% vs. 66.81%), while vanilla inflation degrades performance by 1.29%. On Tiny ImageNet, where the generative model quality is lower (DDPM, FID=18.61), AdaInf still recovers and exceeds the no-inflation baseline (+1.15%), whereas vanilla inflation causes a severe 6.18% drop.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the augmentation graph framework from HaoChen et al. (2021) and Wang et al. (2022b) to the setting where training and test distributions differ due to data inflation, establishing the first generalization bounds for inflated contrastive learning. It departs from prior work on leveraging generated data for representation learning (Jahanian et al., 2022; Tian et al., 2023; Azizi et al., 2023), which typically assumes generated data are beneficial or requires problem-specific sampling designs, by showing that naive data inflation can actually degrade performance and that the training protocol (augmentation strength, mixing ratio) must be co-adapted. The paper also connects to the broader literature on data augmentation in contrastive learning (Tian et al., 2020; Luo et al., 2023), where stronger augmentations are known to help, by revealing that this principle reverses under data inflation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that generated data from a good diffusion model can harm contrastive learning challenges a widely held assumption in the field and provides a principled explanation (distribution gap + labeling error trade-off) for when and how to use synthetic data. The complementary roles between data inflation and augmentation offer a practical, zero-cost strategy (AdaInf) that practitioners can immediately adopt, particularly in data-scarce settings. Limitations include evaluation only on small-scale datasets (CIFAR-10/100, Tiny ImageNet) and the 94.7% CIFAR-10 record requiring 500k training steps (~5000 epochs); open questions include whether the adaptive strategy generalizes to ImageNet-scale data, whether it interacts well with advanced architectures (ViTs), and whether the optimal mixing ratio can be determined automatically without manual tuning.
