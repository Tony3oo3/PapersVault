## Problem

Classical statistics warns against overparameterization and recommends strong regularization to align train and test loss, yet modern ML practice consistently shows that larger models, more data, and training to near-zero training loss all improve generalization. No prior theoretical result proved that wider random feature models are strictly better for arbitrary task eigenstructures, nor characterized a class of tasks for which overfitting (interpolation) is not merely beneficial but obligatory for achieving near-optimal test error.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes n training samples with noisy labels, k random feature weights, and the ground-truth task eigenstructure (kernel eigenvalues λ_i and target eigencoefficients v_i), producing closed-form estimates of train and test MSE, the optimal ridge δ*, and the optimal fitting ratio R_tr/te* = E_tr/E_te. The fitting ratio serves as the natural optimization variable in place of ridge δ because it remains order-unity as n diverges, and the 'omniscient' nature of the estimates means they require the true eigenstructure rather than being computable from finite data alone. For empirical validation, eigenstructure is extracted from full-dataset kernel matrices (up to 30,000×30,000) of convolutional NTKs on image datasets.

## Method

The paper derives a closed-form omniscient risk estimate for RF regression by reframing it as KRR with a stochastic kernel, then applying a Gaussian universality ansatz and a key random matrix identity to compute the eigenstatistics of the stochastic kernel, yielding Equations (3)–(4) that express train and test error in terms of task eigenstructure, n, k, and δ. Theorem 1 (more features and more data strictly improve test error at optimal ridge) follows by elementary algebra on this estimate. Taking the infinite-feature limit recovers the standard KRR eigenframework, which is then solved analytically for powerlaw eigenstructure (λ_i ∝ i^{−α}, v_i² ∝ i^{−β}), yielding Theorem 2 (optimal fitting ratio bounded away from 1 for all powerlaw tasks) and Corollary 1 (interpolation is obligatory iff β ≥ α and relative noise is below a threshold). The theory is validated on MNIST, SVHN, CIFAR10, and FashionMNIST with Myrtle convolutional NTKs, where experimentally measured exponents α, β predict test error as a function of fitting ratio with high quantitative accuracy, confirming R_tr/te* ≈ 0 for these low-noise vision tasks.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical result (Theorem 1) proves that for RF regression, the optimal test error is monotonically non-increasing in both the number of features k and the number of samples n, with strict improvement whenever the target has a nonzero learnable component. The second main result (Theorem 2) shows that for KRR tasks with powerlaw eigenstructure (λ_i ∝ i^{-α}, v_i² ∝ i^{-β}), the optimal fitting ratio R_tr/te* is bounded away from 1, and Corollary 1 gives the sharp condition R_tr/te* → 0 iff σ_rel² ≤ (β−α)/(α(α−1)). Empirically, the RF eigenframework (Equations 3–4) closely matches measured train and test errors for both synthetic Gaussian data and CIFAR-10 with random ReLU features at n=256, and the KRR theory (Lemma 10) quantitatively predicts test error as a function of R_tr/te for MNIST, SVHN, and CIFAR-10 using only the two measured exponents α and β, with R_tr/te* ≈ 0 in all three cases (i.e., near-interpolation is obligatory).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper that does not benchmark against competing learning methods; instead, its empirical contribution is validating closed-form risk predictions against experiments. The RF eigenframework (Equations 3–4) is shown to closely track measured train and test MSE across varying n, k, and δ for both synthetic Gaussian data (α=β=1.5) and CIFAR-10 with random ReLU features (n=256, k up to 10⁴), confirming the Gaussian universality ansatz. For the KRR overfitting results, the paper compares its Lemma 10 prediction (test error as a function of R_tr/te, parameterized by measured α, β, σ_rel²) against actual KRR experiments with the Myrtle convolutional NTK on MNIST, SVHN, CIFAR-10, and FashionMNIST, finding excellent quantitative agreement after a single multiplicative prefactor fit at R_tr/te=0. The paper extends Nakkiran et al. (2021), who proved monotonicity only for isotropic covariates, to arbitrary task eigenstructure, and subsumes the ridgeless results of Bach (2023) and the isotropic results of Mei & Montanari (2019) as special cases of its general RF eigenframework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the KRR eigenframework (Sollich 2001; Bordelon et al. 2020; Simon et al. 2021) to the finite-feature RF regression setting, deriving a general closed-form risk estimate that subsumes prior special-case results (Mei & Montanari 2019 for isotropic covariates, Bach 2023 for zero ridge, Maloney et al. 2022 for teacher-student). It resolves a conjecture of Nakkiran et al. (2021) by proving monotonic improvement with features and data for arbitrary task eigenstructure, not just isotropic. The 'obligatory overfitting' result departs from the benign overfitting literature (Belkin et al. 2018; Liang & Rakhlin 2020; Muthukumar et al. 2020) by showing that near-interpolation is not merely compatible with good generalization but is *required* to achieve optimal test error under powerlaw eigenstructure, a condition the authors empirically validate on standard CV datasets using convolutional NTKs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first general theoretical justification for the 'more is better' philosophy in modern ML, showing that infinite width is provably optimal for RF regression and that overfitting is obligatory (not just benign) for a broad class of tasks. The empirical finding that two scalars (α, β) extracted from powerlaw eigenstructure quantitatively predict test error as a function of the fitting ratio across MNIST, SVHN, CIFAR-10, and FashionMNIST is striking and suggests a compact characterization of task difficulty for kernel learners. Key limitations include restriction to the kernel regime (no feature learning), reliance on the Gaussian universality ansatz, and the unexplained origin of powerlaw eigenstructure in natural data. Open questions include whether these results extend to feature-learning networks of finite width and to non-powerlaw task structures.
