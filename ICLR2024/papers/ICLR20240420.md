## Problem

Existing self-supervised pre-training methods for protein-ligand interactions (e.g., Uni-Mol, SMT-DTA, ELECTRA-DTA) use two-tower architectures that learn individual protein and ligand representations, leaving the interaction module to be trained only during fine-tuning. This design fails to capture the intricate inter-molecular atomic interactions (π-stacking, hydrogen bonds, salt bridges, etc.) that determine binding affinity and docking conformation, resulting in poor generalization on downstream binding tasks.

## Input / Output

The model takes a protein pocket 3D structure (residues within 8 Å of the ligand) and a ligand 3D structure in its unbound 'primary state'—generated via RDKit or by adding Gaussian noise to dihedral angles—rather than the bound conformation. This deliberate use of the unbound ligand forces the model to learn conformational adaptation and interaction patterns rather than trivial rigid-body alignment. The output consists of three representation streams (pocket, ligand, and pairwise binding embeddings) that feed into task-specific heads: an MLP for affinity regression, cross-entropy for active/inactive classification, or distance-map regression followed by gradient-descent pose optimization for docking.

## Method

BindNet freezes two pre-trained Uni-Mol encoders and trains only an N-layer 3D-invariant Transformer interaction module that operates on both atom-wise and pairwise representations in the frozen encoder's output space. Two complementary pre-training objectives drive learning: (1) Atomic Pairwise Distance Map Prediction (ADP), which regresses the inter-molecular atom-atom distance matrix from the pairwise binding embeddings, providing fine-grained structural supervision; and (2) Mask Ligand Reconstruction (MLR), which masks atom embeddings in the torsion-state ligand representation and reconstructs them from the primary-state ligand and pocket embeddings in representation space (not atom-token space), capturing conditional dependencies and conformational changes during binding. The key architectural insight is that by using the unbound ligand as input and predicting the bound-state distance map, the interaction module must learn genuine interaction physics rather than simple translation/rotation. Pre-training is conducted on 458,252 BioLip complexes with both losses weighted equally, and the resulting interaction-aware representations transfer to affinity prediction, virtual screening, and docking with frozen encoders.

## Results

On the primary LBA 30% binding affinity task, BindNet achieves RMSE 1.340, Pearson 0.632, and Spearman 0.620, outperforming the best prior pre-training method Uni-Mol (RMSE 1.434, Pearson 0.565) and the best structure-based supervised method ProNet (RMSE 1.463, Pearson 0.551). On LBA 60%, BindNet reaches RMSE 1.230 and Pearson 0.793. On ligand efficacy prediction (LEP), BindNet attains AUROC 0.882 and AUPRC 0.870, surpassing Uni-Mol (0.823/0.787) by a substantial margin. On molecular docking (CASF-2016), BindNet achieves 45.26% success at the 1.0 Å threshold and 89.12% at 3.0 Å, exceeding Uni-Mol (43.16%/87.02%) and all classical docking tools.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BindNet outperforms all 19 baselines on the LBA affinity prediction task across both 30% and 60% sequence-identity splits, with the largest gains over pre-training methods (Uni-Mol: +0.094 RMSE on LBA 30%) and structure-based supervised methods (ProNet: +0.123 RMSE). On LEP, BindNet leads Uni-Mol by 5.9 points in AUROC (0.882 vs. 0.823) and 8.3 points in AUPRC (0.870 vs. 0.787). On DUD-E virtual screening, BindNet surpasses pre-training baselines CoSP and Uni-Mol on all RE metrics, but trails supervised methods DrugVQA (AUC 0.972) and AttentionSiteDTI (AUC 0.971) on AUC, which the authors attribute to decoy bias in DUD-E. On the bias-corrected AD dataset, this advantage reverses: DrugVQA and AttentionSiteDTI drop to AUC ~0.47–0.48 (near random), while BindNet maintains AUC 0.64 and leads all methods. On molecular docking, BindNet exceeds Uni-Mol at every RMSD threshold (e.g., 45.26% vs. 43.16% at 1.0 Å) and all classical docking tools (AutoDock Vina, Smina, Vinardo, AutoDock4).

## Relation to Prior Work

BindNet builds directly on Uni-Mol's frozen 3D molecular and pocket encoders, operating entirely in their representation space with a trainable Transformer-based interaction module on top. It departs from the dominant two-tower pre-training paradigm (ELECTRA-DTA, SMT-DTA, Uni-Mol) that learns individual protein and ligand representations before a lightweight interaction head, and from CoSP's contrastive alignment of pocket-ligand pairs, by instead formulating pre-training as predicting the final complex structure from a primary (unbound) ligand and pocket. The two novel objectives—atomic pairwise distance map prediction and representation-space mask ligand reconstruction—draw on ideas from geometric deep learning scoring functions and masked autoencoder literature, respectively, but are specifically designed to supervise inter-molecular interaction patterns rather than individual molecular properties.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most compelling finding is the out-of-distribution evaluation showing that supervised 3D structure-based methods (Atom3D-GNN) degrade catastrophically (RMSE 1.6→12.5) when the input conformation type changes, while BindNet and Uni-Mol remain stable—suggesting supervised methods memorize structural distributions rather than learning transferable interaction physics. The AD dataset experiment further reinforces this by showing supervised virtual screening methods collapse to near-random performance when decoy bias is removed. Open questions include whether the interaction-aware pre-training formulation extends to protein-protein and antigen-antibody binding, whether the reliance on BioLip (with acknowledged overlap to downstream data) limits true generalization claims, and whether the frozen-encoder constraint is necessary or merely a design choice that could be relaxed for further gains.
