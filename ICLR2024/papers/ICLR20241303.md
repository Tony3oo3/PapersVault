## Problem

Nighttime optical flow estimation is severely degraded by weakened texture and amplified noise that violate brightness constancy assumptions, causing invalid motion feature matching. Existing domain adaptation approaches directly transfer knowledge in either input visual space or output motion space, but fail due to a large domain gap (intrinsic feature distribution misalignment) between auxiliary and nighttime domains. The paper identifies the need for an intermediate common-latent space that bridges this gap to enable effective directional knowledge transfer.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three modalities during training: auxiliary daytime image frame pairs (for appearance adaptation), accumulated event camera data spatially aligned with nighttime frames (for boundary adaptation), and the target nighttime image frame pair. At inference time, only the single nighttime optical flow model is used, taking a nighttime frame pair and producing a dense 2D optical flow field. The event data is obtained either via a physically coaxial optical system with a beam splitter or through stereo rectification with pixel-offset fine-tuning to ensure spatial alignment with the nighttime frames.

## Method

ABDA-Flow constructs two complementary common-latent spaces to bridge the domain gap. For appearance adaptation, a pre-trained Uretinex-Net decomposes daytime and nighttime images into reflectance maps, projecting both into a reflectance-aligned common space where motion distributions are shown to be similar; motion knowledge is then transferred via KL-divergence alignment of cost volumes, intra-domain pixel-level consistency, and inter-domain residual transfer. For boundary adaptation, the paper theoretically derives that the spatiotemporal gradient of the image (−∇I·U) equals the accumulated event signal (ΔL_ev), establishing a spatiotemporal gradient-aligned common space; a Euclidean-distance correlation metric between the two gradient maps generates an attention map that classifies motion degradation levels, and contrastive learning pulls normal nighttime motion features toward event-domain features while pushing degraded ones away. Training proceeds in three stages (pre-train daytime/event flow, appearance adaptation, boundary adaptation), and the total objective combines photometric, adversarial, KL-divergence, alignment, classification, contrastive, and motion-consistency losses.

## Results

On the real Dark-GOF dataset, ABDA-Flow achieves 0.85 EPE and 7.94% Fl-all, outperforming the best frame-based baseline GyroFlow (0.92 EPE, 9.85% Fl-all) and supervised GMA (1.14 EPE, 13.60% Fl-all). On Dark-DSEC, ABDA-Flow reaches 0.74 EPE and 11.85% Fl-all, surpassing E-RAFT (0.82 EPE, 13.81% Fl-all) and EV-FlowNet (3.21 EPE, 63.25% Fl-all). On the synthetic Dark-KITTI2015, ABDA-Flow achieves 3.47 EPE and 16.13% Fl-all, more than doubling the performance of the next-best method DarkFlow-PWC (7.56 EPE, 35.75% Fl-all).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ABDA-Flow consistently outperforms all frame-based baselines on both real datasets. On Dark-GOF, it beats GyroFlow (0.92→0.85 EPE) and GMA (1.14→0.85 EPE), with a particularly large margin over unsupervised SMURF (2.87 EPE) and supervised PWC-Net (10.26 EPE). On Dark-DSEC, ABDA-Flow (0.74 EPE) surpasses the best event-based method E-RAFT (0.82 EPE) and the frame-based GMA (1.48 EPE). On the synthetic Dark-KITTI2015, ABDA-Flow (3.47 EPE) more than halves the error of DarkFlow-PWC (7.56 EPE) and significantly outperforms all Selfflow and SMURF variants (10.03–18.01 EPE). The event-based E-ABDA variant (0.78 EPE on Dark-DSEC) also beats EV-FlowNet (3.21 EPE) by a wide margin, confirming the benefit of the common boundary space for event-to-frame knowledge transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior nighttime optical flow methods (DarkFlow-PWC, GyroFlow) that perform direct domain adaptation in either visual space or motion space, arguing that the large domain gap caused by heterogeneous feature distributions makes such direct transfer ineffective. Instead, it introduces a common-latent-space paradigm, building on Uretinex-Net's retinex decomposition to construct a reflectance-aligned space between daytime and nighttime domains, and on the Taylor-expanded optical flow model of Paredes-Vallés & de Croon to derive a spatiotemporal gradient-aligned space between nighttime frames and accumulated events. The framework unifies two auxiliary modalities (daytime RGB and event camera) under a single appearance-boundary adaptation architecture, which is a conceptual departure from prior work that typically leverages only one auxiliary source.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The common-space adaptation paradigm offers a principled alternative to direct domain transfer for degraded-scene understanding, and the authors note it could generalize to other adverse conditions beyond nighttime. The introduction of the LLFE paired frame-event dataset and the demonstration that appearance (global motion) and boundary (local motion) knowledge are complementary provide useful building blocks for future multi-modal nighttime perception. However, the framework's complexity (seven loss terms, three training stages, paired event data requirement) limits immediate practical adoption, and the evaluation relies heavily on synthetic noise models and relatively weak unsupervised baselines, leaving open whether the gains persist against stronger supervised nighttime flow methods with ground-truth annotations.
