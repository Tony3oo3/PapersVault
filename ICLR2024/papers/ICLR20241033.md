## Problem

In model-based reinforcement learning, the reward model is a critical but underexplored bottleneck: for sparse-reward tasks, the standard MSE loss incentivizes the model to predict zero everywhere rather than attempt to predict the exact timestep of a sparse reward, causing policies to stall or fail. This paper identifies this failure mode across multiple state-of-the-art MBRL algorithms (DreamerV3, TD-MPC, MBPO) and asks whether relaxing the requirement for exact-timestep reward prediction can unlock better policy learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw reward sequences from collected episodes (e.g., 64×64 RGB images for Earthmoving, single images for RoboDesk/Hand/Crafter, plus proprioceptive observations where available) and applies a temporal smoothing kernel to the reward signal before storing episodes in the replay buffer. The agent trains exclusively on these smoothed rewards and never sees the original reward values. The smoothing kernel must satisfy the constraint that its coefficients sum to 1, preserving the total reward over an episode. The final output is the action sequence produced by the underlying MBRL planner or policy network, unchanged from the base algorithm.

## Method

DreamSmooth applies a temporal smoothing function f to each episode's reward sequence upon collection, replacing the exact reward r_t with a weighted average of neighboring rewards r_{t-L:t+L} where the weights sum to 1. Three kernels are investigated: Gaussian (symmetric, parameterized by σ), uniform (symmetric, parameterized by window δ), and exponential moving average (asymmetric, parameterized by α), with the implementation requiring only a single additional line of code to any existing MBRL algorithm. The key insight is that smoothing converts the hard problem of predicting a reward at the exact timestep into the easier problem of predicting when a reward is approximately obtained, which is sufficient for policy learning. Theoretically, the paper proves via the Ng et al. (1999) reward-shaping theorem that past-only smoothing (EMA) preserves optimality, and shows that for any smoothing kernel the discounted sum of rewards over a trajectory is invariant, implying optimality under the original reward for any smoothing function. Empirically, Gaussian and uniform smoothing outperform EMA on most sparse-reward tasks because they allow the model to be early or late, though on Crafter the symmetric kernels introduce false positives that degrade performance.

## Results

DreamSmooth (Gaussian and Uniform variants) significantly improves both sample efficiency and final performance of DreamerV3 on the three sparse-reward manipulation tasks (RoboDesk, Hand, Earthmoving), where the vanilla DreamerV3 reward model fails to predict most sparse rewards. On Crafter, DreamSmooth predicts sparse rewards more accurately in 15 out of 19 subtasks, yet the symmetric Gaussian/Uniform kernels actually degrade task performance due to increased false-positive reward predictions. On the dense-reward DMC and Atari benchmarks, DreamSmooth achieves comparable performance to the unmodified baselines, confirming it does not hurt performance where reward prediction is not a bottleneck. DreamSmooth also enables TD-MPC and MBPO to solve the Hand task, which the vanilla algorithms fail to complete even with proprioceptive state observations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DreamerV3 (Hafner et al., 2023), DreamSmooth-Gaussian and DreamSmooth-Uniform clearly win on RoboDesk, Hand, and Earthmoving, where the baseline's reward model omits most sparse rewards and the policy gets stuck or fails repeatedly. However, DreamSmooth-Gaussian and Uniform lose on Crafter due to symmetric kernels causing the reward model to anticipate future rewards, producing many false positives that mislead policy learning; DreamSmooth-EMA performs comparably or slightly better on Crafter. Against TD-MPC (Hansen et al., 2022), DreamSmooth+TD-MPC wins decisively on the Hand task, enabling both pixel-observation and state-observation variants to complete the full task sequence, whereas vanilla TD-MPC fails to consistently solve even the first subtask. Against MBPO (Janner et al., 2019), DreamSmooth+MBPO wins on Hand (learning the first task with reward smoothing vs. failing without it) and ties on the dense-reward DMC tasks, confirming the method's generality across MBRL architectures with different planning mechanisms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DreamerV3 (Hafner et al., 2023) as its primary backbone, and also applies to TD-MPC (Hansen et al., 2022) and MBPO (Janner et al., 2019), positioning itself as a drop-in improvement to existing MBRL algorithms rather than a new architecture. While prior work has extensively improved dynamics/world models (prototype-based, object-centric, contrastive, masked auto-encoding representations), the reward model has been largely overlooked; Babaeizadeh et al. (2020) noted reward prediction's importance but only in dense-reward settings. The paper draws on neuroscience evidence that humans plan with rough reward estimates (Fiorillo et al., 2008; Klein-Flügge et al., 2011) and connects its optimality proof for EMA smoothing to the classical reward shaping framework of Ng et al. (1999).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single line of code (temporal smoothing of collected rewards) yields substantial gains in sample efficiency and final performance on sparse-reward manipulation tasks across three different MBRL algorithms, without degrading dense-reward benchmarks. However, the Crafter results reveal an important open question: symmetric smoothing kernels (Gaussian, Uniform) that require anticipating future rewards can increase false-positive predictions, actually hurting performance in environments with many sparse reward sources. This false-positive/false-negative trade-off, and whether asymmetric or adaptive smoothing can resolve it, remains an open direction. The technique's universality is also somewhat limited by the Crafter counterexample, suggesting practitioners must be cautious about blindly applying smoothing.
