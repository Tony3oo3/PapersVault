## Problem

Existing subject-driven text-to-image generation methods (e.g., DreamBooth, Textual Inversion) map a subject's reference images to a single entangled text embedding that conflates identity-relevant information (the subject itself) with identity-irrelevant information (background, pose, lighting). This entanglement causes two failure modes: the irrelevant information dominates generation and overrides text prompts, while the identity information is not faithfully preserved, leading to subject identity drift. No prior work explicitly disentangles these two information streams during finetuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240922/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of 3–5 reference images of a target subject (typically 512×512 RGB) and a text prompt containing a special subject token (e.g., 'a S* backpack on the beach'). During finetuning, each reference image also produces a per-image visual embedding via a CLIP image encoder and a learnable adapter. At inference, only the shared textual identity-preserved embedding is required; optionally, a reference image's identity-irrelevant embedding can be added with a user-defined weight η to inherit pose or background characteristics, enabling controllable generation beyond pure text conditioning.

## Method

DisenBooth replaces the single entangled conditioning vector with two disentangled embeddings used jointly during the Stable Diffusion denoising process: a shared textual identity-preserved embedding f_s (obtained from the CLIP text encoder on a special token P_s) and per-image visual identity-irrelevant embeddings f_i (obtained from a CLIP image encoder, a learnable element-wise mask that filters out identity information, and an MLP adapter with skip connection). The primary denoising loss L1 conditions the U-Net on f_s + f_i, while two auxiliary objectives enforce disentanglement: a weak denoising loss L2 (weighted by λ₂=0.01) requires f_s alone to partially reconstruct each image, ensuring it captures the shared subject identity, and a contrastive loss L3 (weighted by λ₃=0.001) minimizes cosine similarity between f_s and f_i to push them into orthogonal subspaces. Finetuning is parameter-efficient, using LoRA (rank 4) on the U-Net plus the adapter, totaling only ~2.9M trainable parameters out of 865.9M. At generation time, the user can optionally blend f_i into the conditioning with a tunable weight η to inherit specific visual characteristics (pose, background) from a chosen reference image, providing a degree of controllability absent in prior finetuning-based methods.

## Results

On DreamBench (30 subjects, 750 prompts, 3,000 generated images), DisenBooth achieves the best CLIP-T score (0.330) among all finetuning methods, outperforming DreamBooth (0.319), TI (0.318), and InstructPix2Pix (0.303). Its DINO score (0.675) is slightly below DreamBooth's (0.685), but human evaluation confirms DisenBooth's superior identity preservation (Identity Avg. Rank 1.37 vs. DreamBooth's 2.20) and overall generation quality (Overall Avg. Rank 1.59 vs. DreamBooth's 2.45). In the extended comparison (Table 7), DisenBooth also leads all non-finetuning baselines (ELITE, E4T, Break-A-Scene) and other finetuning methods (CustomDiffusion, SVDiff) on CLIP-T.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DisenBooth outperforms DreamBooth on CLIP-T (0.330 vs. 0.319), Identity Avg. Rank (1.37 vs. 2.20), and Overall Avg. Rank (1.59 vs. 2.45), while DreamBooth retains a marginal DINO advantage (0.685 vs. 0.675) due to overfitting identity-irrelevant background information. Against TI, DisenBooth wins on every metric (DINO 0.675 vs. 0.546, CLIP-T 0.330 vs. 0.318, Identity Rank 1.37 vs. 3.73). InstructPix2Pix, a text-guided editing method, performs worst on CLIP-T (0.303) and cannot handle complex subject transformations. In the extended Table 7 comparison, DisenBooth (CLIP-T 0.330, DINO 0.675) surpasses CustomDiffusion (0.314/0.675), SVDiff (0.318/0.668), Break-A-Scene (0.313/0.639), ELITE (0.291/0.606), and E4T (0.262/0.241, which suffers severe out-of-domain degradation on non-human subjects). The pretrained Stable Diffusion reference achieves the highest CLIP-T (0.352) but the lowest DINO (0.362), confirming it is not customized for any subject.

## Relation to Prior Work

DisenBooth is a direct extension of DreamBooth's finetuning paradigm for subject-driven text-to-image generation, replacing the single entangled text embedding with a pair of disentangled embeddings: a shared textual identity-preserved embedding and per-image visual identity-irrelevant embeddings. It builds on Stable Diffusion's latent diffusion architecture and adopts LoRA for parameter-efficient tuning, while departing from prior finetuning methods (DreamBooth, TI) and non-finetuning methods (ELITE, E4T, InstantBooth) by introducing two novel auxiliary objectives—weak denoising and contrastive embedding—to explicitly enforce disentanglement. The approach also contrasts with image editing methods like InstructPix2Pix, which the paper shows are ill-suited for the complex transformations required in subject-driven generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a fundamental limitation in subject-driven generation: entanglement of identity and non-identity information in a single embedding, which simultaneously causes overfitting to irrelevant factors (background, pose) and identity drift. The disentanglement framework provides practical controllability—users can blend identity with different reference images via a weight parameter—making it a useful tool for creative applications. However, the disentanglement is coarse (only identity vs. everything else), and finer-grained separation (pose, background, style) remains open. The method also inherits all limitations of the base Stable Diffusion model and requires per-subject finetuning, limiting scalability to large numbers of subjects.
