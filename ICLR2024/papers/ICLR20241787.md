## Problem

Decision Transformers (DT) are data-hungry and underperform in low-data offline RL regimes, while prior attempts to initialize DT with pre-trained language models (e.g., Wiki-RL) failed to surpass pure DT or value-based baselines like CQL. The paper asks whether the sequential modeling inductive bias of pre-trained LMs can be effectively transferred to offline RL to close this gap, particularly when in-domain data is scarce.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of (return-to-go, state, action) tuples of context length K, where states are continuous vectors (MuJoCo, Kitchen, Reacher2d) or pixel observations processed through a 3-layer CNN (Atari), and return-to-go is a scalar target. It outputs the next action — a continuous vector for MuJoCo/Kitchen tasks or a discrete action index for Atari. The backbone is GPT-2 small (12 layers, 768 hidden, 124M total params) or GPT-2 medium (24 layers, 1024 hidden) for Kitchen, with only ~3.5M trainable parameters via LoRA.

## Method

LaMo initializes a Decision Transformer with pre-trained GPT-2 weights and applies four coupled modifications: (1) replacing the standard linear input/output projections with 3-layer MLPs using GELU activations to bridge the domain gap between language tokens and continuous state/action vectors; (2) freezing all pre-trained Transformer weights and injecting LoRA low-rank adapters (rank 16–32) into Q, K, V attention matrices, so only 0.7% of parameters are updated; (3) adding an auxiliary next-token prediction loss on WikiText during RL fine-tuning, which temporarily swaps in the original LM projections to regularize training and prevent overfitting; and (4) jointly optimizing the decision loss (MSE on actions) and the language loss with a tunable weight λ. The key insight is that constraining adaptation to a low-rank subspace while simultaneously training on language tasks preserves the sequential modeling structure learned during pre-training, enabling strong few-shot generalization in the RL domain that full fine-tuning or frozen weights cannot achieve.

## Results

On sparse-reward tasks (Kitchen, Reacher2d), LaMo achieves an average normalized score of 47.9 at 100% data (↑31% over DT's 36.5) and 31.1 at low-data ratios (↑86% over DT's 16.7), surpassing all value-based baselines including CQL (10.6) and IQL (35.4). On dense-reward Atari tasks, LaMo reaches 226.0 average at 100% data (↑24% over DT) and 105.1 at 10% data (↑99% over DT). On dense-reward MuJoCo tasks, LaMo achieves 63.3 at 100% data (↑9% over DT) and 57.4 at low-data ratios (↑46% over DT), closing the gap with CQL (63.1) and IQL (64.1). In the extreme low-data regime (0.5% of Hopper data), LaMo scores 57.0 versus CQL's 37.9 and DT's 35.8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaMo decisively outperforms DT across all 8 tasks and all data ratios, with average improvements ranging from 9% (MuJoCo at 100% data) to 99% (Atari at 10% data). Against Wiki-RL (the closest prior work using pre-trained LMs for offline RL), LaMo achieves substantially higher scores in every setting, e.g., 47.9 vs. 23.8 on sparse-reward tasks at 100% data and 57.4 vs. 51.4 on MuJoCo at low-data ratios. On sparse-reward tasks, LaMo surpasses all value-based baselines (CQL, IQL, TD3+BC) in average performance, with CQL being the strongest competitor at 35.4 (IQL) vs. LaMo's 47.9. On dense-reward MuJoCo at 100% data, CQL (63.1) and IQL (64.1) remain slightly ahead of LaMo (63.3), but LaMo pulls ahead at lower data ratios (57.4 vs. CQL's 51.3 at low-data). On Atari, LaMo outperforms CQL (189.1) and BCQ (65.3) at 100% data, and the gap widens dramatically at 10% data where LaMo scores 105.1 vs. CQL's 79.8. LaMo also outperforms the recent Diffusion-QL method on all three MuJoCo tasks (57.4 vs. 40.8 average), with the largest margin at 0.5% data (e.g., Hopper: 57.0 vs. 13.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

LaMo builds directly on Decision Transformer (DT) as its base architecture and GPT-2 as its pre-trained backbone, but departs from the closest prior work, Wiki-RL (Reid et al., 2022), which also initialized DT with a pre-trained LM yet achieved only parity with vanilla DT. The key conceptual departure is a four-component design: freezing pre-trained weights and adapting via LoRA (0.7% trainable parameters), replacing linear input/output projections with MLPs to bridge the domain gap, and adding an auxiliary language prediction loss to regularize training. This positions LaMo as a more principled integration of cross-domain sequential modeling knowledge into offline RL, rather than simply reusing LM weights as a warm start.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that pre-trained language models can provide genuine inductive bias for offline RL, particularly in the low-data regime where LaMo achieves up to 86% improvement over DT at 10% data on sparse-reward tasks. However, limitations remain: CQL still outperforms LaMo on dense-reward MuJoCo tasks at full data scale, the auxiliary language loss only significantly helps in sparse-reward settings, and experiments are confined to GPT-2 small/medium with 3 seeds. Open questions include whether larger LLMs (LLaMA, GPT-4) would further improve offline RL, how to better exploit language reasoning for decision-making, and whether the observed benefits generalize beyond D4RL-style benchmarks.
