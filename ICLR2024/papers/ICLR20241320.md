## Problem

Diffusion-based super-resolution models produce unstable results because the reverse sampling process introduces randomness; even deterministic ODE samplers require a random boundary condition x_T ~ N(0,I), so quality fluctuates across runs and users cannot guarantee a good one-shot result. No principled method existed for selecting the boundary condition that would yield the highest-probability-density SR sample.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241320/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference the method takes a low-resolution image y and a single pre-computed noise vector x̃_T (in the HR image space) and outputs one deterministic super-resolution image x̃_0 = h_θ(x̃_T, y). The key unusual aspect is that x̃_T is computed once from a small reference set of R HR-LR pairs and then shared across all LR images regardless of content, so it acts as a model-level constant rather than a per-image input. The paper demonstrates this at 64×64→256×256 (bicubic degradation) and 128×128→512×512 (real-world degradation) resolutions.

## Method

The paper proves via Bayes' rule that the optimal boundary condition x_T* for the conditional diffusion ODE is approximately independent of the specific LR image, because the implicit classifier p_θ(y | h_θ(x_T, φ)) is near-uniform for a well-trained model. To approximate x_T*, it formulates an argmin over x_T of the summed LPIPS distance between ODE-projected samples h_θ(x_T, y_i) and ground-truth HR images z_i across a reference set of R pairs (Eq. 19). Since h_θ involves solving a diffusion ODE with no closed form, a Monte Carlo strategy samples K random x_T candidates, evaluates the aggregate LPIPS for each, and selects the best as x̃_T (Eq. 20). At test time, x̃_T is fed into any standard diffusion ODE solver (DDIM or DPM-Solver) together with the new LR image to produce a deterministic, high-quality SR result. The approach is entirely plug-and-play: no retraining is needed, and it works across different diffusion-based SR architectures (vanilla SR3-style UNet and StableSR/DiffIR built on Stable Diffusion).

## Results

On bicubic-SR (DIV2k-test), DDIM-100+x̃_T achieves the best LPIPS of 0.1032, surpassing the DDPM-1000 upper bound (0.1075) and all other sampling methods on the same SR3 model. On Urban100, DDIM-50+x̃_T reaches LPIPS 0.1164, also beating DDPM-1000 (0.1165). For real-SR, applying x̃_T to StableSR's DDIM-50 sampler improves PSNR from 21.37 to 22.13 on RealSR and LPIPS from 0.3065 to 0.2999, while DiffIR's D-4+x̃_T gains 0.49 dB PSNR on RealSR (25.33→25.82) over its original D-4 sampler.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GAN-based SR methods (ESRGAN, RankSRGAN), the proposed DDIM-100+x̃_T achieves lower LPIPS (0.1032 vs. 0.1082 for ESRGAN on DIV2k-test) while maintaining competitive PSNR, indicating better perceptual quality. Compared to other diffusion-based SR methods (SRDiff), the method substantially reduces LPIPS (0.1032 vs. 0.1286 on DIV2k-test). Within the same SR3 model, DDIM-100+x̃_T outperforms the standard DDPM-1000 sampler on LPIPS (0.1032 vs. 0.1075) while using only 100 steps instead of 1000, and also beats DDPM-100 (0.1257) and DDIM-50 (0.1483) by large margins. For real-SR, StableSR with DDIM-50+x̃_T improves over its official DDPM-200 setting on both LPIPS (0.3169 vs. 0.3189) and PSNR (19.55 vs. 19.42 on DIV2k-test; 22.13 vs. 21.37 on RealSR). DiffIR's D-4+x̃_T shows consistent gains over D-4 alone, particularly on RealSR where PSNR improves from 25.33 to 25.82 and LPIPS drops from 0.2604 to 0.2419. The method does not beat DDPM-100 on PSNR (28.48 vs. 29.16 on DIV2k-test), but achieves significantly better perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.1032 vs. 0.1257), reflecting the PSNR-perceptual quality trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the diffusion ODE framework from Song et al. (2021b) and the ODE solvers DDIM/DPM-Solver, but shifts focus from model architecture (the emphasis of SR3, StableSR, DiffIR) to the sampling procedure itself. The key conceptual departure is treating the boundary condition x_T of the diffusion ODE as an optimizable variable rather than a random draw from N(0,I), and proving that the optimal x_T* is approximately independent of the LR input. This reframes a previously overlooked source of randomness (the initial noise) as a lever for deterministic, high-quality sampling without any retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature means any existing diffusion-based SR model can be 'boosted' with a one-time Monte Carlo search over K candidates, requiring no architectural changes or additional training. The main limitation is that x̃_T has the same spatial dimensions as the LR image, preventing direct application to arbitrary resolutions; the authors flag combining with resolution-arbitrary sampling as future work. The independence assumption (x_T* shared across all LR images) is empirically validated but remains an approximation, and its behavior under distribution shift or very different degradation types is not fully explored.
