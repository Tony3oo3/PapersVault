## Problem

Training neural operators like FNO on high-resolution PDE data is memory- and compute-intensive, yet standard mixed-precision tools (e.g., PyTorch AMP) fail on FNO because they do not handle the complex-valued spectral-domain operations (FFT, tensor contraction, iFFT), leading to numerical overflow and poor memory efficiency. Moreover, it was unclear whether reducing precision in the Fourier transform would cause the kind of catastrophic error accumulation seen in classical pseudo-spectral solvers, making mixed-precision FNO appear infeasible.

## Input / Output

The method operates on discretized grid fields (128×128 for Navier-Stokes and Darcy Flow, 256×512 for spherical SWE) and irregular 3D point clouds (64×64×64 for Shape-Net Car and Ahmed-body), producing discretized solution fields (vorticity, pressure, or velocity) at the same or higher resolution. A key property is discretization convergence: models trained at 128×128 can be evaluated zero-shot at 256×256, 512×512, or 1024×1024 without retraining, and the mixed-precision variant preserves this property.

## Method

The paper proves that the precision error of a half-precision FNO block is O(εM) while the inherent discretization error is O(n^{-2/d}), showing the two are asymptotically comparable and justifying mixed-precision training. Practically, the method performs the three complex-valued FNO operations (forward FFT, spectral tensor contraction, inverse FFT) in float16, using a greedy algorithm to select the contraction order that minimizes intermediate tensor memory, while PyTorch AMP handles the remaining real-valued operations. To prevent the numerical overflow that causes naive mixed-precision FNO to produce NaNs, a tanh pre-activation is inserted before each forward FFT, which bounds the L∞ norm and Lipschitz constant of the input while minimally altering frequency-domain amplitude and phase. A precision schedule (first 25% mixed-precision, middle 50% AMP-only, final 25% full-precision) further improves final accuracy over both full-precision and constant mixed-precision baselines. The approach is applied to three FNO variants—TFNO, SFNO, and GINO—achieving up to 50% memory reduction and 58% throughput improvement with <0.1% accuracy loss.

## Results

Across five PDE datasets (Navier-Stokes, Darcy Flow, Spherical Shallow Water, Shape-Net Car, Ahmed-body), the proposed mixed-precision FNO reduces GPU memory by up to 50% and improves training throughput by up to 58% (1.58×) over full-precision training, with L² loss increasing by at most 0.28%. On Navier-Stokes, mixed-precision FNO achieves 0.004 L² error vs. 0.003 for full-precision FNO, while reducing memory from 9380 MB to 4832 MB. A precision-scheduling variant (mixed → AMP → full precision) further improves zero-shot super-resolution, achieving 0.00170 L² at 1024×1024 resolution versus 0.00213 for full-precision FNO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PyTorch's Automatic Mixed Precision (AMP), the proposed method achieves substantially greater memory reduction (up to 50% vs. ~20–25% for AMP alone) because AMP does not handle the complex-valued FFT and tensor contraction operations that dominate FNO's memory footprint; the combined AMP + Half-Prec FNO pipeline also yields super-linear memory savings by avoiding redundant casting. Against U-Net baselines, FNO consistently achieves lower L² error (0.003 vs. 0.111 on Navier-Stokes; 0.010 vs. 0.024 on Darcy Flow), and mixed-precision FNO delivers more memory reduction than AMP applied to U-Nets (50.4% vs. 20.9% on Navier-Stokes). Against BF16, the proposed FP16-based method avoids the significantly higher error degradation observed with BF16 on Navier-Stokes, and BF16 is unsupported for FFT operations in PyTorch, making it impractical for FNOs. The precision-scheduling variant outperforms full-precision FNO in zero-shot super-resolution (0.00170 vs. 0.00213 L² at 1024×1024), demonstrating that the mixed-precision training routine can actually improve generalization when combined with a staged precision schedule.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mixed-precision training literature (AMP, BF16, TF32) from standard neural networks to the spectral-domain operations of Fourier Neural Operators, a gap left by prior work like FourCastNet that applied AMP only to real-valued modules. It builds directly on the FNO architecture family (TFNO, SFNO, GINO) and their discretization-convergence properties, but departs from the conventional assumption that Fourier-based methods require full precision by proving that the inherent discretization error from using the DFT is asymptotically comparable to the precision error from half-precision arithmetic. The tanh pre-activation stabilizer and the greedy contraction-order optimization for complex-valued tensors are novel engineering contributions not present in standard mixed-precision toolkits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50% memory reduction and 58% throughput improvement with negligible accuracy loss make high-resolution PDE training substantially more accessible, enabling larger batch sizes and higher-resolution training on the same hardware. The precision-scheduling technique (mixed → AMP → full) that actually beats full-precision accuracy in zero-shot super-resolution is a practical recipe likely to be widely adopted. Open questions include whether the theoretical bounds and stabilization strategy extend to non-FNO operator architectures (DeepONet, GNO), to deeper FNO stacks where round-off might accumulate, and to even lower precisions (FP8) where the paper shows the precision error can no longer be bounded below the discretization error.
