## Problem

Two-layer neural networks (MLPs, CNNs, self-attention) admit equivalent convex formulations that guarantee global optimality, but the induced regularizers—nuclear norm for linear activations and a constrained (quasi-)nuclear norm for ReLU activations—become prohibitively expensive or NP-hard to compute at moderate scales. This computational barrier has prevented convex neural networks from being trained beyond small problems, leaving a gap between the theoretical optimality guarantees and practical scalability.

## Input / Output

For image classification, inputs are RGB images (CIFAR-10 at 32×32, Fashion-MNIST at 28×28) represented as patch matrices X_i ∈ R^{K×h} for CNNs or as a data matrix X ∈ R^{n×d} for MLPs, paired with class labels. The BM factorization outputs factor matrices U ∈ R^{d×m} and V ∈ R^{c×m} (or their CNN/self-attention analogues) rather than a single weight matrix, with the number of factors m controlling the rank of the implicit solution. For the theoretical experiments, a three-class spiral dataset with d=2, c=3 is used to validate the optimality gap bound.

## Method

The paper applies the Burer-Monteiro (BM) factorization to convex neural networks, replacing the intractable nuclear-norm or constrained-nuclear-norm regularizer with a simple Frobenius-norm penalty on rectangular factor matrices U and V such that Z = UV^T. For ReLU networks, the constrained nuclear norm translates into a linear inequality constraint (2D_j − I)XU_j ≥ 0 on the left factor, yielding a tractable non-convex problem with no spurious local minima under mild conditions. The key theoretical contribution is a novel relative optimality gap bound (Theorem 3.4) that certifies when any stationary point of the BM factorization is a global optimum, generalizing prior results from Mardani et al. (2013). For self-attention, the paper shows for the first time that linear self-attention with m ≥ d² + dc heads has no spurious local minima. Practically, the BM factorization enables layerwise training of convex gated-ReLU CNNs whose latent representations scale as mP channels instead of the intractable Pac channels, allowing five-stage convex training to match end-to-end non-convex deep CNNs on CIFAR-10 (81.5% vs 81.6%) and Fashion-MNIST (91.6% vs 91.2%).

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the 5-stage BM factorized convex two-layer gated ReLU CNN achieves 81.5% test accuracy with m=4 factors, compared to 81.6% for a 6-layer end-to-end non-convex ReLU CNN. On Fashion-MNIST, the 3-stage BM convex CNN reaches 91.6% test accuracy with m=4, outperforming the 91.2% of a 4-layer end-to-end non-convex ReLU CNN. Increasing the BM factorization size m from 1 to 4 consistently improves performance on CIFAR-10 (80.9% → 81.5%) but has a smaller effect on Fashion-MNIST (91.5% → 91.6%). The relative optimality gap bound (Theorem 3.4) is validated empirically on a three-class spiral dataset, showing it provides a useful proxy for detecting when GD converges to the global minimum versus a saddle point.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its BM factorized convex CNN (trained layerwise) against two end-to-end non-convex ReLU CNN baselines. On CIFAR-10, the 5-stage BM convex CNN with m=4 achieves 81.5%, essentially matching the 81.6% of the 6-layer end-to-end non-convex CNN from Kiliçarslan & Celik (2021) — a 0.1% gap. On Fashion-MNIST, the 3-stage BM convex CNN with m=4 achieves 91.6%, outperforming the 4-layer end-to-end non-convex CNN from Bhatnagar et al. (2017) by 0.4%. The key practical advantage is that the BM convex approach enables layerwise training with verifiable global optimality at each stage, whereas the baselines require full end-to-end non-convex optimization. Without the BM factorization, the convex CNN's induced regularizer is intractable and the latent representation for layerwise learning would have O(Pac) channels (upwards of 10⁴), making the approach infeasible.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: (1) the convex reformulation of two-layer neural networks (Pilanci & Ergen 2020; Sahiner et al. 2021b; Ergen & Pilanci 2020; Sahiner et al. 2022), which showed that training ReLU, linear, and self-attention networks is equivalent to a convex program with an induced nuclear-norm-like regularizer, and (2) the Burer-Monteiro factorization for SDPs and matrix factorization (Burer & Monteiro 2003, 2005; Boumal et al. 2020; Haeffele et al. 2014), which replaces a matrix variable with a low-rank factor to make non-convex optimization tractable. The key departure is that prior BM applications to neural networks were limited to over-simplified linear or quadratic-activation settings (Kawaguchi 2016; Haeffele & Vidal 2017; Du & Lee 2018); this paper is the first to apply BM to practical non-linear (ReLU) architectures including CNNs and self-attention. It also generalizes the optimality condition of Mardani et al. (2013) from a binary certificate to a continuous relative optimality gap bound, and extends the convex CNN formulation from global average pooling to arbitrary linear pooling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is that BM factorization makes convex neural networks trainable at moderate scales where the induced regularizer (especially the NP-hard constrained nuclear norm for ReLU) was previously intractable, and the layerwise training scheme shows convex two-layer CNNs can match end-to-end non-convex deep CNNs on CIFAR-10 and Fashion-MNIST. The relative optimality bound (Theorem 3.4) provides a verifiable, easily computable certificate of how close a stationary point is to the global optimum, which is useful for practitioners using first-order methods. Open questions include whether these results scale to larger datasets (ImageNet), deeper architectures, and whether the small experimental margins (0.1–0.4%) hold under stronger baselines; the theoretical no-spurious-local-minima result for self-attention with sufficient heads is clean but the required head count (m* ≤ d²+dc) may be impractical for large d.
