## Problem

Fairness-aware GNNs can be corrupted by adversarial perturbations to the input graph, but existing GNN attacks target only prediction utility and ignore the fairness dimension. Moreover, no prior fairness attack considers the practical requirement that the attack remain unnoticeable in terms of utility change, which would otherwise alert the model owner to the manipulation. This paper formulates the first unnoticeable fairness attack problem for GNNs, addressing two key challenges: designing a surrogate loss that generalizes across heterogeneous fairness objectives, and enforcing a utility-preservation constraint during the discrete structure perturbation.

## Input / Output

The attacker receives the clean adjacency matrix A, node attribute matrix X, ground truth labels on a training subset, and sensitive attribute labels partitioning nodes into groups, but does not observe the victim model's architecture or parameters (gray-box setting). The attack budget Δ caps the number of edge flips (Frobenius norm constraint ‖A′−A‖_F ≤ 2Δ), while the utility budget ε bounds the absolute change in cross-entropy loss on the training set. The output is a discrete binary adjacency matrix A′ with at most Δ edges flipped, which is then fed to the victim GNN (either at test time for evasion or at training time for poisoning) to elicit biased predictions.

## Method

G-FairAttack formulates the fairness attack as a bilevel discrete optimization and solves it with a sequential greedy edge-flipping procedure. The key surrogate loss uses Total Variation (TV) of the output distribution across sensitive groups, proven (Theorem 1) to upper-bound all three prevalent fairness losses—demographic parity, mutual information, and Wasserstein-1 distance—so a single surrogate model can mimic any unknown victim fairness objective. To enforce the unnoticeable utility constraint, the paper derives a closed-form projected gradient descent update (Theorem 2) in the continuous domain, then adapts it to the discrete setting via zeroth-order difference estimators, yielding a scoring function that balances fairness-loss gain against utility-loss change per edge flip. A fast computation strategy reduces per-iteration cost from O(n⁴) to O(d̄n² + dₓ·a·n) by incrementally updating the GCN aggregated feature matrix Z for only the affected neighborhood and restricting the candidate edge set to top-a importance-scored edges.

## Results

On fairness-aware victim models, G-FairAttack substantially increases demographic parity (Δdp, larger = less fair) while keeping accuracy changes minimal. For example, on Facebook, G-FairAttack raises Δdp from 0.27% to 5.55% for EDITS (a 20× increase), from 3.06% to 6.84% for Reg, and from 2.00% to 3.23% for FairGNN, while accuracy drops by less than 1%. On Pokec_z, Δdp increases from 1.89% to 4.25% (EDITS) and from 2.41% to 6.03% (Reg). The utility loss variation remains below 0.5% of the clean utility loss, confirming the unnoticeable constraint is satisfied.

## Baselines & Comparisons

G-FairAttack outperforms FA-GNN (Hussain et al., 2022), the first dedicated fairness attack on GNNs, on all three fairness-aware victim models (Reg, FairGNN, EDITS) across Facebook and Pokec_z. For instance, on Facebook, G-FairAttack achieves Δdp of 6.84% for Reg versus 4.67% for FA-GNN, and 5.55% for EDITS versus 0.60% for FA-GNN (a 9× gap). Against Gradient Ascent (adapted from Zügner & Gunnemann, 2019), G-FairAttack wins on Reg (6.84% vs. 3.99% on Facebook) and EDITS (5.55% vs. 3.25% on Facebook), confirming that the total-variation surrogate loss and non-gradient scoring function are more effective than raw gradient ascent for fairness objectives. The Random baseline is consistently the weakest, often failing to increase Δdp beyond the clean level for fairness-aware models. Notably, FA-GNN slightly outperforms G-FairAttack on vanilla GCN (8.73% vs. 8.13% Δdp on Facebook), which the authors attribute to the surrogate loss including a fairness term that mismatches the untrained-for-fairness GCN; this gap can be narrowed by reducing the fairness weight α.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of adversarial attacks on GNNs (Zügner et al. 2018; Zügner & Günnemann 2019; Xu et al. 2019), which have focused almost exclusively on degrading prediction utility, to the underexplored dimension of algorithmic fairness. It builds directly on the first fairness attack on GNNs by Hussain et al. (2022, FA-GNN) and the concurrent FATE framework (Kang et al. 2023), but departs from both by introducing an unnoticeable utility-change constraint and a theoretically grounded surrogate loss (total variation) that uniformly upper-bounds demographic parity, mutual information, and Wasserstein distance fairness objectives. The non-gradient sequential greedy optimization also departs from the gradient-based meta-learning approaches (Metattack) used in prior GNN attacks, motivated by a formal proposition showing gradient-based edge selection is not guaranteed to improve the objective in the discrete domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work reveals a concrete vulnerability in fairness-aware GNNs: even models with built-in fairness mechanisms (regularization, adversarial training, edge rewiring) can be silently corrupted into producing biased predictions while maintaining normal accuracy, making detection difficult. The total variation loss as a universal surrogate provides a principled, task-agnostic way to attack any fairness-aware GNN without knowing its specific fairness objective, which has practical implications for red-teaming deployed graph systems. Open questions include effective defense mechanisms against such unnoticeable fairness attacks, scalability to very large graphs, and extension to black-box settings where ground-truth labels are unavailable. The paper's focus on two sensitive groups and binary classification, while stated as easily extensible, leaves the multi-group/multi-class case unverified empirically.
