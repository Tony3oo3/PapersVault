## Problem

Dual ascent methods for constrained learning converge in objective value but provide no guarantee that the final primal iterate is feasible; prior theory requires randomizing over all iterates to obtain feasibility bounds, which is impractical and hinders explainability. This paper closes the gap between theory and practice by characterizing when the last or best iterate of a dual ascent algorithm is near-feasible and near-optimal without randomization, despite non-convexity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled tabular data (x, y) with a separate protected-attribute vector z (e.g., gender and race in COMPAS) and a set of constraint specifications (e.g., KL-divergence invariance bounds c for each protected-feature transformation ρ_i). It produces a constrained neural-network predictor f_θ (a 2-layer network with sigmoid activations) and the associated dual variables λ. The constraints are statistical expectations over the data distribution, estimated via stochastic supergradients during training, and the output predictor is the Lagrangian minimizer at the best dual iterate rather than a randomized mixture.

## Method

The key insight is to view the non-convex parametrized problem (P_p) as a finite-dimensional parametrization of a convex functional problem (P_u) over a compact set F ⊂ L², then use the unparametrized solution φ* as a reference to bound the sub-optimality and constraint violation of Lagrangian minimizers f_θ(λ_p*). Under a ν-near-universality assumption (the parametrization approximates F within L₂ distance ν), the authors decompose the constraint-violation gap into a dual-variable perturbation term (bounded via strong concavity μ_g and smoothness β_g of the unparametrized dual function) and a hypothesis-class perturbation term (bounded via the Fenchel conjugate of the perturbation function). Lemma 3.1 expresses μ_g and β_g in terms of problem parameters (strong convexity μ_0, Lipschitz constant M, smoothness β, and LICQ constant σ), yielding Theorem 3.1: both infeasibility and sub-optimality scale as O(√ν) times condition-number and dual-norm factors. A best-iterate convergence analysis (Lemma 4.1) then extends these bounds to the practical setting of approximate Lagrangian minimization and stochastic supergradient updates, showing that the best dual iterate achieves near-feasibility without storing or randomizing over the full iterate sequence.

## Results

The paper's main contribution is theoretical: Theorem 3.1 bounds the constraint violation of Lagrangian minimizers as ‖ℓ(f_θ(λ_p*)) − ℓ(φ*)‖_∞ ≤ M[1 + κ₁κ₀(1+Δ)]√(2mMν/μ₀ · (1+‖λ_p*‖₁)), showing that richer parametrizations (smaller ν) yield tighter feasibility guarantees. Experimentally, on the COMPAS dataset with a 2-layer NN, the last iterate predictor achieves constraint satisfaction comparable to the randomized predictor (which samples uniformly from all primal iterates), while the unconstrained model achieves slightly higher test accuracy at the cost of worse counterfactual fairness. Increasing parametrization richness (via higher-dimensional feature projections) reduces maximum constraint violation by up to an order of magnitude, consistent with the theoretical prediction that ν governs the bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three predictors on the COMPAS recidivism dataset: (1) an unconstrained model trained without fairness constraints, (2) a last-iterate constrained model (final primal iterate of dual ascent), and (3) a randomized constrained model (uniformly samples from all primal iterates at prediction time). The unconstrained model achieves slightly higher average test accuracy than both constrained models, but at the cost of significantly worse counterfactual fairness (higher KL divergence under protected-attribute interventions). The key empirical finding is that the last iterate and randomized predictors produce nearly identical constraint violation profiles, directly validating Theorem 3.1's prediction that rich parametrizations make the last iterate near-feasible without randomization. Additionally, increasing model capacity (by projecting data into higher-dimensional spaces) reduces the maximum constraint violation by up to an order of magnitude, confirming the theoretical role of the near-universality constant ν in governing feasibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PAC constrained learning framework of Chamon & Ribeiro (2020) and Chamon et al. (2023), which established that dual ascent methods converge in objective value for non-convex constrained learning, by adding the missing feasibility guarantees for primal iterates. It directly addresses the cyclostationary infeasibility identified in Cotter et al. (2019, Section 6.3.1), where primal iterates oscillate between feasible and infeasible states, making randomization over all iterates the only theoretical recourse. The key conceptual departure is the observation that non-convex parametrized problems are approximations of convex functional programs, enabling the use of perturbation analysis and Fenchel conjugate duality to bound constraint violation of Lagrangian minimizers without randomization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This result provides the first theoretical justification for the widely observed empirical practice of using the last or best iterate in dual constrained learning, eliminating the need to store and randomize over all primal iterates—a significant practical simplification for fairness, robustness, and federated learning applications. The non-trivial finding that richer parametrizations simultaneously reduce approximation error and make the optimization problem easier (by tightening feasibility bounds) has broader implications for model capacity selection. Limitations include reliance on LICQ, strong convexity of the objective functional, and curvature assumptions on the dual function that are difficult to verify in practice; the empirical validation is limited to a single dataset (COMPAS) and a 2-layer network. Open questions include incorporating estimation error from empirical Lagrangians and characterizing dual curvature via alternative means to relax the unparametrized problem assumptions.
