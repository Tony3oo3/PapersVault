## Problem

Existing VFL frameworks such as FATE are industry-grade and not designed for tracking research progress, while individual defense methods are each evaluated under specific tasks and settings without a unified comparison metric. No prior work provides a comprehensive benchmark that simultaneously covers model utility, communication efficiency, and robustness to diverse attacks and defenses across multiple VFL configurations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241341/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes vertically partitioned features (split by feature dimension rather than sample) from 13 datasets spanning image, tabular, and text domains, configured via a JSON file specifying model architectures, communication protocols, attacks, and defenses. It produces model predictions alongside a novel suite of unified evaluation metrics—Defense Capability Score (DCS), Type-level DCS, and Comprehensive DCS—that map each defense's performance on an attack-performance vs. model-performance plane to a single comparable score, in addition to standard communication rounds, per-round data volume, and execution time measurements.

## Method

VFLAIR is a lightweight, modular VFL framework supporting both aggVFL (non-trainable global aggregation) and splitVFL (trainable global model) with 29 local model architectures, 5 communication protocols (FedSGD, FedBCD, CELU-VFL, Quantize, Top-k), and Paillier encryption for tree-based VFL. The key methodological contribution is a unified defense evaluation metric: DCS measures each defense's weighted Euclidean distance to an ideal point (zero attack performance, full model utility) on an AP-MP graph with an adjustable trade-off parameter β, then aggregates into T-DCS (per attack type) and C-DCS (across all four attack types). The paper benchmarks 11 attacks (label inference, feature reconstruction, targeted and non-targeted backdoor) against 8 defenses (G-DP, L-DP, GS, GPer, dCor, CAE, DCAE, MID) across MNIST, CIFAR-10, and NUSWIDE under multiple VFL settings, revealing that MID, L-DP, and G-DP are most broadly effective, splitVFL is less vulnerable than aggVFL, and FedBCD reduces the attack surface compared to FedSGD.

## Results

On the NUSWIDE dataset (aggVFL, FedSGD), MID with λ=10000 achieves the highest Comprehensive Defense Capability Score (C-DCS) of 0.7508, followed by G-DP (0.1) at 0.7447 and L-DP (0.1) at 0.7410. For main task performance, splitVFL with FedSGD reaches 97.3% MP on MNIST and 88.8% on NUSWIDE, while FedBCD (Q=5) reduces communication rounds by ~24% (180→143 on MNIST) with negligible MP loss. Feature Reconstruction (FR) attacks are the hardest to defend, with T-DCS_FR values around 0.58 across all defenses, compared to T-DCS_LI values of 0.73–0.82.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks 8 defense methods against 11 attacks across 3 datasets. MID (Mutual Information Defense, Zou et al. 2023) consistently ranks first in C-DCS across all datasets and settings, achieving 0.7508 on NUSWIDE while preserving high MP. G-DP and L-DP (Dwork 2006; Fu et al. 2022a) are the strongest general-purpose alternatives with C-DCS of 0.7447 and 0.7410 respectively, effective across LI, FR, TB, and NTB attacks. dCor (Sun et al. 2022; Vepakomma et al. 2019) performs well against LI and TB attacks (T-DCS_LI=0.8155) but is notably weaker on NTB attacks (T-DCS_NTB=0.8051 vs. 0.8707 for MID). GS (Aji & Heafield 2017) is strong for LI attacks but underperforms on LRB backdoor attacks (T-DCS_TB=0.6415). GPer (Yang et al. 2022a) matches DP methods on label-related attacks but is not evaluated against FR or NTB. The paper also shows that splitVFL is less vulnerable than aggVFL (positive DCS gap) and FedBCD is less vulnerable than FedSGD, providing architectural recommendations beyond individual defense choices.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by providing the first comprehensive research-oriented benchmark for Vertical Federated Learning, explicitly positioning itself against industry-grade VFL platforms (FATE, Fedlearner, PaddleFL, Pysyft, FedTree, FedML) that are too heavy-weight for tracking research progress, and against existing FL benchmarks (FedEval, FedScale, FedAudio) that target only Horizontal FL. Rather than building on a single prior method, VFLAIR integrates 11 previously published attacks and 8 defense strategies into a unified modular framework, and introduces the DCS/T-DCS/C-DCS evaluation metrics as a novel way to compare defenses across heterogeneous attack types under a single scalar score. The framework also unifies two VFL model partition paradigms (aggVFL and splitVFL) and multiple communication protocols (FedSGD, FedBCD, CELU-VFL, Quantize, Top-k) that had previously been evaluated in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VFLAIR fills a critical gap by giving the VFL research community a lightweight, extensible tool for systematic comparison of attacks, defenses, and system design choices that was previously unavailable, and the DCS metrics offer a principled way to rank defenses without cherry-picking attack-specific evaluations. Key practical findings—MID, L-DP, and G-DP being broadly effective; splitVFL being less vulnerable than aggVFL; and FedBCD reducing attack surface compared to FedSGD—provide concrete deployment guidance. Limitations include only 3 datasets used for the full attack-defense benchmark, limited cryptographic support (only Paillier HE for tree-based VFL), and the absence of multi-party attack scenarios beyond 2-party settings, leaving open questions about how these findings generalize to larger coalitions and more complex cryptographic protocols.
