## Problem

Spectral Contrastive Loss (SpeCL) has two key limitations: it enforces orthogonality between negative-pair representations (which could be relaxed for greater discriminativeness), and it is vulnerable to wrong connections in the positive-pair graph caused by noisy views or noisy labels, with no built-in mechanism to mitigate their adverse effects. The paper asks whether a simple modification to SpeCL can simultaneously relax the orthogonality constraint and implicitly correct erroneous positive-pair weights.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairs of augmented RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR/SVHN/digit datasets, 64×64 for ImageNet-100 and DomainNet) and produces a d-dimensional representation vector per sample. In the supervised setting, the representation is used with a linear probe or class-mean features to predict class labels; in UDA, a linear classifier trained on source-domain representations is applied to target-domain samples. The representation dimension d is theoretically tied to p+r−1 (number of domains plus classes minus one) in the UDA analysis, and the loss is computed over all pairwise similarities within a batch rather than a single anchor-positive-negative triplet.

## Method

The core modification adds an additive constant τ to the negative-pair term of SpeCL, i.e., replacing (f(x)ᵀf(x⁻))² with (f(x)ᵀf(x⁻)+τ)². Algebraically, this is equivalent to appending a zero-mean regularization term 2τ·‖E[f(x)]‖² to the loss, and in the graph formulation it uniformly subtracts τ|E|/N² from every entry of the positive-pair adjacency matrix, preserving the magnitude ordering of edge weights while altering their ratios. In UDA, this uniform reduction tightens the target-domain error bound by a multiplicative factor of (1−τ)². In supervised learning, the global minimizer of the modified loss yields a neural-collapse-like geometry where class-mean features form an equiangular frame with inter-class angle arccos(−τ/(r−τ)), and the regularization implicitly reduces off-diagonal (mislabeled) entries in the noise transition matrix, effectively lowering the noise rate. The approach is validated with PreAct-ResNet-18 and ResNet-50 backbones across self-supervised, supervised, UDA, and noisy-label benchmarks.

## Results

On self-supervised linear probing, zero-mean regularization (τ=1.0) improves CIFAR-100 from 52.66% to 56.63% and SVHN from 89.77% to 91.24% over vanilla SpeCL. On supervised top-1 accuracy, τ=1.0 lifts CIFAR-100 from 65.59% to 69.59% (+4.0%) and CIFAR-10 from 93.47% to 94.38%. For unsupervised domain adaptation across four digit datasets, τ=0.1 raises the average accuracy from 71.12% to 76.60% (+5.5%). Under 80% symmetric label noise on CIFAR-10, τ=0.1 achieves 80.29% top-1 accuracy, substantially outperforming GCE (52.25%) and Focal loss (13.54%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla SpeCL (HaoChen et al., NeurIPS 2021), zero-mean regularization consistently wins across all four tasks: +4.0% on CIFAR-100 supervised, +5.5% average on UDA (τ=0.1), and +4.8% on CIFAR-10 at 80% label noise. Against GCE (Zhang et al., NeurIPS 2018) on noisy-label classification, SpeCL with τ=0.1 achieves 80.29% vs. 52.25% on CIFAR-10 at 80% noise, and 53.90% vs. 9.13% on CIFAR-100 at 80% noise. Against Focal loss (Lin et al., ICCV 2017), the gap is even more dramatic: 80.29% vs. 13.54% on CIFAR-10 at 80% noise. Against InfoNCE (van den Oord et al., 2018) in the appendix, the modified InfoNCE with τ=0.5 reaches 87.32% on CIFAR-10 self-supervised vs. 86.56% for vanilla InfoNCE, confirming the 'weight reduction' principle generalizes beyond SpeCL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Spectral Contrastive Loss (SpeCL) of HaoChen et al. (NeurIPS 2021) by adding a single additive constant τ to the negative-pair term, which algebraically decomposes into a zero-mean regularization on representations. The supervised formulation adapts the class-structured positive pairs from Supervised Contrastive Learning (Khosla et al., NeurIPS 2020), while the UDA analysis builds on the stochastic block model framework of Shen et al. (ICML 2022). The paper also draws explicit connections to margin-based losses (ArcFace, SphereFace, CosFace) and shows the same 'uniform weight reduction' principle applies to InfoNCE, positioning zero-mean regularization as a unifying mechanism across contrastive loss families. The optimal supervised solution at τ=1 recovers the Equiangular Tight Frame structure central to Neural Collapse (Papyan et al., 2020), linking spectral contrastive learning to the terminal-phase geometry of deep networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical takeaway is that a single hyperparameter τ ∈ [0,1] simultaneously relaxes the orthogonality constraint on negative pairs, enforces zero-mean representations, and implicitly reduces wrong connections in positive-pair graphs—providing a clean, interpretable knob for contrastive training. The theoretical guarantee that τ tightens the UDA error bound by a factor of (1−τ)² and that it reduces effective label noise rate is a useful design principle, though the empirical gains on standard benchmarks (CIFAR, SVHN) are modest (1–3%). Open questions include whether the benefits persist at ImageNet scale with full 1000 classes, whether the stochastic block model assumptions hold for real-world augmentation graphs, and whether the uniform weight reduction is optimal compared to adaptive or class-aware corrections of wrong connections.
