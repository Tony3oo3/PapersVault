## Problem

Standard in-context learning (ICL) relies on human-crafted few-shot demonstrations, which introduces two practical problems: performance is highly sensitive to which examples are selected and in what order, and generating demonstrations—especially with step-by-step rationales for chain-of-thought prompting—is labor-intensive and sometimes infeasible. The paper asks whether LLMs can generate their own demonstrations, eliminating the need for any human-provided training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240244/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single test question and a task-specific instruction as input. In a first generation call, the LLM produces k similar examples (with answers, and optionally step-by-step reasoning) formatted with structured delimiters (e.g., [[Question1:...Answer1:...]]) to facilitate automated answer extraction. In a second call, these model-generated demonstrations are prepended to the test input to elicit the final answer, reasoning trace, or code. Because demonstrations are generated per test sample, they are customized to each input rather than shared across the dataset as in standard ICL.

## Method

SEC (Self-Contemplation Prompting) is a two-stage prompting strategy that replaces human-crafted demonstrations with LLM-generated ones. In stage 1, the LLM is given the test input plus an instruction to generate k similar examples (with answers, and for CoT-SEC, with step-by-step reasoning), constrained by an explicit output format template. In stage 2, these generated demonstrations serve as the few-shot context for a second prompt that produces the final prediction. The key distinction from ICL is that demonstrations are conditioned on the specific test input, making them analogous to kNN-retrieved examples but without requiring a retrieval index or external dataset. The approach is a zero-shot paradigm in nature—no annotated data is used—yet it bridges the gap between zero-shot prompting and few-shot ICL, achieving comparable accuracy across arithmetic, commonsense, multi-task NLU, and code generation benchmarks on GPT-3.5, GPT-4, and Llama2 34B.

## Results

On GPT-3.5, CoT-SEC achieves 33.5% on MATH (vs. 31.2% for CoT-ICL), 77.0% on GSM8K (vs. 77.4%), 71.4% on MMLU (vs. 69.6%), and 54.6% on C-Eval (vs. 53.1%), while Vanilla SEC reaches 75.6% on HumanEval (vs. 73.8% for Vanilla ICL). On GPT-4, CoT-SEC achieves 41.9% on MATH, 92.1% on GSM8K, 96.2% on ARC, and 86.5% on MMLU, all within 1 point of CoT-ICL. Across all six benchmarks and three model backbones (GPT-3.5, GPT-4, Llama2-34B), SEC significantly outperforms zero-shot baselines while matching or exceeding ICL with hand-crafted demonstrations, all without any human-annotated examples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against zero-shot baselines, SEC delivers large gains: CoT-SEC improves over Zero-shot CoT by +1.8 on MATH, +3.6 on GSM8K, +2.8 on ARC, +10.9 on MMLU, and +4.1 on C-Eval (GPT-3.5). Against Vanilla ICL, Vanilla SEC wins on GSM8K (65.4 vs. 57.1) and HumanEval (75.6 vs. 73.8) but trails slightly on MATH (18.1 vs. 20.3) and ARC (85.9 vs. 86.5). Against CoT-ICL, CoT-SEC is competitive overall: it outperforms on MATH (33.5 vs. 31.2), MMLU (71.4 vs. 69.6), and C-Eval (54.6 vs. 53.1), is nearly tied on GSM8K (77.0 vs. 77.4), and trails by 1.0 point on ARC (86.9 vs. 87.9). Against Auto-CoT (which requires access to the full test set and clustering), CoT-SEC is within 0.5 points on GSM8K (77.0 vs. 77.5) and 0.9 points on ARC (86.9 vs. 87.8), while needing only two queries per test sample versus Auto-CoT's intensive multi-query pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEC positions itself as a direct alternative to the standard ICL paradigm (Brown et al., 2020) and its CoT extension (Wei et al., 2022b), replacing human-crafted demonstrations with LLM-generated ones tailored to each test input. Conceptually, it shares the input-conditioned demonstration idea with kNN prompting (Xu et al., 2023) but achieves it through generation rather than retrieval, and differs from Self-Prompting (Li et al., 2022) by avoiding a pre-generated corpus and clustering step. The CoT-SEC variant builds on zero-shot CoT (Kojima et al.) by asking the model to produce rationales as part of its self-generated demonstrations, while requiring only two queries per test sample compared to Auto-CoT's (Zhang et al., 2022b) full-test-set access and intensive clustering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the working assumption that human-annotated demonstrations are a necessary component of effective ICL, showing that for many tasks contemporary LLMs can self-generate demonstrations that match or slightly exceed hand-crafted ones. This has practical implications for reducing annotation cost and prompt-engineering effort, and raises the broader open question of whether supervised training data may become dispensable as LLMs grow more capable. However, the gains are modest (comparable rather than dramatically superior), SEC degrades on weaker models and out-of-distribution tasks (e.g., base-5 arithmetic), and the error analysis reveals that incorrect self-generated demonstrations remain a significant failure mode. Future work on refining the quality and relevance of model-generated demonstrations, and on understanding when SEC's input-conditioned advantage is most pronounced, would strengthen the case for reducing reliance on human annotation.
