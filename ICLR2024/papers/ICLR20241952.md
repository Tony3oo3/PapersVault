## Problem

Diffusion generative models have achieved strong results in Euclidean domains like images and video, but learning distributions over continuous functions defined on curved Riemannian manifolds (spheres, meshes, graphs) remains largely unexplored. Existing approaches for generative modeling of fields either rely on adversarial training, latent parametrizations, or operate only in ambient Euclidean space, leaving the general non-Euclidean setting open.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241952/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

MDF takes as input a manifold geometry (discretized as a mesh, graph, or pointcloud) and a training collection of fields f: M → Y mapping manifold points to a signal space (e.g., scalar values, RGB, or 3D coordinates). It produces new continuous fields on the same manifold by sampling from the learned distribution. In the generalized setting (molecular conformers), each function lives on a different graph, and the output is a 3D atomic coordinate assignment. For conditional inference, partial observations of the field can be provided to constrain generation.

## Method

MDF defines an intrinsic coordinate system on the manifold using the first k eigen-functions of the Laplace-Beltrami Operator, which serve as a Fourier-like positional encoding replacing ambient-space coordinates. A field is explicitly parametrized as a set of coordinate-signal pairs, and the forward diffusion process corrupts only the signal portion while keeping the eigen-function coordinates fixed. The score network is a PerceiverIO transformer that cross-attends between a context set (a random subset of the field) and a query set to predict the noise in the query signals. This intrinsic formulation makes the model invariant to rigid and isometric transformations of the manifold, and it generalizes naturally to settings where each function is defined on a different manifold (e.g., molecular graphs of varying topology) without requiring domain-specific geometric assumptions like torsional angles.

## Results

On GEOM-QM9 molecular conformer generation, MDF achieves 95.30% recall coverage and 0.124 mean AMR (recall), outperforming the previous SOTA Torsional Diffusion (92.80%, 0.178) without modeling torsional angles. On the ERA5 climate dataset, MDF obtains COV 0.347 and MMD 0.00498, roughly tripling the coverage of GASP (0.114) while halving its MMD (0.00964). On fixed-manifold experiments (bunny, human mesh), MDF consistently surpasses DPF in both COV and MMD, with the gap widening as mean curvature increases (e.g., on the human mesh GMM: MDF COV 0.575 vs DPF 0.472).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DPF (Zhuang et al., 2023), MDF wins on COV across all three fixed manifolds (wave, bunny, human) and all three datasets (GMM, MNIST, CelebA-HQ), with the advantage growing with curvature: on the human mesh GMM, MDF achieves COV 0.575 vs DPF's 0.472, and MMD 0.00108 vs 0.00120. Against GASP (Dupont et al., 2022b), MDF dominates on ERA5 (COV 0.347 vs 0.114; MMD 0.00498 vs 0.00964) and CelebA-HQ on the bunny (MMD 0.11101 vs 0.38979). On GEOM-QM9, MDF outperforms all four molecular baselines—CGCF, GeoDiff, GeoMol, and Torsional Diffusion—in recall coverage (95.30% vs 92.80% best) and recall AMR (0.124 vs 0.178 best), while matching Torsional Diffusion on precision coverage (91.50% vs 92.70%) but improving precision AMR (0.169 vs 0.221). Notably, MDF achieves these results without any domain-specific geometric assumptions (e.g., torsional angles), making it more generalizable than GeoMol and Torsional Diffusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

MDF extends the explicit field parametrization of Diffusion Probabilistic Fields (DPF, Zhuang et al. 2023) from Euclidean domains to Riemannian manifolds by replacing ambient-space Fourier positional encodings with eigen-functions of the Laplace-Beltrami Operator as an intrinsic coordinate system. This draws on spectral geometry tools previously used for fitting single functions on manifolds (Koestler et al. 2022; Grattarola & Vandergheynst 2022) and for positional encodings in Graph Transformers (Maskey et al. 2022; Sharp et al. 2022), but repurposes them for the fundamentally different task of learning a *distribution* over functions rather than fitting one. The paper also positions itself as lifting Riemannian generative modeling (which learns distributions of points on a manifold) to function spaces, and contrasts with adversarial (GASP) and latent-parametrization approaches for Euclidean function generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MDF provides a general-purpose recipe for diffusion generative modeling on arbitrary curved geometries (meshes, graphs, point clouds), enabling applications to climate modeling on the sphere, PDE solving on surfaces, and molecular conformer generation without domain-specific structural assumptions. The intrinsic coordinate system yields invariance to rigid and isometric transformations, a practical advantage over ambient-space approaches. Key limitations include the quadratic attention cost of the transformer score network (mitigated by PerceiverIO), iterative DDPM-style sampling, and the need to precompute LBO eigen-decompositions. Open directions include inverse PDE problems, scaling to larger and more diverse manifold collections, and incorporating faster samplers.
