## Problem

Most real-world graphs exhibit hierarchical community structures, yet existing graph generative models (autoregressive, diffusion, GAN-based) largely ignore this multi-scale organization. While hierarchical generative models exist for domain-specific data (audio, images, molecular motifs), no data-driven generative model for generic graphs effectively incorporates hierarchical community structure while remaining scalable to large graphs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241487/cropped_page2_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a set of training graphs (topology only, no node/edge attributes) and first applies the Louvain algorithm to produce a two-level hierarchical decomposition (L=2) with communities and bipartites at each level. It outputs a full adjacency matrix constructed block-wise: community sub-graphs are generated autoregressively row-by-row, then cross-community bipartite edges are predicted simultaneously. At higher levels, edges carry integer-valued weights (sums of child edges); at the leaf level, edges are binary.

## Method

HiGen generates graphs coarse-to-fine across hierarchical levels, where at each level communities are generated in parallel via an autoregressive row-by-row process and bipartite (cross-community) edges are predicted simultaneously by a separate GNN. The key theoretical contribution (Theorem 3.2) proves that a multinomial distribution over edge weights can be factorized into a chain of binomial and multinomial distributions via a recursive stick-breaking process, enabling efficient O(|V_C|) autoregressive generation per community rather than O(|V_C|²). Node and edge embeddings are computed by GraphGPS (a hybrid MPNN + Transformer architecture) with positional/structural encodings, and parent-level node embeddings are concatenated to capture long-range global structure. Output distributions are modeled as K=20 mixture models of binomial×multinomial (for communities) or multinomial (for bipartites), with Bregman-divergence-based losses for training.

## Results

HiGen achieves state-of-the-art or near-state-of-the-art MMD scores across all four structure-based metrics (Degree, Clustering, Orbit, Spectral) on four benchmark datasets. On Protein, HiGen dominates all baselines with scores of 0.0012 (Deg), 0.0435 (Clus), 0.0234 (Orbit), and 0.0025 (Spec), substantially outperforming the next-best SPECTRE (0.0056, 0.0843, 0.0267, 0.0052). On Ego, HiGen achieves the best scores on all five metrics including GNN-based RBF (0.0454) and Fréchet Distance (5.24), compared to EDGE's 0.0658 and 15.76. On Enzyme, HiGen achieves the best Clustering (0.038) and Orbit (7.2e-4) scores, though DiGress retains a slight edge on Degree (0.004 vs 0.012). Sampling speed is also significantly improved: HiGen samples a Protein graph in 1.33s vs GRAN's 46.04s (34.6× speedup).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiGen outperforms all autoregressive baselines (GraphRNN, GRAN) by a wide margin across every dataset and metric, with the largest gaps on Protein (e.g., Orbit 0.0234 vs GRAN's 0.3458) and Ego (FD 5.24 vs GRAN's 489.96). Against the GAN-based SPECTRE, HiGen wins decisively on Protein (all four metrics) and Ego, while on SBM the two are competitive (HiGen leads on Orbit and Spec but trails slightly on Degree). Among diffusion models, HiGen beats DiGress on Clustering and Orbit for Enzyme (0.038 vs 0.083; 7.2e-4 vs 0.002) and on all Ego metrics, though DiGress retains a narrow Degree advantage on Enzyme (0.004 vs 0.012). HiGen also surpasses GDSS, GraphARM, and EDGE on all shared metrics, with the most striking margin on Ego Fréchet Distance (5.24 vs EDGE's 15.76). On the large-scale 3D Point Cloud dataset (up to 5K nodes), HiGen-s outperforms GRAN on Clustering (0.282 vs 0.51), Orbit (0.0345 vs 0.21), and Spectral (0.00546 vs 0.00745), though GRAN retains a slight Degree edge (0.0175 vs 0.0348). Overall, HiGen achieves the best or second-best score on 22 out of 24 reported metric-dataset combinations, with its only losses being Degree on SBM (to SPECTRE/DiGress) and Enzyme (to DiGress).

## Relation to Prior Work

HiGen extends the autoregressive graph generation paradigm (GraphRNN, GRAN) by replacing the global sequential traversal of the adjacency matrix with a hierarchical, community-parallel decomposition. It draws on the stick-breaking factorization of multinomial distributions (Linderman et al., 2015) to derive a row-by-row autoregressive process for integer-valued edge weights, and on hierarchical generative models for specific domains (molecular motifs in Jin et al., 2020; images in Reed et al., 2017) but generalizes to unstructured graphs without domain-specific priors. The encoding backbone leverages GraphGPS (Rampašek et al., 2022) and GAT (Liao et al., 2019), while the hierarchical structure is obtained via the Louvain modularity algorithm rather than being learned end-to-end.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HiGen addresses two persistent limitations of autoregressive graph generators: sensitivity to node ordering and O(n) or O(n²) sequential generation steps, reducing the latter to O(n_c · log n) where n_c is the largest community size. The 34× sampling speedup on Protein graphs and the ability to match or exceed GRAN with ~4× fewer parameters suggest a practical path toward scaling graph generation to large graphs. Open questions include whether the Louvain-derived hierarchy is optimal (it is a heuristic, not learned), how the method extends to attributed or multi-relational graphs, and whether the assumed conditional independence between bipartites holds in dense or highly correlated community structures.
