## Problem

Existing protein representation learning methods encode amino acid sequences and 3D backbone structures but largely neglect protein surface characteristics, which are critical for molecular interactions such as ligand binding, enzymatic catalysis, and signal transduction. This paper addresses the gap by proposing the first pre-training strategy that jointly incorporates sequence, 3D structure, and surface information into protein representation learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241794/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three modalities: a protein amino acid sequence (processed by ESM-1b), a protein 3D structure graph with residue nodes and sequential/radius/kNN edges (processed by GearNet-Edge-IEConv), and a protein surface point cloud of 16,384 randomly sampled points annotated with atom categorical embeddings and top-K nearest-neighbor distances as 'chemical colors.' The INR decoder predicts SDF values for K query coordinates near the molecular surface (500,000 SDF training points per protein, independent of the point cloud input), and the final output is a per-residue protein representation embedding usable for downstream tasks, alongside a reconstructed surface mesh via marching cubes on a 128³ voxel grid.

## Method

ProteinINR is a generalizable Implicit Neural Representation that uses a Transformer encoder to compute INR parameters from three token streams: point cloud embeddings (via DSPoint encoder with high-frequency fusion), structure embeddings (from GearNet-Edge-IEConv), and spatially-arranged latent codes (rearranged into a 3D voxel grid and processed with 3D convolutions to inject spatial inductive bias, extending Spatial Functa to 3D). A locality-aware INR decoder then predicts SDF values for query coordinates, trained with clamped L2 loss (clamp δ=0.2, following DeepSDF). The pre-training follows a 'series-fusion' continual strategy: (1) pre-train the sequence encoder (ESM-1b), (2) pre-train the structure encoder on surfaces by minimizing SDF regression loss through ProteinINR, and (3) pre-train the structure encoder on 3D structures via multi-view contrastive learning. The key insight is that INR-based surface reconstruction serves as a self-supervised pre-training objective that forces the structure encoder to internalize surface geometry and chemistry, complementing the contrastive structure pre-training.

## Results

On the Enzyme Commission (EC) task, ESM-GearNet-INR-MC achieves the best Fmax of 89.6 and AUPR of 90.3, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art ESM-GearNet-MC (89.0 / 89.7). Across all downstream tasks combined (sum of Fmax, AUPR, and FC accuracy), ESM-GearNet-INR reaches 530.3 and ESM-GearNet-INR-MC reaches 528.1, both surpassing ESM-GearNet-MC (521.9). On Gene Ontology sub-tasks, ESM-GearNet-INR attains the highest AUPR scores (GO-BP: 33.4, GO-MF: 62.6, GO-CC: 36.9), while ESM-GearNet-MC retains the best FC accuracy (53.8 vs. 50.8). ProteinINR reconstructs protein surfaces with an average Chamfer distance of 0.003 on a 30,000-sample test set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to the sequence-only baselines ESM-1b and ESM-2, ESM-GearNet-INR-MC improves EC Fmax by 2.7 and 2.2 points respectively, and GO-BP Fmax by 6.6 and 4.6 points, demonstrating that adding structure and surface modalities substantially boosts function prediction. Against the structure-only GearNet baseline (no pre-training), the full model gains 8.0 Fmax on EC, 7.0 Fmax on GO-BP, 7.9 Fmax on GO-MF, and 4.0 accuracy on Fold Classification. The most direct comparison is with ESM-GearNet-MC (the prior state-of-the-art combining sequence + structure pre-training): adding surface pre-training via ProteinINR raises the total sum score from 521.9 to 528.1 (INR-MC) or 530.3 (INR only), with the largest gains on GO AUPR metrics (e.g., GO-MF AUPR 57.9 → 62.6, GO-CC AUPR 32.4 → 36.9). However, ESM-GearNet-MC retains the best FC accuracy (53.8 vs. 50.8), suggesting surface features are less informative for global fold classification. The ablation in Table 3 further confirms that pre-training on surfaces before structures (INR-MC order) outperforms the reverse order (MC-INR) by 19.6 sum points, validating the proposed continual pre-training sequence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the protein representation learning lineage (ESM for sequences, GearNet for structures, ESM-GearNet for series-fusion) by introducing a third pre-training modality: protein molecular surfaces. The surface learning mechanism is built on TransINR's Transformer-based generalizable INR framework, adapted to the protein domain with a DSPoint-based point encoder, a locality-aware INR decoder from Lee et al. (2023), and 3D spatial inductive biases inspired by Spatial Functa. The key conceptual departure from prior surface-related protein work (MaSIF, dMaSIF, HOLOProt) is that those methods treat surfaces as fixed-resolution meshes or point clouds for encoding, whereas ProteinINR uses INRs as a pre-training objective that forces the structure encoder to internalize surface geometry, making the surface a training signal rather than an input feature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Protein surfaces govern molecular interactions (ligand binding, catalysis, signal transduction), yet prior protein representation learning has largely ignored them; this paper provides the first evidence that surface-aware pre-training yields measurable gains on function prediction tasks. A notable limitation is that the GearNet encoder operates only on alpha-carbon coordinates, so the surface information (derived from full-atom geometry) is partially lost, and the method depends on known or predicted 3D structures. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to small-molecule drugs, whether surface latent codes can drive generative protein design, and how to better integrate surface and structure pre-training given that the encoder's alpha-carbon focus creates a representational bottleneck.
