## Problem

Diffusion probabilistic models suffer from exposure bias: the network is trained on ground-truth corrupted samples but must operate on its own predictions during iterative denoising, causing errors to accumulate over hundreds of sampling steps. While prior work (Ning et al., 2023) addressed this by retraining with input perturbation, this is computationally expensive and sub-optimal. No prior work had systematically studied this training-inference discrepancy or proposed a training-free remedy.

## Input / Output

The Time-Shift Sampler is a training-free modification to the sampling loop of any pre-trained diffusion model, requiring only two additional hyperparameters: a window size w defining the search range for shifted time steps and a cutoff threshold t_c below which shifting is disabled. It takes standard Gaussian noise as input and produces a generated image, with the only computational overhead being a variance computation and a nearest-neighbor lookup at each denoising step (typically <15% additional sampling time). The method is model-agnostic, integrating seamlessly into DDPM, DDIM, PNDM, DPM-Solver, and DEIS samplers without any architectural changes.

## Method

The core insight is that during inference, the network-predicted next state x̂_{t-1} may not optimally align with the nominal time step t-1; an alternate time step t_s within a local window may exhibit superior coupling with the predicted state. Theoretically, minimizing the KL divergence between the predicted state distribution and the forward-process Gaussian at t_s yields an optimal variance σ_{t_s} ≈ σ_{t-1} − ‖e‖²/(d(d−1)), where e is the network prediction error and d is the input dimension. In practice, the algorithm computes the variance of the predicted sample x_{t-1} at each step and selects the time step τ within [t−w/2, t+w/2] whose noise-schedule variance (1−α̅_τ) is closest to this value, then uses τ as the next sampling step. For time steps below the cutoff t_c, where the theoretical assumptions break down and nearby time steps converge to the same distribution, the original time step is retained, making the shift operation selective to the high-noise regime where it matters most.

## Results

The headline result is FID=3.88 on CIFAR-10 with TS-F-PNDM using only 10 sampling steps, a 44.49% improvement over F-PNDM (6.99) and better than vanilla DDIM with 100 steps (4.23). Across all sampling methods (DDIM, DDPM, S-PNDM, F-PNDM) and both time-selection schemes (uniform and quadratic), the Time-Shift Sampler consistently reduces FID by 7–46% on CIFAR-10 and CelebA. Improvements are most pronounced at low step counts (5–20 steps), where uniform time selection yields gains of 20–48%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TS-DDIM outperforms DDIM (Song et al., 2021) across all step counts on both CIFAR-10 and CelebA, with the largest gains at 10 steps under uniform selection (18.71→12.21 on CIFAR-10, 17.18→10.61 on CelebA). TS-F-PNDM achieves FID=3.88 at 10 steps on CIFAR-10, a 44.49% improvement over F-PNDM (Liu et al., 2022a) and surpassing DDIM-100 (4.23). Against the training-required method ADM-IP (Ning et al., 2023), TS-DDIM with ADM backbone achieves FID=10.73 at 10 steps versus ADM-IP's 20.95, while requiring no retraining and matching sampling time. TS-DDIM also outperforms Analytic-DPM (Bao et al., 2022b) at 10 steps on both CIFAR-10 (11.93 vs. 14.00) and CelebA (9.36 vs. 15.62). For high-order solvers, TS-DPM-solver-2 (Lu et al., 2022) and TS-DEIS (Zhang & Chen, 2023) show more modest but consistent gains (4–12%) over their unshifted counterparts, likely because these solvers already exploit the semi-linear ODE structure to reduce per-step error.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the exposure bias framework from autoregressive sequence models (Ranzato et al., 2016; Schmidt, 2019) to diffusion probabilistic models, where prior work (Ning et al., 2023) addressed it exclusively through training-time input perturbation requiring costly retraining. The Time-Shift Sampler departs from this by operating entirely at inference time, building on the DDPM/DDIM/PNDM sampling pipeline and the KL-divergence-based variance analysis from Analytic-DPM (Bao et al., 2022b). The theoretical contribution (Theorem 3.1) derives the optimal shifted time step by minimizing KL divergence between the predicted state and the forward-process marginal, connecting the exposure bias problem to a concrete variance-matching criterion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-in nature of the Time-Shift Sampler makes it immediately applicable to any existing diffusion sampling pipeline without retraining, which is a significant practical advantage over ADM-IP. The consistent FID improvements across DDPM, DDIM, S-PNDM, F-PNDM, DPM-Solver, and DEIS on multiple datasets demonstrate broad applicability. However, the method introduces two hyperparameters (window size and cutoff value) that require dataset- and step-count-dependent tuning, and the authors acknowledge that analytical derivation of optimal values remains open. Future work could explore whether the time-shift concept generalizes to other Markovian generative processes or to latent diffusion models at scale.
