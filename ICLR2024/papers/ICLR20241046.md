## Problem

In Byzantine-robust federated learning with non-IID data, existing state-of-the-art methods like Centered Clipping achieve a minimax optimal error of O(δζ_mean²) under mean gradient heterogeneity, but no algorithm was known to be minimax optimal under the stronger uniform gradient heterogeneity condition, and no prior method was adaptive to the Byzantine fraction δ. This paper asks whether a simpler, δ-adaptive algorithm can achieve a better O(δ²ζ_max²) rate on the uniform heterogeneity class C_UH(ζ_max), and whether this rate is in fact minimax optimal.

## Input / Output

Each worker computes a local minibatch stochastic gradient and updates its own momentum vector m_i^t = (1−α)m_i^{t−1} + αg_i^t, then sends this d-dimensional momentum to the central server. The server applies a screening test with radius τ_t (set to Θ(ζ_max) + a decaying stochastic noise term, independent of δ) to remove anomalous vectors, then averages the survivors to produce the aggregated momentum used for the global parameter update x^t = x^{t−1} − ηm^t. The final output is x̂^T sampled uniformly from the iterate sequence, targeting ‖∇f(x̂^T)‖² ≤ ε.

## Method

Momentum Screening (MS) combines standard momentum SGD with a single geometric screening test applied once per round to the momentum vectors: a vector m_i is rejected if the d-dimensional sphere of radius τ_t centered at m_i contains fewer than n/2 of all received vectors, and the aggregation is the simple average of the survivors. The key insight is that under ζ_max-uniform gradient heterogeneity, all honest workers' momenta lie within a ball of diameter O(ζ_max) (Proposition 3), so setting τ_t = Θ(ζ_max) + Õ(stochastic noise) guarantees no honest worker is ever screened out while bounding the number of undetected Byzantine vectors, yielding an aggregation error of O(δ²τ_t²) = O(δ²ζ_max²) (Proposition 2). This quadratic dependence on δ (versus the linear δ in CClip) makes MS minimax optimal for C_UH(ζ_max) and strictly better than O(δζ_mean²) whenever δ ≤ (ζ_mean/ζ_max)². All hyperparameters (η, α, τ_t) are independent of δ, making the algorithm adaptive to the Byzantine fraction without prior knowledge of it.

## Results

On the worst-case best test accuracy across five Byzantine attacks (δ=3/20), Momentum Screening (MS) achieves 94.5% on FC/MNIST, 99.0% on VGG11/MNIST, 44.6% on FC/CIFAR10, and 82.8% on VGG11/CIFAR10. The most dramatic improvement is on VGG11/CIFAR10, where MS maintains 82.8% worst-case accuracy while CClip drops to 22.7% and simple averaging to 19.2%. In the varied Byzantine fraction experiment (Figure 1), MS consistently outperforms CClip by a large margin, particularly for small δ, consistent with the theoretical δ² vs δ dependence on the Byzantine fraction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MS outperforms all four baselines on every model/dataset combination in the worst-case test accuracy column. Against CClip (Karimireddy et al., 2022), the most direct comparator, MS achieves 82.8% vs 22.7% on VGG11/CIFAR10 (a 60-point gap), 99.0% vs 95.3% on VGG11/MNIST, 44.6% vs 35.4% on FC/CIFAR10, and 94.5% vs 93.2% on FC/MNIST. Against KRUM with bucketing (Blanchard et al., 2017), MS leads by 42.2 points on VGG11/CIFAR10 (82.8 vs 40.6) and 10.8 points on FC/CIFAR10 (44.6 vs 33.7). Against Coordinate Median with bucketing (Yin et al., 2018), MS leads by 63.7 points on VGG11/CIFAR10 (82.8 vs 19.1). Against RFA with bucketing (Pillutla et al., 2022), MS leads by 62.5 points on VGG11/CIFAR10 (82.8 vs 20.3). The advantage over CClip grows with smaller Byzantine fractions, consistent with the theoretical δ² vs δ rate, and MS's hyperparameters are adaptive to δ while CClip's are not.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the screening idea from Alistarh et al. (2018) and the momentum-based robust aggregation framework of Centered Clipping (CClip) from Karimireddy et al. (2021, 2022), but departs from both by replacing the clipping mechanism with a simple distance-based screening test applied to momentum vectors. While prior work (Karimireddy et al., 2022) established minimax optimality O(δζ_mean²) for the mean-gradient-heterogeneity class C_MH(ζ_mean), this paper targets the stronger uniform-gradient-heterogeneity class C_UH(ζ_max) and derives the first minimax optimal rate O(δ²ζ_max²) along with a matching algorithm-independent lower bound. The key conceptual departure is that MS is adaptive to the Byzantine fraction δ (no hyperparameters depend on it), unlike CClip or bucketing whose theoretically justified parameters explicitly require knowledge of δ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The quadratic dependence on δ (vs. linear for CClip) means MS provides meaningfully better robustness when the Byzantine fraction is small, which is the more common practical regime. The adaptivity to δ removes a significant practical burden since the true number of Byzantine workers is rarely known in deployment. However, the improvement is conditional on δ ≤ (ζ_mean/ζ_max)², and the paper acknowledges no minimax optimality guarantee for the broader C_MH(ζ_mean) class; when ζ_max/ζ_mean is large (e.g., outlier workers) or δ = Ω(1), CClip may still be preferable. Open questions include constructing a single algorithm that is minimax optimal for both heterogeneity classes simultaneously, and extending MS to multiple local updates to reduce communication cost without degrading the O(δ²ζ_max²) rate.
