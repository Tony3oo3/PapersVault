## Problem

Existing machine unlearning methods for deep models rely on full label supervision for both the forgetting and remaining data to guide information removal and preservation. In practice, labels are often unavailable during unlearning due to privacy constraints, annotation costs, or semi-supervised training settings, making supervised unlearning infeasible. This paper addresses the gap of performing effective unlearning without any supervision information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242119/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained deep model (CNN or ResNet-18) decomposed into a representation extractor and a classifier, along with unlabeled forgetting data and unlabeled remaining data. No labels are required at any stage; the VAEs operate on the extractor's embedding space rather than raw pixels. The output is an adjusted representation extractor with the original classifier kept frozen, optionally followed by a one-epoch supervised repair if partial labels for remaining data become available.

## Method

LAF trains two VAEs (h on the full training set representations, h_f on the forgetting data representations) to model the distribution of the extractor's output space, then fixes them as distributional references. The extractor unlearning loss L_UE minimizes a normalized L2 reconstruction error for remaining data representations (preserving knowledge) while maximizing it for forgetting data representations (erasing knowledge), with normalization preventing catastrophic divergence. A contrastive representation alignment loss L_RA uses cosine similarity to pull post-unlearning representations of remaining data toward their pre-unlearning counterparts while pushing forgetting data representations apart, thereby preserving the frozen classifier's predictive capability without any labels. The two losses are optimized alternately to handle their differing magnitudes, and an optional supervised repair step fine-tunes the full model when partial labels are accessible.

## Results

On the data removal task, LAF+R (with supervised repair) achieves 84.74% Test accuracy on CIFAR-10 and 93.56% on SVHN, closely matching the Retrain golden standard (87.20% and 93.41%) while using no labels during unlearning. The label-agnostic LAF (no supervision at all) reaches 82.22% Test on CIFAR-10 and 92.32% on SVHN, remaining within 2–5% of fully supervised baselines. LAF+R attains the best or second-best result in 34 out of 44 evaluations across all three unlearning tasks (data removal, class removal, noisy label removal), while the strongest supervised baseline SISA achieves best/second-best in only 18 of 44. On ASR (membership inference attack success rate), LAF+R on CIFAR-10 achieves 57.74%, nearly identical to Retrain's 57.48%, indicating effective privacy protection without labels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LAF+R substantially outperforms all five supervised baselines on the harder CIFAR-10 and SVHN datasets in data removal: it achieves 84.74% Test on CIFAR-10 versus SISA (54.30%), Unrolling (61.31%), T-S (77.71%), Boundary (51.23%), and SCRUB (25.18%). SISA is the strongest baseline on ASR for DIGITS (33.78% vs. LAF+R's 49.42%), reflecting its exact-unlearning guarantee, but it sacrifices remaining-data accuracy (66.78% Trainr on CIFAR-10 vs. LAF+R's 79.57%). SCRUB and Boundary collapse on CIFAR-10 (Test < 52%), indicating they fail to scale to deeper architectures. The label-agnostic LAF (no labels) remains competitive: on CIFAR-10 it achieves 82.22% Test and 57.65% ASR, within 2% of Retrain and matching or exceeding NegGrad and Unrolling. In class removal, LAF+R achieves near-zero Testf (0.10% on DIGITS, 0.15% on CIFAR-10) matching Retrain's 0%, while LAF without labels retains 2.15% Testf on CIFAR-10, showing the limitation of fully unsupervised class-level forgetting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the machine unlearning literature (SISA, Unrolling, T-S, SCRUB, Boundary) by removing the universal dependency on label supervision during the unlearning process. It repurposes variational autoencoders (originally for generative modeling) as distribution approximators for representation spaces, and borrows contrastive learning (cosine-similarity-based) to align post-unlearning representations with the original model's space. The key conceptual departure is shifting the unlearning target from the classifier (which requires labels) to the representation extractor, enabling a supervision-free optimization objective built from reconstruction losses of two VAEs trained on remaining and forgetting data representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: in real-world privacy scenarios (GDPR, CCPA), labels for the data to be forgotten or retained may be inaccessible, expensive to re-annotate, or legally restricted, making all existing supervised unlearning methods inapplicable. However, the paper's own results reveal a significant limitation: without labels, LAF cannot fully eliminate a forgotten class (Testf remains 2–25% vs. 0% for retraining), and its overall accuracy is consistently below supervised baselines. Open questions include whether representation-level unlearning can be made label-free for class-level removal, whether the VAE-based distribution approximation scales to larger models/datasets, and whether the efficiency cost of training two VAEs can be amortized across multiple unlearning requests.
