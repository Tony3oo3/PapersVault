## Problem

Prevailing text-guided visual diffusion models employ an unconditional forward process while relying on a text-conditional reverse process, creating an inconsistency that constrains the precise conveyance of textual semantics in generated samples. Additionally, these models neglect the cross-modal context—the interactions and alignments between text and visual modalities—within the diffusion trajectory itself, limiting semantic fidelity in both generation and editing tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240556/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For text-to-image generation, the method takes a free-form text prompt and produces a 256×256 RGB image via a 64×64 base diffusion model followed by a super-resolution stage, trained on LAION-400M. For text-to-video editing, it takes a source video (8–24 frames) plus an edited text prompt and produces a temporally consistent edited video, using Stable Diffusion v1.4 as the base model with DDIM inversion for structural preservation. The context-aware adapter encodes both modalities via CLIP ViT-B/32 and models their interactions with multi-head cross-attention (8 layers for images, plus spatio-temporal attention for video).

## Method

ContextDiff introduces a context-aware trajectory adapter that injects cross-modal context into both the forward and reverse diffusion processes. A relational network r_φ(x_0, c, t) (implemented as cross-attention between CLIP text and image embeddings) produces a bias term k_t·r_φ(x_0, c, t) added to the forward process mean at every timestep, where k_t = √(ᾱ_t)·(1−√(ᾱ_t)) controls magnitude. The reverse process is adapted to match this contextualized forward process: during training, a denoising network predicts x̂_0 from the contextually-noised x_t, and the predicted x̂_0 feeds back into the adapter to compute the sampling trajectory correction. The paper provides full theoretical derivations generalizing this to both DDPMs (with a simplified x_0-prediction objective proven to be an upper bound on NLL) and DDIMs (with a deterministic sampling rule that subtracts the current-step bias and adds the previous-step bias). Theoretically, ContextDiff achieves a tighter variational bound on negative log-likelihood than standard DDPMs, and a toy Gaussian analysis shows the adapter provably reduces estimation error by increasing the effective signal-to-noise ratio in the forward process.

## Results

On MS-COCO text-to-image generation (256×256), ContextDiff achieves a zero-shot FID-30K of 6.48, setting a new state-of-the-art that surpasses Imagen (7.27), eDiff-I (6.95), and ERNIE-ViLG 2.0 (6.75). On text-to-video editing, ContextDiff attains the best CLIP-text score (0.274) and CLIP-temporal consistency (0.970), outperforming Tune-A-Video (0.260/0.934), FateZero (0.252/0.954), and ControlVideo (0.258/0.961). The context-aware adapter also generalizes to class-to-image generation on ImageNet (FID 2.97 vs. LDM's 3.60) and layout-to-image on MS-COCO (FID 34.58 vs. LDM's 40.91), and reduces NLL on CIFAR-10 from 3.01 to 2.63.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ContextDiff achieves the best zero-shot FID-30K (6.48) on MS-COCO, outperforming the closest continuous-diffusion baselines RAPHAEL (6.61) and ERNIE-ViLG 2.0 (6.75) by 0.13 and 0.27 FID points respectively, and substantially beating Imagen (7.27) and DALL-E 2 (10.39). In text-to-video editing, ContextDiff leads all three baselines on both CLIP-text (0.274 vs. 0.260/0.252/0.258) and CLIP-temporal (0.970 vs. 0.934/0.954/0.961) metrics, with the largest margin over Tune-A-Video (0.014 CLIP-text, 0.036 CLIP-temp). For class-to-image on ImageNet, the context-aware adapter improves LDM from FID 3.60 to 2.97 and IS from 247.67 to 273.04, surpassing ADM-G (FID 4.59) and BigGAN (FID 6.95). In layout-to-image generation, LDM+adapter (FID 34.58) beats Frido (37.14), LDM (40.91), and VQGAN+T (56.58). On CIFAR-10 density estimation, the adapter reduces NLL from 3.01 (DDPM) to 2.63, confirming the theoretical advantage of contextualized forward processes.

## Relation to Prior Work

ContextDiff extends the DDPM/DDIM framework by introducing a cross-modal context-aware trajectory adapter that conditions both the forward and reverse diffusion processes, departing from the standard practice of using an unconditional forward process with a text-conditional reverse process. The diffusion backbone follows Imagen's architecture (64×64 base + super-resolution), and the video editing setup builds on Stable Diffusion v1.4 with DDIM inversion, similar to Tune-A-Video and FateZero. Unlike prior trajectory-optimization methods (Rectified Flow, VDM, PriorGrad) that modify the forward process using only unimodal information, ContextDiff is the first to incorporate cross-modal interactions (via CLIP encoders and cross-attention) as a bias term in the diffusion trajectory, providing a theoretically grounded generalization to both DDPMs and DDIMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine theoretical inconsistency in conditional diffusion models—the mismatch between unconditional forward and conditional reverse processes—and shows that resolving it with a lightweight (188M parameter) adapter yields consistent SOTA improvements across text-to-image and text-to-video editing. The theoretical proof that ContextDiff achieves a tighter NLL bound than vanilla DDPMs provides a principled justification beyond empirical gains. Open questions include whether the CLIP-based cross-modal context captures sufficiently fine-grained semantic relationships for compositional generation, whether the adapter's benefits persist at much larger model scales, and whether the approach extends naturally to other condition modalities beyond text (e.g., audio, 3D structure). The practical overhead is modest (0.83 vs 0.79 s/img training, 13.4 vs 13.2 s/img inference over Imagen), making it a low-cost drop-in improvement.
