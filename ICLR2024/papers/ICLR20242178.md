## Problem

Standard NeRF pipelines assume global shutter cameras, but most consumer devices (phones, action cameras) use rolling shutters that capture rows sequentially, introducing motion-dependent distortions. These distortions simultaneously corrupt NeRF training and degrade camera pose estimation (e.g., via COLMAP), while existing RS correction networks require expensive large-scale pre-training and generalize poorly to new scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242178/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of rolling shutter RGB images (e.g., 40–80 frames at 768×480, captured at 30 Hz with known scanline readout times of ~3.7–29.5 μs) plus camera intrinsics as input. It outputs an undistorted NeRF, a continuous-time camera trajectory as a cubic B-Spline in SE(3), and can render global shutter images at arbitrary frame rates or synthesize novel views. Notably, it also handles unordered image sequences provided the mutual readout-direction angles exceed 30°.

## Method

USB-NeRF jointly optimizes NeRF MLP weights and a continuous-time camera motion trajectory parameterized by cubic B-Splines in SE(3), where each row of a rolling shutter image is rendered with its own interpolated pose from the spline (via the De Boor-Cox formula and exponential-map composition of relative twists between control knots). The photometric loss compares rendered RS images—constructed by rendering each row at its timestamp-specific pose—against the captured RS images, making the entire pipeline differentiable and trainable end-to-end without pre-training. Training uses Adam with 200K steps, 7200 random pixels per step, and a coarse-to-fine positional encoding schedule (20K–100K steps) inherited from BARF, with control-knot poses initialized from COLMAP. The key distinction from BARF is replacing one pose per frame with a continuous trajectory that assigns a distinct pose to every scanline, explicitly modeling the RS image formation process.

## Results

On rolling shutter effect removal, USB-NeRF achieves PSNR of 31.90 (Carla), 31.85 (Blue Room), 34.89 (Living Room), and 30.57 (White Room), substantially outperforming the best learning-based baseline (SUNet: 23.32–26.64 PSNR on Unreal-RS) and the closest competitor CVR (31.74 PSNR on Carla). For trajectory estimation, USB-NeRF-cubic achieves ATE of 0.0013 m (Blue Room), 0.0035 m (Living Room), and 0.0136 m (TUM-RS), compared to 0.0640–0.1593 m for COLMAP and 0.0640–0.1210 m for RSBA. On novel view synthesis, USB-NeRF reaches 28.99–33.33 PSNR, exceeding the best baseline (NeRF+DSUN at 18.08–18.96 PSNR) by over 10 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

USB-NeRF outperforms all ten baselines across every dataset and metric. Against learning-based RS correction methods (DSUN, SUNet, RSSR, CVR), USB-NeRF achieves comparable PSNR on Carla-RS (31.90 vs. CVR's 31.74) where those networks were trained, but gains 8–13 dB PSNR on the domain-shifted Unreal-RS datasets (e.g., 31.85 vs. SUNet's 23.32 on Blue Room), demonstrating superior generalization without pre-training. Against learning-free DiffSfM, USB-NeRF leads by 7–17 dB PSNR. Against NeRF and BARF (which ignore the rolling shutter model), USB-NeRF improves PSNR by 11–16 dB, confirming that explicit RS modeling is essential. For trajectory estimation (ATE), USB-NeRF-cubic achieves 0.0013–0.0136 m on synthetic/real sequences, beating COLMAP (0.0486–0.1931 m), RSBA (0.0640–0.1210 m), and NW-RSBA (0.1374 m on TUM-RS) by 3–15×, with NW-RSBA failing entirely on the more complex Unreal-RS sequences.

## Relation to Prior Work

USB-NeRF sits at the intersection of three research threads: NeRF-based 3D scene representation (Mildenhall et al., 2020), joint pose-and-scene optimization via bundle adjustment (BARF, Lin et al., 2021), and continuous-time SE(3) trajectory parameterization for rolling shutter cameras (Spline Fusion, Lovegrove et al., 2013). The key departure from prior NeRF variants (NeRF++, GNeRF, BARF, L2G-NeRF) is that they all assume a global shutter model where each image has a single pose, whereas USB-NeRF models the per-row pose variation inherent to rolling shutter capture. Unlike learning-based RS correction methods (DSUN, SUNet, RSSR, CVR) that require large pre-training datasets and suffer from poor generalization, USB-NeRF formulates the RS image formation process directly within the differentiable rendering pipeline, requiring no pre-training. It also subsumes prior RS bundle adjustment methods (RSBA, NW-RSBA) by jointly recovering the trajectory and the full 3D scene rather than sparse geometry alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since the vast majority of consumer cameras (smartphones, action cameras) use rolling shutters, this work addresses a practical gap that limits the applicability of NeRF to real-world video. The simultaneous recovery of undistorted 3D scenes, accurate camera trajectories, and high-frame-rate global shutter video from a single RS sequence is a unified solution that avoids the error propagation of cascaded pipelines (RS correction → SfM → NeRF). Open questions include scalability to longer sequences (the paper uses subsets of TUM-RS), handling dynamic scenes with RS distortions, and whether the per-row rendering cost limits real-time applications. The method also assumes known camera intrinsics and a pinhole model, which may not hold for wide-angle or fisheye lenses common in action cameras.
