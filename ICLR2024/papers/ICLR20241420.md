## Problem

Existing attention-based MIL models for whole slide image (WSI) classification either treat tiles as independent (permutation invariant) or model inter-tile dependencies only implicitly, ignoring the spatial and contextual relationships that are critical in cancer histopathology. Because tumor cells and their microenvironment are not randomly distributed, a tile's diagnostic value depends heavily on its neighborhood, yet no prior method explicitly enforces this bio-topological context as a structural constraint during attention computation.

## Input / Output

Input WSIs are segmented into 256×256 non-overlapping tiles at ×20 magnification, each encoded to a 1024-dimensional embedding by a SimCLR-fine-tuned ResNet-18. The model outputs a slide-level class probability (e.g., cancerous vs. normal, or LUAD vs. LUSC) and per-tile attention weights that serve as interpretable heatmaps localizing diagnostically significant regions. The attention maps are produced at tile granularity and can be thresholded to generate predicted tumor masks for quantitative localization evaluation (Dice, specificity).

## Method

CAMIL is a five-stage pipeline: (1) tissue segmentation and tiling, (2) self-supervised feature extraction via SimCLR-trained ResNet-18, (3) a Nystromformer module that approximates full self-attention in O(n) to capture global inter-tile dependencies, (4) a neighbor-constrained attention module, and (5) a sigmoid-gated fusion of local and global representations followed by a classification head. The key novelty is the neighbor-constrained attention: a weighted adjacency matrix is constructed over the 8-neighborhood of each tile, with edge weights s_ij = exp(−‖h_i − h_j‖²) computed from the frozen feature embeddings, and this similarity mask is element-wise multiplied with the query–key dot-product attention matrix so that each tile attends only to spatially adjacent, feature-similar neighbors. This injects an explicit bio-topological prior, suppressing attention to distant or dissimilar tiles and recalibrating each tile's score by its neighborhood context. The final slide representation adaptively blends the local neighbor-constrained features with the global Nystromformer features via a sigmoid gate, then applies a learned attention-weighted sum pooling before a linear classifier trained with cross-entropy on slide-level labels.

## Results

CAMIL achieves the best slide-level AUC on CAMELYON16 (95.9%) and TCGA-NSCLC (97.5%), and the best ACC on CAMELYON16 (91.7%) and CAMELYON17 (84.3%). On CAMELYON17, CAMIL's AUC of 88.1% is marginally below DTFD-MIL's 88.4% but still outperforms all other baselines. For localization on CAMELYON16, CAMIL achieves a Dice score of 0.515 and specificity of 0.980, slightly behind DTFD-MIL (Dice 0.525) but well ahead of TransMIL (Dice 0.103) and DSMIL (Dice 0.259).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAMIL outperforms all five baselines on CAMELYON16, achieving at least 0.9% higher AUC and 1.2% higher ACC than the best prior method (TransMIL: 0.950 AUC, 0.905 ACC). On TCGA-NSCLC, CAMIL matches GTP's ACC (0.916) and edges out TransMIL by 0.1% AUC (0.975 vs 0.974), while substantially surpassing DSMIL (0.954 AUC) and DTFD-MIL (0.964 AUC). On CAMELYON17, CAMIL achieves the highest ACC (0.843 vs 0.815 for DSMIL, the next best) but trails DTFD-MIL by 0.3% in AUC (0.881 vs 0.884), the only metric where a baseline edges out CAMIL. CLAM-SB and CLAM-MB are consistently the weakest baselines across all three datasets, with CAMIL exceeding them by 2–4% in AUC. GTP's MinCUT pooling degrades sharply on CAMELYON17 (AUC 0.762), suggesting its complexity-reduction strategy loses fine-grained detail on larger, more heterogeneous datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAMIL extends the attention-based MIL framework (Ilse et al., 2018) by introducing an explicit neighbor-constrained attention mechanism that incorporates bio-topological spatial context, departing from both permutation-invariant models (CLAM) that ignore tile ordering and transformer-based approaches (TransMIL, GTP) that model spatial dependencies only implicitly. The architecture combines a Nystromformer module for global context with a local neighbor-attention module weighted by a similarity-based adjacency matrix, effectively bridging the gap between global and local information in WSI classification. The feature extraction pipeline builds on SimCLR-based self-supervised pretraining (Chen et al., 2020) as used in DSMIL (Li et al., 2021a).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CAMIL addresses a clinically relevant gap: pathologists inherently assess tiles in their tissue context, yet most MIL models treat tiles as independent or model dependencies only implicitly. The ablation studies confirm that both the global (Nystromformer) and local (neighbor-constrained attention) components are necessary, suggesting that explicit spatial priors provide complementary information beyond what transformers capture implicitly. However, the localization performance (Dice score) trails DTFD-MIL, and the Nystromformer introduces a trade-off between slide-level accuracy and fine-grained localization. Open questions include whether the neighbor-constrained attention generalizes to multi-class subtyping beyond binary tasks, whether the fixed 8-neighborhood assumption holds across different tissue architectures, and whether the approach scales to even larger WSIs without the Nystromformer approximation.
