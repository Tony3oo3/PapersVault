## Problem

Near-optimal strongly adaptive regret in the bandit setting was previously shown to be impossible with only one query per round, and all known algorithms achieving O(√I) regret required O(log T) queries. This paper asks whether a constant number of queries suffices, and answers affirmatively: two queries per round are enough for multi-armed bandits and three for bandit convex optimization.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a loss vector (bounded in [0,1] for MAB, or a convex G-Lipschitz function for BCO) and selects an arm (or point in a convex domain K) each round. Unusually, it may query one or two additional arms/points in parallel to the played arm, observing their losses without incurring them; the query choice must be made before any loss information is revealed. The output is the arm/point played plus a theoretical strongly adaptive regret guarantee of Õ(√(nI)) for MAB and Õ(√I) for BCO, uniform over all contiguous intervals I.

## Method

StABL runs O(log T) EXP3 base learners on geometric intervals (powers-of-two lengths) and aggregates them with an EXP3 meta-algorithm. The key mechanism is the second query: instead of uniform exploration, it samples from a carefully constructed distribution P(t) that is the average of (i) a squared-max-weight distribution controlling the meta-learner's variance and (ii) the average of base learners' weight distributions controlling the base learners' variance. This single importance-sampling distribution yields an unbiased loss estimator with bounded variance simultaneously for both the base EXP3 instances and the meta EXP3, which is what makes the two-query scheme work where one query provably cannot. For BCO, a third query provides a small random perturbation to construct a bounded-variance sparse gradient estimator, enabling the same meta-learner framework in the continuous setting.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 1) shows that StABL achieves O(√(nI log n) · log^1.5 T) strongly adaptive regret for n-armed bandits using only 2 queries per round, matching the known Ω(√(nI)) lower bound up to logarithmic factors. For bandit convex optimization (Theorem 3), the method achieves O(dGD√I · log² T) adaptive regret with 3 queries per round, independent of the condition number κ. Empirically, on the BBOB SPHERE function (32-dim, 1000 trials), StABL maintains a consistent performance-profile advantage over EXP3-Uniform throughout optimization, while EXP3-Uniform's advantage over Uniform collapses after ~600 trials.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StABL dominates all prior methods on query efficiency: it requires only 2 queries per round versus O(log T) for FLH and SAOL, and O(log log T / ε) for EFLH, while achieving a tighter leading-term regret bound (√(nI) vs. √(nT) for FLH, and I^(1/2+ε) for EFLH). Compared to SAOL, StABL trades a slightly worse log T dependence (log^1.5 T vs. log T) for a constant query budget, which is the key practical improvement. Against EFLH, StABL achieves the optimal √I dependence (rather than I^(1/2+ε)) at the cost of a larger log T factor, but with a fixed 2 queries rather than a growing number. In the empirical expert-advice experiment, StABL recovers quickly after the best arm changes at each of 4 intervals, while non-adaptive EXP3 degrades sharply; StABL with naive uniform exploration is consistently worse than StABL with the designed importance-sampling distribution, confirming the theoretical variance argument. In the BBOB HPO experiment, StABL tracks EXP3-Uniform's early advantage but maintains it past the ~600-trial turning point where EXP3-Uniform's performance curve abruptly stops, demonstrating superior adaptivity in a real downstream task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the strongly adaptive regret framework of Hazan & Seshadhri (2009) and Daniely et al. (2015), which relied on O(log T) independent base learners and a meta-learner to achieve near-optimal regret over all intervals. The key departure is replacing the standard multiplicative-weights meta-learner with an EXP3-type algorithm and using a single additional query for exploration under a carefully designed importance-sampling distribution, thereby reducing the query complexity from O(log T) (or O(log log T) in Lu & Hazan 2023) to a constant 2 for MAB and 3 for BCO. The geometric interval set decomposition and the two-point gradient estimation from Agarwal et al. (2010) are retained as structural components, but the variance-control mechanism via the mixed observation distribution is the novel technical contribution that bypasses the Ω(log log T) lower bound on the number of base algorithms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides a tight characterization of query efficiency for adaptive regret minimization: two queries are both sufficient and (by the Daniely et al. lower bound) necessary, closing a gap that had persisted since 2015. Practically, this is significant for settings where each arm evaluation is expensive, such as hyperparameter optimization of large models, where reducing queries from O(log T) to 2 directly translates to fewer full model trainings. Open questions include whether the log^{1.5} T dependence can be reduced (the full-information best is √log T), whether BCO can be done with only two additional queries, and how to extend the framework to dynamic regret in the bandit setting where exploiting convexity for the meta-learner remains unresolved.
