## Problem

Fine-tuning text-to-image diffusion models with reward models trained on human feedback is effective for alignment, but excessive optimization against these proxy objectives causes reward overoptimization, degrading both text-image alignment and image fidelity. The paper identifies that even reward models trained on large-scale human preference data (ImageReward, PickScore) frequently misalign with human judgment, and there is no principled mechanism to detect and correct this miscalibration before it compounds during optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240369/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt x₀, a generated image y, and a finite set of semantically contrastive prompts X (syntactically similar but semantically distinct from x₀, e.g., 'a photo of three dogs' for input 'a photo of two dogs'). It produces a calibrated reward score in [0,1] via softmax normalization, which is then used as the optimization signal for fine-tuning Stable Diffusion v2.1 via LoRA. The contrastive prompt set is generated per-prompt using GPT-4 with category-specific few-shot examples targeting known failure modes (counting, composition, spatial relations, text rendering, etc.).

## Method

TextNorm calibrates a reward model's output by computing a softmax over the original prompt's reward and the rewards of a finite set of contrastive prompts, treating the relative comparison as a confidence measure: if contrastive prompts yield similar rewards, the model is uncertain and the score is down-weighted. The contrastive prompts are constructed to be syntactically similar but semantically distinct, leveraging the hypothesis that prompts differing in both syntax and semantics contribute negligibly to the softmax. On top of per-model normalization, the paper proposes two ensemble strategies—simple averaging and an uncertainty-regularized variant that penalizes variance across normalized rewards—to combine ImageReward and PickScore. The calibrated rewards are then used as optimization objectives in three settings: best-of-n sampling, reward-weighted regression (SFT), and DDPO (RL fine-tuning), with checkpoint selection also guided by the calibrated scores.

## Results

On the TIA2 benchmark, TextNorm (an ensemble of ImageReward and PickScore with confidence-calibrated rewards) achieves the best alignment across all three prompt sets. On the comprehensive set (C100), TextNorm reaches 0.788 AUROC and 0.841 AP@5, outperforming the strongest baseline ImageReward (0.761 AUROC, 0.755 AP@5). Gains are most pronounced on the counting set (C150), where TextNorm achieves 0.807 AUROC and 0.877 AP@5 versus PickScore's 0.731 AUROC and 0.711 AP@5. On the composition set (C300), TextNorm attains 0.844 AUROC and 0.944 AP@5, surpassing PickScore (0.785 AUROC, 0.848 AP@5) by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TextNorm outperforms all four baselines on every metric across all three prompt sets. CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) and BLIP-2 (Li et al., 2023), which are pre-trained vision-language models not fine-tuned on human preference data, are consistently the weakest, with AUROC values below 0.72 on all sets and particularly poor performance on counting (0.595 and 0.565 respectively). ImageReward (Xu et al., 2023) is the strongest baseline on the comprehensive set (0.761 AUROC) and composition set (0.774 AUROC), but lags significantly on counting (0.657 AUROC), where TextNorm achieves a 15-point gap. PickScore (Kirstain et al., 2023) is the overall strongest single baseline, especially on counting (0.731 AUROC) and composition (0.785 AUROC), yet TextNorm still surpasses it by 7.6 and 5.9 AUROC points respectively. The largest relative improvement from TextNorm occurs on the counting set, where the confidence-calibrated ensemble nearly doubles the AP@5 of the weakest baselines (0.877 vs. 0.570–0.622), indicating that reward miscalibration is most severe for quantity-related prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reward overoptimization literature (Gao et al., 2023; Black et al., 2023) from language modeling to text-to-image generation, where the proxy-objective problem has received less systematic attention. The TextNorm calibration mechanism repurposes the softmax-based confidence estimation idea from Hendrycks & Gimpel (2017) — originally for out-of-distribution detection — to the novel setting of calibrating text-image reward scores via semantically contrastive prompts. The ensemble component is concurrent with Coste et al. (2023) on reward model ensembles for language modeling, but here it operates over heterogeneous reward models (ImageReward, PickScore) normalized by TextNorm rather than identically trained models. The TIA2 benchmark fills a gap left by prior evaluation efforts (DALL-Eval, VISOR) that focused on object detection or embedding similarity rather than holistic human-judged alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TIA2 benchmark provides the community with a standardized, human-annotated evaluation suite for reward models in text-to-image generation, addressing a practical need as more models are fine-tuned with learned rewards. The finding that even reward models trained on large-scale human preference data (ImageReward, PickScore) exhibit substantial misalignment — particularly on counting and composition prompts — cautions against treating these models as reliable optimization targets. Key limitations include reliance on an external LLM for contrastive prompt generation, evaluation on a single base model (SD v2.1), and the observed trade-off where RL fine-tuning with TextNorm improves alignment at the cost of image quality. Open questions include whether multi-objective reward formulations (alignment + quality) can resolve this trade-off, and whether TextNorm generalizes to other diffusion architectures or reward model families.
