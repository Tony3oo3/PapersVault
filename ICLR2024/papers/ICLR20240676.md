## Problem

Clinical predictive models built on EHR data struggle to incorporate medical knowledge for personalized predictions because generating patient-specific knowledge graphs from raw EHR records is difficult. Existing graph-based approaches (e.g., GRAM, MIMIC) rely on simple hierarchical parent-child relations and do not link local EHR graphs to comprehensive external biomedical knowledge bases, leaving rich relational information among medical entities untapped.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240676/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes patient EHR visit sequences where medical concepts are mapped from ICD-9/10 codes to 285 CCS condition codes, 231 CCS procedure codes, and from NDC codes to 269 ATC level-3 drug codes. These structured codes are expanded into personalized temporal knowledge graphs by merging concept-specific KGs (sourced from GPT-4 prompting and UMLS subgraph sampling) with inter-visit connectivity. Predictions are produced via three patient representations—graph embedding (mean-pooled over all nodes), node embedding (mean-pooled over direct EHR nodes only), and a joint concatenation—fed through an MLP to yield binary (mortality, readmission), multi-class (LOS, 10 bins), or multi-label (drug) outputs.

## Method

GRAPHCARE operates in three stages. First, concept-specific KGs are generated for every medical concept by (a) prompting GPT-4 with a template to extract up to 100 entity-relation-entity triples per run (χ=3 runs aggregated) and (b) sampling κ-hop subgraphs from UMLS, followed by agglomerative clustering of nodes and edges using cosine similarity of GPT-3 word embeddings (threshold δ=0.15) to reduce redundancy. Second, a personalized KG is composed per patient by merging the clustered concept-specific KGs of all their EHR codes, adding a patient node connected to direct EHR nodes, and introducing inter-visit edges that link nodes appearing in different visits if they share a relation in the global graph. Third, a Bi-attention Augmented (BAT) GNN processes the temporal graph: it computes node-level attention (α) over a multi-hot visit-subgraph vector and visit-level attention (β) with an exponential decay coefficient λ_j=exp(−γ(J−j)) that up-weights recent visits, then performs convolution by aggregating both attention-weighted node embeddings and edge-weighted relation embeddings across all visit-subgraphs. Attention initialization uses cosine similarity between node embeddings and task-specific keyword embeddings (e.g., 'terminal condition' for mortality) to seed W_α as a diagonal matrix, guiding the model toward clinically relevant nodes from the start.

## Results

On MIMIC-III, GRAPHCARE with the proposed BAT GNN achieves AUROC of 70.3% (mortality) and 69.7% (readmission), F1 of 37.5% (LOS) and 66.8% (drug recommendation), improving over the average of all baselines by 17.6%, 6.6%, 7.9%, and 10.8% respectively. On the larger MIMIC-IV dataset, it reaches AUROC of 73.1% (mortality) and 68.5% (readmission), F1 of 34.2% (LOS) and 63.9% (drug), with improvements of 4.1%, 2.1%, 3.5%, and 1.8% over baselines. GRAPHCARE also demonstrates strong data efficiency: trained on only 0.1% of MIMIC-III (36 patients), it matches the LOS accuracy of StageNet trained on 2.0% of the data (~720 patients).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRAPHCARE w/ BAT outperforms all 12 EHR prediction baselines (GRU, Transformer, RETAIN, GRAM, Deepr, StageNet, AdaCare, GRASP, SafeDrug, MICRON, GAMENet, MoleRec) and 4 alternative GNN backbones (GAT, GINE, EGT, GPS) across all four tasks on both datasets. The largest gains appear on MIMIC-III, where the 17.6% AUROC improvement on mortality and 10.8% F1 improvement on drug recommendation reflect the benefit of richer external knowledge when the EHR dataset is smaller. On MIMIC-IV, improvements are more modest (4.1% AUROC for mortality, 1.8% F1 for drug), consistent with the larger dataset providing more signal from EHR alone. Among graph-based baselines, GRAM and GAMENet show the most resilience under data scarcity (due to their use of internal EHR graphs or external ontologies), but GRAPHCARE still leads by a clear margin. The BAT GNN itself outperforms GAT, GINE, EGT, and GPS when used as the backbone, with ablation showing that node-level attention (α), visit-level attention (β), edge weights, and attention initialization each contribute 0.3–2.8 points of performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of EHR prediction work that incorporates knowledge graphs (GRAM, MIMIC, GCT) by departing from their reliance on simple hierarchical parent-child relations toward rich, multi-relational personalized KGs. It repurposes LLMs (GPT-4) as open-world knowledge extractors—a concept explored in NLP knowledge-base completion (Petroni et al., Wang et al.)—and combines LLM-generated triples with subgraph sampling from established biomedical KGs (UMLS), a combination not previously applied to patient-specific graph construction. The BAT GNN introduces a bi-attention mechanism (visit-level decay-weighted attention plus node-level attention with task-specific initialization) that goes beyond standard GAT-style node attention used in prior healthcare GNNs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the data-efficiency result: GRAPHCARE trained on 0.1% of patient records matches baselines trained on 20× more data, which is compelling for settings with limited EHR availability (new hospitals, rare conditions). The finding that GPT-KG and UMLS-KG have low triple overlap and complement each other suggests a general principle for combining parametric and symbolic knowledge sources. Open questions include how to improve subgraph sampling from large KGs (2-hop sampling actually degrades performance), whether task-specific KG construction can further narrow the knowledge to what matters for a given prediction, and whether the framework generalizes beyond MIMIC to other EHR systems with different coding conventions.
