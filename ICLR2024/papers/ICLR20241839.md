## Problem

Prior work established that a tensor spectral estimator (best rank-one approximation) outperforms a matrix unfolding approach for multi-view clustering under the nested matrix-tensor model, but the precise performance gap between these two methods was unknown. The tensor approach is computationally NP-hard, making the tractable unfolding alternative practically important, yet its detection threshold and recovery quality had not been rigorously characterized. This paper fills that gap by deriving the exact algorithmic threshold and BBP-type phase transition for the unfolding method.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a 3D tensor X ∈ R^{p×n×m} generated from the nested matrix-tensor model X = (μy^T + Z) ⊗ h + W, where p is the feature dimension, n is the number of data points, and m is the number of views, with all dimensions growing proportionally. The output is a unit-norm vector ŷ ∈ R^n whose entries approximate the true class labels ±1/√n, from which cluster assignments are read off by sign. The clustering accuracy is characterized asymptotically as Φ(√(ζ/(1−ζ))), where ζ is the alignment between the estimated and true label vectors.

## Method

The paper deploys random matrix theory tools—Stein's lemma, deterministic equivalents of resolvents, and Stieltjes transform analysis—to derive the limiting spectral distribution of the matrix unfoldings T^(2)T^(2)⊤ and T^(3)T^(3)⊤ of the nested tensor. A key technical step is a centering-and-rescaling of the Gram matrix to handle the divergent spectrum caused by the rectangular (n₂ × n₁n₃) shape of the unfolding, yielding a cubic polynomial equation for the Stieltjes transform whose coefficients depend on the rescaled SNR ρ_T = β_T²n_T/√(n₁n₂n₃). The spike eigenvalue location and eigenvector alignment ζ are then extracted via residue calculus, revealing a BBP-type phase transition at a threshold ρ_T that depends on β_M and the aspect ratios c_ℓ. The analysis shows the unfolding method requires β_T = Θ(n_T^{1/4}) for non-trivial recovery, in contrast to β_T = Θ(1) for the tensor spectral estimator, and a weighted-mean estimator (when the third-mode vector is known) provides an optimal upper bound on achievable performance.

## Results

The paper derives that the unfolding (matrix SVD) approach exhibits a BBP-type phase transition requiring β_T = Θ(n_T^{1/4}) for non-trivial signal recovery, whereas the tensor rank-one approach operates in the non-trivial regime at β_T = Θ(1). The clustering accuracy of the unfolding estimator converges almost surely to Φ(√(ζ/(1−ζ))), where ζ is an explicit function of the SNR parameters ρ_T and β_M (Theorem 5). In the multi-view clustering experiment with (p,n,m) = (150, 300, 60), the tensor approach achieves near-optimal accuracy (nearly coinciding with the Theorem 4 upper bound for ‖h‖ = 1.5), while the unfolding approach shows a later phase transition and lower peak accuracy. The performance gap between the two methods narrows as ‖h‖ increases from 0.5 to 1.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the unfolding (matrix SVD) approach against the tensor rank-one approximation method of Seddik et al. (2023a) on the nested matrix-tensor model for multi-view clustering. The tensor approach consistently outperforms the unfolding approach: it has an earlier phase transition (operating at Θ(1) SNR versus Θ(n_T^{1/4}) for the unfolding) and achieves higher peak clustering accuracy. For ‖h‖ = 1.5, the tensor approach nearly matches the theoretical upper bound (Theorem 4, assuming z is known), while the unfolding approach remains noticeably below it. For ‖h‖ = 0.5, both methods show a visible gap to the upper bound, but the tensor approach still leads. The practical trade-off is that the tensor approach requires solving an NP-hard rank-one tensor approximation, whereas the unfolding approach is a standard SVD and is computationally tractable; the authors suggest using the unfolding estimate as an initialization for tensor power iteration in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the nested matrix-tensor model of Seddik et al. (2023a), which analyzed the tensor spectral estimator (best rank-one approximation) for multi-view clustering, by providing the first rigorous random matrix theory analysis of the tractable matrix unfolding (SVD) alternative. It generalizes the classical spiked rank-one tensor model of Montanari & Richard (2014) through a nested structure where a shared low-rank matrix is observed across multiple views with unknown rescalings. The analysis deploys Stieltjes transforms, deterministic equivalents, and Stein's lemma in the spirit of recent random tensor theory (Goulart et al., 2022; Seddik et al., 2022), but applied to the matrix unfoldings rather than the full tensor, revealing a BBP-type phase transition at a different SNR scaling than the tensor approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first precise theoretical quantification of the performance gap between tensor and matrix spectral methods in multi-view clustering, showing the tensor approach operates at Θ(1) SNR while the unfolding approach requires Θ(n_T^{1/4}) SNR. However, since the tensor approach is NP-hard with no known efficient algorithm achieving its theoretical threshold, the practical gap is narrower than the theory suggests; for moderately easy problems, the cheaper unfolding method performs comparably. The model's restriction to linear rescaling of views (f_k(M) = h_k M) limits generality, and extending the analysis to arbitrary view transformations remains an open question that would significantly broaden applicability.
