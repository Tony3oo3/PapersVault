## Problem

Entropy is the standard confidence metric for selecting trustworthy samples during test-time adaptation, but the paper shows it fails under spurious correlation shifts: even extremely low-entropy samples can be harmful to adaptation when the model's prediction relies on train-time-only correlated (TRAP) factors rather than genuinely label-relevant (CPR) factors. The paper provides a theoretical proposition characterizing harmful samples via disentangled latent factors and demonstrates empirically that entropy-based selection degrades performance on benchmarks with strong spurious correlations.

## Input / Output

DeYO takes a single RGB image (224×224 for ImageNet-based benchmarks) as input and produces a class label prediction along with updated normalization-layer parameters (BN, GN, or LN depending on the backbone). Unusually, for each input image the method also generates a patch-shuffled variant (4×4 grid by default) used solely to compute the PLPD score via one additional forward pass—no extra backpropagation is required. The method operates fully online, processing one test sample at a time and updating only the normalization parameters before moving to the next sample.

## Method

DeYO introduces Pseudo-Label Probability Difference (PLPD), a confidence metric that quantifies how much the pseudo-label's predicted probability drops after applying a patch-shuffling transformation that destroys object shape (a key CPR factor). The method combines two mechanisms: (1) sample selection, which retains only samples whose entropy falls below a threshold τ_Ent AND whose PLPD exceeds a threshold τ_PLPD, and (2) sample weighting, which assigns higher loss weights to samples with both low entropy and high PLPD via a sum of two exponential terms. The theoretical analysis (Proposition 1) shows that a sample is harmful for entropy-minimization adaptation when its latent factor vector aligns more with TRAP factors than CPR factors relative to the class-mean difference, and PLPD directly measures the CPR-factor contribution that entropy alone cannot capture. Patch-shuffling was chosen over pixel-shuffling and center occlusion because it selectively removes shape information while preserving other image components.

## Results

On ImageNet-C (severity 5, mild scenario), DeYO achieves 48.6% average accuracy, outperforming EATA (47.8%), SAR (43.9%), and Tent (43.1%) across all 15 corruption types. On the biased ColoredMNIST benchmark, DeYO reaches 67.39% worst-group accuracy—the only method to exceed random guessing (50%)—compared to 17.98% for EATA and 9.80% for Tent. On Waterbirds, DeYO achieves 73.92% worst-group accuracy, surpassing the pre-adaptation model (64.90%) and the next-best SENTRY (60.90%). In wild scenarios (mixed shifts, label shifts, batch size 1), DeYO consistently leads, e.g., 59.4% vs 57.1% (SAR) on mixed shifts with ViT-Base-LN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeYO outperforms all five baselines across every evaluated benchmark. Against Tent (Wang et al., 2021a), DeYO gains +5.5% on ImageNet-C average accuracy (48.6% vs 43.1%) and a dramatic +57.6% on ColoredMNIST worst-group accuracy (67.39% vs 9.80%). Against EATA (Niu et al., 2022), the closest competitor on ImageNet-C, DeYO leads by +0.8% average (48.6% vs 47.8%) but pulls far ahead under spurious correlation shifts: +49.4% on ColoredMNIST worst-group (67.39% vs 17.98%) and +21.5% on Waterbirds worst-group (73.92% vs 52.38%). Against SAR (Niu et al., 2023), DeYO improves ImageNet-C average by +4.7% and ColoredMNIST worst-group by +55.0%. Against MEMO (Zhang et al., 2022), DeYO leads by +24.7% on ImageNet-C average and +61.2% on ColoredMNIST worst-group. Against SENTRY (Prabhu et al., 2021), DeYO surpasses it by +13.0% on Waterbirds worst-group (73.92% vs 60.90%) and +51.6% on ColoredMNIST worst-group. Notably, on Waterbirds, all baselines except SENTRY perform *worse* than the pre-adaptation model on worst-group accuracy, while DeYO improves it by +9.0%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the entropy-based sample selection paradigm in test-time adaptation (Tent, EATA, SAR) by introducing PLPD, a shape-aware confidence metric that measures how much a prediction depends on object shape via a patch-shuffling transformation. Conceptually, it draws on the disentangled-factors framework of Wiles et al. (2022) to formalize why entropy alone cannot distinguish CPR (commonly positively correlated) from TRAP (train-time only positively correlated) factors, and on the consistency-under-augmentations literature (SENTRY, FixMatch) for the idea that transformation-induced prediction changes signal reliability. The key departure from prior TTA work is the theoretical proposition that low-entropy samples can actively reduce class discriminability, motivating a dual-criterion (entropy + PLPD) selection and weighting scheme rather than entropy alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that entropy-based filtering can degrade performance below the pre-adaptation model (especially under spurious correlation shifts) challenges a foundational assumption in TTA and UDA, prompting practitioners to reconsider confidence metrics beyond softmax probability. DeYO's practical simplicity—a single additional forward pass with patch-shuffling—makes it easy to adopt, and it is the first TTA method to exceed random guessing on ColoredMNIST worst-group accuracy. Open questions include whether the linear-classifier theoretical analysis extends faithfully to deep networks, whether PLPD generalizes beyond shape to other CPR factors (e.g., texture, spatial layout), and whether the approach transfers to non-visual modalities such as audio or text.
