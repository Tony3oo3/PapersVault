## Problem

Existing dense matching networks rely on either feature aggregation (aligning semantically similar descriptors) or cost aggregation (enforcing spatial coherence in flow estimates), but these two processes encode fundamentally different information and have not been jointly exploited. The paper demonstrates that features preserve spatial structure while cost volumes highlight matching similarity, and that their separate use leaves substantial performance gains unrealized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241117/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images (source and target) at 512×512 resolution and produces a dense correspondence flow field defined at every pixel. At inference, a dense zoom-in strategy partitions the coarsely aligned image pair into k×k local windows at multiple scales, computes per-pixel confidence scores via cycle consistency, and selects the highest-confidence flow through transitive composition with the coarse prediction.

## Method

UFC unifies feature and cost aggregation through two interleaved attention layers. The integrative self-attention concatenates feature descriptors D and cost volume C into a joint token, computes a single attention map from QK^T, but applies separate value projections V_D and V_C so that one attention distribution simultaneously aggregates both features and cost volume. The cross-attention layer then uses the aggregated cost volume (after a 1×1 convolution) directly as the attention map—equivalent to a sharpened matching distribution—to condition features on both images. These layers are stacked N times and processed in a coarse-to-fine pyramid where upsampled coarser features and cost volumes are added to the next level, progressively refining correspondences. At inference, multi-scale dense zoom-in with confidence-based selection further improves fine-grained accuracy.

## Results

On SPair-71k (semantic matching), UFC achieves 64.4% PCK at α_bbox=0.1, outperforming the strongest baselines CATs++ (59.8%), VAT (55.5%), and SCorrSAN (55.3%). On PF-PASCAL, UFC reaches 97.9% PCK at α_img=0.1, surpassing CATs++ (96.8%) and VAT (96.2%). For geometric matching on HPatches, UFC attains 7.88 AEPE (lower is better) and 89.36% PCK, while on ETH3D it achieves 2.24 average disparity error, marginally outperforming PDC-Net+(MS) (2.27) and substantially beating PDCNet (MS) (2.38) and GMFlow (3.96).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UFC outperforms all dense matching baselines on SPair-71k at every PCK threshold, with the largest gap at α_bbox=0.01 (8.40 vs. 4.31 for CATs++, a 4.09-point margin) and a consistent 4.6-point lead over CATs++ at α_bbox=0.1 (64.4 vs. 59.8). On PF-PASCAL, UFC leads CATs++ by 1.1 points at α_img=0.1 (97.9 vs. 96.8) and matches CATs++ at α_img=0.15 (81.2). For geometric matching on HPatches, UFC (7.88 AEPE) substantially outperforms dense baselines PDCNet (MS) (15.14), GLU-Net (25.05), and GMFlow (42.85), though sparse methods like ECO-TR (2.52) and PUMP (2.87) report lower AEPE due to their quasi-dense evaluation protocol. On ETH3D, UFC (2.24 avg.) edges out PDC-Net+(MS) (2.27) and PDCNet (MS) (2.38) while significantly surpassing GMFlow (3.96) and DMP (6.62), confirming strong generalization to geometric deformations. The paper notes that VAT and NeMF report slightly higher SPair-71k scores at α_bbox=0.15 but evaluate at higher input resolution (512×512) or specialize in fine-grained correspondences, making direct comparison less straightforward.

## Relation to Prior Work

UFC unifies two previously separate lines of work in dense matching: feature aggregation via self/cross-attention (LOFTR, COTR, GMFlow) and cost aggregation via attention or 4D convolutions (CATs, CATs++, VAT, NC-Net). The key conceptual departure is treating these not as sequential pipeline stages but as mutually reinforcing processes within a single attention mechanism, where features disambiguate the noisy cost volume and the cost volume sharpens feature alignment. The dense zoom-in inference strategy borrows the coarse-alignment idea from RANSAC-Flow (Shen et al., 2020) but replaces homography estimation with direct flow warping, and adopts COTR's cycle-consistency confidence score for multi-scale decision-making.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear empirical and visual argument that feature and cost aggregation encode fundamentally different information (semantic/spatial structure vs. pairwise matching similarity), and that their joint integrative treatment yields substantially better correspondences than either alone or naive sequential application. This reframing may influence how future dense matching architectures are designed, moving beyond the binary choice of 'attention on features' vs. 'attention on cost volume.' Limitations include a speed-accuracy tradeoff in the number of attention layers and pyramid levels, and the dense zoom-in inference brings only marginal gains on semantic matching due to sparse keypoint evaluation protocols. Open questions include whether the integrative formulation extends naturally to 3D correspondence or video matching, and how it scales to very high-resolution inputs without the zoom-in overhead.
