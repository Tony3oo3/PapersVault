## Problem

Structured pruning retains neurons important for the training distribution, causing pruned models to lose robustness to unseen input variations (corruptions, distribution shifts) disproportionately relative to their clean accuracy. Existing robustness work targets dense networks, and prior robust pruning methods assume access to the specific input perturbations at training time. The paper asks whether sparsity and robustness to *unseen* OOD inputs can be jointly optimized without requiring those variations during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241817/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained dense CNN (ResNet50, MobileNet V1/V2 for classification; SSD512 with ResNet50 backbone for detection) and outputs a sparsified model with improved robustness ratios. For classification, the pruned model maps RGB images to class labels; for detection, it maps RGB images to bounding boxes. The key output metric is the robustness ratio R_C = acc_C / acc_val and R_V2 = acc_V2 / acc_val, measuring how much the gap between clean and OOD performance closes after pruning. The method is agnostic to the pruning criterion and can be applied at sparsity ratios from ~20% to 80%.

## Method

AdaSAP is a three-step optimization paradigm that unifies pruning and robustness through the lens of loss-landscape sharpness. In the warmup phase, it applies *adaptive* weight perturbations where each neuron's perturbation ball radius ρ_i is inversely proportional to its importance score (Eq. 2), so unimportant neurons are pushed into flatter regions before they are removed. The optimal perturbation is derived via a first-order Taylor expansion of a per-neuron min-max objective (Eqs. 3–5), yielding a gradient update evaluated at w_i + ε̂_i. In the pruning phase, any structured channel-pruning criterion (magnitude, Taylor, HALP) removes the lowest-scoring neurons, incurring minimal loss because those neurons already sit in flat regions. In the final fine-tuning phase, *uniform* sharpness-aware optimization (ASAM) is applied across the entire network to further flatten the loss landscape and encourage robustness. The core insight is that pruning is a special case of directional robustness (zeroing weights), so flatness simultaneously mitigates pruning-induced performance loss and improves OOD generalization.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with ResNet50, AdaSAP_P achieves 78.29% top-1 validation accuracy at 77% model size, outperforming the dense model (77.32%) while maintaining a robustness ratio R_C of 0.55 (matching dense) and R_V2 of 0.84. On ImageNet-C, AdaSAP_P reaches 43.22% at 77% size versus 40.73% for magnitude pruning and 39.68% for Taylor pruning at the same sparsity. For latency-constrained pruning (Table 2), AdaSAP_L achieves 77.93% validation accuracy at 1.1x speedup with R_C = 0.55, surpassing HALP (77.45%, R_C = 0.53) and SMCP (77.57%, R_C = 0.53). On object detection (Pascal VOC), AdaSAP improves mAP from 0.774 to 0.795 and corrupted mAP from 0.583 to 0.620 at 40% model size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaSAP_P consistently outperforms all parameter-based pruning baselines across every sparsity level tested. Against Taylor pruning (Molchanov et al., 2019), AdaSAP_P gains +1.07% Val and +3.37% IN-C at 20% size (74.63 vs 73.56; 37.30 vs 33.93), and +1.24% Val and +3.54% IN-C at 77% size (78.29 vs 77.05; 43.22 vs 39.68). Against magnitude pruning (Han et al., 2015), the gap is +0.83% Val and +2.07% IN-C at 20% size, widening to +0.97% Val and +2.49% IN-C at 77% size. Against Greg (Wang et al., 2020) and ABCPruner (Lin et al., 2020), AdaSAP_P leads by 2.8–3.1% Val and 5.8–6.3% IN-C at the 62% size setting. For latency-based pruning, AdaSAP_L outperforms HALP (Shen et al., 2022) by +0.91% Val and +2.90% IN-C at 2.6x speedup, and outperforms SMCP (Humble et al., 2022) by +0.66% Val and +2.91% IN-C at 1.6x speedup. In the ablation comparing against SAM (Foret et al., 2020), AdaSAP_P achieves +0.76% Val and +1.34% IN-C over Taylor+SAM at 20% size, confirming that adaptive (non-uniform) perturbations are critical. On MobileNet-V1/V2, AdaSAP_P outperforms PolarReg (Zhuang et al., 2020) by +0.62% Val and +0.76% IN-C at 88% size, and AdaSAP_L outperforms MetaPruning (Liu et al., 2019), AutoSlim (Yu & Huang, 2019), and AMC (He et al., 2018) on validation accuracy at comparable speedups.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaSAP extends the Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) and its scale-invariant variant ASAM by replacing uniform perturbation balls with importance-adaptive ones, where neurons likely to be pruned receive larger perturbation regions to be pushed into flatter minima before removal. Conceptually, the paper reframes structured pruning as a special case of directional robustness (zeroing out weights and activations), thereby unifying the goals of compression and OOD robustness through a shared sharpness-based optimization objective. This departs from prior robust pruning methods (Sehwag et al., Vemparala et al.) that assume access to corruption types at training time, and from prior SAM-based compression work (Na et al., Zhou et al.) that applies uniform perturbations without per-neuron adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, plug-in optimization paradigm that can be combined with any structured pruning criterion to simultaneously improve clean accuracy and OOD robustness, addressing what the authors identify as an open problem at the intersection of compression and generalization. The 2× training cost from dual backward passes is a notable practical limitation, and the evaluation relies on ImageNet-C-style corruptions that may not fully capture real-world deployment challenges. Open questions include whether the adaptive perturbation strategy transfers to unstructured pruning, training from scratch, and more diverse corruption distributions beyond the benchmarked sets.
