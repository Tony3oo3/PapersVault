## Problem

Moderate-sized decoder-only LLMs (7B–13B parameters) lag significantly behind supervised encoder-decoder translation models like NLLB-54B, with prior fine-tuning efforts yielding only 3–4 BLEU/COMET improvements over zero-shot baselines. The paper identifies that the root cause is an unsuitable training recipe: applying the conventional paradigm of massive parallel data to LLMs actually dilutes their pre-existing multilingual knowledge through catastrophic forgetting, rather than improving translation quality.

## Input / Output

The model receives a source sentence preceded by a fixed English prompt template specifying the source and target languages (e.g., 'Translate this from German to English:'), and generates the target-language translation autoregressively via causal language modeling. Loss is computed only on the target tokens, not on the prompt or source sentence. Inference uses beam search with beam size 5, and evaluation is conducted on 10 translation directions (en↔de, en↔cs, en↔is, en↔zh, en↔ru) using sacreBLEU and COMET metrics.

## Method

ALMA introduces a two-stage fine-tuning recipe for decoder-only LLMs (LLaMA-2-7B/13B) that deliberately avoids large parallel corpora. Stage 1 performs full-weight fine-tuning on ~1B–20B tokens of mixed monolingual data (sourced from OSCAR) across the target languages plus English, using temperature-based sampling to balance language proportions, which builds cross-lingual proficiency without requiring any parallel data. Stage 2 fine-tunes on only 58K high-quality human-written parallel examples (from WMT test sets and Flores-200 dev/test splits), using either full-weight updates or LoRA (rank 16, updating 0.1% of parameters on down-projection layers). The key empirical finding is that LLaMA-2 peaks with as few as 10K–100K parallel examples and degrades with more, so the recipe exploits this by pairing modest monolingual exposure with a small, curated parallel set—achieving >12 BLEU/COMET gains over zero-shot and surpassing NLLB-54B and GPT-3.5-D with only 7B/13B parameters.

## Results

ALMA-13B-LoRA (the best configuration) achieves an average of 31.87 BLEU and 87.00 COMET on en→xx, and 35.82 BLEU and 84.59 COMET on xx→en, across 10 translation directions (de, cs, is, zh, ru ↔ en). This represents an improvement of over 12 BLEU and 12 COMET over LLaMA-2-7B's zero-shot performance (13.86 BLEU / 68.88 COMET on en→xx). Notably, only 1B monolingual tokens of fine-tuning (~18 hours on 16 MI200 GPUs) followed by 58K human-written parallel examples is sufficient to match or exceed NLLB-54B and GPT-3.5-D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALMA-13B-LoRA outperforms NLLB-54B by +0.95 BLEU and +1.96 COMET on en→xx (31.87/87.00 vs. 30.92/85.04), and by +6.87 BLEU and +7.93 COMET on xx→en (35.82/84.59 vs. 28.95/76.66). It also surpasses GPT-3.5-D by +2.91 BLEU and +2.41 COMET on en→xx, while marginally trailing GPT-3.5-T (−0.30 BLEU, +0.44 COMET on en→xx) and GPT-4 (which lacks Icelandic results). Against prior LLM-based translation work, ALMA dominates: BigTranslate-13B (fine-tuned on 300M parallel sentences) scores only 18.13 BLEU / 70.91 COMET on en→xx, while ALMA-7B achieves 29.89/86.49 — a gap of over 11 BLEU and 15 COMET. Bayling-13B and TIM/SWIE (which target only 2 languages) are also substantially outperformed across all directions. On cross-lingual understanding tasks (XNLI, XStoryCloze, XWinograd), ALMA's monolingual fine-tuning improves non-English performance over LLaMA-2 baselines and surpasses XGLM-7.5B and BLOOM-7B, though XLM-R-Large remains competitive on XNLI.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in LLM-based translation (BigTranslate, Bayling, TIM, SWIE) that fine-tunes decoder-only LLMs on hundreds of millions of parallel sentences, arguing instead that such massive parallel data actually dilutes the model's pre-existing multilingual knowledge. It repurposes the long-standing NMT insight that monolingual target-language data aids translation (Tan et al., PolyLM, Li et al.) and combines it with the data-quality principle from LIMA and related work, formalizing both into a systematic two-stage recipe (monolingual fine-tuning → small high-quality parallel fine-tuning). The paper also positions against the encoder-decoder SoTA (NLLB) by showing that a 7B/13B decoder-only model, properly trained, can match or exceed a 54B encoder-decoder system without the latter's reliance on large parallel corpora.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that excessive parallel data causes catastrophic forgetting in LLMs challenges a core assumption in machine translation and suggests a fundamentally different training paradigm for LLM-based translation. Practically, the recipe is highly accessible: only 1B monolingual tokens (~18 hours on 16 GPUs) plus 58K human-written parallel examples are needed to match NLLB-54B across 10 directions, making strong multilingual translation feasible for resource-constrained settings. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the 5 tested language pairs to truly low-resource or morphologically complex languages, whether the monolingual stage can be further compressed, and whether the results hold when the 'human-written' parallel data is replaced by larger but noisier filtered corpora.
