## Problem

Distributed DRL systems like IMPALA use concurrent actor-learner scheduling that introduces non-determinism: the frequency at which actors receive updated policies mid-rollout depends on hardware speed, causing learning curves to diverge across different hardware settings even with identical hyperparameters. This makes scaling results unpredictable and undermines reproducibility in a field where implementation details already matter greatly.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240271/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agents consume 84×84 greyscale Atari frames with 4 stacked frames and an action repeat of 4, under the standard evaluation protocol with sticky actions (25% probability) and a maximum of 108,000 frames per episode. The policy network (IMPALA's ResNet architecture) outputs a discrete action over the full action space. Training runs for 200M frames across 57 Atari games with three random seeds per configuration.

## Method

Cleanba's core contribution is a deterministic synchronization mechanism that replaces IMPALA's fully asynchronous actor-learner communication with a bounded queue (max size 1) between rollout data and policy parameters. This ensures the learner always trains on rollout data from the second-latest policy (π_i → D_{π_{i-1}} → π_{i+1}), making the data composition fully predictable regardless of hardware speed. The architecture also decouples the number of simulation environments from the number of CPU threads via EnvPool, so changing hardware only affects wall-clock speed, not data efficiency. Built on JAX and EnvPool, the platform supports multi-GPU training via pmap and multi-node scaling via jax.distributed, implementing both PPO and IMPALA variants under this unified architecture.

## Results

On 57 Atari games (200M frames, sticky actions), Cleanba's IMPALA and PPO achieve approximately 165% median human-normalized score (HNS), matching monobeast IMPALA's 165% and outperforming moolib IMPALA's 140%. Under the base 1-GPU/10-CPU setting, Cleanba IMPALA trains 6.8× faster than monobeast and 1.2× faster than moolib; under the max 8-GPU/40-CPU setting, it is 5× faster than monobeast (1 GPU/80 CPU) and 2× faster than moolib (8 GPU/80 CPU). Cleanba also produces near-identical learning curves across 1-GPU and 8-GPU hardware, whereas moolib's curves diverge significantly under the same hyperparameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against monobeast IMPALA, Cleanba IMPALA ties on median HNS (~165%) but is dramatically faster: 6.8× under the base 1-GPU/10-CPU setting (because Cleanba actors run on GPUs while monobeast's run on CPUs) and 5× under the workstation 8-GPU setting. Against moolib IMPALA, Cleanba wins on both performance (165% vs. 140% median HNS) and speed (1.2× base, 2× workstation), and critically on reproducibility—moolib's learning curves diverge meaningfully between 1-A100/10-CPU and 8-A100/80-CPU settings even with identical hyperparameters, while Cleanba's curves remain near-identical. Against CleanRL PPO (Sync), Cleanba PPO under the Cleanba (asynchronous) architecture is faster but suffers lower data efficiency due to stale rollout data (PPO performs 16 gradient updates per rollout vs. 4 for IMPALA), whereas Cleanba IMPALA shows no such data-efficiency penalty, making the asynchronous architecture a net win for IMPALA but a trade-off for PPO.

## Relation to Prior Work

Cleanba builds directly on IMPALA's actor-learner architecture (Espeholt et al., 2018) and PPO (Schulman et al., 2017), but departs from both by introducing a deterministic synchronization mechanism that eliminates the non-deterministic policy-version mixing during rollouts. Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of CleanRL's single-file simplicity (Huang et al., 2022b) and Podracer Sebulba's multi-node scalability (Hessel et al., 2021), using JAX and EnvPool as the implementation substrate. The key architectural departure from IMPALA is replacing asynchronous parameter broadcasting with a blocking queue-based protocol that guarantees the learner always trains on the second-latest policy's rollout data, making the data composition fully predictable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper addresses a gap in distributed DRL where reproducibility across hardware configurations was largely unexamined, showing that even with identical hyperparameters, the speed of policy broadcasting can produce meaningfully different learning curves. The finding that parallelizing actor-learner computation degrades PPO's data efficiency but leaves IMPALA unaffected is a useful practical insight for practitioners choosing between algorithms in distributed settings. Limitations include Atari-only evaluation, specific hyperparameter settings, and the inability to fully control confounding factors like optimizer and framework differences across baselines. Open questions include whether the synchronization mechanism generalizes to continuous control, multi-agent settings, or non-policy-gradient algorithms, and whether the PPO data-efficiency penalty can be mitigated with tuned hyperparameters.
