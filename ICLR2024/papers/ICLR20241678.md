## Problem

Conventional collaborative filtering methods based on empirical risk minimization optimize average performance, leaving tail users (those with low satisfaction) underserved—a critical risk for user retention in recommender systems. While conditional value-at-risk (CVaR) minimization directly targets tail performance, the non-smooth check function in its formulation destroys the item-wise separability required for scalable alternating least squares, making it intractable at web scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241678/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes implicit feedback as a set of (user, item) interaction pairs, where each user's preferred items define their positive set. During training, all interactions are used; during evaluation, each user's 80% interactions are revealed to optimize their embedding against fixed item embeddings, and the held-out 20% serve as the ground-truth ranking list. The output is a pair of low-dimensional user and item embedding matrices (dimension 32–512 depending on dataset) from which per-user ranked item lists are produced via score-and-sort.

## Method

SAFER_2 applies convolution-type smoothing (with a Gaussian kernel) to the CVaR check function, yielding a strictly convex, twice-differentiable surrogate that preserves block multi-convexity. A primal-dual splitting reformulation then decouples the smoothed composite loss: the dual variable z_i for each user is computed in closed form as z_i = 1 − K_h(ξ − ℓ_i) (where K_h is the kernel CDF and ℓ_i is the user's loss), and the primal update reduces to a re-weighted ALS identical in structure and complexity to iALS. The quantile threshold ξ is estimated via a sub-sampled Newton-Raphson step with backtracking line search, keeping per-epoch cost at O(|U_b|L + |S|d² + (|U|+|V|)d³). A condition-number-based Tikhonov regularization strategy introduces a single tunable hyperparameter for numerical stability across all users and items.

## Results

On ML-1M, SAFER₂ achieves R@50 of 0.4804 (α=1.0) and 0.2428 (α=0.3), outperforming iALS (0.4697 and 0.2391) on both average and tail metrics. On ML-20M, SAFER₂ reaches R@50 of 0.5308 (α=1.0) and 0.2152 (α=0.3), beating iALS (0.5263 and 0.2085) and Mult-VAE (0.5313 and 0.2091) on the tail metric. On the largest dataset MSD, SAFER₂ matches iALS on average R@50 (0.3585 vs. 0.3590) while improving tail R@50 from 0.0963 to 0.0983. Runtime per epoch is competitive: 3.45 s on ML-20M and 57.0 s on MSD, versus 3.16 s and 53.5 s for iALS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAFER₂ consistently outperforms iALS on tail metrics (α=0.3) across all three datasets: R@50 improves from 0.2391→0.2428 (ML-1M), 0.2085→0.2152 (ML-20M), and 0.0963→0.0983 (MSD), while maintaining or slightly exceeding average-case performance. Against Mult-VAE, SAFER₂ wins on all tail metrics and most average metrics, with the notable exception of ML-20M R@100 (α=1.0) where Mult-VAE edges ahead (0.6565 vs. 0.6501). The unsmoothed CVaR-MF baseline (subgradient method) degrades substantially on every metric—e.g., R@50 drops from 0.4697 (iALS) to 0.4495 (CVaR-MF) on ML-1M—confirming that the convolution-type smoothing is essential for practical CVaR optimization. In terms of runtime, SAFER₂ adds only ~9% overhead over iALS per epoch (3.45 s vs. 3.16 s on ML-20M), while CVaR-MF is faster per epoch (1.67 s) but requires far more epochs to converge, making SAFER₂ superior in wall-clock time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) scalable matrix factorization via implicit ALS (iALS, Hu et al. 2008; Rendle et al. 2022), (2) CVaR-based risk-averse optimization (Rockafellar and Uryasev, 2000), and (3) convolution-type smoothing from quantile regression (Fernandes et al. 2021; He et al. 2021; Tan et al. 2022). The key conceptual move is applying the quantile-regression smoothing technique to the CVaR ramp function, which restores the block separability that the non-smooth max(0,·) destroys, and then using a primal-dual (convex-conjugate) splitting to decouple the per-user dual variables from the per-item ALS subproblems. This departs from prior risk-averse CF work (Singh et al. 2020; Wen et al. 2022; Shivaswamy and Garcia-Garcia 2022) that relies on gradient-based optimization and does not achieve iALS-level parallelism over both users and items.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that SAFER_2 delivers tail-user quality improvements at essentially the same per-epoch cost as iALS, making risk-averse recommendation feasible at web scale without architectural changes. The negative result that direct CVaR-MF (subgradient) underperforms even ERM-MF across all datasets underscores that naive risk-averse formulations are not merely slower but actively harmful, validating the smoothing approach. Open questions include: whether the bandwidth h can be adapted per-user or per-epoch rather than fixed, whether the approach extends to explicit-feedback or sequential models, and whether the lack of a convergence guarantee (only empirical convergence with sufficiently large h) can be resolved theoretically. The d³ dependency remains a bottleneck for very high-dimensional embeddings, though the SAFER_2++ variant partially addresses this.
