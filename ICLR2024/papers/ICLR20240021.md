## Problem

Existing computationally tractable information-theoretic generalization bounds are largely restricted to average-case (expected) scenarios, leaving a gap for high-probability PAC bounds that remain tractable. Moreover, these bounds depend on stringent bounded-loss assumptions that break down for long-tailed loss distributions such as cross-entropy, which are standard in modern deep learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a training dataset of n i.i.d samples, the learned hypothesis weights, and the resulting samplewise loss values; in the supersample setting it additionally requires n×2 paired samples split by random binary indicators into training and test losses. The output is a family of PAC generalization upper bounds expressed entirely in terms of one-dimensional entropy quantities—Shannon or Rényi entropy of individual losses H(L^w), conditional loss entropy H(L^w|Y), joint loss entropy H(R^W), and loss-difference entropy H(R̃_Δ^W)—rather than the high-dimensional mutual information terms (I(W;S), I(X;T^w|Y)) used in prior work. This reduction to 1D variables makes the bounds estimable via simple kernel density or Monte-Carlo methods on scalar loss values.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces high-dimensional information measures with one-dimensional loss entropies by exploiting the Markov chain W → R → Δ (where R is the collection of samplewise losses) and the data-processing inequality, which guarantees that H(R^W) ≤ I(W;U|S̃_l) ≤ I(W;S). The proof technique constructs a typical subset of the loss space of size O(e^{H(L^w)}) via McDiarmid's concentration, decomposes the generalization error into three terms over this subset, and bounds each term using multinomial concentration inequalities and union bounds, yielding PAC bounds that scale as Õ(√(H(L^w)/n)). For data-dependent settings, the authors derive analogous bounds under both leave-one-out (Theorem 3) and supersample (Theorem 4) frameworks using Rényi-α entropy of loss differences. A key practical innovation is a thresholding strategy (Theorem 6) that splits each samplewise loss into a bounded component L^κ = min(L,κ) and an unbounded residual L^{-κ} = max(L−κ,0), applying fast-rate 1/n techniques to the bounded part and square-root 1/√n techniques to the residual, thereby handling long-tailed cross-entropy losses without the vacuous scaling by the maximum attainable loss B^W.

## Results

On synthetic 2D Gaussian datasets with a 5-layer MLP, the proposed loss entropy metrics H(L^w) and H(L^w|Y) achieve Pearson correlations of 0.5611 and 0.6350 with the generalization gap, substantially outperforming the information bottleneck metrics I(X;T^w) (0.3712) and I(X;T^w|Y) (0.3842) from Kawaguchi et al. (2023). On real-world tasks (4-layer CNN on binarized MNIST, ResNet-50 on CIFAR-10, and SGLD on MNIST), both the Square-Root bound (Theorem 4) and Fast-Rate bound (Theorem 6) track the true generalization gap more tightly than the Binary KL bound from Hellström & Durisi (2022), with the Fast-Rate bound consistently outperforming the Square-Root bound, particularly in the more challenging CIFAR-10 and SGLD scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Binary KL bound (Theorem 7 in Hellström & Durisi, 2022), the paper's Square-Root and Fast-Rate bounds are consistently tighter (lower) across all three learning scenarios (MNIST-Adam, CIFAR10-SGD, MNIST-SGLD), with the advantage most pronounced in early training epochs where the Binary KL bound is especially loose due to its use of a universal constant C. The Fast-Rate bound (Theorem 6) further outperforms the Square-Root bound (Theorem 4), particularly on CIFAR-10 and SGLD, validating the thresholding strategy for handling long-tailed cross-entropy loss distributions. Against Kawaguchi et al. (2023), the loss entropy metrics H(L^w) and H(L^w|Y) demonstrate markedly stronger predictive power for generalization error (correlations 0.5611 and 0.6350) compared to the information bottleneck quantities I(X;T^w) and I(X;T^w|Y) (correlations 0.3712 and 0.3842), a gap of roughly 0.17–0.25 in Pearson correlation. The paper also notes that H(L^w|Y) outperforms H(L^w), supporting the conditional bound (Theorem 2) over the unconditional one (Theorem 1) when the label space is finite.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the supersample and leave-one-out frameworks (Steinke & Zakynthinou 2020; Rammal et al. 2022; Hellström & Durisi 2022; Wang & Mao 2023) by replacing high-dimensional mutual information terms (I(W;S), I(X;T^w|Y), I(R^W;Û|S̃_s)) with one-dimensional loss entropies (H(L^w), H(L^w|Y), H(R^W), H(R̃_Δ^W)), leveraging the data-processing inequality along the Markov chain W→R→Δ. It also builds on Kawaguchi et al. (2023) data-independent information bottleneck bounds, showing that loss entropy provides a tighter and computationally tractable substitute for I(X;T^w|Y). The thresholding strategy in Theorem 6 is a novel combination of fast-rate (Catoni-style) and square-root bounds, specifically designed to handle long-tailed loss distributions where previous fast-rate bounds degrade.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is computational tractability: estimating entropy of a 1D loss variable is feasible where estimating mutual information between high-dimensional model weights and datasets is intractable for modern neural networks. The removal of the bounded loss assumption makes these bounds applicable to cross-entropy and other unbounded losses common in practice. However, the data-independent bounds (Theorems 1-2) require the model to be fixed and independent of training data, limiting their direct applicability to standard supervised training. Open questions include whether the threshold κ in Theorem 6 can be chosen adaptively, whether the discrete loss assumption can be fully relaxed for continuous-valued losses, and whether the loss entropy framework extends to multi-task or distribution-shift settings.
