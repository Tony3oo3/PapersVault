## Problem

While deep music generation has achieved high quality on short segments (a measure to a phrase), generating an entire song with recognizable global structure—verse-chorus form, phrase development, and cadences—remains unresolved. As compositions extend in length, the number of interacting music representations grows exponentially, making it essential to organize them in a structured, hierarchical way that mirrors how music is actually composed.

## Input / Output

The model takes a Form specification (key progression and phrase labels at measure resolution, up to 256 measures) as the top-level input, plus optional external latent codes for chord progression, rhythmic pattern, and accompaniment texture at lower levels. It produces a four-level hierarchical output: Form (8×M×12 image), Reduced Lead Sheet (2×γM×128 at beat resolution), Lead Sheet (2×δγM×128 at step resolution), and Accompaniment (2×δγM×128 at step resolution), all represented as multi-channel piano-roll images. Generation proceeds top-down with autoregressive inpainting at each level, where lower-level models operate on 128-beat or 128-step windows (32 or 8 measures respectively) and are conditioned on the full upper-level context.

## Method

The paper defines a four-level computational hierarchical music language (Form → Reduced Lead Sheet → Lead Sheet → Accompaniment) inspired by Schenkerian compositional hierarchy, where each level captures semantics at a specific musical scope from whole-song form down to individual notes. Four 2D-UNet diffusion models are trained in a cascaded fashion, each conditioned on its upper levels via three mechanisms: channel concatenation of the upper-level image (background condition), cross-attention with top-S similar prior segments selected by phrase-type matching (autoregressive condition), and cross-attention with pre-trained external latent codes (external condition), all using classifier-free guidance. At inference, whole-song generation proceeds by first sampling the full Form unconditionally, then autoregressively inpainting each lower level with a hop of b_k/2, where the first half of each window is fixed and the second half is denoised conditioned on background, autoregressive, and external inputs. The key insight is that this hierarchical decomposition serves as a strong structural inductive bias, making training decomposable (each model sees a manageable scope) and enabling interpretable, multi-granularity control over the generated music.

## Results

On the POP909 dataset, the proposed cascaded diffusion model (Cas.Diff.) achieves the highest Inter-Phrase Latent Similarity (ILS) on lead melody pitch (2.05), chord (1.32), and accompaniment texture (1.19), substantially outperforming both baselines. The closest competitor, TFxl(REMI)+ph.l., leads only on lead melody rhythm ILS (1.71 vs. 1.49). Subjective evaluation (57 raters, 5-point scale) confirms the model significantly outperforms baselines on Boundary Clarity and Phrase Similarity for whole-song generation, while also matching or exceeding them on Naturalness and Musicality for both short-term (8-measure) and whole-song (32-measure) samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two phrase-level generation models adapted for whole-song generation: (1) Polyffusion + phrase labels, a diffusion-based model using iterative inpainting, and (2) TFxl(REMI) + phrase labels, a Transformer-XL model with REMI representation. On the ILS structural metric, the proposed cascaded diffusion model wins on 3 of 4 sub-metrics: lead melody pitch (2.05 vs. 1.89 for TFxl, 0.60 for Polyff.), chord (1.32 vs. 0.68, 0.52), and accompaniment texture (1.19 vs. 0.74, 0.61). TFxl(REMI) narrowly outperforms the proposed method on lead melody rhythm ILS (1.71 vs. 1.49), while Polyffusion is consistently the weakest baseline across all metrics. In subjective evaluation, the proposed model significantly outperforms both baselines on whole-song structural criteria (Boundary Clarity, Phrase Similarity) and matches or exceeds them on music quality (Naturalness, Musicality) for both short-term and whole-song generation, indicating that the hierarchical inductive bias does not sacrifice local musical quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Polyffusion (Min et al., 2023) for its 2D-UNet diffusion backbone, cross-attention conditioning, and iterative inpainting strategy, and borrows the multi-scale cascaded generation idea from Mousai (Schneider et al., 2023). However, it departs from both by introducing an explicit four-level hierarchical music language (Form → Reduced Lead Sheet → Lead Sheet → Accompaniment) inspired by Schenkerian analysis and Tagg's pop-music hierarchy, where each diffusion stage conditions on the output of the stage above. Prior structured generation work (Music Transformer, REMI, Polyffusion) either learns structure implicitly via attention or uses flat external labels; this paper makes the compositional hierarchy itself the generative decomposition, combining explicit structural inductive bias with implicit local modeling via diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a principled hierarchical decomposition of music composition can be used as a training and generation scaffold for whole-song symbolic music, yielding recognizable verse-chorus form, cadences, and phrase-level similarity/contrast that flat phrase-level models fail to produce. The efficiency analysis (linear time and log-parameter complexity vs. quadratic for end-to-end) suggests the cascaded design scales favorably to longer compositions. Key limitations remain: the model is restricted to pop music, 256 measures, and a single homophonic texture; it does not handle meter changes, multi-track arrangements, or audio output. Open questions include whether the hierarchical language generalizes to other genres, whether the reduced lead sheet level is truly necessary or could be replaced by learned intermediate representations, and how the approach extends to polyphonic or multi-instrument settings.
