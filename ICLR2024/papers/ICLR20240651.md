## Problem

Spiking neural networks suffer from catastrophic forgetting during continual learning, and existing solutions fall into two unsatisfying categories: neuroscience-inspired methods (replay, metaplasticity) that only implicitly protect knowledge without explicit guarantees for neuron populations, and mathematical projection methods that require operations like SVD or matrix inversion which cannot be implemented by neuronal operations on neuromorphic hardware. The paper asks how the concept of orthogonal projection can be realized with purely neuronal operations to explicitly preserve old-task knowledge during new-task learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes static images (grayscale or RGB) encoded as real-valued pixel values fed at each of T discrete time steps (T=20 for DSR, T=6 for BPTT/OTTT) as input currents to LIF neurons, along with a task identifier in task-incremental settings. Beyond the standard class label output, the method internally produces projected presynaptic activity traces (x̂ = x − HᵀHx) that drive synaptic weight updates, and continuously updated lateral synaptic weights H that encode the principal subspace of neural activities. The architecture ranges from fully connected networks (784-800-800-10) to ResNet-18 with multi-head classifiers, and the number of lateral subspace neurons per layer is a tunable hyperparameter (roughly 1/5 to 1/2 of the presynaptic input dimension).

## Method

HLOP adds recurrent lateral connections with skew-symmetric weights (H and −Hᵀ) between each layer's feedforward neurons and a set of 'subspace neurons.' Hebbian and anti-Hebbian learning (a two-stage Oja's-rule variant: ΔH = yxᵀ + yx̃ᵀ) updates H online from streaming data so that H converges to the principal component matrix of presynaptic activity traces. Orthogonal projection is then implemented neurally: the activity trace x is propagated to subspace neurons (y = Hx), the response is fed back through the recurrent connection (x⁻ = −Hᵀy), and the modified trace x̂ = x + x⁻ = x − HᵀHx is used for synaptic weight updates, ensuring updates are orthogonal to the learned subspace. For successive tasks, only newly expanded subspace neurons are Hebbian-trained (using integrated postsynaptic responses from both old and new neurons to avoid re-learning existing subspaces), while consolidated neurons project the traces; after each task, new neurons are merged into the consolidated set. The method is agnostic to the specific SNN training algorithm (DSR, BPTT with surrogate gradients, or OTTT with eligibility traces) since it only modifies presynaptic traces, and can optionally use lateral spiking neurons with rate-coded high-frequency bursts instead of linear neurons for full neuromorphic compatibility.

## Results

HLOP achieves nearly zero forgetting (BWT ≈ -0.3% to -3.7%) across all four evaluation settings, dramatically outperforming the baseline which suffers 4–60% backward transfer. On 10-split CIFAR-100, HLOP reaches 78.58% average accuracy with only -0.26% BWT, approaching the multitask upper bound of 79.70%. On the most challenging 5-Datasets setting (distinct input domains), HLOP achieves 88.65% accuracy with -3.71% BWT, compared to 45.12%/-60.12% for the baseline. The method works consistently across three SNN training paradigms (DSR, BPTT+SG, OTTT) and three error propagation schemes (BP, FA, SS), with BWT always below -1.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HLOP outperforms all three baselines on every dataset where they are compared. Against EWC (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017), HLOP achieves substantially higher accuracy and less forgetting: on 5-Datasets, HLOP reaches 88.65% ACC / -3.71% BWT versus EWC's 57.06% / -44.55%, a gap of over 31 points in accuracy. Against HAT (Serra et al., 2018), HLOP wins on both accuracy and BWT across CIFAR-100 (78.58 vs 73.67 ACC), miniImageNet (63.40 vs 50.11 ACC), and 5-Datasets (88.65 vs 72.72 ACC). Against GPM (Saha et al., 2021), the closest competitor, HLOP consistently achieves lower BWT (e.g., -0.26% vs -1.37% on CIFAR-100; -3.71% vs -15.52% on 5-Datasets) and higher accuracy, with the largest gap on 5-Datasets where distinct input distributions make GPM's small-batch SVD-based subspace estimation particularly biased. The paper attributes HLOP's advantage over GPM to its streaming Hebbian learning that builds the projection subspace from large data rather than a small batch, and to its purely neuronal implementation that is compatible with neuromorphic hardware.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected lines of work: (1) Hebbian learning rules (Oja's rule, Pehlevan et al.'s Hebbian/anti-Hebbian subspace algorithm) that extract principal subspaces from streaming data, and (2) orthogonal gradient projection methods for continual learning (Saha et al.'s GPM, Zeng et al.) that mathematically guarantee knowledge preservation but require SVD or matrix inversion. The key departure is showing that the projection matrix P = I - M^T M can be realized entirely through recurrent lateral connections with skew-symmetric weights updated by Hebbian learning, eliminating the need for non-neuronal operations. Unlike prior neuroscience-inspired continual learning methods (replay, metaplasticity/regularization), HLOP provides an explicit, population-level guarantee of knowledge preservation rather than implicit or per-synapse constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first demonstration that orthogonal projection—a concept with strong mathematical guarantees—can be implemented with purely neuronal operations, opening a path toward continual on-chip learning on neuromorphic hardware where SVD or matrix inversion is infeasible. The nearly zero forgetting across diverse training methods (DSR, BPTT-SG, OTTT), error propagation schemes (BP, FA, SS), and network architectures (FC, CNN, ResNet-18) suggests broad applicability. Key limitations include the manual specification of subspace neuron count (a hyperparameter analogous to GPM's threshold), the restriction to task- and domain-incremental settings (class-incremental requires replay), and additional training overhead from lateral weight updates that is not yet parallelized in standard frameworks. Open questions include adaptive determination of subspace size via neuronal mechanisms, extension to STDP-based Hebbian learning, and combination with context-dependent modules for class-incremental learning.
