## Problem

Conventional structured pruning follows a fixed procedure—keep the most important neurons, discard the rest—which causes substantial accuracy drops that necessitate expensive fine-tuning, especially problematic for large models where even one epoch is costly. The paper argues this accuracy loss is largely an artifact of the overlying pruning procedure rather than the importance metric itself, and that the discarded neurons carry recoverable information that is simply thrown away. Additionally, the joint space of training and pruning has been underexplored, with no principled way to factorize training to reduce wall-clock time while maintaining competitive post-pruning performance.

## Input / Output

The method operates on structured pruning groups—sets of layers that must be pruned jointly due to architectural dependencies (e.g., residual connections)—rather than individual layers. Given a trained network, an agnostic importance vector (l1-norm, Taylor, LAMP, or even random scores), and a target group cardinality m ≤ n, it produces a pruned network where the n original neuron pairings are fused down to m via weighted aggregation. Batch normalization layers are absorbed into preceding layers before fusion, and the resulting fused neurons preserve the BN activation state by resetting γ=1, β=0, σ=1, μ=0.

## Method

Intra-Fusion redefines the pruning procedure by formulating neuron fusion as an Optimal Transport problem: the source distribution is supported on all n neuron pairings in a group, the target is the m most important pairings (or K-means/GMM centroids), and the ground cost is the normalized l1 distance between concatenated weight-and-bias vectors of each pairing. Solving the OT problem yields a transport map T whose columns serve as weighted aggregation coefficients (summing to unity) for combining source neurons into each target neuron, effectively recycling information from discarded neurons into survivors. The approach is agnostic to the importance metric—it only requires a ranking—and works without any data or fine-tuning. Beyond data-free pruning, the paper introduces PaF (Prune-and-Fuse) and FaP (Fuse-and-Prune) strategies that split the training dataset into subsets, train models in parallel, then fuse and prune, achieving 1.3–1.4× overall speedup with competitive accuracy.

## Results

In the data-free (no fine-tuning) setting, Intra-Fusion recovers up to +60% test accuracy over conventional structured pruning at high sparsity levels. For example, on ResNet18/CIFAR-10 at 40% neuron sparsity in Group 6, conventional pruning with ℓ₁ drops to 78.7% while Intra-Fusion maintains 92.3%; at 70% sparsity in Group 9 with LAMP importance, conventional pruning collapses to 10.2% while Intra-Fusion retains 89.0%. Even with full fine-tuning available, Intra-Fusion still yields a consistent +0.24% to +0.96% accuracy gain over conventional pruning across sparsity levels. The split-data training approach (FaP/PaF) achieves a 1.81× speedup in model training time with overall pipeline speedups of 1.31× (FaP) and 1.42× (PaF) while matching or slightly exceeding whole-data model accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare Intra-Fusion against other named pruning methods; instead, the sole baseline is conventional structured pruning (keep top-m neurons, discard the rest) using the same importance metric. Intra-Fusion consistently outperforms conventional pruning across all tested importance metrics (ℓ₁, Taylor, LAMP), architectures (VGG11-BN, ResNet18, ResNet50), and datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet) in the data-free setting, with gains growing sharply at higher sparsity (e.g., +35% at 70% sparsity in volatile groups). The gains are agnostic to the importance metric: even with random uniform scores as the importance signal, Intra-Fusion still improves accuracy by +0.1% to +16% over random conventional pruning. In the data-driven (fine-tuned) setting, Intra-Fusion still yields a smaller but consistent +0.24% to +0.96% gain, confirming the benefit is complementary to fine-tuning rather than subsumed by it. The only cases where Intra-Fusion underperforms are in highly resilient groups (e.g., Group 10 of ResNet18) at low sparsity, where the margin is within ±1%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on OTFusion (Singh & Jaggi, 2020), which introduced Optimal Transport for fusing independently trained networks and included a brief proof-of-concept of fusing a network with its own pruned version. It also extends the structured pruning literature (l1-norm, Taylor, LAMP, CHIP importance metrics) by challenging the invariant 'keep the most important neurons, discard the rest' procedure that has persisted across all prior importance-metric work. The split-data training component draws on federated learning and data-parallelism ideas, but repurposes them in a pruning-and-fusion pipeline (PaF/FaP) that has no direct prior counterpart.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The data-free pruning capability is practically significant for settings where fine-tuning is prohibitively expensive (large language models, privacy-sensitive data), and the agnosticism to importance metrics makes Intra-Fusion a drop-in replacement for any existing pruning pipeline. However, the evaluation is limited to relatively small CNNs (VGG11-BN, ResNet18, ResNet50) and single-group prunings, and the method can actually degrade performance in some groups (e.g., Group 7), raising questions about robustness. Open questions include whether the approach scales to transformer architectures, whether full-model (multi-group) pruning preserves the large single-group gains, and whether the split-data factorization remains beneficial when T1 >> T2 as the authors speculate.
