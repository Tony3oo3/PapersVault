## Problem

Generative error correction (GER) for ASR uses LLMs to map N-best hypotheses to transcriptions, but operates entirely on text-only modality, ignoring acoustic information and introducing data uncertainty. Existing fusion strategies (early concatenation of speech tokens, mid-level cross-attention) suffer from modality laziness and sequence-length mismatch between audio and text. The paper asks how to effectively fuse acoustic information into LLMs at the decision level during auto-regressive decoding to reduce this uncertainty without retraining the LLM.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an N-best hypotheses list (≥5 candidates from Whisper-large-v2 beam search) formatted via a prompt template for LLaMA-7B, alongside the raw speech signal processed by a Whisper-tiny ASR model to produce per-step decoder-level logits over a shared vocabulary. The output is a token-by-token transcription generated through auto-regressive decoding, where each step combines calibrated LLM logits with uncertainty-weighted ASR logits. The ASR decoder must be fine-tuned with LLaMA's tokenizer and word embeddings to align the decoding space, but the fusion mechanism itself is training-free.

## Method

UADF (Uncertainty-Aware Dynamic Fusion) performs step-wise late fusion during auto-regressive decoding in two stages. First, temperature scaling calibrates both LLM and ASR logits so their confidence scores match actual token-level accuracy, mitigating the over-confidence that would otherwise distort uncertainty estimates. Second, the LLM's predictive entropy at each decoding step dynamically determines the ASR contribution weight via sigmoid(U_t^llm) − β, so when the LLM is uncertain about the current token, more acoustic information is incorporated into the final decision. The per-step output is a softmax of the weighted sum of calibrated LLM and ASR logits, with β defaulting to 0.5. Only two temperature parameters (found via binary search on a small validation set) and β require tuning; the fusion mechanism itself is training-free, distinguishing it from static-weight late fusion or reinforcement-learning-based approaches like MSRL.

## Results

On ATIS, UADF achieves 1.24% WER (23.0% relative WERR over the GER baseline of 1.61%), and on WSJ it reaches 2.47% WER (12.7% WERR over GER's 2.83%). On the noisy CHiME-4 dataset, UADF attains 6.08% average WER (9.9% WERR over GER's 6.75%), outperforming static late fusion (6.17%, 8.6% WERR). In the audio-visual speech recognition setting on LRS3, UADF applied to AV-HuBERT achieves 9.9% WER (22.7% WERR), surpassing static fusion (10.1%, 21.1% WERR) and matching the reinforcement-learning-based MSRL baseline while remaining training-free.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UADF consistently outperforms the GER baseline (Chen et al., 2023b) across all three datasets: ATIS (1.24% vs. 1.61%), WSJ (2.47% vs. 2.83%), and CHiME-4 (6.08% vs. 6.75%). Early fusion with speech tokens (Fathullah et al., 2023) actually degrades performance below GER (2.16%/2.02% on ATIS vs. 1.61%), confirming the modality laziness problem, while UADF achieves the best WER by a wide margin. Mid fusion via cross-attention (Whispering-LLaMa, Radhakrishnan et al., 2023) improves over GER on WSJ (2.59% vs. 2.83%) but still lags behind UADF (2.47%) and underperforms GER on ATIS (1.75% vs. 1.61%). In the AVSR setting, UADF applied to AV-HuBERT (Shi et al., 2022b) achieves 9.9% WER, beating static fusion (10.1%) and matching MSRL (Chen et al., 2023c) without requiring an additional reinforcement learning training phase. The unimodal V-HuBERT lip-reading model (Shi et al., 2022a) serves as a visual-only reference, and UADF's dynamic fusion effectively reuses this modality to counteract the audio-dominant modality laziness observed in AV-HuBERT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the recently introduced Generative Error Correction (GER) / Hypotheses-to-Transcription (H2T) paradigm (Chen et al., 2023b), where an LLM with a LoRA adapter maps an ASR N-best list to a corrected transcription, by addressing its fundamental limitation: the LLM operates purely on text and ignores the underlying acoustic signal. It departs from prior multimodal fusion strategies for LLM-enhanced ASR—early fusion via speech-token concatenation (Fathullah et al., 2023) and mid fusion via cross-attention in the LLM decoder (Radhakrishnan et al., 2023a, Whispering-LLaMa)—by proposing step-wise late fusion at the decision level during auto-regressive decoding. The dynamic weighting mechanism is grounded in the theoretical connection between uncertainty estimation and optimal multimodal fusion established by Zhang et al. (2023c), while the calibration step draws on temperature scaling for ensemble logit alignment (Kumar et al., 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UADF offers a training-free, plug-in solution to the modality laziness problem that plagues early and mid fusion in LLM-enhanced ASR, making it practically attractive for practitioners who already have a GER pipeline and a separate ASR model. The demonstration that even a modest Whisper-tiny ASR model can yield 23% relative WER reduction when fused via UADF suggests the approach is robust to the quality of the auxiliary acoustic model. However, the evaluation is confined to relatively small, clean-domain datasets (ATIS, WSJ) and a single small ASR model, leaving open whether the gains persist at scale (larger LLMs, larger ASR backbones, diverse domains) or in streaming/real-time settings. The extension to AVSR is promising but relies on a specific pre-trained lip-reading model and a small number of SNR conditions.
