## Problem

Zero-shot talking avatar generation from a single portrait and speech has been hindered by reliance on domain-specific heuristics such as warping-based motion representations and 3D Morphable Models, which prevent direct end-to-end learning from data distribution and lead to unnatural, low-diversity results. The paper asks whether these priors can be entirely eliminated in favor of a purely data-driven framework that still achieves natural, diverse, and lip-synced generation.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single 256×256 portrait image (providing identity and appearance) and a speech clip (processed into wav2vec 2.0 features) as input, and outputs a 256×256 talking avatar video of 125–250 frames. During training, the reference frame is randomly sampled within the video clip rather than fixed to the first frame, and pseudo-sentences are generated by sampling random subsequences for data augmentation. The framework also accepts head pose vectors (pitch, yaw, roll) or textual instructions (encoded by CLIP) as alternative or additional conditions.

## Method

GAIA decomposes generation into two stages: (1) a VAE with separate motion and appearance encoders (the motion encoder consumes facial landmarks, the appearance encoder consumes an RGB frame) and a shared decoder, trained adversarially to disentangle per-frame motion from shared appearance; (2) a Conformer-based diffusion model that predicts the motion latent sequence conditioned on wav2vec 2.0 speech features (added element-wise to each block) and a reference motion latent (injected via cross-attention), with head poses added through a learned linear layer for pose-controllable generation. The key architectural choice is to represent motion as a learned latent from landmarks rather than warping fields or 3DMM coefficients, eliminating all domain priors. The VAE is trained with L1 reconstruction, KL regularization, and a discriminator, while the diffusion model is trained with a standard denoising objective plus an MSE loss on predicted head poses. The framework scales from 150M to 2B parameters and is trained on a filtered 1.2K-hour, 16K-speaker dataset with automated quality controls for frontal orientation, motion smoothness, and lip-speech alignment.

## Results

On the speech-driven talking avatar generation task (evaluated on TalkingHead-1KH), GAIA achieves the best subjective scores across all five dimensions: naturalness (4.362/5), lip-sync quality (4.332/5), motion jittering (4.345/5), visual quality (4.320/5), and motion diversity (4.243/5), substantially outperforming all baselines. Objectively, GAIA attains a Sync-D of 8.528 (close to the real-video reference of 8.548) and the best Motion Stability Index (MSI) of 1.181, while its FID of 22.924 is comparable to SadTalker (22.322). For the video-driven VAE evaluation, GAIA achieves the best FID (15.730 self-reconstruction, 15.200 cross-reenactment), lowest AKD (1.442), and highest MSI (0.856 self-reconstruction, 1.102 cross-reenactment) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAIA decisively outperforms all three video-driven baselines (FOMM, HeadGAN, face-vid2vid) on every metric in both self-reconstruction and cross-reenactment settings, with the largest gains in cross-reenactment FID (15.200 vs. 28.093 for face-vid2vid) and AKD (2.003 vs. 3.630). Among speech-driven baselines, GAIA wins on all five subjective MOS scores by a wide margin over MakeItTalk (e.g., 4.362 vs. 2.148 naturalness) and Audio2Head (4.362 vs. 2.355), and also surpasses SadTalker (4.362 vs. 2.884) and PC-AVS (4.362 vs. 2.797). On objective lip-sync (Sync-D), GAIA (8.528) is marginally better than SadTalker (8.606) and PC-AVS (8.341) but slightly behind Audio2Head (8.508); on FID, GAIA (22.924) is marginally worse than SadTalker (22.322) but far better than MakeItTalk (28.894) and Audio2Head (28.607). PD-FGC achieves the highest motion diversity score (4.512) but relies on an external driving video and has the worst FID (58.943) and MSI (0.478), whereas GAIA achieves competitive diversity (4.243) without any driving video.

## Relation to Prior Work

GAIA departs from the dominant paradigm in zero-shot talking avatar generation, where prior methods (MakeItTalk, SadTalker, Audio2Head) rely on domain-specific heuristics such as warping-based motion representations or 3D Morphable Models as intermediate representations between speech and video. Instead, it adopts a purely data-driven two-stage architecture: a VAE that disentangles motion (from facial landmarks) and appearance (from RGB frames) per frame, followed by a score-based diffusion model (following Song et al., ICLR 2021) with a Conformer backbone to generate motion latents conditioned on wav2vec 2.0 speech features. The framework also draws on adversarial VAE training (Esser et al., 2021; Rombach et al., 2022) for perceptual quality and extends the conditioning mechanism to support pose control and text-instructed generation via CLIP embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating domain priors, GAIA demonstrates that naturalness, diversity, and lip-sync quality can be achieved through end-to-end learning from a large, carefully filtered dataset (16K speakers, ~1.2K hours after filtering), with clear scalability from 150M to 2B parameters. The framework's flexibility—enabling pose-controllable, fully controllable, and text-instructed generation from the same architecture—positions it as a general-purpose motion generator rather than a single-purpose talking-head model. Key limitations include reliance on pretrained landmark and head-pose extractors (hindering full end-to-end learning) and the fact that the majority of raw data (~86%) must be discarded through aggressive filtering, raising questions about data efficiency. Open questions include whether the landmark-based motion encoding can be replaced by direct pixel-level disentanglement and whether the approach extends to multi-person or full-body scenarios.
