## Problem

While it is widely hypothesized that pre-training provides factual knowledge and fine-tuning provides behavioral alignment, this intuition has not been directly tested because both stages are conventionally performed at the same model scale. The paper asks: what happens if we combine the knowledge learned by a large model during pre-training with the behavioral changes learned by a small model during fine-tuning (or vice versa)?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240522/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a text prompt, the method computes per-token conditionals by multiplying the log probabilities of a large pre-trained model (N-scale) by the log-probability ratio (behavioral delta) between a small fine-tuned model and its corresponding small pre-trained model (both M-scale). The output is a generated text response sampled autoregressively using a per-timestep approximation of the sequence-level partition function, requiring one forward pass from the N-scale model and two from M-scale models per token. No new parameters are trained; all three models are used as fixed, off-the-shelf components.

## Method

EFT is grounded in the observation that any fine-tuned language model can be expressed as the solution to a KL-constrained RL problem relative to its pre-trained base, yielding a factorization of fine-tuned logits into base log probabilities (pre-training knowledge) and an implicit reward or 'behavior delta' (fine-tuning knowledge). By computing the base log probabilities at one scale (N) and the behavior delta at another scale (M), EFT emulates the result of pre-training and fine-tuning at independently chosen scales without any additional training. In the up-scaling case (N > M), this approximates fine-tuning the large model simply by reweighting the large base model's conditionals with the small model's behavioral ratio, capturing most of the factuality gains of a large fine-tuned model at a fraction of the cost. The per-timestep partition function approximation makes sampling tractable, and the size asymmetry enables an adaptation of speculative decoding where the small fine-tuned model proposes token blocks that the full EFT model verifies in parallel, achieving approximately 2.5× speedup while preserving exact samples.

## Results

Using EFT up-scaling (small fine-tuned model + large base model), the paper shows that scaling pre-training primarily improves factuality while scaling fine-tuning primarily improves helpfulness, across Llama-1, Llama-2, and Falcon model families. On HumanEval, up-scaling Llama-2-7B-chat to 70B closes 84% of the solve-rate gap between Llama-2-7B-chat and Llama-2-70B-chat. Up-scaling produces nearly 70% of the factuality gains of the large fine-tuned model over the small fine-tuned model, without any additional training. Speculative decoding adapted to EFT achieves a ~2.5× speedup (from 6.0 to 13.8 tokens/sec) while preserving sample quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares EFT up-scaled and down-scaled models against the small fine-tuned model (lower bound) and large fine-tuned model (upper bound) across three model families. For Llama-2, EFT up-scaling (Llama-2-base 70B + Llama-2-chat 7B delta) achieves normalized factuality scores approaching the Llama-2-chat 70B baseline on ELI5, while maintaining helpfulness above the Llama-2-chat 7B level. For the Llama-1/Vicuna family, up-scaling with Vicuna-7B and Llama-1-base 65B similarly recovers most of the factuality gap to Vicuna-33B. For Falcon, up-scaling Falcon-7B-instruct with Falcon-180B-base captures substantial gains in dialogue, QA, and code generation without fine-tuning the 180B model. In all cases, EFT up-scaling consistently outperforms the small fine-tuned model on factuality and is competitive on helpfulness, while down-scaling (large fine-tuned delta + small base) primarily boosts helpfulness at the cost of factuality.

## Relation to Prior Work

EFT builds directly on the KL-constrained RL formulation of fine-tuning from DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) and its Bayesian interpretation (Korbak et al., 2022), which together justify factoring a fine-tuned model's logits into base-model logits plus a 'behavior delta.' The closest prior work is DExperts (Liu et al., 2021), which uses log-probability differences between expert and anti-expert models for controlled generation, and contrastive decoding (Li et al., 2023), which subtracts a small model's logits from a large model's. EFT departs from both by interpreting the log-probability ratio as a log-importance weight that reweights an *arbitrary* model's conditional distribution, eliminating the scaling hyperparameter and enabling independent scale selection for the knowledge and behavior components. The speculative decoding adaptation (Leviathan et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2023a) is repurposed to accelerate up-scaled sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The up-scaling technique offers a practically significant shortcut: combining a small fine-tuned model's behavior delta with a large base model's knowledge captures roughly 70% of the factuality gains of actually fine-tuning the large model, without any additional training or hyperparameters. This is particularly impactful in settings where fine-tuning a 70B+ model is computationally prohibitive but a 7B fine-tuned model is readily available. The clean empirical separation—pre-training scale drives factuality, fine-tuning scale drives helpfulness—provides a useful mental model for practitioners allocating compute across the two stages. Open questions include whether the per-timestep partition function approximation degrades for longer sequences, whether up-scaling generalizes to modalities beyond text, and whether the behavior delta remains transferable when the fine-tuning objective changes substantially (e.g., from dialogue to code).
