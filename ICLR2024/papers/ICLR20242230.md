## Problem

Lexicographic multi-objective RL problems with continuous state-action spaces lack principled, decomposed learning algorithms: existing approaches either restrict to discrete action spaces, rely on constrained optimization that trades off conflicting subtasks, or learn monolithic policies that preclude reuse and interpretation of subtask solutions. Moreover, it was unknown what scalar reward function these lexicographic methods actually optimize, hampering principled understanding.

## Input / Output

The method consumes individually pre-trained subtask Q-functions (DNN approximations of Q_i*) and sampling networks (π_i*) obtained from isolated Soft Q-Learning runs, along with their retained replay buffers. It produces a composed arbiter policy π_≻(a|s) ∝ exp(Q_≻(s,a)) that outputs continuous actions while guaranteeing zero probability for constraint-violating actions. A key property is that the zero-shot composition requires no environment interaction—only the log-transform of constraint indicators applied to pre-trained Q-functions—while offline adaptation reuses filtered replay buffer transitions without new rollouts.

## Method

PSQD introduces a subtask transformation that maps each higher-priority subtask's Q-function through a constraint indicator c_i(s,a) ∈ {0,1} and its natural logarithm, yielding transformed Q-functions ln(c_i) that assign −∞ to constraint-violating actions and 0 to permitted ones. This makes the lexicographic problem scalarizable: the global Q-function decomposes as Q_≻ = Σ_{i=1}^{n-1} ln(c_i) + Q_n, and the arbiter policy factors as a product of indicators times exp(Q_n). The algorithm learns incrementally from highest to lowest priority, restricting each subtask's action space to the global action indifference space (intersection of all higher-priority indifference spaces) and performing off-policy soft Q-learning with importance sampling within that restricted space. Convergence is guaranteed by a contraction mapping theorem (Theorem 3.1), and the offline adaptation step filters replay buffers to discard constraint-violating transitions, enabling fully offline fine-tuning of pre-trained subtask solutions.

## Results

In the 2D navigation environment, PSQD is the only method that maintains zero collisions (subtask 1 cost = 0) throughout learning of the lower-priority navigation task, while all baselines accumulate increasing collision costs. After offline adaptation using only retained pre-training data, PSQD's mean return matches the RRT planning oracle, substantially outperforming the zero-shot composition which gets trapped. In the 9-dimensional Franka Emika Panda task, PSQD avoids the forbidden volume even in the zero-shot setting and improves further through adaptation, whereas the SQL composition baseline incurs high collision costs by moving through the forbidden workspace.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PSQD decisively outperforms all four baselines on the core criterion of lexicographic priority satisfaction: it is the only method that maintains zero collision cost for the high-priority obstacle-avoidance subtask while simultaneously learning the lower-priority navigation task. Prioritized SAC and Prioritized PPO (with various KL-regularization weights β) all trade off subtask performance, accepting obstacle collisions to reach the goal faster, and none converge to a priority-respecting solution. Soft Q-decomposition (SQD), which concurrently learns on-arbiter-policy Q-functions for all subtasks, also fails to maintain the priority constraint due to the illusion-of-control problem. The SQL composition baseline (Haarnoja et al., 2018a), which simply sums pre-trained Q-functions, violates priorities in both the 2D and high-dimensional Panda settings, moving through the forbidden workspace and incurring high collision costs. PSQD's lower-priority subtask reward is slightly worse than baselines that cut through the obstacle, but this is the expected and correct trade-off under lexicographic priorities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Q-decomposition (Russell & Zimdars, 2003) for decomposed multi-objective RL and Soft Q-Learning / MaxEnt RL (Haarnoja et al., 2017, 2018b) for continuous-space policy learning, but departs from both by introducing a subtask transformation (log of constraint indicator functions) that scalarizes lexicographic priorities into a sum of transformed Q-functions. Prior lexicographic MORL methods (Skalse et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2022; Li & Czarnecki, 2019) are restricted to discrete action spaces or rely on constrained optimization that trades off subtasks, while SQL composition (Haarnoja et al., 2018a) handles continuous spaces but ignores priorities entirely. PSQD is positioned as the first algorithm to solve lexicographic MORL in continuous state-action spaces with a decomposed, interpretable, and reusable structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The scalarization result provides a principled, closed-form reward function for lexicographic MORL, resolving a long-standing ambiguity about what objective prior lexicographic algorithms actually optimize. The zero-shot composition and offline adaptation capabilities offer a practical pathway for building complex robot controllers from independently learned skills without retraining from scratch. Key limitations include the manual selection of ε thresholds (which are scale-dependent and domain-specific) and the reliance on pre-trained subtask Q-functions whose quality bounds the final solution. Open questions include automatic ε calibration, scalability to many subtasks with overlapping indifference spaces, and validation on more complex real-world robotic tasks beyond the 2D navigation and single-arm experiments presented.
