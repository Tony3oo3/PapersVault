## Problem

Existing 3D graph diffusion models operate directly in the high-dimensional product space of topology and geometry, which is arduous for score-based models to capture because real-world 3D graphs (e.g., molecules) lie on constrained low-dimensional manifolds. This paper asks the orthogonal question of *in what latent space* 3D graphs should be diffused, providing theoretical bounds showing that a lower-dimensional, low-reconstruction-error, symmetry-preserving latent space provably improves diffusion performance.

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, the model samples from a learned latent distribution and decodes into a 3D molecular graph (atom types, bond types, and 3D coordinates). For conditional generation, SE(3)-invariant quantum properties (polarizability, orbital energies, dipole moment, heat capacity) are fed as features to the score estimator, while for equivariant protein-binding generation, the full 3D protein structure is encoded jointly with the ligand and fed into the geometric decoder to produce a conformation that is equivariant to the protein's rigid-body pose. The latent space is a concatenation of a topological embedding (permutation-invariant) and a geometric embedding (SE(3)-invariant), both learned by pretrained autoencoders.

## Method

The core architecture is a cascaded 2D-3D graph autoencoder that decomposes the 3D graph into topology and geometry, training a hierarchical motif-based topological AE (permutation-invariant) and an E(n)-equivariant geometric AE (SE(3)-invariant) separately to disentangle the intertwined symmetry constraints and bypass the optimization barrier of a one-shot AE. A standard score-based diffusion model is then trained in the resulting low-dimensional Euclidean latent space, and sampling proceeds by drawing a latent code and cascading through the topological decoder followed by the geometric decoder (with teacher forcing during AE training). Theoretical analysis (Propositions 1–2) formalizes the trade-off: the TV bound in latent space improves over the original space only when latent dimensionality is reduced and reconstruction error is low, while the symmetry-preservation factor α is minimized by equivariant architectures. Graph self-supervised learning (contrastive node-dropping) is optionally added as an auxiliary multi-task objective during AE pretraining to regularize the latent space and improve out-of-distribution conditional generation.

## Results

On unconditional 3D molecule generation, the method achieves 100% chemical validity on both QM9 and Drugs (up to 181 atoms), with a mean validity score of 93.37, outperforming all 10+ baselines. In distribution matching, it achieves NLL of −3.48 (vs. EDM's −1.22) and lower total variation distances on 5 of 7 molecular property metrics. Training is 12× faster on QM9 (12 s vs. 146 s per epoch) and 34× faster on Drugs (10 min vs. 349 min) compared to EDM. For protein-binding generation on CrossDocked, it achieves the best QED (0.60), best diversity (0.80), and best top-10% Vina score (−12.34) while being 35× faster than TargetDiff.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method decisively outperforms all non-diffusion baselines (ENF, G-Schnet) and most diffusion baselines on validity: it reaches 100% valid molecules on both QM9 and Drugs, versus 91.9%/92.6% for EDM, 94.8%/– for GCDM, and 97.9%/78.0% for MiDi. Among the closest competitors, GeoLDM (93.8% QM9, 99.3% Drugs) and GCDM (94.8% QM9, 89.0% Drugs) trail on the mean validity score (93.08 and 92.30 vs. 93.37), with the gap driven by the method's perfect validity on both datasets. On distribution matching (Table 3), the method beats EDM on NLL (−3.48 vs. −1.22), MW (2.52 vs. 2.89), PSA (1.22 vs. 2.37), and energy (1.87 vs. 2.81), though it is slightly worse on ALogP and QED. For protein-binding generation (Table 6), the method achieves the best QED (0.60 vs. 0.56 for Pocket2Mol, 0.48 for TargetDiff) and best top-10% Vina score (−12.34 vs. −9.92 for DiffSBDD, −9.66 for TargetDiff), while trailing DecompDiff (64.4%) and TargetDiff (58.1%) on HiAff. Training speed is a consistent advantage: 12×/34× faster than EDM and 35× faster than TargetDiff, with the gap widening for larger molecules.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent diffusion paradigm (Rombach et al., 2022) from Euclidean image data to the non-Euclidean 3D graph domain, where prior work (EDM, GDM, MiDi, GCDM) has focused on building equivariant diffusion directly in the original topology-geometry product space. The key architectural departure is a cascaded 2D-3D autoencoder that disentangles permutation and SE(3) symmetry constraints into separate topological (Jin et al., 2020) and geometric (Zhu et al., 2022a) AEs, inspired by sub-band coding theory, rather than attempting a one-shot AE with intertwined symmetries. The theoretical analysis (Propositions 1–2) provides the first formal conditions under which latent-space diffusion provably outperforms original-space diffusion for 3D graphs, bridging the manifold hypothesis literature with equivariant generative modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 12–34× training speedup and 100% topological validity make latent 3D graph diffusion practically attractive for drug discovery pipelines where repeated retraining on new targets is common. The finding that graph self-supervised learning improves out-of-distribution generalization only when it increases latent-space homogeneity (semantic alignment) opens a concrete direction for designing property-specific regularization. However, the approach inherits the AE reconstruction bottleneck (geometric RMSE of 0.69 still limits conformational accuracy), and the authors acknowledge that explicit prior-based latent space regularization beyond GSSL remains an open problem. The method is also validated primarily on small-to-medium molecules (≤181 atoms), leaving scalability to macromolecular complexes untested.
