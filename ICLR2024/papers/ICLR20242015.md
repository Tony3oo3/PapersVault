## Problem

Prevailing multi-modal LLMs treat visual input as an unsupervised prompt and rely on a small adapter to bridge vision and language, leaving the LLM's reasoning capacity underutilized and restricting the model to image-to-text comprehension only. This inequitable treatment of modalities prevents unified multi-modal generation and comprehension under a single learning paradigm.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242015/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

LaVIT accepts 224×224 RGB images and text (or combinations thereof) as input. Images are converted into a dynamic-length sequence of discrete visual token IDs (averaging ~94 tokens, versus 256 for fixed-length tokenization) that share the same autoregressive vocabulary space as text tokens. Outputs include text captions, VQA answers, and generated images; the latter are produced by autoregressively sampling visual tokens and then decoding them through a conditional denoising U-Net (initialized from Stable Diffusion v1.5) to recover pixel-level detail.

## Method

The core contribution is a dynamic visual tokenizer comprising a token selector (a lightweight MLP with Gumbel-Softmax relaxation that decides which ViT patches carry informative semantics) and a token merger (L=12 stacked blocks of causal self-attention plus cross-attention that compresses unselected patches onto retained ones based on embedding similarity). The merged visual part features are quantized into discrete codes via a learnable codebook of size 16,384, and the tokenizer is trained to reconstruct the original ViT patch features using cosine similarity loss. In Stage-2, LLaMA-7B is jointly trained with a unified next-token prediction objective over interleaved visual and text token sequences; for image-conditioned text generation, continuous merger outputs are fed as input to preserve fine-grained detail while discrete codes serve as the prediction target. This design enables the LLM to treat images as a 'foreign language,' achieving both multi-modal understanding and generation without architectural modification.

## Results

LaVIT (LLaMA-7B) achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot performance across all evaluated vision-language understanding benchmarks: CIDEr 83.0 on Flickr30k, 114.2 on NoCaps, VQA accuracy of 66.0% on VQAv2, 54.6% on OKVQA, 46.8% on GQA, and 38.5% on VizWiz. For zero-shot text-to-image generation on MS-COCO-30K, LaVIT achieves an FID of 7.4, surpassing all other multimodal LLMs and approaching the dedicated text-to-image specialist Parti-20B (FID 7.2) while using only 0.2B vs. 2B training pairs. The dynamic visual tokenizer uses only ~94 tokens on average (vs. 256 for fixed-length), reducing pre-training time by 40% while improving downstream accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaVIT outperforms all 18 compared baselines on every benchmark where both methods report results. Against the strongest adapter-style baseline BLIP-2 (Vicuna-7B), LaVIT gains +8.1 CIDEr on Flickr30k (83.0 vs. 74.9), +6.7 on NoCaps (114.2 vs. 107.5), +5.5 on GQA (46.8 vs. 41.3), and +13.2 on VizWiz (38.5 vs. 25.3). Against the concurrent autoregressive method Emu (LLaMA-13B), which uses a larger model and 2.6B image-text pairs, LaVIT leads by +14.0 on VQAv2 (66.0 vs. 52.0), +16.4 on OKVQA (54.6 vs. 38.2), and +4.3 on VizWiz (38.5 vs. 34.2). On text-to-image generation (Table 2), LaVIT achieves FID 7.4, beating Emu (11.7) by 4.3 FID points and CM3Leon-7B (10.8) by 3.4, while remaining within 0.2 FID of the 20B-parameter Parti specialist. Against Flamingo-9B, LaVIT improves OKVQA by +9.9 (54.6 vs. 44.7) and VizWiz by +9.7 (38.5 vs. 28.8). The only method LaVIT does not surpass is Parti-20B on FID (7.4 vs. 7.2), but Parti is a dedicated 20B text-to-image model, not a general-purpose multimodal LLM.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaVIT departs from the dominant adapter-style MLLM paradigm (Flamingo, BLIP-2, Kosmos) where visual features are projected into a frozen LLM's space and images serve only as unsupervised prompts. It also differentiates from the concurrent Emu, which jointly trains vision and language in an LLM but uses a regression objective for visual features, creating an inconsistent optimization landscape. LaVIT synthesizes ideas from VQ-VAE/VQ-GAN (discrete codebook quantization), DynamicViT (learned token sparsification), and standard LLM next-token prediction into a single unified framework where both modalities are treated as discrete token sequences under one generative objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a truly unified discrete-token representation enables a single 7B model to perform both multi-modal understanding and generation at levels competitive with or exceeding much larger specialists (e.g., FID 7.4 vs. Parti-20B's 7.2), suggesting that architectural unification can substitute for raw scale. The dynamic token length (avg. 94 vs. 256) offers a practical 40% training speedup while improving accuracy, making the approach more deployable. Key limitations include inheritance of LLM hallucination, reliance on noisy web-scale captions, and restriction to 224×224 resolution; open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions, longer interleaved sequences, and additional modalities like video or audio.
