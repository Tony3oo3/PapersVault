## Problem

Existing DRL methods such as DR3 and InFeR unboundedly maximize the representation rank of value networks to combat capacity loss, but this indiscriminate maximization produces overly complex function approximators that overfit, demand more data, and degrade sample efficiency. No principled mechanism exists to adaptively control the representation rank to an appropriate level. This paper identifies the Bellman equation as an implicit constraint that naturally bounds the representation rank, providing a theoretical foundation for adaptive regularization.

## Input / Output

The value network takes a state-action pair (s, a) as input and produces a Q-value via the inner product of a penultimate-layer representation φ(s,a) ∈ ℝ^N and a final-layer weight vector w ∈ ℝ^N. The BEER regularizer operates on the cosine similarity between the current representation φ(s,a) and the expected next-step representation φ̄(s',a'), computed from the same batch of transitions. The regularizer is a scalar penalty added to the standard value approximation loss, with a single hyperparameter β (fixed at 1e-3 across all experiments) controlling its strength. The policy network outputs an action, while the value network outputs the Q-value used for both policy updates and the regularization computation.

## Method

The paper derives an upper bound on the inner product (and hence cosine similarity) between representations of consecutive state-action pairs directly from the Bellman equation (Theorem 1), showing that the bound depends on the discount factor γ, the norms of the representations, and the ratio ‖r‖²/‖w‖². This bound is then imposed as a hard constraint in the value function optimization, which is converted into a ReLU-based penalty regularizer (BEER) that activates only when the cosine similarity exceeds the derived upper bound. Unlike DR3 and InFeR, which unboundedly maximize rank, BEER adaptively reduces similarity (thereby increasing rank) only when the Bellman-derived constraint is violated, leaving the value approximation loss to govern optimization when the bound is already satisfied. The regularizer is integrated into the critic update of DQN (for discrete actions) or DPG (for continuous actions) without adding any learnable parameters, and is validated on 12 challenging DMControl tasks where it outperforms DR3, InFeR, SAC, and TD3 by large margins.

## Results

On 12 challenging DeepMind Control (DMC) tasks, BEER achieves an average peak score of 535.8 over 1M steps, outperforming DR3 (223.3), InFeR (272.2), SAC (335.0), and TD3 (265.4) by 140%, 96.8%, 60%, and 101.9% respectively. On the Lunar Lander illustrative task, BEER exhibits a lower approximation error than both DQN and InFeR in the latter training stage (0.9–1×40K steps) while maintaining a more balanced representation rank. On the Grid World task, BEER achieves the highest representation rank among tested algorithms, the lowest approximation error, and the fastest convergence to the terminal state.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BEER is compared against five baselines across discrete and continuous control tasks. Against DR3 (Kumar et al., 2021), which unboundedly maximizes representation rank via inner-product regularization, BEER achieves 140% higher average scores on the 12 challenging DMC tasks (535.8 vs. 223.3), with particularly large gaps on Acrobot-Swingup (260.5 vs. 46.2) and Humanoid-Run (107.1 vs. 1.1). Against InFeR (Lyle et al., 2022), which maximizes rank through an auxiliary task, BEER outperforms by 96.8% on average and shows substantially lower approximation error on both Lunar Lander and DMC tasks. Against the strong off-policy baselines SAC (Haarnoja et al., 2018) and TD3 (Fujimoto et al., 2018), BEER wins by 60% and 101.9% respectively, despite using only pure DPG without TD3's double-Q clipping or target policy smoothing. On the simpler Lunar Lander and Grid World tasks, BEER (integrated with DQN) outperforms DQN (Mnih et al., 2015) in both approximation error and final performance, while also achieving a more balanced representation rank than InFeR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of work on representation rank in DRL (DR3, InFeR) that unboundedly maximizes rank, instead deriving an upper bound on cosine similarity between consecutive state-action representations directly from the Bellman equation. The theoretical foundation builds on the matrix form of the Bellman equation (Agarwal et al., 2019) and the representation rank concept (Kumar et al., 2021; Lyle et al., 2022), but reframes the problem from 'maximize rank' to 'adaptively constrain rank within Bellman-implied limits.' Practically, the BEER regularizer is integrated into standard value-based algorithms (DQN for discrete, DPG for continuous), positioning it as a drop-in penalty rather than a new algorithmic architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key insight that unbounded rank maximization can degrade performance by inducing over-complexity is a useful corrective to the prior literature's emphasis on rank as a purely beneficial quantity. The Bellman-derived bound provides a principled, parameter-free criterion (beyond a single coefficient β) for when to stop increasing rank, which is more interpretable than heuristic regularization. Limitations include: the bound involves expectations over next-state representations that must be approximated in practice, the method is only validated on value-based RL (not model-based or offline settings), and the comparison with TD3/SAC is somewhat confounded since BEER uses pure DPG without standard tricks like double Q or target policy smoothing. Open questions include whether the bound remains informative in high-dimensional or model-based settings and whether the adaptive control generalizes beyond the specific cosine-similarity formulation.
