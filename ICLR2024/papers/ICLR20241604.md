## Problem

Existing subject-driven image generation methods either require per-subject test-time fine-tuning (e.g., DreamBooth, Textual Inversion) or cannot accept interleaved multi-image and text input in a unified sequence. This prevents the broader vision of treating images as a 'foreign language' that a generation model can natively understand and compose with text, especially for multi-entity scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241604/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

KOSMOS-G accepts a single interleaved sequence where text tokens and image embeddings (from a CLIP ViT-L/14 at 224×224) are mixed using <image>/</image> delimiters, with each named entity in the caption followed by its corresponding segmented image. The output is a 512×512 RGB image produced by the frozen Stable Diffusion v1.5 U-Net and VAE decoder, meaning the model only needs to produce condition embeddings compatible with the existing CLIP text encoder interface.

## Method

KOSMOS-G follows a three-stage 'align before instruct' pipeline: (1) multimodal language modeling pre-trains a 1.6B-parameter Transformer MLLM (MAGNETO backbone) on interleaved vision-language data; (2) an AlignerNet (a 225M-parameter encoder-decoder Transformer with learned latent queries) maps the MLLM's variable-length output space to the CLIP text encoder space using text-only captions as an anchor, so image embeddings are aligned implicitly; (3) compositional instruction tuning fine-tunes both MLLM and AlignerNet jointly using a score distillation objective where the frozen U-Net provides differentiable gradients, training the model to faithfully reproduce entity contents from segmented images in new contexts. Because no U-Net parameters are modified, KOSMOS-G can be dropped in as a direct CLIP replacement, enabling immediate compatibility with ControlNet, LoRA, and other U-Net-level techniques.

## Results

On DreamBench (single-entity subject-driven generation), zero-shot KOSMOS-G achieves CLIP-I 0.847, DINO 0.694, and CLIP-T 0.287, with CLIP-I being the highest among all compared methods including fine-tuning approaches. On MS-COCO text-to-image generation, KOSMOS-G-1.9B achieves FID 10.99, outperforming other CLIP-aligned vision-language-to-image models (GILL-8B: 12.20, Emu-14B: 11.66) but trailing dedicated T2I models like SDv1.5 (9.34) and Imagen-3.4B (7.27).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DreamBench, KOSMOS-G (zero-shot, single image input) achieves the best CLIP-I score (0.847) among all methods, surpassing fine-tuning baselines DreamBooth (0.803) and BLIP-Diffusion (0.805) by ~4–5 points, as well as the zero-shot SuTI (0.819). However, KOSMOS-G trails SuTI on DINO (0.694 vs. 0.741) and CLIP-T (0.287 vs. 0.304), and also lags behind DreamBooth and BLIP-Diffusion on CLIP-T (0.287 vs. 0.305/0.302). KOSMOS-G clearly outperforms Textual Inversion (0.569/0.780/0.255), Re-Imagen (0.600/0.740/0.270), and zero-shot BLIP-Diffusion (0.594/0.779/0.300) on all three DreamBench metrics. On MS-COCO, KOSMOS-G-1.9B (FID 10.99) beats the other CLIP-aligned VL2I models GILL-8B (12.20) and Emu-14B (11.66) but remains behind the dedicated T2I models SDv1.5 (9.34) and Imagen-3.4B (7.27), which are not subject-driven generation models.

## Relation to Prior Work

KOSMOS-G builds directly on KOSMOS-1's multimodal language modeling backbone and the Stable Diffusion v1.5 U-Net as a frozen image decoder, departing from prior subject-driven generation methods (DreamBooth, Textual Inversion) that require per-subject test-time fine-tuning. Its key architectural departure is replacing the CLIP text encoder with an MLLM via a novel AlignerNet (inspired by GlueGen) that aligns the MLLM's output space to CLIP's embedding space using text as an anchor, then performing compositional instruction tuning with score distillation (borrowed from DreamFusion). Unlike BLIP-Diffusion or SuTI, which handle single-entity or fixed-template inputs, KOSMOS-G natively supports interleaved multi-image and text prompts through the MLLM's autoregressive architecture, positioning it as the first model to treat images as a 'foreign language' in the generation pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The seamless CLIP-replacement property is the paper's most practically significant contribution: because no U-Net parameters are modified, KOSMOS-G can be dropped into any existing diffusion ecosystem (ControlNet, community LoRAs, custom decoders) without retraining, potentially unlocking a large space of new applications. The zero-shot multi-entity subject-driven generation capability addresses a genuine gap—prior methods either require fine-tuning or cannot handle multiple reference images in a single prompt. Limitations include reliance on the older SD v1.5 backbone, modest quantitative gains over fine-tuning baselines on single-entity benchmarks (DINO 0.694 vs. DreamBooth's 0.668), and the alignment being constrained to CLIP's 768-dim space, which may cap expressiveness. Open questions include whether this approach scales to higher-resolution decoders, whether the CLIP-space bottleneck limits fidelity for complex multi-entity compositions, and whether the compositional instruction tuning data pipeline (BLIP-2 captions + MPT-7B entity extraction + CLIPSeg segmentation) can be improved to reduce error propagation.
