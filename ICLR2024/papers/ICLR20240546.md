## Problem

Diffusion models using noise-prediction or v-prediction objectives exhibit infinite Lipschitz constants with respect to the time variable near t=0, caused by dσ_t/dt → ∞ under standard noise schedules (linear, quadratic, cosine). This singularity threatens training stability and inference accuracy—particularly for fast samplers like DDIM and DPM-Solver that rely on network smoothness for integration—yet the well-posedness of the diffusion process itself had received limited prior attention.

## Input / Output

The U-Net network receives a noisy image x_t = α_t x_0 + σ_t ε (256×256 RGB) and a timestep condition as input, outputting a noise estimate ε_θ. The key modification in E-TSDM is that the timestep condition fed to the network is not the raw t value but a quantized value f_T(t) that maps each t in [0, t̃) to the left boundary of its sub-interval, while t ≥ t̃ passes through unchanged. For the super-resolution variant, a 64×64 low-resolution image is additionally concatenated as input to produce a 256×256 output.

## Method

E-TSDM (Early Timestep-shared Diffusion Model) uniformly divides the near-zero interval [0, t̃) into n equal sub-intervals and assigns a single shared timestep condition (the left endpoint) to all timesteps within each sub-interval, during both training and inference. This forces the Lipschitz constant to zero within each sub-interval, eliminating the singularity without any architectural modification, loss-function change, or additional computational cost. The training loss replaces the raw timestep t with f_T(t) = max{t_{i-1} ∈ T : t_{i-1} ≤ t} for t < t̃, while the reverse sampling process uses the same mapping. Theorem 4.1 provides an error bound of σ_t̃ K(x)Δt + B(x)Δσ_max, showing the introduced approximation error converges at least at 1/2-order in Δt = t̃/n, making it controllable. Default hyperparameters are t̃=100 and n=5, and the method generalizes across noise schedules, continuous-time formulations, v-prediction, and latent diffusion models.

## Results

On FFHQ 256×256, E-TSDM-large achieves a FID-50k of 4.22, surpassing DDPM (6.88), LDM (4.98), and Soft Truncation (5.54), approaching StyleGAN2+ADA+bCR (3.62). Across five noise schedules (linear, quadratic, cosine, cosine-shift, zero-terminal-SNR) in both discrete and continuous settings, E-TSDM consistently reduces FID-10k by 2–9 points over the DDPM baseline (e.g., 6.62 vs. 9.50 on linear, 9.69 vs. 13.79 on quadratic). For fast sampling, E-TSDM yields over 33% FID reduction with DPM-Solver and DDIM (e.g., DPM-Solver-3 at NFE=50: 13.52 vs. 24.48; DDIM at NFE=200: 13.71 vs. 23.16).

## Baselines & Comparisons

E-TSDM outperforms the DDPM baseline (Ho et al., 2020) on every evaluated dataset and noise schedule, with FID-10k improvements ranging from ~1 point (cosine: 26.08 vs. 27.17) to ~4 points (linear: 6.62 vs. 9.50) on FFHQ. Against LDM (Rombach et al., 2022), E-TSDM-large achieves a lower FID-50k (4.22 vs. 4.98) on FFHQ, and when applied directly to LDM, E-TSDM further reduces FID-50k from 4.98 to 4.61. E-TSDM-large also beats Soft Truncation (Kim et al., 2021; 5.54) and P2-DM (Choi et al., 2022; 6.92) on FFHQ FID-50k, though it does not surpass the GAN-based StyleGAN2+ADA+bCR (Karras et al., 2020; 3.62). For fast sampling, E-TSDM combined with DPM-Solver-3 at NFE=50 achieves FID-10k of 13.52 versus 24.48 for DDPM (a 45% reduction), and with DDIM at NFE=200 achieves 13.71 versus 23.16 (a 41% reduction), confirming the paper's claim of >33% FID improvement for acceleration methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) and the score-based SDE formulation (Song et al., 2021b), but departs from prior numerical stability work (Song et al., 2021a; Vahdat et al., 2021; Kim et al., 2022) that addressed the singularity of the transition kernel q_{0t} near t=0. The key distinction is that this paper identifies a different and previously overlooked source of instability: the infinite Lipschitz constants of the noise-prediction network ε_θ(x,t) with respect to the time variable, caused by dσ_t/dt → ∞ as t→0. The proposed E-TSDM method also contrasts with alternative approaches like regularization, noise schedule modification, and remapping, which the paper shows to be less effective or to introduce other problems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of Lipschitz singularities as a fundamental issue in diffusion models opens a new axis of analysis for understanding why diffusion processes behave as they do, complementing the well-studied transition kernel singularity. The practical impact is significant: a trivially simple fix (sharing timestep conditions in a few sub-intervals, with no architecture or loss changes) yields substantial FID improvements across datasets and a 33%+ reduction in FID for fast samplers like DDIM and DPM-Solver. Open questions include whether the approach scales to large-scale text-to-image models, whether forcing zero Lipschitz within sub-intervals introduces subtle degradation, and whether the previously unexplained phenomenon of baseline performance worsening with more NFE is indeed caused by Lipschitz issues.
