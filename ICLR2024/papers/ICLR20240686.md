## Problem

Existing 3D molecular denoising pre-training methods (coordinate denoising and fractional denoising) rely on ad-hoc noise designs that assume isotropic or partially isotropic force fields, causing the learned force fields to deviate significantly from true molecular physics. This physical inconsistency limits the generalization and robustness of the resulting molecular representations on downstream tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240686/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D equilibrium molecular conformations (Cartesian coordinates) along with per-bond, per-angle, and per-torsion force field parameters (k^B, k^A, k^T, ω) sourced from Open Force Field v2.0.0. During pre-training, the network outputs atom-wise Cartesian force field predictions (3N-dimensional vectors); during fine-tuning, it produces molecular property predictions. A key implementation detail is that the regression target is computed via random slicing—projecting the force field onto N_v random Gaussian vectors—rather than explicitly forming the Jacobian of the Cartesian-to-relative coordinate transformation, making the target computable with RDKit in O(max{N, M, F}) per molecule.

## Method

SliDe derives a quadratic energy function E_BAT from classical mechanical intramolecular potential (bond stretching, angle bending, torsion) and uses it to define BAT noise: independent Gaussian perturbations on every bond length, angle, and torsion angle with variances set by the corresponding force field parameters, yielding a sampling distribution that closely approximates the true Boltzmann conformation distribution. The denoising objective regresses the Cartesian force field ∇_x E_BAT, but since E_BAT is defined in relative coordinates, a Jacobian of the coordinate transformation would normally be required; SliDe circumvents this via a random slicing technique (Lemma 3.2) that replaces the full Jacobian computation with N_v scalar projections computed through simple coordinate noise additions and RDKit relative-coordinate lookups. The network is a Geometric Equivariant Transformer (GET) that extends TorchMD-NET with edge updates incorporating Bessel-embedded bond lengths and spherical-harmonic-embedded angles/torsions, feeding angular information into the attention-based vertex update. A regularization term [GNN(x+τv)−τv]² is added during pre-training to align with the Noisy Nodes auxiliary task used at fine-tuning.

## Results

SliDe achieves a 42% improvement in force field correlation coefficient (0.895) over Frad (0.631) and Coord (0.616) when compared against DFT ground-truth forces on 1000 PCQM4Mv2 molecules. On QM9, SliDe sets new state-of-the-art MAE on 10 of 12 properties, reducing mean MAE by 12.4% over the previous best (Frad), with particularly strong gains on energetic/thermodynamic tasks (e.g., U₀: 4.28 meV vs 5.33 meV for Frad). On MD17 force prediction (1000-sample setting), SliDe achieves the best MAE on 5 of 8 molecules (e.g., Aspirin: 0.1740 vs 0.2087 for Frad). On ANI-1x energy prediction, SliDe yields a 42.3% pre-training improvement (1.362→0.786 kcal/mol) versus 32.7% for the Noneq baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SliDe outperforms both prior denoising methods (Coord and Frad) on force field correlation (0.895 vs 0.616/0.631) and on 10 of 12 QM9 properties, with the largest margins on energetic tasks (U₀: 4.28 vs 5.33 meV; H: 4.26 vs 5.55 meV). On MD17 force prediction, SliDe beats Frad on Aspirin (0.1740 vs 0.2087), Ethanol (0.0882 vs 0.0910), Naphthalene (0.0483 vs 0.0530), and Salicylic Acid (0.1006 vs 0.1081), but loses on Malonaldehyde (0.1538 vs 0.1415) and Uracil (0.0825 vs 0.0760). Against supervised baselines, SliDe surpasses TorchMD-NET (ET) on all QM9 properties and on most MD17 molecules, and outperforms SphereNet, PaiNN, DimeNet++, E(n)-GNN, and SchNet across the board. On ANI-1x, SliDe's 42.3% pre-training improvement exceeds Noneq's 32.7%, and SliDe also outperforms Noneq without pre-training (1.362 vs 1.50 kcal/mol). The only QM9 property where SliDe does not lead is R² (0.3405 vs Frad's 0.3419 and Coord's 0.4496), and ZPVE where SphereNet (1.120) and TM (1.18) remain stronger.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the denoising-based molecular pre-training lineage initiated by coordinate denoising (Zaidi et al., 2023) and fractional denoising (Feng et al., 2023), both of which established the equivalence between denoising and force field learning but relied on coarse-grained, ad-hoc noise designs. SliDe departs from this line by grounding the noise distribution and regression target in the classical mechanical intramolecular potential (bond stretching, angle bending, torsion), yielding a fine-grained BAT noise that distinguishes atom and bond types. Architecturally, it extends TorchMD-NET (Thölke & Fabritiis, 2022) with edge updates and angular embeddings to form the Geometric Equivariant Transformer (GET), and introduces a random slicing technique (inspired by sliced Wasserstein distance) to avoid explicit Jacobian computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 42% improvement in force field correlation over prior denoising methods, combined with the consistent ordering of physical consistency matching downstream performance (SliDe > Frad > Coord), provides strong evidence that physical alignment in pre-training is a meaningful axis of improvement for molecular representations. The sliced denoising formulation is computationally efficient (O(max{N,M,F}) per molecule via RDKit) and the hyperparameters can be tuned without training neural networks, making the approach practical. Limitations include the omission of electrostatic and van der Waals terms, reliance on equilibrium conformations, and the quadratic approximation of torsion potentials; open questions include whether the framework extends to intermolecular interactions, non-equilibrium pre-training at scale, and whether the physical consistency gap to DFT-supervised force fields (noted in Appendix B.5) can be further closed.
