## Problem

Instruction-based image editing methods like InstructPix2Pix rely on brief, ambiguous human commands (e.g., 'make it more healthy') and a frozen CLIP text encoder trained for static descriptions, making it difficult to capture the essential visual transformation intended by the user. The resulting guidance is insufficient to steer the diffusion model toward the correct editing goal, especially for global optimization and local modifications where context matters.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB input image and a short natural-language editing instruction (e.g., 'add contrast to simulate more light') and produces a single edited image at the same resolution. Internally, the MLLM first derives a concise expressive instruction (typically under 32 tokens) that explicitly describes the intended visual change, which is then converted into latent visual guidance tokens (N=8 [IMG] tokens) consumed by the diffusion model's cross-attention layers.

## Method

MGIE couples an MLLM (initialized from LLaVA-7B) with a latent diffusion model (initialized from StableDiffusion-v1.5) in an end-to-end pipeline. The MLLM, given the input image and instruction, generates a concise expressive instruction via cross-entropy loss, then produces N trainable [IMG] tokens whose hidden states are mapped by a 4-layer Transformer edit head into a latent guidance vector U. This U is injected into the UNet via cross-attention (as K and V) alongside the input image latent (concatenated with the noisy target), while classifier-free guidance with separate scales for image (α_V=1.5) and instruction (α_X=7.5) balances preservation and editing. Only the MLLM's word embeddings and LM head are trainable, keeping the bulk of the LLM frozen for parameter efficiency, and the overall objective jointly optimizes the instruction derivation loss and the diffusion denoising loss (L_all = L_ins + 0.5·L_edit).

## Results

In the fine-tuned setting (Table 2), MGIE achieves the best performance across all four datasets: on MA5k (global photo optimization), it reduces L1 to 0.082 and LPIPS to 0.235 while raising SSIM to 72.91; on MagicBrush (local editing), it reaches 90.65 DINO, 95.28 CVS, and 31.73 CTS. In the zero-shot setting (Table 1), MGIE similarly leads on GIER (SSIM 59.24, CVS 88.59) and MagicBrush (DINO 82.22, CVS 91.14, CTS 30.40). Inference cost is competitive: 9.2 s per image at batch size 1 on a single A100 GPU, versus 6.8 s for InsPix2Pix.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is InsPix2Pix (Brooks et al., CVPR 2023), which uses a frozen CLIP text encoder to condition a diffusion model on brief editing instructions. MGIE consistently outperforms InsPix2Pix on every metric across all four datasets in both zero-shot and fine-tuned settings; the largest gains appear on MA5k (SSIM 72.91 vs. 67.12, LPIPS 0.235 vs. 0.267) and MagicBrush (DINO 90.65 vs. 87.99, CVS 95.28 vs. 93.83), where visual-aware expressive instructions resolve ambiguity that CLIP's static-description training cannot capture. The paper also introduces an internal ablation baseline, LGIE, which uses LLaMA-7B to generate expressive instructions from text alone (no image input). MGIE beats LGIE on all metrics in every setting, confirming that visual perception is essential: for example, on GIER fine-tuned SSIM, MGIE reaches 68.68 versus LGIE's 65.30, and on MagicBrush fine-tuned DINO, MGIE scores 90.65 versus LGIE's 88.09. Human evaluation further corroborates these results, with over 53% of annotators preferring MGIE's expressive instructions for practicality and 57% for reduced hallucination compared to LGIE.

## Relation to Prior Work

MGIE builds directly on the InstructPix2Pix framework (CLIP text encoder + latent diffusion with classifier-free guidance) but replaces the frozen CLIP encoder with a trainable MLLM (initialized from LLaVA-7B) that jointly perceives the input image and the editing instruction. The [IMG] token mechanism and edit head design are adapted from GILL, which used similar visual tokens for image generation from scratch, here repurposed to condition a diffusion model on an existing image. The key conceptual departure is that prior instruction-based editing methods treat the brief human command as the sole textual signal, whereas MGIE inserts an intermediate 'expressive instruction' derivation step where the MLLM translates ambiguous commands into explicit, visually-grounded descriptions before the diffusion model acts on them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that visual-aware guidance is not merely helpful but consistently superior to language-only guidance across all four editing benchmarks and all three architectural ablations (frozen, fine-tuned, end-to-end), establishing MLLM-in-the-loop as a viable paradigm for instruction-based editing. A notable limitation is that the MLLM component (LLaVA-7B) adds ~3 seconds of inference overhead and requires 40GB GPU memory, which may constrain deployment. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video editing, whether lighter MLLMs (1–3B) retain the visual-perception advantage, and whether the end-to-end training signal is sufficient for more complex multi-step editing chains.
