## Problem

While many structural and positional encodings have been proposed to improve GNN performance, the question of which structural properties yield the most effective encoding remains open. Existing approaches (spectral encodings, substructure counts, random-walk distances) encode different geometric information but lack a principled geometric perspective on what makes an encoding effective, and it is unclear whether local structural and global positional encodings capture complementary information.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard graph G=(V,E) with node feature matrix X∈R^{|V|×m} and computes, for each node v, a 5-dimensional Local Curvature Profile vector [min, max, mean, std, median] of the Ollivier-Ricci curvature values over all edges incident to v. This vector is concatenated to the original node features as a preprocessing step before feeding the augmented graph into any downstream GNN (GCN, GIN, or GAT), which then produces node or graph class labels. The curvature computation requires solving an optimal transport problem per edge with complexity O(|E|·d_max³).

## Method

The paper introduces Local Curvature Profiles (LCP), a local structural encoding that characterizes each node's neighborhood geometry via summary statistics of the Ollivier-Ricci curvature (ORC) of its incident edges. ORC is computed by measuring the Wasserstein-1 distance between uniform probability measures on the 1-hop neighborhoods of adjacent nodes, normalized by their graph distance. The key insight is that curvature information across the full distribution (not just extremes) is more informative than what curvature-based rewiring exploits, since rewiring only modifies the most positively or negatively curved edges. The paper proves LCP strictly improves expressivity beyond the 1-WL test using the 4×4 Rooke and Shrikhande graphs as a separating pair, and empirically shows LCP outperforms Laplacian eigenvectors, random-walk encodings, substructure counts, and curvature-based rewiring (BORF) across graph and node classification tasks. Combining LCP with global positional encodings (Laplacian eigenvectors or random-walk transition probabilities) yields further gains, confirming that local structural and global positional information are complementary.

## Results

On graph classification, LCP achieves the highest accuracy across all four datasets and all three GNN architectures (GCN, GIN, GAT). For GCN, LCP yields 35.4% on Enzymes, 67.7% on Imdb, 79.0% on Mutag, and 70.9% on Proteins, representing gains of 10–20 percentage points over no encoding and 4–14 points over the second-best method (SUB). On node classification (Cora, Citeseer), LCP remains competitive (e.g., 88.9% on Cora with GCN) but gains are smaller and other encodings occasionally match or exceed it. Combining LCP with global positional encodings (LA or RW) further improves performance on most datasets, with the largest joint gain being +7 points on Mutag (GCN, LCP+RW: 86.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LCP outperforms all three structural/positional encoding baselines (Laplacian eigenvectors LA, Random Walk RW, and substructure counts SUB) on every graph classification dataset across all three architectures, with the largest margins on Imdb (+14–19 pts over LA/RW/SUB for GCN) and Mutag (+8–10 pts). Against curvature-based rewiring (BORF, Nguyen et al. 2023), LCP achieves significantly higher accuracy on all six main datasets; for example, GCN(LCP) reaches 35.4% on Enzymes versus 26.0% for GCN on BORF-rewired graphs, and 79.0% on Mutag versus 68.2%. On node classification, LCP beats the Curvature Graph Network (Ye et al. 2020) by a wide margin: GCN(LCP) achieves 88.9% on Cora and 77.1% on Citeseer versus 83.1% and 71.7% for CurvGN-1. However, on node classification LCP does not uniformly dominate the positional encodings—GIN(LA) slightly edges out GIN(LCP) on Cora (80.1% vs. 79.8%), and GAT(RW) matches GAT(LCP) on Citeseer (75.8% vs. 76.6% within confidence intervals). The paper also shows that combining LCP with RW or LA positional encodings yields the best overall results in most settings, confirming that local structural and global positional information are complementary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Ollivier's Ricci curvature (ORC), previously applied in GNNs for graph rewiring (Topping et al., Nguyen et al., Fesser & Weber) or as message-passing weights (Ye et al., Lai et al.), into a node-level structural encoding. The key conceptual departure is shifting from using curvature to modify graph topology or attention mechanisms to using it as a static feature: the Local Curvature Profile (LCP) summarizes the curvature distribution of a node's incident edges into five statistics. It positions itself alongside other structural encodings (substructure counts, degree distributions) and positional encodings (Laplacian eigenvectors, random walks), arguing that curvature-based local structural information is complementary to global positional information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that LCP outperforms curvature-based rewiring on all tested datasets challenges the assumption that modifying graph structure is more effective than augmenting node features with geometric information, and suggests that the local nature of curvature is better preserved when encoded per-node rather than used to make global edge-level decisions. The demonstrated complementarity between local structural and global positional encodings provides a practical recipe for combining encoding types. Open questions include the precise relationship between ORC and the k-WL hierarchy (the paper shows LCP distinguishes graphs that 2-WL and 3-WL cannot), the optimal curvature notion for different graph topologies, and the high computational cost of exact ORC (O(|E|d_max^3)) which limits scalability to large or dense graphs.
