## Problem

Existing federated causal discovery methods are constrained by either identifiable functional causal model assumptions (e.g., linear Gaussian, additive noise) or homogeneous data distributions across clients. In practice, data is often heterogeneous across domains due to different interventions, collection conditions, or environments, and the underlying causal mechanisms may follow arbitrary functional forms. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a constraint-based FCD method that simultaneously handles arbitrary causal models and heterogeneous data without requiring identifiable functional forms.

## Input / Output

The method operates on horizontally-partitioned data where each of K clients holds n_k samples over the same d features, and the client/domain index U serves as a discrete surrogate variable appended to the variable set (yielding d'=d+1 variables). Each client transmits only its local sample size and a covariance tensor of dimension d'×d'×h×h (where h is the number of random Fourier features) to the server in a single communication round; no raw data or iterative parameter exchange is required. The output is a fully oriented causal DAG over the d observed variables, with the surrogate variable used internally for skeleton discovery and direction determination but removed from the final graph.

## Method

FedCDH introduces a surrogate variable U (the client/domain index) to model distribution shifts across heterogeneous clients, then performs skeleton discovery via a Federated Conditional Independence Test (FCIT) and direction determination via a Federated Independent Change Principle (FICP), both operating solely on aggregated summary statistics. The key technical mechanism is the use of random Fourier features to approximate Gaussian (for continuous variables) and delta (for the discrete surrogate variable) kernels, which transforms the inherently indecomposable n×n kernel matrices into h×h covariance matrices that are sample-wise decomposable across clients (i.e., the global covariance tensor is simply the sum of local ones). FCIT approximates the conditional independence test statistic using ridge-regression residuals and derives a decomposable asymptotic null distribution approximated by a two-parameter Gamma distribution. FICP estimates normalized HSIC between changing causal models using specially-designed covariance matrices constructed from the same summary statistics, exploiting causal asymmetry to orient edges where both endpoints have changing causal modules. Both submodules are nonparametric, making no assumption on the functional form of causal relationships.

## Results

FedCDH is evaluated on synthetic datasets (linear Gaussian and general functional models) with d ∈ {6, 12, 18, 24, 30} variables, K ∈ {2, 4, 8, 16, 32} clients, and n_k ∈ {25, 50, 100, 200, 400} samples per client, as well as two real-world datasets (fMRI Hippocampus with d=6 brain regions over 84 days, and HK Stock Market with d=10 stocks). Performance is measured via Skeleton F1, Skeleton SHD, Direction F1, and Direction SHD. The paper reports that FedCDH generally outperforms all baselines across all settings, with performance improving as sample size increases. No single numerical summary table is provided in the main text; results are presented as line plots (Figure 3 for linear Gaussian, Figure A3 for general functional models).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedCDH is compared against four FCD baselines: NOTEARS-ADMM (linear optimization-based), NOTEARS-MLP-ADMM (nonlinear optimization-based), FedDAG (nonlinear optimization-based with FedAvg), and FedPC (constraint-based with voting). FedCDH generally outperforms all four baselines on both skeleton and direction metrics (F1 and SHD) across varying numbers of variables, clients, and samples. The key advantage over NOTEARS-ADMM and NOTEARS-MLP-ADMM is that FedCDH does not require identifiable functional causal models (e.g., linear Gaussian or additive noise), handling arbitrary nonlinear functions. Compared to FedDAG, FedCDH avoids convergence and nonconvexity issues inherent in continuous optimization. Against FedPC, FedCDH's main advantage is its ability to handle heterogeneous data via the surrogate variable U, whereas FedPC assumes homogeneous distributions and uses a simple voting mechanism. On the real-world fMRI Hippocampus and HK Stock Market datasets, FedCDH also demonstrates superior performance, confirming its applicability to practical heterogeneous scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the centralized heterogeneous causal discovery framework of CD-NOD (Huang et al., 2020), which introduced the surrogate variable and independent change principle, into the federated setting. It departs from existing federated causal discovery methods (NOTEARS-ADMM, FedDAG, FedPC) by replacing iterative optimization or voting mechanisms with a single-round summary-statistics protocol built on random Fourier features, making the kernel-based conditional independence test and independent change principle decomposable across clients. The key conceptual departure is treating data heterogeneity not as a nuisance to be averaged away but as a signal that aids causal direction identification, while simultaneously protecting privacy through covariance tensors rather than raw data sharing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practically important gap: real-world federated data is often both decentralized and heterogeneous (e.g., hospitals with different patient populations), yet prior FCD methods required either homogeneous distributions or identifiable causal model assumptions. The single-round communication protocol and nonparametric design broaden applicability, but the method is limited to horizontally-partitioned data and requires sufficiently large sample sizes for the asymptotic null distribution to be reliable. Open questions include extending to vertically-partitioned settings, handling time-varying (rather than domain-indexed) heterogeneity, and incorporating secure computation without prohibitive overhead.
