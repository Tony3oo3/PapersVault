## Problem

Backpropagation's reliance on the chain rule forces recursive gradient computation, constraining both network architecture design and training pipeline flexibility on modern hardware. Existing non-BP alternatives (forward-forward, evolution strategies, feedback alignment, HSIC bottleneck) either lack scalability across architectures, suffer from instability at high parameter dimensionality, or cannot handle weight-sharing in convolutional and recurrent layers. The paper seeks a unified, architecture-agnostic gradient estimation method that requires only a single forward propagation and can be applied at arbitrary granularity (layer, neuron, or computation-graph segment).

## Input / Output

The method consumes a modular neural network (any architecture decomposed into L modules), a training dataset, a loss function, and per-module noise distribution parameters (e.g., Gaussian covariance). It produces an unbiased gradient estimate for each module's parameters using only the final scalar loss value and the local module input x^l and injected noise z^l — no intermediate activations or backward-pass tensors are needed. Because gradient computation is fully localized per module, estimates for different layers can be computed in parallel and combined into a single update, or used for independent module-level updates (e.g., fine-tuning only inserted blocks in a pre-trained network).

## Method

The core mechanism is a generalized push-out likelihood ratio (ULR) estimator: by injecting zero-mean noise z^l into each module's output, the parameter θ^l is pushed out of the loss function and into the conditional density of the module output, yielding ∇_{θ^l} E[L] = E[−L · D_{θ^l}^T φ^l · ∇_z ln f^l(z^l)], which requires only one forward pass and no recursive chain-rule computation. The paper derives explicit estimators for CNNs (via convolution operators), RNNs (summing per-timestep estimates to avoid BPTT), GNNs (perturbing feature extraction), and SNNs (excluding the discontinuous spike from the module). Three variance-reduction techniques — layer-wise noise injection (stochastic block coordinate descent), antithetic variable sampling, and quasi-Monte Carlo noise generation — make the estimator practical at scale. Additionally, ULR enables computation graph rearrangement: inserting noise at a chosen module splits a single recursive BP pass into two parallelizable subgraph gradient computations, which is demonstrated for BPTT decoupling in RNNs and for skipping frozen layers during domain-adaptation fine-tuning.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, ULR-WL achieves 64.8% clean accuracy on ResNet-5 (vs. 64.4% for BP) and 77.3% on VGG-8 (vs. 79.8% for BP), with an average robustness improvement of 9.53% over BP across natural noise, adversarial attacks, and distribution shift. On the larger CIFAR-100 dataset, ULR-WL reaches 64.9% (vs. 65.2% BP, 27.3% ES). For domain adaptation on Office-31, ULR achieves 1.2% higher accuracy and 1.1× speedup over BP, while BPTT rearrangement in RNNs yields up to 4.9% accuracy gain and 3.7× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ULR-WL matches or slightly exceeds BP on clean data (64.8% vs. 64.4% on ResNet-5; 77.3% vs. 79.8% on VGG-8) while delivering a substantial 9.53% average robustness improvement across all corruption types. ES (Salimans et al., 2017) fails catastrophically on several architectures—scoring only 35.1% on ResNet-5 and 34.4% on GAT—while ULR-WL remains competitive with BP, demonstrating far superior gradient estimation stability. FF (Hinton, 2022) fails entirely to optimize ResNet-5 due to the weight-sharing incompatibility, whereas ULR handles shared parameters naturally. In the broader comparison (Fig. 10), ULR-WL surpasses the runner-up non-BP method HSIC (Ma et al., 2020) by 9.0% on ResNet-5/CIFAR-10, and also outperforms FA (Nøkland, 2016). On the larger CIFAR-100 benchmark, ULR-WL (64.9%) is within 0.3% of BP (65.2%) while ES collapses to 27.3%, confirming ULR's scalability advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the likelihood ratio (LR) gradient estimation method of Peng et al. (2022), which was restricted to MLPs, into a unified framework (ULR) applicable to CNNs, RNNs, GNNs, and SNNs by treating each architecture's parametric modules as instances of a single push-out LR theorem. It positions itself within the perturbation-based family of BP alternatives (ES, FF, SPSA) and shows that several of these methods are special cases of the push-out LR technique, while also establishing a bidirectional equivalence between ULR and the policy gradient theorem in RL. The variance reduction techniques (antithetic variables, QMC) are borrowed from the Monte Carlo integration literature and adapted to the hierarchical structure of neural networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ULR offers a practical, architecture-agnostic alternative to backpropagation that requires only forward passes, enabling localized module training (e.g., fine-tuning inserted blocks without traversing a pre-trained network) and computation graph rearrangement for hardware parallelization. The consistent robustness gains (~9.5% average) across architectures suggest the injected noise acts as an implicit regularizer, though this has been validated only on relatively small datasets (CIFAR-10, MNIST, Ag-News, Cora) and modest model sizes. Open questions include whether the method scales to ImageNet-scale training, whether the multiple forward copies needed for low-variance estimates remain feasible at large parameter counts, and whether the robustness advantage persists under stronger adversarial settings or larger models.
