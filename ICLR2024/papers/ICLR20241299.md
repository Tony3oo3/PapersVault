## Problem

Standard reward models (RMs) in RLHF are trained with a pairwise ranking objective but are rarely evaluated on whether they can consistently distinguish correct instruction-response pairings from mismatched ones. This paper identifies that RMs exhibit near-random consistency on semantically similar but distinct instructions, and that this inconsistency has a measurable trickle-down effect, degrading the usefulness of downstream RLHF-generated responses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241299/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The CONTRAST INSTRUCTIONS benchmark takes quadruples (I^A, I^B, r^A, r^B) where I^A and I^B are lexically similar instructions (SimCSE cosine similarity in [0.75, 0.9]) with different human-preferred responses. The RM consumes concatenated instruction-response strings and outputs a scalar reward; consistency is measured as the fraction of pairs where the RM correctly ranks the matched pairing above the cross-pairing (C_res for response consistency, C_ins for instruction consistency). For the RLHF downstream evaluation, the policy model takes a single instruction and generates a free-form text response, which is then scored on relevance, usefulness, and factuality by human raters.

## Method

The paper introduces CONTRAST INSTRUCTIONS, an automatically constructed benchmark from four open-source preference datasets (StackExchange, WMT, RealSumm, Twitter) that pairs lexically similar instructions with different ground-truth responses to stress-test RM consistency. To improve consistency without extra training cost, CONVEXDA generates five synonym-substituted augmentations per response, embeds them with SimCSE, projects to 2D via PCA, and selects the convex-hull vertex as the training example, preserving training efficiency while focusing on the most representative perturbation. REWARDFUSION operates at inference by retrieving similar training examples via Faiss (threshold δ=0.95) and computing a similarity-weighted average of their reward scores with the target. The key empirical finding is that standard LLaMa-7B RMs score ~53.6% on C_res and ~49.4% on C_ins (near random), while humans reach ~82%; combining both techniques yields modest gains (~57.3% / ~54.1%), and RLHF models trained with the more consistent RM produce statistically significantly more useful responses (57.8% vs. 50.9% usefulness, p<0.01).

## Results

On CONTRAST INSTRUCTIONS, standard LLaMa-7B reward models achieve only 53.6% response consistency (C_res) and 49.4% instruction consistency (C_ins) averaged across four benchmarks, performing near random chance (50%) and far below human performance (82.8% C_res, 81.7% C_ins). The proposed CONVEXDA + REWARDFUSION combination improves C_res to 57.3% and C_ins to 54.1%, but the gap to humans remains large (~25 points). In downstream RLHF, training with a more consistent RM (CONTRAST Ft.) improves MT-Bench scores from 3.44 to 3.89 (one-turn) and 2.39 to 3.18 (multi-turn), and increases response usefulness from 50.9% to 57.8% (p<0.01).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the standard ranking-objective RM (Eq. 1) trained with LoRA on LLaMa-7B, which the paper's proposed methods (CONVEXDA during training + REWARDFUSION at inference) improve by +3.7 points on C_res (53.6→57.3) and +4.7 points on C_ins (49.4→54.1) on average. The StackLLaMa official checkpoint (marked ♠ in Table 3) achieves 50.1% C_res on StackExchange, which the proposed method improves to 53.6%. Multi-task training across all four datasets does not help and slightly worsens performance (53.0 vs. 53.6 C_res), suggesting limited cross-task transfer. Against the human upper bound (~82–83%), even the enhanced RM remains ~25 points behind, indicating that the standard ranking objective itself is a fundamental limitation. In the downstream RLHF comparison, the CONTRAST Ft. RM consistently outperforms the standard RM on MT-Bench (3.89 vs. 3.44 one-turn; 3.18 vs. 2.39 multi-turn) and on human-judged usefulness (57.8% vs. 50.9%), while showing negligible differences in relevance and factuality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the standard RLHF reward modeling pipeline (Ziegler et al., 2019) and the broader consistency literature in NLP (Ribeiro et al., 2020; Elazar et al., 2021), but repurposes the concept of consistency specifically for preference-based reward models. The CONTRAST INSTRUCTIONS benchmark draws on contrast-set evaluation (Gardner et al., 2020) and is constructed from existing human preference datasets (StackExchange, WMT, RealSumm, Twitter). The proposed techniques—CONVEXDA (convex-hull-based data augmentation) and REWARDFUSION (retrieval-augmented reward scoring at inference)—adapt ideas from active learning and retrieval-augmented inference respectively, without modifying the standard ranking objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals that standard RMs perform near random chance on consistency while humans reach ~80%, and that this inconsistency has a measurable trickle-down effect on downstream RLHF response quality (usefulness improves significantly with a more consistent RM). Critically, it shows that standard RM evaluation metrics (ranking accuracy) do not capture consistency, suggesting the field may be overestimating RM quality. However, the proposed fixes (CONVEXDA, REWARDFUSION) yield only modest gains, leaving the core question—'what type of reward modeling would most benefit RLHF'—wide open. Limitations include reliance on 7B-scale models, a single RLHF dataset (StackExchange) for the trickle-down experiments, and a small human evaluation panel (3 annotators, 100 samples for the consistency benchmark).
