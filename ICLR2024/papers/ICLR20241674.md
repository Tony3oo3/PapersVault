## Problem

Compressed gradient descent (CGD) methods for non-convex optimization rely on scalar stepsizes that ignore the geometric structure of the objective's smoothness. This paper is the first to analyze fixed matrix-valued stepsizes in CGD for non-convex objectives, showing that exploiting the smoothness matrix (especially its block-diagonal layer-wise structure in neural networks) yields strictly better convergence than any scalar-stepsize alternative.

## Input / Output

The method requires a differentiable, bounded-below objective f: R^d → R satisfying an L-matrix-smoothness condition (Assumption 2), the smoothness matrix L (which may be block-diagonal with blocks L_i corresponding to network layers), and an initial point x^0. The output is an ε-stationary point whose quality is measured in a determinant-normalized D-norm ‖·‖_{D/det(D)^{1/d}}, a normalization that makes convergence bounds comparable across different stepsize matrices by fixing the volume of the associated ellipsoid to 1.

## Method

The paper proposes two algorithms: det-CGD1 (x^{k+1} = x^k − D S^k ∇f(x^k)) and det-CGD2 (x^{k+1} = x^k − T^k D ∇f(x^k)), where D is a fixed positive definite stepsize matrix and S^k, T^k are i.i.d. unbiased random sketch matrices. The optimal D for det-CGD2 is given in closed form as (E[T^k L T^k])^{-1}, while for det-CGD1 it is the solution of a convex semidefinite program. For block-diagonal smoothness (layer-wise structure), the sketches and stepsize are designed per-layer, and a Bernoulli sampling scheme achieves 'compression for free'—reducing per-iteration communication by a factor of q without increasing total communication complexity. A unified convergence theorem (Theorem 1) guarantees O(1/K) stationarity in the determinant-normalized norm, and distributed versions (Theorem 3) extend the analysis to federated settings with weighted stationarity bounds.

## Results

The paper establishes an O(1/K) convergence rate for both det-CGD1 and det-CGD2 in the single-node setting (Theorem 1), with the convergence factor governed by det(D)^{1/d} where D is the matrix stepsize. In the distributed setting (Theorem 3), the method achieves ε-stationarity with iteration complexity depending on det(D)^{1/d} and a neighborhood term Δ^∞/n. A key practical finding is 'compression for free': using Bernoulli-q layer sampling (row 12 of Table 1) achieves the same communication complexity d·det(L)^{1/d} as the uncompressed det-CGD2 (row 9), while reducing per-iteration bits by a factor of q when all layers have equal width. Empirically (Figure 1), D-det-CGD1 and D-det-CGD2 with optimal diagonal stepsizes under rand-1 sketch outperform both standard DCGD and DCGD with matrix smoothness in iteration and communication complexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two main baselines: standard DCGD (Khirirat et al., 2018) with scalar stepsize, and DCGD with matrix smoothness (Safaryan et al., 2021) which exploits the smoothness matrix structure but still uses a scalar stepsize. In Table 1, det-CGD1 with no compression (row 1, complexity d·det(L)^{1/d}) strictly improves over standard GD (row 13, complexity d·λ_max(L)), since det(L)^{1/d} ≤ λ_max(L) with equality only when L is a scalar multiple of identity. The Bernoulli-sampled det-CGD2 (row 12) achieves the same communication complexity as the fully uncompressed det-CGD2 (row 9), meaning compression is obtained at zero cost in total communication. In the distributed empirical comparison (Figure 1), both D-det-CGD1 and D-det-CGD2 with optimal diagonal stepsizes under rand-1 sketch converge faster in both iterations and total communicated bits than standard DCGD and DCGD with matrix smoothness, confirming the theoretical advantage of the matrix stepsize over scalar alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Compressed Gradient Descent (DCGD) framework of Khirirat et al. (2018) by replacing the scalar stepsize with a fixed positive-definite matrix, drawing on the matrix smoothness assumption introduced by Safaryan et al. (2021) for convex distributed optimization. The convergence analysis follows the unified non-convex SGD framework of Khaled & Richtárik (2020), adapted to handle matrix-valued updates. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of determinant normalization to make convergence bounds comparable across different stepsize matrices, and the design of layer-wise block-diagonal sketches and stepsizes that exploit neural network structure to achieve 'compression for free' via Bernoulli sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'compression for free' result—showing that Bernoulli layer-wise sampling matches the total communication complexity of uncompressed det-CGD2 while reducing per-iteration bits by a factor of q—offers a practical path to reducing communication in federated learning without sacrificing convergence rate. However, the practical impact is tempered by the need to know or estimate the smoothness matrix L (or its block-diagonal approximation) in advance, and the limitation that the D-norm bound does not directly guarantee a small Euclidean gradient norm. Open questions include how to estimate L efficiently in practice, whether the theoretical gains persist in deep network training with non-block-diagonal smoothness, and how the methods interact with adaptive or momentum-based variants.
