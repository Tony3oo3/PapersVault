## Problem

Existing diffusion-based sampling methods either fix the forward or backward process, limiting their ability to adapt both dynamics to the problem at hand. While training both simultaneously is desirable, it introduces non-uniqueness in the solution space since infinitely many forward-backward pairs can satisfy the marginal constraints. The paper seeks a principled framework that unifies variational inference and optimal transport on path space, resolves this non-uniqueness, and yields a practical sampler with strong theoretical guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method requires a prescribed annealing schedule (π_t)_{t∈[0,T]} whose scores ∇ln π_t and unnormalised densities π̂_t are available in closed form, a prior distribution π_0, and a noise level σ. It outputs approximate samples from the target π_T by simulating the learned SDE, and an unbiased estimate of ln Z via a Radon-Nikodym derivative along the sampled path. At optimality, the partition function estimator achieves zero variance, and the method supports both overdamped and underdamped (Hamiltonian) formulations.

## Method

CMCD learns a control vector field ∇φ_t so that the forward SDE dY_t = (σ²∇ln π_t + ∇φ_t)dt + σ√2 dW_t interpolates from prior π_0 to posterior π_T, while the backward drift is constrained to b_t = -σ²∇ln π_t + ∇φ_t to restore uniqueness. Training minimises a KL divergence between the forward and backward path measures, yielding a tractable objective (Eq. 24) that is minimised end-to-end via stochastic gradient descent over a neural network parameterising ∇φ_t. The framework is shown to be an infinitesimal analogue of iterative proportional fitting for Schrödinger bridges, and the learned dynamics connect to the Jarzynski equality and Crooks fluctuation theorem, providing a controlled version that suppresses free-energy fluctuations. Unlike sequential annealing approaches (ULA, MCD, AFTMC), CMCD learns the entire time-dependent control field simultaneously rather than step-by-step, and unlike EM/IPF it avoids sequential accumulation of approximation errors.

## Results

On six standard target distributions (following Geffner & Domke 2023), both overdamped (CMCD-OD) and underdamped (CMCD-UD) variants consistently outperform all baselines in ELBO across discretisation steps K ∈ {8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256}, with the advantage most pronounced at low K. On the funnel and gmm targets with known partition functions, CMCD recovers ln Z more consistently than PIS, DDS, and SMC, even at low K. For sample quality measured by the entropy-regularised OT distance W₂^γ, CMCD achieves lower (better) distances than competing methods. The paper reports results averaged over 30 random seeds; specific numerical values are presented in Figure 1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMCD (both overdamped and underdamped) wins against all 11 baselines on the primary ELBO metric across all six target distributions, with the gap widening at lower discretisation steps K. Among underdamped baselines, ULA (Wu et al. 2020; Thin et al. 2021) and MCD (Doucet et al. 2022b) are the closest competitors, but CMCD-UD surpasses both, and CMCD-OD further outperforms the underdamped baselines on most targets. Among overdamped baselines, UHA (Geffner & Domke 2021; Zhang et al. 2021) and LDVI (Geffner & Domke 2023) trail CMCD-OD, with LDVI being the strongest single baseline. For ln Z estimation on funnel and gmm, CMCD outperforms PIS (Barr et al. 2020; Vargas et al. 2023b), DDS (Vargas et al. 2023a), and SMC (Del Moral et al. 2006; Zhou et al. 2016), recovering the log-partition more consistently especially at low K where SMC and PIS degrade. The paper does not report any setting where a baseline outperforms CMCD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads—optimal transport (Schrödinger bridges, IPF/Sinkhorn) and variational inference (hierarchical VAEs, EM)—under a single framework based on divergences on path space (Framework 1'). It builds directly on Tzen & Raginsky's infinite-depth SDE limit of hierarchical VAEs, Bernton et al.'s Schrödinger bridge samplers, and Zhang & Chen's path integral sampler, showing that score-based generative models, Föllmer drift sampling, and denoising diffusion samplers are all special cases of the same variational objective. The key departure from prior work is that CMCD learns both forward and backward drifts simultaneously (resolving nonuniqueness via a score constraint), rather than fixing one direction as in all previously surveyed methods, and it establishes an exact EM ↔ IPF equivalence (Proposition 3.1) that had not been formally proven before.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unifying framework gives practitioners a principled way to design new sampling objectives by choosing divergences and reference processes, and the EM=IPF result clarifies a long-standing conceptual gap between the ML and OT communities. The connection to Jarzynski's equality and Crooks' fluctuation theorem provides a zero-variance guarantee for normalizing constant estimation at optimality, which is a clean theoretical property absent from prior annealed importance sampling methods. Open questions include the choice of optimal annealing schedules π_t, the practical cost of training to the zero-variance regime, and whether the framework extends naturally to discrete or high-dimensional settings where the SDE formulation becomes less tractable.
