## Problem

ASH, the current state-of-the-art post-hoc OOD detection method, combines pruning and scaling of penultimate activations but lacks a mechanistic explanation for why it works. The paper identifies that pruning actually harms OOD detection by reducing ID energy scores more than OOD scores, and that only the scaling component provides the separation benefit. Additionally, existing training-time OOD enhancement methods (e.g., AugMix, LogitNorm) require substantial additional computational cost relative to their gains.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet) processed by a pretrained CNN (ResNet-50 or DenseNet-101). At inference, SCALE intercepts the D-dimensional penultimate activation vector, computes a sample-specific scaling factor r(a) = Q(a)/Q_p(a) from the activation's percentile statistics, and multiplies all activations by exp(r) before the final linear layer. Because the scaling is uniform across all dimensions, the argmax of the logits is preserved, guaranteeing zero ID accuracy loss while the energy-based OOD score (T·log Σ exp(z_k/T)) is modified to better separate ID from OOD samples.

## Method

SCALE is a post-hoc enhancement that replaces ASH's rectified scaling (prune + scale) with pure scaling: all D penultimate activations are multiplied by exp(r(a)) where r(a) = Q(a)/Q_p(a) is the ratio of total activation sum to the sum of the top-p-percentile activations (p=0.85). The theoretical analysis under rectified Gaussian assumptions (μ^ID/σ^ID > μ^OOD/σ^OOD) proves that r(a^ID) > r(a^OOD), so ID activations are amplified more than OOD activations, widening the energy-score gap. For training-time enhancement, ISH applies the same r(a^i) as a per-sample gradient weighting exclusively on the final linear classifier weights W during a 10-epoch fine-tuning phase, leaving the forward pass and all other layer gradients unchanged. This 'intermediate tensor shaping' uses ID-ness as a learning-rate weighting factor, emphasizing samples with stronger in-distribution characteristics during optimization, and requires only one-third the extended training epochs of competing methods like AugMix.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K (ResNet-50), SCALE achieves FPR@95 of 59.76 / AUROC of 81.36 on Near-OOD and FPR@95 of 16.53 / AUROC of 96.53 on Far-OOD, while preserving the full ID accuracy of 76.18% (no trade-off). On CIFAR-10 (DenseNet-101), SCALE reaches FPR@95 of 17.84 and AUROC of 96.16, outperforming ASH-S by 3.28 FPR@95 and 0.91 AUROC. For training-time enhancement, ISH (10 extra epochs) attains the highest AUROC on the OpenOOD v1.5 benchmark: 84.01% on Near-OOD and 96.79% on Far-OOD, surpassing AugMix (83.55/95.99) with only 100 total epochs versus 180.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCALE outperforms all post-hoc baselines on ImageNet: it beats ASH-S (the prior SOTA) by 2.27 FPR@95 and 1.73 AUROC on Near-OOD, and by 0.33 FPR@95 on Far-OOD, while uniquely maintaining the original ID accuracy of 76.18% (vs. 75.51% for ASH-S and 75.58% for ReAct). Against scoring-only methods (MSP, EBO, MLS, GEN, RMDS, TempScale), SCALE improves Near-OOD AUROC by 4.2–5.5 points and Far-OOD AUROC by 6.8–11.3 points. On CIFAR-10/100, SCALE surpasses DICE by 12.37/16.14 FPR@95 and 3.07/5.29 AUROC respectively, and beats ASH-S by 3.28/2.03 FPR@95. For training-time enhancement, ISH (90+10 epochs) dominates LogitNorm (90+30 epochs) by 9.39/5.25 AUROC on Near/Far-OOD, outperforms AugMix (180 epochs) by 0.46/0.80 AUROC with 44% fewer total epochs, and exceeds RegMixup (90+30) by 3.16/0.85 AUROC. ISH also surpasses CIDER (90+30) by a large margin (84.01 vs. 68.97 Near-OOD AUROC), though CIDER reports no ID accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly decomposes ASH (Djurisic et al., 2023), the current state-of-the-art post-hoc OOD detection method, into its pruning and scaling components, showing that only scaling contributes to OOD separation while pruning is actively harmful. This positions the work as a theoretical clarification of the ReAct → DICE → ASH lineage of activation/weight modification methods, culminating in the simpler SCALE (scaling-only) post-hoc method and ISH, a training-time method that repurposes the scaling factor as a per-sample learning-rate weight for the final linear layer, conceptually distinct from LogitNorm's logit-space calibration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean separation of scaling from pruning provides practitioners with a simpler, more interpretable post-hoc OOD detection tool (SCALE) that eliminates the ID accuracy trade-off inherent in ASH, ReAct, and DICE, making it easier to deploy in safety-critical pipelines where both ID and OOD performance matter. ISH's use of only 10 additional fine-tuning epochs (vs. 30–90 for LogitNorm, CIDER, or AugMix) to reach SOTA AUROC on both near- and far-OOD is practically attractive. Open questions include whether the rectified-Gaussian activation assumption holds for non-convolutional architectures (e.g., ViTs) or non-semantic-shift OOD settings, and whether the percentile p can be learned rather than fixed via validation.
