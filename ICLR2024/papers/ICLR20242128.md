## Problem

Training a text-to-video diffusion model from scratch demands massive video-text pairs and prohibitive compute, making it inaccessible to most researchers. Existing training-free adaptations of text-to-image models (e.g., Tune-A-Video, Text2Video-Zero) rely on sparse cross-frame attention that ensures appearance consistency but still produces visible structural flickering and discontinuous inter-frame transitions, failing to deliver smooth, continuous video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242128/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt and a per-frame motion sequence (depth maps from MiDaS, Canny edges, or human pose skeletons) as joint conditions, and outputs a video at 512×512 resolution. Short videos are 15 frames and long videos approximately 100 frames, generated in roughly 2 and 10 minutes respectively on a single NVIDIA RTX 2080Ti using DDIM sampling with 50 timesteps. The motion sequence provides coarse structural guidance per frame, while the text prompt controls appearance and semantics.

## Method

ControlVideo adapts the pre-trained ControlNet (built on Stable Diffusion) to video by inflating the main U-Net along the temporal axis (replacing 3×3 kernels with 1×3×3) and replacing self-attention with fully cross-frame attention, where all N frames jointly attend to each other as a single 'larger image' to maximize appearance consistency. The key novelty is an interleaved-frame smoother that splits the video into overlapping three-frame clips, alternately processing even-indexed and odd-indexed clips at different denoising timesteps: at each selected step, the predicted clean latents are decoded to RGB, the middle frame of each clip is replaced by a RIFE interpolation of its two neighbors, and the result is re-encoded to latent space before continuing DDIM denoising. For long videos, a hierarchical sampler first generates sparse key frames with full cross-frame attention for long-range coherence, then synthesizes the intermediate short clips conditioned on adjacent key-frame pairs, keeping memory usage within a commodity GPU. The entire pipeline is training-free, requiring no finetuning of any model weights.

## Results

On 125 motion-prompt pairs from DAVIS, ControlVideo conditioned on depth maps achieves the best frame consistency (FC = 97.22×10⁻²), prompt consistency (PC = 31.81×10⁻²), and lowest warping error (WE = 5.81×10⁻²) among all compared methods. With Canny edges, ControlVideo reaches FC = 96.83×10⁻² and WE = 2.75×10⁻², substantially reducing warping error compared to Text2Video-Zero (8.76×10⁻²). In the user study, raters prefer ControlVideo over Tune-A-Video and Text2Video-Zero on video quality (73.6% and 76.0%), temporal consistency (83.2% and 81.6%), and text alignment (68.0% and 65.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Tune-A-Video (which fine-tunes Stable Diffusion on a source video and uses DDIM inversion for structure), ControlVideo wins decisively on frame consistency (96.83 vs. 94.53 with Canny; 97.22 vs. 94.53 with depth) and warping error (2.75 vs. 18.16; 5.81 vs. 18.16), though Tune-A-Video edges ahead on prompt consistency with Canny (31.57 vs. 30.75). Against Text2Video-Zero (which uses first-only cross-frame attention on ControlNet without fine-tuning), ControlVideo improves frame consistency by ~1.7 points (96.83 vs. 95.17 Canny; 97.22 vs. 95.99 depth) and reduces warping error by roughly 3× (2.75 vs. 8.76 Canny; 5.81 vs. 10.36 depth), while prompt consistency is essentially tied. Follow-Your-Pose is compared only qualitatively on pose-conditioned generation, where ControlVideo produces more consistent appearance (e.g., stable faces, persistent background objects) compared to its flickering counterparts. The user study confirms these quantitative advantages, with 83.2% of raters preferring ControlVideo over Tune-A-Video and 81.6% over Text2Video-Zero on temporal consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

ControlVideo extends the controllable text-to-image paradigm of ControlNet to the video domain by inflating the U-Net along the temporal axis and replacing self-attention with cross-frame attention, while remaining entirely training-free. It departs from prior zero-shot video methods (Tune-A-Video, Text2Video-Zero) that rely on sparser cross-frame mechanisms (first-only or sparse-causal attention) by empirically demonstrating that fully cross-frame interaction—treating all frames as a single 'large image'—yields superior consistency and quality. The interleaved-frame smoother, which leverages RIFE-based frame interpolation at a few denoising timesteps, is a novel contribution not present in prior work, addressing the video continuity gap that cross-frame attention alone cannot close.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By requiring no training and running on a single commodity GPU (RTX 2080Ti), ControlVideo democratizes controllable video generation for researchers and artists who lack access to large-scale video datasets or multi-GPU clusters. The paper's key limitation is that when text prompts semantically conflict with the input motion sequence (e.g., moonwalk poses paired with 'Iron Man runs'), the output tends to follow the motion rather than the text, leaving open the question of how to adaptively reconcile competing conditioning signals. The empirical finding that a simple two-timestep interpolation smoother dramatically reduces structural flicker (warping error drops from 8.76 to 2.75) suggests that lightweight post-hoc temporal corrections may be a broadly useful ingredient for diffusion-based video pipelines.
