## Problem

Modeling the joint distribution of images and dense modalities (e.g., depth) with diffusion models is hindered by a severe data imbalance: labeled dense maps (~2M) are orders of magnitude fewer than text-image pairs (5B in LAION), making from-scratch training unable to generalize. Naive fine-tuning of pre-trained text-to-image diffusion models for joint generation causes catastrophic forgetting of RGB quality, as the large initial loss on the new modality destabilizes the entire network.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a text prompt plus either an RGB image, a depth map, or both (with asymmetric masks for channel-wise inpainting), and produces a jointly denoised RGB image and dense map at 512×512. For latent diffusion, the dense modality is encoded and decoded by the same VAE as RGB without retraining the autoencoder, which may cause some quality loss. For tile-based panorama generation, the output is a 2048×512 cylindrical RGBD panorama spanning 180° horizontally, assembled from overlapping tiles with random offsets and boundary-weight decay.

## Method

JointNet creates a trainable copy of the pre-trained diffusion U-Net for the dense modality branch and densely connects it to the frozen RGB branch via zero-initialized convolution layers at every downsampling, middle, and upsampling stage, exchanging intermediate features bidirectionally. The key design principle is 'output preservation': at initialization, the zero-initialized connections vanish, so both branches independently predict noise using the original weights, preventing the sudden loss spike that causes catastrophic forgetting in direct channel-extension baselines. Training minimizes the sum of L2 noise-prediction losses over both branches on 64 A100 GPUs for ~24 hours, with depth/normal labels estimated online via MiDaS and Omnidata on COYO-700M. A second fine-tuning stage unfreezes the RGB branch at a lower learning rate (1e-5) with 50% text dropout to learn mutual conditioning between modalities, and a third stage adds masked-input conditioning for channel-wise inpainting.

## Results

For joint RGBD generation, JointNet (Depth) achieves FID 14.38, IS 38.33, and CLIP 25.71 on 30K MSCOCO prompts, closely matching the base Stable Diffusion v2.1 (FID 16.15, IS 39.85, CLIP 26.17) while substantially outperforming LDM3D (FID 32.13, IS 29.40). For mono-view depth estimation on MegaDepth, JointNet reports AbsRel 0.1203 and RMSE 0.0754, comparable to MiDaS DPT-Hybrid-384 (AbsRel 0.0568, RMSE 0.0601). For tile-based panorama generation, JointNet achieves the lowest intra-LPIPS (0.578) among tile-based methods while running in only 17 seconds, versus 49 s (Multi-Diffusion) and 101 s (Sync-Diffusion). For depth-conditioned image generation, JointNet reaches FID 10.61, comparable to ControlNet (10.14) and close to the heavier SD v2.0d (8.75).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LDM3D, JointNet (Depth) wins decisively on FID (14.38 vs. 32.13) and IS (38.33 vs. 29.40), though LDM3D edges ahead on CLIP similarity (26.54 vs. 25.71), likely due to its LAION-400M training data being CLIP-filtered. Compared to the Direct Extend baseline (naive channel extension), JointNet achieves a slightly higher FID (14.38 vs. 14.15) but better IS (38.33 vs. 37.08) and avoids the catastrophic forgetting observed in Direct Extend during early training. For mono-view depth estimation, JointNet is worse than MiDaS DPT-Hybrid-384 on both AbsRel (0.1203 vs. 0.0568) and RMSE (0.0754 vs. 0.0601), though the authors note MiDaS was trained directly on MegaDepth while JointNet was not. In panorama generation, JointNet outperforms both Multi-Diffusion (intra-LPIPS 0.578 vs. 0.641; 17 s vs. 49 s) and Sync-Diffusion (0.578 vs. 0.626; 17 s vs. 101 s) on both coherence and speed. For depth-to-RGB generation, JointNet (FID 10.61) is slightly behind ControlNet (10.14) and notably behind SD v2.0d (8.75), though the latter requires substantially heavier fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

JointNet directly extends ControlNet's copy-and-zero-initialize architecture, but repurposes the copied branch from a conditioning signal processor into a symmetric joint-denoising partner, with the original RGB branch frozen to preserve generation quality. It departs from LDM3D's approach of directly extending input/output channels, which the paper shows causes catastrophic forgetting of RGB generation in early training steps. The 'output preserving principle'—ensuring neither branch experiences a sudden loss spike at initialization—is the key design insight that distinguishes JointNet from both naive channel extension and standard ControlNet-style conditioning. For downstream applications, the paper builds on SDEdit for depth refinement, Repaint-style mask blending for channel-wise inpainting, and tile-based diffusion strategies for large-scale generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

JointNet demonstrates that a single fine-tuned diffusion model can serve as a unified interface for joint RGBD generation, bidirectional dense prediction (image-to-depth and depth-to-image), and coherent large-scale panorama synthesis, reducing the need for separate specialized models. The practical tile-based generation technique (whole-image tile in early denoising steps, random offsets, boundary decay) achieves the best content coherence at the lowest inference time among compared methods. Key limitations include doubled inference cost from the dual-branch architecture, depth accuracy that trails dedicated estimators like MiDaS in AbsRel, and the current restriction to exactly two modalities—extending to three or more would require additional copies and further increase compute. Open questions include whether distillation can recover the 2× speed penalty and whether more elegant multi-modality architectures exist beyond naive replication.
