## Problem

Despite the success of Bayesian inference in explaining perception, the circuit-level implementation of complex, structured priors over natural stimuli remains poorly understood. Existing neural sampling models are restricted to simple distributions (Gaussian scale mixtures, basic multimodal posteriors) and lack a mechanism for flexibly reusing the same prior across different inference tasks. This paper asks how a recurrent circuit can implicitly encode high-dimensional nonlinear manifold priors and combine them with diverse evidence sources to perform task-specific posterior inference.

## Input / Output

The circuit operates on N-dimensional latent variable vectors (e.g., a 10-dimensional ambient space encoding a 3-dimensional swiss-roll manifold, or 784-dimensional MNIST pixel vectors). External evidence enters as either a linear sensory constraint with Gaussian uncertainty (M_s^T(x - x_c) = 0) or a discrete contextual cue selecting among mixture components; both are gated by the oscillation phase (1-β_t) and added as likelihood gradient terms. Output is a sequence of samples from the prior or posterior, with one new sample generated per oscillation cycle, where the sampling rate is set by the period T of the global oscillator.

## Method

The architecture replaces the standard all-to-all DM denoiser with a recurrent circuit of multi-compartment pyramidal neurons whose dendritic trees (tree-structured ANNs with ReLU nonlinearities) compute the DM reverse transition mean μ_θ. A global sinusoidal signal β_t modulates both the dendritic computation and additive somatic Gaussian noise, creating an oscillatory cycle: during the descending phase (low β_t), noise pushes the state off the data manifold; during the ascending phase (high β_t), the dendritic attractor dynamics pull it back, yielding one sample per cycle. For posterior inference, separate feedforward sub-circuits compute the likelihood gradient g_t(x_{t-1}, y) and inject it as an additive drive scaled by (1-β_t), shifting the transition mean to μ_θ + (1-β_t)g_t. Training uses the standard DM denoising objective (L2 noise prediction) via gradient descent, and the same trained circuit supports flexible inference by gating different evidence sources in or out. The key biological departure from standard DMs is that the same operator handles both forward (noise injection) and reverse (denoising) phases, avoiding unrealistic state jumps while preserving sample independence.

## Results

On the 2D swiss-roll manifold (3D latent in 10D ambient space), the neural circuit generates samples with autocorrelation steadily near zero even at the shortest lags, and marginal densities that pass Kolmogorov-Smirnov goodness-of-fit tests against the uniform ground truth (p = 0.145 along the nonlinear dimension, p = 0.125 along the linear dimension, n = 1000). The KL divergence between the neural circuit's approximate posterior and the exact DM posterior is largest at the start of the reverse process (high noise regime) but approaches zero over the cycle, confirming that the temporal-causality approximation (evaluating the likelihood at x_{t-1} rather than μ_θ(x_{t-1})) introduces minimal sampling bias. The model also produces qualitatively good samples from a high-dimensional MNIST prior (Suppl. B.6), and successfully samples from multimodal context-conditioned and jointly sensory+context-conditioned posteriors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical circuit-modeling paper without formal baseline comparisons against competing ML methods. The primary quantitative comparison is between the neural circuit's sampling dynamics and the exact diffusion-model reverse process: the KL divergence between the two sample distributions (10,000 samples, 225 bins) is largest at the start of the reverse phase where noise and flow fields are large, but diminishes to near zero as the attractor dynamics pull samples onto the manifold (Fig. 2E). In the supplementary dendritic morphology ablation (Table S1, Fig. S1), shallower/wider trees (depth 2, branching [59,59]) converge fastest in KL divergence, while deeper/narrower trees (depth 10) learn more slowly, though the 7-layer network outperforms the 5-layer one, showing non-monotonic scaling with depth. Skip connections improve performance in deeper architectures but can degrade it in shallower ones, with optimal performance at intermediate depths under L1 regularization. No head-to-head comparison against other neural sampling frameworks (e.g., Echeveste et al. 2020, Chen et al. 2023) is provided.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate literatures: diffusion models (Sohl-Dickstein et al. 2015; Ho et al. 2020; Dhariwal & Nichol 2021) and biologically-motivated neural sampling circuits (Fiser et al. 2010; Berkes et al. 2011; Orbán et al. 2016; Savin et al. 2014). Prior circuit models of probabilistic inference were restricted to simple distributions (Gaussian scale mixtures, bimodal percepts), while standard DMs assume all-to-all connectivity and i.i.d. Gaussian initialization. The key architectural departure here is mapping the DM reverse operator onto dendritic tree nonlinearities (following Poirazi et al. 2003) with an oscillatory global signal replacing the discrete variance schedule, and replacing the DM forward process with a noise-modulated version of the same recurrent dynamics for reinitialization. The modular gating of separate likelihood sub-circuits for sensory and contextual evidence extends the classifier-guided posterior sampling of Dhariwal & Nichol (2021) into a biologically-plausible multi-source framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first circuit-level model that can represent complex high-dimensional priors (nonlinear manifolds, multimodal mixtures) while supporting flexible, task-switchable inference through input gating — addressing a gap the authors identify between simple neural sampling models and the statistical complexity of natural stimuli. The proposed oscillatory-phase-dependent neural signatures (activity shifting from likelihood-dominated to prior-dominated across the cycle) offer a concrete, testable prediction for multi-electrode recordings. However, the learning problem is explicitly deferred to future work, all demonstrations use low-dimensional toy manifolds or MNIST, and no experimental validation is provided. Open questions include whether the architecture scales to truly high-dimensional natural image priors, how the dendritic tree morphology is determined by learning, and whether the oscillatory gating mechanism maps onto specific cortical microcircuitry.
