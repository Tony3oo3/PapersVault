## Problem

Diffusion models are trained to match data distributions via a log-likelihood objective, but most practical applications care about downstream objectives—such as aesthetic quality, image compressibility, or prompt-image alignment—that likelihood does not capture. Because exact likelihood computation is intractable for diffusion models, standard RL algorithms cannot be directly applied. The paper asks whether diffusion models can be optimized directly for arbitrary black-box reward functions without resorting to approximate reward-weighted likelihood objectives.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (e.g., 'a dolphin riding a bike') and produces a single 512×512 RGB image via the standard iterative denoising sampler. A black-box reward function r(x₀, c) is evaluated on the final generated image and its associated prompt, returning a scalar that drives the RL update. Prompts are drawn from task-specific distributions: 398 ImageNet-1000 animal categories for compressibility/incompressibility, 45 common animals for aesthetic quality, and 45 animals × 3 activities (riding a bike, playing chess, washing dishes) for prompt-image alignment.

## Method

DDPO reframes the T-step denoising process as a multi-step MDP where the state is (c, t, x_t), the action is x_{t−1}, the policy is the isotropic Gaussian reverse process p_θ(x_{t−1}|x_t, c), and the reward r(x₀, c) is received only at the final denoising step. The critical insight is that this formulation makes the policy a simple Gaussian rather than the intractable full distribution p_θ(x₀|c), enabling exact per-step log-likelihood and gradient computation. Two policy gradient estimators are provided: DDPO_SF (score-function/REINFORCE, one gradient step per data collection round) and DDPO_IS (importance-sampling with PPO-style clipping, allowing multiple optimization steps per round). For prompt-image alignment, the paper uses LLaVA to generate a textual description of each image and computes BERTScore recall between that description and the original prompt as the reward, replacing the need for human annotation. The model is finetuned jointly across many prompts (up to 398) using only the UNet weights of Stable Diffusion v1.4, with classifier-free guidance applied during both training and sampling.

## Results

DDPO (both the score-function and importance-sampling variants) consistently outperforms both RWR weighting schemes across all three reward functions (JPEG compressibility, incompressibility, and LAION aesthetic quality), as measured by attained reward versus number of reward queries (Figure 4). In the direct comparison with DPOK (Figure 8), DDPO_IS outperforms DPOK across all four prompts on both ImageReward and LAION aesthetic score, without using KL regularization. In the aesthetic quality comparison with universal guidance (Table 1), DDPO_IS at 20k reward queries achieves 6.63±0.03 mean aesthetic score, substantially exceeding universal guidance (6.14±0.05) and the base model (5.95±0.03) on the prompt 'wolf'.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RWR (Lee et al., 2023), which corresponds to reward-weighted likelihood maximization, DDPO shows a clear and consistent advantage on all three tasks (compressibility, incompressibility, aesthetic quality); the paper attributes this to DDPO's use of exact per-step log-likelihoods in a multi-step MDP formulation versus RWR's reliance on an approximate variational bound. Against DPOK (Fan et al., 2023), DDPO_IS outperforms DPOK across the board on all four test prompts (color, count, composition, location) on both ImageReward and LAION aesthetic score, and does so without KL regularization, which DPOK employs. Against universal guidance (Bansal et al., 2023), DDPO_IS achieves a 0.49-point improvement in LAION aesthetic score (6.63 vs. 6.14) while being roughly 30× faster at inference (4 seconds vs. ~2 minutes per image on an A100). Within the DDPO family, the importance-sampling variant (DDPO_IS) slightly outperforms the score-function variant (DDPO_SF), likely due to its ability to perform multiple gradient updates per data collection round.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the policy gradient formulation for diffusion models introduced by Fan & Lee (2023), who proved equivalence between their shortcut fine-tuning method and a policy gradient algorithm restricted to a GAN-like discriminator reward. DDPO extends this to arbitrary black-box reward functions and contrasts with the concurrent DPOK (Fan et al., 2023), which also uses policy gradients but trains a separate model per prompt and considers only a single preference-based reward. The reward-weighted regression (RWR) baseline traces back to Lee et al. (2023) and Peters & Schaal (2007), while the VLM-based automated reward draws on RLAIF (Bai et al., 2022b) from the language model alignment literature. The key conceptual departure is reframing the full denoising trajectory as a multi-step MDP, which enables exact per-step log-likelihoods and avoids the theoretical gap in RWR's use of a variational bound as a proxy for likelihood.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DDPO provides a principled, theoretically grounded framework for optimizing diffusion models on arbitrary downstream objectives without requiring new training data or human annotation, with the VLM-based reward (LLaVA + BERTScore) offering a practical path to automated prompt-image alignment. The demonstrated generalization from 45 animals × 3 activities to unseen animals, objects, and activities is encouraging but the prompt diversity remains narrow, and the emergent cartoon-like style shift suggests the reward function may be inadvertently shaping the output distribution in unintended ways. The most pressing open question is reward overoptimization: the paper documents clear failure modes (degeneration to noise, typographic attacks on the VLM) but defers to KL-regularization or early stopping, acknowledging no general solution exists. Future work should address whether these findings scale to larger models, broader prompt distributions, and more complex compositional tasks.
