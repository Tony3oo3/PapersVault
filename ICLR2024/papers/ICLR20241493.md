## Problem

Traditional federated learning aggregation methods (e.g., FedAvg) only combine local models from the current round, which under Non-IID data produces a global model that deviates significantly from all clients' local models, losing their specific data knowledge. This paper identifies that cross-round local models from the same client can converge to different local optima due to nonconvexity and varying starting points, offering an underexploited opportunity to reduce inter-client divergence at aggregation time.

## Input / Output

The server maintains a rolling cache of K most recent local models per client (K=3 in experiments), replacing the oldest entry each round when a client participates. At aggregation time, the server selects exactly one model from each client's cache (and fixes previously selected models for non-participating clients), then averages the selected set to produce the global model. The unusual requirement is persistent per-client model storage across rounds, with memory cost KNC where C is model size, though the authors note this is manageable since K is small and non-participating models can be offloaded to disk.

## Method

FedCDA formulates cross-round model selection as a combinatorial optimization: choose one local model per client from its K-round cache to minimize the variance (divergence) of the selected set, derived from an L-smooth upper bound on the global objective with the gradient term approximated as zero near convergence. To handle the O(K^N) exact search, two approximations are applied: (1) only re-selecting models for the P participating clients while fixing non-participating clients' previously chosen models, reducing complexity to O(K^P); and (2) a stochastic greedy batch strategy that partitions participating clients into B batches and selects sequentially, yielding O(BK^{P/B}) complexity. The method is applied after a warmup phase (e.g., 50 rounds of FedAvg) to ensure local models are near convergence, and the theoretical analysis bounds the approximation error by 4Lε²+2LDε where ε is the distance to local optima, proving O(1/√T) convergence on non-convex objectives.

## Results

FedCDA achieves the best test accuracy on nearly all 18 evaluation settings across Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100 under both Shards and Dirichlet Non-IID partitions. On the most challenging setting (CIFAR-10, Dirichlet α=0.1, ResNet-18), FedCDA reaches 62.46% accuracy, outperforming the best baseline FedExP (60.63%) by 1.83%. On CIFAR-100 with 100 clients and Dirichlet α=0.1, FedCDA achieves 47.38%, surpassing the next-best FedDF (41.04%) by 6.34%. The method shows the largest gains on larger models (ResNet-18) and more heterogeneous data distributions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedCDA outperforms all 9 baselines on 17 out of 18 settings in Table 1, with the sole exception being Fashion-MNIST Dirichlet α=0.1 where FedDF (80.03%) narrowly beats FedCDA (78.63%). The largest margins appear on CIFAR-100: under Dirichlet α=0.1, FedCDA (39.38%) exceeds the best baseline FedProx (32.96%) by 6.42%, and under Shards S=8, it surpasses FedDF (25.84%) by 2.32%. On CIFAR-10 Dirichlet α=0.1, FedCDA (62.46%) leads FedExP (60.63%) by 1.83% and FedDF (54.28%) by 8.18%. In the 100-client CIFAR-100 experiment (Table 2), FedCDA achieves 47.38% under Dirichlet α=0.1, beating FedDF (41.04%) by 6.34%. The paper notes that improvements are more pronounced with larger models (ResNet-18 vs. simple CNN) and more heterogeneous data, consistent with the theoretical prediction that cross-round diversity benefits aggregation when local optima differ across clients.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedCDA departs from all existing FL aggregation methods (FedAvg, FedMA, FedLAW, FedDF, GAMF, etc.) that operate exclusively on the current round's local models, by introducing cross-round divergence-aware selection. Conceptually, it builds on the observation that non-convex DNNs yield different local optima across rounds when clients receive different global models, and on the L-smoothness upper-bound formulation to derive a closed-form aggregation objective. The paper positions itself as orthogonal to model fusion (parameter re-ordering), adaptive weighting, and ensemble distillation, and can be combined with those techniques at the final averaging step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedCDA offers a practical, drop-in replacement for the aggregation step that consistently improves accuracy under Non-IID settings without requiring a server-side proxy dataset or additional gradient computations, making it attractive for real-world FL deployments. The theoretical guarantees (bounded approximation error, convergence rate O(1/√T)) provide confidence beyond empirical validation. Key open questions include handling heterogeneous smoothness constants across clients, incorporating sharpness-awareness into the selection criterion (flatter optima generalize better), and whether the warmup threshold behavior generalizes to very large client populations or streaming settings.
