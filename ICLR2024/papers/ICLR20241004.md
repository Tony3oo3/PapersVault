## Problem

Modern LLMs (Llama, Falcon, PaLM) have adopted smoother activations like SiLU and GELU over ReLU for marginal training-time gains, but this forfeits the large activation sparsity that ReLU naturally induces, making inference significantly more expensive. The paper asks whether ReLU can be reinstated in already-pretrained models without meaningful performance loss, and how the resulting sparsity can be exploited to reduce inference computation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241004/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on pretrained transformer LLMs (OPT 1.3B/2.7B/6.7B, Llama 7B, Falcon 7B) by surgically modifying their activation functions, then fine-tuning on RefinedWeb text. At inference, the input is a standard text prompt/token sequence, and the output is generated tokens; the key structural difference is that ReLU activations zero out entire rows of the down-projection (and, in stage 2, up-projection and QKV) weight matrices, so only non-zero rows are loaded from memory and multiplied, reducing both FLOPS and I/O transfer. The sparsity is semi-structured at the row level, making it directly exploitable by existing sparse GEMV kernels on GPUs and Apple Silicon.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage 'relufication' procedure. Stage 1 replaces the FFN activation (GELU in Falcon, SiLU in Llama) with ReLU and fine-tunes on 30B tokens; stage 2 additionally inserts ReLU layers immediately after normalization layers (LayerNorm/RMSNorm), creating sparsity before the QKV and up-projection matrix multiplications, followed by 50B tokens of fine-tuning. The paper first validates the approach by training OPT 1.3B from scratch on 100B tokens with ReLU, SiLU, and GELU, showing near-identical performance but dramatically different sparsity (90%+ for ReLU vs. near-zero for SiLU). A key empirical finding is that pre-activation distributions remain stable during the short fine-tuning, allowing sparsity to be predicted before fine-tuning. The paper further identifies 'aggregated sparsity'—the phenomenon where a large fraction of neurons remain unused across many consecutive tokens—which enables weight-reuse strategies and improves speculative decoding speedup, and proposes shifted ReLU (ReLU(x−b)) to push sparsity even higher by shifting the pre-activation distribution relative to the zero cutoff.

## Results

After relufication (replacing non-ReLU activations with ReLU and inserting ReLU after normalization layers), the paper achieves up to 3× FLOPS reduction with minimal accuracy loss: OPT 6.7B drops from 4.5G to 2.8G FLOPS (avg zero-shot accuracy 59.8%→58.6%), Falcon 7B from 6.6G to 2.2G FLOPS (66.8%→64.8%), and Llama 7B from 6.6G to 2.9G FLOPS (68.4%→66.4%). On MMLU five-shot, relufied Falcon 7B (ReLU) scores 27.9% at 62% FLOPS vs. 27.7% for the original GELU model, and relufied Llama 7B (ReLU) scores 34.7% at 72% FLOPS vs. 35.1% for the original SiLU model. In Appendix E, the relufied OPT 6.7B at 2.8G FLOPS outperforms the equivalent-FLOPS dense model by over 2% in zero-shot accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original dense models (OPT, Llama 7B, Falcon 7B), relufication at stage 2 reduces FLOPS by 38–67% while losing only 1.2–2.4% average zero-shot accuracy, and stage 1 (FFN ReLU only) loses just 0.4–1.6% at 38–55% FLOPS reduction. On MMLU, the gap is even smaller: relufied Falcon 7B (ReLU) actually edges out the original GELU model (27.9% vs. 27.7%) at 62% FLOPS, and relufied Llama 7B (ReLU) trails the SiLU original by only 0.4% (34.7% vs. 35.1%) at 72% FLOPS. Against structured 2:4 weight pruning baselines (SparseGPT, Wanda, magnitude pruning) on Llama-7B at ~50% FLOPS, relufied models substantially outperform: ReLU(s2) achieves 66.4% avg zero-shot accuracy vs. 60.1–60.8% for all three pruning methods, with the gap most pronounced on TriviaQA (48.5% vs. 19.3–26.8%). The paper also shows in Appendix E that a relufied large model (e.g., OPT 6.7B at 2.8G FLOPS) outperforms the equivalent-FLOPS dense smaller model by >2%, making relufication strictly superior to simply deploying a smaller dense model under the same compute budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) the original Transformer's use of ReLU, which was later displaced by GELU/SiLU/SwiGLU based on small-scale studies (Shazeer, 2020; Narang et al., 2021); (2) activation sparsity research (Kurtz et al., 2020; Jaszczur et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2023b) that demonstrated ReLU's sparsity benefits but assumed models were already trained with ReLU; and (3) speculative decoding (Leviathan et al., 2023) for memory-bound inference. The paper's key departure is 'relufication'—a surgical replacement of non-ReLU activations in already-pretrained LLMs followed by brief fine-tuning—bridging the gap between the sparsity literature and the reality that most deployed models (Llama, Falcon, PaLM) use non-ReLU activations. It further connects aggregated activation sparsity to speculative decoding, a link not previously explored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: relufication offers a concrete, low-cost procedure (30–50B tokens of fine-tuning, ~3–5% of original training) to reduce inference FLOPS by up to 3× on existing LLMs without retraining from scratch, which is critical for deployment on resource-constrained devices. The conceptual finding that activation function choice has negligible impact on performance at scale (1B+ parameters, 100B+ tokens) challenges the field's investment in GELU/SiLU/SwiGLU as meaningfully superior to ReLU. Open questions include whether the 1–2% performance gap fully closes with more fine-tuning, whether shifted ReLU's sparsity degradation during fine-tuning can be mitigated, and whether the approach extends to MoE architectures or 100B+ parameter models. The paper also leaves the QKV projection sparsity (lower than FFN) as an underexplored avenue.
