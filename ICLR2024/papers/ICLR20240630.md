## Problem

Traditional additive mixture models require convex combinations of components, which forces an unnecessarily large number of components to model distributions with 'holes' or complex structure. Allowing negative mixture weights (subtractive mixtures) could drastically reduce the number of components needed, but ensuring the resulting function remains a valid non-negative probability distribution is non-trivial. This paper addresses how to represent, learn, and perform tractable inference on deep subtractive mixtures while guaranteeing non-negativity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes i.i.d. data samples (continuous or discrete) along with a region graph (RG) structure that defines how variables are hierarchically partitioned into sub-regions. Input layers compute parametric functions over variable subsets (e.g., Gaussians, splines, categoricals, embeddings, or Binomials), and the circuit architecture is determined by the RG topology (linear tree, binary tree, or randomized tree). The output is a normalized probability distribution c²(X)/Z supporting exact marginalization, conditioning, and autoregressive sampling in a single feed-forward pass, with the normalization constant Z computed via O(K²) integrals over products of input-layer functions.

## Method

The core mechanism is squaring a non-convex linear combination of components: c²(X) = (Σᵢ wᵢ cᵢ(X))², where mixture weights wᵢ are unconstrained real numbers, guaranteeing non-negativity without ad-hoc per-family constraints. In the deep setting, the paper formalizes circuits as tensorized computational graphs (input, Hadamard-product, and sum layers) built on tree region graphs, and derives Algorithm 1 that recursively squares each layer via Kronecker products (W⊗W for sum layers, element-wise squaring for product layers), preserving structured-decomposability and enabling tractable marginalization in O(L·M²) time. Training uses MLE with batched stochastic gradient descent, where the log-likelihood decomposes into -|D|log Z + 2Σ log|c(x)|, requiring Z to be computed only once per batch. The key theoretical result proves an exponential separation: there exist non-negative functions compactly encoded by a single NPC² for which any structured-decomposable monotonic PC requires size 2^Ω(|X|), establishing that negative parameters (not just squaring) provide the expressiveness gain.

## Results

On five multivariate density estimation benchmarks (Gas, Electricity, Higgs, BSDS500, and one additional dataset), NPC²s with Gaussian input layers and binary tree region graphs achieve the best average test log-likelihoods among all tractable models, outperforming monotonic PCs on four of the five datasets. On the Gas dataset, NPC²s with binary tree RGs surpass TTDE (a Born-machine-style tensor-train model). In the GPT-2 distillation experiment, NPC²s achieve test log-likelihoods significantly closer to GPT-2's own training log-likelihood (≈ −52) than monotonic PCs, with the difference being statistically significant for most layer sizes K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the intractable normalizing flow baselines (RealNVP, MADE, MAF, NSF), NPC²s are positioned as a tractable alternative that achieves competitive log-likelihoods while uniquely supporting exact marginalization and conditioning in a single feed-forward pass. Among tractable baselines, NPC²s with Gaussian input layers and binary tree region graphs outperform monotonic PCs (MPCs) on four of five multivariate datasets, with the gap attributed to the expressiveness gain from negative parameters rather than squaring alone (squared monotonic PCs, MPC², show smaller improvements). On the Gas dataset specifically, NPC²s with binary tree RGs surpass TTDE (Novikov et al., 2021), which is the closest architectural analogue (a Born machine / tensor-train density estimator). In the GPT-2 distillation setting, NPC²s (corresponding to squared inhomogeneous HMMs / Born machines) achieve test log-likelihoods significantly closer to GPT-2's own score (≈ −52) than monotonic PCs (inhomogeneous HMMs), with p-values confirming significance for most layer sizes. On 2D synthetic data, NPC²s with spline input layers clearly outperform both MPCs and MPC²s in capturing complex target densities (e.g., ring distributions), while on discrete data the advantage is most pronounced when using parameter-efficient Binomial input layers rather than already-expressive categoricals or embeddings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the probabilistic circuit (PC) framework by lifting the non-negativity constraint on sum-layer parameters and introducing a squaring operation that guarantees a valid distribution without ad-hoc per-family constraints. It builds directly on the tensorized circuit formalism of Mari et al. (2023) and the structured-decomposability-based squaring algorithm of Vergari et al. (2021), generalizing both to non-monotonic circuits and deriving a tighter O(L·M²) marginalization bound. A key conceptual contribution is showing that Born machines (tensor networks with the Born rule), PSD kernel models, and square-root density estimators are all special cases of the proposed NPC² class, unifying previously disconnected literatures under the circuit language and explaining their tractability via structured-decomposability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exponential expressiveness separation (Theorem 1) provides a rigorous justification for why subtractive mixtures can be fundamentally more parameter-efficient than additive ones, not merely a practical heuristic. The unification of Born machines, PSD models, and NPC²s opens a pathway for cross-pollination between tensor network and probabilistic circuit communities, e.g., importing better learning schemes or more flexible factorization structures. Open questions include recovering a latent-variable interpretation for NPC²s (which is invalidated by negative parameters), developing regularization to address the observed overfitting in LLM distillation, and extending the lower bound from structured-decomposable to general decomposable monotonic PCs.
