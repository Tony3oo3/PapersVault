## Problem

Fitting the spike-level input/output relationship of a detailed biophysical cortical pyramidal neuron previously required temporal convolutional networks with millions of parameters, suggesting either that cortical neurons are extraordinarily complex or that the TCN's inductive biases are misaligned with actual neuronal computation. This paper asks whether a biologically motivated recurrent architecture with appropriate inductive biases—leaky memory units and nonlinear dendritic integration—can capture the same I/O mapping with orders-of-magnitude fewer parameters, and what computational capabilities such a design unlocks for long-range sequence modeling.

## Input / Output

On NeuronIO, the ELM consumes 1278 binary pre-synaptic spike channels (values in {-1, 0, +1}) at 1 ms resolution and predicts both somatic membrane voltage (continuous) and output spikes (binary) simultaneously. On neuromorphic and LRA benchmarks, it processes spike-encoded digit sequences (700 channels), grayscale pixel sequences (8 or 16 levels), or one-hot token sequences, producing a class logit vector via a linear readout over the memory state. The recurrent cell operates with a fictitious elapsed time Δt (in ms) between consecutive inputs, allowing the same architecture to handle variable temporal resolutions from 1 ms to 100 ms binning.

## Method

The ELM neuron is a single recurrent cell with four components: (1) current-based synapse dynamics that low-pass-filter binary spike inputs with a shared timescale τ_s; (2) d_m leaky memory units, each with its own learnable timescale τ_m, updated via m_t = κ_m·m_{t-1} + (1−κ_m)·λ·MLP(s_t, m_{t-1}) where κ_m = exp(−Δt/τ_m); (3) a multilayer perceptron (one hidden layer of size 2d_m) that performs highly nonlinear integration of synaptic input into memory updates, analogous to dendritic computation; and (4) a linear output readout. The Branch-ELM variant inserts a virtual dendritic branching stage that sums d_brch neighboring synapses into d_tree branch activations before the MLP, exploiting near-linear computation within branches to reduce parameters ~7×. Ablations reveal that ~20 memory units with timescales reaching ≥25 ms and strong nonlinear integration are necessary for accurate cortical I/O fitting, while long-range LRA tasks (e.g., Pathfinder-X at 16k context) require scaling to 150 memory units with timescales initialized on a log scale matched to sequence length.

## Results

On the NeuronIO dataset, the Branch-ELM neuron achieves 0.9924 spike-prediction AUC with only 8,104 trainable parameters, a ~1,200× reduction over the ~10M-parameter TCN previously required, and the vanilla ELM crosses the 0.991 AUC threshold at 53K parameters (~200× fewer than TCN, ~6× fewer than the 266K LSTM baseline). On the Long Range Arena benchmarks, the ELM neuron (150 memory units, ~100K parameters) achieves 77.29% accuracy on the notoriously difficult Pathfinder-X task (16K context length), where both Transformer and Longformer completely fail, and outperforms Chrono-LSTM on all six LRA tasks. On the SHD-Adding neuromorphic task, the ELM neuron reaches 82% test accuracy at 2ms bin size while the LSTM fails entirely (10% accuracy).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On NeuronIO, the ELM/Branch-ELM dramatically outperforms the TCN (Beniaguev et al., 2021) in parameter efficiency: 8K–53K parameters vs. ~10M, while also achieving better voltage prediction RMSE. The ELM also beats the LSTM baseline (266K params, 0.9913 AUC) with fewer parameters and higher AUC (0.9918 at 53K). Classic LIF and ALIF models (Table S4) achieve only 0.9117 and 0.9115 AUC respectively, far below the 0.991 threshold, confirming the need for the ELM's multi-timescale memory and nonlinear integration. On LRA, the ELM beats Chrono-LSTM on all six tasks (e.g., 77.29 vs. FAIL on Pathfinder-X, 80.3 vs. 75.4 on Text) and substantially outperforms Transformer and Longformer (e.g., 84.93 vs. 57.46 on Retrieval). However, the purpose-built S4 and Mega architectures (both ~600K params, multi-layered) still outperform the single-layer ELM on most LRA tasks (e.g., Mega achieves 97.98% vs. 77.29% on Pathfinder-X), though the ELM is the only non-purpose-built model to reliably solve Pathfinder-X. On SHD-Adding, the ELM (82% at 2ms bin) vastly outperforms the LSTM (10%) and SNN (~8%), with the gap widening at smaller bin sizes where the LSTM completely fails.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the phenomenological neuron modeling line initiated by Poirazi et al. (2003), who proposed representing dendritic integration as a two-layer ANN, and by Beniaguev et al. (2021), who used a temporal convolutional network with ~10M parameters to fit a biophysical pyramidal neuron's spike-level I/O relationship. The key departure is replacing the feedforward TCN with a recurrent cell that combines explicit learnable memory timescales (in the spirit of Leaky-RNNs and Chrono-LSTM) with a single MLP for nonlinear synaptic integration, achieving the same fit with 53K (or 8K for Branch-ELM) parameters. The architecture also draws on the broader trend in sequence modeling toward explicit timescale parameterization (S4, Mega, Chrono-LSTM) but applies it within a single-cell recurrent architecture rather than multi-layered stacks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 200× parameter reduction over TCN for matching biophysical neuron I/O suggests that biologically-motivated inductive biases (slow leaky memory, nonlinear dendritic integration) are far more important than raw model capacity for capturing cortical neuron computations, raising fundamental questions about the computational sophistication of individual neurons. The ELM's ability to solve Pathfinder-X (77% at 16k context) where Transformers and Chrono-LSTM fail entirely is notable, though S4 and Mega still outperform it on most LRA tasks, indicating that a single ELM cell has limits. Key open questions include whether networks of smaller ELM neurons can match deep architectures, what minimal dendritic nonlinearity is truly required, and whether the BPTT-trained results transfer to biologically plausible learning rules.
