## Problem

Most fact verification methods rely on annotated claim-evidence pairs for training, making them expensive and slow to develop. While unsupervised and self-supervised learning have advanced rapidly in other NLP domains, only a handful of strategies have been proposed specifically for textual fact verification, leaving a gap in how to learn high-quality claim-fact alignments without human labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240343/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a batch of unlabeled textual claims and a knowledge graph represented as (head, relation, tail) triples (e.g., Wikidata5m). A frozen pre-trained language model (e.g., GPT-2-XL) produces a per-claim feature tensor via global average pooling, while a Relational Graph Attention Network (3 layers, hidden size 512) maps the KG into node embeddings that are combined by an MLP into per-fact vectors. The output is a set of claim-fact relevance scores from which top-K facts are selected; for downstream evaluation, a linear probe on the distilled features yields a verification label (SUPPORTED / REFUTED / NOT ENOUGH INFO).

## Method

SFAVEL distills semantic knowledge from a frozen language-model teacher into a smaller knowledge-graph student via a three-term self-supervised loss: (1) a claim-fact distillation loss that minimizes L2-normalized MSE between teacher and student fact embeddings, (2) an intra-sample contrastive loss that pulls top-K positive fact embeddings toward the claim representation while pushing negative facts away, and (3) a pairwise scoring loss that enforces a margin between positive and negative fact scores. Negative instances are generated two ways: in-batch perturbation (randomly swapping the head or tail entity of a positive triple while preserving entity type) and in-knowledge-base sampling (drawing facts at least H hops away from the positive set to encourage broader exploration). The scoring function is simply the L2 distance between the claim embedding and each fact embedding, and the top-K facts by score form the positive sub-graph used in all three loss terms. This entirely unsupervised pretraining, followed by a lightweight linear probe, achieves state-of-the-art on FEVER (+8% accuracy) and FB15k-237 (+5.3% Hits@1) without any annotated training data.

## Results

On the FEVER benchmark, SFAVEL with a GPT-2-XL backbone achieves 89.48% label accuracy and 86.15% FEVER score on the test set using only a linear probe, outperforming the best supervised method (ProoFVer) by +10.01% label accuracy and the best prior unsupervised method (Jobanputra) by +9.23%. On FB15k-237, SFAVEL achieves +5.3% Hits@1 over the previous state-of-the-art. In low-data settings, SFAVEL reaches 76.14% dev accuracy with only 1% of labeled data (GPT-2-XL backbone), surpassing prior state-of-the-art when 5% of labels are available.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFAVEL outperforms all 13 baselines across both benchmarks. On FEVER, the largest gap is against GEAR (Zhou et al., 2019) at +17.88% test label accuracy (89.48 vs. 71.60), while the closest competitor is the unsupervised Jobanputra (2019) method at 80.25% test LA, which SFAVEL surpasses by +9.23%. Among supervised baselines, ProoFVer (Krishna et al., 2021) is the strongest at 79.47% test LA, still trailing SFAVEL by +10.01%. On FB15k-237, SFAVEL improves Hits@1 by +5.3% over MoCoSA (He et al., 2023), the prior best at 29.2% Hits@1, and also surpasses SimKGC (Wang et al., 2022; 24.6% Hits@1), KG-S2S (Chen et al., 2022; 25.7%), StAR (Wang et al., 2021; 20.5%), and KG-BERT (Yao et al., 2019). Notably, SFAVEL is the only fully unsupervised method in the comparison, yet it exceeds every supervised baseline by at least +12.63% test label accuracy on FEVER.

## Relation to Prior Work

SFAVEL sits at the intersection of three research threads: knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015; Zagoruyko & Komodakis, 2017), contrastive representation learning (MoCo, He et al., 2020; Frosst et al., 2019), and graph-based fact verification (GEAR, KGAT, DREAM). It departs from prior unsupervised fact verification work (Jobanputra, 2019; LaPraDoR, Xu et al., 2022) by introducing a task-specific contrastive loss with multiple positive keys and a scoring margin, rather than relying on generic retrieval objectives. The key conceptual departure is using a frozen pre-trained LM as a teacher to distill semantic alignment signals into a small relational graph attention network, eliminating the need for any annotated claim-evidence pairs while preserving the structural reasoning that supervised KG methods provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a fully unsupervised method outperforms the best supervised baseline (ProoFVer) by nearly 10% label accuracy on FEVER challenges the assumption that annotation is necessary for high-quality claim-fact alignment, and the low-data regime results (competitive performance at 1% labeled data) have practical implications for domains where annotation is expensive or infeasible. Limitations include dependence on a specific knowledge base (Wikidata5m), reliance on a frozen LM backbone whose quality bounds the ceiling, and evaluation primarily on a single benchmark with a linear probe. Open questions include generalizability to multi-hop reasoning, other knowledge bases, and whether the distillation framework can be extended to open-domain evidence retrieval without a structured KG.
