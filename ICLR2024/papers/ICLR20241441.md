## Problem

Temporal abstractions that capture environment dynamics (e.g., k-step reachability relations) fail to scale when the transition relation depends on many state variables, while spatial abstractions that group states into sets lack a temporal mechanism to propose intermediate subgoals when the low-level policy is still non-optimal. This paper addresses the gap by combining both abstractions in a single hierarchical framework that can handle complex continuous control dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 30-dimensional state vector s_t from the Ant environments, a task goal g*, and an oracle ψ(s) that selects task-relevant dimensions (e.g., x,y for Ant Maze; x,y,z for Ant Fall; x,y,θx,θy,θz for Ant Maze Cam). It produces three hierarchical policies operating at different timescales (k=30, l=10, 1), a reachability-aware spatial abstraction N represented as a partition of the state space into disjoint hyperrectangles, and continuous 8-dimensional environment actions. The abstraction is refined iteratively after each episode using a forward model and abstract interpretation.

## Method

STAR is a three-level feudal HRL algorithm where the Commander selects an abstract goal G from a discrete set-based goal space every k steps using Q-learning, the Tutor selects a concrete intermediate subgoal g ∈ S every l steps to bridge the gap between the current state and the abstract goal, and the Controller executes actions via TD3. The spatial abstraction N maps states to sets (hyperrectangles) and is refined after each episode: a forward model F_k predicts k-step reachability, and Ai2 abstract interpretation computes over-approximations of the reached state set from each abstract goal; when the pairwise reachability property is violated (i.e., not all states in G_i reach G_j under the low-level policy), G_i is recursively split. The key insight is that the Tutor's temporal abstraction compensates for the non-optimality of the low-level policy during early training when the spatial abstraction is coarse, while the reachability-aware refinement progressively tightens the abstraction to faithfully represent environment dynamics. Theoretical results establish a suboptimality bound for policies trained under reachability-aware abstractions and prove that a finite number of refinements yields such an abstraction.

## Results

STAR is evaluated on three continuous control tasks (Ant Maze, Ant Fall, Ant Maze Cam) with 30-dimensional state spaces and 8-dimensional action spaces, using success rate (distance to goal < 5) as the metric, averaged over 10 runs. STAR achieves the highest success rate with the fewest timesteps across all three tasks, outperforming all four baselines. Notably, GARA (spatial abstraction only) is unable to solve even the easiest task (Ant Maze), while STAR successfully solves all three tasks including the most complex one (Ant Maze Cam) which requires 5 oracle dimensions (x, y, θx, θy, θz).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STAR outperforms all four baselines across all three tasks. GARA (Zadem et al., 2023), which uses only spatial abstraction via reachability analysis with a two-level hierarchy, is unable to solve Ant Maze (the easiest task) because its coarse initial abstraction makes goal sets too distant for the low-level agent to reach. HIRO (Nachum et al., 2018) learns less efficient policies due to the absence of any spatial abstraction, sampling goals directly from the full state space. HRAC (Zhang et al., 2023) fails on both Ant Fall and Ant Maze Cam because its k-step reachability relation becomes too complex to learn when the oracle includes more than 2 dimensions (z for Ant Fall; θx, θy, θz for Ant Maze Cam). LESSON (Li et al., 2021) fails specifically on Ant Maze Cam because the orientation angles (θx, θy, θz) vary rapidly across k steps, violating the slow-dynamics assumption that underpins its latent goal representation. STAR's combination of a three-level feudal hierarchy (temporal abstraction via the Tutor agent) with reachability-aware spatial abstraction (via the Commander) allows it to scale to tasks where the high-level dynamics depend on 3–5 oracle dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

STAR extends GARA's set-based reachability-aware spatial abstraction by inserting a middle-level 'Tutor' agent that provides temporal abstraction, addressing GARA's scalability limitation when goal sets are too distant for the low-level policy to reach. The feudal architecture and policy correction mechanism are inherited from HIRO (Nachum et al., 2018), while the Tutor and Controller networks reuse HRAC's (Zhang et al., 2023) TD3-based implementation and hyperparameters. Conceptually, the paper synthesises two previously separate lines of work—spatial goal-space discretisation (GARA) and temporally-abstract goal sampling (HRAC, LESSON)—into a single three-level hierarchy, and provides the first suboptimality bound for policies learned under a reachability-aware set-based abstraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that neither spatial nor temporal abstraction alone suffices for complex continuous-control tasks: GARA fails even on the simplest Ant Maze, while HRAC degrades as the oracle dimensionality grows. The theoretical guarantee that a finite sequence of reachability-aware refinements converges to a valid abstraction gives practitioners a principled stopping criterion. However, the results are limited to three Ant-based tasks with 30-dimensional state spaces, the suboptimality bounds are loose (scaling linearly in trajectory length), and the approach assumes deterministic dynamics—extending to stochastic or non-Markovian environments remains open, as do questions about how the abstraction refinement interacts with the growing Q-table in the Commander.
