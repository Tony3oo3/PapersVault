## Problem

Bilevel programs with discrete (binary) upper-level decisions are computationally intractable because the lower-level value function φ(x) has no closed-form expression when the lower level involves integer variables, and gradient-based methods are inapplicable to discrete formulations. Existing solvers like MiBS rely on cutting-plane approaches that scale poorly, and no learning-based method had been explored for this class of problems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241255/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a bilevel program where the upper-level decision x ∈ {0,1}^n (the 'binary tender') links to a lower-level LP or MILP, and produces the optimal solution (x*, y*) along with a certified upper bound on the optimal objective. The neural network input is the binary vector x (or the augmented vector [x, 1−x] for ISNN), and its scalar output is the approximate value function φ̃(x). The final single-level reformulation is a mixed-integer program solvable by off-the-shelf solvers, with the ReLU activations either linearized via big-M constraints (GNN) or replaced by supermodular linear inequalities requiring no auxiliary binary variables (ISNN).

## Method

The core pipeline is: (1) enhanced sampling that solves a random quadratic program over the BP's feasible region (optionally constrained by a current upper bound) to generate high-quality (x, φ(x)) pairs near the optimum; (2) supervised training of either a general neural network (GNN) or a novel input supermodular neural network (ISNN) with ReLU activations and passthrough connections, where ISNN enforces non-negative weights on W₁:₍ₖ₊₁₎ and D₂:ₖ to guarantee a supermodular output; (3) embedding the trained network's mixed-integer representation as an optimality cut g(y,x) ≥ φ̃(x̃) into a single-level reformulation. The key architectural insight is that for ISNN, supermodularity of φ̃ allows the optimality constraint to be expressed as linear inequalities via marginal-value cuts (Proposition 7) without any auxiliary binary variables or big-M coefficients, unlike the GNN path. An iterative outer loop (Algorithm 2) repeatedly resamples, retrains, and resolves to tighten the upper bound, with theoretical guarantees of universal approximation and exact fitting conditions for both network types.

## Results

For bilevel programs with binary tender (n = 10–60), the proposed neural bilevel algorithm achieves a 0% objective gap (true optimum) at n = 10 for both LP and MILP lower levels. At n ≥ 40, computational time is significantly shorter than the state-of-the-art MiBS solver, while the objective difference (gap vs. MiBS's 1-hour solution) exceeds 15% for LP lower levels but stays below 5% for MILP lower levels (except n = 30). ISNN consistently outperforms GNN, reducing the objective gap by more than 10% when n ≥ 40. Increasing the number of iterations (N_iteration) monotonically decreases the average objective gap, validating the enhanced sampling strategy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is MiBS (Tahernejad et al., 2020), a branch-and-cut solver for mixed-integer bilevel linear programs, run with a 1-hour time limit. On LP lower-level instances, the proposed method (both GNN and ISNN) matches MiBS exactly at n = 10 (true optimum) but shows a >15% objective gap at n = 30, 50, 60; however, its computational time is dramatically shorter than MiBS's 1-hour limit for n ≥ 40. On MILP lower-level instances, the proposed method actually outperforms MiBS at n = 20, 40, and 50, where MiBS reports incorrect optimal solutions, while the neural approach finds better feasible solutions in much less time. ISNN consistently beats GNN across all instance sizes and lower-level types, with the advantage growing to >10% relative gap reduction at n ≥ 40. The paper also compares three sampling strategies (random, Latin hypercube, enhanced) in Appendix D.3, showing that enhanced sampling guarantees feasible samples and yields a near-linear number of samples under a fixed 30-minute budget, whereas random sampling is unstable and often fails to find enough feasible points for n = 30 or 40.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Input Convex Neural Network (ICNN) paradigm of Amos et al. (2017) from continuous convexity to discrete supermodularity, introducing the Input Supermodular Neural Network (ISNN) whose architecture guarantees a supermodular output mapping. It also builds on the mixed-integer linear representation of ReLU networks (Fischetti & Jo, 2018; Serra et al., 2018) to embed the learned value function into a solvable single-level formulation, and adapts the supermodular set-function linearization of Nemhauser & Wolsey (1981) to avoid big-M auxiliary variables for ISNN. Unlike Molan & Schmidt (2022), who approximate the lower-level optimal decision y*, this paper directly learns the value function φ(x), enabling a cleaner optimality-cut formulation. The enhanced sampling strategy departs from naive random or Latin hypercube sampling by formulating sample generation as a tractable optimization subproblem constrained near the current best solution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first neural-network-based approach to bilevel programs with discrete (binary) tender variables, a setting where gradient-based methods are inapplicable and existing cutting-plane solvers like MiBS struggle at moderate dimensions. The ISNN architecture and its representability guarantees offer a principled way to inject structural knowledge (supermodularity) into the approximation, consistently outperforming unconstrained GNNs. Open questions include scalability beyond n=120 (where sampling time dominates and accuracy degrades), the tightness of the approximation error bound (Proposition 6) in practice, and whether the supermodularity assumption holds broadly enough across application domains to justify ISNN over GNN. The method's reliance on solving the lower-level problem at each sample point also limits its applicability to settings where the follower's problem is itself computationally expensive.
