## Problem

Laplacian eigenvector-based positional encodings for graphs suffer from two fundamental issues: non-uniqueness (multiple valid eigendecompositions of the same Laplacian) and instability (small Laplacian perturbations can produce entirely different eigenspaces when eigenvalues are close). Existing methods address basis invariance but overlook stability, because they perform a 'hard partition' of eigenspaces that is highly sensitive to perturbations, leading to poor out-of-distribution generalization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the d smallest eigenvalues (λ ∈ ℝ^d) and their corresponding eigenvectors (V ∈ ℝ^{n×d}) from the normalized Laplacian EVD, rather than the raw adjacency matrix. It outputs an n×p node-level positional encoding tensor that is concatenated with node features and fed into a base GNN (GIN) for downstream graph-level regression or classification. The number of eigenvectors d is a hyperparameter, and the stability guarantee explicitly depends on the eigengap γ = λ_{d+1} − λ_d between the d-th and (d+1)-th smallest eigenvalues.

## Method

SPE replaces the hard partitioning of eigenspaces with a soft, eigenvalue-dependent partition: m permutation-equivariant functions φ_ℓ: ℝ^d → ℝ^d map eigenvalues to per-eigenvector weights, and the term V diag(φ_ℓ(λ)) V^⊤ is computed for each channel before concatenation into an n×n×m tensor processed by a permutation-equivariant network ρ (e.g., GIN). The key insight is that requiring φ_ℓ to be Lipschitz-continuous ensures that as two distinct eigenvalues converge (the degenerate points causing instability), their eigenvectors are processed increasingly similarly, so eigenvector 'swaps' at degeneracy produce negligible output change. This yields a provable Hölder-continuity bound on the positional encoding w.r.t. the input Laplacian, with the bound governed by the eigengap γ and the Lipschitz constants of φ_ℓ and ρ. The architecture is shown to be universally expressive for all continuous basis-invariant functions (subsuming BasisNet) and capable of counting 3-, 4-, and 5-cycles, while empirical results confirm a trade-off where higher stability reduces training fit but improves OOD generalization.

## Results

On ZINC (molecular property regression), SPE with all eigenvectors achieves a test MAE of 0.0693±0.0040, substantially outperforming the best baseline SignNet (Full) at 0.0853±0.0026 and BasisNet (Full) at 0.1555±0.0124. On Alchemy, SPE (Full) achieves 0.108±0.001 MAE, edging out BasisNet (Full) at 0.110±0.001. On the DrugOOD out-of-distribution benchmark, SPE shows consistent OOD-Test AUC improvements over unstable baselines, most notably on the Scaffold domain where SPE reaches 69.64±0.49 versus SignNet's 66.43±1.06 and BasisNet's 66.32±5.68.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPE outperforms all three baselines on ZINC, the most demanding regression benchmark: it beats SignNet (Full) by 18.7% in MAE (0.0693 vs. 0.0853) and BasisNet (Full) by 55.5% (0.0693 vs. 0.1555). PEG, the only other provably stable method, is far less expressive and achieves 0.1444 MAE with 8 eigenvectors (worse than No PE's 0.1772 only slightly, and much worse than SPE). On DrugOOD, the unstable methods SignNet and BasisNet suffer large OOD performance drops on the Scaffold domain (66.43 and 66.32 AUC respectively) compared to SPE (69.64) and even No PE (68.00), confirming that stability directly translates to better domain generalization. On Alchemy, all methods perform similarly (within 0.004 MAE), suggesting the task is less sensitive to positional encoding quality. In the cycle counting task (Figure 3), SPE significantly outperforms SignNet on 3-, 4-, 5-, and 6-cycle counting, demonstrating that the soft-partition design retains high expressivity while maintaining stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPE sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work on Laplacian-based positional encodings: BasisNet/SignNet (Lim et al., 2023), which achieved basis invariance through hard partitioning of eigensubspaces but suffered from instability, and PEG (Wang et al., 2022a), which achieved provable stability by treating all eigenvectors homogeneously but lost expressivity. The key departure is the 'soft partition' mechanism—applying eigenvalue-dependent, continuous weighting functions φ_ℓ to eigenvectors before recombining them—which simultaneously preserves the distinctness of eigensubspaces (expressivity) and ensures smooth transitions at degenerate eigenvalue points (stability). The theoretical framework also draws on permutation equivariant network design (DeepSets, GIN) and domain adaptation theory (Wasserstein distance bounds) to connect stability to out-of-distribution generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPE is the first positional encoding architecture that is simultaneously provably stable and universally expressive for basis-invariant functions, resolving a fundamental tension that prior methods could only address one-sidedly. The empirical validation on DrugOOD (particularly the Scaffold domain, where unstable methods like BasisNet drop from ~86 to ~78 AUC while SPE maintains ~88) provides concrete evidence that stability directly translates to OOD robustness in practical drug discovery settings. Open questions include whether the eigengap-dependent stability bound (γ = λ_{d+1} − λ_d) becomes a practical bottleneck for graphs with dense spectra, whether the O(n³) eigendecomposition cost limits scalability to very large graphs, and whether the soft-partition principle extends beyond Laplacian eigenvectors to other spectral decompositions (e.g., adjacency, signless Laplacian).
