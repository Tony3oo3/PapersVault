## Problem

Existing dynamic scene reconstruction methods either model 3D point motion (limiting them to single articulated objects or requiring depth maps) or directly predict the SDF at each time step, which prevents recovery of scene flow. No prior approach can simultaneously handle unconstrained scenes with multiple non-rigid objects and extract the 3D motion (scene flow) of the scene surface.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240918/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB video (10 fixed cameras at 1920×1080 for CMU Panoptic; 4–12 cameras at 1024×1024 for Tensor4D) along with known camera poses and intrinsics, processing 24-frame clips. It outputs a 3D mesh extracted via marching cubes on a 512³ grid, the SDF flow field, and the scene flow (angular velocity ω and translational velocity v) derived analytically per surface point by solving a local least-squares linear system over ~200 neighboring points. Projected scene flow yields optical flow as a byproduct.

## Method

Instead of predicting the SDF or deforming 3D points, the network directly estimates the SDF flow ∂s/∂t — the first-order time derivative of the signed distance function — via an MLP f(x,t). The SDF at any time is recovered by integrating the SDF flow from an initial SDF f₀(x), and density is computed through the VolSDF Laplace CDF parameterization within a standard NeRF volume rendering pipeline. The key theoretical contribution is a linear relation ∂s/∂t = −(ω×x + v)ᵀ n(x) linking SDF flow to scene flow, which allows the 6-DoF rigid motion (ω, v) to be solved analytically from the SDF flow of ≥6 surface points via a least-squares system. Training minimizes RGB photometric loss plus an eikonal constraint on the SDF normal, and the linear SDF-flow/scene-flow relationship can optionally be used as an additional consistency regularizer to improve both reconstruction and motion estimation.

## Results

On the CMU Panoptic dataset, the proposed SDF flow method achieves an average overall distance of 15.2 mm, outperforming NDR (20.3 mm) and Tensor4D (21.1 mm) across all five scenes. In terms of accuracy, the method reaches 11.8 mm on average versus 18.2 mm (NDR) and 16.5 mm (Tensor4D), while completeness is 18.7 mm versus 22.3 mm and 25.6 mm respectively. For scene flow evaluation, the derived optical flow achieves an average EPE of 2.04 pixels compared to NDR's 3.69 pixels, confirming that the SDF flow yields more accurate 3D motion estimation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two NeRF-based dynamic scene reconstruction methods: NDR (Cai et al., 2022), which uses a bijective mapping to a canonical space, and Tensor4D (Shao et al., 2023), which decomposes 4D space into 2D planes. The proposed SDF flow method wins on all metrics across all five CMU Panoptic scenes. Against NDR, the improvement is most pronounced on accuracy (11.8 vs. 18.2 mm, a 35% reduction) and overall distance (15.2 vs. 20.3 mm). Against Tensor4D, the method achieves a 44% improvement in completeness (18.7 vs. 25.6 mm) and a 28% improvement in overall distance (15.2 vs. 21.1 mm). For scene flow, the SDF flow-derived optical flow achieves 2.04 px average EPE versus NDR's 3.69 px, a 45% reduction, demonstrating that the analytical SDF-flow-to-scene-flow relationship produces more faithful 3D motion than NDR's bijective mapping approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the NeRF rendering pipeline (Mildenhall et al., 2021) and the SDF-based density parameterization from VolSDF (Yariv et al., 2021), but departs fundamentally from prior dynamic scene methods that model 3D point trajectories (NeRFies, D-NeRF) or map to higher-dimensional spaces for topology changes (NDR/HyperNeRF). Instead of predicting the SDF at each timestep as in Tensor4D, the authors propose representing the scene by the time derivative of the SDF (SDF flow), which naturally encodes surface evolution and topology changes. The key conceptual departure is the derivation of a closed-form linear relationship between SDF flow and scene flow (angular velocity + velocity), enabling analytical scene flow recovery without explicit supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The linear SDF-flow-to-scene-flow relationship is a clean theoretical contribution that provides a free regularization signal for both geometry and motion estimation, and could inform future dynamic scene representations beyond this specific pipeline. The method handles unconstrained multi-object dynamic scenes without depth maps, addressing a gap left by template-based or single-object approaches. Limitations include growing time complexity from numerical integration (mitigated potentially by fixed-window integration) and dependence on multi-view coverage. Open questions include whether the approach extends to monocular video and whether the scene flow regularization can be exploited more broadly in dynamic NeRF training.
