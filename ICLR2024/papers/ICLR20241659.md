## Problem

Bayesian optimization allows users to encode prior knowledge only through GP kernel hyperparameters (smoothness, output scale, noise), which cannot express beliefs about where the optimum lies, what the optimal value is, or preference relations between configurations. No existing framework integrates arbitrary user beliefs into the surrogate model in a Bayesian-principled manner, leaving knowledgeable practitioners unable to leverage domain expertise to accelerate optimization under tight budgets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241659/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes noisy black-box function evaluations y = f(x) + ε, a user-specified prior ρ over a function property (e.g., a Gaussian over the optimum location, a bound on the optimal value, or pairwise preference constraints), and the search space X ⊂ ℝ^d. It outputs the next query point x′ by maximizing a belief-weighted MC acquisition function, and internally maintains a non-Gaussian belief-weighted posterior p(f|D,ρ) represented as a weighted collection of pathwise-updated function samples rather than a closed-form GP posterior.

## Method

ColaBO introduces a belief-weighted prior p(f|ρ) ∝ p(f)ρ(f), where ρ(f) is a user-defined likelihood that assigns higher probability to functions whose properties (argmax location, max value, or preference ordering) match the user's belief. Samples from this prior are obtained via rejection sampling on draws from the standard GP prior (approximated with Random Fourier Features for scalability), retaining each draw with probability proportional to ρ(f). The posterior p(f|D,ρ) ∝ ρ(f)p(f|D) is then constructed by applying the deterministic pathwise update (decoupled posterior sampling) to each retained sample, so that ρ shapes the prior draw while D shapes the update independently. MC acquisition functions (LogEI, MES) are computed by averaging utilities over these belief-weighted samples, making the framework applicable to any MC acquisition function and any type of user belief without modifying the acquisition function's mathematical form.

## Results

On synthetic benchmarks with well-located priors (10% offset from optimum), both ColaBO-LogEI and ColaBO-MES achieve drastic speed-ups over their vanilla counterparts, reaching near-terminal performance substantially earlier and matching πBO across all five functions (Hartmann 4D/6D, Levy 5D, Rosenbrock 6D, Stybtang 7D). On poorly located priors, ColaBO recovers vanilla baseline performance within the 20D-iteration budget, whereas πBO remains misled. On the PD1 deep learning HPO tasks (CIFAR, LM1B, WMT), ColaBO finds configurations with near-terminal performance within roughly one-third of the 10D budget, outperforming all baselines on CIFAR and LM1B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla LogEI and vanilla MES, ColaBO variants show substantial acceleration on well-located priors (Figures 5, 7), with ColaBO-MES struggling similarly to vanilla MES on Rosenbrock (6D) where both underperform. Against πBO, ColaBO is comparable on well-located priors but clearly more robust on poorly located priors (Figure 6), recovering baseline performance within budget while πBO is more frequently misled. Against decoupled Thompson sampling (Wilson et al., 2020), ColaBO with accurate priors achieves faster convergence. On the LCBench 6D tasks (Figure 8), πBO is the overall best-performing method, with ColaBO-LogEI second; ColaBO substantially improves on non-prior baselines for 3 out of 5 tasks. On PD1 tasks (Figure 7), both ColaBO variants outperform πBO in initial convergence speed, with πBO offering competitive but slower early progress on CIFAR and LM1B.

## Relation to Prior Work

ColaBO builds on the decoupled posterior sampling framework of Wilson et al. (2020), which separates prior draws from deterministic data-dependent updates, and on the MC acquisition function paradigm (Wilson et al., 2017; Balandat et al., 2020) that computes utilities via function samples. It generalizes the prior-over-optimum concept introduced in PiBO (Hvarfner et al., 2022b) and BOPrO (Souza et al., 2021), which treated user beliefs as heuristic modifications to acquisition functions, by instead formulating the user belief as a proper Bayesian prior on the surrogate model, yielding a belief-weighted posterior p(f|D,ρ) ∝ ρ(f)p(f|D). This departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) the prior is applied to the surrogate rather than the acquisition function, making it orthogonal to the choice of acquisition function, and (2) it extends from priors over the optimum alone to arbitrary function properties (optimal value, preference relations).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ColaBO provides the first general, Bayesian-principled mechanism for domain experts to inject diverse types of knowledge (optimum location, optimal value bounds, preference relations) into BO, addressing a practical gap where knowledgeable practitioners with tight budgets could not leverage their expertise beyond kernel hyperparameters. The robustness finding—that ColaBO recovers baseline performance under misleading priors while PiBO frequently degrades—makes it a safer default for practitioners uncertain about their own beliefs. Key limitations include substantial computational overhead (tens of seconds per iteration), RFF approximation bias that is particularly severe for Matérn-5/2 kernels, and exponential scaling of the prior with dimensionality. Open questions include whether variational or pre-trained surrogate approaches can eliminate the MC sampling bottleneck, and whether the framework extends naturally to multi-fidelity or constrained settings.
