## Problem

Existing lip-to-speech methods produce low-intelligibility, unnatural speech on challenging in-the-wild datasets (LRS2, LRS3) with hundreds of speakers and unrestricted vocabulary, largely due to the inherent ambiguity of lip motion where multiple phonemes map to the same visual pattern. Prior approaches either ignore the text modality entirely or use ground-truth text only as a training-time loss, failing to resolve phoneme ambiguity at inference time.

## Input / Output

The method takes a silent talking-face video at 25 fps, which is preprocessed into an 88×88 grayscale mouth-crop video and a single 224×224 full-face image. At inference, a pre-trained lip-reading network independently predicts the spoken text from the mouth video, and this text is used to guide generation. The output is an 80-band mel-spectrogram at 16 kHz, converted to a raw audio waveform by a DiffWave vocoder; the system can generate up to ~18 seconds of speech from a single forward pass.

## Method

LipVoicer trains a diffusion model (MelGen) with a DiffWave residual backbone to generate mel-spectrograms conditioned on a video embedding (face image via ResNet-18 concatenated with lip video via 3D-conv + ShuffleNet-V2 + TCN), using classifier-free guidance during training but no text modality. At inference, a pre-trained lip-reader predicts the spoken text, and a fine-tuned ASR (Conformer with diffusion timestep embeddings) serves as a classifier whose gradient ∇log p(text|x_t) steers the denoising steps via classifier guidance, ensuring the generated speech matches the predicted transcription. A gradient normalization factor γ_t balances the magnitude mismatch between the diffusion noise prediction and the ASR gradient. This design decouples text accuracy (handled by the ASR) from voice characteristics and synchronization (handled by MelGen), and allows swapping either module without retraining.

## Results

On LRS3, LipVoicer achieves a WER of 21.4%, substantially outperforming Lip2Speech (57.4%), SVTS (82.4%), and VCA-GAN (90.6%), while approaching the ground-truth WER of 1.0%. On LRS2, LipVoicer reaches 17.8% WER versus 51.4% (Lip2Speech) and 100.7% (VCA-GAN). In human evaluation (MOS) on LRS3, LipVoicer scores 3.44–3.56 across intelligibility, naturalness, quality, and synchronization, compared to 1.99–2.71 for all baselines and 4.36–4.45 for ground truth. LipVoicer also matches ground-truth STOI-Net (0.92 vs. 0.93) and achieves DNSMOS of 3.11 vs. 2.27–2.42 for baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LipVoicer decisively outperforms all three baselines on every objective metric across both LRS2 and LRS3. On WER (the primary intelligibility metric), LipVoicer achieves 21.4% on LRS3, reducing the error by 36 points over Lip2Speech (57.4%), 61 points over SVTS (82.4%), and 69 points over VCA-GAN (90.6%); on LRS2 the gap is similarly large (17.8% vs. 51.4% and 100.7%). In human MOS evaluation on LRS3, LipVoicer scores 3.44–3.56 across all four criteria, exceeding the best baseline (Lip2Speech at 2.20–2.69) by roughly 1.2–1.3 points and approaching ground truth (4.36–4.45). On speech quality (DNSMOS) and intelligibility (STOI-Net), LipVoicer (3.11 / 0.92) nearly matches ground truth (3.30 / 0.93) while baselines lag significantly (2.27–2.42 / 0.63–0.67). On synchronization (SyncNet LSE-C), LipVoicer (6.239) surpasses SVTS (6.018) and both Lip2Speech (5.231) and VCA-GAN (5.255), though it remains slightly below ground truth (6.880). The ablation in Table 6 confirms that the ASR classifier guidance is the critical component: removing it (ω₂=0) causes WER to jump from 21.4% to 86.2%, essentially matching the worst baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

LipVoicer sits at the intersection of diffusion-based audio generation (building on DiffWave's residual backbone and DDPM framework) and classifier guidance techniques from image synthesis (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021; Ho & Salimans, 2021). Its key departure from prior lip-to-speech work (VCA-GAN, SVTS, Lip2Speech) is the use of a pre-trained lip-reading model to extract text at inference time, which then steers the diffusion process via an ASR-based classifier guidance mechanism — a strategy not previously applied in this domain. The feature extraction architecture follows VisualVoice (Gao & Grauman, 2021), and the gradient normalization for classifier guidance is adapted from Guided-TTS (Kim et al., 2022), but the combination of these components with lip-reading-derived text as an inference-time signal is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LipVoicer demonstrates that incorporating the text modality at inference time is critical for intelligibility in in-the-wild lip-to-speech, reducing WER from 51–100% (baselines) to ~18–21% on LRS2/LRS3. The modular design — where the lip-reader and ASR can be swapped without retraining — makes the system practical and future-proof as these components improve. However, the paper acknowledges a naturalness-intelligibility trade-off: ASR guidance can slightly degrade perceived naturalness, and the approach introduces a social risk of speech manipulation if adversarial text is injected. Open questions include whether the naturalness gap can be closed, whether the method extends to non-English or multi-speaker scenarios, and how it performs when the lip-reader itself is unreliable.
