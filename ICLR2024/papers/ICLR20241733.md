## Problem

The Conformer, while the dominant ASR encoder, processes sequences at a constant frame rate and incurs high quadratic attention cost, limiting both efficiency and scalability. Additionally, its use of LayerNorm can cause modules to become 'dead' (outputs near zero) and discards vector-length information, while standard Adam fails to adapt updates to varying parameter scales, slowing convergence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241733/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 80-dimensional Mel filter-bank features at 100 Hz (25 ms frames, 10 ms shift), first downsampled to 50 Hz by a Conv-Embed module, then processed through six U-Net-like stacks at frame rates of 50, 25, 12.5, 6.25, 12.5, and 25 Hz before a final Downsample module outputs 25 Hz encoder representations. These feed a pruned transducer decoder that emits at most one symbol per frame during beam search (beam size 4), producing transcribed text without external language model rescoring.

## Method

Zipformer replaces the constant-rate Conformer encoder with a U-Net-like structure of six cascaded stacks that downsample and upsample the sequence to progressively lower frame rates (down to 6.25 Hz in the middle), using learnable weighted averaging for downsampling and frame repetition for upsampling. Each Zipformer block decomposes multi-head self-attention into a shared Multi-Head Attention Weight (MHAW) module feeding one Non-Linear Attention (NLA) module and two Self-Attention (SA) modules, halving the quadratic attention cost while adding a Bypass module for learnable input-output mixing. LayerNorm is replaced by BiasNorm, which normalizes by RMS after subtracting a learnable channel-wise bias and scales by exp(γ), preserving length information and preventing gradient-sign oscillation that kills modules. Swish is replaced by SwooshR/SwooshL, which maintain non-vanishing slopes for negative inputs to avoid vanishing Adam denominators. Training uses a new ScaledAdam optimizer that multiplies each parameter update by its current RMS scale and adds an explicit gradient term for learning the parameter scale, paired with an Eden learning-rate schedule that depends on both step and epoch indices to keep per-data parameter change constant across batch sizes.

## Results

On LibriSpeech, Zipformer-L achieves 2.06%/4.63% WER (test-clean/test-other) with 148.4M parameters and 107.7 GFLOPs, outperforming the original Conformer-L (2.1%/4.3%) while using over 50% fewer FLOPs (107.7 vs. 294.2 GFLOPs). With extended training (170 epochs on 8 A100 GPUs), Zipformer-L* reaches 2.00%/4.38%, the first model to approach the original Conformer-L results. On Aishell-1, Zipformer-L achieves 4.28% CER, and on WenetSpeech it reaches 7.24%/12.06% (Test_Net/Test_Meeting), both outperforming all compared baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Zipformer outperforms all nine compared baselines on LibriSpeech. Against Squeezeformer-L (2.47%/5.97%), Zipformer-L achieves 2.06%/4.63% with 37% fewer parameters and 67% fewer FLOPs. Against E-Branchformer-L (2.14%/4.55%), Zipformer-L wins on test-clean (2.06% vs. 2.14%) and is competitive on test-other (4.63% vs. 4.55%) while using 62% fewer FLOPs (107.7 vs. 284.4). Against Branchformer (2.40%/5.50%), Zipformer-L leads by 0.34%/0.87%. Against the original Conformer-L (2.10%/4.30%), Zipformer-L is within 0.04% on test-clean and 0.33% on test-other, and Zipformer-L* (2.00%/4.38%) essentially matches it. Against the reproduced Conformer-L (2.46%/5.55%), Zipformer-L leads by 0.40%/0.92%. On Aishell-1, Zipformer-L (4.28% CER) beats Conformer in ESPnet (4.9%), Conformer in WeNet (4.61%), and Branchformer (4.43%). On WenetSpeech, Zipformer-L (7.24%/12.06%) outperforms Conformer-MoE(32e) (7.99%/13.69%) and all other baselines, while Zipformer-S (8.6%/13.97%) with only 32.3M parameters already beats Conformer in ESPnet (8.90%/15.90%).

## Relation to Prior Work

Zipformer is a comprehensive redesign of the Conformer encoder, the dominant ASR architecture since 2020. It shares the U-Net-like temporal downsampling concept with Squeezeformer but employs more aggressive and variable downsampling ratios across stacks, and it departs from Conformer's monolithic block by decomposing multi-head self-attention into reusable weight-computation and aggregation modules, adding a Non-Linear Attention pathway, and introducing Bypass connections. Orthogonally to the architecture, the paper introduces ScaledAdam, a parameter-scale-invariant optimizer derived from Adam, which the authors argue is a key enabler for removing per-module normalization layers and stabilizing training without them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Zipformer is notable as the first model to approach the originally reported Conformer-L results on LibriSpeech (2.00/4.38 vs. 2.1/4.3 WER), which had been difficult for other groups to reproduce, while simultaneously achieving over 50% inference speedup and lower peak memory. The ScaledAdam optimizer is a general-purpose contribution that could benefit training beyond ASR, and the public release of code in icefall lowers the barrier for adoption. Open questions include whether ScaledAdam's benefits transfer to other modalities and architectures, whether the specific multi-resolution frame-rate schedule is optimal, and whether the encoder-centric focus leaves room for further gains from decoder or joint-training innovations.
