## Problem

Dynamic Sparse Training (DST) algorithms require high topology-update frequency to maintain accuracy, which in turn demands large learning rates and batch sizes, causing prohibitive memory consumption and recompilation overhead on edge devices. The paper identifies the root cause as dormant neurons—convolution filters with large negative weights that produce all-zero post-ReLU outputs and block gradient flow—which persist and accumulate throughout DST training because standard magnitude-based pruning cannot remove them.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 128×128 scaled inputs for ImageNet on mobile hardware) and outputs class labels along with a trained sparse model at a target sparsity ratio (90%, 95%, or 98%). It operates exclusively on ReLU-based CNNs (ResNet-32/34/50, VGG-19) with fixed layer-wise sparsity (uniform or ERK distribution) to avoid hardware reconfiguration, and the deployed model is compiled to static OpenCL/C++ code for direct execution on mobile CPUs and GPUs.

## Method

NeurRev augments the standard DST prune-grow cycle with a two-step Search-and-Awake operation that targets the pruning side rather than the growing side. In the Search step, the algorithm tracks weight changes (Δθ) between consecutive NeurRev intervals to identify neurons whose weights have not updated (zero gradient), then sorts and selects the p·100% most negative weights among them. In the Awake step, it surgically prunes exactly p·‖θ‖₀ of those identified large negative weights, releasing the corresponding neurons from their dormant all-zero-output state and restoring gradient flow. The Prune&Grow step temporarily operates at a lower target sparsity (s−p) to accommodate the additional pruning, and random growing is used to minimize peak memory during backpropagation. Because the revitalization is data-driven (based on actual weight-change patterns and post-ReLU behavior) rather than purely magnitude-based, it removes the specific harmful weights that standard DST heuristics miss, reducing the number of topology updates needed and cutting recompilation overhead by up to 112× on mobile devices.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-32 and ERK sparsity, NeurRev achieves 93.84% / 92.93% / 90.84% top-1 accuracy at 90% / 95% / 98% sparsity, outperforming the strongest baseline RigL-ITOP (93.70% / 92.78% / 90.40%) by up to 0.44 points. On CIFAR-100 (ERK), NeurRev reaches 71.96% / 69.92% / 66.82%, surpassing RigL-ITOP by up to 2.68 points at 98% sparsity. On ImageNet-1K with ResNet-50, NeurRev achieves 75.7% top-1 at 80% sparsity (ERK) and 73.4% at 90% sparsity, beating RigL (75.1% / 73.0%) at equal training FLOPs. NeurRev also achieves 1.8–2.1× end-to-end training acceleration over dense training on a Samsung Galaxy S21, with up to 112× less recompilation overhead than other DST methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeurRev consistently outperforms all 11 baselines across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet. Against static pruning methods (LT, SNIP, GraSP), NeurRev gains 1.0–3.5 points on CIFAR-10 and 2.5–9.5 points on CIFAR-100 at 98% sparsity, confirming that dynamic topology updates are essential. Among DST baselines, NeurRev beats SET (Mocanu et al.) by ~1.0 point on CIFAR-10 at 90% sparsity and DSR (Mostafa et al.) by ~0.9 point, while surpassing Deep-R (Bellec et al.) by over 2 points. The closest competitors are RigL (Evci et al.) and RigL-ITOP (Liu et al.): NeurRev edges them out by 0.14–0.62 points on CIFAR-10 and 0.55–2.68 points on CIFAR-100 under ERK sparsity. On ImageNet-1K (ResNet-50, 90% sparsity, uniform), NeurRev achieves 73.3% vs. RigL's 72.0% and Top-KAST's 73.0%, while at 80% sparsity it reaches 75.9% vs. MEST's 75.4% and RigL's 74.6%. SNFS (Dettmers et al.) is only compared on ImageNet, where NeurRev surpasses it by 1.8 points at 90% sparsity (73.4% vs. 72.3% under ERK).

## Relation to Prior Work

NeurRev extends the Dynamic Sparse Training (DST) lineage initiated by SET, RigL, and MEST, which all follow a prune-grow regime to search for optimal sparse subnetworks. While prior DST work has focused almost exclusively on designing better weight-growing criteria (gradient-based in RigL, momentum-based in SNFS, random in MEST), NeurRev deliberately targets the 'long-neglected pruning part,' replacing magnitude-based pruning with a data-driven criterion that identifies and removes large negative weights responsible for dormant neurons. The framework adopts random growing from MEST to minimize peak memory, and uses ERK sparsity distribution from RigL, positioning itself as a complementary improvement to the existing DST pipeline rather than a replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in making DST viable on resource-constrained edge devices: by reducing dormant neurons, NeurRev cuts the required topology update frequency by 12–96×, translating to up to 112× less recompilation overhead on mobile hardware while matching or exceeding baseline accuracy. This addresses a real deployment bottleneck that algorithmic-level DST papers have largely ignored. Limitations include the method's dependence on ReLU (the dormant neuron mechanism is specific to zero-output saturation), evaluation limited to CNNs on image classification, and modest accuracy gains (0.1–0.5%) over strong baselines like RigL-ITOP. Open questions include whether the dormant neuron concept generalizes to non-ReLU activations, transformer architectures, or non-convolutional tasks.
