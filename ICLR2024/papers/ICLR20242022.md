## Problem

Robot fleets generate massive heterogeneous data locally but cannot centralize it due to bandwidth and storage constraints, yet each robot's locally trained policy captures useful skills. Existing model-merging methods (Git Re-Basin, Sinkhorn Rebasin) handle permutation invariance only for feedforward networks in supervised settings, while robot policies use recurrent architectures where permutation symmetries also span timesteps, making naive weight averaging fail. The paper asks how to merge RNN-parameterized policies from distributed, non-i.i.d. datasets without transmitting raw data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N separately trained Elman RNN policy weight vectors (each trained via behavior cloning on a local trajectory dataset of observation-action pairs) and produces a single merged policy weight vector. Critically, the alignment step samples trajectories from each robot's own local dataset rather than a shared common dataset, so no raw data ever leaves the robot. Observations can be segmented tool/object point clouds, frozen ResNet image features, or state vectors; the merged policy outputs 6-DoF end-effector translation and rotation commands at 6 Hz.

## Method

FLEET-MERGE merges N RNN policies by iteratively aligning each policy's hidden-layer neurons to a common reference model (the average of all aligned policies) using soft permutation matrices. At each epoch, a subset of models is selected; for each, the soft permutation is updated by gradient descent on the behavior-cloning loss of an interpolated policy, with the gradient computed through the RNN's recurrent dynamics (Eq. 3.3). After T gradient steps, the soft permutation is projected back onto the Birkhoff polytope via entropy-regularized Sinkhorn iterations (soft projection) and then onto hard permutation matrices (hard projection), preserving the discrete symmetry structure. This merging-to-a-common-reference design removes order dependence, pools information from all N models at each step, and supports iterative merging (multiple rounds interleaved with local training), connecting directly to the FedAvg protocol. The key theoretical contribution is proving that RNNs are invariant to hard permutation transformations across all layers (Proposition 3.1) and that for ReLU networks, the ℓ₂-norm-minimizing model within the scaled-permutation orbit is unique up to pure permutations (Proposition B.2), justifying the hard projection step.

## Results

FLEET-MERGE outperforms all baselines by over 50% in multi-task policy merging, consolidating the behavior of policies trained on 50 distinct Meta-World manipulation tasks with good performance on nearly all training tasks at test time. In one-shot merging, FLEET-MERGE achieves significantly smaller performance barriers (mode connectivity) than naive averaging or GITREBASIN on both FLEET-TOOLS and Meta-World, and outperforms all baselines across all levels of data heterogeneity (Dirichlet parameter α). In iterative merging, FLEET-MERGE is resilient to lower merging frequencies and smaller participation ratios (as low as 25% of agents per round), with performance upper-bounded only by joint training on pooled data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLEET-MERGE consistently outperforms NAIVEAVERAGE, which directly averages weights without accounting for permutation invariance, showing that naive averaging produces high performance barriers and poor multi-task success. Against GITREBASIN (Ainsworth et al., 2022), which uses hard permutation alignment via linear assignment problems, FLEET-MERGE achieves lower performance barriers and higher multi-task success rates across all data heterogeneity levels (Dirichlet α), attributed to its use of soft (Sinkhorn) permutations and merging to a common reference model. Compared to SOFTUPDATE (the direct two-model extension of Peña et al., 2022), FLEET-MERGE enjoys superior warm-starting performance on FLEET-TOOLS, benefiting from its multi-model reference-based design and local-dataset-based alignment. FLEET-MERGE also outperforms the SINGLEDATASET baseline (zero-shot adaptation from a single unrelated task), confirming that merging multiple locally-trained policies captures complementary skills that no single policy possesses. In iterative merging (Figure 4), FLEET-MERGE maintains the best absolute performance and resilience to dataset heterogeneity on both FLEET-TOOLS and Meta-World, while remaining robust to reduced communication (lower merging frequency and participation ratio).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the permutation-aware model merging literature (Git Re-Basin, Sinkhorn Rebasin) from feedforward networks to RNN-parameterized policies, where permutation symmetries arise both across layers and across timesteps. It departs from prior federated learning work (FedAvg, Matched Averaging) by replacing hard permutation alignment with soft Sinkhorn-based projections and by merging to a common reference model rather than sequentially. The key conceptual departure from prior work is applying these alignment techniques to robotic control policies (behavior cloning with Elman RNNs) rather than supervised learning tasks, and introducing the FLEET-TOOLS benchmark for compositional tool-use manipulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Fleet-level robot learning is a pressing practical problem: deployed robots generate terabytes of heterogeneous data that cannot be centralized under real-world bandwidth constraints, yet the fleet should collectively acquire diverse skills. The paper demonstrates that transmitting only trained weights (megabytes) rather than raw data (terabytes) can yield a merged policy competitive with joint training across 50 Meta-World tasks. Key limitations include evaluation only in simulation with behavior cloning (no online RL or real-world fleet deployment), and the FLEET-TOOLS benchmark relies on hand-scripted keypoint experts without closed-loop feedback. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger fleets, more complex contact-rich tasks requiring feedback, and whether the permutation-alignment overhead remains tractable as network depth and fleet size grow.
