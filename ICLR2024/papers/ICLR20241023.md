## Problem

Velocity-based generative models like Critically Damped Langevin Dynamics (CLD) operate in phase space but still produce curved trajectories because they must converge to a Gaussian equilibrium at the terminal time, making sampling inefficient at low NFE budgets. This paper asks whether Stochastic Optimal Control can construct a more favorable path measure in phase space that yields straighter trajectories and enables earlier estimation of the target data point, thereby reducing the number of function evaluations needed for high-quality generation.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 2d-dimensional phase-space sample (position x₀ and velocity v₀ drawn from a correlated Gaussian with covariance Σ₀) rather than a d-dimensional position-only sample, requiring 6 input channels (3 position + 3 velocity) in the U-Net backbone. For conditional generation, the initial velocity is replaced by a linear interpolation between the random v₀ and a data-derived velocity, and for inpainting, a mask is applied to the estimated data point at each step. The output is a generated RGB image (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 256×256 for AFHQv2, 64×64 for ImageNet-64), and the sampling-hop property additionally yields a usable data estimate at any intermediate time step tᵢ < t_N.

## Method

AGM formulates the generative bridge as a Stochastic Optimal Control problem over a linear momentum system (dx/dt = v, dv/dt = a + noise), where the optimal acceleration a* is derived analytically via the Lyapunov equation and takes the form g_t²P₁₁((x₁−x_t)/(1−t) − v_t), producing straighter position and velocity trajectories than CLD. Training regresses a neural network on the force term F_t, which decomposes into a data-dependent component (4x₁(1−t)²) and a noise-dependent component involving both ε₀ and ε₁, so the network implicitly encodes information about both position and velocity noise. The key structural consequence is the sampling-hop property: because the force term linearly depends on x₁, one can algebraically invert it at any time step to recover an estimate x̃₁ using only the current state, velocity, and network output, without integrating to t=1. For deterministic sampling, an Exponential Integrator (EI) of order w=2 is used to simultaneously update both position and velocity channels, mitigating the one-step delay inherent in underactuated momentum systems; for stochastic sampling, the Symmetric Splitting Sampler from CLD is employed.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, AGM-ODE achieves 2.46 FID with 50 NFE and 2.60 FID with 20 NFE, outperforming CLD+EI (2.26 FID at 50 NFE) and matching EDM (1.84 FID at 35 NFE) within a small margin. At extremely low NFE (5), AGM-ODE achieves 11.93 FID, substantially outperforming EDM (>100), VP+EI (15.37), DDIM (26.91), and Analytic-DPM (51.47). On ImageNet-64, AGM-ODE reaches 10.10 FID at 40 NFE and 10.55 FID at 20 NFE, surpassing MFM (12.97 FID at 40 NFE) and FM-OT (14.45 FID at 138 NFE). On CIFAR-10 SDE, AGM-SDE achieves 2.46 FID at 1000 NFE, comparable to CLD (2.44) and VE (2.43).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AGM-ODE decisively outperforms all 1st-order dynamics baselines at 5 NFE on CIFAR-10 (11.93 FID vs. EDM >100, VP+EI 15.37, DDIM 26.91, Analytic-DPM 51.47), demonstrating the sampling-hop advantage of leveraging velocity information. Against the closest 2nd-order competitor CLD+EI, AGM-ODE wins by a large margin at 10 NFE (4.60 vs. 13.41) and 20 NFE (2.60 vs. 3.39), attributed to straighter trajectories from the SOC-derived bridge. At 20 NFE, AGM-ODE (2.60) slightly trails EDM (2.23) but beats VP+EI (3.03) and DDIM (3.50). On ImageNet-64, AGM-ODE (10.10 FID at 40 NFE) outperforms MFM (12.97 at 40 NFE) and FM-OT (14.45 at 138 NFE) by a wide margin. In the SDE setting on CIFAR-10, AGM-SDE (2.46 FID at 1000 NFE) is essentially tied with CLD (2.44) and VE (2.43), but at 20 NFE AGM-SDE achieves 7.9 FID while CLD-SDE exceeds 100, confirming the trajectory-straightening benefit at low NFE.

## Relation to Prior Work

AGM extends the phase-space generative modeling framework introduced by CLD (Dockhorn et al., 2021) by replacing its equilibrium-convergent dynamics with a Stochastic Optimal Control formulation rooted in Chen & Georgiou's (2015) stochastic bridge theory for linear momentum systems. This yields straighter position-velocity trajectories that avoid the unnecessary curvature CLD exhibits when converging to a Gaussian prior. The method also draws on Bridge Matching and Flow Matching for its training objective, and adopts the Exponential Integrator (Zhang & Chen, 2022) as its ODE sampler, but departs from both by incorporating velocity as an additional estimation channel for the target data point (sampling-hop), a capability absent from 1st-order dynamics models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sampling-hop mechanism—recovering a realistic data estimate from both position and velocity at an early time step—offers a principled route to reducing NFE without sacrificing quality, and the paper demonstrates this concretely with competitive FID scores at 5–10 NFE on CIFAR-10 and AFHQv2. However, the authors acknowledge that AGM lags behind EDM and other SOTA methods when sufficient NFE is available, and the evaluation is limited to unconditional generation with a single network architecture family (NCSN++/ADM). Open questions include whether the sampling-hop idea transfers to conditional or text-to-image settings, whether the SOC-derived trajectory structure generalizes beyond linear momentum dynamics, and whether the performance gap at higher NFE can be closed with the training improvements (augmentation, noise scheduling) the authors note but do not implement.
