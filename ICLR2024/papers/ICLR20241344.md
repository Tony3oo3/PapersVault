## Problem

State-of-the-art vision-language generative models require hundreds of millions to billions of paired image-text samples, which are either expensive to annotate or low-quality when scraped from the web. Meanwhile, vast unimodal image and text corpora remain entirely unused in generative vision-language training. The paper asks whether unpaired image-only and text-only data can provide effective supervision for joint text-to-image and image-to-text generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241344/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For image-to-text, a 256×256 RGB image is encoded into 256 discrete VQGAN tokens (16×16 grid, 8192-entry codebook) and fed to the encoder alongside empty text embeddings, producing an autoregressive caption. For text-to-image, a text prompt (max 64 SentencePiece tokens) is embedded via a frozen T5-XXL encoder and combined with masked image tokens to produce a 256×256 image through 24 steps of parallel MaskGIT-style decoding with classifier-free guidance. During training, the model consumes three data streams simultaneously: a small set of paired image-text examples (e.g., 3M from CC3M), unpaired images (e.g., 55M from WebLI), and unpaired texts (e.g., 55M from WebLI), with the latter two providing cycle-consistency supervision.

## Method

ITIT unifies I2T and T2I in a single framework with a shared ViT-based image-text encoder, an autoregressive text decoder (GIT-style with causal attention), and a parallel image decoder (MAGE/Muse-style with self-attention). The core innovation is a cycle-consistency training paradigm on unpaired data: for T2I2T, an unpaired text prompt is used to synthesize a pseudo-image via T2I inference, which is then used to reconstruct the original text via I2T; for I2T2I, an unpaired image is used to synthesize a pseudo-caption via I2T inference, which is then used to reconstruct the original image via T2I. Because the intermediate generation steps involve non-differentiable argmax operations, the paper employs straight-through estimation (and Gumbel-softmax for T2I2T) to backpropagate gradients through the cycle. A small paired dataset (3M) anchors the alignment between image and text spaces, while the large unpaired datasets provide scaling signal, enabling ITIT to match or exceed baselines trained on 100× more paired data.

## Results

On MS-COCO, ITIT-H (868M params) trained with only 3M paired image-text pairs (CC3M) plus 55M unpaired images and 55M unpaired texts (WebLI) achieves FID 10.4 for text-to-image generation and CIDEr 125.3 (fine-tuned) / 38.2 (zero-shot) for image captioning. This is competitive with DALL-E 2 (FID 10.39, 650M pairs) and PARTI (FID 10.71, 5000M pairs) for T2I, and beats GIT-B trained from scratch (CIDEr 89.0, 10M pairs) for I2T. The paper further shows that with only 4M paired + 398M unpaired Shutterstock samples, ITIT matches a baseline trained on 398M paired samples, demonstrating ~100x reduction in paired data requirements.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For text-to-image generation, ITIT-H (FID 10.4) matches DALL-E 2 (FID 10.39) and beats PARTI (10.71), StableDiffusion (12.60), GLIDE (12.24), and Make-A-Scene (11.84), all while using only 3M paired samples versus 35M–5000M for those methods. Against the same-architecture baseline Muse† (750M params, 3M pairs, no unpaired data), ITIT-H improves FID from 23.7 to 10.4, demonstrating the substantial benefit of cycle-consistency training with unpaired data. For image captioning, ITIT-H (CIDEr ft 125.3) substantially outperforms GIT-B trained from scratch (89.0, 10M pairs) but remains below BLIP (136.7), SimVLM-huge (143.3), and GIT-CLIP (144.8), which all use 129M–800M paired samples. In the unified T2I+I2T setting, ITIT-H is competitive with CoBIT-Base (FID 10.4 vs. 10.35, CIDEr ft 125.3 vs. 135.4) despite using 3M paired data versus 5200M, and trails the much larger CM3Leon (7B, FID 4.88) which benefits from a far larger model and 340M paired samples. The key takeaway is that ITIT achieves near-SOTA performance with roughly 100× fewer paired image-text pairs, and the gap to the strongest baselines narrows further when scaling unpaired data (e.g., 398M unpaired Shutterstock samples close the gap to a 398M-paired baseline).

## Relation to Prior Work

ITIT unifies the I2T framework from GIT (autoregressive text decoder with causal attention) and the T2I framework from Muse (parallel masked image decoder) into a single architecture with a shared image-text encoder and modality-specific decoders. The key departure from prior work is the introduction of cycle consistency losses (T2I2T and I2T2I) to provide supervision on unpaired image-only and text-only data, extending the cycle-consistency concept from image-to-image translation (CycleGAN) and CLIP pre-training (CycCLIP) into the generative vision-language training loop. Unlike prior efforts that use unpaired data only for encoder pre-training or text-decoder fine-tuning, ITIT integrates unpaired data directly into the full generative training procedure, and unlike Gorti & Ma's earlier text-image-text cycle approach, it achieves competitive performance through a unified cross-entropy formulation and straight-through gradient estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~100x reduction in required paired data (3M vs. 398M) has direct practical implications for the cost and feasibility of training vision-language generative models, particularly when high-quality paired data is expensive to annotate or when large-scale paired data suffers from poor pairing quality. The finding that unpaired data scales comparably to paired data, and that the method is robust to low-quality pairings (WebLI), opens the door to leveraging the vast reservoirs of unimodal web data. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger model sizes and more diverse datasets, whether the 2x training overhead can be reduced, and whether the cycle consistency principle can be extended to other multimodal generative settings (e.g., video, audio).
