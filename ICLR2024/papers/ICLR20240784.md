## Problem

Existing Bayesian MCMC methods for deep neural networks sample from the posterior without awareness of energy landscape flatness, so they cannot distinguish between flat and sharp modes of equal energy. Under practical computational budgets, this leads to samples trapped in sharp modes with poor generalization. Prior flatness-aware methods (Entropy-SGD, SAM) introduce substantial overhead through nested Markov chains, doubled gradient computations, or min-max formulations, making them impractical for large-scale Bayesian inference.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard supervised training dataset (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, ImageNet at 224×224, MNIST) and a neural network (ResNet-18/50) with parameters θ ∈ ℝ^d. It produces a sample set S containing both model parameters θ and guiding variables θ_a collected after burn-in, which are jointly used for Bayesian marginalization to yield class predictions and predictive uncertainty. Notably, the gradient for θ_a is computed analytically as (1/η)(θ_a − θ) without backpropagation, so the total gradient cost matches that of standard d-dimensional SGLD despite operating in 2d dimensions.

## Method

Entropy-MCMC introduces an auxiliary guiding variable θ_a whose stationary distribution is a smoothed posterior p(θ_a|D) ∝ exp F(θ_a; η) obtained via local entropy (equivalently, Gaussian convolution of the original posterior), which suppresses sharp modes. The key insight is coupling θ and θ_a into the joint distribution p(θ, θ_a|D) ∝ exp{−f(θ) − (1/2η)||θ − θ_a||²}, whose marginals are exactly the original posterior and the smoothed posterior respectively (Lemma 1), eliminating the need for nested Markov chains or Monte Carlo approximation of the local entropy integral. Sampling proceeds via SGLD on the joint energy, where the θ-update receives a flatness-aware gradient revision (1/η)(θ − θ_a) that pulls parameters toward flat basins, while θ_a is updated with a closed-form gradient requiring no backpropagation. The paper proves (Theorem 1) that EMCMC converges at a rate comparable to standard SGLD (only a constant-factor slowdown from the doubled dimension), and strictly faster than Entropy-SGD and Entropy-SGLD, whose convergence is degraded by the nested-chain error term.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, EMCMC achieves 95.69% accuracy and 0.162 NLL, outperforming all baselines including bSAM (95.53%, 0.165) and SGLD (95.47%, 0.167). On CIFAR-100, EMCMC reaches 79.16% accuracy and 0.840 NLL, surpassing bSAM (78.92%, 0.870) and SGLD (78.79%, 0.854). On ImageNet with ResNet50, EMCMC attains 77.10% Top-1 accuracy and 0.895 NLL, beating SGLD (76.68%, 0.921) and SGD (76.05%, 0.960). For OOD detection on CIFAR100-SVHN, EMCMC achieves 81.14% AUROC and 87.18% AUPR, the best among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMCMC outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and metric reported. Against the strongest flatness-aware optimizer bSAM, EMCMC gains +0.16% accuracy and −0.003 NLL on CIFAR-10, and +0.24% accuracy and −0.030 NLL on CIFAR-100. Compared to the standard MCMC baseline SGLD, EMCMC improves by +0.22% accuracy and −0.005 NLL on CIFAR-10, and +0.37% accuracy and −0.014 NLL on CIFAR-100, while maintaining comparable computational overhead. The nested-loop methods Entropy-SGD and Entropy-SGLD are consistently the weakest baselines (e.g., Entropy-SGLD drops to 94.46% on CIFAR-10 and 77.98% on CIFAR-100), confirming the theoretical prediction that nested Markov chains slow convergence and degrade sample quality. On OOD detection (CIFAR100-SVHN), EMCMC leads by +2.0% AUROC over the next-best Entropy-SGD (79.15%) and by +8.6% over SAM (74.56%), demonstrating that flat-basin sampling yields superior uncertainty calibration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on SGLD (Welling & Teh, 2011) as its MCMC backbone and the local entropy concept (Baldassi et al., 2016) for measuring flatness, but departs sharply from Entropy-SGD (Chaudhari et al., 2019) and Entropy-SGLD (Dziugaite & Roy, 2018), which approximate the local entropy integral via nested Markov chains. The key conceptual move is reframing the intractable integral as a joint distribution over (θ, θ_a) whose marginals are exactly the original and smoothed posteriors, eliminating nested loops entirely. The paper also positions itself against flatness-aware *optimization* methods (SAM, bSAM) that return a single point, arguing that Bayesian marginalization over the full flat basin is a strictly richer objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EMCMC provides a drop-in, SGLD-cost replacement that provably biases sampling toward flat basins, with convergence guarantees showing it outpaces nested-chain flatness-aware methods by a large margin in the strongly convex regime. The main limitation is that the theoretical analysis requires strong log-concavity (unimodality), which real DNN posteriors violate; the multi-modal case is left to future work. Open questions include whether the flatness bias remains beneficial at very large scale (e.g., billion-parameter models) and how the method interacts with modern architectures that use normalization layers or attention mechanisms where the energy landscape geometry may differ substantially.
