## Problem

Dynamic Sparse Training (DST) methods like RigL achieve dense-level generalization at 85–99% sparsity, but the resulting unstructured sparsity patterns are difficult to accelerate on real-world hardware. Structured sparsity (e.g., N:M) enables strong hardware acceleration but typically removes salient weights, degrading generalization. The paper addresses this tension by learning a structured, hardware-friendly sparsity pattern end-to-end during sparse training without sacrificing accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241297/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method trains standard image classifiers (ResNet-18/50, Wide ResNet-22, MobileNet-V3, ViT-B/16) on RGB images from CIFAR-10 (32×32) and ImageNet (224×224 crops), producing class labels as output. The trained model's weight matrices are stored in a condensed representation: for each active neuron, only the k non-zero weight values and their input indices are kept (an n×k dense matrix plus an n×k index matrix), rather than the full dense weight matrix. This condensed form is what enables the demonstrated 3.4×/2.5× CPU and 1.7×/13.0× GPU inference speedups at 90% sparsity.

## Method

SRigL extends RigL by enforcing a constant fan-in constraint: every neuron in a layer must have exactly k non-zero incoming weights, making weight saliency a per-neuron (not per-layer) decision. A key insight from empirical analysis is that RigL implicitly ablates entire neurons at sparsities above 90%, effectively reducing layer width; SRigL makes this explicit via a neuron ablation mechanism controlled by a hyperparameter γ_sal, which prunes a neuron if fewer than γ_sal·k of its weights are salient and redistributes its weights to remaining neurons. A theoretical analysis in the appendix shows that constant fan-in sparsity yields strictly lower output-norm variance than unstructured (Bernoulli or constant-per-layer) sparsity at the same density, confirming the constraint does not impair training dynamics. The method is fully sparse-to-sparse: no dense weights or gradients are stored or computed during training, and the condensed representation (active weights + indices per neuron) enables embarrassingly-parallel matrix-vector products for inference.

## Results

On ResNet-50/ImageNet, SRigL achieves 75.01% top-1 accuracy at 80% sparsity and 72.71% at 90% sparsity, closely matching unstructured RigL (74.98% and 72.81%) while enforcing a constant fan-in structured constraint. At 93.75% sparsity, SRigL reaches 70.56%, compared to the dense ResNet-50 baseline of 76.7%. On ViT-B/16/ImageNet, SRigL with neuron ablation achieves 77.5% at 80% and 76.0% at 90% sparsity, closely matching RigL (77.9% and 76.4%). Real-world acceleration at 90% sparsity reaches 3.4×/2.5× over dense/unstructured on CPU (online inference) and 1.7×/13.0× on GPU (batch size 256).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRigL outperforms the most directly comparable structured DST method DSB-16 at all sparsity levels (76.60% vs 76.33% at 50%, 75.55% vs 74.04% at 75%). Against unstructured DST baselines, SRigL matches RigL within 0.1% at 80% (75.01% vs 74.98%) and 90% (72.71% vs 72.81%) sparsity, while clearly surpassing SET (72.9%/69.6%), DeepR (71.7%/70.2%), DSR (73.3%/71.6%), and Top-KAST (74.76%/70.42%) at 80%/90% sparsity. SRigL slightly trails MEST at 80% (75.01% vs 75.39%) but edges ahead at 90% (72.71% vs 72.58%). The closest structured competitor, Chase, outperforms SRigL at both 80% (75.27% vs 75.01%) and 90% (74.03% vs 72.71%) sparsity, though Chase requires the structured sparsity level to be fixed before training. Against dense-training methods, SRigL beats SNFS(ERK) at 80% (75.01% vs 75.2%) and AST+GC (75.01% vs 73.2%), but SR-STE achieves higher accuracy at 75% (76.2% vs 75.55%) and 93.75% (71.5% vs 70.56%) sparsity at the cost of dense training compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

SRigL extends RigL (Evci et al., 2021), the state-of-the-art unstructured DST method, by imposing a constant fan-in constraint (a special case of N:M sparsity) during sparse-to-sparse training. Unlike SR-STE (Zhou et al., 2021), which achieves N:M sparsity but requires dense parameters and gradients throughout training, SRigL maintains sparse parameters end-to-end. The method also departs from prior structured DST approaches like DSB (Jiang et al., 2022) by learning a per-neuron fan-in constraint rather than block-level structure, and introduces a neuron ablation mechanism motivated by the authors' empirical discovery that RigL implicitly reduces layer width at high sparsities — a behavior they claim to identify for the first time within a DST framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper bridges a critical gap between the theoretical FLOPs savings of sparse training and real-world hardware acceleration, demonstrating 3.4×/2.5× CPU and 1.7×/13.0× GPU speedups over dense/unstructured baselines with a straightforward implementation. The neuron ablation discovery provides a mechanistic explanation for why structured sparsity previously underperformed unstructured DST at high sparsity levels, and the theoretical analysis showing constant fan-in sparsity yields smaller output-norm variance than unstructured sparsity offers a principled justification for the constraint. Limitations include reliance on CUDA kernels from a separate work for GPU acceleration, evaluation limited to image classification, and the fact that the CPU implementation is a naive PyTorch loop whose practical ceiling is unclear. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to language models, whether more optimized kernels or hardware support would close the remaining gap to dense performance at extreme sparsities, and whether the γ_sal hyperparameter sensitivity observed in ViT-B/16 generalizes to other transformer architectures.
