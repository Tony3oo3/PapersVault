## Problem

In general-sum Markov Games, each agent has its own value function and no unified optimal value function characterizes equilibrium behavior, breaking the greedy-policy or minimax structure that underpins sample-efficient algorithms for single-agent RL and zero-sum MGs. Prior MARL algorithms with function approximation either solve constrained optimization within data-dependent sub-level sets or complex multi-objective minimax subproblems, and no unified framework covers both model-free and model-based settings for all three equilibrium notions (NE, CCE, CE). This paper asks whether a single algorithmic framework can achieve provable sample efficiency for NE/CCE/CE in general-sum MGs while avoiding these complex subproblems.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates in an episodic setting with K episodes of horizon H and n agents, consuming a hypothesis class F (Q-functions in model-free mode, transition kernels in model-based mode), an equilibrium-solving oracle for normal-form games, and historical trajectories from prior episodes. It outputs a joint policy (a distribution over pure joint policies) that constitutes an ε-NE, ε-CCE, or ε-CE, along with a sublinear regret bound of Õ(nH√K·Υ_{F,δ} + nd_MADC·√K + nd_MADC·H). A key structural detail is that the inner optimization subproblem is single-objective and unconstrained over the hypothesis space, unlike prior work that requires data-dependent constraints or minimax formulations.

## Method

MAMEX (Multi-Agent Maximize-to-EXplore) extends the single-agent MEX framework by shifting the optimization from hypothesis space to policy space. In each episode, for every pure joint policy π, it constructs a payoff V̄_i(π) = sup_f {V_f^{(i),π}(ρ) − η·L^{(i),k−1}(f,π,τ^{1:k−1})} by solving a regularized single-objective maximization over the hypothesis class, where the first term encourages exploration (large value under hypothesis f) and the second penalizes inconsistency with historical data (mean-squared Bellman error or negative log-likelihood). It then computes the desired equilibrium (NE/CCE/CE) of the resulting normal-form game over pure policies using an equilibrium oracle, and samples a pure policy to execute. The paper introduces the Multi-Agent Decoupling Coefficient (MADC) as a complexity measure bounding prediction error by training error, showing that MAMEX achieves sublinear regret whenever MADC is finite—a condition satisfied by multi-agent Bellman eluder dimensions, Bilinear Classes, and witness rank models. The model-free instantiation is the first provably efficient model-free algorithm for learning Nash equilibrium in general-sum MGs with function approximation.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) establishes that MAMEX achieves a sublinear regret of Õ(nH√K · Υ_{F,δ} + n·d_MADC·√K + n·d_MADC·H) for learning NE/CCE/CE in general-sum Markov Games under general function approximation, where d_MADC is the proposed Multi-Agent Decoupling Coefficient. Specialized to linear Bellman complete MGs, the regret becomes Õ(ndHR²√K + nHR²√K log|Π^pur| + ndH²). For zero-sum linear MGs, MAMEX yields Õ(dH³√K + H³√K log|Π^pur|), matching the information-theoretic lower bound Ω(dH^{3/2}√K) in the dependence on d. The model-based version with multi-agent witness rank d achieves Õ(nHd√K/κ_wit² + nH√K) regret.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For zero-sum linear MGs, MAMEX achieves Õ(dH³√K) regret, matching the information-theoretic lower bound in d but with a higher polynomial order in H (H³) compared to Xie et al. (2020)'s Õ(d^{3/2}H²√K). For linear mixture MGs, MAMEX gives Õ(dH⁵√K) versus the minimax-optimal Õ(dH√K) of Chen et al. (2022d), matching in d and K but incurring an extra H⁴ factor. In the single-agent linear MDP setting, MAMEX recovers the state-of-the-art Õ(dH√K) regret of Zanette et al. (2020) up to an additional log|Π^pur| factor. Algorithmically, MAMEX differs from prior MARL methods by requiring only a single-objective unconstrained optimization oracle plus a normal-form game equilibrium oracle, avoiding the data-dependent constrained optimization of Wang et al. (2023) and the complex multi-objective minimax subproblems of Foster et al. (2023) and Chen et al. (2022b). The model-free version of MAMEX is the first provably sample-efficient model-free algorithm for learning all three equilibria (NE, CCE, CE) in general-sum MGs with general function approximation.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAMEX extends the single-agent Maximize-to-Explore (MEX) framework (Liu et al., 2023) to general-sum Markov Games by shifting the optimization from the hypothesis space to the policy space, where an equilibrium-solving oracle replaces the greedy policy step that is unavailable in multi-agent settings. The Multi-Agent Decoupling Coefficient (MADC) generalizes the decoupling coefficient (Dann et al., 2021) and its multi-agent zero-sum variant (Xiong et al., 2022) to the general-sum case, while the multi-agent extensions of Bellman eluder dimension, Bilinear Classes, and witness rank provide concrete model classes where MADC is finite. Unlike prior MARL algorithms that require constrained optimization within data-dependent sub-level sets (Wang et al., 2023) or complex minimax/multi-objective subproblems (Foster et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2022b), MAMEX reduces each iteration to a single-objective regularized optimization plus a normal-form game equilibrium computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first unified framework that provably learns all three equilibrium notions (NE, CCE, CE) in general-sum MGs under general function approximation, and the first model-free algorithm with sample-efficiency guarantees for Nash equilibrium in this setting. The MADC provides a clean, interpretable complexity measure that subsumes several existing structural conditions, giving a unified lens for understanding exploration hardness in MARL. Limitations include extra polynomial factors in H compared to specialized algorithms for particular model classes, and the requirement that the pure policy space be finite (or covered), which may be restrictive for continuous action spaces. Open questions include whether the equilibrium oracle requirement can be relaxed to no-regret dynamics, and whether the framework extends to partially observable or stochastic games.
