## Problem

Deep Ensembles improve accuracy and uncertainty quantification but require M forward passes at inference, scaling linearly with ensemble size. Existing fast-ensembling methods like Bridge Network (BN) only predict outputs on low-loss subspaces between pairs of models, requiring quadratic numbers of bridges and incurring extra training cost for subspace learning. This paper asks whether a single lightweight network can directly transport one ensemble member's output distribution to the full ensemble's output distribution without pairwise subspaces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241661/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet/ImageNet64) and produces a K-dimensional class probability distribution. At inference, the image passes through only one source ResNet to obtain a feature vector h_1 and logit z_1; the logit is then annealed by a random temperature T drawn from a Beta-derived distribution to form the diffusion starting point. The lightweight score network operates in the K-dimensional logit space (not image space), and the final output is Softmax(Z_0) where Z_0 is the single-step diffusion result, approximating the ensemble-averaged probability vector.

## Method

DBN constructs a conditional Diffusion Schrödinger Bridge (I²SB) in logit space between the annealed source model logit (Z_1 = z_1/T) and the target ensemble logit (Z_0 = log of the averaged softmax probabilities). A lightweight score network ε_φ(h_1, Z_t, t), built from depthwise separable convolutions and inverted residual blocks, is trained to estimate the score ∇_Z log p_t(Z_t|x) by minimizing a denoising objective over the Gaussian bridge posterior q(Z_t|Z_0, Z_1). Progressive distillation (Salimans & Ho, 2022) is then applied recursively to collapse the multi-step reverse SDE into a single diffusion step, so inference requires only one source-model forward pass plus one score-network evaluation. To scale beyond the capacity of a single bridge (~3 ensemble members), multiple DBNs sharing the same source model are trained on disjoint subsets of ensemble members and their outputs are averaged, keeping the additional inference cost minimal since the source model is reused.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, a single DBN targeting DE-3 achieves 92.98% accuracy and 0.2403 NLL at only 1.166× the FLOPs of a single model, closely matching DE-3 (93.06% ACC, 0.2252 NLL) which requires 3× FLOPs. On TinyImageNet, +2DBN(DE-5) even surpasses DE-3 (65.34% vs 63.78% ACC) at 1.418× FLOPs. On ImageNet, +1DBN(DE-3) reaches 69.79% ACC and 1.2140 cNLL at 1.244× FLOPs, exceeding DE-2 (69.56% ACC) at less than half the compute. The DEE metric confirms DBN captures the equivalent of 2.36–3.03 ensemble members with a single lightweight score network.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DBN decisively outperforms Bridge Network (BN) on both accuracy and efficiency: on CIFAR-10, +1DBN(DE-3) achieves 92.98% ACC at 1.166× FLOPs, while +2BN_medium(DE-3) reaches only 91.91% ACC at 1.411× FLOPs — a 1.07-point accuracy gap at 17% lower compute. Against ensemble distillation (ED) and EnD², which operate at single-model FLOPs (×1.000), DBN trades a modest 16.6% FLOPs increase for substantially better uncertainty metrics (NLL 0.2403 vs 0.3505 for ED; DEE 2.36 vs <1). Compared to the oracle DE-3 (3× FLOPs), a single DBN recovers 99.9% of the accuracy (92.98 vs 93.06) and 93.7% of the NLL improvement at only 39% of the inference cost. On CIFAR-10-C (out-of-distribution), +1DBN(DE-3) achieves 88.91% ACC, matching DE-2 (88.85%) and exceeding +2BN_medium (87.80%) while using fewer FLOPs. The one area where DBN lags is ECE, which remains higher than DE baselines (0.0666 vs 0.0469 for DE-3 on CIFAR-10), suggesting the diffusion bridge does not fully replicate the calibration benefits of true ensemble averaging.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a direct successor to the Bridge Network (BN) of Yun et al. (2023), which reduces ensemble inference cost by learning low-loss subspaces between pairs of ensemble members and training a lightweight network per pair. DBN departs from BN in two key ways: it eliminates the need for pairwise low-loss subspace learning (avoiding quadratic growth in the number of bridge networks) and instead learns a single stochastic path in logit space from one source model to the full ensemble output, using the Diffusion Schrödinger Bridge framework (Bortoli et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2023). The diffusion bridge is trained within the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) and distilled to a single step via progressive distillation (Salimans & Ho, 2022), making inference cost comparable to a single forward pass plus a lightweight score network evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DBN offers a practical path to near-DE-3 accuracy and uncertainty quality at roughly 1.17× the FLOPs of a single model, compared to BN's 1.41× for only DE-2-level performance, making it attractive for resource-constrained deployment of ensemble-based uncertainty estimation. A notable limitation is that a single DBN saturates at approximately three ensemble members' worth of diversity, requiring multiple bridges (and proportional training time) for larger ensembles, and the ECE metric degrades relative to true ensembles. Open questions include whether the diffusion bridge formulation can be extended to non-classification tasks, whether the temperature-annealing trick generalizes beyond logit spaces, and whether the training cost of diffusion models can be further reduced to make the approach competitive with simpler distillation in low-budget settings.
