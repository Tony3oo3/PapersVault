## Problem

Existing SAT instance generators either produce trivially easy instances that fail to match the computational hardness of real-world references, or rely on restrictive assumptions (e.g., HardSATGEN requires unsatisfiable reference instances and hand-crafted heuristics). A deeper issue is that construction-based split-merge methods tend to replicate similar substructures, which creates small unsatisfiable cores and causes hardness degradation. The paper asks whether one can generate hard instances by directly interpolating between two existing reference instances rather than constructing from scratch.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of reference SAT instances represented as bipartite literal-clause graphs (LCGs) of potentially different sizes (82–450 variables, 327–5424 clauses), along with a pretrained NeuroSAT-style graph network. It outputs a single interpolated CNF formula whose clause substructures are partially replaced from the second instance into the first, controlled by a mixing ratio (default 5%). The output preserves the satisfiability phase of the source instance and handles unmatched outlier variables by creating new variables in the target graph.

## Method

MixSATGEN is a two-stage graph interpolation pipeline. In the matching stage, a NeuroSAT-style message-passing network (pretrained via unsupervised SAT solving) extracts literal node embeddings; variable-level similarity is computed by aggregating literal-pair inner products over both phase alignments, then relaxed into a stochastic assignment matrix via Gumbel-Sinkhorn, where the Gumbel noise (controlled by λ and τ) injects tunable randomness to prevent hardness degradation from overly similar substructures. The matching is further refined by online optimization of Lawler's QAP objective over the soft assignment. In the mixing stage, clause pairs are iteratively selected and exchanged using a three-level confidence scheme (global confidence in the source graph, local confidence among candidate clauses in the target, then global confidence among the refined set), continuing until the target replacement ratio is reached. The key insight is that moderate randomness in the correspondence map avoids the formation of small unsatisfiable cores that deterministic matching would produce, thereby preserving computational hardness.

## Results

On the HARD dataset, MixSATGEN (λ=0.5, τ=1) best maintains computational hardness on satisfiable instances, achieving solver runtimes of 567.64s (hard-a, ref 254.22s), 470.22s (hard-b, ref 1382.71s), 775.46s (hard-c, ref 1204.43s), and 42.65s (hard-d, ref 852.85s) with CaDiCaL. All other learning-based baselines (G2SAT, GCN2S) and hand-crafted baselines (CA, PS) collapse to trivially easy instances (~0.01s). On structural metrics (Table 1), MixSATGEN achieves relative errors as low as 0.09% (VCG modularity, EASY) and 0.92% (VCG modularity, HARD), outperforming all baselines on most graph statistics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MixSATGEN decisively outperforms all hand-crafted baselines (CA, PS) and G2SAT, which all produce trivially easy instances solvable in ~0.01s regardless of reference hardness. GCN2S partially maintains hardness on hard-a (857.47s vs. 254.22s reference) but completely fails on all other hard instances. HardSATGEN excels on unsatisfiable instances (e.g., 2267.95s on hard-e vs. 2629.03s reference) but is entirely inapplicable to satisfiable benchmarks (marked N/A). MixSATGEN achieves the best hardness preservation on satisfiable hard instances (hard-a through hard-d) while remaining competitive on unsatisfiable ones, demonstrating general applicability that HardSATGEN lacks. On structural metrics, MixSATGEN (λ=0) achieves the lowest relative errors on VIG clustering (8.49% EASY, 4.90% HARD), VIG modularity (1.01% EASY, 6.84% HARD), and LCG modularity (1.50% EASY, 1.30% HARD), surpassing all baselines including GCN2S and HardSATGEN.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant construction-based paradigm for SAT instance generation (G2SAT, GCN2S, HardSATGEN), which generates instances from scratch via node split-merge pipelines, and instead proposes graph interpolation that modifies existing instances in place by exchanging clause substructures. The matching stage repurposes the NeuroSAT message-passing architecture for feature extraction and frames variable alignment as a linear assignment problem solved via Gumbel-Sinkhorn, building on the Lawler's QAP formulation from deep graph matching. The key conceptual departure is treating generation as a shortest-edit-path problem between two reference graphs rather than as a generative modeling task, which eliminates the need for unsatisfiable reference instances that HardSATGEN requires.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical efficiency advantage (roughly 1/2000 the generation time of G2SAT) and handles both satisfiable and unsatisfiable benchmarks, addressing a limitation of HardSATGEN. However, the hardness maintenance results are mixed: MixSATGEN performs well on satisfiable hard instances but shows significant degradation on unsatisfiable ones, and the Gumbel noise's benefit is instance-dependent rather than uniform. Open questions include scalability beyond hundreds of variables (constrained by the O(n²) affinity matrix), whether the interpolation approach can be combined with learned solvers for data augmentation, and whether multi-graph matching could further improve structural fidelity.
