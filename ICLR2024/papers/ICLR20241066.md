## Problem

Multi-region neural dynamics are nonlinear, non-stationary, and involve directed communication between brain regions, yet existing multi-region models (e.g., mp-rSLDS) rely on piecewise-linear dynamics that require many discrete states to approximate a single nonlinear vector field, reducing interpretability and increasing latent dimensionality. No prior model simultaneously captures switching nonlinear dynamics, explicit per-region decomposition, and directed cross-region communication signals in a unified probabilistic framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241066/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-region calcium imaging time series (single-cell mesoscope data with ~hundreds of neurons per region, or mesoscale widefield data across 8 bilateral cortical regions) along with observed stimulus input sequences. It produces per-region continuous latent trajectories, a global discrete state sequence selecting the active dynamical regime, and—critically—directed 'message' vectors between every pair of regions (m_t^{kj}) plus per-region input effects (m_t^{ku}), which decompose additively into each region's trajectory flow and can be decoded for stimulus or decision-related information content.

## Method

MR-SDS is a switching nonlinear state-space model in which the global continuous latent is decomposed into K per-region sub-vectors, and the dynamics function for each region is an additive sum of a local nonlinear term f_kk, cross-region communication terms f_kj, and an input term f_ku, all parameterized by feed-forward networks conditioned on a discrete state z_t that switches between M dynamical regimes. Inference uses a non-causal Transformer encoder with per-region embeddings to approximate the smoothing posterior; two schemes are provided—mean-field (Transformer outputs x posteriors, then Forward-Backward over an HMM for z) and structured (Transformer outputs z posteriors and smoothing potentials, reusing the generative dynamics for x). Training maximizes an ELBO augmented with beta-upweighting and a multi-step 'overshooting' objective that forces the emission network to pass through multiple timesteps of the generative dynamics, preventing posterior collapse and coupling dynamics and emissions during learning. Co-smoothing dropout training (dropping 25% of neurons per region on a fraction of trials) ensures the latent representation generalizes to held-out neurons, enabling the co-smoothing evaluation metric.

## Results

On the mesoscope dataset (3 regions, 178 trials), MR-SDS achieves a co-smoothing test MSE of 0.00377, outperforming the best multi-region linear baseline (mr-rSLDS with 10 latent dims per region at 0.00407) by ~7% and single-region LFADS (0.00435) by ~13%. On the widefield dataset (8 regions, 63 trials), MR-SDS achieves 7.49e-05 MSE, beating mr-PCA (9.08e-05) by ~17% and mr-rSLDS (1.57e-04) by ~52%. In the Lotka-Volterra simulation, recovered communication profiles match ground truth with a correlation coefficient of 0.99, and learned vector fields closely match true dynamics across both discrete states.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MR-SDS outperforms all multi-region linear baselines (mr-rSLDS, mr-SLDS, mr-LDS, mr-ar-rSLDS) on both datasets, with the largest margin on widefield data (~52% lower MSE than mr-rSLDS). Even when mr-rSLDS is given 10 latent dimensions per region (vs. 2 for MR-SDS), MR-SDS still achieves lower co-smoothing MSE on the mesoscope dataset (0.00377 vs. 0.00407), demonstrating that nonlinear dynamics provide expressivity that cannot be matched by simply increasing linear latent dimensionality. Against single-region baselines, MR-SDS beats both single-region rSLDS (0.00409) and LFADS (0.00435) on mesoscope, and single-region rSLDS (1.17e-04) and LFADS (9.24e-05) on widefield, while retaining per-region interpretability and communication estimates that single-region models lack. In simulations, MR-SDS recovers Lotka-Volterra communication profiles with 0.99 correlation and accurately reconstructs double-well accumulation/return dynamics, whereas mr-rSLDS shows visibly degraded vector field recovery and lower per-trial r² values.

## Relation to Prior Work

MR-SDS is positioned as a direct generalization of mp-rSLDS (Glaser et al., 2020), replacing piecewise-linear dynamics with nonlinear vector fields and adding explicit cross-region communication decomposition and nonlinear emissions. It draws on Dong et al. (2020) for the collapsed variational inference scheme over discrete switching states, on PLNDE (Kim et al., 2021) for the nonlinear low-dimensional dynamics philosophy (extended here to discrete time with multiple regions and switching), and on Ye & Pandarinath (2021) for Transformer expressivity, though it constrains the Transformer to an encoder-only inference role. The multi-region structure with per-region latents and additive communication terms follows the lineage from Semedo et al. (2014) and Glaser et al. (2020), while the switching nonlinear dynamics component extends the single-region models of Dong et al. (2020) and Ansari et al. (2021) to the multi-region setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The model provides an interpretable, low-dimensional decomposition of multi-region neural dynamics into local dynamics, directed cross-region messages, and input effects, enabling quantitative analysis of information flow that linear switching models cannot capture without inflating latent dimensionality. On real data, 2D latents per region suffice where rSLDS required 10D, and the communication profiles reveal functionally meaningful structure (e.g., PPC as a hub between early visual and upstream areas). Key limitations acknowledged by the authors include: no accounting for unobserved regions, non-causal learned interactions, fixed number of regions, and the inherent lumping-vs-splitting ambiguity of switching models. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to alternative communication types (e.g., oscillatory coupling), whether the number of regions can be learned, and how to handle unobserved neurons or regions.
