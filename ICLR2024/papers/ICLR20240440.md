## Problem

Existing OOD detection methods lack a unified theoretical perspective and rely on two-stage designs (pre-train then post-process) where frozen classification weights carry inherent bias that limits ID/OOD discriminability. Additionally, methods that synthesize OOD supervision in feature space may not correspond to any real OOD image, limiting open-world generalization. The paper asks whether natural OOD signals can be extracted directly from ID data to enable end-to-end joint optimization of classification and OOD detection.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images resized to 224×224 as input and produces two outputs: a standard class label from the classification head and an OOD probability score from a separate binary OOD head. During training, the classification head is applied patch-wise across the spatial dimensions of the feature map (without global pooling) to generate a confidence score map, from which self-supervised ID/OOD labels are extracted. At inference, the OOD score is computed as Sigmoid(f_ood(GAP(X))), yielding a single probability that the image belongs to the in-distribution.

## Method

SSOD is grounded in a probabilistic framework derived from Dempster-Shafer evidence theory that decomposes the posterior P(w_i|x) into an ID classification factor and an OOD factor P(x∈S_ID|x), making explicit that standard softmax networks only model the first factor. The key mechanism exploits the local receptive field property of convolutions: each spatial position in the feature map corresponds to a specific image patch, so the classification head applied patch-wise yields per-patch confidence scores. Patches with confidence ≥95% on the ground-truth class serve as ID supervision, while patches with confidence ≤5% serve as OOD supervision (background regions), with intermediate patches discarded. A lightweight binary OOD head is trained on these self-supervised patch labels via cross-entropy, jointly with the standard classification loss (weighted by α=1.0), all in a single end-to-end pass without any explicit OOD data or synthetic feature generation.

## Results

On ImageNet (ResNet-50 backbone), SSOD achieves an average FPR95 of 31.89% and AUROC of 92.26% across iNaturalist, SUN, Places, and Texture, outperforming the best prior method (MCM with CLIP-L) by 6.28% FPR95 and 0.77% AUROC. On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18, trained from scratch), SSOD reduces average FPR95 to 5.19% and raises AUROC to 98.94%, improving over the best baseline (CSI) by 19.01% FPR95 and 3.04% AUROC. On hard OOD benchmarks, SSOD achieves top-ranked FPR95 on OpenImage-O (55.41%) and competitive performance on ImageNet-O (79.80%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSOD outperforms all 13 compared baselines on average FPR95 across the four ImageNet OOD datasets, beating the strongest prior method MCM (CLIP-L) by 6.28% FPR95 (31.89% vs. 38.17%) while using a simple ResNet-50 backbone instead of a large CLIP model. On CIFAR-10, SSOD dominates all baselines by a wide margin, reducing average FPR95 from 24.20% (CSI, the best prior) to 5.19% and raising AUROC from 95.90% to 98.94%. On the hard OOD benchmark OpenImage-O, SSOD achieves the best FPR95 (55.41%) among all methods, outperforming ViM (50.45% FPR95) and ReAct (60.79% FPR95). The one notable exception is the Texture dataset, where KNN (w/o CL) achieves a much lower FPR95 (11.56%) due to domain overlap between ImageNet and Texture; SSOD still reports 45.32% FPR95, better than all other methods except KNN. On ImageNet-O (adversarial OOD), SSOD is slightly behind ReAct (72.85% vs. 79.80% FPR95) and ViM (76.00% FPR95), which the authors attribute to the adversarial nature of that dataset causing misclassification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Dempster-Shafer evidence theory to derive a unified probabilistic decomposition of robust classification into an ID factor (standard softmax) and an OOD factor (probability of belonging to the in-distribution), providing a common lens under which score-based methods (MSP, ODIN, Energy), feature-based methods (ReAct, KNN), and synthetic-OOD methods (BAL, VOS) can all be interpreted. It departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm (pre-train on ID, then post-process for OOD) by proposing end-to-end joint training, and from synthetic OOD generation approaches by instead sampling natural OOD supervision directly from the background regions of ID images, leveraging the local receptive field property of convolutional networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value of SSOD lies in eliminating the need to collect or synthesize explicit OOD data, making OOD detection accessible in settings where only ID labels are available, and the unified probabilistic framework gives the field a clearer vocabulary for comparing and combining existing techniques. Key limitations include its reliance on CNN spatial locality (limiting direct transfer to ViT/transformer backbones), degraded performance on Texture due to semantic overlap with ImageNet, and reduced effectiveness on adversarial OOD data like ImageNet-O. Open questions include whether the background-as-OOD-proxy principle generalizes to non-convolutional architectures and to truly open-world scenarios where OOD data shares no visual similarity with training backgrounds.
