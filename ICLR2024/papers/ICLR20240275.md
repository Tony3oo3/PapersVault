## Problem

Offline RL in partially observable settings, where policies must condition on entire observation histories rather than known states, cannot leverage trajectory stitching because two trajectories almost never share identical histories. The paper asks: under what conditions can offline RL with observation histories still achieve efficient sample complexity, and how can we explicitly enforce those conditions?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240275/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes observation histories (sequences of past observations and actions) of variable length, processed by task-specific encoders: a single-layer RNN for tabular gridworld, a three-layer CNN (80×80 first-person pixels) feeding a three-layer RNN for ViZDoom, and a randomly initialized GPT-2 small transformer for Wordle text tokens. The encoder produces a fixed-dimensional embedding that serves as a compact history representation; the policy head outputs an action distribution conditioned on this embedding, and the bisimulation loss provides an auxiliary training signal that shapes the embedding geometry.

## Method

The paper first proves that standard pessimistic offline RL (PEVI) on observation-history MDPs incurs suboptimality scaling as |H|, which is exponential in horizon H, because the history space is combinatorially large. It then shows that if an encoder φ maps histories to a compact space Z such that the induced distances approximate the π*-bisimulation metric, the same algorithm achieves near-optimal sample complexity scaling with |Z| instead. Practically, the bisimulation loss trains φ by minimizing the discrepancy between embedding distances and a learned approximation of the bisimulation metric, where the true reward/dynamics and optimal policy are replaced by a fitted reward model, a fitted transition model, and the current policy as a proxy. This loss is added on top of existing offline RL objectives (CQL, IQL, ILQL) with a target network for stability, and the encoder simultaneously parameterizes the dynamics model used in the loss, creating a joint training loop.

## Results

On the ViZDoom visual navigation task, adding the bisimulation loss to CQL improves mean reward from 0.64±0.17 to 0.71±0.14 on the base task and from 0.43±0.08 to 0.58±0.09 on the harder task (agent spawns farthest from goal). Similarly, IQL+Bisimulation outperforms IQL on the hard task (0.61±0.11 vs. 0.48±0.07). On the Wordle natural language task, ILQL+Bisimulation achieves a score of -2.19±0.03, marginally outperforming ILQL (-2.21±0.03) and substantially outperforming fine-tuning (-2.83±0.05) and filtered fine-tuning (-3.02±0.06).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its bisimulation loss against four offline RL baselines: CQL, IQL, MOPO, and ILQL. On the ViZDoom hard task, CQL+Bisimulation (0.58) outperforms CQL (0.43) by 0.15, and IQL+Bisimulation (0.61) outperforms IQL (0.48) by 0.13, with the improvement being larger than on the base task, suggesting compact representations matter more for longer trajectories. Both bisimulation-augmented methods also outperform the model-based MOPO baseline (0.41 on hard task), indicating that using a learned model in a bisimulation loss is more effective than in traditional model-based optimization. On the Wordle task, ILQL+Bisimulation (-2.19) only marginally beats ILQL (-2.21), and the authors show that ILQL already implicitly minimizes the bisimulation loss during standard training, explaining the small gap. Imitation learning baselines (BC, Filtered BC) are consistently outperformed by all offline RL methods, confirming the value of trajectory stitching in partially observed settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pessimistic offline RL framework (PEVI from Jin et al. 2021a, instantiated via CQL and IQL) from fully-observed MDPs to observation-history MDPs (POMDPs), where prior near-optimal sample complexity guarantees break down due to the exponential history space. It bridges the bisimulation metric literature (Ferns et al. 2012; Castro 2019) and the representation-learning-for-RL line (Zhang et al. 2021) with offline RL theory, showing that learning encoders whose induced distances approximate the optimal-policy bisimulation metric restores efficient sample complexity. The practical algorithm adapts the joint encoder-policy training of Zhang et al. (2021) into an auxiliary loss on top of standard offline RL objectives, rather than replacing them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is, to the authors' knowledge, the first theoretical treatment showing when and how practical offline RL algorithms can be provably efficient in POMDPs, filling a gap between the MDP offline RL theory and the POMDP setting that is common in real applications (autonomous driving, dialogue, robotics). The bisimulation loss is a simple, drop-in addition to existing algorithms, and the finding that successful offline RL runs sometimes already implicitly minimize this loss (as in the Wordle task) suggests a deeper connection between standard training dynamics and representation quality that remains open. A key limitation is that bisimulation is shown to be sufficient but not necessary for efficiency, and the theory is restricted to tabular POMDPs; extending the analysis to function-approximation settings and characterizing when standard offline RL naturally learns useful representations are important future directions.
