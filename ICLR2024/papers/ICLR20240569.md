## Problem

Existing latent variable models for neuroscience either handle a single data modality or collapse all modalities into one shared latent space, making it difficult to isolate which latent features are shared across versus independent to neural activity and behavior. Approaches that do partition shared and independent subspaces either lack temporal structure or rely on inflexible linear dynamical systems, limiting their applicability to complex behavioral modalities like high-dimensional images or multi-joint limb tracking.

## Input / Output

The model consumes simultaneously recorded multi-modal time-series organized as observation-by-time matrices across trials: one modality is neural data (Poisson spike counts or Gaussian calcium traces) and the other is a behavioral/stimulus signal (e.g., 16 limb position coordinates, event-based visual stimulus frames, or reaching trajectories). The latent space is parameterized in a pruned Fourier domain with F frequency components (F < T time-bins), and the output is a partitioned set of shared and modality-specific latent trajectories, per-modality reconstructions, and a loadings matrix W that linearly maps latents to neural log-rates, providing interpretable per-neuron tuning to shared versus independent dynamics.

## Method

MM-GPVAE parameterizes latent time-series in the Fourier domain using a small number of pruned frequencies, which diagonalizes the RBF GP covariance matrix (avoiding costly matrix inversion), enforces temporal smoothness by discarding high frequencies, and retains mean-field factorization over frequencies for tractable variational inference. The multi-modal extension partitions the latent space into shared (z_S) and modality-specific (z_A, z_B) subspaces via a block-structured loadings matrix W, where the neural modality is decoded linearly through a pointwise exponential nonlinearity into a Poisson likelihood (preserving GPFA-style interpretability), while the behavioral modality is decoded through a deep neural network into a Gaussian likelihood (preserving GP-VAE expressivity). Training maximizes an ELBO comprising the Poisson neural likelihood, Gaussian behavioral likelihood, Fourier-domain GP prior, and variational entropy, with all parameters (encoder, decoder, loadings, and GP hyperparameters) learned end-to-end via amortized variational inference.

## Results

On simulated MNIST data with 100 Poisson neurons, the MM-GPVAE accurately recovers all three latent dimensions (shared, neural-independent, image-independent) and provides better held-out reconstructions of both images and neural rates than single-modality baselines (GP-VAE and GPFA). The Fourier-domain parameterization dramatically improves latent identification over the standard GP-VAE, with visibly smoother and more accurate latent trajectories on 60 held-out trials (Fig. 1d). On the hawkmoth dataset, removing the shared latent dimension causes approximately a 70% reduction in image reconstruction accuracy and a ~5% reduction in neural rate reconstruction, confirming meaningful shared structure. On the Drosophila dataset, the shared subspace contributes more to overall data variability than either independent subspace for both modalities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard VAE (Kingma & Welling, 2013), the Fourier GP-VAE produces substantially smoother latent trajectories and lower MSE on latent identification for 60 held-out trials, while all three models retain comparable image reconstruction quality. Against the standard GP-VAE (Casale et al., 2018), the Fourier-domain variant dramatically improves latent identification, and the multi-modal MM-GPVAE reconstructs held-out images with lower pixel MSE than a GP-VAE trained on images alone, by leveraging latent information from the spiking modality. Against GPFA (Yu et al., 2008), the MM-GPVAE achieves better held-out neural rate reconstruction when image data is included, and identifies the shared latent more accurately than either single-modality model. Against TNDM (Hurwitz et al., 2021) and PSID (Sani et al., 2021), the MM-GPVAE achieves lower MSE on both image and neural rate reconstruction on the simulated rotating/scaling MNIST task (Fig. 6), and on the primate reaching dataset provides three interpretable subspaces (neural-only, behavioral-only, shared) with clear behavioral separation in both the behavioral and shared subspaces, whereas TNDM and PSID each provide only two subspaces with separation limited to a single 'relevant' subspace.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work: the Fourier-domain Gaussian Process Factor Analysis (GPFA) framework for neural population activity (Yu et al., 2008; Aoi & Pillow, 2017; Keeley et al., 2020a) and the GP-prior variational autoencoder (GP-VAE) for high-dimensional observations (Casale et al., 2018). The key architectural departure is transplanting the spectral (Fourier) parameterization from the linear GPFA setting into the non-linear GP-VAE, and then extending the result to a multi-modal model that explicitly partitions latents into shared and modality-independent subspaces for both data types simultaneously. This contrasts with prior multi-modal LVMs like TNDM (Hurwitz et al., 2021) and PSID (Sani et al., 2021), which use RNN or linear state-space dynamics and linear behavioral decoders, and which do not isolate a shared subspace for the behavioral modality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MM-GPVAE fills a practical gap for systems neuroscientists who need interpretable, temporally structured latent variables that are explicitly decomposed into shared and independent components across neural and behavioral data — a capability that prior multi-modal LVMs lack or handle only for the neural modality. The Fourier-domain parameterization offers a concrete computational advantage (avoiding matrix inversion, enabling frequency pruning) that makes the GP-VAE more tractable and improves latent identifiability. However, the GP prior's smoothness assumption limits applicability to paradigms with abrupt or switching dynamics, and the dimensionality selection procedure (PCA for neural, cross-validation for behavioral) remains somewhat ad hoc. Open questions include how the model scales to very long recordings, whether the shared/independent partition is uniquely identifiable in practice, and how it compares to alternative temporal priors (switching dynamics, RNNs) in multi-modal settings.
