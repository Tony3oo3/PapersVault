## Problem

In privacy-preserving machine learning, training data is often aggregated into bags where only the average response per bag is shared, but two natural loss functions—bag-level (fitting aggregate predictions to aggregate labels) and instance-level (fitting individual predictions to the bag average)—are used without a clear theoretical understanding of when each generalizes better. The paper addresses this gap by establishing a precise bias-variance characterization of both estimators and identifying the conditions under which one dominates the other.

## Input / Output

The learner receives individual feature vectors x_i in full but only the bag-average response ȳ_a for each of m non-overlapping bags of fixed size k, rather than individual labels. The output is a model parameter vector θ (linear or neural network) trained on this partially-observed data, with the paper providing exact asymptotic formulas for the prediction risk decomposed into bias and variance. In the differential privacy extension, the output additionally includes privatized aggregate responses ỹ_a = ȳ_a + Laplace(C√log n / kε), and the optimal bag size k* is derived as a function of the privacy budget ε and regularization parameter ρ.

## Method

The central insight (Lemma 2.1) is that for quadratic loss, the instance-level loss decomposes exactly as the bag-level loss plus a data-dependent regularization term R(θ) that penalizes within-bag variance of model predictions, making instance-level loss a regularized version of bag-level loss. This motivates an interpolating loss L_int(θ) = L_bag(θ) + ρ·R(θ) with ρ ∈ [0,1] that continuously spans from bag-level (ρ=0) to instance-level (ρ=1), with the optimal ρ balancing bias against variance. For linear regression in the proportional regime (n/d → ψ), the authors apply the Convex-Gaussian-Minimax-Theorem to derive closed-form asymptotic expressions for bias and variance as explicit functions of bag size k, signal-to-noise ratio, overparametrization ψ, and ρ. The analysis reveals that bag-level loss is unbiased but high-variance while instance-level loss introduces bias to reduce variance, with a derived SNR threshold determining which estimator achieves lower risk. The framework is extended to label differential privacy via a Laplace mechanism on truncated aggregate responses, yielding a phase transition in the optimal bag size: small ρ favors k=1 (more bags, less instability) while large ρ favors the maximum k (less DP noise per bag).

## Results

The paper provides a precise asymptotic characterization (Theorem 2.5) of the bias and variance of the interpolating estimator in the proportional regime (n/d → ψ). For the bag-level estimator (ρ=0), the bias vanishes and the variance converges to σ²/(ψ/k − 1); for the instance-level estimator (ρ=1), the bias converges to (1−1/k)(1+(2−ψ)/(k(ψ−1))) and the variance to σ²/(k(ψ−1)). On the Boston Housing dataset (13 features, 4-layer ReLU network with 64 neurons per layer), the optimal ρ shifts from 0 at bag size 80 to 1 at bag size 240, with intermediate bag sizes (e.g., 120–200) achieving the lowest test loss at ρ ∈ (0,1), confirming that the interpolating estimator outperforms both extremes for moderate bag sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three estimators: the bag-level loss (ρ=0), the instance-level loss (ρ=1), and the proposed interpolating loss (ρ∈(0,1)). Theoretically, the bag-level estimator is unbiased but has higher variance, while the instance-level estimator introduces bias but reduces variance; Lemma 3.2 provides an explicit SNR threshold below which the instance-level estimator achieves lower prediction risk. Empirically on Boston Housing, for small bag sizes (k=80) the bag-level loss (ρ=0) is optimal, while for large bag sizes (k=240) the instance-level loss (ρ=1) is optimal. Crucially, for intermediate bag sizes the interpolating estimator with ρ∈(0,1) achieves strictly lower test loss than either extreme, demonstrating that the regularization interpretation (Lemma 2.1) translates into a practical generalization advantage. The paper also shows that in the differentially private setting, jointly optimizing over (k, ρ) yields the best risk at ρ=1 with the largest feasible bag size, indicating that the instance-level loss combined with larger bags provides the best privacy–accuracy trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the well-studied learning-with-label-proportions (LLP) literature, which has separately developed bag-level methods (Rueping 2010, Yu et al. 2014 EPRM) and instance-level methods (Yu et al. 2013, Dulac-Arnold et al. 2019) without a unifying theoretical framework. The key departure is showing that the instance-level loss is algebraically equivalent to the bag-level loss plus a data-dependent regularization term, thereby unifying the two approaches under a single bias-variance lens. The paper further extends this framework into the differential privacy setting, building on the label-DP notion of Chaudhuri & Hsu (2011) and the Laplace mechanism, but applying them specifically to aggregate responses rather than individual labels—a setting not previously analyzed with precise asymptotics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first precise asymptotic characterization of the bias-variance trade-off between bag-level and instance-level estimators in the proportional regime, giving practitioners a principled way to choose between them (or interpolate) based on SNR, bag size, and overparametrization. The DP mechanism with a phase transition in optimal bag size (singleton bags optimal at low regularization, large bags optimal at high regularization) is a practically useful insight for privacy-preserving ML pipelines. Limitations include the restriction to linear models with Gaussian features, a single empirical dataset (Boston Housing), and the proportional regime assumption; extending the theory to non-linear models, structured bagging, and full (not just label) differential privacy remain open.
