## Problem

Existing GNN models for predicting the stiffness tensor of lattice metamaterials lack grounding in physical principles: they are not equivariant to rigid-body rotations and can predict negative deformation energy, violating the thermodynamic law of energy conservation. This makes them unreliable for out-of-distribution lattice topologies or for design tasks where self-consistency across orientations is essential. The paper also notes the absence of a large, publicly available dataset of structure–property relationships for strut-based lattices to support physics-informed model development.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a geometric graph representing the fundamental unit cell of a periodic lattice: node positions in a reduced [0,1]³ coordinate system, edge adjacency with periodic boundary shifts (encoding how edges wrap across the unit cell), and a scalar strut radius per edge. The output is the full 4th-order stiffness tensor C_ijkl (21 independent components due to minor and major symmetries), assembled in Mandel notation as a 6×6 symmetric matrix. The critical structural constraint on the output is positive semi-definiteness, ensuring non-negative strain energy for all admissible deformations.

## Method

The architecture builds on the MACE higher-order equivariant message-passing backbone, where edge embeddings are formed via tensor products of node features and spherical-harmonic-expanded edge vectors weighted by Gaussian-embedded edge scalars, and multi-body messages (up to 4-body) are constructed through repeated tensor products with Clebsch-Gordan symmetrization. The key contribution is a positive semi-definite (PSD) readout layer: the equivariant network outputs spherical components (2×0e + 2×2e + 1×4e) that are converted to a Cartesian 4th-order tensor, arranged into a 6×6 Mandel-notation matrix, and then passed through a matrix-square operation (A²) to guarantee non-negative eigenvalues. The use of Mandel (rather than Voigt) notation is essential because it preserves orthonormality of the SO(3) rotation representation in the 6D basis, which the paper proves is a necessary condition for the PSD layer to remain equivariant. Training employs dynamic data augmentation (each lattice is randomly rotated at every dataset access) and a normalized component-wise loss; the model requires L_max=4 spherical expansion to capture the anisotropic 4th-order structure, and higher body-order messages (ν≥2) yield substantial accuracy gains over 2-body TFN-style messages.

## Results

On the Lattice Metamaterials Dataset (635,454 geometries), the equivariant MACE+ve model achieves the best performance across all metrics: L_comp=3.61, L_dir=4.21, L_dir,rel=0.21, L_equiv=0, and λ_%^-=0 (zero negative eigenvalues). The equivariant MACE class consistently outperforms non-equivariant CGC and NNConv baselines by all error metrics. Inference for 5000 lattices takes 15 s with MACE versus 1.1×10⁴ s for finite element analysis, a ~733× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the CGC/mCGC baseline from Meyer et al. (2022), the equivariant MACE+ve model reduces L_comp by 19% (3.61 vs. 4.47 for CGC+tr) and L_dir by 20% (4.21 vs. 5.23), while achieving zero equivariance loss (vs. 0.15) and zero negative eigenvalues (vs. 26% for CGC+tr). Against the NNConv baseline from Ross & Hambleton (2020), MACE+ve reduces L_comp by 35% (3.61 vs. 5.57 for NNConv+tr) and L_dir by 39% (4.21 vs. 6.90), with zero equivariance loss (vs. 0.25). The key advantage of the equivariant architecture is that it achieves these gains without requiring data augmentation (MACE+tr only marginally improves over MACE), and the PSD layer eliminates unphysical negative eigenvalue predictions at negligible accuracy cost (L_comp 3.88→3.61). The paper also demonstrates that inductive bias (equivariance by architecture) is superior to observation bias (data augmentation) and learning bias (penalty terms) for enforcing physical constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the MACE equivariant message-passing architecture (Batatia et al., 2022) from computational chemistry to the mechanical engineering task of predicting 4th-order stiffness tensors of lattice metamaterials. It departs from prior GNN approaches for lattice property prediction (Meyer et al., 2022; Ross & Hambleton, 2020), which were non-equivariant and relied on static data augmentation, by encoding SE(3) equivariance inductive bias directly in the architecture. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a positive semi-definite (PSD) layer operating in Mandel/Kelvin notation, which the authors prove preserves equivariance—a property that would be broken under the more common Voigt notation—thereby guaranteeing energy conservation in the predicted stiffness tensor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first equivariant model for 4th-order tensor prediction that provably satisfies the thermodynamic constraint of non-negative deformation energy, closing a gap where prior ML surrogates could predict unphysical negative eigenvalues (30% of cases in the base MACE model). The publicly released dataset of 635K lattice geometries with FE-computed elastic properties and the Mandel-notation PSD layer technique are reusable assets for the broader community working on physics-constrained tensor prediction. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other 4th-order tensors (piezo-optical, elasto-optical, flexoelectric), to non-linear or large-deformation regimes, and to more complex lattice topologies beyond strut-based geometries. The 3-order-of-magnitude speedup over FE makes gradient-based inverse design of anisotropic stiffness practically feasible.
