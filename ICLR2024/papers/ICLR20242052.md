## Problem

Existing explanation methods for CNNs do not faithfully reflect the model's actual inference process, making them susceptible to adversarial manipulation that can completely alter the explanation while preserving the prediction. Additionally, prior methods analyze models at the individual neuron level with linear assumptions about channel significance, overlooking the nonlinear interactions between neurons that jointly form concepts, and they discard information carried by inactive neurons after ReLU activation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242052/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained CNN (ResNet50 or VGG16) and a standard 224×224 RGB image, and produces saliency maps at three resolutions: low (7×7), high (28×28), and input-scale (224×224), all bilinearly interpolated to 224×224 for evaluation. Beyond the final saliency map, SRD also yields high-resolution Effective Receptive Fields (ERFs) at every intermediate layer, enabling both local (where) and global (what/how) explanations. The analytical unit is the Pointwise Feature Vector (PFV) — a C-dimensional vector along the channel axis at each spatial location — rather than individual scalar neuron values.

## Method

SRD decomposes each PFV into weighted contributions (sharing ratios) from PFVs in its receptive field at the prior layer, where the sharing ratio μ is computed as the normalized inner product between the affine-transformed source PFV and the target PFV. This decomposition is applied recursively from the encoder output back to the input, constructing ERFs at every layer as weighted sums of constituent ERFs, with the final saliency map obtained as a weighted sum of the encoder-output ERFs. The method operates on pre-activation values rather than post-activation values, motivated by the Activation-Pattern-Only Prediction (APOP) observation that classification accuracy is largely preserved using only the binary on/off activation pattern, demonstrating that inactive neurons carry essential information lost after ReLU. The forward pass builds ERFs while the backward pass computes sharing ratios, and the two are proven equivalent. For the final MLP classifier, the method delegates to Grad-CAM-style weighting to obtain class-specific contributions, then applies mean subtraction and ReLU to ensure class-discriminative saliency.

## Results

On ImageNet-S50 (752 samples), SRD-high achieves the best Pointing Game score (0.948 on VGG16, 0.952 on ResNet50), best Attribution Localization (0.566 on VGG16, 0.579 on ResNet50), and best Fidelity (0.206 on VGG16, 0.142 on ResNet50) among all compared methods. SRD-input achieves the best Sparseness (0.788 on VGG16, 0.724 on ResNet50) and best Stability (0.099 on VGG16, 0.104 on ResNet50), indicating the most robust explanations under random perturbation. In the adversarial robustness experiment, SRD attains the highest SSIM and PCC scores, retaining the target object in its saliency map while all other gradient-based methods lose it.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRD outperforms all 13 baselines on the majority of metrics. On VGG16, SRD-high beats the best CAM-based method (ScoreCAM, Att.=0.582) on Fidelity (0.206 vs. 0.167) and matches or exceeds GradCAM on Pointing Game (0.948 vs. 0.945), while SRD-input dominates on Sparseness (0.788 vs. 0.741 for GuidedBackprop) and Stability (0.099 vs. 0.100 for GuidedBackprop). On ResNet50, SRD-high achieves the best Attribution Localization (0.579) and Fidelity (0.142), surpassing LayerCAM-high (0.423 Att., 0.135 Fid.) by a wide margin, and SRD-input leads on Pointing Game (0.953) and Stability (0.104). The key differentiator is robustness: SRD-input's Stability score of 0.099–0.104 is roughly 4–7× lower than CAM-based methods (0.45–0.83), and in the targeted adversarial attack experiment, SRD retains the highest SSIM and PCC while all other gradient-based methods (Gradient, GradInput, GuidedBackprop, InteGrad, LRP_z+) lose the target object entirely in their manipulated saliency maps.

## Relation to Prior Work

SRD extends the Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP) lineage by replacing scalar neuron-level analysis with a vector-based Pointwise Feature Vector (PFV) unit, and by propagating relevance through pre-activation values to capture contributions to both active and inactive neurons. It also builds on the Effective Receptive Field concept from Luo et al. (2016) but introduces instance-level sharing ratios to make ERFs more faithful. The method deliberately departs from CAM-based approaches (Grad-CAM, LayerCAM, ScoreCAM) that assume linear channel independence, and from gradient-based methods (Saliency, Guided Backprop, Integrated Gradients) whose reliance on gradients is questioned during inference. The APOP observation provides a novel empirical justification for why pre-activation and inactive-neuron information must be preserved in any faithful explanation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The APOP finding—that binary activation patterns alone can recover most classification accuracy without any input values—challenges the field's implicit assumption that neuron magnitudes, not just on/off states, carry the essential decision information. This has implications beyond XAI, potentially informing model compression, pruning, and understanding of what information deep networks actually encode. However, the method is currently limited to CNNs (ResNet, VGG), relies on Grad-CAM for the final MLP layer, and the global explanation (how the model decides) remains preliminary. Open questions include whether the APOP phenomenon generalizes to transformers and other architectures, and whether the vector-based decomposition scales efficiently to very deep or wide networks.
