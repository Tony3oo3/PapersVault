## Problem

While scaling up representations has revolutionized 2D vision and NLP, scalable 3D representation learning remains largely unexplored. Existing 3D pretraining methods either rely on small-scale backbones (≤72M parameters) or use non-unified architectures that resist scaling, and none leverage the rich 2D pretrained priors or image-text aligned models that drive progress in other modalities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241825/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pretraining, Uni3D consumes triplets of (point cloud, rendered image, text) where each point cloud is 10,000 colored points sampled from a mesh surface, each image is one of 10 rendered views uniformly covering the shape, and the text is a shape description. The model outputs a single 3D feature embedding aligned to the CLIP image-text space, which downstream tasks repurpose for classification (via cosine similarity to text embeddings), linear probing, part segmentation (via feature propagation from intermediate ViT layers), point cloud painting (via gradient-based RGB optimization), and cross-modal retrieval (via kNN in the shared embedding space).

## Method

Uni3D replaces the ViT patch embedding with a PointBERT-style point tokenizer (FPS + kNN local grouping followed by a tiny PointNet per patch) while keeping the rest of the transformer structurally identical to a 2D ViT. This architectural equivalence enables two key advantages: (1) direct initialization from any 2D pretrained ViT (EVA, DINO, CLIP) to stabilize training of large 3D models, and (2) straightforward scaling from 6M to 1B parameters using standard ViT depth/width/head configurations. Training uses a symmetric triplet contrastive loss (point↔text, point↔image, image↔text) where only the 3D encoder is learnable and the CLIP image/text encoders are frozen; the best CLIP teacher (EVA-CLIP-E, 5B params) provides the alignment target. Practical training employs FLIP (50% random token masking to halve compute), precached CLIP embeddings enabling batch size 1152, and DeepSpeed ZeRO-1 with fp16, allowing the 1B model to converge in ~20 hours on 24 A100 GPUs.

## Results

On zero-shot shape classification, Uni3D achieves 88.2% Top-1 accuracy on ModelNet40, 65.3% on ScanObjectNN, and 55.3% on Objaverse-LVIS (best setting with LVIS shapes in training), substantially outperforming prior methods. On ScanNet zero-shot recognition, Uni3D reaches 45.8% average Top-1 accuracy without any real-world training data, surpassing CLIP² (38.5%) which was trained on 1.6M real-world triplets. For few-shot part segmentation on ShapeNetPart, Uni3D achieves 75.9% mIoU_C in the 1-shot setting, matching PointBERT trained on 10% of the training set (76.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Uni3D outperforms all prior 3D representation methods across every benchmark. On zero-shot classification (Table 1), Uni3D beats OpenShape-PointBERT by +6.7 points on Objaverse-LVIS Top-1 (53.5 vs. 46.8), +2.9 on ModelNet40 (87.3 vs. 84.4), and +11.7 on ScanObjectNN (63.9 vs. 52.2); it surpasses ULIP-PointBERT by a wide margin of +26.7 on Objaverse-LVIS. On ScanNet zero-shot recognition (Table 2), Uni3D (45.8% avg) outperforms CLIP² (38.5%), CLIP2Point w/TP (35.2%), and PointCLIP w/TP (26.1%), despite being trained only on synthetic data while the latter two use 1.6M real-world triplets. For few-shot part segmentation (Table 3), Uni3D's 1-shot result (75.9% mIoU_C) is comparable to PointBERT trained on 10% of the training set (76.4%), and its 2-shot result (78.2%) approaches PointBERT at 20% (79.6%), despite using two orders of magnitude fewer training samples. Uni3D also dominates in few-shot linear probing on Objaverse-LVIS (Table 9), achieving 46.5% at 1-shot versus 24.0% for OpenShape-PointBERT and 18.9% for retrained ULIP.

## Relation to Prior Work

Uni3D extends the multi-modal contrastive pretraining paradigm established by ULIP and OpenShape (aligning point cloud features with frozen CLIP image-text features) but makes two critical architectural departures: it replaces specialized 3D backbones (PointBERT, SparseConv) with a vanilla ViT structurally identical to its 2D counterpart, and it leverages 2D pretrained models (EVA, DINO) as initialization. This design choice enables direct transfer of well-studied 2D/NLP scaling strategies (Tiny→Giant) and the rich ecosystem of pretrained 2D transformers, which prior 3D works could not exploit due to their non-unified, architecture-specific backbones. The point tokenizer is inherited from PointBERT, and the contrastive objective follows the ULIP/OpenShape formulation, so the novelty lies in the unified architecture enabling scale and cross-modal initialization rather than in the learning objective itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Uni3D demonstrates for the first time that 3D representation learning can follow the same scaling trajectory as 2D vision and NLP, with continuous improvements from 6M to 1B parameters and the ability to benefit from future CLIP/2D model progress by simply swapping teachers. The 88.2% zero-shot accuracy on ModelNet40 approaching supervised performance, combined with strong open-vocabulary part segmentation and cross-modal retrieval, positions Uni3D as a practical 3D foundation model. Key limitations include reliance on synthetic training data (no real-world 3D scans in pretraining), object-level (not scene-level) scope, and the point tokenizer remaining a PointBERT-era design that may bottleneck further scaling. Open questions include whether Objaverse-XL (10M+ objects) yields further gains, whether the 2D initialization advantage persists at even larger scales, and how to extend this to scene-level and dynamic 3D understanding.
