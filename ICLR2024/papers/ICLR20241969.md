## Problem

Traditional MT evaluation treats quality assessment as a regression problem producing absolute scores, which are hard to annotate consistently and require reference translations that are often unavailable in practice. The pairwise ranking formulation—predicting which of two translations is better—had only been explored in the reference-based setting, leaving the reference-free pairwise scenario unexplored despite being the most practical use case for comparing MT systems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241969/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a single concatenated text string of the form 'Source: S Translation 0: T0 Translation 1: T1' and produces a binary probability indicating which translation is better. The multilingual T5 encoder attends over all tokens of the source and both translations simultaneously, followed by mean pooling and a logistic regression head. No reference translation is provided at inference time, and the output is a relative judgment rather than an absolute quality score.

## Method

MT-Ranker uses a multilingual T5 encoder (Base/Large/XXL) with mean pooling and a logistic regression classifier to perform binary pairwise ranking. Training proceeds in three stages without any human-annotated quality scores: (I) pretraining on XNLI where entailed vs. non-entailed hypotheses provide indirect supervision to prefer translations consistent with the source; (II) fine-tuning on pairs of human reference translations vs. machine translations from WMT Direct Assessment datasets (2017–2020); (III) weakly supervised fine-tuning on synthetic data generated two ways—BERTScore-labeled MT pairs from DA datasets and perturbation-based pairs (word drop, MLM word replacement, backtranslation via M2M100, and post-backtranslation word replacement) from MT-PRISM. This three-stage curriculum progressively exposes the model to the full spectrum of translation quality differences, with the synthetic perturbation stage being the most critical for final performance.

## Results

On the WMT20 Shared Metrics Task (DA20), MT-Ranker-XXL achieves an average segment-level Kendall's Tau of 22.0 on X-to-En and 52.2 on En-to-X language pairs, outperforming the best supervised baselines by 4.4 and 3.8 points respectively. On the MQM benchmarks, MT-Ranker-XXL achieves average Kendall's Tau of 22.3 (MQM20), 12.4 (MQM21), and 27.7 (MQM22), setting new state-of-the-art results. On the fine-grained ACES benchmark, MT-Ranker-XXL achieves an ACES-Score of 18.46, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art KG-BERTScore (17.49) without using any human-annotated training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MT-Ranker-XXL outperforms all eight baselines across every benchmark. Against unsupervised baselines, it surpasses SENTBLEU (Post, 2018) by a wide margin (e.g., 22.0 vs. 12.8 avg on DA20 X-to-En) and BERTScore (Zhang, 2020) by roughly 4 points on the same setting. Among supervised baselines, it beats COMET-QE (Rei, 2020) by 6.9 points on DA20 X-to-En (22.0 vs. 15.1) and by 1.3–2.8 points on MQM20–22. It also outperforms OPENKIWI-XLMR (Kepler, 2019) by 4.4 points on DA20 X-to-En and T5Score (Qin, 2022) by 3.8 points on DA20 En-to-X. On the MQM benchmarks, MT-Ranker-XXL edges out COMETKiwi (Rei, 2022) by 1.8 points on MQM22 (27.7 vs. 25.9) and UniTE (Wan, 2022) by 6.5 points. On the ACES benchmark, it surpasses the previous state-of-the-art KG-BERTScore (Wu, 2023) by 0.97 in ACES-Score (18.46 vs. 17.49), with particularly strong gains on omission (0.97 vs. 0.81) and punctuation (0.97 vs. 0.26) error categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pairwise ranking formulation for MT evaluation, first proposed by Ye et al. (2007) and later explored by Duh (2008) and Guzmán et al. (2014, 2015), from the reference-based setting to the reference-free setting. It departs from the dominant regression/scoring paradigm (BLEU, BERTScore, COMET, T5Score) by reframing evaluation as a binary better-worse classification over translation pairs. The training strategy borrows perturbation heuristics from BLEURT (Sellam et al., 2020) for synthetic data generation and repurposes cross-lingual NLI (XNLI) as an indirect supervision signal, a connection not previously exploited for MT evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Reference-free pairwise ranking addresses the most practical evaluation scenario (references unavailable) while matching the actual use case of comparing MT systems, and doing so without any human quality annotations is a meaningful reduction in annotation cost. However, the system shows clear limitations on untranslated phenomena (negative correlations on two of three subtypes) and cannot handle wrong-language errors, leaving open questions about robustness to subtle quality differences and very low-resource language pairs. The modest but consistent margins over supervised baselines (1–4 Kendall's Tau points) suggest the pairwise formulation captures signal that regression-based scoring misses, but whether this advantage persists as model scale or data volume increases remains to be seen.
