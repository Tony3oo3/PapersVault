## Problem

Spatio-temporal prediction in data-scarce cities is hindered by the lack of a pre-trained model paradigm suited to urban settings, where regions are geographically disjoint, data distributions diverge across cities, and even intra-city patterns vary by region function. Existing fine-grained transfer methods rely on elaborate matching designs (auxiliary similarity data, multi-task learning) rather than a general mechanism for automated knowledge retrieval, leaving a gap between the success of pre-trained models in NLP and their absence in spatio-temporal urban computing.

## Input / Output

The diffusion model consumes a noise-corrupted parameter vector (the flattened, tokenized weights of a spatio-temporal prediction model), a composite region prompt (spatial embeddings from a TuckER-trained urban knowledge graph plus temporal embeddings from a masked autoencoder trained on only 3 days of target data), and a diffusion timestep k. It outputs the full parameter set of a target prediction model (STGCN, GWN, or STID), which is then used directly for multi-step spatio-temporal forecasting. Parameter tokenization is achieved by computing the GCD of element counts across all layers, reshaping each layer into uniform-dimension tokens, and concatenating them in network-adjacency order to preserve structural relationships.

## Method

GPD recasts spatio-temporal few-shot learning as generative pre-training in parameter space: it first trains a separate prediction model for every region in data-rich source cities, then pre-trains a Transformer-based denoising diffusion model (DDPM) to generate those optimized parameter vectors from Gaussian noise conditioned on region prompts. The denoising network treats the tokenized parameter sequence as a token stream and applies self-attention to capture inter-layer and intra-layer interactions, with the prompt injected via one of several conditioning strategies (pre-conditioning, inductive-bias pre-conditioning that routes spatial/temporal prompts to their respective parameter groups, or attention-based adaptive conditioning). The key insight is that the iterative denoising process mirrors the optimization trajectory from random initialization, and that parameter-space knowledge is more transferable than data-space knowledge because it abstracts away city-specific distributional noise. At inference, the pre-trained diffusion model generates tailored parameters for each target-city region in a single forward pass over K denoising steps, requiring only a few days of target data for the temporal prompt.

## Results

GPD achieves the best MAE and RMSE across all four evaluated datasets (Washington D.C., Baltimore, METR-LA, Didi-Chengdu) in the few-shot spatio-temporal prediction setting. Compared to the strongest baseline ST-GFSL, GPD reduces MAE by 4.31% (Washington D.C.), 17.1% (Baltimore), 2.1% (METR-LA), and 8.17% (Didi-Chengdu), yielding an average improvement of 7.87%. The advantage is particularly pronounced for long-horizon predictions: on Baltimore, GPD improves MAE by up to 22.1% at step 6 versus only 5.9% at step 1 over ST-GFSL. Using two source cities consistently outperforms using a single source, with up to 30.1% MAE reduction at step 6 for NYC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPD outperforms all six baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the strongest competitor ST-GFSL (Lu et al., 2022), GPD achieves the largest margin on Baltimore (MAE 6.110 vs. 7.365, a 17.1% reduction) and Didi-Chengdu (MAE 2.366 vs. 2.570, 8.17% reduction), while the gap is smaller on METR-LA (3.119 vs. 3.184, 2.1%). TPB (Liu et al., 2023) is the second-strongest baseline on Didi-Chengdu (MAE 2.654) but is still surpassed by GPD by 11.1%. DASTNet (Tang et al., 2022) and RegionTrans (Wang et al., 2019) are consistently outperformed by 10–20% in MAE across datasets, reflecting the limitations of coarse-grained or similarity-matching transfer. MAML (Finn et al., 2017) performs competitively on METR-LA (MAE 3.257) but lags behind GPD on all other datasets, particularly Baltimore (7.978 vs. 6.110). The gap widens substantially at longer prediction horizons, where GPD's parameter-space generative approach transfers long-term temporal patterns more effectively than fine-tuning or meta-learning baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper recasts spatio-temporal few-shot learning as generative pre-training in parameter space, drawing on two distinct lineages: the DDPM diffusion framework (Ho et al., 2020) for conditional generation, and the hypernetwork paradigm (Ha et al., 2017) for producing network parameters. It departs from prior cross-city transfer methods (RegionTrans, DASTNet, ST-GFSL) that operate in data-space—matching regions, adversarially aligning distributions, or meta-learning initializations—by instead learning a distribution over optimized model parameters and generating new ones conditioned on region prompts. The analogy to LLM pre-training plus prompting is explicit: the diffusion model is the 'pre-trained' component, and city/region prompts enable few-shot adaptation without fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parameter-space generative approach offers a principled way to handle the fundamental heterogeneity across cities that data-space methods struggle with, and its model-agnostic design means it can immediately benefit from future advances in spatio-temporal architectures. However, the framework requires first training a large collection of source-city models (one per region), which is computationally non-trivial, and the 3-hour training time on a single GPU, while acceptable, limits rapid iteration. Open questions include how the approach behaves when source and target cities are extremely dissimilar, whether the GCD-based parameter tokenization generalizes to much deeper or more heterogeneous architectures, and whether the prompt design (KG embeddings + self-supervised temporal embeddings) can be replaced by more expressive conditioning such as LLM-derived city descriptions.
