## Problem

Privacy research on LLMs has focused almost exclusively on memorization—extracting verbatim training data—while ignoring the far broader threat that LLMs can infer personal attributes from arbitrary text presented at inference time. No prior work had formally studied or empirically quantified this inference-based privacy risk, despite LLMs' rapidly growing capabilities and proliferation across consumer platforms.

## Input / Output

The method takes a collection of user-written Reddit comments (up to ~3000 tokens per profile, restricted to 381 attribute-rich subreddits) and feeds them through a fixed adversarial prompt template with a system prompt framing the model as an 'expert investigator.' The model outputs structured top-3 guesses for each of 8 personal attributes (age, education, sex, occupation, relationship status, location, place of birth, income) preceded by step-by-step reasoning. Categorical attributes are constrained to predefined option lists, while free-text attributes (location, occupation) are evaluated via Jaro-Winkler similarity and GPT-4-assisted semantic matching. The fixed prefix/suffix prompt design deliberately excludes vector-database-style encoding to isolate pure inference capability.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a comprehensive empirical evaluation rather than a new architecture. The authors construct the PersonalReddit dataset (520 profiles, 5814 comments, 1184 human-labeled attribute values with certainty and hardness scores) and evaluate 9 state-of-the-art LLMs (GPT-4, GPT-3.5, Llama-2 7B/13B/70B, PaLM-2, Claude-2, Claude-Instant) in a zero-shot setting using a single fixed prompt template. They formalize two threat models: a passive free-text inference adversary (A1) that scrapes existing posts, and an active adversarial chatbot adversary (A2) that steers conversations to elicit private information. Key findings include GPT-4 achieving 85.5% top-1 and 95.2% top-3 accuracy—near human performance—at 100× lower cost and 240× lower time than human labelers. The paper further demonstrates that state-of-the-art text anonymization (Azure Language Service) reduces accuracy by only 7–41% depending on hardness level, and that current model alignment rejects fewer than 11% of privacy-invasive prompts, leaving both major mitigation avenues ineffective.

## Results

On the PersonalReddit dataset (520 profiles, 1066 labels with certainty ≥ 3), GPT-4 achieves 85.5% top-1 and 95.2% top-3 accuracy across 8 personal attributes, approaching human labeler performance. Per-attribute, GPT-4 reaches 97.8% on sex, 92.7% on place of birth, 91.5% on relationship status, 86.2% on location, 78.3% on age, 71.6% on occupation, 67.8% on education, and 62.5% on income. The inference requires only ~27.5 minutes and ~$20 USD for the full dataset, representing a 240× time and 100× cost reduction over human labeling (112 man-hours, ~$2250). On the PAN 2018 gender classification task, GPT-4 achieves 90.2% accuracy in zero-shot, surpassing the best prior specialized model (82.21%) by 8 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On PAN 2018 (gender identification from 100 tweets per profile), GPT-4 in zero-shot achieves 90.2% accuracy, outperforming the best PAN 2018 competition entry (a specialized model trained on 3000 profiles) by 8 percentage points (90.2% vs. 82.21%), with all three top competition entries within 1.2% of each other. On the ACSIncome tabular benchmark, GPT-4 in zero-shot nearly matches finetuned XGBoost classifiers on place-of-birth, income, and gender, despite not being trained on the ~100k data points. Against human labelers on PersonalReddit, GPT-4's 95.2% top-3 accuracy nearly matches human ground truth, and the model correctly infers 51 labels that humans could only determine using subreddit metadata (which was withheld from the model). After applying Azure Language Service anonymization, GPT-4's location accuracy drops from 86.2% to only ~55%, and the accuracy decrease shrinks from 41.1% at hardness 1 to just 7.7% at hardness 5, showing that current anonymization is largely ineffective for subtler inferences. Across model providers, alignment-based refusals are minimal: Meta Llama-2 and OpenAI GPT refuse 0% of privacy-invasive prompts, Anthropic Claude 2.8%, and Google PaLM 2 only 10.7% (partly due to unrelated safety triggers).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant LLM privacy literature focused on training data memorization (Carlini et al., 2021; 2023) by formalizing a distinct threat: inference-based privacy violations where pretrained LLMs extract personal attributes from text presented at inference time. It extends the long-standing NLP task of author profiling (PAN competitions, Rangel et al., 2013–2018) from 1–3 attributes on Twitter using specialized feature extractors to 8 attributes on Reddit using zero-shot LLM prompting, while reframing the task through a privacy-threat lens. The adversarial chatbot threat model is entirely new, with no prior formalization of active LLM-driven information extraction from users.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that state-of-the-art anonymization tools reduce location accuracy only from ~86% to ~55% (and barely at all for harder, inference-based samples) challenges the assumption that removing explicit PII is sufficient protection against LLM inference. The 100× cost and 240× time reduction versus human labelers makes large-scale profiling economically feasible for the first time, with direct implications for GDPR compliance and platform design. Open questions include whether stronger alignment specifically targeting privacy-invasive prompts can close the gap, whether anonymization methods leveraging LLMs themselves can keep pace, and how the adversarial chatbot threat scales to real (non-simulated) deployments.
