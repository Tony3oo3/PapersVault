## Problem

Existing federated learning incentive mechanisms distribute post-training monetary rewards, which suffer from timeliness (clients may exit mid-training) and feasibility (monetizing contributions is difficult) challenges. Few works have explored training-time model rewards, and those that do (CGSV, Rank) use heuristic approaches lacking theoretical convergence analyses or impractical assumptions like access to a global validation set. The paper asks: how should each client be rewarded locally at each FL iteration to achieve a global incentivization objective while still guaranteeing convergence to the optimal model?

## Input / Output

The method takes N clients' local datasets (heterogeneously partitioned via Dirichlet label skew, quantity skew, feature noise, or IID), per-client contribution measures p_{i,t} that can vary across FL iterations (e.g., participation rate, dataset size, or custom domain-specific metrics), and initial model parameters. It produces differentiated client models where each client's model is updated using a proportion of other clients' local model updates commensurate with their contribution, plus a shared reference model. The contribution measure is fully agnostic and plug-and-play, requiring no modification to the algorithm for different valuation schemes.

## Method

IAFL modifies the standard FedAvg aggregation step so that each client i receives a model update Δθ_{i,t} computed by averaging local updates from a randomly sampled subset S_{i,t} of size ⌈γ_{i,t}(N−1)⌉ other clients (plus their own update), where the reward rate γ_{i,t} = min{(p_{i,t}/p_ceil)^{1−κ}, 1} scales with the client's contribution. The sharing coefficient κ ∈ [0,1] controls the equity-incentive trade-off: larger κ makes rewards more uniform across clients. To address the theoretically identified error propagation problem (where low-quality updates from low contributors degrade all client models, preventing convergence), IAFL introduces a stochastic reference-model recovery: at each iteration, with probability q, a client's model is reset to a reference model θ_ref trained with a user-defined reward rate γ'_ref. The paper proves that this local proportional-aggregation design ensures higher-contributing clients receive better performance guarantees (Theorem 1), and that the stochastic recovery guarantees asymptotic convergence of all client models to the global optimum (Theorem 2) provided contributions do not diminish too quickly.

## Results

IAFL achieves the highest Incentivized Participation Rate (IPR_accu) in 24 out of 29 experimental settings, with 17 settings reaching a perfect 100% IPR_accu, meaning every client receives a model at least as good as their standalone model. On the Pearson correlation metric (measuring how well model rewards align with contributions), IAFL performs best in 15 out of 29 settings, achieving correlations of 0.39–0.72 under feature skew and homogeneous partition where all other baselines drop to near-zero or negative values. In the partial-participation experiment (Table 3), IAFL achieves ρ=0.94, IPR_accu=1.00, and highest client accuracy of 53%, substantially outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IAFL decisively outperforms FedAvg Finetune under label distribution skew, the most challenging regime: on CIFAR-10 #C=3, IAFL achieves 46% IPR_accu versus 0% for FedAvg Finetune, and on CIFAR-100 Dir(0.5), IAFL reaches 100% versus 37%. Against LG-FedAvg, IAFL wins on IPR_accu in most label-skew settings (e.g., CIFAR-10 #C=3: 46% vs 1%; CIFAR-100 #C=30: 64% vs 14%), though LG-FedAvg edges ahead on Pearson correlation under quantity skew (e.g., CIFAR-10: 0.95 vs 0.80). CGSV and Rank both suffer from negative or near-zero Pearson correlations under feature skew and homogeneous partition (e.g., CIFAR-100 feature skew: CGSV −0.05, Rank 0.20, IAFL 0.72), while IAFL maintains strong positive correlations (0.39–0.72) across all heterogeneity types. In the partial-participation experiment (Table 3), IAFL achieves ρ=0.94 and IPR_accu=1.00, compared to LG-FedAvg (ρ=0.90, IPR=0.00) and CGSV/Rank (ρ=−0.74, IPR=0.94), and delivers the highest client accuracy of 53% versus 31% for CGSV/Rank and 7% for LG-FedAvg.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FedAvg framework by replacing the uniform global model broadcast with contribution-proportional partial aggregation, drawing on game-theoretic mechanism design (Stackelberg games, contract theory) that previously relied on post-training monetary rewards. It departs from the closest prior work on training-time model incentives—CGSV (Xu et al., 2021) and Rank (Kong et al., 2022)—by providing the first theoretical convergence analysis for such rewards and identifying the error propagation phenomenon that their heuristic approaches overlook. The stochastic reference-model recovery strategy is a novel addition that resolves the tension between differentiated rewards and asymptotic optimality, a problem not addressed in any prior FL incentive mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in FL: post-training monetary incentives suffer from timeliness and feasibility issues, and training-time model rewards had previously lacked theoretical grounding. The error propagation finding—that low-contributing clients' poor updates degrade even high-contributing clients' models, preventing convergence—reveals a fundamental tension between incentivization and optimality that the field had not formally characterized. Open questions include how to jointly determine contribution measures and reward strategies between server and clients, how to handle diminishing returns in model performance as training progresses, and the ethical trade-offs between collaborative fairness (rewarding contribution) and equitable fairness (equal access to the best model).
