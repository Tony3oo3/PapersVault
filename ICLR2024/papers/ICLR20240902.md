## Problem

Existing generative models for protein backbone design on SE(3)^N, such as FrameDiff and RFDiffusion, rely on diffusion-based training that requires expensive SDE simulation or Riemannian divergence computation, and are restricted to starting from a fixed random prior. This limits both training efficiency and the ability to condition on informative source distributions (e.g., predicted structures for equilibrium conformation generation). The paper asks whether simulation-free flow matching can be made to work natively on the SE(3)^N group, supporting both deterministic and stochastic dynamics with arbitrary source-to-target mappings.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a protein backbone parameterized as N frames in SE(3) (each frame a 3×3 rotation matrix and 3D translation vector), centered by subtracting the global center of mass to ensure translation invariance. At inference, it starts from samples of a source distribution on SE(3)^N (uniform Haar for de novo generation, or predicted structures from OmegaFold/ESMFold for conformation generation) and integrates a learned ODE or SDE backward in time to produce N×4 heavy-atom coordinates (N, Cα, C, O) in Angstroms. The velocity network is parameterized to predict the starting point x̂₀ rather than the velocity directly, enabling auxiliary atomic-level losses (backbone regression and local pairwise distances) to be applied on the predicted structure.

## Method

FOLDFLOW introduces three progressively more powerful conditional flow matching models on SE(3)^N. FOLDFLOW-BASE derives a closed-form conditional vector field on SO(3) by exploiting the bi-invariant metric: instead of computing the expensive matrix logarithm, it converts the target rotation to axis-angle form, parallel-transports it to the source tangent space, and divides by t to obtain the geodesic velocity—eliminating autograd overhead. FOLDFLOW-OT replaces the independent coupling with a Riemannian optimal transport plan (computed via minibatch OT), constructing shorter McCann interpolant paths; the paper proves the existence and uniqueness of the Monge map on SE(3)^N under standard regularity assumptions. FOLDFLOW-SFM extends this to stochastic dynamics by modeling a guided Brownian bridge on SO(3) (Doob's h-transform), but replaces the intractable exact bridge density with a simulation-free isotropic Gaussian on SO(3) centered at the geodesic midpoint, yielding a training objective whose gradient provably equals the intractable marginal loss. All three models use the AlphaFold2 structure module (invariant point attention) as the velocity network and are trained with a combined flow-matching loss on SO(3) and R³ plus auxiliary atomic-level losses, with an inference-time velocity annealing trick (scaling by ct) that substantially improves designability.

## Results

On protein backbone generation (up to 300 residues, evaluated via ProteinMPNN + ESMFold self-consistency), FOLDFLOW-OT achieves the highest designability among non-pretrained models at 82.0% fraction (scRMSD 1.81 Å), substantially outperforming FrameDiff-Retrained (61.2%, 2.99 Å) and FrameDiff-Improved (55.5%, 2.93 Å). FOLDFLOW-SFM achieves the best novelty with 54.4% of designable proteins having TM-score < 0.5 to the training set and the lowest average max TM-score (0.411), surpassing even RFDiffusion (0.708 fraction but with a much larger training set). All FOLDFLOW variants run at ~2.67 iters/sec during inference, roughly 2× faster than FrameDiff-Retrained (1.28 iters/sec).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FrameDiff (the closest non-pretrained baseline), all three FOLDFLOW variants win on designability: FOLDFLOW-OT reaches 82.0% vs. FrameDiff-Retrained's 61.2% and FrameDiff-Improved's 55.5%, a gap of ~21 and ~27 percentage points respectively. On novelty, FOLDFLOW-SFM (54.4% fraction, 0.411 avg max TM) decisively beats both FrameDiff-Improved (29.6%, 0.457) and FrameDiff-Retrained (10.8%, 0.684), and even surpasses RFDiffusion (70.8%, 0.449) on the avg max TM-score metric despite using a ~9× smaller training set. FOLDFLOW-OT achieves the best scRMSD (1.81 Å) among all non-pretrained methods, roughly 1.1 Å lower than FrameDiff-Retrained (2.99 Å). Against RFDiffusion, FOLDFLOW-OT narrows the designability gap to 0.149 (vs. 0.357 for FrameDiff-Retrained) while using only 10 GPU days versus RFDiffusion's 1800 GPU days and 17M vs. 60M parameters. On the BPTI equilibrium conformation task, FOLDFLOW with an informed prior achieves W₂ = 4.379 (all angles) and 0.406 (residue 56), outperforming both FOLDFLOW with a random prior (4.446 / 0.557) and FrameDiff (4.844 / 0.800), demonstrating the advantage of starting from a sampleable source distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

FOLDFLOW extends Riemannian flow matching (Chen & Lipman, 2023) to the SE(3)^N setting by deriving a closed-form conditional vector field on SO(3) that avoids expensive matrix logarithm computation, and adapts the OT-CFM coupling strategy (Tong et al., 2023b) to the Riemannian setting via a proven Monge map on SE(3)_0^N. It departs from the dominant SE(3) diffusion paradigm (FrameDiff, RFDiffusion) by using simulation-free flow matching, which enables starting from any invariant source distribution rather than being restricted to a fixed noise prior. The stochastic component draws on guided diffusion bridges on Lie groups (Jensen et al., 2022) but introduces a simulation-free isotropic Gaussian approximation on SO(3) that closely matches the true Brownian bridge, and the architecture follows the AF2 structure module used by FrameDiff.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FOLDFLOW provides a practical and theoretically grounded alternative to diffusion-based protein backbone generation, offering faster and more stable training while enabling applications like equilibrium conformation generation from informed priors—something diffusion models cannot easily do. The finding that stochasticity (FOLDFLOW-SFM) improves novelty at the cost of designability, while OT coupling (FOLDFLOW-OT) maximizes designability, gives practitioners a clear knob to tune for their specific needs. Open questions include extending to conditional generation (sequence- or structure-guided), scaling beyond 300 residues, and whether the simulation-free SO(3) bridge approximation degrades at higher diffusion coefficients or longer sequences.
