## Problem

Existing self-supervised learning methods enforce consistency at the image level, which causes entanglement of object representations in scene-centric datasets and creates a mismatch with dense nearest-neighbor retrieval at test time. Even object-centric datasets underperform because global bootstrapping cannot capture fine-grained object-level structure. CrIBo addresses this by being the first end-to-end online SSL method that explicitly enforces cross-image consistency at the object/object-part level, compatible with both scene-centric and object-centric data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairs of augmented RGB views (512×512 crops) from scene-centric (COCO, ~118k images) or object-centric (ImageNet-1k, ~1.3M images) datasets, processed by a ViT-S/16 or ViT-B/16 backbone. The primary output is a dense spatial feature map (H×W×d) from the ViT, which at test time supports k-NN patch classification for segmentation, linear probing for pixel-wise masks, or finetuning with Segmenter/YOLO-S for segmentation and detection. Object-level representations are intermediate products obtained by aggregating local tokens via Sinkhorn-based joint-space clustering into K semantically coherent groups per view.

## Method

CrIBo operates a three-step pipeline within a DINO-style student-teacher self-distillation framework. First, a Sinkhorn-Knopp joint-space clustering algorithm partitions the concatenated dense representations of two augmented views into K clusters, yielding object representations with free cross-view correspondence (the k-th cluster in view 1 matches the k-th in view 2). Second, a pair of memory banks stores object representations from previous iterations, and nearest-neighbor retrieval identifies cross-image object matches; a cycle-consistency criterion (query→NN in memory→jump to other view→NN in batch→jump back) filters out semantically invalid pairs that arise from low-level feature matching early in training. Third, three self-distillation losses are combined: a global cross-view loss (stabilizing early training), a cross-view object-level loss (aligning object representations across augmented views), and a cross-image object-level loss (aligning each object with its retrieved cross-image NN). The method benefits from an overclustering regime (K=32–64, far exceeding the number of objects per image), which isolates object parts and expands the pool of valid cross-image matches.

## Results

On the primary dense nearest neighbor retrieval task, CrIBo (ViT-S/16, COCO, 300 epochs) achieves 23.4 mIoU on ADE20K and 58.1 mIoU on Pascal VOC at full data (1/1), outperforming CrOC (18.0/47.1), DINO (13.0/31.9), and MAE (6.8/15.9). On object-centric pretraining (IN1K, ViT-S/16, 800 epochs), CrIBo reaches 28.3/73.2 mIoU, surpassing CrOC (20.6/64.2), TimeT (24.6/62.7), and DINO (18.4/46.1). At the ViT-B/16 scale (IN1K, 400 epochs), CrIBo achieves 30.0/74.9 mIoU, beating Hummingbird (28.3/70.5) and DINO (22.0/54.9). In linear segmentation (Table 2), CrIBo (ViT-B/16, IN1K) reaches 34.7 mIoU on ADE20K and 74.9 on Pascal VOC, and in end-to-end finetuning (Table 3) it attains 46.0/83.4 mIoU on the same datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CrIBo consistently outperforms all baselines across both scene-centric and object-centric pretraining settings. Against its closest competitor CrOC (same research group, cross-view clustering without cross-image bootstrapping), CrIBo gains +5.4 mIoU on ADE20K and +11.0 on Pascal VOC (COCO, ViT-S/16), and +7.7/+9.0 (IN1K, ViT-S/16). Against DINO, the gap is even larger: +10.4/+26.2 mIoU on ADE20K/VOC (COCO) and +9.9/+27.1 (IN1K, ViT-S/16). MAE is far behind at all data ratios, confirming that masked reconstruction is poorly aligned with dense NN retrieval. Against Hummingbird (the only method using cross-image attention for in-context learning), CrIBo wins on ADE20K (30.0 vs 28.3) and Pascal VOC (74.9 vs 70.5) at ViT-B/16, though Hummingbird uses only 300 epochs vs CrIBo's 400. Against TimeT (which uses video data), CrIBo leads by +3.7/+10.5 mIoU on ADE20K/VOC (IN1K, ViT-S/16). The advantage is most pronounced in the scene-centric regime, where CrIBo's object-level cross-image bootstrapping directly addresses the entanglement problem that plagues global-level methods on multi-object images.

## Relation to Prior Work

CrIBo builds directly on the DINO self-distillation framework (Caron et al., 2021) for its student-teacher training mechanism and on CrOC's (Stegmüller et al., 2023) joint-space Sinkhorn clustering for identifying semantically coherent object regions. It departs from prior cross-image bootstrapping methods (NNCLR, ORL) that operate exclusively at the image level, and from prior object-level SSL methods (CrOC, SlotCon, ORL) that enforce only cross-view consistency, by introducing the first end-to-end online approach that enforces cross-image consistency at the object/object-part level. The cycle-consistent matching criterion is a novel filtering mechanism that addresses the cold-start problem of nearest-neighbor retrieval when the encoder is randomly initialized, extending the adaptive bootstrapping idea from Lebailly et al. (2023) to the object level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CrIBo addresses a concrete gap: prior cross-image SSL methods assume object-centric datasets and suffer from object entanglement on scene-centric images, which constitute the bulk of web-scale data. By operating at the object level with cross-image consistency, the method aligns pretraining with the in-context NN retrieval evaluation paradigm while remaining compatible with scene-centric pretraining. Limitations include restriction to ViT backbones, evaluation on only COCO and ImageNet-1k, and no exploration of larger models (ViT-L/H) or larger datasets (YFCC100M, Conceptual 12M), leaving open whether the object-level cross-image paradigm scales. The finding that unsupervised overclustering outperforms ground-truth COCO masks for the clustering step suggests the method benefits from finer granularity than available annotations, pointing toward potential improvements with richer supervision or larger K.
