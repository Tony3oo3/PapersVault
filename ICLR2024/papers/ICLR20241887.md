## Problem

Continual Test-Time Adaptation (CTTA) requires a pre-trained model to adapt to a sequence of shifting target domains without source data, but existing model-based methods suffer from error accumulation due to noisy pseudo labels under dynamic distributions, while prompt-based methods lack sufficient capacity to retain long-term domain-shared knowledge, leading to catastrophic forgetting. The paper identifies that no prior approach explicitly separates and manages domain-specific versus domain-shared knowledge in a single adaptation framework.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled target-domain RGB images sequentially (each image seen exactly once, no source data access), with resolution constraints of 224×224 for ViT-based classification and 960×540 for segmentation. A teacher-student pair processes the same image: the student sees the original while the teacher sees an augmented version (random scale factors from [0.5, 2.0]). Outputs are class labels for classification CTTA or pixel-level segmentation masks for dense prediction, with online evaluation after each input.

## Method

ViDA injects two parallel bottleneck branches into linear/conv layers of a frozen pre-trained model: a low-rank branch (middle dimension d_l=1) that learns domain-shared, task-relevant features resistant to distribution shift, and a high-rank branch (middle dimension d_h=128) that captures domain-specific knowledge fitting the current distribution. A Homeostatic Knowledge Allotment (HKA) strategy uses MC Dropout uncertainty on each sample to dynamically set fusion scale factors λ_h and λ_l, increasing domain-specific weight when uncertainty is high and domain-shared weight when low. Training uses a teacher-student consistency loss with EMA (α=0.999) on pseudo labels from the teacher, updating only the ViDA parameters. At inference, the linear ViDA branches are re-parameterized into the base model, adding zero extra parameters while preserving model plasticity.

## Results

On ImageNet-to-ImageNet-C CTTA with ViT-base, ViDA achieves a mean error of 43.4%, outperforming the source model (55.8%) by 12.4% and the previous SOTA VDP (50.8%) by 7.4%. On CIFAR-10-to-CIFAR-10C, the method reduces mean error to 20.7% (vs. 28.2% source), and on CIFAR-100-to-CIFAR-100C to 27.3% (vs. 35.4% source). For segmentation CTTA on Cityscapes-to-ACDC, ViDA achieves 61.9% mean mIoU over 3 rounds, surpassing CoTTA (58.6%) by 3.3% and showing continual improvement across repeated domain cycles (61.1→62.2→62.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViDA outperforms all 10 baselines across every evaluated benchmark. On ImageNet-C (ViT-base), it beats VDP (50.8%) by 7.4%, TENT (51.0%) by 7.6%, and CoTTA (54.8%) by 11.4% in mean error; Pseudo-label actually degrades below the source model (61.2% vs. 55.8%). On CIFAR-10C, ViDA (20.7%) surpasses TENT (23.5%), CoTTA (24.6%), and VDP (24.1%) by 2.8–3.9%. On CIFAR-100C, the gap widens: ViDA (27.3%) beats TENT (32.1%) by 4.8% and CoTTA (34.8%) by 7.5%. For segmentation on ACDC, ViDA (61.9% mIoU) exceeds CoTTA (58.6%), VDP (58.2%), and DePT (49.7%), while TENT and DePT show progressive degradation over repeated rounds. On the WideResNet-28 CIFAR-10C benchmark, ViDA (15.8%) edges out SATA (16.1%), CoTTA (16.2%), and EcoTTA (16.8%). For foundation models, ViDA improves DINOv2 by 4.8% and SAM by 5.2% on CIFAR-10C, where CoTTA actually degrades both (−4.3% and −0.1% respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the CTTA framework established by CoTTA (Wang et al., 2022), adopting its teacher-student architecture with EMA-updated pseudo labels, and on the re-parameterization technique from RepVGG (Ding et al., 2021) to ensure zero inference overhead. It departs from prior model-based CTTA methods (CoTTA, EcoTTA) that rely on noisy pseudo labels for full parameter updates, and from prompt-based methods (VDP, DePT) that use limited trainable parameters, by introducing a dual-adapter architecture where high-rank and low-rank bottleneck branches explicitly separate domain-specific and domain-shared knowledge. The Homeostatic Knowledge Allotment strategy, which uses MC Dropout uncertainty to dynamically weight the two branches, represents a novel mechanism not present in prior CTTA or PEFT literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, parameter-efficient solution to two persistent CTTA problems (error accumulation and catastrophic forgetting) that works across ViT, CNN, and foundation model backbones without increasing inference cost. The demonstration that the method improves domain generalization on unseen domains (7.6% reduction) suggests the low-rank branch captures genuinely transferable representations, opening a path toward continual adaptation that also benefits zero-shot generalization. Limitations include the fixed uncertainty threshold (Θ=0.2), the additional MC Dropout forward passes during adaptation, and reliance on pseudo labels from the teacher that remain potentially noisy under extreme distribution shifts. Open questions include whether the rank dimensions could be learned adaptively per domain and how the approach scales to truly open-ended continual scenarios with unbounded domain sequences.
