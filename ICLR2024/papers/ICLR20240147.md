## Problem

Learning language-conditioned policies for complex sequential tasks is inefficient when environments vary in object count, initial configurations, and planning horizon. Prior work either learns hierarchical policies that cannot plan at test time, or assumes access to symbolic state predicates. This paper addresses the gap of automatically discovering a symbolic abstract action space from language AND inducing a latent state abstraction (without manual predicate annotation) that supports planning-compatible transition and feasibility prediction.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes paired demonstration trajectories (segmented object point clouds of 512 points each, or 13×13 grid factorized observations) aligned with natural language instructions parsed by GPT-4 into symbolic tuples like (pick-up, red, bowl, sink). At test time, given a single language goal (e.g., 'place the blue mug in the sink') and the current raw state, the system outputs a sequence of abstract actions (the high-level plan) which is then refined step-by-step into primitive actions (e.g., pick object X, place on surface Y) by per-action low-level policies. The feasibility model outputs a binary score per abstract action at each latent state, and the transition model predicts the next latent state as a sequence of per-object embeddings.

## Method

PARL operates in three stages. First, GPT-4 parses all training instructions into a compositional symbolic action space of verb schemas and object-level concepts (colors, categories, locations). Second, an object-centric architecture is trained end-to-end: a Point Cloud Transformer (PCT) encodes each segmented object into a 352-d latent vector, and a 12-layer Transformer (384 hidden dim, 12 heads) takes the concatenated sequence of object embeddings, learnable action-token embeddings, and a feasibility query token to jointly predict the next abstract state (via object-position outputs) and a binary feasibility label (via the query-position output fed to an MLP). Training uses L2 loss on predicted vs. encoded states plus binary cross-entropy on feasibility, with failure trajectories providing negative examples. Third, at test time, a breadth-first search (with beam pruning by sequence feasibility) enumerates abstract action sequences in the discrete action space, using the learned transition and feasibility models to score candidate plans, and executes the best plan in closed-loop with replanning after each low-level action.

## Results

On BabyAI, PARL (high only, with oracle low-level controller) achieves 87–94% success rate across longer-horizon tasks (Key-Door, All-Objects, Two-Corridors) and 91% on novel concept combinations, while all end-to-end baselines (BC and A2C) score 0%. With learned low-level policies (high+low), PARL still achieves 39–52% success, far exceeding the A2C baselines (0–44%). On Kitchen-Worlds, PARL outperforms Goal-Conditioned BC on the geometrically challenging Sink task (61% vs. 39%) and on novel concept combinations (57% vs. 14%), while matching or slightly exceeding it on All-Objects novel environments (86% vs. 82%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On BabyAI, all end-to-end baselines (BC and A2C) completely fail (0% success) on the longer-horizon and novel-combination tasks, as the low-level action space is too large and the planning horizon too long for direct policy learning. The High-Level A2C baseline (which predicts abstract actions but uses an oracle controller) achieves 27–44% on longer steps but drops to 0% on novel concept combinations, whereas PARL (high only) maintains 87–94% across all settings, demonstrating that learning a planning-compatible transition and feasibility model is critical versus learning a high-level policy. On Kitchen-Worlds, PARL outperforms Goal-Conditioned BC by 22 percentage points on the Sink task (61% vs. 39%), where geometric reasoning about object placement feasibility is required, and by 43 points on novel concept combinations (57% vs. 14%), highlighting the advantage of the object-centric factorized representation over a scene-level autoregressive decoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

PARL sits at the intersection of language-guided hierarchical RL (Andreas & Klein 2015; Corona et al. 2021), latent transition model learning for planning (Chiappa et al. 2017; Schrittwieser et al. 2020), and neuro-symbolic planning (Konidaris et al. 2018; Silver et al. 2021). It departs from prior latent-dynamics work by learning the transition model at a discovered abstract action level rather than the primitive action level, and from neuro-symbolic planners by using a latent (not explicitly symbolic) state abstraction that can capture geometric constraints. The use of a pretrained LLM (GPT-4) to decompose instructions into compositional verb-object symbols is a practical repurposing of LLMs for structure discovery, building on the idea that language provides a natural factorization of the action space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that a planning-compatible latent transition model with learned feasibility can generalize to longer horizons and novel concept compositions where end-to-end policies completely fail, suggesting that explicit planning structure is essential for compositional generalization in sequential decision-making. A key limitation is the reliance on pre-segmented trajectories aligned with language instructions, which restricts applicability to settings where such alignment is available. Future directions include automatic trajectory segmentation and integration with vision-language foundation models for open-vocabulary object recognition, which would extend the approach beyond the closed concept sets used here.
