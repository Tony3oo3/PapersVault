## Problem

Existing neural methods for individualized treatment effect estimation either operate in discrete time (requiring regular sampling) or, in the continuous-time setting (TE-CDE), provide only point estimates without uncertainty quantification. This is a critical gap for medical decision-making, where physicians need credible intervals to assess whether a treatment will improve a patient's trajectory with sufficient probability. No tailored neural method existed that simultaneously handles irregularly sampled continuous-time trajectories and provides rigorous Bayesian uncertainty estimates.

## Input / Output

The method consumes patient trajectories recorded at arbitrary, non-regular timestamps (outcomes Y_t, covariates X_t, and binary treatment indicators A_t) along with an arbitrary future sequence of treatments a'_τ over a prediction window Δ. Rather than outputting a single point estimate, it produces the full posterior predictive distribution of the potential outcome at time t̄+Δ, from which equal-tailed credible intervals (e.g., 95%, 99%) can be extracted. The distribution decomposes into model uncertainty (variance across neural SDE weight trajectories) and outcome uncertainty (likelihood variance Σ from the prediction head), both of which are reported separately.

## Method

BNCDE is an encoder-decoder architecture where both the encoder and decoder consist of a neural CDE coupled with a latent neural SDE. The encoder's neural CDE is driven by the patient history [Y_t, X_t, A_t] (interpolated via cubic Hermite splines), while the decoder's neural CDE is driven by the future treatment sequence a'_τ. The key architectural insight is that the neural SDEs do not model the hidden states but instead parameterize the posterior distribution of the neural CDE weights, with the SDE solutions serving as stochastic weight processes. Training maximizes the ELBO with independent Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process priors, where the KL divergence between the variational SDE and the prior SDE is computed analytically on path space (exploiting the shared constant diffusion coefficient σ), making variational inference tractable. The coupled system of CDEs and SDEs is solved end-to-end via a numerical SDE solver, and the prediction head maps the final hidden state to a Gaussian likelihood (μ, Σ) for the potential outcome.

## Results

The paper evaluates BNCDE against TE-CDE with MC dropout on a simulated lung cancer tumor growth model (Geng et al., 2017) with irregularly sampled observations, across prediction windows Δ=1, 2, 3 days. BNCDE produces faithful 95% and 99% posterior predictive credible intervals (empirical coverage at or above the nominal level), while TE-CDE's intervals are systematically overconfident (coverage below nominal). BNCDE also yields significantly sharper (narrower) credible intervals, lower MSE in point estimates, and more informative model uncertainty (MSE decreases more systematically with deferral rate). No specific numerical values are reported in the text; all quantitative comparisons are presented in Figures 2–5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is TE-CDE (Seedat et al., 2022) augmented with MC dropout for model uncertainty, as no other method provides uncertainty quantification in continuous time. BNCDE wins on all four evaluation axes: (1) Faithfulness — BNCDE's credible intervals achieve empirical coverage at or above the nominal (1−α) level, whereas TE-CDE's intervals are overconfident, covering fewer outcomes than the nominal level guarantees, a gap that widens for longer prediction windows (Δ=3). (2) Sharpness — BNCDE's median CrI widths are significantly narrower than TE-CDE's across all quantiles and prediction windows. (3) Point-estimate accuracy — BNCDE achieves lower MSE than TE-CDE, and this advantage is robust to increasing noise in the data-generating process. (4) Model-uncertainty informativeness — in a deferral-rate analysis, withholding samples with the highest MC variance from BNCDE systematically reduces MSE, while TE-CDE's MC-dropout variance is nearly uninformative (MSE decreases almost randomly). The paper also confirms BNCDE's superiority under informative sampling (γ=2) using inverse intensity weighting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper couples two established neural differential equation families—neural CDEs (Kidger et al., 2020) for processing irregularly sampled patient trajectories and neural SDEs (Li et al., 2020) for variational Bayesian inference—into a single architecture for treatment effect estimation. It directly extends TE-CDE (Seedat et al., 2022), the only prior neural method for continuous-time treatment effect estimation, by replacing point-estimate weight optimization with latent neural SDEs that parameterize the full posterior over CDE weights, and by adding an aleatoric likelihood head. The resulting BNCDE is, to the authors' knowledge, the first Bayesian neural CDE, bridging the neural ODE/CDE uncertainty-quantification literature (which had only MCMC, Laplace, or Gaussian-variational approaches) with the time-varying causal inference literature (which had relied on ad-hoc MC dropout).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Uncertainty-aware treatment effect estimation in continuous time is a practical necessity for clinical decision-making, where overconfident point estimates can lead to harmful treatment choices; BNCDE is the first tailored neural method to deliver both faithful credible intervals and sharp interval widths in this setting. Key limitations remain: the method is a black box without explainability, does not address confounding bias (a general open problem in time-varying causal inference), and the computational cost of solving high-dimensional SDEs during training is a bottleneck. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very high-dimensional CDEs, how to integrate confounding adjustments (e.g., inverse propensity weighting) without inflating variance, and whether the OU-process prior is optimal or whether richer prior families would improve calibration.
