## Problem

Existing simplicial neural networks (SNNs) perform sequential feature aggregation across all layers during training, incurring memory and time costs that scale quadratically with the number of simplices, making them infeasible for large simplicial complexes. This paper asks whether one can retain the full expressive power of message-passing SNNs (equivalent to the simplicial Weisfeiler-Lehman test) while achieving constant training-time and memory independent of complex size.

## Input / Output

The method takes a K-simplicial complex specified by oriented or unoriented incidence matrices B_k and initial feature matrices X_k ∈ R^{N_k×D_k} for each simplex order k = 0,…,K. It produces per-simplex embeddings H_k[σ_k] that are then fed into task-specific heads for trajectory prediction (next-node in oriented edge sequences), simplicial closure prediction (probability that an open simplex closes), or graph classification (class label from a histogram of node embeddings). A key structural detail is that the aggregation step is entirely parameter-free and executed once before training, so the only learned components are MLPs operating on the precomputed features.

## Method

SaNN decouples structural aggregation from learning: it recursively precomputes t-hop simplicial-aware features by aggregating initial simplex features from upper, lower, boundary, and co-boundary neighborhoods using injective, parameter-free functions (summation via sparse incidence matrices, followed by concatenation as the read-out), then applies learnable MLPs g_k^(t) to transform each hop's features and concatenates across hops via Θ_k to form the final embedding. The theoretical contribution proves that when all aggregation, combination, and transformation functions are injective, SaNN is strictly more powerful than the WL graph isomorphism test and as powerful as the SWL test (equivalently, MPSN), despite never applying nonlinear transformations during the aggregation loop. This yields a training-time complexity of O(T(3N_k D_k² + N_k D_{k-1}² + N_k D_{k+1}²)) versus O(T(2N_k²D_k + N_k N_{k-1}D_{k-1} + N_k N_{k+1}D_{k+1} + …)) for sequential SNNs, making the forward pass nearly constant in the number of simplices. The model is further shown to be permutation equivariant, orientation equivariant (with odd activations), and simplicial-aware of the highest order K.

## Results

On simplicial closure prediction (relative AUC-PR), SaNN achieves 15.45 on email-Enron (vs. MPSN 14.51, SCNN 14.17), 30.22 on contact-high-school (vs. MPSN 30.83), and 32.89 on primary-school (vs. MPSN 33.05), while being the only method that completes on the larger NDC-Classes (2.79) and Math-SX (6.88) datasets where MPSN and SCNN run out of memory. On trajectory prediction, SaNN achieves the best accuracy on Ocean (35.0% vs. SCoNe 30.0%, SCNN 28.5%) and is competitive on Synthetic, Planar, and Mesh. On graph classification, SaNN matches or exceeds MPSN on Proteins (77.6% vs. 76.5%) and Reddit-B (91.7% vs. 92.2%), while all methods show significant statistical overlap. SaNN's per-epoch training time is orders of magnitude lower (e.g., 3 s vs. 255 s for MPSN on Enron).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest SNN baseline MPSN (Bodnar et al., 2021), SaNN wins on email-Enron simplicial closure (15.45 vs. 14.51 relative AUC-PR) and is within 0.6–1.6 points on high-school and primary-school, while MPSN runs out of memory on NDC-Classes and Math-SX. SCNN (Yang et al., 2022a) is consistently weaker than both SaNN and MPSN on simplicial closure (e.g., 14.17 vs. 15.45 on Enron) and also OOMs on the two largest datasets. For trajectory prediction, SaNN outperforms SCoNe (Roddenberry et al., 2021) on Ocean (35.0% vs. 30.0%) and is within 0.5–0.7% on the other three datasets, while the non-deep Projection baseline is far behind (27.15% on Ocean). In graph classification, SaNN is competitive with MPSN (e.g., 77.6% vs. 76.5% on Proteins, 91.7% vs. 92.2% on Reddit-B) and clearly outperforms GNN baselines GIN, GraphSage, and DGCNN on most datasets, though it trails MPSN on NCI1 (74.9% vs. 82.8%). The scalable GNN SPIN (Doshi & Chepuri, 2022) is outperformed by SaNN on Proteins (77.6% vs. 75.6%) and Reddit-B (91.7% vs. 88.4%), confirming that the simplicial-aware pre-aggregation provides a genuine expressiveness advantage over pairwise-only graph models.

## Relation to Prior Work

SaNN draws on two distinct lineages: (1) simplicial neural networks (MPSN, SCoNe, SCNN, S2CNN) that perform sequential, parameterized aggregation over upper, lower, boundary, and co-boundary simplices during training, and (2) scalable GNN simplifications (GAMLPs, SPIN, SIGN, SGCN) that precompute neighborhood features before training. The key departure is extending the pre-aggregation philosophy from pairwise graph neighborhoods to all four types of simplicial adjacency, while proving that this simpler architecture retains SWL-level expressiveness under injectivity conditions—a non-trivial result since the pre-aggregated form does not mirror the sequential WL/SWL update structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: existing SNNs scale quadratically with the number of simplices and run out of memory on datasets with ~1 million simplices, whereas SaNN achieves near-constant training time, making higher-order representation learning feasible at scale. The theoretical result that no learnable parameters are needed in the aggregation step to match SWL expressiveness challenges the assumption that sequential nonlinear message passing is essential for higher-order expressiveness. Open questions include whether the precomputation cost (which still depends on complex size) becomes a bottleneck for extremely large or dense complexes, and whether the injectivity conditions (summation + concatenation + MLPs) are the only viable choices or whether approximate injective aggregators could further reduce feature dimensionality.
