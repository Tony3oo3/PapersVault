## Problem

Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) suffer from spectral bias, making them unable to reliably capture high-frequency and multi-scale components in PDE solutions. Existing Gaussian process PDE solvers use simple stationary kernels (SE, Matérn) that lack the flexibility to model arbitrary frequency content, while the spectral mixture kernel—though known to encode frequency information—has not been leveraged for PDE solving. Additionally, GP solvers face a severe computational bottleneck when the large number of collocation points needed to resolve high frequencies makes the full covariance matrix intractable.

## Input / Output

The method takes a PDE (differential operator plus source term), Dirichlet boundary conditions, and a domain specification as input. Collocation points are placed on a structured grid (e.g., 400×400 for 2D problems) rather than randomly sampled, which is essential both for capturing high frequencies and for enabling the Kronecker-product computation. The output is the solution values as a d-dimensional tensor on the grid, all required derivatives computed via GP conditional mean, and the learned mixture weights and frequency parameters that reveal the dominant spectral content of the solution.

## Method

GP-HM models the power spectrum of the PDE solution as a mixture of student-t (or Gaussian) distributions, then applies the Wiener-Khinchin theorem to derive the corresponding covariance function—a product of Matérn kernels modulated by cosines (or the spectral mixture kernel for the Gaussian case). Component weights are optimized in the log domain, which is equivalent to placing a Jeffreys prior on each weight, automatically pruning irrelevant frequency components and driving the surviving ones toward ground-truth frequencies. To achieve scalability, the covariance is factorized as a product kernel over input dimensions, and collocation points are placed on a grid, yielding a Kronecker-product structure in the covariance matrix. This reduces the O(M³) cost of matrix inversion to O(Σⱼ Mⱼ³ + (Σⱼ Mⱼ)M) via per-dimension Cholesky factorizations and tensor-matrix products, with no low-rank approximation. Derivatives of the solution are predicted via GP conditional mean using kernel derivatives, which also preserve the Kronecker structure, and the full model is trained by maximizing a weighted log joint probability over solution values, boundary fit, and PDE residual.

## Results

GP-HM achieves relative L2 errors at the 10⁻³ to 10⁻⁷ level across all 11 benchmark PDE test cases (1D/2D Poisson, Allen-Cahn, and Advection), with GP-HM-GM reaching as low as 3.99×10⁻⁷ on 1D Poisson u₁ and GP-HM-StM reaching 8.97×10⁻⁷ on u₄. By contrast, standard PINN and W-PINN fail on nearly every equation (errors ≥ 1.0), while RFF-PINN improves to ~10⁻⁵ on the easiest 1D case but degrades to ~1.0 on most 2D and multi-scale problems. GP solvers with plain SE or Matérn kernels produce errors 3–5 orders of magnitude larger than GP-HM, confirming that the spectral mixture design is essential for high-frequency recovery.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GP-HM decisively outperforms all ML-based baselines: standard PINN and W-PINN fail on virtually every test case (errors ≥ 1.0, meaning the solution is not recovered at all), while GP-HM achieves 10⁻³–10⁻⁷ errors. RFF-PINN (Wang et al., 2021b) is the strongest ML competitor, reaching 2.00×10⁻⁵ on 1D Poisson u₂, but degrades to 1.69×10⁰ on 2D u₆ and 7.99×10⁻¹ on 2D u₇, where GP-HM achieves 6.46×10⁻⁵ and 1.02×10⁻³ respectively — a gap of 4–5 orders of magnitude. Rowdy (Jagtap et al., 2022) with adaptive activations performs no better than vanilla PINN in most cases. The spectral method (Boyd, 2001) using 200 trigonometric bases achieves 2.36×10⁻² on 1D u₁ but fails on multi-scale solutions (errors > 1.0), confirming that fixed trigonometric bases are insufficient. GP-HM also beats GP-SE and GP-Matérn by 3–5 orders of magnitude, demonstrating that the spectral mixture kernel design (not just the GP framework) is what enables high-frequency recovery.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the spectral mixture kernel (Wilson & Adams, 2013) — originally designed as a general-purpose stationary GP kernel — as a principled frequency-extraction mechanism for PDE solutions, grounded in the Wiener-Khinchin theorem. It builds on the GP-PDE solver framework of Chen et al. (2021) and Long et al. (2022b) but introduces two critical departures: grid-based collocation with a product covariance (enabling Kronecker product structure) and log-domain weight optimization (equivalent to a Jeffreys prior for automatic sparsity). The Kronecker product scalability draws on structured GP inference (Saatcci, 2012; Wilson & Nickisch, 2015), but the paper is the first to exploit the natural grid structure of PDE collocation to make this practical without low-rank approximations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GP-HM provides a principled, architecture-agnostic alternative to PINNs for the high-frequency and multi-scale PDE regime where neural networks fundamentally struggle due to spectral bias, achieving 1e-3 to 1e-7 relative L2 errors where PINNs fail at O(1). The automatic frequency selection via the implicit Jeffreys prior eliminates the need to manually specify dominant frequencies, and the Kronecker product approach scales to 160K+ collocation points without approximation. Key limitations include restriction to 1D/2D in experiments, the requirement for grid-based (rather than arbitrary) collocation, slow convergence requiring tens of thousands of iterations, and the counterintuitive finding that second-order optimizers (L-BFGS) underperform first-order (ADAM). Open questions include extension to 3D+ problems, coupled PDE systems, and practical engineering applications.
