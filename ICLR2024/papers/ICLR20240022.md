## Problem

No non-asymptotic (finite-time) convergence guarantees existed for on-policy federated reinforcement learning with heterogeneous environments, where agents interact with different MDPs and their behavior policies change over time. The interplay of Markovian sampling, linear function approximation, multiple local updates (client drift), environmental heterogeneity, and time-varying policies made prior off-policy analyses inapplicable, leaving it unknown whether federated on-policy methods converge and at what rate.

## Input / Output

Each of N agents samples state-action tuples (s, a, r, s', a') from its own MDP with a shared state-action space S×A but distinct reward functions r^(i) and transition kernels P^(i). The Q-value is approximated linearly as Q_θ(s,a) = φ(s,a)ᵀθ with ‖φ(s,a)‖₂ ≤ 1, and the policy is derived via a Lipschitz-continuous improvement operator Γ (e.g., softmax with temperature). The output is a single shared parameter vector θ that simultaneously approximates every agent's optimal parameter θ*_i, with the approximation radius scaling as O(ε_p + ε_r)/(1−γ) where ε_p and ε_r quantify transition-kernel and reward heterogeneity.

## Method

FedSARSA runs SARSA semi-gradient updates locally at each agent and performs periodic server-side averaging with projection every K steps, combining on-policy TD control with the FedAvg communication pattern. The key theoretical contribution is a finite-time error bound (Theorem 2) showing that the mean-squared error contracts at rate (1−α_t w) per step, with a noise term scaled by 1/N that yields linear speedup, and a heterogeneity bias term of order Λ(ε_p, ε_r). To handle the nonstationarity of on-policy trajectories, the proof introduces a virtual backtracking technique that fixes the policy τ steps in the past and bounds the divergence between real and virtual observations. A novel perturbation bound (Theorem 1) for the nonlinear projected Bellman fixed point characterizes how far apart agents' optimal parameters can be under heterogeneity, and a refined variance-reduction argument shows that averaging semi-gradients from N non-identical time-varying Markov chains achieves the 1/N variance reduction needed for linear speedup.

## Results

FedSARSA achieves a finite-time error bound of Õ(1/(NT) + Λ²(ε_p, ε_r)/H²) with a linearly decaying step-size, demonstrating an N-fold linear speedup over single-agent SARSA (which achieves O(1/T)). With a constant step-size, the algorithm converges exponentially to a ball of radius O(ε_p + ε_r) around each agent's optimal parameter. The perturbation bound (Theorem 1) shows that agents' optimal parameters differ by at most (Rε_r + Hσε_p)/w, where w = O(1−γ), confirming that the universal policy is near-optimal for all agents when heterogeneity is small. Simulations with |S|=100, |A|=100, d=25, γ=0.2 confirm the theoretical predictions across heterogeneity levels ε_p, ε_r ∈ {0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1, 2} and N ∈ {5, 10, 20} agents.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedSARSA is the first FRL method to simultaneously handle environmental heterogeneity, linear speedup, linear function approximation, Markovian sampling, and adaptive (on-policy) behavior policies. Compared to Khodadadian et al. (2022), which achieves linear speedup for federated Q-learning under Markovian sampling, FedSARSA extends the analysis to heterogeneous environments and on-policy methods where trajectories are nonstationary. Compared to Wang et al. (2023a), which handles heterogeneity for federated TD(0) (policy evaluation only), FedSARSA addresses the harder planning task with time-varying policies. Compared to Jin et al. (2022), which considers heterogeneity in transition dynamics, FedSARSA additionally provides linear speedup and handles reward heterogeneity. The simulations confirm that increasing N from 5 to 20 agents reduces MSE by roughly a factor of 4 (consistent with the 1/N speedup), while increasing heterogeneity from 0 to 2 raises the asymptotic MSE floor proportionally, matching the O(ε_p + ε_r) theoretical prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedSARSA extends the single-agent finite-time analysis of linear SARSA (Zou et al., 2019) into the federated setting, while building on the server-client aggregation framework of FedAvg (McMahan et al., 2017) and the heterogeneity-aware federated TD(0) analysis of Wang et al. (2023a). The key departure from prior FRL work (Khodadadian et al., 2022; Woo et al., 2023) is the shift from off-policy to on-policy learning, which introduces time-varying behavior policies and nonstationary transition kernels that invalidate existing Markovian-sampling analyses. The paper also introduces a novel backtracking technique to handle this nonstationarity and provides the first perturbation bound on nonlinear projected Bellman fixed points, extending the linear perturbation theory of Wang et al. (2023a) to the nonlinear SARSA setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provably sample-efficient on-policy algorithm for federated RL, establishing that N-fold linear speedup is achievable even when agents operate in heterogeneous environments with time-varying policies. The characterization of the convergence region radius as O(ε_p + ε_r) provides a concrete criterion for when federated collaboration is beneficial versus counterproductive. Open questions include whether the results extend to nonlinear function approximation, asynchronous or partial-participation communication, and whether the near-optimal universal policy can be efficiently personalized per agent without losing the speedup guarantee. The backtracking technique may also prove useful for other nonstationary stochastic approximation problems.
