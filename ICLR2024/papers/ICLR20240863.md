## Problem

Diffusion models trained on long-tailed data inherently produce majority (high-density) samples because their score-matching objective and Tweedie-based denoising are biased toward high-likelihood regions, making it computationally expensive and impractical to generate minority samples that carry unique attributes. This limitation is particularly costly in applications like medical imaging (rare lesion synthesis), fairness-aware generation, and creative AI where low-density instances are the target.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method consumes standard Gaussian noise (same as any DDPM sampler) plus two conditioning hyperparameters: an ordinal minority class label l̃ ∈ {0,…,L−1} specifying the desired uniqueness level, and a classifier scale w controlling guidance strength. In the class-conditional extension, a semantic class label c is additionally provided. The output is a generated image (64×64 to 256×256 depending on the backbone) that is steered toward low-density regions of the data manifold, with the degree of uniqueness controllable via l̃ and w.

## Method

The paper first proves that the optimal score network under DSM training yields a posterior-mean denoiser (via Tweedie's formula) that is inherently biased toward majority features, especially under strong perturbation. It then defines a 'minority score' metric as the expected LPIPS distance between a clean sample and its one-shot Tweedie reconstruction, which is provably equivalent (up to scaling) to the noise-prediction error and requires only a few network evaluations. For generation, the authors discretize minority scores into L ordinal categories, train a lightweight U-Net classifier to predict the category from noised inputs, and apply classifier guidance by adding w·∇log p_ψ(l̃|x_t) to the pretrained score function during ancestral sampling. This 'minority guidance' is label-agnostic (no minority annotations needed), works in both unconditional and class-conditional settings, and provides a free knob for controlling the uniqueness of generated features.

## Results

On CelebA 64×64, minority guidance achieves the best cFID (26.98) and sFID (8.23) among all baselines, outperforming Sehwag et al. (cFID 27.97, sFID 10.17) and the label-based ADM-ML (cFID 51.66). On LSUN-Bedrooms 256×256, it reduces cFID to 41.52 versus 56.45 (StyleGAN) and 62.83 (ADM). On the Brain MRI benchmark, minority guidance achieves the best cFID (19.73), sFID (13.36), and Recall (0.47) across all methods. On ImageNet 256×256, it matches Sehwag et al. on cFID (13.83 vs. 13.43) while substantially improving sFID (7.82 vs. 9.88) and Recall (0.45 vs. 0.42).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CelebA, minority guidance beats Sehwag et al. (the closest competitor) on cFID (26.98 vs. 27.97) and sFID (8.23 vs. 10.17) while sacrificing some Recall (0.34 vs. 0.42); it also substantially outperforms the label-based ADM-ML (cFID 51.66) and BigGAN (cFID 80.41). On LSUN-Bedrooms, the method achieves the best cFID (41.52) by a wide margin over StyleGAN (56.45), LDM (63.61), and ADM (62.83), though at the cost of the lowest Recall (0.10 vs. 0.13–0.16). On ImageNet 256×256, minority guidance is nearly tied with Sehwag et al. on cFID (13.83 vs. 13.43) but clearly wins on sFID (7.82 vs. 9.88) and Recall (0.45 vs. 0.42), indicating better spatial fidelity and diversity. On the Brain MRI benchmark, the method dominates both ADM and StyleGAN2-ADA across all four metrics (cFID 19.73, sFID 13.36, Prec 0.84, Rec 0.47), with StyleGAN2-ADA performing particularly poorly (sFID 23.07, Prec 0.53). Overall, minority guidance is the only method that is label-agnostic (no minority annotations required) while matching or exceeding the performance of label-dependent baselines like ADM-ML and Sehwag et al.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the classifier guidance framework of Dhariwal & Nichol (2021) and the Tweedie denoising formula, repurposing the inherent majority-bias of Tweedie's posterior mean as a signal for sample uniqueness. It departs from Sehwag et al. (2022), which also guides diffusion sampling toward low-density regions but is restricted to class-conditional settings requiring a class predictor, and from label-dependent minority generators (Yu et al., 2020; Lin et al., 2022; Qin et al., 2023) by introducing a fully label-agnostic 'minority score' metric. The connection to diffusion-based anomaly detection (Wolleb et al., 2022; Wyatt et al., 2022) is acknowledged but inverted: those methods use reconstruction error to flag anomalies in existing data, whereas this work uses the same principle to steer generation toward underrepresented regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The label-agnostic, controllability-rich framework (via the minority class index l̃ and guidance scale w) offers a practical tool for generating rare instances in high-stakes domains like medical imaging, where synthesizing examples of rare pathologies can directly improve diagnostic model robustness. A key limitation is the need to train a minority classifier on the full dataset, which becomes computationally prohibitive for very large corpora; the authors identify data-restricted scenarios as a promising future direction. The open question of whether the minority score can be computed more efficiently (e.g., without a separate classifier) or adapted to text-to-image and other modalities remains unresolved.
