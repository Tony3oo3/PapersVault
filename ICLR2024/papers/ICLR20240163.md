## Problem

Existing unsupervised object-centric learning methods decompose 2D images, producing multi-view-inconsistent segmentations that fail to capture the 3D structure of dynamic scenes. No prior approach simultaneously achieves 3D-consistent, unsupervised object decomposition of dynamic scenes and enables downstream geometric manipulation such as scene editing without additional training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240163/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sparse set of V RGB images (512×512) at the initial timestamp with known camera poses, plus a monocular video of T frames from a moving camera. It produces per-object 4D voxel grids (N slots × spatial resolution) encoding occupancy probabilities, time-invariant slot features, rendered novel views, and 2D decomposition maps. Notably, it operates with as few as V=1 initial views and requires no depth, segmentation, or geometric priors.

## Method

DynaVol introduces object-centric 4D voxelization, where an extra dimension over the 3D spatial grid encodes the probability of each object occupying a voxel, enabling natural 3D-consistent separation. Three network components work together: a bidirectional deformation network maps voxel features between canonical and current time, a volume slot attention module (3D CNN encoder + iterative slot attention with GRU) aggregates local voxel features into global time-invariant object slots, and a compositional NeRF decoder renders by querying per-object densities from the voxel grid and per-object colors from an MLP conditioned on slot features. Training proceeds in three stages: a 3D voxel warmup with cycle-consistency loss, a 3D-to-4D expansion via a connected-components algorithm that clusters voxels by motion and appearance similarity, and end-to-end 4D voxel optimization with the full compositional renderer. This architecture enables direct scene editing (object removal, trajectory modification, color swapping) by manipulating voxel occupancy values or replacing deformation fields post-training.

## Results

On the primary scene decomposition task (FG-ARI), DynaVol (V=5) achieves 93.96–96.06 across all 8 synthetic Kubric scenes, dramatically outperforming SAVi (3.10–7.87), uORF (22.58–38.65), and SAM (46.80–71.68). For novel view synthesis on synthetic scenes, DynaVol (V=1) reaches 26.80–31.83 PSNR, surpassing D-NeRF (V=1) by up to 18.5 dB on 3ObjRand and DeVRF-Dyn by 11–13 dB. On real-world scenes, DynaVol averages 23.50 PSNR / 0.844 SSIM, outperforming HyperNeRF by 5.7% in PSNR and 7.2% in SSIM, and D²NeRF by 6.4% in PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynaVol wins decisively against all 8 baselines across both tasks. In scene decomposition (FG-ARI), DynaVol (V=5) scores 93.96–96.06, beating SAM (46.80–71.68) by 22–49 points, uORF (22.58–38.65) by 55–73 points, and SAVi (3.10–7.87) by 86–93 points. In novel view synthesis on synthetic scenes, DynaVol (V=1) outperforms D-NeRF (V=1) by 1.3–18.5 dB PSNR (most dramatic on 3ObjRand: 31.10 vs. 12.62) and DeVRF-Dyn by 11.0–13.6 dB. On real-world scenes, DynaVol surpasses HyperNeRF (22.23 avg PSNR) by 1.27 dB and D²NeRF (22.09) by 1.41 dB, while NeuralDiff (20.07) trails by 3.43 dB. Notably, DynaVol's performance is nearly identical with V=1 vs. V=5 initial views (e.g., 31.83 vs. 32.11 PSNR on 3ObjFall), indicating robustness to monocular input, whereas D-NeRF and DeVRF degrade substantially under the same constraint.

## Relation to Prior Work

DynaVol unifies three previously separate research threads: 2D unsupervised scene decomposition via slot attention (SAVi, STEVE), 3D object-centric representation learning (uORF, ObSuRF), and dynamic NeRF rendering (D-NeRF, DeVRF). Its key architectural departure is the introduction of a 4D voxel grid that encodes per-object occupancy probabilities at each spatial location, combined with a volume slot attention mechanism that aggregates local voxel features into global time-invariant slot representations. The canonical-space deformation module extends D-NeRF's deformation network to operate on voxel grids rather than continuous coordinates, while the compositional NeRF decoder queries density directly from voxels and color from an MLP conditioned on slot features. The three-stage training (3D warmup → connected-components expansion → 4D optimization) is a practical contribution that addresses the difficulty of jointly learning object identity and geometry from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DynaVol demonstrates that explicitly meaningful 3D object-centric representations enable capabilities fundamentally unavailable to 2D decomposition methods: 3D-consistent segmentation across views, novel view synthesis, and direct scene editing (object removal, trajectory modification, color swapping) without retraining. However, the approach requires per-scene overfitting (~3 hours on a single GPU), which limits scalability, and real-world performance still trails dedicated SOTA renderers like 4K4D. Open questions include whether the 4D voxel representation can be combined with more efficient rendering backends, how the method handles scenes with many interacting objects or topological changes, and whether the connected-components initialization can be replaced by a fully differentiable alternative to enable end-to-end training.
