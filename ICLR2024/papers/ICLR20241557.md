## Problem

Most sparse GLM algorithms require offline batch training over a fixed dataset, making them impractical for real-time scenarios where observations are dynamically added or removed (e.g., edge computing, fraud detection, dynamic pricing). Existing data-varying algorithms are tailored to specific loss/regularizer combinations and cannot dynamically update regularization hyperparameters across online rounds. This paper presents the first no-regret algorithm for sparse GLMs that handles simultaneous addition and deletion of multiple observations while adaptively updating data-dependent regularization parameters.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an initial model trained on X^(i) = {X^◇ ∪ X^-}, a set of observations X^+ to incorporate, a set X^- to eliminate, and a validation set for hyperparameter selection. It produces updated model weights β^(ii) and optimal regularization parameters λ^(τ) for each online round. Unusually, the algorithm processes multiple data points per round (chunk updating) rather than one at a time, and the regularization parameters are re-optimized at every round rather than being fixed a priori, with the solution path traced through a continuous (s,t) parameter space from the old to the new dataset.

## Method

SAGO recasts the data-varying learning task as a bilevel optimization: the inner problem tracks how the optimal β evolves as observations are swapped via parameterizers μ(t) and ν(s) that smoothly interpolate between the old and new datasets, while the outer problem selects the optimal regularization λ using a validation loss. The inner dynamics are characterized as a first-order PDE system in (s,t), and the outer dynamics as a first-order ODE in λ, both solved numerically along a chosen integral path Ω. At event points where the active set changes (a coefficient enters or leaves the model), the algorithm resets the active set using thresholding conditions derived from KKT optimality, ensuring piecewise-smooth trajectory tracking. The PDE-ODE procedure is executed segment-by-segment, and Theorem 1 guarantees that the final solution at (s̄,t̄) is exactly equivalent to batch retraining on the new dataset, yielding zero regret with finite convergence.

## Results

SAGO achieves the lowest validation loss across all 8 datasets and all 3 online rounds, with notably smaller standard deviations than all baselines. For example, on MiniBooNE (Round 1), SAGO reaches 0.389±0.009 validation loss versus 0.439±0.066 (ColdGrid) and 0.393±0.017 (ColdHyBi); on creditcard (Round 1), SAGO achieves 0.692±0.004 versus 0.714±0.017 (ColdGrid). The computational advantage is also clear: Figure 4 shows SAGO's per-sample updating time is substantially lower than all batch-retraining baselines, and Figure 5 confirms linear scaling with the number of varying observations (n₊+n₋).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAGO outperforms all six baselines (ColdGrid, ColdHyBi, ColdByO in Rounds 1–2; WarmGrid, WarmHyBi, WarmByO in Round 3) on every dataset and every round in terms of validation loss. The largest margins appear on MiniBooNE (e.g., 0.389 vs. 0.439 for ColdGrid in Round 1, a ~11% relative improvement) and house_16H (26.25 vs. 27.91 for ColdGrid). Among the baselines, ColdHyBi (hypergradient-based bilevel, Feng & Simon 2018) is typically the strongest competitor, but SAGO still edges it out on most datasets while exhibiting far smaller variance (e.g., ±0.004 vs. ±0.069 on creditcard). The FISTA-based batch retraining (Beck & Teboulle 2009) used by all baselines requires full re-optimization each round, whereas SAGO's PDE-ODE trajectory tracing avoids repeated iterations, yielding the computational speedup shown in Figure 4. Bayesian optimization (Snoek et al. 2012) for λ selection (ColdByO/WarmByO) is the weakest baseline on most datasets, confirming that SAGO's integrated outer-level ODE for hyperparameter tuning is more effective than black-box search.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data-varying (incremental-decremental) learning paradigm previously applied to specific models—Lasso (Garrigues & Ghaoui, 2008), logistic regression (Tsai et al., 2014), and SVMs (Gu et al., 2018)—to the full class of sparse Generalized Linear Models with arbitrary sparsity-enforcing penalties. It builds directly on Li & Gu (AAAI 2022), which used ODE-based dynamic updating for group lasso, by generalizing to a PDE-ODE framework that simultaneously handles addition and deletion of observations and dynamically updates regularization parameters via a bilevel optimization formulation. A key departure from prior data-varying algorithms is the ability to adjust hyperparameters (not just model weights) at each online round, and the first exact no-regret guarantee for Poisson and gamma regression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The no-regret equivalence guarantee (SAGO output is provably identical to batch retraining) for a broad class of sparse GLMs is a meaningful theoretical advance, and the PDE-ODE formulation provides a principled, general mechanism for tracking solution trajectories under data changes. However, the practical scope is limited to tabular data and linear (or basis-expanded) models, and the computational cost depends on the number of event points and the chosen PDE/ODE solver. Open questions include scalability to very high-dimensional settings, handling of non-convex penalty discontinuities (where warm-starting is needed), and whether the framework extends naturally to non-linear deep models or streaming settings with adversarial data changes.
