## Problem

Existing debiasing methods for recommender systems (IPS, DR, and variants) are theoretically guaranteed to be unbiased only under the Stable Unit Treatment Values Assumption (SUTVA), which requires that a user-item pair's potential outcome is unaffected by treatments assigned to other pairs. In practice, neighborhood effects—where a user's rating or click is influenced by other users' interactions with the same or related items—violate this assumption, causing all prior estimators to produce biased loss estimates and suboptimal prediction models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes user-item pair features x_{u,i}, the binary exposure indicator o_{u,i}, a neighborhood treatment representation g_{u,i} (a scalar or low-dimensional vector summarizing the exposure statuses of neighboring user-item pairs via a mapping φ), observed feedback r_{u,i}, and estimated propensity scores p_{u,i}(g) for the joint treatment (o_{u,i}, g_{u,i}). It outputs a debiased prediction matrix R̂ and an unbiased estimate of the ideal loss L_ideal^N(R̂), which is an integral over the treatment representation space G weighted by a pre-specified density π(g). The kernel-smoothing step requires a bandwidth h and a symmetric kernel K (Gaussian or Epanechnikov), making the estimators applicable even when g is continuous rather than discrete.

## Method

The paper formulates the neighborhood effect as an interference problem in causal inference, extending the potential outcome to r_{u,i}(o_{u,i}, g_{u,i}) where g_{u,i} = φ(o_{N_{(u,i)}}) is a learned treatment representation that compresses the high-dimensional neighborhood exposure vector into a lower-dimensional summary (Assumption 1). On this basis, a novel ideal loss L_ideal^N(R̂) is defined as an integral over g ∈ G of the per-g loss, forcing the prediction model to perform well across varying neighborhood conditions. Two kernel-smoothing based estimators are proposed: N-IPS, which reweights observed samples by the inverse joint propensity p(o=1, g_{u,i}=g | x_{u,i}) with a kernel K((g_{u,i}−g)/h) to handle continuous g, and N-DR, which additionally imputes missing losses via a model m(x_{u,i}, φ_g) trained with a kernel-weighted loss. Theoretical analysis establishes that both estimators have bias O(h²) and variance O(1/(|D|h)), with optimal bandwidth h* = O(|D|^{−1/5}), and generalization error bounds via Rademacher complexity. A density-ratio-based propensity estimation procedure is also provided for the mixed binary-continuous joint treatment, decomposing 1/p_{u,i}(g) into a standard propensity term and a density ratio estimated by a classifier on labeled positive/negative samples.

## Results

On the three real-world datasets (Coat, Yahoo! R3, KuaiRec), the proposed N-IPS, N-DR-JL, and N-MRDR-JL estimators consistently outperform all 12 debiasing baselines across MSE, AUC, and NDCG metrics. The best N-MRDR-JL[NB,Epanechnikov] variant achieves MSE 0.044, AUC 0.802, and NDCG@50 0.587 on KuaiRec, and MSE 0.194 on Yahoo! R3. On the semi-synthetic MovieLens-100K experiment, N-MRDR reduces the relative estimation error by 4–5× compared to MRDR (e.g., RE drops from 0.2578 to 0.0622 on the ONE metric), confirming unbiased estimation under interference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed N-IPS, N-DR-JL, and N-MRDR-JL estimators uniformly outperform their non-neighborhood counterparts: N-IPS beats IPS (Schnabel et al., 2016) on all nine metric-dataset combinations, N-DR-JL beats DR-JL (Wang et al., 2019) on all nine, and N-MRDR-JL beats MRDR-JL (Guo et al., 2021) on all nine. Against the strongest non-neighborhood baselines, N-MRDR-JL[NB,Epanechnikov] achieves the best MSE on Yahoo! R3 (0.194 vs. 0.196 for N-IPS[NB,Gaussian] and 0.210 for SDR) and KuaiRec (0.044 vs. 0.046 for DR and DR-BIAS), while N-IPS[LR,Gaussian] achieves the best NDCG@5 on Coat (0.678 vs. 0.643 for DAMF and MR). The proposed methods also outperform recent debiasing approaches including CVIB (Wang et al., 2020), DIB (Liu et al., 2021), ASIPS (Saito, 2020a), DAMF (Saito & Nomura, 2022), DR-BIAS/DR-MSE (Dai et al., 2022), MR (Li et al., 2023a), TDR/TDR-JL (Li et al., 2023b), and StableDR (Li et al., 2023e) on at least one metric per dataset. Notably, the advantage is most pronounced on KuaiRec (the largest dataset with 4.67M records), where N-MRDR-JL[NB,Epanechnikov] improves AUC from 0.788 (SNIPS) to 0.802 and NDCG@50 from 0.576 (SNIPS) to 0.587, suggesting the neighborhood effect is more impactful at scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard debiasing framework for recommender systems (IPS, DR, MRDR) by relaxing the SUTVA/no-interference assumption that all prior methods rely on. It draws on the causal inference literature on interference (Ogburn & VanderWeele, Aronow & Samii, Forastiere et al.) to formalize the neighborhood effect as a treatment on neighboring units, then introduces a learnable treatment representation φ to reduce the high-dimensional neighborhood exposure vector to a manageable summary. The proposed N-IPS and N-DR estimators are direct kernel-smoothing extensions of Schnabel et al.'s IPS and Wang et al.'s DR, respectively, with the key addition of a bandwidth parameter h that smooths over the continuous neighborhood treatment representation g. The paper also theoretically connects to prior debiasing methods by showing exactly when (g ⊥ o | x) the old and new ideal losses coincide, and when they diverge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first formal treatment of selection bias under interference in recommender systems, showing that widely-used debiasing estimators are provably biased when neighborhood effects exist — a condition that is clearly violated in practice (e.g., social influence on ratings, viral effects on clicks). The theoretical contributions (bias/variance rates, optimal bandwidth O(|D|^{-1/5}), Rademacher-based generalization bounds) give practitioners concrete guidance on estimator design. A key open question is the choice of the hypothesis space G for the treatment representation g, which currently relies on domain knowledge and is acknowledged as a significant practical limitation. Future work could explore learning G end-to-end or developing data-driven neighborhood definitions.
