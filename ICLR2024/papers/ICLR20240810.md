## Problem

Existing vision-language foundation models are trained with only one or two of three complementary pre-training objectives—contrastive learning, image-to-text generation, and text-to-image generation—despite all three being mutually relevant and trainable on the same image-text pairs. No prior work had unified all three objectives in a single framework, leaving their potential synergies unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240810/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CoBIT accepts three input formats: raw images (288×288, split into 18×18 patches) for understanding tasks, discrete image tokens (256×256 images tokenized into a 32×32 grid of 8192-class ViT-VQGAN codes) for autoregressive image generation, and text tokens (SentencePiece, 64k vocabulary, max length 64). Outputs span generated 256×256 images (decoded token-by-token through ViT-VQGAN), generated captions, class labels, retrieval similarity scores, and VQA answers, with the model switching between encoding and decoding modes depending on the task.

## Method

CoBIT introduces a unicoder-decoder architecture where an image unicoder and a text unicoder each share the same Transformer parameters across encoding (bi-directional or causal attention mask) and decoding (causal attention mask) modes, differing only in input embedding and mask. A cross-attention decoder then fuses the autoregressive features from the generating unicoder with the encoded features from the conditioning unicoder. All three objectives—contrastive (with an attention pooler for image features and CLS token for text), I2T generation, and T2I generation (with conv-shaped sparse attention for long image token sequences)—are optimized jointly with loss weights λ_T2I:λ_I2T:λ_Con = 1:0.2:0.1, and classifier-free guidance (α=2.0) is applied for T2I. The text unicoder is initialized from a pretrained CoCa unimodal text decoder, while the image unicoder and cross-modal decoder are trained from scratch.

## Results

In zero-shot evaluation, CoBIT-Large achieves 82.7% ImageNet classification accuracy, 44.8 CIDEr on MS-COCO captioning, and 9.37 FID on MS-COCO text-to-image generation, outperforming all comparable-scale baselines on generation tasks. After fine-tuning, CoBIT-Large reaches 86.44% ImageNet linear probing accuracy, 78.3 VQA score, 86.0 SNLI-VE accuracy, 139.5 CIDEr on MS-COCO captioning, and 4.62 FID on MS-COCO T2I generation. The model demonstrates that unifying contrastive, I2T, and T2I objectives in a single framework yields competitive or superior performance across all three task families simultaneously.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On zero-shot text-to-image generation, CoBIT-Large (9.37 FID) outperforms all comparable-scale baselines including Parti-750M (10.71), DALL-E 2 (10.39), Make-A-Scene (11.84), and LDM-1.4B (12.63), and even beats larger models like CM3Leon-7B (10.82). On zero-shot ImageNet classification, CoBIT-Large (82.7%) surpasses CLIP (76.2%), ALIGN (76.4%), and FILIP (78.3%) but trails Florence (83.7%) and CoCa-Large (84.8%), which use larger batch sizes. For zero-shot captioning, CoBIT-Large (44.8 CIDEr) significantly outperforms SimVLM (32.2) but falls short of VLKD (58.3), which leverages a pre-trained LLM decoder. In fine-tuning, CoBIT-Large achieves the best ImageNet linear probing (86.44%) among all baselines, beating CLIP (85.4%), ALIGN (85.5%), BEIT3 (84.2%), and PALI (84.3%). However, on fine-tuned VQA (78.3) and SNLI-VE (86.0), CoBIT-Large trails OFA (91.0/91.2, which uses both image and text premises) and SimVLM (86.2/86.3), while matching BLIP on VQA. On fine-tuned T2I generation, CoBIT-Large (4.62 FID) beats OFA (10.5) and X-LXMERT (29.9) but is outperformed by CoDi (3.22) and Parti-20B (3.22), both of which are substantially larger models.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoBIT carves new ground by unifying three pre-training objectives—cross-modal contrastive learning (CLIP, ALIGN), image-to-text generation (CoCa, PaLI), and text-to-image generation (Parti, DALL-E)—that prior unified models like OFA and Unified-IO only combined in pairs. The key architectural departure is the 'unicoder' module, inspired by UniLM and T5's shared-parameters-for-encoding-and-decoding idea, which replaces the standard encoder-decoder pipeline with a single Transformer that switches between bi-directional and causal attention masks depending on the task. This design lets the same parameters serve as both a feature extractor and an autoregressive generator, a repurposing of the NLP unicoder concept to the vision-language setting that had not been attempted for joint T2I and I2T generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoBIT provides the first empirical evidence that contrastive, I2T, and T2I objectives are jointly optimizable in a single model without substantial mutual degradation, opening a path toward more compact and versatile VL foundation models. The ablation revealing a mild but real contradiction between T2I and I2T losses (Table 4) is an important caveat: the unification is not perfectly harmonious, and resolving this tension remains an open question. Practical limitations include reliance on noisy web-crawled data (raising bias and deepfake concerns) and the fact that the ~1B-parameter model, while competitive, still trails the largest specialized systems (Parti-20B, DALL-E 2) on absolute T2I quality.
