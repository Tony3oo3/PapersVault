## Problem

Channel-independent (CI) methods have outperformed channel-dependent (CD) methods in multivariate time series forecasting, but they entirely ignore cross-variate information. Existing CD methods attempt to model channel dependence without prior knowledge of which variates lead or lag and by how many steps, causing them to overfit static patterns that shift over time. The paper asks whether there exists an explicit, locally stationary form of channel dependence—specifically, lead-lag relationships—that can be exploited to improve forecasting without the overfitting risks of generic CD modeling.

## Input / Output

LIFT takes a C-variate lookback window of length L (e.g., L=336) and produces H future steps for all C variates (H ∈ {24, 48, 96, 192, 336, 720}). It operates as a plug-and-play refinement module: a black-box backbone first generates preliminary predictions from the lookback window, and LIFT then refines those predictions using the actual observed values of leading indicators. The lookback window is instance-normalized (mean/std per variate) before lead estimation and frequency-domain processing, and the final output is denormalized back to the original scale. The Lead Estimator is non-parametric and can be precomputed once on training data, so at inference only the lightweight Lead-aware Refiner adds computation.

## Method

LIFT consists of three stages. First, a non-parametric Lead Estimator computes pairwise cross-correlation coefficients for all lag values simultaneously via the Wiener-Khinchin theorem (FFT of the product of one variate's spectrum and the conjugate of the other's), reducing complexity from O(L²) to O(L log L); it then selects the top-K leading indicators per variate by maximum absolute cross-correlation and identifies the corresponding leading step. Second, a target-oriented shift aligns each leading indicator's lookback and predicted sequences by its estimated leading step so that the K indicators are temporally aligned with the target variate in the horizon window, with sign flipping for negative correlations. Third, the Lead-aware Refiner (Adaptive Frequency Mixer) operates in the frequency domain: it computes FFTs of the target's preliminary prediction and the aligned leading indicators, generates state-conditioned frequency-domain filters via a linear layer parameterized by N learned variate states, and mixes three signal types—direct influence (U_k), shared-latent-factor interpolation (Δ_k = U_k − V), and the target's own spectrum (V)—through a complex-valued linear projection before inverse FFT and denormalization. This design adaptively filters out irrelevant frequency components from leading indicators while preserving informative ones, and the entire module can be jointly trained end-to-end or attached to a frozen pretrained backbone.

## Results

LIFT improves its backbone by an average of 5.4% in MSE across six datasets (Weather, Electricity, Traffic, Solar, Wind, PeMSD8) and six forecast horizons (H=24 to 720). The average improvement over channel-independent backbones (PatchTST, DLinear) is 7.9%, while the average improvement over channel-dependent backbones (Crossformer, MTGNN) is 3.0%. The largest single improvement is 12.3% (PeMSD8, H=720, DLinear+LIFT vs. DLinear), and the smallest is 1.4% (Weather, H=720, PatchTST+LIFT vs. PatchTST).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LIFT consistently outperforms all four backbones it is paired with across all six primary datasets and all six horizons. Against the CI backbone PatchTST, LIFT yields the largest gains on challenging datasets where channel dependence is hard to model: e.g., 7.3% on Traffic (H=24) and 11.0% on Solar (H=24, 48). Against DLinear, improvements are similarly strong (e.g., 12.3% on PeMSD8 H=720, 11.0% on Solar H=24/48). Against the CD backbone Crossformer, LIFT still provides 3.0% average improvement (e.g., 4.1% on Solar), indicating that existing CD models cannot fully exploit dynamic lead-lag relationships. Against MTGNN, LIFT improves by 3.0% on average, with the largest gain of 12.3% on PeMSD8 (H=720), where MTGNN's static graph structure suffers most from distribution shifts. Notably, LightMTS (DLinear+LIFT) surpasses the more complex PatchTST by 3.2% on Weather and 0.7% on Electricity, but loses to PatchTST on Traffic (0.386 vs. 0.367 at H=96), where the 862 variates with complex temporal patterns demand a stronger backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the crossroads of channel-independent (CI) and channel-dependent (CD) MTS forecasting, arguing that the CI dominance over CD methods stems from CD methods' lack of structured prior knowledge about inter-variate relationships. Rather than adopting the static graph structures of MTGNN or the fixed cross-dimension attention of Crossformer, LIFT identifies a specific, interpretable form of channel dependence—locally stationary lead-lag relationships—and exploits it via efficient cross-correlation estimation (Wiener-Khinchin theorem) and frequency-domain filtering. The method is deliberately designed as a plug-and-play module orthogonal to the backbone, distinguishing it from prior CD architectures that entangle channel and temporal modeling in a single network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LIFT offers a principled, low-overhead mechanism (~10% parameters, ~14% inference time) to selectively incorporate channel dependence into any forecasting backbone, addressing the overfitting risk that has plagued CD methods. The lead-lag perspective reframes channel dependence as a cross-time phenomenon, opening a new design axis for future CD Transformers and graph-based models. However, the method's reliance on sufficient variate diversity limits its applicability: it degrades on datasets with only 7 variates (ETT, ILI), and the frequency-domain filtering, while ablation-validated, adds conceptual complexity that may not generalize to all signal types. Open questions include whether the non-parametric lead estimation can be further refined for very long lookback windows and whether the state-based filtering generalizes to non-stationary regimes beyond the tested datasets.
