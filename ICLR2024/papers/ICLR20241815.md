## Problem

Existing distributed saddle point optimization methods are restricted to smooth, unconstrained objectives in Euclidean space, while practical ML problems (adversarial training, GANs, sparse regression) involve non-smooth regularization and constraints. Standard primal averaging in federated settings destroys the structure (e.g., sparsity, low-rankness) that regularization is meant to induce — the so-called 'curse of primal averaging.' No prior method addresses composite saddle point optimization under the distributed paradigm.

## Input / Output

The method takes a composite saddle point objective min_x max_y f(x,y) + ψ₁(x) − ψ₂(y), where f is the average of M client-specific smooth functions and ψ₁, ψ₂ are possibly non-smooth regularizers (e.g., ℓ₁ norm for sparsity, nuclear norm for low-rankness), along with a distance-generating function defining the Bregman geometry and a compact domain. It produces an ergodic primal-dual solution (x̂, ŷ) obtained by averaging intermediate dual-extrapolation steps projected through the generalized proximal operator, with convergence measured by the duality gap. The structured output (sparse or low-rank) is preserved because aggregation occurs in the dual space rather than by averaging primal solutions.

## Method

FeDualEx adapts Nesterov's dual extrapolation (an extra-step primal-dual algorithm) to composite SPP by introducing a novel generalized proximal operator that replaces the vanilla Bregman divergence with the generalized Bregman divergence of Flammarion & Bach (2017), which absorbs the non-smooth regularization into the distance-generating function ℓ_t(z) = ℓ(z) + tηψ(z). Each client performs K local two-step proximal evaluations in the dual space, and the server aggregates the resulting dual variables before projecting back to the primal via the conjugate gradient mapping — this dual aggregation preserves regularization-induced structure that primal averaging would destroy. The key technical insight is that using the generalized Bregman divergence directly inside the proximal operator (rather than adding the composite term separately) is necessary for the dual extrapolation analysis to close, as the extra-step gradient evaluation and composite term otherwise produce non-cancelling terms that accumulate. Under homogeneous full-participation assumptions, the paper proves the first convergence rate for federated composite SPP, bounding the expected duality gap by O(1/RK) + O(1/(K^{1/4}R^{3/4})) + O(1/√(MRK)) + O(1/√R), and shows the sequential reduction recovers O(1/√T) stochastic and O(1/T) deterministic rates matching existing smooth and composite baselines.

## Results

Theoretically, FeDualEx achieves a duality gap bound of O(βB/(RK)) + O(β^{1/2}G^{1/2}B^{3/4}/(K^{1/4}R^{3/4})) + O(σB^{1/2}/(M^{1/2}R^{1/2}K^{1/2})) + O(β^{1/2}G^{1/2}B^{3/4}/R^{1/2}) for federated composite SPP, the first such rate in the distributed setting. Empirically, on ℓ₁-regularized bilinear SPP, FeDualEx converges to a duality gap of ~10⁻¹ while FedDualAvg and FedMiD remain above 10⁰, and achieves ~0.7 sparsity (vs. ~0.95 for FedMiP). On the nuclear-norm SPP, FeDualEx quickly reaches the optimal rank of 10. For universal adversarial training of a 3-layer CNN on CIFAR-10, FeDualEx yields 50.38% attack sparsity versus 99.31% for distributed PGDA, with faster convergence and higher validation accuracy on unattacked data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FeDualEx clearly outperforms FedDualAvg and FedMiD (Yuan et al., 2021) on composite SPP: while both dual-averaging-based methods stall at duality gaps above 10⁰, FeDualEx converges to ~10⁻¹, confirming that convex-optimization methods are insufficient for saddle point problems. On sparsity, FeDualEx achieves ~0.7 (fraction of zero entries) versus ~0.95 for its primal twin FedMiP, validating the advantage of dual-space aggregation in preserving regularization-imposed structure. Extra Step Local SGD (Beznosikov et al., 2020) and SCAFFOLD-S (Hou et al., 2021) fail to converge on composite non-smooth SPP entirely, as they are designed only for smooth unconstrained objectives in Euclidean space. Against PGDA (Shafahi et al., 2020) on universal adversarial training, FeDualEx converges faster, achieves higher validation accuracy on unattacked data, and produces dramatically sparser attacks (50.38% vs. 99.31% sparsity on CIFAR-10 CNN), whereas PGDA's averaged attack is dense and smoothed to near-zero values. The sequential deterministic version of FeDualEx matches the O(1/T) rate of CoMP (He et al., 2015) and dual extrapolation (Nesterov, 2007), while the stochastic sequential version achieves O(1/√T), matching Mishchenko et al. (2020) but extending to non-Euclidean composite settings for the first time.

## Relation to Prior Work

FeDualEx sits at the intersection of three research threads: Nesterov's dual extrapolation (an extra-step primal-dual method achieving O(1/T) rates for smooth SPP), the generalized Bregman divergence framework (Flammarion & Bach, 2017) that absorbs non-smooth regularization into the distance-generating function, and the dual-space aggregation principle from FedDualAvg (Yuan et al., 2021) that overcomes the 'curse of primal averaging.' Prior distributed SPP methods (Beznosikov et al., 2020; Hou et al., 2021) handle only smooth unconstrained objectives in Euclidean space, while federated composite optimization (Yuan et al., 2021) covers only convex problems. The paper's key technical departure is replacing the vanilla Bregman divergence inside the proximal operator with the generalized Bregman divergence, a change the authors show is necessary to prevent composite terms from accumulating and breaking the dual-extrapolation analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first convergence guarantee for federated composite saddle point optimization, a setting that naturally arises in adversarial training, GANs, and multi-agent RL where non-smooth regularization (L1 for sparsity, nuclear norm for low-rankness) is standard. The practical demonstration that dual-space aggregation preserves sparsity (0.7 vs. 0.95 non-zero ratio) while also achieving faster SPP convergence than primal-space methods validates the design choice. The main limitation is the homogeneous (i.i.d.) assumption; the authors explicitly note that extending to heterogeneous data is a significant open problem, as the quadraticity-based techniques used for federated composite convex optimization do not generalize to saddle functions whose Jacobians are not positive semidefinite. The sequential byproduct—first non-Euclidean stochastic composite SPP rate—also opens a path for single-machine composite SPP beyond the Euclidean setting.
