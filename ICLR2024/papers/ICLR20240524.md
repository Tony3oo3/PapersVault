## Problem

Embodied AI agents rely on general-purpose vision backbones like CLIP that encode abundant scene details (object textures, colors, spatial relationships) most of which are irrelevant to the specific task, introducing noise that slows convergence and degrades generalization. No prior parameter-efficient mechanism exists to selectively filter these representations to retain only task-relevant visual cues while jointly training the policy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240524/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For Object Navigation, the agent consumes RGB frames (encoded by a frozen CLIP ResNet-50 into a 2048×7×7 tensor, compressed to 32×7×7), a 32-dim goal object-type embedding, and a previous-action embedding, which are concatenated into a 1568-dim goal-conditioned vector E. For Object Displacement, the input additionally includes depth, segmentation mask, end-effector state, and a query image, yielding a 1536-dim E. The codebook compresses E into a 10-dim hidden vector h (a convex combination of 256 learnable 10-dim codes), which is upsampled back to the original dimensionality before feeding a recurrent state encoder that outputs a distribution over the discrete action space and a scalar state value estimate.

## Method

The core contribution is a task-conditioned codebook bottleneck inserted between the fused goal-conditioned embedding E and the recurrent policy network. A single-layer MLP with softmax produces a probability simplex P over K=256 learnable latent codes (each D_c=10 dimensional), and the hidden representation h = P^T C is a weighted convex combination of these codes, enforcing a severe information bottleneck (1568-dim → 10-dim → upsampled). Dropout (rate 0.1) is applied to P before the convex combination to regularize codebook usage and prevent collapse onto a few codes. The entire module is trained end-to-end with PPO (or DD-PPO for manipulation), jointly optimizing the codebook, the visual/goal/action encoders, and the policy to maximize task reward without any auxiliary objectives. Placing the codebook after goal fusion (rather than after the visual encoder alone) is critical: ablations show the bottleneck only improves performance when it conditions on the goal, enabling task-specific filtering rather than generic scene compression.

## Results

EmbCLIP-Codebook achieves state-of-the-art Success Rate across all four Object Navigation benchmarks: 73.72% on ProcTHOR-10k (vs. 67.70% EmbCLIP), 58.33% on ArchitecTHOR (vs. 55.80%), 55.00% on RoboTHOR (vs. 51.32%), and 78.40% on AI2-iTHOR (vs. 70.00%). Episode Length drops substantially (e.g., 182→136 on ProcTHOR, 121→86 on AI2-iTHOR) and trajectory curvature is halved or more (e.g., 0.58→0.23 on ProcTHOR), indicating smoother, more efficient navigation. On Object Displacement in ManipulaTHOR, m-VOLE-Codebook reaches 86.00% SR versus 81.20% for m-VOLE. The new SEL metric (Success Weighted by Episode Length) shows the largest gains, e.g., 36.00→43.69 on ProcTHOR and 21.45→26.76 on AI2-iTHOR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EmbCLIP (Khandelwal et al., CVPR 2022), the primary baseline, EmbCLIP-Codebook wins on Success Rate across all four navigation benchmarks (by 3.5–8.4 points) and on Episode Length and Curvature, but slightly trails on SPL (e.g., 48.37 vs. 49.00 on ProcTHOR, 54.39 vs. 57.10 on AI2-iTHOR), which the authors attribute to SPL's distance-based formulation not accounting for rotational actions. Against m-VOLE (Ehsani et al., ECCV 2022) on Object Displacement, m-VOLE-Codebook improves SR from 81.20% to 86.00% at the cost of a slightly longer episode (65.10 vs. 59.60). In the appendix comparison (Table 8), EmbCLIP-Codebook also outperforms SGC (Singh et al., ICCV 2023) on ArchitecTHOR (58.33% vs. 53.80% SR) and AI2-iTHOR (78.40% vs. 71.40% SR), and beats NRC (Wallingford et al., ICLR 2023) on RoboTHOR (55.00% vs. 50.10% SR), confirming consistent gains over both scene-graph and NeRF-based representation approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on EmbCLIP (Khandelwal et al., 2022), which established frozen CLIP embeddings as a strong visual backbone for embodied AI, and on m-VOLE (Ehsani et al., 2022) for object displacement. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant paradigm of learning representations through proxy objectives (contrastive losses, auxiliary tasks) or large-scale pretraining by instead inserting a tiny learnable codebook (256×10 latent codes) as a task-conditioned bottleneck, jointly optimized with the policy via PPO. The design draws on cognitive psychology's selective attention and top-down processing, and on vector quantization / information bottleneck literature, but is distinguished by being trained end-to-end with the task reward rather than pre-trained separately (as in NRC/Wallingford et al., 2023) or applied to fully-observable environments (MiniGrid, Atari).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The codebook's decoupling of visual cue extraction from decision-making has a practical consequence: when transferring to a new visual domain (e.g., ProcTHOR → Habitat), only the adaptation module needs lightweight finetuning while the policy and codebook remain frozen, yielding a 13.8% SR improvement over the non-bottlenecked baseline in this setting. This suggests a general principle for embodied agents: compressing the task-conditioned representation before the recurrent state encoder can improve both sample efficiency and cross-domain adaptability. Limitations include continued dependence on the pretrained encoder's perceptual ceiling (failure cases where small or occluded objects are missed), evaluation restricted to navigation and displacement tasks, and the codebook's interpretability remaining at the level of nearest-neighbor visualization rather than explicit semantic labels. Open questions include whether the approach extends to instruction-following or multi-step manipulation, and whether the bottleneck size (K=256, D_c=10) is optimal or task-dependent.
