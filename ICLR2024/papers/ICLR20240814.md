## Problem

Protein-protein docking remains comparatively under-investigated relative to protein-ligand docking, and existing deep learning approaches face a trade-off: one-shot regression methods (e.g., EquiDock) produce a single pose without characterizing the distribution of possible binding states, while generative models (e.g., DIFFDOCK-PP) sacrifice efficiency and stability for higher accuracy. No prior work formulates rigid protein-protein docking as an energy-based optimization problem with a differentiable energy landscape amenable to efficient sampling.

## Input / Output

The method takes two unbound protein structures (ligand and receptor) at residue level, using alpha-carbon 3D coordinates and residue type embeddings as initial node features, with the receptor held fixed and the ligand parameterized by an SE(3) transformation. It outputs the optimal SE(3) pose (rotation R ∈ SO(3) and translation t ∈ R³) as the lowest-energy point, along with a continuous energy score that serves as a confidence measure. Notably, the method can also accept an initial pose from another docking method (e.g., EquiDock) and refine it via energy minimization, functioning as a plug-in.

## Method

EBMDock constructs a differentiable energy function as a statistical potential: a modified Equiformer (EquiformerPP) with SE(3)-equivariant cross-attention extracts residue features from ligand and receptor k-NN graphs, and a Mixture Density Network predicts per-pair Gaussian mixture parameters (μ, σ, α) for the distance distribution between each ligand-receptor residue pair on the predicted binding interface. The energy of a given SE(3) pose is the mean negative log-likelihood of the actual inter-residue distances under these predicted GMMs, making the energy fully differentiable with respect to (R, t) without requiring additional neural network forward passes during sampling. Training uses contrastive divergence—minimizing the energy of the ground-truth pose while maximizing the energy of negative samples generated via short-run Langevin dynamics—combined with weighted focal loss for binding interface prediction and L2 regularization on energy values. At inference, Langevin dynamics sampling (gradient descent with Gaussian noise) iteratively refines random or externally provided initial poses toward the energy minimum, with an additional intersection loss term to prevent atom clashes, yielding a 100× speedup over traditional docking software.

## Results

On the DIPS-Het test set (100 samples), EBMDock(5) achieves a mean CRMSD of 9.09 Å and median of 7.95 Å, outperforming EquiDock (11.73/10.94) and DIFFDOCK-PP (12.09/11.87) while running ~5× faster than DIFFDOCK-PP (8.3s vs 30.9s) and ~100× faster than HDock (782s) and PatchDock (2232s). With ground-truth binding interface, EBMDock(5)-interface reaches a mean CRMSD of 2.89 Å and DockQ of 0.63, approaching HDock's 2.04 Å CRMSD and 0.88 DockQ. On DB5.5 (253 samples), EBMDock(5) achieves CRMSD 14.79 Å and DockQ 0.05, again surpassing EquiDock (17.15/0.03) and DIFFDOCK-PP (17.56/0.04). The EquiDock+plugin variant improves EquiDock's CRMSD from 11.73 to 10.16 and DockQ from 0.12 to 0.17, demonstrating EBMDock's utility as a refinement module.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EBMDock outperforms EquiDock on all three metrics (CRMSD 9.09 vs 11.73, IRMSD 8.98 vs 11.43, DockQ 0.22 vs 0.12) while remaining comparable in speed (8.3s vs 3.9s). Against DIFFDOCK-PP, EBMDock wins on CRMSD (9.09 vs 12.09) and IRMSD (8.98 vs 13.79) but trails slightly on mean DockQ (0.22 vs 0.24), though it is ~4× faster (8.3s vs 30.9s). Compared to traditional software, EBMDock is ~94× faster than HDock and ~270× faster than PatchDock, though HDock still achieves superior accuracy (CRMSD 2.04, DockQ 0.88) due to its use of templates and physics-based scoring. AlphaFold-Multimer achieves better CRMSD (6.45 vs 9.09) and DockQ (0.48 vs 0.22) but at ~234× the inference time (1940s vs 8.3s) and relies on MSA features. When given ground-truth interface, EBMDock narrows the gap to HDock substantially (CRMSD 2.89 vs 2.04, DockQ 0.63 vs 0.88), and as a plugin it improves EquiDock's CRMSD by 13% and DockQ by 42%.

## Relation to Prior Work

EBMDock departs from the two dominant deep-learning formulations of rigid protein-protein docking—regression (EquiDock, HMR) and diffusion-based generation (DIFFDOCK-PP)—by recasting the problem as energy minimization over SE(3) poses. It inherits the SE(3)-equivariant graph representation from EquiDock (Ganea et al., 2022) and the distance-likelihood statistical potential with Gaussian mixture models from DeepDock (Méndez-Lucio et al., 2021), but combines them under a contrastive-divergence training objective with Langevin dynamics sampling, a framework previously applied to generative modeling and molecular design but not to protein docking. The binding interface prediction module draws on surface-aware geometric deep learning (Sverrisson et al., 2021; Tubiana et al., 2022) to restrict energy computation to relevant residue pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first energy-based learning framework for protein-protein docking, offering a principled probabilistic view of the pose space and enabling efficient iterative refinement that can plug into existing one-shot predictors (e.g., EquiDock+plugin). The 100× speedup over traditional docking software and the low-variance, stable predictions make it practically attractive for large-scale screening. However, the large performance gap between predicted and ground-truth binding interfaces (CRMSD ~9 vs. ~2.9) reveals that interface identification remains the dominant bottleneck, and the rigid-body, residue-level assumptions limit biological fidelity. Open questions include extension to flexible and multi-protein docking, atom-level resolution, and whether the energy landscape can be further shaped to reduce spurious local minima.
