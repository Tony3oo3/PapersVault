## Problem

Integer-order continuous GNNs (GRAND, GraphCON, CDE, GREAD) model node feature evolution via first- or second-order ODEs, yielding Markovian updates that depend only on the current state and converge exponentially fast to a stationary equilibrium. This limits their ability to capture long-range memory in feature propagation and exacerbates oversmoothing in deep architectures. The paper asks whether replacing the integer-order derivative with a fractional-order Caputo derivative can introduce memory-dependent dynamics that improve graph representation learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an undirected graph with an N×d node feature matrix and an N×N edge weight matrix, evolving features along a continuous time axis t∈[0,T] via a fractional differential equation. The fractional order β∈(0,1] is a tunable hyperparameter (not a learned parameter) that controls the degree of memory in the update process. Output is the evolved feature matrix X(T) at the terminal time, decoded by a learnable head into node class labels (primary task) or graph-level predictions (secondary task on Fake-NewsNet).

## Method

FROND replaces the integer-order derivative in the continuous GNN dynamical system dX/dt = F(W, X(t)) with the Caputo fractional derivative D_t^β X(t) = F(W, X(t)), where β>0 is a real-valued order. This single modification generalizes GRAND, GRAND++, GraphCON, CDE, and GREAD into their fractional counterparts (F-GRAND, F-CDE, etc.) while preserving the original models as the β=1 special case. The paper provides a non-Markovian random walk interpretation of F-GRAND-l, showing that the walker can revisit historical positions with positive probability when β<1, and proves that convergence to the stationary distribution is algebraic (Θ(t^{-β})) rather than exponential, which analytically explains oversmoothing mitigation. Numerically, the system is solved with a fractional Adams-Bashforth-Moulton predictor (reducing to Euler when β=1), where the non-local integral kernel creates dense skip-connection-like dependencies between all prior time steps, and the full history of F(W, X^{(j)}) must be retained at each iteration.

## Results

F-GRAND-l achieves 84.8% accuracy on Cora, 98.1% on Airport, and 92.4% on Disease, consistently outperforming its integer-order counterpart GRAND-l (83.6%, 80.5%, 74.5% respectively) across nearly all 10 node classification datasets. On large heterophilic graphs, F-CDE improves over CDE by 1.4–2.0% (e.g., 93.06% vs 91.64% on Roman-empire, 82.68% vs 80.70% on Workers). The optimal fractional order β varies by dataset topology, ranging from 0.1 (Amazon-ratings) to 0.98 (Computer), with notably smaller β values (0.5–0.6) preferred for tree-structured/fractal datasets like Airport and Disease. F-GRAND-l maintains stable performance up to 128 layers with virtually no degradation, whereas GRAND-l exhibits significant performance drops, validating the theoretical slow algebraic convergence (Θ(t^{-β})) that mitigates oversmoothing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

F-GRAND-l dominates all baselines across the 10 node classification datasets. Against GCN (81.5% on Cora) and GAT (81.8%), F-GRAND-l gains 3.0–3.3 points on Cora and similar margins elsewhere. On tree-structured datasets where HGCN and GIL are specifically designed, F-GRAND-l still wins decisively: 98.1% vs 85.4% (HGCN) and 98.1% vs 91.5% (GIL) on Airport, and 92.4% vs 89.9% (HGCN) and 92.4% vs 90.8% (GIL) on Disease. The most direct comparison is against GRAND-l (β=1 special case): F-GRAND-l improves by 1.2 points on Cora, 0.6 on Citeseer, 0.6 on Pubmed, and a dramatic 17.6 points on Airport and 17.9 on Disease, confirming that the fractional memory mechanism is the source of the gain. F-GRAND-nl similarly outperforms GRAND-nl on 8 of 10 datasets (e.g., 74.7% vs 70.9% on Citeseer, 96.1% vs 90.9% on Airport). On heterophilic graphs, F-CDE beats CDE on 5 of 6 datasets (Roman-empire 93.06% vs 91.64%, Workers 82.68% vs 80.70%, Amazon-ratings 49.01% vs 47.63%), with the sole tie on Questions (75.17% for both, where β=1.0 was optimal).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continuous GNN paradigm (GRAND, GRAND++, GraphCON, CDE, GREAD), which models node feature evolution via integer-order ODEs, by replacing the integer-order derivative with the Caputo fractional derivative D_t^β for any β > 0. The framework is a strict generalization: setting β to an integer recovers the original model exactly, so all prior integer-order continuous GNNs are special cases. The paper explicitly distinguishes itself from Maskey et al. (2023), who apply fractional operators to the graph Laplacian while keeping integer-order time dynamics, and from Liu et al. (2022), who use fractional derivatives in gradient-based weight updates rather than in the feature evolution equation itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FROND framework provides a principled, unified mechanism for incorporating memory-dependent dynamics into continuous GNNs, with a clean theoretical guarantee that the non-Markovian random walk interpretation yields algebraic (rather than exponential) convergence to stationarity, directly addressing oversmoothing in deep architectures. The connection between the fractional order β and the fractal dimension of real-world graph topologies is an intriguing but still speculative direction that could guide hyperparameter selection. Open questions include whether the β–fractality relationship can be made rigorous, whether the full-memory computational cost can be further reduced for very large graphs, and whether the framework extends naturally to other graph learning tasks beyond node and graph classification.
