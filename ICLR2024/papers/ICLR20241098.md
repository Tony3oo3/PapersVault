## Problem

Implicit GNNs capture long-range dependencies by solving fixed-point equations, but they are impractical on large graphs due to two bottlenecks: full-batch training that requires loading the entire graph into GPU memory, and the large number of iterations needed to converge to the equilibrium. Existing mini-batch sampling methods for traditional GNNs cannot be directly applied because they isolate subgraphs, severing the cross-partition information flow that gives implicit GNNs their long-range advantage.

## Input / Output

The method takes an undirected graph with an adjacency matrix and node feature matrix, augments it with partition-level coarse nodes and inter-partition edges, then constructs mini-batches via PPR-based node sampling (target nodes plus top-k auxiliary nodes). The forward pass produces node representations as the (approximated) fixed-point solution of the implicit layer, which are fed to a classifier to yield node class labels. The backward pass uses implicit differentiation with phantom gradient estimation, avoiding explicit storage of intermediate hidden states.

## Method

SEIGNN introduces two key mechanisms. First, a mini-batch training scheme that partitions the graph into k subgraphs and adds one coarse node per partition, connected via coarse-original edges (each original node links to its partition's coarse node) and coarse-coarse edges (coarse nodes of adjacent partitions are linked). This preserves global and long-range information flow across mini-batches while enabling subgraph-level training. Second, a stochastic unbiased solver that truncates the Neumann series expansion of the fixed-point solution at step t, then stochastically extends the sum by sampling Bernoulli(α) variables at each subsequent step, amplifying included terms by 1/α^(i−t) to guarantee E[Ẑ*] = Z*. The backward pass employs phantom gradient estimation for efficient and stable gradient computation without inverting the Jacobian explicitly.

## Results

SEIGNN achieves the best test accuracy across all six large-graph datasets: 55.8% on Flickr, 97.8% on Reddit, 66.9% on Yelp, 98.8% on PPI, 77.9% on ogbn-arxiv, and 76.4% on ogbn-products. Compared to the strongest baseline USP, SEIGNN gains up to 1.5% absolute accuracy on Flickr and 5.2% on ogbn-arxiv. In terms of efficiency, SEIGNN reduces training time per epoch by ~8× on Reddit (6.21s vs. 52.78s for USP) and ~2.2× on ogbn-arxiv (3.79s vs. 7.05s), while also using significantly less GPU memory (e.g., 9.21 GB vs. OOM on ogbn-products).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEIGNN outperforms all nine baselines across all six datasets. Against the closest competitor USP (which also uses mini-batch training with a stochastic proximal solver), SEIGNN gains 1.5% on Flickr (55.8 vs. 54.3), 1.0% on Reddit (97.8 vs. 96.8), 0.8% on Yelp (66.9 vs. 66.1), 0.3% on PPI (98.8 vs. 98.5), 5.2% on ogbn-arxiv (77.9 vs. 72.7), and 2.8% on ogbn-products (76.4 vs. 73.6). The full-batch implicit GNNs IGNN and MGNNI are consistently outperformed by both USP and SEIGNN, with MGNNI even hitting OOM on ogbn-products. Among traditional GNNs, GraphSAINT is the strongest baseline on PPI (98.0%) but still trails SEIGNN by 0.8%; on Flickr, the best traditional GNN (GraphSAINT at 51.5%) is 4.3% below SEIGNN. The paper also shows that directly applying existing mini-batch methods (ClusterGCN, GraphSAGE) to implicit GNNs yields substantially worse results (71.31% and 68.84% on ogbn-arxiv) compared to SEIGNN's coarse-node-augmented approach (77.94%), confirming that the coarse-node design is critical for preserving long-range information under mini-batch training.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEIGNN extends the implicit GNN lineage (IGNN, MGNNI, CGS) by addressing their fundamental scalability bottleneck: full-batch training that requires loading entire graphs into GPU memory. It draws on the Neumann series equilibrium derivation from EIGNN and the PPR-based mini-batch sampling from Shadow-GNN, but departs from both by introducing coarse-level partition nodes into each mini-batch to preserve long-range information propagation that naive subgraph sampling destroys. For the backward pass, it combines implicit differentiation (DEQ) with phantom gradient estimation (Geng et al., 2021), and its stochastic solver builds on the unbiased estimation framework of USP (Li et al., 2023) while replacing the proximal solver with a Bernoulli-sampled Neumann truncation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work makes implicit GNNs practically viable on graphs with millions of nodes (e.g., ogbn-products with 2.5M nodes) where prior methods like MGNNI simply run out of memory, and it does so without sacrificing the long-range dependency capture that motivates implicit layers in the first place. The coarse-node mini-batch design is a reusable idea that could benefit other GNN architectures beyond implicit models, as demonstrated by the compatibility experiments with ClusterGCN and GraphSAGE. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other graph-level tasks (link prediction, graph classification), how the number of partitions k should be chosen in practice, and whether the stochastic solver's variance can be further reduced for tasks requiring very tight equilibrium approximations.
