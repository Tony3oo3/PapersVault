## Problem

Existing conditional 3D molecule generation methods (EDM, GEOLDM, EEGSDE) require retraining a new diffusion model or time-dependent energy function for every new property or combination of properties, making them inefficient and inflexible. Moreover, when multiple target properties conflict, these methods lack a principled mechanism to balance competing objectives. The paper asks whether one can achieve flexible, multi-objective 3D molecule generation using only off-the-shelf unconditional diffusion models and time-independent property predictors, without any additional training.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a set of target property values (e.g., specific HOMO-LUMO gap in meV, dipole moment in Debye), a pre-trained unconditional GEOLDM diffusion model, and time-independent property prediction networks trained separately on the QM9 dataset. It outputs 3D molecules represented as atom coordinates in R^{N×3} and atom-type feature vectors in R^{N×d}, decoded from the latent space via an equivariant GNN decoder. Guidance is applied only during the 'semantic stage' (steps 400–0 of a 1000-step schedule), and MC samples are drawn from a Gaussian N(ẑ₀, r_t²I) around the Tweedies posterior mean rather than using a point estimate.

## Method

MUDM reframes conditional 3D molecule generation as an inverse problem and computes the conditional score ∇log p_t(z_t|y) as a plug-in guidance term added to the pre-trained unconditional score. For single-objective guidance, it estimates the intractable p_t(y|z_t) via Monte Carlo sampling from a Gaussian q(z₀|z_t)=N(ẑ₀, r_t²I) centered at the Tweedies posterior mean, yielding a low-variance gradient of the property loss. For multi-objective guidance, it models inter-property dependencies as a directed acyclic probabilistic graph and, for each dependent property, computes a revised expected molecule z₀′ = ẑ₀ − r_t²∇ℓ_parent(ẑ₀, y_parent) (Proposition 2) before evaluating the child property's gradient. The final guidance is a weighted sum of per-property gradients evaluated at their respective (possibly revised) z₀ estimates, with weights as fixed hyperparameters. The paper proves the resulting conditional score is orthogonally equivariant when the property loss is invariant, and introduces a property checker and time-travel refinement to stabilize guidance during the early chaotic denoising steps.

## Results

On the QM9 dataset, MUDM achieves the lowest MAE across all six single-objective quantum property generation tasks, with particularly large margins on Δε (85 meV vs. 487 for EEGSDE, 587 for Cond. GeoLDM, 671 for Cond. EDM) and ε_HOMO (72 meV vs. 302/340/371). For multi-objective generation, MUDM outperforms baselines in most of the seven property-pair combinations, achieving an average ~30% reduction in MAE per property. Notably, MUDM requires no additional training beyond the pre-trained GEOLDM diffusion model and time-independent property predictors, whereas baselines require retraining a new diffusion model or time-dependent energy function for each property or property combination.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MUDM outperforms all three baselines (Conditional EDM, Conditional GeoLDM, and EEGSDE) on every single-objective task, with MAE reductions of 3–8× over the best trained baseline (EEGSDE). For example, on polarizability α, MUDM achieves 0.43 Bohr³ versus 2.37 (GeoLDM) and 2.50 (EEGSDE); on HOMO-LUMO gap Δε, MUDM reaches 85 meV versus 487 (EEGSDE). In multi-objective generation (Table 2), MUDM wins on most property pairs but shows a few exceptions: it underperforms EEGSDE on C_v when paired with μ (MAE1: 1.466 vs. 0.981) and on ε_HOMO when paired with Δε (MAE2: 489 vs. 323), suggesting that the probabilistic-graph dependency modeling is most effective when property correlations are moderate rather than very strong. The key practical advantage is that MUDM requires zero additional training for new properties or combinations, while Conditional EDM/GeoLDM need ~2 days of A100 training per property and EEGSDE requires training a time-dependent energy function per property.

## Relation to Prior Work

MUDM builds on the equivariant diffusion model lineage for 3D molecules (EDM, GEOLDM) and the inverse-problem formulation of conditional generation via score functions (DPS, Chung et al. 2022; Song et al. 2023). It departs from prior conditional 3D molecule generation methods (EDM, GEOLDM, EEGSDE), which all require additional training—either retraining the diffusion model per property or learning time-dependent energy functions—by instead combining an off-the-shelf unconditional diffusion model with time-independent property predictors through posterior approximation and Monte Carlo sampling. The multi-objective extension introduces a probabilistic graph over properties to handle dependencies and conflicts, a formulation not present in prior 3D molecule generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free paradigm eliminates the need to retrain diffusion models for each new property or property combination, making it substantially more practical for exploring diverse molecular design objectives. The probabilistic graph framework for multi-objective guidance offers a principled way to handle potentially conflicting properties, though it is currently limited to DAG structures. Key limitations include evaluation only on QM9 (small organic molecules up to 29 atoms), degraded atom stability (0.66–0.72), and the need for hand-tuned guidance weights. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger molecules, realistic drug-relevant properties (chirality, binding affinity), and cyclic property dependency graphs.
