## Problem

The dominant SFT+RLHF alignment paradigm requires massive human annotation (tens of thousands of demonstrations and millions of preference labels) and iterative online preference collection to counteract reward hacking, making it costly and non-scalable. Prior RLAIF methods like Constitutional AI use principles only to generate synthetic training data but do not condition the reward model on principles at inference time, limiting controllability and still inheriting dependency on RLHF warm-up. The paper asks whether RLAIF with an instructable reward model can fully replace RLHF to align a base language model from scratch.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241984/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a user prompt and a set of natural-language principles (e.g., 'The response should be concise and directly address the question') as joint input to the reward model, which outputs a scalar reward score. During RL training, k=3 principles are randomly sampled per prompt, with prompt-dependent ratios (e.g., higher sampling of 'Consistent Reasoning' for math prompts). The policy model outputs an aligned text response optimized via PPO against the principle-conditioned reward signal, with an additional KL penalty against the initial policy to prevent drift.

## Method

SALMON introduces an instructable reward model trained on synthetic preference data where each preference pair is explicitly paired with a human-written principle (both positive and negative formulations), enabling the model to interpret arbitrary textual preference guidelines at inference time. During RL, the reward model receives the prompt, response, and a set of principles as conditioning, so swapping principles at RL-time directly steers the reward signal without retraining. To combat reward hacking (self-praise, high-level advice, over-education), the authors craft targeted RL-time intervention principles that prohibit specific undesirable behaviors via natural language, eliminating the need for iterative online human preference collection. The full pipeline combines SELF-ALIGN (6 ICL exemplars for SFT bootstrapping) with SALMON RL (31 total principles), using PPO with QLoRA on LLaMA-2-70b to produce Dromedary-2, which outperforms LLaMA-2-Chat-70b on MT-Bench (7.4 vs. 6.9) despite using 6 demonstrations and 0 preference annotations versus 27,540 and 1,418,091 respectively.

## Results

Dromedary-2-70b achieves an MT-Bench score of 7.37 (GPT-4 judged), surpassing LLaMA-2-Chat-70b (6.88) by 0.49 points and representing the best non-distilled open-source model. On general capabilities, Dromedary-2-70b scores 51.4 on BBH Direct, 66.3 on BBH CoT, 40.6 on HumanEval P@1, and 64.3 on TydiQA GP, all substantially exceeding LLaMA-2-Chat-70b (43.1, 52.2, 35.0, 27.9 respectively). On TruthfulQA, Dromedary-2 achieves 0.84 Tru*Inf (truthful and informative), a new state-of-the-art among open-source models, compared to 0.64 for LLaMA-2-Chat-70b. On AlpacaEval, Dromedary-2 achieves an 88.32% win rate over Text-Davinci-003 using only 6 human-annotated exemplars.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dromedary-2-70b decisively outperforms LLaMA-2-Chat-70b (the strongest non-distilled open-source baseline) across all major benchmarks: MT-Bench 7.37 vs 6.88, BBH Direct 51.4 vs 43.1, HumanEval 40.6 vs 35.0, TydiQA 64.3 vs 27.9, and TruthfulQA Tru*Inf 0.84 vs 0.64. Against Guanaco-33b/65b, Dromedary-2 wins on MT-Bench (7.37 vs 6.53) and AlpacaEval (88.32% vs 71.80%). Compared to OpenAssistant (30b/33b), Dromedary-2 leads on MT-Bench (7.37 vs 6.4–6.6) and AlpacaEval (88.32% vs 66.52%). Dromedary-2 also surpasses Vicuna-33b (KD) on MT-Bench (7.37 vs 7.13), BBH (51.4 vs 41.2), and HumanEval (40.6 vs 21.1), though Vicuna slightly edges it on AlpacaEval (88.99% vs 88.32%). On HHH Eval, Dromedary-2 with helpful principles achieves 0.88 overall MC accuracy, matching LLaMA-2-Chat (0.88) and exceeding Anthropic-LM's PM (0.86) and CD (0.77). Notably, Dromedary-2 slightly underperforms the LLaMA-2-70b base model on BBH Direct (51.4 vs 53.1), suggesting alignment training introduces a small reasoning regression, while it marginally beats the base on TydiQA (64.3 vs 63.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

SALMON extends the RLHF training framework (Ouyang et al., 2022) by replacing the fixed reward model with an instructable one whose scoring is conditioned on human-defined principles at inference time. It builds directly on SELF-ALIGN (Sun et al., 2023b) for the SFT initialization stage and borrows the principle-driven synthetic preference collection from Constitutional AI (Bai et al., 2022b), but departs critically: in CAI, principles are used only to generate training data for a fixed reward model, whereas SALMON makes the reward model itself instruction-following, enabling dynamic preference steering during RL without retraining. This positions the work as the first demonstration that RLAIF can fully replace RLHF from scratch for general alignment (helpfulness, honesty, harmlessness), rather than merely augmenting an already-RLHF-trained model's safety.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic supervision-efficiency result—6 in-context exemplars and 31 principles outperforming 27,540 demonstrations and 1.4M preference annotations—reframes what is practically feasible for aligning large models and opens a path toward scalable oversight as future models exceed human evaluative capacity. The instructable reward model introduces a new, interpretable axis of control (natural-language principles) that can be adjusted to combat reward hacking, emphasize specific HHH dimensions, or reduce false refusals without collecting fresh human data. Key open questions include the non-triviality of principle design, the need for context-dependent principle selection, and the model's residual hallucination and reliability issues that the current paradigm does not address.
