## Problem

The fastest-known algorithm for sampling from log-concave distributions over polytopes (Mangoubi & Vishnoi 2022) requires O(md^{ω-1}) arithmetic operations per Markov chain step due to dense matrix inversion and determinant computation of the regularized log-barrier Hessian. This paper closes the gap between the per-step cost and the inherent sparsity of the polytope constraints by showing the Hessian changes slowly enough to exploit fast linear solvers from the interior-point literature.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a polytope K defined by m linear inequalities (A ∈ R^{m×d}, b ∈ R^m), an oracle returning values of a convex function f (either L-Lipschitz or β-smooth), a w-warm start point in Int(K), and parameters (δ, R, L or β). It outputs a single sample from π(θ) ∝ e^{-f(θ)} on K within total variation error δ. The per-step cost is O(nnz(A) + d²) arithmetic operations plus T_f for the f-oracle, compared to the prior O(md^{ω-1}) + T_f, with the number of steps unchanged at Õ((md + dL²R²) log(w/δ)).

## Method

The paper implements the soft-threshold Dikin walk with three key technical ingredients. First, it proves a Frobenius-norm self-concordance bound showing the regularized log-barrier Hessian Φ(θ) = α^{-1}A^⊤S(θ)^{-2}A + η^{-1}I_d changes slowly (‖(Φ(z)−Φ(θ))Φ(θ)^{-1}‖_F = O(1) w.h.p.) at each walk step, by reducing to the unregularized log-barrier case via a limiting argument. Second, it deploys the Lee & Sidford (2015) efficient inverse maintenance algorithm to maintain a linear system solver for Φ(θ) in O(nnz(A) + d²) per step, replacing the dense O(md^{ω-1}) matrix inversion. Third, it computes a randomized estimate for the determinantal term in the Metropolis acceptance rule by sampling Y_j with mean log det Φ(z) − log det Φ(θ) via the maintained solver, then plugging these into a piecewise-polynomial (sigmoid) series expansion—using a Taylor series at 0 for small values and an exponential series at +∞ for large values—to obtain an unbiased estimate of a smoothed acceptance probability that preserves the target stationary distribution.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 2.1) reduces the per-iteration cost of the soft-threshold Dikin walk from O(md^{ω-1}) to O(nnz(A) + d²) arithmetic operations, while keeping the number of Markov chain steps unchanged. In the regime m, nnz(A) = O(d²), L, r = O(1), R = O(d), T_f = O(d²), the total runtime improves from Õ(d^{5+ω}) (Mangoubi & Vishnoi 2022) to Õ(d⁶). In the Bayesian Lasso logistic regression setting (m = O(d), r = O(1/√d), β = L = O(d), R = O(1)), the runtime is Õ(d⁴), improving by a factor of d^{ω-2} over the prior best bound of Õ(d^{2+ω}).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper's method strictly dominates the Soft-Threshold Dikin Walk of Mangoubi & Vishnoi (2022) in per-iteration cost, reducing it from O(md^{ω-1}) to O(nnz(A) + d²) while preserving the same number of iterations Õ(md + dL²R²) or Õ(md + dβR²). Against the Dikin Walk of Narayanan & Rakhlin (2017), this paper matches the iteration count for the Lipschitz/smooth setting but improves per-iteration cost by the same factor, yielding a total improvement of d^{ω-2} (or d^{ω-1} when A is O(m)-sparse and m ≥ d²). Compared to Hit-and-run (Lovász & Vempala 2006), this paper has higher per-iteration cost (O(nnz(A)+d²) vs. O(md)) but fewer iterations in the Lipschitz/smooth regime, resulting in a net improvement of min((R/r)², d/(r²L²)) when T_f = Ω(d²) and m = O(d). Against Proximal Langevin MC (Brosse et al. 2017), this paper achieves a dramatic improvement in total runtime (e.g., Õ(d⁶) vs. Õ(d^{10+ω}δ^{-6}) in the first regime), as the Langevin method requires far more iterations and also needs gradient oracle access (T_{∇f}) rather than just function values (T_f).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the soft-threshold Dikin walk of Mangoubi & Vishnoi (2022), which itself builds on the Dikin walk of Kannan & Narayanan (2012) and Narayanan & Rakhlin (2017). The key technical departure is showing that the soft-threshold regularized log-barrier Hessian—unlike the unregularized log-barrier Hessian studied by Laddha et al. (2020)—also satisfies a strong self-concordance property in Frobenius norm, enabling the deployment of efficient inverse maintenance solvers from the interior-point literature (Karmarkar 1984; Lee & Sidford 2015). A second departure is the development of a randomized Taylor-series-based estimator for the determinantal term in the Metropolis acceptance rule, which replaces the expensive dense matrix determinant computation with O(nnz(A) + d²) operations per step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The per-iteration improvement from O(md^{ω-1}) to O(nnz(A) + d²) yields a factor of at least d^{ω-2} speedup (and d^{ω-1} for sparse polytopes with m ≥ d²), which is practically significant for Bayesian inference, differentially private optimization, and integration over high-dimensional polytopes. Two open questions are highlighted: whether the d² term in the per-step cost can be eliminated with a different fast linear solver, and whether the overall sampling runtime (number of steps × per-step cost) can be brought down to match the near-matrix-multiplication-time bounds now known for linear programming. The paper is purely theoretical with no empirical validation, so practical gains depend on the constants hidden in the tilde-O notation.
