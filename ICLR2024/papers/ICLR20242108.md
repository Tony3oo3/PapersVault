## Problem

Existing graph-level anomaly detection methods operate exclusively from spatial perspectives (node features, topological pooling) and have never provided spectral analysis of anomalous graphs. This paper identifies that the Rayleigh Quotient of the graph Laplacian exhibits statistically distinct distributions between normal and anomalous graphs, yet this spectral signal has been entirely unexplored for graph-level anomaly detection, leaving both the framework design unexplainable and performance unsatisfying.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of molecular graphs, each specified by an adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{n×n} (binary, undirected) and a node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×F} (one-hot encoded atom types), with graph sizes ranging from ~22 to ~262 nodes across the 10 TUDataset benchmarks. It produces a binary graph-level prediction (active vs. inactive compound) and a combined graph embedding formed by concatenating the explicit Rayleigh Quotient representation and the implicit Chebyshev wavelet representation, followed by a final MLP. The class-balanced focal loss is applied at the graph level to handle the severe class imbalance (anomalous ratios of 4–8%).

## Method

RQGNN combines two complementary spectral components. The Rayleigh Quotient Learning (RQL) component explicitly computes the per-node Rayleigh Quotient RQ(X,L) = diag(X̃ᵀLX̃ / X̃ᵀX̃) after a two-layer MLP feature transform, then maps it through another MLP to produce a graph-level representation. The Chebyshev Wavelet GNN with RQ-pooling (CWGNN-RQ) implicitly captures spectral information by applying q=4 Chebyshev-approximated wavelet filters (each capturing iK-hop spectral bands) to the transformed node features, then pools node representations using the Rayleigh Quotient values as attention weights (RQ-pooling) instead of uniform averaging. The key theoretical insight (Proposition 1) proves that the accumulated high-frequency spectral energy of the graph Laplacian equals the Rayleigh Quotient, justifying both the explicit RQ computation and the spectral GNN design. Training uses a class-balanced focal loss that reweights each sample by the inverse of the expected number η(n_y) = (1−β^{n_y})/(1−β) to counteract the extreme class imbalance.

## Results

On 10 real-world molecular graph datasets (TUDataset), RQGNN achieves an average AUC of 0.8710 and average Macro-F1 of 0.7397, outperforming the strongest baseline iGAD by 1.44% in AUC and 6.74% in Macro-F1. The best individual AUC is 0.9023 on P388 and the best Macro-F1 is 0.7963 on P388. RQGNN also generalizes beyond the biochemistry domain, achieving 0.9378 AUC / 0.7640 F1 on COLORS-3 and 0.8808 AUC / 0.7709 F1 on DBLP_v1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RQGNN outperforms all 10 baselines on all 10 datasets in both AUC and Macro-F1. Against spectral GNNs, the gains are largest: RQGNN leads ChebyNet by 20.72% in average AUC and 24.40% in average F1, and leads BernNet by 25.28% in AUC and 25.33% in F1, showing that simple spectral filters without wavelet decomposition and RQ-pooling cannot capture anomalous spectral properties. Against graph classification models (GMT, Gmixup, TVGNN), RQGNN leads by 8.38–20.59% in AUC and 19.67–25.48% in F1, confirming that standard classification frameworks fail on imbalanced anomaly detection. Among dedicated anomaly detection baselines, OCGIN, OCGTL, GLocalKD, and HimNet (which use one-class classification) are outperformed by 19.78–34.82% in AUC. The closest competitor is iGAD (NeurIPS 2022), which incorporates substructure-aware random walk kernels; RQGNN still leads iGAD by 1.44% in average AUC and 6.74% in average Macro-F1, with the F1 gap driven by the explicit Rayleigh Quotient learning component and RQ-pooling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the spatial GNN paradigm that dominates graph-level anomaly detection (OCGIN, OCGTL, GLocalKD, HimNet, iGAD) by introducing spectral analysis as the first spectral GNN framework for this task. It builds on the graph wavelet convolution framework of Hammond et al. (2011) and the Chebyshev polynomial approximation from ChebyNet (Defferrard et al., 2016), but repurposes them through a novel RQ-pooling mechanism that uses the Rayleigh Quotient as node importance weights. The class-balanced focal loss adapts the expected-number reweighting from Cui et al. (2019) and focal loss from Lin et al. (2017) to the graph anomaly detection setting. The key conceptual departure is the theoretical proof that accumulated spectral energy equals the Rayleigh Quotient, providing a principled bridge between a classical mathematical quantity and a learnable graph representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that spectral properties (specifically the Rayleigh Quotient distribution) carry discriminative signal for graph-level anomalies that spatial methods miss, offering an explainable and theoretically grounded design principle for future spectral GNNs in anomaly detection. However, the evaluation is concentrated on molecular graph datasets from TUDataset, and generalization to other graph domains (social networks, biological networks) remains underexplored despite brief experiments on COLORS-3 and DBLP_v1. Open questions include whether the Rayleigh Quotient distributional gap holds for structurally different graph types, whether the spectral perspective complements or subsumes substructure-based approaches like iGAD, and whether the framework extends to multi-class or semi-supervised anomaly detection settings.
