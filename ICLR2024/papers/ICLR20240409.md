## Problem

Diffusion models inherently perform multi-task learning across thousands of denoising tasks (one per timestep), yet prior work has only addressed this from an optimization perspective (loss weighting, task clustering) while leaving the architectural design of MTL in diffusion models unexplored. The paper argues that implicit timestep conditioning alone is insufficient to mitigate negative transfer between conflicting denoising tasks, and that explicit architectural mechanisms are needed to establish task-specific information pathways.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240409/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on noisy latent representations (32×32×4 from a Stable Diffusion VAE for DiT experiments, or pixel-space for ADM) at a given timestep t, optionally conditioned on a class label (ImageNet) or CLIP text embeddings (MS-COCO), and outputs a noise prediction ε_θ(x_t, t). The routing mask is a fixed, precomputed binary tensor of shape T×C (one row per timestep, one column per channel) that is looked up at inference and training time based on the input timestep, requiring no learned parameters. Experiments use 256×256 images with T=1000 training timesteps and 250-step DDPM sampling.

## Method

DTR applies task-specific binary channel masks at the input of each residual block (or reformulated DiT block), creating distinct information pathways per denoising task without adding parameters. The mask is constructed via a sliding window of size C_β = ⌊βC⌋ channels that shifts across the channel dimension as timestep t increases, with the shift position quantized as ⌊(C−C_β)·((t−1)/T)^α⌋. The hyperparameter α > 1 biases the window toward higher timesteps (allocating more task-dedicated channels where global structure is learned), while β controls the trade-off between task-specific and shared channels (β=0.8 used throughout). This design encodes two diffusion priors: Task Affinity (adjacent timesteps share overlapping channels via the sliding window) and Task Weights (higher timesteps receive more dedicated capacity via the α exponent). For DiT, the standard block is reformulated so that the mask is applied to the input before attention and MLP, with a complementary skip connection (1−m)⊙z added at the output to preserve unmasked channels.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, DiT+DTR reduces FID from 12.59 (DiT baseline) to 8.90, while improving IS from 134.60 to 156.48 and Precision from 0.73 to 0.77. On FFHQ 256×256 unconditional generation, FID drops from 10.99 to 7.32. On MS-COCO text-to-image, FID improves from 7.70 to 7.04. All gains are achieved with zero additional parameters and negligible computational overhead (e.g., DiT-L/2 goes from 80.71 to 80.73 GFLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DTR consistently outperforms the DiT baseline across all three datasets and all metrics (FID, IS, Precision, Recall), with the largest gain on ImageNet where FID drops by 3.69 points (12.59→8.90). Naive random task routing (R-TR) actually degrades performance substantially (ImageNet FID worsens to 16.18), confirming that ignoring task affinity is harmful. Against the multi-experts strategy (DiT-B/2×4, 521M params, FID 9.48), DTR with a single DiT-L/2 (458M params) achieves a better FID of 8.90, demonstrating that explicit task routing in one model beats parameter-separated experts. Compared to MaxRoaming (Pascal et al., 2021), which optimizes random channel masks, DTR is dramatically superior (FFHQ FID 7.32 vs. 39.90), as MaxRoaming's randomness conflicts with the structured task affinity in diffusion. With extended training (2M iterations + ANT-UW loss weighting), DiT-L/2+DTR reaches FID 2.33, matching DiT-XL/2 (FID 2.27) trained for 7M iterations with 48% fewer parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the task routing framework (Strezoski et al., 2019; Ding et al., 2023) from general multi-task learning into the diffusion model setting, where each denoising timestep is treated as a distinct task. Prior work on MTL in diffusion models (Hang et al., 2023; Go et al., 2023a) addressed negative transfer exclusively through optimization techniques (loss weighting, task clustering), while the paper's contribution is the first architectural intervention: a structured sliding-window channel mask that encodes two diffusion-specific priors—task affinity between adjacent timesteps and higher capacity allocation to early (high-noise) timesteps. The method is validated on both UNet-based (ADM) and Transformer-based (DiT) architectures, positioning it as an architecture-agnostic add-on rather than a new model family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is notable: DiT-L + DTR + ANT-UW matches DiT-XL performance (FID 2.33 vs. 2.27) while using 32% fewer parameters and 71% fewer training iterations (2M vs. 7M), a result that could reduce compute costs for practitioners training large diffusion models. The CKA analysis provides mechanistic evidence that DTR induces the intended representation structure (timestep-specific features at high timesteps, shared features at low timesteps, diagonal affinity in later blocks), lending interpretability beyond black-box metric improvements. Key limitations include the fixed, non-learnable masks (the authors suggest RL or evolutionary optimization as future work) and the fact that the method has only been validated at 256×256 resolution, leaving open whether the structured routing generalizes to higher resolutions or video generation.
