## Problem

Existing manipulation concept discovery methods (e.g., LLM+CLIP, human-annotated key states) rely on linguistic semantics or manual annotation to define sub-goals, lacking a principled, self-supervised mechanism to autonomously identify which physical states constitute meaningful manipulation concepts. The paper argues that the decision to conceptualize a physical procedure should depend on the informativeness of its representation for low-level physical states and state changes, not on how we name it.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled state-action trajectories (RGB image states with continuous actions) from ManiSkill2, using 500 training trajectories per task with segments of 60 timesteps per batch item. It produces up to 10 discrete manipulation concepts per task (codebook vectors in a VQ-VAE), key states at segment boundaries (the last state of each sub-trajectory), and a consistent partitioning of all trajectories into semantically meaningful sub-trajectories. The discovered key states are then fed into a CoTPC policy for downstream manipulation, and the method also outputs a Human Intuition Similarity (HIS) score measuring temporal alignment with ground-truth key states.

## Method

InfoCon adapts a VQ-VAE with an autoregressive transformer encoder that maps state-action sequences to latents, which are assigned to K trainable concept vectors via cosine similarity with a temperature-scaled softmax. The core novelty is a dual informativeness objective: (1) a generative goal loss that trains a predictor to synthesize the key (end) state of a sub-trajectory given the current state and assigned concept, and (2) a discriminative goal loss comprising a hyper-network-generated compatibility function (binary classification of whether a state falls within the concept's process) and an actionable informativeness term that maximizes mutual information between the gradient of the compatibility function and the next action. A custom time-step embedding preserves the spherical geometry of normalized latents while encoding absolute time at trajectory boundaries, and a reconstruction regularization with entropy loss pretrains the codebook to avoid over-smoothing before the full generative/discriminative losses are activated.

## Results

On four ManiSkill2 manipulation tasks, InfoCon achieves success rates of 96.6% (P&P Cube, seen), 63.0% (Stack Cube, seen), 53.8% (Turn Faucet, seen env + seen faucet), and 63.6% (Peg Insertion, seen), matching or exceeding the oracle GT Key States baseline on 7 of 9 evaluation settings. On unseen environments, InfoCon delivers 81.0% (P&P Cube), 47.0% (Stack Cube), and 17.8% (Peg Insertion), consistently outperforming all non-oracle baselines. The Human Intuition Similarity (HIS) scores (2.60, 8.98, 18.03, 9.05) confirm that discovered key states align closely with human-annotated ones, far better than LLM+CLIP (57.51–164.29).

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfoCon outperforms the oracle GT Key States on P&P Cube (96.6 vs 75.2 seen; 81.0 vs 70.0 unseen), Stack Cube (63.0 vs 58.8 seen; 47.0 vs 46.0 unseen), and Peg Insertion (63.6 vs 52.8 seen; 17.8 vs 16.8 unseen), while GT retains a narrow edge on Turn Faucet (56.4 vs 53.8 seen; 31.0 vs 17.8 unseen-unseen faucet). Against AWE, InfoCon achieves substantially higher success rates on Stack Cube (63.0 vs 45.4 seen; 47.0 vs 18.0 unseen) and Peg Insertion (63.6 vs 31.6 seen; 17.8 vs 4.3 unseen), with comparable performance on P&P Cube and Turn Faucet. LLM+CLIP is surpassed on all tasks except a near-tie on Turn Faucet unseen-unseen faucet (17.8 vs 18.0), with the largest gaps on Stack Cube unseen (47.0 vs 21.0) and Turn Faucet seen (53.8 vs 35.8). Decision Transformer and Last State baselines are significantly outperformed across all settings, confirming that self-discovered key states provide meaningful guidance beyond simple end-state or sequence-modeling approaches. Notably, InfoCon is the only non-oracle method to exceed 60% on both Stack Cube and Peg Insertion in seen environments, and the only method to reach 81% on P&P Cube unseen.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the VQ-VAE architecture (Van Den Oord et al., 2017) for its discrete codebook, gradient-preserving assignment, and EMA update mechanisms, and on CoTPC (Jia et al., 2023) for the downstream concept-guided policy training framework. It departs from LLM-based subgoal discovery (Di Palo et al., 2023) and geometric waypoint methods (AWE, Shi et al., 2023) by formulating manipulation concepts through an information-theoretic lens: a concept must simultaneously serve as a generative goal (predicting the end state of its sub-trajectory) and a discriminative goal (scoring state compatibility and informing the next action via gradient). This dual framing goes beyond prior self-supervised skill discovery methods that rely on single-signal criteria such as mutual information maximization, change-point detection, or linear-motion approximations, positioning InfoCon as a more principled and multi-faceted characterization of what makes a manipulation concept meaningful.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

InfoCon demonstrates that fully self-supervised concept discovery from unlabeled demonstrations can match oracle (human-annotated) key states in policy performance across four manipulation tasks, substantially reducing the annotation burden that plagues current hierarchical manipulation pipelines. The finding that Human Intuition Similarity (HIS) correlates only weakly with downstream policy success is a notable observation: it suggests that data-driven concepts may capture physically useful structure that human linguistic semantics do not fully endorse, motivating further investigation into what makes a concept 'good' beyond human alignment. Key limitations include evaluation on only four ManiSkill2 tasks with a fixed codebook size of 10, no exploration of hierarchical or compositional relationships between discovered concepts, and reliance on the CoTPC policy architecture for downstream evaluation. Open questions include whether the generative/discriminative informativeness framework scales to longer-horizon or multi-object tasks, and whether discovered concepts can be composed or related to form richer task structures.
