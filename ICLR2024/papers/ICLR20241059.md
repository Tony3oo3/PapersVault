## Problem

Long-term novelty-based exploration in deep RL is hampered by a fundamental tension: parametric density estimators suffer from catastrophic forgetting and slow adaptation, while non-parametric episodic memories (e.g., NGU's slot-based memory) are reset every episode and cannot track visitation over thousands of episodes. Existing methods that combine short-term and long-term novelty signals (like NGU) add architectural complexity and inherit the encoding deficiencies of their underlying representation (e.g., RND's sensitivity to uncontrollable noise). The paper asks whether a single, unified non-parametric mechanism can provide robust long-term novelty estimation that scales with increasingly powerful learned representations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB observations (210×160 greyscale frames for Atari; first-person 3D views for DM-HARD-8) along with the agent's action history, producing a scalar intrinsic reward inversely proportional to the estimated soft-visitation count of the current state embedding. The embedding function f_θ maps each observation to a D-dimensional vector, which is then queried against a fixed-size memory of cluster atoms (5×10⁴ for Atari, 2×10⁵ for DM-HARD-8). The intrinsic reward is normalized by a running standard deviation estimate and added to the extrinsic reward to train the policy, while the policy itself outputs a discrete action (Atari) or continuous action (DM-HARD-8).

## Method

RECODE replaces NGU's per-episode slot memory with a persistent, fixed-size set of 'atoms' (cluster centers) each carrying a visitation count, updated via a DP-means-inspired streaming rule: new embeddings are either assigned to the nearest atom (updating its position as a count-weighted convex combination) or, if sufficiently far away and a stochastic coin-flip succeeds, inserted as a new atom while evicting a low-count one (with probability ∝ 1/c²) and redistributing its count to its nearest neighbor. Soft visitation counts are computed as a kernel-weighted sum over all atoms within an adaptive distance threshold d_ema, avoiding the k-NN displacement pathology. A per-step count discount γ handles non-stationarity from concurrent representation learning, and an exponential moving average of k-NN distances adapts the bandwidth. For representation learning, CASM extends inverse dynamics by feeding a causally-masked transformer randomly masked sequences of actions and observation embeddings, forcing the encoder to build redundant, trajectory-level representations that suppress uncontrollable features; the final embedding fed to RECODE is the transformer input f_θ(o_t) to avoid trajectory leakage.

## Results

On the Atari hard-exploration suite, MEME-RECODE-AP achieves a mean Human Normalized Score (HNS) of 715.5 (95% CI: 606.1–833.9) versus 543.6 for MEME-NGU-AP (p=0.0185), with a mean capped HNS of 100 vs. 95.7 (p=0.0419). RECODE is the first agent to reach Pitfall!'s end screen (77,737 vs. 44,948, p=0.0043). On DM-HARD-8, MEME-RECODE-CASM achieves superhuman performance (HNS > 100) in 6 out of 8 tasks, including the first superhuman result on Push Blocks (4.06 vs. human 8.40), setting a new state-of-the-art on this benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NGU (Badia et al., 2020), RECODE achieves a statistically significant improvement in aggregate Atari performance (Mean HNS 715.5 vs. 543.6, p=0.0185) and wins decisively on Pitfall! (77,737 vs. 44,948, p=0.0043) and Q*Bert (182,638 vs. 57,751, p=0.0465), though it loses on Hero (141,638 vs. 187,244, p=0.0465). On DM-HARD-8, RECODE-CASM substantially outperforms NGU-CASM, achieving superhuman scores in 6/8 tasks versus 0/8 for NGU-CASM, with the largest gaps on Push Blocks (4.06 vs. 0.24) and Throw Across (9.72 vs. 0.00). Against BYOL-Explore (Guo et al., 2022) on DM-HARD-8, RECODE-CASM matches or exceeds performance on 6 of 8 tasks, including the first superhuman result on Push Blocks. On the noisy Montezuma's Revenge ablation, RECODE maintains near-identical performance to its noise-free setting while NGU degrades significantly, confirming RECODE's robustness to uncontrollable observation noise. Against Q*bert (Chrabaszcz et al., 2018) on Atari, RECODE achieves higher final scores across all eight hard-exploration games, with particularly large margins on Pitfall! and Q*Bert.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper decomposes novelty-based exploration into two sub-problems—representation learning and visitation count estimation—and addresses each independently. For counting, RECODE adapts the DP-means clustering algorithm (Kulis & Jordan, 2011) to the streaming, non-stationary RL setting, replacing NGU's two-mechanism design (episodic memory + RND) with a single clustering-based memory that naturally spans both short- and long-term horizons. For representation, CASM extends the 1-step inverse dynamics loss of Pathak et al. (2017) by introducing masked multi-step prediction with a causal transformer, drawing on the masked language model paradigm (Devlin et al., 2018) to encourage encoding of higher-level, trajectory-level features while suppressing uncontrollable observation noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical takeaway is that exploration performance is bottlenecked by representation quality rather than the novelty estimation mechanism itself, and that a single non-parametric clustering approach can replace the fragile two-signal design of NGU while being robust to observation noise that degrades RND-based methods. The first solution of Pitfall! and superhuman performance on 6/10 DM-HARD-8 tasks demonstrate the approach's effectiveness on long-horizon, partially observable tasks. However, the method remains dependent on a suitable representation (CASM's controllability prior may not transfer to real-world domains), concurrent training constrains the effective memory horizon via the discount factor, and the paper acknowledges that more complex procedural generation and truly open-ended environments remain untested.
