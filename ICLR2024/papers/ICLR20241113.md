## Problem

Discrete generative models for protein design face a fundamental tension: energy-based models are hard to train and sample from due to training instabilities and slow mixing, while discrete diffusion models require multiple noise levels, complex noise schedules, and are inefficient for the small, high-entropy antibody datasets typical in therapeutic discovery. No existing non-autoregressive method efficiently produces novel, diverse, and biophysically valid antibody sequences from limited training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241113/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For unconditional discovery, the model starts from random Gaussian noise in a 6237-dimensional space (297 aligned positions × 21 one-hot tokens covering 20 amino acids plus a gap token); for constrained design, a starting antibody sequence with a binary projection matrix fixes conserved regions while freeing others to vary. Antibody heavy and light chains are aligned via ANARCI to fixed lengths of 149 and 148 positions respectively, with gap tokens encoding insertions/deletions. The output is a discrete one-hot antibody sequence recovered by argmax over the denoised continuous sample, yielding variable-length proteins that respect the natural length distribution.

## Method

Discrete Walk-Jump Sampling (dWJS) smooths discrete one-hot sequences with isotropic Gaussian noise at a single level σ (≈0.5), then decouples two independently trained components: an energy-based model (3-layer Conv1D CNN) trained via contrastive divergence on the smoothed distribution drives Langevin MCMC 'walk' steps, while a 35-layer ByteNet denoiser trained with a denoising autoencoder objective performs the one-step 'jump' back to the discrete manifold. This decoupling eliminates the need for noise schedules, replay buffers, ℓ₂ penalties, and rejection sampling that plague standard EBM and diffusion training, reducing the entire hyperparameter space to a single σ. The critical noise level σ_c is derived geometrically from pairwise distances in the data, and the method supports both unconditional sampling (discovery) and constrained sampling with fixed-position projection (design). A novel Distributional Conformity Score (DCS) based on conformal transducers and joint KDE over biophysical properties provides a sample-to-distribution quality metric analogous to FID for protein generation.

## Results

On the functional antibody design task (hu4D5 CDR H3 redesign for HER2 binding), dWJS (energy-based) achieves a 70% in vitro binding rate (total_bind) and 96% predicted binding probability (p_bind), substantially outperforming AbDiffuser (22% binders, 57% after post-hoc filtering) and LaMBO-2 (25% binders). On the distribution learning task (paired OAS), dWJS (score-based) achieves the best DCS of 0.49 and lowest W_property of 0.065, while dWJS (energy-based) achieves the best W_property of 0.056 and 100% uniqueness. In wet-lab expression, 97–100% of dWJS-generated antibodies are successfully expressed and purified, compared to 42% for a standard EBM baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

dWJS outperforms all diffusion-based baselines on the functional design task: it achieves 70% in vitro binding versus 25% for LaMBO-2 and 22% (57% after post-hoc filtering) for AbDiffuser, while requiring no post-hoc filtering. On in silico distribution learning (Table 2), dWJS (energy-based) achieves the lowest W_property (0.056) and highest uniqueness (1.0), while dWJS (score-based) achieves the best DCS (0.49) and highest IntDiv (65.1) among non-language-model methods. IgLM (Shuai et al., 2021) achieves a slightly higher DCS (0.533) but at the cost of very low internal diversity (IntDiv 34.6) and 43× slower autoregressive sampling. ESM2 (Lin et al., 2023) performs poorly in ab initio generation (DCS 0.061, W_property 0.15), producing repetitive, non-antibody-like sequences despite high edit-distance scores. GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 are outperformed on both W_property and DCS, with GPT-3.5 also showing only 66% uniqueness. DEEN (Saremi et al., 2018) is outperformed on W_property (0.087 vs 0.056), edit distance (50.9 vs 58.4), and internal diversity (42.7 vs 55.3), confirming that decoupling the walk and jump steps with separately trained models is beneficial.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Neural Empirical Bayes (NEB) formalism of Saremi & Hyvärinen (2019) to discrete data for the first time, decoupling the Langevin MCMC 'walk' step (parameterized by a contrastive-divergence-trained energy-based model) from the 'jump' denoising step (parameterized by a separately trained score-based network). It departs from discrete diffusion models (SeqVDM, AbDiffuser, LaMBO-2) by requiring only a single noise level rather than a multi-scale noise schedule, and from traditional EBM training by eliminating replay buffers, ℓ₂ penalties, and rejection sampling. The Distributional Conformity Score is a new evaluation metric that fills a gap analogous to FID in image generation, building on conformal prediction theory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 70% first-round binding rate for trastuzumab CDR H3 redesign and 97–100% expression rate demonstrate that a single-noise-level, decoupled walk-jump framework can match or exceed multi-scale diffusion methods in a high-stakes wet-lab setting, while being substantially simpler to train (one free hyperparameter σ) and faster to sample. The DCS provides the field a principled, sample-to-distribution quality metric that correlates with in-vitro success, addressing a long-standing evaluation gap in protein generation. Open questions include whether the single-noise-level sufficiency generalizes beyond antibodies to other protein classes or modalities (e.g., images), and whether the fast-mixing MCMC property holds at larger sequence lengths or more complex energy landscapes.
