## Problem

Jiang et al. (2020) empirically showed that a dozen or more proposed generalization bounds for neural networks are not uniformly tight, but no formal explanation existed for why. This paper asks whether uniformly tight generalization bounds are mathematically possible in the overparameterized setting at all, and provides impossibility theorems showing they are not for algorithm-independent bounds, and are fundamentally limited for algorithm-dependent ones.

## Input / Output

The theoretical framework takes a hypothesis class H (e.g., a VC class of dimension d, or linear functionals over the finite field F_q^d), a finite collection of H-realizable distributions, a learning algorithm, and a training set of size n, and produces impossibility theorems quantified as lower bounds on the estimation error ε that any estimator must incur. The key quantitative result is that for n ≤ d/2, no estimator achieves ε < 1/8 − o(1) for at least 49% of (algorithm, distribution) pairs, and for multiclass classification with q=11 labels, even algorithm-dependent estimators are limited to accuracy ≈ 0.45.

## Method

The paper introduces the notion of estimability — the existence of a function E(h, S) predicting population loss from the hypothesis and training set — and proves its equivalence (up to a factor of 2) to the existence of uniformly tight generalization bounds. Theorem 1/2 establishes non-estimability by constructing a 'Bayes-like Random ERM' distribution over ERM algorithms and showing that the joint distribution of (S, true labeling) equals the joint distribution of (S, random ERM output), so the estimator must simultaneously predict zero loss and high loss on the same sample, yielding a contradiction. Theorem 3 extends the analysis to algorithm-dependent bounds via a coupling argument: if samples from two distribution families are close in total variation distance, the estimator must produce similar outputs, but the true losses differ by at least α − ε, forcing estimation failure on a non-negligible fraction of inputs. Quantitative applications to linear functionals over finite fields (parity functions for q=2, 11-class classification for q=11) show that even with complete knowledge of the algorithm, estimation accuracy is bounded by roughly 0.45, barely better than a random guess.

## Results

The paper establishes impossibility theorems for generalization bounds in the overparameterized setting. Theorem 2 shows that for any hypothesis class of VC dimension d with sample size n ≤ d/2, no estimator can achieve accuracy better than ε ≥ (d−n)/(4d) − o(1) (i.e., at least 1/8 − o(1) when n = d/2) for at least 50% of (algorithm, distribution) pairs. Theorem 4 shows that for multiclass classification with q = 11 labels (comparable to MNIST/CIFAR), algorithms with linear bias are not (0.45, 0.4, n)-estimable — barely better than a random guess. Theorem 5 strengthens this for parity functions (q = 2), showing non-estimability at (0.25, 0.14, n) for randomized and (0.25, 0.32, n) for deterministic algorithms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper positions its impossibility results relative to two prior works that also show vacuous generalization bounds: Nagarajan & Kolter (2019c) and Bartlett & Long (2021). The authors argue their results are strictly more general and stronger: prior work only covers uniform-convergence-based bounds for a single linear classifier (one neuron) under Gaussian distributions, while this paper covers any type of algorithm-dependent bound, many algorithms, and any architecture in the overparameterized setting using the same distribution across all sample sizes. Regarding specific bounds in the literature (VC bounds, Rademacher bounds, norm-based bounds, PAC-Bayes, sharpness measures), the authors take an expansive position that all fall within the scope of Theorem 2 and are therefore not uniformly tight, though they acknowledge this is a matter of scholarly debate. The paper does not propose a new bound to compare against existing ones; rather, it establishes fundamental limits that constrain what any such bound can achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides a mathematical explanation for the empirical failures documented by Jiang et al. (2020), who showed that over a dozen proposed generalization bounds (VC, Rademacher, PAC-Bayes, margin, sharpness-based) are not tight for large neural networks. It generalizes and strengthens the impossibility results of Nagarajan & Kolter (2019c) and Bartlett & Long (2021), which were limited to uniform-convergence bounds for linear classifiers under Gaussian distributions, by proving limitations for arbitrary algorithm-dependent bounds, arbitrary architectures, and any distribution. The paper introduces the formal notion of 'estimability' as a unifying framework, connecting the existence of tight generalization bounds to a learnability-estimability trade-off that had not been previously articulated.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fundamentally reshapes the landscape of generalization theory by proving that uniformly tight bounds of the standard form are mathematically impossible in the overparameterized setting without explicit distributional or algorithmic assumptions. The learnability-estimability trade-off (Theorem 3) provides a clear conceptual framework: an algorithm that learns well on some distributions provably cannot be certified as doing so by any bound that depends only on the algorithm and training set. Open questions include which specific published bounds fall into which impossibility category, whether the paper's definition of overparameterization is the most natural one, and how to formulate bounds that are tight for specific (algorithm, distribution) pairs without resorting to validation-set-style post hoc measurement.
