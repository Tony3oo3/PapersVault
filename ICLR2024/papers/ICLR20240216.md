## Problem

Existing backdoor attacks on contrastive learning (CL) rely on fixed trigger patterns (e.g., patches or frequency-domain perturbations) that fail to maintain high similarity between trigger-injected data and the target class in the embedding space. This failure stems from two CL-specific mechanisms that fixed triggers ignore: data augmentation can destroy the trigger pattern, and the uniformity objective actively pushes distinct instances apart, severing the trigger–target association. The paper asks what trigger design can survive these mechanisms to achieve high attack success rates.

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean unlabeled image dataset D, a small set of reference images x_r from the target class (e.g., 500 images for CIFAR-10), and a data augmentation set T as inputs. It produces a learned DNN backdoor generator g_ψ(·) that maps any input image to its backdoored version, constrained within an ℓ∞ ball of radius ε=8/255. At deployment, the generator is applied to 1% of the training data (the poisoning rate) to create the poisoned dataset that the victim will use for CL pre-training.

## Method

The core contribution is a bi-level trigger optimization (BLTO) that jointly learns a sample-specific trigger generator and simulates the victim's CL dynamics. The inner optimization trains a surrogate feature extractor (SimSiam with ResNet-18/34) on the poisoned dataset, replicating the alignment and uniformity pressures the real victim will face. The outer optimization updates the DNN generator g_ψ to maximize the cosine similarity between augmented views of backdoored images and augmented views of target-class reference images in the surrogate's embedding space. The two levels are solved via interleaved gradient steps (Algorithm 1), with periodic re-initialization of the surrogate to escape local optima. Because the trigger is optimized against the full CL training loop—including random crops, color jitter, and Gaussian blur—the resulting triggers capture global semantic structure aligned with the input image, making them resilient to augmentation and less penalized by the uniformity objective, which explains the 99% ASR at 1% poisoning rate.

## Results

On ImageNet-100, BLTO achieves 99.82% ASR with BYOL and 96.45% ASR with SimCLR at only 1% poisoning rate, while maintaining backdoor accuracy (BA) within 0.3% of the clean model. On CIFAR-10, the attack reaches 91.27% ASR (SimCLR), 94.78% ASR (BYOL), and 84.63% ASR (SimSiam), compared to at most 34.76% for the strongest baseline (CTRL). The attack also transfers well across unseen data distributions, retaining 99.42% ASR even when only 1% of the victim's training data matches the poisoned distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two primary attack baselines, BLTO dominates across all datasets and CL strategies: on ImageNet-100 with BYOL, BLTO achieves 99.82% ASR versus 43.16% (CTRL) and 13.86% (SSL backdoor); on CIFAR-10 with SimCLR, it reaches 91.27% versus 32.18% (CTRL) and 20.81% (SSL backdoor). Against backdoor detection methods, BLTO evades both SL-targeted (Neural Cleanse, anomaly index 1.34 < threshold 2) and CL-targeted defenses (SSL-Cleanse, index 1.56; DECREE, P1-norm 0.20–0.23 > threshold 0.1; ASSET, TPR only 5.39%). Among mitigation methods, I-BAU reduces ASR most (from ~90% to 56.87%) but at a cost of ~10% BA drop; CLP and knowledge distillation leave ASR at 87.57% and 81.08% respectively. Against Narcissus (a clean-label SL backdoor attack), BLTO substantially outperforms it on all three CL methods (e.g., 91.27% vs. 73.01% ASR on SimCLR/CIFAR-10). Overall, BLTO wins every head-to-head comparison on ASR while maintaining BA within 0.5% of the clean model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CL backdoor attack paradigm established by Saha et al. (2022, SSL backdoor) and Li et al. (2023, CTRL), which poison unlabeled training data with fixed triggers (patches or frequency-domain perturbations) borrowed from supervised learning. The key departure is the identification that these fixed triggers fail in CL because the uniformity mechanism actively pushes backdoored instances apart from the target class, and data augmentations can destroy local trigger patterns. Rather than designing a new fixed trigger, the paper repurposes bi-level optimization (common in adversarial training and hyperparameter optimization) to iteratively co-train a DNN-based trigger generator against a surrogate CL pipeline, so the trigger is shaped to survive the specific dynamics of contrastive learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: achieving ~99% ASR at 1% poisoning rate on ImageNet-100 while evading all tested CL-targeted defenses (SSL-Cleanse, DECREE, ASSET) suggests that current defense pipelines for self-supervised learning are inadequate against adaptive, optimization-based triggers. The analysis linking attack failure to the uniformity mechanism provides a concrete design principle for future defenses. Open questions include whether the computational cost of bi-level optimization (training a surrogate CL model repeatedly) limits real-world deployability, whether the DNN generator's global semantic triggers can be detected by more sophisticated methods, and whether dedicated CL defenses can be designed that specifically counteract the alignment-without-uniformity strategy this attack exploits.
