## Problem

Existing video editing methods built on image diffusion models rely on extending self-attention across frames to enforce temporal consistency, but this only implicitly preserves motion and fails to maintain fine-grained inter-frame correspondences. The result is edited videos with visible jittering, color shifts, and structural inconsistencies that degrade professional usability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240065/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source video (384×672 or 512×512, 40–200 frames) and a target text prompt, and produces an edited video of the same length and resolution that adheres to the prompt while preserving the original spatial layout and motion. It operates entirely in the latent space of a pretrained Stable Diffusion model and requires no training or fine-tuning; the frame-level editing is delegated to a pluggable diffusion-based image editing technique (PnP-Diffusion by default).

## Method

TokenFlow exploits the observation that diffusion features of natural videos exhibit the same temporal redundancy as RGB pixels: corresponding regions across frames are encoded with similar self-attention tokens. In a pre-processing stage, each frame is DDIM-inverted and tokens are extracted from every self-attention layer at every denoising timestep; inter-frame correspondences are then established via nearest-neighbor search in token space. At each denoising step during generation, the method (i) randomly samples keyframes and jointly edits them using an extended self-attention block that concatenates keys across keyframes for global appearance coherence, and (ii) propagates the resulting edited tokens to all frames by warping them through the pre-computed correspondences, linearly interpolating between the nearest past and future keyframe tokens. This explicit feature propagation enforces the original video's inter-frame structure on the edited features, achieving fine-grained temporal consistency that attention inflation alone cannot provide.

## Results

TokenFlow achieves a warp-error of 3.0×10⁻³ and a CLIP score of 0.33 on the 61 text-video pairs (DAVIS + Internet videos), tying with PnP-Diffusion and Text2Video-Zero on edit fidelity while substantially outperforming them on temporal consistency (warp-error 11.3 and 12.5 respectively). In the user study (2000–3000 judgments per baseline via Amazon Mechanical Turk), TokenFlow is preferred over every baseline, with win rates ranging from 70% (vs. Gen-1) to 94% (vs. per-frame PnP-Diffusion). The only method with a lower warp-error is Rerender-a-Video (1.8×10⁻³), but the paper notes this is misleading because that method explicitly optimizes for optical-flow warping and produces visible long-range artifacts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TokenFlow clearly outperforms Tune-a-Video (warp-error 30.0 vs. 3.0; user pref. 82%) and per-frame PnP-Diffusion (warp-error 11.3 vs. 3.0; user pref. 94%), confirming that neither fine-tuning the image model nor independent frame editing yields fine-grained temporal consistency. Against the attention-inflation methods Text2Video-Zero (warp-error 12.5) and Fate-Zero (warp-error 6.9), TokenFlow reduces warp-error by 4× and 2.3× respectively, and wins the user study by 78% and 71%. Gen-1 (a dedicated video diffusion model) is beaten on user preference (70%) and matches on CLIP score (0.32 vs. 0.33), but its frame quality is noted as significantly lower. Rerender-a-Video achieves the lowest warp-error (1.8) because it explicitly optimizes for optical-flow warping, yet TokenFlow still wins the user study (71%) due to fewer long-range artifacts from unreliable distant-frame flow estimation. On edit fidelity (CLIP score), TokenFlow ties the best baselines at 0.33, showing that the feature-propagation mechanism does not sacrifice prompt adherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

TokenFlow sits at the intersection of two lines of work: (1) training-free video editing via pre-trained image diffusion models (Tune-A-Video, Text2Video-Zero, Fate-Zero, Pix2Video), which rely on inflating self-attention across frames for global appearance consistency, and (2) classical video consistency methods that exploit temporal redundancies in natural videos (patch-based propagation, layered atlases). The paper departs from both by arguing that extended self-attention is insufficient for fine-grained temporal consistency and that optical-flow-based propagation is unreliable for distant frames. Instead, it introduces a feature-space propagation mechanism (nearest-neighbor token correspondence in the diffusion model's self-attention outputs) that explicitly preserves the original video's inter-frame correspondences during editing, building on the empirical observation that diffusion features exhibit the same temporal redundancy structure as RGB.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play nature of TokenFlow (composable with any diffusion-based image editing method) makes it practically attractive for video editing pipelines that need temporal consistency without retraining. The empirical finding that diffusion feature consistency correlates with RGB consistency, and that simple NN propagation in feature space outperforms attention-based consistency enforcement, offers a useful design principle for future video generation/editing systems. Key limitations remain: the method cannot handle edits requiring structural changes, inherits artifacts when the underlying image editor fails to preserve structure, and the LDM decoder introduces residual high-frequency flickering. Open questions include whether the feature-consistency principle generalizes beyond Stable Diffusion to other architectures (e.g., DiT-based models) and whether it can be extended to handle more complex, structure-altering edits.
