## Problem

Diffusion-based layout generation models achieve state-of-the-art FID scores but exhibit more pronounced misalignment and overlap compared to earlier transformer-based models. Discrete diffusion models cannot leverage differentiable aesthetic constraint functions due to quantized geometric attributes, while continuous diffusion models struggle with task unification because Gaussian noise and the data distribution (canvas range, probability simplex) occupy different sample spaces. This paper addresses both limitations simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240363/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a binary condition mask that specifies which attributes (class labels, sizes, positions) of which elements are fixed, enabling a single model to handle unconditional generation, class-conditional generation, size-and-class-conditional generation, completion from a partial layout, and refinement of a noisy layout. Each element is represented as a continuous 4D bounding box (center x, y and width, height ratios in [0,1]) plus a continuous (N+1)-dimensional label vector that is not confined to the probability simplex during diffusion, allowing unified Gaussian noise corruption. Layouts are padded to a fixed maximum length with a dedicated padding class to handle variable element counts.

## Method

LACE formulates layout generation as a continuous DDPM process over a unified state space where both geometric attributes and categorical labels are treated as continuous variables, eliminating the need for attribute-specific corruption strategies. The key innovation is applying differentiable aesthetic constraint functions—a novel global alignment loss that learns alignment patterns from ground-truth alignment masks, and a pairwise overlap loss combining IoU with center-distance penalty—directly to the x₀-prediction computed via reparameterization from the predicted noise. A time-dependent weight ω_t = (1−ᾱ_t) deactivates constraints at noisier timesteps to prevent convergence to local minima. Task unification is achieved through masked-input training (analogous to DiffMAE), where binary condition masks fix subsets of attributes during training. A lightweight post-processing step applies threshold-based global alignment optimization to the raw output for further visual refinement without sacrificing FID.

## Results

On PubLayNet, LACE (global) with post-processing achieves FID of 4.56 (C→S+P), 2.53 (C+S→P), 5.63 (Completion), and 7.43 (U-Cond), with an alignment score of 0.074, outperforming the strongest discrete-diffusion baseline LayoutDM (FID 7.95, 4.25, 7.65, 13.9) and the best transformer baseline BART (FID 9.36, 5.88, 9.58, 16.6). On Rico, LACE (global) w/ post reaches FID 3.24 (C→S+P), 2.16 (C+S→P), 4.30 (Completion), and 4.51 (U-Cond) with alignment 0.035, again surpassing LayoutDM (FID 3.55, 2.22, 9.00, 6.65). In the refinement task, LACE achieves FID 1.65 on PubLayNet and 1.79 on Rico, substantially outperforming LayoutDM (4.25 and 2.77) and the task-specific RUITE model (6.39 and 263.23).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LACE (global) w/ post achieves the best FID across all four tasks on both PubLayNet and Rico, outperforming the strongest discrete-diffusion baseline LayoutDM by 30–47% in FID (e.g., 4.56 vs. 7.95 on PubLayNet C→S+P; 4.30 vs. 9.00 on Rico Completion). Against the best transformer-based task-agnostic model BART, LACE reduces FID by roughly 50% on PubLayNet (4.56 vs. 9.36 for C→S+P) and by 56% on Rico Completion (4.30 vs. 8.87). On the unconditional generation task, LACE w/ post achieves an alignment score of 0.074 on PubLayNet and 0.035 on Rico, compared to 0.195 and 0.162 for LayoutDM and 0.116 and 0.090 for BART, indicating substantially better visual alignment. In the refinement task (Table 2), LACE achieves FID 1.65 on PubLayNet and 1.79 on Rico, far surpassing LayoutDM (4.25 / 2.77) and the task-specific RUITE model (6.39 / 263.23). LACE also outperforms the task-specific LayoutGAN++ by a wide margin (FID 4.56 vs. 24.0 on PubLayNet C→S+P), demonstrating that the unified continuous-diffusion formulation with aesthetic constraints is more effective than dedicated per-task models.

## Relation to Prior Work

LACE builds on the DDPM continuous diffusion framework (Ho et al., 2020) and the x0-reparameterization from DDIM (Song et al., 2020) to generate layout attributes in a continuous state space, departing from the discrete diffusion approach used by LayoutDM (Inoue et al., 2023) and LDGM (Hui et al., 2023). The key conceptual departure is that continuous space enables differentiable aesthetic constraint functions (alignment and overlap losses) during training, which is impossible with quantized geometric attributes in discrete models. For task unification, LACE borrows the masked-input strategy from DiffMAE (Wei et al., 2023) and applies it to continuous diffusion, whereas prior continuous diffusion layout models (He et al., 2023; Chai et al., 2023) were restricted to single tasks. The continuous label vector representation follows CARD (Han et al., 2022), allowing classification logits to be corrupted with Gaussian noise without modality-specific tricks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LACE addresses a concrete weakness of diffusion-based layout generators: their tendency to produce misaligned and overlapping elements despite strong FID scores, a gap that transformer-based models like LayoutTransformer handled better. The time-dependent constraint weight and the distinction between local and global alignment losses are practical contributions that make constraint-based training stable and more faithful to real design patterns. However, the model remains limited to rectangular bounding boxes, a closed label set, and a fixed maximum element count, and lacks content or background awareness—open directions the authors identify for future work. Whether the continuous-space advantage over discrete diffusion generalizes to richer element representations (arbitrary shapes, text, images) remains an open question.
