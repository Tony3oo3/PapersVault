## Problem

Time series forecasting models are typically specialized per task and domain, requiring extensive retraining, unlike LLMs that generalize across NLP tasks in few-shot or zero-shot settings. The core challenge is that LLMs operate on discrete text tokens while time series are continuous, and the knowledge to interpret temporal patterns is not naturally present in LLM pre-training, making direct application infeasible without resource-intensive fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240950/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series X ∈ R^{N×T} (N channels, T time steps) which is normalized via RevIN, segmented into overlapping or non-overlapping patches of length L_p, and embedded into a d_m-dimensional space. A natural-language prompt encoding dataset context, task instruction, and computed input statistics (trend direction, top-5 autocorrelation lags) is prepended as a prefix. The output is a forecast Ŷ ∈ R^{N×H} for H future steps, obtained by flattening the LLM's output patch representations (after discarding the prefix tokens) and applying a single linear projection. Only the patch embedder, cross-attention reprogramming layer, and output projection are trainable (~6.6M parameters, ~0.2% of Llama-7B); the backbone LLM remains entirely frozen.

## Method

TIME-LLM reprograms time series patches into the LLM's native embedding space via multi-head cross-attention, where patch embeddings serve as queries and a small set of learned text prototypes (V' ≪ V rows of the LLM's pre-trained word embedding matrix) serve as keys and values, producing reprogrammed representations that activate the frozen LLM's pattern-recognition capabilities. To augment reasoning, the paper introduces Prompt-as-Prefix (PaP), which prepends a structured natural-language prompt (dataset caption, task instruction, and input statistics) before the reprogrammed patches, providing declarative guidance for the LLM's transformation without requiring the time series itself to be verbalized. The frozen LLM processes the concatenated prompt-and-patch sequence, and the output patch representations are projected to forecasts. Training uses standard supervised loss (MSE for long-term, SMAPE for short-term) on only the lightweight adapter modules, requiring just a few epochs on a small time series dataset, making the approach data-efficient and avoiding any backbone fine-tuning.

## Results

On long-term forecasting across 8 benchmarks (ETTh1, ETTh2, ETTm1, ETTm2, Weather, ECL, Traffic, ILI), TIME-LLM achieves the best MSE on 7 of 8 datasets, with average MSE reductions of 12% over GPT4TS and 20% over TimesNet. On the M4 short-term benchmark, TIME-LLM attains the best SMAPE (11.983), MASE (1.595), and OWA (0.859), outperforming GPT4TS by 8.7%. In few-shot (10% data) and zero-shot cross-domain settings, TIME-LLM's advantage over GPT4TS grows to 5% and 22% MSE reduction respectively, and it surpasses LLMTime by over 75% in zero-shot.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TIME-LLM outperforms GPT4TS (the closest LLM-based competitor that fine-tunes the backbone) by 12% average MSE on long-term forecasting, 8.7% on M4 short-term, 5% on 10% few-shot, and 22% on zero-shot cross-domain transfer. Against the strongest task-specific Transformer, PatchTST, TIME-LLM achieves a 1.4% average MSE reduction on long-term forecasting and ties or slightly leads on most individual datasets. Compared to TimesNet, the margin widens to 20% MSE reduction on long-term and 10% on M4. Against simpler baselines like DLinear, FEDformer, and Autoformer, improvements exceed 12–33% MSE. In zero-shot settings, TIME-LLM surpasses LLMTime (a 7B LLM-based zero-shot forecaster) by over 75% MSE, demonstrating that reprogramming with text prototypes and Prompt-as-Prefix is far more effective than direct tokenization of time series values for activating LLM reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

TIME-LLM extends the model reprogramming paradigm (Chen, 2022) from vision and speech to time series, but introduces a key departure: rather than directly editing input samples into the source modality (as in Voice2Series), it learns a small set of text prototypes via cross-attention to bridge the continuous-to-discrete gap. It also builds on PatchTST's patching mechanism for input tokenization and output projection, while introducing Prompt-as-Prefix as a novel prompting strategy that avoids the numerical-precision limitations of directly verbalizing time series values. Unlike GPT4TS, which fine-tunes the LLM backbone, TIME-LLM keeps the language model entirely frozen, training only ~0.2% of total parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that time series forecasting can be effectively cast as a 'language' task solvable by an off-the-shelf frozen LLM, with strong few-shot and zero-shot generalization that specialized models lack. This opens a path toward multimodal foundation models that handle both language and sequential data without domain-specific retraining. Open questions include whether optimal reprogramming representations can be discovered more systematically, whether continued pre-training with explicit time series knowledge would further close the gap, and whether the approach extends to other sequential tasks (classification, anomaly detection). A practical limitation is the inference cost of a 7B backbone, though quantization and smaller variants partially mitigate this.
