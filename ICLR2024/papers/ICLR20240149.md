## Problem

Top-K sparse softmax gating mixture of experts has become the dominant mechanism for scaling deep learning architectures via conditional computation, yet its theoretical properties remain entirely uncharacterized. The core difficulty is that the TopK function partitions the input space into multiple regions with distinct gating behaviors, creating a structural complexity absent from standard softmax or Gaussian gating analyses. This paper provides the first rigorous convergence analysis of maximum likelihood estimation under this gating mechanism.

## Input / Output

The model consumes i.i.d. pairs (X_i, Y_i) with X_i ∈ R^d and Y_i ∈ R, and produces a conditional density g_G(Y|X) formed by blending K active Gaussian experts selected via the top-K sparse softmax gate. A key structural constraint emerges under over-specified settings: the number of experts activated in the estimated density (K̄) must exceed the total cardinality of Voronoi cells associated with the true parameters for density convergence to hold, meaning K̄ can be strictly larger than the true K. Parameter estimates are reported under a novel Voronoi metric whose rate depends on the solvability of polynomial equation systems when multiple fitted components approximate a single true expert.

## Method

The analysis proceeds by partitioning the input space into C(k*,K) regions where the top-K expert selection is fixed, then proving (via Lemmas 1–2) that for sufficiently large n the estimated and true partitions coincide almost surely. Density estimation rates follow from van de Geer's M-estimation framework combined with covering number and bracketing entropy bounds, yielding parametric O~(n^{-1/2}) Hellinger convergence in both exact-specified and over-specified settings. For parameter estimation, the authors design two novel Voronoi metrics: D1 under exact specification (giving uniform O~(n^{-1/2}) rates for all parameters) and D2 under over-specification, which captures an intrinsic interaction between softmax gating parameters and expert parameters via partial differential equations. This interaction forces the parameter rates to depend on r̄(m), the smallest degree at which a certain polynomial system has no non-trivial solution, causing rates to degrade to O~(n^{-1/2·r̄(|C_j|)}) for gating/bias parameters and O~(n^{-1/r̄(|C_j|)}) for expert parameters when a true expert is fitted by multiple components.

## Results

Under the exact-specified setting (true number of experts k* known), both density estimation and all parameter estimations (exp(β*₀ᵢ), β*₁ᵢ, a*ᵢ, b*ᵢ, σ*ᵢ) achieve the parametric rate of Õ(n^{-1/2}). Under the over-specified setting (k > k*), density estimation remains at Õ(n^{-1/2}), but parameter rates degrade: β*₁ⱼ and b*ⱼ are estimated at Õ(n^{-1/2·r̄(|Cⱼ|)}) while a*ⱼ and σ*ⱼ are estimated at Õ(n^{-1/r̄(|Cⱼ|)}), where r̄(m) is the smallest r such that a system of polynomial equations has no non-trivial solution (r̄(2)=4, r̄(3)=6). A key condition is that the number of experts selected in the top-K̄ gating (K̄) must exceed the total cardinality of Voronoi cells associated with the true parameters to guarantee density convergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or head-to-head model comparisons. The key theoretical comparison is between the top-K sparse softmax gating MoE and the dense softmax gating MoE (Nguyen et al., 2023): the paper shows that parameter estimation rates under both models are identical under both exact-specified and over-specified settings, meaning the sparsity introduced by top-K gating does not sacrifice convergence rates. The paper also extends the Voronoi metric framework from Nguyen et al. (2023) for softmax gating and Ho et al. (2022) for input-free gating to the more complex top-K sparse softmax gating structure, requiring new loss functions D₁ and D₂ that account for the region-partitioning behavior of the TopK function. A notable practical finding is that top-1 sparse softmax gating (K=1) avoids the slow parameter estimation rates under over-specification because the gating value becomes binary (0 or 1), eliminating the interaction between softmax weights and expert functions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convergence analysis of MLE in Gaussian mixture-of-experts models from prior work on input-free gating (Ho et al., 2022), softmax gating (Nguyen et al., 2023), and Gaussian gating (Nguyen et al., 2024) to the top-K sparse softmax gating function, which partitions the input space into combinatorially many regions with distinct behaviors. The analysis leverages Voronoi cell frameworks from Manole & Ho (2022) and M-estimation theory from van de Geer (2000), but introduces novel Voronoi metrics (D1, D2) specifically designed to handle the discontinuous TopK operator and the interaction between softmax gating weights and expert parameters. Unlike prior gating functions that are smooth or globally defined, the top-K sparse softmax gating creates a piecewise structure that requires matching input-space partitions between the estimated and true models before standard density estimation arguments can be applied.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical analysis of the top-K sparse softmax gating MoE, a model architecture that underpins many large-scale deep learning systems (Switch Transformers, GLaM, etc.), and the finding that sparsity introduces no statistical penalty on convergence rates provides a formal justification for the widespread use of sparse gating in practice. The paper also reveals that top-1 gating avoids the slow parameter estimation rates under over-specification because the binary gating eliminates the gating-expert interaction, partially explaining the empirical success of Switch Transformers. Open questions include extending the analysis to misspecified settings (where the non-convex parameter space complicates MLE convergence), establishing EM algorithm guarantees for this model class, and developing practical methods for estimating the true number of experts k* and the optimal K.
