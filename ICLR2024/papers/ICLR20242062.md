## Problem

Simulating realistic multi-agent driving scenarios for self-driving development requires a traffic model that can react to arbitrary actions chosen by a black-box AV system at each timestep. Prior generative traffic models either use regression objectives that may lack the flexibility to represent the full distribution over next states, or fail to properly model intra-timestep coupling between agents, making them unsuitable for closed-loop interactive simulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242062/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model is seeded with only the initial position, heading, dimensions, and object class of each agent plus vector map polylines (lanes, sidewalks, crosswalks), requiring no historical motion. It outputs a categorical distribution over 384 discrete action tokens per agent per timestep, where each token encodes a relative position and heading change at centimeter-level resolution (~1 cm expected discretization error). Agents select actions sequentially within each timestep, so the output for a given agent conditions on actions already chosen by other agents at the same timestep.

## Method

Trajeglish discretizes continuous trajectories using a 'k-disks' tokenization that samples template state-to-state transitions from data while enforcing a minimum separation ε, achieving 1 cm resolution with only 384 tokens—far outperforming grid-based and k-means alternatives. The architecture is an encoder-decoder transformer: the encoder processes per-agent initial states via an MLP and map objects via a VectorNet encoder with latent query attention, while the decoder is a causal transformer that predicts the next action token autoregressively over a flattened multi-agent sequence. Critically, the model is not permutation-equivariant to agent order, since the ordering encodes the sequential action-selection process within a timestep, enabling explicit modeling of intra-timestep interaction. Training uses standard next-token prediction with cross-entropy loss, optionally with noisy tokenization (nucleus sampling over distance-based logits) to mitigate exposure bias during autoregressive rollouts.

## Results

On the WOMD Sim Agents Benchmark, Trajeglish achieves a state-of-the-art realism meta metric of 0.5339, surpassing the prior best (MVTE* at 0.5168) by 3.3%. The most significant gain is on the interaction metric, where Trajeglish scores 0.5811 versus 0.5289 for MVTE*, a 9.9% improvement. Trajeglish also leads on map-based metrics (0.6667 vs. 0.6486) but trails on kinematic metrics (0.4019 vs. 0.4202) and minADE (1.872 m vs. 1.656 m for MTR_E*), indicating that the discrete tokenization introduces slight heading quantization at the cost of superior multi-agent interaction modeling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Trajeglish outperforms all four primary baselines on the realism meta metric and interaction metric. Against Wayformer (Diverse), Trajeglish improves the realism meta metric from 0.4720 to 0.5339 (+13.1%) and interaction from 0.4935 to 0.5811 (+17.8%), while Wayformer retains a slight edge on kinematic metrics (0.3613 vs. 0.4019 in Trajeglish's favor actually—Wayformer Diverse scores 0.3613, so Trajeglish wins here too). Against MTR_E*, Trajeglish wins on realism (0.5339 vs. 0.4911) and interaction (0.5811 vs. 0.4905) but loses on kinematic metrics (0.4019 vs. 0.4180) and minADE (1.872 m vs. 1.656 m). Against MTR+++, the gap is similar: Trajeglish leads on realism and interaction but trails on kinematics. Against Joint-MultiPath++, Trajeglish dominates on realism, interaction, and map-based metrics while MultiPath++ achieves a lower minADE (2.052 m vs. 1.872 m). The consistent pattern is that Trajeglish's autoregressive, intra-timestep-aware factorization yields substantially more realistic multi-agent interactions at a small cost to per-agent kinematic smoothness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the discrete sequence modeling paradigm (next-token prediction) from language modeling and RL (Trajectory Transformer, MotionLM) to the N-agent closed-loop traffic simulation task, where prior work has relied on VAEs, GANs, diffusion models, or regression-based approaches. The key architectural departure from concurrent MotionLM is targeting offline multi-agent simulation rather than online 1-2 agent prediction, and from Scene Transformer is replacing masked regression with tokenized autoregressive generation. The novel k-disks tokenization strategy, inspired by k-means++ and Poisson disk sampling, achieves centimeter-level discretization with a compact vocabulary of 384 tokens, outperforming grid-based and k-means alternatives by a wide margin.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Topping the WOMD Sim Agents Benchmark with a 9.9% improvement in interaction metrics validates that autoregressive discrete modeling is a competitive paradigm for traffic simulation, and the clean factorization naturally supports closed-loop interaction with external AV systems. However, the paper reveals that WOMD (~1.5B tokens) is severely data-constrained relative to model capacity, suggesting substantial gains are possible with more data. Open questions include whether the kinematic metric gap (discretization-induced heading error) can be closed, whether the approach scales to 128-agent scenarios without the sliding-window hack, and whether the intra-timestep interaction modeling generalizes to higher-frequency or continuous-time settings.
