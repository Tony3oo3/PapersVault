## Problem

The state-of-the-art algorithm for differentially private federated linear contextual bandits (Dubey & Pentland, 2020) suffers from three fundamental gaps: privacy leakage through its data-dependent adaptive communication schedule, an incorrect regret bound due to miscalculating total injected noise (off by a factor of M^{1/4}), and an ungrounded logarithmic communication cost claim that breaks under privacy noise. Additionally, no existing method achieves the regret of a centralized super-agent while protecting individual users without a trusted server.

## Input / Output

Each silo receives a stream of T users with d-dimensional context vectors and a finite action set; the algorithm takes privacy budgets (ε, δ), a fixed batch size B, and regularization λ as hyperparameters. At each synchronization round (every B steps), each silo uploads noisy partial-sum statistics (bias vectors and covariance matrices) through a local randomizer, an optional shuffler, and a server-side analyzer, then receives synchronized private estimates to update its local LinUCB model. The output is a per-round action recommendation per user plus a theoretical group regret bound that decomposes into a non-private term and a privacy-cost term scaling with ε, δ, M, d, and T.

## Method

The paper proposes Private-FedLinUCB, a generic federated LinUCB algorithm with a fixed-batch (data-independent) communication schedule that eliminates the side-channel privacy leakage of adaptive schedules. Its privacy protocol decomposes the classic tree-based mechanism into a local randomizer (releasing noisy partial sums at each batch), an optional shuffler, and a server-side analyzer that aggregates and reconstructs prefix sums. Under silo-level LDP (no shuffler), the protocol achieves correct (ε,δ)-DP with regret Õ(M^{3/4}√(T/ε)), fixing the SOTA gaps. Under shuffle DP, two schemes close the M^{1/4} gap to match centralized regret Õ(√(MT/ε)): one uses a new amplification lemma for Gaussian mechanisms on n>1 datasets (extending the 'hiding among clones' technique), and the other integrates the vector-sum shuffle protocol of Cheu et al. (2021) into the tree structure by expanding each partial sum into O(1)-norm data points before shuffling. The key design choice of communicating partial sums rather than prefix sums ensures each data point participates in only O(log K) shuffle mechanisms, keeping the composed privacy cost logarithmic rather than linear in the number of rounds.

## Results

Under silo-level LDP, the proposed algorithm achieves group regret Õ(√T · (Md)^{3/4} · log^{1/4}(1/δ) / √ε) with O(√(MT)) communication rounds (Theorem 5.1). Under the shuffle model (SDP), the regret improves to Õ(√(MT) · d^{3/4} · log^{3/4}(Mκ/δ) / √ε) (Theorems 5.3, 5.5), matching the central DP benchmark of Shariff & Sheffet (2018) up to logarithmic factors. Empirically, on synthetic (M=100) and real MSLR-WEB10K (M=10) bandit instances, SDP-FedLinUCB consistently achieves lower time-averaged group regret than LDP-FedLinUCB, and both converge toward the non-private FedLinUCB baseline as ε increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the state-of-the-art federated LCB algorithm of Dubey & Pentland (2020), which the authors identify as having three fundamental gaps: (i) privacy leakage through the adaptive communication schedule, (ii) an incorrect regret bound that is M^{1/4} factor too optimistic (claimed Õ(√(MT/ε)) vs. correct Õ(M^{3/4}√(T/ε))), and (iii) an ungrounded O(M log T) communication cost claim that fails under privacy noise. Compared to the central DP benchmark of Shariff & Sheffet (2018), the silo-level LDP result incurs an M^{1/4} multiplicative penalty in privacy cost, while the SDP result (via either the new amplification lemma or the vector-sum shuffle protocol) closes this gap and matches the central DP regret up to log factors. Empirically, SDP-FedLinUCB outperforms LDP-FedLinUCB on both synthetic and real-data instances, and both private variants approach the non-private FedLinUCB baseline as the privacy budget ε grows (e.g., from 0.2 to 5 on MSLR-WEB10K).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the LinUCB algorithm (Abbasi-Yadkori et al., 2011) and the tree-based mechanism for continual private statistics release (Chan et al., 2011; Dwork et al., 2010), combining them into a distributed federated framework. It directly corrects and extends Dubey & Pentland (2020), the first work on federated LCBs under privacy, by replacing their adaptive communication schedule with a fixed-batch one and their prefix-sum release with partial-sum (p-sum) release. For the shuffle model, the paper introduces a new amplification lemma for Gaussian mechanisms operating on datasets of size n>1, refining the 'hiding among the clones' technique of Feldman et al. (2022) and the group-privacy-based amplification of Lowy & Razaviyayn (2021), and integrates the vector-sum shuffle protocol of Cheu et al. (2021) into the tree-based mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first correct privacy, regret, and communication guarantees for federated LCBs under silo-level LDP and SDP, closing a gap where the prior state-of-the-art had fundamental errors (privacy leakage via adaptive schedule, M^{1/4} regret overestimate, ungrounded communication cost). The p-sum communication design and the new amplification lemma are reusable building blocks for other federated online learning problems under shuffle privacy. Key open questions include achieving O(log T) communication cost with correct privacy guarantees (the fixed-batch schedule incurs O(√(MT)) cost), extending to user-level DP, and handling non-unique users across rounds.
