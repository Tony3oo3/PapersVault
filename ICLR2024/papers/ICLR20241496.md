## Problem

Face forgery detectors trained on third-party datasets are vulnerable to backdoor attacks, yet no comprehensive investigation exists for advanced backdoor attacks in this domain. Two specific obstacles make extending standard backdoor attacks to face forgery detection non-trivial: (1) a backdoor label conflict in blending artifact detection methods, where triggers embedded in real faces are transferred to synthetic fake faces via blending transformations, creating contradictory label associations; and (2) the need for high trigger stealthiness, since users are extremely sensitive to small artifacts on facial images.

## Input / Output

The method takes clean face images (from FF++ training videos at varying resolutions) and produces poisoned samples by embedding a translation-sensitive trigger pattern confined to facial landmark regions. The trigger is generated by an FCN-based generator that outputs patterns of arbitrary size to accommodate different face resolutions. The final output is a backdoored binary classifier (Xception, SBI, or Face X-ray) that normally performs real/fake classification but misclassifies triggered fake faces as real, evaluated via AUC (clean performance) and the proposed BD-AUC metric (attack success rate).

## Method

The Poisoned Forgery Face framework is a clean-label backdoor attack with three key components. First, a translation-sensitive trigger is generated by maximizing the L1 discrepancy between the trigger and its translated version, which is analytically reduced to a convolution with a kernel K(v) whose center value is (2v+1)²−1 and all other entries are −1; this ensures the trigger appearance changes after the blending transformation, resolving the label conflict. Second, a scalable FCN-based trigger generator is trained with this convolution loss to produce triggers at arbitrary resolutions. Third, a landmark-based relative embedding method confines the trigger to facial landmark regions and uses a pixel-wise relative ratio (α = a·x_k/255 with a ≤ 0.05) rather than a fixed scalar, aligning perturbation magnitude with local image intensity for improved stealthiness. The poisoned real-face samples (10% poisoning rate) are mixed with clean data to train the detector under standard supervised loss, embedding the backdoor without modifying any labels.

## Results

On the primary FF++→FF++ evaluation, the proposed method achieves 84.52% BD-AUC on SBI (vs. 68.13% for the best baseline Blended, +16.39%) and 79.82% on Face X-ray (vs. 72.10% for Blended, +7.72%), while maintaining competitive clean AUC (92.06% and 77.70% respectively). Cross-dataset transferability is strong: on FF++→CDF, BD-AUC reaches 97.38% (SBI) and 98.96% (Face X-ray). In terms of stealthiness, the method achieves the highest PSNR (35.19), lowest L∞ (10.84), and lowest human-identified manipulation ratio (37.38%) among all compared attacks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the six baselines, the proposed method's advantage is most pronounced on blending-artifact detectors (SBI and Face X-ray), where existing attacks suffer from the backdoor label conflict. On SBI (FF++→FF++), Ours achieves 84.52% BD-AUC, outperforming Blended (68.13%, +16.39%), LC (61.59%, +22.93%), SIG (61.18%), ISSBA (51.07%), and Badnet (48.47%). On Face X-ray (FF++→FF++), Ours reaches 79.82% BD-AUC vs. Blended (72.10%, +7.72%), SIG (60.33%), ISSBA (57.57%), LC (58.27%), and Badnet (48.12%). On the deepfake-artifact detector Xception, most baselines already achieve near-perfect BD-AUC (>99%), so Ours (99.65%) is competitive but slightly below LC (99.97%) and Blended (99.73%) on FF++→FF++; however, Ours maintains the highest clean AUC (85.18%) and best cross-dataset AUC (78.26% on DFD). The frequency-domain baseline FTrojan (Appendix A.6) is outperformed by Ours across all three detectors and all three dataset splits. In stealthiness, Ours achieves the lowest L∞ (10.84 vs. 12.41 for Blended, 40.00 for SIG) and lowest human-identified manipulation ratio (37.38% vs. 50.45% for ISSBA, 81.98% for Blended, 88.28% for SIG).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the backdoor attack literature (BadNets, Blended, ISSBA, SIG, LC, FTrojan) to the specific domain of face forgery detection, building on the initial threat identification by Cao & Gong (2021). It departs from prior backdoor attacks by addressing a structural vulnerability unique to blending artifact detection methods (SBI, Face X-ray): the 'backdoor label conflict' where triggers in real faces become similar to their transformed versions in synthetic fake faces, causing standard triggers to associate with the wrong label. The scalable trigger generator borrows the FCN architecture from Hu et al. (CVPR 2022), while the relative pixel-wise embedding modifies the unified scalar blending ratio from Chen et al. (2017) to improve stealthiness in the face-specific context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work reveals that current face forgery detectors—particularly blending artifact detection methods that were previously considered more resilient—are vulnerable to clean-label backdoor attacks that resist all four tested defenses (FT, FP, NAD, ABL), with BD-AUC remaining above 81% post-defense. The practical implication is significant: an attacker who compromises a training dataset can silently disable a face forgery detector for targeted images without altering labels. Open questions include whether defenses specifically designed for translation-sensitive triggers exist, how the attack generalizes to video-level temporal detection, and whether the kernel-size sensitivity limits real-world applicability across diverse forgery pipelines.
