## Problem

Existing NeRF-based dynamic scene reconstruction methods model motion exclusively from the Eulerian (location-based) view, which captures scene flow at fixed spatial points but makes it intractable to extract the motion of individual objects or parts. This limits downstream applications such as part tracking, re-animation, and scene editing, since no meaningful structural decomposition of the dynamic scene is produced. The paper asks whether motion itself can serve as an unsupervised cue for discovering rigid parts in a dynamic scene, and how to represent motion in a way that makes such decomposition tractable.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular image sequence (single moving camera) with known camera poses as input, assuming piece-wise rigid motion in the scene. It produces a full dynamic scene representation enabling novel view rendering, a rigid part segmentation (each part assigned a group index with a shared SE(3) transformation), and per-part motion trajectories in canonical space. Training completes in approximately 26 minutes on a single RTX 3090 GPU using 20k iterations with 4096 rays per step, and the group merging post-processing step adaptively determines the final number of parts without user specification.

## Method

MovingParts constructs a hybrid Eulerian-Lagrangian dynamic NeRF with three modules: a canonical radiance field (feature volume + MLP) encoding static geometry and appearance, an Eulerian module Ψ_E(x,t) that maps a world-space position at time t back to canonical space via a learned SE(3) rigid transform, and a Lagrangian module Ψ_L(x_c,t) that forward-tracks canonical particles to world space. The key architectural novelty is a motion grouping network within the Lagrangian module that uses Gumbel-softmax with the straight-through estimator to hard-assign each particle's motion feature to one of a fixed number of learnable slots, enforcing that all particles in the same group share a single rigid transformation. A cycle-consistency loss is applied at the motion-feature level (rather than displacement level) to enforce reciprocity between the two views, which the ablation shows is critical for clean part separation. A post-training group merging algorithm uses Absolute Pose Error between decoded rigid transformation sequences to hierarchically merge over-segmented groups, stopping at the largest cost jump to determine the final part count.

## Results

On the D-NeRF 360° synthetic dataset, MovingParts achieves PSNR 32.18, SSIM 0.97, and LPIPS 0.04 in 26 minutes on a single GPU, matching the best SSIM and LPIPS among all compared methods while ranking second in PSNR (behind TiNeuVox at 32.67). Crucially, it is the only method that simultaneously performs high-quality dynamic reconstruction and rigid part discovery. On the motion grouping evaluation with ground-truth masks, the method achieves mIOU above 85% on most scenes, demonstrating consistent part tracking across the full 120-frame sequence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MovingParts outperforms D-NeRF (PSNR 32.18 vs 30.43, SSIM 0.97 vs 0.95, LPIPS 0.04 vs 0.07) and NDVG (PSNR 32.18 vs 30.54) on all three metrics while training in 26 minutes versus 20 hours for D-NeRF. Against TiNeuVox, the only method with a higher PSNR (32.67), MovingParts matches SSIM (0.97) and LPIPS (0.04); the paper notes TiNeuVox's PSNR advantage stems from a temporal enhancement module that makes the canonical space time-varying, and without it TiNeuVox drops to 31.47 PSNR. Compared to K-Planes (PSNR 31.61) and HexPlane (PSNR 31.04), MovingParts achieves higher PSNR while also providing part discovery. The only other part-discovery method, WIM (Noguchi et al., 2022), performs dramatically worse on reconstruction (PSNR 15.72, SSIM 0.83) due to motion ambiguity under monocular input without a canonical space, whereas MovingParts maintains near-state-of-the-art rendering quality while discovering parts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the D-NeRF lineage (Pumarola et al., 2020) by introducing a dual Lagrangian view alongside the standard Eulerian deformation field, drawing an explicit analogy to fluid simulation where Eulerian and Lagrangian perspectives are complementary. The motion grouping module adapts the attention-based hard clustering from GroupViT (Xu et al., 2022) to 3D canonical particles, while the feature-volume + MLP hybrid follows the fast NeRF trend (TiNeuVox, Plenoxels). The closest prior work, Watch-It-Move (Noguchi et al., 2022), also uses a Lagrangian formulation for part discovery but requires dense multi-view input and imposes ellipsoid geometric priors, whereas MovingParts operates from monocular input with no shape priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MovingParts is the first monocular NeRF method to simultaneously achieve high-quality dynamic reconstruction and unsupervised rigid part discovery, opening a path toward structured dynamic scene understanding that directly supports downstream tasks like part tracking, robotic manipulation, and scene editing. The rigid-body assumption, however, limits applicability to non-rigid or deformable scenes, and the heuristic group-merging post-processing lacks theoretical guarantees. The authors acknowledge that storing motion in fixed-length feature vectors makes very long sequences challenging, pointing toward a unified encoding-decoding scheme as future work.
