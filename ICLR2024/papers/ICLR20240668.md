## Problem

RLHF has become the dominant paradigm for aligning LLMs with human preferences, but the training pipeline—supervised fine-tuning, reward model learning, and PPO-based policy optimization—operates on private user feedback data with no formal privacy guarantees. Since LLMs are known to memorize and regurgitate training data, aligning models on user preferences raises the critical question of whether the final policy can leak information about individual users. This paper initiates the study of achieving (ε,δ)-differential privacy across the entire multi-stage alignment process.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a private dataset D split into three disjoint subsets: D1 (input–reference pairs for SFT), D2 (input–generation-pair–preference-bit tuples for reward model training), and D3 (unlabeled input prompts for RL). For the sentiment task, inputs are partial IMDb review prefixes and outputs are positive-sentiment completions scored by a RoBERTa reward model; for the summarization task, inputs are Reddit TL;DR posts and outputs are summaries scored by a DP-trained reward model. The final output is the aligned policy π whose weights satisfy (ε,δ)-DP with respect to D, evaluated via average reward and ROUGE-L on held-out test sets.

## Method

The framework applies DPSGD independently at each of three stages—DP supervised fine-tuning, DP reward model training, and DP PPO (DPPPO)—and composes them via the parallel composition theorem, assuming each user contributes to at most one of D1, D2, D3. A key technical insight is that the reward model must itself be DP-trained: if it were not, the PPO gradients would be functions of the entire D2 dataset, invalidating the privacy amplification by subsampling that DPSGD relies on. DPPPO sets T_PPO=1 (one PPO epoch per batch) and uses large batch sizes to keep the total number of model updates comparable to non-private PPO while preserving the subsampling-based privacy accounting. All stages use LoRA for parameter-efficient fine-tuning, which stabilizes DP hyperparameters and constrains policy drift from the SFT reference model. The PPO objective includes a KL penalty β·KL(π‖LM^sft) to prevent the aligned policy from deviating too far from the SFT model.

## Results

On the IMDb positive review generation task, the DP GPT-2-Large model achieves an average reward of 3.20±0.23 at ε=4, compared to 3.32±0.06 for the non-private aligned model and -0.24 for the pre-trained model. On the Reddit TL;DR summarization task (RLHF), the best private model (GPT-2-Large, ε=2) reaches a mean reward of 1.14, versus 1.49 for non-private alignment and -0.06 for the pre-trained model. Across both tasks, larger models and larger ε values generally improve performance, with the privacy-utility gap being smaller for sentiment generation than for summarization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against other alignment methods; instead, it benchmarks its DP framework against two natural baselines: the pre-trained model (no alignment) and the non-private alignment (ε=∞). On IMDb, the DP GPT-2-Large at ε=4 (reward 3.20) closes most of the gap to non-private alignment (3.32), losing only ~0.12 in reward while providing formal (ε,δ)-DP guarantees. On Reddit TL;DR, the gap is wider: the best private model (GPT-2-Large, ε=2, reward 1.14) trails non-private alignment (1.49) by ~0.35, reflecting the greater difficulty of the summarization task. Notably, GPT-2-Large at ε=4 (3.20) actually outperforms non-private GPT-2-Medium (3.45) is not the case, but GPT-2-Large at ε=8 (3.38) slightly exceeds non-private GPT-2-Large (3.32), suggesting that for larger models the DP penalty is negligible. The authors note that the non-monotonicity in the summarization results (e.g., GPT-2-Large performing best at ε=2 rather than ε=8) likely reflects insufficient hyperparameter tuning rather than a fundamental limitation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: the RLHF alignment pipeline (Ziegler et al., 2020; Ouyang et al., 2022) and differential privacy in deep learning via DPSGD (Abadi et al., 2016). Prior DP work on language models focused on private fine-tuning or synthetic data generation, while prior DP+RL work addressed Q-learning, offline RL, or constrained MDPs. The paper's conceptual contribution is showing that the full three-stage RLHF pipeline (SFT, reward model training, PPO) can be made differentially private, with a key technical insight that the reward model itself must be trained privately to preserve the subsampling amplification guarantee in the PPO stage. It also adapts PPO to the DP setting (DPPPO) by restricting to T_PPO=1 and using larger batch sizes to maintain privacy accounting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LLMs increasingly interact with users and collect feedback via telemetry, the privacy of the alignment data becomes a practical concern that this paper addresses for the first time with formal guarantees. The encouraging finding that larger pre-trained models yield better privacy-utility trade-offs suggests the approach will become more practical as base models improve. However, the empirical validation is limited to GPT-2-scale models on relatively simple tasks (sentiment completion, summarization), and the authors acknowledge that longer DP training, better hyperparameter tuning, and much larger models (e.g., LLaMA) could substantially improve results. Open questions include adapting the framework to the online setting, tightening privacy guarantees, and whether the reward model truly must be private or whether alternative analyses can relax this requirement.
