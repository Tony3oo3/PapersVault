## Problem

Energy-based models (EBMs) suffer from a noticeable sample quality gap compared to GANs and diffusion models, and the MCMC sampling required for training and inference is computationally expensive. While Diffusion Recovery Likelihood (DRL) improved EBM training by operating on a sequence of noisy marginals, it still requires ~30 Langevin steps per noise level and leaves a quality gap with state-of-the-art generative frameworks. This paper tackles both the quality gap and the sampling cost simultaneously.

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, the model starts from Gaussian white noise at the highest noise level and progressively denoises through 6 noise levels to produce 32×32 or 64×64 RGB images. For conditional generation, a class label is injected via a one-hot embedding added to the time embedding, and classifier-free guidance weight w controls the quality-diversity tradeoff. For inpainting, a partially masked image is noised up to the highest level and denoised back, with unmasked pixels reset at each step. The initializer network directly predicts the noised image ŷ_t (rather than noise ε or clean x_0), and the log signal-to-noise ratio λ_t is used as the noise-level conditioning input instead of the raw timestep index.

## Method

CDRL jointly trains a sequence of conditional EBMs and Gaussian initializer models at each of 6 noise levels using a cooperative scheme: the initializer proposes an initial sample ŷ_t from x_{t+1}, a few (15) Langevin steps from the EBM refine it to ỹ_t, the EBM is updated by maximizing recovery likelihood on the pair (y_t, x_{t+1}), and the initializer is updated by regressing toward ỹ_t (the EBM-refined sample) rather than the ground-truth y_t. Three practical designs further improve performance: (1) a cosine noise schedule that emphasizes low-SNR regions critical for high-level structure, (2) a variance-reduction trick that fixes the same Gaussian noise ε for both x_t and x_{t+1} so they become deterministic linear interpolations between x_0 and ε, and (3) a final Tweedie's-formula denoising step with an amplified gradient coefficient of 2.0. The initializer uses a U-Net with halved channels relative to the EBM, and the cooperative training lets the initializer amortize MCMC transitions, reducing required Langevin steps from 30 to 15 per noise level while achieving FID 3.68 on CIFAR-10 and 9.35 on ImageNet 32×32.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, CDRL achieves a FID of 4.31 (3.68 with the large architecture), substantially outperforming the prior best EBM method DRL (FID 9.58) and the next-best EBM CLEL-large (FID 8.61). On ImageNet 32×32, CDRL achieves FID 9.35, while DRL fails to converge entirely. Notably, CDRL requires only 90 total MCMC steps (6 noise levels × 15 Langevin steps) versus DRL's 180, halving the sampling cost while more than doubling the quality. For OOD detection on CIFAR-10, CDRL achieves AUROC scores of 0.75/0.78/0.84 on CIFAR-10 interpolation/CIFAR-100/CelebA, outperforming all explicit density baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDRL decisively outperforms the closest EBM baseline DRL (Gao et al., 2021), reducing CIFAR-10 FID from 9.58 to 4.31 while also halving the MCMC steps from 180 to 90. Among EBM methods, CDRL surpasses CLEL-large (8.61), EGC (5.36), VAEBM (12.16), and all other EBM baselines by wide margins. On ImageNet 32×32, DRL fails to converge while CDRL achieves FID 9.35, making it the best-performing EBM on this dataset. Compared to non-EBM generative models, CDRL (4.31) is competitive with DDPM (3.17) and StyleGAN2-ADA (2.92) but trails DDPM++ (2.47) and NCSN++ (2.20) on CIFAR-10. For OOD detection, CDRL achieves the highest AUROC across all three OOD datasets (0.75/0.78/0.84), beating CLEL-Base (0.72/0.72/0.77) and VAEBM (0.70/0.62/0.77). On CelebA-HQ 256×256, CDRL (FID 10.74) is competitive with VQGAN+Transformer (10.2) but trails LDM (5.11), indicating room for improvement at higher resolutions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper fuses two previously separate lines of EBM research: the diffusion recovery likelihood (DRL) framework of Gao et al. (2021), which trains a sequence of EBMs on progressively noisier data, and the cooperative MCMC teaching scheme of CoopNets (Xie et al., 2018a), which jointly trains a top-down generator and an EBM. The key conceptual move is generalizing cooperative training from a single marginal EBM to the multi-noise-level conditional setting of DRL, where a learned initializer at each noise level amortizes the MCMC transitions of the corresponding conditional EBM. The paper also adapts classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022) to the EBM setting, showing that the linear combination of conditional and unconditional energy gradients works analogously to the diffusion model case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CDRL narrows the long-standing sample-quality gap between EBMs and diffusion models/GANs to near-parity (FID 3.68 on CIFAR-10 vs. 2.20 for NCSN++), while retaining EBM-specific capabilities like compositional generation, OOD detection, and likelihood-based scoring that diffusion models lack. The practical finding that halving MCMC steps (30→15) with an initializer actually improves quality, and that further reducing to 8 steps at inference costs little, makes EBMs substantially more tractable. Open questions include scaling to high-resolution generation (the paper only reaches 64×64 and 256×256 in latent space), whether the cooperative training scheme extends to non-Gaussian initializers, and whether the residual MCMC dependency can be eliminated entirely.
