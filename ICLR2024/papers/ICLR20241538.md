## Problem

Existing conditional diffusion models that incorporate depth maps or bounding boxes confine object placement to a 2D plane, making them unable to correctly render multiple objects at different z-axis depths with proper occlusion. Additionally, global conditions such as style or semantics from exemplar images are applied uniformly across the entire generated image, with no mechanism to localize them to specific spatial regions without explicit structural cues.

## Input / Output

The model consumes two monocular depth maps (from MiDaS) representing foreground and background objects, two exemplar RGB images (encoded via CLIP image encoder into global semantic tokens), a free-form text prompt, and a binary foreground mask M. At inference, images are generated at 768×768 using DDIM sampling with 50 steps and classifier-free guidance scale 7; during training, inputs are resized and center-cropped to 512×512. The binary mask is not fed as a spatial condition to the network but is instead flattened and repeated to construct a Boolean attention mask that governs which regions of the latent each semantic token can attend to.

## Method

CnC builds on a frozen Stable Diffusion backbone with a cloned trainable encoder E′ and center block C′ (initialized from Uni-ControlNet), and introduces two complementary modules. The local fuser employs Depth Disentanglement Training (DDT): synthetic image triplets {I_f, I_b, M} are created by masking the salient object and inpainting the remainder with SD's inpainting module, then extracting separate depth maps for I_f and I_b; these two depth maps pass through independent ResNet streams (a late-fusion design) before being concatenated and injected into E′ via feature denormalization, teaching the model relative z-axis occlusion. The global fuser projects CLIP image embeddings of I_s and I_b into N foreground and N background tokens each, concatenates them with text embeddings, and applies soft guidance: in every cross-attention layer, the similarity matrix S is element-wise multiplied by a Boolean matrix M′ constructed from the flattened mask M, forcing foreground tokens to attend only to foreground latent positions and background tokens to background positions, thereby localizing global semantics without any explicit spatial conditioning signal. The local and global fusers are trained independently (28 and 24 epochs respectively) and then jointly finetuned for 9 epochs with per-condition dropout to encourage generalization across condition combinations.

## Results

On the COCO-Stuff validation set (5K images), CnC Finetuned achieves the best FID (17.254), IS (32.131), and CLIP Score (25.940) for the semantics-only condition, and the best FID (18.191), IS (29.304), and CLIP Score (25.880) for the combined depth+semantics condition. For depth-only generation, ControlNet retains the best FID (17.303) and IS (31.652), while CnC Finetuned leads on CLIP Score (26.870). In reconstruction, CnC Finetuned achieves the lowest LPIPS (0.6509 on COCO-Stuff, 0.6216 on Pick-a-Pic) and MAE (0.0990, 0.1027) across both datasets, outperforming Uni-ControlNet and T2I-Adapter by a large margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CnC Finetuned substantially outperforms all four baselines on the semantics and depth+semantics conditions. Against T2I-Adapter, the gap is most dramatic: CnC Finetuned reduces FID from 35.812 to 17.254 (semantics) and from 30.611 to 18.191 (depth+semantics), while also improving IS by roughly 8–9 points. Against Uni-ControlNet, CnC Finetuned wins on every metric for both semantics (FID 17.254 vs 23.632; IS 32.131 vs 28.364) and depth+semantics (FID 18.191 vs 18.945; IS 29.304 vs 28.218; CLIP 25.880 vs 24.839). For depth-only generation, ControlNet retains the best FID (17.303) and IS (31.652), but CnC Finetuned leads on CLIP Score (26.870 vs 25.741), suggesting better text alignment when multiple depth maps are processed. GLIGEN, which only supports a single depth map, is outperformed by CnC Finetuned on CLIP Score for depth (26.870 vs 25.815) and is not directly comparable on the multi-condition settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

CnC builds directly on the ControlNet/Uni-ControlNet paradigm of freezing a Stable Diffusion backbone and training a cloned encoder-center block to ingest additional conditions, but departs in two key ways. First, it introduces depth disentanglement training (DDT), which uses synthetic image triplets (foreground, inpainted background, mask) to teach the model 3D relative depth ordering—something prior single-depth-map or bounding-box approaches (ControlNet, GLIGEN, T2I-Adapter) cannot express. Second, it proposes soft guidance, a cross-attention masking mechanism that localizes CLIP image-embedding semantics to specific regions without requiring explicit structural cues, addressing a gap left by Uni-ControlNet and Composer, which accept multiple conditions but cannot disentangle overlapping global semantics spatially.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical recipe for depth-aware multi-object composition and region-specific semantic injection in latent diffusion models, which is directly useful for creative and design workflows requiring precise spatial control. However, the framework is currently limited to a strict foreground/background binary, and the reliance on synthetic triplets (generated via SD inpainting) introduces a circular dependency on the generative prior. Open questions include whether DDT generalizes to more than two depth layers, whether soft guidance scales to many simultaneous semantic sources, and whether the approach transfers to non-SD backbones or higher resolutions.
