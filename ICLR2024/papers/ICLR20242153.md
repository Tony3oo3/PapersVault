## Problem

Mean-field Langevin dynamics (MFLD) provides powerful tools for single-objective optimization over probability measures, but extending it to distributional minimax (saddle-point) problems was largely unexplored. The natural descent-ascent MFLD (MFL-DA) lacks convergence guarantees—simultaneous updates can cycle or diverge even for simple matrix games—and existing results either fail to prove convergence or require unrealistic assumptions such as compact manifolds or near-static flows where one player updates infinitely faster than the other. The paper asks whether symmetric MFLD algorithms can be constructed for distributional minimax with global convergence to the mixed Nash equilibrium and uniform-in-time control over discretization errors.

## Input / Output

The algorithms take a bilinear interaction potential Q(x,y) defining the saddle-point objective, Gaussian base measures ρ^μ and ρ^ν, a temperature λ controlling entropic regularization, and N initial particle positions sampled from the base measures. The output is a set of N particles representing the mixed Nash equilibrium (μ*, ν*) of the entropy-regularized problem, with convergence measured via the Nikaidô-Isoda error and squared Wasserstein distance. For the zero-sum Markov game application, the scheme iteratively produces the regularized Markov perfect equilibrium policy and value function V* at each state, with linear convergence rate γ^k up to optimization and Bellman errors.

## Method

The paper proposes two symmetric algorithms. MFL-AG (single-loop) replaces the MFL-DA drift with a weighted historical average of the functional gradient, inspired by dual averaging: the drift at time t is (1/B_t)∫₀ᵗ β_s ∇(δL/δμ)(μ_s,ν_s)(X_t) ds rather than the instantaneous gradient. This rolling average slows the dynamics so the flow can track its proximal target distribution, yielding O(1/t) average-iterate convergence to the MNE (optimized at β_t = t). MFL-ABR (double-loop) runs an inner Langevin loop with the gradient anchored at the previous outer-loop output, then performs a discretized best-response flow update in the outer loop, achieving linear last-iterate convergence. The key technical novelty is a uniform-in-time propagation of chaos analysis that handles the dependency of particle interactions on the entire history of distributions (μ_s, ν_s)_{s≤t}, which cannot be integrated out as in standard MFLD, requiring a step-by-step atomic-measure argument with a uniform law of large numbers.

## Results

The paper establishes average-iterate convergence of MFL-AG to the mixed Nash equilibrium with rate O(1/t) for the weighted scheme β_t = t^r (optimized at r = 1), and O(log t / t) for unweighted averaging. For the discretized algorithm, the squared Wasserstein distance to the MNE is bounded by O(1/K) + O(√η) + O(1/√N), where K is the number of epochs, η the time step, and N the particle count. MFL-ABR achieves linear (last-iterate) convergence in the outer loop: NI(μ_k, ν_k) ≤ 2(C_μ + C_ν)exp(−βk) + 12λ^{−1/2}(C_μ^{3/2} + C_ν^{3/2})exp(−αλτ) + Cβ. In the zero-sum Markov game application, the two-step scheme converges linearly as ‖V^{(k)} − V*‖_∞ ≤ γ^k‖V^{(0)} − V*‖_∞ + (ε_L + ε_Q)/(1−γ).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MFL-AG and MFL-ABR against the standard mean-field Langevin descent-ascent (MFL-DA) dynamics on a 1D bilinear objective with sigmoid nonlinearity Q(x,y) = (1 + e^{−(x−y)²})^{−1}, using 1,000 particles. MFL-DA converges to a distribution much more rapidly in terms of W_1 distance between successive iterates, but converges to a suboptimal solution. In the 3-point Nikaidō-Isoda error comparison (Figure 1e), MFL-AG gradually reduces its NI error to zero, dominating MFL-DA which retains nonzero error, and MFL-ABR subsequently also reaches zero error. The authors conclude that despite slower convergence rates, MFL-AG and MFL-ABR substantially outperform ordinary descent-ascent in long-term optimality, validating the theoretical convergence guarantees that MFL-DA lacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends mean-field Langevin dynamics (MFLD) from single-objective optimization to distributional minimax problems, drawing on three distinct lineages: dual averaging methods (Nesterov, Xiao, Nitanda et al.) which inspire the weighted drift averaging in MFL-AG; the best-response flow of Lascu et al. (2023) which motivates the double-loop MFL-ABR; and the uniform-in-time propagation of chaos framework of Chen et al. (2022) and Suzuki et al. (2023), which is extended here to handle the novel challenge of history-dependent proximal distributions. The paper departs from prior minimax MFLD work (Domingo-Enrich et al. 2020, Ma & Ying 2021, Lu 2022) that either fails to establish convergence, requires unrealistic compact-manifold assumptions, or restricts to near-static flows where one player updates infinitely faster than the other. Conceptually, MFL-AG with uniform weights connects to fictitious play (Brown 1951), but the continuous-time Langevin formulation with entropic regularization and the resulting Fokker-Planck structure are new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first symmetric MFLD for distributional minimax with global convergence guarantees, filling a gap that has left the field without principled continuous-time algorithms for problems like GAN training and zero-sum Markov games. The uniform-in-time propagation of chaos result is technically significant because the history-dependent proximal distributions invalidate the standard lifted-measure approach, requiring new step-by-step perturbative arguments. Limitations include the restriction to convex-concave functionals, the need for entropic regularization, the absence of a particle-discretization analysis for MFL-ABR, and 1D toy experiments that do not validate scalability. Open questions highlighted by the authors include developing single-loop algorithms with last-iterate convergence, extending to nonconvex-nonconcave parametrizations, and applications to multi-agent reinforcement learning.
