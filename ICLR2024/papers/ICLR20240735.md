## Problem

Training LLMs from scratch is prohibitively expensive, yet existing fusion techniques are ill-suited for LLMs: ensembles require deploying all source models at inference time, and weight merging assumes identical architectures. This paper asks whether the collective knowledge of architecturally diverse LLMs can be externalized and transferred into a single target model that surpasses every source.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240735/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes K source LLMs (e.g., Llama-2 7B, OpenLLaMA 7B, MPT 7B) with differing architectures and tokenizers, a compact training corpus (MiniPile, ~1.8B tokens), and a target LLM initialized from one of the sources. It produces a single fused LLM whose parameters are updated via one epoch of continual training (~33 hours on 8×A100). A notable constraint is that source LLMs must be run in inference mode over the entire corpus to extract per-token probability matrices before any training begins, making the pipeline two-phase: distribution extraction followed by target-model optimization.

## Method

FUSELLM externalizes each source LLM's knowledge as a per-token probabilistic distribution matrix over its vocabulary, then aligns these matrices across heterogeneous tokenizers using a minimum-edit-distance (MinED) strategy that relaxes the exact-match constraint of prior work. Aligned matrices are fused via a MinCE function (selecting the distribution with the lowest cross-entropy against gold labels) or an AvgCE weighted average. The target LLM is then continually trained with a combined objective L = λ·L_CLM + (1−λ)·L_Fusion (λ=0.9), where L_Fusion minimizes the KL divergence between the target's output distribution and the fused source distribution at every token position. This distribution-level supervision, rather than raw text supervision, accelerates convergence by ~3.9× in token efficiency and enables the fused model to exceed all individual sources on reasoning, commonsense, and code-generation benchmarks.

## Results

FUSELLM achieves an average of 41.75 on Big-Bench Hard (BBH), 64.56 on Common Sense (CS), and 15.56 on MultiPL-E (ME), representing relative improvements of +5.16%, +1.25%, and +6.36% over the Llama-2 7B target model, respectively. The continual-training-only baseline (Llama-2 CLM) yields only modest gains of +1.86% (BBH), +0.16% (CS), and +1.37% (ME), confirming that the distribution-fusion objective drives most of the improvement. On the simulated same-architecture experiment (Pythia 1B), FUSELLM achieves the lowest average perplexity (0.7034 BPB) compared to ensemble (0.7262) and weight merging (0.7112).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FUSELLM outperforms the Llama-2 7B target model on all three benchmarks (BBH: 41.75 vs 39.70; CS: 64.56 vs 63.76; ME: 15.56 vs 14.63), with the largest gain on code generation (+6.36%). Against OpenLLaMA 7B and MPT 7B, FUSELLM wins decisively on BBH (41.75 vs 33.87/33.38) and CS (64.56 vs 59.78/61.13) but trails on ME (15.56 vs 18.11/17.26), which the authors attribute to Llama-2's weaker code pre-training and limited code data in the continual-training corpus. Compared to the LLM-Blender ensemble method (Jiang et al., 2023), FUSELLM substantially outperforms the Rank&Fuse variant on BBH (41.75 vs 24.48) and ME (15.56 vs 0.06), though LLM-Blender (Rank-only) edges out FUSELLM on ME (17.85 vs 15.56) while still losing on BBH (37.17 vs 41.75). In the same-architecture Pythia 1B experiment, FUSELLM achieves the lowest average perplexity (0.7034 BPB) versus ensemble (0.7262) and weight merging (0.7112), demonstrating its advantage over both traditional fusion paradigms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model fusion literature (ensemble methods and weight merging) by introducing a distribution-level approach that operates on the generative probability matrices of source LLMs rather than their parameters or outputs. It builds directly on multi-teacher knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015) in using teacher distributions as training signal, but departs in two key ways: the target model is not constrained to be smaller than the sources, and the goal is for the target to surpass all sources rather than merely approach them. The token alignment mechanism extends Fu et al. (2023) by replacing exact-match constraints with a minimum edit distance (MinED) strategy to handle vocabulary mismatches across architecturally diverse tokenizers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is clear: combining existing open-source LLMs avoids the astronomical cost of training from scratch while potentially exceeding any single model's capabilities. However, the improvements are modest (5.16% on BBH, 1.25% on commonsense, 6.36% on code generation), and the method's effectiveness is sensitive to target model choice and training corpus composition—using the strongest source as target can actually degrade performance. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more than three source models, how to optimally select the continual training corpus for downstream task relevance, and whether the fusion function becomes more critical as source capabilities diverge further. The finding that three architecturally diverse 7B models can outperform a single 13B model via distillation ('different can also be more') suggests diversity in pre-training may be as valuable as scale.
