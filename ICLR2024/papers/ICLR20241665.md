## Problem

Despite the empirical success of diffusion models, it remains theoretically unclear whether gradient-based algorithms like gradient descent can provably learn the score function with neural networks. Existing theoretical results for diffusion models are algorithm-agnostic, providing guarantees only under the assumption of an already-accurate score estimator, leaving a gap between theory and the practical training procedure.

## Input / Output

The method consumes triples (t_j, X_{0,j}, X_{t_j}) where X_{0,j} is drawn from the unknown data distribution p_0, t_j is sampled uniformly from [T_0, T], and X_{t_j} is generated from the Gaussian transition kernel of the OU forward process. The output is a d-dimensional score estimate s_W(x,t) = (α(t)/h(t))·Π_D(f_W(x,t)) − x/h(t), where f_W is a two-layer ReLU network with fixed second-layer weights. A key structural challenge is that the input X_t is unbounded (unlike standard supervised learning), the output is vector-valued, and the time variable t enters as an additional input dimension, making the problem a regression with noisy labels rather than standard supervised learning.

## Method

The paper proposes a parametric form for the score estimator based on the score decomposition ∇log p_t(x) = (α(t)/h(t))·E[X_0|X_t=x] − x/h(t), which transforms denoising score matching into a regression problem where X_0 serves as a noisy label and E[X_0|X_t] is the true label. A two-layer ReLU network with fixed second-layer weights is trained by gradient descent, and the NTK framework is used to couple the neural network dynamics to a kernel regression problem in the RKHS induced by the NTK. Three challenges specific to score matching are addressed: unbounded input is handled via a truncation argument exploiting the Gaussian tail of the OU process; vector-valued output is controlled through a vector-valued localized Rademacher complexity bound; and the time variable is incorporated by treating (x, t−T_0) as the joint input to the kernel. An early-stopping rule for the kernel regression, extended to the multi-output setting, yields the first generalization (sample complexity) bounds for GD-trained neural networks in the score-matching problem.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or numerical benchmarks. The headline result (Theorem 3.12) establishes the first generalization error bound for GD-trained two-layer ReLU networks performing score matching in diffusion models. The bound decomposes into: an exponentially small tail term O(R^{d-2} e^{-R²/4}), an approximation error dA²(R_H, R) from the RKHS universal approximation theorem, a coupling error Õ(d^{10} N⁹ C_max^{12} / (√m λ₀² δ⁴ Δ²)) between neural network training and kernel regression, a label mismatch term involving vector-valued Rademacher complexity, and an early-stopping error ε(N, T̂). The width requirement is m = Ω((dN)⁶ C_max⁶ / (λ₀¹⁰ δ³ Δ²)) and the learning rate is η = O(λ₀ / ((dN)² C_max⁴)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not perform empirical comparisons against other methods, as it is a theoretical analysis. In terms of prior theoretical work, the paper positions itself against Chen et al. (2023b) and Oko et al. (2023), which are identified as the only prior results providing score estimation guarantees under L² norm. However, those works are algorithm-agnostic (they do not analyze any specific training procedure), whereas this paper provides the first algorithm-dependent (GD-specific) generalization bound. The paper also contrasts with the broader NTK literature (Arora et al. 2019a,b; Du et al. 2018,2019; Allen-Zhu et al. 2019b), noting that none of those works handle noisy labels, unbounded inputs, or vector-valued outputs — the three challenges specific to score matching. Kuzborskij & Szepesvári (2022) is cited as the only prior work studying GD-trained networks with additive noise, but it does not address the diffusion-model-specific structure (time variable, score decomposition).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected theoretical literatures: diffusion model analysis (which assumed access to accurate score estimators and studied algorithm-agnostic properties) and NTK-based deep learning theory (which established convergence and generalization for supervised learning but did not handle noisy labels, unbounded inputs, or vector-valued outputs). The key conceptual move is reformulating denoising score matching (Vincent, 2011) as a regression problem with noisy labels via the score decomposition (Lemma 3.1), then adapting NTK coupling arguments (Jacot et al., 2018; Arora et al., 2019) to handle three challenges absent from standard supervised learning: unbounded input (addressed via truncation and Gaussian tail bounds), vector-valued output (via matrix-valued NTK and vector-valued Rademacher complexity), and an additional time variable (via the shifted input (x, t−T₀)). The early-stopping rule extends Raskutti et al. (2014) to the multi-output setting, and the RKHS approximation theorem builds on Bach (2017) for Lipschitz functions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithm-dependent (GD-specific) generalization bound for neural network score estimation, closing a gap between the algorithm-agnostic diffusion model theory and the practical reality of training with gradient descent. Combined with existing distribution recovery results, it yields the first end-to-end guarantee from a provably efficient training algorithm to sample quality. However, the practical relevance is currently limited: the analysis covers only a two-layer ReLU network (not U-nets or transformers), assumes compact support, uses deterministic GD (not SGD/Adam), and the dimension dependence is acknowledged as sub-optimal. Open questions include extending to manifold-structured data, deeper architectures, stochastic optimization, and understanding whether the NTK regime is necessary or whether finite-width networks can be analyzed directly.
