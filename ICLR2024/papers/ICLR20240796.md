## Problem

Domain Randomization (DR) for sim-to-real transfer in RL requires manually tuning the sampling distribution over dynamics parameters: too narrow a distribution yields poor generalization, while too wide a distribution produces overly conservative policies that fail to solve the task. Existing automated DR methods (LSDR, AutoDR) either require a reference target distribution, are confined to uniform distributions, or demand additional Monte-Carlo policy evaluations, making them data-inefficient. The paper asks whether the training dynamics distribution can be automatically shaped during RL training without real-world data or extra environment interactions.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a state vector augmented with the 5 most recent state-action pairs (history-dependent policy to handle latent dynamics), and outputs normalized joint acceleration commands in [-1,1] per joint. A user-defined binary success indicator σ(τ) (e.g., distance-to-goal threshold or return lower bound) is the only task-specific input required beyond the RL reward. The method produces a single generalizable policy π_θ and a converged Beta-distribution parameterization φ over the randomized dynamics parameters, deployable zero-shot to the real robot.

## Method

DORAEMON formulates DR as a constrained optimization: maximize the differential entropy of the training dynamics distribution ν_φ(ξ) subject to maintaining an in-distribution success rate G(θ,φ) ≥ α, solved iteratively with a KL-divergence trust region between successive distribution updates. The policy is trained with SAC using history-dependent actions and an asymmetric actor-critic (critic conditioned on true dynamics parameters), while the distribution update reuses naturally collected training trajectories via an importance-sampling estimator—no extra rollouts are needed. A backup optimization problem (Eq. 6) backtracks the distribution within the trust region when the success constraint is violated, preventing performance collapse from excessive randomization. The dynamics distribution is parameterized as uncorrelated univariate Beta distributions over bounded physical parameter ranges, though the formulation generalizes to any parametric family with computable entropy and KL divergence.

## Results

On the PandaPush Sim2Real task (7-DoF Franka Panda arm pushing a box with unknown center of mass), DORAEMON achieves a 60% success rate and 2.68±1.01 cm distance to target, outperforming LSDR (46.67%, 7.38±8.69 cm), AutoDR (26.67%, 4.17±2.07 cm), No-DR (13.33%), and Fixed-DR (0%). In Sim2Sim evaluation on the same task, DORAEMON reaches 66.57% success rate versus 37.77% for LSDR and 30.45% for AutoDR. Across six MuJoCo locomotion/control benchmarks (CartPole, Hopper, Walker2D, HalfCheetah, Swimmer, SwingUpCartpole), DORAEMON consistently achieves higher global success rates and faster convergence than all baselines, with the advantage being most pronounced in high-dimensional dynamics spaces (e.g., Walker2D with 13 randomized parameters).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DORAEMON outperforms both LSDR (Mozian et al., IROS 2020) and AutoDR (Akkaya et al., 2019) across all evaluated environments and both Sim2Sim and Sim2Real settings. On the PandaPush Sim2Real task, DORAEMON achieves 60% success rate versus 46.67% for LSDR and 26.67% for AutoDR, with a substantially lower distance to target (2.68 cm vs. 7.38 cm and 4.17 cm respectively). In the six MuJoCo Sim2Sim benchmarks, DORAEMON consistently reaches higher global success rates and converges faster; LSDR tends to stall at intermediate entropy values due to its M-projection formulation, while AutoDR suffers from data-inefficient single-dimension updates and higher variance across seeds. Fixed-DR (uniform distribution over the full search space) fails entirely on PandaPush (0% success) and underperforms DORAEMON on all MuJoCo tasks, confirming the importance of the gradual curriculum that DORAEMON provides. The authors attribute AutoDR's limitations to its inability to update all parameter dimensions simultaneously and its reliance on boundary-only performance signals, whereas DORAEMON leverages all sampled dynamics parameters via importance sampling to update the full distribution at each step.

## Relation to Prior Work

DORAEMON carves new ground at the intersection of domain randomization and self-paced curriculum learning. It departs from LSDR (Mozian et al., 2020) by eliminating the need for a reference target distribution and expensive Monte-Carlo policy evaluations, and from AutoDR (Akkaya et al., 2019) by supporting arbitrary parametric distributions (not just uniform) and updating all parameter dimensions simultaneously via importance sampling. Conceptually, it repurposes the constrained optimization structure of self-paced learning (Klink et al., 2020a,b) to the latent MDP setting of DR, where the agent must learn a context-agnostic history-dependent policy rather than a context-conditioned one, and where no target task distribution is given.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DORAEMON addresses a practical bottleneck in sim-to-real RL: the tedious manual tuning of DR distributions that directly determines whether a policy generalizes to the real world. By reducing this to a single binary success criterion and an α threshold, it makes DR substantially more accessible while being more sample-efficient than prior automated methods. Key limitations include potential catastrophic forgetting when the backup optimization backtracks the distribution, and performance degradation as the distribution approaches maximum entropy. Open questions include whether multimodal distributions could isolate infeasible dynamics regions, whether prior knowledge of real parameters could bias distribution growth, and whether the approach scales to even higher-dimensional or unbounded dynamics spaces.
