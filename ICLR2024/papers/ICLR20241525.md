## Problem

Despite widespread empirical use of transfer learning for outlier detection, there is no fundamental understanding of when and how knowledge transfers from a source to a target rare-class distribution, nor what rates are achievable in finite-sample regimes. This paper establishes the first information-theoretic limits (minimax lower bounds) for supervised outlier transfer learning under Neyman-Pearson classification and shows they are adaptively achievable.

## Input / Output

The procedure consumes n₀ i.i.d. samples from the common class μ₀, n_S samples from the source rare class μ₁,S, and n_T samples from the target rare class μ₁,T (with n_T allowed to be zero). It outputs a 0-1 decision rule ĥ from a hypothesis class H of finite VC dimension d_H that satisfies a relaxed Type-I error constraint R_{μ₀}(ĥ) ≤ α + ε₀. The theoretical guarantees are expressed as bounds on the target-excess error E_{1,T}(ĥ) in terms of sample sizes, VC dimension, and the outlier transfer exponent ρ, with the key rate being min{(d_H/n_S)^{1/2ρ}, (d_H/n_T)^{1/2}}.

## Method

The paper introduces an outlier transfer exponent ρ that quantifies how effectively source samples reveal the target decision boundary, adapted from Hanneke & Kpotufe's transfer exponent but tailored to the Neyman-Pearson constraint structure. A key structural insight (Proposition 3.6) shows that, unlike in balanced classification, source and target distributions that appear very different can share identical optimal classifiers, enabling fast transfer even across seemingly dissimilar sources. The minimax lower bound (Theorem 4.5) is derived via Tsybakov's method with a Gilbert-Varshamov packing over the VC-shattered domain, showing the rate is governed by min{(d_H/n_S)^{1/2ρ}, (d_H/n_T)^{1/2}} plus an irreducible gap Δ. An adaptive procedure (Theorem 4.6) matches this lower bound up to logarithmic factors by comparing empirical risks of source-trained and target-trained classifiers and selecting the better one, requiring no prior knowledge of ρ or Δ.

## Results

The paper establishes a minimax lower bound (Theorem 4.5) showing that for any hypothesis class of VC dimension $d_{\mathcal{H}} \geq 3$, the target-excess error of any outlier-transfer learner is at least $c \cdot \min\{\Delta + (d_{\mathcal{H}}/n_S)^{1/(2\rho)},\; (d_{\mathcal{H}}/n_T)^{1/2}\}$ with constant probability, where $\rho$ is the outlier transfer exponent and $\Delta$ bounds the excess error of the optimal source classifier. Theorem 4.6 then shows this rate is achievable up to logarithmic factors by an adaptive procedure (4.4) that requires no prior knowledge of $\rho$ or $\Delta$. In the special case where source and target share the same optimal classifier ($\Delta = 0$), the rate simplifies to $\min\{(d_{\mathcal{H}}/n_S)^{1/(2\rho)},\; (d_{\mathcal{H}}/n_T)^{1/2}\}$, meaning source samples alone can drive target error to zero asymptotically.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The theoretical contributions are positioned relative to three prior works: (1) Scott (2019) showed consistency of transfer in outlier detection under the restrictive assumption that source density ratios are monotonic functions of target density ratios; this paper's Proposition 3.6 generalizes the equivalence condition to a broader class of distributions via the restricted hypothesis class $\mathcal{U}^*$. (2) Hanneke & Kpotufe (2019) established minimax rates for transfer in standard classification using a transfer exponent; this paper adapts that framework to Neyman-Pearson outlier detection, showing the outlier transfer exponent $\rho$ can differ substantially from the standard transfer exponent (e.g., $\rho=1$ vs. $\rho>1$ for the same distributions). (3) Cannon et al. (2002) derived convergence rates for supervised Neyman-Pearson classification without transfer; this paper extends their empirical risk minimization formulation to the transfer setting and characterizes the fundamental limits via the outlier transfer exponent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the transfer exponent framework of Hanneke & Kpotufe (2019) from traditional classification to the Neyman-Pearson (outlier detection) setting, and builds on Scott (2019)'s initial consistency results for outlier transfer by deriving the first finite-sample minimax bounds. It also builds on Cannon et al. (2002) for the empirical Neyman-Pearson formulation. The key conceptual departure from prior transfer learning work is that in Neyman-Pearson classification, the optimal decision rule depends on density-ratio level sets rather than the distributions themselves, so seemingly very different source and target rare-class distributions can share the exact same optimal classifier—a phenomenon with no analogue in standard classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous finite-sample characterization of when and how much information a related but imperfect rare-class source can provide for target outlier detection, filling a gap between the purely asymptotic consistency results of Scott (2019) and the practical need for rate guarantees. The conceptual insight that outlier transfer is fundamentally different from traditional transfer (dissimilar distributions can be 'close' in the transfer sense) should reshape how practitioners think about source selection for anomaly detection. Open questions include extending the results beyond finite VC dimension hypothesis classes to nonparametric or neural network settings, and understanding whether the adaptive procedure's logarithmic gap to the minimax rate can be closed.
