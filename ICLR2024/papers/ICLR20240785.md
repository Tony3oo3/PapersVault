## Problem

Standard VAEs employ a Gaussian prior whose tails decay too rapidly, causing over-regularization that forces encoded points into low-density regions and fails to preserve heavy-tailed structures present in real-world data. Existing remedies (β-VAE, alternative priors) either merely rescale the KL regularizer or require numerical integration, and none provide a principled joint optimization framework for heavy-tailed models.

## Input / Output

The model accepts 64×64 or 128×128 RGB face images (CelebA), 32×32 natural images (CIFAR-100-LT), or univariate/bivariate data points, and produces reconstructed images, generated images, and m-dimensional latent vectors z. A notable design choice is that generation samples from an alternative t-prior p*_ν(z) with data-dependent scale τ² rather than the original prior, and the entire model is governed by a single hyperparameter ν (degrees of freedom) that simultaneously controls tail heaviness and regularization strength.

## Method

t³ VAE replaces the Gaussian prior, encoder, and decoder with multivariate Student's t-distributions, yielding a joint model p_{θ,ν}(x,z) of power form that generalizes the exponential form of the standard VAE. Drawing on information geometry, the paper reformulates the ELBO as joint minimization of divergence between model and data distribution manifolds, then substitutes KL divergence with γ-power divergence (γ = −2/(ν+n+m)), producing a new γ-loss objective with an approximate closed form amenable to Monte Carlo estimation via a t-reparametrization trick. The γ-loss decomposes into an MSE reconstruction term plus a regularizer equivalent to γ-power divergence from the encoder to an alternative t-prior, where ν controls both the target scale τ² and the regularizer coefficient α. From a Bayesian perspective, the model is equivalent to introducing a latent precision λ ~ χ²(ν) shared by both x and z, and the framework extends naturally to a two-level hierarchical t³ HVAE for higher-resolution image generation.

## Results

On CelebA 64×64 reconstruction, t³ VAE (ν=10) achieves the lowest FID of 39.4 overall, outperforming the standard VAE (57.9) by 32% and showing the largest gains on rare classes (e.g., Bald: 66.5 vs. 85.8 for VAE). On the long-tailed CIFAR100-LT dataset, t³ VAE achieves FID 97.5 at ρ=1 and 128.7 at ρ=100, compared to 256.1 and 287.3 for the standard VAE, with the performance gap widening as imbalance increases. For CelebA generation, t³ VAE achieves FID 50.6, beating the next-best β-VAE (51.8) and the standard VAE (64.7). On synthetic heavy-tailed data, t³ VAE is the only model whose generated samples are statistically indistinguishable from the true distribution in the tail regions (MMD p-values > 0.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

t³ VAE outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset and metric reported. On CelebA reconstruction, the closest competitor is β-VAE (β=0.05) at FID 40.4 vs. 39.4, but t³ VAE pulls ahead on rare classes (Bald: 66.5 vs. 69.3; Gray: 67.2 vs. 71.1). Tilted VAE (τ=50) is second overall (42.6) but trails t³ VAE by 3.2 FID. The standard VAE (57.9) and FactorVAE (59.8) are substantially worse, while the other t-based models—Student-t VAE (78.4) and DE-VAE (58.9)—perform comparably to or worse than the Gaussian VAE, confirming that simply using univariate t-distributions without the joint power-form structure is insufficient. On CIFAR100-LT, t³ VAE's advantage grows with imbalance: at ρ=100 it achieves 128.7 FID versus 160.6 (β-VAE), 193.2 (Tilted VAE), and 287.3 (VAE), demonstrating that heavy-tailed modeling specifically benefits long-tailed class distributions. On CelebA generation, t³ VAE (50.6) narrowly beats β-VAE (51.8) and clearly outperforms Tilted VAE (59.2) and VAE (64.7). On the synthetic heavy-tailed MMD tests, t³ VAE is the only model that fails to reject the null hypothesis in both left and right tail regions, while all other models (including VAE-st) are rejected at p < 0.001 in at least one tail.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads: the information-geometric reformulation of the ELBO as joint minimization of KL divergence between statistical manifolds (Han et al., 2020) and Eguchi's (2021) theory of γ-power divergence, where power families play the role that exponential families play for KL. Prior t-based VAEs (Student-t VAE, VAE-st, DE-VAE) modified only one or two of the three distributions and still relied on the ELBO, often requiring numerical integration for the KL regularizer. t³ VAE departs by placing multivariate Student's t-distributions in all three roles (prior, encoder, decoder) and replacing KL with γ-power divergence, yielding a closed-form objective (γ-loss) that is amenable to Monte Carlo estimation via a t-reparametrization trick, with no numerical integration bottleneck.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, single-hyperparameter (ν) control over regularization strength that naturally interpolates between Gaussian VAE (ν→∞) and plain autoencoder (ν→2), addressing over-regularization without ad-hoc weighting as in β-VAE. The strong results on imbalanced CIFAR-100-LT and rare CelebA classes suggest heavy-tailed latent models are particularly suited to long-tailed real-world data, with practical implications for any VAE-based pipeline dealing with class imbalance. Open questions include whether the order-γ² approximation in the γ-loss remains tight in very high-dimensional or highly non-Gaussian settings, and whether the information-geometric framework extends naturally to other divergence-based inference beyond VAEs.
