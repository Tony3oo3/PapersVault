## Problem

Variational Quantum Algorithms (VQAs) lack a principled way to enforce physical symmetries (e.g., particle number, total spin) as hard constraints, with existing approaches relying on soft penalty terms in the Hamiltonian that can still yield unphysical states. The Hamming Weight (HW) preserving ansatz, which restricts evolution to a C(n,k)-dimensional subspace, was previously under-theorized: its expressivity, universality conditions, and required circuit depth were not rigorously characterized, limiting its adoption beyond narrow applications like quantum data loaders.

## Input / Output

For ground state energy estimation, the method takes a molecular Hamiltonian (obtained via OpenFermion in STO-3G basis) along with the number of orbitals n and electrons k, and outputs an energy estimate bounded below by the true ground state energy. For feature selection, it takes an n×n QUBO information matrix Q (built from mutual information and conditional mutual information scores) and a target feature count k, outputting a binary selection vector of exactly k features. In both cases, the quantum state is confined to the C(n,k)-dimensional Hamming-weight-k subspace, and the initial state is the Hartree-Fock state (not the all-zeros state used by HEA).

## Method

The paper establishes a theoretical pipeline for HW preserving ansatz design: (1) quantum optimal control theory via Dynamical Lie Algebra (DLA) determines whether a given gate is universal in the d_k-dimensional subspace (universality requires dim(g) = d_k²); (2) overparameterization theory (Larocca et al., 2023) links the critical parameter count M_c to dim(g), guiding circuit depth selection; (3) a trainability theorem shows gradient variance scales as 16k²(n−k)²/(n⁴d_k), confirming no barren plateaus for small d_k. Using this framework, the authors derive a new BS gate with a Hermitian generator H_BS containing complex off-diagonal entries (a=1/2, b=(1+i)/(2√2)), which achieves full DLA dimension d_k² under both nearest-neighbor and full connectivity—unlike the RBS gate whose DLA dimension is only d_k(d_k−1)/2. The ansatz is validated on unitary approximation (confirming the d_k² parameter threshold) and applied to ground state energy estimation (H₂, LiH, H₂O, NH₃) and feature selection (four UCI datasets), where it matches or exceeds UCCSD and HEA while operating in a strictly smaller search space.

## Results

On ground state energy estimation, the HW preserving ansatz (BS gate with full connectivity) achieves energy errors below 1×10⁻¹⁰ Ha across H₂, LiH-8, LiH, and H₂O, far exceeding the chemical accuracy threshold of 1.6×10⁻³ Ha. On feature selection (selecting top-3 features), the BS gate achieves the best mutual-information scores on all four datasets: 1.136 (Wine Quality), 3.418 (Heart Disease), 1.198 (Titanic), and 8.617 (Dry Bean), outperforming the classical pyQUBO solver at every tested penalty coefficient. The RBS gate with full connectivity requires fewer parameters than BS to reach exact energy on ground state estimation, while RBS with nearest-neighbor connectivity fails entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HEA (Kandala et al., 2017), the HW preserving ansatz matches performance on H₂ (d_k=6) but clearly outperforms it on LiH (d_k=495) and H₂O (d_k=1001), where HEA's 2ⁿ search space grows exponentially while the HW subspace remains polynomial. Against UCCSD (Romero et al., 2018), the HW preserving ansatz achieves comparable or slightly better energy estimates with fewer parameters, and unlike UCCSD it is not tied to a specific chemical ansatz structure. Against the classical pyQUBO solver (Zaman et al., 2021), both BS and RBS gates consistently outperform pyQUBO on all four feature selection datasets; pyQUBO with small α (0.5, 1) produces invalid over-selected solutions, while large α (50–1000) yields valid but suboptimal scores (e.g., 0.983 vs. 1.136 on Wine Quality). The key advantage of the HW preserving approach is that it enforces the constraint as a hard structural property of the circuit, eliminating the need for any penalty tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revisits the Hamming Weight (HW) preserving ansatz originally introduced for quantum orthogonal neural networks (Kerenidis et al., 2021) and the RBS gate used in quantum hedging (Cherrat et al., 2023), repositioning them as a general-purpose tool for incorporating hard symmetry constraints into VQAs. It departs from prior work by providing a rigorous theoretical foundation—combining Dynamical Lie Algebra (DLA) analysis, quantum optimal control, and overparameterization theory—that was previously absent for HW preserving circuits. The paper also extends the gate design space beyond the RBS and XY-mixer gates by deriving a new universal BS gate through the DLA framework, and contrasts its performance against HEA and UCCSD baselines on standard quantum chemistry and feature selection benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic theoretical toolkit (DLA dimension, overparameterization bound, trainability variance) for designing and sizing HW preserving ansatze, giving practitioners concrete guidance on gate selection, connectivity, and circuit depth for constrained VQAs. The finding that the simpler RBS gate outperforms the universal BS gate on easier problems (ground state energy) with fewer parameters is a practical nuance that challenges the assumption that universality is always desirable. Key limitations include the deep circuit decomposition of the BS gate into elementary gates, the restriction to Hamming-weight symmetries (not general symmetry groups), and the fact that all experiments are classical simulations rather than NISQ hardware runs, leaving open whether the theoretical advantages persist under realistic noise.
