## Problem

Existing discriminative learning-based dense correspondence methods only maximize the data term (matching likelihood) without explicitly modeling the prior term, limiting their ability to learn the matching field manifold and generalize to challenging scenarios such as textureless regions, repetitive patterns, large displacements, and noise. This paper asks whether a generative framework can explicitly model both the data and prior terms to produce more robust correspondences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240464/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images (resized to 512×512) and extracts frozen VGG-16 features at four scales (H/4 through H/32) to construct a global matching cost and a local matching cost volume. The diffusion model operates at 64×64 resolution for the initial denoising stage, then a flow-upsampling stage refines the correspondence to 256×256. The output is a dense correspondence (flow) field mapping every source pixel to its target location; during inference, multiple stochastic samples are averaged to reduce variance.

## Method

DiffMatch formulates dense correspondence as a conditional denoising diffusion problem: a forward process adds Gaussian noise to the ground-truth flow field, and a modified U-Net (based on Nichol & Dhariwal, 2021) learns to reverse this process conditioned on two signals — an initial correspondence F_init obtained via soft-argmax over the global matching cost (providing long-range matching hints) and a local matching cost volume C^l (encoding pixel-wise interactions in a constrained search window). The training objective is a standard L2 denoising loss between the predicted clean flow and the ground truth. To overcome the limited input resolution of the diffusion backbone, a cascaded pipeline is used: the 64×64 denoising model is fine-tuned as a 256×256 flow-upsampling model by replacing F_init with a downsampled ground-truth flow, injecting fine-grained details with minimal additional training. Inference uses DDIM sampling with 5 steps and averages multiple stochastic hypotheses to improve accuracy.

## Results

On HPatches, DiffMatch achieves 18.84 AEPE (lower is better), outperforming PDCNet+ (18.91), GOCor (20.16), and GLU-Net (25.05). On ETH3D, DiffMatch achieves 3.12 AEPE, slightly edging out PDCNet+ (3.14) and substantially outperforming GOCor (3.72) and GLU-Net (5.17). Under ImageNet-C corruptions (severity 5), DiffMatch achieves 34.86 average AEPE on HPatches and 26.45 on ETH3D, the best among all compared methods. On MegaDepth, DiffMatch achieves 59.73 AEPE versus 63.97 for PDC-Net+.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffMatch achieves the best average AEPE among all dense matching methods on both HPatches (18.84) and ETH3D (3.12), narrowly beating PDCNet+ (18.91 and 3.14 respectively). The advantage over PDCNet+ is most pronounced on ETH3D at high displacement rates (r=13: 3.86 vs 4.20; r=15: 4.54 vs 6.25), where the generative prior better handles large displacements. Against GOCor, DiffMatch improves HPatches AEPE by 1.32 (18.84 vs 20.16) and ETH3D by 0.60 (3.12 vs 3.72). The gap widens substantially against earlier methods: GLU-Net (25.05/5.17), PDCNet (24.04/3.60), DGC-Net (33.26/6.19), and DMP (30.64/6.62). COTR† (dense evaluation without confidence thresholding) is far behind at 45.52/12.44, while COTR* (confident-only, 7.75/2.12) is not directly comparable. Under ImageNet-C corruptions, DiffMatch also leads on average (34.86 on HPatches, 26.45 on ETH3D), with the largest gains over PDCNet+ in noise and weather categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffMatch is the first work to frame dense correspondence as a conditional generative problem, departing from the dominant discriminative paradigm (GLU-Net, GOCor, PDCNet, COTR) that regresses the matching field by maximizing only the data term (likelihood). It builds directly on the DDPM/DDIM diffusion framework and the U-Net denoising architecture from improved DDPM, and adapts the cascaded diffusion pipeline from high-fidelity image generation to a flow-upsampling setting. The key conceptual departure is injecting the matching prior into the generative process via two conditioning signals (initial correspondence from global cost and local matching cost), rather than relying on the network to implicitly learn smoothness constraints from data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly modeling the matching field prior through a generative process yields more robust correspondences under textureless regions, repetitive patterns, large displacements, and image corruptions, where discriminative methods degrade. A notable practical bonus is natural pixel-wise uncertainty estimation via sampling variance, which correlates well with actual matching errors. However, the method operates at lower resolution (256) than prior discriminative approaches, leading to slightly worse performance on small-displacement ETH3D intervals, and the authors acknowledge that higher-resolution inference, better feature extractors, and zoom-in/patch-match techniques are needed to close this gap. The modest clean-data margins (e.g., 18.84 vs 18.91 AEPE on HPatches) suggest the generative prior's advantage is most pronounced under distribution shift rather than in standard settings.
