## Problem

Existing deep clustering methods are designed for balanced datasets, yet real-world data exhibits long-tailed class distributions that cause pseudo-labeling approaches to produce noisy, degenerate assignments. The paper introduces 'deep imbalanced clustering' as a new problem setting and identifies that prior OT-based pseudo-labeling methods either enforce uniform cluster sizes (ignoring imbalance) or ignore sample confidence, making them unsuitable for imbalanced scenarios.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB images and a known number of clusters K, processing them through a DINO-pretrained ViT-B16 backbone (finetuning the last block). It outputs a soft pseudo-label matrix Q ∈ R^{N×K} where each sample's weight is at most 1/N and the total transported mass equals a progressively increasing fraction ρ ∈ [ρ₀, 1], along with updated visual representations. A memory buffer of 5120 historical predictions stabilizes the mini-batch OT optimization, and two augmented views per sample are cross-utilized for equivariance.

## Method

P²OT formulates pseudo-label generation as a single convex optimization that simultaneously enforces three constraints: an inequality on per-sample weights (Q1_K ≤ 1/N·1_N), a KL divergence penalty against a uniform cluster-size prior (allowing imbalanced assignments while preventing degenerate solutions), and a total mass constraint 1_N^T Q 1_K = ρ that progressively increases via a sigmoid ramp-up to implement curriculum learning from confident to difficult samples. To solve this efficiently, the authors introduce a virtual cluster that absorbs the unselected (1−ρ) mass and replace the KL term with a weighted KL where the virtual cluster's weight λ_{K+1}→∞, transforming the problem into a standard unbalanced OT. The resulting problem is solved by a custom scaling algorithm with the recursion a ← α/(Mb), b ← (β/(M^T a))^∘f (where f = λ/(λ+ε)), which is shown to be 2× faster than the Generalized Scaling Algorithm of Chizat et al. The overall training alternates between generating pseudo-labels via P²OT and updating the representation with a cross-entropy loss L = ⟨Q, −log P⟩_F, with the loss normalized by ρ to account for the partial mass.

## Results

On the imbalanced CIFAR-100 (R=100), P²OT achieves 38.2% ACC, 69.6 NMI, and 32.0 F1, outperforming the previous best (SCAN) by 1.0 ACC and 0.6 F1. On the out-of-distribution ImageNet-R (R=13), P²OT reaches 25.9% ACC and 27.3 F1, improving over SCAN* by 2.3 ACC and 4.5 F1. On the fine-grained iNaturalist subsets, P²OT gains 5.9 ACC on iNature100 (R=67) and 3.4 ACC on iNature500 (R=111) over the strongest baseline, with consistent F1 improvements of 1.5 and 1.2 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

P²OT outperforms all ten baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest pseudo-labeling baseline SCAN (Van Gansbeke et al., 2020), P²OT gains 1.0 ACC on CIFAR100, 4.1 ACC on ImgNet-R, 5.5 ACC on iNature100, and 3.2 ACC on iNature500. The two-stage variant SCAN* (Van Gansbeke et al., 2020) is competitive on ImgNet-R (23.6 vs. 25.9 ACC) but degrades severely on iNature500 (19.0 vs. 32.2 ACC), confirming that naive two-stage pseudo-labeling fails under imbalance. Mutual-information methods IIC (Ji et al., 2019) and CC (Li et al., 2021) are consistently 10–20 ACC points behind P²OT, as are PICA (Huang et al., 2020) and DivClust (Metaxas et al., 2023). The long-tailed representation method BCL (Zhou et al., 2022) and the DINO backbone alone (Caron et al., 2021) serve as upper-bound references without explicit clustering, and P²OT surpasses both. SPICE (Niu et al., 2022) collapses to near-random accuracy (6.74% on CIFAR100, 0.74% on iNature1000) due to its equal-per-class confident-sample selection being incompatible with imbalanced distributions. The ablation variant SLA (Tai et al., 2021) also fails, assigning all mass to a single cluster in early training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of OT-based pseudo-labeling for deep clustering initiated by Asano et al. (2020), which imposed a strict equality constraint on cluster sizes, and Tai et al. (2021), which added a total mass constraint for label annealing. The key departure is the P²OT formulation that simultaneously incorporates a KL-divergence constraint (allowing imbalanced cluster sizes while preventing degenerate solutions) and a progressive partial transport constraint (ramping up total mass ρ from a small initial value to 1), unifying imbalance-awareness and confident-sample selection in a single convex optimization. The reformulation into a standard unbalanced OT problem via a virtual cluster and weighted KL constraint, solvable by a custom scaling algorithm that is 2× faster than the generalized scaling algorithm of Chizat et al. (2018), provides a clean computational bridge to existing OT solvers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By framing deep imbalanced clustering as a first-class problem and providing a challenging multi-dataset benchmark (long-tailed CIFAR100, out-of-distribution ImageNet-R, and large-scale iNaturalist subsets), the paper addresses a gap between balanced-clustering theory and real-world data distributions. The progressive ρ mechanism eliminates the need for sensitive confidence-threshold hyperparameter tuning that plagues prior pseudo-labeling methods, and the ablation confirms that both the KL constraint and the progressive mass are independently necessary. Open questions include whether the fixed sigmoid ramp-up for ρ can be made adaptive to model learning progress (as the authors note), and whether the approach extends to settings where the number of clusters K is unknown or the imbalance ratio is extreme beyond R=111.
