## Problem

Diffusion-guided 3D generation via score distillation sampling (SDS) suffers from slow convergence, poor quality (missing attributes, distorted geometry), and low diversity. The paper identifies the root cause as a fundamental misalignment: SDS samples diffusion timesteps uniformly at random throughout 3D optimization, whereas both the 3D formation process (coarse structure → fine details) and the diffusion model's own sampling process (sequential denoising from high to low noise) are inherently ordered. This random sampling leads to supervision misalignment, out-of-distribution score estimation at early stages, and high-variance gradients at late stages.

## Input / Output

The method is a drop-in replacement for the timestep sampling strategy in any existing SDS-based 3D generation pipeline (DreamFusion, Magic3D, Zero123, ProlificDreamer, etc.), requiring no changes to the 3D representation (NeRF, NeuS, DMTet) or the pre-trained diffusion model (Stable Diffusion, Zero123, DeepFloyd-IF). For text-to-3D, the input is a free-form text prompt; for image-to-3D, a single reference image. The output is a differentiable 3D representation optimized over N iterations (typically ~2500 steps with TP-SDS vs. ~10000 with vanilla SDS), which can be rendered from arbitrary viewpoints or exported as a mesh.

## Method

DreamTime proposes Time Prioritized Score Distillation Sampling (TP-SDS), which replaces uniform random timestep sampling with a weighted non-increasing schedule that mirrors DDPM's sequential denoising. At each optimization iteration i, the timestep is determined by t(i) = argmin_{t'} |Σ_{t=t'}^{T} W(t) − i/N|, where the prior weight function W(t) = (1/Z)·W_d(t)·W_p(t) controls the descent speed. W_d(t) = √((1−α̅_t)/α̅_t) is derived from the explicit SDS loss and corresponds to the inverse square root of the diffusion model's signal-to-noise ratio, while W_p(t) = exp(−(t−m)²/(2s²)) is a Gaussian that suppresses both very large t (coarse, low-information gradients) and very small t (high-variance gradients that destabilize 3D-consistent optimization). This design ensures early iterations focus on global structure with large noise (avoiding the OOD problem of low-frequency initial renderings), middle iterations concentrate on informative content generation, and late iterations avoid the high-variance regime that causes artifacts.

## Results

The paper reports that TP-SDS requires approximately 75% fewer optimization steps than uniform-t SDS to reach comparable R-Precision scores on 153 COCO object-centric prompts, as measured by CLIP B/32, B/16, and L/14. In a user preference study across five 3D generation methods (DreamFusion, TextMesh, Fantasia3D, Magic3D, Zero-1-to-3) with 415–100–100–100–50 prompts respectively and 10 raters each, TP-SDS consistently achieves higher preference percentages than the SDS baseline. The paper is primarily qualitative in its main comparisons (Figures 7, 9, 10, 17–22), demonstrating improvements in convergence speed, attribute fidelity, geometric accuracy, and generation diversity without reporting a single standard numerical benchmark table.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TP-SDS is evaluated as a drop-in replacement for the uniform-t SDS loss across eight baselines. Against DreamFusion (SDS), the primary baseline, TP-SDS wins on all three axes: faster convergence (~75% fewer steps to equal R-Precision), better attribute/geometry fidelity (e.g., generating roller skates on a mantis that SDS misses), and higher diversity (visually distinct objects under different seeds vs. mode collapse). Against ProlificDreamer's VSD, TP-VSD produces sharper details and less color distortion (Figures 1, 17), and outperforms ProlificDreamer's multi-stage random t-sampling (2–5 stages) in Figure 16. Against HiFA's exponential t-annealing (power 0.5 and variants), TP-SDS yields cleaner geometry and fewer artifacts (Figure 16a). When applied to Magic3D, Zero-1-to-3, DreamWaltz, TextMesh (NeuS), and Fantasia3D (DMTet), TP-SDS consistently improves over each method's original SDS schedule in the user preference study (Figure 15) and qualitative comparisons (Figures 1, 18, 19, 22), with no reported case where TP-SDS underperforms the baseline. The method is orthogonal to the choice of 3D representation and foundation model, so it does not 'lose' to any baseline on a shared metric; rather, it uniformly improves each one.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DreamFusion's Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) framework for diffusion-guided 3D generation and on DDPM's sequential denoising process. The key departure is identifying a conceptual mismatch: SDS samples timesteps uniformly (mimicking DDPM *training*), but 3D model optimization is more analogous to DDPM *sampling* (sequential, coarse-to-fine denoising). Rather than modifying the diffusion model, adding new loss terms, or changing the 3D representation—as concurrent works like ProlificDreamer, HiFA, or Zero123 do—DreamTime intervenes solely at the optimization schedule level, making it orthogonal and composable with those approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a ~75% reduction in optimization steps, improved geometry/texture quality, and alleviated mode collapse, all from a single-line change to the timestep sampling strategy that is universally applicable across NeRF, NeuS, DMTet representations and multiple diffusion backbones. However, the method introduces two hyperparameters (m, s) whose optimal values depend on the diffusion model and task, and the quantitative evaluation remains relatively thin (R-Precision on 153 prompts, user preference studies) compared to the breadth of qualitative claims. Open questions include whether the Gaussian weight function W_p can be derived more principledly, whether the approach transfers to feedforward 3D generation, and how it interacts with emerging 3D-native diffusion models that may eliminate the need for SDS altogether.
