## Problem

Pre-trained RL agents for logic synthesis can actively degrade search performance on novel hardware designs due to distribution shift between training and test netlists. The paper demonstrates that a straightforward MCTS+Learning baseline (augmenting Monte Carlo tree search with a pre-trained policy) underperforms pure MCTS on 11 out of 20 test circuits, revealing that learned recommendations misdirect search toward suboptimal synthesis recipes when the input design is sufficiently different from training data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unoptimized And-Invert-Graph (AIG) netlist—where AND gates are nodes and NOT gates are dashed edges—alongside a training dataset of previously synthesized AIGs (23 circuits spanning 100–46K nodes). The state at each search step is a two-tuple: the original AIG and the partial sequence of synthesis actions taken so far (variable length up to L=10). The output is a 10-step synthesis recipe (a sequence drawn from 7 ABC transformations: rf, rm, rw, rwz, rs, rsz, b) that minimizes the area-delay product of the resulting optimized AIG, evaluated against a 7nm technology library.

## Method

ABC-RL pre-trains an RL policy agent via imitation learning (cross-entropy against MCTS policies) using a dual-branch encoder: a 3-layer GCN for AIG embeddings and a single-attention BERT transformer for variable-length partial recipe embeddings. At inference, the key novelty is a lightweight retrieval mechanism that computes the minimum cosine distance between the test AIG's GCN embedding and all training-set embeddings, then passes this similarity score through a sigmoid with tunable threshold δ_th and temperature T to produce a modulation parameter α ∈ [0,1]. This α exponentially down-weights the learned policy in the UCT term (U* = π_θ(s,a)^(1−α) · U_MCTS(s,a)), smoothly interpolating between full agent guidance (α=0, familiar circuit) and pure MCTS search (α=1, novel circuit). Both hyperparameters are tuned via grid search on a held-out validation set, and the retrieval step requires no retraining—only the pre-computed GCN embeddings from the training set.

## Results

ABC-RL achieves a geometric mean area-delay product (ADP) reduction of 25.3% over the resyn2 baseline across 20 test circuits spanning MCNC, EPFL arithmetic, and EPFL random control benchmarks, outperforming the next-best method (MCTS+Learning at 20.7%) by 4.6 percentage points. At iso-QoR, ABC-RL delivers a geometric mean 3.8× runtime speed-up over MCTS, with individual circuits reaching up to 9× speed-up (e.g., 9.0× on A2/div). ABC-RL wins on 16 of 20 test circuits, with the four exceptions being marginal losses to Online-RL. On EPFL benchmarks specifically, ABC-RL achieves 28.85% geo-mean ADP reduction, improving over MCTS by 5.99% and over SA+Pred by 6.12%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ABC-RL outperforms all five baselines on overall geo-mean ADP reduction. Against the current SOTA search-only method MCTS (Neto et al., 2022), ABC-RL improves geo-mean ADP reduction from 19.8% to 25.3% (+5.5 pp) while also achieving a 3.8× iso-QoR speed-up, meaning it reaches the same quality in roughly a quarter of the synthesis runs. Against SA+Pred (Chowdhury et al., 2022), which uses simulated annealing with a learned QoR predictor, ABC-RL gains 5.6 pp in geo-mean ADP reduction (25.3% vs. 19.7%). Against the RL-based baselines DRiLLS (Hosny et al., 2020) and Online-RL (Zhu et al., 2020), both of which train agents online without leveraging past design data, ABC-RL achieves a substantial 9.2 pp improvement (25.3% vs. 16.1%). Against the adapted chip-placement method MCTS+L+FT (Mirhoseini et al., 2021), which fine-tunes the pre-trained agent during test-time search, ABC-RL still wins by 2.0 pp geo-mean (25.3% vs. 23.3%), confirming that retrieval-guided α-modulation is more effective than online fine-tuning under the constrained 100-run synthesis budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AlphaGo-style MCTS+Learning paradigm (Silver et al., 2016) to logic synthesis, building on the MCTS search framework established by Flowtune (Neto et al., 2022) for exploring synthesis recipe spaces. The key conceptual departure is identifying distribution shift between training and test netlists as a first-class problem: prior work assumed a pre-trained policy agent would uniformly improve search, but this paper shows it can actively degrade performance on novel designs. The retrieval-guided α modulation (sigmoid of nearest-neighbor cosine distance) is a lightweight mechanism not present in prior MCTS+Learning or retrieval-augmented RL work, which either fine-tune online or retrieve full trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that pre-trained RL agents hurt search on 11/20 novel designs challenges the default assumption in ML-for-EDA that more learned guidance is always better, and the retrieval-based fix is practical because it requires no retraining and adds negligible inference overhead (~11ms). However, the evaluation scale is modest (20 test circuits, 56 total netlists, 7-action ABC space), and it remains open whether the approach generalizes to larger action spaces, different synthesis tools, or other EDA stages. The 9× iso-QoR speedup on EPFL arithmetic benchmarks is practically significant for design iteration, but the small dataset limits confidence in the magnitude of gains.
