## Problem

Existing evaluations of Federated Domain Generalization (FDG) methods are limited in three critical ways: they use few and undiverse datasets, restrict the number of clients to equal the number of domains (an unrealistic assumption), and ignore the effect of communication rounds. This paper addresses these gaps by proposing a scalable benchmark that tests FDG methods under high client heterogeneity, large client counts (up to 100), and diverse real-world datasets.

## Input / Output

The benchmark spans 7 datasets: five image datasets (PACS, IWildCam, CelebA, Camelyon17, FEMNIST) producing class labels, and two text datasets (CivilComments for toxicity classification, Py150 for next-token code completion). A key design choice is the Heterogeneous Partitioning scheme, which distributes domain-labeled samples across an arbitrary number of clients C while controlling the degree of domain-based client heterogeneity via a single parameter λ ∈ [0,1], where λ=0 yields complete heterogeneity (each client sees at most one domain) and λ=1 yields i.i.d. homogeneous clients. This allows the same dataset to be evaluated at C=1 (centralized) or C=100 (federated) with matched total data.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a two-step Heterogeneous Partitioning algorithm: Step 1 greedily constructs a complete-heterogeneity partition P₀ by assigning whole domains to the client with the fewest samples (when C≤D) or splitting the largest-average domain among clients (when C>D), provably optimal for C≥D and a linear-time greedy approximation for the NP-hard C<D case. Step 2 interpolates per-domain sample counts between the homogeneous allocation (n_d/C) and the complete-heterogeneity allocation via a convex combination parameterized by λ, then samples without replacement to guarantee a true set partition. The benchmark methodology evaluates 14 methods across four dimensions—dataset type, dataset difficulty (quantified via R_DG and R_FL metrics), client heterogeneity level, and number of clients—using held-out-domain validation for model selection and early stopping, with ResNet-50 for images and DistilBERT/CodeGPT for text.

## Results

Across 7 datasets and 14 methods, FedAvg-ERM emerges as a surprisingly strong baseline: on PACS (C=100, λ=0) it achieves 95.4% test accuracy, matching or exceeding all DG-adapted and FDG methods (FedDG 94.3%, FedADG 93.5%). On the much harder IWildCam (D=243, C=100, λ=0), FedAvg-ERM reaches 19.6% macro-F1 while the best FL method (Scaffold) achieves 17.8% and the best FDG method (FedDG) 16.7%. On subpopulation-shift datasets, FL methods do surpass ERM: on CelebA (C=100, λ=0), AFL reaches 83.1% vs. FedAvg-ERM's 44.6%, and on CivilComments, AFL reaches 44.0% vs. 33.0%. Performance degrades sharply as client heterogeneity increases (λ→0) and as the number of clients grows, with DG accuracy dropping from ~90% to as low as 10% when C increases from 1 to 200 on PACS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central finding is that FedAvg-ERM (McMahan et al., 2017) is a remarkably strong baseline that most DG methods fail to beat in the FL setting. On general DG datasets (PACS, IWildCam), centralized DG methods (IRM, Fish, Mixup, MMD, DeepCoral, GroupDRO) all degrade substantially when moved from C=1 to C=100, with Fish and Mixup suffering the most (e.g., Fish drops from 94% to 35% on PACS at λ=0). Among the four purpose-built Federated DG methods, FedDG (Liu et al., 2021) performs best on PACS (94.3% at λ=0, close to FedAvg-ERM's 95.4%) but requires sharing local data amplitude information, raising privacy concerns; FedADG (Zhang et al., 2021) is competitive on PACS (93.5%) but relies on a hard-to-tune GAN with tens of hyperparameters; FedSR (Nguyen et al., 2022) and FedGMA (Tenison et al., 2022) underperform ERM across all settings. FL methods addressing client heterogeneity (FedProx, Scaffold, AFL) help convergence on hard datasets: Scaffold achieves the best IWildCam score among non-ERM methods (26% at λ=0.1 vs. FedAvg-ERM's 30%), and AFL dominates on subpopulation-shift datasets (CelebA: 83.1% vs. 44.6% for ERM; CivilComments: 44.0% vs. 33.0%). Overall, no single method consistently outperforms FedAvg-ERM across all 7 datasets and heterogeneity levels, confirming that Federated DG remains an open problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two well-studied but previously separate areas: centralized domain generalization (DG) benchmarks (DomainBed, WILDS) and federated learning (FL) benchmarks (LEAF, FLamby, PFLBench). Prior DG benchmarks ignore FL-specific challenges like client heterogeneity and communication constraints, while prior FL benchmarks ignore train-test domain shift. The paper's Heterogeneous Partitioning method unifies and supersedes existing partitioning schemes (Shards, Dirichlet, Semantic) by providing a single parameterized framework that controls domain-based client heterogeneity while guaranteeing true set partitions. It also extends the limited FDG evaluations in prior work (which assumed C=D and small client counts) to a general setting with arbitrary C, controlled heterogeneity λ, and diverse datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that Federated DG remains largely unsolved: no purpose-built FDG method consistently outperforms the simple FedAvg-ERM baseline, and performance degrades sharply (from ~90% to ~10%) as client count increases from 1 to 200. The non-monotonic effect of communication rounds on DG accuracy (an implicit regularization phenomenon) is an open question with no theoretical explanation. The released Heterogeneous Partitioning tool and extendable codebase provide the community with a standardized, reproducible evaluation framework, but the core algorithmic challenge—simultaneously handling both train-test domain shift and client heterogeneity at scale—remains wide open.
