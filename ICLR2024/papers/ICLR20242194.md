## Problem

While memorization (exact interpolation of a finite dataset) by neural networks has been extensively studied, robust memorization—maintaining correct classification under input perturbations up to a budget μ—remains largely unexplored. The paper addresses three fundamental gaps: the computational complexity of finding robust memorization networks, the minimal network structure (width, depth, parameters) required for optimal robustness at any budget below the separation bound, and how to bound parameter magnitudes when the budget approaches the theoretical maximum λ_D/2.

## Input / Output

The method takes a finite classification dataset D = {(x_i, y_i)}_{i=1}^N with x_i ∈ R^n, y_i ∈ [L], and a robust budget μ < λ_D/2 (where λ_D is the minimum L_∞ distance between points of different labels). It produces an explicitly constructed ReLU feedforward network F: R^n → R that classifies correctly for all inputs within the L_∞ ball of radius μ around each data point. The construction is polynomial-time and yields specific architecture parameters: width 3n+1, depth 2N+1, and O(Nn) non-zero parameters for the general case, or width O(n), depth O(N log n), and O(Nn log n) parameters for the Lipschitz-controlled binary case.

## Method

The paper makes three core contributions. First, it proves NP-hardness of robust memorization for depth-2, width-2 ReLU networks by reducing from reversible 6-SAT, showing that even deciding existence is computationally intractable for simple architectures. Second, it explicitly constructs an optimal robust memorization network by building per-sample sub-networks E_i(x) = y_i − (y_i/(λ_D−2μ))·Σ_j(σ(x_i^(j)−x^(j)−μ)+σ(x^(j)−x_i^(j)−μ)) that equal y_i inside the robust ball, drop below y_i outside, and are ≤0 near other-class points, then combining them via F(x) = max_i{E_i(x), 0} implemented with ReLU. Third, for binary classification, it constructs a network F(x) = y_{w_N}·σ(1 − (2/λ_D)||x−x_{w_N}||_∞) where w_N is the nearest-neighbor index, achieving the optimal Lipschitz constant 2/λ_D with parameter values bounded by O(max||x||_∞ + 1/λ_D), avoiding the parameter explosion that occurs in the general construction when μ approaches λ_D/2.

## Results

The paper establishes three main theoretical results. First, it proves NP-hardness: deciding whether a depth-2, width-2 ReLU network robustly memorizes a dataset with budget α is NP-hard (Theorem 4.1). Second, it constructs an optimal robust memorization network with width 3n+1, depth 2N+1, and exactly (N−1)(12n+5)+2 = O(Nn) non-zero parameters that works for any budget μ < λ_D/2 (Theorem 4.8), with a matching width lower bound of n (Theorem 4.3). Third, for binary classification, it constructs a network with O(Nn log n) parameters and O(n) width whose Lipschitz constant is exactly 2/λ_D (optimal), with parameter values bounded by O(max‖x‖_∞ + 1/λ_D) (Theorem 5.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is with Li et al. (2022), the closest prior work on robust memorization. This paper improves on three axes: (1) the achievable budget is raised from μ < λ_D/4 to the optimal μ < λ_D/2; (2) the parameter count is reduced from O(Nn log(n/λ_D) + N poly log(N/λ_D)) to O(Nn), eliminating all logarithmic factors in n, λ_D, and N; (3) the result generalizes from binary to multi-class (L-label) classification. Compared to standard memorization results (Vardi et al. 2021 with Õ(√N) parameters, Zhang et al. 2021 with O(N+n) parameters), robust memorization is shown to be strictly harder: fixed-width networks (width 12) that suffice for memorization cannot guarantee robustness (Theorem 4.3), and depth-2 width-N networks that suffice for memorization also fail for robustness (Proposition 4.6). The Lipschitz-based construction (Theorem 5.2) trades O(Nn log n) parameters for controlled parameter magnitudes, making it more practical when μ is close to λ_D/2 where the Theorem 4.8 construction's parameter values diverge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the memorization literature (Huang & Babri 1998, Vardi et al. 2021, Yun et al. 2019) to the robust setting, building most directly on Li et al. (2022), who first constructed explicit robust memorization networks but were limited to a suboptimal budget of λ/4 and required O(Nn log(n/λ) + N poly log(N/λ)) parameters. The key departures are: (1) achieving the optimal budget λ/2 with a tighter O(Nn) parameter count, (2) proving NP-hardness specifically for robust memorization (not reducible from the memorization NP-hardness results), and (3) introducing a Lipschitz-constant-based characterization that yields controlled parameter magnitudes for binary classification. The width lower bound (width ≥ n) and the depth-2 width-N insufficiency result sharpen the known gap between memorization and robust memorization requirements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper provides the first systematic theoretical treatment of robust memorization, establishing both hardness (NP-hardness for simple architectures) and constructive existence (polynomial-time explicit constructions) results. The gap between the O(Nn) upper bound and the Ω(√(Nn)) lower bound from Li et al. (2022) remains open, and extending the Lipschitz-based construction from binary to multi-class classification is identified as a challenge. For practitioners, the results clarify that constant-width networks (sufficient for memorization) fundamentally cannot guarantee robustness, and that robustness inherently demands more expressive architectures — a theoretical justification for the empirical observation that wider/deeper networks are needed for adversarial robustness.
