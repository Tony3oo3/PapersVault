## Problem

While Learning Rate Rewinding (LRR) consistently outperforms Iterative Magnitude Pruning (IMP) in finding lottery tickets, the mechanism behind this advantage remains unexplained. Specifically, it is unclear whether LRR's edge comes from better mask identification, more effective parameter optimization, or both, and how overparameterization in early pruning iterations facilitates these gains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242010/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a dense, randomly initialized CNN (ResNet18 or ResNet50) and standard RGB image classification data (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, Tiny-ImageNet at 64×64, ImageNet at 224×224) with class labels, and produces a sparse subnetwork at a target sparsity (up to 96%) that retains competitive classification accuracy. The theoretical analysis reduces the problem to a single hidden ReLU neuron with d-dimensional Gaussian input learning a univariate target, enabling closed-form gradient flow solutions. Experiments also decouple mask and parameter optimization by fixing random or cross-method masks to isolate each component's contribution.

## Method

The paper derives closed-form solutions for the gradient flow dynamics of a single hidden ReLU neuron, proving that LRR provably succeeds in more initial sign configurations than IMP because it inherits corrected parameter signs from the trained overparameterized model, while IMP's weight rewinding resets to problematic signs with probability 3/4. Empirically, the authors disentangle mask learning from parameter optimization on ResNet18/50 across four benchmarks, showing LRR both identifies superior masks and optimizes diverse masks (including random ones) more effectively. The key mechanism is that LRR enables early and stable parameter sign switches during initial pruning iterations when the network is still overparameterized, whereas IMP must re-derive signs from scratch each iteration. A practical modification—rewinding batch normalization parameters and adding warmup after each pruning cycle—further stabilizes LRR on larger-scale problems like CIFAR-100 and Tiny ImageNet.

## Results

The paper demonstrates that LRR consistently outperforms IMP (WR variant) across all benchmarks (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet, ImageNet) at all sparsity levels, with the gap widening at higher sparsities on more complex datasets. LRR identifies a superior sparse mask that, when optimized with IMP, still achieves higher accuracy than the IMP mask optimized with IMP (Fig. 3). On random masks, LRR-rand outperforms IMP-rand, confirming that LRR's advantage is in parameter optimization flexibility rather than mask identification alone. The theoretical single-neuron analysis shows LRR reduces the failure probability from 3/4 (IMP) to 1/2 by inheriting correct parameter signs from the overparameterized training phase.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LRR outperforms IMP/WR (Frankle & Carbin, 2019; Frankle et al., 2020a) on all four benchmarks at all sparsity levels, with the advantage most pronounced on CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet where IMP fails to identify stable sign configurations even at high sparsities. Even when constrained to use IMP's identified mask, LRR still improves over IMP's own training (Fig. 4), demonstrating that the advantage lies in parameter optimization flexibility rather than mask quality alone. On random masks, LRR-rand outperforms IMP-rand, confirming the benefit is mask-agnostic. When alternative pruning criteria (Synflow, SNIP) are used iteratively, LRR still benefits from continued training over weight rewinding, showing the mechanism is independent of the specific pruning criterion. The paper also shows that rewinding only weight magnitudes while preserving LRR-learned signs matches LRR's performance, isolating sign information as the critical factor that IMP's rewinding step destroys.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the iterative prune-train framework of the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis (Frankle & Carbin, 2019) and its variants Weight Rewinding (Frankle et al., 2020a) and Learning Rate Rewinding (Renda et al., 2020), providing a mechanistic explanation for why LRR outperforms IMP. The theoretical analysis extends gradient flow results for single ReLU neurons (Yehudai & Ohad, 2020; Du et al., 2018) to a trainable outer weight setting and, crucially, to the context of iterative pruning with overparameterization. Unlike prior work that attributes IMP's success to linear mode connectivity (Paul et al., 2023) or weight correlation (Liu et al., 2021a), this paper identifies parameter sign switching as the key differentiator, supported by both closed-form solutions and extensive experiments that disentangle mask identification from parameter optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sign-switching mechanism provides a concrete, testable explanation for LRR's advantage that could inform the design of more flexible sparse training algorithms, potentially enabling optimization of diverse mask structures from scratch. However, the theoretical guarantees are limited to a single hidden neuron with balanced initialization, and the empirical evidence, while spanning multiple datasets and architectures, does not fully isolate sign switching from other confounding factors (e.g., learning rate schedule effects, BN interactions). Open questions include whether the sign mechanism remains dominant in very deep or non-convolutional architectures, and whether the early-iteration sign stabilization observed here can be exploited to design pruning algorithms that explicitly preserve sign information across iterations.
