## Problem

Prior generalization analysis for zeroth-order optimization was restricted to ZO-SGD with a single 2-point Gaussian gradient estimator, leaving ZO-GD, ZO-SVRG, and alternative estimators (1-point, coordinate-wise) without stability guarantees. This paper asks whether a unified proof framework can yield generalization bounds across all these algorithms and estimators under convex, strongly convex, and non-convex conditions.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes as input a training dataset of size n, a loss function with known Lipschitz constant L and smoothness constant β, and a choice of zeroth-order gradient estimator (1-point, 2-point forward/central, or coordinate-wise) with a smoothing parameter μ. The output is a uniform stability bound ε_gen on the gap between population and empirical risk, expressed as a function of iteration count T, sample size n, dimension d, and estimator-specific constants (e.g., Γ_d^K, A_d, C_d). The framework also produces optimized model parameters w_T as a byproduct of the ZO update rule.

## Method

The paper introduces a generic growth recursion (Lemma 3) that decomposes the ZO update into a first-order component governed by an expansive factor η (determined by convexity) plus an approximation-error term η'_t introduced by the gradient estimator. The key insight is that 1-point and 2-point random-direction estimators inflate η' by terms involving E‖u‖² and E‖u‖³, which destroy the contraction property under convex and strongly convex conditions, while coordinate-wise estimates contribute only a fixed dβμ term independent of ‖w−w'‖, preserving the first-order contraction. The framework then applies this recursion under the same-sample and different-sample events (probability 1−1/n and 1/n respectively) to derive closed-form bounds for ZO-SGD, ZO-GD, and ZO-SVRG. The result is that coordinate-wise estimates recover the same generalization rates as first-order algorithms (e.g., O(log T/n) for convex, O(1/n) for strongly convex), whereas random-direction estimates degrade to non-convex-like rates.

## Results

The paper's headline result is that ZO-SGD, ZO-GD, and ZO-SVRG with coordinate-wise gradient estimation achieve generalization bounds identical to their first-order counterparts: O(T^{βC/(βC+1)}/n) (non-convex), O(log T/n) (convex), and O(1/n) (strongly convex) for ZO-SGD, and O(S^{3βC}/n), O(S^{2βC}/n), O(S/n) for ZO-SVRG. In contrast, 2-point estimation yields a uniform O(T/n) bound for ZO-SGD across all convexity conditions, and 1-point estimation gives O(T/n^{1-c/(d²LC√n)}), both strictly looser than the coordinate-wise result. Empirically on the Australian dataset (nonconvex logistic regression and a two-layer neural network), coordinate-wise estimators consistently exhibit smaller generalization errors than 2-point estimators, and ZO-SGD shows the smallest generalization error among ZO-GD, ZO-SGD, and ZO-SVRG as iterations increase.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Hardt et al. (2016), which established first-order SGD generalization bounds of O(T^{βC/(βC+1)}/n) (NC), O(log T/n) (C), and O(1/n) (SC), this paper shows that ZO-SGD with coordinate-wise estimation recovers exactly the same bounds, meaning the zeroth-order approximation error does not degrade generalization when using deterministic coordinate directions. In contrast, the prior ZO result from Nikolakakis et al. (2022) only covered ZO-SGD and ZO-GD with 2-point Gaussian estimation, yielding a uniform O(T/n) for ZO-SGD and O(T^{βC}/n) for ZO-GD (NC only), without distinguishing convexity conditions. The 1-point and 2-point random-direction estimators in this paper introduce an additional expansive factor η′ that prevents the contraction property under convex and strongly convex settings, resulting in bounds of the same order as the non-convex case. The coordinate-wise estimator avoids this issue entirely because its approximation error (dβμ) is independent of ‖w−w′‖, so no new expansive factor is generated. Empirically, on the Australian dataset, coordinate-wise estimators produce smaller training-test loss gaps than 2-point estimators for all three algorithms (ZO-GD, ZO-SGD, ZO-SVRG), and ZO-SGD consistently achieves the lowest generalization error as iterations grow, corroborating the theoretical ordering of bounds in Table 1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the stability-based generalization framework of Hardt et al. (2016) for first-order SGD to the zeroth-order setting, building directly on Nikolakakis et al. (2022), who provided the first generalization bound for ZO-SGD under a 2-point Gaussian estimate. The key departure is a unified proof framework that covers three algorithms (ZO-SGD, ZO-GD, ZO-SVRG) across three estimator families (1-point, 2-point, coordinate-wise) and three convexity regimes, whereas prior work addressed only a single algorithm-estimator pair. The algorithmic components draw on the broader ZO optimization literature (Nesterov & Spokoiny, Ghadimi & Lan, Liu et al., Ji et al.), but the generalization analysis for ZO-SVRG is entirely new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that coordinate-wise estimation preserves the contraction property of first-order updates under convex and strongly convex conditions, while 1-point and 2-point estimators introduce new expansive factors that degrade bounds to non-convex levels—provides practitioners with a clear theoretical rationale for choosing coordinate-wise ZO methods when generalization is a concern. This is the first generalization analysis for ZO-SVRG, filling a notable gap. Limitations include empirical validation only on small-scale problems (Australian tabular dataset, two-layer neural network) and somewhat loose theoretical bounds; open questions include whether the framework extends to more complex ZO variance-reduction methods (ZO-SAGA, ZO-SARAH) and whether the coordinate-wise advantage persists in very high-dimensional regimes where d function evaluations per step become prohibitive.
