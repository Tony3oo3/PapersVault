## Problem

Existing XAI techniques for time series forecasting rely on a bottom-up approach that examines individual time points in isolation, failing to provide a holistic, human-interpretable understanding of the entire predicted trajectory. This limits the applicability of ML in high-stakes domains (healthcare, pharmacology, finance) where users need to comprehend how the overall shape of a trajectory—and specific properties like peak values or inflection times—changes with input features.

## Input / Output

The model takes a vector of static covariates (e.g., patient age, weight, drug dosage) as input and produces a continuous trajectory represented as a cubic spline over a fixed time horizon [0, T]. Beyond the trajectory itself, TIMEVIEW outputs a composition—a minimal sequence of dynamical motifs (e.g., 'increasing and convex', 'decreasing and concave')—and the coordinates of transition points (local minima, maxima, inflection points) between motifs. The trajectory is defined by B-Spline coefficients, so it is a smooth continuous function rather than a sequence of discrete predictions, and the composition is uniquely determined for every input feature vector.

## Method

TIMEVIEW represents predicted trajectories as cubic splines expressed in a B-Spline basis, with a fully-connected neural network encoder mapping static features to the B-Spline coefficient vector. The key theoretical contribution is proving that a set of seven dynamical motifs (defined by the signs of the first and second derivatives) is compatible with cubic splines, guaranteeing a unique composition for every predicted trajectory. Composition extraction is performed analytically: on each knot interval the spline reduces to a cubic polynomial, whose first and second derivatives are quadratic and linear respectively, so the motif assignment is determined by finding the roots of these polynomials. Training minimizes MSE between predicted and observed trajectory values plus an L2 penalty on B-Spline coefficients to keep compositions stable. This architecture realizes bi-level transparency: Level 1 tracks how the motif sequence (trend) changes with input perturbations, and Level 2 tracks how transition point coordinates (properties) shift, both visualized through an interactive tool with colored composition bands and 2D contour plots.

## Results

TIMEVIEW achieves MSE of 0.13 on Airfoil, 0.24 on flchain, 0.04 on Stress-Strain, 0.31 on Tacrolimus, 0.00 on Tumor, 0.02 on Sine, and 0.04 on Beta. It is the best-performing transparent method on 5 of 7 datasets (Airfoil, Stress-Strain, Tumor, Sine, Beta) and outperforms all closed-form expression methods (PySR, SINDy) across the board. Compared to the best black-box models (XGB-T, LGBM-T, CatBoost-T), TIMEVIEW incurs only a small performance gap of 0.02–0.04 MSE on most datasets while providing full bi-level interpretability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against transparent baselines, TIMEVIEW dominates: it outperforms Linear-T on all 7 datasets (e.g., 0.13 vs 0.37 on Airfoil, 0.02 vs 0.99 on Sine), beats GAM-T on all 7 (e.g., 0.04 vs 0.09 on Stress-Strain, 0.02 vs 0.54 on Sine), and surpasses DecisionTree-T on 5 of 7 (winning on Airfoil 0.13 vs 0.36, Tumor 0.00 vs 0.22, Sine 0.02 vs 0.10, Beta 0.04 vs 0.34; losing only on flchain 0.24 vs 0.21 and tying on Tacrolimus at 0.31). Against closed-form expression methods, TIMEVIEW wins on every dataset, with particularly large margins over SINDy (which fails to converge on Stress-Strain and achieves MSE >1.0 on Sine and Beta) and PySR (e.g., 0.04 vs 0.48 on Stress-Strain, 0.02 vs 0.05 on Sine). Against black-box models, TIMEVIEW is competitive but slightly behind: the best tree-based models (XGB-T, LGBM-T, CatBoost-T) achieve 0.02–0.04 lower MSE on most real datasets (e.g., XGB-T 0.09 vs TIMEVIEW 0.13 on Airfoil; XGB-T 0.20 vs TIMEVIEW 0.24 on flchain), while on synthetic datasets the gap narrows to 0.00–0.04 (e.g., Tumor: both achieve 0.00; Sine: LGBM-T 0.00 vs TIMEVIEW 0.02). Overall, TIMEVIEW achieves near-black-box accuracy while being the only method that satisfies bi-level transparency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant bottom-up XAI paradigm for time series (saliency scores at individual time points) by proposing a top-down 'bi-level transparency' framework that decomposes trajectory understanding into trends (level 1) and properties of those trends (level 2). Conceptually, it repurposes classical spline theory (B-Spline basis functions, cubic polynomial composition) and formalizes trajectory shape via a new mathematical object—motifs and compositions—drawing on ideas from dynamical systems (bifurcations) and function sketching. It positions itself against naive adaptations of static transparent models (Linear-T, GAM-T, DecisionTree-T) and closed-form expression discovery (PySR, SINDy), showing that the former cannot model diverse trajectory shapes while the latter often fails to converge or produce interpretable results.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bi-level transparency framework provides a principled, human-aligned vocabulary for discussing trajectory predictions, potentially enabling feature importance, counterfactual, and surrogate methods to operate on compositions and transition points rather than individual time values. This could unlock ML adoption in high-stakes domains (pharmacology, oncology, finance) where regulatory and trust requirements demand more than post-hoc saliency maps. Key limitations include restriction to static features (no time-series inputs or exogenous variables), a fixed knot schedule shared across all trajectories, and reliance on a small set of seven dynamical motifs that may not capture all application-relevant shapes. Extending the framework to recurrent or attention-based inputs, and to richer motif vocabularies, are natural open directions.
