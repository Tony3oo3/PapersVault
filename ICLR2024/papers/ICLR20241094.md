## Problem

Existing label-only membership inference attacks (Gap, Decision-Boundary) achieve negligible TPR at low FPR regimes (e.g., 1.1% at 1% FPR on CIFAR-10), and it remains unclear whether data poisoning can amplify membership leakage in this setting. The paper further shows that naively combining Truth Serum's static label-flipping poisoning with existing label-only attacks actually degrades performance to 0% TPR, revealing a fundamental mismatch between static poisoning and the label-only threat model.

## Input / Output

The attacker takes a challenge point (x, y), black-box label-only query access to the target model, and access to the underlying data distribution D from which poisoned replicas (x, y') are constructed before training begins. The distinguishing test requires only 64 queries to the target model (the challenge point plus 63 close neighbors selected via KL-divergence on shadow model confidences), yielding a misclassification score f(x)_y that is thresholded to produce an IN/OUT decision. This is roughly 39× more query-efficient than prior Decision-Boundary attacks requiring ~2,500 queries per challenge point.

## Method

Chameleon is a three-stage attack: (1) Adaptive Poisoning — the attacker iteratively adds poisoned replicas (x, y') to shadow OUT models until their average confidence on the true label y drops below a threshold t_p, automatically determining the per-point poison count (averaging 0.6–3.5 replicas) rather than using a fixed k; (2) Membership Neighborhood — close neighbors are selected by requiring KL divergence between their and the challenge point's confidence distributions (approximated as Gaussians on logits) to be below t_nb on both IN and OUT shadow models, ensuring these neighbors exhibit similar confidence shifts under poisoning; (3) Distinguishing Test — the attacker queries the target model on the challenge point and its 64 neighbors, computing the fraction of misclassified labels as the membership score. The key insight is that adaptive per-point poisoning creates a reliable IN/OUT separation (IN models still classify correctly while OUT models misclassify) that static poisoning fails to achieve, and that the theoretical analysis (Theorem B.1–B.2) confirms TPR first increases then decreases with poison count, matching Chameleon's empirical behavior.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, Chameleon achieves 22.8% TPR at 1% FPR, compared to 1.3% for the Decision-Boundary attack and 0.0% for the Gap attack — a 17.5× improvement. At the more stringent 0.1% FPR, Chameleon reaches 8.3% TPR on CIFAR-10 (vs. 0.08% for Decision-Boundary), a 104× improvement. On CIFAR-100, the gap widens further: Chameleon achieves 52.5% TPR at 1% FPR versus 3.6% for Decision-Boundary. The attack also requires only 64 queries to the target model, making it ~39× more query-efficient than the Decision-Boundary attack's ~2,500 queries.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Chameleon decisively outperforms both prior label-only MI baselines across all datasets and FPR levels. The Gap attack (Yeom et al., 2018), which simply predicts any misclassified point as a non-member, achieves 0.0% TPR at every FPR threshold on all three image datasets, rendering it entirely ineffective in the low-FPR regime. The Decision-Boundary attack (Choquette-Choo et al., 2021; Li & Zhang, 2021), the prior state-of-the-art for label-only MI, achieves only 1.1–1.3% TPR at 1% FPR on GTSRB and CIFAR-10, while Chameleon reaches 11.4% and 22.8% respectively — improvements of 10.4× and 17.5×. On CIFAR-100, the improvement is 14.6× (52.5% vs. 3.6% at 1% FPR). In terms of AUC, Chameleon scores 76.3% on CIFAR-10 versus 62.8% for Decision-Boundary and 57.7% for Gap. Additionally, Chameleon is ~39× more query-efficient (64 queries vs. ~2,500), and the adaptive poisoning strategy requires on average only 1.4 poisoned replicas per challenge point on CIFAR-10, causing less than 2% test accuracy degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data-poisoning-enhanced membership inference line initiated by Truth Serum (Tramèr et al., 2022) and LiRA (Carlini et al., 2022) from the confidence-based setting to the more realistic label-only setting, where the target model returns only hard labels. A key departure is the finding that Truth Serum's static label-flipping poisoning, when naively combined with existing label-only attacks (Choquette-Choo et al., 2021; Li & Zhang, 2021), actually degrades TPR to 0% at 1% FPR, motivating a fundamentally redesigned adaptive poisoning strategy that selects the number of poisoned replicas per challenge point. The attack also repurposes the KL-divergence-based shadow model framework from LiRA to construct a 'membership neighborhood' of close neighbors, using their misclassification rates as a label-only proxy for confidence-based distinguishing tests.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The label-only setting is the more common real-world scenario (e.g., loan approval, medical diagnosis), yet prior attacks achieved negligible TPR at low FPRs; Chameleon's 17.5× improvement at 1% FPR makes this threat practically relevant. The first theoretical analysis of how poisoning affects label-only MI leakage (Theorem B.1–B.2) provides a principled understanding of why over-poisoning is harmful, guiding future attack design. Open questions remain: how to achieve low-FPR label-only MI without poisoning (which requires shadow model training and adds computational overhead), and whether poisoning strategies can be designed that avoid shadow models entirely. DP-SGD effectively neutralizes the attack but at a 35% accuracy cost, highlighting the utility-privacy tradeoff practitioners must navigate.
