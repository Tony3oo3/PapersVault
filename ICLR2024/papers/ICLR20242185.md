## Problem

Existing generative models based on dynamical transport (flows, diffusions) connect only two probability densities at a time, requiring separate models for each pair when multiple marginals are involved. This paper addresses the multimarginal generative modeling problem: given K+1 prescribed marginal densities, learn a single joint distribution that recovers all of them as marginals while simultaneously identifying multi-way correspondences among the data, a task with applications to all-to-all image translation, style transfer, and data decorruption.

## Input / Output

The method takes K+1 marginal densities (one fixed as a standard Gaussian base ρ_0, the rest from datasets at 32×32 for MNIST or 64×64×3 for AFHQ/Flowers/CelebA) along with a coupling ρ(x_1,…,x_K) linking the data marginals, and a differentiable path α(t) traversing the K-simplex Δ^K. It produces a single weight-shared velocity field b(t,x) that, when integrated along any chosen path on the simplex, transports samples between any pair of marginals; it also yields joint samples (x_0,…,x_K) matching all prescribed marginals and extracts multi-way correspondences by evaluating the conditional expectation fields g_k at the simplex vertices. Unlike standard two-marginal models, the interpolation coordinate is a vector α ∈ Δ^K rather than a scalar t, and the network outputs K+1 vector fields simultaneously from one architecture.

## Method

The core contribution is the barycentric stochastic interpolant x(α) = Σ_k α_k x_k defined over the K-simplex, which generalizes the two-marginal interpolant by replacing scalar time with a simplex coordinate. Theorem 1 shows the density ρ(α,x) satisfies K+1 coupled continuity equations with fluxes g_k(α,x) = E[x_k | x(α)=x], each of which is the unique minimizer of a tractable square-loss regression objective, enabling efficient unsupervised learning via a single weight-shared U-Net. Corollary 2 then shows that specifying any differentiable path α(t) on the simplex yields a valid transport equation with velocity b(t,x) = Σ_k α̇_k(t) g_k(α(t),x), decoupling the learning of g_k from the choice of path. This decoupling enables a Benamou-Brenier-style optimization (Corollary 3) over α(t) to minimize the Wasserstein-2 transport cost without retraining the vector fields. The multimarginal structure means a single model solves C(K,2) pairwise transport problems simultaneously, and training on the full simplex (rather than just edges) empirically produces translations that better preserve source-image characteristics and reveal emergent style transfer across domains.

## Results

The paper is primarily a framework contribution with qualitative demonstrations rather than quantitative benchmarking. In the 2-marginal Gaussian-to-checkerboard transport problem, optimizing the simplex path α(t) via the Benamou-Brenier objective (Corollary 3) reduces the path length over 300 optimization steps, yielding more accurate samples at a fixed number of function evaluations (5 midpoint steps) compared to the linear interpolant. For image translation, a 4-marginal model (K=3) trained on AFHQ-2, Oxford Flowers, and CelebA at 64×64 resolution demonstrates emergent all-to-all style transfer with a single shared U-Net, and MNIST experiments with K=7 marginals show smooth class-to-class digit translation. No standard quantitative metrics (FID, IS, accuracy) are reported.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not include a formal comparison against named baselines or competing methods. The closest comparative element is the ablation in Figure 2 (right), which contrasts three training regimes on MNIST: (i) learning only on a single simplex edge (equivalent to a standard two-marginal model), (ii) learning on all simplex edges (equivalent to multiple independent two-marginal models in a shared representation), and (iii) learning on the entire simplex interior. The authors report that full-simplex training empirically produces translations that better preserve source-image characteristics (e.g., the shape of a '0' emerging in translated digits), but no numerical metric quantifies this difference. The path-optimization experiment (Figure 1) is compared only against the default linear interpolant α=[1−t, t], showing a reduction in transport cost without a formal baseline from prior work. The paper positions itself as a new framework rather than a method to be benchmarked against existing approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the stochastic interpolant framework (Albergo & Vanden-Eijnden, 2023; Albergo et al., 2023) from the standard two-marginal setting to K+1 marginals by lifting the scalar time variable to a simplex coordinate α ∈ Δ^K. While multimarginal optimal transport (Pass, 2014; Gangbo & Świech, 1998) has studied existence of Monge-type joint distributions and Wasserstein barycenters (Cuturi & Doucet, 2014; Noble et al., 2023), those works focus on computing the barycenter or transport plan rather than providing a learnable generative model with velocity fields. The key conceptual departure is showing that the velocity field decomposes into K+1 conditional expectations over the simplex, enabling all-to-all transport between any pair of marginals with a single shared model, and that the path on the simplex can be optimized independently via a Benamou-Brenier-type objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, unified approach to multimarginal generative modeling that was previously fragmented across optimal transport theory and two-marginal generative methods, with practical applications to all-to-all image translation, style transfer, and data decorruption. The decoupling of velocity learning from path design is a clean theoretical contribution that could inform schedule optimization in diffusion models (analogous to Karras et al., 2022). However, the empirical evidence is primarily qualitative (image examples without quantitative FID or transport-cost benchmarks), the velocity class in Corollary 3 is acknowledged to be insufficient for optimal transport, and it remains open whether the framework scales to high-resolution image generation or whether the emergent multi-way correspondences are stable and meaningful at scale.
