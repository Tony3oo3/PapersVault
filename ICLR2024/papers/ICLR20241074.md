## Problem

Kernel surrogate models for neural networks require computationally expensive empirical NTK (eNTK) computation, and existing approximations like the pseudo-NTK have not been systematically evaluated for faithfulness to the underlying NN. Prior evaluations relied on flawed metrics such as test accuracy or similarity to the correct class, which cannot distinguish true coupling from coincidental performance. This paper asks: which approximate eNTK kernel functions produce surrogate models that are most faithfully correlated with the original network, and can random projections tune the computational cost without sacrificing faithfulness?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241074/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained neural network (MLP, CNN, ResNet18/34, MobileNetV2, or BERT-base), the full training dataset, and a test input. It computes per-class Jacobians of the network output with respect to all parameters at every training point, then constructs an N×N kernel matrix. The output is a per-class, per-training-point attribution score (a weighted kernel similarity that sums exactly to the kGLM logit for that class), the kGLM's softmax prediction, and a ranked list of most-similar training examples. The cosine normalization constrains all kernel values to [-1, 1], ensuring identical inputs always yield maximum similarity.

## Method

The paper introduces the trace NTK (trNTK), which approximates the eNTK by performing a block-trace over the C×C output-class blocks and applying cosine normalization, reducing memory from O(N²C²) to O(N²C). It further introduces random projection variants (proj-trNTK, proj-pNTK) that project Jacobians into a K-dimensional subspace (K=10240) via Johnson-Lindenstrauss matrices, allowing users to trade off time and memory complexity independently of model parameter count. The surrogate model is a kernel general linear model (kGLM) trained with cross-entropy loss on the same data as the NN. Faithfulness is measured via Kendall-τ rank correlation between the kGLM and NN softmax probabilities for the correct class, supplemented by test accuracy differential and misclassification coincidence rate. Across ResNet18/CIFAR10 and BERT-base/COLA, the trNTK achieves the highest and most consistent τ_K (≥0.77) and near-zero TAD, outperforming the unnormalized trNTK, embedding kernel, conjugate kernel, and projection variants.

## Results

The trNTK achieves a Kendall-τ rank correlation (τ_K) of at least 0.70 across all eight experiments (ranging from 0.700 for MobileNetV2/CIFAR-10 to 0.882 for CNN/FMNIST2), with test accuracy differentials (TAD) within ±2.2% of the underlying NN. In the head-to-head kernel comparison (Table 2), trNTK attains the highest τ_K on both ResNet18/CIFAR-10 (0.776) and BERT-base/COLA (0.809), while the projection variant proj-trNTK achieves nearly identical faithfulness (0.737 and 0.800) at a fraction of the computational cost (1.12 h vs. 389 h for ResNet18; 22 min vs. 1200 h for BERT-base).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Embedding kernel (Em, Akyürek et al. 2022), trNTK achieves higher τ_K on both ResNet18 (0.776 vs. 0.768) and BERT-base (0.809 vs. 0.65), but Em attains a higher misclassification coincidence rate (R_Miss 0.80 vs. 0.75 on ResNet18; 0.86 vs. 0.67 on BERT-base), suggesting Em captures shared error patterns better even though its overall rank correlation is lower. Against the Conjugate Kernel (CK, Fan & Wang 2020), trNTK dominates on τ_K by a wide margin on both architectures (0.776 vs. 0.630 for ResNet18; 0.809 vs. 0.52 for BERT-base), while CK achieves a higher R_Miss on BERT-base (0.91 vs. 0.67) but a slightly lower one on ResNet18 (0.73 vs. 0.75). The unnormalized trNTK⁰ consistently underperforms the cosine-normalized trNTK on τ_K (e.g., 0.658 vs. 0.776 on ResNet18), confirming that the normalization is critical for faithfulness. The proj-trNTK retains most of trNTK's faithfulness (τ_K 0.737 vs. 0.776 on ResNet18; 0.800 vs. 0.809 on BERT-base) while reducing computation by roughly 350× on ResNet18 and 3200× on BERT-base, making it the practical recommendation for large models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: (1) the NTK equivalence between gradient-trained neural networks and kernel machines (Jacot et al., 2018; Domingos, 2020), and (2) kernel-based data attribution for explain-by-example (Yeh et al., 2018; Park et al., 2023). It departs from prior surrogate-model evaluations (Mohamadi & Sutherland, 2022; Long, 2021) that relied on test accuracy as a faithfulness metric, instead introducing Kendall-τ rank correlation between surrogate and NN softmax outputs. The trNTK itself was introduced in passing by Chen et al. (2022), but this paper is the first to explicitly define, analyze, and evaluate it as a kernel function, and the first to extend it with random-projection variants (building on TRAK's Johnson-Lindenstrauss framework) to tune computational cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, open-source pipeline for computing efficient kernel surrogates in PyTorch, with the proj-trNTK reducing computation from ~389 GPU-hours to ~1 hour for ResNet18/CIFAR10. A notable conceptual finding is that cosine-normalized trNTK attributions are driven by the bulk of training data rather than a sparse set of high-attribution outliers, challenging the sparsity assumption underlying many explain-by-example methods. Key limitations include the lack of a guarantee that the surrogate 'reasons' identically to the NN, the reduction of vector outputs to a single scalar for Kendall-τ, and the absence of human-subject evaluation. Open questions include whether path-kernel approximations (eNTK at multiple training checkpoints) would improve faithfulness, and whether KNN-based surrogates could recover sparse explanations.
