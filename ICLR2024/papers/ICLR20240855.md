## Problem

In thresholding problems (reserve price setting, hiring bars, crowdsourcing task difficulty), the learner must choose a threshold γ in a latent space where the reward g(γ,v) is only observed if the unknown latent value v exceeds γ. This paper asks: how many adaptive queries are needed to find an ε-optimal threshold, and under what conditions on the reward function and value distribution is this even possible? The key gap is that prior work on posted-price query complexity assumed g(γ,v)=γ, while here g can depend on both γ and v, and the censored feedback structure makes the problem fundamentally harder.

## Input / Output

The learner adaptively selects thresholds γ_t ∈ [0,1] and receives censored scalar feedback b_t = g(γ_t, v_t)·1_{v_t ≥ γ_t}, where each v_t is a fresh draw from an unknown distribution F supported on [0,1]. The learner never observes v_t directly—only the reward when v_t ≥ γ_t, or 0 otherwise. The output is a single threshold γ̂ whose expected utility U(γ̂) is within ε of the optimum U(γ*), with probability at least 1−δ. The reward function g: [0,1]² → [0,1] is unknown but belongs to a known class (monotone or right-Lipschitz in γ).

## Method

The paper establishes a complete characterization via three pillars. First, an impossibility result: using a 'needle in the haystack' construction, it shows that for monotone g and general F, the query complexity is infinite because the learner can only observe feedback in {0, γ, 5/8, 1} and cannot pinpoint the unique optimal α among infinitely many candidates. Second, tight Õ(1/ε³) upper bounds under two sufficient conditions—(monotone g + Lipschitz F) or (right-Lipschitz g + general F)—achieved by building a CDF-adaptive discretization grid Γ_A with O(1/ε) points (each interval carrying ~ε CDF mass) and estimating U(γ) at each grid point via the DKW inequality. Third, a matching Ω(1/ε³) lower bound is proved by perturbing a smooth Lipschitz base distribution F₀ on n = Ω(1/ε) disjoint O(ε)-length intervals, creating n hard distributions each requiring Ω(1/ε²) queries to distinguish via Hellinger distance arguments. The online extension reduces the problem to a continuous-arm one-sided Lipschitz bandit, solved by discretizing [0,1] into O(L/ε) arms and running Poly INF, yielding a tight Θ(T^{2/3}) regret bound.

## Results

The paper establishes tight query complexity bounds for learning an ε-optimal threshold. For monotone reward functions with general value distributions, the query complexity is infinite (Theorem 3.1), meaning no finite number of queries suffices. Under either (i) monotone reward + Lipschitz CDF or (ii) right-Lipschitz reward + general distribution, the query complexity is tightly characterized as Θ̃(1/ε³) (Theorems 4.1–4.3). In the adversarial online learning setting, the paper achieves a tight Θ(T^{2/3}) regret bound (Theorem 5.1), matching the lower bound derived from the offline query complexity via online-to-batch conversion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper, so there are no empirical baselines. The closest prior results are Kleinberg & Leighton (2003) and Leme et al. (2023b), which study the special case g(γ,v)=γ (posted pricing / second-price auction). Those works do not require the value distribution to be Lipschitz, making lower-bound constructions via point masses straightforward. In contrast, this paper handles a strictly more general reward function g(γ,v) that can depend on both the threshold and the latent value, and proves matching lower bounds under the stronger constraint that both g and F are Lipschitz. The impossibility result (Theorem 3.1) shows that without either Lipschitzness of F or right-Lipschitzness of g, the problem is fundamentally unsolvable in finite queries — a gap not addressed by prior work. The online regret bound Θ(T^{2/3}) is tight and improves upon the generic O(√T) bound one would get from treating the problem as a standard finite-arm bandit, by exploiting the one-sided Lipschitz structure of the utility function.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the pricing query complexity framework of Leme et al. (2023b), where the reward is simply g(γ,v)=γ, to a broader setting where the reward g(γ,v) can depend on both the threshold and the latent value. The impossibility proof adapts the 'needle in the haystack' construction from Auer et al. (1995) and recent works on bilateral trade and first-price auctions, while the online upper bound reduces to a one-sided Lipschitz bandit problem solvable by Poly INF (Audibert & Bubeck, 2010). A key technical departure from prior lower-bound constructions (Kleinberg & Leighton, 2003; Leme et al., 2023b) is that those works could exploit point masses in the value distribution, whereas this paper proves matching lower bounds even when the CDF is Lipschitz, requiring a novel perturbation of a smooth base distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a complete and tight characterization of query complexity for threshold learning under censored feedback: infinite in the most general monotone case, and Θ̃(1/ε³) under either Lipschitz CDF or right-Lipschitz reward. This resolves a natural question about when the problem is tractable and gives practitioners (auction designers, hiring platforms, crowdsourcing systems) clear structural requirements. Open questions include extending to multi-dimensional thresholds, non-adversarial online settings with distributional assumptions, and whether the discretization-based algorithms can be made computationally more efficient. The impossibility result also cautions against assuming monotonicity alone suffices for learnability in censored-feedback problems.
