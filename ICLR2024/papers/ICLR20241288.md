## Problem

Diffusion models have a sequential chain structure with hundreds of denoising modules, raising the question of whether errors accumulate across steps (error propagation). Previous works attributed this to the cascade architecture by intuition alone, without rigorous mathematical justification, and without explaining why some other sequential models (e.g., CRF, CTC) are immune to the same problem. This paper develops a formal theoretical framework to prove that standard DMs do suffer from error propagation and derives a practical regularization to mitigate it.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard DDPM architectures for RGB image generation at 32×32 (CIFAR-10, ImageNet) and 64×64 (CelebA), taking a standard Gaussian noise sample as input and producing a generated image as output. During training, the regularization additionally consumes both forward-process samples (noised real data via q(x_t|x_0)) and backward-process samples (model-generated denoised variables) to estimate an MMD-based cumulative error at each timestep, with a bootstrapping mechanism that limits the backward chain to L≪T steps rather than the full T=1000 iterations.

## Method

The paper defines three theoretical elements: a modular error (KL divergence between the model's one-step denoising distribution and the true posterior), a cumulative error (KL divergence between the model's and true marginal distributions at each timestep), and a propagation equation proving E_t^cumu ≥ E_{t+1}^cumu + E_t^mod, which implies an amplification factor μ_t ≥ 1 confirming error propagation. To reduce this, the authors introduce a regularization loss L_reg based on the MMD upper bound of the cumulative error, weighted exponentially (w_t ∝ exp(ρ(T−t))) to emphasize later denoising steps where errors are most severe. Because computing the full backward chain is infeasible, a bootstrap algorithm inspired by temporal difference learning initializes the backward variable at a random future timestep s via the forward process (warm start from real data) and then runs only L≈5 denoising steps, trading a small bias for a large speedup. The total training objective is a linear combination of the standard DDPM noise-prediction loss and this regularization term.

## Results

The proposed regularization achieves FID scores of 2.93 on CIFAR-10 (32×32), 2.55 on ImageNet (32×32), and 1.22 on CelebA (64×64), outperforming all baselines on every dataset. Compared to vanilla DDPM, the method reduces FID by 18.83% on CIFAR-10, 29.55% on ImageNet, and 29.47% on CelebA. All improvements over baselines are statistically significant (p < 0.01 under t-test). The MMD-based cumulative error dynamics also flatten from an exponential uptrend to a near-horizontal line after applying the regularization, confirming reduced error propagation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four baselines on all three datasets. Against ADM-IP (Ning et al., 2023), which adds Gaussian input perturbation to reduce exposure bias, the proposed regularization achieves a 9.84% lower FID on CIFAR-10 (2.93 vs. 3.25) and similar margins on ImageNet (2.55 vs. 2.72) and CelebA (1.22 vs. 1.31), despite ADM-IP using the stronger ADM backbone rather than DDPM. Against Consistent DM (Daras et al., 2023), the gap is 0.38 FID on CIFAR-10 (2.93 vs. 3.31) and 0.61 on ImageNet (2.55 vs. 3.16). Against FP-Diffusion (Lai et al., 2022), the advantage is 0.54 FID on CIFAR-10 (2.93 vs. 3.47) and 0.73 on ImageNet (2.55 vs. 3.28). The paper attributes its advantage over ADM-IP to not imposing any assumption on the distribution form of propagating errors, and distinguishes itself from consistency-based regularizers (Consistent DM, FP-Diffusion) by targeting robustness to input errors rather than improving per-step score estimation accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DDPM (Ho et al., 2020) and introduces a theoretical framework (modular error, cumulative error, propagation equation) to rigorously analyze error propagation in diffusion models, departing from prior works (Ning et al., 2023; Li et al., 2023; Daras et al., 2023) that attribute the problem to cascade structure without formal justification. Conceptually, it distinguishes itself from consistency-based regularizations (Lai et al., 2022; Daras et al., 2023) that improve per-module score accuracy, arguing instead that the key to mitigating error propagation is making modules robust to accumulated input errors. The practical method draws on MMD (Gretton et al., 2012) as a computable proxy for intractable KL divergences and borrows the bootstrapping idea from temporal difference learning (Tesauro et al., 1995) to avoid full backward sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous theoretical account of why error propagation affects diffusion models (μ_t ≥ 1), filling a gap where prior explanations relied on intuition alone. The practical regularization yields substantial FID reductions (18–29%) on small-image benchmarks, but the approach is validated only on 32×32 and 64×64 images with a U-Net backbone, leaving open whether the theory and method transfer to continuous-time formulations, larger architectures, or higher-resolution generation. The reliance on two assumptions (Gaussian output of ε_θ and non-increasing entropy) and the bias introduced by bootstrapping are acknowledged but not fully stress-tested, and the comparison baselines (vanilla DDPM, ADM-IP) are relatively weak compared to state-of-the-art diffusion methods.
