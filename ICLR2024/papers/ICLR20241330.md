## Problem

Optimization-based 3D generation methods that distill 2D diffusion priors via Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) typically require hours of per-sample optimization due to costly NeRF rendering, and the occupancy pruning technique used to accelerate NeRF in reconstruction settings is ineffective under the ambiguous SDS supervision of generative tasks. This paper asks whether a different 3D representation can fundamentally simplify the optimization landscape for SDS-based generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241330/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For image-to-3D, the method takes a single RGB image with a foreground mask (preprocessed via U2-Net background removal and object recentering) and produces a textured polygonal mesh in ~2 minutes; for text-to-3D, it takes a text prompt and produces the same output in ~4 minutes. The exported mesh is decimated to 10^5 faces with isotropic remeshing (average edge length 0.015), UV-unwrapped via Xatlas, and carries a baked texture image. All experiments run on a single NVIDIA V100 with under 8 GB GPU memory.

## Method

The framework has two stages. Stage 1 adapts 3D Gaussian Splatting into a generative setting by initializing a small set of Gaussians (5000 for image-to-3D, 1000 for text-to-3D) and applying a progressive densification schedule (every 100 or 50 steps) that aligns with the gradual shape emergence of SDS optimization, rather than the reconstruction-style pruning of NeRF. The SDS loss uses Zero-1-to-3 XL (image-to-3D) or Stable Diffusion (text-to-3D) as the 2D prior, with linearly annealed timesteps and a reference-view RGBA loss for image conditioning. Stage 2 extracts a textured mesh via block-wise local density queries (16^3 overlapping blocks, each querying an 8^3 grid to form a 128^3 density field) followed by Marching Cubes, then refines the UV texture using an SDEdit-inspired multi-step denoising process with a pixel-wise MSE loss instead of SDS, which avoids the over-saturated blocky artifacts that latent-space SDS gradients produce through mipmap sampling.

## Results

On the image-to-3D task, DreamGaussian achieves a CLIP-similarity of 0.738 in just 2 minutes, compared to Zero-1-to-3's 0.647 in 20 minutes — a ~10× speedup with higher quality. Even the Stage-1-only variant (1 minute) reaches 0.678, already surpassing Zero-1-to-3. In the user study (scale 1–5), DreamGaussian scores 4.31 on reference-view consistency and 3.92 on overall model quality, outperforming all baselines including Zero-1-to-3 (3.48 / 3.11). For text-to-3D, the method produces comparable quality to DreamFusion while running in ~4 minutes versus hours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamGaussian outperforms all three inference-only baselines (One-2-3-45, Point-E, Shap-E) on CLIP-similarity by 0.14–0.15 points while being only marginally slower (2 min vs. 27–78 s). Against the primary optimization-based baseline Zero-1-to-3, DreamGaussian achieves a higher CLIP-similarity (0.738 vs. 0.647) in 1/10th the time (2 min vs. 20 min). The enhanced Zero-1-to-3* variant (with an additional mesh fine-tuning stage) still leads on CLIP-similarity (0.778 vs. 0.738) but requires 30 minutes — 15× longer. In the user study, DreamGaussian wins on both reference-view consistency (4.31 vs. 3.48 for Zero-1-to-3) and overall quality (3.92 vs. 3.11), confirming the quantitative advantage. For text-to-3D, DreamGaussian produces results comparable to DreamFusion (which uses NeRF and takes hours) while completing in ~4 minutes on a single GPU.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023), originally designed for reconstruction, into the generative setting by replacing NeRF as the differentiable 3D representation in Score Distillation Sampling pipelines (DreamFusion, Magic3D, Zero-1-to-3). The key conceptual departure is the argument that occupancy pruning—standard for accelerating NeRF in reconstruction—is ineffective under the ambiguous SDS supervision of generative tasks, whereas progressive densification of Gaussians naturally aligns with the gradual emergence of structure during generation. For texture refinement, the paper departs from prior SDS-based fine-tuning by borrowing the SDEdit-style multi-step denoising approach and applying it in UV space with a pixel-wise MSE loss, avoiding the over-saturated artifacts that SDS gradients produce through mipmap sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× speedup (2 minutes vs. 20 minutes for image-to-3D) makes optimization-based 3D content creation practically deployable, and the output is a ready-to-use textured polygonal mesh rather than an implicit NeRF, directly enabling downstream applications like rigging and animation. The mesh extraction algorithm from Gaussians via block-wise local density querying is a new algorithmic contribution that had no prior formulation. Open questions include whether the remaining quality gap versus the best optimization-based methods (CLIP 0.738 vs. 0.778) can be closed, and whether the multi-face Janus problem, baked lighting, and blurry back-view textures can be resolved within this Gaussian Splatting framework or require fundamentally different supervision signals.
