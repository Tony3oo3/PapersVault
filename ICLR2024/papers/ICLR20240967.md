## Problem

Existing density-based OOD detection methods either impose restrictive distributional assumptions (e.g., Gaussian in GEM, Gibbs-Boltzmann in Energy) to make normalization tractable, or bypass the partition function entirely, leading to misalignment between the scoring function and true data density. The paper asks whether a unified theoretical framework can guide density function design across a broad exponential family without committing to any single prior, while keeping the normalization computationally tractable.

## Input / Output

The method consumes penultimate-layer feature vectors z (dimension 342 for DenseNet-101 on CIFAR, 1280 for MobileNetV2/ResNet-50 on ImageNet) extracted from a pre-trained classifier on RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet). It produces a normalized density score p̂_θ(z) = (1/K)Σ_k exp(-d_φ(z, μ(η_k)))/Φ_IS(k; S), which is compared against a threshold λ to yield a binary ID/OOD decision. The partition function Φ(k) is estimated via importance sampling over a subset (α=10%) of ID training features, making the method fully post-hoc with no retraining.

## Method

CONJNORM reframes density function design as selecting the optimal l_p norm coefficient p ∈ (1,3] by leveraging the conjugate relationship between the l_p norm (as cumulant function ψ) and the l_q norm (as Bregman divergence generator φ, where q = p/(p−1)). The resulting class-conditional density is g_θ(z,k) = exp(−d_φ(z, μ(η_k))), where d_φ is the Bregman divergence induced by φ(z) = ½‖z‖_q², and μ(η_k) is the class mean in feature space. The partition function Φ(k) is estimated unbiasedly via Monte Carlo importance sampling with a uniform proposal over ID training data, avoiding the intractable integral over the high-dimensional feature space. The optimal p is identified by searching over (1,3] using Tiny-ImageNet-200 as auxiliary OOD data, with the conjugate condition q = p/(p−1) empirically validated to outperform non-conjugate q values by ~10% FPR95. This generalizes prior methods: p=2 recovers the Gaussian/Mahalanobis case, while p≠2 captures non-Gaussian structure in the feature space.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, CONJNORM achieves a FPR95 of 28.27% and AUROC of 92.50%, outperforming the strongest baseline ASH (41.40% FPR95) by 13.13 percentage points. On CIFAR-10, it reaches 13.92% FPR95 and 97.15% AUROC, beating ASH (15.05% FPR95) and KNN (17.43% FPR95). On ImageNet-1K with MobileNetV2, CONJNORM+ASH attains 28.31% average FPR95 and 92.94% AUROC across four OOD datasets, surpassing the best prior method ASH (38.67% FPR95) by over 10 percentage points. The method also leads on hard OOD detection (78.05% avg FPR95 vs. 81.12% for KNN) and long-tailed settings (52.00% avg FPR95 vs. 58.11% for KNN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CONJNORM outperforms all ten baselines across every evaluated dataset and metric. The closest competitors are ASH and KNN: on CIFAR-100, CONJNORM reduces FPR95 from 41.40% (ASH) and 41.52% (KNN) to 28.27%, a gap of over 13 points. On CIFAR-10, the margin over ASH narrows to 1.13 points (13.92% vs. 15.05% FPR95), while the gap over KNN is 3.51 points. Logit-based methods (MSP, ODIN, Energy) and distance-based methods (Maha, GEM) are consistently outperformed by 15–50+ FPR95 points, confirming the paper's argument that these scores are misaligned with true data density. On ImageNet-1K, CONJNORM+ASH achieves 28.31% average FPR95 versus 38.67% for ASH and 45.02% for ReAct, with particularly large gains on Textures (12.70% vs. 13.12% for ASH) and iNaturalist (24.08% vs. 39.10% for ASH). In hard OOD and long-tailed settings, CONJNORM also leads, beating KNN by 3.07 and 6.11 FPR95 points respectively, demonstrating robustness beyond standard benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate families of post-hoc OOD scoring functions—logit-based (Energy, MSP), distance-based (Mahalanobis), and density-based (GEM)—under a single Bregman divergence framework derived from the exponential family of distributions (Forster & Warmuth, 2002; Banerjee et al., 2005). It departs from GEM's strict Gaussian assumption and Energy's implicit constant-partition assumption by parameterizing the cumulant function as an l_p norm, whose conjugate l_q norm (via 1/p + 1/q = 1) determines the Bregman divergence and hence the density function. The partition function, which is intractable in general, is estimated via Monte Carlo importance sampling rather than being assumed away, building on the density-based post-hoc formulation of Morteza & Li (2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Bregman divergence framework provides the first principled, distribution-agnostic guideline for designing OOD density scores, replacing ad-hoc choices (Gaussian, Gibbs-Boltzmann) with a data-driven search over a single hyperparameter p. The empirical finding that the optimal p consistently falls in (2, 3) rather than at p = 2 (Gaussian) offers concrete evidence that the Gaussian assumption underlying GEM and Mahalanobis methods is suboptimal. Key limitations include the manual search for p (no automatic learning procedure), evaluation restricted to CNN backbones without Vision-Language Models, and the uniform class-prior assumption. Open questions include whether p can be learned end-to-end, whether the framework extends to transformer-based or VLM features, and whether richer Bregman divergences beyond the l_p/l_q conjugate pair yield further gains.
