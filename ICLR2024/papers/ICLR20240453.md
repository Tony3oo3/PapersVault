## Problem

Ensemble-based methods like WiSE-FT achieve strong OOD generalization, but the underlying mechanism remains unexplained, and the mysterious superiority of weight space ensembles over output space ensembles in OOD settings has no theoretical account. Conventional OOD theory emphasizes learning invariant features and discarding spurious ones, yet this perspective cannot explain why combining models that each rely on different spurious features yields better OOD performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240453/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The practical method (BANG) takes a pre-trained CLIP ViT-B/16 model and a fine-tuned variant, averages their weights, and produces class labels on RGB images from ImageNet and its OOD variants (IN-V2, IN-R, IN-A, IN-S, ObjectNet). The theoretical model operates on a bilinear structure w^T x Φ where x ∈ R^{d×(d_s+d_v)} concatenates invariant and spurious feature vectors, and Φ is a binary feature-selection mask. The synthetic MultiColorMNIST dataset uses 42×42×3 pixel images containing 32 color patches (each a spurious feature) alongside a digit shape (invariant feature) for 10-class classification.

## Method

The paper provides a theoretical analysis in a multi-class setting with multiple spurious features, showing that ensembles improve OOD accuracy by incorporating a more diverse set of spurious features, which weakens each individual spurious feature's contribution and reduces the probability of correlated failures under distributional shift. This explains the 'FalseFalseTrue' phenomenon (where the ensemble corrects samples both individual models get wrong) and the WSE-over-OSE gap: in WSE, overlapped features get amplified coefficients (×4 vs ×2 in OSE), which further diversifies the effective feature set. Building on the finding that imbalanced model confidence degrades ensemble performance, the authors propose BANG, which fine-tunes the pre-trained model using label smoothing and/or MixUp to prevent overconfidence before performing weight-space averaging, yielding ~1.9pp average OOD improvement over vanilla WiSE-FT across five ImageNet variants.
![Method overview](../imgs/ICLR20240453/cropped_page26_idx20.webp)

## Results

On five ImageNet OOD variants (IN-V2, IN-R, IN-A, IN-S, ObjectNet), BANG(Mixup+LS) achieves 64.9% average OOD accuracy, improving over WiSE-FT (63.0%) by 1.9pp and over zero-shot CLIP (58.1%) by 6.8pp. On the synthetic MultiColorMNIST dataset with 32 spurious features, the output-space ensemble of two models consistently outperforms individual models across all distributional shift levels (e.g., 55.25% vs. ~49% at p=0.80), while on SingleColorMNIST (1 spurious feature) the ensemble yields no gain, confirming the role of spurious feature diversity. The FalseFalseTrue ratio — the fraction of OOD samples where both individual models err but the ensemble is correct — is substantial across all OOD datasets, sometimes exceeding the overall accuracy improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is WiSE-FT (Wortsman et al., 2022), which averages the pre-trained CLIP and fine-tuned CLIP in weight space. BANG(Mixup+LS) outperforms WiSE-FT by 1.9pp in average OOD accuracy (64.9% vs. 63.0%), with the largest gains on ImageNet-A (+6.0pp: 58.2% vs. 52.2%) and ObjectNet (+1.6pp: 58.9% vs. 57.3%), while slightly trailing on IN-R (79.7% vs. 78.7% is actually a gain; the paper notes IN-R and IN-S show negative ImproveContri for TF+FT groups due to overconfidence). BANG also substantially outperforms zero-shot CLIP (58.1% avg OOD) and vanilla fine-tuning (53.8% avg OOD). The paper attributes WiSE-FT's suboptimal performance to the fine-tuned model's overconfidence on OOD data (e.g., 0.37 accuracy but >0.7 average confidence on ImageNet-A), which biases the weight-space average toward the overconfident model's incorrect predictions. On MultiColorMNIST, the ensemble of two independently trained models beats each individual model by 6–7pp at p=0.80, while on SingleColorMNIST (single spurious feature) the ensemble provides no improvement, validating the theoretical prediction that spurious feature diversity is the key mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical framework of Rosenfeld et al. (2020) and Wald et al. (2022) on spurious features in OOD generalization from a single-feature, two-class setting to a multi-class, multi-spurious-feature setting, and applies it to explain weight space ensembles (WiSE-FT, Wortsman et al., 2022). It deliberately departs from the dominant IRM/invariant-learning paradigm (Arjovsky et al., 2019) by arguing that diversifying spurious features across ensemble members—rather than eliminating them—is the mechanism behind ensemble OOD gains. The paper also provides the first theoretical account of why weight space ensembles outperform output space ensembles in OOD, a gap left open by prior empirical observations (Wortsman et al., 2022; Ramé et al., 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The spurious-feature-diversification perspective offers a practical, easily implementable alternative to the difficult invariant-learning approaches that have struggled on real-world datasets, and the BANG method (label smoothing / MixUp during fine-tuning before weight averaging) provides a simple ~1.9pp OOD improvement over WiSE-FT. However, the theoretical analysis relies on a bilinear model with orthogonal features and infinite samples, and the empirical validation on MultiColorMNIST is synthetic; whether the mechanism transfers faithfully to deep, non-linear architectures with correlated features remains an open question. The finding that fine-tuned models' overconfidence degrades weight-space ensembles is a useful diagnostic that may inform future calibration-aware ensemble design.
