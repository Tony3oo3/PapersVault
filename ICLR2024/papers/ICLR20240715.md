## Problem

Designing effective quantum feature maps for real-world datasets remains a major obstacle for quantum kernels on NISQ hardware. Prior approaches either search only over discrete circuit layouts (evolutionary/Bayesian methods) or optimize only continuous variational parameters (TEK), but none jointly optimizes both while simultaneously addressing the vanishing similarity (kernel concentration) issue that arises when encoding high-dimensional data into limited-qubit quantum circuits.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240715/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled dataset (n samples with d features and binary/multi-class labels) plus quantum hardware constraints (qubit count N, available gate set G, and topology) as input. Before quantum processing, feature selection (mRMR) reduces each sample from d to p dimensions (p ≪ d) to fit the N-qubit register and suppress kernel concentration. The output is an optimized quantum feature map — a specific circuit layout S* and variational parameters θ* — from which the quantum kernel matrix W* is constructed and used for classification; the final reported metric is test accuracy.

## Method

QuKerNet decouples the joint discrete-continuous optimization of quantum feature maps into a two-stage pipeline. In stage one (layout search), circuit layouts are encoded as multi-channel images (one channel per gate type, with pixel positions mirroring qubit/gate placement), and a 2-layer MLP predictor is trained in a supervised fashion to predict kernel-target alignment (KTA) — a computationally cheap O(n²·p) surrogate for the O(n³) training accuracy — on M sampled layouts; the predictor then scores M′ new layouts and the top-k are retained. In stage two (fine-tuning), m encoding gates in each of the k candidate circuits are randomly replaced with variational parameters θ, which are optimized to maximize KTA. The search space is built from Hardware Efficient Ansatz blocks with structured rules for single-qubit rotation gates (alternating R_X/R_Y/R_Z by qubit parity) and two-qubit CNOT/identity entangling layers, constrained so that N·L₀ ~ O(p) to avoid vanishing similarity.

## Results

QuKerNet-2 (full pipeline with layout + parameter optimization) achieves the highest test accuracy across all three benchmark datasets: 86.80% on Tailored MNIST, 94.40% on Tailored Credit Card, and 91.33% on the synthetic dataset. On the synthetic dataset designed to probe quantum advantage, QuKerNet-2 outperforms the classical RBFK by ~20 percentage points (91.33% vs. 71.67%) and the parameter-only TEK by ~18 points (91.33% vs. 73%). The progressive improvement across QuKerNet stages (training → candidate → fine-tune) confirms that jointly optimizing circuit layout S and variational parameters θ is essential for peak performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QuKerNet outperforms all five baselines on every dataset. Against the classical RBFK, QuKerNet gains +3.2 pp on Tailored MNIST (86.80 vs. 83.60), +1.6 pp on Tailored CC (94.40 vs. 92.80), and a decisive +19.7 pp on the synthetic dataset (91.33 vs. 71.67), suggesting a potential quantum advantage. Against the fixed-layout HEAK quantum kernel, QuKerNet achieves substantially higher accuracy (e.g., 70% vs. 40% under real-device noise on the synthetic set). Against TEK, which optimizes only variational parameters θ on a fixed layout, QuKerNet-2 leads by ~18 pp on the synthetic dataset (91.33% vs. 73%), demonstrating that layout optimization S is critical. Against the adaptive classical NKN (Sun et al., 2018), QuKerNet dominates by large margins (e.g., 94.40% vs. 47.67% on CC), while NKN shows signs of overfitting with near-100% training accuracy. Against Bayesian Optimization (Optuna) as an alternative search strategy, QuKerNet's neural-predictor-based search consistently yields better final kernels (86.80 vs. 85.60 on MNIST; 94.40 vs. 93.00 on CC; 91.33 vs. 89.34 on synthetic), confirming the advantage of the learned surrogate over heuristic search.

## Relation to Prior Work

QuKerNet extends the quantum kernel design literature by unifying two previously separate optimization streams—circuit layout search (Altares-Lopez et al., 2021) and variational parameter tuning (Hubregtsen et al., 2022)—into a single discrete-continuous joint optimization problem. It borrows the image-based circuit encoding from neural predictor QAS (Zhang et al., 2021) and repurposes kernel-target alignment (Cristianini et al., 2001) as a computationally cheap surrogate label, avoiding the expensive full kernel evaluation that would be required for supervised training of the predictor. The paper explicitly positions prior layout-only and parameter-only methods as special cases of its framework, and distinguishes itself from QAS by noting that quantum kernels' evaluation cost makes direct adaptation of QAS pipelines non-trivial.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical bottleneck for quantum kernel methods on NISQ devices: the vanishing similarity (kernel concentration) problem that degrades performance on high-dimensional data, and the combinatorial explosion of feasible circuit layouts. By combining feature selection with a learned surrogate, QuKerNet makes automated quantum feature map design feasible within current hardware constraints. However, the empirical validation is limited to small-scale experiments (≤300 samples, 8 qubits in main results), and the 'quantum advantage' claims rest on a synthetic dataset constructed to favor quantum kernels. Open questions include scalability to larger datasets and qubit counts, validation on real quantum hardware beyond a single IBM device, and whether the neural predictor generalizes across fundamentally different problem structures.
