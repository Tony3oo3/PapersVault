## Problem

In potential games with multiple Nash equilibria, standard worst-case metrics like the Price of Anarchy provide no information about which equilibrium learning dynamics will actually select, since certain equilibria are reachable only from measure-zero initial conditions. Moreover, no rigorous comparison existed between two optimal no-regret dynamics (gradient descent vs. replicator dynamics) in any single game setting, leaving open whether one can be shown to be approximately optimal under random initialization even when PoA is unbounded.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a finite potential game (payoff matrix), an interior initial mixed strategy profile in the joint simplex, and a QRD parameter q ≥ 0 that interpolates between gradient descent (q=0), replicator dynamics (q=1), and log-barrier dynamics (q=2). The primary outputs are the limiting Nash equilibrium, the Average Price of Anarchy (APoA) computed as a weighted average over regions of attraction, and the geometric separatrix (stable manifold of the mixed NE) that partitions the strategy simplex into basins of attraction for each pure equilibrium. The APoA is bounded between the PoA and the Price of Stability, and its tightness depends on the interplay between risk- and payoff-dominance.

## Method

The paper establishes pointwise convergence of all QRD to Nash equilibria in perfectly-regular potential games (PRPGs, which include almost all finite PGs) via Lyapunov analysis and the finiteness of restricted equilibria. For performance analysis in symmetric 2×2 PRPGs, the key technical tool is an explicit invariant function Ψ_q(x,y) = ψ_q(x) − ψ_q(y) that remains constant along QRD trajectories; its zero-level set factorizes into the stable manifold (separatrix) and the unstable manifold (diagonal x=y) of the mixed NE. By analyzing the curvature and position of the separatrix relative to the diagonal, the paper proves that GD outperforms RD in expected social welfare if and only if the payoff-dominant equilibrium is also risk-dominant (α > 0.5), and that the APoA of GD is tightly bounded by 2 in this regime despite unbounded PoA. The invariant function approach is then validated experimentally on higher-dimensional diagonal PRPGs (up to 20×20), where GD consistently outperforms RD with APoA typically around 1.1–1.2.

## Results

In the 2×2 symmetric PRPG setting, the APoA of gradient descent (GD) is provably upper-bounded by 2 (Theorem 4.8) when payoff- and risk-dominant equilibria coincide, despite the PoA being unbounded. In higher-dimensional diagonal PRPGs (m=3 to 20), GD achieves APoA of 1.11±0.02 (max 1.22) at m=20, consistently outperforming replicator dynamics (RD) which achieves 1.21±0.04 (max 1.28) at the same dimension. The APoA values remain well below the theoretical bound of 2 across all tested dimensions, with GD's advantage over RD growing slightly as dimension increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between gradient descent (GD, q=0) and replicator dynamics (RD, q=1) as two canonical no-regret learning dynamics. In the 2×2 symmetric PRPG class, Theorem 4.6 establishes that GD achieves higher expected social welfare than RD if and only if the payoff-dominant equilibrium is also risk-dominant (α > 0.5); the reverse holds when they differ (α < 0.5), with equality only at α = 0.5. In the higher-dimensional diagonal PRPG experiments (Section 5), where the payoff-dominant equilibrium is also the least risky by construction, GD consistently outperforms RD across all dimensions m=3,5,10,20, with APoA gaps of approximately 0.04–0.09 in mean and 0.04–0.10 in maximum values. This empirical pattern supports the conjecture that the 2×2 theoretical result generalizes to larger settings when risk- and payoff-dominance are properly defined. The paper also notes that log-barrier dynamics (q=2) and higher-q QRD variants exhibit monotonic RoA behavior consistent with the GD/RD comparison, though a full analytical proof for all q remains open.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the average-case performance analysis of Panageas & Piliouras (2016), who introduced the APoA metric and studied only replicator dynamics in restricted potential games, to the full family of Q-replicator dynamics (QRD) including gradient descent and log-barrier dynamics. The convergence framework builds on Mertikopoulos & Sandholm (2018) for the QRD equations and Swenson et al. (2020) for regular potential games, while the technical proof of the comparative performance result leverages invariant functions (constants of motion) from Nagarajan et al. (2020) and Piliouras & Wang (2021). The key departure from prior work is providing the first rigorous head-to-head comparison between GD and RD in any game setting, showing that the optimal choice depends on whether risk- and payoff-dominance coincide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first formal criterion for selecting between two standard no-regret learning dynamics based solely on game-theoretic properties (risk vs. payoff dominance), which is practically actionable for multi-agent system designers. The tight APoA bound of 2 for GD despite unbounded PoA demonstrates that average-case analysis can be far more informative than worst-case metrics. However, the rigorous results are confined to 2×2 symmetric PRPGs; the higher-dimensional experiments (diagonal games up to 20×20) are purely empirical, and the non-symmetric case shows that monotonicity of regions of attraction in q breaks down, leaving a significant gap for general games. Open questions include extending the invariant function approach to games with limit cycles and establishing rigorous bounds beyond the 2×2 setting.
