## Problem

Cross-modal tasks like image captioning typically require expensive paired multimodal data, and while multi-modal contrastive models (CLIP, ImageBind) create shared embedding spaces that theoretically allow unimodal training, the direct interchangeability of embeddings across modalities is undermined by a poorly understood geometric structure. The paper identifies and formally explains two specific obstructions — a constant modality gap vector and Gaussian alignment noise — that prevent naive substitution of one modality's embeddings for another's.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes only unimodal data: text captions (for image captioning) or images (for text-to-image generation), encoded by a frozen contrastive encoder (CLIP ViT-B/32 or ImageBind) into 512-d embeddings. At inference, the encoder is swapped to the other modality (e.g., image encoder for captioning), and the decoder produces either a text caption (via GPT-2 with an MLP prefix mapping from 512-d to 768-d) or a generated image (via a StyleGAN2-based generator). No paired data is ever seen during the pre-training phase, making this a true zero-shot cross-modal evaluation.

## Method

The C³ method builds on a theoretical characterization of the multi-modal contrastive space geometry: e_x − e_y = c_⊥ + ε, where c_⊥ is a constant modality gap vector orthogonal to the embedding span (caused by dimensional collapse at initialization and the absence of gradient in ineffective dimensions during contrastive optimization) and ε is zero-mean Gaussian alignment noise (caused by the temperature-controlled stable region of the contrastive loss). The three steps are: (1) Connect — use a pre-trained contrastive model to establish the shared space; (2) Collapse — subtract the per-modality embedding mean (e′ = e − E[e]) to eliminate the constant gap, reducing the residual difference to pure alignment noise; (3) Corrupt — inject explicit Gaussian noise N(0, σ²I) into the training input so the decoder learns invariance to the residual alignment noise, making cross-modal inference robust. The approach is encoder-agnostic and modality-agnostic, demonstrated across image, audio, and video captioning plus text-to-image generation.

## Results

On image-free zero-shot image captioning (MS-COCO), C³ achieves BLEU-1 of 71.0, BLEU-4 of 27.7, METEOR of 25.0, ROUGE-L of 52.0, CIDEr of 93.3, and SPICE of 18.3, setting a new state of the art. For language-free text-to-image generation, C³ achieves FID of 19.6 and IS of 26.0, outperforming LAFITE (FID 20.9, IS 24.9) and DALL-E (FID 27.5, IS 17.9). The method generalizes to audio and video captioning via ImageBind embeddings, achieving BLEU-1 of 29.5 (Clotho) and 31.4 (MSR-VTT) in zero-shot settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C³ outperforms all baselines on every reported metric in the image-free zero-shot captioning setting. The closest competitor, CapDec (Nukrai et al., 2022), which adds Gaussian noise but does not explicitly collapse the modality gap, trails C³ by 1.8 BLEU-1, 1.3 BLEU-4, and 1.5 CIDEr points. WS-ClipCap-Multi (Tam et al., 2023), which uses paraphrased decoding, is 5.5 BLEU-1 and 18.7 CIDEr points behind C³. Earlier methods that do not leverage the contrastive embedding space (ZeroCap, MAGIC, ESPER) are substantially weaker, with CIDEr scores of 34.5, 49.3, and 78.2 respectively versus C³'s 93.3. For text-to-image generation, C³ improves FID from 20.9 (LAFITE) to 19.6 and IS from 24.9 to 26.0, while DALL-E and CogView lag by 7–8 FID points. In the low-data regime (1% fine-tuning), C³ surpasses the fully supervised ClipCap baseline by 7.4 BLEU-1 and 25.3 CIDEr points, demonstrating the largest gains when paired multi-modal data are scarce.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate empirical observations into a single theoretically-grounded framework: CLIP's contrastive representation space (Radford et al., 2021) provides the shared embedding geometry, Zhang et al. (2023) showed that mean subtraction removes the modality gap, and Nukrai et al. (2022) / Zhou et al. (2022c) showed that noise injection improves cross-modal decoding. Prior works (Liang et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2023) identified the modality gap empirically but could not explain its geometric structure or why it persists after optimization. The key departure is the theoretical explanation via dimensional collapse at initialization (no gradient in ineffective dimensions) and the stable region of contrastive loss (alignment noise), which reframes the gap as a constant vector orthogonal to the embedding span rather than a vague distributional shift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical characterization of the multi-modal contrastive space geometry (e_x - e_y = c_⊥ + ε) provides a principled foundation for designing cross-modal applications, moving the field beyond ad-hoc tricks like paraphrased decoding, memory retrieval, and prior networks. The C³ method's simplicity (mean subtraction + Gaussian noise) and broad applicability across image, audio, video captioning and text-to-image generation make it a practical default for low-data cross-modal settings. Open questions include whether the geometry holds for next-generation multi-modal models (e.g., LLM-based), whether the modality gap can be eliminated at the encoder level rather than corrected post-hoc, and whether the dimensional collapse explanation generalizes to non-contrastive pre-training objectives.
