## Problem

When language models are offered as a service with hidden system prompts, users can observe outputs but not the underlying prompt. This paper formalizes 'language model inversion'—recovering the input prompt from the model's next-token probability distribution—and demonstrates for the first time that these distributions are largely invertible, revealing that next-token probabilities retain substantial residual information about all preceding tokens, not just the immediately preceding one.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241408/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The inverter takes a single next-token probability vector (32,000-dim for Llama-2) as input and produces the full prompt text as output. In restricted API settings where only text output and a logit-bias parameter are exposed, the paper recovers the full distribution via per-token binary search, requiring |V| × precision-bits API calls. The probability vector is consumed as a fixed-length sequence of 42 pseudo-embedding tokens (each 768-dim) rather than a single vector, preserving the full rank of the distribution.

## Method

The core architecture is a T5-base encoder-decoder (222M parameters) trained with supervised learning on 2.33M prompt–probability pairs from the newly constructed Instructions-2M dataset. The key design choice is 'unrolling' the log-probability vector into a sequence of pseudo-embeddings: the 32K-dim vector is split into chunks of size d=768, each chunk passed through a position-specific MLP to produce a 768-dim vector, yielding a 42-token encoder input. This avoids the severe rank loss that would result from projecting the full vocabulary-sized vector into a single embedding. The decoder then generates the prompt via standard autoregressive decoding, conditioned on the encoder's representation through cross-attention. For closed APIs, a binary-search procedure (Algorithm 1) extracts each token's logit by finding the minimum logit bias that makes that token the argmax, enabling full distribution reconstruction from text-only access.

## Results

On the Instructions-2M test set, the proposed inversion method achieves a BLEU of 59.2 and token-level F1 of 77.8 on Llama-2 7B, recovering 26.6% of prompts exactly. On the RLHF chat variant, it achieves BLEU 58.3, token F1 75.8, and 23.4% exact match. The method substantially outperforms all baselines: the best jailbreak oracle reaches only BLEU 54.4 (raw LM) and 14.9 (chat), while few-shot GPT-4 achieves just BLEU 3.0–6.1. Out-of-distribution evaluation on Alpaca Code Generation and Anthropic HH confirms strong generalization, with token F1 of 74.9 and 55.8 respectively on the raw LM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baseline families: a trained sample inverter, few-shot prompting with GPT-3.5/GPT-4, and manually written jailbreak strings (both mean and oracle). The proposed method dominates all baselines on every metric. On the raw Llama-2 7B, the oracle jailbreak is the closest competitor (BLEU 54.4, token F1 68.4, exact 36.5%), but the inversion method still leads by ~5 BLEU and ~9 F1 points while achieving lower exact match (26.6% vs 36.5%). On the RLHF chat model, the gap is dramatic: all jailbreak strings achieve 0% exact match and at most BLEU 14.9, while the inversion method reaches BLEU 58.3 and 23.4% exact match. Few-shot GPT-4 is the weakest baseline, scoring only BLEU 3.0–6.1 and token F1 18.5–25.4, indicating that even a powerful LLM cannot infer the prompt from top-K log-probabilities without dedicated training. The sample inverter (which inverts from a single sampled token rather than the full distribution) achieves reasonable BLEU (25.6) but zero exact recoveries, confirming that the full probability vector contains substantially more information than a single argmax output.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the encoder-decoder inversion paradigm from Morris et al. (2023), which recovered text from embedding vectors, to the setting of next-token probability distributions from autoregressive language models. The conditioning mechanism follows Dumoulin et al. (2018) on feature-wise cross-attention, but the key architectural novelty is 'unrolling' the high-dimensional probability vector into a sequence of pseudo-embeddings to avoid rank collapse. Conceptually, the work bridges computer vision logit inversion (Dosovitskiy & Brox, 2016) and model inversion (Fredrikson et al., 2014) with the LM-as-a-service threat model, but departs from prior model inversion by targeting the specific prompt rather than training data, and from jailbreaking by operating on the probability distribution rather than generated text.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that LM-as-a-service systems leak hidden prompts through next-token probabilities, and that even text-only APIs with logit bias support are vulnerable via a binary-search extraction procedure. A critical practical finding is that RLHF alignment, which neutralizes all tested jailbreak strings on chat models, does not meaningfully protect against distribution-based inversion, shifting the security burden to API design (disabling top-logits, setting temperature to 0). Open questions include the upper bound of recoverable information, whether larger inverter backbones (beyond 222M parameters) close the remaining gap, and whether iterative refinement can improve reconstruction quality. The scale-independence of inversion across GPT-2 sizes (117M–1.5B) suggests the vulnerability is structural rather than an artifact of model capacity.
