## Problem

Existing backdoor-based watermarking methods for DNN IP protection require access to the original training data for injection and verification, which is often unavailable due to privacy, safety, or federated learning constraints. Moreover, these watermarks are fragile against minor model modifications such as fine-tuning, pruning, and model extraction, severely limiting their practical utility. The paper asks whether a watermark can be injected and verified using only a single out-of-distribution image, without any i.i.d. training data, while remaining robust to removal attacks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained CNN (e.g., WRN-16-2 or ResNet-18) and a single publicly available OoD image (e.g., a city scene), which is 'patchified' into 32×32 crops via sequential augmentation (cropping, rotation, shearing, color jittering) to form a surrogate dataset. The pre-trained model's own predictions serve as pseudo-labels for these crops, eliminating the need for ground-truth annotations. The output is a watermarked model whose ownership is verified in a black-box manner by checking whether the model misclassifies trigger-patched OoD verification samples to a secret target label, with a T-test on output logits providing statistical confirmation.

## Method

The core insight is that a single OoD image, under strong augmentation, contains sufficient diverse visual knowledge to manipulate a pre-trained model's decision boundary without i.i.d. data. The surrogate dataset is split into clean and poisoned subsets; the poisoned subset receives a trigger pattern and is assigned a secret target label. Fine-tuning is then performed with an adversarial weight perturbation (AWP) objective that simulates removal attacks during training: the loss is maximized over a layer-wise constrained perturbation ball (‖v_l‖ ≤ γ‖w_l‖) and minimized over the weights, with a KL-divergence term preserving representation similarity between clean and poisoned samples under perturbation. The optimization alternates between a v-step (gradient ascent with projection onto the perturbation ball) and a w-step (gradient descent on the perturbed parameters). Because the watermark is encoded in OoD regions of the representation space, subsequent fine-tuning or pruning on i.i.d. data has minimal impact on the OoD decision boundary, making the watermark substantially harder to erase than one injected via i.i.d. poisoning.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the proposed OoD-based watermark achieves 95.66% OoDWSR (trojan_wm) and 94.23% OoDWSR (trojan_8x8) with less than 3% standard accuracy degradation, using only 20 fine-tuning epochs on a single augmented OoD image. Against the strongest removal attack (RT-AL), OoDWSR retains 57.52% and 24.19% for the two triggers, far exceeding the non-watermarked baseline (~5%). Against model extraction (knockoff), OoDWSR remains above 90% on CIFAR-10 and 76% on GTSRB, with T-test p-values below 0.05 in all settings, confirming statistical ownership verification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external watermarking methods but instead contrasts its OoD-based injection against the traditional ID-based (i.i.d. data poisoning) approach and a clean (non-watermarked) baseline. Under the strongest removal attack (RT-AL) on CIFAR-10, ID poisoning drops to 4.13% and 3.42% IDWSR for trojan_wm and trojan_8x8 respectively, while OoD poisoning retains 57.52% and 24.19% OoDWSR — a 14× and 7× improvement in watermark persistence. The weight perturbation (WP) ablation (Table 6) further shows that adding adversarial weight perturbation during injection boosts post-attack OoDWSR from 19.94% to 57.52% (trojan_wm) and from 12.81% to 24.19% (trojan_8x8) under RT-AL, without degrading standard accuracy. Against model extraction (knockoff), OoDWSR stays above 90% on CIFAR-10 and 76% on GTSRB, while the non-watermarked model's OoDWSR is only 4.87% and 2.20%, yielding p-values below 0.05 in all tested configurations. The key finding is that OoD-injected watermarks are inherently harder to erase than ID-injected ones because fine-tuning on in-distribution data has minimal effect on the out-of-distribution decision boundary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: single-image representation learning (Asano & Saeed, 2023; Asano et al., 2019), backdoor-based DNN watermarking (Adi et al., 2018; Zhang et al., 2018; Goldblum et al., 2022), and adversarial weight perturbation for robust optimization (Wu et al., 2020). It repurposes the single-image augmentation pipeline originally designed for knowledge distillation into a watermark injection mechanism, and adapts the AWP projection framework from robust generalization to the watermark-removal defense setting. The key departure from prior backdoor watermarking work is the elimination of any in-distribution training data requirement, replacing it with a single publicly available OoD image as the secret key, which prior methods (e.g., Li & Wang, 2022) attempted via costly data-free distillation generators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is significant for scenarios where the model owner lacks access to training data—federated learning servers, model marketplaces, and third-party IP protection units—where existing backdoor watermarking methods are inapplicable. The counterintuitive finding that OoD-injected watermarks are substantially more robust than ID-injected ones against removal attacks (e.g., 57.5% vs. 4.1% OoDWSR after RT-AL on CIFAR-10) challenges the assumption that in-distribution data is necessary for durable watermarks. However, all experiments are confined to small-scale benchmarks (CIFAR-10/100, GTSRB) with lightweight architectures (WRN-16-2, ResNet-18), leaving open whether the approach scales to ImageNet-scale models or transformer architectures, and whether the single-image constraint remains sufficient for high-dimensional feature spaces.
