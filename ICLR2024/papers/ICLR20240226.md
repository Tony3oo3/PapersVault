## Problem

Existing SDS-based text-to-3D methods suffer from three interrelated issues: random timestep sampling produces inconsistent and divergent gradients late in training when the NeRF is nearly converged, NeRFs inherently render cloudy geometry and flickering textures, and achieving high-resolution detail typically requires a two-stage pipeline (NeRF → mesh extraction → fine-tuning) that sacrifices the flexibility of implicit representations. HIFA addresses all three within a single-stage optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240226/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (or a single input image for the image-to-3D reconstruction variant) and produces a 3D NeRF parameterized by an MLP with instant-ngp positional encoding, rendered at 512×512 resolution. For image-to-3D, it follows the SyncDreamer protocol of hallucinating 16 auxiliary views and applies an image reconstruction loss only on the given view while using score distillation on the remaining randomly sampled views. The NeRF encodes ray direction via spherical harmonics for view-dependent shading and includes a separate background network.

## Method

HIFA introduces four coupled modifications to the SDS+NeRF pipeline. First, it reformulates the SDS gradient as an explicit latent-residual loss and extends it with an image-space reconstruction term (L_SDS+), providing supervision at both the low-resolution latent and full-resolution pixel levels. Second, it replaces random timestep sampling with a square-root annealing schedule (t = t_max − (t_max − t_min)·√(iter/total_iter)) that starts at high noise for global structure and decays to low noise for fine detail, avoiding the divergence caused by large timesteps on a nearly-converged NeRF. Third, it adds a z-variance regularization that minimizes the variance of importance-sampled z-coordinates along each ray, pushing the density distribution toward a Dirac delta and yielding crisp surfaces without the hollow-geometry artifacts of prior distortion losses. Fourth, it applies a 3-tap kernel smoothing (K=[1,1,1]) to the coarse-stage PDF before refined-stage importance sampling, broadening the sampling window near density peaks to eliminate multi-view flickering at no additional computational cost.

## Results

This paper presents exclusively qualitative/visual evaluations with no quantitative metrics (e.g., FID, PSNR, user studies) reported in the main text. The authors demonstrate visual superiority through rendered multi-view comparisons (Figs. 4, 13–18) and ablation studies (Figs. 5–7), showing that HIFA produces sharper geometry, reduced flickering, and more photo-realistic textures than baselines in a single-stage NeRF optimization. Ablations confirm that the square-root timestep annealing outperforms random, linear, and cosine schedules; the z-variance loss eliminates cloudy artifacts better than the Mip-NeRF 360 distortion loss; and the combined latent+image SDS loss avoids the color bias seen with image-space-only supervision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All comparisons are qualitative (visual renderings and extracted meshes), with no numerical benchmarks. Against DreamFusion, Magic3D, and Fantasia3D (text-to-3D), HIFA shows visibly sharper textures, more natural lighting, and fewer geometric artifacts in single-stage NeRF optimization (Figs. 4, 14–18). Against ProlificDreamer using NeRF-only representation (no mesh fine-tuning), HIFA eliminates the flickering and improper geometry artifacts observed in ProlificDreamer's first stage (Fig. 13), and adding HIFA's z-variance loss, image-space loss, and square-root annealing to ProlificDreamer further improves its output (Figs. 19–20). For image-to-3D reconstruction, HIFA (using SyncDreamer's 16-view hallucination protocol) produces more photo-realistic novel views with reasonable geometric details compared to Zero-1-to-3, Magic123, and SyncDreamer (Fig. 9). The paper does not report any case where a baseline outperforms HIFA, but the absence of quantitative metrics limits the strength of these claims.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) framework introduced by DreamFusion (Poole et al., 2022) for text-to-3D generation with NeRFs, and builds on the NeRF rendering pipeline (Mildenhall et al., 2020). It departs from prior SDS-based methods (DreamFusion, Magic3D, Fantasia3D, ProlificDreamer) in three key ways: (1) computing the distillation loss in both latent and image spaces rather than latent space alone, (2) replacing random timestep sampling with a square-root annealing schedule, and (3) adding z-variance regularization and kernel smoothing to the NeRF rendering to achieve crisp geometry and flicker-free textures in a single-stage optimization, avoiding the two-stage mesh-extraction pipelines used by Magic3D and ProlificDreamer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-stage formulation preserves NeRF's flexibility for detailed appearances (e.g., fur) that are lost when extracting explicit meshes, while the timestep annealing and z-variance techniques are general-purpose improvements applicable to any SDS-based text-to-3D or image-to-3D pipeline. However, the paper's evidence is almost entirely qualitative (visual comparisons without quantitative metrics like FID or user studies), making it difficult to assess the magnitude of improvements. Open questions include whether these techniques transfer to other 3D representations (e.g., 3D Gaussian Splatting), whether the single-stage approach can match two-stage methods at very high resolutions, and whether the per-asset optimization paradigm can be amortized for faster generation.
