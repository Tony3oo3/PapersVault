## Problem

Training Physics-Informed Neural Networks requires multiple types of training points (experimental, PDE collocation, and IC/BC collocation points) with different training dynamics, yet prior work has only addressed the selection of one point type in isolation. This paper tackles the gap of jointly optimizing all training point types while automatically adjusting their proportions during training, leveraging cross-information among point types that had not been considered before.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240577/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a PDE specification (operator N with parameters β), IC/BC conditions, a fixed experimental point budget |X_s|, and a combined collocation budget |X_p|+|X_b|=k, along with a PINN architecture. Unlike prior methods where the user manually fixes the number of points per type, PINNACLE freely allocates the CL budget among PDE and IC/BC point types during training. The output is a set of selected training points in an augmented space Z = X × {s,p,b} that unifies all point types, a trained PINN, and the resulting PDE solution.

## Method

PINNACLE defines an augmented input space Z that encodes all training point types (experimental, PDE collocation, IC/BC collocation) as distinct elements, enabling joint analysis via the combined empirical NTK eigenspectrum whose off-diagonal blocks capture cross-type interactions. The key criterion is the 'convergence degree' α(Z), defined as the RKHS norm of the residual change ΔR(·;Z), which measures how much a candidate point set accelerates convergence across the entire augmented space; the paper proves that maximizing α(Z) yields a lower generalization error bound. In practice, α(Z) is computed via Nystrom approximation of the eNTK eigenfunctions, and two selection variants are proposed: SAMPLING (probability proportional to per-point convergence degree) and K-MEANS++ (diversity-aware batch selection in an embedded feature space). The algorithm alternates between a point selection phase and a training phase, re-computing the eNTK after each training segment to account for its evolution, and retains a portion of previously selected points to maintain stability.

## Results

The paper evaluates PINNACLE (both Sampling and K-Means++ variants) across three problem settings: forward PDE solving (1D Advection, 1D Burger's), inverse PDE parameter recovery (2D Navier-Stokes), and transfer learning with perturbed ICs (1D Advection). In all settings, both PINNACLE variants achieve lower mean prediction error than all baselines (Uniform Random, Hammersley, RAD, RAD-All). For the 1D Advection forward problem, PINNACLE learns all solution stripes while baselines get stuck at only the first few near the ICs. In the inverse Navier-Stokes problem, PINNACLE recovers the correct unknown parameters while other algorithms fail to converge to them.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baselines come from Wu et al. (2023), which provides both the Hammersley sequence sampler (best non-adaptive PDE CL point selection) and the RAD algorithm (best adaptive residual-based PDE CL point selection). The authors also extend RAD to RAD-All, where IC/BC points are adaptively sampled in the same manner as PDE CL points, making it the closest competitor to PINNACLE's joint selection approach. PINNACLE outperforms all four baselines (Uniform Random, Hammersley, RAD, RAD-All) across every problem setting tested. The performance gap is most dramatic in the 1D Advection forward problem, where PINNACLE captures the full multi-stripe solution while baselines only resolve the first few stripes near the initial condition. In the inverse Navier-Stokes problem, PINNACLE is the only method that successfully recovers both unknown PDE parameters, while all baselines fail to converge. For transfer learning with a 5× tighter budget, PINNACLE exploits the pre-trained solution structure to achieve lower error than any baseline. The paper also notes that PINNACLE outperforms baselines even in settings where only one CL point type needs to be selected (e.g., hard-constrained PINNs), indicating the benefit is not solely from cross-type information.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NTK-based analysis of PINN training dynamics (Wang et al., 2021a; 2022c; Gao et al., 2023) by introducing an augmented input space that unifies all training point types (experimental, PDE collocation, IC/BC collocation) into a single kernel, enabling the off-diagonal eNTK blocks—which encode cross-type interactions—to inform point selection. Prior PINN point selection works (Nabian et al., 2021; Wu et al., 2023; Peng et al., 2022) addressed only PDE collocation points, while active learning approaches (Jiang et al., 2022) handled only experimental points; PINNACLE is the first to jointly optimize all types under a shared budget. The Nystrom approximation machinery and eNTK evolution tracking are adapted from kernel methods (Williams & Seeger, 2000; Gittens, 2011) and wide-NN theory (Lee et al., 2019), respectively, but applied here to the PINN-specific composite loss structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing a theoretically grounded criterion (convergence degree) linked to generalization bounds, PINNACLE offers a principled alternative to the ad-hoc heuristics (causal learning, curriculum scheduling, manual loss reweighting) that practitioners currently rely on for PINN training. The automatic discovery of causal learning patterns and IC-to-PDE point proportion shifts validates the NTK cross-information framework and suggests it could generalize to more complex PDE systems. Limitations include testing primarily on 1D and 2D PDEs with relatively simple architectures, reliance on periodic eNTK recomputation that may be costly for high-dimensional problems, and the Nystrom approximation error that grows with pool size. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 3D+ PDEs, deep operator learning, and whether the convergence degree criterion remains informative when the eNTK evolves rapidly (e.g., with narrow networks or aggressive learning rates).
