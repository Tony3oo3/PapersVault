## Problem

Despite the high fidelity of modern diffusion models, synthetic training data still lags behind real data when used to train image classifiers, due to a distributional misalignment between generated and target data. Prior work addresses this gap with heuristic prompt engineering or expensive inversion methods, but lacks a principled theoretical framework explaining what governs synthetic data efficacy. The paper asks: what are the fundamental factors controlling the utility of synthetic training data, and how can they be explicitly optimized?

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a text prompt of the form 'photo of [classname], [BLIP-2 caption], [intra-class CLIP visual guidance]' along with real target images (used only for VAE latent prior extraction and CLIP feature averaging, not for generation). It produces 512×512 synthetic RGB images with class labels, generated via Stable Diffusion 1.5 fine-tuned with LoRA (30 UniPC steps, guidance scale 2.0, image strength 0.75). The synthetic images are then used to train a ResNet-50 classifier from scratch (or pre-trained for small datasets), with the number of synthetic samples matching or exceeding the original real dataset size.

## Method

The paper recasts training data synthesis as a distribution-matching problem, decomposing it into data distribution matching q(x)=p_θ(x) and conditioned class likelihood matching q(y|x)=p_θ(y|x), grounded in a generalization bound that identifies distribution discrepancy and training set cardinality as the two pivotal factors. Three concrete mechanisms implement this framework on Stable Diffusion 1.5: (1) an MMD-based distribution matching loss, derived by showing the standard diffusion training objective is an upper bound on MMD in the VAE latent space, which is added as an explicit regularization term (γ=0.05) during LoRA fine-tuning to tighten the variational bound; (2) intra-class visual guidance, where CLIP image encoder features are averaged per class and concatenated with text embeddings to condition the denoising UNet's cross-attention layers via LoRA, injecting low- and high-level visual priors beyond text prompts; and (3) latent prior initialization, where VAE-encoded latent codes of real samples replace the standard Gaussian prior to guide the reverse diffusion process toward the target distribution. Scaling the synthetic dataset from 1× to 10× the real data size further closes the performance gap, reaching 76.0% top-1 on ImageNet-1K versus 79.6% for real data.

## Results

Training solely with synthetic data equivalent to 1× the original real data size, the method achieves 70.9% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K (ResNet-50), closing the gap to real-data training (79.6%) to under 10%. Scaling to 10× synthetic data pushes accuracy to 76.0%, reducing the gap to 3.6% top-1 and 1.5% top-5. On fine-grained benchmarks, the method achieves 64.3% on CUB, 81.8% on Cars, and 89.2% on PET, all substantially outperforming prior synthesis methods. When augmenting real data with an equal amount of synthetic data, the method improves top-1 accuracy by 2.1% on ImageNette and 1.9% on ImageNet-100 over real-data-only training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all six baselines across every benchmark where they overlap. Against the closest Stable Diffusion-based competitor CiP (Lei et al., 2023), the gains are +10.1% on IN-10, +17.6% on IN-100, and +16.8% on IN-1K top-1. Against FakeIt (Sarıyıldız et al., 2023), the improvements are even more dramatic on fine-grained tasks: +30.6% on CUB, +34.7% on Cars, and +13.3% on PET, reflecting the importance of class-likelihood alignment for hard decision boundaries. Against the larger Imagen model (Azizi et al., 2023), the method achieves +1.7% on IN-1K (70.9% vs. 69.2%) despite using the smaller SDv1.5 backbone. Against earlier generative models (BigGAN, VQ-VAE-2, CDM), the margins on IN-1K range from +7.9% (vs. CDM) to +28.2% (vs. BigGAN). Notably, when scaling to 10× synthetic data, the method surpasses real-data training on three of four OOD benchmarks (ImageNet-Sketch, ImageNet-R, ImageNet-A) even before matching in-distribution accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Stable Diffusion as its generative backbone and on the denoising diffusion probabilistic model framework (Ho et al., 2020) to derive a theoretical equivalence between the standard diffusion training loss and an upper bound on MMD between real and synthetic data distributions. It departs from prior synthetic-data-for-classification work (FakeIt, CiP, Azizi et al.) that relied on heuristic prompt engineering, instead grounding the synthesis pipeline in a principled distribution-matching objective derived from first principles of supervised learning. The latent prior initialization borrows from SDEdit (Meng et al., 2021), and the MMD-based distribution matching draws conceptual inspiration from dataset distillation methods (Zhao & Bilen, 2023), but applies it to the generative model training objective rather than to pixel-level optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical lens (distribution matching with two factors: distribution discrepancy and set cardinality) that explains why prior synthetic-data approaches underperform and guides concrete improvements. The finding that scaled synthetic data outperforms real data on OOD benchmarks (ImageNet-Sketch, -R, -A) before matching in-distribution accuracy is a notable observation suggesting that mild distributional 'noise' in synthetic data may act as a regularizer for generalization. However, the method still requires access to real data for fine-tuning the diffusion model (latent prior, visual guidance features), limiting its applicability in truly data-free settings, and the OOD advantage is demonstrated only with ResNet-50 and SD 1.5, leaving open whether it generalizes across architectures and generative models.
