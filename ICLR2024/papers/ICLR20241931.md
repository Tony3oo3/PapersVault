## Problem

Most multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) algorithms are model-free, suffering from poor sample efficiency due to non-stationarity and exponentially growing joint action spaces. While planning-based model-based RL (e.g., MuZero) achieves superhuman performance in single-agent settings, extending it to multi-agent environments is nontrivial: existing single-agent model designs fail to encode multi-agent biases (nearly-independent agents, parameter sharing), and the joint action space (e.g., ~10^42 in SMAC-27m_vs_30m) makes standard MCTS infeasible.

## Input / Output

Each agent receives a 1D vector observation (stacked over 4 timesteps to handle partial observability) and produces a discrete action; the joint policy is the product of per-agent policies. During centralized training, the model additionally consumes global state information, joint actions, and team reward to compute value and reward predictions, but at execution time each agent only needs its local observation and the shared network parameters (CTDE). The model outputs per-agent policy priors, a global value estimate, and a team reward prediction at each unrolled step.

## Method

MAZero extends MuZero to cooperative multi-agent settings with a centralized-value, individual-dynamic architecture: a shared representation network maps each agent's local observation to an individual latent state, a Transformer-based communication block (3 stacked attention layers) generates cooperative features from all agents' state-action pairs, and shared dynamic/policy networks operate on local information for distributed execution. Two novel techniques address the exponential action space: (1) Optimistic Search Lambda (OS(λ)) replaces the mean Q-value in MCTS selection with a quantile-based optimistic estimate (top (1−ρ) values) weighted by a depth-decay factor λ, exploiting the deterministic model to focus on generalization error rather than environmental stochasticity; (2) Advantage-Weighted Policy Optimization (AWPO) is a new policy loss that reweights behavior cloning by exp(A/α) where A is the optimistic advantage, derived as the cross-entropy to the KL-constrained policy improvement solution. The model is trained end-to-end with reward, value, consistency (EfficientZero-style), and AWPO losses using prioritized replay in an asynchronous pipeline.

## Results

On the SMAC benchmark (8 scenarios: 4 easy, 4 hard), MAZero outperforms all six baselines in win rate at every environment-step checkpoint (Figure 3). In easy tasks, MAZero and MAMBA (the model-based baseline) both exhibit markedly superior sample efficiency over model-free methods, with MAZero showing a more stable training curve. In the hard task 2c_vs_64zg (2 agents, action space up to 70 each), MAZero attains a higher win rate with significantly fewer samples than all other methods. In terms of computational efficiency (Figure 4), MAZero achieves comparable or better win rates than MAMBA at lower cumulative runtime, since it avoids the data-augmentation overhead of Dreamer-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the model-based baseline MAMBA (Egorov & Shpilman, 2022), MAZero wins on all 8 SMAC scenarios in sample efficiency and surpasses MAMBA in overall performance on hard tasks, especially 2c_vs_64zg; MAZero also outperforms MAMBA in cumulative runtime (Figure 4). Against the five model-free baselines—QMIX (Rashid et al., 2020), QPLEX (Wang et al., 2020a), RODE (Wang et al., 2020b), CDS (Li et al., 2021), and MAPPO (Yu et al., 2022)—MAZero achieves substantially higher win rates at equivalent environment steps across all 8 SMAC scenarios, with the gap most pronounced in hard tasks. On the secondary Google Research Football benchmark (Table 11), MAZero reaches 0.619±0.114 score at 2M steps versus CDS (0.426±0.083), RODE (0.290±0.104), and QMIX (0), confirming its sample-efficiency advantage beyond SMAC. In single-agent large-action-space environments (LunarLander with 400 actions, Walker2D with ~280K actions), MAZero with OS(λ)+AWPO outperforms its ablated variant and continuous-control baselines TD3/SAC, validating the generality of the proposed techniques.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAZero extends the MuZero paradigm (learned model + MCTS planning) from single-agent to multi-agent cooperative settings, building on Sampled MuZero's action-sampling framework for large action spaces and EfficientZero's asynchronous pipeline with consistency loss. It departs from prior multi-agent MBRL work (MAMBA, MAMBPO, Tesseract) by adopting MCTS planning rather than data augmentation, and introduces a centralized-value/individual-dynamic architecture with parameter sharing and an attention-based communication block to exploit the nearly-independent and homogeneous biases of multi-agent environments. The two novel techniques—OS(λ) for optimistic value estimation in deterministic models and AWPO for advantage-weighted policy optimization—address the specific challenges of exponential joint action spaces that prior single-agent planning methods did not encounter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first empirically validated extension of the MuZero planning paradigm to cooperative multi-agent environments, offering a principled path toward sample-efficient MARL that avoids the data-augmentation overhead of Dreamer-based methods. The results on SMAC demonstrate clear sample-efficiency gains over model-free baselines and competitive performance with MAMBA at lower computational cost, but the evaluation is limited to a single benchmark family (SMAC) and deterministic environments. Open questions include whether the deterministic-model assumption and OS(λ) generalize to stochastic multi-agent settings, how the approach scales to heterogeneous agents or partially cooperative/competitive scenarios, and whether action-space dimensionality reduction (noted as future work) is necessary for truly large-scale deployments.
