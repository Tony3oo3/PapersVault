## Problem

CycleGAN and other distribution-matching-based unsupervised domain translation methods suffer from a fundamental identifiability failure: the solution space contains measure-preserving automorphisms (MPAs) that can swap cross-domain sample correspondences without altering the data distribution, producing content-misaligned translations. Prior work identified this issue but could not establish identifiability without imposing restrictive structural assumptions (e.g., linearity or optimal-transport form) on the ground-truth translation functions.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes unpaired images from both domains along with discrete auxiliary variable labels (e.g., digit identity for MNIST, shoe type for Shoes, gender/hair color for CelebA-HQ/Bitmoji) that partition each domain into sub-domains; these labels are only needed at training time and can be obtained from available metadata or automatically annotated via CLIP. At test time, only a single source-domain image is required, and the output is a single translated image in the target domain. The auxiliary variable alphabet size I is a design choice that directly affects identifiability guarantees.

## Method

DIMENSION replaces CycleGAN's single marginal distribution matching with I separate conditional distribution matchings, each enforced by its own pair of GAN discriminators over sub-domains defined by auxiliary variable values u_i. The key theoretical insight is that a unified MPA is unlikely to exist when multiple sufficiently diverse conditional distributions must be matched simultaneously, formalized via the Sufficiently Diverse Condition (SDC). Theorem 1 proves that under SDC, the optimal solution of the DIMENSION loss recovers the ground-truth translation functions with probability at least 1−ρ^{I choose 2}, and Theorem 2 extends this to a relaxed condition (r-SDC) yielding an error bound of 2rL under Lipschitz continuity. The loss retains the cycle-consistency term from CycleGAN and is trained with standard alternating GAN optimization, making the implementation a straightforward extension of the CycleGAN training loop.

## Results

On the four translation tasks (Edges→Rotated Shoes, Rotated Shoes→Edges, MNIST→Rotated MNIST, Rotated MNIST→MNIST), DIMENSION achieves the best LPIPS scores across all tasks (0.29, 0.35, 0.11, 0.09), substantially outperforming the next-best baselines (e.g., 0.38 for E→rS, 0.25 for M→rM). For FID (domain fidelity), DIMENSION is the best or second-best on all six domain directions, with particularly strong results on the challenging CelebA-HQ→Bitmoji (32.03) and Bitmoji→CelebA-HQ (20.50) tasks. The synthetic Gaussian-mixture experiments further confirm that DIMENSION eliminates the content misalignment (translation error) that CycleGAN-Loss exhibits, with TE decreasing as the number of auxiliary variables I increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIMENSION decisively wins on LPIPS (content alignment) across all four paired tasks, with margins of 0.09–0.24 over the strongest baselines (e.g., 0.11 vs. 0.25 for MNIST→Rotated MNIST, a 56% relative improvement). On FID (domain fidelity), DIMENSION is best on 4 of 6 domain directions (Edges, Rotated Shoes, CelebA-HQ, Bitmoji) and second-best on the two MNIST directions, where U-GAT-IT edges it out slightly (11.78 vs. 13.95 for M→rM). The most dramatic gap appears on the CelebA-HQ↔Bitmoji task: DIMENSION achieves FID 32.03/20.50, while CycleGAN (with identity) scores 196.52/85.05 and StarGAN-v2 scores 35.44/282.98, reflecting the severe content-misalignment and domain-fidelity failures of vanilla distribution matching. Among the more recent baselines (GP-UNIT, Hneg-SRC, OverLORD, ZeroDIM), DIMENSION consistently outperforms on both LPIPS and FID, confirming that diversified conditional distribution matching addresses the identifiability gap that these methods only partially mitigate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CycleGAN's distribution-matching framework for unsupervised domain translation, but addresses a fundamental theoretical gap: the non-uniqueness of solutions caused by measure-preserving automorphisms (MPA), a problem identified by Galanti et al. (2018) and Moriakov et al. (2020) but left unresolved. Prior identifiability results (Gulrajani & Hashimoto, 2022; de Bézenac et al., 2021) required restrictive structural assumptions on the translation functions (linearity, optimal transport), whereas this work establishes identifiability without such assumptions by matching multiple diverse conditional distributions induced by auxiliary variables. The auxiliary-variable mechanism draws conceptual parallels to nonlinear ICA (Hyvarinen et al., 2019), and the practical use of CLIP for annotation connects to recent disentanglement work (Gabbay et al., 2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous identifiability theory for UDT without structural assumptions on the translation functions, providing a principled explanation for why CycleGAN and variants produce content-misaligned translations and a concrete remedy. The practical method (DIMENSION) is simple to implement—just add conditional GAN losses over auxiliary-variable subsets—and shows clear improvements in content alignment (LPIPS) across multiple tasks. Key limitations include the requirement for auxiliary variables (mitigated by CLIP but not eliminated), the assumption of deterministic bijective ground-truth translations (excluding probabilistic/multi-style settings), and the need for the Sufficiently Diverse Condition to hold. Open questions include whether identifiability can be established without any auxiliary information and how to extend the framework to probabilistic translation mechanisms.
