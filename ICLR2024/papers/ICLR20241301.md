## Problem

Existing MLLMs fail to comprehend demonstrative instructions—sequences of interleaved, highly related images and text that require attending to fine-grained complementary details (e.g., a logo on a product) rather than just primary visual content. The root cause is that Visual Prompt Generators (VPGs) like Q-Former are trained on image-caption pairs, introducing an inductive bias that causes them to extract only the primary visual content sufficient for captioning while neglecting the subtle details essential for multi-image reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241301/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes interleaved image-text sequences as input, where each instruction contains an average of 3.5–3.7 images tightly coupled with surrounding text (e.g., storyboards, textbooks, surveillance pairs, recipe steps). The output is a natural language response, evaluated zero-shot via ROUGE-L for open-ended generation and Accuracy for multiple-choice tasks. A notable constraint is that the images within a single instruction are semantically interdependent—reordering them changes the task meaning—unlike weakly related web-crawled interleaved data used in prior pretraining.

## Method

VPG-C is a lightweight module (~6.3M parameters, 0.09% of the full model) inserted at the intermediate layer (L/2) of a frozen LLM. It first extracts instruction-specific guidance from the LLM's intermediate hidden state via a linear projection, then conditions a new set of learnable queries with this guidance to re-run the same frozen Q-former, recovering residual visual details that the original task-agnostic pass missed. These residual details are reintegrated into the LLM via a skip connection at layer L/2+1. For training, the paper proposes a synthetic discriminative strategy: cross-attention maps identify under-attended regions, SAM segments objects, ChatGPT generates contextually appropriate editing descriptions, and Blended Diffusion produces synthetic image pairs. VPG-C is then fine-tuned on the task of describing subtle differences between original and edited images, requiring no supervised demonstrative instruction data and completing in ~7 hours on a single A100 GPU.

## Results

On the proposed DEMON benchmark (31 tasks, 7 categories), VPG-C achieves the highest zero-shot score across all categories, with particularly large margins in Visual Relation Inference (25.90 vs. 13.85 for the next-best OpenFlamingo) and Multimodal Dialogue (37.50 vs. 33.58 for InstructBLIP). On the MME benchmark, VPG-C attains 1299.24 perception and 321.07 cognition scores, surpassing all instruction-tuned baselines despite using only 6.3M tunable parameters (0.09% of the model) and no supervised demonstrative instruction data. The synthetic discriminative training strategy requires only ~7 hours on a single A100 GPU with 64K synthetic image pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VPG-C outperforms all 8 baselines across every one of the 7 DEMON task categories. The largest absolute gains are over InstructBLIP (the strongest baseline) in Visual Relation Inference (+14.41, 25.90 vs. 11.49) and Multimodal Dialogue (+3.92, 37.50 vs. 33.58), and over LLaVA in Multimodal Dialogue (+29.71, 37.50 vs. 7.79). Notably, VPG-C achieves these gains while tuning only 6.3M parameters (0.09% of the model) via synthetic discriminative training, whereas LLaVA, mPLUG-Owl, and InstructBLIP fine-tune the full LLM or Q-Former on 158K–204M supervised instruction pairs. On the MME benchmark, VPG-C leads in cognition (321.07 vs. Otter's 306.43) and perception (1299.24 vs. BLIP-2's 1293.84), confirming that the completed residual details benefit single-image tasks as well. The paper also shows that even models trained on interleaved vision-language data (OpenFlamingo, Otter, mPLUG-Owl) struggle on demonstrative instructions, suggesting that web-crawled interleaved data (e.g., MMC4) is too weakly related to capture the tight image-text dependencies in demonstrative contexts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the BLIP-2 Q-Former architecture as its visual prompt generator, but identifies a fundamental limitation shared by all VPG-based MLLMs (BLIP-2, InstructBLIP, LLaVA, mPLUG-Owl, Otter): the image-captioning training objective biases VPGs toward primary visual content while neglecting fine-grained details essential for demonstrative instructions. Rather than fine-tuning the VPG on massive instruction data (as InstructBLIP does) or the full LLM (as LLaVA does), VPG-C introduces a lightweight skip-connection module that leverages intermediate LLM representations to guide the frozen Q-Former toward recovering residual visual details. The synthetic discriminative training strategy repurposes Blended Diffusion and SAM to generate targeted editing tasks, avoiding the need for expensive supervised demonstrative instruction data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DEMON benchmark (31 tasks, 7 categories, 20 scenarios) is the first comprehensive evaluation of demonstrative instruction following, filling a clear gap left by single-image benchmarks like MME and LVLM-eHub. The finding that models trained on interleaved web data (mmc4) still fail on demonstrative instructions challenges the assumption that exposure to multi-image sequences is sufficient for deep visual-linguistic association. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video or longer contexts, whether the synthetic training strategy can be made more diverse, and why all current models fail on image-choice questions entirely. The 6.3M-parameter efficiency (0.09% of model) makes this practically attractive for deployment, though the main evidence rests on the authors' own benchmark.
