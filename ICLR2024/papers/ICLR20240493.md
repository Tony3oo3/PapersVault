## Problem

While MLLMs have been extensively benchmarked on high-level visual tasks (captioning, VQA, classification), their abilities on low-level visual perception—such as detecting blur, noise, exposure issues, and aesthetic attributes—remain entirely unexplored. No systematic benchmark exists to measure whether these general-purpose foundation models can perceive, describe, or quantitatively assess low-level image quality in a manner aligned with human judgment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240493/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

All three tasks take a single RGB image (resized to 224×224 or 336×336 depending on the MLLM's visual encoder) paired with a text prompt. For perception, the prompt is a multiple-choice question with shuffled options; for description, it is an open-ended instruction to describe low-level appearance; for assessment, it is a simple 'Rate the quality of the image' prompt. The assessment output is notably non-standard: rather than decoding a token, the method extracts the raw logit values at the score position for the two most frequent response tokens ('good' and 'poor') and applies a softmax to produce a continuous probability in [0,1] as the quality score.

## Method

Q-Bench comprises three complementary evaluation axes. (1) LLVisionQA (2,990 images) tests perception via multiple-choice questions spanning three types (Yes/No, What, How) crossed with four quadrants of low-level concerns (distortion vs. other attributes × global vs. in-context), with correctness judged by a 5-round GPT voting protocol to handle diverse MLLM output formats. (2) LLDescribe (499 images) tests description ability by comparing MLLM outputs against expert-written golden descriptions (~58 words) using single-modal GPT scoring on completeness, preciseness, and relevance over 5 rounds. (3) For quantitative assessment, the paper proposes a softmax-based strategy that replaces the standard argmax decoding with a softmax over the logits of the two anchor tokens ('good' and 'poor'), yielding a continuous quality score that correlates far better with human MOS than direct token outputs—validated across 7 IQA datasets (81,284 images total) spanning in-the-wild, AI-generated, and artificially distorted categories. All 15 open-source MLLMs and GPT-4V are evaluated zero-shot under a unified prompt template.

## Results

On the LLVisionQA perception task (test subset), GPT-4V achieves 73.36% overall accuracy, outperforming the best open-source MLLM InternLM-XComposer-VL (64.35%) by ~9%, while Senior-level Humans reach 81.74%. On the LLDescribe description task, even the best MLLM (InternLM-XComposer-VL, 4.21/6.0 total) only reaches an acceptable standard, with most models scoring around 3.0–3.5 across completeness, precision, and relevance. On the assessment task, InternLM-XComposer-VL achieves 0.541/0.581 average SRCC/PLCC across 7 IQA datasets, outperforming CLIP-ViT-Large-14 (0.354/0.368) by ~20% and NIQE (0.387/0.398) by ~15%, all under zero-shot settings with the proposed softmax-based strategy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among open-source MLLMs, InternLM-XComposer-VL consistently leads across all three tasks: 64.35% on perception, 4.21/6.0 on description (driven by 1.87/2.0 relevance), and 0.541/0.581 average SRCC/PLCC on assessment. GPT-4V outperforms the best open-source model by ~9% on perception (73.36% vs. 64.35%) and is on par with Junior-level Humans (74.31%) but still trails Senior-level Humans (81.74%) by 8%. On the assessment task, all MLLMs using the proposed softmax strategy substantially outperform both NIQE (0.387 avg SRCC) and CLIP-ViT-Large-14 (0.354 avg SRCC), with the gap being largest on generated images (AGIQA-3K: 0.67–0.73 SRCC for top MLLMs vs. 0.562 for NIQE). A notable weakness shared across nearly all MLLMs is degraded performance on distortion-related questions (e.g., LLaVA-v1.5 scores only 47.98% on Distortion vs. 67.30% on Other attributes), and all models exhibit a systematic bias toward answering 'Yes' over 'No' in binary judgments (e.g., mPLUG-Owl: 95.82% on Yes-questions vs. 26.67% on No-questions). The softmax-based strategy is validated against argmax decoding in Table 9, showing consistent improvements (e.g., LLaVA-v1 jumps from 0.038 to 0.462 SRCC on KONiQ-10K), confirming that probability-based scoring is essential for meaningful IQA from MLLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by being the first systematic benchmark targeting low-level visual perception in MLLMs, a dimension largely ignored by existing MLLM evaluation suites (MMBench, SEED-Bench) that focus on high-level semantic understanding. It repurposes established paradigms—multiple-choice VQA, image captioning, and no-reference IQA—but reorients them toward low-level attributes (blur, noise, exposure, color, composition) rather than high-level content. The GPT-assisted evaluation process is adapted from MMBench (Liu et al., 2023c), while the softmax-based score extraction is a novel contribution that bridges MLLM token probabilities with traditional IQA correlation metrics (SRCC/PLCC).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Q-Bench provides the field with the first standardized, multi-faceted evaluation of MLLMs on low-level vision, revealing that even the best open-source models (InternLM-XComposer-VL) remain unstable and imprecise on distortions, and that GPT-4V still trails experienced human raters by ~8%. The softmax-based strategy is a practical, model-agnostic technique that dramatically improves IQA correlation over naive argmax decoding, making it immediately adoptable for future MLLM-based quality evaluators. Open questions include whether targeted fine-tuning on low-level datasets can close the gap to human performance, whether the GPT-assisted evaluation introduces systematic biases, and how these abilities scale with model size or training data.
