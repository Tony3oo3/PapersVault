## Problem

Directly training Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) with batch normalization is hindered by Temporal Covariate Shift (TCS), where the distribution of layer inputs changes across both spatial layers and the temporal dimension due to membrane potential accumulation. Existing SNN BN methods either normalize independently at each time-step (ignoring temporal dependencies) or share statistics across all time-steps (implicitly requiring future information), and none align with the neuron dynamics of membrane potential accumulation in LIF models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240272/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts static RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet) directly encoded as temporal spike streams over T time-steps (typically 2–6), or neuromorphic event streams (DVS-CIFAR10 at 48×48). At the output layer, no spiking mechanism is applied; instead, the temporal average of pre-synaptic inputs across all time-steps is fed into a softmax to produce a class label. The effective mini-batch size for statistics computation is b = N·H·W (batch × spatial dimensions) per channel.

## Method

TAB normalizes pre-synaptic inputs using temporal accumulated batch statistics (μ_{1:t}, σ²_{1:t}) computed over an expanding window [1, t] via a moving-average update, rather than per-time-step or global-over-T statistics. This is motivated by a theoretical derivation: solving the LIF membrane potential ODE yields a closed-form solution containing a temporal accumulation term (a weighted sum of past inputs) that is absent in the standard discrete LIF model; TAB reintroduces this accumulation into the normalization step, establishing a natural link between BN and neuron dynamics. Per-time-step learnable parameters (ω[t], γ[t], β[t]) provide flexibility to capture varying temporal characteristics, and the accumulated statistics are computed online without storing data from all previous time-steps. Training uses surrogate gradients (triangle-shaped) to handle the non-differentiable Heaviside spiking function, with the full network trained end-to-end via backpropagation through time.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, TAB achieves 94.73% top-1 accuracy with ResNet-19 at only 2 time-steps, surpassing TEBN's 94.71% at 6 time-steps. On CIFAR-100, TAB reaches 76.31% at T=2 versus TEBN's 75.86% at the same latency. On DVS-CIFAR10, TAB achieves 76.7% with a 7-layer CNN at T=4, outperforming TEBN's 75.10% at T=10. On ImageNet, TAB attains 67.78% with ResNet-34 at T=4, a 3.49% improvement over TEBN (64.29%) at the same latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAB consistently outperforms all three prior SNN-specific BN methods: BNTT (per-time-step independent BN), tdBN (shared global statistics), and TEBN (shared statistics with per-time-step scaling). On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-19, TAB (94.73% at T=2) exceeds TEBN (94.57% at T=2) by 0.16% and tdBN (92.34% at T=2) by 2.39%. On DVS-CIFAR10, TAB achieves 76.7% at T=4 versus TEBN's 75.10% at T=10, a 1.6% gain with fewer than half the time-steps. Against ANN-to-SNN conversion baselines (RMP, RTS, QCFS, PTL, SlipReLU, SNNC-AP), TAB with only 2–6 time-steps surpasses methods requiring 16–2500 time-steps on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet. Among surrogate-gradient SOTA methods (Dspike, TET, PLIF), TAB with ResNet-19* achieves 96.09% at T=6 on CIFAR-10, beating Dspike (94.25%), TET (94.50%), and TEBN (95.60%) at the same latency. On ImageNet, TAB (67.78% at T=4) exceeds TET (64.79% at T=6) and tdBN (63.72% at T=6) by 3.0% and 4.1% respectively, while also outperforming the best ANN-to-SNN method SlipReLU (66.61% at T=32) by 1.17% with 8× fewer time-steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAB extends the batch normalization lineage (Ioffe & Szegedy, 2015) into the SNN domain by addressing the Temporal Covariate Shift (TCS) that arises from membrane potential accumulation over time. It departs from three prior SNN-specific BN methods: BNTT (per-time-step independent statistics), tdBN (global statistics shared across all T time-steps), and TEBN (global statistics with per-time-step scaling weights). The key conceptual departure is using temporal accumulated statistics over an expanding window [1,t], which the authors justify theoretically by deriving the closed-form solution of the LIF dynamics ODE and showing that the commonly used discrete LIF model omits an accumulation term that TAB effectively reintroduces in the normalization step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TAB provides a principled, theoretically grounded BN method for directly training SNNs that achieves competitive accuracy with fewer time-steps than prior methods, which is practically important for neuromorphic hardware where latency (number of time-steps) directly impacts energy efficiency. The theoretical connection to LIF dynamics gives the method a natural justification beyond empirical tuning, though the paper acknowledges the accumulated statistics are an approximation rather than an exact replication of the ODE-derived term. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other neuron models beyond LIF, whether the approximation gap matters at scale, and whether the per-time-step learnable parameters (ω, γ, β) introduce excessive parameter overhead in very deep or wide networks.
