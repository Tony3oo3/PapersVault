## Problem

Linear attentions reduce Transformer complexity from O(n²d) to O(ndd′) but consistently underperform softmax attention by 4–6 perplexity points on language modeling and fail to recover performance when converting finetuned or pretrained models. The paper identifies two missing properties as the root cause: low-entropy 'spikyness' in attention weight distributions and monotonicity with respect to query-key dot products, both of which prior fixed kernel feature maps (1+ELU, ReLU, Performer, cosFormer) fail to exhibit.

## Input / Output

Hedgehog operates on standard query, key, and value vectors of head dimension d (typically 64) from text sequences or image patches, applying a trainable single-layer MLP feature map φ_mlp: R^d → R^{2d} (with negation mapping) to both queries and keys before computing linear attention via the kernel trick. For conversion settings, the input is a pretrained or finetuned Transformer's frozen weights plus task-specific data; the output is a linear-attention variant that produces next-token predictions, classification scores, or text summaries with O(nd²) complexity instead of O(n²d). The distillation loss consumes softmax attention weight matrices as soft cross-entropy targets during the training phase.

## Method

Hedgehog replaces fixed kernel functions with a trainable single-layer MLP feature map φ_mlp(x) = [exp(Wx+b), exp(−Wx−b)] that maps d-dimensional queries and keys to 2d-dimensional features, preserving O(nd²) complexity. The key training signal is an attention-weight distillation loss: for each head and layer, the linear attention weights computed via the learned feature maps are trained to match the corresponding softmax attention weights via cross-entropy (soft-label) loss, with the base Transformer frozen. This two-stage procedure—first distill attention weights, then unfreeze and finetune the full model—recovers both the spiky (low-entropy) and monotonic properties of softmax attention. The paper further validates that a 2nd-degree Taylor polynomial approximation to exp() recovers these properties but at O(nd³) cost, motivating the learnable MLP as an efficient alternative. For pretrained conversion (e.g., Llama-2 7B), Hedgehog MLPs constitute only 0.495% of model parameters and are combined with LoRA for parameter-efficient finetuning.

## Results

On WikiText-103 language modeling (125M decoder-only), Hedgehog achieves 20.8 PPL, closing 68.6% of the gap to the standard Transformer (18.6 PPL) and outperforming prior linear attentions by up to 6 PPL. On Long Range Arena, Hedgehog achieves the best average accuracy of 59.66% among all subquadratic Transformers. For finetuned-conversion, Hedgehog recovers 99.3% of original BERT-base GLUE performance, and for pretrained-conversion, Hedgehog-GPT-2 achieves a state-of-the-art 16.7 PPL on WikiText-103 among 125M subquadratic models. On Llama-2 7B with LoRA, Hedgehog achieves 47.4 ROUGE-1 versus 2.8 for T2R on SAMSum summarization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hedgehog outperforms all prior linear attention baselines across every evaluation setting. Against 1+ELU (Katharopoulos et al., 2020) and Performer (Choromanski et al., 2020), Hedgehog achieves 100% Associative Recall accuracy versus 17% for both, and 59.2 vs. 28.1/24.7 Matthew's correlation on BERT-FT CoLA. Against T2R (Kasai et al., 2021), Hedgehog recovers 99.3% of GLUE performance versus T2R's 88.9%, achieves 16.7 vs. 19.4 PPL on GPT-2 WikiText-103, and 47.4 vs. 2.8 ROUGE-1 on Llama-2 SAMSum. Against cosFormer (Qin et al., 2022), Hedgehog leads by 4.4 points on LRA average (59.66 vs. 55.23) and 19.3 points on CoLA. Against modern subquadratic sequence models H3 (Fu et al., 2023) and Hyena (Poli et al., 2023), Hedgehog-GPT-2 achieves 16.7 PPL versus 18.5 for both. On LRA, Hedgehog also edges out Skyformer (59.66 vs. 59.50) and Nyströmformer (59.66 vs. 58.95), while the standard softmax Transformer (Vaswani et al., 2017) remains slightly ahead on WikiText-103 (18.6 vs. 20.8 PPL) but is outperformed on LRA average (54.39 vs. 59.66).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the kernel-based linear attention framework established by Katharopoulos et al. (2020), which showed that softmax's exp() can be replaced with a feature map φ(x)ᵀφ(x') to achieve O(nd²) complexity, and the trainable-MLP feature map idea from Kasai et al.'s Transformer-to-RNN (2021). The key departure is a diagnostic: the authors identify two specific properties—low-entropy 'spikiness' and dot-product monotonicity—that prior fixed feature maps (1+ELU, ReLU, Performer, cosFormer) systematically lack, and propose an attention-weight distillation loss that trains the MLP feature maps to explicitly mimic softmax weights rather than relying on a hand-designed kernel. A secondary contribution is showing that 2nd-degree Taylor polynomial approximations to exp() recover both properties and match softmax performance, but at O(nd³) cost, motivating the learnable O(nd²) alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Hedgehog provides a general, drop-in recipe for converting any pretrained Transformer (demonstrated on GPT-2 125M and Llama-2 7B) into a linear-attention variant that retains >99% of original task performance, which is practically important for deploying LLMs with longer contexts or lower inference cost. The distillation loss is model-agnostic and could in principle be applied to other attention variants or modalities. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 70B+ models, how well it interacts with quantization or positional interpolation for context extension, and whether the learned feature maps generalize across architectures rather than just within a single model family. The paper also leaves open the theoretical question of whether truly subquadratic softmax approximation is fundamentally limited (citing SETH-based lower bounds).
