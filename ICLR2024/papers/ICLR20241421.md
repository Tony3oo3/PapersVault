## Problem

Large-scale pre-training datasets are typically curated with ad-hoc heuristics rather than learned methods, and it is unclear how to systematically design filtering networks that produce high-quality training sets. The paper identifies that a model's downstream task performance (e.g., ImageNet accuracy) is uncorrelated with its effectiveness as a data filter, meaning the standard practice of using the best-performing CLIP model as a filter is suboptimal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241421/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The DFN operates pointwise on individual image-text pairs from a large uncurated pool (DataComp's 12.8B CommonPool or a 42B combined pool), computing a cosine similarity between image and text embeddings to produce a binary keep/discard decision. The induced datasets (DFN-2B at ~2B samples, DFN-5B at ~5B samples) are then used to train CLIP models at various scales (ViT-B/16 through ViT-H/14) at 224×224 or 378×378 resolution. Because filtering is pointwise, it parallelizes linearly with pool size and can be integrated directly into training pipelines at minimal marginal cost.

## Method

The DFN is a ViT-B/32 CLIP model trained on HQITP-350M (357M human-verified image-text pairs), initialized from OpenAI's checkpoint, then fine-tuned on COCO, Flickr30k, and ImageNet-1k with OpenAI templates, using weight ensembling to prevent overfitting. The key empirical finding is that data quality for training the filter matters far more than the filter's own task accuracy: even a small fraction of unfiltered data 'poisons' the DFN's filtering ability, while a model with 30% lower ImageNet accuracy can filter as well as OpenAI's CLIP. Standard ML interventions—augmentation, larger batch size, more training steps, and fine-tuning on target domains—improve filtering performance, and fine-tuning the DFN on ImageNet induces datasets with better robustness than directly adding ImageNet to the training set. The resulting DFN filters the 12.8B CommonPool by retaining the top 15% of samples by image-text similarity, producing DFN-2B, which yields state-of-the-art CLIP models (81.4% ImageNet zero-shot for ViT-L/14, 84.4% for ViT-H/14 on DFN-5B).

## Results

On the DataComp xlarge benchmark (12.8B samples seen), a ViT-L/14 trained on DFN-2B achieves 81.4% zero-shot ImageNet accuracy and 0.669 average across 38 evaluation tasks, surpassing DC-1B (79.2%, 0.663) and OpenAI WIT-400M (75.5%, 0.617). Scaling to DFN-5B with a ViT-H/14 at 378×378 resolution yields 84.4% ImageNet zero-shot accuracy and 0.710 average, the best result in its compute class. At the large scale (1.28B samples), DFN-2B with ViT-B/16 reaches 67.8% ImageNet and 0.560 average, outperforming DC-1B (63.1%, 0.537) by 4.7 points on ImageNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DFN-2B outperforms DC-1B at every DataComp scale: at xlarge, 81.4% vs 79.2% ImageNet (+2.2 pts) and 0.669 vs 0.663 average; at large, 67.8% vs 63.1% (+4.7 pts); at medium, 37.1% vs 29.7% (+7.4 pts). Against OpenAI WIT-400M (75.5% IN, 0.617 avg at xlarge), DFN-2B gains +5.9 pts on ImageNet and +0.052 average. DFN-5B (ViT-H/14-378px) achieves 84.4% ImageNet, beating MetaCLIP (80.5%), WebLI (83.1%), and DC-1B+CLIPA-v2 (83.1%) by 3.9, 1.3, and 1.3 points respectively, while also leading on average (0.710 vs 0.692, 0.684, 0.667). In the DFN ablation (Table 1), CLIP ViT-B/32 trained on CC12M (0.289 IN, 0.335 avg) outperforms M3AE ViT-B/16 (0.237 IN, 0.297 avg) and all binary image classifiers, confirming CLIP as the strongest filtering backbone. Notably, DFN-2B with a ViT-L/14 surpasses LAION-2B with a ViT-G/14 (82.2% vs 80.1% IN) using 16× less compute, and a ViT-B/16 on DFN-2B matches OpenAI's ViT-L/14 with 4× less compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the CLIP architecture (Radford et al., 2021) and the CLIP-filtering paradigm already used in LAION and DataComp-1B, where a pre-trained image-text model scores and thresholds candidate pairs. The key departure is treating the filtering model as a first-class trainable object: the authors show that standard ML interventions (augmentation, fine-tuning, weight ensembling, better initialization) improve filtering quality, and that the DFN's own task performance is decoupled from its filtering performance. This reframes dataset construction from a fixed heuristic pipeline (CLIP score + clustering + keyword rules) into a single learnable network that can be iteratively improved with the same tools used for model development.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The released DFN-2B and DFN-5B datasets, combined with a simple and publicly reproducible recipe (train a small CLIP on high-quality data, then filter a large pool), lower the barrier to constructing billion-scale pre-training sets and shift the field's focus from ad-hoc heuristics toward principled, learnable data curation. A key open question the authors flag is the lack of a direct optimization target for dataset quality — alignment is a weak proxy, and it is unclear what the right signal would be for other modalities (speech, video, text). The finding that high-quality data is better spent training the filter than augmenting the final training set also raises questions about optimal allocation of scarce annotated data in the broader ML pipeline.
