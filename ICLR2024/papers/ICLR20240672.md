## Problem

Learning 4D (space-time) neural scene representations from a single traversal of a dynamic driving environment is fundamentally challenging because each 3D point is observed only once, violating NeRF's multi-view consistency assumption. Existing approaches either require costly ground-truth annotations to segment and track dynamic objects or depend on pre-trained optical flow models, limiting scalability. The paper asks whether static-dynamic decomposition and scene flow estimation can both emerge purely from self-supervision without any external flow or segmentation signals.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three frontal camera images (resized to 640×960), first-return LiDAR point clouds, and camera poses from a single driving sequence; optionally, pre-trained DINOv1/DINOv2 features (PCA-reduced from 768 to 64 dimensions) are provided as additional supervision. LiDAR rays are decoupled from pixel rays to handle sensor asynchronization, and per-image appearance embeddings (16-dim) absorb exposure variations. The output is a 4D hash-grid scene representation (≈30M static + 10M dynamic + 10M flow parameters) that renders novel-view RGB, predicts per-point 3D scene flow vectors, decomposes density into static and dynamic components, and optionally produces a 4D semantic feature field for downstream perception tasks.

## Method

EmerNeRF parameterizes three iNGP hash grids: a time-independent static field H_s(x), a time-varying dynamic field H_d(x,t), and a flow field H_v(x,t) that predicts 6-dimensional forward/backward flow vectors. A density regularization term (minimizing E[σ_d]) forces the dynamic branch to emit density only where objects actually exist, enabling self-supervised static-dynamic decomposition. The flow field is used to warp and aggregate dynamic features from t−1, t, and t+1 via a weighted sum; this temporal aggregation indirectly supervises the flow through reconstruction losses, so accurate scene flow emerges without any explicit flow annotation. For semantic features, the paper identifies and removes detrimental positional-embedding (PE) patterns in ViT outputs by learning a globally-shared 2D PE map U that is bilinearly sampled and decoded by a single-layer MLP, added to the volume-rendered PE-free feature to recover the full PE-containing target. All components are trained jointly end-to-end with RGB, sky, shadow, dynamic-density, cycle-consistency, feature, and LiDAR depth (expected-depth + line-of-sight) losses over 25k iterations.

## Results

On the NOTR Dynamic-32 split, EmerNeRF achieves 28.87 PSNR / 0.814 SSIM for full-image scene reconstruction and 26.19 PSNR for dynamic-only reconstruction, outperforming HyperNeRF (25.17 / 22.93) and D²NeRF (24.35 / 21.78) by +3.70 and +4.41 PSNR respectively. For novel view synthesis, EmerNeRF reaches 27.62 PSNR (full) and 24.18 PSNR (dynamic-only), a +2.91 PSNR gain over the best prior method. On the Static-32 split, EmerNeRF scores 29.08 PSNR, surpassing StreetSurf (26.66) by +2.42 PSNR. For scene flow estimation, EmerNeRF achieves 0.014 m EPE3D and 93.92% Acc5, dramatically outperforming NSFP (0.365 m, 51.76%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EmerNeRF decisively outperforms both D²NeRF and HyperNeRF on all dynamic scene metrics: it gains +3.70 PSNR over HyperNeRF and +4.52 PSNR over D²NeRF on full-image reconstruction, with even larger margins on dynamic-only PSNR (+3.26 and +4.41 respectively). The gap is consistent in novel view synthesis, where EmerNeRF leads by +2.91 PSNR over HyperNeRF and +3.45 over D²NeRF on full-image metrics. On static scenes, EmerNeRF surpasses StreetSurf (designed specifically for static outdoor driving) by +2.42 PSNR and iNGP by +4.62 PSNR, demonstrating that its hybrid static-dynamic architecture does not sacrifice static fidelity. For scene flow estimation, EmerNeRF's emergent flow field achieves 0.014 m EPE3D versus NSFP's 0.365 m (a 26× reduction), with Acc5 jumping from 51.76% to 93.92%, all without any explicit flow supervision. Notably, EmerNeRF requires no pre-trained optical flow models or ground-truth object annotations, whereas D²NeRF and HyperNeRF rely on deformation-based assumptions and NSFP requires per-timestep Chamfer loss optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

EmerNeRF extends the line of dynamic NeRF work (HyperNeRF, D²NeRF, SUDS) that partitions 4D scenes into static and dynamic components, but departs fundamentally by making this decomposition fully self-supervised via density regularization rather than relying on ground-truth object masks or pre-trained segmentation. For scene flow, it contrasts with NSFP (which optimizes per-timestep with Chamfer loss) and flow-supervised NeRFs (which use pre-trained optical flow models) by letting flow estimation emerge implicitly from reconstruction losses through a temporal feature aggregation mechanism. The PE pattern removal for ViT features is a novel contribution not addressed in prior feature-lifting NeRF work (Lerf, Decomposing NeRF, CLIP-Fields), which assumed 2D foundation model features could be directly volume-rendered without accounting for transformer positional encoding artifacts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-supervised formulation removes the scalability bottleneck of requiring expensive per-object annotations for dynamic scenes, making 4D driving scene reconstruction applicable to large in-the-wild datasets. The PE pattern discovery is broadly applicable: any pipeline lifting ViT features into 3D/4D space (semantic NeRFs, open-vocabulary detection, robot memory) likely suffers from the same fixed 2D pattern artifacts, and the simple learnable additive prior offers a drop-in fix. Open questions include whether the emergent flow generalizes to more complex motion (deformations, multi-body interactions), whether the method scales to multi-traversal or large-scale city-level scenes, and whether the PE pattern issue persists or evolves as foundation models change architecture.
