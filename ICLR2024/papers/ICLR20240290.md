## Problem

Existing magnitude-based pruning methods for hierarchical ViTs like Swin Transformer suffer from two fundamental flaws: (1) local attention weights within different windows are compared at a global magnitude level, causing locally critical weights to be pruned simply because their absolute values are small relative to other windows, and (2) a single magnitude threshold ignores the distinct weight distributions across hierarchical levels that are essential for extracting coarse-to-fine-grained features. These issues lead to unbalanced and suboptimal pruning outcomes.

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained Swin Transformers (T, S, B) with 224×224 RGB image inputs for ImageNet-1K classification. Crucially, the pruning decision itself is data-independent: it requires only the weight values of the network, not any input images or forward passes, thereby eliminating dependence on the patch merging process. The output is a structurally sparsified model with reduced parameters and FLOPs that still produces class labels, followed by a 30-epoch fine-tuning stage to recover accuracy.

## Method

DIMAP groups network layers into functional modules (QKV-M, PRJ-M, MLP-M, AUX-M) and derives a data-independent weight importance metric by analyzing the Frobenius distortion incurred at the module level. The key theoretical result shows that pruning a single weight in layer l of an L-layer network incurs output distortion bounded by ‖W⁽ˡ⁾ − W̃⁽ˡ⁾‖_F · ∏_{j≠l} ‖W⁽ʲ⁾‖_F, which leads to the importance score Imp(W_pj) = (W_pj)² / Σ_{i≤pj}(W_i)² — a ratio of the squared weight to the cumulative squared norm of all remaining weights in that layer. This metric provides a fair global ranking across modules while preserving the local ranking within each layer, generalizing layer-wise magnitude pruning. The method enables one-shot pruning without sensitivity analysis or extra parameters, and the pruned model is fine-tuned with AdamW (30 epochs, cosine schedule) to recover performance.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, DIMAP achieves a top-5 accuracy drop of only 0.07% when removing 52.5% parameters and 52.7% FLOPs from Swin-B (83.28% top-1, 96.39% top-5). For Swin-S, pruning 33.2% FLOPs and parameters actually yields a 0.03% top-5 accuracy *improvement* over the unpruned model (96.26% vs. 96.23%). At the lightest pruning level (~14%), all three Swin variants (T, S, B) show negligible or even positive accuracy changes, confirming DIMAP's effectiveness across model scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIMAP consistently outperforms existing ViT compression methods at comparable or lower compute budgets. Swin-B-DIMAP3 (83.28%) surpasses VTP (80.70%) by 2.58% with 6.3M fewer parameters and 2.7G fewer FLOPs, and beats UVC (80.60%) by 2.68% with 0.7G fewer FLOPs. Against SViTE (81.56%), Swin-B-DIMAP3 achieves 1.72% higher accuracy while using 10.3M fewer parameters and 3.5G fewer FLOPs. In the mid-range, Swin-S-DIMAP2 (82.99%) edges out X-Pruner (82.00%) by 0.99% with 0.2G fewer FLOPs, and Swin-S-DIMAP3 (82.63%) beats WDPruning (82.41%) and AutoFormer (82.40%) at similar FLOPs. At the lighter end, Swin-T-DIMAP2 (81.11%) matches AdaViT (81.10%) with fewer FLOPs (3.0G vs. 3.9G) and outperforms A-ViT (80.70%) and ViT-Slim (80.60%). The only method that slightly exceeds a DIMAP variant at similar FLOPs is NViT-S (82.19% at 4.2G) vs. Swin-T-DIMAP2 (81.11% at 3.0G), though NViT-S uses a different base architecture (DeiT-S vs. Swin-T).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Frobenius distortion analysis framework from Park et al. (2020) and the norm-based capacity control theory of Neyshabur et al. (2015) to the specific setting of hierarchical vision transformers. It departs from standard magnitude-based pruning (Han et al., 2015) by arguing that absolute weight values are an invalid comparison metric across local attention windows and across hierarchical levels with different patch sizes. The key conceptual departure is elevating the unit of comparison from individual layers or windows to functional modules (QKV-M, PRJ-M, MLP-M), enabling a data-independent weight importance metric derived from distortion minimization rather than raw magnitude.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DIMAP provides a principled, calibration-free pruning method for hierarchical ViTs that avoids the need for input data or sensitivity analysis, making it practical for one-shot compression. However, the authors acknowledge a critical limitation: unstructured weight pruning does not yield realistic GPU acceleration, which significantly limits practical deployment value. Open questions include whether the module-level distortion framework transfers to other hierarchical architectures (e.g., PVT, CoaT), how it performs on dense prediction tasks like detection and segmentation, and whether combining it with structured or token-level pruning can bridge the gap to real speedup.
