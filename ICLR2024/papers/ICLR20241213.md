## Problem

Manually creating open-domain instruction data for LLM fine-tuning is expensive, time-consuming, and produces instructions skewed toward easy or moderate difficulty, with insufficient high-complexity examples. This paper asks whether an LLM itself can automatically mass-produce instructions of varying and increasing complexity to replace human annotation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241213/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial instruction-response dataset (e.g., Alpaca's 52k samples) and iteratively evolves each instruction through 4 rounds, where each round randomly applies one of six evolving operations guided by specific prompts. Failed evolutions are filtered by an elimination step that checks for no information gain, unanswerable instructions, degenerate responses, or prompt leakage. The final fine-tuning dataset merges all rounds, shuffles samples for even difficulty distribution, and is downsampled to match baseline data size (70k) for fair comparison.

## Method

Evol-Instruct uses an LLM (ChatGPT) to iteratively rewrite instructions into more complex versions through two complementary strategies: In-Depth Evolving (five operations — add constraints, deepening, concretizing, increase reasoning steps, complicate input) increases difficulty of a single instruction, while In-Breadth Evolving (mutation) generates a completely new instruction in the same domain to expand topic diversity. A key design choice is constraining each evolution to add only 10–20 words and make the instruction 'a bit harder,' ensuring gradual difficulty progression rather than jumping to extreme complexity that would harm generalization. After evolution, the same LLM generates responses for the evolved instructions, and an Elimination Evolving step discards failed evolutions. The merged multi-round dataset is then used to fine-tune LLaMA 13B (or other bases like Llama-2 70B, Mistral-7B) with standard supervised fine-tuning, demonstrating that the method is agnostic to both the base model and the evolving model.

## Results

WizardLM-13b achieves an average score of 58.96 across nine benchmarks, outperforming all open-source 13B baselines. The most notable gains are in code generation (HumanEval pass@1: 24.0 vs. 12.5 for Vicuna and 9.2 for Alpaca) and math reasoning (GSM8k pass@1: 37.15 vs. 24.34 for Vicuna and 8.35 for Alpaca). On GPT-4-based evaluations, WizardLM scores 75.31 on AlpacaEval and 6.35 on MT-Bench, surpassing Vicuna (70.43 and 6.21) and Alpaca (33.25 and 4.78). WizardLM still trails ChatGPT-3.5 (avg. 76.15) on most benchmarks but narrows the gap substantially on code and math.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WizardLM-13b (avg. 58.96) outperforms all five open-source 13B baselines: Alpaca (43.44), Vicuna (54.60), Baize (51.46), CAMEL (51.29), and Tulu (52.46). The largest margins are on HumanEval, where WizardLM scores 24.0 versus 12.5 (Vicuna), 9.2 (Alpaca), and 21.3 (Tulu), and on GSM8k, where it reaches 37.15 versus 24.34 (Vicuna) and 8.35 (Alpaca). On the GPT-4-judged AlpacaEval, WizardLM (75.31) beats Vicuna (70.43) by ~5 points and Alpaca (33.25) by over 40 points. On the human-evaluated WizardEval, WizardLM (89.1) surpasses Vicuna (86.9) and Alpaca (76.6), with all Kappa scores above 0.6 indicating strong annotator agreement. Against ChatGPT-3.5 (avg. 76.15), WizardLM remains behind on most benchmarks (e.g., MMLU 52.92 vs. 70.0, GSM8k 37.15 vs. 80.8) but narrows the gap on code (24.0 vs. 48.1) and matches or exceeds it on TruthfulQA (50.55 vs. 47.0).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the open-domain instruction-tuning lineage initiated by InstructGPT and continued by Alpaca (Self-Instruct) and Vicuna (ShareGPT). While Alpaca generates instructions in a single Self-Instruct pass from 175 seeds and Vicuna relies on human-shared conversations, Evol-Instruct introduces an iterative evolutionary mechanism that progressively increases instruction complexity through six LLM-prompted operations (five in-depth, one in-breadth). The key conceptual departure is treating instruction data generation as a multi-round optimization process with explicit difficulty control, rather than a one-shot generation or a human annotation pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that LLM-generated instruction data with controlled complexity progression can outperform human-created data (ShareGPT/Vicuna) on multiple benchmarks, challenging the implicit assumption that human annotation is necessary for high-quality instruction tuning. The WizardEval benchmark addresses a real gap in difficulty-balanced evaluation. Open questions include whether the evolution process scales beyond four rounds, whether the gains persist when the base model is already very strong, and whether the reliance on a capable LLM (ChatGPT or Llama-2-70B) for the evolver limits accessibility. The finding that a 7B Mistral model with Evol-Instruct outperforms 13B LLaMA models without it suggests data quality can partially substitute for model scale in the instruction-tuning regime.
