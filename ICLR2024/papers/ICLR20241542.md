## Problem

Premise selection in interactive theorem proving has traditionally relied on symbolic 'hammer' tools like Sledgehammer, which combine handcrafted relevance heuristics, external automated theorem provers, logic projection, and proof reconstruction. These pipelines are complex, require substantial domain-specific engineering for each proof assistant and logic, and exhibit diminishing returns with additional compute. The paper asks whether a generic, data-driven transformer trained contrastively on textual representations can match or exceed these symbolic methods without any of that engineering overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241542/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a proof state (a high-level Isabelle text string describing current goals and established facts) and a database of available premises (typically 30K–50K from a 433K-entry library) as input, and produces a ranked list of the top K_R = 1024 most relevant premises. The two-stage pipeline first retrieves 1024 candidates via cosine similarity in an embedding space (SELECT), then re-ranks them by scoring each (proof_state, premise) pair with a cross-attention model (RERANK). The final ranked list is consumed by Isabelle tactics (smt, metis, auto, etc.) at multiple subset sizes (powers of 2 up to 2^10) to attempt single-step proof closure.

## Method

Magnushammer uses a shared decoder-only transformer backbone (GPT-2 style with rotary position embeddings, 38M or 86M non-embedding parameters) with three specialized linear projection heads: one for proof-state embeddings, one for premise embeddings, and one for the RERANK relevance score. The SELECT stage is trained with a modified InfoNCE loss where each batch contains N proof states, N positive premises, and M = 3N additional mined negatives (facts never used for any proof state in the batch), yielding N−1+M negatives per query. The RERANK stage is trained with binary cross-entropy, using 15 hard negatives per positive pair sampled from the top-1024 SELECT retrievals that are not ground-truth premises. The backbone is pre-trained on the Pile (GitHub + arXiv) for 1M steps, then fine-tuned by alternating SELECT and RERANK update steps on the 4.4M-instance MAPL dataset, with the RERANK negative set recomputed every 1000 steps to track the evolving SELECT model.

## Results

On the PISA benchmark (single-step), Magnushammer achieves a 59.5% proof success rate, substantially outperforming Sledgehammer (38.3%), OpenAI embeddings (36.1%), TF-IDF (31.8%), and BM25 (30.6%). On the multi-step PISA task, combining Magnushammer with Thor raises the state-of-the-art proof rate from 57.0% to 71.0%. On miniF2F (single-step), Magnushammer reaches 34.0% versus Sledgehammer's 10.4% and Sledgehammer+heuristics' 20.9%. The method is also data-efficient: fine-tuning on just 0.1% of the MAPL dataset (~4K samples) already surpasses Sledgehammer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Magnushammer decisively outperforms Sledgehammer, the dominant symbolic premise selection tool, by 21.2 points on PISA single-step (59.5% vs. 38.3%) and by 23.6 points on miniF2F single-step (34.0% vs. 10.4%), and also beats the Sledgehammer+heuristics variant (20.9%) by 13.1 points. Against non-learned retrieval baselines, Magnushammer surpasses BM25 (30.6%) and TF-IDF (31.8%) by roughly 28 points on PISA, and even outperforms the generic OpenAI text-embedding-ada-002 model (36.1%) by 23.4 points, confirming that contrastive fine-tuning on formal proof data yields gains beyond superficial text similarity. In the multi-step setting, replacing Sledgehammer with Magnushammer inside Thor lifts PISA proof rate from 57.0% to 71.0% and miniF2F test rate from 29.9% to 37.3%, while using 4× fewer parameters than competing approaches like DSP (62B Minerva model). On miniF2F multi-step, DSP (39.3%) still edges out Thor+Magnushammer (37.3%), though the authors note DSP relies on orthogonal ideas (informal proof outlines) and hypothesize the gap would close if DSP also used Magnushammer. Thor+Sledgehammer+auto (Wu et al., 35.2% test) is also outperformed by Thor+Magnushammer (37.3%). Overall, Magnushammer wins or ties on every head-to-head comparison except the single miniF2F multi-step cell against DSP.

## Relation to Prior Work

Magnushammer adapts the two-stage SELECT-RERANK architecture from dense information retrieval (Contriever, ColBERT-style re-ranking) to the formal theorem proving domain, replacing the entire symbolic pipeline of Sledgehammer (logic projection → external ATP solving → proof reconstruction) with a purely neural, text-based contrastive retrieval system. The SELECT stage follows the batch-contrastive learning paradigm of HOList and CLIP, with the key addition of mined hard negatives; the RERANK stage mirrors the cross-encoder scoring of Nogueira and Cho (2019). Conceptually, the paper departs from the long line of premise selection work (kernel methods, k-NN, decision trees, and earlier deep models like DeepMath) by showing that a generic transformer trained contrastively on high-level Isabelle text, with no hand-engineered features or logic-specific components, can substantially outperform the most sophisticated symbolic hammer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 21-point improvement over Sledgehammer on PISA (59.5% vs. 38.3%) and the data-efficiency finding (beating Sledgehammer with only 4K training examples) suggest that the field's reliance on complex symbolic pipelines for premise selection may be unnecessary, opening the door to cross-proof-assistant deployment without per-logic engineering. The released MAPL dataset (4.4M instances, 433K premises) is the largest premise selection corpus and the first for Isabelle, providing a critical resource for future work. Key open questions include whether the approach transfers to other proof assistants (Lean, Coq), whether integrating premise selection directly into a generative LM (rather than as a separate retrieval module) yields further gains, and whether the method scales to harder benchmarks beyond PISA and miniF2F.
