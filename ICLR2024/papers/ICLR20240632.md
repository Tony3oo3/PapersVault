## Problem

Optimizing practical non-decomposable objectives like worst-case (min) recall, H-mean, and fairness-constrained recall on deep networks is challenging: theoretical methods (e.g., CSST) require expensive retraining from scratch for each objective and hurt mean recall, while empirical semi-supervised methods (e.g., DASO, ABC) achieve good mean recall but perform suboptimally on these worst-case and constrained metrics. No inexpensive fine-tuning technique existed to steer a pre-trained model toward a desired non-decomposable objective without full retraining.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained model (a CNN feature extractor g and a linear classification layer W) along with labeled data D and unlabeled data D̃ from long-tailed image datasets (CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet-100/1k, STL-10), and produces a fine-tuned classifier that maps images to class labels. The fine-tuning primarily updates the linear layer W (with optional low-learning-rate backbone updates), and the unlabeled set's pseudo-labels are refreshed each cycle. The procedure is designed to be inexpensive, adding only ~2 minutes of compute over the pre-trained baseline.

## Method

SelMix introduces a selective mixup fine-tuning procedure that determines which class pairs (i,j) to mixup based on a gain matrix G, where each entry G_ij is the directional derivative of the target non-decomposable objective ψ along the weight-update direction induced by (i,j) mixup. The gain is approximated tractably via the confusion matrix and per-class feature centroids z_k, avoiding the need to differentiate through the non-smooth argmax in the confusion matrix definition. A scaled softmax of G (with negative entries masked) yields the sampling distribution P_SelMix, which is periodically re-estimated from a validation set to adaptively steer optimization. Training alternates between computing P_SelMix and performing SGD steps on batches formed by sampling class pairs from P_SelMix and drawing individual samples uniformly within those classes, using a Manifold Mixup loss in feature space. Theoretical analysis provides a convergence guarantee (O(1/t) rate) under mild assumptions and an online-learning regret bound showing P_SelMix outperforms any fixed (non-adaptive) mixup policy.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 LT (semi-supervised, ρ=100), SelMix achieves 79.1% Min Recall, 85.4% Mean Recall, and 85.1% H-mean, outperforming the best empirical baseline ABC (74.1% Min Recall, 85.1% Mean Recall) and the theoretical baseline CSST (71.7% Min Recall, 81.1% Mean Recall). On CIFAR-100 LT (ρ=10), SelMix reaches 57.8% Min Head-Tail Recall versus 48.4% for CSST and 40.9% for DASO, while maintaining competitive mean recall (59.8%). For coverage-constrained objectives, SelMix is one of only two methods (alongside CSST) that satisfies the minimum coverage constraint, achieving 0.095 min coverage on CIFAR-10 versus 0.086 for ABC and 0.056 for FixMatch(LA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SelMix outperforms all empirical baselines (DARP, CReST, CReST+, ABC, CoSSL, DASO) on Min Recall by 5–13 points on CIFAR-10 (79.1% vs. best 74.1% for ABC) and by 9–17 points on CIFAR-100 Min HT Recall (57.8% vs. best 46.4% for ABC). Against the theoretical baseline CSST, SelMix gains 7.4 points in Min Recall on CIFAR-10 (79.1% vs. 71.7%) and 9.4 points in Min HT Recall on CIFAR-100 (57.8% vs. 48.4%), while also substantially improving H-mean (85.1% vs. 76.9% on CIFAR-10). On mean recall, SelMix matches or slightly exceeds the best empirical methods (85.4% vs. 85.1% for ABC on CIFAR-10; 59.8% vs. 60.6% for DASO on CIFAR-100, a negligible gap). For coverage-constrained objectives, SelMix is the only method that simultaneously satisfies the minimum coverage constraint and achieves the highest mean recall (85.7% on CIFAR-10, 59.6% on CIFAR-100), whereas most empirical baselines (DARP, CReST, ABC, DASO) fail to meet the coverage threshold. In the supervised setting (Table 3), SelMix fine-tuning of MiSLAS improves Min Recall from 72.5% to 79.2% on CIFAR-10 and from 15.2% to 41.3% on CIFAR-100 compared to MiSLAS Stage-2, and on ImageNet-1k LT it lifts Min HT Recall from 29.7% to 45.1%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two prior research threads: (1) theoretical frameworks for optimizing non-decomposable objectives like min-recall and H-mean (Narasimhan & Menon 2021; CSST/Rangwani et al. 2022), which require expensive from-scratch training, and (2) empirical semi-supervised long-tail methods (DASO, ABC, CReST, CoSSL) that improve mean accuracy but underperform on worst-case and fairness metrics. SelMix departs from both by repurposing Manifold Mixup (Verma et al. 2019) with a class-pair sampling distribution derived from a confusion-matrix-based gain signal, turning mixup from a uniform regularization into a targeted fine-tuning mechanism. The theoretical grounding draws on online learning (Hedge/EXP3) to justify the adaptive softmax-of-gains policy, and the pre-training backbone uses FixMatch with logit adjustment (Menon et al. 2020) for debiased pseudo-labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that SelMix offers a ~2-minute fine-tuning step that can substantially improve worst-case recall and fairness-constrained objectives on already-deployed models, avoiding the cost of retraining from scratch that theoretical methods demand. This makes non-decomposable objective optimization accessible in real-world pipelines where models are already trained and only need metric-specific calibration. Open questions include whether the small-variance assumption (Theorem 4.1) holds for very deep or poorly pre-trained backbones, whether the approach extends to multi-label or hierarchical classification, and how the gain-matrix computation scales beyond ~1000 classes. The paper also notes that the method is complementary to other mixup variants (CutMix, PuzzleMix), suggesting a broader composability that remains unexplored.
