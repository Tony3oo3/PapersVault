## Problem

Existing neural field methods for large-scale 3D scene reconstruction train a separate network per scene, requiring hundreds of views and hours of optimization, while generalizable alternatives rely on complex multi-view fusion networks that limit scalability and flexibility. This paper asks whether a generalizable scene prior can be learned from single-view RGB-D inputs alone, enabling fast reconstruction of new scenes with far fewer views and no learnable fusion module.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240119/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes posed RGB-D images (RGB + depth map) with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics; depth is backprojected into 3D keypoints via Farthest Point Sampling (512 points per frame). It outputs a signed distance function and a radiance field that together yield a textured 3D mesh and novel-view RGB renderings. A distinctive aspect is that multi-view fusion is performed by simply merging per-frame keypoint fields in volumetric space—no learnable fusion network is required—making the pipeline flexible to arbitrary frame counts and preprocessing choices.

## Method

NFPs uses a two-stage generalizable prior: (i) a geometric network with a PointConv encoder over KNN neighborhoods and an MLP decoder maps depth-derived keypoints to SDF values, trained with depth rendering loss, approximated SDF loss, and eikonal regularization; (ii) a texture network with a UNet/ResNet34 encoder splats RGB features onto the same keypoints, and an MLP decoder (conditioned on the SDF gradient as surface normal) predicts view-dependent radiance via volumetric rendering. The continuous surface representation replaces dense voxel grids with distance-weighted spatial interpolation over discrete keypoints, augmented by a distance-aware MLP to suppress contributions from points far from the surface. For per-scene fine-tuning, the pretrained prior initializes a compact set of ~40,000 learnable keypoints (vs. >4M voxels in Go-surf), and a prior-guided pruning schedule (Algorithm 1) progressively removes grids whose predicted SDF exceeds a decaying threshold, concentrating optimization near surfaces.

## Results

On ScanNet surface reconstruction, NFP with per-scene optimization achieves an F-score of 0.954 (Precision 0.947, Recall 0.962) in just 15 minutes, outperforming Go-surf (F-score 0.950, 35 min) and Neural-RGBD (F-score 0.925, 240 min). The feed-forward prior alone (no per-scene optimization) reaches F-score 0.737 in under 5 minutes, matching RGB-based methods that require 400+ frames and hours of optimization. For novel view synthesis, NFP achieves the best PSNR of 26.88 dB, surpassing NeRFusion (26.49), NeRFingMVS (26.37), and Go-surf (25.47), while also outperforming Go-surf by a large margin on LPIPS (0.244 vs. 0.420).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On surface reconstruction (Table 1), NFP with per-scene optimization (F-score 0.954) outperforms Go-surf (0.950) and Neural-RGBD (0.925) on all metrics, and substantially surpasses MonoSDF* (0.926) and ManhattanSDF* (0.907), while being 4–48× faster (15 min vs. 35–720 min). Against RGB-only methods (ManhattanSDF 0.602, MonoSDF 0.747, NeuRIS 0.696, FastMono 0.710, HelixSurf 0.755), NFP leads by 0.20–0.35 F-score. The feed-forward prior alone (F-score 0.737, no optimization) already matches or exceeds most RGB-based baselines in under 5 minutes. For novel view synthesis (Table 3), NFP achieves the best PSNR (26.88 dB) and beats Go-surf by 1.41 dB PSNR and 0.176 LPIPS, though NeRFusion retains a slight edge on SSIM (0.915 vs. 0.909) and LPIPS (0.209 vs. 0.244). In the MVS comparison (Table 7), NFP achieves F-score 0.875 and Normal Consistency 0.878, outperforming MonoNeuralFusion (0.677 / 0.842) and all classical MVS methods (FastMVSNet, PointMVSNet, Atlas, GPMVS, DeepVideoMVS, NeuralRecon) by large margins on Precision (0.917) and Recall (0.889).

## Relation to Prior Work

NFP builds on the SDF-based volume rendering framework of NeuS and Yariv et al. for decoding geometry and texture, but departs fundamentally from both per-scene NeRF optimization (which trains a separate network per scene) and multi-view generalizable NeRF methods (IBRNet, NeRFusion, MVSNeRF) that rely on intricate learnable fusion networks to aggregate multi-view inputs. The key architectural departure is a single-view, feed-forward scene prior that maps each RGB-D frame independently to a local SDF and radiance field, with full scene reconstruction achieved by directly merging per-view fields in volumetric space without any fusion module. The two-stage training paradigm (geometry prior first, then texture prior) and the use of a compact keypoint/surface representation instead of dense voxel grids are the paper's core methodological contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: NFP reduces per-scene reconstruction time from hours (640+ min for ManhattanSDF) to 15 minutes, and the feed-forward prior alone produces usable geometry in under 5 minutes, enabling rapid scene capture on consumer devices. The surface/keypoint representation (≈40K learnable points vs. 4M+ voxels in Go-surf) offers a 100× reduction in optimization complexity, which is critical for scaling to larger scenes. A key limitation is the requirement for posed RGB-D input; the authors acknowledge that adapting to pure RGB via SfM-generated depth remains unexplored. Open questions include whether the single-view prior generalizes beyond indoor scenes and whether the direct-fusion strategy degrades with very large view counts or significant camera motion.
