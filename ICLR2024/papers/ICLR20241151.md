## Problem

Dataset distillation formulated as bilevel optimization via backpropagation through time (BPTT) suffers from severe gradient instability (due to products of Hessian matrices with negative eigenvalues in the non-convex inner loop), high memory demands, and long-term dependency challenges. Prior approaches either replace the inner loop with surrogates (NTK, NNGP) that are restricted to MSE loss and wider networks, or modify the outer-loop objective with proxy metrics that yield subpar performance. The paper asks whether a simple, direct bilevel optimization method can achieve state-of-the-art without such approximations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard image classification dataset (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100 and CUB200, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet and ImageNet-1K) and produces a small synthetic dataset of 1–50 images per class, initialized from a Gaussian distribution rather than real images. Both the pixel values and soft labels are jointly optimized. When using the Boost-DD extension, the output is a nested dataset where any prefix of the blocks forms a high-performing subset at a smaller budget, enabling a single versatile dataset across multiple IPC settings.

## Method

RaT-BPTT randomly places a fixed-size truncated backpropagation window (M steps) along the inner-loop unrolling chain (T steps), so the meta-gradient is a random subsample of the full BPTT gradient that covers the entire training trajectory while limiting Hessian products to fewer than M factors. This simultaneously stabilizes gradients (by avoiding long Hessian chains with negative eigenvalues) and preserves long-range dependencies (by randomly sampling across the full trajectory), yielding faster optimization and higher accuracy than either full BPTT or fixed-window T-BPTT. The method uses the Higher package for meta-gradient computation, Adam for both inner and outer loops, and standard ConvNet architectures (3-layer for 32×32, 4-layer for 64×64). A complementary Boost-DD algorithm constructs the distilled dataset iteratively in blocks, applying a reduced learning rate (β·α) to previously distilled blocks when adding new ones, producing nested datasets with minimal inter-correlation and near-optimal subset performance across budgets.

## Results

RaT-BPTT achieves state-of-the-art test accuracy across most benchmarks without any inner-loop approximations. On CIFAR-10 (IPC10/IPC50), it reaches 69.4%/75.3%, surpassing the best narrow-network baselines (RCIG: 66.8%/—, FRePO: 63.5%/70.7%). On CIFAR-100 (IPC10/IPC50), it achieves 47.5%/50.6%, outperforming RCIG (35.5%/—) and FRePO (41.3%/41.5%) on narrow networks. On ImageNet-1K (IPC1/IPC2), it reaches 10.77%/12.05%, substantially exceeding TESLA (7.7%/10.5%) and DM (1.5%/1.7%). When evaluated on wider networks (transfer setting), performance further improves, e.g., 71.0% on CIFAR-10 IPC10 and 51.0% on CIFAR-100 IPC50.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RaT-BPTT outperforms all narrow-network baselines on CIFAR-10 IPC10 (69.4% vs. RCIG 66.8%, FRePO 63.5%, MTT 65.3%) and IPC50 (75.3% vs. FRePO 70.7%, FTD 73.8%, TESLA 72.6%), and on CIFAR-100 IPC10 (47.5% vs. FRePO 41.3%, FTD 43.4%, TESLA 41.7%). On CUB200, it achieves 13.8% (IPC1) and 17.7% (IPC10), beating FRePO narrow (16.9% IPC1) and all modified-objective methods (MTT 2.2%, DSA 1.3%). On ImageNet-1K, it substantially exceeds TESLA (10.77% vs. 7.7% at IPC1; 12.05% vs. 10.5% at IPC2) and DM (1.5%/1.7%). The closest competitor is RCIG, which leads on Tiny-ImageNet IPC1 (25.6% wide vs. 22.1%) and CIFAR-10 IPC1 (53.9% wide vs. 53.2%), but drops to 22.4% and 49.6% respectively when re-evaluated on narrow networks, where RaT-BPTT wins. The paper's key advantage is that RaT-BPTT requires no inner-loop surrogate (NTK, NNGP, random features) and works equally well on narrow and wide architectures, whereas surrogate-based methods (KIP, RFAD, FRePO, RCIG) are biased toward wider networks and degrade 3–4% on practical narrow ConvNets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revisits the foundational backpropagation-through-time (BPTT) approach to dataset distillation (Wang et al., 2018) and refines it with a randomized truncated window (RaT-BPTT), drawing on the truncated BPTT framework from Shaban et al. (2019) and the original T-BPTT from Williams & Peng (1990). It deliberately departs from the two dominant prior strategies—inner-loop surrogates (NTK, NNGP, random features) and modified outer-loop objectives (gradient matching, trajectory matching)—by arguing that neither approximations nor proxy metrics are necessary; a careful randomization of the truncation window in direct bilevel optimization suffices. The Boost-DD component extends the task by addressing intercorrelation in jointly-optimized distilled data, a structural issue identified in prior work (Nguyen et al., 2021b) but not previously resolved.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is that RaT-BPTT provides a simpler, architecture-agnostic distillation method that works for both narrow and wide networks without requiring model pools or MSE-loss restrictions, making it broadly applicable. However, GPU memory consumption remains substantial due to multi-step unrolling, and the intercorrelation problem is only partially addressed by Boost-DD (residual correlations persist between blocks). Open questions include whether the hardness tail of the data distribution can be better distilled at scale, whether the random window design can be further optimized (e.g., non-uniform sampling), and whether the approach extends efficiently to larger models via checkpointing or reversible computation.
