## Problem

Structured weight matrices (low-rank, block-sparse, block-low-rank) are widely used to reduce DNN complexity, but each format is defined disjointedly with no unified representation, and their structural parameters (block widths and locations) live in non-differentiable discrete spaces. This makes it impossible to systematically learn the optimal per-layer structure via gradient descent, forcing practitioners to rely on hand-crafted designs that may be suboptimal for a given network.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard DNN weight matrices (square n×n or rectangular m×n) from architectures like ViT-Base, MLP-Mixer, GPT-2, or ResNet-18, and replaces them with K rank-1 blocks whose widths, locations, and content vectors are jointly learned. The output is a set of GBLR matrices parameterized by 2K structural scalars (row/column widths and locations) and 2Kn content vectors, with the total FLOPs bounded by 2Ks where s is the average block width. At inference, only cropped content parameters are stored, so the Gaudi mask introduces zero overhead.

## Method

The paper introduces the Generalized Block-Low-Rank (GBLR) format, which represents a matrix as a sum of K overlapping rank-1 blocks at arbitrary cyclic positions, and proves that low-rank, block-sparse, and block-low-rank matrices are all special cases (Theorem 1). To make the discrete boxcar mask differentiable, the authors parameterize it in the frequency domain via a Gaussian-Dirichlet (Gaudi) function — the product of a Gaussian window and the Dirichlet kernel — whose derivatives with respect to width and location are bounded almost everywhere, including at zero width (Theorem 3). Training uses proximal gradient descent with an L1 penalty on the width vector to enforce a global FLOPs budget, combined with AdamW for the content parameters and a progressive increase of the Gaussian smoothing parameter σ from 1 to 100 to transition from loose to tight masks. The learned structures are layer-specific and non-uniform: for example, in ViT-Base, Value layers receive ~3× the FLOPs of Query layers, and blocks concentrate in regions corresponding to more important attention heads.

## Results

On ImageNet (ViT-Base trained from scratch), Gaudi-GBLR achieves 78.51% top-1 accuracy at 5.65 GFLOPs, matching the dense baseline (78.57%) while reducing FLOPs by 67%. On WikiText103 (GPT-2 fine-tuning), Gaudi-GBLR achieves 19.24 perplexity at 43.7% relative FLOPs, outperforming even the dense baseline (19.36) at 100% FLOPs. On ResNet18 (ImageNet), GBLR reaches 69.31% accuracy at 1.01 GFLOPs, surpassing the ALDS low-rank baseline (69.22% at 1.03 GFLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gaudi-GBLR consistently outperforms all three hand-designed structured matrix baselines across tasks. Against Monarch (Dao et al., 2022, BLR format), Gaudi-GBLR reduces WikiText103 perplexity from 20.56 to 19.24 at comparable FLOPs (43.7% vs 43.75%), and achieves higher accuracy at every FLOPs level on ImageNet fine-tuning and CIFAR-10/100 training from scratch. Against Pixelfly (Chen et al., 2022, BSP-LR format), Gaudi-GBLR shows superior accuracy-FLOPs trade-offs on both ImageNet and CIFAR benchmarks, with the gap widening at lower FLOPs budgets. Against ALDS (Liebenwein et al., 2021, layer-wise low-rank), GBLR achieves 69.31% vs 69.22% top-1 accuracy on ResNet18/ImageNet at slightly lower GFLOPs (1.01 vs 1.03), demonstrating that the learned generalized structure captures more expressiveness than a fixed low-rank constraint. Notably, Gaudi-GBLR even surpasses the unstructured dense GPT-2 baseline in perplexity (19.24 vs 19.36) while using only 43.7% of the FLOPs, suggesting the learned structure acts as an implicit regularizer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the previously disjoint families of structured matrices for DNNs—low-rank (LR), block-sparse (BSP), and block-low-rank (BLR)—under a single Generalized Block-Low-Rank (GBLR) format, showing each is a special case. It departs from prior hand-crafted approaches (Monarch for BLR, Pixelfly for BSP-LR, SVD-based low-rank) by making the structural parameters (block widths and locations) differentiable via a frequency-domain Gaussian-Dirichlet (Gaudi) kernel, enabling end-to-end learning by proximal gradient descent. The Gaudi mask parameterization is conceptually related to DiffStride's differentiable boxcar but differs in design: here the width and location inherit exact gradients in the frequency domain rather than relying on an approximated time-domain mask.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled, single-loop alternative to iterative SVD-based or multi-loop layer-wise compression methods, and the learned structures (neither BSP nor BLR) suggest the optimal matrix structure for a given layer is richer than any single hand-designed format. Practical impact is demonstrated on ViT-Base, MLP-Mixer, GPT-2, and ResNet-18, with ~30% FLOPs at near-dense accuracy and a smaller generalization gap. Open questions include scalability to much larger models (70B+), whether the learned block patterns have interpretable semantic meaning beyond multi-head attention allocation, and how the method interacts with other compression axes (quantization, pruning) in a joint pipeline.
