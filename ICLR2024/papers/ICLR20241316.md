## Problem

While prior work demonstrated that unlabeled in-the-wild data can improve OOD detection empirically, there was no formal understanding of *how* or *why* unlabeled data provably helps. The core difficulty is that wild data is a heterogeneous mixture of ID and OOD samples under a Huber contamination model, with no clean OOD labels available, making it non-trivial to design and theoretically justify effective OOD learning algorithms.

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled ID images (e.g., CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32) and unlabeled wild images that form a mixture P_wild = (1−π)P_in + πP_out (default π=0.1). Gradients are computed using penultimate-layer weights of a Wide ResNet-40-2 classifier trained on the ID data. The output is a scalar OOD score g_θ(x) from a binary classifier, thresholded at 0 to produce an ID-vs-OOD decision, evaluated via FPR95 and AUROC on held-out OOD test sets.

## Method

SAL (Separate And Learn) is a two-stage framework. In the filtering stage, it computes per-sample gradients of the ID classifier on all wild data, subtracts a reference gradient (the mean ID gradient), forms a gradient matrix, and performs SVD to extract the top singular vector v; each wild sample receives a filtering score τ_i = ⟨∇ℓ_i − ∇̄, v⟩², and samples exceeding a threshold T are flagged as candidate outliers. The theoretical justification (Theorem 1) shows that under a (γ,ζ)-discrepancy condition between P_wild and P_in, OOD gradients project onto v with provably larger magnitude than ID gradients. In the classification stage, a binary OOD classifier g_θ is trained with sigmoid loss to assign positive scores to ID data and negative scores to the filtered candidate outliers, jointly with the ID classification loss. Theorem 3 provides a generalization bound showing the learned OOD classifier's risk approaches the optimal OOD risk when data sizes are sufficient and the optimal ID risk is small.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 as ID (Wide ResNet-40-2, 100 epochs, π=0.1), SAL achieves an average FPR95 of 1.88% and AUROC of 99.51% across five OOD test sets (SVHN, PLACES365, LSUN-C, LSUN-RESIZE, TEXTURES), with ID accuracy of 73.71%. The best per-dataset FPR95 is 0.02% on LSUN-RESIZE and 0.06% on LSUN-C, while the hardest OOD set (TEXTURES) yields FPR95=5.73% and AUROC=98.65%. Compared to the closest wild-data baseline WOODS, SAL reduces average FPR95 from 7.80% to 1.88% (a 5.92-point improvement) while maintaining comparable ID accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAL outperforms all 11 baselines on average FPR95 and AUROC. Against the closest wild-data method WOODS (Katz-Samuels et al., 2022), SAL reduces average FPR95 from 7.80% to 1.88% (−5.92 pts) and improves AUROC from 97.43 to 99.51, with the largest gap on PLACES365 (FPR95: 3.53 vs 29.58) and TEXTURES (5.73 vs 9.12). Against the best ID-only method KNN+ (Sun et al., 2022), SAL achieves a 44.52-point FPR95 reduction (1.88 vs 46.40), demonstrating the substantial benefit of leveraging unlabeled wild data. Among other wild-data methods, SAL also dominates OE (FPR95 1.88 vs 18.89) and Energy w/ OE (1.88 vs 19.72). SAL's ID accuracy (73.71%) is slightly below WOODS (75.22%) and KNN+ (73.78%), which the authors attribute to using only 25,000 labeled samples (the other 25,000 are reserved for constructing the wild mixture) versus the full 50,000 used by ID-only baselines. An ablation replacing SAL's SVD-based filtering score with GradNorm (Huang et al., 2021) degrades average FPR95 from 1.88% to 23.84%, confirming the importance of the proposed filtering mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on WOODS (Katz-Samuels et al., 2022), which first formalized OOD detection with unlabeled wild data under a Huber contamination model but relied on constrained optimization without an explicit outlier-filtering mechanism. SAL departs from WOODS by introducing a two-stage pipeline: a gradient-matrix SVD-based filtering step to separate candidate outliers, followed by binary OOD classifier training. The filtering score is conceptually related to GradNorm (Huang et al., 2021) but replaces the raw gradient norm with a projection onto the top singular vector, and the theoretical analysis draws on robust statistics (outlier-robust estimation, discrepancy-based separation) rather than the positive-unlabeled learning literature, which the authors explicitly distinguish from their setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous theoretical guarantees explaining *how* and *when* unlabeled wild data provably improves OOD detection, filling a gap the authors identify as absent from prior work. The near-perfect FPR95 (1.88%) on CIFAR-100 without requiring clean auxiliary OOD data is practically significant for deployment scenarios where only in-the-wild data is available. However, the theoretical conditions (sufficient sample sizes, small optimal ID risk, non-trivial distribution discrepancy) may be restrictive, and the empirical validation is limited to CIFAR-scale datasets with a single architecture and default mixture ratio π=0.1. Open questions include whether the SVD-based filtering generalizes to more heterogeneous or high-dimensional OOD distributions, whether the discrepancy condition ζ holds in realistic deployment settings, and whether the approach scales to ImageNet-level benchmarks.
