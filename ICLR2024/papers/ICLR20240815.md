## Problem

Existing local windowed attention mechanisms in transformers (e.g., Swin, Longformer) constrain all attention heads within a block to the same window size, capturing only local information at the layer level and requiring explicit shifting or pooling strategies to recover global context. This paper asks whether a single attention module can simultaneously model multiple local and global context scales within every transformer block, and whether doing so in a Masked Autoencoder decoder yields better general-purpose audio representations.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 200×80 log-scaled mel spectrograms (2 seconds of 16 kHz audio, 80 mel bins in 50–8000 Hz) partitioned into non-overlapping patches (default 4×16, yielding 250 patches). During pre-training, 80% of patches are randomly masked and the decoder reconstructs the masked patches via MSE; during downstream evaluation, the decoder is discarded and fixed-length scene embeddings are produced by mean-pooling encoder features over 2-second chunks, yielding a duration-independent vector for a shallow MLP classifier.

## Method

The core contribution is the Multi-Window Multi-Head Attention (MW-MHA) module, in which each of h attention heads computes self-attention over non-overlapping windows of a distinct size (e.g., [2, 5, 10, 25, 50, 125, 250, 250] for 250 patches), so that a single block simultaneously captures information at multiple local and global granularities. MW-MHA is placed exclusively in the decoder (not the encoder), because the 80% masking ratio means the encoder processes only 20% of patches, making windowed attention there largely redundant. The window sizes are chosen as all non-unary factors of the total patch count plus two global heads, a rule that scales automatically with patch size. PWCCA analysis reveals that decoder heads sharing the same window size across layers learn correlated feature representations, producing a decoupled feature hierarchy where each block independently captures local and global information. This design yields consistent improvements over standard MAEs across 10 downstream tasks, better scaling with patch size, encoder/decoder complexity, and reduced pre-training data.

## Results

On the HEAR benchmark subset of 10 diverse audio tasks, the largest proposed model MW-MAE-L-4x16-8l achieves a normalized overall score of 92.6±0.2, outperforming all compared methods including BEATs-iter3 (85.7±0.3), HuBERT-large (73.4±0.3), and WavLM-large (64.0±0.2). At the base configuration, MW-MAE-B-4x16-4l (89.2±0.2) outperforms the directly comparable MAE-B-4x16-4l (88.1±0.2) and AudioMAE-B-4x16-4l (86.1±0.3). The method also demonstrates superior scaling: a 90% reduction in pre-training data causes only a 13.5% performance drop for MW-MAE versus 28.17% for standard MAE, and MW-MAE with a 2-layer decoder (88.3±0.2) matches MAE with a 4-layer decoder.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MW-MAE-L-4x16-8l (92.6±0.2) outperforms all six compared baselines by substantial margins: W2V2-large (74.0±0.4, +18.6), WavLM-large (64.0±0.2, +28.6), HuBERT-large (73.4±0.3, +19.2), SSaST-base (71.7±0.2, +20.9), BEATs-iter3 (85.7±0.3, +6.9), and AudioMAE (62.9±0.3, +29.7). At the directly comparable base configuration, MW-MAE-B-4x16-4l (89.2±0.2) beats MAE-B-4x16-4l (88.1±0.2) by 1.1 points and AudioMAE-B-4x16-4l (86.1±0.3) by 3.1 points. The advantage is most pronounced on tasks requiring local temporal structure: MW-MAE-L achieves 76.1% on Crema-D (emotion recognition) versus 73.8% for MAE-L, and 71.2% on NSynth Pitch versus 69.0%, while also leading on FSD50K (53.5 vs 51.8 mAP) and VoxLingua107 (51.9 vs 46.9). Notably, MW-MAE outperforms speech-specific models (WavLM, HuBERT, W2V2) trained on much larger corpora (94k–100k hours) on emotion recognition and speaker count tasks, despite being trained on only 5k hours of AudioSet.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Masked Autoencoder (MAE) paradigm from He et al. (2022) to audio by introducing a Multi-Window Multi-Head Attention (MW-MHA) module in the decoder, where each attention head operates on non-overlapping windows of different sizes simultaneously. It departs from prior windowed-attention approaches (Swin Transformer, CSWin, pooling attention) and the AudioMAE shifting-window design of Huang et al. (2022), where all heads within a block share a single window size, by enabling heterogeneous local-global context within a single transformer block. The audio-specific MAE protocol, masking strategy, and evaluation setup follow Niizumi et al. (2022), positioning this as a targeted architectural modification rather than a new pre-training paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MW-MHA module offers a simple, parameter-matched architectural change that consistently improves audio representation quality across 10 diverse downstream tasks, with particularly notable gains in data efficiency (13.5% vs 28.2% performance drop at 90% data reduction) and patch-size adaptability. The PWCCA analysis revealing a decoupled feature hierarchy—where same-window-size heads across decoder layers learn correlated representations—provides mechanistic insight into why the approach works. Open questions include whether the benefit generalizes beyond audio (the ImageNet proof-of-concept is underpowered), whether the modest encoder fine-tuning gain from MW-MHA is practically meaningful, and whether the 9% training throughput overhead can be eliminated through optimized kernel implementations.
