## Problem

While DPSGD is the standard algorithm for differentially private training, it is unclear whether it alone can achieve near instance-optimal results for every dataset under privacy constraints. This paper shows that for linear classification, DPSGD without feature preprocessing incurs an optimality gap proportional to the maximum Euclidean norm of features, which can be arbitrarily large relative to the dataset diameter. In contrast, non-private convex optimization does not suffer from this issue, revealing a fundamental gap between private and non-private optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a dataset of n feature vectors in R^d with binary labels {−1, +1} and privacy parameters (ε, δ), where features can be raw pixel images (28×28 grayscale for MNIST/Fashion-MNIST) or pretrained WRN penultimate-layer representations (for CIFAR-100 fine-tuning). The output is a linear classifier parameterized by a weight vector w ∈ R^d and bias b ∈ R, which maps a feature vector to a class label. The privacy budget ε is split across three phases (mean estimation, quantile estimation, and DPSGD), and the algorithm requires n = Ω(√(d log(1/δ) log R) log d / ε) samples for the theoretical guarantees to hold.

## Method

DPSGD-F augments standard DPSGD with a private feature preprocessing pipeline that reduces the optimality gap from depending on the maximum feature norm R to depending on the dataset diameter diam(D). The algorithm first privately estimates the feature mean μ̂ using instance-optimal mean estimation (Huang et al., 2021), then privately estimates a quantile τ of the distances ‖x_i − μ̂‖₂ to obtain a data-dependent norm bound. Features are then translated by μ̂, augmented with a bias coordinate scaled by τ, and clipped to norm √2τ, ensuring all gradient norms are bounded by Θ(τ) rather than Θ(R). Standard DPSGD is then run on the preprocessed features with the clipping norm set so no gradients are actually clipped, and the solution is mapped back to the original parameter space. A counterexample in Section 4 proves that no choice of clipping norm, batch size, learning rate, or initialization can make plain DPSGD avoid an Ω(1) error when μ > nε, establishing that preprocessing is provably necessary.

## Results

DPSGD-F consistently outperforms standard DPSGD across all three datasets at both ε=1 and ε=2. The most dramatic gains appear on Fashion-MNIST (77.2% → 84.0% at ε=1, a 6.8-point improvement) and MNIST (87.4% → 92.0% at ε=1, a 4.6-point improvement), where DPSGD-F nearly closes the gap to non-private SGD (92.9% and 85.0% respectively). On CIFAR-100 with pretrained WRN features, DPSGD-F improves the previous state-of-the-art from 70.6% to 71.6% at ε=1. At ε=2, DPSGD-F reaches 92.3% on MNIST, 84.5% on Fashion-MNIST, and 74.4% on CIFAR-100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is standard DPSGD (Abadi et al., 2016), which DPSGD-F outperforms on all three datasets at both privacy levels. The gap is largest on Fashion-MNIST (6.8 points at ε=1) and MNIST (4.6 points at ε=1), and smallest on CIFAR-100 (1.0 point at ε=1), suggesting the benefit of feature preprocessing is most pronounced when features are more concentrated relative to their maximum norm. The second comparison is to the previous state-of-the-art private fine-tuning result from De et al. (2022), which reported 70.6% on CIFAR-100 at ε=1; DPSGD-F improves this to 71.6%, a 1-point gain. Notably, the best privacy budget allocation for the preprocessing step is very small (ε_F = 0.02 or 0.05), indicating that only a tiny fraction of the total privacy budget is needed for the feature translation step to yield substantial accuracy gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DPSGD framework (Abadi et al., 2016) and its convergence analysis for generalized linear models (Song et al., 2020), which established dimension-independent rates but with dependence on the maximum feature norm R. It departs from the implicit assumption that DPSGD alone is sufficient by showing a provable counterexample where the error scales with R rather than the dataset diameter. The proposed DPSGD-F algorithm composes existing private estimation tools—instance-optimal mean estimation (Huang et al., 2021) and private quantile estimation (Dick et al., 2023)—with the standard DPSGD phase, creating a new algorithmic pipeline rather than a new optimization primitive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a fundamental limitation of DPSGD for private linear optimization: without feature preprocessing, the optimality gap is provably tied to the maximum feature norm, which can be arbitrarily worse than the dataset diameter. This reframes feature engineering as a theoretically necessary component of private training, not merely a heuristic. However, the empirical gains are modest (e.g., 70.6% → 71.6% on CIFAR-100 at ε=1), and the theory is restricted to linear models with margin losses. Key open questions include whether the diameter-vs-norm gap persists for deep non-linear models, whether the preprocessing budget allocation can be automated, and whether the results extend to larger-scale datasets where the R/n² term becomes negligible.
