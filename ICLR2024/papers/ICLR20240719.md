## Problem

Unlike large language models that handle diverse tasks through a unified architecture, vision foundation models (e.g., SAM, DINOv2) require task-specific structures and fine-tuning for downstream perception tasks. SAM alone cannot provide semantic categories or disambiguate among multiple mask proposals, and naively combining foundation models yields subpar results across tasks with varying semantic granularity. The paper asks whether off-the-shelf VFMs can be composed in a training-free, in-context paradigm to solve diverse segmentation tasks from a single example.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference image with a binary mask (the in-context example) and a target image, both resized to 518×518 for segmentation tasks or 896×504 for video object segmentation. It produces a pixel-level segmentation mask on the target image, with the number of merged output masks being controllable to handle single instances, multiple instances of the same semantics, or part-level outputs. For VOS, a reference memory of prior frames' features and predictions is maintained with a time-decayed scoring mechanism to track objects across the video.

## Method

Matcher is a training-free three-stage pipeline that composes DINOv2 (ViT-L/14) as an all-purpose feature extractor with SAM (ViT-H) as a class-agnostic segmenter. Stage 1 (Correspondence Matrix Extraction) computes a dense cosine-similarity matrix between DINOv2 patch features of the reference and target images. Stage 2 (Prompts Generation) applies bidirectional bipartite matching—forward matching from reference mask points to target features, then reverse matching back to verify points land on the reference mask—to eliminate outliers, followed by a robust prompt sampler that clusters matched points via k-means++ and samples part-level, instance-level, and global point/box prompts for SAM. Stage 3 (Controllable Masks Generation) scores SAM's mask proposals using an instance-level matching module that formulates mask selection as an Optimal Transport problem (computing EMD between dense semantic features inside masks) combined with purity and coverage metrics, then merges the top-k highest-scoring masks to produce the final output.

## Results

Matcher achieves 52.7% and 60.7% mean mIoU on COCO-20i (one-shot and few-shot), 87.0% and 89.6% mIoU on FSS-1000, and 33.0% and 40.0% mIoU on the proposed LVIS-92i benchmark, all without any training. On the more challenging one-shot object part segmentation benchmarks, Matcher reaches 42.9% mean mIoU on PASCAL-Part and 34.7% on PACO-Part. For video object segmentation, Matcher attains 79.5 J&F on DAVIS 2017 val and 86.1 J&F on DAVIS 2016 val, competitive with models trained on video data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against specialist models, Matcher (52.7% one-shot on COCO-20i) surpasses the best specialist MSANet (51.1%) by 1.6% and outperforms HSNet (41.2%) and VAT (41.3%) by over 11%, all without any task-specific training. Against the strongest generalist SegGPT, Matcher trails by 3.4% on COCO-20i (52.7% vs 56.1%), but this is expected since SegGPT was trained on COCO; on the cross-dataset LVIS-92i benchmark, Matcher leads SegGPT by a large margin of 14.4% (33.0% vs 18.6%). Against the SAM-only baselines PerSAM and PerSAM-F, Matcher wins by a substantial margin: +29.2% mean mIoU on COCO-20i, +11.4% on FSS-1000, and +10.7% on LVIS-92i, demonstrating that relying solely on SAM limits generalization for semantically-driven tasks. On one-shot part segmentation (Table 2), Matcher outperforms PerSAM by +12.8% on PASCAL-Part and +13.5% on PACO-Part, and beats SegGPT by +7.1% and +21.2% respectively. For video object segmentation (Table 3), Matcher (79.5 J&F on DAVIS 2017) surpasses SegGPT (75.6) and PerSAM-F (71.9) without video training, approaching models trained with video data like XMem (87.7).

## Relation to Prior Work

Matcher positions itself against two lines of work: specialist few-shot segmentation models (HSNet, VAT, FPTrans, MSANet) that require task-specific training, and generalist in-context models (Painter, SegGPT) that unify tasks but still need supervised training. The key departure is from PerSAM/PerSAM-F, which rely solely on SAM for one-shot personalization; Matcher instead couples DINOv2's all-purpose patch-level features (for semantic correspondence) with SAM's promptable segmentation (for precise mask generation), bridging them via bidirectional matching, a robust prompt sampler, and an optimal-transport-based instance-level matching module. The EMD computation follows Bonneel et al. (2011), and the overall design draws conceptual inspiration from LLM in-context learning, where a single model handles diverse tasks without fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a carefully designed training-free pipeline combining two off-the-shelf VFMs can match or exceed trained specialist models on few-shot segmentation, and substantially outperform trained generalist models on cross-dataset generalization (LVIS-92i: +14.4% over SegGPT). The large gap between PerSAM (SAM alone) and Matcher (DINOv2+SAM) reveals that SAM's class-agnostic masks are fundamentally insufficient for semantically-driven tasks without an explicit semantic matching component. The authors acknowledge a key limitation: instance-level discrimination is constrained by the image encoder's features, limiting performance on instance segmentation. The framework also serves as a useful evaluation harness for assessing new VFMs on general segmentation capability, and the introduced benchmarks (LVIS-92i, PASCAL-Part, PACO-Part) fill gaps in cross-dataset and fine-grained part segmentation evaluation.
