## Problem

White-box deep networks, in which each layer has a mathematically interpretable role, have only been demonstrated at scale in supervised settings. The fundamental obstacle for unsupervised tasks like autoencoding is constructing decoders that invert the encoder's distributional transformations, which unrolled-optimization methods cannot directly provide. This paper bridges that gap by establishing a quantitative equivalence between compression and denoising, enabling a principled construction of both encoder and decoder layers.

## Input / Output

The model takes 224×224 RGB images patchified into 196 tokens of 256 dimensions, with 75% of patches randomly masked during training. It produces a full-image reconstruction as output, while the intermediate token representations are structured to be sparse and supported on low-dimensional Gaussian mixture subspaces (12 subspaces of dimension 64 in the Base model). Notably, CRATE-MAE-Base uses only ~30% of the parameters of ViT-MAE-Base (44.6M vs. 143.8M) while maintaining competitive reconstruction and downstream classification performance.

## Method

The key insight is that, for data with low-dimensional structure, a gradient step on the lossy coding rate R^c (a compression primitive) is equivalent to a gradient step on the score function (a denoising primitive), both implementing a projection onto the data's support. This equivalence allows the encoder to be constructed as unrolled optimization (MSSA compression + ISTA sparsification, identical to CRATE) and the decoder to be derived as the time-reversal of the probability flow ODE, yielding anti-compression (negative MSSA) and dictionary-multiplication layers. Each encoder layer transforms tokens toward a learned Gaussian mixture codebook structure, and each decoder layer reverses this transformation in the opposite order. Training uses the standard masked autoencoding loss, and the architecture achieves sample-wise (deterministic) autoencoding rather than distribution-wise generation, with layer normalizations ensuring the first-order discretization approximations hold.

## Results

CRATE-MAE-Base (44.6M params) achieves 96.8% top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-10 and 80.3% on CIFAR-100 via fine-tuning, using only ~30% of the parameters of ViT-MAE-Base (143.8M params). Under linear probing, CRATE-MAE-Base reaches 80.9% on CIFAR-10 and 60.1% on CIFAR-100. The paper also demonstrates that CRATE-MAE's encoder layers progressively compress and sparsify representations (Fig. 5), and that the learned attention maps capture semantically meaningful image regions (Fig. 7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is ViT-MAE (He et al., 2022), compared at both Small and Base model sizes. CRATE-MAE consistently trails ViT-MAE-B in fine-tuning by 1.7–14 points (e.g., 96.8% vs. 98.5% on CIFAR-10, 78.5% vs. 92.5% on Oxford Flowers-102), but does so with only ~30% of the parameters (44.6M vs. 143.8M). In linear probing, CRATE-MAE-B (80.9%) slightly exceeds ViT-MAE-S (79.9%) on CIFAR-10, showing that the smaller-parameter white-box model can match a larger black-box model in the most transfer-friendly setting. On Oxford Flowers-102 linear probing, CRATE-MAE-B (61.8%) falls short of both ViT-MAE-S (66.8%) and ViT-MAE-B (66.4%). Overall, the paper positions CRATE-MAE as a parameter-efficient, interpretable alternative that achieves competitive (not state-of-the-art) accuracy, with the key advantage being that every layer has a mathematically defined role in transforming the data distribution toward a structured representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CRATE white-box transformer (Yu et al., 2023a), which was previously limited to supervised classification, into the unsupervised autoencoding setting by deriving a mathematically interpretable decoder. The key conceptual bridge is a quantitative theorem showing that, under low-dimensional structure, a gradient step on the compression (rate reduction) objective is equivalent to a score-following (denoising) step, allowing the CRATE encoder to be reinterpreted as a structured diffusion process whose time-reversal (via the probability flow ODE of Song et al., 2021) yields the decoder. The training paradigm is borrowed from masked autoencoders (He et al., 2022), but the architecture is derived from first principles rather than empirical design, making it the first white-box deep network applicable to large-scale unsupervised representation learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that white-box, mathematically interpretable architectures can be competitive in unsupervised representation learning while using roughly 30% of the parameters of standard ViT-MAE, and that the learned representations carry both explicit geometric structure and emergent semantic meaning. The compression-denoising equivalence provides a unifying theoretical lens connecting rate reduction, diffusion, and autoencoding that may inform future architecture design. Limitations include a notable absolute performance gap versus ViT-MAE (e.g., ~7 points on CIFAR-100 fine-tuning), theoretical analysis restricted to an uncorrelated-token simplification, and the authors' own caveat that results are not optimally engineered. Open questions include whether the white-box property yields practical advantages (e.g., better generalization, robustness, or controllability) beyond interpretability, and whether the approach scales to larger model sizes and more complex modalities.
