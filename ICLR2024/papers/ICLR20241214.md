## Problem

Most diffusion models are limited to generating a single data modality, yet real-world applications involve heterogeneous multi-modal data (images, labels, segmentation maps, text). There is no principled framework for jointly modeling and generating multiple modalities within a unified diffusion model, nor a theoretical loss that naturally integrates multi-task objectives into the diffusion training objective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241214/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts heterogeneous multi-modal inputs—e.g., a clean image paired with a randomly masked version, an image with its class label, or a source image with a target image—and simultaneously generates all modalities in the reverse process. Inference supports both unconditional generation (all modalities initially missing) and conditional generation (some modalities provided as fixed inputs). Experiments use 64×64 and 128×128 ImageNet, Cityscapes segmentation maps, night2day image pairs, and CLIP representations at 1024 dimensions, with the shared diffusion space defined by the U-Net latent representation.

## Method

MT-Diffusion generalizes DDPM by defining the forward noising transition as a Gaussian whose mean aggregates encoded information from all N modalities (weighted by time-dependent coefficients w_t^(i)), rather than depending only on the previous latent. The reverse process uses a shared U-Net backbone that predicts the intrinsic noise ε, with N modality-specific decoder heads attached to intermediate or output U-Net blocks to reconstruct each modality (e.g., a classifier head for labels, a convolutional head for masked images). A new multi-modal ELBO (Theorem 1) decomposes into the standard DDPM noise-matching term plus per-modality KL divergences (cross-entropy for discrete labels, MSE for continuous data), yielding a simple training loss that combines noise prediction with weighted modality reconstruction. Encoders are non-parametric or fixed (identity for same-space data, cross-attention through the U-Net for heterogeneous data like labels), keeping the forward process parameter-free while the decoders are trainable components of the reverse model.

## Results

On ImageNet-128, MT-Diffusion with constrained restoration (MT-Diffusion*) achieves FID 2.15, sFID 3.86, Precision 0.72, and Recall 0.68, substantially outperforming the ADM released checkpoint (FID 8.46, sFID 4.92) and even the classifier-guided ADM (FID 3.56, sFID 4.63). On ImageNet-64 at 1M iterations, MT-Diffusion-X (masked-image training) reaches IS 34.53 and FID 9.85, far exceeding the unconditioned ADM (IS 15.64, FID 23.22). For masked image recovery, MT-Diffusion achieves LPIPS scores of 0.035–0.133 across 5–20 masked patches, dramatically outperforming SDEdit (0.400–0.513). On Cityscapes image translation, MT-Diffusion attains 0.95 per-pixel accuracy and 0.70 class IoU, surpassing pix2pix (0.85, 0.32) and single-task diffusion (0.72, 0.32).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ADM (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021), MT-Diffusion clearly wins on generation quality at 128 resolution: MT-Diffusion* achieves FID 2.15 vs. ADM*'s 8.46 and sFID 3.86 vs. 4.92, while also matching or exceeding ADM on Precision (0.72 vs. 0.67) and Recall (0.68 vs. 0.66). At 64 resolution with only 1M iterations, MT-Diffusion-X (IS 34.53, FID 9.85) dramatically outperforms unconditioned ADM (IS 15.64, FID 23.22), demonstrating superior training efficiency from multi-task masked-image learning. For masked image recovery, MT-Diffusion achieves LPIPS scores near zero (0.035–0.133), while SDEdit (Meng et al., 2022) scores 0.400–0.513—worse than the clean-masked reference—because SDEdit does not enforce unmasked-region consistency. On Cityscapes translation, MT-Diffusion (per-pixel acc. 0.95, class IoU 0.70) substantially outperforms CycleGAN (0.58, 0.16) and pix2pix (0.85, 0.32), though InternImage-H (class IoU 0.86) remains stronger as a much larger web-scale foundation model. The joint image-label setting shows that MT-Diffusion-E* (FID 11.28, sFID 16.08) beats ADM on all metrics after single-task finetuning, and the associated classifier consistently outperforms the pretrained ADM classifier in top-1 and top-5 accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) from single-task to multi-modal generative modeling by defining a forward aggregation process that integrates information from multiple modalities into a shared diffusion space, and a reverse process with a shared U-Net backbone augmented by modality-specific decoder heads. The resulting multi-modal ELBO (Theorem 1) reduces to the standard DDPM loss in the single-task case, providing a principled unification. The authors explicitly connect their formulation to classifier guidance and classifier-free guidance, showing that those mechanisms are special cases of their more general multi-task aggregation, while noting that guided diffusion still handles only a single generation task. For large-scale experiments, the framework is instantiated on top of the Latent Diffusion / Stable Diffusion architecture (Rombach et al., 2022), extending it with additional decoder heads for joint image-representation generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MT-Diffusion provides the first principled, ELBO-grounded framework for jointly generating heterogeneous modalities (e.g., images and labels, images and CLIP representations) within a single diffusion model, opening a research direction that prior single-task diffusion work did not address. The masked-image training experiment is practically useful: adding a random inpainting auxiliary task improves both training efficiency and final generation quality on ImageNet, while simultaneously yielding a strong constrained image-restoration capability at no extra inference cost. Key limitations include testing only two modalities, some results being purely qualitative (especially the Stable Diffusion-based image-representation experiments), and the negative-transfer question remaining largely unexplored. Scaling to more than two modalities, incorporating more diverse task types, and better architectural designs for the encoder-decoder interface are all left as open problems.
