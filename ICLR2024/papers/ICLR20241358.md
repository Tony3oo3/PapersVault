## Problem

Existing text-to-motion generation methods rely on the SMPL model with a fixed skeletal rig, making them inapplicable to the broad spectrum of non-rigged 3D characters (e.g., stylized creatures, non-humanoid shapes). Auto-rigging approaches produce inconsistent skeleton structures across characters, preventing a unified motion representation. The core challenge is to generate realistic, text-driven animations for heterogeneous skeleton-free meshes while preserving geometric integrity without any pre-defined rig.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a non-rigged 3D mesh (V vertices with positions and normals, V×6 features) and a natural language motion description as input. It produces a handle-based motion sequence (local translations (K−1)×3, local rotations (K−1)×6 in 6D representation, global translation 3, global rotation 3 per frame) plus a per-frame mesh-specific adaptation vector (K×3 applied only to local translations), which together drive the mesh via Linear Blend Skinning with K=30 predicted handles. The text is encoded by CLIP's penultimate layer (multiple tokens matching word count), and the mesh is encoded into a 512-d deformation feature by concatenating three GCN-level features (64+128+256 dims).

## Method

TapMo comprises two synergistic components: a Mesh Handle Predictor (3-layer GCN + 3-layer MLP) that adaptively clusters mesh vertices into K=30 semantic handles with skinning weights, and a Shape-aware Motion Diffusion (DDPM with Transformer Decoder denoiser) that generates text-guided motion conditioned on both CLIP text tokens and the mesh deformation feature. The key architectural insight is generating a mesh-specific adaptation δ̂ (applied only to local translations) alongside the base motion, which compensates for shape variation and prevents mesh distortion. Training is weakly-supervised: the Handle Predictor uses Skinning Loss, Pose Loss, and a novel Root Loss (anchoring the first handle to the hip); the Diffusion Model combines a fully-supervised Motion Loss with a Spring Loss (penalizing handle-distance changes via adaptive spring coefficients) and an Adversarial Loss (discriminator on arm vertices), followed by ARAP-based pseudo-label fine-tuning of the adaptation layer. This design eliminates the need for ground-truth handle annotations or character-specific motion data, enabling generalization to unseen meshes.

## Results

On the HumanML3D text-to-motion benchmark, TapMo (Ours) achieves R-Precision Top-1 of 0.542 and FID of 0.515, outperforming the MDM-structured variant (TapMo MDM: 0.498 / 1.058) by over 50% in FID. On geometry quality for skeleton-free character animation, TapMo reduces ARAP-Loss to 0.271 (seen) and 0.329 (unseen), a 69.8% and 58.2% reduction over MDM-SR respectively, and achieves a Handle-FID of 0.046, 71.7% lower than MDM-SFR (0.163). In a user study with 100 participants, over 84% of users preferred TapMo's animations over both baselines across overall quality, text-motion matching, and geometry quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MDM-SR (MDM + skeleton-based retargeting via Baran & Popović), TapMo achieves 69.8% lower ARAP-Loss on seen characters (0.271 vs. 0.897) and 58.2% lower on unseen characters (0.329 vs. 0.787), but MDM-SR retains a lower Handle-FID (0.012 vs. 0.046) because it drives a standard SMPL skeleton with minimal global distortion. Against MDM-SFR (MDM + SfPT pose transfer), TapMo dominates on both metrics: 73.1% lower ARAP-Loss on seen characters (0.271 vs. 1.006) and 71.7% lower Handle-FID (0.046 vs. 0.163). On the HumanML3D text-to-motion benchmark (Table 1), TapMo (Ours) achieves the best R-Precision Top-1 (0.542) among all compared methods, surpassing ReMoDiffuse (0.510), T2M (0.457), and MDM (0.320), though ReMoDiffuse attains a lower FID (0.103 vs. 0.515) due to its retrieval-augmented design. The ablation rows in Table 2 confirm that each component (Spring Loss, Adversarial Loss, and adaptation fine-tuning) contributes incremental geometry improvements, with the full pipeline achieving the best ARAP-Loss of 0.271.

## Relation to Prior Work

TapMo sits at the intersection of two research threads: skeleton-free pose transfer (SfPT, Liao et al. 2022) and text-conditioned motion diffusion (MDM, Tevet et al. 2022). From SfPT, it inherits the GCN-based handle prediction and skinning weight learning, but departs by adding a root handle for global motion control and repurposing the predictor's intermediate features as shape-aware conditioning. From MDM, it adopts the diffusion framework but replaces the SMPL joint representation with a handle-based motion representation and introduces mesh-specific adaptation parameters. The key conceptual departure from all prior text-to-motion work (T2M, TEMOS, MotionDiffuse, ReMoDiffuse) is the elimination of any fixed skeletal rigging assumption, enabling animation of arbitrary non-rigged meshes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first pipeline to close the loop from natural language to animated skeleton-free 3D character, removing the manual rigging bottleneck that has limited animation to pre-rigged SMPL humans or hand-crafted rigs. The weakly-supervised training strategy (Spring Loss + Adversarial Loss) is a practical solution to the data scarcity problem for non-humanoid characters, though it inherently biases the model toward human-like locomotion patterns, limiting applicability to quadrupeds or radically different body plans. Open questions include whether the handle-based representation scales to characters with fundamentally different articulation (e.g., tentacles, multiple limbs), and whether combining this pipeline with emerging text-to-3D mesh generation (DreamFusion, Magic3D) can yield a fully end-to-end text-to-animated-character system.
