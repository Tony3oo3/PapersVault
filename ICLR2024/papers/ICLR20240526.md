## Problem

State-of-the-art perceptual similarity metrics like DreamSim, which rely on ensembles of ViT-based feature extractors, are highly vulnerable to adversarial perturbations—tiny imperceptible changes can cause large shifts in computed distances and flip 2AFC predictions. Prior robust perceptual metrics (e-LPIPS, R-LPIPS) offer only empirical defenses without provable guarantees, leaving no certifiably robust perceptual distance metric available for safety-critical applications such as copy detection or image retrieval.

## Input / Output

LipSim takes two RGB images (a reference and a comparison) and outputs a scalar perceptual distance in [0, 2] computed as 1 minus cosine similarity of their projected embeddings. In the 2AFC evaluation setting, the input is a triplet (reference x, two variations x₀ and x₁) and the output is a binary prediction (which variation is more similar) together with a certified robustness radius R(h,x) = M_{H,x} / ‖f(x₀) − f(x₁)‖₂, which certifies invariance of the prediction for all perturbations within an ℓ₂ ball of that radius. The feature extractor produces embeddings that are Euclidean-projected onto the unit ℓ₂ ball to satisfy the 1-Lipschitz cosine distance condition.

## Method

LipSim replaces the ViT-based DreamSim backbone with a 1-Lipschitz neural network composed of 20 Conv-SLL and 7 Linear-SLL layers (SDP-based Lipschitz Layers from Araujo et al., 2023), followed by a 1-Lipschitz translation β and Euclidean projection onto the unit ℓ₂ ball, ensuring the full pipeline f = π_{B₂(0,1)} ∘ β ∘ φ⁽ˡ⁾ ∘ … ∘ φ⁽¹⁾ is 1-Lipschitz with unit-norm outputs. Training proceeds in two stages: first, knowledge distillation on ImageNet-1k where DreamSim acts as teacher and the 1-Lipschitz student mimics its embeddings (with a parallel jittered augmentation pipeline to reduce color bias via RMSE loss); second, fine-tuning on the NIGHT 2AFC dataset with a hinge loss (margin 0.5) to widen classification margins and boost certified robustness. The key theoretical contribution (Theorem 1) proves that under these conditions the certified radius is M_{H,x}/‖f(x₀)−f(x₁)‖₂, which is strictly tighter than the generic Tsuzuku et al. Lipschitz-margin bound of 2ε, and requires only a single forward pass to compute—no Monte Carlo sampling.

## Results

On the NIGHT dataset, LipSim achieves a natural 2AFC score of 85.58%, lower than DreamSim's 96.16%, but dramatically outperforms all baselines under adversarial attack. Under ℓ₂-APGD with ε=2.0, LipSim retains 72.20% accuracy while DreamSim collapses to 0.93%, DINO to 19.29%, and CLIP to 1.20%. For certified robustness, LipSim-DreamSim (margin 0.5) achieves 66.39% certified accuracy at ε=36/255 and 44.90% at ε=72/255, whereas DreamSim provides no certified guarantee (0.0% on BAPPS). On the KNN classification task (Imagenette), LipSim achieves 85.32% top-1 accuracy versus DreamSim's 99.03%, but remains competitive on top-5 (97.20% vs 99.82%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LipSim trades natural accuracy for certified robustness: it scores 85.58% on the NIGHT 2AFC task versus DreamSim's 96.16%, a gap of roughly 10.6 points. However, under ℓ₂-APGD attacks the picture reverses dramatically — at ε=2.0, LipSim maintains 72.20% while DreamSim drops to 0.93%, a gap of over 71 points. Among the individual ViT-based baselines, DINO is the most robust (19.29% at ε=2.0) but still far below LipSim; CLIP and OpenCLIP are even more fragile. On the BAPPS dataset (Table 3), LipSim provides non-trivial certified scores (31.20% at ε=36/255) while DreamSim yields 0.0% certified accuracy, confirming that the Lipschitz construction is essential for provable guarantees. On the KNN classification task, DreamSim substantially outperforms LipSim on top-1 (99.03% vs 85.32%), indicating that the 1-Lipschitz constraint sacrifices some discriminative power in embedding space, though the top-5 gap narrows (99.82% vs 97.20%). Compared to the empirical defense R-LPIPS, LipSim achieves higher natural scores on NIGHT (85.58% vs 70.56%) and higher adversarial robustness (79.99% vs 58.50% under ℓ₂-MIA ε=1.0), while also providing certified guarantees that R-LPIPS lacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: state-of-the-art perceptual similarity metrics (DreamSim, which concatenates ViT-based embeddings from DINO, CLIP, and OpenCLIP) and 1-Lipschitz neural network construction (specifically the SDP-based Lipschitz Layer from Araujo et al., 2023). Prior robust perceptual metrics (e-LPIPS, R-LPIPS) relied on heuristic defenses like random transformations or adversarial training without formal guarantees, while earlier certified approaches for structured outputs (Kumar & Goldstein, 2021; Shao et al., 2023) used randomized smoothing that produced excessively loose bounds due to the triangle inequality approximation. LipSim departs from both by distilling DreamSim's ViT-based knowledge into a 1-Lipschitz CNN backbone and deriving a tighter certified radius specific to the cosine-distance-on-unit-ball formulation, avoiding the Monte Carlo sampling overhead of smoothing-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first perceptual similarity metric with provable certified robustness, which has direct implications for security-sensitive applications like perceptual hashing, copy detection, and image retrieval where an attacker could craft imperceptible perturbations to bypass similarity-based filtering. The main limitation is the natural accuracy trade-off: LipSim scores 85.58% on NIGHT versus DreamSim's 96.16%, and the certified radii (36/255 to 108/255) are modest. Open questions include whether the 1-Lipschitz distillation approach can close the natural accuracy gap, whether the framework generalizes to other 2AFC datasets beyond NIGHT and BAPPS, and whether the SLL architecture can scale to deeper or ViT-based backbones without losing the Lipschitz guarantee.
