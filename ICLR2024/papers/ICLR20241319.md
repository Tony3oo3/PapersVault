## Problem

Causal inference with high-dimensional complex data faces three intertwined challenges: covariate dimensionality far exceeding sample size, unknown and highly nonlinear functional forms for the outcome and propensity score, and missing values in the covariates. Existing methods address these issues in isolation—regularization handles high dimensionality under linear assumptions, while deep networks capture nonlinearities but only in low-dimensional settings—leaving no unified framework that simultaneously tackles all three difficulties.

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular data with p covariates (p can greatly exceed n, e.g., p=1000 with n=800), a binary treatment indicator, and a continuous or binary outcome, optionally with missing covariate entries under MAR or MNAR mechanisms. Beyond the ATE point estimate, it produces a doubly robust AIPW estimator with asymptotically valid confidence intervals, a CATE function for heterogeneous effects, and a sparse set of covariates identified as relevant to the treatment and outcome models respectively, with the guarantee that treatment-relevant covariates are a subset of outcome-relevant ones.

## Method

Causal-StoNet embeds the binary treatment variable as a visible neuron within an intermediate hidden layer of a Stochastic Neural Network (StoNet), which is a probabilistic DNN constructed by adding Gaussian noise to each hidden unit so that the network decomposes into a composition of simple linear and logistic regressions. A mixture Gaussian prior (spike-and-slab) is placed on every weight and bias to induce sparsity, enabling consistent covariate selection even when p >> n. Training proceeds via adaptive stochastic gradient Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (SGHMC), which alternates between backward-sampling the latent hidden-layer variables and updating network parameters through stochastic approximation, thereby jointly imputing missing data and estimating model parameters. The learned propensity score and outcome functions are plugged into the AIPW formula to yield a doubly robust ATE estimator whose asymptotic normality and uniform validity are established under mild regularity conditions.

## Results

On the ACIC 2019 benchmark (10 synthetic datasets, 200 covariates), Causal-StoNet achieves an out-of-sample ATE MAE of 0.0542 (SD 0.0132), substantially outperforming DSE (0.1632), ARBE (0.1335), TMLE-ensemble (0.1316), and DONUT (0.5290). On the Twins dataset (46 covariates, true ATE = −2.48%), Causal-StoNet estimates −2.32% (SD 0.42%), the closest to ground truth with the smallest variance among all methods. For CATE estimation on a heterogeneous ACIC dataset, Causal-StoNet achieves the lowest ATE error (0.0118) though it slightly trails CMDE (0.0823) and CEVAE (0.0867) on PEHE (0.0893).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Causal-StoNet decisively outperforms all ATE-focused baselines on the ACIC 2019 benchmark: it reduces out-of-sample MAE by ~3.0× over DSE (0.0542 vs. 0.1632), ~2.5× over ARBE (0.0542 vs. 0.1335), and ~10× over DONUT (0.0542 vs. 0.5290). TMLE with Lasso is the closest competitor (0.0867 out-of-sample), still 60% worse than Causal-StoNet. On the Twins dataset, Causal-StoNet (−2.32%, SD 0.42%) is closest to the true ATE of −2.48%, while TMLE-ensemble (−12.90%) and TMLE-Lasso (−11.03%) are severely biased. For CATE estimation, Causal-StoNet achieves the best ATE error (0.0118) but is marginally outperformed by CMDE (0.0823) and CEVAE (0.0867) on PEHE (0.0893), while clearly beating GANITE (0.1913), X-Learner-RF (0.1877), and both CFRNet variants (0.1158–0.1182) on PEHE. On TCGA-BRCA (110 covariates, 845 samples), Causal-StoNet yields the tightest estimate (−0.1243, SD 0.0058) compared to ARBE (SD 0.1021) and DSE (SD 0.0243), and it identifies clinically validated biomarkers (pathology N stage, ALDH3A2) through its sparse structure selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

Causal-StoNet unifies two previously separate research threads: stochastic neural networks (StoNet) from Liang et al. (2022) and Sun & Liang (2022), which provide a probabilistic deep learning framework with latent variables, and sparse deep learning from Sun et al. (2022) and Liang et al. (2018b), which impose mixture Gaussian priors for consistent variable selection in high dimensions. Prior DNN-based causal inference methods (Shi et al., 2019; Farrell et al., 2021) either lack consistency guarantees or require low-dimensional settings with high smoothness, while Lasso-based approaches (Belloni et al., 2014) assume linearity. The key architectural departure is embedding the treatment variable as a visible neuron within a hidden layer, creating a natural forward-modeling decomposition of the joint distribution that simultaneously yields the propensity score and outcome function, with the AIPW estimator from Robins et al. (1994) and Farrell (2015) providing the final causal inference step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper provides the first unified framework that simultaneously handles high-dimensional covariates, unknown nonlinear functional forms, and missing data in causal inference with full theoretical guarantees (consistency, asymptotic normality, uniformly valid confidence intervals). The forward-modeling structure makes extensions to multi-level treatments, continuous treatments, and mediation analysis straightforward, addressing practical scenarios that prior methods handle only in isolation. Limitations include the computational cost of SGHMC-based training, the need to pre-specify network architecture, and theoretical conditions (r_n ≺ n^{3/16}) that may be restrictive for extremely sparse or dense networks. Open questions include scalability to very large networks, sensitivity to hyperparameter choices (noise variances, prior parameters), and how the method compares to more recent orthogonalization-based approaches at scale.
