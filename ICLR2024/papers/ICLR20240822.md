## Problem

Existing synthetic EHR generation methods either handle only static (categorical) features or only temporal (numerical) features, and all assume data completeness. In real-world EHRs, records frequently lack an entire modality (e.g., static measurements without corresponding temporal data, or vice versa), a setting the authors formalize as missing modality not at random (MMCAR). No prior method can jointly generate heterogeneous static and temporal EHR features while training on data where one modality is absent for some records.

## Input / Output

The model consumes heterogeneous tabular EHR records containing both static features (e.g., race, gender, admission type; m dimensions) and temporal features (e.g., heart rate, lab values; T×d dimensions with d up to 7,307), where some records may have only one modality present. At generation time, it takes random Gaussian noise (optionally conditioned on a label via classifier-free guidance) and produces a complete synthetic record with both static and temporal components decoded from their respective latent embeddings. The temporal dimensionality is extremely high (d > 5000), which is why the model operates in a compressed latent space rather than directly on raw features.

## Method

FLEXGEN-EHR uses a dual encoder-decoder framework (MLP for static, 1D-CNN/LSTM for temporal) to produce separate latent embeddings, which are concatenated into a fused representation z = [z^T; z^S] on which a latent diffusion model is trained. The key innovation for handling missing modalities is a Gromov-Wasserstein optimal transport module that learns a soft probabilistic coupling Γ between the static and temporal embedding spaces using cosine similarity matrices and label information as global correspondence, without requiring known one-to-one pairings. Once Γ is solved, a linear transformation A is learned via least squares to map between the two latent spaces, enabling imputation of missing embeddings (e.g., z_i^S = A z_i^T Γ^{-1}) so that all records—complete or partial—can contribute to diffusion training. The diffusion model uses 50 timesteps with a linear noise schedule and classifier-free guidance for conditional generation, and the final synthetic records are produced by splitting the generated fused latent vector and passing each half through its respective decoder.

## Results

On four EHR benchmarks (MIMIC-III Mortality, MIMIC-III ARF, eICU Mortality, eICU ARF), FLEXGEN-EHR achieves the best fidelity across all datasets, improving R² by up to 0.032 and reducing MMD by up to 0.049 over the strongest baseline (EHR-M-GAN). In downstream utility, FLEXGEN-EHR wins all 4/4 dataset-metric tests, with average improvements of 6.37% AUROC and 7.16% AUPR over the best baseline. Privacy (membership inference accuracy) remains comparable to all baselines, hovering near the ideal 0.5 threshold.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLEXGEN-EHR outperforms all six baselines on every fidelity and utility metric across all four datasets. The strongest baseline, EHR-M-GAN (a GAN with separate encoders for static and temporal features), is consistently beaten: FLEXGEN-EHR improves R² by 0.019–0.032 and reduces MMD by 0.014–0.049, and gains 6.37% AUROC / 7.16% AUPR on average in downstream prediction. LDM (latent diffusion without the dual-encoder or OT alignment) is the second-strongest method but still trails FLEXGEN-EHR by 0.02–0.04 in R², confirming that the dual encoder-decoder plus optimal transport alignment contributes meaningfully. TABDDPM, a recent tabular diffusion model, performs notably poorly on MIMIC-III Mortality (R² = 0.395, MMD = 1.636), suggesting it struggles with the high dimensionality (d = 7,307) of that dataset. GAN-based baselines (MEDGAN, CORGAN) and VAE lag substantially behind, with MMD values 1.5–2.4× higher than FLEXGEN-EHR on MIMIC-III Mortality. In the missing-modality setting (Section 5.5), FLEXGEN-EHR maintains near-full-data fidelity while EHR-M-GAN (forced to use kNN imputation) shows significant degradation in both R² and AUROC as the fraction of missing samples increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

FLEXGEN-EHR builds on the latent diffusion model (LDM) framework and the tabular diffusion approach of TabDDPM, but departs from both by introducing a dual encoder-decoder architecture (MLP for static, CNN for temporal features) that projects high-dimensional EHR data into a compact latent space before diffusion. It extends the heterogeneous EHR generation line (MedGAN, CorGAN, EHR-M-GAN) by jointly modeling static and temporal modalities rather than treating them separately, and introduces a Gromov-Wasserstein optimal transport module to impute missing modalities—a capability absent from all prior EHR generative models. The key conceptual departure from EHR-M-GAN is replacing the assumption that autoencoder and BLSTM representations are directly compatible with an explicit alignment step in embedding space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Missing modality (MMCAR) is a pervasive and practically important problem in real EHRs, and prior generative methods either discard incomplete records or rely on naive imputation, both of which introduce selection bias. FLEXGEN-EHR's OT-based imputation offers a principled alternative that preserves all training samples, which could accelerate methodological development in privacy-sensitive settings where data sharing is restricted. However, the missing-modality evaluation only compares against EHR-M-GAN with kNN imputation, leaving open whether the OT approach would outperform more sophisticated imputation strategies (e.g., iterative multiple imputation, model-based imputation). The paper also acknowledges the inherent speed limitation of diffusion-based generation and the privacy-utility trade-off, both of which remain active areas for future work.
