## Problem

Existing single-view-to-multiview generation methods either produce visually plausible but geometrically inconsistent novel views (e.g., Zero123) or rely on slow per-shape distillation with textual inversion that does not guarantee reconstruction quality. The core unsolved challenge is how to design a diffusion model that generates multiview-consistent images for arbitrary objects in a single forward pass, without requiring per-object optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241091/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 256×256 RGB image of an object (assumed centered at the origin within a unit cube) along with a roughly estimated elevation angle, and produces N=16 multiview-consistent images at fixed viewpoints with azimuths evenly distributed over [0°, 360°] and a fixed elevation of 30°. These generated images can then be fed into a vanilla NeuS for ~10 minutes of training to obtain a 3D mesh. The system also generalizes to non-photorealistic inputs such as sketches, cartoons, and ink paintings, though it assumes a perspective projection and a fixed intrinsic camera matrix.

## Method

SyncDreamer extends DDPM to model the joint probability distribution of N multiview images by constructing N synchronized noise predictors that share a single UNet backbone (initialized and frozen from Zero123). At every denoising step, each predictor denoises its assigned view while exchanging information with all other views through a 3D-aware feature attention mechanism: a 3D spatial feature volume is built by projecting a 32³ vertex grid onto all N noisy views, a 3D CNN processes this volume, and for the current target view a pixel-aligned view-frustum volume (32×32×48) is extracted via interpolation. New depth-wise attention layers are inserted on intermediate UNet feature maps to attend along the depth dimension of this frustum, enforcing local epipolar consistency while the shared spatial volume enforces a global constraint that all views observe the same object. Training is supervised on Objaverse (~800k objects) with 16 target views and 16 random input views per object; only the 3D-aware attention parameters are learned (80k steps, batch 192, 8×A100), while the Zero123 UNet and text-attention layers remain frozen to preserve generalization.

## Results

On the Google Scanned Object (GSO) dataset, SyncDreamer achieves the best single-view reconstruction quality with a Chamfer Distance of 0.0261 and Volume IoU of 0.5421, outperforming all six baselines. For novel-view synthesis, SyncDreamer attains PSNR 20.05, SSIM 0.798, and LPIPS 0.146, while also producing 1123 COLMAP-reconstructed points (vs. 95 for Zero123), demonstrating substantially stronger multiview geometric consistency. The method also generalizes to non-photorealistic inputs such as sketches, cartoons, and ink paintings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SyncDreamer outperforms all six baselines on both Chamfer Distance and Volume IoU. Against the closest competitor Zero123 (used as backbone), SyncDreamer reduces Chamfer Distance from 0.0339 to 0.0261 (23% improvement) and increases Volume IoU from 0.5035 to 0.5421. Magic123, which combines Zero123 with RealFusion's SDS loss, achieves CD 0.0516 and IoU 0.4528, still trailing SyncDreamer by roughly 50% in CD. The 3D generative models Point-E (CD 0.0426, IoU 0.2875) and Shap-E (CD 0.0436, IoU 0.3584) produce incomplete meshes with significantly lower IoU. One-2-3-45 (CD 0.0629, IoU 0.4086) captures general geometry but loses fine details due to multiview-inconsistent inputs from Zero123. RealFusion (CD 0.0819, IoU 0.2741) shows the weakest reconstruction quality, though it does produce more COLMAP points (4010) in the NVS task due to its SDS-based consistency, at the cost of visually implausible images.

## Relation to Prior Work

SyncDreamer extends the DDPM diffusion framework to model the joint probability distribution of N multiview images in a single reverse process, departing from both distillation-based approaches (DreamFusion, RealFusion, Magic123) that optimize a NeRF against SDS loss, and autoregressive render-and-generate methods (MVDream, Viewset Diffusion) that produce views sequentially. The backbone UNet is initialized from Zero123 (itself finetuned from Stable Diffusion on Objaverse), and the key architectural novelty is a 3D-aware feature attention mechanism that constructs a shared spatial volume from all target views and applies depth-wise attention along epipolar lines to synchronize intermediate denoising states across views. This positions the work as a direct, non-autoregressive alternative to prior multiview generation methods, requiring no external depth estimation or radiance field prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SyncDreamer offers a simpler and more predictable single-view-to-3D pipeline than distillation methods: no textual inversion, no SDS loss tuning, and reconstruction quality can be assessed from the generated images before running NeuS. The ability to produce multiple diverse yet consistent instances from one input (unlike distillation, which converges to a single shape) opens possibilities for creative 3D content generation. Key limitations include fixed viewpoints (30° elevation, 16 azimuths), sensitivity to foreground object size due to a fixed intrinsic matrix during training, and occasional low-quality stochastic failures. Open questions include generalizing to arbitrary viewpoints, handling orthographic-projection inputs common in 2D design, and improving texture detail that tends to be coarser than Zero123 due to the multiview consistency constraint.
