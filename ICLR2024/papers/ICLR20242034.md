## Problem

Training CLIP-style models on web-scale datasets like LAION-400M incurs prohibitive compute costs, and existing pruning methods (e.g., CLIP-score filtering) assess only individual sample quality without considering how samples relate to their neighbors. The paper identifies that the marginal information gain of a data point depends on the density of surrounding points: dense regions of the embedding space can be pruned more aggressively while sparse, complex regions should retain more samples. Prior work on concept-level pruning (SSP-Pruning) was limited to curated datasets like ImageNet and had not been scaled to noisy web-scale corpora.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes image-text pairs from web-scale datasets (LAION-CAT-440M at 440M examples, DataComp Medium at 128M examples) and produces pruned subsets of varying sizes (e.g., 84M, 112M, 166M, 222M from LAION). Images are resized to 224×224 and embedded via a distilled DINOv2-L/14 encoder into 1024-dimensional vectors for clustering; captions are not directly used in the pruning criterion. The output is a pruned image-text dataset where the per-cluster sample count is determined by a complexity-weighted allocation solved via quadratic programming, and within each cluster the least prototypical (farthest from centroid) examples are retained.

## Method

Density-Based Pruning (DBP) extends SSP-Pruning by replacing its fixed cluster balancing score with a concept-complexity criterion. For each k-means cluster, complexity is defined as the product of inter-cluster distance (average cosine distance to the l=20 nearest neighbor centroids) and intra-cluster distance (average cosine distance of members to the centroid), capturing both how isolated a concept is and how internally diverse it is. This complexity score is converted to a probability distribution via a softmax (τ=0.1), and a constrained quadratic program (solved with qpsolvers) allocates the target dataset size N across clusters while respecting upper bounds from actual cluster sizes. Within each cluster, samples are ranked by distance to the centroid and the least prototypical (hardest) examples are kept. A critical prerequisite is semantic deduplication via SemDeDup, because in web-scale datasets duplicates dominate clusters and make distance-to-centroid an unreliable difficulty signal; the full three-stage pipeline is: SemDeDup → CLIP-score filtering → DBP.

## Results

On LAION-CAT-440M, DBP with 112M examples (27.7% training cost) achieves 64.09% ImageNet zero-shot accuracy, outperforming OpenCLIP-ViT-B/32 (62.93%) trained on the full 400M dataset. On DataComp Medium, the full pipeline (SemDeDup + CLIP-L/14 score + DBP) with 19.2M examples achieves 33.35% ImageNet, 37.26% VTAB, and 26.82% Retrieval, beating the prior SOTA (T-MARS) on three of four task families. The 166M DBP subset further reaches 65.46% ImageNet accuracy at 41.6% compute, surpassing training on the full LAION-CAT-440M (64.07%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against T-MARS (the prior SOTA on DataComp Medium), DBP wins on ImageNet (33.35 vs 33.00), VTAB (37.26 vs 36.30), and Retrieval (26.82 vs 22.50) but loses on ImageNet distribution shifts (24.73 vs 27.00), which the authors attribute to shorter effective training. On LAION, DBP at 112M examples (27.7% compute) beats OpenCLIP-ViT-B/32 on ImageNet by 1.16 p.p. (64.09 vs 62.93), and at 166M examples (41.6% compute) beats the full LAION-CAT-440M training by 1.39 p.p. (65.46 vs 64.07). DBP consistently outperforms SSP-Pruning (its direct predecessor) by 1.2 p.p. average accuracy at 112M and 0.9 p.p. at 166M, confirming that complexity-aware cluster sampling is more effective than fixed cluster balancing. Against CLIP-score filtering alone on LAION-50M (30M subset), DBP gains 5.34 p.p. ImageNet accuracy for CLIP-B/32 (43.41 vs 38.07) and 5.60 p.p. for CLIP-L/14 (53.21 vs 47.61). The DataComp baseline (Image-based ∩ CLIP Score L/14 top 30%) is outperformed by 3.65 p.p. on ImageNet (33.35 vs 29.70) and 2.66 p.p. on VTAB (37.26 vs 34.60).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SSP-Pruning (Sorscher et al., 2022), which clusters embeddings and removes the most prototypical examples, by replacing its fixed cluster balancing score with a complexity-aware criterion based on intra-cluster and inter-cluster distances. It also incorporates SemDeDup (Abbas et al., 2023) as a necessary deduplication precursor, which was not required for the curated ImageNet setting where SSP-Pruning was originally developed. The method is positioned against CLIP-score filtering and T-MARS as complementary stages in a three-step pipeline (deduplication → CLIP-score filtering → density-based pruning), departing from prior work that treats each sample's importance independently by instead considering the local density and conceptual complexity of the embedding space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a simple, reproducible pipeline (k-means clustering + two distance metrics + quadratic program) reduces CLIP training compute by over 70% while improving zero-shot performance, making large-scale multimodal pretraining more accessible to academic labs. However, retrieval and ImageNet distribution shift tasks still show smaller gains, suggesting the method may benefit from longer training or task-specific tuning. Open questions include whether the complexity-based sampling criterion generalizes beyond CLIP-style contrastive training to other pretraining paradigms, and whether the approach scales to even larger datasets (LAION-5B) or to text-only and video modalities.
