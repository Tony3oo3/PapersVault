## Problem

Despite a large body of work on state and history representations in RL, the relationships among seemingly distinct abstraction frameworks (Q*-irrelevance, bisimulation, information states, belief states) remain unclear, leaving practitioners with a 'paradox of choice' over which method to use. This paper shows that these frameworks are all grounded in a common self-predictive condition and provides the first theoretical explanation for why stop-gradient targets prevent representational collapse when learning such representations end-to-end.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240936/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder f_φ maps either a full state vector (MDP, e.g., 17–376-dim MuJoCo states) or a truncated observation-action history (POMDP, e.g., 9240-dim in MiniGrid with 50+10 step rollouts) to a fixed-dimensional latent z ∈ ℝ^d. A separate latent transition model g_θ: Z×A→Z predicts the next latent state, and the stop-gradient target f_φ̄(h') (detached or EMA copy) serves as the regression target for this prediction. The actor and critic operate entirely in latent space, so the encoder is shared across all components and updated by both the RL loss and the auxiliary ZP loss simultaneously.

## Method

The paper establishes a unified implication graph linking Q*-irrelevance, self-predictive (model-irrelevance), and observation-predictive (belief) abstractions, proving that ZP + Q* implies RP (Thm. 2), so explicit reward prediction is unnecessary. It then analyzes the practical ℓ2 and f-divergence ZP objectives, showing they are upper bounds of the ideal distribution-matching objective that become tight only in deterministic environments (Props. 1–2). The key optimization insight is that stop-gradient (detached or EMA) ZP targets provably preserve EZP stationary points in stochastic settings (Prop. 3) and prevent representational collapse by maintaining the encoder's initial rank under linear dynamics (Thm. 3). The resulting minimalist algorithm adds a single auxiliary loss ‖g_θ(f_φ(h),a) − f_φ̄(h')‖² (or KL variant) to any model-free RL objective (TD3/DDPG for MDPs, R2D2 for POMDPs), removing reward models, planning, multi-step predictions, projections, and metric learning used by prior self-predictive methods.

## Results

On standard MuJoCo (500k steps), the minimalist φ_L (ZP-ℓ₂, ZP-FKL, ZP-RKL) matches or slightly outperforms ALM(3) in Ant, HalfCheetah, and Walker2d, while greatly outperforming the model-free φ_{Q*} baseline (TD3) across the benchmark except Humanoid. In distracting MuJoCo (1.5M steps, distractor dims 2⁴–2⁸), φ_O degrades much faster than φ_L as distractor dimensionality increases, and φ_{Q*} performs worse than φ_O. On 20 sparse-reward MiniGrid POMDP tasks, end-to-end ZP (φ_L) significantly outperforms R2D2 (φ_{Q*}) on average IQM return but falls short of OP (φ_O), and end-to-end training matches or exceeds phased training (RP+ZP, RP+OP). EMA stop-gradient ZP targets preserve encoder rank (near full-rank 128 in MiniGrid), while online targets cause severe rank collapse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ALM(3) (Ghugare et al., 2022), the minimalist φ_L achieves comparable or slightly better sample efficiency in Ant, HalfCheetah, and Walker2d at 500k steps, but ALM(3) retains an edge in Humanoid due to its SVG policy optimization exploiting first-order gradients from latent dynamics. Against TD3 (Fujimoto et al., 2018) as the φ_{Q*} model-free baseline, all ZP variants (ℓ₂, FKL, RKL) substantially outperform it across MuJoCo tasks and in distracting settings, confirming that the self-predictive auxiliary signal provides the primary benefit. Against OFENet (Ota et al., 2020) as the φ_O observation-predictive baseline, ZP methods lose in clean deterministic MiniGrid (where OP has an advantage) but win in distracting MuJoCo where φ_O degrades faster with higher-dimensional distractors. Against R2D2 (Kapturowski et al., 2018) on MiniGrid POMDPs, end-to-end ZP significantly outperforms the model-free baseline in average IQM return, while OP further improves upon ZP. Against the phased training baselines from Seyedsalehi et al. (2023), end-to-end ZP and OP training yield equal or superior sample efficiency, with end-to-end ZP markedly excelling over its phased counterpart (RP+ZP).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies a broad family of state and history representation methods in RL—bisimulation (Gelada et al., 2019), information states (Subramanian et al., 2022), belief states, successor features, and self-supervised approaches (Schwarzer et al., 2020; Guo et al., 2020)—under a single 'self-predictive abstraction' condition, showing they all optimize closely interconnected properties. It extends Tang et al.'s (2022) theoretical analysis of self-predictive learning to action-dependent latent transitions, POMDPs, and EMA targets, and simplifies ALM(3) (Ghugare et al., 2022) by removing the reward model, planning, and multi-step predictions to yield a minimalist end-to-end algorithm. The paper also builds on R2D2 (Kapturowski et al., 2018) as its POMDP baseline and on the phased training framework of Seyedsalehi et al. (2023) for comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified implication graph and the theorem showing that end-to-end ZP training implicitly induces reward prediction (Thm. 2) provide practitioners with a principled, simplified recipe for representation learning that removes many ad-hoc components (reward models, projections, metric learning, multi-step predictions). The stop-gradient theory (Thm. 3) explains a widely used but previously unexplained practical choice, extending the understanding of representational collapse beyond the self-supervised learning literature. However, the theoretical guarantees hold only under linear assumptions, and empirical validation is limited to MuJoCo and MiniGrid; whether the minimalist algorithm scales to high-dimensional pixel-based POMDPs or long-horizon tasks remains an open question. The paper's guidelines are explicitly preliminary, and the interaction between representation learning and policy optimization in more complex settings is left for future work.
