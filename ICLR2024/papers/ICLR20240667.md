## Problem

Existing multimodal LLMs handle only one audio type (speech, audio events, or music) in isolation, and no single end-to-end model can perceive and reason over general audio containing all three simultaneously. Furthermore, when such models are instruction-tuned, they suffer from task over-fitting: the intrinsic conditional language model becomes biased toward deterministic outputs like transcriptions and captions, causing the model to ignore novel instructions and lose the cross-modal emergent abilities (zero-shot QA, storytelling, co-reasoning) that the underlying LLM possessed.

## Input / Output

SALMONN takes a variable-length general audio waveform (any mixture of speech, audio events, and music) and a free-form text instruction prompt as input. Both encoders operate at 50 Hz, and the window-level Q-Former processes 17-frame windows (~0.33 s) with one query each, yielding a variable-length token sequence (e.g., 88 tokens for 30 s of audio) that is prepended to the text prompt. The model produces a single free-form text response—transcription, translation, caption, answer, or story—without requiring an explicit ASR intermediate step, enabling end-to-end speech-grounded NLP tasks.

## Method

SALMONN fuses a frozen Whisper-Large-v2 speech encoder and a frozen BEATs non-speech audio encoder via frame-by-frame concatenation at 50 Hz, then applies a window-level Q-Former (N=1 query, L=17 frames per window) that converts each window into one augmented audio token with monotonic temporal alignment, feeding a frozen Vicuna-13B LLM adapted through LoRA (rank 8, scaling 4.0; ~33 M trainable parameters, ~0.24% of the model). Training proceeds in three stages: (1) cross-modal pre-training on ASR and audio captioning to align the randomly initialised Q-Former and LoRA with the frozen encoders and LLM; (2) instruction tuning on 12 diverse speech/audio/music tasks (~4400 h, ~2.3 M samples); and (3) a novel activation tuning stage that fine-tunes on just 12 long stories generated by discounting the LoRA scaling factor, regularising the intrinsic conditional LM P(Y|X) to restore cross-modal emergent abilities while preserving performance on trained tasks. The key theoretical insight is that task over-fitting arises because instruction tuning with short, deterministic responses biases P(Y|X) toward transcriptions, making P(Y|X,I) small for diverse zero-shot instructions; activation tuning with long, diverse responses counteracts this bias without catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

On trained Level 1 tasks, SALMONN with activation tuning achieves competitive results: ASR %WER of (2.1, 4.9, 10.0) on LibriSpeech test-clean/test-other/GigaSpeech (vs. Whisper's 2.2/5.1/9.2), En2Zh BLEU4 of 33.1, and AAC METEOR/SPIDEr of 24.0/40.3. On untrained Level 2 tasks, SALMONN outperforms the Whisper+Vicuna cascaded baseline on En2De (18.6 vs. 16.5 BLEU4) and En2Ja (22.7 vs. 15.6 BLEU4), while remaining competitive on KE (0.32 vs. 0.31 accuracy). On novel Level 3 tasks, activation tuning dramatically improves performance: Story diversity jumps from 7.77 to 82.57 (FR from 0.00 to 1.00), and SAC accuracy rises from 0.02 to 0.50 (FR from 0.04 to 0.73).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ASR, SALMONN (2.1/4.9/9.1 %WER) marginally outperforms Whisper-Large-v2 (2.2/5.1/9.2) on all three test sets. On En2Zh speech translation, SALMONN (34.4/33.1 BLEU4) trails the CoVoST2 reference (38.9) by ~4–6 BLEU points. For phone recognition, SALMONN (4.2 %PER) is notably worse than the specialised WavLM model (3.1 %PER), and for overlapped speech recognition, SALMONN (20.7/23.0 %WER) is far behind the dedicated multi-talker system (7.6 %WER). On the untrained Level 2 translation tasks, SALMONN beats the Whisper+Vicuna cascaded baseline by 2–7 BLEU points on En2De and En2Ja, demonstrating that end-to-end audio grounding avoids error propagation from ASR. However, on SQQA (0.41 vs. 0.77 accuracy) and SF (0.41 vs. 0.46), SALMONN still lags the cascaded system, which the authors attribute to LoRA adaptation causing partial forgetting of text-based commonsense knowledge. On the novel Level 3 tasks (Story, SAC), no prior baseline exists, and activation tuning is essential to make SALMONN functional at all.

## Relation to Prior Work

SALMONN extends the Q-Former-based connection module from BLIP-2 (used for fixed-size image encoders) to a window-level formulation that handles variable-length audio sequences, preserving temporal resolution critical for speech recognition. It departs from prior audio-LLM work (AudioGPT, MacawLLM, Listen-Think-Understand) by being the first single end-to-end model that jointly processes speech, audio events, and music via a dual-encoder (Whisper + BEATs) architecture, rather than relying on pipelines or supporting only one sound type. The LoRA-based adaptation of Vicuna follows the parameter-efficient fine-tuning paradigm established for visual LLMs (InstructBLIP, Video-LLaMA), but the paper's key conceptual departure is identifying and formalizing the 'task over-fitting' problem in cross-modal instruction tuning and proposing activation tuning as a solution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of task over-fitting — where cross-modal instruction tuning collapses the LLM's emergent abilities into a narrow distribution dominated by ASR/AAC outputs — is a broadly applicable diagnostic for any multimodal LLM that combines a text backbone with new sensory encoders. The proposed activation tuning (12 self-supervised steps) is a remarkably cheap fix, but its generality across architectures, modalities, and task distributions remains untested. Limitations include modest absolute performance on the novel level-3 tasks (SAC accuracy 0.50), degradation in text-based commonsense (SQQA below the Whisper+Vicuna cascade), and reliance on a single 13B backbone. Open questions include whether the dual-encoder design is optimal versus a unified audio encoder, whether activation tuning scales to more complex reasoning tasks, and whether the approach transfers to other sensory modalities (e.g., video).
