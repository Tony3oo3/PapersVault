## Problem

Existing fully test-time adaptation (FTTA) methods can only handle minor distribution shifts (e.g., Gaussian noise) and rely on heuristic entropy minimization without theoretical guarantees. Recent theoretical results prove that OOD generalization is infeasible without additional information, yet no practical framework exists for incorporating limited labeled test samples into the streaming TTA setting while simultaneously avoiding catastrophic forgetting across multiple domains.

## Input / Output

The method consumes streaming test image batches (e.g., 100 samples per step) along with a frozen source-pretrained ResNet-18-BN model and a fixed annotation budget B (e.g., 300–500 labels total across the entire test stream). At each time step, the model outputs class predictions for incoming samples and updates its parameters in-place; the frozen source model additionally generates pseudo-labels for low-entropy samples to serve as a source-domain proxy. Unlike standard TTA, the method requires an oracle (human or self-supervised) to label a small subset of high-entropy samples selected via incremental clustering, with the total number of label requests bounded by B.

## Method

SimATTA partitions each streaming batch into high-entropy samples (selected for active labeling via a novel incremental clustering algorithm based on weighted K-means that adaptively grows cluster centroids to cover new distributions) and low-entropy samples (pseudo-labeled by the frozen source model to approximate source-domain data). The key insight is that selective entropy minimization on low-entropy samples—rather than on all test data—prevents catastrophic forgetting, while active labels on high-entropy samples drive adaptation to new domains. The training objective is a weighted cross-entropy loss over both sample pools, with weights approximately matching sample proportions (w ≈ λ) as derived from VC-dimension-based generalization bounds. Theoretical analysis (Theorem 1–2, Corollary 3–4) proves that incorporating budgeted active labels strictly tightens the test-domain error bound compared to pure TTA, and that including source-like low-entropy samples conditionally reduces the source-domain error bound, providing the first learning-theory guarantees for the ATTA setting.

## Results

On PACS (domain-wise stream, post-adaptation), SimATTA with budget B≤300 achieves 98.80% (P), 84.47% (A), 82.25% (C), and 81.52% (S) accuracy, substantially outperforming the best TTA baseline CoTTA (98.62/65.48/63.10/53.78) by 19+ points on target domains while maintaining source accuracy. On VLCS, SimATTA (B≤300) reaches 99.93/69.50/66.67/77.34, exceeding the best TTA baseline (Tent step=1: 84.73/43.86/33.61/53.11) by 25–44 points on target domains. Against ADA baselines with full target dataset access, SimATTA (B≤300) achieves 98.89/84.69/83.09/83.76 on PACS, matching or exceeding CLUE (96.69/83.97/84.77/86.91) while using only streaming data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SimATTA decisively outperforms all four TTA baselines (Tent, EATA, CoTTA, SAR) on both PACS and VLCS, with target-domain gains of 19–44 points over the best TTA method (e.g., 84.47% vs. 66.85% on PACS-A; 77.34% vs. 53.11% on VLCS-V). Tent with 10 steps suffers severe catastrophic forgetting, dropping to 13.59% on PACS-S, while SimATTA maintains 81.52%. Against ADA baselines that have access to the full pre-collected target dataset, SimATTA (B≤300) achieves 98.89/84.69/83.09/83.76 on PACS, beating Random (96.21/81.19/80.75/84.34) and Kmeans (93.71/79.31/79.64/83.92) while being competitive with CLUE (96.69/83.97/84.77/86.91) and Entropy (96.31/88.00/82.48/80.55). On VLCS, SimATTA (99.93/69.47/69.57/78.87) outperforms all ADA baselines including Entropy (97.74/69.29/69.25/75.26) and Kmeans (98.61/67.57/70.77/74.49). The key distinction is that SimATTA achieves these results using only streaming data with a budget of ≤300 labels, whereas ADA methods can repeatedly query the entire target dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fully test-time adaptation (FTTA) line of work (Tent, EATA, CoTTA, SAR) by formally integrating active learning into the streaming test-time setting, creating the new ATTA problem formulation. It departs from standard FTTA's reliance on unsupervised entropy minimization by showing that a small budget of actively selected labels can theoretically and empirically close the gap to active domain adaptation (ADA) methods, while remaining source-free and real-time. The theoretical analysis builds on the HΔH-distance framework of Ben-David et al. (2010) and VC-dimension theory, while the algorithm repurposes entropy minimization—typically a cause of catastrophic forgetting—as a mechanism to identify source-like samples that prevent forgetting. The paper explicitly positions itself as distinct from active online learning (Saran et al., 2023), which resets models each round, and from SFADA methods like SALAD, which require model gradients and pre-collected target data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical guarantee that incorporating budgeted active labels during test-time adaptation strictly reduces the generalization error bound compared to pure unsupervised TTA, giving practitioners a principled justification for the ATTA setting. A key open question is the practical availability of the labeling oracle: while the paper discusses scenarios like autonomous driving and chatbots, the assumption of timely human or self-supervised labels remains a significant constraint. The reliance on pre-trained model quality for low-entropy sample selection (as a proxy for source data) is a vulnerability—if the pre-trained model has systematic errors, training on those low-entropy samples may reinforce them. Future work on scaling ATTA to large language models and on alternative CF-prevention mechanisms beyond selective entropy minimization are identified as important directions.
