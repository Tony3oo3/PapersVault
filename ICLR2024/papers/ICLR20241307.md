## Problem

Uncertainty-based active learning relies on quantifying how close a sample is to the decision boundary, but computing this distance is intractable for multiclass deep networks. Existing proxies (entropy, margin, BALD) either lack rigorous theoretical grounding or incur prohibitive computational cost, leaving a gap between the ideal margin-based criterion and practical implementations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241307/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pool of unlabeled samples (28×28 grayscale MNIST, 32×32 RGB CIFAR-10/100/SVHN, 64×64 Tiny ImageNet, 224×224 FOOD-101/ImageNet, or tabular OpenML features) together with the parameters of a trained deep network. It outputs a batch of q samples to query, selected via a k-means++-style seeding procedure that balances LDM-based uncertainty scores with cosine-distance diversity in the last-layer feature space. The LDM score itself is a scalar in [0,1] computed per sample through Monte-Carlo evaluation of the disagree metric over Gaussian-perturbed hypotheses.

## Method

The paper defines the Least Disagree Metric (LDM) as the infimum of the disagree metric ρ(h,g) over all hypotheses h that flip the prediction at x₀, providing a principled measure of how easily a sample's label can be flip-flopped by a minimal decision-boundary perturbation. An asymptotically consistent estimator L_{N,M} is derived under mild assumptions (Polish hypothesis space, Lipschitz disagree metric, coverage assumption), approximating ρ via M Monte-Carlo samples and the infimum via N sampled hypotheses. In practice, the hypothesis set is constructed by drawing Gaussian perturbations w ~ N(v, Iσ²) around the SGD-learned parameters v, with multiple variance scales σ_k to balance the probability of obtaining a disagreeing hypothesis against the scale of ρ. The LDM-S algorithm then selects a diverse batch using a modified k-means++ seeding: the first sample is the argmin LDM, and subsequent samples are drawn with probability proportional to γ_x · min_{x'∈Q} d_cos(z_x, z_x')², where γ_x is an exponentially decaying weight that partitions the pool into top-q LDM and remainder groups with equal total mass.

## Results

LDM-S achieves the best or second-best performance across all 10 datasets (3 OpenML, 6 image benchmarks, and ImageNet), with performance measured as mean test accuracy improvement over Random sampling. In the Dolan-Moré performance profile, LDM-S attains R(0) = 35% (i.e., it is the best algorithm on 35% of all experimental runs), significantly exceeding all other methods (all below 15%). On CIFAR-10, LDM-S yields +1.34% over Random versus +0.00% for Entropy and +0.43% for Margin; on ImageNet, LDM-S achieves +0.96% versus −0.21% for Entropy. Runtime is comparable to Entropy, with only a 3–6% increase on most image datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LDM-S outperforms all 8 baselines in the Dolan-Moré profile and penalty matrix across the full suite of 10 datasets. Entropy-based sampling is the weakest baseline, underperforming Random on OML#6 (−2.46%), OML#156 (−1.29%), CIFAR-10 (0.00%), Tiny ImageNet (−0.61%), FOOD-101 (−0.86%), and ImageNet (−0.21%). Margin-based sampling is competitive on smaller datasets (e.g., +4.11% on OML#6) but degrades on larger ones (e.g., +0.14% on ImageNet). Coreset selection is particularly poor on OML#156 (−19.53%) and CIFAR-10 (−3.71%). DBAL (MC-dropout + BALD) is competitive on SVHN (+2.46%) but fails on OML#156 (−14.40%). ENS (ensemble variation ratio) is competitive on OML#6 (+4.43%) but underperforms on most image datasets. BADGE and BAIT are competitive on MNIST (+3.01% and +3.37% respectively, close to LDM-S's +3.33%) but fall behind on CIFAR-10 (+0.90% and +0.60% vs. LDM-S's +1.34%) and ImageNet (+0.71% vs. +0.96%). LDM-S matches or exceeds all baselines on every dataset where it is evaluated, with statistically significant margins (p < 0.05) in the majority of comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

The LDM is a per-sample generalization of Hanneke's (2014) disagreement metric, which measures the probability of label disagreement between two hypotheses over the entire input distribution; here, the infimum is taken over all hypotheses that flip the prediction at a specific point x₀. For batch diversity, the paper adapts k-means++ seeding (Arthur & Vassilvitskii, 2007) with an EXP3-inspired exponentially decaying weight that balances LDM uncertainty against feature-space distance. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant Euclidean-distance-to-decision-boundary paradigm (margin-based, adversarial-perturbation approaches) by reframing 'closeness to the boundary' as 'ease of label flip-flop under parameter perturbation,' and grounds this in the Bayesian interpretation of SGD (Bernstein-von Mises) to justify Gaussian sampling around the learned parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides one of the few uncertainty measures for deep active learning with a formal asymptotic consistency guarantee, addressing a long-standing gap where most competing measures (entropy, margin, BALD, BADGE) rely on heuristic approximations without theoretical backing. Practically, LDM-S matches the speed of entropy-based sampling (3–6% overhead) while consistently achieving the best or near-best performance across 10 datasets and 5 architectures, making it a drop-in replacement for existing pipelines. Open questions include obtaining a rigorous sample complexity bound for LDM-S, replacing the ad-hoc multi-σ Gaussian schedule with a principled posterior sampler, and understanding whether the consistency guarantee extends beyond the 2D linear-classifier setting where it is fully proven.
