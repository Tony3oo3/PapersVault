## Problem

Diffusion models trained on long-tailed data suffer from degraded tail-class generation due to mode collapse and lack of diversity. Existing calibration methods like CBDM rely on the model's own conditional predictions to transfer knowledge from head to tail classes, introducing training bias and reducing robustness over long training runs. The paper asks whether a direct, data-driven score calibration from head to tail samples can replace this model-prediction-dependent augmentation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241156/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes mini-batches of RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet, 256×256 for ImageNet) with class labels, re-sampled with balanced class proportions. During inference, it takes Gaussian noise (and an optional class label for conditional generation) and produces 50k synthetic images via a 100-step DDIM sampler. A key operational detail is that the denoising target for each noisy sample can be swapped to a different clean sample within the same batch, governed by a Gaussian-kernel similarity score and a label-probability boundary condition.

## Method

The paper reformulates denoising score matching as a one-to-many distributional matching problem, where the score for a noisy sample x_t is estimated as a weighted average over multiple clean targets in a reference batch, with weights determined by a Gaussian kernel and an adjustable label-probability factor q(y_0)^β. The T2H (noisy Tail to clean Head) operation selectively replaces the denoising target of a noisy tail sample with a clean head sample from the batch when the head's label probability exceeds the tail's, effectively injecting head-class diversity into tail-class score estimation. A Batch Re-sample strategy equalizes class appearance probabilities in the training batch, which Proposition 3.1 shows is necessary to overcome the exponentially small transfer strength imposed by the product of head and tail label probabilities. The transfer is restricted to intermediate diffusion steps (t ≈ 500–800) where score variance peaks and semantic formation occurs, and the approach is validated to be equivalent to setting β=1 in the continuous score-mixing formulation.

## Results

On CIFAR10LT (imb=0.01), T2H achieves a FID of 6.89 and IS of 9.75, outperforming CBDM (FID 7.27, IS 9.37) and base DDPM (FID 10.72, IS 9.37). Under a more extreme imbalance (imb=0.001), T2H reaches FID 11.56, improving over CBDM by more than 1.0 FID points (12.71). On CIFAR100LT, T2H attains FID 6.68 and IS 12.94, surpassing CBDM (FID 7.82, IS 12.40) and base DDPM (FID 10.25, IS 12.96).

## Baselines & Comparisons

T2H consistently outperforms all four baselines on FID across every dataset and imbalance setting. Against the closest diffusion-based competitor CBDM, T2H improves FID by 0.38 on CIFAR10LT (imb=0.01), 1.15 on CIFAR10LT (imb=0.001), 1.14 on CIFAR100LT, 0.26 on TinyImageNet200LT, and 2.70 on ImageNetLT. Against base DDPM, the FID gains are larger (3.83 on CIFAR10LT, 3.44 on CIFAR100LT), reflecting the substantial benefit of oriented calibration over unmodified denoising. On IS, T2H also edges out CBDM on all datasets (e.g., 9.75 vs 9.37 on CIFAR10LT, 12.94 vs 12.40 on CIFAR100LT), while the GAN-based baselines (CBGAN, gSR-GAN) are substantially weaker on both metrics, with CBGAN achieving FID 37.23 on CIFAR10LT. The advantage of T2H over CBDM grows with increasing imbalance, suggesting the data-driven score transfer is more robust than CBDM's reliance on the model's own conditional predictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CBDM (Qin et al., 2023), which regularizes tail-class scores by aligning them with head-class model predictions during training. The key departure is replacing this prediction-based calibration with a Bayesian weighted denoising score-matching framework that selects clean head samples as denoising targets for noisy tail samples (T2H), thereby bypassing the model's conditional capacity entirely. The multi-target score estimation perspective from Xu et al. (2023) motivates the formulation, and the Batch Re-sample strategy addresses the head-class dominance in the reference batch that Proposition 3.1 shows constrains transfer strength. The method also connects to the broader long-tail GAN literature (CBGAN, gSR-GAN) by providing a diffusion-native alternative that does not depend on GAN-specific architectural choices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in providing a more robust and simpler training strategy for long-tail diffusion generation that does not require the model to have learned strong conditional representations before knowledge transfer can occur—addressing a chicken-and-egg problem inherent in CBDM. The training stability results (Figure 6) showing that T2H suppresses the overfitting degradation seen in CBDM over long training runs is practically important for large-scale generation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multimodal long-tail settings (acknowledged in the paper's future work), how it interacts with classifier-free guidance at inference, and whether the Gaussian-kernel similarity assumption holds well for high-dimensional or structured data beyond natural images.
