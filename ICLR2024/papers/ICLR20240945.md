## Problem

Label-free Concept Bottleneck Models (CBMs) rely on GPT-3 for concept set generation and CLIP for concept activation, introducing inherent instability: small perturbations to concept words, text embeddings, or input images cause large shifts in concept weights and predictions, undermining their faithfulness as explanatory tools. No prior work provides a formal definition of what makes a concept 'faithful' in the vision-language setting or a method to enforce such faithfulness.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (32×32 for CIFAR, variable for CUB and Places365) and a GPT-3-generated concept set (ranging from 128 concepts for CIFAR-10 to 2202 for Places365) as input. It produces two outputs: a class prediction probability vector and a concept weight vector of dimension k (one weight per concept in the set). The concept set is encoded via CLIP's text encoder to form a concept activation matrix, while the image is processed by a frozen backbone (CLIP RN50, ResNet-18, or ResNet-50) to extract features that are projected into concept space.

## Method

The paper defines a Faithful Vision-Language Concept (FVLC) via four formal properties: top-k overlap similarity with the vanilla concept, top-k overlap stability under concept-space perturbations, KL-divergence closeness of predictions, and prediction stability under perturbations. To construct the FVLC, they formulate a min-max optimization that minimizes KL divergence between FVLC and vanilla CBM predictions while enforcing the other three constraints as regularizers, using a novel ℓ₁-norm surrogate loss on top-k entries to handle the non-differentiable top-k overlap function. The inner maximization over perturbation radii R₁ and R₂ is solved via PGD (inspired by Madry et al.), while the outer minimization over the concept projection matrix W̃_c uses standard SGD. The image encoder and backbone are frozen; only the concept bottleneck layer weights are optimized, allowing seamless integration with the existing Label-free CBM pipeline without architectural changes.

## Results

On the four benchmark datasets, FVLC maintains accuracy within ~0.5% of the vanilla Label-free CBM (e.g., 86.32% → 86.70% on CIFAR-10, 65.42% → 65.14% on CIFAR-100) even under the most challenging combined perturbation (10% word replacement + word embedding noise + input noise). In terms of stability, FVLC achieves an average ~90% reduction in both TCPC (Total Concept Perturbation Change) and TOPC (Total Output Perturbation Change) compared to the baseline Label-free CBM. For example, under WP1(10%)+WP2+IP on CIFAR-10, TCPC drops from 1.36E-01 (base) to 1.43E-02 (FVLC), and TOPC drops from 1.05E-01 to 1.11E-02.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FVLC is compared against two baselines: the Label-free CBM (Oikarinen et al., 2023) and P-CBM (Yuksekgonul et al., 2022). Against P-CBM, FVLC (built on Label-free CBM) substantially outperforms on accuracy: 86.32% vs. 84.50% on CIFAR-10 and 65.42% vs. 56.00% on CIFAR-100, with P-CBM not evaluated on CUB or Places365. Against the Label-free CBM baseline under perturbations, FVLC matches or slightly exceeds accuracy (e.g., 86.70% vs. 85.96% on CIFAR-10 under combined WP1(10%)+WP2+IP) while achieving a dramatic ~90% average reduction in both TCPC and TOPC stability metrics. Under word embedding perturbation (WP2) on Places365, FVLC actually improves accuracy from 43.54% to 44.55%, suggesting the faithfulness regularization acts as a beneficial regularizer. The ablation study (Table 3) confirms that all three regularizers (L2, L3, L4) contribute, with L4 (stability of explanation) providing the largest single gain in TCPC reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Label-free CBM framework (Oikarinen et al., 2023), which itself builds on the original Concept Bottleneck Model (Koh et al., 2020), by identifying and formally addressing the instability introduced by GPT-3-based concept generation and input perturbations. Rather than proposing a new architecture, it introduces a min-max optimization over the concept bottleneck layer weights (W̃c) with a surrogate loss for the non-differentiable top-k overlap function, drawing on PGD-style inner-loop optimization from adversarial robustness literature. The key conceptual departure from adversarial robustness is the dual requirement: the method must preserve both prediction stability AND interpretability stability (top-k concept ordering), which the authors argue cannot be achieved by standard adversarial training alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first formal definition (FVLC) with four verifiable properties for what constitutes a 'faithful' concept in vision-language interpretation, filling a gap where label-free CBMs were widely adopted without stability guarantees. The ~90% average reduction in concept and output perturbation change across four datasets and three repeated concept spaces is well-supported by ablations, making this a practical tool for deploying interpretable models in high-stakes domains like healthcare. Limitations include reliance on GPT-3 for concept generation (the instability source is mitigated but not eliminated), small perturbation radii (σ=0.001), and additional computational cost from PGD iterations. Open questions include scalability to larger VLMs, whether the approach generalizes to non-CLIP backbones, and how it performs under more aggressive or semantically meaningful perturbations.
