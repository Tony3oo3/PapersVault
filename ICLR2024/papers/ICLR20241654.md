## Problem

GFlowNets excel at generating diverse samples proportional to rewards but often fail to consistently reach high-reward regions because their from-scratch trajectory generation over-explores the vast search space. This over-exploration hinders training efficiency since GFlowNets rely heavily on self-generated experiential data. The paper asks whether augmenting GFlowNet training with local search in object space can bias samples toward high-reward solutions without sacrificing diversity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of building blocks (12–18 blocks with 2 stems for molecules; nucleotide alphabets for sequences) and a pre-trained reward proxy model (e.g., MXMNet for HOMO-LUMO gap, binding affinity predictors for sEH/TFBind8/RNA tasks). It outputs complete molecule graphs (5–6 blocks) or nucleotide sequences (length 8 or 14) constructed via prepend-append MDPs. Crucially, the local search operates on full trajectories (action sequences) rather than final objects, backtracking K steps and reconstructing K steps, so the intermediate partial states are part of the refinement process.

## Method

LS-GFN augments standard GFlowNet training with a three-step iterative loop: (A) sample M complete trajectories from the forward policy P_F; (B) refine each trajectory for I iterations by backtracking K steps using the backward policy P_B and reconstructing K steps using P_F, then accepting or rejecting the refined trajectory via deterministic (greedy) or stochastic (Metropolis-Hastings) filtering; (C) train the GFlowNet on the accumulated dataset using trajectory balance with reward-prioritized replay. The key insight is that local search provides intra-mode exploitation while GFlowNet's from-scratch generation provides inter-mode exploration, and because the local search uses the same learned P_F and P_B, its capability co-evolves with the GFlowNet throughout training. The method is orthogonal to the choice of GFlowNet objective (TB, DB, SubTB, MaxEnt) and requires only a backward policy, which is already trained in standard GFlowNet formulations.

## Results

On the six biochemical benchmarks, TB+LS-GFN achieves 100% accuracy on QM9 and sEH, 97.05% on TFBind8, and dramatically improves RNA-binding accuracy from 33.47% (TB) to 87.28% (L14-RNA1), 21.88% to 56.63% (L14-RNA2), and 32.70% to 75.75% (L14-RNA3). In terms of mode discovery, TB+LS-GFN finds 5,228 modes on sEH (vs. 706 for TB) and 32 modes on L14-RNA1 (vs. 10 for TB), representing a 5–7× increase. The method also outperforms RL baselines (PPO, A2C, SQL) and the MARS sampler on both mode count and sample uniqueness across all tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against prior GFlowNet objectives (DB, MaxEnt, TB, SubTB, GTB), LS-GFN consistently improves accuracy, with the largest gains on RNA-binding tasks: TB+LS-GFN reaches 87.28% on L14-RNA1 versus 33.47% for TB alone, and 56.63% on L14-RNA2 versus 21.88%. On mode discovery, TB+LS-GFN finds 5,228 modes on sEH compared to 706 (TB) and 676 (MaxEnt), and 32 modes on L14-RNA1 versus 10 (TB) and 10 (MaxEnt). Against RL baselines (PPO, A2C, SQL) and the MARS sampler, LS-GFN outperforms all on both the number of modes discovered and sample uniqueness; RL methods tend to collapse into non-diverse local optima with high duplication rates, while LS-GFN maintains a unique fraction near 0.95–1.00. The method is orthogonal to the training objective: plugging LS-GFN into DB, MaxEnt, SubTB, or TB all yield improvements, though the magnitude varies (e.g., SubTB+LS-GFN shows only modest gains on TFBind8 at 76.54% vs. 76.53% for SubTB alone). Overall, LS-GFN wins or ties on every task-metric combination reported in the main tables.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GFlowNet training framework (Bengio et al., 2021; Malkin et al., 2022) by inserting a local search refinement step between trajectory sampling and loss minimization. It builds directly on Shen et al. (2023), adopting their prioritized replay training (PRT) and SSR edge-flow parametrization, and on Zhang et al. (2022b) for the backtracking/reconstruction mechanism and Metropolis-Hastings acceptance criterion. The key conceptual departure from prior GFlowNet work is the explicit separation of inter-mode exploration (forward-policy sampling from scratch) and intra-mode exploration (local search via backward-then-forward reconstruction), which prior methods addressed only implicitly through exploration hyperparameters or replay strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The local search augmentation is simple, orthogonal to the choice of GFlowNet training objective (TB, DB, MaxEnt, SubTB), and yields large, consistent gains in both accuracy and mode diversity across six biochemical benchmarks. A notable open question is the dependence on backward-policy quality: when the acceptance rate drops too low, the local search becomes ineffective, and the paper suggests but does not fully explore remedies like ε-greedy backward policies or dedicated backward-policy fine-tuning. Broader implications include whether this inter/intra-mode decomposition generalizes to other amortized sampling frameworks beyond GFlowNets, and whether the approach scales to larger combinatorial spaces where the backward policy may be harder to learn.
