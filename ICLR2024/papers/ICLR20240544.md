## Problem

Existing GAN-based entity alignment methods suffer from mode collapse and an incomplete objective that prevents them from generating new entities with concrete features (neighborhood, attributes, images). The paper provides a theoretical decomposition of the EEA objective into reconstruction, distribution-matching, and prediction-matching terms, showing that prior methods only partially exploit the generative signal, and proposes a framework that fully leverages all three terms to improve both alignment accuracy and entity synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240544/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-modal entity features from source and target KGs—relational graph triplets, attribute triplets, and constant pretrained image embeddings from a vision model—along with random noise vectors for unconditional synthesis. Outputs include per-modality sub-embeddings, a fused joint embedding, and (uniquely among EEA methods) decoded concrete features: binary neighborhood/attribute vectors of length |X| and nearest-neighbor image retrieval. The decoders produce probability distributions over all candidate entities or attributes rather than raw pixel data, since image features are fixed pretrained vectors.

## Method

GEEA introduces a Mutual Variational Autoencoder (M-VAE) with four encode-decode flows (x→x, y→y for self-supervised reconstruction; x→y, y→x for supervised mutual reconstruction) sharing parameters across all flows. Instead of a GAN discriminator, distribution matching is performed via KL divergence between per-modality latent variables and a fixed standard normal, which the paper proves (Proposition 2) indirectly aligns the two KG embedding distributions while avoiding mode collapse. Prior reconstruction uses per-modality sub-VAEs plus decoders to recover concrete features, while post reconstruction feeds reconstructed sub-embeddings through the EEA fusion layer with an MSE loss to ensure cross-modal consistency. The total loss jointly optimizes prediction matching (negative sampling), distribution matching, prior reconstruction, and post reconstruction, and the paper proves (Proposition 1) that maximizing the generative terms necessarily minimizes the EEA prediction objective.

## Results

On the DBP15K multi-modal benchmarks, GEEA achieves state-of-the-art Hits@1 of 76.1% (ZH-EN), 75.5% (JA-EN), and 77.6% (FR-EN), with corresponding MRR scores of 82.7%, 82.7%, and 84.4%. On the more challenging FB15K-DB15K and FB15K-YAGO15K datasets, GEEA reaches Hits@1 of 34.3% and 29.8% respectively, outperforming the best baseline MCLEA by 4.8 and 4.4 points. GEEA also excels under low-data regimes, surpassing the second-best method by 36.1% in Hits@1 when only 10% of aligned pairs are available for training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GEEA outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the closest competitor NeoEA(MCLEA) (a GAN-based method), GEEA gains 3.8–5.9 points in Hits@1 and 3.1–5.7 points in MRR on DBP15K, and 4.8–4.4 points in Hits@1 on FB15K-DB15K and FB15K-YAGO15K. The multi-modal baselines EVA and MCLEA are surpassed by 4.6–9.3 and 4.6–6.5 points in Hits@1 respectively on DBP15K, confirming that the generative objectives (prior/post reconstruction and distribution matching) provide substantial gains over purely discriminative or contrastive training. Single-modal methods (MUGNN, AliNet, decentRL) are outperformed by 17–28 points in Hits@1, highlighting the importance of multi-modal information. On the single-modal OpenEA 100K benchmark, GEEA(SEA) improves over NeoEA(SEA) by 1.5–2.8 points in Hits@1, and over SEA by 4.4–10.7 points. For dangling entity detection on DBP2.0, incorporating GEEA as the embedding backbone improves F1 by 0.9–1.9 points over the original Sun et al. (2021) methods across all three detection strategies (NNC, MR, BR).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repositions embedding-based entity alignment (EEA) within the generative modeling framework, building directly on VAE (Kingma & Welling, 2013) and using MCLEA (Lin et al., 2022) as the encoder backbone. It departs from prior GAN-based EEA methods (Pei et al., 2019a,b; Guo et al., 2022b) by identifying their mode collapse and inability to produce concrete features, replacing the discriminator with a mutual VAE (M-VAE) that supports four encode-decode flows. The theoretical contribution is an ELBO decomposition showing that reconstruction and distribution-matching objectives jointly minimize the EEA prediction-matching term, providing a principled justification that prior discriminative methods lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces entity synthesis (both conditional and unconditional) as a new capability for knowledge graphs, enabling generation of concrete features (neighborhoods, attributes, images) for dangling entities or from random noise — a task no prior EEA method addressed. This has practical implications for knowledge integration, fact checking, and virtual world generation (e.g., NPC design in games). Limitations include higher parameter counts, degraded performance on cross-KG benchmarks (FB15K) versus cross-lingual ones (DBP15K), and the ablation revealing that the explicit prediction loss remains critical (performance collapses to near-random without it), which tempers the narrative that generative objectives alone suffice. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger KGs and whether new encoders can directly generate raw data rather than relying on pretrained image embeddings.
