## Problem

Existing multimodal intent recognition datasets are limited in scale, restricted to single-turn dialogues, and lack out-of-scope (OOS) samples that naturally arise in real-world open-ended conversations. MIntRec (2022), the first multimodal intent dataset, contains only 2,224 single-turn utterances with 20 intent classes and no OOS annotations, making it insufficient for studying multi-party, multi-turn conversational intent understanding and system robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241811/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample consists of a text utterance (avg. 7.9 words, max 46), a video clip (avg. 3.0s, max 19.9s) segmented at the speaker's region of interest, and a corresponding audio waveform (16 kHz). For multi-turn settings, the system additionally retrieves all prior utterances from the same speaker within the dialogue as context, concatenated with the current utterance using segment embeddings (text) or zero-vector separators (video/audio). The output is a 30-class fine-grained intent label (under two coarse categories: Express Emotions/Attitudes and Achieve Goals) combined with a binary in-scope/out-of-scope decision determined at inference via per-class probability thresholds.

## Method

The framework extracts modality-specific features using BERT_LARGE (1024-d), Mask R-CNN with Swin Transformer backbone for frame-level RoI video features (256-d), and WavLM for audio embeddings (768-d). For multi-turn dialogues, speaker-specific context is incorporated by concatenating prior utterance embeddings with the current turn, using segment embeddings for text and 1-D zero vectors for nonverbal modalities. Multimodal fusion is performed via MAG-BERT (adaptation gate) or MulT (cross-modal attention) to capture cross-modal interactions. Training combines cross-entropy loss on in-scope samples with outlier exposure (OE) loss on OOS samples (uniform soft targets over K classes), plus the fusion-specific loss. At inference, the DOC open-world classifier assigns per-class thresholds from a half-Gaussian fit on training probabilities; a sample is flagged OOS if its maximum class probability falls below the corresponding threshold, otherwise it receives the argmax class label.

## Results

On the MIntRec2.0 dataset (15,040 utterances, 30 intent classes + OOS), the best multimodal fusion method (MAG-BERT) achieves 55.17% F1 and 60.58% ACC for in-scope classification in the single-turn w/o OOS setting, outperforming the BERT_LARGE text baseline (51.60% F1, 59.30% ACC) by 3.57% F1. With OOS training, MAG-BERT reaches 62.47% F1-OOS for out-of-scope detection. In the multi-turn context setting, improvements from multimodality shrink to ~0.3–1.0% F1, indicating context modeling remains underexploited. Humans with 100 dialogues of prior knowledge achieve 71.03% ACC and 75.63% WF1, a >30% absolute gap over ChatGPT (35.27% ACC zero-shot) and >60% over the few-shot MAG-BERT baseline (9.82% ACC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAG-BERT (Rahman et al., 2020) and MulT (Tsai et al., 2019) serve as the two multimodal fusion baselines; both consistently outperform the BERT_LARGE text-only baseline by 1–4% F1 on in-scope classification and by 3–8% F1-OOS on out-of-scope detection in the single-turn w/o OOS setting, confirming that nonverbal modalities carry complementary intent signal. In the multi-turn context setting, however, the multimodal advantage narrows to ~0.3–1.0% F1, and MulT even shows a slight F1-OOS decrease (62.93 vs. 63.56 for text), suggesting cross-modal fusion does not yet effectively exploit conversational context. DialogueRNN (Majumder et al., 2019), tested as a temporal context model in Appendix C, collapses to near-chance performance (F1 ≈ 0.67–2.75), confirming that directly modeling temporal context with fused multimodal representations is ineffective for this task. ChatGPT (zero-shot and 10-dialogue few-shot) achieves 35.27% and 34.53% ACC respectively, far below humans-10 (64.34% ACC) and humans-100 (71.03% ACC), revealing a >30% absolute gap between LLMs and humans on this high-level cognitive task. The NLP open-intent baselines DA-ADB (Zhang et al., 2023b) and KNNCL (Zhou et al., 2022) underperform the BERT_LARGE text classifier on most in-scope metrics but achieve slightly higher F1-OOS scores, indicating they are better calibrated for rejection but weaker at fine-grained intent discrimination.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends MIntRec (Zhang et al., 2022), the first multimodal intent recognition dataset, by scaling from 2,224 to 15,040 utterances, expanding the intent taxonomy from 20 to 30 fine-grained classes, and introducing three critical dimensions absent in the predecessor: multi-turn dialogue context, multi-party speaker identity, and out-of-scope (OOS) detection. For the benchmark framework, it repurposes well-established components—MAG-BERT and MulT for cross-modal fusion, Outlier Exposure for OOS training, and DOC for threshold-based OOS inference—rather than proposing new fusion architectures. The paper also positions itself against the broader landscape of intent datasets (ATIS, SNIPS, CLINC150) and multimodal conversation datasets (MELD, IEMOCAP), noting that none simultaneously provide multimodal signals, multi-turn structure, multi-party annotation, and OOS labels at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MIntRec2.0 fills a critical gap as the first large-scale benchmark combining multimodal signals, multi-turn context, multi-party speakers, and OOS detection, making it a likely standard reference for multimodal conversational intent research. The finding that humans achieve 71% accuracy with only 7% of training data while the best multimodal fusion method reaches ~60% highlights a substantial and persistent gap in machine understanding of high-level intent. A notable negative result is that DialogueRNN, a purpose-built method for multimodal temporal context modeling, performs near-randomly, suggesting that simply fusing multimodal representations over time is insufficient and that effective context integration remains an open problem. The 30%+ absolute gap between ChatGPT (text-only) and humans also underscores that current LLMs cannot substitute for genuine multimodal intent understanding, pointing to future work on integrating nonverbal cues into large language models.
