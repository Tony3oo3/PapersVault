## Problem

Causal discovery from observational data remains difficult: purely data-driven algorithms (NOTEARS, DAG-GNN) are ineffective on real-world datasets, while LLM-based metadata reasoning lacks grounding in empirical evidence. Deep Structural Causal Models (DSCMs) enable rich counterfactual inference but require the causal graph to be known a priori and cannot handle unmeasured confounding. No prior framework unifies LLM-based hypothesis generation with deep probabilistic causal modelling in an iterative, self-correcting loop.

## Input / Output

The CMA takes as input a dataset D (scalar variables and/or 2D brain MRI slices from ADNI) along with variable names and domain metadata encoded in the LLM's training corpus. It produces a trained DSCM/DCGM parameterised by conditional normalizing flows and CVAEs, a causal graph encoded as a JSON file (nodes with parent arrays, optionally with chain-component annotations for associative links), and a persistent 'memory' text file tracking graph amendments and their impact on data likelihood across iterations. The trained model supports atomic interventions and counterfactual queries in both the scalar and imaging spaces.

## Method

The CMA iterates four stages: (1) hypothesis generation, where GPT-4 proposes an initial DAG from variable names; (2) model fitting, where a Modelling Engine constructs a DSCM or Deep Chain Graph Model via Kahn's topological sorting to order module construction, then maximises data likelihood using conditional rational-spline normalizing flows (for singleton nodes) and multivariate normalizing flows (for chain components), with CVAE recognition models for high-dimensional image variables; (3) post-processing, where the LLM compares successive graphs and likelihoods to generate a textual 'memory'; and (4) hypothesis amendment, split into a global phase (LLM critiques the whole graph) and a local phase (pairwise edge assessment/adjustment with pre-computed probability outputs, optionally augmented via RAG with domain-specific documents). The key architectural novelty is the iterative coupling where the LLM acts simultaneously as prior, critic, and post-processor over DSCM training, plus a novel parameterisation of chain graphs with deep learning elements to represent unmeasured confounding while remaining compatible with the abduction-action-prediction counterfactual procedure.

## Results

The CMA achieves the best (lowest) NHD/BHD ratio across all three causal discovery benchmarks: 0.46 on Arctic Sea Ice (12 nodes, 48 true edges), 0.21 on the Alzheimer's Disease benchmark (11 nodes, 19 true edges), and 0.63 on Sangiovese (15 nodes, 55 true edges). On the AD benchmark, the CMA predicts only 16 edges with an NHD of 0.07, substantially outperforming GPT-4 (23 edges, NHD 0.11) and all data-driven methods. In the synthetic neuropathic protein experiment, the correctly specified DAG achieves a mean data likelihood of 4402.68 ± 26.70, significantly higher than misspecified graphs (3857.53 and 3757.55), validating the data-driven module's reward signal up to the Markov equivalence class.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The CMA outperforms all four baseline families across all three benchmarks. Against data-driven methods, the CMA's ratio of 0.46 on Arctic Sea Ice beats DAG-GNN (Temporal) at 0.74 and NOTEARS at 0.82; on Sangiovese, the CMA (0.63) substantially outperforms NOTEARS (0.96) and DAG-GNN (0.79). Against metadata-driven (LLM-only) baselines, the CMA improves over GPT-4 by 0.22 ratio points on Arctic Sea Ice (0.46 vs. 0.68), 0.06 on AD (0.21 vs. 0.27), and 0.02 on Sangiovese (0.63 vs. 0.65). Notably, the CMA also proposes edges absent from the ground-truth graphs (e.g., LW→HFLX in Arctic Sea Ice, biological sex→tau pathology in AD), suggesting it can detect missing causal relationships that neither pure data-driven nor pure metadata-driven methods capture. The ADNI case study further demonstrates the CMA's unique ability to perform counterfactual inference in the imaging space, a task infeasible for all baseline methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work: Deep Structural Causal Models (DSCMs) for data-driven causal modelling (Pawlowski et al., 2020) and LLM-based metadata reasoning for causal graph construction (Kiciman et al., 2023; Long et al., 2023a,b). Prior LLM-for-causality work used language models in a single stage (as a prior, a post-processor, or a critic), while prior DSCMs required the causal graph to be known a priori and assumed no unmeasured confounding. The CMA departs from both by placing the LLM in an iterative loop as prior, critic, and post-processor over DSCM training, and by generalising the DSCM to a Deep Chain Graph Model (DCGM) that parameterises associative (non-causal) links with conditional multivariate normalizing flows to account for unmeasured confounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CMA is the first framework to jointly perform causal discovery and counterfactual inference over multi-modal data (tabular variables plus medical images), which neither pure data-driven algorithms nor standalone LLMs can achieve. The real-world AD application yields biologically plausible counterfactuals and surfaces edges (e.g., biological sex → tau pathology via USP11; sTREM2 → tau pathology) that were absent from expert consensus, suggesting the framework can identify gaps in domain knowledge. Key limitations remain: LLMs struggle to fully automate chain-graph representation (requiring human intervention for bidirectional edges), the imaging component uses 2D slices rather than 3D volumes, and the Markovian DAG assumption restricts expressiveness for feedback-loop systems. Open questions include whether the iterative LLM–DSCM loop scales to larger variable sets and whether the DCGM parameterisation can be made fully end-to-end.
