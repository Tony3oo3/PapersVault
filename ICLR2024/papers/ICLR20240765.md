## Problem

While weight-space symmetries (permutation and scaling) are well-studied in the context of loss landscapes, their impact on the Bayesian posterior distribution of deep neural networks remained largely unexplored. In particular, the interaction between scaling symmetries and L2 regularization was subject to a widespread misconception that regularization effectively eliminates scaling redundancy, and no systematic framework existed to quantify how these symmetries inflate the posterior with functionally equivalent modes, degrading uncertainty estimates.

## Input / Output

The method consumes thousands of independently trained neural network checkpoints (ResNet-18, ResNet-20, OptuNet) on image classification tasks (MNIST 28×28 grayscale, CIFAR-100 32×32 RGB, TinyImageNet 64×64 RGB) as the reference posterior, alongside 100 samples from each approximate Bayesian method under comparison. Outputs include layer-wise MMD scores (using 20 Gaussian and Laplace kernels, reported as parameter-weighted medians), symmetry-removed weight tensors via a posteriori permutation sorting and norm normalization, and standard uncertainty metrics (AUPR, FPR95, ECE, ACE, Brier, mutual information). The MMD is computed on layer-wise marginals rather than the full high-dimensional posterior for tractability.

## Method

The paper develops a mathematical formalism (Proposition 1) expressing the Bayesian posterior as a continuous mixture over scaling parameters of a discrete mixture over permutation groups, cleanly separating the two symmetry types' effects. It then introduces the scaled-representation problem (minimizing L2 mass under scaling transformations), proving it is log-log strictly convex with a unique global minimum, and empirically demonstrating that SGD-trained networks with weight decay consistently fail to reach this minimum—challenging the prior belief that L2 regularization neutralizes scaling symmetries. For posterior quality assessment, the authors compute Maximum Mean Discrepancy using 20 diverse kernels (10 Gaussian, 10 Laplace) on layer-wise marginals, comparing Dropout, viBNN, SWAG, Laplace, SGHMC, pSGLD, and Deep Ensembles in both single-mode and multi-mode (10 independently trained models) settings. They also release the Checkpoints dataset containing thousands of real-world model weights to facilitate further exploration.

## Results

Across three image classification tasks (MNIST-OptuNet, CIFAR-100-ResNet-18, TinyImageNet-ResNet-18), multi-mode posterior estimation methods consistently achieve substantially lower MMD (posterior discrepancy) than their single-mode counterparts. On CIFAR-100, multi-mode Laplace reaches an MMD of 0.6% and multi-mode SWAG 5.0%, versus 5.7% and 6.7% for their single-mode versions, while Deep Ensembles (the reference) achieve 0.0%. For OOD detection, multi-mode SWAG attains the best AUPR on CIFAR-100 (89.1%) and TinyImageNet (96.5%), while DE achieves the lowest FPR95 on MNIST (12.8%) and CIFAR-100 (45.6%). The paper further demonstrates that removing weight-space symmetries (NS metric) reduces MMD by only a small margin, indicating that symmetries contribute to but do not fully explain posterior complexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks eight posterior estimation methods against a Deep Ensembles reference (1000 independently trained checkpoints). Multi-mode Laplace achieves the best non-DE posterior quality with MMD of 0.6‰ on CIFAR-100 and 0.5‰ on TinyImageNet, closely followed by multi-mode Dropout (0.7‰) and multi-mode SWAG (5.0‰ on CIFAR-100). Single-mode methods are uniformly worse: the best single-mode method (Laplace, 5.7‰ MMD on CIFAR-100) is roughly 10× further from the reference than multi-mode Laplace. For OOD detection, multi-mode SWAG leads in AUPR (89.1% on CIFAR-100, 96.5% on TinyImageNet), while DE achieves the best FPR95 on MNIST (12.8%) and CIFAR-100 (45.6%). HMC (Izmailov et al., 2021), evaluated in Appendix B on ResNet-20/CIFAR-10, achieves MMD/D of 2.3‰ versus DE's 0.0‰, and while it produces the highest OODMI (90.5%), its accuracy (90.3%) and Brier score (15.0) are inferior to DE (94.3%, 8.7). The paper concludes that posterior quality (MMD) correlates strongly with OOD detection performance but only weakly with aleatoric uncertainty metrics, and that removing permutation/scaling symmetries (NS) reduces MMD by only ~10–20%, suggesting symmetries are a contributing but not dominant source of posterior complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on weight-space symmetries in neural networks (Hecht-Nielsen, Neyshabur et al., Entezari et al.) from the loss-landscape perspective to the Bayesian posterior, developing the first mathematical formalism showing how permutation and scaling symmetries create redundant, functionally equivalent modes in the posterior distribution. It builds on the posterior estimation literature (HMC, variational inference, Laplace, SWAG, deep ensembles) but departs from the HMC-as-gold-standard framing of Izmailov et al. (2021) by arguing that independently trained checkpoints are a more scalable and empirically faithful reference for high-dimensional posteriors. The work also connects to Wiese et al. (2023), who showed symmetries could reduce posterior support for MCMC, but goes further by providing a full mixture-of-distributions decomposition (Proposition 1) and a convex optimization problem (the scaled-representation problem) that quantifies the residual effect of scaling symmetries under L2 regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first large-scale empirical exploration of BNN posteriors on real-world vision tasks (MNIST through TinyImageNet) and releases the Checkpoints dataset, a practical resource for the uncertainty-quantification community. The finding that HMC-based estimation violates the permutation-invariance corollary of Proposition 1 challenges the assumption that HMC faithfully samples the full symmetric posterior, with implications for how the field interprets MCMC-based BNN results. However, the authors acknowledge that the complexity of the posterior extends well beyond what weight-space symmetries explain, and the correlation between posterior estimation quality (MMD) and OOD detection performance remains imperfect, leaving open the question of what additional structure governs posterior quality. The scaled-representation problem, while theoretically clean, has limited practical impact since the mass gap between converged networks and the optimal scaled representation is small in absolute terms.
