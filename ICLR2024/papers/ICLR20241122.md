## Problem

Sparse mixture-of-experts (MoE) Transformers scale model capacity without proportional compute increases, but their discrete token-to-expert routing introduces training instability, token dropping, expert imbalance, and difficulty scaling the number of experts. These issues worsen in out-of-distribution settings such as small inference batches, novel inputs, and transfer learning, limiting the practical benefits of MoE architectures.

## Input / Output

The model takes RGB images (224×224 for pretraining on JFT-4B, 384×384 for ImageNet finetuning) and produces class labels for image classification. For the contrastive learning evaluation, the frozen vision tower produces image embeddings that are scored against text embeddings for zero-shot classification and image-text retrieval. Internally, images are split into patch tokens forming a sequence of m tokens of dimension d, which the Soft MoE layers process as weighted combinations rather than discrete assignments.

## Method

Soft MoE replaces the discrete router in sparse MoE layers with a fully differentiable soft assignment mechanism. For each of n·p slots, it computes a convex combination of all m input tokens using dispatch weights D (softmax over token indices of XΦ), processes each slot through its assigned expert (allocated round-robin), and then recovers output tokens as convex combinations of slot outputs using combine weights C (softmax over slot indices of XΦ). This eliminates token dropping, expert imbalance, and the need for sort/top-k operations, making the layer per-sequence deterministic and hardware-friendly. The total number of slots (not experts) determines computational cost, so increasing the number of experts adds parameters without increasing FLOPs. The Soft MoE blocks replace the second half of the Transformer's MLP layers, and L2 normalization with a trainable scale factor is applied to the routing logits to maintain stability at large model dimensions.

## Results

Soft MoE B/16 (3.7B params, 128 experts) matches ViT H/14 (669M params) on ImageNet 10-shot (82.9% vs 83.3%) and finetuning (88.5% vs 88.9%) while being 5.7× faster at inference (1.5 vs 8.6 ms/img) and 10× cheaper in FLOPs (32 vs 334 GFLOPS/img). Soft MoE L/16 (13.1B params) outperforms ViT H/14 on all three metrics — JFT P@1 (63.0% vs 59.7%), ImageNet 10-shot (84.3% vs 83.3%), and ImageNet finetuning (89.2% vs 88.9%) — while being ~2× faster at inference (4.8 vs 8.6 ms/img). On the training-cost/performance Pareto frontier, Soft MoE dominates both dense ViTs and sparse MoEs (Token Choice, Expert Choice) across all model sizes from S/32 to H/14.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the dense ViT baselines (Zhai et al., 2022), Soft MoE achieves a clear win on the efficiency frontier: Soft MoE B/16 with 5.5× more parameters than ViT H/14 matches its few-shot and finetuning accuracy while being 5.7× faster at inference, and Soft MoE L/16 outperforms ViT H/14 by 3.3 points on JFT P@1, 1.0 point on 10-shot, and 0.3 point on finetuning while being 1.8× faster. Against the Expert Choice sparse MoE baseline (Zhou et al., 2022), Soft MoE dominates on the training-cost/performance Pareto frontier (Figure 3), achieving higher accuracy at every FLOPs and wall-clock budget level; the paper attributes this to Soft MoE's immunity to token dropping and expert unbalance, which plague discrete routing. In the contrastive (LIT) setting, Soft MoE-L/16 outperforms ViT-L/16 by 1.1 points on ImageNet zero-shot (83.8% vs 82.7%) and 2.4 points on CIFAR-100 zero-shot (79.9% vs 77.5%), confirming that soft-routing representations transfer well to image-text alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the entire lineage of Sparse MoE routing (Token Choice, Expert Choice, GShard, Switch Transformers) by replacing the discrete token-to-expert assignment problem with a fully differentiable soft assignment computed via softmax-weighted averages of all input tokens. While prior MoE work treats routing as a combinatorial optimization requiring auxiliary losses, capacity constraints, and heuristic balancing, Soft MoE sidesteps these by making every slot a convex combination of all tokens, eliminating token dropping and expert unbalance by construction. The authors also draw conceptual parallels to multi-headed attention (experts as non-linear heads) and token-merging methods (Perceiver, etc.), but distinguish Soft MoE by its goal of scaling expert capacity rather than reducing sequence length.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Soft MoE offers a practical path to scaling model capacity (40× more parameters) with negligible inference overhead (2% increase), addressing the core motivation for MoEs while removing their well-known training pathologies. The finding that one slot per expert is optimal—rather than amortizing slots across experts—suggests expert diversity, not slot multiplicity, drives performance, which has implications for how future MoE architectures allocate parameters. Key open questions include whether Soft MoE can be adapted to autoregressive decoders (the token mixing breaks causality) and whether the memory cost of many experts becomes prohibitive at extreme scales. The positive transfer to contrastive image-text learning suggests the learned representations are broadly useful beyond classification.
