## Problem

Diffusion-based time series forecasting models suffer from instability due to their stochastic sampling, which can produce low-fidelity samples from low-density regions of the data manifold. Existing guidance methods (classifier or classifier-free) require external labels unavailable at inference time. The paper identifies that the forward diffusion process—gradually corrupting data toward noise—intuitively mirrors the smoothing of fine-grained time series into coarser representations, suggesting that intrinsic multi-granularity structure in the data itself can serve as a free guidance signal.

## Input / Output

The model takes a multivariate time series context window (e.g., 24 hours of 1-hour observations for Solar/Electricity/Traffic, 48 hours for KDD-cup, 30 days for Wikipedia) and internally generates coarse-grained versions via non-overlapping sliding-window averaging at multiple levels (e.g., 4h, 12h, 24h, 48h), each replicated to align with the original timeline. Output is a probabilistic forecast: samples from the conditional distribution of future finest-grained timesteps given history, with optional coarser-grained predictions. Each granularity level is encoded by a separate GRU, and the denoising network is shared across all levels.

## Method

MG-TSD introduces a multi-granularity guided diffusion loss where coarse-grained data at G−1 levels serve as targets at intermediate diffusion steps, constraining the sampling trajectory to preserve trend structure. The key mechanism is a partially shared variance schedule: for granularity g, the first N*_g diffusion steps have α=1 (no noise added), and the remaining N−N*_g steps reuse the finest-grained schedule, effectively 'injecting' the coarse target at step N*_g. The guidance loss for each granularity is derived as a variational lower bound on the log-likelihood of the coarse target at its corresponding intermediate step, reducing to a standard noise-prediction MSE with a shifted forward process. A heuristic share-ratio selection rule identifies N*_g as the diffusion step minimizing CRPS between the coarse target and intermediate samples from a pre-trained TimeGrad. Training uses a weighted sum of denoising losses across all granularities with a shared denoising network, and inference iteratively denoises from pure noise at the finest granularity while conditioning on per-level RNN hidden states.

## Results

MG-TSD achieves the lowest CRPS_sum across all six real-world datasets, with headline values of 0.3081 (Solar), 0.0149 (Electricity), 0.0323 (Traffic), 0.1837 (KDD-cup), 0.1159 (Taxi), and 0.0529 (Wikipedia). The most notable improvement is on KDD-cup, where MG-TSD reduces CRPS_sum from 0.2902 (TimeGrad) to 0.1837, a 37% relative reduction. On Electricity, MG-TSD achieves 0.0149 CRPS_sum versus 0.0232 for TimeGrad (36% relative improvement). The model also leads on NRMSE_sum and NMAE_sum across all datasets in the extended benchmark (Tables 4–5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MG-TSD outperforms all eight baseline families on every dataset and every metric (CRPS_sum, NRMSE_sum, NMAE_sum). Against the closest probabilistic diffusion baseline TimeGrad (Rasul et al., 2021), MG-TSD improves CRPS_sum by 7.6% on Solar (0.3081 vs. 0.3335), 35.8% on Electricity (0.0149 vs. 0.0232), 22.0% on Traffic (0.0323 vs. 0.0414), 36.7% on KDD-cup (0.1837 vs. 0.2902), 7.7% on Taxi (0.1159 vs. 0.1255), and 4.7% on Wikipedia (0.0529 vs. 0.0555). The normalizing-flow baselines (LSTM-MAF and Transformer-MAF from Rasul et al., 2020) and the Gaussian copula baselines (GP-Copula, GP-Scaling, Vec-LSTM from Salinas et al., 2019) are consistently outperformed by larger margins, with GP-Copula trailing by 14.7% on Solar and 52.8% on KDD-cup. TACTiS (Drouin et al., 2022) is notably weaker, especially on KDD-cup (0.5406 vs. 0.1837) and Traffic (0.1093 vs. 0.0323). Among the deterministic/Transformer baselines evaluated in the extended tables, Autoformer (Wu et al., 2021) and PatchTST (Nie et al., 2022) are outperformed on all datasets; the other diffusion-based baselines D3VAE (Li et al., 2022) and TimeDiff (Shen & Kwok, 2023) show substantially higher errors, with TimeDiff reaching 1.5985 NRMSE_sum on Solar versus 0.6178 for MG-TSD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends TimeGrad (Rasul et al., 2021), which first applied conditional denoising diffusion models to multivariate probabilistic time series forecasting with an RNN encoder, by introducing a multi-granularity guidance mechanism that constrains intermediate diffusion states. The guidance concept draws from classifier guidance and classifier-free guidance in image diffusion (Nichol et al., 2021; Ho & Salimans, 2022), but departs from those methods by using the data's own coarse-grained structure—obtained via sliding-window smoothing—as the guidance signal, eliminating the need for external labels unavailable at inference. The key conceptual departure is the observation that the forward diffusion process (gradual noise injection) is structurally analogous to the smoothing process from fine-grained to coarse-grained representations, enabling a principled mapping between diffusion steps and granularity levels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical limitation of diffusion-based time series forecasters—output instability from stochastic sampling—without requiring external supervision, making the approach broadly applicable across domains. The consistent but moderate improvements over TimeGrad (e.g., ~7–35% CRPS reduction depending on dataset) and the ablation showing diminishing returns beyond 4–5 granularity levels provide useful practical guidance. Open questions include whether the heuristic share-ratio selection can be made fully automatic, whether the approach generalizes to non-stationary or irregularly-sampled series, and whether the multi-granularity guidance principle transfers to other generative tasks beyond time series.
