## Problem

The dominant paradigm in geometric deep learning—message passing along combinatorial structures—suffers from over-smoothing, over-squashing, and the need for graph rewiring to capture long-range dependencies. Existing simplicial and cellular neural networks inherit these limitations because they aggregate information along edges or faces. This paper asks whether geometric information from the ambient embedding space (node coordinates in R^n) can be exploited directly, without any message passing, to produce interpretable and theoretically grounded representations of simplices.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242055/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes embedded simplicial complexes where node features serve as coordinates in R^n, along with a specified set of k-chains (defaulting to the standard oriented k-simplex basis if none are provided). Integration is approximated via finite Riemann sums over each embedded k-simplex, producing an m×ℓ integration matrix whose (i,j)-entry is the integral of the j-th neural k-form over the i-th chain. A readout layer (column sum, L1, or L2 norm) collapses this matrix into a fixed-length vector independent of the number of simplices, which is then passed to a classifier MLP.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces message passing with integration of learnable differential k-forms over embedded simplices. A neural k-form is defined by an MLP ψ: R^n → R^{C(n,k)} whose output components serve as the scaling functions of a general k-form ω^ψ = Σ_I ψ_I dx_I; integrating this form over an embedded k-simplex yields a scalar that acts as a geometrically meaningful feature. A tuple of ℓ such MLPs induces an integration matrix X_φ(β, ω^ψ) = [∫_{β_i} ω_j^ψ], which is differentiable in the MLP parameters and thus trainable by standard backpropagation. The paper proves a universal approximation theorem for neural k-forms (any compactly supported k-form can be approximated to arbitrary precision by a one-hidden-layer neural k-form) and shows that permutation and orientation equivariance follow directly from the multi-linearity of integration. The entire pipeline—MLP forward pass, Riemann-sum integration, matrix construction, readout, and classification—is implemented in PyTorch-Geometric and requires no graph rewiring or iterative message passing.

## Results

On the TU graph classification benchmarks (Table 1), NkF achieves competitive accuracy with only ~4K parameters: 93.42% on Letter-low (best), 67.69% on Letter-med (best), and 62.93% on Letter-high (best), while remaining within 1–3% of the strongest baselines on BZR, COX2, and DHFR. On the larger MoleculeNet datasets (Table 2), NkF substantially outperforms all message-passing GNNs in AUROC using only 9K parameters: 83.50 on BACE (vs. 69.52 GAT), 86.41 on BBBP (vs. 76.51 GAT), and 76.70 on HIV (vs. 68.70 GCN). The method uses no message passing and requires an order of magnitude fewer parameters than the GNN baselines (4K–9K vs. 5K–282K).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EGNN (Satorras et al., 2021), NkF trades a small accuracy drop on BZR (78.77 vs. 79.51) and DHFR (64.42 vs. 64.02, essentially tied) for a large parameter reduction (4K vs. 1M) and wins on Letter-low (93.42 vs. 93.20) and Letter-med (67.69 vs. 65.91). Against GAT (Veličković, 2022; Veličković et al., 2018), NkF is competitive on COX2 (80.30 vs. 80.73) but trails on BZR (78.77 vs. 81.48) and DHFR (64.42 vs. 73.02); however, on MoleculeNet NkF surpasses GAT by 14 AUROC points on BACE (83.50 vs. 69.52) and 10 points on BBBP (86.41 vs. 76.51). Against GCN (Kipf & Welling, 2017), NkF wins on Letter-low (93.42 vs. 81.38), Letter-med (67.69 vs. 62.00), and Letter-high (62.93 vs. 43.06), while being roughly tied on BZR and COX2; on MoleculeNet NkF leads GCN by 16.7 AUROC on BACE and 7.7 on HIV. Against GIN (Xu et al., 2019), NkF wins on Letter-low (93.42 vs. 85.00), Letter-med (67.69 vs. 67.07), and Letter-high (62.93 vs. 50.93), and dominates on MoleculeNet (83.50 vs. 42.91 on BACE). Overall, NkF is most competitive on datasets where node coordinates carry strong geometric signal, and its advantage is most pronounced on the larger MoleculeNet benchmarks where it uses roughly 15–30× fewer parameters than the GNN baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs fundamentally from the message passing paradigm that dominates geometric deep learning (GCN, GAT, GIN, EGNN, and simplicial/cell complex variants). Rather than extending message passing to higher-order structures, it repurposes classical differential geometry—specifically the integration of differential k-forms over embedded simplices—as a replacement for message passing, with MLPs parameterizing the scaling functions of the forms. The cochain matrix formalism from simplicial neural networks (Ebli et al., Bodnar et al.) is retained as the output data type, but the entries are now produced by integration of globally-defined forms rather than by iterative aggregation. The universal approximation result translates the classical Cybenko/Hornik theorem into the language of neural k-forms, providing a theoretical guarantee absent from most message passing architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the assumption that message passing is necessary for competitive geometric deep learning, showing that a coordinate-based integration approach with 4K–9K parameters can match or exceed GNNs with 5K–282K parameters on molecular graph classification. Key limitations include the hard requirement for node coordinates (ruling out many real-world graphs), benchmarking restricted to classification tasks, and inability to handle equivariant features. Open questions include whether the approach scales to regression, higher-dimensional simplicial complexes, and long-range dependency tasks, and whether the convolutional 1-form extension sketched in the appendix can recover equivariance without sacrificing the message-passing-free design.
