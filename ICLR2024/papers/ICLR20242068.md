## Problem

Extending diffusion models to tabular data is challenging because tabular features have complex, varied distributions and mix continuous and discrete types, making it difficult to learn joint column probabilities. Existing solutions either apply lossy encodings (one-hot, analog bit) that degrade quality or run separate diffusion processes per data type, preventing the model from capturing cross-type co-occurrence patterns. Additionally, prior work lacks a unified, comprehensive evaluation protocol for comparing synthetic tabular data generators.

## Input / Output

The method takes a mixed-type tabular table (numerical and categorical columns) and produces synthetic rows of the same schema. Each column is tokenized into a d-dimensional vector (d=4), so a row with M columns maps to a latent vector z ∈ R^(1×Md) that the diffusion model operates on. For missing value imputation, the input is a partially observed row where masked numerical columns are filled with column means and masked categorical columns are set to uniform one-hot vectors; the output is the completed row after denoising only the masked dimensions in latent space.

## Method

TABSYN first trains a VAE with Transformer encoders/decoders that maps mixed-type tabular data into a regularized continuous latent space, using per-column tokenizers (linear projections for numerical, embedding lookups for categorical) and an adaptive β-scheduling strategy that dynamically reduces the KL-divergence weight when reconstruction stalls. A score-based diffusion model (VE-SDE) is then trained in this latent space via denoising score matching, with a 5-layer MLP denoiser and sinusoidal timestep embeddings. The key theoretical contribution is a linear noise schedule σ(t)=t, proven (Proposition 1) to yield zero Euler approximation error in the reverse probability-flow ODE, enabling high-quality sampling in fewer than 20 NFEs versus 50–1000+ for competing diffusion methods. For imputation, the unconditional model is adapted with a known/unknown masking and resampling strategy (following Repaint) that re-noises known dimensions while denoising unknown ones at each reverse step.

## Results

On six mixed-type tabular datasets, TABSYN reduces the average column-wise density estimation error to 1.08% (vs. 7.72% for the best baseline STaSy, an 86% improvement) and the average pair-wise column correlation error to 1.73% (vs. 5.34% for TabDDPM, a 67.6% improvement). TABSYN also achieves the highest α-Precision (98.67% average) and β-Recall (48.90% average) among all methods, and requires fewer than 20 NFEs for sampling versus 50–200 for STaSy and 1,000 for TabDDPM. On the Machine Learning Efficiency task, TABSYN achieves a 7.23% average gap to real-data performance, the smallest among all generative baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TABSYN outperforms all seven generative baselines on both column-wise density estimation and pair-wise correlation estimation across all six datasets. The most competitive diffusion-based baselines are STaSy (7.72% avg density error) and TabDDPM (5.34% avg correlation error); TABSYN reduces these to 1.08% and 1.73% respectively. TabDDPM is particularly unstable, collapsing on the News dataset (78.75% density error, 0.00 α-Precision), while TABSYN maintains consistent performance. GReaT (language-model based) fails on News due to length limits and shows high variance across datasets. GOGGLE and CoDi, despite their architectural sophistication (GNN-based VAE and dual-diffusion with contrastive learning, respectively), are substantially outperformed by TABSYN on both low-order statistics and high-order fidelity metrics (α-Precision, β-Recall). On the MLE task, the performance gap narrows (TABSYN 7.23% vs. TabDDPM 9.14% average gap to real data), suggesting that some columns are less critical for downstream prediction, which the authors argue motivates the multi-metric evaluation protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

TABSYN sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: latent diffusion models (Rombach et al., 2022; Vahdat et al., 2021) that operate in a VAE-learned embedding space, and score-based diffusion via SDEs (Song et al., 2021; Karras et al., 2022). For tabular data specifically, prior diffusion-based methods (STaSy, TabDDPM, CoDi) either treat one-hot categorical features as continuous values or maintain separate diffusion processes per data type, making it difficult to capture cross-type column correlations. TABSYN departs from these by first mapping all mixed-type columns into a unified continuous latent space via a Transformer-based VAE with adaptive β-scheduling, then applying a simplified VE-SDE with a linear noise schedule (σ(t)=t) whose optimality for Euler-step approximation is formally proven. The VAE design draws on β-VAE (Higgins et al., 2016) and Transformer architectures for tabular data (Gorishniy et al., 2021), while the denoising MLP follows TabDDPM's architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first comprehensive multi-metric benchmark (low-order statistics, high-order α-precision/β-recall, C2ST detection, MLE, imputation, privacy) for tabular synthesis, addressing what the authors identify as a gap in prior evaluations. The practical speed advantage (<20 NFEs vs. 50–1000 for competing diffusion methods) makes latent diffusion a viable option for real-world tabular pipelines where sampling latency matters. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very wide tables (hundreds of columns), whether conditional variants for targeted imputation can match dedicated discriminative models, and whether the linear noise schedule's advantage persists under different numerical solvers (e.g., higher-order ODE integrators). The finding that an unconditional generative model can outperform a trained XGBoost classifier on 4/6 imputation tasks hints at a broader question about when generative vs. discriminative approaches are preferable for structured prediction.
