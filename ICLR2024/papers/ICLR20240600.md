## Problem

Self-supervised learning for ECG representation has largely treated spatial (cross-lead) and temporal (within-lead) dimensions in isolation, either through contrastive learning with augmentations that can distort ECG semantics or through masked reconstruction that patchifies only along one axis. This paper asks whether a generative SSL framework can explicitly capture the joint spatio-temporal structure inherent in multi-lead ECG signals, and whether such a representation generalizes across lead configurations and downstream cardiac tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240600/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 10-second ECG segments resampled to 250 Hz, where each of the L leads is independently split into non-overlapping patches of 75 samples, yielding L×n total patches per sample. The architecture is lead-count agnostic: the same pre-trained encoder accepts 12-lead, 6-lead (limb), or single-lead inputs without structural modification. During pre-training the output is the reconstructed raw signal values of masked patches; during downstream evaluation the encoder's pooled representation is passed through a linear head to produce class probabilities for arrhythmia or myocardial infarction categories.

## Method

ST-MEM builds on the MAE architecture (ViT-B encoder + lightweight decoder) but introduces three ECG-specific modifications. First, spatio-temporal patchifying splits each lead into temporal patches independently, so the encoder's self-attention operates across patches from all leads simultaneously, forcing it to model inter-lead spatial relationships. Second, a lead-wise shared decoder processes each lead's embedding sequence independently during reconstruction, preventing the decoder from trivially copying unmasked cross-lead information and thereby increasing reconstruction difficulty for the encoder. Third, lead-indicating modules—per-lead embeddings added to every patch and learnable [SEP] tokens bracketing each lead's patch sequence—give the model explicit cues to distinguish which lead each embedding originates from. Pre-training uses 75% random patch masking with MSE loss on the masked patches, trained on a combined 188,480-ECG corpus (Chapman, Ningbo, CODE-15); downstream, only the encoder plus a linear classifier is fine-tuned.

## Results

On the fine-tuning evaluation, ST-MEM achieves 0.933 AUROC and 0.655 macro-F1 on PTB-XL (5-class arrhythmia/MI), and 0.980 AUROC and 0.857 macro-F1 on CPSC2018 (9-class arrhythmia), outperforming all baselines. In linear evaluation (frozen encoder + linear probe), ST-MEM reaches 0.838 AUROC on PTB-XL and 0.938 AUROC on CPSC2018, with F1 gains of 0.064–0.366 (PTB-XL) and 0.292–0.561 (CPSC2018) over the next-best baseline. In low-resource settings (1% labeled data), ST-MEM attains 0.815 AUROC on PTB-XL and 0.897 AUROC on CPSC2018, surpassing all other SSL methods and the supervised baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ST-MEM outperforms all five baselines across both linear evaluation and fine-tuning on PTB-XL and CPSC2018. Against the contrastive baselines, ST-MEM's advantage is most pronounced: MoCo v3 achieves only 0.739 AUROC (linear) and 0.913 AUROC (fine-tuning) on PTB-XL versus ST-MEM's 0.838 and 0.933, while CMSC lags further at 0.797/0.877 AUROC. Among generative baselines from MaeFE, MLAE (spatial patchifying) reaches 0.915 AUROC on PTB-XL fine-tuning and 0.973 on CPSC2018, the closest competitor, yet ST-MEM still leads by 1.8 and 0.7 AUROC points respectively. MTAE+RLM (temporal patchifying with random lead masking) achieves 0.911/0.960 AUROC, also below ST-MEM. In the low-resource 1% setting, ST-MEM (0.815/0.897 AUROC) surpasses even the original CPC pre-trained model (0.740–0.773/0.754–0.762 AUROC), which had access to PTB-XL and CPSC2018 during its own pre-training, confirming the generality of the learned representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

ST-MEM extends the MAE framework (He et al., 2022) to ECG by introducing spatio-temporal patchifying, which unifies the temporal-only (MTAE) and spatial-only (MLAE) patchifying strategies from MaeFE (Zhang et al., 2022) into a single fine-grained representation. It departs from prior spatio-temporal patchifying work (Sawano et al., 2022) by adding lead-indicating modules—lead-wise shared decoder, learnable lead embeddings, and SEP embeddings—that explicitly encode which lead each patch originates from, addressing the challenge that naive spatio-temporal patchifying makes reconstruction too easy by allowing cross-lead information leakage. The paper also positions itself against contrastive learning baselines (MoCo v3, CLOCS/CMSC), arguing that generative reconstruction better preserves ECG semantics that are easily distorted by data augmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: ECG representation learning that generalizes across lead configurations (12-lead, 6-lead limb, single-lead) without retraining, which is critical as wearable devices increasingly produce reduced-lead signals. The demonstration that the learned representation genuinely encodes spatial anatomy (frontal vs. horizontal plane separation via GMM clustering at 0.895 accuracy) provides mechanistic evidence beyond black-box performance gains. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to other multivariate time-series domains (the HAR experiment is preliminary), whether partially fine-tuning or other transfer strategies could further improve downstream adaptation, and whether the lead-indicating modules generalize to non-standard lead configurations beyond the 12-lead system.
