## Problem

Iterative Magnitude Pruning (IMP) is the state-of-the-art global pruning method, yet it trains only a single model per cycle, leaving potential performance on the table. While prior work established that two IMP solutions from the same matching ticket are linearly connected, it remained unclear whether the convex hull of three or more such particles also forms a low-loss subspace that could yield a superior single sparse network.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240305/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a dense neural network (e.g., WRN-28-2, VGG-13, ResNet-50), a training dataset, and a target sparsity level, and produces a single sparse network consisting of pruned weights and a binary mask. Pruning is applied exclusively to convolutional layers (batch normalization and fully-connected layers are left intact), and the output is a single model with the same inference cost as vanilla IMP, despite internally training N parallel particles per cycle. The mask is updated iteratively at a fixed pruning ratio (default α=0.2) across cycles until the target sparsity is reached.

## Method

SWAMP modifies IMP in two complementary ways: (1) it trains N particles (default N=4) concurrently from the same matching ticket, each with a different SGD noise (i.e., different mini-batch ordering), and (2) it replaces standard SGD within each particle with Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA), which accumulates a moving average over the last 25% of the training trajectory. After all particles complete their training, their final weights are averaged to produce a single weight vector, from which the next pruning mask is derived by magnitude-based selection. The key insight is that as sparsity increases across IMP cycles, particles converge into the same wide basin of attraction, making weight averaging effective and producing flatter local minima (confirmed via Hessian trace) with superior generalization. SWAMP preserves the linear connectivity between successive cycle solutions—a critical property for IMP's success—while achieving performance comparable to an ensemble of two IMP solutions at single-model inference cost.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (WRN-28-2), SWAMP achieves 94.74% accuracy at 50% sparsity, 94.88% at 75%, 94.73% at 90%, and 94.23% at 95%, consistently outperforming all baselines. On CIFAR-100 (WRN-32-4), SWAMP reaches 77.29% at 50% sparsity and 76.48% at 95%, maintaining a 1–2% advantage over the strongest competitor (Lottery Pools) across all sparsity levels. On ImageNet (ResNet-50), SWAMP improves over IMP by 0.31% on IN-Val and 1.39% on IN-R at 88.9% sparsity, demonstrating robustness on large-scale, hard-to-prune datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SWAMP outperforms all eight baselines across every sparsity level on both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100. The closest competitor is Lottery Pools (Yin et al., 2023), which also averages consecutive IMP tickets; SWAMP leads by 0.35% at 50% sparsity and 0.80% at 95% on CIFAR-10, and by 0.98% at 50% and 1.34% at 95% on CIFAR-100. Against the direct IMP baseline, SWAMP gains 0.77% at 95% sparsity on CIFAR-10 and 1.88% on CIFAR-100, with the gap widening at higher sparsity. One-shot methods (SNIP, SynFlow, GraSP) and dynamic sparse training (RigL, DST) trail SWAMP by 1.5–5.5% depending on sparsity and dataset. IMP+SAM, which combines IMP with sharpness-aware minimization, is competitive at moderate sparsity but degrades sharply at 95% (92.03% vs. SWAMP's 94.23% on CIFAR-10), while SWAMP maintains stable performance. On ImageNet, SWAMP consistently beats IMP by 0.1–1.4% across four evaluation splits (IN-Val, IN-V2, IN-R, IN-S), with the largest gains on the more challenging IN-R and IN-S benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Iterative Magnitude Pruning (IMP) lineage from Frankle & Carbin (2019) and Frankle et al. (2020) by replacing the single-particle training step with concurrent training of multiple particles from the same matching ticket, followed by weight averaging. The key conceptual bridge is the linear mode connectivity result of Frankle et al. (2020) and Paul et al. (2023), which established pairwise connectivity between IMP solutions; SWAMP generalizes this to the convex hull of three or more particles. The Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA) mechanism from Izmailov et al. (2018) is repurposed to accumulate additional virtual particles along each trajectory, complementing the explicit multi-particle averaging. Unlike Lottery Pools (Yin et al., 2023), which averages tickets from consecutive IMP cycles, SWAMP averages particles within a single cycle sharing the same matching ticket, making it a drop-in modification of the standard IMP loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SWAMP provides a simple, broadly applicable modification to IMP that consistently outperforms all competing pruning methods (one-shot, dynamic, and ensemble-based) across diverse architectures, datasets, and sparsity levels, including extreme sparsity regimes where IMP degrades. The empirical demonstration that the convex hull of multiple particles from a shared matching ticket forms a low-loss subspace—extending the pairwise connectivity result—opens a theoretical question the authors explicitly flag as future work: why does multi-particle averaging succeed where the landscape is non-convex at low sparsity but becomes flat at high sparsity? A practical limitation is the linear scaling of training cost with particle count, though the authors mitigate this via distributed parallelization and a cost-efficient SWAMP+ variant that uses multiple particles only in the high-sparsity regime.
