## Problem

Diffusion-based planners like Diffuser struggle with long-horizon offline RL tasks due to their dense, flat planning scheme, which is computationally expensive and produces trajectories with inadequate coverage of the dataset distribution. Enabling hierarchical planning within a diffusion framework—so that both the efficiency of hierarchical decomposition and the global coherence of diffusion-based generation are preserved—remained an open problem. Additionally, simply increasing the kernel size to enlarge the receptive field (an alternative to hierarchy) was shown to degrade out-of-distribution generalization.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes offline trajectory data formatted as 2D state-action arrays, which are split into subsampled high-level sequences (every K-th state) and dense low-level segments of length K+1. At test time, the input is the current state (or a start-goal pair for OOD tasks), and the output is a full action sequence: the high-level diffuser first produces a sequence of H subgoal states, and the low-level diffuser then generates dense state-action trajectories connecting each adjacent subgoal pair, with all low-level segments sampled in parallel. The high-level planner's generated actions are discarded; only the subgoal states are passed downstream.

## Method

Hierarchical Diffuser (HD) consists of two jointly trained diffusion models: a Sparse Diffuser (SD) that models the distribution over subsampled trajectories (every K-th state, giving a 'jumpy' high-level plan with a larger effective receptive field at lower computational cost) and a low-level Diffuser that models dense trajectory segments between adjacent subgoals. At test time, the high-level planner samples subgoals via guided denoising (using a return-prediction guidance function), and the low-level planner refines each subgoal pair into a dense action trajectory by conditioning on both endpoint states during denoising. A variant (HD-DA) augments the high-level input with dense actions to improve return prediction accuracy. The key insight is that sparsifying the high-level sequence reduces the diffusion model's burden of learning high-dimensional dense trajectory distributions, while the low-level fine-tuning stage recovers the fine-grained dynamics, yielding both superior performance and a 10× planning speedup over flat Diffuser on long-horizon tasks.

## Results

On long-horizon planning (Table 1), Hierarchical Diffuser (HD) achieves 139.9 on Maze2D single-task average and 149.9 on Multi2D multi-task average, outperforming Diffuser (119.5 and 129.4) and the prior best hierarchical diffusion method HDMI (123.5 and 132.8). On AntMaze, HD reaches 88.8 average, substantially exceeding Diffuser's 36.0 (which completely fails on AntMaze-Large with 0.0) and HiGoC's 79.3. On offline RL with dense rewards (Table 2), HD-DA achieves 84.6 average on Gym-MuJoCo and 72.5 on FrankaKitchen, surpassing Diffuser (77.5 and 53.1) and HDMI (82.6 and 69.2 on Mixed). HD also achieves a 10× planning speedup over Diffuser on medium and large maze settings with horizons beyond 250 steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HD outperforms the non-hierarchical Diffuser by 17.1% on Maze2D single-task (139.9 vs 119.5) and 15.8% on Multi2D (149.9 vs 129.4), with the gap widening on larger mazes (Maze2D-Large: 155.8 vs 123.0). Against the closest hierarchical diffusion competitor HDMI, HD gains 13.3% on Maze2D (139.9 vs 123.5) and 13.1% on Multi2D (149.9 vs 132.8), and achieves >20% improvement on the hardest tasks (Maze2D-Large: 155.8 vs 128.6; Multi2D-Large: 165.5 vs 135.4). On AntMaze, HD (88.8) surpasses the best non-diffusion hierarchical method HiGoC (79.3) by 12%, while Diffuser completely fails on AntMaze-Large (0.0). For dense-reward offline RL, HD-DA (84.6) beats Diffuser (77.5) by 9.2% on Gym-MuJoCo and achieves a 36.5% improvement over Diffuser on FrankaKitchen (72.5 vs 53.1), demonstrating superior generalization. Flat learning baselines (MPPI, IQL, CQL, BCQ, BEAR, DT, TT, MoReL) are consistently outperformed, with IQL reaching only 47.0 on Maze2D and 77.0 on Gym-MuJoCo, confirming the advantage of both hierarchical structure and diffusion-based planning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Diffuser framework (Janner et al., 2022), which uses a single diffusion model as both world model and planner, by introducing a minimal hierarchical structure: the high-level planner simply subsamples trajectories at every K-th timestep ('jumpy' planning), while a low-level planner refines each inter-subgoal segment. This departs from prior hierarchical diffusion methods like HDMI (Li et al., 2023), which require explicit graph searching or complex subgoal extraction, and from non-diffusion hierarchical planners (HiGoC, IRIS) that lack the test-time flexibility of guided diffusion sampling. The core insight is that subsampling alone provides a larger effective receptive field at lower computational cost, addressing Diffuser's known weaknesses in coverage and efficiency without architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 10× planning speedup and consistent 9–12% performance gains over Diffuser across D4RL benchmarks make hierarchical diffusion a viable default for offline RL planning. The compositional OOD experiment reveals a fundamental tension in Diffuser—increasing kernel size improves in-distribution performance but catastrophically degrades generalization (0% success vs. HD's 100%)—suggesting that receptive field and generalization are not simply tradeable via model capacity. Open questions include whether the fixed-interval subgoal strategy generalizes to continuous, non-uniform task structures, whether the approach extends to visual/observation-based settings, and how the value function's accuracy constrains the maximum useful jump step K.
