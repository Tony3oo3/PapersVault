## Problem

The two most natural activation functions for inducing sparse hidden layers—shifted ReLU (ReLU_τ) and soft thresholding (ST_τ)—suffer from a previously unreported training instability: when initialized at the Edge of Chaos, their Gaussian process variance map has a fixed point that is only stable from the left (V'_φ(q*)=1 with V''_φ(q*)>0), causing exponential variance growth with depth and making very deep networks effectively untrainable. No prior work had identified or addressed this failure mode for sparsifying activations at initialization.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard image inputs—28×28 grayscale MNIST digits or 32×32 RGB CIFAR-10 images—normalized to unit variance, and produces class labels alongside highly sparse hidden activations (up to 85% zeros) that are maintained throughout training and inference. The networks are deliberately very deep (100-layer DNNs of width 300, 50-layer CNNs with 300 channels) to stress-test the initialization theory, and the key output metric beyond accuracy is the fractional activation sparsity measured on the test set.

## Method

The paper proves that ReLU_τ and ST_τ necessarily have V'_φ(q*)=χ_{1,φ}=1 at the EoC with strictly positive curvature V''_φ(q*)>0, making the variance fixed point unstable to the finite-width stochasticity of q^l. The fix is a simple magnitude clipping: CReLU_{τ,m} caps the output at m (zero below τ, linear between τ and τ+m, constant m above), and CST_{τ,m} applies the same clipping symmetrically to both signs. This clipping introduces a strict inequality χ_{1,φ} > V'_φ(q*), so the EoC condition χ_{1,φ}=1 now guarantees V'_φ(q*)<1 and local stability of the variance fixed point. The clipping magnitude m is chosen to keep both V'_φ(q*) and V''_φ(q*) sufficiently small to prevent the variance map from developing secondary fixed points or extended near-identity regions that cause exploding gradients. Networks are initialized at the EoC with q*=1 and trained via SGD, achieving up to 85% activation sparsity with near-full accuracy on MNIST and 70–85% sparsity on CIFAR-10.

## Results

On MNIST (100-layer DNN, width 300), CReLU_{τ,m} and CST_{τ,m} maintain 93–95% test accuracy at activation sparsity up to 85%, matching the standard ReLU baseline (94% at 50% sparsity). On CIFAR-10 (50-layer CNN, 300 channels), the clipped activations retain full ReLU-baseline accuracy (70%) at 70% sparsity, with 85% sparsity incurring only a ~4% drop to 65–66%. In stark contrast, the non-clipped ReLU_τ and ST_τ fail to train (≈10% accuracy) at sparsity ≥70% and ≥50% respectively, confirming the theoretical instability prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baselines are the non-clipped shifted ReLU (ReLU_τ) and soft thresholding (ST_τ), which are the most natural sparsifying activations. On MNIST DNNs, ReLU_τ degrades from 94% at 50% sparsity to 76% (±0.37) at 60% and collapses to 10% at 70%, while ST_τ fails at every tested sparsity (≥50%). The proposed clipped variants CReLU_{τ,m} and CST_{τ,m} maintain 92–95% accuracy at 85% sparsity, a 4–5× improvement in usable sparsity range over their non-clipped counterparts. On CIFAR-10 CNNs, the gap is equally stark: ReLU_τ drops to 10% at 70% sparsity and ST_τ fails at 50%, whereas CReLU_{τ,m} and CST_{τ,m} retain 63–68% accuracy at 85% sparsity. The standard ReLU (τ=0) serves as the accuracy ceiling (94% MNIST, 70% CIFAR-10), and the clipped activations match or slightly exceed this at moderate sparsity, confirming that the clipping modification preserves expressivity while restoring variance-map stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Gaussian process / Edge-of-Chaos initialization framework (Lee et al. 2018; Poole et al. 2016; Xiao et al. 2018) to analyze sparsity-inducing activation functions at random initialization, a setting the authors note has not been previously studied. It departs from prior activation-sparsity work (Georgiadis 2019; Kurtz et al. 2020), which focused on fine-tuning pre-trained networks to increase sparsity, by instead asking whether networks can be trained from scratch with highly sparse hidden layers. The paper identifies a previously unreported incompatibility between the two most natural sparsifying activations (shifted ReLU and soft thresholding, the latter from Donoho 1995) and EoC initialization, and resolves it with a simple magnitude-clipping modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that the most intuitive sparsifying activations are fundamentally untrainable in deep networks at EoC is a useful negative result that redirects the design space toward clipped variants, which the paper shows can sustain 85% activation sparsity with near-full accuracy on MNIST. However, empirical validation is limited to small-scale benchmarks (MNIST, CIFAR-10) and simple architectures (DNNs, 1D CNNs), leaving open whether the theory and the clipping prescription transfer to transformers, ResNets, or large-scale vision models. The paper also identifies a second failure mode at high sparsity with small clipping magnitude that remains unexplained, and notes that approximate dynamical isometry is still unattainable with these activations.
