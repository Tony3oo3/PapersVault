## Problem

Existing Bayesian optimization of risk measures (VaR, CVaR) restarts from scratch for every new task, ignoring optimization results from prior tasks. While meta-BO has been extensively studied for vanilla BO, no prior work extends it to risk measures, which are not directly observed, do not follow well-known stochastic processes, and require invariance to scaling and vertical-shifting of the blackbox function. The paper also addresses the challenge of robustness to prior harmful tasks that could degrade current-task optimization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes noisy zeroth-order evaluations of a blackbox function f(x,z) at chosen (x,z) pairs, the GP posterior mean and variance from each prior task (not raw function values), the known distribution of the environmental random variable Z, and a risk level α ∈ (0,1). It produces an input query (x_t, z_t) at each BO iteration and a recommended decision variable x*_t whose instantaneous regret provably vanishes as t→∞. Notably, the risk measure ρ_f(x;α) is never directly observed—it is only bounded via confidence intervals—so the recommendation strategy must work without ever seeing the true risk value.

## Method

The core contribution is the Versatile Query Set (V-set) V_t, a set of candidate inputs defined by two conditions: an Overlapping Condition (OC) ensuring the input's risk-measure confidence interval overlaps with that of the optimum (parameterized by λ), and an Uncertainty Condition (UC) excluding inputs with well-estimated risk measures (parameterized by η). Any choice of x_t ∈ V_t yields sublinear cumulative regret R_T ≤ (η(1−λ)+1)√(C₁Tβ_Tγ_T), providing inherent robustness to harmful prior tasks. To exploit prior tasks, the algorithm computes a probable local maximizer set P_{τ|V_t} for each prior task τ (inputs in V_t whose upper confidence bound of ρ_{f_τ} exceeds the maximum lower confidence bound over V_t), assigns each input a priority equal to the number of prior tasks whose P_{τ|V_t} contains it, and selects x_t by maximizing ρ_{u_t}(x;α) among the highest-priority inputs. This design is invariant to scaling and vertical-shifting, and the informativeness of prior tasks is adaptively controlled: as V_t shrinks with more observations, prior tasks naturally become less influential without requiring explicit weight decay.

## Results

Across 8 experiments (6 synthetic benchmarks and 2 real-world datasets: Yacht Hydrodynamics and Portfolio Optimization), meta-VBO is evaluated by the regret at the recommended input r(x*_t) over 30 random repetitions. With useful prior tasks (positive scaling or vertical shifting), meta-VBO achieves faster convergence to low regret than V-UCB, and this advantage persists even when the prior task set T_all includes both useful and harmful tasks. In the extreme case of only harmful tasks (negative scaling or horizontal shifting), meta-VBO does not outperform V-UCB but still converges to a low-regret solution, confirming robustness. The theoretical cumulative regret bound with the chosen hyperparameters (λ=0, η=1) is R_T ≤ 2√(C_1 T β_T γ_T), which is 2× the V-UCB bound, reflecting the trade-off for maximum V-set flexibility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is V-UCB (Nguyen et al., 2021b), which is equivalent to meta-VBO with no prior tasks (T = ∅). When the prior task set contains only useful transformations (positive scaling or vertical shifting), meta-VBO consistently achieves lower regret at the recommended input across all 8 experiments, demonstrating that transferred optimization information accelerates convergence. With the mixed set T_all (combining useful and harmful tasks), meta-VBO still outperforms V-UCB, indicating that the priority-based selection mechanism effectively filters out uninformative or harmful prior tasks. In the worst case where T contains only harmful tasks (negative scaling or horizontal shifting), meta-VBO does not beat V-UCB but maintains sublinear regret, confirming the theoretical no-regret guarantee. The one notable exception is Hartmann-6D with T_harmful-neg-scale, where meta-VBO performs poorly, likely due to the large 5-dimensional input space amplifying the misleading signal from negated functions. Figure 3 further shows that the percentage of harmful tasks whose probable local maximizer set contains the selected query decreases over iterations, providing an adaptive mechanism that naturally diminishes the influence of harmful priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: Bayesian optimization of risk measures (VaR/CVaR) via GP-UCB-type algorithms (V-UCB, CV-UCB) and meta-BO that transfers optimization outcomes across tasks (Dai et al., 2022; Feurer et al., 2018; Ramachandran et al., 2019). It departs from prior meta-BO by introducing the versatile query set (V-set), where any input in the set yields a no-regret guarantee, thereby decoupling the no-regret property from a specific acquisition-function maximizer and creating a natural interface for prioritizing inputs based on prior-task information. The RKHS-based confidence bounds from Chowdhury and Gopalan (2017) and the risk-measure confidence intervals from Nguyen et al. (2021a,b) provide the theoretical scaffolding, while the problem formulation with environmental random variables follows Cakmak et al. (2020).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithm to combine meta-learning with risk-measure BO, filling a gap where prior meta-BO methods (which rely on function-gap similarity or Gaussian posterior transfer) cannot be directly applied because risk measures do not follow a GP posterior. The V-set mechanism offers a principled way to incorporate prior-task knowledge without explicit weight decay, and the invariance to scaling/vertical-shifting addresses a practical concern that prior meta-BO methods (e.g., RM-GP-UCB) do not handle. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other BO variants (federated, preferential, constrained, Nash-equilibrium BO) as the authors note, and whether the moderate empirical gains over V-UCB persist in higher-dimensional or more complex real-world settings beyond the 1D–5D decision spaces tested here.
