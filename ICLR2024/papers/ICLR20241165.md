## Problem

Pruning is the dominant paradigm for reducing model memory at the start of training, but it suffers from an inherent capacity disadvantage: aggressive pruning produces degenerate zero embeddings and drops entire nodes/filters, limiting achievable accuracy at high compression. The paper asks whether parameter sharing can overcome this capacity gap, and in doing so identifies two critical flaws in the state-of-the-art RPS method ROAST—extreme sensitivity to initialization standard deviation (causing divergence or slow learning) and the inability to recover full-model accuracy at zero compression (lack of Pareto-continuity).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241165/cropped_page6_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard neural network architecture (ResNet-20, VGG-11, GAT, DeepFM, AutoInt) and a target memory budget expressed as a compression factor (original/compressed), and produces a compressed model that retains the original architecture's forward-pass computation. Weights are not stored directly but are derived on-the-fly from a single shared parameter array of size m via two hash functions: a positional hash h that maps each weight to a location in the array, and a sign hash g that assigns ±1. Inputs are standard RGB images (32×32 CIFAR, 64×64 Tiny-ImageNet), graph adjacency/feature data, or categorical feature vectors for recommendation; outputs are class labels or click-through probabilities.

## Method

STABLE-RPS introduces two provable fixes over ROAST. First, a gradient-scaling scheme multiplies each parameter-array gradient by Γ[j] = (count of weights mapped to j) / (Σ λᵢ)², where λᵢ are the per-component multiplicative scaling factors; this provably restores the full-model stability range of gradient descent (0, 2/L_f) and eliminates sensitivity to the initialization standard deviation. Second, a RANDOMFOLD mapping flattens all model chunks into a single global array, partitions it into blocks of size m, and applies a random hash to each partition index with circular wrap-around, yielding an optimal load factor of ⌈n/m⌉ and guaranteeing Pareto-continuity (zero collisions at 1× compression, recovering the exact full model). Theoretically, the paper reduces the linear-regression residual under compression to the norm-preservation quality of the correlation vector ρ, and proves that STABLE-RPS's expected variance is strictly lower than pruning's unless all feature correlations are perfectly balanced—explaining why even uninformed parameter sharing outperforms highly informed pruning (LTR) at high compression.

## Results

Across 1300+ experiments on CIFAR-10 (ResNet-20), CIFAR-100 (VGG-11), and Tiny-ImageNet, STABLE-RPS consistently outperforms all moderately informed pruning methods (MAG, SNIP, SYNFLOW, GRASP) at high compression ratios and is competitive or superior at low compression. At 1000× compression on CIFAR-10/ResNet-20, STABLE-RPS achieves ~53% accuracy while LTR (the strongest pruning baseline) suffers layer collapse and drops below moderately informed methods. STABLE-RPS also eliminates ROAST's sensitivity to initialization standard deviation: at 1000× compression, ROAST accuracy ranges from 10.0% to 56.0% depending on init-stdev, while STABLE-RPS remains stable at 52.1–54.8% across all init-stdev values. On graph (PPI, OGB-ARXIV) and recommendation (Criteo-Kaggle) domains, STABLE-RPS similarly dominates pruning baselines, with particularly large gaps in embedding table compression.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against moderately informed pruning (SNIP, SYNFLOW, GRASP, MAG), STABLE-RPS — which uses no data, no initialization information, and no model-specific knowledge — is consistently superior at high compression (≥10×) and competitive or better at low compression, demonstrating that the capacity advantage of parameter sharing outweighs the information advantage of informed pruning. Against the highly informed LTR (Lottery Ticket Rewinding), STABLE-RPS is slightly worse at low compression (1–3×) but significantly better at high compression (≥100×), where LTR suffers layer collapse and can fall below moderately informed methods. Against ROAST (the prior SOTA RPS method), STABLE-RPS fixes two critical issues: it eliminates the extreme sensitivity to initialization standard deviation (ROAST diverges at init-stdev=0.001, dropping to 10% accuracy, while STABLE-RPS maintains 52–91%) and achieves Pareto-continuity (recovering full-model accuracy at 1× compression, which ROAST cannot do). Against HashedNet's element-wise mapping, STABLE-RPS achieves better accuracy at high compression due to its optimal load factor and recovers full-model accuracy at zero compression. Against low-rank decomposition (Jaderberg et al.), STABLE-RPS achieves far higher compression ratios before accuracy degrades, as low-rank methods hit a hard capacity wall. Against SMALL-MODEL (reduced hidden dimensions), STABLE-RPS shows a significantly larger capacity advantage, with the gap widening at higher compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the randomized parameter-sharing (RPS) lineage from HashedNet (Chen et al., 2015) through ROBE and ROAST (Desai et al., 2023), identifying two critical flaws in the current SOTA method ROAST—sensitivity to initialization hyperparameters and lack of Pareto-continuity—and proposing STABLE-RPS as a provably corrected variant. It positions itself in direct opposition to the dominant pruning paradigm (LTH, LTR, SNIP, SYNFLOW, GRASP), arguing that parameter sharing offers a fundamentally superior memory-accuracy tradeoff due to inherent capacity advantages that no amount of pruning intelligence can overcome. The theoretical analysis connecting norm preservation under hashing to linear model residual provides a new analytical lens absent from prior RPS work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that an uninformed, data-agnostic parameter-sharing method outperforms highly informed iterative pruning (LTR) at high compression ratios challenges the core assumption that more information in the compression process should yield better results, pointing to a fundamental capacity ceiling for sparse models. However, practical adoption is currently limited: STABLE-RPS requires the same FLOPs as the uncompressed model, and experiments are confined to relatively small architectures (ResNet-20, VGG-11) on CIFAR-scale datasets. Key open questions include developing post-training parameter-sharing schemes, reducing computational cost (not just memory), designing informed parameter-sharing strategies, and whether the capacity advantage persists at ImageNet scale with larger models.
