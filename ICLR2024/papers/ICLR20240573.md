## Problem

Existing position embedding (PE) scaling methods for extending Transformer context windows (e.g., Position Interpolation, YaRN) are designed for a fixed scaling factor, creating a dilemma: small factors limit extrapolation beyond training length, while large factors degrade performance within the native context window. Length extrapolation methods like ALiBi can generalize to longer sequences in language modelling but underperform on practical long-context tasks requiring distant information retrieval. No prior approach simultaneously achieves strong extrapolation to 4×–8× training length and preserves in-window performance.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pre-trained RoPE-based LLM (e.g., LLaMA-2-7B) and takes text sequences of a fixed training length L^Train (e.g., 4k tokens). During training, a continuous scaling factor t′ is randomly sampled per batch, and the RoPE frequency basis is computed by integrating a neural ODE from t=1 to t′; position indices are then randomly sampled from the expanded range [1, t′·L] to maintain consistency with the scaled frequencies. At inference, frequency bases are pre-cached for K discrete t values (e.g., {1,2,3,4}), and the nearest upper-bound basis is selected for a given sequence length, introducing negligible latency (~27.8 vs 28.3 tokens/s on A100).

## Method

CLEX first establishes a unified view (Theorem 1) showing that position-index scaling (PI) and frequency-basis scaling (YaRN) are equivalent formulations of scaling the RoPE frequency basis θ by a factor α(t). It then formulates PE scaling as a continuous dynamical system dz(t)/dt = g(z(t), t) where z(t) = log θ_t, and parameterizes g with a lightweight up-and-down projection (W_up ∈ R^{d/2×λd}, W_down ∈ R^{λd×d/2}, SiLU activation) plus a constant prior ξ_t inherited from YaRN for faster convergence. The key training strategy samples t′ uniformly from [1, t^Train] at each step, integrates the ODE to obtain the frequency basis, and applies position extrapolation by randomly sampling L^Train indices from [1, t′·L] to reconcile the mismatch between the scaled frequencies and fixed position indices. This continuous, adaptive dynamics—rather than a single discrete scaling factor—enables the model to generalize smoothly to lengths far beyond training, achieving 4×–8× extrapolation without in-window degradation.

## Results

On language modelling with LLaMA-2-7B, CLEX-16k achieves a PPL of 5.52 at its 16k training length and maintains 5.64 at 64k (4× extrapolation) with 59.94% next-token accuracy, showing virtually no degradation. CLEX-4k matches its 4k PPL (5.86) at 16k (5.87), a 4× extrapolation, and outperforms all PE-scaling baselines trained on 16k within the 16k range. On LongBench (tested at 16k context), CLEX-7B-4k (trained on only 4k) achieves an average of 23.25 on summarization and 57.94 on code completion, matching or exceeding models trained on 16k–32k contexts such as CodeLLaMA-7B-16k (20.06, 60.11) and LongChat-v1.5-7B-32k (22.45, 54.15).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLEX-16k dramatically outperforms all PE-scaling baselines at 64k: it achieves PPL 5.64 vs. 8.75 for PI, 6.22 for Yarn(t=32), and >100 for Naive FT, while maintaining the best short-length PPL (5.52 at 16k). At 4k training length, CLEX-4k matches or beats all 16k-trained PE-scaling methods within the 16k range (PPL 5.87 vs. 5.72 for PI, 5.73 for Yarn) and extrapolates to 16k with negligible loss (5.87 vs. 5.86 at 4k), whereas PI and Yarn suffer significant degradation beyond their training length. On LongBench, CLEX-7B-4k (trained on 4k, tested at 16k) achieves 23.25 on summarization, surpassing CodeLLaMA-7B-16k (20.06) and LongChat-v1.5-7B-32k (22.45), and is competitive with GPT-3.5-Turbo-16k (23.9). ALiBi-based models (Baichuan-13B-4k, ALiBi-7B-4k) show catastrophic failure on LongBench (e.g., 7.23 avg on single-doc QA, 5.98 on multi-doc QA) despite good language modelling PPL, confirming that ALiBi's local-bias mechanism hinders practical long-context tasks. CLEX-7B-4k also outperforms ALiBi-7B-4k by large margins across all LongBench categories (e.g., 57.94 vs. 32.61 on code completion), demonstrating that continuous frequency-basis scaling preserves long-range information better than attention-bias extrapolation.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLEX unifies the two main families of RoPE-based context extension—position index scaling (PI) and frequency basis scaling (YaRN, CodeLLaMA)—under a single theoretical framework (Theorem 1), showing that all position index transformations are equivalent to frequency basis transformations. It then departs from prior discrete, fixed-factor scaling by formulating the frequency basis transition as a continuous dynamical system over the length scaling factor, learned via a lightweight neural ODE with Yarn's constant dynamics as a convergence-accelerating prior. This positions CLEX as a generalization of both PI and YaRN: rather than committing to one hand-designed α(t), the model learns the continuous trajectory adaptively, enabling fine-grained extrapolation beyond the training length without degrading shorter-length performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CLEX provides a practically deployable, drop-in mechanism for RoPE-based LLMs that achieves 4×–8× context extrapolation with negligible inference overhead, validated across LLaMA-2, Phi-2, and Mixtral. The finding that extrapolation ability is largely independent of training data volume (0.25B vs. 2B tokens) suggests that context extension can be accomplished with minimal fine-tuning compute, which is significant for resource-constrained settings. Open questions include whether the proposed scaling law (increase model size for >200k contexts) holds in practice, whether the approach transfers to non-RoPE positional encodings, and whether the learned continuous dynamics can be distilled into closed-form heuristics for zero-shot deployment.
