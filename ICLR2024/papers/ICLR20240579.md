## Problem

Fine-tuning multilingual LLMs for low-resource languages is blocked by two barriers: physical borders (data-sharing restrictions like GDPR that prevent cross-region data pooling) and linguistic borders (non-IID data distributions arising from typological diversity). Prior federated learning work for multilingual NLP has focused almost exclusively on high-resource languages, leaving low-resource settings—where compute is scarce, labeled data is minimal, and memorization risk is high—largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw text (news articles for classification, premise–hypothesis sentence pairs for NLI) tokenized to a maximum sequence length of 512, and produces a discrete class label (10 news categories, 3 NLI relations, or 4 MasakhaNEWS topics). Critically, the text is not fed directly to the frozen XLM-RoBERTa backbone; instead, a small prompt encoder first generates a sequence of virtual token embeddings that are interleaved with the discrete token embeddings before the combined sequence enters the PLM, so the model's input is an augmented token sequence rather than the raw text alone.

## Method

The core mechanism is Federated Prompt Averaging: a server maintains a global virtual prompt encoder (≈1.2 M parameters), while each of K clients trains its own local prompt encoder on private, language-specific data with the 270 M-parameter XLM-RoBERTa backbone kept frozen. In each communication round, a subset of clients fetches the global encoder, fine-tunes it locally via gradient descent on the task loss L(x,y;h_k)=Loss(D(v⊕x),y), and sends back only the updated prompt-encoder weights; the server then performs a data-size-weighted FedAvg aggregation. Because only the prompt encoder is transmitted, communication drops from ~110 GB (full-model FL) to ~479 MB per round—a 99% reduction—making the paradigm viable on bandwidth-constrained edge devices. The paper also benchmarks Federated LoRA as an alternative PEFT variant and introduces a language-distance metric (negative log cosine similarity to a token-count-weighted composite of the pre-training corpus) to quantify how much performance degrades as the target language diverges from the pre-trained language, showing that the federated prompt approach degrades far more gracefully than local monolingual fine-tuning.

## Results

On the XNLI task (15 languages), Federated Prompt Tuning achieves 39.83% average accuracy, outperforming monolingual prompt tuning (32.94%) by 6.9% and centralized prompt tuning (34.86%) by 5.0%. On News Classification (5 languages), PE_FL(IID) reaches 83.6% average accuracy versus 64.3% for PE_Monolingual and 77.5% for PE_Centralized. On MasakhaNEWS (African languages), PE_FL with LoRA achieves 81.0% average accuracy, surpassing both PE_Monolingual (73.7%) and PE_Centralized (79.3%). The method trains only ~1.2M parameters (0.43% of the 278M XLM-RoBERTa model), reducing communication cost from 110,592 MB to 479 MB (99% reduction).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the monolingual prompt tuning baseline (Lester et al., 2021), Federated Prompt Tuning wins on every language in the XNLI evaluation, with the largest gains on low-resource languages: Swahili improves from 26.0% to 37.1% (+11.1 pp) and Urdu from 28.1% to 37.8% (+9.7 pp). Against the centralized prompt tuning baseline, the federated approach also outperforms on all 15 XNLI languages, with the average gap of 5.0 pp (39.83% vs. 34.86%). Compared to full-parameter Federated Averaging (McMahan et al., 2017) on the NC task, the proposed PE_FL(IID) achieves 83.6% average accuracy versus 88.4% for full FL(IID), a 4.8 pp trade-off that comes with a 99% reduction in trainable parameters (1.2M vs. 278.7M) and communication cost (479 MB vs. 110,592 MB). Under the more realistic Non-IID setting, PE_FL(Prompt Tuning) retains 80.7% average on NC, still substantially above PE_Monolingual (64.3%) and PE_Centralized (77.5%). On MasakhaNEWS, PE_FL(LoRA) (81.0%) outperforms both baselines, while PE_FL(Prompt Tuning) (76.4%) trails PE_Centralized (79.3%) slightly, suggesting LoRA is more effective for the African language subset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two well-established techniques—virtual prompt tuning (Lester et al., 2021) and Federated Averaging (McMahan et al., 2017)—into a unified paradigm for multilingual NLP under data-sharing restrictions. It departs from prior multilingual FL work (Weller et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2022) that targeted high-resource languages and full-model federated fine-tuning, by restricting trainable parameters to a lightweight prompt encoder and explicitly addressing low-resource languages. The paper also positions itself against the 'curse of multilinguality' (Conneau et al., 2020), arguing that parameter-efficient federated aggregation can mitigate the capacity dilution that harms underrepresented languages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 99% reduction in communication overhead (from ~110 GB to ~479 MB) makes multilingual fine-tuning feasible on bandwidth-constrained edge devices in low-resource regions, which is a practically significant deployment win. The language-distance analysis provides a useful diagnostic for understanding when and why federated prompt tuning helps most. However, the paper explicitly acknowledges the absence of differential privacy, secure aggregation, or gradient-inversion defenses, leaving the privacy story incomplete. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models (7B+), extends to generative tasks, and whether the gains persist under stronger non-IID heterogeneity or more aggressive client dropout.
