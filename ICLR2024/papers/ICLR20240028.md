## Problem

Whitening loss provides a theoretical guarantee against dimensional collapse in self-supervised learning, but it is only one specific spectral transformation over the embedding covariance. The paper asks whether other functions beyond whitening can also prevent collapse and how they modulate the embedding spectrum. Additionally, IterNorm—an approximate whitening method via Newton's iteration that works well in supervised learning—unexpectedly suffers severe dimensional collapse and training failure when applied to SSL, leaving a gap between theory and practice.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two randomly augmented views of a single RGB image (standard augmentations including random crop, color jitter, Gaussian blur, and solarization) and produces a d-dimensional embedding per view through a CNN backbone (ResNet-18 or ResNet-50) followed by a 3-layer MLP projection head. The training objective combines an MSE loss between L2-normalized IterNorm-transformed embeddings and a trace loss on the transformed outputs. For downstream evaluation, the frozen backbone is probed with a linear classifier for image classification or fine-tuned with Mask R-CNN (C4 backbone) for COCO object detection and instance segmentation.

## Method

The paper introduces Spectral Transformation (ST), a framework that maps the eigenvalues of the embedding covariance matrix through a unary function g(·) during the forward pass, with whitening (g(λ)=λ^{-1/2}) as a special case. The proposed INTL method uses IterNorm—a Newton's iteration that approximates the whitening matrix without explicit eigen-decomposition—as the spectral transformation, combined with a novel trace loss that penalizes the deviation of each diagonal element of the transformed output's covariance from 1. The key insight is that IterNorm alone fails in SSL because small eigenvalues cannot be sufficiently stretched toward 1 with a fixed iteration count, and increasing iterations causes numerical divergence; the trace loss creates a synergy that drives all eigenvalues toward equality (equal-eigenvalue spectrum), which is theoretically proven to be a strictly convex optimization with a unique minimum at uniform eigenvalues. This equal-eigenvalue modulation is stronger than hard whitening's full-rank constraint but weaker than soft whitening's identity constraint, and it avoids the numerical instability of explicit eigen-decomposition.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, INTL achieves 92.60% top-1 and 90.03% 5-nn accuracy with ResNet-18, matching or surpassing all baselines on 5-nn. On ImageNet-100, INTL reaches 81.68% top-1 and 73.46% 5-nn, the best among all compared methods. On full ImageNet with ResNet-50, INTL achieves 74.3% top-1 at 800 epochs using only batch size 256 with EMA, matching BYOL (74.3%) which requires batch size 4096. For COCO object detection, INTL attains 40.7 AP, outperforming the next best baselines (W-MSE, Barlow Twins, MoCo v2) at 39.2 AP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

INTL achieves the best 5-nn accuracy across all three small/medium datasets (90.03% on CIFAR-10, 61.90% on CIFAR-100, 73.46% on ImageNet-100), outperforming the next best (CW-RGP at 89.67%, Barlow Twins at 59.40%, Barlow Twins at 72.14%) by 0.36%, 2.50%, and 1.32% respectively. On top-1 accuracy, INTL leads on ImageNet-100 (81.68% vs. BYOL's 80.32%) and is competitive on CIFAR-10 (92.60%, slightly below MoCo V2's 92.94% but above BYOL's 92.58% and Barlow Twins' 92.10%). On full ImageNet with ResNet-50, INTL at batch size 256 with EMA matches BYOL's 74.3% at 800 epochs while using 1/16th the batch size, and without EMA at batch 512 it reaches 73.1%, surpassing Barlow Twins (73.2% at batch 2048) and Zero-CL (72.6% at batch 1024). For COCO transfer, INTL achieves 40.7 AP on detection and 35.4 AP on instance segmentation, beating all baselines including W-MSE (39.2/34.8), Barlow Twins (39.2/34.3), and MoCo v2 (39.2/34.3) by 1.5 and 1.1 AP respectively. INTL also outperforms contrastive methods (SimCLR, MoCo V2, SwAV) and asymmetric methods (BYOL, SimSiam, DINO) on COCO, demonstrating that the spectral transformation approach yields superior transferable representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the whitening loss paradigm (W-MSE, Shuffled-DBN, CW-RGP) into a broader Spectral Transformation (ST) framework, showing that whitening is merely one instance (a power function with exponent -1/2) of a family of spectrum-modulating functions. It repurposes IterNorm (Huang et al., 2019), originally designed as an efficient whitening approximation for supervised learning, as another ST instance in the SSL setting, and identifies that a simple trace loss is needed to make it functional there. The joint embedding architecture and MSE loss on L2-normalized vectors follow the SimSiam formulation, while the theoretical positioning places INTL between hard whitening (full-rank constraint) and soft whitening (identity constraint) as an 'equal-eigenvalues' modulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ST framework provides a principled lens for understanding *why* various collapse-prevention mechanisms work, unifying hard and soft whitening under a single spectral-modulation perspective. The equal-eigenvalues characterization offers a new intermediate constraint that is theoretically stronger than full-rank but more flexible than full whitening, potentially guiding future method design. Limitations include primary validation on CNN backbones (ViT results are in appendix), the trace loss coefficient β being empirically regressed rather than derived, and the framework's applicability beyond joint embedding architectures remaining unexplored. Open questions include whether the ST perspective extends to other SSL paradigms (e.g., masked image modeling) and whether the equal-eigenvalues optimum is truly the best spectral target for downstream task performance.
