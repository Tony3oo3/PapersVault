## Problem

Existing training-free metrics (zero-cost proxies) for neural architecture search suffer from three key limitations: unreliable correlation with ground-truth performance, poor generalisation across different search spaces and tasks, and a bias toward larger models. A fundamental issue is that standard activation patterns used by methods like TE-NAS and Zen-Score have a cardinality upper bound equal to the batch size S, causing metric values to saturate and become indistinguishable when inputs are high-dimensional or networks are deep.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241225/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small batch of input images (e.g., 32 CIFAR-10 images at 32×32) passed through a randomly initialized network from a cell-based search space, requiring only forward passes with no training. It outputs a scalar SWAP-Score (the cardinality of distinct sample-wise activation pattern vectors) that ranks candidate architectures, and optionally a regularised score incorporating a Gaussian penalty on parameter count Θ. When deployed as SWAP-NAS, the final output is a complete cell-based neural network architecture (encoded as an upper-triangular adjacency matrix) ready for standard supervised training.

## Method

SWAP-Score transposes the activation pattern matrix from the standard intermediate-value-wise orientation (vectors of length V per sample, upper bound S) to a sample-wise orientation (vectors of length S per intermediate value, upper bound V). Since V grows exponentially with network depth and input dimensionality while S is fixed by the batch size, this yields a dramatically higher cardinality upper bound, enabling fine-grained discrimination between architectures. A Gaussian regularisation function f(Θ) = exp(−(Θ−μ)²/σ) is multiplied onto the score to control model size and improve correlation in cell-based spaces. The full SWAP-NAS pipeline integrates this metric into an evolutionary search over DARTS-style cell adjacency matrices, using tournament selection, two mutation operators (operation type change and connectivity shift), and crossover to explore the search space in approximately 6 minutes on CIFAR-10 and 9 minutes on ImageNet.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (DARTS search space), SWAP-NAS-C (μ=1.5, σ=1.5) achieves 2.48±0.09% test error with 4.3M parameters in only 0.004 GPU days (6 minutes), outperforming TE-NAS (2.63%, 0.03 GPU days) and DARTS (3.00%, 4 GPU days). On ImageNet, SWAP-NAS (μ=25, σ=25) achieves 24.0% top-1 / 7.6% top-5 error with 5.8M parameters in 0.006 GPU days (9 minutes), beating TE-NAS (24.5% top-1, 0.17 GPU days) and DARTS (26.7% top-1, 4 GPU days). In terms of metric correlation, regularised SWAP-Score achieves a Spearman coefficient of 0.90 with CIFAR-100 validation accuracy on NAS-Bench-201, significantly higher than the second-best metric NWOT (0.80).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SWAP-NAS-C achieves the best test error (2.48%) among all training-free and one-shot methods on CIFAR-10, beating TE-NAS (2.63%) by 0.15% and DARTS (3.00%) by 0.52%, while being 6.5× faster than TE-NAS (0.004 vs. 0.03 GPU days) and 1000× faster than DARTS (0.004 vs. 4 GPU days). On ImageNet, SWAP-NAS achieves 24.0% top-1 error, outperforming TE-NAS (24.5%), DARTS (26.7%), and FairDARTS (26.3%), while being 2.3× faster than the previous fastest training-free method QE-NAS (0.006 vs. 0.02 GPU days). For metric correlation, regularised SWAP-Score outperforms all 15 existing training-free metrics (including NWOT, Zen-Score, ZiCo, SynFlow, SNIP, and zero-cost proxies) across five search spaces and seven tasks, with the largest gap on NAS-Bench-201 CIFAR-100 (0.90 vs. 0.80 for NWOT). The regularised variant shows particularly strong gains in cell-based spaces (NAS-Bench-101/201/301, TransNAS-Bench-101-Micro) but does not improve correlation in the stack-based TransNAS-Bench-101-Macro space. SWAP-NAS-A demonstrates model size control by producing a smaller network (3.06M vs. 4.3M params) at a small accuracy cost (2.65% vs. 2.48%), confirming the regularisation mechanism works as intended.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the network expressivity literature (Montufar et al., 2014; Xiong et al., 2020) by transposing the standard activation pattern counting from intermediate-value-wise to sample-wise, raising the cardinality upper bound from the number of input samples S to the number of intermediate values V. It positions itself within the training-free NAS metric family (NWOT, Zen-Score, TE-NAS, ZiCo) but departs from all of them by operating on sample-wise patterns rather than per-sample or gradient-based statistics. The evolutionary search component is adapted from Real et al. (2019) with SWAP-Score replacing trained evaluation, and a Gaussian regularisation term is added to enable model size control—a capability absent from prior zero-cost proxies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6-minute CIFAR-10 search (0.004 GPU days) is a ~6.5× speedup over the prior fastest training-free NAS (TE-NAS) and ~1000× faster than DARTS, making NAS accessible on modest hardware. The finding that regularisation—intended solely for model size control—also improves Spearman correlation in cell-based spaces (e.g., 0.90 vs 0.80 for NWOT on NAS-Bench-201) is a useful practical observation, though its mechanism is not fully explained. Open questions include whether SWAP-Score generalises to non-ReLU activations (the authors note GELU as future work), transformer-based search spaces, and whether the correlation gains from regularisation hold under distribution shift between search and deployment tasks.
