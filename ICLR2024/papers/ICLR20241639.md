## Problem

Score matching methods for density estimation require computing derivatives and Hessians of the log density function, and when the density is parameterized by a deep network, backpropagating through these derivative terms forces storage of the full computational graph, creating a severe memory bottleneck that limits model depth. Existing mitigations (DSM, SSM) reduce Hessian computation cost but still retain the memory overhead of storing intermediate activations for the derivative terms, restricting the expressiveness of score-based models in deep learning applications.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw data samples—RGB images (64×64×3 CelebA, 32×32×3 CIFAR-10), grayscale images (28×28 MNIST), or tabular vectors (UCI Parkinson/RedWine/WhiteWine, 100- or 200-dimensional Gaussians)—and produces either a score function ∇log p̃_θ(x), a log-likelihood estimate, or generated samples. A key structural detail is that the DEQ layer operates on a hidden fixed point z* ∈ ℝⁿ obtained by iterating a contraction mapping to convergence (typically ~12–19 Picard iterations), and all derivative computations (first- and second-order) are performed solely at this fixed point without referencing any intermediate iterates.

## Method

The paper replaces the core intermediate layers of existing score matching architectures (SSM VAE, DKEF, NICE, NCSN) with a deep equilibrium block defined by the fixed-point equation z* = f_θ(z*, x), where f_θ is a contraction mapping enforced by normalizing the Frobenius norm of the weight matrix below one. First- and second-order derivatives of the log density with respect to the input are computed via the implicit function theorem, yielding expressions involving (I − ∂f_θ/∂z|_{z*})^{-1} applied to Jacobian and Hessian terms of f_θ, all evaluated only at the fixed point. To avoid the expensive explicit matrix inversion, the authors adopt a phantom gradient strategy: after finding z* with autograd disabled, they perform K=2 additional forward iterations from z* with autograd enabled, which Proposition 2 shows converges to the exact implicit derivative as K→∞ via a Neumann series expansion. This yields constant memory usage regardless of the effective depth of the DEQ block, while the theoretical analysis (new in the DEQ context) establishes convergence of both first- and second-order phantom gradient approximations.

## Results

On CelebA, DEQ-SSM VAE achieves a FID of 54.17 at 100k iterations, matching the 16-layer augmented model (54.27) while using only 5.4 GB of memory versus 16 GB, and with 9.7M parameters versus 19M. On CIFAR-10, DEQ-SSM VAE reaches FID 68.13 (vs. 77.35 for the baseline SSM VAE) with 4.4 GB memory. For generative modeling, DEQ-SSM-VR NCSN achieves FID 40.99 on CIFAR-10, nearly matching the full SSM-VR NCSN (40.31) while reducing total training memory from 40.4 GB to 20.1 GB. On density estimation (UCI datasets), DEQ-SM DKEF achieves the best test log-likelihood across Parkinson (-12.4), RedWine (-12.2), and WhiteWine (-13.9) with the lowest memory footprint.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the SSM baseline (Song et al., UAI 2020), the DEQ variants consistently improve FID (e.g., 54.17 vs. 61.85 on CelebA) and log-likelihood (e.g., -12.4 vs. -15.1 on Parkinson) while using comparable or less memory. Against DSM (Vincent, 2011) and CP (Martens et al., 2012), DEQ-SM DKEF achieves substantially better SM loss and test log-likelihood on all three UCI datasets (e.g., -152.1 SM loss vs. -71.2 for DSM on Parkinson). Against the original SM objective (Hyvärinen, 2005), DEQ-SM DKEF improves log-likelihood by 2–3 nats on UCI datasets while using less memory (1.3 GB vs. 1.2 GB for 3-layer SM, but 3.6 GB for 16-layer SM). Against Approx BP (Kingma & LeCun, 2010) on MNIST, DEQ-SSM achieves a test log-likelihood of -1766 versus -2288 for Approx BP. Against the standard NCSN (Song & Ermon, 2019), DEQ-SSM-VR NCSN matches FID within 0.7 points (40.99 vs. 40.31) while halving total training memory (20.1 GB vs. 40.4 GB) and reducing per-iteration time from 871 ms to 481 ms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two established research threads: score matching methods (Hyvärinen 2005; Song et al. 2020 SSM; Vincent 2011 DSM) that estimate densities without normalization constants but incur prohibitive memory costs from storing computational graphs of score/Hessian terms, and Deep Equilibrium Models (Bai et al. 2019) that achieve constant memory by representing infinite-depth networks as fixed-point equations. Prior work on reducing score matching costs (DSM, SSM, curvature propagation) focused on the *computational* cost of Hessian terms, while this paper addresses the orthogonal *memory* bottleneck by leveraging DEQ's implicit differentiation. The key technical departure is extending implicit differentiation to second-order derivatives in the DEQ setting (Proposition 1, eq. 9) and using the phantom gradient (Geng et al. 2021) to avoid explicit matrix inversion, which had not been analyzed for higher-order derivatives in prior DEQ literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical recipe for training deeper score-based models (VAEs, NCSN, DKEF, NICE) with 3–4× memory reduction while matching or exceeding the performance of much deeper shallow architectures, which is directly relevant to scaling diffusion models and density estimators. The contraction constraint (Frobenius norm < 1) and the reliance on simple Picard iteration with K=2 phantom gradient are practical limitations that may restrict expressiveness or convergence speed in very high-dimensional settings. Open questions include whether more sophisticated fixed-point solvers (Anderson acceleration, neural solvers) further improve the trade-off, whether the contraction constraint can be relaxed, and whether the approach extends naturally to multi-step or conditional score matching formulations used in modern diffusion models.
