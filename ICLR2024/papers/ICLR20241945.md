## Problem

In federated learning, client-side step sizes are extremely sensitive to the specific dataset, model architecture, and data heterogeneity level, requiring extensive grid-search tuning for each new task. Even adaptive optimizers like Adam and Adagrad fail to transfer well across settings, and no prior method provides a client-side step size that automatically adapts to each client's local function geometry without any manual tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241945/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client receives a local dataset (500 samples for image tasks, 500 for text) and the current global model parameters, then performs K local gradient steps using mini-batches of size 64 (image) or 16 (text). At each local step, the client computes a self-calibrating step size from the ratio of its own parameter displacement to the change in stochastic gradient, requiring no knowledge of smoothness constants or distance-to-solution. The server aggregates the resulting local parameters via simple averaging (or FedAdam/FedProx) to produce the next global model.

## Method

Δ-SGD assigns each client i a per-iteration step size η_{t,k}^i = min{γ‖x_{t,k}^i − x_{t,k−1}^i‖ / (2‖∇̃f_i(x_{t,k}^i) − ∇̃f_i(x_{t,k−1}^i)‖), √(1+δθ_{t,k−1}^i)·η_{t,k−1}^i}, where the first term approximates the inverse of the local smoothness of f_i and the second term bounds how quickly the step size can grow. This makes each client's step size adapt to its own data distribution and model landscape without requiring global L-smoothness or any problem constants. The paper proves O(1/√T) convergence to a first-order stationary point in a general nonconvex setting under a 'strong growth of dissimilarity' assumption, avoiding the standard global L-smoothness requirement. The method is orthogonal to server-side choices (FedAvg, FedAdam, FedProx, MOON) and requires only a single hyperparameter δ (default 0.1) that has minimal impact on final accuracy.

## Results

On the standard non-iid setting (Dirichlet α=0.1), Δ-SGD achieves 84.5% on CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18), 61.1% on CIFAR-100 (ResNet-18), and 64.1% on CIFAR-100 (ResNet-50), matching or exceeding all baselines without any task-specific tuning. On the easier MNIST and FMNIST tasks, Δ-SGD reaches 98.1% and 86.4% respectively, again at or near the best. Under extreme heterogeneity (α=0.01), Δ-SGD degrades gracefully (e.g., 31.6% on CIFAR-10) while Adam drops from 85.0% to 22.1% and Adagrad from 84.1% to 12.5%. On text classification (DistillBERT), Δ-SGD achieves 90.7% on AGnews and 98.6% on DBpedia, while SGDM, Adam, and Adagrad completely fail at random-level accuracy (25.0% and 7.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Adam, Δ-SGD wins on 4 of 5 image tasks at α=0.1 (FMNIST 86.4 vs 80.4, CIFAR-100 ResNet-18 61.1 vs 55.4, CIFAR-100 ResNet-50 64.1 vs 58.2, MNIST 98.1 vs 96.4), while Adam edges out Δ-SGD by 0.5% on CIFAR-10 (85.0 vs 84.5). Against Adagrad, Δ-SGD dominates across all settings with margins of 6–40 percentage points (e.g., FMNIST 86.4 vs 46.3, CIFAR-100 ResNet-50 64.1 vs 48.0), and Adagrad collapses to near-random under extreme heterogeneity (α=0.01: 12.5% on CIFAR-10). Against SPS (Stochastic Polyak Step Size), Δ-SGD wins on all image tasks at α=0.1 with margins up to 21.9% (CIFAR-100 ResNet-50: 64.1 vs 42.2), and SPS is particularly unstable (1.0% on CIFAR-100 ResNet-18 at α=1). The most dramatic separation appears in the cross-domain text classification experiment, where SGDM, Adam, and Adagrad all fail completely (25.0%/7.1% = random), while Δ-SGD achieves 90.7% (AGnews) and 98.6% (DBpedia) using the same untuned step size rule.

## Relation to Prior Work

Δ-SGD adapts the local-smoothness-based step size from Malitsky & Mishchenko (2020) to the federated setting, where each client independently computes its own step size from the ratio of consecutive parameter updates to gradient differences. It is positioned as orthogonal to server-side adaptive methods (FedAdam, FedYogi) and loss-modification approaches (FedProx, MOON), and unlike prior client-adaptive methods (AdaAlter, Wang et al.) it requires no modification to the server aggregation rule. The closest concurrent work is Mukherjee et al. (2023), which applies Stochastic Polyak Stepsize in FL, but Δ-SGD avoids the need to estimate the distance to the optimum or the optimal function value.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in FL deployment: the expensive, task-specific grid search for client step sizes that is currently standard practice. The convergence guarantee is the first for FL with client-dependent step sizes, and the empirical robustness across datasets, architectures, heterogeneity levels, and even domain shifts (image→text) without re-tuning is a strong practical selling point. Limitations include the non-standard 'strong growth of dissimilarity' assumption in the theory, the restrictive γ = O(1/(K√T)) condition, and the fact that the step size is a single scalar rather than coordinate-wise (unlike Adam/Adagrad). Open directions include per-coordinate adaptivity, asynchronous updates, and integration with more complex local algorithms.
