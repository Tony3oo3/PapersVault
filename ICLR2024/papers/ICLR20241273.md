## Problem

Existing offline preference-based RL methods recover transition-wise rewards from trajectory-level preference labels, a fundamentally ill-posed decomposition that produces inaccurate rewards and misguides subsequent TD-based policy optimization. This paper asks whether one can bypass reward learning entirely and optimize policies directly at the trajectory level where preferences are naturally defined.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241273/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of labeled trajectory pairs (as few as 15–30 preference labels) and a large unlabeled trajectory dataset of full-horizon (state, action) sequences. It produces a deployable state-to-action policy via behavioral cloning on iteratively improved trajectories, and as a byproduct, synthetic high-preference trajectories that can augment standard offline RL datasets. Unlike prior methods that output a reward function, FTB outputs trajectories directly, and the policy is extracted offline without any TD learning at inference time.

## Method

FTB formulates offline PbRL as a conditional generation task: a classifier-free diffusion model (Trajectory Diffuser) is trained to denoise a high-preference trajectory conditioned on a low-preference one, using a 1D convolutional trajectory encoder, U-Net with multi-head cross-attention, and a timestep encoder. To overcome the scarcity of labeled pairs and misaligned performance gaps, Preference Augmentation trains a trajectory-level Bradley-Terry score model on the labeled set, scores all unlabeled trajectories, clusters them into K ascending blocks, and samples training pairs from neighboring blocks to ensure consistent improvement magnitude. At inference, the flow-to-better process iteratively applies the diffusion model to top-k trajectories (with a filter threshold to retain only genuinely improved samples), and a simple MLP policy is extracted via behavioral cloning on the final improved set. This avoids TD learning with inaccurate rewards while producing trajectories that are simultaneously higher-preference, dynamically valid, and novel relative to the original dataset.

## Results

On D4RL locomotion tasks, FTB achieves an average normalized score of 85.5±1.8, trailing only IQL (90.3) among all methods but surpassing TD3+BC (83.4) and all offline PbRL baselines. On MetaWorld manipulation tasks, FTB achieves a 0.70±0.02 success rate, outperforming IQL (0.61), TD3+BC (0.56), and all PbRL methods by a wide margin. Among offline PbRL methods specifically, FTB dominates: it beats the next-best PbRL method (OPRL) by 6.0 points on D4RL (85.5 vs. 79.5) and by 0.33 on MetaWorld (0.70 vs. 0.37). FTB also serves as an effective data augmentation tool, improving TD3+BC from 82.8 to 87.5 average on D4RL when combined with the trajectory diffuser.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FTB consistently outperforms all four offline PbRL baselines. Against OPRL (Shin et al., 2022), FTB gains +6.0 on D4RL average (85.5 vs. 79.5) and +0.33 on MetaWorld (0.70 vs. 0.37). Against PT (Kim et al., 2023), the margin is even larger: +14.6 on D4RL (85.5 vs. 70.9) and +0.37 on MetaWorld. Against IPL (Hejna & Sadigh, 2023), FTB leads by +33.2 on D4RL (85.5 vs. 52.3) and +0.48 on MetaWorld. Against IQL+rψ, FTB wins by +16.1 on D4RL and +0.60 on MetaWorld. Among standard offline RL methods, IQL (Kostrikov et al., 2022) slightly edges FTB on D4RL average (90.3 vs. 85.5), but FTB surpasses IQL on MetaWorld (0.70 vs. 0.61) and matches or beats it on individual tasks like hopper-medium-expert (111.1 vs. 107.4). As a data augmentation method, FTB's trajectory diffuser improves TD3+BC from 82.8 to 87.5 average on D4RL, outperforming SYNTHER (Lu et al., 2023) which only achieves 81.6.

## Relation to Prior Work

FTB departs from the dominant two-step paradigm in offline PbRL (learn a transition-wise reward via Bradley-Terry, then run an off-the-shelf offline RL algorithm like IQL or TD3+BC) by reframing the problem as a conditional generation task: a classifier-free diffusion model (Trajectory Diffuser) directly maps a low-preference trajectory to a higher-preference one, bypassing TD learning entirely. The diffusion architecture draws on DDPM (Ho et al., 2020) and classifier-free guidance (Nichol et al., 2022), while the trajectory-level scoring and block-based sampling for Preference Augmentation repurpose the Bradley-Terry model at the trajectory level rather than the transition level. Among generative-model-for-RL work (Diffuser, Decision Diffuser, SYNTHER), FTB is the first to use a diffusion model for end-to-end trajectory improvement in the offline PbRL setting, and it additionally leverages the generated trajectories as a data augmentation source for standard offline RL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that offline PbRL can avoid the well-known failure mode of inaccurate transition-wise rewards misguiding TD-based policy optimization, which is particularly acute when preference labels are scarce (15–30 pairs here vs. millions in prior work). The finding that trajectory-level preference scores correlate far more strongly with ground truth than transition-wise rewards (Section 4.2) challenges the assumption that reward decomposition is necessary. However, the method's computational cost (1–2 days on an RTX 4090, quadratic scaling with horizon) and its dependence on a broad data distribution (performance degrades on narrow-distribution NeoRL tasks) limit immediate adoption. Open questions include whether latent-space diffusion can reduce compute, whether the approach extends to very long-horizon or partially observable settings, and whether the gains hold under genuinely noisy human preferences at scale.
