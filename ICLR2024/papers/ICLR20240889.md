## Problem

GFlowNets suffer from a credit assignment problem when training with only terminal energy, making it hard to identify which actions contributed to a low-energy outcome. The existing partial inference approach (FL-GFN) addresses this by evaluating intermediate state energies as local credits, but this requires expensive or infeasible energy evaluations at every intermediate state and can produce uninformative signals when energies are sparse or highly variable along trajectories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240889/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial state and a terminal energy function (evaluated only at complete objects) as input, and produces generated objects—molecules, RNA sequences, sets, bags, or independent sets—sampled from the Boltzmann distribution. The potential function architecture mirrors the flow model (e.g., a feedforward network or MPNN with the same hidden dimensions), with its input extended to encode the state transition s_t → s_{t+1}. Training is fully online: the potential is updated from trajectories collected during GFlowNet policy training, requiring no separate energy evaluations at intermediate states.

## Method

LED-GFN decomposes the terminal energy into a sum of learnable potential functions defined on individual state transitions (Eq. 3), then reparameterizes the GFlowNet flow function using these potentials to form a modified detailed-balance or sub-trajectory-balance loss (Eq. 4). The potentials are trained with a dropout-based squared loss (Eq. 5) that simultaneously enforces the sum of potentials to approximate the terminal energy and minimizes variance along the trajectory by randomly dropping individual potentials during training. This dropout mechanism prevents the model from relying on a few specific transitions to satisfy the energy constraint, yielding dense and uniformly distributed local credits. The overall training alternates between sampling trajectories from the current policy, updating the potential model on a buffer of collected trajectories, and updating the GFlowNet policies using the learned potentials as local credit signals, all within a single online loop.

## Results

LED-GFN consistently outperforms all baselines across five domains. On molecule generation, LED-GFN achieves the highest average top-100 score and number of modes among all methods including PPO, GAFN, and FL-GFN. On bag generation with subTB, FL-GFN fails to improve over GFN while LED-GFN shows consistent gains, and on RNA sequence generation, FL-GFN makes only minor improvements over GFN whereas LED-GFN produces noticeable gains. On ideal local credit tasks (set generation, maximum independent set), LED-GFN matches FL-GFN performance. Time cost analysis shows LED-GFN incurs only ~10% overhead over GFN (6.61s vs. 5.80s per round), compared to ~97% overhead for FL-GFN (11.43s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LED-GFN outperforms standard GFN (DB, TB, subTB) across all five tasks, with the largest gains on bag generation and RNA sequence generation where credit assignment is most challenging. Against FL-GFN (the primary partial-inference baseline), LED-GFN clearly wins when intermediate energy is uninformative (bag generation, RNA) but matches FL-GFN on idealized tasks (set generation, maximum independent set) where intermediate energy already provides complete action attribution. LED-GFN also outperforms the RL-based PPO baseline and GAFN on both molecule and RNA generation in terms of top-100 score and mode count. In the ablation comparing alternative energy decomposition schemes (Figure 10), the proposed least-squares-based LED-GFN outperforms the proxy model approach (Jain et al., 2022), LSTM-based return decomposition (Arjona-Medina et al., 2019), and uniform return smoothing (Gangwani et al., 2020) on both molecule and set generation. The only scenario where LED-GFN does not clearly win is the ideal local credit setting, where it ties FL-GFN rather than surpassing it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the partial inference paradigm for GFlowNets introduced by FL-GFN (Pan et al., 2023a), which assigns local credits by evaluating the energy function on intermediate states. LED-GFN departs from FL-GFN by replacing the energy-based local credit with a learned potential function that decomposes the terminal energy into per-transition contributions, drawing directly on return decomposition techniques from sparse-reward RL (Arjona-Medina et al., 2019; Ren et al., 2022). The reparameterization of the flow model mirrors FL-GFN's formulation, but the key novelty is the dropout-regularized least-squares training of potentials, which enforces smoothness along trajectories. The framework is compatible with all standard GFlowNet objectives (DB, TB, subTB), positioning it as a drop-in enhancement rather than a new training paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LED-GFN addresses a practical bottleneck in GFlowNet training: the need to evaluate energy on intermediate states, which can be computationally expensive (e.g., wet-lab assays for RNA) or simply undefined for incomplete objects. The finding that learned potentials match FL-GFN's performance even when intermediate energies provide ideal local credits suggests that the specific form of the local credit matters less than its informativeness and smoothness. Open questions include a theoretical characterization of when learned potentials are sufficient, whether the approach extends to continuous or very long-horizon settings, and whether the dropout-based variance regularization is optimal compared to other smoothing strategies. The ~10% overhead over vanilla GFlowNet makes this practically attractive for the growing GFlowNet community.
