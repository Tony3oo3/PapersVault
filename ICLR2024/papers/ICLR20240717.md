## Problem

Diffusion-based text-to-image models like Stable Diffusion require 20–100+ inference steps to produce acceptable images, and prior distillation methods (e.g., Progressive Distillation) fail in the one-step regime, yielding poor FID scores. No functional one-step diffusion-based T2I generator with SD-level quality had been demonstrated at scale, leaving a significant gap between multi-step diffusion quality and the speed of GAN-based one-step generators.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240717/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text prompt, encoded via a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 text encoder, and produces a 512×512 RGB image in a single forward pass (~0.09s on A100) by performing one Euler step from a Gaussian noise sample in the 8×-downsampled latent space. Optionally, the 512×512 output can be upsampled to 1024×1024 and refined by SDXL-Refiner for higher resolution and finer detail. The same random noise preserves pose and lighting across different text prompts, enabling latent-space interpolation.

## Method

The core insight is that direct distillation from Stable Diffusion fails because its probability flow ODE has curved trajectories and a sub-optimal coupling between noise and image samples. The paper applies a text-conditioned reflow procedure (from Rectified Flow) to straighten these trajectories and refine the noise-to-image coupling, producing a 2-Rectified Flow that serves as a far more distillable teacher. The one-step student is then trained by minimizing a similarity loss (L2 followed by LPIPS) between the teacher's full ODE output and a single Euler step x + v(x|T), with classifier-free guidance applied to the velocity field. For the larger InstaFlow-1.7B variant, two U-Nets are stacked in series (with redundant blocks removed via ablation) to increase capacity while keeping inference at 0.12s. The entire pipeline uses pure supervised learning with no adversarial training, costing only 199 A100 GPU days.

## Results

On MS COCO 2017-5k, InstaFlow-0.9B achieves an FID of 23.4 and CLIP score of 0.304 in just 0.09 seconds per image, dramatically outperforming the previous one-step state-of-the-art Progressive Distillation (FID 37.2). The larger InstaFlow-1.7B further improves to FID 22.4 at 0.12s. On MS COCO 2014-30k, InstaFlow-0.9B reaches FID 13.10 in 0.09s, surpassing StyleGAN-T (FID 13.90 at 0.10s), while InstaFlow-1.7B achieves FID 11.83 at 0.12s. The full training pipeline costs only 199 A100 GPU days, compared to 6,250 for training SD from scratch.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InstaFlow-0.9B (FID-5k 23.4) decisively outperforms Progressive Distillation-SD at 1 step (FID-5k 37.2) by a margin of 13.8 FID points at the same 0.09s inference time, and also beats PD at 2 steps (26.0) and 4 steps (26.4) despite using fewer steps. On MS COCO 2014-30k, InstaFlow-0.9B (FID 13.10, 0.09s) surpasses StyleGAN-T (FID 13.90, 0.10s), the first time a distilled one-step diffusion model matches or beats a large-scale GAN in the ≤0.1s regime. However, GigaGAN (FID 9.09, 0.13s) still leads on MS COCO 2014, and the multi-step SD 1.5 teacher (FID 20.1, 0.88s) retains a quality advantage over InstaFlow-0.9B. Compared to autoregressive models (Parti-20B, FID 7.23) and other diffusion models (Imagen, FID 7.27; eDiff-1, FID 6.95), InstaFlow trades some FID for orders-of-magnitude speedup (0.09s vs. 6.4–32s). The 1.7B variant narrows the gap to the 25-step teacher to just 2.3 FID points (22.4 vs. 20.1) while remaining 7× faster.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Rectified Flow (Liu et al., ICLR 2022), whose reflow procedure had previously been validated only on small datasets like CIFAR-10, to the large-scale text-to-image setting by applying it to Stable Diffusion's probability flow ODE. The key conceptual departure from prior distillation work (Progressive Distillation, Consistency Models) is the diagnosis that direct distillation fails due to sub-optimal noise-image coupling caused by curved ODE trajectories, and that a single reflow step straightens these trajectories sufficiently to make one-step distillation viable. Unlike GAN-based fast generators (StyleGAN-T, GigaGAN) that require adversarial training, InstaFlow achieves comparable speed and quality through pure supervised learning on a least-squares objective, and unlike Progressive Distillation's multi-stage compression, it distills directly from a reflowed teacher in one shot.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

InstaFlow establishes that one-step diffusion-based text-to-image generation is practically achievable at SD-level quality (FID 13.1 on MS COCO 2014-30k in 0.09s), closing a gap that prior distillation methods could not bridge. The finding that reflow is the critical enabler—rather than more complex distillation objectives or adversarial losses—provides a clear, reusable recipe for accelerating other diffusion-based generative models. Limitations include struggles with complex multi-object compositions and the fact that the 2-Rectified Flow teacher was not fully converged, suggesting further gains are possible with more compute. Open questions include whether additional reflow iterations or larger reflow training budgets yield diminishing returns, and whether the approach transfers cleanly to video generation or other modalities.
