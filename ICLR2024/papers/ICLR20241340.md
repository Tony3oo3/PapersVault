## Problem

Designing effective discrete prompts for LLMs requires substantial human expertise, and existing automatic methods either depend on gradient or token-probability access (unavailable through black-box APIs) or over-emphasize either exploration (wasting resources on diverse candidates) or exploitation (getting trapped in local optima). The paper addresses the gap of a gradient-free, black-box-compatible prompt optimizer that balances exploration and exploitation while producing human-readable prompts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241340/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial population of N prompts (a mix of manually crafted instructions and LLM-generated variations), a development set for fitness evaluation (200 samples for classification, 100 for generation, 50 for BBH), and a target LLM (Alpaca-7b or GPT-3.5 text-davinci-003). It iteratively evolves the population over T steps and outputs a single optimized discrete prompt — a natural-language instruction string — selected as the argmax over the final population's dev-set scores. For classification tasks, one demonstration per class is prepended to constrain the output label space.

## Method

EvoPrompt connects LLMs with evolutionary algorithms by using LLMs as the evolutionary operators (mutation and crossover) while EAs govern the selection and update process. Two instantiations are provided: a Genetic Algorithm variant that uses roulette-wheel selection to pick two parent prompts, then instructs GPT-3.5 to perform crossover (inheriting segments from both parents) followed by mutation (introducing random alterations), and a Differential Evolution variant that identifies the differing parts between two randomly selected prompts, mutates only those differences (preserving shared components), combines them with the current best prompt, and crosses over with the base prompt. The key insight is that LLMs can execute these evolutionary operators on discrete natural-language prompts while preserving coherence and readability — something traditional token-level EAs cannot achieve. The population is updated by retaining the top-N prompts (GA) or by 1-vs-1 replacement (DE), and the process requires no access to model parameters or gradients.

## Results

On 7 language understanding datasets (Alpaca-7b), EVOPROMPT (DE) achieves 77.05% average accuracy, outperforming the best manual instruction baseline (71.07%) by ~6 points and APE (73.80%) by ~3.3 points. On BIG-Bench Hard (22 tasks, GPT-3.5), EVOPROMPT (DE) reaches 75.03% average normalized accuracy versus 71.49% for the standard CoT baseline and 71.85% for APE, with up to 25% improvement on individual tasks. On summarization (SAMSum), EVOPROMPT (DE) improves ROUGE-1 from 35.92 (MI) to 39.46 on Alpaca-7b and from 43.95 to 46.49 on GPT-3.5. On text simplification (ASSET), EVOPROMPT (DE) achieves SARI 46.21 (Alpaca) and 47.40 (GPT-3.5), surpassing the manual baseline by over 3 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvoPrompt (DE) outperforms all baselines on the 7-dataset language understanding benchmark: it beats MI (Zhang et al., 2023b) by 6.0 points in average accuracy (77.05 vs. 71.07), NI (Mishra et al., 2022b) by 8.8 points, and APE (Zhou et al., 2022) by 3.3 points. Against APO (Pryzant et al., 2023), which is only reported on 4 datasets, EvoPrompt (DE) wins on SST-2 (94.75 vs. 93.87) and Subj (75.55 vs. 70.55) while matching on CR (91.40 vs. 91.20). PromptSource (Bach et al., 2022) is only available for 3 datasets and is dominated by EvoPrompt on all of them (e.g., 73.82 vs. 45.43 on AG's News). On summarization (SAMSum), EvoPrompt (DE) surpasses the MI baseline (Sanh et al., 2021) by 3.5 ROUGE-1 points on Alpaca and 2.5 on GPT-3.5, and beats APE by 4.0 ROUGE-1 on Alpaca. On simplification (ASSET), EvoPrompt (DE) edges out APE by 0.31 SARI on Alpaca and 0.69 on GPT-3.5, while improving over the MI baseline (Zhang et al., 2023c) by 3.18 and 3.60 SARI respectively. On BBH, EvoPrompt (DE) achieves 75.03% average versus 71.85% for APE and 71.49% for the standard CoT baseline, with the largest single-task gain reaching 25%.

## Relation to Prior Work

EvoPrompt builds on two research threads: (1) automatic discrete prompt optimization, where prior methods like APE emphasize exploration via Monte Carlo search and APO/Grips emphasize exploitation via pseudo-gradient edits, and (2) the emerging line of work showing LLMs can imitate individual evolutionary operators (mutation in Lehman et al., crossover in Meyerson et al.). The key departure is providing a *general* framework that connects LLMs with complete EA algorithms (GA and DE) rather than isolated operators, and using the EA's selection/update mechanisms to balance exploration and exploitation in a principled way that neither APE nor APO achieves.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's generality—being instantiable with different EAs through customized evolutionary and selection processes—opens a path toward using LLMs as interpretable interfaces for a broader class of conventional optimization algorithms (PSO, ACO, Quality-Diversity), which the authors explicitly flag as future work. Practical limitations include evaluation on only two LLMs (GPT-3.5, Alpaca-7b) and two EA variants, and the average BBH improvement (3.5%) is modest despite the 25% peak. An open question is whether the framework's advantages persist as base LLMs improve, since the value of prompt engineering may diminish with stronger models.
