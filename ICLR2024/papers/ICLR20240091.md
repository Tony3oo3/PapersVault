## Problem

Existing hypergraph neural networks suffer from poor controllability: they achieve good results only with 1–2 layers, and adding more layers causes rapid performance degradation due to uncontrolled message smoothing. While graph ODE-based methods (GDE, CGNN, GraphCON) successfully bridge graph evolution and dynamic systems to enable deeper networks, directly extending these to hypergraphs is non-trivial because diffusion in pairwise (graph) and high-order (hypergraph) structures follow fundamentally different paradigms. This paper addresses the gap by introducing a theoretical framework for hypergraph dynamic systems and a practical ODE-based architecture that supports stable, controllable long-range diffusion.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a hypergraph defined by its incidence matrix H ∈ {0,1}^{|V|×|E|}, a vertex feature matrix Z_v ∈ R^{|V|×c}, and a hyperedge feature matrix Z_e ∈ R^{|E|×c} (which, when unavailable, is initialized as the degree-normalized aggregation of vertex features). It produces vertex representations Y_v and hyperedge representations Y_e at a user-chosen termination time T, which are then fed to a linear classifier for semi-supervised vertex classification. A key design detail is that the control step (the only learnable component) is applied every s iterations rather than every layer, while the parameter-free diffusion step runs at every time step, keeping the per-layer cost dominated by sparse matrix–vector products with H.

## Method

HDS^ode formulates hypergraph representation learning as a continuous ODE dX/dt = g(X) + AX, where g is a learnable control function (a single FC layer per vertex/hyperedge) and A is a parameter-free diffusion matrix built from the incidence matrix with teleport probabilities α_v and α_e. The ODE is discretized via Lie-Trotter splitting into alternating control and diffusion steps: the control step adds a residual update σ(WX+b), while the diffusion step performs degree-normalized aggregation between vertices and hyperedges (X_v ← (1−α_v)X_v + α_v D_v^{-1}HX_e and symmetrically for hyperedges). Stability is guaranteed by construction: the eigenvalues of A lie in the closed left half-plane (Proposition 5.1), and the multiplicity of zero eigenvalues equals the number of connected components (Proposition 5.2), so the diffusion step alone converges to a class-separable fixed point. This design allows the network to be stacked to 16+ layers without over-smoothing, in contrast to HGNN/HGNN+ which degrade beyond 4 layers, and the diffusion step with α_v=α_e=1 reduces to a parameter-free HGNN+ layer, establishing a formal connection to existing hypergraph message-passing.

## Results

On the transductive semi-supervised vertex classification task across 7 hypergraph benchmarks, HDS^ode achieves the best test accuracy on all datasets, with an average rank of 1.0. Headline results include 68.92% on Cora-CA, 83.05% on DBLP-CA, 76.75% on News20, 44.26% on IMDB4k-CA, 42.30% on IMDB4k-CD, 93.85% on DBLP4k-CC, and 69.52% on DBLP4k-CP. A key distinguishing property is that HDS^ode maintains stable performance with 16 or more layers, whereas HGNN and HGNN+ peak at 2 layers and degrade sharply beyond 4 layers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HDS^ode outperforms all 8 baselines on every one of the 7 transductive datasets. Against the strongest hypergraph baselines, it beats HGNN+ (avg. rank 3.7) by margins of 0.3–1.8% (e.g., 68.92 vs. 66.85 on Cora-CA, 69.52 vs. 68.76 on DBLP4k-CP) and HGNN (avg. rank 4.0) by 0.3–1.3%. Against graph ODE methods, HDS^ode surpasses GDE (avg. rank 4.9) by 1.3–2.5% and GraphCON (avg. rank 4.4, OOM on News20 and DBLP4k-CC) by 1.5–2.4% where comparable. Against the unified hypergraph frameworks UniGCN (5.4) and UniSAGE (4.7), gains range from 0.3% to 2.5%. The graph-only baselines GCN (7.6) and GraphSAGE (8.0) trail by 2–5% on most datasets, confirming that hypergraph structure is essential for high-order correlation modeling. The paper further shows that HDS^ode's advantage grows with depth: while HGNN and HGNN+ collapse beyond 4 layers, HDS^ode stabilizes at 16+ layers, a property no baseline shares.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: graph ODE methods (GDE, CGNN, GraphCON) that model continuous representation dynamics on pairwise graph structures, and hypergraph neural networks (HGNN, HGNN+, UniGNN) that capture high-order correlations but suffer from rapid performance degradation beyond 2–3 layers. The key conceptual move is extending the ODE-based continuous dynamics framework from graphs to hypergraphs via a control-diffusion decomposition discretized with Lie-Trotter splitting, where the diffusion step is parameter-free and governed by a carefully designed incidence-matrix-based operator. The paper also formalizes the relationship to HGNN+ by showing that masking the control term and setting teleport probabilities to 1 recovers a linear HGNN+ layer, positioning HDS^ode as a strict generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in hypergraph learning: existing HGNNs cannot be deepened without over-smoothing, limiting their ability to capture long-range correlations. By providing eigenvalue-based stability guarantees and a parameter-free diffusion step, HDS^ode offers a principled path to deeper hypergraph architectures. Open questions include whether the framework extends to partial differential equations or time-varying hyperedge structures (noted by the authors), how it scales to very large hypergraphs where the O(|V|+|E|)c² control step cost becomes prohibitive, and whether the teleport probability heuristics (small α_v, large α_e) generalize beyond the tested datasets. The modest 1–2% gains over HGNN+ on most benchmarks suggest the practical ceiling may be limited to settings where long-range propagation is critical (e.g., few-shot label regimes).
