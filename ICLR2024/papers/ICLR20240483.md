## Problem

Prevailing fidelity metrics for evaluating GNN explanations (Fid_+, Fid_-, Fid_Δ) implicitly assume that the model can make accurate predictions on subgraphs obtained by removing or isolating the explanation, but these subgraphs are often out-of-distribution relative to the training data. This paper identifies this OOD vulnerability as a fundamental flaw, provides an information-theoretic formalization of explainability that exposes when existing metrics fail, and introduces a robust class of surrogate fidelity measures that remain well-behaved under distribution shift.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained GNN model (used as a black-box predictor), an input graph G̅, and a candidate explanation subgraph Ψ(G̅) as input, and outputs a non-negative fidelity score. Unlike the original metrics that perform a single binary edge-removal operation, the proposed measures require M Monte Carlo samples per graph (default M=50) to estimate the expected prediction difference under stochastic edge erasure with rate α₁ (for Fid_{α1,+}) or stochastic edge addition from the non-explanation subgraph with rate α₂ (for Fid_{α2,-}). The empirical estimator converges at rate β=1/2 in the number of observations, matching the original metrics.

## Method

The paper first formalizes explainability via conditional mutual information I(Ŷ; G̅ | 1_Ψ(G̅)), defining when a subgraph is a sufficient statistic of the input with respect to the label. It then introduces a generalized fidelity class Fid_{α1,+} = E(P̂(Y) − P̂^{α1,+}(Y)) and Fid_{α2,−} = E(P̂(Y) − P̂^{α2,−}(Y)), where P̂^{α1,+} is the model's prediction on the graph with only a fraction α₁ of explanation edges removed (rather than all), and P̂^{α2,−} is the prediction on the explanation subgraph augmented with a fraction α₂ of non-explanation edges (rather than the explanation alone). This stochastic edge sampling keeps the perturbed subgraphs in-distribution, directly addressing the OOD failure mode. Proposition 3 proves that Fid_{α1,α2,Δ} is monotonically aligned with the information-theoretic explainability measure for a general class of motif-based tasks, whereas the original Fid_Δ is not. The parameters α₁ and α₂ are set to 0.1 and 0.9 respectively in experiments, and the approach requires no model retraining or access to internal parameters.

## Results

The proposed robust fidelity measures (Fid_{α1,+}, Fid_{α2,-}, Fid_{α1,α2,Δ}) achieve Spearman correlation coefficients of |r| ≥ 0.990 with the gold-standard edit distance across all four datasets (Tree-Cycles, Tree-Grid, BA-2motifs, MUTAG) for both GCN and GIN models, with most values reaching exactly ±1.000. In contrast, the original Fid_+ metric exhibits highly inconsistent correlations ranging from 0.095 to 1.000 (with mixed signs), and Fid_Δ ranges from -0.286 to 1.000 depending on dataset and architecture. The AUC-based edge classification metric (used in prior work) achieves perfect -1.000 correlation, confirming the validity of the gold standard. The proposed metrics use α₁ = 0.1 and α₂ = 0.9 with 50 Monte Carlo samples per evaluation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed robust fidelity measures against the original Fid₊ (Pope et al., 2019) and Fid₋/Fid_Δ (Yuan et al., 2022) using Spearman correlation with gold-standard edit distance as the evaluation criterion. The proposed Fid_{α₁,+} consistently achieves |r| ≥ 0.990 across all 8 model-dataset combinations, whereas the original Fid₊ shows highly variable correlations (0.095 to 1.000) with inconsistent signs, indicating it frequently fails to rank explanations correctly. Similarly, Fid_{α₂,−} achieves |r| ≥ 0.993 in all settings, while the original Fid₋ ranges from −0.276 to 1.000 with mixed signs. The combined Fid_{α₁,α₂,Δ} achieves |r| ≥ 0.990 universally, outperforming Fid_Δ which ranges from −0.286 to 1.000. The most dramatic failures of the original metrics occur on Tree-Cycles (Fid₊ = 0.229, Fid_Δ = 0.105 for GCN) and MUTAG (Fid₊ = −0.190, Fid_Δ = −0.105 for GCN), where the proposed metrics still achieve near-perfect ±1.000 correlation. The only exception is GIN on Tree-Grid, where both original and proposed metrics achieve |r| = 1.000, attributed to GIN's limited generalization on that node classification task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the fidelity-based evaluation framework for GNN explainability introduced by Pope et al. (2019) for Fid_+ and Yuan et al. (2022) for Fid_-, identifying a fundamental out-of-distribution flaw: when explanation subgraphs are removed or isolated, the resulting inputs fall outside the training distribution, invalidating the classifier's predictions used in the metric. The authors ground their fix in an information-theoretic formalization of explainability (mutual information as sufficient statistic), drawing on Fano's inequality and reverse Fano's inequality to connect task-level and classifier-level explainability. Unlike concurrent approaches that address OOD through adversarial robustness (OAR) or fine-tuning (GInX-Eval), the proposed stochastic edge-sampling mechanism operates as a black-box, deterministic-in-expectation correction that requires no model retraining or internal parameter access.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard fidelity metrics (Fid_+, Fid_-, Fid_Δ) can rank a random or adversarial explanation above the ground-truth explanation undermines the reliability of results published across major GNN explainability platforms (GraphFramEx, GraphXAI, GNNX-BENCH, DIG). The proposed fix is lightweight and practical, but the theoretical well-behavedness guarantees (Proposition 3) require motif-based classification with specific distributional assumptions, limiting generality. A key open limitation acknowledged by the authors is that all fidelity-based measures ignore explanation size, so redundant explanations (e.g., the entire graph) still achieve trivially optimal scores. Extending the information-theoretic framework beyond subgraph explanations to other explanation granularities or non-graph modalities remains an open direction.
