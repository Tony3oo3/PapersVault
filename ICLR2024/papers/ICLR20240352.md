## Problem

When training data contains composite class labels (e.g., {husky, wolf}) due to visual ambiguity, existing DNN methods either force singleton predictions (partial label learning) or generate composite sets based on high prediction entropy rather than training-data evidence. No prior method quantifies the predictive uncertainty specifically attributable to composite labels in the training set, leaving a gap in understanding how much a model's vagueness stems from ambiguous training evidence versus other uncertainty sources.

## Input / Output

The network takes a standard RGB image and outputs a non-negative evidence vector of dimension K+η, where K is the number of singleton classes and η is the number of composite sets forming a partition of the class space. From this evidence vector, the method derives a predicted label (singleton or composite set via argmax over evidence), a projected class probability vector via the GDD expectation, and three uncertainty measures: vagueness (belief mass on composite sets), vacuity (lack of total evidence), and dissonance (conflicting evidence). The composite sets are constrained to form a partition, which enables the analytical normalization constant of the Grouped Dirichlet Distribution.

## Method

HENN replaces the standard softmax output layer with a Softplus activation that produces non-negative evidence values for both singleton classes and composite sets, parameterizing a Grouped Dirichlet Distribution (GDD) over the class-probability simplex. Training uses a novel Uncertainty Partial Cross Entropy (UPCE) loss, defined as the expectation of partial cross-entropy under the GDD, which has a closed-form expression involving digamma functions and generalizes the standard UCE loss for singleton labels. A KL-divergence regularization term is added to prevent the network from simultaneously inflating evidence for both a singleton class and its parent composite set, a pathology proven to arise from UPCE alone (Proposition 1). The key theoretical contribution is showing that UPCE has a lower bound equal to PCE evaluated at the GDD mean, ensuring the network learns to assign high projected probabilities to classes within the ground-truth composite set. The vagueness measure—sum of belief masses on composite sets—provides a principled, interpretable uncertainty quantification that is distinct from vacuity and dissonance.

## Results

On TinyImageNet (M=10, 3×3 kernel), HENN achieves 84.4% OverJS, 93.4% CompJS, and 82.5% singleton accuracy, outperforming all five baselines. The most striking gain is in CompJS (composite Jaccard Similarity), where HENN exceeds the next-best baseline (DNN at 66.9%) by over 26 percentage points on TinyImageNet and by 15–20 points on Living17 and Nonliving26. Singleton accuracy improvements are more modest but consistent: HENN leads DNN by 2.7% on TinyImageNet, 2.3% on Living17, and 2.5% on Nonliving26. HENN also achieves the highest AUROC for separating composite from singleton samples using its vagueness measure, outperforming ENN's vacuity/dissonance and DNN's entropy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HENN outperforms all five baselines on every metric across all three main datasets. Against DNN (EfficientNet-b3), HENN gains ~1% OverJS and 2–3% Acc, but the CompJS advantage is dramatic: 93.4% vs. 66.9% on TinyImageNet (+26.5 pts), 96.5% vs. 81.0% on Living17 (+15.5 pts), and 96.8% vs. 62.0% on Nonliving26 (+34.8 pts). ENN is competitive on singleton accuracy (80.7% vs. 82.5% on TinyImageNet) but lags significantly on CompJS (63.5% vs. 93.4%), confirming that standard evidential networks cannot capture composite-label uncertainty. RAPS (conformal prediction) is the closest competitor on OverJS and Acc but produces overly broad prediction sets, yielding only 43.6% CompJS on TinyImageNet versus HENN's 93.4%. E-CNN (Dempster-Shafer) and PiCO (contrastive partial-label learning) are substantially weaker across all metrics, with E-CNN scoring below 35% CompJS on every dataset, indicating that neither DST-based set prediction nor contrastive disambiguation effectively handles the composite-label setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

HENN extends Evidential Neural Networks (ENNs), which model classification uncertainty via Dirichlet distributions (multinomial opinions), to the richer framework of Subjective Logic hyper-opinions represented by Grouped Dirichlet Distributions (GDDs). This generalization introduces a new uncertainty type—vagueness—that captures uncertainty arising specifically from composite class labels in training data, a dimension absent from standard ENN uncertainty measures (vacuity, dissonance). The UPCE loss function generalizes the UCE loss used in ENNs, and the KL-divergence regularization addresses a theoretical deficiency (evidence inflation) identified through Proposition 1. The paper positions itself against partial label learning (which predicts only singleton labels) and conformal prediction (which generates sets based on entropy rather than composite training evidence), arguing that neither provides the composite-label-specific uncertainty quantification that HENN offers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of vagueness as a distinct uncertainty type fills a genuine gap: existing measures (vacuity, dissonance, entropy) conflate different sources of uncertainty and cannot specifically flag predictions that are uncertain because the training evidence was composite. The practical implication is that safety-critical systems (medical imaging, surveillance) can now distinguish 'I'm uncertain because the image is blurry' from 'I'm uncertain because the classes are genuinely hard to separate.' Limitations include the assumption that composite sets form a partition of the class space (restricting to disjoint superclasses), the UAP assumption in theoretical propositions, and evaluation on only four datasets with synthetic blurring. Open questions include whether the partition assumption can be relaxed to overlapping composite sets, how the method scales to very large class hierarchies, and whether the vagueness measure remains discriminative under more natural (non-synthetic) label ambiguity.
