## Problem

State-of-the-art text-to-image diffusion models demand prohibitive training resources—SDv1.5 requires ~6,250 A100 GPU days (~$320,000) and RAPHAEL requires ~60,000 A100 GPU days (~$3,080,000)—creating a significant barrier for the AIGC community and startups to develop their own high-quality generators. The paper asks whether a competitive T2I model can be trained at a fraction of this cost without sacrificing image quality or semantic alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242246/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text prompt encoded by a frozen Flan-T5-XXL (4.3B) into 120 text tokens (longer than the standard 77 to accommodate denser captions) and produces photorealistic RGB images up to 1024×1024 pixels. Multi-aspect augmentation across 40 aspect-ratio buckets (0.25 to 4) enables arbitrary aspect ratio generation, and inference uses DPM-Solver with 20 steps. The diffusion process operates in the latent space of a frozen LDM VAE, so the actual pixel-level generation is decoupled from the Transformer's denoising.

## Method

PIXART-α decomposes T2I training into three sequential stages: (1) pixel dependency learning via a class-conditional DiT-XL/2 pretrained on 1M ImageNet images, (2) text-image alignment on 10M SAM images with LLaVA-generated dense captions, and (3) aesthetic fine-tuning on 14M high-quality images (JourneyDB + internal) at increasing resolutions (256→512→1024). Architecturally, it modifies DiT by inserting a cross-attention layer (zero-initialized output projection) between self-attention and FFN for text injection, and replacing per-block adaLN MLPs with a single global time-embedding MLP shared across all blocks plus lightweight layer-specific trainable embeddings (adaLN-single), reducing parameters by 26% and GPU memory by 21%. A reparameterization trick initializes the layer-specific embeddings so the model directly loads pretrained class-conditional DiT weights, transferring ImageNet pixel-distribution knowledge without retraining. The data pipeline uses LLaVA-7B to auto-label the SAM dataset, raising average noun count per image from 6.4 (LAION) to 29.3 and valid distinct noun ratio from 8.5% to 18.6%, enabling faster text-image alignment convergence.

## Results

PIXART-α achieves a zero-shot FID-30K of 7.32 on MSCOCO, matching Imagen (7.27) while using only 10.5% of its training time (753 vs. 7,132 A100 GPU days) and 1.25% of its training data (25M vs. 2B images). On T2I-CompBench, PIXART-α attains the best or second-best score on 5 out of 6 compositional metrics (e.g., Color 0.6690, Texture 0.6477, Complex 0.3433). In a 50-participant user study, PIXART-α outperforms SDv2 by 7.2% in perceived image quality and 42.4% in text-image alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SDv1.5 (LDM), PIXART-α improves FID from 9.62 to 7.32 while requiring only 12% of the training time (753 vs. 6,250 A100 GPU days) and 1.25% of the training data (25M vs. 2B images). Against Imagen, PIXART-α achieves a near-identical FID (7.32 vs. 7.27) with roughly 10% of the GPU days. Against RAPHAEL, PIXART-α trades a modest FID gap (7.32 vs. 6.61) for a 98.75% reduction in training time and 99.5% less data, at a cost of ~$28,400 versus ~$3,080,000. On T2I-CompBench, PIXART-α surpasses SDXL on Color (0.6690 vs. 0.5879), Shape (0.4927 vs. 0.4687), Texture (0.6477 vs. 0.5299), and Complex (0.3433 vs. 0.3237), while SDXL leads on Spatial (0.2133 vs. 0.2064). In the user study, PIXART-α is preferred over DALL-E 2, SDv2, SDXL, and DeepFloyd on both image quality and text-image alignment. Visual comparisons in Figures 8–9 show PIXART-α performing on par with or exceeding RAPHAEL, Midjourney, and ERNIE-ViLG 2.0 on artist-selected prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

PIXART-α builds directly on the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture by adding cross-attention layers for text conditioning and replacing the per-block adaLN class-condition MLPs with a shared adaLN-single module plus layer-specific embeddings, enabling direct loading of ImageNet-pretrained class-condition weights via a reparameterization trick. The training pipeline departs from the standard practice of training T2I models from scratch on billions of images by decomposing learning into three sequential stages (pixel dependency, text-image alignment, aesthetic refinement) and by substituting raw LAION captions with LLaVA-generated dense pseudo-captions on the SAM dataset. The latent-space diffusion framework and VAE encoder are inherited from Stable Diffusion, while the text encoder (Flan-T5-XXL) follows Imagen's recipe.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The headline result—matching or exceeding SDv1.5-level FID with only 12% of the training time and 1.25% of the training data—dramatically lowers the barrier for startups and individual researchers to build competitive T2I models, with a 90% reduction in CO₂ emissions. The paper candidly notes that FID may be a misleading metric for aesthetic quality and that the model still struggles with precise counting, hand anatomy, and in-image text rendering, leaving room for future refinement. Open questions include whether the three-stage decomposition and high-density captioning strategy generalize to video generation or other modalities, and whether scaling PIXART-α's 0.6B parameters further would close the remaining gap to larger models like RAPHAEL.
