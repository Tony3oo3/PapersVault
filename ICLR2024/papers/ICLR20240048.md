## Problem

Deep neural networks exhibit unwarranted confidence on out-of-distribution inputs, and while Neural Collapse (NC) properties have been well-characterized for in-distribution data during the terminal phase of training, no prior work has identified how NC geometry interacts with OOD data. The paper introduces a fifth NC property (NC5: ID/OOD orthogonality) and asks whether the emergent Simplex ETF structure can be directly exploited as a post-hoc OOD detection criterion without modifying the training procedure.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image, extracts its penultimate-layer feature vector from a pre-trained DNN, and projects it onto a PCA subspace fitted on the training set's features. The output is a scalar OOD score (lower = more likely OOD) compared against a threshold determined from the ROC curve on validation data. For vision transformer models where the penultimate layer dimension is smaller than the number of classes, the score is additionally multiplied by the maximum logit to inject class-based calibration.

## Method

NECO is a post-hoc OOD detection score that computes the ratio of the L2 norm of a feature's projection onto the top-d principal components of the ID training data to the L2 norm of the full feature vector. The key insight (NC5) is that during the terminal phase of training, OOD data becomes increasingly orthogonal to the ID class-mean Simplex ETF structure, so OOD features project to near-zero in the principal subspace while ID features retain large projections. Unlike ViM and NuSA, which project onto the null space of the classifier weight matrix, NECO projects onto the principal space of the ID data, circumventing the need to explicitly identify the null space. The PCA dimension d is selected per dataset-model pair to approximate the Simplex ETF subspace, and a theoretical theorem (NC1+NC2+NC5 imply NECO) guarantees that the OOD global mean maps to zero while ID samples remain non-zero. The method requires no retraining, only a one-time PCA fit on the training set's penultimate-layer representations.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with ViT-B/16, NECO achieves an average AUROC of 94.05% and FPR95 of 27.51% across five OOD datasets (ImageNet-O, Textures, iNaturalist, SUN, Places365), outperforming the second-best method ViM (93.56% AUROC, 29.46% FPR95) by 1.95% in FPR95. On SwinV2, NECO achieves the best FPR95 on all five OOD datasets, reducing it by 7.47% on average compared to the second-best method. On CIFAR-100 (ViT), NECO reaches 99.44% average AUROC and 2.47% FPR95, and on CIFAR-10 (ViT) it achieves 95.41% AUROC and 19.43% FPR95, surpassing all baselines on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NECO outperforms all 12 baselines on average FPR95 for ViT-B/16 on ImageNet-1K (27.51% vs. 29.46% for ViM, the closest competitor), and achieves the highest average AUROC (94.05% vs. 93.56% for ViM). The only per-dataset AUROC loss is on iNaturalist, where Mahalanobis edges NECO by 0.33% (99.67 vs. 99.34), though NECO still wins on FPR95 for that dataset. For SwinV2, NECO achieves the best FPR95 on every OOD dataset (59.19% average vs. 66.66% for Softmax score) but ranks third in average AUROC (80.56%), behind Energy+ReAct (81.44%) and Mahalanobis (80.99%). On CIFAR-100 with ViT, NECO dominates with 99.44% average AUROC and 2.47% FPR95, beating the next-best ViM (99.16% / 2.84%) and ASH-P (98.90% / 3.74%). With ResNet-18 on CIFAR-100, NECO improves the best baseline FPR95 by 8.67% (39.12% vs. 45.56% for ViM), though it slightly underperforms on the CIFAR-100-ID/CIFAR-10-OOD sub-task due to label overlap between the two datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

NECO builds directly on the Neural Collapse framework (Papyan et al., 2020) by introducing a fifth property (NC5: ID/OOD orthogonality) and using the Simplex ETF geometry as the detection mechanism, rather than the null-space projection used by ViM (Wang et al., 2022) or NuSA (Cook et al., 2020). While Haas et al. (2023) showed that collapsed models incidentally detect OOD better, this is the first work to explicitly derive an OOD score from NC structure. Conceptually, NECO inverts the ViM/NuSA logic: instead of measuring how far a sample falls into the null space (outside ID structure), it measures how much of the sample's norm lies within the principal (ETF) subspace, with the theoretical guarantee that OOD data projects to near-zero in that subspace.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The NC5 orthogonality property provides a geometric explanation for why overparameterized networks separate ID and OOD data, and the theoretical result (Theorem 4.1) gives practitioners confidence that the method is not merely heuristic. The method is trivially implementable post-hoc (one PCA + a norm ratio), making it easy to deploy on existing production models without retraining. Limitations include degraded performance on DeiT (whose distillation training may impede collapse) and on CIFAR-100→CIFAR-10 (label overlap), and the paper raises the open question of whether training strategies can be explicitly designed to maximize NC5 for improved OOD detection.
