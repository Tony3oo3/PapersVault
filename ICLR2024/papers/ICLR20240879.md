## Problem

Prior work established that neural network generalization error scales with the intrinsic dimension of the training set, but the scaling rate differs drastically between natural and medical imaging domains with no theoretical explanation. This paper asks: what measurable, model-independent dataset property accounts for this domain-dependent discrepancy in generalization behavior?

## Input / Output

The method consumes binary classification image datasets (resized to 224×224, linearly normalized to [0,1]) and trained CNN models, producing a set of scaling laws rather than a single prediction. A key intermediate output is the empirical label sharpness K̂_F, computed as the maximum ratio of label difference to L2 image distance over M=1000 class-balanced random samples (<1 sec to compute). The final outputs include a model-independent generalization scaling law (log L vs. d_data and K_F), a negative correlation between K_F and adversarial robustness across FGSM budgets, and a theoretical bound d_repr ≲ d_data relating learned representation dimensionality to dataset dimensionality.

## Method

The paper introduces label sharpness K_F (the Lipschitz constant of the ground-truth labeling function) as a new measurable dataset property and shows it explains the generalization scaling discrepancy between imaging domains. By proving K_f converges to K_F in probability as N→∞ (Theorem 2), the authors reduce the generalization bound to L ≈ O(K_F · N^{−1/d_data}), a model-independent scaling law where K_F is the only term differing between domains with matched d_data and N. They further derive an adversarial robustness scaling law (expected robust radius ≳ Ω(1/K_F)) and a representation-dimension scaling law L ≈ O(N^{−1/d_repr}), then reconcile the two to show d_data upper-bounds d_repr. All theoretical results are validated across six CNN architectures (ResNet-18/34/50, VGG-13/16/19) on eleven datasets (seven medical, four natural) over training set sizes N ∈ {500,…,1750}, with MLE and TwoNN estimators for intrinsic dimension.

## Results

The paper establishes that generalization error scales as L ∝ K_F · N^(−1/d_data), where K_F is the proposed label sharpness metric. Medical image datasets exhibit substantially higher label sharpness (K̂_F ≈ 2.5×10⁻⁴) than natural image datasets (K̂_F ≈ 1×10⁻⁴), explaining the steeper generalization degradation with d_data observed for medical data. For adversarial robustness, the Pearson correlation r between FGSM test-loss penalty and K̂_F ranges from 0.18 (ResNet-34, ε=8/255) to 0.77 (ResNet-18, ε=1/255), confirming that higher label sharpness implies greater vulnerability to attack. Finally, d_repr ≤ d_data holds empirically across all six models and eleven datasets, validating the theoretical upper bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical-empirical analysis paper rather than a method comparison, so there are no traditional baselines. The central comparison is between natural and medical imaging domains: at similar d_data and training set size N, models trained on medical data show substantially worse generalization error, which the authors attribute to the ~2.5× higher K_F of medical datasets. The proposed scaling law L ∝ K_F · N^(−1/d_data) is validated within each domain (log L ≈ −(1/d_data)·log N + const.), and a likelihood analysis (Appendix C.1) confirms that including K_F increases the probability of the observed generalization data under the model. For adversarial robustness, medical datasets (higher K_F) are consistently more susceptible to FGSM attacks across all six architectures and four ε budgets, corroborating prior findings by Ma et al. (2021). The d_repr ≤ d_data bound (Theorem 5) is verified empirically for all model–dataset–size triplets, with d_repr typically well below d_data, consistent with the information-bottleneck interpretation that learned representations discard irrelevant detail.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the intrinsic-dimension scaling law framework of Bahri et al. (2021) and Pope et al. (2020) by introducing a new measurable dataset property—label sharpness K_F—and proving that the model's Lipschitz constant K_f converges to K_F for large training sets, yielding a model-independent generalization scaling law. It also builds on Ansuini et al. (2019) to derive an analogous scaling law for learned representation intrinsic dimension d_repr, and on Tsuzoku et al. (2018) to connect K_F to adversarial robustness. The work is directly motivated by Konz et al. (2022), which empirically observed that generalization scaling differs drastically between natural and medical images without a theoretical explanation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, fast-to-compute metric (label sharpness) that partially explains why medical image models generalize worse and are more vulnerable to adversarial attacks than natural image models at similar intrinsic dimensions—a gap that has practical implications for deploying AI in clinical settings. However, the authors acknowledge they cannot definitively establish causation between K_F and the observed scaling discrepancy, and the analysis is limited to binary classification with CNNs on 2D images. Open questions include whether the framework extends to segmentation, detection, 3D volumes, non-convolutional architectures, and other imaging domains such as histopathology or satellite imagery.
