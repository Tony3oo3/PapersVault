## Problem

Diffusion models suffer from exposure bias — an input mismatch where the network is trained on ground-truth noisy samples x_t but at inference receives its own imperfect predictions x̂_t — yet this issue lacked a rigorous analytical treatment. Specifically, no prior work provided an explicit closed-form sampling distribution accounting for prediction error, a quantitative metric for exposure bias, or a clear identification of the root cause. This paper fills that gap by analytically deriving the variance inflation in the sampling distribution and attributing it to the x_θ^t − x_0 prediction error.

## Input / Output

Epsilon Scaling is a training-free, plug-in modification to the sampling loop of any pre-trained diffusion model: it takes the standard inputs (a random Gaussian noise vector x_T ~ N(0,I) and, for conditional generation, a class label) and produces synthetic RGB images at resolutions ranging from 32×32 (CIFAR-10) to 256×256 (FFHQ, CelebA-HQ, ImageNet). The only change to the sampling equation is dividing the network's ε_θ output by a scalar schedule λ(t) at each reverse step, adding zero extra forward passes or computational cost. The method is evaluated across six diffusion frameworks (ADM, DDIM, EDM, LDM, DiT, PFGM++) and both stochastic and deterministic samplers.

## Method

The paper first models the denoising prediction error x_θ^t − x_0 as a Gaussian N(0, e_t²I) and analytically derives the single-step sampling distribution q_θ(x̂_t|x_{t+1}), showing its variance exceeds the training variance (1−α̅_t)I by the term (√α̅_t·β_{t+1}/(1−α̅_{t+1})·e_{t+1})². This variance gap, which compounds across multi-step sampling, is identified as the root cause of exposure bias, and a metric δ_t = (√β'_t − √β̄_t)² is proposed to quantify it. The practical solution, Epsilon Scaling, exploits the empirical observation that the L2-norm of ε_θ during sampling is consistently larger than during training (Fig. 2): by dividing the network output by a schedule λ(t) (typically a uniform constant b ≈ 1.005–1.02, or a linear function kt+b for short sampling chains), the sampling trajectory is pulled back toward the training vector field. The scaling factor is found via a lightweight coarse-to-fine search over b (6–10 FID evaluations with 10k samples) and is insensitive to small perturbations, making the method broadly applicable without retraining.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 32×32, ADM-ES achieves 2.17 FID under 100-step unconditional sampling, a 35.6% improvement over the ADM baseline (3.37 FID). On FFHQ 128×128, the improvement is even more dramatic: ADM-ES reduces FID from 14.52 to 6.77 at 100 steps (53% reduction). The method is training-free and adds no computational overhead, with the optimal scaling factor b typically in the range [0.995, 1.017] depending on the dataset and step count.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ADM (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021), Epsilon Scaling consistently wins across all five datasets and all step counts, with the largest relative gains at low step counts (e.g., 59.35→6.77 FID on FFHQ at 100 steps). Against EDM (Karras et al., 2022), ADM-ES achieves a better FID (2.17) with 5× fewer NFEs (100 vs 511 for EDM-VP), and on the EDM baseline itself, Epsilon Scaling improves FID from 3.81 to 2.80 (Euler, 35 steps, unconditional). Against the Restart sampler (Xu et al., 2023a, FID 2.21 at 115 steps) and SA-Solver (Xue et al., 2023, FID 2.63 at 95 steps), ADM-ES achieves 2.17 FID at 100 steps, establishing state-of-the-art among stochastic samplers. Against the prior exposure-bias method ADM-IP (Ning et al., 2023, FID 2.38 at 100 steps), ADM-ES improves to 2.17 without requiring retraining. On DDIM (Song et al., 2021), Epsilon Scaling improves FID from 4.06 to 3.38 (CIFAR-10, 100 steps, η=0) and from 6.73 to 4.01 (η=1); on LDM (Rombach et al., 2022), it reduces FID from 9.31 to 7.36 (CelebA-HQ 256×256, 100 steps); on DiT (Peebles & Xie, 2023), it improves FID from 2.57 to 2.52 (ImageNet 256×256, 100 steps); and on PFGM++ (Xu et al., 2023b), it reduces FID from 37.82 to 17.91 at 9 steps (D=128). The method never degrades any baseline, confirming its generality across UNet, Transformer, and Poisson Flow architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the foundational DDPM/DDIM/EDM diffusion frameworks and extends the nascent line of work on exposure bias in diffusion models initiated by Ning et al. (2023) and Li et al. (2023a), which proposed training-time regularisation and time-step shifting respectively. It departs from these prior approaches by providing the first closed-form analytical derivation of the sampling distribution under prediction error, identifying the x_0 prediction error as the root cause, and proposing a training-free, plug-in solution (Epsilon Scaling) that requires no retraining. The analytical treatment draws on Analytic-DPM (Bao et al., 2022) for modelling the prediction uncertainty, while the empirical validation spans the full spectrum of modern diffusion architectures from ADM to DiT and PFGM++.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous quantitative framework (the δ_t metric) for diagnosing exposure bias in diffusion models, which was previously only qualitatively described. Epsilon Scaling's training-free, architecture-agnostic nature makes it immediately deployable on any pre-trained diffusion model, and the consistent FID gains across six different frameworks suggest broad practical utility. A key limitation is that the method corrects only the magnitude error of the predicted noise vector, not its direction, so exposure bias cannot be fully eliminated. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to non-image modalities (audio, video, 3D), whether it composes well with classifier-free guidance or distillation, and whether the analytical variance-gap framework can inform better training objectives.
