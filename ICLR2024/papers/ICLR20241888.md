## Problem

Single-step adversarial training (e.g., FGSM) suffers from catastrophic overfitting, where adversarial test accuracy collapses to 0% within a few epochs. Existing remedies that enforce local linearity of the loss (GradAlign, LLR, CURE) all require Double Backpropagation, tripling training time. The paper asks whether local linearity can be enforced efficiently—without differentiating gradients—while still preventing CO even under large perturbation budgets and long training schedules.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100 and SVHN, up to 288×288 for ImageNet) and produces class labels via a standard cross-entropy classifier. During training, each sample requires three additional forward passes (at points x_a, x_b, and their convex combination x_c) within the ℓ∞ ball of radius ε around the input, in addition to the FGSM adversarial forward pass. The regularization is a plug-in that can be attached to any single-step AT method (N-FGSM, GAT, TRADES, AdvMixUp) without modifying the base attack or architecture.

## Method

ELLE enforces local linearity by sampling two random points x_a, x_b uniformly in the ℓ∞ ball B[x, ε, ∞], a random α ∈ [0,1], and penalizing the squared deviation of the loss from its linear interpolation: E_lin = |L(f(x_c), y) − (1−α)L(f(x_a), y) − αL(f(x_b), y)|² where x_c = (1−α)x_a + αx_b. Because this term is computed purely from forward-pass loss values, its gradient with respect to model parameters requires only a single backward pass—no Double Backpropagation—making it roughly 3× faster than GradAlign, LLR, or CURE. A Taylor expansion (Proposition 1) shows E_lin approximates the second-order directional derivative (curvature) of the loss, so minimizing it implicitly smooths the loss landscape. The adaptive variant ELLE-A initializes λ=0 and spikes it to λ_max whenever E_lin exceeds mean + 2σ of its running history, applying regularization only when non-linearity suddenly appears. This adaptive schedule, combined with the plug-in design, allows ELLE-A to avoid CO across all tested ε values, training schedules (short 30-epoch, long 200-epoch, and 1000-epoch), and architectures (PreActResNet-18, WideResNet-28-10, ResNet-50).

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with PreActResNet-18, ELLE-A achieves 44.32% AutoAttack accuracy at ε=8/255 and 18.03% at ε=16/255, closely matching GradAlign (44.66%) and CURE (18.25%) while using less than 50% of the training time (6.29 min vs. 15.89 min for GradAlign). When combined with N-FGSM data augmentation, N-FGSM+ELLE-A improves adversarial accuracy by +5.17% on SVHN (26.35% vs. 21.18% at ε=12/255), +1.07% on CIFAR-10, and +3.20% on CIFAR-100 at the largest ε. On ImageNet (ResNet-50), ELLE-A avoids the catastrophic overfitting that FGSM suffers at ε=8/255 (5.23% vs. 12.40% PGD-50-10 accuracy), and N-FGSM+ELLE-A further improves to 14.58%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ELLE-A matches GradAlign at ε=8/255 on CIFAR-10 (44.32% vs. 44.66% AA) but decisively outperforms it at larger ε where GradAlign suffers catastrophic overfitting (e.g., CIFAR-100 ε=10: 16.39% vs. 0.00% AA; CIFAR-100 ε=26: 2.87% vs. 1.00% AA). ELLE matches CURE at ε=16/255 (18.28% vs. 18.25% AA) while achieving substantially higher clean accuracy (59.73% vs. 52.49%). Compared to the multi-step reference AT PGD-10, ELLE-A closes most of the gap at ε=8 (44.32% vs. 46.95%) but remains ~6.7% behind at ε=16 (18.03% vs. 24.77%). N-FGSM+ELLE-A consistently outperforms N-FGSM alone across all datasets and ε values (e.g., +5.17% on SVHN at ε=12, +1.07% on CIFAR-10 at ε=12), and avoids the Delayed CO that N-FGSM exhibits for ε>18/255 on CIFAR-100 and ε>8/255 on SVHN in long schedules. GAT and SLAT both suffer CO at moderate-to-large ε (GAT: 0.54% AA at ε=16; SLAT: CO for ε≥10/255 on CIFAR-10), whereas ELLE-A remains stable in all tested configurations. The key efficiency advantage is that ELLE avoids Double Backpropagation, reducing per-epoch training time from ~15.89 min (GradAlign) to ~6.29 min, a 2.5× speedup over other local-linearity methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on enforcing local linearity of the loss to prevent Catastrophic Overfitting (CO) in single-step adversarial training, building directly on GradAlign (Andriushchenko & Flammarion, 2020), LLR (Qin et al., 2019), and CURE (Moosavi-Dezfooli et al., 2019). The key departure is that all prior local-linearity regularizers require Double Backpropagation (differentiating input gradients w.r.t. model parameters), incurring a ×3 runtime penalty; ELLE instead uses a simple convex-combination identity (Eq. 1) that only requires forward passes, while Proposition 1 establishes the theoretical link to second-order directional derivatives (curvature). The adaptive variant ELLE-A further departs from fixed-λ regularizers by dynamically scaling the penalty based on detected spikes in the local linearity error, and the 'Delayed CO' observation formalizes the perturbation-wise and duration-wise conditions under which CO emerges.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ELLE provides a practical, drop-in regularization that makes local-linearity enforcement roughly 3× cheaper than prior methods while matching or exceeding their adversarial accuracy, lowering the barrier to robust single-step training. The adaptive λ mechanism (ELLE-A) and the demonstration that ELLE can be plugged into N-FGSM, GAT, TRADES-single, and AdvMixUp-single to rescue them from CO make it broadly applicable. Limitations include the need for three extra forward passes per sample (memory overhead) and the open question of whether large-ε robustness is meaningful for human oracles. Future work on more memory-efficient local-linearity enforcement and on principled ε upper bounds is identified as needed.
