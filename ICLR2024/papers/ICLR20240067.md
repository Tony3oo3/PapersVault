## Problem

Activation patching is the standard localization tool in mechanistic interpretability, yet the literature uses inconsistent corruption methods, evaluation metrics, and patching strategies with no consensus on best practices. This paper is the first systematic study of how these methodological hyperparameters affect interpretability outcomes, showing that different choices can lead to materially different localization and circuit discovery results.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240067/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained decoder-only language model, a clean prompt with a known target token, and a corrupted prompt generated either by adding Gaussian noise to key token embeddings (GN) or by swapping key tokens with semantically related ones of equal length (STR). It performs three forward passes—clean (caching component activations), corrupted (recording baseline outputs), and patched (restoring one component's clean activation)—and outputs a per-component patching effect score, typically normalized to [0,1] under logit difference, which is aggregated into layer-by-layer or head-by-head localization heatmaps and circuit discovery tables.

## Method

The paper systematically varies three degrees of freedom in activation patching: corruption method (Gaussian noise vs. symmetric token replacement), evaluation metric (probability, logit difference, KL divergence), and patching granularity (single-layer vs. sliding window of 3–10 adjacent layers). These combinations are tested across five interpretability tasks—factual recall, IOI circuit discovery, greater-than, Python docstring completion, and basic arithmetic—in GPT-2 small, GPT-2 XL, and GPT-J 6B. A central finding is that GN corruption pushes the model into out-of-distribution regimes that disrupt internal mechanisms (evidenced by broken attention patterns in Name Mover heads and spurious negative detections), while STR preserves in-distribution behavior. They further show that probability as a metric structurally cannot detect negative components when corruption drives the correct-token probability near zero, whereas logit difference retains sensitivity. Finally, sliding window patching yields 20–75% stronger localization peaks than summing individual layer effects, indicating non-linear interactions that inflate apparent single-layer importance.

## Results

For factual recall in GPT-2 XL, GN corruption produces MLP patching peaks that are 2×–5× higher than STR, and the clear layer-16 peak under GN is not salient under STR regardless of metric. In IOI circuit discovery (GPT-2 small), STR and GN detect different sets of attention heads: e.g., under probability, STR detects 3/4 S-Inhibition heads while GN detects only 2/4, and GN spuriously flags head 0.10 as negative. Sliding window patching yields 1.40×, 1.75×, and 1.59× higher peak effects than single-layer summation for window sizes 3, 5, and 10. On the metric comparison, probability assigns 4.33× more patching effect to the last subject token than logit difference (1.22×) under STR, and probability completely misses the Negative Name Mover head 11.10 (effect −0.022, within 2 SD of mean 0.003) while logit difference detects it.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Meng et al. (2022), the paper shows that replacing their GN corruption with STR fundamentally changes the factual recall localization picture: the prominent layer-16 MLP peak under GN disappears under STR, with GN peaks 2×–5× higher across all window sizes. Against Wang et al. (2023) on the IOI circuit, STR and GN produce inconsistent head detections (Table 1); notably, corrupting S2 (as in Wang et al.) causes both methods to miss 2 of 3 Name Mover heads, while corrupting S1+IO recovers all three NMs under every metric. Against Hanna et al. (2023) on the greater-than circuit, STR correctly identifies heads 6.9, 7.10, 8.11, 9.1 matching their findings, whereas GN yields a mostly disjoint and noisy set of heads with little overlap. Against Heimersheim & Janiak (2023) on Python docstrings, GN produces highly noisy localization while STR gives cleaner results, with heads 3.0 and 3.6 consistently highlighted across all methods. Against Stolfo et al. (2023) on arithmetic in GPT-J, the relationship inverts: STR provides up to 4× sharper MLP concentration for addition and subtraction, the opposite of the factual recall setting where GN dominated, underscoring that the GN-vs-STR gap is task-dependent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as the first systematic meta-study of activation patching methodology, building directly on the three-pass causal intervention framework of Vig et al. (2020), the Gaussian-noise corruption and sliding-window patching of Meng et al. (2022), and the symmetric-token-replacement corruption and logit-difference metric of Wang et al. (2023). Rather than applying these techniques to discover new circuits, it treats the hyperparameters themselves—corruption method, evaluation metric, and window size—as the object of study, asking how much interpretability conclusions depend on these choices. It departs from prior circuit-discovery papers by explicitly testing whether results are robust across methodological variants, and by providing conceptual arguments (OOD behavior under GN, non-negativity of probability) for why certain choices are preferable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that GN and STR can detect entirely different sets of attention heads in the IOI circuit, and that GN may push the model into OOD regimes that disrupt internal mechanisms, raises a reliability concern for a substantial body of prior interpretability work that relied on GN corruption. The practical recommendations (prefer STR, use logit difference, interpret sliding-window results as joint effects) are immediately actionable for practitioners. Key limitations include experiments restricted to decoder-only models up to 6B parameters, tentative (not conclusive) OOD evidence, and only one patching direction (clean→corrupt). Open questions include whether these methodological sensitivities persist at larger scale, whether the reverse patching direction (corrupt→clean) shows different behavior, and whether more principled corruption or metric choices can be derived from first principles.
