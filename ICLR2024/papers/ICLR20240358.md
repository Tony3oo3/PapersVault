## Problem

Using diffusion models to generate high-quality object detection data requires encoding geometric conditions (bounding boxes, camera views) that go beyond standard text prompts, yet prior layout-to-image methods rely on purpose-built modules (RoI Align, layout attention) that limit flexibility and cannot easily incorporate additional geometric controls. Moreover, no prior work had demonstrated that L2I-generated images could actually improve object detector performance, especially under annotation-scarce conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240358/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a geometric layout consisting of N bounding boxes (each with a semantic class label and 2D corner coordinates), an optional camera view descriptor, and random Gaussian noise. Continuous box coordinates are discretized onto a spatial grid (e.g., 400×228 bins) and converted into learned location tokens, then serialized into a text prompt following the template 'An image of {view} camera with {boxes}' where each box becomes a (class, top-left token, bottom-right token) phrase with random ordering. The output is a photorealistic RGB image at 256×256, 512×512, or 800×456 resolution that is consistent with the input layout, generated via 100-step PLMS sampling with classifier-free guidance.

## Method

GEODIFFUSION encodes all geometric conditions as text prompts by discretizing continuous coordinates into a grid of learned location tokens (initialized with 2D sine-cosine embeddings) that are inserted into the text encoder vocabulary of a pre-trained Stable Diffusion (LDM) model. The text encoder and U-Net are jointly fine-tuned with AdamW, while the VQ-VAE remains frozen, requiring no additional architectural modules beyond the new tokens. A foreground prior re-weighting mechanism multiplies the standard denoising loss element-wise by a spatial mask that assigns higher weights to foreground pixels, with an area-based component (w/c^p) that gives smaller objects proportionally more emphasis to counteract the foreground-background imbalance. This text-prompted approach is more flexible than ControlNet (which requires separate parameter copies per condition) and more transferable than GLIGEN (which needs dedicated attention layers trained from scratch), enabling unified handling of bounding boxes, camera views, and even 3D geometric controls within a single prompt.

## Results

On NuImages (800×456, 64 epochs), GeoDiffusion achieves 10.99 FID and 34.5 mAP, outperforming the strongest baseline ControlNet (23.26 FID, 22.6 mAP) by +12.27 FID and +11.9 mAP, while approaching the Oracle real-image mAP of 48.2. For trainability, GeoDiffusion is the only L2I method that consistently improves detector performance across nearly all classes, yielding +1.4 mAP over real-only training (38.3 vs. 36.9), with the largest gains on rare classes (+2.8 trailer, +3.6 construction, +2.9 bus). On COCO-Stuff (512×512), GeoDiffusion reaches 18.89 FID and 30.6 mAP, surpassing GLIGEN (21.04 FID, 22.4 mAP) and ControlNet (28.14 FID, 25.2 mAP). The method also achieves 4× training acceleration (64 vs. 256 epochs) compared to GAN-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoDiffusion decisively outperforms all seven baselines on both fidelity and trainability metrics. Against GAN/VAE-based L2I methods (LostGAN, LAMA, Taming), GeoDiffusion achieves 5–6× lower FID and 5–10× higher mAP, reflecting the quality gap between diffusion and classical generative models. Among diffusion-based competitors, GeoDiffusion beats GLIGEN by +5.69 FID and +13.2 mAP, and ControlNet by +12.27 FID and +11.9 mAP on NuImages, with similar margins on COCO-Stuff (+2.15 FID, +8.2 mAP over GLIGEN). ReCo, the closest prior diffusion-based L2I method, is surpassed by +16.11 FID and +17.4 mAP. Critically, on trainability (Table 3), GeoDiffusion is the only method that yields a positive mAP improvement over real-only training (+1.4), while all baselines (LostGAN through ControlNet) produce equal or slightly negative gains (−0.5 to −1.3 mAP), confirming that only GeoDiffusion's generated images are genuinely useful for detector training. On COCO inpainting (Table 6), GeoDiffusion also surpasses Stable Diffusion, ControlNet, and GLIGEN by +1.4, +1.2, and +0.7 mAP respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoDiffusion builds on the Latent Diffusion Model (Stable Diffusion) as a pre-trained backbone and borrows the coordinate-discretization idea from Pix2seq, but departs fundamentally from prior layout-to-image methods (LostGAN, LAMA, Taming, GLIGEN, ControlNet, ReCo) that rely on purpose-built architectural modules (RoI Align, layout attention, gated self-attention) to encode bounding boxes. Instead, it translates all geometric conditions—bounding box coordinates, semantic classes, and extra conditions like camera views—into a unified text-prompt 'foreign language,' leveraging the transferability of the pre-trained text encoder. The foreground prior re-weighting loss is a novel addition not present in prior L2I or diffusion-based generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that layout-to-image generated images can consistently improve object detector training, particularly for rare/long-tailed classes where annotation scarcity is most acute (e.g., +3.6 mAP for construction vehicles). The practical impact is significant: a 4× training speedup over prior L2I baselines, extensibility to 3D geometric controls and domain adaptation (weather, time-of-day), and a simple text-prompt interface that avoids the architectural complexity of methods like ControlNet or GLIGEN. Key limitations remain: generated data can only augment but not replace real data, and the method struggles with high-variance classes (pedestrians) and occlusion. Open questions include whether detectors can be trained solely on generated data, and how to integrate GeoDiffusion with automatic annotation or perception-based generation pipelines.
