## Problem

Existing transferable adversarial attacks on Vision Transformers (e.g., TGR, PNA) regularize or discard gradients at the coarse layer level, yet substantial gradient disparities persist even within the same functional region across channels. The paper identifies that mild gradients—not the extreme gradients previously targeted—are the primary cause of overfitting that reduces transferability, and that ViTs exhibit distinct high-frequency attention patterns in the frequency domain that remain unexploited for transferable attacks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242020/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean RGB image (224×224, from the ILSVRC 2012 validation set) and a target class label, and produces a single adversarial perturbation bounded by an L∞ norm of ε/255 (default ε=16). The perturbation is crafted on a surrogate ViT model via iterative optimization (10 steps) and directly transferred to attack unseen target ViT and CNN models without access to their parameters. During optimization, the input is additionally transformed through DCT/IDCT frequency-domain operations with a spatially varying mask to generate N=20 frequency-diverse gradient estimates per iteration.

## Method

GNS-HFA combines two complementary mechanisms. GNS performs fine-grained gradient editing on the Multi-head Self-Attention weight gradients (W^Q, W^K, W^V): it computes per-channel absolute mean gradients, identifies 'mild' channels whose mean falls below μ + u·σ, and rescales their gradients by multiplying with tanh(|(g_l − μ)/σ|), adaptively suppressing small gradients that cause overfitting while preserving large attack-relevant gradients. HFA leverages the observation that ViTs attend more to high-frequency features by constructing a coordinate-dependent mask over the DCT spectrum, adding normalized noise to ensure sufficient high-frequency content, and computing N frequency-domain gradient samples whose averaged sign determines the adversarial update direction. Together, GNS stabilizes the surrogate model's gradient landscape during backpropagation while HFA aligns the perturbation direction with ViTs' frequency-sensitive decision boundaries, yielding an average ASR improvement of 33.54% on ViTs and 42.05% on CNNs over prior state-of-the-art.

## Results

GNS-HFA achieves an average Attack Success Rate (ASR) improvement of 37.80% over all nine competitive baselines across 8 ViT and 7 CNN target models, with 33.54% average improvement on ViT models and 42.05% on CNN models. Against the primary ViT-specific baseline TGR, GNS-HFA gains 15.04% on average (9.72% on ViT, 20.35% on CNN). Against the best CNN transferable attack SSA, the improvement is 12.32% on average (13.94% on ViT, 10.71% on CNN). The largest absolute gains appear on adversarially-trained CNN ensembles (e.g., Inc-v3-adv-4: 55.80% vs. TGR's 38.40% with ViT-B/16 surrogate).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GNS-HFA outperforms all nine baselines on the vast majority of model pairs, with the only exceptions being near-ties on the surrogate's own architecture (e.g., 99.60% vs. TGR's 100.00% on PiT-B→PiT-B). Against TGR (the primary ViT-specific baseline), GNS-HFA achieves a 15.04% average ASR gain, with the largest margins on adversarially-trained CNN ensembles (e.g., +17.4 pp on Inc-v3-adv-4) and smaller margins on the surrogate's own model. Against SSA (the strongest CNN-oriented transferable attack), GNS-HFA leads by 12.32% on average, with particularly large gains on ViT targets (13.94%) where SSA's frequency-domain approach was not designed. PNA, which discards gradients entirely, is consistently the weakest ViT-specific baseline, trailing GNS-HFA by 20+ pp on most targets. Among the general-purpose FGSM variants (BIM, PGD, DI-FGSM, TI-FGSM, MI-FGSM, SINI-FGSM), SINI-FGSM is the closest competitor but still lags GNS-HFA by 15–25 pp on most models, confirming that gradient-level editing (GNS) combined with frequency-domain exploration (HFA) substantially exceeds what input-diversity or momentum-based strategies can achieve for ViT transferability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper refines the ViT-specific transferable attack line initiated by TGR (Zhang et al., CVPR 2023) and PNA (Wei et al., AAAI 2022), both of which manipulate gradients at the layer level. The key conceptual departure is the claim that mild (small-magnitude) gradients, rather than the extreme gradients targeted by TGR, are the primary drivers of overfitting and reduced transferability in ViTs. Additionally, the paper transplants the frequency-domain exploration idea from SSA (Long et al., ECCV 2022), originally designed for CNNs, into the ViT setting by constructing coordinate-based DCT masks that exploit ViTs' observed preference for high-frequency features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reframe from extreme to mild gradients challenges the working assumption in the ViT adversarial attack literature and offers a more targeted mechanism for preventing overfitting during surrogate-model training. The 15% average improvement over TGR across 15 target models is practically meaningful for security assessment of ViT-based systems. However, the evaluation is limited to a 1000-image ImageNet subset, and the frequency masking strategy (Eq. 6) appears somewhat ad hoc, raising questions about whether the high-frequency emphasis is a fundamental ViT property or an artifact of the specific models tested. Open questions include whether the mild-gradient insight generalizes to other architectures (e.g., Mamba-based vision models) and whether the frequency-domain component adds genuine value beyond what GNS alone achieves.
