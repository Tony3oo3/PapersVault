## Problem

Progressive growth of Transformers for faster pre-training has two unresolved issues: existing growth schedules under-explore the interaction among all four expandable dimensions (hidden_dim, ffn_dim, head_num, layer_num), and existing growth operators (e.g., Net2Net) fail to achieve strict function preservation when Layer Normalization is present, while also locking in a specific weight initialization that constrains training dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241519/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a smaller pre-trained Transformer checkpoint, a standard pre-training corpus (Wikipedia+BookCorpus for BERT, OpenWebText for GPT-2), and a multi-staged growth schedule specifying the target hyperparameter tuple (hidden_dim, ffn_dim, head_num, layer_num) at each stage boundary. It produces a full-size pre-trained Transformer whose downstream performance matches or exceeds a from-scratch baseline trained for the same total steps. A tunable mask-strength parameter φ (number of steps to linearly ramp masks from 0 to 1) controls how quickly newly added structures become active after each growth event.

## Method

MSG introduces external binary masks that zero out the contributions of all newly added neurons, layers, or attention heads immediately after growth, guaranteeing strict function preservation regardless of how new weights are initialized. For fully-connected layers, new output neurons are masked to 0; for Layer Normalization, the mean and variance are computed only over unmasked dimensions (Eq. 17), resolving the LN dilemma that breaks Net2Net-style operators; for depth growth, a masked residual connection (Eq. 20) skips the new layer entirely at mask=0; for attention heads, new heads' value vectors are masked to 0 so their outputs vanish. Masks are then linearly increased to 1 over φ steps, letting new structures gradually take over while existing neurons provide initial gradient guidance. New weights are initialized from N(0, 0.02) (or stacked copies for depth), decoupling initialization from the function-preservation mechanism. Growth schedules are constructed by grid-searching a 'pre-training rate' γ = Δloss/Δt over candidate intermediate structures, expanding exactly one dimension per stage to maximize wall-time savings (achieving 2.2× on BERT-large and 1.8× on BERT-base).

## Results

MSG achieves up to 2.2× speedup on Bert-Large (70h48min → 32h10min) while improving GLUE average from 82.2 to 83.2 and SQuAD F1 from 87.9 to 88.4 over the full-training baseline. On Bert-Base, MSG reaches 1.8× speedup (26h10min → 14h36min) with GLUE 81.0 vs. 80.7 for full training. On GPT-2, MSG achieves 1.4× speedup (53h1min → 37h59min) with zero-shot perplexity of 41.20 vs. 41.31 for full training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Bert2BERT (Chen et al., 2022; Qin et al., 2022b) on identical schedules, MSG consistently wins: on Bert-Large it improves GLUE by +1.1 (83.2 vs. 82.1) and SQuAD F1 by +0.8 (88.4 vs. 87.6); on Bert-Base it gains +0.5 GLUE (81.0 vs. 80.5) and +0.5 SQuAD F1 (87.2 vs. 86.7); on GPT-2 it reduces zero-shot perplexity from 42.24 to 41.20 and fine-tuned perplexity from 25.13 to 24.14. The authors attribute MSG's advantage to strict function preservation (eliminating post-growth recovery time) and random initialization (avoiding the symmetry problem inherent in Net2Net-style weight copying). Against LiGO (Wang et al., 2022), MSG achieves higher speedup ratios: 1.8× vs. 1.69× on Bert-Base and 2.2× vs. 1.82× on Bert-Large, though the comparison is indirect since LiGO uses a one-stage growth strategy while MSG uses multi-stage. MSG also outperforms the no-grow full-training baseline on Bert-Large in both speed and quality, demonstrating that progressive growth with proper operators can be strictly better than training the target architecture from scratch.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the progressive structural growth line of work (Stacking, CompoundGrow, Staged, Bert2BERT, LiGO) by introducing a masking mechanism that achieves strict function preservation across all four Transformer growth dimensions (hidden_dim, ffn_dim, head_num, layer_num), solving the Layer Normalization dilemma that rendered prior Net2Net-style operators non-strict. Unlike previous methods that couple function preservation to specific weight initialization (copying or zeroizing), MSG decouples these concerns, making the growth operator agnostic to how new weights are initialized. The schedule design methodology (grid-searching the pre-training rate γ across intermediate structures) also departs from prior work that either hand-designed schedules or focused on single-stage growth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.2× pre-training speedup on BERT-large with equal or better downstream performance is practically significant given the enormous compute costs of LLM pre-training, and the FLM-101B validation demonstrates the approach scales to 100B+ parameters. The strict function preservation property and initialization independence make MSG a robust backbone for future research on growth operators and learned initialization. Open questions remain: optimal multi-dimension simultaneous growth within a single stage, self-adaptive schedules that respond to training dynamics, and whether the heuristic schedule rules generalize beyond BERT/GPT-2 architectures. The paper acknowledges that the best initialization strategy for grown weights is still unresolved.
