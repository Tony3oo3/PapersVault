## Problem

Existing diffusion-model-based inverse problem solvers operate in pixel space, demanding large computational resources and training data. Latent diffusion models (LDMs) offer efficiency but their nonlinear encoder-decoder makes it difficult to apply existing posterior sampling solvers directly; the concurrent PSLD method handles only linear problems and produces artifacts under measurement noise. The paper asks how to leverage pre-trained LDMs as generative priors for general (linear and nonlinear) inverse problems with noisy measurements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240303/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes noisy measurements y ∈ R^m from an arbitrary forward operator A(·) (e.g., Radon transform for CT, convolution for deblurring, downsampling for super-resolution, masking for inpainting) plus a pre-trained LDM (VQ-4 autoencoder with 64×64×3 latent space and a DDIM score network). It outputs a reconstructed pixel-space image x₀ = D(z₀) at 256×256×3 (natural) or 256×256 (CT). The entire reverse sampling loop runs in the low-dimensional latent space, and the LDM is fine-tuned on ~2000 CT slices for medical imaging while natural-image experiments use off-the-shelf FFHQ/CelebA-HQ LDM checkpoints.

## Method

ReSample replaces the soft gradient-based data consistency of DPS/Latent-DPS with a hard-constrained optimization: at selected time steps (every ~10 steps, only in the latter two of three sampling stages), it solves min_z ½‖y − A(D(z))‖² initialized from the Tweedie estimate ẑ₀(z_{t+1}), using pixel-space conjugate-gradient in the middle stage and latent-space gradient descent in the final stage. After obtaining the measurement-consistent sample ẑ₀(y), a novel stochastic resampling step maps it back onto the noisy data manifold at time t by drawing from a tractable Gaussian posterior that conditions on both ẑ₀(y) and the unconditional DDIM sample z′_t, with a tunable variance σ_t² controlling the prior-vs-data tradeoff. The paper proves this resampling is unbiased (Theorem 1) and has strictly lower variance than simple stochastic encoding (Lemma 1), with variance vanishing as t→0 (Theorem 2). Early stopping at a noise-level threshold τ prevents overfitting to measurement noise, and the skipped-step mechanism balances accuracy against the cost of repeated optimization.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ, ReSample achieves PSNR of 30.45 dB (SR 4×) and 32.77 dB (random 70% inpainting), with LPIPS of 0.144 and 0.082 respectively, outperforming all pixel-space and latent-space baselines. On the LDCT medical imaging benchmark, ReSample reaches PSNR of 35.91 dB (abdominal), 37.82 dB (head), and 31.72 dB (chest) for CT reconstruction, surpassing the best supervised baseline (PnP-UNet) by 3+ dB. For nonlinear deblurring on CelebA-HQ, ReSample achieves 30.18 dB PSNR, a 3.4 dB improvement over DPS (26.81 dB), highlighting its advantage on complex forward operators.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReSample outperforms all eight baselines across every task and metric. Against the strongest pixel-space baseline DPS, ReSample gains +2.04 dB PSNR on SR 4× (30.45 vs 28.41) and +0.29 dB on inpainting (32.77 vs 32.48), with the gap widening to +3.37 dB on nonlinear deblurring (30.18 vs 26.81). The concurrent latent-space method PSLD-LDM is outperformed by +2.84 dB on SR and +5.70 dB on inpainting PSNR, confirming that hard data consistency via optimization is substantially more effective than PSLD's gradient-based soft consistency. The plug-and-play ADMM-PnP baseline lags far behind (21.08 dB vs 30.45 dB on SR), and the supervised CT baselines PnP-UNet and FBP-UNet are surpassed by 3.1–5.9 dB PSNR on the LDCT benchmark. DDRM and DMPS, which are competitive on linear tasks, are outperformed by 0.96–1.97 dB on SR and 3.93–5.08 dB on inpainting, with ReSample's advantage growing for nonlinear problems where DDRM and DMPS are not applicable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-model-based inverse problem solving framework (DPS, MCG, DDRM) from pixel space to the latent space of pre-trained LDMs, addressing the key challenge that the decoder's nonlinearity breaks the linear manifold assumptions underlying prior methods. It departs from the concurrent PSLD work (Rout et al., 2023) by replacing soft gradient-based data consistency with hard optimization-based data consistency, and introduces a stochastic resampling scheme with theoretical variance-reduction guarantees to map measurement-consistent samples back onto the noisy data manifold. The hard data consistency step itself draws on optimization-based approaches previously used with GAN priors (Bora et al., 2017; Jalal et al., 2021), but the combination with LDMs and the resampling mechanism is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the substantial memory efficiency gain (26% memory increment vs. 140% for PSLD and up to 964% for pixel-space DDRM), making LDM-based inverse problem solving feasible in memory-constrained settings like medical imaging. The method's ability to handle both linear and nonlinear forward operators with strong measurement consistency (50× lower measurement loss than Latent-DPS) broadens the applicability of diffusion priors beyond the linear regime where most prior methods operate. The main open question is the computational overhead of solving the hard data consistency optimization at each resample step, which the authors acknowledge as a significant challenge for future work; the skipped-step mechanism mitigates but does not eliminate this cost.
