## Problem

Existing machine-based event forecasting methods, which rely on BM25 retrieval over news corpora, still trail behind human forecaster performance because they cannot distinguish articles that directly address a forecasting question from those merely topically related, nor do they account for the temporal dynamics that make recent news more informative. The paper argues that the key bottleneck is identifying a concise, semantically relevant, and temporally appropriate subset of news snippets from a vast corpus.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241355/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a structured forecasting query (question text, answer choices, start/end dates, question type) and a large news article corpus (Common Crawl, 2016–2022) as input. BM25 first retrieves K=50 candidate articles, which are then re-ranked and summarized down to N=10 concise passages. The output is a discrete event outcome: a True/False label, a multiple-choice letter, or a binned numerical value (continuous predictions are discretized into R equal-width bins, with the median of the predicted bin returned at inference).

## Method

AutoCast++ introduces a zero-shot ranking-based retriever-reader pipeline built on Fusion-in-Decoder (FiD) over T5. The retriever combines two zero-shot GPT-3 signals: a binning-based relevance score (LLM rates question–article relevance on a 0–4 scale, averaged over multiple inferences) and a recency score derived from the rate of change in human forecaster accuracy across successive article timestamps, yielding a composite retrieval score that prioritizes both semantic relevance and temporal proximity to the question's expiry date. Selected articles are condensed via zero-shot GPT-3 abstractive summarization before being fed to the FiD reader. The reader is trained with a composite loss: standard autoregressive decoder cross-entropy plus a human-alignment term that applies a causal self-attention layer over chronologically ordered article representations and minimizes KL divergence between the model's inferred confidence at each time step and the corresponding human forecaster probability, regularizing the encoder to track the temporal evolution of evidence.

## Results

On the Autocast benchmark, AutoCast++ (0.2B) achieves 66.7% T/F accuracy, 43.8% MCQ accuracy, and 19.8 absolute error on numerical predictions, outperforming the FiD Static baseline (62.0%, 29.6%, 24.5) by 8%, 48%, and 19% respectively. The 0.8B variant reaches 67.3% T/F, 44.0% MCQ, and 19.9 Num., while the 2.8B variant achieves 67.9% T/F, 44.1% MCQ, and 19.8 Num. Notably, the compact 0.2B model already surpasses the much larger 2.8B FiD Static baseline on all three metrics, suggesting that retrieval quality matters more than model scale for this task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoCast++ dominates all four baselines across every model size and metric. Against FiD Static (the closest architectural match, also T5-based with top-10 retrieval), AutoCast++ gains 48% on MCQ (43.8 vs. 29.6 at 0.2B), 8% on T/F (66.7 vs. 62.0), and 19% on numerical error (19.8 vs. 24.5). Against FiD Temporal, which retrieves one article per day and is computationally heavier (4.3B at the large scale), AutoCast++ wins on T/F and MCQ at every size while matching or slightly beating it on numerical predictions (19.8 vs. 19.5 at 2.8B). The no-retrieval baselines UnifiedQA-v2 and T5 are consistently far behind on MCQ (23–29% vs. 44%) and T/F (45–61% vs. 67%), confirming that retrieval quality is the primary driver of forecasting performance. The 0.2B AutoCast++ model outperforms the 2.8B FiD Static on all three metrics, indicating that the zero-shot re-ranking and summarization pipeline compensates for model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Fusion-in-Decoder (FiD) retriever-reader framework (Izacard & Grave, 2021) to the event forecasting task introduced by the Autocast benchmark (Zou et al., 2022), replacing the standard BM25 + cross-encoder retrieval pipeline with a zero-shot LLM-based relevance re-ranker and a recency-aware scoring mechanism derived from human forecaster accuracy dynamics. The reader retains the FiD architecture on T5 but adds a human alignment loss that regularizes encoder representations against intermediate crowd-forecasting probabilities, and a binning strategy that converts numerical prediction into a classification problem. The use of GPT-3 in a zero-shot fashion for both relevance scoring and abstractive summarization departs from the supervised or fine-tuned retrieval approaches common in open-domain QA, positioning the work at the intersection of LLM-augmented retrieval and temporal event prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that retrieval quality, not model scale, is the primary bottleneck for news-based event forecasting: a 0.2B model with improved retrieval outperforms a 2.8B FiD Static baseline, and the 48% relative MCQ gain comes almost entirely from the LLM-enhanced retriever. However, absolute performance remains well below human forecasters, and numerical prediction (the hardest question type) sees only modest gains, suggesting that the current paradigm of retrieving and summarizing news snippets may have a ceiling. Open questions include whether the human alignment loss generalizes beyond the Autocast dataset's specific crowd-forecasting structure, whether the recency scoring transfers to domains with different temporal dynamics, and whether the approach scales to longer-horizon or multi-event forecasting scenarios.
