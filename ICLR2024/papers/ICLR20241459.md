## Problem

Self-supervised learning models are typically pretrained, fine-tuned, and evaluated on the same domains, yet they generalize poorly to unseen target domains due to distribution shifts. Existing Unsupervised Domain Generalization methods that address this gap all rely on domain labels to constrain comparisons and on domain-specific architectural components (e.g., domain-specific negative queues or decoders), making them impractical to collect, rigid to deploy, and non-scalable to numerous domains. What is missing is a simple, label-free, and architecture-agnostic mechanism to suppress style-driven spurious correlations within SSL pretraining.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a batch of N RGB images drawn from multiple source domains during the unsupervised pretraining stage; each image is further augmented into V views (SimCLR), 2 global + V local crops (SWaV), or M masked + 1 unmasked views (MSN). A critical constraint is that color augmentations (jitter, equalize, posterize, solarize, grayscale) must be applied batch-wise rather than sample-wise, otherwise the unique standardized style imposed by BSS would be broken. The output is a set of domain-invariant SSL representations (D-dimensional embedding vectors) that are subsequently fine-tuned via linear probing or full fine-tuning to produce class-label predictions on unseen target domains.

## Method

Batch Styles Standardization extends Fourier-based Augmentations by transferring the style of a single randomly selected 'style' image to every other image in the batch, rather than independently randomizing each image's amplitude as in standard FA. Concretely, BSS computes the 2-D FFT of each image, decomposes it into amplitude A and phase P, substitutes the low-frequency amplitude components (a central region whose area is governed by a sampled ratio r ∈ U(r_min, r_max)) of every image with those of the style image, and reconstructs via inverse FFT while keeping the original phase intact—thereby preserving semantics while harmonizing style. This batch-level style transfer simulates a 'pseudo-domain' in which all examples share one visual appearance, directly reducing the risk that the contrastive or clustering objective exploits domain/style cues as a shortcut. Integration is method-specific: for SimCLR, BSS is applied V times independently so that all positives and negatives within the same view column share one style; for SWaV, BSS is applied only to the global views that feed the Sinkhorn-Knopp assignment while local views receive standard FA; for MSN, BSS is applied to unmasked views and FA to masked views. No domain labels, domain-specific negative queues, or domain-specific decoders are required, making BSS a drop-in augmentation module for any contrastive or non-contrastive SSL pipeline.

## Results

On PACS, SimCLR with BSS achieves 44.91% average accuracy at 1% labeled data and 56.40% at 5%, outperforming BrAD (43.80%, 52.03%) and CycleMAE (39.82%, 45.43%) without requiring domain labels or ImageNet transfer learning. On Camelyon17 WILDS, SimCLR w/BSS reaches 95.00% accuracy at 100% labels, setting a new state-of-the-art that surpasses even SWaV trained with additional unlabeled target data (91.40%). On DomainNet, SWaV w/BSS achieves 37.27% per-domain-averaged accuracy at 1% labels, exceeding BrAD (27.45%) and CycleMAE (24.71%). BSS consistently improves over FA by 1.5–5.9% across all datasets and label fractions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On PACS at 1% and 5% labels, SimCLR w/BSS outperforms all UDG baselines without ImageNet transfer: it beats BrAD† by +1.11% and +4.37% respectively, CycleMAE† by +5.09% and +10.97%, DiMAE† by +10.18% and +15.49%, and DARLING by +18.75% and +17.20%. At 10% and 100% labels, CycleMAE† and DiMAE† still lead (62.87% and 71.41% vs. 60.04% and 70.23%), but they use different experimental settings (ViT-base, full fine-tuning, ImageNet transfer). On DomainNet at 1% labels, SimCLR w/BSS (32.27% overall) surpasses BrAD (25.85%), CycleMAE (24.08%), and DiMAE (21.85%), while SWaV w/BSS (34.32%) further extends the lead. On Camelyon17 WILDS, SimCLR w/BSS achieves SOTA at all label fractions (92.27–95.00%), exceeding DiMAE (89.81–90.40%), DARLING (70.44–72.43%), and even SWaV trained with additional unlabeled target data (91.40% at 100%). The consistent 1.5–5.9% gains of BSS over FA across all three datasets confirm that batch-level style standardization provides a meaningful advantage over independent per-sample Fourier augmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Fourier-based Augmentations (FA) from Xu et al. (2021) and Yang & Soatto (2020) by repurposing their style-transfer capability from an independent per-image augmentation into a batch-level standardization operation (BSS). Conceptually, it departs from prior UDG methods (DARLING, BrAD, DiMAE, CycleMAE) that rely on domain labels and domain-specific architectural components (negative queues, decoders) to enforce domain-invariance, instead simulating a shared 'pseudo-domain' by transferring one random image's style to all batch members. The method is integrated into three SSL frameworks—SimCLR, SWaV, and MSN—each requiring different placement of BSS (all views for SimCLR, global views only for SWaV, unmasked views only for MSN) to align with where spurious domain/style correlations would otherwise arise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BSS offers a practical simplification for UDG: eliminating both domain labels and domain-specific network components makes the approach more scalable to many domains and easier to deploy in settings where domain annotations are unavailable. The ablation studies (domain purity, harder-negative histograms, batch-size sensitivity, prototype homogeneity) provide mechanistic evidence for why style standardization helps, which is valuable for understanding SSL representation geometry. However, the performance gains over plain FA are modest (typically 1–5%), the evaluation is limited to three datasets, and the paper acknowledges that other style-transfer techniques (GAN-based, AdaIN) could serve the same purpose, leaving open whether Fourier-based standardization is specifically optimal or merely convenient.
