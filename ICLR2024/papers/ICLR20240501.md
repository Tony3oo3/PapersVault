## Problem

Sub-4 bit weight-only quantization of LLMs is hindered by activation outliers that amplify rounding errors in sensitive weight channels. Conventional per-output-channel (per-OC) quantization spreads the outlier effect across all groups within each output channel, making it difficult to isolate. Moreover, the dominant sensitivity dimension (input vs. output channel) varies across layers, modules, and network depth, so a fixed grouping strategy is suboptimal.

## Input / Output

The method consumes full-precision weight matrices of each linear layer and a small calibration set of activations (256 samples × 512 tokens from The Pile or a task-specific corpus such as GSM8k/MBPP). It produces low-bit quantized weights (INT3 or INT4, group size 128, uniform asymmetric) along with a per-layer binary decision specifying whether that layer uses per-IC or per-OC grouping. The calibration data is used only during quantization to compute reconstruction errors and Hessian approximations; at inference time only the quantized weights and their scales are needed.

## Method

The paper introduces per-IC quantization, which groups consecutive weights within each input channel rather than each output channel, creating a 1:1 mapping between hidden dimensions and quantization groups so that outlier-induced sensitivity is confined to a single group. On top of this, AdaDim formulates the choice of quantization dimension as a binary optimization: for each linear layer, it quantizes the weights twice with RTN (once per-IC, once per-OC), computes the L2 reconstruction error against the full-precision layer output on calibration data, and selects the dimension with lower error. This requires only two forward passes per layer. The selected dimension is then used as the grouping axis for subsequent GPTQ iterative quantization with Hessian-based error compensation. A key side benefit is that per-IC GPTQ enables hardware-efficient activation reordering (scales remain contiguous in row-major memory layout) without the static-groups constraint that degrades per-OC accuracy.

## Results

On INT3 weight-only quantization (w3g128), AdaDim applied to RTN yields a 4.7% MMLU accuracy boost on LLaMA-V2-7B, surpassing both AWQ and GPTQ. For instruction-tuned models, GPTQ-ada achieves up to 62.2% pass@1 on HumanEval (WizCoder-Python-13B with target calibration), outperforming AWQ (60.36%) and vanilla GPTQ (56.71%). On Vicuna-33B, GPTQ-ada bridges the FP16-to-INT3 MMLU gap down to ~2% (57.08 vs. 59.22 FP16), the largest improvement among all methods tested. RTN-ada reduces reconstruction error by up to 6× compared to per-OC RTN in outlier-sensitive layers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPTQ, AdaDim (GPTQ-ada) consistently improves accuracy: on Vicuna-33B MMLU it gains +1.4% (57.08 vs. 55.68), and on HumanEval (WizCoder-13B, target calibration) it gains +5.5% (62.2 vs. 56.71). Against AWQ, GPTQ-ada surpasses it on Vicuna-33B MMLU (57.08 vs. 55.30) and on HumanEval 13B (62.2 vs. 60.36), while RTN-ada also edges out AWQ on HumanEval 13B (60.98 vs. 60.36); however, AWQ remains competitive or slightly ahead on smaller models (e.g., Vicuna-7B MMLU: AWQ 45.79 vs. RTN-ada 46.25 is close, but GPTQ-ada 47.45 wins). Against SpQR, RTN-ada achieves comparable perplexity at similar average bit-precision (e.g., LLaMA-65B w4g128: RTN-ada 3.61/5.68 vs. SpQR 3.68/5.70 on WikiText2/C4), but SpQR requires an additional FP16 sparse outlier kernel while AdaDim does not. The per-IC kernel is slower than optimized per-OC kernels (LUT-GEMM, AWQ) but still faster than the cuBLAS FP16 baseline, and is applied selectively per-layer rather than globally.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GPTQ line of work by introducing per-input-channel (per-IC) quantization as an alternative to the standard per-output-channel (per-OC) grouping that underpins virtually all prior weight-only quantization methods (GPTQ, AWQ, RTN). The key conceptual departure is recognizing that activation outliers propagate along the input dimension of the weight matrix, so grouping weights along that same IC direction isolates the outlier effect within a single quantization group rather than letting it permeate all groups as in per-OC. The AdaDim framework then adaptively selects per-IC or per-OC per layer via a two-option reconstruction-error search, positioning itself as a lightweight augmentation to existing methods rather than a replacement. The paper explicitly notes incompatibility with AWQ's activation-scaling mechanism, which relies on per-OC grouping to identify and protect sensitive weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in offering a simple, drop-in improvement (two extra forward passes per layer) to widely-used quantization pipelines, with a working per-IC LUT-GEMM kernel demonstrating that the approach is not purely theoretical. However, the per-IC kernel remains slower than optimized per-OC kernels (Table 10), and the gains are most pronounced at INT3 where the outlier problem is most acute; at INT4 the landscape is more competitive with no clear winner. Open questions include whether a fully optimized per-IC kernel can close the latency gap, whether the approach generalizes beyond the LLaMA/WizardLM family, and how AdaDim interacts with emerging sparse-quantized representations like SpQR that handle outliers through a different mechanism entirely.
