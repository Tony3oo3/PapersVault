## Problem

Tensor products of irreducible representations (irreps) are the core operation in E(3)-equivariant neural networks, but their computational cost scales as O(L^6) with the maximum irrep degree L, limiting practical models to L=2 or 3. Since higher-degree irreps consistently improve accuracy on molecular and materials tasks, this bottleneck prevents scaling equivariant models to the representation richness needed for state-of-the-art performance on large systems.

## Input / Output

The method operates on equivariant features expressed as collections of irreps up to degree L (a vector of dimension (L+1)^2 per channel) and 3D atomic positions that define spherical harmonic filters. It produces the full Clebsch-Gordan tensor product decomposed into output irreps of all degrees from 0 to L₁+L₂, which then feed into downstream prediction heads for energy (scalar) and per-atom forces (type-1 irrep). The approach is drop-in compatible with existing equivariant architectures (EquiformerV2, MACE, SEGNN) and supports channel-wise or channel-mixing combinations across C feature channels.

## Method

The key insight is a mathematical equivalence between Clebsch-Gordan coefficients and Gaunt coefficients (integrals of three spherical harmonics), which reframes the irrep tensor product as pointwise multiplication of two spherical functions expanded in the spherical harmonic basis. By changing basis from spherical harmonics to a 2D Fourier basis (exploiting the sparsity that only m=±v terms are nonzero), this multiplication becomes a 2D convolution computable in O(L² log L) via the convolution theorem and FFT. The full pipeline is: (1) convert spherical harmonic coefficients to 2D Fourier coefficients in O(L³), (2) perform the 2D convolution via forward FFT, element-wise multiplication, and inverse FFT, and (3) convert back to spherical harmonic coefficients in O(L³), yielding an overall O(L³) complexity versus the original O(L^6). The method generalizes to weighted feature interactions (via reparameterized weights), equivariant convolutions (with additional sparsity from the eSCN rotation trick), and many-body interactions (via divide-and-conquer parallelization of multiple 2D convolutions).

## Results

On the OC20 S2EF benchmark, EquiformerV2+Gaunt-Selfmix (L=6) achieves 276 meV energy MAE, 20.1 meV/Å force MAE, and 1.95% EFwT, improving over EquiformerV2 (L=6) by 16.8% relative on EFwT (1.95% vs 1.67%) while maintaining comparable efficiency. On the 3BPA dataset, MACE-Gaunt achieves 2.9 meV energy error and 9.2 meV/Å force error at 300K, competitive with MACE (3.0 / 8.8), while delivering 43.7× speed-up over e3nn (vs. 33.2× for MACE) and reducing memory to 5.8% of e3nn (vs. 32.8% for MACE). Efficiency comparisons in Figure 1 show the Gaunt Tensor Product achieves multiple orders of magnitude speed-up over e3nn for L>7, and further acceleration over eSCN for equivariant convolutions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On OC20 S2EF, EquiformerV2+Gaunt-Selfmix outperforms all baselines on EFwT, the primary metric: 1.95% at L=6 vs. 1.67% for EquiformerV2, 1.45% for eSCN, and 1.41% for SCN4-tap2-band, with the largest gap over non-equivariant baselines like SchNet (0.00%) and DimeNet++ (0.01%). On 3BPA, MACE-Gaunt is slightly behind MACE on force error at 300K (9.2 vs. 8.8 meV/Å) and energy at 600K (10.6 vs. 9.7 meV), but achieves 43.7× speed-up over e3nn versus MACE's 33.2×, and reduces memory to 5.8% of e3nn versus MACE's 32.8%, making it far more deployment-friendly. In the efficiency comparisons (Figure 1), the Gaunt Tensor Product achieves multiple orders of magnitude speed-up over e3nn for L>7 in feature interactions, further acceleration over eSCN for convolutions (by combining both sparsity insights), and consistently outperforms the MACE implementation across all L and ν settings for many-body interactions. The sanity check on N-body simulation with SEGNN confirms that the Gaunt parameterization does not degrade accuracy relative to the standard Clebsch-Gordan tensor product.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Clebsch-Gordan tensor product as implemented in e3nn (Geiger & Smidt, 2022), which is the standard computational primitive for E(3)-equivariant neural networks. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that Clebsch-Gordan coefficients are proportional to Gaunt coefficients (integrals of three spherical harmonics), thereby recasting the tensor product as pointwise multiplication of spherical functions. This perspective, combined with the sparsity insight from eSCN (Passaro & Zitnick, 2023) for equivariant convolutions, enables a change of basis to a 2D Fourier representation where the convolution theorem and FFT reduce the full tensor product from O(L⁶) to O(L³). The method is positioned as a drop-in replacement across three major operation classes (feature interactions, convolutions, many-body interactions) used in architectures like MACE, EquiformerV2, and SEGNN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(L⁶)→O(L³) complexity reduction is practically significant because it removes the primary bottleneck that has limited equivariant models to low irrep degrees (L=2 or 3) in practice, opening the door to higher-degree representations that prior work has shown improve accuracy. The 43.7× speedup and 82.3% memory reduction on MACE, combined with competitive or improved accuracy on OC20 and 3BPA, suggest this technique will be widely adopted as a standard component in equivariant ML force field pipelines. An open question is whether the Gaunt coefficient parameterization (which excludes pseudovectors from output) could matter in tasks where parity-sensitive features are critical, though the sanity check and the observation that top-performing models already avoid even-parity irreps suggest this is unlikely to be a practical limitation.
