## Problem

Existing continuous-time dynamic graph (CTDG) models for link prediction rely on complex modules (random walks, temporal point processes, ODEs) that are computationally expensive and prone to overfitting. More critically, they encode interaction sequences independently and struggle to capture the 'shift' phenomenon, where node interaction patterns change over time (e.g., researchers suddenly collaborating with unfamiliar experts on a new topic). The paper asks whether moving from the time domain to the frequency domain can more effectively reveal these periodic and shifting interaction patterns.

## Input / Output

Given a node pair (u, v) at timestamp t, the model samples L first-hop historical neighbors for each node ordered by temporal proximity (zero-padded if fewer than L exist), then encodes node features, edge features, relative timestamps (t_n - t rather than absolute t), and a novel node interaction frequency (NIF) feature that records both the appearance frequency of each neighbor in the other node's history and the node pair's own historical interaction count. The output is a single link prediction probability produced by a 2-layer MLP with softmax over the concatenated time-aware node representations h_u^t and h_v^t.

## Method

FreeDyG introduces two key components. First, a Node Interaction Frequency (NIF) encoding that explicitly captures both the proportion of common neighbors and the frequency of the node pair's own past interactions, going beyond DyGFormer's simple co-occurrence counting. Second, a Frequency-Enhanced MLP-Mixer layer that applies 1D FFT along the temporal dimension of the encoded embedding sequence, multiplies the resulting complex frequency components by a learnable complex tensor W to adaptively amplify or suppress specific frequencies, and transforms back via IFFT before passing through standard MLP-Mixer token-mixing and channel-mixing sublayers. The paper proves (Theorem 1) that this frequency-domain multiplication is equivalent to global circular convolution in the time domain, providing a theoretical justification for the mechanism. A learnable weighted aggregation (rather than average pooling) produces the final time-aware node representations, and the entire model is trained with binary cross-entropy loss under a supervised setting.

## Results

FreeDyG achieves the best average rank (1.0) across all seven datasets under random negative sampling for transductive link prediction, with AP scores of 99.26 (Wiki), 99.48 (Reddit), 89.61 (MOOC), 92.15 (LastFM), 92.51 (Enron), 94.91 (Social Evo), and 96.28 (UCI). Under the more challenging historical negative sampling, FreeDyG maintains an average rank of 1.28, with notable gains over the second-best method DyGFormer (e.g., 91.59 vs. 82.23 AP on Wiki, 86.10 vs. 82.17 on UCI). The model also achieves the best average rank (1.71) under inductive negative sampling. FreeDyG attains these results with a small parameter count and moderate per-epoch training time, as shown in Figure 2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeDyG outperforms all nine baselines in the majority of settings, with the closest competitor being DyGFormer (Yu et al., 2023). Under random negative sampling, FreeDyG beats DyGFormer on all seven datasets (e.g., 99.26 vs. 98.82 on Wiki, 89.61 vs. 87.23 on MOOC). Under historical negative sampling, FreeDyG wins on 6 of 7 datasets but loses to DyGFormer on LastFM (79.71 vs. 81.57 AP); under inductive negative sampling, FreeDyG loses to DyGFormer on Reddit (90.74 vs. 91.11), LastFM (72.19 vs. 73.97), and Social Evo (97.57 vs. 97.68). RNN-based methods (JODIE, DyRep) and random-walk-based CAWN are consistently outperformed by 5–20 AP points across datasets. EdgeBank, a memory-based transductive-only method, is substantially outperformed (e.g., 90.37 vs. 99.26 on Wiki). Self-attention-based methods (TGAT, TCL) and the MLP-based GraphMixer are also consistently surpassed, with FreeDyG achieving the best average rank (1.0) versus DyGFormer (2.14) under random sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

FreeDyG extends the CTDG link prediction line of work by shifting the representational focus from the temporal domain (used by JODIE, TGN, TGAT, DyGFormer) to the frequency domain via FFT. Architecturally, it builds on MLP-Mixer (Tolstikhin et al., 2021) as its non-linear backbone and extends DyGFormer's (Yu et al., 2023) neighbor co-occurrence mechanism with a more explicit node interaction frequency encoding that captures both common-neighbor proportions and pairwise interaction counts. The paper deliberately departs from methods relying on RNNs, self-attention, random walks, temporal point processes, or ODEs, arguing these are computationally expensive and prone to overfitting, in favor of a simpler MLP-based design augmented with a learnable complex-valued frequency filter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frequency-domain perspective offers a principled way to capture periodic interaction patterns and the 'shift' phenomenon (e.g., collaboration patterns changing when new research topics emerge), which prior temporal-domain methods struggle to model explicitly. The practical appeal is that FreeDyG achieves competitive or superior performance with fewer parameters and moderate training time compared to attention- or walk-based methods, making it attractive for deployment. However, the model currently handles only edge-addition events (not deletions), and the NIF encoding can introduce false positives under historical negative sampling, suggesting the frequency-enhancing layer is critical for robustness. Open questions include whether the FFT-based approach generalizes to heterogeneous or multi-relational dynamic graphs and whether the learnable frequency filter can be interpreted to reveal which periodicities drive specific domains.
