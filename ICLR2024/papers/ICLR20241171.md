## Problem

Vision transformers incur quadratic O(n²) self-attention cost at every layer, yet the paper's CKA analysis reveals that attention maps and MSA representations are highly correlated across adjacent layers (cosine similarity up to 0.97 for CLS-token attention in layers 3–10 of ViT-T/16). This redundancy means most MSA computations are unnecessary, yet no prior method exploits cross-layer attention correlation to skip entire MSA blocks while preserving or improving accuracy across both classification and dense prediction tasks.

## Input / Output

SKIPAT is a drop-in plug-in that does not alter the input/output interface of the underlying transformer: it accepts the same tokenized image (n×d embeddings after patch projection) or video patch sequence and produces the same final-layer representation. For classification, input is a 224×224 RGB image yielding a class label; for segmentation, a 2048×512 image yielding a pixel-wise mask; for denoising, a 256×256 noisy image or 7×128×128 video patch yielding a clean image/video. The total number of transformer layers remains unchanged—only the MSA blocks in selected layers are replaced by the lighter parametric function.

## Method

SKIPAT replaces the MSA block in layers 3–8 of a ViT with a lightweight parametric function Φ that maps the previous layer's MSA output Z_{l-1}^{MSA} to an approximation Ẑ_l^{MSA}. The function consists of a channel-expanding linear layer (d→2d), a 5×5 depth-wise convolution (which encodes local token relations analogous to local attention), a channel-reducing linear layer (2d→d), and an Efficient Channel Attention module, with GeLU activations between stages. The output is added residually to Z_{l-1} and fed into the unchanged MLP block, so the MLP still learns independently at each layer. This reduces per-block complexity from O(n²d) to O(nd²), yielding 19–25% throughput gains on ViT while simultaneously improving top-1 accuracy by 0.1–0.4% over the baseline. The method is architecture-agnostic, applying identically to isotropic (ViT), hierarchical (PvT), and hybrid (LIT) backbones, and generalizes to dense prediction (ADE20K segmentation), image denoising (SIDD via Uformer), and video denoising (DAVIS via UniFormer).

## Results

On ImageNet-1K (224×224), SKIPAT achieves 73.3% top-1 accuracy with ViT-T/16 (vs. 72.8% baseline) at 6.9K img/s throughput (vs. 5.8K), and 82.2% with ViT-B/16 (vs. 81.8%) at 1.5K img/s (vs. 1.2K), improving both accuracy and throughput simultaneously. On ADE20K semantic segmentation, ViT-T+SKIPAT reaches 40.6 mIoU (vs. 37.3% baseline) with 15% fewer FLOPs and 44% higher throughput. On SIDD image denoising, Uformer-B+SKIPAT achieves 39.94 PSNR (vs. 39.89 baseline) with 25% higher throughput on average across all Uformer variants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SKIPAT outperforms all token-sampling baselines (A-ViT, ATS, PS-ViT, Dynamic ViT, SPViT) on both accuracy and throughput for the same backbone: on ViT-T/16, SKIPAT reaches 73.3% top-1 vs. 72.7% (ATS) and 72.6% (PS-ViT), while also achieving the highest throughput at 6.9K img/s. Against Rev-ViT (which matches ViT-S accuracy at 79.8%), SKIPAT achieves 80.0% with 19% higher throughput (3.8K vs. 3.2K baseline). On hierarchical and hybrid backbones, SKIPAT improves PvT-T by 1.0% accuracy and 19% throughput, and LIT-S by 0.5% accuracy and 15% throughput. For dense prediction on ADE20K, ViT-S+SKIPAT (45.3 mIoU) surpasses Swin-T (45.8 mIoU) with 3× fewer FLOPs and 1.7× higher throughput, and matches ConvNeXt-T (46.7 mIoU) within 1.4 mIoU while being 3.4× faster. On SIDD denoising, Uformer-B+SKIPAT (39.94 PSNR) approaches Restormer (40.02 PSNR) with 2× fewer FLOPs and 1.2× higher throughput. The only comparison where SKIPAT does not lead is on-device latency at 384×384, where the 34% speedup is still substantial but the absolute latency (15.22 ms) remains higher than the 224×224 case (4.76 ms).

## Relation to Prior Work

SKIPAT builds directly on the ViT architecture and borrows the depth-wise convolution design from ResNeXt to construct its lightweight parametric function. It departs from three existing efficiency paradigms for ViTs: token sampling (which breaks spatial continuity for dense prediction), hybrid architectures (which require custom block design), and efficient attention (which modifies the attention computation itself). Instead, SKIPAT exploits a previously underexplored redundancy—high cross-layer correlation in attention maps (cosine similarity up to 0.97)—to skip entire MSA blocks and replace them with a simple FC-DwC-FC function, drawing a conceptual parallel to attention-sharing observations in NLP (Vig & Belinkov, 2019; Xiao et al., 2019) but applying a parametric approximation rather than direct copying.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SKIPAT offers a practical, architecture-agnostic plug-in that improves both throughput and accuracy simultaneously across classification, segmentation, denoising, and video tasks, which is unusual for efficiency methods that typically trade accuracy for speed. The 26% reduction in self-supervised pretraining time and validated on-device latency gains make it relevant for both research and deployment. Open questions include whether the fixed layer-skip schedule (layers 3–8) generalizes to much deeper or differently structured transformers, whether the parametric function's regularizer effect (rather than the skip itself) drives the accuracy gains, and whether the approach extends to tasks requiring very fine-grained spatial attention (e.g., instance segmentation at high resolution).
