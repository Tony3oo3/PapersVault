## Problem

Existing multimodal molecular pretraining methods treat 2D graphs and 3D conformations as independent signals encoded by separate models, performing only coarse-grained molecule-level alignment via contrastive or cross-prediction objectives. This ignores the intrinsic atomic-relation-level correlation between the two modalities and fails to foster fine-grained structural understanding of molecular sub-structures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240527/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pretraining, the model receives a single blended n×n relation matrix where each (i,j) entry is randomly drawn from one of three relation encodings (shortest path distance, edge type, or 3D Euclidean distance) according to a fixed multinomial distribution (default 2:2:6), and it must recover all three full relation matrices as prediction targets. During finetuning, the model accepts either 2D-only input (SPD + edge type) or combined 2D+3D input (all three relations summed as attention bias) and outputs molecular property predictions (classification or regression). The outer product projection module maps atom embeddings to n×n relation matrices, with separate heads for SPD/edge-type classification and 3D-distance regression.

## Method

MOLEBLEND introduces a 'blend-then-predict' self-supervised paradigm over a single 12-layer Transformer (768 hidden, 32 heads). In the encoding stage, three relation matrices (SPD, edge type, 3D distance) are element-wise randomly mixed into one unified n×n matrix via multinomial sampling, which is then injected as an additive attention bias into every Transformer layer, explicitly binding 2D and 3D information at the atomic-pair level from the first layer. In the prediction stage, an outer product projection module transforms contextual atom representations into n×n relation matrices through two independent linear projections, outer product, flattening, and modality-specific heads, recovering SPD and edge type as classification targets and 3D distance as a 3-dimensional regression target. A noisy-node coordinate denoising loss serves as an additional regularization term. Theoretically, the paper proves via mutual information decomposition that this single objective simultaneously subsumes contrastive (cross-modality alignment), generative (inter-modality prediction), and mask-then-predict (intra-modality reconstruction) objectives, plus a novel multimodal mask-then-predict component.

## Results

On MoleculeNet 2D classification (scaffold split), MOLEBLEND achieves an average ROC-AUC of 76.16, outperforming the best 2D baseline Mole-BERT (74.04) and the best multimodal baseline GraphMVP under Transformer (74.86), with SOTA on 5 of 8 tasks (e.g., BACE 83.7 vs. 81.5, Tox21 77.8 vs. 76.8). On QM9 3D regression, MOLEBLEND achieves SOTA on 8 of 12 tasks with particularly large margins on electronic properties (Gap: 34.75 vs. 41.77 MAE; HOMO: 21.47 vs. 25.74; LUMO: 19.23 vs. 21.41). On MoleculeNet 2D regression, MOLEBLEND achieves the best RMSE on all three tasks (ESOL 0.831, FreeSolv 1.910, Lipo 0.638).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOLEBLEND outperforms all 13 baselines on the MoleculeNet 2D classification average (76.16 vs. 74.86 for the best multimodal Transformer baseline GraphMVP and 74.04 for the best 2D baseline Mole-BERT), winning on 5 of 8 individual tasks with the largest margin on BACE (+2.2 over GraphMVP-Transformer). On QM9 3D tasks, MOLEBLEND achieves SOTA on 8 of 12 properties, with dramatic gains on electronic properties (Gap −7.02 MAE, HOMO −4.27, LUMO −2.18 over MoleculeSDE), but trails on Alpha (0.060 vs. 0.054), Mu (0.037 vs. 0.026), and R2 (0.417 vs. 0.151). On 2D regression (ESOL, FreeSolv, Lipo), MOLEBLEND achieves the best RMSE across all three tasks, beating 3D InfoMax by 0.063 on ESOL and 0.427 on FreeSolv. The paper also implements 3D InfoMax and GraphMVP under the same Transformer backbone to isolate the effect of the pretraining objective, showing MOLEBLEND's advantage is not merely architectural. Ablations confirm that the blend-then-predict objective outperforms single-modality mask-then-predict (e.g., BACE 83.41 vs. 81.97 for Edge mask) and that combining it with noisy-node regularization yields the best overall performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOLEBLEND builds directly on the single-Transformer architecture and three-relation representation scheme (shortest path distance, edge type, 3D Euclidean distance) introduced by Luo et al. (2022), and adopts the Gaussian Basis Kernel encoding for 3D distances from Uni-Mol (Zhou et al., 2023) and noisy-node regularization from Godwin et al. (2022). The key departure is from prior multimodal molecular pretraining methods (GraphMVP, 3D InfoMax, MoleculeSDE, UnifiedMol), which encode 2D and 3D modalities in separate models and align them only at the molecule level via contrastive or cross-modal prediction. MOLEBLEND instead blends the three relation types into a single atom-pair matrix at the input level, feeding one unified Transformer, and recovers all three relation types as prediction targets — a 'blend-then-predict' paradigm that the paper shows theoretically unifies contrastive, generative, and mask-then-predict objectives under a single mutual-information maximization framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The fine-grained relation-level alignment offers a principled alternative to molecule-level contrastive alignment, and the theoretical decomposition showing that a single blend-then-predict objective subsumes three previously separate pretraining paradigms provides a useful unifying lens for the field. However, the QM9 results reveal that MOLEBLEND does not achieve SOTA on all 12 tasks (e.g., Alpha, Mu, R2), and the blending-ratio ablation shows relatively flat performance across ratios, suggesting the specific blending mechanism may be less critical than the overall multimodal integration. Open questions include whether this approach scales to larger molecules beyond the 9-heavy-atom QM9 regime, whether the random per-position sampling is truly necessary versus simply concatenating all three relations, and how the method performs when 3D conformations are of variable quality.
