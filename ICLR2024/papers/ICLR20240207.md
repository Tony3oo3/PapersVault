## Problem

Naively interpolating absolute position embeddings (e.g., via bicubic upsampling) while using window attention causes a silent bug: the learned tiled/repeated embedding patterns become misaligned with the fixed-size attention windows at higher resolutions, leading to significant accuracy drops. The paper identifies this issue in two state-of-the-art models (Hiera and ViTDet) and shows that the bug explains why ViTDet requires costly relative position embeddings and why Hiera underperforms ViT on detection despite stronger pretraining.

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images pretrained at 224×224 and finetuned at resolutions from 256×256 up to 1280×1280 for detection, as well as video clips (16–32 frames at 224–320px) for action recognition. The critical constraint is the resolution mismatch between pretraining and finetuning: the position embedding must be resized to match the new token grid while preserving alignment with fixed-size attention windows (e.g., 8×8 in Hiera stages 1–2, 14×14 in ViTDet). Outputs span classification labels, bounding boxes, instance segmentation masks, and spatio-temporal action localizations depending on the downstream task.

## Method

The paper introduces 'absolute win,' a position embedding strategy that decomposes the embedding into two learned components: a small window embedding (one window's worth of tokens, e.g., 8×8) that is tiled across the spatial grid, and a small global embedding (the feature map size after window pooling, e.g., 7×7) that is bicubic-interpolated to the target resolution. The full position embedding is their sum, making it a drop-in replacement for standard absolute embeddings while guaranteeing correct window alignment at any resolution. For detection (ViTDet and the new HieraDet), the pretrained model's original position embeddings are tiled to serve as the window embedding, and relative position embeddings are retained only in the few global attention layers (3–4 out of 48), eliminating the need for relpos in all window attention layers. This yields up to 43% inference speedup over ViTDet while improving COCO box mAP, and establishes HieraDet as state-of-the-art for ImageNet-1k-pretrained detection at 61.7 box mAP.

## Results

On COCO detection (minival, multi-scale test), HieraDet-H with absolute win achieves 61.7 box mAP and 53.3 mask mAP using only ImageNet-1k MAE pretraining, establishing a new state-of-the-art for ImageNet-pretrained models. On ImageNet-1k classification, Hiera-L with absolute win reaches 86.9% top-1 accuracy at 384px finetuning (vs. 86.4% without the fix), and Hiera-H reaches 87.3%. On Kinetics-400 video classification at 32×320px, Hiera-L with absolute win achieves 88.0% accuracy, outperforming VideoMAE's ViT-L (86.1%). The fix also yields up to 43% inference speedup over the original ViTDet by removing most relative position embeddings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original Hiera (Ryali et al., 2023), absolute win improves COCO detection by +1.2 box mAP and +1.4 mask mAP for Hiera-L (55.0→56.2 box, 48.6→50.0 mask), and by +1.5 box mAP for Hiera-B (52.2→53.7). Against ViTDet (Li et al., 2022a), HieraDet-H with absolute win surpasses ViTDet-H by +0.3 box mAP (60.7 vs. 60.4) single-scale and +0.4 box mAP (61.7 vs. 61.3) multi-scale, while being significantly faster due to fewer relative position embeddings. Against Swin (Liu et al., 2021b) and MViTv2 (Li et al., 2022b), HieraDet-L outperforms both by +3.0 box mAP (60.1 vs. 57.1) on COCO single-scale, and Hiera-B with absolute win gains +1.3% over Hiera-B on ImageNet-1k at 384px (85.8 vs. 85.0), a larger resolution benefit than Swin-B (+1.0%) or MViTv2-B (+0.8%). On Kinetics-400, Hiera-L with absolute win (88.0%) outperforms VideoMAE's ViT-L (86.1%) by 1.9% at 32×320px. On LVIS v1, HieraDet-H achieves 48.9 AP_mask, approaching the 2021 challenge winner (49.2) and substantially outperforming ViTDet-H (48.1) and Detic (41.7).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper identifies a bug at the intersection of three widely-used components in modern vision transformers: window attention, absolute position embeddings, and high-resolution finetuning via interpolation. It builds directly on Hiera (Ryali et al., 2023), which uses window attention with absolute position embeddings, and ViTDet (Li et al., 2022a), which adds window attention to a ViT backbone for detection. The key departure from prior work is the observation that the standard bicubic interpolation of absolute position embeddings silently breaks the learned window-aligned patterns, and that the common workaround of adding relative position embeddings (as in ViTDet) is partially compensating for this bug rather than addressing a genuine architectural need. The proposed 'absolute win' fix decomposes the embedding into a tiled window component and an interpolated global component, making it a drop-in replacement for standard absolute position embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: the fix is a ~3-line change that simultaneously improves accuracy and enables removal of relative position embeddings, yielding up to 43% inference speedup and compatibility with Flash Attention. This establishes a best practice for any future architecture combining window attention with absolute position embeddings, and the paper demonstrates the fix across classification, detection, instance segmentation, and video tasks at multiple model scales. Open questions include whether other architectures (e.g., Swin with relative embeddings) could benefit from switching to the absolute win formulation, and whether the smooth-periodicity regularization hinted at in Appendix E.3 could offer an alternative fix. The paper also raises the broader question of how much of the perceived need for relative position embeddings in detection is actually an artifact of this interpolation bug.
