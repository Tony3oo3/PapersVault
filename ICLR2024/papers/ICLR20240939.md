## Problem

LoRA reduces fine-tuning parameters by constraining the weight change ΔW to a low-rank factorization, but its parameter count is lower-bounded by the rank-1 decomposition and is inherently tied to both the chosen rank (an integer) and the dimensions of each weight matrix. This means the compression ratio cannot exceed mn/(m+n) per layer and varies across architectures, limiting how compactly task-specific adapters can be stored for large-scale multi-task LLM deployment.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pre-trained model (GPT-2, LLaMA-2, ViT, or ResNet) and task-specific training data (text sequences for NLG/instruction tuning, or RGB images for classification) and produces a compact adapter consisting only of two coefficient vectors (α, β) plus a single scalar seed for the PRNG. Unlike LoRA, which stores full A and B matrices per layer, NOLA's adapter is architecture-agnostic in size: the same number of coefficients works regardless of the weight matrix shape, enabling storage of thousands of task adapters in GPU memory. At inference, the basis matrices are regenerated on-the-fly from the seed and multiplied by the coefficients to reconstruct ΔW, which can then be merged into the frozen weights.

## Method

NOLA reparameterizes LoRA's rank-decomposition matrices A ∈ R^{m×r} and B ∈ R^{r×n} as linear combinations of k and l frozen pseudo-random basis matrices respectively: A = Σᵢ αᵢAᵢ and B = Σⱼ βⱼBⱼ, where the basis matrices are generated by a PRNG with a fixed seed. Only the coefficient vectors α and β are optimized during training, decoupling the trainable parameter count (k + l) from both the rank r and the weight dimensions m, n. This breaks LoRA's rank-1 lower bound, allowing compression ratios beyond mn/(m+n) per layer. The random basis is generated in chunks on the GPU at each iteration to avoid storing it, and can be shared across layers to match LoRA's training speed. Coefficients can be further quantized to 4-bit (or 2-bit with quantization-aware training) without significant accuracy loss, since the basis matrices themselves remain at full FP16 precision.

## Results

On LLaMA-2 70B (8-bit), NOLA fine-tunes with only 0.57M trainable parameters (95% fewer than rank-one LoRA's 12.94M) while matching MMLU accuracy (69.4% vs. 69.5%) and achieving a validation loss of 0.90 vs. 0.92. On GPT-2 Large E2E NLG, NOLA with 0.036M parameters achieves a BLEU of 70.1, matching LoRA rank-4 (0.77M, BLEU 70.4) with 20× fewer parameters. On ViT-B image classification, NOLA-MLP with 47K parameters matches or exceeds LoRA(r=4) with 141K parameters across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, CUB-200, and Caltech-101 in both 5-shot and 10-shot settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA (Hu et al., 2021), NOLA matches or exceeds accuracy with 3–20× fewer parameters: on LLaMA-2 70B, NOLA (0.57M) ties LoRA rank-1 (12.94M) on MMLU (69.4 vs. 69.5), and on GPT-2L E2E, NOLA (0.036M) achieves BLEU 70.1 vs. LoRA r=4 (0.77M) at 70.4. Against QLoRA (Dettmers et al., 2023), NOLA is equivalent in setup (both use 8-bit base model) but requires 95% fewer trainable parameters while matching MMLU. Against PRANC (Nooralinejad et al., 2022), NOLA wins on both training-from-scratch benchmarks: 82.4% vs. 81.5% on CIFAR-10/ResNet20 and 64.66% vs. 61.08% on ImageNet100/ResNet18, while also training 4.6× faster. Against Adapters (Houlsby et al., 2019) and AdapterFusion (Pfeiffer et al., 2021), NOLA with 0.096M parameters outperforms Adapter^L (0.370M, BLEU 66.3) and Adapter^H (11.090M, BLEU 67.3) on E2E NLG. Against Prefix-Tuning (Li & Liang, 2021), NOLA with 0.036M parameters nearly matches PreLayer (0.770M) on E2E BLEU (70.1 vs. 70.3). Against HashedNet (Chen et al., 2015), NOLA substantially outperforms on ImageNet100 (64.66% vs. 52.96%) with comparable parameter budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

NOLA combines two established ideas: LoRA's low-rank factorization of weight deltas (Hu et al., 2021) and PRANC's reparameterization of weights as linear combinations of frozen random basis matrices (Nooralinejad et al., 2022). The key departure is applying PRANC's random-basis mechanism specifically to LoRA's A and B matrices rather than to full weight tensors, which preserves the low-rank structure's computational benefits while decoupling the parameter count from both the rank hyperparameter and the weight matrix dimensions. This positions NOLA as a strict superset of LoRA's expressiveness (breaking the rank-1 lower bound) while remaining compatible with QLoRA-style quantization of the base model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation—efficiently storing and switching between many task-specific LLMs within GPU memory—is increasingly relevant as organizations deploy dozens or hundreds of fine-tuned models. The 20× compression over rank-1 LoRA on LLaMA-2 70B without accuracy loss is a concrete, well-evidenced win across NLP and vision tasks. Open questions include a theoretical understanding of why NOLA's A×B factorization covers more of the weight space than PRANC's flat reparameterization (only empirically demonstrated here), the optimal rank choice (acknowledged as needing more study), and whether the random-basis approach generalizes to non-transformer architectures beyond the ResNet experiments in the appendix.
