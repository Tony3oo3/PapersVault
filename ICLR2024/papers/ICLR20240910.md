## Problem

Existing classifier guidance methods for diffusion models (classifier guidance and classifier-free guidance) require expensive extra training with labeled data, while training-free alternatives like universal guidance have not matched their quantitative performance. This paper asks whether off-the-shelf pretrained classifiers can be made to guide diffusion generation as well as or better than fine-tuned classifiers, without any additional training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240910/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a class label (or text prompt for CLIP-guided generation) and operates on top of a pretrained diffusion model (DDPM, EDM, or DiT) at resolutions from 64×64 to 256×256. A key design choice is that the off-the-shelf classifier (ResNet-50/101 or CLIP) receives the predicted denoised sample x̂₀(t) rather than the noisy intermediate x̂_t; for latent-space models like DiT, the VAE decoder is applied to the latent before classification. The output is a generated image, with the classifier gradient injected into the reverse-diffusion mean at each step.

## Method

The paper proposes four pre-conditioning techniques to make off-the-shelf classifiers effective for diffusion guidance, grounded in a calibration-based theoretical framework (Proposition 4.1 links small calibration error to accurate gradient estimation under smoothness). First, the classifier input is switched from the noisy sample to the predicted denoised sample x̂₀(t), improving calibration from ECE 0.36 to 0.28. Second, ReLU activations in the classifier are replaced with Softplus (β=3) to increase smoothness, further reducing ECE to 0.07. Third, a joint-vs-conditional direction correction uses asymmetric logit temperatures (τ₁=1, τ₂=0.5) so the gradient targets the class mode rather than a potentially low-density region, as shown by a closed-form mixed-Gaussian analysis (Proposition 4.2). Fourth, a sine-modulated guidance schedule γ_t = σ_t + γσ_T·sin(πt/T) amplifies guidance during the high-noise phase where off-the-shelf classifiers are best calibrated. Together these designs let a standard PyTorch ResNet-101 outperform fine-tuned classifier guidance (FID 2.19 vs 2.97) and classifier-free guidance (FID 2.43) on ImageNet 128×128 with no extra training.

## Results

On ImageNet 128×128 with 250 DDPM steps, the proposed off-the-shelf ResNet-101 guided sampling achieves FID 2.19 and IS 187.83, outperforming the fine-tuned classifier-guided baseline (FID 2.97), classifier-free diffusion (FID 2.43), and the unguided DDPM baseline (FID 5.91). The method also improves EDM (FID 2.22 vs. 2.35 at 36 ODE steps) and DiT (FID 2.12 vs. 2.27) with minimal extra sampling time (e.g., 8.4 vs. 8.0 GPU hours for EDM). The calibrated ResNet reduces FID from 7.17 to 5.19 compared to uncalibrated recurrent guidance at the same computational cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed off-the-shelf classifier guidance outperforms all five baselines across every evaluated setting. Against the fine-tuned U-Net classifier guidance of Dhariwal & Nichol (2021), the ResNet-101 method reduces FID from 2.97 to 2.19 (a 26% relative improvement) while eliminating the 200+ GPU-hour fine-tuning cost. Against classifier-free diffusion (Ho & Salimans, 2022), the method achieves FID 2.19 vs. 2.43, and can further enhance CFG in the DiT setting (FID 2.12 vs. 2.27 baseline). Against the recurrent universal guidance of Bansal et al. (2023), the calibrated ResNet achieves FID 5.19 vs. 7.17 at the same 14.1 GPU-hour sampling time, showing that calibration is more effective than increasing recurrent steps. For EDM (Karras et al., 2022), the guided variant consistently improves FID across 36, 18, and 10 ODE steps (e.g., 2.22 vs. 2.35 at 36 steps) with only 0.4 GPU hours extra. For DiT (Peebles & Xie, 2022), the method reduces FID from 2.27 to 2.12 while slightly improving recall (0.59 vs. 0.57), demonstrating compatibility with latent-space transformer architectures without requiring retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classifier-guided diffusion framework of Dhariwal & Nichol (2021) and the classifier-free guidance of Ho & Salimans (2022) by replacing their trained/fine-tuned time-dependent classifiers with off-the-shelf pretrained networks (ResNet-50/101) in a fully training-free manner. It departs from prior training-free guidance methods (Bansal et al., 2023; Wallace et al., 2023) by introducing a calibration-based design space—predicted denoised sample inputs, Softplus smoothing, joint-vs-conditional direction weighting, and a sine guidance schedule—rather than relying on recurrent guidance iterations or architectural invertibility. The theoretical grounding draws on Sobolev-space smoothness conditions (Proposition 4.1) to connect calibration error to gradient estimation quality, and on a mixed-Gaussian closed-form analysis (Proposition 4.2) to justify the joint-direction weighting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a training-free plug-in that consistently improves FID across three architecturally distinct diffusion models (DDPM, EDM, DiT) with negligible extra compute, and can further boost classifier-free guidance. The counterintuitive finding that fine-tuned time-dependent classifiers are *less* calibrated than off-the-shelf ones at high noise levels challenges the standard assumption that diffusion-specific fine-tuning is necessary for effective guidance. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond class-conditional ImageNet to more complex conditioning (detection, VQA), to video/3D generation, and whether the calibration framework can guide the design of new classifier architectures specifically optimized for diffusion guidance.
