## Problem

Existing 3D single object tracking methods on LiDAR point clouds are category-specific, requiring separate networks trained and evaluated per object category, which incurs redundant parameters and limits generalization. The paper asks whether a single network with shared parameters can simultaneously track objects across all categories, and identifies that direct cross-category training fails due to varying object sizes, shapes, and the resulting inconsistent foreground-background ratios and offset distributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240105/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a template point cloud from the first frame along with its known 3D bounding box dimensions (w, h, l), and search region point clouds from subsequent frames. Search regions are generated by expanding the previous frame's prediction by a scale factor α of the target's own dimensions (rather than a fixed distance), ensuring consistent foreground-background ratios regardless of object size. The output is a 4-parameter 3D tracking box (x, y, z, θ), since the target's size is fixed from the template; in the Siamese variant, intermediate seed points with per-point classification scores are also produced via a 3D RPN.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-part unification strategy integrated into both Siamese (SiamCUT, built on P2B) and motion-centric (MoCUT, built on M²Track) paradigms. First, AdaFormer replaces the fixed-radius PointNet++ backbone with a deformable group vector-attention block: a group regression module predicts per-group 6D transformation vectors (3 scaling + 3 rotation parameters) that warp the default spherical receptive field into an ellipsoidal shape adapted to local geometry, and a vector-attention mechanism then aggregates features within these deformable groups. Second, unified model inputs crop search regions at a scale proportional to the template's dimensions, eliminating category-dependent background interference. Third, the learning objective normalizes 3D offsets by the target's (w, h, l) to unify the numerical range of prediction targets, and defines positive/negative samples via a shape-aware scaled cube (factor β) around the target center, balancing the sample distribution across categories.

## Results

On KITTI, MoCUT (motion-centric) achieves a mean Success/Precision of 65.8%/85.0% using a single shared network across all four categories (Car, Pedestrian, Van, Cyclist), outperforming its category-specific baseline M²Track (62.9%/83.4%) by ~3% in Success. SiamCUT (Siamese) reaches 54.0%/74.6%, a ~12% Success improvement over its P2B baseline (42.4%/60.0%). On NuScenes, MoCUT achieves 51.19%/64.63% mean Success/Precision, surpassing M²Track (49.32%/62.73%). On Waymo Open Dataset, MoCUT attains 61.9%/69.7% (Vehicle) and 32.4%/49.9% (Pedestrian), outperforming all category-specific baselines including CXTrack and M²Track.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoCUT outperforms its direct baseline M²Track by +2.9% Success and +1.6% Precision on KITTI mean, and by +1.87/+1.90 on NuScenes, while also surpassing CXTrack (67.5/85.3) in Precision (85.0 vs 85.3 is a near-tie) and SyncTrack (64.1/81.9) by +1.7/+3.1. SiamCUT dramatically outperforms its P2B baseline (+11.6/+14.6 mean Success/Precision), with the largest gains on Pedestrian (+19.5/+26.6) and Van (+22.3/+26.5), demonstrating the cross-category training benefit for data-scarce categories. However, SiamCUT trails the strongest Siamese SOTA methods (CXTrack, TAT, SyncTrack) in absolute performance, as the Siamese paradigm is more sensitive to shape/size variation. On Waymo, MoCUT beats all category-specific baselines including M²Track (61.9 vs 61.1 Vehicle mean Success) and CXTrack (61.9 vs 57.1), confirming cross-dataset generalization. The paper's key claim is that a single shared-parameter model can match or exceed per-category models, with the advantage most pronounced for small-data categories (Van, Cyclist) where cross-category training provides implicit data augmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D single object tracking (SOT) literature, which has been dominated by category-specific models built on Siamese (P2B) or motion-centric (M²Track) paradigms, by proposing the first cross-category unified tracker. Architecturally, it draws on the vector-attention mechanism from Point Transformer (Zhao et al., 2021) and the dynamic grouping ideas from 3D detection (DBQ-SSD, CAGroup3D), integrating them into a novel AdaFormer block with deformable group regression. Conceptually, it bridges the gap between 2D generic object tracking (GOT), which already achieves category-agnostic tracking via Siamese matching, and 3D SOT, where geometric distortion from normalization and point cloud sparsity have prevented a similar unification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single shared-parameter model replaces four (or more) category-specific networks, reducing deployment complexity while matching or exceeding per-category performance. The finding that the Siamese paradigm benefits substantially more (~12%) than the motion-centric paradigm (~3%) from unification suggests that appearance-based tracking is more sensitive to geometric variability, a useful insight for future tracker design. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to datasets with more diverse categories (e.g., animals, drones), whether the reliance on known template size/shape limits applicability to truly open-vocabulary tracking, and whether the deformable group concept transfers to other point cloud tasks beyond tracking.
