## Problem

Training generalist robot agents requires scaling simultaneously along three axes—high-dimensional visual inputs, long temporal horizons, and generalization to novel tasks—but existing methods handle at most one or two of these. Language-conditioned diffusion planners like Diffuser fail when applied directly to pixel or VAE latent spaces because the generative objective has no notion of task relevance, producing physically infeasible trajectories, and the computational cost of high-dimensional diffusion is prohibitive. The paper asks whether a hierarchical diffusion architecture can efficiently and accurately scale along all three axes for language-conditioned control.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB observations from two camera views (static and gripper) in the CALVIN benchmark, which are encoded by a frozen HULC visual encoder into 32-dimensional latent vectors, alongside a natural language instruction encoded by a frozen 11B-parameter T5-XXL model into 4096-dimensional embeddings. The high-level diffusion policy outputs a subsampled sequence of 32-dimensional latent goal states (a 'latent plan') at a configurable temporal stride c; the frozen low-level policy then maps each goal state plus the current observation to a discrete robot action, executing c steps before the high-level policy resamples a new plan. Inference uses DDIM with 20 diffusion steps, achieving 3.3×–15× faster rollout than comparable diffusion baselines.

## Method

LCD is a two-level hierarchical policy where a temporal U-Net diffusion model (high-level policy) generates coherent multi-step latent goal plans in the frozen encoder space of a goal-conditioned low-level policy (HULC), conditioned on language via cross-attention. The key architectural insight is that the LLP's visual encoder—trained end-to-end via hindsight relabeling to minimize action reconstruction error—provides a task-aware, control-optimized 32-dimensional representation that is far more suitable for diffusion than raw pixels or generic VAE latents, as confirmed by ablations showing Diffuser fails in both 1D and 2D VAE spaces. A theoretical suboptimality bound (Proposition 3.1) shows that under Lipschitz transition dynamics, the performance gap from operating in the encoder's latent space is bounded by the LLP's reconstruction error ε, and since the LLP is trained to minimize exactly this error, the abstraction is near-optimal. Training proceeds in two stages: first the LLP is trained and frozen, then the HLP is trained on cached latent plans (enabling 100–200× memory savings) using the standard ε-prediction diffusion objective with L1 loss, with temporal abstraction via subsampling at stride c to flexibly redistribute computational load between levels.

## Results

On the CALVIN multi-task long-horizon control benchmark (34 tasks), LCD achieves 88.7±1.5% success rate on single-task (horizon 1) and an average horizon length of 2.88±0.19 tasks completed, outperforming the prior best method SPIL (84.6%, 2.67) by 4.1% and 0.21 respectively. LCD also surpasses the next-best diffusion model (Diffuser-1D) by 41.4% on horizon-1 tasks and by 2.14 in average horizon length. Inference is 3.3×–15× faster and training is 1.5×–3.7× faster than comparable diffusion-based baselines (Diffuser-1D/2D) thanks to DDIM, temporal abstraction, and a 32-dimensional latent space.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LCD outperforms all five baseline families on every horizon of the CALVIN benchmark. Against the strongest non-diffusion baseline SPIL (Zhou et al., 2023), LCD gains +4.1% on horizon-1 (88.7 vs. 84.6) and +0.21 in average horizon length (2.88 vs. 2.67). Against HULC (Mees et al., 2022), which shares the same low-level policy architecture, LCD improves by +6.1% on horizon-1 and +0.31 in average horizon length, confirming that the diffusion high-level policy adds value beyond the shared LLP. Against the most directly comparable diffusion baselines, Diffuser-1D (Janner et al., 2022, VAE latent) and Diffuser-2D (Stable Diffusion latent), LCD achieves 41.4% and 51.3% higher success on horizon-1 respectively, and completes 2.14 and 2.40 more tasks on average. In terms of efficiency, LCD's inference time of 0.336 s is 3.3× faster than Diffuser-1D (1.11 s) and 15× faster than Diffuser-2D (5.02 s), while training is 1.6× and 3.7× faster respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Diffuser (Janner et al., 2022) by moving diffusion-based planning from high-dimensional pixel/VAE latent spaces into a low-dimensional, task-aware latent space derived from a frozen goal-conditioned policy encoder (HULC). The theoretical grounding draws on Nachum et al. (2019)'s suboptimality framework for hierarchical RL, adapted here to justify diffusion in the LLP encoder space under Lipschitz dynamics. Unlike prior language-conditioned control methods (MCIL, HULC, SPIL) that rely on discrete skill decomposition or CVAE latent plans, LCD uses a continuous diffusion model as the high-level planner, enabling flexible long-horizon generation without predefined skills, while DDIM and temporal subsampling address the computational bottleneck that made prior diffusion-for-control approaches impractical at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the key bottleneck for diffusion-based control is not the diffusion architecture itself but the choice of representation: task-aware encoders from goal-conditioned policies outperform general-purpose VAEs (both from-scratch and internet-scale) for planning. This insight, combined with the 3.3×–15× inference speedup, makes diffusion-based hierarchical planning practically viable for robotics research groups without massive compute. Open questions remain: the theory assumes optimal expert data (violated in CALVIN), the suboptimality bound does not account for the diffusion model's own approximation error, and whether the approach extends to multi-level hierarchies or truly open-ended task generalization beyond the CALVIN/CLEVR-Robot settings is untested.
