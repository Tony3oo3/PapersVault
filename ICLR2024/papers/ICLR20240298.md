## Problem

ELECTRA-style pre-training jointly trains an auxiliary (generator) model with the main (discriminator) model to create a natural learning curriculum for replaced token detection, but the auxiliary model is discarded after training, wasting approximately 67% of computation and 30% of memory. This overhead scales with model size and is compounded by the difficulty of balancing the two models' optimizations, often requiring multiple hyper-parameter search rounds. The paper asks whether the curriculum effect of joint training can be replicated without incurring the auxiliary model's training cost.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text sequences (512 tokens, 128K BPE vocabulary, from Wikipedia and BookCorpus) and a frozen, pre-trained auxiliary language model (e.g., a 4-layer or 6-layer MLM model) that is used only for inference to generate replacement token distributions. The output is a pre-trained transformer encoder (BERT-base: 12 layers, 768 hidden; or BERT-large: 24 layers, 1024 hidden) trained solely on the replaced token detection objective, ready for single-task fine-tuning on GLUE benchmarks. A key practical detail is that the auxiliary model's output distribution is modified at inference time via temperature scaling, so no retraining of the auxiliary model is ever required.

## Method

Fast-ELECTRA replaces the jointly-trained auxiliary model with a frozen, pre-trained language model and constructs the learning curriculum through temperature scaling of its output distribution. Specifically, replacement tokens are sampled from Softmax(log p_aux(·|i, x_masked)/T), where the temperature T follows an exponential decay schedule T = 1 + (T₀−1)·exp(−u/τ) over training progress u, starting at T₀=2 and decaying toward 1. This annealing mimics the natural curriculum of joint training (easy-to-hard replacements) without any gradient flow through the auxiliary model, reducing its computation cost to 1/3 (inference only) and memory cost to ~1/30 (no optimizer states or activations). The training objective simplifies to min_θ L_RTD(θ), eliminating the loss-weight hyper-parameter λ that balances the two models. Optionally, offline preprocessing can pre-generate all corrupted sequences per epoch, driving auxiliary computation and memory to zero during training.

## Results

In the Base setting, Fast-ELECTRA achieves an average GLUE score of 88.7, with 89.4/88.8 MNLI (m/mm) accuracy, 71.4 CoLA Matthews correlation, and 91.6 STS-B Spearman correlation, matching or slightly exceeding METRO (88.6) and AMOS (88.6). In the Large setting, Fast-ELECTRA reaches 89.3 average GLUE score with 90.1/90.2 MNLI and 72.1 CoLA, edging out METRO ReImp (89.2). In terms of efficiency, Fast-ELECTRA reduces auxiliary model computation by 67% and overall computation by 20–25%, while cutting auxiliary model memory by 97% and overall memory by 10–20%, saving over 50 hours of wall-clock training time out of 200 hours total.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fast-ELECTRA outperforms the original ELECTRA (85.5 avg) by 3.2 points in the Base setting and surpasses all MLM-based baselines (BERT 83.1, RoBERTa 83.3) by 5+ points. Against the strongest ELECTRA-style competitors, Fast-ELECTRA edges out METRO (88.6) and AMOS (88.6) by 0.1 in the Base setting, with the largest margin on CoLA (71.4 vs. 70.6/70.7). In the Large setting, Fast-ELECTRA (89.3) beats METRO ReImp (89.2) by 0.1 on average and by 2.4 points on CoLA (72.1 vs. 69.7), while also surpassing RoBERTa (87.8) and XLNet (87.0) by 1.5 and 2.3 points respectively. The method also demonstrates superior training efficiency: it achieves the same downstream performance as original ELECTRA with over 50 hours less wall-clock time, and is more robust to hyper-parameter choices (auxiliary model depth, curriculum schedule) than the jointly-trained ELECTRA design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends ELECTRA (Clark et al., 2020), which introduced replaced token detection (RTD) with a jointly-trained auxiliary model to generate token replacements. The key departure is replacing the jointly-trained auxiliary model with a fixed, pre-trained model whose output distribution is smoothed via temperature scaling on an exponential decay schedule, inspired by simulated annealing. The most direct comparison is METRO (Bajaj et al., 2022), the most comprehensive recent ELECTRA-style method, which the authors re-implement as a baseline. The paper also situates itself within the broader family of ELECTRA variants (COCO-LM, AMOS, DeBERTaV3) that modify the RTD objective or auxiliary model design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for practitioners running ELECTRA-style pre-training at scale: eliminating the jointly-trained auxiliary model reduces overall computation by 20–25% and memory by 10–20%, while also removing the delicate hyper-parameter balancing between auxiliary and main model optimization. The offline preprocessing option (zero auxiliary cost during training) is particularly attractive for large-scale and multi-round pre-training. Limitations include evaluation only at BERT-base and BERT-large scale, and the auxiliary model still requires a separate pre-training pass (though it can be reused). Open questions include whether the fixed-auxiliary approach remains effective when scaling to tens of billions of parameters, and whether the temperature schedule can be further simplified or made adaptive.
