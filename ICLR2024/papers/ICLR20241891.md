## Problem

Forward-only learning algorithms such as PEPITA and Forward-Forward replace backpropagation's backward pass with a second forward pass, but they lack an analytical explanation for why they work, exhibit a performance gap relative to backpropagation, and have only been validated on very shallow networks. Additionally, no formal connection had been established between these forward-only rules and the better-understood Feedback Alignment framework, leaving their learning dynamics and alignment mechanisms uncharacterized.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard image inputs (28×28 grayscale MNIST, 32×32 RGB CIFAR-10/100) and produces class labels (10 or 100 classes). Unusually, each training step requires two forward passes: a clean pass on the original input and a modulated pass where the input is perturbed by a fixed random projection of the output error (PEPITA) or replaced by a corrupted hybrid image (FF). For the theoretical analysis, inputs are D-dimensional standard Gaussian vectors with scalar regression targets generated by a fixed teacher network.

## Method

The key insight is that PEPITA's weight update, obtained via a Taylor expansion of the small error perturbation, reduces to an Adaptive Feedback Alignment (AFA) rule where the feedback matrix is modulated by the forward weights (W₁F rather than a fixed F). The authors derive closed-form ODEs for the learning dynamics in the infinite-dimensional teacher-student limit, revealing an alignment phase where the student weights align with the evolving AF matrix before error decreases. Empirically, they generalize Weight Mirroring to PEPITA by factorizing the feedback matrix into layer-wise components, and show this significantly improves alignment, accuracy, and convergence. They further reformulate PEPITA as a sum of Hebbian and anti-Hebbian terms (PEPITA-Hebbian), demonstrating it is analytically equivalent to Forward-Forward with top-down feedback when the goodness measure is the negative sum of squared activities, thereby unifying the two forward-only frameworks.

## Results

On a 1-hidden-layer fully connected network, the best PEPITA variant (with activation normalization and weight mirroring) achieves 98.42% test accuracy on MNIST, 53.80% on CIFAR-10, and 24.20% on CIFAR-100. PEPITA with weight mirroring alone reaches 27.07% on CIFAR-100, a >2% improvement over standard PEPITA (24.69%) and a 4.3% gain over FA (22.75%). The paper further demonstrates that PEPITA can be extended to train networks with up to 5 hidden layers when combined with activation normalization, a significant improvement over the original 1-hidden-layer limitation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Backpropagation (BP), PEPITA with its best improvements (Norm+WM) trails by 0.3% on MNIST (98.42 vs 98.72), 3.8% on CIFAR-10 (53.80 vs 57.60), and 5.2% on CIFAR-100 (24.20 vs 29.36), confirming a persistent performance gap. Against Feedback Alignment (FA), PEPITA+Norm+WM nearly matches on MNIST (98.42 vs 98.48) and beats FA on CIFAR-100 (24.20 vs 22.75), but still lags on CIFAR-10 (53.80 vs 56.76); PEPITA+WM without normalization achieves an even larger CIFAR-100 margin (27.07 vs 22.75). Against DRTP, PEPITA dominates across all three datasets, with the largest gap on CIFAR-100 where PEPITA+WM reaches 27.07% versus DRTP's 16.99% (a >10% absolute improvement). On convergence rate (slowness metric), PEPITA+WM+pre-mirroring achieves the fastest convergence among all forward-only methods on MNIST (0.040) and CIFAR-100 (1.999), outperforming FA (0.081 and 4.946 respectively), though BP remains fastest overall.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three families of neuro-inspired learning rules—Forward-Forward (Hinton, 2022), PEPITA (Dellaferrera & Kreiman, 2022), and Feedback Alignment (Lillicrap et al., 2016)—by showing that PEPITA is well-approximated by an 'adaptive feedback alignment' (AFA) rule, and that PEPITA's Hebbian reformulation is mathematically equivalent to FF's weight update under a squared-activity goodness measure. The theoretical analysis builds on the statistical-mechanics framework for online learning in teacher-student setups (Saad & Solla, Biehl & Schwarze, Goldt et al.), extended here to track the time-evolution of the adaptive feedback matrix. The Weight Mirroring extension (Akrout et al., 2019) is generalized to handle the dimensional mismatch between feedback and feedforward weights in PEPITA, a setting not covered by the original WM algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first analytical characterization of why forward-only algorithms work, revealing that the key mechanism is alignment between forward weights and (adaptive) feedback connections—a shared principle across FF, PEPITA, and FA. This unification suggests that future improvements to any one of these algorithms may transfer to the others. However, significant gaps remain: PEPITA+WM still trails BP by ~4% on CIFAR-10, the time-local Hebbian variant (PEPITA-TL) drops to 92.78% on MNIST, and the WM factorization of the feedback matrix lacks a clear biological counterpart. The authors identify theoretical analysis of deep-network dynamics and more biologically plausible alternatives to the feedback factorization as key open directions.
