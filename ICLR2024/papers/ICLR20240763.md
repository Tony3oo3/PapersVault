## Problem

Despite the empirical success of contrastive learning, the theoretical connection between the standard InfoNCE loss and spectral clustering remained unproven. Prior work (HaoChen et al., 2021) required a modified spectral contrastive loss and an additional linear transformation on embeddings, leaving the practical SimCLR/CLIP pipeline without a clean spectral characterization. This paper closes that gap by proving the standard InfoNCE loss itself performs spectral clustering on the augmentation-defined similarity graph, with no loss modification or post-hoc transformation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a collection of n objects (32×32 RGB images for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet, or image-text pairs for CLIP analysis) and a similarity graph π implicitly defined by the data augmentation sampling process (e.g., 1/9 probability per augmentation type in SimCLR). It produces embedding vectors on the unit sphere in Z, where the Gram matrix under a chosen kernel approximates π. For downstream evaluation, a frozen encoder feeds a linear probe that outputs class labels. The similarity graph is never explicitly constructed; it is encoded entirely in the stochastic pairing of positive samples during training.

## Method

The core proof proceeds in two steps: (1) the InfoNCE loss is shown to equal the cross-entropy between two Markov random fields—one on the similarity graph π and one on the embedding kernel matrix K_Z—when subgraphs are constrained to unit out-degree (matching the single-positive-sample structure of InfoNCE); (2) when the kernel is Gaussian, minimizing this cross-entropy reduces to tr(Z^T L(π) Z) + log R(Z), which is spectral clustering on π with a repulsion regularizer. The framework extends to CLIP by treating image-text pairs as a bipartite pair graph, yielding generalized spectral clustering. Motivated by the maximum entropy principle, the authors derive that exponential kernels are the natural family for InfoNCE-type losses and propose Kernel-InfoNCE, which replaces the Gaussian kernel with mixtures of Gaussian and Laplacian kernels (via weighted sum or concatenation), achieving consistent gains over SimCLR on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and TinyImageNet.

## Results

Using linear evaluation (top-1 accuracy after freezing the encoder and training a linear probe), the proposed Simple Sum Kernel achieves 91.72% on CIFAR-10, 68.62% on CIFAR-100, and 39.38% on TinyImageNet at 400 epochs, outperforming the SimCLR baseline (90.60%, 66.29%, 37.83%) by 1.12, 2.33, and 1.55 percentage points respectively. At 200 epochs, the Simple Sum Kernel reaches 89.82%, 66.67%, and 36.61%, again surpassing SimCLR (88.11%, 62.57%, 34.00%) across all three datasets. The Concatenation Sum Kernel and Laplacian Kernel also consistently outperform SimCLR, with the Simple Sum Kernel achieving the best average performance overall.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is SimCLR (Chen et al., 2020), reproduced with ResNet-50 backbone and identical augmentation/training protocol. All four Kernel-InfoNCE variants (Laplacian, γ=0.5 Exponential, Simple Sum, and Concatenation Sum) outperform SimCLR on every dataset at both 200 and 400 epochs. The Simple Sum Kernel (a weighted average of Gaussian and Laplacian kernels) delivers the largest gains: +1.71 pp on CIFAR-10, +4.10 pp on CIFAR-100, and +2.61 pp on TinyImageNet at 200 epochs. The paper also mentions MoCo (He et al., 2019) as an additional baseline in supplementary experiments, though the main comparison in Table 1 is against SimCLR. Notably, the improvements are most pronounced on CIFAR-100 (the hardest dataset), where the Simple Sum Kernel gains over 4 percentage points, suggesting the mixture-of-kernels approach is particularly effective when class discrimination is more challenging.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the MRF-based dimension reduction framework of Van Assel et al. (2022) and the augmentation graph analysis of HaoChen et al. (2021), but makes a critical conceptual leap: while HaoChen et al. required a modified spectral contrastive loss to establish the spectral clustering equivalence, this paper proves that the standard InfoNCE loss used in SimCLR already performs spectral clustering on the similarity graph, without any loss modification or post-hoc linear transformation. The analysis is then extended to CLIP's multi-modal setting via a bipartite pair graph, and the maximum entropy principle is invoked to motivate a new Kernel-InfoNCE loss using mixtures of exponential kernels (Gaussian + Laplacian) as a drop-in replacement for the standard Gaussian kernel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exact equivalence between InfoNCE and spectral clustering provides the first clean theoretical explanation for why contrastive learning produces class-separated embeddings, directly linking the augmentation process to the graph structure being clustered. However, the practical gains from Kernel-InfoNCE are modest (roughly 1–2% over SimCLR on CIFAR-10/100 and TinyImageNet), and the theoretical framework assumes a finite, explicitly defined similarity graph that is rarely available in practice. Open questions include whether the spectral clustering interpretation extends to other contrastive objectives (e.g., NT-Xent with in-batch negatives as hard negatives), whether the kernel mixture approach generalizes to larger-scale datasets like ImageNet, and whether the equivalence holds under the asymmetric sampling schemes used in practice.
