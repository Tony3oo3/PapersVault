## Problem

Denoising diffusion probabilistic models require a full forward pass through the entire U-Net at every denoising step, incurring hundreds to thousands of redundant computations per sample. The paper asks whether all diffusion steps truly demand equal computational resources, hypothesizing that less critical steps could be handled by smaller networks without quality loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240517/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes standard Gaussian noise (and optionally a class label for conditional generation) and iteratively denoises it over T steps to produce a single RGB image at resolutions ranging from 32×32 (CIFAR-10) to 128×128 (CelebA-HQ). The unusual aspect is that the network width changes dynamically across steps: each step selects a different sub-network width from a slimmable supernet, so the same input tensor is processed by networks of varying channel counts (2/8 to 8/8 of the full ADM width) depending on the step index.

## Method

DDSM generalizes the standard DDPM reverse process by replacing the fixed network θ with a step-dependent function F(t) that returns a different sub-network at each denoising step. The backbone is a slimmable U-Net (following ADM architecture) where all convolutions are replaced with slimmable convolutions supporting 7 discrete widths; it is trained by simultaneously optimizing the largest, smallest, and a randomly sampled sub-network per iteration, so all intermediate widths inherit good performance without separate training. An NSGA-II evolutionary search then assigns the optimal width to each of the T steps, minimizing a weighted combination of FID and total FLOPs. At inference, the searched strategy selects a contiguous channel slice from the supernet at each step, which is GPU-friendly and requires no retraining. The approach is orthogonal to step-skipping (DDIM, EDM) and latent-space compression (LDM), and can be stacked with them for additional speedups.

## Results

DDSM achieves substantial FLOPs reductions while matching or improving FID across all five datasets: 49% saving on CIFAR-10 (FID 3.552 vs. 3.713), 61% on CelebA-HQ (FID 6.039 vs. 6.755), 59% on LSUN-bedroom (FID 5.289 vs. 5.027), 71% on AFHQ (FID 6.249 vs. 7.244), and 76% on ImageNet (FID 24.714 vs. 27.000). On CIFAR-10, DDSM reduces average GFLOPs from 12.14 to 6.20 and GPU latency from 0.93 s to 0.59 s per image. On ImageNet, the reduction is from 82.06 to 19.40 GFLOPs (4.75 s → 3.22 s latency), demonstrating that the step-aware strategy scales to large-scale generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary ADM baseline, DDSM wins on both FID and FLOPs across all five datasets, with the largest gains on ImageNet (76% FLOPs reduction, FID 24.71 vs. 27.00) and AFHQ (71% reduction, FID 6.25 vs. 7.24). Compared to Diff-pruning (Fang et al., 2023) on CelebA 64×64 with 100 steps, DDSM achieves a lower FID (6.04 vs. 6.24) and substantially fewer GFLOPs (19.6 vs. 26.6), indicating that step-aware channel slicing is more efficient than Taylor-expansion-based whole-network pruning. When combined with DDIM, DDSM further reduces total TFLOPs by 48–62% at T=10–1000 steps while also improving FID (e.g., 7.13 vs. 7.91 at T=100). Similarly, DDSM+LDM on CelebA cuts GFLOPs from 5.52 to 2.22 (60% saving) with a marginal FID improvement (10.76 vs. 10.91). Finally, DDSM+EDM on CIFAR-10 achieves 45–56% total TFLOPs savings over EDM alone at 50 and 100 steps, confirming orthogonality with modern samplers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DDPM reverse-diffusion formulation (Ho et al., 2020) by replacing the single shared network with a step-dependent functional F(t) that selects different-sized sub-networks per denoising step, and adapts the slimmable network training paradigm (Yu & Huang, 2019) from image recognition to diffusion denoising. Unlike prior diffusion acceleration work that reduces the number of steps (DDIM, DPM-Solver) or the input dimensionality (latent diffusion), DDSM attacks the problem from the model-width axis, making it orthogonal and composable with those approaches. The evolutionary search (NSGA-II) for step-aware strategies builds on the NAS literature but is applied here to a per-step width assignment problem rather than a single architecture selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 49–76% FLOPs reduction without quality loss (and sometimes with slight FID improvement) makes diffusion inference substantially more practical for deployment, and the plug-and-play compatibility with DDIM, latent diffusion, and EDM broadens its applicability. The dataset-dependent step-importance patterns (e.g., CIFAR-10 favors small models early while CelebA favors large models early) are an interesting empirical observation, but the paper explicitly acknowledges the lack of theoretical explanation for why certain steps demand more capacity. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to transformer-based diffusion backbones, whether the 2–3× training overhead is acceptable at scale, and whether a principled (non-search-based) method could predict step importance from dataset properties.
