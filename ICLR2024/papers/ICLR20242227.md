## Problem

Personalized federated learning (PFL) has made progress on label distribution heterogeneity, but feature heterogeneity—where clients' data differ in appearance, style, or acquisition conditions—remains largely unaddressed. The core open question is how to determine the optimal mixing ratio between local and global updates so that each client's personalized model best fits its own feature distribution without being distracted by heterogeneous features from other clients.

## Input / Output

Each client provides local image data (28×28 digits, 256×256 natural/medical images, or 96×96 histology patches) and receives the global model from the server each communication round. Beyond the standard model parameters, the method additionally extracts penultimate-layer feature vectors h(x_i) from both the local model and the global model to construct m×m feature matrices (e.g., 256×256 or 512×512), whose traces determine the per-client mixing ratio. The final output is a personalized model per client that produces class labels (classification) or pixel-wise segmentation masks (retinal fundus images).

## Method

LG-Mix updates each client's model as w_c(t+1) = w_c(t) + λ_c(t)·Δw_c(t) + (1−λ_c(t))·Δu(t), where the mixing ratio λ_c(t) is set by the relative NTK-based convergence rates of local versus global updates. The key theoretical result (Proposition 1) shows that the trace of the Gram/NTK matrix governs the gradient-descent convergence rate, so a higher trace implies faster convergence and thus greater importance for that update. To avoid computing the prohibitively large n×n NTK matrix, the authors approximate tr(H) by the trace of an m×m feature matrix Σ = Σ_i h(x_i)h(x_i)^T built from penultimate-layer features, reducing cost from O(n²) to O(nm). The ratio is then λ_c(t) = tr(Σ_c(t)) / (tr(Σ_c(t)) + tr(Σ(t))), optionally smoothed over history rounds for stability. The approach is architecture-agnostic (applied to CNNs, ResNet-18, DenseNet-121, and U-Net) and is accompanied by a convergence proof in the over-parameterized regime showing linear error decay governed by the smallest eigenvalues of the local and global Gram matrices.

## Results

LG-Mix achieves the best average accuracy across all five datasets. On Digits-5, it reaches 97.10% (vs. 95.93% for FedAlt, the second-best); on Office-Caltech10, 79.31% (vs. 75.13% for FedBN); on DomainNet, 62.74% (vs. 57.73% for FedBN, the largest gap of 5.01%); on Camelyon17, 98.75% (vs. 97.91% for FedAlt). On the Retinal segmentation task, LG-Mix achieves 87.75% Dice (vs. 83.82% for PerFedAvg) and 4.15 HD (vs. 14.12 for L2SGD), with a 15.05% Dice improvement on the most heterogeneous client (E).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LG-Mix outperforms all nine baselines on all five datasets. The closest competitors are FedAlt (best on Digits-5 at 95.93% and Camelyon17 at 97.91%) and FedBN (best on Office-Caltech10 at 75.13% and DomainNet at 57.73%). LG-Mix improves over FedAlt by 1.17% on Digits-5 and 0.84% on Camelyon17, and over FedBN by 4.18% on Office-Caltech10 and 5.01% on DomainNet (the largest margin in the paper). On the Retinal segmentation task, LG-Mix surpasses PerFedAvg (the second-best) by 3.93% Dice and reduces HD from 14.12 (L2SGD) to 4.15, with a particularly dramatic 15.05% Dice gain on client E which uses a unique dual-imaging setting. Methods that only personalize the output layer (FedBABU, FedRep) or use knowledge distillation (FedHKD) show the weakest performance, confirming that feature heterogeneity requires personalization across multiple layers rather than a single head.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the local-global model mixing paradigm introduced by APFL (Deng et al., 2020) and L2SGD (Hanzely et al., 2020) by replacing their fixed or heuristically-learned mixing ratios with an adaptive, data-driven criterion. The key theoretical foundation comes from NTK convergence analysis (Jacot et al., 2018; Huang et al., 2021), where the authors show that the trace of the Gram matrix governs the convergence rate of gradient descent, and repurpose this as a signal for weighting local versus global updates. Unlike prior PFL methods that target label distribution shift or personalize specific architectural components (BN layers in FedBN, output heads in FedBABU/FedRep), LG-Mix explicitly addresses feature heterogeneity through a data-dependent mixing mechanism that operates across all layers without architectural modifications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Feature heterogeneity in PFL—where clients differ in input appearance rather than label distribution—remains under-explored, and this paper provides both a principled theoretical framework (NTK-based convergence rate) and a computationally efficient approximation (feature matrix trace) to address it. The consistent outperformance across five diverse datasets (natural images, medical histology, retinal segmentation) with up to 5% accuracy gains and 15% Dice improvement on an outlier client demonstrates practical relevance, particularly in multi-institutional medical imaging. Limitations include the over-parameterized theoretical regime, reliance on relatively small CNN backbones (no transformer experiments), and the need to maintain and update feature matrices during training, which adds overhead. Open questions include whether the NTK-based criterion generalizes to class distributional shift, larger architectures, and non-iid label settings simultaneously.
