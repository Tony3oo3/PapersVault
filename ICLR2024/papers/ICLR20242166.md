## Problem

Plug-and-Play and deep unrolling methods replace proximal operators with generic denoisers, but these denoisers are not guaranteed to be proximal operators of any function, leaving the implicit prior uncharacterized and convergence of iterative schemes unprovable. This paper asks: can we design networks that are provably exact proximal operators of a learned (potentially nonconvex) regularizer, and can we train them to recover the correct log-prior of the data distribution?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242166/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes noisy measurements y = A(x) + v with a known linear operator A and produces a restored signal x via PnP-ADMM or PnP-PGD iterations. Uniquely, it also outputs an explicit log-prior function R_θ evaluable at arbitrary points (by inverting the LPN), providing direct interpretability of the learned regularizer. Training requires only i.i.d. samples from the data distribution (no paired clean/noisy data), with Gaussian noise added synthetically at a chosen level σ that scales the learned regularizer.

## Method

The LPN architecture is defined as the gradient of an input convex neural network (ICNN) with softplus activations and non-negative weights, which by the Gribonval–Nikolova characterization guarantees the network is an exact proximal operator of some (potentially nonconvex) function R_θ. Training uses a novel proximal matching loss m_γ(‖f_θ(y)−x‖) that approximates the Dirac function; Theorem 3.2 proves that minimizing this loss as γ→0 recovers the MAP denoiser, i.e., the proximal of the log-prior, from i.i.d. samples alone. The regularizer R_θ is recovered at arbitrary points by adding a quadratic term to the ICNN (making the gradient map bijective) and solving a strongly convex inversion problem. Because the LPN is a proximal operator by construction, PnP-ADMM with LPN converges to a critical point of the variational objective under only minimal, verifiable assumptions (softplus activations, 0<α<1, ρ>‖AᵀA‖), without requiring nonexpansivity or Lipschitz constraints on the denoiser.

## Results

On CelebA deblurring, LPN achieves 33.0±2.9 PSNR / 0.92±0.03 SSIM at σ_blur=1, σ_noise=0.02, matching the best PnP baseline (PnP-GS/Prox-DRUNet) while clearly outperforming PnP-BM3D (31.0 PSNR) and PnP-DnCNN (32.3 PSNR). On Mayo-CT compressed sensing at 1/16 compression rate, LPN achieves 38.03 PSNR / 0.919 SSIM, dramatically outperforming the adversarial regularizer AR (29.71 PSNR / 0.712 SSIM) by over 8 dB. For sparse-view CT reconstruction, LPN reaches 34.14 PSNR / 0.891 SSIM, beating AR (33.48 / 0.890) and approaching the task-specific supervised UAR (34.76 / 0.897) without access to the forward operator.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CelebA deblurring (Table 1), LPN ties or slightly trails PnP-GS (Prox-DRUNet, Hurault et al. 2022) by ≤0.2 dB PSNR across all four blur/noise settings, while clearly beating PnP-BM3D (Venkatakrishnan et al. 2013) by 2–5 dB and PnP-DnCNN (Zhang et al. 2017) by 1–3 dB. On Mayo-CT sparse-view CT reconstruction, LPN outperforms the operator-agnostic adversarial regularizer AR (Lunz et al. 2018) by 0.66 dB PSNR, and approaches the task-specific supervised UAR (Mukherjee et al. 2021) within 0.62 dB without ever seeing the forward operator during training. The most striking margin appears in compressed sensing: at 1/16 compression, LPN surpasses AR by 8.3 dB PSNR (38.03 vs 29.71) and 0.207 SSIM, and at 1/4 compression it leads AR by 6.1 dB (44.05 vs 37.94). FBP (Willemink & Noël 2019) serves only as a weak baseline (21.29 PSNR) and is not competitive. Overall, LPN matches or beats all unsupervised baselines across every task, with the largest gains in high-compression-rate settings where the learned nonconvex prior provides the strongest regularization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three threads: the Plug-and-Play (PnP) framework that replaces proximal steps with learned denoisers (Venkatakrishnan et al., 2013), Input Convex Neural Networks that parameterize convex functions (Amos et al., 2017), and the characterization of continuous proximal operators as gradients of convex functions (Gribonval & Nikolova, 2020). It departs from gradient-step denoisers (Cohen et al., 2021a; Hurault et al., 2022b), which only approximately enforce proximal structure via Lipschitz regularization, by guaranteeing exact proximal operators by construction. The proximal matching loss is a novel training objective that provably recovers the MAP denoiser (equivalently, the proximal of the log-prior) from i.i.d. samples, in contrast to standard L2/L1 losses that recover the posterior mean/median.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to explicitly evaluate the learned regularizer R_θ at arbitrary points provides a level of interpretability absent from all prior PnP or unrolling methods, opening the door to uncertainty quantification, sampling, and robustness analysis. The convergence guarantee for PnP-ADMM with LPNs requires no assumptions on the denoiser beyond its construction (softplus activations, 0<α<1), which is a meaningful simplification over prior PnP convergence results that demand contractivity or nonexpansivity. Open questions include whether the proximal matching loss scales efficiently to very high-dimensional data, how the learned prior behaves under distribution shift, and whether the framework extends naturally to equivariant or diffusion-based settings.
