## Problem

Diffusion-based sampling methods such as PIS and DDS require complete trajectories to compute their KL-divergence training objective, with the only learning signal arriving at the terminal step. This creates severe credit assignment problems and high gradient variance, especially for the long sequential chains that diffusion models require. The paper asks whether the training process can be decomposed into shorter partial trajectory segments with intermediate learning signals to stabilize and accelerate convergence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241843/cropped_page5_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unnormalized density function μ(x) over a D-dimensional continuous space (only zeroth-order or first-order gradient information is required) and produces samples from the normalized target π(x) = μ(x)/Z via a discrete-time Gaussian forward process with N=100 steps. It additionally yields a valid lower-bound estimate of log Z through importance-weighted trajectory scoring. The forward policy is a unimodal Gaussian transition with a neural-network drift, and the backward policy is fixed (derived from the reference process), so only the drift and a scalar flow function are learned.

## Method

DGFS introduces a neural-network 'flow function' F_n(x_n; θ) that amortizes the intractable intermediate marginal densities of the target process, enabling a subtrajectory balance loss (inherited from GFlowNet theory) that regresses the product of F_m and forward transitions to the product of F_n and backward transitions over any partial segment x_{m:n}. This allows parameter updates from incomplete trajectories, reducing gradient variance. Additionally, a forward-looking parameterization of the log flow function—log F_n = (1−n/N) log p_n^ref + (n/N) log μ + NN(x_n; θ)—injects intermediate learning signals at every step rather than only at the terminal state. The final training objective is a length-weighted average of subtrajectory balance losses over all (m,n) pairs, and the drift is parameterized as f(·,n)/σ = NN₁(·,n) + NN₂(n)·∇log μ(·), combining a learned component with a Langevin-style gradient term.

## Results

DGFS is evaluated on five continuous sampling benchmarks (MoG, Funnel, Manywell, VAE, Cox) using absolute estimation bias of the log normalizing factor (lower is better). DGFS achieves the best performance among all diffusion-based samplers, with biases of 0.019 (MoG), 0.274 (Funnel), 0.904 (Manywell), 0.180 (VAE), and 8.974 (Cox). It also outperforms MCMC (SMC), variational inference (VI-NF), and normalizing-flow-based methods (CRAFT) across all five tasks. The only method that beats DGFS on most tasks is FAB with a replay buffer, which benefits from annealed importance sampling and larger networks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGFS outperforms the two closest diffusion-based baselines—PIS and DDS—on every task where both have results: e.g., on VAE, DGFS achieves 0.180 bias versus 2.049 (PIS) and 1.740 (DDS), and on Cox, DGFS reaches 8.974 versus 11.28 (PIS) while DDS fails entirely. Against the MCMC baseline SMC, DGFS is substantially better on all five tasks (e.g., 0.904 vs. 22.36 on Manywell; 8.974 vs. 99.61 on Cox). DGFS also beats the normalizing-flow-based CRAFT on all tasks (e.g., 0.180 vs. 0.521 on VAE) and VI-NF on all tasks. The only method that consistently outperforms DGFS is FAB with a replay buffer (0.003 on MoG, 0.032 on Manywell, 0.19 on Cox), which the authors attribute to its use of annealed importance sampling, larger networks, and buffer-based training. The paper also shows that DGFS's gradient variance is significantly lower than PIS's (Figure 2), and that DGFS captures all modes in the Manywell and MoG tasks where PIS misses modes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: diffusion-based sampling via stochastic optimal control (PIS, DDS) and the GFlowNet framework for amortized inference. While Lahlou et al. (2023) established the theoretical connection between continuous-space GFlowNets and diffusion processes, and Madan et al. (2022) introduced subtrajectory balance for discrete GFlowNets, this work is the first to combine both ideas into a practical algorithm (DGFS) that trains diffusion samplers using partial trajectories and intermediate forward-looking signals (from Pan et al., 2023a). The key departure from PIS/DDS is replacing the trajectory-level KL objective—which provides learning signal only at the terminal step—with a subtrajectory balance loss that enables credit assignment at every intermediate step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The credit assignment bottleneck in diffusion-based sampling is a practical limitation that has constrained the field; DGFS's ability to train from partial trajectories and receive intermediate signals addresses this directly, yielding consistently lower gradient variance and better partition function estimation across diverse benchmarks. The off-policy exploration capability (enabled by the GFlowNet formulation rather than KL) opens a path toward better mode coverage in high-dimensional multimodal targets, though this is only preliminarily demonstrated. Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly high-dimensional scientific problems (e.g., protein conformation), how to design more sophisticated intermediate signals beyond the current linear interpolation, and whether combining DGFS with prioritized replay buffers can further reduce the cost of querying the target density.
