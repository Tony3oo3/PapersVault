## Problem

OT-based generative models span two families—OT Loss (WGAN) and OT Map (OTM, UOTM)—that share adversarial training objectives but have never been analyzed within a single framework, leaving the role of each component (cost function, convex divergence terms) unclear. Additionally, the best-performing OT-based model, UOTM, exhibits severe sensitivity to the cost-intensity hyperparameter τ, with FID scores ranging from 2.71 to 218.02 across a modest τ range, limiting its practical usability.

## Input / Output

The generator T_θ takes a standard Gaussian noise sample x ∈ R^d (same dimension as the data) and produces a generated image; for CIFAR-10 this is a 32×32 RGB image and for CelebA-HQ a 256×256 RGB image. In the NCSN++ backbone, an auxiliary 256-dimensional Gaussian variable z is injected into intermediate feature maps via an attention module to parameterize a stochastic transport map, while in the DCGAN backbone a deterministic bicubic upsampling Q maps a 192-dimensional latent to image space. The discriminator is a potential function v_φ evaluated on both real data and generated samples, with the transport cost c(x, T_θ(x)) = τ‖x − T_θ(x)‖² coupling the two.

## Method

The paper first unifies WGAN, WGAN-GP, OTM, and UOTM under a single training algorithm (Algorithm 1) parameterized by a cost function c, convex functions g₁, g₂, g₃, and a regularizer R, then shows via ablation that strictly convex g₁, g₂ (e.g., Softplus) stabilize training through failure-aware adaptive sample weighting of the potential gradient, while the quadratic cost term prevents mode collapse by guiding the generator toward cost-minimizing pairs. The key new contribution, UOTM-SD, introduces a monotone-increasing schedule on α (the coefficient of the Csiszár divergence terms in the UOT objective) during training, so that the optimization gradually transitions from a relaxed UOT problem (soft marginal constraints, which prevent mode collapse early on) toward the strict OT problem (hard marginal constraints, which enforce accurate distribution matching late in training). Theoretically, the α-scaled UOT optimal transport plan is proven to weakly converge to the OT optimal plan as α → ∞, and the UOTM potential is shown to be equi-Lipschitz under mild convexity conditions, explaining its stable convergence. Empirically, UOTM-SD with linear scheduling achieves FID 2.51 on CIFAR-10 and 5.99 on CelebA-HQ-256, outperforming UOTM and dramatically reducing τ-sensitivity (FID range 2.51–4.62 vs. 2.71–218.02 for UOTM).

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (32×32), UOTM-SD with linear scheduling achieves a FID of 2.51, outperforming the previous best OT-based model UOTM (FID 2.97) and OTM (FID 4.15). On CelebA-HQ-256, UOTM-SD achieves a FID of 5.99, surpassing UOTM (6.31) and OTM (13.56). UOTM-SD also demonstrates substantially improved τ-robustness: while UOTM's FID ranges from 2.71 to 218.02 across τ values, UOTM-SD (Linear) stays between 2.51 and 4.62 over the same range.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UOTM-SD (Linear) achieves FID 2.51 on CIFAR-10, improving over UOTM (2.97) by 0.46 FID points and over OTM (4.15) by 1.64 points, establishing it as the best-performing OT-based generative model. On CelebA-HQ-256, UOTM-SD (FID 5.99) outperforms UOTM-SP (6.31), UOTM-KL (6.36), and OTM (13.56). Among non-OT-based methods, UOTM-SD is competitive with top GANs (RGM: 2.47, StyleGAN2 w/ ADA: 2.92) but trails the best diffusion models (LSGM: 2.10, Score SDE VE: 2.20) on CIFAR-10. The τ-robustness ablation (Table 7) shows UOTM-SD (Linear) maintains FID between 2.51–4.62 across τ ∈ {2e-4, 5e-3}, whereas UOTM degrades catastrophically to FID 218.02 at τ=5e-3 and 15.19 at τ=2e-4. OTM, while τ-stable (FID 4.15–7.43), consistently underperforms UOTM-SD at every tested τ value. The ablation on schedule intensity (Table 10) confirms that linear scheduling with (α_min, α_max)=(1/5, 5) is optimal, and that removing scheduling entirely (α-constant UOTM) degrades FID to 3.73–3.99.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate families of OT-based GANs—OT Loss methods (WGAN, WGAN-GP) that use Wasserstein distance as a training objective, and OT Map methods (OTM, UOTM) that directly learn the transport map—under a single algorithmic framework parameterized by a cost function and convex wrapper functions. It builds most directly on UOTM (Choi et al., 2023a), which leverages the semi-dual of the Unbalanced Optimal Transport problem, and on the UOT mathematical foundation from Liero et al. (2018). The paper departs from prior work by providing a systematic ablation of each component (cost intensity τ, convexity of g₁/g₂) and by introducing a simple α-scheduling of the divergence terms (UOTM-SD) that gradually transitions the UOT objective toward the standard OT objective during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified framework and component-level analysis give practitioners clear guidance on why UOTM outperforms WGAN/OTM (strictly convex wrappers provide adaptive sample-weighting and equi-Lipschitz stability; the cost term prevents mode collapse), which had previously been understood only empirically. The discovery that UOTM is highly τ-sensitive (FID ranging from 2.71 to 218.02 across a 25× range of τ) and that a simple monotone α-schedule resolves this is practically valuable for anyone deploying OT-based generators. Open questions include whether the convergence guarantee extends from transport plans to transport maps, whether g₃ ≠ Id offers further gains, and whether the approach scales to higher-resolution or video generation tasks.
