## Problem

In federated learning, clients have heterogeneous and unknown participation rates, causing standard FedAvg to converge to a biased objective that favors frequently-participating clients rather than minimizing the true global objective. Existing corrections (FedVarp, MIFA) require O(Nd) additional memory to store every client's most recent update, which is impractical at scale. The paper asks whether a lightweight method with the same memory footprint as vanilla FedAvg can provably minimize the original objective under unknown, uncontrollable, and heterogeneous participation.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a local non-IID image dataset (generated via Dirichlet(0.1) class splits) and a binary participation history indicating which past rounds it joined. The weight computation per client requires only three scalar state variables (interval count M_n, current interval length S_n^diamond, and previous weight ω_{t-1}^n), making it essentially memory-free relative to the model. The output is a trained global CNN model plus per-client, per-round adaptive aggregation weights that are multiplied into each client's update before transmission to the server, compatible with secure aggregation and differential privacy.

## Method

FedAU extends FedAvg by replacing uniform aggregation weights with per-client weights estimated online from each client's own participation history. The key insight is that the optimal weight for client n is 1/p_n (inverse participation probability), which can be approximated by the average interval between consecutive participations. A cutoff parameter K caps each interval at K rounds, creating a bias-variance tradeoff: smaller K yields more samples (lower variance) but introduces bias, while larger K reduces bias at the cost of higher variance. The convergence analysis introduces a novel weight-error term in the descent lemma, bounded via a bias-variance decomposition of the cutoff interval estimator, yielding a convergence rate of O(√(Q/(NIT)) + log²T/T) with linear speedup in N. Experiments on four image classification benchmarks with Bernoulli, Markovian, and cyclic participation patterns show FedAU outperforms both memory-free baselines and 250×-memory methods like FedVarp and MIFA.

## Results

FedAU (K=50) achieves the highest test accuracy across nearly all dataset–participation-pattern combinations. Under Bernoulli participation, FedAU reaches 89.6% on SVHN, 77.3% on CIFAR-10, 52.1% on CIFAR-100, and 62.7% on CINIC-10, outperforming the standard FedAvg baselines (Average participating / Average all) by 2–6 percentage points. Under cyclic participation, the advantage is most dramatic: FedAU gains over 9 points on CIFAR-100 (51.0% vs. 41.1% for MIFA) and over 4 points on CINIC-10 (60.9% vs. 50.2% for MIFA). The only exception is CIFAR-10, where FedVarp (77.9%) slightly edges out FedAU (77.3%) under Bernoulli participation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedAU (K=50) outperforms both standard FedAvg baselines (Average participating and Average all) by 2–6 points on test accuracy across all four datasets and all three participation patterns, confirming that adaptive weighting corrects the bias from heterogeneous participation. Against FedVarp, FedAU wins on SVHN, CIFAR-100, and CINIC-10 under all patterns, but loses narrowly on CIFAR-10 (77.3% vs. 77.9% Bernoulli; 77.2% vs. 77.8% Markovian). Against MIFA, FedAU's advantage is largest under cyclic participation, where MIFA degrades severely (e.g., 41.1% vs. 51.0% on CIFAR-100, 50.2% vs. 60.9% on CINIC-10), while under Bernoulli and Markovian patterns the gap is smaller (1–4 points). Notably, FedAU also surpasses the idealized 'known participation statistics' baseline in most settings (e.g., 52.1% vs. 50.6% on CIFAR-100, 62.7% vs. 60.5% on CINIC-10 under Bernoulli), because it adapts to each client's actual empirical participation path rather than relying on the model-based probability. All comparisons are made with the same 1× memory footprint for FedAU versus 250× for FedVarp and MIFA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FedAvg framework by introducing adaptive aggregation weights that correct for heterogeneous client participation, building on the observation (from Theorem 1) that standard FedAvg implicitly minimizes a participation-weighted objective rather than the true FL objective. It departs from prior variance-reduction approaches (FedVarp, MIFA) that require N× additional memory to store all clients' historical updates, instead using a lightweight online estimation of participation intervals with a bias-variance tradeoff controlled by a cutoff parameter K. The theoretical analysis draws on techniques from adaptive gradient methods (Reddi et al., 2021) for bounding weight error terms, but applies them to the fundamentally different problem of participation-frequency debiasing rather than per-coordinate gradient scaling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: real FL systems have unknown, uncontrollable, and heterogeneous client participation, yet most convergence analyses assume known or coordinated sampling. FedAU provides the first provable convergence guarantee for FedAvg with unknown participation statistics at the same memory cost as vanilla FedAvg, with linear speedup in the number of clients. Key limitations include the theoretical requirement of time-independent (Bernoulli) participation, while Markovian and cyclic patterns are only validated empirically; the bounded-gradient or nearly-optimal-weights assumption (Assumption 5) is also somewhat restrictive. Open questions include extending the analysis to time-dependent participation processes and integrating adaptive weighting into other FL algorithm families (e.g., SCAFFOLD, adaptive local SGD).
