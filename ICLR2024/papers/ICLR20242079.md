## Problem

Physics-based motion learning policies trained on reference trajectories tend to memorize specific instances rather than learn underlying dynamics, resulting in poor generalization to unseen motions and difficulty interpolating between sparse data points. Existing structured representations like PAE capture local periodicity but lack temporal propagation structure, so they cannot predict future states or express global motion consistency across an entire trajectory.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242079/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes sliding windows of H=51 timesteps from a 27-dimensional state space (base linear/angular velocities, projected gravity, 18 joint positions) and produces a compact 4c=32-dimensional latent parameterization (frequency, amplitude, offset vectors of c=8 channels) plus a time-varying phase vector. During online tracking, user-proposed trajectory segments are evaluated against a prediction-loss threshold; accepted targets yield decoded joint-position tracking references, while rejected targets trigger a fallback that propagates latent dynamics to generate safe alternative references from the previous motion.

## Method

FLD extends PAE by enforcing a quasi-constant parameterization assumption: frequency, amplitude, and offset remain fixed along a trajectory while only the latent phase φ_t advances linearly. The key training mechanism is a multi-step forward prediction loss (Eq. 5) that propagates latent dynamics over N=50 steps with a decay factor α, computing reconstruction error in the original motion space at each step—this forces the network to maintain globally consistent sinusoidal parameters rather than re-estimating them locally. This reduces the parameter count needed to express a trajectory from 4c×(|τ|−H+1) (PAE) to just 4c (FLD), enabling autoregressive motion synthesis and interpolation in the latent space. For motion learning, a PPO policy tracks FLD-decoded targets, and a fallback mechanism evaluates incoming user targets by computing L_FLD^N over a buffer of recent inputs; if the prediction loss exceeds a threshold ε_FLD derived from training statistics, the target is rejected and the controller reverts to propagating latent dynamics from the last accepted state, ensuring safe execution.

## Results

FLD reduces the number of parameters needed to express a trajectory of length |τ| from 4c×(|τ|−H+1) (PAE) to just 4c, a dramatic compression that enables compact motion representation. In motion prediction on an unseen diagonal run, FLD achieves the lowest relative error, with the advantage growing substantially in long-horizon prediction where PAE and a feed-forward baseline accumulate compounding errors. For online tracking on the MIT Humanoid, the ALPGMM skill sampler achieves 0.80±0.03 general tracking performance (uniformly sampled latent targets) versus 0.72±0.01 for the offline-only sampler, while maintaining competitive expert performance (0.87±0.04 vs. 0.88±0.03).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLD is compared directly against PAE (Starke et al., 2022), a VAE, a feed-forward predictor, and raw state representations. Against PAE, FLD achieves strictly lower prediction error on unseen motions (especially at long horizons) and produces a more structured latent manifold with explicit concentric-cycle geometry, whereas PAE's local sinusoidal reconstruction yields only a weaker, locally consistent pattern. Against the VAE, FLD's latent space exhibits far stronger temporal regularity (concentric cycles vs. scattered points), and the parameter count drops from c×(|τ|−H+1) to 4c. Against the feed-forward baseline, FLD generalizes to unseen motions while the FF model overfits and fails to capture spatial-temporal structure. In tracking, the FLD-informed policy with ALPGMM sampling matches or exceeds the offline-only policy on general performance (0.80 vs. 0.72) while retaining near-identical expert performance, demonstrating that the latent dynamics structure enables broader motion coverage without sacrificing precision on known targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

FLD is a direct generative extension of the Periodic Autoencoder (PAE, Starke et al. 2022), which already parameterizes latent trajectories as sinusoidal functions via a differentiable FFT. The key architectural departure is enforcing multi-step latent dynamics propagation (advancing the phase φ while holding frequency, amplitude, and offset constant), converting PAE's purely local reconstruction into a predictive model that captures temporal structure across the full trajectory. On the learning side, the paper builds on the DeepMimic/AMP/ASE lineage of physics-based motion tracking with PPO in Isaac Gym, and borrows the ALPGMM curriculum learning strategy from the adaptive learning progress literature for skill sampling. The fallback mechanism, which uses the FLD prediction loss as an online safety gate, is a novel contribution not present in prior motion tracking work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The quasi-constant parameterization assumption (Assumption 1) is the paper's central limitation: it holds well for periodic and quasi-periodic locomotion but breaks down for aperiodic transitions, forcing either underparameterized reconstruction or very long observation windows. The fallback mechanism is practically valuable for real-robot deployment but relies on a hand-tuned threshold ε_FLD derived from training statistics, and its safety guarantees are informal. Open questions include how to handle genuinely aperiodic motions without manual period detection, how to design motion-dependent reward functions for diverse skills, and whether the learning-progress-based exploration can be made to converge rather than remain open-ended. The compact 4c-parameter representation (vs. d×|τ| for raw trajectories) is a meaningful efficiency gain for downstream control, but the evaluation is limited to a single humanoid platform and a single motion dataset.
