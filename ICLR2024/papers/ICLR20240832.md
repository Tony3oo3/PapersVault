## Problem

CTC training treats all perfect alignments equally and all imperfect alignments equally, providing no mechanism to prioritize alignments based on application-critical properties such as token emission time (latency) or word error rate. This inherent dichotomy means that two models with identical CTC scores can exhibit very different latency or WER, and existing solutions for controlling alignment properties either require modifying the forward-backward algorithm or are restricted to perfect alignments only.

## Input / Output

The method takes 16KHz, 16-bit audio waveforms as input, processed through architectures ranging from 30.7M (Conformer) to 90M (Wav2Vec2) parameters, and produces transcriptions over a 29-character English vocabulary (lowercase letters, apostrophes, spaces, and a blank token). The key intermediate object is the T-length alignment over the expanded vocabulary V' = V ∪ {∅}; AWP operates on these alignments during training to shape the model's alignment distribution, and at inference the model is decoded via beam search (beam size 100) with combined character-level and 5-gram word-level language model scoring.

## Method

AWP is a plug-and-play framework that augments the standard CTC loss with a hinge-based pairwise loss over sampled alignments. At each training step, N alignments are sampled from the pre-trained CTC model's output probabilities; a property-specific function f_prop transforms each sampled alignment into a 'better' counterpart (e.g., shifting tokens one step earlier for latency, or correcting the minimum-substitution word for mWER), producing N (original, improved) pairs. The AWP loss penalizes cases where the original alignment has higher probability than the improved one, with a tunable margin λ. The total training objective is L = L_CTC + α·L_AWP, where α controls the trade-off. Crucially, the CTC loss and its forward-backward algorithm remain unmodified, and the framework applies to both perfect and imperfect alignments, requiring only a few lines of code to implement for any new property.

## Results

On the low-latency task (LS-960, Stacked ResNet), AWP reduces drift latency (DL) from 278 ms to −79 ms and total latency (TL) from 708 ms to 351 ms, with a modest WER increase from 4.06% to 4.38%. At the largest scale (Internal-280K), AWP cuts DL from 249 ms to 50 ms and TL from 679 ms to 480 ms while keeping WER at 2.71% (vs. 2.6% baseline). For the mWER task, AWP yields a relative WER improvement of 4.5% on LibriSpeech Test-Other (5.82% → 5.56% for Wav2Vec2) and 4% on Test-Other for Stacked ResNet (7.46% → 7.16%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the low-latency task (LS-960, Stacked ResNet), AWP outperforms all three prior methods on both latency and WER: it achieves DL = −79 ms vs. 186 ms (Peak First CTC), −76 ms (TrimTail), and 63 ms (Bayes Risk CTC), while maintaining the lowest WER at 4.38% compared to 4.41%, 4.46%, and 4.78% respectively. At a matched WER of ~4.7–4.8% (Table 7), AWP still achieves the best DL (−96 ms) versus −59 ms (TrimTail), 64 ms (Bayes Risk), and 186 ms (Peak First CTC). For the mWER task, AWP is competitive with the application-specific MWER_OPT baseline: on Stacked ResNet, AWP achieves 2.57% WER on Test-Clean vs. 2.54% for MWER_OPT, but outperforms it on Test-Other (7.16% vs. 7.31%). The Conformer experiment further confirms that AWP generalizes across architectures, reducing DL from 2 ms to −172 ms with no WER degradation (3.75% → 3.74%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the CTC criterion (Graves et al., 2006) and positions itself against a line of work that attempts to control CTC alignments through modifications to the forward-backward algorithm (Bayes Risk CTC, Peak First CTC, FastEmit) or through input-level penalties (TrimTail). Unlike these methods, which are property-specific and require intervention in the core CTC computation, AWP introduces a property-agnostic hinge loss over sampled alignment pairs that leaves the CTC loss untouched. It also extends the MWER optimization line (Graves & Jaitly, 2014; Prabhavalkar et al., 2018), which previously required extensive optimization or was designed for soft-alignment models, by showing that a simple pairwise hinge loss can achieve comparable WER improvements while remaining applicable to hard-alignment CTC models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the framework's simplicity (a few lines of code) and generality: a single mechanism handles both latency and WER optimization across three architectures and data scales up to 280K hours, without modifying the CTC loss. The main limitation is that the property function f_prop must be carefully designed for each target property, and the framework currently optimizes one property at a time. Open questions include formalizing which properties are amenable to this treatment, extending to multi-property optimization (e.g., jointly balancing latency and WER), and adapting the framework to other alignment-free criteria like RNN-Transducer or to non-ASR domains.
