## Problem

Nonprehensile manipulation of general unseen objects (pushing, toppling, pivoting) requires both shape-general object representations and diverse closed-loop motions, yet prior RL methods achieve only one: shape-general approaches are limited to simple open-loop primitives like poking, while motion-rich approaches rely on bounding-box representations that cannot generalize across geometries. Additionally, high-dimensional point cloud inputs make end-to-end RL training computationally expensive and unstable, preventing scaling to massively parallel GPU simulations.

## Input / Output

The encoder consumes a 512×3 object point cloud (divided into 16 patches of 32 points via FPS+kNN) plus a 9-dim hand state (position + 6D orientation), producing 16 patch embeddings of 128 dimensions. The policy additionally ingests robot joint position/velocity, the previous action (6-dim subgoal residual + 14-dim impedance gains), object pose relative to goal, and physical parameters (mass, friction, restitution), all tokenized via MLP for cross-attention against the patch embeddings. The output is a 6-dim end-effector subgoal residual (3D translation + 3D axis-angle rotation) and 14-dim joint-space gains (7 proportional + 7 damping), converted to joint torques via a variable impedance controller. The student model replaces full point clouds with partial occluded views and adds a 2-layer GRU for temporal aggregation.

## Method

CORN pretrains a lightweight patch-based transformer encoder on a contact-prediction task: given an object point cloud and gripper pose, the network classifies which of 16 spatial patches intersect the gripper, using binary cross-entropy on labels generated by sampling gripper poses near or in collision with objects. This contact-focused objective is motivated by the insight that manipulation success depends primarily on where forces can be applied, not on reconstructing full geometry. The architecture uses FPS to select 16 representative points, kNN to form 32-point patches, a lightweight MLP (instead of PointNet) for patch tokenization, and a 2-layer transformer (128 hidden dim, 4 self-attention heads) with hand state injected as extra tokens for cross-modal attention. For policy training, the encoder is frozen and a PPO policy with 16-head cross-attention over patch embeddings is trained in Isaac Gym across 4096 parallel environments, using an action space of end-effector subgoals with variable impedance gains for dense closed-loop control. Sim-to-real transfer is achieved via DAgger distillation from a privileged teacher to a student that operates on partial point clouds with a GRU memory, enabling zero-shot deployment on 16 unseen real-world objects at 71.3% success rate.

## Results

In simulation (4096 parallel environments, 2B interaction steps), CORN achieves 88.0% success rate by step count and 88.5% by wall-clock time, substantially outperforming end-to-end training (54.4% steps, 31.4% time) and all other pretrained-representation baselines. The patch-based transformer architecture is critical for time efficiency: PointNet (PN) reaches only 70.2% in steps and a mere 0.223% in time due to its computational overhead, while the large Point2Vec encoder (P2V) matches CORN in steps (85.6%) but collapses to 3.55% in time. In the real world, the distilled student policy achieves 71.3% success (57/80) across 16 diverse unseen objects with zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CORN decisively outperforms end-to-end training (E2E) by ~34 percentage points in steps (88.0% vs. 54.4%) and ~57 points in time (88.5% vs. 31.4%), confirming that contact-based pretraining is essential for data- and time-efficient policy learning. Against the strongest representation baseline P2V (Point2Vec), CORN matches in step efficiency (88.0% vs. 85.6%) but is ~25× faster in wall-clock time (88.5% vs. 3.55%), demonstrating that the lightweight patch-transformer architecture is the key enabler for massively parallel GPU training. P2V-LITE, which uses the same architecture but a different (Data2Vec-style) pretraining objective, lags CORN by ~19 points in steps (68.8% vs. 88.0%), showing that the contact-prediction task is more informative than generic shape reconstruction for manipulation. The rotation-estimation pretraining (ROT, from in-hand reorientation literature) underperforms CORN by ~24 points in steps, and the geometry-unaware STATE baseline (bounding box only) fails entirely, highlighting that nonprehensile manipulation demands fine-grained geometric awareness beyond pose. The PointNet architecture (PN) achieves comparable step performance (70.2%) but is ~400× slower in time (0.223%), making it impractical for large-scale parallel RL.

## Relation to Prior Work

CORN builds on two complementary lines of work: the variable-impedance action space and curriculum scheme from Kim et al. (2023), which enables diverse closed-loop motions but relies on a bounding-box object representation that limits shape generalization, and the patch-based transformer architecture from the Point2Vec/PointMAE lineage (Zeid et al., 2023; Pang et al., 2022), which provides an efficient point-cloud backbone. The key conceptual departure is in the pretraining objective: rather than reconstructing full shape geometry (as in Point-BERT, PointMAE) or predicting object rotation (as in in-hand reorientation work by Huang et al., 2021; Chen et al., 2022), CORN trains the encoder to predict which patches are in collision with the gripper—a task-specific signal that the authors argue is more directly relevant to nonprehensile manipulation than holistic shape understanding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 71.3% zero-shot real-world success rate across 16 diverse unseen objects demonstrates that contact-informed pretraining plus an efficient patch-based encoder can bridge the sim-to-real gap for nonprehensile manipulation without per-object tuning. The dramatic wall-clock efficiency gap (CORN at 88.5% success in 24 h vs. PointNet-based at 0.223%) highlights that architecture choice, not just pretraining quality, is the bottleneck for massively parallel GPU RL. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-object scenes, dynamic environments, or more dexterous end-effectors, and whether the contact-prediction pretraining signal generalizes beyond tabletop rearrangement to more complex contact-rich tasks like in-hand manipulation.
