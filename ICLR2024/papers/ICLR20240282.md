## Problem

Existing neural subset selection methods model the utility function F(S) using only the subset S, ignoring the background information contained in the superset V. The paper identifies this as a fundamental oversight: from a probabilistic perspective, the correct target is the conditional distribution P(Y|S,V), not P(Y|S), and prior architectures lack a principled mechanism to incorporate superset context while respecting the hierarchical permutation symmetry between S and V.

## Input / Output

The method takes a ground set V of n elements, each encoded as a d-dimensional vector (768-d BERT embeddings for Amazon products, 4096-d flattened pixels for Double MNIST, 1922-d ACNN features for PDBBind complexes, or 256-d DeepDTA features for BindingDB drug-target pairs), and produces a probability vector Y ∈ [0,1]^|V| indicating the likelihood of each element belonging to the optimal subset. At inference, the optimal subset S* is extracted via TopN rounding of Y. The key structural constraint is that S ⊂ V, so the model must simultaneously process the subset and its parent set under a nested permutation symmetry group.

## Method

INSET builds on the EquiVSet energy-based model framework but introduces a theoretically grounded information aggregation module. The paper first defines the symmetry group G as the wreath product of the subset permutation group H and the superset nested-set permutation group R, then proves (Theorem 3.5) that P(Y|S,V) is G-invariant if and only if it can be expressed as f(ξ, M(S,V)) where M is an invariant sufficient representation. Practically, M(S,V) is approximated by two parallel DeepSets modules: one summing element embeddings within S (size nᵢ) and one summing embeddings across all of V (size n), whose outputs are passed through separate feed-forward networks θ₁ and θ₂ and combined by addition before a final non-linear activation σ. This simple additive fusion of subset and superset representations ensures the output is invariant to permutations within S, permutations of the nested subsets of V, and reordering of V's elements, while retaining all information relevant to predicting Y.

## Results

INSET is evaluated using the Mean Jaccard Coefficient (MJC) across product recommendation, set anomaly detection, and compound selection tasks. On set anomaly detection, INSET achieves 0.707 on Double MNIST (a 23% relative improvement over the best baseline EquiVSet at 0.575), 0.580 on CelebA, and 0.721 on Fashion-MNIST. On compound selection, INSET reaches 0.935 on PDBBind and 0.734 on BindingDB. On the Amazon Baby Registry product recommendation task, INSET achieves the best MJC in 10 out of 12 categories, with gains of up to 13.5 points over EquiVSet (e.g., 0.885 vs 0.819 on Feeding).

## Baselines & Comparisons

INSET outperforms all four baselines (PGM, DeepSet, Set Transformer, EquiVSet) on every task and dataset reported. The largest margin is against EquiVSet on Double MNIST anomaly detection, where INSET achieves 0.707 MJC versus 0.575 (a 23% relative gain), and on Fashion-MNIST where INSET reaches 0.721 versus 0.645. Against Set Transformer, INSET leads by 0.195 on Double MNIST and 0.140 on F-MNIST, while the gap narrows on compound selection (e.g., 0.935 vs 0.919 on PDBBind). Against PGM, INSET dominates on anomaly detection tasks (e.g., 0.707 vs 0.300 on Double MNIST) and also leads on compound selection (0.935 vs 0.910 on PDBBind). Against DeepSet, INSET consistently wins by wide margins across all tasks. On the 12-category Amazon Baby Registry product recommendation benchmark, INSET achieves the best MJC in 10 of 12 categories, with the only exception being Furniture where Set Transformer edges ahead (0.176 vs 0.169).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the EquiVSet framework (Ou et al., 2022) for neural subset selection under the Optimal Subset Oracle by identifying a critical omission: prior methods model the utility function as F(S) or the conditional as P(Y|S), ignoring the superset V. Drawing on the probabilistic symmetry theory of Bloem-Reddy & Teh (2020) and the universal approximation results for nested sets from Maron et al. (2020), the authors formalize the correct inductive bias as invariance under a wreath product group acting jointly on (S, V), and show that the invariant sufficient representation of V must be aggregated into the subset representation. The practical instantiation (INSET) is a minimal architectural change—adding a second DeepSets pooling layer over V and combining it with the existing one over S—grounded in this theoretical analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical contribution provides a principled, group-theoretic justification for why superset information is necessary in subset selection, moving the design of set-based models from heuristic to symmetry-driven. The practical takeaway is that a single additional pooling layer yields consistent gains across product recommendation, anomaly detection, and drug discovery, suggesting that many existing set-function models may be underfitting by discarding background context. Limitations include the absence of pairwise element interactions (only sum-pooling is used), the simplification of compound selection to a single bioactivity filter, and the reliance on the EquiVSet variational framework whose own weaknesses (e.g., poor performance on Furniture/Safety categories) propagate to INSET. Open questions include whether richer equivariant architectures or attention-based interactions between S and V elements could further close the gap.
