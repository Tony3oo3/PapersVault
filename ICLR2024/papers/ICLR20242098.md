## Problem

Existing approximate machine unlearning methods suffer from two critical limitations: instability, where performance degrades unpredictably as the amount of forgetting data varies, and a lack of generality, where methods designed for image classification fail to transfer effectively to image generation tasks (e.g., diffusion models). The paper asks whether a single principled mechanism can address both limitations simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242098/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained model (ResNet-18/VGG-16/Swin-T for classification; DDPM or Stable Diffusion V1.4 for generation), a forgetting dataset specifying data points, classes, or text-prompt concepts to erase, and a remaining dataset for regularization. It produces an unlearned model where only a subset of weights (determined by a binary saliency mask, typically 50% sparsity) has been modified. For generation tasks, the forgetting concept is specified via text prompts (e.g., 'an image of airplane' or NSFW prompts from I2P), and the output model is evaluated by an external classifier (ResNet-34/50) on generated images.

## Method

SalUn introduces gradient-based weight saliency for machine unlearning: it computes the gradient of a forgetting loss (cross-entropy for classification, MSE for diffusion) with respect to all model weights at the pre-trained checkpoint, then applies a hard threshold (set to the median gradient magnitude) to produce a binary mask identifying salient weights. During unlearning, only the salient weights are updated via a random-labeling objective (random image labels in classification; misaligned text-concept pairings in generation), while non-salient weights remain frozen. This plug-and-play masking is combined with a regularization term on the remaining dataset to preserve generalization. The key insight is that restricting updates to gradient-salient weights stabilizes unlearning across varying forgetting data amounts and enables the same mechanism to work in both classification and diffusion-based generation, where prior methods either over-forget or under-forget.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, SalUn achieves the smallest average performance gap with exact unlearning (Retrain) across all 8 baselines: 1.15 at 10% random data forgetting and 2.65 at 50%, compared to the next-best baselines ℓ1-sparse (2.26 / 5.72) and BE (2.76 / 3.72). For class-wise forgetting on Imagenette with Stable Diffusion, SalUn reaches 99.82% average unlearning accuracy (UA) with a FID of 1.22, outperforming ESD (99.40% UA, FID 1.49) and FMN (42.54% UA, FID 1.30). In the NSFW concept-erasure task on SD V1.4, SalUn generates the fewest harmful images across all nudity categories, approaching near-100% unlearning accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SalUn outperforms all 8 baselines on the average performance gap (Avg. Gap) metric in both 10% and 50% random data forgetting on CIFAR-10. At 10%, the closest competitors are ℓ1-sparse (2.26) and BS (2.67), while SalUn achieves 1.15; at 50%, the gap widens for baselines (BS jumps to 8.13, ℓ1-sparse to 5.72) while SalUn remains at 2.65, demonstrating superior stability as forgetting data increases. For image generation on Imagenette (Table 2), SalUn achieves 99.82% average UA with FID 1.22, beating ESD (99.40% UA, FID 1.49) on both metrics and dramatically outperforming FMN (42.54% UA, FID 1.30) on UA while also matching its FID. In the NSFW concept-erasure task on Stable Diffusion V1.4, SalUn generates the fewest harmful images across all nudity body-part categories, significantly outperforming ESD (the second-best method) particularly on 'male breast' and 'female breast' categories. The ablation in Table A1 confirms that integrating the weight saliency mask into RL (i.e., SalUn) reduces the Avg. Gap from 7.15 to 1.15 at 10% forgetting, validating that the saliency mechanism—not just the RL base—is the key driver of improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the random labeling (RL) unlearning method of Golatkar et al. (2020) as its foundational building block, and extends the concept-erasing line of work for diffusion models (ESD, FMN) by reframing it within a principled machine unlearning framework. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of 'weight saliency'—a gradient-based mask derived from the forgetting loss—that directs unlearning updates to a subset of model weights, drawing an explicit analogy to input saliency in model explanation. Unlike prior MU methods that operate on the full model or rely on sparsity patterns, SalUn uses a hard-thresholded gradient saliency map to decompose weights into 'salient' (updated) and 'intact' (frozen) components, a mechanism borrowed from the modularity and weight-pruning literature but repurposed for unlearning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SalUn addresses a practical gap: existing approximate MU methods are unstable across varying forgetting-data quantities and fail entirely when applied to diffusion-based image generation, where the need to erase harmful concepts (e.g., NSFW content) is urgent. The near-exact-unlearning performance (0.2% gap on CIFAR-10) and ~100% unlearning accuracy for nudity-related prompts in Stable Diffusion make this a practically deployable technique. Open questions include scalability to language models and graph neural networks, whether the 50% default sparsity threshold generalizes to larger architectures, and whether the approach remains effective under adversarial re-learning attacks on the unlearned model.
