## Problem

No public dataset or method exists for the task of generating a dance follower's motion that responds simultaneously to a leader's movements and background music. Existing solo dance generation methods lack the interactive coordination scope, and group dance methods only handle weak, replicated-motion interactions rather than the strong, responsive coordination required in duet ballroom dancing. Additionally, GPT-based motion generators produce skating artifacts (inconsistent lower-body movement vs. global displacement) when confronted with out-of-distribution music or leader motion patterns.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 54-dimensional music feature vector (MFCC, MFCC delta, constant-Q chromagram, onset strength, onset beat) per frame, the leader's quantized motion tokens across four body-part VQ-VAEs, and the follower's own previous tokens, all processed through Look-Ahead Transformers with a 29-token (~4 s) future window. Output is a sequence of discrete tokens for the follower's upper body, lower body, left hand, right hand, and relative translation, which are decoded into SMPL-X joint rotations (via a rotation-matrix decoder) and a global root position computed as the leader's root plus the decoded relative translation. The VQ-VAE codebook size is 512 with a temporal downsampling factor of 4, and the GPT operates on a block size of 30 tokens.

## Method

Duolando is a two-stage pipeline: five 1D-CNN VQ-VAEs (four body parts + relative translation) quantize continuous motion into discrete tokens, and a 12-layer GPT (block size 30, dim 768) autoregressively predicts the next follower tokens conditioned on music, leader motion, and prior follower state. A key architectural element is the Look-Ahead Transformer (LAT), which uses a banded attention mask of window L to propagate future conditioning information backward, enabling the follower to anticipate the leader's upcoming moves. Interaction coordination is enforced via a 10×10 block-wise lower-triangular attention mask that integrates all ten input streams while preserving causal order. To address out-of-distribution skating artifacts, the paper introduces an off-policy RL fine-tuning stage that replaces the standard on-policy actor-critic loss with an energy-based objective π_θ(a|s)=σ(Q(s,a)), where Q is approximated by a two-step reward accumulation; the step-wise reward specifically penalizes frames where the decoded lower-body velocity is inconsistent with the global translation, directly suppressing skating.

## Results

On the DD100 test set, the full Duolando model achieves FID_k=25.30, FID_g=33.52, FID_cd=9.97, contact frequency (CF) of 52.36%, Beat Echo Degree (BED) of 0.2858, and Beat-Align Score (BAS) of 0.2046. The ground truth reference values are FID_k=6.56, FID_cd=3.41, CF=74.25%, BED=0.5308, and BAS=0.1839. The off-policy RL stage reduces FID_k by 76% (from 106.72 to 25.30) and the skating ratio by 69% (from 1.06% to 0.33%) compared to the variant without RL. In the user study, Duolando is preferred over its ablated variants in 62–83% of pairwise comparisons, but only surpasses ground truth in ~15% of cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Duolando against two state-of-the-art solo dance generation methods: Bailando (CVPR 2022) and EDGE (CVPR 2023). Both solo baselines are fundamentally inadequate for the duet task: their FID_cd values are in the thousands (6643 and 5894) versus Duolando's 9.97, and their contact frequencies are near zero (7.13% and 6.82%) versus 52.36%, confirming that ignoring the leader's motion makes interactive coordination impossible. On solo quality metrics, Duolando substantially outperforms both: FID_k of 25.30 vs. 78.52 (Bailando) and 69.14 (EDGE), and FID_g of 33.52 vs. 36.19 and 44.58 respectively. On rhythmic alignment (BAS), Duolando (0.2046) also edges out both solo methods (0.1930 and 0.1875). The ablation variants further show that the relative translation module is critical for interaction (reducing FID_cd from 7970 to 21.68 and boosting CF from 7.76% to 57.43%), while the off-policy RL is essential for eliminating skating artifacts (reducing FID_k from 106.72 to 25.30 and skating ratio from 1.06% to 0.33%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VQ-VAE + autoregressive GPT pipeline established by Bailando (Siyao et al., 2022) for solo music-to-dance generation into a fundamentally different interactive setting: generating a follower's motion conditioned on both music and a leader's pre-specified motion. It departs from prior solo dance methods (Bailando, EDGE, DanceFormer) by introducing interaction-coordinated attention over leader tokens and explicit relative translation prediction, and from group dance methods (GDanceR) by requiring strong, asymmetric leader-follower coordination rather than symmetric replication. The off-policy RL strategy for GPT finetuning is a novel departure from the on-policy actor-critic approach used in Bailando, motivated by the need to handle out-of-distribution leader motions without ground-truth labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of dance accompaniment as a formal task, supported by the DD100 dataset (117 minutes across 10 ballroom/ballet genres) and a benchmark with interaction-specific metrics (contact frequency, beat echo degree, cross-distance FID), provides the field with a much-needed evaluation framework for interactive motion generation. The off-policy RL technique for GPT—establishing an explicit probability target via energy-based policy modeling—could generalize to other autoregressive generation tasks facing OOD robustness challenges. However, the generated follower still lags significantly behind ground truth (FID_k 25.30 vs. 6.56; only 15% user preference over GT), and the dataset's scope (5 pairs, ballroom/ballet only) limits generalizability to broader interaction types or dance styles.
