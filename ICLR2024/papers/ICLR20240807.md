## Problem

Object-centric representations are widely conjectured to enable compositional generalization, yet it remains unclear when this conjecture actually holds. The paper identifies two core challenges: (1) achieving slot identifiability on a slot-supported training subset where latents may be highly dependent, and (2) extending that identifiability to out-of-distribution combinations of slots. No prior theoretical result addresses both challenges simultaneously for unsupervised object-centric learning.

## Input / Output

The model takes multi-object RGB images (Spriteworld renderer, two objects on a black background, each specified by x/y position, size, color, and shape) and produces K slot vectors of M dimensions each plus a reconstructed image. Training data is drawn from a slot-supported subset of the latent space—each object's full configuration range is observed, but not all joint combinations—making the OOD evaluation set consist of novel slot pairings never seen during training. The encoder-decoder pair must invert each other on both the training subset and the full Cartesian product of slot spaces.

## Method

The paper proves that an autoencoder with a compositional and additive decoder (slot-wise renders summed without cross-slot interactions) trained to minimize reconstruction loss on a slot-supported subset will slot-identify ground-truth latents (Thm.1), and that additivity alone guarantees the decoder generalizes to all OOD slot combinations (Thm.2). The key practical contribution is a compositional consistency regularizer (L_cons) that samples two ID latents from a batch, shuffles their slots to form an OOD combination, decodes it, and penalizes the encoder for failing to recover the shuffled slots—thereby forcing the encoder to invert the decoder OOD (Thm.3). In practice, slot matching between the re-encoded and target latents uses the Hungarian algorithm, and the decoder's additivity is implemented by replacing Slot Attention's softmax mask normalization with slot-wise sigmoid functions. Experiments on Spriteworld confirm that standard Slot Attention fails OOD without these modifications, while the full model (additive decoder + L_cons + deterministic inference) achieves near-optimal OOD identifiability and reconstruction.

## Results

On the Spriteworld multi-object dataset (2 objects, 5 latents each), the full method (additive decoder + compositional consistency loss + deterministic inference) achieves OOD slot identifiability R² of 0.94 ± 0.02 and OOD reconstruction R² of 0.92 ± 0.02, compared to 0.81 ± 0.09 and 0.71 ± 0.02 for standard Slot Attention. In-distribution performance remains near-optimal (R² ≈ 0.99) across all variants. The compositional consistency loss alone (without additivity or determinism) improves OOD identifiability from 0.81 to 0.92, while additivity alone yields only a marginal gain (0.81 → 0.83).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole baseline is standard Slot Attention (Locatello et al., 2020), which uses a non-additive masked decoder (softmax-normalized masks), no compositional consistency regularization, and stochastic slot re-initialization. Standard Slot Attention achieves near-perfect in-distribution performance (R² ≈ 0.99) but degrades substantially out-of-distribution: OOD identifiability drops to 0.81 and OOD reconstruction to 0.71. The paper's full method (additive decoder + L_cons + deterministic inference) recovers OOD identifiability to 0.94 and OOD reconstruction to 0.92, a gain of +0.13 and +0.21 respectively. The ablation reveals that compositional consistency is the dominant factor (contributing most of the OOD improvement), while additivity alone provides only a marginal +0.02 gain in OOD identifiability, and deterministic inference provides an additional +0.02 on top of the other two modifications.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the identifiability framework of Brady et al. (2023), which proved that autoencoders with compositional decoders learn object-centric representations under compositionality and irreducibility assumptions on the generator. The key departure is extending from full-latent-space identifiability to a partially observed setting (slot-supported subsets) and, crucially, adding an OOD generalization component that prior identifiability results did not address. The compositional consistency loss (L_cons) is a new regularization that enforces encoder-decoder invertibility on novel slot combinations, drawing conceptual inspiration from compositional replay in neuroscience and consistency regularization in VAEs. Empirically, the paper uses Slot Attention as its testbed, modifying its standard softmax-masked decoder to be additive and adding deterministic inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide provable guarantees that object-centric autoencoders generalize compositionally to unseen slot combinations, filling a gap where the field had only conjectures. The empirical finding that standard Slot Attention fails OOD compositional generalization without all three modifications (additive decoder, consistency regularization, deterministic inference) is practically important for practitioners building object-centric models. However, the additive decoder assumption is inherently limiting—it forbids slot interactions during rendering, precluding occlusion and complex multi-object scenes—so the theory does not yet cover realistic settings. A key open question is how to introduce slot interactions while preserving compositional generalization guarantees, and how to scale the consistency loss beyond naive uniform slot shuffling.
