## Problem

Conventional uncertainty measures like Shannon entropy and the Gini coefficient are invariant to label relabeling and assign low uncertainty to overconfident but incorrect predictions, making them unreliable for misclassification detection. No existing method learns data-driven patterns in the distribution of soft-predictions to distinguish correctly from incorrectly classified samples, leaving a gap between the model's confidence and its actual reliability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240870/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference time, the method consumes only the C-dimensional soft-prediction vector p̂(x) from a pre-trained classifier and a pre-computed C×C symmetric uncertainty matrix D* (computed offline from a validation split of positive and negative samples). It produces a scalar REL-U score s(x) = p̂(x) D* p̂(x)ᵀ, which is thresholded to yield a binary misclassification decision. No input perturbation or gradient access is required at test time, and the D* matrix is dataset- and model-specific but fixed once computed.

## Method

Building on Rao's diversity coefficient, the paper defines a learnable uncertainty measure s_d(x) = p̂(x) D p̂(x)ᵀ where D is a C×C distance matrix whose entries d(i,j) are learned from data rather than fixed a priori. A contrastive objective minimizes expected uncertainty over positive (correctly classified) samples while maximizing it over negative (misclassified) samples, subject to zero diagonal, non-negativity, symmetry, and a bounded Frobenius norm. The constrained optimization admits a closed-form solution: D* = (1/Z) ReLU(λ·E[p̂(X₋)ᵀ p̂(X₋)] − (1−λ)·E[p̂(X₊)ᵀ p̂(X₊)]), where λ balances the two terms and Z normalizes the Frobenius norm. This learned matrix can assign different uncertainty magnitudes to different class-pair confusions (e.g., high uncertainty for dog↔cat misclassifications), a fine-grained capability that entropy-based methods lack since they treat all off-diagonal entries uniformly. The Gini coefficient used by Doctor is recovered as the special case where d is the Hamming distance.

## Results

The primary metric is FPR at 95% TPR (lower is better) for misclassification detection. On the strongest setting (ResNet-34, CIFAR-10, CrossEntropy), REL-U achieves 14.1% FPR, outperforming Doctor (14.3%), ODIN (19.4%), and MSP (25.8%). Across all 20 model/dataset/training-loss combinations in Table 1, REL-U is best in 11/20 experiments and tied with the second-best in 4 of the remaining 9. On the more challenging CIFAR-100 with RegMixUp (highest accuracy, 80.8%), REL-U achieves 37.7% FPR versus Doctor's 40.9% and ODIN's 45.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REL-U consistently outperforms MSP (Hendrycks & Gimpel, 2017) across all 20 settings, with gaps ranging from ~3 to ~23 percentage points in FPR at 95% TPR. Against ODIN (Liang et al., 2017), REL-U wins in 14/20 experiments, with the largest advantage on Mixup-trained models (e.g., 19.0 vs. 38.2 on ResNet-34 CIFAR-10). Against Doctor (Granese et al., 2021), REL-U wins in 13/20 experiments, with the most notable gains on CrossEntropy (18.3 vs. 21.5 on DenseNet-121 CIFAR-10) and RegMixUp (18.2 vs. 21.8 on ResNet-34 CIFAR-10); the two methods are nearly tied on LogitNorm settings where both struggle. In the Bayesian comparison (Table 3), REL-U outperforms both MCDropout (Gal & Ghahramani, 2016) and Deep Ensembles (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2016) on all four model/dataset combinations, e.g., 14.1 vs. 14.8 (Deep Ensembles) and 25.8 (MCDropout) on ResNet-34 CIFAR-10. Against conformal prediction (Angelopoulos et al., 2021, Table 4), REL-U achieves substantially lower FPR in most settings (e.g., 18.3 vs. 31.6 on DenseNet-121 CIFAR-10 CrossEntropy), though conformal prediction's guarantees are a different objective. REL-U's advantage is most pronounced when the base model is well-calibrated and trained with standard cross-entropy or RegMixUp, while it shows smaller or no gains under LogitNorm where the base model accuracy drops.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Rao's (1982) diversity coefficient—originally defined with a fixed distance function—by making the pairwise class distance matrix D learnable from positive (correctly classified) and negative (misclassified) instances via a contrastive objective. It explicitly positions against entropy-based detection methods (Shannon entropy, Gini coefficient/Doctor, MSP, ODIN), arguing that these are invariant to label relabeling and fundamentally fail on overconfident misclassifications. The paper shows that Doctor's Gini-based score is a special case of its framework (Hamming distance), and that the learned D* matrix captures class-specific confusion patterns (e.g., dog↔cat) that no distribution-level scalar can express.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The closed-form solution to the constrained optimization makes REL-U practically attractive: no iterative training is needed, only a single pass over a validation set to compute D*. The method's robustness to calibration (unlike Doctor, which benefits substantially from temperature scaling on ImageNet) and its consistent advantage under distribution shift (CIFAR-10 vs CIFAR-100, CIFAR-10 vs CIFAR-10C) suggest it captures structural confusion patterns rather than surface-level confidence. Open questions include vulnerability to targeted adversarial attacks, the minimum number of tuning samples needed for reliable D* estimation, and whether the framework extends naturally to multi-class rejection or sequential decision settings.
