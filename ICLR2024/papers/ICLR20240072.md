## Problem

Document understanding research is constrained by datasets that are predominantly sourced from PDFs with narrow document type coverage, failing to capture the heterogeneity of real-world scanned or photographed documents. Additionally, no principled method exists for discovering a document taxonomy in an open-ended way, as pre-defining categories based on human knowledge is impractical given the vast diversity of document forms. ADOPD addresses both gaps by introducing a data-driven taxonomy discovery pipeline and a densely annotated, multi-lingual dataset of 120K diverse document images.

## Input / Output

The input is a high-resolution document page image (sourced from Laion-HR, multilingual, with English and CJK documents), processed at 1024×1024 resolution via re-scaling and padding. The output is a four-tuple per image: class-agnostic entity segmentation masks (polygon format, capturing visually coherent regions like banners, figures, logos), text region-of-interest bounding boxes (grouped by semantic meaning, MSCOCO format), a natural-language caption, and two levels of semantic tags (global document-level and local region-level). Entity masks are deliberately open-ended in naming (e.g., 'doc-in-doc', 'infographic') to avoid imposing rigid semantic constraints.

## Method

The core contribution is an iterative, data-driven taxonomy discovery loop (Alg. 1) that alternates between balanced in-distribution sampling (K-means clustering of the current taxonomy into 100 clusters, then CLIP-based zero-shot selection per cluster) and out-of-distribution outlier detection (Euclidean distance to nearest K-means centroid over accumulated image embeddings). LLMs (GPT-4) are prompted with four modality-specific strategies (visual, textual, layout, multimodal) to predict new labels and generate captions, with a prompt-based summarizer consolidating outputs into 10 tags per image. Human-in-the-loop annotators validate and clean labels at each iteration, and a CropFormer model is iteratively retrained on accumulated annotations to reduce labeling cost. For evaluation, the paper benchmarks Mask2Former/CropFormer (Doc2Mask), Faster R-CNN/Deformable-DETR/Cascade MR-CNN with DINOv2+ViT-Adapter backbones (Doc2Box), ViT+GPT-2 encoder-decoder (Doc2Seq), and CLIP ViT-G/14 (Doc2Tag), demonstrating cross-dataset generalization to DocLayNet and RVL-CDIP.

## Results

On Doc2Mask, CropFormer with ViTL backbone pretrained on SA-1B achieves the best mAP of 39.56 (AP50: 45.72, AP75: 40.33, mAR: 42.4). On Doc2Box, Cascade MR-CNN with DINOv2-P14 + ViT-Adapter backbone achieves the highest mAP of 67.0 (AP50: 82.7, AP75: 73.2, mAR: 77.8). For Doc2Seq, GPT-4 attains a CIDEr score of 84.7, while the best fine-tuned model (ViTBase-P16-384+GPT2) reaches B@1 of 23.4 and CIDEr of 11.0. On RVL-CDIP per-class classification, CLIP ViT-G/14 fine-tuned with ADOPD data improves average accuracy from 86.57% to 91.38% over the standard supervised baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For Doc2Mask, CropFormer consistently outperforms Mask2Former across all backbone/pretraining combinations (e.g., 39.56 vs 34.39 mAP with HornetL+EntitySeg), attributed to its use of image crops alongside full-image input. For Doc2Box, Cascade MR-CNN with DINOv2-P14+ViT-Adapter (67.0 mAP) surpasses both Deformable-DETR (65.5 mAP) and Faster R-CNN (61.4 mAP), though Deformable-DETR achieves higher mAR (81.6 vs 77.8). The critical dataset ablation (Table 4a) shows ADOPD pretraining dramatically improves Doc2Mask over EntitySeg-only pretraining: CropFormer SwinL jumps from 13.46 to 36.03 mAP, and Mask2Former SwinL from 16.27 to 32.81 mAP. On cross-dataset DocLayNet evaluation, ADOPD-pretrained Cascade MR-CNN achieves 88.9% mAR after fine-tuning (vs 88.1% for ImageNet-pretrained), while zero-shot ADOPD models already reach 80.4% mAR. For RVL-CDIP classification, CLIP ViT-G/14+ADOPD supervised training improves average accuracy to 91.38% over 86.57% for standard CLIP, though zero-shot performance slightly decreases (77.73% vs 79.58% Recall@5). BLIP and BLIP2 baselines on Doc2Seq achieve CIDEr scores of 18.3 and 16.5 respectively, far below GPT-4's 84.7, highlighting the gap between general VLMs and document-specific captioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior document layout datasets (PubLayNet, DocBank, DocLayNet, M6Doc) that rely on pre-defined, narrow taxonomies (1–7 categories) sourced primarily from PDFs, by introducing a data-driven taxonomy discovery pipeline that leverages CLIP, LLMs, and K-means-based OOD detection to iteratively expand document categories to over 1,000 types. Rather than building on a single prior method, it repurposes existing models (CropFormer, Mask2Former, DINOv2, BLIP) as annotation assistants and evaluation baselines, while the core novelty lies in the collection methodology—combining human-in-the-loop curation with model-assisted pseudo-labeling from Laion-HR web images. The multi-task decomposition framing (Doc2Mask, Doc2Box, Doc2Seq, Doc2Tag) unifies previously separate document subtasks under a single dataset, extending the scope beyond the bounding-box-only annotations dominant in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ADOPD addresses a critical bottleneck in document understanding: the homogeneity of existing datasets (mostly English PDFs with limited layout types) that biases models away from real-world diversity. The data-driven taxonomy discovery approach—using LLMs for label prediction, K-means OOD scoring for outlier selection, and HITL for validation—offers a scalable template for constructing future datasets in other domains. Key limitations include the 120K scale (smaller than PubLayNet's 360K or DocBank's 500K), the reliance on web-scraped images of variable quality, and the acknowledged gap between LLM-generated and human-written captions that complicates Doc2Seq evaluation. Open questions include whether the taxonomy discovery process converges to a stable set of categories, how well the multi-lingual (CJK) annotations generalize, and whether the class-agnostic entity segmentation formulation is sufficient for downstream structured document understanding.
