## Problem

Existing inductive knowledge graph methods can generalize to unseen entities but require a fixed relation vocabulary, preventing transfer to KGs with entirely different relation sets (e.g., training on Freebase and inferring on Wikidata). The fully-inductive setting—where both entities and relations at inference are unseen—remains largely unsolved, blocking the pre-train-and-fine-tune paradigm that has proven successful in language and vision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241243/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ULTRA consumes only the structural topology of a multi-relational KG (no entity or relation input features, no text descriptions) plus a query triple (h, q, ?). It outputs a scalar score p(h, q, v) for every candidate tail entity v in the full entity set, enabling ranking-based link prediction. Because no graph-specific embeddings are learned, the same pre-trained weights can be applied to any KG of any size (1k–120k nodes, 5k–1M edges) with an arbitrary relation vocabulary in a single forward pass.

## Method

ULTRA builds transferable relation representations through a three-step pipeline: (1) it lifts the original graph to a 'graph of relations' whose nodes are relation types and whose edges encode four fundamental interactions (tail-to-head, head-to-head, head-to-tail, tail-to-tail) computed via sparse matrix multiplication; (2) it applies a labeling-trick GNN (GNN_r, a 6-layer DistMult message-passing network) on this relation graph, initializing the query relation node with an all-ones vector to obtain conditional, relative relation representations R_q that require no input features; (3) it feeds R_q as starting relation features into an entity-level NBFNet (GNN_e) that performs standard inductive link prediction. The only learnable parameters are embeddings of the four fundamental interactions, linear update layers, and a 2-layer MLP per GNN_e layer (~177k parameters total), making the model graph-agnostic and enabling zero-shot transfer to any unseen KG. Pre-training on a mixture of three KGs (FB15k-237, WN18RR, CoDEx-Medium) with binary cross-entropy over positive/negative triplets yields a single checkpoint that generalizes inductively to 50+ diverse graphs.

## Results

Pre-trained on only 3 KGs (FB15k-237, WN18RR, CoDEx-Medium), ULTRA achieves a zero-shot average MRR of 0.395 and Hits@10 of 0.556 across 40 unseen graphs, outperforming the aggregate Supervised SOTA (0.344 MRR / 0.486 H@10) by ~15% in MRR. On the 27 inductive (new entity and/or relation) graphs, zero-shot ULTRA reaches 0.435 MRR vs. 0.342 for supervised baselines, with the largest gains (~3×) on small inductive graphs like FB-25 and FB-50. Fine-tuning for 1000–2000 steps further boosts the total average to 0.422 MRR / 0.592 H@10, surpassing supervised SOTA even on transductive graphs (0.379 vs. 0.348 MRR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ULTRA is compared against the best published supervised SOTA per dataset, which varies across 13 different model families (NBFNet, ComplEx RP, A*Net, RED-GNN, NodePiece, InGram, LR-GCN, DacKGR, ComplEx-NNE+AER, BiQUE, RotatE, INDIGO/GraL, and MTDEA-NBFNet). On the 27 inductive graphs (new entities and/or relations), zero-shot ULTRA decisively outperforms every baseline: e.g., on FB-25 it achieves 0.388 MRR vs. InGram's 0.133, on ILPC Small 0.302 vs. NodePiece's 0.130, and on NELL V1 0.785 vs. RED-GNN's 0.637. On the 13 transductive graphs, zero-shot ULTRA trails the SOTA on larger graphs (e.g., ConceptNet100k: 0.082 vs. BiQUE's 0.320; AristoV4: 0.182 vs. ComplEx RP's 0.311) due to size generalization, but fine-tuning closes or reverses the gap (ConceptNet100k: 0.310; AristoV4: 0.343). On the 3 pre-training graphs, fine-tuned ULTRA matches or exceeds the SOTA (WN18RR: 0.480 vs. NBFNet's 0.551; CoDEx-Medium: 0.372 vs. ComplEx RP's 0.352). Overall, the single pre-trained ULTRA model (177k parameters) beats the aggregate of dataset-specific models (often 10M+ parameters) on average, demonstrating that transferable relational-structure representations can substitute for graph-specific memorization.

## Relation to Prior Work

ULTRA builds on the NBFNet message-passing architecture and the labeling trick for symmetry-breaking, but departs from prior inductive methods (GraIL, NBFNet, NodePiece, RED-GNN) that require a fixed relation vocabulary by introducing a graph-of-relations layer with conditional representations. It extends the graph-of-relations idea from InGram (Lee et al., 2023) by replacing InGram's degree-discretization featurization—which only transfers to similar relational distributions—with a labeling-trick GNN that produces query-conditioned relation representations requiring no input features. Unlike ISDEA/MTDEA's double-equivariance framework, ULTRA achieves full inductive transfer (new entities AND relations) with only a one-step pre-computation overhead, making it compatible with any off-the-shelf inductive link predictor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first credible demonstration that a single small model (177k parameters) pre-trained on three graphs can zero-shot transfer to 50+ unseen KGs with entirely different relation vocabularies and outperform graph-specific supervised SOTA on average, establishing a viable pre-train/fine-tune paradigm for KG reasoning. Key limitations include underperformance on large transductive graphs (size generalization), the finding that adding more pre-training graphs does not consistently improve results (suggesting model capacity or optimization bottlenecks), and the absence of input features that could further boost performance. Open questions center on scaling laws for GNNs, whether larger models with more diverse pre-training data can close the gap on large transductive graphs, and how to incorporate heterogeneous features (text, numerical) in a transferable way.
