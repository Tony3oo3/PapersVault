## Problem

Existing handcrafted graph stream summarization structures (TCM, GSS, Auxo) suffer severe hash collisions and unacceptable query errors under small memory budgets (below 100KB), which are the most common deployment constraints. No neural data structure had been designed for graph streams, leaving the structural information (connectivity between nodes) unexploited compared to flat data stream sketches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242056/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The Mayfly consumes a one-pass stream of edges, each specified by an origin node ID, destination node ID, and incremental weight, and writes them into a fixed-size 2D storage matrix M of dimensions l_s × l_s × 2l_n (e.g., 32×32×16 for 64KB). Queries are answered by reading from M via learned addresses and decoding; the same stored matrix supports edge weight estimation, node in/out weight sums, binary connectivity labels, path flow values, and subgraph aggregated weights without retraining—only the decoding pathway changes per query type.

## Method

The Mayfly is a memory-augmented network with three modules—Representation (MLP encoding node IDs into bidirectional vectors), Addressing (SparseMax over a learnable matrix to produce sparse 2D row/column addresses simulating differentiable hashing), and Decoding (MLP with a MinGain term that extracts low-magnitude bits from stacked memory noise). Storage is additive: each streaming edge's concatenated origin/destination representation vector, scaled by its weight, is written to the addressed slot in M. Training uses one-shot meta-learning in two phases: a larval phase on synthetic streams with varied Zipf distributions (α ∈ [0.3, 0.8]) to learn general summarization, followed by a short metamorphosis phase on real stream segments to adapt to the target distribution. Extending to new query types (node, connectivity, path, subgraph) requires only constructing a new information pathway through M and training a lightweight decoding module, leaving all other parameters frozen.

## Results

On Lkml (B=64KB, stream length 800K), the fully-adapted Mayfly (Imago) achieves an AAE of 9.62, compared to 110.56 for TCM — an 11.5× reduction. On the billion-scale Twitter dataset (B=0.5GB, 10M edges), Imago attains an AAE of 8.50 and ARE of 2.18%, versus TCM's AAE of 245.25 and ARE of 63.02%. Even the zero-shot Subimago (larval-phase only) outperforms TCM on all tested configurations, e.g., AAE of 77.07 vs. 110.56 on Lkml at 800K length. On heavy-edge queries (top 5%/10%), Imago achieves AAE ≈ 14.5 on Enron (B=64KB, 400K) while GSS and TCM yield 23.9 and 55.6 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Mayfly against two handcrafted SOTA baselines: TCM (Tang et al., SIGMOD 2016) and GSS (Gou et al., IEEE TKDE 2022). Imago (fully-adapted Mayfly) dominates TCM across all datasets, budgets, and stream lengths — e.g., on Twitter (B=0.5GB, 10M edges) Imago achieves AAE 8.50 vs. TCM's 245.25 (≈29× lower), and on Lkml (B=64KB, 800K) AAE 9.62 vs. 110.56 (≈11.5× lower). GSS is particularly problematic under tight budgets: its hit rate drops below 20% in all tested cases (vs. 100% for TCM and Imago), making direct comparison infeasible for low-weight edges. On heavy-edge queries where GSS can still respond, Imago still wins: AAE ≈ 14.5 vs. GSS ≈ 23.9 and TCM ≈ 55.6 on Enron (B=64KB, 400K). Notably, even the zero-shot Subimago (trained only on synthetic larval tasks, no real-data adaptation) outperforms TCM once stream length exceeds 200K, demonstrating strong generalization without dataset-specific tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

Mayfly extends the neural data structure lineage initiated by Neural Bloom Filters (Rae et al., 2019) and Meta-Sketch (Cao et al., 2023) into the graph stream domain, where prior neural approaches did not account for graph structural information (node-edge relationships). Architecturally, it builds on memory-augmented networks (Neural Turing Machines, Graves et al., 2014/2016) for its storage matrix and content-based read mechanism, and on one-shot meta-learning (Vinyals et al., 2016; Santoro et al., 2016) for its training paradigm. The key departure from both NBF/MS and from handcrafted graph stream sketches (TCM, GSS, Auxo) is the joint node-edge storage paradigm in a 2D memory matrix combined with configurable information pathways that enable multiple query types without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first neural data structure for graph stream summarization, opening a new direction that could replace handcrafted sketches in low-budget streaming analytics (social networks, cybersecurity, network traffic). The information pathway concept—where query type is determined by how memory is read rather than by retraining—has potential applicability beyond graph streams to other multi-query summarization settings. However, practical adoption is constrained by GPU dependency for competitive throughput, the need for a metamorphosis phase on real data, and the fact that baselines (TCM, GSS) are somewhat limited in scope; GSS in particular suffers from <20% hit rates under tight budgets, making direct comparison difficult. Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly massive graphs without the hash-partitioning workaround used for Twitter, and whether the meta-learning training cost is justified over simpler learned-index alternatives.
