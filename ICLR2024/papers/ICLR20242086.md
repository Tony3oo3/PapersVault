## Problem

Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) produces over-smoothed, over-saturated results with limited fine detail because its loss inadvertently distills a noisy residual term (δ_N − ε) that requires a large CFG scale (e.g., s=100) to suppress, which itself causes over-saturation. The paper identifies this noise distillation as the root cause and provides a principled decomposition of the diffusion score that explains why SDS behaves this way and how to fix it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242086/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt and optimizes a differentiable parametric image generator—either a NeRF (for 3D output, optimized over 25,000 iterations with resolution ramping from 64×64 to 512×512) or a 64×64×4 latent tensor (for 2D image output, optimized for 1,000 iterations). The pretrained Stable Diffusion 2.1-base model serves as the score function throughout, and the method operates with a nominal CFG scale of 7.5 rather than the 100 typically required by SDS.

## Method

The paper decomposes the CFG score into three interpretable components: a condition direction δ_C (steering toward the prompt), a domain correction δ_D (steering from rendered/OOD images toward the real-image distribution), and a denoising direction δ_N (removing added noise). The key insight is that SDS's subtraction of the added noise ε leaves a noisy residual δ_N − ε that causes over-smoothing, forcing reliance on large CFG to make δ_C dominate. NFSD eliminates this residual by approximating δ_D separately: for t < 200, δ_D ≈ ε_φ(z_t; ∅, t) (since δ_N is negligible at low noise levels); for t ≥ 200, δ_D ≈ ε_φ(z_t; ∅, t) − ε_φ(z_t; y=p_neg, t) using a negative prompt describing unrealistic/blurry qualities. The resulting NFSD loss uses only δ_D + s·δ_C, removing the noisy term entirely and enabling high-quality distillation at the standard CFG scale of 7.5 with no additional computational cost.

## Results

On COCO2014 (5K samples), NFSD achieves a FID of 35.18 compared to SDS-100's 39.04, confirming better image quality. In a user study with 15 prompts, NFSD attains the highest scores for both prompt alignment (65.51%) and image quality (70.14%), outperforming all four competing text-to-3D methods. Qualitatively, NFSD produces sharper, more detailed NeRFs and 2D images using a standard CFG scale of 7.5, whereas SDS requires CFG=100 to achieve comparable (but still inferior) results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NFSD outperforms all five compared methods. Against DreamFusion (SDS), NFSD improves FID from 39.04 to 35.18 and dominates the user study on both prompt alignment (65.51% vs 46.09%) and image quality (70.14% vs 3.19%), with the gap driven by NFSD's elimination of the noisy δ_N−ε residual that causes over-smoothing. Against ProlificDreamer (VSD), NFSD achieves comparable or better visual quality (70.14% vs 37.68% image quality) while avoiding the substantial computational overhead of fine-tuning an additional LoRA diffusion model during optimization. Against Magic3D and Fantasia3D, NFSD leads on both user-study metrics (65.51% vs 42.03% and 61.74% for prompt alignment; 70.14% vs 17.75% and 27.83% for image quality). Against Latent-NeRF, the comparison is qualitative (Figures 9, 16), where NFSD produces higher-resolution outputs with more intricate surface details. Notably, NFSD achieves these gains with a nominal CFG of 7.5, whereas SDS requires CFG=100, and the paper demonstrates that the high CFG in SDS is a workaround for the undesired noise distillation rather than a fundamental requirement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DreamFusion's Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) loss, reinterpreting the CFG-guided score gradient through a three-way decomposition into condition (δ_C), domain correction (δ_D), and denoising (δ_N) components. This decomposition provides a unified theoretical lens that explains why SDS requires large CFG values (to suppress the noisy δ_N − ε residual) and simultaneously rationalizes subsequent improvements: VSD (ProlificDreamer) implicitly cancels δ_N by fine-tuning a second model, while DDS cancels both δ_N and δ_D by subtracting two SDS terms. NFSD's departure is minimal in implementation—replacing the full CFG score minus noise with an explicit δ_D approximation plus s·δ_C—but this small change eliminates the need for the large CFG scale that causes over-smoothing and over-saturation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The score decomposition offers a clean, interpretable framework that unifies several previously ad-hoc improvements to SDS, giving practitioners a principled understanding of which gradient components matter and which are harmful. Practically, NFSD achieves quality comparable to VSD without the computational overhead of fine-tuning a second diffusion model, making it an attractive drop-in replacement for existing text-to-3D pipelines. However, the Janus (multi-face) problem and limited diversity remain unresolved, and the paper acknowledges the absence of standardized evaluation metrics for text-to-NeRF generation, leaving open questions about how to systematically measure and improve 3D generation quality beyond visual inspection.
