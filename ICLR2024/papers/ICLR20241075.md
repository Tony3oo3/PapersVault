## Problem

Neural networks scale to rich, high-dimensional data but lack principled uncertainty quantification and struggle with sequential learning (catastrophic forgetting, inability to incorporate new data without retraining). Gaussian processes offer these capabilities but fail to scale to large or complex data sets. Previous NN-to-GP conversion methods (Khan et al., 2019; Immer et al., 2021) rely on subset approximations that discard information from most training points and require full retraining to incorporate new data, limiting their applicability to real-world sequential learning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a fully trained neural network (at its MAP weights), a set of M inducing points sampled from the training inputs, and optionally a batch of new data points for dual updates. It produces a sparse GP posterior parameterized by two compact objects: a vector α_u ∈ ℝ^M and a matrix B_u ∈ ℝ^{M×M}, from which predictive means and variances are computed in O(M³) time. For continual learning, it additionally outputs a memory buffer of per-task tuples (Z_s, u_s, B̄_s^{-1}) that summarize all prior tasks without retaining raw data. The kernel is the Neural Tangent Kernel computed via Jacobian-vector products of the trained network, so no separate kernel hyperparameters need to be tuned.

## Method

SFR converts a trained NN into a sparse GP by (1) linearizing the network around its MAP weights with a zero-mean formulation, yielding the NTK as the kernel, and (2) applying the Laplace approximation in function space (rather than weight space) to obtain dual parameters α_i and β_i from the first and second derivatives of the negative log-likelihood at each training point. The key innovation is projecting these per-point dual parameters onto M inducing points via kernel-weighted sums (Eq. 13), producing sparse dual parameters that capture contributions from all N training points while reducing complexity from O(N³) to O(M³) without any variational inference or second optimization loop. For continual learning, the sparse dual parameters define a Mahalanobis-distance regularizer (Eq. 15–16) that keeps the current network's function values close to previous tasks' MAP predictions, with covariance structure that relaxes regularization where no prior data exists. For incorporating new data, SFR performs simple additive updates to α_u and B_u (Eq. 17) in O(N_new²M) time, avoiding any retraining of the underlying network.

## Results

On the continual learning benchmarks (single-head setting), SFR achieves 89.22% accuracy on Split-MNIST (40 pts/task), 94.19% on Split-MNIST (200 pts/task), 81.96% on Split-FashionMNIST, and 95.58% on Permuted-MNIST, outperforming or matching all function-space baselines. On Fashion-MNIST image classification, SFR (M=3200) achieves NLPD 0.29±0.02 and 91.74% accuracy, substantially outperforming the GP subset (NLPD 0.79) and Laplace GLM-Kron (NLPD 1.09). SFR's dual updates incorporate new data in 0.02–0.82 s versus 7.5–30.6 s for full retraining on UCI regression tasks, while on HouseElectric (1M+ points) SFR achieves NLPD −0.153 in 6129 s versus 0.024 in 11982 s for SVGP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFR decisively outperforms all weight-space regularization baselines (Online-EWC, SI, VCL) on Split-MNIST and Split-FMNIST, where those methods collapse to near-chance accuracy (~20%) while SFR maintains 89–94%. Among function-space methods, SFR beats DER by ~4 points on S-MNIST (89.22 vs 85.26 at 40 pts/task) and FROMP by ~14 points, while roughly tying DER on S-FMNIST (81.96 vs 82.03). On P-MNIST, SFR (95.58) is within 0.2 points of the best baseline S-FSVI (95.76) but avoids the heavy variational inference cost. For uncertainty quantification on FMNIST, SFR (NLPD 0.29) outperforms GP Predictive (0.47), GP Subset (0.79), and all Laplace variants (1.09–2.42), while matching their AUROC (~0.96–0.97). In the RL experiment, SFR's posterior sampling converges in fewer episodes than Laplace-GGN, NN ensembles, and DDPG, demonstrating superior sample efficiency under sparse rewards.

## Relation to Prior Work

SFR builds on the NN-to-GP conversion line (Khan et al., 2019; Immer et al., 2021b), which linearizes a trained network via the GGN and interprets the result as a GP. The key departure is the adoption of a dual parameterization (from Adam et al., 2021, originally for sparse variational GPs) instead of the standard GP posterior formulation, which sets the mean to the NN prediction. This dual form, combined with the zero-mean linearization of Eq. (9), allows SFR to sparsify via inducing points without a second optimization loop (unlike Ortega et al., 2023) and to incorporate new data via simple additive updates (following Chang et al., 2023). For CL, SFR's regularizer extends the sparse function-space VCL formulation of Chang et al. (2023) to the NN setting, replacing subset-based methods (DER, FROMP, S-FSVI) with a compact representation that encodes information from all training points.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SFR offers a principled bridge between the scalability of NNs and the sequential-learning utilities of GPs (uncertainty quantification, forgetting mitigation, incremental data incorporation) in a single framework. The dual parameterization is the central insight: it simultaneously enables sparsification without re-optimization, a CL regularizer that captures the full dataset at M inducing points, and O(N_new·M) dual updates for new data. Key limitations remain: the NTK-based kernel is only a local linearization around the MAP weights, so the model degrades after multiple dual updates; the authors report that dual updates did not improve predictive performance on image data, suggesting the NTK approximation is insufficient outside the training manifold; and there is no straightforward way to specify a prior covariance function as in vanilla GPs. Open questions include whether the NTK can be made more accurate (e.g., via higher-order Taylor expansions) and whether the approach extends to non-i.i.d. or streaming settings.
