## Problem

Existing RLHF pipelines treat helpfulness and harmlessness as a single preference dimension, forcing crowdworkers to resolve an inherent tension between the two objectives and making it difficult to balance them during training. This conflation leads to biased annotations, suboptimal model behavior (e.g., over-refusal or over-compliance), and no principled mechanism for dynamically adjusting the trade-off as the model's safety level changes across training iterations.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes text prompts (user questions) and produces text responses from a fine-tuned 7B-parameter LLM policy. During training, the method requires two decoupled preference datasets: a helpfulness dataset with pairwise rankings and a harmlessness dataset with pairwise rankings plus binary safety labels (safe/unsafe) per response, annotated across 14 predefined harm categories. The Cost Model outputs a scalar cost value where zero serves as the decision boundary between safe and unsafe responses, enabling the Lagrangian constraint to be evaluated in expectation form.

## Method

Safe RLHF decouples human preference annotation into independent helpfulness and harmlessness rankings, then trains a standard Reward Model on the helpfulness data and a novel Cost Model on the harmlessness data. The Cost Model augments the Bradley-Terry pairwise loss with a classification term that enforces a zero threshold: unsafe responses must yield positive cost values and safe responses negative ones, giving the model an interpretable safety boundary. The RL phase formulates alignment as a constrained optimization—maximizing expected reward subject to expected cost ≤ 0—and solves it via the Lagrangian method, where a multiplier λ is updated each step based on the moving-average cost, dynamically scaling the safety penalty. This replaces static reward-shaping weights with an adaptive mechanism that automatically reduces the safety penalty once the model satisfies the constraint, preventing over-optimization of harmlessness at the expense of helpfulness.

## Results

After three rounds of Safe RLHF fine-tuning on Alpaca-7B, the final model (Beaver-v3) achieves Elo score improvements of +244.91 (GPT-4) and +363.86 (Human) in helpfulness, and +268.31 (GPT-4) and +237.98 (Human) in harmlessness over the base model. The probability of harmful responses on the evaluation set drops from 53.08% (Alpaca-7B) to 2.45% (Beaver-v3). The unified Cost Model achieves 85.83% safety classification accuracy and 70.44% ranking accuracy, while the unified Reward Model reaches 73.95% ranking accuracy across all three iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against conventional RLHF (PPO with single-dimension annotation), Safe RLHF achieves a significantly larger improvement in harmlessness while PPO yields a notable gain in helpfulness; the decoupled annotation also improves inter-rater agreement (69.00% helpfulness, 66.53% safety vs. 61.65% for single-dimension) and raises the researcher approval rate from below 80% to above 90%. Against Reward Shaping with seven static coefficients (ν = 0.01 to 100), Safe RLHF outperforms all variants: extreme weights (ν = 5, 10, 100) over-optimize harmlessness at the expense of helpfulness, low weights (ν = 0.01, 0.5) over-optimize helpfulness at the expense of safety, and even moderate weights (ν = 1, 2) remain inferior to Safe RLHF's dynamic Lagrangian balancing. Against the CM-classifier baseline (using safety classifier logits as cost signals, following Glaese et al., 2022), Safe RLHF's Cost Model substantially outperforms in improving harmlessness, demonstrating that the integrated preference-plus-classification design is critical. Overall, Safe RLHF wins on the joint helpfulness-and-harmlessness objective across all three baselines, with the largest margin over Reward Shaping.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first formal integration of Safe RL (constrained MDPs, Chow et al. 2017) with the RLHF pipeline (Ouyang et al. 2022), addressing the helpfulness-harmlessness tension that prior alignment work (Bai et al. 2022a; Ganguli et al. 2022) handled through ad-hoc static weighting or separate training stages. It departs from conventional RLHF in two key ways: decoupling helpfulness and harmlessness during human annotation (following Ji et al. 2024b), and replacing the single reward model with a dual Reward Model + Cost Model architecture where the Cost Model embeds a classification term to establish a zero-threshold safety boundary. The Lagrangian dual formulation for dynamic balancing replaces the static Reward Shaping approach used in prior multi-objective LLM alignment (Glaese et al. 2022; Touvron et al. 2023b).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Safe RLHF provides a principled, automatically-adaptive mechanism for balancing safety and utility that avoids the manual hyperparameter tuning required by static weighting, making it more deployable across different safety thresholds. The Cost Model's dual role (preference ranking + binary safety classification) is a clean design that enables the dynamic constraint enforcement. Key limitations include restriction to single-turn 7B models, substantial financial cost (~$190K total), and reliance on the LLaMA-1/Alpaca base which predates Llama-2. Open questions include extension to multi-turn dialogue, additional preference dimensions beyond helpfulness/harmlessness, and whether the Lagrangian approach scales to larger models where the helpfulness-harmlessness tension may manifest differently.
