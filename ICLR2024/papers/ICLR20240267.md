## Problem

Augmentation-based self-supervised representation learning (SSRL) methods rely on transformation invariance assumptions that require domain-specific augmentations, making them inapplicable across modalities (e.g., color jittering fails on pathology images, rotation is meaningless for tabular data) and potentially unsafe in critical applications. Masking-based alternatives require specific backbone architectures like transformers. The paper asks whether high-quality representations can be learned without any augmentations or architectural constraints.

## Input / Output

The method accepts raw data in any modality—RGB images (e.g., 100×80 Kvasir endoscopy images, 32×32 CIFAR-10), multivariate time series (e.g., 3-axis accelerometer signals, EEG channels, patient monitoring streams), or tabular records (e.g., 27-feature particle physics events)—and produces a fixed-dimensional embedding z (typically 2048-dim for images, 64-dim for MIMIC-III, 256-dim for tabular). No augmentations are applied to the input; instead, the input is fed simultaneously to the encoder and to K randomly initialized projector networks that generate the reconstruction targets. The random projectors reuse the encoder's architecture but scaled down (e.g., 2-block CNN for a 3-block encoder, 2-layer MLP for a 4-layer encoder).

## Method

Learning From Randomness (LFR) trains an encoder f_θ by requiring simple predictors h_φ^(k) to reconstruct the outputs of K randomly initialized projector networks g^(k) from the learned representation z = f_θ(x). The divergence between projector outputs and predictor outputs is measured by a Batch-wise Barlow Twins (BBT) loss, which computes an m×m cosine similarity matrix across the batch (rather than the d×d matrix in standard Barlow Twins) and penalizes off-diagonal entries to enforce instance-level matching. Training follows an EM schedule: the encoder is updated for one step (E-step) while the predictors are updated for multiple steps (M-step) to approach their optimal solution given the current representation. To ensure the random projectors capture diverse features, K projectors are selected from N ≫ K randomly initialized candidates using a Fast Determinantal Point Process over batch-level behavioral signatures, with additional diversity encouraged through Beta-distribution weight initialization and frozen DropConnect.

## Results

LFR achieves the best self-supervised performance across 6 of 7 main datasets: HAR (93.1% acc), Epilepsy (97.9% acc), MIMIC-III (46.6 Cohen's Kappa), Income (85.2% acc), Theorem (51.6% acc), and Kvasir (74.9% acc). On HEPMASS, LFR (90.1%) is comparable to the best baselines (Autoencoder 90.7%, SCARF 90.1%). Notably, on the Income dataset LFR (85.2%) even outperforms the supervised baseline (81.5%), and on Kvasir it beats all augmentation-based methods by 2+ points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LFR outperforms the time-series-specific TS-TCC on HAR (93.1 vs 91.2) and Epilepsy (97.9 vs 97.6), and substantially on MIMIC-III (46.6 vs 38.5 Cohen's Kappa). Against tabular-specific baselines, LFR beats SCARF on Income (85.2 vs 84.2) and Theorem (51.6 vs 48.5), and STab on Theorem (51.6 vs 50.7), while matching SCARF on HEPMASS (90.1 vs 90.1). The domain-agnostic DACL is consistently outperformed by LFR across all seven datasets, with the largest gaps on MIMIC-III (46.6 vs 40.9) and Kvasir (74.9 vs 72.0). Augmentation-based SimSiam and SimCLR underperform LFR on all non-natural-image datasets; on Kvasir, LFR leads SimSiam by 2.3 points. On CIFAR-10 (Appendix D.6), where standard image augmentations are well-established, SimSiam (89.2%) and SimCLR (86.7%) substantially outperform LFR (64.3%), confirming that LFR's advantage is most pronounced in domains lacking reliable augmentation pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

LFR departs from the dominant augmentation-based SSRL paradigm (SimCLR, SimSiam, BYOL) and masking-based approaches (MAE, BERT) by proposing a pretext task that requires neither domain-specific augmentations nor particular backbone architectures. The loss function is a direct adaptation of Barlow Twins (Zbontar et al., 2021), modified from a d×d redundancy matrix to an m×m batch-wise cosine similarity matrix with an ensemble summation over multiple random projectors. The training procedure borrows the EM alternation strategy from BYOL/SimSiam predictor optimization, and the diversity selection of projectors repurposes Determinantal Point Processes from recommendation systems. Conceptually, LFR is closest to autoencoder-based SSRL in that it uses a reconstruction-style objective, but the targets are outputs of randomly initialized networks rather than the input data itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LFR addresses a practical gap: many critical application domains (medical imaging, tabular data, time series) lack well-established augmentation pipelines, and naive augmentations can produce unrealistic or unsafe examples. The paper demonstrates that augmentation-free SSRL can match or exceed domain-specific methods on their home datasets, which is practically valuable for practitioners without deep domain expertise. However, the authors acknowledge that for well-understood domains like natural images, augmentation-based methods (SimSiam: 89.2% vs LFR: 64.3% on CIFAR-10) still substantially outperform LFR, limiting its universal applicability. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large datasets (ImageNet-scale), whether the random projectors truly approximate 'arbitrary downstream tasks' in a principled sense, and the privacy/fairness risks of learning with minimal human oversight.
