## Problem

Existing image-to-3D methods rely on either 2D diffusion priors (high detail but poor 3D consistency, e.g., Janus artifacts) or 3D-aware priors (consistent geometry but oversimplified shapes due to small, low-quality 3D datasets). No prior work combines both types of priors to simultaneously achieve high generalizability and strong 3D coherence in single-image 3D reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241879/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single unposed RGB image as input, segments the foreground object with a Dense Prediction Transformer, and extracts a monocular depth map for regularization. It produces a textured 3D mesh via a two-stage pipeline: a coarse Instant-NGP NeRF rendered at 128×128, followed by a fine DMTet mesh rendered at 1024×1024, yielding high-resolution geometry with disentangled texture. The reference view is assumed to be frontal (90° elevation, 0° azimuth) with a fixed camera radius of 1.8 m and 40° FOV.

## Method

Magic123 introduces a joint 2D and 3D diffusion prior for novel-view guidance via score distillation sampling. The 2D prior (Stable Diffusion V1.5) is conditioned on a textual-inversion token representing the object, while the 3D prior (Zero-1-to-3) is conditioned on the reference image and novel-view camera poses; their SDS gradients are combined with a tunable trade-off parameter λ_2D (default 1.0) that controls the balance between imagination/detail and 3D consistency. The coarse stage optimizes an Instant-NGP NeRF at 128×128, and the fine stage initializes a DMTet differentiable mesh from the NeRF output and refines it at 1024×1024 with reduced prior weights to alleviate oversaturation. Both stages are additionally supervised by reference-view reconstruction loss, depth regularization, and normal smoothness, with identical hyperparameters across all examples.

## Results

Magic123 achieves top-1 performance across all three metrics (CLIP-Similarity, PSNR, LPIPS) on both the NeRF4 and RealFusion15 datasets. On NeRF4, it reaches 0.80 CLIP-Similarity, 24.62 PSNR, and 0.03 LPIPS; on RealFusion15, it reaches 0.82 CLIP-Similarity, 19.50 PSNR, and 0.10 LPIPS. The closest competitor is Zero-1-to-3 (the 3D prior embedded within Magic123), which scores 0.62/23.96/0.05 on NeRF4 and 0.75/19.49/0.11 on RealFusion15. The largest margin over all baselines appears in CLIP-Similarity (3D consistency), where Magic123 leads Zero-1-to-3 by 0.18 on NeRF4 and 0.07 on RealFusion15.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Magic123 outperforms all seven baselines on every metric across both datasets. The most meaningful comparison is against Zero-1-to-3 (the 3D prior used inside Magic123): Magic123 gains +0.18 CLIP-Similarity on NeRF4 (0.80 vs 0.62) and +0.07 on RealFusion15 (0.82 vs 0.75), while PSNR is nearly tied on RealFusion15 (19.50 vs 19.49) and slightly ahead on NeRF4 (24.62 vs 23.96). Against the 2D-prior-based methods (RealFusion, NeuralLift, 3DFuse), Magic123 shows large margins in CLIP-Similarity (e.g., 0.82 vs 0.67 for RealFusion on RealFusion15) and LPIPS (0.10 vs 0.14), confirming that the joint 2D+3D prior substantially improves 3D consistency over 2D-only guidance. Point-E and Shap-E, which are text-to-3D rather than image-to-3D methods, are far behind on all metrics (PSNR < 1.3), serving as lower-bound references. Qualitatively, Magic123 notably outperforms Zero-1-to-3 on complex/uncommon objects (dragon statue, colorful teapot) where the 3D prior alone produces oversimplified geometry, and outperforms RealFusion on all examples where 2D-only guidance causes Janus problems and merged geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

Magic123 sits at the intersection of two parallel lines of image-to-3D research: 2D-prior-based methods (DreamFusion, RealFusion, NeuralLift) that leverage large-scale text-to-image diffusion for generalizability, and 3D-prior-based methods (Zero-1-to-3, 3DGen) that use view-conditioned diffusion trained on 3D datasets for geometric consistency. The paper adopts the coarse-to-fine NeRF→DMTet pipeline from Magic3D and the SDS loss from DreamFusion, but its core contribution is a joint 2D+3D prior with a single trade-off parameter (λ_2D) that balances generalizability against 3D consistency—something neither prior line of work attempted. Textual inversion from RealFusion is retained to anchor texture to the reference image, and Zero-1-to-3 serves as the 3D prior backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The joint-prior formulation offers a practical, single-knob control (λ_2D) over the generalizability–consistency trade-off that has been a persistent tension in image-to-3D, and the fact that one fixed hyperparameter set works across all test objects without per-scene tuning is notable for reproducibility. However, the method still requires ~1 hour of per-object optimization on a 32 GB GPU, produces over-saturated textures (especially at 1024×1024 in the fine stage), and suffers boundary artifacts from segmentation errors—limitations the authors acknowledge but do not resolve. Open questions include whether replacing the NeRF stage with Gaussian Splatting or sampling fewer diffusion timesteps can cut runtime substantially, and whether the approach can be made feed-forward or more diverse via variational score distillation.
