## Problem

Existing diffusion-based methods for Bayesian inverse problems (DPS, DDRM) do not actually sample from the true posterior; they produce samples under the prior that are inconsistent with the observations, and SMCdiff is restricted to inpainting under an unrealistic reversibility assumption. This paper addresses the gap by providing the first provably consistent SMC sampler for general Bayesian linear inverse problems with denoising diffusion priors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241715/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy observation y ∈ R^{d_y}, a linear forward operator A ∈ R^{d_y × d_x} (decomposed via SVD into orthonormal basis V), a noise level σ_y ≥ 0, and a pre-trained denoising diffusion model as the prior p_0. It outputs N weighted particle samples approximating the full posterior p(x|y) in the original coordinate system. For the noisy case (σ_y > 0), the algorithm reduces the problem to a noiseless inverse problem at an intermediate diffusion time τ where the observation noise matches the diffusion noise level, then propagates particles back to time 0 via the backward kernel.

## Method

MCGdiff exploits the structure of the diffusion prior to define a sequence of intermediate linear inverse problems: at each diffusion step t, the distribution φ_t^y(x_t) ∝ p_t(x_t)·q̄_{t|0}(x̄_t|y) is a valid posterior for a noisy observation of X_t, and consecutive posteriors differ only slightly as the noise level decreases. An auxiliary particle filter (APF) samples from these intermediate posteriors, with proposal kernels that combine the backward diffusion transition p_t(x_t|x_{t+1}) with the forward diffusion density q̄_{t|0}(x̄_t|y) via Gaussian conjugation, yielding closed-form weights. For general linear operators, the method works in the SVD basis of A, where the observation model becomes diagonal, and each coordinate i has its own intermediate time τ_i. Unlike SMCdiff, MCGdiff does not require the forward and backward processes to be exact reverses, and it handles arbitrary linear observation models rather than only inpainting.

## Results

The primary quantitative evaluation uses the Sliced Wasserstein (SW) distance between algorithm-generated samples and the true posterior (obtained analytically for Gaussian Mixture priors or via NUTS for Funnel Mixture priors), reported as 95% confidence intervals over 20 seeds with 10⁴ samples per method. MCGdiff achieves the lowest SW distance in every tested configuration, outperforming DPS, DDRM, and RNVP on both the GM setting (d_x=800, d_y=1) and the FM setting (d_x=10, d_y=1). The paper further demonstrates that DPS and DDRM, when run with random initialization, generate a significant number of samples outside the support of the target posterior, whereas MCGdiff's empirical distribution converges to the true posterior as the number of particles increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCGdiff is compared against three baselines: DPS (Chung et al., ICLR 2023), DDRM (Kawar et al., NeurIPS 2022), and RNVP (Dinh et al., ICLR 2017). MCGdiff outperforms all three on the Sliced Wasserstein metric in every tested configuration (both GM and FM priors, noiseless and noisy settings). The paper provides a critical diagnostic: DPS and DDRM, despite using the same denoising diffusion prior, produce samples that follow the prior distribution rather than the posterior—many generated samples land in regions of very low likelihood, meaning they are not consistent with the observations y. This failure mode is absent in MCGdiff, whose SMC weighting by the forward-diffused observation density ensures samples are drawn from the correct posterior. RNVP, a normalizing flow baseline, also underperforms MCGdiff, though it does not exhibit the same systematic inconsistency with observations. The theoretical contribution reinforces the empirical gap: MCGdiff is the first provably consistent algorithm for conditional sampling from denoising diffusion posteriors, while SMCdiff (Trippe et al., 2023) requires an assumption (forward-backward process coincidence) that fails in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

MCGdiff sits at the intersection of sequential Monte Carlo (building on the auxiliary particle filter framework of Pitt & Shephard, 1999) and denoising diffusion models (using the backward process kernels of Song et al., 2021a). It departs from DDRM and DPS, which modify the backward process to incorporate observations, by instead weighting the backward process with the forward-diffused observation density via an SMC scheme. It also extends SMCdiff (Trippe et al., 2023) from the specific inpainting problem to general linear inverse problems, and crucially removes the requirement that forward and backward process marginals coincide—an assumption that fails in practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that widely-used methods (DDRM, DPS) produce samples from the prior rather than the true posterior, meaning they do not actually solve the Bayesian recovery problem—a finding with direct implications for practitioners relying on these methods for uncertainty quantification. MCGdiff provides the first provably consistent algorithm for conditional sampling from denoising diffusion posteriors, with a non-asymptotic KL bound. Open questions include computational scalability of the SMC particle count for high-dimensional imaging problems, and whether the approximate treatment of the noisy case (when forward/backward coincidence fails) introduces meaningful bias in practice.
