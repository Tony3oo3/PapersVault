## Problem

Most graph representation learning methods rely on neural message-passing architectures that cannot answer probabilistic queries, provide uncertainty estimates, or naturally marginalize over missing vertex attributes. While tractable probabilistic models like Sum-Product Networks exist for Euclidean data, no framework combines the inductive bias of graph structure with the tractable inference capabilities of probabilistic circuits for graph-structured data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240991/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes graphs with vertex attributes (categorical or continuous) where some attributes may be missing, and produces per-vertex posterior distributions over latent mixture components that serve as probabilistic embeddings. Unlike standard DGNs that require imputation before processing, GSPN directly marginalizes missing attributes by setting their distribution units to 1 during inference. For supervised tasks, a global readout aggregates vertex posteriors across all layers into a graph-level prediction, and the model can additionally answer counterfactual queries (e.g., how the pseudo log-likelihood changes if a vertex attribute is altered).

## Method

GSPN constructs, for each vertex v, a computational tree of height L induced by graph traversal, and associates each tree node with a valid SPN (typically a Naive Bayes model with a single categorical latent variable). The key architectural insight is that the mixture weights (sum unit parameters) of a parent SPN are computed as a permutation-invariant, learnable transformation—specifically a Switching Parent decomposition with a shared transition matrix—of the posterior probabilities of the child SPNs' sum units, mirroring message passing in DGNs but within a fully probabilistic framework. Training maximizes a pseudo log-likelihood (product of per-vertex conditional distributions) via backpropagation, with weight sharing across vertices at the same tree height. The framework supports probabilistic shortcut connections (convex combinations of emission parameters across layers) to mitigate vanishing gradients, and extends to supervised graph-level prediction by adding a root SPN whose prior is a learnable aggregation of all vertex posteriors across all layers.

## Results

In the scarce supervision setting (0.1% labeled data), GSPN_U+DS achieves MAE of 2.24±0.5 on benzene, 3.36±0.0 on ethanol, and 3.93±0.0 on uracil, consistently outperforming or matching GAE+DS and DGI+DS while GIN (fully supervised) is highly unstable (e.g., 41.4±45.6 MAE on benzene). On ogbg-molpcba, GSPN_U+DS reaches 4.00±0.5 AP, slightly below GIN's 4.12±0.2. For missing data, GSPN_U achieves NLL of 4.17±0.02 on benzene and 3.77±0.03 on ethanol, improving over the structure-agnostic GMM (4.31, 3.81) and the unimodal Gaussian (9.96, 5.72). In graph classification, GSPN_U+DS attains 90.5±1.1% on REDDIT-BINARY and 78.1±2.5% on COLLAB, ranking second overall on two of four datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GIN (Xu et al., 2019), GSPN_U+DS dramatically outperforms on all seven chemical regression tasks (e.g., 3.93 vs 19.7 MAE on uracil) but is slightly worse on ogbg-molpcba (4.00 vs 4.12 AP). GAE (Kipf & Welling, 2016) and DGI (Velickovic et al., 2019) are both unstable in the scarce supervision regime—GAE produces 233.3 MAE on salicylic acid and 387.7 on uracil, while DGI reaches 409.7 on uracil—whereas GSPN_U+DS remains stable and competitive across all tasks. In the missing-data experiment, GSPN_U beats the structure-agnostic GMM (Bishop, 2006) on 6 of 7 datasets (e.g., 4.17 vs 4.31 NLL on benzene), with the only exception being uracil where GMM edges ahead (3.11 vs 3.17). In graph classification, GSPN_U+DS outperforms DGCNN (Zhang et al., 2018), DiffPool (Ying et al., 2018), ECC (Simonovsky & Komodakis, 2017), and GraphSAGE (Hamilton et al., 2017) on all four datasets, and also beats CGMM (Bacciu et al., 2020), E-CGMM (Atzeni et al., 2021), and iCGMM (Castellana et al., 2022) on NCI1 and REDDIT-BINARY, though iCGMM retains a small edge on REDDIT-5K (55.6 vs 55.3) and COLLAB (78.9 vs 78.1). GIN remains the strongest single baseline on NCI1 (80.0 vs 76.6) and REDDIT-5K (56.1 vs 55.3) in the supervised graph classification setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

GSPN unifies two previously separate research threads: the tractable probabilistic modeling framework of Sum-Product Networks (SPNs) and Probabilistic Circuits, and the message-passing computational structure of Deep Graph Networks. The hierarchical composition of SPNs along graph-induced trees mirrors the layer-by-layer message passing of MPNNs, while the Switching Parent decomposition from mixed memory Markov models provides the probabilistic aggregation mechanism. This departs from the Contextual Graph Markov Model (CGMM) lineage, which trains incrementally layer-by-layer without global optimization, and from standard DGNs that lack any probabilistic semantics. The key architectural insight is that parent SPN parameters are learnable transformations of children's posterior mixing probabilities, creating an end-to-end differentiable probabilistic message-passing scheme.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GSPN provides the first fully probabilistic graph representation learning framework that can tractably answer marginal, conditional, and likelihood queries on graphs, enabling native handling of missing data through marginalization rather than ad-hoc imputation. This opens a path toward trustworthy graph ML systems with calibrated uncertainty, particularly valuable in domains like chemistry and medicine where labels are expensive and data is often incomplete. Key limitations include the pseudo-likelihood approximation (conditioning on potentially repeated vertex realizations in cyclic graphs), the restricted expressiveness of the mean-based aggregation (limiting graph distinguishability power), and the inability to model edge types. Open questions include whether richer aggregation schemes can recover the expressiveness of powerful DGNs while preserving tractability, and whether the pseudo-likelihood bias is practically significant.
