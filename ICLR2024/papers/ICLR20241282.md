## Problem

Most retrieval-augmented language models retrieve only short, contiguous text chunks, which limits their ability to represent and leverage large-scale discourse structure. This is especially problematic for thematic or multi-hop questions that require integrating knowledge from multiple, potentially distant parts of a document, such as understanding an entire book or synthesizing information across a full research paper.

## Input / Output

The system takes a text corpus (books, NLP papers, or medium-length passages) and a natural-language query. The corpus is preprocessed into a hierarchical tree of ~100-token leaf chunks and progressively higher-level summaries; at inference time, the query embedding is matched against all tree nodes simultaneously (collapsed tree) until a token budget (e.g., 2000 tokens, ~top-20 nodes) is filled. The retrieved multi-level context—mixing raw leaf text and intermediate summaries—is concatenated and passed to a reader LLM (GPT-3, GPT-4, or UnifiedQA) to produce a final answer.

## Method

RAPTOR recursively embeds (SBERT), clusters (GMMs with UMAP dimensionality reduction, BIC for model selection, and EM for parameter estimation), and summarizes (GPT-3.5-turbo) text chunks to build a tree from the bottom up, where each parent node is a language-model summary of its clustered children. A key design choice is soft clustering via GMMs, allowing a chunk to contribute to multiple parent summaries, and a two-step UMAP process (global then local n_neighbors) to capture both broad themes and fine-grained groupings. At query time, the collapsed tree strategy flattens all layers into a single pool and retrieves nodes by cosine similarity until a token threshold is reached, outperforming the layer-by-layer tree traversal because it adapts the granularity mix to each question. The full tree is essential: ablations show that querying only leaf nodes or only upper layers underperforms the full multi-level retrieval, confirming that both granular details and high-level summaries contribute to answer quality.

## Results

On QuALITY, RAPTOR+GPT-4 achieves 82.6% accuracy on the full test set and 76.2% on the hard subset, a 20.3-point absolute improvement over the previous best (CoLISA at 62.3%). On QASPER, RAPTOR+GPT-4 sets a new state-of-the-art F-1 Match of 55.7%, surpassing CoLT5 XL (53.9%) and LongT5 XL (53.1%). On NarrativeQA, RAPTOR+UnifiedQA achieves a new SOTA METEOR score of 19.1 (vs. 11.1 for the prior best Retriever+Reader), while also reaching ROUGE-L 30.8, BLEU-1 23.5, and BLEU-4 6.4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAPTOR consistently outperforms all retrieval baselines (BM25, DPR, SBERT) across every dataset and every reader model tested. On QASPER, RAPTOR beats DPR by 1.8–4.5 F-1 points and BM25 by 5.5–10.2 points depending on the LLM (GPT-3, GPT-4, UnifiedQA). On QuALITY, RAPTOR+GPT-4 (82.6%) surpasses CoLISA (62.3%) by 20.3 points and DPR+DeBERTaV3-large (55.4%) by 27.2 points; on the harder subset the gap over CoLISA is 21.5 points. On NarrativeQA, RAPTOR+UnifiedQA (METEOR 19.1) outperforms the recursively summarizing model of Wu et al. (2021) (METEOR 10.6) by 8.5 points and the Retriever+Reader system of Izacard & Grave (2022) (METEOR 11.1) by 8.0 points, though it trails the latter slightly on ROUGE-L (30.8 vs. 32.0). Against the older BiDAF (Kočisky et al., 2018) and BM25+BERT (Mou et al., 2020) baselines on NarrativeQA, RAPTOR leads by 24.6 and 15.3 ROUGE-L points respectively. On QASPER, RAPTOR+GPT-4 (55.7 F-1) exceeds CoLT5 XL (53.9) and LongT5 XL (53.1), establishing a new state-of-the-art.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAPTOR extends the retrieval-augmented generation framework (Lewis et al., 2020; Karpukhin et al., 2020) by replacing flat chunk retrieval with a hierarchically structured index. It departs from prior recursive summarization approaches (Wu et al., 2021; LlamaIndex) that group text by positional adjacency, instead using GMM-based semantic clustering to group chunks by topical similarity regardless of their location in the document. The combination of UMAP dimensionality reduction, BIC-selected GMM clustering, and LLM-based summarization at each level is the paper's core architectural contribution, positioned as a solution to the 'lost in the middle' problem where flat retrieval of short contiguous chunks fails on thematic or multi-hop questions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RAPTOR addresses a practical and well-motivated limitation of standard RAG systems: the inability to retrieve context at multiple levels of abstraction simultaneously, which is critical for questions requiring holistic document understanding. The linear scaling in both build time and token cost makes it feasible for large corpora, and the technique has seen broad adoption in the RAG ecosystem. Open questions include the 4% summary hallucination rate (though it does not propagate), the dependence on a specific LLM (GPT-3.5-turbo) for summarization, and whether the GMM clustering assumption holds well for highly heterogeneous or non-English corpora. The modest controlled improvements (2–5 points with the same reader) suggest the gains are most pronounced when paired with strong generative models.
