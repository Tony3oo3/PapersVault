## Problem

Exact Bayesian posterior sampling for diffusion models in linear inverse problems is intractable, and existing approximate methods either accumulate uncontrolled errors over successive sampling iterations or require training additional neural networks per task. No prior approach provides a global asymptotic consistency guarantee for general linear inverse problems while remaining computationally efficient and training-free.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240474/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy linear measurement y = Ax + n (with known operator A and noise level σ), a pretrained score-based diffusion model, and a tunable particle count M, and produces a sample from the posterior p(x|y). All experiments use 256×256×3 RGB images with N=1000 diffusion steps; the particle size M controls the compute-quality trade-off (M=1 gives FPS, M=20 is the default for FPS-SMC). The method is fully zero-shot—no retraining or task-specific optimization is required.

## Method

The core insight is that Bayesian posterior sampling in a diffusion model is equivalent to a reverse-time Bayesian filtering problem, established by constructing a measurement sequence {y_k} that shares the same noise as {x_k} through the linear operator A. At each backward step, the conditional posterior p_θ(x_{k-1}|x_k, y_{k-1}) is the product of two Gaussians (the diffusion transition and the measurement likelihood), yielding a closed-form Gaussian that can be sampled directly. The FPS-SMC variant extends this to M particles with a tailored proposal distribution that incorporates the measurement, reducing variance compared to standard particle filtering; the resampling weights simplify to p_θ(y_k|x_{k+1}). Under perfect score estimation and vanishing time steps, FPS-SMC is proven to converge weakly to the true posterior as M→∞, providing a global consistency guarantee for diffusion posterior sampling on general linear inverse problems.

## Results

On FFHQ 256×256, FPS achieves the best FID across four of five tasks: Super Resolution (26.66), Inpainting-box (26.13), Gaussian Deblur (30.03), and Motion Deblur (26.18), substantially outperforming DPS (39.35, 33.12, 44.05, 39.92 respectively). On ImageNet 256×256, FPS similarly leads in FID for Super Resolution (47.32), Inpainting-box (33.19), Gaussian Deblur (54.41), and Motion Deblur (52.22). The sole exception is Inpainting-random (92% pixel masking), where DPS achieves the best FID (21.19 on FFHQ, 35.87 on ImageNet) while FPS is second-best (35.21, 42.68). FPS-SMC (M=20 particles) matches or slightly improves FPS on most metrics, confirming the theoretical consistency guarantee.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FPS outperforms DPS (Chung et al., 2022a) on four of five FFHQ tasks in FID (e.g., 26.66 vs. 39.35 on Super Resolution, 26.13 vs. 33.12 on Inpainting-box), but DPS wins on the extreme Inpainting-random task (FID 21.19 vs. 35.21), where 92% of pixels are masked. Against DDRM (Kawar et al., 2022), FPS achieves substantially lower FID across all four shared tasks (e.g., 26.66 vs. 62.15 on Super Resolution, 30.03 vs. 74.92 on Gaussian Deblur). MCG (Cardoso et al., 2023) is consistently outperformed by FPS on every reported task, with FID gaps of 10–60 points (e.g., 26.13 vs. 40.11 on Inpainting-box). PnP-ADMM (Chan et al., 2016) and Score-SDE (Song et al., 2020b) are both dominated by FPS across all tasks, with PnP-ADMM showing particularly poor FID on Inpainting-box (151.9 vs. 26.13). The same pattern holds on ImageNet, where FPS leads in FID on Super Resolution (47.32 vs. 50.66 DPS), Inpainting-box (33.19 vs. 38.82 DPS), Gaussian Deblur (54.41 vs. 62.72 DPS), and Motion Deblur (52.22 vs. 56.08 DPS), while DPS again wins Inpainting-random (35.87 vs. 42.68).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes Bayesian posterior sampling in diffusion models as a reverse-time Bayesian filtering problem, a conceptual bridge not previously drawn in the diffusion inverse-problems literature. It builds directly on sequential Monte Carlo / particle filtering machinery (Doucet et al., 2001) and adopts the shared-noise construction between the data and measurement sequences from Song et al. (2021). Unlike projection-type methods (DDRM, DPS) that enforce data consistency heuristically at each step, or score-approximation methods (DPS, SNIPS) that estimate the posterior score, FPS derives an exact Gaussian conditional at each filtering step, eliminating the need for score-gradient approximations or additional training. The paper positions itself alongside concurrent Monte Carlo diffusion samplers (Trippe et al. 2022, Wu et al. 2023, Cardoso et al. 2023) but distinguishes itself by constructing an explicit {y_k} sequence that makes all transition probabilities tractable, enabling a variance-reduced proposal distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The filtering equivalence provides the first (along with concurrent works) global asymptotic consistency guarantee for diffusion posterior sampling on general linear inverse problems, addressing a long-standing theoretical gap where prior methods lacked end-to-end error bounds. Practically, FPS requires no retraining, no optimization, and integrates with any Markovian sampler (DDPM, DDIM), making it a drop-in replacement that scales compute via particle count. Key limitations include restriction to linear inverse problems (the {y_k} construction is intractable for nonlinear operators) and the absence of finite-sample convergence bounds, which the authors flag as future work. The modest empirical gap between M=1 (FPS) and M=20 (FPS-SMC) on perceptual metrics (FID, LPIPS) suggests the single-particle approximation is already near-optimal for practical image tasks, raising the question of when the full SMC machinery is truly necessary.
