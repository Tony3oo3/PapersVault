## Problem

Existing LLM pruning methods (SparseGPT, Wanda) minimize layer-wise reconstruction error and apply a uniform sparsity rate across all layers. This leads to two issues: pruning errors accumulate layer by layer, degrading model output, and uniform sparsity ignores the fact that layers contribute unequally to final performance, risking removal of critical weights. The paper asks whether sparsity can be allocated per-layer in a differentiable, blockwise manner to reduce overall performance degradation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes frozen pre-trained LLM weights and a small calibration set (128 sequences × 2048 tokens from C4) to compute activation statistics for weight importance sorting. It outputs binary pruning masks for every linear projection in the model, where each layer receives its own learned sparsity rate that collectively satisfies a target global sparsity (e.g., 50%). Only a tiny set of learnable parameters (D=100 combination coefficients per row or per layer) is optimized; the original weights remain frozen throughout.

## Method

BESA replaces layer-wise reconstruction with a blockwise objective that minimizes the Frobenius-norm difference between dense and pruned transformer block outputs, plus an ℓ₂ sparsity penalty enforcing the target sparsity. To make per-layer sparsity differentiable without learning millions of binary masks, it parameterizes each layer's sparsity as a convex combination α = Σ β_d · p_d over D candidate rates, where β_d lives on a simplex. Weights within each row are pre-sorted by Wanda's importance metric (|W_ij|·‖x_j‖₂), and the element-wise pruning probability P(W_i,ĵ) is derived from the β coefficients, encoding the prior that less important weights are pruned first. A straight-through estimator provides gradients through the binary mask, allowing simple gradient descent to optimize β_d per layer. The blockwise sequential pipeline (process one transformer block, then the next) keeps GPU memory bounded, enabling pruning of 7B–180B models on a single A100-80GB in ~5 hours. The framework also jointly optimizes with OmniQuant-style 4-bit min-max quantization by substituting quantized weights into the same blockwise reconstruction loss.

## Results

At 50% unstructured sparsity, BESA achieves WikiText2 perplexity of 6.86 on LLaMA-7B, 4.33 on LLaMA-65B, and 4.09 on LLaMA2-70B, consistently outperforming both SparseGPT and Wanda across all seven model sizes and three perplexity benchmarks (WikiText2, C4, PTB). On zero-shot common-sense tasks (PIQA, BoolQ, HellaSwag, WinoGrande, ARC-e, ARC-c), BESA achieves the highest average accuracy among all pruning methods, and for LLaMA-65B even slightly exceeds the dense model (73.73 vs. 73.71). The full pruning of LLaMA-65B completes in 4.5 hours on a single A100-80GB GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BESA is compared against two layer-wise pruning baselines: SparseGPT (Hessian-based importance with layer reconstruction) and Wanda (weight×activation magnitude importance). BESA wins on every model size and every perplexity benchmark. The advantage is most pronounced on smaller models: on LLaMA-7B WikiText2, BESA achieves 6.86 ppl versus 7.22 (SparseGPT) and 7.26 (Wanda), a gap of ~0.36–0.40 ppl; on LLaMA2-7B the gap widens to 6.60 vs. 6.99/6.92. On PTB the improvement is even larger (e.g., LLaMA2-7B: 44.09 vs. 108.71 for SparseGPT). On zero-shot tasks, BESA's average accuracy exceeds both baselines across all seven models, and for LLaMA-65B it even marginally surpasses the unpruned dense model (73.73 vs. 73.71). The paper attributes these gains to block-wise (rather than layer-wise) error minimization and differentiable per-layer sparsity allocation, which prevents error accumulation and avoids uniformly pruning critical layers.

## Relation to Prior Work

BESA departs from the layer-wise pruning paradigm established by SparseGPT and Wanda, which minimize per-layer reconstruction error with a uniform sparsity rate across all layers. It adopts Wanda's weight-activation importance metric (|W|·|x|₂) as the sorting criterion and OmniQuant's learnable min-max quantization for joint compression, but introduces a fundamentally different optimization structure: block-wise reconstruction loss with differentiable, per-layer sparsity allocation via learnable simplex coefficients. This connects to adaptive sparsity allocation work (STR, LATS) in vision, but BESA is the first to make sparsity a differentiable variable in the LLM pruning setting, avoiding the extensive retraining those methods require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BESA provides a practical, single-GPU pipeline (5 hours for LLaMA2-70B on one A100) that consistently outperforms SparseGPT and Wanda across all tested model sizes and benchmarks, with the added benefit of jointly optimizing pruning and quantization. However, the unstructured sparsity it produces does not naturally map to the n:m structured patterns that enable cuSPARSELt acceleration on NVIDIA GPUs, and the speedup evidence relies on a ViTCoD simulator rather than real hardware. Open questions include whether the block-wise differentiable allocation generalizes to structured pruning (channel/row-level), whether it extends to multimodal or non-LLM architectures, and whether the moderate perplexity gains (typically 0.2–0.5 ppl) translate to meaningful downstream task improvements at higher sparsity levels (70%+).
