## Problem

GAN-based training for text generation has historically been incompatible with modern Transformer architectures, as prior work relied on RNNs or produced only sparse sequence-level loss signals that led to unstable training and model collapse. This left open-ended text generation with LLMs unable to benefit from adversarial training, despite GANs offering a principled solution to exposure bias and repetitive generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240025/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes 128-token sequences from The Pile and generates K=32 branching continuations of depth d=16 tokens each, all processed in parallel via a branching self-attention schema. At inference, the model takes a 32-token context and produces a 96-token continuation using greedy decoding (matching the training-time sampling), with an optional repetition penalty of 1.2 and beam size 8. The branching mechanism means each forward pass simultaneously advances all K sequences by one token, so total compute scales with depth d rather than with K×d.

## Method

Branch-GAN introduces a branching self-attention schema where K generated sequences share a single forward pass: each token in a branch attends to all preceding tokens in the original sequence plus all preceding tokens in its own branch, enabling massively parallel generation of dense per-token reward signals. The generator is trained with a combined objective: standard causal language modelling (MLE) loss on the original sequence plus a four-case RL loss on the branching tokens, where the loss magnitude is modulated by the discriminator's dual value heads (I_n estimating the next token's score if from the original text, B_n if from a generated branch). The discriminator makes three predictions per timestep—a discriminative score D_n and two value-head estimates—and is trained with binary cross-entropy on both the discriminative and value-head predictions. This dual value head design replaces the unstable REINFORCE gradient estimation with a dense, per-token advantage signal that the authors found eliminated generator collapse entirely. The entire setup is applied to pre-trained Pythia checkpoints using less than 0.01% of the original Pile training data (12.8M tokens over 4 epochs).

## Results

In the human evaluation (Table 1), Branch-GAN 1B* (greedy) achieves 68.7% on Wikipedia and 76.0% on Fictional Stories when compared to original texts, substantially outperforming Pythia 6.9B* (top-p) at 24.0% and 27.3% respectively — a model with over 16× more parameters. Branch-GAN 410M (greedy) reaches 49.7% (Wiki) and 51.7% (Fiction), still far above all Pythia baselines. On automatic metrics (Table 3), Branch-GAN 1B* achieves a TTR of 73.8 and Self-BLEU of 1.4, closely matching original texts (TTR 71.8, Self-BLEU 2.4), while Pythia baselines show TTR of 36.5–45.6 and Self-BLEU of 3.9–4.0, indicating severe repetitiveness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Pythia baselines (Biderman et al., 2023), Branch-GAN achieves dramatic gains: Branch-GAN 410M (greedy) scores 49.7% on Wikipedia versus Pythia 6.9B* (top-p) at 24.0%, a 25.7-point advantage despite Pythia 6.9B having ~17× more parameters. On Fiction, the gap widens to 51.7% vs. 27.3%. Against Llama-2 7B (Touvron et al., 2023), Branch-GAN 1B* (greedy) leads by 36.7 points on Wikipedia (68.7% vs. 32.0%) and 37.3 points on Fiction (76.0% vs. 38.7%). Against GPT-4 (OpenAI, 2023), Branch-GAN 1B* (greedy) trails by 12.3 points on Wikipedia (68.7% vs. 81.0%) but narrows the gap to 2.7 points on Fiction (76.0% vs. 73.3%), where GPT-4 actually underperforms Branch-GAN. In the head-to-head comparison against Branch-GAN 1B* itself, GPT-4 still wins (88.3% on Wiki, 86.7% on Fiction), confirming it remains the strongest baseline overall.

## Relation to Prior Work

Branch-GAN extends the line of GAN-based language modeling (SeqGAN, ColdGANs, cooperative GANs) by making the adversarial training loop compatible with Transformer parallelism through a branching self-attention scheme that generates K short continuations per sample in d forward passes. It departs from prior RNN-based approaches that relied on sequential discriminators for dense rewards, instead achieving density through parallel branching. The dual value-head RL loss (Equation 1) is a novel stabilization mechanism that replaces the REINFORCE-style gradient estimation used in earlier work, which the authors found caused generator collapse. The method also inherits the MLE-plus-adversarial hybrid training paradigm from Che et al. (2017) and Guo et al. (2018) but applies it in a setting where the generator and discriminator are both auto-regressive Transformers initialized from the same pre-trained checkpoint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is that a 410M-parameter Branch-GAN model outperforms a 6.9B Pythia baseline (16× more parameters) in human evaluation, suggesting adversarial fine-tuning could be a highly data-efficient path to improving generation quality without scaling model size. The paper also provides evidence that perplexity on held-out data is a misleading proxy for perceived text quality, a point with broad implications for how the community evaluates open-ended generation. However, the evidence base is limited: experiments use small models (≤1B), a single training corpus (Pile, <0.01% of tokens), 16-token branching depth, and only 100 evaluation contexts per domain. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 7B+ models, whether it works without MLE pre-training, and whether the perplexity-quality dissociation persists at larger scale or with more diverse training data.
