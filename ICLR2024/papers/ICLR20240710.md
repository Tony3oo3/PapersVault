## Problem

Traditional path planning relies on explicit optimization objectives (e.g., shortest distance) and search-based algorithms (A*, Dijkstra) that assume linearly accumulative edge costs, failing to capture complex real-world user intentions. Existing data-driven approaches either generate paths unconditionally (inapplicable to conditional OD-pair planning) or still depend on search-based frameworks as a performance bottleneck. This paper addresses the gap by proposing the first end-to-end, search-free, data-driven path planning method that directly learns and samples from the distribution of real-world paths.

## Input / Output

The method takes an origin vertex, a destination vertex, and an undirected road network graph (~2,000–2,700 vertices) as input, augmented with Node2Vec vertex embeddings and geo-coordinate-based spatial features (distance to destination, direction cosine). It outputs a planned path as a vertex sequence whose length is sampled from a fitted Gaussian mixture model; connectivity is enforced via a beam search post-processing step over the denoised vertex distributions. The planning is iterative: the model predicts one vertex at a time, appending it to the prefix, until the destination is reached or a maximum length is hit.

## Method

GDP reformulates path planning as conditional sampling p̃(x) = p_θ(x)·h(x|ori,dst), where p_θ is a diffusion-based unconditional path generator and h is a self-attention sequence model encoding OD-pair and spatial context. The key architectural novelty is a graph-constrained categorical diffusion process whose transition matrix C_τ = e^{(A−D)τ} is derived from the heat conduction PDE on the graph, providing locality at small τ (probability stays near the original vertex) and convergence to uniformity at large τ, unlike generic categorical diffusion that redistributes uniformly. Each vertex in a path is diffused independently, and a 1D U-net (with Node2Vec embeddings) models inter-vertex dependencies during the reverse denoising step. Training minimizes a combined loss of KL divergence between the model's and true reverse posteriors, an auxiliary cross-entropy term, and a connectivity penalty that encourages adjacent vertices to be neighbors in the graph. At inference, the conditional sampling loop alternates between the attention-based prior evidence model and the diffusion denoiser, with the window size doubling each iteration to amortize forward/reverse diffusion calls.

## Results

On the primary path planning task, GDP reduces DTW by 30.9% (City A) and 15.9% (City B) relative to Dijkstra's algorithm, while increasing LCS by 80.8% and 82.6% respectively. The closest competitor, Amap's Navigation API (Navi), achieves only −11.1% DTW improvement and 45.9% LCS improvement on City A. For unconditional path generation, GDP achieves the best NLL (24.697 on City A, 23.740 on City B), KLEV (5.724, 6.128), and JSEV (2.518, 2.724) across all metrics, outperforming the strongest sequence model CSSRNN by 11.0% on NLL (City A). The paper reports overall improvements of 14.2%–43.5% over strong baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GDP decisively outperforms Dijkstra's algorithm (DA) on both cities, reducing DTW from 2.434 km to 1.683 km (City A) and from 2.883 km to 2.426 km (City B), while nearly doubling LCS (16.730 vs. 9.254 on City A). NMLR (Jain et al., NeurIPS 2021) performs worst among planning baselines, with DTW 3.7× worse than Dijkstra (9.109 vs. 2.434 km on City A), confirming that Markov-property-based edge weighting is insufficient. Key Segment (KS, Tian et al., PVLDB 2023) is the second-best planning baseline but still trails GDP by 480 m (City A) and 366 m (City B) in DTW. For unconditional generation, GDP beats CSSRNN (Wu et al., IJCAI 2017) on NLL by 11.0% (24.697 vs. 27.742 on City A) and on KLEV by 22.1% (5.724 vs. 7.348), while MTNet (Wang et al., PVLDB 2022) is outperformed on NLL by 64.5% (24.697 vs. 69.859) and on JSEV by 10.0% (2.518 vs. 2.795). HMM (Yin et al., IEEE T-ITS 2018) is the weakest generation baseline, with NLL over 10× higher than GDP, reflecting the limitation of first-order Markov assumptions for capturing high-order path dependencies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of categorical diffusion models (Austin et al. 2021; Hoogeboom et al. 2021) and graph-structured heat conduction (Chamberlain et al. 2021), adapting the former's forward/reverse framework by replacing the generic transition matrix with a graph Laplacian exponential C_τ = e^{(A−D)τ}. For the conditional planning formulation, it follows Janner et al. (2022) in decomposing the conditional probability into an unconditional generative model times a prior evidence term. The key departure from prior data-driven path planning work (Jain et al. 2021; Tian et al. 2023; Wang et al. 2022a) is the complete elimination of the search-based framework: instead of learning edge weights and running A* or Dijkstra, GDP plans paths directly as conditional samples from a learned path distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By bypassing the search-based framework, GDP removes the linearly-accumulative cost assumption that has been a fundamental bottleneck for capturing non-linear, high-order path dependencies (e.g., non-linear battery discharge, scenic preferences). The practical limitation is that connectivity is not guaranteed by the generative process itself and requires a beam-search post-processing step, and the hit ratio, while high (94–99%), is not perfect. The authors explicitly flag designing a post-process-free algorithm as an open problem. Broader applicability is tempered by evaluation on only two datasets from a single source (Didi Gaia), leaving open questions about generalization to different road network topologies, scales, or domains like robotics.
