## Problem

Imputing missing values in tabular data with high fidelity and utility remains an open problem, as existing discriminative methods require specifying conditional distributions and generative methods (GAN/VAE-based) often need complete training data or assume specific missingness patterns. Masked autoencoding, a powerful self-supervised paradigm proven in language and vision, had not been adapted to the inherently incomplete nature of tabular imputation, where data is already partially masked before training begins.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240359/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an incomplete tabular dataset of n rows with d features (each continuous or categorical) along with a binary mask vector per row indicating which values are observed, and produces a fully imputed dataset where missing entries are replaced by predicted scalar values. The encoder consumes only the observed (unmasked) values via linear projection plus sinusoidal positional encoding, while the decoder receives mask tokens at missing positions and outputs per-feature scalar predictions. The reconstruction objective is MSE computed over both re-masked and unmasked positions, and the method operates without requiring any complete data during training.

## Method

REMASKER extends the MAE framework with a 're-masking' strategy: during training, in addition to naturally missing values, a random subset (e.g., 25–50%) of observed values is additionally masked out, and the model is trained to reconstruct only these re-masked values. The encoder is a deep Transformer (8 blocks, width 64) applied solely to unmasked values, while the shallow decoder (4 blocks) processes the concatenated sequence of observed embeddings and shared learnable mask tokens to predict all masked positions. This asymmetric encoder-decoder design, combined with the re-masking objective, provably encourages the encoder to learn missingness-invariant representations—empirically validated via CKA similarity between representations of complete and incomplete inputs. Unlike vision MAE, the reconstruction loss includes both re-masked and unmasked values, since tabular data lacks the spatial redundancy that makes unmasked-patch loss counterproductive in images.

## Results

On 12 UCI benchmark datasets under MAR with 0.3 missingness ratio, REMASKER consistently outperforms all 13 baselines in both imputation fidelity (RMSE, Wasserstein distance) and utility (AUROC). On the letter dataset, REMASKER achieves RMSE of 0.0611, WD of 0.0217, and AUROC of 0.7892 with its default configuration (encoder depth 8, decoder depth 4, embedding width 64). Performance degrades gracefully with increasing missingness: even at 0.7 missingness ratio, RMSE remains below 0.1 on letter. When used as the base imputer within HyperImpute's ensemble framework, REMASKER further improves performance (e.g., letter AUROC rises from 0.7899 to 0.7935).

## Baselines & Comparisons

REMASKER outperforms all 13 baselines (HyperImpute, MIWAE, EM, GAIN, ICE, MICE, MIRACLE, MissForest, Mean, Median, Frequent, Sinkhorn, SoftImpute) on at least one metric across all 12 datasets, and is the only method to lead on all three metrics (RMSE, WD, AUROC) simultaneously on most datasets. HyperImpute is the closest competitor, as it is an ensemble method that automatically selects the best imputer per column; REMASKER's single-model Transformer backbone achieves comparable modeling capacity. GAN-based methods (GAIN) and VAE-based methods (MIWAE) are consistently outperformed, particularly at higher missingness ratios where their training instability and variational bound limitations become more pronounced. Iterative discriminative methods (MissForest, MICE, MIRACLE, ICE) struggle with the intricate inter-feature correlations that the Transformer's self-attention captures. Notably, REMASKER's performance advantage over baselines grows with the missingness ratio, and it performs best under MCAR, slightly less under MAR, and least under MNAR, consistent with the distributional disparity between re-masked and truly missing values.

## Relation to Prior Work

REMASKER extends the masked autoencoding (MAE) framework—established in vision (He et al., 2022) and language (Devlin et al., 2018)—to the tabular data imputation domain, where prior work (MET, Majmundar et al., 2022) applied MAE only under the assumption of complete data. The key conceptual departure is the 're-masking' strategy: rather than treating missing values as a special case, the method randomly masks additional observed values during training, creating a unified reconstruction objective that naturally accounts for incompleteness. The Transformer backbone builds on prior tabular Transformer work (TabTransformer, Huang et al., 2020; SAINT, Somepalli et al., 2021), but the paper positions itself as the first to combine extended MAE with Transformer specifically for imputation, distinguishing it from both discriminative imputers (MissForest, MICE, MIRACLE) and generative imputers (GAIN, MIWAE, HI-VAE) that model conditional or joint distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple self-supervised masked modeling approach can match or exceed 13 diverse imputation methods across 12 heterogeneous datasets, with the advantage growing as missingness increases (up to 70%), suggesting masked modeling is a viable and scalable paradigm for tabular imputation. The theoretical analysis showing that re-masking encourages missingness-invariant representations (validated via CKA similarity) provides a principled explanation for why the method works. However, the MSE-based objective biases toward point estimates over distributional fidelity (visible in RMSE vs. WD trade-offs), and the method is not optimized for downstream tasks, leaving open questions about task-aware imputation and handling MNAR scenarios where the distributional gap between re-masked and truly missing values is largest.
