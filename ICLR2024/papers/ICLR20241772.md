## Problem

Optimal competitive algorithms for the online knapsack problem (OKP) treat items inequitably based on their arrival time: two items with identical value density have different admission probabilities depending on when they arrive. This paper formalizes time fairness for OKP, proves that the natural time-independent fairness (TIF) notion is infeasible even with perfect predictions, and introduces a relaxed conditional fairness notion (α-CTIF) under which it derives Pareto-optimal trade-offs between fairness and competitiveness.

## Input / Output

The algorithm processes items arriving sequentially, each with a value v_j and weight w_j, making an irrevocable accept/reject decision at arrival time. The knapsack capacity is normalized to 1, and value densities are bounded in [L, U]. The decision at each step depends on the current knapsack utilization z_j (fraction of capacity filled) through a threshold function. For the learning-augmented variant LA-ECT, a single scalar prediction d̂_γ is obtained by querying a black-box predictor with γ/2, representing the predicted critical value density threshold from the offline optimal solution.

## Method

The paper introduces α-conditional time-independent fairness (α-CTIF), which requires that admission probability depends only on value density (not arrival time) for items arriving while utilization falls within a subinterval of length α. The ECT algorithm uses a threshold function Ψ^α(z) that is constant at L over the fair utilization region [0, α] and then jumps to an exponential form Ue^{β(z−1)} beyond it, where β is the Lambert W solution matching the lower bound. This discontinuity makes the algorithm maximally selective outside the fair region, achieving the Pareto-optimal β-competitiveness. The learning-augmented LA-ECT replaces the constant fair-region threshold with the predicted critical value d̂_γ, yielding a three-piece threshold function that is 2/γ-consistent and (ln(U/L)+1)/(1−γ)-robust, with a near-optimality lower bound showing no 1-CTIF algorithm can simultaneously improve both guarantees.

## Results

In the no-prediction setting (U/L ∈ {100, 500, 2500}), ECT[α=0.66] outperforms the baseline α-CTIF algorithm by an average of 20.9% in empirical competitive ratio across all experiments, while ZCL-Randomized—despite being theoretically optimal (ln(U/L)+1-competitive and 1-CTIF)—performs significantly worse than deterministic algorithms empirically. In the prediction setting (U/L=500), LA-ECT[γ] with perfect predictions (σ=0) is nearly 1-competitive, substantially outperforming both ZCL and ECT. With increasing prediction error (σ=1/2, σ=1), LA-ECT[γ=0.33] maintains a worst-case competitive ratio roughly on par with ECT while performing better than both ZCL and ECT on average across all error regimes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares ECT and LA-ECT against ZCL (Zhou et al., 2008), ZCL-Randomized, and a simple baseline α-CTIF algorithm. ECT[α] wins against the baseline algorithm by 20.9% average improvement in competitive ratio at α=0.66, and matches or beats ZCL in empirical performance while providing explicit fairness guarantees that ZCL lacks (ZCL is only 1/(ln(U/L)+1)-CTIF). ZCL-Randomized, despite being theoretically optimal in both competitiveness and fairness (1-CTIF), loses empirically to all deterministic algorithms due to the high probability of sampling a poor threshold in its one-shot randomization. In the learning-augmented setting, LA-ECT[γ] with perfect predictions nearly matches OPT (≈1-competitive), decisively beating both ZCL and ECT. Under prediction errors, LA-ECT[γ=0.33] ties ECT in worst-case competitive ratio while winning on average, and LA-ECT[γ=1] degrades to unbounded competitive ratio as error increases, confirming the theoretical robustness bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the online knapsack problem (OKP) by introducing a time-fairness constraint, drawing on the time-independent fairness (TIF) notion from Arsenis and Kleinberg (2022) for prophet inequalities and Buchbinder et al. (2009) for secretary problems. It builds directly on the optimal ZCL threshold-based algorithm of Zhou et al. (2008) and the learning-augmented OKP framework of Im et al. (2021) using frequency predictions. The key conceptual departure is showing that TIF is infeasible for OKP (even with perfect predictions), motivating the relaxed α-conditional time-independent fairness (α-CTIF) notion, and then constructing a Pareto-optimal deterministic algorithm (ECT) whose threshold function uses a discontinuity to be more selective outside the fair region.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to formalize and study fairness in OKP under adversarial inputs, providing a clean impossibility result for the natural TIF notion and a tight Pareto-optimal trade-off between fairness and competitiveness via the Lambert W function. The practical relevance to cloud resource allocation (static vs. dynamic pricing) is concrete, and the learning-augmented LA-ECT algorithm shows that predictions can simultaneously improve both fairness and efficiency. Open questions include extending the fairness framework to other online problems (one-way trading, bin packing, single-leg revenue management) and exploring group fairness in OKP, which the authors identify as practically relevant but unaddressed.
