## Problem

Private stochastic optimization for nonsmooth nonconvex objectives has remained largely unexplored. Existing private nonconvex optimization results rely on smoothness assumptions to control sensitivity, and the differentially private counterpart of the recent breakthroughs in nonsmooth nonconvex optimization (e.g., O2NC, zeroth-order Goldstein stationarity) was entirely missing. This paper is the first to provide a zeroth-order algorithm that finds a Goldstein (δ,ε)-stationary point of a nonsmooth nonconvex objective while satisfying Rényi differential privacy.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a dataset of M data points and can only query function values f(x, z) (no gradient access). It outputs a (δ,ε)-stationary point of the objective F(x) = E_z[f(x,z)] in the Goldstein sense, while guaranteeing (α, αρ²/2)-Rényi DP for all α > 1. The data complexity is M = Õ(d/(δε³) + d^{3/2}/(ρδε²)), and a notable regime is that when ρ ≥ √d·ε, the privacy term is dominated by the non-private term, meaning privacy is achieved at no additional data cost.

## Method

The algorithm builds on the O2NC framework (Cutkosky et al., 2023) with projected Online Subgradient Descent as the OCO subroutine, applied to the uniform smoothing F̂_δ of the objective. The key technical contribution is a private variance-reduced gradient oracle that decomposes ∇F̂_δ(x_t) into an initial GRAD estimate plus a telescoping sum of DIFF estimates between successive iterates, each computed with d i.i.d. two-point zeroth-order samples per data point to achieve optimal O(d) dimension dependence. Because the OCO domain is bounded by D = δ/T, both estimators have sensitivity O(dL/T), and the tree mechanism (Dwork et al., 2010) is used to privately release the running sum with only Õ(d²L²/(ρ²T²)) noise per step—reducing noise by a factor of T² over naively adding Gaussian noise to a single zeroth-order gradient estimator. This combination of variance reduction, uniform smoothing, and the tree mechanism yields the state-of-the-art data complexity matching the non-private zeroth-order rate.

## Results

The paper's main result (Theorem 4.9) establishes that the proposed algorithm finds a (δ,ε)-stationary point with (α, αρ²/2)-Rényi DP using M = Õ(d(F*L²/(δε³) + L³/ε³) + d^(3/2)(F*L/(ρδε²) + L²/(ρε²))) data points. The non-private term O(dδ^(-1)ε^(-3)) matches the state-of-the-art rate of Kornowski & Shamir (2023) for zeroth-order nonsmooth nonconvex optimization. Notably, when ρ ≥ √d·ε, the privacy term is dominated by the non-private term, meaning privacy is achieved 'for free' without additional data cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental comparisons. In terms of theoretical rates, the non-private component O(dδ^(-1)ε^(-3)) matches the optimal rate of Kornowski & Shamir (2023), the current state-of-the-art for zeroth-order nonsmooth nonconvex optimization. The privacy overhead introduces an additional d^(3/2)ρ^(-1)δ^(-1)ε^(-2) term, which is strictly better than the naive approach of adding Gaussian noise directly to zeroth-order gradient estimators (which would yield an extra ε^(-1) factor, giving O(d^(3/2)ρ^(-1)δ^(-1)ε^(-3))). Compared to Arora et al. (2023) for private smooth nonconvex optimization, the present work extends the framework to the nonsmooth setting while maintaining the 'privacy for free' regime when ρ ≥ √d·ε. The paper does not provide empirical validation or head-to-head comparisons with prior algorithms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: the O2NC framework for nonsmooth nonconvex optimization (Cutkosky et al., 2023), optimal zeroth-order nonsmooth nonconvex optimization (Kornowski & Shamir, 2023), and the tree mechanism for continual-observation privacy (Dwork et al., 2010). The key departure from prior work is the design of a variance-reduced zeroth-order gradient oracle (combining a GRAD estimator and a DIFF estimator) whose reduced sensitivity, when paired with the tree mechanism, yields optimal privacy guarantees. This contrasts with the naive approach of adding Gaussian noise directly to a standard zeroth-order gradient estimator, which the paper shows incurs an extra ε^{-1} factor in data complexity. The paper is explicitly the first to address the joint setting of private, zeroth-order, nonsmooth, and nonconvex stochastic optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithm for private zeroth-order nonsmooth nonconvex optimization, filling a gap where prior private nonconvex results assumed smoothness and prior zeroth-order nonsmooth results lacked privacy guarantees. The 'privacy for free' regime (ρ ≥ √d·ε) is practically meaningful, indicating that for sufficiently strong privacy parameters the data complexity matches the non-private optimum. Open questions include whether the rate is optimal (no lower bounds are established), whether the requirement to sample d i.i.d. uniform vectors per data point can be eliminated, and whether efficient first-order private algorithms exist for the nonsmooth nonconvex setting. The purely theoretical nature (no experiments) limits immediate practical impact but provides a blueprint for future implementations.
