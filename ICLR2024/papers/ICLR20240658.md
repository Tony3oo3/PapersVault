## Problem

Current EEG deep learning models are designed for specific datasets and tasks, with small parameter counts that limit their perceptual capacity and cross-task generalizability. No unified large-scale EEG model exists that can handle the heterogeneity of real-world EEG data—mismatched electrode counts, variable recording lengths, diverse task designs, and low signal-to-noise ratio—while learning generic representations through unsupervised pre-training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240658/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts raw multi-channel EEG signals with arbitrary channel counts (any subset of the international 10-20 system) and arbitrary time lengths, segmented into fixed 200-sample (1-second) channel patches with a maximum sequence length of 256 patches. During pre-training, the output is a distribution over 8192 discrete neural tokens from a learned codebook; for downstream tasks, a task-specific head on the pooled Transformer output produces binary/multi-class labels or continuous regression values (e.g., joint angles).

## Method

LaBraM segments raw EEG into channel patches, encodes each patch with a 1D temporal convolutional encoder, and adds learnable temporal and spatial embeddings (indexed by 10-20 system positions) to handle heterogeneous electrode configurations. A vector-quantized neural tokenizer is first trained to map patches to discrete codebook entries by reconstructing the Fourier amplitude and phase spectrum (rather than raw signals, which fail to converge due to EEG's low SNR). Pre-training then uses BERT-style masked EEG modeling: a fraction of patches are replaced with a learnable mask token, and the Transformer encoder predicts the corresponding discrete neural tokens, with a symmetric masking strategy that reuses the same forward pass for the complementary mask to improve efficiency. The architecture incorporates scaled Transformer modifications (layer-norm on queries/keys, bias-free QKV) for stable training at scale, and three model sizes (5.8M–369M parameters) are pre-trained on over 2,500 hours of EEG from ~20 diverse datasets.

## Results

On TUAB (abnormal detection), LaBraM-Base (5.8M params) achieves 0.8140 Balanced Accuracy, 0.8965 AUC-PR, and 0.9022 AUROC, surpassing the strongest baseline BIOT (0.7959 / 0.8792 / 0.8815) by 1.8 / 1.7 / 2.1 points respectively. On the more challenging TUEV (6-class event type classification), LaBraM-Base achieves 0.6409 Balanced Accuracy and 0.6637 Cohen's Kappa, outperforming BIOT (0.5281 / 0.5273) by over 11 and 13 points. Scaling to LaBraM-Huge (369M) further improves TUAB AUROC to 0.9162 and TUEV Cohen's Kappa to 0.6745. On SEED-V (emotion recognition) and MoBI (gait prediction), LaBraM-Huge achieves 0.4102 accuracy and 0.3145 R² score, respectively, again leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaBraM-Base (5.8M) outperforms all six baselines on every metric across all four downstream tasks. The most dramatic gap is on TUEV, where LaBraM-Base's 0.6409 Balanced Accuracy exceeds the next-best BIOT (0.5281) by 11.3 points and the remaining baselines (SPaRCNet, ContraWR, CNN-Transformer, FFCL, ST-Transformer) by 20–24 points. On TUAB, LaBraM-Base leads BIOT by 1.8 points in Balanced Accuracy and 2.1 points in AUROC, with the gap widening to 3.0 / 3.5 points for LaBraM-Huge. On SEED-V, LaBraM-Base (0.4095 accuracy) beats BIOT (0.3802) by ~3 points, while on MoBI, LaBraM-Huge achieves the best Pearson correlation (0.5632) and R² (0.3145), edging out ST-Transformer (0.5442 / 0.2911) which was the strongest baseline on that regression task. Notably, all baselines are task-specific or small-scale models (0.79M–3.5M params), whereas LaBraM is a unified pre-trained model that handles heterogeneous channel counts and time lengths without architecture modification.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaBraM synthesizes three distinct research threads: the VQ-VAE codebook mechanism (Van Den Oord et al., 2017) adapted via neuro2vec's (Wu et al., 2022) Fourier spectrum prediction objective to create a discrete EEG tokenizer; BERT-style masked modeling (Devlin et al., 2018) repurposed as the pre-training objective over those discrete tokens; and ViT-style patch segmentation (Dosovitskiy et al., 2021) combined with learnable spatial embeddings to handle heterogeneous electrode configurations. Prior EEG self-supervised efforts—BENDR (contrastive), BrainBERT (masked SEEG spectrograms, 43.6 h), ContraWR (sleep staging), and BIOT (biosignal transformer)—were either task-specific, small-scale, or used continuous reconstruction. LaBraM departs by unifying heterogeneous multi-task EEG data (2,500+ h, ~20 datasets) under a single VQ-tokenized masked-modeling objective at up to 369M parameters, with Transformer modifications from Dehghani et al. (2023) for stable large-scale training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first EEG foundation model pre-trained at this scale, and the scaling-law observations are practically important: the Base model plateaus (or even degrades) with more data on TUAB, while the Huge model shows a clear upward trend, suggesting that 2,500 hours is far from sufficient and that larger models will benefit from orders-of-magnitude more EEG data. The channel-patch + spatial-embedding design provides a concrete recipe for handling the heterogeneity that has historically prevented cross-dataset EEG pre-training. Key open questions remain: whether emergent abilities appear at larger scale, whether parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA, adapters) can replace full fine-tuning, and whether the learned codebook can be aligned with natural language or other modalities to enable truly multimodal brain-signal understanding.
