## Problem

Standard conformal prediction provides coverage guarantees only under exchangeability, a condition frequently violated by time series, spatial data, and distribution drift. Concurrently, conformal risk control (CRC) extends guarantees to arbitrary monotone losses (e.g., F1-score, false negative rate) but still requires exchangeable data. No existing method could control the expected value of a general monotone loss when the data is non-exchangeable.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained model's scores on a calibration set {(x_i, y_i)}_{i=1}^n, a set of non-negative weights w_i ∈ [0,1] (which may be data-dependent, e.g., derived from embedding similarity to the test point), and a target risk level α ∈ [A,B]. It outputs a prediction set C_λ̂(X_{n+1}) = {y : s(X_{n+1}, y) ≤ λ̂} or interval [f(x)−λ̂, f(x)+λ̂], where λ̂ is selected by a weighted empirical risk threshold. The loss L(λ;(x,y)) must be nonincreasing in λ and bounded in [A,B] with A < B < ∞; the guarantee degrades gracefully by an additive (B−A)·Σw̃_i·d_TV(Z,Z^i) term.

## Method

The core result (Theorem 1) unifies non-exchangeable conformal prediction (Barber et al., 2023) with conformal risk control (Angelopoulos et al., 2023a) by replacing the uniform 1/n weighting in CRC with arbitrary data-independent weights w_i and a weighted empirical risk R̂_n(λ) = (1/N_w)Σw_i·L(λ;(x_i,y_i)), where N_w = Σw_i. The threshold λ̂ is chosen as inf{λ : (N_w/(N_w+1))·R̂_n(λ) + B/(N_w+1) ≤ α}, and the resulting guarantee is E[L(λ̂;(X_{n+1},Y_{n+1})] ≤ α + (B−A)·Σw̃_i·d_TV(Z,Z^i), where the (B−A) factor arises from a new TV-distance lemma bounding expectation differences of bounded functions. When data is exchangeable, d_TV = 0 and the bound recovers standard CRC exactly. For practical weight selection, the paper proposes a maximum-entropy regularized minimization of the coverage gap, which under a Lipschitz drift assumption yields exponential-decay weights w_i ∝ ρ^{n+1−i}; in the QA experiment, weights are instead set by sentence-embedding dot-product similarity between calibration and test questions.

## Results

On the synthetic multilabel classification task (α=0.2), non-X CRC achieves mean FNR of 0.196 and 0.182 under changepoints and distribution drift respectively, compared to 0.246 and 0.225 for standard CRC, which exceeds the target threshold in both non-exchangeable settings. Under i.i.d. data, both methods perform comparably (0.181 vs. 0.191 mean FNR). On the Natural Questions open-domain QA task (α=0.3), both methods achieve similar test risk (0.30 ± 0.015), but non-X CRC produces smaller prediction sets (23.0 ± 1.47 vs. 24.6 ± 1.83 average set size), indicating tighter uncertainty estimates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is standard Conformal Risk Control (CRC; Angelopoulos et al., 2023a), which assumes exchangeable data. On the synthetic multilabel classification task, non-X CRC clearly outperforms CRC in both non-exchangeable settings: under changepoints, CRC's mean FNR of 0.246 exceeds the α=0.2 target by 23%, while non-X CRC achieves 0.196; under distribution drift, CRC reaches 0.225 vs. non-X CRC's 0.182. Under i.i.d. data (Setting 1), both methods are comparable, with non-X CRC being slightly more conservative (0.181 vs. 0.191). On the ELEC2 electricity monitoring task, both non-exchangeable variants (non-X CRC and non-X CRC + WLS) maintain the desired loss threshold during distribution drift periods where standard CRC + LS underestimates λ̂ and exceeds the target. On Natural Questions QA, both methods achieve the same risk level (0.30 ± 0.015), but non-X CRC yields smaller prediction sets (23.0 vs. 24.6 average size), demonstrating that data-dependent weighting produces tighter uncertainty estimates without sacrificing risk control.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two parallel lines of conformal prediction research: non-exchangeable conformal prediction (Barber et al., 2023), which handles distribution drift via total variation distance bounds on coverage, and conformal risk control (Angelopoulos et al., 2023a), which extends guarantees from miscoverage to arbitrary monotone loss functions under exchangeability. The key theoretical contribution (Theorem 1) generalizes the TV-distance penalty from the coverage setting to the risk-control setting, yielding a bound on expected loss that degrades gracefully with non-exchangeability. Additionally, the paper provides a maximum entropy principle for selecting calibration weights, offering a principled justification for the exponential decay weights previously suggested heuristically by Barber et al.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework is practically relevant because real-world deployments (time series, spatial data, sequential decision-making) routinely violate exchangeability, and the natural loss of interest is often not miscoverage (e.g., F1-score in QA, false negative rate in medical screening). A key limitation is that the TV-distance term in the bound is typically unknown, so the guarantee is only tight when weights are chosen to downsample distant calibration points; estimating or bounding this term in general settings remains open. The paper also notes that the data-dependent weight relaxation (using embedding similarity in the QA experiment) technically violates the theorem's assumptions, leaving a gap between theory and practice that future work could close. Broader implications include providing a template for uncertainty quantification in LLMs and reinforcement learning, where i.i.d. assumptions are clearly violated.
