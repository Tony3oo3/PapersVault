## Problem

Despite growing interest in tabular deep learning, gradient-boosted decision trees (GBDT) remain the dominant solution, and prior retrieval-augmented tabular DL models (SAINT, NPT, etc.) provide only marginal gains over a well-tuned MLP while being significantly more complex and computationally expensive. The paper identifies that the specific design of the attention mechanism's similarity and value modules—often overlooked in prior work—is the critical factor determining whether a retrieval component actually helps on tabular data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240732/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

TabR takes heterogeneous tabular features (numerical, binary, categorical) as input and, crucially, requires access to the full training set (or a subset) as retrieval candidates at both training and inference time, making it a non-parametric-in-part model. The output is a class label (binary or multiclass) or a continuous regression value. At inference, the model retrieves the top-m (default m=96) nearest training objects by a learned key-space L2 distance and aggregates their label embeddings plus a correction term, so deployment requires storing key representations and labels for all training objects.

## Method

TabR is a feed-forward network (encoder E → predictor P) with a single-head, kNN-like retrieval module R inserted as a residual branch after E. The key architectural insight is a key-only L2-distance similarity module S(x̃, x̃ᵢ) = −‖W_K(x̃) − W_K(x̃ᵢ)‖² (no queries, no dot product, no d^{-1/2} scaling), which the authors show is essential—removing any of the three ingredients (context labels, key-only representation, L2 distance) collapses performance back to MLP level. The value module V(x̃, x̃ᵢ, yᵢ) = W_Y(yᵢ) + T(k − kᵢ) combines the context object's label embedding with a DNNR-inspired correction term that translates key-space differences into label-space adjustments. A 'context freeze' optimization (freezing retrieved neighbors after a few epochs) enables near-7× speedup on large datasets with minimal metric loss, and the model supports online updates by simply adding new training objects to the candidate pool without retraining.

## Results

On the default benchmark of 11 tabular datasets (ranging from 10K to 1.2M objects), TabR achieves the best average rank of 1.3±0.6 among all tabular DL models, outperforming MLP-PLR (2.0±1.0), NPT (3.6±1.0), and SAINT (3.8±1.5). On the Grinsztajn et al. (2022) benchmark of 43 middle-scale tasks (≤50K objects) originally designed to showcase GBDT superiority, TabR wins 26/43 tasks against XGBoost, 23/43 against CatBoost, and 26/43 against LightGBM — a notable milestone for tabular DL. The simpler TabR-S variant (no feature embeddings, linear encoder) still achieves average rank 1.9±0.7, beating MLP-PLR on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TabR decisively outperforms all prior retrieval-based tabular models: versus NPT (closest competitor), TabR improves CA RMSE from 0.474 to 0.400 and WE RMSE from 1.947 to 1.690; versus SAINT, TabR improves CA from 0.468 to 0.400 and WE from 1.933 to 1.690. Against non-retrieval baselines, TabR beats MLP on 9/11 datasets and MLP-PLR on 5 datasets (CA, OT, BL, WE, CO) while matching it on most others. Against GBDT (Table 4), tuned TabR outperforms XGBoost on 6/11 datasets (CH, CA, HO, HI, WE, CO) with average rank 1.3 vs 2.5, and the default TabR-S configuration is competitive with tuned GBDT (rank 2.0 vs 2.4–2.5). On the Grinsztajn benchmark (Table 5), TabR wins 26/43 tasks vs XGBoost (vs. only 12/43 for MLP-PLR and 6/43 for MLP), confirming that retrieval enables DL to surpass GBDT on middle-scale tabular tasks. TabR is also dramatically more efficient than prior retrieval models: training time on the OT dataset is 1h47m for TabR vs 10h45m for NPT and 5h37m for SAINT.

## Relation to Prior Work

TabR departs from prior retrieval-augmented tabular models (SAINT, NPT, ANP, DKL) that relied on multi-head vanilla self-attention over all training points, showing those designs yield only marginal gains over MLP at much higher computational cost. The architecture is conceptually rooted in kNN and DNNR (Differential Nearest Neighbors Regression), whose correction-term idea directly inspires the value module. The critical design departure is replacing the query-key dot product with a key-only L2 distance and incorporating context labels into the value, a combination the authors show was overlooked in prior work. The encoder/predictor backbone leverages embeddings for numerical features from Gorishniy et al. (2022), positioning TabR as a retrieval component layered on top of the strongest known parametric tabular DL baseline (MLP-PLR).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TabR marks a milestone for tabular deep learning by outperforming GBDT ensembles on the Grinsztajn et al. (2022) benchmark, which was originally constructed to demonstrate GBDT's superiority over DL on middle-scale tasks. The finding that a single-head, key-only L2-based attention module outperforms multi-head Transformer-style retrieval is a useful design principle for the broader retrieval-augmented ML community. Key open questions include scaling to tens or hundreds of millions of objects (the retrieval step remains a bottleneck), systematic study of continual/online updates, and whether the L2/key-only design generalizes beyond tabular data. Practical limitations include the need for careful candidate-set design in production (temporal constraints, related-object leakage) and the inherent overhead of storing and retrieving from training data.
