## Problem

Existing convergence guarantees for finding local minimax points in nonconvex-nonconcave minimax problems (e.g., two-timescale GDA) require the Hessian ∇²_yy f to be nondegenerate, excluding meaningful degenerate local optima that arise in over-parameterized settings. This paper asks whether a first-order method can provably converge to local minimax points without this nondegeneracy assumption, and whether it can avoid undesirable non-optimal stationary points.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a C² minimax objective f(x,y) with saddle-gradient F = (∇_x f, −∇_y f), a step size η, and a timescale parameter τ ≥ 1 that scales the x-update relative to the y-update. The analysis is conducted in both continuous-time (an ODE approximation of EG) and discrete-time (the iterative update), with the requirement that τ exceed a problem-dependent threshold τ* and η satisfy bounds such as η < 1/L (Lipschitz constant of DF) or η < (√5−1)/(2L) for the avoidance guarantee. The output is a local minimax point (x*, y*) satisfying the refined second-order necessary condition involving the restricted Schur complement.

## Method

The paper introduces a restricted Schur complement S_res that unifies second-order necessary and sufficient conditions for local minimax points even when ∇²_yy f is singular, and defines strict non-minimax points analogously to strict saddle points. A new spectral analysis (Theorem 4.3) characterizes the eigenvalues of the timescaled Jacobian H_τ = Λ_τ DF as τ→∞ without the nondegeneracy assumption, revealing a novel class of 2q eigenvalues scaling as ±iσ_j√ε. To resolve stability questions where tangential information is insufficient, the paper introduces hemicurvature ι_j = lim Re(1/λ_j), which captures the curvature of eigenvalue trajectories near the origin. Using a higher-order ODE approximation ż = −(I + sΛ_τ DF)^{−1}Λ_τ F, the paper shows via dynamical systems theory that two-timescale EG is strict linearly stable at points satisfying S_res ⪰ 0 and B ⪯ 0, and that it almost surely avoids strict non-minimax points by the stable manifold theorem. A negative result shows that two-timescale GDA, by contrast, almost surely avoids certain non-strict local minimax points where the hemicurvature is too small.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental benchmarks. The headline results are: (1) a refined second-order necessary condition for local minimax points via the restricted Schur complement (Proposition 3.2), valid even when ∇²_yy f is degenerate; (2) a complete spectral characterization of the timescaled matrix H_τ without the nondegeneracy assumption (Theorem 4.3), revealing a new class of O(√ε) eigenvalues; (3) proof that two-timescale EG is strict linearly stable at local minimax points satisfying the refined condition (Theorems 5.2, 5.6); and (4) proof that two-timescale EG almost surely avoids strict non-minimax points (Theorem 5.7), while two-timescale GDA provably avoids some non-strict local minimax points (Theorem 4.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is theoretical: two-timescale EG (this work) versus two-timescale GDA (Jin et al. 2020; Fiez & Ratliff 2021). Two-timescale GDA converges to strict local minimax points only under the nondegeneracy condition det(∇²_yy f) ≠ 0, and Theorem 4.4 shows it almost surely avoids non-strict local minimax points where the hemicurvature ι_j < η/2 (e.g., the bilinear f_b(x,y)=xy). In contrast, two-timescale EG converges to these same non-strict points (Examples 2–4) and requires only the weaker Assumption 2 (at least one of S_res or ∇²_yy f is nondegenerate). The paper also notes that prior two-timescale EG analyses (Zhang et al. 2022; Mahdavinia et al. 2022) showed behavior similar to GDA, whereas this work demonstrates the specific advantage of EG in the degenerate regime. No empirical baselines or quantitative benchmarks are provided.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the local convergence theory for minimax optimization initiated by Jin et al. (2020) and Fiez & Ratliff (2021), who showed that two-timescale GDA converges to strict local minimax points under a nondegeneracy condition on ∇²_yy f. The key departure is replacing GDA with extragradient (EG) and introducing the restricted Schur complement and hemicurvature to handle the degenerate case where ∇²_yy f is singular. The analysis draws on the dynamical systems framework of Lee et al. (2016, 2019) for gradient descent in minimization, adapting the strict-saddle-avoidance machinery to the minimax setting via a new notion of strict non-minimax points.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a gap between the second-order necessary and sufficient conditions for local minimax points by showing that two-timescale EG converges to a broader class of optima than previously known, including degenerate points relevant to over-parameterized training. The negative result that two-timescale GDA almost surely avoids certain non-strict local minimax points (Theorem 4.4) is practically significant: it explains why EG/optimistic methods outperform GDA in GAN training and adversarial robustness in degenerate regimes. Open questions include how large the timescale parameter τ must be, whether the gap between necessary and sufficient conditions can be fully closed, and whether the restrictive step-size conditions (s₀ < 1/L) can be removed.
