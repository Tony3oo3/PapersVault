## Problem

Existing unsupervised graph anomaly detection methods rely on local inconsistency mining (LIM), which assumes anomalies are dissimilar to their neighbors, but GNN message passing inherently smooths connected nodes toward similarity, directly conflicting with this intuition. Prior attempts to mitigate this conflict either discard message passing entirely (losing valuable structural anomaly signals) or use attention to block information flow, which only works for contextual anomalies and contradicts the cluster-like nature of structural anomalies. The paper addresses how to design an anomaly mining approach that seamlessly integrates with message passing in fully unsupervised settings.

## Input / Output

The method takes an attributed graph (adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{N×N} and node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×F}) and produces a per-node anomaly score vector S ∈ R^N, where higher values indicate greater abnormality. The score is computed as the average of two components: a local score from MLP-based contrastive learning and a global score from adaptive message passing against a learned normal context. Notably, the method requires no subgraph sampling at inference time and uses only MLPs (no GNNs), making it memory-efficient enough to handle ogbn-products (2.4M nodes, 61.8M edges) where most baselines run out of memory.

## Method

GADAM is a two-stage framework that decouples LIM from message passing. Stage 1 performs conflict-free LIM using an MLP encoder with L2 normalization, sampling-free contrastive pair construction (the full adjacency subgraph serves as the positive instance, a row-shuffled embedding as the negative), and a parameter-free cosine-similarity discriminator, yielding local anomaly scores directly from positive-pair similarity. Stage 2 converts the problem to a binary classification task using high-confidence normal/abnormal node sets (selected by local score thresholds) as pseudo-labels, and introduces a hybrid attention mechanism for adaptive message passing: pre-attention measures deviation of a node's local anomaly score difference from the normal-node baseline, while post-attention measures feature similarity with neighbors; the two are dynamically weighted with a schedule that shifts emphasis from pre- to post-attention over training epochs. The global anomaly score is derived from each node's alignment with a global normal context (mean embedding of high-confidence normal nodes), and the final score averages both stages.

## Results

GADAM achieves state-of-the-art ROC-AUC on 8 out of 9 benchmark datasets, with headline values of 0.9556 (Cora), 0.9415 (Citeseer), 0.9603 (ACM), and 0.8499 (ogbn-Products). The average improvement over the best remaining baseline ranges from 5.98% (BlogCatalog, Reddit) to 24.54% (ACM). GADAM is the only method that successfully runs on both large-scale OGB datasets (ogbn-Arxiv: 0.8122, ogbn-Products: 0.8499), where most baselines encounter out-of-memory errors. The sole exception is Pubmed, where Sub-CR (0.9629) marginally outperforms GADAM (0.9581).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GADAM outperforms all 10 baselines on 8 of 9 datasets in ROC-AUC. The largest margin is on ACM, where GADAM (0.9603) surpasses the next-best ANEMONE (0.8802) by 8 percentage points, and on ogbn-Arxiv where GADAM (0.8122) beats CoLA (0.8041) by 0.8 points while all other baselines OOM. On Pubmed, Sub-CR (0.9629) narrowly edges out GADAM (0.9581) by 0.5 points, making it the only dataset where GADAM is not best. Among autoencoder-based methods (DOMINANT, AnomalyDAE, AdONE), GADAM leads by 10–20+ points on most datasets. Among contrastive-learning methods (CoLA, ANEMONE, SL-GAD, CONAD, Sub-CR), GADAM consistently leads except on Pubmed. Among conflict-aware methods (ResGCN, ComGA), GADAM achieves substantially higher scores, e.g., 0.9556 vs. 0.8479 (ResGCN) and 0.8840 (ComGA) on Cora. GADAM also demonstrates superior scalability, being the only method that completes on both ogbn-Arxiv (169K nodes) and ogbn-Products (2.4M nodes) without OOM.

## Relation to Prior Work

GADAM builds directly on the contrastive learning paradigm for local inconsistency mining (LIM) introduced by CoLA (Liu et al., 2021) and extended by ANEMONE, SL-GAD, CONAD, and Sub-CR. It departs from prior conflict-aware methods (ResGCN, ComGA, SpecAE) that attempt to mitigate message passing damage while still using GNNs for LIM, by fully decoupling the two processes: an MLP-based contrastive stage for conflict-free local detection, followed by a separate semi-supervised stage that re-introduces message passing under a global consistency objective. The hybrid attention mechanism, which dynamically blends anomaly-score-difference (pre-attention) and feature-similarity (post-attention) signals, is a novel design not present in prior adaptive message passing approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and quantifies a fundamental tension that has been underappreciated in the UGAD literature: GNN message passing actively undermines the LIM principle by smoothing node representations toward their neighbors, effectively erasing the very signal that LIM-based detectors rely on. The practical impact is significant—GADAM handles the 2.4M-node ogbn-products dataset where nearly all baselines run out of memory, and achieves 6–25% ROC-AUC improvements across nine benchmarks. Open questions include whether the two-stage pseudo-labeling approach generalizes to truly heterogeneous or temporal graphs, and whether the global consistency objective could be further refined beyond the simple mean-of-normal-nodes context vector.
