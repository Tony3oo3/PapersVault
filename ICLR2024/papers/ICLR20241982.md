## Problem

Existing SNN pruning methods either focus on weight pruning without directly targeting energy consumption, or perform neuron pruning only in a structured manner on shallow networks. None of these approaches exploit the fact that in convolutional SNNs, individual neurons can be pruned in an unstructured (fine-grained) way, which is fundamentally different from weight pruning and unlocks additional sparsity that neuromorphic hardware can exploit for energy savings.

## Input / Output

The method accepts static RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet) or neuromorphic event streams (128×128 for DVS-CIFAR10, split into 10 temporal slices and integrated into frames) and outputs class labels. The pruned network is characterized by binary masks over both weights and neurons, with energy consumption quantified in average synaptic operations (SOPs) per inference instance rather than FLOPs, reflecting the event-driven nature of neuromorphic hardware.

## Method

The framework jointly learns unstructured binary masks for both synaptic weights and individual neurons via a scaled-sigmoid reparameterization σ(βα) with a gradually increasing scale factor β (from 5 to 1000), followed by a fine-tuning phase with hard Heaviside masks. The key technical challenge is that the energy penalty term involves a product of three continuous masks (presynaptic neuron, weight, postsynaptic neuron), which is ill-posed; the authors resolve this by decomposing it into two independent l1-norm sub-constraints (E_n for neurons, E_w for weights) with constant per-element SOP coefficients. This decomposition, combined with the observation that in convolutional layers pruning a single neuron does not eliminate an entire channel (unlike in fully-connected layers), enables fine-grained neuron sparsity that weight pruning alone cannot capture. The final objective minimizes task loss plus λ-weighted l1 penalties on both mask types, trained end-to-end with standard optimizers (Adam or SGD).

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the proposed method achieves 90.65% top-1 accuracy (only 2.19% loss) with just 8.50M SOPs, a 91.31× energy efficiency gain over the dense network (776.12M SOPs), while retaining only 0.63% of connections. On DVS-CIFAR10, it reaches 78.3% accuracy (4.1% loss) at 6.75M SOPs (95.90× efficiency gain). On ImageNet with SEW ResNet18, it achieves 58.99% accuracy (4.19% loss) at 116.88M SOPs (11.45× efficiency gain). The method consistently outperforms all baselines in the accuracy-vs-SOPs trade-off across all three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CIFAR-10, the proposed method dominates all baselines in the accuracy–SOPs trade-off: at the sparsest setting it achieves 90.65% accuracy with 8.50M SOPs, surpassing STDS (90.21%, 26.81M SOPs), GradR (89.32%, 41.89M SOPs), and ESLSNN (90.90%, 108.89M SOPs) while requiring far fewer synaptic operations. On DVS-CIFAR10, the method reaches 81.9% accuracy at 47.81M SOPs, outperforming STDS (79.8%, 38.85M SOPs) by 2.1% accuracy at comparable energy cost, and ESLSNN (77.5%, 79.65M SOPs) by 4.4% accuracy with 40% fewer SOPs. On ImageNet, the method achieves 61.89% at 311.70M SOPs versus STDS's 58.06% at 182.49M SOPs and ADMM's 50.57% at 173.10M SOPs, yielding 3.83% and 11.32% higher accuracy respectively at similar or lower sparsity. The paper also highlights that fewer remaining weights do not guarantee lower energy: GradR retains fewer parameters (0.26M) than STDS (0.28M) yet requires 41.89M vs. 26.81M SOPs, validating the paper's connectivity-based energy model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SNN pruning literature (GradR, STDS, ADMM, ESLSNN) by introducing unstructured neuron pruning as a complement to unstructured weight pruning, specifically targeting energy efficiency rather than model size. Prior SNN pruning work was largely adapted from ANN methods and focused on weight sparsity or structured channel-level pruning; the only prior neuron pruning for SNNs (Wu et al., 2019) was limited to shallow fully-connected networks. The sigmoid reparameterization for binary masks follows the continuous sparsification approach from ANN pruning (Savarese et al., 2020), while the SOPs-based energy model draws on established neuromorphic hardware metrics from Loihi, TrueNorth, and TianJic. The key conceptual departure is formulating pruning as a joint energy-constrained optimization over both neuron and weight masks, with a decomposition trick to avoid the ill-posedness of the combined penalty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As deep SNNs (SpikingResNet, SpikingVGG) grow in scale to match ANN performance, their inherent energy advantage on neuromorphic hardware erodes; this work demonstrates that targeted unstructured sparsification can recover a 91× energy reduction with minimal accuracy loss, making deep SNNs practically viable on energy-constrained edge devices. The finding that connectivity (not just parameter count) governs energy consumption provides a useful diagnostic for practitioners choosing pruning strategies. Limitations include the linear SOPs energy model, which may not hold for memory-access-dominated or analog-based architectures, and the method's dependence on careful hyperparameter scheduling (β, λ). Open questions include whether the approach composes with quantization or temporal pruning, and whether the self-organizing multiresolution neuron structure observed in visualizations can be exploited for further compression.
