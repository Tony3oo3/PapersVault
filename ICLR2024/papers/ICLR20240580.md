## Problem

Existing distribution matching algorithms for diffusion and flow models (e.g., DSBM, Rectified Flow) only minimize kinetic energy between boundary distributions, limiting the design of optimality to the ℓ₂ cost. Many scientific applications—crowd navigation, opinion dynamics, molecular simulation—require task-specific state costs V_t that penalize or reward intermediate states, but prior GSB solvers (e.g., DeepGSB) either relax the feasibility constraint or lack convergence guarantees, leading to unstable training and poor boundary preservation.

## Input / Output

The method requires only samples from the source distribution μ and target distribution ν (no density access needed), plus a user-specified state cost function V_t(x) that can encode mean-field interactions, geometric constraints, or latent-space regularizers. It outputs a learned SDE drift field u_t^θ that transports μ to ν while respecting V_t, along with the induced boundary coupling p_{0,1}. Notably, the state cost can be conditioned on the pair (x_0, x_1), enabling per-pair path optimization, and the algorithm operates in dimensions up to ~12K (e.g., 1000-d opinion vectors, 64×64 images).

## Method

GSBM alternates between two stages: (1) matching the drift u_t^θ to the current marginal p_t via an explicit or implicit matching loss, and (2) refining p_t by solving a Conditional Stochastic Optimal Control (CondSOC) problem for each boundary pair (x_0, x_1) drawn from the current coupling. The key insight is that Stage 2 decomposes the global GSB problem into independent per-pair SOC problems with fixed endpoints, which are solved efficiently via a Gaussian path approximation parameterized by a small number of spline control points (K ≤ 30) for the mean and variance—requiring no SDE simulation and enabling full parallelization. An optional path integral resampling step (via Girsanov's theorem) debiases the Gaussian approximation when the true optimal path deviates significantly. The paper proves local monotonic convergence of the objective and that the GSB optimum is a fixed point of the algorithm, distinguishing GSBM from prior methods that only approach feasibility in the limit.

## Results

On the AFHQ dog→cat unpaired image translation task, GSBM achieves a FID of 12.39, outperforming DSBM (14.16) by 12.5%. On the LiDAR surface navigation task, GSBM achieves an objective value of 6209 versus DeepGSB's 7747 (a 20% reduction). On the 1000-dimensional opinion depolarization task, GSBM achieves approximately half the objective value of DeepGSB while maintaining closer proximity to the target distribution. Across all crowd navigation tasks (Stunnel, Vneck, GMM), GSBM maintains feasible boundary marginals throughout training, whereas DeepGSB exhibits instability and occasional divergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DeepGSB (Liu et al., 2022), GSBM consistently wins on both feasibility and optimality: on the LiDAR surface task, GSBM's objective (6209) is 20% lower than DeepGSB's (7747), and on opinion depolarization, GSBM achieves roughly half the objective value while staying closer to the target p₁. Critically, GSBM maintains feasible boundary marginals (W₂(p₁^θ, ν) ≈ 0) throughout training, whereas DeepGSB diverges on multiple crowd navigation tasks. Against DSBM (Shi et al., 2023), GSBM achieves a lower FID (12.39 vs. 14.16) on AFHQ and produces more semantically meaningful interpolations by optimizing the conditional path p_{t|0,1} with a latent-space state cost V_t, while adding only 0.5% wall-clock overhead. Against NLSB (Koshizuka & Sato, 2023), GSBM provides guaranteed feasibility (hard boundary constraints) versus NLSB's relaxed feasibility, requires only samples (not densities) from μ and ν, and scales to dimensions ≥12K versus NLSB's 5D limit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Schrödinger Bridge Matching (SBM) line of work—encompassing DSBM (Shi et al., 2023), Rectified Flow (Liu et al., 2023b), and flow/action matching (Lipman et al., 2023; Neklyudov et al., 2023)—to handle nontrivial task-specific state costs V_t in the Generalized Schrödinger Bridge (GSB) problem previously formulated by Chen et al. (2015) and Liu et al. (2022). The key conceptual departure is casting Stage 2 of the alternating optimization as a Conditional Stochastic Optimal Control (CondSOC) problem, for which prior SBM methods (which assume V_t = 0) are recovered as special cases. Unlike DeepGSB (Liu et al., 2022), which relaxes feasibility and requires density access, GSBM enforces boundary constraints as hard constraints and operates purely on samples, while the path integral resampling step draws on Kappen's (2005) information-theoretic control framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GSBM provides a principled, scalable algorithm for distribution matching with task-specific optimality that was previously only accessible through less stable or less scalable methods. The demonstration that the CondSOC step adds merely 0.5% wallclock overhead (Table 5) makes the framework practically attractive for high-dimensional applications (12K-dim opinion modeling, 64×64 image translation). Key limitations include the requirement that V_t be differentiable and the reliance on quadratic control cost for convergence guarantees, both acknowledged by the authors. An open question the paper highlights is how to design appropriate V_t for new tasks—the latent-space cost via β-VAE and Slerp is a promising but ad hoc choice, and a systematic theory for state cost design remains absent.
