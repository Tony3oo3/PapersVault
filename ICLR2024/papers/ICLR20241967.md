## Problem

Existing post-training pruning methods for LLMs (SparseGPT, Wanda) suffer from channel corruption—entire input or output channels get pruned away—causing performance drops comparable to structured pruning. Additionally, converting unstructured sparsity to hardware-friendly N:M semi-structured sparsity (e.g., 2:4) causes further degradation, and the prior greedy channel permutation approach is computationally infeasible for models with tens of billions of parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241967/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained LLM weight matrices (LLaMA 7B–65B, LLaMA2 7B–70B, OPT 1.3B) along with 128 calibration samples (2048 tokens each, typically from C4) to compute input activation norms. It outputs sparse weight matrices at a user-specified sparsity ratio (unstructured or N:M) plus a set of input-channel permutation indices that must be applied to the preceding layer's output channels (or via a modified RMSNorm for residual connections). No fine-tuning or retraining is required; the pruned model is directly deployable.

## Method

The method has two plug-and-play components. First, RIA (Relative Importance and Activations) defines a per-weight pruning score as the sum of the weight's magnitude normalized by its input-channel sum and its output-channel sum, multiplied by the l2-norm of the corresponding input activation raised to a tunable power a (default 0.5); this relative normalization prevents entire channels from being pruned. Second, Channel Permutation converts the unstructured mask into N:M semi-structured sparsity by reordering input channels: a heuristic step alternately assigns sorted channels into contiguous blocks, then a Linear Sum Assignment step (solved via the Hungarian algorithm) refines the one-to-one channel-to-block matching to maximize retained importance. The full pipeline runs in ~10 minutes for pruning plus ~1 hour for permutation on LLaMA2-70B, versus ~1.5 hours for SparseGPT and 3+ days for the prior greedy permutation.

## Results

At 50% unstructured sparsity on Wikitext2, RIA achieves PPL 4.11 on LLaMA2-70B, outperforming Wanda (4.22) and SparseGPT (4.25), with the dense model at 3.32. Under 2:4 N:M semi-structured sparsity with Channel Permutation, RIA on LLaMA2-70B reaches PPL 5.11, and on zero-shot tasks achieves 62.16 average accuracy, surpassing the dense model (63.32) on BoolQ (85.14 vs 83.70), MNLI (49.08 vs 45.81), and RTE (68.95 vs 67.87). The method delivers 1.22× (cuTLASS) and 1.63× (cuSPARSELT) inference speedup on NVIDIA A100 GPUs with 2:4 sparsity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RIA consistently outperforms all three main baselines across every model size tested. Against Wanda (Sun et al., 2023), RIA achieves a 17% improvement in preventing performance drop from the dense model (e.g., LLaMA2-70B: 4.11 vs 4.22 PPL; LLaMA2-13B: 5.83 vs 5.99). Against SparseGPT (Frantar & Alistarh, 2023), the improvement is 50% in preventing performance drop (LLaMA2-70B: 4.11 vs 4.25; LLaMA-65B: 4.38 vs 4.57). Against magnitude pruning (Zhu & Gupta, 2017), RIA is dramatically superior, with gaps of 1–2 PPL points on large models (e.g., LLaMA2-70B: 4.11 vs 5.36) and orders-of-magnitude differences on smaller models (OPT-1.3B: 18.08 vs 1712). Against LLM-Pruner (Ma et al., 2023) for structured pruning, RIA with semi-structured sparsity is far more effective: at 50% sparsity on LLaMA2-7B, RIA achieves PPL 11.27 while LLM-Pruner's structured approach collapses to NaN, and even at 25% sparsity RIA (5.90) vastly outperforms LLM-Pruner (28.88). Under N:M constraints with Channel Permutation, RIA(2:4+CP) on LLaMA2-70B surpasses the dense model on 3 of 5 zero-shot tasks, a result no baseline achieves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the one-shot post-training pruning framework established by SparseGPT (Hessian-based, O(d³)) and Wanda (activation-aware, O(d²)) by proposing RIA, a new pruning metric that normalizes weight magnitudes by both input and output channels to prevent the 'channel corruption' the authors identify in both prior methods. For N:M semi-structured sparsity, it departs from Pool & Yu's (2021) greedy channel permutation—which is infeasible at LLM scale—by reformulating the problem as a linear sum assignment solvable via the Hungarian algorithm, reducing per-matrix permutation time from hours to seconds. The two components (RIA and Channel Permutation) are designed to be independently composable with any existing pruning metric, positioning the work as a modular upgrade rather than a full pipeline replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling real hardware acceleration (1.2×–1.6× on A100 via cuTLASS/cuSPARSELT) through N:M sparsity without the performance collapse that has historically limited semi-structured pruning to research settings. The observation that a 50%-pruned LLaMA2-70B with RIA+CP can match or exceed the dense model on 3 of 5 zero-shot benchmarks suggests substantial redundancy in current LLMs, though this finding is limited to a single model size and five tasks. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-Transformer architectures, higher sparsity levels beyond 50%, and whether the modest speedup (well below the theoretical 2×) will improve as sparse-matrix libraries mature. The integration with quantization (GPTQ/AWQ) shows slight additional degradation, leaving the combined compression pipeline underexplored.
