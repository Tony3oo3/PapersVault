## Problem

Offline RL agents are highly vulnerable to state observation perturbations from sensor errors or adversarial attacks, yet existing defenses either train over-conservative policies against worst-case disturbances or apply conservative smoothing that degrades rapidly with increasing noise scale. No prior work has explored using diffusion models as state denoisers (rather than trajectory generators) to recover actual states for decision-making in state-based offline RL tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241512/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DMBP takes a noised state vector (perturbed by Gaussian noise, uniform noise, or adversarial attacks within an ℓ∞ ball), the previously denoised state trajectory τ_{t-1}^{ŝ} of length N, and the last-step agent action a_{t-1} as inputs. It outputs a denoised state estimate ŝ_t obtained by averaging 50 parallel reverse-diffusion runs, which is then fed into any offline RL policy for action generation. For the infilling variant, the input additionally includes a binary mask m indicating which state dimensions are unobserved, and the output is a complete state vector with missing dimensions reconstructed.

## Method

DMBP repurposes a conditional diffusion model as a denoiser rather than a generator, using a redesigned variance schedule β_i = e^{-b/(i+a)} + c that restricts noise to small-to-medium scales and limits diffusion steps to K=10 (denoising) or K=100 (infilling). The architecture combines a U-net to extract hierarchical trajectory information with an MLP-based noise predictor that also receives the noised state, last action, last denoised state, and diffusion timestep. To prevent error accumulation during sequential online denoising, the paper introduces a non-Markovian loss that jointly optimizes noise prediction at the current timestep and at M future timesteps using the model's own denoised outputs as conditions, trading off single-step accuracy against long-horizon condition consistency. For incomplete observations, DMBP adapts the Repaint inpainting procedure by splitting each diffusion step into a forward pass on known dimensions and a reverse pass on unknown dimensions, with U resampling iterations to harmonize the two.

## Results

On D4RL Mujoco benchmarks, DMBP as a plug-in module recovers most of the performance lost to state observation noise across all five baseline offline RL algorithms. For example, on Hopper medium-replay with 0.10 Gaussian noise, CQL drops from 96.9 (clean) to 47.5, while DMBP-CQL recovers to 89.1; on Walker2d expert with 0.10 noise, BCQ drops from 111.6 to 77.9, while DMBP-BCQ recovers to 110.3. On medium-replay datasets, DMBP-strengthened algorithms achieve scores within 5–10 points of their noise-free counterparts even at 0.15 noise scale. Against adversarial attacks (MAD, MinQ) at 0.05 norm, DMBP-strengthened CQL on Hopper expert reaches 46.5 (MAD) and 46.2 (MinQ) versus 29.0 and 39.2 for base CQL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMBP is evaluated as a plug-in denoising module applied on top of five offline RL baselines (BCQ, CQL, TD3+BC, Diffusion QL, RORL), so the primary comparison is each algorithm with vs. without DMBP rather than a head-to-head between methods. DMBP consistently and substantially improves all five baselines: on Hopper medium-replay at 0.10 noise, the improvement ranges from +51.1 (BCQ: 15.7→66.8) to +17.3 (RORL: 85.9→103.2). Notably, DMBP-strengthened non-robust baselines (e.g., CQL, TD3+BC) frequently match or exceed the SOTA robust method RORL without DMBP under the same noise conditions, suggesting that state denoising is a more effective robustness strategy than conservative smoothing alone. On expert datasets with high noise (0.10–0.15), recovery is less complete (e.g., HalfCheetah expert at 0.10: best DMBP score is 47.5 vs. clean 108.5), but on medium-replay datasets the recovery is near-complete (within 5–10 points of clean performance). Against adversarial attacks (MAD, MinQ) in Table 2, DMBP-strengthened CQL on Hopper expert reaches 46.5/46.2 versus 29.0/39.2 for base CQL, and DMBP-RORL on Walker2d expert at 0.10 noise reaches 103.5 (U-rand) and 103.2 (MinQ), outperforming all other baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

DMBP repurposes the conditional diffusion model (Ho et al., 2020) as a state denoiser rather than a trajectory generator or policy regularizer, which is how prior diffusion-based RL methods (Diffuser, Decision Diffuser, Diffusion QL) employ it. It departs from the robust offline RL line (RORL, adversarial training) that trains policies to be invariant to perturbations, instead recovering the true state before the policy acts. The state infilling procedure directly adapts the Repaint inpainting algorithm (Lugmayr et al., 2022) from image pixels to state-vector dimensions, and the non-Markovian loss function extends the classical DDPM training objective to account for sequential error accumulation along RL trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DMBP provides a modular, plug-and-play denoising layer that can strengthen any existing offline RL algorithm against sensor noise and adversarial attacks without retraining the policy, which is practically important for real-world robotics where sensor failures and attacks are inevitable. The non-Markovian loss and the finding that the pure MDP assumption (N=1) is insufficient for accurate sequential denoising challenge a common simplification in model-based RL. Open questions include how the approach scales to very high-dimensional state spaces, whether the diffusion start timestep can be learned automatically rather than hand-tuned per noise scale, and whether the framework extends to continuous-time or partially observable settings.
