## Problem

In multi-task learning for dense prediction, the high-dimensional unstructured feature space shared across tasks is prone to capturing noisy, geometrically-inconsistent cross-task correlations that hurt performance. Existing MTL architectures focus on designing cross-task attention or interaction modules but do not enforce any physical structure on the shared representations, leaving them susceptible to overfitting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241384/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input (no multi-view or camera calibration required) and jointly predicts dense per-pixel outputs: semantic segmentation, depth, surface normals, boundaries, part segmentation, and saliency. The 3D regularizer internally renders at low resolution (56×72 for NYUv2, 64×64 for PASCAL-Context) and upsamples via bilinear interpolation to ground-truth resolution. Critically, the regularizer is discarded at inference, so the deployed model incurs zero additional computational cost over the base MTL backbone.

## Method

The paper introduces a 3D-aware regularizer that projects shared encoder features onto a tri-plane representation (xy, yz, xz planes) via a lightweight tri-plane encoder, then queries arbitrary 3D coordinates by bilinear interpolation across the three planes and summing the retrieved features. For each task, a small MLP (neural task field) maps the aggregated 3D representation to a density value and a task-specific vector, which are then integrated along rays via differentiable volume rendering to produce per-pixel predictions. The regularizer is trained jointly with the base MTL model by adding per-task rendering losses weighted by a ramped-up hyperparameter α_t, but is entirely removed at inference. The approach is architecture-agnostic and has been validated by plugging it into both a CNN-based backbone (MTI-Net with HRNet-48) and a transformer-based backbone (InvPT with ViT-L), yielding consistent improvements across all tasks on NYUv2 and PASCAL-Context.

## Results

On NYUv2 with a ViT-L backbone, the method (plugged into InvPT) achieves 54.87 mIoU on semantic segmentation, 0.5006 RMSE on depth, 18.55 mErr on surface normals, and 78.30 odsF on boundary detection, outperforming the InvPT baseline on all four tasks. With an HRNet-48 backbone (plugged into MTI-Net), it reaches 46.67 mIoU, 0.5210 RMSE, 19.93 mErr, and 78.10 odsF, surpassing all CNN-based MTL methods. On PASCAL-Context with ViT-L, the method obtains 79.53 mIoU (Seg), 69.12 mIoU (PartSeg), 84.94 maxF (Saliency), 13.53 mErr (Normal), and 74.00 odsF (Boundary), again improving over InvPT on every task. The regularizer adds no inference cost since it is discarded at test time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against 10 baselines spanning CNN-based (Cross-Stitch, PAP, PSD, PAD-Net, ATRC, MTI-Net) and transformer-based (InvPT, TaskPrompter, TaskExpert) MTL methods. Against the strongest CNN-based baselines ATRC and MTI-Net (HRNet-48), the method wins on all four NYUv2 tasks, e.g. +0.34 mIoU over ATRC on Seg and +0.15 mErr improvement on Normal. Against the transformer-based InvPT (ViT-L), it consistently improves all tasks: +1.31 mIoU on Seg, +0.0177 RMSE reduction on Depth, +0.49 mErr on Normal, and +0.20 odsF on Boundary. On PASCAL-Context, the ViT-L variant beats InvPT by +0.50 mIoU (Seg), +1.51 mIoU (PartSeg), +0.13 maxF (Saliency), +0.62 mErr (Normal), and +1.00 odsF (Boundary). Against the more recent TaskPrompter and TaskExpert (2023), the method is slightly behind on Seg (54.87 vs 55.30/55.35 mIoU on NYUv2) but clearly ahead on Depth (0.5006 vs 0.5152/0.5157 RMSE) and competitive or better on Normal and Boundary, suggesting the 3D-aware regularization is complementary to those prompting/expert approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes 3D neural rendering techniques—specifically tri-plane representations (Chan et al., 2022) and NeRF-style differentiable volume rendering (Mildenhall et al., 2020)—as a training-time regularizer for multi-task learning, rather than for their original purpose of novel view synthesis. It departs from the dominant MTL literature (cross-task attention, task-specific gating, loss balancing) by introducing a geometrically-structured bottleneck: shared encoder features are projected into a 3D tri-plane space and rendered back to 2D predictions, enforcing that all tasks share a consistent 3D density manifold. The method is explicitly orthogonal to prior MTL work on cross-task interfaces and to scene-specific NeRF extensions (Zhi et al., 2021; Kundu et al., 2022), as it operates from a single view, generalizes across scenes, and is discarded at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that imposing 3D geometric structure on shared MTL representations reduces noisy cross-task correlations—offers a principled, architecture-agnostic regularization strategy with zero inference overhead, making it broadly applicable to dense prediction pipelines. However, the performance gains are modest (roughly +1–2 mIoU on segmentation, ~0.02 RMSE on depth) and the training cost increases by ~30–50% in time and memory. Open questions include whether adaptive loss balancing or gradient surgery could compound the 3D regularization benefit, whether higher-resolution tri-plane encodings would yield larger gains, and whether the approach extends to non-rigid or specular scenes where the current volume-rendering assumptions break down.
