## Problem

Pre-training on web-scale data has transformed language and vision, but has not taken hold in reinforcement learning because the most abundant embodied data—videos—lack the action labels required by imitation learning and offline RL methods. Prior approaches (VPT, ACO, BCO, RLV) all rely on some ground-truth action-labeled data to train an inverse dynamics model before labeling larger action-free datasets. LAPO addresses this gap by recovering the structure of the true action space purely from observed dynamics, with zero action labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240373/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequences of action-free RGB observation frames (o_{t-k}, ..., o_t, o_{t+1}) with k=1 additional pre-transition context frame, sampled from expert-policy rollouts (~8M frames per Procgen game). It produces 128-dimensional continuous latent action vectors that are vector-quantized into 8 discrete latents with 16-dimensional embeddings (2 codebooks × 4 latents × 64 embeddings), a latent-action policy mapping observations to these quantized latents, a forward dynamics model (U-Net/ResNet, ~8M params), and an inverse dynamics model (IMPALA-CNN, 4× channels). The latent policy is subsequently decoded into true discrete actions via a small supervised decoder (~200 labeled transitions) or online RL fine-tuning.

## Method

LAPO jointly trains an inverse dynamics model (IDM) and a forward dynamics model (FDM) via a purely unsupervised predictive-consistency objective: the IDM, which observes both o_t and o_{t+1}, predicts a latent action z_t that is passed to the FDM, which only observes o_t and must predict o_{t+1} from (o_t, z_t). Both are optimized to minimize the next-state prediction error ‖ô_{t+1} − o_{t+1}‖². The critical design choice is applying vector quantization to z_t, creating an information bottleneck that prevents the IDM from simply copying the future observation and instead forces it to encode only the compressed transition information—effectively recovering the true action structure. After IDM training, a latent-action policy is obtained via behavior cloning on IDM-generated pseudo-labels, and is then decoded to the true action space either offline (supervised decoder on ~200 labeled transitions) or online (PPO fine-tuning of the final layers), recovering or exceeding expert performance within 4M environment steps.

## Results

On the 16 Procgen environments, LAPO's latent policy decoded online recovers full expert performance within 4M frames, while PPO trained from scratch reaches only 44% of expert performance in the same budget. In 9 of 16 tasks, LAPO actually exceeds the expert (itself trained for 25M frames) within just 4M frames. For offline decoding, a latent→true action decoder trained on fewer than 256 action-labeled transitions matches the performance of a policy trained from scratch for 4M PPO steps, demonstrating extreme data efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares LAPO against two baselines: PPO from scratch and ILPO (Edwards et al., 2019). Against PPO from scratch (4M steps), LAPO's online-decoded latent policy recovers 100% of expert performance and exceeds it in 9/16 tasks, while PPO reaches only 44% of expert — a roughly 2×+ gap in relative performance. Against ILPO, LAPO wins decisively: ILPO's policy immediately collapses in several Procgen tasks due to mode collapse in its discrete latent space, and in the tasks where it does not collapse, its online-decoded policy performs no better than PPO from scratch. The authors attribute ILPO's failure to the visual diversity of procedurally-generated levels, which a small set of discrete latents cannot adequately model, whereas LAPO's continuous, high-dimensional latent space with vector quantization captures stochasticity and unobserved information more effectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

LAPO carves new ground at the intersection of inverse dynamics modeling and unsupervised representation learning. While semi-supervised methods (VPT, BCO, RLV) train an IDM on action-labeled data before labeling unlabeled footage, and ILPO jointly learns a discrete latent policy with a forward dynamics model, LAPO removes all reliance on ground-truth action labels by training a continuous-latent IDM and FDM jointly under a predictive-consistency objective with a vector-quantization bottleneck. The closest conceptual ancestor is ILPO, but LAPO departs by using an IDM (rather than a joint policy) to avoid mode collapse, and by using continuous quantized latents rather than a small discrete set, enabling richer modeling of stochastic and partially-observable transitions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LAPO demonstrates that the structure of a true action space can be recovered from pure video with zero action labels, opening a path toward web-scale pretraining of generalist RL policies and world models from the vast quantities of unlabeled video available online. Key limitations remain: partial observability fragments the latent space, stochasticity degrades representation quality, and scaling to web-scale video (with its heterogeneous policies and goals) is untested. The ~200-labeled-transition decoder result is striking for data efficiency, but the offline decoder plateaus below online RL performance, suggesting that state-conditional decoding remains an open challenge for fully offline deployment.
