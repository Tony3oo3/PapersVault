## Problem

Modern MIP solvers apply a fixed, instance-agnostic presolving configuration regardless of the input instance, yet presolving is one of the most impactful components of the solver pipeline. No prior work has explored instance-specific presolving or introduced learning into the presolving stage, leaving a clear gap: can tailoring presolver parameters per instance yield significant solver improvements, and if so, how to do so efficiently enough for real-world deployment?

## Input / Output

The method takes a MIP instance and encodes it as a bipartite constraint-variable graph (edges where A_{i,j}≠0), processed by a two-interleaved GCNN into a graph embedding. The output is a vector of 42 presolving parameters (14 presolvers × 3: priority as a continuous ordering value, max-rounds as an integer, and timing as an integer mask), which is directly injected into SCIP's presolving module. Inference completes in under 0.5 s, negligible compared to solving times of hours or days.

## Method

L2P is a two-stage supervised framework: an offline simulated annealing (SA) search finds the optimal 42-parameter presolving configuration per training instance (taking hours to days), and a neural network learns to predict these configurations from the instance graph in milliseconds. The feature extractor is a two-interleaved GCNN following Gasse et al. (2019). The inference network uses a shared-bottom architecture with three output branches; the priority branch (trained with ListMLE for ranking) feeds into the max-rounds and timing branches (trained with Cross-Entropy) via a knowledge-based residual learning (KRL) unit, where priority acts as prior knowledge and the residual network learns the correction. A dynamic loss averaging scheme adaptively reweights the three branch losses based on their relative convergence rates, preventing the slower-converging priority branch from being under-trained while avoiding overfitting in the faster-converging branches.

## Results

On the medium CORLAT dataset, L2P reduces solving time by 34.74% (from 31.02s to 20.24s) compared to SCIP's default presolving, with 55% effectiveness (instances improved). On the hard Anonymous dataset, L2P achieves a 51.29% reduction in PD integral (from 68319.60 to 33278.48), the largest improvement across all benchmarks. On two private industrial datasets, L2P reduces cumulative solving time by 4.06% and 18.27% respectively, approaching the SA oracle (4.96% and 21.62%) while running in seconds instead of hours. Inference time is under 0.5s per instance, negligible compared to solving times of hours or days.

## Baselines & Comparisons

L2P consistently outperforms both SMAC3 and FBAS across nearly all datasets. On CORLAT, L2P achieves 34.74% time reduction vs. SMAC3's 22.34% and FBAS's 16.69%; on the hard Anonymous dataset, L2P reaches 51.29% PD integral improvement vs. SMAC3's 37.85% and FBAS's 19.23%. FBAS fails entirely (0% improvement) on MIK, Item Placement, and Load Balancing, while L2P still extracts 3.02%, 4.96%, and 5.11% respectively. SMAC3 is the strongest baseline but is outperformed by L2P on every dataset except the easy Set Covering and Max Independent Set (where all methods show ~0% improvement due to minimal redundancy). Compared to the SA oracle, L2P achieves 71–150% of SA's improvement (e.g., 34.74% vs. 48.63% on CORLAT; 28.41% vs. 49.81% on MIRP large) while running in <0.5s instead of hours to days, making it the only practical online method that approaches oracle quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new sub-problem within ML4CO: while prior learning-based MIP work (Gasse et al. 2019, He et al. 2014, Zarpellon et al. 2021) targets the branch-and-bound stage (branching, cutting, node selection), L2P focuses exclusively on the presolving stage that precedes B&B. It repurposes the bipartite-graph GCNN feature extractor from Gasse et al. (2019) and the knowledge-based residual learning (KRL) framework from Zheng et al. (2021b) into a supervised setting where simulated annealing provides labels. Conceptually, it departs from automatic configuration work (SMAC3, ParamILS) that seeks a single robust parameter set across all instances, instead learning a per-instance mapping from MIP structure to presolver parameters (priority, max-rounds, timing).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that the default instance-agnostic presolving in mature solvers like SCIP is a non-trivial performance bottleneck, with instance-specific tailoring yielding up to ~35% solving-time reduction on medium-scale benchmarks and ~51% PD-integral improvement on large industrial instances. However, the practical impact is tempered by several limitations: experiments are confined to the open-source SCIP solver (commercial solvers like Gurobi/CPLEX lack accessible presolving APIs), the MIPLIB benchmark showed negligible improvement (suggesting well-curated instances are already near-optimal for default presolving), and the method requires per-dataset retraining. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to non-linear MIP, scales to solvers with different presolver architectures, and whether the gains persist as solvers themselves evolve.
