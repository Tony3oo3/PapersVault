## Problem

Existing learned neural scene representations (SRT, RUST, NeRF-VAE) are restricted to static scenes, ignoring the dynamics present in the vast majority of real-world video. In monocular video, camera motion and scene dynamics are strongly correlated over time, making it difficult to learn disentangled latent representations that allow independent control over viewpoint and scene content. This paper addresses how to learn latent dynamic neural scene representations from monocular real-world videos with separate, controllable camera and dynamics factors.

## Input / Output

The model takes 3 monocular RGB frames (128×128) from a dynamic scene as input and produces a set-based latent scene representation Z (768 tokens of dimensionality 768) along with two low-dimensional control latents: an 8-dim camera pose latent ĉ and an 8-dim scene dynamics latent d̂. These latents condition a transformer decoder to synthesize a novel target view. On synthetic DySO data, the camera and dynamics latents are estimated from views *different* from the target (same camera but different dynamics, and vice versa), while on real SSv2 video they are estimated from the target frame itself since no cross-view counterparts are available.

## Method

DyST uses a CNN+transformer encoder to produce a set-based scene representation Z, a joint Camera & Dynamics Estimator (a single transformer that cross-attends into Z) that simultaneously outputs ĉ via global average pooling and d̂ via a learned dynamics token, and a transformer decoder that cross-attends into Z conditioned on the concatenated [ĉ, d̂] to render the target view. The key training innovation is the latent control swap: to generate target y_{d1}^{c1}, the camera pose is estimated from y_{d2}^{c1} (matching camera, different dynamics) and the dynamics from y_{d1}^{c2} (matching dynamics, different camera), forcing the model to route all camera information through ĉ and all dynamics information through d̂. This swap is applied only on the synthetic DySO dataset (1M scenes, each with 5 cameras × 5 dynamics = 25 views), while real SSv2 video is co-trained without the swap using the target view for both estimators. The model is trained end-to-end with an L2 novel-view-synthesis loss, alternating gradient steps between DySO and SSv2 batches, achieving a camera contrastiveness of R^cam = 0.06 and dynamics contrastiveness of R^dyn = 0.42, demonstrating effective disentanglement.

## Results

On the synthetic DySO dataset, DyST achieves a PSNR of 26.0 dB and LPIPS of 0.34 for novel view synthesis under latent control swapping, substantially outperforming the no-swap baseline (18.6 dB PSNR, 0.45 LPIPS). The learned latent spaces show strong disentanglement: the camera contrastiveness metric R_cam reaches 0.06 (a 16.7× smaller distance between matching vs. non-matching camera views) and the dynamics contrastiveness R_dyn reaches 0.42 (2.4× smaller), confirming effective separation of camera pose from scene dynamics. On real-world SSv2 videos, DyST qualitatively synthesizes intermediate frames with accurate camera and object motion, and PCA analysis reveals that the first camera principal components capture horizontal/vertical translation and planar rotation, while dynamics components capture object rotation and translation.

**Table 1: Evaluation of alternative training techniques on DySO (y_{d'}^c / y_d^{c'} case).**

| Method | PSNR ↑ | LPIPS ↓ | R_cam ↓ | R_dyn ↓ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| No swap | 18.6 | 0.45 | 0.72 | 1.26 |
| 50% swap | 25.4 | 0.36 | 0.26 | 1.07 |
| Latent averaging | 22.9 | 0.42 | 0.54 | 0.96 |
| **DyST (full swap)** | **26.0** | **0.34** | **0.06** | **0.42** |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not include a direct quantitative comparison against external methods (e.g., RUST, SRT, NeRF-VAE); instead, the primary quantitative evaluation is an ablation of the latent control swap training scheme (Table 1). DyST with full latent control swap outperforms all alternatives across every metric: it achieves 7.4 dB higher PSNR than the no-swap baseline (26.0 vs. 18.6), 0.6 dB higher than 50% swap (26.0 vs. 25.4), and 3.1 dB higher than latent averaging (26.0 vs. 22.9). On the contrastiveness metrics, DyST's R_cam of 0.06 is an order of magnitude better than no-swap (0.72) and 4.3× better than 50% swap (0.26), while R_dyn of 0.42 is 3× better than no-swap (1.26). The no-swap baseline shows R_dyn > 1, indicating that dynamics information actually leaks into the camera latent, confirming that without the swap scheme the model fails to disentangle the two factors. The qualitative results on SSv2 (Figs. 4–7) demonstrate that the learned separation transfers to real-world video, enabling controllable camera freezing, dynamics freezing, and cross-video motion transfer, capabilities not demonstrated by prior static-scene methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

DyST extends the set-latent scene representation framework of SRT (Sajjadi et al., 2022b) and the pose-free camera estimation of RUST (Sajjadi et al., 2023) to dynamic scenes, adding a Dynamics Estimator alongside the Camera Estimator. The key conceptual departure is the 'latent control swap' training scheme, which enforces disentanglement of camera pose and scene dynamics by estimating each from a different view than the target, a structural inductive bias absent in prior static-scene methods. Unlike dynamic NVS methods (D-NeRF, H-NeRF, NeRFlow) that require posed multi-view video, or monocular methods (Neural Scene Flow Fields, TöRF) that rely on optical flow/depth priors, DyST operates on unposed monocular video via sim-to-real co-training on the new DySO synthetic dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is a first step toward learning disentangled latent representations of dynamic scenes from the most common real-world data source (monocular video), enabling independent post-hoc control over camera and object motion without any explicit pose or flow annotations. The DySO dataset and the latent control swap scheme provide reusable tools for the community. However, significant limitations remain: the L2 loss caps generation quality (especially for dynamic objects), the method assumes a single moving object, and it does not handle changing lighting or long trajectories. Open questions include whether the disentanglement generalizes to multi-object scenes and whether diffusion-based decoders can improve the currently blurry novel view synthesis.
