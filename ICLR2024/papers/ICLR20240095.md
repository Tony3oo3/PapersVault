## Problem

Existing implicit neural representation (INR) architectures have inference cost (MAC) that scales linearly with network depth, which is the primary determinant of reconstruction quality. This creates an inescapable trade-off: achieving high-fidelity signal reconstruction requires deep networks, which in turn demands proportionally high per-sample compute. The paper asks whether inference cost can be fundamentally decoupled from depth while preserving (or even improving) reconstruction capability.

## Input / Output

The network consumes spatial or temporal coordinates (2D for images at resolutions up to 8192×8192, 1D for audio sequences, 3D for occupancy grids) which are pre-decomposed into L hierarchical levels via axis partitioning with per-level bases B_i. At inference, each layer i only processes C_i = ∏_{k=0}^{i} B_k data points rather than all N points, with the remaining positions filled by nearest-neighbor upsampling and tile replication. The output is a per-coordinate signal value (RGB pixel, audio sample, or occupancy indicator), and in the meta-learning setting an additional 128-dim instance-specific latent vector is injected as an extra bias modulation.

## Method

ASMR combines three mechanisms into a SIREN backbone: (1) multi-resolution coordinate decomposition, where each axis is partitioned into L hierarchical levels with bases B_i such that ∏B_i = N, mapping coordinates into a higher-dimensional space that exposes repetitive data patterns; (2) hierarchical modulation, where each level i>0 has an independent linear modulator M_i(x_i) = ω₀W_{M_i} that projects the decomposed coordinate to a bias vector added to the corresponding layer's pre-activation; and (3) activation-sharing inference, where layer i computes on only B^i points and the result is upsampled via nearest-neighbor and tile-replication operations (implemented with einops) to full resolution before the next layer. A geometric-sum argument proves per-sample MAC ≈ 2M (where M depends only on hidden width), yielding near O(1) complexity in depth L. The only added parameters are the single-layer linear modulators, making the parameter overhead negligible relative to the backbone.

## Results

On the Kodak dataset, ASMR achieves 33.09±2.70 dB PSNR and 0.892±0.021 SSIM with only 1.85K MACs, outperforming SIREN (32.40 dB, 132.4K MACs) by 0.7 dB while using 71× fewer MACs. On the 8192×8192 Pluto megapixel image, ASMR-BIG reaches 38.73 dB PSNR with 0.008M MACs versus SIREN-BIG's 35.66 dB at 4.205M MACs, a >500× MAC reduction with a 3 dB quality gain. On LibriSpeech audio, ASMR achieves 61.66±1.61 dB PSNR at 1.0K MACs, surpassing SIREN (46.21 dB, 33.0K MACs) by over 14 dB. ASMR also matches SIREN's 3D shape reconstruction quality (89.57 vs 89.27 IoU on ShapeNet) while reducing MACs by >50×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASMR outperforms all five baselines in Table 3 (FFN, SIREN, MFN, WIRE) on both PSNR and SSIM on the Kodak dataset, while operating at 70–75× lower MACs; the gap is most pronounced against MFN (5.5 dB PSNR advantage) and least against SIREN (0.7 dB). On the Pluto megapixel image, ASMR-BIG surpasses SIREN-BIG by 3.07 dB PSNR at 525× fewer MACs, and also beats KiloNeRF by 10.8 dB PSNR at comparable parameter counts. On LibriSpeech audio, ASMR dominates Instant-NGP by 14.4 dB PSNR (61.66 vs 47.30) at the same ~33K parameter budget. On ShapeNet 3D occupancy, ASMR matches SIREN's IoU (89.57 vs 89.27) while cutting MACs from 2.1M to 0.039M. In the meta-learning experiment on CIFAR-10, ASMR successfully learns a shared global representation enabling 3-step reconstruction, whereas Instant-NGP fails to capture shared structure due to its hashed encoding's nonlinear coordinate distortion.

## Relation to Prior Work

ASMR builds on SIREN as its backbone and draws its multi-resolution coordinate decomposition from LoE (Hao et al., 2022) and the base-change interpretation from Bauer et al. (2023), while adopting the bias-only modulation mechanism from COIN++ (Dupont et al., 2022b). The key departure is the combination of these ingredients into a hierarchical modulation scheme paired with activation-sharing inference, which no prior INR architecture achieves. Unlike KiloNeRF and LoE, which reduce per-sample cost by distributing tiny MLPs or weight tiles across a grid (at the expense of parameter count), ASMR amortizes computation across the data grid while keeping a single shared network, and unlike hybrid methods like Instant-NGP, it remains purely implicit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near O(1) inference complexity with respect to depth is a genuinely useful architectural property for hardware-constrained deployment of INRs, and the 500× MAC reduction with simultaneous quality improvement on megapixel images is a strong efficiency result. However, the method's strong inductive bias toward rasterized data limits its applicability to continuous signals (e.g., SDFs), and expressivity still scales with network width, leaving open the question of whether both depth and width can be decoupled from inference cost. The meta-learning experiment also suggests ASMR could serve as a backbone for dataset-level compression, but this direction is only briefly explored.
