## Problem

Existing video evaluation metrics (FVD, VIS) are direct adaptations of image metrics that merely swap in a video embedding network (I3D), resulting in three critical limitations: they predominantly capture spatial quality while underemphasizing temporal naturalness, they are hard-constrained to 16-frame inputs by the embedding network, and they produce a single composite score that obscures whether a model fails at realism, diversity, or temporal coherence. No prior metric can independently assess the temporal and spatial aspects of generated video.

## Input / Output

The method takes a real video dataset and a generated video dataset of arbitrary length (no 16-frame cap), encoding each frame independently with a DINO image embedding network to produce per-frame features in R^{d×T}. It outputs three bounded scores in [0,1]: STREAM-T (temporal naturalness, higher is better), STREAM-F (fidelity/precision, higher is better), and STREAM-D (diversity/recall, higher is better). Unlike FVD which is unbounded and only comparative, STREAM provides absolute quality estimates that can be interpreted individually.

## Method

STREAM applies FFT along the temporal axis of per-frame DINO features, then splits evaluation by frequency: the zero-frequency (mean) amplitude represents each video as a single spatial point for P&R-based fidelity/diversity scoring (STREAM-F and STREAM-D with k=5 k-NN), while non-zero frequency amplitudes encode temporal dynamics. For STREAM-T, each feature dimension's frequency amplitudes are fit to a power-law distribution C·ζ^{-α} via least squares, the skewness of this distribution is computed analytically via the Moment Generating Function, and the final score is the average Pearson correlation between histograms of real and fake skewness values across all feature dimensions. This design ensures STREAM-T is invariant to spatial noise (since it ignores the DC component) and STREAM-S is invariant to temporal reordering (since it uses only the mean), achieving clean separation of concerns that video embedding networks like I3D cannot provide due to their mixed spatio-temporal encoding.

## Results

On UCF-101 (16-frame, 128×128), STREAM reveals that TATS achieves the highest fidelity (STREAM-F = 0.912) and temporal naturalness (STREAM-T = 0.983) among six video generative models, despite FVD (693.27) and VIS (34.39) suggesting mediocre performance. All models exhibit low diversity (STREAM-D ≤ 0.33), with MoCoGAN scoring 0.000. For 128-frame long video generation, all models show a dramatic drop in temporal naturalness (e.g., TATS drops from 0.983 to 0.030), and STREAM is the only metric that can evaluate videos beyond 16 frames without modification. STREAM achieves Spearman's rank correlation of 0.9 with human realism judgments and 0.6 for temporal naturalness, outperforming FVD (0.7 and 0.5 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STREAM is compared against FVD and VIS, the two standard video evaluation metrics. Against FVD, STREAM demonstrates that FVD overemphasizes spatial quality over temporal naturalness: FVD scores up to 822 for random translation (a spatial distortion) yet shows only modest increase (14.38→24.93) when temporal information is entirely removed via stop scenes. FVD is also constrained to 16-frame inputs due to its I3D embedding network, whereas STREAM evaluates videos of arbitrary length. Against VIS, experiments show that VIS cannot discern different levels of temporal distortion (local swap, global swap, stop scenes), while STREAM-T responds monotonically to increasing temporal degradation. In terms of human alignment, STREAM achieves Spearman correlations of 0.9 (realism) and 0.6 (temporal naturalness) versus FVD's 0.7 and 0.5, confirming STREAM's superior correspondence to human perceptual quality. Notably, STREAM re-ranks models relative to FVD/VIS: TATS appears mediocre under FVD but is revealed as the best in fidelity and temporal naturalness for short videos, while PVDM's FVD advantage (415.70) does not translate to superior temporal or fidelity scores under STREAM.

## Relation to Prior Work

STREAM departs from the dominant paradigm of video evaluation (FVD, VIS) that relies on video-specific embedding networks like I3D, instead using an image embedding network (DINO) applied frame-by-frame combined with FFT along the temporal axis to decouple spatial and temporal assessment. For the spatial component, it extends the Precision and Recall framework of Kynkäänniemi et al. (2019) to video by operating on the zero-frequency (mean) amplitude of frame features, while the temporal component introduces a novel power-law skewness comparison of FFT amplitude spectra. Conceptually, the paper positions itself as correcting a fundamental conflation in existing video metrics: the assumption that a single composite score (FVD) can meaningfully capture both spatial realism and temporal naturalness simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The revelation that FVD—the de facto standard for video generation evaluation—is 3–5× more sensitive to spatial distortions than to the complete removal of temporal flow challenges a core assumption in the field and suggests that many published model comparisons may be misleading. STREAM's ability to evaluate videos of arbitrary length (unlike FVD's 16-frame constraint) and its bounded scores make it a practical tool as video generative models push toward longer sequences. However, the experimental validation is limited to relatively older architectures (MoCoGAN, DIGAN, TATS, VideoGPT, MeBT, PVDM) and does not test on recent diffusion-based video models, leaving open whether the metric's behavior generalizes to the current state of the art. The human evaluation, while showing strong correlation (0.9 for realism), uses only 81 raters scoring 5 videos per model, which limits statistical confidence.
