## Problem

Detecting machine-generated texts (MGTs) from human-written texts (HWTs) is difficult because the distributional gap is subtle, especially across diverse LLMs. When training a deep kernel MMD (MMD-D) on MGTs from multiple LLM populations, the intra-class distance term in the MGT side causes the MMD variance to grow sharply, leading to unstable discrepancy estimation and degraded detection performance. The paper identifies and addresses this high-variance optimization failure specific to multi-population training data.

## Input / Output

For paragraph-based detection (two-sample test), the method takes a set of test paragraphs (each sentence treated as an instance) and a reference set of HWT paragraphs, producing a p-value via permutation testing with the trained kernel. For sentence-based detection (single-instance detection), it takes a single test sentence and a referenced HWT set, computing a biased MMD distance between the sentence and the reference set, then reporting AUROC across all test sentences. Notably, the kernel is trained with the modified MPP objective but testing reverts to standard MMD to ensure the distance is zero under the null hypothesis rather than negative.

## Method

MMD-MP replaces the standard MMD training objective with a multi-population proxy (MPP) that removes the intra-class kernel term k(y,y') from the MGT side, retaining only E[k(x,x') − 2k(x,y)]. The deep kernel is then trained by maximizing a regularized test-power criterion J = MPP / σ, where σ is estimated from the U-statistic variance of the modified kernel terms. The key insight is that the k(y,y') term enforces implicit 'pairing rules' for aggregating MGTs that, when MGTs span multiple LLM populations, inadvertently scatter HWT instances and inflate variance; removing it decouples the two populations' aggregation. The paper provides theoretical guarantees including asymptotic normality of the MPP estimator, a test-power expression, and a uniform convergence bound for the optimization. Two detection procedures are built on the trained kernel: a permutation-based two-sample test for paragraphs and a per-sentence MMD distance to a reference HWT set for single-instance detection.

## Results

On HC3 with 3,100 training paragraphs (paragraph-based detection), MMD-MP achieves test power of 93.21% on ChatGPT, 89.36% on GPT3-S, and 79.68% on Neo-S, outperforming MMD-D by up to 4.23 points. For sentence-based detection (AUROC, 3,100 paragraphs), MMD-MP reaches 96.20% on ChatGPT and 95.08% on GPT3-S, surpassing the SOTA model-based detector ChatGPT-D by 1.20 points. On unknown LLMs (trained on ChatGPT/GPT2, tested on Neo-L, GPT-j-6b, GPT4all-j), MMD-MP achieves test power of 61.79–77.69%, exceeding MMD-D by 23.61–27.65 points, demonstrating strong transferability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMD-MP consistently outperforms MMD-D (Liu et al., ICML 2020) across all settings: by 1.45–4.23 points in test power on known LLMs (Table 1) and by a dramatic 23.61–27.65 points on unknown LLMs (Table 5), confirming that removing the intra-class MGT term stabilizes the kernel and improves generalization. Against classifier-based two-sample tests, MMD-MP surpasses C2ST-L (Cheng & Cloninger, 2022) by 3.39 points on ChatGPT and 18.71 points on Neo-S, and C2ST-S (Lopez-Paz & Oquab, 2017) by 30.38 points on ChatGPT. For sentence-based detection (Table 3), MMD-MP beats the SOTA model-based detector ChatGPT-D (Guo et al., 2023) by 1.20 AUROC on ChatGPT and outperforms the CE-Classifier (same architecture + binary head) by 0.61–2.64 AUROC, showing the MMD-MP objective itself is more discriminative than cross-entropy training. All metric-based baselines (Log-Likelihood, Rank, Log-Rank, Entropy, DetectGPT from Solaiman et al. 2019, Gehrmann et al. 2019, Mitchell et al. 2023) are substantially outperformed, with MMD-MP exceeding the best metric-based method (Likelihood at 89.82) by 6.38 AUROC on ChatGPT. MMD-O (bandwidth-optimized Gaussian kernel MMD) performs near-randomly (26–27 test power), highlighting the necessity of the deep kernel.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on deep kernel MMD (MMD-D, Liu et al. 2020) for two-sample testing, extending it to the MGT detection setting where training data spans multiple LLM populations. It departs from MMD-D by identifying that the intra-class distance term k(y,y') in the MGT population creates a hidden optimization pathology—implicit 'pairing rules' that destabilize the kernel when MGTs are non-IID across populations—and removes this term during training (yielding MPP). The detection framework also connects to the broader MGT detection literature (metric-based: DetectGPT, log-likelihood; model-based: ChatGPT-D, CE-classifiers), but positions itself as a distributional-discrepancy approach rather than a classifier or scoring-model approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-population problem is increasingly practical as the LLM ecosystem diversifies (GPT-2, GPT-3, GPT-Neo, GPT-4, open-source variants), and the 23–27% test-power gain on *unknown* LLMs demonstrates that MMD-MP's reduced reliance on specific MGT types translates to genuine transferability. A key open question is whether the same intra-class-removal principle applies to other kernel-based discrepancy measures or to settings where the HWT reference itself is multi-population. The method also requires a frozen feature extractor and a reference HWT set, limiting its applicability in fully unsupervised or streaming scenarios.
