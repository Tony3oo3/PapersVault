## Problem

Existing single-cell pre-trained models (scBERT, scGPT, Geneformer) treat genes as tokens and cells as sentences, modeling only intra-cell gene-gene conditional probabilities. This overlooks three fundamental differences from NLP: scRNA-seq data is a bag-of-genes rather than a sequence, cell-cell interactions are biologically critical yet entirely unmodeled, and single-cell datasets are orders of magnitude smaller and noisier than text corpora. No prior pre-trained transformer captures inter-cell relations or jointly leverages spatially-resolved transcriptomic data.

## Input / Output

The model consumes cell-by-gene count matrices from scRNA-seq (sparse, ~90% zeros) and spatially-resolved transcriptomic (SRT) data that additionally provides 2D cell coordinates within a field-of-view. Pre-training uses a 13,500-gene intersection across all datasets, while SRT data covers only ~1,000 genes, so masking and reconstruction are restricted to genes measured in each platform. At inference, CellPLM processes all cells in a tissue simultaneously (batch size up to 48,082) rather than attending over gene tokens, yielding ~100× faster embedding generation than gene-token models like Geneformer or scGPT.

## Method

CellPLM inverts the token hierarchy by treating cells as tokens and tissues as sentences, so that self-attention naturally encodes cell-cell relationships. A gene expression embedder aggregates learnable per-gene embedding vectors weighted by expression counts (bag-of-words style), then a linear-complexity Flowformer encoder with 2D sinusoidal positional encodings derived from SRT coordinates captures inter-cell interactions across up to 10,000 cells per tissue. The latent space employs a 16-component Gaussian mixture prior rather than an isotropic Gaussian, preserving biological cluster structure while a batch-aware decoder (conditioned on a learnable batch embedding) removes technical artifacts. Pre-training optimizes a denoising variational lower bound under a cell-level masked language modeling objective, where a fraction of cells are selected and a subset of their gene expressions are masked for reconstruction via reparameterization and Monte Carlo sampling.

## Results

On scRNA-seq denoising, fine-tuned CellPLM achieves the lowest RMSE of 0.725 (PBMC 5K) and 0.391 (Jurkat), outperforming the next-best method DCA (0.775 / 0.423) and the pre-trained scGPT (0.901 / 0.711). On spatial transcriptomic imputation, fine-tuned CellPLM reaches Pearson correlation of 0.318 (Lung2) and 0.328 (Liver2), surpassing the best baseline SpaGE (0.227 / 0.253) by ~40%. For cell type annotation, CellPLM attains F1 of 0.766 (MS) and 0.749 (hPancreas), exceeding scGPT (0.703 / 0.718) and CellTypist (0.667 / 0.708). Inference speed is over 100× faster than Geneformer (0.85 s vs. 428 s) and ~150× faster than scGPT (0.85 s vs. 129 s) for 48,082 cells.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CellPLM consistently outperforms all 21 baselines across every evaluated task. In scRNA-seq denoising, fine-tuned CellPLM beats the strongest non-pre-trained baselines DCA (0.775) and scVI (0.777) by ~5–6% RMSE on PBMC 5K, and surpasses the pre-trained scGPT (0.901) by 19.6% RMSE; even zero-shot CellPLM (0.854) exceeds 6 of 8 non-pre-trained baselines. For spatial transcriptomic imputation, fine-tuned CellPLM leads SpaGE (0.227), stPlus (0.177), gimVI (0.130), and Tangram (0.123) by 40–160% in Pearson correlation on Lung2, and by similar margins on Liver2. In cell type annotation, CellPLM (F1 0.766 on MS, 0.749 on hPancreas) outperforms the pre-trained scGPT (0.703 / 0.718) and scBERT (0.599 / 0.685), as well as non-pre-trained methods CellTypist (0.667 / 0.708), ACTINN (0.628 / 0.705), SingleCellNet (0.637 / 0.739), and TOSICA (0.578 / 0.656). In perturbation prediction, CellPLM achieves the lowest RMSE on both Adamson and Norman Perturb-Seq datasets compared to GEARS and scGen. On zero-shot clustering, CellPLM embeddings yield the highest ARI and NMI against PCA, Geneformer, scGPT, and even scVI trained on the target dataset, while being over 100× faster at inference than Geneformer and scGPT.

## Relation to Prior Work

CellPLM departs from the dominant paradigm in single-cell pre-training (scBERT, scGPT, Geneformer, xTrimoGene) that treats genes as tokens and cells as sentences, instead proposing a 'cell language model' where cells are tokens and tissues are sentences. It builds on Gaussian mixture variational autoencoders (Dilokthanakul et al., 2016) for the probabilistic latent space, the denoising variational lower bound (Im et al., 2017) as the optimization objective, and the selective gene-level masking strategy from SpaFormer (Wen et al., 2023). The batch-aware decoder design is inherited from scVI (Lopez et al., 2018), and the efficient transformer implementation uses Flowformer (Wu et al., 2022) to handle the large number of cell tokens per tissue. The key conceptual departure is incorporating spatially-resolved transcriptomic data as a new modality during pre-training, which no prior single-cell foundation model has attempted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CellPLM addresses a fundamental mismatch in how single-cell data has been modeled by NLP-inspired architectures, and the 100x inference speedup makes cell-level representation learning practically viable for large cohorts. The joint pre-training on scRNA-seq and SRT data opens a path toward spatially-aware foundation models, though the current SRT data is limited to a single platform (CosMx) and a small gene panel (~1,000 genes). Key limitations include the restricted 13,500-gene set from inner-joining heterogeneous scRNA-seq sources, and the pre-training corpus (~11.4M cells) remains orders of magnitude smaller than NLP corpora. Open questions include whether the cell-token paradigm scales to multi-omics (proteomics, epigenomics) and whether the GMM prior's number of components should be adaptive per tissue rather than fixed.
