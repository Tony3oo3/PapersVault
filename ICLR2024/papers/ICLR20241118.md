## Problem

Text-based RL for molecular design has accumulated many algorithmic components (KL regularization, replay buffers, various architectures, different molecular grammars) from prior work, but lacks a systematic empirical study identifying which choices actually improve performance. Additionally, recent advances in language modeling (transformers, large-scale pretraining) have not been incorporated into molecular RL methods, and the relative importance of pretraining data quality versus size remains unclear for this domain.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reward function (e.g., docking score via QuickVina 2, QED, SA, or combinations thereof) that scores the final molecule, and autoregressively generates a molecular text string token-by-token until an [EOS] token is emitted. The state at each step is the full concatenation of previously generated tokens, making the MDP non-Markovian in the graph representation due to many-to-one text-to-molecule mappings. Evaluation allows up to 25,000 unique and 40,000 total oracle calls, with performance measured by average score of top-k (k=1, 10, 100) molecules plus diversity and redundancy metrics.

## Method

ChemRLformer pretrains an autoregressive language model (GPT-style transformer, GRU-based RNN, or fully-connected network) on molecular text datasets via next-token prediction, then fine-tunes it as an RL policy using REINFORCE with a sparse terminal reward. The key algorithmic components are a hill-climb replay buffer (sampling from the highest-scoring molecules seen so far) and a Log P regularizer that penalizes overconfident actions to encourage exploration. Through extensive ablation across 25 tasks, the paper establishes that SMILES outperforms SELFIES, pretraining data quality (ChEMBL) matters far more than data size (outperforming 100× larger ZINC subsets), transformer and RNN architectures perform comparably, and KL regularization toward the pretrained policy provides no benefit when sufficient oracle calls are available.

## Results

On 22 PyTDC tasks, ChemRLformer (SMILES + Transformer + REINFORCE + hill-climb buffer + Log P regularization) achieves a normalized top-100 score of 0.745, compared to 0.422 for the pretrained model alone, demonstrating a ~76% relative improvement from RL fine-tuning. On 5 protein docking targets (QuickVina 2), the full method reaches a normalized top-100 docking score of 12.490 versus 10.811 for pretraining alone. The best pretraining dataset (ChEMBL, 1.2M curated molecules) outperforms agents pretrained on ZINC 100M (100× larger), confirming that data quality matters more than quantity. The hill-climb replay buffer provides a 13% improvement in top-100 rewards over a standard buffer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not provide a single head-to-head numerical comparison table against prior methods (REINVENT, REINVENT 2.0, Taiga2.0, MoGPT, etc.); instead, it frames its contribution as an empirical ablation study identifying which design choices actually help. The most direct comparison is between the pretrained model alone and the full ChemRLformer with RL fine-tuning: RL improves the normalized PyTDC top-100 score from 0.422 to 0.745 (+76%) and the docking top-100 score from 10.811 to 12.490 (+15%). Among text representations, SMILES consistently outperforms SELFIES across all architectures and tasks, while SELFIES agents show higher diversity but also higher redundancy (exploring a smaller region of molecular space). Among architectures, Transformer and RNN (GRU) achieve comparable performance, while FC underperforms on PyTDC tasks but exploits a reward-hacking shortcut on docking (generating long C/N chains). For algorithmic choices, the hill-climb replay buffer yields a 13% top-100 improvement over a standard buffer, Log P regularization outperforms entropy regularization for exploration, and KL regularization to the pretrained policy provides no measurable benefit when sufficient oracle calls are available. In an appendix comparison, vanilla REINFORCE outperforms PPO on PyTDC tasks, attributed to PPO's value function being poorly suited to the sparse, terminal-only reward structure of molecular optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

ChemRLformer extends the text-based RL framework for molecular design established by REINVENT (Olivecrona et al., 2017) and REINVENT 2.0 (Blaschke et al., 2020), which formulated molecule generation as autoregressive token prediction trained with REINFORCE. The paper departs from prior work by systematically ablating each design choice (text grammar, architecture, replay buffer strategy, regularization) across 25 tasks, and by replacing the GRU-based policy with GPT-style transformers. Rather than proposing a fundamentally new algorithm, ChemRLformer is a curated combination of the most effective components from the REINVENT lineage, validated through extensive empirical comparison against both text-based and graph-based RL baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with a clear, evidence-based recipe for text-based molecular RL, demystifying which components actually help (hill-climb buffer, Log P regularization, high-quality pretraining data) and which do not (KL regularization, actor-critic algorithms like PPO). The discovery that RL agents exploit docking score reward functions to generate chemically trivial molecules is an important warning for the field's evaluation methodology. Open questions include designing more robust reward functions that resist hacking, whether better pretraining objectives beyond next-token prediction could further improve performance, and whether the quality-over-size finding generalizes to other combinatorial optimization domains.
