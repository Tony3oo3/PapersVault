## Problem

Transformer-based time series forecasters embed multiple variates of the same timestamp into a single temporal token, which fuses distinct physical measurements, produces meaningless attention maps due to time-unaligned events, and degrades performance with larger lookback windows—sometimes falling below simple linear models. The paper argues that the Transformer architecture is not inherently ineffective for time series but is improperly applied, with its components assigned to the wrong dimensions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240437/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a multivariate lookback window X ∈ R^{T×N} (T time steps, N variates) and transposes it so that each variate's full time series becomes an independent token, yielding N tokens of dimension T. An MLP embeds each series into a D-dimensional variate token, and a final MLP projection decodes each token back to S predicted time steps, producing Ŷ ∈ R^{S×N}. No positional encoding is required because temporal order is implicitly captured by the feed-forward network's neuron permutations, and the number of variate tokens can vary between training and inference.

## Method

iTransformer inverts the standard Transformer encoder without modifying any native component: self-attention operates across variate tokens to capture multivariate correlations, while the shared feed-forward network processes each variate token independently to learn series-global representations (amplitude, periodicity, frequency). Layer normalization is applied per variate token rather than per timestamp, which handles non-stationarity and reduces discrepancies from inconsistent physical measurements. The architecture is encoder-only with L stacked blocks (LayerNorm + Self-Attention + LayerNorm + FFN), and the final prediction is produced by a linear MLP projection from the D-dimensional token to the S-step prediction. The framework is plug-and-play with any efficient attention mechanism (Reformer, Informer, FlashAttention, Flowformer), and an efficient training strategy randomly samples a subset of variates per batch to reduce memory while preserving full-variate prediction at inference.

## Results

iTransformer achieves the best average MSE on 5 out of 7 dataset groups: ECL (0.178), Traffic (0.428), Weather (0.258), Solar-Energy (0.233), and PEMS (0.119). On the low-dimensional ETT (7 variates), RLinear edges ahead with MSE 0.380 vs. iTransformer's 0.383, and on Exchange (8 variates), DLinear leads with 0.354 vs. 0.360. The largest margins appear on high-dimensional datasets: on PEMS, iTransformer (0.119) outperforms the next-best SCINet (0.121) and TimesNet (0.148), and on Traffic (862 variates) it beats PatchTST (0.481) by ~11% in MSE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

iTransformer outperforms all 10 baselines on ECL (321 variates), Traffic (862 variates), Weather, Solar-Energy, and PEMS (170–883 variates), with the most dramatic wins on high-dimensional data: on PEMS it beats SCINet (0.121) and TimesNet (0.148) by 1–20% in MSE, and on Traffic it surpasses PatchTST (0.481) by 11%. On the low-dimensional ETT (7 variates), RLinear narrowly leads (0.380 vs. 0.383 MSE), and on Exchange (8 variates) DLinear is slightly better (0.354 vs. 0.360), suggesting that with very few variates the multivariate-correlation advantage of attention is less critical. Crossformer, which explicitly models cross-variate dependencies via a redesigned attention, is consistently outperformed by iTransformer on all datasets except Weather where they tie (0.258 vs. 0.259), indicating that the inverted architecture captures multivariate correlations more effectively than cross-dimension patching. Among Transformer-based baselines, iTransformer dominates Autoformer (0.227→0.178 on ECL), FEDformer (0.214→0.178), and Stationary (0.193→0.178), while the gap to PatchTST (0.205→0.178) is most pronounced on PEMS where PatchTST's patching mechanism struggles with rapid fluctuations. Linear baselines (DLinear, RLinear, TiDE) remain competitive on small-variate datasets but degrade sharply on high-dimensional ones (e.g., RLinear MSE 0.514 on PEMS vs. iTransformer's 0.119), confirming that explicit multivariate correlation modeling via attention is essential at scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the vanilla Transformer encoder (Vaswani et al., 2017) but departs fundamentally from how prior time-series forecasters apply it. While Autoformer, FEDformer, Informer, and PatchTST all embed multiple variates at the same timestamp into a single temporal token and apply attention along the time axis, iTransformer inverts this: each entire variate series becomes one token, attention captures cross-variate correlations, and the FFN encodes temporal representations. The paper positions itself as the fourth category of Transformer modifications (after component adaptation, inherent processing like patching/channel independence, and joint component-architecture changes) by modifying no native components at all—only the dimension on which they operate. It also draws on the channel-independence insight from PatchTST and the linear-forecaster critique (DLinear, TiDE) to argue that the FFN on the temporal dimension subsumes the role of linear layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reframes the 'Transformers vs. linear models' debate by showing the issue was architectural misapplication rather than inherent Transformer inadequacy, providing a simple drop-in inversion that consistently improves Transformer, Reformer, Informer, Flowformer, and FlashAttention variants by 17–39% MSE. The variate-token formulation enables generalization to unseen variates and flexible variate counts at inference, opening a path toward time-series foundation models. Open questions include whether the quadratic attention over variates scales to thousands of channels (mitigated but not eliminated by efficient attention and partial-variate training), whether large-scale pre-training across heterogeneous domains preserves the inverted structure's advantages, and how the approach extends to other time-series tasks beyond forecasting (anomaly detection, imputation, classification).
