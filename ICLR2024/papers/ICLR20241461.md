## Problem

Standard policy gradient methods assume fully observable Markovian dynamics, but in partially observable settings the hidden state acts as a time-varying unmeasured confounder that simultaneously affects actions, rewards, and future transitions, rendering naive off-policy gradient estimators biased. No prior work has established a policy gradient method for confounded POMDPs in the offline setting with both statistical and computational guarantees, particularly when state and observation spaces are continuous and require non-parametric function approximation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an offline dataset of N i.i.d. trajectories (o_0^n, (o_t^n, a_t^n, r_t^n)_{t=1}^T) generated by a behavior policy that depends on the unobserved state, along with user-specified function classes (e.g., RKHS, neural networks) for bridge functions and test functions. It outputs a history-dependent policy π_θ = {π_{θ_t}}_{t=1}^T where each π_{θ_t} maps the current observation and full history (O_1, A_1, ..., O_t, H_{t-1}) to a distribution over a finite action space, selected uniformly from all K gradient-ascent iterates. The policy parameter θ ∈ R^{d_Θ} is a concatenation of per-step parameters, with d_Θ = Σ_t d_{Θ_t} growing with horizon length T.

## Method

The key identification result shows that the policy gradient ∇_θ V(π_θ) can be expressed as E[Σ_a b_{∇V,1}^{π_θ}(a, O_1)] using gradient-bridge functions that satisfy a sequence of conditional moment restrictions linking observable variables (A_t, O_t, H_{t-1}, O_0) to the unobserved state S_t. These moment restrictions form a sequential non-parametric instrumental variables problem, solved via a min-max estimation procedure (Dikkala et al., 2020) where bridge functions b_t are estimated backward from t=T to t=1 by alternating minimization over the bridge class and maximization over a test function class. A completeness assumption on the conditional distribution of O_0 given (S_t, A_t, H_{t-1}) ensures the bridge functions are identifiable, while a baseline covariate O_0 collected before the decision process provides the instrumental variable. The estimated gradient drives a gradient ascent update θ^{(k+1)} = θ^{(k)} + η_k ∇_{θ^{(k)}} V̂(π_{θ^{(k)}}), with global convergence guaranteed under β-smoothness, Fisher-information positive definiteness, and L-smoothness of the policy value, yielding a suboptimality bound of O_P(1/√N + 1/√K) + ε_approx.

## Results

The paper establishes a non-asymptotic policy gradient estimation error bound (Theorem 2): sup_θ ‖∇_θ V(π_θ) − ∇̂_θ V(π_θ)‖_{ℓ²} ≲ τ_max C_{π^b} T^{5/2} M_B M_F d_Θ √(log(c₁T/ζ)·γ(F,B)/N), achieving the optimal O_P(1/√N) rate. The suboptimality bound (Theorem 3) decomposes into optimization error O(√d_Θ·T^{4.5}/√K), statistical error O(τ_max C_{π^b} T^{5.5} d_Θ/√N), a compound error term, and an approximation error ε_approx. In the simulation study (Figure 2), the proposed method converges to the in-class optimal policy value, while the naive method plateaus at a suboptimal value due to irreducible bias from ignoring the unobserved state, and behavioral cloning performs significantly worse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three methods in a simulation study using RKHS with a Gaussian kernel: (1) the proposed policy gradient method with bridge-function identification, (2) a naive method that treats the observation O_t as the state and applies standard off-policy gradient estimation, and (3) behavioral cloning. The proposed method successfully converges to the in-class optimal policy value as iterations increase, because its gradient estimator is uniformly consistent over the policy class. The naive method fails to reach the optimal policy regardless of the number of iterations K, as it suffers from an irreducible bias at each step due to the confounding effect of the unobserved state S_t (i.e., E[R_t | S_t, O_t, A_t=a] ≠ E[R_t | O_t, A_t=a] in general). Behavioral cloning performs significantly worse than both gradient-based methods since it merely imitates the behavior policy rather than optimizing for the true reward. The paper does not provide a tabular comparison with specific numerical values; the comparison is qualitative via the convergence curves in Figure 2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the bridge function framework from proximal causal inference (Miao et al., 2018; Tchetgen Tchetgen et al., 2020) and its recent applications to off-policy evaluation in confounded POMDPs (Bennett & Kallus, 2021; Shi et al., 2022; Miao et al., 2022) to the novel task of directly identifying and estimating the policy gradient, rather than the policy value. Computationally, it adopts the minimax estimation procedure for conditional moment restrictions from Dikkala et al. (2020) to solve the resulting non-parametric instrumental variables problem. The key departure from prior confounded POMDP policy learning work (Guo et al., 2022; Lu et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2022) is that those methods either lack computational algorithms or require restrictive memorylessness assumptions on the unobserved confounder, whereas this paper identifies the gradient directly and avoids such assumptions through the history-dependent policy formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide a complete policy gradient method for confounded POMDPs in the offline setting with both statistical (non-asymptotic gradient estimation error) and computational (global convergence) guarantees, filling a significant gap between the rich MDP policy gradient literature and the limited POMDP results. The suboptimality bound matching MDP rates (O_P(1/√N) + O_P(1/√K)) suggests that the confounding and partial observability do not fundamentally worsen the statistical efficiency, which is reassuring for practitioners. Open questions include whether the bound is minimax optimal, whether full coverage can be relaxed to partial coverage via pessimism, and how the method performs in practical settings with high-dimensional continuous state and observation spaces.
