## Problem

Existing diverse RL methods either require expensive online interaction or use task-agnostic diversity objectives that produce poor-performing policies on heterogeneous offline data. Conversely, standard offline RL methods impose conservative constraints on the entire dataset, offering no mechanism to extract or control the distinct behavioral styles naturally present in multi-source data. SORL addresses this gap by jointly optimizing policy quality and behavioral diversity from a single offline heterogeneous dataset.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241998/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SORL takes as input a fixed offline dataset of state-action trajectories collected from multiple behavioral sources (e.g., human players in Atari or a basketball game) and a hyperparameter m specifying the number of output policies. It produces m distinct policies, each parameterized by a shared backbone network with a LoRA adapter (rank 9) that encodes a specific behavioral style. The dataset is consumed as weighted trajectories rather than hard partitions, with each trajectory receiving a soft posterior weight per style, and the output policies are evaluated on quality (episode return), diversity (entropy of style assignments), and consistency (in-distribution action probability).

## Method

SORL is a two-step framework. First, an EM-based style clustering alternates between an E-step that computes each trajectory's posterior probability of belonging to each style by averaging the style policy's action likelihoods across all steps (avoiding numerical instability from long-horizon multiplication), and an M-step that updates each style policy via weighted behavior cloning where the weight is the trajectory's posterior for that style. Second, advantage-weighted style learning solves a KL-constrained optimization for each style: each policy π^(i) maximizes expected advantage A^μ(s,a) (computed from the full-dataset policy) while remaining within a KL budget ε of its style policy μ^(i). The closed-form solution π^(i)* ∝ μ^(i)(a|s)·exp(A^μ(s,a)/λ) is learned by minimizing a weighted cross-entropy loss where trajectories are weighted by their style posterior and actions are reweighted by the exponential of the advantage, effectively combining AWR's performance improvement with style-specific data weighting to produce high-quality yet behaviorally distinct policies.

## Results

On six Atari games (SpaceInvaders, MsPacman, MontezumaRevenge, Enduro, Riverraid, Frostbite), SORL achieves the highest diversity (0.84–1.05) and consistency (93.5%–96.2%) across all games, and secures the top quality score in 4 out of 6 games (e.g., 622.2 on MsPacman, 1931.2 on Riverraid). On the real-world basketball game Dunk City Dynasty, SORL outperforms both baselines on all three metrics simultaneously: quality 5.3 vs. 1.7 (Off-RLPMM) and 5.0 (InfoGAIL), diversity 1.05 vs. 0.51 and 0.73, and consistency 40.7% vs. 38.6% and 39.8%. In the didactic Grid Shooting environment, SORL's two learned policies exhibit clearly distinct reward profiles (shooting: 2.6 vs. 1.1; stars: 2.2 vs. 6.3) with winning rates of 51.0% and 59.3%, respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SORL is compared against two diverse-behavior baselines (Off-RLPMM and InfoGAIL) and three standard offline RL methods (CQL, AWR, IQL). Against Off-RLPMM, SORL wins on diversity in 5 of 6 Atari games (e.g., 0.96 vs. 0.22 on SpaceInvaders, 0.84 vs. 0.00 on Enduro) and on consistency across all games, while Off-RLPMM edges out SORL on quality in SpaceInvaders (412.5 vs. 387.1) and Frostbite (2183.2 vs. 2056.0). Against InfoGAIL, SORL dominates on diversity (0.84–1.05 vs. 0.00 in nearly all Atari games) and consistency, while InfoGAIL achieves competitive or higher quality in some games (e.g., 2584.7 vs. 2056.0 on Frostbite) but fails to produce any behavioral diversity. In the real-world Dunk City Dynasty experiment, SORL outperforms both baselines on all three metrics simultaneously (quality 5.3 vs. 1.7/5.0; diversity 1.05 vs. 0.51/0.73; consistency 40.7% vs. 38.6%/39.8%). Against standard offline RL baselines (CQL, AWR, IQL in Appendix G), SORL achieves the highest quality in 4 of 6 Atari games, confirming that the diversity constraint does not sacrifice task performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

SORL sits at the intersection of three research threads: offline RL (CQL, IQL, AWR) which optimizes quality but ignores dataset diversity; diverse/quality-diversity RL (MAP-Elites, diverse policy gradient) which requires costly online interaction; and multi-modal imitation learning (InfoGAIL, RLPMM) which handles heterogeneous data but lacks a policy improvement step. The paper's core departure is combining EM-based style clustering (adapted from RLPMM) with advantage-weighted regression (from AWR/TRPO) in a fully offline two-stage pipeline, using soft trajectory weighting rather than hard partitioning. The LoRA-based parameter sharing across style-specific policies is a practical architectural choice borrowed from the LLM adaptation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SORL provides a principled recipe for extracting multiple high-performing, behaviorally distinct policies from a single heterogeneous offline dataset without any online interaction, which is directly applicable to game AI, opponent modeling, and autonomous driving where online access is restricted. The consistency metric is a useful addition that guards against out-of-distribution diversity inflation. Key limitations include the fixed number of policies m, the assumption that styles are disjoint (no shared behaviors), and EM convergence only to a saddle point. The authors note future directions toward flexible policy behavior formulations and adaptive task switching, but the current framework is relatively rigid for real-world deployment where behavioral boundaries are fuzzy.
