## Problem

Quantifying multimodal interactions (redundancy, uniqueness, synergy) has traditionally required either full labeled multimodal data or post-hoc analysis of trained models. In the semi-supervised setting where only labeled unimodal data and unlabeled paired multimodal data are available, synergy S cannot be computed exactly because the full joint distribution p(x₁,x₂,y) is unknown. This paper derives rigorous lower and upper bounds on S that depend solely on the available semi-supervised data, enabling interaction quantification, performance prediction, and model selection without ever training a multimodal model.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three data sources: labeled unimodal datasets D₁={(x₁,y)} and D₂={(x₂,y)} for each modality, and unlabeled paired multimodal data D_M={(x₁,x₂)}. For high-dimensional continuous modalities (images, video, text, audio), features are first discretized via clustering to finite supports, and unimodal classifiers are trained to estimate disagreement. Outputs include exact redundancy R and uniqueness U₁, U₂ (via convex optimization), two synergy lower bounds S_R and S_U, a synergy upper bound S̄ (via min-entropy coupling approximation), and derived performance bounds P̲_acc and P̄_acc on the Bayes-optimal multimodal classifier. The full computation takes under 1 minute and 180 MB memory on datasets of 1,000–20,000 points.

## Method

The core mechanism builds on Partial Information Decomposition (PID) with the Bertschinger et al. definition, where R, U₁, U₂ are computed exactly via convex optimization over distributions matching unimodal marginals. For synergy, the paper exploits two structural relationships: (1) S = R − I_p(X₁;X₂) + I_p(X₁;X₂|Y), yielding lower bound S_R by minimizing conditional MI over auxiliary distributions r matching all three pairwise marginals (a tractable convex program); and (2) a triangle-inequality argument linking modality disagreement α(f₁,f₂) to U+S via Pinsker's inequality, yielding lower bound S_U = α·c − max(U₁,U₂). The upper bound S̄ is derived by relaxing the min-entropy coupling problem (shown NP-hard for |Y|≥4) to a variant admitting O(k log k) greedy approximations. Finally, Feder–Merhav bounds combined with Fano's inequality convert total information R+U₁+U₂+S into accuracy bounds on the optimal multimodal classifier, enabling performance prediction and fusion-model selection purely from semi-supervised data.

## Results

On 10 real-world MultiBench datasets (700K+ datapoints), the derived lower bounds S_R and S_U track true synergy S from below, and the upper bound S_bar tracks S from above, with the bounds always holding. On synthetic bitwise distributions (100K samples), the lower bounds have an average gap of 0.18 bits from true S, while the upper bound has an average gap of 0.62 bits (tightening to 0.24 when S > 0.6). For performance prediction (Table 3), the estimated average multimodal accuracy closely matches actual model performance: e.g., on MOSEI the estimate is 80% vs. actual 82–88%, on MUSTARD 121% (upper) vs. actual 79%, and on MIMIC 102% vs. actual 92%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper, so there is no direct method-vs-method comparison. Instead, the validation compares the derived bounds against ground-truth synergy S (computed with oracle access to the full joint distribution p) and the estimated multimodal performance against actual trained model accuracies. The lower bounds S_R and S_U are always ≤ S and the upper bound S_bar is always ≥ S across all 10 datasets, confirming correctness. For performance estimation, the estimated average P_M falls within or near the range of actual model performances: on MOSEI the estimate (80%) sits between best unimodal (82%) and best complex fusion (88%); on MUSTARD the estimate (121%) exceeds all actual models (74–79%), indicating room for improvement; on MIMIC the estimate (102%) is close to the actual 92% for all model types. The paper also shows that higher estimated P_M correlates with larger gains from unimodal to multimodal (ρ = 0.53 excluding MIMIC outlier) and from simple to complex fusion (ρ = 0.77), providing actionable guidelines for data collection and model selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Partial Information Decomposition (PID) framework of Bertschinger et al. (2014) and Liang et al. (2023a), which compute redundancy and uniqueness via convex optimization over pairwise marginals, to the semi-supervised setting where the full joint distribution is unavailable. The key theoretical departure is connecting synergy estimation to two previously separate threads: (1) min-entropy coupling approximations (Cicalese & Vaccaro, 2002) for the upper bound, and (2) modality disagreement of unimodal classifiers (building on Sridharan & Kakade, 2008) for a second lower bound. Unlike prior interaction-quantification work that measures what a trained model captures (Shapley values, integrated gradients), this paper treats interactions as properties of the data distribution itself, enabling analysis before any model is chosen or trained.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: the derived bounds allow practitioners to estimate optimal multimodal model performance, decide whether collecting labeled multimodal data is worthwhile, and select between simple and complex fusion architectures—all without training a single model. However, limitations remain: the upper bound is NP-hard to compute tightly (requiring min-entropy coupling approximations), the bounds can be loose when no single interaction dominates (as on ENRICO), and continuous high-dimensional modalities require discretization via clustering that introduces approximation error. Open questions include tightening the upper bound, extending to self-supervised and contrastive learning settings, and applying the framework to fairness and privacy-preserving multimodal learning.
