## Problem

Naive Neural SDE implementations frequently fail to train or produce unstable results because the choice of drift and diffusion functions is underexplored—non-Lipschitz or arbitrary neural-network diffusion terms can cause absence of strong solutions, stochastic destabilization, or divergent Euler discretizations. Additionally, no theoretical guarantees exist for Neural SDE robustness under distribution shift, a critical concern for irregular time series data where missing values and irregular sampling intervals naturally induce such shifts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241499/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes irregularly-sampled multivariate time series (e.g., 37 ICU variables over 48 hours, or 34 sepsis-related variables with only ~10% observed timestamps) and univariate/multivariate classification samples (up to 28-dimensional, variable-length sequences). A controlled path X(t) is constructed from the observed data points via cubic spline interpolation, and the latent state z(t) can be evaluated at any continuous time t. The final output is a task-specific prediction (interpolated values, class label, or forecast) obtained by passing z(T) through a two-layer feed-forward network, with the key property that the model remains stable even when up to 70% of input values are missing.

## Method

The paper proposes three theoretically grounded Neural SDE classes that constrain the diffusion structure: (i) Neural LSDE with additive time-dependent noise σ(t)dW and a time-independent drift γ(z), (ii) Neural LNSDE with linear multiplicative noise σ(t)z·dW, and (iii) Neural GSDE in geometric form dz/z = γ(t,z)dt + σ(t)dW, which yields nonnegative solutions with 0 as an absorbing state—mirroring ReLU network behavior. A controlled path X(t) from the underlying time series is incorporated into the drift via a nonlinear neural network ζ(t, z(t), X(t)), enabling the model to capture sequential observations. The key theoretical contribution is proving existence/uniqueness of strong solutions and deriving non-asymptotic Wasserstein-distance bounds showing that output distribution differences decay exponentially with integration depth T under input perturbations of magnitude ρ, providing formal robustness guarantees against missing-data-induced distribution shift.

## Results

On the PhysioNet Mortality interpolation task, Neural LSDE achieves the lowest MSE of 3.111×10⁻³ at 90% observed values, outperforming the previous best mTAND-Full (4.798×10⁻³) by 35%. On PhysioNet Sepsis classification, Neural LNSDE reaches 0.911 AUROC (with observation intensity) and 0.881 (without), surpassing ANCDE (0.900/0.823) and Neural CDE (0.880/0.776). On Speech Commands, Neural LSDE attains 92.7% accuracy, edging out LEAP (92.2%) and Latent-ODE (91.2%). Across 30 UEA/UCR datasets, Neural LNSDE maintains the highest average accuracy at every missing rate: 72.7% (0%), 72.3% (30%), 71.7% (50%), and 70.3% (70%), demonstrating minimal degradation under increasing data loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed Neural SDEs consistently outperform all 18 baselines across every task. On PhysioNet Sepsis, Neural LNSDE (0.911 AUROC with OI) beats the strongest CDE-based competitor ANCDE (0.900) by 1.1 points, and the gap widens dramatically without OI (0.881 vs. 0.823, a 5.8-point margin), showing the diffusion term compensates for missing observation-intensity information. On Speech Commands, Neural LSDE (92.7%) surpasses LEAP (92.2%) and Latent-ODE (91.2%), while the naive Neural SDE (Tzen & Raginsky, 2019) and Latent SDE (Li et al., 2020) are not reported due to training instability. On the 30-dataset robustness benchmark, Neural LNSDE leads at every missing rate, degrading only 2.4 points from 0% to 70% missing, whereas RNN drops 11 points, Neural CDE drops 4.4 points, and the naive Neural SDE drops only 1.4 points but from a much lower baseline (52.6% → 51.2%). GRU-ODE, the strongest ODE-based baseline, trails Neural LNSDE by 5.6 points on regular data and by 4.8 points at 70% missing. The ablation (Table 5) confirms that removing the controlled path (ζ) or replacing the nonlinear diffusion with an affine one causes 10–20 point accuracy drops, validating both design choices.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Neural ODE → Neural CDE → Neural SDE lineage by identifying that the naive Neural SDE formulation (free neural networks for both drift and diffusion) is fundamentally unstable, and instead proposes three theoretically well-defined SDE classes—Langevin-type, Linear Noise, and Geometric—where the diffusion structure is constrained to specific forms (additive, linear multiplicative, or geometric) while neural networks parameterize only the drift. The controlled-path mechanism from Neural CDEs (Kidger et al., 2020) is incorporated into the drift term to capture sequential observations, and the Langevin SDE's invariant measure theory (Chau et al., 2021) is leveraged to derive Wasserstein-distance robustness bounds. The key departure from prior Neural SDE work (Tzen & Raginsky, 2019; Liu et al., 2019) is the shift from 'let the network learn everything' to 'constrain the SDE structure to a class with provable stability, then learn within that class.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide non-asymptotic robustness guarantees (Wasserstein distance bounds decaying exponentially with SDE depth) for Neural SDEs under distribution shift caused by missing data, filling a gap where prior Neural SDE papers offered no such theoretical protection. The practical implication is that practitioners handling irregular clinical or sensor time series now have a principled way to choose SDE structure that guarantees graceful degradation under missingness rather than catastrophic failure. A notable limitation is higher memory usage compared to CDE-based methods, and the theoretical guarantees require specific conditions (e.g., |σ|² > 2L_γ for LNSDE) that may not always hold in practice. Open questions include whether the robustness framework extends to jump SDEs or rough path-driven SDEs, and whether the Geometric SDE's ReLU-like structure can be exploited for more efficient architectures.
