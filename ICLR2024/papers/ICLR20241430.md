## Problem

Existing periodicity detection methods suffer from two fundamental limitations: limited robustness to real-world complications (multiple and irregular periods, trends, noise, outliers, and missing data) and memorylessness, where each time series is analyzed in isolation with no reuse of prior inferences or labeling information. No prior method explicitly handles irregular periods (e.g., monthly patterns with varying day counts), and pipeline-based approaches accumulate errors across sequential stages.

## Input / Output

The method takes N time series of length L that share common period structure (e.g., N daily time points forming N series across days), where each series may contain missing values, irregular sampling, and non-periodic components. The output is a set of Kumaraswamy-distributed posterior parameters q(ω_j | x^{1:N}) for each Ramanujan basis, where ω_j ≈ 1 indicates the corresponding exact period is present. During inference, only the self-attention over time series followed by cross-attention with the Ramanujan dictionary (the red dashed box in Fig. 2) is required, making it a single forward pass without iterative optimization.

## Method

AmortizedPeriod formulates periodicity detection as an amortized variational inference problem over a Bayesian generative model that jointly decomposes time series into periodic (Ramanujan dictionary with a reparameterized horse-shoe prior), trend (thin-plate Gaussian Markov random field), noise (Gaussian), and outlier (Student's t) components. The key reparameterization maps the horse-shoe local shrinkage to ω_j ~ Beta(0.5, 0.5), whose posterior proximity to 1 directly indicates period presence. A structured inference network based on multihead cross-attention blocks (CAB) maps observations to all posterior parameters, with attention computed across time series and Ramanujan bases (not the time dimension), ensuring permutation equivariance. The network is trained by maximizing the ELBO in a self-supervised manner, with an optional semi-supervised term that maximizes the likelihood of correct period indicators and minimizes it for incorrect ones. The structured q-distribution preserves the hierarchical dependency of the generative model (e.g., α and τ are jointly Gaussian given hyperparameters), avoiding the accuracy loss of mean-field approximations.

## Results

AmortizedPeriod achieves the highest micro F1-score across all four datasets while also having the shortest inference time. On the challenging synthetic dataset (v_ε=1, δ%=0.1), it reaches 0.57 test F1 versus 0.28–0.29 for AutoPeriod and RobustPeriod and 0.17 for RPD. On the real-world Yahoo and App Flow datasets, it scores 0.87 and 0.87 test F1 respectively, outperforming the best baseline by 5% and 27%. Inference time is approximately 0.03 seconds per time series, compared to 441–591 seconds for RobustPeriod and 5–8 seconds for RPD, representing at least 55% less computation than the next fastest method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AmortizedPeriod decisively outperforms all three baselines on every dataset. Against AutoPeriod (Vlachos et al., 2005), it gains 46–49 percentage points in test F1 on the synthetic datasets and 12–42 points on the real datasets, while being 4–5× faster. Against RobustPeriod (Wen et al., 2021), the F1 advantage is 46–47 points on synthetic data and 5–31 points on real data, with a dramatic 10,000–18,000× speedup (0.03 s vs. 441–591 s), since RobustPeriod's sequential pipeline (HP filter → wavelet decomposition → Huber-periodogram → Huber-ACF) is extremely costly. Against RPD (Tenneti & Vaidyanathan, 2015), the F1 gap is 53–59 points on synthetic data and 3–27 points on real data, with a 150–260× speedup. The largest relative gains occur on the noisy synthetic data, where the joint Bayesian modeling of trend, noise, and outliers provides the most benefit over the sequential or purely algebraic approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: Ramanujan subspace-based period decomposition (Vaidyanathan 2014; Tenneti & Vaidyanathan 2015, 2016), which was previously cast as a sparse recovery problem solved iteratively per time series, and amortized variational inference (Zhang et al. 2018; Ganguly et al. 2022), which learns a neural encoder to approximate Bayesian posteriors in a single forward pass. It departs from the dominant time-frequency pipeline paradigm (AutoPeriod, RobustPeriod) by jointly modeling periods, trends, noise, and outliers within a single Bayesian generative model rather than processing them sequentially, and it introduces the horse-shoe prior's reparameterized Beta variable ω_j as a direct indicator of period presence. The cross-attention inference network, which operates over time series and Ramanujan bases rather than the time dimension, is a novel architectural choice for this domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AmortizedPeriod is the first periodicity detection method to simultaneously address all six robustness criteria (multiple periods, irregular periods, trends, noise, outliers, missing data), and its amortized inference eliminates the per-series iterative optimization that plagues existing approaches, yielding >55% speedup. The finding that merely 15% of labeled data substantially boosts performance opens a practical path for semi-supervised deployment in settings where expert annotation is scarce. Limitations include the need to train a neural network (unlike traditional methods), the attention mechanism's fixed maximum period P_max, and evaluation on only four datasets with three baselines, leaving open questions about scalability to very long or highly multivariate series and real-time streaming settings.
