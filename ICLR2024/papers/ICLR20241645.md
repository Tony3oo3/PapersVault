## Problem

Existing causal fairness methods assume the causal graph is fully known with no unobserved confounding, an assumption that is unrealistic in practice and can lead to severe fairness violations. When unobserved confounding is present, path-specific causal fairness effects (direct, indirect, spurious) become unidentifiable, rendering standard fairness estimates biased. To the best of the authors' knowledge, no prior work has derived sensitivity bounds for causal fairness (as distinct from counterfactual fairness) under unobserved confounding.

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular data with a binary sensitive attribute A, discrete observed confounders Z, discrete mediators M, and a continuous or discrete outcome Y, along with a user-specified sensitivity parameter Γ_M (controlling the assumed strength of unobserved confounding) and a fairness threshold vector γ = [γ_DE, γ_IE, γ_SE]. It produces a fair predicted outcome ŷ from a 3-layer feed-forward neural network, together with sharp upper and lower bounds on each of the three path-specific causal fairness effects (DE, IE, SE) for every pair of sensitive-attribute values (a_i, a_j) and outcome value y. Two pre-trained density estimators g_A: Z→A and g_M: (A,Z)→M are required at training time to compute the conditional probabilities needed for bound evaluation.

## Method

The framework bridges the gap between level-3 counterfactual queries (causal fairness) and level-2 interventional queries (sensitivity models) via a two-step procedure. First, counterfactual unnesting decomposes each nested counterfactual expression (e.g., P(y_{a_j, m_{a_i}} | a_i)) into a sum of identifiable terms and single-intervention counterfactual effects, using the ancestral set factorization and counterfactual factorization theorems of Correa et al. (2021). Second, the Generalized Marginal Sensitivity Model (GMSM) is applied to each unidentifiable interventional term, yielding sharp (optimal) upper and lower bounds on DE, IE, and SE that are independent of the distribution and dimensionality of the unobserved confounders. Training solves a constrained optimization problem—minimize prediction loss subject to worst-case fairness bounds |CF_E^±| ≤ γ_CF for all three effects—via augmented Lagrangian optimization, where the Lagrangian is updated each outer iteration and the neural predictor is updated via gradient descent over nested inner epochs. The prediction model is a 3-layer feed-forward network with leaky ReLU activations and dropout, and the same architecture is used for the pre-trained density estimators g_A and g_M.

## Results

On the synthetic U_DE dataset (confounding level Φ=2), the proposed Fair robust model achieves the tightest bounds closest to zero across all three path-specific causal fairness effects: DE ∈ [0.00, 0.01], IE ∈ [−0.02, −0.01], and SE = 0.00 (mean ± std over 5 runs). The Standard model, by contrast, shows substantial unfairness with DE ∈ [0.06, 0.14] and IE ∈ [−0.25, −0.14]. On the real-world US Survey of Prison Inmates dataset, the Fair robust model assigns similar predicted sentence lengths across race groups, whereas the Standard model assigns significantly longer sentences to non-white offenders.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper notes that no existing baselines for causal fairness under unobserved confounding exist, so it compares against two internal baselines: (1) Standard, trained only on prediction loss without any fairness constraint, and (2) Fair naive, which enforces causal fairness under the (incorrect) assumption of no unobserved confounding. The Standard model fails to achieve causal fairness, with DE bounds spanning [0.06, 0.14] and IE bounds spanning [−0.25, −0.14], indicating substantial direct and indirect discrimination. Fair naive reduces the DE interval to [0.05, 0.06] and IE to [−0.04, −0.02], but still shows non-negligible unfairness. The proposed Fair robust model narrows the DE interval to [0.00, 0.01] and IE to [−0.02, −0.01], with SE at exactly 0.00, and exhibits the lowest variability across runs (std ≤ 0.02 vs. up to 0.05 for Fair naive). The authors emphasize that all three models use identical neural network architectures, so the fairness improvement is attributable solely to the tailored constrained-optimization objective incorporating the GMSM-based bounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected research streams: causal fairness (which assumes no unobserved confounding, per Zhang & Bareinboim 2018 and Plecko & Bareinboim 2022) and causal sensitivity analysis (which provides sharp bounds under unobserved confounding at the interventional level, per Frauen et al. 2023b). The key technical contribution is a counterfactual unnesting step—decomposing nested counterfactuals (Pearl's ladder level 3) into single-intervention effects (level 2) plus identifiable terms—using the ancestral set and counterfactual factorization theorems of Correa et al. (2021). This departs from Kilbertus et al. (2019), the only prior sensitivity study for fairness, which addressed counterfactual fairness (not causal fairness), modeled only covariate-level confounding, and relied on additive noise models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first sharp (optimal) bounds on path-specific causal fairness effects under unobserved confounding, giving practitioners a concrete refutation strategy: if the bounds on DE, IE, or SE exclude zero even at a plausible sensitivity parameter, the model is provably unfair regardless of the unobserved confounders' distribution. The neural framework with augmented Lagrangian training translates these bounds into a practical worst-case fairness guarantee during model training. Limitations include the focus on binary sensitive attributes and discrete mediators/confounders (extensions to continuous features are sketched but not fully validated), the need for pre-trained density estimators, and the fact that the sensitivity parameter Γ must be set by domain knowledge or heuristics. Open questions include scaling to richer causal graphs with multiple unobserved confounders per edge and data-driven calibration of Γ in high-stakes settings.
