## Problem

Continuous prompts learned on one language model cannot be directly transferred to another because different models learn distinct embedding spaces with different dimensions and geometries. Existing transfer approaches (neural projectors, discretization) either require task supervision on the target model, parallel prompt embeddings, or are specific to particular model pairs, making them inapplicable in zero-shot settings. The paper asks whether the implicit 'task semantics' encoded in a continuous prompt can be generalized across models without any target-side training.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a set of m=5 source prompt embeddings (learned via OptiPrompt on a source model) and a shared vocabulary of 17,230 tokens (the intersection of source and target vocabularies) used as anchors. It produces m target prompt embeddings in the target model's embedding space, normalized to match the target model's word-embedding mean and standard deviation. The relative representation uses top-k=8192 anchor dimensions (selected by highest cosine value), and the source and target embedding dimensions may differ (e.g., 768 vs. 1024 vs. 128 for ALBERT).

## Method

The core mechanism is an encode-then-search strategy grounded in relative representations. First, each source prompt embedding is encoded into a k-dimensional relative vector by computing its cosine similarity to every anchor embedding in the source model. Second, target prompt embeddings are randomly initialized and optimized via gradient descent to maximize the cosine similarity between the source relative vector and the target's own relative vector (computed against the target model's anchor embeddings). This alignment objective is model-agnostic and requires no task supervision or learned projector. A final normalization step rescales the searched embeddings to the target model's word-embedding statistics. The multi-source extension sums the cosine alignment terms across S source models, providing a more robust estimate of task semantics by averaging out model-specific prompt variations.

## Results

On the LAMA-TREx factual probing benchmark (41 relation sub-tasks, micro-average accuracy), the proposed zero-shot relative-space transfer method achieves 49.82% accuracy when transferring from BERT_base to BERT_base, and 45.17% from RoBERTa_base to RoBERTa_base, substantially outperforming the neural projector baseline (26.82% and 35.37% respectively) and the discretization baseline (12.93% and 13.51%). The method surpasses the manual prompting baseline (30.64% for BERT_base, 20.48% for RoBERTa_base) in same-family transfer settings. Multi-source transfer (e.g., BERT_base + BERT_large) further boosts accuracy to 47.78% on BERT_large, approaching the direct tuning upper bound of 51.97%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the discretization baseline (Khashabi et al., 2022), which projects each continuous prompt embedding to its nearest-neighbor token, the proposed method achieves 3–40× higher accuracy across all source-target pairs (e.g., 49.82% vs. 12.93% for BERT_base→BERT_base), confirming that discrete token neighbors cannot capture the implicit task semantics in continuous prompts. Against the neural projector baseline (Su et al., 2022), which trains a two-layer MLP to map source embeddings to target embeddings using anchor words, the proposed zero-shot method consistently outperforms by 10–25 percentage points (e.g., 45.17% vs. 35.37% for RoBERTa_base→RoBERTa_base; 50.21% vs. 4.77% for BERT_large→BERT_large), and crucially requires no task supervision or training on the target model. The method also surpasses the manual prompting baseline from LAMA (Petroni et al., 2019) in same-family transfers (49.82% vs. 30.64% for BERT_base; 45.17% vs. 20.48% for RoBERTa_base), demonstrating that transferred continuous prompts encode richer task semantics than hand-crafted discrete templates. However, the method still falls short of the direct tuning upper bound (e.g., 49.82% vs. 50.56% for BERT_base), and cross-family transfers (e.g., BERT→RoBERTa) show larger gaps, indicating that relative-space alignment is most effective within the same model family.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the relative representation framework from latent space communication (Moschella et al., 2023; Norelli et al., 2022) to the problem of continuous prompt transfer between language models, using shared vocabulary tokens as anchors to define a model-agnostic coordinate system. It departs from prior continuous prompt transfer work (Su et al., 2022), which required training a neural projector with task supervision on the target model, by formulating transfer as a zero-shot search in relative space. The conceptual lineage also draws on cross-lingual word embedding alignment (Faruqui & Dyer, 2014; Artetxe et al., 2018), where relative geometric structure is assumed to be preserved across differently learned embedding spaces. For prompt induction on source models, the paper adopts OptiPrompt (Zhong et al., 2021), situating its contribution squarely within the continuous prompt tuning paradigm (Lester et al., 2021; Li & Liang, 2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot formulation removes the need for task supervision or parallel prompt embeddings on the target model, making prompt transfer practical in settings where the target model is a black box or too large for gradient-based tuning. The finding that larger source models paradoxically produce less transferable prompts (due to greater model-specificity in their expressive embedding spaces) offers a useful design principle: small models may serve as better 'soft prompt engineers' for larger ones. However, the evaluation is confined to factual probing on LAMA with relatively small encoder models (BERT/RoBERTa/ALBERT), and the preliminary GPT-2 results show substantially lower transfer accuracy, leaving open whether the approach scales to modern large language models or generation tasks. The multi-source transfer improvement, while consistent, is modest (1–10 points), and the method's dependence on a large shared vocabulary (8192 anchors) may limit applicability to models with very different tokenizers.
