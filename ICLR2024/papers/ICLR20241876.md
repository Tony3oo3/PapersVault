## Problem

Existing CNN-based and Transformer-based time series forecasting methods model 1D temporal variations directly, which is inherently complex due to overlapping short-term fluctuations, trends, and multi-periodic patterns. While real-world series exhibit clear multi-periodicity (e.g., daily and weekly cycles), no prior method leverages this structure to decouple the 1D series into simpler 2D representations that separate short-term and long-term variations for more efficient and effective modeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241876/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate look-back window X_I ∈ R^{t×d} (t = 336 or 720, d variates) and produces a future prediction X_O ∈ R^{T×d} for T ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720}. Processing is channel-independent: each of the d dimensions is decoupled and forecast separately, then stacked. The look-back window is zero-padded to align with detected period lengths before reshaping into 2D tensors, and the final prediction is produced by a parameter-shared linear layer applied to concatenated outputs from multiple period-specific branches.

## Method

PDF consists of three blocks: (1) a Multi-periodic Decoupling Block that applies FFT to identify k dominant frequencies (selected by both amplitude and frequency value), reshapes the 1D series into k 2D tensors of size f_i × p_i, then extracts long-term patches via 'period patching' (dividing along the period axis and aggregating across the frequency axis so each patch spans all periods) and short-term slices via 'frequency slicing' (splitting along the frequency axis); (2) a Dual Variations Modeling Block with a parallel Transformer encoder for long-term patches and a stack of Conv1d+SELU blocks for short-term slices, whose outputs are summed; (3) a Variations Aggregation Block that concatenates the k period-specific outputs and maps them through a shared linear projection. The key efficiency gain comes from period patching reducing the patch count from t/s to max(p_i)/s, making computational complexity depend only on the maximum decoupled period length rather than the full look-back window.

## Results

On seven real-world long-term forecasting datasets (ETTh1, ETTh2, ETTm1, ETTm2, Weather, Electricity, Traffic) with prediction lengths T ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720}, PDF(720) achieves the best or second-best result in 52 out of 56 MSE/MAE entries, while PDF(336) achieves 64 such entries. Overall, PDF(720) yields a 14.59% MSE reduction and 10.77% MAE reduction versus Transformer-based baselines, a 24.61% MSE reduction and 19.91% MAE reduction versus CNN-based baselines, and a 7.05% MSE reduction and 5.51% MAE reduction versus Linear-based baselines. In terms of computational efficiency, PDF reduces MACs by 34.64% compared to PatchTST and 74.38% compared to Crossformer, with the gap widening to 54.12% and 99.71% respectively at t=960, T=720 on ETTh1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDF(720) outperforms all Transformer-based baselines (PatchTST, FEDformer) across nearly all dataset-prediction-length combinations, with the largest gaps on Traffic (e.g., MSE 0.350 vs. 0.360 for PatchTST and 0.587 for FEDformer at T=96) and ETTh2 at longer horizons. Against CNN-based baselines (TimesNet, MICN), PDF(720) shows the most dramatic improvements, with MICN particularly struggling on ETTh2 (MSE 0.956 vs. 0.378 at T=720) and ETTm2 (MSE 0.502 vs. 0.345 at T=720). Compared to Linear-based baselines, PDF(720) wins on most settings, with the sole exception being TiDE on the Traffic dataset (e.g., MSE 0.336 vs. 0.350 at T=96), which the authors attribute to TiDE's use of static covariate prior knowledge. DLinear is consistently outperformed by PDF across all datasets and prediction lengths. In the patch-information ablation (Table 2), PDF(336) with period patching (24 patches of length 14) outperforms both PatchTST(336) with 42 patches and a longer-patch variant PatchTST(336)* with 24 patches, confirming that long-term semantic content within patches matters more than raw patch length. On computational efficiency (Table 3), PDF reduces MACs by 34.64% over PatchTST and 74.38% over Crossformer on average, with the advantage growing substantially at larger look-back windows (e.g., 99.71% MAC reduction vs. Crossformer at t=960, T=720 on ETTh1).

## Relation to Prior Work

PDF builds directly on the 2D temporal variation modeling paradigm introduced by TimesNet (Wu et al., 2023), which also uses FFT to detect periods and reshapes 1D series into 2D tensors, but departs by replacing TimesNet's convolution-only architecture with a parallel Transformer + Conv1d design. The period patching strategy is a conceptual departure from PatchTST's sequential patching: rather than slicing the time axis into fixed-length segments, PDF aggregates across all periods within each patch, so each patch encodes inter-period (long-term) semantics. The frequency-slicing for short-term variations and the multi-periodic decoupling via a hybrid amplitude-and-value frequency selection criterion extend the single-period approach of TimesNet to handle multi-periodic series simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is decoupling computational complexity from the look-back window length: PDF's encoder complexity is O((max(p_i)/p)²) rather than O((t/p)²), meaning arbitrarily long look-back windows do not inflate cost. This is validated by the 34–74% MACs reduction over PatchTST and Crossformer, and the ability to use t=960 with only 24 patches. However, the method's reliance on detectable periodicity limits its applicability to non-periodic or weakly periodic series, and the frequency selection heuristic (top-k by amplitude plus top-k by value) remains ad hoc. An open question is whether the period-patching idea generalizes to settings where periodicity is non-stationary or multi-scale in a way that a fixed set of k periods cannot capture.
