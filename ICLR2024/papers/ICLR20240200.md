## Problem

No single link prediction algorithm consistently outperforms across datasets from different domains, yet the underlying reasons for this disparity have not been understood from a data-centric perspective. The paper asks: what fundamental data factors drive link formation, how do they interact, and how do these interactions explain the observed model performance gaps?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240200/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on standard undirected graphs with optional node features (CORA, CITESEER, PUBMED, OGBL-COLLAB, OGBL-PPA, OGBL-DDI, plus newly introduced POWER and PHOTO datasets). Rather than producing a single link prediction score, the framework outputs a diagnostic decomposition: which of the three data factors (local structural proximity, global structural proximity, feature proximity) dominates on a given dataset, and how GNN4LP models perform differentially on edges governed by each factor. The practical output includes a four-category dataset taxonomy and a decoupled model architecture.

## Method

The paper identifies three data factors for link prediction—local structural proximity (LSP), global structural proximity (GSP), and feature proximity (FP)—and validates their importance empirically using corresponding heuristic algorithms (CN, Katz, Feature Homophily). It then proposes a latent space model where link probability depends on pairwise latent distance (encoding structural proximity) and a feature proximity parameter β_ij, and proves that each factor provides a meaningful bound on link formation probability. A key theoretical result (Lemma 2) establishes an incompatibility: node pairs with many common neighbors tend to have low feature proximity and vice versa, meaning high LSP and high FP rarely co-occur on the same edge. This incompatibility explains why GNN4LP models, which learn pairwise structural representations, systematically underperform on feature-dominated edges compared to vanilla GNNs like GraphSAGE. As a practical remedy, the paper proposes a decoupled SEAL architecture that routes original node features and structural embeddings (via Double-Radius Node Labeling) through separate GNNs before integration, reducing the conflicting training signals.

## Results

On the newly introduced POWER dataset (GSP-dominated), the Katz heuristic achieves 29.85 Hit@10, substantially outperforming the best GNN4LP model BUDDY (19.88±1.37) and vanilla SAGE (6.99±1.16). On the PHOTO dataset (LSP+FP dominated), SAGE (18.61±5.97) and CN (18.34) lead, while Katz drops to 7.07, confirming that no single method dominates across data categories. The proposed decoupled SEAL achieves up to 1.03% Hit@10 improvement over the original SEAL on OGBL-COLLAB, with a reduced performance gap versus SAGE on feature-dominated edges. GNN4LP models (BUDDY, NeoGNN, NCNC) consistently outperform SAGE on structurally-dominated edges but underperform on edges where feature proximity is the primary signal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares heuristics (CN, Katz, FH), vanilla GNNs (SAGE, GCN, MLP), and GNN4LP models (BUDDY, NeoGNN, NCNC, SEAL) across eight datasets spanning four data-factor categories. On POWER (Category 4: GSP-only), Katz (29.85) decisively beats BUDDY (19.88) by ~10 points and SAGE (6.99) by ~23 points, showing that deep models fail when only global structure matters. On PHOTO (Category 1: LSP+FP), SAGE (18.61) and CN (18.34) are competitive with BUDDY (18.09), while Katz (7.07) is the weakest, confirming that GSP heuristics are ineffective when local structure and features dominate. The decoupled SEAL (the paper's proposed modification) consistently outperforms the original SEAL by up to 1.03% on OGBL-COLLAB and narrows the performance gap with SAGE on feature-dominated edges, validating the incompatibility hypothesis. Across all datasets, no single GNN4LP model is universally best: BUDDY leads on OGBL-DDI/PPA (LSP-dominated), while Katz or SAGE can be competitive on other categories, reinforcing the paper's central claim that model choice must align with the dataset's dominant data factor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent space model of Sarkar et al. (2011) by incorporating a feature proximity parameter and supporting arbitrary degree distributions, enabling a unified theoretical treatment of local structural proximity (LSP), global structural proximity (GSP), and feature proximity (FP) as three distinct data factors. It departs from the dominant model-centric perspective in GNN4LP research (SEAL, NeoGNN, NCNC, BUDDY, NBFNet) by shifting focus to a data-centric analysis of why no single model dominates across datasets. The proposed decoupled SEAL architecture directly modifies the original SEAL design (Zhang & Chen, 2018) by separating feature and structural inputs into independent GNN branches, motivated by the identified incompatibility between these factors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies an overlooked vulnerability in GNN4LP models: they consistently underperform on edges dominated by feature proximity due to conflicting training signals from the structural-feature incompatibility. This provides actionable guidance for model design (decoupled architectures) and benchmark selection (four-category dataset taxonomy with new POWER and PHOTO datasets filling previously uncovered scenarios). A key limitation is the theoretical assumption that feature proximity acts as an additive noise parameter rather than being adaptively integrated in the same latent subspace, which may not hold in all real-world settings. Open questions include designing GNN4LP models specifically tailored to different dataset categories and a more careful fairness discussion of the biased prediction toward structural over feature proximity.
