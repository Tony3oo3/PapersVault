## Problem

Existing ODE-based diffusion samplers (DDIM, DPM-Solver++, EDM) use fixed coefficients predetermined by the ODE formulation and discrete reverse timesteps, which are not optimal—particularly at small NFEs where the per-step integration approximation is coarse. This paper asks whether optimizing these coefficients via a mean-squared-error criterion can improve sampling quality without any modification to the pre-trained model.

## Input / Output

The method operates entirely on pre-trained diffusion models with no retraining. For unconditional generation the input is a single random Gaussian noise vector; for text-to-image generation over StableDiffusion V2 it additionally consumes a text prompt. Output is a synthetic image at the model's native resolution (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 64×64 for FFHQ/AFHQv2/ImageNet-64, 512×512 for StableDiffusion V2). The optimized per-timestep stepsizes are computed once per model–timestep configuration and stored for reuse across all subsequent sampling runs.

## Method

IIA introduces free stepsize coefficients in front of selected gradient quantities within existing ODE solver update expressions and optimizes them by minimizing an MSE that compares a coarse-grained integration step (the modified solver with free stepsizes) against a fine-grained reference obtained by applying the original solver over M uniformly-spaced sub-steps within the same interval. For DDIM, two quantities are appended to the base update: the difference of clean-data estimates and the difference of noise estimates between consecutive timesteps. For EDM, the improved-Euler update is reformulated (Lemma 1) into a weighted sum of [z_i − D_θ(z_i,t_i)] and [D_θ(z_i,t_i) − D_θ(z̃_{i+1},t_{i+1})], with the fixed Euler stepsizes replaced by optimized coefficients that can also incorporate historical gradient directions. Because the objective is quadratic in the stepsizes, the optimization reduces to solving a small linear system using a finite batch of initial noise samples (|B|=200 for EDM; 20 text prompts for text-to-image), and the resulting coefficients are computed once and reused, adding negligible per-sample overhead.

## Results

On StableDiffusion V2 text-to-image generation (COCO 2014 validation), IIA-DPM-Solver++ achieves the best FID of 12.97 at 10 NFEs, outperforming DPM-Solver++ (15.82), DDIM (14.78), and PLMS (24.42). IIA-DDIM achieves FID 13.21 at 10 NFEs vs. DDIM's 14.78. For EDM-based sampling on CIFAR-10, IIA-EDM reduces FID from 14.42 (EDM) to 4.90 at 11 NFEs, and from 29.34 to 12.78 on FFHQ at the same NFE. The gains are most pronounced at low NFEs (<25), with diminishing returns at higher NFEs where the original solvers' integration is already accurate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IIA-DDIM consistently outperforms DDIM (Song et al., ICLR 2021) on FID across all tested NFEs: 13.21 vs. 14.78 at 10 NFEs, 13.14 vs. 14.65 at 20 NFEs, and 13.85 vs. 14.69 at 40 NFEs on StableDiffusion V2. IIA-DPM-Solver++ beats DPM-Solver++ (Lu et al., 2022) by a wider margin at low NFEs (12.97 vs. 15.82 at 10 NFEs) and a smaller margin at higher NFEs (13.68 vs. 14.29 at 40 NFEs). Both IIA methods substantially outperform PLMS (Liu et al., ICLR 2022), which achieves FID 24.42 at 10 NFEs — nearly double the IIA-DPM-Solver++ score. For EDM (Karras et al., NeurIPS 2022), IIA-EDM achieves dramatic FID reductions at low NFEs (e.g., 4.90 vs. 14.42 on CIFAR-10 at 11 NFEs; 12.78 vs. 29.34 on FFHQ at 11 NFEs), but BIIA-EDM slightly underperforms EDM at NFE > 25 on ImageNet-64. IIA-IPNDM shows modest improvement over IPNDM (Zhang & Chen, 2022) on CIFAR-10 but near-identical performance on LSUN Bedroom and Church.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the line of ODE-based diffusion samplers—DDIM, DPM-Solver++, and EDM—whose update coefficients are fixed by the ODE formulation and chosen timesteps. The key departure is treating those coefficients as optimizable variables: by constructing a mean-squared-error objective that compares a coarse single-step update against a fine-grained multi-step approximation of the same integral, the authors solve a small quadratic program to obtain data-dependent stepsizes. The idea of refining numerical integration parameters is related to Bar-Sinai et al. (2019) and Li et al. (2023) in the PDE-solving literature, while the use of historical gradient vectors in BIIA-EDM draws an analogy to SGD with momentum. Unlike DEIS/IPNDM, which change the polynomial order of the noise estimator, IIA keeps the solver structure intact and only adjusts scalar coefficients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The technique is practically attractive because the one-time optimization cost is negligible (tens of seconds) and the resulting stepsizes can be cached for unlimited sampling, making it a drop-in acceleration for any pre-trained diffusion model at low NFEs (<25). A notable open question the authors flag is why, for large NFEs, a more accurate per-step integration approximation (BIIA-EDM) can actually yield slightly worse FID than the original EDM, suggesting that integration accuracy and perceptual quality are not monotonically linked. The method's reliance on a fine-grained reference trajectory (parameter M) and a finite set of initial noise samples for the MSE approximation also leaves room for further study on robustness across architectures and conditioning regimes.
