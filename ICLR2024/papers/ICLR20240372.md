## Problem

Existing extragradient-type algorithms for nonconvex-nonconcave min-max problems under the weak Minty variational inequality (weak MVI) assumption require ρ > -1/(2L) for convergence, limiting the class of solvable problems. The paper identifies that this bound arises from using only a single exploration step per iteration and asks whether multi-step exploration can further relax the condition. The gap is both theoretical (tighter ρ bounds) and practical (algorithms that escape limit cycles in highly non-monotone landscapes).

## Input / Output

The method consumes a smooth (possibly nonconvex-nonconcave) min-max objective f(x,y) through its saddle gradient operator F, an initial point z⁰, a stepsize γ ∈ (0, 1/L], a parameter σ_k ≤ ρ, and tolerance thresholds ε₁, ε₂. The output is a sequence of exploration points z̄ᵏ converging to the saddle point z* with an O(1/√k) best-iterate rate on ‖Fz̄ᵏ‖². Notably, the number of gradient evaluations per outer iteration is not fixed: the adaptive variant (MDEG) performs a variable number of inner GDA steps until the estimated projection distance stops increasing, making the per-iteration cost data-dependent.

## Method

The paper reframes each extragradient update as a projection onto the boundary hyperplane of a weak MVI halfspace D(z̄ᵏ), where the halfspace is guaranteed to contain the solution z*. The key generalization replaces the single forward step z̄ᵏ = zᵏ - γFzᵏ with an n-step gradient descent trajectory z₀ᵏ → z₁ᵏ → … → zₙᵏ, using the final point zₙᵏ as the exploration point. Under the constraint Σᵢ γ_{k,i}L ≤ 1, the lower bound on ρ improves from -1/(2L) to -(1-1/e)/L as n grows, with the optimal invariant stepsize δ = 1/n per sub-step. The adaptive algorithm (MDEG) further exploits the full trajectory by continuing exploration until the signed distance d(zᵏ, D(zᵢᵏ)) stops increasing, effectively projecting onto the farthest separating hyperplane found along the path. A fallback tolerance ε₂ recovers plain GDA when the algorithm stagnates in intractable local regions (e.g., ρ < -1/L pockets), preventing incorrect convergence to spurious limit cycles.

## Results

The paper establishes that multi-step extragradient with n exploration steps relaxes the weak MVI parameter from ρ > −1/(2L) (AdaptiveEG+) to ρ > −(1−1/e)/L ≈ −0.632/L in the limit as n→∞, while maintaining an O(1/√k) best-iterate convergence rate. For the practically relevant n=2 case with equal stepsizes (δ₁=δ₂=1/2), the bound improves to ρ > −5/(9L) ≈ −0.556/L; with optimized unequal stepsizes (δ̂₁≈0.522, δ̂₂≈0.645), it reaches ρ > −0.5834/L. Numerical experiments on three structured examples confirm that the proposed Max Distance Extragradient (MDEG) converges where AdaptiveEG+ and EG+ Adaptive fail, including a problem with ρL≈−0.886 exhibiting limit cycles and a highly non-monotone problem with local ρL≈−3.04.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against AdaptiveEG+ and CurvatureEG+ (Pethick et al., ICLR 2022) and EG+ Adaptive (Böhm, TMLR 2022). AdaptiveEG+ is limited to ρ > −1/(2L) = −0.5/L and fails on Example 1 where ρL ∈ (−0.6, −0.5); the proposed 2-step EG with δ₁=δ₂=1/2 converges there with a guaranteed bound of ρ > −5/(9L). On Example 2 (ρL≈−0.886, two limit cycles), both MDEG and CurvatureEG+ converge to the stationary point, while AdaptiveEG+ and EG+ Adaptive get trapped in the attracting limit cycle; MDEG escapes the cycle within the first iteration using 30 exploration steps. On Example 3 (local ρL≈−3.04, beyond all theoretical guarantees), MDEG with the ε₂ tolerance mechanism bypasses the intractable region and converges, outperforming all other tested algorithms in operator evaluations. The key advantage of MDEG over CurvatureEG+ is that it relies solely on global Lipschitz information (L) without requiring curvature estimates, while achieving comparable or better convergence on the tested problems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the extragradient lineage (Korpelevich 1976 → EG+ by Diakonikolas et al. 2021 → AdaptiveEG+ by Pethick et al. 2022) by replacing the single exploration step with multiple gradient-descent sub-steps before the projection update. The key conceptual bridge is the projection interpretation from Pethick et al.: each update projects onto a hyperplane defined by the weak MVI inequality, and multi-step exploration simply accumulates more halfspace constraints to better separate the iterate from the solution. The paper also connects to Past Extragradient (Popov 1980) and anchoring methods, showing they are special cases of the same projection framework, and introduces Cartesian ovals as the geometric object governing the feasible gradient directions under multi-step Lipschitz constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The relaxation of the weak MVI condition from ρ > −1/2L to ρ > −(1−1/e)/L ≈ −0.632/L meaningfully expands the class of nonconvex-nonconcave saddle-point problems (e.g., certain GAN training dynamics) for which first-order methods have convergence guarantees. The adaptive Max Distance Extragradient (MDEG) algorithm provides a practical tool for escaping limit cycles without requiring knowledge of ρ, which is valuable for adversarial training and robust optimization. Open questions include whether the global optimal ρ bound converges to 1−1/e as the number of exploration steps grows (numerical evidence suggests it does not), and whether alternative subroutines beyond repeated GDA steps could yield tighter bounds or better approximations of the proximal point operator.
