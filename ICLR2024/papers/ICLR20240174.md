## Problem

Existing continuous treatment effect estimation methods (e.g., VCNet, ATTNet) assume the treatment is directly observed, but real-world measurements are often contaminated with error, leading to attenuation bias in causal effect estimates. While nonparametric deconvolution approaches (Huang & Zhang, 2023) address this, they are computationally intensive and do not scale to high-dimensional covariates. The paper fills this gap by proposing a general variational framework that corrects for treatment measurement error while remaining compatible with modern neural network-based ADRF estimators.

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular data consisting of covariates X ∈ R^d, an error-contaminated treatment measurement S = T + U (where the true T is never observed), and a continuous outcome Y. The measurement error U is assumed independent of T, X, and potential outcomes, with a known parametric form (e.g., Gaussian or Laplace). The output is the average dose-response function ψ(t) = E[Y^t] evaluated over the treatment domain, from which the optimal treatment per individual can be derived; evaluation uses AMSE over the interval [-0.3, 1.3] and APE comparing estimated vs. ground-truth optimal treatments.

## Method

The framework treats the unobserved true treatment T as a latent variable in a Bayesian data generation model (X → T → S, X → Y, T → Y) and performs variational inference by maximizing the ELBO. Three neural network modules are jointly trained: (1) a mixture density network parameterized by θ_T estimates the prior p(T|X), which simultaneously serves as the generalized propensity score for confounding adjustment; (2) an outcome prediction head g(x,t;θ_Y) (instantiated as VCNet or ATTNet) models p(Y|T,X); and (3) a proposal network q_φ(t|s,y) approximates the posterior of T given the observed measurement S and outcome Y. The ELBO decomposes into an expected outcome prediction loss, a measurement-error likelihood term weighted by α, and a KL divergence between the posterior and prior weighted by β, all optimized via the reparameterization trick. At inference, the reparameterized (error-corrected) treatment value from q_φ replaces the noisy S in the outcome model, yielding an unbiased ADRF estimate ψ̂(t) = (1/N)Σ μ̂(t, X_i).

## Results

On the semi-synthetic IHDP dataset, VF-VCNet achieves the best AMSE of 0.068±0.027 (vs. 0.129 for VCNet and 0.090 for DRNet), while VF-ATTNet achieves the best APE of 0.044±0.049. On TCGA, VF-VCNet attains the best AMSE (0.019±0.014) and APE (0.036±0.017), substantially outperforming all baselines. In 1-D and 6-D simulation experiments across varying observation counts (500–10000) and measurement error standard deviations (0.5–2.5), both VF-VCNet and VF-ATTNet consistently achieve the lowest AMSE and APE, with the advantage growing as measurement error increases. On the News dataset, results are mixed: VF-VCNet improves AMSE over VCNet (0.714 vs. 1.141) but VF-ATTNet underperforms ATTNet (0.589 vs. 0.296 AMSE) due to the complex beta-distributed conditional prior and limited sample size (3000).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VF-VCNet consistently outperforms its base method VCNet on IHDP (AMSE 0.068 vs. 0.129, a 47% reduction) and TCGA (AMSE 0.019 vs. 0.046, a 59% reduction), and also improves on News (AMSE 0.714 vs. 1.141). VF-ATTNet beats ATTNet on IHDP APE (0.044 vs. 0.088) and TCGA AMSE (0.042 vs. 0.056), but underperforms ATTNet on News AMSE (0.589 vs. 0.296) due to the complex beta-distributed prior and limited 3000-sample size. Against TARNet and DRNet, both proposed methods achieve lower AMSE on IHDP and TCGA, with VF-VCNet showing the largest margins (e.g., 0.019 vs. 0.042 for TARNet on TCGA). In 1-D simulations, the nonparametric baselines MC-Naive and MC-SIMEXLC (Huang & Zhang, 2023) are also compared; MC-SIMEXLC achieves the third-best performance but at significantly higher runtime due to bootstrap procedures, while the proposed methods maintain the best AMSE and APE with substantially lower computational cost. The key finding is that the variational framework's advantage grows with measurement error magnitude, as non-VI baselines suffer from attenuation bias that worsens with larger error variance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural network-based continuous treatment effect estimation literature (TARNet, DRNet, VCNet, ATTNet), which universally assumes error-free treatment observation, by wrapping these architectures in a variational inference framework that treats the true treatment as a latent variable. The variational machinery draws directly from the VAE/ELBO formulation (Kingma & Welling, 2014), while the prior p(T|X) doubles as the generalized propensity score in the sense of Hirano & Imbens (2004). The paper departs from the nonparametric measurement-error treatment effect literature (Huang & Zhang, 2023; Zhu et al., 2022), which relies on deconvolution kernels or instrumental variables and struggles with high-dimensional covariates, by offering a scalable, modular alternative that can plug in any differentiable outcome prediction head.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practically important gap: treatment measurement error is pervasive in observational studies (e.g., self-reported drug intake in NHANES), yet the dominant neural network-based ADRF estimators ignore it, leading to attenuation bias. The modular design—where the variational layer is architecture-agnostic—makes it straightforward to adopt as new outcome prediction heads emerge. Key limitations remain: the method requires a known (or parametrically specified) error distribution, demands more data than its base models due to the additional posterior network, and struggles when p(T|X) is complex and unsupervised (as seen with VF-ATTNet on the News dataset). Open directions include covariate-dependent measurement errors and unobserved confounders beyond the ignorability assumption.
