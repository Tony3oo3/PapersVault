## Problem

SIM2REAL adapted models make overconfident mispredictions on real data, and paradoxically, as adaptation methods improve in transfer performance, their propensity for overconfident errors increases. Existing calibration techniques like temperature scaling require labeled target data or separate calibration splits, which are unavailable in the unsupervised SIM2REAL setting where only unlabeled REAL images are accessible at training time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241394/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes labeled SIM source images (e.g., 1024×1024 crops for GTAV→Cityscapes SemSeg, standard-resolution images for VisDA ObjRec) and unlabeled REAL target images, producing per-pixel class predictions with confidence scores (SemSeg) or class labels with confidence (ObjRec). At test time, the model takes REAL images and outputs calibrated predictions. The method is a drop-in training-time patch that does not alter the input format or output structure of the base adaptation method.

## Method

AUGCAL is a training-time patch that makes two minimally invasive modifications to any existing SIM2REAL adaptation pipeline: (1) replacing vanilla SIM images with strongly augmented views via PASTA or RandAugment (AUG intervention) that provably reduce the SIM2REAL distributional distance, and (2) adding a DCA calibration loss (difference between mean predicted confidence and empirical accuracy) computed on the augmented SIM predictions (CAL intervention). The theoretical justification derives an upper bound on target calibration error via importance sampling that decomposes into a source-target Rényi divergence term (reduced by AUG) and a source calibration loss term (reduced by CAL), showing that coupling both interventions yields a tighter bound than either alone. The final optimization objective is the sum of cross-entropy on augmented SIM views, the standard UDA adaptation loss, and a weighted calibration loss on augmented SIM predictions, with λ_CAL=1 recommended to balance performance and calibration.

## Results

On GTAV→Cityscapes (DAFormer), AUGCAL applied to EntMin+MIC improves mIoU from 65.71 to 70.31 while reducing ECE from 5.34 to 3.43 and improving PRR from 45.93 to 62.66. For HRDA+MIC, AUGCAL retains mIoU (75.56→75.90) while reducing ECE from 2.86 to 2.45 and OC from 89.72 to 88.26. On VisDA (ViT-B), AUGCAL applied to SDAT+MIC improves mAcc from 92.53 to 92.87, reduces ECE from 7.67 to 6.84, and improves PRR from 63.78 to 67.80. Across all settings, AUGCAL consistently reduces miscalibration (ECE, IC-ECE) and overconfidence (OC) while retaining or improving adaptation performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AUGCAL is applied as a training-time patch on top of three SIM2REAL adaptation baselines: EntMin+MIC (Vu et al., 2019), HRDA+MIC (Hoyer et al., 2022b), and SDAT+MIC (Rangwani et al., 2022). Against EntMin+MIC on GTAV→Cityscapes, AUGCAL wins decisively on all metrics: +4.6 mIoU (65.71→70.31), −1.91 ECE (5.34→3.43), −4.76 IC-ECE, and +16.73 PRR (45.93→62.66). Against HRDA+MIC, AUGCAL achieves a smaller but consistent gain: +0.34 mIoU, −0.41 ECE, −2.83 IC-ECE, and +1.44 PRR. On VisDA, AUGCAL applied to SDAT+MIC improves mAcc by +0.34, reduces ECE by −0.83, IC-ECE by −2.20, OC by −3.39, and improves PRR by +4.02. In comparison with post-hoc Temperature Scaling (Guo et al., 2017) on VisDA, AUGCAL clearly outperforms: TS tuned on clean or PASTA-augmented validation splits fails to reduce ECE (8.19 or 8.54 vs. AUGCAL's 7.24) and actually increases IC-ECE and OC, whereas AUGCAL reduces all calibration error metrics simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

AUGCAL sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: SIM2REAL unsupervised domain adaptation (HRDA, EntMin, SDAT) and training-time calibration objectives (DCA, MDCA, MbLS). Rather than proposing a new adaptation algorithm, it patches existing ones with two minimally invasive changes—replacing clean SIM inputs with strongly augmented views (PASTA or RandAugment) and adding a calibration loss on those augmented predictions. The theoretical motivation draws on importance sampling and Rényi divergence to show that the target calibration error is upper-bounded by a sum of source-target distributional distance and source calibration loss, providing a principled reason why the AUG+CAL combination yields a tighter bound than either intervention alone. This departs from post-hoc calibration approaches like temperature scaling, which the paper shows to be ineffective (and even harmful) in the SIM2REAL setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical reliability concern for safety-critical deployments (e.g., autonomous driving) where SIM2REAL adapted models must not only be accurate but also produce trustworthy confidence scores. The finding that stronger adaptation methods paradoxically increase overconfidence on mispredictions is a useful diagnostic for the field, suggesting that performance gains in domain adaptation may come at a calibration cost that goes unmeasured in standard benchmarks. Limitations include the narrow scope (two tasks, two shifts, a small set of AUG choices validated via MMD), the dependence on an AUG transform that provably reduces SIM2REAL distance (not always available), and the fact that the theoretical bound is loose in practice. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other domain shifts (e.g., medical imaging, robotics), whether the calibration benefit persists under distribution shifts beyond covariate shift, and whether the DCA loss can be replaced by more principled uncertainty estimates.
