## Problem

Training high-resolution diffusion models directly in pixel space remains challenging due to the computational cost of re-encoding full-resolution inputs at every denoising step. Existing approaches either cascade separately trained super-resolution models or rely on a pre-trained autoencoder's latent space, both of which introduce multi-stage pipelines with exposure bias or lossy compression. No prior pixel-space method has achieved competitive quality beyond 512×512 resolution.

## Input / Output

The model receives a stack of noisy images at R different resolutions (e.g., 64², 256², 1024²) plus a text embedding from a frozen FLAN-T5 XL encoder (or a class label for ImageNet), and jointly denoises all resolutions in parallel to produce a full-resolution output (up to 1024×1024 images or 16-frame 256×256 video clips). Unlike cascaded methods, there is no sequential dependency between resolutions at inference time—all R outputs are generated simultaneously from the same NestedUNet forward pass. The down-sampling operators D^r are simple bilinear interpolation (spatial) or first-frame indexing (temporal), not learned components.

## Method

MDM defines a joint diffusion process in an extended space where a single data point x is simultaneously corrupted at R resolutions via resolution-specific noise schedules, and a single NestedUNet denoises all resolutions in one forward pass. The NestedUNet recursively nests lower-resolution latents inside higher-resolution ones: at each level, the current resolution's features are merged with the output of the inner (lower-resolution) NestedUNet via skip connections, enabling extensive parameter sharing across scales. Training uses a multi-resolution denoising loss (Eq. 3) that sums weighted L2 losses across all resolutions, with weights inversely proportional to resolution size, combined with a progressive training schedule that starts at the lowest resolution and gradually adds higher-resolution terms—mirroring progressive GAN growth. The architecture allocates the majority of parameters and self-attention layers to the lowest-resolution inner UNet (64×64), following the SDXL design principle, while higher-resolution outer layers add minimal parameter overhead.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, MDM with classifier-free guidance (CFG=1.5) achieves a FID of 3.51, outperforming ADM (10.94), CDM (4.88), and LDM-4 (10.56), and matching LDM-4 with CFG (3.60). On zero-shot MS-COCO 256×256, MDM (CFG=1.35) achieves FID 13.43, beating LDM-8 (23.31) and LDM-8 with CFG (12.63 is close), though trailing DALL-E 2 (10.39) and IMAGEN (7.27), which are trained on substantially larger datasets. MDM is trained on only CC12M (12M images), making it competitive despite the data gap. Training curves (Fig. 4) show MDM converges significantly faster than Simple DM, Cascaded DM, and LDM baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet 256×256, MDM (FID 3.51 with CFG) clearly outperforms ADM (10.94) and CDM (4.88), and edges out LDM-4 with CFG (3.60). Against the Cascaded DM baseline (trained from the same 64×64 starting point), MDM achieves substantially better FID despite having fewer total parameters and using half the inference steps, which the authors attribute to exposure bias in the cascaded approach. On zero-shot COCO, MDM (13.43) beats LDM-8 (23.31) and is close to LDM-8 with CFG (12.63), but trails DALL-E 2 (10.39) and IMAGEN (7.27); however, both DALL-E 2 and IMAGEN are trained on datasets orders of magnitude larger than CC12M. In controlled training-curve comparisons (Fig. 4), MDM with progressive training converges faster and reaches lower FID/CLIP than Simple DM, Cascaded DM, and LDM baselines, with the multi-resolution loss and progressive schedule each contributing independently to the improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

MDM extends the standard DDPM framework by generalizing the diffusion process to an extended multi-resolution space, building directly on the progressive signal transformation scheduler from f-DM (Gu et al., 2022). The NestedUNet architecture draws from UNet's skip-connection design and SDXL's principle of concentrating computation at lower resolutions, while the progressive training schedule is adapted from Progressive GANs (Karras et al., 2017). The key departure from prior work is that MDM unifies what cascaded diffusion (DALL-E 2, IMAGEN) and latent diffusion (LDM, SDXL) handle with separate models or autoencoders into a single pixel-space model with shared parameters across resolutions, avoiding the exposure bias and lossy compression issues of those approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MDM demonstrates that a single pixel-space diffusion model trained on only 12M images (CC12M) can achieve competitive high-resolution (1024×1024) text-to-image generation without relying on cascaded pipelines or separately trained autoencoders, offering a simpler and more transparent training/inference recipe. The paper explicitly acknowledges it is not claiming SOTA and leaves large-scale evaluation as future work, while noting that MDM and latent diffusion are complementary (MDM could be built on top of autoencoder codes). Open questions include whether the weight-sharing architecture can be further optimized, whether the multi-resolution loss generalizes beyond spatial resolution to other structured dimensions, and whether the approach scales to the very large datasets and model sizes where current SOTA methods operate.
