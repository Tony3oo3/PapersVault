## Problem

Local Bayesian Optimization methods for policy search (e.g., MPD) treat the RL problem as a black-box, using only scalar policy return evaluations and discarding the rich trajectory data and sequential MDP structure. This limits their effectiveness in high-dimensional continuous control, where the curse of dimensionality already makes global GP modeling infeasible. The paper asks whether injecting RL-specific knowledge—specifically the action-value function—into the GP prior can make local BO substantially more sample-efficient for deterministic policy optimization.

## Input / Output

The method operates on deterministic linear policies in continuous MDPs (MuJoCo locomotion tasks). Beyond the standard scalar return J(π_x), each rollout of a policy point x yields an empirical discounted state-occupation measure d̂^π_x and a trajectory of transitions, which are used to train Q-function critics and to evaluate the advantage mean function. The GP is local—centered at a moving central point θ—and its mean function m̂_φ(x) is recomputed whenever θ changes, so the model is inherently non-stationary across outer-loop iterations. The output is a descent direction Σ_θ^{-1}μ_θ that updates θ, plus the optimized linear policy parameters.

## Method

ABS augments the MPD local BO framework with an Advantage Mean Function derived from the performance difference lemma: m̂_φ(x) = Ĵ(π_θ) + E_{s~d̂^π_θ}[Â^π_θ(s, π_x(s))], which expresses the return of a nearby policy π_x as the central return plus an advantage-weighted correction. A new theoretical bound (Theorem 3.3) shows that for Lipschitz MDPs with deterministic Lipschitz policies, the unmodeled residual is O(sup_s ‖π_x(s)−π_θ(s)‖), justifying the GP to model only this second-order term rather than the full objective. A key corollary reveals that the posterior gradient mean μ_θ under this mean function equals the deterministic policy gradient plus a data-correction term, formally bridging BO and policy gradient methods. For the Q-function, an ensemble of five DroQ critics is aggregated via FTRL softmax weighting based on a novel R² validation score that evaluates each critic on trajectories from *other* policies (not just π_θ), and the worst-performing critic is periodically reset to avoid primacy bias.

## Results

ABS is evaluated on six MuJoCo locomotion tasks (InvertedPendulum, Swimmer, Hopper, HalfCheetah, Walker2d, Ant) using 5 random seeds per algorithm. The advantage mean function's validation score (R̃²) remains consistently positive, explaining on average approximately 50% of the variance in observed policy returns, while the test score (R̂²) is positive quite frequently though occasionally negative. In terms of policy return, ABS consistently outperforms MPD across all tasks, with the relative advantage growing in high-dimensional settings (Walker2d, Ant) where the curse of dimensionality hampers both ARS and MPD. The ablation study confirms that adaptive aggregation of the 5-critic DroQ ensemble outperforms both a single critic and uniform average ensembling, particularly in early training when transition data is scarce.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares ABS against two baselines: MPD (Nguyen et al., 2022), the state-of-the-art local BO method using a constant mean function, and ARS (Mania et al., 2018), an evolutionary random-search method. ABS consistently outperforms MPD on all six MuJoCo tasks in both discounted (Figure 3) and undiscounted (Figure 5) policy return, demonstrating that the advantage mean function provides meaningful guidance for local BO schemes. Against ARS, ABS is competitive in low-dimensional tasks (InvertedPendulum, Swimmer, Hopper) but substantially outperforms it on the high-dimensional Walker2d and Ant tasks, where the advantage mean function can focus the search on more promising regions of the parameter space while ARS struggles to identify descent directions. The ablation (Figure 4) further shows that combining adaptive FTRL-based aggregation with resetting the worst-performing critic yields the best overall performance, confirming that the Q-function ensemble strategy is a key contributor to ABS's advantage over both baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Maximum Probability of Descent (MPD) framework for local Bayesian Optimization by replacing its uninformative constant GP mean with an RL-aware 'advantage mean function' derived from the performance difference lemma (Kakade & Langford, 2002). It departs from prior BO-for-RL work (Eriksson et al., 2019; Nguyen et al., 2022) that treats policy search as a black-box problem, instead injecting the action-value function into the GP prior to exploit the sequential MDP structure. The theoretical grounding builds on Saleh et al. (2022)'s deterministic policy optimization bounds, which the authors specialize to Lipschitz MDPs and extend to linear policies with bounded state spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ABS provides a principled way to perform exploration with deterministic policies, which is critical for deployment on physical systems like robots where stochastic exploration is unsafe. The elegant result that the GP posterior gradient mean reduces to the deterministic policy gradient (Corollary 3.3.1) offers a unifying theoretical lens connecting BO and policy gradient methods. However, the method is currently limited to linear policies due to computational constraints, and scaling to deep policies remains an open challenge. The adaptive critic aggregation via FTRL with validation-based weighting is a practical contribution, but the overall framework's applicability beyond MuJoCo locomotion tasks is untested.
