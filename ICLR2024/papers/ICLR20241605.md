## Problem

Existing social intelligence benchmarks for AI systems are either static (single-turn, non-interactive) or narrowly focused on specific tasks, making them inadequate for evaluating the dynamic, goal-driven, multi-dimensional nature of real social interactions. There is no general-purpose, interactive environment that can systematically assess how language agents coordinate, collaborate, compete, and navigate conflicting social norms while pursuing private goals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241605/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent receives a scenario context, a character profile (personality traits, moral values, decision-making style, secrets), and a private social goal, with asymmetric observability of the other agent's profile determined by their relationship type (stranger through family). Agents produce turn-based actions from five categories (speak, non-verbal communication, physical action, none, leave) over up to 20 turns, and the episode is scored across seven dimensions with heterogeneous ranges: GOAL [0-10], BEL [0-10], KNO [0-10], SEC [-10,0], REL [-5,5], SOC [-10,0], and FIN [-5,5].

## Method

SOTOPIA procedurally constructs a large task space by cross-producting 90 GPT-4-generated social scenarios, 40 character profiles, 90 relationship pairs, and per-agent social goals, with manual validation ensuring plausibility and constraint consistency. Agents (LLMs prompted with scenario, character, and interaction history at temperature 1) interact in round-robin turn-based episodes, and performance is assessed via SOTOPIA-EVAL, a seven-dimensional framework inspired by sociology, psychology, and economics that goes beyond goal completion to capture believability, knowledge acquisition, secret-keeping, relationship preservation, social rule adherence, and financial outcomes. GPT-4 is validated as an automated evaluator proxy for human AMT annotators, showing strong Pearson correlations (r=0.71) on goal completion for model outputs, enabling scalable evaluation. A challenging subset (SOTOPIA-hard, 20 tasks selected via reward-gap difficulty) is used for human-vs-model comparisons, revealing that GPT-4 significantly underperforms humans on goal completion and exhibits strategic naivety (e.g., bidding at exact target prices) and over-helpfulness (rephrasing before answering).

## Results

On SOTOPIA, GPT-4 achieves the highest scores across most dimensions: GOAL 7.62/10, BEL 9.28/10, KNO 3.73/10, REL 1.94/5, and FIN 0.81/5, outperforming GPT-3.5, Llama-2-70b-chat, and MPT-30b-chat. On the challenging SOTOPIA-hard subset, humans significantly outperform GPT-4 on goal completion (6.15 vs. 4.85, p<0.05), while GPT-4 produces 45.5 words per turn versus humans' 16.8. GPT-4 serves as a reasonable proxy for human evaluation, with Pearson correlations of 0.78 (GOAL), 0.62 (FIN), and 0.56 (REL) against human judgments on model outputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4 outperforms GPT-3.5 on GOAL (7.62 vs. 6.45), FIN (0.81 vs. 0.46), REL (1.94 vs. 1.23), and KNO (3.73 vs. 3.40), with a marginal BEL advantage (9.28 vs. 9.15). GPT-4 dominates Llama-2-70b-chat across nearly all dimensions (e.g., GOAL 7.62 vs. 5.38, BEL 9.28 vs. 8.10), a finding that diverges from static benchmarks where Llama-2-70b is on par with or better than GPT-3.5. MPT-30b-chat trails all other models on GOAL (4.10), BEL (6.17), and KNO (2.11), though it achieves the best (least negative) SEC score (-0.07). Notably, GPT-4 does not outperform other models on SEC or SOC, where all models show negative scores, indicating persistent vulnerability to leaking secrets and violating social norms. On SOTOPIA-hard, humans beat GPT-4 on GOAL (6.15 vs. 4.85) while matching it on BEL (9.10 vs. 9.25), suggesting models struggle with strategic communication and goal persistence in challenging social scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from static social intelligence benchmarks (SocialIQA, ToMi, FauxPas) by introducing an interactive, multi-turn, goal-oriented evaluation environment, and from task-specific dialogue systems (Wang et al. 2019, FAIR et al. 2022) by covering a diverse, procedurally generated task space. It draws conceptual inspiration from multi-agent game theory (DecPOMDP formulation, mixed-motive games) and from LLM-based social simulation work (Park et al. 2023, CAMEL, ChatDev), but distinguishes itself by providing a systematic, multi-dimensional evaluation framework (SOTOPIA-EVAL) grounded in sociology, psychology, and economics literature rather than merely showcasing LLM capabilities. The LLM-as-judge component builds on the emerging practice of using GPT-4 for automated evaluation, validated here against human annotators on specific dimensions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SOTOPIA provides the first general-domain, interactive, goal-oriented benchmark for social intelligence, filling a gap the authors identify between static benchmarks and narrow task-specific evaluations. The finding that GPT-4 significantly underperforms humans on SOTOPIA-hard (goal completion 4.85 vs. 6.15) and that Llama-2-70b-chat underperforms GPT-3.5 in interactive settings despite parity on static benchmarks challenges assumptions about what static evaluations capture. Key limitations include the restriction to dyadic interactions, only five relationship types, 40 characters, and 90 scenarios, leaving open questions about multi-party dynamics, richer social graphs, and whether SOTOPIA-EVAL can serve as a training signal for improving social intelligence. The validated GPT-4-as-judge pipeline also opens the door to scalable automated evaluation of social agent progress.
