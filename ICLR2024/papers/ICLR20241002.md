## Problem

Most existing structure learning methods for time series assume discrete-time evolution and regular sampling intervals, which can lead to incorrect inferred relationships when the underlying process is continuous-time or when observations arrive at irregular intervals. This mismatch is particularly problematic in domains like biology, finance, and climate science where both continuous dynamics and irregular sampling are common.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N i.i.d. multivariate time series with potentially irregular observation times (no imputation required) and produces a posterior distribution over D×D adjacency matrices encoding structural dependencies between variables, along with learned neural drift and diffusion functions parameterizing the latent SDE. The graph is defined through non-zero partial derivatives of the drift and diffusion with respect to each variable, and the method naturally handles arbitrary observation times without requiring uniform intervals or data imputation.

## Method

SCOTCH models the latent state as an Itô diffusion with neural network drift and diffusion functions that explicitly take the graph as input via node embeddings and edge-weighted interactions, with the diffusion constrained to be diagonal to enable structural identifiability. A variational inference framework jointly approximates the graph posterior (using a Frobenius-norm sparsity prior) and the latent posterior process (a separate SDE with time-dependent drift conditioned on the observed series). The training objective is an ELBO combining the observation likelihood with a KL divergence between the posterior and prior SDE processes, optimized via pathwise gradients with Euler-Maruyama discretization. Theoretically, the paper proves structural identifiability of the SDE under global Lipschitz and diagonal diffusion assumptions, preservation of identifiability in the latent formulation, and consistency of the variational recovery in the infinite-data limit.

## Results

On the DREAM3 100-dimensional gene expression benchmark, SCOTCH achieves a mean AUROC of 0.677, outperforming Rhino (0.636), NGM (0.580), CUTS (0.534), and PCMCI+ (0.520). On the synthetic Glycolysis SDE, SCOTCH reaches 0.711 AUROC versus 0.595 for NGM and 0.520 for Rhino. Under irregular sampling (30% missing) on Lorenz-96, SCOTCH attains 0.728 AUROC, surpassing NGM (0.679) and CUTS (0.604). On Netsim (brain fMRI), SCOTCH ties Rhino+NoInst at 0.95 AUROC on complete data but trails full Rhino (0.99) due to its inability to model instantaneous effects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCOTCH outperforms all five baselines on the DREAM3 benchmark (mean AUROC 0.677 vs. Rhino 0.636, NGM 0.580, CUTS 0.534, PCMCI+ 0.520, VARLiNGaM not applicable due to dimensionality constraints). On the synthetic Glycolysis SDE, SCOTCH (0.711) substantially exceeds NGM (0.595), CUTS (0.580), Rhino (0.520), and VARLiNGaM (0.508), demonstrating the advantage of SDEs over ODEs for multimodal dynamics. Under 30% missing observations on Lorenz-96, SCOTCH (0.728) leads NGM (0.679) and CUTS (0.604), while discrete methods (Rhino 0.571, VARLiNGaM 0.510, PCMCI+ 0.499) degrade significantly. On Netsim, SCOTCH (0.95) ties Rhino+NoInst (0.95) on complete data but falls short of full Rhino (0.99), which the authors attribute to SCOTCH's inability to model instantaneous effects. Overall, SCOTCH consistently wins or ties on every dataset where it is directly comparable, with the sole exception being Netsim where instantaneous effects are critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCOTCH sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) neural SDEs with variational inference for probabilistic modeling (Li et al., 2020; Tzen & Raginsky, 2019a), and (2) Bayesian structure learning with graph priors and graph-conditioned neural functions (Geffner et al., 2022; Annadani et al., 2023). The key conceptual departure is from NGM (Bellot et al., 2022), which uses neural ODEs for continuous-time structure learning but assumes Gaussian observations and cannot handle multiple time series or multimodal dynamics. SCOTCH replaces the ODE with a latent SDE and adds a diagonal-diffusion assumption that enables structural identifiability, while also naturally accommodating irregularly sampled observations that discrete-time methods (Rhino, VARLiNGaM, PCMCI+) cannot handle without imputation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled SDE-based framework for Bayesian structure learning with provable identifiability and consistency guarantees, filling a gap between Hansen & Sokol's (2014) identifiability theory (which did not address learning) and NGM's ODE-based approach (which cannot model intrinsic stochasticity). The practical impact is significant for domains with irregularly sampled, non-Gaussian temporal data (single-cell genomics, climate science, healthcare). Key open limitations include the inability to model instantaneous effects, linear scaling with series length, and the restriction to time-homogeneous (stationary) systems—addressing non-stationarity without breaking identifiability remains an open theoretical challenge.
