## Problem

Conditional diffusion models suffer from low output diversity when sampled with high classifier-free guidance (CFG) scales for optimal image quality, or when trained on small datasets, as most samples converge to a narrow set of modes regardless of the random seed. Reducing the CFG scale degrades image quality, and collecting larger datasets is often infeasible, leaving a persistent quality-diversity trade-off that existing sampling strategies do not resolve.

## Input / Output

CADS operates as a drop-in sampling-time modification on top of any pretrained conditional diffusion model, accepting the same conditioning signals the model was trained on (class embeddings, pose images, text embeddings, or face-ID vectors) plus a random noise sample, and producing a synthetic image at the model's native resolution (e.g., 256×256 or 512×512 for ImageNet). No architectural changes or retraining are required; the only added computation is a single element-wise noise injection and optional rescaling applied to the conditioning vector at each reverse-diffusion step.

## Method

CADS corrupts the conditioning signal at each reverse-diffusion step via ŷ = √γ(t)·y + s·√(1−γ(t))·n, where γ(t) is a piecewise-linear annealing schedule that transitions from 0 (full noise) at early steps to 1 (no noise) at later steps, and s controls the initial noise magnitude. This breaks the statistical dependence on the condition during early sampling—so the model follows the unconditional score and explores the full data distribution—then gradually restores condition alignment in later steps. An optional rescaling step (controlled by mixing factor ψ) normalizes the corrupted condition back toward its original mean and standard deviation to prevent divergence at high noise scales. Theoretically, the noise injection acts as a Langevin-dynamics-like perturbation and smooths the conditional score via an ℓ₂-regularization effect on the inverse covariance, preventing domination by strong modes. CADS is compatible with any base sampler (DDPM, DDIM, DPM++, PNDM, UniPC) and any conditioning type, and outperforms the simpler alternative of dynamically modulating the CFG weight (Dynamic CFG) because the added stochasticity produces richer sampling dynamics.

## Results

CADS achieves a new state-of-the-art FID of 1.70 on class-conditional ImageNet 256×256 generation (w_CFG=2) and 2.31 on ImageNet 512×512 (w_CFG=2.5), surpassing all prior methods including MDT-G (1.79), DiT-G++ (1.83), and VDM++ (2.12/2.65) without any retraining. Across six conditional generation tasks (DeepFashion, SHHQ, ImageNet 256/512, ID3PM, Stable Diffusion), CADS consistently improves FID by 2–11 points and Recall by 0.13–0.46 over standard DDPM sampling at the same guidance scale. Condition alignment is preserved: top-1 class accuracy drops only from 0.98 to 0.96, while MPJPE and CLIP-score remain unchanged.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CADS outperforms all prior class-conditional ImageNet methods in Table 2: it beats MDT-G (FID 1.79) and DiT-G++ (1.83) on 256×256, and VDM++ (2.65) and SimpleDiffusion (3.02) on 512×512, while also achieving the highest Recall (0.64) at 256×256. Against the base DiT-XL/2-G model (FID 2.27 at w_CFG=1.5), CADS improves FID to 1.70 at w_CFG=2 purely through modified sampling, with no architectural or training changes. As a sampler-agnostic technique, CADS consistently improves every tested base sampler: DDIM (FID 18.84→9.80), DPM++ (18.65→9.63), PNDM (20.23→14.60), and UniPC (18.90→10.10) on ImageNet 256. Compared to the simpler Dynamic CFG alternative (which modulates the guidance weight over time), CADS achieves substantially better FID (9.47 vs. 18.42) and Recall (0.62 vs. 0.39) on ImageNet, and fewer visual artifacts on pose-to-image (FID 7.73 vs. 9.58). On Stable Diffusion, where the model already produces diverse outputs, CADS still improves FID from 49.48 to 44.16 and Recall from 0.25 to 0.37, though the gains are smaller than on more constrained tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) and classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022), identifying that the well-known diversity-quality trade-off at high guidance scales is fundamentally an inference-time phenomenon rather than a training-time one. It departs from prior approaches that address diversity through architectural changes, larger training datasets, or guidance weight modulation (Dynamic CFG), instead proposing a minimal sampling-time intervention: scheduled Gaussian noise added to the conditioning vector. The theoretical grounding connects condition annealing to Langevin dynamics and score smoothing via Tweedie's formula, distinguishing it from cascaded diffusion models that add noise once at inference start and from Sehwag et al. (2022) which targets low-density regions specifically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CADS provides a drop-in, retraining-free sampling technique that resolves the long-standing diversity-quality trade-off in conditional diffusion models, achieving SOTA FID (1.70 at 256×256, 2.31 at 512×512) on ImageNet using only a pretrained DiT-XL/2 model. Its practical impact is significant because the method requires only an additive operation, is compatible with all existing samplers (DDIM, DPM++, PNDM, UniPC), and works across diverse conditioning types (class labels, poses, text, face IDs). The authors note that challenges remain for dense spatial conditioning (e.g., segmentation maps), and the finding that training with noisy conditions actually diminishes CADS's effectiveness suggests the inference-time framing is essential. Open questions include optimal annealing schedules for different NFE budgets and extension to multi-condition or iterative generation settings.
