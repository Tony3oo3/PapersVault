## Problem

Image-text matching datasets suffer from inherent ambiguity due to many-to-many correspondences and sparse annotations, with up to 88% of true positives mislabeled as negatives in COCO. Deterministic embeddings cannot capture this ambiguity, and the prior probabilistic approach (PCME) is hampered by expensive Monte Carlo distance approximation and loss saturation under abundant false negatives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241561/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images and text captions as input, encoding each into a D-dimensional Gaussian distribution parameterized by a mean vector μ and a diagonal variance vector σ² via separate ViT (visual) and Transformer (textual) backbones with dual heads. At retrieval time, the closed-form sampled distance (CSD) between two distributions is computed as the sum of the squared Euclidean distance between means and the L1 norm of summed variances, enabling efficient approximate nearest-neighbor search (e.g., FAISS) without sampling. The learned σ² also serves as a per-sample uncertainty measure for interpretability and prompt filtering.

## Method

PCME++ introduces a Closed-Form Sampled Distance (CSD), d(Zv, Zt) = ||μv − μt||²₂ + ||σv² + σt²||₁, derived as the expected squared L2 distance between two independent Gaussians, replacing PCME's Monte Carlo matching probability with an exact O(1) computation. The training objective is a pairwise binary cross-entropy NLL loss over all image-caption pairs in a mini-batch, regularized by a Variational Information Bottleneck (VIB) term to prevent variance collapse. Two soft-label strategies exploit the pairwise loss structure: Pseudo-Positives (PP) reassign high-confidence near-matches as soft positives to counter false-negative penalty, and Mixed Sample Data Augmentation (MSDA) applies Mixup/CutMix to 25% of visual inputs with the mixing ratio λ directly smoothing the match label m_vt ∈ [0,1]. The architecture follows CLIP (ViT visual + 12-layer Transformer text) with a duplicated final layer for the log σ² head and Generalized Pooling Operator for feature aggregation, initialized from pre-trained CLIP weights.

## Results

On COCO Caption with ViT-B/32, PCME++ achieves 40.1 EC mAP@R, 49.7 R-Precision, and 537.0 RSUM, outperforming VSE∞ (40.0/49.5/536.5) and InfoNCE (39.0/48.7/532.6). The advantage grows substantially with backbone scale: at ViT-L/14, PCME++ reaches 42.1 EC mAP@R and 554.7 RSUM, while VSE∞ collapses to 20.2 mAP@R and 424.3 RSUM, and InfoNCE drops to 35.6 mAP@R and 520.6 RSUM. Under 50% noisy correspondence, PCME++ retains 35.7 EC mAP@R and 511.0 RSUM, far exceeding VSE∞ (18.0/394.1) and DAA (0.3/20.9). For zero-shot ImageNet classification, PCME++ with uncertainty-based prompt filtering achieves 41.82% top-1 accuracy versus 35.50% for InfoNCE with all 80 prompts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCME++ consistently outperforms all deterministic baselines (VSE∞, InfoNCE, DAA, P2RM) across all three backbone sizes, with the gap widening dramatically at ViT-L/14 where VSE∞ collapses (20.2 vs. 42.1 EC mAP@R) and InfoNCE degrades (35.6 vs. 42.1). Against the prior probabilistic method PCME, PCME++ gains 0.9 EC mAP@R and 4.3 RSUM at ViT-L/14, attributed to the closed-form CSD replacing Monte Carlo matching probability. The FN-mitigation methods DAA and P2RM underperform their own VSE∞ baseline on every backbone, confirming that regularization-based deterministic approaches cannot resolve the false-negative problem. Under 50% noisy correspondence, PCME++ (35.7 EC mAP@R, 511.0 RSUM) substantially outperforms VSE∞ (18.0, 394.1), DAA (0.3, 20.9), and the dedicated NC methods NCR (34.0, 508.5) and DECL (513.5 RSUM). Among efficient retrieval methods in Table C.1, PCME++ (L/14) achieves the best recall scores (88.8 EC R@1, 65.9 CxC R@1, 81.8 COCO 1K R@1) while trailing only VSE∞ (WSL) and VinVL on precision metrics (mAP@R, R-P), suggesting backbone architecture rather than the probabilistic formulation limits precision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends PCME (Chun et al., 2021), which first introduced probabilistic embeddings for image-text matching using Monte Carlo approximation of a matching probability. The key departure is replacing the sampling-based matching probability with a closed-form sampled distance (CSD) that decomposes into a Euclidean mean distance plus an L1 variance term, making probabilistic ITM 33% faster and compatible with approximate nearest-neighbor (ANN) systems like FAISS. The paper also draws on the hedged instance embedding framework of Oh et al. (2019) for the VIB regularization, and positions its pairwise loss design as enabling straightforward application of pseudo-positives and mixed sample data augmentation—techniques that are non-trivial to incorporate into triplet or batch-wise contrastive losses used by VSE∞, InfoNCE/CLIP, and their FN-mitigation variants (DAA, P2RM).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-documented but underappreciated problem: 88.2% of caption-to-image positives in COCO are labeled as negatives, and deterministic methods with hardest negative mining produce twisted embedding spaces that worsen as backbone capacity increases. The CSD formulation is a practical contribution because it makes probabilistic embeddings deployable at scale (FAISS-compatible), and the uncertainty output provides interpretability and user-controllable rejection of retrieved items. However, the prompt-filtering application, while promising (30.43→41.82 top-1), relies on the ImageNet validation set and is explicitly not true zero-shot, limiting its immediate impact. Open questions include whether the approach scales to billion-scale retrieval indices, whether the learned uncertainty generalizes beyond COCO-style ambiguity, and whether the modest COCO RSUM gains (e.g., 537.0 vs. 536.5) translate to meaningful improvements in downstream applications at scale.
