## Problem

Learning from Label Proportions (LLP) requires training an instance-level classifier when only aggregate bag-level label counts are available, a setting driven by privacy constraints in advertising and medicine. Existing methods either fit bag-averaged predictions to proportions without exploiting covariate structure, or use pseudo-labeling without enforcing bag constraints, leading to significant degradation at large bag sizes where supervision is extremely weak (e.g., only ~20 bags for bag size 2048).

## Input / Output

The method consumes instance covariates (raw tabular features or pre-computed embeddings from SimCLR for images or MET for Criteo), disjoint bag compositions specifying which instances belong to each bag, and the integer count of positive labels per bag. It outputs a soft binary classification score per instance from an MLP instance head; the bag-level head is discarded at test time. Bags are formed by sampling instances without replacement, and the method handles bag sizes from 8 to 2048 across tabular and image modalities.

## Method

The algorithm iterates two stages. In the Pseudo Labeling step, it constructs a Gibbs distribution (Ising model) whose energy encodes bag-level constraints via least-squares penalties on the sum of instance labels versus the given bag count, and covariate similarity via K-NN pairwise terms weighted by a Matérn or RBF kernel; sum-product Belief Propagation on this sparse factor graph yields marginal pseudo labels. In the Embedding Refinement step, it trains an MLP with a novel Aggregate Embedding loss that simultaneously minimizes instance-level cross-entropy against thresholded pseudo labels and bag-level cross-entropy against true bag proportions (computed via average pooling of penultimate-layer representations through a separate bag head). The penultimate-layer embedding from this MLP becomes the new covariate representation for the next BP iteration, progressively refining the K-NN graph. The key insight is drawing an explicit parallel to parity-check decoding in coding theory, where bag counts serve as generalized parity checks and K-NN constraints provide additional redundancy, enabling the method to scale to 1M samples with linear complexity in bag size and K.

## Results

On the UCI tabular datasets (Adult, Bank Marketing), the proposed method (Ours-Itr-2) consistently outperforms all baselines across all six bag sizes (8–2048), with the largest gains in the large-bag regime: 84.95% AUROC on Adult and 81.01% on Marketing at bag size 2048, versus 62.58% and 65.60% for the nearest baseline DLLP (~22% and ~15% absolute improvements). On the 1M-sample Criteo dataset, the method achieves 74.97% AUROC at bag size 8, a 0.86-point gain over DLLP (74.11%). On CIFAR-S (10% positive class), the method outperforms DLLP by up to ~22 points at bag size 1024 (74.45% vs. 52.01%), while on the balanced CIFAR-B it remains competitive (within ~1 point) across all bag sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five baselines (DLLP, EasyLLP, GenBags, LLP-FC, LLP-VAT) on every dataset and bag size where all methods are comparable. Against DLLP (Ardehaly & Culotta, 2017), the gap widens dramatically at large bag sizes: at bag size 2048 on Adult, Ours-Itr-2 achieves 84.95% vs. DLLP's 62.58% (~22-point gain), and on Marketing 81.01% vs. 65.60% (~15-point gain). EasyLLP (Busa-Fekete et al., 2023) degrades most severely at large bags (e.g., 50.23% on Marketing at bag size 1024, near random), while the proposed method maintains 80.56%. GenBags (Saket et al., 2022) is the closest competitor on tabular data at small bag sizes but still trails by 0.5–3 points; on CIFAR-B at bag sizes ≥512, GenBags slightly edges out the proposed method (e.g., 82.73% vs. 80.43% at bag size 1024). On image datasets, LLP-FC (Zhang et al., 2022) and LLP-VAT (Tsai & Lin, 2020) are outperformed by 3–22 points, with LLP-VAT additionally suffering out-of-memory failures at bag sizes ≥512. On the challenging 1M-sample Criteo dataset, the method achieves a 0.86-point AUROC gain over DLLP at bag size 8, a meaningful improvement given that the dataset has seen only ~2% total AUC progress over seven years.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper addresses the well-studied Learning from Label Proportions (LLP) problem by drawing a novel analogy to parity-check decoding in communication systems, where bag-level label counts play the role of parity constraints and instance labels are the unknown message bits. Prior LLP methods either fit bag-averaged soft scores to proportions (DLLP, EasyLLP), use consistency regularizers (LLP-VAT), or reduce LLP to label-noise learning (LLP-FC); the closest in spirit is Poyiadzi et al.'s label propagation, but that method lacks explicit bag constraints and an embedding refinement loop. The key departure is formulating a joint Gibbs distribution that simultaneously encodes bag constraints and KNN-based covariate similarity, then marginalizing it via Sum-Product Belief Propagation to obtain pseudo labels, followed by an iterative embedding refinement step using a novel dual-head (instance + bag) Aggregate Embedding loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method achieves up to 15% AUROC gains over the strongest baseline (DLLP) in the large-bag regime where supervision is weakest, and scales to 1M samples with minimal overhead from the BP step, making it practical for privacy-sensitive deployment. The differential privacy analysis showing that larger bags yield better privacy-utility tradeoffs reinforces the method's relevance to real-world privacy-preserving ML. Open questions include a complete theoretical understanding of why loopy BP converges well on this particular graph structure, whether alternative (non-quadratic) energy potentials could further improve pseudo-label quality, and whether the approach extends cleanly to multi-class or non-disjoint bag settings.
