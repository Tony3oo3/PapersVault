## Problem

Text-prompt-based TTS systems face two unresolved challenges: the one-to-many mapping between text prompts and speech (since text cannot fully describe all voice variability, causing over-fitting or mode collapse during training), and the scarcity of text prompt datasets because writing prompts requires costly human annotation. No prior mechanism specifically addresses the one-to-many issue, and existing prompt datasets are limited to ~20K sentences or are not publicly available.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242248/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the system takes a natural language text prompt describing voice attributes (gender, pitch, speed, volume) and the text content to be spoken, producing a synthesized speech waveform via a latent diffusion TTS backbone (NaturalSpeech 2). During training, a reference speech segment is additionally provided to the style module to extract a fixed-length reference representation (8 query tokens via cross-attention), which the variation network learns to predict from the prompt representation alone. The text prompts are generated by a 4-stage LLM pipeline (GPT-3.5-TURBO) that produces diverse phrasings through keyword, sentence-template, and combination stages with attribute placeholders.

## Method

The core contribution is a diffusion-based variation network (12-layer Transformer Encoder with FiLM conditioning on the diffusion timestep) that predicts the reference speech representation from the text prompt representation by denoising from Gaussian noise, thereby sampling diverse voice variability not captured by the text prompt. The variation network is trained with L1 loss to reconstruct the reference representation extracted by a 6-layer reference speech encoder, conditioned on the prompt representation, noised reference representation, and diffusion step. A BERT-based text prompt encoder extracts fixed-length prompt representations (8 query tokens) via cross-attention, which are concatenated with the predicted reference representation to condition the TTS backbone through cross-attention. For the data-scale challenge, a fully automated prompt generation pipeline uses an SLU model to tag speech with attribute labels and then instructs an LLM through four stages—keyword generation, placeholder-based sentence templates, multi-attribute sentence combination, and dataset instantiation—to produce high-quality, diverse text prompts without human annotation.

## Results

On the 44K-hour MLS dataset, PromptTTS 2 achieves a mean attribute control accuracy of 93.33% across gender, speed, volume, and pitch, outperforming PromptTTS (91.54%) and InstructTTS (91.47%). In speech quality evaluation, PromptTTS 2 attains a MOS of 3.88±0.08, surpassing PromptTTS (3.77±0.09) and InstructTTS (3.80±0.07), with a CMOS advantage of +0.191 and +0.157 respectively. The LLM-based prompt generation pipeline also yields higher intention classification accuracy (96.38% mean) compared to human-written PromptSpeech prompts (92.74%) and TextrolSpeech's LLM prompts (94.71%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PromptTTS 2 consistently outperforms both PromptTTS and InstructTTS on all four attribute control dimensions, with the largest gains on speed (+2.98% over PromptTTS, +2.07% over InstructTTS) and pitch (+3.91% over PromptTTS, +3.07% over InstructTTS). In speech quality (MOS), PromptTTS 2 (3.88) beats PromptTTS (3.77) by 0.11 and InstructTTS (3.80) by 0.08, confirmed by positive CMOS scores. For prompt generation quality, the LLM pipeline's text prompts achieve 96.38% mean classification accuracy, exceeding both human-authored PromptSpeech prompts (92.74%) and TextrolSpeech's multi-stage LLM prompts (94.71%), indicating superior prompt expressiveness. In the Face2Voice extension, PromptTTS 2 generates voices matching facial images in 31.78% of cases versus 20.17% for SP-FaceVC, and wins or ties in 80.88% of CMOS comparisons. The variation network in PromptTTS 2 also reduces unwanted voice variability from text content and TTS backbone sampling (similarity 0.873 and 0.914 vs. 0.662/0.799 and 0.718/0.796 for baselines), confirming that voice variability is more cleanly isolated in the diffusion-based sampling step.

## Relation to Prior Work

PromptTTS 2 extends the text-prompt-based TTS paradigm introduced by PromptTTS (Guo et al., 2023) and InstructTTS (Yang et al., 2023), which condition speech synthesis on natural language descriptions of voice characteristics. It builds on NaturalSpeech 2 (Shen et al., 2023) for the latent diffusion TTS backbone and reference speech encoder, and on score-based generative modeling (Song et al., 2020) for the variation network that predicts voice variability representations from text prompt embeddings. The key departure from prior work is the explicit treatment of the one-to-many mapping between text prompts and voice realizations via a diffusion-based variation network, and the replacement of human-annotated text prompt datasets with an LLM-driven generation pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The variation network provides a principled mechanism for sampling diverse voice realizations from a single text prompt, addressing a gap that prior text-prompt TTS systems left unaddressed. The LLM-based prompt generation pipeline significantly reduces the cost and labor of constructing training data, making text-prompt TTS more accessible to the research community. However, the system is currently limited to four coarse-grained attributes (gender, pitch, speed, volume) and English speech, and the Face2Voice extension remains preliminary. Open questions include whether the variation network can capture finer-grained prosodic and emotional variability, how the approach scales to multilingual settings, and whether the LLM-generated prompts generalize to more complex or compositional voice descriptions.
