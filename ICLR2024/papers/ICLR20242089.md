## Problem

Existing neural operators parameterize inter-neuron linear operators via spectral or wavelet transforms, which struggle with boundary conditions and require artificial lifting/projecting layers. CNN-based alternatives handle boundaries flexibly but act as high-pass filters and demand large parameter counts. The paper asks: can one parameterize these linear operators directly in the spatial domain with a structure that is both mathematically principled and naturally boundary-aware?

## Input / Output

Inputs are grid-discretized fields in the linear finite element space V_h(Ω) at resolutions ranging from 64×64 (Navier-Stokes) to 256×256 or 512×512 (Darcy), with multi-channel support for time-dependent problems (e.g., 10 sequential vorticity frames). Outputs are solution fields in the same finite element space. Boundary conditions (Dirichlet, Neumann, periodic) are enforced structurally via padding schemes in the convolutional multigrid rather than encoded in the data, and the MgNO-HIGH-IN variant accepts 512×512 input while producing 256×256 output, enabling zero-shot super-resolution without high-resolution training data.

## Method

MgNO redefines neural operator layers so that each inter-neuron connection W_ij is a bounded linear operator in L(Y,Y), yielding a new universal approximation theorem that eliminates lifting and projecting layers. The key architectural choice is to parameterize W_ij via a multi-channel V-cycle multigrid structure built from standard 3×3 convolutions: smoothing iterations (learnable B kernels), restriction (stride-2 convolution), and prolongation (transposed convolution) at J hierarchical levels. This design gives O(log(d)·n²) parameters and O(d²·n²) complexity, where n is the channel count. Boundary conditions are handled by selecting the appropriate padding (zero, reflect, or circular) in the A and B convolutions, so the network preserves Dirichlet, Neumann, or periodic constraints without any training-phase enforcement. The full network stacks L such multigrid-parameterized layers with GELU activations and a local linear bias term B^ℓ h^{ℓ-1} + b^ℓ, trained end-to-end with supervised L² or H¹ loss.

## Results

On the Darcy benchmarks, MgNO (0.57M params, 6.6 s/iter) achieves relative L² errors of 0.176×10⁻² (smooth), 0.339×10⁻² (rough), and 0.715×10⁻² (multiscale), with corresponding H¹ errors of 0.576, 1.380, and 1.756 (×10⁻²) — the best across all baselines. On Navier-Stokes (NS2 setup), MgNO reaches 1.63 relative L² error versus 4.57 for FNO and 2.64 for U-NO. On Helmholtz, MgNO achieves L²=0.71×10⁻² and H¹=4.02×10⁻², outperforming FNO (1.69/9.92) and U-NO (1.26/8.03) while using fewer parameters than U-NO (7.58M vs 16.39M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MgNO outperforms all eight baselines on every benchmark. Against FNO (Li et al., 2020), MgNO reduces Darcy rough L² error from 1.613 to 0.339 (×10⁻²) and Navier-Stokes NS2 error from 4.57 to 1.63, while using fewer parameters (0.57M vs 2.37M on Darcy). Against U-NO (Rahman et al., 2022), MgNO achieves lower errors on all Darcy sub-benchmarks (e.g., 0.339 vs 1.023 on rough L²) and on Navier-Stokes NS2 (1.63 vs 2.64), despite using 16.39M fewer parameters. Against MWT (Gupta et al., 2021), MgNO improves Darcy multiscale H¹ error from 7.245 to 1.756 and runs 3.3× faster (6.6 vs 21.7 s/iter). Against GT (Cao, 2021), MgNO reduces Darcy smooth L² from 0.945 to 0.176 and runs 5.8× faster. Against LSM (Wu et al., 2023), MgNO achieves lower errors on all Darcy benchmarks and Helmholtz (L² 0.71 vs 2.55). Against DilResNet (Stachenfeld et al., 2021), MgNO dominates on Darcy smooth (0.176 vs 4.104 L²) and rough (0.339 vs 7.347), though DilResNet is slightly better on Darcy multiscale L² (1.417 vs 0.715 — actually MgNO still wins). Against UNet (Ronneberger et al., 2015), MgNO achieves substantially lower errors with 30× fewer parameters. Against sFNO-v2 (Benitez et al., 2023), MgNO achieves lower Helmholtz L² (0.71 vs 1.72) and H¹ (4.02 vs 10.40) with fewer parameters (7.58M vs 12.0M).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: the MgNet line of work (He & Xu, 2019; He et al., 2023) that showed V-cycle multigrid for elliptic PDEs can be represented as a convolutional network, and the neural operator framework (FNO, GNO, U-NO) that learns maps between function spaces. The key conceptual departure is redefining neural operators via bounded linear operators between Banach spaces, which eliminates the lifting and projection layers that prior architectures (GNO, FNO) require for universal approximation. The multigrid V-cycle, parameterized as multi-channel convolutions with appropriate padding, serves as the concrete realization of these linear operators, naturally encoding boundary conditions through padding schemes rather than data-dependent encoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MgNO achieves state-of-the-art accuracy with dramatically fewer parameters (0.57M vs. 2–30M for baselines) across Darcy, Navier-Stokes, and Helmholtz benchmarks, while naturally handling Dirichlet, Neumann, and periodic boundary conditions without special data encoding. The discretization invariance property (zero-shot super-resolution) and the rapid convergence without overfitting despite minimal capacity are practically valuable and theoretically intriguing. Open questions include extending to irregular domains (requiring algebraic multigrid), understanding why the architecture resists overfitting so effectively, and whether the multigrid structure generalizes beyond elliptic-type problems to hyperbolic or parabolic PDEs.
