## Problem

While LLMs implicitly store factual knowledge in their parameters, there is no comprehensive benchmark that systematically probes the breadth, depth, and reasoning limits of this knowledge across multiple dimensions. Existing evaluations either test general knowledge breadth (e.g., MMLU) or truthfulness in generation (e.g., TruthfulQA), but none jointly assess multi-hop composition, temporal updating, adversarial robustness, structured-data reasoning, domain-specific knowledge, and multilingual transfer in a unified factual-verification framework.

## Input / Output

Each input is a multiple-choice factual question (rewritten from a declarative claim by human annotators) paired with a task instruction, presented in one of four prompt formats (zero-shot, zero-shot CoT, few-shot, few-shot CoT). The model must output one of three labels — Yes (Factual), No (Non-Factual), or Not Sure Enough (Not Enough Information) — along with supporting evidence. The 20,713 questions are partitioned into seven tasks with controlled difficulty axes (e.g., number of reasoning hops, evidence type, adversarial modification strategy), enabling fine-grained per-subset accuracy and macro-F1 reporting.

## Method

Pinocchio is constructed by sourcing claims from seven existing datasets (FEVER, FEVEROUS, Symmetric/FM2, VitaminC, PolitiFact, PubHealth/SciFact, XFact/CHEF) and converting them into three-option factual questions via human annotation, with quality controlled through meta-review (92.4% label accuracy) and inter-annotator agreement (85.6% IAA). The evaluation protocol tests 10 LLMs of varying sizes and training stages (pretrained, instruction-tuned, RLHF) under four base prompt strategies, then extends to advanced techniques including self-consistency voting, self-refinement with iterative feedback, complexity-based CoT, and declarative-claim prompting on a 1,400-question Pinocchio-Lite subset. A key design choice is stratifying questions within each task by difficulty (e.g., 1-hop vs. multi-hop, text-only vs. table evidence, auto-generated vs. manually crafted adversarial examples) so that performance degradation can be attributed to specific reasoning bottlenecks rather than overall task difficulty.

## Results

On the Pinocchio benchmark (20,713 factual questions across 7 tasks), GPT-3.5-Turbo achieves the best overall performance with 47.0% accuracy and 44.8% F1 under Few-shot CoT prompting, followed by Text-Davinci-003 (45.5% acc, 42.5% F1) and Vicuna-13B (45.2% acc, 38.4% F1). LLMs without instruction tuning (OPT-6.7B, BLOOM-7B, LLaMA-7B) underperform instruction-tuned models by an average of 16.0%, with OPT-6.7B scoring only 18.8% accuracy. Few-shot prompting yields a 7.3% average improvement over zero-shot, and CoT provides a 2.1% average boost for instruction-tuned models, though it can degrade performance for untuned models like BLOOM-7B (dropping from 29.7% to 6.6% accuracy).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a benchmark paper evaluating 10 existing LLMs rather than proposing a new method, so comparisons are across models. GPT-3.5-Turbo (RLHF-tuned) leads all models by 1.5–28.2% accuracy over the other LLMs, with Text-Davinci-003 and Vicuna-13B forming a second tier (45.2–45.5% accuracy). Instruction-tuned open-source models (Vicuna-7B/13B, ChatGLM-6B, Alpaca-7B) consistently outperform their base counterparts (LLaMA-7B, BLOOM-7B, OPT-6.7B) by 6–28% accuracy, confirming that instruction tuning and RLHF significantly improve factual knowledge retrieval. Scaling from Vicuna-7B to Vicuna-13B yields a 5.4% average accuracy gain, suggesting parameter count matters for factual storage. Across the 7 task domains (Table 3), GPT-3.5-Turbo performs best on Adversarial (67.4% F1) but weakest on Temporal (33.9% F1) and Multi-lingual (41.1% F1), with Real-World, Domain-Specific, and Multi-lingual tasks averaging 6.4% lower than the other four tasks, indicating persistent gaps in up-to-date, specialized, and cross-lingual factual knowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the line of work treating language models as implicit knowledge bases (Petroni et al., 2019; Jiang et al., 2020c) and on existing fact-checking datasets (FEVER, FEVEROUS, VitaminC, PolitiFact, etc.), but departs from prior probing methods (cloze tests, rank-based retrieval) by reframing fact verification as a multiple-choice question-answering task that directly elicits LLMs' internal factual knowledge. Unlike TruthfulQA, which tests resistance to imitative falsehoods, or MMLU, which gauges breadth across subjects, Pinocchio unifies seven distinct dimensions of factual reasoning (multifaceted, structural, adversarial, temporal, real-world, domain-specific, multilingual) into a single 20K-question benchmark. The prompting strategy analysis extends recent work on CoT, self-consistency, and self-refinement by applying them specifically to factual verification rather than general reasoning tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even the strongest evaluated model (GPT-3.5-Turbo) achieves only ~47% overall accuracy, exposing a critical gap between LLMs' conversational fluency and their actual factual grounding—a bottleneck for deployment in high-stakes domains like healthcare, finance, and law. Key open questions include how to enable temporal knowledge updates without retraining, how to improve structured-data (table) reasoning, and whether retrieval-augmented approaches can close the gap on real-world and domain-specific facts. The finding that CoT degrades performance for untuned models and that fine-grained labels cause a ~24% accuracy drop suggests that current prompting paradigms are misaligned with the demands of precise factual verification.
