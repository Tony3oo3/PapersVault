## Problem

Existing robust fine-tuning (RFT) methods such as vanilla RFT and TWINS optimize both adversarial and natural objectives through the same feature extractor, producing significantly divergent gradient directions that destabilize optimization, limit adversarial robustness, and make performance highly sensitive to hyperparameters like learning rate. No prior method disentangles these conflicting optimization paths while remaining parameter-efficient and free of manual hyperparameter search.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241466/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained adversarially robust feature extractor (ResNet-18/50, ViT, or DeiT trained on ImageNet-1K at 224×224) and a downstream RGB image dataset (resized to 224×224) as input, and outputs an adversarially robust classifier with a standard linear head. During inference the LoRa branch is entirely dropped, so the deployed model is just the fine-tuned FE plus classifier with no extra latency. Adversarial training and evaluation both use a PGD budget of 8/255 with 10 steps.

## Method

AutoLoRa introduces a low-rank (LoRa) branch composed of two rank-decomposition matrices (B ∈ R^{d×r} and A ∈ R^{r×v}, r=8 by default, <5% extra parameters) that carries the natural CE objective, while the frozen-gradient FE carries only the adversarial objective, thereby eliminating the divergent gradient conflict. The FE additionally receives natural knowledge indirectly through a KL distillation loss between its adversarial logits and the natural soft labels produced by the LoRa branch (acting as a teacher). Two heuristic schedulers remove the need for hyperparameter search: (1) an LR scheduler inspired by AutoAttack's step-size controller that halves the LR whenever robust validation accuracy fails to improve over 75% of the interval between checkpoint epochs, and (2) a graduated-optimization scheduler where λ₁ (natural weight) decreases and λ₂ (distillation weight) increases as the LoRa branch's standard training accuracy rises, forcing the model to solve the easier natural problem before tackling the harder adversarial one.

## Results

Using ResNet-18, AutoLoRa achieves consistent PGD-10 robust accuracy gains over both Vanilla RFT and TWINS across all six downstream datasets, with the largest improvements on CIFAR-100 (32.71% vs. 30.37% TWINS, +2.34%) and DTD-57 (27.34% vs. 24.87% TWINS, +2.47%). With ResNet-50, the gains are also consistent, most notably on DOG-120 where AutoLoRa reaches 17.44% AA versus 14.41% for TWINS (+3.03%). Standard accuracy (SA) is within ~1% of TWINS in most cases, indicating that the robustness gains come at minimal cost to generalization. All improvements are statistically significant (p < 0.05) in the majority of dataset-metric combinations per t-tests.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoLoRa is compared against two baselines: Vanilla RFT (Zhang et al., ICML 2019) and TWINS (Liu et al., CVPR 2023, the previous SOTA RFT method). On PGD-10 robust accuracy, AutoLoRa wins on all six datasets for both ResNet-18 and ResNet-50, with gains of +0.90 to +3.85% over Vanilla RFT and +0.76 to +2.47% over TWINS (ResNet-18). The most striking improvement is on DOG-120 with ResNet-50, where AutoLoRa achieves 17.44% AA versus 14.41% for TWINS (+3.03%) and 12.73% for Vanilla RFT (+4.71%). On standard accuracy, AutoLoRa is within 0.16–1.31% of TWINS (sometimes slightly lower), indicating the robustness gains come at negligible cost to clean accuracy. The paper also shows that TWINS is extremely sensitive to the initial learning rate (AA drops from 25.45% to 1.00% when LR changes from 0.01 to 0.03 on CIFAR-100), whereas AutoLoRa without its automated LR scheduler maintains 27.2–27.5% AA across the same LR range, demonstrating substantially improved training stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the LoRa low-rank adaptation architecture (originally designed for parameter-efficient LLM fine-tuning) as a gradient-disentanglement mechanism in adversarial robustness fine-tuning, separating natural and adversarial objective gradients that previously flowed through the same feature extractor. It builds directly on the TRADES-style vanilla RFT loss (Zhang et al., 2019) and departs from TWINS (Liu et al., 2023), which partially mitigates gradient conflict via dual batch normalization, by fully routing the natural objective through a separate LoRa branch. The automated learning-rate scheduler adapts the step-size strategy from AutoAttack (Croce & Hein, 2020), and the λ₁/λ₂ scheduling implements the graduated optimization principle (Hazan et al., 2016) of solving a simplified sub-problem before the full adversarial objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AutoLoRa's practical value lies in eliminating the time-consuming per-task grid search that TWINS requires, offering a plug-and-play pipeline that converts any pre-trained FE into an adversarially robust model with fixed hyperparameters. The gradient-divergence diagnosis provides a concrete mechanistic explanation for why RFT is unstable and hyperparameter-sensitive, which should inform future robust fine-tuning designs. Limitations include evaluation restricted to image classification (no NLP or multimodal tasks), the LoRa branch adding up to ~5% extra parameters during training, and the heuristic schedulers lacking theoretical guarantees. The authors identify extending RFT to LLMs and label-flipping attacks as open directions.
