## Problem

MCMC sampling from unnormalized densities in high dimensions is severely hampered when the target is far from log-concave, with probability mass concentrated in small, ill-conditioned pockets separated by vast empty regions. Prior sequential methods (annealing, diffusion models) require carefully tuned noise schedules, and the joint multimeasurement approach of Saremi & Srivastava (2022) was shown to actually worsen the condition number relative to single-measurement sampling. The paper asks whether one can guarantee log-concave sampling at every step of a sequential procedure without any schedule, using only a fixed noise level.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unnormalized density p(x) ∝ exp(−f(x)) in R^d (access to f and optionally ∇f), a single isotropic noise level σ, and a number of measurements m. It produces samples from p(x) via the empirical Bayes estimator E[X|Y_{1:m}], which depends on the full history only through the running empirical mean ȳ_{1:m}, keeping the memory footprint at O(d). The 2-Wasserstein distance between the output distribution and p(x) is bounded by σ²d/m, so accuracy is controlled by the effective noise σ̃ = σ/√m.

## Method

The core contribution is sequential multimeasurement sampling (SMS): rather than sampling all m noisy measurements jointly (which worsens conditioning), the joint density p(y_{1:m}) is factored into conditionals p(y_t | y_{1:t−1}) and sampled sequentially. The key theoretical result (Theorem 1, 'once log-concave, always log-concave') shows that for a broad class of targets (compact support plus Gaussian noise), each conditional is strongly log-concave and its condition number monotonically decreases as t grows, so sampling becomes progressively easier. The inner loop at each t uses underdamped Langevin MCMC (BAOAB integration) with the gradient of log p(y_t|y_{1:t−1}), which is expressed in terms of the smoothed score function g(ȳ_{1:t}; σ/√t). The final 'jump' from accumulated noisy measurements to a clean sample is the empirical Bayes estimator, and the entire scheme is governed by a single noise parameter σ with no annealing schedule.

## Results

The paper evaluates SMS (Sequential Multimeasurement Sampling) using the sliced 2-Wasserstein distance on two test densities (elliptical Gaussian and Gaussian mixture) across varying dimensions d. SMS achieves consistently lower sliced 2-Wasserstein distance than all baselines, with the gap widening as d increases for the Gaussian mixture. The theoretical upper bound on the 2-Wasserstein distance between the target p_X and the empirical Bayes output is W₂² ≤ σ²d/m, which is tight for the Gaussian mixture example. A qualitative tunneling experiment shows SMS walkers reach a low-mass mode (20% of total mass) in a small number of steps, while identical Langevin MCMC walkers without smoothing remain trapped at the dominant mode (80% mass) after 100K steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMS outperforms JMS (Joint Multimeasurement Sampling, Saremi & Srivastava 2022) on both test densities, consistent with the theoretical result that sequential sampling monotonically improves the condition number while joint sampling worsens it (κ_{σ,m} = 1 + mσ⁻²τ_max²). SMS also beats single-measurement walk-jump (m=1, Saremi & Hyvärinen 2019), which becomes highly ineffective as dimension increases for the non-log-concave Gaussian mixture. Against unsmoothed Langevin MCMC (Sachs et al. 2017, σ=0), SMS achieves lower sliced 2-Wasserstein distance for at least one σ value at every tested dimension, and the advantage grows with d. Among MCMC algorithms used in the inner loop (Figures 5–6), the three unadjusted ULD variants (Sachs et al., Cheng et al., Shen & Lee) all converge faster and to lower W₂ than MALA (Roberts & Tweedie 1996), with Sachs et al. performing best and nearly matching the oracle sample variance. The plug-in score estimator (F.1) with n=500 MC samples approaches analytic score performance at d=2, while the Langevin score estimator (F.2) catches up at d=8 but both underperform the analytic score overall.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the walk-jump sampling framework (Saremi & Hyvärinen, 2019) and its multimeasurement generalization (Saremi & Srivastava, 2022) from the generative modeling setting (where samples from the target are available) to the fundamental problem of sampling from an unnormalized density with no samples. A key conceptual departure is the correction of a prior intuition: the authors prove that joint multimeasurement sampling (JMS) actually worsens the condition number relative to single-measurement sampling, motivating their sequential multimeasurement sampling (SMS) scheme where the non-Markovian history is tracked only through a running empirical mean. The framework is positioned against diffusion models (fixed noise vs. annealing schedule) and annealed importance sampling, and is agnostic to the inner MCMC algorithm, with Langevin dynamics (particularly underdamped variants) serving as the primary experimental vehicle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'once log-concave, always log-concave' guarantee (Theorem 1) provides a clean theoretical reduction of general sampling to a sequence of log-concave sampling problems controlled by a single noise parameter, a property no other sampling framework offers. The observed tunneling phenomenon—where the same Langevin MCMC algorithm escapes between modes when embedded in the SMS scheme but gets trapped without smoothing—suggests practical value for multimodal distributions that plague standard MCMC. Major open questions remain: whether the log-concave sampling strategy is optimal, how to efficiently estimate smoothed score functions for general (non-Gaussian) targets in high dimensions, and whether the framework scales beyond the low-dimensional test densities studied here.
