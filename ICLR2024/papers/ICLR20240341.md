## Problem

Current speech quality estimators rely on supervised learning with expensive human-annotated MOS labels, limiting scalability and generalizability to new domains. Existing self-supervised alternatives like SpeechLMScore depend on speech language models whose perplexity is insensitive to minor per-frame degradation. The paper asks whether a VQ-VAE trained solely on clean speech can produce a quality score that correlates well with human perception without any labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240341/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a speech spectrogram X ∈ R^{F×T} (treated as a T-length 1-D signal with F channels) and applies instance normalization before encoding. For quality estimation, it outputs a scalar VQScore computed as the mean cosine similarity between encoder embeddings and their nearest codebook vectors across all T frames. For speech enhancement, it outputs a reconstructed spectrogram X̂ ∈ R^{F×T} decoded from the quantized embeddings, with Transformer layers added to the encoder/decoder to improve reconstruction fidelity.

## Method

The core architecture is a VQ-VAE with a 1-D CNN encoder/decoder and a codebook of size V with dimension d, where the key insight is that the quantization error (distance between encoder output Z and its nearest codebook entry Z_q) correlates far better with perceptual quality than the reconstruction error in signal space. Cosine similarity in the code space (z) is used for quality estimation to group similar phonemes regardless of volume, while L2 distance is used for speech enhancement to preserve amplitude information. For self-supervised SE, a two-step training procedure is introduced: first, a VQ-VAE is trained on clean speech; second, a student model initialized from the teacher undergoes adversarial training where the most confusing perturbation δ is found to maximize cross-entropy between student and teacher token predictions, then the student is fine-tuned to minimize that loss, making the encoder robust to out-of-domain noisy inputs without any (noisy, clean) pairs.

## Results

For speech quality estimation, VQScore_(cos,z) achieves Pearson correlation coefficients of 0.5865 (Tencent_wR), 0.7159 (Tencent_woR), 0.4880 (IUB_cosine), and 0.8604 (IUB_voices) with real subjective quality scores, trained only on 460 h of LibriSpeech clean with no quality labels. For speech enhancement on the mismatched DNS1 test set, Proposed+AT achieves OVRL scores of 2.750 (Real), 3.162 (Noreverb), and 2.456 (Reverb), outperforming Demucs by 0.18–0.28 OVRL in the more challenging Real and Reverb conditions. On the matched VoiceBank-DEMAND test set, Proposed+AT reaches PESQ 2.38 and OVRL 2.941, surpassing NyTT (PESQ 2.30, OVRL 2.736) and MetricGAN-U (PESQ 2.13, OVRL 2.660).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the self-supervised baseline SpeechLMScore, VQScore wins on 3 of 4 test sets (IUB_cosine: 0.4880 vs 0.3913; IUB_voices: 0.8604 vs 0.6891; Tencent_woR: 0.7159 vs 0.7079) and is essentially tied on Tencent_wR (0.5865 vs 0.5910), while using only ~7% of the training data. Against the supervised DNSMOS P.835, VQScore is competitive: it wins on IUB_cosine (0.4880 vs 0.3938) and IUB_voices (0.8604 vs 0.8181) but trails on Tencent_wR (0.5865 vs 0.6566) and Tencent_woR (0.7159 vs 0.7769). For speech enhancement on the mismatched DNS1 test set, Proposed+AT outperforms Demucs by 0.18 OVRL (Real: 2.750 vs 2.570) and 0.28 OVRL (Reverb: 2.456 vs 2.175), and is competitive with the supervised CNN-Transformer (2.750 vs 2.620 Real; 3.162 vs 3.124 Noreverb; 2.456 vs 2.409 Reverb). Proposed+AT significantly outperforms NyTT and MetricGAN-U on the matched VoiceBank-DEMAND set (OVRL 2.941 vs 2.736 and 2.660; PESQ 2.38 vs 2.30 and 2.13). In the frame-level SNR estimation experiment, the self-supervised VQScore-based metric achieves 0.789 correlation, surpassing the supervised Li et al. baseline (0.721).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the VQ-VAE architecture (Van Den Oord et al., 2017) — originally designed for discrete representation learning — as a unified framework for both speech quality estimation and self-supervised speech enhancement, drawing conceptual inspiration from autoencoder-based anomaly detection (An & Cho, 2015) and the SpeechLMScore approach (Maiti et al., 2022). The key departure from prior autoencoder-based quality estimators is the use of quantization error in the discrete code space rather than reconstruction error in the signal space, and from prior self-supervised SE work (Wang et al., 2020) is the replacement of cycle-consistency losses with codebook-based representation sharing plus adversarial self-distillation. SoundStream's (Zeghidour et al., 2021) k-means codebook initialization is adopted as a practical detail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By requiring only clean speech for training, the proposed framework eliminates the expensive collection of MOS ratings and (noisy, clean) pairs, making both quality estimation and SE more scalable and domain-adaptable. The finding that code-space quantization error (cosine similarity) correlates more strongly with perceptual quality than signal-space reconstruction error offers a useful conceptual insight into where in the representation hierarchy quality information lives. However, the VQ module introduces measurable speech distortion (lower SIG in listening tests), and the authors acknowledge that frame-level weighting and TTS-specific global naturalness remain unaddressed — open questions that limit immediate deployment in production TTS evaluation pipelines.
