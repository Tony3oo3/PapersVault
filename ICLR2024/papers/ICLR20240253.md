## Problem

Previous social bias detection in pre-trained language models treats them as black boxes, relying on prompt templates or probing to observe biased outputs, while existing debiasing methods require costly retraining on anti-stereotypical datasets and risk catastrophic forgetting. No prior work had identified which specific internal neurons are responsible for the uneven distribution of predictions across demographic groups, leaving both interpretability and targeted mitigation unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240253/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fill-in-the-blank prompt (e.g., 'The Gender of this lonely person is [MASK]') constructed from 17 templates, 600 judgmental modifiers, and 9 demographic dimensions, yielding 193,800 prompts across 114 Judged Unfair Targets. It operates on the feed-forward network neurons at the masked token position of a BERT/RoBERTa model, producing a per-neuron IG² attribution score that quantifies each neuron's contribution to the absolute logits gap between the two demographics in the Unfair Target pair. The downstream BNS step converts these scores into a binary suppression mask (threshold σ = 0.2 × max IG²) that zeros out flagged neuron activations at inference time.

## Method

The core contribution is IG² (Integrated Gap Gradients), which extends Integrated Gradients from attributing a single model output to attributing the *logits gap* between two demographic predictions. For each FFN neuron w_j^(l) at the masked token, IG² integrates the gradient of |P(d₁|α·w̄) − P(d₂|α·w̄)| with respect to w_j^(l) as α sweeps from 0 to 1 (Riemann approximation, m=20 steps), so neurons that disproportionately widen the demographic gap receive high scores. Derived from this, Bias Neuron Suppression (BNS) is a training-free debiasing step that simply sets the activation of every neuron whose IG² score exceeds σ to zero, requiring no gradient updates or auxiliary data. The key insight is that social bias is a distributional (pairwise) property rather than a singular prediction, so the attribution target must be the gap itself; this makes IG² generalizable to any imbalanced-output scenario. Verification experiments confirm that suppressing identified neurons reduces the logits gap by ~23% while amplifying them increases it by ~29%, whereas manipulating the same number of random neurons has negligible effect.

## Results

On the StereoSet intrasentence benchmark, BNS (Bias Neuron Suppression) achieves the best ICAT scores across all three models: 81.82 on BERT-Base-cased, 77.81 on RoBERTa-Base, and 84.79 on FairBERTa, while preserving language modeling ability (LMS drops by <1 point from the original model). For example, on RoBERTa-Base, BNS improves ICAT from 68.85 to 77.81 and reduces SS from 62.46 to 57.43, with LMS remaining at 91.39 (vs. 91.70 original). The method is training-free, requiring only suppression of a small number of identified neurons (fewer than 4 on average for most demographic dimensions).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BNS outperforms all three published baselines on the combined ICAT metric across every model. Against DPCE (Kaneko & Bollegala, 2021), BNS wins decisively: DPCE actually *worsens* the stereotype score for BERT (56.93→62.41) and RoBERTa (62.46→64.09) while BNS improves both, and BNS achieves ICAT 81.82 vs. 58.97 on BERT. Against AutoDebias (Guo et al., 2022), BNS achieves comparable or better SS (e.g., 52.78 vs. 53.03 on BERT) but retains LMS at 86.64 versus AutoDebias's 50.74, yielding ICAT 81.82 vs. 47.62. Against FairBERTa (Qian et al., 2022), which requires expensive retraining on anti-stereotypical data, BNS applied to FairBERTa pushes ICAT from 76.06 to 84.79 while being entirely training-free. The intuitive Union_IG baseline (suppressing all neurons relevant to either demographic) achieves the best SS (e.g., 51.01 on BERT) but catastrophically destroys language modeling (LMS drops to 31.47), confirming that BNS's targeted gap-based attribution is essential for preserving utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Integrated Gradients (Sundararajan et al., 2017) from singular output attribution to pairwise distributional gap attribution, formulating social bias as an uneven logit distribution across demographics rather than a single biased prediction. It departs from the dominant black-box paradigm in bias detection (prompt/probing-based methods) and from retraining-based debiasing (FairBERTa, DPCE, AutoDebias) by introducing a training-free, neuron-level interpretability and mitigation pipeline. The authors explicitly position their work as filling a gap: prior fairness research has not focused on interpretability at the internal unit level of PLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that debiasing via anti-stereotypical retraining (FairBERTa) does not reduce the total number of social bias neurons but rather migrates them from deep to shallow layers offers a new mechanistic explanation for why such methods work, potentially guiding more targeted future interventions. The practical impact is significant: BNS achieves competitive fairness (ICAT 84.79 on FairBERTa) while being training-free and preserving language modeling ability (LMS 91.05 vs. 91.90 baseline), in stark contrast to Union_IG which collapses LMS to 37.36. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to decoder-only LLMs, larger architectures, and whether the binary zero-suppression can be replaced by more refined interventions; the current evaluation is limited to 12-layer BERT/RoBERTa on the intrasentence subset of StereoSet.
