## Problem

Empirical work (von Oswald et al., 2022) showed that one-layer transformers with linear self-attention trained on synthetic linear regression tasks learn to implement one step of gradient descent, but the theoretical justification for why this specific algorithm emerges as the global minimizer of the pre-training loss remained unproven. This paper closes that gap by providing a rigorous characterization of the global minimizer and further investigates how changes to the data distribution (non-isotropic covariates, nonlinear target functions) alter the learned algorithm.

## Input / Output

The transformer receives a sequence of n in-context exemplars, each encoded as a (d+1)-dimensional token [x_i; y_i] where x_i ∈ R^d and y_i ∈ R, followed by a query token [x_{n+1}; 0]. The model is a single linear self-attention layer of width d+1 (with key, query, and value matrices in R^{(d+1)×(d+1)}) followed by a linear head h ∈ R^{d+1}, producing a scalar prediction ŷ. The pre-training loss is the expected squared error over the joint distribution of the context and the test pair, with x_i drawn i.i.d. from N(0, I_d) and y_i = w^T x_i + ε_i where w ~ N(0, I_d) is resampled per sequence.

## Method

The paper proves that the global minimizer of the pre-training loss implements one step of gradient descent from zero initialization on the least-squares objective, with a specific learning rate η determined by the data distribution. The key proof technique rewrites the loss to show the effective linear predictor M_{:,1:d}^T G_D w should match ηX^T y, then demonstrates this by showing the gradients of the loss with respect to the predictor parameters are identical to those of a surrogate loss targeting ηX^T y, using rotational invariance of the isotropic Gaussian to establish that certain cross-moment expectations are scalar multiples of the identity. Extending to non-isotropic covariates (x_i ~ N(0, Σ)), a change of variables reduces the problem to the isotropic case, yielding one step of preconditioned GD with preconditioner Σ^{-1}. For nonlinear target functions satisfying a rotation-invariance and negation-symmetry assumption (e.g., random neural networks), the same one-step-GD result holds, indicating the architecture constrains the learned algorithm regardless of response nonlinearity.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 1) proves that the global minimizer of the pre-training loss for a one-layer transformer with linear self-attention implements exactly one step of gradient descent on a least-squares linear regression objective, with learning rate η = E[ŵ_D^T X^T ȳ] / E[ȳ^T XX^T ȳ]. Theorem 2 extends this to non-isotropic covariates, showing the minimizer implements one step of preconditioned GD with preconditioner Σ^{-1}. Theorem 3 shows that even with nonlinear target functions (e.g., random neural networks), the minimizer still implements one step of GD. In the empirical validation (Appendix F), at unit scaling the MSE between the trained transformer and ground truth is ~2.6, while the MSE between the transformer and the GD baseline (using the theoretically predicted η) is only ~0.14, confirming the theoretical prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical paper, so there is no standard benchmark comparison table. The empirical validation in Appendix F compares the trained one-layer linear self-attention transformer against a one-step GD baseline using the theoretically predicted learning rate η (rather than a line-searched η as in von Oswald et al., 2022). At unit input scaling, the transformer's predictions are much closer to the GD baseline (MSE ≈ 0.14) than to the ground truth (MSE ≈ 2.6), and as inputs are scaled up, the gap to ground truth grows rapidly while the gap to the GD baseline grows slowly — confirming the transformer implements GD rather than ridge regression or OLS. The paper also positions its results relative to concurrent work by Ahn et al. (2023) and Zhang et al. (2023), which prove the same isotropic GD result but additionally cover multi-layer transformers and training dynamics; the unique contribution here is the nonlinear target function setting (Theorem 3), which neither concurrent work addresses for the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first rigorous theoretical proof that the global minimizer of the pre-training loss for a one-layer linear self-attention transformer implements one step of gradient descent, confirming the empirical observation of von Oswald et al. (2022). It builds on the expressive-power constructions of Akyürek et al. (2023) and Garg et al. (2022), which showed transformers *can* represent GD, by showing GD is not merely representable but is the *optimal* algorithm under the training objective. The paper is concurrent with and largely independent of Ahn et al. (2023) and Zhang et al. (2023), which arrive at similar isotropic and preconditioned GD results, though this paper additionally extends to nonlinear target functions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a critical theoretical gap in understanding in-context learning by showing that the architecture (linear self-attention) and the covariate distribution jointly determine the learned algorithm, while the response distribution (even when nonlinear) has minimal effect due to the model's representational constraints. This suggests that capacity-limited transformers will default to simple iterative methods regardless of task complexity, which has implications for understanding why practical LLMs may struggle with tasks requiring more sophisticated algorithms. Open questions include extending the analysis to multi-head and multi-layer settings with nonlinear targets, and understanding how these results generalize beyond synthetic Gaussian data to realistic language tasks.
