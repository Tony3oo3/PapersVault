## Problem

Robust Adversarial Reinforcement Learning (RARL) trains a protagonist against an adversary in a zero-sum Markov game, but finding the Nash equilibrium requires solving a strongly non-convex non-concave saddle point optimization that is unstable and prone to convergence failure, especially in high-dimensional continuous control. The paper asks whether the optimization complexity can be systematically reduced while preserving the robustness guarantees of a fully rational adversary.

## Input / Output

Both agents observe the same continuous state vector from MuJoCo environments (e.g., 80-dimensional quadruped observations including egocentric state, torso velocity, IMU data, and goal displacement). The adversary outputs continuous force vectors applied to specific body parts (e.g., 2D forces on feet and torso for Cheetah, 1D wind force on all bodies for Quadruped-Maze), while the protagonist outputs continuous joint-torque actions. A key structural detail is that both policies are conditioned on the adversary's temperature α, which is sampled from a learned gamma distribution at each training iteration, making the effective input a (state, temperature) pair rather than just a state.

## Method

QARL formulates an entropy-regularized zero-sum Markov game whose solution is a Quantal Response Equilibrium (QRE), where the adversary's temperature coefficient α directly controls its rationality: α→∞ yields a fully random adversary (reducing the problem to plain maximization), while α→0 recovers the standard Nash equilibrium with maximal robustness. The core mechanism is an automatic curriculum that maintains a gamma distribution p_ω(α) over the adversary's temperature, updated each iteration by solving a constrained optimization that minimizes KL divergence to a sharp target distribution (approximating α=0) subject to (i) the protagonist's expected performance not dropping below a threshold ξ and (ii) the distribution not shifting more than ε in KL divergence from its previous value. Policies are approximated with temperature-conditioned SAC actors and critics, where the soft Q-function is parameterized to accept α as an additional input, enabling a single network to represent the full rationality spectrum. The protagonist's own temperature β follows the standard SAC adaptive schedule rather than the curriculum, keeping the protagonist near-fully rational at all stages so it can reliably solve the task while the adversary's challenge gradually intensifies.

## Results

Across 15 MuJoCo control problems, QARL achieves a +4.2±0.5% average performance improvement over the SAC baseline (the only method that improves nominal performance) and a +48.7±1.2% robustness improvement. All adversarial baselines degrade nominal performance: RARL (-5.7%), Force-Curriculum (-14.7%), SAC MixedNE-LD (-16.6%), and CAT (-18.0%). In the quadruped maze navigation task, QARL resists wind forces 4× stronger than the training force, while RARL and other baselines fail to do so.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QARL outperforms all four baselines on both performance and robustness across the 15 MuJoCo environments. Compared to RARL (Pinto et al., 2017), QARL improves performance by 9.9 percentage points (+4.2% vs. -5.7%) and robustness by 38.6 percentage points (+48.7% vs. +10.1%), and avoids RARL's collapse to suboptimal policies in the sparse-reward Cartpole-Swingup and the quadruped maze. Against SAC MixedNE-LD (Kamalaruban et al., 2020), which uses Langevin dynamics to escape local optima, QARL gains 20.8 points in performance and 28.7 points in robustness. Versus CAT (Sheng et al., 2022), which applies a hand-crafted linear curriculum on adversary strength, QARL improves performance by 22.2 points and robustness by 37.0 points. The authors' own Force-Curriculum baseline, which applies the same automatic curriculum scheme but over adversary force magnitude rather than rationality, is also outperformed by QARL on both metrics (+4.2% vs. -14.7% performance; +48.7% vs. -3.3% robustness), confirming that modulating rationality via entropy temperature is more effective than clipping action magnitudes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Robust Adversarial Reinforcement Learning (RARL) framework by connecting entropy-regularized zero-sum Markov games to Quantal Response Equilibria (QRE), a bounded-rationality generalization of Nash equilibria previously established in game theory (McKelvey & Palfrey, 1995) and entropy-regularized stochastic games (Savas et al., 2019; Cen et al., 2021). While prior work addressed RARL's saddle-point instability through heuristic fixes—Langevin dynamics (Kamalaruban et al., 2020), extragradient methods (Cen et al., 2021), or hand-crafted force-strength curricula (Sheng et al., 2022)—this paper provides a theoretically grounded mechanism for modulating adversary difficulty via the SAC temperature parameter. The temperature-conditioned value functions and policies (Corollary 4.2) are derived from the soft action-value framework of Schaul et al. (2015) and Haarnoja et al. (2017), repurposing SAC's existing temperature machinery as a rationality dial rather than a mere exploration knob.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The automatic curriculum generation via constrained KL-divergence optimization over a gamma distribution offers a principled, self-tuning alternative to manual annealing schedules, reducing the hyperparameter burden of adversarial training. The substantial robustness gains (+48.7% over SAC) and the qualitative result in the quadruped maze (learning to anchor a leg against 4× training force) suggest the approach could meaningfully reduce the cost of robustifying agents for real-world deployment. Limitations include evaluation restricted to MuJoCo/DMC continuous control, the assumption of a zero-sum two-agent setting, and the reliance on SAC-style stochastic policies; open questions include whether the rationality curriculum extends to multi-agent or partially observable settings, and whether the gamma-distribution parameterization is optimal compared to other distributional families.
