## Problem

Despite impressive problem-solving abilities of LLMs and LMMs, their mathematical reasoning in visual contexts has not been systematically evaluated. Existing benchmarks are either purely textual, focus on a single visual domain (e.g., geometry diagrams or bar charts), or contain only a small fraction of math-requiring questions, leaving a comprehensive vision-language mathematical reasoning evaluation largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240935/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each example pairs an image from one of 19 visual context types (geometry diagrams, function plots, tables, natural images, puzzle test figures, etc.) with a question and optional answer choices. Questions are split into multiple-choice (55.2%) and free-form (44.8%, with integer, float, or list answers). Evaluation follows a three-stage protocol: the model generates a free-form response, GPT-4 extracts the short answer text (>99.5% accuracy on a 200-example pilot), and the extracted answer is normalized and scored for exact-match accuracy.

## Method

MATHVISTA is constructed around a three-axis taxonomy: seven mathematical reasoning types (algebraic, arithmetic, geometry, logical, numeric common sense, scientific, statistical) × five task types (FQA, GPS, MWP, TQA, VQA) × 19 visual context types. The 6,141 examples are drawn from 28 existing multimodal datasets (9 MathQA + 19 VQA, each capped at 400 examples) plus three newly annotated datasets (IQTest for logical reasoning on puzzle figures, FunctionQA for algebraic reasoning over function plots, PaperQA for scientific reasoning on academic figures). Twelve foundation models are evaluated under three setups: text-only LLMs (ChatGPT, GPT-4, Claude-2) with zero/few-shot CoT and PoT prompting; augmented LLMs additionally receiving Bard-generated captions and EasyOCR text; and LMMs (seven open-source + Bard + GPT-4V) receiving the image directly. A human baseline (60.3% accuracy, AMT annotators with high-school+ education) and qualitative error analysis of Bard (49.6% hallucination rate in wrong explanations) provide interpretive context, while GPT-4V's self-verification, self-consistency, and multi-turn chatbot behaviors are explored as emergent capabilities.

## Results

On MATHVISTA (testmini, 1,000 examples), GPT-4V achieves a state-of-the-art overall accuracy of 49.9%, substantially outperforming Multimodal Bard (34.8%) by 15.1 percentage points. The best text-only LLM (2-shot CoT GPT-4) reaches only 29.2%, while the best augmented LLM (2-shot PoT GPT-4 with Bard captions + EasyOCR) scores 33.9%. Human annotators (high school diploma or higher) achieve 60.3% accuracy, leaving a 10.4% gap above GPT-4V. Among open-source LMMs, LLaVA-LLaMA-2-13B leads at 26.1%, well below both proprietary models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4V (49.9%) dominates all 12 evaluated models, outperforming the second-best Multimodal Bard (34.8%) by 15.1 points and the best open-source LMM LLaVA-LLaMA-2-13B (26.1%) by 23.8 points. GPT-4V surpasses human performance (60.3%) on specific sub-tasks including GPS (50.5% vs 48.4%), TQA (65.2% vs 63.2%), and algebraic reasoning (53.0% vs 50.9%), but still trails humans by 10.4 points overall, particularly on logical reasoning (21.6% vs 40.7%) and numeric commonsense (20.1% vs 53.8%). Among text-only LLMs, 2-shot CoT GPT-4 (29.2%) leads, but augmenting GPT-4 with Bard captions and EasyOCR OCR text (2-shot PoT, 33.9%) narrows the gap to Bard to within 0.9 points. The ablation shows that augmented LLMs gain ~4–7 points over their text-only counterparts, yet remain below native multimodal models, indicating that external visual tools (captions + OCR) cannot fully substitute for direct visual perception. Open-source LMMs (IDEFICS 19.8% to LLaVA 26.1%) cluster in a narrow band well below proprietary models, suggesting that current open architectures lack the integrated visual-mathematical reasoning needed for MATHVISTA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by consolidating 28 existing multimodal datasets (spanning geometry QA, chart understanding, math word problems, and scientific figure QA) plus three newly created datasets (IQTest, FunctionQA, PaperQA) into a single unified benchmark for mathematical reasoning in visual contexts. Prior mathematical reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, MathQA, LILA) are text-only, while prior visual reasoning datasets (ChartQA, FigureQA, GeoQA) each target narrow visual contexts or reasoning types in isolation. The paper also departs from prior LMM evaluations by introducing an LLM-based answer extraction protocol and by providing the first comprehensive quantitative and qualitative study of GPT-4V's mathematical reasoning capabilities, including analyses of self-verification and self-consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MATHVISTA establishes a challenging and comprehensive standard (6,141 examples, 7 reasoning types, 19 visual context types) that will likely become a go-to benchmark for evaluating multimodal mathematical reasoning, given that even GPT-4V achieves only 49.9% versus 60.3% human accuracy. The finding that hallucination accounts for nearly half of Bard's incorrect explanations, and that open-source LMMs (≤26%) trail proprietary models by a wide margin, highlights concrete failure modes and a significant capability gap that should guide future model development. Open questions include whether the 10.4% human-model gap can be closed through better visual perception, more rigorous multi-step reasoning, or new training objectives, and whether the benchmark's heterogeneity (only 85.6% annotated, varied annotation formats) limits fine-grained analysis.
