## Problem

Existing post-hoc interpretation methods (LIME, Kernel-Shap, L2X) explain structured output models by treating each output variable in isolation, ignoring the structural dependencies between output variables that are central to how these models operate. In structured models, a feature can influence a target output indirectly through its effect on correlated output variables, so ignoring these interactions leads to incomplete or misleading feature attributions. This paper is the first to design an interpreter specifically for structured output models that incorporates output-variable correlations during training.

## Input / Output

The method takes an arbitrary input feature vector x (text feature vectors of length 1001–1836, or RGB image pixels for segmentation) and a target output index t, and produces a k-hot binary vector of the same dimensionality as x, where the 1-positions mark the k most important features for the t-th output. The structured black-box model is treated as fully opaque—no access to its architecture or parameters is required. The interpreter is a globally-trained function over the entire input space, so once trained it can be applied to any new input in a single forward pass, unlike perturbation-based methods that require many evaluations per sample.

## Method

SOInter consists of two components: an interpreter block (a DNN followed by a Gumbel-Softmax unit that produces a differentiable k-hot selection vector) and an energy block (a structured prediction energy network that scores the consistency of input-output pairs). The training loss is energy-based: it substitutes the target output in the perturbed prediction with the original target output, then penalizes the energy difference between this hybrid vector and the full perturbed prediction, effectively measuring whether the selected features suffice to reproduce the target given the structural context of all other outputs. Training proceeds iteratively—updating the interpreter parameters, querying the black-box for new perturbed predictions, and fine-tuning the energy network with a structured hinge loss to maintain its calibration on the evolving perturbed inputs. The key insight is that the energy function's structural terms (capturing inter-output correlations) naturally propagate the influence of indirect feature effects into the interpreter's gradient signal, something that gradient-based or perturbation-based methods on a single output cannot capture.

## Results

On the Bibtex multi-label dataset, SOInter achieves a relative post-hoc F1 of 0.68, outperforming Lime (0.60), L2X (0.13), and its own ablation SOInter-1 (0.64). On Enron, SOInter reaches 0.57 relative post-hoc F1 versus 0.35 for Lime and 0.06 for L2X. On the synthetic energy-function datasets, SOInter attains the highest accuracy and lowest median rank (closest to the ideal 2.5) across most input sizes and target outputs, confirming that incorporating structural dependencies among output variables improves feature-selection quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOInter consistently outperforms both Lime and L2X on the Bibtex and Enron datasets in terms of relative post-hoc F1, with the largest gap over L2X (0.68 vs. 0.13 on Bibtex; 0.57 vs. 0.06 on Enron). Against Lime, SOInter gains 0.08 on Bibtex and 0.22 on Enron relative post-hoc F1, indicating that leveraging inter-output correlations yields meaningfully better feature selections than the local surrogate approach. On IMDB, the absolute F1 values are very low for all methods (plain F1 = 0.05), and SOInter-1 actually edges out SOInter on relative post-hoc F1 (0.31 vs. 0.24), suggesting that in this particular regime the structural information provides less benefit. On the synthetic energy-function benchmarks (Figures 1 and 4), SOInter achieves the highest exact-match accuracy and the lowest median rank across nearly all input sizes and target outputs, while Kernel-Shap and L2X degrade more sharply as the input dimensionality grows. The ablation SOInter-1 (which ignores other output variables) performs competitively with Lime but is consistently surpassed by full SOInter, confirming that the energy-based structural term is the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

SOInter builds on two key threads: Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization (Jang et al., 2017) to make discrete k-hot feature selection differentiable, and structured prediction energy networks (Gygli et al., 2017) to model inter-output dependencies. It departs from existing post-hoc interpretation methods (Lime, Kernel-Shap, L2X) by introducing an energy-based loss that explicitly penalizes the interpreter when the selected features fail to preserve the target output's value *in the context of all correlated outputs*, rather than treating each output in isolation. The two-phase training (pre-train energy network, then jointly fine-tune with the interpreter) is a novel architectural choice not present in prior interpretation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first interpretation method designed specifically for structured output models, addressing a gap where existing techniques ignore inter-output correlations that can cause a feature to influence the target indirectly through other outputs. Limitations include the assumption that zeroing unselected features is a valid perturbation (which may confuse black-boxes where zero carries semantic meaning), the need for two neural networks increasing deployment memory, and the reliance on an approximate energy network that may not perfectly capture the black-box's internal structure. Open directions include extending the interpreter to select coarse conceptual elements rather than individual features, and handling structured models where the output space has continuous or hierarchical structure beyond binary variables.
