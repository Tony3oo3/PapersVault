## Problem

Language models frequently produce contradictory responses when generating an answer versus validating that same answer (e.g., answering "15" to "7+8?" but "False" to "7+8=15, True or False?"). This generator-validator inconsistency (GV-consistency) undermines trust in LMs, yet no systematic benchmark or improvement method existed. The paper finds even GPT-4 achieves only 76% GV-consistency across diverse tasks, with open models like Alpaca-30B at just 60%.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a task input x and constructs a generator query x_G (eliciting a free-form response) and a validator query x_V (eliciting a binary True/False or A/B judgment). To prevent the trivial solution of always answering "correct," randomness is injected either by randomizing the generator's correctness target (r ∈ {-1,1} for correct vs. incorrect) or by randomizing the A/B option ordering in the validator. The GV-consistency score is the fraction of (x_G, y_G, x_V, y_V) tuples where the validator's binary judgment aligns with the randomized label r.

## Method

Consistency fine-tuning is a label-free, iterative procedure: (1) generate paired generator-validator responses from the LM using randomized prompts, (2) filter to retain only GV-consistent pairs, and (3) fine-tune the LM via maximum likelihood on the filtered set, optimizing log p(y_G|x_G) + log p(y_V|x_V). The generator and validator learn from each other — the validator learns to endorse the generator's outputs while the generator learns to produce responses the validator endorses. The procedure is iterated, with each round's fine-tuned model generating the next round's data, progressively enlarging the consistent subset. Applied via LoRA (rank 32) to Alpaca-30B, this improves GV-consistency from 60% to 94% over three iterations and also boosts generator quality by 14% and validator accuracy by 8.5% without any labeled data.

## Results

Consistency fine-tuning improves GV-consistency of Alpaca-30B from 65.1% (base) to 94.1% (iter3) on average across 6 tasks, with the first iteration alone yielding a 16% gain (65.1% → 85.9%). The self-training baseline (SELFTRAIN) fails to improve consistency, fluctuating around the base level (63.4%). Beyond consistency, the method improves generator quality by 14% on average and validator accuracy by 8.5% on average without any labeled data. Gains extrapolate to unseen domains: consistency improves by 15% on average across out-of-distribution settings (unseen QA types, unseen harmful question categories, unseen writing styles).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares consistency fine-tuning against two baselines. SELFTRAIN (Xie et al., 2020), which fine-tunes on all generated generator-validator pairs without filtering for consistency, fails to improve GV-consistency and actually slightly decreases it (63.4% vs. 65.1% base average), with per-task scores fluctuating around the base level. CC-FT (inspired by Keskar et al., 2019), which prepends the consistency label to prompts and fine-tunes on all data, improves consistency to 69.1% average—above both BASE and SELFTRAIN—but substantially underperforms consistency fine-tuning (85.9% at iter1, 94.1% at iter3). The key advantage of filtering over label-conditioning is that the filtered dataset contains only high-quality consistent examples, whereas CC-FT must model both consistent and inconsistent classes, diluting the learning signal. On generator and validator accuracy (Table 5), consistency fine-tuning outperforms both SELFTRAIN and BASE across most tasks, with the largest gains in arithmetic (generator: 0.668→0.727) and prompt prioritization (generator: 0.418→0.837; validator: 0.817→0.996).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by defining generator-validator consistency (GV-consistency) as a distinct, measurable property of LMs, explicitly differentiating it from prior consistency work on prompt rephrasing (Elazar et al., 2021; Zhou et al., 2022) and logical consistency (Mitchell et al., 2022; Jung et al., 2022). Methodologically, it repurposes the co-training paradigm (Blum & Mitchell, 1998) and self-training (Xie et al., 2020) by treating the generator and validator as two complementary 'views' that bootstrap each other, but departs from Constitutional AI (Bai et al., 2023) by improving both the generator and validator rather than assuming a frozen, correct critic. The GAN analogy is explicitly invoked and inverted: where GANs optimize for generator-discriminator disagreement, GV-consistency optimizes for agreement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GV-consistency is a prerequisite for trusting LMs in high-stakes pipelines like RLHF reward modeling and LM-as-a-judge evaluation, where an inconsistent validator silently corrupts the training signal. The finding that even GPT-4 achieves only 76% consistency, and that a simple unlabeled-data filtering procedure can close most of the gap while simultaneously improving generator and validator accuracy, offers a practical, low-cost intervention for practitioners. Open questions include whether consistency fine-tuning can improve factual correctness when both generator and validator are weak (the 'consistently wrong' regime), whether the approach scales to more expressive (non-binary) validator feedback, and whether the gains hold for models where the generator and validator are architecturally decoupled rather than sharing the same weights.
