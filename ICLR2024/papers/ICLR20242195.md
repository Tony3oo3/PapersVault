## Problem

Existing automatic jailbreak methods (e.g., GCG) generate semantically meaningless token sequences that are easily detected by perplexity-based defenses, while handcrafted prompts like DAN lack scalability and adaptability to updated models. The paper asks whether it is possible to automatically generate jailbreak prompts that are both stealthy (semantically meaningful, low perplexity) and effective across models and samples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242195/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a prototype handcrafted DAN-style jailbreak prompt, a set of 520 malicious questions from AdvBench, and a white-box victim LLM (Vicuna-7b, Guanaco-7b, or Llama2-7b-chat) whose log-likelihood is used for fitness evaluation. It outputs an optimized jailbreak prompt per question that, when prepended to the malicious query, elicits a non-refusal response. The fitness signal is the negative log-likelihood of a target response prefix (e.g., 'Sure, here is how to...'), and termination triggers when no refusal keywords appear in the model's top-K response tokens or a max iteration count is reached.

## Method

AutoDAN formulates jailbreak prompt generation as a discrete optimization problem solved by a hierarchical genetic algorithm (HGA) with two nested levels. At the sentence level, a momentum word scoring scheme tracks the average fitness contribution of each word across iterations and replaces lower-scoring words with higher-scoring near-synonyms; at the paragraph level, a multi-point crossover exchanges sentences between parent prompts selected via softmax-based roulette, followed by LLM-based mutation (GPT-4 rewrites the prompt while preserving length and meaning). The population is initialized by applying LLM-based diversification to the prototype DAN prompt, which preserves the logical structure of known-effective jailbreaks while introducing word-level diversity. This hierarchical design—optimizing word choices first, then sentence combinations—avoids the local optima that trap the flat AutoDAN-GA variant, as shown by continued loss reduction in Fig. 2. The key insight is that lexical-level words lack direct gradient correlation with the loss, making evolutionary search over discrete text more appropriate than token-level gradient optimization, and that semantically meaningful prompts are inherently more transferable across models.

## Results

On the AdvBench dataset (520 harmful requests), AutoDAN-HGA achieves keyword-based ASR of 0.977 (Vicuna-7b), 0.985 (Guanaco-7b), and 0.608 (Llama2-7b-chat), outperforming GCG by up to 15.4 percentage points on Llama2 (0.608 vs. 0.454). In terms of stealthiness, AutoDAN-HGA produces prompts with sentence perplexity of 37–54, comparable to handcrafted DAN (~23) and orders of magnitude lower than GCG (458–1532), enabling it to bypass perplexity-based defenses that reduce GCG's ASR to 0.000 on Llama2 while leaving AutoDAN unaffected. Cross-model transferability shows AutoDAN-HGA retains 0.70–0.74 ASR when transferring between open-source models, versus only 0.12–0.14 for GCG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GCG (Zou et al., 2023), AutoDAN-HGA wins on both attack effectiveness and stealthiness: it achieves higher ASR on all three models (most notably 0.608 vs. 0.454 on the more robust Llama2-7b-chat, a +15.4 pp gain) while producing prompts with 10–30× lower perplexity (39–54 vs. 458–1532). This stealthiness advantage is critical in practice: under a perplexity-based defense, GCG's ASR collapses to 0.000 on Llama2, whereas AutoDAN-HGA maintains 0.608. Against handcrafted DAN (walkerspider, 2022), AutoDAN-HGA achieves roughly 250% higher ASR (e.g., 0.608 vs. 0.023 on Llama2) while preserving comparable perplexity, demonstrating that the genetic algorithm successfully automates the prompt crafting process without sacrificing semantic meaningfulness. In cross-model transferability, AutoDAN-HGA transfers at 0.70–0.74 ASR between open-source models, far exceeding GCG's 0.12–0.14, which the authors attribute to AutoDAN's lexical-level optimization avoiding gradient-based overfitting to the source model.

## Relation to Prior Work

AutoDAN builds directly on GCG (Zou et al., 2023), which first formulated jailbreak prompt generation as an adversarial optimization problem using gradient-guided token search, and on the handcrafted DAN prompt series (walkerspider, 2022) that demonstrated stealthy but non-scalable jailbreaks. The key conceptual departure is replacing token-level gradient optimization with a hierarchical genetic algorithm operating at the word and sentence level, motivated by the observation that words in natural-language prompts lack direct gradient correspondence. This positions AutoDAN as a bridge between the two prior paradigms: it inherits GCG's automated, loss-driven optimization framework while recovering the semantic fluency of handcrafted prompts, using LLM-based diversification for both population initialization and mutation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that a simple, widely-deployed perplexity-based defense is ineffective against semantically coherent jailbreak prompts, which has direct implications for how LLM providers should design input filtering. The finding that lexical-level optimization yields better cross-model transferability than token-level gradient optimization offers a useful conceptual insight for adversarial robustness research. However, the method's effectiveness degrades sharply on the most robust models (GPT-4 achieves near-zero ASR, and Llama2 with system prompts resists the attack), and the computational cost (~12 minutes per sample on A100) limits large-scale deployment. Open questions include whether the hierarchical GA can be adapted to black-box settings without white-box log-likelihood access, and whether more sophisticated defenses beyond perplexity can catch semantically meaningful but adversarially structured prompts.
