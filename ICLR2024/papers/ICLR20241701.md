## Problem

Generating complete 360° indoor panoramas from narrow field-of-view images remains challenging because existing GAN-based methods suffer from mode collapse, unstable training, and inability to produce multiple structurally coherent objects in large masked regions. Additionally, panorama-specific constraints—particularly wraparound consistency at the image boundaries and the need to synthesize semantically meaningful objects rather than generic textures—are not addressed by standard diffusion-based inpainting approaches designed for smaller, localized masks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a partially visible RGB panorama (512×1024 equirectangular projection) with an arbitrary mask as input and simultaneously produces a completed RGB panorama and a corresponding depth panorama at the same resolution. Notably, depth is never required at inference time—the fourth latent channel is simply filled with random noise—yet the model was trained jointly on RGB-D data. The pipeline is two-stage: the LDM operates at 256×512, and a super-resolution GAN upscales the result to 512×1024. Because the model is trained unconditionally and the stochastic DDPM sampling is inherent, a single input can yield multiple diverse, plausible completions.

## Method

PanoDiffusion introduces a bi-modal latent diffusion architecture where separate VQ-VAE encoders compress RGB (3 channels) and depth (1 channel) into a concatenated 4-channel latent vector that is jointly denoised by a single UNet under the standard DDPM noise-prediction objective. The key insight is that depth serves purely as a training-time structural guide: at inference, the depth latent channel is replaced by random noise, yet the model still produces high-quality RGB and depth outputs. To enforce wraparound consistency, a progressive camera-rotation mechanism rotates the latent representation and mask by 90° horizontally at each of the 200 denoising steps, so the two ends of the panorama are repeatedly brought into contact during generation. During training, a circular-shift data augmentation (random horizontal rotation) further teaches the network view-invariant scene structure. The model is initialized from the LSUN-Bedrooms pre-trained LDM and trained unconditionally on Structured3D, with a separate super-resolution GAN handling the final 2× upscale.

## Results

On the Structured3D dataset, PanoDiffusion achieves FID 21.55 and sFID 26.95 on camera masks, a 67% relative FID improvement over the best baseline (BIPS, FID 31.70). It consistently leads across all four mask types (camera, NFeV, layout, random box), with the strongest margin on random box masks (FID 16.13 vs. 25.35 for BIPS). For depth synthesis with fully masked input, PanoDiffusion achieves RMSE 276, AbsREL 0.1355, and Delta1.25 0.9060, outperforming NLSPN (RMSE 284) and CSPN (RMSE 374). The model also generalizes zero-shot to the Matterport3D dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PanoDiffusion decisively outperforms all eight baselines across every mask type and metric. Against the strongest panorama-specific baseline BIPS (ECCV 2022), PanoDiffusion reduces FID by 32% on camera masks (21.55 vs. 31.70) and by 36% on random box masks (16.13 vs. 25.35), while also achieving higher density (0.867 vs. 0.769) and coverage (0.708 vs. 0.660). OmniDreamer (CVPR 2022), the other panorama outpainting method, is substantially weaker (FID 65.47 on camera masks), reflecting its transformer-based sampling limitations. General-purpose inpainting methods (LaMa, TFill, RePaint, Inpaint Anything) perform poorly on large panoramic masks, with FIDs ranging from 69–130, confirming that standard inpainting architectures struggle with the scale and semantic demands of 360° outpainting. For depth synthesis, PanoDiffusion beats NLSPN (ECCV 2020) on RMSE (276 vs. 284) and AbsREL (0.1355 vs. 0.1692) with fully masked input, and surpasses CSPN (ECCV 2018) on all four depth metrics. Notably, PanoDiffusion's performance is robust to depth availability at test time (FID 21.55 fully masked vs. 21.90 fully visible), whereas BIPS degrades sharply (FID 68.79 vs. 29.74) when depth is removed.

## Relation to Prior Work

PanoDiffusion builds directly on the Latent Diffusion Model (Rombach et al., 2022) and DDPM (Ho et al., 2020) framework, adapting the latent-space denoising paradigm to the specific geometry of equirectangular 360° panoramas. It departs from prior panorama outpainting methods (BIPS, OmniDreamer) that relied on GANs, and from general diffusion-based inpainting (RePaint, LaMa, TFill) that do not address wraparound consistency or the need to generate multiple semantically coherent objects in large masked regions. The bi-modal latent structure—concatenating RGB and depth VQ latents into a single 4-channel tensor—is a novel architectural choice that differs from both depth-conditioned cross-attention approaches and parallel dual-LDM designs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides a practical pipeline for generating complete indoor RGB-D panoramas from partial views, which is directly useful for AR/VR scene creation, 3D reconstruction, and lighting estimation where full 360° captures are expensive or unavailable. The finding that depth aids training but is dispensable at inference (and that providing it at inference slightly hurts due to SNR imbalance) offers a useful design principle for multi-modal generative models. Limitations include evaluation primarily on indoor Structured3D scenes at 512×1024 resolution, a heuristic 90°-per-step rotation schedule, and 200 denoising steps; open questions include whether the bi-modal approach and rotation mechanism generalize to outdoor panoramas, higher resolutions, or other multi-modal combinations.
