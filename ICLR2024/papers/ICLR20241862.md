## Problem

Predicting a molecule's ground-state 3D conformation from its 2D graph is critical for understanding its properties, yet existing approaches are either computationally prohibitive (DFT, molecular dynamics) or imprecise (force fields). Prior deep learning methods either generate ensembles of conformations requiring additional screening or rely on intermediate variables like distance matrices, making them indirect and suboptimal. This paper seeks an efficient, end-to-end predictive model that maps a 2D molecular graph directly to its single lowest-energy 3D conformation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241862/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 2D molecular graph as input, where each atom is tokenized by the MoleBERT Tokenizer into a chemically context-aware discrete ID (distinguishing, e.g., aromatic vs. aldehyde carbons) rather than a simple atom-type label, and positional information is encoded via Laplacian eigenvectors computed solely from the adjacency matrix. No edge features are used. The output is an n×3 matrix of 3D Cartesian coordinates for the ground-state conformation; training and evaluation use the Mean Absolute Error on interatomic distances to ensure rotational and translational invariance, with C-RMSD (after Kabsch alignment) as the primary conformation-level metric.

## Method

GTMGC is an encoder-decoder Graph Transformer whose core innovation is the Molecule Structural Residual Self-Attention (MSRSA) module, which augments standard dot-product attention scores with two residual bias terms: an adjacency-matrix bias (B^A) capturing 1-hop local structure and a row-subtracted interatomic distance matrix bias (B^D) encoding spatial proximity via a 'distance relevance' assumption. Each bias is computed as a Hadamard product of the attention scores with a learnable per-head scalar times the respective structural matrix, adding minimal parameters while preserving the vanilla Transformer architecture. The encoder (using only B^A) produces a rough conformation estimate G_cache, whose distance matrix D_cache is fed into the decoder (using both B^A and B^D) to refine the final prediction. Training combines MAE losses on both the encoder's and decoder's predicted distance matrices, encouraging the encoder to learn informative representations and provide the decoder with accurate spatial initialization.

## Results

On the Molecule3D benchmark (random split, test set), GTMGC achieves D-MAE 0.433 Å, D-RMSE 0.721 Å, and C-RMSD 0.713 Å, improving 18.34%, 20.86%, and 28.63% over the previous best method (GPS) on the three metrics respectively. On the scaffold split, GTMGC achieves D-MAE 0.400 Å, D-RMSE 0.679 Å, and C-RMSD 0.693 Å, with 21.71%, 20.79%, and 22.83% improvements. On QM9 (test set), GTMGC reaches D-MAE 0.264 Å, D-RMSE 0.470 Å, and C-RMSD 0.367 Å, outperforming all baselines including RDKit ETKDG and GPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GTMGC outperforms all 23 baselines across every metric on both Molecule3D splits and QM9. Against the previous state-of-the-art GPS (Rampašek et al., 2022), GTMGC reduces C-RMSD from 1.038 to 0.713 Å (31.3% improvement) on Molecule3D random split and from 1.136 to 0.693 Å (39.0% improvement) on scaffold split. Compared to the RDKit ETKDG algorithm (the strongest non-learned baseline), GTMGC achieves roughly 28–46% lower C-RMSD depending on dataset and split. Notably, on the scaffold split, DeeperGCN-DAGNN (the prior SOTA) degrades severely (D-MAE 0.763 vs. 0.571 on random split), indicating overfitting, while GTMGC actually improves (D-MAE 0.400 vs. 0.433), demonstrating superior generalization to unseen molecular scaffolds. On QM9, GTMGC's C-RMSD of 0.367 Å is 45.5% lower than RDKit ETKDG (0.689 Å) and 44.9% lower than GPS (0.666 Å), confirming the method's effectiveness on smaller molecules as well.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Graph Transformer (GT) architecture lineage (Dwivedi & Bresson 2020; GPS by Rampašek et al. 2022) and the Molecule3D benchmark (Xu et al. 2021d) that formalized ground-state conformation prediction from 2D graphs. It departs from prior GT-based molecular models (MAT, Geometric Transformer) that either replace the attention mechanism with distance/bond embeddings or apply distance-based gating on softmax outputs; instead, GTMGC retains pure dot-product self-attention and injects structural information (adjacency matrix, row-subtracted distance matrix) as learnable residual bias terms. The input tokenization via MoleBERT (Xia et al. 2023) replaces conventional atom-type IDs, and the encoder-decoder design with a cached distance matrix in the decoder distinguishes it from single-pass prediction approaches like DeeperGCN-DAGNN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GTMGC achieves 18–28% improvements over the previous SOTA on Molecule3D and outperforms RDKit's classical algorithms, demonstrating that a compact 9M-parameter GT model can match or exceed purpose-built conformation generation tools. The MSRSA module's demonstrated transferability to molecular property prediction (HOMO-LUMO gap, QM9 properties) suggests it could become a reusable building block for molecular GT architectures. Key limitations include the decoder's reliance on a rough cached distance matrix rather than ground-truth geometry, the dependency on a separately trained MoleBERT tokenizer, and evaluation limited to molecules up to ~4M in size; whether the approach scales to very large or flexible molecules (e.g., peptides, drug-like compounds with many rotatable bonds) remains open.
