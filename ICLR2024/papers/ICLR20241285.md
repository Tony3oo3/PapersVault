## Problem

Standard knowledge distillation objectives minimize forward KLD, which forces the student to cover all modes of the teacher distribution. For generative LLMs with complex, multi-modal output spaces, this causes low-capacity students to overestimate low-probability regions and produce degenerate or unfaithful text. White-box KD for LLMs (where the teacher's full output distribution is available) had been largely unexplored, with prior work focused on small classification models or black-box API imitation.

## Input / Output

The method takes instruction prompts (text) as input and produces free-running text responses as output, evaluated on instruction-following benchmarks (Dolly, SelfInst, Vicuna, Super-NaturalInstructions, UnnaturalInstructions). Training consumes a dataset of ~12.5K instruction-response pairs (from Dolly-15K) plus a pre-training corpus (OpenWebText or RoBERTa corpus) to preserve general language modeling ability. The teacher model's per-token output distribution over the full vocabulary is required at each generation step, making this a white-box distillation setting with a maximum sequence length of 512 tokens.

## Method

MINILLM replaces the standard forward KLD objective with reverse KLD (KL[q_θ||p]), which induces mode-seeking behavior: the student focuses on the teacher's major modes rather than spreading probability mass over void regions. The reverse KLD is optimized via Policy Gradient, decomposed into a single-step term (computed exactly over the vocabulary to reduce variance) and a long-horizon term. Three stabilization strategies are introduced: teacher-mixed sampling (mixing α=0.2 of the teacher distribution into the student's sampling to prevent reward hacking), length normalization of the accumulated reward to avoid degenerate short outputs, and importance-weighted gradient estimation. Training follows a two-phase pipeline analogous to RLHF: first SFT on the instruction data to initialize, then policy optimization with a combined gradient of the single-step term, the length-normalized long-horizon term, and a pre-training language modeling loss to preserve canonical NLP performance.

## Results

MINILLM achieves the highest Rouge-L scores across all five evaluation sets (Dolly, SelfInst, Vicuna, S-NI, UnNI) for every model family and size tested (GPT-2 120M–760M, OPT 1.3B–6.7B, LLaMA 7B). For the largest student (LLaMA-7B distilled from LLaMA-13B), MINILLM scores 29.0/23.2/20.7/35.5/40.2 on Dolly/SelfInst/Vicuna/S-NI/UnNI, outperforming the best baseline (SeqKD) by 1.5–2.6 points. In several cases the student exceeds the teacher (e.g., LLaMA-7B MINILLM reaches 40.2 on UnNI vs. 38.5 for the 13B teacher), and the improvement is consistent from 120M to 13B parameters, demonstrating strong scalability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MINILLM consistently outperforms all three baselines (SFT w/o KD, word-level KD, and SeqKD) across every model family and evaluation set. Against SeqKD (Kim & Rush 2016; Chiang et al. 2023; Taori et al. 2023), the gap is largest on out-of-distribution sets: on UnNI, LLaMA-7B MINILLM scores 40.2 vs. 37.6 for SeqKD (+2.6), and OPT-6.7B reaches 36.7 vs. 28.2 for SeqKD (+8.5). Against word-level KD (Sanh et al. 2019; Song et al. 2020), MINILLM leads by 1.5–10 points depending on model size and dataset; the advantage is most pronounced for smaller students (e.g., GPT-2-120M on S-NI: 25.3 vs. 19.7 for KD). Against SFT w/o KD, MINILLM gains 1.3–4.8 points, with the largest margins on Vicuna and UnNI where open-ended generation quality matters most. Notably, MINILLM students sometimes surpass their teachers (marked with *), particularly on Vicuna and UnNI, which the authors attribute to reduced exposure bias from the policy-gradient training procedure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the standard knowledge distillation framework (Hinton et al., 2015; Sanh et al., 2019; Kim & Rush, 2016) that minimizes forward KLD between teacher and student distributions, and departs by replacing it with reverse KLD, a mode-seeking objective previously studied in generative modeling (Huszár, 2015; Czarnecki et al., 2019) but not applied to LLM distillation. The optimization leverages the Policy Gradient Theorem (Sutton et al., 1999) with three stabilization strategies—single-step decomposition, teacher-mixed sampling, and length normalization—drawing on ideas from imitation learning and RLHF (Ouyang et al., 2022). The paper also provides an alternative derivation via maximum-entropy Inverse Reinforcement Learning (Ziebart et al., 2008), connecting KD to reward-based policy optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MINILLM provides a practical, well-motivated recipe for compressing LLMs from 13B to 120M parameters while consistently outperforming standard KD baselines across three model families (GPT-2, OPT, LLaMA) and five evaluation datasets, making it directly applicable to resource-constrained deployment. The finding that the distilled student can exceed the teacher on Rouge-L (attributed to reduced exposure bias from policy-based training) and that reverse KLD yields better calibration than forward KLD distillation are practically useful insights. Open questions include whether the diversity loss inherent to mode-seeking objectives becomes problematic for creative or open-ended generation tasks, and whether the approach extends to multi-turn dialogue or non-instruction settings.
