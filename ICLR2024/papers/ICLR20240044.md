## Problem

Optimal control of robotic systems with hybrid action spaces (mixing continuous and discrete variables) remains unsolved by existing Approximate Dynamic Programming methods, which either require differentiable dynamics or cannot handle discrete actions. Deep RL approaches for hybrid actions are data-inefficient and produce policies with poor generalization, particularly as the number of actuators grows. The paper addresses the absence of a model-based ADP algorithm that can efficiently handle arbitrary hybrid action spaces without differentiability assumptions on the system model.

## Input / Output

The method requires a deterministic (or stochastic) dynamics model f(s,a) and reward function r(s,a) as black-box evaluators—no differentiability is assumed—and operates on state vectors that may mix continuous and discrete variables. It produces an optimal policy π*(s) mapping states to hybrid actions, along with the state-value and advantage functions stored in compact TT format. The dynamics simulator should ideally support batch evaluation of state-action pairs for parallel computation, and continuous variables are discretized (100 points in experiments) for the TT representation.

## Method

TTPI (Generalized Policy Iteration using Tensor Train) represents the state-value and advantage functions in Tensor Train format, exploiting the observation that these functions exhibit low-rank structure even when the underlying dynamics and reward are non-smooth or discontinuous. The value function is updated via TT-Cross, a gradient-free, nonparametric algorithm that approximates the Bellman operator output by querying only O(ndr²) function evaluations rather than the full tensor. Policy retrieval is performed by TTGO, which transforms the advantage function into a nonnegative TT distribution and deterministically extracts the argmax over hybrid (continuous + discrete) action variables via prioritized top-N sampling along each mode. The key architectural choice is explicitly modeling the advantage function in TT format, enabling batch computation of argmax A(s,a) across many states simultaneously with cost linear in the number of action dimensions, thereby eliminating the policy-retrieval bottleneck that limits prior ADP methods for hybrid control.

## Results

On the Catch-Point (CP) benchmark, TTPI achieves a perfect trajectory quality score (μ=1) and 100% success rate in just 30 seconds of training, compared to HyAR's 0.15±0.02 μ and 92%±4% success rate requiring 4 hours. On the Hard-Move (HM) benchmarks with 16, 24, and 32 action variables, TTPI achieves 100% success rate in 850s, 946s, and 1743s respectively, while HyAR drops to 12%±5% success at HM(12) and 0% at HM(16), and HPPO/PDQN fail entirely (NA) beyond HM(8). In the real-world Pusher-Slider experiment, TTPI achieves 100% success with position errors under 5 cm and orientation errors under 4° even under external disturbances and additional weight.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TTPI decisively outperforms all three Deep RL baselines (HyAR, HPPO, PDQN) on both training time and policy quality. On the CP task, TTPI trains in 30 seconds versus 0.5–4 hours for baselines, while achieving a perfect μ=1 trajectory score compared to 0.13–0.16 for the baselines. On HM(8), TTPI matches HyAR's success rate (100%) but trains ~5× faster (850s vs 8h) and slightly exceeds its μ (0.93 vs 0.92); HPPO and PDQN collapse to 8% success. The gap widens dramatically at higher action dimensions: at HM(12), HyAR drops to 12%±5% success while TTPI maintains 100%, and at HM(16) HyAR fails entirely (0%) whereas TTPI still achieves 100% success. HPPO and PDQN cannot even complete training beyond HM(8). The key advantage stems from TTPI's model-based approach: TT-Cross exploits the low-rank structure of value functions to approximate them accurately with far fewer evaluations, and TTGO provides efficient global policy retrieval over hybrid action spaces without requiring differentiability.

## Relation to Prior Work

TTPI extends the line of TT-based ADP work (Gorodetsky et al., 2015; Boyko et al., 2021) by removing the differentiability and continuous-action assumptions that limited those methods, using TT-Cross for nonparametric function approximation and TTGO for policy retrieval over hybrid action spaces. It departs from Deep RL approaches for hybrid actions (HyAR, HPPO, PDQN) by exploiting the system model directly rather than learning from data, trading model-free generality for dramatically faster convergence. The algorithm is a novel instantiation of Generalized Policy Iteration where both the value and advantage functions are maintained in TT format, enabling algebraic operations and TTGO-based optimization that naturally accommodate mixed continuous-discrete variables without architectural modifications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TTPI fills a concrete gap: no prior ADP method could handle hybrid action spaces, and existing Deep RL baselines struggle with generalization and training efficiency on these problems. The real-world pusher-slider experiment (100% success under disturbance and added weight) demonstrates practical viability for underactuated manipulation tasks that are notoriously difficult for hybrid control. Key limitations remain: the method requires access to a (preferably parallelizable) dynamics model, may not scale to very high-dimensional state spaces like images, and the stochastic extension is acknowledged as an open problem. Future work on formulating the approach as RL with gradient-based TT learning could broaden applicability to model-free and high-dimensional settings.
