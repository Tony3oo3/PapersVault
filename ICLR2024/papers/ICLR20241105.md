## Problem

Solving inverse problems with pretrained diffusion priors requires sampling from an intractable posterior, and existing score-modification methods (DPS, ΠGDM) rely on a crude unimodal Gaussian approximation of the conditional score that is loose at many diffusion steps. This paper addresses the gap by deriving a principled variational objective that avoids posterior score approximation entirely, while also establishing a connection to regularization-by-denoising that makes sampling interpretable and tunable.

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded measurement y (e.g., masked image, blurred image, Fourier magnitude), a known forward operator f (linear or nonlinear), and a pretrained unconditional guided diffusion score network on 256×256 ImageNet. The optimizer is initialized with the degraded input image, and the output is a single restored image per run. No task-specific retraining is required; the same pretrained prior handles inpainting, super-resolution, deblurring, phase retrieval, HDR, and MRI reconstruction by simply changing f and the measurement noise model.

## Method

RED-diff formulates inverse problem sampling as KL minimization between a Gaussian variational distribution q(x₀|y) and the true posterior, which decomposes into a measurement-matching loss plus a score-matching regularization over the entire forward diffusion trajectory. A key technical result (Proposition 2) shows that with an SNR-based weighting (λ_t = λ/SNR_t) and zero dispersion, the regularization gradient reduces to a simple stopped-gradient expression λ_t(ε_θ(x_t;t) − ε) that requires no backpropagation through the score network, making each iterate a lightweight forward pass. Sampling is then performed via stochastic optimization (e.g., Adam) over the estimate μ, with timesteps traversed in descending order so that high-level semantics are established before fine details. The SNR weighting rescales the noise-prediction residual into a signal-domain form, balancing bias (fit to observations) against variance (fit to prior) through a single hyperparameter λ.

## Results

On ImageNet inpainting (Table 1), RED-diff achieves 23.29 dB PSNR, 0.87 SSIM, 0.86 KID, 0.10 LPIPS, and 72.0% top-1 accuracy, outperforming all baselines by significant margins (e.g., KID 0.86 vs. 2.5 for DDRM and 15.28 for DPS). RED-diff is also the fastest method at 0.05 s/step with a max batch size of 30, compared to 0.1–0.24 s/step and batch sizes of 15–25 for alternatives. On nonlinear tasks (Table 2), RED-diff achieves 45.0 dB PSNR on deblurring vs. 6.4 dB for DPS, and 25.23 dB on HDR vs. 7.94 dB, demonstrating large gains where score-modification methods struggle.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RED-diff consistently outperforms DPS (Chung et al., 2022a) across all linear and nonlinear tasks: on inpainting it gains +2.0 dB PSNR and reduces KID from 15.28 to 0.86; on deblurring it achieves 45.0 dB PSNR vs. DPS's 6.4 dB, and on HDR 25.23 dB vs. 7.94 dB. Against ΠGDM (Song et al., 2023), RED-diff wins on every metric in Table 1 (e.g., KID 0.86 vs. 4.50, PSNR 23.29 vs. 20.30) and is ~5× faster per step (0.05 s vs. 0.24 s) because it avoids score-network inversion. DDRM (Kawar et al., 2022a) is the closest competitor on KID (2.5 vs. 0.86) but still trails RED-diff by 2.6 dB in PSNR and requires SVD computations that limit its batch size. For compressed-sensing MRI (Table 4), RED-diff beats CSGM-Langevin (Jalal et al., 2021) by 0.8–1.8 dB PSNR at all undersampling rates while running 3× faster (0.114 vs. 0.344 s/iter). In the plug-and-play comparison (Table 7), RED-diff surpasses DPIR (Zhang et al., 2021) by ~10 dB PSNR and 35 KID points, and edges out DiffPIR (Zhu et al., 2023) on KID (7.92 vs. 7.96), LPIPS (0.106 vs. 0.140), and top-1 (90.0 vs. 70.0), though DiffPIR achieves slightly higher PSNR (27.29 vs. 26.06) on inpainting.

## Relation to Prior Work

RED-diff departs from the score-modification paradigm used by DPS and ΠGDM, which approximate the intractable conditional score via a Gaussian around the MMSE estimate, by instead formulating inverse problem sampling as KL minimization over a variational distribution. This variational reformulation, grounded in the score-matching equivalence from Song et al. (2021a), naturally yields a per-timestep regularization structure that the authors connect to the RED framework (Romano et al., 2016), though with the key difference of using the full diffusion trajectory rather than a single denoiser. The method thus bridges two previously separate lines of work—diffusion-based inverse problem solvers and regularization-by-denoising—under a unified variational/optimization lens, while building on the VP-SDE formulation and pretrained score networks from the DDPM/score-SDE literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By recasting sampling as stochastic optimization, RED-diff enables the use of off-the-shelf optimizers (Adam, SGD) with lightweight iterates that avoid backpropagating through the score network, yielding significant memory and speed advantages over DPS and ΠGDM. The SNR-based weighting mechanism provides an interpretable knob for the fidelity-perceptual-quality trade-off, and the descending timestep strategy offers a practical recipe for structured generation. Key limitations include the mode-seeking nature of the Gaussian variational approximation (limited diversity), evaluation restricted to 256×256 ImageNet with a single pretrained unconditional model, and the open question of whether more expressive variational families (multimodal, normalizing-flow-based) or 3D generation tasks can further benefit from this framework.
