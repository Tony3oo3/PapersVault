## Problem

In continual finite-sum minimization, a model must maintain ε-accuracy on the prefix-sum objective at every stage as new data arrives, but existing approaches are prohibitively expensive: SGD requires O(n/ε) first-order oracles overall, while variance-reduction methods (SVRG, Katyusha) require O(n²log(1/ε)). The paper asks whether a first-order method can achieve subquadratic dependence on n while retaining strong accuracy guarantees, and establishes that no natural first-order method can beat O(n²log(1/ε)) FOs.

## Input / Output

The method receives a sequence of n strongly convex, L-smooth, G-Lipschitz functions f_1,…,f_n revealed one at a time, along with an accuracy target ε. At each stage i it must output a point x̂_i ∈ D that is ε-optimal for the prefix-sum g_i(x) = (1/i)Σ_{j=1}^i f_j(x), meaning E[g_i(x̂_i)] − g_i(x_i⋆) ≤ ε. The constraint is that each x̂_i must be produced using only first-order oracle queries (individual gradient evaluations ∇f_j(x)) on functions with index ≤ i, and the total number of such queries across all n stages is the primary complexity metric.

## Method

CSVRG (Algorithm 1) combines sparse full-gradient recomputation with a novel 3-FO stochastic gradient estimator. A full gradient ∇g_i(x̂_i) is computed only at a geometrically spaced subsequence of stages (controlled by parameter α), requiring ⌈log n/α⌉ such expensive steps; between them, a stale gradient ∇̃_i is updated with a single FO via ∇̃_i = (1−1/i)∇̃_{i−1} + (1/i)∇f_i(x̂_prev). At each stage, an inner subroutine (FUM, Algorithm 2) performs T_i projected gradient steps using the estimator ∇_i^t = (1−1/i)(∇f_{u_t}(x_i^t) − ∇f_{u_t}(x̂_prev) + ∇̃_{i−1}) + (1/i)∇f_i(x_i^t), where u_t is sampled uniformly from [i−1]. The critical design choice is subtracting ∇f_{u_t}(x̂_prev) evaluated at the stale point, which makes the estimator unbiased while bounding its variance by O(L²‖x_i^t − x_i⋆‖² + L²G²α²/μ² + L²ε/μ). With T_i = O(GL²/(μ^{5/2}i√ε) + L^{2/3}G^{2/3}/(ε^{1/3}μ) + L²/μ²) and α = με^{1/3}/(20G^{2/3}L^{2/3}), the overall complexity is Õ(n/ε^{1/3} + log n/√ε) FOs, and a matching lower bound of Ω(n/ε^{1/4}) is proven for all natural first-order methods.

## Results

CSVRG achieves Õ(n/ε^{1/3} + log n/√ε) overall first-order oracle (FO) complexity for continual finite-sum minimization under strong convexity, significantly improving upon SGD's O(n/ε) and variance-reduction methods' O(n²log(1/ε)). In the practically important regime ε = O(1/n), CSVRG requires only Õ(n^{4/3}) FOs versus O(n²) for all prior methods. The paper also establishes a lower bound of Ω(n/ε^{1/4}) for natural first-order methods, showing CSVRG's ε-dependence is near-tight. Experimentally on ridge regression (LIBSVM), CSVRG matches Katyusha/SVRG suboptimality while using only ~4% of their FOs, and substantially outperforms SGD/SGD-sparse at the same FO budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SGD, CSVRG improves the ε-dependence from O(n/ε) to Õ(n/ε^{1/3}), a substantial gain for small ε. Against SVRG, SAGA, SARAH, and Katyusha, CSVRG reduces the n-dependence from quadratic O(n²log(1/ε)) to near-linear Õ(n log n/ε^{1/3}), which is the paper's headline contribution. In the ε = O(1/n) regime (motivated by statistical error matching), CSVRG needs Õ(n^{4/3}) FOs while all prior methods require O(n²). Experimentally on ridge regression with LIBSVM data, CSVRG achieves a suboptimality gap comparable to Katyusha and SVRG while performing only ~4% of their total FOs, and it significantly outperforms SGD and SGD-sparse at the same FO budget. The paper further proves that no natural first-order method can achieve O(n^{2−α}log(1/ε)) for any α > 0, confirming that the quadratic n-dependence of VR methods is inherent to that class.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces continual finite-sum minimization as a formal optimization problem, building directly on the variance reduction framework of SVRG (Johnson & Zhang, 2013) and the convergence analysis machinery of Katyusha (Allen-Zhu, 2017/2018). The key departure is that rather than solving a single finite-sum problem, the method must produce ε-optimal solutions for every prefix-sum stage, which the authors show cannot be handled efficiently by naively applying existing VR methods at each stage (yielding O(n²log(1/ε)) FOs). CSVRG's design—sparsely computing full gradients and using a novel three-FO gradient estimator that subtracts a stale gradient—adapts SVRG's control-variate idea to the streaming prefix setting while borrowing Katyusha's telescoping convergence analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result is most impactful in the ε = O(1/n) accuracy regime, which matches the unavoidable statistical error of empirical risk minimization in the strongly convex case; here CSVRG requires Õ(n^(4/3)) FOs versus O(n²) for all prior methods. The lower bound proving that no natural first-order method can achieve O(n^(2-α)log(1/ε)) FOs establishes that the quadratic n-dependence of standard VR methods is fundamental, not an artifact of suboptimal design. Key open questions include bridging the gap between the O(n^(4/3)) upper bound and the Ω(n^(5/4)) lower bound at ε = 1/n, and extending the analysis beyond strong convexity, which the authors explicitly flag as a limitation.
