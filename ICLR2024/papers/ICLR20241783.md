## Problem

Learned lossless compression methods face a fundamental trade-off: autoregressive and continuous-latent models achieve superior compression ratios but incur high sequential computation that makes them impractical on CPUs, while fast discrete-deterministic autoencoders (e.g., VQ-VAE with tANS) suffer from limited representation power and difficult optimization of the discrete latent space. No existing framework simultaneously delivers a competitive compression ratio and the low per-symbol time complexity required for general-purpose computation devices.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images at various resolutions (32×32 for CIFAR-10/ImageNet32, 64×64 for ImageNet64, up to 2048×1370 for CLIC2020) and produces a single lossless bitstream. The backbone is deliberately shallow (one 2× downsampling/upsampling convolution plus one residual block with 64 latent channels), quantizing the latent into 2 channels of 256 codewords each (32-dim codewords), so a 32×32 image maps to a 2×16×16 discrete latent grid. All timing benchmarks are measured on a CPU (Intel i7-6800K), reflecting the target deployment on general-purpose hardware rather than GPUs.

## Method

The framework combines two key innovations. First, the Finite-State AutoRegressive (FSAR) entropy coder replaces expensive masked-convolution context models with a low-order (typically N≤2) finite-state Markov prior over the discrete latent space, implemented entirely as an N-dimensional lookup table that is generated post-training by enumerating all state combinations through a small MLP; at decode time this adds only 1–2 extra memory reads to the tANS state transition, keeping per-symbol cost comparable to plain tANS while capturing spatial dependencies. A Learnable State Number (LSN) module uses α-entmax to sparsify the state space end-to-end, halving lookup-table memory without degrading BPD. Second, Straight-Through Hardmax Quantization (STHQ) performs hardmax (nearest-codeword) selection in the forward pass but backpropagates through a Gumbel-softmax reparameterization, enabling global codebook updates via a simple L2 regularization on the minimum distance while avoiding the entropy gap and codebook divergence that plague relaxed-VQ objectives. The full system—backbone, codebook, and FSAR prior—is trained end-to-end with a single loss combining the L2 term, the data likelihood, and the FSAR latent prior.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the proposed FSAR(O2) achieves the best practical BPD of 5.06, outperforming the non-autoregressive tANS baseline (5.25) by 3.6% and the VQ-VAE baseline (5.52) by 8.3%. Decompression time is 2.65 ms per image, only 0.13 ms slower than tANS (2.52 ms) but ~239× faster than MaskConv2D (633.99 ms). On ImageNet32 and ImageNet64, FSAR(O2) achieves 5.46 and 5.07 practical BPD respectively, maintaining the same speed advantage. When combined with PILC for full image compression, PILC+STHQ+FSAR reaches 4.18 BPD on CIFAR-10, a 2.3% improvement over PILC alone (4.28), while matching the BPD of traditional WebP (4.52) and FLIF (4.29) at comparable speeds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the non-autoregressive tANS baseline, FSAR(O2) improves practical BPD by 3.6% on CIFAR-10 (5.06 vs 5.25) at only a 5% speed cost (2.65 ms vs 2.52 ms decompression). Compared to VQ-VAE, FSAR(O2) reduces BPD by 8.3% (5.06 vs 5.52) with comparable speed. Against autoregressive baselines, FSAR(O2) achieves similar theoretical BPD to MaskConv2D (5.05 vs 5.02) but is ~239× faster in total decompression (2.65 ms vs 633.99 ms), and outperforms Checkerboard on both BPD (5.06 vs 5.11) and speed (2.65 ms vs 6.34 ms). In the full codec comparison (Table 6), PILC+STHQ+FSAR beats PILC by 2.3% BPD on CIFAR-10 (4.18 vs 4.28) and outperforms L3C (4.77) and PNG (5.78) on all datasets, while matching WebP and FLIF in BPD at similar speeds. However, the method loses to bitswap (3.84 BPD) and IDF/IDF++ (3.26–3.34 BPD) on compression ratio, though it is orders of magnitude faster (2.38 MB/s vs 0.003–0.010 MB/s). On high-resolution Kodak and CLIC20 benchmarks, PILC+STHQ+FSAR (3.66/3.51 BPD) is slightly worse than WebP (3.20/3.08) and FLIF (2.88/2.82), indicating the shallow backbone limits performance on large images.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the autoencoder-based learned lossless compression framework by replacing the heavy autoregressive context models (MaskConv, Checkerboard) with a lookup-table-based finite-state Markov model (FSAR) that integrates seamlessly with tANS, adding only a few memory operations per symbol. The quantization scheme (STHQ) departs from VQ-VAE's K-means-like loss and RVQ's entropy-based ELBO by combining a hardmax forward pass with Gumbel-softmax backward gradients, enabling global codebook updates without the entropy gap that degrades RVQ/SQ-VAE. Conceptually, the work bridges the efficiency of discrete deterministic latent spaces (fast tANS decoding) with the compression quality of autoregressive latent spaces, achieving both simultaneously through the compact Markov prior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in making autoregressive latent coding feasible on CPUs: FSAR achieves comparable BPD to MaskConv-based methods while being ~300× faster, and the 6% compression gain over non-autoregressive baselines comes at negligible speed cost. However, the lookup table memory (O(C^N)) remains a practical bottleneck even with LSN, and the shallow backbone limits performance on high-resolution images where flow models (IDF++) still dominate in BPD. Open questions include whether FSAR can be extended to higher-order or non-spatial dependencies without prohibitive memory, and whether the STHQ optimization advantage generalizes to other discrete latent variable architectures beyond VQ.
