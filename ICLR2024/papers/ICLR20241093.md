## Problem

Existing large-scale image representation learning either depends on expensive human-labeled classification datasets (ImageNet, JFT) or uses noisy web image-text pairs with contrastive objectives (CLIP, ALIGN), which cannot exploit the classification loss because each image has varying free-form text. The paper asks whether structured entity labels can be automatically extracted from noisy alt-text at web scale to enable supervised pre-training over millions of fine-grained classes, and whether combining this with contrastive learning yields stronger image representations.

## Input / Output

The model takes 224×224 RGB images and text as input; for the contrastive branch, text is formatted as 'entity name, entity description' (sourced from Wikidata) and tokenized with the OPT tokenizer (vocab 50,265, max length 76). The image encoder produces a fixed-dimension embedding (512-d for B/16 and L/14, 1024-d for H/14), while the supervised branch predicts over 2.1M entity classes using a sampled softmax with 512k candidate entities per batch. The multi-task model simultaneously outputs both the image embedding (for retrieval and zero-shot classification) and entity class scores.

## Method

MOFI's core contribution is a data pipeline that converts 8.4B noisy web image-text pairs into the I2E dataset (1.1B images, 2.1M entities) via three steps: (1) named entity recognition and linking using hyperbolic graph embeddings trained on Wikipedia's link graph to disambiguate entities, (2) CLIP-based filtering to remove entities irrelevant to the paired image, and (3) sampling to balance the highly skewed entity frequency distribution. Training uses a ViT backbone (B/16, L/14, or H/14) with a separate text encoder, optimized with a combined loss: a large-margin cosine classification loss over 2M entities (with sampled softmax, margin 0.15, temperature 1/32) plus a CLIP-style symmetric contrastive loss, balanced with λ=0.5. The key insight is that treating extracted entities as discrete classification labels—rather than free-form text—enables supervised pre-training at a scale (2M classes) far exceeding prior datasets, and that multi-task training combining both objectives outperforms either alone, achieving 86.66% mAP on GPR1200 versus CLIP's 72.19%.

## Results

MOFI-H/14 achieves 86.67% mAP@all on GPR1200, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art of 72.19% from CLIP-L/14 (OpenAI) by 14.48 points. On ImageNet zero-shot classification, MOFI-H/14 reaches 78.46% top-1 accuracy versus 75.27% for CLIP-L/14. For linear probe on ImageNet, MOFI-B/16 achieves 81.32% top-1 accuracy, outperforming CLIP-B/16 (I2T) by 1.78 points. On the ImageNet-1K image retrieval task, MOFI-B/16 improves Acc@1 by 10.84 points over CLIP-B/16 trained on the original I2T data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLIP (Radford et al., 2021), MOFI achieves a decisive win on GPR1200: MOFI-H/14 scores 86.67% mAP versus 72.19% for CLIP-L/14, a 14.48-point gap, and also leads on ImageNet zero-shot (78.46% vs. 75.27%). Against DINOv2 (Oquab et al., 2023), MOFI wins substantially on GPR1200 (86.67% vs. 61.23% for DINOv2-L/14) but loses on ImageNet-1K retrieval (77.60% vs. 78.04% Acc@1), which the authors attribute to DINOv2's curated in-domain training data. Against the supervised baselines from the GPR1200 paper (Schall et al., 2021), MOFI-B/16 outperforms Swin-B by 17.13 points on GPR1200 All (83.33% vs. 66.20%). Against the specialized retrieval models DOLG (Yang et al., 2021) and MSTFC (Mari et al., 2022) on R-Oxford and R-Paris, MOFI-B/16 with GLDv2 fine-tuning (71.05/46.24 on R-Oxford Med/Hard) remains below DOLG (80.5/58.8) and MSTFC (80.3/60.3), confirming that task-specific architectures still hold an edge on narrow-domain retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOFI builds directly on CLIP's contrastive image-text pre-training framework (ViT architecture, shared embedding space, contrastive loss) but departs from it in two fundamental ways: it replaces raw noisy alt-text with structured entity labels extracted via NER and entity linking, and it introduces a supervised classification objective (large-margin cosine loss with sampled softmax) alongside the contrastive loss. The dataset construction pipeline is conceptually related to JFT and IG-3.6B in using semi-automatic labeling at scale, but achieves 66× more classes by leveraging Wikidata's entity space rather than a fixed taxonomy. The multi-task combination of supervised and contrastive objectives echoes CoCa's addition of a captioning head to CLIP, but here the second objective is a 2M-way classification rather than generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The I2E dataset (1.1B images, 2.1M entities) is a major new resource that demonstrates noisy web-scale image-text pairs can be converted into structured supervision with a simple, cost-effective pipeline (NER + entity linking + CLIP filtering), opening a path to scale classification-style pre-training far beyond what human labeling allows. The 14.5-point mAP gain over CLIP on GPR1200 and the 4.3-point zero-shot gain on ImageNet with a smaller training set suggest that label structure matters more than raw data volume for retrieval. A notable limitation is the SOP domain drop, where fine-grained product entities are hard to extract from short commercial text, pointing to an open question about how to handle long-tail and domain-specific entity extraction. The approach also implicitly validates entity mining as a data-cleaning step for contrastive training, aligning with recent findings that data quality improvements can rival or exceed architectural changes.
