## Problem

Contrastive learning shows limited improvement for ordinal regression because predefined strong augmentations (color jittering, Gaussian blurring) easily distort or erase the subtle ordinal content information—such as wrinkles for age estimation or microaneurysms for diabetic retinopathy grading—that is essential for predicting ordered categories. As a result, features extracted by contrastive learning between weakly and strongly augmented views lack the discriminative ordinal content needed for effective ordinal regression.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (resized to 256×256, center-cropped to 224×224) and their ordinal labels as input. The StyleGAN2-based generative model produces content-preserving augmented images that vary in style but retain ordinal-specific features; for class-imbalanced datasets like diabetic retinopathy, the number of augmentations is dynamically adjusted per severity level (e.g., [1,3,2,5,5]). These augmented images are fed alongside originals into a VGG16-based ordinal regression network, whose output is an ordinal prediction regularized by a supervised contrastive loss.

## Method

The core contribution is a generative augmentation framework that replaces predefined strong augmentations with a GAN-based approach grounded in the minimal change principle. A sparse mask M (regularized via L1 loss) partitions latent factors into ordinal content factors z_o and non-ordinal style factors z_n, where the mask limits how many factors are influenced by the ordinal label y through a Deep Sigmoidal Flow transformation (z_on = z + M ⊙ f_y(z)). During augmentation, z_o is held fixed to preserve ordinal content while z_n is randomly sampled to introduce style diversity, generating instances that belong to a specific ordinal category but exhibit varied appearances. These generated instances serve as strong augmented views in a supervised contrastive loss, which is integrated as a plug-and-play regularization term into existing ordinal regression methods. The generative model is trained with a combined GAN loss and sparsity loss, while the downstream ordinal regression model is trained with its original loss plus the contrastive loss at a small coefficient (1e-4 to 1e-5).

## Results

On the Adience age estimation dataset, OCP-CL improves POE from 60.5% to 63.7% accuracy (+3.2%) and reduces MAE from 0.47 to 0.43. On Diabetic Retinopathy, MWR achieves the best result at 75.1% accuracy with 0.37 MAE after adding OCP-CL. On SkyFinder weather prediction, POE gains the largest improvement, reaching 64.1% accuracy (+2.2%) and 0.42 MAE. In the linear evaluation (Table 2), OCP-CL outperforms all four contrastive learning baselines (SupMoCo, SupCon, S-LooC, SupCReg) across all three datasets, e.g., 65.2% vs. 63.6% on DR and 57.5% vs. 56.1% on SkyFinder.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OCP-CL is a plug-and-play augmentation module that consistently improves all five ordinal regression baselines (OR-CNN, CNNPOR, SORD, POE, MWR) across all three datasets, with the largest gains on POE (up to +3.2% accuracy on Adience) and the smallest on MWR (which already models ordinal relationships via reference images, leaving less room for improvement). In the linear evaluation against four contrastive learning frameworks, OCP-CL wins on every dataset: on Diabetic Retinopathy it reaches 65.2% accuracy vs. SupMoCo (63.6%), S-LooC (63.0%), SupCReg (62.7%), and SupCon (60.5%); on Adience it achieves 53.1% vs. S-LooC (52.4%), SupMoCo (51.9%), SupCon (51.7%), and SupCReg (51.2%); on SkyFinder it scores 57.5% vs. SupMoCo (56.1%), S-LooC (55.6%), SupCReg (55.1%), and SupCon (53.9%). The gap over SupCon is particularly large (up to 4.3% on DR), confirming that standard supervised contrastive learning with predefined augmentations is insufficient for ordinal tasks. The method also outperforms S-LooC, which was specifically designed to handle augmentation invariance violations, by 1.6–2.4% accuracy, suggesting that generative content-preserving augmentation is more effective than per-augmentation head decomposition for ordinal data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of contrastive learning, ordinal regression, and disentangled generative modeling. It builds on supervised contrastive learning (Khosla et al., 2020) for the loss formulation, the minimal change principle from causal disentanglement literature (Xie et al., 2022; Kong et al., 2022) as the core constraint for separating ordinal content from style factors, and Deep Sigmoidal Flows (Huang et al., 2018) as the label-conditioned transformation in the generative model. The key departure from prior work is replacing predefined strong augmentations (color jitter, Gaussian blur) with a GAN-based generative augmentation that provably preserves ordinal content via the minimal change constraint, positioning the method as a plug-and-play module orthogonal to existing ordinal regression architectures (OR-CNN, CNNPOR, SORD, POE, MWR).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and previously underappreciated failure mode: standard contrastive learning augmentations silently destroy the subtle ordinal content (wrinkles, microaneurysms, cloud patterns) that ordinal regression depends on, making the contrastive signal uninformative for these tasks. The plug-and-play design means practitioners can adopt the method without modifying their ordinal regression backbone, and the consistent 1–5% gains across three diverse datasets and five baselines demonstrate broad applicability. Open questions include whether the GAN-based generator (StyleGAN2) can be replaced by more stable or higher-fidelity models (e.g., diffusion models), whether the approach extends to non-image modalities (text, audio ordinal tasks), and how the method scales to very large ordinal spaces where the sparsity constraint on ordinal factors may become insufficient.
