## Problem

Existing room rearrangement methods either assume complete bird's-eye visibility or rely on greedy, sub-optimal planners that fail to minimize agent traversal, handle blocked-goal and swap cases, or scale beyond a handful of objects. No prior work addresses the full task-planning problem from an egocentric view under partial observability, where the agent must simultaneously search for unseen objects (outside the field of view or inside closed receptacles) and rearrange visible ones while optimizing both the number of steps and overall traversal.

## Input / Output

The agent receives RGB-D egocentric images in the untidy current state, a pre-captured goal state (object/receptacle semantic labels with 3D centroids in a fixed global frame), a 2D occupancy map for navigation, and a 3D scene map for centroid augmentation. Only objects within the current egocentric view are visible; unseen objects are identified by set-difference between goal and current object lists. The output is a sequence of hybrid actions, each a discrete index (selecting an object to move or a receptacle to search) paired with a continuous parameter specifying the placement or search location, executed via Dijkstra path planning on the 2D map.

## Method

The planner is a modular pipeline with three components. First, a Search Network fine-tunes RoBERTa-Large embeddings on object-room-receptacle pairs, using a two-stage Sorting Network (3-class cross-entropy) to filter implausible receptacles and a Scoring Network (MSE) to rank the remaining candidates, predicting the most probable location for each unseen object. Second, a Graph-based State Representation builds fully connected spatial graphs for current and goal states with Dijkstra path-length edge features, encoded by a Siamese Graph Convolution Network and decoded by a Residual Geodesic Distance Network to produce compact, scene-invariant embeddings that scale to 20+ objects. Third, a Parameterized Deep-Q Network with hybrid action space plans the interleaved search-and-rearrangement sequence, trained with a novel Cluster-Biased Return Decomposition (CB-RD) proxy reward that k-means clusters per-step rewards into three groups for sample-efficient episodic reward estimation, outperforming uniform random sampling (RRD) and hierarchical dense rewards in both convergence speed and final performance.

## Results

On the RoPOR benchmark (up to 20 objects, partial observability, swap cases), the proposed method achieves SNS of 0.73–0.95 and ATC of 11.98–48.32 m across configurations, significantly outperforming Weihs et al. (SNS 0.002–0.058, ATC 16.18–36.69 m), Gadre et al. (SNS 0–0.008, ATC 18.11–37.01 m), and Sarch et al. (SNS 0–0.046, ATC 16.18–35.58 m). Under ground-truth perception (Ours-GT), the method achieves SNS of 0.95–0.98 and ATC of 10.57–45.32 m, outperforming Ghosh et al. (SNS 0.62–0.92, ATC 13.58–52.56 m) on both metrics. The method is the only one that simultaneously handles complete visibility, both types of partial observability (OOF and OPR), and swap cases without an explicit buffer at scale (20 objects).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Weihs et al. (CVPR 2021), Gadre et al. (CVPR 2022), and Sarch et al. (ECCV 2022), the proposed method achieves 10–40× higher SNS and 20–60% lower ATC under complete visibility (e.g., 5 objects: Ours SNS 0.74 vs. Weihs 0.018, Gadre 0.024, Sarch 0.058). All three baselines completely fail (SNS = 0) on swap cases without explicit buffer and on objects placed inside closed receptacles (OPR), while the proposed method handles both. Under partial observability (OOF), the proposed method's Search Network achieves ENR of 0.47–0.75, far exceeding the implicit exploration of the baselines which incur 30–50% higher ATC. Against Ghosh et al. (IJCNN 2022), which assumes complete visibility and ground-truth positions, Ours-GT achieves higher SNS (0.95–0.98 vs. 0.88–0.92) and lower ATC (10.57–45.32 m vs. 13.58–52.56 m) at all object counts, with the gap widening as object count increases. Ghosh et al. fails entirely on any partial observability scenario (SNS = 0 for unseen objects), whereas the proposed method maintains consistent performance across all observability conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Deep RL-based rearrangement planner of Ghosh et al. (2022) from a complete-visibility, ground-truth-position setting to a practical partial-observability, egocentric-view formulation, adding an LLM-based Search Network (fine-tuned RoBERTa embeddings) to predict receptacles for unseen objects and a graph-based state representation to make the planner scalable. It also modifies the proxy reward training of Ren et al. (2022) by replacing uniform random step sampling with cluster-biased sampling (CB-RD) for improved sample efficiency. Conceptually, the paper departs from the perception-and-commonsense-reasoning line (Weihs et al. 2021, Gadre et al. 2022, Sarch et al. 2022) by treating rearrangement as a joint search-and-planning optimization problem rather than a sequential detect-then-move pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RoPOR benchmark addresses a real gap in the field: prior benchmarks (RoomR) cap at 5 objects and exclude swap cases, partial observability, and objects inside receptacles, making them inadequate for evaluating planning quality. The modular architecture (Search Network + Graph Siamese Encoder + Parameterized DQN with proxy reward) is a practical template for embodied rearrangement that transfers across simulators (Ai2Thor → Habitat) without retraining. Key open questions include handling occluded objects (a stated limitation), removing the assumption of perfect motion/manipulation, and validating the approach in real-world settings where perception errors compound over long horizons.
