## Problem

Diffusion models are trained as unconstrained neural networks approximating the score function, yet the true score is by definition a gradient (a conservative vector field). Whether this conservativity constraint matters for sampling quality and density estimation has been debated empirically with contradictory results, and no analytical characterization of the permissible modeling freedom existed. This paper provides the first theoretical answer by deriving exact necessary and sufficient conditions on the non-conservative remainder of the learned vector field.

## Input / Output

The method operates on data samples from a low-dimensional manifold of intrinsic dimension d embedded in R^D, with a forward SDE (drift f, diffusion coefficient g) transforming the data to a known terminal distribution p(x,1) from which x_1 is sampled. The backward ODE produces generated data points and, via the instantaneous change of variables formula, exact log-densities. For local manifold analysis, the output is the intrinsic dimensionality d, estimated as the number of saturating singular values of the flow Jacobian Y_t = ∂φ_t(x_1)/∂x_1 as t→0, equivalently the rank of exp(∇f̃_θ(x_0, 0)).

## Method

The core contribution is an orthogonal decomposition in L²(p) of any diffusion model vector field s_θ into a conservative component ∇φ and a remainder r_θ satisfying the gauge freedom condition ∇·r_θ + r_θ^T ∇log p = 0, derived from the Fokker-Planck equation. This shows that exact sampling and density estimation are achieved if and only if the conservative component equals the true score, making conservativity neither necessary nor sufficient. For intrinsic dimensionality estimation, Lemma 1 relates the eigenvalues of P_t = Y_t Y_t^T to the eigenvalues of ∇f̃_t under a commutativity assumption, yielding Theorem 2: when s_θ is conservative and exact, rank[exp(∇f̃_θ(x_0,0))] = d. Empirically, the method is validated on spheres, tori, and Swiss rolls embedded in R^5, confirming that only conservative vector fields produce the predicted singular-value saturation pattern needed to recover d, while non-conservative models (even with Jacobian-symmetry penalties) fail.

## Results

For a 2D Gaussian embedded in R^5, the conservative diffusion model correctly yields 2 saturating singular values of Y_t (matching the true intrinsic dimensionality d=2) while the non-conservative model shows all 5 singular values saturating, making ID estimation impossible. For spheres with intrinsic dimension d = D/2 − 1 embedded in R^D, the conservative model perfectly recovers the true intrinsic dimension across multiple embedding dimensions, whereas the non-conservative model fails regardless of model capacity or the addition of a Jacobian-symmetry penalty. The theoretical counter-example in Section 4 further demonstrates that a non-conservative vector field (with a rotation-field remainder) can satisfy the gauge freedom condition and still produce exact samples and densities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary empirical comparison is between a conservative diffusion model (s_θ = ∇‖ψ_θ‖²) and a non-conservative one (s_θ = ψ_θ, an unconstrained MLP) for intrinsic dimensionality estimation. The conservative model consistently and exactly recovers the true intrinsic dimension across all tested manifolds (2D Gaussian in R^5, spheres, tori, Swiss rolls at D=3 and D=5), while the non-conservative model fails in every case — all singular values saturate rather than the expected (D−d) collapsing to zero. Notably, adding a Jacobian-symmetry penalty term (‖∇s_θ − ∇s_θᵀ‖_F, as proposed by Chao et al. 2023 and Cui et al. 2022) does not rescue the non-conservative model's ID estimation. The paper also notes that prior ID estimators based on diffusion models (Wenliang & Moran 2022; Batzolis et al. 2022) lack theoretical guarantees or fail on spheres, whereas the present method provides a provable link between conservativity and correct ID recovery via Theorem 2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SDE-based diffusion framework of Song et al. (2021) and the intrinsic dimensionality estimation approach of Horvat & Pfister (2022) for normalizing flows, extending the latter to diffusion models. It departs from a long line of empirical studies (Salimans & Ho 2021, Chao et al. 2023, Wenliang & Moran 2022, Cui et al. 2022) that addressed the conservativity question with contradicting results, by providing the first analytical characterization of the gauge freedom in the diffusion vector field. The orthogonal decomposition into conservative and gauge-free components (Theorem 1) draws on Hilbert space projection theory applied to the Fokker-Planck equation, offering a clean theoretical resolution where prior work could only offer empirical observations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper resolves a long-standing and actively debated question in the diffusion model community with a clean theoretical answer: conservativity is neither necessary nor sufficient for exact sampling or density estimation, but the gauge freedom condition (11) is the correct constraint. The practical limitation is that enforcing the gauge freedom condition requires access to the true score, making it difficult to implement directly; the authors acknowledge this and leave penalty-based enforcement for future work. The intrinsic dimensionality estimation application, while demonstrated only on toy manifolds (spheres, tori, Swiss rolls in low dimensions), opens a promising direction for using diffusion models as geometric probes of data structure. An open question is whether the commutativity assumption in Theorem 2 holds broadly enough for real-world high-dimensional data.
