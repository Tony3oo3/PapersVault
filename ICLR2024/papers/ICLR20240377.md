## Problem

Convolution models with long filters achieve state-of-the-art quality on long-sequence tasks but lag behind optimized Transformers in wall-clock time. The FFT convolution algorithm, while asymptotically efficient at O(N log N), suffers from two hardware bottlenecks: it fails to exploit specialized tensor core matrix multiply units, and incurs expensive I/O between memory hierarchy levels when sequences are too long to fit in SRAM for kernel fusion.

## Input / Output

The method takes a real-valued input sequence u ∈ R^{B×H×N} and a pre-computed complex-valued frequency-domain kernel k_f ∈ C^{H×N}, producing a real-valued convolved output y ∈ R^{B×H×N}. It operates in half-precision (fp16/bf16) and scales across four orders of magnitude in sequence length (256 to 4M), with the kernel broadcast across the batch dimension. The convolution is causal (zero-padded), and the system also supports fused gated convolutions of the form y = v ⊙ ((u ⊙ w) * k) common in Hyena and M2-BERT architectures.

## Method

FLASHFFTCONV adapts the order-p Monarch FFT decomposition to convolutions by broadcasting matrix multiply operations across the sequence dimension rather than the batch/hidden dimensions, reducing SRAM requirements and enabling kernel fusion for sequences up to 32K in half-precision. The FFT is rewritten as p matrix-matrix multiply operations (with permutation steps becoming simple transposes) that map directly onto tensor cores, with a cost model selecting the optimal order p to balance FLOP cost against I/O cost as sequence length changes. Domain-specific optimizations include a real-to-real FFT algorithm that halves the FFT length, skipping zero-padded portions of the outermost matrix multiplies for causal convolutions, and fusing elementwise gating operations. Two architectural extensions—partial convolutions (shorter kernels) and frequency-sparse convolutions (zeroing portions of k_f in frequency space)—are implemented by simply skipping blocks in the matrix decomposition, reducing both memory footprint and compute.

## Results

FLASHFFTCONV achieves up to 7.93× speedup and 5.60× memory savings over PyTorch FFT convolution across sequence lengths from 256 to 4M. Under a fixed compute budget, it improves M2-BERT-base GLUE score by 3.3 points (77.6→80.9) and Hyena-s PILE perplexity by 2.3 points (13.4→11.1), matching models with twice the parameters. It achieves 96.1% accuracy on Path-512 (256K sequence length), the first model to solve this task (previous best was 50%). End-to-end, it reaches 62.3% FLOP utilization and outperforms FlashAttention-v2 in wall-clock throughput by 1.1×–1.5× at sequence lengths 2K–16K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PyTorch FFT convolution, FLASHFFTCONV wins across all sequence lengths tested (256 to 4M), achieving 4.78×–6.54× speedup for standard convolutions and up to 7.93× for gated convolutions (the common pattern in Hyena/M2-BERT architectures), with memory savings of 2.63×–8.21×. The Fusion-Only/cuFFTdx ablation (kernel fusion without tensor cores) is consistently slower than full FLASHFFTCONV, confirming that the Monarch decomposition's use of tensor cores is the key differentiator. Against FlashAttention-v2, FLASHFFTCONV achieves lower FLOP utilization (62.3% vs. 72.1% at 8K) but higher end-to-end throughput (35.2K vs. 27.8K tokens/s at 8K) because convolutions require fewer total FLOPs than attention, yielding a 1.1×–1.5× wall-clock speedup at sequence lengths 2K–16K. In quality comparisons under fixed compute budget, FLASHFFTCONV-trained models outperform PyTorch-trained counterparts by 3.3 GLUE points (M2-BERT-base) and 2.3 PPL points (Hyena-s), a gap equivalent to doubling model parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Monarch decomposition (Dao et al., 2022) — originally designed for general structured matrix multiplication — specifically to FFT convolutions in sequence modeling, with the key systems insight of broadcasting matrix multiplies along the sequence dimension rather than batch/hidden to enable kernel fusion at longer lengths. It also incorporates the classic real-to-real FFT trick (Sorensen et al., 1987) to halve FFT cost, and positions itself as the convolutional analogue of FlashAttention's IO-aware optimization for attention. The partial and frequency-sparse convolution extensions draw on the broader sparsity-in-deep-learning literature but are novel in mapping sparsity patterns directly onto blocks of the Monarch decomposition for free compute savings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FLASHFFTCONV closes the wall-clock gap between convolutional sequence models and Transformers, enabling practical deployment of long-convolution architectures across language, DNA, vision, and audio. The ability to process 4M-token sequences (first DNA model embedding the longest human genes at single-nucleotide resolution) and solve Path-512 (previously unsolved) opens new application frontiers. Limitations include support only for A100/H100 (not V100), requirement that N be a power of 2, and diminishing speedups at very long sequences where I/O dominates. Open questions include whether the Monarch-based approach generalizes to non-GPU accelerators, whether the frequency-sparse patterns transfer across architectures, and whether further hardware co-design (e.g., custom FFT units) could push utilization beyond the current ~62%.
