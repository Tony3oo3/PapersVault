## Problem

While memorization capacity of fully connected networks has been extensively studied, the theoretical memorization capacity of multi-head attention (MHA) layers in Transformers remains largely unexplored. This paper asks how many input examples a single-layer MHA can memorize as a function of the number of heads H, context size n, and head dimension d_h, and introduces practical linear-independence assumptions (Kruskal rank on queries, full-rank contexts) that are weaker than the standard General Position assumption yet validated on real ViT models.

## Input / Output

The MHA layer consumes a key/context matrix E ∈ R^{n×d} of n token embeddings and a single query vector e ∈ R^d, producing a prediction vector ŷ ∈ R^{d_out}. The critical structural constraint is n < d (context size strictly less than embedding dimension), which holds in prominent models like ViT (197/768), BERT (512/768), and GPT-3 (2048/12288). Each training example pairs one context matrix and one query with a label, and the model must reproduce all T labels exactly; the query may or may not be among the context tokens (self-attention vs. cross-attention).

## Method

The paper proves that an MHA with H heads, embedding dimension d, head dimension d_h, and context size n < d can memorize Ω(H·min(n, d_h)) examples under two mild assumptions: query vectors have Kruskal rank ≥ n and each context matrix has rank n. The proof proceeds inductively on the number of heads, showing each new head increases the rank of the intermediate representation matrix Z by r−1 (where r = min(n, d_h)) without disturbing previously memorized examples. The key mechanism is the saturation property of softmax: by adding a large-magnitude rank-one perturbation W⁺ to the key-query weights, the softmax for previously handled examples is driven to a one-hot vector independent of the new head's tuning weights, while the new head's weights W* are freely designed to fit r−1 fresh examples. A tightness result (Proposition 2) shows the bound is achieved when contexts are shared, and a comparison with two-layer ReLU networks (Proposition 3) demonstrates MHA is at least as powerful as an FCN of comparable size under the same assumptions.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 1) establishes that a single-layer MHA with H heads, embedding dimension d, and context size n < d, equipped with Θ(Hd²) parameters, can memorize Ω(Hn) examples under mild linear-independence assumptions. Synthetic experiments confirm memorization increases linearly with H (R² ≥ 0.98 for the best linear fit), monotonically with context size n, and monotonically with head dimension d_h only up to d_h = n. On ImageNet with ViT, the paper validates that Assumptions 1 and 2 hold after a single attention layer (random or pretrained), while the standard General Position assumption fails in all tested configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is theoretical: Proposition 3 upper-bounds the memorization of a two-layer ReLU network with input dimension (n+1)d and hidden dimension m at O(nm/d_out + m) examples, while Theorem 1 lower-bounds MHA memorization at Ω(Hn) examples with the same order of parameters (m = Θ(H), d_out = 1). This shows MHA is at least as powerful as a same-size ReLU FCN for memorization under Assumptions 1 and 2. Empirically, the paper contrasts its proposed assumptions against the standard General Position assumption used in prior FCN memorization work (Baum 1988; Bubeck et al. 2020): General Position fails in all four ViT configurations tested, while the paper's weaker Kruskal-rank and full-rank assumptions hold after just one attention layer. The synthetic experiments (Figure 2) also confirm that increasing d_h beyond n provides no additional memorization benefit, aligning with the theoretical prediction and with Bhojanapalli et al. (2020) on expressivity limits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the memorization capacity literature from fully connected networks (Baum 1988; Bubeck et al. 2020; Vershynin 2020) to the multi-head attention (MHA) component of transformers, isolating it from the ReLU feed-forward layer. It departs from the standard General Position assumption used in FCN memorization work, introducing instead a Kruskal-rank condition on query vectors and a full-rank condition on context matrices, motivated by empirical observations on ViT. While Kim et al. (2023) recently studied transformer memorization with a single attention head and ReLU FFN, this is the first work to explicitly characterize MHA memorization across multiple data points and multiple heads, and Bhojanapalli et al. (2020) studied MHA expressivity only on a single input sequence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a single MHA layer with Θ(Hd²) parameters can memorize Ω(Hn) examples provides the first theoretical lower bound on how attention alone contributes to a transformer's memorization capacity, with direct implications for privacy (training data extraction) and generalization. The finding that the commonly assumed General Position fails on real ViT models while the weaker Kruskal-rank assumption holds is practically important, as it means prior FCN memorization theory does not directly transfer to transformers. Open questions include extending the analysis to multi-layer and sequence-to-sequence settings, establishing matching upper bounds, and understanding whether the theoretical capacity is achievable under standard optimization (gradient descent).
