## Problem

Long-sequence model benchmarks such as Long Range Arena (LRA) evaluate architectures by training from random initialization, which the authors show grossly underestimates model capabilities and exaggerates performance gaps between architectures. This practice ignores the inductive bias provided by pretraining objectives, making it impossible to fairly compare architectures like Transformers and State Space Models on their true long-range dependency modeling abilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240075/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on diverse 1D sequences of varying lengths (2K to 65K tokens), including character-level text, flattened grayscale or RGB images (up to 1024² pixels), raw speech waveforms (16K samples), EKG/PPG signals (4K samples), and synthetic path-tracing sequences. All 2D data are flattened to 1D without any spatial inductive bias, and the output is a class label (binary or multi-class) or a continuous regression value for physiological signal prediction.

## Method

The core contribution is Self-Pretraining (SPT): before supervised fine-tuning, the model is pretrained on the downstream task's own training data using standard denoising objectives (masked token prediction for bidirectional models, next-token prediction for causal models), with masking ratios tuned per modality (50% for visual, 15% for language, 10% for ListOps). This data-driven initialization requires no additional data or architectural changes. The authors show SPT improves vanilla Transformers by ~30% on LRA, closing the gap with S4, and improves S4 by 20 absolute points on PathX-256. They further demonstrate that with SPT, simpler diagonal linear RNNs match S4's structured parameterization, rendering hand-crafted biases (HiPPO initialization, low-rank corrections) largely redundant. Analysis of learned convolution kernels reveals task-specific decay patterns that differ from fixed HiPPO kernels, confirming that data-driven priors adapt to each task's structure.

## Results

On Long Range Arena, self-pretrained (SPT) Transformers with rotary embeddings achieve 86.21% average accuracy, surpassing S4 trained from scratch (86.09%) and matching SPT S4 (86.75%). SPT improves vanilla Transformers by ~30% absolute over training from scratch (53.66% → 81.34% avg). On the challenging PathX-256 task (65K sequence length), SPT S4 achieves 87.11% accuracy, a 20-point improvement over the best previously reported result of 67.82%. SPT also enables diagonal linear RNNs (DLR) to reach 83.37% average, matching S4 trained from scratch (82.85%) without any hand-crafted parameterization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original LRA trained-from-scratch baselines (Tay et al., 2020a), SPT Transformers improve average accuracy from 53.66% to 81.34–81.81%, a ~30-point gain with no architectural changes. SPT Transformers with rotary embeddings (86.21% avg) match S4 from scratch (86.09%) and slightly trail SPT S4 (86.75%), while SPT S4 improves PathX-256 from 67.82% to 87.11%. Compared to hybrid architectures SPADE (87.40%) and MEGA (88.21%), SPT Transformers remain within 1–2 points on average, showing that purely attention-based models with pretraining can be competitive without SSM layers. Pythia 70M (68.04%) underperforms SPT Transformers (86.21%) due to tokenization mismatch, though it substantially outperforms its random-init counterpart (62.76%), confirming that pretraining on large corpora helps but task-specific SPT is more effective. For DLR (Gupta et al., 2022b), SPT closes the gap to S4: DLR+SPT reaches 83.37% avg, matching S4 from scratch (82.85%) and trailing SPT S4 (84.47%) by only 1.1 points, demonstrating that S4's structured parameterization becomes largely redundant under pretraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) the LRA benchmark and the subsequent line of work showing that Transformers underperform SSMs like S4 on long-range tasks, motivating architectural innovations (MEGA, SPADE, DLR, HiPPO-based initializations), and (2) the self-pretraining literature (El-Nouby et al., 2021; He et al., 2022; Krishna et al., 2023) demonstrating that denoising pretraining on downstream task data alone can rival large-corpus pretraining. The key departure is methodological: rather than proposing a new architecture, the authors apply standard self-pretraining (causal or masked denoising) to existing models trained on LRA, showing that the apparent architectural gap between Transformers and SSMs is largely an artifact of random initialization. This reframes S4's structured parameterization and HiPPO initialization not as essential inductive biases but as one way to achieve what data-driven pretraining accomplishes more generally.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a widely-held assumption in the long-sequence modeling community—that Transformers are fundamentally inadequate for long-range dependencies and require architectural modifications to compete with SSMs. By showing that vanilla Transformers match S4 on LRA with SPT, and that S4's hand-crafted biases become largely redundant under pretraining, it provides a practical and inexpensive protocol (SPT on task data) that should be adopted as standard for evaluating new sequence architectures. Open questions include whether the findings extend to genuinely longer sequences where SSMs' log-linear complexity advantage becomes critical, whether SPT gains persist at much larger model scales, and whether the approach generalizes to other benchmark suites (algorithmic reasoning, graphs) where training from scratch is also standard practice. The compute-tied experiments, while showing SPT still helps, reveal that the full gains require additional compute beyond the TFS baseline, a practical limitation for resource-constrained settings.
