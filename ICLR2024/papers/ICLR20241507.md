## Problem

Existing gradient-based attribution methods (IG, BIG, AGI) rely on linear integration paths or targeted adversarial attacks that may cross multiple decision boundaries before reaching the target class, producing biased or overfitted explanations. The paper identifies, for the first time, that the decision boundary exploration inherent in attribution is structurally consistent with transferable adversarial attacks, yet this connection has never been exploited to improve attribution quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241507/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image (224×224 from ImageNet) and a pre-trained DNN (Inception-v3, ResNet-50, VGG-16, or ViT-B/16) as input, and produces a pixel-level attribution heatmap where each pixel's value represents its importance to the model's predicted class. The attribution is computed via a nonlinear integration path that iteratively perturbs the input in the frequency domain (via DCT/IDCT) over 10 steps with 20 frequency-domain approximate samples per step, requiring no reference/baseline image and no target-class specification beyond the model's own prediction.

## Method

AttEXplore unifies attribution with transferable adversarial attacks through Model Parameter Exploration (MPE), which treats input feature modification as equivalent to parameter activation adjustment (L(x;w) ≡ L(w;x)). The core mechanism is a novel nonlinear integration path A = ∫ Δx^t ⊙ g(x^t) dt, where at each step the input is perturbed via a frequency-domain transformation: DCT maps the image to frequency space, Gaussian noise and random multiplicative perturbations (N(1,σ)) are applied to explore different frequency bands, and IDCT maps back to spatial domain. The integration direction is determined by the sign of the averaged gradient across N=20 frequency-perturbed copies, while the gradient magnitude g(x^t) is the mean gradient over those copies. Unlike AGI's targeted attack (which must cross other classes' boundaries), AttEXplore's transferable-attack-inspired exploration generates general perturbations that cross the current decision boundary more stably, avoiding the bias from traversing irrelevant class boundaries.

## Results

On ImageNet (1000 samples), AttEXplore achieves the best Insertion Score across all three CNN models: 0.4732 (Inception-v3), 0.4197 (ResNet-50), and 0.3186 (VGG-16), outperforming the prior SOTA AGI by 4.89%, 4.01%, and 6.01% respectively. On average across all baselines, AttEXplore improves Insertion Score by 19–24% while maintaining competitive Deletion Scores. In terms of computational efficiency, AttEXplore processes 47.28 FPS, roughly 54× faster than AGI (0.88 FPS) and 14× faster than BIG (3.38 FPS), while also achieving the lowest INFD faithfulness scores (e.g., 0.671 on ResNet-50 vs. AGI's 1.003).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AttEXplore outperforms the primary SOTA baseline AGI on both Insertion and Deletion Scores across all three models (e.g., +4.89% Insertion and −1.42% Deletion on Inception-v3), confirming that the frequency-based MPE integration path avoids the multi-boundary-crossing issue inherent in AGI's targeted attack. Against Grad-CAM, AttEXplore improves Insertion by 5.36% (Inception-v3) while reducing Deletion by 72.5%, indicating far more focused attributions. Compared to BIG, AttEXplore gains 32.6% Insertion on Inception-v3, showing that the nonlinear MPE path is substantially more effective than BIG's linear boundary search. AttEXplore also surpasses SaliencyMap, DeepLIFT, GIG, EG, Fast-IG, IG, and SG on Insertion Score by 19–24% on average, though GIG and SG achieve slightly lower (better) Deletion Scores on individual models; the authors note Insertion is the more reliable metric for attribution quality. On ViT-B/16 (Appendix C), AttEXplore extends its lead with 0.470 Insertion vs. AGI's 0.425, demonstrating generalizability beyond CNNs.

## Relation to Prior Work

AttEXplore extends the gradient-based attribution lineage (IG → BIG → AGI) by replacing AGI's targeted adversarial attack with a transferable, frequency-domain-based attack for decision boundary exploration. The key conceptual departure is the observation that transferable adversarial attacks—which craft general perturbations to cross multiple decision boundaries—align naturally with the attribution goal of identifying features that shift the current boundary, whereas AGI's targeted approach risks crossing irrelevant boundaries. The frequency-based input transformation (DCT + random spectral masking) is directly adapted from SSA (Long et al., 2022), repurposing an adversarial-attack technique as an attribution mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, faster (47 FPS vs. 0.88 FPS for AGI) attribution method with modest but consistent gains over AGI across CNN and ViT architectures, and the transferable-attack-to-attribution framing could inspire further hybrid methods. However, the improvements over AGI are relatively small (4–6% Insertion Score), the evaluation is limited to 1000 ImageNet samples, and the frequency-domain component's necessity is not fully isolated in ablations. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-image modalities, whether the DCT-based exploration is truly superior to simpler input augmentations, and how the method behaves under distribution shift or with larger model families.
