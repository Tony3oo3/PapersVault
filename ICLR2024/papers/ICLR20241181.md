## Problem

While allocation harms (demographic disparities in resource allocation) are well-studied in supervised learning, the effects of group underrepresentation on self-supervised and contrastive learning representations remain largely unexplored. This paper asks whether underrepresented groups suffer representation harm in contrastive learning—specifically, whether their learned embeddings collapse toward semantically similar majority groups—and whether this representation harm is a causal contributor to downstream allocation harms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241181/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For the image experiments, the method consumes 32×32 RGB images from CIFAR-10 (10 classes, ~50k training images) and produces 128-dim CL embeddings via a ResNet-34 backbone; underrepresentation is simulated by subsampling one class to 1% of its original count. For the text experiments, ~400k English biographies from BIASBIOS (28 occupations × 2 genders) are encoded by BERT with SimCSE loss. The similarity-graph theory operates on an SBM-defined graph where edges encode sample similarity. Downstream, a linear probe trained on 75% of test data produces class predictions, and the paper outputs three harm metrics: representation harm (RH/GRH, a ratio of cosine distances), total effect (TE), and natural indirect effect (NIE) from causal mediation analysis.

## Method

The paper combines empirical measurement, causal mediation analysis, and asymptotic theory. Empirically, SimCLR and SimSiam are trained on balanced vs. class-undersampled CIFAR-10, and SimCSE on the naturally imbalanced BIASBIOS dataset; representation harm is quantified as the ratio of inter-class cosine distances under underrepresentation versus balanced training. A causal mediation analysis (Pearl's framework) then decomposes downstream allocation harm into a natural indirect effect (mediated through the CL representations) and a reverse natural direct effect (from undersampling alone), showing that the NIE is significant for semantically similar class pairs. Theoretically, under a layer-peeled assumption and a stochastic block model on the similarity graph, the paper proves (Theorem 3.2) that as n→∞ all representations within a block collapse to a single vector (neural collapse), and the geometry of the K collapsed vectors is determined by a weighted CL objective whose solution depends intricately on block sizes—smaller (underrepresented) blocks with high connectivity to a neighbor collapse toward that neighbor's representation, while disconnected blocks move apart.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, underrepresenting the automobile class to 1% causes its CL representation to collapse toward trucks, yielding a Representation Harm (RH) metric of 0.78 ± 0.009 (vs. 1.0 for no harm), the worst among all class pairs. Causal mediation analysis shows this representation harm accounts for a Natural Indirect Effect (NIE) of 0.062 ± 0.001 in misclassification (automobile→truck) and a 15.5 ± 0.2% reduction in deer classification accuracy. On BIASBIOS, gender representation harm is observed for related occupation pairs: GRH(attorney, paralegal) = 0.94 and GRH(surgeon, dentist) = 0.95, indicating underrepresented gender representations collapse toward semantically similar occupations rather than the same-gender majority within the same occupation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a new CL method but investigates representation harm across existing CL methods. SimSiam (Chen & He, 2021) reproduces the same representation harm pattern as SimCLR on CIFAR-10: RH between automobiles and trucks is 0.834 ± 0.025 and 0.882 ± 0.0, and NIE values for the automobile→truck misclassification reach 0.145 ± 0.002, confirming the phenomenon is not specific to SimCLR. Boosted Contrastive Learning (BCL; Zhou et al., 2022), a proposed mitigation for underrepresentation, only partially alleviates the harm: RH between automobiles and trucks improves from 0.78 ± 0.009 to 0.829 ± 0.001, but downstream accuracy still drops by 5.3 ± 0.2% for automobiles and 3.7 ± 0.01% for trucks, demonstrating that BCL does not fully eliminate representation harm. The theoretical SBM analysis further shows that when connectivity between two groups is low (α = 0.1), representations do not collapse even under severe underrepresentation, whereas high connectivity (α = 0.4) leads to complete collapse (identical representations) when the minority proportion drops below 2⁻⁶.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural collapse literature (Fang et al., 2021b) from balanced to imbalanced contrastive learning settings, showing that underrepresentation causes inter-group collapse (minority representations merging with semantically similar majority groups) rather than merely within-group collapse. It departs from the dominant algorithmic fairness literature, which has focused almost exclusively on allocation harms in supervised learning, by introducing representation harm as a distinct and measurable phenomenon in self-supervised learning. The theoretical analysis builds on stochastic block models (Holland et al., 1983) and the layer-peeled assumption, while the causal mediation framework (Pearl, 2022) is repurposed to decompose downstream allocation harm into a representation-mediated indirect effect and a direct undersampling effect.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that CL representations themselves are a source of harm, not merely a neutral substrate for downstream classifiers, which has direct implications for the broad adoption of SSL in high-stakes applications (e.g., CLIP-powered generative models like DALL-E 2). The negative result that boosted contrastive learning (BCL) does not fully eliminate representation harm highlights that the problem remains open, particularly in the absence of group annotations that most practical CL settings lack. A key open question is whether techniques from supervised learning that improve minority-group performance without demographic labels can be transferred to the SSL setting, and whether the theoretical prediction that connectivity structure determines collapse targets can guide practical 'surgery' interventions on data or loss.
