## Problem

Despite the widespread empirical success of truncation sampling methods (nucleus, top-k, ε) in preventing degenerate neural text generation, no prior work provided a rigorous theoretical explanation for why discarding low-probability tokens is effective. The paper asks: what mathematical property of language model errors makes threshold-based truncation sufficient to guarantee that all sampled tokens have nonzero true probability, and can we do better than a coarse threshold?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240485/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a text prefix, the method consumes the model's predicted next-token distribution p̂ and the embedding (softmax) matrix W ∈ R^{v×d} to produce a filtered candidate set from which the next token is sampled. The filtering is non-monotonic in probability: unlike threshold methods, BAT can reject a higher-probability token while accepting a lower-probability one, depending on linear dependencies in W. In practice, the full LP is approximated via SVD of W (reducing d constraints to ~20), and high-probability tokens above 1−exp(−δ) are accepted without solving the LP, yielding ~0.1 s amortized cost per token.

## Method

The paper first proves that if the model's log-probability underestimation is bounded by δ, then probability overestimation is additively bounded by 1−exp(−δ), which directly justifies threshold-based truncation (Corollary 1). It then exploits the softmax bottleneck: since softmax∘W is a linear map R^d → Δ_v, model outputs are confined to a d-dimensional subspace, and a cross-entropy-minimizing model satisfies W^T p* = W^T p̂. The key insight of BAT (Basis-Aware Threshold) sampling is that a token i has nonzero true probability if no valid distribution p with p_i = 0 simultaneously satisfies the threshold constraint (p_j ≤ p̂_j exp(δ) ∀j) and the basis-aware constraint (W^T p = W^T p̂). This feasibility check is solved as a linear program per candidate token, and the δ parameter is set per-context via existing heuristics (η, ε, or nucleus), yielding variants BA-η, BA-ε, and BA-nucleus.

## Results

On low-entropy open-ended generation (MAUVE on Open Web Text), BA-η sampling consistently outperforms η sampling across all GPT-2 model sizes: 87.8 vs 85.0 (Small), 92.2 vs 90.4 (Medium), 88.4 vs 86.0 (Large), and 89.6 vs 87.1 (XL). Human annotators narrowly prefer BA-η generations over η generations (43% vs 38% wins at η=0.002; 48% vs 47% at η=0.024). No single sampling method dominates across all model sizes and entropy settings, but BA-η shows the most consistent advantage in the low-entropy (near-greedy) regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against η sampling, BA-η achieves a consistent 1.8–2.8 MAUVE point improvement across all four GPT-2 sizes in low-entropy generation (Table 1), and human annotators prefer BA-η generations in pairwise comparisons (43% vs 38% at the most restrictive setting). Against ε sampling, the paper reports no consistent advantage for BA-ε; in fact, BA-ε can accept zero tokens for certain prefixes under sufficiently low-entropy parameters, making it less robust than plain ε sampling. Against nucleus sampling, BA-nucleus was found to be significantly worse than its vanilla counterpart in preliminary experiments, likely because nucleus's threshold-selection mechanism is inconsistent with the theoretical framework used to derive BAT. Overall, the paper's method (BA-η) clearly wins against η in the low-entropy regime but does not uniformly outperform all threshold-based baselines across every setting and model size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the softmax bottleneck framework of Yang et al. (2018), which identifies the low-rank structure of the output layer as a source of probability estimation errors, and on the empirical observation that truncation sampling (nucleus, top-k, ε, η) mitigates degenerate generation (Holtzman et al., 2020; Fan et al., 2018; Hewitt et al., 2022). The key departure is providing the first formal theoretical explanation for *why* threshold-based truncation works—via a log-probability underestimation bound that implies an additive overestimation bound—and then exploiting the softmax matrix structure to go beyond thresholds, yielding the non-monotonic BAT sampling method that can reject high-probability tokens while retaining low-probability ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical grounding fills a notable gap: truncation sampling has been empirically dominant for years without a principled explanation, and this paper shows it is essentially a coarse proxy for discarding tokens outside the true support. BAT sampling offers a more expressive alternative, but its practical adoption is limited by the computational cost of solving linear programs at each step (even with SVD approximation, ~0.1s/token amortized) and by failure modes in extreme low-entropy settings where no tokens pass the LP feasibility check. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models with higher-rank softmax matrices, whether the LP can be replaced by cheaper approximations, and whether the gains persist in high-entropy or multi-turn generation settings beyond the GPT-2 pilot study.
