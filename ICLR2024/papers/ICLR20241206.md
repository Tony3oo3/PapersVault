## Problem

Motion data is extremely scarce for non-human skeletons (animals, imaginary creatures), often limited to a single animation sequence per character. Existing single-instance motion synthesis methods rely on GANs (prone to mode collapse) or nearest-neighbor approaches (limited generalization), and standard diffusion architectures like transformers or deep UNets overfit when trained on a single sequence due to their global receptive fields.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241206/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single motion sequence of arbitrary skeletal topology, represented as a tensor D ∈ R^{N×F} containing 6D joint rotations, 3D root positions, and binary foot contact labels per frame. At inference, it accepts random Gaussian noise x_T ~ N(0,I) and optionally a reference motion with an ROI mask for composition tasks. Outputs are synthesized motion sequences of arbitrary length (even 60+ seconds from a 10-second input), with the same feature structure as the input, while static features like bone lengths remain fixed.

## Method

SinMDM is a shallow UNet (depth 1, one residual block, 256 channels) whose global attention layers are replaced by QnA local attention layers that use learned queries shared across overlapping k-size windows, yielding a temporally narrow receptive field that prevents overfitting on a single sequence. The model follows the DDPM framework with x₀-prediction (predicting the clean motion rather than noise), a cosine noise schedule over 1000 steps, and a simple L2 diffusion loss. The key architectural insight is that limiting the receptive field—achieved through both shallow depth and local (rather than global) attention—allows the network to simultaneously learn from multiple local temporal segments, promoting diversity while retaining fidelity to the input's motion motifs. The lightweight design (5.26M parameters) enables training on a single mid-range GPU, and all applications (in-betweening, expansion, harmonization, style transfer, crowd animation) are handled at inference time via ROI masking or low-frequency guidance without retraining.

## Results

On the Mixamo benchmark, SinMDM achieves a Harmonic Mean of 0.84, substantially outperforming Ganimator (-0.51) and GenMM (0.56). SinMDM leads in Global Diversity (1.42 vs. 1.24 for Ganimator, 0.4 for GenMM), Inter Diversity (0.13 vs. 0.09 for Ganimator), and Intra Diversity Diff (0.03 vs. 0.13 for Ganimator), while using only 5.26M parameters (vs. 21.7M for Ganimator) and 1.5h total training time (vs. 6.0h). On the HumanML3D benchmark, SinMDM achieves balanced SiFID, Inter Diversity, and Intra Diversity Diff scores, whereas vanilla MDM overfits (perfect fidelity, low diversity) and MDM trained on crops diverges from the input motifs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Ganimator (Li et al., 2022), SinMDM wins on 7 of 10 metrics: Global Diversity (1.42 vs. 1.24), Inter Diversity (0.13 vs. 0.09), Intra Diversity Diff (0.03 vs. 0.13), parameter count (5.26M vs. 21.7M), iteration time (0.09s vs. 0.36s), total training time (1.5h vs. 6.0h), and Harmonic Mean (0.84 vs. -0.51). SinMDM loses only on Local Diversity (1.00 vs. 1.17) and ties on Coverage (94.3). Against GenMM (Li et al., 2023), SinMDM achieves higher Global Diversity (1.42 vs. 0.40) and Local Diversity (1.00 vs. 0.35), reflecting GenMM's limited generalization from its nearest-neighbor approach, though GenMM achieves higher Coverage (98.7 vs. 94.3) and requires no training. Against MDM (Tevet et al., 2023) on the HumanML3D benchmark, vanilla MDM trained on a single motion exhibits complete overfitting (perfect SiFID and intra-diversity but very low inter-diversity), while MDM trained on short crops diverges from the input motifs; SinMDM achieves balanced scores across all three deep-feature metrics. A user study further confirms SinMDM is significantly preferred over both Ganimator and GenMM on diversity, fidelity, and quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

SinMDM adapts the DDPM framework and the motion-prediction (rather than noise-prediction) strategy from MDM (Tevet et al., 2023) to the single-instance setting, but departs from MDM's transformer architecture in favor of a shallow QnA-based UNet, motivated by the need for a narrow receptive field to prevent overfitting on a single sequence. The narrow-receptive-field principle is borrowed from single-image GANs (SinGAN's patch-based discriminator) and from QnA's shift-invariant local attention (Arar et al., 2022), but the specific combination—a degenerated UNet with QnA replacing global attention—is novel to the motion domain. The paper positions itself against Ganimator (GAN-based, Li et al. 2022) and GenMM (nearest-neighbor motion-graphs, Li et al. 2023), arguing that diffusion's gradual denoising avoids mode collapse while remaining lightweight enough for single-sequence training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for animators working with non-human or fantastical characters (dragons, ostriches, jaguars) where no motion datasets exist and each skeleton is unique; SinMDM enables diverse, faithful motion synthesis from a single reference clip and supports in-betweening, expansion, style transfer, and crowd animation all at inference time without retraining. Key limitations remain: the model cannot synthesize out-of-distribution motions, cannot enforce a specific ordering of sub-motions, and suffers from relatively long iterative inference time. An open question is whether the narrow-receptive-field constraint can be relaxed (e.g., via curriculum or hierarchical approaches) to capture longer-range motion structure without sacrificing diversity.
