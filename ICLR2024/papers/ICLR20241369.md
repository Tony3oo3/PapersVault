## Problem

Mainstream AI-generated text detectors consistently fail on short texts (SMSes, Tweets, reviews) while performing well on long texts, and existing online detectors simply impose minimum-length requirements rather than solving the underlying difficulty. The paper identifies that extremely short machine-generated texts are so simple and human-like that clear binary labels become unreliable, creating a 'partially unlabeled' regime that standard binary classification training cannot handle.

## Input / Output

The detector accepts text passages of highly variable length — from single-sentence tweets (~25 tokens) to full multi-paragraph articles — and outputs a binary attribution (AI-generated vs. human-written). During training, the Text Multiscaling module randomly deletes sentences from longer training texts to produce shorter variants, so the model sees a continuous spectrum of lengths. The length-sensitive prior π(l) is computed per-sample based on token count, meaning the loss function adapts its regularization strength depending on how short or long each training example is.

## Method

The MPU framework reformulates AI text detection as a partial Positive-Unlabeled problem, where short machine-generated texts are treated as partially 'unlabeled' rather than definitively negative. The key technical contribution is a length-sensitive Multiscale PU Loss that replaces the constant positive prior in standard nnPU loss with a length-variant prior π̃(l), computed via an abstract recurrent discriminator modeled as a Markov chain over discrete confidence states (i/l for i=0..l) with a band-sparse transition matrix parameterized by a small token-level positive probability p. A Text Multiscaling module generates shorter training variants by randomly masking sentences (Bernoulli trials at probability p_sent), ensuring the length-sensitive loss is exercised across the full length spectrum. The final training objective is a weighted sum of the standard PN classification loss and the MPU loss (controlled by γ), applied during finetuning of pretrained BERT or RoBERTa classifiers.

## Results

On the HC3-English benchmark, RoBERTa-MPU achieves 98.40±0.31 F1 on full-length texts and 85.31±1.80 F1 on sentence-level (short) texts, outperforming all baselines on both scales. The most dramatic gains appear on short texts: RoBERTa-MPU improves over plain RoBERTa-Finetuned by 26.7 F1 points (85.31 vs. 58.60) and over the zero-shot GLTR method by 45.1 points (85.31 vs. 40.19). On the TweepFake short-text dataset, RoBERTa-MPU reaches 91.4% accuracy, surpassing the RoBERTa-Stylo baseline (91.1%) that requires an additional stylometric feature extraction module. On HC3-Chinese-Sent, RoBERTa-MPU achieves 89.37±1.94 F1, outperforming RoBERTa-Finetuned (83.07±6.85) by over 6 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoBERTa-MPU outperforms all seven baselines on both long-text and short-text HC3-English benchmarks. Against the strongest short-text baseline RoBERTa-Stylo (Kumarage et al., 2023), MPU gains 3.85 F1 on short texts (85.31 vs. 81.46) and 1.92 F1 on full texts (98.40 vs. 96.48), while requiring no additional stylometric feature extraction module. The zero-shot methods show the largest short-text degradation: GLTR (Gehrmann et al., 2019) drops to 40.19 F1 on sentences, PPL (Guo et al., 2023) to 62.04, and DetectGPT (Mitchell et al., 2023) to 63.32, all far below MPU's 85.31. The OpenAI detector (OpenAI, 2023b) achieves 69.27 F1 on short texts, still 16 points below MPU. Plain finetuned BERT (Devlin et al., 2018) and RoBERTa (Liu et al., 2019) perform competitively on long texts (97.62 and 97.42 F1) but collapse on short texts (57.65 and 58.60 F1 respectively), highlighting the core problem MPU addresses. The consistent pattern across all baselines is a severe short-text performance drop that MPU's length-sensitive PU loss specifically mitigates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Positive-Unlabeled (PU) learning framework—specifically the non-negative PU (nnPU) loss of Kiryo et al. (2017) and the unbiased PU (uPU) loss of Du Plessis et al. (2014)—for AI-generated text detection, a task previously treated exclusively as binary classification. The key conceptual departure is modeling short machine-generated texts as partially 'unlabeled' due to their high resemblance to human text, and introducing a length-sensitive prior π(l) estimated via an abstract recurrent discriminator, replacing the constant prior assumed in standard PU losses. The Text Multiscaling module (sentence-level random deletion) complements the loss by diversifying training corpus lengths, distinguishing itself from word-level EDA augmentation by preserving sentence-level semantics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: mainstream AI-text detectors (GLTR, DetectGPT, finetuned RoBERTa) degrade sharply on short texts (tweets, SMS, reviews), yet these are the formats most prevalent in daily mobile media. The PU reframing offers a principled explanation for this degradation and a training recipe that improves short-text F1 by ~25 points on HC3-En-Sent without sacrificing long-text performance, validated across English and Chinese. However, the 'partially unlabeled' characterization remains somewhat heuristic, the recurrent prior model is highly simplified (binary token contributions, hard clipping), and the HC3 benchmark itself contains a spacing artifact (Appendix C) that inflates all reported scores. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to next-generation LLMs with even more human-like short outputs, and whether the PU framing is truly the right lens versus, e.g., confidence-weighted or curriculum-based training.
