## Problem

Despite heavy-ball momentum's widespread empirical success in accelerating SGD training, prior theoretical results for stochastic heavy ball (SHB) were predominantly negative, showing no convergence improvement over vanilla SGD under constant learning rates. Whether SHB can achieve √κ accelerated convergence under large-batch settings with decaying learning rates remained an open question with no rigorous non-asymptotic analysis, leaving a gap between practice and theory.

## Input / Output

The method operates on quadratic objectives f(w) = ½wᵀHw − bᵀw under anisotropic gradient noise (E[n_t n_tᵀ] ⪯ σ²H), taking as input an initial parameter vector w₀, a multistage step decay schedule parameterized by decay factor C, stage length K, and number of stages n = T/K, a momentum coefficient β = (1−1/√κ)², and a fixed minibatch size M. The output is the last iterate w_T along with a non-asymptotic bound decomposing expected excess risk into a bias term exp(−Ω̃(T/√κ)) and a variance term Õ(dσ²/(MT)), where the bias decays exponentially after T = Õ(√κ) iterations.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a novel bounding technique for the non-commutative product of 2×2 update matrices ‖T_{T−1}T_{T−2}…T₀‖, exploiting the fact that each T_t differs from a base matrix T₀ by a diagonal perturbation Δ_t = diag(δ_t, 0). By showing that products of the form (T_{s₁}Δ_{s₁′}T_{s₂}Δ_{s₂′}…) retain sign-structure and monotonicity properties, the paper establishes ‖T_{T−1}T_{T−2}…T₀‖ ≤ ‖T_{T−1}^T‖ under the step decay schedule, reducing the analysis to a single-matrix power. This is combined with a bias-variance decomposition adapted from SGD to SHB, where the contraction term involves these matrix products. The resulting multistage step decay scheduler (η′_ℓ = (1/L)·C^{−(ℓ−1)}, K = T/log_C(T√κ)) ensures SHB first exponentially decreases loss with large learning rates, then retains the reduced loss with small learning rates, yielding √κ acceleration in the bias term while maintaining the near-optimal Õ(dσ²/(MT)) variance rate.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with batch size M=128×16 and 100 epochs, SHB with step decay achieves 89.78% test accuracy on ResNet18, 92.46% on DenseNet121, and 89.57% on MobilenetV2, outperforming SGD by 3.2–6.0 percentage points. On the ridge regression (a4a) task, SHB+step decay reduces the excess loss to 0.13×10⁻² at batch size M=512, compared to 2.44×10⁻² for SGD+step decay and 2.10×10⁻² for SGD+constant. The theoretical guarantee establishes a bias term decaying as exp(−Ω̃(T/√κ)) and a variance term of Õ(dσ²/(MT)), yielding overall convergence within log factors of the statistical minimax rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SHB with step decay against two baselines: vanilla SGD (Robbins & Monro, 1951) and SGD with step decay (Ge et al., 2019). On CIFAR-10 at the large-batch setting (M=128×16, 100 epochs), SHB wins decisively over SGD on all three architectures, gaining +5.96 pp on ResNet18 (89.78% vs 83.82%), +3.18 pp on DenseNet121 (92.46% vs 89.28%), and +5.20 pp on MobilenetV2 (89.57% vs 84.37%). On the ridge regression task at M=512, SHB+step decay (0.13×10⁻²) outperforms SGD+step decay (2.44×10⁻²) by roughly 19× in excess loss, and outperforms SGD+constant (2.10×10⁻²) by roughly 16×. At smaller batch sizes (M=8), the gap narrows substantially (SHB+step decay 0.06 vs SGD+step decay 0.04), consistent with the theory that the variance term dominates when the batch is small. The key takeaway is that the acceleration benefit of heavy-ball momentum is most pronounced in the large-batch regime, which is precisely the setting relevant to distributed and federated learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the bias-variance decomposition framework developed for vanilla SGD (Bach & Moulines, 2013; Ge et al., 2019; Pan et al., 2021) to stochastic heavy-ball momentum, adapting the step decay schedule design from those works to the multistage SHB setting. It departs from the long line of negative results for SHB with constant learning rates (Loizou & Richtárik, 2017; Kidambi et al., 2018; Jain et al., 2018a) by identifying the learning rate schedule as the missing ingredient that enables acceleration. The anisotropic gradient noise assumption (Dieuleveut et al., 2017; Pan et al., 2021) and the heavy-ball update rule (Polyak, 1964) are inherited directly from prior work, while the core technical novelty lies in bounding products of non-commuting 2×2 update matrices that arise from the momentum recursion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first non-asymptotic convergence bound for SHB with step decay on quadratic objectives that explicitly relates iteration count T, condition number κ, and convergence rate, resolving a long-standing gap between the empirical success of momentum in large-batch training and the absence of positive theory. The practical implication for distributed and federated learning—where reducing communication rounds is critical—is significant, as the √κ acceleration means fewer iterations are needed to reach a target accuracy. However, the analysis is restricted to quadratic objectives and a specific anisotropic noise model, leaving open whether the √κ rate and the specific schedule design transfer to general non-convex or non-quadratic objectives, and whether the log-factor gaps to the deterministic optimum can be closed.
