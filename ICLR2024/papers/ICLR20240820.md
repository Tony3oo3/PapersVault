## Problem

Constrained bi-level optimization (BLO) problems with lower-level constraints coupling both upper- and lower-level variables are central to machine learning applications such as adversarial training and federated learning, yet existing gradient-based methods either rely on computationally expensive Hessian computations (implicit gradient approaches) or lack non-asymptotic convergence guarantees. The key open question is whether a Hessian-free, single-loop algorithm with provable convergence rates can be developed for this general constrained setting without requiring strong convexity of the lower-level objective.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes the UL objective F(x,y), LL objective f(x,y), and LL constraint mapping g(x,y) — where g explicitly depends on both UL variable x and LL variable y (the coupled-constraint setting) — along with closed convex feasible sets X⊆ℝⁿ and Y⊆ℝᵐ, initial points, and step-size sequences. It outputs the BLO solution (x*, y*) and a stationarity residual R_k that measures distance to the approximate KKT conditions of the penalized reformulation. Notably, the method requires only first-order gradient evaluations of f and g (no Hessian or Hessian-vector products), and the LL objective need only be convex (not strongly convex), accommodating non-singleton lower-level solution sets.

## Method

The core contribution is a proximal Lagrangian value function v_γ(x,y,z) defined as the optimal value of a strongly-convex-strongly-concave min-max problem that regularizes the LL Lagrangian with quadratic proximal terms in both the primal (θ) and dual (λ) variables. This yields a smooth single-level reformulation of the constrained BLO as a minimization of F(x,y) subject to the smooth constraint f(x,y) − v_γ(x,y,z) ≤ 0. The proposed LV-HBA algorithm performs, at each iteration, a single gradient descent-ascent (GDA) step on the min-max subproblem to obtain an approximate saddle point (θ^{k+1}, λ^{k+1}), then updates (x,y,z) via projected gradient steps using this saddle point as a proxy for the exact one in the gradient formula of v_γ. The strongly-convex-strongly-concave structure ensures the gradient approximation error is controllable with only first-order LL data, eliminating all Hessian computations. Non-asymptotic convergence is established with O(1/√K) rate for the saddle-point gap and O(1/K^{(1−2p)/2}) for the stationarity residual, under merely convex (not strongly convex) LL objectives.

## Results

On the SVM hyperparameter optimization task, LV-HBA achieves 75.07% accuracy on diabetes (vs. GAM's 74%) in 6.65s (vs. GAM's 33.06s, ~5× faster) and 75.4% on fourclass (vs. GAM's 75.2%) in 6s (vs. 30s). On the data hyper-cleaning task with gisette, LV-HBA reaches 94.6% accuracy (vs. GAM's 94.2%) in 100s (vs. 200s). In federated loss function tuning, LV-HBA requires only 65 communication rounds to reach 90% test accuracy, compared to 527 for E-AiPOD and 625 for FedNest (~8–10× fewer rounds).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LV-HBA consistently outperforms all three baselines across all experimental settings. Against GAM (Xu & Zhu, 2023) on SVM hyperparameter optimization, LV-HBA achieves higher accuracy (75.07% vs. 74% on diabetes; 75.4% vs. 75.2% on fourclass) while running approximately 5× faster (6.65s vs. 33.06s on diabetes). On data hyper-cleaning with gisette, LV-HBA edges out GAM in both accuracy (94.6% vs. 94.2%) and speed (100s vs. 200s). Against E-AiPOD and FedNest in federated loss function tuning, LV-HBA dramatically reduces communication rounds: 65 vs. 527 (E-AiPOD) and 625 (FedNest) for 90% test accuracy, and 588 vs. 2256 and 3010 for 94% accuracy. In synthetic experiments with merely convex LL objectives, LV-HBA converges faster and to a more precise solution than both AiPOD and E-AiPOD, which require strong convexity for convergence guarantees. The advantage is most pronounced in the federated setting, where LV-HBA's single-loop Hessian-free design translates to an order-of-magnitude reduction in communication cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the value function reformulation lineage initiated by Ye & Zhu (1995) and the Moreau envelope approach of Gao et al. (2023) by introducing a proximal Lagrangian value function that handles constrained lower-level problems with coupled UL-LL constraints. It departs from prior constrained BLO methods (IG-AL, SIGD, AiPOD, GAM) that all rely on implicit gradient computation requiring LL strong convexity and Hessian evaluations, and from BVFSM (Liu et al. 2023a) which is Hessian-free but requires sequential subproblem solving without non-asymptotic guarantees. The key conceptual move is embedding the LL constraints into a strongly-convex-strongly-concave proximal min-max problem whose value function is continuously differentiable, enabling a single-loop Hessian-free algorithm with only first-order LL data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By simultaneously achieving Hessian-free computation, single-loop structure, non-asymptotic convergence rates, and merely convex (not strongly convex) LL objectives, LV-HBA removes the two most restrictive assumptions in prior constrained BLO algorithms, making it directly applicable to ML settings like hyperparameter optimization and federated learning where Hessian computations are prohibitive and LL strong convexity is often violated. The non-singleton compatibility broadens applicability to problems with multiple LL optima. Open questions include stochastic extensions (the authors explicitly flag this as future work), scalability to very high-dimensional non-convex LL settings, and whether the penalty parameter schedule c_k = c(k+1)^p can be replaced by adaptive schemes. The empirical gains (e.g., ~8x fewer communication rounds in federated tuning) are encouraging but tested on moderate-scale problems.
