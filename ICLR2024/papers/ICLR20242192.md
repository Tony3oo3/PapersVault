## Problem

Book-length summarization (>100K tokens) requires chunking and combining partial summaries, yet this task has never been systematically evaluated due to three gaps: existing datasets like BookSum are contaminated in LLM pretraining data, prior evaluation methods fail to capture coherence errors specific to the chunk-and-combine setting, and no reliable automatic metric exists. This paper is the first to study the coherence of LLM-based book-length summarizers under different prompting workflows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242192/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The summarization pipeline takes a book-length document (average 190K tokens) and splits it into non-overlapping chunks that fit within the LLM's context window (e.g., 2048 or 88K tokens), producing a compressed summary of a target length G_n (900–1200 tokens). BOOOOKSCORE evaluation is both source-free and reference-free: it takes only the generated summary, iterates sentence-by-sentence, and outputs a scalar coherence score (proportion of error-free sentences, 0–100) along with per-sentence error annotations and type labels from an eight-category taxonomy.

## Method

The paper evaluates two zero-shot prompting strategies for book-length summarization: hierarchical merging (summarize each chunk, then recursively merge summaries in a tree until one remains) and incremental updating (iteratively update a running global summary chunk-by-chunk, compressing when it exceeds a length limit). For automatic evaluation, BOOOOKSCORE prompts GPT-4 with a few-shot error-identification prompt containing definitions and 42 annotated examples across eight coherence error types (entity omission, event omission, causal omission, discontinuity, salience, language, inconsistency, duplication). At each step, the LLM receives the full summary plus one target sentence and outputs either 'No confusion' or a question with an error type; the final score is the fraction of sentences flagged as error-free. Human validation confirms BOOOOKSCORE's precision (78.2%) matches that of human annotators (79.7%), enabling systematic evaluation of model, chunk size, and strategy choices at a fraction of the cost of human annotation.

## Results

On 100 recently-published books, Claude 2 (chunk 2048) achieves the highest BOOOOKSCORE of 91.1 under hierarchical merging, followed by GPT-4 (89.1) and GPT-3.5-Turbo (84.2). Under incremental updating, Claude 2 with an 88K chunk size reaches 90.9, while GPT-4 drops to 82.5 and LLaMA-2-7B-Instruct fails entirely. BOOOOKSCORE's precision (78.2%) closely matches human annotator precision (79.7%), and using human annotations in the formula yields nearly identical system-level scores (82.1 vs 82.4 for incremental; 89.4 vs 90.8 for hierarchical).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks BOOOOKSCORE against two existing reference-free metrics, BLANC and SUPERT, on all hierarchical summaries. Both metrics show nearly negligible differences across models (BLANC ranges from 0.013 to 0.025; SUPERT from 0.345 to 0.384), making them unable to distinguish model quality. Critically, both BLANC and SUPERT rate Claude 2 (88K chunk) summaries as inferior to GPT-3.5-Turbo (2048) summaries, directly contradicting qualitative analysis and BOOOOKSCORE results. In contrast, BOOOOKSCORE clearly separates models: Claude 2 (91.1) > GPT-4 (89.1) > GPT-3.5-Turbo (84.2) > Mixtral (81.5) > LLaMA-2 (72.4) under hierarchical merging, a 18.7-point spread versus less than 0.012 for BLANC. The authors conclude that existing reference-free metrics are inadequate for book-length summarization, motivating the need for BOOOOKSCORE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the hierarchical merging strategy from Wu et al. (2021) to zero-shot prompting and draws on chain-of-density prompting (Adams et al., 2023) for its incremental updating variant, both of which are already implemented in LangChain but have never been systematically compared. For evaluation, it synthesizes best practices from SNaC (Goyal et al., 2022a), LongEval (Krishna et al., 2023), and FActScore (Min et al., 2023), but deliberately departs from SNaC's taxonomy to capture error types unique to the chunk-and-combine book-length setting (causal omissions, salience errors). The LLM-as-judge paradigm from AlpacaEval and G-Eval is extended here to a fine-grained, sentence-level coherence evaluation that is both source-free and reference-free.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BOOOOKSCORE provides the first scalable, validated automatic metric for book-length summarization coherence, saving $15K and 500 hours of human evaluation and enabling systematic ablations over chunk size, prompting strategy, and base model. Key open questions remain: the paper does not evaluate faithfulness (only coherence), the error taxonomy is derived solely from GPT-4 and may not generalize to future models, and no recall validation was performed. The finding that coherence scores do not correlate with human preference judgments (detail vs. structure trade-off) suggests that a single scalar metric may be insufficient for user-facing summarization, pointing toward multi-dimensional evaluation as a necessary future direction.
