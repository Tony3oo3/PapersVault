## Problem

Existing dynamic novel view synthesis methods either learn a 6D plenoptic function without explicitly modeling scene motion (leading to parameter interference or missed correlations across spacetime) or explicitly model deformation of a fixed canonical structure (limiting scalability to complex real-world dynamics). No prior approach simultaneously achieves end-to-end training, real-time rendering, and high-fidelity photorealistic synthesis for complex dynamic scenes with volumetric effects and varying lighting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240365/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of calibrated video frames (multi-view or monocular) along with camera poses and an initial point cloud (from COLMAP for real scenes, or 100k random points for synthetic D-NeRF scenes). It produces a 4D Gaussian scene representation that can render photorealistic novel views at any arbitrary timestamp and camera position in real time (114 FPS on Plenoptic Video). A Gaussian filter with marginal p(t) < 0.05 is applied during rendering to limit each primitive's temporal influence, and the framework natively supports variable-length videos without per-frame retraining.

## Method

The core contribution is representing a dynamic scene as a collection of 4D Gaussians whose covariance is a full 4D ellipsoid parameterized by a 4D scaling vector and a 4D rotation decomposed into left and right isotropic quaternions. The 4D rotation is the key mechanism: it allows each Gaussian to tilt in spacetime, so that the conditional 3D mean μ_{xyz|t} traces a 3D trajectory over time, naturally encoding object motion without explicit deformation fields. View-dependent, time-evolving appearance is modeled via 4D Spherindrical Harmonics (4DSH), which factor as the product of 3D spherical harmonics and a 1D Fourier series over time. Rendering factorizes each 4D Gaussian into a conditional 3D Gaussian (projected to a 2D splat via the standard perspective Jacobian) and a marginal 1D temporal Gaussian, then accumulates color and opacity with a tile-based rasterizer. Training is fully end-to-end on the entire video using only rendering loss, with spacetime-aware densification that splits Gaussians in both spatial and temporal dimensions.

## Results

On the Plenoptic Video benchmark (real multi-view), 4DGS achieves 32.01 PSNR, 0.014 DSSIM, and 0.055 LPIPS at 114 FPS, outperforming the best prior method HexPlane (31.70 PSNR, 0.075 LPIPS) and the closest 4DGS variant by Wu et al. (31.02 PSNR, 36 FPS). On the D-NeRF monocular synthetic benchmark, 4DGS reaches 34.09 PSNR and 0.02 LPIPS, surpassing V4D (33.72 PSNR) and 4DGS by Wu et al. (33.30 PSNR). The method is the only one to achieve real-time rendering (>30 FPS) while delivering the highest visual quality on both benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the Plenoptic Video benchmark, 4DGS (Ours) wins on all three quality metrics against every baseline: it surpasses HexPlane (31.70 PSNR, 0.075 LPIPS) by +0.31 PSNR and −0.020 LPIPS, K-Planes-hybrid (31.63 PSNR) by +0.38 PSNR, and the closest 4DGS variant by Wu et al. (31.02 PSNR, 0.150 LPIPS) by +0.99 PSNR and −0.095 LPIPS. In terms of speed, 4DGS (Ours) renders at 114 FPS, roughly 3× faster than 4DGS (Wu et al.) at 36 FPS and 7× faster than MixVoxels-L at 16.7 FPS, while all NeRF-based methods (DyNeRF, HexPlane, K-Planes, NeRFPlayer, HyperReel) operate below 2 FPS. On the D-NeRF monocular benchmark, 4DGS (Ours) achieves 34.09 PSNR and 0.02 LPIPS, edging out V4D (33.72 PSNR, 0.02 LPIPS) by +0.37 PSNR and 4DGS by Wu et al. (33.30 PSNR, 0.03 LPIPS) by +0.79 PSNR and −0.01 LPIPS. The method also outperforms TiNeuVox (32.67 PSNR) and all K-Planes variants (31.05–31.61 PSNR) by a clear margin, confirming consistent superiority across both multi-view real and monocular synthetic settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) from static to dynamic scenes by formulating a coherent 4D Gaussian primitive with full 4D rotation (parameterized by a pair of quaternions) rather than treating space and time independently. It departs from prior dynamic scene methods in two key ways: (1) unlike plenoptic-function approaches (HexPlane, K-Planes, DyNeRF) that learn a 6D function without explicit motion, and (2) unlike deformation-based methods (D-NeRF, NeRFPlayer, Deformable 3D Gaussians) that assume a canonical representation with learned warping, this work treats the spatio-temporal volume as a unified 4D entity where 4D rotation naturally induces 3D displacement. The introduction of 4D Spherindrical Harmonics (SH × Fourier series) for time-evolving view-dependent appearance is a novel component not present in prior Gaussian-based or NeRF-based dynamic scene methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to achieve real-time (114 FPS) photorealistic novel view synthesis of complex dynamic scenes with volumetric effects, which is practically significant for AR/VR and autonomous driving applications. The end-to-end training paradigm (processing entire videos without frame-by-frame or multi-stage optimization) simplifies the pipeline and enables variable-length video handling. Key limitations include difficulty reconstructing distant backgrounds without adequate point cloud initialization, and the method has not been tested on very long videos or scenes with significant occlusion changes. Open questions include whether the 4D representation scales gracefully to longer temporal extents and whether the approach can handle scenes where objects enter or leave the field of view.
