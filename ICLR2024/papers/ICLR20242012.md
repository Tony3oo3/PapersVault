## Problem

Learning equilibria in non-stationary multi-agent systems with bandit feedback is fundamentally harder than its single-agent counterpart: testing whether a small gap has emerged incurs O(1) regret per test (since unilateral deviations can be O(1) even when the gap is tiny), non-uniqueness of equilibria breaks replay-based change detection, and the bandit feedback setting precludes gradient-based online learning. No prior work provides a general, parameter-free algorithm with sublinear dynamic regret for arbitrary non-stationary games under bandit feedback.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a non-stationary Markov game environment (state space S, m players with action spaces A_i, horizon H, T episodes) where only the reward of the chosen joint action is revealed (bandit feedback). It requires two black-box oracles with PAC guarantees: LEARN_EQ (learns an ε-equilibrium in a stationary game with C_1(ε) samples) and TEST_EQ (certifies whether a given policy is an ε-equilibrium with C_2(ε) samples). The output is a sequence of joint Markov policies achieving dynamic equilibrium regret of Õ(Δ^{1/4}T^{3/4}) when the total-variation budget Δ is known, and Õ(Δ^{1/5}T^{4/5}) when it is unknown, while inheriting the base oracle's dependence on the number of agents.

## Method

The core contribution is a black-box reduction that wraps any stationary equilibrium learner into a non-stationary one. When the non-stationarity budget is known, Algorithm 1 alternates between running LEARN_EQ and committing to the learned policy for a tuned interval T_1, balancing learning cost against drift. When the budget is unknown, Algorithm 2 uses a doubling-block structure with multi-scale testing: within each block of length 2^n, TEST_EQ is invoked at geometrically spaced gap thresholds ε(q)=√(c_2/2^q) with probability inversely proportional to the gap, so larger changes are detected faster. The TEST_EQ oracle itself is constructed by reducing equilibrium testing to single-agent RL: for NE/CCE, it learns the best response in the induced MDP; for CE, it constructs an extended MDP M' with state space S×A where the optimal policy corresponds exactly to the best strategy modification, enabling a clean reduction. The scheduling is specifically designed for PAC (rather than regret) assumptions, differing from Wei & Luo (2021), and the regret analysis partitions each block into near-stationary intervals with a modified threshold Δ_{I_k}=O(max{|I_k|^{-1/2}, 2^{-n/4}}) that yields tighter bounds than prior work.

## Results

The paper provides the first black-box dynamic regret guarantees for non-stationary multi-agent reinforcement learning with bandit feedback. When the non-stationarity budget (total variation Δ) is known, Algorithm 1 achieves Õ(Δ^{1/4} T^{3/4}) regret; when Δ is unknown, Algorithm 2 achieves Õ(Δ^{1/5} T^{4/5}) regret. For the switching-number L measure, both algorithms achieve Õ(L^{1/4} T^{3/4}) regret. Concretely, for general-sum Markov games (CCE), the regret is (H^7 S^3 A_max Δ)^{1/4} T^{3/4}, and for zero-sum Markov games (NE), it is (H^7 S(A+B) Δ)^{1/4} T^{3/4}.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or dataset evaluations. The contribution is the first black-box reduction framework for non-stationary MARL with bandit feedback, so there is no prior method to directly benchmark against. The paper positions itself relative to Wei & Luo (2021), whose single-agent multi-scale testing framework (MALG) is adapted but does not extend to the multi-agent setting due to non-uniqueness of equilibria and the incompatibility of bandit feedback with gradient-based testing. It also contrasts with Anagnostides et al. (2023) and Cardoso et al. (2019), which handle non-stationary multi-agent games but only under full-information feedback or with different regret notions (NE-regret vs. dynamic equilibrium regret). The key advantage over prior single-agent non-stationary RL results is that the approach inherits the favorable dependence on the number of agents from the base oracle (e.g., breaking the curse of multi-agents), and it is the first to handle bandit feedback in the multi-agent non-stationary setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the black-box multi-scale testing paradigm of Wei & Luo (2021) from non-stationary single-agent RL to non-stationary multi-agent games with bandit feedback, a setting where the standard test-based and online-learning approaches from single-agent non-stationary RL break down. The key departure is identifying that bandit feedback makes testing an arbitrarily small equilibrium gap incur O(1) regret per test (unlike single-agent bandits), and that non-uniqueness of equilibria invalidates replay-based anomaly detection. The approach composes with existing stationary MARL oracles (zero-sum, general-sum, potential, congestion, Markov games) via PAC-guaranteed LEARN_EQ and TEST_EQ interfaces, inheriting their favorable dependence on the number of agents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of non-stationary MARL with bandit feedback across multiple game types, providing a versatile black-box framework that will automatically benefit from future improvements in stationary MARL algorithms. The paper leaves two important open questions: designing no-regret oracles (where each agent achieves no-regret regardless of others' policies) and establishing lower bounds for dynamic regret in non-stationary multi-agent systems. The practical motivation (e.g., traffic routing with navigation) is concrete, though the current guarantees require knowledge of or adaptation to the non-stationarity budget, and the approach is purely theoretical without empirical validation.
