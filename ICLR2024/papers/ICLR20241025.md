## Problem

Existing text-guided NeRF editing methods suffer from slow training due to full-resolution rendering requirements and from loss functions (notably SDS) that cause color saturation, mode-seeking, and failure to preserve source structure during local edits. There is no efficient approach that combines fast latent-space optimization with an editing-aware loss that respects both the target prompt and the original scene geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241025/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view source images (encoded to 64×64×4 latent tensors via the Stable Diffusion VAE), a target text prompt, a source text prompt, and a binary mask (generated by CLIPSeg or SAM and resized to 64×64) as inputs. It produces an edited TensoRF-based NeRF scene whose parameters can be decoded through the Stable Diffusion VAE decoder to render 512×512 RGB novel views. Operating at 64×64 latent resolution rather than 512×512 pixel resolution is the key efficiency constraint that reduces GPU memory to 8 GB and fine-tuning time to ~14 minutes.

## Method

ED-NeRF trains a TensoRF backbone to predict 4-channel latent features (rather than RGB) via volume rendering, supervised by VAE-encoded source latents. A refinement layer (4 ResNet blocks + 2 self-attention blocks, mirroring the VAE decoder front and encoder rear architectures) is appended to the rendered latent map to recover the inter-pixel interactions that the VAE encoder introduces but that per-ray NeRF rendering lacks. For editing, the paper extends Delta Denoising Score (DDS) to 3D by computing the difference between two SDS gradients—one conditioned on the target prompt applied to the rendered edited latent, and one conditioned on the source prompt applied to the original source latent—thereby canceling the pure-noise component that causes SDS artifacts. A binary mask is applied pixel-wise to the DDS gradient to restrict updates to the target region, and a masked reconstruction loss (with λ_om=100 outside the mask and λ_im=0.01 inside) prevents unwanted deformations beyond the mask boundary. The total editing objective jointly updates both NeRF parameters θ and refinement layer parameters φ for 5k steps.

## Results

ED-NeRF achieves the best CLIP Directional Score of 0.2265, outperforming the strongest baseline Instruct-NeRF2NeRF (0.2053) by ~10%. In the user study (5-point scale), ED-NeRF scores 3.88 on text-guidance and 4.09 on preservation, substantially ahead of all baselines (best: 3.29 and 3.08 respectively), while achieving 3.64 on view consistency (second-best after NeRF-ART's 3.79). In terms of efficiency, ED-NeRF requires only 14 minutes of fine-tuning and 8 GB GPU memory, compared to 90 minutes and 15 GB for Instruct-NeRF2NeRF, and 6–15 minutes at 17–18 GB for CLIP-based methods (which must downsample to 252×189).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ED-NeRF outperforms all four baselines on CLIP Directional Score, text-guidance, and preservation metrics. Against CLIP-NeRF (0.1648) and Mask SDS (0.1409), the gains are most pronounced, with ED-NeRF achieving 0.2265 and 0.2265 respectively, reflecting the superiority of diffusion-based editing over CLIP-based guidance. Compared to Instruct-NeRF2NeRF (0.2053), ED-NeRF leads by 0.021 in CLIP Directional Score and by a large margin in preservation (4.09 vs. 3.08), while also being ~6× faster (14 min vs. 90 min) and using less memory (8 GB vs. 15 GB). The only metric where ED-NeRF trails a baseline is view consistency (3.64 vs. NeRF-ART's 3.79), though it still exceeds Instruct-NeRF2NeRF (3.28) and Mask SDS (3.56). Overall, ED-NeRF achieves the best or near-best score on every metric while being the most efficient method in both time and memory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Latent-NeRF (Metzer et al., 2023), which first trained NeRF in the latent space of a diffusion model but only for unsupervised virtual asset generation, to real-world scene reconstruction by introducing a refinement layer that compensates for the lack of inter-pixel interactions in NeRF's per-ray rendering. It further generalizes the 2D Delta Denoising Score (DDS) editing formulation (Hertz et al., 2023) into the 3D domain, defining the editing gradient as the difference between two SDS scores conditioned on target and source prompts. The method departs from prior CLIP-based editing (CLIP-NeRF, NeRF-ART) and image-translation-based editing (InstructNeRF2NeRF) by operating entirely in the 64×64 latent space, eliminating the need for full-resolution rendering during fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical efficiency gains are significant for practitioners: 8 GB GPU memory and ~14 minutes of fine-tuning versus 15–18 GB and up to 90 minutes for competing methods, making text-guided 3D editing accessible on consumer hardware. The refinement layer analysis—showing that latent-space pixel interactions from ResNet and self-attention layers must be explicitly modeled for faithful reconstruction—provides a useful diagnostic for anyone working with latent NeRF on real scenes. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more semantically distant edits (the paper acknowledges failure on prompts like 'statue → black hole'), whether improved diffusion backbones would resolve these limitations, and whether the binary mask requirement can be relaxed for fully autonomous editing.
