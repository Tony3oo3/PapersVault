## Problem

Despite the apparent simplicity of recognizing physically grounded spatial relations (e.g., 'cup on saucer'), no existing computer vision method has convincingly outperformed a naive bounding-box-only baseline on this task. Prior work focused on semantically defined relations that suffer from linguistic bias, and existing benchmarks (Rel3D, SpatialSense) are either synthetic or suffer from annotation inconsistencies due to ambiguous relation definitions. The paper asks whether transformer architectures, with their explicit pairwise attention, can finally exploit visual information to predict precise spatial relations in-the-wild.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241405/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 224×224 RGB image, two 4-dimensional bounding box coordinates (subject and object), and a relation predicate as input, and produces a binary True/False prediction for whether that specific relation holds between the two objects. The task is formulated as K-way binary classification (one prediction head per relation class), supporting the multi-label property where multiple relations can hold simultaneously. The proposed RelatiViT architecture does not use object category embeddings (300-dim GloVe vectors), relying solely on visual and spatial information.

## Method

RelatiViT is an end-to-end ViT-based architecture that localizes subject and object queries by masking the image to create three views: the full context image, a subject-only masked image, and an object-only masked image. Patch embeddings from all three images are concatenated into a single long sequence and fed through a shared ViT-Base encoder (IBOT-pretrained), where self-attention simultaneously performs context aggregation (queries attend to surrounding scene) and pair interaction (subject tokens attend to object tokens) without any separate decoder or RoI Align step. A max-pooling layer then produces relation-grounded embeddings for each query, which are fed into a binary classifier trained with cross-entropy loss. The key design insight is that the unified sequence formulation lets the transformer's long-range attention naturally model pairwise spatial relationships across all encoder layers, outperforming cascading architectures (RegionViT, CNNTransformer, CrossAttnViT) that separate feature extraction, context aggregation, and pair interaction into distinct modules.

## Results

RelatiViT achieves 80.09% Avg. Acc. and 82.05% F1 on Rel3D, and 80.02% Avg. Acc. and 78.32% F1 on SpatialSense+, making it the first method to convincingly outperform the naive bbox-only baseline (74.35% / 77.55% Avg. Acc.) on both datasets. The margin over the strongest existing method (RUNet at 75.03% on Rel3D and 75.81% on SpatialSense+) is approximately 5 percentage points. Even the most advanced Vision Language Models fall well short, with GPT-4V reaching only 68.26% Avg. Acc. on SpatialSense+.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RelatiViT outperforms all existing spatial relation prediction methods on both Rel3D and SpatialSense+. Among the 'vision+bbox+class' group, RUNet is the strongest baseline (75.03% on Rel3D, 75.81% on SpatialSense+), yet RelatiViT exceeds it by ~5.1 and ~4.2 percentage points respectively; DRNet, VTransE, and MotifsNet all perform at or below the bbox-only baseline, suggesting shortcut learning toward bounding box coordinates. The 'vision'-only methods (Vip-CNN, PPR-FCN) perform worst among existing approaches, confirming that CNN backbones struggle to extract relation-grounded representations. Among Vision Language Models evaluated only on SpatialSense+, GPT-4V achieves the highest score (68.26% Avg. Acc.) but still trails RelatiViT by over 11.7 points, while MiniGPT-4, LLaVA, and Gemini score between 50–59%. Notably, RelatiViT uses only visual features (no bounding box or class inputs), yet surpasses methods that explicitly incorporate bounding box coordinates and object categories, demonstrating that the end-to-end ViT architecture with self-attention is the critical factor for capturing physically grounded spatial relations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant CNN-based paradigm for spatial relation prediction (RoI Align + MLP classifiers as in DRNet, MotifsNet, RUNet) by systematically exploring transformer architectures, arguing that the attention mechanism's explicit pairwise modeling is architecturally aligned with the SRP task. It also carves new ground on the benchmark side: while Rel3D (synthetic) and SpatialSense (real but inconsistently annotated) existed, the paper introduces SpatialSense+ with precise, physically grounded relation definitions that eliminate the linguistic biases and object-centric/viewer-centric ambiguities of prior datasets. The work repurposes the ViT encoder (pretrained via IBOT) not as a generic feature extractor feeding a downstream head, but as an end-to-end relation model where subject, object, and context tokens interact through self-attention across all layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to convincingly surpass the naive bbox-only baseline on in-the-wild spatial relation prediction, resolving a long-standing failure mode that prior works had accepted as inherent. The finding that even GPT-4V, Gemini, LLaVA, and MiniGPT-4 perform poorly (50–68% avg accuracy) on this task highlights a significant gap in current VLM spatial reasoning capabilities, making SpatialSense+ a useful diagnostic benchmark for future multi-modal models. For robotics and manipulation planning, where physically grounded spatial understanding is essential, the RelatiViT architecture and its design principles (end-to-end ViT, masked-image query localization, unified self-attention for context and pair interaction) provide a concrete starting point. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to 3D/point-cloud settings, whether it scales to open-vocabulary relations, and whether the ~80% ceiling reflects a fundamental limit of 2D image information or a remaining architectural gap.
