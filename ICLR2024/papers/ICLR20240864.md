## Problem

Generating stable inorganic crystal structures with generative models requires handling both discrete (element identities) and continuous (3D coordinates) data while respecting translational and permutation symmetries. Prior approaches either build crystal-specific architectures with hard-coded invariance constraints (e.g., CDVAE) or train language models from scratch, forgoing the sample efficiency and text-conditioning flexibility of pretrained LLMs. This paper asks whether parameter-efficient fine-tuning of a general-purpose LLM on text-encoded crystals can match or exceed dedicated generative models in producing physically valid, energetically stable materials.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a text prompt (optionally specifying a target composition, space group, or stability class) followed by, for infilling, a partial crystal string where one element is replaced by [MASK]. Crystals are encoded as newline-separated strings with lattice lengths at one decimal place, lattice angles as integers, and fractional coordinates at two decimal places; LLaMA-2's BPE tokenizer splits all numbers into individual digit tokens rather than multi-digit substrings. The model outputs either a full crystal string (parseable into a CIF file) or a single element symbol for the masked position.

## Method

The core method fine-tunes LLaMA-2 (7B, 13B, 70B) via LoRA (rank 8, alpha 32) on ~45K–128K crystal strings from the Materials Project, using a multitask curriculum that randomly samples between unconditional/text-conditional generation (2/3 of batches) and element infilling (1/3). Translational invariance is encouraged through random uniform shifts of fractional coordinates during training, while permutation augmentation is deliberately omitted to preserve informative atom ordering. No crystal-specific tokens or architectural modifications are introduced; the model relies entirely on LLaMA-2's pretrained digit-level tokenization and general compression biases to learn coordinate-level symmetries. The paper introduces the IPT (Increase in Perplexity under Transformation) metric to quantify learned translational invariance and shows that larger models achieve lower IPT, indicating that scale improves symmetry learning from augmentations alone.

## Results

The strongest model, fine-tuned LLaMA-2 70B at temperature τ=0.7, achieves 49.8% metastable rate (M3GNet E_hull < 0.1 eV/atom) and 10.6% DFT-verified stable rate (E_hull < 0.0 eV/atom), roughly doubling CDVAE's 28.8% and 5.4% respectively. Structural validity reaches 99.6% and compositional validity 95.4% at this setting, with ~90% of all sampled structures obeying physical constraints on atom positions and charges. The 13B model at τ=0.7 also shows strong DFT stability (14.4%), while the 7B model achieves 35.1% metastable rate at τ=1.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CDVAE (Xie et al., 2021), LLaMA-2 70B at τ=0.7 substantially outperforms on the key stability metrics: 49.8% vs 28.8% metastable rate (M3GNet) and 10.6% vs 5.4% DFT-verified stable rate, while also improving compositional validity (0.954 vs 0.867) and matching near-perfect structural validity (0.996 vs 1.000). CDVAE retains a slight edge in recall (0.991 vs 0.858), reflecting the coverage cost of lower-temperature sampling. Against the from-scratch language model baselines LM-CH and LM-AC (Flam-Shepherd & Aspuru-Guzik, 2023), LLaMA-2 models achieve higher structural validity (0.996 vs 0.848/0.958) and compositional validity (0.954 vs 0.835/0.889), though LM-AC reports a lower wdist(N_el) (0.09 vs 0.44), indicating better match to the training distribution's element-count statistics. Notably, the LM baselines do not report stability metrics, making direct stability comparison impossible, but the LLaMA-2 models' 35–50% metastable rates demonstrate a clear advantage in generating physically meaningful structures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the observation by Flam-Shepherd & Aspuru-Guzik (2023) that language models can generate molecular structures as text, but departs from their from-scratch training by instead fine-tuning pretrained LLaMA-2 models with LoRA adapters. It contrasts with the dominant diffusion-based approach (CDVAE and successors) that enforces crystal symmetries through architectural invariance/equivariance, showing that data augmentation plus pretrained LLM inductive biases can achieve comparable or better symmetry learning without purpose-built architectures. The use of LLaMA-2's digit-level BPE tokenization is a practical choice that avoids the crystal-specific tokenizers used by prior LM-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple, general-purpose fine-tuning recipe (PEFT on string-encoded crystals) can match or exceed purpose-built generative models for crystal stability, opening the door to multitask capabilities (unconditional generation, text-conditional generation, infilling) within a single model and to future multimodal training on scientific text. The proposed IPT metric provides a new way to quantify symmetry learning in discrete-token models. Key limitations include modest absolute DFT-verified stability rates (~10%), sensitivity to tokenization and prompt design, hallucination of non-existent elements, and the high compute cost of the 70B model; open questions include whether classifier-free guidance or Bayesian optimization can improve conditional generation and whether the approach generalizes beyond inorganic crystals.
