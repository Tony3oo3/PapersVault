## Problem

Deep vision models produce features at very low spatial resolution (e.g., 7×7 from a 224×224 input in ResNet-50, or 14×14 in ViT-S/16) because they aggressively pool information over large receptive fields, making dense prediction tasks like segmentation and depth estimation difficult. Existing upsampling approaches—bilinear interpolation, resize-convolutions, deconvolutions, and task-specific modules—either blur spatial detail, incur steep computational costs at large upsampling factors, or transform the feature space rather than faithfully recovering lost resolution.

## Input / Output

FeatUp takes a frozen backbone's low-resolution feature map (e.g., 14×14×384 from ViT-S/16) together with the original 224×224 RGB input image as guidance, and produces a high-resolution feature map (224×224) in the same semantic space as the original features, enabling drop-in replacement without retraining downstream probes. The implicit variant can be queried at arbitrary resolution beyond the input image size, while the JBU variant operates as a single forward pass. The input image is consumed both as a high-frequency spatial guide for the bilateral filter and as the source of Fourier color features in the implicit network.

## Method

FeatUp's core insight, drawn from NeRF's multi-view consistency, is that high-resolution features can be reconstructed by enforcing consistency across multiple low-resolution 'views' obtained by jittering the input image (pads, zooms, flips) and extracting features from each. A learned downsampler (either a normalized blur kernel or an attention-based spatially-adaptive kernel) maps the predicted high-res features back to low-res, and a Gaussian likelihood loss with spatially-varying adaptive uncertainty supervises reconstruction across all views. Two upsampler architectures are provided: (1) a feedforward stack of learned Joint Bilateral Upsamplers with a novel CUDA kernel that is up to 10× faster and uses two orders of magnitude less memory than PyTorch baselines, and (2) a small 3-layer MLP with Fourier positional and color features overfit per-image to represent features at arbitrary resolution. The JBU variant generalizes across images and adds only ~0.16M parameters, while the implicit variant uses over two orders of magnitude fewer parameters than an explicit 224×224 feature buffer.

## Results

On linear probe transfer learning with frozen ViT-S/16 features upsampled 16× (14×14 → 224×224), FeatUp(Implicit) achieves 47.37 mIoU on COCO-Stuff segmentation and 1.04 RMSE on depth estimation, while FeatUp(JBU) achieves 43.41 mIoU and 1.09 RMSE. For CAM quality on ImageNet, FeatUp(Implicit) attains the best Average Drop (8.84) and Average Increase (5.60), outperforming all baselines. In end-to-end SegFormer training on ADE20K, FeatUp(JBU) reaches 44.2 mIoU with only +0.16M parameters and +1.70 GFLOPs, surpassing all competing upsamplers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FeatUp consistently outperforms all seven baselines across every evaluated task. Against the strongest forward-pass upsampler CARAFE (mIoU 42.39, RMSE 1.09), FeatUp(JBU) gains +1.02 mIoU and matches RMSE while improving CAM A.D. from 10.24 to 9.83; FeatUp(Implicit) extends this to +4.98 mIoU and 1.04 RMSE. SAPA (mIoU 41.17) and Resize-conv (mIoU 42.95) trail FeatUp(JBU) by 2.24 and 0.46 mIoU respectively. In end-to-end SegFormer training on ADE20K, FeatUp(JBU) (44.2 mIoU) beats FADE (43.6), CARAFE (42.4), IndexNet (41.5), A2U (41.5), and Bilinear (39.7) while adding fewer parameters (+0.16M) than IndexNet (+12.6M) and FADE (+0.29M). DIP, an inference-time implicit method, underperforms even Bilinear on segmentation (39.86 vs 42.40 mIoU), confirming that image super-resolution priors do not transfer to high-dimensional feature maps. The Strided and Large Image baselines degrade severely at 16× upsampling (mIoU 40.54 and 36.44) and become computationally infeasible beyond 8×.

## Relation to Prior Work

FeatUp draws a direct conceptual analogy to NeRF's multi-view consistency principle, repurposing it from 3D scene reconstruction to the problem of upsampling 2D feature maps by enforcing consistency across jittered views of low-resolution features. The JBU variant generalizes the 2007 Joint Bilateral Upsampling filter to high-dimensional signals with a learnable MLP-based range kernel, while the implicit variant adapts NeRF-style implicit neural representations with Fourier positional and color features. The paper positions itself against a broad family of learned upsamplers (CARAFE, SAPA, FADE, IndexNet, A2U) and image super-resolution methods (DIP, LIIF, ZSSR), arguing that these either blur features, are computationally intractable at large upsampling factors, or are not designed for high-dimensional feature spaces. A key practical departure is the contribution of an efficient CUDA kernel for the spatially adaptive JBU operation, which is orders of magnitude faster and less memory-intensive than existing PyTorch implementations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FeatUp addresses a fundamental bottleneck in dense prediction: deep backbones sacrifice spatial resolution for semantic quality, and existing upsampling methods either blur features or are computationally infeasible at large factors. The drop-in nature (no retraining of downstream models) and model-agnosticism (works with ViT, ResNet, DINO, CLIP, DeepLabV3) make it broadly applicable, and the CUDA kernel removes a practical barrier to using JBU in large-scale pipelines. Limitations include halo artifacts in the implicit variant and reduced fine-detail sensitivity in the JBU variant, and the paper does not explore 3D features, video, or extremely high upsampling factors beyond 16x. An open question is whether the multi-view consistency principle can be extended to other modalities or to jointly learning the backbone and upsampler.
