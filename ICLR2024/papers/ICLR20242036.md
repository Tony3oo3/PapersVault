## Problem

Non-watertight 3D surfaces (clothing, paper, leaves) are underexplored in reconstruction and generative modeling because existing methods either handle only watertight meshes via SDF or rely on unsigned distance fields (UDF) whose zero-levelset extraction is non-trivial and error-prone. No prior representation simultaneously supports fast differentiable rasterization with material/lighting optimization and diffusion-based generative modeling for open surfaces of arbitrary topology.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242036/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For reconstruction, the method consumes 72 multiview RGB images (512×512) with binary segmentation masks rendered under realistic PBR lighting, and jointly outputs a non-watertight or watertight mesh, per-vertex PBR material parameters (albedo, roughness, metalness), and an environment lightmap. For generation, it takes Gaussian noise and produces a full G-SHELL grid (SDF values, grid node offsets, and mSDF interpolation coefficients) from which a mesh is extracted. The grid operates at 128 resolution for tetrahedral grids or 80 for FlexiCubes, and the mSDF is stored on the same 3D grid as the SDF rather than on a separate surface mesh.

## Method

G-SHELL parameterizes general 3D shapes by jointly learning a watertight template (via SDF on a deformable 3D grid) and a manifold signed distance field (mSDF) defined in 3D space, where the mSDF sign encodes whether a point lies on the open surface and its magnitude gives geodesic distance to the boundary. A key efficiency gain comes from a modified Marching-Cubes-like look-up table that enumerates all SDF-sign and mSDF-sign configurations per cell, enabling direct extraction of non-watertight meshes in a single pass without the costly two-stage isosurface-to-isoline pipeline. For reconstruction, the extracted mesh is rendered with Nvdiffrecmc's occlusion-aware differentiable rasterizer, allowing end-to-end joint optimization of geometry, material, and lighting from pixel supervision plus a custom mSDF regularization that encourages hole opening while preventing holes from becoming too large. For generation, G-MeshDiffusion extends MeshDiffusion's 3D U-Net to simultaneously predict SDF values, grid deformations, and bounded interpolation coefficients α∈[0,1] (derived from mSDF) on a 2R-resolution cubic grid, avoiding the scale-sensitivity issues of raw mSDF values.

## Results

On DeepFashion3D-v2 non-watertight garment reconstruction, G-SHELL (tetrahedral grid) achieves a mean PSNR of 33.04 dB and a bi-directional Chamfer distance of 0.177 cm, outperforming all baselines. The FlexiCubes variant (G-SHELL FC) further improves PSNR to 33.28 dB and Chamfer to 0.203 cm. For unconditional mesh generation on Cloth3D, G-MeshDiffusion achieves the best MMD (1.05/1.36 ×10⁻³ for upper/lower garments) and MV-FID (88.98/79.01) scores, substantially outperforming watertight-only baselines MeshDiffusion and GET3D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

G-SHELL outperforms all seven baselines on both PSNR and Chamfer distance for non-watertight mesh reconstruction. Against the closest UDF-based competitor NeUDF (31.07 PSNR, 0.286 cm Chamfer), G-SHELL gains +1.97 dB PSNR and reduces Chamfer by 0.109 cm. Against the watertight SDF baseline NeuS (30.29 PSNR, 0.770 cm), the improvement is +2.75 dB and −0.593 cm, with NeuS failing to reconstruct inner clothing surfaces. Against Nvdiffrecmc with DMTet (31.68 PSNR, 0.662 cm), G-SHELL improves by +1.36 dB and −0.485 cm, showing the benefit of native non-watertight modeling over forcing a watertight template. NeAT performs worst overall (27.67 PSNR, 0.534 cm). For generation, G-MeshDiffusion beats MeshDiffusion by 0.54/0.83 ×10⁻³ on MMD-CD and by 102/112 on MV-FID (lower/upper garments), and beats GET3D by 0.21/0.32 ×10⁻³ on MMD-CD and by 16.7/23.5 on MV-FID, confirming that native non-watertight generation captures single-sided geometry far better than watertight-only models.

## Relation to Prior Work

G-SHELL builds directly on the deformable tetrahedral grid representation of DMTet and the Marching Tetrahedra extraction framework, but introduces a fundamentally new parameterization: a manifold signed distance field (mSDF) defined on the watertight template surface, where the sign encodes whether a point belongs to the open surface. This departs from the dominant UDF-based approach (NeuralUDF, NeUDF, MeshUDF) for non-watertight meshes, which requires non-trivial workarounds for isosurface extraction and is sensitive to noise. For generation, the paper extends MeshDiffusion's 3D U-Net diffusion architecture by adding an mSDF interpolation coefficient channel, and for reconstruction it leverages Nvdiffrecmc's differentiable rasterization rather than the volumetric rendering used by implicit baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

G-SHELL is the first representation to enable both differentiable rasterization-based reconstruction (with joint geometry, material, and lighting optimization) and diffusion-model-based unconditional generation for non-watertight meshes, closing a significant gap where prior methods could only handle one or the other. The ~6× training speedup and ~40× inference speedup over UDF-based baselines make non-watertight mesh reconstruction practically viable. Key limitations remain: the representation cannot model self-intersecting or non-orientable surfaces, requires higher grid resolution for tiny topological holes, and the generation pipeline is constrained by 3D U-Net memory at high resolutions. Open questions include whether more efficient architectures (triplane features, latent diffusion) can scale G-SHELL generation, and whether indirect illumination modeling can be integrated without breaking the optimization landscape.
