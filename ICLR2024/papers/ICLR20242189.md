## Problem

While DNN quantization has successfully reduced the precision of weights and activations, the accumulation step in multiply-accumulate (MAC) operations still relies on high-precision (FP32/FP16) data types, making it the dominant computational bottleneck in modern hardware. Prior attempts to use low-precision accumulators caused significant accuracy degradation, leaving a gap between the quantized multiplication cost and the unquantized accumulation cost that this paper closes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242189/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-trained full-precision DNNs (ResNets, BERT, LLaMA-2-7B) and fine-tunes them so that all forward GEMM operations (convolutions and matrix multiplications) use a custom FMAq operation with quantized product and accumulator in floating-point formats (e.g., M7E4 for 12-bit, M4E3 for 8-bit), with a fixed chunk size of 16. Inputs are standard RGB images (ImageNet 224×224, CIFAR-10, MNIST) and text sequences (SQUAD, MMLU, MLM over OSCAR), and outputs are the corresponding task predictions (top-1 class, span answer, multiple-choice selection). Weights and activations can optionally be quantized to FP8 (M4E3 with flex-bias) alongside the low-bit accumulator.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces each FMA with FMAq(x,w,s) = Q_acc(Q_prod(x·w) + s), where Q_prod and Q_acc are floating-point quantizers using a 'floor' operation (not stochastic rounding) to remain hardware-efficient, with the accumulator given a smaller exponent bias (b_acc = b_prod − ½·log₂(chunk-size)) to reduce overflow risk. For 12-bit accumulators, the paper proposes a two-stage fine-tuning: first optimize with underflow events disabled (so small values are not forced to zero), then enable underflow and fine-tune briefly, which avoids the 'stuck-at-zero' trap that prevents small weights from escaping the underflow region. For bit-widths below 12 bits, the paper introduces fine-grained gradient estimation that backpropagates through the entire recursive accumulation graph, using novel STE variants (Immediate/DIFF, Immediate/OF, Recursive/OF) that zero out gradients only on overflow, underflow, or full-swamping events rather than on every quantization step. The recommended Immediate/DIFF STE is agnostic to the internal FMAq implementation, making it applicable to black-box hardware, and achieves near-baseline accuracy even with 8-bit (M4E3) accumulators on fully-connected and attention layers.

## Results

On ImageNet classification with FP8 weights/activations and 12-bit (M7E4) accumulators, the dual-stage fine-tuning method achieves 69.70% top-1 for ResNet18, 73.42% for ResNet34, and 76.22% for ResNet50, within 0.2–0.5% of the FP8 baseline (69.90%, 73.49%, 76.25% respectively). On SQUAD v1, LBA BERT-Base and BERT-Large show negligible f1 degradation (−0.09% and +0.26% vs. full-precision baselines). For below-12-bit accumulators, fine-grained STEs (Immediate/DIFF) enable an 8-bit (M4E3) fully-connected network to reach 97.67% on MNIST, compared to 18.28% with naive identity STE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Wang et al. (2018), which uses 16-bit FP accumulators with chunk-based accumulation and stochastic rounding, the proposed 12-bit LBA method achieves substantially higher accuracy: 69.70% vs. 66.95% on ResNet18 (+2.75%) and 76.22% vs. 71.72% on ResNet50 (+4.50%), while also using fewer accumulator bits. Against Ni et al. (2020) (WrapNet), which uses 12-bit integer accumulators with extreme 7-bit/2-bit W/A quantization, the proposed method achieves 69.70% vs. 63.84% on ResNet18 (+5.86%), though WrapNet trades accuracy for even cheaper W/A hardware. Compared to the FP8 baseline (32-bit accumulator), the 12-bit LBA method incurs at most 0.5% top-1 degradation (ResNet34: 73.42% vs. 73.49%), making it the only LBA approach that matches non-LBA accuracy on ImageNet. On SQUAD, the LBA BERT-Large model actually slightly exceeds the baseline (90.66% vs. 90.40% f1), while BERT-Base is essentially tied (87.52% vs. 87.53%). On MMLU with LLaMA-2 7B, the M7E4 LBA configuration scores 45.1 vs. 45.3 baseline, a 0.2-point gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DNN quantization literature, which has predominantly targeted weights and activations visible to high-level frameworks, to the previously underexplored internal FMA accumulation operation. It builds on the FP8 format work (Wang et al. 2018; Kuzmin et al. 2022) and prior low-precision accumulation studies (Wang et al. 2018 for FP16 accumulators; Ni et al. 2020 WrapNet for 8/12-bit integer accumulators), but departs from them by proposing a practical two-stage fine-tuning scheme (excluding underflow first, then enabling it) rather than the chunk-based accumulation and stochastic rounding those works recommended. The fine-grained gradient estimation through the full accumulation graph, using novel STE variants (Immediate/DIFF, Recursive/OF), represents a new direction beyond the standard identity or overflow STEs used in W/A quantization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical finding that 12-bit accumulators (M7E4) can be deployed on ImageNet ResNets and BERT/LLaMA-2 models with negligible accuracy loss via simple fine-tuning has direct hardware implications, reducing gate count by ~63% compared to FP32 accumulation. Key limitations remain: the fine-grained STE methods are currently infeasible for convolutional layers due to shared-weight recomputation costs, and no tested STE fully closes the accuracy gap below 8-bit accumulators. Open questions include whether the two-stage underflow-aware fine-tuning generalizes to architectures beyond ResNets and BERT, whether the DIFF STE's black-box-agnostic property can be exploited for proprietary hardware, and whether the accumulation-order dependency (sequential vs. chunk-based) can be further exploited for even lower bit-widths.
