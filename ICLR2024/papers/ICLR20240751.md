## Problem

Estimating causal effects from observational data is severely limited when unobserved confounders exist, as back-door adjustment cannot block all confounding paths. The standard front-door adjustment can handle unobserved confounding but requires the mediator to have no unblocked back-door paths from the treatment or to the outcome—a condition rarely met in practice. This paper relaxes that restriction by introducing the conditional front-door (CFD) adjustment, where back-door paths through the mediator are permitted as long as they are blocked by observed confounders, and further addresses the practical challenge that the CFD variable itself is often unmeasured.

## Input / Output

CFDiVAE consumes proxy variables X (n≥3) of the latent CFD adjustment variable, the treatment T, observed confounding variables W (m≥1), and the outcome Y. A key structural assumption is that W and X are naturally separable: W is pre-treatment (e.g., age) while X is post-treatment (e.g., follow-up test results), making the separation easy to satisfy in practice. The method outputs a learned representation of the latent CFD variable (dimension D_L, typically set equal to D_X) and an ATE estimate computed via the CFD adjustment formula. The latent variable prior is restricted to an exponential family distribution, and at least three independent proxies are required for identifiability.

## Method

The paper first establishes the CFD criterion (Definition 4) and proves the corresponding adjustment formula (Theorem 3) via do-calculus, showing that P(y|do(t)) is identifiable when back-door paths through the mediator are blocked by observed confounders W. For representation learning, CFDiVAE builds on the identifiable VAE framework of Khemakhem et al. (2020) by conditioning the prior p(Z_CFD|T,W) on both the treatment and observed confounders, which provides the additional information needed for identifiability. The generative model factorizes as p(X|Z_CFD)·p(Z_CFD|T,W) with a Gaussian prior, and the encoder q(Z_CFD|T,W,X) is trained by maximizing the ELBO. Theorem 4 formally proves that the parameters (f, S, λ) are identifiable under conditions including an injective observation function, linearly independent sufficient statistics, and an invertible matrix of prior parameter differences across nk+1 distinct (T,W) points. For ATE estimation, discrete data uses the full CFD adjustment formula directly, while continuous linear data employs a two-step regression: first regressing Z_CFD on T and W to obtain β_{T,Z_CFD}, then regressing the residual noise e_{Z_CFD} (which is independent of the unobserved confounder U) on Y to obtain β_{Y,Z_CFD}, yielding ATE = β_{T,Z_CFD}·β_{Y,Z_CFD}.

## Results

CFDiVAE achieves an estimation bias of 2.97±2.09% at N=10k and 2.14±3.38% at N=20k, substantially outperforming all baselines when sample size is ≥2k. The best front-door baselines (FindFDSet/ListFDSet) plateau around 16.4% bias, while back-door methods (LinearDRL, CausalForest, ForestDRL, XLearner) remain stuck near 21–22% regardless of sample size. VAE-based baselines CEVAE and TEDVAE show high variance at small N (e.g., CEVAE at 102.63% for N=0.5k) but converge to ~21–23% at large N, still far above CFDiVAE. CFDiVAE also maintains ~3% bias across all causal strength levels of the unobserved confounder (scaling factor 0.0–2.0), whereas back-door methods degrade sharply as confounding strength increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CFDiVAE decisively outperforms all eight baselines at N≥2k, with the gap widening as sample size grows. Against back-door methods (LinearDRL, CausalForest, ForestDRL, XLearner, KernelDML), CFDiVAE reduces estimation bias from ~21% to ~2–3% at N=10k–20k, because back-door adjustment cannot block the unobserved confounder U. Against the front-door baselines FindFDSet and ListFDSet (which require a known DAG and find only standard front-door sets), CFDiVAE achieves roughly 5–6× lower bias (2.97% vs. 16.49% at N=10k), since the CFD criterion is less restrictive and the learned representation captures the mediator more accurately. Against VAE-based methods CEVAE and TEDVAE, CFDiVAE is superior at all sample sizes ≥2k (e.g., 2.97% vs. 21.29% for CEVAE and 22.63% for TEDVAE at N=10k), benefiting from the identifiable VAE formulation that uses T and W as conditioning variables. The only regime where CFDiVAE underperforms is very small N (0.5k–1k), where it shows 86% and 40% bias respectively, comparable to or worse than CEVAE/TEDVAE at those sizes, though all VAE-based methods struggle at low sample sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Pearl's front-door criterion by introducing a conditional front-door (CFD) criterion that relaxes the requirement that no back-door paths exist between the treatment and mediator, instead allowing them to be blocked by observed confounders W. For learning the latent CFD adjustment variable from proxies, it builds directly on the identifiable VAE framework of Khemakhem et al. (2020), adapting their identifiability theorem to the conditional setting where T and W serve as additional observed variables for the prior. It also extends the VAE-based causal inference lineage (CEVAE, TEDVAE) by replacing their back-door adjustment with the more flexible CFD adjustment, thereby addressing unobserved confounding that those methods cannot handle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CFD criterion broadens the class of DAGs for which causal effects are identifiable via front-door adjustment, making the approach applicable to more realistic settings where observed confounders interact with the mediator. However, the method's practical utility is constrained by its dependence on large sample sizes (performance degrades sharply below 2k samples), the requirement of at least three independent proxies, and the restriction to linear models for continuous data. Open questions include extending the approach to nonlinear data-generating processes, relaxing the exponential family assumption on the latent variable, and developing robustness guarantees when proxy quality is limited.
