## Problem

Existing AI-text detectors either require token probabilities (unavailable for GPT-3.5-turbo and GPT-4), depend on supervised training that must be re-run for each new model release, or provide only a binary verdict without supporting evidence. This paper addresses the need for a training-free, explainable, and model-agnostic detection method that works in both black-box and white-box settings and generalizes to newly released LLMs without retraining.

## Input / Output

The method takes a candidate text passage (typically 1000–2000 characters) and an LLM API endpoint; optionally, the original generation prompt (golden prompt) is prepended to the truncated prefix before re-prompting. It outputs a detection score (BScore for black-box, WScore for white-box) that is thresholded into a human/AI binary label, plus a set of overlapping n-grams (length 4–25) between the original remainder and the K regenerated continuations, which serve as human-readable evidence for the classification decision.

## Method

DNA-GPT is built on the Likelihood-Gap Hypothesis: conditioned on a preceding text X, the log-likelihood of machine-generated continuations exceeds that of human-written continuations by a positive gap Δ, which Pinsker's inequality links to a lower bound on the KL divergence between the two distributions, guaranteeing separability. Concretely, the candidate text is truncated at ratio γ (default 0.5), the prefix X is fed to the target LLM K times (default 5–10) to produce a set of regenerated continuations Ω, and the original remainder Y₀ is compared against Ω. In the black-box setting, BScore computes a length-normalized, weighted n-gram intersection (f(n)=n·log n, n₀=4, N=25) between Y₀ and each Yₖ; in the white-box setting, WScore computes the average log-ratio p(Y₀|X)/p(Yₖ|X). A principled sample-complexity bound K=Ω(σ log(1/δ)/Δ²) justifies the small number of re-prompting iterations needed for reliable detection.

## Results

In the white-box setting with text-davinci-003 (K=10, γ=0.5), DNA-GPT achieves near-perfect detection: AUROC of 100.00 on Reddit-ELI5 and Xsum, 99.94 on Scientific Abstracts, and 99.87 on PubMedQA, with TPR at 1% FPR reaching 96.67–100.00 across all four datasets. In the black-box setting with GPT-3.5-turbo (K=20, γ=0.7), DNA-GPT achieves AUROC of 99.61 (Reddit), 98.02 (Sci Abs), 97.08 (PubMedQA), and 97.12 (Xsum), with TPR of 87.50, 82.00, 51.33, and 33.33 respectively. Even without the golden prompt (w/o P), the method maintains AUROC above 95 on all datasets for GPT-3.5-turbo. The method also generalizes to German (WMT16) and open-source models (LLaMa-13B, GPT-NeoX-20B), and remains robust under 50% text revision (AUROC drops only from 99.09 to 98.48).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DNA-GPT consistently outperforms both supervised baselines (GPTZero and OpenAI's classifier) in the black-box setting. On GPT-3.5-turbo outputs, DNA-GPT achieves AUROC 99.61 vs. GPTZero's 96.85 and OpenAI's 94.36 on Reddit-ELI5, with TPR at 1% FPR of 87.50 vs. 63.00 and 48.50 respectively. The gap is most dramatic on the newer Scientific Abstracts dataset, where GPTZero's TPR collapses to 5.50 (AUROC 88.76) while DNA-GPT maintains 82.00 TPR (AUROC 98.02), exposing the vulnerability of training-based classifiers to unseen domains. In the white-box setting, DNA-GPT (WScore) dramatically surpasses DetectGPT: AUROC of 100.00 vs. 54.21 on Reddit and 99.87 vs. 57.78 on PubMedQA, with TPR of 96.67–100.00 versus DetectGPT's 0.67–2.38. The only case where a baseline matches or slightly exceeds DNA-GPT is OpenAI's classifier on Scientific Abstracts TPR (94.00 vs. 82.00 for GPT-3.5-turbo), though DNA-GPT still leads on AUROC in the w/o-prompt setting (99.56 vs. 99.25). Overall, DNA-GPT wins on the majority of dataset-model combinations across both AUROC and TPR metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant paradigms in AI-text detection: training-based classifiers (GPTZero, OpenAI's classifier) that require periodic retraining as models evolve, and training-free methods like DetectGPT that rely on token probability access (now largely unavailable from commercial APIs). DNA-GPT repurposes n-gram analysis—previously used as a static text feature in GLTR and related work—into a dynamic similarity measure between the original text and model regenerations, grounded in a formal Likelihood-Gap Hypothesis connecting the detection signal to total-variation and KL-divergence gaps between machine and human distributions via Pinsker's inequality. The truncation-then-re-prompting pipeline is conceptually distinct from DetectGPT's perturbation-based curvature measurement, requiring only black-box text I/O rather than probability access.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: as major labs (OpenAI, Google) increasingly close-source their models and remove token probability APIs, a training-free black-box detector with built-in explainability addresses a real deployment gap that trained classifiers cannot fill without constant retraining. However, the method's long-term viability is uncertain—it is validated only on early-2023 GPT-architecture models at moderate text lengths (1000–2000 characters), and the authors explicitly disclaim applicability to non-decoder-only architectures. Open questions include robustness to increasingly sophisticated prompt engineering, very long or very short texts, and whether the Likelihood-Gap Hypothesis persists as future models incorporate more human-like stochasticity or RLHF-based diversity.
