## Problem

As generative models increasingly populate the web with synthetic content, future training datasets will inevitably contain a mixture of real and self-generated data. While prior work demonstrated model collapse when retraining purely on synthetic data, no theoretical stability guarantees existed for the mixed-data regime. This paper asks: under what conditions does iterative retraining on a mixture of real and synthetic data remain stable rather than diverge?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240478/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The procedure takes a fixed real dataset (e.g., 50k CIFAR-10 images at 32×32 or 70k FFHQ-64 images at 64×64) and a hyperparameter λ ∈ [0, ∞) controlling the fraction of synthetic samples relative to real ones. At each retraining iteration, the current model generates ⌊λ·n⌋ synthetic samples, which are concatenated with the full real dataset; the model is then finetuned (not retrained from scratch) for a fixed number of images (10⁶ for CIFAR-10, 1.4×10⁶ for FFHQ-64). The output is the updated parameter vector θ_t and, theoretically, a bound on the distance to the optimal fixed point θ* that shrinks geometrically in t.

## Method

The paper formulates iterative retraining as a fixed-point iteration θ_{t+1} = G_λ^n(θ_t) where G_λ maximizes a weighted log-likelihood over real and synthetic data. The key theoretical result (Theorem 1) shows that if the initial model's Wasserstein distance ε to the data distribution is small and λ(1 + Lε/α) < 1/2, then the Jacobian of G_λ at the fixed point has spectral norm strictly less than 1, guaranteeing linear convergence. The proof leverages the Implicit Function Theorem to derive a closed-form Jacobian, then bounds it via Kantorovich-Rubenstein duality. Theorem 2 extends this to finite samples by adding a statistical error term a/√n · √(log bt/δ), showing the iterate remains within a neighborhood of θ* with high probability. Empirically, the theory is validated on CIFAR-10 and FFHQ-64 using EDM, DDPM, and OTCFM, confirming stability for small λ and divergence for λ ≥ 1.

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is theoretical: Theorem 1 proves that iterative retraining converges to the optimal model θ* at a linear rate (λ(α+εL)/(α−λ(α+εL)))^t whenever λ(1+Lε/α) < 1/2, and Theorem 2 extends this to finite-sample settings with high-probability bounds. Empirically, EDM on CIFAR-10 remains stable (FID bounded) for all tested λ from 0 to 1 over 20 retraining iterations, while EDM on FFHQ-64 is stable only for λ < 0.5. Training exclusively on synthetic data (λ=∞) always leads to FID divergence and model collapse across all model-dataset combinations (EDM, DDPM, OTCFM on CIFAR-10, FFHQ-64, 8 Gaussians, Two Moons). For EDM, precision decreases and recall increases with retraining iterations, while OTCFM on CIFAR-10 shows the inverse precision trend.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against competing generative model architectures in a head-to-head benchmark; instead, it compares different retraining strategies (varying λ) and different model families (EDM, DDPM, OTCFM) under the same iterative retraining protocol. Against prior work on model collapse (Alemohammad et al., 2023; Shumailov et al., 2023), this paper's key distinction is that stability can be guaranteed without injecting fresh real data—merely retaining a sufficient fraction of the original real dataset (small λ) combined with a well-trained initial model (small ε) is sufficient. Theorem 1 provides the first explicit sufficient condition λ(1+Lε/α) < 1/2 for local stability, whereas prior works only demonstrated collapse without characterizing the stable regime. Empirically, the paper confirms that the λ=0 baseline (retraining on real data only) always yields stable FID, while λ=1 (equal real/synthetic) causes FID divergence on FFHQ-64 but remains stable on CIFAR-10 for EDM, illustrating that the critical λ threshold depends on the initial model quality ε and dataset complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the empirical model-collapse observations of Shumailov et al. (2023) and Alemohammad et al. (2023) by providing the first theoretical stability framework for iterative retraining of likelihood-based generative models on mixed real-and-synthetic datasets. While Alemohammad et al. advocated injecting fresh real data to prevent collapse, this paper shows that retaining a sufficient proportion of the *original* real data (without any new data) is theoretically sufficient for stability, formalized through a fixed-point analysis of the maximum-likelihood retraining operator. The analysis also draws conceptual parallels to the performative prediction literature (Perdomo et al., 2020), though the required regularity assumptions are notably weaker.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As web-scale datasets increasingly contain synthetic content, practitioners training next-generation generative models will inevitably operate in the mixed-data regime studied here; the paper's sufficient condition (λ(1+Lε/α) < 1/2) gives a concrete, model-agnostic criterion for how much original real data to retain. A key open question is whether this condition is also necessary—the paper proves only sufficiency, leaving a gap between the known necessity of finite λ and the specific bound. Additionally, the finite-sample analysis (Theorem 2) relies on a somewhat strong local generalization assumption, and understanding how statistical error accumulates over many retraining iterations remains an important direction for future work.
