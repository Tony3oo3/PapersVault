## Problem

Training non-hierarchical vision transformers for high-resolution dense prediction tasks is prohibitively expensive due to the quadratic complexity of global self-attention over thousands of tokens. Existing workarounds—hierarchical backbones, approximate attention, or training on low-resolution crops with test-time tiling—either alter the architecture, sacrifice performance, or introduce slow multi-pass inference with blocking artifacts. The paper asks whether a vanilla ViT can be trained cheaply yet still process full-resolution inputs in a single forward pass at test time.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (single for segmentation and depth, image pairs for optical flow) at high resolutions such as 1280×720 (BDD-100k) or 1920×1080 (Spring). During training, only N randomly placed rectangular windows of tokens are retained (e.g., 2 windows of 22×22 tokens ≈ 968 out of 3600 total), while at test time the full token set is processed in one forward pass. For binocular tasks, the second frame's windows are sampled asymmetrically using a flow-guided greedy algorithm with controlled stochasticity, ensuring matched pixels are visible in both inputs. Outputs are pixelwise predictions—segmentation masks, depth maps, or flow fields—produced by a ConvNeXt head applied independently to each window's feature map.

## Method

Win-Win replaces random token masking (as in MAE) with a structured multi-window scheme: N non-overlapping rectangular windows are randomly sampled per training image, preserving both local intra-window and global inter-window token interactions that are essential for dense prediction. Empirically, two square windows of identical size (N=2) outperform more elaborate configurations, yielding a 3–4× training speedup and 2× memory reduction versus full-resolution training while matching or exceeding its accuracy. The approach relies on RoPE rotary positional embeddings for translation equivariance and a convolutional head that reshapes windowed token features into spatial maps. At test time, masking is simply removed and a per-head softmax temperature τ is tuned on full-resolution training images to compensate for the increased token count, restoring attention statistics. For optical flow, the second frame's windows are selected via a greedy algorithm over a noise-perturbed binning of ground-truth flow endpoints, with a stochasticity parameter (optimal at 0.3) balancing supervision density against overfitting to the flow.

## Results

On semantic segmentation (BDD-100K, 1280×720), Win-Win with 2 windows of 22×22 tokens (968 total) achieves 63.6 mIoU, outperforming full-resolution ViT (63.2 mIoU, 3600 tokens) and ViT-Det (63.3 mIoU) while using only ~27% of the tokens and 4× less training time. On the Spring optical flow benchmark (1920×1080 Full-HD), Win-Win achieves state-of-the-art EPE of 0.475, beating CroCo-Flow (0.498) which uses a 3× larger backbone, with over an order of magnitude faster inference. On MPI-Sintel, Win-Win reaches 1.15 EPE (clean) in a single forward pass, competitive with tiling-based methods like FlowFormer++ (1.07) and CroCo-Flow (1.09).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On semantic segmentation (BDD-100K), Win-Win (63.6 mIoU) outperforms full-resolution ViT (63.2), ViT-Det (63.3), random-token masking (59.9), and crop+tiling (62.6) while training 4× faster and using half the memory. On MPI-Sintel optical flow, Win-Win (1.15 EPE clean) beats PWC-Net+ (3.45), RAFT (1.61), CRAFT (1.44), SKFlow (1.30), and FlowFormer (1.16), but trails FlowFormer++ (1.07) and GMFlow+ (1.03) which use task-specific cost volumes or coarse-to-fine designs. On the Spring benchmark, Win-Win achieves the best EPE (0.475) and best large-displacement error (s40+: 3.639), beating CroCo-Flow (0.498 EPE, 4.046 s40+) despite using a ViT-Base vs. ViT-Large backbone, though CroCo-Flow retains an edge on the 1px outlier metric (4.565 vs. 5.371). The key practical advantage is that Win-Win requires a single forward pass at test time, whereas all tiling-based competitors (FlowFormer, FlowFormer++, CroCo-Flow) need multiple overlapping forward passes, making Win-Win over 10× faster at inference on Full-HD images.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CroCo/CroCo-Flow self-supervised pre-training framework and DPT-style convolutional heads, but introduces a fundamentally different training strategy for high-resolution ViTs. It departs from the three dominant approaches to reducing ViT training cost—hierarchical/sparse attention (ViT-Det, Swin), sub-quadratic attention approximations, and crop-based training with test-time tiling—by instead using structured multi-window masking that preserves vanilla global self-attention in all blocks. The key conceptual departure from masked image modeling (MAE) is the use of spatially contiguous rectangular windows rather than random tokens, which the authors show is essential for both local interaction modeling and compatibility with convolutional prediction heads.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Win-Win offers a simple, general training recipe that achieves 4× faster training and 2× less memory while enabling direct full-resolution inference in a single forward pass, eliminating the tiling artifacts and multi-pass overhead that plague crop-based approaches. The finding that just two square windows suffice—outperforming more elaborate multi-window or variable-size strategies—makes the method easy to adopt across dense prediction tasks. Open questions include whether the approach extends to even higher resolutions (e.g., 4K+), whether the softmax temperature correction is sufficient for very large train/test token-count gaps, and whether it generalizes to non-ViT architectures or video tasks with temporal dimensions.
