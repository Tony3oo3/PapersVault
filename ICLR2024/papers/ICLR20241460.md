## Problem

In knowledge distillation, the standard practice of training the teacher to maximize its own accuracy does not necessarily produce the best student, revealing a fundamental discrepancy between common teacher training and the ideal student-oriented objective. The paper asks whether a teacher can be trained via empirical risk minimization to approximate the true label distribution of its training data (the quantity that actually benefits the student), and how to practically enforce this.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241460/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB training images with one-hot class labels (CIFAR-100 at 32×32, Tiny-ImageNet at 64×64, ImageNet at 224×224) and a teacher network architecture (e.g., WRN40-2, ResNet34, ResNet152). The output is a teacher network whose soft predictions approximate the true label distribution of the training data, which is then used to distill a smaller student (e.g., WRN40-1, ResNet18, ShuffleNetV2) via standard KD or feature-distillation losses. Notably, the teacher is trained on the same data it distills from, and the training objective explicitly trades off teacher accuracy for better student transfer.

## Method

The paper proves that under a mixed-feature data distribution, the ERM minimizer approximates the true label distribution of training inputs provided the feature extractor is Lipschitz-continuous and robust to feature transformations. Building on this theory, SoTeacher augments the standard cross-entropy loss with two regularization terms: (1) Lipschitz regularization that constrains the Lipschitz constant of each trainable network component (computed via 1-norm), and (2) consistency regularization via temporal ensembling that penalizes the MSE between the current prediction and the running average of previous predictions for each input, with a linearly ramped weight. The total loss is ℓ = ℓ_Stand + λ_LR·ℓ_LR + λ_CR·ℓ_CR, requiring only ~5% additional training time and no extra model evaluations, and it consistently improves student accuracy across datasets, architectures, and distillation algorithms while often reducing the teacher's own accuracy.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, SoTeacher consistently improves student accuracy across all three teacher-student architecture pairs: WRN40-2/WRN40-1 (73.73% → 74.35%), WRN40-2/WRN16-2 (74.87% → 75.39%), and ResNet32x4/ShuffleNetV2 (74.86% → 77.24%), while teacher accuracy often decreases (e.g., 76.38% → 74.95% for WRN40-2). On Tiny-ImageNet, student top-1 accuracy improves from 66.19% to 66.83%, and on ImageNet from 71.30% to 71.45%. The gains are most pronounced for heterogeneous architecture pairs (ResNet32x4/ShuffleNetV2 gains +2.38%), suggesting SoTeacher is particularly beneficial when the student has limited capacity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SoTeacher against three baselines: Standard (vanilla ERM teacher training), no-CR (SoTeacher without consistency regularization), and no-LR (SoTeacher without Lipschitz regularization). SoTeacher outperforms Standard on student accuracy in every setting tested, with the largest gain of +2.38% on ResNet32x4/ShuffleNetV2 (CIFAR-100) and +0.64% on WRN40-2/WRN16-2. Notably, SoTeacher often reduces teacher accuracy (e.g., 76.38% → 74.95% for WRN40-2 on CIFAR-100), confirming that the regularization is specifically tailored toward student performance rather than teacher performance. Both ablations (no-CR and no-LR) individually improve over Standard, but SoTeacher combining both regularizations achieves the best student accuracy on average, validating the theoretical requirement for both Lipschitz continuity and transformation robustness. On Tiny-ImageNet, SoTeacher improves student top-1 by +0.64% over Standard, while on ImageNet the gain is smaller (+0.15%), which the authors attribute to the large dataset already facilitating true label distribution learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two existing perspectives on knowledge distillation: the statistical view (Menon et al., 2021; Dao et al., 2021) that teacher soft labels approximate the true label distribution, and the feature-learning view (Allen-Zhu & Li, 2020) that multi-view data structure enables knowledge transfer. It departs from prior work on alleviating teacher overfitting (early stopping, cross-fitting, joint training) by instead proving that standard ERM can learn the true label distribution of training data under Lipschitz-continuity and transformation-robustness conditions on the feature extractor, then operationalizing these conditions through Lipschitz regularization and temporal-ensembling-based consistency regularization. The modified softmax and mixed-feature data distribution are novel analytical tools that connect the two perspectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuinely underexplored component of the KD pipeline—teacher training—and provides both a theoretical justification for why the standard practice (maximizing teacher accuracy) is suboptimal and a simple, low-overhead (~5%) fix. However, the practical gains are modest (0.5–2.5% student accuracy), the ImageNet improvement is marginal, and the theoretical analysis only establishes existence of a suitable minimizer without characterizing optimization dynamics. Open questions include whether the Lipschitz/robustness conditions can be verified or enforced more directly in practice, how the approach extends to transfer learning or mixture-of-experts settings, and whether the modest gains scale with larger models or more complex distillation objectives.
