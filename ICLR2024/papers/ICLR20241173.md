## Problem

While Maximum Update Parameterization (μP) provides principled hyperparameter scaling for first-order optimizers (SGD, Adam) that maintains feature learning as network width grows, no analogous theory existed for second-order methods like K-FAC and Shampoo. These methods introduce additional hyperparameters (damping terms) and structured preconditioners whose interaction with width was uncharacterized, leaving practitioners without guidance on how to scale initialization, learning rates, and damping for wider models.

## Input / Output

The method takes a neural network architecture (width M, depth L, activation function), a choice of second-order optimizer (K-FAC with exponents e_A, e_B or Shampoo with exponent e), and standard supervised training data. It produces explicit per-layer scaling exponents for initialization variance (b_l), learning rate (c_l), and damping term (d_X), yielding a width-independent hyperparameter configuration. The practical output is a set of transferable hyperparameters: once tuned on a narrow model, the same learning rate and damping value apply directly to arbitrarily wider models without re-tuning.

## Method

The paper derives μP conditions for second-order optimization by evaluating the order of the one-step update ΔW_l h_{l-1} in the infinite-width limit, using the push-through identity to reduce K-FAC and Shampoo preconditioner expressions to width-independent kernel matrices. For K-FAC (e_A = e_B = 1), the Kronecker-factored preconditioning absorbs the width-dependent scaling that first-order methods require, so constant learning rates (c_l = 0) suffice across all layers; for Shampoo (e = 1/2), the input layer learning rate must scale as M^{-1/2}. The damping analysis reveals that K-FAC's standard trace-ratio heuristic violates μP at input/output layers and requires rescaling to trace-based dampings (ρ_A ∝ tr(h^T h), ρ_B ∝ tr(δ^T δ)), while Shampoo's standard largest-eigenvalue heuristic is already μP-consistent. A further finding identifies a novel implicit bias: zero-initializing the last layer causes K-FAC to converge to the NNGP solution in a single update, trapping training in the kernel regime at large batch sizes, whereas SGD and Shampoo naturally escape this regime from the second step onward.

## Results

Under μP, wider models consistently match or outperform narrower ones, whereas under SP this 'wider is better' property breaks down. On ResNet18 (CIFAR-100) with K-FAC at width=16, μP yields a +4.00 pp improvement over SP (74.26 → 78.26), and at width=8 a +1.98 pp gain (74.96 → 76.94). On ResNet50 (ImageNet) with K-FAC at width=8, μP adds +0.13 pp over SP (78.63 → 78.76). The paper further demonstrates that optimal learning rates and damping terms tuned on narrow models transfer directly to wider models under μP, eliminating the need for re-tuning at scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Standard Parameterization (SP, i.e., PyTorch defaults) against the proposed μP for both K-FAC and Shampoo across MLP, CNN, Myrtle-5, ResNet, and CBOW architectures. Under SP, increasing width often degrades performance (e.g., ResNet18 K-FAC drops from 74.96 at width=8 to 74.26 at width=16), while under μP the wider model consistently wins (78.26 at width=16). The gap between μP and SP grows with width, reaching +4.00 pp for ResNet18 K-FAC at width=16. For Shampoo, the improvement is more modest but still positive at wider widths (e.g., +1.04 pp for ResNet18 at width=8). A key practical finding is that under μP, the optimal learning rate and damping term remain constant across widths, whereas under SP the optimal learning rate shifts (decreases) as width increases, making hyperparameter transfer impossible without re-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Maximum Update Parameterization (μP) framework of Yang & Hu (2021) and Yang et al. (2021), which was previously limited to first-order SGD and entry-wise adaptive methods (Littwin & Yang, 2023), to the two dominant second-order optimizers: K-FAC (Martens & Grosse, 2015) and Shampoo (Gupta et al., 2018). The authors derive the abc-parameterization conditions (initialization variance, learning rate, damping term scales) that guarantee stable feature learning in the infinite-width limit for these preconditioned gradient methods. A key departure from prior μP work is the identification of a novel implicit bias: K-FAC with zero initialization of the output layer converges to the NNGP solution in a single step, a phenomenon absent in first-order methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first quantitative recipe for scaling second-order optimization to wider models, enabling practitioners to transfer learning rates and damping terms from narrow to wide architectures without re-tuning. The finding that K-FAC's standard damping heuristics violate μP conditions (requiring a rescaled damping) and that zero initialization creates an NNGP trap under K-FAC are practically important warnings for large-scale training. Limitations include the focus on width rather than depth, the restriction to MLP/CNN architectures (no Transformer analysis), and the theoretical derivation being limited to the one-step update, leaving multi-step dynamics to empirical verification.
