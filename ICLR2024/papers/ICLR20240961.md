## Problem

Label smoothing is a widely adopted regularization technique for deep learning classifiers, yet its implications for model privacy have remained entirely unexplored. This paper is the first to investigate how the smoothing factor in label smoothing affects the success of model inversion attacks (MIAs), which exploit a classifier's encoded knowledge to reconstruct class-representative samples and infer sensitive training data. The central question is whether a standard training hyperparameter can inadvertently amplify or mitigate privacy leakage.

## Input / Output

The target model takes 224×224 RGB facial images and outputs identity class probabilities over 530 (FaceScrub) or 1,000 (CelebA) classes. The MIA (Plug & Play Attacks) consumes the target model's prediction scores and a pre-trained StyleGAN2 generator, optimizing latent vectors in the GAN's 1024×1024 image space to produce synthetic face images that reveal the target identity's visual characteristics. Attack quality is measured via FaceNet embedding distance (δ_face), Inception-v3 top-1 accuracy on synthetic samples, and a novel knowledge extraction score (ξ_train) that trains a surrogate ResNet-50 on attack outputs and evaluates it on the original training data.

## Method

The paper demonstrates that positive label smoothing (α>0) tightens same-class clusters in the model's penultimate embedding space while increasing inter-class separation, providing clearer gradient guidance that accelerates and improves MIA success—particularly in low-data regimes. Conversely, negative label smoothing (α<0) penalizes the model's confidence in non-target classes, causing class embeddings to overlap and, critically, producing highly unstable gradient directions during the attack's optimization stage (low cosine similarity between consecutive image gradients), which causes the inversion to fail. The defense requires no architectural modification or complex loss function—only a small negative smoothing factor (α=−0.05) applied after initial training epochs—yet it reduces attack accuracy from ~94% to ~14% on FaceScrub while retaining ~91% test accuracy, outperforming state-of-the-art defenses MID and BiDO in the utility-privacy trade-off. Ablation across PPA's three stages (sampling, optimization, selection) shows the effect is concentrated in sampling and optimization, with selection barely affected.

## Results

On FaceScrub with ResNet-152, positive label smoothing (α=0.1) amplifies model inversion attacks, increasing PPA's Acc@1 from 94.3% to 95.2% and reducing the FaceNet feature distance δ_face from 0.71 to 0.63, while the knowledge extraction score ξ_train rises from 61.2% to 71.0%. Negative label smoothing (α=−0.05) dramatically suppresses attack success, dropping Acc@1 to 14.3%, increasing δ_face to 1.23, and reducing ξ_train to 16.5%, all with only a 3.4% test accuracy reduction (94.9%→91.5%). On CelebA, the same trends hold: positive LS boosts Acc@1 by 11.1 points, while negative LS collapses it to 26.4% with a 3.5% test accuracy cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Negative LS (α=−0.05) substantially outperforms both state-of-the-art defenses on the utility-privacy trade-off. Against MID (Wang et al., 2021c), negative LS reduces PPA's Acc@1 from 92.0% to 14.3% on FaceScrub (a 77.7-point gap) while sacrificing only 3.4% test accuracy versus MID's 3.8%; on CelebA, negative LS drops Acc@1 to 26.4% versus MID's 78.0%. Against BiDO (Peng et al., 2022), negative LS achieves a far lower Acc@1 (14.3% vs. 45.4% on FaceScrub; 26.4% vs. 63.7% on CelebA) while retaining higher test accuracy (91.5% vs. 87.1% on FaceScrub; 83.6% vs. 79.9% on CelebA). The knowledge extraction score ξ_train confirms the gap: negative LS yields 16.5% on FaceScrub versus 73.0% for MID and 59.3% for BiDO, indicating that the synthetic images generated against negative-LS models contain far less discriminative class information. Notably, negative LS requires no architectural modifications or additional loss terms, unlike MID (information bottleneck) and BiDO (HSIC dependency regularization), making it a strictly simpler defense.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-established lines of work: model inversion attacks (MIAs), particularly the high-resolution Plug & Play Attacks (PPA) framework, and label smoothing (LS) regularization as introduced by Szegedy et al. and generalized to negative factors by Wei et al. The key departure is that prior MIA literature focused on improving attack performance or designing dedicated defenses (MID, BiDO), while this paper is the first to study how a standard training hyperparameter—the LS factor—systematically shifts a model's vulnerability. It repurposes the negative-LS formulation (which Wei et al. explored for label-noise robustness) as a privacy defense, connecting it mechanistically to embedding-space geometry and gradient stability during attack optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a single hyperparameter change (α = −0.05) provides a defense against MIAs that outperforms architecture-modifying defenses like MID and BiDO while requiring no additional loss terms or model modifications. However, the scope is limited to face recognition and gradient-based attacks, and the utility cost (3–4% test accuracy drop) is non-trivial. Open questions include whether boundary-aware attack objectives can circumvent negative LS, whether other regularization methods (dropout, weight decay, mixup) exhibit similar dual privacy effects, and whether the information-reduction mechanism generalizes to membership inference or model stealing attacks. The absence of a theoretical framework for MIAs remains a fundamental gap that this empirical study highlights but does not fill.
