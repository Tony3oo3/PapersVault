## Problem

Existing universal domain adaptation methods align all samples within an annotated category to a single class prototype, ignoring significant intra-class concept shifts (e.g., fighter jets vs. civil aircraft both labeled 'airplane'). Forcing visually dissimilar samples to converge degrades adaptation performance and makes it harder to distinguish unknown classes from known ones. No prior UniDA method learns sub-class structure within categories to enable more fine-grained, semantically reasonable alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241236/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images as input, encoded by a frozen CLIP-pretrained ViT-B/16 into input-oriented embeddings. These embeddings are not used directly for classification; instead, they serve as queries to a content-addressable memory that retrieves and weight-averages the top-K most relevant sub-prototypes into a task-oriented embedding. This task-oriented embedding is then passed to a two-layer FC UniDA classifier producing a class label (one of the known classes or 'unknown'), and optionally to a reconstruction decoder that generates an image visualization of the retrieved sub-prototypes for interpretability.

## Method

MemSPM introduces a learnable content-addressable memory structured as an N×S×D matrix, where each of N memory items contains S partitioned sub-prototypes of dimension D. An attention-based addressing operator computes cosine-similarity weights between the input-oriented embedding and all sub-prototypes, then applies an adaptive top-K threshold (differentiated via a ReLU-based trick from Gong et al.) to restrict retrieval to only the most relevant sub-prototypes, producing a domain-invariant task-oriented embedding. This two-stage embedding (input-oriented → task-oriented) strips domain-specific information before classification. For domain alignment, the method adopts DCC's cycle-consistent matching on the task-oriented embeddings, with a total loss combining cross-entropy, CDD alignment, regularization, and an auxiliary MSE reconstruction loss that enables visualizing what each sub-prototype has learned. The memory is initialized with a uniform distribution and trained end-to-end, with the encoder kept frozen to stabilize early training of the large randomly-initialized memory.

## Results

Under the UniDA setting with CLIP (ViT-B/16) backbone, MemSPM+DCC achieves 88.5% H-score on Office-31, 84.2% on Office-Home, 80.5% on VisDA, and 63.3% on DomainNet. In the OSDA scenario, it reaches 95.6% on Office-31 and 76.5% on Office-Home. In the PDA scenario, it attains 87.9% on VisDA and 74.5% on Office-Home. The method achieves state-of-the-art H-scores on Office-31 and Office-Home across all three scenarios, while remaining competitive on DomainNet and VisDA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest CLIP-based baseline GLC (Qu et al., 2023), MemSPM+DCC wins on Office-31 UniDA (+3.7%), Office-Home UniDA (+1.6%), Office-31 OSDA (+5.5%), and VisDA PDA (+1.7%), but trails slightly on DomainNet UniDA (−1.2%), VisDA UniDA (−0.2%), Office-Home OSDA (−0.3%), and Office-Home PDA (−0.9%). Against DCC (Li et al., 2021) with CLIP, MemSPM+DCC achieves large gains: +9.8% on Office-Home UniDA, +13.7% on Office-31 OSDA, +8.1% on VisDA PDA, and +6.4% on Office-31 UniDA. Against ImageNet-pretrained baselines (UAN, CMU, OVANet, UMAD, GATE, UniOT), MemSPM+DCC leads by 10–30+ points on most benchmarks, reflecting the advantage of the CLIP backbone. On DomainNet UniDA, GLC retains a narrow 1.2% edge over MemSPM+DCC, making it the one setting where the proposed method does not achieve the top score among CLIP-based methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

MemSPM builds directly on Domain Consensus Clustering (DCC, Li et al. 2021), adopting its cycle-consistent matching and Contrastive Domain Discrepancy loss as the adaptation backbone, and is evaluated as 'MemSPM+DCC' throughout. The memory mechanism draws from the content-addressable memory literature (Gong et al. 2019, Sukhbaatar et al. 2015, Rae et al. 2016), repurposing the ReLU-based differentiable threshold trick from anomaly detection for sub-prototype retrieval. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant UniDA paradigm (UAN, OVANet, GATE, GLC) that treats each annotated category as a single prototype, instead introducing a partitioned memory that learns multiple sub-prototypes per category to capture intra-class concept shift. The use of a frozen CLIP encoder as the input-oriented feature extractor is a practical departure from prior UniDA methods that train the encoder end-to-end.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and underexplored failure mode in UniDA—forcing visually dissimilar samples (e.g., fighter jets and turboprop aircraft) into a single class center—and shows that sub-prototype mining mitigates this with consistent gains across four benchmarks and three shift scenarios. The reconstruction-based visualization of memory contents offers a degree of interpretability that prior UniDA methods lack. However, the method's heavy dependence on CLIP pre-training (a 19.9% drop without it, and inability to train from scratch with ResNet-50) limits its generality and raises open questions about whether the sub-prototype structure can be learned with weaker or self-supervised encoders. The fixed number of sub-prototypes per memory item (S) may not suit all categories, and scalability to very large label spaces (e.g., full DomainNet with 345 classes) remains to be fully validated.
