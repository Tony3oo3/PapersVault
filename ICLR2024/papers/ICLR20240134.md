## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models consistently fail to suppress negative target content explicitly requested for omission in prompts (e.g., 'a man without glasses' still generates glasses). Existing solutions either require fine-tuning the generator (causing catastrophic neglect of the removed concept in future prompts), paired training data, or introduce unwanted structural and stylistic changes to the rest of the image. No training-free method effectively removes the unwanted content while preserving the positive target.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (containing both positive and negative targets) and optionally a real image (for real-image editing via Null-Text Inversion) or a noise vector (for generated-image editing). The prompt is encoded by a CLIP text encoder (768×77 embeddings for SD) or T5 (for DeepFloyd-IF). The output is a single edited or generated image with the negative target suppressed. No extra network parameters are introduced; the entire optimization operates on the text embeddings and takes approximately 35 seconds on a single GPU, applied only during the first 20 of the diffusion timesteps.

## Method

The key empirical finding is that [EOT] padding embeddings carry significant, redundant, and highly correlated semantic information about the entire prompt, including the negative target, so simply zeroing the negative target token embedding is insufficient. The method has two training-free steps. First, soft-weighted regularization (SWR) constructs a negative target embedding matrix χ = [c^NE, c_0^EOT, …, c_{N-|p|-2}^EOT], performs SVD, and applies the shrinkage σ̂ = e^{−σ}·σ to each singular value, which preferentially attenuates the largest singular values (corresponding to the negative target information) while preserving the low-rank structure of the [EOT] embeddings. Second, inference-time text embedding optimization (ITO) refines the SWR-processed embeddings at each of the first 20 diffusion timesteps using two cross-attention losses: a positive target preservation loss L_pl = ‖Â_t^PE − A_t^PE‖² that anchors the positive target's attention pattern, and a negative target suppression loss L_nl = −‖Â_t^NE − A_t^NE‖² that weakens the negative target's attention, combined as L = λ_pl·L_pl + λ_nl·L_nl (λ_pl=1, λ_nl=0.5) over 10 inner gradient steps per timestep. The approach is model-agnostic, requiring no fine-tuning or paired data, and generalizes to both latent-space (Stable Diffusion) and pixel-space (DeepFloyd-IF) diffusion models.

## Results

On real-image editing with a random negative target, the proposed method achieves the best Clipscore (0.6857↓) and DetScore (0.0384↓), with a competitive IFID of 166.3. On generated-image editing with a random negative target, it achieves the best Clipscore (0.6647↓), IFID (176.4↑), and a strong DetScore (0.1321↓). For the specific 'Car' negative target, the method attains the best DetScore (0.0419↓) and competitive Clipscore (0.7426↓) and IFID (206.8↑). On the GQA-Inpaint dataset, the method achieves 92.8% CLIP Accuracy (Top-1) and 83.3% (Top-5) without requiring paired training data, substantially outperforming Inst-Inpaint (80.5%/60.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines on the primary metrics (Clipscore and DetScore) across nearly all evaluation settings. On real-image editing, it reduces DetScore from 0.3381 (SD) to 0.0384, a ~89% reduction, while Negative prompt only achieves 0.2402 and Inst-Inpaint 0.1125. On generated-image editing with a random negative target, the method achieves the best Clipscore (0.6647 vs. 0.7602 for Inst-Inpaint and 0.7619 for Negative prompt) and the highest IFID (176.4 vs. 169.0 for Negative prompt). The sole exception is the Tyler Edlin style removal, where ESD achieves a lower Clipscore (0.6954 vs. 0.7402), though ESD requires fine-tuning the SD model and suffers from catastrophic neglect on other prompts. On the GQA-Inpaint dataset, the method achieves 92.8% CLIP Accuracy (Top-1) without paired training data, compared to 80.5% for Inst-Inpaint which requires paired source-target images. Negative prompt achieves the best IFID on real-image editing (175.8) but at the cost of altering image structure and style, whereas the proposed method better preserves the positive target content.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of fine-tuning diffusion models to erase concepts (ESD, Concept-ablation, Forget-Me-Not) or requiring paired training data (Inst-Inpaint), instead proposing a fully training-free approach that operates solely on text embeddings at inference time. It builds on Null-Text Inversion for real-image inversion and draws its soft-weighted regularization formula from the WNNM low-rank analysis framework, repurposing singular-value shrinkage to suppress semantic content in the [EOT] padding embeddings. The key conceptual departure from attention-based steering methods (P2P, SEGA) is the identification of [EOT] embeddings as carriers of redundant negative-target information, making them a necessary target for suppression alongside the explicit negative-target token.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature of the method makes it immediately applicable to any pre-trained diffusion model without the catastrophic forgetting or compute cost of fine-tuning, and its generalization to both latent-space (SD) and pixel-space (DeepFloyd-IF) models broadens its practical relevance. The empirical finding that [EOT] padding tokens encode significant prompt semantics challenges the common assumption that padding is semantically inert, which could inform future work on prompt engineering and embedding manipulation. The main limitation is the ~35-second per-image inference cost from iterative optimization, which restricts real-time applications, and the method's dependence on concise prompts (longer abstract descriptions dilute the suppression effect).
