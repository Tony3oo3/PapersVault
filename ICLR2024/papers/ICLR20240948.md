## Problem

Existing continual learning methods—Bayesian-based, regularization-based, and memory-replay-based—were developed from distinct perspectives without a unified framework or shared terminology, making it difficult to compare or combine them. Moreover, these methods predominantly constrain model updates to prevent forgetting but neglect the risk of over-memorization, where outdated information occupies model capacity and impairs both new learning and recall of relevant old knowledge.

## Input / Output

The method operates on sequential task data in a standard CL setup: RGB images (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, Tiny-ImageNet at 64×64) or grayscale images (P-MNIST, R-MNIST) with class labels, processed by a ResNet-18 backbone. A memory buffer of 500 or 2000 samples from prior tasks is maintained for replay-based baselines. The FIM is computed once per completed task and reused throughout subsequent training, keeping the per-step overhead minimal. Output is a standard class prediction over the full set of classes seen so far.

## Method

The paper first unifies existing CL methods under a single Bregman-divergence-based objective L^CL = L_CE + α·D_Φ(h_θ(x), z) + β·D_Ψ(θ, θ_old), showing that EWC, VCL, NCL, CPR, ER, and DER are all recoverable by choosing specific Φ, Ψ, and divergence settings. Building on this, the proposed refresh learning plug-in performs a two-step unlearn-then-relearn cycle at each CL step: the unlearn step solves a Fokker-Planck PDE with FIM preconditioning (D(θ)=F^{-1}) to selectively increase the loss on the current mini-batch, erasing less critical parameters faster while preserving important ones; the relearn step then performs a standard gradient descent update on the same mini-batch. Theoretically, one unlearn step approximately minimizes the FIM-weighted gradient norm of the CL loss, promoting flatter loss landscapes and improved generalization without requiring Hessian computation.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 Task-IL, DER+++refresh achieves 94.64% overall accuracy, improving over DER++ (93.88%) by 0.76 points. On CIFAR-100 Task-IL, DER+++refresh reaches 77.71% vs. DER++'s 75.64% (+2.07 points), and on Tiny-ImageNet Task-IL it achieves 54.06% vs. 51.91% (+2.15 points). The refresh plug-in consistently improves all tested baselines: ER gains +2.08 points on CIFAR-100 Task-IL (75.45 vs. 73.37), oEWC gains +2.11 points (23.31 vs. 21.20), and CPR(EWC) gains +1.47 points (22.90 vs. 21.43) on the same setting. The method adds only ~0.81× computational overhead over the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The refresh learning plug-in is applied to four representative baselines (oEWC, CPR(EWC), ER, DER++) and consistently improves all of them across every dataset and evaluation protocol. Against the strongest baseline DER++, DER+++refresh gains +1.72 points on CIFAR-10 Class-IL (74.42 vs. 72.70), +2.07 on CIFAR-100 Task-IL (77.71 vs. 75.64), and +2.15 on Tiny-ImageNet Task-IL (54.06 vs. 51.91). ER+refresh outperforms ER by +2.08 points on CIFAR-100 Task-IL (75.45 vs. 73.37) and +2.21 on Tiny-ImageNet Task-IL (50.85 vs. 48.64). Among regularization-based methods, oEWC+refresh surpasses oEWC by +2.58 points on CIFAR-10 Task-IL (66.89 vs. 64.31), and CPR(EWC)+refresh beats CPR(EWC) by +2.13 on CIFAR-10 Task-IL (67.36 vs. 65.23). The method also improves backward transfer: DER+++refresh achieves −14.23 BWT on CIFAR-100 Task-IL vs. −14.72 for DER++, and −25.05 vs. −27.21 on Tiny-ImageNet Task-IL, indicating reduced forgetting of previously learned tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three major families of continual learning methods—Bayesian (VCL, NCL), regularization (EWC, CPR), and memory-replay (ER, DER)—under a single Bregman-divergence-based optimization objective, revealing that they share a common mathematical structure despite their distinct origins. The refresh learning mechanism is derived by combining the Fokker-Planck gradient flow formulation from Ma et al. (2015) and Patterson & Teh (2013) with the energy-function posterior framework of Wibisono (2018), applied to the CL setting with FIM preconditioning. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior CL work by arguing that existing methods over-prioritize retention and neglect over-memorization, motivating a deliberate unlearn-then-relearn step that has no direct analogue in the standard CL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Bregman-divergence unification offers a clean theoretical lens for comparing and combining CL methods, potentially guiding the design of hybrid approaches. The refresh learning plug-in is practical (one extra FIM-preconditioned gradient step per iteration, ~0.81× overhead) and shows consistent 1–2% gains across datasets and baselines, making it an easy addition to existing CL pipelines. Limitations include evaluation only on ResNet-18 with relatively small datasets (CIFAR-10/100, Tiny-ImageNet), reliance on a diagonal FIM approximation, and the theoretical guarantee (Theorem 4.1) resting on first-order Taylor approximations that may not hold in deep networks. Open questions include whether the unlearn-relearn mechanism scales to larger architectures and longer task sequences, and whether the neuroscience-inspired selective forgetting can be formalized beyond the FIM-weighted heuristic.
