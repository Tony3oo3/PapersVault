## Problem

Diffusion models still produce low-quality generations, but there is no proper sample-wise metric to identify them—traditional metrics like FID and IS capture distributional properties rather than individual image quality. Integrating Bayesian uncertainty quantification with diffusion models is non-trivial because of the large-scale networks involved and the intricate multi-step reverse diffusion process that complicates uncertainty propagation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240685/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given an initial noise vector x_T ~ N(0, I) (and optionally a text prompt for text-to-image tasks) and a pre-trained diffusion model, BayesDiff produces both the generated image x_0 and a pixel-wise uncertainty map Var(x_0) of the same spatial dimensions (c×h×w). The pixel-wise uncertainty can be aggregated (e.g., by summation) into a scalar image-wise score for filtering. The method operates at resolutions from 128×128 to 512×512 and is compatible with multiple samplers (DDPM, DDIM, DPM-Solver) at various NFE settings.

## Method

BayesDiff applies Last-Layer Laplace Approximation (LLLA) to the pre-trained noise prediction network, yielding a pixel-wise variance γ_θ²(x_t, t) of the predicted noise at each timestep with minimal added cost. The core theoretical contribution is an uncertainty iteration principle: by applying variance and covariance propagation rules to the reverse diffusion update equation, pixel-wise uncertainty is iteratively propagated from x_T to x_0, with the key challenge of estimating Cov(x_t, ε_t) resolved via Monte Carlo sampling from the estimated Gaussian distribution of x_t. The framework is sampler-agnostic, with explicit iteration rules derived for DDPM, DDIM, Analytic-DPM, and 2-order DPM-Solver (the latter requiring a conditional-independence trick to handle its intermediate hidden state). For practical deployment, BayesDiff-Skip performs uncertainty quantification only at a predefined subset of timesteps, achieving ~5× speedup while preserving the ranking of high-uncertainty samples.

## Results

BayesDiff's uncertainty-based filtering (selecting the bottom 84% by uncertainty, i.e., removing the top 16% highest-uncertainty images) consistently improves FID and Precision over random selection across all model-dataset-sampler combinations. On U-ViT/ImageNet-256 with 2-order DPM-Solver, FID drops from 7.24 to 6.81 and Precision rises from 0.698 to 0.705. On U-ViT/ImageNet-512, FID improves from 17.72 to 16.87. The method adds no more than 2× the sampling time when using BayesDiff-Skip with a skipping interval of 4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against other uncertainty estimation methods; instead, the baseline is random selection of the same number of images from the generated pool. BayesDiff's uncertainty-based filtering wins on FID across all four model-dataset-sampler configurations, with the largest gain on U-ViT/ImageNet-512 (17.72 → 16.87, a 4.8% relative reduction). Precision improves in every setting (e.g., 0.698 → 0.705 on ImageNet-256), confirming that lower-uncertainty images are more faithful to the training distribution. Recall is essentially unchanged or marginally lower (e.g., 0.658 → 0.657), consistent with the expected fidelity-diversity tradeoff: filtering out high-uncertainty (cluttered) images sacrifices a small amount of diversity for a meaningful gain in sample quality. The empirical distribution of image-wise uncertainty is approximately Gaussian, and the 3-sigma-inspired threshold (μ+σ, top 16%) provides a principled, dataset-agnostic filtering criterion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two established lines of work: Bayesian uncertainty quantification in deep networks (specifically last-layer Laplace approximation from Kristiadi et al., 2020 and Daxberger et al., 2021a) and the reverse diffusion process of score-based generative models (Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2021). The key conceptual contribution is the 'uncertainty iteration principle,' which propagates pixel-wise Bayesian uncertainty through each denoising step—a problem that prior Bayesian generative models (VAEs, VDMs) did not face because they lack the multi-step iterative reverse process. The method is sampler-agnostic, with explicit derivations for DDPM, DDIM, Analytic-DPM, and 2-order DPM-Solver, and the BayesDiff-Skip variant addresses the computational overhead of Monte Carlo covariance estimation at every step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BayesDiff provides a post-hoc, training-free uncertainty estimator for pre-trained diffusion models, making it immediately deployable without retraining—practically important given the scale of modern text-to-image systems. The dual utility of pixel-wise uncertainty (filtering low-fidelity images via a simple 3-sigma criterion, and actively rectifying artifacts or generating diverse variants of successful samples) broadens its applicability beyond mere quality scoring. Open questions include whether the Gaussian assumption on intermediate states x_t holds well for very high-resolution or multi-modal generation, whether the uncertainty estimates are well-calibrated in an absolute sense, and how the method interacts with classifier-free guidance or other conditioning mechanisms. The ~60% rectification success rate for flawed text-to-image generations, while promising, is demonstrated primarily on Stable Diffusion and would benefit from validation across more architectures and prompt distributions.
