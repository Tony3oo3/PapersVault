## Problem

Physics-informed machine learning models (PINNs and variants) are notoriously slow or infeasible to train with gradient descent, yet the literature lacked a principled, rigorous explanation of why. Prior attributions to non-convexity, spectral bias, or loss-weight imbalance were empirical or heuristic; this paper identifies the precise operator-theoretic cause: the condition number of the Hermitian square of the PDE's differential operator, composed with the model's tangent kernel integral operator, directly governs the gradient descent convergence rate.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a PDE specified by its differential operator D and boundary conditions on a bounded domain, together with a parametric ansatz (learnable Fourier features or an MLP in the NTK regime), and produces an approximate PDE solution via gradient descent on the physics-informed loss. The key analytical output is the condition number κ(A) of the matrix A = ⟨Dφᵢ, Dφⱼ⟩ + λ⟨φᵢ, φⱼ⟩, which determines the number of GD steps as O(κ(A) log(1/ε)). The preconditioning step rescales model parameters by a matrix P derived from the spectral properties of D*D, transforming the effective operator so that κ(PᵀAP) ≈ 1, and the resulting training dynamics become independent of the PDE's spectral scale (e.g., maximum Fourier frequency K or advection speed β).

## Method

Under the assumption that the tangent kernel remains approximately constant along the optimization path (guaranteed for wide networks in the NTK regime, or exactly for linear models), the full gradient descent update is shown to be well-approximated by a simplified linear iteration θ_{k+1} = (I − ηA)θ_k + η(Aθ₀ + B), whose convergence rate is (1 − c/κ(A))^k. The central theorem (Theorem 2.4) then proves that κ(A) equals the condition number of the operator A∘TT*, where A = D*D is the Hermitian square of the PDE operator and TT* is the kernel integral operator associated with the model's tangent kernel. Preconditioning amounts to choosing a parameter transformation P such that the new kernel integral operator PᵀTT*P approximates A^{-1} (the Green's function of D*D), reducing κ to O(1). For Fourier features on the Poisson, Helmholtz, and linear advection equations, this yields explicit diagonal preconditioners (rescaling modes by 1/k² or 1/|m+βk|) that reduce the condition number from O(K⁴) or O(β²) to a constant, enabling exponential loss decay independent of model size. The paper further reinterprets existing strategies—optimal λ selection, hard boundary conditions, quasi-Newton optimizers, and domain decomposition—as implicit forms of operator preconditioning within this unified framework.

## Results

For the 1D Poisson equation with learnable Fourier features, the unpreconditioned matrix A has condition number κ(A) ≥ K⁴ (where K is the maximum frequency), while the preconditioned matrix satisfies κ(Ã) ≤ C independent of K, approaching the optimal value of 1 as the preconditioning parameter γ → ∞. For the 2D linear advection equation, the unpreconditioned condition number scales as κ(A) ~ β² with advection speed β, whereas the preconditioned version remains O(1). Empirically, the preconditioned Fourier model achieves a training loss of ~10⁻¹⁰ on the advection equation, compared to ~10⁻² for an MLP (5 layers, 50 neurons, Adam, 10000 epochs) from Krishnapriyan et al. (2021), and the unpreconditioned Fourier model fails to converge even at the slowest tested speed β = 6π.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary empirical baseline is the MLP architecture (5 hidden layers, 50 neurons, tanh activation, trained with Adam for 10000 epochs) from Krishnapriyan et al. (2021), which was specifically designed to characterize PINN failure modes on the linear advection equation. On this problem, the preconditioned Fourier model decisively outperforms the MLP: it reaches a loss of ~10⁻¹⁰ with fast exponential decay, while the MLP plateaus at an unacceptably large loss of ~10⁻² even after 10000 epochs. The unpreconditioned Fourier model fails completely at all tested advection speeds (β = 6π, 30π, 60π), while the MLP only partially succeeds at the slowest speed (β = 6π) before also failing at higher speeds. The preconditioned Fourier model produces visually indistinguishable solutions from the exact solution at all speeds, whereas both baselines produce qualitatively wrong solutions at higher β. The paper also shows that the preconditioning overhead is modest (~14–17 ms per step vs. ~9–11 ms for plain SGD), making the approach practically viable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NTK-based analysis of Wang et al. (2022b) from continuous-time gradient flow to discrete-time gradient descent, identifying the Hermitian square of the PDE's differential operator (composed with the tangent kernel integral operator) as the precise source of training difficulty. It departs from prior empirical attributions of PINN training failure (non-convexity, spectral bias, loss balancing) by providing a rigorous operator-conditioning characterization that unifies seemingly disparate strategies—hard boundary conditions, domain decomposition, second-order optimizers, and λ-tuning—under a single preconditioning lens drawn from classical numerical analysis. The framework builds on the PINN loss formulation of Raissi et al. (2019) and the NTK constancy results for wide networks, but reframes the training problem as one of ill-conditioning rather than landscape complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous operator-theoretic explanation for why PINNs are hard to train, connecting a deep learning optimization problem to the well-studied condition number of a differential operator. This opens a rich toolbox from numerical analysis (preconditioning, domain decomposition, multigrid) to the PINN community, and the demonstration that a simple spectral rescaling of Fourier features transforms training from infeasible to exponentially fast is practically significant. Key limitations include restriction to linear PDEs and the NTK (lazy) regime, with no universal preconditioning strategy for nonlinear models or feature-learning networks. Extending the analysis to nonlinear PDEs, high-dimensional problems where feature learning matters, and developing data-driven preconditioners for general architectures remain important open questions.
