## Problem

Standard Q-learning algorithms fail in factorisable MDPs (FMDPs) where the action space is a Cartesian product of discrete sub-action spaces, due to combinatorial explosion of atomic actions. While DecQN's value-decomposition mitigates this by learning per-dimension utilities, the paper identifies two unresolved issues: the decomposition increases target variance (despite reducing overestimation bias), and exploratory sub-actions in one dimension can undervalue optimal sub-actions in other dimensions (a credit assignment problem).

## Input / Output

The method takes a state vector (e.g., 223-dimensional for the dog task) and outputs a global action composed of independently selected sub-actions from each of N factorised dimensions. Each critic network produces utility values for every sub-action in every dimension, and the Q-value for a global action is the mean of the selected utilities. The continuous action space is discretised via Bang-Off-Bang into n_i bins per dimension (default n_i=3), so the total atomic action space is ∏n_i but only Σn_i utility values need to be learned.

## Method

REValueD combines two mechanisms on top of DecQN's value-decomposition. First, an ensemble of K critics is used where the learning target takes the mean utility across all K target networks; Theorem 2 proves this reduces target variance by a factor of 1/K while leaving the (already-reduced) overestimation bias unchanged. Second, a regularisation loss is added that minimises the Huber loss between each selected sub-action's utility and its target-network value, weighted by w_i = 1 − exp(−|δ_i|) where δ_i is the per-sub-action TD residual; large residuals (indicating the transition was likely influenced by other exploratory sub-actions) trigger stronger regularisation to prevent undervaluing optimal sub-actions. The total loss is L_DecQN(θ) + β·L_reg(θ) with β=0.5, and during training a single critic is sampled per step for ε-greedy exploration while test-time action selection averages over the ensemble.

## Results

On the discretised DeepMind Control Suite, REValueD consistently outperforms DecQN and BDQ across the majority of tasks, with the largest gains on the most challenging environments. On the humanoid tasks (N=21 sub-action spaces), REValueD achieves 915.80±6.12 on Humanoid-Stand and 874.33±3.63 on Humanoid-Walk, compared to 604.82±85.1 and 670.62±34.1 for DecQN. On the dog tasks (N=38), REValueD reaches 821.17±8.10 on Dog-Run versus 625.68±12.5 for DecQN. REValueD also maintains strong performance as the number of sub-actions per dimension increases to n=75 or larger, where DecQN has only just begun to learn.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REValueD outperforms DecQN (Seyde et al., 2022) on virtually all DM Control Suite tasks, with the most dramatic improvements on high-dimensional action spaces: on Dog-Run, REValueD achieves 821.17 vs. 625.68 for DecQN (+31%), and on Humanoid-Stand, 915.80 vs. 604.82 (+51%). Against BDQ (Tavakoli et al., 2018), REValueD wins on most tasks; BDQ completely fails to learn in the humanoid environments and underperforms DecQN on several others. Against DecQN-Sum (the sum-based value-decomposition from Tang et al., 2022), REValueD consistently achieves higher returns, consistent with the theoretical finding (Theorem 3) that the sum decomposition increases overestimation bias relative to the mean decomposition. The ensemble component alone (DecQN+Ensemble) already provides substantial gains over vanilla DecQN (e.g., +37% on Humanoid-Stand), and the regularisation loss adds a further 5–10% improvement on top, confirming both components contribute meaningfully. In stochastic variants (Gaussian noise on rewards or states), the same performance hierarchy is maintained, with REValueD retaining its advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

REValueD builds directly on DecQN (Seyde et al., 2022), which transplanted value-decomposition from multi-agent RL into single-agent factorisable MDPs, and on BDQ (Tavakoli et al., 2018) for branching action architectures. Its theoretical core extends the classical overestimation-bias analysis of Thrun & Schwartz (1993) and Hasselt (2010) to the decomposed setting, while its two practical fixes—an ensembleble of critics (following the variance-reduction line of Anschel et al., 2017; Liang et al., 2022) and a credit-assignment-motivated regularization loss (drawing on MARL credit-assignment literature of Weiss, 1995; Wolpert & Tumer, 1999)—are each standard tools repurposed here. The paper's distinctive contribution is the clean theoretical characterization of the bias/variance trade-off specific to the mean-of-utilities decomposition, plus the observation that the regularization loss must operate at the individual-utility level rather than on the global Q-value.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result matters because high-dimensional discrete action spaces (up to 38 sub-action dimensions in the dog task) are a genuine bottleneck for value-based RL in robotics, and REValueD's ~25% wall-time overhead buys large asymptotic gains precisely where prior methods (DecQN, BDQ) stall. The clean Theorem 2 guarantee—that an K-critic ensembleble reduces target variance by exactly 1/K while leaving overestimation bias untouched—gives practitioners a principled dial for stability. Open questions the paper flags include whether distributional RL (C51-style) can further tame the uncertainty from explororatory sub-actions, and whether more sophisticated per-dimension exploration (rather than uninformative ε-greedy over the full Cartesian product) could close the remaining gap on the hardest tasks.
