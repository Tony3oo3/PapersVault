## Problem

Despite the explosion of predicted protein structures from AlphaFold and ESMFold, there is no standardized, comprehensive benchmark for systematically evaluating Geometric GNNs on protein structure representation learning. Prior work has focused almost exclusively on global (graph-level) representations for fold or function classification, leaving the quality of local (node-level) representations largely unexplored, and lacking a common framework to compare architectures, featurisation schemes, and pretraining strategies under consistent protocols.

## Input / Output

Protein structures are consumed as Cα-based geometric graphs with k-NN edges (k=16), progressively enriched with virtual dihedral/bond angles (κ, α), backbone torsion angles (φ, ψ, ω), and sidechain torsion angles (χ₁–χ₄) across five featurisation tiers. Outputs span node-level predictions (per-residue amino acid identity, binary binding-site labels, 13-class PTM labels) and graph-level predictions (1195-class fold, multi-label GO terms, 384-class reaction, binary developability), as well as denoising targets (uncorrupted coordinates, dihedral angles, or amino acid sequences) used during pretraining and as auxiliary losses.

## Method

ProteinWorkshop is a highly modular benchmark that unifies six Geometric GNN architectures (SchNet, EGNN, TFN, MACE, GCPNet, GearNet-Edge) plus ESM-2-650M augmented with structural features, evaluated across five featurisation schemes and eight downstream tasks at both node and graph granularity. Pretraining is performed on 2.27 million non-redundant AlphaFoldDB representative structures using denoising-based objectives—coordinate denoising (Gaussian noise σ=0.1 on Cα positions), torsional denoising (noise on backbone dihedrals with pNeRF reconstruction), sequence mutation (25% corruption), inverse folding, and pLDDT regression—followed by fine-tuning on supervised tasks. The key empirical findings are that (1) auxiliary sequence and structure denoising consistently improve performance across 50 of 60 model-task combinations, (2) equivariant GNNs benefit more from pretraining than invariant ones, and (3) incorporating backbone torsion angles in the input representation improves both pretraining and downstream performance more than architecture choice alone.

## Results

Across 10 downstream tasks, equivariant GNNs (EGNN, GCPNet) attain the best performance on 5 tasks, while auxiliary sequence and structure denoising improve results over no-auxiliary baselines for 50 out of 60 model–task combinations. GCPNet with full backbone torsion features (Cα+κ,α,θ,ψ,ω) and structure-denoising pretraining achieves the best Superfamily fold accuracy (57.17%) and lowest inverse-folding perplexity (6.55). Notably, ESM-2-650M augmented with structural featurisation matches state-of-the-art protein-specific GNNs on (super)family fold classification (72.71% vs. 72.58% for GCPNet) and gene ontology prediction (GO-MF: 0.583 vs. 0.497 for GCPNet).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GCPNet (protein-specific equivariant) is the strongest GNN overall, benefiting most from structure-denoising pretraining: it improves Superfamily fold accuracy from 49.81% (no pretraining) to 57.17% and inverse-folding perplexity from 7.56 to 6.55 when using full backbone torsion features. GearNet (protein-specific invariant) is competitive on GO prediction (GO-MF 0.497 with structure denoising) but trails GCPNet on fold classification (48.23% vs. 57.17% Superfamily). ESM-2-650M augmented with structural features is surprisingly competitive, matching or exceeding GCPNet on Superfamily fold (72.71% vs. 72.58%) and GO-MF (0.583 vs. 0.497), though it cannot perform node-level tasks like PPI or metal-binding site prediction. General-purpose invariant SchNet is consistently outperformed by equivariant counterparts (e.g., GO-BP: 0.355 vs. 0.397 for GearNet with best featurisation). Higher-order equivariant models TFN and MACE could not be pretrained on AlphaFoldDB due to computational constraints, but MACE's training was stabilised by structure denoising on GO and Reaction tasks where other runs failed to converge. EGNN, as a general-purpose equivariant baseline, sits between invariant and protein-specific equivariant models, confirming that equivariance alone is insufficient without protein-specific inductive biases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the denoising-based pretraining and regularisation framework of Godwin et al. (2021) and Zaidi et al. (2023) from small molecules to protein structures, and leverages FoldComp (Kim et al., 2023) for storage-efficient access to large-scale predicted structure corpora. It positions itself against prior protein structure benchmarks (ATOM3D, TorchProtein) by explicitly noting their focus on graph-level tasks and lack of systematic pretraining evaluation, and by adding node-level annotation tasks (inverse folding, PPI/metal/PTM site prediction) that prior benchmarks omitted. The benchmark unifies a wide architectural design space — from simple invariant SchNet to higher-order equivariant TFN and MACE (evaluated for proteins for the first time) — under a common featurisation and training protocol, filling a gap the authors identify as a 'robust, standardised benchmark' need in the field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ProteinWorkshop provides the first comprehensive, modular benchmark that jointly evaluates local (node-level) and global (graph-level) representational power of geometric GNNs on protein structures, with large-scale pretraining on 2.27M AlphaFoldDB structures. The finding that a sequence-based ESM-2-650M model augmented with structural features matches protein-specific GNNs on fold and GO prediction challenges the assumption that geometric inductive biases are strictly necessary for structure-based tasks, though this holds only for specific graph-level tasks. Open questions include whether contrastive or generative pretraining objectives (which the authors did not benchmark due to computational cost) might outperform denoising, whether the benchmark extends naturally to biomolecular complexes, and whether the compute limitations that prevented pretraining of spherical equivariant GNNs (TFN, MACE) will be resolved as hardware improves.
