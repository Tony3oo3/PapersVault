## Problem

It remains unclear whether standard minibatch SGD on a standard two-layer ReLU network can learn the XOR function (a canonical multi-index function with k=2, L=2) in near-optimal Õ(d) samples. Prior theoretical results achieving this sample complexity either modify the training algorithm (layer-wise training, non-standard architectures inducing quadratic NTK) or assume knowledge of the data's coordinate structure, breaking rotational invariance. The NTK regime provably requires Θ(d²) samples, leaving a gap between kernel methods and the conjectured optimal SGD complexity.

## Input / Output

The input is a Boolean vector x ∈ {-1,1}^d drawn uniformly from the d-dimensional hypercube, and the target label is y = -x₁x₂, depending only on the first two coordinates. The model is a 2-layer ReLU network of polynomial width p trained with minibatch SGD (minibatch size m ≥ d/θ, step size η ≤ θ) on the logistic loss, with initialization scale θ = 1/log^C(d). The result guarantees population error o(1) after Õ(d) samples and O(log(d)/η) iterations, with the algorithm being rotationally invariant so the result extends to any basis of the hypercube.

## Method

The paper proves a two-phase analysis of SGD dynamics. In the signal-finding phase, the network is small enough that the loss is well-approximated by its first-order expansion at zero output, causing neurons to evolve approximately independently; a symmetrization argument over paired samples (z+ξ, -z+ξ) shows that the signal-direction component w_sig grows exponentially while the orthogonal and opposing components remain bounded. In the signal-heavy phase, an inductive hypothesis ensures signal components dominate non-signal components, and the key mechanism is that simultaneous training of both layers creates positive feedback: neurons with large w_sig acquire large second-layer weights a_w, which amplifies their growth rate relative to non-signal neurons, maintaining the dominance gap. Under a clean surrogate loss derivative (depending only on the 2-dimensional signal component z), the four cluster margins grow and rebalance until all achieve ω(1) margin, yielding o(1) population loss.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 3.1) proves that standard minibatch SGD on a polynomial-width 2-layer ReLU network with logistic loss achieves population error $(\log d)^{-\Theta(1)}$ (i.e., $o(1)$) on the XOR function $y = -x_i x_j$ over the $d$-dimensional Boolean hypercube, using $\tilde{O}(d)$ samples and $\Theta(d \cdot \text{polylog}(d))$ iterations. Specifically, with initialization scale $\theta = 1/\log^C(d)$, minibatch size $m \geq d/\theta$, width $p \in [1/\theta, d^C]$, and step size $\eta \in [d^{-C}, \theta]$, the network reaches the target loss within $C\log(d)/\eta$ iterations with probability $1 - d^{-\omega(1)}$. The proof establishes a two-phase dynamics: a signal-finding phase of $\Theta(\log d / \eta)$ iterations where neurons independently grow in the signal direction, followed by a signal-heavy phase of $\Theta(\log\log d / \eta)$ iterations where SGD balances the four cluster margins.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines, so comparisons are to prior theoretical sample-complexity guarantees for learning XOR via neural networks. The NTK (linearized) regime requires $\Theta(d^2)$ samples (Wei et al., 2019; Ghorbani et al., 2021; Abbe et al., 2023), which this paper improves upon by a factor of $\tilde{\Theta}(d)$. Wei et al. (2019) and Bai & Lee (2019) / Chen et al. (2020) also achieve $\tilde{O}(d)$ samples, but require either finding the global optimum of a finite network, training an infinite-width network, or using a non-standard architecture that places SGD in a quadratic NTK regime. Abbe et al. (2023) achieves $\tilde{O}(d)$ with a layer-wise SGD algorithm but assumes knowledge of the coordinate system under which the data is structured, making it non-rotationally invariant. This paper is the first to achieve $\tilde{O}(d)$ sample complexity with a standard two-layer ReLU network trained via standard simultaneous-layer minibatch SGD on the logistic loss, without architectural modifications or coordinate-system assumptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of the multi-index function learning literature (Abbe et al., 2021a; 2022; 2023; Damian et al., 2022; Barak et al., 2022) and the NTK/feature-learning literature (Wei et al., 2019; Bai & Lee, 2019; Chen et al., 2020). It departs from prior work by using standard minibatch SGD on a standard two-layer ReLU network without layer-wise training, architecture modifications, or coordinate-system assumptions, whereas most prior results achieving Õ(d) sample complexity for XOR rely on at least one such non-standard ingredient. The two-phase analysis (signal-finding, then signal-heavy) is a new technical framework that goes beyond the mean-field and SDE approaches of Mei et al. (2018b; 2019) and Ben Arous et al. (2022), which break down after constant time in this setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first proof that standard SGD on a standard two-layer network achieves near-optimal Õ(d) sample complexity for XOR on isotropic Boolean data, closing a gap between what kernel methods can do (Θ(d²)) and what was previously provable for standard training. The two-phase framework and the insight that simultaneous layer training creates a positive feedback loop amplifying signal neurons provide a template for analyzing more general multi-index functions with k≥2 and L≥2, which remains open. Limitations include the restriction to the XOR function (k=2, L=2), two-layer architecture, and Boolean data; extending the signal-heavy inductive argument to deeper networks or continuous data distributions is a natural but non-trivial next step.
