## Problem

Conventional model evaluation requires labeled, i.i.d. test sets that are often unavailable in real-world deployment due to distribution shift and missing ground-truth labels. Existing Automated Model Evaluation (AutoEval) methods that bypass labeling suffer from overconfidence in model outputs, and most incur substantial storage and computational overhead by requiring access to training sets, multiple retrained models, or auxiliary self-supervised objectives. The paper asks whether a simpler, training-free, and resource-efficient AutoEval framework can be built directly from the classifier's own output on the unlabeled test set.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained classifier f (any architecture: CNN or transformer) and an unlabeled OOD test set D_u of N samples, plus a collection of synthetic shifted test sets with known accuracies for calibrating a linear regressor. No training-set access, model retraining, or auxiliary tasks are required at evaluation time. The output is a single scalar predicted accuracy for the OOD set, obtained by applying the fitted linear regressor to the MDE statistic computed over D_u; evaluation is quantified via Spearman's ρ, Pearson's r, R², and mean absolute error against ground-truth accuracy.

## Method

The core contribution is Meta-Distribution Energy (MDE), which treats the classifier as an energy-based model with free energy Z(x;f) = −T·log Σ_j exp(f_j(x)/T) derived from the softmax denominator. Rather than averaging raw energies, MDE re-normalizes each sample's energy into a meta-probabilistic distribution over the dataset: MDE = −(1/|N|) Σ_i log [exp(Z(x_i;f)) / Σ_i exp(Z(x_i;f))], yielding a smoother, temperature-calibrated statistic that mitigates overconfidence. A simple linear regressor (w, b) is fit on (accuracy, MDE) pairs from synthetic shifted sets, then applied to the unlabeled OOD set to predict accuracy. Theorem 3.1 provides a theoretical guarantee: under a well-trained model, the per-sample difference MDE^i − NLL^i equals f_{y_i}(x_i)/T − max_j f_j(x_i)/T, which is zero for correct predictions and negative for incorrect ones, establishing a direct link between MDE and classification accuracy.

## Results

MDE achieves Spearman's rank correlation ρ > 0.981 on vision datasets (CIFAR-10: ρ=0.989, R²=0.973; CIFAR-100: ρ=0.988, R²=0.966; TinyImageNet: ρ=0.994, R²=0.977; ImageNet: ρ=0.985, R²=0.960) and ρ > 0.846 on text (MNLI: ρ=0.850, R²=0.680). On natural shifted datasets, MDE reduces the average MAE from 5.25 (prior SOTA NuclearNorm) to 3.14, a ~40% improvement. On WILDS benchmarks, MDE achieves MAE of 2.93 (Camelyon17), 1.62 (RxRx1), and 2.24 (FMoW), consistently outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MDE outperforms all training-free baselines (ConfScore, Entropy, Frechet, ATC) across every dataset and backbone, with the largest margins on TinyImageNet (ρ=0.994 vs. 0.948 for ConfScore) and MNLI (ρ=0.850 vs. 0.692 for ConfScore). Against the training-must methods AgreeScore and ProjNorm, MDE achieves comparable or better correlation while requiring no additional model training or storage of the training set. Compared to the prior SOTA NuclearNorm, MDE wins on R² across all vision datasets (e.g., CIFAR-10: 0.973 vs. 0.935; TinyImageNet: 0.977 vs. 0.969) and dramatically on MNLI (R²=0.680 vs. 0.496, ρ=0.850 vs. 0.668). On natural shifted datasets, MDE reduces average MAE by ~40% over NuclearNorm (3.14 vs. 5.25), with particularly large gains on CIFAR-10 (1.78 vs. 3.42) and TinyImageNet (2.15 vs. 3.84). MDE also consistently outperforms the closely related AvgEnergy metric, confirming that the meta-distribution re-normalization provides a smoother and more discriminative representation. On WILDS benchmarks (Camelyon17, RxRx1, FMoW), MDE achieves the lowest MAE among all methods, e.g., 1.62 on RxRx1 vs. 3.67 for NuclearNorm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the classifier-as-energy-based-model equivalence established by Grathwohl et al. (2019) and Xie et al. (2016), repurposing the Gibbs free energy of a softmax classifier as a dataset-level evaluation statistic. It departs from prior AutoEval methods (ConfScore, Entropy, ATC, NuclearNorm, ProjNorm) by replacing raw confidence or matrix-norm measures with a meta-distribution re-normalization of per-sample energies, which the authors argue mitigates overconfidence and yields a smoother representation. The theoretical grounding via Theorem 3.1 (connecting MDE to negative log-likelihood loss) is novel within the AutoEval literature, where prior methods largely lack such a direct loss-function linkage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MDE offers a training-free, plug-and-play accuracy predictor that reduces mean absolute error by ~40% over the prior SOTA (NuclearNorm) while requiring no access to training data or additional model training, making it practical for evaluating large-scale and off-the-shelf models including LLMs. Key limitations remain: the method depends on a calibration set of synthetic shifts to fit the linear regressor, and performance degrades under extreme adversarial or severely imbalanced conditions. Open questions include reducing the calibration sample requirement toward a one-shot version and extending the energy framework to handle distribution shifts where per-sample energy no longer reflects information content.
