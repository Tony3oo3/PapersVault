## Problem

Existing characterizations of Bayes-optimal classifiers under group fairness constraints (Menon & Williamson, 2018) only cover Demographic Parity and Equalized Opportunity, leaving Equalized Odds and composite multi-attribute criteria unaddressed. Additionally, most post-processing fairness methods require access to sensitive attributes at inference time, which is impractical in real-world deployments where such attributes are unavailable due to privacy constraints.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained score-based classifier p̂(Y|X) and an auxiliary model p̂(A|X) (for DP/EOp) or p̂(Y,A|X) (for EO), both fitted on training data or provided by a third party. It outputs a modification rule κ(X) — a single threshold on one bias score for DP/EOp, or a 2D linear rule over two bias scores for EO — which flips the base classifier's prediction on selected instances. Crucially, at inference time only X is needed; the sensitive attribute is never observed, since bias scores are computed entirely from the estimated conditional distributions.

## Method

The paper defines an instance-level bias score s_k(X) = f_k(X)/η(X), where f_k(X) captures how much flipping the prediction at X would shift the group disparity for criterion k, and η(X) = 2p(Y=Ŷ|X)−1 measures the unconstrained classifier's confidence. Theorem 1 proves (via a generalized Neyman-Pearson argument) that the Bayes-optimal constrained classifier is obtained by thresholding a linear combination of these bias scores: κ(X) = 1{Σ_k z_k s_k(X) > 1}. The practical MBS algorithm computes plug-in bias scores from estimated conditionals, then fits the threshold (DP/EOp) or 2D linear rule (EO) on a labeled validation set to satisfy the target fairness level δ while maximizing accuracy. For DP this is an O(N_val log N_val) sweep over sorted scores; for EO it reduces to searching O(M²) candidate lines through validation points or O(K N_val log N_val) over fixed angular directions.

## Results

On CelebA (6 tasks with EO constraints), MBS achieves 79.3–85.1% test accuracy while reducing EO from 13.7–44.9% (unconstrained) to 1.7–10.6% depending on the target δ, consistently outperforming H16 by 8–10 percentage points in accuracy. On Adult Census, MBS achieves better accuracy–DP and accuracy–EO trade-offs than the in-processing baseline Z17 across all constraint levels (δ = 10%, 5%, 1%). On COMPAS, MBS outperforms Z17 but underperforms J19 for DP due to the small dataset size (5,278 instances) limiting reliable estimation of p(Y|X) and p(A|X).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Z17 (in-processing fairness constraints), MBS consistently achieves higher accuracy at every DP and EO level on both Adult Census and COMPAS, while also offering the flexibility to adjust the fairness–accuracy trade-off post-hoc without retraining. Against J19 (post-processing, DP only), MBS wins on Adult Census but loses on COMPAS, where the small validation set (1,056 instances) makes the threshold selection less robust. Against H16 (group-aware thresholding for EO), MBS gains 8–10 percentage points in accuracy across all six CelebA tasks (e.g., 80.9% vs. 71.7% for target 'Attractive' / sensitive 'Male' at δ=10), while achieving comparable or lower EO. Against P22 (fair contrastive learning, state-of-the-art for CelebA), MBS matches or exceeds P22 on most tasks: for example, at δ=5, MBS achieves 80.1% ACC / 2.2% EO vs. P22's 79.1% / 6.5% for (a,m), and 84.2% / 4.1% vs. 84.1% / 4.8% for (b,y), though P22 edges out MBS slightly on (e,y) at δ=1 (83.5% / 1.6% vs. 83.6% / 1.8%). A key practical advantage is that MBS does not require access to sensitive attributes at inference time, unlike J19 and H16.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bayes-optimal fair classifier characterization of Menon & Williamson (2018), which covered only Demographic Parity and Equalized Opportunity, to the full family of group fairness metrics including Equalized Odds and composite criteria involving multiple sensitive attributes. Conceptually, it departs from both in-processing approaches (Zafar et al., 2017; Donini et al., 2018) that modify training, and from prior post-processing methods (Hardt et al., 2016a; Jiang et al., 2019; Jang et al., 2022) that require sensitive attributes at inference time. The key conceptual move is introducing an instance-level 'bias score' that reduces the optimal modification to a simple threshold (for DP/EOp) or 2D linear rule (for EO), analogous to the Neyman-Pearson lemma applied to fairness constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that MBS enables flexible accuracy-fairness trade-off adjustment without retraining and without accessing sensitive attributes during inference, which is a significant deployment advantage in privacy-sensitive settings. However, the method requires auxiliary models estimating p(A|X) or p(Y,A|X), and performance degrades on small datasets (COMPAS) where these estimates are unreliable. Open questions include whether the characterization extends cleanly to multi-class targets, how the method behaves under distribution shift between training and deployment, and whether the auxiliary model requirement can be further relaxed (the CLIP zero-shot experiment is a promising but limited first step).
