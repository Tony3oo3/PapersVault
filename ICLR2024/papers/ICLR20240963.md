## Problem

The Fast Equilibrium Conjecture states that SGD on scale-invariant (normalized) networks mixes to equilibrium in polynomial time O(1/(ηλ)) rather than the exponential time expected from classical Langevin dynamics. Prior proofs by Wang & Wang (2022) and Li et al. (2022c) required non-generic assumptions—isolated minima, a unique attracting basin, and analyticity of the minima set—that do not hold in general. This paper removes all such assumptions to establish the conjecture in full generality.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a scale-invariant C⁴ loss L, a noise matrix σ satisfying a controllability condition on the tangent space of the minima manifold, initial parameters x₀ with bounded norm, and hyperparameters in the regime η ≤ O(λ) ≤ O(1) with ηλ → 0. The output is a basin-local equilibrium measure ν^i supported on the minima cone Γ^i of the unique basin containing x₀, with a Lipschitz-distance bound dist(P_{X_t}, ν^i) < ε for all t in [K log(1+λ/η)/(ηλ), e^{c/√(ηλ)}]. Crucially, the equilibrium is local (concentrated near the basin of the initial point) and temporary, differing from the global Gibbs measure that would emerge only after exponentially long times.

## Method

The proof rests on three pillars: (1) a fast-entry argument showing trajectories reach O(1/(ηλ))-neighborhoods of the minima manifold within O(1/(ηλ)) steps; (2) a large-deviation-based confinement result (Proposition 4.2) proving that with probability → 1, trajectories remain in their basin for e^{C(ηλ)^{-1/2}} time, using an adaptation of the Dembo-Zeitouni upper LDP to handle the degenerate diffusion and the extra control variable γ_t = |X_t|⁴η⁻²; and (3) a mixing argument via the Katzenberger model showing that conditioned on staying in the basin, the distribution converges uniformly to ν^i. To remove the analyticity assumption, the authors replace Kliemann's condition (requiring maximal Lie algebra dimension everywhere) with the Arnold-Kliemann condition (requiring it at only one point), then verify Harris recurrence and regularity to establish convergence in total variation.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 2.4) establishes that under only generic assumptions (scale invariance, regular critical locus, controllability), the SGD trajectory on a scale-invariant loss with weight decay mixes to a local equilibrium measure ν^i supported on the minima manifold Γ^i within a polynomial time window of O(1/(ηλ)) steps, rather than the exponential e^(O((ηλ)^(-1/2))) predicted by classical Langevin analysis. Empirically, on reduced MNIST (1280 samples, 1786-param CNN, η=λ=1/32, 0.8M steps) and reduced CIFAR-10 (1280 samples, 2658-param CNN, η=1/1024, λ=1/32, 1.28M steps), the within-group variance V11 and V22 stabilize at similar but distinct values while the cross-group variance V12 stabilizes at a substantially larger value, confirming that trajectories remain confined to their initial basin. Additionally, SWAP (stochastic weight averaging in parallel) is shown to degrade after a few thousand steps, validating the prediction that the equilibrium support is a curved manifold rather than a convex set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines to compare against; its contribution is removing non-generic assumptions from prior theoretical results. Compared to Li et al. (2022c), which required both a unique basin (Assumption 1.3.i) and analyticity of the minima set (Assumption 1.3.ii), this paper proves the same fast equilibrium result under only the generic Assumption 1.2 (scale invariance, regular critical locus, controllability). Compared to Wang & Wang (2022), which required all minima to be isolated points and the noise to be standard isotropic Gaussian, this paper handles general (possibly degenerate) noise structures and non-isolated minima manifolds. The key technical advance is replacing the semi-classical spectral analysis of Simon (1983) used by both prior works with a probabilistic large deviation argument (adapted from Dembo & Zeitouni, 2010) that handles degenerate diffusion with an extra control variable, yielding the exponential lower bound on basin exit time without requiring analyticity or isolated minima.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper completes the proof of the Fast Equilibrium Conjecture (Li et al., 2020) by removing all non-generic assumptions that previous partial proofs required. Li et al. (2022c) proved the conjecture assuming a unique basin and analyticity of the minima set, while Wang & Wang (2022) assumed isolated minima and isotropic Gaussian noise; this work eliminates all of these simultaneously. The key technical departure is replacing the semi-classical spectral analysis (Simon, 1983; Bovier et al., 2004) used by prior authors with a purely probabilistic large-deviation argument (Dembo-Zeitouni, 2010) adapted to handle degenerate diffusion with an extra control variable, and replacing Kliemann's condition with the weaker Arnold-Kliemann condition to drop analyticity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This result establishes that the polynomial-time fast equilibrium of SGD with normalization is a generic phenomenon, not an artifact of restrictive geometric assumptions, which strengthens the theoretical foundation for why normalization layers accelerate training in practice. The three-stage equilibrium phenomenon—where the distribution is locally trapped in the initial basin for practical time windows but eventually converges to a global Gibbs equilibrium in exponential time—provides a rigorous explanation for the gap between empirical observations and classical Langevin-based estimates. Open questions include understanding the dependence of mixing time on network dimension, architecture, and noise structure, and whether the large-deviation framework extends to non-scale-invariant settings. The experimental prediction that SWAP (stochastic weight averaging in parallel) fails once SGD mixes to a curved local manifold is a concrete, testable consequence that could guide practical averaging strategies.
