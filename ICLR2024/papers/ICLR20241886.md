## Problem

Open-source LLMs still struggle with complex mathematical reasoning, and existing approaches force a choice between natural language rationales (which handle planning and abstract reasoning but fail at precise computation) and program synthesis (which excels at rigorous operations but lacks semantic analysis and error recovery). No prior open-source method synergistically combines both modalities within a single reasoning trajectory, leaving a significant gap with closed-source models like GPT-4 on competition-level benchmarks.

## Input / Output

The model receives a mathematical problem as plain text (including Asymptote diagram descriptions for geometry problems) and produces an interleaved trajectory of the form r₁a₁o₁…rₙ₋₁aₙ₋₁oₙ₋₁rₙ, where each rᵢ is a natural language rationale, aᵢ is a Python program calling external tools (sympy, rational, matrices, etc.), and oᵢ is the execution output fed back into the model. The final rationale rₙ must contain the answer enclosed in \boxed{}. Inference uses greedy decoding with a maximum of 3 tool-execution rounds and a 2,048-token sequence limit, averaging 1.02 interaction rounds per problem.

## Method

TORA introduces an interleaved tool-integrated reasoning format where the model alternates between generating natural language rationales for analysis/planning and writing Python programs that invoke external solvers (sympy, rational, matrices) for computation, with execution outputs fed back for multi-round self-correction. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) imitation learning on TORA-CORPUS, a 16k-sample dataset of interactive tool-use trajectories synthesized by GPT-4 on GSM8k and MATH training sets; and (2) output space shaping, which samples 64 diverse trajectories per question from the imitation-learned model, retains valid ones, and corrects invalid ones by truncating at the error point and completing with a larger teacher model (CodeLLaMA-34B), then retrains on the combined 69k annotations. This shaping step is critical for preventing the model from overfitting to a single trajectory per problem and for mitigating improper tool-use behavior, yielding 3–5% absolute gains on MATH. The approach is applied to LLaMA-2 and CodeLLaMA (7B–70B), with CodeLLaMA variants showing ~5% additional improvement from continued code pretraining.

## Results

TORA achieves 13%–19% absolute average improvements over the best open-source baselines across 10 mathematical reasoning datasets at all model scales (7B–70B). On the competition-level MATH dataset, TORA-CODE-7B reaches 44.6% accuracy, surpassing the previous open-source SOTA WizardMath-70B (22.7%) by 22% absolute. TORA-CODE-34B achieves 50.8% on MATH, exceeding GPT-4's CoT result (42.5%) by 8.3% absolute and approaching GPT-4 with code (51.8%). On GSM8k, TORA-70B attains 84.3%, outperforming ChatGPT with both CoT (80.8%) and PAL (78.6%) prompting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TORA consistently outperforms all open-source baselines across every model scale. On MATH, TORA-CODE-7B (44.6%) surpasses WizardMath-70B (22.7%) by 22% absolute and CodeLLaMA-34B PAL (23.9%) by 20.7% absolute, demonstrating that tool-integrated reasoning at 7B scale exceeds rationale-only or program-only approaches at 34–70B scale. TORA-CODE-34B (50.8% MATH) beats GPT-4 CoT (42.5%) by 8.3% absolute and is within 1% of GPT-4 PAL (51.8%), making it the first open-source model to exceed 50% on MATH. On GSM8k, TORA-70B (84.3%) outperforms ChatGPT CoT (80.8%) and ChatGPT PAL (78.6%), while TORA-CODE-34B (80.7%) matches ChatGPT CoT. Compared to Toolformer (7B), TORA-7B achieves substantially higher accuracy on SVAMP (68.2% vs. 29.4%) and MAWPS (88.8% vs. 44.0%), indicating that interleaved rationale-program reasoning is far more effective than calculator-only tool use. TORA also shows superior out-of-distribution generalization: TORA-70B reaches 74.0% on TabMWP while WizardMath-70B (49.8%) underperforms even the base LLaMA-2-70B (57.5%).

## Relation to Prior Work

TORA synthesizes two previously separate lines of mathematical reasoning research: natural language chain-of-thought (Wei et al., 2022) and program-aided language models like PAL (Gao et al., 2022) and Program of Thoughts (Chen et al., 2022). The key conceptual departure is the interleaved trajectory format that alternates rationales and program-based tool calls within a single reasoning chain, drawing inspiration from ReAct-style agent paradigms (Yao et al., 2023) but specialized for mathematical computation with symbolic solvers like SymPy. On the training side, TORA extends the knowledge distillation / imitation learning paradigm used by WizardMath and Platypus with a novel 'output space shaping' step that combines nucleus sampling of diverse valid trajectories with teacher-model correction of invalid ones, addressing the rigidity of single-trajectory imitation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TORA establishes that open-source models can match or exceed GPT-4's CoT performance on competition-level math (TORA-CODE-34B at 50.8% vs. GPT-4 CoT at 42.5%), and the output space shaping technique offers a practical, data-efficient training recipe applicable beyond mathematics. However, the failure mode analysis reveals that 38% of errors stem from fundamental reasoning mistakes and 21% from diagram misinterpretation, indicating that tool integration alone cannot resolve the core reasoning bottleneck. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to domains requiring visual-spatial reasoning (geometry), how to handle problems that resist formalization into programs, and whether the 3-round tool interaction limit is sufficient for the hardest Level-5 MATH problems where a gap to GPT-4 persists.
