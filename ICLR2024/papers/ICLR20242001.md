## Problem

Existing post-training quantization (PTQ) methods designed for dense 2D RGB images suffer severe performance degradation when applied to 3D LiDAR-based detectors, because point clouds exhibit extreme sparsity (~90% zero pixels), much larger arithmetic ranges (1504×1504×40 voxel space vs. 8-bit RGB), and extreme foreground-background imbalance. Standard entropy calibration clips geometrically critical activation values, causing up to 84.5% accuracy loss on long-range detection, and no PTQ method had been specifically designed for LiDAR-based 3D object detection.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained FP32 3D detector (CenterPoint-Pillar, CenterPoint-Voxel, or fully sparse FSD) and a small unlabeled calibration set of 256 point cloud frames (~0.16% of Waymo training data). It outputs per-layer INT8 quantization parameters (scale factors, zero-points, and adaptive rounding values θ) that, when applied, allow the quantized model to accept raw LiDAR point clouds and produce 3D bounding boxes with near-FP32 accuracy. Notably, the Pillar Feature Net in CenterPoint-Pillar is kept in FP32 because its 3D coordinate inputs (range ±10² m, precision 0.01 m) would lose critical information under INT8.

## Method

LiDAR-PTQ is a three-component layer-wise PTQ pipeline. First, a sparsity-aware calibration uses Max-min range (instead of entropy) plus a lightweight grid search over candidate scale factors to initialize quantization parameters, preserving the wide dynamic range that encodes geometric distance information. Second, a Task-guided Global Positive Loss (TGPL) optimizes activation quantization parameters in model space by using the FP model's own predictions as pseudo-labels: top-K boxes filtered by a confidence threshold and NMS define positive positions via Gaussian center-peak assignment, and a combined focal loss (classification heatmap) plus L1 loss (regression) drives the quantized model's final output toward the FP output without any labeled data. Third, an adaptive rounding-to-nearest operation (inspired by AdaRound) introduces a per-weight learnable offset θ that is jointly optimized with a local layer-wise reconstruction loss to minimize rounding error. The overall per-layer loss is a weighted sum of the local reconstruction term and TGPL, and the process proceeds sequentially from backbone to head, requiring only ~3 GPU-hours versus ~94 GPU-hours for QAT.

## Results

On the Waymo Open Dataset (LEVEL_2 mAPH), LiDAR-PTQ achieves 60.12 mAPH on CenterPoint-Pillar and 65.18 mAPH on CenterPoint-Voxel, nearly matching the FP32 baselines (60.32 and 65.25 respectively) with only a 0.20 and 0.07 mAPH drop. The quantized INT8 model delivers a 3× inference speedup on NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin (e.g., CP-Pillar: 38.4 FPS → 113.4 FPS). The quantization process itself takes only ~3 GPU-hours, making it 30× faster than QAT (~94 GPU-hours) while achieving comparable or superior accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LiDAR-PTQ outperforms all three 2D-vision PTQ baselines by substantial margins. On CenterPoint-Pillar, it beats BRECQ by 3.87 mAPH (60.12 vs 56.27), QDrop by 1.98 mAPH (60.12 vs 58.14), and PD-Quant by 1.06 mAPH (60.12 vs 59.06). On CenterPoint-Voxel, the gaps widen: +4.47 mAPH over BRECQ, +2.95 mAPH over QDrop, and +1.18 mAPH over PD-Quant. Against QAT (which requires full labeled training data and ~94 GPU-hours), LiDAR-PTQ achieves on-par accuracy (60.12 vs 60.08 on CP-Pillar; 65.18 vs 65.20 on CP-Voxel) while being 30× faster to run. The paper attributes PD-Quant's suboptimal performance to its BN-based activation adjustment being ineffective for point clouds whose arithmetic range is tightly coupled to geometric distance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PTQ lineage from 2D RGB vision (AdaRound, BRECQ, QDrop, PD-Quant) to 3D LiDAR-based object detection, identifying that the core calibration assumptions of these methods break down under point cloud sparsity (~90% zeros) and larger arithmetic ranges. It departs from prior 2D PTQ by replacing entropy-based calibration with a sparsity-aware max-min initialization, introducing a task-guided global positive loss (TGPL) that supervises quantization parameters via the final detection output rather than layer-wise reconstruction, and adapting AdaRound's rounding optimization with a local reconstruction term. The empirical diagnosis of why entropy calibration catastrophically fails on LiDAR (contradicting 2D experience) is the key conceptual departure from the prior PTQ literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first PTQ method to achieve near-FP32 accuracy on LiDAR 3D detection with a 3× inference speedup on edge hardware (Jetson AGX Orin), making INT8 deployment of grid-based detectors like CenterPoint practical for real-time autonomous driving without requiring labeled data or full retraining. The 30× speedup over QAT makes it cost-effective for industrial adoption. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to lower bit-widths (INT4/INT2), transformer-based 3D detectors, and whether the TGPL design transfers to other sparse-data modalities beyond LiDAR.
