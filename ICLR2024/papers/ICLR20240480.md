## Problem

No general-purpose, open-source, and computationally affordable pre-trained model exists for acoustic music understanding. Existing approaches either target only music tagging with supervised labels, rely on cumbersome billion-parameter generative models like Jukebox (making inference prohibitively slow), or lack publicly available code and checkpoints. A key unresolved challenge is how to capture the distinctive tonal and pitched characteristics of music that differ fundamentally from speech, which standard speech SSL paradigms do not address.

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw 24 kHz music audio waveforms truncated to 5-second segments (roughly a 2-bar musical context), with a convolutional 1D encoder producing frame-level representations at 75 Hz. During pre-training, the model outputs predictions over 8 discrete acoustic codebooks (1024 entries each from EnCodec) at masked positions and a continuous CQT spectrogram reconstruction. At inference, frozen transformer layer embeddings serve as fixed-length feature vectors for downstream probing heads (MLP for frame-level tasks, 3-layer BiLSTM for source separation), with a 30-second sliding window for long recordings.

## Method

MERT adapts the HuBERT MLM architecture (1D conv encoder + transformer) to music by introducing a dual-teacher multi-task pre-training objective: an acoustic teacher (EnCodec RVQ-VAE) provides 8-layer discrete tokens at 75 Hz for cross-entropy prediction at masked positions, while a musical teacher (CQT spectrogram) provides continuous pitch/harmonic targets reconstructed via MSE loss, with the total loss being a weighted sum of both. The key insight is that combining a deep learned acoustic codec with an explicit harmonic inductive bias (CQT) captures complementary information that neither provides alone, as confirmed by ablations showing CQT alone dramatically improves key detection from 15.6% to 65.0% accuracy. For training stability at scale (95M→330M parameters), the authors employ Pre-LN normalization, attention relaxation (a scale constant in softmax), and an in-batch noise mixup augmentation that adds random shorter audio excerpts to each sample, which specifically benefits the RVQ-VAE teacher setting. The model is trained on 160K hours of internet-mined music (or 910 hours of public Music4All for the public variant) using fairseq on 64 A100 GPUs with fp16.

## Results

MERT-330M^RVQ-VAE achieves an overall average score of 64.7 across 14 downstream MIR tasks, matching the combined previous SOTA (64.5) from 10 different models while using only 6.6% of Jukebox-5B's parameters. The smaller MERT-95M^RVQ-VAE variant reaches 63.7, already surpassing data2vec-base^music (59.9) and HuBERT-base^music (55.8) by 3.8 and 7.9 points respectively. On individual tasks, MERT-330M attains 78.1 ROC on MTG Instrument, 29.9 AP on MTG Top50, and 94.4% accuracy on NSynth pitch classification, setting new SOTA on 4 metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MERT-330M^RVQ-VAE (64.7 avg) outperforms the two retrained speech SSL baselines by a wide margin: data2vec-base^music (59.9) by 4.8 points and HuBERT-base^music (55.8) by 8.9 points, confirming that the multi-task acoustic+musical teacher paradigm is more effective than either K-means-only or EMA-based pseudo-label strategies for music. Against the supervised/contrastive music baselines (MusiCNN, CLMR, MULE), MERT-330M leads on MTG Instrument ROC (78.1 vs. 76.6 for MULE) and MTG Top50 AP (29.9 vs. 30.6 for MULE, a narrow loss), while MULE retains an edge on MTG MoodTheme AP (15.4 vs. 14.0) and MTG Genre AP (20.4 vs. 18.6). Compared to Jukebox-5B/JukeMIR, MERT-330M achieves comparable or better results on all shared tasks (e.g., 94.4% vs. 91.6% on NSynth pitch, 79.3% vs. 79.7% on GTZAN genre) while being ~15× smaller in parameter count, making it far more practical for inference. On source separation (MUSDB18), MERT-330M trails the dedicated SOTA (SDR 9.3/10.8/10.4/6.4) but substantially exceeds Jukebox-5B (5.1/4.9/4.1/2.7) and data2vec (5.5/5.5/4.1/3.0), showing that the general-purpose representation captures useful separation cues. The MERT-95M-public variant, trained solely on the 910-hour Music4All dataset, reaches 63.0 avg—nearly matching the full-data MERT-95M (62.9)—demonstrating that the paradigm converges effectively even with limited, less diverse training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

MERT directly adapts the HuBERT masked-prediction-of-hidden-units paradigm from speech SSL to music audio, replacing HuBERT's K-means-on-MFCC teacher with a dual-teacher design: an RVQ-VAE (EnCodec) for discrete acoustic tokens and a CQT spectrogram reconstruction loss for pitch/harmonic inductive bias. This departs from prior music SSL efforts (MusiCNN, CLMR, MULE) that relied on supervised tagging labels or contrastive objectives, and from Jukebox/JukeMIR which used a 5B-parameter hierarchical autoregressive decoder. The architectural backbone follows wav2vec 2.0's convolutional relative positional embedding, while the multi-task loss combining acoustic MLM and musical reconstruction is the paper's core methodological contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MERT provides the first open-source, computationally affordable (95M–330M parameters, ~7% of Jukebox) general-purpose pre-trained model for acoustic music understanding, achieving SOTA or near-SOTA across 14 diverse MIR tasks under a unified probing protocol. The finding that a CQT musical teacher is critical for pitch-sensitive tasks (key detection jumps from ~15% to ~65% accuracy) and that a 910-hour public dataset yields competitive performance with 160K hours of proprietary data are practically significant for the research community. Key open questions include whether the 5-second training context limits performance on long-range musical structure, whether the observed inverse-scaling at 330M on certain tasks (e.g., beat tracking) reflects a fundamental architectural limitation, and whether the multi-teacher paradigm can be extended to additional musical dimensions (rhythm, form, lyrics).
