## Problem

Existing retrieval-augmented LLMs are designed for unstructured text and fail on structured tables due to diverse data types, large table sizes, and complex relational semantics. Prior open-domain table reasoning systems require task-specific fine-tuning of both retriever and reasoner, limiting transferability to new tables, while closed-domain methods assume gold tables are provided. The paper addresses the gap of building an end-to-end, fine-tuning-free framework for open-domain table reasoning that scales to large table corpora.

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural language query (a question for QA or a statement for fact verification) and a corpus of candidate tables (e.g., 24,680 tables for Open-WikiTables, 26,177 for FEVEROUS). It produces a natural language answer or an entailment/contradiction judgment. Critically, the LLM never sees full tables: a ROWSELECTOR uses BM25 to select only top-k relevant rows per table, and the CODER generates SQL programs that are executed against the full database, so the LLM operates on schema plus a few sampled rows rather than raw table contents.

## Method

OPENTAB decomposes open-domain table reasoning into a BM25-based RETRIEVER and an LLM-powered REASONER consisting of three sub-modules: a CODER that generates three progressively complex SQL programs (basic column selection, intermediate row filtering, advanced aggregation/text operations) via simple-to-complex prompting; a ROWSELECTOR that BM25-reranks rows to fit within token limits; and a READER that parses SQL execution results alongside table schema and generated queries to produce the final answer. The key architectural innovation is Generative Reranking & Sequential Reasoning (GRSR), which sequentially generates SQL for each retrieved table and then reranks tables by Cross-Encoder similarity between the query and the generated SQL, effectively filtering out hallucinated SQLs on irrelevant tables. The entire pipeline requires no fine-tuning—only 2-shot in-context learning for both CODER and READER—making it off-the-shelf deployable on new databases.

## Results

On the open-domain Open-WikiTables dataset with gpt-3.5-turbo, OPENTAB achieves 56.5% execution accuracy at top-10 retrieval, outperforming the best baseline (BINDER at 37.5%) by 21.5 percentage points. On FEVEROUS (open-domain fact verification), OPENTAB reaches 69.5% accuracy at top-1 and 56.3% at top-5 with gpt-3.5-turbo. In the closed-domain setting, OPENTAB achieves 64.1% on WikiTableQuestions, surpassing the best fine-tuned method (OmniTab at 63.3%) without any task-specific training, and 71.0% on OpenWikiTable versus 57.9% for the LLM-SQL baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OPENTAB consistently outperforms all baselines across both open- and closed-domain settings. On open-domain Open-WikiTables (gpt-3.5-turbo, top-10), OPENTAB (56.5%) beats BINDER (37.5%) by 19 points and LLM-SQL (10.9%) by over 45 points; the gap widens further with falcon-180B where OPENTAB reaches 43.7% versus BINDER's 22.7%. On closed-domain WikiTableQuestions, OPENTAB (64.1%) surpasses all fine-tuned methods including OmniTab (63.3%), TaCube (61.1%), TAPEX (60.4%), and T5-3B/UnifiedSKG (51.9%), as well as the non-fine-tuned DATER (45.3%) and BINDER (42.8%). For table retrieval, BM25 (used by OPENTAB) achieves competitive recall against fine-tuned DPR-BERT (e.g., 0.893 vs 0.933 at Recall@10 on Open-WikiTables) while dramatically outperforming non-fine-tuned DPR-BERT which scores near zero due to poor transferability. On FEVEROUS, OPENTAB leads at top-1 (69.5% vs 67.2% for LLM-Answer) and top-5 (56.3% vs 51.5% for LLM-SQL), though the margin narrows compared to Open-WikiTables.

## Relation to Prior Work

OPENTAB sits at the intersection of retrieval-augmented generation and LLM-based table reasoning, but deliberately departs from both threads. Prior open-domain table QA systems (Chen et al. 2021b, Herzig et al. 2021, Kweon et al. 2023) rely on fine-tuned dense retrievers and fine-tuned table encoders, while LLM-based table reasoners like BINDER and DATER operate exclusively in the closed-domain setting where the gold table is provided. OPENTAB repurposes the text-to-SQL paradigm as an intermediate reasoning step (rather than an end goal) and pairs it with a simple BM25 retriever, creating a fully fine-tuning-free pipeline that bridges the gap between these two previously separate research directions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that OPENTAB offers an off-the-shelf, fine-tuning-free solution for open-domain table reasoning, which is highly desirable for industrial deployments where proprietary table corpora change frequently and task-specific training is infeasible. The GRSR strategy and simple-to-complex prompting are reusable techniques that address two concrete failure modes (LLM hallucination in SQL generation and brittle single-query generation). Open questions remain around scalability to truly massive table stores (millions of tables), generalization beyond SQL-expressible operations, and whether the Cross Encoder reranking component can be replaced by purely generative signals as LLMs improve.
