## Problem

Existing VLM benchmarks rely on rigid, hand-crafted datasets and rule-based metrics that are labor-intensive, limited in scale, and unable to assess open-ended or value-aligned behaviors. This paper asks whether LLMs can serve as both automated data curators and automated judges to comprehensively evaluate VLMs' alignment with human capabilities and values at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241904/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes COCO images enriched with four types of visual symbolic representations—captions, bounding-box coordinates, instance relationships, and optical character descriptions—which are serialized into structured text prompts for GPT-4. It produces question-answer-reasoning triplets (28.5K human-verified, 3,504K raw) spanning 4 capacities and 16 sub-skills, and for evaluation outputs per-skill quantitative accuracy scores plus qualitative rankings derived from a five-round Swiss tournament ELO system.

## Method

Auto-Bench employs two LLM roles: GPT-4 as curator, prompted with skill-specific instructional templates and the visual symbolization text to generate capacity-targeted QA triplets with chain-of-thought reasoning, and GPT-3.5 Turbo as judge, using ability-specific prompts to assess semantic alignment (open-ended) or option matching (closed-ended) between VLM responses and ground-truth answers. The visual symbolization step is critical—rather than feeding raw images, the pipeline encodes spatial, relational, and textual scene information into a text sequence that GPT-4 can reason over, enabling generation of questions that probe fine-grained perception, commonsense/expert reasoning, planning, and value alignment. Human verification (800 triplets) confirms 85%+ agreement with LLM judgments, and a user study against five existing VQA datasets validates data quality. The 3,504K raw triplets also serve as SFT training data, yielding a +29.7% accuracy gain on counting for MiniGPT-4.

## Results

On Auto-Bench, InstructBLIP achieves the highest average perception accuracy (34.7%) and competitive planning (19.4%), while BLIP2 leads in reasoning (65.3% average across commonsense and expert-knowledge sub-skills). VPGTrans dramatically outperforms all other models on value alignment, scoring 66.5% on privacy and 52.3% on security, whereas all other models remain below 20%. The hardest sub-skills across all eight models are text recognition (12.4% average) and object counting (17.9% average). GPT-3.5 Turbo as judge achieves over 85% agreement with human evaluators, and SFT on the curated data improves MiniGPT-4's counting accuracy by +29.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the eight VLMs evaluated, InstructBLIP (Dai et al., 2023) dominates perception with 34.7% average accuracy, outperforming BLIP2 (Li et al., 2023c) by 12 percentage points and LLaVA (Liu et al., 2023a) by 18.2 points, largely due to its 1.6M instruction-tuning samples. BLIP2 leads in reasoning (65.3% avg), edging out LLaMA-Adapter V2 (Gao et al., 2023) at 62.2%, while MiniGPT-4 (Zhu et al., 2023) and mPLUG-Owl (Ye et al., 2023) trail at 49.9% and 40.5% respectively. VPGTrans (Zhang et al., 2023) is the clear winner on value alignment (66.5% privacy, 52.3% security), surpassing the next-best model (LA-V2 at 17.9% privacy) by over 48 points, attributed to its Vicuna backbone and minimal projector fine-tuning that preserves LLM safety regularization. For planning, LLaVA ranks first qualitatively (rank 1) but InstructBLIP leads quantitatively (19.4% vs. 12.8%), while Otter (Li et al., 2023a) and mPLUG-Owl remain at the bottom. In the data-quality user study, Auto-Bench samples outperform TallyQA (Acharya et al., 2019) on counting (1.37 vs. 1.63 mean rank) and TextVQA (Singh et al., 2019) on text (1.45 vs. 1.55), and surpass the template-generated TDIUC (Kafle & Kanan, 2017) on perception (2.40 vs. 2.81) and reasoning (2.53 vs. 2.68), while remaining competitive with human-annotated VQAv2 (Goyal et al., 2017) and OK-VQA (Marino et al., 2019).

## Relation to Prior Work

Auto-Bench synthesizes two emerging ideas—LLM-based data generation (as in LLaVA's GPT-4-curated instruction data) and LLM-as-judge evaluation (as in MT-Bench/Chatbot Arena)—into a unified pipeline for VLM benchmarking, but departs from prior VLM benchmarks (MME, MMBench, LVLM-eHub, Seed-Bench) in two key ways: it replaces manual annotation with GPT-4 prompted on rich visual symbolizations (captions, bounding boxes, instance relationships, OCR) rather than captions alone, and it replaces rule-based scoring with GPT-3.5 semantic judgment. The value-alignment evaluation dimension (privacy, security refusal) has no direct precedent in prior VLM benchmarks, which focus exclusively on task accuracy. Conceptually, the paper repurposes the RLHF-aligned nature of LLMs as a proxy for human preference in both data curation and model assessment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Auto-Bench provides the largest automated VLM evaluation dataset (3.5M+ triplets across 16 sub-skills) and the first systematic measurement of value alignment in VLMs, revealing that 7 of 8 evaluated models fail to refuse privacy- or security-violating questions—a gap that prior benchmarks never surfaced. The 3.5M raw triplets also serve as SFT data, with a demonstrated +29.7% accuracy gain on counting for MiniGPT-4, suggesting the pipeline can simultaneously diagnose and remediate model weaknesses. Key limitations include reliance on COCO images (limiting domain generalizability), acknowledged data-leakage risk from GPT-4-generated training data shared with evaluated models, and dependence on a single LLM (GPT-3.5) as judge, whose agreement with humans (~85–90%) leaves room for systematic bias. Open questions include whether the pipeline scales to non-COCO domains (e.g., medical, satellite imagery) and whether LLM-as-judge generalizes beyond the GPT family.
