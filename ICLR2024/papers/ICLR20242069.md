## Problem

Dual-encoder (DE) models are widely used in retrieval but have been considered insufficient for extreme multi-label classification (XMC), where SOTA methods linearly scale parameters with the number of labels via per-class classifiers. This paper challenges that belief by showing the real bottleneck is the training loss: standard contrastive losses like InfoNCE penalize confidently predicted positives, which is particularly harmful on imbalanced XMC datasets where some labels are easy to predict while others are ambiguous.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242069/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a query text (e.g., a product title, Wikipedia article, or EU law document) and a set of label texts (e.g., product titles, Wikipedia categories, or EuroVoc descriptors) as input, both encoded by a shared 66M-parameter DistilBERT encoder into a common embedding space. It produces a ranked list of labels from a space of up to 1.3M labels, evaluated via top-k precision, recall, and propensity-scored precision metrics. Input sequences are truncated to 128 tokens for long-text datasets and 32 tokens for short-text datasets.

## Method

The core contribution is a DecoupledSoftmax loss that modifies InfoNCE by excluding other positive labels from the softmax denominator when computing the loss for a given positive, thereby decoupling positive-positive interactions and preventing the model from penalizing confidently predicted easy labels. This is extended to a SoftTop-k loss that applies a differentiable soft top-k operator (based on a scaled sigmoid with binary-search threshold) to the full score vector, directly optimizing top-k prediction accuracy. To handle the computational cost of using all negatives at million-label scale, the paper provides a memory-efficient distributed implementation combining gradient caching (processing labels in micro-batches of size η) with all-gather/all-reduce operations across GPUs. Trained with these losses, a pure DE model matches or outperforms SOTA XMC methods by up to 2% on P@1 while being 20× smaller in trainable parameters.

## Results

On the largest XMC benchmarks, DecoupledSoftmax DEXML achieves P@1 of 85.78 on LF-Wikipedia-500K and 58.40 on LF-AmazonTitles-1.3M, outperforming the previous SOTA (DEXA: 84.92 and 56.63 respectively) by up to 2% on P@1 and 2.5% on PSP@5. The model is 20× smaller in trainable parameters than SOTA methods that use per-class classification heads. On smaller datasets, DEXML is competitive but slightly trails ensemble-based methods like XR-Transformer×3 on EURLex-4K (P@1: 86.78 vs. 88.41) and DEXA/NGAME on LF-AmazonTitles-131K (P@1: 42.52 vs. 46.42/46.01).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEXML (DecoupledSoftmax) outperforms all SOTA XMC methods on the two largest datasets: it beats DEXA by 1.77% P@1 on LF-AmazonTitles-1.3M (58.40 vs. 56.63) and by 0.86% on LF-Wikipedia-500K (85.78 vs. 84.92), while also surpassing NGAME (56.75/84.01 P@1) and all ensemble-based methods (XR-Transformer×3, ECLARE×3, SiameseXML×3). Compared to the DE-only variants of NGAME and DEXA (which use the same encoder without a classification head), DEXML improves P@1 by up to 6.5% (58.40 vs. 51.92 on 1.3M). On smaller datasets, DEXML is competitive but does not always win: XR-Transformer×3 edges it out on EURLex-4K (88.41 vs. 86.78 P@1), and DEXA/NGAME lead on LF-AmazonTitles-131K P@1 (46.42/46.01 vs. 42.52), though the paper attributes this gap to NGAME's label-propensity-aware score fusion module. The key differentiator is parameter efficiency: DEXML uses only the 66M-parameter DistilBERT encoder shared between query and document, whereas SOTA methods like NGAME and DEXA add per-label classification vectors that scale linearly with the number of labels, making DEXML 20× smaller on LF-AmazonTitles-1.3M.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repositions dual-encoder (DE) models—originally developed for open-domain QA retrieval (DPR, ANCE, RocketQA)—as a viable standalone solution for extreme multi-label classification (XMC), a task where SOTA methods (NGAME, DEXA) augment DE encoders with per-class classification heads or auxiliary parameters that scale linearly with the number of labels. The key departure is identifying the loss function, not the architecture, as the bottleneck: the authors show that standard InfoNCE/SoftmaxCE penalizes confidently predicted positives in multi-label settings, and propose DecoupledSoftmax (a simple modification excluding other positives from the softmax denominator) and a differentiable SoftTop-k loss. The memory-efficient distributed training builds on gradient caching (Gao et al., 2021) and hard-negative mining (ANCE, Xiong et al., 2021), while the SoftTop-k operator draws on a differentiable top-k formulation by Ahle (2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 66M-parameter DistilBERT-based DE model matches or exceeds SOTA XMC methods (which use 20× more trainable parameters) on the largest public benchmarks, and avoids the need for per-class heads that must be retrained when new labels appear—making the approach more suitable for dynamic, real-world retrieval systems. The unification of XMC and dense retrieval under a single parameter-efficient framework simplifies the methodological landscape. Key open questions remain: whether the approach retains its advantage at billion-scale label spaces, whether performance can be maintained without the computationally expensive all-negatives loss, and whether hierarchical DE architectures can incorporate built-in fast search. The evaluation is limited to O(million) label spaces, leaving web-scale applicability unverified.
