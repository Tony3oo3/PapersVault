## Problem

Masked Autoencoding (MAE) reconstructs raw pixel values, treating images as continuous signals, yet visual understanding relies on discrete, object-centric groupings analogous to words in language. This pixel-level focus limits MAE's instance-awareness and its ability to improve dense prediction tasks like detection and segmentation relative to language models' gains from word-level pre-training. The paper asks whether learning to reconstruct regions—discrete pixel groupings—can serve as a more semantically grounded pre-text task.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image patchified at 16×16 and k binary region maps (default k=8, same spatial resolution as the image) generated by the Felzenszwalb-Huttenlocher clustering algorithm or SAM. Both the image and region maps are masked at a 75% ratio using a shared random mask. The transferable output is the pre-trained ViT pixel encoder; during training the model also produces reconstructed pixel patches (via L2 regression) and reconstructed binary region maps (via cross-entropy), with the region decoder operating on compressed 1D region queries rather than full spatial maps.

## Method

R-MAE introduces Masked Region Autoencoding (RAE) as a parallel pre-text task to MAE's pixel reconstruction. The key architectural challenge is the one-to-many mapping between pixels and regions plus the need for permutation equivariance across randomly ordered regions. The paper's 'length variant' resolves this by encoding each region map into a single 1D embedding via average pooling, concatenating k embeddings as DETR-style region queries along the sequence-length axis, and decoding them through a three-layer ViT decoder (self-attention, cross-attention, feed-forward) that attends to pixel features from a lightweight neck. The final decoder layer replaces standard cross-attention with a spatial expansion that adds each region query to all context value vectors, followed by a 3-layer MLP head predicting the binary region map. In the full R-MAE framework, the pixel encoder feeds the region branch (asymmetric pixel→region design), masks are shared between branches to prevent information leakage, and both reconstruction losses are optimized jointly with equal weight, adding only ~1% FLOPs over vanilla MAE.

## Results

Pre-trained on ImageNet for 1600 epochs, R-MAE achieves 52.3 AP^b and 46.4 AP^m on COCO object detection and instance segmentation, and 47.5 mIoU on ADE20K semantic segmentation, using only 9.8b FLOPs (1× relative compute). This outperforms the MAE baseline (50.1 AP^b, 44.6 AP^m, 45.9 mIoU) by +2.2/+1.8/+1.6 points respectively, while adding only ~1% FLOPs. R-MAE is the most efficient method among all compared MAE variants, achieving the best detection and segmentation scores at 1× compute, whereas competitors like Long-Seq MAE and SemMAE require 4.3× FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R-MAE outperforms the MAE baseline [31] on all three metrics (52.3 vs 50.1 AP^b, 46.4 vs 44.6 AP^m, 47.5 vs 45.9 mIoU) with negligible extra compute. Against the strongest MAE variant, Long-Seq MAE [32], R-MAE wins on both detection (52.3 vs 52.1 AP^b) and segmentation (46.4 vs 46.2 AP^m) while using 4.3× fewer FLOPs. R-MAE also beats MixedAE [11] on detection and instance segmentation (52.3 vs 51.5 AP^b, 46.4 vs 44.5 AP^m) but trails on ADE20K semantic segmentation (47.5 vs 49.8 mIoU), where MixedAE's multi-task design gives it an edge. Compared to non-MAE pre-training methods, R-MAE surpasses BEiT [6] by +2.5 AP^b and +2.0 AP^m, MoCo v3 [16] by +4.4 AP^b and +3.7 AP^m, and supervised pre-training by +4.4 AP^b and +3.5 AP^m. On LVIS long-tail detection, R-MAE shows a larger relative gain on rare classes (26.7 vs 25.4 AP_rare), suggesting its region-aware priors are less tied to category frequency.

## Relation to Prior Work

R-MAE extends Masked Autoencoding (MAE) by adding a parallel region-reconstruction branch, drawing the conceptual analogy that regions serve as a visual analogue of words in NLP. Architecturally, the 'length variant' directly adapts DETR's object-query mechanism—pooling each region into a single embedding and decoding via cross-attention—to handle the one-to-many, permutation-equivariant mapping between images and regions. This departs from prior region-based pre-training (contrastive detection, RoI-based Siamese learning) by adopting a reconstructive autoencoding paradigm, and from multi-modal MAE variants (e.g., MultiMAE) by treating regions as a lightweight, self-computable signal rather than an external modality requiring a separate encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

R-MAE demonstrates that incorporating discrete, region-level structure into self-supervised pre-training consistently improves dense prediction (detection, segmentation) with negligible compute overhead (~1% FLOPs), and unlocks a natural interactive-segmentation capability. However, the paper acknowledges that regions may lack the semantic richness of words, and the strongest results depend on SAM-generated regions—which are themselves initialized from MAE and computationally expensive—leaving open the question of what property of high-quality regions actually drives the gain. The notable drop in linear-probing accuracy (60.6 vs. 68.0) while fine-tuning performance matches MAE suggests the learned representations trade global class-discriminative power for local, instance-level structure, a trade-off that may limit applicability to classification-heavy pipelines.
