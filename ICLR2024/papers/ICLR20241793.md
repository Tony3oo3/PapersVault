## Problem

Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) variants flatten the loss landscape over perturbed parameter regions to improve domain generalization, but their theoretical upper bound on unknown-domain risk contains an unoptimizable f-divergence term between source and target distributions. This paper argues that parameter-space perturbation alone is insufficient: without also exploring the data space, the model cannot guarantee flat minima that transfer to unobserved domains, particularly when source domain information is limited.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images from one or more source domains (e.g., 32×32 CIFAR-10, or ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50 features for PACS/OfficeHome/DomainNet) paired with class labels. During training, each source image is additionally perturbed along a direction that maximizes the sum of its loss and a ρ′-weighted gradient norm, producing a synthetic 'worst-case' dataset that emulates an unknown domain. The model outputs standard multi-class predictions; no target-domain data is accessed at test time.

## Method

UDIM augments SAM-based optimization with a data-space inconsistency term that minimizes the loss-landscape discrepancy between the source domain and a perturbed surrogate of unknown domains within the flat region N_{s,θ}^{γ,ρ}. Concretely, each source input x is perturbed as x̃ = x + ρ_x · ∇_x(ℓ(x,θ) + ρ′‖∇_θ ℓ(x,θ)‖₂)/‖∇_x(·)‖₂, and the resulting perturbed dataset D̃_s is used to compute a gradient-norm term ρ′‖∇_θ L_{D̃_s}‖₂ plus a gradient-variance matching term ‖Var(G_{D̃_s}) − Var(G_{D_s})‖₂ (restricted to classifier parameters and computed via BackPACK). The full objective is min_θ [max_{‖ε‖≤ρ} L_{D_s}(θ+ε) + ρ′‖∇_θ L_{D̃_s}(θ)‖₂ + ‖Var(G_{D̃_s})−Var(G_{D_s})‖₂ + λ₂‖θ‖₂], which Theorem 3.2 proves is an upper bound on the population risk over all domains with no unoptimizable terms. A SAM warm-up phase precedes full UDIM optimization to ensure the flat region N_{s,θ}^{γ,ρ} is sufficiently wide before the inconsistency term is activated.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-C, UDIM w/ GAM achieves 75.3% average accuracy across 5 corruption severity levels, outperforming GAM (72.8%) by 2.5 points and SAM (71.8%) by 3.5 points. On PACS in the Single Source Domain Generalization (SDG) setting, UDIM w/ SAM reaches 74.7% average accuracy, a ~10-point improvement over SAM alone (64.8%), and UDIM w/ GAM reaches 74.9% vs. GAM's 65.4%. In the Leave-One-Out (LOOODG) setting on PACS, UDIM w/ SAM achieves 88.7% average, surpassing the best non-UDIM baseline (LTD at 86.4%) by 2.3 points. The paper reports up to 9.9% accuracy gains in the most restrictive domain-information settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UDIM consistently outperforms all SAM-based baselines (SAM, SAGM, GAM) across every dataset and evaluation scenario, with the largest gains in the SDG setting where domain information is most restricted (e.g., +9.9% on PACS SDG Photo: 67.4% vs. SAM's 46.7%). Against non-sharpness baselines, UDIM w/ SAM surpasses the best LOOODG methods (ITTA 85.8%, LTD 86.4%) by 2.3–2.9 points on PACS LOOODG average, and dominates all SDG baselines by 5.9–10.0 points. On CIFAR-10-C, UDIM w/ GAM (75.3% avg) outperforms the best augmentation-based method (OrgMixup, 70.3%) by 5.0 points and the best robust-optimization method (GroupDRO, 68.7%) by 6.6 points. UDIM does not lose to any baseline in any reported setting; the closest competitor is ITTA on PACS LOOODG (85.8% vs. UDIM w/ SAM's 88.7%), a 2.9-point gap. The paper notes that improvements are statistically significant and grow larger as the number of available source domains decreases, confirming UDIM's advantage under information-limited conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SAM-based optimization line (Foret et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2023b) by adding a data-space perturbation component that emulates unknown domains, addressing a gap the authors identify in Theorem 3.1: parameter-space flatness alone leaves an unoptimizable f-divergence term in the generalization bound. The inconsistency objective (Eq. 3) is adapted from Parascandolo et al. (2020), who used it as a diagnostic metric rather than an optimization target, and the gradient-variance matching term (Eq. 10) is borrowed from Fishr (Rame et al., 2022) to avoid expensive Hessian computation. The key conceptual departure is treating data perturbation as a first-class optimization variable alongside parameter perturbation, rather than as a fixed augmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean theoretical result (Theorem 3.2) showing that SAM + UDIM yields a fully optimizable upper bound on population risk is a meaningful step toward closing the gap between SAM's PAC-Bayes guarantees and actual DG performance. The method is practically attractive because it is orthogonal to existing SAM variants (SAM, SAGM, GAM) and the largest gains appear in single-source DG, the most restrictive and practically relevant setting. Limitations include the reliance on BackPACK for per-sample gradients (restricting applicability to architectures where this is feasible), the approximation of worst-case domain via a single perturbation direction, and the fact that the data perturbation radius ρ_x is tuned per dataset. Open questions include whether the inconsistency-aware perturbation direction generalizes beyond the first-order Taylor approximation and whether the approach extends to non-i.i.d. or sequential domain settings.
