## Problem

Applying deep reinforcement learning to safety-critical autonomous systems remains challenging because DNN-parameterized policies are hard to verify and can violate physics laws, while existing residual frameworks focus on stability rather than safety. No systematic method exists for constructing CLF-like rewards that yield mathematically provable safety guarantees, nor for building end-to-end critic and actor networks that strictly comply with partially available physics knowledge about the action-value function and action policy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a continuous system state vector s(k) ∈ R^n, a known linear model (A, B) obtained from simplification or system identification, and h safety constraints (D, v, v̄, v̄) defining the safety set X. It outputs a residual control action a(k) = a_drl(k) + F·s(k), where the model-based component F·s(k) is computed offline via LMI solving (maximizing the safety envelope volume) and the data-driven component is learned online. The key output guarantee is that if the initial state lies within the safety envelope Ω = {s | sᵀPs ≤ 1}, the state remains in Ω ⊆ X for all future time steps, with the condition r(s(k), s(k+1)) ≥ α−1 verifiable in the final training episode.

## Method

Phy-DRL embeds three invariant designs into an actor-critic DRL framework. First, a residual action policy a(k) = a_drl(k) + F·s(k) where F = R·Q⁻¹ is solved from LMIs that simultaneously ensure the safety envelope is a subset of the safety set and that Ā = A + BF is contractive (ĀᵀPĀ < αP). Second, a safety-embedded reward R = s(k)ᵀĀᵀPs(k) − s(k+1)ᵀPs(k+1) + w(s(k), a_drl(k)) converts multi-dimensional safety constraints into a scalar via the ellipsoidal safety envelope, enabling a Lyapunov-based proof that V(s(k)) ≤ 1 for all k. Third, physics-knowledge-enhanced DNNs use Taylor-series input augmentation (Algorithm 1) to generate non-redundant monomial representations, combined with link editing (removing connections whose monomials are absent from the model mismatch) and activation editing (freezing activations on knowledge-only rows) to guarantee that the end-to-end critic and actor networks strictly comply with available physics knowledge about Q and π. The linear model (A,B) serves triple duty: computing F, constructing the reward via P, and providing the invariant knowledge sets K_Q and K_π for NN editing.

## Results

On the cart-pole system (75,000 training steps), Phy-DRL successfully renders the safety envelope invariant (validating Theorem 5.3), while the purely data-driven DRL with the same CLF reward and the sole model-based policy both achieve substantially smaller safe areas (Figure 3). On the quadruped robot, Phy-DRL requires only 10⁶ training steps versus 10⁷ for the purely data-driven DRL (a 10× reduction), and maintains safety in all four tested environments (snow/wet roads at various velocities), whereas the DRL policy violates safety constraints in every environment. Phy-DRL also features fewer learning parameters and faster, more stable training convergence toward the safety guarantee.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the purely data-driven DRL baseline (trained with the CLF reward from Westenbroek et al. 2022), Phy-DRL wins decisively on both safety and efficiency: on the quadruped robot, the DRL policy violates safety constraints in all four environments despite 10× more training steps (10⁷ vs. 10⁶), while Phy-DRL maintains safety in all four. On the cart-pole system, the DRL baseline achieves a much smaller safe area than Phy-DRL at the same 75,000-step budget. Against the PD controller from Da et al. (2021), Phy-DRL dominates: the PD policy can only constrain the quadruped to the safety envelope in the most favorable environment (d: −0.4 m/s wet road), whereas Phy-DRL achieves safety-set-level constraint in all environments and envelope-level constraint in the two more reasonable-velocity environments. The sole model-based (linear) policy, while safe in environment (d), fails in the other three, confirming that the residual DRL component is essential for handling model mismatch. Overall, Phy-DRL is the only method that simultaneously guarantees safety across all tested conditions while requiring the fewest training steps and learning parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

Phy-DRL sits at the intersection of three research threads: residual action policies (Johannink et al. 2019, Cheng et al. 2019) that combine model-based and data-driven control, CLF-based reward design for DRL (Westenbroek et al. 2022) that previously yielded only stability guarantees, and physics-informed neural networks (Karniadakis et al. 2021) that embed PDE constraints into training losses. The paper departs from prior residual frameworks by targeting safety (not just stability) and from physics-informed NNs by enforcing compliance through architectural editing (link and activation masking) rather than loss-function penalties, since the action-value function lacks a compact governing PDE. The actor-critic backbone follows standard DRL (Lillicrap et al. 2016, Haarnoja et al. 2018), with the novelty concentrated in the invariant-embedding designs layered on top.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mathematically provable safety guarantee via the safety envelope and LMI-computed matrices F and P offers a principled path to certifying DRL policies for safety-critical deployment, addressing a core limitation of purely data-driven approaches. However, the framework depends on a linear model (A, B), and the authors acknowledge that large model mismatch can invalidate the safety envelope, leaving open how to handle faulty or highly nonlinear models. The practical verification of the safety condition (requiring full coverage within Ω) remains unresolved, and the NN editing mechanism, while elegant, has only been validated on two relatively low-dimensional systems (cart-pole and quadruped), leaving scalability to high-dimensional or partially observable settings as an open question.
