## Problem

Fine-tuning pretrained LLMs on a single task causes the model to rapidly lose its general in-context learning abilities on unseen tasks, a phenomenon the paper traces to 'format specialization' — the model overfits to the output format of the fine-tuning task (e.g., only producing 'True'/'False' labels) within the first few hundred steps, before it has even learned the task's semantic content. No existing fine-tuning technique prevents this format lock-in while still achieving strong supervised performance on the target task.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text inputs formatted with task-specific templates (e.g., '[premise] question: [hypothesis] Is it true or false?') and optionally prepends a learned soft prompt (100 trainable embeddings) and/or a 1-shot natural-language example. At inference, the soft prompt is retained only when the evaluation task shares the same output format as the fine-tuning task; otherwise it is removed and the model receives the raw input. Outputs are free-form text decoded via beam search (width 4), evaluated with task-appropriate metrics (accuracy, exact match, BLEU, ROUGE-2).

## Method

ProMoT (PROmpt Tuning with MOdel Tuning) is a two-stage fine-tuning framework that decouples format learning from semantic learning. In Stage 1, the pretrained model is frozen and a small soft prompt (100 tokens, learning rate 0.2) is trained for 5000 steps to absorb the task-specific output format. In Stage 2, the soft prompt is frozen and prepended to every input while the full model is fine-tuned for 1000 steps, forcing the model's own parameters to learn transferable semantic skills rather than format. The key empirical insight, validated via gradient-alignment analysis, is that format learning dominates the gradient signal at the very start of standard fine-tuning (cosine similarity ≈ 1 at step 0, dropping to ~0.2 by step 400), so offloading it to a low-capacity prompt first prevents the model from encoding format into its weights. At inference, the removable soft prompt allows the same fine-tuned model to handle both same-format and different-format tasks without retraining.

## Results

On RTE fine-tuning, ProMoT achieves 92.78% accuracy (vs. 92.06% for standard fine-tuning) while improving the normalized average in-context learning score from 15.43 (standard FT) to 20.10 (+2.58 over pretrained's 17.52). On WMT14 En-Fr translation, ProMoT reaches 41.30 BLEU (vs. 41.80 standard FT) and lifts the normalized average from 9.15 (standard FT, −8.37 vs. pretrained) to 18.87 (+1.35 over pretrained). Adding a 1-shot in-context prompt during training further boosts the normalized average to 22.26 (+4.74) on RTE and 19.91 (+2.39) on WMT14 En-Fr. ProMoT also outperforms standard fine-tuning on 3 of 4 supervised tasks (RTE, SNLI, OpenbookQA) and matches it on WMT14 En-Fr within 0.5 BLEU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProMoT is compared against three baselines: the pretrained model, standard full fine-tuning, and prompt tuning (Lester et al., 2021). Against standard fine-tuning, ProMoT matches or exceeds supervised accuracy (92.78 vs. 92.06 on RTE; 41.30 vs. 41.80 BLEU on WMT14 En-Fr) while dramatically reducing catastrophic forgetting: the normalized average in-context score drops from 17.52 to 15.43 under standard FT on RTE but rises to 20.10 under ProMoT; on WMT14 En-Fr the gap is even starker (9.15 for standard FT vs. 18.87 for ProMoT). Against prompt tuning, ProMoT wins on both supervised performance (92.78 vs. 91.34 on RTE; 41.30 vs. 39.28 on WMT14 En-Fr) and in-context generalization, since prompt tuning leaves the frozen model's in-context abilities unchanged. Against the pretrained model itself, ProMoT improves the normalized average on both fine-tuning settings (RTE: +2.58; WMT14 En-Fr: +1.35), with particularly large gains on semantically related tasks such as summarization after NLI fine-tuning and cross-lingual translation after En-Fr fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProMoT builds directly on prompt tuning (Lester et al., 2021) as its Stage 1 mechanism, repurposing soft prompts not as a standalone parameter-efficient method but as a format-absorbing prelude to full fine-tuning. It positions itself as orthogonal to multi-task fine-tuning approaches (Wei et al., 2021; Chung et al., 2022) that improve generalization through data diversity, and distinct from parameter-efficient methods (LoRA, adapters) that preserve in-context learning by leaving the model largely untouched but forgo the opportunity to improve it. The paper also departs from prior catastrophic forgetting studies (Ramasesh et al., 2022; Zhai et al., 2023) by focusing on format-specific specialization across vastly different task types (classification vs. generation) rather than same-format task sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of format specialization as the dominant early fine-tuning signal (gradient alignment ≈ 1 at step 0) provides a concrete mechanistic explanation for why in-context learning degrades so rapidly, and the two-stage fix is simple enough to be widely adopted as a drop-in modification to existing fine-tuning pipelines. The finding that ProMoT can *improve* in-context performance on semantically related but format-different tasks (NLI → summarization, En-Fr → En-De/En-Ro) suggests fine-tuning can be a net positive for generalization rather than a trade-off. Open questions include whether the soft prompt reliably absorbs format at larger scales (>15B, where the paper's experiments stop), whether there is a theoretical bound on format absorption capacity, and how the method behaves with very long or highly structured formats.
