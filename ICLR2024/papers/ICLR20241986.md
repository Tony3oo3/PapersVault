## Problem

Existing post-training quantization (PTQ) methods for LLMs restrict the equivalent transformation between pre- and post-quantization weights to scaling (and sometimes translation), which limits the optimization space and produces large quantization errors, particularly in low-bit or small-model settings. No prior method had explored full affine transformations—combining scaling, rotation, and translation—as the equivalent transformation, leaving a significant gap in how well the weight distribution can be aligned with the quantization grid.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained LLM (OPT, LLaMA-1, LLaMA-2 at 125M–30B scale) and a small calibration set of 128 segments of 2048 tokens sampled from the WikiText2 training split. It outputs low-bit quantized weights (W2A16, W3A16, W4A16, W4A4, with optional group sizes of 64 or 128) where the learned affine transformation matrix is merged into the linear-layer weight and bias parameters, and for weight-activation quantization the diagonal elements after LayerNorm are absorbed into the LayerNorm scale and bias, yielding zero additional inference overhead.

## Method

AffineQuant left-multiplies a learnable affine transformation matrix A to the weight matrix W and right-multiplies A⁻¹ to the activation X, optimizing A (and a translation shift δ) to minimize the Frobenius-norm MSE between pre- and post-quantization transformer-block outputs. The key technical challenge is maintaining invertibility of A during gradient-based optimization; the paper invokes the Levy-Desplanques theorem (strictly diagonally dominant matrices are invertible) and introduces a Gradual Mask (GM) that initializes A as diagonal and progressively unfreezes off-diagonal elements within a band of width (e/t)×hidden_size, scaled by a stability factor α. In forward propagation the GM element-wise scales A to suppress off-diagonal magnitudes, and in backward propagation it modulates the effective learning rate of those elements, guaranteeing strict diagonal dominance at every epoch. The approach subsumes AWQ, SmoothQuant, OmniQuant, and RPTQ as special cases (diagonal-only, permutation, or α→0 limits), and the affine matrix is applied per attention head and per linear layer, excluding the MLP intermediate layer where the nonlinearity breaks equivalence.

## Results

On W4A4 weight-activation quantization of LLaMA2-7B, AffineQuant achieves a C4 perplexity of 15.76, reducing it by 2.26 compared to OmniQuant (18.02) and 61.51 compared to SmoothQuant (77.27). On zero-shot tasks with LLaMA-30B at 4/4-bit, AffineQuant reaches 58.61% average accuracy, surpassing OmniQuant (56.63%) by 1.98 percentage points. For weight-only quantization on OPT models, AffineQuant consistently achieves the lowest PPL across all bit-widths (W2–W4) and model sizes (125M–30B), with the largest gains in low-bit settings (e.g., 30.56 vs 35.66 PPL on OPT-125M W3A16).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AffineQuant consistently outperforms OmniQuant across all evaluated configurations, with the largest margins in low-bit W4A4 settings (e.g., 2.26 PPL reduction on LLaMA2-7B C4, 1.98% accuracy gain on LLaMA-30B zero-shot). Against SmoothQuant, the improvement is dramatic in W4A4 quantization (e.g., 15.76 vs 77.27 PPL on LLaMA2-7B C4), as SmoothQuant's simple scaling is insufficient for such aggressive compression. Compared to GPTQ and AWQ on weight-only quantization (Table 1), AffineQuant achieves lower PPL in nearly all configurations, with AWQ showing instability at extreme settings (e.g., 263.10 PPL on OPT-2.7B W3A16). Against FlexRound on W4A16 zero-shot tasks (Table 7), AffineQuant is competitive, slightly outperforming on LLaMA-13B (65.99% vs 65.73% avg) but marginally behind on LLaMA-7B (62.89% vs 62.93%), indicating the affine transformation's advantage is most pronounced under tighter bit budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

AffineQuant unifies the lineage of equivalent-transformation PTQ methods—SmoothQuant's per-channel scaling, Outlier Suppression+'s translation, AWQ's activation-aware scaling, OmniQuant's learnable scale-and-shift, and RPTQ's column reordering—into a single full affine transformation matrix applied to weights. The key conceptual departure is elevating the optimization from 1D per-channel parameters to a full matrix, thereby subsuming all prior transformations as special cases (diagonal-only, permutation, or diagonal-plus-shift). The paper further introduces a gradual mask mechanism grounded in the Levy-Desplanques theorem to guarantee strict diagonal dominance and hence invertibility of the transformation matrix throughout gradient-based optimization, a stability concern absent from prior equivalent-transformation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By expanding the optimization space from per-channel scalings to full affine matrices, AffineQuant consistently reduces quantization error, with the largest gains in the most challenging regimes (2-bit, small models, W4A4), making edge deployment of LLMs more practical. The theoretical guarantee via the Levy-Desplanques theorem and the gradual mask provide a principled solution to the matrix-invertibility problem that prior methods sidestepped by restricting to diagonal or permutation matrices. Limitations include substantial optimization time (up to ~17 hours for OPT-6.7B in double precision), the exclusion of the MLP's second linear layer due to the nonlinearity of the activation function, and the need for careful tuning of the stability factor α per model size and bit-width. Open questions include whether more effective optimization of high-dimensional affine matrices (e.g., factorized or low-rank parameterizations) can reduce calibration cost, and whether the approach extends to architectures with more complex non-linearities.
