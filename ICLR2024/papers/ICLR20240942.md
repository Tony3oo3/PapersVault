## Problem

Marker-based label projection methods (e.g., EasyProject) inject special markers into source sentences before machine translation, which degrades translation quality and leads to errors such as untranslated or split entity spans. This paper asks whether label projection can be reformulated as a constrained decoding problem that preserves the quality of a clean (marker-free) translation while still correctly inserting the right number of markers at the right positions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240942/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an annotated source sentence with m labeled spans and a pre-computed marker-free translation template y^tmpl (generated by NLLB-3.3B). It produces the same template text with exactly m marker pairs inserted at valid positions, scored by a smaller NLLB-600M model conditioned on the marker-annotated source. The search space is restricted to O(n²) per span (via 1-projection approximation) rather than O(n^{2m}), and the output must satisfy two hard constraints: the plain text matches y^tmpl exactly and the bracket pairs are well-formed.

## Method

CODEC separates translation from marker insertion: it first translates the source sentence without markers to obtain a high-quality template, then runs a constrained decoding pass that inserts markers into that fixed template. The m-span projection is decomposed into m independent 1-projection sub-problems, each with an O(n²) search space. A heuristic prunes unlikely opening-marker positions by comparing log-probabilities of template tokens conditioned on the source with and without markers (Eq. 5). A branch-and-bound depth-first search finds top-k hypotheses using a novel length-adaptive heuristic lower bound (Eq. 7–8) that prunes partial hypotheses more aggressively than the exact bound. Finally, a re-ranking step selects the best hypothesis using both the generative log-probability and a span-level score (log-probability of translating the source span given the projected target span).

## Results

On the MasakhaNER2.0 cross-lingual NER benchmark (18 African languages), CODEC achieves an average F1 of 70.4 in the translate-test setting and 67.1 in translate-train, outperforming the English fine-tuning baseline (FTEn, 54.5) by +16.0 and +12.7 F1 respectively. In the translate-train setting, CODEC (67.1) surpasses EasyProject (64.9) and Awes-align (63.6), with the largest per-language gain being +37.2 F1 on chiShona. On the ACE-2005 Event Argument Extraction task, CODEC achieves 53.8 average F1, beating EasyProject (52.6), Awes-align (52.8), and FTEn (49.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CODEC outperforms EasyProject (the prior state-of-the-art marker-based method) by +2.2 F1 on average in translate-train (67.1 vs. 64.9) and is the only marker-based method applicable to translate-test, where it achieves 70.4 F1. Against Awes-align (alignment-based), CODEC wins by +3.5 F1 in translate-train (67.1 vs. 63.6) and by +4.6 F1 in translate-test (70.4 vs. 65.8), with CODEC beating Awes-align in 13 of 18 languages in the translate-test setting. GPT-4 (evaluated on 200 examples per language) scores 60.4 F1 on average, below both CODEC settings. The two exceptions where CODEC underperforms FTEn are Chichewa (−2.7) and Kiswahili (−4.6), likely because entities in these languages are often identical to their English forms, making English-only fine-tuning sufficient. On the ACE-2005 EAE task, CODEC (53.8) edges out EasyProject (52.6) and Awes-align (52.8), with the clearest advantage in Arabic EAE (33.6 vs. 26.9 for EasyProject).

## Relation to Prior Work

CODEC builds directly on EasyProject (Chen et al., 2023a), a marker-based label projection method that injects special markers into source sentences before machine translation, and on branch-and-bound constrained decoding for NMT (Stahlberg & Byrne, 2019). The key departure is a two-phase decomposition: first translate the clean source sentence (preserving quality), then use a customized constrained decoding algorithm to insert markers into the already-translated template, enforcing both template fidelity and correct marker count as hard constraints. This positions CODEC as combining the accuracy of marker-based projection with the translation-quality preservation and translate-test applicability of alignment-based methods like Awes-align, while requiring only a translation model rather than a separate word aligner.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: CODEC is the first label projection method that works in both translate-train and translate-test settings, and experiments show translate-test yields substantial additional gains (e.g., +16 F1 average over translate-train for NER). This reframes how practitioners approach cross-lingual transfer for span-level tasks, suggesting that inference-time translation plus projection can outperform training-time data augmentation alone. Open questions include the re-ranking failure mode (over 60% of Chinese errors stem from the re-ranking step failing to select the correct hypothesis from top-k), the sensitivity to translation style (e.g., 'Chinese journalists' vs. 'journalists from China' changing entity type), and whether the approach extends to nested or overlapping spans, which the current formulation explicitly excludes.
