## Problem

Existing instruction-following dataset curation relies on two paradigms—Scaling-Inputs (amplifying input-output pairs per task) and Scaling Input-Free Tasks (enlarging tasks without separate inputs)—each with distinct failure modes: the former makes models overly sensitive to inputs and prone to instruction non-compliance, while the latter generalizes poorly to tasks requiring supplementary inputs. No prior approach diversifies the task instructions for a fixed input, which is the key mechanism for teaching models to adapt outputs based on specific instructions rather than input content.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes raw text inputs sampled from SuperNI (953 inputs) and the Dolma pretraining corpus (510 inputs), along with 757 human-written task instructions from SuperNI's training set. It produces 68,014 (instruction, input, output) triples where each of the 1,463 inputs is paired with an average of 46.48 distinct instructions, yielding outputs annotated by ChatGPT/GPT-4 and filtered for validity. The dataset is structured so that the same input appears under many different instructions, inverting the conventional one-instruction-many-inputs layout.

## Method

MUFFIN introduces the Scaling Tasks per Input paradigm through three complementary mechanisms. First, Instruction Brainstorm uses a two-step facet-based procedure: ChatGPT identifies diverse textual facets (attributes) of each input, then each facet serves as a directional hint to brainstorm multiple task instructions, with two prompt variants (follow-attribute and shift-attribute) producing complementary task categories. Second, Instruction Rematching reuses SuperNI's human-written instructions by employing GPT-4 for binary relevance classification against each input, with a Flan-T5 small model pre-filtering to reduce API costs to ~35%. Third, Classification Expansion addresses the LLM bias toward generation tasks by prompting ChatGPT to generate plausible wrong outputs, then reformulating the original generation task as a multiple-choice classification with randomly shuffled option letters. Post-processing removes overlapping instructions (ROUGE-L > 0.7) and non-answerable instances, and the final dataset randomly selects between original and expanded classification versions to maintain task balance.

## Results

On T5-3B, MUFFIN achieves an average score of 37.13 across four zero-shot benchmarks (SuperNI-Test, MMLU, T0-Eval, BBH), outperforming the strongest LLM-generated baselines: Self-Instruct (32.71), Unnatural Instruct (32.02), and Dynosaur (30.61). For T5-11B, MUFFIN reaches 44.58 average, surpassing Self-Instruct (36.55), Unnatural Instruct (36.13), and Dynosaur (35.50), with a 8.03-point improvement over the strongest baseline versus 4.42 for T5-3B. MUFFIN also approaches the human-annotated SuperNI upper bound (40.01 for T5-3B, 48.59 for T5-11B), particularly on T0-Eval where it achieves 44.45 EM (T5-3B) and 57.74 EM (T5-11B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MUFFIN consistently outperforms all LLM-generated baselines on 3 of 4 benchmarks (SuperNI-Test, MMLU, T0-Eval) for both T5-3B and T5-11B, with the largest margins on T0-Eval (e.g., 44.45 EM vs. 38.57 for Self-Instruct and 34.49 for Unnatural Instruct on T5-3B). Against the Scaling-Inputs baselines (Unnatural Instruct, Dynosaur), MUFFIN leads on MMLU (29.75 vs. 18.03 and 20.38 EM) and T0-Eval (44.45 vs. 34.49 and 38.81 EM), while being competitive on SuperNI-Test. Against Scaling Input-Free Tasks baselines (Self-Instruct, Alpaca, Alpaca-GPT4, WizardLM, Dolly, LongForm), MUFFIN dominates across nearly all metrics, with particularly large gaps on MMLU (29.75 vs. 23.55 for Self-Instruct, 8.02 for Alpaca) and T0-Eval. MUFFIN approaches but does not fully match the human-annotated SuperNI upper bound (37.13 vs. 40.01 average for T5-3B; 44.58 vs. 48.59 for T5-11B), though it surpasses SuperNI on T0-Eval EM for T5-11B (57.74 vs. 54.23). The paper also shows that mixing MUFFIN with SuperNI actually degrades performance, suggesting the Scaling Tasks per Input paradigm is more critical than data quality alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against two established paradigms for instruction-following dataset curation: Scaling-Inputs (SuperNI, Unnatural Instruct, Dynosaur) and Scaling Input-Free Tasks (Self-Instruct, Alpaca, WizardLM). It introduces a third paradigm, Scaling Tasks per Input, which diversifies task instructions for each fixed input rather than amplifying inputs per task or expanding input-free tasks. The instruction brainstorming mechanism departs from Self-Instruct and Unnatural Instruct's demonstration-based generation by using input-facet-oriented prompting, while the instruction rematching step repurposes SuperNI's human-written instruction pool with LLM-based relevance filtering. The classification expansion technique addresses a known imbalance in LLM-synthesized data (over-representation of generation tasks) by converting generation instances into multiple-choice classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's most notable finding is that dataset paradigm matters more than data quality: mixing MUFFIN with the higher-quality human-annotated SuperNI actually degrades performance, suggesting that inconsistent training paradigms harm instruction-following more than noise does. This challenges the common assumption that adding more high-quality data is always beneficial. However, the practical impact is somewhat limited: the performance gains over the strongest baselines (Self-Instruct, Unnatural Instruct) are moderate (roughly 4–8 points on average), and the evaluation is restricted to T5 and Llama2 at 3B–13B scale. Open questions include whether the Scaling Tasks per Input paradigm generalizes to much larger models, whether the facet-based generation approach scales to multimodal inputs, and whether the paradigm advantage persists when data budgets are very large.
