## Problem

Contrastive learning objectives are almost universally designed around exactly two views per sample (pairwise tasks), and the popular Multi-Crop extension that averages over all view pairs only reduces estimator variance without improving the mutual information bound. The paper asks whether contrastive objectives can be designed to genuinely exploit M views simultaneously, accessing more total information about the underlying generative factor, and whether this changes the optimal compute allocation between batch size and view multiplicity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240315/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes M augmented views of each of K unique images per batch (total V = K×M views), where views are generated by a SimCLR-style augmentation policy (random crops, color jitter, Gaussian blur). The encoder is a ResNet-50 that maps each view to a fixed-dimensional embedding in space C. A key design choice is the Fixed Batch setting, where total views V is held constant while M increases and K decreases proportionally (e.g., M=8, K=1024 gives the same 8192 total views as M=2, K=4096), enabling a direct compute comparison against standard SimCLR.

## Method

The paper derives two families of poly-view contrastive losses that operate on all M views jointly rather than decomposing into pairwise terms. First, it generalizes the NWJ mutual information estimator to the One-vs-Rest MI (between one view and the set of all other M−1 views), yielding Arithmetic PVC and Geometric PVC losses whose MI gap is provably monotonically non-increasing in M (Theorem 2.3). Second, it invokes the Fisher-Darmois-Koopman-Pitman theorem to show that the learned representation is a sufficient statistic of each view with respect to the generative factor, producing a Sufficient Statistics contrastive loss where the aggregation function Q is the mean of the other views' embeddings. Both families recover SimCLR at M=2, and the Sufficient Statistics loss additionally recovers SigLIP for a specific choice of F^(2). Empirically, Geometric PVC at batch size 256 with M=8 trained for 128 epochs outperforms SimCLR at batch size 4096 trained for 1024 epochs on ImageNet1k, establishing a new compute Pareto front.

## Results

On ImageNet1k with ResNet-50 and linear probe evaluation, the headline result is that Geometric PVC at batch size 256 (K=512 unique samples, M=8 views) trained for 128 epochs outperforms SimCLR at batch size 4096 (M=2) trained for 1024 epochs — an 8× reduction in training epochs and 16× reduction in batch size. In the Fixed Batch setting (total views V=KM held constant), increasing multiplicity from M=2 to M=8 while reducing unique samples K from 4096 to 512 yields a new compute Pareto front where Geometric PVC and Sufficient Statistics dominate. In the Growing Batch setting (K fixed at 4096), gains over SimCLR are minor when measured by relative compute. Across all multiplicities M∈{2,3,4,6,8,12,16} at 128 epochs, increasing M is never harmful, with Geometric PVC performing strongest overall.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SimCLR (Chen et al., 2020a), Geometric PVC at M=8, batch 256, 128 epochs clearly wins on ImageNet1k linear probe accuracy, matching or exceeding SimCLR at batch 4096, 1024 epochs — the paper's central empirical claim. Against Multi-Crop (Caron et al., 2020; Tian et al., 2020a), Geometric PVC and Sufficient Statistics outperform in the Fixed Batch setting, while Multi-Crop outperforms Sufficient Statistics in the Growing Batch setting; Multi-Crop is compute-equivalent to SimCLR and does not achieve the same Pareto gains. Against the all-pairs formulation of Tian et al. (2020a), the poly-view objectives (Arithmetic/Geometric PVC, Sufficient Statistics) provide tighter MI lower bounds (smaller MI Gap) that improve monotonically with M, whereas Multi-Crop's MI Gap increases with M due to the log(K) factor. In the synthetic 1D Gaussian validation, Geometric PVC achieves the lowest MI Gap among all methods, confirming the theoretical predictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the information-theoretic foundations of contrastive learning (InfoNCE, NWJ estimator) from the standard two-view setting to a general M-view 'poly-view' formulation, deriving new non-decomposable objectives (Arithmetic/Geometric PVC) that improve the MI lower bound itself, unlike Multi-Crop which only reduces gradient variance. It additionally introduces a sufficient-statistics perspective on contrastive learning, showing that the Sufficient Statistics objective recovers SimCLR at M=2 and generalizes naturally to higher multiplicity. The work departs from prior multi-view contrastive methods (Tian et al. 2020a, Caron et al. 2020) by arguing that linear combinations of pairwise tasks cannot improve the MI bound, and by providing a theoretical framework (One-vs-Rest MI, MI Gap monotonicity) that unifies these objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical finding—that reducing batch size from 4096 to 256 while increasing view multiplicity from 2 to 8 yields a new compute Pareto front, with 128-epoch training outperforming 1024-epoch SimCLR—challenges the entrenched assumption that contrastive learning demands large batches and long training. However, the empirical validation is limited to ResNet-50 on ImageNet1k with M≤16, and the authors acknowledge open questions about transfer to ViTs, other SSL paradigms (BYOL, DINO), and whether per-multiplicity hyperparameter tuning would shift conclusions. The theoretical contributions (monotonic MI Gap, sufficient statistics connection) provide a principled framework for future work on view multiplicity design.
