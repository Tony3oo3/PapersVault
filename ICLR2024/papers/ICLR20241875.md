## Problem

Existing training-free diffusion ODE solvers (DPM-Solver, DPM-Solver++, UniPC) apply fixed solving strategies uniformly across all timesteps, leading to suboptimal sample quality when the NFE budget drops below 10. The paper identifies that the optimal strategy for each solver component (Taylor order, prediction type, derivative estimation, corrector) varies significantly across timesteps, but no systematic framework exists to study or jointly optimize these per-step decisions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241875/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen pre-trained diffusion U-Net (no retraining) and a random Gaussian noise vector, optionally conditioned on a text prompt (Stable Diffusion) or class label (classifier/classifier-free guidance). It outputs a generated image at the target resolution (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 64×64 for ImageNet-64, 256×256 for MS-COCO) and, as a byproduct of the search, an optimized solver schedule — a per-timestep sequence specifying timestep placement, prediction type, Taylor expansion order, derivative estimation method, scaling coefficient, and corrector usage. The entire pipeline is constrained by a user-specified NFE budget (4–10 in experiments).

## Method

USF decomposes each ODE step in the exponential-integral (λ-domain) formulation into six independently searchable decisions: timestep discretization, neural prediction type (noise vs. data), starting point, Taylor expansion order, derivative estimation (Taylor-difference order + scaling coefficient), and corrector usage. This unification subsumes DPM-Solver, DPM-Solver++, UniPC, and DEIS as special cases (Table 1). To navigate the exponentially large schedule space, S³ employs a predictor-based multi-stage evolutionary search: a lightweight LSTM-based predictor trained with pair-wise ranking loss estimates schedule quality, guiding mutation/crossover in each iteration, while a three-stage workflow progressively expands the evaluated population and retrains the predictor to improve generalization. The key empirical finding is that low-order derivative estimation with data prediction and non-uniform timestep placement (denser at intermediate t) yield the largest gains at tight budgets, achieving 2.69 FID at 10 NFE and 6.86 FID at 5 NFE on CIFAR-10.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, S³ achieves 2.69 FID at 10 NFE and 6.86 FID at 5 NFE, substantially outperforming the best baseline (3.90 and 23.44 FID respectively). On ImageNet-64, the method reaches 16.68 FID at 10 NFE versus 24.23 for the best baseline, and on LSUN-Bedroom it achieves 3.76 FID at 10 NFE versus 8.52. Applied to Stable Diffusion on MS-COCO 256×256, S³ achieves a 2× acceleration (5 NFE matching 10 NFE baseline quality, 16.84 vs. 20.59 FID) without retraining the neural network.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three families of SOTA training-free samplers: DPM-Solver (Lu et al., 2022a), DPM-Solver++ (Lu et al., 2022b), and UniPC (Zhao et al., 2023), evaluating 9 specific configurations in total. S³ outperforms all baselines across every dataset and NFE budget (4–10), with the largest margins at very low budgets: on CIFAR-10 at 5 NFE, S³ achieves 6.86 FID versus 23.44 for the best baseline (a 3.4× improvement), and on ImageNet-64 at 4 NFE it reaches 33.84 versus 76.69. The gap narrows at higher NFE budgets (e.g., CIFAR-10 at 10 NFE: 2.69 vs. 3.90), but S³ still wins consistently. On Stable Diffusion (MS-COCO 256×256), S³ at 5 NFE (16.84 FID) matches or exceeds the best baseline at 10 NFE (17.22 FID), demonstrating a 2× speedup. Notably, the best-performing baseline configuration varies across datasets and NFE budgets, highlighting that no single fixed solver setting is universally optimal — a key motivation for the per-timestep schedule search.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the family of exponential-integral-based ODE solvers for diffusion models (DPM-Solver, DPM-Solver++, UniPC, DEIS) under a single framework (USF) that decomposes each sampling step into six independent decisions: timestep, starting point, prediction type, Taylor order, derivative estimation, and corrector usage. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the observation that optimal solver strategies are not constant across timesteps, motivating the search for per-step 'solver schedules' rather than a single fixed configuration. The search methodology (S³) borrows the predictor-based evolutionary search paradigm from AutoML/NAS literature and applies it to the novel problem of optimizing solver schedules under an NFE budget.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: achieving 2.69 FID at 10 NFE and 6.86 FID at 5 NFE on CIFAR-10, and a 2× acceleration on Stable Diffusion without retraining, makes very few-step sampling genuinely viable for production use. The unified framework also provides a systematic vocabulary for comparing and combining solver strategies, which should accelerate future method design. Limitations include the search overhead (thousands of GPU-hours for schedule evaluation), the empirical pruning of the search space, and the fact that the predictor-based approach may not generalize across model architectures or noise schedules without re-searching. Open questions include whether the per-timestep strategy insights transfer to score-based SDE solvers or consistency models, and whether the search can be made cheaper via learned surrogates.
