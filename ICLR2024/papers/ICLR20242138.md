## Problem

Accurate long-range dynamics modeling in interactive environments is critical for model-based RL and planning, but existing approaches face two fundamental obstacles: sequential unrolling makes parallel training infeasible for long horizons, and nonlinear latent dynamics (MLP, GRU) suffer from vanishing or exploding gradients through time. This paper addresses both issues by leveraging Koopman theory to linearize nonlinear controlled dynamics in a high-dimensional complex-valued latent space, enabling efficient parallel training via convolution while providing explicit control over gradient stability.

## Input / Output

The model takes a starting state (state vector from MuJoCo or RGB image from DMC) and a sequence of actions over a prediction horizon τ, and outputs the predicted future state sequence and reward sequence. Both states and actions are encoded into a complex-valued latent space via neural network encoders, and the dynamics are propagated through a single diagonal Koopman operator. The key structural detail is that the entire τ-step prediction is computed as one circular convolution with a Vandermonde kernel (computed via FFT), rather than τ sequential matrix multiplications, so the computational cost scales near-linearly with horizon length rather than quadratically.

## Method

The method decouples state and control observables so that latent dynamics follow the linear form g(x_{t+1}) = Kg(x_t) + Lf(u_t), where K is a diagonal Koopman matrix with complex eigenvalues λ_j = μ_j + iω_j. Discretizing the continuous-time dynamics via Zero-Order Hold and unrolling τ steps reduces the prediction to a row-wise circular convolution between a Vandermonde matrix (powers of the eigenvalues) and the encoded action sequence, computable in O(m·τ·log τ) via FFT. The real parts μ_j of the eigenvalues directly control gradient decay through time (Theorem 3.1: gradient norm scales as e^{kΔt·μ_j}), so setting μ_j to small negative values (e.g., −0.2) prevents both vanishing and exploding gradients over long horizons. The imaginary parts ω_j are initialized as increasing frequencies (ω_j = jπ) to cover a wide spectral range. Training uses a combined Koopman consistency loss in latent space and a state-prediction loss in observation space, with the encoder, decoder, and Koopman parameters learned end-to-end.

## Results

On D4RL MuJoCo environments (hopper, walker2d, halfcheetah), the Koopman-based dynamics model achieves state prediction MSE of 0.007–0.910 over a 100-step horizon, competitive with the best GRU baseline (0.006–0.850) while significantly outperforming MLP (0.300–4.500), Transformer (0.060–3.300), and DSSM (0.030–1.200). In terms of training speed, the Koopman model is the fastest across all sequence lengths, achieving 565 iterations/s at length 10 and 24.56 iterations/s at length 500 on halfcheetah-expert-v2, roughly 2× faster than GRU and 3–4× faster than MLP at longer horizons. For model-based planning (TD-MPC) and model-free RL (SAC+SPR), the Koopman dynamics model outperforms the MLP-based counterparts across all tested environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the MLP-based dynamics model, the Koopman method achieves 5–50× lower state prediction MSE across all nine D4RL environments (e.g., 0.007 vs 0.500 on hopper-expert, 0.910 vs 4.500 on walker2d-full-replay), and is also the only method that can be stably trained beyond 10 time steps without gradient explosion. Compared to the causal Transformer, Koopman is consistently superior, with the gap widening on harder (full-replay) datasets. Against DSSM, Koopman wins on 7 of 9 environments for state prediction, with the largest margins on hopper-full-replay (0.070 vs 0.180) and walker2d-full-replay (0.910 vs 1.200). The GRU baseline is the closest competitor: Koopman is slightly worse on hopper-expert (0.007 vs 0.006) and halfcheetah-expert (0.300 vs 0.280) but wins on walker2d-expert (0.090 vs 0.100) and halfcheetah-full-replay (0.700 vs 0.750), while being ~2× faster in training. For model-based planning, Koopman TD-MPC (horizon 20) outperforms vanilla TD-MPC (horizon 5) on all four DMC environments with lower variance, and for model-free RL, Koopman-based SPR outperforms both MLP-based SPR and vanilla SAC on all five DMC pixel-space tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies Koopman operator theory—previously used primarily in control theory and system identification (Brunton et al., 2021; Korda & Mezić, 2018)—to the RL dynamics modeling problem, specifically for controlled, interactive environments. It departs from Diagonal State Space Models (DSSM; Gu et al., 2022a) by grounding the diagonal linear dynamics in the Koopman eigenfunction framework with explicit control input, rather than in HiPPO-derived convolution kernels for sequence modeling. The spectral analysis of the Koopman matrix unifies and generalizes prior stabilization techniques including unitary RNNs (Arjovsky et al., 2016) and structured state-space models (S4; Gu et al., 2022b), while the convolution-based reformulation of the unrolled dynamics enables parallel training that is absent in recurrent baselines (GRU, MLP) and distinct from the attention-based parallelism of Transformers. The method is integrated into two established RL frameworks—TD-MPC for model-based planning (Hansen et al., 2022) and SPR for self-predictive representations in model-free RL (Schwarzer et al., 2020)—replacing their MLP-based latent dynamics with the diagonal Koopman operator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple diagonal linear model, properly initialized in the Koopman eigenspectrum, can match or exceed nonlinear recurrent and attention-based dynamics models over 100-step horizons while being 2× faster, with the speed advantage growing with horizon length—a practical efficiency win for long-range prediction that is a known bottleneck in model-based RL. The gradient analysis (Theorem 3.1) provides a clean, interpretable mechanism for controlling gradient magnitude through time via eigenvalue real parts, offering a principled alternative to ad-hoc stabilization tricks. However, the model is restricted to deterministic environments, the RL and planning results are preliminary with modest gains over strong baselines, and the authors identify distribution shift between training and evaluation as an unresolved issue. Open directions include stochastic Koopman extensions for uncertainty-aware dynamics, integration into full model-based RL (e.g., Dreamer-style), and broader task coverage beyond MuJoCo/DMC continuous control.
