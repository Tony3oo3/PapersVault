## Problem

Existing methods for recovering causal structure in the presence of latent variables rely on strong assumptions—non-Gaussianity of all noise terms, purity (no edges between observed variables), and at least two pure children per latent variable—that frequently fail in real-world settings. This paper addresses the gap by proving identifiability of the full causal structure (over both latent and observed variables) under two sets of milder assumptions that simultaneously avoid all three of these constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241073/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes purely observational samples of a fixed set of observed variables from a linear latent variable model (no observed variable is a parent of any latent one) and outputs a partially directed acyclic graph (PAG) over the union of identified latent and observed variables. The latent variables are not given a priori; they are discovered from the data, and each is assigned at least two measured indicators (pure children or constructed linear combinations) that serve as proxies for the PC-MIMBuild skeleton-finding step. The final graph is identified up to Markov equivalence without additional distributional assumptions.

## Method

The approach decomposes structure recovery into two sub-problems: (1) identifying latent variables and (2) inferring causal relations. For identification, the paper introduces the notion of generalized pure pairs (pure pairs plus pseudo-pure pairs where one observed variable causally influences the other while both share a latent parent) and proves that under Assumption 1, a pair is a generalized pure pair if and only if it satisfies the tetrad constraint among candidate variables. Case I (one pure child per latent, arbitrary distribution) discriminates pure from pseudo-pure pairs via a graphical tetrad test (Lemma 2), while Case II (no pure child required, partial non-Gaussianity) uses a Darmois-Skitovich-based independence test on linear combinations (Lemma 3) and converts pseudo-pure pairs into pure ones via a linear transformation (Corollary 2). For causal relation inference, the paper performs pre-processing to classify observed variables as determined (fully characterized by their pair membership) or undetermined, constructs auxiliary indicators for undetermined variables, and then runs a modified PC-MIMBuild that restricts separation-set searches to latent variables and orients latent-to-observed edges early to enable more Meek-rule orientations.

## Results

On synthetic data with structure G₁ (one pure child per latent, arbitrary noise), the proposed method achieves an error rate of 0.00 at N=2000, compared to 1.0 for all baselines (BPC, FOFC, GIN), with LO=0.00, LC=0.00, WI=0.00. On G₂ (no pure child, partial non-Gaussianity), the method reaches Err=0.2 at N=2000 (LO=0.03, LC=0.00, WI=0.03), again outperforming all baselines which remain at Err=1.0. On the real-world HolzingerSwineford1939 dataset, the method achieves perfect recovery (LO=LC=WI=0.00), and on Teacher Burnout it achieves LO=0.25, WI=0.06, substantially better than BPC (LO=0.42, WI=0.13) and GIN (LO=0.75, WI=0.34).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All three baselines fail completely (Err=1.0) on both synthetic graphs G₁ and G₂ because their required assumptions (three-pure-children for FOFC, non-Gaussianity and purity for GIN, three-pure-children for BPC) are violated by design in these test cases. The proposed method is the only one that achieves correct or near-correct recovery: on G₁ it reaches zero error at N=2000, and on G₂ it reduces error to 0.2. On the real-world HolzingerSwineford1939 dataset, the proposed method ties BPC with perfect recovery (LO=LC=WI=0), while FOFC and GIN fail. On the more challenging Teacher Burnout dataset (32 observed, 12 latent variables), the proposed method substantially outperforms BPC (LO 0.25 vs 0.42; WI 0.06 vs 0.13) and GIN (LO 0.25 vs 0.75; WI 0.06 vs 0.34), while FOFC completely fails (LO=1.00). The paper notes that on graphs where baselines' assumptions do hold (e.g., G₃ with three pure children), BPC and FOFC also succeed, confirming the proposed method's compatibility with stronger assumptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of latent causal structure learning initiated by Silva et al. (2006) PC-MIMBuild, which required the three-pure-children assumption, through successive relaxations by Kummerfeld & Ramsey (2016), Cai et al. (2019), and Xie et al. (2020, 2023b). The key departure is the introduction of pseudo-pure pairs and generalized pure pairs as first-class identification tools, enabling the simultaneous elimination of the non-Gaussianity, purity, and two-pure-children assumptions that prior methods required in some combination. Rather than recovering only the latent causal subgraph, the paper targets the full causal structure over both latent and observed variables, modifying PC-MIMBuild with pre-processing steps that exploit the identified pure/pseudo-pure structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that real-world systems (e.g., financial markets, psychometric questionnaires) routinely violate the purity and non-Gaussianity assumptions, making prior methods inapplicable; this paper provides the first identifiability guarantees and efficient algorithms for such settings. However, the assumptions still require at least one generalized pure pair per latent variable and specific graphical conditions (e.g., Assumption 1(b) requiring latent neighbors), and the expedient for choosing between Case I and Case II is acknowledged as imperfect. Open questions include extending to nonlinear or cyclic models, handling cases where observed variables are parents of latent ones, and developing rigorous assumption-testing procedures rather than the heuristic currently proposed.
