## Problem

Existing saliency methods for language models are designed for text classification with masked encoders and do not align with the autoregressive generation objective of LLMs, which must explain token selection across the entire vocabulary. Moreover, these methods rely heavily on linearity assumptions (Taylor expansion for gradients, linear combinations of attention weights) that introduce significant inaccuracies as LLM depth increases. The role of individual prompt tokens in shaping LLM outputs, and how this can be exploited for controlled generation, remains largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (token sequence), a designated target token, and an alternative token as input, operating on the LLM's model weights without any additional training. It produces per-token contrastive saliency scores that explain why the LLM generates the target token over the alternative at the next position. For manipulation tasks, the output is a modified prompt where high-saliency trigger tokens are neutralized (replaced with a space token) or sentiment cues are substituted with key tokens ("positive"/"negative"), yielding controlled text continuations of up to 20 tokens.

## Method

TDD projects all input token hidden states from the final layer through the LM head into the embedding space, yielding interpretable token distributions over the vocabulary for every position (not just the last token). TDD-forward computes the target-vs-alternative probability difference r_i at each position i and takes successive differences (c_i = r_i − r_{i−1}) to attribute saliency to individual tokens, requiring only a single forward pass due to the decoder-only architecture. TDD-backward reverses the direction by progressively incorporating tokens from the end of the prompt, while TDD-bidirectional sums both directional estimates. Unlike gradient-based or attention-rollout methods, TDD avoids linearity assumptions entirely by working directly with the non-linear LM head projections, and it provides contrastive explanations aligned with autoregressive generation. The approach is applied to zero-shot toxic language suppression (identifying and neutralizing toxic triggers) and sentiment steering (substituting sentiment cues) by selecting the top-saliency tokens for intervention.

## Results

On the BLiMP benchmark (11 linguistic minimal-pair datasets), TDD-backward achieves the highest AOPC scores across all five LLMs, reaching 70.46% (GPT2), 70.61% (GPTJ), 70.20% (BLOOM), 71.29% (Pythia), and 63.71% (LLaMA2), surpassing the best baseline (Con-GI) by 4–7 percentage points. TDD also yields the lowest Sufficiency scores (e.g., 52.67% on Pythia vs. 59.94% for Con-GI), confirming more faithful explanations. In zero-shot toxic language suppression on RealToxicityPrompts, TDD reduces overall toxicity to 0.20 (vs. 0.24 for Con-GN and 0.26 for FFNControl) while preserving fluency. For sentiment steering on OpenWebText, TDD achieves 87% negative-sentiment and 78% positive-sentiment outputs, outperforming FFNControl (82%/68%) and Con-GI (85%/75%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TDD consistently outperforms all six baseline families across every LLM and dataset. Against the strongest saliency baselines, Con-GI and Con-GN (Yin & Neubig, 2022), TDD-backward gains approximately 5–7% in AOPC (e.g., 71.29 vs. 64.59 on Pythia) and 5–7% lower Sufficiency, indicating substantially more faithful token attributions. The attention-rollout baseline (Abnar & Zuidema, 2020) and Integrated Gradients (Sundararajan et al., 2017) trail TDD by a similar margin, confirming that gradient- and attention-based approximations are less aligned with autoregressive generation objectives. In the controlled-generation tasks, TDD surpasses FFNControl (Geva et al., 2022) by 5% on negative-sentiment steering (87% vs. 82%) and 10% on positive-sentiment steering (78% vs. 68%), while also achieving the lowest toxicity score (0.20 vs. 0.26) on RealToxicityPrompts. Style-prompting baselines (SP/ASP from Reif et al., 2022) and the WORDFILTER blacklist (Gehman et al., 2020) are substantially weaker, with toxicity scores of 0.47/0.41 and 0.36 respectively, and sentiment percentages barely above the unmodified GPT2 baseline (51–57% vs. 48–52%). Causal ablations (swapping target/alternative tokens, random saliency assignment) confirm that TDD's advantage stems from accurate trigger identification rather than the mere act of token replacement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Logit Lens line of work (Nostalgebraist, 2020; Geva et al., 2021, 2022; Dar et al., 2023), which showed that token hidden states can be projected into the vocabulary space via the LM head, by repurposing these projected token distributions as a saliency medium. It departs from gradient-based methods (IG, Con-GN, Con-GI) and vector-based methods (Rollout, GlobEnc) that rely on linearity assumptions or misalign with autoregressive generation objectives, instead computing saliency from distribution dynamics over the full vocabulary. The contrastive explanation framing (target vs. alternative token) follows Yin & Neubig (2022), but replaces their contrastive gradients with distribution differences, eliminating the Taylor-approximation error that plagues gradient-based approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TDD offers a principled, generation-aligned alternative to gradient and attention-based saliency that consistently outperforms baselines across five LLM families (GPT2 through LLaMA2), and its single-forward-pass variant (TDD-forward) matches or exceeds methods requiring backpropagation. The practical demonstrations on toxic language suppression and sentiment steering show that better saliency directly translates to better controlled generation, outperforming parameter-manipulation methods like FFNControl. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to encoder-decoder architectures, whether it extends naturally to multi-token (sequence-level) saliency rather than single next-token prediction, and whether the backward/bidirectional variants' n-fold computational cost limits their use in very long contexts.
