## Problem

Decision Transformer (DT) relies on a full attention module to mix information across trajectory tokens, but offline RL data follows a Markov decision process where the true dependence structure is local and banded rather than global. The paper shows that DT's attention matrix is an overparameterized artifact of the Q/K parameterization that fails to reflect the Markovian forgetting property, and asks whether a simpler local filtering mechanism is sufficient for decision-making in MDPs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240648/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a trimodal token sequence of length 3K−1 consisting of interleaved return-to-go scalars, state vectors, and action vectors over K timesteps (K=8 for DC vs. K=20 for DT). Each modality is embedded to a common hidden dimension d via separate linear projections. The output is the predicted next action at the current timestep, produced by a final linear projection applied to the state token after passing through all MetaFormer blocks. In the Atari domain, raw observations are 4-frame-stacked RGB images processed by a CNN encoder before entering the sequence model.

## Method

DC replaces DT's attention token mixer with 1D depthwise causal convolution: three separate filters (one each for RTG, state, and action positions) of length L=6 are applied independently per hidden dimension, yielding only 3dL parameters per block versus 2dd′+d² for attention. The filters are static (input-independent), so they encode dataset-level local associations rather than per-sequence attention weights, and L=6 is chosen to span exactly the current and previous (RTG, s, a) triple, imposing an inductive bias aligned with the Markov assumption. The overall architecture follows the MetaFormer template (LN → Conv → residual → LN → FFN → residual) stacked N times, with a hybrid variant appending a final attention block for long-horizon credit assignment in Atari. Training minimizes MSE between predicted and true actions over K timesteps, and inference conditions on a target RTG that is decremented by realized rewards at each step.

## Results

On D4RL MuJoCo locomotion tasks, DC achieves a mean expert-normalized return of 82.2, outperforming DT (76.4), ODT (81.0), IQL (76.5), and CQL (77.6). On D4RL AntMaze, DC reaches 81.8 mean return, a 24% improvement over DT (65.8) and surpassing CQL (79.0). In the Atari domain, DC achieves a mean game-normalized score of 86.2 versus DT's 62.2 (a 39% gain), while the hybrid DC variant reaches 94.7. DC also reduces training time by 75% on Atari (193s vs 764s per epoch) and uses 96% fewer token-mixer parameters than DT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DC outperforms all eight baselines on the MuJoCo locomotion mean (82.2) and AntMaze mean (81.8). The most dramatic gains are over DT: DC scores 92.5 on hopper-medium vs DT's 68.4 (+35%), and 81.8 vs 65.8 on AntMaze (+24%). Against the strongest return-conditioned BC baseline ODT, DC leads by 1.2 points on locomotion (82.2 vs 81.0) and by a large margin on AntMaze (81.8 vs 57.7). Among value-based methods, DC surpasses CQL (77.6 locomotion, 79.0 AntMaze), IQL (76.5, 75.8), and TD3+BC (75.3, 75.0) on both domain means. In the Atari domain, DC (86.2) and DC-hybrid (94.7) substantially exceed DT (62.2), CQL (64.9), and BC (44.9), with the largest gap on Breakout (352.7 vs 242.4 for DT). For online finetuning, ODC achieves the best locomotion mean (72.68) and antmaze mean (73.91) over ODT (70.34, 73.35) and IQL (67.56, 73.2), with notably smaller offline-to-online performance drops (δ = 0.05 locomotion, 6.46 AntMaze) compared to ODT (1.28, 15.63).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Decision Transformer (DT), which reframed offline RL as return-conditioned sequence modeling, and on the MetaFormer framework (Yu et al., 2022) that abstracts Transformer blocks into a general token-mixer + FFN structure. The key departure is replacing DT's self-attention token mixer with 1D depthwise causal convolution filters, motivated by the observation that MDP trajectories exhibit a banded (local) dependence structure due to the Markov property, making full attention an overparameterization. It positions itself alongside other return-conditioned BC methods (RvS, Decision S4, ODT) and value-based baselines (CQL, IQL, TD3+BC), but distinguishes itself by focusing on accurate local-association modeling rather than global sequence modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a drastically simpler token mixer (3dL parameters vs. 2dd' + d² for attention) not only matches but substantially outperforms DT across MuJoCo, AntMaze, and Atari, while reducing training time by up to 75% and token-mixer parameters by 95–99%. The superior generalization to out-of-distribution target RTGs suggests DC has a more appropriate model complexity, implying DT was overfitting to spurious long-range patterns. A key limitation is that pure convolution is not immediately suited for long-horizon reasoning (meta-learning, partial observability), though the proposed hybrid conv+attention architecture partially addresses this. Open questions include whether the local-filtering principle extends to other MDP-related sequence tasks (e.g., value function prediction, reward modeling) and whether the Markov inductive bias can be relaxed adaptively per environment.
