## Problem

Existing multimodal large language models can perceive images and generate free-form text but cannot ground language to specific visual regions or accept spatial references (e.g., bounding boxes) as input. This limits their ability to resolve co-reference ambiguity, support pointing-based interaction, and produce spatially precise vision-language predictions. The paper asks whether grounding and referring can be unlocked as native capabilities of an MLLM rather than bolted on as external modules.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242014/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts 224×224 RGB images (encoded by a 24-layer vision encoder with 14×14 patches, resampled to 64 image embeddings) alongside text prompts in a special 'hyperlink' format where text spans are wrapped in <p>…</p> tokens and linked to bounding boxes expressed as <box><loc_tl><loc_br></box> sequences. The image is discretized into a 32×32 grid of 7×7-pixel bins, yielding 1,024 location tokens added to the vocabulary; a <grounding> token signals the model to produce spatial output. Outputs include generated location tokens (convertible back to continuous coordinates via bin centers), free-form text descriptions, and grounded captions where text spans are inline-linked to their bounding boxes.

## Method

KOSMOS-2 is a 24-layer MAGNETO Transformer (1.6B parameters) trained with autoregressive language modeling, built on KOSMOS-1's architecture but augmented with 1,024 location tokens and a Markdown-style 'hyperlink' data format that binds text spans to discretized bounding-box coordinates within the same token sequence. The key training resource is GRIT (91M images, 115M text spans, 137M bounding boxes), constructed via a two-step pipeline: (1) spaCy extracts noun chunks from captions and GLIP grounds them to bounding boxes (confidence > 0.65, NMS applied), then (2) dependency-tree traversal expands noun chunks into referring expressions, retaining only non-contained spans. The model is trained for 60K steps (~25B tokens) on GRIT mixed with text corpora, image-caption pairs, and interleaved data, followed by instruction tuning on LLaVA-Instruct, Unnatural Instructions, FLANv2, and grounded instruction templates. Unlike prior approaches that attach a separate detector or grounding head, KOSMOS-2 treats grounding as a foundational generative capability: the same autoregressive decoder that produces text also emits location tokens, enabling open-ended applications like grounded captioning, grounded VQA, and pronoun-based referring without task-specific architectures.

## Results

On phrase grounding (Flickr30k Entities), KOSMOS-2 achieves 78.7 R@1 zero-shot, outperforming the zero-shot GRILL by 59.8 points and the finetuned VisualBERT by 7.4 points. On referring expression comprehension, KOSMOS-2 reaches 61.65 accuracy on RefCOCOg test (zero-shot), surpassing GRILL (47.50) but trailing finetuned FIBER (87.32) by 25.7 points. For referring expression generation on RefCOCOg, KOSMOS-2 achieves 60.3 CIDEr zero-shot, exceeding the finetuned SLR baseline (59.2). On image captioning (Flickr30k), KOSMOS-2 scores 80.5 CIDEr with only 1.6B parameters, substantially outperforming Flamingo-9B (61.5) and BLIP-2 (71.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

KOSMOS-2 decisively outperforms GRILL (the only other zero-shot grounding model) across all grounding benchmarks: 78.7 vs. 18.9 R@1 on Flickr30k Entities phrase grounding and 61.65 vs. 47.50 accuracy on RefCOCOg test. Against finetuned grounding models (FIBER, MDETR, GLIP, OFA), KOSMOS-2 trails by 15–26 points on RefCOCO/RefCOCO+ comprehension, which the authors attribute to shorter spatial expressions in those datasets; however, the gap narrows on RefCOCOg (61.65 vs. 87.32 for FIBER). On phrase grounding, KOSMOS-2 surpasses finetuned VisualBERT (78.7 vs. 71.3 R@1) but remains below GLIP (87.1) and FIBER (87.4). For referring expression generation, KOSMOS-2 zero-shot (60.3 CIDEr) beats finetuned SLR (59.2 CIDEr) but falls short of SLR+Rerank (66.2). On image captioning, KOSMOS-2 (80.5 CIDEr) outperforms Flamingo-3B (60.6), Flamingo-9B (61.5), and BLIP-2 (71.6) by large margins, while on VQAv2 it matches KOSMOS-1 (51.1 vs. 51.0) but lags BLIP-2 (65.0) by 13.9 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

KOSMOS-2 extends KOSMOS-1 (Huang et al., 2023), a from-scratch multimodal LLM, by adding grounding and referring as native autoregressive capabilities rather than bolted-on modules. It adopts the discrete location-token discretization from Pix2Seq (Chen et al., 2021) but departs from specialized grounding models (GLIP, MDETR, FIBER) by embedding spatial grounding directly into the causal language modeling objective via a Markdown-style 'hyperlink' representation. Unlike instruction-tuning MLLMs (LLaVA, InstructBLIP) that retrofit vision onto pretrained LLMs, KOSMOS-2 trains grounding from scratch alongside language and perception, positioning spatial grounding as a foundational capability rather than a downstream task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GRIT dataset (91M images, 137M bounding boxes) is orders of magnitude larger than existing grounding corpora and provides a reusable resource for the community. The paper demonstrates that a 1.6B model can achieve competitive zero-shot grounding without object queries, proposals, or post-processing, suggesting that autoregressive MLLMs can subsume specialized grounding architectures. However, performance on RefCOCO/RefCOCO+ remains below finetuned specialized models, and the paper acknowledges that understanding diverse, short human expressions (e.g., 'left bottom') remains an open challenge. The broader vision of grounding as a convergence point for language, perception, and world modeling toward AGI is ambitious but only partially validated here.
