## Problem

Verified adversarial training faces a fundamental tension: over-approximating the worst-case loss (e.g., via IBP) yields easily verifiable networks at the cost of standard accuracy, while adversarial training preserves accuracy but produces networks that are hard to verify. Recent methods like SABR and STAPS couple attacks with over-approximations in increasingly complex ways, but it remains unclear whether the specific coupling mechanism matters or whether a simpler property—expressivity, i.e., the ability to span the adversarial-to-verified loss range via a single parameter—is the key driver of performance.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard image classification benchmarks: MNIST (28×28 grayscale), CIFAR-10 (32×32 RGB), TinyImageNet (64×64 RGB, 200 classes), and downscaled ImageNet (64×64 RGB, 1000 classes), with ℓ∞ perturbation radii ranging from 1/255 to 8/255. The trained CNN7 network (7 layers with BatchNorm after each layer) outputs class logits, and verified robustness is assessed via branch-and-bound (OVAL framework with α-β-CROWN bounding and UPB branching) over the full perturbation ball, producing a binary robustness certificate per input.

## Method

The paper defines a class of 'expressive losses' parameterized by an over-approximation coefficient α∈[0,1] that continuously interpolates between the adversarial loss (α=0) and the IBP verified loss (α=1), and proves that SABR satisfies this definition. Three minimalistic instantiations are proposed via convex combinations: CC-IBP combines adversarial and IBP lower bounds on logit differences inside the loss function; MTL-IBP takes a weighted sum of the adversarial and verified losses (a multi-task scalarization); and Exp-IBP takes the geometric mean of the two losses. All three use IBP for bounds and randomly-initialized single-step FGSM for attacks, trained with the Shi et al. (2021) pipeline (specialized initialization, ℓ₁ regularization, ramp-up). The key empirical finding is that all three attain state-of-the-art robustness-accuracy trade-offs on CIFAR-10, TinyImageNet, and ImageNet64, and that the best α does not correspond to the tightest approximation of the branch-and-bound loss—over- or under-approximations can both be optimal depending on the setting.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ε=2/255), CC-IBP achieves 80.09% standard and 63.78% verified robust accuracy, outperforming STAPS (79.76/62.98) and SABR (79.89/63.28). On TinyImageNet (ε=1/255), the three expressive losses improve standard accuracy by at least 8.58% and verified robust accuracy by at least 3.93% over the best prior results (e.g., CC-IBP: 38.61/26.39 vs. STAPS: 28.98/22.16). On downscaled ImageNet (ε=1/255), Exp-IBP reaches 22.73% standard and 13.30% verified robust accuracy, surpassing SortNet (14.79/9.54) by 7.94% and 3.76% respectively. The only setting where a literature method wins is CIFAR-10 ε=8/255, where the specialized SortNet architecture achieves 40.39% verified robust accuracy vs. 35.44% for MTL-IBP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All three expressive losses (CC-IBP, MTL-IBP, Exp-IBP) achieve state-of-the-art robustness-accuracy trade-offs on CIFAR-10 (ε=2/255), TinyImageNet, and ImageNet64, outperforming STAPS, SABR, IBP-R, CROWN-IBP, COLT, AdvIBP, and SortNet. On TinyImageNet, the improvements over the best prior ReLU-based methods (STAPS/SABR) are substantial: +9.63% standard accuracy and +4.23% verified robust accuracy for CC-IBP. On ImageNet64, Exp-IBP surpasses SortNet by 7.94% standard and 3.76% verified robust accuracy. The sole exception is CIFAR-10 ε=8/255, where SortNet's specialized architecture achieves 40.39% verified robust accuracy versus 35.44% for MTL-IBP (a 4.95% gap). MTL-IBP also significantly outperforms the closely-related AdvIBP (Fan & Li, 2021) on all benchmarks, confirming that a properly tuned scalarization with specialized initialization and regularization is more effective than the COLT-like stage-wise procedure used in that work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the verified training literature under a single conceptual property—expressivity—which it defines as the ability of a parametrized loss to continuously interpolate between the adversarial loss and the verified (IBP) loss via a single coefficient α. It builds directly on SABR (Müller et al., 2023), which computes IBP bounds over small boxes around adversarial attacks, and on CROWN-IBP (Zhang et al., 2020), which already employed convex combinations between different bound types, but abstracts these into a general definition and shows that three trivially-constructed convex combinations (CC-IBP, MTL-IBP, Exp-IBP) satisfy it. The MTL-IBP loss connects to multi-task learning scalarization (Kurin et al., 2022; Xin et al., 2022), and the Exp-IBP form leverages the log-concavity of cross-entropy. The paper departs from prior work by arguing that the specific functional form of the expressive loss is less important than the expressivity property itself, and by explicitly challenging the assumption that tighter approximations of the worst-case loss yield better trade-offs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The expressivity framework provides practitioners with a simple, principled tuning knob (α) for navigating the robustness-accuracy trade-off, and the finding that three minimalistic convex combinations match or exceed more complex methods (SABR, STAPS) suggests that the field may have been over-engineering loss designs. The counterintuitive result that better approximations of the branch-and-bound loss do not necessarily improve performance challenges a widely-held assumption and redirects attention toward the role of the verifier's precision in determining the optimal α. Key limitations include restriction to ReLU networks and ℓ∞ perturbations, use of a single 7-layer CNN architecture, and the persistent large gap between verified and empirical robustness (e.g., 13.30% verified vs. 57.70% empirical on ImageNet). Open questions include whether expressivity generalizes to non-ReLU activations, other perturbation norms, and whether the α sensitivity patterns observed on CIFAR-10 hold at larger scale.
