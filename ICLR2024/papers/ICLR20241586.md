## Problem

Existing SPD and Grassmann neural networks lack the mathematical framework to generalize a broad class of DNN building blocks (FC layers, convolutional layers, MLR) to these matrix manifolds in a principled way. While spherical and hyperbolic spaces benefit from rich gyrovector space structures, analogous generalizations for SPD and Grassmann manifolds remained incomplete, and key computational issues such as backpropagation through the Grassmann logarithmic map in the projector perspective were unresolved.

## Input / Output

For action recognition, 3D skeleton sequences are converted to sets of SPD matrices by computing covariance matrices from joint-coordinate feature vectors within temporal pyramid windows (output sizes 34×34, 21×21, 11×11 for HDM05, FPHA, NTU60 respectively), and the network produces action class labels. For node classification, d-dimensional node features are linearly mapped and reshaped into Grassmann embeddings on Gr_{14,7} (projector perspective), and the network outputs node class labels. The SPSD variant operates in structure space Gr_{n,p} × Sym_p^+ where each SPSD matrix is decomposed into a subspace and an SPD shape component.

## Method

The core contribution is a gyrovector-space-based generalization of DNN layers to SPD and Grassmann manifolds. FC layers are constructed by interpreting the k-th output dimension as a pseudo-gyrodistance to an SPD hypergyroplane containing the origin and orthogonal to the k-th axis, with explicit orthonormal bases derived for Affine-Invariant, Log-Euclidean, and Log-Cholesky metrics. Convolutional layers extend this by treating multi-channel receptive fields as block-diagonal SPD concatenations. MLR is developed on SPSD manifolds via structure space Gr_{n,p} × Sym_p^+, where binary/inverse operations, inner products, and pseudo-gyrodistances are defined component-wise. For Grassmann GCNs, a key technical result (Proposition 3.12) expresses the projector-perspective logarithmic map through the ONB-perspective map (computed via SVD), enabling exact backpropagation without approximation; the GCN then uses left Grassmann gyrotranslations for feature transformation, weighted tangent-space aggregation at the identity, and QR-based nonlinearity.

## Results

On the human action recognition task, GyroSpd++ achieves 79.78% on HDM05, 96.84% on FPHA, and 95.28% on NTU60, while GyroSpsd++ (using MLR in structure spaces) reaches 78.52%, 97.90%, and 96.64% respectively, setting new state-of-the-art on FPHA and NTU60. On node classification, Gr-GCN++ (projector perspective) achieves 82.8% on Airport, 80.3% on Pubmed, and 81.6% on Cora, consistently outperforming its ONB-perspective variant Gr-GCN-ONB (81.9%, 76.2%, 68.1%) with the largest gap on Cora (+13.5 points). Model sizes are competitive: GyroSpd++ uses 0.76 MB vs. 6.58 MB for SPDNet on HDM05.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On action recognition, GyroSpd++ outperforms all SPD baselines on FPHA (+1.12 over SPSD-AI) and NTU60 (+1.01 over MLR-AI), and marginally beats SPSD-AI on HDM05 (79.78 vs. 79.64). GyroSpsd++ extends the lead on FPHA (+2.18) and NTU60 (+2.37) but trails SPSD-AI on HDM05 (78.52 vs. 79.64). Against non-SPD methods (HypGRU, MS-G3D, ST-TR, TGN), GyroSpd++ leads by 9.5–25.6 points on HDM05, 6.0–38.6 points on FPHA, and 2.2–7.0 points on NTU60. On node classification, Gr-GCN++ beats SPD-GCN on Pubmed (80.3 vs. 78.7) but trails it on Cora (81.6 vs. 82.3) and Airport (82.8 vs. 82.6, essentially tied). Gr-GCN++ is outperformed by HamGNN (95.9) and GAT (92.9) on the highly hyperbolic Airport dataset, consistent with prior findings that hyperbolic embeddings excel on tree-like graphs, but it achieves the best accuracy on Pubmed among all compared methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper completes the gyrovector space program for matrix manifolds initiated by Nguyen (2022b) and Nguyen & Yang (2023), which established that SPD and Grassmann manifolds possess gyrovector space structures analogous to hyperbolic spaces. It directly adapts the hyperplane-distance interpretation of FC layers from Shimizu et al. (2021) to SPD manifolds, and extends the GCN message-passing framework of Chami et al. (2019) and Zhao et al. (2023) to Grassmann geometry. The key departure from prior SPD/Grassmann network work (SPDNet, GrNet, ManifoldNet) is that those approaches lack a unifying algebraic framework, whereas this paper derives all building blocks—FC layers, convolutional layers, MLR, and GCN operations—from the gyrovector space structure, enabling principled generalization of any Euclidean DNN component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical and theoretically grounded toolkit for building neural networks on SPD and Grassmann manifolds, with the Grassmann logarithmic map backpropagation solution in the projector perspective being a concrete implementation contribution that unblocks gradient-based learning in popular frameworks. However, the authors acknowledge that using different Riemannian metrics across layers (AI for convolution, LE for MLR) is not clearly optimal, and the GCN's hybrid design (aggregation in tangent space, other operations on the manifold) limits modeling capacity as shown in hyperbolic analogues. Open questions include finding principled criteria for metric selection per layer and developing fully manifold-resident GCN operations on Grassmann spaces.
