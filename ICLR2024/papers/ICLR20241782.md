## Problem

Prior driving topology reasoning methods relied on complex graph-based models to predict lane-lane and lane-traffic connections, yet the paper demonstrates through ablation that topology scores are fundamentally bottlenecked by detection quality rather than topology prediction complexity. Additionally, the OpenLane-V2 topology metric contains a loophole where unmatched false positives default to confidence 1.0, artificially inflating scores.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241782/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB camera images (7 views for subset_A, 6 views for subset_B) resized to 1550×2048 and downsampled by 0.5. It produces up to 300 3D lane centerlines parameterized as Bezier curves with 4 control points within a bounded 3D region (±51.2m X, ±25.6m Y, −8m to 4m Z), 2D traffic element bounding boxes in the front view, and two binary adjacency matrices for lane-lane and lane-traffic topology. No post-processing is applied at inference.

## Method

TopoMLP pairs two strong detectors with two simple MLP topology heads. The lane detector (PETR-inspired) encodes 3D position embeddings into multi-view features and uses a 6-layer transformer decoder to predict Bezier control point offsets from 300 learnable 3D anchor queries. The traffic detector follows Deformable DETR with reference boxes, optionally initialized with YOLOv8 proposals to handle small objects. For topology, lane-lane prediction concatenates pairwise lane query features (augmented with MLP-embedded lane coordinates) and applies a 3-layer MLP for binary classification; lane-traffic prediction first projects lane features into the 2D perspective view via a camera transformation matrix before concatenation with traffic query features and MLP classification. The key empirical finding is that with strong detection, these simple MLPs with position embeddings outperform graph-based topology models, and the authors further propose a correctness-factor-adjusted TOP metric to close the evaluation loophole.

## Results

On OpenLane-V2 subset_A, TopoMLP with ResNet-50 backbone achieves 38.2 OLS, surpassing the previous best TopoNet (35.6 OLS) by 2.6 points. With the YOLOv8-enhanced variant (TopoMLP*), the OLS rises to 41.2, and with Swin-B backbone trained for 48 epochs, it reaches 43.7 OLS. The topology scores improve substantially over TopoNet: TOP_ll goes from 4.1 to 7.2 and TOP_lt from 20.8 to 22.8 under the same ResNet-50 setting. On subset_B, TopoMLP (ResNet-50) achieves 38.7 OLS vs. TopoNet's 33.2, with a particularly large gap in lane-lane topology (7.6 vs. 2.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TopoMLP outperforms all four baselines on OLS across both OpenLane-V2 subsets. Against the closest competitor TopoNet (Li et al., 2023), TopoMLP with ResNet-50 wins on TOP_ll (7.2 vs. 4.1, +3.1), TOP_lt (22.8 vs. 20.8, +2.0), and DET_t (50.0 vs. 48.1, +1.9), while TopoNet has a marginal edge on DET_l (28.5 vs. 28.3, −0.2). The gap widens dramatically on subset_B, where TopoMLP's TOP_ll reaches 7.6 versus TopoNet's 2.5. STSU (Can et al., 2021) and MapTR (Liao et al., 2022) trail by 12+ OLS points, while VectorMapNet (Liu et al., 2023a) is the weakest baseline at 20.8 OLS, primarily due to near-zero topology scores. The YOLOv8-enhanced variant (TopoMLP*) further boosts TOP_lt from 22.8 to 30.1, demonstrating that stronger traffic detection directly translates to better lane-traffic topology reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

TopoMLP builds directly on PETR's 3D position embedding mechanism for lane queries and Deformable DETR's deformable decoder for traffic element detection, combining them into a unified query-based framework. It departs from the prevailing graph-based topology modeling approaches (TopoNet, CenterLineDet, TopoRoad) by replacing complex graph neural networks with simple pairwise MLPs inspired by human-object interaction detection, arguing that strong detection makes elaborate topology reasoning unnecessary. The optional integration of YOLOv8 proposals as anchor initialization for the traffic detector represents a practical hybridization that breaks from purely end-to-end query-based detection paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central empirical finding—that detection quality is the dominant bottleneck for topology reasoning, with GT topology yielding only 0.5–2.6% improvement over predicted topology—redirects research attention toward better detectors rather than more complex topology heads. The identification of a confidence-gaming loophole in the OpenLane-V2 topology metric is a practical concern for the community, as it means reported SOTA numbers may overstate true reasoning ability. Open questions include whether the simple-MLP sufficiency holds beyond OpenLane-V2's scale, whether the proposed correctness-factor metric correction will be adopted, and whether the YOLOv8 dependency introduces brittleness in deployment settings where a separate detector is impractical.
