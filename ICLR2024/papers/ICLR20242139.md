## Problem

When class-level distributions appear balanced but hidden subpopulations within classes are imbalanced, standard imbalanced-learning and spurious-correlation methods fail because they operate at the class level or require explicit subpopulation annotations. This paper tackles the under-explored problem of discovering and rebalancing such implicit subpopulations without any subpopulation labels, which is critical for fairness and generalization in sensitive applications like medical diagnosis and autonomous driving.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images with class labels (no subpopulation annotations required) and produces a class prediction via LogSumExp aggregation of K subpopulation-specific logits, alongside a learned soft subpopulation assignment matrix V ∈ R^{N×K}. The multi-head architecture splits the penultimate-layer features and final classifier weights into K equal groups, so no additional parameters are introduced relative to a standard ResNet-18 backbone. Evaluation uses a subpopulation-balanced test set where each latent subpopulation is equally represented.

## Method

SHE (Scatter and HarmonizE) introduces an optimal data partition defined as the mapping ν that maximizes the interaction information I(X;Y;ν(X,Y)), i.e., the gain in predictive ability from input to label after partitioning. An empirical risk (Eq. 1) is shown to be statistically consistent with this partition objective via Rademacher complexity bounds (Thm. 3.3), and a LogSumExp aggregation of per-subpopulation logits provably yields a subpopulation-balanced prediction (Thm. 3.4). In practice, the partition is represented by a soft weight matrix V optimized end-to-end, a diversity regularization term prevents subpopulation collapse, and a multi-head strategy splits existing network channels into K groups to generate per-subpopulation predictions without increasing model capacity. The full loss (Eq. 2) combines the weighted cross-entropy, an entropy term on the partition, and the diversity penalty, all trained jointly with standard SGD.

## Results

SHE achieves consistent top-1 accuracy improvements over all 14 baselines across five benchmark settings. On COCO, SHE reaches 64.56% (vs. best baseline BCL at 62.83%, a +1.72% gain); on CIFAR-100 with IR=100/50/20, it achieves 54.52%/56.87%/60.72% (gains of +1.50%/+1.35%/+1.53% over the best baselines); and on tieredImageNet it reaches 50.14% (+1.42% over PaCO). In the per-split analysis on COCO, SHE achieves 42.09% on the Few split, a 4.42% improvement over the best baseline (GRASP at 37.29%), while also matching the best on the Many split (67.71%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SHE outperforms all 14 baselines on every dataset and imbalance ratio tested. Among imbalanced-learning baselines, BCL (contrastive learning) is the strongest, reaching 62.83% on COCO and 53.02% on CIFAR-IR100, yet SHE still leads by 1.72% and 1.50% respectively; other imbalanced methods (PaCO, IFL, DB, TDE, ETF-DR) show only marginal gains over ERM (≤0.4%). Spurious-correlation baselines (LfF, Focal, EIIL, ARL, GRASP, JTT, MaskTune) generally underperform ERM or match it, with MaskTune being the worst at 60.23% on COCO and 51.63% on CIFAR-IR100, because their assumption that ERM relies on spurious features does not hold under pure subpopulation imbalance. In the combined class+subpopulation imbalance setting (Table 4), SHE combined with logit adjustment (SHE_w/LA) achieves 68.11% on COCO, 61.67% on CIFAR-IR100, and 55.73% on tieredImageNet, surpassing the best standalone baseline (BCL) by 1.19%, 1.80%, and 1.50% respectively. On spurious-correlation benchmarks (CelebA, Waterbirds) without group annotations, SHE_w/GDRO achieves 77.9%/91.7% and 81.9%/91.3% (Worst/Mean Acc), outperforming JTT (40.6%/88.0% and 71.8%/92.3%) and MaskTune (78.0%/91.3% and 80.7%/92.1%) on Worst Acc while remaining competitive on Mean Acc.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a distinct problem—implicit subpopulation imbalance—situated between class imbalance (which operates on explicit class frequencies) and spurious correlation (which assumes subpopulations are causally independent of labels). Conceptually, it repurposes information-theoretic tools (interaction information, mutual information) from the information bottleneck and InfoMax literature, but redirects them toward learning a data partition that maximizes predictive gain rather than optimizing representations. The multi-head architecture borrows from transformer attention, and the LogSumExp aggregation draws on ensemble diversity principles, but the combination with an information-theoretic partition objective and subpopulation-balanced prediction is novel. The closest methodological relatives are EIIL and ARL, which also infer subgroups without annotations, but SHE optimizes predictive ability (interaction information) rather than invariance or loss-space stability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap where class-balanced datasets still harbor hidden imbalanced subpopulations (e.g., medical imaging across demographics, autonomous driving in rare conditions), a setting where standard class-level rebalancing is blind. The theoretical guarantee that LogSumExp aggregation yields subpopulation-balanced predictions, combined with the fact that no extra parameters are introduced, makes the approach attractive for deployment. Open questions include whether the learned subpopulations remain meaningful at scale (larger K, more complex domains), how the method interacts with other distributional shifts simultaneously, and whether the fixed K=4 default generalizes across tasks. The ethics statement also raises the dual-use concern of subpopulation discovery technology.
