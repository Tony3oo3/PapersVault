## Problem

Semidefinite programming (LipSDP) provides tighter Lipschitz constant upper bounds for neural networks than naive spectral norm products, but standard interior-point SDP solvers require memory that grows super-linearly with network size, making them infeasible for networks with thousands of neurons. Even chordal-sparsity-based decompositions fail to scale to practical architectures on CIFAR-10 or ImageNet, leaving the spectral norm product as the only viable bound for large networks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the weight matrices and layer dimensions of a (convolutional) neural network, constructing a block-tridiagonal SDP constraint matrix whose size equals the total number of neurons across all layers. It outputs a scalar Lipschitz constant upper bound that is guaranteed to be no worse than the spectral norm product of the weight matrices, and the iterative procedure can be terminated at any step to return a valid (if not fully converged) bound. For vector-output networks, the final layer's output weight matrix V replaces the scalar output vector v in the constraint matrix.

## Method

The core transformation converts LipSDP into an eigenvalue optimization problem (EP-LipSDP) by introducing redundant quadratic constraints (Δz_i² ≤ ‖W_i‖_op²) that create an explicit trace bound, then applying the exact penalty method to move the semidefinite constraint into the objective as ρ·λ_max⁺(C) with penalty parameter ρ equal to the trace bound. This yields a nonsmooth problem with only box constraints, solvable by first-order subgradient methods (ADAM) within PyTorch. Three add-on techniques enable practical scaling: (1) Lanczos iteration to approximate the extreme eigenvalue without forming the full matrix, (2) native sparse matrix-vector multiplication exploiting the near-tridiagonal structure of the constraint, and (3) an analytical initialization derived via recursive Schur complement and block inversion that exactly recovers the spectral norm product bound, ensuring the optimization only improves from there. The approach scales to AlexNet on ImageNet for the first time, reducing the Lipschitz bound by 73% over the norm product.

## Results

On MNIST-DNN, LipDiff achieves a Lipschitz constant estimate of 4.90, closely matching LipSDP's 4.82 (the exact-eigenvalue variant LipDiff-Ex reaches 4.86). On MNIST-CNN and CIFAR10-CNN, where LipSDP triggers out-of-memory errors, LipDiff produces estimates of 14.76 and 14.82 respectively, reducing the naive spectral norm product bound by 40% and 58%. On ImageNet (AlexNet), combining scalar and vector-output LipDiff across sub-networks yields a 73% reduction in the Lipschitz constant estimate compared to the matrix norm product bound—the first successful scaling of SDP-based Lipschitz estimation to ImageNet-scale networks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the spectral norm product bound (the only prior method applicable to ImageNet-scale networks), LipDiff consistently achieves tighter Lipschitz estimates: 48% reduction on MNIST-CNN, 58% on CIFAR10-CNN, and 73% on AlexNet (ImageNet). Against LipSDP (solved via Mosek), LipDiff-Ex matches the SDP solution on MNIST-DNN (4.86 vs. 4.82), confirming theoretical equivalence (Theorem 1), but LipSDP fails with out-of-memory errors on both MNIST-CNN (4021×4021 SDP) and CIFAR10-CNN (22529×22529 SDP), where LipDiff runs successfully within 60 GB GPU memory. The analytical initialization is critical: LipDiff-Rand (random init) produces a Lipschitz estimate of 2201.66 on MNIST-CNN versus 14.76 for LipDiff, showing that without the specialized initialization the subgradient method fails to converge meaningfully. Sparse matrix multiplication saves ~90% running time at CIFAR10 scale, while the Lanczos eigenvalue approximation trades a small precision loss for significant speedup over exact eigenvalue computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LipSDP (Fazlyab et al., 2019), the standard SDP formulation for Lipschitz constant estimation, and departs from prior scalability efforts that exploited chordal sparsity (Xue et al., 2022; Newton & Papachristodoulou, 2021) by instead transforming the SDP into a nonsmooth eigenvalue optimization problem via exact penalty methods and redundant trace constraints. The eigenvalue reformulation is conceptually inspired by Dathathri et al. (2020), who applied a similar transformation to SDPs for local robustness verification, and by the spectral bundle method literature (Helmberg & Rendl, 2000; Ding & Grimmer, 2023; Liao et al., 2023). The key technical departure is the introduction of redundant quadratic constraints (Δz_i² ≤ ‖W_i‖²_op) to create an explicit trace bound, which enables an exact penalty parameter and makes the problem compatible with autodiff frameworks like PyTorch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that SDP-based Lipschitz constant estimation scales to ImageNet-scale networks (AlexNet), a milestone that was previously blocked by the memory requirements of interior-point SDP solvers. The practical impact is significant: by reformulating the problem as an eigenvalue optimization, the method becomes GPU-friendly and compatible with the modern ML toolchain, lowering the barrier for practitioners to use SDP-based analysis. Limitations include the requirement for slope-restricted activations, the fact that the ImageNet result is on a single architecture (AlexNet) with a modest 18.4% improvement on the feature extractor, and the reliance on first-order methods whose convergence speed is not theoretically guaranteed. Open questions include whether this analysis can inform the design of less conservative Lipschitz-bounded network architectures and whether the approach extends to non-slope-restricted activations.
