## Problem

Exact attention computation in transformers requires quadratic time and memory in sequence length, and prior theoretical results show that sub-quadratic approximation is impossible in the worst case without bounded entries or low stable rank. Existing subquadratic methods like KDEFormer require bounded-entry assumptions and rely on impractical kernel density estimation, while none support causal masking. This paper asks whether a practical near-linear time algorithm can achieve spectral approximation guarantees under only mild, fine-grained structural assumptions on the attention matrix.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three n×d matrices (Q, K, V) and a binary large-entry mask M^H (with nnz = n^{1+o(1)} nonzeros) that can be generated by sortLSH, a predefined pattern, or ExpanderSketch. It outputs a sampling matrix S ∈ R^{m×n} with m = n^{o(1)} rows and an approximate diagonal D̃, from which the attention output D̃^{-1}AS^T·SV approximates the exact D^{-1}AV with operator-norm error bounded by ε·‖D^{-1}A‖_op·‖V‖_op. For causal masking, the algorithm recursively partitions Q and K into halves until sequence length drops below a threshold (4,096 in experiments), combining unmasked and causal sub-problems at each level.

## Method

HyperAttention replaces KDE-based importance sampling with uniform column sampling to estimate the diagonal row-sum matrix D, which is the key simplification over KDEFormer. The algorithm first uses Hamming sorted LSH (sortLSH) to hash queries and keys into ordered buckets, concentrating large attention entries near the diagonal of a permuted matrix, and constructs a sparse block-diagonal mask M^H. For each row, the masked (large) entries are computed exactly, while the unmasked portion is estimated by averaging m uniform samples with a per-row cap C_i that controls variance; a lower cap τ/κ prevents underestimation. The matrix product D̃^{-1}AV is then approximated by sampling rows of V proportional to their squared ℓ₂-norms (standard AMM). The theoretical guarantee depends on two fine-grained parameters—α (max squared column norm of the softmax matrix) and κ (ratio of max to min unmasked row sums)—which can remain small even when entries or stable rank are large, and the overall runtime is O(d·n^{1+o(1)} + d·nnz(M^H)).

## Results

On ChatGLM3-6B-32K at 32k context length, replacing ~20 attention layers with HyperAttention increases perplexity from 5.6 to 6.3 while achieving a 50% speedup in attention computation. At 131k sequence length, HyperAttention delivers up to 54× speedup over FlashAttention without causal masking and 5.4× (22× on a single layer) with causal masking. On LongBench task-specific evaluation, replacing half the attention layers (14 of 28) degrades most tasks by less than 13%, with summarization remaining nearly unchanged (60.29 → 58.84) and code completion slightly improving (107.16 → 103.19). The empirical assumption α = n^{o(1)} is verified: for T2T-ViT (n=3136), α ≈ 8.18, and for ChatGLM3, α/n decreases monotonically as n grows from 1k to 9k.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole quantitative baseline is FlashAttention-2 (exact attention), used for both perplexity/speedup measurements and as the '0 layers' row in the LongBench evaluation. HyperAttention consistently trades a small quality loss for large speed gains: at 32k context, replacing 20 of 28 layers costs ~0.7 perplexity (5.6→6.3) for a 50% runtime reduction, while replacing all 28 layers costs ~6.4 perplexity for a 2.3× speedup. At 131k, the speedup reaches 54× (no mask) or 5.4× (causal mask) with no perplexity reported at that scale. On LongBench, the quality degradation is task-dependent: summarization and code completion are most robust (within ~2–4 points even at 28 layers), while single-document QA degrades most sharply (96.07→53.30 at full replacement). The paper also positions HyperAttention qualitatively against prior approximate methods (KDEFormer, PolySketchFormer, sparse/low-rank attention), noting that it is the first to combine near-linear time guarantees with causal masking support and practical GPU efficiency, though no head-to-head numerical comparison against those methods is provided.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on KDEFormer (Zandieh et al., 2023), which achieved subquadratic attention approximation via kernel density estimation under a bounded-entries assumption, and on the near-linear-time result of Alman & Song (2023) that used impractical polynomial sketching under the same assumption. The key departure is twofold: (1) it removes the bounded-entries and bounded-stable-rank requirements by introducing two fine-grained parameters (max column ℓ₂-norm in the normalized attention matrix and a row-norm ratio after heavy-entry removal), and (2) it replaces the KDE-based importance sampling with simple uniform sampling plus LSH-based large-entry detection (sortLSH), making the algorithm both theoretically near-linear and practically implementable. The matrix multiplication component leverages the standard row-norm sampling of Drineas & Kannan (2001), and the LSH machinery is inherited from Reformer (Kitaev et al., 2020) and KDEFormer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that HyperAttention is the first provably near-linear attention approximation that supports causal masking and does not require bounded attention entries, closing a gap between theory (which needed strong assumptions) and practice (which needed impractical KDE solvers). Empirically, it delivers a 50% inference speedup on ChatGLM3 at 32k context and up to 54× speedup on a single layer at 131k without causal masking, though the causal-masking speedup drops to ~5.4× and perplexity degrades modestly (5.6→6.3). Open questions include whether the column-norm and row-norm-ratio assumptions hold universally across architectures and training stages, whether the approach remains effective during fine-tuning (not just inference), and whether the recursive causal-masking overhead can be further reduced for very long sequences.
