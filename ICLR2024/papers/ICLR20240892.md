## Problem

While instruction tuning is the standard alignment step for LLMs, prior work has relied on heuristic automation (e.g., ChatGPT distillation) or manual curation to build training datasets, leaving it unclear what characteristics define 'good' instruction tuning data and how to select it automatically. This paper asks: what makes good data for alignment, and can we systematically curate a small, effective dataset that matches or exceeds models trained on 10× more data?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240892/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a large instruction-response data pool (e.g., 300K samples from X_sota combining ShareGPT, UltraChat, and WizardLM) and a data budget m (6K or 10K). Each sample is scored by two lightweight LLaMA-1 7B scorers (complexity and quality), and the target model's own hidden-state embeddings are used for diversity filtering. The output is a curated subset of m samples, which is then used for SFT (and optionally DPO) to produce an aligned LLM checkpoint.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-dimensional data measurement framework combining EVOL COMPLEXITY, EVOL QUALITY, and an embedding-based Repr Filter. For complexity and quality, the paper evolves a single seed sample into M+1 variants of increasing difficulty or response quality using ChatGPT prompts (adding constraints, deepening, concretizing, etc.), then asks ChatGPT to rank and score all variants in a single prompt to capture fine-grained differences; these scores train a small LLaMA-1 7B scorer that predicts complexity or quality for arbitrary samples. The selection strategy is 'score-first, diversity-aware': all samples are sorted by the combined evol score s = c × q, then iteratively selected using a cosine-distance threshold (τ = 0.9) computed with the target model's own sentence representations to discard redundant examples. This simple pipeline requires no human annotation of the full pool and achieves state-of-the-art alignment with only 6K SFT samples, outperforming models trained on 10–50× more data.

## Results

DEITA-Mistral-7B trained with only 6K SFT samples achieves 7.22 MT-Bench and 80.78% AlpacaEval, outperforming Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.1 (6.84 MT-Bench) and zephyr-beta-sft (5.32 MT-Bench) which use 200K+ samples. With 10K SFT samples, DEITA-Mistral-7B reaches 7.32 MT-Bench, the best among open-source SFT models at 7B/13B scale. After adding DPO (6K SFT + 10K DPO), DEITA-Mistral-7B achieves 7.55 MT-Bench and 90.06% AlpacaEval, comparable to zephyr-beta (7.34, 90.60%) which trains on 30x more data. On the Open LLM Leaderboard, DEITA-Mistral-7B_6K+DPO achieves 69.86 average, surpassing zephyr-beta (66.36).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEITA-LLaMA1-13B₆K (6.46 MT-Bench) outperforms WizardLM-13B (6.35, 70K samples) and Vicuna-13B-v1.3 (6.39, 125K samples) by 0.07–0.11 points while using 12–21x less data, and beats TAGLM-13B (6.09) and Random-Select (6.03) by 0.37–0.43 points at the same or smaller budget. DEITA-LLaMA2-13B₆K (6.65) matches LLaMA2-13B-Chat (6.65) which uses >100K SFT plus >1M RLHF data, and surpasses Vicuna-13B-v1.5 (6.57) and Tülu 2 13B (6.70) at 10K. DEITA-Mistral-7B₁₀K (7.32) exceeds Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.1 (6.84) by 0.48 points and zephyr-beta-sft (5.32) by 2.0 points using only 10K vs. 200K+ samples. With DPO, DEITA-Mistral-7B₆K+DPO (7.55 MT-Bench, 90.06% AlpacaEval) is comparable to zephyr-beta (7.34, 90.60%) which trains on 30x more data, and trails only the non-public Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 (7.60, 93.65%). On the Open LLM Leaderboard, DEITA-Mistral-7B₆K+DPO achieves 69.86 average, surpassing zephyr-beta (66.36) and all SFT-only baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Evol-Instruct technique from WizardLM (Xu et al., 2023), repurposing the evolution-based data augmentation not for generating training data but for creating graded complexity/quality examples to train lightweight scorers. It positions itself against a family of data selection methods for instruction tuning—LIMA (manual curation), Alpagasus (LLM direct scoring), TAGLM/Instag (tag-based complexity and diversity), and IFD (loss-based complexity)—arguing that prior approaches rely on heuristics or expensive per-sample LLM annotation without a principled multi-dimensional framework. The embedding-based Repr Filter for diversity draws on semantic deduplication ideas, while the overall score-first, diversity-aware selection strategy is a simple but novel combination of these components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10x data reduction for competitive alignment performance is practically significant, making high-quality instruction tuning accessible with far less compute and annotation cost. The controlled studies provide actionable guidance: perplexity as a complexity proxy actively hurts performance, quality matters most for lower-quality data pools, and diversity is essential regardless of pool quality. Open questions include whether the evolution-based scorers generalize to domains beyond the Alpaca seed, whether the approach transfers to multilingual or multimodal alignment, and whether the performance ceiling observed in data scaling (performance declining beyond ~3K samples) reflects a fundamental limit or an artifact of the specific data pool composition.
