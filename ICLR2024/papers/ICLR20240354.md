## Problem

Standard transformer blocks interweave attention and MLP sub-blocks with skip connections, normalisation layers, and value/projection parameters in precise arrangements, making them brittle to modification. The paper asks whether these components can be removed without sacrificing per-update training speed or downstream performance, a question that also helps bridge the gap between signal propagation theory (which typically analyses simplified architectures) and the complex blocks used in practice.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240354/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The simplified blocks consume standard text token sequences (sequence length 128 for CodeParrot and Pile experiments, 512 for Languini) and produce next-token logits (decoder-only GPT-style) or masked-token logits (encoder-only BERT-style), identical in format to standard transformers. The key difference is structural: the SAS block retains only query and key parameters in attention (halving attention FLOPs and parameters), and the SAS-P variant additionally computes attention and MLP in parallel, yielding 15–16% fewer total parameters and up to 16% faster wall-clock throughput at matched model width (d=768, 12 heads).

## Method

The paper progressively strips the Pre-LN block: (1) the attention skip is removed by replacing the attention matrix with Shaped Attention, A(X) ← αI + βA(X) − γC, which biases attention toward the identity at initialisation to prevent rank collapse; (2) value and projection matrices W^V, W^P are fixed to the identity, justified by a reparameterisation W = W_init + βΔW showing that downweighted residuals (small β) recover the training speed lost by removing skips, and that during training the residual-skip ratios β/α converge to zero for most layers; (3) the MLP skip is removed by switching to a parallel block layout where attention and MLP operate on the same input and are summed without a residual path, with the MLP branch explicitly downweighted (β_FF ≈ 0.1) to replicate the implicit downweighting of Pre-LN skips. The resulting SAS-P block contains no skip connections, no sequential sub-blocks, no value/projection parameters, and optionally no normalisation layers, yet matches Pre-LN per-update convergence and GLUE performance while using 16% fewer parameters and 16% higher throughput.

## Results

On the Crammed BERT setup (16-layer, 768-width, Pile pre-training + GLUE fine-tuning), SAS-P achieves a GLUE score of 78.3±0.4 with 101M parameters and 1.16× training throughput, while SAS achieves 78.4±0.8 with 101M parameters and 1.09× throughput, compared to the Pre-LN (Crammed) baseline of 78.9±0.7 with 120M parameters. On CodeParrot (18-layer GPT, 768-width), SAS and SAS-P match or slightly outperform the Pre-LN block in per-update convergence speed and wall-clock runtime. On the Languini autoregressive benchmark (12-layer, 512 sequence length), SAS reaches the same final perplexity as the Parallel block (22.37 vs 22.39) while using 15% fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Pre-LN (Crammed) baseline, SAS and SAS-P match GLUE performance within statistical significance (78.4±0.8 and 78.3±0.4 vs 78.9±0.7) while using 16% fewer parameters (101M vs 120M) and achieving 9–16% faster throughput. Compared to the Parallel block (Zhao et al., 2019), SAS-P achieves a comparable GLUE score (78.3 vs 78.5) but with 16% fewer parameters and higher throughput (1.16× vs 1.05×). Against V-SkipInit (He et al., 2023), the paper's methods clearly win on both training speed (1.09–1.16× vs 0.95×) and GLUE (78.3–78.4 vs 78.0), and the gap widens at greater depths (72 layers). On CodeParrot and Languini, SAS and SAS-P match or slightly outperform the standard Pre-LN block in per-update convergence speed, confirming that the simplifications do not sacrifice training dynamics. The paper notes that removing normalisation (SAS-P, no norm) yields the highest throughput (1.20×) but causes instabilities during fine-tuning, so the authors recommend keeping normalisation for downstream task performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on training transformers without skip connections initiated by He et al. (2023), who showed that Value-SkipInit and Shaped Attention (Noci et al., 2023) enable skipless training but at significantly reduced per-update convergence speed. The key departure is the empirical and theoretical observation that restricting or removing value and projection parameters (fixing them to identity) recovers the lost training speed, effectively replicating the implicit downweighting that Pre-LN skip connections provide. The paper further combines these simplifications with the parallel block architecture (Zhao et al., 2019; Wang & Komatsuzaki, 2021) to eliminate sequential sub-blocks, arriving at a block that removes skip connections, value/projection parameters, and sequential structure simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that value and projection matrices converge to identity during training and can be fixed to identity without performance loss challenges a core assumption in transformer design and offers a concrete 15% parameter reduction with 16% throughput gain. However, the results are validated only at 100–300M parameter scale, and normalization layers could not be removed without downstream task degradation, leaving open whether these simplifications hold at the multi-billion-parameter scale where parallel blocks reportedly improve further relative to Pre-LN. The paper also highlights a limitation of signal propagation theory: it motivates the modifications at initialization but cannot explain why identity value/projection parameters train faster than trainable ones, or the full role of normalization beyond initialization.
