## Problem

Fine-tuned LLMs become overconfident, especially on small datasets, making their predictions unreliable in safety-critical applications. While Bayesian inference can mitigate overconfidence, applying it to full LLMs is computationally intractable, and no efficient Bayesian method existed specifically for the small set of LoRA adapter parameters that parameter-efficient fine-tuning introduces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240074/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates post-hoc on a standard LoRA fine-tuned LLM checkpoint (e.g., LLaMA2-7B with rank-8 adapters on queries, values, and output layer), requiring no modification to the pre-training or fine-tuning pipeline. Given a text question in multiple-choice or True/False format, it produces a calibrated probability distribution over answer choices via Bayesian model averaging (Monte Carlo sampling over the linearized logit posterior with full covariance). The prior precision λ is optimized either via the closed-form Laplace model evidence on the training set alone (no validation set required) or via stochastic gradient descent on validation NLL.

## Method

Laplace-LoRA applies a post-hoc Laplace approximation with Kronecker-factored (KFAC) structure to the LoRA adapter parameters, treating the product BA as two separate linear layers. Because one Kronecker factor is d×d (d=4096 in LLaMA2-7B), the method uses a low-rank (rank n_kfac) representation of the large factor, computed incrementally via low-rank SVD without ever materializing the full matrix. Prediction uses the linearized Laplace model with the Woodbury identity to compute the logit covariance in O(n_lora·n_kfac) time, and the Bayesian model average is obtained by Monte Carlo sampling from the resulting Gaussian over logits. The prior precision is tuned via the Laplace marginal likelihood (requiring no validation set) or via gradient descent on validation NLL using the reparametrized model average. This reduces the Bayesian inference dimensionality from ~7B full weights to ~6M LoRA parameters, with only ~1-5% additional memory overhead over standard LoRA fine-tuning.

## Results

On LLaMA2-7B fine-tuned with LoRA on six common-sense reasoning tasks, full Laplace-LoRA (LA) achieves dramatic reductions in Expected Calibration Error (ECE) and Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL) while maintaining accuracy. For example, on Winogrande-small, LA reduces ECE from 31.2 (MAP) to 2.1 and NLL from 3.15 to 0.60; on ARC-Challenge, ECE drops from 31.0 to 7.4 and NLL from 3.28 to 0.88. LA achieves the best NLL across all six datasets and the best ECE on five of six, with accuracy within ~1% of standard fine-tuning. Under out-of-distribution evaluation (MMLU subjects), LA similarly reduces ECE by 50–60% relative to MAP (e.g., 34.2→15.8 on CS, 35.2→24.7 on Law).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Laplace-LoRA (LA) decisively outperforms all four baselines on ECE and NLL across all six in-distribution tasks. Monte-Carlo Dropout (Gal & Ghahramani, 2016) offers only marginal improvements over MAP (e.g., ECE 29.4 vs 31.2 on WG-S), while Checkpoint Ensembles (Chen et al., 2017) provide slightly larger but still modest gains (ECE 29.7 on WG-S). Deep Ensembles (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017) occasionally improve accuracy (e.g., 68.2 vs 66.3 on ARC-C) but their ECE/NLL reductions remain limited (ECE 24.7 on WG-S vs LA's 2.1). Temperature Scaling (Guo et al., 2017), evaluated in the validation-set setting (Table 2), is the most competitive baseline—achieving ECE of 12.8 on WG-S and NLL of 0.68—yet LA still edges it out with ECE 7.8 and NLL 0.66 on WG-S, and similar margins on other tasks. The gap between LA and all baselines is most dramatic on harder tasks (WG-S, ARC-C, WG-M) where MAP ECE exceeds 23, while LA keeps ECE below 8.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: parameter-efficient fine-tuning via LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) and post-hoc Laplace approximation for Bayesian neural networks (MacKay, 1992; Ritter et al., 2018; Daxberger et al., 2021a). Prior Bayesian language model work either targeted pre-training (Tran et al., 2019; Cinquin et al., 2021) or defined priors over attention weights (Fan et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2021), both of which are computationally prohibitive at LLM scale. The key departure here is restricting Bayesian inference to the small set of LoRA adapter parameters and using a low-rank Kronecker-factored approximation to the Fisher, making the method scalable to 7B+ models while remaining fully post-hoc (i.e., no changes to the standard fine-tuning pipeline).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Laplace-LoRA provides a drop-in calibration improvement for any existing LoRA fine-tuned checkpoint with only ~1% time and ~1–5% memory overhead, which is practically significant given that overconfidence in fine-tuned LLMs is a well-documented problem especially on small datasets. The method's post-hoc nature means it can be combined with other fine-tuning improvements (e.g., Mixout, KL regularization) without conflict. Open questions include whether the low-rank KFAC approximation remains accurate for much larger models (70B+), whether it helps in RLHF/instruction-tuning settings where the fine-tuning objective is non-standard, and whether the dramatic in-distribution ECE reductions (e.g., 31→2 on Winogrande-small) persist in more realistic multi-turn or open-ended generation settings.
