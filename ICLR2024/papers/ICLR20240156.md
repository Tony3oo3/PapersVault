## Problem

Existing safe offline RL methods enforce only soft constraints (expected cumulative cost below a threshold), which permits individual state-wise safety violations unacceptable in safety-critical applications. Enforcing hard zero-violation constraints is far more difficult in the offline setting because it requires simultaneously balancing safety satisfaction, reward maximization, and behavior regularization, leading to tightly coupled and unstable training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240156/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed offline dataset of (state, action, next_state, reward, cost) tuples collected from safety-critical environments (Safety-Gymnasium, Bullet-Safety-Gym, MetaDrive), with no online interaction. At deployment, given a current environment state, the diffusion-based policy samples N candidate actions and selects the one with the lowest feasible Q-value Q_h* as the final safe action, providing a safety filter at inference time.

## Method

FISOR decouples the original coupled safe offline RL problem into three independent supervised learning stages. First, it learns an offline approximation of the Hamilton-Jacobi reachability feasible value function via a reversed expectile regression on the feasible Bellman operator, identifying the largest feasible region S_f* without environment interaction. Second, it learns optimal reward advantages using IQL-style expectile regression. Third, it extracts the optimal policy—which has a closed-form weighted behavior cloning structure with distinct weighting schemes in feasible (reward-weighted, safety-masked) and infeasible (safety-risk-weighted) regions—by training a diffusion model with a simple weighted regression loss that augments the standard denoising objective with the feasibility-dependent weight function, thereby avoiding the need for a time-dependent classifier. This weighted regression is proven to be equivalent to exact energy-guided diffusion sampling, yielding the optimal policy distribution directly.

## Results

On the DSRL benchmark (26 tasks across Safety-Gymnasium, Bullet-Safety-Gym, and MetaDrive), FISOR is the only method that achieves normalized cost below 1 (safety) in all tasks, with average costs of 0.35 (SafetyGym), 0.10 (BulletGym), and 0.25 (MetaDrive). Among safe methods, FISOR achieves the highest average reward on SafetyGym (0.30 vs. TREBI's 0.28) and BulletGym (0.39 vs. TREBI's 0.56, though TREBI is unsafe on several individual tasks). FISOR also attains the best individual-task rewards in the majority of the 26 tasks while maintaining zero constraint violations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FISOR outperforms all six baselines on the primary safety criterion: it is the only method with normalized cost < 1 across all 26 tasks. TREBI (diffusion-based, classifier-guided) is the closest competitor on safety (average cost 1.36/2.45/3.18) but still violates constraints on several individual tasks (e.g., CarButton1 cost 3.75, CarPush2 cost 2.65). BCQ-Lag and CPQ, which use Lagrangian or conservative Q-learning approaches, suffer severe constraint violations (average costs 11.38 and 10.58 on SafetyGym) due to coupled training instability. COptiDICE achieves competitive rewards (0.63 on MetaDrive) but is unsafe (cost 10.26). CDT meets constraints on some tasks but remains unsafe on others (cost 4.31 on SafetyGym). Among methods that are safe on all tasks, FISOR achieves the highest average reward on SafetyGym (0.30 vs. TREBI 0.28) and BulletGym (0.39 vs. no other fully-safe method), confirming its advantage in simultaneously guaranteeing safety and maximizing return.

## Relation to Prior Work

FISOR builds on three distinct research threads: (1) safe offline RL methods (CPQ, COptiDICE, CDT, TREBI) that enforce soft constraints via Lagrangian or distribution-correction formulations; (2) Hamilton-Jacobi reachability from safe-control theory (Bansal et al., 2017; Fisac et al., 2019; Yu et al., 2022a), which had previously been applied only in online RL settings; and (3) diffusion-based policy representations with energy-guided sampling (Lu et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2023c). The key departure is transplanting HJ reachability into the offline setting via a reversed expectile regression to identify the largest feasible region, then decoupling the traditionally coupled safety-reward-regularization optimization into three independent supervised losses. The theoretical contribution showing that a weighted regression loss in diffusion training is exactly equivalent to energy-guided sampling (eliminating the need for a time-dependent classifier) extends the diffusion-policy literature in a clean way.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FISOR is the first safe offline RL method to enforce hard (state-wise zero-violation) constraints while maintaining competitive reward performance, addressing a critical gap for safety-critical applications where even probabilistic violations are unacceptable. The decoupled training pipeline (feasible region identification → advantage learning → weighted diffusion policy extraction) offers a practical recipe that avoids the instability plaguing Lagrangian-based approaches. A noted limitation is that limited offline data size can degrade performance, and the authors suggest extending to an offline-to-online framework as future work. The broader implication is that reachability-based safety certificates, long confined to online control, can now serve as a principled foundation for offline policy learning.
