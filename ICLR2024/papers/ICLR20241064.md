## Problem

Gradual Domain Adaptation (GDA) requires a sequence of intermediate domains to bridge source and target, but in practice these are often unavailable. Existing generative approaches (normalizing flows, optimal transport, linear interpolation) produce synthetic samples with ambiguous labels and suffer from training instability when combined with iterative pseudo-labeling. The paper asks: can intermediate domains be generated along a principled transport path that preserves label information, enabling stable fine-tuning rather than fragile self-training?

## Input / Output

The method operates in a pre-extracted feature space (semi-supervised UMAP embeddings) rather than raw pixels, taking labeled source features, unlabeled target features, and a pre-trained source classifier as input. It produces a sequence of T intermediate domain samples with their original labels preserved, and a gradually fine-tuned target classifier. Three auxiliary networks are trained beforehand: a denoising score matching network for the target score function, a rectified flow network for the transport vector field, and the classifier itself. The number of intermediate domains T is determined dynamically by monitoring the Wasserstein-2 distance to the target within mini-batches using a Sinkhorn solver.

## Method

GGF unifies three energy functions into a single Wasserstein gradient flow that transports source samples toward the target distribution while preserving labels. The distribution-based energy uses KL divergence simulated via Langevin Monte Carlo with a denoising score matching network to drive samples toward the target density. The classifier-based energy combines cross-entropy and logit-entropy losses, whose gradient with respect to inputs acts as a regularizer that prevents label switching and decision-boundary collapse during transport. The sample-based energy employs a rectified flow network trained via flow matching to provide a low-noise, straight-line transport direction that counteracts the diffusion noise inherent in Langevin sampling. At each intermediate domain (constructed after α iterations of the combined flow), the classifier is fine-tuned directly on the transported samples with their preserved labels, rather than using pseudo-labels. The total number of intermediate domains is set by halting the flow when the Wasserstein distance to the target reaches a local minimum, balancing feature shift against label preservation.

## Results

On the three GDA benchmarks, GGF achieves 86.16% on Portraits, 67.72% on MNIST 45°, and 54.21% on MNIST 60°, outperforming the best GDA baselines (CNF: 84.57%, 62.55%, 42.18%) by up to 11.9 points on the hardest shift. As a plug-in enhancement for UDA, GGF boosts RSDA from 70.9% to 73.1% average accuracy on Office-Home (+2.2%) and from 75.8% to 77.6% on VisDA-2017 (+1.8%). On Office-Home, CoVi+GGF reaches 73.6% average, surpassing the previous best standalone method CoVi (73.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GGF outperforms all GDA-specific baselines on Portraits (86.16% vs. CNF 84.57%, GOAT 83.17%, GST 81.45%) and MNIST 45° (67.72% vs. GST 66.45%, CNF 62.55%, GOAT 61.45%), but notably loses to GST on MNIST 60° (54.21% vs. 56.87%), where GST benefits from four real intermediate domains. On Office-Home, GGF as an add-on consistently improves existing UDA methods: RSDA+GGF (73.1%) surpasses standalone RSDA (70.9%), CST (73.0%), FixBi (72.7%), SENTRY (72.2%), and LAMDA (72.0%), while CoVi+GGF (73.6%) edges out CoVi (73.1%) and all other baselines. On VisDA-2017, RSDA+GGF (77.6%) exceeds RSDA (75.8%), MDD (74.6%), and CDAN (70.0%). The theoretical foundations (Otto 2001, Roberts & Tweedie 1996, Vincent 2011, Liu 2022/2023) are not direct baselines but underpin the three energy components of GGF.

## Relation to Prior Work

GGF extends the Gradual Domain Adaptation (GDA) paradigm introduced by Kumar et al. (2020) by addressing the critical gap of generating intermediate domains when they are unavailable, a problem that prior GDA methods (CNF, GOAT, GIFT) tackle via one-step interpolation or normalizing flows. The method's core mechanism is a unified Wasserstein gradient flow combining three energies: a KL-divergence-based distribution shift (simulated via Langevin Monte Carlo with denoising score matching), a classifier-based potential for label preservation, and a rectified-flow-based sample energy for noise reduction. This departs from prior GDA work in two key ways: it enables fine-tuning with preserved labels rather than self-training with pseudo-labels, and its theoretical analysis bounds target error via the gradient flow's transport cost and continuous discriminability rather than Wasserstein distances over joint distributions across given intermediate domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GGF provides a principled, theoretically grounded framework for the common real-world scenario where only source and target domains are available, eliminating the need for pre-existing intermediate domains. The three-energy design addresses two practical failure modes of prior synthetic-domain methods (label ambiguity and excessive diffusion noise), and the generalization bound offers actionable guidance for hyperparameter selection (α, T, η). However, the method requires training multiple auxiliary networks (score network, rectified flow) and operates in a pre-extracted feature space (UMAP), limiting its plug-and-play applicability; improvements on large-scale UDA benchmarks (Office-Home, VisDA) are modest (~0.5–2%), and the reliance on pre-trained features raises questions about end-to-end learnability in raw pixel space.
