## Problem

Knowledge distillation literature has largely assumed either full white-box access to teacher logits or pure black-box access, leaving a gap in understanding how much statistical efficiency is gained at intermediate levels of teacher information disclosure. This paper asks: what are the fundamental minimax limits of knowledge transfer when the teacher reveals only hard labels, only the probability of the sampled label (partial soft labels), or the full logit vector (soft labels), over finite input and label spaces?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241349/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The student receives n i.i.d. samples drawn from (ρ × π*)^n over finite alphabets S and A, where ρ is an arbitrary input distribution the student never observes directly. At the three progressive levels, the per-sample data format is (s,a), (s,a,π*(a|s)), or (s,a,π*(·|s)) respectively. The output is a student classifier π̂(·|s) ∈ Δ(A|S), and performance is measured by the ρ-weighted total variation TV(π̂, π*|ρ), which the student must minimize without knowing ρ.

## Method

The paper establishes matching minimax lower and upper bounds at each of three information levels using information-theoretic arguments: Assouad's lemma for the hard-label case (rate √(|S||A|/n)), a constructive Bayes-risk lower bound with a randomized prior over sign-flipped distributions for the partial soft-label case (rate |S||A|/n), and a missing-mass argument for the full soft-label case (rate |S|/n, free of |A|). The key technical insight is that at the partial soft-label level, the natural cross-entropy loss CE_ptl is provably asymptotically biased—its minimizer converges to π*²/‖π*‖² rather than π*—while a novel empirical squared error logit loss SEL_ptl that matches normalized logits in squared-error fashion achieves the optimal rate. At the full soft-label level, any KL divergence minimizer (equivalently, the cross-entropy minimizer CE_ful) is minimax optimal. Numerical simulations on adversarial instances designed to match the lower-bound constructions confirm the predicted convergence rates and the bias of CE_ptl.

## Results

The paper establishes three minimax rates for knowledge transfer over finite domains S and A with n samples: √(|S||A|/n) for hard labels (achieved by MLE), |S||A|/n for partial soft labels (achieved by the proposed SEL_ptl loss), and |S|/n for full soft labels (achieved by KL divergence minimization). A key negative result shows that the naive cross-entropy adaptation CE_ptl is asymptotically biased (Ω(1) error) under the partial soft label protocol, converging to [π*(·|s)]²/Σ[π*(a|s)]² rather than π*(·|s). Numerical simulations in the non-asymptotic regime confirm the predicted rates: log-log slopes of β̂ ≈ −0.5 for CE_sgl and β̂ ≈ −1 for both SEL_ptl and CE_ful.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four learners across three information levels. CE_sgl (MLE) is minimax-optimal at the hard-label level with rate √(|S||A|/n), but cannot exploit additional privileged information. CE_ptl (naive weighted cross-entropy) is the natural adaptation for partial soft labels but provably fails: it converges to a squared distribution [π*]²/Σ[π*]² rather than π*, yielding Ω(1) TV error with probability 1 (Theorem 4.3). The proposed SEL_ptl (empirical squared error logit loss) overcomes this bias and achieves the optimal |S||A|/n rate, outperforming CE_ptl by a factor of √n in convergence speed. CE_ful (KL divergence minimization) is optimal at the soft-label level, achieving the |A|-free rate |S|/n. In the classic regime experiments (Figure 1), CE_ptl's risk plateaus at a positive constant while all other learners converge to zero, and the performance ranking CE_sgl ≼ SEL_ptl ≼ CE_ful holds consistently.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes Paninski's (2008) construction of hard instances for discrete distribution learning to the multi-input setting, and adapts the squared error logit (SEL) loss from Ba & Caruana (2014) into an empirical variant (SEL_ptl) for a partial-observation regime. It deliberately departs from the dominant knowledge distillation literature (Hinton et al., 2015; Lopez-Paz et al., 2015) by treating the teacher as an arbitrary reference policy π* rather than a model trained on the same data, by requiring no architectural awareness, and by framing the problem purely as conditional density estimation over finite domains. The three-level information hierarchy (hard labels → partial soft labels → full soft labels) unifies settings that prior work addressed in isolation, and the minimax analysis follows the information-theoretic direction of the data processing inequality rather than the class-similarity, label-smoothing, or bias-variance arguments that have dominated KD explanations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that the most natural loss for the partial-soft-label setting (weighted cross-entropy CE_ptl) is provably asymptotically biased with probability 1, a negative result that challenges the default assumption that cross-entropy is universally appropriate for probabilistic matching. The matching minimax rates (√(|S||A|/n), |S||A|/n, |S|/n) provide a clean information-theoretic benchmark for any future knowledge transfer method. Key open questions include extending the analysis to function approximation for large S and A (where the equivalence of CE and SEL may break down), incorporating student pre-training into the convergence analysis, and handling mildly dependent samples beyond the i.i.d. assumption.
