## Problem

In PINN-based PDE solvers, random collocation points introduce Monte Carlo statistical errors that can dominate the approximation error, particularly for low-regularity solutions or high-dimensional problems. Existing adaptive sampling methods refine collocation points using error indicators but treat the solution and the training set as separate optimization targets. This paper asks whether the residual minimization and the optimal training set selection can be unified into a single adversarial objective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242135/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a PDE defined by a differential operator L, a source term s(x), boundary conditions g(x) on ∂Ω, and a computational domain Ω (typically [-1,1]^D). It produces two outputs: a neural network u_θ(x) approximating the PDE solution, and a learned probability density p_α(x) (a bounded KRnet normalizing flow) that generates adaptive collocation points. Unlike standard PINN where collocation points are fixed or refined by a separate heuristic, here the sampling distribution is co-optimized with the solution and evolves throughout training, concentrating mass where the residual is large while maintaining a smooth profile.

## Method

AAS formulates a minmax problem: min_θ max_{p_α} ∫Ω r²(x;θ) p_α(x) dx − β∫Ω |∇p_α|² dx, where the neural network u_θ minimizes the residual and the normalizing-flow PDF p_α (a bounded KRnet) maximizes it subject to an H₁ regularization that replaces the Lipschitz constraint from the Wasserstein dual. The adversarial structure mirrors WGAN: p_α acts as a critic that identifies where the residual-induced distribution deviates from uniformity (i.e., the Wasserstein distance), while u_θ minimizes the residual, jointly driving the residual profile toward uniformity and reducing Monte Carlo variance. Training alternates between j steps of gradient descent on θ (using samples from p_α) and j steps of gradient ascent on α (using importance sampling with a lagged PDF p_{α'}), then regenerates the collocation set. A key theoretical result (Theorem 1) proves the minmax optimal value is zero under a surjectivity assumption on the PDE operator, with the residual converging to zero in L² and the renormalized residual distribution converging to uniform in Wasserstein distance.

## Results

On the 1-peak Laplace problem, AAS achieves a mean square error of 2.97e-05, outperforming the best baseline DAS-R (1.93e-04) by ~6.5× and standard PINN (9.74e-04) by ~33×. On the 2-peak problem, AAS reaches 1.09e-04 MSE versus DAS-G's 1.51e-03 (~14× improvement) and PINN's 3.22e-02 (~296× improvement). On the 10-dimensional nonlinear PDE, AAS achieves 1.31e-03 MSE, beating DAS-G (9.55e-03) by ~7× and standard PINN (1.01) by ~771×. The residual variance consistently decreases during training, confirming the variance-reduction mechanism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AAS outperforms all three baseline families across all three test problems. Against standard PINN (Raissi et al., 2019), AAS reduces MSE by 1–3 orders of magnitude, demonstrating that the adversarial sampling mechanism is essential for problems with localized features or high dimensionality. Against RAR (Lu et al., 2021), which is only evaluated on the 10D problem, AAS achieves a ~750× error reduction (1.31e-03 vs. 9.83e-01). Against the closest competitor DAS (Tang et al., 2023), AAS consistently wins: on the 1-peak problem it beats DAS-R by 6.5× (2.97e-05 vs. 1.93e-04), on the 2-peak problem it beats DAS-G by 14× (1.09e-04 vs. 1.51e-03), and on the 10D nonlinear problem it beats DAS-G by 7.3× (1.31e-03 vs. 9.55e-03). The advantage is most pronounced on the 2-peak problem, where the adversarial mechanism more effectively concentrates samples on both peaks simultaneously compared to the non-adversarial DAS strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own DAS-PINNs framework (Tang et al., 2023) by replacing the sequential adaptive sampling procedure with a simultaneous minmax optimization, drawing a direct structural analogy to WGAN where the normalizing-flow PDF model acts as the critic. It departs from prior adaptive sampling methods (RAR, DAS) that treat training-set refinement as a separate outer loop, and from adversarial PDE approaches (Zang et al., 2020; Zeng et al., 2022) that use discriminators on the function space rather than the sampling distribution. The key conceptual departure is embedding the Wasserstein distance between the residual-induced distribution and the uniform distribution directly into the loss, giving the adaptive sampling a principled optimal-transport interpretation rather than a heuristic error-indicator-driven one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified minmax formulation provides the first theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) that the joint optimization of solution and sampling distribution can drive the objective to zero, with the residual profile converging to uniformity in Wasserstein distance. Practically, the ~6–10× error reductions over DAS and RAR across 2D and 10D benchmarks demonstrate that variance reduction through residual smoothing is a significant and previously underexploited source of accuracy. Open questions include whether the surjectivity assumption (A1) holds for general PDE operators, how the H1 regularization (a weaker proxy for the Lipschitz constraint) behaves in higher dimensions, and whether the adversarial training stability issues typical of GANs limit scalability to very large or multi-physics problems.
