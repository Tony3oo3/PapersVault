## Problem

Large-scale video-language pretraining has been hampered by the absence of a dataset that simultaneously offers high video-text semantic correlation, substantial temporal dynamics, and sufficient scale. Existing ASR-based datasets (HowTo100M, HD-VILA, YT-Temporal) suffer from weak video-text alignment, while WebVid10M, though better aligned, is limited to 10M pairs with relatively static scenes, making it inadequate for current data- and model-scaling studies.

## Input / Output

ViCLIP consumes video clips (720P, ~10 s average, sampled as 8–12 frames) paired with generated English captions (~17.6 words on average) and produces aligned video-text embeddings via a shared embedding space. For downstream use, the same embeddings support zero-shot action recognition (class labels from Kinetics/SthSthV2), bidirectional video-text retrieval (R@k on five benchmarks), and—when plugged into VideoChat—free-form text responses to video queries. The text-to-video generation branch takes a text prompt and outputs 16-frame video clips, while the interleaved InternVid-ICL subset (7.1M entries) feeds multi-turn video-centric dialogue pretraining.

## Method

The core contribution is a scalable multi-scale captioning pipeline that replaces ASR transcripts with visually grounded descriptions: at the fine scale, Tag2Text captions individual frames at low fps and a T5 language model summarizes them into a coherent video description; at the coarse scale, BLIP2 captions the central frame under a single-frame-bias assumption. These captions are paired with 234M clips (from 7.1M YouTube videos collected via ~6K action queries and segmented by PySceneDetect) to form InternVid. ViCLIP, a ViT-L video encoder with spatiotemporal attention plus a CLIP-initialized text encoder, is trained with InfoNCE contrastive loss and MAE-style random patch masking to reduce compute; the final 0.5 epochs use unmasked frames to bridge the pretraining–downstream gap. Ablations show that the combined coarse+fine captions outperform either alone, and that diversity sampling (DIV) and CLIPScore filtering (FLT) of the 10M subset surpass even the 200M unfiltered set in zero-shot recognition, revealing that false-negative clips from the same source video can degrade contrastive learning.

## Results

ViCLIP trained on InternVid-10M-FLT achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot action recognition on Kinetics, scoring 64.80/75.70 (top-1/AVG) on K400, 62.20/73.53 on K600, and 54.30/66.38 on K700, surpassing EVA-CLIP-E (69.80 AVG on K400) by over 5 points. In fine-tuned action recognition, ViCLIP with InternVid-200M+K710 reaches 88.7% top-1 on K400 and 74.2% on SthSthV2. For zero-shot video retrieval, ViCLIP+InternVid-10M-FLT attains 42.4 R@1 (T2V) and 41.3 R@1 (V2T) on MSR-VTT, outperforming CLIP4Clip+HowTo100M (32.0 T2V) by over 10 points. In text-to-video generation, adding InternVid-Aes-18M to WebVid10M reduces FVD from 705.25 to 616.51 and improves IS from 13.97 to 21.04 on UCF-101.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViCLIP+InternVid-10M-FLT decisively outperforms all image-based CLIP variants: it beats CLIP+CLIP400M by 6.4 points (top-1) and 5.6 points (AVG) on K400, and surpasses the much larger EVA-CLIP-E (trained on LAION-2B) by 5.9 AVG points on K400. Against the video-specific baseline CLIP4Clip+HowTo100M, ViCLIP+InternVid-10M-FLT gains 10.4 R@1 on MSR-VTT T2V (42.4 vs. 32.0) in zero-shot retrieval, demonstrating that caption-video correlation matters more than dataset scale. In fine-tuned retrieval, ViCLIP+InternVid-200M leads CLIP4Clip by 8.1 R@1 on MSR-VTT T2V (53.7 vs. 45.6). For text-to-video generation, the t2v baseline+InternVid-Aes-18M outperforms VideoCrafter2 (FVD 616.51 vs. 910.87) and VideoFusion3 (616.51 vs. 639.90) while also achieving higher IS (21.04 vs. 18.26/17.49). In the video dialogue setting, VideoChat-ViCLIP (avg 2.64) surpasses both VideoChat+Eva-g (2.29) and Video-ChatGPT (2.38), with the largest gains in correctness of information (+0.63) and temporal understanding (+0.42). The one area where ViCLIP does not lead is linear probing on K400, where TVTSv2-H (73.1) slightly edges out ViCLIP-L+InternVid-200M (71.7), though ViCLIP still far exceeds VideoMAEv2-H (25.8).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP paradigm (Radford et al., 2021) to video by replacing the image encoder with a ViT using spatiotemporal attention and adding MAE-style patch masking for efficiency, while retaining the InfoNCE contrastive objective. It departs from prior large-scale video-text datasets (HowTo100M, HD-VILA, YT-Temporal) that rely on ASR transcripts with weak video-text semantic alignment, instead generating captions via a multiscale pipeline (Tag2Text for frame-level descriptions, BLIP2 for coarse single-frame captions, T5 for summarization). The work also builds on the observation from WebVid10M (Bain et al., 2021) that video-text correlation matters more than raw quantity, but addresses WebVid's limited scale and low action diversity by sourcing 7M YouTube videos via action-based queries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

InternVid provides a much-needed high-quality, high-scale video-text resource that bridges the gap between ASR-based datasets (large but noisy) and alt-text datasets (clean but small), enabling video-text contrastive pretraining at a scale previously unavailable. The finding that 10M carefully filtered clips outperform 200M unfiltered clips in zero-shot recognition—attributed to false negatives from same-video clips—raises an open question about how to structure large-scale contrastive training to avoid intra-video redundancy. Limitations include YouTube-only sourcing (excluding surveillance, sports, movies), the observation that retrieval gains plateau beyond ~50M clips, and the fact that the simple contrastive-only objective (no matching head) may not fully exploit the data's potential.
