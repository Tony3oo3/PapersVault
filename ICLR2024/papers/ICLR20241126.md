## Problem

While first-order Taylor linearization of pre-trained ResNets has been shown to match non-linear fine-tuning performance, no computationally efficient method existed to linearize Transformer architectures at scale. The Jacobian-Vector Product required for linearized inference was prohibitively expensive for Transformers, preventing the practical exploitation of linearity benefits—model composition, deterministic unlearning, and convex-optimization-based differential privacy—for the dominant Transformer model family.

## Input / Output

The Tangent Transformer (T-ViT-L/16) consumes standard RGB images (16×16 patches) identically to the original ViT-L/16 and outputs class scores over the target dataset's label set. The key structural difference is that the modified forward pass simultaneously produces both the original network output and the JVP term at every layer, so the final prediction is the sum of the frozen pre-trained output and the linearized correction. Inference cost is at most 2× the original model, though caching intermediate activations during training reduces this to the original cost.

## Method

TAFT replaces a pre-trained ViT with its first-order Taylor approximation around the pre-trained weights, yielding a model linear in the new parameters Δw. The central technical contribution is deriving closed-form linearizations of attention (via directional derivatives through softmax), LayerNorm, fully-connected layers, and GeLU, all computable in a single modified forward pass where each layer propagates both the original activation and the incoming JVP—eliminating any backpropagation. Training minimizes a Rescaled Square Loss with L2 regularization on Δw, reducing optimization to a convex ridge-regression problem. Because the model is linear in Δw, composing N independently trained Tangent Transformers by averaging their Δw weights is exactly equivalent to output ensembling at O(1) inference cost, enabling parallel training, zero-cost shard subtraction for unlearning, and stronger DP-SGD utility guarantees from convexity.

## Results

On 7 downstream image classification datasets, TAFT (fine-tuning the last attention block) achieves an average accuracy of 84.3%, differing from non-linear fine-tuning (NLFT-1, 85.0%) by only 0.7%, while outperforming the classification-head-only baseline (FC, 81.6%) by 2.7%. For datasets close to ImageNet (Caltech-256, MIT-67, Oxford Pets, Stanford Dogs, CUB-200), TAFT actually exceeds NLFT by 1.2–6.8 points. In the parallel training/composition setting (Table 2), composing 50 T-ViTs trained on disjoint shards yields 75.7% average accuracy, outperforming Model Soup (62.2%) by 13.5% while maintaining O(1) inference cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Model Soup (Wortsman et al., 2022b), TAFT's linear weight composition outperforms non-linear weight averaging by 9.1%, 13.0%, and 13.5% on 10, 25, and 50 shards respectively (e.g., 81.3% vs 72.2% average on 10 shards), with the gap widening as sharding increases because Model Soup lacks a theoretical link between weight averaging and output ensembling. Against SISA (Bourtoule et al., 2021) for machine unlearning, TAFT's shard-subtraction approach is uniformly better across all datasets and all numbers of shards removed, winning by an average of 11.0%. Against Linearized ResNet-50 (Achille et al., 2021), TAFT on ViT-L/16 outperforms by 7.3% on standard fine-tuning (e.g., 95.9% vs 85.5% on Caltech-256) and by 9.0% on the 10-shard composition task, attributed to the stronger inductive priors of transformer architectures. In the differential privacy setting (Table 3), TAFT substantially outperforms NLFT under strong privacy constraints (ε=1): 46.3% vs 11.0% on CUB-200 and 4.3% vs 1.4% on Stanford Cars for full-block fine-tuning, confirming the advantage of convex optimization under DP-SGD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the tangent model / linearized fine-tuning framework from convolutional networks (Achille et al., 2021; Liu & Soatto, 2023) to Transformer architectures, which is the first computationally efficient linearization of ViTs. The key technical contribution is deriving closed-form Jacobian-Vector Product expressions for attention, LayerNorm, and GeLU layers, enabling a single modified forward pass to compute the linearized output at the same asymptotic cost as the original model. Conceptually, it departs from prior linearization work (limited to ResNets) by showing that the same RSL loss and ridge-regularized training recipe transfers to Transformers, and it positions TAFT as a linear alternative to prompt-tuning, LoRA, and adapter methods that lack weight-space linearity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: TAFT enables O(1)-cost model composition (replacing O(N) ensembling), zero-cost deterministic unlearning via shard subtraction, and convex-optimization-based differential privacy with strong theoretical guarantees — all while matching non-linear fine-tuning accuracy on most downstream tasks. Key limitations include the ~2× inference overhead of the modified forward pass, degraded performance on tasks far from the pre-training distribution (Stanford Cars, FGVC-Aircrafts), and the known SISA-style trade-off where sharding hurts minority-class accuracy. Open questions include whether the approach scales to generative or language models, whether multi-block linearization can close the gap on distant tasks, and whether the composition guarantees hold under distribution shift.
