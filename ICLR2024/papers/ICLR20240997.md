## Problem

Training language models on copyrighted or restricted data creates significant legal risk, yet restricting training to only permissively licensed data (public domain, MIT/Apache/BSD, CC-BY) severely degrades performance due to the scarcity and narrow domain coverage of such data. This paper tackles the resulting risk-performance tradeoff by asking whether high-risk data can be leveraged at inference time without being baked into model parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240997/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, SILO takes a text prefix x and queries a nonparametric datastore (up to 1B tokens of high-risk text) to produce a next-token probability distribution P(y|x). For kNN-LM, the datastore stores per-token key-value pairs where keys are last-layer transformer embeddings of preceding context and values are the next token; the nonparametric distribution is computed via L2-distance-weighted counting over FAISS-retrieved neighbors, then interpolated with the parametric LM output using a fixed λ. For RIC-LM, the datastore stores 1024-token text blocks (512-token sliding window) retrieved via BM25 and prepended to the input. For downstream classification, the model produces a class label via zero-shot PMI scoring over label words.

## Method

SILO trains a 1.3B LLaMA-based decoder-only transformer on the newly curated Open License Corpus (OLC, 228B tokens spanning public domain, permissive software, and CC-BY data across 8 domains), then augments it at inference with a nonparametric datastore of higher-risk text. Two retrieval mechanisms are compared: kNN-LM, which interpolates the parametric next-token distribution with a nonparametric one computed from FAISS IndexIVFPQ nearest-neighbor search over token-level embeddings, and RIC-LM, which retrieves the most similar text block via BM25 and feeds it in-context to the parametric LM. The key architectural insight is that placing all copyrighted data exclusively in the datastore (never in parameters) provides free per-token attribution, guaranteed instance-level opt-out, and compliance with fair use and GDPR, while the nonparametric component compensates for the extreme domain generalization gap caused by OLC's skewed distribution (dominated by code and legal text). kNN-LM outperforms RIC-LM because the nonparametric encoder is more robust to domain shift than the parametric LM, and kNN-LM scales more rapidly with datastore size, closing 90% of the average perplexity gap with the Pythia baseline trained on unrestricted data.

## Results

On 8 out-of-domain language modeling domains, parametric-only SILO (PDSW) achieves an average perplexity of 19.0, while adding a kNN-LM datastore reduces this to 12.0, closing 91% of the gap with Pythia (11.3). kNN-LM outperforms Pythia on 4 of 8 domains (CC News: 8.0 vs 12.0; Enron Emails: 5.9 vs 6.9; MIMIC-III: 6.6 vs 13.1; Github: 2.4 vs 2.4), while RIC-LM only reduces the average gap by 27%. On zero-shot text classification across 10 datasets, kNN-LM raises SILO's average accuracy from 57.0% to 71.1%, matching Pythia's 70.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is Pythia-1.4B, a parametric LM of similar size trained on the Pile (mostly non-permissive data). With kNN-LM retrieval, SILO outperforms Pythia on 4 of 8 out-of-domain perplexity benchmarks (CC News by 4.0 ppl, Enron Emails by 1.0, MIMIC-III by 6.5, and ties on Github), but still lags on NIH ExPorter (15.0 vs 11.1), Wikipedia (14.5 vs 9.1), Books3 (17.4 vs 12.6), and Amazon (26.0 vs 23.0). The average gap shrinks from 7.7 ppl (parametric-only) to 0.7 ppl (kNN-LM), a 91% reduction. On zero-shot classification, kNN-LM SILO (71.1% avg) marginally beats Pythia (70.8%), winning on 7 of 10 tasks (e.g., AGN 79.5 vs 71.2, CR 83.1 vs 80.3) but losing on SST-2 (74.4 vs 79.7) and Rotten Tomatoes (67.9 vs 80.4). RIC-LM is consistently weaker than kNN-LM across all domains, reducing the average gap by only 27% versus 91%.

## Relation to Prior Work

SILO combines two well-studied retrieval mechanisms—kNN-LM (Khandelwal et al., 2020) and retrieval-in-context (Shi et al., 2023; Ram et al., 2023)—but repurposes them for a fundamentally different goal: legal risk management rather than pure performance. Prior work on copyright mitigation focused on filtering training data to permissive licenses, while prior nonparametric LM work studied performance gains without addressing attribution or opt-out. The paper's key conceptual departure is the architectural segregation of low-risk data into parameters and high-risk data into a mutable datastore, which yields inherent per-token attribution and guaranteed opt-out—capabilities that post-hoc unlearning or influence-function methods lack. The introduction of the Open License Corpus (228B tokens) and the 'extreme domain generalization' framing (arising from OLC's heavy skew toward code and legal text) are also novel contributions not present in prior retrieval-augmented LM literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SILO offers a concrete, implementable path for LM developers to reduce legal exposure while retaining competitive performance, which is timely given the wave of copyright lawsuits against major AI labs. The OLC dataset itself is a valuable resource for the community, and the finding that kNN-LM closes 90% of the performance gap with an unrestricted model (Pythia) suggests the risk-performance tradeoff is less severe than previously assumed. Key limitations temper the impact: kNN-LM inference is roughly 100× slower than a parametric-only model, the approach does not fully eliminate legal risk (data in the datastore still requires licensing), and the heavily skewed OLC distribution may exacerbate fairness issues from older public-domain texts. Open questions include scaling the datastore to trillions of tokens, improving runtime efficiency, and inferring licenses from web-crawled data at scale to build more diverse permissive corpora.
