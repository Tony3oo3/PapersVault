## Problem

Prior theoretical results on benign overfitting in neural network classification were restricted to linearly separable data distributions, and no rigorous proof of grokking (the delayed transition from catastrophic to benign overfitting) existed in any neural network setting. This paper asks whether both phenomena can provably co-occur in a genuinely non-linear, non-separable classification task, and provides the first such characterization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241695/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model is a two-layer ReLU network of width m operating on high-dimensional inputs x ∈ ℝ^p drawn from an XOR cluster distribution: positive-class points come from ½N(+μ₁,I)+½N(−μ₁,I) and negative-class points from ½N(+μ₂,I)+½N(−μ₂,I), where μ₁ ⊥ μ₂. A constant fraction η of training labels are randomly flipped. The second-layer weights a_j are sampled i.i.d. from Unif{±1/√m} and held fixed during training; only the first-layer weight matrix W ∈ ℝ^{p×m} is updated by gradient descent on logistic loss. The output is a binary prediction sgn(f(x;W)) ∈ {±1}, and the paper tracks both training accuracy (which reaches 100% after one step) and test accuracy (which transitions from ~50% to near-100%) as a function of the GD step index t.

## Method

The core contribution is a trajectory-based analysis of individual neuron feature learning under GD. After one step, the network output is shown (via the strong law of large numbers over the random initialization) to approximate a linear classifier sgn(⟨Σyᵢxᵢ,·⟩), which fits all training points due to the near-orthogonality of high-dimensional data (p ≥ Cn²‖μ‖²) but achieves only random test accuracy because linear classifiers cannot resolve the XOR structure. Over subsequent steps, the proof tracks how positive neurons (a_j > 0) progressively align their weights with ±μ₁ while negative neurons (a_j < 0) align with ±μ₂, using a notion of (ν,κ)-alignment that measures the net activation imbalance between opposing clusters. This feature alignment is what enables generalization: once positive neurons dominate the response to μ₁-cluster inputs and negative neurons dominate μ₂-cluster inputs, the network achieves near-zero test error while still interpolating the noisy training labels. The analysis requires the step size to be large relative to the initialization scale (ω_init·nm^{3/2}p ≤ α‖μ‖²) to trigger the one-step catastrophic overfitting, and an upper bound t ≤ √n to control the ratio of per-sample sigmoid losses so that flipped labels do not distort the feature learning dynamics.

## Results

Theorem 3.1 establishes that after a single GD step (t=1), the two-layer ReLU network achieves 100% training accuracy on noisily labeled XOR cluster data while test accuracy is bounded within [½(1−n^{−Ω(1)}), ½(1+n^{−Ω(1)})], i.e., indistinguishable from random guessing. After Cn^{0.01} steps, the network maintains 100% training accuracy while test error drops to exp(−Ω(n^{2.01})), guaranteeing near-zero test error for large n. This time separation between catastrophic overfitting (t=1) and benign overfitting (t ≥ Cn^{0.01}) constitutes the first rigorous proof of grokking in a neural network setting and the first theoretical guarantee of benign overfitting on a non-linearly separable distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without a traditional method-vs-baseline comparison table. The key implicit comparisons are: (1) Against random guessing — at t=1 the network's test accuracy is provably within n^{−Ω(1)} of 50%, confirming catastrophic overfitting. (2) Against linear classifiers — the proof shows the network at t=1 approximates the linear classifier sgn(⟨Σyᵢxᵢ, ·⟩), which can interpolate near-orthogonal training data but is fundamentally incapable of exceeding 50% test accuracy on XOR cluster data due to the opposing cluster means within each class. (3) Against prior benign overfitting theory (Frei et al. 2022b; Xu & Gu 2023; Kou et al. 2023) — those results required linearly separable distributions and did not exhibit a time gap between overfitting and generalization; this paper is the first to prove benign overfitting in a genuinely non-linear setting. (4) Against Frei et al. (2022a), who studied the same XOR cluster distribution but showed that early stopping achieves generalization *without* overfitting; this paper demonstrates the opposite trajectory — the network first overfits catastrophically, then groks to benign overfitting while maintaining 100% training accuracy throughout.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the benign overfitting literature for two-layer ReLU networks (Frei et al., 2022b; Xu & Gu, 2023; Kou et al., 2023), which was previously restricted to linearly separable data distributions, to the fundamentally non-linear XOR cluster setting. It also builds on Frei et al. (2022a), who studied GD dynamics on the same XOR distribution but showed generalization via early-stopping without overfitting; here, the network instead catastrophically overfits first and then groks to benign overfitting. The trajectory-based analysis framework and near-orthogonality arguments are inherited from the linearly separable benign overfitting line of work, but the feature-learning dynamics are qualitatively different due to the non-separability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous proof of grokking in a neural network setting and the first theoretical guarantee of benign overfitting for a non-linearly separable distribution, filling a significant gap in the overfitting theory landscape. The finding that catastrophic overfitting and benign overfitting can coexist in a single GD trajectory (with a clear time separation) reveals a mechanism absent from all prior benign overfitting results. Key limitations include the requirement of an upper bound on training steps (t ≤ √n) and high-dimensional near-orthogonal data; understanding the long-time behavior and the role of dimensionality remain open. The result also shows grokking can occur without weight decay, highlighting the role of implicit regularization in GD.
