## Problem

Existing VAE-based structural inference methods require a global view of the entire graph, making them computationally infeasible beyond ~100 nodes, while correlation-based methods scale well but are restricted to one-dimensional features and cannot recover directed edge structures. This paper addresses both limitations simultaneously by combining a per-node dynamics encoder with partial correlation statistics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240846/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes M trajectories of n nodes spanning T time steps, where each node carries a d-dimensional feature vector (d can be 1 or 4, as in the Springs and NetSims simulations). The VDE processes single-node features independently at each time step rather than the full graph, which is what enables linear scaling in n. The output is an unsymmetrical n×n matrix of p-values (from Student's t-tests on partial correlations) that serves as the directed adjacency matrix, with smaller p-values indicating stronger evidence for a directed edge.

## Method

SIDEC is a two-stage pipeline. First, a Variational Dynamics Encoder (VDE) is trained per-node with a hybrid loss combining a reconstruction term (predicting v_i^{t+τ} from v_i^t) and an autocorrelation loss derived from the variational approach to conformational dynamics (VAC), which maximizes the leading dynamical eigenvalue of the latent representation. The VDE follows the Information Bottleneck principle, encoding minimal sufficient statistics of node dynamics into a 1D latent variable μ_i^t, thereby reducing multi-dimensional features to a scalar suitable for correlation analysis. Second, partial correlation coefficients are computed from the collected latent variables across all nodes and time steps using the inverse covariance matrix formulation (r_{ij|S} = -d_{ij}/√(d_{ii}d_{jj})). To recover directed edges, the method reorganizes latent variables temporally: for each target node i, its current-time encoding is paired with all other nodes' previous-time encodings, breaking the inherent symmetry of partial correlations and yielding an unsymmetrical p-value matrix that estimates the directed adjacency structure.

## Results

On the StructInfer VN_SP benchmark (4D features), SIDEC achieves 97.6% AUROC at 15 nodes and maintains 95.0–95.7% AUROC up to 250 nodes, while all five VAE-based baselines (NRI, MPM, ACD, iSIDG, RCSI) hit out-of-memory errors at 150+ nodes. On VN_NS (1D features), SIDEC sustains 96.1–98.7% AUROC across all graph sizes up to 250 nodes, whereas baselines fail at 200+ nodes. On six synthetic biological networks (BoolODE), SIDEC achieves the highest AUROC on all six (89.0–92.3% on linear/cycle networks; 68.5–78.5% on bifurcating/trifurcating networks), outperforming the closest competitor RCSI by 1.5–4.8 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIDEC outperforms all five baselines (NRI, MPM, ACD, iSIDG, RCSI) on every dataset where all methods can execute, and is the only method that scales to 250 nodes without OOM. On VN_SP (4D features), SIDEC leads by 0.6–1.6 points at 15 nodes (97.6% vs. RCSI's 97.0%) and by 5–11 points at 100 nodes (95.7% vs. RCSI's 90.7%), with the gap widening as graph size increases. On VN_NS (1D features), the advantage is more dramatic: SIDEC achieves 98.5% at 50 nodes versus RCSI's 74.1% (a 24.4-point gap), and remains the sole method functional at 200–250 nodes. On the six BoolODE biological networks, SIDEC wins on all six, with the largest margins on BF-CV (78.5% vs. RCSI's 73.7%) and TF (68.5% vs. RCSI's 65.0%). The key differentiator is scalability: all VAE-based baselines require processing the full n×n graph in latent space, causing OOM at 150+ nodes, whereas SIDEC's per-node VDE encoding combined with PCOR computation scales linearly with node count.

## Relation to Prior Work

SIDEC departs from the dominant VAE-based structural inference paradigm (NRI, MPM, ACD, iSIDG, RCSI), where the VAE's latent space directly encodes edge representations and the adjacency matrix is reconstructed within it. Instead, it repurposes the Variational Dynamics Encoder (Hernandez et al., 2018) to encode per-node feature dynamics into latent variables, then shifts structural reconstruction to partial correlation coefficients (Whittaker, 1990) computed over those latents. This reframes the VAE's role from a structural learner to a dynamics encoder and dimensionality reducer, while the correlation machinery handles the actual graph recovery. The temporal reordering of latent variables to infer directed edges is a novel extension not present in either the VAE-based or correlation-based lineages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a concrete scalability bottleneck: VAE-based structural inference methods become infeasible beyond ~100–150 nodes due to full-graph latent processing, while SIDEC's per-node encoding scales to 250+ nodes without memory blowup. The combination of VDE and PCOR also unifies handling of 1D and multi-dimensional features and directed graphs under a single framework, removing the uniform edge prior that constrains VAE-based methods. Key limitations include validation exclusively on synthetic data (StructInfer, BoolODE), the empirical choice of τ=1 without a principled selection criterion, and the reliance on complete node observability. Open questions include applicability to real-world noisy biological data, handling of unobservable nodes, and whether the temporal reordering generalizes to non-Markovian or multi-scale dynamics.
