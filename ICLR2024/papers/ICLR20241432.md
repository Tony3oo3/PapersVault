## Problem

GAN-based inpainting methods struggle with large missing regions due to their one-shot generation paradigm, while iterative probabilistic models (autoregressive, denoising diffusion) achieve better quality but require thousands of iterations and massive compute. This paper addresses the gap by seeking a method that combines GANs' optimization efficiency with probabilistic models' tractable uncertainty estimation for high-quality, low-cost large-hole inpainting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241432/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 7-channel input at 512×512 resolution: 3 RGB channels, the initial binary mask, the updated mask from the previous iteration, a normalized uncertainty map, and a time-step encoding. At each of several iterations (2 during training, 4 during testing), it outputs a 6-channel tensor (3-channel mean prediction and 3-channel log-variance), an updated binary mask, and a refined uncertainty map. The final iteration uses the deterministic mean as the inpainted output, while intermediate iterations apply continuous Gaussian sampling to maintain diversity.

## Method

The Pixel Spread Model (PSM) iteratively predicts all pixels in parallel and retains only those with the lowest uncertainty for subsequent iterations, gradually propagating reliable content from known to unknown regions. The core novelty is decoupled probabilistic modeling: the mean term is optimized implicitly via adversarial and perceptual losses (enabling accurate predictions in few steps), while the variance term is optimized explicitly via Gaussian negative log-likelihood with a stop-gradient on the mean, yielding a natural uncertainty measure without the high inference cost of full diffusion. The architecture is a U-Net with a StyleGAN2 decoder and uncertainty-guided attention blocks at 32×32 and 16×16 resolutions, where a learnable function of the uncertainty map is added to attention scores to bias information flow toward more informative pixels. The pixel spread scheme follows a predict–pick–sample loop: the network estimates mean and variance, a mask schedule selects the lowest-uncertainty missing pixels to promote to known status, and continuous sampling (μ + α·σ·z) fills the newly promoted regions. With only 74M parameters and ~250 ms inference per 512×512 image, PSM outperforms LDM by 1.1 FID on Places2 large masks while being 10× faster and using 20% of the parameters.

## Results

On Places2 (512×512), PSM achieves FID 0.72 (small mask) and 1.68 (large mask), outperforming all baselines including LDM (FID 2.76 large mask) and MAT (FID 2.90 large mask). On CelebA-HQ (512×512), PSM reaches FID 2.34 (small mask) and 4.05 (large mask), again leading all compared methods. PSM uses only 74M parameters (20% of LDM's 387M) and runs ~10× faster (250 ms vs. ~3 s per image), while being trained on only 20M images versus 50M+ for prior methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PSM outperforms all 14 compared baselines on every metric across both datasets and both mask settings. Against the strongest diffusion baseline LDM (387M params), PSM (74M params) improves FID by 1.08 on Places2 large mask (1.68 vs. 2.76) and by 1.81 on CelebA-HQ large mask (4.05 vs. 8.15 for LaMa; LDM not reported on CelebA-HQ), while also achieving substantially higher P-IDS (25.33 vs. 12.11) and U-IDS (39.30 vs. 33.02) on Places2 large mask. Versus the best GAN baseline MAT (62M params), PSM gains 1.22 FID on Places2 large mask (1.68 vs. 2.90) and 0.81 FID on CelebA-HQ large mask (4.05 vs. 4.86). PSM also surpasses Stable Diffusion (860M params) by 0.43 FID on Places2 large mask (1.68 vs. 2.11) and 12.69 P-IDS points (25.33 vs. 12.69 on small mask). Inference speed is ~10× faster than LDM (250 ms vs. ~3 s) and ~1000× faster than RePaint (250 ms vs. 250 s), making PSM the most efficient method among top performers.

## Relation to Prior Work

PSM carves new ground by fusing two previously separate paradigms for iterative image generation: GAN-based implicit distribution modeling (for the mean/prediction term) and explicit Gaussian probabilistic modeling (for the variance/uncertainty term). Conceptually, it extends the progressive inpainting line (Zhang et al. 2018a, Guo et al. 2019, Li et al. 2020) that selects high-confidence pixels for iterative refinement, but replaces their ad-hoc confidence mechanisms or separate accuracy-assessment networks with a single decoupled probabilistic output. The architecture itself (U-Net encoder + StyleGAN decoder + multi-scale attention) is assembled from established components, while the uncertainty-guided attention is a targeted modification of standard self-attention to prevent missing-pixel self-attention from degrading valid regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 74M-parameter model that outperforms 387M LDM and 860M Stable Diffusion on large-hole inpainting while running 10× faster (250 ms vs. ~3 s) offers a compelling efficiency-quality trade-off for production inpainting pipelines. The striking generalization to unseen mask types (FID 5.27 vs. 47.64 for LaMa, 95.65 for Stable Diffusion) suggests the iterative uncertainty-selection mechanism provides robustness that one-shot or diffusion-based approaches lack. Open questions include whether the method can incorporate high-level semantic priors to handle cases where only minimal visual hints remain, and whether the modest diversity (slightly lower recall than LDM) can be improved without sacrificing the fidelity gains that the decoupled design provides.
