## Problem

Autonomous driving perception models suffer severe performance degradation when transferred across domains (e.g., different LiDAR sensors or cities), and existing solutions either require expensive target-domain data collection and annotation (UDA) or rely on generic simulation engines that produce unrealistic scene backgrounds. This paper tackles the zero-shot setting where no real target-domain data is available at all, asking whether source-domain reconstruction followed by target-domain simulation can bridge the gap without any target-domain acquisition cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242259/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes multi-frame LiDAR point clouds from the source domain (Waymo: 64-beam top LiDAR with vFoV [-17.6°, 2.4°] plus four side LiDARs with vFoV [-90°, 30°]) along with their bounding-box annotations, plus only the target domain's LiDAR sensor specifications (beam count, vertical FoV, rotation frequency, drop-rate). It produces scene-level 3D meshes via implicit SDF reconstruction, then renders approximately 26K–29K simulated point-cloud samples per target domain (KITTI, nuScenes, ONCE) that match the target sensor's scan geometry. The final output is 3D object detection (AP_BEV, AP_3D at IoU 0.7) evaluated on real target-domain validation sets.

## Method

ReSimAD is a three-stage reconstruction-simulation-perception pipeline. First, a LiDAR-only implicit neural reconstruction (LINR) trains a multi-layer network to encode a Signed Distance Field from sparse point clouds, using volume rendering with NeuS's close-range model and a log-L1 depth loss; multiple frames per sequence are consolidated after removing dynamic objects and registering via LiDAR poses, with side-LiDAR points up-weighted to compensate for the top LiDAR's narrow vertical FoV. Second, the extracted meshes are loaded into CARLA as static background, traffic participants are matched by size via statistical function mapping to the target domain's vehicle distribution, and PCSim reproduces the target LiDAR's exact scan pattern (channels, vFoV, rotation, drop-rate) via ray casting. Third, a standard 3D detector (PV-RCNN/PV-RCNN++) is trained solely on the simulated points and evaluated zero-shot on real target-domain data. The key insight is that the 3D mesh serves as a domain-invariant geometric representation that decouples scene structure from sensor-specific point distributions, enabling cost-free re-simulation under arbitrary target sensor configurations.

## Results

On the Waymo→KITTI cross-domain 3D detection task, ReSimAD achieves 81.01/58.42 AP_BEV/AP_3D with PV-RCNN and 82.06/61.65 with PV-RCNN++ under a zero-shot setting (no target-domain data), closing 71–107% of the gap to the fully-supervised Oracle. On Waymo→nuScenes, ReSimAD reaches 37.85/21.33 (PV-RCNN) and 40.73/23.72 (PV-RCNN++), and on Waymo→ONCE it achieves 70.97/48.59 and 74.52/53.91 respectively. The simulated data also boosts fully-supervised fine-tuning (SFT) to 88.30/82.71 on KITTI, matching or exceeding the Oracle baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ST3D (UDA, which has access to real unlabeled target-domain data), ReSimAD (zero-shot, no target data) achieves comparable or superior results: on PV-RCNN++ Waymo→KITTI, ReSimAD (82.06/61.65) dramatically outperforms ST3D (62.55/17.53) by +19.51/+44.12 AP; on Waymo→nuScenes and Waymo→ONCE, ReSimAD also leads on both backbones. The sole exception is PV-RCNN on Waymo→KITTI where ST3D (84.10/64.78) slightly edges ReSimAD (81.01/58.42). Against CARLA-default (generic simulation without sensor matching), ReSimAD consistently wins by large margins (e.g., +22.32/+24.21 AP_BEV/AP_3D on PV-RCNN Waymo→KITTI). Against Sensor-like (sensor-parameter-matched simulation on CARLA's default background), ReSimAD outperforms by 5.98%–27.49% across all settings, demonstrating that realistic reconstructed scene geometry is critical beyond just matching LiDAR beam parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines LiDAR-based implicit neural reconstruction (building on StreetSurf, NeuS, and DeepSDF) with sensor-level LiDAR simulation (via PCSim) to create a reconstruction-simulation-perception pipeline for cross-domain 3D detection. It departs from the UDA line of work (ST3D, LiDAR-Distillation, Bi3D) that requires unlabeled target-domain data, and from direct CARLA simulation that generates synthetic scenes without grounding in real-world geometry. The key conceptual departure is using source-domain reconstruction as an intermediate domain-invariant representation (3D meshes) that decouples scene geometry from sensor-specific characteristics, then re-injecting target-domain sensor parameters during simulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pipeline offers a practical zero-shot pathway for AD manufacturers to transfer perception models to new sensor configurations or unseen cities without any target-domain data acquisition or annotation, directly addressing a costly bottleneck in product iteration. The finding that simulated data from reconstructed real scenes also improves fully-supervised and pre-training results suggests broader utility beyond zero-shot transfer. Limitations include dependence on reconstruction quality (some sequences show poor CD scores), the requirement that source-domain vFoV covers target-domain vFoV, and the gap to Oracle performance that remains non-trivial. Open questions include generalization to more extreme domain shifts (e.g., different urban densities) and whether the reconstruction step can be made more robust to dynamic occlusion.
