## Problem

Image restoration is ill-posed, and existing diverse restoration methods attempt to sample from the posterior distribution of natural images given a degraded input. However, this posterior is often heavy-tailed along semantically interesting directions, so a small number of random samples (e.g., 5) will almost always be dominated by the most probable mode and fail to convey the true range of plausible solutions. The paper initiates the study of 'meaningfully diverse' restoration, asking how to present a small set of outputs that covers the perceptual range of possibilities rather than merely reflecting their likelihood.

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded RGB image (256×256) — either a bicubic-downsampled and optionally noisy version for super-resolution, or an image with a masked region for inpainting — along with N independent noise samples that seed N parallel diffusion trajectories. It produces a set of N (typically 5) restored images that are mutually diverse in semantic content while each remaining consistent with the degraded input. The guidance operates entirely at inference time on the diffusion model's clean-image predictions, requiring no retraining and incurring only negligible computational overhead per timestep.

## Method

The core contribution is an inference-time diversity guidance mechanism for diffusion-based restoration. At each diffusion timestep t, the N images in the batch are simultaneously denoised, and each clean-image prediction x̂_{0|t}^i is updated by adding η·(t/T)·∇d(x̂_{0|t}^i, x̂_{0|t}^{i,NN}), where x̂_{0|t}^{i,NN} is the nearest neighbor of x̂_{0|t}^i within the batch under a clamped squared-L2 pixel distance d. The t/T schedule reduces guidance strength as the process approaches completion, and the clamping bound D prevents pushing already-dissimilar samples further apart. This mechanism is model-agnostic and was applied to RePaint, DDRM, DDNM, and DPS. The paper also analyzes three post-processing sub-sampling strategies (K-means clustering, density-weighted Uniformization, and Farthest Point Strategy) for selecting N representatives from a large pool of posterior samples, with user studies confirming that FPS and the guidance-based generation significantly outperform random posterior sampling in perceived diversity while maintaining comparable image quality and coverage of likely solutions.

## Results

Across all four diffusion-based restoration methods (DDRM, DDNM, DPS, RePaint) and both tasks (super-resolution and inpainting), the proposed diversity guidance consistently increases LPIPS diversity by 15–60% while maintaining or slightly improving NIQE image quality scores. For example, on CelebAMask-HQ noisy 16× SR, DDRM's LPIPS diversity rises from 0.19 to 0.25 and NIQE improves from 8.47 to 7.85, while LR-PSNR drops only from 31.59 to 31.00 dB. User studies confirm that guided restorations are perceived as significantly more diverse than vanilla posterior sampling, with quality rated as at least comparable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares each diffusion-based restoration method (DDRM, DDNM, DPS, RePaint) in its vanilla posterior-sampling form against the same method augmented with the proposed diversity guidance. Guidance wins on LPIPS diversity in every configuration tested: DDRM gains +0.06 (noisy) and +0.06 (noiseless) on CelebAMask-HQ; DDNM gains +0.08 on noiseless CelebAMask-HQ; DPS gains +0.05 (noisy) and +0.03 (noiseless) on CelebAMask-HQ. Image quality (NIQE) is maintained or improved in nearly all cases, with the sole exception being DPS on PartImageNet noiseless SR where NIQE worsens from 15.27 to 20.48 (though it was already poor). Consistency with the degraded input (LR-PSNR) drops by at most ~1 dB in most settings, with a larger drop for DDNM noiseless CelebAMask-HQ (81.24→75.04 dB). The GAN-based inpainting method MAT achieves the lowest diversity (LPIPS Div. 0.03 on CelebAMask-HQ), confirming that all diffusion-based methods with guidance substantially outperform it in semantic diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in diverse image restoration, where methods (RePaint, DDRM, DDNM, DPS, and others) aim to sample from the posterior distribution P(X|Y) and rely on repeated sampling for diversity. It reframes the objective from likelihood-faithful sampling to perceptual-range coverage, drawing on the Farthest Point Strategy from progressive image sampling (Eldar et al., 1997) for post-processing and on gradient-based guidance mechanisms from diffusion model training (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021) for an inference-time diversity intervention. The heavy-tailed posterior analysis connects to prior observations of mode collapse in generative models, but is the first to systematically quantify this effect (via kurtosis) specifically for restoration posteriors and to propose remedies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a foundational assumption in the diverse restoration literature—that posterior sampling is the natural and sufficient strategy for presenting solution diversity to users—and provides both a diagnostic (kurtosis analysis) and a practical fix (inference-time guidance with negligible overhead) applicable to any diffusion-based restoration pipeline. The user studies strongly validate the approach, showing FPS-guided sets are perceived as significantly more diverse without sacrificing coverage of likely solutions. Open questions include attribute-specific diversity (e.g., varying only hair color while fixing identity), extension to non-diffusion restoration methods, and whether the guidance can be adapted for interactive or streaming settings where the user progressively explores the solution space.
