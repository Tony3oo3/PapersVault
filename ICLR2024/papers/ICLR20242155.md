## Problem

Mainstream image classifiers extract class-agnostic features and only incorporate class information at the final fully connected layer, making it impossible to directly explain where the model looks for a particular class. Post-hoc explanation methods (Grad-CAM, RISE) are low-resolution and misaligned with the model's actual decision process, while self-interpretable models (ProtoPNet, ProtoTree) require bespoke architectures and multi-stage training. The paper asks whether interpretability can be achieved using standard Transformer encoder-decoder architectures and standard end-to-end training.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image (e.g., ~800×1200 for CUB-200-2011) and produces a class label over C fine-grained categories. Additionally, it outputs C cross-attention maps (one per class-specific query), each with 8 heads at the feature-map resolution (e.g., 25×38 for CUB), revealing where each class searches for its distinctive patterns in the image. The feature map is D×H×W (256×H/32×W/32), and the cross-attention maps are superimposed on the input for visualization without any additional inference cost.

## Method

INTR builds on the DETR Transformer encoder-decoder, replacing its object proposal queries with C learnable class-specific query vectors (one per class) fed into the decoder. Each decoder layer's self-attention encodes context among candidate classes, while cross-attention allows each class query to retrieve class-specific features from the image feature map. Classification is performed by taking the inner product of each class-specific feature vector with a single shared class-agnostic vector w, and the argmax yields the predicted label. The key theoretical insight is that this shared-w design forces each class query to produce distinct cross-attention weights for correct classification, making the attention maps a faithful interpretation rather than a post-hoc approximation. Multi-head cross-attention (8 heads) further enables each head to specialize on a different visual attribute (e.g., tail pattern, breast color, beak shape), which is particularly valuable for fine-grained species discrimination.

## Results

INTR achieves comparable classification accuracy to a fine-tuned ResNet-50 across eight fine-grained datasets, with the notable exceptions of CUB-200-2011 (71.8% vs. 83.8%, a 12-point gap attributed to complex backgrounds) and iNaturalist-2021 Fish (81.1% vs. 71.9%, a 9.2-point advantage). On the Oxford Pet dataset, INTR slightly outperforms ResNet (90.4% vs. 89.5%). For interpretation quality, INTR substantially outperforms post-hoc methods on the CUB dataset: it achieves an insertion score of 0.786 and deletion score of 0.01, compared to Grad-CAM (0.61 / 0.31) and RISE (0.56 / 0.30). In a human study on CUB, INTR's attention heads were identified as capturing the correct attribute 74.7% of the time, versus 42.2% for ProtoPFormer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the ResNet-50 accuracy baseline, INTR is competitive on six of eight datasets (within ~3 points) but drops 12 points on CUB-200-2011 (71.8% vs. 83.8%), which the authors attribute to CUB's complex backgrounds and small objects rather than a failure in species recognition. On interpretation quality, INTR decisively outperforms both post-hoc methods: on the CUB dataset it achieves an insertion score of 0.786 and deletion score of 0.01, versus Grad-CAM (0.61 / 0.31) and RISE (0.56 / 0.30), indicating its cross-attention maps are far more faithful to the model's actual decision process. In qualitative comparisons (Figure 3), INTR localizes fine-grained attributes (e.g., beak patterns, eye markings) at full resolution without requiring pre-defined prototype patch sizes, an advantage over ProtoPNet and ProtoTree. In the quantitative human study (Appendix G), INTR's attention heads were recognized as capturing the correct species attribute 74.7% of the time, nearly double ProtoPFormer's 42.2%. Overall, INTR trades a modest accuracy cost on some datasets for substantially more faithful, higher-resolution, and attribute-level interpretability that no compared baseline matches.

## Relation to Prior Work

INTR repurposes the DETR encoder-decoder architecture by replacing object proposal queries with learnable class-specific queries, shifting class-specific information from the output (fully connected layer) to the decoder input. This departs from both post-hoc explanation methods (Grad-CAM, RISE), which are low-resolution and misaligned with model internals, and self-interpretable models (ProtoPNet, ProtoTree, ProtoPFormer, Concept Transformers), which require specialized architectures, multi-stage training, or human-annotated concepts. The key conceptual departure is the use of a single shared class-agnostic vector w (rather than per-class vectors) for the final logit computation, which the authors prove mathematically forces distinct cross-attention patterns across classes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

INTR offers a minimal, reproducible recipe for interpretable fine-grained classification that requires no auxiliary supervision, no multi-stage training, and no pre-defined patch sizes, making it accessible to practitioners who want built-in interpretability without architectural complexity. The demonstration that multi-head cross-attention naturally decomposes into semantically distinct 'attributes' (e.g., tail pattern vs. breast color) rather than just object parts, and that limiting queries to visually similar classes reveals nuances matching biologists' identifications, suggests potential for scientific discovery in taxonomy. However, the 12% accuracy drop on CUB relative to ResNet-50, the requirement that all C queries be processed jointly (limiting scalability to very large class spaces), and the qualitative-heavy evaluation (with limited quantitative validation via a 7-rater study on 20 images) leave open questions about robustness and generalizability beyond fine-grained settings.
