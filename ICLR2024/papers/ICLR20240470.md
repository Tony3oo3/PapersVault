## Problem

In hidden-action principal-agent problems, the principal must learn an optimal contract without knowing the agent's actions, costs, or outcome distributions. Prior work by Zhu et al. (2023) yields regret bounds that are nearly linear in the number of rounds T when the outcome space is large, and leaves open whether better guarantees are achievable when the agent's action space is small. This paper resolves that open problem by showing polynomial-in-instance-size sample complexity when the number of actions is constant.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives an additive approximation error ρ, a confidence parameter δ, and a payment bound B ≥ 1, and operates under the constraint that the principal only observes the realized outcome ω at each round (never the agent's action). It outputs a contract p ∈ [0,B]^m whose expected utility is within ρ of the best bounded contract with probability at least 1−δ. In the online variant, the same procedure is wrapped in an explore-then-commit scheme (Algorithm 5) that produces a Õ(T^{4/5}) cumulative regret guarantee, improving over the Õ(T^{1−1/(2m+1)}) bound of Zhu et al. (2023) for any m ≥ 3.

## Method

The Discover-and-Cover algorithm overcomes the fundamental challenge that the principal never observes the agent's action by introducing meta-actions—groups of actions whose outcome distributions are within 5εn in ℓ∞ norm. An Action-Oracle subroutine samples q outcomes under a candidate contract, builds an empirical distribution, and either maps it to an existing meta-action or triggers a discovery/merge event that restarts the covering procedure (at most 2n times total). The Try-Cover subroutine then progressively tightens upper and lower bound polytopes for each meta-action's best-response region by performing binary search (Find-HS) along line segments to locate approximate separating hyperplanes, requiring O(n²q(log(Bm/η) + C(m+n+1,m))) rounds per invocation. Finally, Find-Contract solves a linear program over each region's intersection with [0,B]^m using the empirical distributions, selects the best region, and returns a convex combination of the optimal contract with the principal's reward vector to convert approximate incentive compatibility into a valid contract with only a small utility loss.

## Results

The Discover-and-Cover algorithm (Theorem 2) learns a ρ-optimal contract in [0,B]^m with probability at least 1−δ using at most Õ(m^n · I · 1/ρ^4 · log(1/δ)) rounds, where I is a term polynomial in m, n, and B. In the online learning setting (Theorem 3), the explore-then-commit algorithm achieves cumulative regret R^T ≤ Õ(m^n · I · log(1/δ) · T^{4/5}) with probability at least 1−δ. Crucially, when the number of agent actions n is constant, the round complexity is polynomial in the outcome space size m, and the regret bound has a T^{4/5} dependence independent of m.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is with Zhu et al. (2023), who provide a regret bound of Õ(√m · T^{1−1/(2m+1)}) for general hidden-action principal-agent problems. For any m ≥ 3, the exponent 1−1/(2m+1) exceeds 4/5 (e.g., for m=3 it is 6/7 ≈ 0.857), meaning Zhu et al.'s bound degrades toward linear regret as m grows, while this paper's T^{4/5} bound remains fixed regardless of m. This paper thus strictly improves the T-dependency when the number of agent actions n is constant, solving an open problem explicitly stated by Zhu et al. (2023). The trade-off is that the m-dependence shifts from a √m prefactor to an m^n prefactor, which is acceptable when n is small (constant) but would be worse for large n. No other empirical or theoretical baselines are directly compared in the paper.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on learning optimal contracts in hidden-action principal-agent problems, building on the foundational results of Ho et al. (2014) and the general-case analysis of Zhu et al. (2023). The core algorithmic machinery borrows the polytope-covering and binary-search-for-separating-hyperplanes ideas from Peng et al. (2019) for repeated Stackelberg games, but requires substantial additional machinery to handle the hidden-action setting where the principal observes only stochastic outcomes rather than the agent's action. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of 'meta-actions' that group together agent actions inducing similar outcome distributions, enabling approximate best-response region identification despite the unobservability of the action itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper resolves an open problem posed by Zhu et al. (2023) by showing that polynomial sample complexity in the number of outcomes m is achievable when the agent's action space is constant, in contrast to the nearly-linear-in-T regret bounds that were previously the best known. The T^{4/5} regret bound (independent of m) versus the prior T^{1-1/(2m+1)} bound represents a meaningful improvement for settings with even a small number of outcomes. A key limitation is that the polynomial dependence on m^n means the algorithm becomes impractical as the number of actions grows, and the bounded-payment assumption (B ≥ 1) is necessary to circumvent an impossibility result (Theorem 1). Open questions include whether the meta-action approach can be extended to handle growing action spaces or to settings with multiple agent types.
