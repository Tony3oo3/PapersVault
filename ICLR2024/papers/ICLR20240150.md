## Problem

Existing hand-object interaction (HOI) denoising methods struggle with two intertwined challenges: (1) interaction noise is highly complex, spanning unnatural hand poses, spatial penetrations, and inconsistent hand-object temporal relations, and (2) denoising models fail to generalize to unseen noise patterns, new objects, and diverse interaction scenarios. Prior approaches like TOCH either cannot clean such complex noise or produce conceptually incorrect results when confronted with out-of-domain inputs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy hand trajectory represented as 21 keypoints per frame (K frames total) and an assumed-accurate object pose trajectory as input. It produces a denoised hand keypoint trajectory and fitted MANO hand mesh parameters (rotation, translation, shape, and pose). A key constraint is that object poses must be accurate; the method only refines the hand. The representation internally uses 'generalized contact points'—N_o object surface points within 5 mm of the hand trajectory—along with their 6D poses to canonicalize all hand-object relations.

## Method

GeneOH Diffusion introduces two key designs. First, the GeneOH representation parameterizes the HOI process through three contact-centric components—canonicalized hand trajectory, hand-object spatial relations (offsets and normals), and hand-object temporal relations (relative velocities decomposed into tangent/normal components with distance-weighted encoding)—all canonicalized via the 6D pose of generalized contact points to align diverse interactions. Second, the 'denoising via diffusion' strategy first adds Gaussian noise to the input to project it into a whitened noise space (the space of training data diffused at various scales), then applies a canonical score-function denoiser to project back to the clean data manifold; a hyperparameter t_diff controls the diffusion level to balance generalization against input fidelity. The full pipeline is a progressive three-stage denoiser (MotionDiff → SpatialDiff → TemporalDiff), where each stage uses a separate canonical denoiser on one GeneOH component, and a formal proof shows each stage's output manifold is nested within the previous one, preventing later stages from undermining earlier refinements.

## Results

GeneOH Diffusion achieves the best performance across all four test sets and all seven metrics. On GRAB, it reaches 9.28 mm MPJPE, 25.27% C-IoU, 1.23 cm³ IV, and 0.57 mm² HO Motion Consistency, substantially outperforming the best baseline (TOCH w/ Aug.) on every metric. The most dramatic gains appear in HO Motion Consistency, where GeneOH Diffusion achieves 0.57 mm² on GRAB and 0.52 mm² on GRAB (Beta) versus 20.37 and 13.09 mm² for TOCH, respectively. On the challenging HOI4D dataset with real-world noise, GeneOH Diffusion reduces IV to 1.99 cm³ and HO Motion Consistency to 9.81 mm², while TOCH actually degrades the input (IV 4.09 vs. input 2.26).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole prior baseline is TOCH (Zhou et al., ECCV 2022), evaluated in three variants: the original, TOCH with MixStyle domain generalization, and TOCH trained on augmented noise (GRAB + GRAB Beta). GeneOH Diffusion outperforms all three variants on every metric across all four test sets. The gap is most extreme in HO Motion Consistency: on GRAB, GeneOH Diffusion achieves 0.57 mm² versus 20.37 (TOCH), 21.29 (MixStyle), and 22.58 (Aug.), a roughly 35× improvement. On the HOI4D dataset with real-world sensing noise, TOCH and its variants actually worsen the input (IV increases from 2.26 to 4.09–4.31 cm³), while GeneOH Diffusion improves it to 1.99 cm³, demonstrating that the prior method cannot handle out-of-domain noise patterns. Similarly, on GRAB (Beta) with unseen Beta-distributed noise, TOCH degrades MPJPE from 17.65 to 24.10 mm, whereas GeneOH Diffusion reduces it to 9.09 mm. Even the augmented TOCH variant, which is the strongest baseline, is consistently outperformed by GeneOH Diffusion by 1–3 mm in MPJPE, 1–2% in C-IoU, and by an order of magnitude in HO Motion Consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the denoising diffusion probabilistic model framework (Ho et al., 2020) but repurposes it not for generation but as a domain-generalization mechanism: by diffusing arbitrary noisy input into a 'whitened noise space' before applying a canonical denoiser, it handles unseen noise patterns without retraining. It departs from prior HOI denoising methods (TOCH, ManipNet, STGAE) that learn pattern-specific noise-to-data mappings, which the authors show fail catastrophically on out-of-domain noise. The GeneOH representation introduces contact-centric canonicalization absent in prior work, and the three-stage progressive denoising (MotionDiff → SpatialDiff → TemporalDiff) with a formal non-undermining proof is a new structural contribution to the HOI refinement literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical bottleneck in hand tracking pipelines where object poses are reliable but hand trajectories are noisy, with clear applications in VR/AR, robotics, and motion retargeting. The main limitation is the assumption of accurate object poses, which restricts applicability to in-the-wild video where both hand and object tracking are uncertain; simultaneous refinement of both remains an open problem. The 'denoising via diffusion' strategy—using a forward diffusion step as a domain-alignment preprocessing before a learned denoiser—is a conceptually transferable idea that could apply to other inverse problems with distribution-shifted inputs.
