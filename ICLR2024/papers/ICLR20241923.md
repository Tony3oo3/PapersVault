## Problem

Building open-vocabulary vision-language models for satellite images requires large text-image pair datasets, but satellite imagery is generated by sensors with no accompanying text annotations, making existing remote-sensing VLMs orders of magnitude smaller (10k pairs vs. 400M for CLIP) and limited to pre-defined concepts. The paper asks whether a large-scale VLM for remote sensing can be trained without any textual annotations at all.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241923/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes 224×224 satellite image patches (NAIP at 1 m/pixel or Sentinel-2 at 10 m/pixel, downloaded as 448×448 for rotation/translation augmentation) and a free-form text query or natural-language question. It produces either an image-level embedding (ViT-B/16 or ViT-B/32) for classification and retrieval, or per-patch embeddings for pixel-level tasks. For segmentation, the highest-scoring patches are fed as point prompts to SAM; for VQA, the pixel-level model replaces GLIP inside the ViperGPT program-execution pipeline, yielding connected-component detections refined by SAM.

## Method

GRAFT's core insight is to use geotagged internet ground images (collected from Flickr) as a semantic intermediary: a ViT satellite encoder is trained with a contrastive loss to map satellite images into the frozen CLIP image-encoder space, so that the shared CLIP text encoder provides language understanding without any satellite-specific text. Because one satellite image covers a larger area than a single ground photo, the authors design a one-to-many contrastive loss (Eq. 2) that pulls a satellite embedding toward all of its co-located ground images while pushing it away from ground images of other satellite images in the batch. A second, pixel-level variant (Eq. 3) uses the geotag-derived pixel location of each ground image to supervise the corresponding ViT patch, enabling localization and segmentation from sparse point supervision. Training uses 10.2 M NAIP and 8.7 M Sentinel-2 ground-satellite pairs, with the satellite encoder initialized from CLIP weights and trained for 10 epochs with AdamW; the CLIP image and text encoders remain frozen throughout.

## Results

On EuroSAT zero-shot classification, GRAFT (ViT-B/16) achieves 63.76% top-1 accuracy, outperforming CLIP (53.59%) by ~10 points and supervised CLIP-RSICD (45.93%) by ~18 points. On BigEarthNet multi-label classification, GRAFT reaches 32.46 mAP versus 23.13 for CLIP and 27.68 for CLIP-RSICD. For text-to-image retrieval, GRAFT (ViT-B/16) achieves 81.56 mAP@100 on EuroSAT and 49.61 mAP@100 on BigEarthNet, surpassing the best one-shot baselines (CACo: 69.90 and 47.40 respectively). On the NAIP-resolution SAT-6 benchmark, GRAFT (ViT-B/32) reaches 72.42% accuracy, a 30-point gain over CLIP (30.39%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRAFT (ViT-B/16) outperforms all eight baselines on nearly every metric in Table 1. Against the natural zero-shot baseline CLIP, GRAFT gains ~10 points on EuroSAT accuracy (63.76 vs. 53.59) and ~17 points on EuroSAT retrieval mAP@100 (81.56 vs. 63.99). Against supervised VLMs CLIP-RSICD and RemoteCLIP, GRAFT wins by 17.8 and 25.2 points on EuroSAT accuracy respectively, demonstrating that unsupervised alignment with 10M+ ground-satellite pairs far exceeds fine-tuning on small annotated datasets. GRAFT also surpasses the strongest one-shot representation-learning baselines (CACo, SeCo) on EuroSAT retrieval by 11.7 and 12.8 mAP@100, and on BigEarthNet retrieval by 2.2 and 9.1 mAP@100. On the NAIP-resolution SAT-6 benchmark, GRAFT (ViT-B/32) achieves 72.42% accuracy versus 40.57% for CLIP-RSICD and 30.39% for CLIP. For zero-shot segmentation (Table 3), GRAFT nearly doubles CLIPSeg's per-class accuracy on NAIP (49.38 vs. 26.99) and improves Sentinel-2 by 59% relative (31.95 vs. 20.02). For VQA with ViperGPT (Table 4), replacing GLIP with GRAFT's pixel-level model yields a 6.4-point average accuracy gain (44.05 vs. 37.69), with the largest improvement on Count questions (44.38 vs. 23.57).

## Relation to Prior Work

GRAFT builds on the CLIP paradigm of contrastive image-text alignment but repurposes it for a domain where text annotations are essentially unavailable. It draws on ground-to-aerial geolocalization work (Lin et al., 2015; Berton et al., 2022) that established the feasibility of linking satellite and ground imagery via geotags, but redirects this pairing from a localization objective to a language-alignment objective. Unlike prior remote sensing VLMs (CLIP-RSICD, RemoteCLIP, RS5M) that require curated text captions, and unlike self-supervised remote sensing encoders (SeCo, CACo, SatMAE) that lack language grounding entirely, GRAFT uses co-located internet ground images as a semantic bridge to the CLIP text space, eliminating the need for any satellite-specific text annotation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a large-scale, zero-shot, open-vocabulary VLM for satellite imagery can be trained without a single text annotation, and it outperforms supervised alternatives by substantial margins (up to 20% on classification, 80% on segmentation). The approach is generalizable to multi-spectral inputs and other sensor modalities, and the 10M+ pair datasets it releases lower the barrier for the remote sensing community. Key limitations include poor handling of dynamic objects due to satellite revisit rates, potential inheritance of CLIP's internet biases, and a text encoder that was never fine-tuned for aerial semantics (e.g., directional language). Open questions include whether daily-revisit sensors, fine-tuning the text encoder on small satellite-caption sets, or combining GRAFT with captioning frameworks like ClipCap can close these gaps.
