## Problem

While kernel regression enjoys well-established minimax optimality results, the classification excess risk of kernel classifiers in Sobolev spaces lacked a complete minimax characterization: the lower bound was only proven for d=1 in Besov spaces (Kerkyacharian & Picard, 1992), and no matching upper bound existed for general Sobolev RKHS interpolation spaces. Additionally, the smoothness parameter s that governs the convergence rate had no practical estimation procedure, limiting the applicability of theoretical guarantees to real datasets.

## Input / Output

The classifier takes n i.i.d. labeled pairs (X_i, Y_i) with X_i in a compact subset of R^d and Y_i ∈ {-1,1}, along with a kernel K defining an RKHS H, and produces a binary prediction sign(f̂(x)). The smoothness estimation procedure consumes the eigendecomposition of the empirical kernel matrix and outputs a scalar estimate of s via logarithmic least-squares on truncated eigencoordinates. For the neural network application, inputs are image features on the unit sphere S^{d-1} (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10 reduced to two classes), and the NTK with L hidden layers and ReLU activations serves as the kernel.

## Method

The paper analyzes kernel classifiers trained via gradient flow, expressed as spectral algorithms with filter function φ_ν(z) = (1−e^{−νz})/z. Under a source condition f*_ρ ∈ [H]^s, eigenvalue decay rate β, and embedding index α_0 = 1/β, the upper bound on classification excess risk is O(n^{−sβ/(2sβ+1)}) with regularization ν ∝ n^{β/(sβ+1)}. A novel minimax lower bound of the same rate is established for Sobolev RKHS H^r(𝒳) via a construction inspired by Hölder-class classification lower bounds, proving rate optimality. Through the NTK uniform convergence result (Li et al., 2023a), the same rate transfers to overparameterized ReLU neural networks trained by gradient flow with squared loss. A practical 'Truncation Estimation' method estimates s by fitting the logarithmic decay of kernel eigencoordinates up to a fixed truncation index, avoiding noise contamination at high frequencies, and is validated on three image datasets where MNIST (s≈0.49) is smoother than CIFAR-10 (s≈0.20) relative to the NTK.

## Results

The paper establishes that the classification excess risk of a gradient-flow kernel classifier is bounded above by C(ln(4/δ))·n^{-sβ/(2(sβ+1))} and that this rate is minimax optimal (matching a corresponding lower bound) in Sobolev RKHS. For overparameterized neural networks with NTK on S^{d-1}, the eigenvalue decay rate is β = d/(d-1), yielding the same rate for network classifiers. Empirically, the proposed Truncation Estimation method estimates the relative smoothness s of real datasets with respect to NTKs: MNIST ≈ 0.486, Fashion-MNIST ≈ 0.437, and CIFAR-10 ≈ 0.199 (averaged over 3-layer NTKs, 50 repeats, 5000 samples, truncation point 100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical paper, so there are no direct method-vs-method accuracy comparisons. The key theoretical comparison is with prior minimax lower bounds: Yang (1999) illustrated a similar lower bound for Besov spaces, but the result was only proved for d=1 by Kerkyacharian & Picard (1992); this paper extends the lower bound to Sobolev RKHS for general d > 1. For the upper bound, the paper builds on Zhang et al. (2023), who established minimax optimality for misspecified kernel ridge regression, and extends it to the classification setting with the 0-1 loss. Empirically, the smoothness estimates across datasets are consistent with known difficulty ordering: MNIST (s ≈ 0.49) is the easiest, Fashion-MNIST (s ≈ 0.44) is intermediate, and CIFAR-10 (s ≈ 0.20) is the hardest, with lower s implying a slower convergence rate n^{-sβ/(2(sβ+1))} and thus harder classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the minimax optimality framework for misspecified kernel ridge regression (Zhang et al., 2023) from regression to binary classification, and builds on the embedding index and interpolation space machinery of Fischer & Steinwart (2020). It leverages the uniform convergence of overparameterized networks to the NTK (Li et al., 2023a) to transfer kernel classifier bounds to neural network classifiers. A key departure from prior work is the establishment of a minimax lower bound for classification in Sobolev RKHS for d > 1, resolving a gap left by earlier Besov-space results (Yang, 1999; Kerkyacharian & Picard, 1992) that only covered d = 1.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The minimax optimality result provides a clean theoretical characterization of how the smoothness parameter s and eigenvalue decay rate β jointly determine the best achievable classification rate, giving practitioners a principled way to assess whether a given kernel is well-suited to a dataset. The proposed Truncation Estimation method for s is a first step toward data-driven model selection in kernel classification, though it is acknowledged to be sensitive to noise and mixed-smoothness structures. Open questions include extending the lower bound to more general RKHS classes beyond Sobolev spaces, developing more robust smoothness estimators, and understanding how these rates manifest in finite-width (non-NTK) neural networks.
