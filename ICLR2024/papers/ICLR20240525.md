## Problem

Existing meta-learned RL objective functions (LPG, LPO) use a static surrogate loss throughout an agent's entire training, ignoring the total and remaining number of training steps. This limits their expressive potential: they cannot implement adaptive schedules (e.g., shifting from exploration to exploitation) that humans naturally employ, and they generalize poorly to unseen training horizons. The paper asks whether conditioning the learned objective on temporal information can unlock more expressive, horizon-adaptive learning algorithms.

## Input / Output

The meta-learned objective consumes standard agent transitions (state, action, reward, done, next state), the policy's action probability, bootstrap vectors, and—critically—the lifetime fraction n/N (and log(N) for TA-LPG, since N is unbounded). For TA-LPO, the lifetime fraction is multiplied element-wise with the existing probability-ratio/advantage input vector to preserve the drift function's theoretical conditions. The output is a parameterized RL objective that, when applied to an agent, produces policy gradient updates whose structure (clipping thresholds, entropy coefficient, update magnitude) changes dynamically as a function of the agent's position in its training lifetime.

## Method

The paper augments LPG and LPO with lifetime conditioning: TA-LPG appends n/N and log(N) to the LSTM input vector, while TA-LPO multiplies the drift function's input by n/N to maintain Mirror Learning convergence guarantees. A key finding is that meta-gradient optimization (truncated backpropagation through a short window) fails to learn any temporal adaptation, because the myopic proxy objective cannot capture end-of-training effects. Instead, the authors use Evolution Strategies with a novel antithetic task sampling scheme—pairing antithetic noise candidates on the same randomly sampled task and applying a rank transformation to normalize fitness across tasks—enabling full-lifetime optimization without memory constraints. The discovered objectives implement dynamic policy importance ratio clipping (shifting from optimistic to pessimistic), entropy annealing schedules that decay more slowly for longer horizons, and update-norm schedules inversely proportional to training horizon, effectively balancing exploration and exploitation across the agent's lifetime.

## Results

TA-LPG consistently outperforms LPG across all training horizons on held-out Grid-Worlds and Minigrid, reaching maximum performance on the 'sparse' task in 1/8 of the training steps required by LPG and continuing to improve on the 'dense' task after LPG has plateaued. TA-LPO, meta-trained only on Space Invaders-MinAtar, significantly outperforms both LPO and PPO across the full MinAtar and Brax environment suites, generalizing to environments and hyperparameter settings (10M vs 50M steps, rollout lengths 128 vs 5) unseen during meta-training. An ablation confirms that meta-gradient optimization of TA-LPG fails to learn any horizon adaptation, while ES-optimized TA-LPG achieves the full temporal-awareness benefit.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LPG (Oh et al., 2020), TA-LPG wins on every evaluated horizon for both Grid-World and Minigrid, with the largest gap on short horizons (1/8× fewer steps to convergence on the sparse task) and a sustained advantage on long horizons where LPG plateaus early. Against LPO (Lu et al., 2022a), TA-LPO wins across all MinAtar and Brax environments, with the advantage growing toward the end of training, suggesting LPO converges prematurely. Against PPO (Schulman et al., 2017), TA-LPO also wins on both suites, with the gap widening in later training steps, consistent with PPO's known early-convergence failure mode. A heuristic learning-rate schedule applied to LPG does not close the gap, confirming that static objectives with handcrafted schedules are insufficient compared to the learned temporally-aware objective. The meta-gradient variant of TA-LPG (using the original LPG optimization) loses to the ES-optimized TA-LPG at all horizons and shows no sign of horizon adaptation in its update norm, establishing ES as the critical meta-optimizer for discovering lifetime-conditioned algorithms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the meta-learned objective function discovery line of work (LPG, LPO) by augmenting the learned loss with explicit temporal information about the agent's position in its training lifetime (n/N). It builds directly on LPG's LSTM-based learned critic and LPO's Mirror Learning drift function, adding lifetime conditioning to both. A key methodological departure is the use of Evolution Strategies with antithetic task sampling for multi-task meta-optimization of LPG, replacing the original meta-gradient approach, motivated by the finding that truncated backpropagation is fundamentally insufficient for discovering long-horizon adaptive behavior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that ignoring the training horizon significantly restricts the expressiveness of discovered RL algorithms, and that the meta-optimization method (ES vs. meta-gradients) is a critical, previously underappreciated factor in whether temporal adaptation can be learned. The discovered algorithms exhibit interpretable behavioral shifts (optimism→pessimism, dynamic entropy annealing) that balance exploration and exploitation across the agent's lifetime. Open questions include whether these temporally-aware objectives scale to more complex settings, whether theoretical robustness guarantees can be established (particularly for LPG), and whether further conditioning signals (optimizer statistics, task identity) yield additional gains.
