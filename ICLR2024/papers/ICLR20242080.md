## Problem

Whether attention mechanisms are necessary for in-context learning to emerge has remained an open question, as prior synthetic ICL studies have predominantly anchored their investigations on transformer architectures. This paper asks: can non-attention architectures (RNNs, CNNs, state space models, linear attention variants) perform ICL, and how do they compare to transformers in statistical efficiency and consistency across varying prompt lengths and task difficulties?

## Input / Output

Each synthetic task presents a prompt of the form (x₁, f(x₁), x₂, f(x₂), …, xₙ) where the model must produce f(xₙ) as a completion. For associative recall, inputs are discrete tokens from a vocabulary of size k; for linear regression, inputs are d-dimensional Gaussian vectors with scalar outputs; for multiclass classification, inputs are d=16 dimensional Gaussian samples from k cluster centers; for image classification, inputs are Omniglot images embedded via a randomly initialized ResNet trained alongside the model. The model is trained from scratch to minimize expected loss over all prompts, with the number of in-context examples swept from 1 to 1024 (training sees at most 31 examples, so longer prompts test extrapolation).

## Method

The paper conducts the first large-scale empirical comparison of 13 causal language modeling architectures—Elman RNN, LSTM, GRU, LightConv, DynamicConv, S4, H3, Hyena, Mamba, RetNet, RWKV, GPT-2, and LLaMA-2—across five synthetic ICL tasks (associative recall, linear regression, multiclass classification, image classification, and language modeling). All models are trained from scratch with matched parameter counts (~500K for synthetic tasks, ~33M for language modeling) using AdamW with cosine decay, sweeping over learning rates, layer depths, and seeds (243 runs per architecture for synthetic tasks). A key experimental design choice is that synthetic tasks make ICL the only viable solution strategy, eliminating the confound of memorization; the image classification experiment additionally introduces bursty vs. non-bursty training prompts to measure each architecture's predisposition toward ICL versus memorization. The authors further measure ICL score (loss difference between early and late token positions) during language modeling on TinyStories and evaluate a natural-language few-shot sentiment task with flipped labels to test override of semantic priors.

## Results

Across all 13 architectures evaluated on synthetic ICL tasks, every model successfully learns in-context at the training prompt length (31 examples), achieving 0.85–0.99 accuracy on associative recall, ~0.01 MSE on linear regression (d=20), and ~0.94 accuracy on multiclass classification (k=8). However, when extrapolating to 1024 in-context examples (32× the training length), performance diverges dramatically: recurrent and convolutional architectures (LSTM 0.99, DynamicConv 0.98, RetNet 0.90, RWKV 0.80) maintain strong performance, while state-space models (S4 0.15, H3 0.14, Hyena 0.04) and both transformers (GPT2 0.09, LLaMA2 0.06) collapse. On the language modeling ICL score (TinyStories, 512-token context), transformers and Mamba lead, with most architectures showing an abrupt improvement window analogous to induction head formation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares 13 architectures (RNN, LSTM, GRU, LightConv, DynamicConv, S4, H3, Hyena, Mamba, RetNet, RWKV, GPT2, LLaMA2) on a common protocol of synthetic ICL tasks. At the training prompt length (31 examples), all architectures are competitive, with transformers (GPT2, LLaMA2), Mamba, Hyena, H3, RetNet, and RWKV achieving near-perfect scores on all three core tasks. The critical differentiation emerges at extrapolation: LSTM and DynamicConv are the most robust, maintaining 0.97–1.00 accuracy at 1023 examples on associative recall, while S4, H3, and Hyena collapse to 0.04–0.15. On linear regression (d=20), RetNet shows the best extrapolation (MSE 0.10 at 1023 examples vs. 0.0 for GPT2/LLaMA2 which spike to 25+). On multiclass classification (k=8), RWKV and RetNet are the most consistent extrapolators (0.93–0.94 at 1023 examples), while LLaMA2 degrades to 0.61. In the image classification experiment with bursty data, LLaMA2 and Hyena show the strongest predisposition toward ICL over memorization, while RetNet, S4, both CNNs, and all RNNs strongly favor memorization. On the natural language ICL task (TinyStories-trained models), Hyena uniquely shows steady improvement in the flipped-label setting, suggesting a stronger ability to override semantic priors than any other architecture tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the synthetic ICL task framework established by Xie et al. (2021), von Oswald et al. (2022), Garg et al. (2023), and Akyürek et al. (2023), which created controlled environments where task resolution depends on prompt utilization rather than memorization. It departs from the near-universal assumption in that literature that models employ an internal attention mechanism, by systematically evaluating thirteen architectures spanning RNNs, CNNs, state space models, linear attention variants, and transformers. The closest prior comparisons are Xie et al. (2021), who found RNNs/LSTMs fail at ICL for image classification, and Chan et al. (2022), who showed LSTMs can perform ICL on synthetic language tasks; this paper subsumes and generalizes both findings across a much broader architecture and task space. The ICL score metric from Olsson et al. (2022) is adopted to connect the synthetic-task results to the language-modeling perspective on ICL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that all thirteen architectures can perform ICL and that several attention alternatives (Hyena, Mamba, RWKV) are competitive with or occasionally surpass transformers—challenges the field's implicit assumption that quadratic self-attention is a prerequisite for in-context learning. This has direct practical implications: if linear-time architectures can match transformer ICL performance, the efficiency gains of SSMs and linear attention come without sacrificing the emergent few-shot capability that motivates large-scale pretraining. However, no single architecture dominates across all tasks and prompt lengths, and performance degrades when extrapolating beyond training-time sequence lengths, leaving open the question of what architectural properties (positional encoding scheme, context access pattern, state compression) most reliably support ICL at scale. The paper also raises the open question of what mechanism in non-attention architectures plays the role analogous to induction heads in transformers.
