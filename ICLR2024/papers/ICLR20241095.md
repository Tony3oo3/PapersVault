## Problem

Quasi-Monte Carlo (QMC) methods that correlate samples to reduce estimator variance are well-established in Euclidean spaces but have no rigorous analogue for random walks on graphs. Existing schemes that improve graph walk mixing (e.g., non-backtracking walks) modify marginal transition probabilities, breaking unbiasedness for non-asymptotic estimators that depend on known walk probabilities. This paper introduces the first rigorously studied QMC scheme that correlates the *directions* of an ensemble of graph walkers while leaving their marginal probabilities unmodified.

## Input / Output

The method takes an undirected graph, a number of walkers m launched from each node, and a termination probability p, and produces unbiased estimates of graph kernels (2-regularised Laplacian or diffusion), the PageRank vector, or k-node graphlet concentrations. The implementation is a trivial drop-in modification: at each timestep, walkers at the same node are assigned to neighbours uniformly *without* replacement instead of with replacement, requiring no additional memory beyond current walker positions. The scheme is fully composable with antithetic termination (which anticorrelates walk lengths), so both direction and length correlations can be induced simultaneously.

## Method

Repelling random walks induce correlations between an ensemble of graph walkers by partitioning co-located walkers into blocks of size d (the node degree) and assigning each block's members to distinct neighbours uniformly without replacement, so walkers are forced to diverge rather than cluster. This modifies the joint transition matrix Q (breaking its Kronecker-product structure) while preserving each walker's marginal transition probability P_ij = 1/d_i, guaranteeing that any estimator unbiased under i.i.d. walkers remains unbiased. A 'transient repelling' variant—where walkers repel only at the first timestep and are independent thereafter—makes theoretical analysis tractable and yields clean variance-reduction theorems for PageRank estimation (any graph) and kernel estimation (trees, 2D grids). The paper further identifies a broad class of 'step-by-step linear' functions whose estimator variance is provably suppressed by transient repulsion, subsuming both the PageRank and GRF coordinate estimators as special cases.

## Results

On kernel regression for node attribute prediction (Table 1), repelling random walks (q-r-GRFs) reduce angular error by up to ~65% on the torus graph (N=4350: 0.000506 → 0.000180), and combining both QMC schemes (q-ar-GRFs) achieves the best results across all five mesh graphs. For PageRank estimation (Table 2), repelling walks consistently reduce L2 error across all 8 test graphs, e.g. from 0.0208 to 0.0196 on Small ER and from 0.000342 to 0.000335 on Eurosis (N=1272). For graphlet concentration estimation (Fig. 4), repelling walks consistently lower MSE on triangle graphlet estimates across all graphs, with the largest gains on denser topologies. The paper proves (Theorem 4.2) that variance reduction for PageRank holds for *any* graph, and (Theorem 3.1) for kernel estimation on trees and 2D grids.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four sampling strategies: vanilla GRFs with i.i.d. walkers (Choromanski, 2023), GRFs with antithetic termination only (Reid et al., 2023a), GRFs with repelling walks only, and GRFs with both QMC schemes. Repelling walks (q-r-GRFs) substantially outperform antithetic termination (q-a-GRFs) on kernel estimation — e.g. on torus, repelling alone reduces error by 64% while antithetic termination alone reduces it by only 5%. The combination (q-ar-GRFs) achieves the lowest error in every setting tested. For PageRank (Table 2), the i.i.d. baseline from Fogaras et al. (2005) is consistently beaten by repelling walks across all 8 graphs, with the theoretical guarantee (Theorem 4.2) holding for arbitrary graph topology. For graphlet concentration (Fig. 4), the i.i.d. baseline from Chen et al. (2016) is also consistently outperformed, though no formal variance-reduction proof is provided for this setting. The key differentiator from prior work (non-backtracking walks, self-repellent walks) is that repelling random walks preserve marginal transition probabilities exactly, ensuring unbiasedness in the non-asymptotic regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces the first quasi-Monte Carlo (QMC) scheme that correlates the directions of an ensemble of graph random walkers, building on the Graph Random Features (GRF) framework of Choromanski (2023) and the antithetic termination QMC scheme of Reid et al. (2023a). It departs fundamentally from prior work on improving random walk mixing (non-backtracking walks, self-interacting/reinforced walks) by preserving marginal transition probabilities exactly, which prior approaches that modify the Markov chain's transition kernel cannot do. This distinction is critical: it guarantees non-asymptotic unbiasedness of estimators, not just asymptotic variance reduction. The mechanism itself is a trivial 'without replacement' modification of neighbour assignment, yet it induces non-trivial correlations in the joint transition matrix that the authors draw an analogy to quantum entanglement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a new QMC paradigm for graph sampling with provable variance reduction across three disparate tasks (kernel estimation, PageRank, graphlet concentrations), and the PageRank guarantee (Theorem 4.2) holds for arbitrary graphs, which is notably strong. The trivial drop-in implementation and compatibility with existing QMC schemes (antithetic termination) make it immediately practical. Key open questions include extending the kernel variance-reduction proof beyond trees and 2D grids to general graphs, providing theoretical analysis for graphlet estimation, and understanding the full (non-transient) repelling scheme analytically. The step-by-step linear function class (Theorem 4.4) opens a broader theoretical framework for identifying which estimators benefit from transient repulsion.
