## Problem

Standard scaled dot-product attention in transformers lacks an explicit mechanism for handling hierarchical syntactic patterns of arbitrary nesting depth, and theoretical results show finite-precision transformers cannot recognize certain context-free languages like Dyck-2. This limits transformers' ability to model the recursive, compositional structure of natural language syntax and may explain their poor data efficiency compared to human language learners.

## Input / Output

For language modeling, the model takes a token sequence and produces a next-token probability distribution; for machine translation, it takes a German source sentence and produces an English translation. The stack attention sublayer consumes a sequence of input vectors and produces, at each timestep, a weighted interpolation of the top stack vector across all possible push/pop action sequences (VPDA runs), rather than a direct weighted sum over input value vectors. The nondeterministic variant's output is a soft-selection over an exponential number of runs, computed tractably via dynamic programming.

## Method

The paper replaces the multi-head SDPA sublayer in a transformer with a differentiable stack that performs soft-selection over sequences of push and pop actions on a stack of vectors, returning the topmost vector of the selected sequence. Two variants are proposed: a superposition stack (related to deterministic PDAs, recognizing only real-time DCFLs) and a nondeterministic stack based on a differentiable vector PDA (dVPDA) that can recognize all CFLs. The key theoretical insight is that the superposition stack is a special case of the dVPDA with |Q|=|Γ|=1, unifying both as soft-selections over VPDA runs. The nondeterministic variant uses Lang's dynamic programming algorithm to compute the expected top stack vector in cubic time, and requires no syntactic supervision. Stack attention is unidirectional (causal by construction), making it a drop-in replacement for SDPA in both encoders and decoders, and is inserted in the middle layer of a 5-layer transformer.

## Results

On the Penn Treebank language modeling benchmark, nondeterministic stack attention (Tf+Nd) achieves 88.54 test perplexity with 9.86M parameters, outperforming the standard transformer baseline (Tf) by 4.3 perplexity points (92.84) despite having ~190K fewer parameters. On the Hardest CFL task (a CFL-complete language with maximal parsing difficulty), Tf+Nd achieves the best in-distribution cross-entropy difference among all models tested, including the previously-proposed VRNS-RNN. On machine translation (Europarl v7 de-en, 100k examples), results are mixed: Tf+Sup achieves the best BLEU (12.81) in the middle parameter tier, but no stack attention variant consistently outperforms the standard transformer across all three size tiers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard transformer (Vaswani et al., 2017), Tf+Nd wins clearly on Penn Treebank (88.54 vs. 92.84 test perplexity) and on all nondeterministic CFL tasks (ww^R, wa^p w^R, Hardest CFL), while Tf+Sup underperforms Tf on PTB (98.67 vs. 92.84) and only wins on the real-time DCFL tasks (w#w^R, Dyck). Against the LSTM baseline (Hochreiter & Schmidhuber, 1997), standard transformers suffer from length generalization issues on CFLs, but Tf+Nd alleviates this on nondeterministic CFLs; however, LSTM still outperforms Tf on most CFL tasks overall. Against the VRNS-RNN (LSTM+Nd, DuSell & Chiang, 2023), Tf+Nd achieves better in-distribution performance on Hardest CFL despite having fewer parameters, while LSTM+Nd retains an edge in out-of-distribution length generalization on DCFLs. On machine translation, neither stack attention variant consistently beats the standard transformer across all three parameter tiers, suggesting that for short sentences (≤150 characters), the latent syntax model provides limited additional benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes two differentiable stack designs—the superposition stack of Joulin & Mikolov (2015) and the nondeterministic vector PDA (dVPDA) of DuSell & Chiang (2023)—from their original role as external memory modules for RNNs into a drop-in replacement for scaled dot-product attention inside transformers. A key theoretical contribution is showing that the superposition stack is a special case of the dVPDA with |Q|=|Γ|=1, unifying the two under a single soft-selection-over-runs framework. Unlike prior syntax-oriented transformers (Shiv & Quirk 2019; Deguchi et al. 2019; Murty et al. 2023b), the method requires no syntactic supervision, and unlike Kim et al. (2017) structured attention, it is unidirectional and thus compatible with causal language modeling and decoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, supervision-free mechanism for giving transformers the theoretical capacity to recognize all context-free languages, addressing a known expressivity gap (Hahn 2020). However, practical impact is tempered by significant computational overhead (24.3 min/epoch vs. 0.8 for standard transformers on PTB) and a notable negative result: stack attention does not improve machine translation quality across three model sizes, suggesting that either natural language at short lengths does not benefit from explicit hierarchical structure, or that the training procedure fails to exploit it. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models and longer sequences, whether parallel CKY-style speedups can close the efficiency gap, and why the benefit appears in language modeling but not translation.
