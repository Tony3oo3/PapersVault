## Problem

TPR-based neuro-symbolic models rely on a simple MLP to decompose input data into roles, fillers, and unbinding operators, but this decomposition is often incomplete for unseen compositions, degrading all downstream TPR functions. The paper identifies this decomposition bottleneck as the root cause of limited systematic generalization in existing TPR approaches, and notes that no prior work explicitly addresses the decomposition problem in TPR-based networks.

## Input / Output

The AID module takes N_inputs input feature vectors (word vectors for sentence-level TPR-RNN, or N_inputs sub-vectors partitioned from a single word vector for word-level FWM and Linear Transformer) and N_com initial TPR components, all projected to a common dimension D_com. It outputs refined TPR components (roles, fillers, unbinding operators) that replace the baseline model's decomposition output and feed directly into the existing TPR encoding/decoding operations. The module is a parameter-shared drop-in replacement applied at both the encoding and decoding stages of the host model, adding minimal computational overhead due to its parallel operations.

## Method

AID performs iterative slot-based competitive attention (N_iters rounds) where each TPR component competes via dot-product attention (with ELU non-linearity) to selectively aggregate information from input features, analogous to Slot Attention but with a critical routing mechanism: each slot is systematically mapped to a specific TPR symbol (e.g., slot 1 → role1, slot 3 → filler) rather than being permutation-invariant. Unlike Slot Attention's random slot initialization, AID conditionally initializes each slot from context-dependent input features via a feed-forward network, providing a differentiable connection between inputs and TPR components and improving training stability. Each iteration applies LayerNorm and a two-layer MLP update to the aggregated features, with a final one-layer MLP (concatenated with dropout-applied initial components) producing the output. The module is integrated into TPR-RNN, FWM, and Linear Transformer by replacing their component-generation step while preserving all original TPR functions, and is trained end-to-end with the host model under standard supervised objectives.

## Results

On the sys-bAbI task, FWM+AID achieves 0.45% WER (w/o sys diff) and 1.21% WER (w/ sys diff), reducing the systematic generalization gap to 0.76 — the lowest among all compared models. On Sort-of-CLEVR, AID-assisted Linear Transformer reaches 98.9% unary accuracy (vs. 69.3% baseline), 78.6% binary, and 63.7% ternary. On WikiText-103, AID reduces perplexity from 37.533 to 37.151 (Linear Transformer) and from 36.659 to 36.253 (Delta Network). On the synthetic SAR task, AID-assisted FWM consistently outperforms DNC and baseline FWM across all values of the combinatorial diversity parameter p.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FWM+AID outperforms all five sys-bAbI baselines on both in-distribution and systematic-difference settings, achieving the lowest WER (0.45% / 1.21%) and the smallest generalization gap (0.76) compared to TXL (5.01), DAM (4.77), STM (3.70), TPR-RNN (7.95), and FWM (2.06). On Sort-of-CLEVR, AID-assisted Linear Transformer dramatically improves over the baseline (69.3% → 98.9% unary, 75.5% → 78.6% binary, 56.4% → 63.7% ternary), with the unary gain of +29.6 points being the most striking. On WikiText-103, AID reduces perplexity for both Linear Transformer (37.533 → 37.151) and Delta Network (36.659 → 36.253), confirming gains even on large-scale language modeling. On the SAR task, AID-assisted FWM consistently surpasses DNC and baseline FWM across all values of p, with the advantage growing as p decreases (i.e., less combinatorial diversity in training). The paper also verifies that these gains are not merely from added parameters: scaling the baseline models to match AID's parameter count (e.g., FWM with D_LSTM=512) does not close the gap to FWM+AID.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two research threads: slot-based iterative attention (Slot Attention, Locatello et al. 2020) and Tensor Product Representation (Smolensky 1990) as implemented in fast-weight memory networks (TPR-RNN, FWM, Linear Transformer). The key departure from Slot Attention is the introduction of a trainable routing mechanism that maps each slot to a specific TPR component (role, filler, unbinding operator) rather than producing permutation-invariant slots, and the use of context-dependent initialization from input features instead of random slot initialization. The paper also distinguishes itself from prior TPR-attention integrations (Schlag et al. 2019; Jiang et al. 2021) by being a task-agnostic drop-in module that addresses the fundamental decomposition problem rather than being tailored to specific tasks like math problem solving or summarization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete weakness in TPR-based neuro-symbolic models—the reliance on simple MLPs for role/filler decomposition—that limits their systematic generalization. The AID module's generality (applicable to TPR-RNN, FWM, and Linear Transformer with minimal modification) and the introduction of the SAR benchmark for measuring compositional generalization under varying training diversity are practically useful contributions. However, the approach is inherently scoped to TPR-based architectures, and the WikiText-103 improvements are modest, suggesting the method's primary value lies in structured reasoning tasks rather than general language modeling. An open question is whether the routing mechanism generalizes to TPR variants with more complex or hierarchical symbolic structures beyond flat role-filler pairs.
