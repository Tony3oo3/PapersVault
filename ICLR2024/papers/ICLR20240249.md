## Problem

Overfitting remains a critical challenge in event-based classification for Spiking Neural Networks due to their weak spatial representation capability. While saliency-guided data augmentation has proven effective for image-based ANNs, no method existed for extracting saliency maps or CAMs from SNNs, and existing event-based mixing strategies ignore the size and location of label-related objects, risking label misalignment during augmentation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240249/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes asynchronous event streams (4D points with spatial coordinates, timestamp, and polarity) converted into fixed-shape frames (e.g., 48×48, 128×128, or 224×224) along with a trained SNN (Spike-VGG11 or SEW Resnet18). It produces two types of outputs: (1) per-pixel saliency maps and class activation maps at an intermediate layer for interpretability, and (2) augmented event streams with modified event positions, dropped events, or mixed event content, paired with proportionally adjusted mixed labels for supervised training.

## Method

The paper derives two spiking relevance propagation rules from the αβ-LRP framework. SLTRP propagates relevance scores backward through spiking layers by decomposing each neuron's output into a membrane-voltage term (from the previous time step) and an input-current term (at the current time step), splitting relevance via a time-dependent ratio γ[t] that satisfies a per-neuron conservation property across all T time steps. SLRP is a time-averaged variant that collapses the temporal dimension, yielding R^(l-1) = R^(l) for spiking layers and reducing computation for static-image-derived datasets. These rules generate saliency maps and CAMs that guide two augmentation operations: RPGDrop, which stochastically removes events with probability proportional to local saliency (disturbing label-relevant regions more), and RPGMix, which translates two event streams to minimize bounding-box overlap of label-related objects, applies a CutMix-style mask to prevent per-pixel information mixing, and computes mixed labels weighted by non-overlapping bounding-box areas. The full EventRPG pipeline combines these with geometric augmentations (flip, translation) and applies them with randomly sampled policies per sample in a batch.

## Results

EventRPG achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on N-Caltech101 (85.62%) and CIFAR10-DVS (85.55%), improving over the identity baseline by 9.92% and 6.70% respectively. On the SL-Animals action recognition dataset (3 sets), EventRPG with SLRP saliency maps reaches 91.59%, outperforming the second-best augmentation method by 4.2%. On mini N-ImageNet, EventRPG achieves 40.90% Top-1 accuracy, surpassing NDA (35.84%) by 5.06%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On object recognition, EventRPG outperforms NDA (Li et al., 2022) by 1.92% on N-Caltech101 (85.62 vs 83.70) and 3.85% on CIFAR10-DVS (85.55 vs 81.70), and beats EventMix (Shen et al., 2023) by 6.15% and 4.10% on the same datasets. EventRPG also surpasses EventDrop (Gu et al., 2021) by 10.58% on N-Caltech101 and 7.82% on CIFAR10-DVS. On N-Cars, EventRPG (96.00%) trails EventMix (96.29%) by 0.29%, which the authors attribute to the binary nature of the dataset lacking clear label-related objects. For saliency-based mixing, RPGMix (81.75% on N-Caltech101) outperforms both Saliency Mix (Uddin et al., 2020; 72.63%) and Puzzle Mix (Kim et al., 2020; 79.38%), while Saliency Mix actually degrades below identity. On action recognition (SL-Animals 3 sets), EventRPG with SLRP saliency maps (91.59%) beats NDA (89.55%) by 2.04% and EventDrop (88.99%) by 2.60%, and surpasses non-SNN baselines SCTFA (Cai et al., 2023; 90.04%), TORE (Baldwin et al., 2022; 85.10%), and EvT (Sabater et al., 2022; 87.45%). For CAM/saliency map quality (Table 1), SLRP/SLTRP methods achieve the best A.I. and A.D. scores on N-Caltech101 and CIFAR10-DVS, outperforming Grad-CAM, Grad-CAM++, and SAM, though Grad-CAM++ edges them on the binary N-Cars task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP) from Bach et al. (2015) to Spiking Neural Networks, introducing two propagation rules (SLTRP and SLRP) that account for the temporal dynamics of LIF/IF neurons. It draws on the saliency-guided augmentation paradigm established in image processing (SaliencyMix, Puzzle Mix) and the first SNN saliency attempt (SAM by Kim & Panda, 2021b), but departs from SAM by grounding relevance scores in a conservation property rather than weighted feature-map addition. On the augmentation side, it builds on event-specific strategies (EventDrop, NDA, EventMix) but replaces their label-agnostic mixing/dropping with relevance-guided operations that preserve label-object alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled mechanism for extracting label-relevant saliency from SNNs, which is a prerequisite for any interpretability- or attention-guided technique in the spiking domain. EventRPG's ~10% accuracy gains over no augmentation on N-Caltech101 and consistent SOTA results across object and action recognition demonstrate that label-aware augmentation meaningfully alleviates overfitting in SNNs, which suffer from weaker spatial representation than ANNs. A key limitation is that the method is restricted to classification tasks; extending it to segmentation, detection, or self-supervised settings remains open. The near-equivalence of SLRP and SLTRP on static-image-derived datasets also raises the question of when temporal resolution in saliency is genuinely necessary versus computationally wasteful.
