## Problem

Modulation-based networks like FocalNet and VAN achieve strong accuracy with low theoretical FLOPs, but their inference latency is unsatisfying on real hardware due to fragmented operations (successive depth-wise convolutions, redundant linear projections) that violate practical efficiency guidelines. The paper asks whether a modulation block can be redesigned to retain the representational benefits of element-wise feature fusion while eliminating the latency overhead that prevents deployment on edge and mobile devices.

## Input / Output

The network consumes standard RGB images at 224×224 for classification (256×256 for MobileViT comparisons) and produces class labels over 1000 ImageNet categories. For dense prediction, the same backbone feeds Mask R-CNN (producing bounding boxes and instance masks on MS COCO) or Semantic FPN (producing pixel-level semantic masks on ADE20K). The architecture is a 4-stage hierarchical design with progressive downsampling factors of 4, 2, 2, 2 via strided convolutions, and the hybrid variant inserts vanilla self-attention blocks only in the last two stages where spatial resolution is small enough to keep attention affordable.

## Method

The EfficientMod block unifies the modulation mechanism and the channel-mixing MLP into a single operation: Output = p(ctx(x) ⊙ v(x)), where v(·) expands channels by a factor r (alternating 1 and 6) and p(·) squeezes them back, eliminating the separate MLP block and its associated fragmented FC layers. The context branch is simplified to a single 7×1 depth-wise convolution with GELU activation sandwiched between two linear projections, operating on C channels rather than rC as in MBConv, which reduces the depth-wise convolution cost by a factor of r. This design inherits the dynamics of self-attention (via element-wise multiplication) and the large receptive field of convolution while maintaining linear complexity in input resolution. Three scales (xxs, xs, s) are instantiated as pure convolutional networks, and a hybrid variant adds unmodified ViT attention blocks in stages 3–4, matching parameter counts to the pure-convolutional counterpart for fair comparison. Training follows the DeiT recipe (AdamW, cosine schedule, 300 epochs, mixup/cutmix) without re-parameterization or distillation, and the block is orthogonal to attention, enabling plug-and-play integration.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, EfficientMod-s achieves 81.0% top-1 accuracy with 12.9M parameters and 1.4G FLOPs, running at 5.5 ms GPU / 23.5 ms CPU latency. The smaller EfficientMod-xs reaches 78.3% at 3.6 ms GPU, and EfficientMod-xxs reaches 76.0% at 3.0 ms GPU. On ADE20K semantic segmentation, the hybrid EfficientMod-s achieves 46.0 mIoU (vs. 42.4 for EfficientFormerV2-s2), and on MS COCO it attains 43.6 AP for detection and 40.3 AP for instance segmentation with Mask R-CNN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EfficientMod-s outperforms the prior SOTA EfficientFormerV2-S2 by 0.6% top-1 accuracy (81.0 vs. 80.4) while being 25% faster on GPU (5.5 vs. 7.3 ms) and using comparable parameters (12.9M vs. 12.7M). It matches EdgeViT-S in accuracy (81.0%) but runs 73% faster on GPU (5.5 vs. 20.5 ms) and 32% faster on CPU. At similar GPU latency (~5.4–5.5 ms), EfficientMod-s surpasses MobileViTv2-1.0 by 2.9% top-1 (81.0 vs. 78.1). On downstream tasks, the hybrid EfficientMod-s beats EfficientFormerV2-s2 by 3.6 mIoU on ADE20K (46.0 vs. 42.4) and by 0.2/0.8 AP on MS COCO detection/instance segmentation; the pure-convolutional variant already exceeds PoolFormer by 4.2 mAP (detection) and 6.3 mIoU (segmentation) without any attention. The main trade-off is that FasterNet-T2 is slightly faster on GPU (4.4 vs. 5.5 ms) but lags by 2.1% accuracy, and MobileOne-S4 is marginally faster (4.8 vs. 5.5 ms) yet 1.6% less accurate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper abstracts the 'modulation mechanism' shared by VAN (Guo et al., 2023) and FocalNet (Yang et al., 2022) — parallel context-modeling and feature-projection branches fused via element-wise multiplication — and then tailors it for efficiency by fusing the MLP into the modulation block and reducing the depth-wise convolution channel count. It departs from the MBConv block (MobileNetV2) by inserting the depth-wise convolution laterally via modulation rather than sequentially, and from self-attention by achieving linear (rather than quadratic) complexity while retaining dynamic, input-dependent behavior. The MetaFormer token-mixer/channel-mixer philosophy (Yu et al., 2022) provides the architectural scaffold, and the hybrid variant borrows vanilla ViT attention for the last two stages, following the pattern established by EfficientFormerV2 and MobileFormer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EfficientMod offers a clean, single-block design that practitioners can drop into efficient networks, and the paper validates it across classification, detection, instance segmentation, and semantic segmentation on multiple hardware platforms. The tentative theoretical argument that l modulation blocks project features into a 2^l-dimensional space provides a principled reason why modulation is especially suited to narrow, shallow efficient networks — though the authors explicitly note this is not a formal proof. Open questions include scalability to larger models (the latency gap vs. MBConv widens with width), whether large-kernel convolutions or attention remain the most efficient route to large receptive fields, and whether the high-dimensional projection property can be further exploited or formalized.
