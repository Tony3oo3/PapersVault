## Problem

Standard learning-to-reject (L2R) methods such as Chow's rule are designed to minimize the 0-1 misclassification error and are shown to be grossly sub-optimal for long-tail evaluation metrics like the balanced error or worst-group error. The paper identifies the root cause: in the L2R setting, the per-class cost coefficients are no longer constants but depend intricately on the rejector itself, breaking the standard cost-sensitive reduction used in non-rejection classification.

## Input / Output

The method requires only a pre-trained class probability estimator (e.g., a ResNet trained with standard softmax cross-entropy) and a rejection cost c; no retraining or loss modification is needed. Given an image, the plug-in procedure outputs either a class label or a rejection decision, with the rejection criterion being a sample-dependent threshold over re-weighted class probabilities rather than a fixed threshold on the maximum probability. For the worst-group variant, the algorithm additionally requires a validation set split into two subsets for alternating plug-in and weight updates.

## Method

The paper derives the Bayes-optimal classifier and rejector for the balanced L2R risk (Theorem 1), showing the optimal rejector applies a sample-dependent threshold to re-weighted class probabilities with distribution-dependent coefficients α* and μ*, fundamentally different from Chow's constant-threshold rule. To mimic this solution without retraining, the plug-in approach (Algorithm 1) alternates between constructing a classifier-rejector pair from the pre-trained probabilities and updating group coverage estimates via a power-iteration heuristic, with a grid search over K−1 multiplier parameters. For general non-linear metrics (Theorem 3), the optimal solution is a stochastic mixture of K+1 deterministic classifier-rejector pairs; for the worst-group error, Algorithm 2 applies exponentiated gradient descent over group weights, invoking the balanced plug-in at each iteration, with a convergence guarantee of O(√(log K / T)) optimality gap.

## Results

The paper reports Area Under the Risk-Coverage Curve (AURC) for both balanced and worst-group error across three long-tailed image classification datasets. The proposed Plug-in[Worst] method achieves the best worst-group AURC on all three datasets: 0.321 on CIFAR-100, 0.213 on ImageNet, and 0.113 on iNaturalist. Plug-in[Balanced] achieves the best balanced AURC on CIFAR-100 (0.292) and iNaturalist (0.108), while on ImageNet both plug-in variants are within 0.001 of the best balanced AURC (0.197–0.198). All plug-in methods use the same base model trained with standard cross-entropy, requiring no changes to the training process.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The plug-in methods substantially outperform both standard L2R baselines: Chow's rule (AURC 0.509 balanced / 0.883 worst on CIFAR-100) and the cost-sensitive softmax CSS loss (0.483 / 0.785), with the plug-in variants reducing worst-group AURC by over 50% on CIFAR-100. Against Chow[BCE] (balanced cross-entropy base loss), Plug-in[Worst] wins on all six dataset-metric combinations, e.g., 0.287 vs. 0.359 balanced and 0.321 vs. 0.570 worst on CIFAR-100. Against Chow[DRO] (DRO-trained base model from Jones et al., 2021), Plug-in[Worst] matches or beats on worst-group error across all datasets (0.321 vs. 0.333 on CIFAR-100, 0.213 vs. 0.211 on ImageNet, 0.113 vs. 0.117 on iNaturalist) and on balanced error for CIFAR-100 and iNaturalist, while being within 0.001 on ImageNet balanced. Crucially, the plug-in methods achieve these results without any modification to the base model training, whereas Chow[BCE] and Chow[DRO] require retraining with a modified loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical Learning to Reject (L2R) framework—rooted in Chow's rule (1970) and the cost-sensitive L2R risk of Cortes et al. (2016)—to group-aware evaluation metrics such as balanced error and worst-group error. The key conceptual departure is showing that the standard cost-sensitive reduction used for standalone classification (Narasimhan et al., 2015) breaks down in the L2R setting because the per-class costs become non-constant and depend intricately on the rejector itself. The paper also extends the DRO framework of Sagawa et al. (2020) from classification to L2R, and builds on the stochastic classifier-rejector machinery for non-linear metrics (Narasimhan et al., 2015; Yang et al., 2020) to characterize the Bayes-optimal solution for general evaluation functions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that the most widely-used L2R method (Chow's rule) is not merely slightly sub-optimal but fundamentally misaligned with equitable performance across head and tail classes, providing a clear theoretical explanation for the group disparities observed empirically. The proposed plug-in approach is practically attractive because it requires no changes to base model training, yet matches or exceeds methods that retrain with balanced or DRO losses. Open questions include whether joint training with surrogate losses can further close the gap, and how the framework extends to hierarchical classification and pairwise ranking with rejection. The rejector-dependent cost structure identified here may also inform other settings where a decision (rejection, deferral) interacts with group-level evaluation.
