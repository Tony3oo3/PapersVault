## Problem

In Bayesian transfer learning, the prior over neural network parameters is typically a Gaussian centered at the pre-trained solution, but this parametric prior can hinder downstream learning when distribution shifts exist between upstream and downstream data. No existing posterior sampling method flexibly combines upstream and downstream information in a way that accounts for model misspecification, which is common when the true data-generating process does not belong to the assumed parametric family.

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-trained feature extractor parameters φ* and a linear-probed head W* (trained on the downstream dataset) as input, along with the downstream training set {x_i, y_i}. It produces M posterior samples θ^(m), each obtained by solving a weighted optimization over both the downstream data and pseudo-labels generated by the linear-probed model. For practical deployment, NPTL-Soup averages the M posterior samples into a single model via the Greedy Soup algorithm, reducing inference from M forward passes to one while retaining most of the BMA performance.

## Method

NPTL adapts the nonparametric learning (NPL) framework to transfer learning by placing a Dirichlet Process prior DP(α, F_π) on the unknown data distribution F_0, where the base measure F_π is constructed from pseudo-labels generated by a linear-probed model applied to downstream inputs—avoiding the need for access to the upstream dataset. The DP posterior yields a finite weighted mixture of downstream data points and pseudo-data points, and each posterior sample is obtained by minimizing a weighted sum of the downstream loss and the pseudo-data loss. To handle numerical instability when the downstream dataset is large, the method replaces the full Dirichlet distribution with a block Dirichlet distribution that partitions data into L blocks, reducing the sampling dimensionality. Unlike MCMC-based samplers, each posterior sample is generated independently, enabling embarrassingly parallel computation. The hyperparameter α, controlling the strength of prior belief in the pre-trained model, is selected via empirical Bayes on a held-out validation set.

## Results

On ResNet-50 transfer learning across five image classification datasets (B200, C101, D47, F102, P37), NPTL achieves 85.4% average accuracy and 0.554 average NLL, outperforming the strongest baselines (Ensemble+L2SP: 85.0% ACC, 0.595 NLL; SGHMC+L2SP: 84.0% ACC, 0.614 NLL). On the smaller ResNet-20x4 setup, NPTL reaches 76.5% average ACC and 0.838 NLL versus 76.2% and 0.853 for the best ensemble baseline. On CIFAR-10-C corruption robustness, NPTL maintains 79.3% average ACC and 0.698 NLL across five severity levels, a clear margin over all baselines (best competitor: Ensemble+L2SP at 76.3% ACC, 0.876 NLL).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NPTL consistently outperforms both SGHMC-based baselines (SGHMC+L2SP and SGHMC+PTYL) on every dataset and metric, with the largest NLL gaps (e.g., 0.554 vs. 0.614 on ResNet-50 average, and 0.698 vs. 0.852 on CIFAR-10-C average). Against the ensemble baselines, NPTL achieves a clear NLL advantage across all settings (e.g., 0.554 vs. 0.595 for Ensemble+L2SP on ResNet-50; 0.371 vs. 0.411 for Ensemble+L2SP on GLUE), while matching or slightly exceeding ensemble accuracy (0.854 vs. 0.850 on ResNet-50; 0.903 vs. 0.895 on ViT-B/16). On the CIFAR-10-C corruption benchmark, NPTL's advantage over all baselines widens with increasing corruption severity, suggesting superior robustness under distribution shift. On language tasks (GLUE), NPTL improves NLL by ~10% over Ensemble+L2SP (0.371 vs. 0.411) and also wins on the task-specific GLUE metric (0.797 vs. 0.789). In the semantic segmentation experiment on PASCAL VOC 2012, NPTL achieves 74.72 Mean IoU, surpassing L2SP (73.16), supervised PTYL (73.72), and self-supervised PTYL (74.15).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Nonparametric Learning (NPL) framework of Lyddon et al. (2018) and Fong et al. (2019) to the transfer learning setting, where prior work (L2SP, PTYL) has relied on parametric Gaussian priors centered at pre-trained weights. The key conceptual departure is replacing the parametric weight-space prior with a Dirichlet Process prior over the data distribution, which avoids assuming the pre-trained model's parametric family is correctly specified for the downstream task. The block Dirichlet distribution from Shin et al. (2021) is repurposed for numerical stability at scale, and the NPTL-Soup variant draws on Model Soups (Wortsman et al., 2022) to reduce inference cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NPTL addresses a genuine practical pain point: parametric Gaussian priors in Bayesian transfer learning can actively hurt downstream performance when upstream and downstream distributions diverge, and the nonparametric formulation provides a principled, theoretically grounded remedy with asymptotic robustness guarantees. The parallelizability of the sampling procedure (unlike sequential MCMC) and the NPTL-Soup single-model variant make the approach more deployable in resource-constrained settings. Limitations include the additional linear-probing cost, the fact that the asymptotic equivalence proof for the block Dirichlet covers only a simplified version, and the need to tune α per dataset. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large foundation models and whether the nonparametric advantage persists under extreme distribution shifts.
