## Problem

Message-passing neural networks (MPNNs) are fundamentally limited in modeling non-local many-body interactions due to finite-range message passing, oversmoothing, and oversquashing, making them inadequate for systems with long-range electronic delocalization such as conjugated molecules and metals. Spectral GNNs and transformers partially address these limitations but either lack equivariance under geometric symmetries, require rigid feature engineering, or incur quadratic computational cost. No existing architecture simultaneously captures structured non-local interactions, preserves group equivariance, and scales efficiently with system size.

## Input / Output

The method accepts either 3D molecular point clouds (atomic positions, nuclear charges) embedded in R³ or pure graphs with node and edge attributes, and produces per-atom or graph-level outputs: molecular energies and forces for quantum systems, a scalar regression value for ZINC solubility, or a classification label for TU datasets. Node features are expanded in irreducible representations of the rotation group O(3) (indexed by angular momentum quantum numbers l̃, m̃), and the matrix operators inherit this block structure, with each block corresponding to a product of representations (ss, sp, pp, …). The diagonal update extracts only the (m,0) component of each diagonal block, making the readout equivariant while the off-diagonal structure encodes non-local correlations.

## Method

MFNs operate in four stages per layer: (1) a local equivariant graph layer (e.g., MACE) computes node features, (2) a matrix construction step builds a sparse, self-adjoint, G-equivariant operator H from pairwise tensor products of node features, (3) a learnable matrix function f_θ is applied to H via a resolvent (pole) expansion f_θ(H) = Σ_s w_s(z_s I − H)^{−1} with learnable complex poles z_s and weights w_s, and (4) a diagonal update extracts non-local features from the diagonal blocks of f_θ(H) to update node states. The key insight is that the resolvent expansion preserves equivariance (since f_θ(ρ(g)Hρ(g)*) = ρ(g)f_θ(H)ρ(g)*) and, combined with selected inversion of the resolvent, reduces the computational cost from O(n³) diagonalization to O(n) for 1D topologies, O(n^{3/2}) for 2D, and O(n²) for 3D graphs. The matrix function is equivalent to an infinite power series in H, making a single MFN layer as expressive as infinite layers of linear message passing, with the degree of non-locality controllable via the imaginary part of the poles (Combes-Thomas theorem).

## Results

On the Guaranteed Non-Local (GNL) cumulene dataset, MFN achieves an out-of-domain energy RMSE of 0.9 meV/atom (11–12 C) and 2.6 meV/atom (15–16 C), dramatically outperforming MACE (16.5 and 12.0) and SpookyNet (31.4 and 23.4). On ZINC, MFN(GCN) achieves a MAE of 0.063 ± 0.002 with 512k parameters, surpassing the previous best PDF (0.066 ± 0.004) and Graphormer (0.122 ± 0.006). On TU datasets, MFN(GCN) attains the best accuracy on MUTAG (91.5 ± 7.35) and PTC_MR (68.9 ± 8.09), while remaining competitive on ENZYMES (72.9 ± 7.55) and PROTEINS (76.18 ± 4.07).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the cumulene GNL dataset, MFN decisively outperforms both local MACE and global-attention SpookyNet across all splits, with the largest gains on out-of-domain extrapolation (e.g., 0.9 vs. 16.5 meV/atom energy RMSE for 11–12 C chains), demonstrating that structured non-locality generalizes to unseen system sizes while attention-based non-locality does not. On ZINC, MFN(GCN) achieves the lowest MAE (0.063) among all 23 compared baselines, edging out PDF (0.066) and substantially outperforming Graphormer (0.122), SAN (0.139), and MPNN (0.145). On TU datasets, MFN(GCN) wins on MUTAG (91.5% vs. PPGNN 90.55%) and PTC_MR (68.9% vs. PDF 68.36%), but trails PDF on ENZYMES (72.9% vs. 73.50%), PROTEINS (76.18% vs. PPGNN 77.20%), and IMDB-B (74.1% vs. 75.60%), indicating that the matrix-function inductive bias is most beneficial for tasks requiring long-range structural reasoning rather than purely local topological features.

## Relation to Prior Work

MFNs unify and extend two previously separate research threads: spectral GNNs (which apply fixed polynomial or rational filters to a fixed graph operator like the Laplacian) and equivariant MPNNs (which handle geometric symmetries but are restricted to finite-range local interactions). The key conceptual departure is that MFNs learn both the graph operator H and the matrix function f_θ simultaneously, and parameterize f_θ via a resolvent (pole) expansion where both poles and weights are learnable — a formulation borrowed from linear-scaling electronic structure methods (PEXSI, selected inversion) but repurposed as a neural network layer. This positions MFNs as inherently non-local (the matrix function admits an infinite power series, analogous to infinite layers of linear message passing) while retaining the equivariance structure of MACE/ACE layers and the structured, controllable non-locality absent in attention-based approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The architecture addresses a fundamental expressivity gap: MPNNs cannot model long-range quantum effects (electron delocalization, spin coupling) without prohibitive depth, and attention-based models capture some non-locality but fail to extrapolate to unseen system sizes. The cumulene results — where MFNs achieve near-perfect energy extrapolation to unseen chain lengths while both local and global-attention baselines degrade — suggest that structured non-locality (governed by the Combes-Thomas exponential decay) is a more generalizable inductive bias than unstructured attention. Key open questions include: the current implementation is cubic-scaling (a prototype), true linear scaling requires incomplete factorization whose approximation error is uncharacterized, and the multivariate matrix function extension (higher correlation order) is left for future work. For force-field development, if the linear-scaling path is realized, MFNs could enable accurate modeling of large conjugated systems, metals, and amorphous materials currently intractable to local models.
