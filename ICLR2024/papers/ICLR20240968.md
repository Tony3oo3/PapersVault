## Problem

Equilibrium Propagation (EP) requires both weight symmetry and infinitesimal nudges to yield unbiased gradient estimates, yet both are difficult to satisfy in physical or neuromorphic substrates. Although a generalized EP formulation exists for non-symmetric dynamical systems, its failure on larger datasets (e.g., CIFAR-10) has never been attributed to specific bias sources because the effects of finite nudge and Jacobian asymmetry are entangled in practice. This paper analytically isolates these two bias contributions and shows that weight asymmetry, not just finite nudge, is the dominant obstacle to scaling EP to real-world tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 32×32 RGB images (Fashion MNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet 32×32) along with a target class label, and produces a class prediction through a linear readout layer. Unusually, the gradient for weight updates is not computed via a separate backward pass but through the network's own relaxation dynamics: a teaching signal β(t) is oscillated in the complex plane, and the gradient is extracted as the amplitude of the first harmonic of the resulting neuronal oscillations. This means the same recurrent network simultaneously performs forward inference and gradient computation, dispensing with the need for transposed connectivity matrices.

## Method

The paper extends Holomorphic EP to non-symmetric, complex-differentiable dynamical systems by expressing the neuronal error vector d_βu* as a Cauchy integral over a circular path in the complex β-plane, which yields exact derivatives at finite teaching amplitude |β|>0 without requiring weight symmetry. To address the residual bias from Jacobian asymmetry, the authors introduce a homeostatic loss L_homeo that penalizes the Frobenius norm of the skew-symmetric part A of the Jacobian J_F at the free fixed point, estimated efficiently via the Hutchinson trace estimator with random Gaussian probes. This loss encourages 'functional symmetry' (J_F ≈ J_F^T) without enforcing strict reciprocal weight tying, making it applicable to architectures without feedback connections. The homeostatic loss is added to the cross-entropy objective with a tunable coefficient λ_homeo, and the combined training procedure uses generalized hEP with N-point Cauchy integral estimates for the gradient. Empirically, this combination lifts asymmetric hEP from 60.4% to 84.3% Top-1 on CIFAR-10 and enables training on ImageNet 32×32 with only a ~5% Top-5 gap to the perfectly symmetric baseline.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, generalized hEP with Jacobian homeostasis and exact dβu* estimation achieves 84.3% Top-1 accuracy, a 23.9-point improvement over hEP without the homeostatic loss (60.4%). On CIFAR-100, the same setup reaches 53.8% Top-1 (vs. 35.2% without homeostasis). On ImageNet 32×32, the method achieves 31.4% Top-1 and 55.2% Top-5 accuracy, with only a ~5% gap to the perfectly symmetric hEP baseline (36.5% Top-1, 60.8% Top-5). Using N=2 Cauchy integral points instead of the exact derivative introduces an additional ~2.9-point drop on CIFAR-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to RBP (Liao et al., 2018), generalized hEP with homeostasis trails by 3.5 points on CIFAR-10 Top-1 (84.3% vs. 87.8%) and by 7.0 points on CIFAR-100 Top-1 (53.8% vs. 60.8%), reflecting the residual bias from imperfect Jacobian symmetry. Against the symmetric hEP baseline (Laborieux & Zenke, 2022), the asymmetric hEP with homeostasis closes most of the gap: 4.3 points on CIFAR-10 (84.3% vs. 88.6%) and ~5 points on ImageNet 32×32 Top-5 (55.2% vs. 60.8%). Critically, the homeostatic loss itself is the dominant factor: without it, hEP on CIFAR-10 collapses to 60.4%, a 24-point deficit. The homeostatic loss has no measurable effect when training with RBP (87.7% vs. 87.8%), confirming it specifically addresses the Jacobian asymmetry problem inherent to EP rather than acting as a generic regularizer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Holomorphic EP (Laborieux & Zenke, 2022), which used Cauchy integrals to eliminate finite-nudge bias but was restricted to symmetric (energy-based) networks, to the fully general non-symmetric vector-field setting of Scellier et al. (2018). It departs from the broader weight-symmetry literature (Kolen & Pollack, 1994; Lillicrap et al., 2016; Launay et al., 2020), which studied asymmetry in the context of backpropagation, by showing that the relevant quantity for EP is not weight symmetry but functional symmetry of the Jacobian at the fixed point. The proposed homeostatic loss draws on the Hutchinson trace estimator and connects to synaptic balancing (Stock et al., 2022) and target propagation (Meulemans et al., 2020), but applies it to all weights simultaneously rather than only feedback weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first clean theoretical decomposition of EP's gradient bias into two independent sources (finite nudge and Jacobian asymmetry) and shows both can be addressed: the former via Cauchy-integral estimation, the latter via a new homeostatic objective. The practical impact is significant for neuromorphic and biologically plausible learning, where perfect weight symmetry is unrealistic, as the homeostatic loss closes most of the performance gap to symmetric networks on ImageNet 32×32. Open questions include whether the required complex-plane oscillations can be implemented in biological or analog hardware, whether the homeostatic loss can be made fully local, and whether the convergence-to-equilibrium requirement limits EP's advantage over other biologically plausible algorithms on digital hardware.
