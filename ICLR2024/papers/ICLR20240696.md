## Problem

Existing molecule-text modeling approaches rely on 1D SMILES sequences or 2D molecular graphs, leaving 3D molecular conformations unexplored despite their critical role in understanding molecular dynamics, protein-ligand interactions, and 3D-dependent properties such as HOMO/LUMO energies. The paper identifies two key challenges for integrating 3D molecular perception into language models: aligning 3D molecular representations into the LM's textual input space, and developing instruction-tuning data that specifically targets 3D-dependent molecular understanding.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 3D atomic coordinates (generated via RDKit MMFF for pretraining or DFT from PubChemQC for evaluation), a 1D SMILES string, and a natural-language instruction prompt, which together form a mixed token sequence fed to Llama2-7B. The Q-Former distills the 3D encoder's per-atom representations into K=8 learnable query tokens that act as soft prompts alongside the SMILES and text tokens. Outputs include free-text captions, numerical property estimates (e.g., HOMO-LUMO gap in eV), descriptive QA answers, and cosine-similarity-based retrieval rankings over molecule-text pairs.

## Method

3D-MoLM couples a frozen Uni-Mol 3D encoder (pretrained on 209M conformations with invariant spatial positional encoding) to Llama2-7B via a Q-Former projector that uses cross-attention between K learnable query tokens and per-atom 3D features, initialized from Sci-BERT. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) multi-objective pretraining of the Q-Former with frozen Uni-Mol on 316K PubChem pairs using molecule-text matching, contrastive learning, and captioning objectives; (2) generative alignment where the full pipeline (Uni-Mol → Q-Former → Llama2) is trained for conditional text generation with LoRA (r=8, α=32) on the LM; (3) instruction tuning on the curated 3D-MoIT dataset, which combines GPT-3.5-enriched PubChem descriptions with PubChemQC's DFT-computed 3D-dependent properties (HOMO, LUMO, HOMO-LUMO gap, SCF energy) to sharpen 3D perception. The ablation study confirms that combining 3D molecular tokens with 1D SMILES in the prompt outperforms either alone, as SMILES preserves the LM's inherent 1D chemistry knowledge while 3D tokens focus on spatially-dependent properties.

## Results

On molecule-text retrieval (PubChem test set), 3D-MoLM† achieves 72.06% M2T accuracy, surpassing the best 2D baseline MoMu-K† (62.07%) by ~10 points. On molecule captioning, 3D-MoLM (specialist) reaches 31.23 ROUGE-L, outperforming the best 2D baseline MoMu-Large (24.76) by 6.47 points. On computed property QA (3D-MoIT test set), 3D-MoLM (specialist) achieves 0.28 eV MAE on HOMO-LUMO Gap and 0.35×10⁴ eV on SCF energy, substantially outperforming 2D-MoLM (0.67 eV and 0.71×10⁴ eV) and Llama2-7B (0.88 eV and 0.70×10⁴ eV), with the largest margins on 3D-dependent quantum properties.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3D-MoLM consistently outperforms all baselines across retrieval, captioning, and QA tasks. In retrieval (Table 2), 3D-MoLM† achieves 72.06% M2T accuracy on the PubChem test set, beating the best 2D baseline MoMu-K† (62.07%) by ~10 points and the best 1D baseline KV-PLM (42.80%) by ~29 points. In captioning (Table 3), 3D-MoLM (specialist) scores 31.23 ROUGE-L vs. 24.76 for MoMu-Large (best 2D) and 23.41 for MolT5-Large (best 1D specialist), while the generalist 3D-MoLM (30.02 ROUGE-L) still surpasses Llama2-7B (28.88) and 2D-MoLM (29.12). On computed property QA (Table 4c), the specialist 3D-MoLM achieves the lowest MAE on all eight properties; the advantage is most pronounced on 3D-dependent quantum properties (HOMO-LUMO Gap: 0.28 vs. 0.67 eV for 2D-MoLM; SCF: 0.35 vs. 0.71×10⁴ eV), while the gap narrows on composition-based properties (Weight: 14.79 vs. 21.48 g/mol). Compared to the non-LM baseline Uni-Mol, 3D-MoLM (specialist) achieves lower MAE on all properties except HOMO-LUMO Gap (0.28 vs. 0.21 eV), demonstrating that the LM's textual knowledge complements 3D structural perception. The GPT-3.5 enrichment variant (3D-MoLM) slightly outperforms the non-enriched variant (3D-MoLM†) on generation tasks but shows a small retrieval drop, attributed to a distribution shift between enriched pretraining texts and original test texts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the molecule-text modeling line (KV-PLM, MoMu, MolT5, MolCA) from 1D SMILES and 2D graph representations to 3D molecular conformations, drawing its architectural blueprint from BLIP-2's Q-Former (adapted from vision-language to 3D-molecule-language) and its instruction-tuning paradigm from InstructBLIP/LLaVA. The 3D molecular encoder (Uni-Mol) is treated as a frozen perception module, mirroring how vision-language models freeze image encoders, while the Q-Former serves as the cross-modal bridge. The key conceptual departure is that prior molecule-text work explicitly 'leaves 3D molecular structures untouched,' and this paper is the first to integrate 3D conformational geometry into a generative LM pipeline for text interpretation tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes a viable template for incorporating 3D structural information into language models, with the 3D-MoIT dataset (particularly its PubChemQC component for 3D-dependent properties like HOMO/LUMO) providing a resource the field lacked. The clear empirical separation between 3D-dependent and 1D/2D-inferable properties validates that 3D perception adds genuine information beyond what SMILES or graphs capture. However, the dataset scale (~300K pairs) is orders of magnitude below vision-language benchmarks, fine-grained structural discrimination remains weak (e.g., confusing isomers), and unexplored LM capabilities like in-context learning and chain-of-thought reasoning are left for future work, leaving open whether the approach scales to more complex biomolecular reasoning.
