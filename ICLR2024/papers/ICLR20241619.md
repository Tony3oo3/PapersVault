## Problem

Large LLMs deliver high response quality but require expensive cloud infrastructure, while smaller edge-deployable models are cheaper but produce lower-quality responses. Prior hybrid inference work (Kag et al., Ding et al.) addressed this tradeoff only for deterministic tasks like image classification, and multi-LLM approaches (ensembles, cascades) call multiple models per query, incurring high overhead. This paper is the first to explore quality-aware query routing between two LLMs for text generation, where the non-deterministic nature of LLM outputs and large performance gaps between models create unique routing challenges.

## Input / Output

The router (a DeBERTa-v3-large encoder, ~300M parameters) takes a single natural language query as input and produces a scalar score in [0,1] via one forward pass, which is compared against a user-defined threshold to decide routing. At test time, the threshold is the sole tunable knob: raising it routes more queries to the small model (greater cost savings) at the expense of quality. The selected model then generates the actual text response autoregressively, so the router adds negligible latency (~0.036s vs. 0.46–14.6s for the LLMs).

## Method

The router is trained with a binary cross-entropy loss where the target label estimates the probability that the small model's response quality (measured by BART score) meets or exceeds the large model's, i.e., Pr[q(S(x)) ≥ q(L(x))]. Three variants are proposed: r_det uses a single hard label per query; r_prob replaces it with a soft label estimated from 10 samples per model to capture LLM decoding randomness; and r_trans further relaxes the threshold to Pr[q(S(x)) ≥ q(L(x)) − t] for a t chosen by grid search to maximize pairwise label separation, which is critical when the large model dominates (e.g., FLAN-t5 800m vs. Llama-2 13b) and the standard signal is near-zero for ~90% of queries. The key insight is that the quality gap is a random variable due to non-deterministic generation, and the data transformation provides a stronger training signal in large-gap regimes where both r_det and r_prob fail to beat random routing.

## Results

On the MixInstruct benchmark, the proposed routers achieve up to 40% cost advantage (fraction of queries routed to the small model) with minimal quality drop measured in BART score. For the small performance gap pair (Llama-2 7b vs. Llama-2 13b), r_prob and r_trans achieve 20% cost advantage with 0% quality drop, and r_trans even achieves 40% cost advantage with only 0.0% drop. For the medium gap pair (Llama-2 13b vs. GPT-3.5-turbo), all routers achieve 20% cost advantage with ≤1% quality drop, and r_trans reaches 40% cost advantage with 2.9% drop. For the large gap pair (FLAN-t5 800m vs. Llama-2 13b), r_trans achieves 40% cost advantage with 10.3% quality drop, substantially outperforming r_det (13.8%) and r_prob (13.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three router variants (r_det, r_prob, r_trans) against three baselines: all-at-large (all queries to the large model), all-at-small (all queries to the small model), and random assignment. In small performance gap settings (Llama-2 7b vs. 13b), r_prob and r_trans actually beat the all-at-large baseline, achieving negative quality drops (i.e., better quality than always using the large model) at 10–20% cost advantage, because the small model occasionally outperforms the large model on easy queries. In the medium gap setting (Llama-2 13b vs. GPT-3.5-turbo), all three routers outperform random assignment, with r_trans achieving the lowest quality drop (2.9%) at 40% cost advantage versus 3.5% for r_det. In the large gap setting (FLAN-t5 800m vs. Llama-2 13b), r_det and r_prob perform only marginally better than random, while r_trans achieves a 3.5 percentage point improvement over r_det and 2.8 points over r_prob at 40% cost advantage, demonstrating that the data transformation is critical when the small model is significantly weaker. The router itself adds negligible latency (0.036s) compared to the LLMs it routes between (0.46s–14.61s).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hybrid inference paradigm introduced by Kag et al. (2022) and Ding et al. (2022) from image classification to LLM text generation, where the key challenges are non-deterministic outputs and the absence of a single ground-truth answer. Unlike prior hybrid inference work that trained small, large, and router models from scratch, this paper operates on pre-trained LLMs and introduces a probabilistic router that accounts for sampling variance, plus a data transformation to handle the extreme class imbalance that arises when the large model dominates the small model. The approach is distinct from ensemble methods (LLM-Blender) and cascades (FrugalGPT) that call multiple LLMs per query, and from speculative decoding which targets per-token speedup rather than whole-query routing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With LLM inference costs dominating deployment budgets, a router that achieves 40% cost savings with negligible quality loss is directly actionable for both platform operators and edge-cloud deployments. The paper identifies several open questions: generalizing to N-model routing with load balancing, out-of-distribution robustness when model pairs or data distributions shift, and task-aware routing signals beyond raw query text. A key limitation is that the router is trained per model pair and per data distribution, and the BART score proxy, while efficient, may not capture all dimensions of response quality that matter to end users.
