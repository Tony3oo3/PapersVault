## Problem

Estimating the k-residual error ||A − A_k||_F of a matrix (or ||x − x_k||_p of a vector) via a small sketch is a natural first step before committing to an expensive low-rank or sparse approximation. For bilinear sketches of the form SAT, the best known upper bound was O(k²/ε⁶) with no non-trivial lower bound, and for the vector case with p > 2, no sparse recovery algorithm or tight sketching dimension bound was known.

## Input / Output

For the matrix problem, A ∈ ℝ^{n×d} arrives entry-by-entry in a data stream, and the oblivious sketch matrices S ∈ ℝ^{s×n} and T ∈ ℝ^{d×t} (independent of A) must have dimensions depending only on k and ε, not on n or d; the output is a (1±ε) estimate of ||A − A_k||_F computed from the small product SAT. For the vector problem, a frequency vector x ∈ ℤ^n evolves via stream updates (i, w), and the algorithm outputs either a (1±ε) estimate of ||x − x_k||_p or a k-sparse vector x̂ satisfying ||x − x̂||_p ≤ (1+ε)||x − x_k||_p, using Õ(ε^{-2p/(p-1)} k^{2/p} n^{1-2/p}) space.

## Method

The key insight for the matrix case is that any projection-cost-preserving (PCP) sketch suffices: the authors set S = S₁S₂ where S₁ is a dense JL matrix of dimension O(k/ε²) and S₂ is a sparse CountSketch or OSNAP matrix, and apply the PCP property twice (once for S acting on A, once for Tᵀ acting on AᵀSᵀ) to show ||A−A_k||_F² ≤ ||SAT−[SAT]_k||_F² ≤ (1+O(ε))||A−A_k||_F², yielding a tight Θ(k²/ε⁴) bound. The matching lower bound is proved via Yao's minimax principle on two carefully constructed Gaussian matrix distributions that differ by a single rank-1 perturbation of magnitude Θ(√ε), showing that any bilinear sketch with s·t < Ω(k²/ε²) cannot distinguish them. For the vector case (p > 2), the algorithm runs CountSketch with b = Θ(ε^{-2p/(p-1)} k^{2/p} n^{1-2/p}) buckets to identify the top-k coordinates, then subtracts their estimated values from a parallel F_p moment estimator; a tiered decomposition of displaced coordinates (grouped by magnitude into O(log(k/ε)) buckets) shows the selection error is O(ε^{1-1/p})·||x_{-k}||_p^p. The sparse sketching matrices (OSNAP with s=2 nonzeros per column) give a 4–7× runtime speedup over the dense Gaussian sketches of Andoni & Nguyen (2013) at comparable accuracy.

## Results

The paper establishes a tight Θ(k²/ε⁴) bound on the size of bilinear sketches (SAT) for estimating the rank-k Frobenius residual ‖A−A_k‖_F, improving the previous O(k²/ε⁶) upper bound and providing the first non-trivial lower bound. For the vector ℓ_p (p>2) residual norm estimation, the paper gives an O(k^{2/p}·n^{1−2/p}·poly(log n)) sketch dimension upper bound with a matching Ω(k^{2/p}·n^{1−2/p}) lower bound for sparse recovery. Empirically, on both MovieLens 100K and KOS datasets, the proposed OSNAP-based sketch achieves nearly identical relative error (ε) to the Gaussian-based method of Andoni & Nguyen (2013) at the same sketch size, while running 4–7× faster (e.g., ~0.105 s vs. ~0.377 s at m=50, k=5 on MovieLens 100K).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole empirical baseline is the Gaussian bilinear sketch of Andoni & Nguyen (SODA 2013), which uses dense random Gaussian matrices S and T of dimension O(k/ε³). On both MovieLens 100K and KOS, the proposed OSNAP-based sparse sketch matches the AN13 error to within 0.001–0.01 in relative error ε at every tested (k, m) configuration, confirming that the tighter O(k²/ε⁴) sketch size does not sacrifice accuracy in practice. The decisive advantage is in runtime: the sparse OSNAP sketch is approximately 3.6× faster at m=50 (e.g., 0.105 s vs. 0.377 s on MovieLens 100K, k=5) and roughly 7× faster at m=100 (0.106 s vs. 0.735 s), because each column of the sketching matrix has only O(1) non-zero entries versus the fully dense Gaussian matrices. The paper also notes that the theoretical improvement is from O(k²/ε⁶) to Θ(k²/ε⁴) total sketch size, with a matching lower bound, whereas AN13 had no known lower bound beyond Ω(k + 1/ε²). For the vector ℓ_p (p>2) problem, the paper introduces the first sparse recovery algorithm with O(k^{2/p}·n^{1−2/p}·poly(log n)) measurements, for which no prior upper bound existed, and proves a nearly matching Ω(k^{2/p}·n^{1−2/p}) lower bound via a reduction from the Gap-infinity problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Andoni & Nguyen (SODA 2013), who introduced the bilinear sketch SAT framework for estimating the rank-k Frobenius residual ||A − A_k||_F with an O(k²/ε⁶) upper bound. The key methodological advance is recognizing that any projection-cost-preserving (PCP) sketch (Cohen et al., STOC 2015) suffices for the SAT construction, immediately yielding the improved O(k²/ε⁴) bound and enabling sparse sketching matrices (CountSketch, OSNAP) for faster updates. For the vector ℓ_p case (p > 2), the paper draws on the frequency-moment estimation literature (Alon et al., 1999; Andoni et al., 2011) and the Gap-infinity communication complexity framework (Bar-Yossef et al., 2004) to establish the first sparse recovery algorithms and a matching lower bound in this regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The tight Θ(k²/ε⁴) bound for bilinear sketches resolves a gap that had persisted since 2013 and provides the first non-trivial lower bound, giving the streaming-algorithms community a complete picture of the memory requirements for residual error estimation. The practical 4–7× speedup from sparse sketches over dense Gaussian sketches is immediately useful for practitioners who need to quickly assess whether a full low-rank computation is worthwhile. Open questions remain: the exact sketching dimension for vector residual norm estimation (as opposed to sparse recovery) for p > 2 is not fully resolved, and the polynomial dependence on n in the vector case leaves room for improvement. The paper does not address adaptive or non-oblivious sketches, which could potentially reduce the dimension further.
