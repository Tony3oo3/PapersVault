## Problem

SAM's plain ViT encoder lacks vision-specific inductive biases (local spatial priors) that are critical for dense prediction tasks, and its foreground-background segmentation pretraining impedes the encoder's ability to learn high-level semantic information needed for multi-class segmentation. The paper asks whether parameter-efficient fine-tuning can simultaneously reinforce local priors and restore high-level semantic learning while preserving SAM's extensive segmentation knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241057/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (processed at 1024×1024 resolution) as input and produces either a binary segmentation mask or pixel-wise multi-class class predictions. The prompt encoder is frozen with constant prompt tokens, converting SAM from a promptable model into an end-to-end segmentation model. For multi-class output, a lightweight classification branch is added to the mask decoder, and pixel-wise predictions are obtained by multiplying N mask tokens with their corresponding class scores.

## Method

Conv-LoRA inserts lightweight 3×3 convolutional layers inside LoRA's low-rank bottleneck (between the encoder W_e and decoder W_d), applied to Q, K, and V matrices in each self-attention layer. Each convolutional expert first upsamples features by a scale factor s_i, applies the convolution, then downsamples back to ViT's default scale, injecting local spatial priors at that specific resolution. A Mixture-of-Experts gating network (top-1 selection with noise-augmented softmax, following Shazeer et al.) dynamically chooses which scale expert to activate per input, with 8 experts covering scaling ratios from 1 to 8. The mask decoder is fully fine-tuned with an added classification branch using Mask2Former-style bipartite matching for multi-class prediction. Only ~4.02M parameters (0.63% of SAM) are trainable, maintaining the PEFT property while outperforming LoRA, VPT, Adapter, and other PEFT baselines across medical, natural, agriculture, and remote sensing benchmarks.

## Results

Conv-LoRA (4.02M trainable params, 0.63% of SAM) achieves the best performance across all four domains in binary semantic segmentation: 92.0 Sα on Kvasir, 77.6 Jac on ISIC 2017, 84.0 Fβ on CAMO, 2.54 BER on SBU, 74.5 IoU on Leaf, and 62.6 IoU on Road. In multi-class segmentation on Trans10K-v2 (12-class), Conv-LoRA reaches 67.09 mIoU, outperforming LoRA (66.01) and VPT (62.81) by a notable margin. The method uses only ~0.02M more parameters than LoRA while delivering consistent gains of 0.5–1.5 points across metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Conv-LoRA consistently outperforms all PEFT baselines (BitFit, Adapter, VPT, LST, SAM-Adapter, SSF, LoRA) across every dataset and domain. The closest competitor is LoRA, which Conv-LoRA surpasses by 0.5–1.5 points on most metrics (e.g., 77.6 vs 76.6 Jac on ISIC 2017; 84.0 vs 82.8 Fβ on CAMO; 2.54 vs 2.74 BER on SBU) with only 0.02M additional parameters. VPT and SSF trail LoRA by roughly 1–2 points on most benchmarks, while LST (11.49M params) underperforms all lighter methods, suggesting its heavier side-network design is counterproductive for SAM. In multi-class segmentation (Trans10K-v2, 12-class), Conv-LoRA achieves 67.09 mIoU vs LoRA's 66.01 and VPT's 62.81, with the gap widening on fine-grained categories like 'jar' (50.51 vs 42.17) and 'box' (72.84 vs 63.67). Conv-LoRA also surpasses several domain-specific models (e.g., PraNet on Kvasir, DeepLabv3 on Leaf) despite using far fewer trainable parameters, though it does not uniformly beat all specialized architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) by inserting lightweight convolutional operations into its bottleneck, repurposing the Mixture-of-Experts gating mechanism (Shazeer et al., 2017) to dynamically select the feature scale at which local spatial priors are injected. It is positioned as a parameter-efficient fine-tuning method specifically for SAM's ViT encoder, departing from prior PEFT-for-SAM work (SAM-Adapter, AutoSAM) by explicitly addressing two structural limitations: the absence of vision-specific inductive biases in plain ViT and the degradation of high-level semantic capacity caused by binary mask pretraining. The multi-class decoder modification borrows the bipartite matching strategy from Mask2Former (Cheng et al., 2022), and the approach is conceptually distinct from ConvPass and ViT-Adapter, which target classification or general dense prediction rather than SAM's specific architecture and pretraining regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, general-purpose PEFT recipe for adapting SAM across medical, agricultural, remote sensing, and natural image domains with only ~0.63% trainable parameters, and its linear probing analysis (54.2% vs. 67.7% ImageNet-1K accuracy for SAM vs. MAE encoders) offers a concrete diagnosis of why SAM struggles with multi-class semantic tasks. However, Conv-LoRA still does not consistently outperform domain-specific SOTA models, and the upscaling/downscaling operations introduce non-trivial computational overhead (1.22 iter/s vs. 1.64 for LoRA). Open questions include whether local priors can be injected without explicit feature rescaling, how to combine Conv-LoRA with other PEFT methods for further parameter reduction, and whether the MoE-based dynamic scale selection generalizes beyond segmentation to other dense prediction tasks.
