## Problem

DNN-based HDR deghosting methods require labeled data pairing dynamic multi-exposure inputs with HDR ground-truths, which are expensive and laborious to collect. Previous self-supervised alternatives (FSHDR, Nazarczuk et al.) construct synthetic pseudo-pairs whose motion and illumination characteristics diverge from real-world conditions, resulting in a noticeable performance gap versus supervised methods. This paper asks whether self-supervised HDR reconstruction can be achieved directly from real multi-exposure images without any synthetic data.

## Input / Output

The method takes three LDR images captured at different exposure values (e.g., −2, 0, +2 EV) as input and produces a single HDR image in the linear domain. A key design choice is that the approach is architecture-agnostic: the same self-supervised training pipeline is applied to four different backbones (AHDRNet, FSHDR, HDR-Transformer, SCTNet), each consuming the concatenated LDR and linear-domain versions of the three inputs. During inference, only the reconstruction network is deployed; the structure-focused network and optical flow alignment used during training are not needed.

## Method

SelfHDR decomposes the latent HDR ground-truth into two constructible supervisory components. The color component is obtained by fusing optically-flow-aligned multi-exposure images with triangle-shaped blending weights, capturing HDR color where alignment succeeds. The structure component is produced by a structure-focused network trained with a structure-preserving loss (against the medium-exposure image, masked to well-exposed regions via Λ₂) and a structure-expansion loss (against the color component, masked to well-aligned regions via a threshold on the tone-mapped difference). The reconstruction network is then trained with an L1 color-mapping loss (masked using the structure component to exclude ghosting regions) and a VGG-based perceptual structure-mapping loss. Elaborate binary masks at each stage prevent misaligned or saturated regions from corrupting supervision, and the two components are complementary rather than simply fusable.

## Results

On the Kalantari et al. dataset, SelfHDR with SCTNet achieves 43.95 PSNR-u / 0.9907 SSIM-u and 64.77 HDR-VDP-2, while SelfHDR with HDR-Transformer reaches 43.94 PSNR-u and 64.98 HDR-VDP-2 (the highest among all methods). Compared to the strongest prior self-supervised baseline (FSHDR_{K=0} at 42.17 PSNR-u), SelfHDR with the same FSHDR backbone gains 1.58 dB in PSNR-u. Against the corresponding fully-supervised counterparts, SelfHDR remains within 0.27–0.53 dB on PSNR-u, with SelfHDR_{HDR-Transformer} even surpassing all supervised methods on HDR-VDP-2 (64.98 vs. 64.63).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two prior self-supervised methods, SelfHDR clearly wins: with the same FSHDR backbone, SelfHDR_{FSHDR} achieves 43.80 PSNR-u versus FSHDR_{K=0}'s 42.17 (a 1.58 dB gain), and outperforms Nazarczuk et al. (42.15 PSNR-u, 63.99 HDR-VDP-2) by a wide margin on all metrics. Against the corresponding fully-supervised counterparts, SelfHDR is competitive: SelfHDR_{AHDRNet} (43.68) marginally exceeds supervised AHDRNet (43.63) on PSNR-u, and SelfHDR_{FSHDR} (43.80) beats supervised FSHDR (43.03) by 0.77 dB. The gap to the strongest supervised baselines is small—SelfHDR_{HDR-Transformer} trails supervised HDR-Transformer by only 0.27 dB on PSNR-u, and SelfHDR_{SCTNet} trails supervised SCTNet by 0.53 dB. Notably, SelfHDR_{HDR-Transformer} achieves the highest HDR-VDP-2 score (64.98) among all methods, surpassing even the supervised SCTNet (64.47) and FSHDR (64.79), suggesting superior perceptual quality despite the absence of ground-truth supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Kalantari et al. (2017) framework for multi-exposure HDR reconstruction, reusing their linear-domain mapping, tone-mapping operator, and pixel-level fusion weights. It departs from the dominant supervised paradigm (AHDRNet, HDR-Transformer, SCTNet) by eliminating the need for HDR ground-truths, and from prior self-supervised/few-shot approaches (FSHDR, Nazarczuk et al.) that synthesize pseudo-inputs and pseudo-targets, by instead extracting supervision directly from real multi-exposure images. The key conceptual move is decomposing the latent HDR ground-truth into a color component (fused from optically-flow-aligned inputs) and a structure component (produced by a separately trained structure-focused network), then using both to supervise a reconstruction network in an architecture-agnostic manner.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the need for costly HDR ground-truth collection, SelfHDR addresses a major practical bottleneck in HDR research and makes self-supervised training viable with only unlabeled dynamic multi-exposure sequences. The architecture-agnostic design means the method can be plugged into any existing reconstruction network, broadening its applicability. However, the method assumes noise-free and blur-free inputs and may fail under drastic irradiance changes, limiting its use in low-light or handheld scenarios. Open questions include whether the approach can be extended to handle sensor noise and motion blur simultaneously, whether adaptive base-frame selection improves robustness, and whether scaling to larger unlabeled datasets yields further gains.
