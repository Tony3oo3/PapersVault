## Problem

While Chow's rule (predicted class probability) is well-understood for classification cascades, naively extending it to generative LMs via sequence-level probability suffers from a length bias: Chow-Sum over-defers longer outputs and Chow-Average over-corrects by over-deferring shorter ones. No systematic study of deferral rules that exploit the richer token-level uncertainty inherent in autoregressive LMs existed prior to this work.

## Input / Output

The cascade first runs a text prompt through a small FLAN-T5 model (e.g., Base, 250M params) with greedy decoding, extracting the per-token probability vector of the generated sequence. These token probabilities are reduced to a fixed-length feature vector via quantiles (e.g., 0.0, 0.4, 0.8) and optionally augmented with the small model's final decoder embeddings (averaged over tokens) and the large model's first-token embedding from its first decoder layer. The output is a binary deferral decision (terminate with the small model's prediction or invoke the large model) followed by the generated text sequence from whichever model is selected.

## Method

The paper identifies that summing or averaging per-token log-probabilities (Chow-Sum, Chow-Average) masks critical token-level uncertainty, such as a few highly uncertain tokens buried among many predictable ones. To capture this, they propose Chow-Quantile, which computes the α-quantile of the per-token probability vector as the deferral score, with different quantiles capturing complementary uncertainty signals (e.g., α=0 detects repetitions and unknown tokens; α=0.8 flags short uncertain outputs). Since no single quantile works across all tasks, they train a post-hoc deferral rule—a 5-layer MLP with 32 hidden units—on the quantile features, using binary logistic loss (target: large model corrects small model's error) for classification tasks and squared loss on BLEURT score differences for translation. Further incorporating the large model's first-token intermediate decoder embedding as an additional input yields significant additional gains, particularly on classification tasks where answers are short.

## Results

The paper evaluates deferral rules for FLAN-T5 Base→Large cascades using AUC-DF (area under the deferral curve) across 18 NLP benchmarks. Post-Hoc-Quantile (a learned 5-layer MLP on token probability quantiles) is the most consistently performant non-embedding method, e.g., achieving 0.711 AUC-DF on MNLI (+10.4% over random) and 0.097 on TriviaQA (+15.47%). Adding the large model's first decoder-layer embedding (Post-Hoc-Embed-1+2) yields further gains, reaching 0.722 on MNLI (+12.24%) and 0.100 on TriviaQA (+19.56%). Chow-Quantile (fixed quantile) consistently outperforms Chow-Sum and Chow-Average, but no single quantile value is optimal across all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares seven deferral strategies: Chow-Sum (sum of per-token log-probs), Chow-Average (length-normalized log-prob), three fixed Chow-Quantile variants (α=0, 0.4, 0.8), Post-Hoc-Quantile (learned MLP on quantile features), and Post-Hoc-Embed-1+2 (learned MLP adding small-model decoder embeddings and large-model first decoder-layer embedding). Chow-Sum suffers from length bias (over-defers long outputs) while Chow-Average over-corrects (over-defers short outputs); both are consistently outperformed by Chow-Quantile. Post-Hoc-Quantile is the most consistently best non-embedding method, winning or tying on 14 of 17 datasets (e.g., +10.4% on MNLI, +15.47% on TriviaQA over random). Post-Hoc-Embed-1+2 achieves the best overall AUC-DF on 13 of 17 datasets, with the largest gains on classification tasks (e.g., +12.51% on ANLI-R1, +12.24% on MNLI) where the first token embedding is most informative. The only dataset where Post-Hoc-Embed-1+2 does not lead is WMT FR→EN, where Post-Hoc-Quantile edges it out by 0.001.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-established cascade deferral framework from classification (where Chow's rule based on predicted class probability is near-optimal) to generative language models, where the output is a variable-length token sequence rather than a single label. It builds directly on the post-hoc deferral rule training methodology of Narasimhan et al. (2022) and the broader LM cascade literature (Dohan et al., 2022; FrugalGPT, Chen et al., 2023b), but departs from prior work by identifying a fundamental length-bias pathology in the naive sequence-probability deferral score and proposing token-level quantiles as a richer uncertainty representation. The use of intermediate embeddings from the large model draws on observations from Schuster et al. (2022) and Ren et al. (2023) about the informativeness of early-layer representations, but applies them in the novel context of cascade deferral.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LM cascades are a practical, black-box strategy for reducing inference costs of large language models, and this paper provides the first systematic study of how to design deferral rules for generative (sequence-output) settings. The finding that no fixed quantile works across datasets, and that a learned post-hoc rule combining multiple quantiles consistently outperforms all simple aggregation strategies, gives practitioners a concrete recipe. The intermediate-embeddings result—where a single first-token embedding from the large model's first decoder layer yields significant additional gains—opens an interesting but underexplored direction. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to FLAN-T5 encoder-decoder models, potential distribution-shift sensitivity of the post-hoc rule, and the non-trivial (if reduced) cost of extracting intermediate embeddings from the large model.
