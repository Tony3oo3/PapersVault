## Problem

Comparing probability measures on the unit circle via Circular Optimal Transport (COT) requires solving a non-trivial optimization problem (finding the optimal cutting point or shift parameter α) for every pair of measures, making pairwise comparisons of many circular measures computationally expensive. This paper addresses the gap by introducing a linearized version of COT that eliminates the per-pair optimization, enabling efficient embedding-based comparison of circular measures while preserving the geometric structure of the circle.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240270/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes circular probability measures (densities or discrete empirical measures on S¹) and a fixed reference measure, typically the uniform (Lebesgue) measure on S¹. For each target measure ν, it produces a scalar-valued displacement function ν̂(x) = F_ν⁻¹(x − E(ν) + 1/2) − x defined on [0,1), which serves as the LCOT embedding. The LCOT distance between two measures is then simply the L²(S¹) norm of the difference of their embeddings, and the embedding is invertible via the pushforward (ν̂ + id)_# μ, enabling direct computation of barycenters and interpolations without additional optimization.

## Method

LCOT linearizes the Circular Optimal Transport metric by fixing a reference measure μ (the uniform distribution on S¹) and computing, for each target measure ν, the optimal Monge map M_μ^ν that pushes μ forward to ν. The key insight is that when μ is the uniform measure and the cost is quadratic, the optimal shift parameter α_{μ,ν} admits the closed-form solution E(ν) − 1/2, so the embedding ν̂(x) = F_ν⁻¹(x − E(ν) + 1/2) − x can be computed in O(N) time for an N-point discrete measure without any iterative optimization. The LCOT distance between ν₁ and ν₂ is defined as ‖ν̂₁ − ν̂₂‖²_{L²(S¹)}, and the paper proves this (raised to the 1/2 power) satisfies all axioms of a metric on P(S¹). This reduces pairwise comparison of K measures from O(K²N log(1/ε)) for COT to O(K²N + KN) for LCOT, and the invertible embedding enables direct computation of barycenters as the circular mean of embedded displacements followed by the exponential map.

## Results

LCOT achieves a computational complexity of O(K²N + KN) for pairwise distance computation among K circular measures with N points each, compared to O(K²N log(1/ε)) for COT, yielding substantial speedups that grow with K and N (Figure 4). In MDS visualizations (Figure 5), LCOT and COT both capture the circular translation geometry intuitively, while OT with a fixed cutting point and Euclidean distance fail to preserve the underlying structure. In the six-class separability experiment (Experiment 2), LCOT produces the most distinct cluster boundaries among all four metrics tested. The LCOT barycenter is computed in closed form via the inverse embedding, avoiding the iterative optimization required by COT barycenters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares LCOT against three baselines: COT (full circular optimal transport via optimization), OT with a fixed cutting point, and the Euclidean L² distance between densities. In computational time (Figure 4), LCOT with a uniform reference is consistently the fastest, with the gap widening as K increases; COT is the slowest due to the binary search for α at each pair. In MDS geometry (Experiment 1, Figure 5), LCOT matches COT in capturing the circular translation structure, while OT (fixed cut) and Euclidean produce distorted embeddings that do not reflect the circle's topology. In the six-class clustering experiment (Experiment 2, Figure 5 bottom row), LCOT achieves the clearest separation between the six von Mises / wrapped skew-normal classes, outperforming COT, OT, and Euclidean. For hue-based image retrieval (Appendix A.5.2), LCOT requires only N+M COT solves (N for dataset preprocessing + one per query) versus MN for COT, while producing visually comparable retrieval quality. Overall, LCOT matches or exceeds COT in geometric fidelity and clustering quality while offering a clear computational advantage, and decisively outperforms OT-with-fixed-cut and Euclidean distance on all qualitative metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

LCOT extends the Linear Optimal Transport (LOT) framework of Wang et al. (2013) and the Cumulative Distribution Transform (CDT) of Park et al. (2018) from Euclidean spaces to the unit circle, leveraging the closed-form solution of the 2-Wasserstein distance between a circular measure and the uniform distribution (α = E(ν) − 1/2). The circular OT problem itself was established by Delon et al. (2010) and Rabin et al. (2011), and the geometric interpretation of the embedding as a logarithmic map connects to the recent linearized OT on manifolds framework of Sarrazin & Schmitzer (2023). The key departure from prior work is the explicit closed-form embedding that eliminates the need for iterative optimization, reducing pairwise comparison from O(K²N log(1/ε)) to O(K²N + KN).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LCOT provides a practical, provably efficient metric for circular probability measures that enables direct application of standard ML algorithms (clustering, classification, MDS) via a linear embedding, with the added benefit of invertibility for barycenter computation without optimization. The computational speedup is particularly relevant for applications involving large collections of circular histograms (e.g., hue-based image retrieval, directional data in geology or astronomy). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to higher-dimensional manifolds (e.g., spheres) with comparable closed-form solutions, and whether the uniform reference is always optimal or whether adaptive reference choices could improve discriminative power.
