limitations: A-LOL requires a reasonable initial reference policy with good data coverage, as exclusively bad data would yield mostly negative-advantage instances; the single-action assumption precludes multi-turn credit assignment, and the value estimate is frozen throughout training rather than updated as the target policy diverges. ## Problem

RLHF via PPO is unstable, data-hungry, and requires continuous generation of new LM data at every training step, while preference-based offline methods (DPO, PRO) demand human-labeled pairwise comparisons and cannot handle multi-dimensional rewards. The paper asks whether rewarded learning comparable to PPO is possible using only pre-existing language data and arbitrary sequence-level scoring functions, without any online data generation or preference annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240549/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-existing input-output pairs (a single output per data point, not pairwise comparisons), a frozen reference LM, and one or more sequence-level scoring functions (pretrained classifiers or human-designed metrics) whose sum forms the reward. The entire output sequence is treated as a single action, which is what enables arbitrary attribute-specific classifiers to serve as rewards. It produces a finetuned LM generating text responses optimized for the composite reward, with the notable property that roughly 33% of training instances are automatically discarded based on negative advantage before any gradient step.

## Method

A-LOL derives its objective from off-policy policy gradients under a single-action episode assumption where the full output sequence constitutes one action and the episode ends upon receiving the sequence-level reward. The training loss multiplies three factors: a sequence-level advantage (reward minus a value estimate from a small multi-head attention + MLP head trained on the frozen reference LM's validation rewards via MSE), a clipped importance weight (ratio of target to reference LM sequence probabilities), and the standard NLL gradient. Data points with negative advantage are discarded entirely, and remaining instances are sampled with priority proportional to their positive advantage, making the method resilient to noisy or suboptimal training data. Variants include per-token importance weighting (A-LOL seq.), a KL penalty replacing the importance weight (A-LOL KL), and a reference-free version that sets the importance weight to 1. The entire method is implemented as a simple multiplicative modification to cross-entropy loss, requiring only a sequence-level reward and a single output per data point.

## Results

On the HHA benchmark (143K train, 8.2K test), A-LOL variants achieve 57.3–57.9 ± 0.3–0.5 average reward, comparable to DPO (58.6 ± 0.8) while exhibiting lower variance and the highest response diversity (A-LOL seq.: Distinct-1/2/3 = .105/.464/.727, closest to test good responses at .099/.471/.773). In GPT-4 pairwise evaluation, A-LOL seq. achieves 73.2% safe win-rate and 54.7% helpful win-rate against test good responses, surpassing DPO (60.2% safe, 41.2% helpful). On the multi-reward Reddit task, A-LOL seq. reaches 3.28/3.18 total reward on upvoted/downvoted data (vs. 2.93/2.87 reference), and on Commonsense Reasoning it improves COMET critic score from 84.6 to 93.0 (human: 93.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023), A-LOL variants achieve comparable average reward (57.3–57.9 vs. 58.6) but with substantially lower variance (±0.3–0.5 vs. ±0.8) and much higher diversity (A-LOL seq. Distinct-2 = .464 vs. DPO's .288); in GPT-4 evaluation, A-LOL seq. wins on both safety (73.2% vs. 60.2%) and helpfulness (54.7% vs. 41.2%). Against PPO (Schulman et al., 2017), A-LOL significantly outperforms on average reward (57.3–57.9 vs. 48.0) and generates far longer, more contextually relevant responses (40–44 tokens vs. 16.9 tokens), as PPO collapses to short safe-only outputs. Against PRO (Song et al., 2024), A-LOL improves average reward by ~3.5 points (57.6 vs. 54.1) with better diversity. Against wBC (Wang et al., 2020) and R GOLD (Pang & He, 2021), A-LOL gains ~2.5–3.4 reward points (57.3–57.9 vs. 54.5–54.8) while maintaining comparable or better diversity. On the multi-reward Reddit task, PPO hacks the reward by optimizing fluency/safety/TF-IDF while collapsing engagement and upvote probability (Distinct-1 drops to .012), whereas A-LOL seq. improves all five reward components simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

A-LOL derives its objective directly from the off-policy policy gradient (Degris et al., 2012) and borrows PPO's importance weight, clipping, and KL-penalty mechanisms, but reframes the entire output sequence as a single action rather than a token-level MDP. This single-action assumption is the key departure from prior offline RL for language (GOLD, wBC), enabling arbitrary sequence-level reward functions without per-token reward decomposition. The paper explicitly positions A-LOL as 'one-step PPO on a fixed rollout' (Appendix A), distinguishing it from preference-based offline methods (DPO, PRO) that require pairwise human comparisons and cannot handle multi-dimensional rewards. The 'leftover lunch' filtering—discarding negative-advantage training instances—is a novel practical twist not present in any of the cited baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A-LOL offers a drop-in replacement for cross-entropy loss that is sample-efficient (discarding ~33% of data), stable across seeds, and robust to noisy or suboptimal training data—properties that make it attractive for practitioners who have pre-collected data but lack the infrastructure for online PPO rollouts. The demonstrated ability to extract useful signal from downvoted Reddit comments and hallucination-heavy Wizard of Wikipedia data suggests practical value in settings where high-quality annotation is expensive or unavailable. Limitations include the frozen value estimate (never updated during training), the single-action assumption that precludes multi-turn credit assignment, and the requirement for a reasonable initial reference policy. Open questions include whether the advantage-based filtering generalizes to multi-turn dialogue or long-form generation where per-step rewards matter, and whether the frozen value estimate becomes a bottleneck as the target policy diverges significantly from the reference.
