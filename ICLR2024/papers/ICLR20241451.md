## Problem

Learning shared latent representations from paired covariance-based neuroimaging data (e.g., simultaneous EEG and fMRI) is challenging because existing deep CCA methods (DeepCCA, VICReg) operate in Euclidean space and discard the symmetry and positive-definiteness structure inherent to SPD matrices, while Riemannian CCA (RieCCA) preserves geometry but is non-deep and does not generalize to unseen data. No prior framework combines deep learning with the geometric structure of SPD manifolds to maximize correlation consistency between paired views in a self-supervised manner.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired views of multivariate time-series data (e.g., simultaneously recorded EEG and fMRI segments), which are first converted to covariance matrices on SPD manifolds of potentially different dimensions n₁ and n₂. A Dimension Adaptation module maps both views to a common dimension n, after which the network produces latent SPD matrix-valued representations Z_i and Z̄_i centered around the identity matrix e and constrained to lie near predefined geodesics exp_e(tv) and exp_e(tw). Performance is quantified by geodesic correlation (a new Riemannian correlation measure on the tangent space at e) and R² measuring adherence to the geodesic constraint.

## Method

DeepGeoCCA introduces geodesic correlation, a new measure defined as the Pearson correlation between Riemannian logarithms of orthogonal projections onto two arc-length geodesics through the identity matrix e, equipped with the affine-invariant Riemannian metric. The framework comprises three modules: a Dimension Adaptation network (SPDNet) to unify input dimensions, a Transformation network that learns non-linear maps centering data around e and projecting them near geodesics, and a Tangent Space Projection via SPDMBN + LOG layers. A key technical contribution is the ε-geodesic constraint, a relaxation that penalizes projections falling outside a double-cone region around the geodesic direction, with a theorem proving the tubular neighborhood width δ is upper-bounded by √((1−ε²)/ε²)·max g_e(log_e(Z_i), u), so ε→1 forces exact geodesic adherence. The overall loss combines a correlation term L_ρ (maximizing geodesic correlation), the relaxed geodesic penalty L_ε, and an optional VICReg-style variance-preserving term L_σ to prevent dimensional collapse, all optimized via matrix backpropagation through SPD-constrained layers.

## Results

On the simultaneous EEG-fMRI dataset (8 subjects, 10-fold CV), TSMNet + DeepGeoCCA achieves a Pearson correlation of 0.58 (±0.08) between learned EEG and fMRI latent representations, with R² = 0.55 (±0.02) confirming geodesic constraint adherence. This significantly outperforms the best non-geometric baseline (SPDNet + DeepCCA at 0.42 correlation) and the Riemannian non-deep baseline (RieCCA at 0.38 correlation). On the downstream motor imagery task, DeepGeoCCA pre-training yields statistically significant improvements over no pre-training (e.g., 66.26% vs. 62.97% on KU S1; 77.58% vs. 74.12% on BNCI S1), though gains over DeepCCA and VICReg pre-training are modest.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeepGeoCCA with TSMNet achieves the highest correlation (0.58) and competitive R² (0.55) on the EEG-fMRI task, significantly outperforming CCA (0.02), RieCCA (0.38), DeepCCA (0.29), and VICReg (0.38) on correlation, with p-values ≤ 0.002 for all comparisons. RieCCA, while geometry-aware, achieves near-zero R² (0.01), indicating its projections do not respect the geodesic structure, whereas DeepGeoCCA's ε-geodesic constraint yields R² of 0.55–0.58. On the motor imagery downstream task, DeepGeoCCA pre-training consistently outperforms the no-pre-training lower bound by 2–4% accuracy across both KU and BNCI datasets, but the advantage over DeepCCA and VICReg pre-training is small (≤1%) and not always statistically significant. The paper notes that RieCCA's higher correlation in simulations (vs. DeepGeoCCA) is attributable to having access to the full training set rather than mini-batches, and that the geometric structure of SPD matrices is essential—CCA on raw data yields near-zero correlation (0.02).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the canonical correlation analysis lineage (Hotelling → DeepCCA → RieCCA) to the specific setting of SPD matrix-valued neuroimaging data. The key conceptual departure from RieCCA (Kim et al., 2014) is the simplification from multi-dimensional geodesic submanifolds anchored at per-modality Fréchet means to single geodesics anchored at the identity matrix e, which the authors show is equivalent via parallel transport and yields a well-defined correlation measure (geodesic correlation) amenable to neural network optimization. The ε-geodesic constraint provides a practical relaxation that bridges the gap between the exact orthogonal projection constraint and what is tractable for gradient-based training, and the overall loss is derived from the Lagrangian of the constrained optimization problem. The variance-preserving term is borrowed directly from VICReg to prevent dimensional collapse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first fully data-driven self-supervised framework for simultaneous EEG-fMRI representation learning, removing the need for extensive domain-knowledge-based feature extraction or transcoder architectures. The geometric structure preservation (symmetry, positive definiteness) is a genuine advantage over Euclidean SSL methods for covariance-based data, and the controllable ε parameter offers a practical knob for trading off correlation against geodesic adherence. However, the EEG-fMRI experiment is limited to 8 subjects, the downstream motor-imagery improvements are statistically significant but modest in magnitude, and the single-geodesic-per-modality design restricts expressiveness. Open questions include whether the framework scales to larger multi-site datasets, whether multi-geodesic extensions recover the full RieCCA expressiveness, and whether the approach generalizes to other SPD-valued modalities (e.g., diffusion tensor imaging).
