## Problem

Tabular data pretraining has been largely overlooked compared to NLP, with existing approaches either textualizing numerical values (losing their intrinsic meaning), finetuning NLP-pretrained LLMs that are ill-suited to tabular structure, or requiring strict schema consistency between training and testing. Additionally, prior pretraining efforts on tabular data have been limited in scale and domain diversity, and neural methods still frequently underperform XGBoost in practical data science applications.

## Input / Output

Each table row is decomposed into individual cells, where every cell is represented as a (data type, column name, column value) triple processed independently by the TabUnit module. A free-form prompt (e.g., 'fill in missing value, <column name> :') is prepended to guide the decoder's generation. The model outputs are generated autoregressively as token sequences, supporting both discrete class labels (via constrained generation over a small vocabulary subset) and continuous numerical predictions, as well as free-text answers for table QA tasks.

## Method

UniTabE processes each cell through a TabUnit module that embeds the column name (mean-pooled), fuses a data-type embedding into it via a gated mechanism, and links the column name vector to each value token via a second gated linking layer—preserving the association between column identity and value within the orderless self-attention. All cell representations are concatenated with a [CLS] token and encoded by a multi-layer Transformer. A deliberately shallow 1-layer LSTM decoder, conditioned on the [CLS] hidden state and the prompt, generates the target sequence autoregressively; this architectural choice forces the encoder to absorb most pretrained knowledge rather than offloading it to the decoder. Pretraining combines two self-supervised objectives: multi-cell-masking (replacing entire cell contents with [MASK] and predicting them, with a 15% masking ratio) and contrastive learning (treating overlapping row subsets as positive pairs and non-overlapping subsets from different rows as negatives, using cosine similarity). The model is pretrained on a 13-billion-example dataset crawled from Kaggle across 303 domains, then finetuned with task-specific prompts for classification, regression, missing-value imputation, and table QA.

## Results

On 7 public tabular classification benchmarks (CG, CA, DS, AD, CB, BL, IO), UniTabE finetune achieves an average AUC of 0.83, outperforming all 13 baselines including XGBoost (0.79), Tapas (0.81), Llama2 13B (0.80), and FT-Transformer (0.80). On 12 Kaggle tasks spanning classification (AUC) and regression (R²), UniTabE finetune surpasses XGBoost on 10 of 12 tasks, with particularly large gains on regression (e.g., R² 0.99 vs. 0.64 on GPP, 0.96 vs. 0.78 on CCL). Even without pretraining (UniTabE scratch), the model matches or exceeds XGBoost on most tasks, and combining UniTabE's learned representations with XGBoost (UniTabE+XGBoost) yields an average AUC of 0.81, confirming the semantic value of the encoder.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniTabE finetune (avg AUC 0.83) outperforms all 13 baselines on the 7 public classification benchmarks. Against the strongest competitors—Tapas (0.81), Llama2 13B (0.80), GANDALF (0.80), and FT-Transformer (0.80)—UniTabE gains 2–3 points in average AUC, with the largest per-dataset margin on DS (0.66 vs. 0.65 for Llama2) and IO (0.76 vs. 0.71 for GANDALF/FT-Transformer). XGBoost (0.79) is beaten by 4 points on average; notably, UniTabE also wins on the Kaggle regression tasks where XGBoost is traditionally strong (e.g., R² 0.99 vs. 0.64 on Gold Price Prediction). TabNet is a clear outlier at 0.61 average AUC, underperforming even random-level baselines on several datasets. The LLM-based Llama2 13B (0.80) trails UniTabE by 3 points, confirming that general-purpose language models do not inherently excel at tabular prediction without domain-specific pretraining. UniTabE scratch (0.81) already matches Tapas, indicating that the TabUnit architecture itself provides substantial benefit independent of pretraining, while the pretraining adds a further 2-point gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniTabE carves new ground at the intersection of NLP pretraining paradigms and tabular data processing. It departs from the dominant textualization strategy (used by TransTab, TaBERT, Tapas, FT-Transformer) that converts numerical and categorical values into text tokens, instead introducing the TabUnit module that processes each cell as a typed key-value pair with gated data-type fusion. The shallow LSTM decoder design draws on the deep-encoder/shallow-decoder principle from neural machine translation (Kasai et al., 2020; Kong et al., 2022), repurposed here to force the Transformer encoder to absorb most pretrained knowledge. The multi-cell-masking objective generalizes token-level MLM to cell-level granularity, and the contrastive learning component adapts supervised contrastive learning (Khosla et al., 2020) from image patches to row-subset blocks in tables.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is one of the first demonstrations that a purpose-built tabular foundation model pretrained on 13B samples can consistently outperform XGBoost—the long-standing industry standard—across diverse classification and regression benchmarks, suggesting a viable path toward tabular foundation models analogous to LLMs in NLP. The schema-flexibility property (handling incremental columns without retraining) addresses a practical pain point in longitudinal data collection (e.g., clinical trials). Open questions include whether the approach scales to very wide tables (hundreds of columns), whether the 13B-sample dataset is sufficient for truly general-purpose tabular reasoning, and how the method performs on multi-modal tabular data (images, time series within cells). The modest margins over XGBoost on some datasets and the gap between zero-shot and finetuned performance suggest the pretraining signal may still be underexploited.
