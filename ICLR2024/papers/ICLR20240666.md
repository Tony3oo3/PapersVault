## Problem

Inverse optimal transport (iOT) infers a ground cost from samples of an entropic OT coupling, but the problem is ill-posed and requires regularization to handle sampling noise. While ℓ₁-regularized iOT has been proposed as a practical method, no rigorous theoretical guarantees existed for when it can stably recover the sparse support of the true cost. This paper fills that gap by deriving the first sparsistency theory for ℓ₁-iOT, establishing precise conditions under which the support is correctly identified from finite samples.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n iid pairs (x_i, y_i) drawn from the entropic OT coupling Sink(c_A, ε), along with the entropic regularization parameter ε and the ℓ₁ penalty λ. The cost is restricted to a linear parameterization c_A(x,y) = Σ A_k C_k(x,y) (e.g., Mahalanobis form x^T A y), with centering constraints on the basis functions to eliminate translation invariances. The output is the estimated sparse cost matrix A_n and its support, with the key guarantee being that Supp(A_n) = Supp(Â) with high probability when n is sufficiently large relative to exp(C‖Â‖₁/ε)·log(1/δ)/λ².

## Method

The core contribution is a certificate z_A* defined via the Hessian of the maximum likelihood functional W(A) — specifically, z_A* = ∇²W(A)_{(:,I)}(∇²W(A)_{(I,I)})^{-1} sign(A)_I — which generalizes the Lasso's irrepresentability condition to the iOT setting where the loss is a Fenchel-Young loss rather than a quadratic. Theorem 5 proves that if this certificate is non-degenerate (‖z_A*_{I^c}‖_∞ < 1), then the ℓ₁-iOT estimator is sparsistent with n^{-1/2} sample complexity, with the proof relying on strong convexity of the Kantorovich dual and concentration inequalities for the finite-sample problem. For Gaussian marginals, closed-form Hessian expressions (Lemma 7) reveal that iOT interpolates between the graphical Lasso (ε→0, recovering conditional independence structure) and the classical Lasso (ε→∞, recovering marginal independence), establishing a concrete link between iOT and graph estimation.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution (Theorem 5) establishes a sample complexity of n^{-1/2} for sparsistency of the ℓ₁-iOT estimator, requiring n ≳ max(exp(C‖Â‖₁/ε)·log(1/δ)·λ⁻², log(2s)) samples. Numerically, for a circular graph with n=80 vertices, sparsistency (zero wrongly estimated coordinates) is unattainable at ε=0.1 due to a degenerate certificate, but is achieved for larger ε values once the sample size N is sufficiently large. In the Gaussian setting, the paper proves that iOT interpolates between the Graphical Lasso (ε→0, recovering conditional independence structure) and the classical Lasso (ε→∞, recovering marginal independence), with the certificate (C) reducing to the respective known certificates in each limit.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without direct method-to-method benchmark comparisons. Instead, the paper positions ℓ₁-iOT relative to two well-studied statistical procedures: the classical Lasso and the Graphical Lasso. Proposition 8 shows that as ε→∞, the iOT certificate converges to the Lasso certificate and the optimization problem reduces to a weighted Lasso, meaning sparsity in A encodes marginal independence between source and target. Proposition 9 shows that as ε→0 (with symmetric positive-definite A and identity covariances), the problem reduces to the Graphical Lasso, where sparsity in A encodes conditional independence. Numerically (Figure 2), the ℓ₁-iOT solver achieves perfect support recovery (0 errors) for ε=0.5 and ε=1.0 with sufficient samples, while failing entirely at ε=0.1, confirming the theoretical prediction that small ε makes the problem ill-posed for sparse recovery. The paper also notes that pairs of vertices at small geodesic distance on the graph are the most likely to be degenerate (i.e., hardest to recover), highlighting a geometric regularity property of iOT along graph structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first rigorous sparsistency analysis for ℓ₁-regularized Inverse Optimal Transport (iOT), building directly on the convex formulation of iOT by Dupuy & Galichon (2014) and the SISTA ℓ₁-penalized solver of Carlier et al. (2023). The theoretical framework generalizes the classical Lasso irrepresentability condition (Zhao & Yu, 2006; Tibshirani, 1996) to the iOT setting, where the 'correlation' structure is governed by the Hessian of the entropic OT value function rather than a fixed design matrix. The sample complexity proof adapts concentration techniques from the entropic OT literature (Rigollet & Stromme, 2022), while the Gaussian closed-form analysis relies on the explicit coupling formula of Bojilov & Galichon (2016).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key conceptual contribution is showing that iOT interpolates between the graphical Lasso (ε→0, recovering conditional independence structure) and the classical Lasso (ε→∞, recovering marginal independence), establishing a previously unrecognized bridge between inverse optimal transport and graph estimation. This provides practitioners with a principled understanding of when ℓ₁-iOT will succeed or fail: the certificate degenerates at small geodesic distances for small ε, explaining the observed instability. Open questions include extending the analysis to general structured convex regularizers (e.g., nuclear norm for low-rank costs), handling non-Gaussian marginals in closed form, and understanding the finite-sample behavior in high-dimensional regimes where s grows with n.
