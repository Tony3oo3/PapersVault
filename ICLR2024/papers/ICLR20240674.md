## Problem

Graph Transformers (GTs), particularly Global GTs, struggle with semi-supervised node classification due to their large parameter count and lack of local inductive bias, leading to overfitting under severe label scarcity. While GTs excel at graph-level tasks, their node-level performance lags behind standard GNNs like GCN, and no effective training strategy has been proposed to unlock the potential of GGTs in this regime.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph defined by an adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{N×N} and a node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×d_x}, along with labels for a small subset of training nodes V_L (e.g., 140 nodes on Cora, 60 on Pubmed). It outputs a probability distribution over C classes for every node in V \ V_L. The distillation operates on per-layer attention coefficient matrices of shape N×N, where the teacher's in-class region is restricted to each node's first-order neighborhood (including itself), while the student attends globally over all N nodes.

## Method

The method pairs a pre-trained Local GT (LGT, restricted to first-order neighborhood attention) as teacher with a Global GT (GGT, full self-attention) as student, performing layer-to-layer attention coefficient distillation only over the in-class (neighborhood) region. Two auxiliary mechanisms shape the student's out-of-class behavior: (1) an out-of-class entropy term that minimizes the entropy of normalized out-of-class attention, simultaneously incentivizing the student to allocate attention beyond the neighborhood and to concentrate it sparsely on a few relevant distant nodes; and (2) top-k pruning that zeros out all but the k largest out-of-class attention coefficients before computing entropy, preventing overfitting from normalizing over many irrelevant nodes. A curriculum schedule then dynamically trades off these forces: the distillation weight γ follows a cosine-decreasing schedule (strict teacher guidance early, relaxed later) while the out-of-class entropy weight β follows a cosine-increasing schedule (minimal exploration early, growing autonomy later), both active over a tunable epoch interval [ep_begin, ep_end].

## Results

The proposed curriculum-enhanced attention distillation method achieves the best node classification accuracy across all seven benchmark datasets: 84.70% on Cora, 76.40% on Citeseer, 83.10% on Pubmed, 39.40% on Actor, 77.03% on Cornell, 85.58% on Texas, and 81.95% on Wisconsin. The method yields a 6.70% relative improvement over GCN on Citeseer and a 34.27% relative improvement over Graphormer on Cora. Compared to the unguided GGT student (54.24% on Cora), the distilled student achieves a 56.16% relative improvement, while also surpassing the LGT teacher (77.40%) by 6.61% on average across all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all ten baselines on every dataset. Among GNN baselines, it surpasses the strongest (APPNP at 84.10% on Cora) by 0.60% and GCN (82.06%) by 2.64% on Cora, with the largest GNN gap on Citeseer (76.40% vs. 72.50% for SuperGAT_SD, a 3.90% absolute gain). Among Graph Transformer baselines, the gains are substantially larger: over Graphormer (63.08% on Cora, OOM on Pubmed/Actor), over NodeFormer (79.02% on Cora, 77.84% on Texas), and over ANS-GT (77.68% on Cora, 74.92% on Cornell). The method also outperforms the LGT teacher model by 6.61% on average and the unguided GGT student by 13.50% on average, confirming that the distillation framework enables the student to surpass both. In the distillation-method comparison (Table 8), the proposed attention distillation outperforms KD (Hinton et al.) by 6.39% and FitNets (Romero et al.) by 7.14% on average across all seven datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the layer-to-layer attention distillation concept from NLP (MobileBERT) to the graph domain, but departs fundamentally from the standard knowledge distillation paradigm of compressing a large teacher into a small student. Instead, it inverts the capacity relationship: a weaker Local GT (limited to first-order neighborhood) guides a stronger Global GT (full attention over all nodes), using attention coefficients as the knowledge medium. The in-class/out-of-class decomposition and the out-of-class entropy loss are novel constructs specific to the graph structure, and the curriculum scheduling of the distillation strength (γ) and exploration drive (β) represents a new integration of curriculum learning with distillation that differs from prior temperature-based curriculum distillation in NLP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap—GTs consistently underperform GNNs on semi-supervised node classification—and provides a principled, interpretable explanation (attention coefficients encode 'higher-level knowledge understanding') for why distilling them helps. However, the experimental scope is limited to seven small-to-medium graphs and uses only the most naive GT (Dwivedi & Bresson) and vanilla Transformer as the actual models, leaving open whether the approach transfers to more sophisticated architectures (Graphormer, NodeFormer) or scales to large graphs. The 'weaker teacher guides stronger student' paradigm is conceptually appealing but its generality beyond the specific LGT→GGT pairing remains untested.
