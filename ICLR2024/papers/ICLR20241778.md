## Problem

The randomized midpoint discretization of the kinetic Langevin diffusion is known to be the most scalable sampling method for strongly log-concave targets in high dimensions with large condition numbers, but existing non-asymptotic W₂-error bounds contain an extraneous √(nmh) factor, require initialization at the potential's minimizer, and lack explicit constants. This paper closes that gap by deriving tighter, fully computable upper bounds that remove the √(nmh) dependence and do not require minimizer initialization.

## Input / Output

The algorithms consume only the gradient ∇f of a twice-differentiable potential satisfying mI ≼ ∇²f ≼ MI, along with a step size h, friction parameter γ, and iteration count n; no Hessian or higher-order derivatives are needed. The output is a sequence of samples whose distribution is guaranteed to be within ε√(p/m) in W₂-distance of the target π ∝ exp(−f), with the number of gradient evaluations made explicit as a function of (ε, κ, p). A key practical detail is that the bounds hold for arbitrary initialization θ₀ (not just the minimizer θ*), with the initialization dependence appearing as an explicit exponential decay term ρⁿW₂(ν₀, π).

## Method

The core technical contribution is a summation-by-parts argument that replaces the standard telescoping-sum technique used in prior RKLMC analyses. Specifically, when the one-step error satisfies a recurrence a_{n+1} ≤ (1−α)a_n + b_n coupled with a Lyapunov-type inequality c_{n+1} ≤ c_n − b_n + C, the classical telescoping bound yields a residual nC, whereas summation by parts converts this to C/α and improves the initialization dependence from c₀ to (1−α)ⁿc₀. The authors first develop and validate this technique on the vanilla Langevin diffusion (RLMC, Theorem 1), then extend it to the kinetic Langevin diffusion with randomized midpoint (RKLMC, Theorem 2) and with Euler-Maruyama discretization (KLMC, Theorem 3). Bounding the resulting discounted sums of squared gradients relies on the Polyak-Lojasiewicz inequality and a potential-decay argument, yielding the final W₂ bounds with small explicit constants and optimal κ-dependence (κ^{1/3} for RKLMC, κ^{1/2} for KLMC).

## Results

The paper provides improved non-asymptotic W₂-error upper bounds for three Langevin Monte Carlo variants under strong log-concavity. For RLMC (Theorem 1), the bound is W₂ ≤ 1.11e^{−mnh/2}W₂(ν₀,π) + (2.4√(κMh)+1.77)Mh√(p/m), removing the √(nmh) factor present in prior work. For RKLMC (Theorem 2), the discretization error scales as O((γh)^{3/2}√(p/m)) with explicit small constants and no requirement to initialize at the potential minimizer. For KLMC (Theorem 3), the iteration complexity improves from O(κ^{3/2}/ε) to O(κ/ε) in the condition number dependence. In Table 1, RKLMC requires the fewest iterations for κ ≥ 10⁵ (e.g., 1.1×10⁸ vs. 1.2×10⁸ for LMC at ε=0.1, κ=10⁵, and 4.5×10⁹ vs. 2.2×10¹² for LMC at ε=0.1³, κ=10⁵).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LMC (Durmus et al., 2019), RLMC achieves a strictly better complexity bound in the regime κ = o(ε⁻⁴): at ε=0.1, κ=10¹, RLMC needs 3.6×10³ iterations versus 1.2×10⁴ for LMC, but the advantage erodes at large κ where RLMC's κ^{4/3} dependence overtakes LMC's κ dependence. Against the original RKLMC analysis (Shen & Lee, 2019), this paper removes the √(nmh) factor from the discretization error, provides explicit small constants (e.g., 0.2(γh)³ and 10(γh)^{3/2} versus unspecified C), and eliminates the requirement to initialize at the potential minimizer. Against He et al. (2020) for RLMC, the same √(nmh) removal applies, yielding a bound that is independent of the number of iterations in the steady-state error term. Against KLMC (Cheng et al., 2018b), Theorem 3 improves the condition-number dependence from O(κ^{3/2}/ε) to O(κ/ε) iterations; numerically, KLMC is the worst performer in Table 1 across all settings (e.g., 8.4×10⁹ vs. 1.1×10⁸ for RKLMC at ε=0.1, κ=10⁵). In the numerical experiments (Figure 1), the randomized midpoint variants (RLMC, RKLMC) outperform their Euler counterparts (LMC, KLMC) for moderate condition numbers, while KLMC becomes competitive only at very large κ.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the randomized midpoint discretization for the kinetic Langevin diffusion introduced by Shen & Lee (2019) and the probabilistic analysis of He et al. (2020), as well as the continuous-time kinetic Langevin framework of Dalalyan & Riou-Durand (2020). The key departure is a novel proof technique based on summation by parts (borrowed from convex optimization convergence proofs) that eliminates the √(nmh) factor present in all prior discretization error bounds for both RLMC and RKLMC. The paper also unifies the analysis of vanilla LMC, RLMC, KLMC, and RKLMC under a single framework, yielding an improved condition-number dependence (κ vs. κ^{3/2}) for the Euler-discretized kinetic Langevin compared to prior work by Cheng et al. (2018) and Dalalyan & Riou-Durand (2020).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The removal of the √(nmh) factor and the explicit dependence on initialization make these bounds more practical and a necessary step toward extending randomized midpoint guarantees to non-convex potentials, where geometric ergodicity no longer holds. The improved κ-dependence for KLMC narrows the gap between Euler and midpoint discretizations, though the authors conjecture that non-synchronous couplings could further reduce the iteration complexity from κ^{3/2}/ε to κ/ε. Limitations include the requirement γ ≥ 5M (stronger than the γ ≥ 2M in prior KLMC work), the reliance on strong convexity for contraction, and the fact that numerical experiments are limited to p=3, leaving open whether the theoretical ordering of algorithms holds in genuinely high-dimensional settings.
