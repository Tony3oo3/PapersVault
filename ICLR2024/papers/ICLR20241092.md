## Problem

Existing graph generative models scale poorly to large graphs because they must model the joint distribution over all O(n²) node pairs, and they struggle to capture both global and local structure simultaneously. Moreover, no prior method has demonstrated the ability to extrapolate to graph sizes outside the training distribution while preserving structural characteristics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241092/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target graph size (number of nodes) as input and produces an unattributed undirected graph. Internally, the model operates on binary node features (cluster size vectors indicating how many nodes each current node expands into) and binary edge features (selection vectors indicating which edges to retain during refinement), both encoded as ±1 continuous values for the diffusion process. The generation proceeds from a single-node graph through L expansion-refinement steps, with the number of steps and intermediate sizes determined by the coarsening sequence.

## Method

The paper introduces iterative local expansion: starting from a single node, the graph is built by repeatedly expanding each node into a cluster of nodes (creating a locally dense expanded graph) and then refining it by selectively removing edges, with each step modeled by a denoising diffusion process over the joint node-and-edge feature vector. The training objective inverts a spectrum-preserving graph coarsening sequence (using Loukas's Local Variation Cost to select contraction sets), yielding a variational lower bound on the graph likelihood. The denoising model is parameterized by a novel Local PPGN architecture that performs edge-wise message passing over triangles, achieving PPGN-level expressivity on locally dense subgraphs while maintaining linear complexity in the number of edges for globally sparse graphs. Spectral conditioning via SignNet embeddings of the coarser graph's Laplacian eigenvectors guides each expansion step, and a deterministic expansion size strategy with reduction fraction inputs enables precise control over the final graph size.

## Results

On the planar graph benchmark (n=64), the method achieves a V.U.N. score of 95.0 and a structural MMD ratio of 2.1, outperforming DiGress (77.5, 5.1) and SPECTRE (25.0, 3.0). On tree graphs, it reaches a perfect V.U.N. of 100 with a ratio of 4.0, surpassing GraphGen (95.0, 33.2) and BiGG (75.0, 5.2). On real-world protein graphs (n≈258), it achieves the best structural ratio of 5.9 versus DiGress (18.0) and SPECTRE (19.0), and on point cloud graphs (n≈1332) it attains a ratio of 7.0 while several baselines (GraphRNN, SPECTRE, DiGress, Ours one-shot) fail entirely due to out-of-memory errors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On planar graphs, the proposed method achieves the highest V.U.N. score (95.0) and best structural ratio (2.1), clearly outperforming DiGress (77.5, 5.1), SPECTRE (25.0, 3.0), and BiGG (5.0, 16.0); autoregressive methods (GraphRNN, GRAN) and bandwidth-restricted methods (EDGE, BwR) produce mostly invalid graphs. On tree graphs, it achieves a perfect V.U.N. of 100 with a ratio of 4.0, beating GraphGen (95.0, 33.2) and DiGress (90.0, 1.6) on validity while DiGress edges it out slightly on the ratio metric. On the SBM dataset, DiGress is the strongest competitor (V.U.N. 60.0, ratio 1.7) and outperforms the proposed method (45.0, 10.2), which the authors attribute to the dataset's lack of fine-grained structure. On protein graphs (n≈258), the method achieves the best ratio (5.9) versus DiGress (18.0) and SPECTRE (19.0), while on point cloud graphs (n≈1332) it is the only method alongside GRAN and BiGG that completes without OOM, achieving a ratio of 7.0 compared to GRAN's 18.8 and BiGG's 38.8.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three established threads: (1) the SDE-based denoising diffusion framework of Song et al. (2021) with Karras et al.'s (2022) preconditioning and Heun sampling, (2) the PPGN architecture of Maron et al. (2020) adapted into a locally efficient variant (Local PPGN) that retains 3-WL expressivity on dense subgraphs while scaling linearly in edges on sparse graphs, and (3) spectrum-preserving graph coarsening from Loukas (2018) used as the forward process to invert. The key conceptual departure from prior graph generation work (autoregressive, one-shot diffusion, hierarchical) is the iterative local expansion paradigm: rather than modeling the full joint distribution over all O(n²) node pairs or building node-by-node, the model learns to reverse a coarsening sequence step-by-step, expanding a few nodes at a time with a local diffusion model. Self-conditioning from Chen et al. (2023a) and spectral conditioning via SignNet from Martinkus et al. (2022) are incorporated as auxiliary improvements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most impactful contribution is that this is the first graph generative model to reliably extrapolate to unseen graph sizes while preserving structural statistics, a capability no prior method (autoregressive, one-shot diffusion, or hierarchical) has demonstrated. The subquadratic scaling for sparse graphs, validated empirically up to 5037 nodes on point clouds, opens the door to generating large-scale relational structures that were previously intractable. Limitations include evaluation of OOD generalization on only two synthetic datasets (planar, tree), the fact that the one-shot baseline occasionally outperforms the iterative version on structureless data (SBM), and the restriction to unattributed graphs. Open questions include whether the approach extends to attributed or directed graphs, whether the spectral conditioning is necessary at scale, and how the method behaves on graphs with very heterogeneous degree distributions.
