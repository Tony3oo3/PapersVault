## Problem

Pretrained VLMs can generate high-level semantic plans but struggle to reason about physical dynamics (object motion, collisions, spatial constraints), while text-to-video models capture low-level visual dynamics but are limited to short-horizon clips. No existing approach combines both capabilities to produce coherent long-horizon video plans (hundreds of frames) that respect scene physics and can be executed by real robots.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241227/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

VLP takes a single RGB image observation of the current scene and a natural language goal instruction (e.g., 'group blocks by color') and produces a long-horizon video plan of hundreds of frames at 192×320 resolution, segmented into 16-frame clips, each accompanied by a generated language subgoal. The video plan is then converted into low-level control actions via a goal-conditioned policy that is queried on each intermediate frame, with a receding-horizon replanning strategy for very long tasks.

## Method

VLP performs a forward tree search (parallel hill climbing) over the joint space of language actions and video rollouts using three submodules: a fine-tuned 12B PaLM-E VLM as a policy that proposes A candidate text actions per step, a text-to-video diffusion model (trained at 24×40 base resolution, upsampled to 192×320) as a dynamics model that synthesizes D candidate 16-frame video rollouts per action, and the same PaLM-E VLM fine-tuned as a heuristic function that predicts remaining steps-to-goal to score and prune branches. The search maintains B parallel plan beams, expanding each by selecting the highest-heuristic video among A×D candidates per step, and periodically replaces the lowest-value beam with a copy of the best beam. A heuristic clipping threshold prevents the planner from exploiting physics violations (teleporting objects, disappearing objects) in the video model. Execution uses a goal-conditioned policy (LAVA architecture with a ResNet goal-image encoder) trained to predict control actions from (current frame, target frame) pairs, applied to every frame of each synthesized video segment.

## Results

On the Language Table execution benchmark (1500 timesteps), VLP achieves 87.3 reward and 64% completion on Move to Area, 95.8 reward and 92% completion on Group by Color, and 65.0 reward and 16% completion on Make Line. For video plan synthesis quality (Table 1), VLP achieves 58–98% accuracy on simulated tasks and 78–100% on real tasks, compared to 2–12% for UniPi. VLP also scales with compute: increasing beam width from 1 to 2 and branching factor from 4 to 16 raises Make Line completion from 0% to 16% (Table 3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLP substantially outperforms all four baselines across every task and metric. Against UniPi (text-to-video without planning), VLP improves Group by Color completion from 4% to 92% and Move to Area completion from 0% to 64%, demonstrating the critical role of the tree-search planning loop and value function. Against LAVA (behavioral cloning on long-horizon text), VLP achieves 92% vs. 2% completion on Group by Color and 64% vs. 22% on Move to Area, showing that hierarchical planning with video dynamics far exceeds end-to-end imitation. Against RT-2 (vision-language-action transformer fine-tuned on long-horizon tasks), VLP dominates on Move to Area (87.3 vs. 18.5 reward) and Group by Color (95.8 vs. 46.0 reward), though RT-2 is the closest competitor on Make Line (36.5 vs. 65.0 reward). Against PaLM-E (VLM planning in language space), VLP achieves 92% vs. 2% completion on Group by Color and 64% vs. 0% on Move to Area, confirming that video-based dynamics modeling is essential for long-horizon execution where pure language planning fails to capture physical constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

VLP composes three existing model families—PaLM-E (as VLM policy and heuristic), a text-to-video diffusion model (as dynamics model, following UniPi), and a goal-conditioned policy (following LAVA)—into a unified forward tree search procedure for long-horizon planning. This departs from language-only planning approaches (SayCan, Inner Monologue) that cannot simulate visual dynamics, and from UniPi's direct long-horizon video generation which lacks any search or value-based pruning. The closest prior work, HiP (Ajay et al., 2023), also combines language, video, and action models but uses hierarchical one-step-at-a-time planning rather than the forward beam search over video sequences that VLP employs, limiting its ability to reason about long-horizon consequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VLP demonstrates that test-time compute (tree search over generated videos) can dramatically improve long-horizon robot task completion, with a clean scaling law where more branching and beams yield better plans. The key open question is whether the 30-minute-per-plan latency can be reduced (via distillation or faster video generation) to make this practical for real-time deployment, and whether the video model's physics errors (object teleportation, appearance/disappearance) will persist at scale or be resolved by larger models. The decomposition into visual goal generation plus goal-conditioned execution also raises the broader question of whether this 'plan in video space, execute with a local policy' paradigm generalizes beyond tabletop manipulation to more complex 3D and contact-rich settings.
