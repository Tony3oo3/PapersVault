## Problem

Existing self-supervised pretraining methods for 3D perception either target a single modality (LiDAR or camera) or, in the case of SimIPU, apply cross-modal contrast in pixel space and fail to transfer to BEV architectures due to the implicit pixel-to-BEV transformation. No unified pretraining framework exists that jointly learns region-level and scene-level representations across both LiDAR and camera backbones for multimodal BEV perception.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240158/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pretraining stage consumes unlabeled LiDAR point clouds (32-beam scans aggregated over up to 10 consecutive frames) and 6 monocular camera images covering 360° FoV. Each LiDAR point is augmented with a 4th dimension encoding its DBSCAN-assigned region ID (or -1 for semantic-less points). The pretrained f_LiDAR and f_Camera backbones are then fine-tuned with CenterPoint or ConvNet heads to produce 3D bounding boxes (10 foreground classes) or BEV map segmentation masks (6 background classes) over a 100m×100m ego-vehicle plane.

## Method

CALICO operates in two sequential stages. First, Point-Region Contrast (PRC) pretrains the LiDAR backbone using a top-down DBSCAN semantic pooling that clusters non-ground points into object-aligned regions, then applies two complementary InfoNCE losses: PLRC (point-level region contrast that uses semantic-less points as additional negatives to relieve class collision) and RAPC (region-aware point contrast that concatenates channel-wise max-pooled region features with each point embedding), balanced by a hyperparameter α. Second, Region-Aware Distillation (RAD) pretrains the camera backbone via contrastive distillation from the frozen LiDAR teacher, where each region's point-level embeddings are normalized by the number of points in that region to prevent large but semantically uninformative clusters from dominating the loss. This two-stage design avoids the gradient interference problem of simultaneous cross-modal contrast and specifically addresses the fact that the implicit LSS pixel-to-BEV transformation renders initial camera features uninformative for direct contrastive learning.

## Results

On nuScenes 3D object detection with 5% labeled data, CALICO (LiDAR+Camera) achieves 47.9 NDS and 41.7 mAP, outperforming the LiDAR-only random initialization baseline (37.4 NDS, 33.1 mAP) by 10.5% and 8.6% respectively. The LiDAR-only PRC component alone reaches 46.0 NDS and 38.2 mAP at 5%, surpassing PointContrast (43.0/36.7) and ProposalContrast (43.1/37.0). On Waymo with 20% data, CALICO achieves 71.6 AP and 68.0 APH, improving over the random initialization baseline by 8.4% and 3.2%. For BEV map segmentation at 5% data, CALICO attains 42.0 mIoU versus 36.3 for the baseline, a 5.7% gain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CALICO consistently outperforms all compared baselines across every data fraction. Against LiDAR-only pretraining methods, PRC surpasses PointContrast (Xie et al., ECCV 2020) by 3.0 NDS at 5% (46.0 vs. 43.0) and ProposalContrast (Yin et al., ECCV 2022) by 2.9 NDS (46.0 vs. 43.1), with the gap narrowing to ~1.1 NDS at 50% data. For multimodal baselines, CALICO beats SimIPU (Li et al., AAAI 2022) by 2.1 NDS at 5% (47.9 vs. 45.8) and by 0.7 NDS at 50% (62.7 vs. 62.0), while also exceeding PRC+BEVDistill (Chen et al., arXiv 2022) by 0.4 NDS at 5% (47.9 vs. 47.5). In the camera-only ablation (Table 7), RAD outperforms BEVDet (Huang et al., 2021) by 5.2 NDS and BEVFormer (Li et al., ECCV 2022) by 3.7 NDS at 10% data. At 100% data (Table 6), PRC matches GCC-3D (Liang et al., ICCV 2021) and BEV-MAE (Lin & Wang, 2022) within 0.1 NDS while substantially outperforming them at 5% data (46.0 vs. 45.4 for RoIAlign-Contrast). On Waymo, CALICO leads ProposalContrast by 5.3 AP (71.6 vs. 66.3) and SimIPU by 3.2 AP (71.6 vs. 68.4) at 20% data.

## Relation to Prior Work

CALICO extends the line of contrastive pretraining for 3D perception (PointContrast, ProposalContrast, GCC-3D) by introducing a top-down semantic pooling strategy (DBSCAN-based clustering) that replaces the bottom-up, heuristic-driven region partitioning used in prior methods. It departs from SimIPU's simultaneous pixel-space cross-modal contrast by adopting a two-stage design: first pretraining the LiDAR backbone with a combined point-region contrast (PRC) objective, then distilling to the camera backbone via region-aware contrastive distillation (RAD). The PRC loss itself builds on PLRC (Bai et al., 2022) for 2D detection, adapting it to 3D point clouds and augmenting negative pairs with semantic-less points, while RAD draws on BEVDistill and contrastive distillation but operates under fully self-supervised conditions without an oracle teacher.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CALICO provides the first unified self-supervised pretraining framework for multimodal BEV perception, addressing a gap where prior methods handled either single-modality or pixel-space cross-modal contrast but not the BEV-space fusion setting. The practical impact is significant for data-efficient autonomous driving: 10.5% NDS and 8.6% mAP gains with only 5% labeled data, plus a 45.3% reduction in adversarial attack success rates. Key limitations include the heuristic nature of semantic pooling (which can miscluster trees or buildings), the assumption of accurate LiDAR-camera calibration, and the lack of theoretical grounding for the design choices. Open questions include whether more principled region definitions (e.g., temporal clustering) could replace the DBSCAN heuristic, and whether the framework extends to other sensor combinations or BEV architectures.
