## Problem

Training neural operators requires generating 10³–10⁵ labeled PDE solutions, and the dominant cost (~95%) is solving the resulting large sparse linear systems. Conventional pipelines solve each system independently, ignoring the structural similarities among systems derived from the same PDE family, which leads to massive redundant computation. This paper is the first to specifically target this data-generation bottleneck for neural operators.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241711/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of large sparse non-symmetric linear systems A⁽ⁱ⁾x⁽ⁱ⁾ = b⁽ⁱ⁾ (matrix sizes from ~2,500 to 40,000+ unknowns) together with their associated PDE parameter matrices P⁽ⁱ⁾ (e.g., GRF coefficients for Darcy flow, Chebyshev coefficients for Poisson). The parameter matrices are used only by the sorting phase via Frobenius-norm distance and do not enter the solver itself. The output is the full set of numerical PDE solutions assembled into a ready-to-use training dataset for downstream neural operator training.

## Method

SKR operates in two phases. First, a greedy nearest-neighbor sorting algorithm arranges the linear systems so that adjacent systems minimize the Frobenius-norm distance in their parameter matrices, maximizing structural correlation between consecutive systems. Second, the systems are solved sequentially with GCRO-DR (Generalized Conjugate Residual with Deflated Restarting), which recycles k approximate eigenvectors from each solved system into the next by computing deflation matrices U_k and C_k via QR factorization, thereby pre-populating the Krylov subspace and reducing the number of Arnoldi iterations. A theoretical analysis grounded in the Kilmer–De Sturler eigenvalue-gap theorem shows that the recycled invariant subspaces are robust to small matrix perturbations, justifying the use of a simple greedy sort rather than an optimal permutation. Across four PDE benchmarks (Darcy flow, thermal, Poisson, Helmholtz) with seven preconditioners and multiple tolerances, SKR achieves up to 13.9× wall-clock speedup and up to 30× fewer iterations relative to GMRES.

## Results

SKR achieves a maximum wall-clock speedup of 13.9× and iteration reduction of 30× over GMRES on the Helmholtz equation (10⁴ size, ASM preconditioning, 1e-7 tolerance). Across all four PDE datasets (Darcy, Thermal, Poisson, Helmholtz) and seven preconditioning strategies, SKR consistently reduces computation time by 1.0–13.9× and iteration counts by 3.9–30×. The acceleration is most pronounced at higher accuracy requirements (e.g., Darcy flow speedup increases from ~2.6× at 1e-2 to ~4.5× at 1e-8 with ASM), and SKR demonstrates superior stability, almost never reaching the maximum iteration limit where GMRES frequently fails to converge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is GMRES (PETSc 3.19.4), the standard Krylov solver for large sparse nonsymmetric systems. SKR outperforms GMRES in virtually all tested configurations across four PDE datasets, seven preconditioners, and multiple tolerance levels. The largest gains appear on the Helmholtz equation, where SKR achieves up to 13.9× wall-clock speedup and 30× fewer iterations (ASM preconditioning, 1e-7 tolerance), and on Darcy flow with BJacobi/SOR preconditioning (up to 4.1× time, 28.6× iterations). The one setting where SKR underperforms is the Poisson equation at low accuracy (1e-5) with ICC or ILU preconditioning, where the speedup ratio drops to ~0.98–0.99× (i.e., SKR is marginally slower), because these factorization-based preconditioners disrupt the inter-system similarity that recycling exploits. At higher tolerances (1e-8, 1e-11), SKR recovers its advantage even with ICC/ILU. SKR also demonstrates superior numerical stability, with near-zero instances of reaching the maximum iteration limit compared to GMRES's frequent non-convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies Krylov subspace recycling—well-established in numerical linear algebra for sequences of related linear systems (Parks et al., 2006; de Sturler, 1996)—to the specific bottleneck of neural operator training data generation. The key departure from prior recycling work is the introduction of a greedy sorting algorithm that reorders the linear systems to maximize inter-system similarity before applying GCRO-DR, since NO parameter matrices do not naturally produce a well-ordered sequence. The theoretical justification for why a suboptimal (greedy) sort suffices draws on perturbation theory for invariant subspaces (Kilmer & De Sturler, 2006), showing that recycled eigenvectors associated with small eigenvalues are robust to moderate matrix perturbations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Data generation accounts for roughly 95% of the NO training pipeline cost, and this paper demonstrates up to 13.9× wall-clock speedup and up to 30× iteration reduction across four PDE families, multiple preconditioners, and matrix sizes up to 40,000×40,000—making large-scale NO training substantially more tractable. Key limitations include restriction to linear PDEs with nonsymmetric matrices (GCRO-DR), a greedy (non-optimal) sorting strategy, and observed degradation when combined with aggressive preconditioners like ICC/ILU at low tolerances. Open questions include extending the framework to nonlinear PDEs (where Newton iterations produce sequences of linear systems), refining the sorting distance metric, and whether the recycling concept transfers to other data-generation bottlenecks in scientific ML.
