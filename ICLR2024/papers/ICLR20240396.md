## Problem

Personalized federated learning (PFL) methods assume test data on each client is in-distribution with its training data, but real-world test data is often out-of-distribution, causing models to fail due to shortcut learning (reliance on spurious correlations). Existing invariant learning schemes designed for centralized settings cannot be directly applied to PFL because the invariance constraint would eliminate both shortcut and personalized features simultaneously, and trivial combinations of invariant learning with PFL local adaptation can even degrade performance when each client has only one training environment.

## Input / Output

Each federated client provides labeled images (e.g., 28×28 MNIST/Fashion-MNIST digits, 224×224 bird or object images) along with an environment indicator (e.g., color probability p^e in Colored-MNIST, background type in WaterBird) and a client/user identifier. The method outputs a per-client class label prediction. A key constraint is that each client typically possesses only one training environment, while the overall federated system pools environments across clients (e.g., two training environments total in CMNIST), making the collaborative shortcut discovery step essential.

## Method

FedSDR is a two-stage provable shortcut discovery and removal method grounded in structural causal models (SCMs) for heterogeneous federated clients. Stage 1 (Shortcut Discovery) solves a bi-level optimization that maximizes the KL divergence between environment-specific classifiers trained on the same features, exploiting the causal signature Z_S ⊥ U | Y, E to collaboratively identify all shortcut features across clients even when each client has only one environment. Stage 2 (Shortcut Removal) trains a per-client personalized invariant predictor by minimizing the conditional mutual information I(Φ_u; Ψ* | Y) between the client's features and the discovered shortcut features, leveraging the second causal signature Z_C^g ⊥ Z_S | Y and Z_C^U ⊥ Z_S | Y. Theoretical analysis under a linear Gaussian model proves that with |E_tr| > d_s total training environments, the method recovers complete shortcut features and yields the optimal personalized invariant predictor for every client, generalizing to unseen OOD test distributions.

## Results

FedSDR consistently achieves the highest worst-case and average test accuracy across all four OOD benchmarks. On CMNIST, CFMNIST, WaterBird, and PACS, FedSDR attains worst-case accuracies of 53.88%, 56.92%, 65.25%, and 52.14% respectively, outperforming the second-best method by approximately 6.5%, 9%, 3.5%, and 2% on worst-case accuracy. On average accuracy, FedSDR reaches 55.59% (CMNIST), 61.88% (CFMNIST), 73.20% (WaterBird), and 56.18% (PACS), also leading all baselines. The method's advantage is most pronounced on CFMNIST where the gap over the next best (FedIIR at 49.16% average) exceeds 12 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedSDR outperforms all 10 FL/PFL baselines on both worst-case and average accuracy across all four datasets. The closest competitors are FedIIR and FedSR (federated domain generalization methods), which achieve the best worst-case accuracy among baselines (47.25% on CMNIST, 48.06% on CFMNIST, 61.75% on WaterBird, 47.03% on PACS) but still trail FedSDR by 6.5–9 percentage points on worst-case. Among PFL methods, FedPAC leads on PACS average (54.20%) and FedRep on WaterBird average (70.23%), yet both remain 2+ points below FedSDR. Notably, PFL methods like Ditto, pFedMe, and FedRep suffer severe worst-case degradation (often below 5% on CMNIST/CFMNIST) because their local adaptation re-introduces shortcut features when only one training environment is available per client. The paper further shows that trivially combining IRM with PFL personalization (IRM†-FT, IRM†-L2) fails to improve OOD performance and can even degrade it (e.g., IRM†-FT drops to 14.32% worst-case on CMNIST vs. 46.38% for IRM† alone), confirming that FedSDR's explicit shortcut discovery and removal is necessary rather than a simple invariance constraint.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedSDR sits at the intersection of invariant learning (IRM, IRM-IB, MRI) and personalized federated learning (pFedMe, Ditto, FedRep), but departs from both in a key way: rather than directly enforcing invariance constraints across environments (which requires sufficient environments and eliminates personalization), it formulates SCMs specific to heterogeneous federated clients—introducing a user indicator U and decomposing invariant features into shared (Z_C^g) and personalized (Z_C^U) components. From these SCMs, it derives causal signatures that enable a two-stage shortcut discovery (via bi-level optimization with KL-divergence across environments) and removal (via conditional mutual information regularization) approach. The proof techniques for Theorem 1 follow Rosenfeld et al. (2021) under a linear data model, while the practical CMI approximation borrows from Jiang & Veitch (2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to formally address shortcut learning within PFL, filling a gap where existing PFL methods assume in-distribution test data and existing invariant learning methods cannot preserve client-specific features. The finding that naively combining invariant learning with PFL personalization (e.g., IRM + fine-tuning) actually degrades OOD performance below either component alone is practically important, as it warns against a seemingly natural design choice. However, the theoretical guarantees are restricted to linear settings, and the practical CMI approximation is a sufficient-but-not-necessary condition, leaving open whether the approach extends to non-linear causal representations and whether it remains effective when environment labels are unavailable in real federated deployments.
