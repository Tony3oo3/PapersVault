## Problem

Gradient-based multi-objective optimization methods (e.g., MGDA) require explicit gradient access, which is unavailable in black-box settings such as API-only large language models or frozen pre-trained vision-language models. Existing black-box approaches—Bayesian optimization and genetic algorithms—either fail in high dimensions or lack convergence guarantees. This paper is the first to design a stochastic gradient algorithm for black-box MOO with provable convergence rates in both convex and non-convex regimes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only scalar function evaluations F_i(x_j) for m objectives at sampled points x_j (no gradients), with the search parameterized by a Gaussian mean μ ∈ R^d and a diagonal covariance Σ. In the multi-task learning application, the input is a low-dimensional vector v ∈ R^d projected through a fixed random matrix A ∈ R^{D×d} to form prompt token embeddings for a frozen CLIP model, and the output is the optimized v that simultaneously minimizes all task losses. The per-iteration computational cost is O(mNd), dominated by N Monte Carlo samples per objective.

## Method

ASMG smooths each black-box objective to its expectation under a Gaussian search distribution p_θ(x) = N(μ, Σ), then estimates the gradients of these smoothed objectives w.r.t. μ and Σ using score-function (REINFORCE-style) Monte Carlo estimators that require only function queries. An adaptive weight vector λ^t is obtained by solving a small m×m quadratic program that minimizes the norm of the composite gradient (analogous to MGDA's common descent direction), and a momentum update on λ ensures a convergent weight sequence and reduces bias from the correlation between λ and the stochastic gradients. The mean update is a natural gradient step μ_{t+1} = μ_t − β_t Σ_t Σ_i λ_i^t ĝ_i^t, while the diagonal covariance is updated in inverse form Σ_{t+1}^{-1} = Σ_t^{-1} + 2β_t Σ_i λ_i^t Ĝ_i^t. The paper proves that a Pareto stationary solution of the Gaussian-smoothed problem is an ε-accurate Pareto stationary solution of the original problem with ε = L_F²‖diag(Σ)‖_1, and establishes O(log T/T) convergence for convex objectives and O(T^{-1/2}) for non-convex objectives.

## Results

On the black-box multi-task learning task (prompt tuning for CLIP), ASMG achieves the highest average classification accuracy across all dimension settings. At d=256, ASMG reaches 81.67% on Office-31 and 74.26% on Office-home, surpassing the zero-shot CLIP baseline (73.28% / 72.48%) by 8.4% and 1.8% respectively. On the synthetic benchmark problems (d=100, N=50), ASMG achieves approximately 10⁻⁴ solution precision on all three test functions, while CMA-ES only reaches 10⁻¹ on the shift l_{1/2}-ellipsoid and both CMA-ES and MMES fail entirely on the mixed ellipsoid-rastrigin 10 problem.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASMG consistently outperforms all five baselines (ES, BES, MMES, CMA-ES, and zero-shot CLIP) on both Office-31 and Office-home across all three prompt dimensions. The strongest baseline is CMA-ES, which ASMG surpasses by 1.62% on Office-31 and 2.46% on Office-home at d=256 (81.67% vs 80.05%; 74.26% vs 71.80%), with similar margins at d=512 and d=1024. ES and BES lag further behind, with ASMG leading by over 6% on Office-31 at d=256. The ablation ASMG-EW (fixed equal weights λ=1/m) underperforms full ASMG by 2.46% on Office-31 and 5.04% on Office-home at d=256, confirming that the adaptive stochastic gradient weighting is critical. On the synthetic benchmarks, ASMG is the only method to achieve high-precision convergence (≈10⁻⁴) on all three test problems, while CMA-ES and MMES fail on the non-convex mixed ellipsoid-rastrigin 10 and ES/BES fail on all three.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes two previously separate research threads: the stochastic gradient approximation framework from Natural Evolution Strategies (Wierstra et al., 2014; Lyu & Tsang, 2021) and the multiple gradient descent algorithm (MGDA, Désidéri, 2012) for finding common descent directions in multi-objective optimization. The key conceptual move is to apply the Gaussian-smoothing-based zeroth-order gradient estimation to the min-max formulation inherited from MGDA, yielding an adaptive composite weight that balances all objectives without requiring gradient access. The momentum-based weight stabilization follows Zhou et al. (2022b), and the diagonal-covariance restriction is a practical departure from full CMA-ES to keep per-iteration cost at O(mNd). The paper positions itself as the first stochastic gradient algorithm for black-box MOO with a convergence guarantee, distinguishing it from BO (limited to low dimensions), GA (lacks convergence theory), and CMA-ES (single-objective or equal-weight aggregation only).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is compelling: as large models (LLMs, CLIP) are increasingly accessed only through inference APIs, gradient-based multi-objective or multi-task optimization becomes infeasible, and this paper provides a principled alternative with provable convergence. The O(log T / T) convex and O(T^{-1/2}) non-convex rates, together with the explicit connection between Gaussian-smoothed and original Pareto conditions, give practitioners confidence that the smoothed solution approximates the true Pareto set as Σ shrinks. Open questions include how the method scales when d is very large (the diagonal-Σ assumption may be too restrictive), whether the PSD requirement on Ĝ_i holds in practice for highly non-convex landscapes, and how ASMG compares to more recent multi-objective BO or scalarization-free approaches at very large query budgets.
