## Problem

While LIMA suggested alignment tuning might be 'superficial,' no prior work directly examined how token-level distributions shift between base and aligned LLMs, leaving unclear what alignment actually changes and whether SFT/RLHF are truly necessary. This paper asks: how effectively can we align base LLMs without any parameter tuning?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240989/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

URIAL takes a user query and prepends a static prefix consisting of a system prompt (defining the assistant's role in terms of helpfulness, safety, and social responsibility) plus K=3 carefully restyled in-context examples. The examples are chosen for their stylistic structure—affirming the query, enumerating detailed bullet points in a 'topic: details' format, and closing with safety disclaimers and an engaging summary—rather than for semantic relevance to the query. The output is a single assistant-style text response generated via greedy decoding from the untuned base model, with the static prefix cacheable in the KV cache for efficient repeated inference.

## Method

The paper first provides direct evidence for the superficial alignment hypothesis by decoding the aligned model's output with the base model at each token position and categorizing shifts: 77.7% of tokens are unshifted (top-1 in both models), 92.2% fall within the top-3, and the remaining 5–8% shifted positions concentrate on stylistic tokens (discourse markers, safety disclaimers) rather than knowledge-bearing content. Building on this, URIAL aligns base LLMs purely through in-context learning with a constant prompt: a system prompt adopted from Llama-2-chat plus three restyled examples that demonstrate the desired response structure (engaging opener, numbered list, safety-aware summary). The key design choice is that examples are curated for style and format rather than retrieved for semantic similarity, and the static prefix can be cached in the KV cache for efficient inference. On strong base models (Mistral-7b, Llama-2-70b), URIAL matches or surpasses SFT and SFT+RLHF aligned counterparts on a six-aspect evaluation protocol (helpfulness, clarity, factuality, depth, engagement, safety).

## Results

On just-eval-instruct (1,000 instructions, 6-aspect GPT-4 scoring from 1–5), URIAL with K=3 constant in-context examples achieves an average score of 4.33 on Llama-2-7b, 4.63 on Mistral-7b, and 4.74 on Llama-2-70b. These scores match or exceed the corresponding SFT/RLHF-aligned models: Mistral-7b-Instruct (SFT) scores 4.44 and Llama-2-70b-chat (RLHF) scores 4.67, while URIAL surpasses both. Token distribution shift analysis shows that 77.7% of tokens are at unshifted positions (top-1 in both base and aligned models) and 92.2% are within the top-3, with only 5–8% of positions showing significant distribution shift, predominantly in stylistic tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Retrieval ICL baseline from Han (2023), URIAL (K=3) on Llama-2-7b improves the average score from 3.72 to 4.33 (+0.61), with the largest gains in safety (2.17→4.29) and depth (3.62→3.88), while also using a static prompt that is more efficient than dynamic retrieval. Against Mistral-7b-Instruct (SFT, Jiang et al. 2023), URIAL on the same Mistral-7b base model outperforms on all six aspects, achieving 4.63 vs. 4.44 average (+0.19), with notable advantages in helpfulness (4.57 vs. 4.36), factuality (4.50 vs. 4.29), and depth (4.18 vs. 3.89). Against Llama-2-70b-chat (RLHF, Touvron et al. 2023), URIAL on Llama-2-70b surpasses the RLHF model by 0.07 in average (4.74 vs. 4.67), winning on helpfulness (4.72 vs. 4.50), factuality (4.65 vs. 4.54), and depth (4.30 vs. 4.28), while the RLHF model retains a slight edge only in safety (5.00 vs. 4.96). On the smaller Llama-2-7b, URIAL (4.33) is slightly below both Vicuna-7b SFT (4.46) and Llama-2-7b-chat RLHF (4.47), indicating that for weaker base models, tuning-based alignment still provides a modest advantage. Human pairwise evaluation (Table 2) confirms near-ties: 71.1% ties between Mistral-7b URIAL and Mistral-7b-Instruct, and 75.1% ties between Llama-2-70b URIAL and Llama-2-70b-chat.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Superficial Alignment Hypothesis from LIMA (Zhou et al., 2023), which provided only indirect evidence (1K SFT examples suffice), by introducing a direct analytical lens: token distribution shift between base and aligned models. Methodologically, URIAL builds on in-context learning for alignment (Han, 2023; Min et al., 2022) but departs from retrieval-based ICL by showing that a small set of static, stylistically curated examples outperforms dynamically retrieved semantically-relevant ones. The work also connects to the broader SFT+RLHF alignment pipeline (Ouyang et al., 2022; Touvron et al., 2023) by questioning its necessity rather than improving upon it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that alignment tuning primarily reshapes stylistic tokens (discourse markers, safety disclaimers) while leaving knowledge-intensive content untouched in 77–92% of positions reframes alignment as a formatting problem rather than a capability-unlocking one, with practical implications for aligning extremely large models where fine-tuning is prohibitively expensive. The observation that SFT can induce catastrophic forgetting (e.g., Mistral-7b-Instruct incorrectly denying Facebook's name change) and that RLHF can produce over-refusal on benign queries highlights real costs of the standard pipeline. Open questions include whether URIAL generalizes to specialized domains (coding, math, agents), whether the token-shift analysis holds for non-English or multimodal settings, and whether the GPT-4-based evaluation protocol introduces residual biases despite 87.8% human agreement.
