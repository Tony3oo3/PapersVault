## Problem

Dimensional collapse in self-supervised learning has been studied primarily as a global property (e.g., via effective rank or covariance decorrelation), but this paper demonstrates that representations can span a high-dimensional space globally while still collapsing locally around individual data points. This local collapse is invisible to global metrics yet degrades downstream performance, and no existing regularizer explicitly targets the local intrinsic dimensionality (LID) of each sample's neighborhood.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240683/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

LDReg operates on encoder representations z = f(x) of a batch of N samples (or 2N for contrastive methods with two augmented views per image). It computes pairwise Euclidean distances within the batch, estimates per-sample LID via the method of moments using k nearest neighbors (default k=64 for ResNet-50, k=128 for ViT-B), and produces a scalar regularization loss added to the SSL objective. The output is the same encoder representation vector, but regularized to have higher local dimensionality; the projector, predictor, and decoder are unaffected.

## Method

The core contribution is a theoretically grounded regularization derived from an asymptotic Fisher-Rao metric on the statistical manifold of lower-tail distance distributions. The paper proves that for smooth growth functions H_{w|θ}(r) = (r/w)^θ, the Fisher-Rao distance between two such distributions reduces to |ln(θ₂/θ₁)|, and that any CDF asymptotically converges to this canonical form parameterized by its LID. This yields the key insight that LID values should be compared on a logarithmic scale and aggregated via the geometric mean (the Fréchet mean under the asymptotic Fisher-Rao metric). The L1 regularization term minimizes −(1/N)Σ ln(LID_i), equivalently maximizing the log of the geometric mean of per-sample LIDs, while the L2 term maximizes the Fréchet variance. In practice, LID is estimated per sample using the method of moments (LID = −μ_k/(μ_k − w_k)) from pairwise distances in encoder space, and the resulting loss is added to the SSL objective with a small hyperparameter β (e.g., 0.01 for SimCLR, 0.005 for BYOL, 5×10⁻⁶ for MAE).

## Results

On ImageNet linear evaluation, LDReg consistently improves representation quality across all tested SSL methods. With ResNet-50 (100 epochs), SimCLR improves from 64.3% to 64.8%, and with ViT-B (200 epochs), SimCLR improves from 67.6% to 68.5% while MAE improves from 72.9% to 73.0%. LDReg also increases the effective rank (a global dimensionality measure) and the geometric mean of local intrinsic dimensionality (LID), e.g., SimCLR's effective rank rises from 470.2 to 529.6 and its mean LID from 18.8 to 20.0 with ResNet-50.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LDReg is applied as a plug-in regularizer on top of existing SSL methods and consistently improves linear evaluation accuracy. The largest gains appear on methods most susceptible to dimensional collapse: SimCLR (ResNet-50) improves by +0.5% (64.3→64.8), SimCLR (ViT-B) by +0.9% (67.6→68.5), and BYOL by +0.9% (67.6→68.5, reported in Table 16). MAE, known to be prone to collapse, sees a smaller but consistent +0.1% gain (72.9→73.0) alongside a notable effective rank increase (283.7→326.1). Additional experiments (Table 15) show MoCo improves by +0.9% (68.7→69.6), VICReg by +0.2% (66.7→66.9), and Barlow Twins by +0.1% (65.5→65.6). A direct comparison against covariance/variance regularizers (Table 16) shows LDReg outperforms them on BYOL: LDReg achieves 68.5% vs. 67.7–67.8% for Cov+Var, while also substantially increasing the geometric mean LID (22.3 vs. 15.5–16.8), confirming that local dimensionality regularization is more effective than pseudo-global covariance-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on dimensional collapse in self-supervised learning, which has primarily been studied as a global phenomenon (via effective rank, covariance off-diagonal regularization, or feature decorrelation in methods like VICReg and Barlow Twins). The key conceptual departure is the observation that representations can span high-dimensional space globally yet collapse locally, motivated by the LID Representation Theorem (Houle, 2017a) and the Method of Moments estimator (Amsaleg et al., 2018). The paper introduces an asymptotic Fisher-Rao metric for comparing local distance distributions, providing a theoretically grounded distance that naturally yields the geometric mean as the Fréchet mean of LID values — a result that distinguishes it from prior work that treated dimensionality as a purely global, covariance-based property.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that BYOL avoids global collapse (high effective rank) yet suffers significant local collapse (low geometric mean LID) challenges the assumption that global non-collapse guarantees representation quality, and suggests that local structure matters for downstream performance. LDReg is a lightweight, generic regularizer (requiring only pairwise distances within a batch) that can be plugged into any SSL method, and the theoretical framework provides principled guidance on how to compare and aggregate intrinsic dimensionality values (log-scale, geometric mean). However, the practical improvements are modest (typically 0.5–1% on linear evaluation), and the method's dependence on batch size for LID estimation (smaller batches yield fewer neighbors) limits its applicability in low-compute settings. Open questions include whether local collapse is a primary or secondary cause of performance degradation relative to other factors, and whether the asymptotic assumptions hold well in finite-batch, finite-epoch practice.
