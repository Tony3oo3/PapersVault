## Problem

GFlowNets learn stochastic policies to sample compositional objects proportionally to a given reward, but they must be trained from scratch for every new reward function, lacking the ability to transfer knowledge across tasks. This paper asks how to realize the pre-training and fine-tuning paradigm—central to modern NLP, vision, and RL—for GFlowNets, enabling a single model to efficiently adapt to downstream tasks with unseen reward functions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241758/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pre-training, the method requires only the compositional object space (a DAG of states and actions) with no task-specific reward; outcomes are the terminal states themselves (identity mapping f). During fine-tuning, a new reward function R(x) = r(y) over outcomes is provided, and the method produces diverse compositional objects sampled proportionally to this reward. The state and action spaces must remain consistent across pre-training and downstream tasks, and the outcome space is discrete (e.g., grid positions, bit sequences, DNA/RNA/peptide sequences).

## Method

The paper introduces an outcome-conditioned GFlowNet (OC-GFN) that extends standard GFlowNet flows and policies with an outcome input y, trained to reach any specified target outcome via a binary success reward. Pre-training uses a GAFlowNet (driven by random network distillation intrinsic rewards) to generate diverse outcomes, then trains the OC-GFN with a contrastive objective (positive off-policy trajectories from GAFN, negative on-policy trajectories from OC-GFN) and an outcome teleportation scheme that propagates the terminal binary reward to every transition for efficient credit assignment in long-horizon tasks. For fine-tuning, the pre-trained OC-GFN is converted to a new reward via a marginalization over outcomes (Eq. 6), which is intractable in high-dimensional outcome spaces; the paper learns an amortized numerator network N(s'|s) and a sampling policy Q(y|s',s) trained with a GFlowNet-like loss to approximate this marginalization efficiently. A key distinction from RL is that GFlowNets learn stochastic policies proportional to reward rather than reward-maximizing policies, so the pre-trained model retains the information needed for adaptation without retraining the policy itself.

## Results

In the unsupervised pre-training stage, OC-GFN achieves near-100% success rate for reaching target outcomes across all evaluated tasks (GridWorld, bit sequences, TF Bind 8, RNA, and AMP generation). In the supervised fine-tuning stage on 30 TF Bind 8 downstream tasks, OC-GFN achieves a mean rank of 1.27 for number of modes and 1.20 for top-K reward, outperforming GFN from scratch (1.97 and 2.00), MCMC (2.77 and 2.80), and DQN (4.00 and 4.00). In bit sequence generation, OC-GFN discovers more modes faster than all baselines across small, medium, and large scales, with pre-training success rates of 98.37%, 97.22%, and 93.75% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OC-GFN consistently outperforms all baselines across every evaluated task. Against GFN trained from scratch (using detailed balance or trajectory balance), OC-GFN discovers more modes faster and achieves higher top-K scores in TF Bind 8, RNA, and AMP generation, with the advantage being most pronounced in tasks where GFN from scratch suffers mode collapse (e.g., GridWorld Figure 3b vs 3c-d). MCMC (Dai et al., 2020) performs competitively in small state spaces but degrades significantly as the outcome space grows, while DQN (Mnih et al., 2015) gets stuck in a single mode due to its reward-maximizing objective and fails to discover diverse solutions. In additional RL comparisons (Appendix C.4), both PPO and SAC underperform GFN from scratch in mode discovery and top-K scores on TF Bind tasks, confirming that reward-maximizing RL is fundamentally limited for diverse candidate generation. The paper also shows that a naive pre-training-then-fine-tuning scheme (pre-train GFN on task A, fine-tune on task B) is not universally effective and can even hurt performance when reward structures differ, whereas OC-GFN's outcome-conditioned pre-training is agnostic to downstream reward structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GFlowNet framework (Bengio et al., 2021; Bengio et al., 2023) by introducing outcome-conditioned flows and policies, drawing a direct analogy to goal-conditioned RL (Kaelbling, 1993; Schaul et al., 2015) to enable reward-free pre-training. It leverages GAFlowNets (Pan et al., 2023b) with random network distillation for intrinsic-motivation-driven exploration during pre-training, and builds upon the detailed balance and sub-trajectory balance objectives as compatible training foundations. The key conceptual departure from prior GFlowNet work is the demonstration that a fully trained OC-GFN can be converted to sample from an arbitrary new reward function via a marginalization over outcomes (Eq. 6), without retraining the policy itself—a property absent in standard RL fine-tuning. The amortized predictor for approximating this intractable marginalization is a novel contribution that enables practical deployment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes the first viable pre-training/fine-tuning paradigm for GFlowNets, opening a path toward data-efficient adaptation to new scientific discovery tasks (e.g., binding to a different protein target) without training from scratch. A key limitation is that the state and action spaces must remain consistent across tasks, and the current formulation handles only discrete outcomes; extending to continuous outcomes or varying state spaces is identified as important future work. The practical impact is significant for domains like drug discovery where reward functions change frequently but the compositional object space is fixed. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to stochastic environments, multi-objective settings, and whether the contrastive training + outcome teleportation recipe is optimal or can be replaced by smoother reward shaping.
