## Problem

The information bottleneck principle, widely used to learn concise representations, implicitly suppresses redundant but relevant features that may be critical for generalization under distribution shift or in low-resource settings. This paper asks whether retaining such supplemental features—rather than squeezing them out—can improve both in-domain and out-of-domain performance without sacrificing predictive accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241027/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10/100), tokenized text sequences (for BERT-based sentiment and similarity tasks), or tabular feature vectors, and outputs class labels or continuous regression scores. A distinctive aspect is that during training, each input is additionally processed into a modified version x′ with salient features erased (5% of image patches or tokens), which serves only as a regularization signal rather than a supervised input. At inference, the final prediction is obtained by averaging the logits from both the mainline and supplemental representation heads.

## Method

InfoR-LSF is a three-stage supervised framework built on a shared backbone (ResNet-18 or BERT) with two lightweight variational encoder heads producing mainline z_M and supplemental z_S representations. Stage 1 trains z_M with a standard VIB objective (maximize I(z_M;y), minimize I(z_M;x)). Stage 2 identifies salient input features x_sf via top-K gradient norm of the loss and erases them (Gaussian fill for images, [MASK] tokens for text) to produce a modified input x′. Stage 3 jointly trains both heads, adding a conditional mutual information regularization term I(z_S; x | x′)—implemented as KL divergence between p(z_S|x) and p(z_S′|x′)—that forces z_S to learn features complementary to z_M rather than re-extracting the same salient features. This alleviates feature suppression and encourages the model to exploit a broader set of relevant input information for prediction.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with only 50 training samples, InfoR-LSF achieves 20.3% test accuracy, outperforming ResNet-18 (17.2%), VIB (18.6%), IFM (17.1%), and FGSM (20.1%). On IMDB sentiment classification with 50 training examples, InfoR-LSF reaches 75.5% accuracy versus BERT's 66.6% and VIBERT's 68.9%, a gain of +8.9 points. In out-of-domain transfer (trained on YELP, linear-probed on 7 target datasets), InfoR-LSF consistently achieves the highest accuracy, with gains of +1.4 to +2.9 points over BERT depending on the target task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfoR-LSF outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and training data size tested. Against the ResNet-18 backbone baseline, the gains are largest in low-resource settings (e.g., +3.1 points at 50 samples on CIFAR-10, +8.9 points at 50 samples on IMDB) and shrink to near-zero at full data (50K samples), consistent with the hypothesis that redundant relevant features matter most when labeled data is scarce. Compared to VIB (Alemi et al., 2017), InfoR-LSF consistently wins on both image and text tasks, confirming that the information bottleneck's suppression of redundant features is harmful in low-resource and OOD scenarios. Against VIBERT (Mahabadi et al., 2021), the text-specific VIB variant, InfoR-LSF achieves +6.6 points on IMDB (50 samples) and +1.9 points on YELP (50 samples). FGSM adversarial training, while competitive at 50 samples on CIFAR-10 (20.1% vs 20.3%), degrades substantially at larger data sizes (65.5% at 3000 samples vs 76.2% for InfoR-LSF), suggesting adversarial robustness does not equate to information retention. IFM (Robinson et al., 2021) is the closest competitor but is still outperformed by 1–4 points across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a deliberate counterpoint to the Information Bottleneck (IB) principle (Tishby et al., 1999; Alemi et al., 2017), which the authors argue implicitly suppresses redundant-but-relevant features that could aid generalization under distribution shift or data scarcity. It builds directly on the variational IB framework (VIB) for its optimization machinery—reparameterization trick, KL-divergence-based losses, and variational encoders—but inverts the compression objective by introducing a supplemental representation head constrained via conditional mutual information to learn features complementary to the mainline representation. The salient-feature-erasing step (gradient-norm-based masking) is a novel mechanism not present in prior IB or InfoMax work, and the three-stage training pipeline distinguishes the method from both standard VIB/VIBERT and data-augmentation approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled alternative to the dominant IB narrative in representation learning, with the practical implication that in low-resource or OOD settings, retaining 'redundant' relevant features can be beneficial rather than harmful. However, the gains are modest (typically 1–4% in-domain, 0.3–2.9% OOD) and come at roughly 2.4× training cost, limiting immediate adoption. Open questions include whether the three-stage process is the most efficient route to information retention, whether the principle extends to generative or self-supervised settings, and whether the benefits persist at scale (the largest dataset tested is 50K CIFAR images). The finding that IB regularization can be counterproductive for image tasks (ablation with β=0) is a useful caution but is dataset-specific.
