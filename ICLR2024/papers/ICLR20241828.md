## Problem

Standard contrastive learning for time series assigns hard binary labels (positive/negative) to all sample pairs, ignoring the inherent correlations between similar time series instances and between values at adjacent timestamps. This forces the model to push similar instances apart and penalize nearby timestamps equally to distant ones, degrading the quality of learned representations. The paper asks how to incorporate data-space similarities into the contrastive objective to better capture these natural structures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241828/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes univariate or multivariate time series of shape T×D and produces timestamp-level representation vectors of shape T×M, from which instance-level embeddings are obtained via max-pooling. A key design choice is that soft assignments are computed on the data space (DTW distance between original, unaugmented time series for instance-wise; absolute timestamp difference for temporal), not on the learned embedding space, making them precomputable and cacheable. The method is plug-and-play: it replaces the loss function in existing CL frameworks (TS2Vec, TS-TCC, CA-TCC) without modifying their architectures or augmentation strategies.

## Method

SoftCLT replaces the hard 0/1 assignments in InfoNCE with continuous soft assignments in [0,1] for both instance-wise and temporal contrastive losses. For instance-wise CL, the soft assignment w_I(i,i') = 2α·σ(−τ_I·D(x_i,x_i')) uses a min-max normalized DTW distance on the data space, with α controlling the upper bound to distinguish same-instance pairs from merely similar different instances. For temporal CL, the soft assignment w_T(t,t') = 2·σ(−τ_T·|t−t'|) decays with timestamp distance, and the sharpness τ_T is scaled by m^k at each hierarchical pooling depth to account for increasing semantic gaps. The final loss is a weighted sum of the two soft losses, and the paper shows it equals a scaled KL divergence between the predicted softmax and the normalized soft assignments, reducing to standard InfoNCE when all soft assignments collapse to 0 or 1.

## Results

On the 125 UCR univariate datasets, SoftCLT applied to TS2Vec achieves 85.0% average accuracy (rank 1.49), improving over TS2Vec (83.0%) by 2.0% and over the previous best T-Loss (80.6%) by 4.4%. On the 29 UEA multivariate datasets, it reaches 75.1% average accuracy (rank 1.86), a 3.9% gain over TS2Vec (71.2%). In transfer learning, TS-TCC+SoftCLT achieves 97.0% accuracy on in-domain (SleepEEG→Epilepsy) and 100% on EMG cross-domain, while CA-TCC+SoftCLT improves average accuracy on the 12 FD transfer scenarios by 10.68%. In anomaly detection, SoftCLT improves F1 by ~2% over TS2Vec on both Yahoo and KPI under normal and cold-start settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SoftCLT consistently outperforms all 29 baselines across every task. In classification, it beats the strongest prior method T-Loss (80.6% UCR, 67.5% UEA) by 4.4% and 7.6% respectively, and the closest competitor TS2Vec by 2.0% and 3.9%. In semi-supervised classification (1% and 5% labels), TS2Vec+SoftCLT and CA-TCC+SoftCLT achieve the best accuracy on 7–8 out of 8 datasets, surpassing CA-TCC (e.g., 90.6 vs 77.3 on HAR at 1% labels) and all semi-supervised baselines (Mean-Teacher, DivideMix, SemiTime, FixMatch). In transfer learning, TS-TCC+SoftCLT achieves 97.0% in-domain accuracy, beating SimMTM (95.49%), CLOCS (95.07%), and TF-C (94.95%), and reaches 100% on the EMG cross-domain task where the next best is SimMTM at 97.56%. In anomaly detection, SoftCLT improves F1 over TS2Vec by 1.9% (Yahoo normal: 74.2 vs 72.3) and 2.5% (KPI normal: 70.1 vs 67.6), and by 2.2% and 1.8% under cold-start. In time-series forecasting (Appendix H), SoftCLT reduces MSE consistently across ETTh1, ETTh2, ETTm1, and Electricity for both TS2Vec and CoST, e.g., 0.120 vs 0.129 average MSE on ETTh1 univariate.

## Relation to Prior Work

SoftCLT generalizes the standard InfoNCE contrastive loss by replacing hard positive/negative assignments with soft assignments computed from data-space distances (DTW for instance-wise) and timestamp differences (for temporal), building directly on the hierarchical contrastive framework of TS2Vec and the temporal-contextual contrasting of TS-TCC/CA-TCC. It departs from prior soft contrastive learning in computer vision (NNCLR, ASCL) by computing soft assignments on the data space rather than the learned embedding space, arguing that TS distances on the data space are meaningful self-supervision signals unlike pixel distances in images. The method is explicitly positioned as a plug-and-play drop-in for existing TS contrastive learning pipelines, requiring no architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature and consistent 2–4% improvements across classification, semi-supervised learning, transfer learning, and anomaly detection make SoftCLT a practical upgrade for existing TS contrastive learning pipelines. The finding that embedding-space soft CL methods (NNCLR, ASCL) actually degrade TS performance while data-space soft assignments help provides a useful conceptual distinction for when and where soft supervision is beneficial. Open questions include whether the DTW-based instance-wise assignment scales efficiently to very long or high-dimensional multivariate series, and whether the assumption of local temporal smoothness (adjacent timestamps are similar) holds in highly non-stationary or adversarially corrupted settings.
