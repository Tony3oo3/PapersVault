## Problem

Quantifying how input uncertainties (e.g., sensor noise, measurement error) propagate through neural networks to produce prediction uncertainties is critical for safety-critical applications, yet existing analytical methods like moment matching are computationally expensive, discard inter-component correlations, and cannot handle distributions without finite moments such as Cauchy. This paper asks whether a simpler, more accurate, and more general propagation scheme exists that works for the full family of stable distributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240400/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a data point x together with an input uncertainty distribution parameterized by a location (mean for Gaussian, median for Cauchy) and a scale (covariance matrix or diagonal variance vector for Gaussian; scale vector for Cauchy) and produces an output distribution with the same parametric form. The location parameter of the output coincides exactly with the standard forward-pass value, enabling direct application to pre-trained networks without retraining. For classification tasks, the output is further converted into pairwise correct-classification probabilities (one-vs-one over all n−1 false classes), which serve as uncertainty scores for selective prediction or as a training loss.

## Method

SDP propagates stable distributions layer-by-layer: affine layers (fully connected, convolutional, pooling) are handled exactly via the reproductive property of Gaussian and Cauchy distributions, while ReLU non-linearities are approximated by local linearization, which the paper proves (Theorem 1) to be the TV-optimal Gaussian (and Cauchy) approximation of a ReLU-transformed distribution. To avoid the prohibitive cost of propagating an n×n covariance matrix through every layer, SDP exploits the chain rule to compute the output scale as Σ_y = J(f)·Σ·J(f)ᵀ using a single Jacobian evaluation of the full network, reducing the per-layer overhead. For classification, the paper introduces a Pairwise Distribution Loss that computes the exact probability P(X_true > X_false) in closed form for both Gaussian (via the error function) and Cauchy (via arctan) distributions, incorporating full covariance information. This is, to the authors' knowledge, the first method enabling propagation of Cauchy (and more generally α-stable) distributions through neural networks, since Cauchy moments are undefined and moment-matching approaches are inapplicable.

## Results

On the Iris propagation benchmark (4 ReLU layers, 10⁶ MC oracle), SDP achieves 0.979±0.020 intersection-of-probability (1-TV) at σ=0.1, outperforming DVIA (0.972±0.022) and MC estimation with k=100 samples (0.808±0.088), while marginal moment matching collapses to 0.261±0.025. On 8 UCI regression tasks, SDP+PNN achieves the narrowest prediction intervals (lowest MPIW) on 5 of 8 datasets while maintaining ~94–95% PICP. For selective prediction on MNIST with EMNIST letters as OOD data, the best configuration (PDLoss Gauss training + SDP propagation + pairwise Cauchy scoring) achieves 18.3% Risk-Coverage AUC, compared to 21.4% for the softmax entropy baseline and a 12.5% perfect-predictor lower bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDP consistently outperforms DVIA [33] (full moment matching with covariances) in 1-TV across all noise levels on Iris, with the gap widening at higher σ (e.g., 0.680 vs. 0.531 at σ=1000), while being substantially cheaper since SDP computes the Jacobian once rather than propagating an n×n matrix per layer. Against marginal moment matching [28,20,29,18,35], SDP with full covariances is dramatically superior (0.680 vs. 0.170 at σ=1000), and even marginal SDP matches or exceeds marginal moment matching in most settings. SDP also outperforms MC estimation with k=100 samples at all noise levels, meaning a single analytical forward pass beats 100 stochastic evaluations. On UCI regression, SDP+PNN beats PNN [9,21,34] and SQR [9] on MPIW for 5/8 datasets while matching or exceeding their PICP. For OOD selective prediction, pairwise Cauchy scores with SDP (18.3% RCAUC) beat softmax entropy (21.4%), orthogonal certificates (24.2%), and Dirichlet outputs [20] (32.0% on pretrained models), though all remain above the 12.5% perfect-predictor bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on sampling-free distribution propagation through neural networks, most directly building on marginal moment matching (assumed density filtering) from Frey & Hinton, Gast & Roth, and Shekhovtsov & Flach, as well as the approximate full-covariance approach DVIA from Wu et al. The key departure is replacing moment matching at non-linearities with a local linearization that is provably TV-optimal for ReLU, and leveraging the Jacobian of the full network (via the chain rule) to compute output covariances in a single step rather than propagating n×n matrices layer-by-layer. The paper also carves new ground by being the first to propagate Cauchy (and more generally α-stable) distributions through neural networks, which is impossible under moment matching since Cauchy moments are undefined.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SDP provides a practical, sampling-free tool for quantifying how input measurement noise propagates to prediction uncertainty, with the added benefit of being applicable to pre-trained models (unlike moment matching, which shifts means and degrades point predictions). The TV-optimality guarantee for ReLU gives a clean theoretical foundation, and the Cauchy propagation opens the door to modeling heavy-tailed input uncertainties. Limitations include the optimality proof being specific to ReLU (other non-linearities are handled empirically), the pairwise classification loss being an approximation for multi-class settings, and the modest empirical margins over DVIA in most configurations. Open questions include whether TV-optimality at individual ReLUs translates to provably better multi-layer propagation, and whether the method extends to non-stable input distributions.
