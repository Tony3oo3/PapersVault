## Problem

Existing video diffusion models oversimplify spatial-temporal interactions by relying on basic 2D+1D separable operations, leading to temporal distortions and poor motion quality. Additionally, reusing image VAEs for video reconstruction causes inter-frame flickering due to lost temporal dependencies, and controllable video generation requires training a separate ControlNet for each visual condition modality, incurring significant cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240081/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a text prompt, an optional visual condition (image, Canny edge map, HED, or depth map) processed through a lightweight adapter, and an optional style reference image encoded by a frozen CLIP image encoder. It produces video clips of 16×256×256 (VersVideo-L, 500M params) or 24×576×320 (VersVideo-H, 2B params) using DDIM sampling with 30 steps and CFG scale 7.5. The style embedding is concatenated with the text embedding and fed into cross-attention modules in both the UNet and ControlNet.

## Method

VersVideo introduces three key components. First, Multi-Excitation Convolution (MEC) replaces standard 3D convolutions with four parallel paths—factorized 2D+1D convolution, spatial-temporal excitation, channel excitation, and motion excitation—each applying dimension pooling and TSM temporal shifts to model spatiotemporal dependencies at low cost. Second, Mixture-of-Experts Spatial-Temporal Attention (MoE-STA) augments factorized spatial and temporal attention with 32 spatial and 4 temporal FFN experts that are randomly assigned tokens during training and weight-averaged each iteration (λ=0.001); at inference all experts collapse into a single FFN with zero extra cost. Third, a Temporal Compensation Module (TCM) VAE decoder inserts MEC-based temporal blocks at multiple decoder levels plus a refinement UNet, trained with VQ-GAN loss and a TCM feature consistency loss to counteract temporal information lost during encoding. A unified ControlNet with per-condition adapters handles all visual conditions through a single shared network, while a CLIP-based linear adapter provides style control.

## Results

On MSR-VTT, VersVideo-H (2B parameters) achieves the best FVD of 421 and CLIPSIM of 0.3014 among all methods trained on WebVid-10M, outperforming SimDA (FVD 456, CLIPSIM 0.2945) and VideoComposer (FVD 580, CLIPSIM 0.2932). VersVideo-L (500M) reports FVD 620 and CLIPSIM 0.2796, competitive with VideoFusion (FVD 581, CLIPSIM 0.2795) despite having roughly 4× fewer parameters. On UCF-101, VersVideo-H achieves IS 81.3, FVD 119, and frame consistency 0.905, ranking second only to Make-A-Video (IS 82.55, FVD 81.25) which uses 9.72B parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VersVideo-H (2B) achieves the lowest FVD (421) and highest CLIPSIM (0.3014) among all methods trained solely on WebVid-10M, beating SimDA (FVD 456, CLIPSIM 0.2945), VideoComposer (FVD 580, CLIPSIM 0.2932), VideoFusion (FVD 581, CLIPSIM 0.2795), and Video-LDM (CLIPSIM 0.2929). The only methods with higher CLIPSIM are Make-A-Video (0.3049) and VideoFactory (0.3005), but both leverage additional large-scale datasets (HD-VILA-10M and HD-VG-130M respectively) beyond WebVid-10M. VersVideo-L (500M) trails SimDA on FVD (620 vs. 456) but matches VideoFusion on CLIPSIM (0.2796 vs. 0.2795) with roughly 4× fewer parameters. On UCF-101, VersVideo-H (IS 81.3, FVD 119) is second only to Make-A-Video (IS 82.55, FVD 81.25, 9.72B params), substantially outperforming VideoFusion (IS 80.03, FVD 173), TATS (IS 79.28, FVD 332), CogVideo (IS 50.46, FVD 626), DVD-GAN (IS 32.97), and MoCoGAN-HD (IS 12.42). In the user study, VersVideo is preferred over VideoFusion 83.3% of the time and over VideoComposer 88.5% of the time in overall video quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

VersVideo extends the Latent Diffusion Model (LDM) framework for video by replacing the standard 2D+1D separable operations in the UNet with Multi-Excitation Convolution (MEC), which draws on the squeeze-and-excitation paradigm (Hu et al., 2017) and temporal shift (Lin et al., 2018) to model spatial-temporal dependencies through parallel excitation paths. The Mixture-of-Experts spatial-temporal attention builds on the weight-averaging ideas from model soups (Wortsman et al., 2022) and expert-averaging for ViTs (Huang et al., 2023b), but applies them to video attention without a router network. For controllable generation, the paper unifies the per-modality ControlNet approach (Zhang & Agrawala, 2023; Chen et al., 2023b) into a single shared ControlNet with lightweight adapters, inspired by Uni-ControlNet (Zhao et al., 2023) in the image domain, and adapts IP-Adapter (Ye et al., 2023) for style conditioning. The TCM-VAE decoder extends the VQ-GAN (Esser et al., 2020) image VAE by injecting temporal compensation modules, addressing a known limitation of reusing image autoencoders for video.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play TCM-VAE decoder is practically valuable: since the encoder is frozen, it can be dropped into existing video diffusion models (demonstrated with VideoComposer and VideoFusion) to reduce flickering without retraining the full pipeline. The unified ControlNet with adapters reduces the cost of supporting multiple visual conditions from training one model per modality to a single shared model, which is a meaningful engineering simplification for production systems. However, the paper acknowledges significant open questions: generated videos are limited to fixed short lengths (16–24 frames), the training data (WebVid-10M) introduces watermarks, image-to-video stability remains imperfect, and the model inherits known image-diffusion failures (hand generation, text rendering). Extending to long-form video synthesis and improving identity preservation in image-to-video remain important directions.
