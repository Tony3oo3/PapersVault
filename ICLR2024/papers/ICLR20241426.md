## Problem

Self-supervised learning (SSL) pre-training is widely used for visual representation learning, but it remains unclear whether the learned representations encode spurious features that harm downstream performance on minority subgroups. The paper shows that common SSL augmentations create higher connectivity between subgroups sharing spurious attributes than between those sharing core features, and that classical debiasing strategies like re-sampling during pre-training do not consistently fix this issue.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241426/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images and produces fixed-dimensional visual representations via a ResNet encoder. During pre-training, each image is augmented twice to form a positive pair; one view is encoded by the full network while the other is encoded by a pruned variant of the same network. The downstream task is linear probing: a logistic regression head is trained on frozen SSL embeddings to predict class labels, with worst-group accuracy as the primary metric.

## Method

LATETVG (Late-layer Transformation-based View Generation) modifies the SSL positive-pair construction by routing one of the two augmented views through a transformed encoder in which a fixed percentage of weights in all layers deeper than a threshold L are zeroed out via magnitude pruning. The contrastive objective (SimSiam or SimCLR) then forces the representation of the pruned view to align with that of the full view, making the encoder invariant to late-layer perturbations. This regularizes the final layers to encode harder, more invariant features rather than easily-learned spurious shortcuts, without requiring any group or label information during pre-training. The paper provides a theoretical analysis showing that when spurious connectivity exceeds invariant connectivity in the augmentation graph, the spectral contrastive loss recovers spurious features with larger margin than core features, and empirically confirms that LATETVG reduces spurious connectivity while increasing invariant connectivity in the learned representation space.

## Results

LATETVG improves worst-group accuracy across all five spurious-correlation benchmarks for both SimSiam and SimCLR pretraining. The largest gains appear on MetaShift (+37.3 pp for SimSiam, from 42.3% to 79.6%) and Spurious CIFAR-10 (+18.0 pp for SimSiam, from 43.4% to 61.4%). On the larger-scale CelebA dataset, LATETVG still yields a +5.6 pp improvement (77.5% → 83.1% for SimSiam). The method also narrows the gap to supervised ERM pretraining by 17–50% relative improvement depending on the dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares LATETVG against two SSL baselines: SimSiam (Chen & He, 2020) and SimCLR (Chen et al., 2020a), both trained with a ResNet backbone and evaluated via linear probing on worst-group accuracy. LATETVG consistently outperforms both baselines on all five datasets for both SSL objectives. The most dramatic wins are on MetaShift, where SimSiam + LATETVG reaches 79.6% worst-group accuracy versus 42.3% for SimSiam alone (+37.3 pp), and on Spurious CIFAR-10 where the gain is +18.0 pp (43.4% → 61.4%). On the easier CMNIST dataset the improvement is modest (+2.4 pp for SimSiam), which the authors attribute to the baseline already performing well. For SimCLR, gains are somewhat smaller than for SimSiam (e.g., +13.8 pp on MetaShift vs. +37.3 pp), but LATETVG still wins on every dataset. Notably, on CelebA the LATETVG-augmented SimSiam model even surpasses the supervised ERM baseline, despite using no labels during pretraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of self-supervised representation learning (SimSiam, SimCLR) and the spurious-correlation/shortcut-learning literature. It builds directly on the SimSiam and SimCLR training pipelines, adding a late-layer pruning-based view generation module (LATETVG) that operates in representation space rather than image space. Conceptually, it departs from prior debiasing methods (GroupDRO, DFR, resampling) by showing that group-aware strategies do not transfer to the SSL pre-training stage, and from prior contrastive-learning robustness work (Robinson et al., 2021) by targeting the augmentation-induced connectivity structure rather than adversarially modifying features. The theoretical analysis draws on the spectral contrastive loss framework (HaoChen et al., 2021) and augmentation-graph analysis (Shen et al., 2022) to formalize how standard augmentations create spurious connectivity that biases downstream linear classifiers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete mechanistic explanation for why SSL representations can be biased toward spurious features: standard augmentations create higher connectivity between subgroups sharing the spurious attribute than those sharing the core attribute, and this is not fixable by simple data rebalancing. The negative result that resampling during SSL pre-training fails to improve worst-group accuracy challenges a natural assumption and redirects attention toward representation-space interventions. Limitations include evaluation on only five relatively small datasets with ResNet backbones and linear-probe evaluation, leaving open whether LATETVG scales to larger architectures (ViTs), non-linear downstream heads, or ImageNet-scale data. The approach's simplicity (magnitude pruning on late layers) makes it easy to adopt, but the optimal pruning percentage and layer depth remain dataset-dependent hyperparameters.
