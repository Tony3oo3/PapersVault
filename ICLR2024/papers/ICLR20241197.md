## Problem

Vision-language models like CLIP, when finetuned on in-distribution datasets via prompt learning, overfit known classes with degraded performance on unknown (OOD) classes, especially under long training schedules. No explicit regularization mechanism has been explored to address this pitfall, and the fundamental challenge is the missing knowledge about the vast space of unknown classes that could provide useful supervision signals to regularize the ID-OOD decision boundary.

## Input / Output

The method takes the text name of any unknown class and synthesizes its image features in the CLIP joint feature space without requiring labeled instances. During finetuning, the model jointly discriminates real in-distribution image features (from the frozen CLIP image encoder) against both known and synthesized unknown class text embeddings, producing class predictions over the combined set of C_b known and C_n unknown classes. The feature generator itself is a lightweight module (one MHCA layer + one LN layer) that consumes the unknown class text feature plus kNN known-class text and image features as auxiliary context.

## Method

OGEN introduces a class-conditional feature generator that synthesizes OOD image features via an 'extrapolating bias': given an unknown class name, it retrieves kNN semantically similar known classes (K=3 by default), samples one image feature per neighbor, and uses Multi-Head Cross-Attention to extrapolate a new feature vector from these anchors, with the unknown class text feature as query. These synthesized features are jointly optimized with real ID features under the standard CLIP contrastive objective, regularizing the decision boundary between known and unknown classes. To further prevent overfitting during long finetuning, an Adaptive Local Mean Teacher (ALMT) self-distillation mechanism enforces prediction consistency between the current feature generator (student) and an EMA teacher computed over a time-adaptive local window (m_t growing from 2 to 9 via a cosine schedule), which avoids both underfit early checkpoints and overfit recent ones. The approach is model-agnostic and plugs into any prompt-learning finetuner (CoOp, CoCoOp, VPT, SHIP, KgCoOp, MaPLe, PromptSRC) without modifying the frozen CLIP backbone.

## Results

On the base-to-new class generalization setting (average across 11 datasets), OGEN consistently improves new class accuracy for all seven prompt-learning baselines, with the largest gain over CoOp (63.22% → 69.54%, +6.32%) and harmonic mean improvements of +4.21 (CoOp), +1.67 (SHIP), and +1.40 (KgCoOp). The best absolute harmonic mean is 80.34% (OGEN-PromptSRC) vs. 79.97% without OGEN. In the cross-dataset setting (ImageNet → 10 targets), OGEN uniformly improves both CoOp and CoCoOp on every target dataset, with the largest gains on distributionally distant datasets like DTD (+2.98%), EuroSAT (+3.14%), and UCF101 (+1.88%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OGEN is applied as a plug-in on top of seven prompt-learning baselines and improves new-class (OOD) accuracy in every case. The largest relative gains appear over CoOp (200-epoch schedule, most severe overfitting): new-class accuracy jumps from 63.22% to 69.54% and harmonic mean from 71.66% to 75.87%. Over the shorter-schedule baselines CoCoOp and VPT (10 epochs), gains are smaller (+1.66% and +1.60% new-class) because these methods overfit less. Among the state-of-the-art methods, OGEN improves SHIP by +2.45% and KgCoOp by +2.08% on new-class accuracy, while gains over MaPLe (+1.23%) and PromptSRC (+0.76%) are more modest, consistent with their shorter training runs (5 and 20 epochs). Base-class accuracy is maintained or slightly improved in most cases (e.g., CoOp 82.69→83.47, SHIP 80.03→80.79), with only minor drops for CoCoOp (80.47→79.86) and PromptSRC (84.26→84.17). In the cross-dataset setting, OGEN-CoOp and OGEN-CoCoOp outperform their unregularized counterparts on all 10 target datasets, with the most pronounced improvements on DTD, EuroSAT, and UCF101 where distribution shift from ImageNet is largest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prompt-learning finetuning line (CoOp, CoCoOp, VPT, SHIP, KgCoOp, MaPLe, PromptSRC) by identifying and addressing a previously under-explored pitfall: overfitting to base classes during long finetuning runs that degrades OOD generalization. The class-conditional feature generator draws on the outlier-synthesis literature (VOS, non-parametric outlier synthesis) but repurposes it in a CLIP-native, class-conditional manner using kNN extrapolation from known-class features. The adaptive self-distillation component (ALMT) extends Mean Teacher (Tarvainen & Valpola, 2017) with a local time window and cosine-scheduled window size, creating a curriculum that avoids both underfit and overfit teacher checkpoints. The approach is explicitly orthogonal to the finetuning method itself, positioning OGEN as a plug-in regularizer rather than a new finetuning paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The overfitting phenomenon documented here is a practical concern for anyone finetuning CLIP with long schedules, and OGEN provides a lightweight, model-agnostic remedy that consistently improves OOD accuracy across 11 datasets and 7 baselines without sacrificing ID performance. The 3.5×–7.5× data-efficiency advantage over replay-based OOD sampling (Appendix D) makes the approach attractive in memory-constrained settings. Open questions remain: the method is only validated on prompt learning (not adaptor tuning or full finetuning), the 'unknown unknowns' generalization is tested on held-out classes within the same dataset rather than truly open-domain scenarios, and the paper does not evaluate OOD detection (rejection) performance, which is a natural next step the authors themselves flag.
