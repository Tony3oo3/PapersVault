## Problem

For equivariant neural networks, the most common point-wise activations (ReLU, tanh, etc.) are not equivariant under many symmetry groups, yet no complete characterization existed of which combinations of representations, coordinate choices, and activations yield exactly equivariant layers. The prior result of Wood & Shawe-Taylor (1996) was restricted to finite groups, could not distinguish isomorphic representations, and covered only a narrow subclass of activation functions, leaving the design space of equivariant architectures largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The theorem takes as input a symmetry group G, a finite-dimensional real representation ρ: G → GL(V), a class F of continuous scalar activation functions, and a basis B of V (i.e., a choice of coordinates). It produces a complete, exhaustive list of maximal admissible pairs (F, M) where M is the group of representation matrices in GL_n(R) and F is the family of scalar functions whose induced point-wise activation commutes with every matrix in M. The classification is given up to isomorphism of representations, so equivalent coordinate choices are identified, and the result applies to both finite and non-finite (e.g., compact continuous) groups.

## Method

The core mechanism is a duality between activation function classes and matrix groups, formalized via two operators: M(F) maps a family of activations to the maximal group of matrices commuting with all induced point-wise activations, and F(M) maps a matrix group to the maximal family of activations commuting with all its matrices. A stabilization lemma proves that composing these operators twice yields a fixed point, so it suffices to classify only the maximal elements. The classification reduces to the structure of multiplicative subgroups of R (trivial, discrete ⟨b^n⟩, discrete ⟨±b^n⟩, dense on R_{>0}, or dense on R), yielding exactly five maximal admissible pairs: (continuous functions, permutation matrices), (odd continuous functions, signed permutation matrices), (semilinear functions, non-negative monomial matrices), (b-multiplicative functions, b-monomial matrices), and (±b-multiplicative functions, ±b-monomial matrices). For compact groups, a separate argument using continuity and compactness shows that any admissible representation is isomorphic to a (signed) permutation representation, which admits the widest activation family (all continuous or odd continuous functions). This framework then yields concrete corollaries: rotation-equivariant (SO(3)) networks with point-wise activations are necessarily invariant, disentangled steerable CNNs admit point-wise activations only for trivial representations, and Invariant Graph Networks admit a broader class of permutation-equivariant layers parameterized by arbitrary subgroups of S_n beyond those previously identified.

## Results

The paper's central result (Theorem 1) establishes that there are exactly five maximal admissible pairs of (activation family, matrix group) for equivariant layers with point-wise activations: (1) continuous functions ↔ permutation matrices, (2) odd continuous functions ↔ signed permutation matrices, (3) semilinear functions ↔ non-negative monomial matrices, (4) b-multiplicative functions ↔ b-monomial matrices, and (5) ±b-multiplicative functions ↔ ±b-monomial matrices. Key corollaries show that rotation-equivariant networks (SO(3)) with point-wise activations can only be invariant (Corollary 3), disentangled steerable CNN feature spaces must be trivial representations (Corollary 2), and E(3)×S_n-equivariant geometric graph networks are necessarily rotation-invariant (Corollary 4). For finite groups, admissible representations reduce to permutation representations up to positive basis scaling (Corollary 1), fully characterizing the representation spaces of Invariant Graph Networks in terms of subgroups of the symmetric group.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines or quantitative comparisons against competing methods. The primary prior work it extends is Wood & Shawe-Taylor (1996), whose characterization theorem had three limitations: (i) it applied only to finite groups, (ii) its classification was redundant (could not identify isomorphic representations), and (iii) it was restricted to a narrow subclass of activation functions. The present paper overcomes all three gaps by generalizing to arbitrary (including continuous) groups, working up to isomorphism of representations, and considering the full class of continuous functions. The paper also subsumes and strengthens the Wood & Shawe-Taylor result as a special case (Theorem 2 for compact groups), and provides the first complete characterization of when disentangled steerable CNNs (Cohen & Welling, 2016b) admit point-wise activations—showing they do so only for trivial representations, a result not previously established.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends and strengthens the characterization theorem of Wood & Shawe-Taylor (1996), which was the only prior result classifying admissible point-wise activations for equivariant networks but was limited to finite groups, produced redundant classifications under isomorphism, and covered only a narrow subclass of activations. The authors generalize to arbitrary (including non-finite and continuous) groups, continuous activations, and isomorphism-aware representation classes, grounding the classification in the structure of multiplicative subgroups of ℝ. The practical implications are then drawn from the steerable CNN / disentangled representation framework of Cohen & Welling (2016b) and the Invariant Graph Network literature of Maron et al. (2018), showing how the theorem constrains or expands the design space of these widely-used architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theorem provides a complete, exhaustive catalog of which point-wise activations can coexist with which representation matrices in an equivariant layer, giving practitioners a definitive reference for architecture design. The most consequential negative result is that rotation-equivariant (SO(3)) networks with point-wise activations are necessarily invariant, ruling out their use for equivariant tasks like segmentation or detection—a significant barrier for a large body of 3D steerable CNN work. The finding that disentangled feature spaces must be trivial representations similarly limits the practical utility of the popular steerable CNN decomposition. Open questions include whether analogous characterizations exist for non-point-wise activations (norm, squashing, tensor-product, gated), for complex-valued networks, and for infinite-dimensional representations where discretization breaks exact equivariance.
