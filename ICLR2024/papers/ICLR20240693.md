## Problem

Differentiable structure learning methods such as NOTEARS and DAGMA enforce acyclicity through a constraint evaluated at every optimization step, incurring O(d³) computational cost that severely limits scalability to large graphs. This paper asks whether one can learn acyclic DAGs entirely without evaluating any acyclicity constraint while still provably converging to acyclic solutions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240693/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a data matrix of n=1000 observations over d variables (linear or non-linear SEM) and outputs a weighted adjacency matrix W ∈ ℝ^{d×d} representing a DAG, decomposed as the element-wise product of a directed adjacency matrix H and a smooth orientation matrix S_{t,ε}(p) parameterized by a priority vector p ∈ ℝ^d. The priority vector encodes a topological ordering where each node feeds into nodes with sufficiently higher priority (difference ≥ ε), and the temperature t is annealed from t_start to t_end≈0 during training to sharpen the sigmoid toward the discrete orientation.

## Method

COSMO introduces a smooth orientation matrix S_{t,ε}(p) whose entries are tempered sigmoids σ_{t,ε}(p_v − p_u) applied to pairwise priority differences, providing a differentiable relaxation of a discrete acyclic orientation matrix. The key insight is that any DAG can be exactly represented as W = H ∘ S_{t,ε}(p) in the limit t→0 (Theorem 1), so acyclicity is guaranteed by construction without ever computing the NOTEARS trace constraint. Training solves a single unconstrained optimization problem with L1 sparsity on H, L2 regularization on H and p, while cosine-annealing the temperature from t_start to t_end. Theorem 2 proves that this annealing monotonically decreases an upper bound e^{dα}−1 on the NOTEARS acyclicity measure, where α=σ(−ε/t), connecting the constraint-free scheme to existing constrained formulations. The per-step cost is O(d²) for constructing W, an order of magnitude faster than the O(d³) matrix-exponential evaluation required by NOTEARS-like methods.

## Results

On ER-4 graphs with 30 nodes and Gaussian noise, COSMO achieves AUC 0.984±0.02 in 88s, comparable to DAGMA (0.985±0.01, 781s) and NOTEARS (0.973±0.02, 5193s). At 100 nodes, COSMO reaches AUC 0.961±0.03 in 99s versus DAGMA's 0.982±0.01 in 661s, while at 500 nodes COSMO achieves 0.933±0.01 in 437s versus DAGMA's 0.980±0.00 in 2485s. COSMO consistently outperforms NOCURL (e.g., 0.984 vs 0.967 AUC at d=30) and dramatically outperforms the unconstrained variant NOCURL-U (0.984 vs 0.694 AUC at d=30), while being 1–2 orders of magnitude faster than all constrained baselines due to its O(d²) per-step complexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COSMO achieves AUC within 0.01–0.05 of DAGMA and NOTEARS on most settings while being 5–100× faster (e.g., 99s vs 661s for DAGMA and 11000s for NOTEARS at d=100, Gaussian). On denser ER-6 graphs and larger sizes, DAGMA retains a 2–5 AUC point advantage (e.g., 0.980 vs 0.933 at d=500, ER-4 Gaussian), but COSMO's quadratic scaling means it remains the only method alongside NOCURL-U that completes within the 20000s wall-time limit at d=500. COSMO consistently outperforms NOCURL on AUC across all tested configurations (e.g., 0.984 vs 0.967 at d=30, 0.985 vs 0.950 at d=100 for Exp noise), confirming that jointly learning priorities and adjacencies without a preliminary constrained solution is more effective. The comparison with NOCURL-U is particularly striking: COSMO's smooth orientation parameterization yields 0.25–0.30 AUC improvement over the ReLU-based unconstrained variant on every dataset, demonstrating the critical role of the tempered-sigmoid formulation. Against GOLEM (Appendix E.17), COSMO achieves zero acyclicity by construction while GOLEM retains non-trivial h(W) values (up to 4.654 at d=100, ER-6), and COSMO matches or exceeds GOLEM's AUC in all reported settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

COSMO builds directly on the NOTEARS framework (Zheng et al., 2018), which established the trace-of-matrix-exponential acyclicity constraint for continuous DAG structure learning, and on NOCURL (Yu et al., 2021), which similarly decouples graph orientation from adjacencies via a priority vector. The key conceptual departure is replacing the cubic-cost acyclicity constraint with a smooth orientation matrix (a tempered sigmoid over priority differences) that guarantees acyclicity by construction, eliminating the need to evaluate the constraint at any optimization step. Unlike NOCURL, which requires a preliminary constrained solution to extract the topological ordering, COSMO jointly learns priorities and adjacencies in a single unconstrained problem with temperature annealing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing per-step complexity from O(d³) to O(d²) while provably guaranteeing acyclicity (Theorem 1) and connecting temperature annealing to a decreasing upper bound on the NOTEARS constraint (Theorem 2), COSMO offers a drop-in, faster replacement for the acyclicity module in continuous causal discovery pipelines. The paper acknowledges that, like all NOTEARS-derived methods using MSE loss, the approach inherits sensitivity to variable variance and may not be directly significant for causal discovery without appropriate loss functions. Open questions include whether the smooth orientation parameterization extends naturally to latent-variable or interventional settings, and whether the priority vector regularization (shown to matter for small graphs) admits a more principled choice.
