## Problem

Uncertainty estimation for semantic segmentation suffers from a gap between theoretical claims and practical validation: methods assert they capture aleatoric or epistemic uncertainty without explicit evidence, aggregation strategies are often neglected or oversimplified, and methods are rarely evaluated across the full spectrum of downstream applications. This leaves fundamental questions unanswered—whether AU/EU separation is feasible in practice, which method components matter, and which method suits which task.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes 3D CT volume crops (64×64×64 from LIDC-IDRI), 2D street-scene images (256×478 from GTA5/Cityscapes), and synthetic 3D toy data, each split into i.i.d. and OoD subsets with controlled ambiguity (multi-rater disagreement) and distribution shift (metadata or domain changes). Each prediction model produces one or multiple segmentation outputs per input, from which pixel-level uncertainty heatmaps are computed and then aggregated via image-level sum, patch-level max, or threshold-level mean into a single scalar score per image for downstream task evaluation.

## Method

VALUES is a systematic evaluation framework built on three requirements: (R1) a controlled environment with multi-rater references for AU and induced distribution shifts for EU, (R2) ablation of four method components (C0 backbone, C1 prediction model, C2 uncertainty measure, C3 aggregation strategy), and (R3) test-beds for five downstream tasks (OoD detection, active learning, failure detection, calibration, ambiguity modeling). The framework derives new uncertainty decompositions for TTA models (showing MI(Y,T|x) captures EU, not AU as previously claimed) and SSN models (showing MI(Y,Z|x) captures AU and expected entropy captures EU). Five prediction models—deterministic softmax, MC-Dropout, 5-model ensemble, TTA, and SSN—are evaluated across three aggregation strategies on three datasets, revealing that aggregation is a critical but neglected component, ensembles are most robust across tasks, and TTA is a lightweight EU alternative.

## Results

On the toy dataset, AU-measures (EE, MI for SSN) achieve NCC scores of 0.82–0.96 versus 0.47–0.80 for EU-measures, confirming clean AU/EU separation in controlled settings. On real-world LIDC-IDRI, this separation degrades substantially (NCC 0.32–0.64), with EU-measures often matching or exceeding AU-measures (e.g., SSN EE=0.64 vs. SSN MI=0.59 on LIDC TEX i.i.d.). For OoD detection (AUROC), EU-measures consistently outperform AU-measures across all datasets (e.g., Ensemble MI achieves 0.95 AUROC on Toy 2 vs. 0.94 for EE), while ensembles and TTA are the most robust prediction models across all five downstream tasks. The aggregation strategy (C3) shows the largest dataset-dependent variability, with differences between aggregation methods sometimes exceeding the standard deviation across all other components.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper systematically compares five prediction models (Deterministic, TTD, Ensemble, TTA, SSN) across three uncertainty types (PU, AU, EU) and three aggregation strategies on four datasets and five downstream tasks. Ensembles are the only prediction model that consistently performs above average across all datasets for OoD detection and failure detection, while TTA closely follows and serves as a lightweight alternative (4–16 forward passes vs. 5 full models). SSNs dominate ambiguity modeling (NCC 0.70 on GTA5/CS i.i.d. vs. ≤0.28 for all other models) but underperform in failure detection, revealing a critical trade-off. For calibration, TTD and SSNs perform best, with TTD achieving near-average or above-average ACE across all settings. A key finding is that TTA's MI-based EU-measure behaves similarly to ensemble MI, resolving prior contradictions in the literature (Wang et al. 2019, Ayhan & Berens 2018 claimed TTA models AU; Hu et al. 2019 claimed EU) by demonstrating TTA is in fact best suited for EU estimation. The aggregation strategy shows the largest dataset-dependent variability, with image-level aggregation best for GTA5/CS, patch-level best for LIDC, and threshold-level intermediate—highlighting that no single aggregation generalizes across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Bayesian uncertainty decomposition framework of Kendall & Gal (2017) and the subsequent proliferation of uncertainty methods for segmentation (MC-Dropout, ensembles, TTA, SSNs), but departs from prior work by treating the *validation* of these methods as the central contribution rather than proposing a new method. It identifies three systematic pitfalls in how the field has evaluated uncertainty—lack of explicit AU/EU separation testing, neglect of the aggregation component (C3), and sparse downstream-task coverage—and constructs a controlled framework (VALUES) that addresses all three simultaneously. The paper also resolves a direct contradiction in the literature: Wang et al. (2019) and Ayhan & Berens (2018) claimed TTA captures aleatoric uncertainty, while Hu et al. (2019) claimed it captures epistemic uncertainty, without either side providing quantitative evidence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first systematic, multi-dataset, multi-task validation of uncertainty methods in segmentation, yielding actionable recommendations (e.g., ensembles are most robust, TTA is a lightweight EU alternative, aggregation strategy is critical and dataset-dependent). A key open question it surfaces is whether the AU/EU separation, which holds cleanly on controlled toy data, can ever be reliably exploited on real-world data where ambiguity and distribution shift are entangled. The finding that aggregation (C3) is a first-order determinant of downstream performance—yet is routinely simplified or omitted in prior work—suggests a large body of existing results may be confounded, motivating re-evaluation under the proposed protocol.
