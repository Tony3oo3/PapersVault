## Problem

Signal delay—the lag between an agent's perception of the environment and its actions—is pervasive in real-world applications (robotics, autonomous driving, finance, telemedicine) yet has been almost entirely ignored in deep reinforcement learning research. Standard DRL algorithms (DDPG, TD3, SAC) and even RNN-based POMDP methods suffer catastrophic performance collapse with even a single-step delay, dropping over 79% when delay exceeds four steps. This paper formalizes the problem as a Delayed-Observation MDP (DOMDP) and develops principled solutions for continuous control.

## Input / Output

The actor receives a delayed state observation s̃_t = s_{t-ΔT} augmented with historical actions a_{t-ΔT:t-1} (concatenated for MLP, fed sequentially for RNN/Transformer), while the critic is trained on the recovered non-delayed true state s_t that becomes available only after post-hoc time-calibration of logged trajectories. The output is a continuous control action in the standard MuJoCo action space. The asymmetric design is critical: the critic's forward pass is never needed during deployment, so it can exploit oracle information unavailable to the actor at inference time.

## Method

The framework builds on actor-critic architectures (primarily SAC) with two core components. First, Delay-Reconciled Training for the Critic: after each episode, historical data is time-calibrated to recover non-delayed states, and the critic is trained on these recovered states while the actor continues to operate on delayed observations—an asymmetric observation design that avoids the gradient bias of symmetric alternatives. Second, State Augmentation for the Actor: historical actions a_{t-ΔT:t-1} are concatenated with the delayed state to form s̄_t, which Theorem 4.1 proves restores the Markovian property, converting the DOMDP back into a standard MDP. Complementary techniques include a detached prediction network (predicting s_t from s̄_t) and a detached encoding network (producing hidden features z_t), both providing auxiliary self-supervised signals that further improve performance under fixed delays but can degrade under unfixed or probabilistic settings.

## Results

On four MuJoCo continuous control tasks (Ant, HalfCheetah, Hopper, Walker2d) with signal delay, the best method (State Augmentation-MLP + Encoding†) achieves 84.5% normalized performance at fixed delay ≥4 steps and 77.9% at unfixed delay ≥4 steps, compared to 8.4% (fixed) and -1.4% (unfixed) for vanilla SAC. Even a single-step delay drops vanilla SAC from 100% to 40.3% (fixed) and 62.5% (unfixed), while delays beyond 4 steps cause near-total failure for all standard algorithms. The Delay-Reconciled Training for Critic alone lifts SAC from 8.4% to 50.8% (fixed) and -1.4% to 48.1% (unfixed) at delay ≥4, and adding State Augmentation-MLP further improves to 75.9% and 77.0% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method (State Augmentation-MLP + Encoding†) substantially outperforms all six baselines at delay ≥4 steps. Against vanilla SAC (8.4% fixed, -1.4% unfixed), the improvement is ~10x; against the strongest existing delay-aware method DATS (25.8% fixed, 30.8% unfixed), it achieves 84.5% and 77.9% respectively. The POMDP baselines RNN Strong (23.7%) and VRM (29.1% fixed) also fall far short, confirming that generic partially-observable approaches do not transfer to the delay-specific structure of DOMDPs. Notably, the asymmetric design (critic sees non-delayed s_t, actor sees delayed s̃_t) is critical: the symmetric variant (both see s̃_t) drops to 38.6% fixed, well below the asymmetric 84.5%. Among the actor encoders, MLP with concatenated historical actions dominates RNN (27.8%) and Transformer (35.1%), and the detached prediction/encoding supervision (†) consistently outperforms non-detached variants (e.g., Encoding† 84.5% vs. Encoding 76.1% fixed). However, in unfixed-delay and probabilistic environments, the explicit prediction/encoding supervision provides diminishing or even negative returns, with State Augmentation-MLP alone (77.0% unfixed) matching or exceeding Prediction† (72.5% unfixed).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the POMDP framework to a new special case—Delayed-Observation MDP (DOMDP)—and draws on control-theoretic results (Katsikopoulos & Engelbrecht, 2003) to prove that all forms of signal delay (inference, action, observation) are equivalent, reducing the problem to a single observation-delay parameter. It departs from prior POMDP approaches (RNN-based, belief-state) by showing they fail catastrophically under delay, and from the sole prior DRLwD work (Chen et al., 2021) by removing the assumption of known reward and handling unfixed delays. The solution builds on the asymmetric actor-critic paradigm (Vinyals et al., Baisero et al.) but introduces two novel components: delay-reconciled critic training using time-calibrated offline data, and state augmentation via concatenated historical actions grounded in a Markovian property theorem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Signal delay is ubiquitous in real-world DRL deployments (autonomous driving, robotics, finance, telemedicine), yet the paper demonstrates that even a single-step delay causes near-total collapse of standard algorithms like SAC and DDPG, making this a critical practical gap. The proposed solutions are simple, general, and pluggable into any actor-critic framework, which increases their adoption potential. However, the work is limited to simulated MuJoCo continuous control; real-world deployment with sensor noise, variable network conditions, and non-actor-critic architectures remains open. The finding that explicit prediction/encoding supervision actively harms performance in complex or stochastic environments is an important caution for practitioners considering model-based augmentation.
