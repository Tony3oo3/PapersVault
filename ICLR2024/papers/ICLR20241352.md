## Problem

Counterfactual data—what would have happened to a patient under an alternative treatment—are fundamentally unobservable, making individual treatment effect (ITE) estimation infeasible without sophisticated generative models. Existing methods such as synthetic control and SyncTwin approximate pre-treatment matching but do not guarantee exact equality between generated counterfactuals and observed factual data in the pre-treatment period, violating the causal principle that effects cannot propagate backward in time.

## Input / Output

The method consumes longitudinal observational data with D dimensions (outcomes plus time-varying covariates) over T time steps, a binary treatment indicator per unit, and a specified intervention time T0. Unlike SyncTwin, which uses only pre-treatment covariates and post-treatment outcomes, SSA-GMM leverages both pre-treatment outcomes and post-treatment covariates to inform the latent state. The output is a full counterfactual trajectory that exactly equals the factual trajectory for t ≤ T0, plus a closed-form ITE estimate with a time-varying variance (Eq. 14) that quantifies confidence in the generated counterfactual relative to the true value.

## Method

The framework models each unit as a static latent state s ∈ R^M observed through time-varying linear observers W^(t) ∈ R^{D×M} with additive Gaussian noise, and places an ω-conditional Gaussian mixture prior over states trained via EM. The key geometric insight is that the counterfactual state must differ from the factual state only along the null space N^(T0) of the first T0 pre-treatment observers, enforcing the no-backward-causation constraint W^(t)(s̄ − s) = 0 for t ≤ T0. Theorem 2.3 then yields a closed-form generator: x̄^(t) = x^(t) + W^(t) N^(T0) ξ, where ξ is drawn from a conditional GMM whose parameters (Eqs. 8–10) are derived analytically via two lemmas on conditioning a multivariate normal and a GMM to an affine subspace. This construction guarantees exact pre-treatment matching (Corollary 2.3.1) and provides closed-form expressions for the ITE expectation and its time-varying variance (Theorem 2.4), enabling uncertainty quantification without Monte Carlo sampling.

## Results

On the simulated LDL cholesterol dataset, SSA-GMM achieves a mean absolute error (MAE) of 0.069–0.076 on ITE estimation across all confounding bias levels (p₀ = 0.1, 0.25, 0.5) and both data sizes (N = 400 and N = 1200), which is essentially at the irreducible noise floor of ≈0.080 for σ = 0.1. The next-best method, SyncTwin, achieves MAE of 0.094–0.308, while all other baselines (SC, RSC, MC-NNM, CFRNet, CRN, RMSN, CGP) range from 0.113 to 1.160. Notably, SSA-GMM's MAE is nearly invariant to confounding bias, whereas baselines degrade substantially as p₀ decreases (e.g., SyncTwin jumps from 0.094 to 0.308 as p₀ goes from 0.5 to 0.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSA-GMM outperforms all eight baselines across every combination of confounding bias and data size. The closest competitor, SyncTwin, achieves MAE of 0.094 at the most favorable setting (N=1200, p₀=0.5) but degrades to 0.308 under strong confounding (N=400, p₀=0.1), whereas SSA-GMM remains stable at 0.072. Synthetic control methods (SC, RSC) are consistently worse, with SC at 0.158–0.341 and RSC at 0.284–0.846. Neural-based methods show the largest gaps: CFRNet (0.113–0.888), CRN (0.371–0.631), RMSN (0.538–0.720), and CGP (0.552–0.825) all lag by at least 2× in MAE. MC-NNM is the worst performer at small sample sizes (MAE up to 1.160) but narrows the gap at N=1200 (0.124). The key differentiator is robustness: SSA-GMM's MAE varies by less than 0.007 across all p₀ values, while every baseline shows significant sensitivity to confounding bias.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant synthetic control method (SCM) family—including SyncTwin, robust SC, and matrix completion approaches—which generate counterfactuals as convex combinations of real control units. Instead, it repurposes the state-space representation from control theory (Brogan, 1991) combined with a Gaussian mixture model prior over latent static states, framing counterfactual generation as sampling from the null space of pre-treatment observers. This yields a probabilistic generative model rather than an optimization-based matching procedure, and it is the first to guarantee exact (not approximate) pre-treatment data matching as a structural property of the construction. The EM-based parameter estimation and the conditional distribution lemmas (Lemmas 2.1–2.2) are standard tools from Bayesian inference and multivariate statistics, applied here in a novel combination for causal counterfactual generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exact pre-treatment matching guarantee and the closed-form ITE expectation/variance formulas offer a level of interpretability and uncertainty quantification that deep-learning baselines (CFRNet, CRN, RMSN) and SCM variants do not provide. However, the method's reliance on linear observers and a GMM prior may limit its applicability to complex, nonlinear clinical trajectories, and all empirical validation is on a single simulated LDL-cholesterol dataset derived from a PK/PD model—no real clinical trial data are used. Open questions include whether the linear observer assumption holds for multi-modal or high-dimensional biomarker data, how the method scales to more than two treatment arms, and whether the EM convergence to local optima (acknowledged by the authors) affects counterfactual quality in practice.
