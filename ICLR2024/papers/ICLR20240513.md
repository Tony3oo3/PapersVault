## Problem

Standard calibration techniques for language models are either post-processing methods (e.g., temperature scaling) that cannot reorder confidence rankings among candidate generations, or training-based methods (e.g., label smoothing) that require fine-tuning the entire model, which is impractical for billion-parameter LMs. Additionally, existing calibration evaluation focuses almost exclusively on short-answer QA, leaving calibration over long-form (sentence- and paragraph-level) generations largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

LITCAB consumes the sequence of last-layer hidden states from the frozen LM (one d-dimensional vector per generated token position) and produces a per-position logit bias vector of dimension V (vocabulary size). The bias is added element-wise to the LM's predicted logits before the softmax, yielding calibrated token probabilities whose geometric mean over the sequence gives a single scalar confidence score. For paragraph-level generations, confidence is estimated per claim by aggregating token probabilities over the span corresponding to each extracted claim.

## Method

LITCAB consists of a single linear layer (parameters W ∈ R^{V×d} and b ∈ R^V) applied independently at each token position to the LM's last-layer hidden state, producing a bias term added to the LM's logits. It is trained with a contrastive max-margin objective that pushes the calibrated probability of correct generations above that of incorrect ones by at least a margin of 1, using references as positives and sampled incorrect model generations as negatives (3 per question for short-form; all generated claims for paragraph-level). This design lets LITCAB reorder confidence rankings—something post-processing methods cannot do—while adding fewer than 2% of the original model's parameters. For paragraph-level calibration, the paper introduces a four-step claim-level evaluation pipeline: claim extraction via GPT-3.5-turbo, span mapping back to the generated text, per-span confidence estimation via geometric mean of token probabilities, and correctness verification via retrieval-augmented GPT-4 judgment against Wikipedia passages.

## Results

On the CAT benchmark (8 text generation tasks spanning phrase, sentence, and paragraph levels), LITCAB reduces the average ECE of Llama2-7B by 32%, from 0.137 to 0.093, and the average Brier score from 0.213 to 0.197. On the NQ task specifically, LITCAB achieves a 41% ECE reduction (0.171 → 0.101). LITCAB achieves the best average ECE (0.093) and Brier (0.197) among all compared methods, while adding fewer than 2% extra parameters to the base LM. When combined with temperature scaling, LITCAB further improves ECE on phrase- and sentence-level tasks (e.g., NQ: 0.101 → 0.083) but slightly degrades paragraph-level calibration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LITCAB outperforms all five baselines on average ECE across the 8 CAT tasks (0.093 vs. 0.137 Original, 0.191 Label Smoothing, 0.145 Temp. Scaling). Against the LM-specific confidence estimation methods (evaluated on the 5 phrase/sentence tasks where they are applicable), LITCAB achieves a lower average ECE of ~0.089 versus 0.125 for Self-Consistency, 0.158 for P(IK), and 0.516 for Verbalization. Label Smoothing (full fine-tuning via LoRA) performs worse than the uncalibrated model on most tasks, likely due to overfitting on small training sets, whereas LITCAB benefits from its data efficiency. Temperature Scaling alone provides only marginal improvement (0.145 avg ECE) and cannot reorder confidence rankings, while LITCAB can. Self-Consistency is competitive on individual tasks (e.g., TruthfulQA ECE 0.060 vs. LITCAB 0.105) but incurs N× higher inference cost and cannot be applied to paragraph-level claim evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

LITCAB sits at the intersection of two calibration literatures: post-processing methods (temperature scaling, Platt scaling) that adjust confidence sharpness without reordering outputs, and training-based methods (label smoothing, mix-up, regularization) that can reorder confidence but require full-model fine-tuning. By training a single linear layer on the LM's last hidden states with a max-margin objective, LITCAB inherits the expressivity of training-based calibration while adding fewer than 2% parameters, effectively repurposing the linear-probe paradigm from classification into sequence-level logit adjustment. On the evaluation side, the paper extends prior calibration benchmarks—which have focused almost exclusively on short-answer QA (Tian et al., 2023; Xiong et al., 2023)—to paragraph-level generation via a claim-level decomposition procedure, filling a gap the authors explicitly identify in the literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value of LITCAB lies in its drop-in simplicity: any LM with accessible hidden states can be calibrated with minimal compute, making hallucination filtering via confidence thresholds feasible for deployed systems. The empirical finding that GPT-2 XL (1.5B) outperforms LLaMA-30B on calibration, and that instruction-tuned Vicuna-13B is worse calibrated than its LLaMA-13B base, challenges the assumption that scale and fine-tuning monotonically improve reliability. Open questions include whether the max-margin objective generalizes to multi-turn or multi-claim interactions, whether the claim-level evaluation (which relies on GPT-4 as a judge and a T5 retriever) introduces systematic bias, and whether LITCAB's benefits persist when the base LM is itself fine-tuned for a downstream domain.
