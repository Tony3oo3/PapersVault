## Problem

Existing methods for deploying fine-tuned LLMs on edge devices face a fundamental tension: QLoRA enables memory-efficient fine-tuning with NF4-quantized weights but produces an FP16 model at inference, while post-training quantization (PTQ) of the merged weights causes significant accuracy degradation at low bit widths (INT3, INT2). The root cause is an imbalance between the number of quantization parameters (one scaling/zero pair per column) and LoRA adaptation parameters (D_in × D_int + D_int × D_out), which prevents the adapter from being absorbed into the quantized representation without breaking the low-bit arithmetic structure.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained LLM weight matrices (e.g., LLaMA-7B through 65B, LLaMA2-7B/13B) stored in FP16, along with instruction-tuning datasets (Alpaca 52K, FLAN v2 320K subset). During fine-tuning, the base weights are quantized to INT4 with group-wise scaling/zero factors, and the input feature vector x is passed through a parameter-free 1D average-pooling operation (group size D_in/L) before interacting with the reduced LoRA matrix A. The output is a single, fully quantized model (INT4, INT3, or INT2) where the LoRA adapter has been algebraically merged into the quantized weight matrix by adjusting only the zero factors, requiring no separate adapter storage at inference.

## Method

QA-LoRA introduces group-wise operators that simultaneously increase the number of quantization parameters (from D_out to L×D_out scaling/zero pairs) and decrease the LoRA adaptation parameters (from D_in×D_int + D_int×D_out to L×D_int + D_int×D_out), where L is the number of groups per column. Concretely, each column of W is partitioned into L groups of D_in/L elements, each quantized independently with its own (α_{l,j}, β_{l,j}) pair; correspondingly, a parameter-free AvgPool1d operation reduces the input vector x from dimension D_in to L, so that matrix A shrinks to L×D_int and all rows within a group share the same adaptation contribution. This structural constraint guarantees that the LoRA offset c_{i,j} is constant within each quantization group, meaning s·AB can be folded into the zero factors via β_new = β − s·(B·A)ᵀ/α while preserving the exact low-bit integer representation. The result is a fine-tuned model that remains fully quantized (e.g., INT4) at inference, achieving >50% speedup over QLoRA's FP16 inference and outperforming QLoRA+GPTQ by up to 15% at 2-bit, all with roughly half the trainable parameters of QLoRA.

## Results

On MMLU 5-shot accuracy with LLaMA-7B fine-tuned on Alpaca, QA-LoRA at INT4 achieves 39.4% average, surpassing QLoRA w/GPTQ (36.0%) by 3.4 points and matching the original QLoRA (38.4%) while remaining fully quantized. The advantage grows substantially at lower bit widths: at INT2, QA-LoRA reaches 27.5% vs. 25.8% for QLoRA w/GPTQ, and on FLAN v2 the gap widens to 33.2% vs. 25.0%. On commonsense QA (LLaMA-7B, 0-shot), QA-LoRA at INT4 scores 61.8% average (comparable to QLoRA's 62.0%), while at INT2 it achieves 53.7% vs. only 38.7% for QLoRA w/GPTQ — a 15-point gain. In terms of efficiency, QA-LoRA uses roughly half the learnable parameters of QLoRA (89M vs. 160M for 7B) and trains ~2× faster (21.5h vs. 40.0h on one V100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

QA-LoRA consistently outperforms QLoRA w/GPTQ (the post-training quantized variant) across all model sizes, bit widths, and datasets. On LLaMA-7B Alpaca 5-shot MMLU, QA-LoRA beats QLoRA w/GPTQ by 3.4 points at INT4 (39.4% vs. 36.0%), 3.4 points at INT3 (37.4% vs. 34.0%), and 1.7 points at INT2 (27.5% vs. 25.8%); on FLAN v2 the INT2 gap widens to 8.2 points (33.2% vs. 25.0%). Against the original QLoRA (4+16, i.e., FP16 inference), QA-LoRA at INT4 matches or slightly exceeds its 5-shot accuracy (e.g., 39.4% vs. 38.4% on LLaMA-7B Alpaca; 49.2% vs. 48.4% on LLaMA-13B) while keeping the deployed model fully in INT4, yielding >50% faster inference. Against PEQA (a sub-4-bit quantization method without LoRA), QA-LoRA at INT4 on LLaMA-7B Alpaca achieves 39.4% 5-shot MMLU vs. PEQA's 34.8%, a 4.6-point margin. The qualitative examples in Appendix A further show that QLoRA w/GPTQ produces incoherent or garbled outputs at INT2 (e.g., random numbers), whereas QA-LoRA maintains coherent, task-relevant responses even at INT2.

## Relation to Prior Work

QA-LoRA extends the LoRA framework (Hu et al., 2021) and QLoRA (Dettmers et al., 2023a) by introducing group-wise operators that simultaneously increase the number of quantization parameters (scaling/zero factors per group) and decrease the number of adaptation parameters (shared within groups). The key departure from QLoRA is replacing NF4 quantization with INT4 and restructuring the low-rank matrices so that the adapter weights can be merged directly into the quantized base weights without converting back to FP16. The paper frames this as resolving a fundamental parameter imbalance: in standard column-wise quantization, each column has only one pair of scaling/zero factors but many LoRA parameters, making post-hoc merging intractable without high-precision arithmetic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QA-LoRA provides a practical, drop-in solution for the full pipeline of fine-tuning and deploying LLMs in low-bit formats, which is critical for edge deployment where both memory and inference speed are constrained. The 2-bit results (6+ point gains over QLoRA w/GPTQ on LLaMA-13B/33B) suggest the group-wise approach is particularly valuable when quantization loss is severe. Limitations include evaluation restricted to LLaMA/LLaMA2 families and language-understanding benchmarks (no generation quality or long-context evaluation), and the method assumes INT4 hardware operators are available. Open questions include whether the group-size hyperparameter L transfers across architectures and whether the approach extends to activation quantization or non-Transformer models.
