## Problem

Koopman autoencoders learn linear latent dynamics for nonlinear systems, but unrolling these dynamics over long horizons produces trajectories that cross (violating solution uniqueness) and cannot capture switching between multiple fixed points. Prior approaches required long training sequences or eigenvalue constraints to stabilize unrolls, which the authors argue are unnecessary for fully observable systems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240043/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes an initial state vector and a sequence of control actions (for controlled systems) and outputs a predicted sequence of future states over arbitrarily long horizons (up to 1000 steps for low-dimensional systems, 300 steps for D4RL robotics). The encoder maps d-dimensional states to n-dimensional latent codes (n >> d, e.g., 128 or 512), and the decoder maps back, so the latent space is over-complete and the encoder/decoder pair is not bijective. For the control task, a separate action decoder head outputs the action sequence alongside the state trajectory.

## Method

The core contribution is Periodic Reencoding, an inference-time mechanism that decodes the latent trajectory back to state space and re-encodes it every k steps, acting as a 'course correction' that mitigates drift accumulated by pure latent-space unrolling. The training objective combines alignment (latent codes match encoder outputs), reconstruction (decoder recovers states), and prediction (decoded unrolled states match ground truth) losses, with the Koopman matrix K and control matrix L parameterized continuously and discretized via the bilinear (Tustin) method. Because the encoder and decoder are not exact inverses, each reencoding step nudges the trajectory back toward the true manifold, while the linear unrolls between reencoding steps remain fully parallelizable. The authors prove analytically that reencoding produces a well-defined state-space dynamical system (Eq. 9) that satisfies the uniqueness theorem, and show via a block-diagonal example that it enables switching between local linear approximations around different fixed points.

## Results

On low-dimensional dynamical systems, periodic reencoding dramatically reduces long-horizon prediction error: for the Pendulum over 1000 steps, MSE drops from 9.20 (no reencoding) to 0.18 (periodic reencoding) with a linear Koopman decoder, versus 55.28 for the MLP baseline. On D4RL locomotion tasks over 300 steps, the Koopman autoencoder with a nonlinear decoder and periodic reencoding achieves MSE of 0.012 on Hopper-v2 (expert), 0.227 on HalfCheetah-v2 (expert), and 0.072 on Walker2d-v2 (expert), substantially outperforming the MLP baseline (0.561, 1.359, 0.285 respectively). In the semi-open-loop control setting, periodic reencoding yields total rewards of 53.5 (Hopper), 64.2 (HalfCheetah), and 61.9 (Walker2d) over 100-step open-loop horizons, compared to 18.4, 15.1, and 18.5 without reencoding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is a multi-step MLP of equivalent capacity to the Koopman encoder+decoder, trained autoregressively. The Koopman autoencoder with periodic reencoding consistently outperforms the MLP by large margins: on the Pendulum (1000 steps), MSE is 0.18 vs. 55.28 (a ~300× improvement); on Lotka-Volterra (1000 steps), 0.40 vs. 83.21 (~200×); on D4RL Hopper-v2 expert (300 steps), 0.012 vs. 0.561 (~47×). The MLP baseline becomes unstable for horizons beyond ~10 steps in the D4RL setting. Among Koopman variants, the linear decoder with linear latent dynamics and periodic reencoding achieves the best results on low-dimensional dynamical systems, while a nonlinear decoder is essential for the higher-dimensional D4RL tasks. The only exception where periodic reencoding does not help is the Parabolic Attractor, which admits a globally linear Koopman embedding by construction, making course correction unnecessary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the deep Koopman autoencoder framework established by Lusch et al. (2018) and Azencot et al. (2020), which learn nonlinear encoder/decoder pairs alongside a linear Koopman matrix to represent NLDS dynamics. Prior works achieved stable long-horizon unrolls by constraining eigenvalues (Azencot et al., 2020; Frion et al., 2023) or training on long sequences, but did not identify the specific failure modes of latent-space trajectory generation. This paper departs from that line by diagnosing two concrete limitations—trajectory crossing (violating uniqueness) and inability to capture switching between fixed points (Lan & Mezić, 2013)—and proposing Periodic Reencoding as a purely inference-time fix that requires no architectural or training changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Periodic Reencoding offers a drop-in inference mechanism that makes linear Koopman autoencoders competitive with or superior to nonlinear dynamics models for long-horizon prediction, without the computational cost of step-by-step reencoding or the training overhead of long sequences. The finding that linear decoders consistently outperform nonlinear ones across all tested environments is a useful practical guideline for the Koopman modeling community. Open questions include whether the approach extends to partially observable systems, how the optimal reencoding period scales with system complexity, and whether the method integrates naturally with model-predictive control loops that require very fast look-ahead.
