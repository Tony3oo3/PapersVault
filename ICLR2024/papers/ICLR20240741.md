## Problem

Existing diffusion models for structure-based drug design incorporate protein-ligand interaction information only in the reverse (denoising) process, while the forward (noising) process applies identical perturbations to all molecules regardless of the target protein. This inconsistency between forward and reverse processes introduces a bias that hinders the model from fully capturing pocket-ligand interactions, ultimately limiting the binding affinity of generated molecules.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240741/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D protein binding site (pocket) specified by atom positions and amino acid types as input, and generates a 3D ligand molecule as output consisting of continuous atom coordinates and categorical atom types (from {C, N, O, F, P, S, Cl}). The number of ligand atoms is sampled from an empirical distribution, and chemical bonds are assigned via post-processing. The protein structure is held fixed throughout both the forward and reverse diffusion processes, with only the ligand atoms being perturbed and denoised.

## Method

IPDIFF introduces a pretrained SE(3)-equivariant interaction prior network (IPNET), supervised by binding affinity signals on PDBbind v2016, which extracts atom-level interactive representations for both protein and ligand. Two mechanisms then integrate these priors into the diffusion framework: (1) prior-shifting, which applies a learnable, interaction-dependent mean shift to the ligand atom positions at every timestep of the forward process, making the noise injection protein-specific and adapting diffusion trajectories to the pocket-ligand geometry; and (2) prior-conditioning, which concatenates IPNET-derived interaction features from the previously estimated protein-ligand complex into the initial hidden states of the SE(3)-equivariant denoising network at each reverse step. The shift coefficient k_t = √(ᾱ_t)·(1−√(ᾱ_t)) ensures trajectory endpoints remain unchanged, and a theoretical analysis shows IPDIFF achieves a tighter negative log-likelihood bound than standard diffusion models that omit interaction information from the forward process.

## Results

On CrossDocked2020, IPDIFF achieves an Avg. Vina Score of -6.42, Vina Min of -7.45, and Vina Dock of -8.57, with 69.5% of generated molecules showing better binding affinity than the reference ligand. It also maintains competitive molecular properties: QED 0.52, SA 0.61, and Diversity 0.74. The method outperforms the strongest diffusion baseline DecompDiff by 13.2% in Avg. Vina Score, 15.6% in QED, and 8.8% in Diversity, while also surpassing the best autoregressive method Pocket2Mol by 24.9% in Avg. Vina Score.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IPDIFF outperforms all five baselines on every binding-affinity metric (Vina Score, Vina Min, Vina Dock, High Affinity). Against the strongest diffusion baseline DecompDiff, IPDIFF improves Avg. Vina Score from -5.67 to -6.42 (13.2%), Vina Min from -7.04 to -7.45 (5.8%), and High Affinity from 64.4% to 69.5%, while also boosting QED from 0.45 to 0.52 (15.6%) and Diversity from 0.68 to 0.74 (8.8%). Compared to the best autoregressive method Pocket2Mol, IPDIFF gains 24.9% in Avg. Vina Score and 21.1 percentage points in High Affinity, though it trails Pocket2Mol on QED (0.52 vs. 0.56) and SA (0.61 vs. 0.74). Against TargetDiff, IPDIFF leads on all seven reported metrics. The paper notes that the QED/SA gap versus Pocket2Mol is acceptable since these are rough screening metrics, and IPDIFF achieves a better overall trade-off between binding affinity and molecular properties than any prior method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based SBDD framework established by TargetDiff (Guan et al., 2023a) and the foundational DDPM formulation (Ho et al., 2020) by identifying and addressing a conceptual asymmetry: prior methods inject protein-ligand interaction information only in the reverse (denoising) process while the forward process perturbs all molecules identically regardless of the target protein. IPDIFF resolves this by pretraining an SE(3)-equivariant interaction prior network (IPNET) on binding affinity signals and then using it to (1) shift forward diffusion trajectories via a learnable adapter (prior-shifting) and (2) condition the reverse denoising on estimated protein-ligand interactions (prior-conditioning). The approach also builds on EGNN-style equivariant message passing and cross-attention for modeling intermolecular interactions, but departs from DecompDiff (Guan et al., 2023b) by requiring no external tools like AlphaSpace2 for subpocket extraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By aligning the forward and reverse processes with respect to protein-ligand interaction information, IPDIFF provides a principled correction to a previously overlooked inconsistency in diffusion-based SBDD, supported by a theoretical argument for tighter NLL bounds. The SOTA result (-6.42 Avg. Vina Score, 69.5% High Affinity) on CrossDocked2020 is practically meaningful for structure-based drug design, and the finding that even a sequence-only IPNET variant retains most of the benefit suggests the interaction prior is robust to architectural choices. Open questions include whether the pretrained IPNET can be replaced by end-to-end learning without the binding-affinity supervision signal, whether prior-shifting can be extended to atom types (not just positions), and whether the framework generalizes to other generative paradigms beyond DDPM.
