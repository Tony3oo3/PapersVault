## Problem

Higher-order numerical solvers for diffusion sampling (e.g., DPM-Solver, PLMS) produce divergence artifacts—unrealistic oversaturated regions—when the number of sampling steps is reduced, limiting achievable acceleration. The paper identifies the root cause as the small stability regions of these methods: when δλ falls outside the stability region, the numerical solution diverges. Existing work lacks a training-free mechanism to expand stability regions while preserving high-order convergence.

## Input / Output

The method is a drop-in, training-free replacement for any existing diffusion ODE solver. It takes a pre-trained diffusion model (pixel-based ADM or latent-based Stable Diffusion/DiT/MDT), a Gaussian noise sample, and optionally a text prompt or class label, plus a single scalar damping coefficient β ∈ (0,1] that controls the trade-off between accuracy and artifact suppression. The output is a generated image (or latent code before VAE decoding); β effectively interpolates between two adjacent-order Adams-Bashforth methods, so e.g. GHVB 1.8 sits between 1st-order DDIM and 2nd-order PLMS2.

## Method

The paper proposes two momentum-based techniques. First, Heavy Ball (HB) momentum is applied to any existing diffusion solver by replacing the direct step update with an exponential moving average of the velocity: v_{n+1} = (1−β)v_n + β·f(x_n), x_{n+1} = x_n + δv_{n+1}. This enlarges the stability region but provably drops convergence to 1st order. Second, Generalized Heavy Ball (GHVB) restores high-order convergence by computing the moving average *before* the multi-step summation rather than after: the AB formula is multiplied by (β+(1−β)Δ) and the number of retained terms fixes the order, yielding methods like GHVB 3.8 (4th-order with β=0.8). The key distinction from HB is the ordering of the EMA relative to the high-order coefficient summation, which preserves the original method's convergence order while still expanding the stability region. Both techniques are training-free, add negligible computation, and are validated on pixel-based (ADM) and latent-based (DiT-XL, MDT, Stable Diffusion) models.

## Results

On DiT-XL (ImageNet256, classifier-free guidance), GHVB 3.8 achieves the best FID of 10.99 at 6 steps, outperforming PLMS4 (13.10), DPM-Solver++ (18.60), and DDIM (55.35). On ADM (pixel-based, classifier guidance), LTSP[PLMS4, GHVB 0.8] achieves FID 37.43 at 10 steps versus 66.77 for DPM-Solver++ and 45.32 for LTSP[PLMS4, PLMS1]. On MDT, GHVB 3.8 reaches FID 9.67 at 6 steps compared to 11.10 for PLMS4 and 56.53 for DDIM. At higher step counts (25 steps), all methods converge to similar FID scores (~5.17–5.24 on DiT, ~1.96–2.03 on MDT), confirming that the gains are most pronounced in the low-step regime where divergence artifacts are most severe.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GHVB 3.8 outperforms PLMS4 (the strongest baseline) at low step counts on both DiT-XL (FID 10.99 vs. 13.10 at 6 steps) and MDT (9.67 vs. 11.10 at 6 steps), with the gap narrowing to near-parity at 25 steps. Against DPM-Solver++, the improvement is dramatic at very low steps (10.99 vs. 18.60 on DiT at 6 steps; 47.10 vs. 66.77 on ADM at 10 steps with HB 0.8). Compared to LTSP (the splitting method), GHVB 3.8 achieves lower FID on DiT at 6 steps (10.99 vs. 13.33), while on ADM, LTSP[PLMS4, GHVB 0.8] (37.43) beats LTSP[PLMS4, PLMS1] (45.32) at 10 steps, showing that adding momentum to the second subproblem helps. Notably, HB 0.8 applied to PLMS4 actually worsens FID at 6 steps on DiT (14.35 vs. 13.10) due to its first-order convergence, whereas GHVB 3.8 maintains fourth-order accuracy while still expanding the stability region. All methods converge to comparable FID at 25 steps, confirming the advantage is specific to the low-step, artifact-prone regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Polyak's Heavy Ball momentum from optimization to expand the stability regions of higher-order ODE solvers (Adams-Bashforth/PLMS, DPM-Solver++) used in diffusion sampling. It builds directly on the line of work accelerating diffusion via numerical ODE methods (DEIS, DPM-Solver, PLMS) and the stability-region analysis framework from numerical ODE theory, but departs by identifying the small stability regions of these solvers as the root cause of divergence artifacts and proposing a momentum-based fix. The GHVB construction generalizes the 1st-order HB modification to preserve higher-order convergence, analogous to how Adams-Bashforth generalizes Euler, while the splitting-method literature (LTSP, STSP) is positioned as a more computationally expensive alternative to the same problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear theoretical diagnosis (stability region size → divergence artifacts) that was previously only empirically observed, giving practitioners a principled way to select solver order and step count. The training-free, drop-in nature of HB/GHVB makes them immediately applicable to any existing diffusion pipeline, and the continuous β parameter offers a tunable accuracy-vs-stability trade-off. Limitations include the assumption that the diffusion ODE's Jacobian eigenvalues are well-approximated by a scalar test equation, and the methods are validated primarily on ImageNet-scale and text-to-image models; whether the stability analysis transfers to SDE formulations or video diffusion remains open.
