## Problem

Mixing multiple object detection datasets for training introduces annotation mismatches—differences in class semantics, annotation instructions, human-machine alignment, and cross-modality labeling—that degrade downstream detector performance. Existing remedies either manually correct labels, apply statistical box rescaling that ignores image content, or generate pseudo-labels that discard the source annotations entirely. The paper formalizes the label transfer problem: given a source dataset with mismatched annotations, learn to rewrite its bounding boxes and validity to match a target dataset's annotation protocol in a data-centric, detector-agnostic way.

## Input / Output

At inference, the label transfer model receives a source image together with its existing bounding boxes and class labels (the source annotations) and outputs target-style bounding boxes plus per-instance validity scores. A class-conditional threshold σ_c is then applied to the validity scores to decide which transferred boxes are retained, producing the final label-transferred source dataset. Notably, the box generator used during training is discarded at inference; the model operates directly on the provided source labels rather than generating new proposals from scratch.

## Method

LGPL repurposes a two-stage detector (Cascade-RCNN with HRNet-w32 backbone) into two specialized components: a box generator (the RPN) trained on the source dataset to synthesize source-style proposals on target images, and a label transfer model (a modified RoI head) trained on the target dataset to map those source-style proposals to target-style boxes and assign validity scores. The key training trick is that the label transfer model is supervised with triplets (x, [StopGrad(f_gen(x)), random class labels], g_tgt(x)), where random class labels force the model to learn validity determination independent of class identity. A class-conditional assigner matches proposals to ground-truth boxes by class and IoU, handling crowded scenes. At inference, the source labels directly replace the box generator, and a stop-gradient operator during training prevents target-dataset gradients from leaking into the box generator. When image domains differ substantially (e.g., synthetic-to-real), an additional α-weighted S-CycConf loss aligns instance features across domains.

## Results

Across four label transfer scenarios (nuScenes→nuImages, Synscapes→Cityscapes, Internal-Dataset→nuImages, MVD+nuImages→Waymo) and three detector architectures (YOLOv3, Deformable DETR, Faster-RCNN), LGPL consistently outperforms all baselines, improving downstream-mAP by an average of 1.88 and downstream-AP^75 by 2.65 over the 'No transfer' baseline. LGPL is the only method that beats 'No transfer' in every single scenario-architecture combination; all other baselines (SN, PL, PL&NF) occasionally degrade performance below the unmodified source labels. On the multi-source MVD+nuImages→Waymo scenario, LGPL achieves 25.09 mAP (YOLOv3), 27.86 (Def-DETR), and 32.74 (Faster-RCNN), surpassing the best baseline (PL&NF) by 1.31, 0.17, and 2.05 mAP respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LGPL outperforms all five baselines in every scenario-architecture combination. Against Statistical Normalization (SN, Wang et al. 2020), LGPL wins by 0.85–4.25 mAP; SN actually degrades performance below 'No transfer' in 7 of 12 settings (e.g., 25.53 vs 26.87 on Synscapes→Cityscapes with YOLOv3). Against Pseudo-labeling (PL, Lee 2013), LGPL wins by 0.43–6.13 mAP; PL is worse than 'No transfer' in 8 of 12 settings, confirming that discarding source labels is harmful. Against PL&NF (Mao et al. 2020), LGPL wins by 0.17–4.25 mAP; PL&NF is the strongest baseline but still fails to beat 'No transfer' in 3 of 12 settings. Against SAM-transfer (Kirillov et al. 2023), LGPL achieves 42.60 vs 38.29 mAP on nuScenes→nuImages with Faster-RCNN, a 4.31 mAP gap, as SAM struggles with oversized source boxes. Against supervised domain adaptation (S-DANN and S-CycConf from Prabhu et al. 2023), LGPL outperforms both by 1.6 and 1.24 mAP on average across two scenarios, demonstrating that data-centric label transfer is more effective than model-centric feature alignment for annotation mismatches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the two-stage object detection architecture (RPN + RoI head from Faster-RCNN/Cascade-RCNN) for a new data-centric label transfer task, extending the pseudo-labeling paradigm of Lee (2013) by conditioning on existing source bounding boxes and class labels rather than discarding them. It deliberately positions itself against model-centric domain adaptation methods (S-DANN, S-CycConf from CARE) that align instance features, arguing that annotation mismatches are a distinct problem from image distribution shifts. The work also differentiates from multi-dataset detection approaches (Zhou et al., 2021; Chen et al., 2023) that optimize average performance across all datasets, whereas LGPL optimizes target-specific performance by treating label transfer as a pre-processing step agnostic to downstream detector architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a data-centric label transfer approach outperforms supervised domain adaptation for closing annotation mismatches challenges the default assumption that model-centric feature alignment is the right tool for cross-dataset training. This has practical implications for teams combining heterogeneous datasets (e.g., autonomous driving stacks mixing Waymo, nuScenes, Cityscapes), where manual annotation reconciliation is costly. Key limitations include the assumption that source labels over-detect (never under-detect) target objects and that class label spaces are already aligned, leaving joint correction of class semantics and bounding boxes as an open problem. The paper also reveals that transfer-mAP (direct comparison to gold labels) poorly correlates with downstream detector performance, suggesting the field needs better evaluation protocols for label transfer quality.
