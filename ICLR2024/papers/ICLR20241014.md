## Problem

Existing theoretical analyses of Federated Averaging (FedAvg) either rely on lazy-training linearization in weight space for convergence guarantees or invoke classical generalization bounds (VC dimension, PAC Bayes, convexity) that require strong assumptions like Lipschitz continuity. No unified framework exists that simultaneously explains both the convergence and the generalization behavior of FedAvg with neural networks, nor one that rigorously quantifies how inter-client communication affects the signal-to-noise tradeoff during feature learning.

## Input / Output

Each client's input is a two-patch vector x = [y·μ_k, ξ_{k,i}] where the signal patch y·μ_k is correlated with the binary label and the noise patch ξ_{k,i} is Gaussian background orthogonal to all signal directions. The two-layer ReLU CNN processes both patches jointly via shared first-layer weights, producing a scalar logit for binary classification. The key analytical output is the effective signal-to-noise ratio χ_k·SNR_k, which determines whether FedAvg achieves o(1) test error or a Θ(1) constant error, and the weighted aggregation coefficients s_{k,k'} derived from prototype cosine similarity for the proposed Weighted FedAvg.

## Method

The paper decomposes every weight vector into a linear combination of the random initialization, signal vectors μ_k, and noise vectors ξ_{k,i}, then tracks the scalar coefficients (γ for signal, ψ/φ for noise) through both local gradient descent and server-side weight averaging. A two-stage dynamics analysis shows that in Stage 1 (constant loss derivative), signal learning grows as Θ(n̄·SNR_k²) while noise memorization is suppressed by a factor of K after each averaging round, because identical signal vectors across clients reinforce each other while independent noise vectors cancel. The effective SNR χ_k = Σ_{k'}⟨μ_k, μ_k'⟩/||μ_k||² quantifies cross-client feature similarity: in the IID case χ_k = K (maximum boost), while in the worst non-IID case χ_k = 1 (no benefit over local training). The paper then proposes a Weighted FedAvg variant that replaces uniform data-size-based aggregation weights with prototype-similarity-based weights s_{k,k'} = exp(sim(p_k, p_{k'})/τ) / Σ_i exp(sim(p_k, p_i)/τ), where p_k is the concatenation of per-class feature prototypes, thereby amplifying contributions from clients with similar signal structure.

## Results

On the Digits feature-skew benchmark (5 datasets), Weighted FedAvg + Personalized BN achieves 86.93% average accuracy, outperforming FedBN (85.20%), FedAvg (82.70%), and SingleSet (82.00%). On CIFAR-10 with pathological label skew, Weighted FedAvg + Personalized BN reaches 67.30% accuracy versus FedBN's 65.71% and FedAvg's 40.16%. On CIFAR-100 with pathological skew, the proposed method achieves 43.51% (vs. FedBN 42.54%, FedPer 29.10%). The synthetic experiments confirm the theoretical prediction that FedAvg achieves near-zero test error while local training (SingleSet) plateaus at a large constant error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Weighted FedAvg + Personalized BN consistently outperforms FedAvg (McMahan et al., 2017) across all evaluated settings, with the largest gap on CIFAR-10 pathological skew (67.30% vs. 40.16%) and SVHN (75.36% vs. 62.86%). Against FedPer (Arivazhagan et al., 2019), the proposed method wins on 4 of 5 Digits datasets and both CIFAR-10 settings, though FedPer edges ahead on MNIST (96.21% vs. 96.11%). Compared to FedBN (Li et al., 2021), the proposed method achieves higher average accuracy on Digits (86.93% vs. 85.20%) and CIFAR-10 pathological (67.30% vs. 65.71%), with the most notable gain on SVHN (+4.32 pp) and MNIST-M (+3.69 pp), while FedBN retains a slight edge on USPS and CIFAR-100 pathological. Against local training (SingleSet), the proposed method wins on all Digits datasets and CIFAR-10, confirming the theoretical prediction that communication improves generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the signal-noise decomposition and two-stage dynamics analysis from centralized feature learning theory (Cao et al., 2022; Allen-Zhu & Li, 2023) into the federated setting, where prior convergence analyses of FedAvg (Song et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2021; Deng et al., 2022) relied on NTK/lazy-training linearization in weight space. The key conceptual departure is tracking how the weight-averaging step differentially amplifies signal (which accumulates across clients sharing similar features) versus noise (which is independent across clients), yielding an 'effective SNR' that naturally encodes data heterogeneity. The proposed Weighted FedAvg, which reweights aggregation by prototype similarity, is a direct algorithmic consequence of this effective-SNR analysis rather than an ad hoc heuristic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first unified theoretical lens under which both convergence and generalization of FedAvg are explained by the same mechanism (signal-vs-noise learning speed), avoiding the separate strong assumptions (convexity, Lipschitz, NTK) that prior FL generalization bounds required. The clean separation result—FedAvg achieves o(1) test error while local training is stuck at Θ(1) under identical conditions—gives a principled, quantitative justification for why communication helps generalization, not just optimization. Limitations are significant: the analysis is restricted to two-layer ReLU CNNs, binary classification, a two-patch data model, and full-batch gradient descent, so extending to deeper architectures, SGD, multi-class settings, and realistic data distributions remains open. The empirical gains of Weighted FedAvg over standard FedAvg are modest (a few percentage points), suggesting the theoretical insight may be most valuable as design guidance rather than as a drop-in accuracy boost.
