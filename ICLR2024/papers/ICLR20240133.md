## Problem

Medical biosignal datasets are typically small, collected in clinical or controlled experimental settings, and lack annotated labels, making it difficult to train generalizable deep neural networks for wearable biosignals. No prior work has pre-trained foundation models on large-scale PPG and ECG data collected from consumer wearable devices in free-living conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240133/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes raw PPG segments (60 s, 64 Hz, 4 optical channels) or ECG segments (30 s, 128 Hz, single Lead I channel) recorded on Apple Watch and produces a 256-dimensional embedding per segment. During evaluation, embeddings are mean-aggregated per participant so each person contributes one sample to linear probing, and the resulting participant-level embedding is used to predict demographics (age, BMI, sex) and ~45 binary health/medication targets via ridge regression.

## Method

The framework is a joint-embedding contrastive architecture with three key design choices: (1) participant-level positive pair selection, where two different segments from the same person form a positive pair, encouraging the encoder to capture participant-specific physiology rather than segment-level artifacts; (2) a stochastic augmentation cascade with modality-specific probabilities (stronger distortions for ECG, which has less within-participant variability); and (3) a regularized InfoNCE loss augmented with KoLeo differential entropy to encourage uniform feature spread, combined with BYOL-style momentum training to increase positive-pair mismatch. The encoder is a 1D-EfficientNet (3.3 M params for PPG, 2.5 M for ECG) with a 256-d embedding and a 1024-unit MLP projection head, trained on ~141 K PPG and ~106 K ECG participants from the Apple Heart and Movement Study using 32 A100 GPUs.

## Results

On the AHMS dataset (~141K PPG participants, ~107K ECG participants), the proposed self-supervised framework with participant-level positive pairs achieves 0.976 AUC for age classification, 0.993 AUC for sex classification, and 0.918 AUC for BMI classification using PPG embeddings (vs. 0.916, 0.951, and 0.797 for ECG). The smooth effective rank of the learned embeddings is 104.13 (PPG) and 113.82 (ECG), substantially exceeding SimCLR (98.87/103.65) and BYOL (51.18/63.67). PPG embeddings consistently outperform a baseline feature set of demographics and heart rate across 45+ health condition and medication targets from AHMS surveys.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its regularized InfoNCE + momentum training framework against two adapted baselines: SimCLR (contrastive, no KoLeo, no momentum) and BYOL (non-contrastive, momentum-based). Against SimCLR, the proposed method wins on smooth effective rank by a meaningful margin (104.13 vs. 98.87 for PPG; 113.82 vs. 103.65 for ECG) and on downstream AUC by small but consistent margins (e.g., BMI classification 0.918 vs. 0.913 for PPG; sex classification 0.951 vs. 0.947 for ECG). Against BYOL, the advantage is much larger: smooth effective rank more than doubles (104.13 vs. 51.18 for PPG; 113.82 vs. 63.67 for ECG), and downstream AUC gaps reach 2.5 points (BMI: 0.918 vs. 0.893 PPG) and 1.2 points (sex: 0.951 vs. 0.939 ECG). The authors attribute the SimCLR gap primarily to the KoLeo regularization term, while the BYOL gap reflects the benefit of the explicit contrastive component in the loss. Notably, the contrastive frameworks (Ours and SimCLR) are described as 'more robust' than the non-contrastive BYOL variant across all evaluations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the joint embedding architecture (JEA) paradigm for self-supervised biosignal learning—previously applied to small clinical datasets of 12-lead ECG, EEG, or PPG from controlled settings—by scaling it to ~141K participants of passive wearable PPG and ~107K of user-initiated ECG from Apple Watch. It combines the contrastive InfoNCE loss (Oord et al., 2019; Chen et al., 2020), momentum training (Grill et al., 2020), and KoLeo entropy regularization (Sablayrolles et al., 2019) into a unified framework, with the key methodological departure being participant-level positive pair selection (two different segments from the same person) rather than the conventional segment-level pairing. The encoder is a compact 1D-EfficientNet, and the paper positions itself as the first foundation-model pre-training effort on large-scale consumer wearable biosignals, as opposed to prior SSL work that relied on hospital or lab-collected data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that passively recorded PPG embeddings from a consumer wearable encode information predictive of dozens of self-reported health conditions and medications beyond what demographics and heart rate alone can explain, suggesting a practical path toward on-device health monitoring without expensive labeled clinical studies. Key open questions include whether modality-specific pre-training (given the clear PPG-vs-ECG differences in within-participant variability) could close the ECG performance gap, how embeddings evolve longitudinally, and whether multi-modal (PPG+ECG) pre-training yields complementary representations. Limitations include reliance on self-reported survey labels (sub-optimal ground truth), a single device platform, and the absence of external validation on independent cohorts.
