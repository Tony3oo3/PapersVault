## Problem

Existing doubly robust estimators in the proximal causal learning framework are restricted to binary treatments, where the indicator function in the Proximal Inverse Probability Weighting (PIPW) estimator becomes a Dirac delta for continuous treatments, rendering empirical estimation infeasible. Additionally, deriving the influence function for continuous treatments involves Gateaux derivatives of implicitly defined bridge functions, and estimating the treatment bridge function q₀ requires re-running optimization for each treatment value, making it computationally prohibitive.

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular observational data with a continuous treatment A, outcome Y, observed covariates X, a treatment-inducing proxy Z, and an outcome-inducing proxy W, under the assumption that unobserved confounders U exist but are not directly measured. It produces the average causal effect β(a) = E[Y(a)] as a continuous dose-response curve, evaluated at 100 equally spaced points across the support of A, along with the nuisance bridge functions h₀(a,w,x) and q₀(a,z,x). A key intermediate output is the estimated policy function p(A|W,X), obtained via kernel density estimation (low-dimensional) or conditional normalizing flows (high-dimensional), which is required before solving the min-max optimization for q₀.

## Method

The paper replaces the infeasible indicator function in the PIPW estimator with a second-order kernel function K_{h_bw}(A−a), yielding the Proximal Kernel Inverse Probability Weighted (PKIPW) and Proximal Kernel Doubly Robust (PKDR) estimators, and proves via Gateaux derivative analysis that the PKDR estimator coincides with the influence function of β(a) under a nonparametric model. For nuisance function estimation, the paper introduces a two-stage procedure: first estimating the policy function p(A|W,X), then reformulating the conditional moment equation for q₀ as a min-max optimization over RKHS-parameterized function classes, solving all treatment values simultaneously in a single optimization rather than one per treatment. The outcome bridge function h₀ is estimated via a separate min-max problem following Kallus et al. (2021). Theoretical analysis establishes that the PKDR estimator achieves O(n^{-4/5}) mean squared error convergence with optimal bandwidth h_bw = O(n^{-1/5}), and the q₀ estimation error is bounded by the Rademacher complexity of the critic class plus the policy estimation error ‖1/p − 1/p̂‖₂.

## Results

The paper reports causal Mean Squared Error (cMSE) across 100 equally spaced treatment points. On the low-dimensional doubly robust study (n=1000), PKDR achieves the best cMSE of 0.08±0.04 in Scenario 1 (no misspecification) and dramatically outperforms all baselines in Scenario 2 (outcome bridge misspecified) with cMSE 0.24±0.19 vs. 0.97–5.71 for baselines. On the high-dimensional study (n=2000, dim(X)=100), PKDR achieves cMSE 0.09±0.04, competitive with the best baselines NMMR (0.05) and MINMAX (0.07). On the real-world Legalized Abortion and Crime dataset (n=1500), PKDR achieves cMSE 0.02±0.00, matching PMMR (0.02) and close to NMMR (0.01).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PKDR (the proposed kernel-based doubly robust estimator) achieves the lowest cMSE in Scenario 1 (0.08 vs. 0.10 for MINMAX, the best baseline) and dramatically outperforms all h₀-only baselines in Scenario 2 where the outcome bridge is misspecified (cMSE 0.24 vs. 0.97 for DFPV and 3.32–5.71 for others), demonstrating the doubly robust advantage. In Scenario 3 (treatment bridge misspecified), PKDR (0.19) is competitive with PMMR (0.14), while PKIPW degrades severely (2.15) due to reliance on the misspecified q₀. In Scenario 4 where both bridges are misspecified, all methods degrade substantially; DFPV (1.02) is the most robust, while PKDR (3.56±3.67) suffers the highest error and variance. On the high-dimensional study (dim(X)=100), PKDR (0.09) is within 0.04 of the best baseline NMMR (0.05) and outperforms PMMR (0.69) and KPV (0.36) by large margins. On the real-world abortion-and-crime dataset, PKDR (0.02) ties with PMMR (0.02) and is close to NMMR (0.01), confirming practical utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the proximal causal learning (PCL) framework of Miao et al. (2018) and Cui et al. (2023), which established doubly robust estimation for binary treatments using treatment and outcome bridge functions. The key departure is replacing the infeasible indicator function (Dirac delta) with a kernel function to handle continuous treatments, drawing on kernel methods previously applied to continuous treatment estimation under unconfoundedness (Kallus & Zhou, 2018; Colangelo & Lee, 2020) but now embedded within the proximal framework that accommodates unmeasured confounders via proxy variables. The minimax optimization for nuisance function estimation adapts the framework of Dikkala et al. (2020) and Kallus et al. (2021), with the novelty of incorporating a separately estimated propensity score to solve for q₀ across all treatments in a single optimization rather than per-treatment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a concrete gap: the doubly robust estimator within the proximal causal framework was previously restricted to binary treatments, leaving a methodological void for the many real-world applications (drug dosing, policy analysis) where treatments are continuous and unmeasured confounders are present. The O(n^{-4/5}) MSE convergence rate is a reasonable trade-off for handling continuous treatments nonparametrically, consistent with existing kernel-based DR literature. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that the policy function estimation introduces variance, particularly under misspecification, and stabilized IPW variants are identified as future work. The broader open question is whether the completeness assumptions (Assumption 3.4) can be relaxed or verified in practice, which remains a fundamental challenge in the proximal framework.
