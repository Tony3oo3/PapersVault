## Problem

Graph rewiring techniques for GNNs face a fundamental trade-off: spatial methods (e.g., k-hop connections, Graph Transformers) preserve locality but destroy sparsity, while spectral methods (e.g., FOSR, GTR) preserve sparsity but add long-range edges that destroy the locality inductive bias of the input graph. No existing rewiring simultaneously satisfies all three desiderata of reducing over-squashing, preserving graph locality, and maintaining sparsity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a static undirected graph with node feature vectors and produces node embeddings usable for graph classification, regression, or link prediction. The rewiring is a one-time preprocessing step that generates a sequence of L graph snapshots (each adding a sparse set of edges at a specific distance level), which are then consumed by a multi-relational MPNN where each snapshot's edges carry a distinct relation type with its own learnable message function. The density parameter ρ ∈ (0,1] controls the fraction of edges added per orbit, allowing the user to trade off connectivity gains against graph density.

## Method

LASER (Locality-Aware SEquential Rewiring) introduces a sequential rewiring paradigm where edges are added across L snapshots, with snapshot ℓ only connecting nodes at shortest-path distance exactly ℓ+1 from each other. Within each distance level, only a fraction ρ of candidate edges are added, prioritized by a connectivity measure μ_k(v,u) = (Ã^k)_vu (the number of walks of length ≤ k between v and u), so that the most 'squashed' pairs receive new connections first. The resulting multi-snapshot graph is consumed by a multi-relational MPNN (equivalent to a relational GNN) where each snapshot's edge set has its own message function, allowing the model to learn distance-aware aggregation. Theoretically, LASER provably improves Jacobian sensitivity (Proposition 5.1) and, on bottleneck graphs like lollipops, preserves the distance matrix more faithfully than spectral rewiring while adding fewer edges (Proposition 5.2). The entire rewiring is computed via matrix powers of the adjacency matrix, yielding O(n³) preprocessing complexity that scales to 100k-node graphs in ~2 hours.

## Results

On the LRGB benchmarks, LASER achieves 0.6440 AP on Peptides-func (vs. 0.5930 for vanilla GCN), 0.3043 MAE on Peptides-struct (vs. 0.3496), and 0.3275 MRR on PCQM-Contact (vs. 0.3234), outperforming all rewiring baselines on all three tasks. On TUDatasets, LASER attains a mean rank of 1.37 across six classification tasks, compared to 3.60 for the next-best method (BORF) and 3.67 for FOSR. LASER also scales to graphs with 100k nodes and 1M edges in ~2 hours, while FOSR and SDRF fail to terminate within 24 hours on the same synthetic benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LASER outperforms all five baselines on every LRGB task. Against the spatial/curvature-based SDRF (Topping et al., 2022), LASER gains +4.9 AP on Peptides-func and +0.036 MAE on Peptides-struct, while SDRF barely improves over vanilla GCN. Against the spectral/effective-resistance GTR (Black et al., 2023), LASER gains +8.7 AP on Peptides-func and +0.058 MAE on Peptides-struct; GTR actually degrades performance below the no-rewiring baseline on Peptides-func. Against the spectral-gap FOSR (Karhadkar et al., 2022), LASER gains +4.9 AP on Peptides-func and +0.024 MRR on PCQM-Contact, where FOSR drops to 0.2783 MRR (well below the 0.3234 baseline). Against the curvature-based BORF (Nguyen et al., 2023), LASER gains +4.3 AP on Peptides-func and avoids the >60-hour timeout that BORF incurs on PCQM-Contact. On TUDatasets, LASER achieves the best mean rank (1.37) and wins on 5 of 6 tasks, with the largest margin on ENZYMES (34.33% vs. 30.57% for SDRF) and PROTEINS (74.38% vs. 72.71% for DiffWire).

## Relation to Prior Work

LASER extends the spectral rewiring line initiated by Karhadkar et al. (2022, FOSR) by replacing a single instantaneous rewiring with a sequential trajectory of locally-constrained rewiring snapshots, drawing an explicit equivalence to multi-relational GNNs (Battaglia et al., 2018) and generalizing L-hop MPNNs (Feng et al., 2022) with a sparsity-controlling density parameter ρ. The key conceptual departure is the formal identification of three desiderata—reduce over-squashing, preserve locality, preserve sparsity—and the demonstration that spatial methods (SDRF, BORF, Graph Transformers) and spectral methods (FOSR, GTR) each satisfy only two of the three, motivating the sequential, locality-aware design. The connectivity measure μ (walk counts) is chosen as a computationally efficient proxy for effective resistance, building on the theoretical link between walk counts and Jacobian sensitivity established by Di Giovanni et al. (2023) and Black et al. (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LASER provides a practical, scalable (O(n³) preprocessing, handles 100k-node graphs in ~2h) rewiring framework that consistently outperforms both spatial and spectral baselines across LRGB and TUDatasets, offering practitioners a drop-in improvement for MPNNs without the density overhead of multi-hop or attention-based approaches. The general sequential-rewiring paradigm with arbitrary connectivity and locality measures opens a design space for future work, including differentiable pipelines and connections to temporal GNNs. A key open question, acknowledged by the authors, is whether observed performance gains are truly attributable to over-squashing mitigation versus the added architectural expressiveness of multi-relational message functions, making causal attribution difficult. The paper also notes that TUDatasets benchmarks have quality issues (small test sets, missing node features) that limit confidence in those results.
