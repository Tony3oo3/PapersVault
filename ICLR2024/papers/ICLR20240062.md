## Problem

Despite growing interest in LLMs as autonomous agents, no systematic benchmark exists for evaluating their agent-environment interaction capabilities. Existing LLM benchmarks target static knowledge, reasoning, or conversation quality, overlooking critical agent skills such as long-horizon planning, understanding randomness, 2D/3D spatial reasoning, and learning from live interactions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240062/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each game provides a unified OpenAI Gym-style text interface: a human-written instruction manual, a rolling window of past observations and actions (history length varies from 2 to 50 steps), a current text observation (for 2D/3D games, visual observations are converted to directional text descriptions via connected-component grouping, e.g., 'grass, 3.87 blocks away to your bottom, taking 38% of screen'), and a flat categorical action list. The LLM outputs a single action per turn, and performance is quantified by three metrics—reward, completion rate, and a game-specific score—averaged over multiple trials to account for stochasticity.

## Method

SmartPlay assembles six games (Two-armed Bandits, Rock Paper Scissors, Tower of Hanoi, Messenger, Crafter, Minecraft creative navigation) into a single benchmark, each targeting a distinct subset of nine identified agent capabilities (long-text understanding, reasoning, rule-following, planning, generalization, understanding odds, learning from interactions, error handling, spatial reasoning) at graded difficulty levels. The key design insight is that games naturally encode problem-solving, probabilistic reasoning, spatial navigation, and well-defined quantifiable outcomes, while procedurally generated worlds (Messenger, Crafter, Minecraft) provide infinite variations that resist dataset contamination. Evaluation follows a simple prompting protocol: the LLM receives the manual, history, and current observation, is asked to 'think step by step,' then selects one action from the provided list. The benchmark is further structured so that each game isolates specific capability dimensions, enabling per-capability score decomposition via weighted averaging across games.

## Results

On the SmartPlay benchmark (7 game settings, scores normalized to human = 1.0), GPT-4-0314 achieves the highest overall performance: 0.98 on Rock-Paper-Scissors, 0.90 on Tower of Hanoi (3-disk), 0.97 on Messenger L2, 0.32 on Crafter, and 0.59 on Minecraft. Despite leading all other models, GPT-4 still shows large gaps versus the human baseline: ~10% on Hanoi, ~40% on Minecraft creative navigation, and ~70% on Crafter. Open-source 13B models (Llama-2-13b, Llama-13b, Vicuna-13b) score less than half of GPT-4 on simple tasks (Bandit, RPS) and roughly 1/8 on harder tasks (Crafter, Hanoi).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4 (both 0613 and 0314 variants) dominates all other proprietary and open-source models by wide margins: on Crafter, GPT-4-0314 scores 0.32 versus 0.07 (text-davinci-003), 0.05 (Claude), and 0.04 (Bard, Llama-2-13b) — roughly 3–8× higher. On Tower of Hanoi, GPT-4-0314 (0.90) outperforms Claude and Bard (0.67) by 23 points and Llama-2-13b (0.37) by 53 points. Among proprietary non-GPT-4 models, Claude leads Bard on RPS (0.47 vs 0.30) and Messenger L2 (0.60 vs 0.40), while text-davinci-003 is strongest on Bandit (1.04) but weakest on RPS (0.40). Open-source Llama-2-13b and Llama-13b perform similarly overall, with Llama-2-13b slightly better on Hanoi (0.37 vs 0.33) and planning-related tasks but worse on learning-from-interaction tasks. Notably, Vicuna-13b (fine-tuned) degrades substantially versus its base Llama-13b on reasoning and planning (Hanoi 0.07 vs 0.33, Messenger L1 0.00 vs 0.16), suggesting instruction fine-tuning can impair agent capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: game-based benchmarks for RL agents (Atari, DeepMind Lab, NetHack, MineDojo) and static LLM evaluation suites (MMLU, HELM). It repurposes existing game environments—Bandits, Rock-Paper-Scissors, Tower of Hanoi, Messenger, Crafter, and Minecraft—into a unified text-observation framework following the directional visual descriptor approach of Wu et al. (2023c), converting 2D/3D observations into language that LLMs can process without vision. The key departure from prior agent benchmarks is the explicit decomposition of agent capabilities into 9 measurable dimensions (spatial reasoning, planning, learning from interactions, etc.) with graded difficulty levels, enabling per-capability analysis rather than a single aggregate score.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SmartPlay fills a clear gap: prior LLM benchmarks test static knowledge and reasoning but ignore the interactive, long-horizon, and stochastic demands of agent behavior. The finding that even GPT-4 achieves only 30% of human performance on Crafter and 60% on Minecraft creative tasks highlights that multi-skill integration and 3D spatial reasoning remain fundamental bottlenecks for LLM agents. The Tower of Hanoi contamination result—LLMs solve the initial configuration but fail on intermediate states—suggests apparent problem-solving may rely on memorized solutions rather than genuine recursive reasoning. Open questions include whether longer context windows or retrieval-augmented approaches can close the planning and spatial-reasoning gaps, and whether the text-observation abstraction itself limits performance compared to native visual input.
