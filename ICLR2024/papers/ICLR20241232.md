## Problem

Existing self-supervised time series models rely on contrastive pretext tasks that provide only a binary similarity signal, making it impossible to learn continuous, fine-grained temporal structure in the latent space. These contrastive tasks are also incompatible with periodic or finite-state signals, where temporally distant points can be nearly identical and temporally close points can be very different. Furthermore, while time-embeddings have been used in transformer-based architectures for positional encoding, they have never been exploited within pretext tasks to explicitly shape the temporal geometry of learned representations.

## Input / Output

The model takes univariate or multivariate time series of length T with C channels and produces timestep-level vector representations of shape T×F (F=128), which are smaller than the 320-dimensional latent spaces used by competing methods. The learned time-embeddings are constrained to be probability distributions (positive components summing to 1) via a sigmoid-and-normalize operation, enabling their use in statistical divergence measures. Downstream, these representations feed into an SVM for classification, a ridge regression for forecasting, or a streaming anomaly scoring procedure, with the hierarchical loss framework allowing users to control temporal granularity from per-timestep to instance-wide.

## Method

T-Rep introduces a learned time-embedding module h_ψ (typically Time2Vec) that maps timestep indices to probability distributions encoding temporal features like trend, periodicity, and distribution shifts; these embeddings are concatenated with linearly projected inputs before a dilated 1D convolutional encoder (TCN). Two novel pretext tasks leverage these time-embeddings: (1) Time-embedding Divergence Prediction, which regresses the Jensen-Shannon divergence between two time-embeddings from the difference of their corresponding representations, forcing latent distances to correlate with temporal distances; and (2) Time-embedding-conditioned Forecasting, which predicts a nearby representation conditioned on the target's time-embedding, encouraging context-awareness and information-dense temporal encodings. These tasks are combined with TS2Vec's instance-wise and temporal contrasting losses under a hierarchical loss framework, with all four tasks weighted equally (α=0.25) and no task-specific tuning performed.

## Results

On anomaly detection, T-Rep achieves 75.7% F1 on Yahoo (point-based) and 66.6% F1 on Sepsis (segment-based), outperforming TS2Vec by 2.4% and 4.8% respectively. On the 30-dataset UEA classification benchmark, T-Rep reaches 70.6% average accuracy with only 128 latent dimensions, matching Minirocket (70.7%, 9996 dims) and beating TS2Vec (69.9%). On ETT forecasting, T-Rep achieves 0.986 MSE and 0.702 MAE, a 24.2% MSE reduction over the supervised SOTA Informer (1.300 MSE) and a 1.8% improvement over TS2Vec (1.004 MSE), while using a latent space nearly 3× smaller than baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

T-Rep consistently outperforms TS2Vec across all three evaluated tasks: anomaly detection (75.7% vs 73.3% F1 on Yahoo; 66.6% vs 61.9% on Sepsis), classification (70.6% vs 69.9% avg accuracy), and forecasting (0.986 vs 1.004 MSE). Against the supervised forecasting SOTA Informer, T-Rep achieves a 24.2% MSE reduction (0.986 vs 1.300) and a 14.4% MAE reduction (0.702 vs 0.820), despite being self-supervised and using no temporal covariates. The supervised TCN baseline (same backbone architecture) is substantially outperformed on forecasting (0.986 vs 2.012 MSE). On classification, T-Rep beats T-Loss (70.6% vs 65.7%), TNC (70.6% vs 67.1%), TS-TCC (70.6% vs 66.7%), and DTW (70.6% vs 65.0%) by 2–5 percentage points. The closest competitor is Minirocket (70.7% vs 70.6%), a purpose-built classifier using 9996 dimensions, whereas T-Rep is a general-purpose representation learner using only 128 dimensions. In missing-data robustness experiments, T-Rep degrades by only 1.3% accuracy at 75% missing data, compared to a 2% average gap for TS2Vec and catastrophic failure for T-Loss (2.6% accuracy at 90% missing).

## Relation to Prior Work

T-Rep builds directly on TS2Vec's contextual consistency framework and hierarchical loss, reusing its instance-wise and temporal contrasting pretext tasks for spatial feature learning. Its key departure is the integration of learned time-embeddings (inspired by Time2Vec, previously used only in supervised transformer settings) into both the TCN encoder and two novel pretext tasks, replacing the purely binary contrastive signal with a continuous, divergence-based notion of temporal distance. This positions T-Rep against the broader contrastive SSL-for-time-series line (T-Loss, TNC, TS-TCC, TS2Vec), which the authors argue cannot capture fine-grained temporal structure or handle periodic/finite-state signals due to sampling bias and the binary nature of positive/negative pair definitions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in medical and sensor monitoring where missing data and periodic signals are the norm, and demonstrates that time-embeddings in pretext tasks yield measurably more robust representations (only 1.3% accuracy drop at 75% missing data vs. T-Loss collapsing to 2.6% at 90%). However, the performance gains over TS2Vec are modest across all three downstream tasks (1.8–4.8%), and the optimal choice of time-embedding architecture (Time2Vec vs. MLP vs. RBF) remains poorly understood, varying by task. Open questions include how to systematically select time-embedding inductive biases, how to optimize the four-task loss weighting (currently fixed at 0.25 each), and whether the approach extends to irregularly sampled or multi-scale clinical time series.
