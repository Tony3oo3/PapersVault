## Problem

While neural networks with feature learning empirically outperform simpler models in high-dimensional settings, it remains unclear under what conditions feature learning provides a genuine statistical advantage over ridge regression. For two-layer linear neural networks in multi-output regression, no prior work identified a proper regularization on the feature mapping that fully exploits feature learning's potential or characterized when the resulting model outperforms normal ridge regression in the over-parametrized regime.

## Input / Output

The method takes n training pairs of d-dimensional input vectors x_i and m-dimensional label vectors, generated from a multivariate linear model with sub-gaussian inputs. The first-layer parameter W is a d×d matrix acting as a linear coordinate transform, and the second layer applies ridge regression in the transformed space. The output is m predictions, one per output dimension, and the criterion R(W) is used to select W by minimizing an upper bound on predictive risk that includes a degrees-of-freedom penalty on the feature mapping.

## Method

The paper proposes a criterion R(W) that upper-bounds the predictive risk of a two-layer linear neural network, where the second layer is a ridge regression estimator and the first layer is a learnable d×d linear transform W. The key regularization term is the degrees of freedom Tr(Σ̂_wx(Σ̂_wx + λI_d)^{-1}), which penalizes the effective complexity of the learned feature space and acts as an upper bound on variance. The paper proves that minimizing R(W) yields a W whose predictive risk converges to zero and achieves near-optimal Bayes risk, with the optimal W aligned to the eigenvectors of Σ_β^{1/2}Σ_xΣ_β^{1/2}. Since R(W) is non-convex, W is optimized via gradient Langevin dynamics. The paper further shows that in regimes where normal ridge regression fails to generalize (slow eigenvalue decay, misaligned signals, large tail eigenvalues, or large signal norms), the selected two-layer network still generalizes, demonstrating the concrete benefit of feature learning.

## Results

The paper's main quantitative results are theoretical: in four constructed high-dimensional examples, the two-layer linear NN selected by the proposed criterion R(W) achieves predictive risk upper bounds of U_NN ≲ log(n)/n (Examples 1–2), U_NN ≲ n^(−2/3) (Example 3), and U_NN ≲ n^(−1/3) (Example 4), while normal ridge regression has B_Norm + V_Norm ≳ 1 in all four cases. In numerical experiments with (d, n, m) = (1000, 500, 1000) and Σ_β = diag(1, 2^(−a), …, 1000^(−a)), the proposed method matches or slightly underperforms ridge regression for small a (isotropic Σ_β) but clearly outperforms it as a increases (anisotropic Σ_β), approaching the Bayes-optimal risk. The criterion R(W) − σ² tracks the actual predictive risk closely (Figure 2), validating its role as a risk estimator.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is normal ridge regression (i.e., the two-layer network with W = I_d, no feature learning). The proposed method strictly outperforms normal ridge regression in all four theoretical scenarios: when eigenvalues of Σ_x decay slowly, when x and β_{*i} are misaligned, when tail eigenvalues of Σ_x are large, and when the output norm ‖y^(i)‖ is large. In each case, ridge regression's predictive risk is bounded below by a constant (≳ 1), while the proposed method's risk vanishes at a polynomial or logarithmic rate in n. In the numerical experiments, the advantage is conditional: when Σ_β is nearly isotropic (small a), the optimal W is close to identity and the proposed method ties or slightly lags ridge regression; as a grows (more anisotropic Σ_β), the gap widens and the proposed method approaches the Bayes-optimal risk. The paper also notes that when Σ_β is exactly isotropic, the upper bound U_NN coincides with that of normal ridge regression, confirming that feature learning provides no benefit in that symmetric case.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the benign overfitting analysis of Tsigler and Bartlett (2020) from single-output ridge regression to multi-output linear regression, and builds on the two-layer linear neural network framework studied by Chatterji et al. (2022), who analyzed generalization under gradient flow. The key departure is proposing an explicit criterion R(W) with a degrees-of-freedom penalty on the feature mapping W, rather than relying on implicit regularization induced by optimization dynamics. The multi-output setting is essential: it allows the criterion to encode signal distribution information (via Σ_β) that enables Bayes risk optimality, a property the authors note was missed by prior implicit-regularization analyses. The criterion also generalizes Mallows' Cp and WAIC from the d ≪ n regime to the high-dimensional d ≥ n interpolation regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to precisely characterize when a two-layer linear neural network with feature learning outperforms normal ridge regression in high-dimensional multi-output regression, and to show that the optimal feature mapping achieves Bayes risk optimality. The practical limitation is significant: the criterion R(W) is non-convex, the setting is restricted to two-layer linear networks with identity activation, and the multi-output requirement (m outputs sharing a common feature map) is not always natural. Open questions include whether standard optimization (SGD) can minimize R(W) effectively, whether the results extend to nonlinear activations like ReLU, and whether the implicit regularization of practical training procedures actually approximates the R(W)-optimal W.
