## Problem

Existing methods for aligning language models with human feedback are limited: supervised fine-tuning (SFT) discards negatively-rated examples, wasting data and constraining the model's ability to learn from errors, while RLHF requires learning an imperfect reward function and solving challenging reinforcement learning optimizations. The paper asks whether all feedback—regardless of polarity—can be leveraged through a simple, scalable training objective that avoids RL entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241621/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the model receives a text prompt followed by one or more model-generated outputs each preceded by a natural-language feedback label (e.g., 'A good summary: {positive} A bad summary: {negative}'), with loss applied only to the model-generated tokens via masking. At inference, the model is prompted with the question plus a single positive feedback token (e.g., 'A helpful answer:') and generates a response conditioned on that signal. The feedback templates are task-specific (summarization, dialogue, shared) and randomly sampled during training to increase diversity.

## Method

Chain of Hindsight (CoH) converts all human preference ratings into templated natural-language feedback sequences and fine-tunes a causal Transformer using the standard next-token prediction objective, identical to pretraining. The model is conditioned on a chain of feedback-example pairs (e.g., 'Bad: {subpar answer} Good: {excellent answer}') and trained to predict the tokens of the model-generated outputs while masking out the feedback tokens so they do not contribute to the loss. To prevent the model from trivially copying the positive example, 0–5% of past tokens are randomly masked during training, and a regularization term on the Pile pretraining corpus preserves general language modeling ability. At inference, providing only the positive feedback label guides generation toward preferred outputs. Unlike SFT (which uses only positive data), SFT-U (which adds an unlikelihood loss), Conditional SFT (which uses a single control token), or RLHF (which requires a reward model and PPO), CoH uses the full spectrum of feedback within a single autoregressive objective, making it straightforward to train and scale.

## Results

On the TL;DR summarization benchmark, CoH achieves an average human-evaluation win rate of 57.5% against the base model and 45.3% against RLHF (Δ=14.5%), substantially outperforming all baselines across accuracy, coherence, and coverage metrics. On the Anthropic HH dialogue benchmark, CoH wins 49.5% of pairwise comparisons against the base model and 36.9% against RLHF (Δ=13.5%), with the largest margin over SFT-U (56.0% vs 13.9%, Δ=42.1%). On few-shot NLP benchmarks (alignment tax evaluation), CoH shows moderate improvements over both the pretrained GPT-J and SFT-finetuned model (e.g., 43.14 vs 42.53 average one-shot accuracy), indicating it is less susceptible to alignment tax.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoH consistently outperforms RLHF (Ouyang et al., 2022) across both summarization and dialogue: on summarization, CoH wins 45.3% vs RLHF's 30.8% (Δ=14.5%); on dialogue, CoH wins 36.9% vs RLHF's 23.4% (Δ=13.5%). Against SFT with unlikelihood (Welleck et al., 2019), CoH achieves the largest margins, winning 61.7% vs 21.4% on summarization (Δ=40.3%) and 56.0% vs 13.9% on dialogue (Δ=42.1%), indicating that unlikelihood loss actively degrades generation quality. Compared to conditional SFT (C-SFT, inspired by Decision Transformer, Chen et al., 2021), CoH wins 42.3% vs 29.6% on summarization (Δ=12.7%) and 36.8% vs 22.1% on dialogue (Δ=14.7%), showing that conditioning on a full sequence of feedback-example pairs is more effective than a single conditional prefix. Against Koala (Geng et al., 2023), which uses SFT on the higher-quality ShareGPT data, CoH trained on open-source preference datasets is on par in human evaluation, and combining CoH with Koala's ShareGPT data yields slightly better results, while C-SFT and plain SFT lag considerably behind Koala.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoH positions itself as a unifying framework that subsumes SFT, conditional SFT (related to Decision Transformer), and SFT with unlikelihood as special cases, while explicitly departing from RLHF by replacing reward-model + PPO optimization with a standard causal language modeling objective. The conceptual lineage draws from Hindsight Experience Replay in RL, but repurposes the 'hindsight' idea for language models by converting preference comparisons into natural-language feedback sequences that the model conditions on. It also connects to instruction tuning and conditional generation (CTRL, Decision Transformer), generalizing from a single control token to a sequence of feedback-example pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoH offers a practically simpler and more scalable alternative to RLHF—using the same training objective as pretraining—while consistently outperforming it on both summarization and dialogue benchmarks, which could shift the default alignment recipe for practitioners who find RLHF fragile and expensive. The finding that CoH exhibits less alignment tax than SFT on few-shot NLP benchmarks is a useful secondary result. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer chains of feedback without prohibitive sequence-length costs, whether it generalizes beyond the two evaluated domains, and how it interacts with online or iterative preference learning.
