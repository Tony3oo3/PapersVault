## Problem

Graph anomaly detection under limited supervision faces two intertwined challenges: (1) consistency training on graphs typically relies on random sampling as noise, making it difficult to calibrate augmentation strength and risking over- or under-augmentation that hinders label propagation to less distinctive instances; (2) class imbalance is usually addressed by reweighting or resampling neighbors, but uncertainty in predicting neighboring labels complicates the selection of truly homophilic neighbors. Existing methods have not jointly addressed both the quality of noise injection for unlabeled data and the structural exploitation of homophily differences between normal and anomalous nodes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph with node feature vectors, edge structure (including multi-relation graphs), and a small set of labeled nodes (1% training ratio for most datasets, 0.01% for T-Social), while all remaining nodes serve as unlabeled data for consistency training. Due to class imbalance, high-quality unlabeled nodes are selected using class-specific confidence thresholds (τ_n for normal, τ_a for anomalous) rather than a single threshold. The output is a binary anomaly prediction per node (normal vs. anomalous) with a continuous score, evaluated via AUROC, AUPRC, and Macro F1.

## Method

CONSISGAD combines a homophily-aware GNN backbone with a learnable data augmentation module trained iteratively. The backbone computes edge-level homophily representations via an MLP on concatenated node embeddings, then aggregates them to capture each node's contextual homophily distribution—exploiting the observation that normal nodes exhibit higher homophily than anomalous nodes. The augmentation module applies attention-based masking to intermediate node representations, using a sharpen function to binarize attention weights at a fixed drop ratio ξ, thereby selectively preserving informative dimensions. The augmentation is guided by two complementary metrics: label consistency (the augmented node retains the same pseudo-label) and distribution diversity (the augmented representation is maximally distant from the original). Training alternates between optimizing the augmentation module (with frozen GNN) using a weighted combination of consistency and diversity losses, and optimizing the GNN (with frozen augmentation) using cross-entropy on labeled nodes plus consistency loss on high-quality unlabeled nodes.

## Results

On Amazon (1% training ratio), CONSISGAD achieves AUROC 93.91%, AUPRC 83.33%, and Macro F1 90.03%, outperforming the strongest baseline GDN (92.16/81.87/89.75) on AUROC and AUPRC. On YelpChi, CONSISGAD reaches AUROC 83.36%, AUPRC 47.33%, and Macro F1 69.72%, surpassing the best baseline BWGNN(hetero) (77.62/39.87/66.54) by 5.74, 7.46, and 3.18 points respectively. The backbone alone (CONSISGAD(GNN)) already surpasses all GAD baselines on YelpChi, T-Finance, and T-Social, confirming the effectiveness of the homophily-aware aggregation design. Under varying supervision (1%–40%), CONSISGAD maintains its lead up to 10% training ratio, with the gap narrowing as more labels become available.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CONSISGAD outperforms all 14 baselines on YelpChi across all three metrics, with the largest margin over BWGNN(hetero) (+5.74 AUROC, +7.46 AUPRC) and GraphConsis (+4.45 AUROC). On Amazon, CONSISGAD leads on AUROC (+1.75 over GDN) and AUPRC (+1.46 over GDN), but trails BWGNN(homo) by 0.45 points on Macro F1 (90.03 vs. 90.48), which the authors attribute to BWGNN's model selection criterion favoring that metric. The homophily-aware backbone alone (CONSISGAD(GNN)) already surpasses all GAD-specific baselines on YelpChi (80.95 AUROC vs. 78.91 for GraphConsis) and T-Finance/T-Social, isolating the contribution of the architectural design. Spectral-based methods (BWGNN, GHRN) are the strongest GAD baselines overall, while node-feature-oriented methods (MLP, GDN) perform well on Amazon where features dominate over topology. On the industrial graph (~1M nodes), CONSISGAD achieves 99.77 AUROC and 69.06 AUPRC, beating GAGA (99.75/65.19) and GDN (99.72/63.78) on AUPRC by 3.87 and 5.28 points respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of consistency training (originally developed for vision and extended to graphs via random augmentation) and graph anomaly detection (GAD), which has been addressed through spatial-centric neighbor selection (CARE-GNN, PC-GNN) and spectral-centric filtering (BWGNN, GHRN). It departs from prior consistency training on graphs by replacing hand-crafted or random augmentation with a learnable, attention-based masking module guided by two explicit metrics—label consistency and distribution diversity—borrowed conceptually from Bo et al. (2022) but reformulated to operate directly on data-augmentation pairs without a validation set. The homophily-aware neighborhood aggregation backbone is a novel architectural choice that exploits the empirically observed difference in homophily distribution between normal and anomalous nodes, rather than the more common strategy of filtering out heterophilic neighbors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two practically critical constraints in GAD—limited labeled data and severe class imbalance—that are common in production fraud-detection settings, validated on an industrial graph from Grab. The learnable augmentation module is a reusable component that could benefit other semi-supervised graph tasks beyond anomaly detection, and the homophily-aware backbone offers a simple, interpretable alternative to complex spectral filters. Open questions include scalability to graphs with tens of millions of nodes (the iterative two-phase training doubles per-iteration cost), whether the approach generalizes to multi-class anomaly detection, and why the backbone underperforms on certain heterophily graphs (Chameleon, Squirrel, Actor) where neighborhood label distributions lack class-discriminative structure.
