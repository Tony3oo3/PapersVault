## Problem

Non-convex constrained optimization problems achieve superior reconstruction quality over convex counterparts in signal restoration, but they lack guarantees for global optima, making solutions non-unique and unverifiable. While convex formulations guarantee global optima, they poorly model real-world sparsity, low-rankness, and noise patterns. This paper introduces a family of invex and quasi-invex functions that provide global optima guarantees in non-convex settings for the first time in constrained inverse problems.

## Input / Output

The method operates on the standard inverse problem formulation y = Ax + η, where A is an acquisition matrix (e.g., CT projection geometry, coded aperture spectral imaging, or identity for denoising) and η is noise. For CT, input is 720-view noisy projections of 3D volumes (2,378 training images from material sciences objects); for spectral reconstruction, input is RGB images from the NTIRE 2022 dataset (1,000 RGB-spectral pairs); for TV filtering, input is noisy 2D images. Output is the restored signal x in each case: a 3D CT volume, a spectral reflectance image, or a denoised image, solved via the constrained program min g(x) s.t. f(Ax−y) ≤ ε.

## Method

The paper defines 'admissible functions' — element-wise mappings h(x) = Σ s(|x[i]|) where s(w)/w² is non-increasing on (0,∞) — and proves (Theorem 3) that they are invex and, critically, closed under summation, a result absent from prior invex literature. This closure property enables constructing complex regularizers and fidelity losses (LL_p, MCP, SCAD, Cauchy, Geman-McClure, Welsh, adaptive robust) as sums of admissible components while preserving invexity/quasi-invexity. The paper then proves global optima for the constrained program (1) under these settings (Theorem 7, with RIP-based recovery guarantees analogous to convex compressive sensing), and extends ADMM (Theorem 9) and accelerated proximal gradient methods to converge at O(1/t) rate on invex/quasi-invex objectives. TV-like extensions are constructed via first-order difference matrices applied to admissible functions, yielding invex/quasi-invex total-variation regularizers for 1D, 2D, and 3D signals.

## Results

In CT reconstruction (Experiment 1), the best invex/quasi-invex configuration (LL_p regularizer + Adaptive Robust loss) achieves PSNR of 40.78 dB, SSIM of 0.9620, and AbsError of 0.1246, substantially outperforming the FISTA-Net ℓ₁-norm baseline (PSNR 37.22, SSIM 0.9520, AbsError 0.4213). In spectral reconstruction (Experiment 2), the Adaptive Robust invex loss reduces RMSE to 2.1315 versus 2.2887 for the ℓ₂-norm baseline. In TV filtering (Experiment 3), the invex TV-LL_p regularizer achieves SSIM of 0.6320 and MS-SSIM of 0.9235 with an ADMM residual of 1.8×10⁻³, compared to 0.6050, 0.9106, and 2.8×10⁻³ for the classical ℓ₁-norm TV.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two primary baselines: FISTA-Net with ℓ₁-norm regularization (Xiang et al., 2021) for CT reconstruction, and MST++ with ℓ₂-norm loss (Cai et al., 2022) for spectral reconstruction. In Experiment 1, all invex/quasi-invex regularizer-loss combinations outperform the FISTA-Net ℓ₁-norm baseline across every metric; the best configuration (LL_p + Adaptive Robust) reduces AbsError from 0.4213 to 0.1246 (70% reduction) and improves PSNR from 37.22 to 40.78 dB. In Experiment 2, the Adaptive Robust invex loss (eq. 7) beats the ℓ₂-norm on AbsError (0.1830 vs. 0.2145), RMSE (2.1315 vs. 2.2887), and LogRMSE (0.2534 vs. 0.2765), though the ℓ₂-norm marginally wins on SqrtError (1.3315 vs. 1.3530 for the best invex loss). In Experiment 3, the invex TV-LL_p regularizer dominates the classical ℓ₁-norm TV on SSIM (0.6320 vs. 0.6050), MS-SSIM (0.9235 vs. 0.9106), and ADMM convergence residual (1.8×10⁻³ vs. 2.8×10⁻³), demonstrating both superior reconstruction quality and faster convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the invexity framework of Hanson (1981) and the initial application of invex regularizers to imaging by Pinilla et al. (2022a), which first demonstrated global optima guarantees for a single invex regularizer paired with an ℓ₂ fidelity term. The key departure is the introduction of 'admissible functions' — a family closed under summation and composition — which is the first such closure result in the invex optimization literature. The paper also reinterprets well-known non-convex losses and regularizers (Cauchy, Geman-McClure, MCP, SCAD, LL_p) as invex or quasi-invex, providing the theoretical guarantees that their empirical success in the literature (Carrillo et al., 2016; Zhang, 2010; Fan & Li, 2001) had always lacked. Algorithmically, it extends ADMM and accelerated proximal gradient methods to the invex/quasi-invex setting with convergence rates matching their convex counterparts, building on the convex ADMM framework of Boyd et al. (2011) and the non-convex ADMM convergence results of Wang et al. (2019b).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic theoretical guarantee of global optimality for a broad family of non-convex constrained restoration problems, bridging the long-standing gap between the superior empirical performance of non-convex regularizers and the safety net of convex optimization. The closure-under-summation result is practically important because it gives practitioners a constructive recipe for building new invex/quasi-invex objectives from known components. However, the empirical gains over convex baselines are modest (roughly 1–2 dB PSNR, 0.001–0.003 SSIM), and the authors acknowledge that applications beyond imaging (e.g., low-rank matrix recovery, deep learning training losses) remain unexplored. Open questions include decoupling the η function from the recovery error bound, improving algorithmic efficiency beyond ADMM/APGM, and whether the invex/quasi-invex lens can inform the design of learned regularizers in unrolled networks.
