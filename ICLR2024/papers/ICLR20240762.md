## Problem

Step-based RL methods achieve high sample efficiency by leveraging per-step rewards and state transitions, but their exploration produces jerky, temporally uncorrelated actions that are difficult to deploy on real hardware. Episodic RL (ERL) generates smooth, correlated trajectories by sampling in a parameter space, but treats each trajectory as an opaque black-box data point, discarding the rich step-based information collected during execution and suffering from poor sample efficiency. The paper asks whether step-based information can be integrated into ERL policy updates without sacrificing the smooth, correlated exploration that makes ERL attractive for robotics.

## Input / Output

The policy network takes the initial robot state (joint positions and velocities) and task goal as input and outputs a trajectory parameter vector w drawn from a full-covariance Gaussian distribution. This parameter vector is mapped through ProDMPs to produce a complete position and velocity trajectory over the episode horizon, which is then tracked by a PD controller to generate per-step joint torques. Unlike step-based methods that emit one action per timestep, TCE commits to an entire trajectory at the start of each episode, and the segment-wise decomposition (K paired time points) is used only during the policy update, not during execution.

## Method

TCE redefines the ERL learning objective by decomposing the trajectory-level likelihood and advantage into K segment-wise counterparts, each defined by a randomly selected pair of time points (t_k, t'_k). For each segment, the method computes a local trajectory likelihood using the ProDMPs basis-function mapping (reducing the D·T-dimensional trajectory distribution to a D·2 sub-distribution) and a segment advantage that sums discounted rewards over the segment plus a value-function bootstrap at the segment boundary. The policy update uses importance sampling with the ratio of new-to-old segment likelihoods weighted by segment advantages, effectively 'opening the black box' by attributing credit to individual trajectory segments. A full-covariance Gaussian policy over the ProDMP parameter space captures both temporal and cross-DoF correlations, and a differentiable Trust Region Projection Layer (TRPL) enforces per-state KL constraints on both mean and covariance to ensure stable updates in the high-dimensional parameter space.

## Results

On the 50-task MetaWorld benchmark, TCE achieves a near-perfect Interquartile Mean (IQM) success rate, surpassing all baselines after approximately 10^7 environment interactions, with nearly two-thirds of runs exceeding 99% success. On the Hopper Jump task, TCE achieves the highest max jump height with the fastest convergence, while on Box Pushing (both dense and sparse rewards), TCE and gSDE lead with TCE showing greater cross-seed consistency. On the highly sparse Table Tennis task, BBRL Cov leads with a 20% higher success rate than TCE, while step-based methods (e.g., TRPL) plateau at only ~15% success. In trajectory smoothness, TCE achieves the lowest maximum jerk (3.4×10³ rad/s³) and dimensionless jerk (61×10⁶), roughly 2–4× smoother than step-based baselines like PPO (9.1×10³) and TRPL (12.9×10³).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TCE outperforms all step-based baselines (PPO, SAC, TRPL, PINK) on MetaWorld, Hopper Jump, and Box Pushing in both success rate and sample efficiency, with the gap most pronounced on contact-rich Box Pushing where PPO and TRPL fail to learn effectively even with dense rewards. Against gSDE, TCE achieves comparable or better success rates on Box Pushing but with significantly tighter confidence intervals across random seeds, whereas gSDE shows high seed sensitivity. Compared to the primary episodic baseline BBRL (Std), TCE converges substantially faster due to its use of per-step intermediate rewards for policy updates, while BBRL Cov (full covariance variant) narrows the gap and ultimately surpasses TCE on the highly sparse Table Tennis task by ~20% success rate. PINK achieves competitive final performance on MetaWorld but at the cost of lower sample efficiency than SAC due to its off-policy training with limited parallel environment utilization. Overall, TCE matches the sample efficiency of SoTA step-based RL while retaining the smooth, temporally-correlated exploration of episodic methods, with its main limitation being moderate performance under extreme reward sparsity.

## Relation to Prior Work

TCE extends the episodic RL framework of BBRL (Otto et al., 2022) by replacing its black-box trajectory-level policy updates with segment-wise updates that exploit per-step rewards and states, effectively bridging the gap between step-based RL (PPO, SAC) and episodic RL. It incorporates ProDMPs (Li et al., 2023) for trajectory generation and paired time-point likelihood estimation, and deploys differentiable Trust Region Projection Layers (Otto et al., 2021) to stabilize full-covariance Gaussian policy updates in the high-dimensional parameter space. The key conceptual departure from prior ERL is decomposing the trajectory likelihood and advantage into K segment-level quantities, enabling the policy to credit or blame specific trajectory portions rather than the entire episode uniformly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TCE demonstrates that the sample-efficiency gap between episodic and step-based RL can be largely closed without sacrificing trajectory smoothness or exploration consistency, which is critical for real-world robotic deployment where jerky motions are impractical. The finding that full-covariance policies meaningfully improve even black-box ERL (BBRL Cov. > BBRL Std.) suggests that correlation modeling is an underexploited axis in episodic methods. However, the method still underperforms on highly sparse-reward tasks (table tennis), requires a low-level tracking controller that limits applicability to locomotion, and operates in an open-loop fashion that lacks adaptability to dynamic environments—key open questions for future work.
