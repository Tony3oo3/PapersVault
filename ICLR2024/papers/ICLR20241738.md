## Problem

Graph-based semi-supervised learning methods such as Laplace and Poisson learning produce degenerate, nearly constant label functions when labeled data is extremely sparse (1–5 labels per class), because long random walks on the graph mix and forget their starting positions. Existing remedies like higher-order Laplacians or re-weighting near labels do not fully prevent this trivial-solution behavior, particularly for unlabeled data that is still governed by the standard Laplace equation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a symmetric KNN graph weight matrix (K=10, Gaussian-like weights in a VAE latent space for image datasets, or citation graph for text), a handful of labeled nodes (1–5 per class), and optionally node feature vectors for V-GPN. It outputs a continuous label function u(x_i) ∈ R^k for every node, from which discrete class predictions are read off by argmax. The only tunable hyperparameter is the variance-enlarging strength λ, and the iteration terminates when the random-walk distribution reaches its invariant measure (‖p_t − p_∞‖_∞ ≤ 1/n).

## Method

VPL augments the standard graph-based SSL objective with a negative variance term −λ·Var[u] (weighted by node degrees q_i = d_i/Σd_j), which converts the Laplace equation on unlabeled nodes into a Poisson equation Lu(x_i) = λq_i(u(x_i) − ū). This yields two algorithms: V-Laplace (hard boundary conditions on labeled nodes, Poisson equation on unlabeled) and V-Poisson (Poisson equation on all nodes with source terms y_i − ȳ on labeled nodes). A key discrete insight (Lemma 4.1) shows the variance term is algebraically equivalent to replacing edge weights w_ij with w_ij − λq_iq_j, which under a mild class-separation assumption amplifies within-class edges and suppresses between-class edges. In the variational (n→∞, h→0) limit, the Euler–Lagrange equation becomes a weighted p-Poisson PDE involving the ∞-Laplacian, and a convexity condition on λ is derived via a Poincaré–Wirtinger inequality. V-GPN embeds the same variance-enlarged propagation into a GAT-style attention network, where the softmax-normalized attention makes D = I and the update simplifies to U^(t) ← WU^(t−1) + λŪ^(t−1).

## Results

On MNIST with 1 label per class, V-Poisson achieves 93.6% accuracy, outperforming Poisson learning (91.3%) and Laplace learning (16.7%) by 2.3 and 76.9 percentage points respectively. On CIFAR-10 with 1 label per class, V-Poisson reaches 41.4% versus 39.1% for Poisson and 10.3% for Laplace. For semi-supervised node classification on Cora with 1 label per class, V-GPN achieves 58.9% accuracy, surpassing GCN (51.1%), GAT (50.5%), and GPN (53.2%) by 7.8, 8.4, and 5.7 percentage points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

V-Poisson consistently outperforms all 11 baselines across MNIST, FashionMNIST, and CIFAR-10. On MNIST with 1 label per class, V-Poisson (93.6%) beats the strongest baseline Poisson learning (91.3%) by 2.3 points and Random Walk (85.0%) by 8.6 points, while Laplace learning (16.7%) and MBO (19.4%) collapse to near-chance levels. On CIFAR-10, V-Poisson (41.4%) surpasses Poisson (39.1%) and Random Walk (37.5%) with 1 label per class, and the gap widens to 3.4 points at 5 labels (56.3% vs. 52.9%). For GNN-based node classification, V-GPN dominates GCN, GAT, and GPN on both Cora and CiteSeer at every label rate; on CiteSeer with 1 label per class, V-GPN (49.5%) exceeds GCN (43.8%) by 5.7 points and GPN (39.8%) by 9.7 points. V-Laplace also improves over standard Laplace learning by large margins (e.g., 92.0% vs. 16.7% on MNIST with 1 label), confirming that the variance-enlarged term is the key driver of the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the graph-based semi-supervised learning lineage from Laplace learning (Zhu et al., 2003) through Poisson learning (Calder et al., 2020), which replaced hard boundary conditions with source terms to handle sparse labels. The key departure is the introduction of a variance-enlarged regularization term that converts the Laplace equation governing unlabeled data into a Poisson equation, and the paper proves this is equivalent to reducing all edge weights by a rank-one perturbation. For the GNN variant (V-GPN), the paper borrows the attention mechanism from GAT (Veličković et al., 2018) and the feature transformation module from GPN (Wan et al., 2021), but replaces their standard message-passing with the variance-enlarged Poisson iteration. The theoretical analysis draws on PDE techniques from the p-Laplacian literature (El Alaoui et al., 2016; Oberman, 2013) to derive a weighted p-Poisson equation in the infinite-data limit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The variance-enlarged regularization provides a simple, single-parameter fix for the well-known trivial-solution problem in graph-based SSL with sparse labels, and the equivalence to edge weight reduction offers a clean mechanistic explanation for why it works. The variational characterization as a weighted p-Poisson equation is a useful theoretical tool that could inform future analysis of related graph-based methods. However, the practical impact is somewhat limited: the method is specific to graph-based (non-deep) SSL pipelines, the tested datasets are relatively small and standard, and the gains over Poisson learning, while consistent, are modest (1–2 percentage points). Open questions include whether the variance-enlarged idea transfers to modern deep semi-supervised methods (e.g., MixMatch, FixMatch) and whether the edge-weight-reduction interpretation generalizes to non-quadratic losses.
