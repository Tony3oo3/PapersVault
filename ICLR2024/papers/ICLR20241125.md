## Problem

Establishing robust pixel-to-point correspondences between RGB images and point clouds for I2P registration is fundamentally difficult because images encode appearance while point clouds encode geometry, making cross-modality metric learning (contrastive, triplet, or InfoCE losses) prone to poor convergence and limited generalization. Existing fully-supervised methods require task-specific training data and still struggle with large viewpoint changes, low overlap, and sparse point density.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241125/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 512×704 for indoor or 512×1280 for outdoor scenes) and a point cloud with a known camera pose, which is projected to a depth map and densified via erosion/dilation. It outputs a set of pixel-to-point correspondences and the SE(3) relative pose (rotation and translation) between the image and point cloud, solved via Kabsch-RANSAC (when Zoe-Depth estimates are available) or PnP-RANSAC. A dense grid of keypoints is uniformly sampled on both the depth map and image for feature extraction and matching.

## Method

FreeReg's core insight is to avoid cross-modality metric learning entirely by unifying image and point cloud modalities through pretrained large-scale models, then performing mono-modality feature matching. For diffusion features, it extracts intermediate UNet decoder representations from Stable Diffusion at denoising step t̂=150: for RGB images via the forward noising process, and for depth maps via ControlNet-conditioned reverse denoising from pure Gaussian noise, using early upsampling layers (0, 4, 6) reduced to 128 dimensions via PCA. For geometric features, it uses Zoe-Depth to estimate metric depth from the RGB image, recovers a point cloud, and extracts FCGF per-point features; the same pipeline is applied to the depth map derived from the input point cloud. The two feature types are fused by weighted concatenation (default w=0.5) and matched with a nearest-neighbor mutual-check matcher, yielding dense and accurate correspondences without any training on the I2P registration task.

## Results

On three public benchmarks (3DMatch, ScanNet, KITTI-DC), FreeReg without any I2P-task training achieves 47.0%/56.8%/58.3% Inlier Ratio and 63.8%/78.0%/70.5% Registration Recall (using Kabsch for indoor, PnP for outdoor), representing a 20.6% average IR improvement, a 3.0× higher Inlier Number, and a 48.6% average RR improvement over the best supervised baselines. The fused feature (w=0.5) outperforms diffusion-only (FreeReg-D) and geometric-only (FreeReg-G) variants by 22.9% and 16.4% RR respectively on 3DMatch. On the concurrent-work protocol (RGBD-Scenes-v2), FreeReg+PnP achieves 57.3% RR vs. 56.4% for 2D3D-Matr, while FreeReg-Ft (after ~1h ControlNet fine-tuning) reaches 68.6% RR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeReg decisively outperforms all supervised cross-modality baselines: LCD (Pham et al., 2020) achieves only 35.1% IR and 4.3 IN on 3DMatch, while FreeReg reaches 47.0% IR and 82.8 IN without any I2P training; SuperGlue (Sarlin et al., 2020) applied cross-modality drops to 11.1% IR due to modality gap; I2P-Matr (Li et al., 2023), the strongest supervised baseline, reaches 24.9% IR on 3DMatch vs. FreeReg's 47.0%. On the concurrent-work RGBD-Scenes-v2 protocol, FreeReg+PnP (57.3% RR) slightly edges out 2D3D-Matr (56.4% RR), and P2-Net (Wang et al., 2021) achieves only 38.4% RR. Against vision foundation backbones (Dino-v2, CLIP, ATTF) used as cross-modality features in Table 6, FreeReg's diffusion features achieve 47.0% IR vs. at most 13.0% for Dino-v2 and 3.3% for CLIP, confirming that diffusion intermediate representations are uniquely suited for cross-modality consistency. FCGF (Choy et al., 2019) as a 3D-only baseline achieves 84.7% RR on 3DMatch point-cloud registration, while FreeReg's geometric features alone reach 87.0% RR, showing competitive 3D matching quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

FreeReg departs from the dominant paradigm in image-to-point cloud (I2P) registration, where prior methods (LCD, P2-Net, 2D3D-Matr) rely on cross-modality metric learning (contrastive/triplet/InfoCE losses) to align 2D and 3D features. Instead, it repurposes pretrained diffusion models (Stable Diffusion + ControlNet) and a monocular depth estimator (Zoe-Depth) to unify the modality: point clouds are projected to depth maps, and intermediate UNet features from the diffusion process are shown to be semantically consistent across RGB images and depth maps. This builds on the emerging line of work using diffusion model internal representations for semantic correspondence (Tang et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2023; Kwon et al., 2022), but extends it to the cross-modality setting and combines it with geometric features from FCGF for fine-grained localization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is that FreeReg achieves strong I2P registration (48.6% RR improvement, 3× inlier number) without any task-specific training, sidestepping the notoriously unstable cross-modality metric learning that plagues prior supervised methods. This suggests pretrained generative models may serve as powerful, modality-agnostic feature extractors for broader cross-modal tasks. However, the ~9.3s runtime and 12.7G GPU memory per pair, the manual selection of diffusion layers and steps, and the dependence on Zoe-Depth's scale accuracy (3.4m error on KITTI) are significant limitations. Open questions include automatic layer/step selection, knowledge distillation for speedup, and whether the diffusion-feature insight generalizes to other cross-modality registration problems (e.g., LiDAR-to-camera, thermal-to-RGB).
