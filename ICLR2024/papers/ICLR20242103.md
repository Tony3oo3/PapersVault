## Problem

Existing SafeRL methods, particularly Lagrangian-based approaches, fail to achieve zero-cost performance when cost thresholds approach zero, especially in vision-only tasks where model inaccuracies and limited sample efficiency compound the problem. Prior world-model-based safe RL methods (e.g., LAMBDA, Safe SLAC) still cannot reach nearly zero-cost across diverse environments because they inadequately model the agent's safety in current and future states, and they either neglect online planning or fail to reconcile errors between cost models and critics.

## Input / Output

SafeDreamer accepts either 64×64×3 RGB images (first-person, forward, and rear views in Safety-Gymnasium) or low-dimensional state vectors as observations, which are encoded into discrete latent representations via a variational autoencoder. At each decision step, the method outputs a single continuous action (scaled to [-1, +1]) selected from a planned action trajectory of horizon H=15, with the first action executed in the real environment. The world model simultaneously predicts reward and cost values at each latent state, enabling joint evaluation of safety and performance during planning.

## Method

SafeDreamer extends DreamerV3's RSSM-based world model with dedicated cost decoders and cost critics alongside the standard reward components, then introduces three planning variants. OSRP performs online safety-reward planning at every timestep using the Constrained Cross-Entropy Method (CCEM) within the world model: it samples trajectories from both a Gaussian distribution and a reward-driven actor, filters them by a cost threshold criterion, and iteratively refines the action distribution toward elite safe trajectories. OSRP-Lag augments this by incorporating a PID Lagrangian multiplier into the sorting key (J^R − λ_p·J^C) when sufficient safe trajectories exist, balancing long-term reward against cost. BSRP-Lag instead uses background planning with the Augmented Lagrangian method to train a safe actor via stochastic backpropagation over imagined latent rollouts, eliminating the need for per-step online planning. The key insight is that integrating Lagrangian relaxation directly into the world-model planning loop allows the algorithm to balance estimation errors between the cost model and cost critic, achieving nearly zero-cost performance that neither model-free Lagrangian methods nor prior model-based safe RL approaches can attain.

## Results

SafeDreamer (BSRP-Lag) achieves nearly zero-cost performance across all five Safety-Gymnasium vision tasks, attaining rewards comparable to LAMBDA while reducing costs by 94.3%. On Car-Racing, it reaches 763.1±1.34 average reward with only 0.04±0.01 average cost (vs. DreamerV3's 580.1±2.35 cost). On FormulaOne, it achieves 25.3±0.51 reward with 0.43±0.08 cost (vs. DreamerV3's 56.36±2.32 cost). On MetaDrive, SafeDreamer achieves a 100% arrival rate with 2.2±0.1 episode cost after 4M steps, compared to PPO-Reward Shaping's 72% arrival rate and 14.1±6.2 cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DreamerV3 (the unconstrained world-model baseline), SafeDreamer achieves comparable or slightly lower reward (763.1 vs. 775.1 on Car-Racing; 25.3 vs. 32.2 on FormulaOne) while reducing cost by over 99% (0.04 vs. 580.1; 0.43 vs. 56.36), demonstrating that safety constraints can be enforced with minimal reward sacrifice. Against LAMBDA (Bayesian world-model SafeRL), SafeDreamer matches reward levels while achieving a 94.3% cost reduction on Safety-Gymnasium vision tasks. Against Safe SLAC, SafeDreamer overcomes the cost floor that Safe SLAC hits due to world-model inaccuracies, reaching near-zero cost where Safe SLAC plateaus. Against MPC:sim (ground-truth simulator planning), SafeDreamer handles dynamic obstacles in SafetyPointButton1 more effectively, as MPC lacks a cost critic within its limited planning horizon. Against model-free baselines (PPO-Lag, TRPO-Lag, CPO, Saute PPO/TRPO), SafeDreamer dominates on both reward and cost, as model-free methods struggle to achieve high reward while maintaining zero violations under tight cost thresholds (b=2.0). Against MBPPO-Lag, SafeDreamer continues to increase reward even after cost drops to low levels, whereas MBPPO-Lag's reward stagnates once cost decreases.

## Relation to Prior Work

SafeDreamer extends the DreamerV3 world model architecture (Hafner et al., 2023) by integrating safety-aware planning and Lagrangian optimization into both online (CCEM-based) and background (actor-training) planning loops. It departs from LAMBDA (As et al., 2022), which applied Bayesian and Lagrangian methods within DreamerV1 but lacked online planning and low-dimensional support, and from Safe SLAC (Hogewind et al., 2022), which embedded Lagrangian into SLAC without exploiting the world model for planning. The key conceptual unification is treating the Constrained Cross-Entropy Method (Wen & Topcu, 2018) as the planning mechanism and PID/Augmented Lagrangian (Stooke et al., 2020; Simo & Laursen, 1992) as the reward-cost balancing mechanism within a single RSSM-based world model, supporting both vision and vector inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SafeDreamer is the first method to achieve nearly zero-cost performance with vision-only input in the Safety-Gymnasium benchmark, demonstrating that world model planning can overcome the fundamental limitation of model-free SafeRL methods under tight cost constraints. The 94.3% cost reduction over LAMBDA while maintaining comparable rewards, and the finding that world model reconstruction quality directly governs cost model accuracy, provide actionable guidance for practitioners. Key limitations include per-task training (no transfer), the need for a non-zero cost threshold (2.0) in vision tasks due to cost model estimation errors, and the computational cost of online planning. Open questions include whether pre-trained world models on multi-task offline data can accelerate safety alignment, and whether the world-model-plus-Lagrangian paradigm transfers to LLM safety alignment.
