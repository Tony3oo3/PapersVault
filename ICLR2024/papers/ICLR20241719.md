## Problem

Current Neural Operators can approximate PDE solution operators but lack a systematic framework for data assimilation: they cannot recursively correct evolving trajectories using sparsely sampled noisy measurements, leading to accumulating errors over long time horizons. While infinite-dimensional observer theory provides a principled correction mechanism for semilinear PDEs, its implementation requires solving computationally intractable Riccati equations and assumes measurements at every time instant, making it impractical for large-scale or partially observed systems.

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial spatial field z_0 at a fixed grid resolution (512 points for 1D KS, 64×64 for 2D NS, 128 for KdV) and a sequence of sparse noisy measurements y(t_k) ∈ R^p sampled at an arbitrary rate α over the prediction horizon. During a warm-up window τ_w, measurements are available at every time step; during the prediction horizon τ_p, only a fraction α of time steps carry measurements. The output is the full estimated trajectory {ẑ(t_k)} where each step is either a pure prediction (no measurement) or a prediction plus a correction term (measurement available), producing a spatial field at the same resolution as the input.

## Method

NODA decomposes the semilinear PDE observer solution (derived from the semigroup representation) into a prediction step and a correction step. The prediction step uses a residual Fourier Neural Operator layer (ẑ_pred = ẑ_{k-1} + W(ẑ_{k-1})) to propagate the state forward in time, inheriting discretization invariance from the FNO. The correction step, applied only when a measurement y(t_k) is available, adds a gated residual K(ẑ_pred)[y(t_k) − E(ẑ_pred)] where E is a learnable measurement operator and K is a generalized gating mechanism (extending Guen & Thome's architecture) that maps the measurement-space error back to the PDE state space via the adjoint of the discretized observation operator. Training is supervised with a two-term loss balancing solution reconstruction over the full trajectory and measurement reconstruction over the warm-up window, with the model initialized at the true initial condition and fed measurements only up to a cutoff t_H before switching to prediction-only rollouts.

## Results

On the 2D Navier-Stokes equation (Re=40, t_f=1000, SNR=∞), NODA achieves an averaged RelMSE of 13×10⁻³, outperforming MNO (26×10⁻³), MWNO (188×10⁻³), FNO (265×10⁻³), and C-LSTM (529×10⁻³). On the 1D Kuramoto-Sivashinsky equation (t_f=120, SNR=∞), NODA achieves 405×10⁻³ RelMSE, marginally beating MNO (410×10⁻³) and substantially outperforming FNO (892×10⁻³) and LSTM (953×10⁻³). Data assimilation further reduces NODA's NS RelMSE from 26×10⁻³ (α=0%) to 9×10⁻³ (α=30%), demonstrating consistent improvement with more measurements. Computationally, NODA takes 1.5× the time of MNO per sample, compared to 2.25× for FNO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NODA consistently outperforms all five baselines on the Navier-Stokes equation, with the largest gaps against FNO (20× lower RelMSE at SNR=∞, t_f=1000) and C-LSTM (40× lower). Against MNO, the closest competitor, NODA achieves roughly 2× lower RelMSE on NS (13 vs. 26 at SNR=∞, t_f=1000) but only marginally beats it on KS (405 vs. 410 at SNR=∞, t_f=120), where MNO benefits from Sobolev norm and dissipativity regularizations. On the KdV equation, NODA also leads all baselines, achieving 61×10⁻³ RelMSE at t_f=200, SNR=∞ versus 92 (MWNO), 112 (FNO), and 254 (GRU). The unique data-assimilation capability of NODA (not available to any baseline) reduces NS RelMSE from 26 to 9×10⁻³ as the measurement ratio α increases from 0% to 30%, a 3× improvement that no competing method can replicate. Computationally, NODA's per-sample cost is 1.5× that of MNO, making it practical for long-horizon rollouts where baselines accumulate error without correction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Neural Operator (NO) framework by grounding a recursive prediction-correction loop in the theory of infinite-dimensional observer design for semilinear PDEs (Afshar et al., 2023). The prediction step reuses the FNO architecture as a residual operator, while the correction step adapts the gating mechanism from learning-based Kalman filters (Guen & Thome, 2020) to approximate the observer gain K. Unlike prior NO approaches (FNO, MNO, MWNO) that perform one-shot or purely autoregressive rollouts, NODA explicitly decouples the semigroup-based temporal evolution from the nonlinear spatial dynamics and inserts a measurement-driven correction at arbitrary time instants, bridging the gap between NO-based PDE solvers and classical data assimilation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NODA provides the first systematic NO-based framework that handles noisy, sparsely sampled measurements during test time, which is critical for practical applications like weather forecasting, remote sensing, and fMRI dynamics where perfect observations are unavailable. The consistent improvement with increasing measurement fraction α (Table 3: 26→9 RelMSE) and the modest computational overhead (1.5× MNO) make it a practical candidate for operational forecasting systems. Open questions include scaling to 3D PDEs, handling genuinely unknown or time-varying measurement operators, and connecting the correction step to principled uncertainty quantification. The framework is also limited to semilinear PDEs, excluding fully nonlinear spatiotemporal couplings.
