## Problem

Understanding the functional organization of higher visual cortex has relied heavily on hypothesis-driven, hand-selected stimuli, which risks biasing results toward pre-existing assumptions about regional selectivity. Prior brain-decoding approaches either reconstruct images/text from observed brain activity or generate activating images at the level of broad cortical regions, but none produce interpretable, voxel-level natural language descriptions of what maximally activates each individual voxel in a data-driven manner.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input the weight matrix of a trained linear probe (one row per voxel, dimension M×N where M is the CLIP embedding dimension and N is the number of voxels) and a reference set of 2 million natural images (LAION-A v2 + Open Images) used for projection. It outputs a natural language caption per voxel describing the visual stimulus that maximally activates that voxel, which can then be fed to a Stable Diffusion 2.1 text-to-image model to synthesize novel activating images. The captioning step is entirely training-free, operating on the encoder weights rather than on any new brain signal or image.

## Method

BrainSCUBA first trains a linear probe on a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 backbone to predict voxel-wise fMRI beta values (MSE loss, ~9000 NSD images per subject). Because the probe is linear over unit-norm CLIP embeddings, the embedding that maximally activates each voxel is analytically derived as the normalized weight vector W_i/||W_i||. Since this optimal embedding typically falls outside the manifold of natural image CLIP embeddings (a modality gap), the method projects it into the natural image space via a softmax-weighted sum over 2M reference images (temperature 1/150), which is equivalent to a single dot-product attention step with the weight as query and image embeddings as keys/values. The projected embedding is then passed to a frozen CLIPCap network (CLIP prefix + GPT-2) to generate the caption. This design makes the entire captioning pipeline training-free after the initial linear probe fit, and the decoupled projection of norm and direction separately improves alignment with the natural image embedding distribution.

## Results

On zero-shot CLIP 5-way semantic classification of generated images, BrainSCUBA achieves a mean of 74.6% (S2) and 72.0% (S5) category match, compared to BrainDiVE's 78.4% and 81.6%, while being ~10× faster per image (3s vs. 25–30s). BrainSCUBA's selectivity is noted to be closer to the true NSD top-1% ground truth (63.2%/61.4%) than BrainDiVE's, suggesting less overfitting to the encoder. In the EBA sub-clustering analysis, human evaluation confirms that Cluster-1 contains significantly more groups of people (88–91% vs. 4–13%) and fewer inanimate objects (11–15% vs. 65–81%) than Cluster-2, revealing fine-grained functional substructure within the body-selective area.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is BrainDiVE (Luo et al., 2023), the state-of-the-art gradient-based method for synthesizing activating images in higher visual cortex. BrainDiVE achieves higher mean semantic classification (78.4%/81.6% vs. 74.6%/72.0%), particularly on Words (61%/80% vs. 55%/34%) and Bodies (69%/77% vs. 54%/73%). However, BrainSCUBA matches or exceeds BrainDiVE on Food (97%/92% vs. 94%/87%) and Places (100%/99% vs. 100%/100%), and its overall selectivity profile is closer to the true NSD top-1% ground truth, suggesting less encoder overfitting. Critically, BrainSCUBA produces interpretable natural language captions (which BrainDiVE does not) and is ~10× faster per image (3s vs. 25–30s), since it avoids iterative gradient-based optimization. Visual inspection also shows BrainSCUBA images are more coherent with fewer artifacts, attributed to text conditioning versus raw gradient signals.

## Relation to Prior Work

BrainSCUBA sits at the intersection of fMRI encoding models and vision-language captioning. It builds on the CLIP-based fMRI encoder paradigm established by Conwell et al. (2023) and Wang et al. (2022), and repurposes the softmax-weighted-sum projection operator from Decap (Li et al., 2023b) to bridge the modality gap between encoder weights and natural image embeddings. The captioning step relies on CLIPCap (Mokady et al., 2021), a frozen GPT-2-based decoder conditioned on CLIP embeddings. The key departure from prior brain-conditioned generation work (BrainDiVE, NeuroGen) is the output modality: rather than synthesizing images via gradient-based optimization over a diffusion model, BrainSCUBA produces interpretable per-voxel text captions, and operates at voxel granularity rather than the broad-region level used by all prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method enables hypothesis-free, data-driven exploration of visual cortex selectivity at voxel resolution, moving beyond the hand-selected-stimulus paradigm that has dominated the field. The discovery of two stable sub-clusters within EBA (group-selective vs. individual-selective) is a genuinely new neuroscientific finding that could generate testable hypotheses. Limitations include dependence on the biases of the pre-trained captioning model and uncertainty about whether language can fully capture the selectivity of every voxel. Open questions include whether more powerful or unconstrained language models would improve caption quality, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the NSD dataset to other fMRI paradigms or species.
