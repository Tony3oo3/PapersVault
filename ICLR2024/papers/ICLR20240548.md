## Problem

Supervised-learning-based RL methods (OCBC, e.g., Decision Transformer, RvS) achieve strong offline RL results, but it remains unclear whether they can perform 'stitching'—recombining pieces of experience from different trajectories to solve state-goal pairs never seen together during training. This paper formalizes stitching as a form of combinatorial generalization and shows that the training distribution (mixture of context-conditioned policies) provably differs from the test distribution (behavioral-cloning policy), meaning SL-based methods have no theoretical guarantee of stitching even with infinite data.

## Input / Output

The policy π(a|s,g) takes a current state s (low-dimensional vector or top-down/first-person RGB image) and a goal state g (or target return r) as input, and outputs a single action a. During training, the goal is sampled from the same trajectory as the state-action pair (standard OCBC sampling); the proposed augmentation replaces this goal with one sampled from a *different* trajectory whose waypoint lies in the same k-means cluster as the original goal. The offline dataset consists of N trajectories collected by a mixture of context-conditioned policies, with contexts (e.g., different navigation regions) hidden from the learner.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a temporal data augmentation scheme that endows OCBC methods with stitching capability in approximately 5 lines of code. Before training, all states in the offline dataset are clustered using k-means (with L2 distance, even for image-based tasks). During training, with probability ε, the original goal g is replaced by an augmented goal g̃: a waypoint w is sampled from the same cluster as g, and g̃ is drawn from the future of w within w's trajectory. Theoretically, under Lipschitz smoothness of the discounted state occupancy, one application of this augmentation approximates the 1-step stitching policy distribution, which has strictly larger support than the training distribution. Empirically, adding this augmentation to DT and RvS improves combinatorial generalization by up to 2.5× on 10 state-based and image-based goal/return-conditioned tasks, while simply scaling data (10×) or model depth does not close the gap.

## Results

On 6 state-based maze tasks (point and ant mazes: umaze, medium, large), adding temporal data augmentation to RvS improves goal-reaching success rate on 5/6 tasks, with improvements of up to a factor of 2.5 over the unaugmented baseline. On 4 image-based tasks (3 goal-conditioned point mazes + 1 return-conditioned Miniworld 'collect' task), temporal augmentation improves RvS on 4/4 tasks and DT on 3/4 tasks. Ablations confirm that scaling DT's training data by 10× (to 10M transitions) or increasing transformer depth does not recover stitching ability, whereas even a single application of temporal augmentation does.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares two OCBC baselines — Decision Transformer (DT) and RvS — against their augmented counterparts (DT + temporal augmentation, RvS + temporal augmentation) on newly constructed stitching datasets. Both DT and RvS fail to perform combinatorial generalisation (stitching) on unseen (state, goal) pairs, even when trained on abundant data or with deeper transformer architectures. Adding temporal data augmentation to RvS recovers stitching on 5/6 state-based tasks and 4/4 image-based tasks, while for DT it helps on 3/4 image-based tasks. An oracle variant of the augmentation (using privileged x,y coordinates for k-means clustering) further improves RvS performance, confirming that the clustering step is a bottleneck in high-dimensional settings. The key finding is that neither more data (10×) nor larger models (more transformer layers) close the gap — only the explicit temporal augmentation does, validating the paper's theoretical claim that stitching requires a distributional shift beyond i.i.d. generalisation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the OCBC family of offline RL methods—primarily Decision Transformer (DT) and RvS—which treat RL as a supervised learning problem of mapping (state, goal) pairs to actions. It departs from prior claims (notably in the DT paper) that these SL-based methods already exhibit the 'stitching' property characteristic of temporal difference learning, by formally showing that stitching is a form of combinatorial generalization that OCBC methods lack by construction. The proposed remedy draws an explicit analogy to data augmentation in SL, but applies it in the temporal dimension: rather than augmenting spatial features, it augments the goal by sampling from overlapping trajectories, requiring only a state-space distance metric (k-means clustering). The paper also connects to the broader combinatorial generalization literature in language models and to the spurious correlations literature in SL, positioning stitching as a sequential-domain analogue of both.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that SL-based offline RL methods cannot perform stitching even in the limit of infinite data or model scale, which reframes a long-standing gap between TD and SL approaches as a generalization problem rather than a capacity or data problem. The practical implication is significant: a ~5-line temporal data augmentation step closes much of this gap across 10 environments, offering a simple path for practitioners using OCBC methods. However, the approach requires a state-space distance metric, which is non-trivial in high-dimensional or image-based settings, and the augmentation is not guaranteed to succeed in all cases. Broader implications extend to any time-series domain (audio, video, text) where combinatorial recombination of sequential segments could improve data efficiency.
