## Problem

Standard MLE training forces text generation models to assign non-zero probability to all training tokens regardless of quality, making them vulnerable to noisy web-crawled data. Existing robustness methods (Loss Truncation, TaiLr) estimate data quality solely from the predicted probability of the target token, ignoring the distribution over non-target tokens. This causes misjudgment: high-entropy contexts with many valid continuations and early-training iterations where the model is still incompetent are incorrectly flagged as noisy, while truly erroneous tokens with concentrated probability on a wrong token are not reliably distinguished.

## Input / Output

ENT operates at the individual token level during autoregressive training, computing a per-token error norm from the model's full vocabulary-size probability vector at each decoding step. Rather than pruning entire sentences (as Loss Truncation does), it selectively removes individual tokens whose error norm exceeds a threshold, preserving the remaining tokens in the same sequence. The method is architecture-agnostic and applies to standard transformer-based generation: GPT-2-large for language modeling, Transformer (fairseq) for multilingual MT, and T5-small/BART-base for summarization fine-tuning.

## Method

ENT replaces the negative log-likelihood quality signal with the L2 norm of the difference between the model's predicted probability distribution and the one-hot ground truth vector at each token position. Tokens whose error norm exceeds a fixed threshold (ENT-Threshold) or the top fraction of tokens in a batch (ENT-Fraction) have their loss zeroed out via an indicator function. The key insight is that the L2 error norm naturally encodes two properties missing from loss-based methods: during early training all error norms are near 1 (uniform distribution), so no truncation occurs until the model has sufficient competence; and for high-entropy contexts where probability mass is spread across many valid synonyms, the L2 norm remains moderate, whereas for truly noisy tokens where probability is concentrated on a single wrong token, the L2 norm spikes. Theoretically, the L2 error norm is a lower bound of the token-level Total Variation Distance estimator (via Pinsker's inequality), making it a tighter and more robust noise indicator than KL-divergence (i.e., standard loss), which is a loose upper bound on TVD.

## Results

On WikiText-103 language modeling, ENT-Threshold reduces validation perplexity to 24.30, a 1.58-point improvement over the MLE baseline (25.88). On multilingual machine translation (opus-100, 8 directions), ENT-Threshold achieves 30.6 average BLEU versus 30.1 for MLE. Under 50% untranslated-text noise injection in En-Fr translation, ENT-Fraction scores 32.4 BLEU, outperforming MLE (28.6) by 3.8 BLEU points. On CNN/Daily Mail summarization with T5-small, ENT-Fraction achieves R-1 42.63 / R-2 19.98 / R-L 39.57, surpassing MLE (42.19 / 19.69 / 39.04).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ENT consistently outperforms both Loss Truncation (Kang & Hashimoto, 2020) and TaiLr (Ji et al., 2023) across all three task settings. In multilingual MT, ENT-Threshold beats Loss Truncation in 7 out of 8 directions (e.g., +2.1 BLEU on En-Es, +0.8 on En-Ru) and matches or exceeds TaiLr in 6 of 8 directions. In language modeling, ENT-Fraction (24.50 PPL) improves over Loss Truncation (24.64) and TaiLr (25.62) by 0.14 and 1.12 perplexity points respectively. Under 50% untranslated-text noise, ENT-Fraction (32.4 BLEU) outperforms Loss Truncation (29.4) by 3.0 BLEU and TaiLr (30.3) by 2.1 BLEU. On CNN/Daily Mail summarization with T5-small, ENT-Fraction surpasses both baselines on all three Rouge metrics, while on BART-base it matches or slightly trails the MLE baseline, suggesting the gains are most pronounced when the pre-trained model can reliably estimate data quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data truncation paradigm for robust text generation, building directly on Loss Truncation (Kang & Hashimoto, 2020) by replacing the loss-based quality metric with the ℓ₂ error norm between the predicted distribution and the one-hot ground truth. The key conceptual departure is that prior methods (Loss Truncation, TaiLr) estimate data quality solely from the target token's predicted probability, ignoring the skewness of the non-target token distribution; ENT captures this full distributional information. Theoretically, the approach is grounded in Pinsker's inequality, showing the ℓ₂ error norm is a lower bound of the token-level Total Variation Distance estimator, connecting it to the TVD-based reweighting framework of Ji et al. (2023) and the gradient-norm approximation from dataset pruning (Paul et al., 2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As web-crawled corpora become the primary training data for large language models, robust training against natural and adversarial noise is increasingly critical. ENT offers a simple, low-variance quality signal that requires minimal hyperparameter tuning compared to loss-based or probability-based methods, making it more scalable to large-scale pretraining where combinatorial hyperparameter search is infeasible. The authors acknowledge that gains are driven by the noisy distribution of training data, so improvements on clean, curated corpora may be smaller. Open questions include extending the token-level truncation to coarser-grained (sentence or document-level) data quality estimation and understanding how the method interacts with other robustness techniques like unlikelihood training or self-correction.
