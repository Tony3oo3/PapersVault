## Problem

State-of-the-art time series models require millions of parameters, making them unsuitable for resource-constrained edge devices. While frequency-domain representations offer compactness, prior work has not exploited the complex-valued structure (simultaneous amplitude and phase encoding) to perform time series tasks as simple interpolation operations, leaving a gap between the theoretical elegance of Fourier analysis and practical lightweight modeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241040/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For forecasting, FITS takes a raw time series look-back window of length L and produces an extended segment of length L+H (look-back plus forecasting horizon), supervised jointly on both the backcast and forecast portions. For anomaly detection, it takes a downsampled segment (downsampled by a factor of 4 via equidistant sampling) and reconstructs the full-length original. In both cases, the input is first normalized via reversible instance normalization (RIN) to zero-mean before rFFT projection, and the output is mapped back to the time domain via irFFT followed by inverse RIN, so supervision is always in the original time domain.

## Method

FITS reformulates forecasting and reconstruction as frequency-domain interpolation: a single complex-valued linear layer learns to scale amplitudes and shift phases simultaneously by multiplying complex numbers, effectively extending or up-sampling the rFFT representation of the input segment. The pipeline is RIN → rFFT → low-pass filter (cutoff aligned to a harmonic of the dominant frequency) → complex linear interpolation at rate η → zero-padding → irFFT → inverse RIN. The interpolation rate η = 1 + H/L for forecasting and η = 4 for reconstruction controls the output length, while the LPF discards high-frequency noise to keep the parameter count under 10k. Weight sharing across channels and the harmonic-based cutoff selection further reduce model size without sacrificing the periodic structure that dominates most real-world time series.

## Results

On the ETT forecasting benchmarks (MSE, lower is better), FITS achieves 0.372/0.404/0.427/0.424 on ETTh1 (horizons 96/192/336/720), outperforming PatchTST (0.385/0.413/0.440/0.456) and DLinear (0.384/0.443/0.446/0.504). On Weather, FITS reaches 0.143/0.186/0.236/0.307, beating all baselines. Critically, FITS achieves these results with only 4.5K–10K trainable parameters and 1.6M–8.9M MACs, compared to 139.7K params for DLinear, 1.5M for PatchTST, and 301.7M for TimesNet, with sub-millisecond inference (0.6 ms GPU). For anomaly detection, FITS achieves near-perfect F1-scores of 99.95% on SMD and 98.9% on SWaT, but underperforms on SMAP (70.74%) and MSL (78.12%) where specialized detectors reach 96.69% and 94.08% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FITS (4.5K–10K params) outperforms PatchTST (1.5M params) on ETTh1, ETTh2, ETTm2, and Weather across all horizons, with improvements of 0.003–0.032 in MSE. Against DLinear (139.7K params), FITS wins on ETTh1 (e.g., 0.424 vs 0.504 at horizon 720) and ETTh2 (0.377 vs 0.588), but is slightly behind on ETTm1 (0.303 vs 0.292 at horizon 96) and Electricity horizon 96 (0.134 vs 0.129). FITS trails PatchTST on Traffic (0.385 vs 0.366 at horizon 96) and ETTm1, where the multi-channel diversity and distribution shift challenge the frequency-interpolation approach. For anomaly detection, FITS dominates SMD (99.95% F1 vs 92.33% Anomaly Transformer) and SWaT (98.9% vs 94.07%), but falls well short on SMAP (70.74% vs 96.69% Anomaly Transformer) and MSL (78.12% vs 94.08% DGHL), which the authors attribute to the binary-event nature of those datasets being poorly suited to frequency-domain reconstruction. Overall, FITS matches or beats SOTA forecasting models while being 50× smaller than DLinear and ~10,000× smaller than TimesNet, at the cost of weaker performance on heterogeneous or event-driven data.

## Relation to Prior Work

FITS carves new ground by reframing time series forecasting and reconstruction as interpolation in the complex frequency domain, rather than the feature-engineering approach taken by prior frequency-aware models (FEDformer, FiLM, TimesNet) that only exploit amplitude/energy information. The key conceptual departure is the use of a single complex-valued linear layer to jointly learn amplitude scaling and phase shifting via complex multiplication, which prior work had not exploited for temporal feature extraction. The paper positions itself against both heavyweight transformer-based models (PatchTST, TimesNet, FEDformer) and the lightweight temporal linear model DLinear, arguing that the frequency domain's compactness—capturing both amplitude and phase in a single complex number per frequency—enables a fundamentally more parameter-efficient formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for edge deployment: achieving SOTA or near-SOTA forecasting with ~10k parameters (50× smaller than DLinear, ~10,000× smaller than mainstream models) opens the door to on-device training and inference on MCUs. However, the approach has clear limitations: it underperforms on binary event data (SMAP, MSL) where frequency representation is less informative, and short-term forecasting on diverse datasets (M4) remains suboptimal due to the short look-back windows. Open questions include whether the frequency-interpolation framing generalizes to non-periodic or highly irregular time series, and whether scaling to complex-valued deeper architectures (e.g., complex-valued Transformers) can close the remaining gaps.
