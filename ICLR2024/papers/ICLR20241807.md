## Problem

Rate-encoded spiking neural networks have been empirically observed to be more adversarially robust than constant-encoded SNNs and even ANNs, but no theoretical explanation or provable guarantee existed for this phenomenon. This paper provides the first certified robustness result for SNNs by establishing a formal connection between rate-encoding and randomized smoothing, and further improves empirical robustness through adversarial training of the smooth classifier.

## Input / Output

The method takes static RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100 and SVHN, 224×224 for ImageNet-100) and rate-encodes each pixel x_i ∈ [0,1] into T independent Bernoulli spike sequences with bias x_i, producing a temporal binary input of shape T×d. At test time, the smooth classifier is estimated via m Monte Carlo evaluations of the rate-encoded network, yielding both a predicted class label and a certified l1-norm radius r = (p_a − 0.5)/(T·3^(T−1)) within which no adversarial perturbation can flip the prediction.

## Method

The key insight is that rate-encoding with T Bernoulli variables per pixel is mathematically equivalent to randomized smoothing with multi-Bernoulli noise, so the rate-encoded SNN acts as an estimator of a smooth classifier g_T(x) = E_{z~Ber^T(x)}[f̃_T(z)]. The paper proves g_T is T·3^(T−1)-Lipschitz under l1-norm (Lemma 4), yielding a certified radius of (p_a − p_b)/(2T·3^(T−1)) that shrinks with latency T, confirming empirical observations. For adversarial training, the Straight-Through Estimator is used to backpropagate gradients through the stochastic rate-encoding node, enabling FGSM and PGD attacks on the smooth classifier with m=1 for computational efficiency. Prediction and certification follow the Cohen et al. (2019) framework with binomial confidence bounds, replacing Gaussian noise with multi-Bernoulli noise in the Monte Carlo estimation.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (T=4, m=10), rate-encoded CLEAN(R) achieves a minimum robust accuracy of 37.74% under the strongest attack (pgd(R)), compared to just 0.1% for constant-encoded CLEAN(C) and 0.49% for CLEAN(C)+RAT. Adversarially trained rate-encoded models further improve: PGD(R) reaches 53.08% minimum robust accuracy, surpassing PGD(C)+RAT (44.44%) by 8.6 points. On CIFAR-100, the same trend holds with PGD(R) at 30.03% vs. PGD(C)+RAT at 29.89%. Certified test accuracy under l₁-norm (Table 3) reaches 73.23% at radius 0.1/108 and 38.78% at radius 0.45/108 on CIFAR-10 for the PGD(R) model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is SNN-RAT (Ding et al., 2022), the state-of-the-art adversarial training method for constant-encoded SNNs. On CIFAR-10, the proposed rate-encoded PGD(R) model (m=10) achieves 53.08% minimum robust accuracy, outperforming PGD(C)+RAT (44.44%) by 8.6 points, while FGSM(R) (52.27%) beats FGSM(C)+RAT (42.76%) by 9.5 points. Even without adversarial training, CLEAN(R) at m=10 (37.74%) vastly exceeds CLEAN(C)+RAT (0.49%), confirming that rate-encoding's inherent Bernoulli stochasticity provides substantial free robustness. On CIFAR-100, the gap narrows: PGD(R) (31.05%) roughly matches PGD(C)+RAT (29.89%), while FGSM(R) (33.46%) leads FGSM(C)+RAT (27.10%) by 6.4 points. The paper also notes that constant-encoded models suffer catastrophic accuracy drops (near 0%) under pgd(C) attacks, whereas rate-encoded models maintain 37–53% accuracy under their corresponding strongest attacks, demonstrating a fundamental robustness advantage of the rate-encoding paradigm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper establishes a conceptual bridge between rate-encoding in spiking neural networks and the randomized smoothing framework (Cohen et al., 2019), showing that a rate-encoded SNN is precisely a smooth classifier under multi-Bernoulli noise. Prior SNN adversarial robustness work (Sharmin et al., 2020; Kundu et al., 2021; Ding et al., 2022) was purely empirical, observing that rate-encoded networks resist attacks but offering no formal guarantee. The adversarial training component follows the smoothed-classifier training paradigm of Salman et al. (2019), adapted here with the Straight-Through Estimator to back-propagate through the stochastic Bernoulli encoding layer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provable robustness certificate for SNNs, providing a theoretical explanation for why rate-encoding outperforms constant encoding under adversarial attacks (smooth classifier vs. base classifier). However, the certified radius is tightly bounded by (p_a − 0.5)/(T·3^(T−1)), which at T=4 yields radii around 0.004 in l₁-norm — far smaller than the empirical robustness observed, leaving a large gap between theory and practice. Open questions include whether tighter Lipschitz bounds can be derived, whether the framework extends to l₂ or l∞ norms, and whether the certified guarantee remains meaningful at the higher latencies (T=32–128) needed for practical SNN deployment.
