## Problem

Existing LLM-based embodied agents translate the entire open world into text, rendering interaction akin to 'a blindfolded text-based game.' This text-only I/O makes agent responses uncontrollable (dependent on fragile prompt engineering) and prevents intuitive visual feedback that humans naturally expect. No prior work trains a large multimodal model to natively perceive and generate visual content for open-world embodied agents.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a single Minecraft snapshot (RGB image) alongside a text instruction or question, with the image encoded into a sequence of visual tokens via a VQ-GAN or CLIP encoder. Outputs are autoregressive sequences over a unified multimodal codebook (8192 visual tokens + 32000 language tokens + 2 special boundary tokens <vis>/</vis>), meaning the model can interleave generated visual tokens (decoded back to images) with text in a single response. For skill planning, the input is a snapshot pair {I₀, I_t} plus task context, and the output is a ranked list of executable high-level skills.

## Method

Steve-Eye couples a frozen visual encoder (CLIP for perception, VQ-GAN for generation) with a LLaMA-2 backbone via a lightweight linear projection module that maps visual token embeddings into the text embedding space. The visual codebook is merged into the language vocabulary to form a unified multimodal codebook, enabling the decoder-only transformer to autoregressively predict both text and visual tokens. Training follows a two-stage instruction-tuning strategy: first, only the projection module and token embeddings are trained on 600K snapshot-text pairs to align modalities; second, the projection module and LLM are jointly fine-tuned (with LoRA) on the full 850K instruction dataset spanning three functions—multimodal perception (200K), foundational knowledge (250K), and skill prediction/planning (400K). The dataset is curated semi-automatically using ChatGPT to generate diverse instruction templates and question-answer pairs from Minecraft snapshots, Wiki pages, and skill trajectories, with loss computed only on answer tokens.

## Results

On the FK-QA benchmark, Steve-Eye-13b achieves a scoring of 7.68/10 and 44.36% accuracy, surpassing Llama-2-70b (7.04, 38.27%) and GPT-Turbo-3.5 (7.42, 41.78%) despite being a much smaller model. On ENV-VC, Steve-Eye-13b with CLIP encoder reaches 92.5% inventory, 82.8% equipment, and 92.1% object-in-sight accuracy, substantially outperforming BLIP-2 (41.6%, 58.5%, 64.7%). On the Skill Planning benchmark (24 tasks × 30 episodes), Steve-Eye achieves mean success rates of 0.40–0.53 on tree-cutting tasks and 0.23–0.47 on mob-interaction tasks, significantly exceeding MineAgent (0.00–0.50) and GPT Assistant (0.00–0.76).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Steve-Eye-13b outperforms BLIP-2 by large margins on ENV-VC (e.g., +50.9% on inventory, +24.3% on equipment) and on Skill Prediction (84.2% vs. 58.8% recall for 'running' status), attributed to the stronger LLM backbone and instruction tuning. Against Llama-2 (7b/13b/70b), Steve-Eye-13b achieves +1.06 scoring and +6.09% accuracy on FK-QA TEXT, showing that domain-specific multimodal instruction tuning compensates for the smaller model size. Steve-Eye also surpasses GPT-Turbo-3.5 on FK-QA (7.68 vs. 7.42 scoring; 44.36% vs. 41.78% accuracy), demonstrating that the curated Minecraft knowledge base is more effective than general-purpose LLM knowledge for domain-specific questions. On Skill Planning, Steve-Eye significantly outperforms MineAgent (which relies on static RL policies) across all 24 tasks, with the largest gaps on long-horizon tasks (e.g., 0.47 vs. 0.00 on 'cooked beef'). Steve-Eye also beats the GPT Assistant baseline (ChatGPT-based planner) on most tasks, confirming that visual perception provides advantages over text-only prompting even with a lighter-weight LLM. In the appendix, Steve-Eye is shown to be competitive with Plan4MC (which uses a skill graph for 100% executable plans and GPT-4), despite using the much smaller LLaMA-2 backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Visual Instruction Tuning paradigm (Liu et al., 2023) to the embodied-agent domain, adapting its two-stage alignment-then-instruction-tuning strategy to a Minecraft-specific setting. It departs from prior LLM-based embodied agents (Voyager, GITM, Plan4MC, DEPS) that either rely entirely on text descriptions of the environment or use visual input only for skill execution while keeping planning text-only. The key conceptual shift is treating the embodied agent as a large multimodal model with unified visual-text I/O, rather than a text-only LLM augmented with separate perception modules, and grounding this in a purpose-built 850K instruction dataset rather than unsupervised video pretraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The three open-world benchmarks (ENV-VC, FK-QA, SPP) and the 850K instruction dataset provide a reusable foundation for evaluating multimodal embodied agents beyond Minecraft, and the finding that a general-purpose CLIP encoder substantially outperforms the domain-specific MineCLIP on fine-grained perception challenges the assumption that domain-specific pretraining is always advantageous. However, skill prediction remains unreliable enough to preclude replacing rule-based game judgment, the approach is validated only in Minecraft's pixel-art visual style, and the paper explicitly defers exploration of more generic environments to future work. Open questions include whether the multimodal I/O advantage persists in photorealistic or 3D-continuous environments and whether the instruction-tuning recipe generalizes without the Minecraft-specific data pipeline.
