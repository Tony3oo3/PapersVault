## Problem

Post-training pruning of large language models has been limited by inaccurate loss-landscape approximations: prior methods like SparseGPT and OBC model only a pruned layer's output reconstruction error, ignoring gradients that connect local removal costs to the true target loss, and they do not extend to structured pruning (removing entire rows and columns) which yields the most direct memory and compute savings. This paper addresses both gaps by scaling Kronecker-factored curvature approximations to LLMs and deriving a unified framework for structured, semi-structured, and unstructured pruning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pretrained LLM weights (OPT-125M through Llama-2-7B), 128 calibration sequences of 2048 tokens from wikitext-2, and a target size α (fraction of weights to retain). It produces a directly deployable compressed model where structured pruning physically reduces matrix dimensions, semi-structured pruning enforces an M:N zero pattern, and unstructured pruning zeros individual elements. Compression is performed in multiple shots (up to 40), with curvature re-estimated and weights updated between shots, so the output is a dense smaller model rather than a sparse mask requiring specialized hardware.

## Method

LLM Surgeon approximates the empirical Fisher per layer as a Kronecker product G⊗A of gradient and activation second-moment matrices, enabling closed-form pruning costs and weight updates without ever materializing the full RC×RC curvature. Unlike prior LLM pruning, it uses backward-pass gradients to tie removal costs to the true loss, and it computes correlated (joint) weight updates for multiple pruned weights simultaneously rather than summing independent updates. For structured pruning, the Kronecker structure means removing R′ rows requires inverting only an R′×R′ matrix rather than an R′C×R′C matrix, making multi-row/column updates tractable. A global threshold dynamically allocates sparsity across layers (pruning more where it hurts less), and a multi-shot schedule with optional interleaved LoRA first-order corrections mitigates the local validity of the quadratic approximation at high sparsity.

## Results

On structured pruning of OPT and Llama-v2 models evaluated on wikitext-2 perplexity (PPL), LLM Surgeon with full correlated updates achieves near-baseline performance at 90% target size (e.g., 28.01 vs. 27.65 PPL for OPT-125m; 5.25 vs. 5.12 for Llama-v2-7B) and maintains reasonable quality down to 50% (49.78 for OPT-125m, 15.38 for Llama-v2-7B). For 2:4 semi-structured pruning at 50% sparsity, LLM Surgeon outperforms SparseGPT across all OPT sizes (e.g., 25.10 vs. 29.44 PPL on OPT-1.3b). For unstructured pruning at 90% sparsity, LLM Surgeon matches or slightly beats SparseGPT (e.g., 5.13 vs. 5.49 PPL on Llama-v2-7B), and at 50% sparsity achieves 6.08 vs. 6.51 PPL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLM Surgeon outperforms all baselines across structured, semi-structured, and unstructured pruning. In structured pruning (Table 1), it beats K-OBD (the strongest prior method using Kronecker-factored curvature) at every sparsity level, with the gap widening at higher compression (e.g., 31.82 vs. 36.54 PPL at 70% on OPT-125m; 7.83 vs. 15.43 on Llama-v2-7B). Magnitude pruning and L-OBD fail catastrophically for structured pruning (PPL > 700 at 90% on OPT-125m). In semi-structured 2:4 pruning (Table 3), LLM Surgeon outperforms SparseGPT on all four OPT models at 50% sparsity, with the largest margin on OPT-1.3b (25.10 vs. 29.44 PPL). In unstructured pruning (Table 4), LLM Surgeon with multi-shot and full correlated updates matches or slightly beats SparseGPT at 90% (5.13 vs. 5.49 PPL on Llama-v2-7B) and clearly wins at 50% (6.08 vs. 6.51 PPL). The paper also notes that concurrent work SliceGPT (Ashkboos et al., 2024) performs structured pruning but ignores gradient information, resulting in lower final performance than LLM Surgeon.

## Relation to Prior Work

LLM Surgeon extends the Optimal Brain Surgeon (OBS) lineage of second-order pruning from the early 1990s to large language models by scaling Kronecker-factored Fisher approximations (KFAC) to billion-parameter architectures, building directly on Eigendamage's structured pruning framework for CNNs. It departs from concurrent LLM pruning work (SparseGPT, Optimal BERT Surgeon) in two key ways: it uses gradient information from backward passes to relate removal costs to the true target loss rather than layer-wise reconstruction error, and it derives correlated (rather than independent) weight updates that account for off-diagonal Fisher structure. The multi-shot schedule with curvature re-estimation and optional LoRA-based first-order corrections further distinguish it from one-shot approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to make structured pruning of LLMs practically viable, enabling 20–30% model size reduction with minimal perplexity degradation without retraining a smaller model from scratch — a significant practical win for deployment under memory and compute constraints. However, the evaluation is limited to wikitext-2 and a handful of model families (OPT, Llama-v2), and 50% structured compression still underperforms a natively smaller model. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other architectures (MoE, Mamba), whether the multi-shot compute overhead is acceptable in practice, and whether the LoRA correction's failure on larger models reflects a fundamental limitation or simply insufficient calibration data.
