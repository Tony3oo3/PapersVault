## Problem

Existing Contrastive Analysis methods rely on Variational Auto-Encoders, which are known to produce trivial or semantically uninformative latent representations, especially when decoders are powerful. Meanwhile, Contrastive Representation Learning has demonstrated far superior ability to learn semantically expressive features, yet has not been adapted to the CA task of separating common (shared) from salient (target-specific) generative factors between two populations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241062/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two image datasets—a background set X (e.g., healthy subjects) and a target set Y (e.g., diseased subjects)—along with stochastic data augmentations that generate multiple views per sample. It produces two latent representations per input: a common vector c on a unit hypersphere (capturing shared patterns) and a salient vector s in Euclidean space (capturing target-specific patterns), where background samples are constrained to map to a fixed information-less vector s′ in the salient space. When fine-grained target attributes are available, the salient encoder outputs exactly D_S dimensions, one per attribute, enabling supervised disentanglement.

## Method

SepCLR reformulates Contrastive Analysis as a constrained InfoMax problem with three Mutual Information terms: maximizing I(x;c)+I(y;c) for the common space, maximizing I(y;s) for the salient space, and enforcing I(c;s)=0 for independence. The common and salient MI terms are estimated via alignment (re-substitution KDE over augmented views) and uniformity (nonparametric KDE over the batch) losses, generalizing the Wang & Isola framework to a multi-view, two-dataset setting. A novel background-contrasting uniformity term (s′-uniformity) repels target embeddings from the information-less vector s′ while maintaining inter-target spread, and a β-weighted L2 penalty enforces the information-less hypothesis on background salient embeddings. To enforce I(c;s)=0 without trivial collapse, the authors propose k-JEM, a kernel-based joint entropy maximization that estimates H(c,s) via product-of-Gaussians KDE over paired (c,s) samples, requiring no distributional assumptions or auxiliary neural networks.

## Results

On the Digits-on-CIFAR-10 benchmark, SepCLR-K-JEM achieves a total separation error (δ_tot) of 32.0, substantially outperforming the best VAE baseline SepVAE (81.2) and cVAE (90.2), while capturing 96.2% B-ACC for digits in the salient space and 73.2% for objects in the common space. On CelebA accessories, SepCLR-K-JEM reaches δ_tot=41.16 versus 80.82 for MM-cVAE, correctly placing glasses/hats discrimination in the salient space (98.57% B-ACC) and sex in the common space (78.00%). On the schizophrenia neuroimaging task, SepCLR-k-JEM achieves the best salient-space MAE for SANS (3.74) and SAPS (2.52) clinical scales and the highest diagnosis B-ACC in the salient space (79.90%), while keeping demographic variables (age, sex, site) in the common space.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SepCLR-K-JEM outperforms all four VAE-based baselines (cVAE, ConVAE, MM-cVAE, SepVAE) across every evaluated dataset. On Digits/CIFAR-10, the total separation error drops from 81.2 (SepVAE, best baseline) to 32.0, a 60% relative reduction, primarily driven by a large gain in common-space object capture (73.2% vs. 36.6% B-ACC). On CelebA, SepCLR-K-JEM achieves 98.57% B-ACC for glasses/hats in the salient space versus 84.60% for MM-cVAE, while also improving sex classification in the common space (78.00% vs. 61.57%). On the schizophrenia MRI task, SepCLR-k-JEM attains the lowest SANS MAE (3.74 vs. 3.95 for ConVAE) and SAPS MAE (2.52 vs. 2.60 for SepVAE) in the salient space, and the highest diagnosis B-ACC (79.90% vs. 79.15% for SepVAE). On CheXpert and ODIR subtyping, SepCLR-k-JEM leads in salient-space subtype B-ACC (61.30% and 68.54% respectively) while keeping sex information in the common space, confirming consistent separation quality across both natural and medical imaging domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

SepCLR replaces the VAE-based Contrastive Analysis framework (cVAE, conVAE, MM-cVAE, SepVAE) with a contrastive learning formulation grounded in the InfoMax principle. It directly builds on the alignment/uniformity decomposition of Wang & Isola (2020) and Rodríguez-Gálvez et al. (2023) to derive InfoNCE-style losses for the common and salient spaces, and on Dufumier et al. (2021) for the supervised attribute-disentanglement extension. The key departure is the k-JEM (kernel-based Joint Entropy Maximization) strategy for enforcing common-salient independence, which avoids the trivial-solution pitfall of standard MI minimization methods (vCLUB, vL1out, vUB, TC) by simultaneously maximizing H(c,s), H(c), and H(s) rather than minimizing I(c;s).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-recognized weakness of VAE-based CA methods—poor semantic expressiveness of latent representations—by leveraging the proven representational power of contrastive learning, with consistent gains across six datasets spanning toy, natural, and medical imaging. The k-JEM independence constraint is a practically useful alternative to MI minimization that requires no auxiliary network or distributional assumptions. However, the authors acknowledge that identifiability of the true generative factors is not guaranteed (a limitation shared by all CA methods), and that adding a reconstruction loss degrades performance, leaving open the question of how to combine generative capability with the contrastive encoder's representational quality.
