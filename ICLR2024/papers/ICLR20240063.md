## Problem

Existing non-convex Online-Within-Online (OWO) meta learning regret bounds (Balcan et al., 2021) suffer from two limitations: for any fixed number of tasks T, the task-averaged regret does not vanish as the per-task iteration count m grows, and the convergence rate with respect to T is slow (involving T^{-1/4} and (log m)log T / √T terms). This paper addresses both issues by providing sharper regret bounds that guarantee vanishing regret in m and faster convergence in T, while also extending the analysis to bounded (non-Lipschitz) loss functions.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives T sequential tasks, each consisting of m adversarially chosen loss functions ℓ_ti: Θ → [0,M] over a compact action space Θ ⊂ ℝ^d. At each round it maintains a probability distribution ρ_ti over Θ (not a point estimate) and a scalar step size λ_t, both updated via meta-learning from prior tasks. The theoretical outputs are task-averaged regret upper bounds of the form O((Bound_I + Bound_II + V)√m + g(m)), where V is a task-similarity measure, plus derived transfer risk and PAC-Bayes generalization bounds for batch/statistical meta learning.

## Method

The core framework decomposes the EWA regret bound U_t(ρ,v) = (v + f_t(ρ)/v)√(mb) + g(m) into two online sub-problems: learning the initialization distribution ρ_t1 over functions {f_t(ρ) = V(ρ,ρ_t*)²} and learning the step size v_t over functions {h_t(v) = v + f_t(ρ_t1)/v}. For piecewise Lipschitz functions, the initialization is learned via FTRL with KL regularization on a discretized simplex (converting the distribution optimization into a tractable convex problem), yielding O(T^{1/2+α}) regret; for non-Lipschitz functions, FTL is used directly on {KL(ρ_t*‖ρ)} with the analytic solution ρ_t1 = (1/(t-1))Σρ_s* (via Frigyik et al.'s Bregman minimizer lemma) and Radon-Nikodym derivative properties to obtain O(log T) regret. The step size is learned in both cases via FTL with primal-dual analysis (Shalev-Shwartz & Kakade, 2008), exploiting the strong convexity of h_t(v) when f_t(ρ_t1) ≥ B² > 0 to achieve O((log T)^{9/2}/T) or O(log T/T) regret respectively. The key technical novelty is the fine-grained lower-bound estimation of B² that guarantees the strong-convexity condition, enabling logarithmic (rather than √T) regret for step-size learning.

## Results

The paper provides two improved task-averaged regret bounds for non-convex OWO meta learning. For bounded piecewise Lipschitz functions (Theorem 2), the regret improves from $O((\frac{\sqrt{m}}{T^{1/4}} + \frac{(\log m)\log T}{\sqrt{T}} + V)\sqrt{m})$ to $O((\frac{1}{T^{1/2-\alpha}} + \frac{(\log T)^{9/2}}{T} + V)\sqrt{m})$ for any $\alpha \in (0, 1/2)$, guaranteeing a vanishing regret w.r.t. $m$ for any fixed $T$. For bounded (not necessarily Lipschitz) functions (Theorem 3), a new algorithm achieves the sharper bound $O((\frac{\log T}{T} + V)\sqrt{m})$, which is the fastest convergence rate w.r.t. $T$ among all existing results. Additionally, a novel PAC-Bayes generalization bound of $O(V/\sqrt{m} + 1/\sqrt{Tm})$ is derived for statistical multi-task learning via the regret analysis.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Balcan et al. (2021) under the same bounded piecewise Lipschitz assumptions, the paper's Theorem 2 improves the initialization regret from $O(m^{d/2}/T^{1/4})$ to $O(1/T^{1/2-\alpha})$ (eliminating the dependence on dimension $d$ and $m$) and the step-size regret from $O((\log m)\log T/\sqrt{T})$ to $O((\log T)^{9/2}/T)$, yielding a faster convergence rate w.r.t. $T$ and a vanishing regret w.r.t. $m$ for any fixed $T$. Compared to Khodak et al. (2019) in the convex Lipschitz setting, the paper's Theorem 3 matches the initialization regret of $O(\log T/T)$ but improves the step-size regret from $O(\log T/\sqrt{T})$ to $O(\log T/T)$, and extends the result to bounded non-Lipschitz functions without requiring convexity. The key technical improvements come from using FTL with primal-dual analysis (Shalev-Shwartz & Kakade, 2008) for the step size and exploiting the analytic form of the FTL solution via Radon-Nikodym derivatives for the initialization in the non-Lipschitz case.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the non-convex OWO meta learning framework of Balcan et al. (2021), which analyzed the EWA algorithm under piecewise Lipschitz assumptions and achieved a task-averaged regret of O((√m/T^{1/4} + (log m)log T/√T + V)√m). The key methodological departure is the use of FTL over KL-divergence functionals (leveraging the Frigyik et al. 2008 minimizer-of-expected-Bregman-divergence lemma) to obtain logarithmic regret for initialization learning in the non-Lipschitz case, combined with primal-dual analysis (Shalev-Shwartz & Kakade, 2008) for logarithmic step-size regret. The paper also draws on the EWA regret bound from Alquier (2021) as the single-task foundation and connects to the PAC-Bayes meta learning literature (Guan & Lu, 2022) to derive a novel multi-task generalization bound via regret analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The improved regret bounds (from O(√m/T^{1/4}) to O(log T/T) for initialization learning) resolve two limitations of prior work: they guarantee vanishing task-averaged regret in m for fixed T, and achieve faster convergence in T. The PAC-Bayes bound for statistical multi-task learning (Theorem 5) is the first to impose a per-task data distribution assumption and connect multi-task generalization error directly to OWO regret, opening a pathway for proving generalization guarantees via online learning analysis. However, the results are purely theoretical with no empirical validation, and the non-Lipschitz bound (Theorem 3) requires the non-vacuous condition B > 0 on KL-divergences, which may not hold when optimal actions differ across tasks (Remark B.1). Open questions include whether the O(log T/T) rate is minimax-optimal and whether the framework extends to other online algorithms like Follow-The-Perturbed-Leader.
