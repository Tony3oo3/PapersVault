## Problem

Despite Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) demonstrating superior performance over Full Finetuning (FT) in image recognition, the conditions under which VPT is preferable (the 'when') and the mechanisms responsible for its success (the 'why') remain unexplored. No prior work has systematically identified which transfer learning scenarios favor VPT or tested competing hypotheses—overfitting, optimization via extra dimensions, or feature preservation—for its advantage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240708/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The study feeds standard RGB images into a ViT-B/16 backbone pretrained on ImageNet-21k and produces class labels across 19 VTAB-1k tasks (Natural, Specialized, Structured). The analytical framework treats the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) between pretraining and downstream distributions and the task objective disparity (e.g., classification vs. counting) as the key scenario-defining inputs, while the output is comparative accuracy under varying training set sizes (400 to 20,000 samples) and one-shot settings, with the percentage of tuned parameters (typically <1%) reported alongside.

## Method

This empirical study categorizes transfer learning into four quadrants by task disparity and data distribution similarity, finding VPT wins in 3 of 4 quadrants under limited data. To isolate the 'why,' the authors introduce two ablation variants: Mixed (prompts added while also finetuning all backbone parameters) and FT-then-PT (full finetuning followed by prompt tuning), which decompose the effects of extra optimization dimensions versus frozen-feature preservation. They show overfitting explains VPT's edge only in high-disparity Structured tasks, while the Mixed and FT-then-PT results rule out the local-minima-escape hypothesis and confirm that VPT's unique mechanism—keeping the backbone frozen while prepending learnable prompt vectors to each transformer layer's input sequence—preserves pretrained features while encoding task-specific information. GradCAM and Integrated Gradients visualizations further demonstrate that prompts direct attention to task-relevant image regions more effectively than full parameter updates.

## Results

On the VTAB-1k benchmark (19 tasks, ViT-B/16 pretrained on ImageNet-21k, 800 training samples), VPT outperforms full finetuning in 16 of 19 tasks. Mean accuracy: VPT achieves 79.53% on Natural (6/7 wins), 82.65% on Specialized (1/4 wins), and 56.09% on Structured (8/8 wins), versus FT's 75.19%, 83.48%, and 48.07% respectively. The largest gap appears on Structured tasks (e.g., dSprites/loc: 78.4% vs 54.0%; Clevr/cnt: 68.2% vs 55.4%), while FT edges out VPT on Specialized tasks with dissimilar data distributions (e.g., Patch Camelyon: 81.2% vs 77.9%). As training data grows from 400 to 10,000 samples, the VPT–FT gap narrows substantially, with FT surpassing VPT in 9 of 12 cases at the largest scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between VPT [49] and full finetuning (FT) [47] across 19 VTAB-1k tasks. VPT wins 16/19 tasks, with the most dramatic gains on Structured tasks (8/8 wins, e.g., dSprites/loc: 78.4% vs 54.0%, a 24.4-point gap) and Natural tasks (6/7 wins, e.g., CIFAR-100: 77.7% vs 64.5%). FT wins only 3/19 tasks, all within the Specialized group where data distributions differ from ImageNet-21k (e.g., Patch Camelyon: 81.2% vs 77.9%). A secondary comparison with LoRA [46] on CIFAR-100 (Table 20) shows LoRA also outperforms FT at small data scales (69.6% vs 44.5% at 400 samples) but the gap narrows to 2.8 points at 10,000 samples, mirroring the VPT–FT trend. The paper also tests two ablation variants—Mixed (prompts + full FT) and FT-then-PT (sequential FT then prompts)—both of which underperform VPT, confirming that the specific mechanism of freezing the backbone while adding prompts is critical rather than merely adding extra parameters or dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a systematic empirical analysis that builds directly on the Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) method of Jia et al. (ECCV 2022) and the broader parameter-efficient fine-tuning literature (adapters, partial tuning, LoRA). While prior work demonstrated VPT's superior performance over full finetuning on VTAB-1k, the conditions under which this advantage holds and the mechanistic reasons behind it remained unexplored. The paper also corrects a claim from E²VPT (Han et al., ICCV 2023) that VPT consistently outperforms FT across all data scales, showing instead that FT closes and eventually surpasses the gap as downstream data grows. Conceptually, it draws on NLP prompt-tuning analyses (e.g., Chen et al. 2022, Wang et al. 2022) for framing but applies a novel quadrant-based categorization along task disparity and data distribution similarity dimensions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with a concrete decision framework (the four-quadrant model) for choosing between VPT and FT based on task disparity and data distribution similarity, which is immediately actionable. The finding that overfitting and local-minima escape are not the primary drivers of VPT's advantage challenges intuitive explanations and redirects attention to the unique way VPT preserves pretrained features while adding task-specific parameters. Key limitations include the restriction to image classification (no segmentation, detection, or captioning), reliance on ViT/Swin backbones, and the use of post-hoc explainability methods (GradCAM, IG) whose reliability is acknowledged as potentially misleading. Open questions include extending the analysis to other visual tasks, investigating attention-position-level interpretability, and exploring ad-hoc explainability of prompt tuning in safety-critical settings.
