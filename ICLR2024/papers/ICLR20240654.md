## Problem

Large pretrained decision-making models for robotics are typically adapted to new tasks via full fine-tuning, which is computationally expensive, prone to overfitting with limited demonstrations, and causes catastrophic forgetting of previously learned skills. While parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) techniques like LoRA and adapters have shown promise in language and vision, their behavior in data-scarce, continual imitation learning for control remains unexplored—particularly because test-task loss does not reliably predict task performance in decision-making.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes 128×128 RGB images from two camera views (agent-front and eye-in-hand), 9-dimensional robot joint states, and natural-language task instructions, producing 7-dimensional continuous actions at each timestep. Actions are parameterized as a 5-mode Gaussian Mixture Model, with the highest-density mode's mean selected deterministically at evaluation. During adaptation, only ~1.17% of parameters (the adapters, fusion module, and policy head) are trainable while the CLIP encoders and GPT-2 decoder remain frozen.

## Method

TAIL inserts lightweight, task-specific adapter modules into a pretrained multi-modal transformer policy (frozen CLIP spatial/instruction encoders + GPT-2 temporal decoder + FiLM fusion), comparing three integration styles: parallel (LoRA low-rank matrices added to weight matrices), sequential (bottleneck adapters with down-up projections and nonlinear activation), and prefix token (learnable virtual tokens prepended to the input sequence). For each new task in a continual stream, a fresh adapter is initialized (from the previous adapter plus small Gaussian noise), trained via behavioral cloning on ~40 demonstrations while all base-model weights stay frozen, and then stored as a swappable plugin. The key finding is that LoRA's parallel integration achieves the best forward transfer (70% average success on LIBERO-10 vs. 48% for full fine-tuning) with zero backward transfer loss, 23% less GPU memory, and ~50% shorter training time, because the low-rank update preserves pretrained representations and resists overfitting in the limited-data regime.

## Results

On the LIBERO-10 long-horizon benchmark, TAIL with LoRA achieves the best forward transfer (FWT) of 0.70±0.10, substantially outperforming Full Fine-Tuning (0.48±0.10), Experience Replay (0.45±0.09), and EWC (0.30±0.16), while maintaining perfect backward transfer (BWT = 0, i.e., no catastrophic forgetting) compared to FFT's BWT of −0.55±0.21. TAIL-LoRA uses only 1.17% of the trainable parameters (2.02M vs. 172.24M) and 23% less GPU memory (15.5G vs. 20.1G) than full fine-tuning. Across the 6-suite continual learning evaluation (Fig. 5), TAIL outperforms the best baselines by over 3× in some comparisons, with LoRA consistently the strongest adapter variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAIL-LoRA decisively outperforms all four baselines on both forward and backward transfer. Against Experience Replay (Chaudhry et al.), TAIL-LoRA achieves 0.70 FWT vs. 0.45, and avoids the −0.49 BWT that ER suffers, while also requiring far less storage (7.8 MB adapter vs. 28 GB replay buffer for the Kitchen dataset). Against EWC (Kirkpatrick et al.), the gap is even wider: TAIL-LoRA's 0.70 FWT nearly doubles EWC's 0.30, and EWC's regularization introduces training instability and extra GPU memory overhead. Against PackNet (Mallya & Lazebnik), which prunes and re-learns parameters per task, TAIL-LoRA is consistently superior across all task suites in the 6-suite continual evaluation, though PackNet adapts reasonably on some individual suites. Against RoboAdapter (Sharma et al.), the closest prior adapter method for robotics, TAIL-LoRA leads by 0.28 FWT (0.70 vs. 0.42), attributed to RoboAdapter's restricted insertion only after feedforward layers rather than the parallel low-rank approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAIL directly ports three parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) techniques from the NLP literature—LoRA (Hu et al., 2021), Bottleneck Adapters (Houlsby et al., 2019), and Prefix Tuning (Li & Liang, 2021)—into the domain of large pretrained decision-making models for robot control. While prior robotics work (RoboAdapter, Liang et al. 2022) has explored single-task adapter insertion, and large pretrained control models (RT-1, RT-2, RoboCat) typically rely on expensive full fine-tuning for adaptation, TAIL is the first to systematically compare these PEFT integration styles under a *continual* imitation learning protocol with multiple sequential task suites. The framework also departs from standard continual learning baselines (Experience Replay, EWC, PackNet) by showing that lightweight task-specific plugins can outperform both rehearsal and regularization-based approaches while using orders of magnitude less storage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that LoRA with only 1.17% of trainable parameters outperforms full fine-tuning in continual imitation learning is practically significant for deploying robots that must adapt to many new tasks without retraining or storing full model copies per task. However, the evidence is confined to the LIBERO tabletop manipulation benchmark and a single model architecture (CLIP + GPT-2), leaving open whether the PEFT-over-FFT advantage persists at larger model scales, in more complex or real-world environments, or in reinforcement learning settings where the loss landscape differs from behavioral cloning. The paper also identifies that the quality of the frozen pretrained encoder is critical—fine-tuning CLIP before applying LoRA degrades performance—suggesting that the interplay between pretraining richness and adapter-based adaptation remains an open design question.
