## Problem

Most continual learning (CL) methods focus on the stability-plasticity dilemma but overlook how dataset bias (spurious correlations between a group attribute and class labels) in one task propagates to other tasks in a sequential CL pipeline. This paper demonstrates that standard CL methods transfer bias both forward and backward across tasks, with the direction and severity governed by whether the method emphasizes stability or plasticity, and that bias accumulates over longer task sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241484/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each task provides data samples as tuples (x_i, a_i, y_i), where x_i is an RGB image (CIFAR-100, CelebA, ImageNet) or text (MultiNLI, WanLI), a_i is a group label identifying a potentially spurious attribute (e.g., color vs. grayscale, gender, presence of negation words), and y_i is the class label. The model outputs class predictions, evaluated with standard accuracy plus two bias-specific metrics: BMR (fraction of correctly classified inputs whose prediction flips under a counterfactual group-label change) and DCA (average maximum accuracy gap across groups within each class). The BGS plug-in additionally consumes group labels to maintain a group-class balanced exemplar memory of fixed capacity K.

## Method

The paper's primary contribution is a systematic empirical analysis of bias transfer in CL, using CKA feature-representation analysis, BMR/DCA bias metrics, and a Normalized F-I metric to quantify the stability-plasticity trade-off across three CL scenarios (Task-IL, Domain-IL, Class-IL) and multiple CL baselines (EWC, LWF, ER, iCaRL, EEIL, PackNet). The key finding is that stability-focused CL methods exacerbate forward bias transfer (bias from past to future tasks) while plasticity-focused methods exacerbate backward transfer, and the same-type bias accumulates monotonically with the number of biased tasks. As a practical contribution, the paper proposes BGS (group-class Balanced Greedy Sampling), a plug-in that greedily stores group-class balanced exemplars across all encountered classes and groups during CL training, then re-trains only the last layer of the CL-trained model using this balanced memory. BGS combines the greedy sampling principle from GDumb with the last-layer retraining principle from DFR, requires no additional hyperparameters, and is compatible with any existing CL method. The paper also releases standardized bias-aware CL benchmarks (Split CIFAR-100S, CelebA⁸, Split ImageNet-100C) and evaluation protocols to facilitate future bias-aware CL research.

## Results

On Split CIFAR-100S (Task-IL), BGS plugged into LWF reduces BMR from 28.25% to 17.97% while improving accuracy from 67.81% to 70.80%. On Split ImageNet-100C (Class-IL), iCaRL(2000)+BGS achieves 41.77% accuracy and 12.92% BMR, a dramatic improvement over iCaRL(2000) alone (36.79% Acc, 34.55% BMR). On CelebA⁸ (Domain-IL), EWC+BGS(160) reduces DCA from 28.41% to 15.97%, though at a cost of ~6.5% accuracy. BGS consistently outperforms naively combining Group DRO with the same CL method across all three scenarios without requiring additional hyperparameter tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BGS consistently reduces bias metrics (BMR or DCA) across all three CL scenarios when plugged into any CL method, with the largest gains on Split ImageNet-100C where iCaRL(2000)+BGS cuts BMR from 34.55% to 12.92% while also boosting accuracy from 36.79% to 41.77%. On Split CIFAR-100S, LWF+BGS(2000) achieves the best overall trade-off (70.80% Acc, 17.97% BMR), outperforming both LWF alone and LWF+Group DRO (65.64% Acc, 26.10% BMR) on both metrics. On CelebA⁸, EWC+BGS(160) achieves the lowest DCA (15.97%) but at a notable accuracy cost (88.48% vs. 94.99%), while LWF+BGS(320) offers a more balanced result (94.21% Acc, 23.12% DCA) that still beats LWF+Group DRO (94.26% Acc, 23.67% DCA) on bias. Group DRO combined with CL methods provides only marginal bias reduction compared to BGS in most settings, and the authors attribute this to the difficulty of jointly tuning hyperparameters for both the CL method and Group DRO. BGS requires no additional hyperparameters beyond the exemplar memory size, making it a drop-in improvement over the baseline CL methods it is paired with.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of continual learning (CL) and debiasing, two well-studied but previously disconnected areas. It builds conceptually on GDumb's greedy balanced sampling and DFR's last-layer retraining to construct BGS, while its empirical framework draws on standard CL baselines (EWC, LWF, ER, iCaRL, EEIL, PackNet) and the Group DRO debiasing technique. The key departure from prior work is identifying bias transfer as a distinct failure mode in CL that is orthogonal to the stability-plasticity dilemma: two prior works (Lesort 2022; Jeon et al. 2023) touched on local spurious features in Domain-IL only, but this paper systematically demonstrates that bias propagates across all three CL scenarios (Task-IL, Domain-IL, Class-IL) and in both temporal directions, with the direction governed by the stability-plasticity emphasis of the CL method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that standard CL methods silently propagate dataset bias to other tasks, a failure mode that is invisible under accuracy-only evaluation and could undermine real-world CL deployments where training data is inevitably biased. The standardized benchmarks (Split CIFAR-100S, CelebA⁸, Split ImageNet-100C) and the BMR/DCA evaluation protocol provide a foundation for future bias-aware CL research. Key limitations remain: BGS only retrains the last layer, so biased feature representations persist in the backbone, and handling multiple or continuous bias types is still nontrivial. The open question of whether a single principled method can simultaneously optimize for stability, plasticity, and debiasing—rather than treating them as separate objectives—remains unresolved.
