## Problem

Existing time series diffusion models (TimeGrad, CSDI, SSSD, TimeDiff) denoise directly from random noise vectors, ignoring the multi-resolution temporal structure that is a cornerstone of traditional time series analysis. While seasonal-trend decomposition has proven effective in transformer and MLP-based forecasters (Autoformer, NBeats, DLinear, N-Hits), its integration into diffusion-based time series generation had not been explored, leaving a gap in how generative models exploit the hierarchical fine-to-coarse patterns inherent in real-world time series.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241914/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a lookback window of past multivariate observations (length L selected from {96, 192, 336, 720, 1440} via validation) and produces a probabilistic forecast of length H (ranging from 14 to 720 steps depending on the dataset's sampling frequency). Internally, both the lookback and forecast segments are decomposed into S fine-to-coarse trend levels via iterative average pooling with increasing kernel sizes, so the network operates at multiple temporal resolutions simultaneously. Instance normalization is applied per window, and the final output is a single sampled trajectory from the learned conditional distribution.

## Method

mr-Diff is a cascaded diffusion model with S stages (default S=5), where each stage s+1 reconstructs the trend component Y_s at a progressively finer resolution. The forward diffusion is standard DDPM applied independently at each resolution level. The key innovation is in the backward denoising: stages proceed from coarsest to finest (easy-to-hard), and each stage's denoising network is conditioned on (i) the same-resolution lookback segment X_s (mapped via a linear layer and enhanced with future-mixup during training) and (ii) the coarser trend estimate Ŷ_{s+1}^0 produced by the previous stage, concatenated along the channel dimension. The denoising network is a convolutional encoder-decoder (not a transformer) that takes the diffused sample, the diffusion-step embedding, and the condition tensor, and outputs a data-prediction estimate of Y_s^0. This design avoids the quadratic complexity of transformer-based diffusion models and allows each resolution to have its own dedicated network, making the model more flexible than Scaleformer's single shared forecasting module across scales.

## Results

On univariate time series forecasting (MAE), mr-Diff achieves the best average rank of 1.7 across 9 datasets, winning on 5 of 9 (Electricity 0.332, Weather 0.032, Exchange 0.094, ETTh1 0.196, ETTm1 0.149) and ranking 2nd on 3 more. On multivariate forecasting, mr-Diff again achieves the best average rank (2.6), with top-2 results on 5 of 9 datasets. Inference efficiency is competitive: with S=3 stages, mr-Diff (14.3 ms at H=168) is faster than TimeDiff (17.3 ms), CSDI (128.3 ms), and SSSD (590.2 ms), with only 1.4M trainable parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

mr-Diff clearly outperforms all four time-series diffusion baselines: it beats TimeDiff (closest competitor, avg rank 4.0) on 7 of 9 univariate datasets, and dominates TimeGrad (avg rank 16.6), CSDI (15.1), and SSSD (14.3) by large margins. Against non-diffusion models, mr-Diff wins on 5 of 9 univariate datasets but loses to NLinear (avg rank 3.3) on NorPool (0.609 vs 0.637), Traffic (0.197 vs 0.192), and Wind (1.168 vs 1.167), and to SCINet (6.7) on Wind. On multivariate forecasting, mr-Diff (avg rank 2.6) edges out NLinear (3.8) and N-Hits (5.1), though NLinear wins on Electricity (0.239 vs 0.252) and ETTh1 (0.418 vs 0.422). The paper notes that on Caiso, mr-Diff shows no improvement over baselines because the dataset lacks complex trend structure to exploit via multi-resolution decomposition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: time series diffusion models (TimeGrad, CSDI, SSSD, TimeDiff) and multi-resolution seasonal-trend decomposition (Autoformer, NBeats, DLinear, N-Hits). It adopts the non-autoregressive conditional diffusion framework and future-mixup conditioning from TimeDiff (Shen & Kwok, 2023), the cascaded multi-stage generation concept from Ho et al. (2022), and the average-pooling-based trend extraction from Autoformer (Wu et al., 2021). The key departure from prior diffusion models is that instead of denoising a single target from pure noise, mr-Diff decomposes the denoising objective into S sub-objectives at progressively coarser resolutions, using each coarser trend as a condition for the next finer one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that multi-resolution temporal structure can be effectively exploited within a diffusion framework, this work opens a path for incorporating domain-specific inductive biases (decomposition, hierarchy, scale) into generative time series models, which prior diffusion approaches largely ignored. The practical impact is moderate: mr-Diff achieves the best average rank among 22 baselines on both univariate and multivariate settings while remaining more efficient than other diffusion models, but the margin over the closest diffusion baseline (TimeDiff) is modest on several datasets. Open questions include whether the fixed number of stages S and linear kernel-size schedule can be made adaptive per dataset, whether the trend-focused (rather than seasonal-focused) decomposition is universally optimal, and whether the approach extends naturally to irregularly-sampled or event-driven time series.
