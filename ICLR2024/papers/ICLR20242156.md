## Problem

Conformal prediction provides distribution-free coverage guarantees for node classification in the transductive setting (fixed graph), but in the more realistic inductive setting—where new nodes and edges arrive over time—message passing with the new structure implicitly shifts GNN embeddings and conformity scores, breaking the exchangeability assumption and invalidating the guarantee. Existing work (NAPS) attempts to fix this via neighborhood-weighted CP but fails on sparse graphs with realistic calibration set sizes, leaving many nodes without any prediction set.

## Input / Output

At each timestep t, the method consumes the current subgraph G_t (node features and adjacency), a pre-trained permutation-equivariant GNN, a fixed calibration set of labeled nodes, and a set of nodes to evaluate. It outputs a prediction set per evaluated node, where the set contains all labels whose conformity score exceeds a quantile threshold computed from calibration scores on the same subgraph. A key structural property is that the threshold is recomputed at every timestep (it is time-dependent), and for edge-exchangeable sequences the quantile is weighted by 1/deg(v) per calibration node. The guarantee is time-independent: a node may be evaluated at any timestep after its first appearance and still receives the same 1−α coverage.

## Method

The core insight is that under node-exchangeability, the distribution shift in conformity scores caused by newly arriving nodes is symmetric across calibration, evaluation, and unobserved nodes, so recomputing all scores conditional to the subgraph at timestep t restores exchangeability and recovers the standard CP guarantee (Proposition 1, Theorem 3). For edge-exchangeable sequences—which can generate sparse graphs unlike node-exchangeable ones that produce dense or empty graphs—the paper proves (Theorem 4) that the sequence decomposes into weighted node-exchangeable subsequences with weights 1/deg(v), and weighted CP via the weighted quantile lemma (Tibshirani et al., 2019) yields a valid 1−α guarantee. The practical algorithm (NodeEx/EdgeEx CP) simply re-runs the GNN on the updated subgraph at each evaluation timestep, recomputes conformity scores for all calibration and test nodes, updates the quantile threshold, and forms prediction sets—no retraining is needed. The approach is orthogonal to the choice of score function (APS, TPS, DAPS) and model architecture (GCN, GAT, APPNP, MLP), and the guarantee holds for any evaluation mask, including predicting upon node arrival, at a fixed time, or at arbitrary node-specific times.

## Results

The primary metric is the average absolute deviation from the target 90% coverage guarantee (lower is better). Under node-exchangeable sequences, NodeEx CP achieves deviations of 0.04–0.56% across all seven main datasets, while Naive CP deviates by 2.69–6.37%. Under edge-exchangeable sequences, EdgeEx CP (weighted NodeEx CP with 1/deg(v) weights) achieves 1.24–3.02% deviation, substantially outperforming both Naive CP (3.46–7.84%) and unweighted NodeEx CP (3.41–5.73%). The guarantee also holds at arbitrary prediction times: the empirical coverage distribution matches the theoretical hypergeometric distribution regardless of whether nodes are predicted upon arrival, at a fixed time, or at node-specific random times.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Naive CP (standard split conformal prediction applied without correcting for the inductive score shift), NodeEx CP reduces the coverage deviation by roughly 10–20× under node-exchangeable sequences (e.g., 0.04% vs. 4.15% on CiteSeer, 0.28% vs. 5.86% on Cora-ML). Under edge-exchangeable sequences, EdgeEx CP similarly outperforms Naive CP by 2–3× (e.g., 0.34% vs. 3.46% on CiteSeer, 1.24% vs. 5.32% on PubMed). The second baseline, NAPS (Clarkson 2023), which restricts calibration to k-hop neighbors of each test node, is shown in the supplementary to be inapplicable for a large fraction of nodes in sparse graphs (up to 70%+ on some datasets receive no prediction set at all), and for the remaining nodes its empirical coverage still deviates significantly from the guarantee. NodeEx/EdgeEx CP thus wins decisively on both validity (coverage deviation) and applicability (works for all nodes regardless of sparsity) compared to both baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the transductive conformal prediction framework for GNNs established by Huang et al. (2023) and Zargarbashi et al. (2023) to the inductive setting, where the graph evolves over time and new nodes/edges cause an implicit shift in conformity scores. The key departure from prior work is the observation that this shift is symmetric under node- or edge-exchangeability, allowing the guarantee to be recovered simply by recomputing scores conditional to the current subgraph—without resorting to the beyond-exchangeability machinery of Barber et al. (2023) or the neighborhood-restricted NAPS of Clarkson (2023), which the paper shows is essentially inapplicable on sparse graphs with realistic calibration set sizes. For edge-exchangeable sequences, the paper leverages the weighted quantile lemma from Tibshirani et al. (2019) with degree-based weights to maintain validity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first conformal prediction method with a valid coverage guarantee for inductive node classification, a setting that better reflects real-world dynamic graphs than the transductive case. The guarantee's independence from prediction time (a node can be evaluated at any timestep after its appearance) offers practical flexibility. Key limitations include the marginal (not conditional) nature of the guarantee, the requirement of node- or edge-exchangeability which real-world graphs may not satisfy, and the failure of the guarantee under adversarially chosen evaluation sets. An open direction is layering the beyond-exchangeability weighting of Barber et al. (2023) on top to handle distribution shift over time, which the authors note is orthogonal to their contribution.
