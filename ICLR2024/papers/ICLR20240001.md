## Problem

Existing label differential privacy (LabelDP) mechanisms based on randomized response suffer from exponentially increasing noise in the high-privacy regime (small ε), rendering them nearly useless when strong privacy guarantees are needed. DP-SGD offers better (linear) noise scaling but over-protects by privatizing both features and labels. No practical method had previously combined the superior noise scaling of central DP with the flexibility of LabelDP, which allows leveraging public input features to reduce unnecessary noise.

## Input / Output

The method takes a training set where features (RGB images at 28×28 or 32×32, or categorical/numerical ad features) are public and only labels are private. It produces trained model weights satisfying (ε,δ)-LabelDP, evaluated via classification accuracy (MNIST, CIFAR-10, FashionMNIST, kMNIST) or AUC (Criteo attribution). A key design constraint is that the denoiser must construct its projection geometry using only unlabeled features, never accessing the private labels of the current batch.

## Method

LabelDP-Pro interleaves standard DP-SGD steps (per-example gradient clipping + Gaussian noise) with a projection-based denoiser that projects the noisy gradient onto a convex hull spanned by per-example per-class gradients computed from an independently sampled batch of public features. Since the true gradient already lies in this convex hull, the projection acts as a no-op on the signal while reducing the effective noise dimensionality from d to O(Kn₂). The ALTCONV variant (using an independent batch for the convex hull) is compatible with privacy amplification by subsampling, unlike SELFSPAN/SELFCONV which use the same batch. The projection is computed memory-efficiently via forward-mode (JVP) and reverse-mode (VJP) autodiff without materializing the full d×Kn₂ gradient matrix, and a coefficient smoothing step (analogous to label smoothing) stabilizes training by preventing the projection from placing excessive weight on incorrect classes.

## Results

On MNIST-10, LabelDP-Pro achieves 92.9% test accuracy at ε=0.2, compared to 87.9% for DP-SGD and ≤10.9% for all RR-based baselines. On CIFAR-10, LabelDP-Pro reaches 30.8% at ε=0.2 versus 29.8% for DP-SGD and 17.9% for the best baseline (ALIBI). The performance gap over baselines widens as ε decreases: at ε=0.1 on CIFAR-10, LabelDP-Pro achieves 27.0% while all baselines remain near random (≤10.0%). At ε=2.0 on MNIST, RR-based methods (96.7%) slightly surpass LabelDP-Pro (95.4%), indicating a crossover threshold ε* around 1.2–1.3 for K=10 classes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LabelDP-Pro consistently outperforms all RR-based baselines (RR, RR-Debiased, ALIBI) in the high-privacy regime (ε < 1.0), with margins of 80+ percentage points on MNIST and 13–17 points on CIFAR-10 at ε=0.1–0.2. Against LP-2ST, LabelDP-Pro wins at ε ≤ 0.5 on both datasets but loses at ε=1.0 on CIFAR-10 (44.8% vs. 60.9%), where LP-2ST's multi-stage training with a learned prior becomes effective. Compared to DP-SGD, LabelDP-Pro achieves a consistent 1.0–5.5 point improvement across all ε values, with the gap largest at small ε (5.5 points on MNIST at ε=0.1) and smallest at large ε (1.3 points on CIFAR-10 at ε=1.0), confirming that the projection denoiser provides the most benefit when DP-SGD noise is highest. In the user-level privacy evaluation on Criteo Attribution, LabelDP-Pro achieves AUC 0.780 at ε=0.1, k=2 versus 0.686 for DP-SGD and 0.562 for RR, with the gap widening as k increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DP-SGD (Abadi et al., 2016) and the LabelDP privacy notion (Chaudhuri & Hsu, 2011), but departs from the dominant randomized-response paradigm by leveraging DP-SGD's superior linear noise scaling in the high-privacy regime. It makes practical a projection-based denoising idea that was only theoretically sketched in Ghazi et al. (2021, LP-Normal-SGD), using advanced autodiff (JVP/VJP) to compute projections onto convex hulls of per-example per-class gradients without materializing them. The ALTCONV denoiser is the first to combine central DP, approximate DP, and the LabelDP setting (exploiting public features) while remaining compatible with privacy amplification by subsampling, unlike prior RR-based mechanisms that are restricted to local DP and pure DP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LabelDP-Pro closes a significant utility gap in the high-privacy regime (ε < 1) where RR-based methods collapse to near-random accuracy, making private training viable for highly sensitive applications like ad attribution. The extension to user-level privacy on the Criteo dataset demonstrates practical relevance beyond toy benchmarks. Key open questions include reducing the ~200-iteration projection bottleneck, extending to regression tasks, and integrating with semi-supervised pipelines where powerful visual priors may not transfer to non-image domains. The threshold ε* above which RR outperforms LabelDP-Pro (and its dependence on K and δ) provides useful guidance for practitioners choosing mechanisms.
