## Problem

No domain generalization algorithm has convincingly outperformed simple empirical risk minimization (ERM) across standard benchmarks, because existing approaches either discard all environment-specific information (invariance methods) or dilute it into coarse marginal embeddings (marginal transfer methods). The paper argues that the in-context learning capability of large language models—where a model adapts its prediction based on a sequence of previously observed examples—provides a principled mechanism to retain fine-grained, example-level environmental signal that prior DG paradigms lose.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240030/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At test time, the model receives a sequence of unlabeled images arriving one-by-one from a single (unseen) test environment; for each query image x_j, the context c_j consists of all previously observed images (x_1, ..., x_{j-1}) from that same environment. A CNN backbone (ConvNet or ResNet-50) featureizes each image, and these features are fed into a decoder-only GPT-2 that attends over the context to produce a class label. Contexts are constructed by grouping samples by environment index rather than natural temporal order, since standard DG benchmarks do not provide such ordering.

## Method

ICRM trains a context-based predictor h(x; c) to minimize an auto-regressive cross-entropy loss over sequences sampled from a single training environment, where each prediction at position j conditions on all prior unlabeled inputs in that sequence. At test time, the model dynamically 'zooms-in' on the test environment's risk minimizer by attending to the growing context of previously seen test inputs, while 'zooming-out' to ERM behavior when no context is available. Theoretically, the paper proves that with an ideal amortization function, ICRM converges to the environment-specific Bayes predictor as context length grows, and that even a single context example yields strictly monotonic improvement over ERM under Markov assumptions. A key architectural insight is that training with context improves the learned featurizer itself, so ICRM outperforms ERM even at zero test-time context. The full pipeline pairs a standard CNN feature extractor with a GPT-2 decoder that performs causal attention over the context sequence to produce the final classification.

## Results

ICRM achieves the highest average OOD test accuracy across all four primary datasets at context length 100: 87.8% on FEMNIST, 96.2% on Rotated MNIST, 90.8% on WILDS Camelyon17, and 39.2% on Tiny ImageNet-C. Notably, ICRM outperforms ERM even at zero context (92.0% vs. 68.6% on WILDS Camelyon17; 38.3% vs. 31.8% on Tiny ImageNet-C), indicating that context-based training improves the learned featurizer. Worst-case accuracy gains are equally pronounced, with ICRM reaching 70.6% on FEMNIST and 88.8% on Rotated MNIST at context 100, versus 59.0% and 80.8% for ERM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICRM outperforms ARM (Zhang et al., NeurIPS 2020) on all four datasets at every context length, with the largest gap on FEMNIST worst-case accuracy (70.6% vs. 58.8% at context 100) and Tiny ImageNet-C average accuracy (39.2% vs. 31.0%). Against TENT (Wang et al., 2020), ICRM wins decisively on WILDS Camelyon17 (90.8% vs. 89.4% at context 100, and 92.0% vs. 67.9% at zero context) and Tiny ImageNet-C (39.2% vs. 2.1% at context 100, where TENT catastrophically degrades). Compared to ERM (Vapnik, 1998), ICRM matches or slightly trails at zero context on the MNIST datasets (78.7% vs. 79.3% on FEMNIST; 93.6% vs. 94.2% on Rotated MNIST), but surpasses ERM by a wide margin once even 25 context samples are available, and by a substantial margin at zero context on WILDS Camelyon17 (92.0% vs. 68.6%) and Tiny ImageNet-C (38.3% vs. 31.8%), demonstrating that context-based training itself improves the featurizer independent of test-time context.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by reframing domain generalization as in-context prediction, bridging two previously separate research threads: DG algorithms (invariance methods like IRM and marginal transfer methods like ARM) and in-context learning in LLMs. It departs from invariance DG, which discards all environment-specific signal, and from marginal transfer DG, which compresses environment information into a coarse embedding, by instead feeding the full sequence of unlabeled same-environment examples as context to a transformer decoder (GPT-2). The theoretical analysis draws on information-theoretic tools (mutual information, entropy decomposition) and Bayesian network Markov blanket arguments, while the practical implementation borrows the attention-based sequence modeling paradigm from neural processes and ICL mechanism studies, but crucially uses only unlabeled context rather than labeled demonstrations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central conceptual contribution—treating environment as context and context as environment—offers a unifying lens that could reshape how both DG and LLM researchers structure their data and objectives. The finding that context-based training improves the learned featurizer even at zero test-time context (outperforming ERM on WILDS Camelyon17 and Tiny ImageNet-C) suggests a practical benefit beyond the obvious in-context adaptation. Key limitations include the requirement that test data arrive from the same or slowly changing environment, the Voronoi-cell assumption for OOD guarantees, and the explicit exclusion of correlation-shift scenarios. Open questions flagged by the authors include preventing ICRM from zooming in on toxic spurious correlations, extending to naturally ordered data (video, text), and understanding how ICL amortizes trade-offs like efficiency-resiliency and specialization-generalization.
