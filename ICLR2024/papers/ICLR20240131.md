## Problem

State-of-the-art scene flow methods face a fundamental trade-off: optimization-based approaches (e.g., NSFP) produce high-quality estimates without human labels but require tens of seconds per frame, while feedforward methods run in milliseconds but depend on expensive human annotations that limit data diversity to short contiguous sequences. No prior method simultaneously achieves real-time inference, state-of-the-art accuracy, and zero human supervision on full-size point clouds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240131/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two temporally successive full-size LiDAR point clouds (averaging ~52K points on Argoverse 2 and ~79K on Waymo Open after ground removal, with real sensors producing up to 1M+ points) and outputs a 3D displacement vector for every point in the first cloud. Unlike most prior work evaluated on 8,192-point subsamples, ZeroFlow is designed to process the complete point cloud in real time (~29 ms on a V100), and ground points are removed via dataset-provided maps before processing.

## Method

ZeroFlow instantiates the Scene Flow via Distillation (SFvD) framework: a slow, label-free optimization teacher (NSFP, which fits per-pair MLPs to minimize truncated Chamfer distance with cycle consistency) generates high-quality pseudo-labels offline in a highly parallelizable step, and a fast feedforward student (FastFlow3D, a PointPillars-style encoder producing bird's-eye-view pseudoimages processed by a 4-layer U-Net) is trained on those pseudo-labels with a uniform-weighted EPE loss. The student architecture is further scaled to an XL variant (110M parameters vs. 6.8M) by halving pillar size, doubling pillar embeddings, and adding a layer. A key empirical finding is that ZeroFlow's performance follows predictable logarithmic scaling laws with both dataset size and model capacity, and that training on diverse non-contiguous frame pairs (enabled by the label-free pseudo-labeling) yields non-trivial gains over contiguous-sequence training.

## Results

On the Argoverse 2 Sensor validation split, ZeroFlow XL 3X achieves a Threeway EPE of 0.054 (Dynamic FG: 0.131, Static FG: 0.018, Static BG: 0.012) at 260.61 ms runtime, setting state-of-the-art on the Argoverse 2 Self-Supervised Scene Flow Challenge. The smaller ZeroFlow 3X variant reaches Threeway EPE 0.064 at just 29.33 ms (34 FPS), surpassing its teacher NSFP (0.067 at 26,285 ms) while running over 1000× faster. On Waymo Open, ZeroFlow 1X achieves Threeway EPE 0.092 at 21.66 ms, outperforming NSFP (0.100 at 90,999 ms) but trailing the optimization-based Chodosh method (0.041 at 93,752 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZeroFlow XL 3X achieves the best Threeway EPE (0.054) among all zero-label methods, narrowly beating the optimization-based Chodosh et al. (0.055) and substantially outperforming NSFP (0.067), while running 135× faster than Chodosh (260 ms vs 35,281 ms) and 101× faster than NSFP (260 ms vs 26,285 ms). The compact ZeroFlow 3X (29.33 ms) also surpasses its NSFP teacher in Threeway EPE (0.064 vs 0.067) at over 896× lower latency. Against the supervised FastFlow3D baseline, ZeroFlow 1X underperforms (0.088 vs 0.071 Threeway EPE), but scaling to 3× data closes the gap (0.064) and the XL variant surpasses it (0.054 vs 0.055). All ZeroFlow variants decisively outperform the self-supervised and classical baselines (ICP 0.204, Gojcic 0.083, Sim2Real 0.157, EgoFlow 0.205, PPWC 0.130, FlowStep3D 0.161) in Threeway EPE. On Waymo Open, ZeroFlow 1X (0.092) beats NSFP (0.100) and all self-supervised baselines but remains behind Chodosh (0.041), which benefits from a refinement step not available to the feedforward student.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZeroFlow positions itself at the intersection of two established scene flow paradigms: label-free test-time optimization (NSFP, which uses cycle consistency and truncated Chamfer distance to fit per-pair MLPs) and supervised feedforward estimation (FastFlow3D, a PointPillars-based U-Net). The conceptual contribution is the Scene Flow via Distillation (SFvD) framework, which repurposes the slow optimizer as a pseudo-label generator to train the fast student, thereby inheriting the quality of optimization-based methods without their runtime cost and the architectural simplicity of feedforward networks without their annotation cost. This departs from prior self-supervised feedforward work (Gojcic, Kittenplon, etc.) that relies on hand-designed rigidity priors and more complex architectures, and from supervised methods that require expensive in-domain human labels limited to short contiguous sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ZeroFlow provides the first practical, scalable pipeline for building scene flow foundation models from raw deployment logs, following predictable logarithmic scaling laws with data size and model capacity. The finding that a higher-quality teacher (Chodosh) yields no meaningful improvement over NSFP suggests the student architecture, not pseudo-label quality, is the current bottleneck—an open question for future work. Limitations include inheriting systematic biases from the teacher (e.g., consistent failures on certain object classes) and the reliance on NSFP's specific prior structure; whether more expressive teachers or different distillation objectives can break this ceiling remains unexplored.
