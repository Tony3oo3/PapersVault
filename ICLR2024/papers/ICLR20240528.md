## Problem

Existing Transformer-based image denoising methods rely on either spatial-wise self-attention (e.g., SwinIR) or channel-wise self-attention (e.g., Restormer), but neither explores how to effectively combine both types of attention in a single network. The gap is that spatial-wise SA captures fine-grained local patch interactions while channel-wise SA models global channel-level context, and a principled mechanism to fuse these complementary representations in a concurrent architecture remains unexplored.

## Input / Output

The network accepts a noisy RGB image (3×H×W) or grayscale image (1×H×W) and outputs a denoised image of the same size via residual learning (output = input + predicted residual). A 3×3 convolution first maps the input to a 48-channel shallow feature, which is then processed through the dual-branch network. FLOPs are reported at a 3×128×128 input resolution, and training uses progressive patch sizes from 128² up to 384² with batch sizes decreasing from 64 to 8.

## Method

Xformer is a concurrent X-shaped network with two parallel 4-level U-shaped encoder-decoder branches: a spatial-wise branch built from window-based multi-head self-attention blocks (STB, window size 16 with shifted windows and relative position encoding) and a channel-wise branch built from cross-covariance attention blocks (CTB, using 3×3 depth-wise convolutions to generate Q/K/V and a learnable temperature parameter). The key novelty is the Bidirectional Connection Unit (BCU), which bridges the two branches at encoder and decoder stages using asymmetric 3×3 convolutions (depth-wise for the spatial branch, standard for the channel branch) to perform interactive, bidirectional feature fusion rather than simple concatenation. The last encoder stage shares parameters across both branches for efficiency, and the final outputs from both branches are concatenated and refined by a 4-block CTB module before a 3×3 convolution produces the residual. Training uses L1 loss with AdamW, cosine annealing from 3×10⁻⁴ to 10⁻⁶, and a progressive training schedule over 300k iterations on 4 A100 GPUs.

## Results

On Gaussian color image denoising, Xformer achieves state-of-the-art PSNR across all four benchmark datasets. On the challenging Urban100 at σ=50, Xformer reaches 30.36 dB, outperforming Restormer (30.02 dB) by 0.34 dB and SwinIR (29.82 dB) by 0.54 dB. On McMaster at σ=50, Xformer scores 30.38 dB vs. Restormer's 30.30 dB. For real-world denoising on DND, Xformer achieves 40.19 dB PSNR and 0.957 SSIM, surpassing Restormer (40.03 dB) and Uformer (40.04 dB), while on SIDD it matches Restormer at 0.960 SSIM with a marginal 0.04 dB PSNR gap (39.98 vs. 40.02).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Xformer consistently outperforms all 25 compared baselines on Gaussian color denoising (Table 3), with the largest margins over CNN-based methods: on Urban100 at σ=50, it gains 2.43 dB over DRUNet (29.61→30.36) and 2.43 dB over BM3D (27.93→30.36). Among Transformer-based methods, Xformer beats Restormer by 0.34 dB on Urban100 (σ=50) and 0.38 dB on Urban100 (σ=25), while using comparable parameters (25.23M vs. 26.11M) and FLOPs (42.2G vs. 38.7G). Compared to SwinIR, Xformer achieves 0.54 dB higher PSNR on Urban100 (σ=50) with 4.76× fewer FLOPs (42.2G vs. 201.2G). For real-world denoising (Table 4), Xformer leads on DND with 40.19 dB PSNR, surpassing Restormer (40.03 dB) and Uformer (40.04 dB), but trails Restormer by 0.04 dB on SIDD (39.98 vs. 40.02 dB) while matching its SSIM (0.960). The ablation confirms that the dual-branch design with BCU contributes +0.12 dB over the no-BCU variant, and the hybrid STB+CTB configuration outperforms either block alone by 0.07–0.27 dB.

## Relation to Prior Work

Xformer sits at the intersection of three prior lines of work: SwinIR's spatial-wise window-based self-attention, Restormer's channel-wise cross-covariance attention, and Conformer's concurrent dual-branch architecture. Rather than choosing one attention paradigm, Xformer runs both in parallel U-shaped branches and introduces the Bidirectional Connection Unit (BCU) to fuse their complementary representations via lightweight convolutions. This departs from prior Transformer-based denoisers (SwinIR, Restormer, Uformer) that each commit to a single attention axis, and from Conformer which pairs CNN with Transformer branches rather than two distinct Transformer block types.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that spatial and channel attention capture genuinely complementary information for denoising, and that a simple bidirectional convolutional bridge (BCU) is sufficient to exploit this complementarity—yielding consistent 0.3–0.4 dB gains over Restormer at comparable FLOPs. However, the BCU design is minimal (two 3×3 convolutions), raising open questions about whether richer cross-branch interaction mechanisms could yield further gains. The approach is validated only on denoising; whether the dual-branch spatial+channel paradigm transfers to other restoration tasks (inpainting, deblurring, super-resolution) remains unexplored. The modest absolute improvements and the reliance on a well-tuned progressive training schedule limit the practical impact beyond the denoising community.
