## Problem

Template-based MOF design methods enforce pre-curated topology templates and building block identities, inherently narrowing the chemical space and potentially excluding optimal materials. Directly applying atomic-level diffusion to MOFs is infeasible because typical MOF unit cells contain hundreds of atoms (vs. <20 in prior crystal diffusion work), making training computationally prohibitive and prone to unphysical structures. The paper asks: can a generative model produce diverse, valid, and novel MOF structures without predefined templates while respecting the hierarchical modular nature of MOFs?

## Input / Output

For generation, the model takes a random latent vector z ~ N(0,I); for inverse design, it optimizes z via gradient ascent on a property predictor (e.g., CO2 working capacity). The output is a coarse-grained MOF with K building blocks (K << N atoms), where each building block is specified by a 32-dimensional contrastive embedding (not a discrete category, since 242k unique blocks exist) and a 3D coordinate (centroid of connection points), plus lattice parameters. This CG structure is decoded to an all-atom MOF via nearest-neighbor retrieval of building block identities, an orientation-optimization assembly algorithm, and UFF force field relaxation, after which gas adsorption properties are computed via GCMC simulations.

## Method

MOFDiff operates on a coarse-grained representation where MOFs are decomposed into building blocks via MOFid's metal-oxo algorithm, with building block identities encoded by a contrastive-learning GemNet-OC encoder (SE(3)-invariant) trained to map geometrically similar blocks (same ECFP4 fingerprint) to nearby 32-dim embeddings. The generative model is a periodic VAE-diffusion hybrid: an SE(3)-invariant periodic GemNet-OC encoder maps the CG MOF to a 256-dim latent code, and an SE(3)-equivariant periodic GemNet-OC denoiser performs score-based denoising using DDPM (variance-preserving) for building block embeddings and a variance-exploding SDE for coordinates, with the lattice fixed throughout. A novel assembly algorithm recovers all-atom structures by optimizing building block orientations via L-BFGS to maximize Gaussian-kernel overlap of compatible (metal–non-metal) connection points, using an annealed schedule (σ: 3→1.65→0.3, k: 30→16→1) for coarse-to-fine refinement. Inverse design is enabled by gradient-based optimization over the latent space using a property predictor MLP trained on BW-DB labels.

## Results

MOFDiff generates 2,998 valid, novel, and unique (VNU) MOF structures out of 10,000 random samples (30% validity rate), with structural property distributions (PLD, surface area, density, void fraction) closely matching the BW-DB reference. For carbon capture inverse design, MOFDiff produces 2,054 VNU MOFs whose top-10 CO₂ working capacities range from 3.61 to 4.89 mol/kg, with MOFDiff-1 achieving 4.89 mol/kg and a CO₂/N₂ selectivity of 197.66. In terms of efficiency, MOFDiff requires only 14.6 GCMC simulations to find one MOF with working capacity > 2 mol/kg, a 75% reduction compared to 58.1 simulations needed for naive random sampling from BW-DB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOFDiff is compared against two baselines: (1) random sampling from the BW-DB dataset (Boyd et al., 2019) and (2) the VAE-based inverse design method of Yao et al. (2021). Against the BW-DB random sampling baseline, MOFDiff achieves a 75% reduction in GCMC simulations needed to find a qualifying MOF (14.6 vs. 58.1 simulations for working capacity > 2 mol/kg). In the head-to-head comparison of top candidates (Table 1), MOFDiff occupies 9 of the top 10 positions by CO₂ working capacity; its best sample (MOFDiff-1, 4.89 mol/kg) outperforms Al-PMOF (3.82 mol/kg, the top MOF selected by Boyd et al. 2019) by 28% and GMOF-1 (2.19 mol/kg, the top candidate from Yao et al. 2021) by 123%. The Yao et al. GMOF candidates (GMOF-1 through GMOF-9) span a wide range from 0.10 to 2.20 mol/kg, with only GMOF-4 (2.20 mol/kg) exceeding 2 mol/kg, while all ten MOFDiff samples exceed 3.6 mol/kg. MOFDiff also demonstrates superior CO₂/N₂ selectivity, with its top samples reaching selectivities of 197–1026 compared to 8.74 for Al-PMOF and 7–11 for the GMOF series.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the crystal diffusion VAE framework of Xie et al. (2022) from small inorganic crystals (<20 atoms) to complex MOF structures (hundreds of atoms) by introducing a coarse-grained building-block representation. It departs from the dominant template-based MOF design paradigm (Yao et al. 2021, Day & Wilmer 2020, Park et al. 2022) that relies on pre-curated topology templates and discrete building block vocabularies, instead learning a continuous contrastive embedding over 242k unique building blocks extracted from data. The diffusion process combines DDPM-style variance-preserving noise for building block identities with variance-exploding noise for coordinates, both processed by SE(3)-equivariant GemNet-OC networks, while a novel assembly algorithm recovers all-atom structures from the CG representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MOFDiff demonstrates that template-free 3D MOF generation via diffusion is feasible and can discover carbon capture MOFs that surpass both random sampling from BW-DB and previously proposed literature MOFs (9 of top 10 in Table 1), with a 75% reduction in expensive GCMC simulations per qualifying candidate. A key limitation is the declining validity rate for MOFs with more building blocks, suggesting the current single-pass diffusion struggles with larger, more complex structures. Open directions include iterative lattice refinement during generation, regularization with known templates, and scaling to experimentally realizable MOFs with larger primitive cells.
