## Problem

Identifiability of latent causal representations from observed data has been established for linear Gaussian models by leveraging changes in causal influences across environments (Liu et al., 2022), but this result is restricted to linear relationships and requires a number of environments that scales quadratically with the number of latent variables (ℓ + ℓ(ℓ+1)/2 in the worst case). This paper asks whether identifiability can be extended to nonlinear (polynomial) causal relationships with general exponential family noise, and whether the environment requirement can be made more practical.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unpaired observed data x (e.g., RGB images from a chemistry dataset or fMRI signals from six hippocampal brain regions) indexed by an environment variable u (which may be a domain label, time index, or batch identifier). It recovers latent causal variables z up to permutation and scaling, the directed acyclic graph structure among them, and the polynomial coefficients λ(u) that parameterize the causal relationships. A key constraint is that the mapping f from latent to observed space must be bijective, and the noise ε must have a characteristic function with a measure-zero zero set.

## Method

The theoretical core proves that latent causal variables are identifiable up to permutation and scaling when causal relationships are polynomial (with Kronecker-product features) and noise follows a two-parameter exponential family (Gaussian, Gamma, Beta, inverse Gaussian, inverse Gamma), requiring only 2ℓ+1 environments. The proof proceeds in three steps: (1) identifying latent noise variables via nonlinear ICA (Sorrenson et al., 2020), (2) showing z is a polynomial function of the recovered noise, and (3) using the change in polynomial coefficients across u to collapse the polynomial ambiguity to permutation and scaling. For the empirical method, a variational inference framework is built where the prior p(z|u) is factorized as conditional Gaussians (for Gaussian noise) or as a product over coefficients and noise (for non-Gaussian noise), with DAG structure enforced by constraining the coefficient matrix to be lower-triangular and sparsity induced via Laplace priors on each coefficient. The objective maximizes the ELBO with KL divergences in closed form, and non-Gaussian noise is handled via implicit reparameterization (Jankowiak & Obermeyer, 2018).

## Results

The primary evaluation metric is the Mean Pearson Correlation (MPC) between recovered and true latent causal variables, supplemented by Structural Hamming Distance (SHD) for graph recovery. On synthetic data across three model settings (linear Beta, linear Gamma, polynomial Gaussian), the proposed method achieves the highest MPC among all compared methods, with SHD approaching zero (correct graph recovery). On the chemistry image dataset, the proposed method outperforms all baselines in MPC and correctly recovers the causal graph z₁→z₂→z₃, verified by intervention experiments. On the fMRI hippocampus dataset, polynomial models with Gaussian noise yield the best MPC, while linear Gaussian models produce suboptimal results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: vanilla VAE, β-VAE, and iVAE. Both VAE and β-VAE assume latent variable independence and thus cannot model causal relationships among latent variables; traversal experiments on the image dataset confirm that changing any single learned variable alters the colors of all objects, indicating failure to disentangle causal structure. iVAE, which has theoretical guarantees for identifying latent noise variables up to permutation and scaling, successfully recovers the root variable z₁ (MPC = 0.963 on image data) but fails to identify non-root variables z₂ and z₃, since it cannot capture causal dependencies. The proposed method outperforms all three baselines in MPC across every model setting (linear Beta, linear Gamma, polynomial Gaussian) on synthetic data and on the image dataset, while also recovering the correct causal graph structure (SHD ≈ 0). The advantage is most pronounced for non-root variables, where iVAE degrades significantly but the proposed method maintains high MPC by explicitly modeling polynomial causal relationships with environment-varying coefficients.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Liu et al. (2022), which established identifiability of latent causal variables under linear Gaussian models with changing causal influences, by generalizing to polynomial causal relationships with two-parameter exponential family noise (Gaussian, Gamma, Beta, inverse Gaussian, inverse Gamma). The proof strategy leverages the nonlinear ICA identifiability results from Sorrenson et al. (2020) and the variational framework of Khemakhem et al. (2020) (iVAE), but shifts the identifiability argument from the observed-data perspective to the latent-noise perspective, which enables the broader model class and reduces the required number of environments from ℓ+(ℓ(ℓ+1))/2 to 2ℓ+1. The paper also departs from prior work by providing a negative result (Corollary 3.2) showing that all polynomial coefficients must change for complete identifiability, and a partial identifiability result (Corollary 3.3) that partitions the latent space into invariant and variant subspaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The generalization from linear Gaussian to polynomial models with exponential family noise significantly narrows the gap between identifiability theory and practical applications, where causal relationships are rarely perfectly linear and noise is rarely Gaussian. The partial identifiability result has direct practical value for domain adaptation and generalization, where one specifically wants to identify invariant latent variables across environments. However, the paper acknowledges that learning the causal graph structure in latent space remains challenging even with identifiability guarantees, and the impact of partial identifiability on recoverable graph structure is left as an open question. The requirement that all coefficients change (Corollary 3.2) may be restrictive in practice, and the paper notes that imposing special graph structures could potentially relax this, but leaves that exploration for future work.
