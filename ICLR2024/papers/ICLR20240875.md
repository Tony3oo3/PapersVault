## Problem

Inferring multidimensional ODE systems in symbolic form from a single noisy, irregularly sampled trajectory remains unsolved: existing transformer-based methods (e.g., NSODE) are restricted to univariate ODEs, while genetic programming and SINDy require per-system optimization and finite-difference derivative approximations that degrade under noise. No prior method combines the scalability of a single pretrained model with the ability to handle arbitrary multivariate nonlinear ODE systems without domain-specific priors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240875/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single observed trajectory {(t_i, x_i)} of a D-dimensional system (D ≤ 6, zero-padded to D_max), where each time point is tokenized as (D+1)×3 numeric tokens (sign, mantissa, exponent per variable plus time). At inference, the model outputs a beam of 50 candidate ODE right-hand-side expressions in prefix notation (e.g., [+, cos, mul, +, 2424, E-3, x1, x2 | mul, x1, x2] for a 2D system), from which the candidate with highest R² reconstruction score is selected. Inference-time affine rescaling maps arbitrary time intervals to [1,10] and initial conditions to unity, so the model generalizes across scales without retraining.

## Method

ODEFormer is an asymmetric encoder-decoder transformer (4 encoder / 16 decoder layers, 16 heads, 512-dim embeddings, 86M parameters) trained end-to-end on 50M synthetically generated ODE trajectories via cross-entropy loss. The encoder processes numeric trajectory tokens without positional embeddings (since time is explicit in the input), while a 2-layer SiLU feed-forward network compresses the concatenated per-time-point embeddings into a fixed-size representation that flexibly handles variable system dimensions. The decoder predicts symbolic expressions in prefix notation to avoid parenthesis prediction, with component functions concatenated and separated by a special token. Key design choices include a three-token number encoding (sign, mantissa, exponent) that balances vocabulary size and precision, and beam sampling at inference with R²-based candidate selection. The model is pretrained once on diverse random ODEs (sampled as unary-binary trees with log-uniform coefficients) and requires no per-system hyperparameter tuning or finite-difference derivative estimation at test time.

## Results

On both the Strogatz dataset (7 2D ODEs) and the newly introduced ODEBench (63 ODEs spanning 1–4 dimensions), ODEFormer achieves the highest average reconstruction accuracy (fraction of predictions with R² > 0.9) across all noise and subsampling levels, outperforming all 8 baselines. On synthetic 1D data, ODEFormer reaches ~85% reconstruction accuracy and ~60% generalization accuracy. Inference takes on the order of seconds, versus minutes for all GP-based baselines (except SINDy). On the univariate ODE comparison with NSODE (Appendix H.2), ODEFormer achieves median R² of 0.99 on Textbook and 0.999 on Classic, with NSODE slightly ahead on the 'Large' dataset (0.998 vs 0.978 median R²) where the test distribution matches NSODE's training distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ODEFormer outperforms all 8 baselines (AFP, FE-AFP, EHC, EPLEX, PySR, SINDy, FFX, ProGED) on average reconstruction accuracy across both Strogatz and ODEBench, with the advantage growing substantially as noise (σ) and subsampling (ρ) increase. PySR is the closest competitor on clean, densely sampled data but degrades more rapidly under corruption. SINDy is the only other method running in seconds but is limited to linear combinations of predefined basis functions. All GP-based methods (AFP, FE-AFP, EHC, EPLEX, FFX) require per-equation hyperparameter tuning and take minutes per prediction, whereas ODEFormer uses a single pre-trained model with no per-instance tuning. Against the closely related univariate model NSODE (Becker et al., 2023), ODEFormer performs comparably on Textbook and Classic datasets (median R² ≈ 0.99–0.999 for both) but trails NSODE on the 'Large' synthetic dataset (median R² 0.978 vs 0.998; accuracy 65.2% vs 78.9% at 128 points, no noise), which is expected since 'Large' matches NSODE's training distribution. Generalization accuracy (evaluated on unseen initial conditions) drops by roughly half for all methods relative to reconstruction, confirming that correctly reconstructing a trajectory does not guarantee symbolic recovery of the true ODE.

## Relation to Prior Work

ODEFormer extends the transformer-based symbolic regression paradigm from univariate to multivariate ODE systems, building most directly on NSODE (Becker et al., 2023) which handled only scalar ODEs. The data generation procedure follows Lample & Charton (2019) for random expression trees, the numeric tokenization (sign/mantissa/exponent) follows Charton (2022), and the variable-dimensional input embedding follows Kamienny et al. (2022). The key departure from prior dynamical SR methods (SINDy, GP-based approaches, ProGED) is that ODEFormer requires no finite-difference derivative estimation and no per-system hyperparameter optimization, operating as a single pretrained sequence-to-sequence model on raw noisy, irregularly sampled trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ODEFormer provides the first practical tool for inferring interpretable multivariate ODE systems directly from noisy observational data, positioning it as a hypothesis generator for dynamical laws across the sciences. The introduction of ODEBench (63 systems spanning 1–4 dimensions, including chaotic attractors) addresses a critical gap left by the small, 2D-only Strogatz dataset. Key open questions remain: the method only handles first-order ODEs with all variables observed, struggles with chaotic systems, and inference from multiple trajectories (which would improve identifiability) is unresolved. The finding that generalization accuracy drops by roughly half relative to reconstruction accuracy highlights that correctly fitting one trajectory is far from sufficient for true dynamical law identification.
