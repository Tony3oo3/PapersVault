## Problem

Existing Lie group equivariant neural network methods rely on the group exponential map being surjective and on straightforward Haar measure sampling, both of which fail for non-compact, non-abelian groups such as GL⁺(n,ℝ) and SL(n,ℝ). This limits equivariant architectures to compact or abelian symmetry groups, excluding the affine and projective transformations that are ubiquitous in computer vision and robotics.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 40×40 grayscale MNIST digit images (padded from 28×28) and outputs a 10-way class label. Training uses only the standard 50,000 untransformed MNIST images with no data augmentation, while evaluation is performed on affNIST (320,000 affine-transformed digits) or homNIST (homography-transformed digits), testing out-of-distribution robustness to transformations the model has never seen during training.

## Method

The paper constructs equivariant convolutional layers for non-compact, non-abelian Lie groups by exploiting Cartan/Polar decomposition: GL⁺(n,ℝ) decomposes as Pos(n,ℝ)×SO(n) and SL(n,ℝ) as SPos(n,ℝ)×SO(n), yielding a factorization of the Haar measure into independent product measures on the SPD matrix space and the compact rotation subgroup. A global parametrization map ξ⁻¹:G→g is built from this decomposition (mapping A to (½ logm(AAᵀ), logm(R)) where A=S^{1/2}R), bypassing the non-surjective group exponential. Monte Carlo sampling for the convolution integral is then performed by independently drawing SPD matrices and uniform SO(n) rotations, requiring only 10 samples versus 100 for prior methods. For affine groups Rⁿ⋊G, the semi-direct product structure separates the translation factor (handled by standard grid convolution) from the linear factor (handled by the decomposition-based sampling), and a ResNet-style architecture with equivariant cross-correlation layers is trained end-to-end on MNIST.

## Results

On the affNIST benchmark (trained on untransformed MNIST, tested on 320K affine-transformed images), the R²⋊SL(2,R) model achieves 98.2% (±0.1) accuracy, outperforming all prior equivariant and capsule-network baselines. The R²⋊GL⁺(2,R) variant reaches 97.4% (±0.2). On the homNIST homography benchmark, the SL(2,R) model achieves 98.3% (±0.1) accuracy. Notably, both models require only 10 Monte Carlo samples per convolution, compared to 100 for the closest prior method (affConv).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The R²⋊SL(2,R) model outperforms the strongest capsule-network baseline, VB CapsNet (98.1%), by 0.1 points on affNIST while using roughly 2× more parameters (370K vs 175K). It surpasses RU CapsNet (97.69%) by 0.5 points and the affine-equivariant affConv of MacDonald et al. (95.08%) by a substantial 3.1 points, while requiring 10× fewer Monte Carlo samples (10 vs 100). The R²⋊GL⁺(2,R) variant (97.4%) also beats affConv by 2.3 points. On the harder homNIST homography task, the SL(2,R) model reaches 98.3% versus 95.71% for affConv, a 2.6-point margin. The GL⁺(2,R) model performs slightly below the SL(2,R) model on affNIST, which the authors attribute to the dataset containing only a small degree of scaling, making the volume-preserving constraint less restrictive and thus more advantageous.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Lie group equivariant CNN framework of Bekkers (2019) and Finzi et al. (2020), which rely on the group exponential map for kernel parametrization and Haar measure sampling. The key departure is that those methods require the exponential to be surjective, limiting them to compact or abelian groups; here, the authors use Cartan/Polar decomposition to split GL+(n,R) and SL(n,R) into SPD-matrix and SO(n) factors, each admitting closed-form parametrization and independent sampling. This is positioned as a generalization of the regular-representation equivariant layer construction (Cohen & Welling, 2016) to non-compact, non-abelian matrix Lie groups, and is contrasted with MacDonald et al. (2022), which still works within the exponential's injectivity radius and requires MCMC sampling on the Lie algebra.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, general recipe for constructing equivariant layers on any matrix Lie group with finitely many connected components, removing the surjectivity bottleneck that has constrained the field to compact/abelian symmetries. The practical impact is demonstrated on affine and homography-invariant classification, where the SL(2,R) model outperforms prior equivariant and capsule baselines while requiring 10× fewer Monte Carlo samples. However, experiments are limited to a single MNIST-derived benchmark, and the authors note that validation on other modalities (point clouds, molecules) and on learned/partial equivariance remain open. The decomposition tools are also directly applicable to LieTransformer-style attention architectures, broadening potential impact beyond convolutional networks.
