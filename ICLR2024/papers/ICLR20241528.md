## Problem

Diffusion-based inverse problem solvers (DIS) achieve strong reconstruction quality but require thousands of network function evaluations (NFEs), making them impractical for clinical use. The data consistency step in methods like DPS relies on the manifold-constrained gradient (MCG), which demands expensive backpropagation through the denoising network and only permits a single gradient step per denoising iteration. The paper asks whether classical Krylov subspace methods, particularly conjugate gradient (CG), can replace MCG to enable multiple efficient optimization steps per sampling iteration while provably preserving the geometric constraint of staying on the data manifold.

## Input / Output

The method consumes undersampled multi-coil k-space measurements (with sub-sampling masks and coil sensitivity maps) or sparse/limited-angle CT projections, together with a pre-trained 2D diffusion model and the corresponding forward operator (Fourier/Radon transform). It outputs a reconstructed 2D MRI image or a 3D CT volume at the native resolution of the training data (e.g., 320×320 for fastMRI, 256×256×256 for AAPM CT). Notably, the method is agnostic to the forward model at inference, supporting both Cartesian and non-Cartesian (NUFFT) trajectories, and handles both noiseless and noisy measurements without requiring singular value decomposition of the forward operator.

## Method

The key theoretical result (Proposition 1) shows that when the clean data manifold is locally approximated by an affine subspace, the Jacobian of the Tweedie denoised estimate equals the orthogonal projection onto that subspace, making the MCG equivalent to a projected gradient step. The critical insight is that if the tangent space at the denoised sample is representable as a Krylov subspace K_l, then M-step CG (with M ≤ l) on the normal equation A*Ax = A*y, initialized at the denoised estimate, is guaranteed to remain within the tangent space—eliminating the need for MCG entirely. The resulting DDS algorithm performs M CG iterations (typically M=5) at each DDIM sampling step, replacing the single MCG step in DPS, and is formulated for both VP and VE parameterizations. For noisy measurements, a proximal regularization term (γ/2)‖y−Ax‖² + (1/2)‖x−x̂_t‖² is minimized via CG on (I + γA*A), and for 3D CT, an ADMM-TV scheme with a CG inner solver enforces z-axis total variation while the diffusion prior handles the axial slices. This yields 80–200× speedup over prior state-of-the-art DIS methods (e.g., 49 NFE vs. 4000 NFE for Score-MRI) while matching or exceeding reconstruction quality.

## Results

On fastMRI knee parallel imaging (Uniform 1D ×4), DDS VP with 49 NFE achieves 34.61 dB PSNR / 0.956 SSIM, surpassing Score-MRI (4000×2×C NFE) at 33.25 dB / 0.857 SSIM while being 80–200× faster. On 3D sparse-view CT (8-view, AAPM 256×256), DDS VP (49 NFE) reaches 33.86 dB / 0.930 SSIM (axial), outperforming DiffusionMBIR (4000 NFE) at 33.49 dB / 0.942 SSIM. In the noisy MRI setting (σ=0.05), DDS VP (49 NFE) achieves 29.47 dB PSNR vs. DPS (1000 NFE) at 24.40 dB, a 5+ dB improvement at ~40× fewer NFEs. Wall-clock time is ~4.7 s (49 NFE) and ~2.25 s (19 NFE) per image on a single RTX 3090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDS consistently outperforms all diffusion-based baselines across both MRI and CT tasks. On noiseless PI MRI (Uniform 1D ×4), DDS VP (49 NFE) beats Score-MRI (4000×2×C NFE) by +1.36 dB PSNR and +0.099 SSIM, and beats DPS (1000 NFE) by +4.05 dB PSNR. On 3D sparse-view CT (8-view axial), DDS VP (49 NFE) surpasses DiffusionMBIR (4000 NFE) by +0.37 dB PSNR while being ~80× faster, and outperforms Score-Med (Song et al., 4000 NFE) by +4.76 dB and MCG (Chung et al., 4000 NFE) by +5.25 dB. In the noisy MRI setting, DDS (49 NFE) achieves 29.47 dB PSNR vs. DPS (1000 NFE) at 24.40 dB, a 5.07 dB gap. Supervised baselines (U-Net, E2E-Varnet for MRI; Lahiri et al., FBCPConvNet for CT) and the CS baseline (TV/ADMM-TV) are all substantially outperformed, with DDS leading by 2–17 dB depending on the task. The key advantage is that DDS achieves state-of-the-art quality at 19–49 NFE, whereas prior methods require 1000–4000 NFE, yielding 80–200× inference speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: diffusion-based inverse problem solvers (Score-MRI, DPS, DDNM, DiffusionMBIR) and classical Krylov subspace optimization (CG, GMRES). The key conceptual departure is from DPS, which imposes data consistency via the manifold-constrained gradient (MCG) requiring expensive backpropagation through the score network. By proving that MCG is equivalent to a projected gradient step on the tangent space at the Tweedie-denoised sample, the authors show that standard CG iterations—guaranteed to remain within that tangent space—can replace MCG entirely. This also distinguishes DDS from DDNM, whose null-space projection via the pseudo-inverse does not preserve the tangent-space constraint and underperforms on complex forward operators like parallel imaging MRI.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 80–200× inference speedup (19–49 NFE vs. 4000 NFE) while matching or exceeding prior SOTA on multi-coil MRI and 3D CT reconstruction makes diffusion-based solvers practically deployable in clinical settings where minutes-long reconstruction is unacceptable. The method's agnosticism to the forward operator (demonstrated on Cartesian, non-Cartesian/NUFFT, and sparse-view CT) and its freedom from step-size tuning address two major practical pain points of prior DIS methods. Open questions include whether the Krylov-subspace tangent-space assumption holds well enough for highly nonlinear forward operators, and whether the VE parameterization's numerical instability at high NFE can be resolved more fundamentally.
