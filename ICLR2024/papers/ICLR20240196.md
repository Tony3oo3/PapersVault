## Problem

Minimizing the maximum of N convex Lipschitz functions is a fundamental problem in robust optimization and machine learning, but no quantum algorithm or quantum lower bound existed for it. The best classical algorithm requires O(Nε^{-2/3} + ε^{-8/3}) first-order queries, and the question of whether quantum computing can reduce the dependence on N remained completely open.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes N convex L_f-Lipschitz functions accessed through a quantum zeroth-order oracle O_f that maps |i⟩|x⟩|y⟩ → |i⟩|x⟩|y + f_i(x)⟩, allowing queries in superposition over function indices. It also requires a target accuracy ε, an initial point x₀, and a domain bound R. The output is a point x⋆ satisfying F_max(x⋆) − min F_max ≤ ε with probability at least 2/3, using Õ(√N·ε^{-5/3} + ε^{-8/3}) oracle queries — a quadratic speedup in N over the classical O(Nε^{-2/3}) bound.

## Method

The algorithm follows the ball optimization acceleration framework of Carmon et al. (2021), using a softmax approximation of the max function and a Ball Regularized Optimization Oracle (BROO) implemented via Epoch-SGD-Proj. The critical quantum speedup replaces the classical bottleneck — sampling from the softmax distribution p_i ∝ exp(f_i(x̄)/ε′) which requires Ω(N) queries per sample — with a quantum Gibbs-sampling subroutine that prepares K copies of the state Σ√p_i|i⟩ using only O(√NK) oracle queries via QuantumMaximumFinding, QuantumStatePreparation, and AmplitudeAmplification. Gradients are obtained through a single-query quantum gradient estimation subroutine rather than a classical first-order oracle. For the lower bound, the paper introduces a multi-round unstructured search problem where each round's solution serves as a key unlocking the next, and shows via quantum progress control that making O(polylog(1/ε)) progress along a chain of length Ω(ε^{-2/3}) requires Õ(√N) queries per step, yielding the Õ(√N·ε^{-2/3}) lower bound.

## Results

The paper establishes a quantum algorithm for minimizing the maximum of N convex, Lipschitz functions with query complexity $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{N}\epsilon^{-5/3} + \epsilon^{-8/3})$ to a quantum zeroth-order oracle, achieving a quadratic speedup in N over the best classical algorithm. A matching quantum lower bound of $\tilde{\Omega}(\sqrt{N}\epsilon^{-2/3})$ is proven, establishing near-optimality in the dependence on N up to poly-logarithmic factors. The gap between the upper and lower bounds is a factor of $\epsilon^{-1}$ in the leading term, which remains an open question.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to the state-of-the-art classical algorithm of Carmon et al. (2021), which requires $O(N\epsilon^{-2/3}+\epsilon^{-8/3})$ first-order queries, the proposed quantum algorithm reduces the dependence on N from linear to square-root, yielding a quadratic quantum speedup in the number of functions. The $\epsilon^{-8/3}$ term remains unchanged, as it arises from the ball optimization acceleration framework rather than the sampling bottleneck. The classical subgradient method of Nesterov (2018) with $O(N\epsilon^{-2})$ complexity is strictly dominated by both the Carmon et al. algorithm and the present quantum algorithm. The quantum lower bound of $\tilde{\Omega}(\sqrt{N}\epsilon^{-2/3})$ matches the upper bound's dependence on N up to poly-logarithmic factors, confirming that the quadratic speedup in N is essentially optimal for quantum algorithms in this setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical ball optimization acceleration framework of Carmon et al. (2021) to the quantum setting, where the key bottleneck—sampling from the softmax distribution over N functions—is replaced by a quantum Gibbs sampling subroutine using amplitude amplification and top-K maximum finding. The outer optimization loop (Monteiro-Svaiter acceleration via BROO) and the inner stochastic optimization (Epoch-SGD-Proj from Hazan & Kale, 2014) remain classical in structure, with quantum speedup confined to the sampling step. For the lower bound, the paper adapts the quantum progress control method of Garg et al. (2021) to the zero-chain hard instance of Carmon et al. (2021), introducing a multi-round unstructured search construction to handle the challenge that random guessing provides polynomially-small but non-negligible success probability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first quantum algorithm with a matching (up to polylog factors) lower bound for minimizing the maximal loss, establishing that the quadratic speedup in N is essentially optimal. The gap between the upper bound (√N ε^{-5/3}) and lower bound (√N ε^{-2/3}) in ε remains open, and the authors note that exploiting smoothness of the individual f_i (as in the classical setting via accelerated variance reduction) could potentially close it, though the mean estimation subroutine resists quantum improvement. More broadly, this work represents a first attempt at quantum speedups for trust-region-based optimization methods, a direction that remains largely unexplored compared to gradient descent, cutting plane, and interior point methods.
