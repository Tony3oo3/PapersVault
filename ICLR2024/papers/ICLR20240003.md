## Problem

State-of-the-art perceptual similarity metrics like LPIPS require expensive human-annotated data or large pre-trained neural networks, while hand-crafted alternatives like MS-SSIM perform poorly on psychophysical benchmarks. This paper questions whether deep features are truly necessary, asking whether a simple linear prediction objective solved at inference time can capture perceptual semantics competitive with learned methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240003/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two RGB images (reference and distorted) of size 64×64×3 and produces, for each image, a perceptual embedding matrix W(x) ∈ R^{N×k} where N is the causal neighborhood size (e.g., N=12) and k = HWC = 1536 pixels. The final output is a scalar distance d(x,y) computed as the mean L2 norm of per-pixel embedding differences. All computation is performed at inference time with no training data, and the entire pipeline is differentiable, enabling gradient-based optimization such as the MAD competition.

## Method

For each pixel i in raster-scan order, LASI defines a causal neighborhood n_i of the N closest preceding pixels by L1 coordinate distance, then solves a weighted least squares problem (Eq. 1) where the weight ω^{ℓ_ij} decays exponentially with Manhattan distance, biasing the fit toward nearby pixels. The solution w_i ∈ R^N serves as the perceptual embedding for pixel i, and the distance between two images is the mean L2 norm of their per-pixel embedding differences. The key insight is that this WLS formulation is a sub-component of the GLICBAWLS lossless compression algorithm (Meyer & Tischer, 2001), and the prediction residual correlates strongly with downstream perceptual task performance—unlike deep features where classification accuracy can correlate negatively with perception. The pseudo-inverse is computed via SVD, giving O(N³) complexity per pixel that parallelizes across pixels, and increasing N consistently improves both the WLS loss and BAPPS scores without any tuning.

## Results

On the BAPPS-2AFC benchmark, LASI (N=12, data-free) achieves 73.8% (Trad.), 81.4% (CNN), 70.2% (SRes.), 60.5% (Deblur.), 62.1% (Color.), and 62.3% (Interp.), within 5.8% of the best unsupervised method in the worst case. On BAPPS-JND, LASI scores 55.9% (Trad.) and 63.2% (CNN) mAP. The method outperforms MS-SSIM on all six 2-AFC categories (up to +20.6% on Trad.) and on JND Trad. by +54.4%, while matching or exceeding unsupervised neural methods like PIM and A-DISTS on most subcategories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the supervised LPIPS, LASI trails by 1.2–6.9% on 2-AFC categories (LPIPS has an unfair advantage on Trad. and CNN since it was trained on those distortion types), but LASI wins on JND Trad. by 9.3% (55.9 vs. 46.9) and loses on JND CNN by 7.4% (63.2 vs. 67.9). Against the unsupervised PIM, LASI is within 5.8% in the worst case (JND CNN: 63.2 vs. 60.1, where LASI actually wins) and loses by only 1.9% on JND Trad. (55.9 vs. 57.8). Against A-DISTS, LASI is essentially tied on SRes. (70.2 vs. 70.8), Deblur. (60.5 vs. 60.2), and Color. (62.1 vs. 62.1), and wins on Interp. (62.3 vs. 61.6). Against the data-free MS-SSIM, LASI outperforms on all six 2-AFC categories (gains of +2.6% to +20.6%) and on JND Trad. by a dramatic +54.4% (55.9 vs. 36.2), while being marginally worse on JND CNN (−0.9%). Overall, LASI matches or exceeds the best unsupervised neural methods on most subcategories while requiring no training data, no pre-trained network, and no human annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes a sub-component of the GLICBAWLS lossless compression algorithm (Meyer & Tischer, 2001) — specifically its adaptive weighted least squares pixel prediction — as a perceptual embedding mechanism, a use far removed from its original compression context. It deliberately departs from the dominant paradigm of extracting deep features from pre-trained networks (LPIPS, PIM, A-DISTS) and from hand-crafted patch statistics (MS-SSIM), positioning itself as a data-free method that requires no training data, no human annotation, and no neural network. The autoregressive, causal-neighborhood formulation means each pixel's embedding is a solution to a WLS regression over all preceding pixels, weighted by spatial proximity, making the method a direct bridge between classical signal compression and modern perceptual similarity measurement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central claim — that a 20-year-old linear prediction algorithm, solved per-pixel at inference time, can match deep learned perceptual metrics within a few percent — challenges the field's working assumption that deep features are necessary for perceptual alignment. However, the evidence is scoped to 64×64×3 patches on the BAPPS benchmark, the margins over the best unsupervised methods are modest (2–5.8%), and the authors themselves note uncertainty about whether BAPPS generalizes beyond small patches. The MAD competition revealing complementary failure modes between LASI and LPIPS is a promising but qualitative hint that the two could be combined, a direction the authors leave open. Practical adoption is further limited by cubic scaling in neighborhood size and the absence of any demonstration on full-resolution images or downstream generative/compression tasks.
