## Problem

Large Language Models can learn new concepts during inference via in-context learning without any fine-tuning, but visual meta-learners have been unable to replicate this: they either require meta-training on similar tasks (in-domain) or fine-tuning on the support set (cross-domain), making them unsuitable for real-time, general-purpose deployment. This paper introduces the 'universal meta-learning' evaluation paradigm and an algorithm that closes this gap by learning new visual concepts in a single forward pass without meta-training or fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an n-way-k-shot support set of labeled RGB images and a single unlabeled query image. Each image is encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-base encoder into a 768-dimensional embedding; support labels are mapped through a fixed 256-dimensional ELMES encoder, while the query uses a learnable 'unknown token' embedding. The concatenated image-label vectors form a sequence fed into a ViT-Large non-causal Transformer, and the output vector at the query position is passed through a shallow MLP to produce a class label prediction.

## Method

CAML reformulates n-way-k-shot classification as non-causal sequence modeling over (image, label) support pairs and an unlabeled query, using three components: a frozen CLIP image encoder, a fixed ELMES class encoder (vectors of equal norm and maximally equiangular separation), and a ViT-Large Transformer encoder with bidirectional attention. The non-causal design preserves permutation invariance over the support set, unlike prior causal sequence approaches (SNAIL, GPICL) that impose an artificial ordering. The ELMES encoding is theoretically justified: it maximizes the probability that learnable attention heads unambiguously detect each class and minimizes detection entropy. The sequence model is pre-trained on episodic few-shot tasks from ImageNet-1k, Fungi, MSCOCO, and WikiArt with both the image encoder and ELMES encoder frozen, requiring only 302M trainable parameters. At inference, no meta-training on benchmark splits or fine-tuning on the support set is performed—classification occurs in a single forward pass.

## Results

CAML achieves 96.2% (5w-1s) and 98.6% (5w-5s) mean accuracy on MiniImageNet, exceeding the in-domain meta-trained P>M>F baseline (95.3% / 98.4%) without any meta-training or fine-tuning. Across 11 benchmarks, CAML outperforms or matches P>M>F on 8 of 11, with notable gains on tiered meta-iNat (81.9% vs 74.8% at 5w-1s), Pascal (82.6% vs 72.2%), and tiered-ImageNet (95.4% vs 93.5%). CAML underperforms P>M>F on CIFAR-fs (70.8% vs 84.3%), Aircraft (63.3% vs 79.8%), and ChestX (21.5% vs 27.0%), attributed to resolution mismatch and fine-grained class granularity limitations of the CLIP embedding space.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAML outperforms all five universal meta-learning baselines (ProtoNet, MetaOpt, MetaQDA, SNAIL, GPICL) on 14 of 22 evaluation settings (11 benchmarks × 2 shot settings), with the largest margins on CIFAR-fs 5w-1s (70.8% vs. next-best 62.9% ProtoNet) and Pascal 5w-1s (82.6% vs. 79.7% SNAIL). Against the in-domain P>M>F baseline (which meta-trains on each benchmark's training set), CAML exceeds or matches performance on 8 of 11 benchmarks, most impressively on tiered meta-iNat (+7.1 pts at 5w-1s) and Pascal (+10.4 pts at 5w-1s). CAML loses to P>M>F on CIFAR-fs (−13.5 pts at 5w-1s), Aircraft (−16.5 pts), and ChestX (−5.5 pts), which the authors attribute to resolution shift and CLIP's inability to capture fine-grained or domain-specific visual distinctions. Among the universal baselines, GPICL and SNAIL (causal sequence models) are the closest competitors on MiniImageNet but fall behind CAML on fine-grained and cross-domain tasks, supporting the paper's hypothesis that causal inductive biases are suboptimal for meta-learning. Freezing the CLIP backbone (CAML, MetaQDA, SNAIL, GPICL) consistently outperforms fine-tuning it (ProtoNet†, MetaOpt†) on out-of-domain benchmarks, confirming that preserving the pre-trained embedding space is critical for universal generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAML draws its core inspiration from in-context learning in LLMs (Brown et al., 2020), repurposing the 'classify a query from a context of demonstrations in a single forward pass' paradigm for visual few-shot classification. It departs from prior causal sequence-modeling meta-learners (SNAIL, GPICL) by using a non-causal Transformer encoder, arguing that causality breaks the permutation symmetry inherent in meta-learning. The ELMES label encoder connects to Equiangular Tight Frames and neural collapse literature, providing a theoretical justification for the label geometry. The 'universal meta-learning' evaluation paradigm is new, positioned between in-domain (meta-trained) and cross-domain (fine-tuned) settings, and explicitly targets the deployment constraints of a ChatGPT-like visual system.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a model with no meta-training or fine-tuning can match or exceed a meta-trained SOTA (P>M>F) on 8 of 11 benchmarks challenges the assumption that in-domain meta-training is necessary for strong few-shot performance, suggesting visual in-context learning may be a viable deployment path. The modular design—frozen foundation-model encoder plus a lightweight sequence model—means CAML automatically benefits from future improvements in visual foundation models. Key limitations remain: performance degrades on out-of-distribution domains (ChestX), low-resolution inputs (CIFAR-fs), and fine-grained classes where CLIP's embedding space lacks sufficient discriminative structure (Aircraft). Open questions include whether the universal setting can be extended beyond classification to dense prediction, and whether the ELMES constraint generalizes to open-vocabulary or variable-way settings.
