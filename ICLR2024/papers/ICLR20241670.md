## Problem

In Federated Learning, clients with noisy or corrupted local data degrade global model accuracy, and this effect is compounded under non-IID data distributions. Existing client selection methods either require additional client-side computations (breaking transparency) or rely on assumptions—such as a clean majority or noise being the sole quality factor—that fail in realistic heterogeneous settings. The paper addresses how to estimate client utility at the server using only a small auxiliary dataset, without imposing any extra operations on clients.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241670/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three server-side inputs: the top-layer weight vectors of each participating client's local model, a small well-labeled auxiliary dataset (e.g., 200 balanced samples from the test set) hosted entirely on the server, and a sparse round-reputation matrix that records whether each client's local model exceeded the round-average accuracy on the auxiliary data. The output is a utility score θ_j ∈ [0,1] per client, used with a threshold (e.g., 0.5) to select a subset for aggregation, along with updated discriminator parameters. Because the auxiliary data and all inference reside on the server, the approach is fully client-transparent—no modifications to local training or additional client-side reporting are needed.

## Method

FedTrans couples two complementary utility signals within a unified Bayesian framework: (1) a weight-based discriminator f^{W_d} (an MLP) trained on synthetic clean/corrupted clients generated from the auxiliary data, which maps top-layer weights to a utility estimate, and (2) a performance-based round-reputation matrix R that tracks whether each client's local model outperforms the round average on the auxiliary data. Round informativeness r_i is modeled as a Beta-distributed latent variable to down-weight early, less reliable rounds. A variational EM algorithm alternates between an E-step (coordinate ascent updating q(s_j) via the discriminator output and q(r_i) via the reputation matrix, using digamma-function-based update rules) and an M-step (training the discriminator via cross-entropy on the soft labels q(s_j) plus the known synthetic labels). The ablation confirms that the Bayesian coupling of both signals outperforms either strategy alone, and the convergence analysis shows the utility-guided selection maintains an O(1/(Tρ)) rate under standard smoothness and convexity assumptions.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (MobileNetV2, Dir(0.5), 30% noisy clients), FedTrans achieves 77.1% top-1 accuracy under Hybrid (across-) noise, outperforming the next-best baseline FedCorr (77.0%) and vanilla FedAvg (69.3%) by up to 7.8%. On Fashion-MNIST (LeNet-5, same settings), FedTrans reaches 88.7% under Hybrid (across-) noise, again leading all baselines. In terms of training efficiency, FedTrans reaches the target accuracy of 63% in 21.7 minutes versus 118.7 minutes for FedAvg, a reduction of over 56% in wall-clock time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedTrans consistently outperforms all seven baselines across all six noise configurations on both CIFAR-10 and Fashion-MNIST. The largest gains are over Oort (up to +14.0% on CIFAR-10 Hybrid across: 77.1% vs 63.1%) and FLDebugger (up to +12.1%), whose weight-distance and training-loss heuristics fail to approximate true client utility under non-IID noise. Among the stronger baselines, FedTrans edges out FedCorr (the closest competitor) by 3.2% on CIFAR-10 Hybrid (intra) (76.9% vs 73.7%) and by 1.4% on FMNIST Hybrid (across) (88.7% vs 87.3%), while matching FedCorr within 0.1% on CIFAR-10 Hybrid (across). FedTrans also surpasses the robust-objective methods Robust-FL and RHFL by 4.0% and 5.6% respectively on CIFAR-10 Hybrid (across), confirming that server-side client selection complements rather than replaces local robustness. Notably, fine-tuning DivFL or FedCorr on the auxiliary data actually degrades their performance (e.g., Fine-tuned FedCorr drops to 71.1% on CIFAR-10 Hybrid across), highlighting that naive use of auxiliary data is inferior to FedTrans's Bayesian utility inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedTrans carves new ground by unifying two previously separate client utility estimation strategies—weight-based discrimination (related to FLDebugger and FedCorr) and performance-based evaluation (related to Oort)—within a single Bayesian framework with variational EM inference. Unlike prior client selection methods that rely on assumptions about majority-correct updates or require client-side computations (e.g., LID scores in FedCorr), FedTrans is explicitly client-transparent, requiring only a small server-side auxiliary dataset. The variational inference approach draws on standard Bayesian tools (mean-field approximation, Beta-Bernoulli conjugacy) but applies them to the novel problem of jointly inferring client utility and round informativeness from a sparse round-reputation matrix.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedTrans addresses a practical deployment concern: real-world FL systems inevitably encounter noisy or corrupted clients, and the standard random-selection paradigm silently degrades global model quality. The client-transparent design makes it drop-in compatible with existing FL pipelines without modifying client code, which is a significant practical advantage over methods requiring client-side cooperation. However, the approach depends on obtaining a small labeled auxiliary dataset (costly in practice), and the per-round variational EM adds server-side computation. An open question is how well the method scales to thousands of clients and larger models, and whether the Bayesian framework's assumptions (e.g., Beta prior on round informativeness) hold under adversarial rather than natural noise.
