## Problem

The Information Bottleneck hypothesis—that DNN training proceeds through distinct fitting and compression phases—has only been verified for tiny networks or special model classes because mutual information estimation between high-dimensional random vectors requires exponentially many samples. This paper addresses the curse of dimensionality by proposing a lossy compression step that reduces data to its intrinsic manifold dimension before applying conventional entropy estimators, making IB analysis feasible for general, real-scale networks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes high-dimensional grayscale images (28×28 MNIST digits), stochastic layer outputs from a CNN classifier (made finite-entropy via Gaussian dropout with noise-to-signal ratio 10⁻³), and discrete target labels. The input is compressed by a convolutional autoencoder to a 4-dimensional latent code, while each layer's output is compressed via PCA to 4 principal components. The output is per-layer mutual information estimates I(X;Lᵢ) and I(Y;Lᵢ) with 95% asymptotic confidence intervals, rendered as information plane plots colored by per-epoch loss dynamics.

## Method

The framework combines three components: (1) stochastic NNs via Gaussian dropout after each layer to ensure finite MI between input and layer outputs; (2) lossy compression of both the input (convolutional autoencoder) and layer outputs (PCA) to reduce dimensionality from hundreds to 4, justified by the manifold hypothesis and the invariance of MI under injective smooth mappings (Statement 1); (3) classical entropy estimation on the compressed representations, with the weighted Kozachenko-Leonenko (WKL) estimator identified as best-performing. Theoretical bounds (Statement 3) quantify the MI loss under lossy compression as I(X;Y) ≤ I(f(X,Z);Y) ≤ I(X;Y) + h(Z) − h(Z|X,Y), showing the gap is controlled by the entropy of discarded components. A synthetic dataset generator with predefined MI values (via correlated Gaussians mapped through smooth nonlinear functions) validates the estimator against MINE, confirming that compression does not degrade accuracy when data lies on a low-dimensional manifold.

## Results

On synthetic high-dimensional datasets (16×16 and 32×32 images of 2D Gaussians and rectangles, 5000 samples), the Weighted Kozachenko-Leonenko (WKL) estimator combined with lossy compression achieves the lowest MI estimation error, while all four classical estimators (KDE ML, KDE LSE, KL, WKL) completely fail on uncompressed 16×16 data. In Table 2, WKL with PCA compression achieves MSE of 0.67 nats on 16×16 images, compared to 2.9×10¹ nats for uncompressed WKL and 4.1×10³ nats for KDE ML on 4×4 images. On the MNIST convolutional classifier, information plane analysis reveals multiple fitting/compression phases (rather than a single large-scale compression phase), with the first compression phase coinciding with an acceleration of the loss function decrease.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares five MI estimation approaches: KDE (ML and LSE bandwidth selection), original Kozachenko-Leonenko (KL), Weighted Kozachenko-Leonenko (WKL, Berrett et al. 2019), and MINE (Belghazi et al. 2018). WKL with lossy compression is the clear winner: it is the only method that produces meaningful MI estimates on 16×16 and 32×32 images, achieving MSE of 0.67 nats where all uncompressed classical estimators fail entirely. MINE, while theoretically capable of implicit compression via its critic network, exhibits training instability and is outperformed by the explicit compression + WKL pipeline on the synthetic benchmarks. The original KL estimator (Kozachenko & Leonenko, 1987) suffers from significant bias in dimensions above 3, which the WKL correction addresses; however, both KL and WKL without compression are rendered useless by the curse of dimensionality on images of 8×8 or larger. The key finding is that the compression step (via autoencoders or PCA) is what enables any of these estimators to function at all in the high-dimensional regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Information Bottleneck analysis framework of Shwartz-Ziv & Tishby (2017) and the stochastic NN approach of Goldfeld et al. (2019) by introducing an explicit lossy compression step (via autoencoders or PCA) before mutual information estimation, thereby addressing the curse of dimensionality that has restricted prior IB analysis to tiny networks or special model classes. It generalizes the entropy upper bound of Butakov et al. (2021) and the two-sided bounds of Greenewald et al. (2023) (which were limited to linear compression and smoothed distributions) to the general nonlinear compression setting, providing new theoretical statements on MI preservation under injective mappings and bounds under lossy compression. The key conceptual departure is treating the manifold hypothesis as a practical enabler: rather than relying on implicit structure exploitation by neural estimators like MINE, the paper explicitly learns the manifold and applies classical entropy estimators to the compressed representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded, practically implementable framework for performing IB analysis on general (not just toy-scale) neural networks, which is a long-standing gap in the field. The empirical finding that training may exhibit multiple alternating fitting/compression phases rather than the canonical two-phase structure challenges the original fitting-compression hypothesis, though this observation rests on a single MNIST experiment and is explicitly flagged as requiring further investigation. The connection between compression phases and loss-function dynamics (accelerated vs. stationary vs. decelerated decrease) is an intriguing but preliminary observation. Open questions include whether the multi-phase structure persists on larger, more complex architectures and datasets, and whether normalizing flows (suggested as future work) can tighten the compression bounds to make the approach more rigorous for high-dimensional settings.
