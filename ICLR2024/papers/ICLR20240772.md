## Problem

Source-free domain adaptation (SFDA) methods must adapt a pretrained model to an unlabeled target domain without access to source data. While data augmentation can enrich target domain information, existing approaches require prior knowledge of class-preserving transformations and incur significant memory and computational overhead when scaling the number of augmented samples. The paper asks how to harness the benefits of data augmentation in SFDA without explicit augmentation, prior knowledge, or additional computational cost.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained feature extractor and classifier (ResNet-50/101 for images, PointNet for point clouds) along with unlabeled target domain data in a closed-set setting. During adaptation, all target features and their predictions are stored in two memory banks to enable K-nearest-neighbor retrieval in feature space; pseudo-labels are assigned via argmax of classifier outputs and used to maintain online class-wise covariance estimates. The output is an adapted model (updated feature extractor and classifier) that produces class predictions for target domain samples, evaluated on 2D images (VisDA, DomainNet, Office-31, VisDA-RSUT) and 3D point clouds (PointDA-10).

## Method

SF(DA)² constructs an augmentation graph in the pretrained model's feature space where vertices are target features and edges connect K-nearest neighbors, encoding the intuition that semantically similar samples are neighbors and represent potential augmentation relationships. The spectral neighborhood clustering (SNC) loss performs spectral clustering on this graph in the prediction space, attracting predictions of neighbors while repelling all other predictions to form distinct clusters. To simulate an infinite number of augmented features without explicit sampling, the implicit feature augmentation (IFA) loss is derived as a closed-form upper bound on the expected SNC loss over features drawn from a Gaussian distribution parameterized by the target feature and its class-wise covariance, using Jensen's inequality and the moment-generating function of a Gaussian. A complementary feature disentanglement (FD) loss maximizes cosine distance between class covariance matrices to ensure the feature space encodes distinct class semantics along separate directions, making the IFA augmentation class-preserving. The final objective jointly optimizes SNC, IFA, and FD losses with hyperparameters α₁ and α₂.

## Results

On VisDA (ResNet-101), SF(DA)² achieves 88.1% per-class accuracy, outperforming the best source-free baselines AaD (87.3%) and CoWA-JMDS (87.0%). On DomainNet-126 (ResNet-50), it reaches 68.3% average accuracy across 7 domain shifts, surpassing SHOT (67.1%) and AaD (63.5%). On the 3D point cloud benchmark PointDA-10 (PointNet), SF(DA)² attains 57.1% average accuracy, exceeding AaD (55.9%) by 1.2%. On the highly imbalanced VisDA-RSUT dataset, it achieves 77.3% per-class accuracy, a 3.9% improvement over AaD (73.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SF(DA)² outperforms all source-free baselines across every evaluated dataset. On VisDA, it beats AaD by 0.8% and CoWA-JMDS by 1.1% in per-class accuracy, with the largest gains on the 'truck' class (64.7% vs. 54.6% for AaD). On DomainNet, the advantage is more pronounced: SF(DA)² exceeds SHOT by 1.2% and AaD by 4.8% in average accuracy, with consistent wins across all 7 domain shifts. On PointDA-10, it surpasses AaD by 1.2% and NRC by 4.5% average accuracy, and notably outperforms the source-present PointDAN (48.7%) by 8.4%. On the imbalanced VisDA-RSUT dataset, SF(DA)² leads AaD by 3.9% and NRC by 4.2%, with particularly strong gains on rare classes (e.g., 'plane': 79.0% vs. 73.9% for AaD). On Office-31, the margin is smaller (89.9% vs. 89.7% for AaD), but SF(DA)² still achieves the best source-free result. The method also outperforms two-stage baselines: SF(DA)²++ reaches 89.6% per-class on VisDA vs. 87.8% for feat-mixup + SHOT++.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes source-free domain adaptation (SFDA) through the lens of data augmentation, drawing on two distinct lineages. From spectral contrastive learning (HaoChen et al., 2021), it adopts the augmentation graph and spectral clustering framework but relocates the graph from input space to the feature space of a pretrained model, using K-nearest neighbors as implicit positive pairs rather than explicit augmentations. From ISDA (Wang et al., 2019), it inherits the idea of deriving a closed-form upper bound over an infinite number of augmented samples, but adapts it to the unlabeled, pseudo-labeled SFDA setting by bounding the expected SNC loss rather than cross-entropy. The method departs from contrastive-learning-based SFDA approaches (DaC, AdaContrast) that require prior knowledge of class-preserving transformations, and from neighborhood-consistency methods (NRC, AaD) by adding a principled spectral clustering objective and implicit augmentation regularizers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The IFA and FD losses offer a practical plug-and-play mechanism: they can be added to existing SFDA methods (SHOT, NRC, AaD, CoWA-JMDS) to yield consistent improvements without requiring explicit data augmentation or prior knowledge of class-preserving transformations. This addresses a real practical bottleneck in SFDA where augmentation strategies are dataset-specific and computationally expensive. Open questions include whether the covariance-based augmentation direction remains reliable when pseudo-labels are highly noisy (early adaptation or extreme imbalance), whether the closed-set assumption limits applicability, and whether the spectral clustering objective generalizes to open-set or multi-source settings. The method's reliance on a well-structured pretrained feature space also raises questions about performance when the source model is weak or the domain shift is very large.
