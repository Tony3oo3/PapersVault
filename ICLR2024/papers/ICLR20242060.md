## Problem

Previous upper bounds for single-channel speech separation were derived exclusively for deterministic models, leaving open whether generative (diffusion) models could exceed these limits. This paper generalizes the bound to random generative models, showing a maximum 3 dB improvement is achievable, and empirically demonstrates that a pretrained diffusion vocoder applied to a deterministic separator's output can surpass the classical deterministic upper bound.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242060/cropped_page7_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-channel mixture of C speakers (C ∈ {2, 3, 5, 10, 20}) at 16 kHz and produces C separated waveforms. Internally, the deterministic separator's output is converted to a Mel-spectrogram to condition the diffusion vocoder, and the final combination of deterministic and generative estimates is performed in the STFT frequency domain using learned complex weights, since the two signals have unaligned phases (the vocoder receives only magnitude information).

## Method

The pipeline chains three components: a pretrained deterministic separator B (SepFormer or Gated-LSTM), a pretrained DiffWave diffusion vocoder GM conditioned on the Mel-spectrogram of B's output, and a learned 6-layer residual CNN F that produces complex mixing coefficients α and β in the frequency domain. F receives as input the concatenated magnitudes and the phase difference between the two estimates, and is trained end-to-end with SI-SDR loss (using Hungarian assignment for C ≥ 10) while B and GM remain frozen. The key insight is that the stochastic nature of the diffusion model provides independent error samples that, when combined with the deterministic estimate via learned frequency-domain weights, can recover information lost by either model alone. Theoretically, the paper proves that the mutual information between the combined estimate and the true source is bounded by 2·I(v; m), yielding a tight 3 dB upper bound over the deterministic limit.

## Results

The proposed method (SepFormer + DiffWave with learned combination) achieves 23.9 dB SI-SDRi on WSJ0 2Mix, surpassing the classical deterministic upper bound of 23.1 dB. On LibriSpeech, GatedLSTM + DiffWave improves from 12.7 dB to 14.2 dB (5Mix), 7.7 dB to 9.0 dB (10Mix), and 4.3 dB to 5.2 dB (20Mix). Averaging five DiffWave runs with different noise seeds yields a small but consistent additional gain (e.g., 24.1 dB on WSJ0 2Mix, 14.4 dB on Libri 5Mix). The derived generative upper bound is 3 dB above the deterministic bound, and the method approaches but does not fully reach this bound for multi-speaker mixtures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SepFormer (22.3 dB on WSJ0 2Mix), the proposed SepFormer + DiffWave achieves 23.9 dB, a 1.6 dB gain, and the 5-run average reaches 24.1 dB, breaking the classical deterministic upper bound of 23.1 dB. Against SepIt (22.4 dB on WSJ0 2Mix, 13.7 dB on Libri 5Mix), the method gains 1.5 dB and 0.5 dB respectively. Against GatedLSTM (12.7 dB on Libri 5Mix, 7.7 dB on Libri 10Mix, 4.3 dB on Libri 20Mix), the method improves by 1.5 dB, 1.3 dB, and 0.9 dB respectively. DiffSep, the only other diffusion-based separation method, achieves only 14.3 dB on WSJ0 2Mix, far below both the proposed method and all deterministic baselines. The ablation with deterministic HiFiGAN shows no significant improvement over the SepFormer baseline, confirming that the gain specifically requires a non-deterministic (stochastic) generative model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deterministic source separation upper bound established by Lutati et al. (2022, SepIt) to the setting of stochastic generative models, showing that a pretrained diffusion vocoder (DiffWave) applied to the output of a deterministic separator can exceed the previously proven bound by up to 3 dB. Conceptually, it departs from DiffSep (Scheibler et al., 2022), which uses a diffusion process as the separation mechanism itself, by instead treating the diffusion model as a post-hoc denoising/refinement stage on top of an existing deterministic backbone (SepFormer or Gated-LSTM). The only learned component is a small convolutional network that infers frequency-domain mixing weights between the deterministic and generative estimates, making the approach a lightweight plug-in rather than a new separation architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a frozen, pretrained diffusion vocoder can be bolted onto any existing deterministic separator with minimal additional training to push past the deterministic performance ceiling, which is a low-cost recipe for practitioners. The theoretical contribution—a tight 3 dB generalization of the deterministic bound under Gaussian noise assumptions—clarifies how much headroom generative models can provide and when (the bound is tight when the vocoder's reconstruction error is below 10⁻²). Limitations include the assumption of sequential processing of uncorrelated chunks (invalidating the bound for full-signal methods like TF-GridNet), the modest absolute gains (1–1.5 dB on 2-speaker mixtures), and the fact that the method requires the diffusion model to be pretrained on the same domain as the target speech. Whether the bound generalizes to other signal domains (images, music) is left as future work.
