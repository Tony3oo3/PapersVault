## Problem

Existing continual learning methods assume either unlimited computational budget or fully labeled data streams, neither of which holds in real-world deployment where data arrives in massive volumes with very sparse annotations and strict per-step time constraints. Supervised CL methods overfit to the few available labels, while semi-supervised CL methods require so many passes over unlabeled data that they collapse under realistic budget limits. The paper introduces 'CL on Diet,' a new setting that simultaneously imposes sparse labeling and a fixed per-time-step computational budget, exposing the failure modes of both supervised and semi-supervised baselines.

## Input / Output

At each time step t, the model receives n^t RGB images from a non-stationary stream, of which only a small fraction (0.5%–10%) carry class labels; the remaining images are unlabeled. The computational budget is specified as a fixed number of forward-backward passes (training iterations) per time step, and the model must update within that budget. The architecture is a ViT encoder (pre-trained on ImageNet-1k) with a classification head for labeled data and an additional MAE reconstruction head for unlabeled data; a task-balanced buffer stores all previously seen labeled samples for replay. Output is a single class label per image, evaluated on the union of all test sets seen so far.

## Method

DietCL jointly optimizes three losses in a single training loop: (1) an MAE reconstruction loss on unlabeled current-step images to capture the in-distribution structure, (2) a masked cross-entropy loss on labeled current-step images where logits for classes not introduced at the current time step are zeroed out, and (3) a standard cross-entropy loss on a task-balanced buffer of all previously seen labeled data to combat forgetting. The key mechanism is a budget allocation strategy: below a cross-validated threshold B, the total per-step budget is split equally among the three losses; above B, the surplus is devoted exclusively to buffer replay, preventing overfitting to the sparse current labels while still allowing the model to consolidate prior knowledge. This joint (rather than sequential) training of labeled and unlabeled data avoids the slow convergence of separate SSL pre-training stages and the overfitting of purely supervised replay, yielding faster convergence and more stable accuracy across the stream.

## Results

On the 20-split ImageNet10k benchmark (1% label rate, 500 steps), DietCL achieves 16.82% last-step accuracy (A(T)) and 24.90% average accuracy (A), outperforming the best supervised method Replay (14.89% / 22.84%) and the best semi-supervised method CaSSLe (5.78% / 13.25%). On CLOC (0.5% label rate, 1000 steps), DietCL reaches 5.63% / 4.98%, and on CGLM (5% label rate, 600 steps), 24.34% / 20.26%. DietCL approaches the joint-training upper bound (18.23% on ImageNet10k) more closely than any other method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DietCL outperforms all ten baselines across all three datasets. Against the strongest supervised method, Replay/ER, DietCL gains +1.93% A(T) on ImageNet10k (16.82 vs 14.89), +0.99% on CLOC (5.63 vs 4.64), and +1.53% on CGLM (24.34 vs 22.81). The gap is most dramatic against semi-supervised methods: CaSSLe, the best semi-supervised baseline, achieves only 5.78% A(T) on ImageNet10k versus DietCL's 16.82% (a 2.9× gap), because CaSSLe's original 500-epoch training regime collapses under the 500-step budget. DualNet and CCIC, which rely on inter-class relationships, struggle with the large number of classes (10K+) and score below 1% on ImageNet10k. Among regularization-based methods, EWC and MAS are weakest (2.19% and 6.73% on ImageNet10k), while L2P (6.30%) and GDumb (4.64%) also fall well short. ER-ACE (10.72%) is the second-best supervised method but still trails DietCL by 6.1% on ImageNet10k.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two recently developed lines of continual learning research: budgeted CL (Prabhu et al., 2023), which imposed per-task computational limits but assumed fully labeled streams, and semi-supervised CL (CaSSLe, DualNet, CCIC), which leveraged unlabeled data but assumed unlimited computation. DietCL departs from both by jointly optimizing labeled and unlabeled data under a fixed per-step FLOP budget, using MAE reconstruction as the self-supervised signal rather than contrastive learning (which the authors show wastes half the budget on dual-view augmentation). The replay-buffer mechanism and masked classification loss build directly on ER and ER-ACE, while the budget-allocation threshold strategy is a practical addition not present in prior budgeted CL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a practically critical gap: real-world streaming systems (social media, surveillance) face both tight processing deadlines and expensive annotation, yet prior CL methods were validated under neither constraint simultaneously. The dramatic collapse of CaSSLe (5.78% vs. 16.82% on ImageNet10k) under a 500-step budget highlights that existing semi-supervised CL methods are effectively unusable in deployment-scale settings. Open questions include whether the MAE-based joint training generalizes to non-ViT backbones or video streams, whether the cross-validated budget threshold remains stable across domains, and whether more sophisticated allocation strategies (e.g., adaptive per-source weighting) could close the remaining gap to the joint-training upper bound.
