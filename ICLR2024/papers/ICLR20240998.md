## Problem

Previous theoretical works analyzed the effects of task similarity and overparameterization on catastrophic forgetting in isolation, leaving unclear how these two factors interact. This paper addresses that gap by deriving an exact analytical model showing that the relationship between task similarity and forgetting is regime-dependent: non-monotonic (peaking at intermediate similarity) in highly overparameterized models, but monotonic (worst at maximal dissimilarity) near the interpolation threshold.

## Input / Output

The model takes two p-dimensional data matrices X₁, X₂ ∈ R^{n×p} where X₂ = X₁O with O a random orthogonal operator rotating X₁ in a random m-dimensional subspace, a shared label vector y ∈ Rⁿ, and dimensionality parameters p (ambient), d = rank(X₁), and m (transformed subspace size). The output is a closed-form polynomial expression in α = m/p (task dissimilarity proxy) and β = 1 − d/p (overparameterization proxy) for the normalized worst-case expected forgetting, with an O(1/p) finite-size correction. The normalization factor ||X||₂²||X⁺Xw*||² accounts for the natural scaling of forgetting with data magnitude and solution norm.

## Method

The paper analyzes two-task continual linear regression trained sequentially with (S)GD, where the second task's data is a random orthogonal transformation of the first in an m-dimensional subspace (the DOTS parameter). The forgetting is upper-bounded by decomposing it into two key expectations of squared monomials involving entries of random orthogonal matrices Q_p and Q_m, which are evaluated exactly using known integral formulas over orthogonal groups (Gorin, 2002). The bound is sharp—saturating when all nonzero singular values of X are identical—yielding an exact worst-case expression. The resulting polynomial reveals that at β→1 (high overparameterization), forgetting equals α²(1−α)² peaking at α=0.5, while at β=0 (interpolation threshold) it reduces to 2α, monotonically decreasing with similarity. Empirical validation uses synthetic linear regression matching the analytical curves and 2-layer MLPs (width 400 and 20/40) on permuted MNIST and EMNIST with varying permutation sizes to confirm the regime-dependent behavior in neural networks.

## Results

The paper's main result (Theorem 3) provides an exact non-asymptotic analytical expression for worst-case expected forgetting as a function of task dissimilarity (α = m/p) and overparameterization (β = 1 − d/p). In the highly overparameterized regime (β→1), forgetting equals α²(1−α)², peaking at α = 0.5 with a value of 0.0625, confirming that intermediate task similarity causes the most forgetting. Near the interpolation threshold (β = 0), forgetting reduces to 2α, exhibiting a monotonic decrease with increasing task similarity. Empirical validation on permuted MNIST with a width-400 2-layer MLP confirms the cap-shaped (non-monotonic) forgetting curve, with peak forgetting occurring around permutation size PS ∈ [16, 24], while a less overparameterized model (width 20) shows a flatter, more uniform forgetting profile.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against competing continual learning algorithms; instead, it positions its theoretical framework relative to prior analytical works. Compared to Evron et al. (2022), who showed intermediate task similarity (45° principal angle) is worst for arbitrary deterministic tasks, this paper resolves the apparent contradiction by showing that the randomness of the orthogonal transformation (Eq. 1) causes the effective principal angle to depend on overparameterization: near the interpolation threshold (p=2, d=1), the expected angle is ~45° (matching Evron et al.), while in the highly overparameterized limit (p→∞), the expected angle approaches 90° (maximal dissimilarity, minimal forgetting). Compared to Goldfarb & Hand (2023), who analyzed only the fully-rotated case (m=p, α=1), this paper generalizes their result by introducing the DOTS parameter α, showing their model is a special case. Compared to Lin et al. (2023), whose generalization bounds assume a data model that degenerates to a null predictor in the high-overparameterization limit, this paper's training-error analysis remains valid across all overparameterization levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-task linear regression framework of Evron et al. (2022) by introducing a structured random orthogonal transformation (parameterized by DOTS = m/p) that captures task similarity, and builds on Goldfarb & Hand (2023) by generalizing their full-rotation (m=p) model to partial rotations. The key conceptual departure is showing that task similarity and overparameterization interact jointly rather than acting independently: prior works analyzed each factor in isolation, while this paper derives an exact closed-form expression for their combined effect on worst-case forgetting. The DOTS parameter also provides a more natural similarity measure for permutation benchmarks than the principal-angle or teacher-distance notions used in Doan et al. (2021) or Lin et al. (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that the similarity-forgetting relationship is non-monotonic (intermediate similarity worst) only in highly overparameterized regimes, but monotonic near the interpolation threshold—resolves an apparent contradiction with Evron et al. (2022) and challenges the common narrative that intermediate similarity is universally the hardest case. The result that even extreme overparameterization (β→1) leaves non-vanishing forgetting (0.0625 at α=0.5) cautions against treating width alone as a forgetting remedy. Open questions include extending the analysis to T≥3 tasks, nonlinear models, and classification settings, as well as rigorously connecting the linear DOTS parameter to NTK feature similarity in deep networks.
