## Problem

Existing multivariate long-term time series forecasting (LTSF) Transformers consistently underperform simpler univariate models, despite having access to inter-series information. The paper argues this stems from an inability to handle pronounced series-wise characteristic differences—varying temporal dependencies, local patterns, and inter-series relationships—causing the model to incorrectly blend distinct series in attention and embedding layers, leading to overfitting and erroneous pattern learning.

## Input / Output

ARM takes a multivariate time series input window X_I ∈ R^{L_I × C} (C series, L_I timesteps) and produces a forecast X_P ∈ R^{L_P × C} for all C series simultaneously. Unlike baseline models that require tuning L_I per prediction horizon, ARM uses a fixed lookback of L_I=720 (104 for ILI) across all horizons (96–720 steps, or 24–60 for ILI). The output is a single multivariate forecast vector per series, with per-series adaptive normalization (learnable EMA mean and multi-window standard deviation) applied before and after the encoder-decoder.

## Method

ARM wraps a vanilla Transformer encoder-decoder with three modules that address series-wise heterogeneity. (1) Adaptive Univariate Effect Learning (AUEL) pre-processes each series with a per-series learnable EMA for output mean, a multi-window weighted standard deviation, and a Mixture-of-Experts predictor that initializes temporal patterns for the forecast region, so the encoder-decoder can focus parameters on inter-series dependencies rather than output levels. (2) Random Dropping, applied only during training, simultaneously zeros a random subset of series in both input and target, constructing an implicit ensemble over all subset-to-subset forecasting relationships and preventing the model from overfitting to incorrect inter-series correlations. (3) Multi-kernel Local Smoothing (MKLS) applies multiple 1D convolutions with different kernel sizes plus a channel-wise cross-attention to compute per-series kernel weights, building reasonable multivariate temporal representations; it is used both as Pre-MKLS (before the Transformer to construct local representations) and Post-MKLS (in parallel with the Transformer to provide adjustable local attention). All three modules are plug-and-play and can be attached to other LTSF architectures (PatchTST, DLinear, Autoformer, Informer) without structural changes.

## Results

ARM (Vanilla) achieves the best MSE across all 10 benchmark datasets at every prediction horizon. On the Multi dataset (strong inter-series dependencies), ARM achieves MSE 0.032 at L_P=96, outperforming the best baseline Informer (0.092) by 65%. On large-scale datasets like Electricity (96), ARM reaches MSE 0.125 vs. PatchTST's 0.129 and DLinear's 0.140. On ETTm1 (96), ARM achieves 0.287 vs. PatchTST 0.293 and Autoformer 0.510, demonstrating consistent gains across both weak- and strong-dependency regimes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARM outperforms all five baselines on every dataset and horizon. PatchTST is the closest competitor, with ARM winning by narrow margins on most datasets (e.g., Electricity 96: 0.125 vs. 0.129; ETTm1 96: 0.287 vs. 0.293), but the gap widens substantially on datasets with strong inter-series dependencies (Multi 96: 0.032 vs. 0.072, a 56% MSE reduction). DLinear is competitive on Exchange (0.081 vs. ARM 0.078) but falls behind on all other datasets. FEDformer, Autoformer, and Informer are consistently outperformed by large margins, with Autoformer and Informer particularly struggling on ETT datasets (e.g., ETTm1 96: Autoformer 0.510, Informer 0.626 vs. ARM 0.287). The Repeat (last-value) baseline is worst overall, confirming that learned temporal modeling is essential. Notably, on the Multi dataset designed with explicit inter-series causal structure, ARM's 0.032 MSE far exceeds even the best univariate model (DLinear 0.067), validating that proper multivariate modeling recovers dependencies that channel-independent approaches miss.

## Relation to Prior Work

ARM builds on the vanilla Transformer encoder-decoder as its core architecture and incorporates a Mixture-of-Experts module (inspired by Switch Transformer) for adaptive temporal pattern learning. Its AUEL module generalizes RevIN's fixed instance normalization into a learnable adaptive EMA, while its Random Dropping strategy and MKLS module address training and representation issues that prior multivariate LTSF Transformers (Informer, Autoformer, FEDformer) left unaddressed. The paper's central positioning departs from the recent line of work (DLinear, PatchTST) that concluded Transformers are ineffective for LTSF by arguing the failure lies not in the architecture but in how multivariate inputs with heterogeneous series characteristics are processed and trained.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ARM provides a modular, plug-and-play framework (A/R/M) that can be integrated into existing LTSF architectures without structural changes, offering a practical path to closing the univariate-vs-multivariate performance gap. The synthetic 'Multi' dataset serves as a useful diagnostic benchmark for inter-series dependency learning. However, the improvements over strong univariate baselines (PatchTST, DLinear) remain modest on most real-world datasets, and the method's reliance on a relatively small model dimension (d=16 or 64) raises questions about scalability to very high-dimensional multivariate settings. Open questions include whether the MoE component is essential versus simpler per-series predictors, and whether the approach generalizes to streaming or probabilistic forecasting settings.
