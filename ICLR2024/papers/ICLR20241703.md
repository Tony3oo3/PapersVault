## Problem

Existing adversarial defenses either retrain classifiers or apply fixed purification modules, but they assume the input image is a fixed discrete signal. In the real world, observers can recapture or rerender a scene from slightly different viewpoints, which naturally disrupts adversarial textures while preserving semantics. Naive image resampling (bilinear/nearest interpolation with uniform or random shifts) partially mitigates attacks but severely degrades clean accuracy without substantially improving robust accuracy, leaving open the question of how to resample in a way that selectively removes perturbations while preserving semantic content.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet) that may be clean or adversarially perturbed, and produces a resampled image of the same spatial dimensions. Internally, the pipeline first encodes the image into pixel-wise feature embeddings, then predicts a 2D shift vector for every pixel via SampleNet, and finally queries the implicit continuous representation at the shifted coordinates to synthesize the output image. The resampled image is then fed to any pre-trained classifier (e.g., WRN28-10, ResNet-50) without modifying the classifier itself, making IRAD a plug-and-play test-time defense.

## Method

IRAD consists of two components trained in a two-stage procedure. First, an implicit continuous representation is built using a local implicit image function: a deep encoder (EDSR) extracts pixel-wise embeddings, and a 5-layer MLP φ predicts the color at any continuous coordinate by aggregating neighboring pixel embeddings weighted by spatial distance. This representation is trained via PGD-based adversarial denoising, which teaches it to smooth out high-frequency adversarial perturbations while retaining semantic structure. Second, SampleNet—a 5-layer MLP that takes a pixel's feature embedding and its integer coordinates as input—predicts a pixel-wise 2D shift vector, replacing the fixed or random shifts of naive resampling. SampleNet is trained after freezing the implicit representation, using cross-entropy loss on both clean and adversarial predictions. The key insight is that the implicit representation inherently suppresses adversarial textures (high-frequency components), while SampleNet learns input-dependent shifts that further realign the resampled image's decision path with that of the clean image. The method can also be combined with DiffPure at reduced time steps (e.g., t=20 instead of t=100), achieving comparable robust accuracy at roughly 5× lower inference cost.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (AutoAttack, ε∞=8/255), IRAD achieves 91.70% standard accuracy (SA) and 89.72% robust accuracy (RA), yielding the highest average of 90.71% among all compared methods. On CIFAR-100, IRAD reaches 76.01% SA and 72.49% RA (Avg 74.25%), and on ImageNet (ε∞=4/255) it attains 72.14% SA and 71.60% RA (Avg 71.87%). IRAD outperforms the two SOTA purification methods—DISCO (85.63% RA on CIFAR-10) and DiffPure (87.54% RA on CIFAR-10)—while also preserving higher clean-image accuracy than both.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IRAD outperforms all seven testing-time baselines (Bit Reduction, JPEG, Randomization, Median Filter, NRP, STL) by a wide margin on both RA and Avg across all three datasets; for example, on CIFAR-10 IRAD's 89.72% RA exceeds JPEG's 50.79% and STL's 57.48%. Against the two SOTA purification methods, IRAD beats DISCO by +4.09% RA and +3.27% Avg on CIFAR-10, and by +3.97% RA and +4.09% Avg on ImageNet, while also maintaining ~2.5% higher SA than DISCO. Compared to DiffPure, IRAD achieves +2.18% RA and +2.10% Avg on CIFAR-10 with ~200× faster inference (0.68 ms vs. 132.8 ms per image). Under the adaptive BPDA attack (Table 3), IRAD still leads all baselines except DiffPure on CIFAR-10, achieving 74.32% RA versus DISCO's 22.60%. In the RobustBench comparison (Table 9), the IRAD+DiffPure(t=20) hybrid reaches 83.74% Avg on CIFAR-10, surpassing the top training-time methods such as Robust_Peng (82.17%) and Stable_Kang (82.51%) while using a standard (non-adversarially-trained) WideResNet-28-10 classifier.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes image resampling—a classical computer graphics operation (Dodgson, 1992)—as a testing-time adversarial defense, a connection not previously explored in the adversarial ML literature. It builds directly on the local implicit image representation of Chen et al. (2021), which DISCO (Ho & Vasconcelos, 2022) already leveraged for adversarial purification, but adds a learned sampling step (SampleNet) that predicts pixel-wise coordinate shifts, going beyond DISCO's fixed-coordinate reconstruction. The paper also extends the diffusion-based DiffPure (Nie et al., 2022) by using IRAD as a post-processing stage, reducing required diffusion steps from 100 to 20 while preserving defense quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IRAD offers a very fast (0.68 ms/image) testing-time defense that generalizes across architectures (WRN, VGG, ResNet) and datasets without retraining, making it practical for deployment where adversarial training is infeasible. The IRAD+DiffPure(t=20) hybrid achieves near-full-DiffPure robustness at 5× lower latency, a practically significant efficiency gain. However, the method's vulnerability under adaptive attacks (RA drops to 0% under AutoAttack-based adaptive adversary, and to 74% under BPDA on CIFAR-10) remains a critical open question, and the paper itself suggests combining resampling with adversarial training or denoising for stronger guarantees. Whether the resampling paradigm extends to 3D or video settings is unexplored.
