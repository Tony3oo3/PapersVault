## Problem

Existing efficient fine-tuning methods for CLIP (CoOp, CLIP-Adapter, Tip-Adapter-F) achieve strong few-shot performance but still require gradient-based training of additional parameters, consuming computational resources that are undesirable on constrained devices. This paper asks whether a completely training-free approach—requiring no SGD, no learnable parameters, and no back-propagation—can match or surpass these training-required methods on few-shot classification, imbalanced learning, and out-of-distribution generalization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images encoded by a frozen CLIP visual encoder (ResNet-50, ResNet-101, ViT-B/32, or ViT-B/16), a small set of labeled training images (1–16 per class by default), and class-name text prompts fed through CLIP's text encoder to produce a zero-shot classifier. The output is a set of per-class logits computed as a weighted sum of the zero-shot text-based logits and a GDA-derived linear classifier, followed by softmax to yield class probabilities. No gradient updates are performed; the only 'fitting' step is computing per-class mean vectors and a shared precision matrix from the few-shot features, which takes approximately 1.6 seconds on a single GPU for 16-shot ImageNet.

## Method

The core mechanism applies Gaussian Discriminant Analysis to CLIP visual features: assuming each class's features follow a Gaussian with a shared covariance, Bayes' rule yields a linear classifier whose weight (Σ⁻¹μᵢ) and bias (log pᵢ − ½μᵢᵀΣ⁻¹μᵢ) are closed-form functions of the class means and precision matrix, eliminating any need for gradient-based optimization. To handle the high-dimensional, small-sample precision matrix estimation, the paper uses the empirical Bayes ridge-type estimator (Kubokawa & Srivastava, 2008), which adds a trace-based regularization to the empirical covariance before inversion, avoiding numerical instability without requiring an optimization loop. The final prediction ensembles the GDA linear classifier with CLIP's zero-shot text classifier via a single hyperparameter α that weights the two logit vectors, integrating visual and textual modality knowledge. For base-to-new generalization, KNN with text embeddings synthesizes pseudo-labeled data for unseen classes; for unsupervised learning, the EM algorithm estimates the GMM parameters directly from unlabeled features, both as minimal modifications that preserve the training-free spirit.

## Results

On 16-shot few-shot classification across 11 datasets, the method achieves 76.05% average accuracy without any training, surpassing the best training-free baseline APE (73.23%) by 2.82% and matching or exceeding the best training-required method Tip-Adapter-F (75.83%) by 0.22%. On out-of-distribution generalization (trained on ImageNet, tested on 4 OOD sets), it achieves 60.37% average, outperforming Tip-Adapter-F (60.21%) and Tip-Adapter (60.08%). On imbalanced learning, it achieves 62.34% overall accuracy on ImageNet-LT and 42.07% on Places-LT, surpassing all fully fine-tuned baselines including Full Fine-tune + Balanced Softmax (60.47% and 37.81% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against training-free baselines, the method wins decisively: it outperforms Zero-Shot CLIP by 17.28%, CALIP by 16.60%, Tip-Adapter by 5.73%, Tip-X by 4.65%, and APE by 2.82% on average across 11 datasets (16-shot). Against training-required methods, it achieves 76.05% vs. Tip-Adapter-F's 75.83% (best training-required), CLIP-Adapter's 74.44%, and CoOp's 73.42%, while requiring zero training time (1.6 sec vs. 6 min for Tip-Adapter-F). On OOD generalization, it edges out Tip-Adapter-F (60.37% vs. 60.21%) and Tip-Adapter (60.08%) without any training. On imbalanced learning, it surpasses all fully fine-tuned baselines including Full Fine-tune + Balanced Softmax (62.34% vs. 60.47% on ImageNet-LT; 42.07% vs. 37.81% on Places-LT), primarily through improved medium- and few-shot accuracy. On base-to-new generalization, it achieves 78.72% harmonic mean vs. KgCoOp (77.00%) and CoCoOp (75.83%). On unsupervised learning, it marginally outperforms UPL (63.46% vs. 63.38%) and POUF (58.98%) on average.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Gaussian Discriminant Analysis (GDA), a classical closed-form probabilistic classifier from Bishop's textbook, for CLIP-based downstream adaptation. It departs from the dominant paradigm of prompt learning (CoOp, CoCoOp) and adapter methods (CLIP-Adapter, Tip-Adapter-F), all of which require gradient-based training, by instead computing class means and a shrinkage-estimated shared covariance (via the Kubokawa–Srivastava empirical Bayes ridge estimator) to derive a linear classifier in closed form. The method also ensembles this GDA classifier with CLIP's original zero-shot text-based classifier, integrating visual and textual modality knowledge without any learned parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for resource-constrained deployment: adaptation requires only 1.6 seconds of computation (vs. minutes of GPU training for competing methods) and zero learnable parameters, making it a genuinely training-free baseline that is difficult to surpass. The identical-covariance GDA assumption provides an implicit mechanism for transferring distributional knowledge from many-shot to few-shot classes, which explains its strong performance on imbalanced learning. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dense prediction tasks (segmentation, detection), whether the shared-covariance assumption holds in more heterogeneous settings, and how it interacts with test-time adaptation or larger foundation models beyond CLIP.
