## Problem

Prior mathematics-specialized language models were either closed-access (Minerva) or significantly lagged behind the closed state-of-the-art, limiting their utility as a research platform. The paper asks whether continued pretraining on a carefully curated, open mixture of mathematical text and code can produce an open base model that matches or exceeds closed models like Minerva on an equi-parameter basis while also generalizing to tool use and formal theorem proving without task-specific finetuning.

## Input / Output

As a base model, LLEMMA is evaluated via few-shot prompting (4-shot for MATH, 8-shot for GSM8k, 3-shot for Lean tactic prediction) with a 4096-token context window. For chain-of-thought solving, it takes a LaTeX or natural-language problem statement and generates a self-contained LaTeX solution ending in a boxed answer; for tool use, it alternates natural-language reasoning steps with executable Python code; for formal proving, it either generates a full Isabelle proof from a formal statement plus informal proof, or predicts the next Lean tactic given a proof state. Outputs are checked by exact string match, SymPy equivalence, or proof-assistant verification.

## Method

LLEMMA is obtained by continued pretraining Code Llama 7B and 34B on Proof-Pile-2, a 55B-token mixture of mathematical code (AlgebraicStack, 11B tokens across 17 languages including Lean, Isabelle, Coq, Python, and Julia), mathematical web text (OpenWebMath, 15B tokens), and arXiv papers (29B tokens), with a 2:4:1 arXiv:web:code ratio selected by minimizing MATH training-set perplexity. A small amount of general-domain data (2% Pile, 3% RedPajama GitHub) is included as regularization. The 7B model is trained for 200B tokens and the 34B for 50B tokens using bfloat16 mixed precision on 256 A100 GPUs with Tensor Parallelism, ZeRO Stage 1, and Flash Attention 2. For the 7B model, the RoPE base period is contracted from 1,000,000 to 10,000 to enable future long-context extension. The key design choice is including formal proof-step data (Lean and Isabelle) and mathematical code alongside natural-language math text, which allows the model to acquire tool-use and formal-proving capabilities purely through the autoregressive language modeling objective without any task-specific finetuning.

## Results

On the MATH benchmark (greedy decoding, 4-shot), LLEMMA-34B achieves 25.0% accuracy, outperforming its Code Llama-34B initialization (12.2%) by 13 points and the proprietary Minerva-8B (14.1%) by 11 points. On GSM8k, LLEMMA-34B reaches 51.5% (vs. 29.6% for Code Llama-34B), and on SAT it scores 71.9% (vs. 40.6% for Code Llama-34B). With majority voting (k=256 for MATH), LLEMMA-34B reaches 43.1% on MATH, nearly matching Minerva-62B (43.4%) at roughly one-fifth the parameter count. On formal theorem proving (miniF2F-test), LLEMMA-7B achieves 26.23% on Lean tactic prediction, surpassing COPRA/GPT-4 (23.36%) and approaching the fine-tuned ReProver (26.50%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLEMMA-7B decisively outperforms its Code Llama-7B initialization on all five benchmarks, with the largest gains on GSM8k (+25.9 pp) and SAT (+43.7 pp), and also surpasses the proprietary Minerva-8B on every metric (e.g., MATH 18.0% vs. 14.1%, GSM8k 36.4% vs. 16.2%). LLEMMA-34B similarly dominates Code Llama-34B across the board (MATH +12.8 pp, GSM8k +21.9 pp, SAT +31.3 pp). Against the larger Minerva-62B, LLEMMA-34B is competitive on GSM8k (51.5% vs. 52.4%) but trails on MATH (25.0% vs. 27.6%) and MMLU-STEM (49.0% vs. 53.9%), despite having roughly one-fifth the parameters. On formal theorem proving, LLEMMA-7B beats the Sledgehammer prover (20.60% vs. 14.72% on miniF2F-valid) and COPRA/GPT-4 (26.23% vs. 23.36% on miniF2F-test), while slightly trailing the fine-tuned ReProver (26.23% vs. 26.50%). In the supervised finetuning ablation (Table 12), LLEMMA-7B + MetaMathQA achieves 25.2% on MATH, outperforming Llama 2-7B + MetaMathQA (19.4%) and Llama 2-7B + WizardMath (10.7%), confirming that the continued-pretraining advantage persists after task-specific finetuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Minerva (Lewkowycz et al., 2022) recipe of continued pretraining on mathematical content, but departs in three key ways: it initializes from Code Llama (giving the model code capabilities from the start), incorporates a substantial code component (AlgebraicStack, 11B tokens) into the training mixture, and evaluates on tool use and formal theorem proving in addition to chain-of-thought problem solving. The continued pretraining paradigm itself follows Rozière et al. (2023) and Beltagy et al. (2019), while the data collection builds on OpenWebMath, RedPajama's ArXiv subset, and the Stack. For formal mathematics, the evaluation protocol follows Jiang et al. (2023) for autoformalization and Polu & Sutskever (2020) / Yang et al. (2023) for tactic prediction, but is the first to demonstrate few-shot (rather than finetuned) tactic prediction with an open model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By releasing both the models and the full training data (Proof-Pile-2, AlgebraicStack), this work provides the first open platform for studying domain-specific mathematical language modeling, removing the closed-access barrier that limited prior work like Minerva. The memorization analysis (showing 30-gram hits do not correlate with accuracy) and the data mixture ablation offer practical guidance for future domain-adaptation efforts. Open questions include whether the continued-pretraining-on-domain-data recipe generalizes to other specialized domains, whether the 2:4:1 arXiv:web:code ratio is near-optimal, and whether the model's formal proving capabilities can be further improved through the long-context extension the authors leave to future work.
