## Problem

Existing neural avatar methods use fixed or implicitly learned frequency encodings that cannot adapt to the pose-dependent detail requirements of human bodies. A single query point may need high-frequency representation when cloth wrinkles are present but low-frequency representation in smooth regions, yet standard positional encodings apply the same frequency transformation regardless of pose context, yielding either noisy artifacts in uniform areas or blurred fine-grained texture in sharp regions.

## Input / Output

The method takes N video frames (e.g., 1080×1920 resolution) with per-frame 24-joint skeleton poses represented as 6D rotation parameters, plus camera intrinsics/extrinsics. Query points are mapped into bone-relative coordinates and discarded if their scaled positions fall outside [-1,1] for all 24 bones, concentrating computation on the body surface. The network outputs per-ray density and color values that are integrated via standard NeRF volume rendering to produce novel-view or novel-pose images, and can also yield SDF-based geometry for shape evaluation.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-branch architecture that explicitly links skeleton pose to frequency-domain feature modulation. A graph neural network (2 GCN layers + per-node 2-layer MLPs, following DANBO) encodes per-bone pose correlations from the joint angles. A two-stage learnable window function—combining a spatial window (Gaussian decay on bone-relative distance) and a feature window (Sigmoid-gated max-pooled point-wise features)—selects and weights relevant bone features for each query point. The aggregated features are passed through an MLP to predict per-layer frequency coefficients θ_i that modulate a stack of Sine-activated fully-connected layers (inspired by pi-GAN/SIREN), enabling the network to adaptively allocate high-frequency capacity where pose demands it (e.g., wrinkles) and suppress it in smooth regions. Training uses L1 photometric loss plus a regularization term on per-bone volume scaling factors, optimized with Adam for ~20 hours on a single RTX 3090.

## Results

On the Human3.6M test set, the proposed method achieves the best scores across all metrics for both novel view synthesis (PSNR 25.06, SSIM 0.921, LPIPS 0.110) and novel pose rendering (PSNR 24.15, SSIM 0.906, LPIPS 0.124). On MonoPerfCap, the full model reaches PSNR 21.70 / SSIM 0.860 (averaged over novel pose and novel view), outperforming DANBO (21.25 / 0.852) and the GNN-only ablation (21.37 / 0.852). On ZJU-MoCap, the method attains the highest average PSNR of 29.55 and SSIM of 0.968 for novel view synthesis, and a Chamfer Distance of 0.0400 for geometry reconstruction, surpassing all template-free baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all nine baselines on every reported metric. Among template-free methods, DANBO is the closest competitor, trailing by 0.37 dB PSNR on novel view (24.69 vs 25.06) and 0.41 dB on novel pose (23.74 vs 24.15), while TAVA is marginally ahead of DANBO on novel view PSNR (24.72) but still below the proposed method. Among template/scan-based methods, ARAH is the strongest baseline (24.63 PSNR novel view, 23.27 novel pose) yet still loses by 0.43 and 0.88 dB respectively. On the ZJU-MoCap benchmark, the method beats HumanNeRF (29.14 PSNR) and MonoHuman (29.21 PSNR) for novel view synthesis, and achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.0400 versus 0.0582 (HumanNeRF) and 0.0885 (MonoHuman) for geometry. Vid2Avatar, a template-based method relying on SMPL priors, achieves the best geometry score (0.0359 CD) but the proposed method is competitive at 0.0400 without any surface template. The LPIPS gap is particularly notable: the proposed method achieves 0.110 (NV) and 0.124 (NP) versus 0.116/0.131 for DANBO, indicating substantially better perceptual fidelity in fine texture and wrinkle detail.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DANBO's GNN-based skeleton encoding and NeRF's volume rendering pipeline, but repurposes the GNN output to drive explicit frequency coefficients in Sine-activated MLP layers rather than to estimate part-level feature spaces as in DANBO. It extends the Sine-activation idea from SIREN and pi-GAN by making the frequency parameters pose-dependent, connecting graph-learned joint correlations to per-layer modulation coefficients. The two-stage window function (spatial + feature) is a new aggregation mechanism that addresses seam artifacts and spurious cross-part correlations not handled in prior articulated NeRF methods like A-NeRF, TAVA, or ARAH.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that pose-dependent frequency assignment is a meaningful and measurable axis of variation in human avatar rendering, validated through frequency histogram comparisons that go beyond standard PSNR/SSIM metrics. However, the method remains person-specific (~20 hours training per subject), requires accurate camera calibration, and cannot model motion-dependent dynamics (e.g., wrinkle formation depends on past motion, not just the current pose). Open questions include whether the frequency modulation framework can be made real-time, generalized across subjects, or extended to handle extreme out-of-distribution poses where even this method produces blurry artifacts.
