## Problem

Hypergraph Neural Networks (HGNNs) excel at modeling high-order correlations but suffer from exponential inference-time growth due to their dependence on hypergraph structure during message passing, making them impractical for large-scale deployment. Existing GNNs-to-MLPs distillation methods (GLNN, KRD) only handle pairwise graph edges and cannot capture the hierarchical, multi-vertex correlations inherent to hypergraphs, leaving a ~16-point accuracy gap between MLPs and HGNNs on hypergraph datasets.

## Input / Output

At inference, the distilled student MLP consumes only the vertex feature matrix (N×c) and produces per-vertex class labels, with no access to the hypergraph incidence matrix—enabling O(LNF²) complexity identical to a vanilla MLP. During training, the teacher HGNN additionally requires the hypergraph structure (incidence matrix H ∈ {0,1}^{N×M}) to generate soft labels and high-order soft targets, but this dependency is entirely eliminated after distillation. The method supports both transductive and inductive (production) evaluation settings on semi-supervised node classification.

## Method

LightHGNN distills a pre-trained teacher HGNN into a student MLP using a combined cross-entropy (on labeled vertices) and KL-divergence (on all vertices' soft predictions) loss. LightHGNN+ adds topology-aware distillation: it first quantifies each hyperedge's reliability by measuring the noise-resistance of its connected vertices' prediction entropy under Gaussian perturbation, then samples a reliable hyperedge subset via independent Bernoulli trials parameterized by these scores. On the sampled hyperedges, vertex-level soft labels are aggregated into high-order soft targets via mean message passing, and an additional KL-divergence loss pulls the student's corresponding high-order predictions toward these targets. This explicit high-order supervision injects task-relevant structural knowledge into the MLP while the reliability-based sampling filters out task-irrelevant hyperedges, simultaneously improving accuracy and resisting over-smoothing (measured via a topology-aware score tracking vertex-hyperedge embedding distance).

## Results

On the combined graph and hypergraph benchmark (Table 3), LightHGNN+ achieves the best average rank of 1.8 across six datasets (Cora, Pubmed, Citeseer, CA-Cora, DBLP-Paper, IMDB-AW), outperforming all GNN, HGNN, and GNNs-to-MLPs baselines. On eight hypergraph datasets under transductive setting, LightHGNNs outperform vanilla MLPs by an average of 16.33% accuracy while incurring only a 0.29% average decline relative to the teacher HGNN. On synthetic hypergraphs with 55,000 vertices, LightHGNNs run 100.5× faster than HGNN (1.37 ms vs. 137.70 ms inference time), demonstrating linear rather than exponential scaling with graph size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LightHGNN+ achieves the best average rank (1.8) across all six datasets in Table 3, beating GCN (rank 5.3), GAT (7.7), HGNN (5.3), HGNN+ (5.2), GLNN (4.3), KRD (5.5), and NOSMOG (5.5). On graph datasets, LightHGNN+ edges out GCN by 0.78% on Cora and 1.62% on Citeseer, and surpasses NOSMOG by 0.48% on Citeseer while matching it on Pubmed (79.16 vs. 80.42, where NOSMOG actually leads). On hypergraph datasets, LightHGNN+ matches or slightly exceeds HGNN (73.54 vs. 73.19 on CA-Cora; 72.93 vs. 72.27 on DBLP-Paper) while running 100× faster at scale. In the production setting (Table 2), LightHGNNs still outperform MLPs by 14.86% on average but show a notable 11.27% decline vs. HGNN on DBLP-Conf inductive testing, where the dataset's 20 hyperedges each link ~982 vertices, creating heavy topology dependency. In ablation with different teacher architectures (Table S10), LightHGNN+ achieves the best average rank (1.43) across HGNN, HGNN+, UniGNN, UniGAT, ED-HNN, UniGCNII, and AllSet teachers, confirming robustness to teacher choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GNNs-to-MLPs distillation paradigm (GLNN, KRD, NOSMOG) from pairwise graphs to hypergraphs, where high-order correlations among more than two vertices make direct distillation non-trivial. LightHGNN is a straightforward extension of soft-label distillation to HGNN teachers, while LightHGNN+ introduces a topology-aware mechanism—reliable hyperedge qualification via noise-resistance scoring and high-order soft-label propagation—that has no direct analogue in the graph-based distillation literature. The method also draws on the over-smoothing literature (Cai & Wang, 2020) to motivate selecting only task-relevant hyperedges rather than blindly smoothing over all structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100× inference speedup and elimination of hypergraph structural dependency at test time make HGNN-quality predictions deployable in latency-sensitive, large-scale industrial settings where full hypergraph message passing is infeasible. However, the inductive setting reveals a clear limitation: on topology-heavy datasets like DBLP-Conf (20 hyperedges linking ~982 vertices each), LightHGNNs lose ~11% accuracy because they cannot exploit unseen topology, an open problem the authors flag for future work. The topology-aware score's observation of a sharp performance cliff at layer 6→7 on IMDB-AW is intriguing but single-dataset and unexplained mechanistically.
