## Problem

Consistency Models (CM) accelerate diffusion sampling to a single forward pass but lack a natural speed-quality trade-off: their multistep sampling degrades in quality as NFE increases due to overlapping time intervals between jumps, and they do not provide access to the score function, precluding likelihood estimation and established controllable-generation techniques from the diffusion community.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241736/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a random noise vector sampled from the prior at time T (and optionally a class label for conditional generation) and produces a synthetic image at 32×32 (CIFAR-10) or 64×64 (ImageNet) in as few as one network evaluation. Uniquely, the same network also outputs the score function ∇log p_t(x_t) via the limit g_θ(x_t, t, t), enabling NLL computation and score-based ODE/SDE sampling. The γ-sampling scheme allows the user to trade off NFE against sample quality and stochasticity in a single unified framework.

## Method

CTM defines a neural jump G_θ(x_t, t, s) that maps any point on the PF ODE trajectory at time t to any earlier time s, parameterized as G_θ = (s/t)x_t + (1−s/t)g_θ(x_t, t, s) so that the initial condition G_θ(x_t, t, t) = x_t holds by construction. The key insight is that as s→t, g_θ(x_t, t, s) converges to the denoiser E[x|x_t], so a single network simultaneously learns the integral (long jump) and the integrand (score/denoiser). Training combines a soft consistency distillation loss—where a random intermediate time u controls how much of the teacher's ODE solution is distilled—additively with a denoising score matching loss (regularizing small jumps) and a GAN loss (refining high-frequency details), all balanced via adaptive gradient-norm weighting. At inference, the novel γ-sampling alternates backward neural jumps (denoise from t_n to √(1−γ²)·t_{n+1}) with forward diffusion (re-noise to t_{n+1}), where γ=0 yields deterministic sampling with O(√T) error independent of NFE, and γ=1 recovers CM's stochastic multistep sampling.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, CTM achieves a new SOTA FID of 1.98 (unconditional, 1 NFE) and 1.73 (conditional, 1 NFE), surpassing EDM (1.82, 35 NFE) and StyleGAN-XL (1.85, 1 NFE). With 2 NFEs, CTM reaches FID 1.63 (conditional), the best among all generative models. On ImageNet 64×64, CTM achieves FID 1.92 (1 NFE) and 1.73 (2 NFE), outperforming all previous non-guided generative models. CTM also sets a new SOTA NLL of 2.43 on CIFAR-10, demonstrating strong density estimation capability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CTM decisively outperforms the closest distillation baseline, Consistency Models (CM/CD), achieving FID 1.98 vs. 3.55 (unconditional, 1 NFE) and FID 1.73 vs. 2.93 (conditional, 2 NFE) on CIFAR-10. On ImageNet 64×64, CTM (FID 1.92, 1 NFE) surpasses CM (6.20), Progressive Distillation (15.39), and even the score-based EDM (2.44, 79 NFE) by a wide margin. CTM also beats the strongest GAN baseline StyleGAN-XL (FID 1.85, 1 NFE) on conditional CIFAR-10 generation (1.73 vs. 1.85) and the score-based EDM (1.82, 35 NFE). Notably, CTM's 2-NFE conditional FID of 1.63 is the best reported among all generative models tested. On density estimation, CTM achieves NLL 2.43, improving over EDM (2.56) and VDM (2.49), leveraging its access to the score function for likelihood computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

CTM unifies two previously separate paradigms for fast diffusion sampling—score-based ODE solving (Song et al., 2020b) and distillation-based direct integral estimation (Consistency Models, Song et al., 2023)—by learning the full Probability Flow ODE trajectory as an anytime-to-anytime jump function. The soft consistency loss generalizes CM's local consistency (s=0) and score matching (s→t) into a single training objective, while the adversarial and DSM auxiliary losses draw on VQGAN's (Esser et al., 2021) recipe for combining reconstruction and GAN objectives. The student network architecture and training configuration are inherited from EDM (Karras et al., 2022), with the key departure being the additional s-embedding and the trajectory-level distillation target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CTM's ability to access the score function via g(x_t, t, t) unlocks likelihood estimation and controllable generation methods from the diffusion community that were previously unavailable to distillation models, while γ-sampling provides a principled knob for trading off sample diversity against fidelity. The theoretical result (Theorem 1) explaining why CM's multistep sampling degrades with increasing NFE—due to overlapping time intervals in successive jumps—resolves a long-standing empirical puzzle and motivates the γ=0 deterministic regime. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions (256×256, 512×512) and other modalities, and whether the student-beats-teacher phenomenon generalizes beyond the specific EDM/CM teacher configurations tested here.
