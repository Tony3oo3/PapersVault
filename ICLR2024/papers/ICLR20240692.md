## Problem

Existing backdoor attacks assume attackers have access to the entire training dataset (one-to-one data collection), which is unrealistic in practice where victims aggregate data from multiple sources and attackers can only poison a subset. Under this more realistic 'data-constrained' setting, prior methods suffer severe attack success rate degradation because neural networks entangle benign and poisoning features during backdoor injection, making the trigger less effective when the distribution of poisoned samples differs from the full training set.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240692/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes clean images from a restricted subset of the training data (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet-50), a frozen pre-trained CLIP model (ViT-B/32), and text prompts of the form 'a photo of a c_i' for each of C classes. It produces an L_p-bounded trigger perturbation (ε=8/255) that is added to clean images to generate poisoned samples; the perturbation is either per-sample (CLIP-CFE) or universal across the accessible set (CLIP-UAP, CLIP-CFA). The poisoned images are then mixed into the victim's training pipeline at a low poisoning rate (0.7%–2%).

## Method

The paper proposes three CLIP-based techniques organized into two independent streams. Clean Feature Suppression (CLIP-CFE) optimizes per-sample noise via PGD to push CLIP's zero-shot prediction toward a uniform distribution, effectively erasing class-specific semantic features so the backdoor model must rely on the trigger rather than benign content. Poisoning Feature Augmentation is achieved through two parallel triggers: CLIP-UAP optimizes a single universal perturbation that maximizes CLIP's misclassification toward the target label (replacing the infeasible proxy-model-based UAP), and CLIP-CFA uses contrastive optimization over CLIP's image encoder to maximize cosine similarity between different poisoned samples (positive pairs) while minimizing similarity between a poisoned sample and its clean counterpart (negative pairs), yielding a label-agnostic trigger. All three techniques use the frozen CLIP model as a general-purpose feature extractor, eliminating the need for a proxy model trained on the full dataset, and can be combined additively to further improve attack success rate.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with VGG-16 under number-constrained backdoor attacks, CLIP-CFA achieves an ASR of 0.988 and CLIP-UAP achieves 0.984, substantially outperforming BadNets (0.878) and Blended (0.825). Adding CLIP-CFE (clean feature suppression) yields up to 229% ASR improvement for CLIP-CFA in the clean-label single-class setting and 187% for BadNets. Benign Accuracy remains stable across all methods (~0.72 on CIFAR-100, ~0.92 on CIFAR-10), confirming the attacks are harmless to clean predictions. Experiments span 3 datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet-50), 3 target models (VGG-16, ResNet-18, MobileNet-V2), and 15+ poisoning settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLIP-UAP and CLIP-CFA consistently outperform both BadNets (2×2 pixel patch) and Blended (image blending) across all three data-constrained settings and all target models. On number-constrained attacks with VGG-16/CIFAR-100, CLIP-CFA reaches 0.988 ASR versus 0.878 for BadNets and 0.825 for Blended; the gap widens dramatically under class-constrained (single-class) settings where Blended degrades most due to stronger benign-poisoning feature entanglement. CLIP-CFE, when added to any trigger, provides the largest relative gains in the hardest settings (e.g., +229% ASR for CLIP-CFA in clean-label single-class on VGG-16), while having negligible impact on Benign Accuracy. Compared to UAP-based attacks (Zhong et al.), which require a proxy model pre-trained on the full training set, CLIP-UAP achieves comparable or superior ASR without that proxy, making it viable in the data-constrained threat model. All methods maintain BA within ±0.002 of each other, confirming that the ASR gains come at no cost to clean accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the poisoning-based backdoor attack literature (BadNets, Blended, UAP-based triggers) by introducing a more realistic 'data-constrained' threat model where the attacker lacks access to the full training set, moving from the one-to-one (O2O) to a many-to-one (M2O) data collection paradigm. It builds directly on CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) as a general-purpose, proxy-free feature extractor and on the Universal Adversarial Perturbation optimization framework (Moosavi-Dezfooli et al., 2017), repurposing both for trigger design and clean-feature erasing without requiring a model pre-trained on the complete dataset. The paper also draws on contrastive learning principles for its CLIP-CFA trigger, which maximizes inter-poisoning-sample similarity while minimizing poisoning-to-clean similarity in CLIP's embedding space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of benign-poisoning feature entanglement as the root cause of attack degradation in constrained settings provides a useful diagnostic lens for understanding why existing backdoor methods fail in realistic multi-source data scenarios. The practical contribution of using a frozen CLIP model as a substitute for a full-dataset proxy is immediately applicable and lowers the barrier for data-constrained attacks. However, the evaluation is limited to image classification on three small-to-medium datasets (CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet-50) with three CNN architectures, and the paper does not explore whether the entanglement analysis or CLIP-based techniques transfer to other modalities (NLP, audio) or to transformer-based victim models beyond a brief ViT-B/32 ablation. Open questions include whether adaptive defenses can detect CLIP-optimized triggers and whether the approach generalizes to fine-grained or out-of-distribution domains where CLIP's zero-shot representations may be less reliable.
