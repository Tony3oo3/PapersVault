## Problem

Generating sparse (l0-bounded) adversarial examples in a black-box, score-based setting is non-trivial because minimizing the l0 norm is NP-hard and the search space is a non-differentiable mixture of discrete (pixel positions) and continuous (pixel colors) variables. Prior score-based sparse attacks like SPARSE-RS rely on stochastic search and require large query budgets to achieve low sparsity on high-resolution tasks such as ImageNet, while the robustness of Vision Transformer architectures to sparse perturbations remains largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242129/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a normalized RGB source image x ∈ [0,1]^{c×w×h}, a target class label (for targeted attacks), and access to the victim model's softmax confidence scores via iterative queries. It pre-generates a fixed synthetic binary color image x' ∈ {0,1}^{c×w×h} once per attack, then outputs a binary perturbation mask u ∈ {0,1}^{w×h} and the resulting sparse adversarial example x̃ = ux' + (1−u)x. The attacker never observes gradients or hard labels—only partial score vectors returned by the model—and the perturbation budget B constrains the number of 1-entries in u.

## Method

BRUSLEATTACK reformulates the mixed discrete-continuous search into a lower-dimensional discrete problem by fixing pixel colors via a pre-specified synthetic binary image x', reducing the search to selecting which pixel positions to perturb (a binary mask u). It then applies a Dirichlet-Categorical Bayesian framework: the concentration parameter α is updated at each round based on historical pixel manipulation outcomes, where pixels whose removal increases the loss are deemed influential and receive higher posterior probability of retention. At each iteration, a fraction b of current pixels are retained via sampling from the posterior, while B−b new pixels are drawn from unselected positions weighted by a dissimilarity map M = |x − x'|/3 that biases selection toward positions with large color contrast to accelerate movement toward the decision boundary. A combined power-and-step-decay scheduler λ_t = λ_0(t^{m₁} + m₂ᵗ) progressively reduces the number of altered pixels per round, transitioning from exploration to exploitation. This yields a query-efficient combinatorial search that consistently outperforms SPARSE-RS by ~10% ASR at 2K–6K queries on ImageNet.

## Results

On ImageNet (targeted setting, 224×224), BRUSLEATTACK achieves 97.7% ASR at 1.0% sparsity with 10K queries against ResNet-50, compared to 94.3% for SPARSE-RS. Against ViT-B/16 under the same conditions, BRUSLEATTACK reaches 86.8% ASR versus 74.3% for SPARSE-RS, a 12.5-point gap. At the tighter 0.4% sparsity level (≈200 pixels), BRUSLEATTACK still leads with 82.7% vs. 79.1% ASR on ResNet-50 at 10K queries. Against defended models (Table 1), BRUSLEATTACK degrades ResNet-50 accuracy to 1.0% at 0.2% sparsity (vs. 4.6% for SPARSE-RS) and reduces RND-defended accuracy to 42.6% (vs. 56.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline SPARSE-RS (Croce et al., 2022), BRUSLEATTACK consistently achieves higher ASR across all sparsity levels (0.4%–1.0%), query budgets (4K–10K), and model architectures on ImageNet; the gap is largest against ViT (up to 12.5 points at 1.0% sparsity, 10K queries). Against decision-based sparse attacks SPARSEEVO (Vo et al., 2022a) and POINTWISE (Schott et al., 2019), BRUSLEATTACK substantially outperforms even with half the query budget (500 vs. 2K queries on CIFAR-10), achieving 90.24% vs. 68.75% ASR at 2.0% sparsity. Against the white-box PGD₀ (Croce & Hein, 2019), BRUSLEATTACK surpasses it at low sparsity thresholds and is comparable at high sparsity, despite operating in a strictly black-box, score-based setting. Against the One-Pixel Attack (Su et al., 2019), BRUSLEATTACK wins at all tested budgets (1, 3, 5 pixels) on CIFAR-10, e.g. 86.4% vs. 62.3% ASR at 5 pixels. Against CASMOPOLITAN (Wan et al., 2021), a Bayesian optimization method for mixed search spaces, BRUSLEATTACK outperforms at 250 queries across all sparsity levels on CIFAR-10. Finally, the adapted l₀-HSJA (Chen et al., 2020) fails to achieve meaningful sparsity (>90% average sparsity even at 20K queries), confirming that naively projecting dense attacks to l₀ is ineffective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sparse adversarial attack line of work (SparseFool, PGD_0, SPARSE-RS) by replacing the stochastic pixel-selection strategy of SPARSE-RS with a Bayesian Dirichlet-Categorical framework that learns pixel influence from historical manipulation outcomes. The key conceptual departure is a problem reformulation that reduces the mixed discrete-continuous search space to a purely discrete combinatorial search over a binary mask, enabled by a pre-specified synthetic color image. The Bayesian inference structure (conjugate Dirichlet prior, posterior update via accumulated pixel-alteration statistics) is adapted from Abbasnejad et al. (2017), while the dissimilarity-map-guided sampling draws on biased-sampling ideas from Brunner et al. (2019).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic comparison of ViT versus CNN robustness under sparse (l0) adversarial perturbations on ImageNet, revealing that ViT's global receptive field makes it substantially harder to fool with few pixels — a finding with direct implications for model deployment choices in security-sensitive MLaaS. The practical demonstration against Google Cloud Vision and the consistent ~10% ASR gain over SPARSE-RS at low query budgets make this a useful tool for vulnerability assessment. Open questions include whether the Bayesian pixel-influence model transfers across architectures or requires re-learning per model, and whether the binary synthetic color image assumption (vs. optimized colors) remains effective under stricter sparsity constraints or multi-channel perturbations.
