## Problem

CLIP achieves remarkable zero-shot accuracy on OOD benchmarks like ImageNet-Sketch and ImageNet-R, which prior work attributes to its large, diverse training data (LAION-400M). However, it remains unclear whether this performance is simply an artifact of high train-test similarity — i.e., whether LAION-400M contains many images that are stylistically and semantically close to these benchmarks, making them effectively in-distribution rather than truly OOD. This paper systematically tests whether removing all such highly similar training images would collapse CLIP's OOD performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes LAION-200M (a deduplicated 199M subset of LAION-400M) image-text pairs plus ImageNet-Train as a reference distribution, and six ImageNet-based test sets (Val, Sketch, A, R, V2, ObjectNet) as targets. It produces pruned training subsets where, for each test sample, no remaining LAION image is more perceptually similar than the nearest ImageNet-Train image. The pruned subsets range from ~199M (val-pruned, removing only ~377K samples) to ~188M (combined-pruned, removing ~12.4M samples). The final output is zero-shot top-1 classification accuracy of CLIP ViT-B/32 models trained from scratch on each pruned split, evaluated on all six test sets.

## Method

The core contribution is a formal 'similarity gap' framework for surgically removing highly similar images from a large training set. Perceptual similarity is quantified as cosine similarity in CLIP ViT-B/16+'s image embedding space, and for each test sample t_i, the nearest-neighbor similarity s_i(D) = max_{x∈D} s(t_i, x) is computed. The similarity gap S(D, T) is the set of all such per-sample similarities across the test set. To align LAION-200M's similarity gap with ImageNet-Train's, the method removes every LAION sample x for which s(t_i, x) > s_i(ImageNet-Train) for any test sample t_i, ensuring the pruned dataset is no more similar to the test set than ImageNet-Train is. CLIP ViT-B/32 is then trained from scratch (32 epochs, batch size 33,600, 8×A100) on each pruned split. The key finding is that even after this aggressive pruning, CLIP retains most of its OOD performance (only 10.8% relative drop on ImageNet-Sketch, 4.8% on ImageNet-R), demonstrating that high train-test similarity is insufficient to explain CLIP's generalization and that other properties of web-scale data (scale, diversity) must drive its ability to learn generalizable representations.

## Results

After pruning LAION-200M to replicate ImageNet-Train's similarity gap to each OOD benchmark, CLIP ViT-B/32's zero-shot top-1 accuracy drops only marginally: 10.8% relative on ImageNet-Sketch (50.21% → 44.78%) and 4.8% relative on ImageNet-R (72.9% → 69.48%), while other benchmarks see <1% change. The 'combined-pruned' split (removing highly similar images for all six test sets simultaneously) retains 68.05% on ImageNet-Val and 44.12% on ImageNet-Sketch, far exceeding what an ImageNet-Train-matched model would achieve (~14% on Sketch per Fang et al., 2022). Additionally, a 100M far-pruned core set of LAION-200M achieves 50.71% average accuracy across all six benchmarks, matching the full LAION-400M model (51.09%) at one-quarter the data size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to OpenAI's original CLIP (Radford et al., 2021), even the most aggressively pruned model (sketch-pruned) still achieves 44.78% on ImageNet-Sketch versus OpenAI's 42.32%, and 69.35% on ImageNet-R versus 69.24%, indicating that high train-test similarity is not the primary driver of CLIP's OOD advantage. Against the L-400M OpenCLIP baseline (Schuhmann et al., 2021), the pruned models remain within 1–5 percentage points on most benchmarks, with the combined-pruned split losing only 5.6 points on ImageNet-R (67.88% vs 73.48%) and 5.3 points on ImageNet-Sketch (44.12% vs 49.39%). In the 100M core-set comparison (Table 3), the all-far-pruned LAION-200M split achieves 50.71% average accuracy, substantially outperforming the SemDeDup 100M baseline (Abbas et al., 2023) at 43.7% average, while matching the full LAION-400M model (51.09%) at one-quarter the training data. The paper's central conclusion is that removing all images more similar to a test set than any ImageNet-Train image causes only a modest performance drop, demonstrating that CLIP's generalization is driven by dataset scale and diversity rather than by highly similar training samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work by Fang et al. (2022) and Nguyen et al. (2022), which established that training data distribution is the primary driver of CLIP's OOD robustness, by isolating one specific distributional property: the similarity gap between training and test sets. It builds directly on CLIP/OpenCLIP's architecture and LAION-400M training pipeline, and leverages the perceptual similarity properties of CLIP's embedding space demonstrated by Fu et al. (2023), Gadre et al. (2023), and Abbas et al. (2023). The key conceptual departure is the formalization of 'highly similar images' via the similarity gap between two training distributions, enabling a surgical pruning intervention that prior data-pruning work (e.g., SemDeDup) did not target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that removing all training samples more similar to OOD test sets than any ImageNet-Train sample barely degrades CLIP's performance challenges the intuitive narrative that web-scale datasets help mainly through containing near-duplicates of benchmark images. This shifts the research focus toward other data properties—diversity, density, compositionality—as the true drivers of generalization, though the paper explicitly leaves these for future work. Limitations include the restriction to ImageNet-based OOD benchmarks, reliance on a single similarity metric (CLIP ViT-B/16+ embeddings), and the fact that the 100M core set is optimized for six specific test sets rather than being a general-purpose subset. The released core set and similarity-gap methodology provide useful tools for future data-efficiency and interpretability research on vision-language models.
