## Problem

Existing LLM alignment methods (SFT, RLHF) rely exclusively on human-annotated, error-free instruction-response pairs and discard harmful data, yet directly exposing models to erroneous pairs risks them learning and replicating toxic patterns. This paper tackles the open question of how LLMs can learn from mistakes without being negatively influenced by the toxic content, motivated by the observation that a model's ability to discriminate (identify) mistakes is substantially stronger than its ability to generate safe responses.

## Input / Output

The method takes text instructions (both helpful and red-teaming/harmful) paired with responses, and produces harmless text responses. During training, the standard instruction-response pairs are augmented with mistake analysis triplets—(instruction, harmful response, natural-language explanation of why the response is harmful)—constructed entirely by the model's own self-critique without external annotators. At inference, a guided prompt prefix explicitly reminds the model to generate 'harmless, ethical, and inoffensive' responses, constraining the generation within an ethical context.

## Method

The core contribution is a four-step pipeline that repurposes harmful responses into alignment signal through self-generated mistake analysis. First, an unaligned model is induced to produce harmful responses by inserting hint keywords (e.g., 'harmful, unethical, offensive') into prompts, achieving a >72% induction success rate. Second, the model is guided to analyze why each harmful response is problematic, exploiting its empirically demonstrated superior discrimination ability over generation. Third, these mistake analysis triplets are fine-tuned with an unguided template (deliberately omitting harmful reminders) alongside standard helpful and harmless SFT data, preventing the model from shortcutting on explicit cues while teaching it to internally discriminate harmful content. Fourth, at inference, a guided prompt reminds the model to produce harmless responses. The theoretical justification via Bayes' theorem shows that optimizing p(T|Y,X) (harmfulness tag given response and instruction) alongside p(Y|X,T) (response given instruction and harmlessness tag) enhances alignment, with the mistake analysis acting as a 'fine-grained mask' that teaches the model what to avoid without requiring external models or human annotations.

## Results

On the PKU-SafeRLHF test set (1,523 red-teaming instructions), the proposed method with Alpaca-induced mistakes and GPT-3.5 analysis achieves a Harmless Score of 7.61 and a Harmless Rate of 74.1%, surpassing the best baseline (CoH with Alpaca mistakes) by 0.74 in Harmless Score and 8.4 percentage points in Harmless Rate. On the AlpacaFarm helpfulness test set (805 instructions), the method maintains a Helpful Score of 6.31, comparable to SFT (6.27) and RLHF (6.30). For attack defense on ChatGLM-6B, the method achieves a Goal Hijacking Rate of 85.3% (+16.9 pp over the base model) while preserving helpfulness (8.26 vs. 8.32).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all three baseline families on both harmlessness and helpfulness metrics. Against SFT and RLHF (Ouyang et al., 2022), the method improves Harmless Score by 0.90–0.92 and Harmless Rate by 10–11 pp while matching or slightly exceeding their Helpful Scores (6.31 vs. 6.27/6.30). Against Critique-Revise (Bai et al., 2022), the method gains 1.01 in Harmless Score and 11.5 pp in Harmless Rate, and also recovers the Helpful Score on harmful instructions (5.60 vs. 5.02), which Critique-Revise degrades. Against CoH (Liu et al., 2023), the closest competitor, the method leads by 0.74 in Harmless Score and 8.4 pp in Harmless Rate (74.1% vs. 65.7%), with a marginal Helpful Score advantage (6.31 vs. 6.28). In the attack-defense setting (Table 2), the method also surpasses CoH and Critique-Revise on Goal Hijacking Rate (85.3% vs. 82.4% and 78.7% respectively), confirming consistent superiority across both alignment and robustness tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant SFT/RLHF paradigm, which trains LLMs exclusively on error-free instruction-response pairs or uses reward models to rank responses, by repurposing harmful responses as positive training signal through model-generated mistake analysis. It builds on the self-critique literature (Saunders et al., 2022; Gou et al., 2023b; Bai et al., 2022b) and the Chain of Hindsight framework (Liu et al., 2023a), but differs in that the critique is self-generated rather than externally provided, and the theoretical grounding via Bayes' theorem (framing mistake analysis as optimizing p(T|Y,X) ∝ p(Y|X,T)) is novel. The method also connects to the broader trend of training with model-generated data in both vision and NLP, but uniquely leverages the model's own discrimination ability to enhance its generation capability without any external knowledge source.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that LLMs can achieve meaningful self-alignment without external reward models or human annotations, reducing a major practical bottleneck in safety alignment. The finding that discrimination consistently and substantially exceeds generation across multiple model families provides a principled justification for the approach. However, the evaluation is limited to 7B-parameter models (Alpaca-7B, ChatGLM-6B) and specific safety benchmarks, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger models or generalizes beyond safety to other alignment dimensions (e.g., factuality, helpfulness trade-offs). The Bayes' theorem assumptions (independence of X and T, stability of p(Y|X) during fine-tuning) are plausible but not rigorously validated, and the method's interaction with RLHF or DPO pipelines remains unexplored.
