## Problem

General-purpose LLMs lack the specialized terminology, domain knowledge, and contextual understanding required for IT operations (O&M) tasks spanning nine sub-domains such as information security, network, and database management. Additionally, no dedicated benchmark exists to systematically evaluate LLM performance on IT operations tasks, making it difficult to measure progress in this domain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240929/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts natural-language IT operations questions (single-turn or multi-turn), raw log messages, and multiple-choice questions across nine O&M domains, with inputs potentially exceeding the 1024-token training length. Outputs include free-form text answers, single-letter multiple-choice selections, standardized log templates (with variable parts replaced by *), and binary anomaly labels. The vocabulary is expanded from 32,000 to 48,553 tokens to prevent IT-specific terms from being fragmented across multiple subword tokens.

## Method

OwL is built on LLaMA2-13b with an expanded tokenizer and trained via supervised instruction tuning on the Owl-Instruct dataset (17,858 dialogues generated via self-instruct with GPT-4 quality filtering). For long-context inference, the paper proposes HMCE, which replaces NBCE's independence assumption with a conditional homogeneous Markov chain, decomposing p(T|S) into pairwise conditional terms p(T|S_i, S_{i-1}) minus p(T|S_i) to handle inputs exceeding the maximum sequence length without fine-tuning. For parameter-efficient multi-domain adaptation, a Mixture-of-Adapter strategy is introduced: a pool of LoRA adapters is gated by a top-k selection function operating on [CLS] hidden states projected through a learned matrix, so each input activates only the most relevant subset of adapters. This allows simultaneous domain-specific and task-specific specialization while sharing frozen base-model parameters.

## Results

On the Owl-Bench Q&A evaluation (scored 1–10 by GPT-4), OwL-13b achieves the highest average score of 8.86, outperforming LLaMA2-13b (8.57), Qwen-7b (8.41), ChatGLM2-6b (8.27), InternLM-7b (8.19), and ChatGLM-6b (8.12). On the LogHub anomaly detection task (zero-shot), OwL achieves F1-scores of 0.446 (BGL) and 0.518 (Spirit), surpassing LogPrompt/ChatGPT (0.384 and 0.450) by an average of 6.5% F1. On the LogHub log parsing task (zero-shot), OwL attains the best fine-level F1 of 0.894, edging out LogPrompt/ChatGPT (0.881) and substantially outperforming traditional parsers like LogStamp (0.580) and LogPPT (0.600).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the Owl-Bench Q&A test, OwL-13b (8.86) leads all open-source baselines by a clear margin, beating the strongest competitor LLaMA2-13b (8.57) by 0.29 points and Qwen-7b (8.41) by 0.45 points; the pairwise GPT-4 judgment confirms OwL wins most head-to-head matchups, though ties are frequent. On the multiple-choice portion of Owl-Bench, OwL ranks just below ChatGPT overall but surpasses it on the system-architecture sub-domain, while clearly outperforming LLaMA2-13b, ChatGLM2-6b, Qwen-7b, and InternLM-7b. On LogHub anomaly detection (zero-shot), OwL outperforms LogPrompt/ChatGPT by 6.5% average F1 (0.446 vs 0.384 on BGL; 0.518 vs 0.450 on Spirit) and dominates all supervised baselines (DeepLog, LogAnomaly, LogRobust) that were trained on 4,000 logs. On LogHub log parsing (zero-shot), OwL achieves the best fine-level F1 (0.894) versus LogPrompt/ChatGPT (0.881) and LogStamp (0.580), while its RandIndex (0.886 avg.) is only marginally below LogStamp (0.919 avg.), confirming strong template extraction without any training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the domain-specific LLM lineage (BloombergGPT for finance, Code LLMs, legal LLMs) to IT operations and O&M, using LLaMA2 as the base model. Technically, it builds on two threads: (1) the Naive Bayes-based Context Extension (NBCE) by Su (2023), which it generalizes to a Homogeneous Markov Context Extension (HMCE) by replacing the independence assumption with a first-order Markov chain to better model sequential context; and (2) LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning, which it extends into a Mixture-of-Adapter strategy that routes inputs to top-k LoRA experts for cross-domain transfer. Data construction follows the Self-Instruct and Baize paradigms, with GPT-4 used as a quality supervisor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The primary contribution is the Owl-Instruct dataset and Owl-Bench benchmark, which fill a genuine gap in the absence of standardized evaluation for LLMs on IT operations tasks. However, the performance gains are modest (8.86 vs. 8.57 average Q&A score; 0.76 vs. 0.71 mean multiple-choice accuracy with MoA vs. LoRA), and the long-context HMCE, while better than NBCE, still yields PPL values (6.10 at 8192) that are far from the no-extension baseline (314.49), suggesting the method is a stopgap rather than a solution. Open questions include whether the nine-domain coverage is sufficient for real-world O&M, whether the GPT-4-scored data quality pipeline introduces systematic biases, and whether the Mixture-of-Adapter routing generalizes beyond the nine domains used in training.
