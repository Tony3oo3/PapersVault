## Problem

Existing data augmentation methods—whether hand-crafted, learned (e.g., AutoAugment, RandAugment), or generative (e.g., diffusion models)—introduce two types of noise: augmented features that deviate from the true data distribution and misassigned labels. No prior framework provides a universal, efficient mechanism to correct both error types post-augmentation without modifying the upstream augmentation pipeline itself, and learned-augmentation approaches that operate directly in feature space are computationally expensive and restricted to differentiable transformations.

## Input / Output

SAFLEX consumes a batch of augmented samples (features x^aug and hard labels y^aug) produced by any upstream pipeline, a validation minibatch, and the current model parameters θ_{t-1}. It outputs per-sample weights w_i ∈ [0,1] and soft labels y_i in the K-dimensional probability simplex, which are then used in the standard training loss. Notably, the augmented features themselves are never modified; only their associated weights and labels are adjusted, making the method a lightweight plug-in that requires no architectural changes to the upstream pipeline.

## Method

SAFLEX formulates the learning of sample weights and soft labels as a bilevel optimization: the outer problem minimizes validation loss while the inner problem trains the model on the combined training and augmented batch. The key insight is that a greedy single-step approximation (replacing the full inner training loop with one SGD update) combined with the linearity of cross-entropy loss in weights and soft labels reduces the bilevel problem to a linear program whose solution is a gradient-matching objective. Concretely, for each augmented sample, the algorithm computes a Jacobian-vector product Π_i = ∇_θ f(x_i^aug) · ∇_θ L(D_val, θ_{t-1}), then sets the soft label via Gumbel-SoftMax(Π_i + β·one_hot(y_i^aug) + g)/τ and the weight to 1 if Π_i·y_i ≥ 0, else 0. This requires only one additional backpropagation pass for the validation gradient plus per-sample Jacobian-vector products, adding roughly 42% wall-clock overhead per epoch. The method extends to contrastive losses (e.g., CLIP's InfoNCE) by substituting contrastive logits for cross-entropy logits in the gradient inner product.

## Results

On eight MedMNIST medical image datasets, SAFLEX combined with RandAugment boosts accuracy by up to 3.6% over RandAugment alone (e.g., TissueMNIST: 62.03% → 64.32%, OrganAMNIST: 76.00% → 79.63%), and with Mixup by up to 2.2% (e.g., OrganSMNIST: 80.99% → 82.14%). On seven tabular datasets, SAFLEX+CutMix consistently outperforms CutMix, with the largest gain on the 10-class Volkert dataset (61.20% → 61.51% for MLP; 68.23% → 68.91% for SAINT). For diffusion-model-generated augmentations, SAFLEX yields an average improvement of ~1.9% on both fine-grained CUB classification and iWildCam OOD generalization. In CLIP contrastive fine-tuning (FLYP), SAFLEX+RandAug improves OOD accuracy from 36.3% to 36.9% (without ensembling) and from 37.6% to 37.8% (with ensembling).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAFLEX consistently outperforms RandAugment across all eight medical image datasets, with the most dramatic gains on TissueMNIST (+2.29%) and OrganAMNIST (+3.63%), where RandAugment actually degrades below the no-augmentation baseline. Against Mixup, SAFLEX improves on 7 of 8 datasets, with the largest margin on OrganSMNIST (+1.15%) and OrganAMNIST (+1.61%). On tabular data, SAFLEX+CutMix beats CutMix on all seven datasets for both MLP and SAINT backbones, with the clearest advantage on discrete-heavy datasets (Click: 52.59%→52.81% MLP; Shrutime: 86.39%→86.90% MLP). For diffusion-based augmentation, SAFLEX improves over InstructPix2Pix by +0.96% (fine-grained) and +2.63% (OOD), over Img2Img (w/o finetune) by +1.97% and +1.96%, and over Img2Img (w/ finetune) by +1.60% and +2.34%. In the CLIP/FLYP contrastive fine-tuning setting, SAFLEX+RandAug edges out FLYP+RandAug by +0.3% (ID) and +0.6% (OOD) without ensembling, confirming compatibility with contrastive losses. The paper also notes that SAFLEX with basic upstream augmentations matches or exceeds the performance of Soft-Augmentation and LP-A3 on MedMNIST, while requiring significantly less training time and no adversarial hyperparameter tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAFLEX repurposes the gradient-matching objective from dataset condensation (Zhao et al., 2020) as the core of a bilevel optimization formulation for augmentation refinement, a connection not previously drawn in the augmentation literature. It departs from learned-augmentation methods like AutoAugment and RandAugment, which search over transformation policies in high-dimensional spaces, and from feature-space approaches like LP-A3 that fine-tune augmented samples directly; instead, SAFLEX only learns low-dimensional sample weights and soft labels, sidestepping differentiability constraints entirely. The closest prior work is Soft-Augmentation (Liu et al., 2023), which also assigns soft labels and weights to augmented samples, but SAFLEX generalizes beyond crop augmentation to arbitrary upstream pipelines including diffusion models and contrastive losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAFLEX offers a genuinely universal plug-in that can be inserted after any existing augmentation pipeline—hand-crafted, learned, or generative—without modifying the pipeline itself, which is a practical advantage for practitioners who have already invested in specific augmentation strategies. However, the method requires a held-out validation set for gradient computation, incurs roughly 42% additional wall-clock time per epoch, and the theoretically continuous solution collapses to discrete 0/1 weights and one-hot labels, raising open questions about whether this discreteness limits generalization in under-parameterized regimes. The modest but consistent gains (1–3.6%) across medical, tabular, diffusion-generated, and contrastive settings suggest the approach is robust but not transformative; future work could explore whether the bilevel formulation admits truly continuous solutions or whether the validation-set dependency can be relaxed.
