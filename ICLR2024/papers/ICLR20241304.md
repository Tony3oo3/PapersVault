## Problem

When comparing face recognition models via empirical ROC curves on finite evaluation datasets, the statistical uncertainty of those curves is typically ignored, leading to potentially misleading conclusions about which model is better. The paper identifies that the standard bootstrap approach for ROC curves (Bertail et al., 2008) fails in the similarity scoring setting because false acceptance/rejection rates are generalized U-statistics rather than i.i.d. averages, causing naive bootstrap confidence bands to systematically underestimate the true uncertainty and often fail to even contain the empirical ROC curve.

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained face recognition encoders (ArcFace, CosFace, AdaCos, CurricularFace with MobileFaceNet backbones) applied to 112×112 RGB face images pre-processed by Retina-Face. Evaluation uses the MORPH dataset (55k images, 13k identities) with ground-truth gender and age labels as sensitive attributes, and RFW for additional experiments. The output is a full ROC curve with a 95% confidence band, confidence intervals for NIST FRVT fairness metrics (max-min, max-geomean, log-geomean, Gini), and a scalar normalized uncertainty score per FAR level, all computed via B=200 bootstrap resamples.

## Method

The core contribution is a recentered bootstrap procedure that corrects for the U-statistic structure of similarity FAR/FRR. The key insight is that the conditional expectation of the bootstrap FRR given the dataset equals a V-statistic version (which includes diagonal self-similarity terms) rather than the empirical U-statistic FRR, so the naive bootstrap distribution is centered around the V-statistic ROC rather than the empirical ROC. The recentered bootstrap uses this V-statistic version as the center, yielding confidence intervals that are properly centered around the empirical ROC curve. The paper proves uniform consistency of the empirical similarity ROC curve, establishes asymptotic validity of the recentered bootstrap (Theorem 1: coverage converges to the nominal level), and defines a normalized uncertainty metric as the bootstrap standard deviation of the recentered error divided by the empirical ROC value. This framework extends naturally to all NIST FRVT fairness metrics, enabling uncertainty-aware comparison of fairness across models and sensitive attributes.

## Results

On 200 synthetic datasets, the recentered bootstrap achieves empirical coverage very close to the nominal 95% level for the ROC curve, while the naive bootstrap of Bertail et al. (2008) produces confidence bands that fail to contain the empirical ROC curve in most cases. Among the four NIST FRVT fairness metrics evaluated (max-min, max-geomean, log-geomean, Gini), the max-geomean metric consistently displays the lowest normalized uncertainty across FAR and FRR variants, making it the most robust choice for fairness evaluation. On the MORPH dataset, the 95% confidence band for ArcFace's ROC curve fully contains CosFace's empirical curve, rendering the two models statistically indistinguishable in performance, while AdaCos's curve falls well outside the band on both dataset splits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary methodological comparison is between the naive bootstrap of Bertail et al. (2008) and the proposed recentered bootstrap. The naive bootstrap systematically underestimates the FRR because the bootstrap FRR is a V-statistic (including diagonal self-similarity terms) rather than a U-statistic, causing the bootstrap ROC curves to center around the V-statistic ROC rather than the empirical U-statistic ROC. As a result, the naive 95% confidence bands do not even contain the empirical ROC curve most of the time (Figures 2–3). The recentered bootstrap corrects this by subtracting the V-statistic ROC instead of the U-statistic ROC, yielding confidence intervals that are properly centered and achieve nominal coverage on synthetic data. In terms of model comparisons, ArcFace and CosFace are shown to be statistically indistinguishable (their ROC curves overlap within the confidence band), while AdaCos is significantly worse on both MORPH splits. For fairness, the max-geomean metric outperforms max-min, log-geomean, and Gini in terms of normalized uncertainty, a finding replicated across different backbones (MobileFaceNet, ResNet) and datasets (MORPH, RFW).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the bootstrap framework for ROC curve uncertainty established by Bertail et al. (2008) from the standard i.i.d. bipartite ranking setting to the similarity scoring setting, where FAR and FRR are generalized U-statistics rather than simple averages. The key conceptual departure is identifying that the diagonal terms in the V-statistic version of FRR cause the naive bootstrap to be systematically miscentered, necessitating a recentering correction. The asymptotic validity of the recentered bootstrap builds on the U-statistics literature (Lee, 1990; Arcones & Gine, 1992) and complements the non-asymptotic confidence bounds of Vogel et al. (2018), which used Hoeffding linearization but did not address practical resampling-based confidence bands.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With face recognition systems under increasing regulatory scrutiny (NIST FRVT, ACLU findings), the ability to make statistically defensible model comparisons and fairness claims is critical; this paper shows that without proper uncertainty quantification, conclusions about which model is better can flip entirely depending on the evaluation dataset. The finding that the max-geomean fairness metric is consistently the most robust across models, datasets, and sensitive attributes provides actionable guidance for the FR community. Open questions include whether the asymptotic guarantees hold well enough in the finite-sample regime typical of real evaluation datasets, and how uncertainty-aware fairness metrics should be incorporated into future regulatory standards, which the authors note are not yet fixed.
