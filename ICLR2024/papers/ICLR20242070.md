## Problem

Transformer-based SNNs have emerged but fail to exploit the low-power spike-driven paradigm because they retain energy-hungry MAC operations (softmax, scale, dot-product) inherited from vanilla Transformers. The prior Spike-driven Transformer achieved SOTA on ImageNet-1K but showed no clear performance advantage over CNN-based SNNs, and no neuromorphic chip has been designed specifically for Transformer-based SNNs. This paper addresses both gaps by proposing a meta-architecture that is fully spike-driven (only sparse addition) while delivering overwhelming performance gains over CNN-based SNNs.

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images (224×224 crops for classification, 1333×800 for detection) or sparse event streams from DVS cameras (346×260 for HAR-DVS). Static images are repeated T times to create the temporal dimension required by LIF neurons; training proceeds at T=1 for 200 epochs, then fine-tunes to T=4 for 20 additional epochs. Outputs span four task types: class labels (ImageNet-1K, HAR-DVS), bounding boxes (COCO via Mask R-CNN or YOLO), pixel-wise segmentation masks (ADE20K, VOC2012 via Semantic FPN), and action labels. The same backbone weights are transferred across tasks, making it the first directly-trained SNN backbone handling classification, detection, and segmentation concurrently.

## Method

Meta-SpikeFormer adopts a Conv+ViT progressive architecture: the first two stages use Conv-based SNN blocks (7×7 SepConv as token mixer + 3×3 Channel Conv instead of Channel MLP), while the last two stages use Transformer-based SNN blocks with a pyramid structure. The key micro-level innovation is the Spike-Driven Self-Attention (SDSA) operator, which replaces softmax and scale with a spiking neuron threshold (s·u_th), and replaces matrix multiplication between Q_S, K_S, V_S with either Hadamard product (SDSA-1/2) or spike-driven matrix multiplication converted to sparse addition (SDSA-3/4), yielding O(ND) linear complexity. Q_S, K_S, V_S are generated via re-parameterized 3×3 convolutions (RepConv) followed by LIF spiking neurons, and the output passes through another RepConv before the residual addition. Membrane Shortcut (connecting membrane potentials rather than spikes) is used for residual connections to preserve both identity mapping and spike-driven computation. The model is trained directly with surrogate gradients (no ANN-to-SNN conversion), achieving 80.0% top-1 on ImageNet-1K with 55M parameters—3.7% above prior SOTA with 17% fewer parameters.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, Meta-SpikeFormer (55M params) achieves 80.0% top-1 accuracy at T=4 with distillation, the first SNN to cross the 80% threshold, surpassing the prior SOTA (Spike-driven Transformer, 76.3%) by 3.7% while using 17% fewer parameters. On COCO detection, it reaches 51.2% mAP@0.5 (75M, T=1), and on ADE20K segmentation it achieves 35.3% mIoU (58.9M, T=4). For event-based action recognition on HAR-DVS, Meta-SpikeFormer (18.3M) attains 47.5% accuracy, outperforming the Conv-based SNN baseline (46.1%) and approaching ANN-level performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Meta-SpikeFormer (55M) decisively outperforms all prior Transformer-based SNNs: it beats Spike-driven Transformer (76.3%, 66M) by 3.7% and SpikFormer (74.8%, 66M) by 5.2% on ImageNet-1K, while also using fewer parameters. Against the strongest CNN-based SNN, MS-Res-SNN (75.3%, 77M), it gains 4.4% accuracy with 28% fewer parameters. Notably, Meta-SpikeFormer (55M, 80.0%) matches the ANN ViT baseline (86M, 79.7%) in accuracy while being 36% smaller and fully spike-driven. On COCO detection, Meta-SpikeFormer (75M) achieves 51.2% mAP@0.5, surpassing EMS-Res-SNN (26.9M, 50.1%) by 1.1% and all ANN2SNN methods (≤45.3%). On VOC2012 segmentation, it reaches 61.1% mIoU, dramatically outperforming Spiking DeepLab (22.3%) and approaching the ANN DeepLab-V3 (66.7%). On HAR-DVS, it edges out Res-SNN-34 (46.1%) by 1.4% and approaches TimeSformer (50.8%) with far fewer parameters (18.3M vs. 121.2M).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Spike-driven Transformer (Yao et al., 2023b), which first introduced spike-driven self-attention into SNNs, by reframing it within the MetaFormer architecture paradigm from Yu et al. (2022b). The key architectural departure is expanding the original four-layer spike coding stage into four full Conv-based SNN blocks (following the Conv+ViT progressive design of CAFormer), and introducing three new SDSA operators (SDSA-2/3/4) that replace the single Hadamard-product-based SDSA-1. The Membrane Shortcut and RepConv-based Q/K/V generation are also novel micro-level design choices that distinguish this work from both the prior Spike-driven Transformer and CNN-based SNN backbones like MS-Res-SNN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Meta-SpikeFormer provides the first unified SNN backbone that handles classification, detection, and segmentation via direct training, and its meta-architecture framing (swappable SDSA operators, modular Conv/Transformer blocks) offers a concrete blueprint for designing Transformer-based neuromorphic chips that currently lack SDSA support. The 80% ImageNet-1K milestone (with distillation) and 3.7% SOTA gap with 17% fewer parameters are meaningful for the SNN community, though the distillation dependency and the fact that individual components (Conv+ViT, linear attention, RepConv) are borrowed from ANN literature limit the conceptual novelty. Open questions include whether the architecture scales to language tasks, whether the SDSA operators generalize beyond vision, and whether the energy advantages persist at larger model scales where spiking firing rates may increase.
