## Problem

Piecewise E(3) equivariant point networks can model scenes where objects move independently, but they require knowing the partition of the input into rigid parts. In practice this partition is unknown and must be predicted, and prior approaches (EON, EFEM, BANANA) either assume a known partition or predict one without controlling the resulting equivariance approximation error. The paper asks: how can one design a piecewise equivariant architecture whose approximation error is provably bounded in terms of the partition prediction's uncertainty and the probability of drawing an invalid subpartition?

## Input / Output

The network consumes a 3D point cloud of n points, each carrying a spatial location and an oriented normal vector (n × 2d features, typically d=3). For segmentation it outputs per-point part labels (24 body parts on human scans, or 3–4 scene object instances on room scans); for classification it outputs a single class label. A key structural detail is that the partition into independently-transforming parts is not provided as input but is predicted internally by the network at each layer, starting from a fine Voronoi partition and progressively coarsening.

## Method

APEN's central insight is that a function equivariant under a finer partition is automatically equivariant under any coarser (true) partition, so exact piecewise equivariance is preserved as long as the predicted partition is a proper subpartition of the ground truth. The architecture is a compositional stack of L=4 layers, each applying a shared E(3) equivariant backbone (Frame Averaging symmetrizing a PointNet) per predicted part, with the partition for layer l+1 derived from layer l's equivariant features via a Gaussian Mixture Model whose variance σ_l increases across layers to encourage merging. The equivariance approximation error is bounded by (λ(Q_simple) + δ(Q))·M, where λ is the probability of drawing a 'bad' partition (containing points from multiple ground-truth parts) and δ quantifies the softness of the predicted assignment. Partition prediction is trained with an L1 loss on per-point vote targets (part-center offsets), and backpropagation through the iterative EM-based GMM fitting is handled via implicit differentiation using the Fisher information matrix.

## Results

On human body part segmentation (24 parts), APEN achieves 94.2% mean IoU on the random split, 92.2% on unseen random sequences, and 93.5% on unseen sequences, outperforming the strongest baseline EPN (89.6%, 77.8%, 84.1%) by 4.6, 14.4, and 9.4 points respectively. On the DynLab one-shot room segmentation task, APEN trained on a single scan outperforms baselines trained on a large synthetic dataset in 7 out of 8 scenes, reaching 92–98% mean IoU versus 66–97% for the best synthetic-trained baseline. On subject classification (out-of-distribution test), APEN achieves 71.4% accuracy versus 32.1% for DGCNN and 28.2% for Vector Neurons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

APEN outperforms all six baselines across every evaluation split. Against the strongest equivariant baseline EPN (Chen et al., 2021), APEN gains 4.6 points on the random split and a substantial 14.4 points on the unseen random sequence split, demonstrating the benefit of piecewise E(3) equivariance for cross-subject generalization. Frame Averaging (Puny et al., 2022), which serves as the per-part backbone inside APEN, scores 83.5/78.1/76.7 as a standalone global-equivariant model, confirming that the piecewise composition is the source of the improvement. Vector Neurons (Deng et al., 2021) and VN-Transformer (Assaad et al., 2023) perform poorly (42.4% and 63.5% on random), suggesting that their SO(3)-equivariant inductive bias is less suited to the articulated, piecewise-rigid structure of human motion. On the DynLab one-shot task, APEN trained on a single scan (e.g., 98.2% on Scene 2) surpasses DGCNN trained on the full synthetic dataset (93.7%), indicating that the piecewise equivariance prior can substitute for large-scale synthetic data. For classification, APEN's 71.4% accuracy more than doubles DGCNN's 32.1% and Vector Neurons' 28.2% on the out-of-distribution PosePrior test set.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of E(3) equivariant point networks (Tensor Field Networks, Vector Neurons, Frame Averaging, EPN) from global to piecewise equivariance, building directly on concurrent and prior work on part-based symmetry (EON, EFEM, BANANA, Liu et al.). The key conceptual departure is the observation that a finer partition which is a proper subpartition of the ground truth guarantees exact equivariance, enabling a compositional bottom-up architecture that starts with a fine Voronoi partition and progressively coarsens. Unlike prior piecewise equivariant methods that predict partitions via invariant features or optimization-based fixed-point procedures without error guarantees, APEN provides the first framework with explicit bounds on the equivariance approximation error in terms of partition prediction uncertainty and the probability of drawing a bad subpartition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The provable bounds on equivariance approximation error fill a gap in the theoretical understanding of piecewise equivariant networks, giving practitioners a principled way to control how much symmetry is violated by partition prediction errors. The one-shot generalization result—where a single-scan-trained model outperforms baselines trained on large-scale synthetic data in 7 of 8 room scenes—suggests that structural symmetry priors can substitute for data scale in certain settings. However, the authors themselves note the bounds are 'initial insights' requiring further analysis, the framework still requires ground-truth segmentation supervision for training, and the expressivity tradeoff of fine partitions (where shared backbones become constant) is acknowledged but not fully resolved. Extending the framework to generative modeling, reconstruction, and tasks without available part labels remain open directions.
