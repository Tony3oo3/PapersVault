## Problem

Existing diffusion-based human motion generation methods (MDM, PriorMDM, GMD) can only enforce spatial constraints on the pelvis trajectory, because their relative pose representations make it impossible to map global joint positions into the model's input space during the denoising process. This limitation prevents applications that require controlling arbitrary joints (e.g., hand position for object interaction, head height for collision avoidance) and makes sparse keyframe constraints particularly difficult to handle.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241111/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a sparse spatial control signal c ∈ R^{N×J×3} specifying xyz positions for a subset of joints at selected keyframes (uncontrolled joints/frames are zeroed out), and produces a full 3D human motion sequence x ∈ R^{N×D} (D=263 on HumanML3D) using relative pose representations internally. The control signal can target any combination of joints (pelvis, feet, head, wrists) simultaneously with a single model, and the sparsity level (1 to 196 keyframes) is flexible at inference time.

## Method

OmniControl builds on the MDM diffusion model and introduces two complementary guidance mechanisms applied at each denoising step. The spatial guidance converts the predicted motion from relative to global coordinates via an analytic function R(·), computes the L2 distance to the control signal, and iteratively perturbs the predicted mean μ_t via gradient descent (K_e=10 iterations in early steps, K_l=500 in later steps) — this lightweight backpropagation through only the conversion function enables many iterations per step, unlike prior classifier-guidance approaches. The realism guidance is a trainable ControlNet-style copy of the Transformer encoder that takes the text prompt and spatial signal as input and outputs per-layer residuals added to the original model's attention features, implicitly amending the whole-body motion to maintain coherence. The spatial guidance enforces precise joint positioning but cannot propagate gradients to uncontrolled joints due to the relative representation, while the realism guidance corrects the remaining joints; together they balance control accuracy and motion realism.

## Results

On HumanML3D with pelvis control, OmniControl achieves FID 0.218, R-precision 0.687, and Avg. error 0.0338, substantially outperforming all baselines. The method reduces FID by 54.1% over PriorMDM (0.475) and Avg. error by 79.2% over GMD (0.1439), while also achieving the lowest foot skating ratio (0.0547). On KIT-ML, OmniControl (on pelvis) achieves FID 0.702 and Avg. error 0.0759, again outperforming PriorMDM (FID 0.851, Avg. err. 0.2305) and GMD (FID 1.565, Avg. err. 0.4070). A single model trained on all joints achieves comparable pelvis control (FID 0.322, Avg. err. 0.0367) while also controlling feet, head, and wrists.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All three baselines (MDM, PriorMDM, GMD) are limited to pelvis-only control due to their reliance on relative pose representations, whereas OmniControl controls any joint with a single model. On pelvis control, OmniControl (on pelvis) dominates across every metric: FID 0.218 vs. 0.698 (MDM), 0.475 (PriorMDM), and 0.576 (GMD); Avg. error 0.0338 vs. 0.5959, 0.4417, and 0.1439 respectively. The most notable margin is against GMD, the previous state-of-the-art for sparse pelvis control, where OmniControl reduces Avg. error by 79.2% and FID by 62.3%. Against PriorMDM, the FID gap (54.1% reduction) highlights the benefit of the hybrid spatial + realism guidance over pure inpainting. Critically, OmniControl's multi-joint capability (controlling feet, head, wrists, and cross-joint combinations) has no direct baseline comparison, as none of the prior methods support spatial constraints on joints other than the pelvis.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniControl builds directly on MDM (Tevet et al., 2023), adopting its relative pose representations and diffusion-based generation framework, but departs from the inpainting-based control strategy used by MDM, PriorMDM, and GMD. The key conceptual shift is inverting the coordinate conversion: rather than converting global control signals into relative positions (which fails because relative pelvis positions are unknown mid-diffusion), OmniControl converts the generated motion into global coordinates for comparison, enabling control over any joint. The spatial guidance follows the analytic gradient approach of Dhariwal & Nichol (2021), while the realism guidance is architecturally inspired by ControlNet (Zhang et al., 2023b), using a trainable copy of the transformer encoder to output per-layer residuals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling a single model to control any joint at any time, OmniControl opens the door to practical applications like connecting generated human motion with surrounding objects and scenes (e.g., reaching for a cup, navigating under a low ceiling), which prior pelvis-only methods could not support. However, significant limitations remain: foot skating is still prevalent, inference takes ~121 seconds due to iterative spatial guidance, and conflicting spatial constraints on multiple joints produce unnatural motions. The long inference time and reliance on ground-truth trajectories as control signals during evaluation also limit immediate real-world deployment, pointing to future work on faster sampling and more robust physical constraints.
