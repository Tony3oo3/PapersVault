## Problem

Existing generalizable NeRF methods for sparse-view 3D modeling rely on camera poses to project 3D points onto input images for 2D-3D feature aggregation, but accurate pose estimation from sparse wide-baseline views remains highly challenging. The paper demonstrates that even state-of-the-art pose estimators produce noisy poses that significantly degrade reconstruction quality, raising the question of whether camera poses are truly indispensable for sparse-view 3D modeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240497/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

LEAP takes 2–5 sparse RGB images at 224×224 resolution with no camera pose information at inference time. It outputs a volume-based neural radiance field at 64³ resolution, defined in the local camera coordinate frame of an arbitrarily chosen canonical input view, enabling novel view synthesis from any target viewpoint specified by a relative camera pose. The entire radiance field is predicted in a single feed-forward pass (~0.3 s on a consumer GPU) without per-scene optimization.

## Method

LEAP replaces pose-based 2D-3D projection with a feature-similarity-driven attention mechanism. A DINOv2-initialized ViT extracts per-pixel features from all views; a multi-view encoder (alternating non-canonical view update via cross-attention and global consensus reasoning via self-attention) aligns features across views so the model is aware of which view serves as the canonical reference. A shared, learnable 16³ neural volume—parameterized to encode geometry and texture priors across scenes—is then updated by cross-attention where each 3D voxel queries all 2D image features and adaptively weights them by learned similarity, followed by self-attention on the volume to capture long-range geometric dependencies. The refined volume is decoded into a 64³ radiance field via 3D convolutions and rendered with standard volumetric integration. Training uses only photometric losses (MSE + LPIPS) with ground-truth poses for rendering supervision, while inference is entirely pose-free.

## Results

On four object-centric test sets, LEAP (pose-free, 0.3 s inference) achieves 29.10 / 29.86 / 28.22 / 26.77 PSNR on OmniObject3D, Kubric-ShapeNet-seen, Kubric-ShapeNet-novel, and Objaverse respectively, outperforming all pose-based baselines using SOTA predicted poses (e.g., +2.1 dB over PixelNeRF-Pred and +2.5 dB over FORGE-Pred on OmniObject3D). LEAP matches or slightly exceeds FORGE with ground-truth poses (29.10 vs 28.93 PSNR on OmniObject3D) while running 50× faster (0.3 s vs 15 min). On the scene-level DTU dataset, LEAP-pretrain reaches 18.07 PSNR, comparable to SPARF (18.57 PSNR) which requires per-scene optimization and dense correspondences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LEAP (pose-free) outperforms PixelNeRF with SOTA predicted poses (RelPose) by 2.1–3.2 dB PSNR across all four object-centric datasets, confirming that noisy pose estimation severely degrades projection-based methods. Against FORGE with predicted poses, LEAP gains 2.5–3.1 dB PSNR and is 50× faster (0.3 s vs 15 min per scene). LEAP matches or slightly exceeds FORGE with ground-truth poses (e.g., 29.10 vs 28.93 PSNR on OmniObject3D, 29.86 vs 31.32 on Kubric-seen), validating the paper's central claim that camera poses are not indispensable for sparse-view 3D modeling. LEAP substantially outperforms the other pose-free baselines: SRT by ~9 dB PSNR and Zero123 by ~12 dB PSNR, though Zero123 takes a single image input rather than multi-view. On DTU (scene-level), LEAP-pretrain (18.07 PSNR) approaches SPARF (18.57 PSNR) which requires per-scene optimization and dense correspondences, while LEAP operates in a single 0.3 s feed-forward pass.

## Relation to Prior Work

LEAP departs from the dominant paradigm in sparse-view 3D modeling (PixelNeRF, IBRNet, FORGE) that relies on camera poses to project 3D points onto 2D images for feature aggregation. It also extends the pose-free line of work initiated by SRT (2D latent representation) and RUST, but introduces a 3D-aware neural volume with attention-based 2D-3D information mapping that implicitly learns geometric relationships. The architecture repurposes DINOv2-initialized ViT features and transformer cross/self-attention mechanisms to replace explicit projection operations, while the neural volume representation draws from Plenoxels-style volume-based radiance fields.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a pose-free method can match or exceed ground-truth-pose baselines while running 400× faster, LEAP challenges a foundational assumption in 3D vision and simplifies the practical pipeline for applications like e-commerce product imaging where poses are unavailable. Key limitations include the bounded volume representation (restricting it to object-centric or small scenes), the need for larger training data than pose-based methods, and degraded performance on very fine-grained geometry. Open questions include extending to unbounded scenes, whether the implicitly learned epipolar priors transfer to fundamentally different camera configurations, and whether the approach scales to denser view counts without the compounding issues that affect pose-based methods.
