## Problem

Sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models add parameters without increasing FLOPs, yet when directly fine-tuned on individual downstream tasks they often underperform dense models of identical computational capacity, likely due to overfitting on task-specific data distributions. This paper investigates whether instruction tuning—a multi-task training stage—can resolve this gap and unlock the full potential of MoE architectures for task-agnostic learning.

## Input / Output

The models consume natural-language instructions (optionally accompanied by few-shot exemplars) as input with a maximum sequence length of 2,048 tokens, and generate text completions or discrete answers up to 512 tokens. Evaluation is conducted via exact-match accuracy under both direct prompting and chain-of-thought prompting across held-out benchmarks (MMLU, BBH) and held-in benchmarks (GSM8K, SVAMP, ASDIV, StrategyQA), with a normalized macro-average reported across four sub-scores.

## Method

FLAN-MOE replaces the feed-forward sub-layer of every other Transformer layer with a sparsely-gated MoE layer containing E independent expert networks, where a learned softmax gating function selects the top-1 or top-2 experts per token, yielding O(E²) effective feed-forward combinations. The key training procedure is instruction tuning on the FLAN collective (1,836 tasks spanning Muffin, T0-SF, Super-NaturalInstructions, and CoT), which the authors show acts as a regularizer that prevents the overfitting MoE models suffer during single-task fine-tuning. The paper systematically compares routing strategies (token-choice top-1/top-2 vs. expert-choice top-2), auxiliary losses (balancing loss vs. Z-loss), and parameter freezing schedules, finding that expert-choice routing and balancing loss are most effective. The largest model, FLAN-ST_32B (259B total parameters, 32.1 GFLOPs/token), surpasses FLAN-PaLM_62B on all four benchmarks while using less than 30% of the per-token FLOPs, demonstrating that instruction tuning is the critical enabler that makes MoE scaling advantageous.

## Results

The flagship model FLAN-ST_32B (32.1 GFLOPs/token, 259B total params) achieves 65.4% on MMLU (5-shot direct), 54.4% on BBH (3-shot direct), 66.3% on Reasoning (8-shot CoT), and a normalized average of 63.6, surpassing FLAN-PaLM_62B (91.6 GFLOPs/token) on MMLU Direct (65.4 vs. 59.6), BBH Direct (54.4 vs. 47.5), and Reasoning CoT (66.3 vs. 59.7) while using only ~35% of the FLOPs. Instruction tuning yields dramatically larger gains for MoE models than for dense models: FLAN-ST_32B improves by +45.2 points over its pre-trained counterpart, whereas FLAN-PaLM_62B improves by only +6.6 points. Across all scales from 0.06G to 13.9G FLOPs, FLAN-MOE models dominate the cost-performance Pareto frontier over FLAN-T5 dense models, with the largest absolute MMLU Direct gains of ~7.1 points at small-to-medium scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against dense T5 baselines (Raffel et al.), FLAN-MOE models at matched FLOPs consistently outperform FLAN-T5: at 13.9G FLOPs, FLAN-Switch_XXL (54.2 norm. avg.) beats FLAN-T5_XXL (51.7), and at 3.6G FLOPs, FLAN-EC_XL (49.4) edges out FLAN-T5_XL (48.0). Against the dense PaLM family (Chowdhery et al.), the headline result is FLAN-ST_32B (32.1G FLOPs) surpassing FLAN-PaLM_62B (91.6G FLOPs) on MMLU Direct (65.4 vs. 59.6), BBH Direct (54.4 vs. 47.5), and Reasoning CoT (66.3 vs. 59.7), though it trails on QA Direct (63.9 vs. 85.3). Among MoE routing strategies, expert-choice (FLAN-EC, Zhou et al.) consistently outperforms token-choice GShard (FLAN-GS, Lepikhin et al.) at the same scale—e.g., at XL: 49.4 vs. 48.7 norm. avg.—and both are substantially outperformed by the ST-MoE architecture (Zoph et al.) with its advanced auxiliary loss and pre-training strategy. The Switch Transformer (Fedus et al.) top-1 gating variant shows the weakest pre-training quality (near-zero BBH scores before instruction tuning) but recovers strongly after FLAN tuning (+36.4 at XXL). Overall, the paper demonstrates that instruction tuning is the critical enabler that unlocks MoE's parameter advantage: without it, MoE models underperform dense counterparts on single-task finetuning, but with it, they dominate across all evaluated benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: sparse Mixture-of-Experts architectures (Switch Transformer, GShard, ST-MoE) and instruction tuning (FLAN, T0, Super-NaturalInstructions). Prior MoE research focused on pre-training stability and single-task finetuning, while instruction tuning work was conducted almost exclusively on dense models. The key conceptual departure is demonstrating that these two techniques are synergistic rather than merely additive: MoE models, which underperform dense counterparts on direct task-specific finetuning, actually benefit disproportionately from instruction tuning, reversing the performance hierarchy. The largest model (FLAN-ST_32B) is built directly on ST-MoE pre-trained weights and fine-tuned on the FLAN collective dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that FLAN-ST_32B matches or exceeds FLAN-PaLM_62B on four benchmarks while using only one-third the FLOPs per token has direct practical implications for deploying large language models at scale, suggesting that sparse architectures combined with instruction tuning can deliver near-frontier performance at a fraction of the compute cost. However, significant limitations remain: all FLAN-MOE models perform poorly on multilingual benchmarks (e.g., 15.5% on MGSM for the largest model), and expert specialization increases with scale, raising concerns about robustness. Open questions include whether decoder-only MoE architectures benefit similarly, how to mitigate the multilingual degradation, and whether the instruction-tuning advantage persists as both model scale and task diversity grow further.
