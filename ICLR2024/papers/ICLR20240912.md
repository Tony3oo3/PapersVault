## Problem

Existing street view generation methods (BEVGen, BEVControl) rely on projecting 3D annotations into 2D BEV maps or image-space layouts, which inevitably loses critical geometric dimensions—height in BEVGen and depth/occlusion in BEVControl. This loss undermines the fidelity needed for downstream 3D perception tasks such as 3D object detection and BEV segmentation, where object height, road elevation, and occlusion patterns are essential.

## Input / Output

The method takes four condition types: a binary BEV road map (8 semantic classes over a w×h meter area), a variable-length set of 3D bounding boxes (each with 8 corner points in R^{8×3} and a class label), a camera pose (3×3 intrinsics, 3×3 rotation, 3×1 translation), and a free-text scene description. It produces 6 synchronized first-person-view street images at 224×400 or 272×736 resolution, and can be extended to 7- or 16-frame video clips. Bounding boxes are filtered per view by a visibility criterion (at least one corner in front of the camera), and the road map remains in BEV format without explicit projection to the camera view.

## Method

MAGICDRIVE builds on Stable Diffusion v1.5 and injects conditions through three distinct encoding pathways: scene-level (camera pose via Fourier embedding + MLP, concatenated with CLIP text embeddings) and foreground (3D boxes encoded per-box via Fourier embedding of corner points plus pooled class-name embeddings, injected through cross-attention) are treated as sequential data, while the background road map is encoded by a trainable additive UNet encoder branch (ControlNet-style) that receives scene-level and box embeddings as auxiliary inputs to implicitly learn BEV-to-FPV view transformation. A cross-view attention module is inserted after each cross-attention layer in the UNet, allowing each view to attend to its immediate left and right neighbors with zero-initialization for stable training. During training, only newly added parameters are optimized (AdamW, lr 8e-5, batch 24), with classifier-free guidance applied by jointly dropping scene-level conditions at rate 0.2 while retaining box and map conditions (which have natural null representations), and unique noise is sampled per view to prevent trivial cross-view copying.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, MAGICDRIVE achieves a FID of 16.20 at 224×400 resolution, substantially outperforming BEVGen (25.54) and BEVControl (24.85). For controllability, MAGICDRIVE attains Road mIoU of 61.05 and Vehicle mIoU of 27.01 (BEV segmentation via CVT), and mAP of 20.85 / NDS of 30.26 (3D object detection via BEVFusion) at 272×736 resolution. As a data augmentation tool, MAGICDRIVE synthetic data boosts BEVFusion's 3D detection mAP by +2.52 (CAM-only) and +2.46 (CAM+LiDAR), and improves CVT's BEV segmentation Vehicle mIoU by +4.34 and Road mIoU by +5.26.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAGICDRIVE outperforms both baselines on all shared metrics. Against BEVGen, MAGICDRIVE reduces FID from 25.54 to 16.20 (a 36% improvement) and dramatically improves Vehicle mIoU from 5.89 to 27.01, while also surpassing it on Road mIoU (61.05 vs. 50.20). Against BEVControl, MAGICDRIVE achieves a lower FID (16.20 vs. 24.85) and marginally better Road mIoU (61.05 vs. 60.80) and Vehicle mIoU (27.01 vs. 26.80) at 224×400. In the data augmentation setting for BEV segmentation, MAGICDRIVE improves CVT's Vehicle mIoU by +4.34 and Road mIoU by +5.26, whereas BEVGen only yields +0.60 on Vehicle mIoU and actually degrades Road mIoU by −2.40. For 3D object detection augmentation, MAGICDRIVE consistently improves BEVFusion across both CAM-only and CAM+LiDAR settings, with the largest gains on smaller and more diverse object classes (e.g., trailer +3.19, cone +3.10, bicycle +3.00 AP).

## Relation to Prior Work

MAGICDRIVE builds directly on the Latent Diffusion Model (Stable Diffusion) pipeline and ControlNet's additive encoder branch, but departs from prior street-view generation methods (BEVGen, BEVControl) that collapse all 3D geometry into a single BEV map or projected 2D layout, thereby losing height or depth information. The key architectural departure is a separation-of-concerns encoding strategy: cross-attention for variable-length 3D bounding boxes (analogous to text tokens), an additive UNet encoder for grid-structured BEV road maps, and Fourier-embedded MLPs for camera poses—each matched to the data's structural properties. For multi-camera consistency, the paper adapts the cross-view attention concept from indoor multi-view diffusion (MVDiffusion) to the sparse, asymmetric camera layout of driving, attending only to adjacent views rather than all views.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MAGICDRIVE provides a practical pipeline for generating high-fidelity, geometry-accurate synthetic street-view data that directly improves downstream 3D perception (BEV segmentation mIoU +4.34, 3D detection mAP +2.52), addressing a real bottleneck in autonomous driving where annotated data is expensive. The conceptual insight that explicit BEV-to-FPV geometric transformation is unnecessary when the additive encoder receives camera pose and 3D box cues alongside the map is a useful simplification that avoids the ill-posed back-projection problem in BEVControl. Open questions include whether detectors can be trained solely on synthetic data, cross-domain generalization to unseen weather or camera configurations, and whether the separate-encoding design generalizes beyond the nuScenes 6-camera setup.
