## Problem

Safety alignment datasets used to train harmless language models inevitably contain label errors from human annotators, yet the cost of manual re-annotation is prohibitive (e.g., ~$2M for ten annotations per instance on Jigsaw Civil Comments). There is no systematic, training-free framework to quantify dataset credibility, identify specific mislabeled instances, and correct them at scale.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a labeled text dataset (binary or multi-class) and a pretrained sentence-transformer that produces embedding vectors for each instance; k-NN relationships are computed in this embedding space under the assumption of k-NN label clusterability. Outputs include a scalar data credibility score in [0,1] (1 = perfect labels), an estimated K×K noise transition matrix, and instance-level flags identifying corrupted labels with proposed corrections. The framework is training-free for the cleaning step itself, requiring only the embedding model and the optimization solver.

## Method

Docta combines two components: (1) noise transition matrix estimation via k-NN consensus vectors, where empirical frequencies of label agreement patterns among 2-NN neighbors (up to 3rd order) are matched to analytical expressions involving the transition matrix T and class prior p through a softmax-parameterized unconstrained optimization; and (2) corrupted label detection using a cosine-similarity scoring function between soft k-NN label distributions and one-hot class indicators, with a per-class threshold derived from Bayes' rule applied to the estimated T. The data credibility metric is defined as 1 minus the normalized Frobenius distance between the estimated T and the identity matrix. Applied to 11 datasets across Jigsaw Civil Comments, Anthropic Harmless/Red-Team, and PKU BeaverTails/SafeRLHF, the framework identifies an average of 6.16% label errors and improves downstream F1/accuracy by 5–20 points when models are fine-tuned on the cleaned data.

## Results

The Docta framework identifies and corrects an average of 6.16% label errors across 11 safety-alignment datasets, with the noisiest dataset (Anthropic Harmless) containing 15.11% errors and the cleanest (Civil Comments Obscene) containing 2.22%. Data credibility scores improve substantially after cleaning, e.g., Anthropic Harmless rises from 50.0 to 76.7 and Jigsaw Civil Comments Toxicity from 73.6 to 80.0. Downstream, fine-tuning BERT on Docta-cleaned data yields 68.81% accuracy on Harmless (vs. 49.72% raw) and 92.14% on BeaverTails (vs. 88.44% raw); GPT-2 achieves 65.82% on Harmless (vs. 50.28% raw). Llama2 on the pairwise Harmless task improves from 63.74% to 70.32% accuracy after cleaning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external label-cleaning baselines; the primary comparison is between models trained on raw (uncleaned) labels versus those trained on Docta-cleaned labels. Docta consistently outperforms raw labels across all four conversation datasets and both base models (BERT, GPT-2) under both i.i.d. and consensus test splits. The most dramatic gain is on Anthropic Harmless, where BERT accuracy jumps from 49.72% to 68.81% (+19.09 pp) and GPT-2 from 50.28% to 65.82% (+15.54 pp) on the i.i.d. split, reflecting the 15.11% label error rate in that dataset. On BeaverTails, BERT improves by 3.70 pp (88.44→92.14) and GPT-2 by 5.31 pp (86.15→91.46). On the consensus test split (where only labels agreed upon by both raw and Docta are retained), gains are smaller but still positive, confirming that the improvements are not an artifact of the cleaning algorithm's own bias. A controlled study with in-house human annotators further validates that 68.71% of human-identified label errors are detected by the algorithm, and the cleaning pipeline reduces human verification cost by approximately 90%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper explicitly positions itself as a synthesis rather than a methodological novelty: it assembles the consensus-vector transition matrix estimator (Zhu et al., 2021b), the training-free corrupted-label detection via k-NN scoring (Zhu et al., 2022a), and the identifiability guarantees (Liu et al., 2023) into a unified, training-free pipeline (Docta) applied to safety-alignment datasets. It departs from the broader noisy-label-learning literature, which typically targets robust loss functions or co-teaching strategies, by focusing exclusively on a data-centric cleaning step that can be plugged into existing RLHF or supervised fine-tuning pipelines. The paper also situates itself alongside toxicity-benchmark work (Blodgett et al., 2021) that flagged noise concerns but did not provide a systematic correction framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that widely-used safety datasets from major labs (Anthropic, PKU) contain 4–15% label errors has direct practical implications for anyone building harmless language models, as these errors propagate into model behavior. The ~90% cost reduction over pure human re-annotation makes large-scale data auditing feasible, and the open-source Docta tool lowers the barrier for practitioners. Key limitations include the reliance on k-NN label clusterability (which may fail for highly ambiguous or borderline examples), the assumption of a constant transition matrix, and the acknowledged risk that the algorithm occasionally flips correct labels. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to multi-annotator disagreement settings, to non-binary harm dimensions, and to the increasingly common LLM-as-annotator paradigm where 'noise' has a different structure than human annotation error.
