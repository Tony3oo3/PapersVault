## Problem

Virus capsid detection in electron microscopy images requires expert-annotated bounding boxes that are expensive and time-consuming to produce, yet existing weakly supervised methods either still demand fine-grained object-level annotations (locations, bounding boxes) or rely on ROI candidates from selective search that limit localization accuracy. This paper asks whether image-level binary annotations alone—significantly easier and faster to obtain—can suffice for accurate virus detection when combined with domain-specific knowledge about virus size and shape.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241388/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224 EM image patch, a known virus radius (in nm, retrieved from literature), and a pre-trained ResNet-101 binary classifier trained on image-level presence/absence labels. It outputs bounding boxes for all detected virus capsids along with per-box detection scores computed by masking all other detected viruses and forwarding the result through the classifier. A non-maximum suppression step exploits the domain constraint that virus particles do not overlap in the image plane.

## Method

The core mechanism is a gradient-based optimization that directly regresses virus positions from a frozen image-level classifier, eliminating the need for ROI candidates or a separate detection network. A fully differentiable Gaussian mask centered at the current position estimate is applied to the input image, and the position is updated via gradient descent to maximize the classifier score. Inspired by score-based generative models, the mask's standard deviation starts large (spanning the full image) and decays exponentially to 0.5× the virus radius, allowing gradients to propagate from distant initial positions to the true virus location. Initialization uses the center of mass of the top 1% GradCAM activations to reduce optimization steps. After each detection, the identified virus is masked out with a circular disk of known radius before the next particle is searched, and the loop terminates when the classifier predicts no remaining viruses.

## Results

On the five virus TEM datasets, Ours(OD) (Faster-RCNN trained on pseudo-labels from the optimization) achieves the highest mAP50 on 4 of 5 viruses: Herpes 91.20, Noro 74.32, Papilloma 78.33, and Rota 78.34, outperforming the fully supervised bounding-box baseline (BB, 89.18 on Herpes) and all weakly supervised methods. The direct optimization variant Ours(Opt) also leads among weakly supervised approaches on most viruses (e.g., 86.98 on Herpes, 70.02 on Papilloma, 71.73 on Rota). The only exception is Adeno virus, where CutLER (68.49) slightly edges out Ours(OD) (58.28). A user study confirms that binary image-level labels are faster to annotate and less error-prone than location or bounding-box labels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ours(OD) outperforms the location-label baseline (Matuszewski & Sintorn, 'Loc') by a wide margin on all viruses, e.g., 91.20 vs. 88.13 on Herpes and 74.32 vs. 0.82 on Noro, where Loc essentially fails. Against GradCAM (Selvaraju) and LayerCAM (Jiang) in both ResNet and ViT variants, Ours(OD) leads on every virus by 10–70 mAP50 points (e.g., 91.20 vs. 78.79 for GradCAM ResNet on Herpes; 78.33 vs. 11.57 for GradCAM ResNet on Papilloma). TS-CAM (Gao) is the weakest CAM-based competitor, scoring below 44 on all viruses, while Reattention (Su) is the strongest weakly supervised baseline (58.49 on Adeno, 55.09 on Noro) yet still trails Ours(OD) by 10–43 points. The classical DoG keypoint detector (Lowe) achieves only 14–22 mAP50 across viruses, confirming that hand-crafted features are unreliable in noisy EM data. The sole exception to Ours(OD)'s dominance is Adeno virus, where CutLER (68.49) surpasses Ours(OD) (58.28), likely because Adeno's larger, high-contrast particles are well-suited to zero-shot segmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the weakly supervised object detection (WSOD) literature by replacing the standard ROI-candidate pipeline (selective search, sliding windows, MIL) with a direct gradient-based optimization of bounding box positions guided by a pre-trained image-level classifier. The shrinking Gaussian mask strategy is explicitly inspired by the progressive noise-schedule of score-based generative models (Song & Ermon, 2019), repurposing a generative-modeling mechanism for localization. In the EM-specific line of work, it departs from Matuszewski & Sintorn's minimal center-point annotations and Devan et al.'s single-virus crop classification by requiring only binary image-level labels, and from Lu et al.'s geometry-constrained network by eliminating the need for a secondary neural network to predict mask parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for virology and EM research groups where expert bounding-box annotation is the bottleneck: the user study demonstrates that binary labels are faster, less error-prone, and require less mental effort, while the detection pipeline recovers competitive or superior mAP at equal annotation budgets. A key limitation is the reliance on known virus size (performance degrades ~20 mAP at 30% size error) and the observation that ViT backbones underperform ResNet-101 on these small datasets, suggesting the method is best suited to the small-data, domain-specific EM setting. The authors identify extending the approach to objects with variable size as the primary open question, and the finding that pseudo-labels can outperform ground-truth bounding boxes (91.2 vs 89.2 mAP50 on Herpes) hints at a broader principle about annotation quality vs. quantity that warrants further investigation.
