## Problem

Classical Graph Neural Networks are designed for static graphs and cannot effectively leverage temporal information from nodes and edges that appear or change at different timestamps. While Temporal GNNs have advanced for node-level tasks like link prediction, graph-level property prediction in temporal graphs—such as forecasting whether a network will grow or shrink—remains largely unexplored, with no principled framework for capturing how global graph structure evolves over time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240287/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of 24-hour snapshot graphs, each annotated with four node features (outgoing/incoming edge weight sums and edge counts). These are processed into two parallel feature streams: three graph-level statistics from the raw snapshots (node count, edge count, average edge weight) and five from the induced Mapper networks (node count, edge count, max/avg cluster size, average edge weight), yielding sequences of shape (7,3) and (7,5) respectively. The output is a binary classification indicating whether the network will have more edges in the next 7-day window than in the preceding 7-day window, evaluated via ROC-AUC.

## Method

GraphPulse operates in three phases: (1) partitioning the temporal graph into daily snapshots, (2) constructing a TDA Mapper network for each snapshot by projecting node features into 2D via t-SNE, applying KMeans clustering within overlapping intervals (cls=5, n_cubes=2, perc_overlap=0.4), and building the 1D nerve of the induced cover, and (3) feeding the fused feature sequences into a hybrid LSTM-GRU model for sequential prediction. The key insight is framing graph evolution as a trajectory in a Newtonian phase space (parameterized by |V| and power-law exponent α for token networks), where Mapper networks provide compressed, noise-robust topological summaries that remain stable under small parameter perturbations—empirically validated by showing Mapper-based dissimilarity scores stay near-constant across phase-space neighborhoods while raw graph dissimilarity scores fluctuate significantly. This topological compression captures essential structural transitions (e.g., emergence of new clusters or connectivity changes) while filtering out per-node noise, and the complementary information from both snapshot and Mapper features yields a median AUC of 0.867, surpassing either feature set alone.

## Results

GraphPulse achieves a mean ROC-AUC of 0.849 across nine temporal network datasets, outperforming the best state-of-the-art temporal GNN (HTGN, mean AUC 0.770) by approximately 10.2%. GraphPulse attains the highest AUC in 8 out of 9 datasets, with the largest margins on Adex (0.8928 vs. 0.7330 for HTGN) and Reddit-Body (0.8692 vs. 0.6557). The only dataset where GraphPulse does not lead is Centra, where HTGN achieves 0.9044 versus GraphPulse's 0.8670. Ablation studies confirm that combining both snapshot graph features and Mapper network features yields a median AUC of 0.8670, surpassing either feature set in isolation (0.7981 for snapshot-only, 0.8501 for Mapper-only).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GraphPulse is compared against two static GNN baselines (GIN, TDA-GIN) and three temporal GNN baselines (EvolveGCN, GRUGCN, HTGN). Against the static baselines, GraphPulse dominates: GIN exceeds 0.5 AUC in only 4 of 9 datasets, and TDA-GIN (which adds Mapper cluster membership as node features) improves in 5 datasets but still trails GraphPulse by 0.15–0.35 AUC on most. Among the temporal GNNs, HTGN is the strongest competitor with a mean AUC of 0.770, followed by EvolveGCN (0.764) and GRUGCN (0.759). GraphPulse outperforms HTGN on 8 of 9 datasets, with the largest gap on Reddit-Body (+0.214 AUC) and Adex (+0.160 AUC); the sole exception is Centra, where HTGN leads by 0.037 AUC. The ablation further shows that the Mapper-based features alone (F_Mapper-RNN, median 0.8501) already surpass the best temporal GNN, and the full GraphPulse pipeline (combining snapshot + Mapper features) pushes the median to 0.8670, confirming that topological and structural features are complementary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Mapper algorithm from Topological Data Analysis—previously applied to static graph representation learning (Hajij et al., 2018; Bodnar et al., 2021)—into the temporal graph setting, which the authors note is the first such use. It departs from the dominant TGNN paradigm (EvolveGCN, GRUGCN, HTGN) that models temporal evolution through learned graph convolutions and recurrent updates, instead framing graph evolution as a trajectory in a Newtonian phase space and extracting topological summaries via Mapper before feeding them to a hybrid LSTM-GRU. The phase space concept draws on dynamical systems theory and is empirically validated against Erdős-Rényi and Barabási-Albert generative models, but for real networks it reduces to a power-law exponent fit, making the connection to the theoretical framework somewhat loose.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces a previously unformalized task—predicting future graph-level properties (edge growth, node growth, density) in discrete-time dynamic graphs—and contributes seven new ERC20 token benchmark datasets, filling a gap the authors identify in the TGNN literature which has focused almost exclusively on node- and edge-level tasks. A key open question is whether the phase space trajectory framework generalizes beyond the small token networks tested here (most under 200 nodes/day) and whether the quadratic tSNE bottleneck limits practical adoption at scale. The finding that Mapper's compressed topological summaries are more stable under small perturbations than raw snapshot graphs suggests a broader principle: topological invariants may be more informative than raw structural features for tracking graph evolution, but this remains to be validated on larger, more heterogeneous temporal networks.
