## Problem

Existing camouflaged object detection (COD) detectors still produce incomplete segmentation (missing key object parts) and ambiguous boundaries in extreme camouflage scenarios where objects are visually indistinguishable from their surroundings. The paper addresses this gap by drawing on the prey-vs-predator evolutionary game: making the 'prey' (generated camouflaged objects) more deceptive to force the 'predator' (detector) to develop more acute detection capabilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241438/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image resized to 352×352 and produces a binary segmentation mask at full resolution, along with multi-scale edge reconstructions (4 levels) as auxiliary outputs. The Camouflageator framework additionally generates synthesized camouflaged images (same resolution as input) that are visually consistent with the original in the background region but have smoothed, less discriminative foreground and edge features. The detector is pre-trained for 100 epochs on real data, then fine-tuned for 30 epochs under the adversarial framework.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary components. On the prey side, Camouflageator is a plug-and-play adversarial training framework that alternates between training a ResUNet generator to synthesize harder-to-detect camouflaged objects (using a background-only fidelity loss, a concealment loss that smooths foreground and edge pixels toward image-level prototypes, and an adversarial loss that pushes the detector to predict an all-zero mask) and training any existing COD detector on the synthesized images. On the predator side, ICEG introduces a Camouflaged Feature Coherence (CFC) module that aggregates intra-layer multi-scale features (parallel 3×3 and 5×5 convolutions with element-wise multiplication) and inter-layer contextual features (channel + spatial attention), constrained by a camouflaged consistency loss that minimizes internal feature variance while maximizing foreground-background feature separation. The Edge-Guided Segmentation Decoder (ESD) combines an edge reconstruction branch with an Edge-Guided Separated Calibration (ESC) module that splits features into foreground and background branches via sigmoid masks from the coarser prediction, then applies adaptive normalization (variational σ and μ derived from edge features) to reinforce edge prominence and eliminate uncertainty fringes.

## Results

On the four COD benchmarks (CHAMELEON, CAMO, COD10K, NC4K), ICEG with ResNet50 achieves Fβ of 0.858, 0.789, 0.747, and 0.814 respectively, outperforming the previous best (FEDER) by 2.1% on average. With the Camouflageator framework (ICEG+), performance further improves to Fβ of 0.863, 0.805, 0.763, and 0.835, setting new state-of-the-art across all four datasets. ICEG also dominates with Res2Net50 (5.2% over BGNet) and Swin Transformer (8.3% over ICON) backbones, and the Camouflageator framework is shown to be plug-and-play, improving PreyNet by 2.8%, FGANet by 2.2%, and FEDER by 2.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICEG outperforms all nine compared baselines across all four datasets and all four metrics. Against the closest ResNet50 competitor FEDER (He et al., CVPR 2023), ICEG gains +0.8% Fβ on CHAMELEON (0.858 vs 0.850), +1.4% on CAMO (0.789 vs 0.775), +3.2% on COD10K (0.747 vs 0.715), and +0.6% on NC4K (0.814 vs 0.808). Against PreyNet (Zhang et al., ACM MM 2022) and FGANet (Zhai et al., NeurIPS 2022), ICEG leads by 1.4–3.9% Fβ depending on dataset. With Res2Net50, ICEG surpasses BGNet (Sun et al., 2022) by 5.2% Fβ on COD10K (0.752 vs 0.714) and SINetV2 (Fan et al., TPAMI 2021) by 7.0%. With Swin Transformer, ICEG beats ICON (Zhuge et al., TPAMI 2022) by 8.3% Fβ on COD10K (0.782 vs 0.720). In the multi-input-scale setting, ICEG outperforms ZoomNet (Pang et al., CVPR 2022) by 1.1% Fβ on COD10K. In the supplementary PVT V2 comparison, ICEG exceeds HitNet (Hu et al., AAAI 2023) by 9.6% Fβ on COD10K (0.805 vs 0.790). The Camouflageator framework further boosts ICEG by 1.4–2.5% Fβ depending on backbone, and improves other detectors (PreyNet, FGANet, FEDER) by 2.2–2.8%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the well-established camouflaged object detection (COD) literature, building on multi-scale feature aggregation methods (PreyNet, FGANet), edge-guided detectors (FEDER, BGNet), and multi-stage frameworks (SegMaR, ZoomNet). It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) it repurposes adversarial training—previously applied to COD only for uncertainty estimation (JCOD) or depth weighting (ADENet)—as a plug-and-play data augmentation strategy that generates harder camouflaged objects to strengthen any existing detector; and (2) it introduces a camouflaged feature coherence module that explicitly models internal object consistency, a dimension not addressed by prior multi-scale or edge-based approaches. The prey-vs-predator framing is a conceptual reorganization of the adversarial training paradigm rather than a fundamentally new mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Camouflageator framework's plug-and-play nature makes it practically valuable: it can be attached to any existing COD detector (demonstrated on PreyNet, FGANet, FEDER, and ICEG) to yield consistent 2–3% improvements without architectural changes, which is useful for practitioners who want to boost performance without redesigning their pipeline. However, the paper acknowledges that transformer-based backbones (PVT V2) benefit less from Camouflageator, suggesting the generator's capacity is insufficient for stronger feature extractors. Open questions include whether the framework extends to other fine-grained detection tasks (saliency, medical segmentation, industrial defects), how to handle objects with near-identical structural information to the background (a documented failure mode), and whether the approach generalizes to degraded imaging conditions (low light, haze).
