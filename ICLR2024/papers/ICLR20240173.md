## Problem

Standard transformer attention captures only pairwise token correlations and provably cannot detect triple-wise connections among words. Sanford et al. (2023) proposed a tensor generalization using column-wise Kronecker products to capture higher-order correlations, but left open the fundamental question of whether this more expressive computation can be performed efficiently. This paper tightly resolves the computational complexity, showing a sharp threshold at entry magnitude B = Θ(∛(log n)) separating near-linear-time solvability from cubic-time hardness.

## Input / Output

The method takes five n×d matrices (Q, K₁, K₂, V₁, V₂) with all entries bounded by B, and produces an n×d matrix T approximating D⁻¹exp(Q(K₁⊘K₂)ᵀ/d)(V₁⊘V₂) in ℓ∞ norm. The column-wise Kronecker product K₁⊘K₂ yields an n²×d matrix whose (i₁,i₂)-th row, j-th entry equals (K₁)_{i₁,j}·(K₂)_{i₂,j}, so the attention matrix A inherently has n³ entries. The natural regime is d = O(log n) and ε_a = 1/poly(n), with the critical transition occurring at B = Θ(∛(log n))—stricter than the √(log n) threshold for standard attention.

## Method

The upper bound applies the polynomial method to approximate exp entry-wise on the low-rank tensor Q⊙K₁⊙K₂/d, yielding a rank-r tensor decomposition U₁⊙U₂⊙U₃ where r = O((g+d)^{3g}) and the polynomial degree g scales with B³. A key algebraic identity (C₁∘C₂ = AᵀB for column-wise Kronecker products) allows all multiplications involving the n²×d Kronecker matrices to collapse to O(nd) operations, giving n^{1+o(1)} total time when B < o(∛(log n)). The lower bound introduces Gap-MaxIP as an intermediate problem, proves its hardness via a four-party Merlin-Arthur communication protocol for 3-disjointness built on algebraic geometry codes, and reduces Gap-MaxIP to tensor attention by encoding 3-way inner products into exponential attention scores. The framework generalizes to k-th order tensors, yielding a natural tradeoff: capturing k-wise correlations requires entries bounded by o(√[k](log n)) for near-linear time, so higher expressiveness demands tighter entry bounds.

## Results

The paper establishes tight computational complexity bounds for approximate tensor attention (ATAttC) with a sharp threshold at B = Θ((log n)^(1/3)). Below this threshold (B = o((log n)^(1/3))), a near-linear time n^(1+o(1)) algorithm exists (Theorem 1.4); above it (B = Ω((log n)^(1/3))), no algorithm runs faster than n^(3−o(1)) assuming SETH (Theorem 1.3). The results generalize to k-th order tensor attention, where the threshold becomes B = (log n)^(1/k), yielding a natural tradeoff between entry boundedness and expressiveness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is with the prior complexity analysis of standard (non-tensor) attention by Alman & Song (2023), which established the analogous threshold B = Θ(√(log n)) for the k=2 case. The tensor generalization (k=3) tightens this threshold to B = Θ((log n)^(1/3)), meaning entries must be smaller for the near-linear algorithm to apply. The tensor attention formulation itself is taken from Sanford et al. (2023), who showed it can detect triple-wise correlations that standard attention provably cannot. For the hardness proof, the paper introduces a new intermediate problem (Gap-MaxIP) that generalizes the approximate Hamming Nearest Neighbor problem used in prior work, since no natural 'distance' function exists for triples of vectors. The algorithmic approach extends the polynomial method from Alman & Song (2023) from low-rank matrices to low-rank tensors, leveraging the column-wise Kronecker product structure to maintain near-linear running time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the computational complexity analysis of Alman & Song (2023) for standard (pairwise) attention to the tensor generalization proposed by Sanford et al. (2023), which uses column-wise Kronecker products to capture triple-wise (and higher-order) correlations. The algorithmic side generalizes the polynomial-method low-rank approximation of exp from the matrix setting to the tensor setting, while the hardness side introduces a new intermediate problem (Gap-MaxIP) and a four-party Merlin-Arthur protocol using algebraic geometry codes, generalizing the three-party protocol of Rubinstein (2018). The key conceptual departure is identifying the entry-boundedness threshold as ∛(log n) for tensor attention (versus √(log n) for regular attention), and showing this tightens to (log n)^(1/k) for k-th order tensors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a tight, SETH-conditional characterization of when tensor attention can be computed in near-linear time, giving practitioners a clear criterion (entry magnitudes below ∛(log n)) for when the more expressive tensor generalization remains tractable. The natural tradeoff between entry boundedness and tensor order suggests a principled way to choose the expressiveness level of attention for a given model's activation magnitudes. Open questions include whether the polynomial-method approach can be made practical at scale, whether the SETH assumption can be removed, and whether the k-th order generalization yields measurable improvements in language modeling quality beyond the expressivity results of Sanford et al. (2023).
