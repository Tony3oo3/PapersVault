## Problem

Existing Zero-Shot Compositional Image Retrieval (ZS-CIR) methods, while avoiding triplet annotation, still require training task-specific textual inversion modules on large image-caption datasets to map query images into CLIP's text embedding space. This introduces a dependency on a specific VLM architecture, produces non-interpretable token embeddings, and relies on rigid template filling that cannot flexibly handle diverse textual modifiers. The paper asks whether CIR can be performed entirely training-free by recombining off-the-shelf VLMs and LLMs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a query image (any resolution, resized for the captioning VLM) and a free-form text modifier describing hypothetical semantic changes (e.g., 'without people and at night-time'). It produces a retrieved target image from a candidate database, with two intermediate textual artifacts: a natural-language caption of the query image and an LLM-generated target caption that encodes the modifier's effect. Because all intermediate representations are plain text, a human can inspect, edit, or intervene at any stage of the pipeline.

## Method

CIReVL is a three-stage, training-free pipeline that abstracts compositional retrieval into the language domain. First, a generative VLM (BLIP-2 with Flan-T5-XXL, or CoCa) captions the query image, replacing learned textual inversion with interpretable natural-language descriptions. Second, an LLM (GPT-3.5-turbo, GPT-4, Llama2-70B, or Vicuna-13B) receives the caption and the text modifier via a simple prompt and generates a unified target-image description, performing flexible semantic reasoning that static templates cannot. Third, a CLIP model (ViT-B/32 through ViT-G/14) encodes the target caption and candidate images into a shared embedding space for cosine-similarity retrieval. The key design insight is full modularity: each component (captioner, LLM, retrieval backbone) can be independently swapped or scaled without retraining, enabling plug-and-play scaling that more than doubles prior ZS-CIR results on CIRCO and CIRR.

## Results

On CIRCO (the cleanest ZS-CIR benchmark with multiple positives), CIReVL achieves mAP@5 of 14.94% with ViT-B/32 and 18.57% with ViT-L/14, substantially outperforming the best trained baseline SEARLE (9.35% and 11.68% respectively). Scaling the retrieval backbone to ViT-G/14 (OpenCLIP) pushes CIRCO mAP@5 to 26.77%, more than doubling the best prior result. On CIRR, CIReVL matches SEARLE on Recall@1 (24.55% vs 24.24% with ViT-L/14) while significantly surpassing it on the subset retrieval metric R_s@1 (60.17% vs 54.89% with ViT-B/32). On Fashion-IQ, CIReVL achieves average R@10 of 28.55% (ViT-L/14) and 32.19% (ViT-G/14), outperforming SEARLE (25.56%) by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CIReVL (training-free) decisively outperforms all four trained baselines on CIRCO: mAP@5 of 14.94% (ViT-B/32) vs. SEARLE's 9.35%, Pic2Word's 8.72% (ViT-L/14), and PALAVRA's 4.61%, with the gap widening to 18.57% vs. 11.68% at ViT-L/14. On CIRR, CIReVL roughly matches SEARLE on Recall@1 (23.94% vs. 24.00% at ViT-B/32; 24.55% vs. 24.24% at ViT-L/14) but clearly wins on the subset-retrieval metric R_s@1 (60.17% vs. 54.89%), reflecting better compositional reasoning. On Fashion-IQ, CIReVL surpasses SEARLE by ~3 points in average R@10 (28.55% vs. 25.56% at ViT-L/14) and Pic2Word by ~4 points (28.55% vs. 24.70%). On GeneCIS, CIReVL (15.9% avg R@1) slightly trails the large-data Combiner(CC3M) (16.8%) but beats Combiner(CIRR) (14.0%) and SEARLE (14.4%), all without any task-specific training. The key takeaway is that CIReVL's training-free, modular pipeline matches or exceeds methods that require training on curated triplets or large image-caption datasets, and its plug-and-play scalability (swapping in ViT-G/14) more than doubles prior CIRCO results without any re-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

CIReVL departs from the dominant ZS-CIR paradigm of training task-specific textual inversion modules (Pic2Word, SEARLE, PALAVRA) that map images into CLIP's text-token embedding space, instead repurposing the 'vision-by-language' paradigm by chaining off-the-shelf captioning VLMs (BLIP-2, CoCa), LLMs (GPT-3.5/4, Llama2, Vicuna), and CLIP retrieval without any task-specific training. Conceptually, it reframes CIR as a modular language-domain problem—captioning, reasoning, and cross-modal retrieval—rather than a joint embedding-space problem requiring learned inversion functions. The approach draws on the broader trend of using language as an abstraction layer for visual reasoning (Zeng et al., 2023; Zhu et al., 2023) but is the first to apply this pipeline end-to-end for compositional retrieval with competitive or superior results to trained baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, modular design eliminates the costly triplet annotation bottleneck and enables plug-and-play scaling of individual components (e.g., swapping CLIP ViT-B/32 for ViT-G/14 more than doubles CIRCO mAP@5), offering a practical path to improving ZS-CIR as better off-the-shelf models become available. The TIFA-based analysis revealing CLIP retrieval as the primary bottleneck—rather than captioning or LLM reasoning—redirects future effort toward better retrieval backbones. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video or 3D retrieval, how sensitive performance is to LLM prompt engineering across diverse modifier types, and whether the human-intervention loop can be automated to close the remaining gap to supervised methods on noisy benchmarks like CIRR.
