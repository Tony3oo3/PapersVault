## Problem

Existing offline reinforcement learning methods are trained and evaluated on singleton environments, leaving their ability to generalize to new environments (with different initial states, transition functions, or reward functions) largely unexplored. This gap is critical for real-world deployment in high-stakes domains like healthcare, robotics, and autonomous driving, where agents will inevitably encounter scenarios not present in their training data. The paper introduces the first benchmark specifically designed to evaluate generalization in offline learning.

## Input / Output

In Procgen, the agent receives 64×64×3 RGB frames and outputs one of up to 15 discrete actions; the context (level seed) determines the initial state and transition function while keeping the reward function fixed. In WebShop, the agent receives a natural language instruction concatenated with the current webpage text state and outputs a discrete action from a state-dependent action set; here the context (instruction) determines both the initial state and the reward function. Datasets are constructed from a limited set of training contexts (200 levels or 398 instructions), and at test time the agent must interact online with entirely unseen contexts (100 new levels or 500 new instructions).

## Method

The paper formulates the problem within contextual MDPs and constructs offline datasets of varying sizes (1M–25M transitions) and skill levels (expert, suboptimal, mixed) from Procgen's 16 procedurally generated games and WebShop's e-commerce environment. It then benchmarks six offline learning algorithms—Behavioral Cloning (ResNet/BERT), BCQ, CQL, IQL, Behavioral Cloning Transformer, and Decision Transformer—by training each on fixed offline datasets and evaluating via online rollouts on unseen contexts. The key empirical finding is that BC consistently outperforms state-of-the-art offline RL and sequence modeling methods on both train and test environments, and that increasing data diversity (more training levels at fixed dataset size) significantly improves generalization while increasing dataset size alone does not. The paper also verifies that on a single environment, offline RL methods recover their expected advantage over BC on suboptimal data, confirming that the generalization gap is specific to the multi-environment setting.

## Results

On Procgen (1M expert dataset, 16 games), behavioral cloning (BC) achieves the highest IQM min-max normalized return on both train and test levels, outperforming BCQ, CQL, IQL, DT, and BCT by a significant margin, while all offline methods lag behind online PPO. On the mixed expert-suboptimal dataset, BC again leads on test levels, though all methods show a similar generalization gap. On WebShop (human demonstrations), BC achieves the highest test score and success rate among BC, BCQ, and CQL, with BCQ showing a slightly higher training success rate but lower test success rate. Increasing data diversity (more training levels at fixed 1M transitions) significantly reduces the generalization gap for all offline methods, while increasing data size (1M→10M transitions at fixed 200 levels) yields only marginal gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BC outperforms all five offline baselines (BCQ, CQL, IQL, DT, BCT) on both train and test environments in Procgen, with the gap being particularly large on test levels for DT and BCT, suggesting transformer-based policies struggle more with generalization despite competitive training performance. On the mixed expert-suboptimal dataset, BC still leads on test levels, contradicting prior singleton-environment results where offline RL typically beats BC on suboptimal data. On WebShop, BC achieves the highest test score and success rate, while BCQ shows higher training success rate (clicking 'buy' only with high confidence) but lower test success rate. All offline learning methods (BC, BCQ, CQL, IQL, DT, BCT) underperform online PPO on both train and test, confirming that the generalization gap is not specific to one algorithm family. The paper also confirms that when training and testing on a single environment (the standard offline RL setting), offline RL methods do outperform BC on suboptimal data, validating that the generalization setting is the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the first benchmark for evaluating zero-shot generalization of offline learning algorithms to unseen environments, a dimension entirely absent from prior offline RL evaluations (D4RL, RL Unplugged, Neorl) which all use singleton environments. It repurposes the CMDP formulation from Hallak et al. (2015) to frame the problem and draws on the extensive online RL generalization literature (Cobbe et al., Packer et al., Raileanu & Fergus) to establish the evaluation protocol, but applies it to offline settings for the first time. The baselines (BCQ, CQL, IQL, DT, BCT) are all well-established methods developed and validated exclusively on same-environment benchmarks, and this paper is the first to stress-test them under distributional shift across environment instances.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that behavioral cloning outperforms all state-of-the-art offline RL and sequence modeling methods in the generalization setting challenges the field's assumption that conservative/pessimistic offline RL is the right tool for real-world deployment where agents will encounter novel scenarios. The observation that data diversity (number of distinct environments) matters far more than data size (number of transitions) provides a concrete, actionable guideline for dataset construction. Key open questions include: why exactly do conservative offline RL methods fail to generalize (the paper hypothesizes risk-aversion to OOD states), whether combining offline RL with online RL generalization techniques (data augmentation, representation learning) can close the gap, and whether these findings extend to continuous action spaces and more complex environments beyond Procgen and WebShop.
