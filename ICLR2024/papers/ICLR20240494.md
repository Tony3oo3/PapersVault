## Problem

Empirical evidence shows that prefixLM (full attention within in-context examples) consistently outperforms causalLM (auto-regressive attention) for in-context learning, but no theoretical explanation existed for this gap. This paper provides a convergence analysis showing that causalLM's ICL dynamics correspond to online gradient descent with non-decaying step sizes, which is provably sub-optimal even as the number of in-context examples grows.

## Input / Output

The model receives a sequence of n input-label pairs formatted as vectors z_j = (x_j, y_j) followed by a query vector z_query = (x_query, 0), and produces the predicted label as the last coordinate of the final layer's output at the query position. In the LSA construction, the input vectors are 16-dimensional (or d-dimensional) with x sampled from U(-1,1)^d and y = wx for a random weight vector w. The key structural difference is that prefixLM allows all prefix tokens to attend to each other while causalLM restricts each token to attend only to preceding positions, and the query attends to all in-context examples but not to other queries.

## Method

The paper proves that a multi-layer Linear Self-Attention (LSA) transformer under the Von Oswald et al. parameter construction (specific K, Q, P, V matrices) exactly simulates multi-step gradient descent on linear regression, with each layer corresponding to one GD step. For prefixLM, the stationary point of this dynamics is the optimal least-squares solution, reached at a linear rate. For causalLM, the auto-regressive mask causes each position j to maintain its own weight vector w_j updated via a restricted sum, and the paper proves these stationary points follow an online gradient descent algorithm with non-decaying step size 1/||x_{j+1}||², which is not guaranteed to converge to the optimal solution. The theoretical results are verified empirically on LSA transformers, standard softmax-attention transformers (linear regression, non-linear regression, multiclass classification), and large language models (T5 DecoderOnly pretrained from scratch, PaLM2, PaLI-X) on MMLU and BBH benchmarks.

## Results

On 5-shot MMLU with T5-XL DecoderOnly, prefixLM achieves 39.5% accuracy versus 30.5% for causalLM (a 9-point gap), and on 3-shot BBH, prefixLM reaches 35.8% versus 32.0% for causalLM. The gap widens with model size: on MMLU, the prefixLM advantage grows from 0.8 points (Base) to 9.0 points (XL). On synthetic linear regression (Table 1), prefixLM-SL achieves 8.6e-3 MSE while causalLM-SL is 22× worse at 1.9e-1, and this pattern holds across non-linear regression and multiclass classification as well.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between prefixLM (full attention within in-context examples) and causalLM (auto-regressive attention) for in-context learning. PrefixLM consistently outperforms causalLM across all experimental settings: on synthetic linear regression, prefixLM-SL achieves 8.6e-3 MSE versus 1.9e-1 for causalLM-SL (22× gap); on non-linear regression, 1.5e-4 vs 2.7e-3 (18× gap); and on multiclass classification, 24.1% error vs 27.0% for the shared-layer variants. On the T5 LLM benchmarks, prefixLM wins on every model size for both MMLU and BBH, with the advantage growing from ~1 point at Base to 9 points at XL on MMLU. The unshared-layer (UL) variants show a similar pattern, with prefixLM-UL at 2.5e-3 MSE on LR versus 1.6e-2 for causalLM-UL. The theoretical analysis explains this gap: prefixLM's stationary point equals the optimal least-squares solution, while causalLM's stationary point corresponds to online gradient descent with non-decaying step sizes, which is provably sub-optimal even as the number of in-context examples grows.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-layer LSA-to-gradient-descent equivalence of Von Oswald et al. (2023) to multi-layer transformers, providing a cleaner formal proof that avoids the 'transformed target' formulation. It also builds on Zhang et al. (2023)'s gradient-flow convergence results but shifts focus from training dynamics to inference-time ICL convergence. The key conceptual departure is distinguishing prefixLM from causalLM attention patterns and showing they correspond to fundamentally different optimization algorithms (batch GD vs. online GD), providing the first theoretical explanation for the empirically observed prefixLM advantage noted by Raffel et al. (2020b) and Tay et al. (2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous theoretical account of why causalLM is suboptimal for ICL: its stationary points correspond to online gradient descent with non-decaying step sizes, which provably fails to reach the least-squares optimum even as the number of in-context examples grows. This explains a practical design choice (mixed/prefix attention in PaLM2, T5) from first principles. However, the theoretical analysis is confined to linear self-attention and linear regression; generalization to softmax attention and non-linear tasks rests entirely on empirical evidence, leaving open whether the online-GD characterization survives in the full non-linear regime. A natural follow-up would be extending the convergence analysis to bounded-depth softmax transformers or to non-linear regression settings.
