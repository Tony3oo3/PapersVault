## Problem

Masked Image Modeling (MIM) methods rely on binary patch masking, which produces a limited set of discrete masking patterns (2^N for N tokens), can inadvertently remove semantically critical regions at high masking ratios, and lacks a principled bound on the information lost. This paper asks whether a continuous, theoretically grounded corruption strategy can replace binary masking while preserving the self-supervised recovery objective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241585/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (e.g., 224×224 for ImageNet-1k) that are divided into 16×16 patches and linearly embedded into D-dimensional token vectors. During pre-training, the network outputs a reconstruction of the complement-projected raw pixels via a single linear head; after pre-training, the encoder's intermediate feature representations Ψ are extracted and fine-tuned end-to-end for downstream classification or segmentation tasks. No auxiliary tokenizer, dVAE, or multi-layer decoder is required at any stage.

## Method

ROPIM replaces binary masking with count sketching: for each image, a random projection matrix P (drawn per Proposition 1 with sketching ratio ρ=K'/K, default ρ=1/7) is applied along the spatial mode of the patch embedding matrix, followed by retraction into the original feature space (Φ' = P†PXW). The projected embeddings are passed through a standard ViT encoder, and a single linear head produces a reconstruction. The reconstruction loss is computed only on the complement subspace (I − P†P), explicitly targeting recovery of the information removed by the lossy projection. This design yields three key advantages over MIM: (i) the projection creates 'continuous' masking that touches all tokens simultaneously with bounded per-token error rather than zeroing out entire patches, (ii) the complement of the projection matrix is analytically available and trivially computed, eliminating the need for MASK tokens or a separate unmasking mechanism, and (iii) Properties 1–4 of count sketches guarantee an unbiased inner-product estimator with variance bounded by 1/K', providing a principled noise bound absent in binary masking.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k with ViT-B/16, ROPIM achieves 83.5% top-1 accuracy after only 300 pre-training epochs, outperforming MAE (83.1% at 800 epochs), CIM (83.3% at 300 epochs + dVAE), and MoCo v3 (83.2% at 600 epochs). With 800 epochs, ROPIM reaches 84.0%, the best result among all compared methods. On ADE20K semantic segmentation, ROPIM achieves 48.5% mIoU with UperNet, surpassing MAE (48.1% at 1600 epochs) and BEiT (47.1% at 800 epochs). On ImageNet100 with ViT-T/16, ROPIM reaches 86.70% top-1 accuracy (ℓ2 loss), exceeding SimMIM (85.08%) and BEiT (85.32%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ROPIM outperforms all 10 baselines on ImageNet-1k top-1 accuracy. Against MAE (83.1% at 800 epochs), ROPIM achieves 83.5% with only 300 epochs — a 0.4% gain at 62% less pre-training time (235h vs 413h). Against CIM (83.3% at 300 epochs), ROPIM gains 0.2% while eliminating the need for a separate dVAE tokenizer, reducing total pre-training time from 600h to 235h. Against MoCo v3 (83.2% at 600 epochs) and BEiT (83.2% at 800 epochs + dVAE), ROPIM leads by 0.3% and 0.3% respectively at 300 epochs. On transfer learning, ROPIM (300 epochs) beats MAE (1600 epochs) by +0.8% on iNaturalist17, +0.6% on CIFAR-10, and +3.2% on CIFAR-100. On ADE20K segmentation, ROPIM achieves 48.5% mIoU vs MAE's 48.1% (1600 epochs) and BEiT's 47.1% (800 epochs). On ImageNet100 with ViT-T, ROPIM (86.70%) surpasses SimMIM (85.08%), BEiT (85.32%), and DINO (84.60%). The only method approaching ROPIM is LGP-MAE (83.9%), but it requires 1600+300 epochs of sequential MIM + contrastive training; when ROPIM replaces MAE in LGP (LGP-ROPIM), accuracy further improves to 84.1%.

## Relation to Prior Work

ROPIM departs from the dominant MIM paradigm (MAE, BEiT, SimMIM) that corrupts inputs via binary patch masking and recovers them with a decoder. Instead, it repurposes count sketching (Weinberger et al., 2009) and orthogonal projection (Charikar et al., 2002) as a 'continuous' corruption mechanism applied along the spatial mode of patch embeddings, with the complement of the projection subspace serving as a principled unmasking operator. This eliminates the need for learnable MASK tokens, auxiliary dVAE tokenizers (as in BEiT/CIM), or large decoders, while retaining the self-prediction objective of MIM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ROPIM demonstrates that a theoretically grounded, computationally negligible corruption step (random orthogonal projection with bounded noise variance) can match or exceed the quality of binary masking at substantially lower pre-training cost (300 epochs vs. 800–1600 for MAE/BEiT). The simplicity of the approach—no extra networks, no tokenizer, no MASK tokens—makes it easy to integrate into existing ViT pipelines and potentially extend to other modalities or hierarchical architectures. Open questions include whether adaptive or learned projection ratios could further improve performance, and whether the bounded-noise property translates to robustness gains in downstream tasks beyond classification and segmentation.
