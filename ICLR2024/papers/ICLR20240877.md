## Problem

Reward model overoptimization in composite RMs has not been systematically studied, and the correlation between component RMs significantly shifts the joint proxy point away from individual overoptimization thresholds. Fixed weightings among component RMs cannot accommodate these differing thresholds, and early stopping is impractical when components overoptimize at different points. This paper asks how to prevent each component RM from exceeding its individual threshold of usefulness while jointly optimizing a composite reward.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a 5-utterance conversation snippet from DailyDialog and generates the next utterance via GPT-2 with top-k (k=20) sampling. Two component reward models score the output: METEOR (lexical overlap with reference) and a fine-tuned RoBERTa intent classifier (binary match). Proxy point thresholds (e.g., METEOR=0.23, Intent=0.48) are either pre-computed by fitting a polynomial surface over multiple training runs or discovered adaptively via Nelder-Mead during a single run, with the KL divergence from the pretrained policy serving as the task reward to be maximized subject to the RM constraints.

## Method

The paper reformulates RLHF as a Constrained MDP where each component RM's value is constrained to hit (equality, ξ-PPO) or not exceed (inequality, μ-PPO) its proxy point threshold, with the negative KL divergence as the task reward. Lagrangian relaxation converts this into a min-max game solved by gradient descent-ascent, where the Lagrange multipliers are learned dynamically rather than fixed, producing a convex combination of task and constraint advantages (bounded via sigmoid/tanh for stability). A key empirical finding is that the joint proxy point—found by fitting a polynomial surface over the METEOR×intent reward space—differs from independently estimated proxy points due to inter-RM correlation. For adaptive threshold identification, a Nelder-Mead simplex search iteratively proposes new threshold pairs, trains ξ-PPO to converge to them, and evaluates performance, converging to the optimal proxy point within a single training run and saving significant computation over the multi-run surface-fitting approach.

## Results

On the DailyDialog dialogue generation task, ξ-PPO and μ-PPO achieve the best evaluation performance over the course of training, with ξ-PPO performing slightly better at the end of training (Fig. 5.1). When trained for 3× longer (384,000 steps vs. 128,000), the constrained methods remain stable while PPO degrades, demonstrating robustness to overtraining. The Nelder-Mead adaptive variant (NM-PPO) achieves strong final performance in a single run, significantly reducing total computation compared to the multi-run proxy-point identification approach. Using correlated proxy points (accounting for RM interaction) yields better performance than independently estimated proxy points, and thresholds set 10% below or above the proxy points both reduce final evaluation score.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its constrained methods against two baselines: standard PPO with KL regularization (the standard RLHF approach) and PPO-SAT (which uses a squared-deviation reward to hit proxy points without formal constraints). Both μ-PPO and ξ-PPO outperform PPO and PPO-SAT in final evaluation performance, with ξ-PPO achieving the best end-of-training score. The advantage is attributed to jointly optimizing the policy and Lagrange multipliers (dynamic RM weightings), which avoids the hyperparameter sensitivity of fixed weightings. All-PPO, which maximizes the composite reward subject to upper-bound constraints, underperforms the other constrained methods due to optimization instability from its 'paradoxical' objective (maximizing a reward its constraints demand it minimize). The constrained methods also prove more robust when training is extended 3× longer, whereas PPO's fixed weightings (tuned for 128k steps) degrade. NM-PPO matches or approaches the multi-run ξ-PPO performance while using roughly 1/3 the total training steps, demonstrating significant computational savings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the overoptimization analysis of Gao et al. (2022) from single reward models to composite RMs, showing that inter-RM correlation shifts the proxy points where each component stops being a useful proxy. It repurposes the constrained MDP / Lagrangian relaxation framework (Altman, 1999; Everett, 1963) — well-studied in safety RL — to the RLHF setting, where the KL-regularized PPO objective is recast as a CMDP with per-RM thresholds. The adaptive Nelder-Mead threshold search (NM-PPO) is a practical addition that avoids the multi-run surface fitting required by prior proxy-point identification, drawing on gradient-free optimization rather than new RL theory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Composite RMs are increasingly the default in production RLHF pipelines, yet the paper identifies that their overoptimization behavior is qualitatively different from single-RM cases due to inter-component correlation — a problem that fixed-weighting or early-stopping cannot handle. The constrained formulation offers a principled, dynamically-weighted alternative to manual hyperparameter tuning of RM coefficients. Key limitations include the need for periodic ground-truth evaluation queries, validation only on DailyDialog with GPT-2 and two component RMs, and the observation that All-PPO's 'paradoxical' objective (maximizing a reward its constraints demand it minimize) causes instability. Open questions include scaling to more RMs, removing the ground-truth access requirement, and applying the approach to larger models and more complex tasks like summarization.
