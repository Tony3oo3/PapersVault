## Problem

Neural-network world models in model-based RL suffer catastrophic forgetting because the data stream from agent-environment interaction is inherently nonstationary as the policy evolves. Achieving Follow-The-Leader (FTL) optimality—fitting all past experiences at every step—requires re-training on the full accumulated dataset, which is computationally prohibitive for lifelong agents. The paper asks whether a world model architecture exists that attains FTL with only constant-cost incremental updates per environment step.

## Input / Output

In the RL setting, the model receives a state-action pair (s, a) ∈ ℝ^{S+A} and predicts the state difference Δs = s′ − s ∈ ℝ^S, which is then integrated as ŝ′ = s + Δt·m̂(Δs|s,a) to produce the next state. In the supervised validation setting, flattened noisy MNIST image patches (2×2 through 7×7) are encoded and a linear layer predicts the clean patch. The feature encoder maps any input to a very high-dimensional sparse vector (e.g., 1000κ dimensions with at most 8κ nonzeros for ρ=3, λ=10), and the final model is a single linear layer W ∈ ℝ^{D×S} over these features.

## Method

The world model is a linear regressor m̂(x) = φ(x)ᵀW on top of a nonlinear random feature encoder called Losse (Locality Sensitive Sparse Encoding), which applies a Johnson-Lindenstrauss random projection followed by soft binning into ρ-dimensional grids with λ bins per axis, producing multi-hot real-valued vectors whose nonzero entries are bounded by κ·2^ρ. The FTL objective (least-squares over all past data) is maintained via two memory matrices A = ΦᵀΦ and B = ΦᵀY that are updated incrementally per sample. The key efficiency gain comes from the sparsity guarantee: at each step, only the small block of weights corresponding to the activated (nonzero) feature indices is updated, and the required matrix inversion is performed on a K×K sub-matrix (K ≤ κ·2^ρ) rather than the full D×D matrix, yielding a closed-form optimal solution in constant time independent of dataset size. This block-wise sparse update (Algorithm 2) provably recovers the exact FTL solution at every step, eliminating catastrophic forgetting by construction.

## Results

On the MNIST image denoising task, Losse achieves the lowest MSE across all patch sizes (0.28–0.40 ×10⁻¹), outperforming the NN baseline (0.33–0.36 ×10⁻¹) at patch sizes ≤36 and all other random feature encoders. In the Dyna MBRL setting, Losse-FTL world models learned online with a single pass of trajectory data surpass or match Full-replay (the closest NN analogue to FTL) on all six environments (Gridworld, Mountain Car, Acrobot, and three MuJoCo tasks), while Online NN and SI baselines fail or underperform. Losse-FTL also achieves the best wall-clock efficiency compared to Coreset at any replay buffer size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Random Fourier Features (Rahimi & Recht, 2007), Losse wins on every patch size, with the gap widening dramatically at larger patches (e.g., 0.40 vs. 1.40 at 49). Against Random ReLU Features (Sun et al., 2018), Losse is consistently better (e.g., 0.28 vs. 0.35 at patch 9), and ReLU's lack of guaranteed sparsity makes it less suitable for efficient online updates. Against Random Tile Coding (Sutton & Barto, 2018), Losse's soft binning provides strictly lower MSE at all patch sizes (e.g., 0.31 vs. 0.51 at 25), confirming that real-valued soft binning outperforms binary one-hot encoding. In the RL setting, Losse-FTL coupled with DQN outperforms the model-free DQN baseline (Mnih et al., 2015) on all discrete tasks, and coupled with SAC (Haarnoja et al., 2018) matches or exceeds Full-replay SAC on MuJoCo continuous control. Synaptic Intelligence (Zenke et al., 2017) and Coreset/reservoir sampling (Vitter, 1985) both reduce forgetting relative to Online NN but remain inferior to Losse-FTL across all environments; Coreset with small replay buffers is more efficient but asymptotically worse, while larger buffers close the gap at higher computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revisits the classic idea of linear regression on nonlinear random features (Rahimi & Recht, 2007; Huang et al., 2006) and reframes it through the lens of Follow-The-Leader online learning (Shalev-Shwartz et al., 2012) within the Dyna MBRL architecture (Sutton, 1990). The key departure from prior work is the introduction of Locality Sensitive Sparse Encoding (Losse), which combines random projection with soft binning to produce high-dimensional sparse features with a provable sparsity bound, enabling constant-time incremental updates via a block-wise closed-form solution. This contrasts with NN-based MBRL methods that rely on replay buffers or periodic retraining to approximate FTL, and with analytic continual learning (Zhuang et al., 2022) that requires pre-trained frozen features and explicit task boundaries. The paper also positions itself against feature-sparsity approaches for reducing forgetting (McCloskey & Cohen, 1989; Pan et al., 2021; Lan & Mahmood, 2023), arguing that sparsity alone is insufficient and that the FTL closed-form solution is what truly eliminates forgetting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded alternative to NN-based world model learning that guarantees no catastrophic forgetting by construction (via FTL) while maintaining constant per-step compute cost, which is attractive for lifelong agents. However, the method is limited to moderate-dimensional state spaces and deterministic dynamics; the authors explicitly note it has not been validated on high-dimensional tasks like Humanoid or image-based observations, where linear models on raw features are unlikely to suffice. A key open question is whether pre-trained encoders can produce compact sparse representations that extend Losse-FTL to high-dimensional or perceptual settings without sacrificing the constant-update guarantee. The practical impact is currently scoped to continuous control benchmarks with moderate state dimensions, where it matches or slightly exceeds full-replay NN baselines.
