## Problem

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods like LoRA produce task vectors with higher mutual cosine similarity (less orthogonality) than full fine-tuning, leading to greater representational interference and suboptimal multi-task model fusion. While full-model tangent-space linearization improves disentanglement, it incurs 2–3× the computational cost of standard fine-tuning. The paper asks whether the disentanglement benefits of linearization can be obtained by linearizing only the small adapter modules rather than the entire backbone.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pre-trained backbone (CLIP-ViT-B-16 for vision or Flan-T5-Base for NLP) and a set of task-specific L-LoRA adapter checkpoints (each ~294K–1.7M trainable parameters, <1% of total). Task vectors are computed as the difference between fine-tuned and initialized adapter weights, then combined via fusion algorithms (task arithmetic, ties-merging, LoraHub, or simple average) with a single scaling factor λ searched over {0, 0.05, …, 1.0}. The output is a single merged model whose adapter parameters are the weighted sum of individual task vectors added to the initial adapter state, evaluated on all constituent tasks simultaneously.

## Method

The core contribution is L-LoRA: a first-order Taylor expansion is applied exclusively to the LoRA adapter parameters φ, yielding a tangent model f_lin(x;φ) = f_{θ₀}(x;φ₀) + ∇_φ f_{θ₀}(x;φ₀)ᵀ(φ−φ₀) that is linear in the trainable parameters. The LoRA modules are then fine-tuned in this linear tangent space rather than the original nonlinear ambient space, while the backbone θ₀ remains frozen. An NTK-based analysis (Appendix B) shows that in the linearized model, parameter updates are directly proportional to task-specific loss gradients through a fixed kernel K(x,x';φ₀), which geometrically encourages task vectors to occupy more orthogonal directions in weight space. Empirically, L-LoRA task vectors exhibit lower pairwise cosine similarity than LoRA vectors (approaching full fine-tuning levels), and the resulting multi-task fusion via task arithmetic or ties-merging achieves normalized scores exceeding both LoRA and full fine-tuning on vision tasks, with only a marginal (~10%) increase in training/inference cost over standard LoRA.

## Results

On CLIP-ViT-B-16 image classification tasks (7 datasets), L-LoRA combined with Task Arithmetic achieves a mean normalized score of 0.77±0.05, surpassing both LoRA (0.64±0.03) and full fine-tuning (0.69±0.06). On Flan-T5-Base NLP tasks (7 GLUE datasets), L-LoRA with LoraHub reaches 0.28±0.08, exceeding LoRA (0.23±0.07) and full fine-tuning (0.24±0.06). L-LoRA uses only 0.32% trainable parameters (294K out of 93M) with negligible additional VRAM overhead (~9.3GB vs. 9.2GB for LoRA), while full fine-tuning requires 100% of parameters and ~12.4GB VRAM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

L-LoRA consistently outperforms standard LoRA across all four fusion methods on vision tasks: Task Arithmetic (0.77 vs. 0.64), LoraHub (0.77 vs. 0.65), Ties-Merging (0.73 vs. 0.63), and Simple Average (0.66 vs. 0.58). On NLP tasks, L-LoRA also beats LoRA with Task Arithmetic (0.26 vs. 0.21), Ties-Merging (0.24 vs. 0.21), and LoraHub (0.28 vs. 0.23), though it slightly trails on Simple Average (0.05 vs. 0.06). Notably, L-LoRA surpasses full fine-tuning on vision tasks with Task Arithmetic (0.77 vs. 0.69) and LoraHub (0.77 vs. 0.61), and on NLP tasks with LoraHub (0.28 vs. 0.24). The paper also shows that as the number of fused tasks increases, L-LoRA's advantage over LoRA grows, indicating superior scalability for multi-task fusion. Cosine similarity heatmaps confirm that L-LoRA task vectors are more orthogonal than LoRA vectors, explaining the reduced task interference during merging.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: parameter-efficient fine-tuning (specifically LoRA) and tangent-space model editing (Ortiz-Jimenez et al., 2023). The key departure is that while prior tangent-space work linearizes the entire pre-trained model to achieve weight disentanglement for task arithmetic, this paper shows that linearizing only the small LoRA adapter modules (yielding L-LoRA) captures most of the disentanglement benefit at a fraction of the computational cost. The method is then evaluated against the standard model fusion toolkit—task arithmetic, ties-merging, LoraHub, and simple averaging—positioning L-LoRA as a drop-in replacement for LoRA that improves downstream fusion quality without changing the fusion algorithm itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that L-LoRA enables multi-task model fusion performance comparable to or exceeding full fine-tuning (e.g., 0.77 vs. 0.69 normalized score with task arithmetic on CLIP) while training and storing only ~0.3% of parameters, making scalable multi-task model construction far more accessible. A clear trade-off is identified: L-LoRA sacrifices single-task accuracy for improved cross-task disentanglement, and the benefit grows with the number of fused tasks, suggesting it is particularly relevant for large-scale multi-task deployments. Open questions include whether a hybrid scheme (some parameters linearized, others nonlinear) could recover single-task performance without losing fusion quality, and whether the weaker NLP results (where L-LoRA still trails full fine-tuning) reflect a fundamental limitation of linearizing low-rank adapters or simply a prompt/template sensitivity issue.
