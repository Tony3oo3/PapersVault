## Problem

Few-shot adaptation methods (PMF, TSA, ETT, FLUTE) rely on hand-designed heuristics to decide which layers to fine-tune and where to insert parameter-efficient adapters, but no systematic approach exists for finding the optimal adaptation architecture. This paper reframes that design question as a neural architecture search problem, asking: given a pre-trained backbone, which subset of layers should be frozen vs. fine-tuned, and at which layers should task-specific adapters be inserted?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240144/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At meta-test time, the method receives a small support set (1–20 labeled images per class in a 5-way task) and a query set of unlabeled images, and produces a class label per query image via a nearest-centroid classifier. Unusually, the method also outputs an architecture decision: from a pre-selected shortlist of N=3 diverse adaptation configurations, it selects the single best one per episode by fine-tuning each candidate on the support set and scoring its support loss. The search space itself is 4^K binary decisions (K layers × 2 bits: adapter on/off, fine-tune on/off), yielding up to 4^16 ≈ 4×10^9 configurations for ResNet-18.

## Method

NFTS defines a per-layer search space with two independent binary decisions—whether to attach a residual adapter (TSA-style for ResNet, prefix-tuning for ViT) and whether to fine-tune the layer's pre-trained weights—realized as a supernet trained via SPOS-style random path sampling with a prototypical loss. The key novelty is a two-stage search: at meta-training time, evolutionary search identifies N=3 architecturally diverse high-performing paths (enforced via a cosine-distance diversity constraint on binary path encodings), and at meta-test time, each candidate is briefly fine-tuned on the episode's support set and the one with the lowest support loss is selected for query classification. This deferred selection handles cross-domain shift better than a single fixed architecture (N=1) while avoiding the overfitting risk of full per-episode NAS (N=4^K), and the framework subsumes PMF, TSA, and ETT as special cases within its search space.

## Results

On Meta-Dataset (multi-domain, 13 datasets), NFTS achieves 80.7% mean accuracy with ResNet-18 and 83.4% with ViT-S, setting new state-of-the-art over TSA (78.4%) and PMF (83.1%) respectively. In the single-domain setting, NFTS reaches 75.2% (ResNet-18) and 79.2% (ViT-S), outperforming TSA by +1.9% and ETT by +1.6%. On the more diverse Meta-Album benchmark (30 datasets, 1000+ labels), NFTS dominates all baselines by over 5% at the 5-way/5-shot operating point (57.59% vs. 51.17% for ProtoNet).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NFTS outperforms all 18 compared baselines on both Meta-Dataset settings and Meta-Album. On Meta-Dataset multi-domain (ResNet-18), NFTS (80.7%) beats the strongest prior methods SSA+TSA (80.1%) by +0.6%, 2LM+TSA (79.5%) by +1.2%, and TSA (78.4%) by +2.3%; the gap widens to +4.1% over URL (76.6%) and +9.3% over SUR (71.4%). For ViT-S multi-domain, NFTS (83.4%) edges out PMF (83.1%) by +0.3% and surpasses CTX (74.2%) by +9.2%. In the single-domain setting, NFTS gains +1.9% over TSA (75.2% vs. 73.3%) for ResNet-18 and +1.6% over ETT (79.2% vs. 77.6%) for ViT-S. On Meta-Album, NFTS leads all baselines across every shot count, with the largest margin at 5-shot where it achieves 57.59% versus ProtoNet's 51.17% (+6.4%) and Fine Tuning's 50.87% (+6.7%). Notably, NFTS also reports results on CIFAR-10/100, MNIST, and MSCOCO (unseen test domains) where several prior methods (e.g., PMF, ETT, ProtoNet) do not, demonstrating broader generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

NFTS builds directly on the SPOS supernet training paradigm (Guo et al., 2020) and the parameter-efficient adaptation architectures of TSA (Li et al., 2022) for ResNets and ETT (Xu et al., 2022) for ViTs, which it treats as special cases within a unified search space. The key departure from prior work (PMF, TSA, ETT, FLUTE) is reframing the heuristic question of 'which layers to fine-tune and where to insert adapters' as a formal NAS problem, solved via evolutionary search over a weight-sharing supernet. The two-stage search design—pre-selecting N diverse architectures at meta-train time and deferring final selection to test time—is a novel adaptation of NAS to the few-shot, cross-domain setting where the test distribution is unknown.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic Auto-ML approach to the adaptation/generalization trade-off in multi-domain few-shot learning, demonstrating that hand-designed uniform adaptation strategies are measurably suboptimal and that the optimal architecture is both complex and domain-dependent. The deferred NAS (N=3) provides a practical middle ground between a single fixed architecture and infeasible per-episode full NAS, with modest test-time overhead. Limitations include a coarse binary search space (adapt/freeze per layer), only two adapter families tested, and the approach's dependence on a pre-trained backbone; future work on richer search spaces, finer granularity, and broader adapter types is explicitly noted as open.
