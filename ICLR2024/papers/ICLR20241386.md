## Problem

Diffusion generative models exhibit bidirectional information interference between image regions during denoising, which directly conflicts with customization tasks (inpainting, personalized generation, style transfer) that require preserving low-level pixel information from a given visual condition. Existing solutions—DreamBooth, Textual Inversion, ControlNet—rely on additional fine-tuning or auxiliary networks and still struggle to simultaneously maintain fidelity to both text and visual conditions, particularly under semantically conflicting prompts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241386/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a face image (visual condition) at 256×256 resolution, pasted onto a 512×512 canvas at a user-specified position on a neutral gray background, together with a text prompt. It outputs a 512×512 generated image that preserves the visual condition's pixel-level identity while incorporating the text description, supporting three settings (normal prompting, style transfer, attribute editing) with task-specific hyperparameters. Each image is generated in approximately 6 seconds on a single NVIDIA 4090 GPU, compared to 732 s for DreamBooth and 3025 s for Textual Inversion.

## Method

COW is a training-free framework that exploits the 'diffusion in diffusion' phenomenon—where pixels from different regions exchange and interfere during denoising—to enforce strictly unidirectional information flow from a visual condition to the rest of the image. It operates in three stages on a frozen Stable Diffusion (sd-v2-1-base) model: (1) Seed Initialization pastes the visual condition onto a neutral background and DDIM-inverts it to obtain a semantically consistent starting latent; (2) Cyclic One-Way Diffusion repeatedly denoises from step t₁ to t₂ while replacing the visual-condition region at each step with its pre-stored inverted latent, then re-injects noise to return to t₁, creating a cyclic 'disturb-and-reconstruct' loop (10 cycles over 50 total steps) that maximizes one-way propagation; (3) Visual Condition Preservation explicitly replaces the condition region at an adjustable step t₃ in the final refinement stage to lock in pixel-level fidelity. No parameters are updated and no auxiliary networks are trained; the entire pipeline is a scheduling and replacement strategy over the frozen model's denoising trajectory.

## Results

COW achieves a 100% face detection rate (tied with SD inpainting) and dominates in human-evaluated Condition Consistency (69.73%) and General Fidelity (51.87%), far surpassing all baselines. On CLIP-T (text-image alignment), COW scores 0.306, competitive with DreamBooth (0.329) and ControlNet (0.305). The method is training-free and generates an image in just 6 seconds, compared to 732 s for DreamBooth and 3025 s for Textual Inversion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COW decisively outperforms all four baselines on the two human-evaluation metrics: Condition Consistency (69.73% vs. 9.60% for DreamBooth, 11.60% for ControlNet, 6.33% for SD Inpainting, 2.73% for TI) and General Fidelity (51.87% vs. 28.73%, 4.73%, 2.07%, 12.60% respectively). On face identity preservation (ID-Distance, lower is better), COW (0.901) beats DreamBooth (1.361), ControlNet (1.194), and TI (1.186), though SD Inpainting achieves a lower distance (0.408) by rigidly preserving pixels at the cost of poor text alignment and overall quality. COW matches SD Inpainting's 100% face detection rate while dramatically exceeding it in text-image alignment (CLIP-T 0.306 vs. 0.300) and both human-evaluated criteria. DreamBooth achieves the highest CLIP-T (0.329) but at 732 s per image and with only 70.50% face detection, indicating it frequently fails to preserve the given face. In inference speed, COW (6 s) is comparable to ControlNet (4 s) and SD Inpainting (5 s) while being 122× faster than DreamBooth and 504× faster than TI.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DDIM's ODE inversion (Song et al., 2020a) to access intermediate latent representations and on Stable Diffusion (Rombach et al., 2022) as the frozen generative backbone. It departs from the dominant learning-based customization paradigm (DreamBooth, Textual Inversion, ControlNet) by reframing the problem through the lens of physical diffusion: the authors observe that pixels within an image inherently exchange information during denoising ('diffusion in diffusion') and propose controlling the direction of this flow rather than adding new parameters or networks. The cyclic one-way diffusion strategy is a novel repurposing of the model's own stochastic dynamics—injecting noise to reset the generative process and re-injecting inverted latents—to enforce unidirectional information propagation from the visual condition outward.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature of COW (6 seconds per image vs. 732s for DreamBooth, 3025s for TI) makes versatile visual-conditioned generation accessible without per-subject fine-tuning, which is practically significant for interactive applications. The conceptual insight that the denoising trajectory can be decomposed into stages with different interference intensities, and that information flow direction can be controlled by cyclic re-injection, opens a new methodological angle for conditioning pre-trained diffusion models. However, the approach is primarily validated on face conditions with a single base model (SD v2.1), struggles with strong semantic conflicts (e.g., front-face condition with 'profile' prompt), and its generalization to non-face visual conditions or other diffusion architectures remains open.
