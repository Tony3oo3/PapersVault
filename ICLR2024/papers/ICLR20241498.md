## Problem

Speech-to-speech translation (S2ST) systems still rely predominantly on encoder-decoder architectures, while the NLP community has demonstrated the superiority of decoder-only language models at scale. Additionally, existing S2ST approaches depend on text transcripts or phoneme supervision, making them inapplicable to unwritten languages. This paper investigates whether a fully language-model-based, unit-centric framework can match or exceed encoder-decoder S2ST while supporting both written and unwritten target languages.

## Input / Output

The system takes raw source-language speech as input and produces target-language speech that preserves the source speaker's voice (voice cloning). Internally, speech is discretized into unsupervised semantic units (HuBERT, 500–1000 clusters) and acoustic units (SoundStream codec, 6 VQ layers, 1024 vocab, 80 Hz at 24 kHz). A key design constraint is that no target-language text transcripts are required, enabling operation on unwritten languages; voice cloning is achieved purely through in-context prompting with source acoustic units rather than explicit speaker embeddings.

## Method

PolyVoice decomposes S2ST into three sequential decoder-only language models: (1) a unit-based cross-lingual LM (U-XLM, 48-layer, 1.6B params) that translates source semantic units into target semantic units, trained with diverse prompt templates over ASR, MT, and S2ST data to enable multi-task learning without auxiliary loss functions; (2) a duration LM that predicts per-unit durations to expand the target unit sequence into a temporally aligned sequence; and (3) a unit-to-speech LM (U-SLM, 12-layer AR+NAR) that, given concatenated source+target semantic units and source acoustic units as an in-context prompt, autoregressively generates target acoustic units which are decoded by SoundStream into waveform. The critical architectural choice is replacing phonemes with unsupervised HuBERT semantic units throughout, which eliminates the need for text supervision and enables cross-lingual transfer to unwritten languages, while the in-context learning mechanism (analogous to VALL-E X) handles voice cloning without speaker embeddings.

## Results

On the Chinese→English EMIME benchmark, PolyVoice achieves an ASR-BLEU of 29.40, ASV score of 0.38 (hyp vs. src), and a naturalness score of 4.10, outperforming VALL-E X (30.66 BLEU, 0.37 ASV, 3.54 naturalness) on voice cloning and speech quality while trailing slightly on translation accuracy. On the English→Spanish CVSS benchmark (unwritten-language setting with no Spanish text), PolyVoice achieves 18.3 ASR-BLEU and 3.60 naturalness. In the architecture ablation, the decoder-only U-XLM yields a +3.9 BLEU improvement over the encoder-decoder counterpart (20.7 vs. 16.8 on GigaS2S data).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is VALL-E X (Zhang et al., 2023b), the most closely related cross-lingual codec LM system. PolyVoice wins on naturalness (4.10 vs. 3.54, a +0.56 improvement) and marginally on ASV voice-cloning score (0.38 vs. 0.37), but loses on ASR-BLEU translation quality (29.40 vs. 30.66, a −1.26 gap). The authors attribute the BLEU deficit to the use of unsupervised semantic units (HuBERT) that retain residual acoustic information, whereas VALL-E X uses supervised phonemes that better preserve semantic content. Against the cascade VALL-E X pipeline, PolyVoice improves ASR-BLEU by +1.91 (29.40 vs. 27.49) and naturalness by +0.66 (4.10 vs. 3.44), though the cascade retains a higher tgt-vs-src ASV (0.58 vs. 0.38) due to its speaker-embedding-based synthesis. In the unwritten-language CVSS setting, PolyVoice achieves 18.3 BLEU without any Spanish text, demonstrating feasibility where text-based baselines cannot operate.

## Relation to Prior Work

PolyVoice adapts the in-context learning prompting strategy from VALL-E X (Zhang et al., 2023b) and VALL-E (Wang et al., 2023) to the S2ST setting, replacing phoneme-based prompts with unsupervised HuBERT semantic units and SoundStream codec units. It departs from the dominant encoder-decoder paradigm in prior S2ST work (Jia et al., 2019; Lee et al., 2022a) by replacing all three pipeline stages (translation, duration prediction, synthesis) with decoder-only GPT-2-style language models. The multi-task learning via prompt engineering over diverse data sources (ASR, MT, S2S) is a novel application of the LM prompting paradigm to speech, avoiding the complex auxiliary loss designs required by prior encoder-decoder S2ST systems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that decoder-only LMs can match or exceed encoder-decoder models for S2ST while simultaneously enabling voice cloning and unwritten-language support through a unified prompting framework. The key open question is the translation quality gap when oracle text is available (76.1 vs 86.8 BLEU), revealing that unsupervised semantic units still carry residual acoustic information that interferes with pure semantic transfer. Future work on better semantic unit extraction, scaling to larger models, and extending to more language pairs will determine whether this LM-based S2ST paradigm can close the gap with text-grounded approaches while retaining its practical advantages for low-resource and unwritten languages.
