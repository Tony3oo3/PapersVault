## Problem

Existing unsupervised constituency parsers achieve similar overall F1 scores yet exhibit low mutual correlation, indicating they capture different aspects of syntactic structure. No prior method effectively combines these heterogeneous parsers into a single stronger model, and standard multi-teacher distillation (e.g., union distillation) suffers from severe over-smoothing when teachers are diverse, degrading student performance below even the best single teacher.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241296/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unlabeled sentence along with the unlabeled binary parse trees produced by K heterogeneous unsupervised parsers (teachers) on that same sentence. It outputs a single unlabeled binary constituency parse tree (no syntactic category labels, binary branching only). A key constraint is that only trees spanning the identical sentence can be averaged, which simplifies the F1 computation since all trees share the same number of constituents (2|s|−1). The distilled student (RNNG/URNNG) then takes only the raw sentence at inference, requiring no teacher queries.

## Method

The paper defines 'tree averaging' as the binary tree maximizing the sum of F1 scores against all K teachers, which reduces to maximizing the total hit count of its constituents across teacher outputs. A CYK-like dynamic programming algorithm performs exact search over the entire binary tree space (a generative MBR formulation, distinct from selective MBR that only picks among teacher outputs) to find this optimal average tree in O(|s|³) time. For efficient inference, the ensemble outputs serve as pseudo-groundtruth to train an RNNG student via cross-entropy, followed by URNNG variational refinement. This ensemble-then-distill pipeline avoids the over-smoothing problem inherent in union distillation: instead of training on the union of all teacher trees (which forces the student to spread probability mass too thinly), the student learns from a single negotiated tree per sentence, yielding substantially lower prediction entropy and higher F1.

## Results

On the Penn Treebank test set, the ensemble of seven unsupervised parsers achieves an F1 score of 70.4±0.6 (corresponding runs), outperforming the best single teacher (ConTest, 62.9±1.6) by 7.5 points. After distilling into an RNNG student with URNNG refinement using the best teachers across runs, the method reaches a new state-of-the-art F1 of 72.8, approaching the oracle binary-tree upper bound of 83.3. Under domain shift to SUSANNE, the ensemble achieves 50.3 F1, matching the PTB-supervised RNNG (50.1) and outperforming all unsupervised baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The ensemble outperforms all seven individual unsupervised parsers on PTB: ConTest (62.9), DIORA (58.9), S-DIORA (57.0), Compound PCFG (55.5), Neural PCFG (51.0), ContexDistort (47.8), and Ordered Neurons (44.3), with the ensemble reaching 70.4 F1. It also beats Selective MBR (66.3), which only selects from teachers' outputs rather than searching the full tree space, and Union distillation (65.6), which suffers from over-smoothing due to heterogeneous teachers. On the SUSANNE domain-shift benchmark, the ensemble (50.3 F1) surpasses the best teacher ContextDistort (41.2) by 9.1 points and Selective MBR (47.4) by 2.9 points, while matching the PTB-supervised RNNG (50.1). Notably, even the ensemble of the worst-performing teacher runs (69.4) still exceeds the best single teacher (ConTest, 62.9) by 6.5 F1 points, demonstrating robustness to teacher quality variance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the RNNG/URNNG framework (Dyer et al., 2016; Kim et al., 2019b) as the student architecture for distillation, and positions itself within the long line of unsupervised constituency parsing methods (DIORA, ConTest, ContexDistort, Compound PCFG, etc.) that it ensembles. Conceptually, it departs from prior MBR decoding work in NLP by formulating a *generative* MBR that searches the entire binary tree space via a CYK-like dynamic program, rather than the *selective* MBR that picks from a finite candidate set. It also departs from standard multi-teacher knowledge distillation (union distillation) by proposing an ensemble-then-distill pipeline that first synthesizes a single best tree before training the student, specifically to avoid the over-smoothing problem that plagues cross-entropy training on heterogeneous teacher outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper largely closes the gap between supervised and unsupervised constituency parsing (72.8 F1 vs. ~76 supervised oracle), and demonstrates that the ensemble is robust enough to transfer to an unseen domain (SUSANNE) at near-supervised levels. The over-smoothing analysis (entropy 11.42 for union distillation vs. 4.93 for ensemble distillation) provides a clear, generalizable diagnosis of why naive multi-teacher distillation fails with heterogeneous teachers, with implications beyond parsing. Open questions include extending the tree-averaging formulation to discontinuous constituents, low-resource and multilingual settings, and other structured prediction tasks (sequences, graphs) where the CYK-style exact decoding may not directly apply.
