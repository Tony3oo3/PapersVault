## Problem

Cascaded multi-scale diffusion models excel at high-resolution image generation but cannot be trained or evaluated via likelihood, because each intermediate scale introduces latent variables that must be marginalized out—an intractable operation in high dimensions. This paper identifies a class of transformations under which the likelihood function is invariant, eliminating the need for marginalization entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241699/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (32×32 to 128×128) and decomposes them via a hierarchical volume-preserving map (Laplacian pyramid or Haar wavelet transform) into S multi-scale latent representations, each fed to a separate U-Net diffusion network. Lower-scale conditioning is injected into higher-scale networks via channel-wise concatenation of a reconstructed image built from the inverse map applied to all preceding scales. Outputs include the exact log-likelihood (bits per dimension), generated samples, typicality-based OOD scores, and lossless compressed bitstreams via bits-back ANS coding.

## Method

The paper defines hierarchical volume-preserving maps—homeomorphisms h: X → Z^(1)×…×Z^(S) whose Jacobian satisfies √det(AᵀA)=1 everywhere—and proves that under such maps the joint likelihood over all scales equals the data likelihood p_θ(x) exactly (Lemma 4.1), removing the intractable marginalization step. Two concrete instances are identified: the Laplacian pyramid (a tight frame, mapping R^d → R^{2d}) and the Haar wavelet transform (an orthogonal map, R^d → R^d). The resulting Probabilistic Cascaded Diffusion Model (PCDM) trains S coupled diffusion processes with a standard VDM-style variational lower bound at each scale, using antithetic time sampling and a learnable noise schedule. A key theoretical result (Theorem 5.1) shows that maximizing this likelihood is equivalent to minimizing an upper bound on a weighted sum of Earth Mover's Distances between true and learned score functions, which can be evaluated in O(N) time rather than the O(N³ log N) cost of direct EMD computation.

## Results

W-PCDM achieves state-of-the-art bits-per-dimension (BPD) across all four density estimation benchmarks: 2.35 on CIFAR-10 32×32, 3.32 on ImageNet 32×32, 2.95 on ImageNet 64×64, and 2.64 on ImageNet 128×128, outperforming the previous best (VDM at 2.49 on CIFAR-10 and Flow Matching at 2.90 on ImageNet-128). For lossless compression on CIFAR-10, W-PCDM achieves 2.37 BPD, surpassing ARDM (2.71) and VDM (2.72). On out-of-distribution detection (AUROC), W-PCDM reaches 0.92 average across SVHN, constant uniform, and uniform distributions, compared to 0.83 for VDM. Sample quality (FID on CIFAR-10) reaches 2.42 with the Karras et al. weighting scheme, improving over VDM's 7.41 under the standard VDM weighting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

W-PCDM outperforms all 30 baselines on every dataset where it is evaluated. On CIFAR-10, it improves over the previous best likelihood model VDM (2.49 BPD) by 0.14 BPD and over i-DODE (2.56) by 0.21 BPD. On ImageNet-32, W-PCDM (3.32) beats Flow Matching (3.53) by 0.21 BPD and VDM (3.72) by 0.40 BPD. On ImageNet-64, the gap widens: W-PCDM (2.95) surpasses Flow Matching (3.31) by 0.36 BPD and VDM (3.40) by 0.45 BPD. On ImageNet-128, W-PCDM (2.64) is the only model reported, beating Flow Matching (2.90) by 0.26 BPD. For lossless compression, W-PCDM (2.37 BPD) improves over ARDM (2.71) by 0.34 BPD and VDM (2.72) by 0.35 BPD. For OOD detection, W-PCDM (0.92 avg AUROC) substantially outperforms VDM (0.83), GLOW (0.41), and PixelCNN++ (0.44), with perfect 1.0 AUROC on both uniform and constant-uniform distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cascaded diffusion model framework of Ho et al. (2022) and the variational diffusion model (VDM) architecture of Kingma et al. (2021) by identifying a specific class of transformations—hierarchical volume-preserving maps—that makes the likelihood function tractable in multi-scale settings. The key conceptual departure from prior cascaded models (which were purely non-probabilistic) is the observation that the Laplacian pyramid and Haar wavelet transform, long-known tools in signal processing, satisfy a volume-preservation condition that eliminates the need for intractable marginalization over intermediate scales. The theoretical analysis builds on the linear-time EMD approximation of Shirdhonkar & Jacobs (2008) and generalizes the orthogonality-based volume preservation observed in Wavelet Flow (Yu et al., 2020) to a broader class of homeomorphisms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work resolves a fundamental barrier that has prevented cascaded diffusion models from being used for likelihood-based training and inference, enabling exact log-likelihood evaluation without expensive marginalization. The connection between likelihood training and EMD score matching provides a principled explanation for why multi-scale priors improve density estimation, and the O(n) evaluation of the EMD upper bound (versus O(n³ log n) for exact computation) opens the door to training with perceptual similarity metrics at scale. An open question the authors highlight is whether EMD-based score matching enjoys the same statistical guarantees (consistency, efficiency, asymptotic normality) as standard L² score matching. Practical limitations include the bits-back compression overhead for single-image encoding and the fact that the volume-preserving property, while critical, is only verified for two specific transform families.
