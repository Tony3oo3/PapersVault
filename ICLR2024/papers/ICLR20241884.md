## Problem

Vanilla score matching provably fails to correctly estimate multimodal distributions because it incorrectly weights the mixture components when the Langevin dynamics are run to stationarity. While noised score functions (as in diffusion models) overcome this limitation, it remained open whether the vanilla score alone can be used for generative sampling. This paper answers affirmatively by proving that data-based initialization combined with early stopping of Langevin dynamics provably corrects the component-weighting bias.

## Input / Output

The method takes an L2(μ)-accurate estimate of the vanilla score function ∇log μ (obtainable via vanilla score matching, MLE, or other procedures) and a small set of M i.i.d. samples from μ used solely for initialization. It outputs an ε_TV-close sample from μ by running discrete-time Langevin Monte Carlo from the uniform distribution over the training set for a finite time T. The score estimate need only be accurate on the support of μ (the standard L2(μ) condition), not globally, and the initialization is the empirical distribution rather than a warm start in χ² divergence as in prior work.

## Method

The core mechanism is running Langevin Monte Carlo with the vanilla score estimate, initialized at the empirical distribution of training samples, and stopped at a carefully chosen finite time T before the chain reaches its (incorrect) stationary distribution. The proof splits on component overlap: when components have high overlap (δ₁₂ ≥ δ), the mixture satisfies a log-Sobolev inequality with constant O(1/(αδ)) (a new technical ingredient, Theorem 2), ensuring rapid mixing to the correct distribution; when overlap is low, the mixture score is provably close to the nearest component's score, so the dynamics remain trapped in the correct component. For general K-component mixtures, a graph-based argument groups components into well-connected clusters and iteratively refines the overlap threshold via a decreasing sequence δ₀ > δ₁ > … > δ_{K-1}. Girsanov's theorem bridges continuous and discrete-time dynamics and handles the L2 score approximation error through a 'bad set' argument showing the chain avoids regions where the score estimate is inaccurate. The result is new even in the unimodal case, as prior work (Lee et al., 2022a) required a polynomially-warm start in χ² divergence rather than the natural empirical distribution initialization.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 1) proves that Langevin Monte Carlo with an L₂-approximate score function, initialized at the empirical distribution of M samples, and run for time T with early stopping, achieves d_TV(μ̂, μ) ≤ ε_TV for mixtures of K strongly log-concave components. The required number of initialization samples scales as M = Ω(p_*^{-2} ε_TV^{-4} K⁴ log(K/ε_TV) log(K/τ)), and the score estimation error must satisfy ε_score ≤ Õ(p_*^{1/2} ε_TV⁴ / ((βκ²K exp(K))² d^{3/2} T^{3/2})). In the 1D simulation (Figure 1), the early-stopped Langevin at T=200 accurately recovers a two-Gaussian mixture, while both the empirical distribution (T=0) and the fully-mixed stationary distribution (T=∞) fail to match the ground truth.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without formal benchmark comparisons against competing methods. The key conceptual comparison is between three sampling strategies: (1) the empirical distribution at T=0, which is a poor fit due to finite sample noise; (2) the fully-mixed Langevin chain at T=∞, which corresponds to vanilla score matching and provably fails for multimodal distributions by incorrectly weighting components (Koehler et al., 2022); and (3) the early-stopped Langevin at intermediate T, which the paper proves succeeds. The simulations in Figures 1 and 2 confirm this: at T=200 (1D) and T=300 (32D), the early-stopped chain matches the ground truth mixture, while T=0 and T=∞ do not. The paper also notes that its result is new even in the unimodal case, extending Lee et al. (2022a) which required a polynomially-warm start in χ²-divergence rather than the natural empirical distribution initialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of Hyvärinen's vanilla score matching (2005) and the data-based initialization trick from contrastive divergence (Hinton, 2002; Xie et al., 2016), proving that their combination resolves the known failure of vanilla score matching in the multimodal setting (Koehler et al., 2022). It extends the unimodal convergence guarantees of Lee et al. (2022a) to mixtures of log-concave distributions, and introduces a new log-Sobolev inequality for well-connected mixtures (Theorem 2) that generalizes prior Poincaré-constant results (Madras & Randall, 2002) and relaxes the chi-square divergence assumptions of Chen et al. (2021). Unlike annealed/denoised score matching approaches (Song & Ermon, 2019; Chen et al., 2023) that require score estimates at multiple noise levels, this work shows the vanilla score alone suffices when paired with empirical-distribution initialization and early stopping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves an explicitly open question—whether multimodal distributions can be sampled using only the vanilla score—and provides the first theoretical guarantee that data-based initialization corrects the component-weighting bias inherent in vanilla score matching. The conceptual insight that early stopping (rather than running to stationarity) is essential for multimodal sampling is a useful reframing for practitioners working with energy-based models. However, the bounds depend exponentially on the number of components K, and the empirical validation is limited to low-dimensional Gaussian mixtures, leaving open whether the approach scales to the high-dimensional, many-component settings relevant to real generative modeling. The authors also note it is unclear what other obstructions beyond multimodality might prevent vanilla-score sampling from succeeding.
