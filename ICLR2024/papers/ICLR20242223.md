## Problem

Large vision-language models (LVLMs) frequently generate object hallucinations—describing objects that do not exist in the input image—yet existing mitigation strategies either require labor-intensive fine-tuning on curated high-quality datasets or are designed for smaller non-autoregressive VLMs and do not transfer to LVLMs. This paper identifies the root causes of hallucination (co-occurrence bias, decoding uncertainty, and positional drift) and asks whether a lightweight post-hoc correction method can rectify hallucinatory descriptions without retraining the underlying LVLM.

## Input / Output

At inference, LURE takes an RGB image and a free-form text description produced by any LVLM. Before passing the description to the revisor, objects whose token-level entropy exceeds a threshold γ or whose position index exceeds η × sequence length are replaced with an [IDK] placeholder tag, forcing the revisor to re-evaluate those specific spans. The output is a single rectified natural-language description of the same image, preserving the original format while removing or substituting hallucinated objects.

## Method

LURE is a denoising-autoencoder-style revisor: a separate LVLM fine-tuned to map hallucinatory descriptions back to ground-truth captions. Training data is synthesized by prompting GPT-3.5 to (1) insert objects likely to co-occur with those already mentioned and (2) replace uncertain or late-position objects with [IDK], guided by three empirically and theoretically validated hallucination factors—co-occurrence score, token uncertainty (−log p), and normalized position index. The revisor is trained with standard autoregressive cross-entropy loss on 5,000 image–caption pairs from LLaVA-150k, requiring only ~10 minutes on a single A100 GPU. At test time, the same [IDK] masking is applied to the target LVLM's output before the revisor reconstructs a corrected description, making LURE a plug-and-play module compatible with any LVLM backbone.

## Results

LURE achieves the lowest CHAIR_S and CHAIR_I scores across all six evaluated LVLMs on MSCOCO. For example, on MiniGPT-4, LURE reduces CHAIR_S from 26.8 (Original) to 19.7 and CHAIR_I from 7.3 to 4.9; on LLaVa, CHAIR_S drops from 54.0 to 27.1 and CHAIR_I from 11.3 to 6.4. In human and GPT evaluations (Table 2), LURE achieves average rankings of 1.25–1.90 (lower is better) versus 2.10–4.79 for the strongest baselines. Additional evaluations on POPE show LLaVa+LURE improving accuracy from ~50% to 77–86% across Random, Popular, and Adversarial splits on MSCOCO, A-OKVQA, and GQA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LURE outperforms all six baselines (Original, Teacher, CoT, Greedy-Decoding, GPT-Ensemble, GPT-Teacher) on both CHAIR_S and CHAIR_I across all six LVLMs. The strongest competing baseline is GPT-Teacher, which uses BLIP-2 short descriptions to guide GPT-3.5 rewriting; LURE still improves over GPT-Teacher by 5.6–13.2 points on CHAIR_S (e.g., 19.7 vs. 25.3 on MiniGPT-4, 27.1 vs. 38.0 on LLaVa). The Teacher baseline (BLIP-2 contextual guidance) and CoT (object-listing then describing) show modest improvements over Original but are consistently outperformed by LURE. GPT-Ensemble, which aggregates descriptions from multiple LVLMs, actually worsens hallucination in several cases (e.g., CHAIR_S increases from 26.8 to 41.0 on MiniGPT-4). In human and GPT evaluations, LURE achieves average rankings of 1.25–1.90, substantially better than the next-best baselines (CoT at 2.10–4.38, GPT-Teacher at 2.16–3.56), confirming that LURE's post-hoc revision strategy is more effective than prompt-based or ensemble-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of work on LVLM hallucination mitigation, which has focused on improving training data quality (Li et al., 2023c; Liu et al., 2023a,d) or fine-tuning with hallucination-aware datasets (Gunjal et al., 2023). LURE instead adopts a post-hoc, denoising-autoencoder-inspired revisor that is model-agnostic and requires only ~10 minutes of training on a single GPU, drawing conceptual inspiration from denoising autoencoders (Vincent et al., 2008) and grounding its design in a statistical decomposition of hallucination into co-occurrence, uncertainty, and position factors. The theoretical analysis borrows from deep learning generalization theory (Carmon et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2022a) and autoregressive time-series error accumulation literature (Hannan et al., 1989; Ing, 2007) to formalize why reducing co-occurrence and sampling more certain objects lowers test error.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LURE offers a practical, lightweight remedy for object hallucination that can be bolted onto any existing LVLM without retraining the base model, which is valuable given the cost and labor of curating high-quality fine-tuning data. The three-factor decomposition (co-occurrence, uncertainty, position) provides actionable diagnostic signals that practitioners can use to identify and target hallucination-prone tokens. However, the method's reliance on GPT-3.5 for synthetic hallucination generation introduces a dependency and potential bias, the CHAIR evaluation is limited to 80 COCO object categories, and the approach has not been tested on non-object hallucinations (actions, spatial relationships, attributes) or video. An open question is whether the post-hoc revisor paradigm generalizes to more complex hallucination types beyond object presence/absence.
