## Problem

LLM-based task planners for embodied agents lack standardized, automated evaluation frameworks; existing studies rely on expensive human evaluation or are confined to simple tabletop manipulation tasks. There is also a notable absence of systematic investigation into influential factors such as pre-trained model selection, in-context example strategies, replanning from failure feedback, and fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242064/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The planner receives a natural language instruction (e.g., 'bring an apple and a cupcake and put them on the coffee table'), the environment state specifying object locations, and a set of in-context examples pairing instructions with ground-truth skill sequences. It outputs an autoregressive sequence of atomic skills drawn from a fixed vocabulary of 214 skills (ALFRED/AI2-THOR) or ~352 skills (WAH-NL/VirtualHome), terminated by a 'done' token. Performance is assessed by executing the skill sequence in a simulator and comparing the final scene state against predefined goal conditions (e.g., INSIDE(apple, fridge): 1).

## Method

LOTA-BENCH introduces a benchmark system comprising a baseline LLM-based task planner, two dataset-simulator pairs (ALFRED + AI2-THOR, and a newly extended WAH-NL + VirtualHome), and automated goal-condition evaluation. The baseline planner leverages in-context learning by constructing a prompt from a role prefix, instruction-skill-sequence example pairs, and the user query; at each step it computes conditional token probabilities over all candidate skills using constrained generation via the Guidance tool (penalizing tokens absent from any skill), selects the highest-probability skill, and appends it autoregressively. The paper then validates three extensions: (1) semantic-similarity-based in-context example selection using Sentence-BERT, which outperforms random and task-specific sampling; (2) feedback-driven replanning where natural-language failure messages from the simulator are injected into the prompt; and (3) LoRA fine-tuning on domain-specific instruction-plan pairs, which yields large gains within-domain (e.g., 13.66%→60.08% for LLaMA 1 30B on ALFRED) but poor cross-domain transfer.

## Results

On ALFRED, GPT-3 (text-davinci-003) achieves the best baseline task success rate of 21.36%, while GPT-4 reaches 40.38% (a 19-point improvement) using a modified chat-style prompt. On WAH-NL, GPT-3 achieves the best baseline subgoal success rate of 40.82%, though GPT-4 underperforms at 34.17%. Fine-tuning LLaMA 1 30B with LoRA on ALFRED dramatically boosts success from 13.66% to 60.08% on valid-unseen tasks, but cross-domain transfer to WAH-NL degrades performance from 32.22% to 10.38%. Semantic Similarity Sampling with LLaMA 1 65B achieves 43.25% subgoal success on WAH-NL, surpassing the GPT-3 175B baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against a single external method but rather benchmarks multiple LLMs and planning strategies. GPT-4 outperforms GPT-3 by 19 points on ALFRED (40.38% vs 21.36%) but underperforms by 6.6 points on WAH-NL (34.17% vs 40.82%), suggesting domain-specific strengths. Among open-source models, LLaMA 2 and MPT perform competitively relative to their sizes, while instruction- and chat-tuned variants do not surpass their base counterparts. For in-context example selection, Semantic Similarity Sampling (LLaMA 1 65B: 43.25%) beats both Task-Specific Sampling and Random Sampling, and surpasses the GPT-3 175B baseline (40.82%) on WAH-NL. Replanning with natural-language feedback improves LLaMA 2 70B from 21.63% to 24.04% on ALFRED but degrades LLaMA 2 13B from 21.15% to 17.79%, indicating a model-size threshold for effective replanning. Fine-tuning yields the largest single improvement (LLaMA 1 30B: +46.42 pp on ALFRED valid-unseen) but fails to transfer across domains, dropping 21.84 pp on WAH-NL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SayCan (Ahn et al., 2023) for its prompt template and skill-scoring mechanism, but replaces SayCan's per-skill probability computation with Guidance-based constrained generation for a ~19× speedup. It positions itself against prior LLM-based planners (ReAct, ProgPrompt, Inner Monologue) that relied on human evaluation or were confined to narrow tabletop settings, arguing that no systematic, reproducible benchmark existed for comparing language-oriented task planners on home-service tasks. The paper also extends the Watch-And-Help dataset by adding crowdsourced natural language instructions (WAH-NL), filling a gap that made the original WAH dataset unusable for language-oriented planning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark provides the first automated, reproducible evaluation framework for LLM-based task planners on home-service embodied agents, enabling fair comparison across model families, sizes, and prompt configurations. Key open questions remain: the decoupling of high-level planning from low-level visual grounding limits real-world applicability, the domain gap between simulators and reality is acknowledged but unaddressed, and the finding that fine-tuned procedural knowledge fails to transfer across task domains (ALFRED→WAH-NL drops from 32% to 10%) raises fundamental questions about the generalizability of LLM planning. The paper also highlights that ~60% of failures stem from high-level planning errors rather than visual/physical grounding, suggesting that context integration and interactive clarification are critical next steps.
