## Problem

State-of-the-art GNNs for knowledge graph reasoning (NBFNet, RED-GNN) achieve strong empirical performance, but the theoretical understanding of what kind of rule structures they can learn remains an open problem. Existing expressivity analyses for GNNs rely on graph isomorphism testing or are restricted to single-relational graphs, leaving the logical expressivity of these methods for multi-relational KG reasoning uncharacterized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242221/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-relational knowledge graph G=(V,E,R) and a query triplet (h,R,?), where the query entity h is assigned a unique initial representation (the labeling trick). For each candidate tail entity t, the GNN computes an L-layer message-passing representation e_t^(L)[h] that encodes subgraphs with h as source and t as sink, then scores the triplet (h,R,t) via a feed-forward network or linear transformation on this representation. The output is a binary prediction (exists/absent) for each candidate, effectively learning a logical formula R(h,x) over the graph.

## Method

The paper unifies NBFNet, RED-GNN, and their variants into a common framework called QL-GNN, showing they all score triplets via the tail entity's L-layer representation under query-entity labeling. The key theoretical result (Theorem 3.2) proves that QL-GNN's logical expressivity is exactly the graded modal logic CML[G,h], meaning it can learn rule structures expressible as formulas with the query entity as a logical constant, including chain-like and branching structures, but not structures with multiple branching points. A contrasting result (Theorem 3.4) shows classical methods like CompGCN can only learn non-structural rules where query and tail subgraphs are independent. To extend expressivity beyond CML[G,h], the paper proposes EL-GNN, which assigns unique initial representations to all entities with out-degree exceeding a threshold d, transforming previously inexpressible rule structures into CML[G,h,c1,...,ck] formulas learnable by the same QL-GNN architecture.

## Results

On real KG reasoning benchmarks, EL-NBFNet achieves the best accuracy on Kinship (84.2%), UMLS (95.3%), and FB15k-237 (33.2%), while EL-RED-GNN leads on WN18RR (50.4%). On synthetic datasets designed to validate the theoretical expressivity bounds, QL-GNN (NBFNet/RED-GNN) achieves 100% accuracy on all CML-learnable rule structures (C3, C4, I1, I2, T) but drops to 54.1% on the non-CML structure U, whereas EL-GNN improves U accuracy to 83.8% (EL-NBFNet). Classical methods (R-GCN, CompGCN) score below 7% on all synthetic rule structures, confirming they cannot learn structural rules.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EL-GNN consistently outperforms both QL-GNN backbones: EL-NBFNet beats NBFNet by 2.3 points on Kinship (84.2% vs 81.9%), 0.7 on UMLS (95.3% vs 94.6%), and 1.2 on FB15k-237 (33.2% vs 32.0%); EL-RED-GNN beats RED-GNN by 1.9 on Kinship (83.9% vs 82.0%) and 0.2 on WN18RR (50.4% vs 50.2%). Against classical GNNs, the gap is dramatic: CompGCN trails EL-NBFNet by 9.1 points on Kinship (75.1% vs 84.2%) and 6.7 on FB15k-237 (26.5% vs 33.2%), while R-GCN is not even reported on real datasets due to its poor performance. Embedding-based methods (RotatE, QuatE) and rule-based methods (Neural LP, DRUM) are uniformly outperformed by EL-GNN across all five real datasets, with the largest gaps on Kinship (EL-NBFNet 84.2% vs QuatE 31.1%) and FB15k-237 (EL-NBFNet 33.2% vs Neural LP 19.0%). On the synthetic expressivity benchmark, the theoretical prediction is confirmed exactly: QL-GNN achieves 100% on all CML-learnable structures but only 40.5–54.1% on the non-CML structure U, while EL-GNN lifts U to 79.7–83.8%, and classical methods score below 7% everywhere.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies SOTA GNNs for KG reasoning (NBFNet, RED-GNN, AdaProp, A*Net) into a common framework called QL-GNN, grounded in the labeling trick from Zhang et al. (2021) and the graded modal logic (CML) expressivity analysis from Barceló et al. (2020). It departs from prior expressivity work that relied on graph isomorphism testing (WL test) by instead characterizing the logical rule structures GNNs can learn, extending the single-relational analysis of Barceló et al. to multi-relational KGs. The proposed EL-GNN builds directly on NBFNet and RED-GNN as backbones, adding entity-level labeling to extend the learnable rule class beyond CML[G,h] to CML[G,h,c₁,…,cₖ].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first formal characterization of exactly which rule structures SOTA KG-reasoning GNNs can learn (CML[G,h]) and proves that classical methods like CompGCN are fundamentally limited to non-structural rules, explaining their empirical gap. The EL-GNN improvement, while modest on real datasets (e.g., +0.012 accuracy on FB15k-237), demonstrates that the theoretical gap is practically exploitable. Open questions include extending the analysis to multiple simultaneous rules (capacity), handling incomplete KGs where rule structures are partially missing, and extracting explainable rules from trained networks.
