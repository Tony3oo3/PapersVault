## Problem

Prior identifiability results in multi-view causal representation learning are limited to pairs of views, assume full observability (each view depends on all latent variables), and often require latent independence. This paper asks whether a unified framework can identify shared content across arbitrary subsets of an arbitrary number of partially observed views, where each view is a nonlinear mixture of only a subset of possibly causally related latents.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241368/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes K simultaneously observed views (e.g., multiple images, an image-text pair, or synthetic multi-view data) where each view x_k is generated by a view-specific diffeomorphic mixing function f_k applied to a subset z_{S_k} of N latent variables. It produces a single view-specific encoder r_k per view mapping to a |S_k|-dimensional latent space, together with learnable binary content selectors φ^{(i,k)} that extract specific content blocks z_{C_i} (defined as intersections of view-specific index sets) from the shared representation. The output is thus a structured set of block-identified content representations rather than a single flat embedding.

## Method

The core contribution is a unified identifiability theory built on a contrastive objective combining content alignment (pulling selected representations from different views toward each other) with entropy regularization (enforcing uniformity and hence invertibility of the encoders). The key architectural insight is that a single view-specific encoder per view, combined with learnable binary content selectors and auxiliary projection functions, can simultaneously identify all content blocks across all subsets of views without training a combinatorial number of networks. Theorem 3.8 proves that minimizing this objective (with an L0 information-sharing regularizer on the selectors) block-identifies every shared content block up to a smooth bijection, without requiring knowledge of content dimensions. An identifiability algebra (intersection, complement, union corollaries) then allows iterative refinement to recover finer-grained latent structure. In practice, the loss is instantiated with InfoNCE or BarlowTwins, and the binary selectors are learned via Gumbel-Softmax.

## Results

On self-supervised disentanglement, the proposed method achieves DCI disentanglement scores of 0.42±0.020 on MPI-3D complex and 0.30±0.04 on 3DIdent, substantially outperforming Ada-GVAE (0.11 and 0.09 respectively). In the controlled numerical validation, content variables are recovered with R²≈1 while independent style variables remain unpredictable (R²≈0), confirming the identifiability theory. On the multi-task disentanglement task with latent causal relations, the method reaches 0.99 classification accuracy within 1k update steps using both linear and nonlinear labeling functions. The multimodal experiment on Multimodal3DIdent simultaneously identifies multiple content blocks (image-image, image-text, and joint) in a single training run, recovering prior two-view results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary empirical baseline is Ada-GVAE (Locatello et al., 2020), an autoencoder-based weakly-supervised disentanglement method. The proposed contrastive encoder-only approach outperforms Ada-GVAE by a wide margin on both image datasets: 0.42 vs. 0.11 DCI on MPI-3D complex (≈3.8× improvement) and 0.30 vs. 0.09 on 3DIdent (≈3.3× improvement). The authors attribute this gap to avoiding image reconstruction, which is particularly difficult on visually complex data, and note that contrastive self-supervised learning has broadly surpassed autoencoder-based representation learning. Additionally, the multimodal experiment demonstrates that a single training run under Theorem 3.8 simultaneously recovers the performance of both von Kügelgen et al. (2021) (image-image content) and Daunhawer et al. (2023) (image-text content), validating the unification claim without requiring separate training for each view pair.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends a family of identifiability results spanning multi-view nonlinear ICA (Gresele et al., 2020), content-style separation via data augmentation (von Kügelgen et al., 2021), weakly-supervised disentanglement (Locatello et al., 2020), and multimodal contrastive learning (Daunhawer et al., 2023) under a single framework that allows an arbitrary number of views, partial observability (each view depends on only a subset of latents), and causally dependent latent variables. The key technical departure is the introduction of view-specific encoders with learnable content selectors and auxiliary projection functions, which enable simultaneous identification of multiple content blocks from a single training run rather than requiring a separate encoder per view subset. The proof technique builds directly on the Darmois construction and the invertibility result from Zimmermann et al. (2021), extending the invariance-and-uniformity argument from two views to arbitrary view subsets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identifiability algebra (Cor. 3.9–3.11) provides a practical graphical criterion for determining exactly which latent variables or groups can be recovered from a given set of views, which is a useful design tool for multi-view and multi-modal systems. The framework also explains why contrastive learning succeeds in multi-view settings by identifying the shared content as the identifiable quantity, connecting the empirical success of methods like InfoNCE and Barlow Twins to a formal identifiability guarantee. However, notable gaps remain: the information-sharing regularizer is highly non-convex, the theory assumes continuous smooth latents (discrete variables are only handled empirically), and the finite-sample behavior of the entropy-maximization objective is not characterized. The paper also opens the question of whether causal structure can be recovered from overlapping but not simultaneously observed marginals, connecting to the structural causal marginal problem.
