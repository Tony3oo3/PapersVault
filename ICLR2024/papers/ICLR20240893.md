## Problem

MLP-based neural fields suffer from spectral bias and slow convergence on high-frequency signals, while grid-based representations avoid this bias but demand large memory for high-resolution volumetric features. Existing approaches combine grids and MLPs sequentially (grid → MLP), forcing grids to carry most of the signal content and requiring high resolutions. The paper asks whether grids can be injected into MLPs in a parallel, modulation-based fashion that preserves MLP compactness while eliminating residual spectral bias.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240893/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes low-dimensional coordinate vectors (2D spatial for images, 5D spatial+direction for static NeRF, 6D spatial+direction+time for dynamic NeRF, or 1D temporal for frame-wise video) and produces per-coordinate signal values (RGB+density, pixel colors, or video frames). The grids used for modulation are single-channel (or channel-wise for video) and queried via bilinear interpolation at arbitrary coordinates, so they remain extremely compact (e.g., 32×32 for images, 10×3 for NeRF view directions, or 10×C for video time). A coordinate-priority scheme selects which subset of input dimensions feeds the grid lookup, avoiding the curse of dimensionality for high-dimensional inputs.

## Method

CAM modulates intermediate MLP features by extracting per-coordinate scalar scale (γ) and shift (β) parameters from two single-channel grids via bilinear interpolation, then applying F̃ = γ(X;Γ)·normalize(F) + β(X;B) at each layer. This injects spectral-bias-free, coordinate-dependent information directly into the MLP's hidden representations rather than feeding grid features sequentially into the network. A key empirical finding is that feature normalization (previously ineffective in neural fields) becomes beneficial when combined with CAM, as the coordinate-dependent modulation acts as a de-regularizer that restores fitting capacity. For video, the normalization unit excludes the channel dimension and the grids output channel-wise factors to keep computation affordable. The approach is plug-and-play: it requires no architectural changes to the base model and adds only a few thousand grid parameters.

## Results

On the D-NeRF dynamic scene benchmark, NerfAcc+CAM achieves 33.78 PSNR with only 0.6M parameters, outperforming the previous SOTA TiNeuVox (32.67, 12M params) by 1.11 dB. On the 360 real-scene dataset, Mip-NeRF 360+CAM reaches 29.98 PSNR, surpassing Zip-NeRF (29.82) and I-NGP (27.06) with 9M parameters. On NeRF synthetic under a 1MB budget (8-bit), CAM achieves 33.27 PSNR, the best among all methods at that memory constraint. For video compression on UVG, CAM improves over FFNeRV by up to 1.10 dB PSNR at 6-bit and achieves rate-distortion performance comparable to the HEVC reference encoder (HM) while supporting real-time GPU decoding (~45 fps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAM consistently outperforms all baselines across tasks. On dynamic NeRF (D-NeRF), NerfAcc+CAM (33.78) beats TiNeuVox (32.67, 12M params) by 1.11 dB and K-planes (31.61, 37M params) by 2.17 dB, while using the fewest parameters (0.6M). On the 360 real-scene dataset, Mip-NeRF 360+CAM (29.98) surpasses Zip-NeRF (29.82, 84M) and I-NGP (27.06, 84M) with only 9M parameters. On NeRF synthetic, CAM improves Mip-NeRF from 33.09 to 33.42 PSNR (32-bit) and from 32.86 to 33.27 (8-bit), outperforming TensoRF (33.14 at 71.9 MB) with 30× less memory. For image representation, FFN+CAM gains +15.73 dB on Text regression and +3.05 dB on Text generalization over the FFN baseline, while I-NGP remains slightly ahead on Natural regression (32.98 vs 32.21) but trails on generalization. In video compression, CAM improves FFNeRV by 0.25–0.60 dB across bit rates and matches or exceeds H.264 and NeRV/HNeRV on the rate-distortion curve, while remaining comparable to the HEVC reference (HM) but with real-time GPU decoding versus HM's ~10 fps CPU decoding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of combining grid representations and MLPs sequentially (grid features fed into a small MLP), instead injecting spectral-bias-free grid representations directly into intermediate MLP features via coordinate-dependent scale-and-shift modulation. It is conceptually related to prior modulation work in neural fields (FiLM, Pi-GAN, COIN++, ModSiren, MFN, FINN), but distinguishes itself by parameterizing modulation factors with lightweight single-channel grids rather than additional networks or positional encodings, thereby avoiding architectural bias. The method is applied as a plug-and-play module on top of existing baselines (Mip-NeRF, NerfAcc, FFNeRV) without modifying their core architectures, and it introduces the empirical finding that feature normalization—previously ineffective in neural field training—becomes beneficial when paired with CAM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CAM offers a practical, low-overhead mechanism to mitigate spectral bias in neural fields while preserving MLP compactness, achieving state-of-the-art results on novel view synthesis (static and dynamic) and video compression with fewer parameters and faster training than grid-heavy alternatives. The coordinate priority hierarchy (time > view direction > space) provides a useful design principle for multi-coordinate modalities, though it is determined empirically and may not generalize to all signal types. Open questions include whether the approach extends to higher-dimensional or non-Euclidean coordinate spaces, whether the normalization synergy with CAM has a deeper theoretical explanation, and whether the modest per-task gains (typically 0.3–1.5 PSNR) compound meaningfully in downstream generative or editing pipelines.
