## Problem

Polynomial Networks (PNs) express outputs as high-degree interactions of input elements using only addition and multiplication, making them compatible with constraints like fully homomorphic encryption and interpretability. However, prior PNs (Π-Nets, R-PolyNets, D-PolyNets) have consistently underperformed modern architectures on standard benchmarks like ImageNet, with a gap of roughly 10% in top-1 accuracy. This paper aims to close that gap by designing a PN architecture that matches the performance of state-of-the-art MLP and Transformer models while remaining entirely free of activation functions.

## Input / Output

For image recognition, the model takes an RGB image and converts it into a sequence of tokens via a pyramid patch embedding that combines multi-scale patch projections (e.g., 14×14 and 7×7 patches merged by elementwise addition), producing a token matrix X ∈ R^{d×s}. The output is a class label over 1000 (or fewer) categories. For the scientific computing task, the model takes discrete initial conditions of an ODE system (e.g., Lotka-Volterra predator-prey populations) and produces the predicted trajectory, with the learned polynomial coefficients directly recovering the symbolic form of the governing equations. All operations are restricted to linear projections and Hadamard (elementwise) products, with no nonlinear activation functions anywhere in the network.

## Method

The core building block is the Mu-Layer, defined as Y = C[(AX) * (BDX) + AX], where * denotes the Hadamard product and A, B, D, C are learnable matrices. Two parallel linear branches (AX and BDX) capture different sets of linear interactions within each token, and their elementwise product introduces multiplicative (second-degree) interactions; the rank factorization Λ = BD with a shrinkage ratio m/l > 1 encourages the two branches to carry complementary information. A Poly-Block stacks two Mu-Layers (with layer normalization between them and a spatial shift in the first), capturing up to fourth-degree interactions per block, so N stacked blocks capture up to 4^N degree interactions. The full architecture uses a pyramid patch embedding to efficiently incorporate fine-grained spatial detail without quadratic FLOP growth, and a multi-stage variant with varying hidden sizes across stages for high-resolution benchmarks. The entire network relies exclusively on linear and multilinear operations, eliminating activation functions and making it compatible with FHE and symbolic recovery of learned dynamics.

## Results

On ImageNet1K, Multi-stage MONet-S (32.9M params, 6.8B FLOPs) achieves 81.3% Top-1 and 95.5% Top-5 accuracy, matching CycleMLP-T (81.3%) and DeiT-S/16 (81.2%) while using no activation functions. The smaller Multi-stage MONet-T (10.3M params, 2.8B FLOPs) reaches 77.0% Top-1, a ~7-point improvement over the best prior polynomial network (R-PolyNets at 70.2%). On ImageNet-C robustness, MONet-S achieves the lowest mean corruption error (49.7) among all compared models, outperforming CycleMLP (53.7) and HireMLP (51.9). On smaller datasets (CIFAR-10, SVHN, Oxford Flowers, Tiny-ImageNet), MONet-T achieves the best or tied-best accuracy across all four benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against prior polynomial networks, MONet delivers a decisive improvement: Multi-stage MONet-T (77.0%) surpasses R-PolyNets (70.2%), D-PolyNets (70.0%), and Π-Nets (65.2%) by 6.8–11.8 points, closing the long-standing gap between activation-free PNs and modern architectures. Among MLP-based models, MONet-S (81.3%) ties CycleMLP-T (81.3%) and beats S2MLP-Dep (80.7%), ResMLP-24 (79.4%), and MLP-Mixer-B/16 (76.4%) while using no activation functions. Against CNNs, MONet-S outperforms ResNet-50 (77.2%) and AA-ResNet-152 (79.1%) with fewer FLOPs (6.8B vs. 4.1B and 23.8B respectively). In the robustness evaluation on ImageNet-C, MONet-S achieves the best mean corruption error (49.7) among all compared models, beating CycleMLP (53.7), HireMLP (51.9), and DeiT (54.6), with particularly strong margins in Weather and Digital corruption categories. On smaller datasets, MONet-T achieves the best accuracy on CIFAR-10 (94.8%), ties for best on SVHN (97.6%), and achieves the best on Oxford Flowers (95.0%), outperforming all polynomial and MLP baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

MONet extends the polynomial network (PN) lineage initiated by Π-Nets (Chrysos et al., 2020) and advanced by R-PolyNets/D-PolyNets (Chrysos et al., 2023), but departs fundamentally from prior PNs by adopting a token-based, MLP-style architecture rather than convolutional or global polynomial expansions. The Mu-Layer's use of a Hadamard product between two linearly-projected branches to capture multiplicative interactions is the key architectural novelty, replacing the activation functions that prior hybrid PNs relied on to reach competitive accuracy. The pyramid patch embedding draws inspiration from FPN (Lin et al., 2017) to reduce the quadratic FLOP cost of small patches, and the spatial shift operation is borrowed from S2MLP (Yu et al., 2022b).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating activation functions entirely, MONet becomes compatible with Leveled Fully Homomorphic Encryption (LFHE), which supports only addition and multiplication — a constraint that renders standard neural networks unusable in privacy-critical settings. The polynomial ODE solver experiment demonstrates that the learned model can explicitly recover symbolic equations (e.g., Lotka-Volterra), offering interpretability that black-box Neural ODEs lack. However, the authors acknowledge that a theoretical characterization of which polynomial expansions MONet can express remains open, and the FHE deployment is still aspirational rather than demonstrated end-to-end. The work opens a path toward activation-free architectures for scientific computing and encrypted inference, but the practical FHE pipeline and broader theoretical grounding are left for future work.
