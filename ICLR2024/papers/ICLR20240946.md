## Problem

While prior work proved that DNN outputs can be faithfully explained by sparse AND-OR interaction primitives, existing extraction methods cannot guarantee that these interactions generalize across different DNNs trained for the same task. The sparse ℓ₁ optimization used to extract interactions is sensitive to initialization (producing only ~21% overlap between two runs) and yields model-specific interactions that may reflect overfitting rather than shared knowledge, undermining their faithfulness as true inference primitives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240946/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes m DNNs trained for the same task (e.g., BERT_BASE and BERT_LARGE for sentiment, LLaMA and OPT-1.3B for language modeling, ResNet-20 and VGG-16 for image classification) and a single input sample. From the sample, 8–10 input variables (tokens or 3×3 image patches) are selected, and all 2ⁿ masked variants are generated by replacing unselected variables with baseline embeddings or zero patches. The output is a set of shared AND-OR interaction subsets S ⊆ N with their numerical strengths, where a shared interaction must exceed a salience threshold in all m DNNs simultaneously.

## Method

The core contribution is a joint optimization loss (Eq. 6) over decomposition parameters {γ_T^(i)} for all m DNNs simultaneously. Each DNN's output is decomposed as v_and(x_T) = 0.5·v(x_T) + γ_T and v_or(x_T) = 0.5·v(x_T) − γ_T, and the loss applies a rowmax(·) operator across the m DNNs' interaction matrices so that only the most salient interaction per subset is penalized—this encourages all DNNs to share the same salient interactions. An additional α·‖I‖₁ term prevents a redundancy shortcut where unselected weak interactions absorb the effect of selected ones. To facilitate optimization, the decomposition is parameterized as γ_T^(i) = γ̄_T + γ̂_T^(i) with a shared component γ̄_T and a bounded model-specific residual, and a bounded Gaussian error term ε_T^(i) absorbs residual noise. The paper also proves that interaction variance under Gaussian noise scales as 2^|T|·σ², motivating the bounded error modeling for high-order interactions.

## Results

The proposed method (Equation 6) achieves significantly higher generalization power (measured by s_and and s_or) than both baselines across all three tasks (SST-2, SQuAD, MNIST). Specifically, on SST-2 with BERT_BASE and BERT_LARGE, the proposed method extracts 25 shared interactions out of k=50 salient interactions per model, compared to only 16 shared interactions for the traditional method. The extracted interactions remain sufficiently sparse (most interactions have negligible effect), and low-order interactions (order 1–3) are disproportionately more likely to be shared across DNNs than high-order ones. The traditional method (Equation 4) exhibits severe initialization sensitivity, with only ~21% overlap between two independently initialized extraction runs on the same model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: (1) the Traditional method (Li & Zhang, 2023b), which minimizes the ℓ₁ norm of interactions (Equation 4) to extract the sparsest explanation, and (2) the original Harsanyi interactions (Ren et al., 2023a), which directly compute AND interactions via the Harsanyi dividend without any joint AND-OR decomposition. Against the Traditional method, the proposed method wins decisively on generalization power: Figure 3 shows consistently higher s_and and s_or curves across all values of k, and the shared-interaction count nearly doubles (25 vs. 16 out of 50 on SST-2). The proposed method is slightly less sparse than Traditional (Figure 2), but the extracted interactions are still well above the noise floor. Against the Harsanyi baseline, the proposed method achieves both higher sparsity and higher generalization power (Figure 7), since Harsanyi interactions do not jointly optimize the AND-OR decomposition and thus cannot enforce cross-model sharing. The Traditional method also suffers from a critical initialization-sensitivity problem (Table 2: only 10.90%–20.90% overlap between two random initializations), which the proposed rowmax-based loss avoids by design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the interaction-primitive framework established by Ren et al. (2024) and Li & Zhang (2023b), which proved that DNN outputs can be sparsely decomposed into AND-OR interactions via the Harsanyi dividend. The key departure is identifying that the standard ℓ₁ sparsity objective (Eq. 4) yields non-unique, initialization-dependent solutions that do not transfer across models, and proposing a rowmax-based loss (Eq. 6) that explicitly penalizes only the most salient interaction per subset across m DNNs, thereby extracting shared primitives. The shared-decomposition parameterization (γ̄ + γ̂) and bounded noise term ε further distinguish the method from prior single-model interaction extraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper adds the missing 'generalization' criterion to the interaction-primitive framework, arguing that only interactions consistently extracted across independently trained DNNs can be considered faithful concepts rather than model-specific overfitting. A practical limitation is the 2ⁿ computational cost per sample (though mitigated by selecting 8–10 variables), and the method requires access to multiple DNNs trained on the same task, which may not always be available. Open questions include whether the shared interactions converge to a unique 'ground-truth' set as model scale increases, and whether the framework extends to more than two models or to tasks where model outputs are not directly comparable (e.g., different label spaces).
