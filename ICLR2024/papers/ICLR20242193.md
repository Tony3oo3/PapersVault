## Problem

Prior stylized ICL experiments showed Transformers can match gradient-based algorithms on real-valued functions, but left open whether attention is truly essential, what the hard limits of this ability are, whether models can exploit highly informative examples for more sample-efficient learning, and whether these findings transfer to pretrained LLMs. This paper addresses all four questions by moving from real-valued to Boolean function classes, whose sample complexity and learnability are well-characterized in learning theory.

## Input / Output

The model receives an autoregressive prefix P_k = (x_1, y_1, …, x_{k-1}, y_{k-1}, x_k) of Boolean input-label pairs with x_i ∈ {0,1}^n and y_i ∈ {0,1}, and must predict the binary label y_k for the query x_k. For pretrained LLMs, each n-bit input is expanded into a sequence of individual '0' and '1' tokens preceded by a natural-language instruction, making the setup directly compatible with standard LLM prompting without any architectural modification. The discrete, finite input space is what enables the same experimental framework to be applied to both from-scratch sequence models and off-the-shelf LLMs.

## Method

Five architectures (Transformer, LSTM, DSS, Hyena, RetNet) are trained from scratch via meta-learning on sequences of Boolean function examples, minimizing cross-entropy over autoregressive label predictions. The test-bed spans well-studied function classes (Conjunctions, Disjunctions, 3-term DNFs/CNFs, Parities, Thresholds) with known PAC-learning algorithms, enabling precise identification of where models succeed (near-optimal sample complexity) and where they fail (Parities at chance level). A key finding is that a single Transformer trained on a 50/50 mixture of teaching-sequence and vanilla examples learns two distinct learning algorithms and adaptively selects the more sample-efficient one based on the in-context sequence. For LLM evaluation, the authors use a frozen GPT-2 with learnable input/output embeddings and direct prompting of GPT-4, GPT-3.5-Turbo, and LLaMA-2-70B with bit-token sequences, showing these models achieve non-trivial accuracy competitive with nearest-neighbor baselines on tasks drawn from an exponentially large function space where memorization is ruled out.

## Results

On simple Boolean function classes (Conjunctions, Disjunctions, Sparse Disjunctions), Transformers achieve 100% accuracy within 70 in-context examples, matching the sample complexity of known PAC-learning algorithms. On intermediate tasks (3-term DNF, 3-clause CNF, Majority), Transformers reach 91–96% accuracy, outperforming all baselines but falling short of perfect. On Parity tasks (PARITY-10,2; PARITY-20,3; PARITY-20), all architectures including Transformers fail to exceed chance-level (~50%) accuracy even with 140 examples, despite known algorithms (Gaussian elimination) being able to solve these with the same budget. In direct LLM evaluation, GPT-4, GPT-3.5-Turbo, and LLaMA-2-70B all match or exceed the nearest-neighbor baseline on Conjunction and Majority tasks up to 7 dimensions, with GPT-4 slightly outperforming NN even at 15 dimensions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Transformers match or slightly outperform all attention-free architectures (DSS, LSTM, RetNet, Hyena) on most tasks, with the largest gap appearing on the Nearest Neighbor task where Transformers achieve 100% versus Hyena's 85.5% and DSS's 81.5%. On 0-1 Threshold functions, Transformers (85.3%) clearly outperform DSS (72.4%) and RetNet (72.9%), while LSTM (83.9%) and Hyena (85.0%) are closer. On Parity tasks, all sequence models (Transformer, DSS, LSTM, RetNet, Hyena) fail at chance level (~50%), while a feedforward network trained with gradient descent achieves 100% on PARITY-(10,2), highlighting a fundamental limitation of autoregressive architectures on this class. In the direct LLM evaluation, GPT-4 outperforms the nearest-neighbor baseline at 15 dimensions for Conjunctions, while GPT-3.5-Turbo and LLaMA-2-70B match or slightly exceed NN up to 7 dimensions. The frozen GPT-2 setup shows that pretraining imparts in-context learning capability: frozen GPT-2 outperforms a randomly initialized Transformer of the same size on Conjunctions and perfectly implements the nearest-neighbor algorithm, confirming that the mechanism is learned during language pretraining rather than task-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the stylized in-context learning framework of Garg et al. (2022) from real-valued regression tasks to Boolean function classes, leveraging the well-understood learnability properties from learning theory to design controlled experiments. It departs from prior work in three key ways: (1) it systematically compares attention-based Transformers against attention-free architectures (DSS, LSTM, Hyena, RetNet), questioning whether self-attention is essential for ICL; (2) it introduces teaching sequences (Goldman & Kearns, 1995) as a new dimension, showing models can learn multiple algorithms and adaptively select among them; and (3) it bridges the gap between stylized settings and practical LLMs by directly evaluating GPT-4, GPT-3.5-Turbo, and LLaMA-2 on discrete Boolean tasks where memorization is essentially impossible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that attention-free architectures match Transformers on most ICL tasks challenges the assumption that self-attention is the critical mechanism for in-context algorithm implementation, with implications for efficient model design. The complete failure on Parity functions—where known algorithms like Gaussian elimination succeed with the same data—reveals a fundamental representational or optimization limitation of sequence models that remains unexplained. The dual-algorithm result (one Transformer implementing two distinct learning strategies and switching based on input distribution) suggests a richer internal structure than previously appreciated. Open questions include: what theoretical property of Parity makes it intractable for ICL, what aspects of pretraining impart algorithmic capabilities to LLMs, and whether the attention-free equivalence holds at larger scales or on more complex tasks.
