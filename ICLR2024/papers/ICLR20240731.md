## Problem

Reconstructing 3D objects from very few (2–4) unposed images with little visual overlap is a long-standing challenge: traditional SfM pipelines fail without sufficient feature matches, and existing neural methods either assume known camera poses or regress poses directly from images without leveraging 3D shape information. PF-LRM addresses this by jointly predicting camera poses and 3D shape in a single feed-forward pass, exploiting the synergy between the two tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240731/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes 2–4 background-removed RGB images of a single object (one designated as reference) along with known camera intrinsics, and produces a triplane NeRF (64×64×32) in the reference view's coordinate frame plus relative rotation-translation poses for all other views. Inference completes in ~1.3 s on a single A100 GPU. Although trained on exactly 4 views, the transformer architecture naturally supports variable view counts at test time, with graceful degradation from 4 down to 2 views.

## Method

PF-LRM is a ~590M-parameter single-stream transformer (36 self-attention layers) that jointly processes DINO ViT-B/16 image patch tokens and learnable triplane position tokens via mutual self-attention, enabling bidirectional information flow between 2D and 3D representations. The key architectural insight is to avoid direct pose regression: instead, an MLP decodes each image patch token into a 3D surface point and opacity, which are supervised online by the NeRF's own geometry (distilled via volume rendering with ground-truth poses during training). Camera poses are then recovered through a differentiable EPro-PnP solver that minimizes weighted reprojection error over the predicted 3D-2D correspondences, avoiding the local-minima pitfalls of regression-based pose losses. Training combines L2+LPIPS rendering loss, point/opacity consistency losses, and the differentiable PnP loss, all without any direct 3D ground-truth supervision, on ~1M objects from Objaverse and MVImgNet.

## Results

On pose prediction, PF-LRM achieves rotation errors of 6.32° (OmniObject3D), 3.99° (GSO), 16.27° (ABO), 15.53° (CO3D), and 10.42° (DTU), with Acc.@15° ranging from 0.850 to 0.962 across these datasets. For reconstruction quality, PF-LRM achieves an average PSNR of 24.8 on novel views (vs. FORGE's 13.4) and 27.2 PSNR when rendered at its own predicted poses, demonstrating that both pose and shape predictions are jointly accurate. The model processes 4 unposed images in ~1.3 seconds on a single A100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PF-LRM outperforms all three baselines by a large margin on every dataset. Against FORGE (Jiang et al., 2022), PF-LRM reduces rotation error by 10–25× (e.g., 3.99° vs. 103.81° on GSO) and improves novel-view PSNR from ~13.4 to ~24.8. Against HLoc (Sarlin et al., 2019), the SfM-based method has a >97% failure rate on sparse inputs (e.g., 99.6% on OmniObject3D), while PF-LRM achieves 96.2% Acc.@15° on the same dataset. Against RelPose++ (Lin et al., 2023a), PF-LRM reduces rotation error by ~10× on GSO (3.99° vs. 107.49°) and by ~1.8× on CO3D (15.53° vs. 28.24°), where RelPose++ was trained on the CO3D training set. Even when RelPose++ is re-trained on Objaverse (the same data as PF-LRM), PF-LRM still achieves substantially lower rotation errors (e.g., 2.46° vs. 45.58° on GSO), confirming that the joint NeRF+pose prediction with differentiable PnP is the key advantage over direct pose regression.

## Relation to Prior Work

PF-LRM builds on the triplane NeRF representation (Chan et al., 2022) and the EPro-PnP differentiable solver (Chen et al., 2022b), but departs from prior joint pose-shape methods (FORGE, SparsePose, FvOR) in two key ways: it replaces direct pose regression with per-patch 3D surface point prediction followed by a differentiable PnP solve, and it uses a single-stream transformer with bidirectional self-attention between image and NeRF tokens rather than the encoder-decoder cross-attention design of LRM. The online distillation of NeRF geometry to supervise per-view point clouds is a novel training mechanism that stabilizes the non-convex PnP loss. The method also inherits the DINO ViT tokenizer and adaptive layer-norm conditioning from the LRM/DMV3D line of work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PF-LRM addresses a practical gap where SfM-based pipelines fail on the 2–4 sparse, wide-baseline views common in e-commerce and consumer capture, delivering feed-forward pose and NeRF reconstruction in ~1.3 s. The large margins over FORGE and RelPose++ (e.g., 6° vs. 69–107° rotation error) suggest that joint shape-pose reasoning with a geometric inductive bias (3D-2D correspondences + differentiable solver) is substantially more robust than direct pose regression at scale. Open questions include lifting the requirement for known intrinsics, handling backgrounds and view-dependent appearance, and removing the need for ground-truth pose supervision during training to exploit in-the-wild video data.
