## Problem

While text-to-image diffusion models generate high-quality images, they lack fine-grained drag-style editing capability. Existing approaches either rely on GANs with limited generalization or use weak text-image feature correspondence that struggles in multi-object or complex scenes. The paper asks whether the strong inter-image feature correspondence already present in a pre-trained diffusion model can be directly exploited for drag-style editing without any additional training.

## Input / Output

The method operates on 512×512 RGB images in the latent space of Stable Diffusion V1.5. Beyond the source image, the user specifies binary masks (m^gen, m^gud) marking the target and source regions for editing, plus a shared mask (m^share) for unedited areas; cross-image tasks additionally require a reference image. The output is a single edited image at the same resolution, with gradient guidance applied only in the first 30 of 50 DDIM sampling steps to preserve texture generation in later steps.

## Method

DragonDiffusion treats image editing as a change of feature correspondence within the pre-trained UNet denoiser, converting editing operations into energy-function-based gradient guidance without any fine-tuning or new modules. A memory bank stores intermediate latents and self-attention keys/values at every DDIM inversion step, which are then reused in two paths: (1) energy functions compute cosine similarity between masked feature regions (local for spatial, global for appearance) to produce conditional gradients, with multi-scale supervision combining the 2nd decoder layer (semantic) and 3rd layer (geometric); (2) visual cross-attention replaces the decoder's self-attention K/V with the stored memory-bank values to enforce content consistency. An optional inpainting energy term (E_opt) prevents the original object from reappearing at its source location during object moving.

## Results

On face keypoint manipulation (CelebA-HQ, 800 aligned faces), DragonDiffusion achieves the best FID of 35.75 (17 points) and 34.58 (68 points), outperforming all baselines in editing quality. In terms of point accuracy (Euclidean distance, lower is better), DragonDiffusion scores 18.51 (17 pts) and 13.94 (68 pts), second only to DragGAN (15.96 / 10.60) but without requiring face alignment. The preparing stage is dramatically faster at 3.62s compared to 48–52s for DragGAN and DragDiffusion, while inference time (15.93s) is comparable to DragDiffusion (19.71s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DragGAN, DragonDiffusion trades a small accuracy gap (18.51 vs 15.96 for 17 pts; 13.94 vs 10.60 for 68 pts) for a substantially better FID (35.75 vs 39.27) and the ability to edit unaligned faces without pre-processing; DragGAN also requires 52.40s of preparing time versus 3.62s. Against DragDiffusion, DragonDiffusion wins on both accuracy (18.51 vs 22.95; 13.94 vs 17.32) and FID (35.75 vs 38.06), while both support unaligned editing. Against UserControllableLT, DragonDiffusion dominates on all quality metrics (accuracy and FID) though UserControllableLT has a much faster inference stage (0.05s vs 15.93s). For object pasting, DragonDiffusion achieves results comparable to the training-based Paint-by-Example method, producing more harmonized lighting and perspective integration than simple copy-paste. For appearance replacing and object moving, DragonDiffusion outperforms Self-Guidance in both editing accuracy and content consistency, avoiding the unexpected artifacts and coarse text-image correspondence issues of the latter.

## Relation to Prior Work

DragonDiffusion builds directly on Stable Diffusion's latent diffusion architecture and the DIFT finding that intermediate UNet features in diffusion models encode high-level image-image correspondence. It departs from GAN-based drag editing (DragGAN, UserControllableLT) by exploiting diffusion models' superior generation quality and diversity, and from text-driven editing (Self-Guidance, Prompt-to-Prompt) by replacing weak text-image correspondence with stronger image-image feature correspondence. Compared to the concurrent DragDiffusion, it avoids LoRA fine-tuning and new architectural blocks, instead formulating editing as a tuning-free energy function over feature correspondence combined with a memory-bank-based visual cross-attention mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that fine-grained, drag-style image editing on diffusion models is achievable without any additional training, which lowers the barrier to interactive editing and broadens the practical applicability of pre-trained T2I models. The unified energy-function framework handles multiple editing operations (moving, resizing, pasting, appearance replacing, point dragging) with a single mechanism, suggesting a general recipe for future editing tasks. Limitations include the requirement for manual mask specification, evaluation at 512×512 resolution on a single base model (SD 1.5), and potential degradation in complex multi-object scenes where feature correspondence may become ambiguous. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to newer, larger diffusion architectures and to video editing.
