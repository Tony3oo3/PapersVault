## Problem

While residual networks are known to converge to neural ODEs at initialization when weights are discretizations of smooth functions, it remains unclear whether this ODE structure is preserved during and after gradient-based training. This paper establishes that the implicit regularization of gradient flow for deep residual networks specifically selects the discretization of a neural ODE among all possible minimizers of the empirical risk, providing a rigorous mathematical foundation for the commonly stated connection between trained ResNets and neural ODEs.

## Input / Output

The method takes n training pairs (x_i, y_i) with x_i ∈ R^d and y_i ∈ R^{d'}, and trains a residual network of depth L with hidden dimension q ≥ max(d, d') and width m, where each residual block is a two-layer perceptron scaled by 1/(L√m). The output is the network prediction F(x) ∈ R^{d'}, and in the large-depth limit (L → ∞) the trained network converges to a neural ODE with Lipschitz continuous weight functions V(s,t) and W(s,t) parameterized by continuous depth s ∈ [0,1]. A critical constraint is that initialization must correspond to a discretization of a neural ODE (e.g., weight-tied Gaussian W_k and zero V_k), and the activation must be C², bounded, and Lipschitz continuous.

## Method

The core result proves that gradient flow training preserves the neural ODE discretization structure: if the network is initialized as a discretization of a neural ODE, it remains one throughout training. The proof proceeds by (1) showing via clipped gradient flow that weights stay bounded and the difference between successive layer weights is O(1/L), (2) applying a generalized Arzelà-Ascoli theorem to the piecewise-constant continuous-depth interpolation of weights to extract a convergent subsequence, and (3) proving uniqueness of the limit via the Picard-Lindelöf theorem, yielding a Lipschitz continuous limiting weight function. For the long-time limit (t → ∞), the paper establishes a Polyak-Lojasiewicz condition requiring only linear overparameterization (m ≥ c₁n), which guarantees exponential convergence of the loss to zero and convergence of the network to a neural ODE that interpolates the training data. The 1/L scaling in the residual branch and the compensating factor L in the gradient flow are essential for maintaining the ODE correspondence as depth grows.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the neural ODE-preserving configuration (GELU activation + weight-tied initialization) achieves 79.9±0.2% test accuracy, while the non-ODE configuration (GELU + i.i.d. initialization) reaches 85.7±0.1%, revealing a ~5.8% performance gap for maintaining the ODE structure. The best overall test accuracy is 88.4±0.5% (ReLU + i.i.d.), but this configuration does not preserve smooth weights. Theoretically, the paper proves that under a Polyak-Lojasiewicz condition (satisfied with linear overparameterization m = Ω(n)), gradient flow converges to a global minimum and the trained network converges to a neural ODE that interpolates the training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external methods but instead ablates two design choices: initialization scheme (weight-tied vs. i.i.d. Gaussian) and activation function (Identity, GELU, ReLU). The neural ODE structure (smooth, Lipschitz-continuous weights across depth) is preserved only when both a smooth activation (Identity or GELU) and weight-tied initialization are used. Switching to i.i.d. initialization breaks the ODE structure for all activations, and using ReLU breaks it even with weight-tied initialization (the authors prove this in Appendix D). The performance cost of maintaining the ODE structure is most visible with GELU: weight-tied initialization yields 79.9% test accuracy versus 85.7% for i.i.d., a 5.8-point gap. With ReLU, the gap is smaller (88.1% vs. 88.4%) but the ODE structure is lost in both cases. The Identity activation shows that the ODE structure can be maintained with minimal accuracy (59.8%), confirming that the theoretical guarantees come at a representational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural ODE framework of Chen et al. (2018) by proving that the ODE structure is not merely an initialization artifact but is preserved throughout gradient flow training for nonlinear residual networks. It builds on the proof technique of Nguyen & Mondelli (2020) for establishing Polyak-Lojasiewicz conditions, adapting it to the residual setting with only linear overparameterization (m = Ω(n)), improving on prior results requiring polynomial width. The closest prior work, Sander et al. (2022b), proved ODE limits for trained residual networks but only under linear activations; this paper removes that restriction. It also departs from Cont et al. (2022), which uses a 1/√L scaling yielding a non-ODE limit and trains only one set of weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This result provides the first rigorous mathematical foundation for the widely-stated but previously unproven claim that trained residual networks and neural ODEs coincide in the large-depth limit, opening the door to transferring approximation and generalization results from the neural ODE literature to the much larger family of residual networks. The linear overparameterization requirement for the PL condition is practically meaningful, as it suggests the ODE structure and convergence guarantees hold in regimes closer to real network sizes. Key open questions include whether the limiting neural ODE minimizes a specific complexity measure (connecting to the broader implicit bias literature), whether i.i.d. initialization leads to a stochastic differential equation limit as suggested by prior work, and whether the performance gap observed with weight-tied initialization on CIFAR-10 can be closed.
