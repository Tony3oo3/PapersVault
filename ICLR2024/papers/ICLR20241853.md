## Problem

In distributional RL, the projection of unconstrained return distributions onto a parametric family imposes a strong inductive bias that shapes generalization behavior, yet no prior work has exploited this diversity across different projections for uncertainty estimation. Existing distributional RL methods lack built-in epistemic uncertainty quantification, and standard deep ensembles trained with shared TD targets produce disagreement that reflects TD errors rather than true distributional errors, undermining their utility for directed exploration.

## Input / Output

The agent receives either structured state observations (bsuite grid-worlds) or 42×42 grayscale pixel observations with 6-frame stacking (VizDoom MyWayHome). It outputs an exploratory action selected by maximizing the sum of the mixture value estimate and a learned epistemic bonus, where the value is the mean of an equal mixture between a K-atom quantile distribution and a K-atom categorical distribution. The bonus is itself a distributional estimate (a second projection ensemble) trained with the average pairwise 1-Wasserstein disagreement between the two main ensemble members as an intrinsic reward signal.

## Method

PE-DQN combines a QR-DQN model and a C51 model into a two-member diverse projection ensemble, where each member is updated toward the projected mixture of the bootstrapped target distribution via its own projection operator (quantile regression loss with importance-sampling correction for QR, KL divergence for C51). The key theoretical contribution is a propagation scheme (Theorem 3) establishing that an upper bound on the total distributional error satisfies a Bellman-like temporal consistency condition relating it to a locally estimable one-step error, enabling a separate bonus network to be trained with ensemble disagreement as intrinsic reward. Action selection follows a UCB rule: argmax_a [E[Z|s,a] + β·b_φ(s,a)], where β decays linearly over training. The paper proves that the combined projection-mixture operator Ω_M T^π is a γ·c̄_p-contraction in the supremum p-Wasserstein metric, and derives explicit bounds on residual approximation error as a function of the number of atoms K, showing that residual ensemble disagreement at convergence is O(1/K).

## Results

On the Behavior Suite (468 environments across 23 tasks), PE-DQN achieves its strongest gains in the exploration capability category, with improvements also observed in several other core capabilities, while using only 4 distributional models compared to 20 in the ensemble baselines. On the deep sea benchmark at grid size 100 (double the maximum in bsuite), PE-DQN reduces median episodic regret by roughly 50% relative to baselines. On the VizDoom MyWayHome task, PE-DQN is the only method that successfully finds the goal object across all 10 seeds in all three sparsity variations (Dense, Sparse, Very Sparse), whereas baselines fail on the sparser variants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PE-DQN is compared against three baselines: BDQN+P (Osband et al., 2019), IDS-C51 (Nikolov et al., 2019), and DLTV QR-DQN (Mavrin et al., 2019). PE-DQN outperforms all three on the exploration-heavy tasks, with the most pronounced gains on deep sea and the VizDoom MyWayHome environments. Notably, PE-DQN achieves these improvements with substantially fewer models (4 total: 2 quantile + 2 categorical) versus 20 DQN models in BDQN+P and IDS-C51. On the broader bsuite evaluation, PE-DQN also improves over baselines in non-exploration categories, suggesting the diverse projection ensemble aids general learning stability. Ablation studies confirm that both the diverse projections (vs. homogeneous QR/QR or C51/C51 ensembles) and the uncertainty propagation scheme (vs. direct disagreement-based selection with independent training) are each indispensable for PE-DQN's performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of distributional RL (Bellemare et al., 2017; Dabney et al., 2018b) and deep ensemble methods for uncertainty estimation (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017; Osband et al., 2019). It builds on the contraction properties of the distributional Bellman operator under categorical and quantile projections (Rowland et al., 2018; Dabney et al., 2018b) and the optimism-in-the-face-of-uncertainty principle for exploration (Auer, 2002; Jin et al., 2018). The key departure from prior ensemble-based distributional RL work (Clements et al., 2019; Eriksson et al., 2022; Nikolov et al., 2019) is the deliberate combination of *different* projection operators within a single ensemble, rather than multiple copies of the same model, and the derivation of a propagation scheme for epistemic uncertainty that reconciles bootstrapped TD targets with optimistic action selection—extending the uncertainty Bellman equation (O'Donoghue et al., 2018) to the distributional setting via error decomposition rather than posterior variance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded mechanism for generating diverse ensembles in distributional RL without relying solely on random initialization, and shows that just 4 distributional models (2 quantile + 2 categorical) can outperform 20-model baselines on exploration-heavy benchmarks. The residual error bounds (Propositions 8–9, Corollary 12) make explicit the trade-off between representational capacity (number of atoms K) and irreducible ensemble disagreement, which is important for practitioners tuning these methods. Open questions include whether non-uniform mixture weights or region-specific model selection could further exploit the strengths of individual projections, and whether the approach extends naturally to more than two projection types or to off-policy settings with more complex function approximation.
