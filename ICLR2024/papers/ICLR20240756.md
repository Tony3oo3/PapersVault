## Problem

SDP relaxations for K-means clustering achieve information-theoretic sharp thresholds for exact recovery under Gaussian mixture models, but require optimizing over a dense n×n positive semidefinite matrix, making them infeasible beyond n ≈ 1000. Nonnegative matrix factorization (NMF) is scalable to large datasets but lacks any provable statistical guarantee. The paper addresses the gap between these two extremes: can one obtain an algorithm as simple and scalable as NMF while retaining the exact-recovery optimality of the SDP?

## Input / Output

The method takes a data matrix X ∈ R^{p×n} (n data points in p dimensions) and a target number of clusters K, and internally operates on the negative Gram matrix A = −X^T X. It produces a low-rank factor matrix U ∈ R^{n×r} (with r ≥ K, typically r = 2K in practice) whose rows serve as latent positions in R^r, from which final cluster assignments are extracted via a rounding step (top-K eigenvectors of U followed by K-means). The per-iteration time and space complexity is O(nr), making it linear in both sample size and dimension.

## Method

The algorithm reparameterizes the SDP membership matrix Z as Z = UU^T with U ≥ 0 (a Burer-Monteiro nonnegative low-rank factorization), converting the convex SDP into a nonconvex problem over an n×r matrix. It then applies an augmented Lagrangian method (ALM) to enforce the row-sum constraint UU^T 1_n = 1_n, and solves the resulting primal subproblem via projected gradient descent onto the set Ω = {U : ‖U‖_F² = K, U ≥ 0}, whose projection has a closed form Π_Ω(V) = √K·(V)_+ / ‖(V)_+‖_F. The key technical contribution is proving that this projected gradient descent achieves local linear convergence with rate γ = 1 − O(K^{−6}) within a neighborhood of the SDP solution, which—combined with the standard ALM dual convergence—yields overall primal-dual linear convergence. This distinguishes the method from prior nonnegative low-rank ALMs (e.g., Kulis et al. 2007) that could not solve the primal update to high accuracy, effectively reducing the algorithm to an inexact ALM with degraded convergence.

## Results

On the UCI benchmark (Table 1), NLR achieves the lowest mis-clustering error across all five datasets: 0.161 on Msplice, 0.230 on Heart, 0.188 on DNA, 0.243 on DNA₁ (t-noise), and 0.235 on DNA₂ (skewed-normal noise), consistently outperforming or matching SDP (0.194, 0.248, 0.196, 0.263, 0.252 respectively). Under the GMM simulation (Figure 2), NLR and SDP achieve identical near-zero mis-clustering error that decays with sample size, while NMF, K-means++, and spectral clustering exhibit large, non-vanishing errors. Computationally, NLR maintains linear time complexity O(nr) per iteration (comparable to NMF and K-means++), whereas SDP scales super-linearly (≈O(n^3.5)) and becomes intractable beyond n≈2,000.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NLR dominates all four baselines on every dataset. Against NMF (Ding et al., 2005), NLR reduces mis-clustering error by roughly 50–60% (e.g., 0.161 vs. 0.372 on Msplice; 0.230 vs. 0.470 on Heart), and NMF fails catastrophically at high dimension (error 0.75 at p=1000 in Table 2). Against K-means++ (Arthur & Vassilvitskii, 2007), NLR consistently achieves 20–40% lower error (e.g., 0.188 vs. 0.294 on DNA) with much smaller variance. Against spectral clustering (Ng et al., 2001), NLR wins on all datasets (e.g., 0.161 vs. 0.288 on Msplice), and SC produces many outliers on real data (CyTOF, CIFAR-10). Against SDP (Peng & Wei, 2007, solved via SDPNAL+), NLR matches or slightly beats SDP on mis-clustering error (e.g., 0.161 vs. 0.194 on Msplice; 0.230 vs. 0.248 on Heart) while being orders of magnitude faster—SDP becomes intractable beyond n≈2,000 due to its O(n^3.5) scaling, whereas NLR scales linearly in n.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three classical K-means relaxations—SDP, NMF, and spectral clustering—by showing they all solve slightly different relaxations of the same mixed-integer program, then proposes a nonnegative low-rank (NLR) formulation that sits at their intersection. It builds directly on the Burer-Monteiro factorization (2003) and the SDP relaxation of Peng and Wei (2007), while inheriting the sharp exact-recovery threshold from Chen and Yang (2021). The key departure from the closest prior work, Kulis et al. (2007), is the use of projected gradient descent to solve the primal ALM subproblem to machine precision, overcoming the inexact-solve bottleneck that previously made the nonnegative low-rank ALM impractically slow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing tension in clustering: SDP provides information-theoretically optimal guarantees but is computationally intractable beyond ~1000 points, while NMF is scalable but lacks any statistical guarantee. By proving that a simple NMF-like projected gradient descent algorithm achieves the same sharp threshold as SDP with linear convergence, the work makes statistically optimal K-means accessible to practical dataset sizes. Limitations include the analysis being restricted to the r=K (exact rank) case under GMM with well-separated centroids, a non-trivial initialization requirement, and the open question of extending guarantees to r>K and non-Gaussian or high-dimensional regimes where the SDP itself may not be optimal.
