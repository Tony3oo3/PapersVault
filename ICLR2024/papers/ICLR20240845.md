## Problem

RLHF alignment of LLMs depends on human-labeled pairwise preferences, which are expensive to collect at scale. Existing alternatives—RLAIF (scoring i.i.d. outputs from the same prompt) and context distillation (generating single outputs from modified prompts for SFT)—each sacrifice a key advantage: RLAIF's outputs are often too similar to label reliably without humans, while context distillation discards the pairwise preference signal that makes RL-based alignment effective. The paper asks whether combining both ideas—directional prompt modification for cleaner contrast plus pairwise preference structure for RL—can yield better alignment without human feedback.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unaligned LLM (LLaMA-7B) and a set of conversation prompts, then constructs two prompt variants per prompt by inserting short descriptive affixes in parentheses before the 'Assistant:' indicator (e.g., '(harmless, helpful response)' vs. '(harmful, unhelpful response)'), while also replacing earlier 'Assistant:' strings with the opposite affix to amplify contrast. The two outputs o+ and o− are generated from these different prompts and automatically labeled as a preference pair (o+ preferred) without any post-hoc scoring. The pipeline then produces a preference model (fine-tuned from the same base LLM), a reward model derived from it, and a final aligned LLM via PPO with KL-divergence regularization.

## Method

RLCD's core innovation is that the two responses in a preference pair need not be generated i.i.d. from the same prompt. Instead, it constructs a positive prompt p+ encouraging the desired attribute and a negative prompt p− encouraging the opposite, keeping their surface forms as similar as possible (differing only in the affix words) to avoid introducing orthogonal biases. Outputs o+ and o− are generated from p+ and p− respectively, and o+ is automatically labeled as preferred by construction, eliminating the noisy post-hoc scoring step in RLAIF. This trades examples near the classification boundary (which are hard to label without humans) for more clearly differentiated pairs with more accurate labels. The resulting preference pairs are used to train a standard Bradley-Terry preference model, which is converted to a reward model and used to fine-tune the base LLM via PPO with KL regularization, following the standard RLHF pipeline. Empirically, RLCD substantially outperforms RLAIF and context distillation baselines on harmlessness, helpfulness, and story outlining tasks at both 7B and 30B preference-data-simulation scales.

## Results

On GPT-4 binary pairwise evaluations (1000 examples per comparison), RLCD_7B is preferred over the unaligned LLaMA-7B baseline by 82.8% on harmlessness, 90.7% on helpfulness, and 76.0% on story outlining. Against RLAIF_7B, RLCD_7B wins by 84.8% on harmlessness and 85.4% on helpfulness. At 30B simulation scale, RLCD_30B still beats LLaMA and Context-Dist_30B across all tasks, but is roughly tied with or slightly below RLAIF_30B on GPT-4 (e.g., 47.8% vs. 52.2% on helpfulness), while human evaluators consistently rate RLCD_30B as equal or better.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the unaligned LLaMA-7B baseline, RLCD wins decisively at both 7B and 30B simulation scales across all three tasks (e.g., 90.7% preference on helpfulness at 7B). Against RLAIF, the gap is largest at 7B scale where RLCD_7B is preferred 84.8% of the time on harmlessness and 85.4% on helpfulness, but at 30B scale the comparison is much closer: RLCD_30B is preferred 60.3% on harmlessness but loses on helpfulness (47.8%) and outlining (35.9%) per GPT-4, though human evaluators still rate RLCD_30B as equal or better. Against Context-Dist (supervised fine-tuning on positive-prompt outputs only), RLCD wins at both scales, with the largest margins on helpfulness (89.5% at 7B, 74.9% at 30B) and outlining (71.8% at 7B, 62.2% at 30B), confirming that the pairwise RL signal from contrastive prompts adds value beyond single-output SFT. The paper also notes that RLAIF_7B's preference model performs below chance on human-labeled harmlessness data (35.6% accuracy), while RLCD_7B achieves 52.4%, suggesting the contrastive generation procedure yields cleaner training signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

RLCD sits at the intersection of two established lines of work on LLM alignment without human labels: RLAIF (Bai et al., 2022b), which simulates pairwise preferences by scoring i.i.d. outputs with an LLM, and context distillation (Huang et al., 2022; Sun et al., 2023), which uses modified prompts to elicit directionally improved outputs for supervised fine-tuning. The key departure is that RLCD modifies the *generation* procedure rather than the *labeling* procedure: it produces two outputs from contrasting positive and negative prompts and labels them by construction, thereby combining the pairwise-preference structure needed for RL with the directional attribute control of context distillation. The downstream pipeline (preference model training + PPO) follows the standard RLHF recipe from Ouyang et al. (2022) and Bai et al. (2022a) unchanged.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RLCD offers a practical, low-cost path to LLM alignment at 7B scale where RLAIF performs poorly (even below chance on harmlessness preference modeling), potentially enabling faster iteration for resource-constrained researchers. The method's simplicity—just writing contrasting parenthetical descriptions—makes it easy to adopt within existing RLAIF workflows. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger pretrained models (the paper only tests up to 30B for data simulation), whether intentionally creating harder examples near the decision boundary (as the discussion suggests) could further improve signal, and whether alternative preference formats (multi-output ranking, fine-grained annotations) would be more effective. The paper also notes that prompt design remains a sensitivity, and results are limited to English.
