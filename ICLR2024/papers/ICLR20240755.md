## Problem

Existing generalization bounds for neural networks either rely on low-rank weight matrices (compression-based) or use (p,q)-norms that grow with layer width, leaving unexplained why networks with full-rank or orthogonal weight matrices generalize well. The paper asks what spectral property of full-rank weight matrices governs generalization and provides a bound that is tight precisely when the condition number is small.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the weight matrices W_j (via their operator norms, singular values, and determinants), the smoothness parameters s_j of Sobolev function spaces (requiring s_j > d_j/2), and the activation functions (which must be s-times differentiable with bounded derivatives and bi-Lipschitz). It produces a Rademacher complexity upper bound of the form O(‖g‖_{H_L}/√n · ∏ G_j E_j ‖W_j‖^{s_{j-1}} / det(W_j^*W_j)^{1/4}), where the determinant in the denominator means the bound shrinks as singular values become more uniform. The bound is completely independent of network width when weight matrices are orthogonal.

## Method

The core technique represents the L-layer network as a product of Koopman operators K_{W_j}, K_{b_j}, K_{σ_j} acting on Sobolev-type RKHS H_{p_j}(R^{d_j}), then bounds the Rademacher complexity by the product of individual operator norms. The key mechanism is that bounding ‖K_{W_j}‖ via a Fourier-domain change of variables introduces a 1/|det(W_j)| factor, so the bound depends on the full spectrum of W_j rather than just its largest singular value. This yields a bound tight when the condition number r_{d,j} = η_{1,j}/η_{d,j} is small, and for orthogonal matrices the entire weight-dependent factor collapses to 1. The paper further shows the bound can be combined layer-wise with existing norm-based bounds (e.g., Golowich et al.) to exploit the empirical observation that lower layers tend to have small condition numbers while upper layers have large ones.

## Results

The main theoretical result is a Rademacher complexity bound of the form O(‖g‖_{H_L}/√n · ∏_j G_j E_j ‖W_j‖^{s_{j-1}} / det(W_j^*W_j)^{1/4}), which is completely independent of network width when weight matrices are orthogonal (the determinant factor reduces to 1). Numerically, on MNIST with only n=1000 training samples, adding a regularization term ‖W_j‖ + 1/det(I+W_j^*W_j) based on the proposed bound improves test accuracy over the unregularized network (Figure 1b). On CIFAR-10 with AlexNet, the condition number r_{d,j} of layer 2 decreases during training (supporting the tightness of the Koopman-based bound for lower layers), while layers 3–4 exhibit large condition numbers (where existing norm-based bounds are tighter).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not outperform existing bounds in a single unified sense; instead, it provides a complementary bound that is tight in a different regime. Specifically, the Koopman-based bound is tight when the condition number r_{d,j} = η_{1,j}/η_{d,j} of W_j is small (all singular values are comparable), whereas the Golowich et al. (2018) bound η_{1,j}(Σ r_{i,j}^{-2})^{1/2} is tight when r_{d,j} is large (one dominant singular value). For orthogonal weight matrices, the proposed bound reduces to 1 per layer, while norm-based bounds still scale as d^{1/p} for the (p,q) norm of the identity. The key practical advantage is that the two families of bounds can be combined layer-by-layer (Proposition 10, Remark 10): applying the Koopman-based bound to lower layers (where condition numbers tend to be small, as confirmed on CIFAR-10/AlexNet) and existing norm-based or compression bounds to higher layers (where condition numbers are large) yields a tighter overall bound than either family alone. Compression-based bounds (Arora 2018, Suzuki 2020) capture low-rank generalization but cannot explain the good generalization of full-rank networks observed by Goldblum et al. (2020), a gap the Koopman-based bound fills.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Koopman operator theory from dynamical systems analysis to derive a new Rademacher complexity bound for neural networks, building on RKHS-based complexity bounding (Mohri et al., 2018) and composition operator boundedness results (Sawano, 2018; Ikeda et al., 2022). It departs from both norm-based bounds (Neyshabur, Golowich, Bartlett, Wei & Ma) and compression-based bounds (Arora, Suzuki) by introducing a determinant factor in the denominator, making the bound tight when condition numbers are small rather than large. The authors explicitly position their result as complementary to existing bounds: norm-based bounds are tight for large condition numbers while their Koopman-based bound is tight for small condition numbers, and the two can be combined layer-by-layer for tighter overall guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled explanation for why full-rank and orthogonal weight matrices generalize well, addressing a gap left by compression-based theories that only explain low-rank generalization. However, practical applicability is limited: the framework requires smooth bi-Lipschitz activation functions (excluding standard ReLU, sigmoid, tanh without modification), the non-injective case necessitates modifying the network architecture, and the G_j factor's dependence on network structure remains incompletely characterized. Open questions include deriving tight bounds for the Koopman operator norm of activation functions at higher smoothness orders, connecting layers rather than bounding them independently, and extending to function spaces on manifolds.
