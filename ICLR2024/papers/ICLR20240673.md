## Problem

Bayesian bilevel optimization (BBO), which nests SGD-based model training inside Bayesian optimization for hyperparameter tuning, lacks any theoretical convergence guarantee. Prior Bayesian optimization analyses rely on noise assumptions (martingale difference sequences, bounded or sub-Gaussian noise) that do not reflect the actual evaluation noise produced by finite SGD iterations, and they guarantee only hyperparameter convergence while ignoring model parameter convergence.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a training set S^tr, a validation set S^val (both drawn from the same distribution D), a compact hyperparameter space Λ, a positive semi-definite GP kernel k with k(x,x)≤1, and a bounded loss function ℓ. It outputs the optimal hyperparameter λ* and the corresponding SGD-trained model parameters θ_{λ*}^N, with the theoretical guarantee being a sublinear cumulative regret R_T = O(√(Tγ_T)) that simultaneously bounds both the hyperparameter suboptimality and the model parameter excess risk. The number of inner SGD steps N per outer BO iteration is a tunable hyperparameter whose optimal scaling (N ≍ T for convex, N ≍ T² for non-convex) is derived as part of the theory.

## Method

The pivotal technical contribution is modeling the SGD excess risk L^val(λ, θ_λ^N, S^val) − L(λ, θ_λ*) as the evaluation noise ε_t in the outer GP regression, rather than assuming i.i.d. Gaussian or martingale-difference noise. Theorem 1 first establishes an excess risk bound of O(N^{-1/2} log^{3/2} N) via a three-term decomposition (generalization gap, optimization error, and training-set estimation error) using uniform stability and concentration inequalities. Lemma 2 then adapts the GP posterior concentration bound to this structured noise, yielding |μ_t(λ) − L(λ, θ_λ*)| ≤ √(B² + σ^{-2} t φ²(N) N^{-1}) σ_t(λ). Theorems 3 and 4 leverage this to derive regret bounds for EI and UCB acquisition functions respectively, with the EI bound achieving O(√(Tγ_T)) when N ≍ T (saving a √γ_T factor over Gupta et al. 2022). For UCB, the balancing coefficient is set to β_t = √(B² + σ^{-2} t φ²(N) N^{-1}), which is adaptable via the N/T ratio and avoids the rapidly escalating coefficients of prior GP-UCB analyses, providing greater flexibility in the inner unit horizon.

## Results

The paper establishes sublinear regret bounds of O(√(Tγ_T)) for Bayesian bilevel optimization with both EI and UCB acquisition functions, achieved by setting the inner SGD horizon N ≍ T (outer BO iterations). Empirically, on MNIST with a 2-conv-layer CNN, the best validation loss is 2.10±0.08 (EI, 60 BO steps, 4000 SGD iterations) and 2.19±0.19 (UCB, 60 BO steps, 4000 SGD iterations). The experiments confirm the theoretical trade-off: too few SGD steps yield high loss due to noisy BO evaluations, while excessively many SGD steps waste computation without further improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against competing hyperparameter optimization methods empirically; instead, it compares theoretical regret bounds against prior Bayesian optimization analyses. Against Gupta et al. (2022), which achieves O(γ_T√T) for EI with the standard predictive-mean incumbent, this paper improves the bound to O(√(Tγ_T)), yielding a saving of √γ_T. Against Chowdhury & Gopalan (2017) for UCB, whose bound is O(B√(Tγ_T) + γ_T√T), this paper tightens it to O(B√(Tγ_T)) by using adaptable β_t coefficients tied to the N/T ratio rather than rapidly escalating coefficients. Against Srinivas et al. (2010) and Nguyen & Gupta (2017), the key distinction is the noise model: prior works assume bounded or sub-Gaussian martingale-difference noise, whereas this paper models the SGD excess risk L^val(λ,θ_λ^N,S^val) − L(λ,θ_λ^*) as the evaluation noise, which does not satisfy E[ε_t|ε_{<t}]=0 and is more faithful to the bilevel training loop. The UCB variant additionally shows greater robustness to fewer SGD steps (N=O(√T) still yields sublinear regret under SE or Matérn-ν kernels with 2ν>d), unlike EI where the regret bound degrades rapidly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected theoretical frameworks: Bayesian optimization (Srinivas et al. 2010; Gupta et al. 2022; Chowdhury & Gopalan 2017) and bilevel optimization for hyperparameter tuning (Franceschi et al. 2018). The key departure from prior BO convergence analyses is replacing the standard martingale-difference or sub-Gaussian noise assumptions with the actual excess risk of SGD-trained parameters, which naturally couples the inner and outer levels. The SGD optimization error bound (Lei & Tang 2018) and GP posterior concentration framework (Srinivas et al. 2010) are adapted and combined to yield the first regret guarantees for the bilevel setting, while the EI analysis follows Gupta et al. (2022) with a tighter √γ_T improvement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first convergence guarantee for Bayesian bilevel optimization, providing practitioners with a principled rule (N ≍ T for convex, N ≍ T² for non-convex) for setting the inner SGD horizon relative to outer BO iterations. The adaptable UCB balancing coefficient β_t = √(B² + σ⁻²tφ²(N)N⁻¹) offers more flexible exploration-exploitation control than the rapidly escalating coefficients in prior GP-UCB analyses. However, experimental validation is limited to a small CNN on MNIST with a single hyperparameter (learning rate), leaving open whether the N ≍ T guidance holds for deeper architectures, multi-hyperparameter spaces, or non-convex loss landscapes at scale. Extensions to alternative acquisition functions, multi-fidelity settings, and larger-scale empirical studies are natural next steps.
