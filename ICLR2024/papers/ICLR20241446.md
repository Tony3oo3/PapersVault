## Problem

While vision-language models (VLMs) excel at recognizing semantic content in images, it remains unclear whether they can identify the visual data-type (e.g., rotation, noise, cartoon style) of an image — a basic perceptual skill critical for data curation and autonomous vision. This paper introduces Visual Data-Type Identification as a new task and demonstrates that this ability does not emerge from scaling: the largest auto-regressive VLMs (up to 80B parameters) perform substantially worse than much smaller contrastive models like CLIP, revealing a fundamental blind spot in current frontier VLMs.

## Input / Output

The task takes a single RGB image of an animal (768×768 for SyntheticTypeIdent; natural-resolution for NaturalTypeIdent) that has been altered by exactly one of 27 data-type transformations spanning four categories (geometric, pixel, style, semantic) and requires the model to output the correct data-type label. For contrastive VLMs (C-VLMs), evaluation computes cosine similarity between the image embedding and 27 fixed text-prompt embeddings (e.g., 'This is a blurred image of an animal'); for auto-regressive LMMs, evaluation uses log-likelihood scoring of each data-type description appended to a 'Describe the image' prompt. Performance is measured via informedness (TPR − FPR) per data-type, averaged across all 27 types, with chance level at 0.

## Method

The paper constructs two controlled benchmark datasets (SyntheticTypeIdent: 50 AI-generated animal reference images × 27 transformations = 1,350 samples; NaturalTypeIdent: 50 curated real photos × 25 transformations = 1,250 samples) and zero-shot evaluates 39 VLMs from 13 families (100M–80B parameters). To diagnose why VLMs fail, the authors perform two analyses: (1) t-SNE visualization of CLIP image embeddings shows clear semantic clustering but near-complete invariance to data-type distinctions, and (2) quantitative analysis of LAION-2B-en reveals a strong positive correlation (Spearman r ≈ 0.55–0.61) between data-type keyword frequency in pre-training captions and model performance, indicating pre-training data imbalance as a root cause. The paper then shows that training-free few-shot adaptation (TIP-Adapter for CLIP, in-context learning for Otter) fails to improve performance, and demonstrates that fine-tuning with a purpose-built dataset (TeDaTy) that explicitly encodes data-type information into image-text captions yields significant gains — up to 2× improvement for Otter on in-distribution data-types — confirming that the limitation is in training data composition rather than model capacity.

## Results

Across 39 VLMs (100M–80B parameters) evaluated zero-shot on 27 data-types, the best C-VLM (CLIP) achieves a mean informedness of 0.47 (SyntheticTypeIdent) and 0.50 (NaturalTypeIdent), while the best LMM achieves only 0.22 and 0.25 respectively. By contrast, the same models achieve 0.89 mean informedness on the semantic animal-identification task, confirming that data-type identification is substantially harder. Power-law fits indicate that current VLMs would need to surpass one trillion parameters to reach a practicable threshold of μ_I > 0.7. Fine-tuning CLIP ViT-B-32 with the TeDaTy dataset improves ID-data-type informedness from 0.451 to 0.669 on SyntheticTypeIdent, while freezing the vision encoder further boosts ID to 0.777.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks 12 model families (39 variants) and finds a consistent hierarchy: contrastively-trained C-VLMs (CLIP, CoCa, BLIP-2-ITM) substantially outperform auto-regressive LMMs (OpenFlamingo, Otter, LLaVA, BLIP-2-LLM, InstructBLIP, IDEFICS, MPlugOwl, Fromage, GILL, Multimodal-GPT) on data-type identification, despite LMMs being orders of magnitude larger. The most striking comparison is IDEFICS-80B (80B parameters) versus CLIP-RN50 (100M parameters), where the 800× larger model achieves roughly half the informedness. Within C-VLMs, scaling from CLIP-RN50 to CLIP-ViT-bigG-14 yields only marginal gains, confirming weak scaling behaviour. Within LMMs, the same weak scaling pattern holds for IDEFICS (9B → 80B). VLMs perform reasonably on style data-types (CARTOON, PENCIL_SKETCH) but systematically fail on simple geometric and pixel data-types (LEFT_ROTATE, GAUSSIAN_NOISE), with no single model dominating across all 27 types. Fine-tuning with TeDaTy data improves CLIP by ~22 points on ID data-types and Otter by ~17 points, while COCO-only fine-tuning degrades performance, confirming that data-type-specific information in training captions is essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing Visual Data-Type Identification as a task complementary to semantic recognition, where the goal is to identify the transformation or style applied to an image rather than its content. It departs from prior work on distribution shifts, OOD detection, and anomaly detection, which only determine the *presence* of a perturbation, by requiring the model to pinpoint its *exact nature* among 27 types. The evaluation framework builds on the contrastive (CLIP-style) vs. generative (LMM) VLM distinction and the log-likelihood scoring paradigm for evaluating auto-regressive models, while the fine-tuning experiments draw on CLIP-tuning literature (Zhai et al., 2022) and in-context learning. Prior work on identifying specific perceptual manipulations (mirroring, cropping) is subsumed and substantially generalized here to a 27-class, 4-category taxonomy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals a fundamental blind spot in frontier VLMs: they excel at semantic content recognition but fail to acquire data-type understanding through scaling, with the largest LMMs (80B IDEFICS) performing worse than 100M CLIP. This challenges the prevailing assumption that stronger language model priors and greater scale automatically improve fine-grained visual understanding, and points to actionable causes—pre-training data imbalance and weak vision-LLM alignment—that future training pipelines must address. Open questions include whether the discriminative-generative gap is the root cause of LMM underperformance, and whether the TeDaTy fine-tuning strategy generalizes beyond the 27 studied data-types to truly open-ended visual conditions. The introduced benchmarks (SyntheticTypeIdent, NaturalTypeIdent) and the TeDaTy dataset provide a reusable testbed for tracking progress on this previously unmeasured capability.
