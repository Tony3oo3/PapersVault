## Problem

Neural codec language models for zero-shot TTS face a scalability bottleneck: codecs like EnCodec produce multiple long token sequences (e.g., 8 streams × 375 tokens for 5 s of speech), forcing cascaded or iterative generative modeling across streams and inflating sequence length. This paper addresses both issues by compressing the token representation and enabling a single autoregressive step to emit all tokens at each timestep.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241960/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a text transcript and a 3-second speech prompt (with its transcript for continuation, or a different transcript for cross-sentence) and produces a raw-waveform speech output. Internally, the codec operates on mel-spectrograms at a 10 Hz codeword rate (8× downsampling) with 32-stage RVQ (codebook size 1024 per depth), and the final waveform is synthesized by an off-the-shelf BigVGAN vocoder. The ByT5-large text encoder is kept frozen; only the 12-layer transformer decoder and the Mel-VAE are trained.

## Method

CLaM-TTS is trained in two stages. First, a Mel-VAE (causal 1D convolutional U-Net) learns a probabilistic residual vector quantizer via mean-field variational inference, where each depth's codebook is optimized independently under a Gaussian likelihood p_ψ(z_t|c_t) = N(z_t; Σ_d e_ψ(c_{t,d};d), σ²I), ensuring all codewords participate in training and avoiding codebook collapse. Second, a latent language model (frozen ByT5-large encoder + trainable 12-layer transformer decoder) predicts a continuous latent vector z_t at each timestep as a Gaussian mixture p_θ(z_t|x, c_{<t}) = Σ_k p_θ(k|x,c_{<t}) N(z_t; μ_θ(k,x,c_{<t}), σ²I), which is then converted to all 32 discrete codes in one shot via the learned quantizer c_t = RQ_ψ(z_t). This eliminates the need for cascaded per-stream modeling. Training uses 100K hours across 11 languages with adversarial losses (multi-length + multi-resolution spectrogram discriminators) on the Mel-VAE and a variational lower-bound loss plus an EOS classifier loss on the language model.

## Results

On the English-only continuation task (LibriSpeech test-clean), CLaM-en achieves WER 2.36, CER 0.79, SIM-o 0.4767, and SIM-r 0.5128, with an inference time of 4.15 s for a 10-second utterance. In subjective evaluation, CLaM-en scores QMOS 3.87 and SMOS 3.49, approaching Ground Truth (4.45 and 4.18) while substantially outperforming YourTTS (2.39 and 2.32). On the multilingual continuation task, CLaM-multi achieves WER ranging from 4.02 (Spanish) to 19.70 (Italian) and SIM-o from 0.40 to 0.60 across 11 languages.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against YourTTS, CLaM-en dominates on every metric: WER drops from 7.57 to 2.36, CER from 3.06 to 0.79, SIM-o rises from 0.3928 to 0.4767, and subjective QMOS/SMOS improve by over 1.0 point. Against Vall-E, CLaM-en wins on WER (2.36 vs. 3.8), CER (0.79 vs. 1.58), SIM-o (0.4767 vs. 0.2875 unofficial), SIM-r (0.5128 vs. 0.3433 unofficial), and inference speed (4.15 s vs. ~6.2 s). Against VoiceBox, the picture is mixed: VoiceBox achieves lower WER (2.0 vs. 2.36) and higher SIM-o (0.593 vs. 0.4767) and SIM-r (0.616 vs. 0.5128), but requires phoneme and duration inputs and is slower (~6.4 s at 64 NFE vs. 4.15 s). In the cross-sentence task, SPEAR-TTS edges out CLaM-en on CER (1.92 vs. 2.87) and SIM-r (0.560 vs. 0.5382), while CLaM-en reports a competitive WER of 5.11. For the codec component, the proposed probabilistic RVQ Mel-VAE outperforms EnCodec at 6 kbps on both PESQ (2.95 vs. 2.59) and ViSQOL (4.66 vs. 4.26), confirming superior speech reconstruction at a higher compression rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural codec language model paradigm for zero-shot TTS (Vall-E, SPEAR-TTS, AudioLM) by replacing sequential per-depth token prediction with a single autoregressive step that outputs a continuous latent vector, subsequently quantized via a probabilistic RVQ into all 32 depth tokens simultaneously. The probabilistic RVQ itself departs from standard EnCodec-style residual quantization by formulating codebook learning through mean-field variational inference, directly addressing codebook collapse. The system also shifts the compression target from raw waveforms (EnCodec) to mel-spectrograms, delegating waveform synthesis to a separate vocoder (BigVGAN), and leverages a frozen pre-trained ByT5 encoder as the text conditioning backbone, following the SoundStorm/Spectron lineage of reusing LLMs for speech.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10 Hz codeword rate (vs. EnCodec's 75 Hz) and single-step multi-token generation substantially reduce sequence length and eliminate cascaded modeling, making neural codec LM-based TTS more tractable to scale. However, the paper acknowledges that VoiceBox (which uses phonemes and explicit duration) still outperforms CLaM-TTS on several metrics, and autoregressive artifacts (word repetition/omission) remain unresolved. Open questions include whether non-autoregressive or hybrid architectures can fix these artifacts, whether the 10 Hz rate generalizes across languages with different phoneme densities, and how instruction-style prompting (gender, accent, emotion) can exploit the full LLM knowledge without additional training.
