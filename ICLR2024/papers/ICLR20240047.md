## Problem

Seven recently proposed post-hoc concept erasure methods for text-to-image diffusion models claim to permanently remove sensitive concepts (artistic styles, celebrity likenesses, objects, NSFW content) from model weights. This paper demonstrates that all seven methods can be fully circumvented without any weight modification, revealing that they perform input filtering rather than true concept excision and providing a false sense of safety.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240047/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The attack requires a small set of example images of the target concept (6 for art style, 30 for objects, 25 for IDs, sourced from Google Images), the frozen weights of the erased model, and a placeholder token string. The output is a single learned word embedding (one vector in the text encoder's embedding space) that, when substituted into a prompt, regenerates the erased concept. No model parameters are modified; the entire attack is a lightweight optimization of one embedding vector on a single A100 GPU for 1,000–5,000 steps.

## Method

The core technique, Concept Inversion (CI), applies Textual Inversion to the erased model to discover a word embedding that reactivates the supposedly removed concept. For weight-modifying methods (ESD, UCE, SA, FMN, AC), standard Textual Inversion suffices: the placeholder embedding is optimized against the standard denoising loss while all model weights remain frozen. For inference-guiding methods, the objective is modified: for Negative Prompt, the loss equates the NP-guided noise prediction with the standard classifier-free guidance prediction; for Safe Latent Diffusion, a novel scheme approximates the recursive safety guidance terms by sampling at 35 evenly spaced timesteps rather than tracking all intermediate steps, avoiding prohibitive memory costs. A key finding is transferability: the learned embeddings also work on the original un-erased SD 1.4 model, confirming that erasure methods merely suppress the concept for specific input embeddings rather than removing the underlying generative capacity.

## Results

Across all seven concept erasure methods, CI recovers erased concepts with no weight modifications. For object concepts (Imagenette, ResNet-50 top-1 accuracy), the average accuracy of erased models drops to 0.04–28.6% (vs. 77.9% for SD 1.4), while CI restores it to 40.3–79.5%. For ID concepts (GIPHY celebrity detector), accuracy drops to 0.0–44.7% (vs. 90.9% for SD 1.4) and CI recovers it to 47.0–76.6%. For NSFW content (I2P benchmark, NudeNet), the average number of detected exposed body parts increases from 26.21 (erased models) to 170.93 (CI), exceeding the original SD 1.4 baseline of 99.75.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper demonstrates that CI circumvents all seven concept erasure baselines. ESD (Gandikota et al., ICCV 2023) achieves the strongest erasure on objects (0.2% avg. accuracy) but CI recovers 60.1%; UCE (Gandikota et al., 2023) similarly drops to 2.9% and CI recovers 59.4%. NP (AUTOMATIC1111, 2022) is the weakest eraser (28.6% residual accuracy) and CI pushes it to 76.2%, the highest CI recovery among the four main-body methods. SLD-Med (Schramowski et al., CVPR 2023) is the most challenging to circumvent (23.0% residual), yet CI still recovers 79.5%—the highest CI accuracy of any method. Among the three appendix-only methods, AC (Kumari et al., ICCV 2023) erases most aggressively (0.04% residual) and CI recovers 45.5%; FMN (Zhang et al., 2023) leaves 3.9% residual with CI reaching 44.6%; SA (Heng & Soh, 2023) leaves 10.6% residual with CI at 40.3%. For ID concepts, AC is the most vulnerable to CI (76.6% recovery), while FMN is the hardest (47.0%), and for NSFW content, AC is most vulnerable (252.63 detected body parts post-CI) while SA is least effective for CI (5.0). The consistent pattern across all seven methods is that erasure is effective only against the exact target prompt text, but a learned soft-prompt embedding bypasses the protection entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Textual Inversion (Gal et al., 2023) — originally designed for personalizing text-to-image generation — as an adversarial attack tool against seven recently proposed concept erasure methods (ESD, UCE, NP, SLD, SA, FMN, AC). Conceptually, it departs from the erasure literature's framing that weight modifications 'permanently remove' targeted concepts, instead demonstrating that these methods perform a form of input filtering: the concept persists in the model but is remapped to different word embeddings. The paper also connects to the broader machine unlearning literature, showing that the gap between claimed and actual forgetting is far larger than previously acknowledged for diffusion-based generative models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that all seven concept erasure methods — spanning fundamentally different algorithmic strategies (fine-tuning, inference guidance, closed-form weight editing) — are uniformly vulnerable to the same Textual Inversion-based attack challenges the core premise of post-hoc sanitization for generative AI. The paper calls for adversarial evaluation protocols (using CI attacks during benchmarking) rather than testing only mild prompt variations, and raises the open question of why diverse methods all converge on input filtering rather than true concept removal. Limitations include the white-box assumption and focus on Stable Diffusion 1.4/2.0, leaving open whether the findings generalize to other architectures or closed-source models. The practical implication is significant: organizations deploying 'sanitized' models may have a false sense of security against motivated adversaries.
