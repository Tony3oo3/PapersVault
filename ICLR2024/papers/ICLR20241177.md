## Problem

Existing domain-agnostic INR generative models (e.g., Functa, GASP, GEM, DPF) synthesize neural network weights while evaluating with fixed positional embeddings, which places an excessive burden on the weights to capture diverse signal details and limits generation quality. This paper identifies that shifting the generative target from MLP weights to adaptive positional embeddings can substantially improve expressive power across signal domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241177/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a random latent vector (sampled via reverse diffusion) or a text-conditioned latent vector (via CLIP encoder + classifier-free guidance) and produces a continuous function parameterized by generated basis fields and a shared MLP. For 2D images the latent is a single 2D plane; for 3D shapes and videos it is a 2D tri-plane. The resulting INR can be queried at arbitrary spatial or spatiotemporal coordinates, enabling arbitrary-resolution image synthesis (e.g., 256², 384², 512² from a single model), 3D occupancy functions, 16-frame 256² videos, and NeRF radiance fields.

## Method

DDMI introduces a Discrete-to-continuous space VAE (D2C-VAE) whose encoder maps discrete data to a shared latent space and whose decoder generates Hierarchically-Decomposed Basis Fields (HDBFs) at three scales from a single U-Net's feature hierarchy. A Coarse-to-Fine Conditioning (CFC) mechanism progressively feeds multi-scale positional embeddings (obtained via bilinear interpolation on the basis fields) into the read-out MLP, encouraging each scale to capture complementary spatial frequencies. A latent diffusion model (2D U-Net with cross-plane attention for tri-plane latents) is then trained in this shared latent space using standard DDPM noise prediction with mixed parameterization. The key architectural shift is that the generative model produces sample-specific basis fields (adaptive PEs) rather than MLP weights, transferring the primary expressive burden from the network parameters to the positional embedding.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ at 256², DDMI achieves an FID of 7.25, outperforming all domain-agnostic INR baselines (GASP: 19.2 at 128², DPF: 13.2 at 64²) and domain-specific INR models (CIPS: 11.4, INR-GAN: 10.3). On AFHQv2 Cat at 256², DDMI reaches FID 8.54 versus CIPS (23.93) and GASP (35.78). For 3D shape generation on ShapeNet, DDMI achieves the best MMD of 1.3 (multi-class) and 1.5 (chair), surpassing GEM (1.4) and DPF (1.6). On SkyTimelapse video generation, DDMI attains FVD 66.25, beating the strongest discrete baseline PVDM (71.46) and domain-specific INR models StyleGAN-V (79.52) and DIGAN (83.11).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDMI consistently outperforms all domain-agnostic INR generative models (Functa, GEM, GASP, DPF) across every image dataset and resolution tested, with the largest margins over Functa (40.4 vs. 9.74 FID on CelebA-HQ 64²) and GEM (30.4 vs. 9.74). Against domain-specific INR models, DDMI beats CIPS by 2.97 average FID across three resolutions on AFHQv2 Cat (8.54 vs. 23.93 at 256²) and INR-GAN by 22.73 FID on the same setting. However, DDMI still trails discrete-representation models like LDM (5.51) and StyleGAN (3.25–6.73) on image FID, though it is the only method that generates at arbitrary scales. For 3D shapes, DDMI achieves the best MMD (1.3 multi-class, 1.5 chair) and highest COV (0.421 multi-class) among all continuous-representation baselines, surpassing GEM (1.4 MMD) and DPF (1.6 MMD). On text-to-shape generation, DDMI (w=3) achieves 91.30% classification accuracy and CLIP-S 30.30, outperforming Diffusion-SDF (88.56%, 28.63) while maintaining comparable diversity (TMD 0.204 vs. 0.169). For video, DDMI's FVD of 66.25 on SkyTimelapse is the best among all compared methods, beating PVDM (71.46) and StyleGAN-V (79.52).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in domain-agnostic INR generation (Functa, GASP, GEM, DPF), which models the distribution over MLP weights while keeping positional embeddings fixed. DDMI inverts this by generating adaptive basis fields that produce sample-specific PEs, shifting the expressive burden from the network weights to the embedding. The diffusion backbone follows the latent diffusion model framework of Rombach et al. (2022) and Vahdat et al. (2021), but the VAE component (D2C-VAE) is a novel asymmetric architecture that maps discrete data to continuous function space via a shared latent representation, rather than the symmetric autoencoders used in prior LDMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conceptual reframing—generating PEs instead of weights—provides a cleaner inductive bias for INR generation and is validated consistently across four modalities (2D images, 3D shapes, NeRF, video) and seven datasets, suggesting the insight generalizes beyond any single domain. The arbitrary-scale generation capability (a single model producing coherent outputs from 64² to 512²) has direct practical value for super-resolution and multi-resolution applications. The main open limitation is generation speed: the diffusion process requires ~1000 NFEs for best quality, and ODE solvers with fewer steps show noticeable degradation, pointing to the need for more compact latent spaces or distillation strategies.
