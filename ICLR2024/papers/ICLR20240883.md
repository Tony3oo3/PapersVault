## Problem

Existing LLM agent environments target narrow, task-specific skills (web navigation, code generation, robot planning) and lack a scalable, reliable way to evaluate open-ended societal analysis and decision-making. Question-answering benchmarks are expensive to maintain and cannot keep pace with the ever-evolving dynamics of real-world society. SocioDojo fills this gap by providing a lifelong learning environment grounded in real-world texts and time series that can assess an agent's analytical power in a continuously updatable manner.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a continuous stream of timestamped messages (news, social media posts, reports, papers) from curated channels, with the world clock set to each message's publication time. It can query a retrieval-based knowledge base (segmented books, journals, encyclopedias via ChromaDB + Instructor-XL embeddings), 30K time series across finance, economics, Google Trends, and polls, and live search/knowledge-graph tools filtered to the current world time. The agent outputs multiline trading scripts (BUY/SELL [ICode] [amount]) executed by a Broker module, along with structured analysis reports containing hypotheses, proofs, intuitions, and references. The hyperportfolio starts at $1M cash and is scored by total asset value at the end date (Aug 1, 2023), with mechanisms like a 5-day no-sell lock, 1% commission, overnight fees, and a 5× return bound to suppress speculation.

## Method

The paper introduces the hyperportfolio task, a POMDP where all high-quality time series (not just financial ones) serve as investable assets whose 'price' is the latest observed value, converting the hard problem of estimating a hidden societal state into a scalable surrogate decision-making task. The Analyst-Assistant-Actuator (AAA) architecture decomposes the workflow into three specialized LLM roles: the Analyst reads incoming messages and formulates analyses, the Assistant retrieves evidence from the knowledge base, time series database, and search tools via multi-round dialog, and the Actuator translates analysis into concrete trading instructions. The key prompting innovation is Hypothesis & Proof (H&P), a zero-shot meta-thinking framework that instructs the analyst to generate out-of-domain hypotheses (e.g., 'cold weather reduces shopping willingness') and iteratively ask the assistant to find supporting or refuting evidence, rather than decomposing the task into atomic fact-checking steps as in Plan-and-Solve or Self-Ask. Ablations show the Analyst role is the primary bottleneck (upgrading it to GPT-4 yields the largest gain), and H&P produces significantly more valid ask calls (mean 1.91 vs. 0.72 for P&S), reflecting deeper hypothesis-driven exploration. The method achieves 32.4% and 30.4% relative improvements over the best baseline (Plan-and-Solve) in Standard and Tracking settings respectively.

## Results

In the Standard setting, H&P achieves 14.3% overall hyperportfolio return (Sharpe ratio 3.07), outperforming the best baseline P&S (10.8%, Sharpe 2.68) by 32.4%. In the Tracking setting, H&P reaches 15.0% return (Sharpe 2.36) versus P&S's 11.5% (Sharpe 2.19), a 30.4% improvement. H&P also leads on most topic-wise returns (Fin, Econ, Web) in both settings, while P&S slightly edges out on Poll in the Standard setting (4.1% vs. 3.6%). The vanilla agent without knowledge base access drops to 3.1% (Standard) and 6.6% (Tracking), confirming the critical role of retrieval-augmented context.

## Baselines & Comparisons

H&P outperforms all four baselines on overall return in both settings. Against P&S (the strongest baseline), H&P gains 32.4% in Standard (14.3% vs. 10.8%) and 30.4% in Tracking (15.0% vs. 11.5%), with the advantage driven primarily by deeper out-of-domain hypothesis generation that surfaces non-obvious investment signals. Self-Ask (9.9%/10.6%) and ReAct (9.4%/8.8%) trail further behind, as their fact-checking question patterns fail to produce the in-depth causal reasoning needed for long-horizon trend prediction. AutoGPT (8.9%/10.3%) performs worst among the prompted baselines in Standard, suggesting its generic planning loop is poorly suited to the open-ended analytical demands of hyperportfolio. Notably, P&S slightly beats H&P on Poll returns in Standard (4.1% vs. 3.6%), and AutoGPT matches H&P on Web in Tracking (13.3% vs. 13.7%), indicating that for narrow, trend-following topics the simpler planning approach can be competitive.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of LLM agent environments, reasoning prompting, and time-series-based evaluation. It departs from prior agent environments (Minecraft, StarCraft, web agents) by using real-world societal data rather than simulated or game-based settings, and from prior LLM reasoning methods (Chain-of-Thought, ReAct, Plan-and-Solve) by rejecting the assumption that open-ended societal analysis decomposes into atomic steps. The hyperportfolio task repurposes portfolio optimization from finance to all time series as a scalable evaluation of societal understanding, while the Hypothesis & Proof prompting draws on the scientific method rather than iterative question-asking or planning frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SocioDojo provides the first large-scale, real-world environment for evaluating an agent's ability to analyze and make decisions about society, addressing a gap where prior evaluation relied on expensive, static question-answering. The finding that the analyst role is the critical bottleneck (not the actuator or assistant) and that adding more information sources does not necessarily improve performance offers practical guidance for agent design. Open questions include whether the daily-close-price information advantage materially distorts results, whether the overnight-rate robustness mechanisms generalize to new time series, and whether the approach scales beyond GPT-3.5/4 to open-weight or smaller models.
