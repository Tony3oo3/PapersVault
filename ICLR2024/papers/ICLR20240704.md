## Problem

Instruction fine-tuning of LLMs on small curated datasets leads to significant overfitting to the training distribution, causing models to produce shorter, less diverse responses that closely mimic the training data. The LLM community has largely relied on standardized training loops optimized for stability rather than generalization, overlooking regularization techniques well-studied in computer vision. This paper asks whether a simple training-time augmentation can close this gap without additional data or compute.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard instruction-response text pairs (single-turn, up to 512 tokens) and produces a fine-tuned language model that generates free-form text responses. The critical detail is that noise is injected only into the embedding layer during the training forward pass; at inference time the model operates on clean embeddings with no perturbation. The noise vector is sampled per-batch and per-sequence, with the scaling factor computed independently for each sequence when batch members have different lengths.

## Method

NEFTune adds a random noise vector to the token embedding vectors during each training forward pass. Specifically, for a batch of shape B×L×d, noise ε is sampled iid from Uniform(−1,1) and scaled by α/√(Ld), where L is sequence length, d is embedding dimension, and α is a tunable hyperparameter (typically 5–15). This scaling rule, borrowed from the adversarial training literature (FreeLB), yields a noise vector with expected Euclidean magnitude of approximately α/√3. The noised embeddings are passed through the rest of the transformer, and gradients are computed as usual; the embedding layer itself is updated alongside all other parameters. The key insight is that this perturbation acts as a regularizer that prevents the model from collapsing to the exact instruction distribution, reducing overfitting (evidenced by higher training loss, lower test loss, and lower ROUGE-L/BLEU on training prompts) while preserving factual capabilities on standard benchmarks.

## Results

On AlpacaEval (GPT-4 evaluator), NEFTune improves LLaMA-2-7B finetuned on Alpaca from 29.91% to 65.70% win rate, and achieves an average improvement of 15.1 percentage points across four instruction datasets (Alpaca, Evol-Instruct, ShareGPT, OpenPlatypus), raising the average from 57.91% to 73.06%. Gains are also observed at larger scales: LLaMA-2-70B on Evol-Instruct improves from 75.03% to 88.81%, and LLaMA-2-Chat (7B) improves from 74.44% to 81.74%. On the OpenLLM Leaderboard tasks (MMLU, ARC, HellaSwag, TruthfulQA), NEFTune preserves model capabilities with no measurable change in scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline throughout the paper is standard instruction finetuning without noise, which NEFTune consistently outperforms by 8–36 percentage points on AlpacaEval depending on the dataset and model size. The only external method explicitly compared is FreeLB (Zhu et al., 2019), an adversarial perturbation approach that also adds noise to embeddings. On LLaMA-1-7B trained on Evol-Instruct, NEFTune achieves 67.45% AlpacaEval win rate (ChatGPT evaluator) versus 63.48% for FreeLB after hyperparameter tuning, a ~4-point advantage. The paper notes that FreeLB uses gradient-based adversarial perturbations (more computationally expensive) while NEFTune uses simple uniform random noise, making NEFTune both simpler and more effective. NEFTune also outperforms standard regularizers (dropout, weight decay) tested in ablation (Table 13), which show no meaningful improvement over the 48.5% baseline, whereas NEFTune reaches 61.9%.

## Relation to Prior Work

NEFTune repurposes the noise scaling rule (α/√(Ld)) from the adversarial training literature (FreeLB, Zhu et al. 2019; Kong et al. 2022) but replaces the gradient-based adversarial perturbation with simple iid uniform noise, converting an adversarial robustness technique into a non-adversarial regularizer for instruction fine-tuning. It sits within the well-established instruction-tuning pipeline (Alpaca, Evol-Instruct, ShareGPT, OpenPlatypus) and is positioned as a drop-in augmentation to the standard forward pass rather than a change to data, architecture, or objective. The paper explicitly contrasts with FreeLB (Table 9) and with other noisy-input training work in image captioning and NLU, while noting that the LLM community has largely neglected regularization in favor of dataset curation and scaling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A trivially simple, zero-compute augmentation producing a ~35-point AlpacaEval jump (29.8% → 64.7%) on LLaMA-2-7B/Alpaca is practically transformative for anyone doing instruction fine-tuning, and the effect persists across datasets, model families, scales (7B–70B), and even RLHF-tuned checkpoints. The overfitting hypothesis (higher training loss, lower test loss, lower ROUGE/BLEU to training responses) is well-supported but the paper concedes there is no conclusive theoretical explanation for why embedding noise specifically helps. Key limitations include reliance on a single LLM judge (AlpacaEval/GPT-4), limited sweeps at larger scales, and unexamined effects on safety behaviors (toxicity, refusal), leaving open whether the gains generalize beyond conversational quality metrics.
