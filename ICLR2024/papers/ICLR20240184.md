## Problem

DP-FTRL, which adds correlated Gaussian noise to clipped gradients, empirically outperforms DP-SGD (independent noise) by large margins, but no prior theory provably establishes this separation in expected utility beyond a factor in the privacy parameter ρ. Additionally, finding the optimal noise correlation matrix requires solving an O(T³) semi-definite program, making it impractical for large-scale training. The paper asks: can we prove correlated noise is strictly better, and can we find near-optimal correlations in closed form?

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a stream of i.i.d. data points with a loss function and data-independent regularization, under a ρ-zCDP privacy budget, a fixed learning rate η, and a fixed ℓ₂ clip norm G. The only tunable hyperparameter beyond standard ones is the correlation parameter ν ∈ (0,1), which controls the exponential decay rate of the anti-correlated noise weights. The output is the final model θ_T along with analytical asymptotic suboptimality bounds that depend on the effective dimension d_eff = Tr[H]/λ_max(H) rather than the ambient dimension d, and the condition number κ = L/μ.

## Method

The paper analyzes DP-FTRL in the frequency domain using the discrete-time Fourier transform B(ω) of the Toeplitz noise correlation sequence β, showing that the asymptotic suboptimality F_∞ decomposes into a product of ∫|B(ω)|² and ∫|B(ω)|⁻² (the latter being the limiting sensitivity γ_∞). For mean estimation, this product is minimized in closed form, yielding anti-correlated weights β*_t = (-1)^t·C(1/2,t)·(1-η)^t that subtract out previously injected noise. The proposed ν-DP-FTRL generalizes this with a single damping parameter ν, giving weights β_t^ν = (-1)^t·C(1/2,t)·(1-ν)^t, which achieves O(η²ρ⁻¹·log²(1/ν)) suboptimality for linear regression—matching the lower bound up to log factors and scaling with the effective dimension d_eff rather than d. For general strongly convex objectives, F_∞ is bounded by a convex program over a function ψ in the frequency domain, solvable via discretization into a second-order cone program. The anti-correlation mechanism prevents noise accumulation along eigen-directions of the Hessian with small eigenvalues, where the gradient signal is too weak to undo prior noise, yielding an exponential separation from DP-SGD when d_eff ≪ d.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (example-level DP), ν-DP-FTRL achieves 69.26% accuracy at ε=10, nearly matching the state-of-the-art Multi-Epoch (ME) mechanism at 70.83% while requiring O(T) per-step cost instead of O(T²). On StackOverflow (user-level DP), ν-DP-FTRL outperforms ME across all ε by ≈0.3 percentage points and reaches 25.3% validation accuracy at ε=8, only 1 point below the non-private baseline. The method provides an average 3pp improvement over all other anytime mechanisms on CIFAR-10, closing 30–80% of the gap to the computationally expensive ME approach.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DP-SGD (Abadi et al., 2016), ν-DP-FTRL outperforms for ε≥4 on CIFAR-10 (63.02% vs. 62.02%) and around ε≈2 on StackOverflow (23.6% vs. 22.6%), though DP-SGD can surpass it at very small ε due to privacy amplification. Against Honaker/TreeAgg (Kairouz et al., 2021a), ν-DP-FTRL provides a significant margin as both are anytime mechanisms, with ν-DP-FTRL achieving the average 3pp improvement over all efficient baselines. Against Optimal CC (Fichtenberger et al., 2023), which is equivalent to ν-DP-FTRL with ν=0, the exponential damping parameter ν is essential for practical gains. Against the state-of-the-art Multi-Epoch ME mechanism (Choquette-Choo et al., 2023b), ν-DP-FTRL nearly matches on CIFAR-10 (69.26% vs. 70.83% at ε=10) and actually outperforms on StackOverflow by ≈0.3pp, all while reducing per-step cost from O(T²) to O(T) and eliminating the O(T³) matrix generation. Against Full Honaker (Honaker, 2015), ν-DP-FTRL is competitive in accuracy while being dramatically more efficient in both memory (O(T) vs. O(T²)) and compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DP-FTRL framework (Kairouz et al., 2021; Denisov et al., 2022) by replacing the expensive O(T³) semidefinite program for optimizing the noise correlation matrix B with a single-parameter analytical family (ν-DP-FTRL) that is O(1) to generate and O(T) per step. It builds on the Toeplitz structure explored by Choquette-Choo et al. (2023b) and recovers the optimal correlations of Fichtenberger et al. (2023) as a special case (ν=0), while adding an exponential damping term (1−ν)^t that is essential for finite asymptotic suboptimality. The key conceptual departure is analyzing the final learning objective F(θ_T) rather than the intermediate squared-error proxy φ(B) used in prior work, which the authors show can be misleading (equal φ does not guarantee convergence).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first provable separation between DP-FTRL and DP-SGD in terms of the effective dimension d_eff (not just the privacy parameter ρ), showing an exponential gap when the Hessian is nearly low-rank — a regime common in overparameterized models. The O(1) analytical formula for noise correlations eliminates the 24-hour SDP computation for T=10⁴, making correlated-noise DP practical at scale. Limitations include asymptotic (T→∞) theory with looser finite-time bounds, experiments on only two moderate-scale benchmarks (CIFAR-10, StackOverflow), and the open question of combining adaptive clipping strategies with correlated noise. The convex-program characterization for general strongly convex functions (Theorem 3.1) suggests further optimization beyond the single-parameter ν family is possible.
