## Problem

Pooling medical imaging datasets from multiple sites requires controlling for nuisance covariates (scanner, site, age, sex, genotype) whose distributions shift and become imbalanced across sources. Existing invariant/equivariant representation learning methods are limited to handling at most two covariates simultaneously, necessitating complicated multi-stage training pipelines that do not scale to the many covariates present in real multi-site studies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242235/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes brain MRI scans (T1-weighted, from ADNI and ADCP) together with per-participant covariates (age, sex, scanner type, site, APOE A1/A2 genotype, years of education) and produces a latent representation that is invariant to categorical nuisances (scanner, site) and equivariant to ordinal/continuous ones (age, genotype). Beyond classification, the learned morphisms enable generating hypothetical images by composing linear transformations in latent space (e.g., 'what would this scan look like if the participant were 10 years older?') and answering counterfactual disease-probability queries without retraining.

## Method

The paper formulates data pooling as learning a Functor F from a source Category (images with covariate-defined morphisms) to a target Category (latent vectors with linear morphisms W ∈ ℝ^{n×n}), where the functor axioms directly yield the training constraints. The loss combines three terms: an autoencoder reconstruction loss (F⁻¹∘F = id), a cross-entropy prediction loss (C∘F), and a structure-preserving term that enforces W^d · F(s₁) ≈ F(s₂) for every pair of samples whose covariate difference is d. This is trained in a single stage with a modified ResNet encoder and fully-connected classifier, in contrast to the two-stage pipeline of the GE baseline, and scales to arbitrary numbers of covariates by simply adding more W matrices. The category-theoretic framing unifies CycleGAN (fully-faithful functor with cycle consistency), SimCLR (invariant functor), and group equivariance as special cases, while the orthogonality constraint on W ensures well-defined inverse morphisms for free.

## Results

On ADNI, the proposed method (invariance only) achieves 81% accuracy with an MMD of 02×10², reducing scanner-related distributional shift by over 80% compared to the best baseline GE (MMD 16×10²) while improving accuracy by 4 points. On ADCP, the method reaches 86% accuracy with MMD 34×10², halving the MMD of GE (70×10²) and outperforming all baselines on both metrics. When extended to equivariance over 5 covariates simultaneously, accuracy remains competitive (80% on ADNI) with MMD at 11×10², demonstrating that the category-theoretic formulation scales to arbitrary numbers of nuisance variables without multi-stage training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest prior method GE (Lokhande et al., 2022), which uses a two-stage group-equivariant pipeline limited to two covariates, the proposed method achieves substantially lower MMD (02 vs. 16 on ADNI; 34 vs. 70 on ADCP) while also improving accuracy (81 vs. 77 on ADNI; 86 vs. 81 on ADCP). Compared to MMD-based invariance (Li et al., 2014), the method reduces MMD by over 90% on ADNI (02 vs. 27) and 60% on ADCP (34 vs. 86) with equal or higher accuracy. CAI (Xie et al., 2017) and SS (Zhou et al., 2017) both fail to reduce MMD below 26 on ADNI and below 85 on ADCP, and CAI drops accuracy to 74% on ADNI. RM (Motiian et al., 2017) achieves the second-best MMD among baselines (22 on ADNI, 77 on ADCP) but still trails the proposed method by 10× and 2.3× respectively. A key practical advantage is that the proposed method handles up to 5 covariates in a single training stage, whereas GE requires a two-stage pipeline and cannot scale beyond two covariates without architectural changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly generalizes the two-stage equivariant representation learning framework of Lokhande et al. (CVPR 2022), which could handle at most one categorical and one continuous covariate, by replacing group-theoretic constraints with a category-theoretic functor formulation that accommodates an arbitrary number of heterogeneous covariates in a single training stage. It also reinterprets CycleGAN and SimCLR as special cases of functor constraints (fully-faithful vs. invariant functors), positioning these seemingly distinct methods within a unified algebraic framework. The broader lineage includes invariant representation learning for domain adaptation (Li et al., 2014; Moyer et al., 2018; 2020) and adversarial invariance (Xie et al., 2017), but the key departure is that the structure-preserving loss is derived directly from functor composition rather than hand-crafted per covariate pair.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical contribution is that multi-site brain imaging data can be harmonized for an arbitrary number of nuisance covariates (scanner, site, age, sex, APOE genotype) without the multi-stage pipelines or exponential parameter growth of prior methods, with MMD reduced by over 80% while maintaining or improving accuracy. A notable limitation is that the method cannot leverage U-Net-style architectures because skip connections bypass the latent-space constraints, restricting it to encoder-decoder or ResNet-style backbones. The broader open question is whether the category-theoretic formalism, which unifies self-supervised learning, image translation, and 3D reconstruction synchronization under one umbrella, can inform more complex architectural designs beyond simple linear morphisms, and whether the approach scales to high-dimensional continuous covariates without discretization.
