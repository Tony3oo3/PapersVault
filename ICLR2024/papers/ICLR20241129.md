## Problem

While incorporating expert demonstrations empirically improves RL sample efficiency, no prior work provided theoretical guarantees quantifying how much this extra information reduces sample complexity. This paper fills that gap by establishing the first provable sample complexity rates for best policy identification (BPI) with demonstrations and for preference-based BPI with demonstrations (RLHF) in finite and linear MDPs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N^E reward-free expert trajectories as offline demonstrations, then interacts with the environment online: either collecting transitions with rewards (BPI setting) or collecting pairwise trajectory preferences under a Bradley-Terry-Luce model (RLHF setting). It outputs a near-optimal policy for the original unregularized MDP, with the behavior cloning policy serving as an intermediate representation of the prior. In the RLHF variant, the agent also selects a sampling policy (the behavior cloning policy) to generate the offline preference dataset before entering the reward-free online phase.

## Method

The core approach reduces BPI with demonstrations to regularized BPI in two stages: first, a behavior cloning policy π^BC is learned via maximum likelihood estimation from the expert demonstrations; second, the agent solves a trajectory-KL-divergence-regularized MDP that penalizes deviation from π^BC, using the solution as an estimate of the optimal policy. To make this efficient, the paper introduces UCBVI-Ent+ (finite MDPs) and LSVI-UCB-Ent (linear MDPs), novel UCB-based algorithms for regularized BPI that achieve Õ(H⁵S²A/(λε)) and Õ(H⁵d²/(λε)) sample complexity respectively, improving over prior UCBVI-Ent rates. For RLHF, the agent collects preferences offline using π^BC, estimates the reward via MLE under a parametric function class, and applies the same regularized BPI solvers—avoiding the pessimism injection required by prior offline RLHF methods. The resulting end-to-end sample complexity is Õ(Poly(S,A,H)/(ε²N^E)) in finite and Õ(Poly(d,H)/(ε²N^E)) in linear MDPs, showing that demonstrations provide a provable 1/N^E acceleration over the no-demonstration baseline.

## Results

The paper establishes that N^E expert demonstrations reduce the sample complexity of best policy identification (BPI) from Õ(Poly(S,A,H)/ε²) in finite MDPs to Õ(H⁶S³A²/(N^E·ε²)), and from Õ(Poly(d,H)/ε²) to Õ(H⁶d³/(N^E·ε²)) in linear MDPs. The new UCBVI-Ent+ algorithm achieves Õ(H⁵S²A/(λε)) for regularized BPI in finite MDPs, improving upon the prior Õ(H⁸S⁴A/(λε)) bound of Tiapkin et al. (2023). Behavior cloning converges at rate Õ(SAH·log²(N^E)/N^E) in trajectory KL-divergence for finite MDPs and Õ(dH·log²(N^E)/N^E) for linear MDPs. The same Õ(H⁶S³A²/(N^E·ε²)) / Õ(H⁶d³/(N^E·ε²)) rates extend to RLHF with demonstrations, matching the RL-with-demonstrations rates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main theoretical comparison is against BPI without demonstrations, which requires Õ(Poly(S,A,H)/ε²) episodes in finite MDPs (Kaufmann et al., 2021; Ménard et al., 2021) and Õ(Poly(d,H)/ε²) in linear MDPs (Taupin et al., 2023). Demonstration-regularized RL achieves a strict 1/N^E improvement, showing that each expert demonstration linearly reduces the number of online episodes needed. Against the prior UCBVI-Ent algorithm (Tiapkin et al., 2023), the new UCBVI-Ent+ improves the regularized BPI rate from Õ(H⁸S⁴A/(λε)) to Õ(H⁵S²A/(λε)), reducing both the horizon and state-space dependence. For the linear setting, LSVI-UCB-Ent is the first algorithm to achieve fast rates for exploration in regularized linear MDPs. In the RLHF setting, the method matches the sample complexity of RL with demonstrations, confirming that preference-based feedback does not add asymptotic difficulty when demonstrations are available, and avoids the pessimism injection required by prior offline RLHF methods (Zhan et al., 2023a).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first theoretical analysis of demonstration-regularized RL, a widely used practical technique (Rajeswaran et al., 2018; Nair et al., 2018) that had previously lacked sample complexity guarantees. It builds directly on the KL-regularized MDP framework of Neu et al. (2017) and Vieillard et al. (2020), and improves upon the UCBVI-Ent algorithm of Tiapkin et al. (2023) by introducing UCBVI-Ent+ with a more favorable O(1/(ελ)) rate versus the prior O(1/ε²). The paper also extends the BPI literature (Kaufmann et al., 2021; Taupin et al., 2023) to the setting with expert demonstrations, and introduces a novel preference-based BPI with demonstration setting that bridges offline preference collection with online reward-free exploration, mirroring the practical RLHF pipeline used in LLM fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that demonstration-regularized RL is provably efficient, with sample complexity improving by a factor of N^E over BPI without demonstrations, and that RLHF with demonstrations is no harder than standard RL with demonstrations—a conceptual result that validates the practical three-phase LLM fine-tuning pipeline (SFT → reward estimation → RL). The avoidance of pessimism injection in favor of KL-regularization is a notable technical departure from prior offline RLHF work (Zhan et al., 2023a; Zhu et al., 2023). Open questions include extending the preference model from pairwise comparisons to rankings and relaxing the white-box preference model assumption, which would bring the theory closer to real-world RLHF deployments where the link function is unknown.
