## Problem

Existing Distilled Feature Field (DFF) methods for manipulation either require dense multi-view camera captures (NeRF-style reconstruction) or rely on single-view features that are insufficient for dexterous manipulation. Moreover, prior DFF-based approaches target simple parallel-gripper grasps of rigid objects, leaving one-shot dexterous manipulation with 24-DoF hands across varying poses, deformations, and object categories largely unaddressed. The paper identifies that the core bottleneck is not visual information or model capacity but the local consistency of 3D features across views.

## Input / Output

The method takes sparse RGBD scans from four fixed Azure Kinect DK sensors (no dense view sweep) and a single manually configured demonstration of a 24-DoF Shadow Dexterous Hand pose on a source object scan. It produces a view-consistent 3D DFF (a continuous feature field over the scene) and, given a target scene, outputs optimized hand joint parameters (22 DoFs after fixing 2 wrist DoFs) via ~300 iterations of energy minimization (~20 s on an RTX 3090). The feature refinement network is trained once for 20,000 iterations (~300 s) on the source scene and then applied unchanged to all target scenes without fine-tuning.

## Method

SparseDFF first back-projects DINOv2 image features onto a merged 3D point cloud from K sparse views, then propagates them to arbitrary query points via inverse-distance-weighted interpolation (analogous to 3D Gaussian Splatting). A lightweight per-point MLP (the feature refinement network) is trained with a contrastive loss that pulls features of spatially overlapping points from different views together while pushing apart non-corresponding points, enforcing local multiview consistency. A Hough-voting-style point-pruning step then removes the bottom 20% of points whose neighborhood features show the least consensus, further improving continuity. For manipulation transfer, query points sampled on the hand surface (denser on fingers) are evaluated through the 3D DFF in both source and target scenes, and the L1 feature disparity is minimized as an energy function over hand joint parameters, augmented with inter-penetration, self-penetration, and pose-limit constraints to ensure physical feasibility.

## Results

The primary metric is grasping success rate (out of 10 real-world trials per configuration). On rigid objects, SparseDFF achieves 100% success on same-object and same-category transfers (Box1→Box1, Drill→Drill, Bowl1→Bowl1/Bowl2) and 60–90% on cross-category transfers (Bowl1→Mugs: 40–80%, BeerBarrel: 90%), while the naive DFF baseline drops to 0% on most cross-category settings. On deformable objects, SparseDFF reaches 100% on same-object and in-context grasps (Monkey, MonkeyScene) and 50–90% on cross-object transfers, versus 0–40% for the DFF baseline. Feature refinement and point pruning ablations confirm both components are necessary for stable optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SparseDFF is compared against two baselines: (1) a naive DFF that directly back-projects DINO features onto the point cloud following Peng et al. (2023), and (2) UniDexGrasp++ (Wan et al., 2023), a state-based dexterous grasping policy evaluated in simulation. Against the DFF baseline, SparseDFF wins decisively on every cross-category and cross-geometry transfer: 100% vs. 0% on Box1→Box2, 60% vs. 30% on Bowl1→CatBowl, and 80% vs. 0% on Bowl1→Mug. The DFF baseline also fails completely on deformable cross-object transfers (0% on BigBear→SmallBear), while SparseDFF achieves 50%. Against UniDexGrasp++, SparseDFF outperforms by large margins across all rigid-object settings (e.g., 100% vs. 7.7% on Box1, 90% vs. 6.2% on BeerBarrel), though UniDexGrasp++ is only evaluated in simulation and not on deformable objects. The consistent pattern is that both baselines degrade sharply as source and target objects diverge in geometry or category, whereas SparseDFF maintains high success rates thanks to its feature refinement network and point-pruning mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

SparseDFF extends the Distilled Feature Field (DFF) paradigm introduced by Kobayashi et al. (2022) by replacing the NeRF-based continuous field reconstruction with a point-cloud-based approach inspired by 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023), where features are first distilled onto discrete 3D points and then propagated via inverse-distance-weighted interpolation. The key departure from prior DFF-based manipulation work (Shen et al., Rashid et al., Ze et al.) is twofold: it operates from sparse RGBD views rather than dense multi-view captures, and it targets 24-DoF dexterous hands rather than parallel grippers. The paper's central conceptual contribution is reframing the bottleneck as local feature consistency rather than visual information or model capacity, addressed through a lightweight contrastive-trained MLP and a Hough-voting-style point pruning mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

One-shot dexterous manipulation with cross-category generalization (e.g., bowl grasp transferred to mugs) is a practically significant capability that prior methods requiring large datasets or dense views could not achieve. The method's reliance on a single demonstration and sparse fixed-camera setup makes it applicable to real-world deployments where extensive data collection is infeasible. However, limitations remain: the feature refinement network must be trained per source scene (~300 s), experiments are confined to tabletop settings with a fixed arm, and the 10-trial evaluation protocol limits statistical confidence. Open questions include whether the approach scales to dynamic or partially observable scenes, whether tactile or force feedback could further improve precision, and whether the contrastive consistency objective generalizes beyond the specific DINO backbone used.
