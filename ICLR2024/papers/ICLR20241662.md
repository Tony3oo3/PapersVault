## Problem

The WL-dimension of subgraph counting and induced subgraph counting had been studied separately with distinct techniques, leaving open whether a unified characterization exists and leaving specific gaps (e.g., whether patterns with k+2 or more nodes can have induced subgraph WL-dimension k for k>1). This paper resolves these questions by showing both problems are special cases of a single framework—graph motif parameters—and provides a complete characterization of the WL-dimension for all such parameters over labeled graphs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled graph (with node labels from a finite alphabet Σ and edge labels from a finite alphabet Δ) and a graph motif parameter Γ, defined as a finite linear combination of homomorphism counts from a fixed support set of labeled graphs. It produces the WL-dimension of Γ (the minimum k such that kWL distinguishes graphs with different Γ-values), and when kWL can distinguish Γ, a uniform function θ_Γ mapping individual k-tuple colors to rationals whose sum over all k-tuples yields the exact count Γ(G). The characterization reduces the problem to computing the maximum treewidth over the support set, making the output a single integer.

## Method

The central result (Theorem 2) states that the WL-dimension of a labeled graph motif parameter Γ equals the maximum treewidth of any labeled graph in its support Supp(Γ). The proof combines three ingredients: (1) extending Neuen's homomorphism-distinguishing closedness for bounded-treewidth graphs to the labeled setting by proving a labeled version of Roberson's oddomorphism theorem via CFI constructions; (2) Seppelt's lemma linking homomorphism indistinguishability to linear combinations of homomorphism counts; and (3) the Dvorák–Dell equivalence between kWL indistinguishability and agreement on all homomorphism counts from graphs of treewidth at most k. As a consequence, the paper shows the motif count can be recovered by summing a uniform function over individual k-tuple colors (Theorem 6), and provides a polynomial-time algorithm for the subgraph counting WL-dimension by reducing the problem to MSO model checking over a finite set of forbidden minors (Robertson–Seymour theorem).

## Results

The paper's central result (Theorem 2) characterizes the WL-dimension of any labeled graph motif parameter Γ as the maximum treewidth among all labeled graphs in its support set. As immediate corollaries: (i) for subgraph counting of pattern H, the WL-dimension equals the hereditary treewidth of H (extending Neuen's unlabeled result to labeled graphs); (ii) for induced subgraph counting of pattern H with k+1 nodes, the WL-dimension is exactly k, resolving an open question from Chen et al. (2020); (iii) counting k-graphlets has WL-dimension k−1 (Proposition 9). The paper further shows (Theorem 6) that when kWL can distinguish a motif parameter, the exact count is computable as a uniform sum over individual k-tuple colors, and provides a polynomial-time algorithm (Theorem 8) for deciding the WL-dimension of subgraph counting for any fixed k.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. Instead, the paper's contributions are framed as extensions and unifications of prior theoretical results. For subgraph counting, the paper extends Neuen (2023)'s characterization (hereditary treewidth = WL-dimension) from unlabeled to labeled graphs, and provides the polynomial-time recognition algorithm that Arvind et al. (2020) left open even for k=2. For induced subgraph counting, the paper resolves the open question from Chen et al. (2020) by showing the WL-dimension is exactly |V(P)|−1 for all patterns, confirming their conjectured upper bound is tight. The unifying framework via graph motif parameters (Curticapean et al., 2017) connects both counting problems as special cases of a single characterization, whereas prior work treated them separately with distinct techniques. The proof machinery builds on Roberson (2022)'s oddomorphism theory and Seppelt (2023)'s homomorphism-distinguishing closure results, both extended here to the labeled setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of research—subgraph counting (Arvind et al. 2020, Neuen 2023) and induced subgraph counting (Chen et al. 2020)—under the general framework of graph motif parameters (Curticapean et al. 2017), showing they are linear combinations of one another via the Lovász theorem. The core technical machinery extends Roberson's (2022) oddomorphism theory and Seppelt's (2023) homomorphism-indistinguishability lemma to the labeled graph setting, building on Neuen's (2023) proof that bounded-treewidth classes are homomorphism-distinguishing closed. The paper also adapts Dvorák's (2010) quantum graph argument to establish the labeled analogue of the kWL–homomorphism-count equivalence, and leverages Courcelle's MSO model-checking metatheorem together with the Robertson-Seymour graph minor theorem to obtain a polynomial-time recognition algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean characterization (WL-dimension = max treewidth in the support) resolves the open question from Chen et al. (2020) on induced subgraph counting and provides a single, unified criterion for all graph motif parameters, eliminating the need for separate analyses. The local computability result (Theorem 6) has direct practical implications for GNN architecture design: it shows that a suitable readout function over individual k-tuple colors suffices to recover exact motif counts, without requiring global pooling over the full color multiset. The main limitation acknowledged by the authors is that the counterexamples witnessing WL-test failure are worst-case constructions that may not reflect typical graph distributions, leaving average-case behavior and practical GNN performance open.
