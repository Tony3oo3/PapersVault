## Problem

Prior LLM watermarking methods, such as the green/red-list approach of Kirchenbauer et al., necessarily bias the output token distribution, creating a trade-off between watermark detectability and text quality. This paper challenges that trade-off by proving the existence of watermarking schemes that leave the output probability distribution exactly unchanged, making the watermark strictly undetectable to users who lack the secret key.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241773/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During generation, the method takes a text prompt, a 1024-bit secret key, and the LLM's per-step probability distribution over the vocabulary; it produces a watermarked token sequence by reweighting each step's distribution. During detection, it takes the candidate text, the secret key, and the LLM's logits (or an approximation thereof, e.g., with an empty prompt), and outputs a per-token score and a cumulative score with a theoretical upper bound on type I error. The context code is derived from the most recent five generated tokens, and a context-code history is maintained to guarantee independence of watermark codes across tokens.

## Method

The core mechanism is an unbiased reweighting function R_E that maps the original token distribution P to a new distribution Q such that E_E[Q] = P, ensuring the marginal output distribution is preserved. Two concrete instantiations are proposed: δ-reweight, which uses inverse-transform sampling to collapse the distribution to a single delta token per watermark code, and γ-reweight, which randomly permutes the vocabulary, rejects the left half of the cumulative probability mass, and doubles the right half. Independence across autoregressive steps is enforced via a context-code history that skips reweighting when a context code repeats, yielding n-shot-undetectability. For detection, the paper derives a maximin variant of the log-likelihood-ratio score that is robust to total-variation perturbations in the watermarked distribution (e.g., from editing), with a provable type-I-error bound via a Chernoff-style tail inequality.

## Results

On text summarization (CNN-DM, BART-large), both δ-reweight and γ-reweight match the no-watermark baseline across all metrics (e.g., BERTScore 32.71 vs. 32.70, ROUGE-1 38.57 vs. 38.56, Perplexity 5.022 vs. 5.024). On machine translation (WMT'14 En-Ro, MBart), the unbiased methods similarly preserve quality (BERTScore 56.3/56.2 vs. 55.9, BLEU 21.7/21.8 vs. 21.8). In contrast, the soft-red-list baseline degrades substantially as watermark strength increases: at δ=2.0, BERTScore drops to 31.21 (TS) and 53.8 (MT), and BLEU falls to 19.5, while Perplexity rises to 6.253.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its two unbiased reweighting methods (δ-reweight and γ-reweight) against the soft-red-list watermarking method from Kirchenbauer et al. (2023), which is the only prior LLM watermarking baseline. The key finding is that both unbiased methods are statistically indistinguishable from the no-watermark baseline on every metric and task, confirming the theoretical guarantee of zero quality degradation. The soft-red-list baseline, by contrast, shows a clear quality–strength trade-off: at δ=0 it matches no-watermark (as expected, since no watermark is applied), but at δ=1.0 it already loses ~0.35 BERTScore points on TS and ~0.8 BLEU on MT, and at δ=2.0 the degradation is severe (−1.49 BERTScore on TS, −2.3 BLEU on MT, +1.23 Perplexity). Thus, the proposed methods strictly dominate the baseline in the sense that they achieve detectable watermarks (with p-values as low as 1.2×10⁻³² in the OPT-6.7B example) while the baseline cannot achieve any watermark strength without sacrificing output quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Kirchenbauer et al. (2023), which introduced the green-list/red-list paradigm for watermarking LLM outputs and demonstrated a trade-off between watermark strength and text quality. The key departure is reframing the problem through the lens of distribution reweighting: rather than adjusting logits with a fixed bias (as in soft-red-list), the authors prove that any reweighting function whose expectation over the watermark code recovers the original distribution yields a provably unbiased watermark. This connects to Aaronson's (2022) sketch of cryptographic pseudorandom functions for watermarking and to concurrent work by Christ et al. (2023) on undetectable watermarks, but provides a stronger guarantee (strict n-shot-undetectability) without relying on computational hardness assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By proving that watermark strength and output quality need not be in tension, this work removes the primary practical objection to deploying watermarks at scale in LLM services. The n-shot-undetectability guarantee means users cannot statistically distinguish watermarked from unwatermarked outputs, which has significant implications for consent and transparency that the authors themselves flag as ethically challenging. Open questions include robustness to paraphrasing and editing attacks (the maximin variant helps but is not attack-specific), the practical difficulty of obtaining accurate model logits at detection time, and whether the context-code-history requirement is feasible in high-throughput production settings.
