## Problem

Prior work (Joshi et al., 2023) showed that 1-hop invariant GNNs underperform equivariant GNNs due to inability to capture global geometry, but no theoretical result existed for the k-hop setting. Additionally, while empirical studies suggested that increasing the steerable feature type L improves equivariant GNN performance, these comparisons did not control for feature dimension, leaving unclear whether higher-type features genuinely carry more information or simply more parameters.

## Input / Output

The method operates on geometric graphs where each node carries a feature embedding and a 3D spatial coordinate. For the synthetic k-chain task, inputs are chains of k+2 nodes with uniform edge distances and binary labels determined by terminal node orientation; outputs are classification accuracies. For molecular tasks (OC20 IS2RE and S2EF), inputs are molecular structures and outputs are adsorption energy (IS2RE) or per-atom forces plus energy (S2EF), evaluated via MAE, cosine similarity, and energy-within-threshold metrics. A key experimental design choice is fixing the total steerable feature dimension while varying L, enabling a controlled comparison of information capacity across feature types.

## Method

The core contribution is Lemma 1, which establishes that any G-equivariant steerable feature f(X) of dimension d can be uniquely decomposed as f(X) = ρ(g_X)·λ(X), where g_X is a group element and λ(X) is a d-dimensional invariant feature. This reduction allows the authors to analyze steerable message-passing purely through invariant features, revealing that k-hop invariant GNNs (Theorem 1) inevitably discard the relative geometry between local neighborhoods, while equivariant GNNs on faithful representations (Theorem 2) retain it. For the L-dependence question, Theorem 3 and Corollary 1 show that when the spatial embedding has full rank (rank 3), the space of equivariant functions into any type-l steerable space is in bijection with (2l+1)-dimensional invariant functions—meaning the information capacity depends only on total dimension, not on the highest type L used. Theorem 4 then proves that on fully connected graphs, two GNNs operating on same-dimension steerable spaces (regardless of irreducible decomposition) can be parameterized to learn identical invariant features. Empirical validation on k-chain, IS2RE, and S2EF confirms that invariant models fail on k-chain regardless of depth, and that at fixed feature dimension, lower-L models (e.g., L=2) match or exceed higher-L models (e.g., L=6) in molecular property prediction.

## Results

On the synthetic k-chain task, all L=0 invariant GNNs (SchNet, DimeNet++, SphereNet, ComENet) achieve ~50% accuracy (chance level) regardless of depth, confirming the theoretical prediction that k-hop invariant GNNs cannot capture geometry between local structures. In contrast, L=1 equivariant GNNs (EGNN, GVP, ClofNet, MACE) reach 90–100% accuracy with sufficient depth. On the IS2RE ablation with fixed steerable feature dimension (~1850), eSCN with L=2 (c=206) achieves 842±13 meV energy MAE, outperforming L=4 (929±15 meV) and L=6 (872±6 meV), supporting the claim that higher-type steerable features do not improve performance when dimension is held constant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central empirical finding is that all L=0 invariant GNNs (SchNet, DimeNet++, SphereNet, ComENet) fail the k-chain task at chance level (~50%) regardless of depth, directly validating Theorem 1's prediction that k-hop invariant GNNs cannot distinguish k-hop identical but globally distinct graphs. L=1 equivariant GNNs (EGNN, GVP, ClofNet, MACE) succeed with sufficient depth (reaching 90–100%), confirming Theorem 2's claim that faithful representations enable capture of inter-neighborhood geometry. On the IS2RE ablation with fixed feature dimension, eSCN L=2 (842 meV MAE) outperforms eSCN L=4 (929 meV) and L=6 (872 meV), and EquiformerV2 L=2 (841 meV) matches L=4 (832 meV), supporting the paper's claim that increasing L at constant dimension does not improve performance. On S2EF, eSCN with L=2 and c=824 (246 meV energy MAE) outperforms L=6 with c=256 (294 meV) on energy, though L=6 achieves slightly better force MAE (21.3 vs. 23.1 meV/Å). The quasi-equivariant models eSCN and EquiformerV2 show intermediate performance on k-chain (60–96%), attributed to equivariance errors, while strictly equivariant models (EGNN, GVP, MACE) show the sharpest depth-dependent transitions from 50% to 100%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GWL/IGWL expressive-power framework of Joshi et al. [20] (ICML 2023) from 1-hop to k-hop message passing, and introduces a key lemma (Lemma 1) that decomposes any steerable feature into a group element plus invariant features, enabling a unified analysis of invariant and equivariant GNNs. It departs from the prevailing empirical narrative that higher-type steerable features (larger L) improve performance by showing that, when feature dimension is held constant, the information-carrying capacity is determined solely by dimension, not by the irreducible type decomposition. The work also connects to the completeness-of-edge-attributes line (ComENet, SphereNet) by explaining why those methods partially succeed despite using only invariant features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that steerable feature type L is irrelevant to information capacity (at fixed dimension and full-rank input) directly challenges the common design heuristic of scaling L in equivariant GNNs, suggesting practitioners should invest in dimension rather than higher-order representations. Open questions remain: whether the equivalence of Theorem 4 extends to non-fully-connected graphs, whether universality (the ability of a given architecture to approximate all equivariant functions) differs across representations of the same dimension, and whether encoding global features beyond k-hop neighborhoods can close the expressiveness gap for invariant GNNs. The lack of regularity in the group-element map g_X also limits the practical implementability of the theoretical correspondence.
