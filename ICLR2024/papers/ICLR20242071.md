## Problem

3D generative models trained on limited text-3D data (even millions of pairs) collapse into stereotype concepts for given prompts, losing the open-vocabulary diversity that V-L pre-trained models offer in 2D. The key challenge is how to map a small 3D dataset onto the broad semantic range of pre-trained text embeddings so that a single prompt like 'a chair' can generate diverse, reasonable outputs rather than a fixed shape.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242071/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts either a free-form text prompt (encoded via frozen CLIP ViT-B/32) or a single reference RGB image at 512×512 with an associated camera pose, and produces a textured 3D mesh represented as DMTet with a texture field. For image-to-3D, the input image is from an arbitrary viewpoint and the model must generate a consistent 3D object whose novel views match the input. Point clouds of 8,192 points are sampled from generated meshes for 3D discrimination.

## Method

The core mechanism is injecting dynamic Gaussian noise into CLIP text embeddings to form Noisy Text Fields (NTFs), where the noise scale σ = f_txt(t) is learned per-prompt and bounded in (0.0002, 0.016), effectively expanding the text latent range to cover the one-to-many text-to-3D correspondence. An NTFGen module trains this noisy field via contrastive alignment (NCE losses on both generated and ground-truth rendered images), while a late-concatenation design bypasses mapping networks to preserve text control over generation. For image-to-3D, an NTFBind module learns view-invariant image latent codes conditioned on camera poses and binds them to NTFs, ensuring multi-view consistency. The generator (GET3D's DMTet + texture field) is supervised by a multi-modal GAN framework: a text-2.5D discriminator (rendered image + camera pose + noisy text) guides texture, and a text-3D discriminator (PointNet over sampled point clouds + text) guides geometry, with StyleGAN-style architectures and R1 regularization.

## Results

On the COCO evaluation set (153 prompts), TextField3D achieves a CLIP R-Precision of 45.8 (ViT-B/32) and 49.0 (ViT-L/14) in a single forward pass (~6.9 V100-sec), outperforming Point-E (41.1/46.8) and Shap-E (41.1/46.4) despite using only ~175K training samples versus their >1M. With 9-shot selection, TextField3D reaches 63.4/67.3, approaching the SDS-based DreamFusion (75.1/79.1) and DreamFields (78.6/82.9) while being orders of magnitude faster (seconds vs. hours). On the harder ThreeStudio set (415 prompts), TextField3D (1-shot) scores 19.04/24.33 versus Shap-E's 13.98/19.51.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TextField3D (1-shot) outperforms both Point-E and Shap-E by 4.7 and 4.7 points on ViT-B/32 CLIP R-Precision, respectively, while using roughly 6× less training data (~175K vs. >1M) and generating ~10× faster (6.9 sec vs. ~60 sec). The 9-shot variant narrows the gap to SDS-based methods (DreamFusion, DreamFields) to within ~12 points on ViT-B/32, but at a fraction of the inference cost (seconds vs. hours). On the more challenging ThreeStudio benchmark, TextField3D (1-shot) beats Shap-E by 5.06 points (ViT-B/32) and 4.82 points (ViT-L/14). In the ShapeNet ablation (Table 2), the full TextField3D pipeline (solution d) achieves the best FID of 26.94 and CLIP-score of 30.35 (ViT-B/32, BLIP-2 captions), improving over the GET3D baseline (FID 41.66) and the TAPS3D fine-tuning approach (FID 35.12). Qualitatively, TextField3D handles compound and adjectival prompts (e.g., 'a brick chimney', 'a green teapot') more faithfully than Shap-E, which fails on simple attribute binding and produces artifacts like extra legs or missing textures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GET3D's DMTet + texture field architecture as its base generator, and adapts the noise-injection technique from text-only training literature (Nukrai et al., 2022; Gu et al., 2022) to the 3D generation setting. It departs from prior 3D supervised methods (SDFusion, TAPS3D, ClipForge) that use clean, deterministic text embeddings, arguing that these lead to 'stereotype concepts' where a single prompt maps to a fixed shape. The key conceptual move is treating the text-to-3D mapping as inherently one-to-many and encoding this uncertainty as dynamic noise in the text latent space (Noisy Text Fields), combined with a multi-modal GAN supervision strategy (text-3D + text-2.5D discriminators) that decouples geometry and texture guidance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses the fundamental data bottleneck in 3D generation: achieving open-vocabulary capability with ~175K training objects versus the >1M required by Point-E and Shap-E, while maintaining real-time inference (~7 seconds). The noise-injection mechanism is a simple, transferable idea that could benefit other conditional generation tasks facing modality imbalance. However, the method still depends on the vocabulary coverage of its training data and fails on action/scene-level prompts (e.g., 'a corgi reading a book'), leaving a gap to true open-vocabulary understanding. An open question is whether the NTF approach scales gracefully as 3D datasets grow, or whether it becomes redundant once data volume approaches V-L pre-training scale.
