## Problem

Existing LLM-based robotics planning methods require a pre-defined skill library (e.g., pick, place, push) that must be hand-engineered or trained a-priori, limiting applicability to contact-rich manipulation tasks where such skills are unavailable or insufficiently expressive. Meanwhile, end-to-end RL can learn complex control but struggles with long-horizon tasks due to the exploration problem. The paper asks whether LLM internet-scale knowledge can guide RL to learn low-level control online without any pre-determined skill set.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language task description (e.g., 'The silver nut goes on the silver peg'), two calibrated RGB-D global camera views for scene understanding, and an RGB wrist camera (84×84) for the RL policy. The LLM outputs a structured plan of (target region label, termination condition) pairs; the Sequencing Module converts these into target joint-space configurations via segmentation, depth projection, and inverse kinematics; and the RL policy outputs continuous end-effector or joint control actions. The system operates entirely from visual input at test time, with no privileged simulator state.

## Method

PSL decomposes long-horizon robotics into three modules: (1) a Planning Module where GPT-4 (temperature 0) generates a zero-shot sequence of (region, termination condition) pairs from the task description; (2) a Sequencing Module that uses SAM segmentation on RGB-D views to estimate the 3D target position, computes a target joint configuration via inverse kinematics, and navigates the robot there using AIT* sampling-based motion planning with point-cloud collision checking; (3) a Learning Module that trains a single shared DRQ-v2 policy on local wrist-camera observations to perform contact-rich interaction at each stage. Three key design choices improve learning: a single policy shared across all stages (avoiding per-stage reward engineering), stage termination conditions evaluated at every timestep as a curriculum mechanism (preventing dithering), and local observations that decouple the RL policy from global scene state, enabling efficient learning and straightforward chaining of pre-trained policies.

## Results

PSL achieves over 85% success rate across 25+ long-horizon robotics tasks spanning four benchmark suites (Robosuite, Kitchen, Meta-World, Obstructed Suite), using only visual input. On multi-stage tasks (3–10 stages), PSL reaches 95% average success rate, compared to 38% for TAMP and 37% for SayCan. On the challenging contact-rich NutAssembly task (4 stages), PSL achieves 96% success versus 23% for SayCan and 20% for TAMP. On two-stage Robosuite tasks, PSL achieves 97–100% success across all six tasks, while end-to-end RL (E2E) and hierarchical RL (RAPS) achieve 0–52% and 0% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PSL decisively outperforms all five baselines on long-horizon tasks. Against E2E (DRQ-v2) and RAPS, PSL wins by a wide margin: both end-to-end and hierarchical RL methods achieve 0% on all multi-stage Robosuite and Kitchen tasks (beyond 3 stages), while PSL reaches 85–100%. Against the classical TAMP baseline (PDDLStream), PSL wins on all contact-rich and multi-stage tasks (e.g., 96% vs. 20% on NutAssembly, 90% vs. 72% on CanBread), though TAMP matches PSL at 100% on the simpler K-MS-3 kitchen task. Against SayCan (LLM planning with pre-defined pick/place skills), PSL wins on all contact-intensive tasks by over 70% (96% vs. 23% on NutAssembly) and on all tasks beyond 3 stages where SayCan drops to 0%. Against MoPA-RL, PSL achieves comparable or better sample efficiency on the Obstructed Suite, though MoPA-RL operates with privileged state input rather than visual input. PSL also demonstrates superior robustness to pose estimation noise, maintaining 75% success at σ=0.1 where SayCan drops to 0%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of LLM-based task planning (SayCan, ProgPrompt), end-to-end visual RL (DRQ-v2, RAPS), and motion-planner-augmented RL (MoPA-RL, GUAPO), but departs from each in a specific way. Unlike LLM planning methods that require a pre-defined skill library, PSL learns low-level control from scratch via RL, using motion planning as the connective tissue between abstract language plans and learned interaction. Unlike MoPA-RL, which lets the RL agent decide when to invoke the motion planner, PSL explicitly decomposes tasks into alternating contact-free (motion planning) and contact-rich (RL) phases, removing the need for the agent to discover this decomposition. The key conceptual contribution is the 'Seq' module: using off-the-shelf vision (SAM + depth) to translate LLM-predicted target regions into robot poses, then achieving them via AIT* motion planning, thereby initializing the RL policy in advantageous local regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PSL is the first framework to enable language-guided RL to solve long-horizon robotics tasks (up to 10 stages) without a pre-defined skill library, achieving >85% success across 25+ tasks from purely visual input. The modular design (Plan/Seq/Learn) means each component can be independently improved, and the use of local wrist-camera observations makes policies more amenable to sim2real transfer. Key limitations include the structural assumption of alternating contact-free/contact-rich phases, difficulty with dynamic tasks due to motion planning latency, and inability to recover from catastrophically wrong LLM plans. Open questions include whether the Plan and Seq modules can themselves be fine-tuned online via RL, and whether the approach extends to tasks requiring fluid, continuous switching between movement and interaction.
