## Problem

Gloss-free sign language translation remains significantly underperforming compared to gloss-based approaches, largely because existing methods lack a mechanism to learn word-level sign representations without manually annotated, sign-ordered glosses. Additionally, the long temporal extent of sign videos (hundreds of frames) and limited dataset sizes make it difficult to fine-tune large pretrained vision and language models directly, creating a tension between leveraging LLM linguistic knowledge and managing computational constraints.

## Input / Output

The model takes a sign language video as input, subsampled at every second frame and spatially cropped to 224×224 pixels, and produces a spoken language sentence (German for Phoenix14T, Chinese for CSL-Daily) generated autoregressively via beam search (width 4). A key practical detail is that input videos can span hundreds of frames, far exceeding the fixed-length short sequences typical of action recognition, which necessitates temporal downsampling within the encoder to keep decoder sequence lengths manageable. For CSL-Daily, vocabulary expansion (252 new tokens via average embedding initialization) and punctuation post-processing are required to handle Chinese characters absent from the XGLM tokenizer.

## Method

Sign2GPT composes a frozen DinoV2 ViT-S/14 spatial backbone (adapted via LoRA on top 3 layers), a 4-layer spatio-temporal sign encoder with local self-attention (window size 7) and strided temporal downsampling, and a frozen XGLM-1.7B decoder adapted through zero-gated cross-attention and LoRA adapters, with only ~16.7M of 1.77B parameters trainable. The key novelty is a pseudo-gloss pretraining stage: spoken sentences are processed with spaCy (lemmatization/word segmentation + POS filtering) to extract pseudo-glosses in spoken-language order, which are then used as prototypes (initialized from fastText embeddings) to train the sign encoder via cosine-similarity alignment with temperature-scaled softmax over both temporal and prototype dimensions, followed by binary cross-entropy on aggregated presence scores. This pretraining requires no sign-order annotations and no manual gloss labels, and the resulting temporally-invariant sign representations are made translation-ready by adding sinusoidal positional encodings before the decoder projection. The gated cross-attention mechanism (with a learnable gate clamped to [0,1] and initialized to zero) ensures sign features are gradually incorporated without disrupting the frozen decoder's linguistic knowledge.

## Results

On Phoenix14T, Sign2GPT with pseudo-gloss pretraining (PGP) achieves BLEU-4 of 22.52 and ROUGE-L of 48.90, improving over the previous gloss-free SOTA (GFSLT-VLP) by ~1.1 BLEU-4 and ~6.4 ROUGE-L. On CSL-Daily, the same configuration reaches BLEU-4 of 15.40 and ROUGE-L of 42.36, a ~4.4 BLEU-4 gain over GFSLT-VLP. Even without pretraining, Sign2GPT alone (BLEU-4 = 19.42 on Phoenix14T) remains competitive with prior gloss-free SOTA, and a lightweight variant using only ~4M trainable decoder parameters (Sign(Z)2GPT) still achieves BLEU-4 of 20.93.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sign2GPT(w/PGP) outperforms all five gloss-free baselines on both datasets. On Phoenix14T, it surpasses GFSLT-VLP (the prior gloss-free SOTA) by 1.08 BLEU-4 (22.52 vs 21.44) and 6.41 ROUGE-L (48.90 vs 42.49), and beats GASLT by 6.78 BLEU-4 and NSLT by 13.52 BLEU-4. On CSL-Daily, the margin is even larger: 4.40 BLEU-4 over GFSLT-VLP (15.40 vs 11.00) and 11.33 BLEU-4 over GASLT (15.40 vs 4.07). Among gloss-based methods, Sign2GPT(w/PGP) exceeds the earliest SL-Transformer (22.52 vs 21.32 BLEU-4 on Phoenix14T) but remains below the stronger gloss-based approaches such as TwoStream-SLT (28.95), SLTUNET (28.47), and MMTLB (28.39), which benefit from manually annotated sign-ordered glosses. The gap to gloss-based SOTA narrows from ~9.5 BLEU-4 (GFSLT-VLP vs TwoStream-SLT) to ~6.4 BLEU-4 (Sign2GPT w/PGP vs TwoStream-SLT), demonstrating the effectiveness of the pseudo-gloss pretraining strategy in closing the gloss-free-to-gloss-based performance gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gloss-free sign language translation line of work (Camgoz et al. 2020a; Yin et al. 2023; Zhou et al. 2023) by replacing the conventional fine-tuned encoder-decoder architecture with a frozen XGLM decoder adapted via LoRA and zero-gated cross-attention, drawing on the parameter-efficient LLM adaptation paradigm established by Flamingo and LLaMA-Adapter. The spatial backbone similarly departs from prior SLT work by using a frozen DinoV2 ViT with LoRA adapters rather than a fully trained CNN or ViT. The key conceptual departure is the pseudo-gloss pretraining strategy, which automatically extracts pseudo-glosses from spoken-language sentences (in spoken order, not sign order) and uses prototype-driven alignment to pretrain the sign encoder—eliminating the need for manually annotated, sign-ordered glosses that prior gloss-based and pseudo-gloss approaches (Sincan et al. 2023; Shi et al. 2022) still required.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a frozen 1.7B-parameter GPT decoder with only ~3.8M trainable parameters, combined with a lightweight sign encoder, can substantially close the gap between gloss-free and gloss-based SLT (e.g., 22.52 vs. 28.95 BLEU-4 on Phoenix14T), suggesting that LLMs' linguistic priors are a viable substitute for expensive gloss annotations. The pseudo-gloss pretraining also shows emergent sign-spotting capability without any localization supervision, opening a path toward automatic gloss generation. However, the moderate pretraining F1 scores (0.44/0.36), evaluation on only two datasets, and language-specific pseudo-gloss extraction (spaCy lemmatization/segmentation) limit generalizability claims, and it remains open whether this frozen-LLM + adapter paradigm scales to low-resource sign languages or longer, more diverse video sequences.
