## Problem

Retrieval-based augmentation for non-knowledge-intensive (NKI) tasks like text classification typically concatenates retrieved passages with the input, inflating sequence length and incurring quadratic attention costs while risking truncation of semantically important context. No prior method efficiently incorporates retrieval knowledge into NKI tasks without this computational penalty.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240947/cropped_page2_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single sentence or sentence pair formatted with prompt templates (e.g., '[CLS] x It was [MASK]. [SEP]') and produces a categorical label via the [MASK] token's predicted word probability. Unlike concatenation-based approaches, the number of retrieved representations (k=64 in experiments) does not increase the input sequence length, since retrievals are fused as dense vectors into hidden states rather than appended as tokens. The retrieval database is a pre-built FAISS/ScaNN index over dense sentence embeddings, queried online at each forward pass.

## Method

ReFusion fuses top-k retrieval representations directly into the hidden states of a transformer backbone (RoBERTa-large or T5) rather than concatenating retrieval text. Two ranking schemes refine the fused representations: a learnable reranker that assigns per-retrieval importance weights via softmax, and an ordered-mask scheme that applies a Gumbel-Softmax differentiable nested dropout independently on each representation dimension across all k retrievals. An adaptive retrieval integrator, inspired by DARTS-style bi-level optimization, searches over which ranking scheme (reranker, ordered mask, or none) to apply at each attention sub-module (key, value, query) across all N layers, relaxing the discrete choice to a continuous softmax over architecture parameters. The lower level fine-tunes the backbone with the selected fusion configuration, while the upper level optimizes the architecture parameters to maximize validation performance, yielding a per-layer fusion strategy without grid search.

## Results

On 15 non-knowledge-intensive (NKI) tasks using RoBERTa-large in a 16-shot setting, ReFusion achieves an overall average accuracy of 74.3%, surpassing LM-BFF (71.8%) by 2.5 points and the retrieval-concatenation baseline CA-512 (69.5%) by 4.8 points. On single-sentence tasks (Avg-S), ReFusion reaches 75.5% vs. LM-BFF's 73.4%; on sentence-pair tasks (Avg-P), it reaches 72.9% vs. LM-BFF's 69.9%. The largest individual gains are on TREC (+5.5 over LM-BFF, 90.3 vs. 84.8) and QNLI (+8.5, 73.0 vs. 64.5). ReFusion also exhibits notably lower standard deviations than competing methods, indicating more stable and robust performance across seeds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReFusion outperforms LM-BFF (Gao et al., 2021) on 13 of 15 tasks, with the most substantial margins on QNLI (+8.5), TREC (+5.5), and SNLI (+3.4), while trailing only on SST-2 (93.4 vs. 92.7 is actually a win; the closest is CoLA where both are low). Against the retrieval-concatenation method CA-512 (Guo et al., 2023), ReFusion wins on all 15 tasks, with particularly large gains on MPQA (+9.8) and QNLI (+6.1), and also achieves lower inference latency (20.2 s vs. 37.4 s per sentence on MPQA). Compared to DART (Zhang et al., 2022), ReFusion wins on most overlapping tasks but is narrowly behind on SST-2 (93.4 vs. 93.5), CR (91.7 vs. 91.8), and MRPC (77.0 vs. 78.3). Against KPT (Hu et al., 2022), ReFusion dominates on all overlapping tasks except QQP, where KPT achieves 71.6 vs. ReFusion's 68.9. On the few-shot SQuAD knowledge-intensive task (Appendix G), ReFusion with T5-base reaches 58.6 F1, outperforming Splinter (54.6) and FewShotBART (55.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant retrieval concatenation paradigm (Izacard & Grave, 2021; Guo et al., 2023b; Ram et al., 2023) that appends retrieved passages to the input sequence, instead fusing retrieval representations directly into model hidden states. It builds on prompt-based fine-tuning (Gao et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2022) as the training framework and borrows the bi-level optimization formulation from differentiable architecture search (Liu et al., 2019a) to select per-layer ranking schemes. The ordered-mask ranking scheme extends nested dropout (Rippel et al., 2014; Cui et al., 2023) from feature-dimension ranking to cross-retrieval ranking, while the reranker scheme introduces a learnable softmax-weighted combination of retrieved representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ReFusion offers a practical efficiency win for retrieval-augmented NLU: it avoids the quadratic attention blowup from longer inputs while achieving modest but consistent accuracy gains (2–3%) over prompt-based baselines across 15 NKI tasks. However, the practical impact is tempered by the fact that inference latency remains substantial (~20s per sentence vs. ~7s baseline), and the 'Hidden' query mode is prohibitively expensive (108–295s). Open questions include whether the bi-level search generalizes to larger models or more ranking schemes, whether the approach transfers to generative tasks, and whether the modest gains justify the added retrieval infrastructure in deployment settings.
