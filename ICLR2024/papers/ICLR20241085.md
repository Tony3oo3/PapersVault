## Problem

Existing LLM evaluation methods struggle to simultaneously satisfy three requirements: generality across diverse real-world scenarios (where gold references are unavailable), flexibility across multiple evaluation protocols (pairwise comparison vs. single-response scoring), and interpretability through natural language explanations. Prior open-source evaluators like PandaLM and Shepherd each support only one protocol, are trained on limited or synthetic data, and provide little to no structured critique, while closed-source alternatives (GPT-4, Claude) are expensive and non-reproducible.

## Input / Output

For pairwise comparison, the model receives a user query followed by two candidate responses and outputs a structured judgment identifying key distinguishing factors (purpose, tone, body, personalization, information richness) before stating a final decision. For single-response evaluation, it receives a query and one response, then produces a multi-paragraph natural language critique pinpointing specific shortcomings, followed by a numerical rating on a 1–10 scale in the format 'Rating: [[n]]'. Notably, the input format deliberately omits the scenario-specific criteria (332 total across 58 scenarios), relying on context distillation so the model internalizes evaluation standards implicitly rather than paraphrasing them.

## Method

AUTO-J is a 13B-parameter model fine-tuned from LLaMA-2-13B-chat using supervised learning on a newly constructed judgment dataset spanning 58 real-world scenarios. Training data is generated by guiding GPT-4 with hand-written, scenario-specific criteria (332 total) to produce evaluation judgments, followed by heuristic filtering and format unification. For single-response training, a 'divide-and-conquer' strategy collects two GPT-4 critiques per sample—one with and one without scenario criteria—then a third inference merges them into a comprehensive critique with a final rating. A key architectural choice is the omission of scenario criteria from the input (analogous to Context Distillation and Ghost Attention), forcing the model to learn evaluation standards implicitly from the output end, which significantly improves generality. To mitigate positional bias in pairwise comparison, each training sample is augmented by swapping response order and relabeling, and the model is trained for 5 epochs with DeepSpeed ZeRO Stage 3, FlashAttention, and BF16/TF32 mixed precision on 8 A100 GPUs.

## Results

On the pairwise response comparison task (Eval-P, 1,392 samples across 58 scenarios), AUTO-J achieves 54.8% overall agreement with human judgment, outperforming all open-source baselines by a large margin (e.g., +8.9% over LLaMA-2-Chat-70B at 45.9%) and surpassing proprietary models ChatGPT (42.7%) and Claude-2 (42.4%) by 12.1% and 12.4% respectively. Only GPT-4 (61.9%) exceeds AUTO-J. On the Best-of-N selection task (Eval-R), AUTO-J's ratings yield the highest GPT-4 scores (8.34 for LLaMA-2-Chat-7B at N=32) and the strongest correlation with GPT-4 ratings (Pearson 0.57, Spearman 0.55), outperforming all baselines including the Open-Assistant reward model (Pearson 0.36).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AUTO-J (13B) is compared against 8 baselines across three evaluation tasks. In pairwise comparison (Eval-P), AUTO-J (54.8%) decisively beats all open-source models: LLaMA-2-Chat-70B (45.9%, +8.9%), SteamSHP (40.6%, +14.2%), PandaLM (38.9%, +15.9%), Vicuna-13B (37.3%, +17.5%), LLaMA-2-Chat-13B (29.0%, +25.8%), and WizardLM-13B (27.8%, +27.0%). It also surpasses proprietary ChatGPT (42.7%, +12.1%) and Claude-2 (42.4%, +12.4%), but trails GPT-4 (61.9%, −7.1%). In critique generation (Eval-C), AUTO-J wins against all baselines including GPT-4, with 218 wins vs. 13 losses against SelFee and 180 wins vs. 51 losses against Vicuna (GPT-4 judged). In Best-of-N selection (Eval-R), AUTO-J achieves the highest GPT-4 ratings (8.34 for LLaMA-2-7B at N=32) and the strongest correlation with GPT-4 (Pearson 0.57), outperforming the Open-Assistant reward model (0.36), SteamSHP (0.13), and all general-purpose LLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-as-judge paradigm (Zheng et al. 2023; Zhou et al. 2023) from closed-source API models to a trainable open-source 13B evaluator, while departing from prior open-source evaluators (PandaLM, Shepherd, SelFee) that each support only a single evaluation protocol and are trained on synthetic or narrow datasets. Methodologically, it draws on context distillation (Bai et al. 2022b) and Ghost Attention (Touvron et al. 2023b) to implicitly encode 332 scenario-specific criteria into the model rather than providing them at inference time, and uses a 'divide-and-conquer' GPT-4 prompting strategy to generate training labels that combine scenario-specific and general critiques. The base model is LLaMA-2-13B-chat, and the training data is sourced from real-world preference datasets (Chatbot Arena, MTBench, OpenAI Summary/WebGPT, Stanford SHP) rather than purely synthetic instructions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AUTO-J provides a practical, reproducible open-source alternative to expensive and opaque API-based evaluation (GPT-4, Claude-2), which is critical for researchers who need frequent model validation at scale. The 58-scenario benchmark with 332 curated criteria and the associated judgment dataset fill a gap in meta-evaluation testbeds, which prior work (MTBench's 80 queries, PandaLM's 999 samples) addressed only partially. Open questions include whether the GPT-4-derived training signal creates a circularity ceiling, whether the approach extends to multilingual settings, and whether the 13B scale is sufficient as base models grow. The 0.97 Spearman correlation with GPT-4 on AlpacaEval system-level ranking suggests strong practical utility for leaderboard construction without API costs.
