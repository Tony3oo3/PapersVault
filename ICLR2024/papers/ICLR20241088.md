## Problem

Existing graph generation methods rely on representations (adjacency matrices, DFS strings, motif compositions) that are either too large, lack inherent hierarchy, or depend on domain-specific rules. The K²-tree, a hierarchical representation originally designed for lossless graph compression, had not been adapted for generative modeling, leaving an opportunity to exploit its compactness and structural hierarchy for autoregressive graph generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241088/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the model consumes a dataset of graphs (or molecular graphs with categorical node and edge features) converted into pruned, tokenized K²-tree sequences. At inference, it takes only a begin-of-sequence token and autoregressively emits a sequence of tokens (vocabulary size 24 for K=2), which is decoded back into a K²-tree and then into a full adjacency matrix or featured molecular graph. The Cuthill-McKee node ordering is applied to the input graphs before K²-tree construction to maximize compression.

## Method

HGGT represents each graph as a K²-tree that recursively partitions the adjacency matrix into K×K submatrices, collapsing all-zero blocks into single leaf nodes. The tree is then pruned (removing above-diagonal redundancy for undirected graphs), flattened via breadth-first traversal, and tokenized by grouping sibling nodes into single tokens, yielding a sequence far shorter than the original matrix. A masked Transformer with a novel tree positional encoding—where each token's positional embedding is the sum of per-level embeddings along the root-to-parent path—autoregressively generates this sequence. For attributed graphs, leaf node attributes are extended from binary to categorical values encoding node and edge features, while non-leaf nodes retain binary zero/non-zero semantics. The framework is domain-agnostic and requires no motif vocabulary or chemical rules.

## Results

On four generic graph benchmarks (Community-small, Planar, Enzymes, Grid), HGGT achieves the lowest MMD on all 12 metrics, with particularly striking results on Grid (Deg. 0.000, Clus. 0.000, Orb. 0.000) and Planar (Deg. 0.000, Clus. 0.001, Orb. 0.000). On molecular generation, HGGT attains 99.22% validity and 0.000 NSPDK on QM9, and 0.001 NSPDK and 1.93 FCD on ZINC250k, the best structural similarity scores among all baselines. HGGT also generates graphs substantially faster than baselines (e.g., 0.03s vs. 0.34s for DiGress on Community-small).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HGGT outperforms all 12 generic graph baselines on every reported metric across all four datasets. The closest competitors are DiGress (0.012/0.025/0.002 MMD on Community-small) and GDSM (0.011/0.015/0.001), both of which are still 10–20× worse than HGGT (0.001/0.006/0.003) on Community-small. On Grid, HGGT achieves near-perfect MMD (0.000 on all three metrics) while the next-best GDSM scores 0.002/0.000/0.000 and DiGress 0.016/0.000/0.004. For molecular generation on ZINC250k, HGGT achieves the best NSPDK (0.001) and FCD (1.93), substantially outperforming GDSM (0.017/12.96) and DiGress (0.042/16.54), though GDSM edges out HGGT on QM9 validity (99.90 vs. 99.22). On QM9, HGGT's novelty (24.01) is lower than DiGress (35.46) and GraphDF (98.54), suggesting a trade-off between structural fidelity and diversity for small molecules.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the K²-tree, originally designed for lossless graph compression (Brisaboa et al., 2009), as a generative representation for autoregressive graph generation. It departs from prior autoregressive approaches (GraphRNN, GRAN) that rely on BFS node ordering over the adjacency matrix, and from string-based or motif-based representations that require domain-specific rules, by offering a hierarchical, domain-agnostic, and compact sequence derived from recursive K×K partitioning of the adjacency matrix. The combination of pruning (exploiting symmetry), sibling-node tokenization, and a dedicated tree positional encoding for a Transformer decoder is the paper's core architectural contribution, positioning HGGT between compression-oriented representations and generative modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The K²-tree representation reduces the sequence length dramatically (e.g., ~419 tokens for Grid vs. ~4106 for GraphRNN vs. ~50,000 for the full matrix), which translates into substantially faster inference (0.03s vs. 0.34–3.5s per sample) and potentially better generalization on structured graphs like grids. The domain-agnostic nature (no chemical rules, no motif vocabulary) makes the approach broadly applicable, though the paper only validates on graphs up to ~400 nodes, leaving open whether the compactness advantage persists at much larger scale. An open question is how well the method handles directed graphs (pruning is omitted) and whether the C-M ordering assumption generalizes to graphs without clear community structure.
