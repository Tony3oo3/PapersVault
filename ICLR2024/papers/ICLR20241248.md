## Problem

Machine unlearning has been studied almost exclusively for classification models, leaving generative models—particularly image-to-image (I2I) generative models—largely unexplored despite their superior data memorization capabilities. This paper is the first to provide a systematic, theoretically grounded framework for unlearning in I2I generative models, addressing both the lack of a principled unlearning objective and the absence of an efficient algorithm that works across diverse I2I architectures (diffusion models, VQ-GAN, MAE).

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained I2I generative model (diffusion, VQ-GAN, or MAE with ViT-based encoder-decoder), partial input images (e.g., center-cropped at 16×16 patch granularity, 256×256 or 224×224 resolution), retain-set and forget-set images, and samples from a Gaussian distribution N(0,Σ) where Σ is approximated as the identity matrix due to the infeasible memory cost of the full covariance for high-resolution images. The output is a modified encoder (decoder weights remain frozen) that, when applied to partial inputs, faithfully reconstructs retain-set images while producing near-noise outputs for forget-set images. Notably, the method can operate without access to the true retain set by substituting images from unrelated classes as a proxy.

## Method

The paper formulates unlearning as minimizing KL divergence between original and target model outputs on the retain set while maximizing it on the forget set, then proves (Lemma 1) that the optimal forget distribution is a zero-mean Gaussian N(0,Σ) sharing the forget set's covariance. To make this tractable, Theorem 1 uses the InfoNCE lower bound on mutual information to show that minimizing the L2 distance between the target encoder's output and the original encoder's output (on retain samples) or the original encoder's output on Gaussian noise (on forget samples) monotonically increases the MI lower bound, thereby reducing the KL divergence. The resulting algorithm (Eq. 10) fine-tunes only the encoder with a weighted L2 loss: ‖E_θ(T(x_r)) − E_θ₀(T(x_r))‖₂ + α‖E_θ(T(x_f)) − E_θ₀(T(n))‖₂, where α=0.25 balances retention and forgetting. This encoder-only modification is substantially cheaper than full-model unlearning and generalizes across diffusion models, VQ-GAN, and MAE despite their different native training objectives.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with 8×8 center-cropped patches (16×16 px each), the proposed method achieves near-original retain-set performance (FID 13.4 vs. 12.2 for diffusion, 15.0 vs. 14.4 for VQ-GAN, 59.9 vs. 56.7 for MAE) while dramatically degrading forget-set reconstruction (FID 107.9 vs. 14.6 for diffusion, 83.4 vs. 14.4 for VQ-GAN, 153.0 vs. 84.1 for MAE). CLIP similarity on the forget set drops from ~0.89 to ~0.69 (diffusion), ~0.77 to ~0.60 (VQ-GAN), and ~0.71 to ~0.67 (MAE), confirming semantic erasure. The method also works without access to the true retain set (Proxy D_R), with only a slight additional FID increase (e.g., 17.9 vs. 13.4 for diffusion retain FID).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four adapted baselines on the forget-set FID (the primary unlearning metric) across all three model architectures. On diffusion models, Ours achieves FID F = 107.9, far exceeding MAX LOSS (45.7), NOISY LABEL (36.9), and RANDOM ENCODER (30.6), while RETAIN LABEL is competitive (104.7) but at the cost of much worse retain-set FID (23.1 vs. 13.4). On VQ-GAN, Ours (FID F = 83.4) beats MAX LOSS (115.2 is actually worse for retain), NOISY LABEL (79.5), and RANDOM ENCODER (72.8), while RETAIN LABEL fails to forget at all (FID F = 23.3). On MAE, Ours (FID F = 153.0) leads all baselines, with RANDOM ENCODER (146.4) and RETAIN LABEL (145.3) trailing slightly. Critically, Ours maintains the best retain-set FID among all methods that achieve strong forgetting, whereas MAX LOSS and RETAIN LABEL sacrifice retain performance (FID R of 34.1 and 23.1 for diffusion, respectively) to partially forget. RANDOM ENCODER is the closest competitor on VQ-GAN and MAE but is substantially weaker on diffusion models (FID F 30.6 vs. 107.9), highlighting the generality advantage of the proposed encoder-space L2 formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the machine unlearning literature—which has been dominated by classification tasks (SISA, NTK-based weight adjustment, gradient-based methods)—to the underexplored domain of image-to-image generative models. Theoretically, it builds on the InfoNCE mutual information lower bound (Belghazi et al., 2018) to derive a tractable L2-loss objective in representation space that provably increases MI, thereby sidestepping the intractability of directly optimizing KL divergence over high-dimensional image distributions. A key departure from prior generative-model unlearning attempts (which maximize training loss or reassign labels on tiny datasets like MNIST) is the insight that only the encoder needs modification, not the full model, and that the forget-set target can be Gaussian noise rather than a re-labeled image.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic, theoretically grounded treatment of unlearning for I2I generative models, providing a unified framework applicable to diffusion models, VQ-GAN, and MAE simultaneously. The practical finding that encoder-only modification suffices, combined with robustness to the absence of retain samples, makes the approach deployable under real data-retention policies. Open questions remain: the method still requires access to forget samples (which may be unavailable), has not been validated on practical scenarios like removing copyrighted or harmful content, and has not been extended to text-to-image or language generation. The reliance on an identity-matrix approximation for the covariance Σ also leaves room for improvement with low-rank or data-driven alternatives.
