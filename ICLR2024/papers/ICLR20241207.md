## Problem

Randomized Smoothing provides certified ℓ2 robustness for arbitrary classifiers, but its certified radius upper bound diminishes at a rate of 1/√d as input dimension grows, imposing a curse of dimensionality that prior work (adversarial training, denoisers, ensembles) has not fundamentally addressed. This paper asks whether certified robustness can be maintained for high-dimensional inputs by performing smoothing in a lower-dimensional space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241207/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 224×224 for ImageNet) and produces a class label together with a certified ℓ2 robustness radius. The input is first down-sampled into two spatially non-overlapping sub-images via 2×2 diagonal pixel-index kernels with stride 2, then each sub-image is resized back to the original spatial resolution via interpolation before being fed to a pre-trained base classifier. Smoothed probabilities are estimated via 100,000 Monte Carlo samples from the Gaussian distribution, and the certified radius is computed from the lower-bound binomial confidence estimates.

## Method

DRS partitions the d-dimensional input into two sub-inputs of dimensions m and n (m+n=d), independently smooths each with isotropic Gaussian noise of standard deviation σ, and defines the smoothed classifier g as the argmax over the sum of the two sub-classifiers' smoothed probabilities. The key theoretical contribution is a tight ℓ2 certified radius R = (σ/√2)(Φ⁻¹(p_A^l) + Φ⁻¹(p_A^r)), whose upper bound decreases at the slower rate (1/√m + 1/√n) rather than 1/√d, effectively mitigating the curse of dimensionality. The down-sampling exploits spatial redundancy in natural images: two 2×2 kernels each retain one diagonal of pixels, preserving most information while halving the effective dimension. DRS is architecture-agnostic and integrates seamlessly with existing RS training strategies (Gaussian augmentation, consistency regularization, diffusion-denoising, SmoothAdv, and model ensembling), consistently improving both clean accuracy and certified robustness over standard RS on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, DRS consistently outperforms RS across all three training strategies (Gaussian augmentation, consistency regularization, and diffusion-denoising) at every certified radius. The largest gains appear at r=0.50, where DRS improves certified accuracy by approximately 7.8% (Gaussian), 2.0% (consistency), and 10.1% (diffusion-denoising) over RS. On ImageNet, DRS achieves gains of roughly 3.6%, 4.4%, and 7.2% at r=0.50 under the same three strategies. Notably, DRS with σ={0.25,0.50} extends certified accuracy to r=2.00 (e.g., 21.1% with diffusion-denoising on CIFAR-10), where RS drops to 0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRS outperforms all three RS baselines across every radius on both CIFAR-10 and ImageNet. Against RS with Gaussian augmentation (Cohen et al., 2019), DRS at σ=0.18 achieves 83.4% vs. 76.2% clean accuracy and 50.2% vs. 42.4% at r=0.50 on CIFAR-10; at σ=0.25, DRS extends certified accuracy to r=2.00 (4.6%) where RS is 0%. Against RS with consistency regularization (Jeong & Shin, 2020), DRS at σ=0.18 improves certified accuracy at r=0.50 from 57.2% to 59.2%, and at σ=0.25 achieves 17.4% at r=2.00 versus 0% for RS. Against RS with diffusion-denoising (Carlini et al., 2023), DRS shows the largest absolute gain: 67.9% vs. 57.8% at r=0.50 and 21.1% vs. 0% at r=2.00 on CIFAR-10. On ImageNet, the same pattern holds with DRS gaining 3.6–7.2% at r=0.50 depending on the training strategy. DRS also integrates seamlessly with model ensemble boosting (Horváth et al., 2022), further improving certified accuracy by 1–3% over single-model DRS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Randomized Smoothing (Cohen et al., 2019) by addressing the curse of dimensionality identified by Kumar et al. (2020) and Wu et al. (2021), where the certified ℓ2 radius degrades at rate 1/√d. Prior work has focused on strengthening the base classifier under Gaussian noise (adversarial training, denoising, ensembles) without modifying the smoothing mechanism itself. DRS departs from this by changing the smoothing geometry: it partitions the d-dimensional input into two spatially non-overlapping sub-images and smooths each in lower dimensions, yielding a superior upper bound proportional to (1/√m + 1/√n) rather than 1/√d. The tightness proof builds directly on the Neyman-Pearson-based Theorem 1 from Cohen et al. (2019), extended to the dual-smoothing setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DRS provides the first principled mechanism to mitigate the curse of dimensionality at the smoothing level rather than the classifier level, offering a theoretically grounded path to better certified robustness for high-dimensional inputs. The approach integrates cleanly with existing training strategies (Gaussian augmentation, consistency regularization, diffusion denoising, model ensembles) and shows consistent gains on both CIFAR-10 and ImageNet. Open questions include whether the spatial-redundancy-based partitioning generalizes beyond natural images, whether the k>2 partitioning optimization (shown to be non-convex) can be made tractable, and whether the √2 improvement factor holds under more aggressive adversarial settings or non-Gaussian noise distributions.
