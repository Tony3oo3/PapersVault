## Problem

LiDAR-based 3D object detection is bottlenecked by expensive 3D bounding box annotation, and existing label-efficient paradigms (semi-supervised, weakly-supervised) each rely on a single type of supervision. The paper identifies that point clouds are textureless (making semantic learning hard) yet geometrically rich and scale-invariant (making geometry estimation easy), suggesting no prior method exploits this asymmetry to combine massive cheap semantic labels with a small number of accurate geometric labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240510/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw LiDAR point clouds and produces 3D bounding boxes with class labels, but the supervision is mixed-grained: the majority of training samples carry only coarse cluster-level labels (a parallelogram defined by three BEV clicks enclosing the object's points), while a small fraction (10% or 1%) carry full 3D box annotations with center, dimensions, and heading. Cluster labels can be generated manually at ~14% the cost of a box, or automatically via PointSAM which projects SAM 2D masks onto 3D points and refines them with connected-component-based separability-aware refinement.

## Method

MixSup redesigns the label assignment module of mainstream detectors to accept cluster-level labels: for center-based detectors (e.g., CenterPoint), the cluster centroid replaces the object center for classification supervision; for box-based detectors (e.g., SECOND, PV-RCNN, FSD), a box-cluster IoU (point-level overlap between proposal points and cluster points) assigns cluster labels to proposals. The training loss decouples supervision so that cluster labels contribute only to classification/confidence while accurate box labels contribute only to regression, enabling seamless integration into any detector without architectural changes. To further reduce annotation cost, PointSAM leverages a pre-trained 2D semantic segmentation model to prompt SAM for over-segmented masks, projects them to 3D, and applies Separability-Aware Refinement (splitting masks across connected components and merging masks within one) to correct projection errors and over-segmentation, yielding automated coarse cluster labels at ~50% of manual annotation cost.

## Results

On nuScenes, CenterPoint with MixSup (10% box labels + cluster labels) achieves 60.73 mAP and 66.46 NDS, reaching 97.31% and 97.45% of the fully supervised counterpart (62.41 mAP, 68.20 NDS). On Waymo Open Dataset, the same setup yields 62.34 Mean L2APH (96.41% of fully supervised 64.66). On KITTI, SECOND+MixSup reaches 74.85 Car mAP (95.20% of fully supervised) and PV-RCNN+MixSup reaches 76.09 Car mAP (91.01%). With only 1% annotation cost on WOD, PV-RCNN+MixSup achieves 56.58 Mean L2APH, far exceeding the 28.27 of the semi-supervised HSDDA baseline at 1% frames.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against weakly-supervised WS3D (Meng et al., 2020/2021) on KITTI Car, MixSup wins on Easy (86.37 vs. 84.04/85.04) and Moderate (76.20 vs. 75.10/75.94) but trails on Hard (72.36 vs. 73.29/74.38), likely due to the limited box budget for geometry. On WOD with SECOND at 10% annotation cost, MixSup (54.23 Mean) edges out ProficientTeacher (54.16) and clearly beats FixMatch (51.45), while matching or exceeding them on Pedestrian (+2.64 over ProficientTeacher) and Cyclist (+1.91). With SST at 10% cost, MixSup (60.74) substantially outperforms all self-supervised pre-training baselines: PointContrast (49.94), ProposalContrast (50.13), and MV-JAR (54.06), gaining 6.7–10.8 points in Mean L2APH. The most dramatic comparison is PV-RCNN at 1% cost, where MixSup (56.58) nearly doubles HSDDA's 28.27, showing that mixed-grained supervision is far more effective than semi-supervised self-training under extreme label scarcity. For PointSAM's panoptic segmentation quality (Table 10), it achieves PQ 63.7, surpassing GP-S3Net (56.0), Panoptic-PolarNet (59.2), and SCAN (60.6), but falling short of the fully supervised CFNet (74.8) and SMAC-Seg (65.2), which is expected since PointSAM uses no 3D annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

MixSup carves out a distinct niche between semi-supervised learning (which generates pseudo labels from unlabeled data) and weakly supervised learning (which relies solely on cheap weak annotations like center clicks). Rather than following either paradigm exclusively, it introduces a mixed-grained supervision strategy that simultaneously exploits massive coarse cluster-level labels for semantic learning and a small set of accurate box-level labels for geometry estimation. The method is architecturally agnostic, integrating seamlessly into anchor-based (SECOND), anchor-free (CenterPoint), two-stage (PV-RCNN), and fully sparse (FSD) detectors through a redesigned label assignment module. PointSAM additionally repurposes the Segment Anything Model for automated 3D instance segmentation, bridging 2D foundation model capabilities to outdoor LiDAR scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: achieving 97.31% of fully supervised performance with only 10% box annotations and cheap cluster labels (at ~14% the annotation cost of a box) offers a compelling path for reducing the annotation bottleneck in autonomous driving. The pilot study's finding that geometry estimation in point clouds is nearly data-saturated at 5–10% of labeled data is a useful empirical principle that could inform annotation budget allocation more broadly. Open questions include how MixSup composes with semi-supervised self-training (only a simple baseline is explored), whether auto-labeling pipelines can replace even the coarse cluster annotations, and whether the performance gap to fully supervised training poses safety risks in deployment.
