## Problem

Conventional episodic control in MARL uses random projections to embed states, which fails to capture semantic similarity and thus recalls only nearly identical states, limiting exploration. Moreover, naively leveraging episodic memory on complex cooperative tasks risks converging to local optima by repeatedly exploiting previously visited high-return states that are not actually goal-reaching. The paper asks how to make episodic memory both semantically coherent for broader recall and selectively incentivizing of desirable transitions to avoid local convergence.

## Input / Output

Each agent receives a local action-observation history τ_i (encoded by a GRU) for action selection, while the global state s_t (a state-vector of dimension 68–322 depending on the SMAC/GRF task) is fed into the episodic memory system. The trainable dCAE encoder maps s_t conditioned on timestep t into a 4-dimensional latent vector x_t, which serves as the key in the episodic buffer D_E storing the highest return H_t and a binary desirability indicator ξ. The system outputs joint Q-values (via a QPLEX mixer satisfying the IGM condition) from which individual agent actions are selected, plus a scalar episodic incentive r^p added to the TD target for desirable transitions.

## Method

EMU introduces two coupled components. First, a deterministic conditional autoencoder (dCAE) replaces the random projection: the encoder f_φ embeds the global state conditioned on timestep into a 4-dim latent, while the decoder f_ψ simultaneously predicts the stored highest return H_t and reconstructs s_t, producing a smoother embedding space where semantically similar states cluster together and enabling safe recall within a broader δ-neighborhood. Second, an episodic incentive r^p = γ·(N_ξ/N_call)·(H(f_φ(s′)) − max_{a′} Q_{θ⁻}(s′,a′)) is added to the TD target, which is non-zero only when the next state s′ has been visited on a desirable trajectory (one achieving the maximum episode return R_max). The paper proves (Theorem 2) that this incentive's gradient converges to the optimal Bellman gradient as the policy approaches optimality, and that unlike the conventional episodic-control regularization it requires no task-dependent scale factor and inherently suppresses overestimation by using a count-based expected value rather than the optimistic maximum.

## Results

On SMAC, both EMU (QPLEX) and EMU (CDS) achieve significant win-rate improvements over their respective base methods across all difficulty levels, with the most pronounced gains on super hard maps (MMM2, 6h_vs_8z, 3s5z_vs_3s6z) where EMU markedly expedites convergence to optimal policies. On Google Research Football, EMU rapidly identifies winning or scoring policies in the early learning phase by leveraging semantically similar memory recall. Against MAPPO, EMU shows an evident performance gap on most SMAC maps, with MAPPO suffering from local convergence on super hard tasks like MMM2 and 3s5z_vs_3s6z. The overall win-rate metric μ̄_w confirms that the dCAE embedding structure provides the best training efficiency across a wide range of δ values compared to random projection or EmbNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMU outperforms all five baselines across both SMAC and GRF benchmarks. Against QMIX and QPLEX, EMU (QPLEX) achieves higher final win-rates and faster convergence, with the gap widening substantially on super hard SMAC maps where QPLEX plateaus at sub-optimal policies. Compared to CDS, EMU (CDS) shows consistent gains, particularly in training efficiency, as the semantic embedding enables more effective memory recall than CDS's diversity-based exploration alone. Against EMC (the closest prior work combining episodic control with MARL), EMU demonstrates that the episodic incentive prevents the local convergence that plagues conventional episodic control — EMC had to reduce its episodic regularization to near zero on super hard SMAC tasks, effectively disabling its memory component. Against MAPPO, EMU maintains a clear advantage on most SMAC maps; MAPPO only achieves comparable performance on 6h_vs_8z but with higher variance across seeds, and fails to escape local optima on MMM2 and 3s5z_vs_3s6z within the same training budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the episodic control lineage (Blundell et al., 2016; Pritzel et al., 2017; Lin et al., 2018) and its recent MARL adaptation in EMC (Zheng et al., 2021) by replacing the random-projection state embedding with a trainable deterministic conditional autoencoder (dCAE) that produces semantically coherent embeddings, and by substituting the conventional episodic regularization with a desirability-gated 'episodic incentive' reward. The method is built on the CTDE value-factorization framework (QPLEX, QMIX, CDS) and provides theoretical guarantees (Theorems 1–2) that the episodic incentive recovers the optimal TD gradient as the policy converges, a property the conventional episodic control lacks due to an inherent bias in its weighted-sum loss structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent practical failure mode in cooperative MARL—convergence to local optima on complex tasks—by showing that naive episodic memory recall can actively harm learning and that a simple desirability-based gating mechanism (the episodic incentive) eliminates the need for task-dependent hyperparameter tuning that EMC required (setting its regularization weight to near zero on super-hard maps). The dCAE embedding's robustness across a wide range of δ values reduces the sensitivity to a key hyperparameter. Open questions include how the method extends to non-cooperative or partially cooperative settings where a natural R_thr is unavailable, whether the episodic incentive generalizes to policy-gradient MARL (e.g., MAPPO), and how it scales to very large agent teams where the joint state dimension grows rapidly.
