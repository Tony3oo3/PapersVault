## Problem

Conformal prediction guarantees marginal coverage only under data exchangeability, an assumption that adversarial perturbations at inference time violate, causing prediction coverage to degrade. While worst-case robustness certification exists for point predictions, no prior work provides a certifiable worst-case coverage guarantee for conformal prediction sets under bounded adversarial perturbations. The paper asks whether integrating domain knowledge and logical reasoning into the prediction pipeline can yield tighter certified coverage than purely data-driven approaches.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (e.g., a traffic sign, CIFAR-10 object, or animal image) as input, which may be adversarially perturbed within an ℓ₂ ball of radius δ. The main classifier and L auxiliary knowledge models each output a scalar probability (e.g., P(stop sign | x), P(octagon shape | x)). The final output is a prediction set C ⊆ {1,…,N_c} of candidate class labels, constructed so that the ground truth label is contained with certified worst-case probability at least 1−α for any perturbation ‖ε‖₂ ≤ δ.

## Method

COLEP combines a data-driven learning component (a main N_c-class classifier plus L binary knowledge models detecting concepts like shape, color, or content) with a reasoning component built from R probabilistic circuits (PCs). Each PC encodes weighted logical implication rules (e.g., IsStopSign ⇒ IsOctagon) as a factor function F(μ) that up-weights assignments conforming to the rules; the PC's root node computes a corrected class probability by marginalizing over all 2^(N_c+L) assignments in a single forward pass, exploiting decomposability and smoothness for tractability. For certification, randomized smoothing yields probability bounds [π̂, π̄] for the learning component, and Theorem 1 analytically propagates these bounds through the PC to obtain [L[π̂^COLEP], U[π̂^COLEP]]. The conformal prediction set is then constructed using a worst-case non-conformity score (Eq. 12) that replaces point probabilities with their certified bounds, guaranteeing coverage 1−α under any ℓ₂-bounded perturbation (Theorem 2) and providing a computable worst-case coverage lower bound τ^COLEP_cer (Theorem 3).

## Results

On GTSRB, CIFAR-10, and AwA2, COLEP consistently achieves higher certified coverage than the SOTA baseline RSCP under ℓ2-bounded perturbations (δ = 0.125, 0.25, 0.50), with the gap widening substantially at δ = 0.5. Under PGD attack (δ = 0.25), standard conformal prediction (CP) drops below the nominal 0.9 coverage level due to violated exchangeability, while COLEP maintains marginal coverage above 0.9. Compared to RSCP, COLEP simultaneously achieves larger marginal coverage and smaller average prediction set size, weakening the typical coverage–set-size tradeoff. The certified coverage of COLEP approaches the theoretical upper bound of 0.9, indicating tight certification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard conformal prediction (CP, Romano et al. 2020), COLEP clearly wins: under PGD attack (δ = 0.25), CP's marginal coverage falls below the nominal 0.9 level on all three datasets because adversarial perturbations violate the exchangeability assumption, whereas COLEP's reasoning-corrected prediction set preserves coverage above 0.9. Against RSCP (Gendler et al. 2022), the SOTA adversarially robust conformal prediction baseline using randomized smoothing, COLEP achieves strictly higher certified coverage across all perturbation radii on GTSRB, CIFAR-10, and AwA2, with the advantage being most pronounced at δ = 0.5 where RSCP's generic smoothing-based bounds become very loose. Under the same PGD attack setting, COLEP also outperforms RSCP on both marginal coverage and average prediction set size, meaning COLEP provides tighter uncertainty quantification without inflating the prediction set. The paper attributes these gains to the knowledge-enabled logical reasoning component (probabilistic circuits encoding implication rules), which corrects the main model's output probabilities in a way that is more targeted than the generic randomized smoothing used by RSCP.

## Relation to Prior Work

COLEP sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) split conformal prediction with adaptive non-conformity scores (Romano et al., 2020), (2) adversarially robust conformal prediction via randomized smoothing (Gendler et al., 2022, RSCP), and (3) learning-reasoning frameworks that encode domain knowledge as logical rules to improve certified robustness (Yang et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2023). The key departure from RSCP is the insertion of a probabilistic-circuit-based reasoning component between the learning models and the conformal calibration step, which tightens the certified coverage bound by exploiting semantic relationships among class and concept predictions. The use of PCs (Darwiche, 2001) rather than Markov Logic Networks or variational inference is motivated by the need for exact, linear-time inference over the knowledge rules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical guarantee that a knowledge-enabled reasoning component provably increases certified conformal coverage over a single model (Thms. 4–5), bridging a gap between worst-case robustness certification and statistical uncertainty quantification. However, practical adoption is constrained by the need for domain experts to hand-design implication rules and their weights, and the evaluation is limited to three relatively small datasets (GTSRB, CIFAR-10, AwA2). Open questions include whether rule design can be automated (e.g., via LLMs or knowledge graphs), how the framework behaves when knowledge rules are noisy or adversarially contaminated, and whether the approach scales to high-dimensional classification tasks like ImageNet with hundreds of classes.
