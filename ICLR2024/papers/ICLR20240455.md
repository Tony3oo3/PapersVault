## Problem

PPO-Clip is the most widely implemented RL algorithm, yet its theoretical understanding is limited to narrow settings (tabular policies, over-parameterized networks) that rely on local convexity or hinge-loss reformulations. The original PPO-Clip objective involves the ratio of two stochastic policies, a non-smooth clip operator, and potentially unbounded score functions—challenges for which no off-the-shelf recursive inequality or convergence tool existed in the literature.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates on a finite-state, finite-action MDP with a parametric policy π_θ(a|s). At each outer iteration n, the old policy π_{θ_{n,1}} is fixed and T inner gradient-ascent steps (k=1,…,T−1) update θ using the clipped surrogate objective with advantage estimates. The convergence guarantee is expressed as liminf ‖∇V(θ_{n,1})‖² ≤ 8L√|A| limsup φ_n a.s., where φ_n captures the combined bias from Markovian sampling and advantage estimation; when φ_n=0 (e.g., Monte-Carlo sampling), convergence to a true stationary point is exact.

## Method

The paper formulates PPO-Clip as a two-scale iterative process and proves stationary-point convergence via a key recursive inequality (Lemma 3.1) for the value function. The central technical device is a divide-and-conquer decomposition: the PPO-Clip gradient is split into the standard policy gradient at θ_{n,1} plus two error terms X_{n,k} and Y_{n,k}. To bound these errors, the authors show that the clip-violation event {π_{θ_{n,k}}/π_{θ_{n,1}} ∉ (1−δ₀, 1+δ₀)} is contained in the event {√π_{θ_{n,1}}(a|s) ≤ k_θ}, a lower-bound condition on the old policy probability, which is measurable with respect to the previous σ-field. This containment, combined with the L-smoothness of the policy (Assumption 3.1) and the bound ‖∇π_θ(a|s)‖ ≤ √(2L·min{π,1−π}), yields explicit bounds on X and Y in terms of cumulative step sizes and the sampling bias φ_n. Under the Robbins-Monro condition on learning rates, classical martingale convergence arguments then establish almost-sure stationary-point convergence, while a weaker step-size condition yields average-iterate and subsequence convergence rates of O(Σε²/Σε) + O(limsup φ_n).

## Results

The paper establishes three main theoretical guarantees for PPO-Clip. Theorem 3.1 shows that the gradient norm converges to a neighborhood of size O(8L√|A| · limsup φ_n) almost surely, where φ_n captures the bias from Markovian sampling and advantage estimation. Theorem 3.2 strengthens this to exact convergence (lim ||∇V(θ_n)|| = 0 a.s.) when the estimation bias vanishes (e.g., Monte-Carlo sampling). Theorem 3.3 provides a convergence rate of O(Σε_t²/Σε_t) + O(limsup φ_n) for both best-iterate and average-iterate convergence, under the weaker learning rate condition that Σε_{n,k} → 0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or comparison tables. The paper positions its results relative to prior theoretical work on PPO-Clip and policy gradient. Compared to Huang et al. (2021) and Yao et al. (2022), which analyzed PPO-Clip under tabular or over-parameterized neural network settings via a hinge-loss/local-convexity framework, this work handles the general non-locally-convex case with unbounded score functions. Compared to Liu et al. (2019), which used NTK conditions for a modified PPO variant without the policy ratio, this paper analyzes the original PPO-Clip with the ratio of two stochastic policies. The convergence guarantee (Theorem 3.2) matches the best existing results for policy gradient (Zhang et al., 2020) in terms of almost-sure stationary point convergence. The paper further notes that PPO-Clip's convergence rate differs from PG only by a factor of T (the number of off-policy inner steps), suggesting PPO-Clip can be T times more sample-efficient when sampling is the bottleneck.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convergence analysis of PPO-Clip beyond the restrictive settings of prior work. Huang et al. (2021) and Yao et al. (2022) analyzed PPO-Clip by recasting it as a hinge loss problem, which requires local convexity and is limited to tabular or over-parameterized neural network settings; Liu et al. (2019) used NTK conditions but analyzed a variant that removes the policy ratio entirely. Here, the authors tackle the vanilla PPO-Clip formulation directly, handling the non-smooth clip operator, unbounded score function, and ratio of two stochastic policies simultaneously through a divide-and-conquer strategy on clip events and a two-scale iterative formulation, building on the stochastic approximation framework of Robbins & Monro (1951) and the policy gradient decomposition of Williams (1992).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PPO-Clip is arguably the most widely deployed RL algorithm (5,500+ GitHub implementations), yet its theoretical guarantees were previously confined to narrow settings; this work provides the first general stationary-point convergence guarantee under minimal assumptions (L-smooth policy, bounded reward, Robbins-Monro step sizes). The key lemma characterizing clip-event probabilities and the resulting recursive inequality may serve as a reusable tool for analyzing other clipped or ratio-based surrogate objectives. Open questions remain: whether the stationary points reached are locally or globally optimal, and whether stronger policy conditions (e.g., Fisher non-degeneracy) can yield last-iterate convergence rates or optimality guarantees.
