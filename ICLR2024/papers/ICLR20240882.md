## Problem

Existing invariance learning methods (IRM, REx, and variants) assume categorically indexed domains, but many real-world tasks—such as CPU utilization prediction across time of day or house price prediction across years—have naturally continuous domain indices. The paper theoretically proves that these methods fail with constant probability when domains are continuous (many domains with few samples each), because finite-sample estimations of E^t[y|Φ(x)] become too noisy to distinguish invariant from spurious features, and the naive fix of discretizing domains ignores the underlying relationships among them.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard input features (RGB images for CMNIST/Yearbook, or tabular vectors for HousePrice, Insurance Fraud, and Alipay Auto-scaling) together with a continuous scalar domain index t (e.g., minute of day, built year, customer age) and produces a discrete class label. Unlike discrete-domain IRM methods that require pre-defined environment partitions, CIL consumes the raw continuous t value directly, and the two auxiliary domain regressors h and g are trained to predict this continuous t via L1 or L2 regression loss rather than classification.

## Method

CIL reformulates the invariance constraint y ⊥ t | Φ(x) by aligning E^y[t|Φ(x)] across classes instead of E^t[y|Φ(x)] across domains, which is estimable because each class contains many samples even when each domain contains very few. Concretely, it trains two domain regressors: h(Φ(x)) predicts t from features alone, and g(Φ(x), y) predicts t from features plus the label; if Φ(x) is truly invariant, both achieve the same regression loss. The overall objective is a minimax problem solved via SGDA: the outer minimization over (w, Φ, h) reduces the classification loss plus the L2 regression loss of h, while the inner maximization over g increases the L2 regression loss of g, driving the gap between the two regressors to zero. The paper provides a finite-sample generalization bound for the SGDA solution under standard convex-concave and stability assumptions, and shows via Proposition 1 and Theorem 2 that CIL succeeds precisely where REx provably fails.

## Results

On the Alipay Auto-scaling dataset, CIL(L2) achieves 71.29% OOD accuracy, outperforming the best discrete-domain baseline IRMv1 (66.49%) by 4.8% and ERM (57.38%) by 13.9%. On HousePrice, CIL(L2) reaches 79.29% OOD accuracy vs. 77.40% for HRM (best continuous baseline) and 74.76% for GroupDRO (best discrete baseline). On the WildTime-Yearbook benchmark, CIL achieves 71.22% OOD accuracy, surpassing the previous SOTA (Fine-tuning at 69.62%) by 1.6%. On continuous CMNIST with 1024 domains, CIL reaches 67.2% OOD accuracy while all existing IRM variants drop to near-random (47–58%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CIL consistently outperforms all baselines across every dataset. On Alipay Auto-scaling, CIL(L2) beats the strongest discrete baseline IRMv1 (66.49%) by 4.8% and the strongest continuous baseline HRM (55.44%) by 15.9%, while ERM suffers the largest OOD drop (57.38%). On HousePrice, CIL(L2) improves over the best continuous baseline HRM (77.40%) by 1.9% and over GroupDRO (74.76%) by 4.5%; notably, IIBNet collapses to near-random (47.48%) in the discrete setting. On Insurance Fraud, CIL(L1) leads by 3.0% over HRM (73.52%) and 3.7% over REx (72.90%). On WildTime-Yearbook, CIL (71.22%) surpasses Fine-tuning (69.62%), CORAL-T (68.53%), and SWA (67.90%), confirming gains on a large-scale vision task. The theoretical analysis (Proposition 1) explains why REx and IRMv1 degrade sharply as domain count increases: with 1024 domains on CMNIST, REx drops to 51.7% and IRMv1 to 50.4%, while CIL maintains 67.2%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Invariant Risk Minimization (IRM) and its many variants (REx, IRMv1, IB-IRM, IRMx, IGA, BIRM, Fisher, SparseIRM) from their native discrete-domain setting to naturally continuous domain indices. The key conceptual departure is reformulating the invariance constraint: instead of aligning E^t[y|Φ(x)] across domains (which requires accurate per-domain estimates), CIL aligns E^y[t|Φ(x)] across classes, which is estimable even when each domain has very few samples. The adversarial minimax formulation with two domain regressors (h and g) draws on the game-theoretic structure of IRM Game but applies it to a fundamentally different quantity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a practical gap that affects any application where the domain index is naturally continuous (time-of-day in cloud auto-scaling, age in insurance, year in vision), showing that all existing IRM methods degrade to near-random performance as the number of domains grows. The theoretical result that REx fails with constant probability when |T| ≥ O(√n) provides a clear diagnostic for practitioners. Limitations include the assumption of discrete class variables, the use of finite discrete time points as proxies for truly continuous domains, and the acknowledged sensitivity of IRM-style methods to overfitting in large networks. Future work on combining CIL with spurious feature diversification for foundation models is an open direction.
