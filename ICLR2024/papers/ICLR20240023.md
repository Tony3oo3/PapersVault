## Problem

Conjugate gradient methods are well-studied in Euclidean spaces and on Riemannian manifolds, but no decentralized version existed for distributed optimization over the Stiefel manifold. Moreover, existing Riemannian conjugate gradient algorithms rely on computationally expensive operations such as retractions, exponential maps, and vector transports, which hinder practical deployment in multi-agent settings.

## Input / Output

Each agent i holds a local data matrix A_i ∈ R^{m_i × d} and an initial point x_{i,0} on the Stiefel manifold St(d,r) (i.e., x^T x = I_r with r ≤ d). The network topology is encoded by a doubly stochastic adjacency matrix W over an undirected connected graph, and a fixed number t of consensus communication steps is chosen per iteration. The algorithm outputs a consensus point x* ∈ St(d,r) shared by all agents, with convergence measured by the Riemannian gradient norm at the induced arithmetic mean and the distance to the global optimum under the quotient metric d_s.

## Method

DRCGD replaces both retractions and vector transports with nearest-point projection operators onto the Stiefel manifold and its tangent space, making each iteration retraction-free and vector-transport-free. The position update performs a t-step consensus via W^t followed by a gradient step along the search direction, then projects back onto St(d,r). The search direction update combines the negative Riemannian gradient with a Fletcher-Reeves-type β coefficient multiplied by the tangent-space projection of the decentralized consensus of previous search directions (Σ_j W_ij^t η_{j,k}), thereby achieving both conjugate-gradient momentum and inter-agent consensus in a single projection. Global convergence to a stationary point is established under an extended assumption bounding the projection-based inner product by the corresponding vector-transport inner product, yielding a convergence rate of O(1/√K) for the gradient norm.

## Results

The paper evaluates DRCGD on synthetic eigenvector problems (d=10, r=5, m_i=1000, eigengap Δ=0.8) and the MNIST dataset (d=784, r=5, n=20 agents), measuring consensus error, gradient norm, objective gap, and distance to the global optimum d_s(x̄_k, x*). Results are presented as convergence plots (Figures 1–4) rather than numerical tables. Across all tested configurations (varying agent counts n∈{16,32}, consensus steps t∈{1,10,∞}, and graph topologies Ring/ER), DRCGD consistently reaches the early-termination threshold d_s ≤ 10⁻⁵ in fewer iterations than both baselines. On MNIST with n=20 and a Ring graph, DRCGD converges substantially faster than DRDGD and DPRGD, whose trajectories are nearly identical to each other.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DRCGD against two first-order decentralized Riemannian methods: DRDGD (Chen et al., 2021, retraction-based) and DPRGD (Deng & Hu, 2023, projection-based). On synthetic data, DRCGD converges faster than DPRGD for both n=16 and n=32 agents (Figure 1), and its advantage grows with the number of consensus steps t, whereas DRDGD and DPRGD show little sensitivity to t (Figure 2). On graph topology, DRCGD benefits from the denser ER(p=0.6) graph over the sparser Ring graph, while DRDGD and DPRGD show nearly identical trajectories regardless of topology (Figure 3). On MNIST (Figure 4), DRCGD converges much faster than both DRDGD and DPRGD, which exhibit almost the same convergence speed to each other. In all four figures and across all four metrics (consensus error, gradient norm, objective gap, distance to optimum), DRCGD dominates both baselines, attributed to its conjugate-gradient search direction that simultaneously achieves consensus and avoids expensive retractions or vector transports.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Riemannian conjugate gradient framework of Sato (2022) to a decentralized multi-agent setting, building directly on the decentralized Riemannian gradient descent of Chen et al. (2021) and the projection-based update strategy of Deng & Hu (2023). The key conceptual departure is replacing the expensive vector transport (or parallel translation) traditionally required to move search directions between tangent spaces with a simple orthogonal projection onto the tangent space, while retaining the Fletcher-Reeves beta formula in a decentralized consensus form. This yields the first retraction-free and vector-transport-free decentralized Riemannian conjugate gradient algorithm on the Stiefel manifold.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result is practically significant for large-scale distributed systems (PCA, eigenvalue estimation, dictionary learning) where agents operate over the Stiefel manifold and computational budget per node is constrained, since eliminating retractions and vector transports reduces per-iteration cost. However, the convergence guarantee relies on an extended assumption (Eq. 20) bounding the projected consensus direction by the differentiated-retraction transport, which is not verified for all practical settings, and the analysis is restricted to the Stiefel manifold rather than general compact submanifolds. Open questions include extending the method to general compact submanifolds, removing the bounded-beta requirement, and developing online/streaming variants on Riemannian manifolds.
