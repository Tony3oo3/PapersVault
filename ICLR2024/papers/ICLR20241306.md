## Problem

COMBINER, a Bayesian INR-based codec that jointly optimizes rate-distortion via the β-ELBO, suffers from three key limitations: its fully-factorized Gaussian posterior underfits data (contradicting the overfitting goal), it cannot adapt to local deviations from global patterns, and its performance is highly sensitive to model choices and variational parameter initialization. These issues prevent it from matching state-of-the-art codecs on established benchmarks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241306/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts arbitrary signals represented as coordinate-to-value mappings: RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10, 768×512 Kodak split into 64×64 patches), audio (48,000-sample clips split into 60 patches of 800 samples), video (128×128×24 clips split into 16×16×24 patches), and 3D protein Cα coordinates (96 residues). It produces a compressed bitstream via relative entropy coding (A* coding) of a weight sample from the variational posterior, with reconstruction achieved by evaluating the INR at each coordinate. High-resolution signals are processed patch-wise with a three-level hierarchical model, and encoding is parallelized across patches.

## Method

RECOMBINER introduces three key extensions to COMBINER: (1) a learned linear reparameterization w = h_w·A that yields a full-covariance-like posterior over INR weights while retaining mean-field computational cost, since A is learned during training and fixed at inference; (2) learnable positional encodings where a low-dimensional latent h_z is upsampled by a small convolutional network φ to provide coordinate-local information to the INR, enabling it to capture fine details without overfitting global weights; and (3) a three-level hierarchical Bayesian model (global → group → patch) over the latent weight representations h_w, where each level models deviations from the parent via factorized Gaussians, enabling information sharing across patches. Training uses coordinate descent with adaptive β tuning to hit a target bitrate, and a random permutation strategy across patches at each hierarchical level enables adaptive bit allocation and parallel encoding.

## Results

RECOMBINER is evaluated via rate-distortion (RD) curves across four modalities. On CIFAR-10 (32×32 images), it outperforms VAE-based codecs at low bitrates and most INR-based codecs across all bitrates. On Kodak (768×512 images, compressed in 64×64 patches), it is competitive with the more complex VC-INR method and significantly narrows the gap to VAE-based codecs. On LibriSpeech audio, it matches VC-INR and outperforms both COIN++ and MP3. On UCF-101 video, it achieves performance comparable to H.264/H.265 at lower bitrates. Ablation studies quantify individual contributions: linear reparameterization yields up to 4 dB PSNR gain on CIFAR-10 at high bitrates and >0.5 dB on Kodak; learnable positional encodings contribute up to 2 dB on Kodak at low bitrates; and the cross-patch permutation strategy provides >1.5 dB at higher bitrates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against COMBINER (Guo et al., 2023), RECOMBINER clearly outperforms on all modalities, with the gap most visible on audio (Figure 13 in Appendix F) and on robustness to model size (Figure 5a). On CIFAR-10, RECOMBINER surpasses COIN (Dupont et al., 2021) and COIN++ (Dupont et al., 2022) across the bitrate range, and notably beats VAE-based codecs (Ballé et al., 2018; Cheng et al., 2020) at low bitrates (<~1 bpp). On Kodak, it outperforms COIN, COIN++, and MSCN (Schwarz & Teh, 2022), is competitive with VC-INR (Schwarz et al., 2023), and reduces the gap to VAE-based methods and classical codecs (VTM, BPG, JPEG2000) but does not fully close it. On LibriSpeech audio, RECOMBINER matches VC-INR and outperforms COIN++ and MP3. On UCF-101 video, it is comparable to H.264 and H.265 at lower bitrates but falls behind when those codecs are configured for best quality. On 3D protein structure (AlphaFold DB), RECOMBINER is competitive with domain-specific codecs Foldcomp, PDC, and PIC, while offering tunable rate-distortion trade-offs that the baselines do not support.

## Relation to Prior Work

RECOMBINER is a direct extension of COMBINER (Guo et al., 2023), which first enabled joint rate-distortion optimization for INR-based compression via a variational Bayesian framework and relative entropy coding. It departs from COMBINER in three conceptual ways: replacing the factorized Gaussian posterior with a linearly-transformed factorized Gaussian (inspired by Oktay et al.'s reparameterization but adapted to the INR compression setting where the transform is learned jointly across many posteriors), augmenting the INR input with learned positional encodings to decouple local detail from global weight patterns, and introducing a hierarchical Bayesian model over patch-level INRs to capture cross-patch dependencies. Within the broader INR compression family (COIN, COIN++, MSCN, VC-INR), RECOMBINER is the only method that avoids quantization entirely, encoding a continuous weight sample via A* coding rather than discretizing and entropy-coding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that an INR-based codec can outperform VAE-based nonlinear transform coding on low-resolution images at low bitrates is notable, as VAE codecs have been the dominant paradigm for learned image compression. The ablation finding that the hierarchical model *without* positional encodings can degrade performance is a useful cautionary result, suggesting that richer priors require complementary architectural changes to be optimizable. The primary open limitation is encoding time (~60–90 minutes per image on a single A100 GPU), which is orders of magnitude slower than VAE-based codecs and severely limits practical deployment. Future work on exact relative entropy coding (e.g., greedy Poisson rejection sampling) and reducing the number of parameters to optimize (e.g., via FiLM-style modulations) could address this bottleneck.
