## Problem

Existing generative RPM solvers either fail to parse interpretable attributes and attribute-changing rules, introduce artificial priors in representation learning, or rely on candidate sets during training that enable shortcut learning. Moreover, most cannot handle discrete rules or abstract global rules shared across RPMs, and are limited to predicting only the bottom-right cell. This paper addresses the gap of building a generative solver that automatically learns interpretable latent concepts, abstracts shared atomic rules, and generates answers at arbitrary positions without requiring distractors in training.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 3×3 grayscale RPM matrix where a subset of cells are given (context images) and one or more cells are missing (target images to predict). During training, optional rule annotations specifying the rule type per attribute (e.g., [Constant, Arithmetic, Distribute Three]) can be provided for a small fraction of samples (5% for non-grid configurations). The output is a generated grayscale image at each missing position, produced by decoding C independent latent concept vectors; the model also exposes the selected atomic rule per concept and the concept-level prediction errors, enabling interpretability tasks like odd-one-out detection.

## Method

RAISE is a conditional deep latent variable model that decomposes RPM reasoning into concept learning, abstract reasoning, and image generation. A CNN encoder extracts C independent latent concepts per image without attribute meta-information; a fully convolutional network h with K global learnable parameter sets ψ (the 'global knowledge set' of atomic rules) predicts target concepts by executing the selected rule on the 3×3 representation matrix. Rule selection uses categorical indicators whose variational posterior combines a context-only prior with a likelihood computed by executing each of the K rules and comparing to the encoded target, preventing model collapse. Training maximizes an ELBO comprising reconstruction loss, a concept regularizer (KL between predicted and encoded target concepts), and a rule regularizer (KL between posterior and prior rule distributions), plus an optional auxiliary loss supervised by rule annotations via a Jonker-Volgenant assignment that maps latent concepts to attributes. This semi-supervised design requires only 5% rule annotations for non-grid configurations and no candidate sets, supporting generation at arbitrary and multiple missing positions.

## Results

On bottom-right answer selection across 7 RAVEN/I-RAVEN configurations, RAISE achieves 90.0%/92.1% average accuracy, outperforming the strongest generative baseline PrAE (80.0%/85.7%) by ~10 points. RAISE reaches near-perfect accuracy on non-grid configurations (97.6–99.3% on RAVEN) and substantially outperforms all baselines on grid configurations (e.g., 77.7% vs. 23.2% for PrAE on 3×3Grid). On held-out OOD configurations, RAISE retains 99.2% on Center-Held-Out and 56.1% on O-IC-Held-Out, the highest among all compared solvers. For arbitrary-position generation, RAISE maintains >99% selection accuracy with 1–15 distractors on non-grid scenes, while baselines drop to 20–60%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAISE outperforms all seven baselines on the average bottom-right selection accuracy. Against the strongest generative solver PrAE (80.0%/85.7%), RAISE gains ~10 points on average, with the largest gap on 3×3Grid (77.7% vs. 23.2%) where PrAE collapses; PrAE remains competitive on Center and L-R (within 2 points). ALANS (54.3%/62.8%) and all three GCA variants (≤32.7%/41.7%) are outperformed by 35–60+ points across nearly all configurations. Among arbitrary-position baselines, Transformer (40.1%/64.0%) is the strongest but drops to 14.7%/25.2% on 2×2Grid and 11.6%/18.6% on 3×3Grid, while RAISE maintains 68.2%/71.3% and 77.7%/78.7% respectively. CLAP (17.5%/32.8%), ANP (7.3%/27.6%), and LGPP (6.4%/16.3%) are consistently outperformed by 50–85+ points. Notably, RAISE achieves these results using only 5% rule annotations on non-grid configurations (semi-supervised), whereas PrAE and ALANS require full rule annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAISE extends the line of DLVM-based RPM solvers by Shi et al. (LGPP, CLAP), which modeled underlying rules as continuous global latent concepts, by introducing discrete atomic rules stored in a global knowledge set and selected via categorical indicators per latent concept. It departs from powerful generative solvers like ALANS and PrAE, which rely on full rule annotations and candidate sets during training, by operating under semi-supervised settings (5% annotations) and requiring no distractors. The compositional decomposition into independent latent concepts and concept-specific rule execution draws on object-centric representation learning and hierarchical Bayesian modeling, while the Jonker-Volgenant assignment for concept-attribute correspondence is a practical addition not seen in prior RPM work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that generative abstract reasoning with interpretable latent concepts and atomic rules can handle realistic RPM datasets with discrete attributes—a setting where prior DLVM approaches (LGPP, CLAP) struggled—while requiring only a small fraction of rule annotations. The held-out configuration experiments provide clean evidence of compositional generalization (applying a rule learned on one attribute to a novel attribute), and the odd-one-out task shows that concept-level prediction errors yield interpretable diagnostics. Key limitations include significant performance degradation on noisy grid configurations (2×2 Grid, 3×3 Grid) where multiple valid solutions exist, and the approach remains confined to RPM-style structured reasoning rather than open-ended visual reasoning.
