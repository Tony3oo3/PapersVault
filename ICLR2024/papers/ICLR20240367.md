## Problem

State-of-the-art visual localization pipelines rely on an image retrieval step that degrades significantly under weather, seasonal, and time-of-day changes, yet existing approaches either target a single domain shift or use generic pixel-level augmentations that do not address these specific adversarial conditions. No prior work has leveraged text-to-image generative models to automatically synthesize targeted scene alterations for training retrieval models while preserving the location-specific geometry required for instance-level recognition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240367/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At training time, the method consumes matching image pairs from SfM-120K (ResNet50 backbone, 768×512 random crops) along with 11 synthetic variants per image generated by InstructPix2Pix at 768px resolution using 20 diffusion steps. At test time, a single query image is encoded into a 128-dim global descriptor (SPoC-pooled local features) and matched against a database of mapping images via ASMK over 3000 local features at 7 scales, producing a ranked list of top-k neighbors whose poses are combined (EWB) or used for 2D-3D matching against a COLMAP SfM model to estimate the 6-DoF camera pose.

## Method

Ret4Loc builds on HOW's contrastive margin loss over aggregated global features but introduces three key changes: (1) strong data augmentations (AugMix, domain randomization, AdamW with cosine schedule) to learn more robust features; (2) synthetic variant generation via InstructPix2Pix using 11 textual prompts covering dawn/dusk/night, rain/snow/sun, and winter/summer, where synthetic tuples replace the query and negatives with variants while keeping the positive original, and features from all variants are aggregated before computing a single contrastive loss (Eq. 2); (3) a geometric consistency score computed as the ratio of LightGlue correspondences preserved after synthetic alteration, used both to filter out geometrically inconsistent variants (threshold τ) and to perform geometry-aware sampling that upweights harder valid tuples. This combination of generative data augmentation with geometry-based quality control is, to the authors' knowledge, the first use of text-to-image models for producing and validating targeted scene alterations in an instance-level recognition task.

## Results

On RobotCar Seasons v2 (EWB top-1), Ret4Loc-HOW-Synth++ achieves 31.3% at 0.5m/5° and 76.4% at 5m/10°, improving over the HOW baseline (29.8%/74.4%) by +1.5 and +2.0 points respectively. On Extended CMU Seasons test, the best model reaches 34.2%/91.2% (0.5m/5° / 5m/10°) versus HOW's 30.5%/87.9%. On Tokyo 24/7 place recognition, Ret4Loc-HOW-Synth++ attains R@1 of 91.1% and R@10 of 97.1%, surpassing HOW (89.2%/96.5%) and FIRe (84.8%/92.7%). With LightGlue re-ranking, Ret4Loc-HOW-Synth++ achieves 42.4%/98.3% on ECMU-test, outperforming Patch NetVLAD (36.2%/96.2%) and SP-SuperGlue (30.7%/96.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ret4Loc-HOW-Synth++ consistently outperforms the HOW baseline across all three main benchmarks: +1.5 pts on RobotCar-v2 (0.5m/5°), +3.7 pts on ECMU-test (0.5m/5°), and +1.9 pts on Tokyo 24/7 R@1. Against FIRe, the gains are even larger, with +5.3 pts on RobotCar-v2 and +6.3 pts on ECMU-test at the 0.5m/5° threshold. The method also surpasses the recent GCL place recognition method by a wide margin (e.g., 34.2% vs 18.2% on ECMU-test 0.5m/5°), suggesting that synthetic variant training could benefit other contrastive-learning-based retrieval methods. With LightGlue re-ranking, Ret4Loc-HOW-Synth++ achieves the best results on ECMU-test (42.4%/98.3%) and Tokyo 24/7 (R@1 97.5%, R@10 98.4%), beating Patch NetVLAD by +6.2 pts on ECMU and +11.5 pts on Tokyo R@1, while SP-SuperGlue remains competitive on RobotCar-v2 (35.4% vs 38.6% at 0.5m/5°). The paper notes that concurrent work by Aiger et al. (2023) replacing ASMK with CANN matching is orthogonal and complementary to Ret4Loc training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the HOW landmark retrieval framework (Tolias et al., 2020) and tailors its training to long-term visual localization, a task where retrieval must be robust to weather, seasonal, and time-of-day shifts. It departs from prior approaches that handle these domain shifts via custom per-condition models, physical simulations (synthetic fog, rain rendering), or paired image translation, by instead using InstructPix2Pix (Brooks et al., 2023) to generate targeted variants from simple textual prompts. The key conceptual departure is the geometric consistency score, which exploits the matching-pair structure of retrieval training data to automatically validate that generative alterations preserve location-specific geometry — a quality-control mechanism not present in prior generative-augmentation work (Sarıyıldız et al., 2023; Dunlap et al., 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to use text-to-image generative models for producing and validating targeted scene alterations in an instance-level recognition task, and the geometric consistency filtering provides a general-purpose quality-control signal that could transfer to other vision tasks where matching pairs are available. The practical impact is significant: consistent 1–5% gains across six outdoor/indoor localization benchmarks, with the technique being relatively cheap (one day of generation, ~8 hours training on a single A100). Limitations include dependence on generative model fidelity (indoor images produce failures), the ~3× overhead of data generation over training, and the fact that success is bounded by the expressivity of the underlying diffusion model. Open questions include whether on-the-fly generation during batch construction becomes feasible as models get faster, and whether the geometric consistency principle extends to tasks without natural matching pairs.
