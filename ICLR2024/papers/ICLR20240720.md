## Problem

Graph Transformers (GTs) with self-attention and Message-Passing GNNs with Virtual Nodes (MPGNN+VN) are two competing architectures for global information exchange in graph learning, but their relative expressivity has been unclear. Prior universality results for GTs relied on the non-uniform setting where network size may depend on graph size, masking whether self-attention offers a genuine architectural advantage. This paper asks: in the uniform setting where a single fixed network must handle graphs of all sizes, does either architecture subsume the other?

## Input / Output

Both architectures consume featured graphs (vertex feature vectors over a domain S^d) optionally augmented with precomputed positional encodings such as LapPE or RWSE, and produce either graph-level scalar/vector predictions (via a readout aggregation) or node-level feature maps. The theoretical analysis restricts to edgeless graphs with two vertex types (features (2,1) and (2,2)) to isolate the global-computation mechanism, while experiments use Erdős-Rényi random graphs for synthetic tasks and real-world superpixel, peptide, code, and molecular graphs for benchmarking.

## Method

The paper establishes that non-uniform universality is not unique to GTs: with injective positional encodings, even 2-layer MLPs and 1-layer MPGNNs are universal approximators, reducing the question to uniform expressivity. In the uniform setting, the authors prove neither GPS nor MPGNN+VN is a universal approximator (via an NP-hardness reduction), then show incomparability by constructing separating functions: GPS cannot compute |V|² because softmax attention produces bounded weighted averages regardless of graph size, while MPGNN+VN can via sum aggregation over all nodes; conversely, MPGNN+VN cannot compute a specific rational function of node-type counts that GPS expresses exactly through a single attention head, because a virtual node only sees an aggregated value rather than individual node features. These theoretical separations are validated with synthetic experiments showing out-of-distribution generalization gaps, and real-world benchmarks (LRGB, OGB) where neither architecture consistently outperforms the other.

## Results

On PascalVOC-SP, GatedGCN+VN achieves 0.4477 F1, slightly surpassing GPS (0.4440) and all other methods. On OGB-MolPCBA, GatedGCN+VN reaches 0.3141 AP, the best result across all architectures, outperforming GPS (0.2907) and LGI-GT (0.3040). On COCO-SP, GPS retains a clear lead (0.3884 F1) over GatedGCN+VN (0.3244), while on OGB-code2 LGI-GT (0.1948) edges out GPS (0.1894) and GatedGCN+VN (0.1855). On the Peptides datasets, all methods are within ~0.01 of each other, with GatedGCN+VN achieving the best Peptides-Func AP (0.6823).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GatedGCN+VN outperforms GPS on PascalVOC-SP (0.4477 vs. 0.4440 F1), Peptides-Func (0.6823 vs. 0.6534 AP), and OGB-MolPCBA (0.3141 vs. 0.2907 AP), while GPS clearly wins on COCO-SP (0.3884 vs. 0.3244 F1) and OGB-code2 (0.1894 vs. 0.1855 F1). Against SAN, GatedGCN+VN dominates on PascalVOC-SP (0.4477 vs. 0.3230) and COCO-SP (0.3244 vs. 0.2592), and also leads on Peptides-Func (0.6823 vs. 0.6439). Compared to Exphormer, GatedGCN+VN wins on PascalVOC-SP (0.4477 vs. 0.3975) and Peptides-Func (0.6823 vs. 0.6527), while Exphormer leads on COCO-SP (0.3454 vs. 0.3244). Against LGI-GT, GatedGCN+VN achieves a higher OGB-MolPCBA AP (0.3141 vs. 0.3040) but trails on OGB-code2 (0.1855 vs. 0.1948). Overall, no single architecture dominates across all six benchmarks, consistent with the paper's theoretical finding that GPS and MPGNN+VN have incomparable uniform expressivity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Kreuzer et al. (2021), who proved non-uniform universal function approximation for graph transformers with positional encodings, and on Cai et al. (2023), who showed MPGNN+VN can approximate self-attention in the non-uniform setting. The key departure is shifting the analysis to the uniform setting, where a single network must handle all graph sizes, and proving that GPS and MPGNN+VN are incomparable in expressivity. The virtual node mechanism traces back to Gilmer et al. (2017), and the GPS architecture to Rampašek et al. (2022), but the paper's contribution is the rigorous uniform-expressivity separation rather than any new architectural component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper corrects a widespread misconception that graph transformers are uniquely universal approximators, showing that non-uniform universality is a property of positional encodings plus non-uniformity, shared even by 2-layer MLPs. The uniform expressivity incomparability result provides a principled theoretical basis for why neither architecture dominates, and the empirical finding that GatedGCN+VN achieves SOTA on MolPCBA while being significantly more efficient than GPS has direct practical implications for model selection. Open questions include whether the theoretical gaps (unbounded aggregation vs. individual node access) manifest more broadly on real-world tasks, and whether hybrid or alternative global-computation mechanisms can subsume both architectures.
