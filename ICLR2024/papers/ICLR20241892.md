## Problem

Existing NL4Opt approaches rely on two-stage NER-to-formulation pipelines that fail on complex real-world operations research problems containing implicit constraints, domain-specific terminologies (e.g., 'backlogging', 'lot-sizing'), and long reasoning chains. No prior LLM-based method could simultaneously handle these three challenges, leaving a gap between elementary LP benchmarks and industrially relevant optimization tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241892/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-form natural language description of a complex OR problem (often containing implicit constraints and specialized terminology) and produces executable Python Gurobi code that solves the optimization problem. Intermediate outputs include mathematical optimization models and LP files. Evaluation is performed via automated code execution against expert-annotated test cases, measuring accuracy, compile error rate, and runtime error rate rather than relying on manual expert assessment.

## Method

Chain-of-Experts (CoE) is a training-free multi-agent framework comprising 11 specialized LLM-based experts (terminology interpreter, modeling experts, programming experts, code reviewer, etc.), each augmented with domain-specific knowledge bases accessed via top-k retrieval. A Conductor orchestrates expert selection through forward thought construction, modeled as a sequential decision-making policy implemented via prompt engineering rather than learned parameters. After forward construction, a Reducer synthesizes all expert comments into a final answer, which is then evaluated by an external code execution environment. If evaluation fails, a backward reflection mechanism backpropagates the error signal in reverse expert order, allowing each expert to self-reflect and identify where the mistake originated. The forward-backward loop iterates until the solution passes all test cases or a maximum trial count is reached.

## Results

On LPWP, Chain-of-Experts (CoE) achieves 58.9% accuracy, surpassing the strongest baseline Reflexion (50.7%) by 8.2 percentage points, while also attaining the lowest compile error rate (3.8%) and runtime error rate (7.7%). On the newly introduced ComplexOR benchmark, CoE reaches 25.9% accuracy (solving 10 out of 37 problems), substantially outperforming the best baseline ReAct (14.6%) and Reflexion (13.5%), with the lowest CE rate (7.6%) and RE rate (6.4%). The traditional tag-BART model, which won the NeurIPS 2022 NL4Opt competition, achieves only 47.9% on LPWP and 0% on ComplexOR, confirming the infeasibility of prior approaches for complex OR problems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoE outperforms all 8 baselines on both datasets across all three metrics (accuracy, CE rate, RE rate). On LPWP, the closest competitor is Reflexion (50.7% accuracy), which benefits from its self-reflection mechanism to achieve the second-lowest CE rate (7.3%), but CoE still leads by 8.2 points in accuracy and 3.5 points in RE rate. On ComplexOR, ReAct (14.6%) and Reflexion (13.5%) are the strongest baselines; ReAct's advantage stems from its ability to access external knowledge bases, while Reflexion's single-agent reflection is insufficient for the multi-step reasoning required. The traditional tag-BART model (NeurIPS 2022 competition winner) achieves 0% on ComplexOR, demonstrating that prior NL4Opt methods cannot handle implicit constraints and domain-specific terminologies. Solo Performance Prompting, despite also using a multi-agent paradigm, underperforms (7.0% on ComplexOR) because its agents lack effective coordination. Ablation results confirm that removing the Conductor, Reducer, or backward reflection each degrade accuracy by 2.3–4.7 points on LPWP, validating the necessity of the full multi-agent orchestration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NL4Opt task (translating natural language OR problem descriptions into mathematical formulations and code) from elementary linear programming problems to complex, real-world OR problems with implicit constraints, domain-specific terminology, and long reasoning chains. It departs from prior two-stage NER-to-formulation pipelines (tag-BART, ensemble CRF+T5) and from single-agent LLM reasoning methods (CoT, ToT, GoT, ReAct, Reflexion) by introducing a structured multi-agent framework where a Conductor dynamically selects specialized experts with domain knowledge bases, and a backward reflection mechanism backpropagates feedback through the expert stack. The multi-agent design draws conceptual inspiration from Solo Performance Prompting and CAMEL, but differs in its use of a learned (prompt-engineered) sequential policy for expert selection, per-expert knowledge bases, and recursive backpropagation of evaluation feedback rather than single-LLM self-reflection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first LLM-based approach specifically targeting complex OR problems, and the introduced ComplexOR dataset (37 annotated problems with test cases) provides a much-needed benchmark beyond elementary LP instances. The Conductor-plus-backward-reflection architecture offers a generalizable pattern for multi-agent reasoning in knowledge-intensive, multi-step domains. However, absolute performance on ComplexOR remains low (25.9% accuracy, solving only 10/37 problems), the dataset is small, and the Conductor's effectiveness depends heavily on prompt engineering rather than learned optimization. Open questions include whether the framework scales to even more complex or multi-objective problems, whether the Conductor policy can be learned end-to-end, and whether the approach generalizes beyond OR to other structured reasoning domains.
