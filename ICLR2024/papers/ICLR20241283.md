## Problem

Pruning large vision-language models (LVLMs) faces a dilemma: global Hessian-based pruning is computationally infeasible for billion-parameter models, while existing layer-wise one-shot methods (SparseGPT, Wanda) apply a uniform sparsity ratio across all layers, ignoring the severe weight/gradient distributional imbalances between vision and language modules. This lack of global perspective leads to suboptimal compression, especially at high sparsity regimes where some layers are far more critical than others.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the weights of a pre-trained modular VL model (e.g., BLIP-2 with EVA-ViT + FlanT5), a small calibration set of 32–128 image-text pairs (from CC3M, ImageNet, or C4), and a target global sparsity ratio p. It outputs a one-shot pruned model with adaptive per-layer sparsity ratios, where the lightweight Q-Former (~5% of parameters) is kept intact and only the vision encoder and language model are sparsified. No iterative retraining is required during pruning; optional fine-tuning can follow.

## Method

ECoFLaP is a two-stage coarse-to-fine pruning framework. In the coarse step, it computes a global importance score for each layer using zeroth-order gradients estimated via the forward-forward algorithm (perturbing weights with Gaussian noise and measuring loss differences), which avoids backpropagation and reduces GPU memory by ~40% compared to first-order gradients. These scores are normalized and converted into per-layer sparsity ratios via a linear allocation that respects a maximum per-layer sparsity cap (p_max = p + 0.1) to prevent layer collapse. In the fine step, it applies layer-wise unstructured pruning using Wanda's local importance score (weight magnitude × input activation norm) with the globally-informed adaptive sparsity for each layer, processing layers sequentially from input to output. The key insight is that zeroth-order forward-forward gradients provide a memory-efficient proxy for global weight correlations, enabling adaptive sparsity without the Hessian or full backpropagation cost of global pruning.

## Results

On BLIP-2 at 50% sparsity, ECoFLaP with zeroth-order gradient achieves a macro-average score of 58.0 across VQA, captioning, and retrieval tasks, compared to 57.4 for SparseGPT and 57.2 for Wanda. The zeroth-order variant uses only 8.93 GB of GPU memory during pruning, a 60% reduction over the first-order variant (22.4 GB) while maintaining near-identical accuracy. On LLaMA 7B at 60% sparsity, ECoFLaP achieves a perplexity of 9.83 on WikiText versus 10.68 for Wanda (10.6% relative improvement). On CLIP at 40% sparsity, ECoFLaP with SparseGPT reaches 61.0 average accuracy across 11 classification datasets, outperforming SparseGPT alone (58.7) and Wanda alone (47.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SparseGPT (layer-wise Hessian-based), ECoFLaP with zeroth-order gradient improves the macro-average by 1.4% (58.0 vs. 57.4), with the largest gains on VQA (58.4 vs. 57.4) and captioning CIDEr (110.2 vs. 108.1), while slightly trailing on retrieval (95.5 vs. 96.4 TR@1). Against Wanda (layer-wise magnitude-based), ECoFLaP wins on every metric: VQA by 0.5 pts, CIDEr by 3.3 pts, and IR@1 by 1.2 pts, with the gap widening to 9.6% on VQA at 60% sparsity. Against UPop (unified progressive pruning with re-training), ECoFLaP achieves 79.1 vs. 76.9 on NLVR2 val and 132.3 vs. 128.9 CIDEr on COCO captions with fine-tuning, while requiring no re-training during the pruning phase itself. Notably, ECoFLaP with zeroth-order gradient matches the memory footprint of layer-wise methods (~9 GB) while delivering global-pruning-level quality, whereas the first-order variant requires 22.4 GB—making the zeroth-order approximation the practical choice for large models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the layer-wise one-shot pruning paradigm (SparseGPT, Wanda) by introducing a two-stage coarse-to-fine framework that first allocates adaptive per-layer sparsity using a global importance score, then performs local layer-wise pruning. It departs from expensive global Hessian-based pruning by replacing second-order information with a zeroth-order gradient computed via the forward-forward algorithm (Hinton, 2022; Malladi et al., 2023), which avoids backpropagation entirely. The work also addresses a gap specific to multimodal models: the severe distributional imbalance in weight magnitudes and gradients between vision and language modules, which causes uniform-sparsity layer-wise pruning to underperform. To the authors' knowledge, this is the first unified sparse approximate solver designed for vision-language multimodal architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ECoFLaP provides a practical, memory-efficient path to compressing large VL models (BLIP-2, CLIP) at high sparsity without the prohibitive cost of global Hessian computation, reducing GPU memory by up to 40% while matching or exceeding first-order baselines. The finding that local importance scores from layer-wise methods are actually worse than a uniform sparsity ratio for determining per-layer allocation is a useful negative result that clarifies why naive layer-wise approaches plateau. Open questions include whether the zeroth-order approximation remains reliable for even larger or architecturally different multimodal models, and whether the one-shot setting can be extended with lightweight iterative refinement to push sparsity further without fine-tuning.
