## Problem

Static connectomes (synaptic, gap-junction, neuromodulatory) cannot capture the dynamic, state-dependent reorganization of neural circuits, where different neurons flexibly join and leave functional communities across time and behavioral contexts. Prior approaches to inferring functional connectivity relied on global correlation measures or step-by-step statistical tests that fail to model the full system of pairwise similarities across multiple animals simultaneously, leaving open how to discover which groups of neurons form transient functional modules and when.

## Input / Output

The method consumes brain-wide calcium activity traces from 189 sensory and inter-neurons recorded across 21 individual C. elegans worms (via NeuroPAL), along with stimulus timing information for three chemosensory cues (NaCl, 2-butanone, 2,3-pentanedione). Rather than operating on raw traces directly, the pipeline first converts them into a 3-way tensor of shape TIME × WORMS × PAIRWISE AFFINITIES (where the affinity dimension has n(n−1)/2 entries from the upper-triangular portion of the symmetric affinity matrix). The output is a set of R=15 tensor components, each decomposing into an affinity pattern (which neuron pairs interact), a temporal factor (when the pattern is active), and a worm factor (which animals exhibit it), with the affinity factors further converted into weighted graphs whose communities constitute the dynamic functional connectome.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-stage unsupervised pipeline that organizes pairwise connections rather than individual neurons. First, a local differential affinity is computed between each neuron pair by comparing the unsigned areas under overlapping derivative 'bumps' (intervals of constant-sign monotonic change), yielding a Jaccard-like similarity in [0,1] that is sensitive to coincident activity changes regardless of sign (capturing both excitatory and inhibitory interactions). Second, these time-varying affinity matrices are stacked into a 3-way tensor (TIME × WORMS × PAIRWISE AFFINITIES) and decomposed via non-negative tensor factorization (NTF) using the HALS algorithm, automatically discovering contiguous time intervals during which a given affinity pattern is preserved across multiple worms without requiring manual window selection. Third, each affinity factor is reshaped into a weighted adjacency matrix and passed to the nested weighted stochastic block model (NWSBM), a Bayesian generative model that infers the number of communities via description-length minimization and MCMC model averaging, requiring no hyperparameter tuning. The key architectural insight is that tensor factorization over affinities (rather than raw traces) introduces non-linearity at the similarity computation stage while letting the factorization handle temporal and inter-animal structure simultaneously.

## Results

On the weighted-LFR benchmark (9 network types, N=125 nodes), NWSBM achieves the highest mean NMI on 6 of 9 networks (e.g., 0.65 on Net 4, 0.54 on Net 6, 0.64 on Net 9), ties NNSED on Net 5 (0.39), and is outperformed by NNSED on Net 7 (0.25 vs. 0.39) and by Louvain/Combo/GNNS100 on Net 8 (0.51 vs. 1.0). Experimentally, silencing the AWB neuron predicted by the algorithm to participate in salt sensing increased NaCl avoidance by 25% (p = 5.7×10⁻¹¹, N = 12 vs. 12 samples), validating the method's ability to predict causal neural interactions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NWSBM (the method adopted in this paper) is compared against five baselines: Louvain, Combo, AS, NNSED, and GNNS100. NWSBM achieves the best NMI on 6 of 9 network types, with particularly large margins on Net 4 (0.65 vs. next-best AS at 0.47) and Net 9 (0.64 vs. NNSED at 0.45). NNSED is the closest competitor, matching NWSBM on Net 5 (both 0.39) and outperforming it on Net 7 (0.39 vs. 0.25). Louvain, Combo, and GNNS100 all achieve a perfect 1.0 NMI on Net 8 (a network with very few, large communities), but perform poorly on most other types (e.g., Louvain scores only 0.04 on Net 4). AS is competitive on Net 4 (0.47) but generally trails NWSBM by 0.1–0.3 NMI across the remaining networks. Overall, NWSBM provides the most consistently strong performance across diverse weighted network topologies, which is critical for the paper's application to neural affinity graphs with heterogeneous edge weights.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two previously unrelated components—non-negative tensor factorization (NTF) and the nested weighted stochastic block model (NWSBM, Peixoto 2018)—into a unified pipeline for discovering time-varying neural communities. It departs from prior C. elegans functional connectivity studies (correlation-based, small-motif, or predictive-model approaches) by organizing pairwise non-linear affinities rather than individual neuron traces, and by formulating the data as a TIME × WORMS × PAIRWISE AFFINITIES tensor rather than the more natural WORMS × NEURONS × TIME tensor. The differential affinity measure, computed from local derivative overlaps, is a novel similarity metric that captures state-dependent interactions missed by global correlation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The approach provides a principled, unsupervised framework for inferring which groups of neurons form functional circuits at specific behavioral epochs, moving beyond static connectome descriptions that cannot explain state-dependent encoding. The experimental validation (silencing AWB increased salt avoidance by 25%, contradicting its canonical role as an aversive olfactory neuron) demonstrates that the method can predict non-obvious causal interactions. Limitations include reliance on the NeuroPAL imaging pipeline, testing in a single small-brain organism, and a single validation experiment; open questions include scalability to larger brains, applicability to fMRI or other modalities, and whether the tensor component count selection strategy generalizes.
