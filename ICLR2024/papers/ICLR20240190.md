## Problem

Existing generative error correction (GER) benchmarks for ASR using LLMs lack specificity on noisy conditions, which are the most common in real-world deployment. While LLM-based GER achieves strong gains on clean speech, performance improvements in noisy scenarios remain limited because the LLM has no awareness of the audio noise that caused the recognition errors. The paper asks whether LLMs can be taught to perform denoising on N-best hypotheses in language space, analogous to how robust ASR systems denoise audio features before recognition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240190/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The primary input is a list of N=5 ASR hypotheses generated by Whisper Large-V2 beam search decoding (beam size 50) from noisy speech, formatted as an instruction template with the 1-best hypothesis and remaining candidates. For the distillation stage, parallel noisy and clean speech audio are also required to extract Whisper encoder embeddings (1280-dim). The output is a single corrected transcription generated autoregressively by the finetuned LLM using greedy decoding (temperature 0.2, top-1 sampling). The noise embedding is computed entirely from the text of the N-best list via SBERT, avoiding direct audio-to-LLM cross-modality integration.

## Method

RobustGER extracts a language-space noise embedding from the N-best hypotheses by measuring their diversity at two granularities: utterance-level (concatenated SBERT sentence-embedding differences across all hypothesis pairs) and token-level (summed SBERT token-embedding differences after forced alignment, analogous to edit distance). This embedding is incorporated into LLaMA-Adapter tuning via element-wise subtraction from the adapter prompt, where the minus sign encodes a 'denoise' instruction. To enhance the embedding's fidelity to actual audio noise, a knowledge distillation stage uses MINE (mutual information neural estimation) to maximize mutual information between the language embedding and noisy Whisper encoder embeddings while minimizing it with clean embeddings, training a linear tuner that modulates the language embedding. The key insight is that worse noise conditions produce higher ASR decoding uncertainty and thus more diverse N-best hypotheses, making list diversity a natural language-space proxy for audio noise level that avoids the cross-modality gap of directly injecting audio embeddings into LLM finetuning.

## Results

On the proposed Robust HyPoradise benchmark, RobustGER with LLaMA-2-7b achieves up to 53.9% relative WER reduction over the Whisper Large-V2 ASR baseline on CHiME-4 (avg. WER 5.9% vs. 12.8%), and consistent gains across all five noisy datasets. The method also generalizes to clean speech, achieving 30.0% WER reduction on clean LS-FreeSound test data. With only 5k training pairs (half the full 9.6k), RobustGER maintains near-full performance (6.3% vs. 5.9% avg. WER on CHiME-4), demonstrating strong data efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RobustGER consistently outperforms the Whisper Large-V2 ASR baseline across all five noisy datasets, with the largest gains on CHiME-4 (53.9% avg. WER reduction) and VB-DEMAND (17.7%), while gains are more modest on the extremely challenging RATS radio-noise dataset (5.5%). Compared to the original GER (HyPoradise) without noise conditioning, RobustGER improves WER by 1.0–2.3 percentage points on average across datasets, with the most notable advantage on VB-DEMAND unseen noise (10.7% vs. 11.9%) and NOIZEUS (12.6% vs. 13.8%), where noise-awareness matters most. The audio-space denoising baseline (+Audio Denoising) provides only marginal improvement over vanilla GER and is consistently outperformed by RobustGER's language-space denoising, confirming the cross-modality gap issue. In the case study (Table 5), RobustGER corrects a phonetically confusable error ('write ups' → 'write offs') that both ChatGPT in-context learning (TAP) and standard GER fail to fix, achieving 0.0% WER versus 15.4% for both baselines. On clean speech, RobustGER still achieves 30.0% WER reduction, demonstrating that the language-space noise embedding does not degrade performance when no noise is present.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GER (Generative Error Correction) benchmark established by Chen et al. (2023b) from clean to noisy ASR conditions, drawing a direct analogy to noise-robust ASR methods that denoise speech features before recognition. Rather than incorporating audio embeddings into LLM finetuning (which prior multimodal LLM work shows degrades performance due to cross-modality gap), it repurposes the LLaMA-Adapter framework (Zhang et al., 2023b) with a novel language-space noise embedding extracted from N-best hypothesis diversity. The audio noise distillation component borrows MINE (Belghazi et al., 2018) from the mutual information estimation literature, applying it in a new context of transferring noise information across modalities without direct audio-to-text alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RobustHP dataset (113K pairs across five noise domains) fills a clear gap in the GER benchmark ecosystem, and the language-space denoising concept offers a generalizable principle for incorporating non-textual conditioning signals into LLMs without suffering cross-modality degradation. The data efficiency finding (5k pairs approaching full-data performance) is practically valuable for deployment. Open questions include whether the N-best diversity proxy generalizes to other ASR backends or non-speech tasks, whether the instruction template sensitivity noted by the authors can be resolved, and whether the approach extends to streaming or low-resource settings where N-best lists may be less informative.
