## Problem

Joint shape matching (JSM) methods require sufficiently large input collections with similar neighboring shapes, typically decouple pairwise from joint matching, and store costly explicit correspondence matrices. GenCorres addresses all three by learning a shape generator that produces a much larger synthetic shape collection, unifying pairwise and joint matching through local geometric priors between adjacent synthetic shapes, and encoding correspondences implicitly in the generator's latent space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241291/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unorganized collection of 3D shapes (raw meshes or point clouds, e.g., 1000 human shapes from DFAUST or 289 animal shapes from SMAL) with no pre-defined inter-shape correspondences. It outputs a mesh generator (Chebyshev convolutional network mapping latent codes to template vertex displacements) that directly provides consistent dense correspondences between any pair of input shapes, plus an implicit VAE generator (PointNet encoder, 8-layer MLP decoder) that enables shape interpolation. The implicit generator is discretized via Marching Cubes on a ~64×77×64 voxel grid, simplified to ~2000 faces for regularization computation.

## Method

GenCorres proceeds in three stages. Stage I learns an implicit shape generator (SALD-style VAE) regularized by two novel losses: a geometric deformation loss that enforces ARAP+ACAP preservation on induced correspondences between adjacent implicit surfaces (computed via a linearly constrained quadratic program exploiting the single normal-direction constraint of implicit representations), and a cycle-consistency loss penalizing 3-cycle distortion among adjacent shape triplets. Stage II initializes a mesh generator by propagating template correspondences along linearly interpolated latent paths, using sequential ARAP registration between intermediate shapes. Stage III refines the mesh generator by minimizing Chamfer distance to input shapes plus an ACAP deformation prior between adjacent generated meshes, enabling the generator to capture large non-isometric deformations while maintaining geometric coherence.

## Results

On the primary JSM task, GenCorres achieves mean/median geodesic errors of 1.30/1.13 cm on DFAUST and 1.02/0.47 cm on SMAL, reducing errors by 62.0%/66.8% and 14.3%/58.0% respectively over the best JSM baseline (CZO). For shape generation quality, GenCorres attains the lowest reconstruction errors (1.83/1.75 cm on DFAUST, 6.85/6.81 cm on SMAL), outperforming the best implicit baseline SALD by 2.7%/10.6% and 2.3%/7.0% in mean/median. On the FAUST pairwise benchmark, GenCorres achieves 1.6 cm error, matching the best spectral learning methods (ULRSSM, NM) and outperforming all template-based approaches (3D-CODED at 2.5 cm, IFS at 2.6 cm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenCorres decisively outperforms both state-of-the-art JSM methods: CZO (Huang et al., 2020a) and MIM (Gao et al., 2021), reducing mean geodesic error by 62% on DFAUST and 14% on SMAL. Against the best pairwise method NeuroMorph (Eisenberger et al., 2021), GenCorres achieves 48% lower mean error on DFAUST (1.30 vs 2.49 cm) and 36% lower on SMAL (1.02 vs 1.59 cm), demonstrating that the data-driven generator approach with local geometric regularization surpasses direct pairwise matching. For shape generation, GenCorres beats all implicit baselines (SALD, DeepSDF) and point-based baselines (LGF, DPM) on reconstruction error, with the largest gains on SMAL (6.85 vs 7.66 cm mean vs SALD). On FAUST pairwise matching, GenCorres ties the top spectral learning methods (ULRSSM, NM at 1.6 cm) while outperforming all template-based methods (3D-CODED at 2.5 cm, IFS at 2.6 cm) and axiomatic methods (BCICP at 6.4 cm, ZO at 6.1 cm). The ablation in Table 2 confirms that the geometric regularization (ARAP+ACAP) is the most critical component: removing it degrades DFAUST error from 1.30 to 7.65 cm, while removing cycle-consistency causes a more modest 8% increase.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenCorres builds directly on SALD's VAE architecture for the implicit generator and ARAPReg's Chebyshev-convolution mesh generator for the explicit generator, but its core contribution is a new coupled training framework that unifies joint shape matching (JSM) and pairwise matching under a single generative model. It departs from classical JSM methods (CZO, MIM) by replacing explicit low-rank matrix recovery with local geometric regularizations (ARAP/ACAP) between adjacent synthetic shapes in the generator's latent space, and from prior shape generators (3D-CODED, point-based GANs) by enforcing cycle-consistency and shape-preserving deformations rather than relying on pre-defined correspondences or permutation-invariant point clouds. The key conceptual shift is that the generator's latent space itself becomes the 'shape collection' over which JSM operates, making the synthetic collection arbitrarily large relative to the input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a compact, differentiable encoding of consistent correspondences via the mesh generator, addressing the storage and scalability bottlenecks of classical JSM. A notable limitation is the requirement for a reasonably large input collection (hundreds of shapes), which restricts applicability to small shape sets where pairwise methods remain preferable. Open questions include defining the geometric regularizations without mesh discretization (currently relying on Marching Cubes), extending the approach to man-made shapes with topological variation, and incorporating intrinsic features to close the remaining gap with spectral learning methods in self-intersection regions.
