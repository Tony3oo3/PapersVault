## Problem

Existing human motion stylization methods operate directly in raw pose space, which is redundant and noisy, and are limited to deterministic outputs with rigid designs that support only one conditioning mode (reference motion or label). They also struggle to generalize across unseen datasets. This paper asks whether a generative framework in the latent space of pretrained autoencoders can unify motion-based, label-based, and prior-based stylization while improving content preservation and cross-dataset generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240313/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 3D motion sequence of 260-D pose vectors (21 joints, 30 FPS) as content, optionally paired with a reference style motion, a discrete style label, or neither (prior-based mode). The content is first encoded by a pretrained 1D-CNN autoencoder into a compact latent code with 4× temporal downsampling (T_z = T/4, D_z = 512). The output is a stylized 3D motion sequence of arbitrary length, with global root velocity predicted separately by a lightweight 1D-CNN regressor from the generated local joint motion.

## Method

The framework is two-stage: a pretrained 1D-CNN autoencoder (with L1 sparsity and temporal smoothness penalties) maps motions to latent codes, and a latent stylization network decomposes each code into a deterministic content code (via a content encoder that erases global statistics through instance normalization) and a probabilistic style code (a Gaussian distribution N_s(μ_s, σ_s) from a style encoder conditioned on an optional label). A convolutional generator recombines them via AdaIN at each layer to produce a valid motion code. Training enforces autoencoding, cycle-consistency reconstruction after swapping content and style between random pairs, and a novel homo-style alignment loss (KL divergence between style spaces of sub-clips from the same sequence) to improve disentanglement. The probabilistic style space enables diverse outputs at inference and supports unconditional re-stylization by sampling from N(0,I) without any style input.

## Results

On the supervised motion-based setting, Ours(A) achieves 94.5% style accuracy and 0.344 geodesic distance on Aberman et al. 2020, and 91.8% style accuracy with 0.569 geodesic distance on the unseen CMU Mocap dataset, substantially outperforming Park et al. 2021 (89.1%/0.531 and 67.4%/0.663) and Aberman et al. 2020 (54.7%/0.804 and 44.5%/0.910). In the unsupervised motion-based setting, Ours(V) reaches 84.0% style accuracy on Aberman and 82.8% on CMU, beating Jang et al. 2022 (83.3% and 79.3%) while also improving content preservation (0.478 vs 0.559 geodesic distance on Aberman). On the zero-shot Xia et al. 2015 dataset, Ours(A) achieves 92.6% style accuracy and 67.4% content accuracy in supervised motion-based stylization, far exceeding Park et al. 2021 (52.7%/44.1%) and Aberman et al. 2020 (36.4%/31.8%). The method is also 14× faster than Jang et al. 2022 (4.76 ms vs 67.56 ms per 160-frame forward pass).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Aberman et al. 2020 (supervised, motion-based), the proposed method wins decisively on all metrics: style accuracy jumps from 54.7% to 94.5% on Aberman and from 44.5% to 91.8% on CMU, while geodesic distance (content preservation) improves from 0.804 to 0.344 and 0.910 to 0.569 respectively. Against Park et al. 2021 (supervised, motion-based and label-based), the proposed method achieves higher style accuracy on CMU (91.8% vs 67.4% motion-based; 91.5% vs 81.3% label-based) and dramatically better content preservation (0.569 vs 0.663 geodesic distance; 0.571 vs 0.693), though Park et al. retains a slight edge in style accuracy on the in-domain Aberman dataset for label-based (97.1% vs 94.2%). Against Jang et al. 2022 (unsupervised, motion-based), the proposed method wins on style accuracy (84.0% vs 83.3% on Aberman; 82.8% vs 79.3% on CMU) and content preservation (0.478 vs 0.559 geodesic distance on Aberman; 0.672 vs 0.725 on CMU), while also being 14× faster at inference. On the zero-shot Xia et al. 2015 dataset, the gap widens further: Ours(A) reaches 92.6% style accuracy and 67.4% content accuracy in supervised motion-based, versus 52.7%/44.1% for Park et al. and 36.4%/31.8% for Aberman et al., confirming superior generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the motion style transfer lineage (Aberman et al. 2020, Park et al. 2021, Jang et al. 2022) by shifting the stylization operation from raw pose space into the latent space of a pretrained 1D-convolutional motion autoencoder, drawing on the broader trend of latent-space content synthesis (Stable Diffusion, Guo et al. 2022a). It departs from prior deterministic style codes by introducing a probabilistic Gaussian style space, and unifies supervised (motion-based, label-based) and unsupervised (motion-based, prior-based) stylization into a single framework via AdaIN conditioning and cycle-consistency. The homo-style alignment loss is a novel self-supervised signal that exploits temporal coherence within a single motion sequence to regularize the style space without requiring style annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 14× speedup over the prior SOTA (Jang et al. 2022) combined with zero-shot generalization to unseen datasets (CMU, Xia) makes latent-space stylization a practical option for real-time character animation pipelines. The unified framework's flexibility—supporting diverse outputs from a single content code, label-conditioned generation, and unconditional re-stylization—addresses a gap where prior methods were rigid in their conditioning options. Limitations include reliance on a single training dataset (Aberman et al. 2020) for style space learning, which constrains the diversity of achievable styles, and the fact that the autoencoder must be pretrained on a large corpus before stylization can begin. Open questions include whether the approach scales to richer style taxonomies and whether the two-stage (autoencoder + stylizer) design can be replaced by a more efficient single-stage alternative without the catastrophic training instability observed in end-to-end experiments.
