## Problem

Existing head avatar reconstruction methods face a fundamental trade-off: 2D warping and mesh-based approaches lack multi-view consistency or cannot capture non-facial details like hair, while NeRF-based methods either require per-identity optimization or GAN inversion, preventing generalization to unseen identities. The key unresolved challenge is achieving both faithful identity reconstruction from a single image and precise, detail-preserving expression control in a single forward pass without test-time optimization.

## Input / Output

The method accepts one or an arbitrary number of 512×512 RGB images as source identity input, along with FLAME expression and pose parameters for driving and a camera pose for rendering. It produces 512×512 reenactment images via a two-stage pipeline: low-resolution (128×128) volume rendering followed by a learned super-resolution network. The system operates in a single forward pass at approximately 15 FPS on an A100 GPU, supporting both same-identity reenactment and cross-identity driving without any per-identity fine-tuning.

## Method

GPAvatar employs a two-branch architecture combining a Point-based Expression Field (PEF) with a canonical tri-planes representation. The PEF assigns learnable 32-dim features to each of FLAME's 5023 vertices; at query time, it retrieves the K=8 nearest points from the entire space (not a local neighborhood), applies frequency positional encoding to relative offsets, and aggregates features with inverse-distance weights, thereby preserving expression details that would be lost through 3DMM parameter encoding/decoding. The canonical encoder, adapted from GFPGAN's UNet+StyleGAN structure, maps input images to 3×32×256×256 tri-planes capturing identity and non-facial regions (hair, shoulders). A Multi Tri-planes Attention (MTA) module uses a learnable query tri-plane to attend across multiple input tri-planes, enabling flexible fusion of N images to fill occlusions or missing regions (e.g., closed eyes). The two feature spaces are concatenated and passed through dense layers to predict density and RGB for two-pass hierarchical volume rendering, followed by a super-resolution network. Training is fully end-to-end on VFHQ (240K frames) with L1, perceptual, and density-norm losses over 150K iterations (~50 GPU-hours on 2 A100s).

## Results

On VFHQ (self-reenactment), GPAvatar One-in achieves PSNR 22.08, SSIM 0.765, LPIPS 0.177, and AED 0.434, outperforming the strongest baseline HideNeRF (PSNR 20.07, SSIM 0.745, LPIPS 0.204, AED 0.521) by a clear margin. On the unseen HDTF dataset, the advantage widens: GPAvatar One-in reaches PSNR 24.21 and AED 0.427 versus HideNeRF's 21.38 and 0.499, demonstrating strong generalization without any fine-tuning. The expression control metrics (AED, AKD) show the largest relative improvements, with GPAvatar reducing AKD from 5.33 (HideNeRF) to 3.53 on VFHQ and from 4.33 to 3.06 on HDTF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPAvatar One-in outperforms all five baselines on every synthesis quality metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, L1) and every expression/pose control metric (AED, APD, AKD) on both VFHQ and HDTF. The closest competitor is HideNeRF, which is the most similar in setup (tri-planes + 3DMM deformation); GPAvatar improves PSNR by ~2 dB and reduces AED by ~0.09 on VFHQ, with even larger gaps on HDTF (PSNR +2.83, AED −0.07). On identity consistency (CSIM), HideNeRF slightly edges out GPAvatar One-in on both datasets (0.794 vs 0.789 on VFHQ; 0.907 vs 0.871 on HDTF), suggesting a minor trade-off between identity preservation and expression fidelity. The 2D warping baseline StyleHeat and mesh-based ROME trail significantly on all metrics, with StyleHeat showing the worst expression control (AED 0.593 on VFHQ). NeRF-based baselines OTAvatar and Next3D, which rely on GAN inversion or per-identity optimization, are the weakest overall, confirming the advantage of the single-forward-pass generalizable approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

GPAvatar sits at the intersection of tri-planes-based NeRF head avatars (GOAvatar, NOFA, HideNeRF) and point-based neural radiance fields (Point-NeRF), but departs from prior one-shot methods by decoupling identity (tri-planes) from expression (a dynamic FLAME point cloud field) rather than encoding both through 3DMM parameters or rendered images. The Multi Tri-planes Attention module extends the single-image tri-planes paradigm to arbitrary multi-image fusion, addressing a gap where prior methods like HideNeRF and GOAvatar only accept one input. The canonical encoder borrows the GFPGAN/StyleGAN architecture, and the overall pipeline follows the NeRF volume-rendering + super-resolution recipe established by recent head avatar work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By avoiding the encode-decode bottleneck of 3DMM parameters, GPAvatar demonstrates that direct geometric point clouds can serve as a more faithful expression driver, a design choice likely to influence future avatar systems. The multi-input MTA capability is practically important for real-world scenarios with occlusions or closed eyes, and the single-forward-pass inference (no per-identity optimization) makes the approach deployable. Open limitations include the absence of shoulder/body control, no explicit manipulation of non-FLAME regions like hair, and pre-real-time speed (~15 FPS), leaving the path to fully real-time, full-body avatars unresolved.
