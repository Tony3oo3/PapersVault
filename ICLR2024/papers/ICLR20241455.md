## Problem

Stress-optimal graph drawing is computationally challenging, and existing neural approaches like DeepGD rely on fully connected message passing with precomputed all-pairs shortest paths, incurring quadratic complexity that makes them infeasible for large graphs. The paper addresses how to achieve state-of-the-art drawing quality with a GNN-based framework that scales sub-quadratically to large graph instances.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241455/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an undirected connected graph and initializes node features with Laplacian eigenvector positional encodings, sine-cosine encoded distances to randomly selected beacon nodes, and random features. It outputs 2D (or 3D) node positions constrained to [0,1]^d via sigmoid and rescaled by a closed-form scale-invariant factor α, along with latent node embeddings that can serve as positional encodings for downstream graph learning tasks. The latent embeddings carry more structural information than the decoded positions alone.

## Method

CoRe-GD builds a spectral-preserving coarsening hierarchy (O(log|V|) levels) and performs a global-first layout optimization: starting from the coarsest supernode graph, it iteratively uncontracts and refines embeddings at finer levels. At each level, a recurrent GNN with GRU convolution alternates between message passing on the original topology (Conv_E) and on a positionally rewired graph (Conv_R), where the rewiring connects nodes that are spatially close in the current decoded layout but far apart in graph topology, enabling direct information exchange with only linear overhead. Training uses a scale-invariant stress loss (with a closed-form optimal scaling factor) and a replay buffer that stores intermediate latent embeddings, allowing the network to be trained on short unrolls while running hundreds of recurrent convolutions at inference without destabilizing.

## Results

CoRe-GD achieves the lowest scale-invariant stress (lower is better) on 5 out of 6 datasets: Rome (233.17), ZINC (5.11), MNIST (129.10), PATTERN (3067.02), and CLUSTER (2827.81). On CIFAR-10, the classical (sgd)² method slightly edges out CoRe-GD (262.52 vs. 262.68). On the scalability test with SuiteSparse graphs, CoRe-GD with hierarchical coarsening achieves stress 42639, outperforming PivotMDS (65404) and sfdp (48386) while remaining competitive with neato (43110) and (sgd)² (42435).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoRe-GD outperforms all six baselines on five of six datasets, with the sole exception being CIFAR-10 where (sgd)² achieves a marginally lower stress (262.52 vs. 262.68). Against the strongest classical baseline (sgd)², CoRe-GD wins on Rome (233.17 vs. 233.49), ZINC (5.11 vs. 5.14), MNIST (129.10 vs. 129.19), PATTERN (3067.02 vs. 3181.51), and CLUSTER (2827.81 vs. 2920.36). Among learned methods, DeepGD—which uses message-passing on a fully connected graph with precomputed pairwise distances—consistently underperforms CoRe-GD on every dataset, despite its quadratic complexity. The mixed-training variant CoRe-GD-mix, trained on a combination of all datasets, achieves near-parity with dataset-specific CoRe-GD and even surpasses all baselines on PATTERN (3066.31), demonstrating generalizability across graph distributions. On the SuiteSparse scalability benchmark, CoRe-GD with coarsening (42639) substantially outperforms PivotMDS (65404) and sfdp (48386), while DeepGD could not be trained or run at all due to GPU memory limitations from its fully-connected message-passing.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoRe-GD sits at the intersection of classical multi-level graph drawing (Meyerhenke et al., 2017; Brandes & Pich, 2007) and learning-based GNN approaches (DeepGD, Wang et al. 2021; DNN², Giovannangeli et al. 2021). It departs from prior neural methods by replacing their quadratic-complexity fully-connected message passing with a hierarchical coarsening strategy borrowed from classical algorithms, combined with a novel positional rewiring technique that adds only linear overhead. The scale-invariant stress loss and the replay buffer for training deep recurrent GNNs are additional departures from prior self-supervised graph drawing formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first neural architecture for graph drawing that achieves sub-quadratic runtime, closing a gap that previously required sophisticated handcrafted multi-level algorithms. The positional rewiring technique and the replay buffer for recurrent GNNs are general-purpose tools that could transfer to other geometric graph problems beyond visualization. Limitations include degradation on out-of-distribution graphs beyond training size, the computational cost of stress evaluation for large graphs, and the fact that performance gains over the best classical baselines (e.g., (sgd)²) remain modest on small graphs. Open questions include whether the latent embeddings can serve as broadly useful positional encodings and whether the framework extends to dynamic or attributed graphs.
