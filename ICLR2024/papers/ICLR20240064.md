## Problem

Pre-trained LLMs are constrained by a fixed context window, and existing training-free extensions (positional encoding scaling like PI, NTK, YaRN) do not address the quadratic computational cost of self-attention, making long-context inference prohibitively memory-intensive. No prior method simultaneously extends the effective context length and reduces the self-attention computation without requiring expensive retraining.

## Input / Output

The method takes an arbitrarily long text sequence (demonstrated up to 64k tokens on Llama-2 with a native 4k limit) and divides it into uniform chunks, each augmented with shared prefix (instruction) and suffix (ending tokens) so every chunk retains global context. It produces two outputs: (1) compact, fixed-length layer-wise embeddings that replace the standard kv-cache for autoregressive generation, and (2) the generated text itself. The chunk size is bounded at half the model's native context limit, and the final embedding length per layer is uniform across all layers after propagative refinement.

## Method

HOMER employs a divide-and-conquer strategy where long inputs are split into chunks processed independently through early transformer layers, then progressively merged at deeper layers via concatenation. Before each merge, a calibrated attention-based token pruning step (using significance scores that subtract position-bias logits from raw attention logits) reduces chunk length to keep computation bounded. A propagative refinement step propagates upper-layer pruning decisions down to lower layers, yielding uniform-length embeddings per layer that integrate seamlessly as kv-cache. A depth-first computation order over the binary merge tree reduces peak memory from linear to logarithmic in input length. The entire scheme is training-free, reuses position IDs across chunks rather than scaling them, and is orthogonal to RoPE-scaling methods (PI, NTK, YaRN), stacking on top of them for additional gains.

## Results

On passkey retrieval with Llama-2-7b-chat, HOMER achieves 77.6% accuracy at 32K context (8× the original 4K limit), while the best baseline (YaRN at 64K) achieves only 2.6%. On Llama-2-13b-chat, HOMER reaches 80.4% at 32K versus 22.4% for YaRN. On the QuALITY question-answering benchmark, HOMER improves accuracy from 32.7% (plain Llama) to 35.8%, and NTK+HOMER reaches 38.8%. In language modeling on PG-19, HOMER is the only method maintaining reasonable perplexity (9.64 for 7b, 7.59 for 13b) at 64K tokens, while all baselines exceed 10² perplexity. Peak GPU memory is reduced by over 70% at 64K inputs (21.3 GB vs >80 GB for baselines).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HOMER decisively outperforms all three RoPE-scaling baselines (PI, NTK, YaRN) on passkey retrieval at context lengths beyond 8K. PI degrades to near-zero accuracy at 16K and is effectively unusable beyond its nominal limit. NTK shows moderate performance at 8K (81.2% for 7b) but collapses to 0% at 16K and 32K. YaRN is the strongest baseline, maintaining 69.6% at 16K and 2.6% at 32K, yet still trails HOMER by 75+ points at 32K. On QuALITY question answering, NTK (37.9%) slightly edges out Plain+HOMER (35.8%) at its 8K limit, but NTK+HOMER achieves the best overall accuracy of 38.8%, confirming the methods are complementary. In language modeling, HOMER is the only method that avoids catastrophic perplexity blowup (>10²) at 32K–64K, whereas YaRN and NTK both degrade severely beyond 16K–32K. The paper also demonstrates that HOMER is orthogonal to RoPE scaling: combining HOMER with YaRN yields consistent additional gains across all tasks and context lengths.

## Relation to Prior Work

HOMER repurposes token reduction techniques from efficient vision transformers (Bolya et al., 2022) for the first time in the LLM context-extension setting, combining them with a hierarchical divide-and-conquer merging strategy. It departs from prior divide-and-conquer methods (SLED, Unlimiformer, LongMem) that process chunks independently by progressively merging adjacent chunks across transformer layers, preserving cross-chunk information. The method is explicitly orthogonal to positional encoding scaling approaches (PI, NTK, YaRN), which address context extension without reducing the quadratic attention cost, and can be stacked on top of them for additional gains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HOMER offers a practical, training-free path to long-context inference on existing pretrained LLMs with logarithmic memory scaling, making extended contexts feasible on a single GPU where baselines exceed 80 GB. The open question is whether small-data finetuning (which the authors note is compatible) could close the remaining performance gap at 32k+ tokens, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the Llama-2 family tested here. A broader limitation is that the method still shows non-trivial degradation (100% → 80% passkey accuracy from 4k to 32k), suggesting information loss in the hierarchical compression that future work may need to address.
