## Problem

Existing traffic forecasting models commit to a single spatial modeling strategy (static graph, dynamic graph, or attention), yet different strategies excel under different traffic conditions—static graphs handle recurring patterns well while dynamic graphs capture non-recurring events. No prior work enables in-situ, context-aware selection among diverse spatial modeling methods during inference, leaving models unable to adapt to spatially isolated roads, highly interconnected networks, or sudden events simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The model consumes T' historical graph signals (N roads × C features, z-score normalized) along with temporal features (time-of-day encoded via Time2Vec) and produces T future graph signals of the same shape. For METR-LA and PEMS-BAY (5-min intervals), T'=T=12 (1-hour windows); for EXPY-TKY (10-min intervals), T'=T=6. The output is a single top-1 expert prediction per node per time step, selected by the gating network rather than an ensemble.

## Method

TESTAM is a Mixture-of-Experts model with three transformer-based experts that differ only in their spatial modeling layer: an identity expert (temporal-only), an adaptive expert (learnable static graph via a memory-augmented meta-graph learner with a hyper-network), and an attention expert (dynamic spatial attention with no spatial restrictions). Each expert block contains temporal attention, spatial modeling, a time-enhanced attention layer that transfers information from the source (historical) domain to the target (future) domain using Time2Vec embeddings of future timestamps as keys, and a feed-forward network. The gating network queries a shared meta-node bank with input signals and computes routing probabilities from the similarity between each expert's output representation and the queried memory, selecting the top-1 expert. Training reformulates routing as a classification problem with two cross-entropy losses using regression-error-derived pseudo labels: a fine-grained worst-route avoidance loss (per-point, penalizing the selected expert when error exceeds a q-th quantile) and a node-wise best-route selection loss (rewarding the selected expert when error falls below the (1−q)-th quantile), enabling both avoidance of poor routing and active pursuit of optimal routing.

## Results

On METR-LA (60-min horizon), TESTAM achieves MAE 3.36, RMSE 7.09, and MAPE 9.67%, outperforming the closest competitor MegaCRN (MAE 3.38, RMSE 7.23, MAPE 9.72%). On PEMS-BAY (60-min), TESTAM reaches MAE 1.85, RMSE 4.33, MAPE 4.31%, again edging out MegaCRN (MAE 1.88, RMSE 4.42, MAPE 4.41%). On the large-scale EXPY-TKY dataset (60-min), TESTAM achieves MAE 6.75, RMSE 11.01, MAPE 31.01%, surpassing MegaCRN (MAE 6.83, RMSE 11.04, MAPE 31.02%) and all other 12 baselines. The advantage is most pronounced at longer prediction horizons, where RNN-based and attention-based baselines degrade significantly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TESTAM outperforms all 12 baselines on RMSE and MAPE across all three datasets at the 60-min horizon, with the largest margins over RNN-based models (DCRNN, AGCRN) and CNN-based STGCN. The closest competitor is MegaCRN (Jiang et al., 2023), which marginally wins on MAE at the shortest horizon (e.g., METR-LA 15-min: 2.52 vs. 2.54; PEMS-BAY 15-min: 1.28 vs. 1.29; EXPY-TKY 10-min: 5.81 vs. 5.84) but loses on RMSE and MAPE at all horizons, with the gap widening at 60-min (e.g., PEMS-BAY RMSE: 4.33 vs. 4.42). Learnable static graph models (Graph-WaveNet, MTGNN, CCRNN) are competitive at short horizons but degrade faster at 60-min, confirming the paper's claim that static graphs struggle with non-recurring patterns. Attention-based models (GMAN, STTN) show particular weakness on EXPY-TKY, where their uniformly distributed attention fails to capture spatial dependencies in the 1,843-road network. Memory-augmented PM-MemNet performs well at intermediate horizons but is outperformed by TESTAM at 60-min on all three datasets. Overall, TESTAM's advantage is most pronounced on the large-scale EXPY-TKY dataset and at longer prediction horizons, supporting the hypothesis that in-situ spatial modeling with diverse graph structures is critical for complex road networks.

## Relation to Prior Work

TESTAM extends the Mixture-of-Experts framework (Shazeer et al., 2017; Dryden & Hoefler, 2022) to traffic forecasting by assigning each expert a fundamentally different spatial modeling strategy—identity (temporal-only), learnable static graph (à la Graph-WaveNet), and dynamic attention (à la GMAN)—rather than the identical architectures typical of prior MoE work. The routing mechanism builds on Spatial MoE's pseudo-label classification loss but adds a second best-route selection loss and a memory-based gating network inspired by pattern-matching memory networks (Lee et al., 2022) and meta-graph learning (Jiang et al., 2023). The time-enhanced attention layer and Time2Vec embedding depart from standard Transformer encoders to eliminate autoregressive decoding while preserving periodic temporal structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that no single spatial modeling method dominates across all traffic conditions, and that in-situ routing among heterogeneous experts yields consistent gains—particularly on large-scale networks (EXPY-TKY) and non-recurring events—validating a practical design principle for spatio-temporal forecasting systems. However, the improvements over the strongest baseline (MegaCRN) are modest (1–3% MAE), the routing mechanism introduces additional hyperparameters (error quantile q, memory size m), and the approach has only been validated on traffic speed forecasting, leaving open whether the heterogeneous-expert MoE paradigm generalizes to other graph-structured time-series tasks. The computational efficiency (224k parameters, second-fastest inference) is a practical advantage that may encourage adoption.
