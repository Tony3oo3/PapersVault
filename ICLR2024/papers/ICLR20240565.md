## Problem

Training LiDAR-based 3D object detectors requires large amounts of expensive 3D annotations, and existing self-supervised pre-training methods are dominated by contrastive learning with ad hoc pair construction. More fundamentally, there is no explicit connection between the raw point cloud input and the bounding box labels, leaving the pre-training objective misaligned with what the backbone actually needs to learn for detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240565/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pre-training, the backbone receives only the LiDAR point cloud (N×3 coordinates in ego-car frame), while the color decoder additionally receives one-hot quantized color labels (128 bins via K-Means) for a 20% seed subset of points. The backbone's output is a set of N×D point embeddings whose format is unchanged from standard detectors (PointRCNN, IA-SSD, PV-RCNN), so the pre-trained weights drop directly into any downstream 3D detector for fine-tuning to produce 3D bounding boxes.

## Method

GPC pre-trains a point-based backbone (e.g., PointNet++) by learning to colorize LiDAR points, framed as a 128-class classification problem with Balanced Softmax loss to handle the heavy ground-pixel imbalance. The key mechanism is a 'hint' strategy: ground-truth colors of a 20% seed subset are injected as additional input to a separate PointNet++ color decoder, reducing the conditional entropy P(Y|X) and forcing the backbone to learn which points should share colors (i.e., segment together) rather than memorizing exact RGB values. This design simultaneously mitigates inherent color variation across scenes and selection bias in the data, since the decoder can 'cheat' on the seed points while the backbone must still discover the spatial grouping. The color decoder is discarded at fine-tuning time, leaving the backbone with semantically meaningful embeddings that directly facilitate 3D object detection.

## Results

On KITTI (in-domain, pre-trained on KITTI), GPC with PointRCNN achieves 62.7% overall moderate AP on just 5% labeled data, surpassing the 55.2% from scratch by +7.5 points. The headline result is that GPC on 20% KITTI (70.7% mod AP) already outperforms training from scratch on 100% KITTI (69.7% mod AP). On the full dataset, GPC still yields a +1.2 point gain (70.9 vs. 69.7). Cross-domain (Waymo→KITTI), GPC improves 5% KITTI from 55.2% to 63.9% mod AP and 20% from 63.5% to 70.1%, again beating 100% scratch (69.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPC consistently outperforms all contrastive learning baselines. Against PointContrast (Xie et al., 2020), GPC wins on every subset: at 5% KITTI, GPC achieves 62.7% mod AP vs. PointContrast's 47.9% (which actually underperforms scratch at 55.2%), and at 100% KITTI, GPC edges ahead at 70.9% vs. 70.6%. Against ProposalContrast (Yin et al., 2022) in the cross-domain Waymo→KITTI setting, GPC leads by 3.9 points at 20% (70.1 vs. 66.2) and 1.5 points at 50% (70.7 vs. 69.2). On PV-RCNN (Table 3), GPC achieves 73.58% overall AP at 100% KITTI, surpassing PatchContrast (72.97%), ALSO (72.96%), and ProposalContrast (72.92%). Against cross-modal image-based methods (PPKT, SLidR, TriCC) on 5% KITTI, GPC gains +7.5 to +8.7 points over scratch, substantially exceeding their gains of +2.2 to +4.7 points. The only setting where GPC is not clearly superior is 100% KITTI with PointRCNN in-domain, where the margin over PointContrast is a narrow +0.3 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant contrastive learning paradigm for 3D point cloud pre-training (PointContrast, ProposalContrast, DepthContrast) by reframing pre-training as a supervised colorization task that leverages the associated RGB modality. It draws conceptual inspiration from image colorization as a proxy task for visual understanding (Larsson et al., 2017) and from multi-modal representation learning, but applies it to LiDAR point clouds with a key twist: the 'grounded' hint mechanism that injects ground-truth colors into a seed subset of points to resolve inherent color variation. Unlike reconstruction-based SSL methods (MAE for point clouds) that reconstruct the input modality itself, GPC reconstructs a different modality (color), bypassing mask design and directly encoding semantic segmentation cues into the backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for autonomous driving where 3D annotation is expensive: GPC with only 20% labeled KITTI data outperforms training from scratch on the full dataset, a result consistent across PointRCNN, IA-SSD, and PV-RCNN architectures and across KITTI→KITTI and Waymo→KITTI transfer settings. The hint mechanism is a simple, well-motivated solution to the color variation problem, and the ablation showing that 0% hints actually degrades below scratch validates the theoretical grounding. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other associated modalities (e.g., depth, semantic labels), whether the K-Means quantization granularity matters at scale, and whether the method remains effective when camera-LiDAR synchronization is imperfect or when operating in environments with extreme color variation (night, heavy rain).
