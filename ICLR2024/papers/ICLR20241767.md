## Problem

Pre-trained expert models fine-tuned on different data subdomains each perform well on their own domain but degrade on others, and test data typically arrives as a mixture of all domains. Traditional ensembling and confidence-based selection assume experts share a common distribution, making them poorly suited for combining experts with complementary (domain-specific) expertise. The paper also addresses the practical cost of querying all K experts at test time.

## Input / Output

The fuser never sees the raw input (image or text); it operates exclusively on expert outputs. For classification, the input is the concatenation of K softmax probability vectors; for generative LLM tasks, it is the concatenation of K averaged last-layer token embeddings (over both prompt and generated tokens). The output is either a class label (classification) or an expert index that determines which expert produces the final generation (generative). FrugalFoE additionally outputs a sequentially selected subset of experts to query, reducing the number of expert evaluations.

## Method

FoE casts expert fusion as a supervised learning problem: concatenate the outputs (or embeddings) of all K experts into a feature vector and train a small three-layer MLP fuser via empirical risk minimization with cross-entropy loss to predict either the class label or the index of the best expert. For generative tasks, the fuser selects which expert to invoke rather than generating text itself, avoiding the need to train a new domain-agnostic LLM. FrugalFoE extends this to a sequential expert-selection problem formulated as a graph shortest-path search analogous to A*, where each vertex represents a subset of queried experts and edge costs combine querying cost (scaled by λ) with a kNN-estimated conditional expected loss. A key practical trick is using kNN classifiers as the fuser for all expert subsets, bypassing the need to train 2^K separate fusers. The paper provides a Fano's inequality lower bound showing that when experts are complementary, the mutual information between expert outputs and the identity of the best expert is high, justifying the use of expert outputs alone for selection.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 super-class classification, FoE achieves 82.13% final accuracy and 75.27% expert selection accuracy, substantially outperforming individual experts (50.04% average), confidence-based fusion (74.07%), and ensemble (76.64%), while approaching the oracle expert (87.63%). On sentiment analysis, FoE reaches 91.88% average accuracy (99.1% expert selection accuracy) versus 79.44% for the best individual expert. On summarization (6 domains), FoE achieves 23.72 average ROUGE-2 versus 11.95 for the best individual expert. FrugalFoE reduces expert queries to 37.5% of all experts on CIFAR-100 while matching full-query accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FoE consistently outperforms all standard combination baselines across every task. On CIFAR-100, FoE (82.13%) beats the ensemble average (76.64%) by 5.5 points and confidence-based fusion (74.07%) by 8.1 points, while closing the gap to the oracle expert (87.63%) to within 5.5 points. Against FrugalML (Chen et al., 2020), which is limited to at most 2 expert queries, FrugalFoE outperforms it when querying the same number of experts on CIFAR-100, and can scale to up to 20 experts via its graph shortest-path formulation. On sentiment analysis and summarization, FoE nearly matches oracle performance (99.1% expert selection accuracy in both), and remarkably, querying only a single expert's embeddings is nearly as effective as querying all experts, suggesting LLM embeddings are highly informative for domain identification. On MMLU with weaker (generalist) experts, FoE (49.85%) still surpasses the best individual LLM (47.35%) but falls further short of the oracle (51.56%), indicating the method benefits most from properly fine-tuned domain experts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from classical ensemble learning and Mixture of Experts, which assume experts are trained on the same distribution or are jointly trained with a gating mechanism. It also distinguishes itself from FrugalML (Chen et al., 2020), which assumes a hierarchy of model quality (one model is better than others), whereas FoE assumes complementary expertise where no single expert dominates across all domains. The key conceptual move is casting expert fusion as a standard supervised learning problem over concatenated expert outputs (or embeddings for generative tasks), rather than requiring joint training or confidence-based heuristics. The frugal extension draws on kNN non-parametric estimation and an A*-inspired graph shortest-path formulation to sequentially select experts, going beyond FrugalML's limitation to at most 2–3 API calls.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With foundation models making domain-specific fine-tuning cheap, the practical bottleneck shifts to handling heterogeneous test distributions where no single expert is optimal; FoE provides a simple, well-grounded recipe (small MLP fuser) that approaches oracle performance across diverse tasks. The finding that LLM embeddings from a single expert nearly suffice for expert selection in summarization and sentiment analysis suggests these representations encode rich domain-identifying information, with implications beyond fusion. Limitations include the need for validation data from all K domains, the assumption that experts are genuinely complementary (not one dominating), and the combinatorial challenge of training fusers for all expert subsets in the frugal setting (mitigated but not eliminated by the kNN workaround). Open questions include scaling to hundreds of experts, handling overlapping or non-complementary expertise, and extending the frugal approach to settings where experts themselves are expensive generative models.
