## Problem

Temperature scaling is the most widely used post-training calibration technique for neural networks, yet it is frequently outperformed by training-time modifications. This paper identifies a specific theoretical bottleneck: for distributions where class supports overlap, even oracle temperature scaling on interpolating models is provably unable to achieve good calibration, degrading to random-guess performance as the number of classes grows. The paper asks whether we can characterize data distributions for which temperature scaling provably fails while training-time modifications still succeed.

## Input / Output

The theoretical framework operates on general k-class distributions over R^d × [k] with class-conditional densities whose supports may overlap, parameterized by an overlap degree α ∈ [0,1]. In experiments, inputs are 32×32 RGB images (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100) or 300-dimensional Gaussian vectors, and the output is a calibrated softmax probability vector over k classes. The d-Mixup variant requires forming convex combinations of d+1 training points (rather than the standard 2-point Mixup), which introduces additional computational overhead per batch but provides stronger neighborhood constraints on model predictions.

## Method

The paper proves two complementary results. First, for ERM interpolators satisfying a local Lipschitz-like (γ-regularity) condition on distributions with class overlaps, the expected KL divergence after optimal temperature scaling is lower-bounded by Θ((1−α−1/k) log k), meaning calibration is asymptotically no better than random when α = O(1). Second, the paper introduces d-Mixup, which generalizes standard Mixup from 2-point to (d+1)-point convex combinations, and proves via a reparameterization-based optimality lemma that d-Mixup interpolators achieve Θ(1) KL divergence independent of the overlap parameter α. The key insight is that training-time calibration constrains model behavior in regions between training points (where overlaps live), whereas temperature scaling can only uniformly rescale logits and thus can at best push overconfident predictions toward uniform random. Experiments on synthetic Gaussian data and image benchmarks with injected label noise confirm that Mixup and d-Mixup maintain stable calibration while ERM+TS degrades sharply with increasing overlap.

## Results

Theoretically, the paper proves that for ERM interpolators on distributions with class overlap parameter α, even oracle temperature scaling yields a calibration error of Θ((1−α−1/k) log k), which is asymptotically no better than random for large k. Empirically, on 300-dimensional Gaussian data with increasing overlap (μ=0.25→0.01), ERM+TS NLL jumps from 0.26 to 4.30 while 4-Mixup NLL rises only from 0.27 to 0.74. On image benchmarks with 50% label noise, ERM+TS NLL reaches 5.95 (CIFAR-10), 8.02 (CIFAR-100), and 4.18 (SVHN), whereas Mixup achieves 1.77, 3.54, and 1.50 respectively. The d-Mixup variants (mixing 3 or 5 points) further improve calibration over standard 2-point Mixup as overlap increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ERM+TS (Guo et al., 2017), the paper demonstrates a fundamental limitation: as class overlap increases (via label noise), ERM+TS NLL degrades sharply (e.g., 2.01→5.95 on CIFAR-10 from 0% to 50% noise) while Mixup degrades only modestly (0.82→1.77). This gap widens with more classes and more overlap, consistent with the theoretical bound that ERM+TS calibration error scales as Θ((1−α) log k). Against standard 2-point Mixup (Zhang et al., 2017), the paper's d-Mixup extension (mixing d+1 points) provides additional calibration benefits, particularly at high overlap: on Gaussian data with μ=0.01, 4-Mixup achieves ECE of 0.10 vs. 0.19 for standard Mixup. Notably, on SVHN at 50% label noise, ERM actually outperforms Mixup in test accuracy yet still suffers a massive NLL gap (4.18 vs. 1.50), confirming the issue is calibration rather than generalization. The paper also shows that applying temperature scaling on top of Mixup provides negligible additional benefit (e.g., Mixup NLL 1.77 vs. Mixup+TS 1.76 on CIFAR-10 at 50% noise), indicating that training-time calibration is the critical component.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the calibration literature initiated by Guo et al. (2017) on temperature scaling and the Mixup data augmentation of Zhang et al. (2017) by providing a theoretical explanation for why post-training calibration (temperature scaling) can be fundamentally insufficient while training-time modifications succeed. It builds directly on the Mixup optimality analysis of Chidambaram et al. (2021), generalizing their lemma to a broader function class and non-asymptotic setting, and complements Zhang et al. (2021) on Mixup calibration by addressing a much wider class of models (beyond linear) and a different class of data distributions (overlapping supports rather than Gaussian). The key departure is the introduction of d-Mixup, which generalizes standard 2-point Mixup to convex combinations of d+1 points to enable rigorous neighborhood constraints in higher dimensions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical characterization of a specific data-distribution property (class overlap) under which even oracle temperature scaling is provably insufficient, explaining a long-standing empirical gap between post-training and training-time calibration methods. This has practical implications for critical applications (medical diagnosis, autonomous driving) where miscalibrated confidence can have severe consequences. Open questions include whether the γ-regularity assumption is too restrictive, whether 2-point Mixup suffices in practice for the neighborhood constraints that d-Mixup provides theoretically (Remark 3.5), and whether non-linear neighborhood constraints could yield even better calibration guarantees. The theory also suggests that the benefit of d-Mixup over standard Mixup may be most pronounced in high-overlap/high-noise regimes, a direction worth further empirical exploration.
