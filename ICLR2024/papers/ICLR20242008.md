## Problem

Micro-expression spotting in long videos is severely degraded by global movements (head rotation, zooming) that overwhelm the subtle, brief (<0.5 s) facial signals. Existing optical-flow-based methods rely on a fixed reference frame, causing OF accuracy to degrade over long sequences and making them overly dependent on facial alignment quality. Furthermore, prior spotting rules (e.g., S-pattern) only capture half of the ME movement and fail to fully exploit the distinctive temporal signature that separates MEs from other micro-movements.

## Input / Output

The method consumes long RGB facial video sequences (30 fps for CAS(ME)², 200 fps for SAMM-LV and CASME II) and produces a set of expression intervals (onset and offset frame indices) for both micro-expressions and macro-expressions. The face is divided into 9 blocks (for global-movement state classification) and 15 regions of interest (for ME/MaE feature extraction), with a nose-tip matching box used for alignment. Predicted intervals are evaluated against ground truth using an IoU ≥ 0.5 matching criterion, reporting precision, recall, and F1-score per expression type.

## Method

The core contribution is a skip-k-frame block-wise MDMO feature computed between f_i and f_{i-k} (k = (N+1)/2, where N is average ME duration), which simultaneously extracts subtle RoI-level motion and eliminates global movements by classifying each frame into one of five states (order, disorder, rotation, adjustment, blink) based on the directional distribution of OF vectors across 9 facial blocks. Under this skip-k-frame strategy, the authors discover that complete ME movements produce a distinctive 'M-pattern' in the OF magnitude curve—two peaks separated by a local minimum—reflecting the symmetric onset-apex and apex-restoration phases. A pixelmatch-based facial alignment algorithm with dynamic reference-frame update (triggered when mismatch pixels exceed M_max or equal zero) reduces inter-frame jitter without the deformation artifacts of landmark-based warping. Spotting is then performed via a six-step rule pipeline: peak detection, M-pattern-based interval combination, expression-level filtering by subtlety and localization (deformation degree and RoI count), global-movement exclusion via nose-tip tracking, cross-RoI interval merging, and duration-based transience filtering (<0.5 s). The OF field is divided into 8 intersecting (rather than disjoint) bins to reduce directional quantization error in the MDMO computation.

## Results

The proposed method achieves F1-scores of 0.2614 (ME), 0.5061 (MaE), and 0.4558 (All) on CAS(ME)², 0.2866 (ME), 0.3724 (MaE), and 0.3419 (All) on SAMM-LV, and 0.8571 (ME) on CASME II. On CASME II, the method substantially outperforms the S-pattern baseline (0.4700) by nearly 39 percentage points in F1-score. On CAS(ME)², the method leads all baselines across ME, MaE, and All categories, with the largest margin over AUW-GCN (0.3834 All F1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all seven baselines on CAS(ME)² across ME, MaE, and All F1-scores, with the closest competitor being AUW-GCN (0.3834 All F1 vs. 0.4558) and OF-FD (0.3436 All F1). On SAMM-LV ME, the method leads by a clear margin (0.2866 vs. 0.2162 for OF-FD, the next best). However, on SAMM-LV MaE and All, the method trails AUW-GCN (0.4293 vs. 0.3724 MaE; 0.3728 vs. 0.3419 All) and OF-FD (0.4149 vs. 0.3724 MaE), which the authors attribute to not extracting RoIs below the eyes that capture blinking-related MaEs. On CASME II, the method achieves 0.8571 F1, nearly doubling the S-pattern baseline (0.4700), demonstrating the superiority of M-pattern over S-pattern for describing complete ME movements. The method also substantially outperforms the fixed-reference-frame baseline (Yuhong, 2021) in ablation, confirming the benefit of the unfixed-RF skip-k-frame strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MDMO feature (Liu et al., 2016) into a block-wise variant with intersecting bins and introduces a state-based global movement elimination strategy, while building on the non-fixed reference frame idea from Liong et al. (2021) by addressing its limitation during the ME restoration phase. It departs from the dominant fixed-reference-frame paradigm (Yuhong 2021, Zhang 2020) and the S-pattern spotting rule (Li et al., 2023b), proposing instead a skip-k-frame M-pattern that captures both onset and restoration of a complete ME. The pixelmatch-based dynamic reference frame alignment replaces OF-based fixed-RF alignment, and the spotting rules operate at the expression level rather than the RoI level, distinguishing it from prior rule-based MES methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The M-pattern provides a more complete and interpretable description of ME dynamics than the previously identified S-pattern, and the block-wise MDMO with five-state classification offers a practical, training-free mechanism for global movement removal that is particularly relevant given the small sample sizes in MES datasets. However, the method remains entirely handcrafted and rule-based, limiting its scalability; it cannot distinguish habitual micro-movements from genuine ME using unimodal data alone, and its MaE performance on SAMM-LV trails the best deep learning baselines by ~5%. Open questions include whether M-pattern generalizes across expression types and cultures, and whether the state-based global movement elimination can be integrated into deep learning pipelines to complement learned representations.
