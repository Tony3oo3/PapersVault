## Problem

Existing instance-reweighting methods for robust learning (e.g., Meta-Weight-Net, L2R, FSR) produce weights that are either free parameters or functions of instance loss, making them unusable at test time for uncertainty estimation. No prior work provides a single learned function that simultaneously captures diverse uncertainty sources—input-dependent label noise, covariate shift, and missing features—while serving both train-time reweighting and test-time applications like selective classification and calibration.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images of varying resolution (512×512 for Diabetic Retinopathy, 28×28 for CIFAR-100, 256×256 for ImageNet-100, 224×224 for ImageNet-1K) as input to two parallel networks. The primary classifier outputs a class label, while the auxiliary U-SCORE network outputs a scalar uncertainty score in [0,1] computed solely from the input image—no labels or loss values are required at test time. This score is thresholded to produce a predict-or-abstain decision for selective classification, or used directly as a confidence measure for calibration and OOD detection.

## Method

REVAR (Reweighting for Dropout Variance Reduction) learns an instance-conditional weight function g_Θ(x) via bilevel optimization: the primary classifier f_θ minimizes a weighted training loss where weights come from g_Θ, while g_Θ itself is optimized to minimize a meta-loss on a held-out validation set. The key technical contribution is a novel variance-minimizing meta-regularizer that computes Monte Carlo Dropout (MCD) variance of the classifier on validation instances and backpropagates it through the U-SCORE parameters, forcing g(x) to identify the most uncertain and informative training examples. This indirect mechanism—minimizing posterior uncertainty on unseen data through reweighting of seen data—causes U-SCORE to downweight high-label-noise samples while upweighting covariate-shift-hard examples, smoothly interpolating between these behaviors as the mix of uncertainty sources changes. The U-SCORE is implemented as a standard CNN (e.g., ResNet-18) updated every K=15 classifier steps, and the entire framework requires only ~1.5× the training cost of standard ERM.

## Results

REVAR achieves the highest AUARC (Area Under Accuracy-Rejection Curve) across all selective classification benchmarks: 94.12 on Diabetic Retinopathy (in-distribution), 89.94 on DR (OOD/country shift), 93.20 on CIFAR-100, 94.95 on ImageNet-100, and 88.20 on ImageNet-1K, outperforming the strongest baselines (SAT, MCD) by 0.5–2.0 points. On calibration (ECE), REVAR achieves 3.8 on DR in-distribution and 2.6 on ImageNet-1K, competitive with AdaFocal (3.6 and 2.1 respectively). For input-dependent label noise, REVAR reaches 71.87 accuracy on Inst.CIFAR-100 and 73.97 on Clothing1M, surpassing the best reweighting baseline FSR (70.21 and 73.86). Applied on top of the PLEX pretrained model, REVAR reduces KL divergence from 0.75→0.71 (IN-100H) and 0.49→0.47 (CF-100H), and improves ECE from 7.5→6.2 on OOD calibration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REVAR consistently outperforms all selective classification baselines (SR, MCD, DG, SN, SAT) on every dataset, with the largest margins on ImageNet-1K (+0.9 over MCD) and DR-OOD (+1.0 over SAT). The two new ablation baselines—VR (ERM + variance reduction in training loss) and MBR (margin-based reweighting)—confirm that both the bilevel reweighting framework and the dropout-variance meta-regularizer are necessary: REVAR beats VR by 1.6 on DR-OOD and MBR by 1.9 on DR-OOD. For calibration, REVAR is competitive but slightly behind AdaFocal on CIFAR-100 (3.1 vs 2.3 ECE) and ImageNet-1K (2.6 vs 2.1), while matching or beating it on DR and ImageNet-100. On input-dependent label noise, REVAR surpasses all reweighting baselines (MWN, L2R, FSR) by 1.7–5.7 accuracy points, demonstrating that loss-based reweighting methods designed for random/instance-independent noise fail under instance-dependent settings. On the PLEX pretrained model, REVAR yields ~4% relative improvement in label uncertainty (KL divergence) and reduces OOD ECE from 7.5 to 6.2, showing gains transfer to large-scale foundation models.

## Relation to Prior Work

REVAR extends the bilevel-optimization reweighting line (MWN, L2R, FSR) by replacing loss-dependent or free-parameter weights with an instance-conditional neural network g(x), making the learned weights applicable at test time without labels. The key conceptual departure is repurposing Monte Carlo Dropout variance—not as a test-time uncertainty score but as a meta-regularization term in the U-SCORE's training objective—thereby forcing g(x) to encode which training instances are most uncertain. This unifies train-time reweighting (for robustness to label noise, covariate shift) and test-time selective classification under a single learned function, something prior bilevel reweighting methods could not do.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a single, interpretable mechanism that captures multiple qualitatively different uncertainty sources (input-dependent label noise, covariate shift, missing features) with the correct directional weighting, and smoothly interpolates between them as their relative magnitudes change. Practically, it delivers consistent gains across selective classification, calibration, and domain adaptation on medical and natural-image benchmarks, and plugs into large pretrained models like PLEX with modest overhead (~1.5× training cost). Open questions include whether the dropout-variance meta-objective generalizes to non-CNN backbones at scale, whether the validation-set requirement can be relaxed, and how the approach interacts with modern foundation-model fine-tuning paradigms beyond the PLEX experiment.
