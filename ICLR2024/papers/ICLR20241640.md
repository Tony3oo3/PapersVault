## Problem

Existing neural implicit surface methods (NeuS, VolSDF, etc.) struggle to simultaneously reconstruct smooth low-frequency room layouts and high-frequency object surfaces in indoor scenes. The vanishing gradient problem in SDF-based representations causes fine object details (e.g., chair legs, thin structures) to be lost even in late training stages, while uniform color and normal losses introduce over-smoothing or discontinuities in planar regions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images at 640×480 resolution with calibrated camera parameters, along with auxiliary 2D cues: monocular normal maps with per-pixel uncertainty (from SNU) and object segmentation masks (from MSeg, where object categories are binarized to 1 and all others to 0). It produces a watertight 3D SDF surface extracted via Marching Cubes, rendered surface normals, and rendered colors. Training is split into two sequential phases (40k + 120k iterations) with a batch of 512 rays and Adam at lr=2e-4 on a single RTX 3090Ti.

## Method

H2O-SDF introduces a two-phase learning framework. Phase 1 (Holistic Surface Learning) re-weights color and normal losses using per-ray normal uncertainty from a pretrained monocular estimator, so planar regions are dominated by normal supervision (yielding smooth planes) while texture-rich regions rely on color (preserving detail). Phase 2 (Object Surface Learning) introduces the Object Surface Field (OSF), a 4-layer MLP that outputs a per-point probability of being on an object surface, trained via three losses: a 2D BCE loss against object masks, a 3D loss that couples OSF to SDF through a scaled sigmoid σ_γ(x)=1/(1+exp(γ·d(x))) to create mutual induction (OSF learns to be high only near SDF zero-level sets on objects, and in turn provides gradient signals to SDF in thin-structure regions where standard volume-rendering gradients vanish), and a refinement loss using MVS point clouds. An OSF-guided importance sampling strategy (sampling proportional to w(x)·osf(x)) further concentrates optimization on object surfaces rather than already-converged room layouts.

## Results

On ScanNet (12 scenes), H2O-SDF achieves a state-of-the-art F-score of 0.799, outperforming the closest competitor HelixSurf (0.755) by 4.4 points and MonoSDF (0.733) by 6.6 points. It also attains the best Precision (0.834) and Recall (0.769) among all compared methods, with the lowest Accuracy (0.032 m) and Completeness (0.037 m) errors. On the generalization datasets, H2O-SDF leads on 7-Scenes (F-score 0.957 vs. MonoSDF 0.934) and Replica (F-score 0.447 vs. NeuRIS 0.410). Training time is 4.5 hours, comparable to NeuRIS (4.5 h) and ManhattanSDF (5.2 h), and well below MonoSDF (21.5 h).

## Baselines & Comparisons

H2O-SDF outperforms all eight baselines on every 3D reconstruction metric (Acc, Comp, Prec, Recall, F-score) across ScanNet, 7-Scenes, and Replica. Against the strongest prior method HelixSurf (CVPR 2023), it gains +4.4 F-score points (0.799 vs. 0.755) and +4.2 Recall points (0.769 vs. 0.727), while also reducing Accuracy error from 0.038 m to 0.032 m. Compared to MonoSDF (NeurIPS 2022), the improvement is +6.6 F-score points with a notable Recall jump from 0.681 to 0.769, indicating substantially better coverage of fine-grained object geometry. Against NeuRIS (ECCV 2022) and ManhattanSDF (CVPR 2022), which rely on normal/Manhattan priors, H2O-SDF improves F-score by ~11 points, showing that the two-phase OSF approach captures object detail that geometric priors alone cannot. Against the base NeuS (NeurIPS 2021), the gain is dramatic (+50.8 F-score points), confirming that the re-weighting scheme and OSF are both critical. On 7-Scenes, H2O-SDF achieves 0.957 F-score vs. MonoSDF's 0.934 and NeuRIS's 0.909; on Replica it leads with 0.447 vs. NeuRIS's 0.410, demonstrating cross-domain generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on NeuS (Wang et al., 2021) as its base SDF-based volume rendering framework, extending it with a two-phase learning strategy that separates holistic room-layout reconstruction from fine-grained object surface learning. It departs from prior indoor scene methods (ManhattanSDF, NeuRIS, MonoSDF, HelixSurf) that rely on external geometric priors (normals, depth, semantics) to guide SDF, instead introducing the Object Surface Field (OSF) as an internal 3D field that mutually guides SDF toward high-frequency object details while suppressing activation on room-layout surfaces. The OSF concept is motivated by ObjectSDF (Wu et al., 2022) but differs fundamentally: rather than transforming SDF values into a semantic field supervised by 2D rendering loss, H2O-SDF introduces a distinct probability field that cross-guides SDF through a 3D loss, avoiding the need for direct SDF value supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent and practically important tension in indoor scene reconstruction: low-frequency room surfaces converge quickly and dominate gradient flow, while high-frequency object details suffer from vanishing gradients. The OSF provides a principled mechanism to redirect learning capacity toward object surfaces without sacrificing room-layout smoothness, and the demonstrated object mesh extraction capability opens a path toward scene editing applications. Limitations include dependence on pre-trained monocular normal estimation (SNU) and semantic segmentation (MSeg) models, as well as MVS point clouds for the refinement loss, which introduces additional failure modes. The two-phase training (160k total iterations) is comparable in cost to baselines but the method has not been tested on large-scale or outdoor scenes, leaving open whether the object/non-object dichotomy generalizes beyond typical indoor settings.
