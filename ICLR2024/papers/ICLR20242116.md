## Problem

Large language models frequently hallucinate, generating content that deviates from real-world facts, which is a critical bottleneck for high-stakes applications. Existing mitigation strategies either require conditioning on retrieved external knowledge, supervised fine-tuning with human labels, or comparing outputs from two separate models (e.g., Contrastive Decoding), making them costly or impractical. The paper asks whether the hierarchical encoding of factual knowledge across transformer layers can be exploited at inference time to improve factuality without any of these additional resources.

## Input / Output

DoLa takes the same text prompt as standard LLM decoding and produces generated text, but modifies the next-token probability computation at each decoding step. It operates entirely within a single model's forward pass by also projecting intermediate-layer hidden states through the vocabulary head (early exit), requiring no second model, external retrieval, or fine-tuning. The only added hyperparameters are a candidate-layer bucket (2–4 options selected via validation) and a repetition penalty for long chain-of-thought generation.

## Method

DoLa computes next-token distributions from both the final (mature) layer and a dynamically selected earlier (premature) layer, then contrasts them in log-space as log(q_N(x_t)/q_M(x_t)) for tokens passing an adaptive plausibility threshold. The premature layer is chosen per decoding step by maximizing Jensen-Shannon Divergence between the mature-layer distribution and each candidate early-layer distribution, ensuring the contrast targets layers where the model is still injecting factual knowledge rather than settled syntactic predictions. This mechanism amplifies factual knowledge localized in higher transformer layers while suppressing lower-layer linguistic patterns, effectively sharpening the probability mass toward factually correct tokens. The approach requires only a single forward pass with early exiting from the same model, adding 1–8% decoding latency and negligible memory overhead compared to vanilla decoding.

## Results

DoLa improves TruthfulQA MC2 accuracy by 12–17% absolute points across LLaMA-7B/13B/33B/65B (e.g., 40.6→63.8 for 7B, 43.3→64.9 for 13B, 49.5→62.3 for 33B, 46.9→64.6 for 65B). On open-ended TruthfulQA, DoLa boosts the combined %Truth×Info score by 12–17% (26.9→40.8 for 7B, 32.4→44.6 for 13B, 31.7→49.1 for 33B, 34.8→49.2 for 65B) while keeping informativeness above 90%. On chain-of-thought tasks, DoLa gains 1–4% on StrategyQA and ~2% on GSM8K for most model sizes. Decoding latency increases by only 1.01×–1.08×, making the overhead negligible.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DoLa consistently outperforms Contrastive Decoding (CD, Li et al. 2022) across nearly all tasks and model sizes: on TruthfulQA MC2, DoLa gains 11–23 points over CD (e.g., 64.9 vs 41.0 for 13B); on open-ended TruthfulQA %Truth×Info, DoLa leads by 0.2–12.5 points (44.6 vs 44.4 for 13B, 49.1 vs 36.7 for 33B, 49.2 vs 43.4 for 65B); and on GSM8K, DoLa improves over CD by 8.9–10.0 points (e.g., 54.0 vs 44.0 for 65B). CD's main failure mode is excessive refusal (e.g., >60% 'I have no comment' for 33B), which inflates its raw truthfulness but collapses informativeness. Against Inference-Time Intervention (ITI, Li et al. 2023), which requires supervised training on TruthfulQA labels, DoLa is competitive: ITI achieves 43.5% %Truth×Info on 7B vs. DoLa's 40.8%, a small gap given that DoLa needs no external labels or fine-tuning. On FACTOR Wiki, CD narrowly beats DoLa for 33B (71.3 vs 70.3), but DoLa wins on all other FACTOR splits. Overall, DoLa dominates CD on every CoT reasoning benchmark (StrategyQA, GSM8K) and on the majority of factuality metrics, while matching or approaching ITI without any training overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

DoLa repurposes the Contrastive Decoding framework of Li et al. (2022) by replacing the model-level contrast (expert vs. amateur LM) with a layer-level contrast within a single model, motivated by interpretability findings that factual knowledge is concentrated in later transformer layers (Tenney et al., 2019; Dai et al., 2022; Meng et al., 2022). It leverages early-exit principles to justify applying the language head to intermediate hidden states, and adopts the Adaptive Plausibility Constraint from CD to avoid false positives/negatives. Unlike Inference-Time Intervention (ITI), which requires supervised training with human labels, DoLa is entirely training-free and requires no external model. A concurrent work, Autocontrastive Decoding (Gera et al., 2023), partially resembles DoLa-static but targets small LMs and requires fine-tuning prediction heads, whereas DoLa operates on large LLMs without any additional training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DoLa offers a remarkably simple, training-free decoding strategy that yields 12–17% absolute improvements on TruthfulQA across four LLaMA sizes with only 1–8% latency overhead, making it a practical drop-in improvement for production LLMs. Its key limitation is that it can only surface knowledge already present in the model's weights—it cannot correct misinformation acquired during pretraining or address non-factuality dimensions like instruction-following or safety. Open questions include whether DoLa composes with RLHF or retrieval-augmented generation, whether the dynamic layer selection generalizes to architectures without residual connections, and whether the approach extends to multimodal or non-autoregressive models.
