## Problem

Conformal prediction for regression struggles when the conditional output distribution is heteroscedastic, multimodal, or skewed: simple distance-to-mean scores produce fixed-width symmetric intervals, while density-estimation approaches are sensitive to kernel and hyperparameter choices, yielding unstable intervals. The paper asks whether one can obtain flexible, distribution-aware conformal sets for regression by reusing the well-understood machinery of classification conformal prediction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a tabular feature vector x and a held-out calibration set, and produces a conformal prediction set that may consist of multiple disjoint intervals in the continuous output space (critical for bimodal label distributions). The output space is discretized into K=50 equidistant bins over [y_min, y_max] observed in training; the final set is obtained by thresholding a linearly interpolated probability density at the (1−α)-quantile of calibration conformity scores, so the set can be non-convex and adapt its width and shape per input.

## Method

R2CCP converts regression to classification by binning the output range into K equidistant classes, then trains a softmax neural network with a novel loss that replaces cross-entropy with an expected distance penalty Σ_k |y_i − ŷ_k|^p · q_θ(ŷ_k|x_i) minus a Shannon entropy regularizer τ·H(q_θ). The distance term preserves the ordinal structure of the continuous output (penalizing probability mass far from the true label more than nearby mass), while the entropy term prevents the network from collapsing to overconfident Dirac-like distributions. The learned per-bin probabilities serve directly as the conformity score σ(x,y) = q̄_θ(y|x) (linearly interpolated between bins), and the standard classification conformal calibration procedure—taking the (1−α)-quantile of calibration scores—yields the final prediction set. This design lets the method adaptively widen, narrow, or split intervals to match heteroscedasticity and bimodality without any post-hoc interval-finding step.

## Results

R2CCP achieves the shortest conformal prediction intervals on 10 of 16 datasets at 90% coverage, with particularly large gains on the MEPS-19/20/21 datasets (length 1.60–1.72 vs. 1.87–2.00 for CQR and 4.47–4.92 for LASSO) and on BIO (1.11 vs. 1.34 for CQR). On the synthetic BIMODAL dataset, R2CCP produces intervals of length 0.46, capturing the two modes while CQR, LASSO, CB, and CHR all produce near-full-range intervals (~2.14). Coverage is maintained at the nominal 90% level across all datasets and all methods, confirming the finite-sample guarantee holds in practice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R2CCP outperforms CQR (Romano et al., 2019) on 7 of 16 datasets, with the largest margins on MEPS-19/20/21 (1.60–1.72 vs. 1.87–2.00) and STOCK (0.92 vs. 1.85); CQR retains the edge on PARKINSONS (0.42 vs. 0.50) and ENERGY (0.19 vs. 0.20). Against KDE (Lei & Wasserman, 2014), R2CCP wins on all datasets where the label distribution depends on the input (CONCRETE, MEPS, BIO, STOCK, PENDULUM) but loses on datasets with input-independent labels (BIMODAL 0.46 vs. 0.35, LOGNORM 1.96 vs. 1.40, SOLAR 3.80 vs. 0.50, FOREST 3.80 vs. 2.90), where KDE's simplicity is sufficient. R2CCP dominates LASSO (Papadopoulos et al., 2002) on every dataset, with gaps as large as 3.3× on MEPS-21 (1.72 vs. 4.92). Versus Conformal Bayes (Fong & Holmes, 2021), R2CCP is superior on MEPS and BIO but CB edges ahead on DIABETES (1.19 vs. 1.34) and CANCER (3.14 vs. 3.21). R2CCP beats CHR (Sesia & Romano, 2021) on MEPS, BIO, STOCK, and PENDULUM, while CHR is marginally better on CONCRETE (0.47 vs. 0.38 is actually R2CCP winning; CHR wins on PARKINSONS 0.68 vs. 0.50 is R2CCP winning). DCP (Chernozhukov et al., 2021) catastrophically fails on MEPS (length ≈ 67) and FOREST (6.00), while R2CCP remains competitive everywhere. Overall, R2CCP's advantage is most pronounced on datasets with complex, input-dependent label distributions, while KDE's simplicity wins on trivially structured synthetic data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies conformal prediction for classification and regression by reformulating regression as a multi-class classification problem over binned outputs, then applying the standard classification CP calibration procedure. The key departure from prior regression-as-classification work (Stewart et al., 2023; Diaz & Marathe, 2019) is the replacement of cross-entropy with a distance-weighted expected loss that respects the ordinal structure of bins, combined with Shannon entropy regularization (Wainwright & Jordan, 2008) to prevent overconfident Dirac-like outputs. This positions the method between distribution-based CP approaches (KDE, histogram regression, conformal Bayesian) and quantile-based methods (CQR), borrowing the calibration machinery from classification CP while using a loss designed for the continuous-output setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a single, simple framework (softmax network + distance-weighted loss + entropy + standard CP calibration) handles heteroscedasticity, bimodality, and skewness without requiring separate algorithmic treatments, and achieves the shortest intervals on 10 of 16 benchmarks while maintaining the finite-sample coverage guarantee. Key limitations include the need for larger networks than CQR, sensitivity to hyperparameters (τ, p, K) that were not tuned per dataset, and the fact that the learned distribution is a smoothed approximation rather than a direct density estimate. Open questions include whether the approach extends to high-dimensional or structured outputs, how to adapt the binning strategy for non-uniform label scales, and whether the ordinal-structure-aware loss generalizes to multi-output or sequential regression settings.
