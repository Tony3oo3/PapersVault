## Problem

Existing continuous optimal transport methods are restricted to classic cost functions (e.g., ℓ²), which cannot incorporate task-specific auxiliary information such as class labels or paired correspondences. General cost functionals, which subsume classic, weak, and regularized OT, have only been solved in the discrete setting and do not provide out-of-sample estimates. This paper fills the gap by deriving a continuous, neural-network-based solver for general OT in high-dimensional spaces.

## Input / Output

For the class-guided task, the method takes a fully labeled source distribution P and a partially labeled target distribution Q (only ≥1 labeled sample per class, typically 10), plus a latent noise vector z ~ N(0, I_Z), and produces a stochastic transport map T(x, z) that maps each source class to the corresponding target class. For the pair-guided task, it takes paired source-target images (e.g., 256×256 RGB) and produces a deterministic map T(x). The class-guided experiments operate at 32×32 resolution; the pair-guided experiments at 256×256. A key practical detail is that the target distribution Q is only partially labeled, while the source P is fully labeled, making the problem semi-supervised.

## Method

The core contribution is a maximin (saddle point) reformulation of the general OT problem: Cost(P,Q) = sup_v inf_T {F̃(T) − ∫v(T(x,z))dP(x)dS(z) + ∫v(y)dQ(y)}, where T: X×Z→Y is a stochastic map implicitly representing a transport plan and v is a dual potential. Theorem 2 guarantees that any optimal saddle point (v*, T*) yields a valid stochastic OT map, and Theorem 3 provides error bounds on the recovered plan in terms of duality gaps, requiring only strong convexity of F (no convexity assumptions on v). Two concrete functionals are constructed: a class-guided F_G that sums per-class energy distances (requiring separate sampling from each class component, which is where label information enters), and a pair-guided F_S that integrates a loss over paired data. Training uses stochastic gradient ascent-descent with a UNet for T_θ and a ResNet for v_ω, with Monte Carlo estimation of all terms in the objective.

## Results

On the class-guided dataset transfer task, the proposed method with the class-guided cost functional F_G achieves 83.22% accuracy on FMNIST→MNIST and 94.62% on MNIST→MNIST-M, dramatically outperforming all baselines which score near random (8–12%) on the unrelated-domain transfer. In terms of FID, the method achieves 5.26 on FMNIST→MNIST and 6.67 on MNIST→MNIST-M, substantially better than discrete OT (>100) and competitive with or better than GAN-based methods. For the pair-guided supervised image-to-image translation on CelebAMask-HQ (256×256), the method achieves an FID of 21.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against six baselines across two task settings. On the unrelated-domain transfer (FMNIST→MNIST), the proposed F_G method achieves 83.22% accuracy versus 8.93% (MUNIT), 12.03% (Aug CycleGAN), 10.67% (Discrete OT/SinkhornLpL1), 10.96% (Neural OT W₂), and 8.02% (Neural OT W₂,γ) — a 7–10× improvement over all baselines, which perform near random guessing. On the related-domain transfer (MNIST→MNIST-M), GAN-based methods (MUNIT 97.95%, Aug CycleGAN 98.2%) slightly outperform the proposed method (94.62%) in accuracy, but the proposed method achieves a substantially lower FID (6.67 vs. 11.68 and 26.87 respectively), indicating better sample quality. Discrete OT (SinkhornLpL1) and Neural OT baselines are clearly outperformed on both accuracy (94.62% vs. 83.26% and 38.77%) and FID (6.67 vs. >100 and 19.43). OTDD (gradient flows) does not report accuracy on FMNIST→MNIST and has FID >100 on MNIST→MNIST-M, making it non-competitive. For the pair-guided image-to-image translation, the method is compared against Pix2Pix and unsupervised Neural OT, achieving competitive FID scores (21.1 on CelebAMask-HQ) with the advantage of being a direct OT-based approach rather than a GAN.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural optimal transport line of work (Korotin et al., 2023b; Rout et al., 2022; Fan et al., 2023), which solved classic and weak OT in the continuous setting, to the general OT formulation of Paty & Cuturi (2020) that was previously only tractable in the discrete regime. The key conceptual move is a generic maximin reformulation via stochastic maps that automatically subsumes all prior continuous OT reformulations, while the duality-gap error analysis removes the convexity assumptions on the dual potential required by earlier works (Makkuva et al., 2020; Fan et al., 2023). On the application side, it departs from GAN-based domain adaptation (CycleGAN, MUNIT) and gradient-flow methods (OTDD) by framing class-preserving transfer as an OT problem with a task-specific energy-distance functional rather than an adversarial or flow-based objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a concrete gap: continuous OT with general cost functionals was previously undefined, and the dataset transfer problem (mapping between labeled source and partially-labeled target while preserving class structure) had no continuous OT solution. The assumption-free error bounds via duality gaps are broadly applicable to any strongly convex functional, including weak OT for which no prior error analysis existed. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that applying the method requires constructing a task-specific estimator for the functional F, which may be non-trivial for new downstream tasks; this positions the construction of novel cost functionals and their estimators as an open research direction. The practical impact is moderate: the method works well on 32×32 digit transfer and 256×256 image translation, but scaling to higher-resolution or more complex generative tasks remains unexplored.
