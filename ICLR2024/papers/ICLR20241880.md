## Problem

Existing RoPE-based extrapolation methods (e.g., Code LLaMA, Dynamic NTK) use specific base values and tuning lengths without a unified theoretical explanation of how base, tuning context, and extrapolation limit interrelate. Surprisingly, the default base of 10000 is the worst value for fine-tuning-based extrapolation, and no prior work identified why both smaller and larger bases improve performance or established a mathematical relationship governing the extrapolation boundary.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241880/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-trained LLaMA2 7B/13B models (head dimension d=128, 16 rotary angles per head) and fine-tunes them on 4K or 16K token text sequences from The Pile with a modified rotary base β, then evaluates perplexity on Books3 at context lengths up to 1M tokens. The output is standard next-token prediction, but the key metric is how far beyond the 4K/16K tuning length the model maintains coherent perplexity—achieving 100K+ with base 1000000 at 4K tuning, or ~1M with base 500 at 16K tuning.

## Method

The paper derives a unified periodic-perspective framework identifying the Critical Dimension d_extra (92 for LLaMA2) as the number of dimensions whose cos/sin periods fit within the training context; dimensions beyond d_extra receive out-of-distribution positional signals during extrapolation, causing attention score explosion. For smaller bases, Theorem 1 shows that reducing β to 2T_train/π, T_train/π, or T_train/2π progressively exposes all dimensions to π/2, π, and 2π of trigonometric input, eliminating the OOD problem and enabling unbounded extrapolation. For larger bases, Theorem 2 gives the extrapolation upper bound T_extra = 2π·β^(d_extra/d), explaining why Code LLaMA's base 1000000 with 4K tuning reaches exactly ~100K. The Extended Scaling Law (Theorem 3) generalizes both to longer tuning contexts, introducing a critical base β₀ that separates the two regimes and predicting when the critical dimension itself updates.

## Results

The paper achieves extrapolation of LLaMA2 7B to ~1M context length using RoPE base=500 with only 16K tuning length (perplexity 51.28 at 1M, or 19.07 with log-scaled attention), and to 100K+ context with base=1000000 and just 4K tuning length. The key finding is that the original RoPE base of 10000 is the *worst* value for fine-tuning-based extrapolation: both smaller (e.g., 500, 652, 1304, 2608) and larger (e.g., 40000, 1000000) bases significantly improve extrapolation performance. On long-context tasks (Table 5, 16K context), base=1000000 with 16K tuning achieves 13.0% GSM8K, 44.24% TOEFL-QA, and 41.33% TopicRetrieval on LLaMA2 7B, surpassing both NTK fixed (5.0%, 34.20%, 31.33%) and NTK dynamic (9.0%, 37.17%, 24.67%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against **Dynamic NTK** (bloc97, 2023a), the proposed Scaling RoPE with base=1000000 and 4K tuning outperforms it by a considerable margin within the extrapolation upper bound (e.g., 34.00% vs. 24.67% on TopicRetrieval, 13.00% vs. 9.00% on GSM8K at 16K context), but degrades more sharply beyond the upper bound, where Dynamic NTK's adaptive scaling provides a gentler decline. With base=500 and 16K tuning, the method surpasses Dynamic NTK beyond 48K context length, where the perplexity curve remains smooth while Dynamic NTK shows clear degradation. Against **Linear PI** (Chen et al., 2023), the proposed method achieves lower perplexity on Books3 across all extrapolated lengths in Figure 1, particularly at 100K+ context where Linear PI's fixed interpolation ratio becomes insufficient. Against **Code LLaMA** (Rozière et al., 2023), which uses base=1000000 with 16K training to reach 100K context, this paper achieves comparable 100K extrapolation with only 4K tuning length, and further extends to ~1M context with base=500 — a capability Code LLaMA does not demonstrate. On short-context benchmarks (Tables 3–4), the proposed method with larger bases (40000–1000000) maintains performance within ~1–2% of the original LLaMA2, while smaller bases (500–2608) show a 2–5% drop on tasks like MMLU and SuperGLUE, indicating a trade-off between extrapolation gain and short-context fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on RoPE (Su et al., 2021) and the growing body of work on extending RoPE-based LLMs beyond their training context, including Dynamic NTK (bloc97), Code LLaMA (Rozière et al., 2023), YaRN (Peng et al., 2023), and Linear Position Interpolation (Chen et al., 2023). It departs from the prevailing 'increase the base' paradigm by demonstrating that reducing the rotary base also improves extrapolation, and unifies both directions under a single periodic-perspective framework centered on the concept of a critical dimension. The paper also connects to the 'limit self-attention' school (Ratner et al., Han et al.) by identifying the specific dimensions responsible for attention score explosion, providing a mechanistic explanation that prior empirical observations lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The critical dimension framework offers a principled, closed-form explanation for why RoPE extrapolation fails and how to fix it, replacing ad-hoc heuristics with a unified scaling law that predicts extrapolation bounds from base value and tuning length. Practically, achieving ~1M context with only 16K tuning (base 500) or 100K with 4K tuning (base 1000000) on LLaMA2 is a significant efficiency win over methods requiring 64K+ tuning. Open questions include whether the critical dimension analysis generalizes to non-RoPE positional encodings, whether the theoretical bounds hold for architectures with different head dimensions, and whether the short-context degradation observed with very small bases (e.g., base 500 on MMLU) can be mitigated without sacrificing extrapolation gains.
