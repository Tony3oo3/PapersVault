## Problem

MinHash-based near-duplicate detection, the dominant approach for over two decades, is fragile to typos and adversarial hash-busting attacks due to its reliance on n-grams, while existing neural embeddings are too large for efficient large-scale deployment and use sub-word tokenization that is similarly vulnerable to character-level perturbations. No prior method has achieved both the robustness of n-gram approaches and the fine-grained similarity discrimination of neural embeddings, and no multilingual benchmark systematically evaluates near-duplicate retrieval under adversarial text manipulations.

## Input / Output

RETSim accepts arbitrary-length text, which is split into 512-character chunks during vectorization; each Unicode character is encoded as a compact 24-bit binary representation via the RETVec character encoder, enabling full multilingual support without language-specific tokenizers. The model produces two outputs in a single forward pass: a 256-dim L2-normalized global embedding (RETSim_Near-Dup) for full-text similarity matching, and a set of 256-dim per-chunk partial embeddings (RETSim_Partial-Dup) for detecting near-duplicate content that appears in only part of a document. Both embedding types are compared via cosine similarity.

## Method

RETSim combines the RETVec character-level vectorizer (encoding each Unicode character as a 24-bit binary vector) with two Gated Attention Unit (GAU) transformer blocks, a Generalized-Mean pooling layer, and a dense projection to 256 dimensions, totaling only 536k parameters—over two orders of magnitude smaller than competing neural embeddings. Training uses Multi-Similarity Loss on the multilingual C4 corpus, where each text chunk is augmented with up to 25% sentence-level and 30% combined word/character-level perturbations (insertions, deletions, substitutions, transpositions, homoglyphs, QWERTY typos, etc.), pulling augmented versions of the same text closer while pushing dissimilar texts apart. The key architectural insight is that character-level encoding, rather than sub-word tokenization, provides inherent resilience to typos and adversarial manipulations, while the GAU blocks (outperforming BERT, T5, ConvNeXt, and MLP alternatives in ablations) provide sufficient sequence modeling capacity at minimal cost. The dual-output design—global averaging of chunk embeddings for full-text matching and direct chunk embeddings for partial matching—allows a single model to serve both retrieval and partial-duplicate detection use cases.

## Results

On the W4NT3D benchmark (41 languages), RETSim_Near-Dup achieves an average Recall@1 of 0.977, outperforming the best neural baseline Multilingual E5-Base (0.937) and MinHash (0.538) by 4.0 and 43.9 points respectively. On the NEWS-COPY deduplication dataset, RETSim_Partial-Dup achieves 0.831 ARI, surpassing the best MinHash configuration (0.783) by 4.8 points. On the CORE Near-Duplicates dataset, RETSim_Partial-Dup achieves 0.945 Macro F1, edging out Multilingual E5-Base (0.941) and MinHash+LSH (0.921). On adversarial spam email clustering, RETSim_Near-Dup achieves 0.949 V-Measure, outperforming USE (0.903) and SimHash+LSH (0.871).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RETSim dominates all baselines on the W4NT3D benchmark, with RETSim_Near-Dup (0.977 avg Recall@1) beating the strongest neural baseline Multilingual E5-Base (0.937) by 4.0 points and MinHash (0.538) by 43.9 points; the only exceptions are Chinese and Japanese, where larger sub-word-tokenized embeddings (LaBSE, E5-Base) slightly edge out RETSim. On NEWS-COPY, RETSim_Partial-Dup (0.831 ARI) outperforms MinHash (0.783), Multilingual E5-Base (0.742), and S-BERT (0.700), with the gap widening for pairs with large length ratios. On CORE Near-Duplicates, RETSim_Partial-Dup (0.945 Macro F1) narrowly beats Multilingual E5-Base (0.941) and clearly surpasses MinHash+LSH (0.921) and LaBSE (0.933). On Wiki-40B deduplication, RETSim_Partial-Dup identifies 12.77% of training examples as having duplicates versus only 0.47% for MinHash+LSH and 2.76% for exact substring matching, revealing far more hidden duplication. On adversarial spam clustering, RETSim_Near-Dup (0.949 V-Measure, 0.747 ARI) outperforms both USE (0.903 V-Measure) and SimHash+LSH (0.871 V-Measure), confirming its robustness against hash-busting attacks in production settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

RETSim extends the RETVec character-level vectorizer (Bursztein et al., 2023) from short-text classification to full-document near-duplicate detection, replacing the dominant MinHash/LSH paradigm that has governed this task for over two decades. It deliberately departs from standard neural text embeddings (LaBSE, USE, E5) that rely on sub-word tokenization and optimize for semantic similarity, instead using a 536k-parameter GAU-based transformer trained with Multi-Similarity Loss on typo-augmented data to produce metric embeddings specifically for syntactic near-duplicate matching. The paper also introduces the W4NT3D benchmark, filling a gap identified in the related work: no prior multilingual benchmark systematically evaluates near-duplicate retrieval under adversarial text manipulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 536k-parameter model outperforms neural embeddings 100–800× larger on near-duplicate detection while running at near-competitive speed on modern GPUs, and it has been deployed in production at Google for spam detection since December 2022. The W4NT3D benchmark exposes a previously underappreciated weakness in large multilingual embeddings (e.g., PaLM 2 Gecko drops to 0.497 Recall@1 on Arabic vs. 0.971 for RETSim), which is important for practitioners relying on these models for deduplication. Open questions include whether the metric-learning-plus-augmentation training regime generalizes to other modalities (images, audio) or to robust semantic embeddings, and whether further engineering optimization can close the remaining 1.5× speed gap with MinHash on high-end GPUs.
