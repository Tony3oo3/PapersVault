## Problem

Pre-trained vision-language models like CLIP propagate societal biases and rely on spurious correlations in their zero-shot predictions, but existing debiasing methods are limited in three ways: they only address spurious correlations (not intrinsic attribute dependencies), are specialized to either supervised or unsupervised training, and are computationally expensive to train. This paper seeks a single general framework that handles both bias types, works with or without ground-truth labels, and trains efficiently.

## Input / Output

The method consumes frozen CLIP image features (X_I) and text features (X_T) extracted from a pre-trained backbone (e.g., ViT-L/14 or ResNet-50), along with target attribute labels Y (ground-truth or pseudo-labels from CLIP's zero-shot cosine similarity) and sensitive attribute labels S (always pseudo-labeled from CLIP). It outputs two learned encoder functions f_I and f_T that map the original CLIP representations to debiased representations Z_I and Z_T, which are then used for zero-shot classification via cosine similarity against text prompts. The representation dimensionality r is set to c−1 where c is the number of target classes, and Random Fourier Features approximate the RBF kernel to avoid O(n³) kernel matrix computation.

## Method

FairerCLIP formulates debiasing as an optimization over functions in a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) with an RBF Gaussian kernel, using a simplified HSIC-based dependence measure that captures all linear and nonlinear statistical relationships. The objective jointly maximizes dependence between representations and the target label Y, penalizes dependence on the sensitive attribute S (weighted by τ_I and τ_T), and preserves image-text alignment (weighted by τ_z). Crucially, fixing either the image or text encoder reduces the problem to a generalized eigenvalue problem with a closed-form solution (Theorem 2), enabling alternating optimization that converges 4×–10× faster than iterative baselines. When ground-truth labels are unavailable, pseudo-labels are initialized from CLIP's zero-shot predictions and the target pseudo-labels are refined after each iteration, while sensitive pseudo-labels remain fixed to avoid removing the very information being debiased. A disentanglement constraint (unit covariance plus RKHS inner-product regularization) ensures compact, numerically stable representations.

## Results

On CelebA with intrinsic dependency (high cheekbones vs. sex), FairerCLIP reduces the Equal Opportunity Difference (EOD) to 0.02% (CLIP ResNet-50) and 0.005% (CLIP ViT-L/14), compared to 1.0% and 6.3% for the best baseline (Contrastive Adapter), while maintaining ~83–84% average accuracy. On spurious correlation benchmarks (Waterbirds and CelebA blonde hair), FairerCLIP achieves the lowest Gap (6.1% and 2.5%) and highest Worst-Group accuracy (86.0% and 85.2%) on CLIP ViT-L/14 with labels, and outperforms the only other label-free baseline (Orth-Cali) in the w/o labels setting. Training time is 32 s on Waterbirds and 222 s on CelebA, roughly 4–37× faster than the next-fastest baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FairerCLIP is compared against six baselines spanning supervised (ERM Linear Probe, ERM Adapter, DFR, Contrastive Adapter) and unsupervised (Zero-shot CLIP, Orth-Cali) debiasing methods. On the intrinsic-dependency task (CelebA high cheekbones), FairerCLIP dramatically outperforms all baselines on EOD (0.02% vs. 1.0% for Contrastive Adapter, the best baseline), while most other methods actually amplify bias (EOD 4–19%). On spurious-correlation Waterbirds with labels, FairerCLIP achieves the best WG accuracy (86.0%) and lowest Gap (6.1%), narrowly beating Contrastive Adapter (85.3% WG, 9.3% Gap) but at a small cost in average accuracy (92.2% vs. 94.5%). On CelebA blonde hair with labels, FairerCLIP wins on both WG (85.2% vs. 83.9%) and Gap (2.5% vs. 6.4%) over Contrastive Adapter. In the label-free setting, FairerCLIP substantially outperforms Orth-Cali on both datasets (e.g., 78.1% vs. 68.8% WG on Waterbirds; 86.1% vs. 76.1% WG on CelebA). On the small-sample CFD experiment (597 samples), all baselines nearly fail at worst-group debiasing, while FairerCLIP achieves a Gap of 21.8% versus 53.7% for Contrastive Adapter. Training time is 4–37× faster than all baselines (32 s vs. 1202 s on Waterbirds).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the RKHS-based invariant representation learning framework of Sadeghi et al. (2022) — specifically its HSIC-based dependence measure with Cholesky factorization and closed-form generalized eigenvalue solution — to the problem of debiasing frozen CLIP image and text representations. It departs from prior CLIP debiasing work (Zhang & Re 2022, Chuang et al. 2023, Kirichenko et al. 2022, Gao et al. 2021) in two key ways: (1) it handles both spurious correlations and intrinsic attribute dependencies, whereas existing methods only address spurious correlations, and (2) it operates uniformly with or without ground-truth labels via pseudo-label initialization and iterative refinement, whereas prior methods are specialized to one setting or the other. The alternating optimization with closed-form solvers replaces the iterative gradient-based training used by most baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FairerCLIP provides a single, unified debiasing framework for CLIP that covers a broader range of bias types and label availability scenarios than any prior method, while being 4–10× faster to train due to closed-form updates. Its ability to function under severely limited data (e.g., 298 training samples on CFD) where gradient-based baselines fail entirely makes it practically relevant for low-resource fairness auditing. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to VLMs beyond CLIP (e.g., Flamingo, LLaVA), whether it scales to multi-class or continuous sensitive attributes, and whether the kernel-based formulation can be extended to handle multiple simultaneous sensitive attributes without combinatorial blowup in the objective.
