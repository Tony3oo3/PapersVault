## Problem

Existing multimodal LLMs fail to unify visual comprehension and generation within a single framework, largely because dominant visual tokenizers (e.g., VQ-VAE) capture only low-level pixel details and produce 2D-contextualized tokens incompatible with the unidirectional autoregressive attention in LLMs. The paper argues that the prerequisite for emergent multimodal capabilities is a visual tokenizer whose discrete codes carry high-level semantics comparable to words and are generated with 1D causal dependency, enabling seamless integration into the LLM's native next-word-prediction objective.

## Input / Output

Images are resized to 224×224 and discretized into exactly 32 causal visual codes (drawn from an 8192-entry VQ codebook) that are appended to the LLM vocabulary as new tokens; videos are sampled as 4–8 frames, each independently tokenized into 32 codes. The model outputs either free-form text (captions, QA answers, stories) or a single generation-embedding token that is decoded into a realistic image via a frozen unCLIP-SD UNet. During multi-turn interaction, the model can interleave image and text outputs in a single autoregressive sequence, producing sequentially generated images or instruction-followed edits without any separate generation head.

## Method

SEED tokenizer converts 2D ViT patch features (16×16 from a frozen EVA-CLIP encoder) into 32 causal semantic embeddings via a Causal Q-Former that uses a causal self-attention mask so each query attends only to preceding queries, then discretizes them through an 8192-entry VQ codebook. Training proceeds in two stages: Stage I aligns the causal embeddings with text features via Noise-Contrastive Estimation; Stage II jointly optimizes the VQ codebook (reconstruction of causal embeddings + MSE alignment of a 1-token generation embedding to the unCLIP-SD image embedding) so the same codes serve both comprehension and generation. SEED-LLaMA is then built by expanding the LLM vocabulary with the 8192 visual codes and performing large-scale multimodal pretraining (LoRA followed by full fine-tuning) on interleaved image-text, video-text, and web-scale interleaved documents under a unified next-word-prediction loss, followed by supervised instruction tuning on diverse task-specific datasets.

## Results

SEED-LLaMA-I (14B) achieves 126.9 CIDEr on COCO captioning, 63.4% on VQAv2, 43.2% on OKVQA, 49.4% on VizWiz, and 53.7% on SEED-Bench, outperforming all prior multimodal LLMs that also support image generation. On text-to-image generation, SEED-LLaMA-I scores 70.68 CLIP similarity on COCO and 66.55 on Flickr30K, surpassing GILL (67.45/65.16) and Emu (66.46/64.82). For image-text retrieval, SEED's causal embeddings achieve R@mean of 93.6 on Flickr30K and 82.5 on COCO, exceeding BLIP-2's 92.9 and 80.3 respectively. On video QA, SEED-LLaMA-I (14B) reaches 45.2% on MSVDQA and 35.3% on MSRVTTQA, substantially outperforming Emu (18.8% and 8.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEED-LLaMA-I (14B) decisively outperforms Emu (14B) across nearly all comprehension benchmarks: 126.9 vs 112.4 CIDEr on COCO, 63.4% vs 52.0% on VQAv2, and 45.2% vs 18.8% on MSVDQA, demonstrating that discrete causal visual tokens with high-level semantics are superior to continuous visual representations for unified comprehension and generation. Against CM3Leon (7B), SEED-LLaMA-I leads by a wide margin on COCO captioning (126.9 vs 61.6 CIDEr), confirming that the SEED tokenizer's high-level semantics address the low-level information bottleneck of prior VQ-based tokenizers. Compared to BLIP-2 (4.1B), SEED-LLaMA-I (14B) achieves comparable VQAv2 scores (63.4% vs 63.0%) while adding image generation capability and substantially outperforming on VizWiz (49.4% vs 29.8%). Against the much larger IDEFICS (80B), SEED-LLaMA-I (14B) wins on COCO (126.9 vs 91.8) and VizWiz (49.4% vs 36.0%) but trails slightly on OKVQA (43.2% vs 45.2%). For text-to-image generation, SEED-LLaMA-I achieves 70.68 CLIP score on COCO, beating GILL (67.45) and Emu (66.46), while remaining close to the unCLIP-SD upper bound (79.30) for image reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on BLIP-2's Q-Former architecture (adapted with causal masking) and unCLIP-SD's latent diffusion decoder, combined with a VQ-VAE codebook, to create a discrete image tokenizer specifically designed for autoregressive LLMs. It departs from prior unified multimodal models (CM3Leon, Emu) by identifying two critical tokenizer design principles: 1D causal dependency (rather than 2D bilateral context) and high-level semantic alignment with text tokens (rather than low-level pixel reconstruction). Unlike CogView or GILL which focus solely on generation, and unlike BLIP-2 or Flamingo which output only text, SEED-LLaMA unifies both comprehension and generation under a single next-word-prediction objective, treating visual codes as new vocabulary entries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a well-designed discrete tokenizer can enable LLMs to perform scalable multimodal autoregression without architectural modifications, and that this unified framework gives rise to emergent multi-turn in-context multimodal generation capabilities. However, generation quality is bounded by the frozen SD-UNet (CLIP scores ~70 vs. unCLIP's ~79), video understanding remains weak, and the 32-token budget limits image fidelity. Open questions include whether end-to-end training of the decoder could close the generation gap, whether the causal dependency principle generalizes to higher-resolution or longer-sequence tokenization, and whether the approach scales to truly open-world AGI-like behavior beyond the demonstrated in-context examples.
