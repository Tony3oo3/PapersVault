## Problem

While plasticity loss is recognized as a fundamental challenge in visual reinforcement learning, the specific roles of data augmentation, individual agent modules (encoder, actor, critic), and training stages in driving or mitigating this loss remain poorly understood. This knowledge gap prevents targeted interventions and leaves the high replay ratio dilemma—where increased data reuse exacerbates plasticity loss—unresolved, as prior work applies static RR values or uniform parameter resets throughout training.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes RGB image observations (84×84 pixels) from DeepMind Control Suite or Atari-100K environments and outputs continuous control actions (DMC) or discrete actions (Atari). Beyond standard I/O, the method internally monitors the Fraction of Active Units (FAU) of the critic network every 50 episodes (5×10⁴ environment steps), using the FAU trajectory as a signal to modulate the replay ratio. The encoder is a CNN (ResNet for DMC, convolutional layers for Atari) that processes raw pixels into a 50-dimensional feature vector feeding the actor-critic heads.

## Method

Adaptive RR dynamically adjusts the replay ratio based on the critic's plasticity level, starting at a low RR (0.5) to prevent catastrophic early-stage plasticity loss and switching to a high RR (2) once the critic's FAU stabilizes (consecutive-checkpoint FAU difference < 0.001). This is grounded in three empirical findings from factorial experiments: (1) data augmentation is the single most effective intervention for preserving plasticity, outperforming Reset, L2-Init, spectral normalization, and CReLU; (2) the critic—not the encoder or actor—suffers the most severe plasticity loss and is the primary bottleneck for sample efficiency, confirmed via plasticity injection diagnostics; (3) plasticity loss in the early bootstrapping phase is irrecoverable, while later-stage decline is benign. The method is applied on top of DrQ-v2 (DMC) and DrQ(ε) (Atari) with all other hyperparameters unchanged, requiring only a lightweight FAU check at each interval to trigger the RR transition.

## Results

On the DeepMind Control Suite (2M env steps), Adaptive RR achieves 880±45 (Cheetah Run), 758±12 (Walker Run), and 784±53 (Quadruped Run), outperforming the best static RR baselines on Walker Run and Quadruped Run. On Atari-100K, Adaptive RR (RR: 0.5→2) achieves 55.8% mean HNS and 48.7% median HNS, substantially surpassing the best static RR baseline (42.3% mean HNS at RR=0.5) and ReDo (42.3% mean HNS at RR=1). Adaptive RR also attains 4 superhuman games and 11 best-of-method games, compared to at most 3 and 3 for any static configuration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DrQ-v2 with static RR=0.5 (Yarats et al., 2021), Adaptive RR improves mean HNS on Atari-100K from 42.3% to 55.8% and median HNS from 22.6% to 48.7%, while also increasing superhuman games from 3 to 4 and best-of-method games from 0 to 11. Against DrQ-v2 with static RR=2, Adaptive RR recovers the plasticity loss that causes RR=2 to underperform RR=0.5 (35.1% vs 42.3% mean HNS on Atari), achieving 55.8% instead. Against Reset (Nikishin et al., 2022) at RR=2, Adaptive RR wins on Walker Run (758 vs 749) and Quadruped Run (784 vs 511) on DMC, while roughly matching on Cheetah Run (880 vs 885). Against ReDo (Sokar et al., 2023) at RR=1, Adaptive RR achieves higher mean HNS on Atari (55.8% vs 42.3%) and more best-of-method games (11 vs 3), though ReDo has a slight edge on median HNS (41.6% vs 48.7% — actually Adaptive RR wins here too). Overall, Adaptive RR dominates all static RR baselines and plasticity-mitigation methods across both continuous and discrete control benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a systematic empirical investigation into plasticity loss in visual RL, building directly on the plasticity-loss mitigation literature (Reset, ReDo, L2-Init, Plasticity Injection) and the high replay-ratio dilemma identified by D'Oro et al. (2022) and Nikishin et al. (2022). Rather than proposing yet another regularization or reset mechanism, it departs from prior work by conducting a factorial decomposition across three orthogonal axes—data augmentation, agent modules (encoder/actor/critic), and training stages—that had not been jointly analyzed. The resulting Adaptive RR method repurposes the critic's Fraction of Active Units as a dynamic scheduling signal, an orthogonal approach to the static high-RR-plus-reset paradigm that dominated prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that data augmentation—not representation learning—is the primary mechanism preserving plasticity, and that the critic (not the encoder) is the critical bottleneck, challenges the long-standing narrative attributing VRL's sample inefficiency to poor visual representation. This reframing could redirect significant research effort away from increasingly elaborate self-supervised pretraining toward simpler data-level interventions and critic-specific plasticity maintenance. Open questions include whether these stage-dependent dynamics hold in more complex multi-task or continual settings, whether FAU remains a reliable plasticity proxy in deeper or transformer-based architectures, and whether Adaptive RR's simple threshold-based switching can be refined for more nuanced training regimes.
