## Problem

Existing feature interaction detection methods operate on a single pre-specified model, but a well-trained model may fail to preserve true feature interactions, and multiple equally accurate models can exhibit dramatically different interaction strengths. This paper argues that feature interactions should be explored across a Rashomon set (a collection of similarly accurate models) rather than attributed to one model, and introduces the Feature Interaction Score (FIS) and its cloud (FISC) to quantify this variability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241076/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained reference model (MLP, logistic classifier, or pre-trained ViT-B-16), a dataset with features and labels, a loss tolerance ε, and a model class specification. It produces per-feature main effect values, pairwise or higher-order FIS values, the FISC range [FIS_min, FIS_max] across the model class, and two visualizations: a Halo plot (mapping joint effects on a sphere of fixed main-effect sum) and a swarm plot (showing the distribution of FIS values across sampled models, colored by loss). For image classification, the input image is first segmented via quickshift into patch-level features before interaction analysis.

## Method

FIS is defined as the difference between the loss change from simultaneously replacing a feature set I and the sum of loss changes from replacing each feature individually, capturing statistical non-additivity. The FISC is the set of all FIS values across models in a mask-based Rashomon set, where alternative models are generated by concatenating a multi-valued mask m onto the backbone model. A greedy search algorithm explores upper and lower bounds of each mask component with a uniform learning rate, halving the step when the loss tolerance ε is exceeded, to empirically approximate FISC bounds. For a sigmoid-activated MLP, the paper derives closed-form analytical bounds on FISC by solving the Rashomon set condition on m. The framework is validated against ArchDetect (showing FIS reduces to ArchDetect's δ under RMSE loss), compared against ANOVA, NID, Shapley-based methods, and VIC, and applied to recidivism prediction (COMPAS), image classification (ViT on COCO), and MXenes battery material property prediction.

## Results

On four synthetic interaction-detection benchmarks (F1–F4), FIS achieves a perfect AUC of 1.0 on all functions, matching the SOTA ArchDetect method and substantially outperforming all other baselines (which score 0.48–0.56 on F2–F4). On the COMPAS recidivism dataset, the greedy search algorithm produces MCR ranges (e.g., 1.123–1.372 for 'prior') that are wider than those from the VIC baseline, indicating a broader exploration of the Rashomon set. For FISC range estimation, greedy sampling yields interaction ranges roughly 10–100× wider than AWP or random sampling (e.g., age vs. currentcharge: −0.00257 to 0.00268 vs. −0.0003 to 0.000351 for AWP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FIS ties ArchDetect (Tsang et al., 2020b) with a perfect AUC of 1.0 on all four synthetic functions, confirming the theoretical equivalence shown in Appendix D. Against the four other single-model baselines—Two-way ANOVA, Neural Interaction Detection, Shapley Interaction Index, and Shapley Taylor Interaction Index—FIS achieves a decisive win on F2–F4 (AUC 1.0 vs. 0.48–0.56), where those methods fail to detect the correct interaction structure. On the COMPAS recidivism task, the paper's greedy search produces wider MCR ranges than the VIC baseline (Dong & Rudin, 2020), e.g., 1.123–1.372 for 'prior' vs. 1.123–1.372 for VIC, and reveals higher-order compensation structures (e.g., 'prior'–'charge' interaction) that VIC's pairwise analysis misses. For FISC range estimation, greedy sampling explores substantially broader interaction ranges than both AWP sampling (Wu et al., 2020) and random weight-initialization sampling (Li & Barnard, 2022a); for instance, the 'gender vs. currentcharge' pair spans −0.01362 to 0.003708 under greedy sampling versus −0.00035 to 0.000211 under AWP and −2.01E-05 to 1.61E-05 under random sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Rashomon set framework (Fisher et al. 2019; Dong & Rudin 2020), which previously characterized variability in feature *importance* across equally accurate models, to the underexplored dimension of feature *interaction* strength. It builds directly on single-model interaction detection methods—most notably ArchDetect from the Archipelago framework (Tsang et al. 2020b), which the authors show is a special case of their Feature Interaction Score under RMSE loss—and on the broader family of interaction attribution tools (Shapley Taylor, Integrated Hessians, Additive Groves, H-statistics). The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a mask-based Rashomon set and a greedy search algorithm to sample models within it, shifting the unit of analysis from one model's interaction profile to a cloud of interaction scores (FISC) spanning a model class.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central claim—that a well-trained model's interaction attribution may not preserve true feature interactions, and that the FISC range across a Rashomon set can recover interactions a single model misses—has practical implications for high-stakes domains like recidivism prediction where interaction structure affects fairness. However, the evidence is somewhat limited: the image classification demonstration uses a single COCO image, the synthetic validation relies on hand-crafted functions, and the mask-based Rashomon set is a specific (though general) characterization that may not capture all model-class structure. Open questions include scalability of the greedy search to very high-dimensional feature spaces, whether the FISC range tightens with more diverse model families, and how to integrate FISC into model selection pipelines that prioritize desired interaction structures.
