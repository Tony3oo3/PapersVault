## Problem

Existing SFT-based alignment methods rely on response-level quality labels (good vs. bad), which tell the model *that* a response is subpar but not *which specific tokens or phrases* cause the quality gap. This limits the model to imitation without understanding the underlying alignment behaviors. RLHF can discriminate at finer granularity but is complex, memory-intensive, and prone to training instability such as reward collapse.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes triples (X, Ŷ, Ỹ): a text query, the model's own initial (subpar) response, and a minimally revised high-quality response generated by ChatGPT. The Levenshtein edit operations between Ŷ and Ỹ determine per-token weights, so the loss operates at token granularity rather than sequence level. At inference, the aligned model takes a standard text prompt and produces a single aligned text response, with no additional inputs required.

## Method

FIGA constructs the SPA dataset by (1) rolling out an initial model (Alpaca-7b) on preference and instruction data, (2) filtering to retain only instances where the model's reward score is subpar and the gap to the reference is large, and (3) prompting ChatGPT to make minimal edits to the model's own output guided by the reference, thereby reducing distribution shift. The core training loss uses Levenshtein distance between the initial and revised responses to assign token-level weights: tokens added or substituted in the revised response receive a positive weight α (encouraging the model to generate them), tokens deleted or substituted in the initial response receive a negative weight β (discouraging them), and unchanged tokens receive weight γ (typically 0, ignoring them). This weighted cross-entropy loss simultaneously increases the probability of desired tokens and decreases the probability of undesired tokens in a single forward pass, functioning as a simplified token-wise advantage function that captures the RLHF objective without requiring a separate critic model, PPO, or multiple model copies.

## Results

FIGA achieves the highest average benchmark score of 34.9 across MMLU, TruthfulQA, CrowS-Pairs, WinoGender, Vicuna, and WizardLM, outperforming the initial Alpaca-7b model by 3.2 points and the strongest PPO baseline (trained on 85K data) by 1.8 points. On the reward score metric, FIGA reaches 4.62, surpassing SFT (4.56) and PPO(85K) (4.54). FIGA also leads on individual benchmarks including TruthfulQA (42.0 vs. 36.7 for PPO(85K)), MMLU (40.8 vs. 40.1 for DPO), and Vicuna (8.6 vs. 8.4 for SFT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FIGA outperforms all five baselines on the average benchmark score. Against SFT (31.1), FIGA gains +3.8 points, with particularly large improvements on TruthfulQA (42.0 vs. 22.0) and MMLU (40.8 vs. 39.3). Against PPO trained on the full 85K dataset (33.1), FIGA still leads by +1.8 points while using only 17K training instances, and achieves a higher reward score (4.62 vs. 4.54). Against CoH (32.7) and DPO (32.7), FIGA gains +2.2 points on average, with the largest margin on TruthfulQA (42.0 vs. 28.2 and 34.8 respectively). Against RRHF (31.3), FIGA leads by +3.6 points, though RRHF achieves a slightly higher WinoGender score (60.0 vs. 59.6). Notably, FIGA is the only method that simultaneously improves both alignment (reward score) and task-solving ability (MMLU) over the initial Alpaca-7b model, whereas SFT degrades TruthfulQA from 33.7 to 22.0.

## Relation to Prior Work

FIGA extends the alignment-without-RL line of work (CoH, RRHF, DPO) by replacing response-level quality labels with token-level quality signals derived from Levenshtein edit operations between a model's initial response and a minimally revised version. Conceptually, it draws on the edit-based re-alignment idea from Liu et al. (2022) and the RLHF framework (Ouyang et al., 2022; Schulman et al., 2017), but reframes the PPO advantage function as a simple, hand-designed token weighting scheme that avoids a learned critic. The SPA dataset construction departs from prior alignment datasets by focusing exclusively on subpar cases and using LLM-based minimal revision to reduce distribution shift, rather than training on the full instruction pool or on human-written references.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FIGA offers a practical, SFT-like training procedure that captures some of the discriminative signal RLHF provides, which is valuable given the well-known instability and engineering complexity of PPO-based alignment. However, the approach is validated only on a 7B model (Alpaca-7b) and relies on GPT-3.5-turbo for the revision step, raising open questions about scalability to larger models and the cost/availability of the revision oracle. A key limitation is that Levenshtein distance captures surface-level edits (addition, deletion, substitution) but may miss semantically important rephrasings that preserve token identity, and the fixed threshold for selecting bad tokens (negative log-likelihood ≤ 0.6) is heuristic. Future work could explore whether the token-weighting principle generalizes to multi-turn dialogue or to settings where no clean 'revision' pair is available.
