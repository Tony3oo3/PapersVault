## Problem

Existing neuron-based OOD detection methods either modify network activations (e.g., ReAct's truncation, which degrades InD accuracy) or reduce neurons to binary activated/inactive states via thresholds, discarding valuable distributional details. The paper asks whether a simple statistical property of continuous neuron activation distributions—specifically, their coverage under InD training data—can simultaneously improve OOD detection and provide a principled criterion for evaluating model generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240412/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained neural network (ResNet-18/50, ViT-b16/t16) and a small subset of InD training images (as few as 1,000 samples, ~1–5% of the training set) to build a per-neuron histogram-based PDF over activation states. At test time, a single RGB image (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet) is forwarded through the unmodified network, and the method outputs either a scalar uncertainty score (NAC-UE, for OOD/InD classification) or a scalar robustness score (NAC-ME, for model selection). The approach is entirely post-hoc: no retraining or architectural modification is required.

## Method

The core mechanism formulates each neuron's activation state as ẑ = σ(z ⊙ ∂D_KL/∂z), combining the raw neuron output z with its KL-divergence gradient (which encodes the neuron's influence on the final prediction and the model's confidence). A Neuron Activation Coverage (NAC) function Φ(ẑ; r) = (1/r)·min(κ(ẑ), r) is then defined from the histogram-approximated PDF κ of each neuron's state under InD training data, where r caps the density to avoid bias from homogeneous samples. For OOD detection (NAC-UE), the per-sample uncertainty is the mean coverage score across all neurons in selected layers; low coverage indicates the input triggers rarely-seen neuron states. For OOD generalization (NAC-ME), model robustness is scored by integrating the NAC function over the full activation space (Riemann approximation), so models whose neurons are broadly exercised by InD data receive higher scores. The key distinction from prior neuron-coverage work is that NAC operates on continuous, influence-weighted states per individual neuron rather than on the binary proportion of activated neurons across the whole network.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, NAC-UE achieves a 10.60% FPR95 reduction (and 4.58% AUROC gain) over the strongest competitor ViM, and a 5.96% FPR95 reduction on CIFAR-10. On ImageNet-1k, NAC-UE attains 95.12% average AUROC on iNaturalist (ResNet-50: 96.52%) and 96.04% on Textures, outperforming all 21 prior post-hoc methods. For OOD generalization, NAC-ME surpasses the standard validation criterion by up to 11.61% Spearman rank correlation with OOD test accuracy on ViT-b16 (TerraInc), while also selecting models with higher OOD test accuracy across all four DomainBed datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NAC-UE outperforms all 21 compared post-hoc OOD detection methods on average across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet-1k. The most direct competitors are ViM (Wang et al., 2022) and KNN (Sun et al., 2022): on CIFAR-100, NAC-UE beats ViM by 10.60% FPR95 and 4.58% AUROC; on ImageNet iNaturalist with ResNet-50, NAC-UE reaches 96.52% AUROC vs. ViM's 89.56% and KNN's 86.41%. Neuron-based methods ReAct and ASH show competitive results on some datasets (e.g., ASH reaches 97.07% on iNaturalist/ResNet-50) but suffer severe InD accuracy degradation, whereas NAC-UE operates post-hoc without modifying the model. For OOD generalization, NAC-ME consistently outperforms the standard InD validation criterion in both rank correlation and selected-model OOD accuracy across ResNet-18/50 and ViT-t16/b16 on all four DomainBed datasets, with the largest gap (19.30% RC) on TerraInc/ViT-b16. NAC-ME also improves upon validation when combined with SoTA training algorithms CORAL (+6.49% RC) and SelfReg (+5.09% RC) on PACS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes two previously separate research threads: (1) the KL-divergence gradient formulation for modeling neuron influence on predictions (Huang et al., 2021b), and (2) neuron coverage criteria from software testing (Pei et al., 2017; Ma et al., 2018). It departs from prior neuron-based OOD methods like ReAct (Sun et al., 2021) and LINe (Ahn et al., 2023), which modify or binarize neuron activations, by instead preserving natural continuous neuron states and characterizing their distributional coverage under InD data. The NAC function is a per-neuron, distribution-aware refinement of the coarser 'proportion of activated neurons' coverage used in prior DNN testing work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NAC provides a unified, post-hoc lens for both OOD detection and model evaluation without modifying the trained network, which is practically important since methods like ReAct and ASH degrade InD accuracy. The finding that only ~1% of training data suffices to build the NAC function makes the approach highly efficient for large-scale deployment. Open questions include whether the coverage-based separation holds for near-OOD (subtle distribution shifts), whether the approach extends to non-classification tasks, and whether the positive NAC–generalization correlation is causal or merely correlational. The method's reliance on a specific layer choice and hyperparameter sensitivity (particularly to r and α) remains a practical limitation.
