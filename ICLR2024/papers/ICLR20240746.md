## Problem

Unsupervised RL has not scaled to complex, high-dimensional environments: pure exploration methods cannot cover the full state space (even 29-dim MuJoCo Ant), while mutual-information skill discovery methods lack a distance metric and thus discover only simple, static behaviors with limited coverage. No prior unsupervised RL method has successfully discovered diverse locomotion skills in pixel-based Quadruped or Humanoid environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240746/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives only raw environment observations—18-dim state vectors (HalfCheetah), 29-dim state vectors (Ant), or 64×64×3 RGB camera images (Quadruped, Humanoid, Kitchen)—with no proprioceptive state, no reward, and no prior knowledge. It outputs a skill-conditioned policy π(a|s,z) parameterized by a compact latent vector z (2-D continuous for Ant/Humanoid, 4-D for Quadruped, 16 or 24 discrete for HalfCheetah/Kitchen) and a lossy state representation φ(s) that preserves temporal distances. The representation φ enables zero-shot goal-reaching by simply setting z ∝ φ(goal) − φ(current) without training a separate goal-conditioned policy.

## Method

METRA replaces the KL-divergence-based mutual information objective with the Wasserstein Dependency Measure (WDM), where the 1-Wasserstein distance is defined over a metric space using temporal distance (minimum environment steps between two states) as the metric d. This makes the objective invariant to state representation, enabling pixel-based environments. Via Kantorovich–Rubenstein duality, a telescoping-sum decomposition, and setting ψ(z)=z, the WDM reduces to a tractable objective: maximize E[(φ(s′)−φ(s))ᵀz] subject to ‖φ(s)−φ(s′)‖₂≤1 for all adjacent state pairs, which is equivalent to a 1-Lipschitz constraint under temporal distance. Training uses SAC with reward r=(φ(s′)−φ(s))ᵀz and dual gradient descent on a Lagrange multiplier for the constraint. Theoretically, the paper proves that linear squared METRA is equivalent to PCA in the temporal embedding space, explaining why φ learns to capture the most temporally spread-out (non-linear) manifolds of the state space.

## Results

METRA achieves the best policy state coverage across all five environments, being the only method that discovers meaningful locomotion skills in pixel-based Quadruped and Humanoid where all prior methods fail. In the discrete-skill comparison (Table 1), METRA achieves 186.75±16.21 policy state coverage on HalfCheetah and 1387.75±77.38 on Ant, compared to 6.75±2.22 (DIAYN) and 6.75±2.06 (DGPO) on HalfCheetah, and 11.25±5.44 (DIAYN) and 7.00±3.83 (DGPO) on Ant. On five zero-shot goal-reaching downstream tasks, METRA outperforms LEXA (state-of-the-art unsupervised goal-reaching) on all tasks, including state-based Ant and pixel-based Humanoid where LEXA struggles. METRA also achieves the best total state coverage against six pure exploration methods (ICM, LBS, RND, APT, Plan2Explore, APS) in most environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

METRA decisively outperforms all 12 baselines across the evaluated environments. Against MI-based skill discovery methods (DIAYN, DADS, CIC), METRA achieves orders-of-magnitude higher state coverage because the Wasserstein objective actively maximizes temporal distances rather than merely ensuring distinguishability; DIAYN and DADS discover simple static behaviors with minimal exploration, while CIC (which adds an exploration bonus) still fails to cover large portions of the state space. Against the closest metric-based competitor LSD, METRA matches or exceeds performance on state-based environments but uniquely scales to pixel-based Quadruped and Humanoid, where LSD's Euclidean distance on image pixels is meaningless. Against six pure exploration methods (ICM, LBS, RND, APT, Plan2Explore, APS), METRA achieves the best total state coverage in most environments; these methods exhibit chaotic random behaviors and fail to fully explore even the 29-dim state-based Ant. Against LEXA (state-of-the-art unsupervised goal-reaching), METRA wins on all five goal-conditioned downstream tasks in a zero-shot manner, while LEXA struggles on Ant and pixel-based Humanoid due to the infeasibility of fully capturing transition dynamics. Against DGPO (diversity-guided policy optimization), METRA achieves ~27× higher policy state coverage on HalfCheetah and ~124× on Ant, confirming that metric-aware Wasserstein distances are far more effective than metric-agnostic KL-based diversity objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

METRA builds directly on the Wasserstein Dependency Measure (WDM) framework of Ozair et al. (2019) and the constrained inner-product skill discovery objectives of LSD (Park et al., 2022) and CSD (Park et al., 2023), from which it inherits both the codebase and the optimization structure. The key conceptual departure is replacing the Euclidean (or normalized Euclidean) distance metric with temporal distance, making the objective representation-agnostic and thus applicable to pixel-based environments. The paper also establishes a formal connection showing that linear squared METRA reduces to PCA under the temporal distance metric, and derives 'Wasserstein variants' of DIAYN, DADS, and CIC by simplifying the WDM objective in different ways, unifying several prior skill discovery methods under a single framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

METRA is the first unsupervised RL method to discover diverse locomotion behaviors in pixel-based Quadruped and Humanoid, demonstrating that the choice of distance metric (temporal vs. Euclidean) is the critical factor for scalability rather than the RL algorithm or objective structure. The paper convincingly shows that both pure exploration and MI-based skill discovery methods fundamentally fail in complex, high-dimensional environments, motivating the compact latent space + temporal metric approach. Limitations include the symmetric Euclidean embedding of potentially asymmetric temporal distances, the restriction to linear latent-space movement (from setting ψ(z)=z), a relatively small update-to-data ratio, and evaluation limited to locomotion/manipulation (no Atari or non-stationary settings). Open directions include combining the full WDM objective with contrastive learning techniques, using asymmetric quasimetrics, and integrating with model-based RL for improved sample efficiency.
