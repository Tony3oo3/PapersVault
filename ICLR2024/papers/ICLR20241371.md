## Problem

Current VideoQA methods treat reasoning tasks as cross-modality matching, requiring extensive paired QA annotations and failing to capture underlying causal structure in temporal dynamics. This limits their ability to perform genuine causal reasoning such as attributing accident causes or predicting counterfactual outcomes, and makes them vulnerable to spurious correlations and language biases in training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241371/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes video clips (32 uniformly sampled frames) and natural-language reasoning questions (attribution, counterfactual, or introspection types) as input. Objects are localized via QDTrack multi-object tracking, and 1024-dim CLIP RN50 features are extracted per agent. At inference, the system outputs a multiple-choice answer by matching the identified causal variable (root cause agent or predicted counterfactual effect) against answer candidates using CLIP similarity, with grammar parsing extracting key nouns from conditional clauses in the question to guide the matching.

## Method

LLCP introduces a Temporal Multivariate Generative Model (TMGM), a CVAE extension where each variable's latent state is generated from three modular sub-networks (f_H for historical state, f_N for neighborhood interactions, f_E for environmental context) fused by a final MLP, trained entirely self-supervised via ELBO on regular (non-accident) videos without any QA annotations. For accident attribution, the model identifies root causes by detecting variables whose reconstruction error (MRE) or reconstruction noise (ORN) deviates significantly from the learned causal mechanism. For counterfactual prediction, it follows Pearl's abduction-action-prediction procedure: estimating the exogenous noise from factual evidence, replacing cause-variable embeddings with counterfactual text-conditioned embeddings, and propagating through the learned dynamics. The paper further proves that Granger causal structure is identifiable in the estimated transition function under the model's assumptions, and uses a frozen CLIP model solely at inference to bridge identified visual variables to textual answer candidates.

## Results

On SUTD-TrafficQA, LLCP+CLIP achieves 33.3% multiple-choice accuracy without any QA pair annotations, outperforming the unsupervised CLIP baseline (27.5%) by 5.8 points and surpassing several supervised methods including CNN+LSTM (30.8%), VIS+LSTM (29.9%), and Avgpooling (30.5%). On Causal-VidQA, LLCP reaches 32.13% average accuracy on counterfactual reasoning questions, improving over the CLIP baseline (23.73%) by 8.4 points while remaining competitive with supervised approaches like B2A (41.51%) and CoMem (39.83%). In simulation experiments, LLCP achieves 63.34% recall and 63.01% F1 for root cause identification under function change, substantially outperforming LSTM-VAE (52.11% recall, 27.81% F1) and VAE (51.92% recall, 27.33% F1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLCP+CLIP (33.3%) outperforms all unsupervised baselines: CLIP (27.5%), OpenFlamingo with 2 examples (28.7%), and OpenFlamingo with 4 examples (29.1%) on SUTD-TrafficQA. Among supervised methods, LLCP beats CNN+LSTM (30.8%), VIS+LSTM (29.9%), and Avgpooling (30.5%), and is nearly on par with I3D+LSTM (33.2%) and BERT-VQA (33.7%), but trails stronger supervised approaches like HCRN (36.5%), Eclipse (37.1%), ATP+CLIP (35.6%), and Tem-Adapter (46.0%). On Causal-VidQA, LLCP (32.13% avg) surpasses the CLIP baseline (23.73%) and EVQA (22.00%) but falls short of the best supervised methods B2A (41.51%), CoMem (39.83%), HME (39.25%), HGA (38.77%), and HCRN (38.49%). The key finding is that LLCP, trained entirely without paired QA annotations, closes the gap to fully supervised methods by 3–8 points on both datasets, demonstrating that causal process learning provides a viable annotation-free alternative for reasoning-based VideoQA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Conditional Variational Auto-Encoder (CVAE) framework with tailored sub-networks that enforce a temporal multivariate causal structure, and adapts Pearl's three-step counterfactual procedure (abduction, action, prediction) to the video domain. It departs from conventional VideoQA methods that frame reasoning as cross-modality matching requiring paired QA annotations, and from prior causal representation learning work (Yao et al., 2022) that focuses on identifying latent variables rather than applying learned causal mechanisms to downstream reasoning tasks. The key conceptual departure is using self-supervised local auto-regression to learn stationary causal processes without any textual supervision, then leveraging reconstruction error and noise outlier detection for root cause identification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that causal reasoning in VideoQA can be achieved without paired QA annotations, which is practically significant given the cost and difficulty of obtaining domain-specific reasoning annotations (e.g., traffic accident attribution). However, the approach relies on the Granger causality assumption (no instantaneous effects, no latent confounders, stationarity), pre-trained CLIP and tracking models, and the real-world performance, while competitive with mid-range supervised methods, remains below the strongest supervised baselines (e.g., 33.3 vs. 46.0 on SUTD-TrafficQA). Open questions include whether causal identifiability holds under less stringent assumptions, whether the framework can be extended to non-stationary or confounded settings, and whether the reliance on pre-trained vision-language models can be eliminated.
