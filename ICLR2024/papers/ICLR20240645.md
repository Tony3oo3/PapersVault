## Problem

Public datasets for ML in EDA are scarce and limited: the prior CircuitNet dataset only covers CPU designs at 28nm planar CMOS, failing to represent the realistic chip design environment with diverse hardware types (CPU, GPU, AI Chip), advanced FinFET technology nodes, and multiple cross-stage prediction objectives. This gap hinders benchmarking, reproducibility, and the adoption of ML techniques by researchers outside the EDA community.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240645/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Features are extracted at multiple stages of the commercial flow (Design Compiler + Innovus at 14nm FinFET) and encoded in two modalities: 2D/3D image arrays on a grid (where one grid equals one pixel, with resolution directly affecting model performance) and heterogeneous hypergraphs from netlists. Image features include macro region, RUDY routing demand, cell density, pin configuration, and power maps (overall and time-decomposed 3D arrays); graph features encode pin positions, instance placements, and topological connectivity. Labels are congestion/DRV maps (2D arrays), IR-drop maps (2D arrays), and net delays (edge features on timing graphs), all provided in both raw EDA document format and Python data format.

## Method

CircuitNet 2.0 generates 10,791 samples across 8 open-source designs spanning CPU (RISCY, RISCY-FPU, zero-riscy, OpenC910-1), GPU (Vortex-large/small), and AI Chip (NVDLA-large/small) through complete commercial synthesis and physical design flows at 14nm FinFET, with parameter variations in frequency, utilization, aspect ratio, macro placement, and power grid spacing to explore the design space. The paper validates the dataset through three realistic ML tasks: (1) multi-modal prediction, where a GNN on timing graphs achieves R²=0.926 for net delay prediction versus 0.522 for an MLP baseline; (2) learning on imbalanced data, demonstrating cross-design generalization via FCN congestion prediction and showing that oversampling the minority (large-design) class improves NRMSE and SSIM; (3) transfer learning between technology nodes and designs, where pre-training on CircuitNet (28nm) accelerates convergence on CircuitNet 2.0 (14nm), and pre-training on CircuitNet 2.0 transfers to CircuitNet-ISPD15 with an initial rapid loss drop reflecting adaptation to unseen designs.

## Results

On CircuitNet 2.0, congestion prediction with FCN achieves NRMSE of 0.036±0.001 and SSIM of 0.872±0.005, outperforming cGAN (NRMSE 0.044±0.003, SSIM 0.796±0.007). For net delay prediction, a GNN model achieves R²=0.9262±0.005, substantially surpassing an MLP baseline (R²=0.5223±0.017), demonstrating the value of graph-structured features. All models show higher performance on CircuitNet 2.0 than on the original CircuitNet, indicating that the more diverse training set (CPU, GPU, AI Chip at 14nm FinFET) leads to better generalization on unseen designs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates four existing methods on CircuitNet 2.0 to validate dataset quality. For congestion prediction, FCN (Liu et al., 2021) achieves the best NRMSE (0.036) and SSIM (0.872), outperforming cGAN (Alawieh et al., 2020) by ~18% in NRMSE and ~9% in SSIM, confirming that deterministic image-to-image translation is more effective than generative approaches for this task. The GNN model (Yang et al., 2022) leverages topological information and is reported to achieve competitive or superior results, though exact numbers are in the appendix. For timing prediction, the GNN adapted from Guo et al. (2022) dramatically outperforms a flat MLP (R² 0.926 vs. 0.522), showing that graph structure is essential for capturing signal propagation. For IR-drop prediction, MAVIREC (Chhabria et al., 2021) is evaluated using ROC-based metrics on the imbalanced hotspot classification task. Across all tasks, models trained on CircuitNet 2.0 generalize better to unseen designs than those trained on the original CircuitNet, validating the dataset's increased diversity (14nm FinFET, CPU/GPU/AI Chip) as a key advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct successor to CircuitNet (Chai et al., 2022; 2023), the first open-source ML-for-EDA dataset, extending it from 28nm planar CMOS CPU-only designs to 14nm FinFET with CPU, GPU, and AI Chip designs generated through complete commercial EDA flows (Design Compiler + Innovus). It also positions itself against isolated EDA contest benchmarks (ISPD, ICCAD) and synthetic generators (BeGAN, OpenRoad-based flows) by providing cross-stage, multi-modal data (images and graphs) from a single unified flow. The ML tasks evaluated (congestion prediction via FCN/cGAN/GNN, IR-drop via MAVIREC, net delay via GNN) are all well-established in the EDA ML literature; the contribution is the dataset and the empirical study of realistic challenges (data imbalance, cross-PDK transfer) rather than new methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The EDA ML community has long lacked a public, large-scale, realistic benchmark comparable to ImageNet in computer vision, and CircuitNet 2.0 is the most substantial step toward filling that gap by covering multiple design types at industrial scale with an advanced FinFET PDK. The paper's empirical findings on data imbalance (oversampling helps when training sets are small) and cross-PDK transfer learning (faster convergence from pre-trained models) provide practical guidance, but the optimal strategies for leveraging such heterogeneous, imbalanced datasets remain open. Limitations include coverage of only one technology node (14nm), a fixed set of eight open-source designs, and NDA constraints that prevent sharing raw tool outputs, leaving questions about generalization to 7nm/5nm and proprietary design flows.
