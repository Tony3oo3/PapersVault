## Problem

In RLHF, proxy reward models are imperfect approximations of true human preferences, and optimizing against them leads to overoptimization—where proxy reward improves while true reward degrades. Gao et al. (2023) showed this problem persists regardless of proxy model size or data scale, but their setup assumed noise-free labels and did not explore whether ensembling reward models with conservative objectives could mitigate the issue. This paper fills that gap by systematically evaluating ensemble-based conservative optimization under both noise-free and 25% label-noise conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241985/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, a policy model (1.4B Pythia), and an ensemble of k=5 proxy reward models (7M, 44M, or 1.3B parameters, derived from Pythia with a scalar head) trained on AlpacaFarm preference data with optional 25% label noise. For BoN, it outputs the single response from n samples (up to 12,500) that maximizes the ensemble reward; for PPO, it outputs a fully optimized policy after 3,000 (or 6,000 for 1.3B RMs) training steps. Performance is evaluated against a 7B AlpacaFarm gold reward model, with KL divergence between initial and optimized policy serving as the optimization-degree axis.

## Method

The core contribution is combining an ensemble of k reward models (trained with identical data and hyperparameters but different random seeds) with three aggregation strategies during policy optimization: mean optimization (average of ensemble outputs), worst-case optimization (WCO, taking the minimum reward across ensemble members), and uncertainty-weighted optimization (UWO, subtracting λ times the intra-ensemble variance from the mean reward). WCO guarantees no overoptimization as long as at least one ensemble member does not overestimate the true reward, while UWO penalizes responses where ensemble members disagree, preventing exploitation of a single faulty model. For BoN, the ensemble reward directly ranks sampled responses; for PPO, it replaces the single reward in the PPO objective, optionally combined with a small KL penalty (β=0.01) to fully eliminate overoptimization. The approach is orthogonal to model and data scaling—gains from ensembling stack on top of gains from larger reward models or more training data—and is robust to ensemble size (4–5 members suffice) and the UWO uncertainty penalty λ.

## Results

For BoN sampling with 44M reward models, ensemble-based conservative optimization (WCO and UWO) eliminates overoptimization entirely and improves final gold reward model performance by ~30% (no label noise) and ~75% (25% label noise) over single reward model optimization. For PPO, WCO and UWO reduce overoptimization and, when combined with a small KL penalty of 0.01, completely prevent overoptimization at no performance cost, whereas single RM optimization requires a 20× larger KL penalty (0.2) to achieve the same effect, incurring a significant performance penalty. Win-rates against single RMs confirm the gains: UWO achieves 63.0% win-rate for PPO with 25% noise vs. 60.2% for mean optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is single reward model optimization (no ensemble), following the setup of Gao et al. (2023). For BoN, WCO and UWO completely eliminate overoptimization while single RM optimization overoptimizes, yielding up to ~75% improvement in final gold reward score under 25% label noise. For PPO without KL penalty, WCO and UWO reduce but do not fully eliminate overoptimization; however, with a KL penalty of 0.01, both methods prevent overoptimization entirely while matching or exceeding single RM performance. In contrast, single RM optimization requires a KL penalty of 0.2 (20× larger) to eliminate overoptimization, which causes a substantial performance drop. Across all KL penalty values tested, WCO and UWO consistently match or outperform the average single RM optimization, with the largest margins at small KL penalties. The gains from ensembling are orthogonal to reward model size scaling, meaning the two approaches can be combined for additional improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the synthetic overoptimization study of Gao et al. (2023) by introducing ensemble-based conservative optimization (worst-case and uncertainty-weighted objectives) as a mitigation strategy, drawing on the deep ensemble uncertainty estimation framework of Lakshminarayanan et al. (2017) and the uncertainty-weighted actor-critic formulation of Wu et al. (2021b). The key conceptual departure is repurposing ensemble disagreement—previously used for exploration or active learning in RL—as a conservative signal during policy optimization to prevent exploitation of faulty reward models. The setup is further extended with 25% label noise to better reflect real-world annotator disagreement, a modification absent from Gao et al.'s original protocol.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that ensembling reward models is far cheaper than scaling up model size (only fine-tuning multiple copies of an existing pretrained model is needed), and the gains are orthogonal to both model and data scaling, making it a composable improvement for RLHF pipelines. However, the study is confined to offline RLHF with relatively small models (1.4B policy, up to 1.3B reward model) and a single dataset (AlpacaFarm), leaving open whether the findings transfer to online RLHF settings where reward models are periodically retrained, to larger-scale deployments, or to other instruction-following benchmarks. The robustness to ensemble size and uncertainty penalty weight is encouraging for adoption, but the absolute performance ceiling remains modest compared to the gold reward model.
