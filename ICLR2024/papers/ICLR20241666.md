## Problem

Standard Slot Attention initializes all slots from a single random distribution, preventing the emergence of specialized slots that bind to specific object types and remain invariant to identity-preserving changes in appearance. This limits the ability to learn grounded, canonical object representations that support systematic generalization, scene composition, and cross-task adaptability.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images at varying resolutions (32×32 for Tetrominoes, 64×64 for CLEVR/Objects-Room/Bitmoji, 128×128 for FFHQ, 224×224 for COCO) and produces a variable number of object slot representations whose count K is dynamically estimated per input via spectral decomposition of the latent covariance matrix. For discovery tasks, each slot yields an RGB+mask reconstruction; for reasoning tasks, slots are mapped to per-object rationales that are combined into a rule-based class prediction. The GSD additionally exposes a grounded vocabulary of M canonical object-type representations that can be sampled to compose novel scenes without an external prompt dictionary.

## Method

CoSA replaces the single slot-initialization distribution of SA with a Grounded Slot Dictionary (GSD) comprising (i) a codebook of M learnable canonical object-level vectors and (ii) M associated parametric Gaussian priors, one per object type. An abstraction module performs spectral decomposition of the latent covariance to project features onto principal components and dynamically estimate the required slot count K via eigenvalue ratios. The K closest codebook entries select which Gaussian priors to sample initial slots from, after which standard iterative slot attention (with GRU updates and skip connections) refines the slots. Training uses a variational ELBO over the discrete codebook assignment and continuous slot priors, with three quantization strategies (Euclidean, Cosine, Gumbel-Softmax) and EMA-based codebook updates to prevent collapse. A spatial broadcast decoder reconstructs the image from slots, while a separate reasoning head maps refined slots to per-object property rationales for rule-based classification.

## Results

On object discovery, CoSA achieves the best foreground ARI across all four benchmark datasets: 0.99 on Tetrominoes, 0.96 on CLEVR, 0.83 on Objects-Room, and 0.32 on COCO, outperforming the strongest baselines (SA, BlockSlot, IMPLICIT) by 2–5 ARI points on CLEVR and Objects-Room. In reconstruction quality (Table 2), CoSA reduces MSE by 50% on CLEVR (3.14 vs. 6.37 for SA) and by 40% on FFHQ (33.37 vs. 55.14). On the visual reasoning task (Table 3), CoSA achieves 60.24% accuracy on the cross-task FMNIST2-Sub target (vs. ~10% for SA, BlockSlot, and CNN), demonstrating a ~5× relative improvement in few-shot task adaptability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Slot Attention (SA), CoSA ties on Tetrominoes (0.99) but clearly wins on CLEVR (0.96 vs. 0.93) and Objects-Room (0.83 vs. 0.78), with a 5-point ARI gain on the latter. Against BlockSlot, CoSA again ties on Tetrominoes and wins on CLEVR (0.96 vs. 0.94) and Objects-Room (0.83 vs. 0.77). Against IMPLICIT, CoSA matches on Tetrominoes and wins on CLEVR (0.96 vs. 0.93), Objects-Room (0.83 vs. 0.78), and COCO (0.32 vs. 0.28). Against DINOSAUR (the only baseline evaluated on COCO), CoSA improves ARI from 0.28 to 0.32. On the reasoning/adaptability task (Table 3), CoSA achieves 60.24% accuracy on the FMNIST2-Sub cross-task target, compared to only 11.06% (SA), 9.71% (BlockSlot), and 10.35% (CNN), while also attaining the best HMC (0.10) on the source task, indicating superior rationale quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Slot Attention (Locatello et al., 2020) by replacing its single random slot initialization distribution with a Grounded Slot Dictionary (GSD) inspired by Vector Quantized VAEs (Van Den Oord et al., 2017). The GSD pairs canonical object-level codebook vectors with parametric Gaussian slot priors, enabling specialized slots that bind to specific object types rather than treating all slots as exchangeable. The paper also introduces spectral decomposition of the latent covariance for dynamic slot number estimation, a mechanism absent from prior slot-based methods that require a fixed K. Conceptually, the work reframes the binding problem's 'compositionality' sub-task as a vocabulary-learning problem, positioning grounded object types as the missing link between transient slot representations and stable object identity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The grounded slot dictionary offers a principled way to achieve invariance to identity-preserving appearance changes without supervision, which is a long-standing desideratum in object-centric learning. The dynamic slot estimation (saving up to 38% FLOPs) and the ability to compose scenes from learned slot distributions without external prompt dictionaries are practical advantages. However, limitations include the maximum-object-area assumption for slot counting, the assumption of no representation overcrowding, and limited variation in slot-prompting. Open questions include whether the GSD scales to open-vocabulary real-world scenes, whether contrastive learning could further disentangle position/shape/texture within the dictionary, and whether the strong few-shot adaptation results on synthetic FloatingMNIST transfer to natural image domains.
