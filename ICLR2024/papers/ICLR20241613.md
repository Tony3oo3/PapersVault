## Problem

In cloud-edge deployment, edge models suffer significant accuracy drops under distribution shift, yet backpropagation-based test-time adaptation is infeasible on resource-limited edge devices. Uploading all test samples to the cloud for adaptation incurs prohibitive communication overhead, and it remains unclear how to leverage a stronger foundation model in the cloud to improve edge model robustness without excessive data transfer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241613/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The edge model receives unlabeled RGB test images (e.g., 224×224 ImageNet images) and performs only forward inference, producing class predictions locally. A filtered subset of samples (those with intermediate entropy) is asynchronously uploaded to the cloud in the background without blocking inference. The cloud returns only the affine parameters of normalization layers (e.g., ~0.0096 MB for ResNet-18 vs. 11.68 MB for full parameters), which the edge applies at the start of the next inference iteration.

## Method

CEMA decomposes adaptation between edge and cloud: the edge performs vanilla forward inference and applies a two-stage entropy-based sample filter that dynamically lowers the high-entropy threshold (Eqn. 2) to exclude unreliable samples and uses a fixed low-entropy threshold (Eqn. 3) to exclude low-informative ones, reducing uploads by ~60% over ETA. In the cloud, a stronger foundation model (e.g., ResNet-101) is first adapted via weighted entropy minimization on the uploaded batch, updating only BN affine parameters. A replay buffer (capacity 10,000) stores uploaded samples for reuse; the edge model is then adapted via a combined loss (Eqn. 6) that aligns its predictions to the foundation model through KL divergence and pseudo-label cross-entropy, plus entropy minimization, using both newly uploaded and replayed samples. Only the updated normalization-layer affine parameters are distributed back to the edge, preserving most pretrained knowledge and reducing download overhead by 99.91%.

## Results

On ImageNet-C, CEMA achieves the best average accuracy of 54.2% at severity level 3 and 40.8% at severity level 5, outperforming the strongest baseline ETA (53.5% and 38.7% respectively). On the realistic out-of-distribution benchmark ImageNet-R, CEMA reaches 27.9% accuracy, surpassing ETA (26.2%) and Tent (23.6%). In terms of communication efficiency, CEMA uploads only 19.1k samples (38%) at severity 3 and 14.4k (29%) at severity 5, reducing communication cost by approximately 60% compared to full-upload methods (Tent, PL, CoTTA) and 27% fewer than ETA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CEMA outperforms all seven baselines on average accuracy across both severity levels of ImageNet-C and on ImageNet-R. Against the strongest competitor ETA, CEMA gains +0.7% at severity 3 (54.2% vs 53.5%), +2.1% at severity 5 (40.8% vs 38.7%), and +1.7% on ImageNet-R (27.9% vs 26.2%), while simultaneously uploading 27% fewer samples than ETA. CEMA also substantially outperforms Tent (+3.0% at level 3, +5.7% at level 5) and PL (+2.4% at level 3, +5.4% at level 5), which require uploading 100% of test samples. The backpropagation-free methods (BN Adaptation, ONDA, LAME) that can run locally on edge devices achieve notably lower accuracy (47.4%, 45.9%, 32.4% at level 3), confirming that offloading adaptation to the cloud with selective sample upload yields superior robustness. On the mixed-corruption setting (Table 3), CEMA achieves 45.6% accuracy with only 286k uploaded samples, beating ETA (44.5%, 384k) and Tent (30.5%, 750k).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time adaptation (TTA) line of work—particularly ETA (Niu et al., 2022), which filters high-entropy samples via a static threshold and adapts via entropy minimization—into a cloud-edge collaborative system. The key departures are: (1) replacing the static entropy threshold with a dynamically decaying one (Eqn. 2) and adding a low-entropy exclusion criterion, (2) offloading all backpropagation to the cloud so the edge performs only forward inference, and (3) introducing replay-based knowledge distillation from a stronger foundation model (e.g., ResNet-101) to guide the edge model (e.g., ResNet-18), rather than adapting the edge model in isolation. The paper also draws on knowledge distillation and replay-buffer ideas from the offline distillation and reinforcement learning literatures, repurposing them for online, communication-constrained adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CEMA addresses a practical gap: real edge deployments (surveillance, industrial IoT) face distribution shifts but cannot afford backpropagation or unlimited bandwidth. By reducing upload volume by ~60% and download volume by 99.91% (only BN affine parameters) while matching or exceeding ETA's accuracy, the paper offers a deployable recipe for latency-sensitive cloud-edge systems. Limitations include the assumption that a stronger foundation model is always available in the cloud and covers the test distribution, the reliance on a stable network (variable-bandwidth handling is discussed but not fully implemented), and evaluation limited to ImageNet-C/R with ResNet/DeiT backbones. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to streaming video, multi-task edge settings, or scenarios where the cloud model itself is periodically retrained.
