## Problem

Existing adversarial patch attacks (APAs) for image classification rely on pushing corrupted image features across a specific model's decision boundary or toward a single target feature point, making them highly model-dependent and poorly transferable to unseen architectures. No prior APA has demonstrated the ability to simultaneously fool both Convolutional Neural Networks and Vision Transformers in a black-box setting, and the point-based feature-space approach of Inkawich et al. (2019) is unstable and sensitive to the choice of target point.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241277/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (ImageNet-1K validation set, 224×224) and places a small adversarial patch (70×70 to 90×90 pixels) at a random non-center location on the image side to avoid occluding the main object. The optimized patch is a fixed pixel array that, when composited onto any image, causes the target network to output a specific target class label with high confidence. Physical transformations (rotations up to 10°, scaling, brightness/blur adjustments, Gaussian noise) are applied during both training and evaluation to ensure the patch remains effective under real-world conditions.

## Method

The core contribution replaces decision-boundary or point-matching objectives with a distribution-level optimal transport loss: the patch is optimized to minimize the Wasserstein-2 (or Sliced-Wasserstein-2 with K=500 projections) distance between the feature distribution of corrupted source-class images and the pre-computed feature distribution of the target class, computed at the last encoder layer of the source model. This is formulated as argmin over the patch δ of the expected OT distance under Expectation over Transformations (EoT) plus a total variation regularization to discourage high-frequency patterns. The key insight is that matching entire feature distributions captures architecture-agnostic class information shared across networks, rather than overfitting to a single model's decision geometry or a specific feature point. The approach generalizes the L2 point-matching method of Inkawich et al. (2019) as a special case (proven in Appendix C) and outperforms all decision-boundary-based and point-based baselines in transferability across CNNs, Transformers, and adversarially trained models.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, the proposed Sliced-Wasserstein patch (SW₂²)₅₀₀⁽¹⁾ achieves a mean targeted success rate (tSuc) of 22.56% (min 8.93%, max 45.31%) when transferring from a single Swin source model to all other model families, outperforming the best baseline GAP (15.46% mean). The exact Wasserstein variant (W₂²)⁽¹⁾ reaches 21.14% mean tSuc with a max of 49.1%. In physical-world hybrid experiments (scanned patches with LGS defense), the proposed method maintains 12.11–12.41% tSuc versus at most 4.26% for the next-best baseline (L2), confirming real-world criticality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed OT-based patch outperforms all eight baselines on mean transferable tSuc. Against decision-boundary-based methods, GAP (15.46% mean) is the strongest competitor but still trails the proposed SW variant by ~7 percentage points; LaVAN (8.67%) and Logit (7.55%) are substantially weaker. The feature-point method L2 (Inkawich et al., 2019) achieves 13.6% mean but is highly unstable—its performance can drop by a factor of four depending on the chosen target point, whereas the distribution-oriented approach is robust to this choice. GAN-based methods (TnT at 2.11%, Casper at 3.81%, TTP at 13.77%, M3D at 5.19%) generally underperform, with TTP being the closest GAN-based competitor but still ~9 points below the proposed method. Notably, the proposed method is the only one that simultaneously achieves meaningful transfer (>10% tSuc) to both modern CNNs (ConvNext) and Vision Transformers (Swin), while all baselines fail to strongly transfer to at least one of these families. Under LGS defense (λ=1.5), the gap widens further: the proposed method retains 14.75–16.39% mean tSuc versus 6.56% for L2 and <1.1% for all other baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two research threads: adversarial patch attacks (GAP, LaVAN, TnT, Casper et al.) that optimize in decision space, and transferable adversarial example methods that operate in feature space (Inkawich et al. 2019, Naseer et al. 2021, Zhao et al. 2023). The key conceptual departure is replacing the point-targeting strategy of Inkawich et al. (2019)—which pushes corrupted features toward a single feature vector—with a distribution-oriented objective using Wasserstein or Sliced-Wasserstein distance to align the entire feature distribution of attacked images with a target class distribution. The authors formally prove in Appendix C that the L2 point method is a degenerate special case of their Wasserstein formulation, positioning their approach as a strict generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that distribution-oriented patch attacks are the only method capable of simultaneously fooling diverse CNN and Transformer architectures in both digital and physical settings, even against LGS defense. A practical limitation is the data dependency: the method requires multiple images of the target class to construct the target distribution, which may not always be available in a true black-box scenario. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other visual tasks (detection, segmentation), whether the target distribution can be learned or approximated without access to target-class images, and whether the advantage persists against stronger, patch-specific defenses beyond LGS.
