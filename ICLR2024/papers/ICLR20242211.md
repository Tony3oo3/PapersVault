## Problem

Existing RL agents struggle with zero-shot generalization to novel tasks because they either rely on memorized policies (model-free) or short-sighted planning (model-based), without the ability to decompose long-horizon tasks into manageable subtasks while focusing computation on relevant environmental aspects. Prior hierarchical planning methods address temporal abstraction (subgoal decomposition) but neglect spatial abstraction (selecting only task-relevant state features), limiting their ability to build generalizable skills across diverse training tasks.

## Input / Output

The agent receives pixel observations from 12×12 gridworld mazes (MiniGrid-BabyAI) containing randomly placed lava traps, encoded via a residual CNN plus a 16-dim bag-of-words embedder. At decision time, it outputs a discrete navigation action and maintains a proxy problem—a directed graph of up to 12 imagined checkpoint states with estimated cumulative reward and discount on each edge—solved by 5 iterations of SMDP value iteration to select the next checkpoint target. The checkpoint generator operates on observation-level inputs (not latent states) and produces binary partial descriptions (6 bits, up to 64 checkpoint 'kinds') fused with an episodic context to reconstruct full states.

## Method

Skipper constructs a proxy problem at decision time whose vertices are checkpoints imagined by a conditional VAE that splits each state into an episodic context and a 6-bit binary partial description, and whose edges are cumulative reward/discount estimates learned via C51 distributional RL on spatially-abstracted partial states (top-4 attention over 8×8 local perceptive fields). A goal-conditioned low-level policy executes each checkpoint transition, while the high-level policy selects the next checkpoint via value iteration over the SMDP proxy graph. Key differentiators include: (1) spatial abstraction via an attention bottleneck that discards irrelevant state aspects for each local estimation, (2) delusion suppression that trains distance estimators on generated non-existent targets to prevent chasing impossible checkpoints, (3) k-medoids pruning to keep proxy graphs sparse and evenly spread, and (4) a theoretical guarantee (Thm. 1) bounding the composite policy value error linearly in the edge-estimation accuracy. All components are trained end-to-end from hindsight experience replay in an off-policy, distributional DQN framework.

## Results

On 12×12 MiniGrid-BabyAI navigation tasks (training difficulty 0.4, 50 training tasks, 1.5M interactions), Skipper-regen achieves the highest zero-shot generalization success rate across all OOD evaluation difficulties (0.25, 0.35, 0.45, 0.55), with statistically significant advantages over all baselines. The model-free baseline overfits on training tasks (high training success rate) but generalizes poorly, while Skipper agents show a consistent gap between training and OOD performance that narrows with more training tasks. Skipper-regen outperforms Skipper-once, attributed to frequent proxy problem reconstruction reducing the chance of being trapped in low-quality plans.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Skipper (both variants) significantly outperforms LEAP (Nasiriany et al., 2019) in zero-shot generalization across all OOD difficulties. LEAP achieves reasonable generalization but requires an expensive pretraining stage, and its original form suffers from delusional plans in the latent space; when augmented with Skipper's generator, distance estimator, and delusion suppression technique, LEAP's generalization improves substantially but still trails Skipper. Skipper also clearly outperforms Director (Hafner et al., 2022), which performs well in single-environment settings but deteriorates rapidly with more training tasks, indicating poor scalability for generalization-focused scenarios. The model-free DQN baseline matches Skipper's OOD performance only when trained on a new task every episode (infeasible in practice), confirming that Skipper's advantage comes from learning transferable skills from a limited set of tasks. The Skipper-regen variant dominates Skipper-once, with the regeneration of proxy problems at each replanning step providing critical adaptability in novel environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

Skipper directly extends the authors' prior consciousness-inspired planning agent (Zhao et al., 2021), which introduced spatial abstraction via attention for decision-time planning, by adding temporal abstraction through learned proxy problems (directed graphs of checkpoints with estimated SMDP transitions). The framework draws on the options/SMDP formalism (Sutton et al., 1999) for temporal abstraction, C51 distributional RL (Dabney et al., 2018) for edge estimation, and conditional VAEs (Sohn et al., 2015) for checkpoint generation, all trained end-to-end via Hindsight Experience Replay (Andrychowicz et al., 2017). It departs from prior hierarchical planning methods (LEAP, Director) by constraining options to goal-conditioned checkpoint transitions rather than free-form subgoal generation, and by introducing a context/partial-description split in the generator to improve cross-task generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Zero-shot generalization remains a central bottleneck for deploying RL agents in real-world settings, and Skipper's divide-and-conquer proxy-problem approach offers a principled way to decompose long-horizon tasks into locally-learnable subtasks with theoretical performance guarantees. However, the experimental validation is confined to fully-observable 12×12 gridworld navigation with sparse rewards, and checkpoints are generated randomly from the partial-description space rather than prioritized for importance—both significant limitations for broader applicability. Open questions include extending the framework to partially observable and continuous-action domains, learning return-aware checkpoint generation, and identifying the precise problem classes where the estimation-accuracy assumption of Theorem 1 holds non-trivially beyond sparse-reward navigation.
