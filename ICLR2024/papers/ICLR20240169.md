## Problem

Neural networks predict output variables independently, producing structured predictions that violate first-order logic constraints (FOLCs) such as transitivity. Existing neuro-symbolic methods for encoding FOLCs—arithmetic circuits (ACs), semantic loss, and semantic probabilistic layers—either fail at scale (AC compilation becomes intractable beyond ~8 variables) or are too slow for practical integration, while MLN inference has long lacked efficient algorithms suitable for neural network embedding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240169/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

LogicMP takes as input grouped unary potentials Φ_u(v_r) derived from any encoding network (e.g., dot products of LayoutLM token embeddings for document images, BLSTM hidden states for text, or GNN outputs for relational graphs), a set of first-order logic formulas with associated weights, and the knowledge base structure. It outputs updated marginal probability distributions Q_r(v_r) for all unobserved variables under the open-world assumption, where the marginals reflect both the neural evidence and the FOLC constraints. The layer is fully differentiable and adds only a single learnable parameter (rule weight) per formula, making it a drop-in replacement for a softmax/sigmoid output layer.

## Method

LogicMP performs mean-field variational inference over a Markov Logic Network with two key accelerations that reduce complexity from O(N^M L^2 D^{L-1}) to O(N^{M'} L^2) where M' ≤ M. First, Theorem 3.1 proves that for clause formulas, only the single true-premise assignment contributes to the grounding message, eliminating the exponential D^{L-1} factor and reducing per-message cost to O(L). Second, the aggregation of grounding messages across all O(N^M) groundings is formalized as Einstein summation (Einsum) by exploiting the structural symmetries of MLNs, converting sequential grounding enumeration into parallel tensor operations (e.g., einsum('ab,bc→ac', Q_C(1), Q_C(1)) for the transitivity rule). The result is a fully differentiable, plug-and-play neural layer that performs T feed-forward iterations of parallel tensor ops, enabling FOLC encoding with up to 262K mutually-dependent variables in 0.03 seconds—where AC-based methods fail during compilation.

## Results

On FUNSD document understanding, LogicMP achieves 83.3/50.1 F1 (full/long), improving over the LayoutLM-Pair baseline (82.0/46.7) by 7.3% relatively on long blocks, while SL and SPL fail entirely due to infeasible AC compilation. On relational graph classification, LogicMP achieves 0.30 AUC-PR on UW-CSE and 0.82 on Cora, improving 173% and 28% over ExpressGNN w/ GS (0.11 and 0.64), respectively, while also achieving near-perfect 0.99 on Kinship. On CoNLL-2003 NER, LogicMP(adj+list) reaches 91.42 F1, outperforming BLSTM w/CRF(mean field) (91.07) and LogicDist(adj+list) (91.18). LogicMP is ~10x faster than ExpressGNN w/ GS, enabling training on 20M groundings in under 2 hours versus >24 hours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LogicMP wins across all three experimental domains. On FUNSD, it outperforms LayoutLM-BIOES (80.1→83.3 full F1), LayoutLM-SPADE (80.1→83.3), LayoutLM-SpanNER (74.0→83.3), and LayoutLM-Pair (82.0→83.3), while SL and SPL both fail due to AC compilation being infeasible beyond sequence length 8; SLrelax (82.0/47.8) is also outperformed. On relational graphs, LogicMP dominates all MLN inference baselines: MCMC, BP/Lifted BP, MC-SAT, and HL-MRF all fail or score near-zero on UW-CSE and Cora, while ExpressGNN w/ GS (the strongest prior method) is beaten by 173% on UW-CSE (0.11→0.30) and 28% on Cora (0.64→0.82). On CoNLL-2003, LogicMP(adj+list) at 91.42 F1 surpasses BLSTM (89.98), BLSTM(lex) (90.77), BLSTM w/CRF (90.94), BLSTM w/CRF(mean field) (91.07), SRelax (90.38), and LogicDist(adj+list) (91.18), with SL and SPL again failing. The key differentiator is efficiency: LogicMP's parallel Einsum formulation enables ~10x speedup over ExpressGNN w/ GS, which is the primary reason for the large margin on UW-CSE and Cora where sufficient training was previously infeasible.

## Relation to Prior Work

LogicMP builds on Markov Logic Networks (Richardson & Domingos, 2006) as the probabilistic formalism for FOLCs and on mean-field variational inference (Wainwright & Jordan, 2008) as the approximate inference algorithm. It departs from ExpressGNN (Zhang et al., 2020), which couples MLNs with GNNs via variational EM, by using closed-form MF iterations that introduce no extra parameters and can be formalized as parallel Einstein summation. It also departs from AC-based neuro-symbolic methods (SL, SPL, DeepProbLog, Scallop) that compile FOLCs into arithmetic circuits, which the paper shows are intractable for general FOLCs like transitivity over 262K variables. The key conceptual departure is exploiting the structural symmetries of MLN groundings to reduce sequential propositional computation to a small number of parallel tensor operations, making MLN inference practical as a differentiable neural layer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LogicMP provides the first fully differentiable, modular, and efficient neural layer for encoding arbitrary FOLCs, enabling plug-and-play integration with any backbone (LayoutLM, BLSTM, GNN) across images, graphs, and text. The practical impact is significant: it handles 262K mutually-dependent variables in 0.03 seconds where AC-based methods fail at compilation, and its ~10× speedup over ExpressGNN enables training on 20M groundings rather than 16K, which the paper shows is the primary reason prior methods underperformed. Open questions include convergence guarantees for the MF iterations (the paper uses fixed T steps without proving convergence), scalability to rules with very high arity where M' approaches M, and whether the open-world assumption is always the right setting for downstream applications.
