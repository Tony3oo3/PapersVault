## Problem

While empirical studies (Mallinar et al., 2022) suggested that neural networks exhibit 'tempered overfitting' when interpolating noisy data—risk neither converging to Bayes-optimal nor diverging to infinity—this behavior had not been rigorously analyzed for neural networks in low dimensions with non-linearly-interpolatable data. Existing theoretical results on benign overfitting were confined to high-dimensional linear/kernel methods or classification settings, leaving the regression case with univariate ReLU networks completely uncharacterized.

## Input / Output

The method takes n i.i.d. samples (x_i, y_i) where x_i ~ Uniform([0,1]) and y_i = f*(x_i) + ε_i with arbitrary nonzero noise, and produces a minimum-ℓ₂-norm piecewise linear interpolator realized as a two-layer ReLU network with an unregularized skip connection. The key output is a characterization of the L_p population risk that reveals a sharp phase transition: tempered (bounded by Θ(1/(2-p))·L_p(f*)) for p ∈ [1,2) but catastrophic (diverging to infinity) for p ≥ 2, with the behavior further depending on whether data is i.i.d. sampled versus placed on a uniform grid.

## Method

The paper characterizes the min-norm interpolator geometrically via a curvature-based analysis: in intervals where consecutive points share the same curvature sign, the interpolator 'overshoots' the linear spline by taking the min (or max) of adjacent linear pieces, creating spikes whose height scales as ℓ_i / max{ℓ_{i-1}, ℓ_{i+1}}. By leveraging the Dirichlet distribution of spacings between i.i.d. uniform points (expressed via i.i.d. exponentials), they bound the L_p loss contributed by these spikes and show the critical dependence on p: the expectation of ℓ_i^{p+1}/max{ℓ_{i-1},ℓ_{i+1}}^p is finite for p < 2 but infinite for p ≥ 2. A further subtlety is that even for p < 2, the risk is tempered only in probability (w.h.p. over S) while its expectation over the training set is infinite, due to the boundary intervals [0,x_1] and [x_n,1] where the interpolator extends linear pieces. On a fixed uniform grid, all spikes are bounded by a constant factor, yielding tempered overfitting for all p ≥ 1, demonstrating that the catastrophic behavior for p ≥ 2 arises specifically from the random spacing of i.i.d. samples combined with label noise.

## Results

The paper establishes a sharp phase transition in overfitting behavior for min-norm univariate ReLU interpolators: for L_p loss with p ∈ [1,2), the population risk converges in probability to Θ(1/(2−p))·L_p(f*), which is tempered but blows up as p→2 (Theorem 2, matched by a lower bound in Theorem 3 for Gaussian noise). For p ≥ 2 (including the standard squared loss), the risk diverges to infinity with probability 1 (Theorem 4). Additionally, even for p < 2, the expectation over training sets E_S[L_p(f̂_S)] = ∞, though the risk is tempered with high probability. On a uniform grid (fixed design), overfitting is tempered for all p ≥ 1 (Theorem 5), showing the catastrophic behavior for p ≥ 2 is driven by random spacing fluctuations rather than noise alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main theoretical comparison is between the min-norm ReLU interpolator and the linear-spline interpolator. Linear splines (Theorem 1) are tempered for all p ≥ 1 in both probability and expectation, while min-norm ReLU nets are tempered only for p < 2 in probability (not in expectation) and catastrophic for p ≥ 2. The min-norm interpolator is more conservative in the number of linear pieces than linear splines, creating 'spikes' in convex/concave regions that drive the worse behavior. Compared to Mallinar et al. (2022), who predicted tempered overfitting based on the expectation of the risk, this paper shows that the expectation is actually infinite even for L_1, while the high-probability behavior is tempered—highlighting that the Mallinar et al. taxonomy needs refinement. The grid-sampling variant (Theorem 5) shows that removing the randomness in input spacing eliminates the catastrophic behavior entirely, contrasting with the i.i.d. setting where the combination of label noise and random point spacing is what causes divergence for p ≥ 2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Boursier & Flammarion (2023) for the geometric characterization of minimum-norm univariate ReLU interpolators, and on Savarese et al. (2019) for the function-space formulation of bounded-norm networks. It departs from the dominant high-dimensional linear/kernel interpolation literature (Hastie et al., Belkin et al., Bartlett et al.) by studying a genuinely non-linear, low-dimensional (univariate) setting where the data is not linearly interpolatable. It also provides the first rigorous treatment of the 'tempered overfitting' phenomenon observed empirically by Mallinar et al. (2022), showing that the picture is more nuanced than their heuristic taxonomy suggested.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals that the nature of overfitting depends critically on the loss function (tempered for L_p with p<2, catastrophic for p≥2), the mode of convergence (tempered in probability but infinite in expectation), and the sampling scheme (tempered on a grid, catastrophic with i.i.d. points for p≥2). These distinctions challenge the coarse benign/tempered/catastrophic taxonomy and highlight that overfitting is a delicate property of the measurement protocol, not just the model. Key open questions include extending the analysis to multivariate inputs, deeper networks, and connecting the min-norm interpolator to the actual implicit bias of gradient descent, which remains poorly understood for regression with ReLU networks.
