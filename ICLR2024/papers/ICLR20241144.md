## Problem

Finetuning pretrained LLMs into Multi-Modal LLMs is computationally expensive, with full-parameter finetuning often causing out-of-memory issues even on 80GB GPUs. Existing parameter-efficient methods like LoRA reduce resource usage but compromise performance on multi-modal tasks. The paper asks whether a minimal set of parameters can be tuned to achieve strong multi-modal performance without the cost of full finetuning or the performance penalty of LoRA.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241144/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The MLLM receives an RGB image (processed by a CLIP-pretrained ViT-L encoder into visual tokens) and a text instruction, producing a free-form text response or image caption. A linear vision-language projector bridges the visual features into the LLM's token space. Evaluation spans VQA (VQAv2), captioning (MSCOCO and Flickr30k CIDEr scores), a comprehensive perception/cognition benchmark (MME), and object hallucination detection (POPE with three difficulty variants).

## Method

The core contribution is tuning only the LayerNorm parameters (both input and post LayerNorm) within each attention block of the LLM, while keeping the vision-language connector, word embedding, and output head active. The key insight is that the LLM-to-MLLM transition is a domain adaptation process, and LayerNorm acts as a natural domain adaptor, requiring only ~2.5% of trainable parameters yet outperforming LoRA by over 20% on average across five benchmarks. A further simplified variant (LayerNorm-simple) freezes even the connector, embedding, and output head, tuning solely LayerNorm at 0.004% of parameters while still surpassing LoRA. The paper additionally shows that finetuning on conversational data alone (20K samples) outperforms other data types by ~50%, and provides theoretical analysis showing LayerNorm tuning yields lower cross-layer cosine similarity (greater expressiveness) and smaller gradient variance compared to full finetuning.

## Results

On MM-VICUNA-7B, LayerNorm tuning achieves 723.2/253.2 on MME (Cognition/Perception), 17.06 on VQAv2, 80.89 CIDEr on MSCOCO, 48.01 CIDEr on Flickr30k, and 76.1/81.1/70.8 on POPE, outperforming LoRA by an average of over 20% across the five benchmarks while using only 3.78% of trainable parameters (vs. 5.92% for LoRA) and 24.2 GB GPU memory (vs. 29.4 GB). The extreme variant LayerNorm-simp., tuning only 0.004% of parameters, still surpasses LoRA by 4.3% on average. On the 13B scale (MM-LLAMA2-CHAT-13B), LayerNorm tuning reaches 929.3/254.3 on MME and 78.9/83.9/74.3 on POPE, the best scores in the entire table, while requiring only 38.3 GB memory versus OOM for full finetuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA (rank 32), LayerNorm tuning wins decisively on all five benchmarks across every model scale tested; on MM-VICUNA-7B, it improves MME Perception from 217.5 to 253.2, MSCOCO CIDEr from 63.93 to 80.89, and POPE (Popular) from 50.4 to 76.1, while using 41.9% fewer trainable parameters and 17.6% less GPU memory. Against full-parameter finetuning, LayerNorm wins on VQAv2, both captioning tasks (MSCOCO, Flickr30k), and POPE, but trails on MME Cognition by ~6.3% on average; notably, LayerNorm-simp. (0.004% parameters) still beats full finetuning on three of five tasks by ~10%. Compared to the external baselines, LayerNorm-tuned MM-VICUNA-7B surpasses MPLUG-OWL on MSCOCO (80.89 vs. 70.70), Flickr30k (48.01 vs. 41.78), and POPE (76.1 vs. 50.9 Popular), though it trails on MME Cognition (723.2 vs. 967.4) and VQAv2 (17.06 vs. 21.38). Against MiniGPT4 and LLaVA-v0, LayerNorm tuning dominates on nearly all metrics, e.g., 723.2 vs. 866.6/502.8 on MME Cognition and 80.89 vs. 58.89 on MSCOCO. The paper also shows that combining LayerNorm tuning with only 20K conversational data outperforms full finetuning on 80K data by 18.4% on captioning tasks, validating the 'less is more' hypothesis on both parameter and data sides.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes a standard architectural component (LayerNorm) as the sole tuning target for MLLM finetuning, drawing a conceptual bridge from the domain adaptation literature where adjusting normalization layers (e.g., BatchNorm) is known to be effective for domain shifts (Li et al., 2016). It departs from the dominant PEFT paradigm (LoRA, adapters, prefix tuning) that injects or activates weight matrices in attention/MLP projections, instead arguing that the text-to-multi-modal transition is fundamentally a domain adaptation problem solvable by recalibrating normalization statistics. The MLLM construction pipeline (CLIP ViT encoder + linear connector + LLM decoder) follows LLaVA, and the evaluation protocol mirrors that of LLaVA, MiniGPT4, and PandaGPT, positioning the contribution squarely within the established MLLM finetuning task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: LayerNorm tuning reduces trainable parameters by 41.9% versus LoRA and GPU memory by 17.6%, enabling MLLM finetuning on consumer-grade hardware, while the LayerNorm-simple variant (0.004% of parameters) still outperforms LoRA on average. The finding that conversational data alone (20K samples) nearly matches full 80K multi-type datasets offers a concrete data-curation strategy. Key limitations include heightened sensitivity to learning rate and training epochs compared to full finetuning, and the method does not uniformly dominate on all benchmarks (notably MME Cognition). Open questions include whether the domain-adaptation framing generalizes to other modality extensions (audio, video) or to architectures without LayerNorm, and whether the expressive-power mechanism (reduced cross-layer similarity) can be further exploited.
