## Problem

Autonomous web navigation has been dominated by online RL requiring billions of exploratory interactions (infeasible in real settings due to safety risks) or domain-specific architectures (LSTM, GNN, self-attention over DOM) that cannot leverage generalization from pre-trained foundation models. Prior offline methods using LLMs (e.g., WebN-T5) achieved sub-optimal performance (48.4% on MiniWoB++) because they used vanilla pre-trained T5 and unimodal HTML inputs, missing the benefits of instruction-following alignment and grounded visual perception.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241166/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

WebGUM consumes four input streams: raw HTML text, a 224×224 webpage screenshot (cropped from 160×160 and white-padded), the action history, and a natural language instruction. The screenshot is split into 16×16 patches yielding 14×14 = 196 local visual tokens per frame, doubled to 392 tokens by a 2-step temporal window. The output is a free-form text action of the form function(selector, text), where function is either click or type, selector is an integer element index, and text is the typed string—avoiding a fixed categorical action space.

## Method

WebGUM jointly finetunes Flan-T5 (an instruction-finetuned encoder-decoder T5) and a ViT-B16 vision encoder on a large multimodal corpus of web navigation demonstrations. The key architectural insight is encoding temporal and local visual perception: instead of a single CLS token per image, ViT produces one token per 16×16 patch, and a 2-step history window provides temporal context for multi-step tasks with dynamic page transitions. The instruction-finetuned base (Flan-T5) replaces vanilla T5, providing stronger HTML comprehension, zero-shot reasoning, and alignment with user instructions—yielding a 25% success rate improvement over vanilla T5. The authors also collect 347K multimodal expert demonstrations (38× larger than prior work) by rolling out finetuned LLM policies and supplementing with Synapse (GPT-3.5-turbo) for difficult tasks, enabling scaling to 94.2% on MiniWoB++ with the full Flan-T5-XL + ViT-B16 model.

## Results

On MiniWoB++, WebGUM (Flan-T5-XL + ViT-B16, 401K episodes) achieves 94.2% average success rate, improving over the previous best offline method WebN-T5 (48.4%) by 45.8% and surpassing the online RL-finetuned SoTA CC-Net (93.5%) by 0.7% despite fully offline training. On WebShop, the 3B-parameter WebGUM achieves 45.0% success rate and 67.5 score, outperforming ReAct with PaLM-540B (40.0% / 66.6). On Mind2Web action prediction, WebGUM-XL achieves 8.5% cross-task success rate, surpassing GPT-4 (2.0%) and MindAct-XL (5.2%) across all generalization categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WebGUM decisively outperforms the prior best offline method WebN-T5 (Gur et al., 2022) by 45.8% on MiniWoB++ (94.2% vs. 48.4%), and even surpasses the online RL-finetuned CC-Net (Humphreys et al., 2022) by 0.7% (94.2% vs. 93.5%) despite using no online exploration. It also exceeds human performance (93.5%) and GPT-4-based RCI (94.0%) by narrow margins. On WebShop, WebGUM (3B params) beats ReAct with PaLM-540B (540B params) by 5.0% in success rate (45.0% vs. 40.0%) and 0.9 in score (67.5 vs. 66.6), demonstrating that instruction-finetuned multi-step reasoning in a smaller model can outperform much larger prompted LLMs. On Mind2Web, WebGUM-XL outperforms both GPT-4 (2.0% SR) and MindAct-XL (5.2% SR) in cross-task success rate (8.5%), showing strong positive transfer from MiniWoB++ training. The one comparison where WebGUM trails is Synapse (98.5% on MiniWoB++), a GPT-3.5-turbo-based ICL agent, though Synapse relies on in-context learning with hand-crafted prompts rather than offline finetuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of treating web navigation as an online RL problem requiring task-specific architectures (LSTM, GNN, self-attention over DOM) by reframing it as a multimodal instruction-following task solvable with a generic encoder-decoder transformer. It builds directly on Gur et al. (2022)'s WebN-T5 (vanilla T5 finetuned on HTML) but makes three key departures: (1) replacing vanilla T5 with instruction-finetuned Flan-T5, (2) adding a ViT encoder with temporal and local visual tokens for screenshot input, and (3) scaling the training corpus from 12K to 347K multimodal episodes. The approach also contrasts with concurrent LLM-agent work (ReAct, Synapse, AdaPlanner) that relies on few-shot in-context prompting of very large models, instead demonstrating that a 3B-parameter finetuned model can match or exceed them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a practical and scalable recipe for web agents: offline supervised training of instruction-finetuned foundation models with multimodal inputs can match or exceed online RL without the safety and cost concerns of live web interaction. The 347K multimodal dataset (38× larger than prior) is a significant community resource. However, the evaluation is primarily on the MiniWoB++ simulator, the WebShop comparison is against few-shot prompted (not finetuned) PaLM-540B, and the authors acknowledge that human-level generalization to diverse real-world websites remains unresolved. Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly open-ended web environments, whether the encoder-decoder architecture advantage over decoder-only models (Flan-PaLM-8B underperforms) is specific to web navigation or general, and how to collect behavioral data at internet scale iteratively.
