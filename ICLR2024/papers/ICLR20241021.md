## Problem

Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs) achieve competitive anomaly detection performance but require simulating the full reverse diffusion chain at inference, making them computationally prohibitive for large-scale or real-time applications. Additionally, no comprehensive evaluation of diffusion-based methods existed for general-purpose anomaly detection across tabular, image, and NLP data in both unsupervised and semi-supervised settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241021/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standardized data vectors (tabular features, 512-dim ResNet-18 image embeddings, or 768-dim BERT NLP embeddings) and produces a scalar anomaly score computed as the mean of the predicted posterior distribution over diffusion time. For interpretability, the model can also generate a 'denoised' data point by following the gradient of the predicted posterior, though this denoising quality is inferior to DDPM's explicit reconstruction. The categorical variant outputs a probability vector over 7 timestep bins, and the final score is the weighted mean of bin indices (a continuous value between 0 and 6).

## Method

The key insight is that anomaly detection only requires estimating how far a point lies from the data manifold, which correlates with diffusion time, eliminating the need to model the full reverse diffusion process. The authors analytically derive that the posterior over diffusion time given a noisy input follows an inverse Gamma distribution with shape parameter a = d/2−1 (fixed by dimensionality) and scale parameter b proportional to the squared distance to the nearest data points. The non-parametric estimator uses k-nearest-neighbor distances to set b, yielding anomaly rankings identical to kNN. For scalability, a parametric MLP (hidden layers [256, 512, 256], ReLU, dropout 0.5) is trained by sampling data points, adding Gaussian noise at uniformly random timesteps, and predicting either the inverse Gamma scale parameter (via NLL) or a categorical distribution over 7 timestep bins (via cross-entropy). The categorical model with the mean of the predicted bin distribution as the anomaly score achieves the best empirical performance, with orders-of-magnitude faster inference than DDPM since it requires only a single forward pass.

## Results

On the 57-dataset ADBench benchmark, DTE (categorical) outperforms all deep learning baselines and DDPM in the semi-supervised setting, with kNN being the only method that slightly edges it out. In the unsupervised setting, DTE remains competitive with both classical and deep methods. Critically, DTE achieves orders of magnitude faster inference time than DDPM (a single forward pass through an MLP vs. simulating the full reverse diffusion chain) while simultaneously outperforming DDPM in AUC ROC. On VisA with supervised ImageNet embeddings, DTE-NP achieves 83.63% mean AUC ROC, DTE-C 81.07%, DDPM 80.47%, and kNN 83.26%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DTE-C outperforms all deep learning baselines (DeepSVDD, DAGMM, DROCC, GOAD, ICL, SLAD, DIF) in both semi-supervised and unsupervised settings, and also surpasses DDPM in AUC ROC while being orders of magnitude faster at inference. Against classical methods (CBLOF, COPOD, ECOD, IForest, LOF, HBOS, LODA, MCD, OCSVM, PCA, FeatureBagging), DTE-C is competitive overall, with kNN remaining the single strongest baseline in the semi-supervised setting. The non-parametric DTE variant produces anomaly rankings identical to kNN, confirming the theoretical connection derived in Section 3.2. Among generative baselines, DTE-C beats both VAE and GAN reconstruction-based approaches as well as the normalizing flow (PlanarFlow) baseline. On image datasets, using pre-trained embeddings (especially self-supervised) substantially improves all diffusion-based methods, with DTE-C achieving 68.53% AUC ROC on CIFAR-10 embeddings vs. 52.66% on raw pixels, while DDPM improves from 55.96% to 67.91%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) but departs from prior diffusion-based anomaly detection work (Wolleb et al., 2022; Wyatt et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2023a), which was restricted to image reconstruction and semi-supervised settings. The key conceptual departure is replacing the full reverse diffusion chain with a direct estimation of the posterior distribution over diffusion time, which the authors show analytically takes the form of an inverse Gamma distribution. This theoretical derivation reveals that the non-parametric estimator reduces exactly to kNN, positioning DTE as a parametric, scalable re-interpretation of nearest-neighbor anomaly detection through the lens of diffusion processes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical contribution is significant: DTE achieves orders-of-magnitude faster inference than DDPM while outperforming it on ADBench, making diffusion-based anomaly detection viable for large-scale and real-time applications. However, the kNN equivalence of the non-parametric variant raises a fundamental question about whether the diffusion framing adds genuine expressivity beyond distance-based methods, or primarily provides a principled training signal for the parametric MLP. Open questions include whether DTE extends to group and contextual anomalies, how it scales to truly massive real-world datasets, and whether the interpretability gap relative to DDPM (acknowledged by the authors) limits adoption in safety-critical domains.
