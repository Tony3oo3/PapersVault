## Problem

Existing label shift adaptation methods that match feature probability distributions on X suffer from high computational cost (O(n_p^3) for kernel methods, GAN training for LTF), while prediction-matching methods on Ŷ introduce avoidable errors from the fitted classifier. The paper asks whether one can match directly on the K-dimensional label distribution Y instead of the d-dimensional feature X, preserving the same theoretical guarantees while drastically reducing computational complexity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled source domain samples (X_i, Y_i) to train a calibrated network and estimate the source class probability p(y), plus unlabeled target domain feature vectors X_i to approximate the weighted expectation in the matching equation. It produces a K-dimensional class probability ratio vector ŵ, from which the target conditional probability q̂(y|x) is computed via a simple reweighting formula, and the final plug-in classifier assigns argmax labels to each target sample. The optimization over ŵ is a K-dimensional nonlinear least-squares problem solved by L-BFGS-B with O(n_q K²) cost per iteration.

## Method

The key insight is that under label shift, the source class probability p(y) equals E_{x~q}[p(y|x) / Σ_k w*_k p(k|x)], so matching p(y) against a weighted average of calibrated network outputs over target samples yields the true ratio w*. The paper proves (Theorem 3.1) this class probability matching on Y is exactly equivalent to the existing feature probability matching on X, guaranteeing the same unique solution w* under linear independence of class-conditional distributions. Practically, CPMCN trains a ReLU network with a softmax head on source data, applies bias-corrected temperature scaling (BCTS) to calibrate its probability outputs, then solves the K-equation matching via BFGS. Theoretical contributions include an identifiability theorem, a weight estimation error bound decomposed into bias (reduced by calibration) and variance (negligibly increased by the extra calibration layer), and a generalization bound for the target-domain classifier.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 under Dirichlet shift, CPMCN achieves the highest target-domain classification accuracy across all shift severities: 95.48% at α=0.1, 92.00% at α=0.5, and 89.15% at α=10. It also attains the lowest weighted MSE (MSE_PROP) for class probability ratio estimation, e.g., 3.61 at α=0.1 versus 7.95 for the closest competitor EM. The advantage over EM is most pronounced under severe shift (α=0.1: +0.90% ACC) and narrows under mild shift (α=10: +0.30% ACC), while the gap over prediction-matching baselines (BBSL, RLLS, ELSA) remains 1.5–2.5% ACC throughout.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPMCN outperforms all six baselines on every metric (MSE_EVEN, MSE_PROP, ACC) across all six Dirichlet shift parameters on CIFAR-100. The closest competitor is the EM-based method of Alexandari et al. (2020), which CPMCN beats by 0.30–0.90% ACC (e.g., 95.48% vs. 94.58% at α=0.1; 89.15% vs. 88.85% at α=10) and by roughly 2× in MSE_PROP under severe shift. The prediction-probability matching methods BBSL (Lipton et al., 2018), RLLS (Azizzadenesheli et al., 2019), and ELSA (Tian et al., 2023) trail CPMCN by 1.5–2.5% ACC and by an order of magnitude in MSE_PROP, confirming that matching on the predicted label Ŷ introduces avoidable prediction error. The feature-probability matching method LTF (Guo et al., 2020) is the weakest baseline, achieving only 65.30% ACC at α=0.1 and suffering from O(n_p³) Gram-matrix inversion. The kernel mean matching method KMM (Zhang et al., 2013) could not produce results within 10,000 seconds, whereas CPMCN (solved via L-BFGS-B) completes in a fraction of that time, consistent with its O(n_q K²) per-iteration complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the label shift adaptation literature by proposing a new matching framework (CPM) that operates on the label variable Y rather than the feature vector X (as in Zhang et al. 2013, Guo et al. 2020) or the predicted label ŷ (as in Lipton et al. 2018, Azizzadenesheli et al. 2019, Tian et al. 2023). The key conceptual departure is the equivalence theorem showing that matching K class-probability equations on Y is mathematically equivalent to matching the full feature distribution on X, thereby inheriting the same identifiability guarantees while reducing computational complexity from O(n_p³) to O(n_q K²) per iteration. The algorithmic component builds directly on Alexandari et al. (2020), who demonstrated that bias-corrected temperature scaling (BCTS) calibration is critical for EM-based label shift methods; CPMCN transplants this calibration requirement into a matching framework rather than an EM loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the computational efficiency: feature-matching methods require Gram matrix inversion or GAN training, making them infeasible at scale, while CPMCN's BFGS-based solver is orders of magnitude faster (Figure 4 shows KMM not completing in 10,000+ seconds while CPMCN finishes in seconds). The generalization bound (Theorem 5.5) provides a clean theoretical justification for why calibrated networks reduce the bias term without meaningfully increasing variance, connecting the practical calibration step to a formal error decomposition. The main open question, acknowledged by the authors, is extending CPM to the relaxed label shift setting where class-conditional distributions also shift (Garg et al. 2023; Tachet des Combes et al. 2020), which would broaden applicability beyond the strict label shift assumption. The paper also notes relevance to long-tailed learning and imbalanced semi-supervised learning, suggesting potential cross-pollination.
