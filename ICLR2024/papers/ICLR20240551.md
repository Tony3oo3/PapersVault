## Problem

Existing mapping functions for unbounded NeRF scenes (inverted sphere mapping in NeRF++, contract mapping in mip-NeRF 360) use fixed manifold shapes that allocate representation capacity in a scene-agnostic manner, causing under-sampling of distant objects when cameras are positioned far from the scene origin. No prior work has designed a mapping function that adapts its geometry to the specific content distribution of a scene, leaving a gap in per-scene optimization for unbounded rendering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240551/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images, COLMAP-estimated camera poses, and the COLMAP point cloud (used solely for RANSAC-based p-value estimation, not for training). It outputs a scene-dependent p-value that deforms the embedding manifold, and novel-view rendered images via standard volume rendering. The method is plug-and-play: it replaces only the coordinate mapping and ray sampling in existing NeRF frameworks (DVGO, TensoRF, iNGP, NeRF) without altering their network architectures or training schedules.

## Method

The paper first unifies existing unbounded mappings under a modified stereographic projection framework, showing that the inverted sphere mapping projects onto a cylindrical manifold and the contract mapping onto a paraboloidal manifold, both of which are fixed and scene-agnostic. The core contribution is a p-norm-based mapping function x_b = x / ||x - Q||_p where the p-value adaptively deforms the embedding manifold: large p makes the surface convex (allocating more capacity to near objects) while small p makes it concave (allocating more to distant objects). The p-value is automatically estimated via a RANSAC procedure that randomly samples point pairs from the COLMAP point cloud, projects them into the embedding space for candidate p values, and selects the p that maximizes the Euclidean distance between projected points (i.e., best utilizes the full embedding volume). A complementary angular ray parameterization θ/θ_max, where θ is the angle subtended at the projection center Q between the ray origin and a sample point, ensures uniform sample spacing in the deformed embedding space, avoiding the over- and under-sampling artifacts of conventional normalized-distance parameterizations.

## Results

On the ×2 (far-camera) scenario of mip-NeRF 360, the proposed p-norm mapping with iNGP achieves 26.35 dB PSNR, a dramatic 11.25 dB improvement over the contract mapping baseline (15.10 dB). Across all four NeRF frameworks (DVGO, TensoRF, iNGP, NeRF) and three datasets (mip-NeRF 360, Tanks and Temples, Free), the method consistently outperforms the contract mapping in ×2 settings, with average PSNR gains of 0.5–1.9 dB for voxel-based methods and up to 11 dB for iNGP. In ×1 (near-camera) settings, the method is competitive but occasionally slightly trails the contract mapping (e.g., NeRF on Tanks and Temples: 20.30 vs. 20.39 dB), as the contract mapping's identity mapping in near regions is well-suited when most content is close to the scene origin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the contract mapping (mip-NeRF 360 / Zip-NeRF), the proposed p-norm mapping wins in the vast majority of settings, with the most striking gain on iNGP for mip-NeRF 360 ×2 where PSNR jumps from 15.10 to 26.35 dB (the contract mapping essentially fails to converge in that scenario). For voxel-based frameworks (DVGO, TensoRF), the method consistently improves PSNR by 0.5–1.9 dB in ×2 settings, attributed to more even allocation of explicit voxel capacity in the deformed embedding space. Against the inverted sphere mapping (NeRF++), which is only applicable to MLP-based NeRF, the method achieves comparable or slightly better PSNR (e.g., 25.68 vs. 24.98 on mip-360 ×1, 25.94 vs. 25.06 on Free). Against F2-NeRF (iNGP), the method wins on both mip-NeRF 360 ×1 (26.65 vs. 25.19 dB) and Free (27.01 vs. 26.55 dB). The one notable exception is NeRF on Tanks and Temples ×1, where the contract mapping edges out the proposed method by 0.09 dB PSNR (20.39 vs. 20.30), which the authors attribute to the contract mapping's identity mapping being well-suited when most scene content lies near the scene origin.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the unbounded NeRF line of work initiated by NeRF++ (inverted sphere mapping) and mip-NeRF 360/Zip-NeRF (contract mapping), which both use fixed coordinate transformations to compress infinite space into a bounded volume. The key departure is a unified geometric analysis via stereographic projection that reveals the inverted sphere and contract mappings correspond to cylindrical and paraboloidal manifolds, respectively, exposing why their fixed capacity allocation fails when cameras deviate from the scene origin. The proposed p-norm mapping generalizes both by deforming the manifold shape through a scene-dependent parameter p (estimated via RANSAC on COLMAP point clouds), paired with an angular ray parameterization that compensates for the resulting non-linear distortion in the embedding space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The stereographic-projection-based decomposition provides a principled geometric lens for understanding and designing unbounded scene mappings, which is valuable beyond this specific method. The dramatic performance gap at ×2 camera displacement (e.g., iNGP+contract drops from 25.25 to 15.10 PSNR while iNGP+p-norm holds at 26.35) highlights a real vulnerability in widely-used contract mappings for free-trajectory and 360° rendering. However, the method's reliance on COLMAP point cloud quality for p-value estimation, its degradation at extreme camera distances (×8), and the acknowledged bias of the angular parameterization toward distant regions leave open questions about robustness in truly unconstrained capture settings. Region-aware or iterative p-value estimation is identified as a natural next step.
