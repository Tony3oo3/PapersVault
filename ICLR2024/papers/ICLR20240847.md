## Problem

Existing knowledge distillation methods for autoregressive language models train the student on a fixed set of output sequences (ground-truth or teacher-generated), creating a train-inference distribution mismatch where the partial sequences the student encounters during autoregressive generation differ from those seen in training. This exposure bias causes cascading prediction errors, and the standard forward KL objective forces a capacity-limited student to cover the teacher's full token-level support, leading to hallucinated low-probability tokens.

## Input / Output

The method consumes input text prompts, a frozen teacher model that provides token-level log-probabilities, and the student's own autoregressively generated output sequences (sampled at temperature γ=1 during training). The training signal is the divergence between teacher and student token-level distributions evaluated on the student's self-generated sequences, with no backpropagation through the student's sampling process. The output is a distilled student model that can be evaluated via greedy sampling, temperature sampling, or beam search depending on the task.

## Method

GKD generalizes distillation by mixing a fixed dataset of outputs (ground-truth or teacher-generated) with on-policy student-generated sequences at a tunable fraction λ, and by allowing arbitrary divergences (forward KL, reverse KL, or generalized JSD(β)) between teacher and student token-level distributions. The core mechanism is an on-policy imitation loop: the student generates sequences, the teacher scores each token position on those sequences, and the student minimizes the chosen divergence without backpropagating through its own sampling distribution, keeping training stable and computationally efficient. The optimal divergence is task-dependent—mode-seeking divergences (reverse KL, JSD(0.9)) improve quality under temperature sampling while forward KL suffices for greedy evaluation. GKD also composes with RL fine-tuning by adding a sequence-level reward term to the on-policy distillation objective, regularizing the policy toward the teacher rather than the initial student.

## Results

On XSum (ROUGE-2, greedy), the T5-XL teacher achieves 22, while GKD-distilled T5-small/base/large students reach 13.4/17.9/19.6 (vs. SFT baselines), yielding a 2.1× relative gain over the best supervised KD baseline. On WMT14 en-de (BLEU), the teacher scores 28 and GKD-distilled T5-small/base achieve 25.58/26.98 (1.7× relative gain). On GSM8K (accuracy with 4-shot CoT), the teacher reaches 27.9% and GKD-distilled T5-base achieves 10.16% (1.9× relative gain). For task-agnostic instruction tuning on FLAN, on-policy GKD with reverse KL yields 2% and 1% absolute accuracy improvements on held-out BBH and MMLU respectively, surpassing the 540B PaLM few-shot performance with a 7000× smaller model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On-policy GKD consistently outperforms all six baselines across every task and student size tested. Against Supervised KD (Sanh et al., 2019) and SeqKD (Kim & Rush, 2016), GKD achieves 2.1×, 1.7×, and 1.9× relative gains on XSum, WMT, and GSM8K respectively, with the gap widening for smaller students (e.g., T5-small at 38× compression). Against ImitKD (Lin et al., 2020) and f-distill (Wen et al., 2023), which are 'mixed' data variants (λ=0.5), on-policy GKD with JSD(0.9) outperforms in both greedy and temperature sampling settings on XSum, and purely on-policy data (λ=1) consistently beats mixed data on WMT and GSM8K. In the RL+distillation setting (Figure 5), on-policy GKD+RLAIF achieves higher ROUGE-2 than RLEF* (Roit et al., 2023) while simultaneously producing more factually consistent summaries than the T5-XL teacher itself. Finally, GKD-distilled T5-small surpasses the few-shot performance of PaLM (540B, Chowdhery et al., 2022) on GSM8K using a 7000× smaller model, demonstrating that on-policy distillation can close the gap to much larger models.

## Relation to Prior Work

GKD unifies and extends the autoregressive distillation literature by reframing it through the lens of imitation learning with an interactive expert (Ross et al., 2011). Prior methods—supervised KD (Hinton et al., 2015), SeqKD (Kim & Rush, 2016), ImitKD (Lin et al., 2020), and f-distill (Wen et al., 2023)—all train on fixed output sequences (ground-truth or teacher-generated), which the paper identifies as a source of exposure bias. GKD departs by introducing on-policy student-generated sequences and a flexible divergence choice (forward KL, reverse KL, JSD), subsuming all prior methods as special cases. The paper further extends the RLHF line of work (Ouyang et al., 2022) by showing that distillation and RL fine-tuning can be jointly optimized, with the regularization target shifted from the initial policy to the teacher policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The on-policy formulation addresses a fundamental train-inference mismatch that has plagued autoregressive distillation, and the practical recipe (no backprop through sampling, simple mixture of on-policy and fixed data) is easy to adopt. The demonstration that GKD combines seamlessly with RLAIF/RLHF opens a path to simultaneously improving task performance and alignment, potentially reducing the 'alignment tax' in LLM training. Limitations include evaluation only on T5/FLAN-T5 encoder-decoder models at relatively modest scale (≤3B teacher), leaving open whether the approach transfers to large decoder-only LLMs. The paper also notes potential extension to audio, video, and text-to-image generation as future directions.
