## Problem

Existing statistical analyses of Wasserstein Autoencoders (WAEs) either ignore the intrinsic low-dimensional structure of data (e.g., natural images), consider only fixed network architectures, or fail to account for model misspecification, yielding bounds that are inefficient for the practical regime where data lies on a low-dimensional manifold within a high-dimensional feature space. This paper bridges the gap by providing convergence rate guarantees that adapt to the intrinsic dimension of the data distribution rather than the ambient feature dimension.

## Input / Output

The method takes n i.i.d. samples from a target distribution μ supported on [0,1]^d and a known prior distribution ν on the latent space [0,1]^ℓ, and produces a learned ReLU encoder E: X→Z and generator G: Z→X along with explicit convergence rate bounds for the expected excess risk. In the empirical validation, inputs are 28×28 RGB images generated from a pre-trained BiGAN (trained on ImageNet) with only d_int free latent entries, and outputs are evaluated via FID scores and reconstruction error. The key structural detail is that the derived rates depend on the upper Minkowski dimension d_μ of supp(μ) rather than the ambient dimension d, with network sizes scaling as sub-polynomial functions of n whose exponents are governed by d_μ and the Hölder smoothness of the true model.

## Method

The core contribution is an oracle inequality that decomposes the WAE excess risk into misspecification, optimization, and generalization errors, followed by a careful balancing act: choosing ReLU network depths (O(log n)) and widths (encoder O(n^{s/(2α_e+s)}), generator O(n^{ℓ/(α_e(α_g∧1)+ℓ)})) as functions of sample size n to simultaneously control approximation and estimation errors. The key insight is that by leveraging the Minkowski dimension d_μ of the data support and Hölder smoothness parameters α_e, α_g of the true encoder-generator pair, the resulting excess risk scales as Õ(n^{-1/(2+d_μ)}) for Lipschitz models, independent of the ambient dimension d. The analysis covers both Wasserstein-1 and squared MMD dissimilarity measures, and further establishes encoding guarantees (encoded distribution converges to ν), decoding guarantees (G∘E approximates identity in L₂(μ)), and data generation guarantees (G_#ν converges to μ) under additional regularity conditions such as uniform Lipschitz continuity of the learned generators.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 8) establishes that for Lipschitz true models (α_e = α_g = 1), the expected excess risk of WAEs scales as Õ(n^{-1/(2+d_μ)}), where d_μ is the upper Minkowski dimension of the data support, independent of the ambient feature dimension d. The proof-of-concept experiment (Section 2) validates this prediction: on synthetic soap-bubble images generated from a pre-trained BiGAN decoder with d_int = 2 vs. d_int = 16 free latent entries, both GAN-WAE and MMD-WAE variants show consistently lower FID scores and reconstruction errors at the same sample sizes (n ∈ {2000, …, 10000}) for the lower intrinsic dimension. The network sizes required scale as O(n^{γ_e}) and O(n^{γ_g}) weights for encoder and generator respectively, with exponents γ_e, γ_g < 1 that depend only on d_μ and the latent dimension ℓ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is primarily theoretical and does not include a direct empirical benchmark against competing generative methods. The qualitative comparison (Section 5.3) positions the WAE rates against the GAN literature: the derived Õ(n^{-1/(2+d_μ)}) rate for Lipschitz models closely matches the rate obtained by Huang et al. (2022) for WGANs, suggesting limited room for further improvement. Unlike Chen et al. (2020) and Dahal et al. (2022), the analysis does not require the target distribution to have a bounded smooth density or to be supported on an affine subspace. Compared to Liu et al. (2021) on Bidirectional GANs, the WAE rates depend on the intrinsic manifold dimension d_μ rather than the full ambient dimension d. The key additional assumption over GAN analyses is the existence of a smooth true encoder-generator pair (A1), which is necessary to guarantee the generative capability of WAEs but does not apply to the simpler WGAN architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the oracle-inequality framework used for WGANs (Huang et al., 2022) and f-WAEs (Chakrabarty & Das, 2021) to practical Wasserstein Autoencoders, incorporating the intrinsic (Minkowski) dimension of the data support into the convergence analysis. It builds on the neural network approximation theory of Nakada & Imaizumi (2020) for Hölder functions on low-dimensional sets and the pseudodimension bounds of Bartlett et al. (2019) to control generalization error. The key departure from prior WAE analysis is the shift from VC-dimension-based parametric bounds (Chakrabarty & Das, 2021) to non-parametric rates that adapt to the data's intrinsic geometry, and from f-WAEs (a theoretical variant) to the GAN/MMD-based WAEs actually used in practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that WAE excess risk scales as Õ(n^{-1/(2+d_μ)}) rather than depending on the ambient pixel dimension d provides a theoretical justification for why WAEs work well on natural images despite their high-dimensional representation, and gives concrete guidance on network sizing (encoder O(n^{γ_e}) weights, generator O(n^{γ_g}) weights) as a function of sample size. However, the analysis requires a strong realizability assumption (A1: a single smooth chart covering the data manifold), and the data generation guarantees (Theorems 14–15) need additional regularity conditions (TV convergence or uniform equicontinuity) that are not directly ensured by the training procedure. Open questions include extending the analysis to multi-chart manifolds (chart-WAEs), handling model misspecification more gracefully, and establishing minimax optimality of the derived rates.
