## Problem

Standard positional embeddings in transformers assume a fixed, known ordering of tokens, which is ill-suited for multivariate time series where channels lack a predetermined spatial order and inter-channel relationships can be instance-specific. This makes conventional PE methods inadequate for capturing the latent spatial and temporal structure in MTS, particularly under channel permutation or distribution shift.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240427/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multivariate time series of shape C×T (C channels, T timepoints), which are first patchified into C×P×L tensors before tokenization. Beyond the primary classification label or regression target (e.g., hand velocity), GAFormer produces interpretable spatial and temporal group assignments per token, revealing how channels cluster together and how time segments are segmented in a data-adaptive manner.

## Method

GAFormer replaces fixed positional embeddings with a data-adaptive Group Embedding (GE) layer: a learned set of K group tokens G is assigned to input tokens via a softmax over projected encoder outputs (GE(X) = SoftMax(Enc(X)·W)·G), producing sparse, instance-specific group assignments that are added to the token representations. The full architecture processes MTS in three stages: (1) a channel-invariant patching tokenization, (2) a spatial group embedding (SGE) encoder that learns across-channel groupings independently at each temporal patch, and (3) a temporal group embedding (TGE) encoder that learns temporal segment groupings after bottlenecking channels into a single token per patch. Because each temporal patch is an independent sample to the SGE module, the model can assign different spatial groupings to different time periods, giving it expressiveness to capture non-stationary inter-channel dynamics. The GE module is also plug-and-play, and the paper demonstrates that adding TGE alone to existing backbones (MVTS, AutoTransformer) yields significant accuracy gains without architectural changes.

## Results

On the UEA multivariate time-series benchmark, GAFormer achieves an average classification accuracy of 56.64%, surpassing the best baseline (TCN at 50.13%) by ~6.5 points. The largest gains appear on Ethanol (41.44% vs. 30.04% for TCN) and MotorImagery (61.0% vs. 54.0% for PatchTST). On neural decoding tasks, GAFormer reaches 92.86% accuracy on Mihi-1 (vs. 90.48% for TCN) and a new state-of-the-art R² of 0.9136 on NLB-Maze (vs. 0.8946 for TCN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAFormer outperforms all transformer-based baselines (MVTS, AutoTrans, PatchTST, NDT, EIT) across every multivariate dataset, with the largest margin over PatchTST on MotorImagery (+7.0%) and Ethanol (+15.6%). Against non-transformer baselines, GAFormer beats TCN by 6.5 points on average UEA accuracy and by 11.4 points on Ethanol, while GRU is competitive on NLB-RTT (R²=0.5951 vs. GAFormer's 0.5433) but trails on all other tasks. The DTW-based baselines (DTWI, DTWD) and nearest-neighbor classifier are consistently outperformed by 5–15 points. Adding TGE alone to MVTS or AutoTrans yields meaningful gains (e.g., +8.0% on SelfRegSCP2 for AutoTrans), confirming that the group embedding module is transferable, but the full GAFormer architecture with both spatial and temporal group embeddings consistently achieves the best results. On neural decoding, GAFormer surpasses NDT and EIT on all four classification sub-datasets and achieves the best R² on NLB-Maze, though GRU retains a slight edge on NLB-RTT.

## Relation to Prior Work

GAFormer builds directly on the channel-invariant patch tokenization design of PatchTST (Nie et al., 2022) and the channel-independent processing philosophy of EIT (Liu et al., 2022), but departs from standard positional embeddings (learnable or sin-cos) by introducing data-adaptive group embeddings that assign tokens to a small set of learned group tokens based on instance-specific similarity. Conceptually, the group embedding mechanism is related to slot attention (Locatello et al., 2020) in its use of fixed-size group tokens, but differs in being explicitly data-adaptive and tailored to the spatiotemporal structure of multivariate time series rather than object discovery in images. The paper positions itself as addressing a gap left by prior positional embedding work: unlike language or vision, MTS channels lack a predetermined spatial ordering, making fixed positional encodings fundamentally ill-suited.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The group embedding module is a lightweight, plug-and-play component that consistently improves existing transformer backbones (MVTS, AutoTransformer) without architectural overhaul, making it practically accessible. The interpretability angle—revealing meaningful spatial (e.g., finger vs. tongue motor regions) and temporal groupings without prior knowledge—offers a path toward explainable time series modeling, particularly valuable in neuroscience and BCI applications. Key limitations include reliance on the channel-independent design, which may underperform on data with intricate inter-channel dynamics, and the need for more data as spatial and temporal dimensions grow. Open questions include whether group embeddings can be made more effective for very high-dimensional MTS and whether the learned groupings can be formalized with quantitative interpretability metrics.
