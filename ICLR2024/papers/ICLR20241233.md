## Problem

Yang et al. (2023) introduced ACVI, a first-order primal-dual method for constrained variational inequalities that combines interior-point and ADMM techniques, but its convergence guarantee required the operator to be L-Lipschitz and its subproblems to be solved exactly via analytic expressions. This paper addresses both limitations: it proves last-iterate convergence under only monotonicity (removing the Lipschitz assumption) and introduces a warm-starting scheme that allows subproblems to be solved approximately while preserving the O(1/√K) rate.

## Input / Output

The method takes a continuous vector field F: X → R^n, a set of linear equality constraints Cx = d, convex inequality constraints φ_i(x) ≤ 0, and hyperparameters (barrier weight μ, penalty β, decay rate δ). It produces the last iterate x_K as an approximate solution to the VI, along with the gap function value G(x_K, C) and dual variable λ. A key practical detail is that the method is projection-free: it never requires computing a projection onto the full constraint set C, instead handling equalities through a fixed projection matrix P_c and inequalities through the log-barrier (or a simple projection in the P-ACVI variant).

## Method

ACVI uses two nested loops: an outer loop that geometrically decays the log-barrier parameter μ_t = δμ_{t-1} (mimicking interior-point path-following), and an inner loop that performs ADMM-style primal-dual updates—solving an x-subproblem (enforcing equality constraints via P_c), a y-subproblem (enforcing inequalities via the log-barrier), and a dual update λ_{k+1} = λ_k + β(x_{k+1} − y_{k+1}). The convergence proof's key insight is identifying an implicit KKT point that ACVI targets at each iteration, which allows bounding the gap function directly without Lipschitz smoothness. For the inexact variant (I-ACVI), subproblems are solved with a fixed number of gradient steps (e.g., GDA or EG) initialized at the previous iteration's solution (warm-starting), and convergence is guaranteed provided the approximation errors satisfy a summability condition. A simplified variant (P-ACVI) replaces the log-barrier y-subproblem with a direct projection when inequality constraints are simple (e.g., L_p balls), eliminating the outer loop entirely.

## Results

The paper establishes an O(1/√K) last-iterate convergence rate for the gap function of ACVI under only monotonicity of the operator (no L-Lipschitz assumption required), resolving an open problem from Yang et al. (2023). Empirically, on the high-dimensional bilinear game (HBG), both ACVI and I-ACVI significantly outperform all projection-based baselines (P-GDA, P-EG, P-OGDA, P-LA) in both CPU time and number of iterations to reach a given relative error. On the constrained GAN (C-GAN) task on MNIST, PI-ACVI achieves lower FID and higher Inception Score faster than P-EG and P-GDA, with a large warm-up (ℓ₀=500) providing a significant convergence speedup over ℓ₀=100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the MNIST constrained GAN (C-GAN), PI-ACVI converges faster than P-EG (Korpelevich, 1976) and P-GDA for both FID (lower is better) and Inception Score (higher is better) measured against wall-clock time; P-EG only improves upon GDA when the rotational component of F is high, while PI-ACVI gives more consistent gains. On the high-dimensional bilinear game (HBG), I-ACVI and exact ACVI outperform P-EG, P-OGDA (Popov, 1980), P-LA (Zhang et al., 2019; Chavdarova et al., 2021), and P-GDA in both CPU time and iteration count to reach a relative error of 0.02, with I-ACVI being faster in wall-clock time despite requiring more iterations than exact ACVI because its subproblem steps are computationally cheaper. A key practical finding is that warm-starting with a large initial iteration budget (K₀=130, K₊=1) converges faster than a uniform budget (K₀=20, K₊=20), demonstrating that the homotopic structure of ACVI makes early accuracy critical. Notably, even when inequality constraints are simple enough for cheap projection (as in the L∞-ball or linear constraints on MNIST), PI-ACVI still outperforms the projected variants of standard VI methods, suggesting the ADMM-based decomposition provides structural advantages beyond just avoiding expensive projections.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the ACVI method of Yang et al. (2023), which combined interior-point log-barrier techniques with ADMM-style primal-dual updates to solve constrained variational inequalities with general (non-simple) constraints. The key departures are: (i) removing the L-Lipschitz smoothness assumption on the operator, requiring only monotonicity, by identifying a relationship between the gap function's reference point and an implicit KKT point; (ii) introducing warm-started inexact subproblem solving (I-ACVI) inspired by Schmidt et al. (2011) for proximal gradients; and (iii) proposing P-ACVI, a simplified variant that replaces the log-barrier y-subproblem with a direct projection when inequality constraints are simple. The broader lineage connects to projection-free methods for VIs, ADMM for large-scale optimization, and interior-point methods for constrained problems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The removal of the L-Lipschitz assumption resolves an open problem in the VI literature, yielding the first last-iterate convergence rate for monotone VIs with general constraints that does not require operator smoothness. Practically, the inexact variant with warm-starting offers a path to handling subproblems without closed-form solutions, and the empirical finding that a large initial warm-up (K₀ or ℓ₀) is critical for convergence provides actionable guidance. Limitations include the requirement that the constraint set be compact and that int(C) ≠ ∅, and the empirical evaluation is limited to bilinear games and a single GAN-on-MNIST experiment. Open questions include whether the O(1/√K) rate is tight for constrained monotone VIs, and whether the warm-starting strategy extends to stochastic or distributed settings.
