## Problem

Current protein generative models can design novel backbones with desired shapes or functional motifs, but they cannot condition on protein dynamics—specifically the low-frequency collective motions that are crucial for biological function. No existing diffusion-based protein design method incorporates dynamical properties such as normal mode displacements into the generation process, leaving a gap between structural design and functional design.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242149/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a target displacement matrix y_D ∈ R^{|C|×3} (the lowest non-trivial normal mode eigenvector components for a selected subset of C residues, extracted via NMA from a reference protein) and optionally a structural motif target y_M ∈ R^{|C_M|×3} (pre-defined relative positions of motif residues). It produces a novel protein backbone at Cα resolution. The NMA loss is constructed to be invariant to global rotations and translations by comparing only relative pairwise angles and relative magnitudes of displacement vectors, making the conditioning target independent of protein length and absolute orientation.

## Method

The key insight is to avoid training a neural network to approximate p(y|x_t) for the dynamics condition—which would require computing eigenvectors of arbitrary symmetric matrices—and instead substitute a simple analytical NMA-based loss. Following the score decomposition ∇_x_t ln p_t(x_t|y) = ∇_x_t ln p(y|x_t) + ∇_x_t ln p_t(x_t), the conditional term is evaluated via a delta-function approximation of the posterior (Bayes point machine) combined with Tweedie's formula to obtain the denoised estimate at t=0, at which point the differentiable NMA loss (pairwise cosine angles + relative amplitudes) is computed. For joint structure+dynamics conditioning, an additional L1 structure loss (after differentiable Kabsch alignment) is added as a third score term. The entire framework is plug-and-play: it modifies only the reverse diffusion sampling loop of an existing unconditional model (demonstrated with Genie and a custom GVP-GNN denoiser) without any retraining, using a differentiable Hinsen force-field NMA implementation for gradient computation.

## Results

For dynamics conditioning with the custom GVP model, conditional samples show a sharp peak at low NMA-loss values compared to unconditional samples, with 90% of samples achieving TM-score < 0.5 (high novelty). For joint structure+dynamics conditioning with Genie on three hinge targets (lysozyme, adenylate kinase, haemoglobin), the proportion of designable samples (scTM > 0.5) is 48%, 78%, and 41% respectively, dropping from 93%, 100%, and 89% when only dynamics conditioning is applied. In the improved conditionally-trained Genie model (Appendix H), motif+NMA conditioning achieves a ~17× enrichment in backbones with NMA-loss < 0.5 (23% vs 1.3%) and a ~3× enrichment in scNMA < 0.5 (7% vs 2%) compared to motif-only conditioning, while the best scMOTIF-RMSD and scRMSD samples all come from the jointly conditioned group.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not perform a head-to-head benchmark against other protein design methods (e.g., RFDiffusion, ProtDiff, FrameDiff); instead, the primary comparison is conditional vs. unconditional sampling with the same model. The unconditional baseline consistently shows higher NMA-loss (e.g., 0.740 vs 0.114 for a representative strain target pair) and no enrichment toward the target dynamics. For the hinge targets, adding structure conditioning on top of dynamics conditioning reduces designability (scTM > 0.5 drops from ~93–100% to 41–78%), which the authors attribute to the difficulty of the structural constraint rather than the dynamics term. In the improved Genie model (Appendix H), motif+NMA conditioning outperforms motif-only on NMA-loss enrichment (~17×) and scNMA enrichment (~3×) while achieving comparable or better motif scaffolding (best scMOTIF-RMSD and scRMSD samples belong to the jointly conditioned group). The authors contextualize their designability numbers by noting that RFDiffusion achieves 0 or near-0 designability for some challenging contiguous functional motifs, suggesting the observed rates are reasonable for discontinuous hinge motifs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reconstruction guidance framework (Chung et al., 2022a; Song et al., 2023) to protein backbone generation by introducing Normal Mode Analysis eigenvectors as a conditioning signal, replacing the typical neural-network conditional score with a differentiable analytical eigenvector computation. It builds directly on Genie (Lin & AlQuraishi, 2023) as the base diffusion model and on Didi et al. (2023) for the conditionally-trained variant in Appendix H. The key departure from prior protein diffusion work (RFDiffusion, ProtDiff, FrameDiff) is that none of those models incorporate dynamical properties; EigenFold (Jing et al., 2023) attempted to embed physical oscillation constraints into the diffusion kernel itself but did not improve sample quality or validate dynamics changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that protein diffusion models can be conditioned on low-frequency collective motions without retraining, opening a path toward designing proteins with prescribed functional dynamics (e.g., hinge motions). Key limitations include the drop in designability under joint structure+dynamics conditioning, the partial loss of dynamics information through the ProteinMPNN/ESMFold inverse-folding pipeline, and the observation that dynamics conditioning shifts secondary structure composition toward β-sheets. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the lowest non-trivial mode, how to preserve NMA scores through sequence design and refolding, and whether the plug-and-play transfer holds for larger or more complex motion targets.
