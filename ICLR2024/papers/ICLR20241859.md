## Problem

Prior EEG-based image decoding relied on supervised learning with limited categories, block-design paradigms that confounded stimulus-related activity with temporal correlations, and analyses restricted to occipital/parietal electrodes while ignoring the inferior temporal cortex critical for object recognition. No existing framework demonstrated zero-shot generalization to unseen image categories from EEG or provided a comprehensive biological plausibility analysis spanning temporal, spatial, spectral, and semantic dimensions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241859/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes raw EEG signals (63 channels, 1000 Hz, epoched 0–1000 ms post-stimulus onset, downsampled to 250 Hz, multivariate noise-normalized) and natural RGB images (resized to 224×224) collected under a rapid serial visual presentation paradigm (100 ms stimulus + 100 ms blank). At test time, the EEG encoder processes a single averaged trial and matches it against pre-computed image templates for 200 unseen concepts, producing a top-k ranking over object categories in a zero-shot setting where no test-category EEG data was used during training.

## Method

NICE (Natural Image Contrast EEG) is a self-supervised contrastive learning framework that aligns EEG and image representations without class labels. The EEG encoder is a compact temporal-spatial convolution (TSConv) network—temporal 1D convolutions, average pooling, then spatial 1D convolutions across all 63 electrodes—optionally augmented with plug-and-play self-attention or graph-attention modules that model inter-electrode correlations disrupted by standard channel-wise convolution. The image encoder is a frozen pre-trained model (CLIP-ViT-L/14, ViT-B/16, or ResNet-50) whose features are computed offline. Training minimizes a symmetric InfoNCE loss with a learnable temperature over batch-level image-EEG pairs, pushing matched pairs together and unmatched pairs apart in a shared embedding space. At inference, a few images per unseen concept are averaged into a template, and test EEG features are matched to templates via cosine similarity, yielding zero-shot 200-way classification.

## Results

On the 200-way zero-shot object recognition task (chance level 0.5%), NICE-GA achieves a subject-dependent top-1 accuracy of 15.6% and top-5 accuracy of 42.8%, surpassing the only external baseline BraVL (5.8% / 17.5%) by 9.8 and 25.3 percentage points respectively. In the more challenging subject-independent setting, NICE achieves 6.2% top-1 and 21.4% top-5, still substantially above BraVL's 1.8% / 7.0%. With the stronger EVA-CLIP image encoder (Appendix A.2), NICE-GA further improves to 21.2% top-1 and 50.4% top-5 in the subject-dependent setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The only external baseline is BraVL (Du et al., 2023), a multimodal brain-visual-linguing feature decoder. NICE-GA outperforms BraVL by 9.8% in top-1 and 25.3% in top-5 accuracy in the subject-dependent setting, and NICE outperforms it by 4.4% top-1 and 14.4% top-5 in the subject-independent setting. Among alternative EEG encoders, the proposed TSConv achieves 13.8% top-1, outperforming ShallowNet (7.9%, +5.9%), DeepNet (9.9%, +3.9%), and Conformer (11.1%, +2.7%), all with p<0.001, while only marginally exceeding EEGNet (13.1%, +0.7%, p>0.05). For image encoders, CLIP-ViT-L/14 (13.8%) outperforms ViT-B/16 (7.9%) and ResNet-50 (8.8%), with the gap to ResNet being highly significant (p<0.001). Notably, pre-training helps ViT (+4.2%, p<0.01) but slightly hurts ResNet (−1.9%, p<0.05), suggesting that the scale and nature of pre-training data matters for EEG-image alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly adapts CLIP's contrastive learning paradigm (InfoNCE loss, learnable temperature, symmetric cross-entropy) to align EEG features with pre-trained image encoder features, repurposing a vision-language alignment strategy for brain-signal decoding. It departs from prior EEG decoding work (BraVL, supervised approaches with limited categories) by framing the problem as self-supervised zero-shot recognition of unseen categories, and from standard EEG architectures (ShallowNet, EEGNet) by introducing TSConv with plug-and-play self-attention and graph-attention spatial modules that preserve inter-electrode correlations. The Gifford et al. (2022) dataset provides the large-scale stimulus-response pairs, but this paper extends the analysis beyond the occipital/parietal focus of that work to include the temporal cortex and a broader 100–600 ms processing window.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first comprehensive biological plausibility analysis (temporal, spatial, spectral, semantic) for EEG-based object decoding, confirming alignment with known visual hierarchy (V1→V2→V4→IT) and identifying theta/beta bands as most informative. Key limitations include the need for multiple repetitions to achieve stable responses (100 ms stimuli), a significant performance drop in subject-independent settings (~14% → ~6% top-1), and the inability to extract useful gamma-band information. Open questions include optimal stimulus duration for single-trial decoding, cross-subject generalization, and whether the framework scales to real-time BCI applications without averaging.
