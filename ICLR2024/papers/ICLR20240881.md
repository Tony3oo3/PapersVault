## Problem

The μP parameterization enables zero-shot hyperparameter transfer across network widths but does not transfer across depths, forcing practitioners to re-tune learning rates and other hyperparameters whenever network depth changes. This paper asks whether a single parameterization can achieve simultaneous hyperparameter transfer across both width and depth for residual architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240881/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (CIFAR-10 at 32×32, Tiny ImageNet at 64×64, ImageNet at 224×224) and produces class predictions through a final linear readout. The critical 'input' to the method is the network architecture itself—width N and depth L are varied independently to test whether training dynamics remain consistent. The output of interest is not merely a prediction but the invariance of loss curves, optimal learning rates, and other hyperparameter optima across all (N, L) combinations.

## Method

The paper proposes scaling each residual branch by 1/√L (square root of depth) in combination with the μP mean-field parameterization (γ = γ₀√N, η = η₀γ₀²N), yielding branch scales β_ℓ = (LN)^(−1/2) for intermediate layers, N^(−1/2) for the final layer, and D^(−1/2) for the input layer, with unit weight variance. Theoretically, the authors extend dynamical mean field theory (DMFT) to show this parameterization admits a well-defined joint infinite-width and infinite-depth limit in which the discrete layer index becomes a continuous 'layer time' τ = ℓ/L, and both feature and prediction updates remain O(1) independent of N and L. They derive exact solutions for the lazy NTK limit (Proposition 2) and for deep linear ResNets (Equation 7), and empirically validate transfer of learning rates, momentum, weight decay, and feature-learning rates across depths and widths on ResNets, convolutional ResNets, and Vision Transformers trained with SGD or Adam on CIFAR-10, Tiny ImageNet, and ImageNet.

## Results

The paper's central empirical finding is that the proposed 1/√L + μP parameterization enables consistent learning rate transfer across both network width (N = 32 to 512) and depth (L = 3 to 30) on CIFAR-10, Tiny ImageNet, and ImageNet, whereas standard μP transfers only across width and diverges at depth 30. The joint infinite-width-and-depth limit is well-defined, with finite-size prediction error decaying as O(L⁻¹) for sufficiently large N and O(L⁻²) for ensemble-averaged logits. Transfer extends beyond learning rates to momentum coefficients, weight decay, the feature-learning rate γ₀, and complex schedules (linear warmup + cosine annealing), and holds for both convolutional ResNets and Vision Transformers trained with Adam. Theoretically, the DMFT analysis yields a closed stochastic integral equation over continuous layer-time τ = ℓ/L, and exact solutions for deep linear ResNets confirm kernel updates converge to the infinite-depth prediction at L ≈ 100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is μP (Yang et al., 2021), which achieves reliable learning rate transfer across width but fails across depth: in Figure 1(a), μP ResNets show consistent optimal learning rates at fixed depth across widths N = 32–512, yet the optimal LR shifts dramatically (and runs diverge) as depth increases from L = 3 to L = 30. The proposed 1/√L + μP parameterization (Figure 1(b)) resolves this, with the same learning rate remaining optimal across the full grid of widths and depths. Standard Parameterization (SP, PyTorch defaults) also fails to transfer across depth (Fig. B.2), with all depth-30 runs diverging. Adding BatchNorm or LayerNorm to μP ResNets partially improves depth consistency (Fig. 2, B.4, B.5) but does not fully restore transfer, whereas the 1/√L scaling achieves more reliable and consistent transfer without requiring normalization layers. For Vision Transformers, the 1/√L + μP scheme with Adam shows transfer across depth and width both with and without LayerNorm (Fig. 3), and the 1/√L models exhibit a stronger non-saturating benefit of increasing depth compared to standard Pre-LN ViTs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two previously separate ideas: the μP parameterization (Yang & Hu, 2021), which achieves hyperparameter transfer across width but not depth, and the 1/√L residual branch scaling (Hayou et al., 2021; Fischer et al., 2023), which was developed for stable signal propagation in deep ResNets. The key conceptual contribution is showing that their combination yields a joint infinite-width-and-infinite-depth scaling limit, characterized via an extension of the dynamical mean field theory (DMFT) of Bordelon & Pehlevan (2022b) where the discrete layer index becomes a continuous 'layer time' variable. The paper departs from prior μP work by explicitly addressing the depth dimension, and from the signal propagation literature by connecting 1/√L scaling to feature-learning dynamics rather than just initialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: if hyperparameters truly transfer across both width and depth, practitioners can tune on small proxy models and scale up without re-tuning, reducing the computational cost of training large models. The theoretical contribution—a DMFT characterization of the joint N→∞, L→∞ feature-learning limit with explicit convergence rates (O(L⁻¹) in square error for predictions, O(L⁻²) for ensemble-averaged kernels)—provides the first rigorous framework for understanding deep feature-learning dynamics. Open questions include why hyperparameters transfer even when predictions differ across scales, whether stochastic kernel limits exist without the 1/√L factor, and what features are actually learned in this limit. Limitations include physics-level (not formal) rigor, experiments limited to 10–20 epochs, and treating time and dataset size as O(1).
