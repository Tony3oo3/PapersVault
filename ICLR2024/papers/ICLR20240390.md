## Problem

Conventional wisdom holds that neural network predictions on out-of-distribution (OOD) inputs are erratic and overconfident, yet prior evidence (e.g., Hendrycks & Gimpel, 2016) suggests softmax outputs actually become less confident on OOD data. This paper asks whether this behavior reflects a more general, predictable pattern in how networks extrapolate, and if so, what mechanism drives it and how it can be leveraged for risk-sensitive decision-making.

## Input / Output

The paper studies three task formulations over high-dimensional inputs (RGB images at 32×32 to 224×224, and text tokenized via DistilBERT): (1) classification producing a class probability vector via cross-entropy, (2) selective classification producing a per-action reward vector via MSE, and (3) regression producing a Gaussian mean and standard deviation via NLL. The key diagnostic output is the distance between model predictions and the optimal constant solution (OCS) — the single prediction minimizing average training loss without observing the input — measured via KL divergence, MSE, or NLL depending on the loss function.

## Method

The paper's core finding is that as inputs become increasingly OOD, network predictions converge toward the OCS, validated across 8 datasets, 3 loss functions, and both CNN and transformer architectures. The proposed mechanism has two components: (1) OOD inputs produce representations with smaller norms that lie outside the low-rank subspaces spanned by weight matrices, causing the input-dependent signal to diminish through successive layers, and (2) the accumulated input-independent constants (biases) in a trained network closely approximate the OCS. Theoretically, for deep homogeneous ReLU networks under gradient flow, they prove that in-distribution features maintain high norm (Proposition 4.1), that OOD features can collapse in magnitude due to near-rank-1 weight matrices (Theorem 4.1), and that the learned bias is determined by margin points whose label marginal approximates the OCS (Proposition 4.2). As a practical application, they show that training a reward-prediction model with MSE for selective classification automatically yields risk-sensitive behavior — the agent increasingly abstains on OOD inputs — because the OCS aligns with the cautious abstain action, unlike standard cross-entropy classification whose OCS never abstains.

## Results

Across 8 datasets (CIFAR-10/CIFAR-10-C, ImageNet/ImageNet-R/Sketch, DomainBed OfficeHome, SkinLesionPixels, UTKFace, BREEDS living-17/non-living-26, WILDS Amazon), the paper demonstrates a clear trend: as the OOD score of the evaluation dataset increases, the distance between neural network predictions and the optimal constant solution (OCS) decreases, holding across 3 loss functions (cross-entropy, MSE, Gaussian NLL) and both CNN and transformer architectures. In the selective classification application on CIFAR-10, the reward prediction approach (which leverages reversion to OCS) increasingly outperforms standard cross-entropy classification as distribution shift increases, while the standard classification approach almost never selects the abstain action. The gap between reward prediction and the oracle policy is substantially smaller than the gap between reward prediction and standard classification, indicating that the OCS-based strategy captures most of the achievable risk-sensitive benefit.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main empirical comparison is in the selective classification setting (Section 5.2), where three approaches are evaluated on CIFAR-10, DomainBed OfficeHome, BREEDS living-17, and BREEDS non-living-26: (1) reward prediction (MSE-trained model predicting per-action rewards, whose OCS aligns with abstaining), (2) standard cross-entropy classification (whose OCS corresponds to never abstaining), and (3) an oracle policy (temperature-scaled classifier with optimal abstain threshold, serving as an upper bound). All three approaches perform similarly on the training distribution, but as OOD shift increases, reward prediction and the oracle both increase their abstain frequency while standard classification almost never abstains. Consequently, reward prediction increasingly outperforms standard classification on OOD inputs in terms of average reward. The paper explicitly notes that its goal is not to beat SOTA selective classification methods but to demonstrate that the choice of loss function (and thus the OCS) fundamentally shapes OOD decision-making behavior, with the reward prediction approach capturing most of the oracle's advantage over standard classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the observation by Hendrycks & Gimpel (2016) that softmax outputs become less confident on OOD inputs into a broader 'reversion to the optimal constant solution' hypothesis applicable to arbitrary loss functions (cross-entropy, MSE, Gaussian NLL) and architectures (CNNs, transformers). Mechanistically, it builds on the theoretical literature on gradient flow in deep homogeneous ReLU networks (Lyu & Li, 2019; Arora et al., 2019; Timor et al., 2023), which shows implicit bias toward low-rank weight matrices, to explain why OOD feature norms collapse and outputs become dominated by accumulated biases. For the decision-making application, it repurposes the well-studied selective classification task as a testbed, contrasting reward-prediction (MSE) and standard classification (cross-entropy) approaches rather than proposing a new selective classification algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a unifying mechanistic explanation for OOD behavior that is well-supported across 8 datasets, 3 loss functions, and 2 architecture families, though it explicitly acknowledges exceptions (adversarial inputs, impulse noise on UTKFace) where the phenomenon breaks down. The practical implication—that choosing an MSE-based reward-prediction loss automatically yields risk-sensitive OOD behavior approaching oracle performance—is elegant but limited to the specific selective classification setup tested. Open questions include whether the OCS-reversion mechanism can be exploited to actively steer OOD behavior, whether it holds under more extreme or compositional distribution shifts, and how to handle the identified failure cases where feature norms increase rather than decrease on OOD inputs.
