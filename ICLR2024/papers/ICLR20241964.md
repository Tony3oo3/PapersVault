## Problem

Existing ML-based blind docking methods show strong performance on PDBBind's time-split benchmark but fail to generalize to unseen protein domains, largely because binding pockets are evolutionarily conserved and prior splits do not rigorously separate train and test binding sites. This paper identifies that the generalization gap is severe (ML methods drop from ~35% to ~7% success on unseen domains) and asks how to close it through better evaluation, scaling, and novel training strategies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241964/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a rigid holo protein structure and a ligand molecule as input, operating on the SE(3) manifold via Riemannian diffusion to generate 3D docked poses. For CONFIDENCE BOOTSTRAPPING, it additionally requires a set of known binders (e.g., from BindingDB) for the target protein domain, but critically does NOT require their ground-truth binding poses. The confidence model evaluates only local interactions (residues within 20 Å of predicted ligand atoms), and the final output is a ranked set of poses with confidence scores; the top-ranked pose is reported with RMSD < 2 Å as the success criterion.

## Method

The paper introduces three complementary contributions: (1) DOCKGEN, a benchmark built on ECOD domain classification that splits proteins by binding-domain identity rather than global sequence similarity, exposing severe train-test contamination in prior splits; (2) DIFFDOCK-L, which scales the original DIFFDOCK by adding ~52% more training data from Binding MOAD, a van der Mer-inspired synthetic augmentation that extracts protein sidechains as surrogate ligands to increase pocket diversity, and a larger 30M-parameter architecture; (3) CONFIDENCE BOOTSTRAPPING, a self-training loop where the diffusion model rolls out the full reverse diffusion process, the confidence model scores the final poses, and these scores are fed back to update the early diffusion steps (where pocket and coarse pose are decided) via a modified denoising score matching loss with time-dependent weighting λ'(t) that emphasizes high-noise steps. The key insight is that the multi-resolution structure of diffusion models allows targeting the generalization failure specifically at the coarse-grained early steps while preserving fine-grained refinement at later steps, and the RL-like objective (maximizing confidence-weighted log-likelihood) is optimized through the tractable DSM upper bound rather than intractable policy gradients.

## Results

On the new DOCKGEN-full benchmark, DIFFDOCK-L(10) achieves 22.6% top-1 RMSD < 2Å, a 3.2× improvement over the original DIFFDOCK(10) (7.1%) and outperforming the best search-based method GNINA(ex.64) at 17.5%. On DOCKGEN-clusters, CONFIDENCE BOOTSTRAPPING applied to the smaller DIFFDOCK-S model raises performance from 9.8% to 24.0%, more than doubling the best search-based baseline (GNINA(ex.64) at 11.8%). On PDBBind, DIFFDOCK-L(10) sets a new state-of-the-art at 43.0% top-1 RMSD < 2Å with a median RMSD of 2.8 Å, compared to 35.0% for the original DIFFDOCK(10).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIFFDOCK-L decisively outperforms all search-based baselines on the DOCKGEN benchmarks: it beats GNINA(ex.64) by 5.1 points on DOCKGEN-full (22.6% vs 17.5%) and by 15.8 points on DOCKGEN-clusters (27.6% vs 11.8%), while also achieving a lower median RMSD (4.3 Å vs 8.1 Å on DOCKGEN-full). The regression-based ML methods EQUIBIND and TANKBIND essentially fail on DOCKGEN (0.0% and 0.5% respectively), confirming that non-generative approaches cannot generalize to unseen binding domains. CONFIDENCE BOOTSTRAPPING applied to the compact DIFFDOCK-S model (2.8 s runtime) reaches 24.0% on DOCKGEN-clusters, surpassing even GNINA(ex.64) (11.8%) at a 124× speed advantage. On PDBBind, DIFFDOCK-L (43.0%) improves over the original DIFFDOCK(10) (35.0%) and GNINA(ex.64) (32.1%). On the PoseBusters benchmark, DIFFDOCK-L also achieves the best blind docking performance, outperforming RosettaFold all-atom and all other blind methods listed in Table 2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DIFFDOCK (Corso et al., 2022), a Riemannian diffusion model for blind docking, by systematically analyzing its scaling laws and proposing two complementary strategies to improve generalization: (1) data augmentation via van der Mer-inspired synthetic sidechain-ligand complexes, and (2) CONFIDENCE BOOTSTRAPPING, a self-training scheme that exploits the multi-resolution denoising ladder of diffusion models to feed confidence-model feedback back into early reverse-diffusion steps. Conceptually, CONFIDENCE BOOTSTRAPPING departs from prior self-training approaches (GAN discriminators, AlphaFold2 self-distillation, McClosky et al.'s parser self-training) by targeting specific stages of the generative process rather than treating the generator as a monolith, and draws an explicit analogy to Monte Carlo tree search in RL. The DOCKGEN benchmark itself departs from prior evaluation protocols (time-based PDBBind splits, UniProt-ID or global sequence-similarity splits) by using ECOD domain classification to ensure true pocket-level novelty between train and test sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DOCKGEN benchmark exposes a critical blind spot: state-of-the-art ML docking methods that achieve 35–38% success on PDBBind drop to 7% or below on genuinely unseen binding domains, revealing that prior benchmarks were masking severe overfitting to a small set of conserved binding modes. CONFIDENCE BOOTSTRAPPING offers a practical path toward domain-specific fine-tuning without requiring ground-truth structures, which is important for virtual screening campaigns targeting novel protein families. However, absolute performance remains low (24% best case), the method requires per-domain fine-tuning (~8 hours on one GPU), and three of eight tested clusters showed no improvement, suggesting that confidence model quality is a bottleneck. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models, whether it can be combined with affinity data (e.g., BindingDB) for unsupervised domain adaptation, and whether the generalization gap can be closed entirely with sufficient data and compute.
