## Problem

Model soups improve generalization and out-of-distribution performance by averaging parameters of multiple fine-tuned models, but averaging sparse models with differing sparsity patterns reduces overall sparsity, negating the computational benefits of pruning. This paper addresses the challenge of simultaneously preserving a fixed sparsity pattern and leveraging the generalization gains of parameter averaging within iterative magnitude pruning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20242247/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained dense model (ResNet-50, WideResNet-20, MaxViT, PSPNet, or T5-small) and produces a single sparse model with a fixed sparsity pattern. All m retrained copies share the identical pruning mask by construction, so the averaged model preserves the exact target sparsity without requiring a re-pruning step. Batch-normalization statistics are recomputed via a forward pass on the full training set after averaging to ensure valid inference for both single and averaged models.

## Method

The key insight is that a single prune-retrain phase of IMP closely mirrors the transfer learning paradigm: pruning abruptly changes the optimization objective (analogous to a domain shift), and retraining adapts the model under new sparsity constraints (analogous to fine-tuning). SMS exploits this by replicating the pruned model m times, retraining each copy with different hyperparameter configurations (random seeds, weight decay, retraining duration, learning rate schedules), and merging them via UniformSoup or GreedySoup. Because all copies originate from the same pruned network with a fixed mask, they share identical sparse connectivity, making parameter averaging sparsity-preserving by design. The method iterates: each subsequent prune-retrain phase begins from the previously averaged model, which both preserves sparsity across phases and improves the individual retraining runs before the next merge. SMS is fully parallelizable across the m models and can be integrated into pruning-during-training methods (GMP, DPF, BIMP) by treating intervals between pruning steps as phases.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 at 98% sparsity (3 prune-retrain phases, WideResNet-20), SMS with UniformSoup and m=10 achieves 73.05% top-1 accuracy, outperforming regular IMP (70.74%) by 2.31 points and IMP_{m×} (71.85%) by 1.20 points. On ImageNet at 90% sparsity (ResNet-50), SMS with m=10 reaches 74.96% top-1 accuracy versus 73.59% for IMP and 74.50% for IMP_{m×}. In the pruning-during-training setting (Table 2), DPF+SMS achieves 74.31% at 90% sparsity on ImageNet, surpassing DNW (74.24%) and BIMP (73.53%), while BIMP+SMS reaches 74.05%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMS consistently outperforms the best single retrained candidate by 1–1.5 points across all phases and datasets, confirming that pruned-and-retrained models are averageable and that the soup generalizes better than any individual member. Against regular IMP (m=1), SMS gains up to 2.3 points on CIFAR-100 at 98% sparsity and 1.4 points on ImageNet at 90%, while also outperforming IMP_{m×} (which uses the same total compute budget of m·k epochs) by 0.5–1.2 points. IMP-RePrune, which averages m independently-run IMP models only at the final phase and re-prunes to restore sparsity, suffers severe degradation (e.g., 63.62% vs. 73.05% on CIFAR-100 98%), demonstrating that maintaining a consistent sparsity mask across phases is critical. In the pruning-during-training setting (Table 2), SMS-enhanced variants of BIMP, GMP, and DPF all improve over their non-SMS counterparts by 0.3–0.6 points; notably, DPF+SMS (74.31%) surpasses DNW (74.24%) at 90% sparsity on ImageNet, and BIMP+SMS (74.05%) exceeds DNW by the same margin. UniformSoup predominantly outperforms GreedySoup, suggesting that when only the random seed is varied, all m models remain in the same loss basin and no subset selection is needed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of research: model soups (Wortsman et al., 2022a), which average fine-tuned models for improved generalization, and Iterative Magnitude Pruning (Han et al., 2015), which compresses networks via repeated prune-retrain cycles. The key conceptual move is recognizing that a single prune-retrain phase mirrors the transfer-learning fine-tuning paradigm that makes model soups work, thereby ensuring all averaged models share identical sparsity by construction. This departs from prior sparse-averaging work (Yin et al., 2022a,b) that required costly re-pruning after averaging due to diverging sparsity patterns, and from prediction ensembling approaches that multiply inference cost by the number of models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SMS offers a simple, modular, and fully parallelizable recipe that practitioners can drop into existing IMP or pruning-during-training pipelines (GMP, DPF, BIMP) with modest code changes, yielding consistent 1–2% accuracy gains at high sparsity without increasing inference cost. A notable limitation is that at very high sparsity (>95%), stability to randomness degrades and the averaging benefit shrinks or vanishes, suggesting a fundamental tension between sparsity and linear mode connectivity. Open questions include whether the technique extends to other compression paradigms (quantization, low-rank factorization), whether the 'soup bootstraps subsequent phases' effect can be exploited for curriculum-style sparsity schedules, and whether learned merging weights (beyond uniform/greedy) could close the remaining gap at extreme sparsity.
