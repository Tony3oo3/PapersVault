## Problem

Multi-task reinforcement learning benefits from sharing representations across tasks, but existing mixture-of-experts approaches (e.g., CARE) lack any inherent guarantee that the learned expert representations remain diverse, risking collapse into redundant encodings. This paper addresses the gap by asking: how can we enforce diversity among shared representations while still allowing each task to interpolate task-relevant information from a common subspace?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20240248/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a state observation (a 7×7×3 grid image in MiniGrid or a 39-dimensional stacked state vector in MetaWorld) together with a task context (one-hot task ID). A task encoder maps the context to a weight vector w_c ∈ R^k, which interpolates the k orthogonal expert representations to produce a single task-specific vector v_c ∈ R^d. This vector is fed to a task-specific (multi-head) or shared (single-head) output function that produces either a discrete action distribution (MiniGrid) or a continuous 4D action plus Q-value (MetaWorld). The same architecture is instantiated separately for the actor and the critic.

## Method

MOORE approximates a state-to-Stiefel-manifold mapping φ(s) with a mixture of k CNN (MiniGrid) or DNN (MetaWorld) experts that produce raw representations U_s ∈ R^{d×k}. The key mechanism is applying the Gram-Schmidt process to U_s, which sequentially projects each expert's representation orthogonal to the subspace spanned by all preceding experts, yielding orthonormal V_s ∈ V_k(R^d) that satisfy the hard constraint h_φ^T(s) h_φ(s) = I_k. This is framed as a constrained optimization (SC-MDP) where the GS process replaces explicit Lagrangian handling. At inference, a learned task encoder produces w_c, and the task-relevant representation v_c = V_s · w_c interpolates within the orthogonal basis, allowing each task to selectively combine expert components. The approach is algorithm-agnostic, demonstrated with both PPO (on-policy, MiniGrid) and SAC (off-policy, MetaWorld), and requires no auxiliary stabilization tricks (e.g., loss masking or gradient surgery) that competing methods rely on.

## Results

On MetaWorld MT10-rand, MOORE (k=4 experts) achieves a final success rate of 88.7±5.6 at 20M steps, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art PaCo (85.4±4.5) and all other baselines. MOORE also demonstrates superior sample efficiency, reaching 63.0±7.2 at 2M steps versus PaCo's 49.8±8.2. On the larger MT50-rand setting, MOORE (k=6 experts) achieves 72.9±3.3 success rate at 20M steps, substantially outperforming PaCo (57.3±1.3) and CARE (50.8±1.0). On MiniGrid, MOORE outperforms all baselines across MT3, MT5, and MT7 scenarios in both multi-head and single-head architectures, and notably exceeds the single-task PPO upper bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOORE outperforms all eight baselines on both MetaWorld MT10-rand and MT50-rand. Against the previous state-of-the-art PaCo (Sun et al., 2022), MOORE gains +3.3 points on MT10 (88.7 vs 85.4) and a substantial +15.6 points on MT50 (72.9 vs 57.3), indicating better scalability to more tasks. CARE (Sodhani et al., 2021), the closest MTRL baseline on MT10, trails MOORE by 12.7 points at 20M steps. Gradient-based PCGrad (Yu et al., 2020) and Soft-Module (Yang et al., 2020) both plateau around 62–63% on MT10, well below MOORE. Single-task SAC and MTSAC serve as lower-bound references, with MOORE exceeding them by 26.8 and 25.8 points respectively at 20M steps on MT10. Notably, MOORE achieves these results without the stability tricks (loss maskout, w-reset, training restarts) that PaCo and other baselines require, suggesting the orthogonal expert structure inherently stabilizes training.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOORE extends the mixture-of-experts paradigm in MTRL established by CARE (Sodhani et al., 2021) and PaCo (Sun et al., 2022), which learn shared expert representations but lack any guarantee of diversity among them. The key departure is the application of the Gram-Schmidt process to orthogonalize expert state representations, drawing on the Stiefel manifold literature (Huang et al., 2018; Chaudhry et al., 2020) where orthogonality has previously been imposed on network weights rather than on learned state encodings. The paper also introduces the SC-MDP formulation, a novel mathematical framing that generalizes the BC-MDP of Sodhani et al. (2021) by explicitly constraining the state mapping to the Stiefel manifold, providing a principled constrained-optimization view of the diversity objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing a hard orthogonality constraint via Gram-Schmidt rather than a soft regularization, MOORE offers a theoretically grounded mechanism for preventing representation collapse in multi-task settings, which is a persistent practical failure mode. The approach's algorithm-agnosticism (validated with both PPO and SAC) and its ability to outperform single-task baselines on MiniGrid suggest that structured diversity in shared representations can yield genuine positive transfer beyond what task-specific training achieves. Open questions include the computational cost of using all experts at inference (soft MoE), whether sparse expert selection can preserve the diversity benefits, and how the approach extends to continual learning where tasks arrive sequentially.
