## Problem

Existing deep tabular ML models (e.g., FT-Transformer, ResNet) suffer from two inherent representation pathologies: entanglement, where learned representations cannot support clear decision boundaries, and localization, where each sample is represented in isolation, overlooking shared global data structure. These issues cause performance inconsistency across tasks (classification vs. regression) and cannot be resolved simply by increasing model capacity, as demonstrated by T-SNE visualizations showing persistent entanglement even with deeper architectures.

## Input / Output

PTARL is a model-agnostic wrapper that takes any deep tabular model F(·;θ) as its backbone and operates on standard tabular inputs with mixed numerical and categorical features (categorical features are integer-encoded into shared-dimension embeddings, numerical features are column-wise normalized). The pipeline is two-stage: in stage 1, the backbone is trained normally and K-Means centroids of its output serve as prototype initialization; in stage 2, the backbone is re-initialized and jointly trained with the PTARL objective, producing final predictions (class labels or regression values) from the head applied to prototype-weighted P-Space coordinates rather than raw backbone outputs.

## Method

PTARL constructs a Prototype-based Projection Space (P-Space) where K global prototypes (initialized via K-Means on the trained backbone) serve as basis vectors, and each sample's representation is expressed as a weighted combination of these prototypes with coordinates r_i produced by a shared 3-layer MLP estimator. The key training signal is an Optimal Transport distance (computed via Sinkhorn with cosine cost) between the empirical distribution of the backbone representation P_i = δ_{G_f(x_i)} and the prototype-weighted distribution Q_i = Σ_k r_i^k δ_{β_k}, which pushes the backbone to align its output with the structured P-Space while preserving instance-level information. Two additional constraints enforce disentanglement: a supervised-contrastive loss on P-Space coordinates (diversifying same-class coordinates while separating different-class ones) and a matrix orthogonalization loss that forces the prototype matrix to be near-orthogonal (diagonal entries ≈ 1, off-diagonal ≈ 0), ensuring each prototype captures an independent direction of variation.

## Results

PTARL achieves consistent improvements over all six baseline deep tabular models across all six datasets (five classification, one regression), with more than 4% average performance gain in every setting. For example, FT-Transformer+PTARL reaches 87.1% accuracy on Adult (vs. 82.7% baseline), 73.8% on Higgs (vs. 68.7%), and 0.448 RMSE on California Housing (vs. 0.486). All improvements are statistically significant at the 95% confidence level under the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. No single deep model consistently outperforms others across all tasks, confirming the performance inconsistency the paper aims to address.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PTARL is a model-agnostic framework, so it is evaluated by coupling with each of the six baseline architectures (MLP, DCNV2, SNN, ResNet, AutoInt, FT-Transformer) and comparing against the same architecture without PTARL. In every one of the 36 model-dataset combinations, PTARL wins: it improves accuracy by 4–5% on classification datasets (e.g., FT-Transformer on Adult goes from 82.7% to 87.1%) and reduces RMSE by 4–30% on California Housing (e.g., SNN drops from 0.898 to 0.631). The largest relative gains appear on the weaker baselines (SNN, MLP) and on the multiclass Helena dataset, where even the best baseline (ResNet at 35.4%) is lifted to 39.9%. Among the baselines themselves, FT-Transformer and AutoInt tend to be the strongest on classification, while DCNV2 and AutoInt lead on regression; however, after PTARL is applied, the gap between models narrows considerably, with all PTARL-augmented variants reaching within ~1% of each other on most datasets. The paper notes that no universal winner exists among the six architectures, which is precisely the inconsistency PTARL helps mitigate.

## Relation to Prior Work

PTARL repurposes prototype learning—well-established in CV (class prototypes for few-shot/zero-shot recognition) and NLP (mean word embeddings as sentence prototypes)—for the first time in the tabular deep learning setting, but with a key departure: instead of class-specific prototypes, it constructs global data prototypes via K-Means over backbone representations to define a shared projection space (P-Space). It builds on the deep tabular model lineage (FT-Transformer, DCN-V2, AutoInt, ResNet, SNN, MLP) as a model-agnostic wrapper rather than proposing a new architecture, and leverages Optimal Transport (Sinkhorn) to align instance-level representations with the prototype-based space. Unlike TransTab and XTab, which inject external cross-table or pretraining information, PTARL is entirely self-contained within the target dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent 4%+ improvement across six diverse architectures and six datasets (binary, multiclass, regression) with statistical significance (Wilcoxon test) makes PTARL a practical, drop-in enhancement for any existing deep tabular pipeline. The identification of representation entanglement and localization as inherent limitations of deep tabular models—unresolved by simply increasing model depth—provides a useful diagnostic lens for the field. Open questions include scalability to very large or high-dimensional tabular datasets, whether the K-Means-based prototype initialization remains effective when the data distribution is highly non-convex, and whether the framework extends naturally to unsupervised or semi-supervised tabular settings where label-based diversification constraints would need redefinition.
