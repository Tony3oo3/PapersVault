## Problem

GFlowNets and other amortized inference methods for probabilistic graphical models require all variables to be fully instantiated to compute a single training signal, causing sampling cost and credit assignment to scale linearly with dimension. For sparse PGMs where the factor structure is known, this global dependency is unnecessary: the paper asks whether the sparsity can be exploited to derive local training objectives that only involve a single variable and its Markov blanket.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241050/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a factorized energy function (a product of local factors φ_k over variable subsets S_k), the corresponding undirected graph G, and optionally a set of observed variables for conditional inference. It produces a trained amortized sampler q_θ parameterized as a masked autoencoder that can perform ancestral sampling in any topological order of a chordal completion of G, and uniquely supports partial inference over arbitrary subsets of variables. Each gradient step requires instantiating only one variable and its Markov blanket rather than the full |V|-dimensional sample, making the per-step memory and compute cost independent of total dimension for sparse graphs.

## Method

The key insight is that perturbing a single variable u in a PGM changes the joint density ratio p(x)/p(x') only through factors involving u (left side) and the conditionals of u and its children in any I-map DAG (right side), yielding a local constraint (eq. 9) whose squared log-ratio defines the Δ-AI loss (eq. 10). Proposition 1 proves that satisfying this constraint for all single-variable perturbations is equivalent to full distributional equality p=q, making the local loss sufficient. The conditionals are parameterized by a single masked autoencoder that amortizes all variables simultaneously, and the method can train over multiple I-map DAG orders at once, enabling partial inference where only variables near the DAG root need to be sampled for a gradient. In the bilevel variational EM setting, the Δ-AI loss trains the amortized posterior q_θ while the generative model p_ψ is updated via maximum-likelihood gradients using samples from q_θ, with both updates requiring only local variable instantiations.

## Results

On synthetic sparse PGMs (Ising ladder, Ising lattice, factor lattice with |V| up to 1024), Δ-AI achieves the fastest wall-clock convergence to the target distribution, significantly outperforming GFlowNet baselines (TB, DB, FL-DB) and even matching or beating training-free MCMC (Gibbs, GWG) in time-to-quality on peaky energy landscapes. On MNIST latent variable modeling (164 binary latents in a pyramid graph with only 8.9% of possible edges), Δ-AI achieves the best held-out NLL and lowest mean prediction entropy among all amortized inference baselines after 12 hours of training, with the vanilla VAE reference at NLL ≈ 85 and entropy ≈ 0.24. On the continuous AMASS human poses dataset (24 joints × 3 angles), Δ-AI again outperforms TB, DB, and FL-DB by a significant margin in wall-clock MMD convergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Δ-AI consistently outperforms all GFlowNet variants (TB, DB, FL-DB) on wall-clock convergence across every synthetic and real-data experiment, because its local loss requires instantiating only a single variable and its Markov blanket rather than the full sample. Against MCMC (Gibbs and Gibbs-With-Gradients), Δ-AI reaches lower linear MMD in less wall-clock time than the mixing time of 10k parallel Gibbs chains, demonstrating a clear amortization benefit on peaky distributions. On MNIST, Δ-AI beats mean-field variational EM (which uses a fully factorized posterior) and both wake-sleep variants (Sleep and importance-weighted IW), with IW being the closest competitor; Gibbs sampling of the posterior is the third-slowest to converge. FL-DB, despite using the factorization as an inductive bias for the flow function, shows the slowest wall-clock convergence among GFlowNet baselines due to the expensive computation of energy at every partially instantiated state. On the continuous AMASS poses dataset, Δ-AI outperforms TB, DB, and FL-DB by a significant margin regardless of whether a single DAG or multiple amortized DAG orders are used.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GFlowNet framework (Bengio et al., 2021; Malkin et al., 2022) for amortized inference in discrete probabilistic models (Zhang et al., 2022) by exploiting the known factor structure of sparse PGMs to replace the global trajectory-level credit assignment with a local constraint that only involves a single variable and its Markov blanket. The theoretical foundation rests on the classical equivalence between Markov networks and Bayesian networks via chordal completions (Hammersley-Clifford theorem), and the resulting loss (Eq. 10) is a local analogue of the trajectory balance and detailed balance objectives. The paper also positions itself within the broader variational EM and wake-sleep literature for latent variable PGMs, and draws a connection to score matching in the continuous limit (Proposition 2).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For sparse PGMs with many variables, Δ-AI reduces both the sampling cost per training step (only a Markov blanket is instantiated) and the memory footprint, enabling amortized inference at scales where full-trajectory GFlowNets or MCMC become prohibitive. The ability to amortize over multiple DAG orders and perform inference over partial subsets of variables is a unique capability not available to any baseline. Key limitations include the requirement of known PGM structure, potential inefficiency when chordalization produces large Markov blankets, and current restriction to discrete variables. Open questions include joint structure learning, scaling to very large graphs with stochastic losses, and practical application to continuous real-world data.
