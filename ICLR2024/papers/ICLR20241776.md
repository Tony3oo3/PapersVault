## Problem

Existing post-training quantization (PTQ) methods for LLMs rely on hand-crafted parameters (e.g., fixed migration strengths, grid-searched scaling factors) that degrade severely at low bit-widths like W4A4 or W2A16, while quantization-aware training (QAT) achieves better accuracy but demands 100k+ samples and hundreds of GPU hours. The paper asks whether QAT-level quantization quality can be attained while preserving PTQ's time and data efficiency (128 samples, single-GPU hours).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241776/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained LLM's full-precision weights and 128 calibration segments of 2048 tokens each (default: WikiText2), requiring only a single A100-40G GPU for 1–16 hours depending on model size (7B–70B). The output is a deployment-ready quantized model in which all learnable parameters (clipping strengths, channel-wise scaling/shifting factors, attention scaling) are algebraically fused into the quantized weights and biases, introducing zero extra parameters or inference-time operations. It supports both weight-only (W2A16, W3A16, W4A16, with optional group-wise variants) and weight-activation (W4A4, W6A6) configurations, using per-channel weight and per-token activation quantization.

## Method

OmniQuant freezes the original full-precision weights and introduces a small set of learnable quantization parameters optimized via block-wise L2 error minimization with SGD/AdamW. Two components work synergistically: (1) Learnable Weight Clipping (LWC) parameterizes the clipping threshold as relative strengths γ, β ∈ [0,1] (via sigmoid) that scale the original weight min/max, making it robust to the weight-distribution shifts induced by the second component; (2) Learnable Equivalent Transformation (LET) extends SmoothQuant's channel-wise scaling by adding channel-wise shifting and further applies a scaling factor to the Q/K matrices inside the attention operation, all optimized end-to-end by gradient descent rather than grid search. The block-wise pipeline sequentially calibrates one transformer block at a time—applying LET to transform activations, quantizing with LWC, and minimizing the output L2 distance to the full-precision block—so the solution space stays small and convergence is fast. Because LET's scaling/shifting factors can be absorbed into adjacent linear layers and LWC's threshold is baked into the quantized weight scale, the final model is hardware-compatible with standard INT quantization kernels (e.g., MLC-LLM) without any architectural modification.

## Results

On weight-only quantization (WikiText2 perplexity), OmniQuant achieves 13.21 on LLaMA-13B at W2A16, compared to GPTQ's 5,500 and RTN's 68,000. At W4A16, OmniQuant reaches 5.21 PPL on LLaMA-13B vs. GPTQ (5.40) and AWQ (5.34). For weight-activation quantization at W4A4, OmniQuant achieves 52.65% average zero-shot accuracy on LLaMA-7B, surpassing OS+ (48.43%), LLM-QAT (41.27%), and SmoothQuant (38.41%). The method completes quantization of the full LLaMA-2 family (7B–70B) on a single A100-40G GPU in 1–16 hours using only 128 calibration samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniQuant dominates all PTQ baselines (RTN, GPTQ, AWQ) across every quantization setting, with the largest margins at extreme low bits: at W2A16 on LLaMA-13B, OmniQuant achieves 13.21 PPL while GPTQ produces 5,500 and RTN produces 68,000. Against weight-activation baselines at W4A4 on LLaMA-7B, OmniQuant (52.65% avg accuracy) outperforms OS+ (48.43%), LLM-QAT (41.27%), and SmoothQuant (38.41%), and even surpasses the QAT-based LLM-QAT+SQ (46.43%) by +6.22%. At W6A6, OmniQuant matches or slightly exceeds OS+ and SmoothQuant (e.g., 64.95% vs. 64.92% and 64.43% on LLaMA-13B). Compared to mixed-precision methods SpQR and SqueezeLLM (Table A15), OmniQuant achieves comparable perplexity at similar average bit-widths while supporting both weight-only and weight-activation quantization with uniform INT formats. The advantage of OmniQuant grows as bit-width decreases, confirming that learnable quantization parameters are more critical than hand-crafted heuristics in extreme compression regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniQuant extends the equivalent transformation paradigm introduced by SmoothQuant (channel-wise scaling to migrate activation outliers into weights) and Outlier Suppression+ (adding channel-wise shifting) by making all transformation parameters learnable through gradient-based optimization rather than relying on hand-crafted migration strengths or grid search. It further generalizes the transformation scope from linear layers to attention Q/K matrices, and introduces Learnable Weight Clipping as a complementary mechanism that optimizes the dynamic range of weights. The block-wise optimization framework is conceptually related to GPTQ's sequential layer quantization, but instead of reconstructing weights, it optimizes a small set of learnable quantization parameters while keeping original weights frozen—bridging the gap between PTQ's efficiency and QAT's adaptability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OmniQuant provides a practical, single-GPU solution (128 samples, 1–16 hours) that achieves QAT-competitive accuracy across a wide range of bit-widths (W2A16 through W6A6) without introducing any extra inference cost, since all learnable parameters are fused into quantized weights. This makes low-bit LLM deployment significantly more accessible for practitioners who lack the compute budget for full QAT. Open questions include whether the block-wise sequential approach can be parallelized for faster calibration, whether the method extends gracefully to sub-2-bit regimes, and how it interacts with emerging hardware that natively supports INT3/INT2 (currently a bottleneck for MLC-LLM deployment). The paper also notes that W4A4/W6A4 weight-activation quantization still lacks out-of-the-box hardware support, limiting real-world speedup claims.
