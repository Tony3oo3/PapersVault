## Problem

Diffusion models capture multi-modal action distributions effectively in offline and imitation learning, but their progressive denoising over many steps makes inference slow—a critical bottleneck for online RL where the agent must sample actions repeatedly from the environment. Consistency models, which solve the equivalent probability flow ODE in far fewer steps, had not been adapted as policy representations for reinforcement learning. This paper is the first to embed consistency models as policies in actor-critic and behavior-cloning algorithms across offline, offline-to-online, and online RL settings.

## Input / Output

The policy takes a continuous state vector s as the condition variable and produces a continuous action vector a by running the consistency function f_θ(s, â_τ, τ) over a short sequence of denoising steps (default N=2, versus N=5 for diffusion). Training consumes an offline dataset D of (s, a, r, s′, done) tuples; the consistency loss matches denoised predictions at two consecutive timesteps τ_n and τ_{n+1} drawn from a Karras-schedule sub-sequence. In the actor-critic variant, the policy also receives Q-value gradients through the generated action, requiring backpropagation through f_θ for each denoising step.

## Method

The consistency model is mapped to an RL policy by setting the condition c to the state s and the generated variable x to the action a, so that f_θ approximates the conditional distribution p_D(a|s) via the probability flow ODE rather than the SDE of diffusion. Two algorithms are proposed: Consistency-BC trains f_θ purely with the consistency loss (matching f_θ at τ_{n+1} to an EMA target f_{θτ} at τ_n), and Consistency-AC adds a Q-learning policy gradient term η·L_q(θ) that maximizes expected Q(s,a) over actions sampled from the consistency policy. A key technical contribution is a k-dependent loss-scaling factor λ(τ_n, τ_{n+1}; k) = ξ/|τ_{n+1}(k) − τ_n(k)| that counteracts the drastic decrease in consistency-loss magnitude as the N(k) schedule refines timesteps, preventing the Q-learning term from being overwhelmed during training. Because the consistency model requires only 2 denoising steps to saturate performance (vs. 5 for diffusion), both training and inference are roughly 1.8× faster, and the shorter backpropagation chain in the actor-critic update further reduces wall-clock time for online interaction.

## Results

On D4RL offline RL (Table 2), Consistency-AC achieves a total average normalized score of 72.1 (77.9 with online model selection), slightly below Diffusion-QL (80.3/83.2) but outperforming CQL (66.2), IQL (69.6), and ARQ (67.4). On Gym tasks specifically, Consistency-AC reaches 85.1 (89.8), competitive with Diffusion-QL (87.9/89.3) and above X-QL (83.7) and IDQL-A (82.1). In online RL from scratch, Consistency-AC (70.4) actually surpasses Diffusion-QL (67.7), while in offline-to-online fine-tuning it trails slightly (88.0 vs. 94.2). Computationally, Consistency-AC saturates at N=2 denoising steps versus N=5 for Diffusion-QL, yielding ~43% training time reduction for BC and ~1.79× faster training per epoch for the actor-critic variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Diffusion-QL (the closest generative-policy baseline), Consistency-AC achieves a slightly lower total average (72.1 vs. 80.3) in offline RL, losing most notably on AntMaze (70.1 vs. 78.7) and Kitchen (45.3 vs. 69.0), but winning on halfcheetah-medium (69.1 vs. 51.1) and halfcheetah-medium-replay (58.7 vs. 47.8). In online RL from scratch, Consistency-AC reverses the trend, scoring 70.4 vs. 67.7 on average, with sharper learning curves on 8/9 Gym tasks. Against non-generative baselines, Consistency-AC clearly outperforms CQL (66.2), IQL (69.6), and ARQ (67.4) on the total average, and matches or exceeds X-QL (83.7 on Gym) and IDQL-A (82.1 on Gym) on most Gym tasks. For the BC-only setting (Table 1), Consistency-BC (69.7 Gym avg) outperforms vanilla Gaussian BC (51.9) on 14/20 tasks and is competitive with Diffuser (75.3), TD3+BC (75.3), and Onestep RL (76.1), while Diffusion-BC (76.3) edges it out slightly. In offline-to-online fine-tuning, Consistency-AC (88.0) trails Diffusion-QL (94.2) but still beats ACA (85.5), SAC (76.8), and AWAC (62.3). The key trade-off is that Consistency-AC uses only N=2 denoising steps (vs. N=5 for Diffusion-QL), cutting training time by ~43% and inference time by ~51% at the cost of a modest performance gap in offline settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts consistency models (Song et al., 2023), which solve the probability flow ODE with few-step generation, as a policy representation for RL, building directly on the diffusion-policy line of work (Wang et al., 2022; Chi et al., 2023) that established score-based generative models as expressive policy classes for capturing multi-modal action distributions. The actor-critic framework (Consistency-AC) mirrors the structure of Diffusion-QL, replacing the iterative denoising policy with a consistency function that maps noisy actions back to clean ones in one or few steps. A key methodological departure is the introduction of a k-dependent loss scaling mechanism (λ(τ_n, τ_{n+1}; k) = ξ/|τ_{n+1}(k) − τ_n(k)|) to balance the consistency loss against the Q-learning loss during training, which the authors show is critical for stable offline RL performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the computational efficiency gain: consistency policies saturate performance with N=2 denoising steps versus N=5 for diffusion, yielding ~43% training time reduction and faster action inference that is particularly valuable in online RL where sampling from the environment is the bottleneck. The finding that the less expressive consistency policy can outperform the more expressive diffusion policy in online-from-scratch RL (8/9 Gym tasks) suggests that excessive expressiveness may hinder convergence when the agent must discover the policy through interaction rather than imitation. Open questions include whether the efficiency advantage scales to higher-dimensional action spaces requiring more denoising steps, how to combine consistency policies with conservative Q-value estimation (e.g., CQL-style regularization), and whether the offline-to-online performance gap can be closed through better alignment strategies.
