## Problem

Continued pre-training (domain-adaptive pre-training) on raw domain-specific corpora successfully endows large language models with domain knowledge, but drastically degrades their prompting ability for question answering. The paper asks how to adapt LLMs to specialized domains while preserving their capacity to respond to natural-language questions, a gap that limits the practical utility of domain-adaptive pre-training for generative models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241896/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes domain-specific raw corpora (PubMed Abstracts, financial stock news, FreeLaw Opinions) and general instruction datasets (LIMA, WizardLM, Orca) as continued pre-training data. Each raw text is truncated to 1,800 tokens and enriched with mined comprehension tasks, then concatenated with general instructions at domain-specific ratios (1:1 for biomedicine and law, 1:2 for finance). The output is an AdaptLLM model that, when prompted with domain-specific questions, produces answers leveraging both the acquired domain knowledge and preserved question-answering ability.

## Method

The core mechanism transforms each raw text into a reading comprehension text by appending a series of tasks mined from its own content using regex-based patterns and verbalizer sets. Six task types are mined: Summarization (title-as-summary), Word-to-Text (domain keywords identified via SentencePiece vocabulary comparison), Natural Language Inference (premise-hypothesis-relation triplets), Commonsense Reasoning (cause-effect pairs), Paraphrase Detection (reformatted as generation tasks to reduce label sensitivity), and Text Completion (sentence-ending continuation). Each mined example is paraphrased into multiple template variations and task-reversed to enhance input-output diversity, with a cap of two examples per sub-category per text to prevent task dominance. The resulting reading comprehension texts are mixed with general instructions and used for continued pre-training of LLaMA-7B/13B or GPT-J-6B, drawing on the analogy that practice after reading improves a human's ability to answer questions about the material.

## Results

AdaptLLM-7B achieves an average prompting score of 47.3 in biomedicine, 63.4 in finance, and 38.5 in law, consistently outperforming both the general LLaMA-7B baseline (44.2, 58.6, 34.2) and vanilla DAPT (41.7, 57.6, 35.0) across all three domains. Notably, AdaptLLM-7B surpasses BloombergGPT-50B (62.5) in finance by 0.9 points despite being ~7x smaller. In biomedicine, AdaptLLM-13B reaches 54.2 average, exceeding MedAlpaca-13B (49.8) by 4.4 points, and in law, AdaptLLM-7B (38.5) outperforms LexGPT-6B (32.1) by 6.4 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLaMA-7B (the general baseline), AdaptLLM-7B wins in all three domains: +3.1 in biomedicine (47.3 vs 44.2), +4.8 in finance (63.4 vs 58.6), and +4.3 in law (38.5 vs 34.2). Against vanilla DAPT, AdaptLLM consistently outperforms, confirming that raw-corpora continued pre-training hurts prompting (e.g., DAPT-7B drops to 41.7 in biomedicine vs 44.2 for LLaMA-7B, while AdaptLLM-7B recovers to 47.3). The headline comparison is AdaptLLM-7B vs BloombergGPT-50B in finance: AdaptLLM-7B achieves 63.4 average vs 62.5, a 0.9-point win with ~7x fewer parameters, though BloombergGPT leads on ConvFinQA (43.4 vs 41.5) and FiQA SA (75.1 vs 72.1). In biomedicine, AdaptLLM-7B beats MedAlpaca-7B by 2.2 points (47.3 vs 45.1), and AdaptLLM-13B beats MedAlpaca-13B by 4.4 points (54.2 vs 49.8), with the largest gap on MQP (73.0 vs 57.4). In law, AdaptLLM-7B outperforms LexGPT-6B by 6.4 points (38.5 vs 32.1), and AdaptLLM-6B (trained from GPT-J-6B) beats both GPT-J-6B (35.9) and LexGPT-6B (32.1), demonstrating the method's model-agnostic applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends domain-adaptive pre-training (DAPT; Gururangan et al., 2020) from encoder-based NLU models to large generative LLMs, and departs from vanilla DAPT by transforming raw domain corpora into reading comprehension texts via regex-based task mining (inspired by van de Kar et al., 2022). It also draws on the instruction-diversity principles of FLAN (Wei et al., 2022) and mixes in general instructions from LIMA, WizardLM, and Orca to preserve prompting ability. Conceptually, the paper positions itself between the three dominant domain-specialization strategies—training from scratch (BloombergGPT), supervised instruction fine-tuning (MedAlpaca), and retrieval-augmented prompting—by offering a continued-pre-training recipe that is cheaper than training from scratch and more knowledge-grounded than instruction fine-tuning alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 7B AdaptLLM matches or exceeds the 50B BloombergGPT on finance tasks, demonstrating that continued pre-training with a simple data transformation can rival expensive from-scratch training. The counterintuitive finding that raw DAPT degrades prompting while improving fine-tuning and knowledge probing provides a useful diagnostic for practitioners adapting LLMs to new domains. Open questions include whether the regex-based mining patterns generalize to domains with less structured text, whether the approach composes well with RLHF alignment (only briefly tested with LLaMA-2-Chat-7B), and whether stacking reading comprehension texts across many domains can yield a genuinely general-purpose model without catastrophic interference.
