## Problem

Existing LLM watermarking methods encode only a single bit (whether text originates from a specific model), making them unable to carry the diverse multi-bit information increasingly needed in practice—such as model version, generation timestamp, or user identifier. Moreover, the naive extension of random vocabulary partitioning to multi-bit messages degrades text quality significantly, and no systematic evaluation framework or principled method existed for this codable watermarking setting.

## Input / Output

During encoding, the method takes a text prompt and a multi-bit message (up to 20 bits, drawn from a message space of size 2^20) and produces a watermarked text of fixed length (e.g., 200 tokens) generated by the target LLM. During decoding, a watermarked text segment is fed to the proxy-LM (GPT-2, 124M parameters) to recover the embedded message; for copy-paste scenarios, a sliding window over the text is used with a threshold of 1−10⁻⁵ to avoid false positives on human-written text. The proxy-LM is decoupled from the generating LLM, enabling open-protocol decoding without access to the source model.

## Method

Balance-Marking replaces the random vocabulary partition of Kirchen et al. (2023a) with a probability-balanced partition guided by a proxy language model: at each generation step, the vocabulary is shuffled using a seed derived from the message and the recent token prefix, then the first k tokens whose cumulative proxy-LM probability reaches a threshold σ=0.5 are designated as the 'green' (available) set. The encoding objective adds a message logit—δ·(log P_w(v|m) − mean over all messages)—to the model logit, steering token selection toward the green set without excessively distorting the LLM's natural distribution. This balancing mechanism implicitly skips low-entropy positions (where one token dominates the proxy-LM probability), preserving text quality while concentrating watermark signal on high-entropy segments. A general Lagrangian framework (Algorithm 1) is provided that works for any message function P_w, and the proxy-LM can be swapped (small model for speed, public model for open protocols, the LLM itself for maximum quality) to suit different deployment scenarios.

## Results

Balance-Marking consistently outperforms Vanilla-Marking across all evaluated dimensions on OPT-1.3B, LLaMA-7B, and LLaMA-13B. At a coding rate of 10 tokens per bit, Balance-Marking achieves a watermark success rate of 95% on OPT-1.3B (vs. 93% for Vanilla-Marking) and 97% on LLaMA-13B, while maintaining lower perplexity (better text quality) at equivalent success rates. Under copy-paste attacks (200-token watermark embedded in 1000-token human text) and 5% substitution attacks, Balance-Marking retains higher detection success rates than Vanilla-Marking. The proxy model GPT-2 (124M) adds ~6.5s encoding and ~3s decoding overhead per 200-token generation, with LLM-self as proxy achieving the highest success rate (96.9% at PPL 3.5) at a modest additional cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is Vanilla-Marking, which extends the random vocabulary partition of Kirchenbauer et al. (2023a) to multi-bit messages by using a hash function seeded with the message. Balance-Marking wins on every metric: (1) at equal PPL, Balance-Marking achieves higher watermark success rate (e.g., ~95% vs. ~85% at PPL≈3.5 on OPT-1.3B, coding rate 10); (2) under copy-paste attacks, Balance-Marking maintains superior detection accuracy across all δ values; (3) under 5% substitution attacks, Balance-Marking again outperforms, though the gap narrows at 10% substitution because Balance-Marking's P_w depends on a 10-token prefix while Vanilla-Marking only uses the last token; (4) in distinguishing watermarked from human-written texts (threshold 1−10⁻⁵), Balance-Marking achieves higher true-positive rates at comparable false-positive rates. The trade-off is computational: Balance-Marking requires a proxy-LM forward pass per token, adding ~6.5s encoding and ~3s decoding over Vanilla-Marking (which uses no proxy, i.e., LM_proxy=∅).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the vocabulary-partition watermarking paradigm introduced by Kirchen et al. (2023a), which controlled token selection during LLM decoding to embed a single-bit signal. The key conceptual departure is replacing random vocabulary splitting with a proxy-LM-guided probability-balanced partition, ensuring that the 'green list' of tokens carries roughly 50% of the model's probability mass at each step. It also positions itself against post-processing watermarking methods (Yoo et al., Yang et al.) that operate after generation, arguing that integrating watermarking into the decoding loop better preserves text quality. Concurrent multi-bit works (Yoo et al. 2023b, Fernandez et al. 2023) still rely on random partitioning, which the authors identify as the primary source of quality degradation when encoding complex messages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first formal mathematical framework and a five-dimensional evaluation protocol for codable LLM watermarking, giving the community a shared vocabulary and benchmarking structure for a rapidly growing area. The proxy-LM design enables an open watermarking protocol where a public small model (e.g., GPT-2) can decode watermarks from any participating LLM without access to the generating model, which has significant practical implications for cross-provider traceability. Limitations include reliance on perplexity as the sole quality metric, increased vulnerability to substitution attacks at higher ratios (10%) compared to the random baseline, and the additional inference cost of the proxy model. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large or proprietary models, how it interacts with fine-tuning or RLHF, and whether more sophisticated quality metrics (human evaluation, semantic fidelity) would change the optimal operating point.
