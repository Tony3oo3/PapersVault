## Problem

Adam and its variants have dominated LLM pre-training for years, but first-order pre-conditioners do not adequately adapt to the heterogeneous curvatures across parameter dimensions. Second-order (Hessian-based) optimizers could in principle address this, yet prior methods incur prohibitive per-step overhead from frequent Hessian computation, preventing any wall-clock speedup on large language models.

## Input / Output

At each step, Sophia consumes the current model parameters, the mini-batch gradient, and the mini-batch loss. Every k=10 steps it additionally computes a diagonal Hessian estimate—either via a Hutchinson Hessian-vector product on a 32-example subset (Sophia-H) or via the GNB estimator that resamples labels from the model's own softmax predictions on a 240-example subset (Sophia-G). The output is an updated parameter vector where each coordinate's update is the EMA gradient divided by the EMA Hessian estimate, then element-wise clipped to a maximum magnitude of 1.

## Method

Sophia pre-conditions the gradient with a lightweight estimate of the diagonal Hessian, updated only every k=10 steps via an exponential moving average, keeping average per-step overhead under 5%. The key mechanism is per-coordinate clipping of the pre-conditioned update: when the Hessian estimate is negative or rapidly changing, the clipped update automatically defaults to stochastic SignGD, safeguarding against non-convexity and saddle points. Two diagonal Hessian estimators are provided—Hutchinson's unbiased estimator (requiring a Hessian-vector product) and the Gauss-Newton-Bartlett estimator (which exploits Bartlett's identities to compute B·∇L̂⊙∇L̂ from a single forward-backward pass with resampled labels, always yielding a PSD diagonal). This aggressive curvature-adaptive pre-conditioning equalizes loss decrease across sharp and flat dimensions, enabling Sophia to converge in 50% fewer steps than AdamW while maintaining the same memory footprint (two states: m and h) and near-identical per-step compute.

## Results

On GPT-2 and GPT NeoX models (125M–6.6B) trained on OpenWebText and The Pile, Sophia achieves 0.03–0.06 lower validation log-perplexity than AdamW at the same number of steps (e.g., 2.524 vs. 2.613 for GPT-2 Large 770M; 1.969 vs. 1.992 for GPT NeoX 6.6B). This translates to a 2× speedup: Sophia reaches the same validation loss as AdamW in 50% fewer steps, total compute, and wall-clock time. Per-step overhead is <5% (3.40s vs. 3.25s for 770M), and memory usage matches AdamW (two optimizer states). On SuperGLUE few-shot evaluation, models pre-trained with Sophia for 200K steps match models pre-trained with AdamW for 400K steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AdamW (the dominant LLM optimizer), Sophia consistently wins across all model sizes: Sophia-G achieves 0.06 lower validation loss on GPT-2 Large (2.524 vs. 2.613) and 0.023 lower on GPT NeoX 6.6B (1.969 vs. 1.992) at the same 100K steps, which the authors show corresponds to a 2× reduction in steps/compute to reach the same loss. Against Lion (a first-order sign-momentum optimizer discovered via symbolic search), Sophia also wins on both 125M (2.875 vs. 2.924) and 355M (2.627 vs. 2.678) models, with the gap widening at larger scales. Against AdaHessian (a prior second-order method using EMA of squared Hessian estimates), Sophia-H outperforms it consistently in the ablation (Figure 7b), attributed to Sophia's EMA-of-Hessian providing better denoising than AdaHessian's EMA-of-second-moment. The gap between Sophia and AdamW grows with model size: a 540M model trained with Sophia-H matches a 770M model trained with AdamW at 100K steps, indicating favorable scaling behavior.

## Relation to Prior Work

Sophia sits at the intersection of second-order optimization (Hutchinson's trace estimator, Gauss-Newton decomposition, Bartlett's identities) and the adaptive first-order optimizer lineage (Adam, Lion, AdaHessian). It departs from prior second-order methods like AdaHessian and Shampoo by estimating the diagonal Hessian only every k=10 steps rather than every step, a design made safe by the per-coordinate clipping mechanism that bounds worst-case update size. The GNB estimator specifically repurposes Bartlett's first and second identities (previously used in Wei et al., 2020) to construct a practical mini-batch Gauss-Newton diagonal estimator compatible with standard auto-differentiation frameworks. The paper explicitly notes that no prior second-order optimizer has demonstrated a wall-clock or total-compute speedup on decoder-only LLMs, positioning Sophia as the first to close this gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 2x reduction in pre-training steps, total compute, and wall-clock time is practically transformative given that LLM pre-training costs scale linearly with compute (e.g., PaLM's $10M budget). The scaling-law advantage—where the Sophia-vs-AdamW gap widens with model size—suggests even larger gains at the 100B+ scale where most production models live. Key limitations include evaluation only up to 6.6B parameters, limited downstream evaluation (4 SuperGLUE subtasks), and restriction to language modeling; whether the speedup transfers to vision, RL, or multimodal pre-training remains open. The finding that Sophia does not require the attention-temperature-scaling trick for stability also hints at broader training-stability benefits worth investigating.
