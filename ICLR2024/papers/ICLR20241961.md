## Problem

ML-based branching policies for exact CO solvers suffer from three deployment barriers: they require extensive training data, depend on GPU inference for competitive performance, and produce uninterpretable black-box models that engender skepticism among optimization researchers. Meanwhile, human-designed policies (e.g., pseudocost branching) are compact and reliable but miss data-driven patterns. The paper asks whether a compact, interpretable, and CPU-efficient policy can match GPU-based SOTA performance on the branching task.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 91-dimensional feature vectors per branching candidate at each B&B node, comprising static features (MILP problem structure) and dynamic features (current solver state), normalized to [0,1] within each node. It produces a single one-line symbolic expression (e.g., s₈₉(4s₁₀+7s₃₅+5s₃₇+3s₃₈+s₅₅+2s₆₅+s₅₇+2s₇₁)) that scores all candidates; the variable with the highest score is branched on. The learned expression is compiled to a lightweight C shared object (~1/80 the size of a Hybrid model) and integrated directly into SCIP, requiring only one CPU core for inference at ~0.04–0.08 ms per decision.

## Method

Symb4CO employs a 2-layer LSTM (hidden size 128) as a sequential generator that outputs expression-tree tokens step-by-step, conditioning on parent and sibling nodes to capture hierarchical structure. Training uses PPO with a risk-seeking objective that optimizes the best-case fitness (top-ε quantile) rather than average performance, where fitness is the behavioral cloning accuracy against full strong branching (FSB) expert demonstrations collected from only 10 training instances. The search space is constrained by hard length bounds (4–64 tokens), inverse-operator restrictions (preventing exp∘log), nontriviality (requiring at least one input feature), and a soft length prior. The operator set is {+, −, ×, ÷, log, exp, pow} with constants {0.2, 0.5, 2.0, 5.0}. The key insight is that the discrete symbolic search space, guided by a powerful neural generator, can discover compact expressions that match GPU-based GNN performance while being trivially deployable on CPU.

## Results

On the four standard MILP benchmarks (Setcover, Cauctions, Facilities, Indset) at Easy difficulty, Symb4CO achieves 1-shifted geometric mean solving times of 7.10s, 1.57s, 37.76s, and 29.21s respectively, outperforming all CPU-based baselines and matching or approaching the GPU-based GNN-GPU (7.74s, 1.74s, 34.95s, 25.13s). Symb4CO wins 67/80, 79/80, 43/80, and 32/80 instances against all other CPU policies on Easy, versus at most 23/80 for the next-best method. Inference time is 0.04–0.08 ms per decision (one CPU core), roughly 40–700× faster than MLP (3–9.6 ms), GNN (5.3–11.6 ms), and Trees (14.7–28.5 ms). Training requires only 10 instances (1,000 samples) versus 10,000 instances (100,000 samples) for all other ML baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the human-designed SCIP defaults (FSB, RPB, PB from Achterberg 2007), Symb4CO is dramatically faster than FSB (e.g., 7.10s vs 96.96s on Setcover-Easy) and consistently outperforms RPB and PB in both time and wins count across all four benchmarks. Against the ExtraTrees model (Alvarez 2017), Symb4CO wins on every benchmark by a wide margin in both time (e.g., 1.57s vs 3.50s on Cauctions) and wins (79/80 vs 0/80). Against the GNN model (Gasse 2019), Symb4CO running on a single CPU core matches or beats the GPU-accelerated GNN-GPU on time (e.g., 1.57s vs 1.74s on Cauctions-Easy) and substantially outperforms the CPU-only GNN (2.20s, 1/80 wins). Against the Hybrid and MLP models (Gupta 2020), Symb4CO achieves lower solving time and far more wins (e.g., 67/80 vs 6/80 on Setcover), while also being ~40× faster at inference (0.04 ms vs 3.0 ms). Against GPLearn (Stephens), the open-source genetic programming library, Symb4CO achieves higher imitation learning accuracy on all four benchmarks (e.g., 43.20% vs 38.56% on Cauctions), confirming that the deep RL-based search outperforms traditional evolutionary symbolic discovery.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the deep symbolic regression framework of Petersen (2019) and Landajuela et al. (2021)—an RNN generator trained with risk-seeking PPO to search over expression trees—to the specific problem of branching variable selection in branch-and-bound solvers. It departs from prior ML-for-branching work (Khalil 2016, Gasse 2019, Gupta 2020) by replacing the learned neural network with a compiled one-line mathematical expression, thereby eliminating GPU dependency and the black-box nature of the policy. The 91-dimensional feature vector and the full-strong-branching imitation target are inherited directly from Gupta et al. (2020), positioning Symb4CO as an extreme distillation of that hybrid model into a symbolic form.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: a one-line expression trained on 10 instances and deployed on a single CPU core matches GPU-based GNN performance, which directly addresses the long-standing deployment gap between ML research and industrial CO solvers. The cross-benchmark generalization of these compact policies (e.g., a Cauctions-trained policy outperforming RPB on Setcover) suggests that the learned scoring functions capture transferable structure rather than overfitting to a specific distribution. Open questions include whether symbolic discovery can handle graph-structured inputs, whether it extends to other solver components (cut selection, pricing, heuristics), and whether automated feature generation can replace the 91 hand-crafted features. The approach is currently limited to the branching task and relies on human-designed features, so its generality as an 'algorithm discovery' framework remains to be demonstrated.
