## Problem

Existing 2D-lifting methods for text-to-3D generation (e.g., DreamFusion, Magic3D) use single-view 2D diffusion models as score distillation priors, which lack multi-view knowledge and 3D-awareness. This leads to two persistent failures: the multi-face Janus problem (redundant faces or parts regenerated from different angles) and content drift (object identity morphs across views, e.g., a chicken becoming a waffle). The paper asks whether a diffusion model trained to jointly generate consistent multi-view images can serve as a generalizable 3D prior that resolves these issues without sacrificing the diversity of 2D diffusion.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt and a set of F camera extrinsic parameters (4×4 matrices normalized to the unit sphere, representing rotation only) and produces F consistent RGB images at 256×256 resolution (downscaled from the 512×512 base Stable Diffusion v2.1). For 3D generation, the multi-view diffusion model is used as an SDS prior to optimize a multi-resolution hash-grid NeRF over 10,000 steps. For the DreamBooth variant, a small collection of 2D subject images is added as fine-tuning input, and the output is a personalized 3D NeRF. The model is trained on 4 orthogonal views per object, though a random-view variant can generate up to 64 views at inference.

## Method

The core architectural change is inflating the existing 2D self-attention layers of Stable Diffusion v2.1 into 3D self-attention by reshaping the token tensor from B×F×H×W×C to B×FHW×C, so that all spatial tokens across all views attend to each other in a single attention operation while inheriting the original 2D attention weights. Camera embeddings are produced by a 2-layer MLP from the extrinsic matrices and injected as residuals into the time embeddings (rather than appended to text embeddings, which proved less robust). Training jointly fine-tunes on 3D-rendered multi-view data from Objaverse (70% of batches, 4 random orthogonal views per object) and 2D text-image pairs from LAION (30%, with 3D attention and camera embeddings disabled), preserving the base model's generalizability. For 3D generation, the multi-view model replaces the single-view diffusion prior in SDS, with three stabilization tricks: linear annealing of the time-step range, fixed negative prompts to avoid low-quality 3D-render styles, and an x₀-reconstruction loss (equivalent to SDS with w(t)=2σ_t/α_t) that enables CFG rescale on x̂₀ to prevent color saturation.

## Results

For multi-view image generation, MVDream achieves an IS of 12.97±0.60 and CLIP score of 30.38±3.50, matching or exceeding the validation set baseline (IS 12.90, CLIP 30.12), while the 3D-data-only variant degrades to IS 12.33 and CLIP 29.69. In the user study for 3D (NeRF) generation across 40 prompts, 78% of 38 users (914 total feedbacks) preferred MVDream's output over all baselines. In terms of runtime, MVDream takes approximately 2 hours on a single V100 GPU, comparable to DreamFusion-IF and Text2Mesh-IF, and substantially faster than Magic3D-IF (~3.5 h) and ProlificDreamer (>10 h).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All four text-to-3D baselines (DreamFusion-IF, Magic3D-IF, Text2Mesh-IF, ProlificDreamer) suffer from severe multi-view inconsistency such as the multi-face Janus problem and content drift, whereas MVDream generates coherent 3D assets with a single set of hyperparameters. Magic3D-IF performs relatively best among baselines thanks to its DMTet second-stage refinement, but still exhibits view-dependent content mismatch; ProlificDreamer produces high per-view texture quality yet the views do not compose into a geometrically reasonable object. In the user study, MVDream is preferred over the best baseline in 78% of cases. For personalized 3D generation, MVDream's multi-view DreamBooth outperforms DreamBooth3D in object detail fidelity (e.g., fur texture, curly hair) because its diffusion prior maintains stronger geometric consistency during NeRF optimization. Compared to the image-to-3D pipeline (SDXL + Zero123-XL), MVDream avoids pose-alignment distortions and produces richer novel-view textures, since Zero123-XL is bounded to simple poses from its 3D training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

MVDream extends the 2D-lifting paradigm for text-to-3D generation (DreamFusion, Magic3D) by replacing the single-view 2D diffusion prior with a multi-view diffusion model that inherently encodes cross-view consistency. Architecturally, it fine-tunes Stable Diffusion's UNet by inflating 2D self-attention into 3D self-attention across views and adding camera embeddings, drawing conceptual inspiration from video diffusion models while finding that temporal attention is insufficient for geometric consistency. The DreamBooth personalization framework is extended to the multi-view setting, streamlining the three-stage DreamBooth3D pipeline into a single fine-tuning step followed by NeRF optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MVDream addresses the two most visible failure modes of 2D-lifting methods (multi-face Janus problem and content drift) with a principled solution, and the 78% user preference over all baselines demonstrates practical robustness. The x0-reconstruction loss reformulation cleanly enables CFG rescale and dynamic thresholding within SDS, a useful trick for the broader text-to-3D community. Key limitations remain: 256×256 resolution, style bias inherited from the Objaverse rendered dataset, and generalizability bounded by the base Stable Diffusion model. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions (e.g., SDXL), whether random-view training can replace the fixed 4-view setup for arbitrary view counts, and how it combines with newer SDS variants like VSD.
