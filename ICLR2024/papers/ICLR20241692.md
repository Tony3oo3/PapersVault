## Problem

On-device applications require condensed datasets of flexible sizes due to fluctuating computational resources, but traditional dataset condensation produces a fixed-size output. Selecting a subset from a larger condensed dataset suffers from a 'subset degradation problem' where accuracy drops sharply compared to directly condensing to that smaller size, and running N separate condensation processes (each requiring ~200K epochs) is infeasible on resource-restricted devices.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20241692/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard labeled RGB image dataset (32×32×3 for CIFAR-10/100/SVHN, 224×224×3 for ImageNet-10) and produces a single synthetic dataset of N images per class. The critical structural property is that any prefix subset of the first k condensed images (k = 1, …, N) constitutes a valid, independently usable condensed dataset of size k, enabling on-the-fly size selection without any additional condensation or storage overhead beyond the N images themselves.

## Method

MDC compresses N separate condensation processes into a single one by adding an 'adaptive subset loss' on top of the standard gradient-matching condensation loss (IDC). At each condensation iteration, a Most Learnable Subset (MLS) is adaptively selected from the N−1 prefix subsets using three components: (i) computing feature distances between each subset and the real dataset via a single forward pass (avoiding N−1 backward passes), (ii) selecting the subset with the highest rate of change in feature distance over a window Δt, and (iii) a freezing judgement that, when the MLS grows larger than its predecessor, freezes the predecessor's elements and updates only the non-overlapping new elements. The synthetic dataset is updated with the sum of the base loss (full N-image set) and the subset loss (MLS), effectively injecting supervision from all N−1 smaller condensation objectives into one pass.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 targeting IPC_10, MDC achieves 58.37% average accuracy across subsets of size 1–10, outperforming the best SOTA baseline DREAM (55.50%) by 2.87% and IDC (53.15%) by 5.22%. Across SVHN, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100 at IPC_10, MDC improves average accuracy by +6.10%, +5.22%, and +3.13% over Baseline-C (single condensation + subset selection), respectively. At IPC_50 on CIFAR-10, the improvement over Baseline-C grows to +7.80% (62.01% vs. 54.21%). On ImageNet-10 at IPC_20, MDC achieves 67.62% average accuracy, surpassing Baseline-C by +1.04%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MDC consistently outperforms all five SOTA condensation baselines on CIFAR-10 IPC_10: it beats DC (Zhao et al., 2021) by 27.16% average, DSA (Zhao & Bilen, 2021) by 22.93%, MTT (Cazenavette et al., 2022) by 14.84%, IDC (Kim et al., 2022b) by 5.22%, and DREAM (Liu et al., 2023b) by 2.87%. The gap is largest at small subset sizes (IPC 1–3), where MDC achieves 49.66% at IPC_1 versus 32.52% for DREAM and 27.49% for IDC, directly addressing the 'subset degradation problem.' Against Slimmable Dataset Condensation (Liu et al., 2023a) on CIFAR-100 IPC_20, MDC exceeds the resource-intensive LBS variant by 3.52% average accuracy (37.02% vs. 33.50%) while requiring only a single condensation process and no SVD. Notably, MDC also surpasses Baseline-B (which requires N separate condensation runs and N× storage) by +5.37% on SVHN, +2.37% on CIFAR-10, and +5.41% on CIFAR-100 at IPC_10, despite using only one condensation process and N images of storage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on gradient-matching-based dataset condensation (Zhao et al., 2021) and the efficient synthetic-data parameterization framework of IDC (Kim et al., 2022b), adding an 'adaptive subset loss' on top of the standard condensation objective. It departs from prior multi-size approaches like Slimmable DC (Liu et al., 2023a), which requires two separate condensation processes and computationally expensive SVD, by compressing N independent condensation runs into a single process with a novel MLS selection mechanism. The paper also positions itself against GAN-based condensation methods that require additional model storage and inference-time computation, offering immediately usable condensed images instead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the 'subset degradation problem' — that naively selecting subsets from a condensed dataset yields substantially worse performance than direct condensation to that size — is a practical concern for on-device scenarios where computational resources fluctuate. The 94.8% training time reduction (15.1h → 0.6h to match baseline accuracy) suggests the adaptive subset loss acts as an implicit regularizer that accelerates convergence, an effect the authors flag as an open question. Limitations include a small accuracy drop on the full synthetic dataset due to the subset loss, and the method's dependence on a specific basic condensation method (IDC), though experiments with DREAM show some generality. Future work on better subset selection metrics and understanding the accelerated learning dynamics could broaden applicability.
